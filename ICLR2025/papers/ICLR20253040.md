## Problem

CLIP models are extremely vulnerable to poisoning backdoor attacks, where an adversary can achieve near-perfect attack success by poisoning only 0.01% of the training data, yet no efficient backdoor sample detection method exists for cleaning million-scale web datasets. Existing detection methods were designed for supervised learning and either fail on CLIP or are too slow for web-scale data (e.g., Cognitive Distillation takes 11.2 hours on CC3M). The paper asks whether the unique representation geometry of CLIP backdoor samples enables simple, fast detection.

## Input / Output

The method requires a pre-trained CLIP model (image encoder such as ResNet-50 or ViT-B-16, plus a text encoder) and an untrusted image-text dataset. For each image-text pair, the image embedding is used as a query point while a randomly sampled batch (size 2048) of image and text embeddings serves as reference points for neighborhood computation. The output is a scalar outlier score per sample (higher = more likely backdoored), from which the practitioner removes a fixed percentage (e.g., 10%) to produce a purified dataset suitable for retraining.

## Method

The core insight is that backdoor-poisoned samples in CLIP occupy a tightly clustered, isolated region in the joint representation space, making their local neighborhoods far sparser than those of clean samples. This sparsity is exploited via classic local density-ratio metrics: SLOF computes the average ratio of a point's k-th neighbor distance to its neighbors' k-th neighbor distances, while DAO generalizes SLOF by raising each ratio to the estimated local intrinsic dimensionality (LID) of the neighbor. Detection is performed by randomly sampling a batch, extracting all image and text embeddings, and scoring each image embedding against the batch using k=16 nearest neighbors. Surprisingly, these general-purpose outlier detectors (k-dist, SLOF, DAO) achieve 99%+ AUC across nine attack types and two architectures, outperforming dedicated backdoor detectors (ABL, CD, SafeCLIP) while running in ~15 minutes on 4 A100 GPUs versus hours for optimization-based alternatives.

## Results

On the CC3M dataset with ResNet-50, local outlier methods (k-dist, SLOF, DAO) achieve 97–100% AUC across all nine attack types (patch, clean-label, Nashville, WaNet, Blend, SIG, MT-S, MT-M, TDPA), consistently outperforming dedicated backdoor detection methods. For example, on the 0.01% patch trigger, SLOF and DAO reach 99.86% AUC versus 27.86% for ABL, 97.17% for CD, and 83.42% for SafeCLIP. On ViT-B-16, local methods still lead (95–99% AUC for most triggers) but with a notable drop on clean-label (69–71% AUC). Filtering 10% of CC3M by DAO score reduces ASR to <1% for most triggers while preserving clean accuracy (e.g., 16.2% vs. 17.0% for patch).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ABL (Li et al., 2021), local outlier methods win decisively: ABL achieves only 27–65% AUC across all triggers on RN50, while k-dist/SLOF/DAO consistently reach 97–100%. Against CD (Huang et al., 2023), the comparison is more nuanced: CD performs well on dirty-label attacks (97–99% AUC) but collapses on clean-label (48.68% on RN50, 50.30% on ViT-B-16) and is catastrophically weak on ViT-B-16 Patch (8.40% AUC), whereas local methods maintain 95%+ AUC in nearly all settings. SafeCLIP (Yang et al., 2024) achieves moderate detection (44–86% AUC) and is notably weaker than local methods on most triggers, though it is competitive on WaNet (85.82% vs. 99.85%). iForest (Liu et al., 2008) is the strongest non-local baseline, reaching 99.73% on Patch and 99.95% on TDPA, but still trails SLOF/DAO by 0.1–0.2% on most triggers. LID (Houle et al., 2018) is the least reliable baseline, ranging from 43% (BLTO on RN50) to 99% (Patch), and is particularly sensitive to poisoning rate changes (dropping to 0.29% AUC at 1% poisoning rate), unlike k-dist which remains at 100%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes classic density-based outlier detection metrics—SLOF, DAO, and k-dist—originally developed for general-purpose anomaly detection, to solve the CLIP backdoor sample detection problem. It departs from the dominant line of backdoor defense research (ABL, Cognitive Distillation, SafeCLIP), which was designed for supervised learning and relies on class labels or optimization-based mask extraction, and shows these methods fail or underperform in the label-free CLIP setting. The attack setup follows Carlini & Terzis (2022), but the detection insight—that poisoned samples form sparse local neighborhoods in the contrastive representation space—is novel and motivates the use of locality-based density ratios rather than trigger-specific or loss-based signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a million-scale web dataset (CC3M) can be purified in 15 minutes on 4 A100 GPUs, making dataset-level backdoor defense feasible for the first time at web scale. The discovery of an unintentional backdoor (birthday cake images) already embedded in a popular OpenCLIP model underscores the urgency of such tooling. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to text-side poisoning, to training-manipulation attacks, and to even larger datasets (CC12M results are consistent but limited in scope). A limitation is that detection requires a pre-trained model to extract representations, so it is inherently post-training and cannot prevent the initial poisoning from affecting the model used for detection.
