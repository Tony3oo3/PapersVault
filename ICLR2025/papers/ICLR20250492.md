## Problem

Existing image-text interleaved datasets (e.g., MMC4, OBELICS) are limited to ~140M documents, predominantly English, and sourced almost exclusively from Common Crawl, restricting content diversity and hindering the scaling of multimodal LLMs. The paper addresses this gap by constructing a 10-billion-level interleaved corpus that spans English and Chinese websites as well as video-centric platforms, while maintaining data quality comparable to LLM training corpora.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw HTML documents from Common Crawl and Chinese websites, plus video keyframes with audio transcriptions from YT-Temporal1B, HD-VILA-100M, HowTo100M, and InternVid. The output is a unified 'streaming data format' where each document is a top-to-bottom, left-to-right interleaved sequence of tagged elements (<text>, <image>, <code>, <table>, etc.), enriched with per-image and per-document meta-annotations (aesthetic score, NSFW probability, political/toxicity scores, fluency, CLIP image-text similarity). This format is degradable: the same documents can be reinterpreted as pure text corpora, image-text pairs, or interleaved sequences without reprocessing.

## Method

The data engine comprises five sequential stages: (1) main body extraction via an improved Trafilatura that handles CJK/Arabic and trims navigation/ad regions, (2) preliminary text filtering using Gopher/C4 heuristics plus custom rules (removing ~80% of documents), (3) minhash-based document deduplication at threshold 0.8, (4) image downloading with Bloom-filter URL deduplication followed by size/aspect-ratio/aesthetic/NSFW/perceptual-hash filtering, and (5) detailed text filtering using WanJuan-CC BERT classifiers plus a human-feedback loop that iteratively refines ~30 English and ~40 Chinese filtering rules. Key engineering innovations include pre-deduplication before expensive image download and GPU filtering (saving ~86 PB·s bandwidth and 4500 A100 GPU-days), a modular parallel pipeline that allocates resources by throughput rather than evenly (1.39× speedup), and a unified streaming format that lets heterogeneous sources (web pages, video storyboards, image-text pairs) be processed identically. The paper further validates the dataset through ablations on image position strategies (natural vs. retrieval-based), curated subset sizes, and comparisons against MMC4/OBELICS/OpenFlamingo/IDEFICS on VQA and captioning benchmarks.

## Results

The paper's 7B model (InternLM2-7B + CLIP-ViT-L, pre-trained on OmniCorpus-CC) achieves 102.8 CIDEr on COCO, 70.6 on Flickr30k, 54.1% on OKVQA, 55.2% on TextVQA, 71.6% on VQAv2, and 57.6% on VizWiz at 4-shot. This outperforms the larger 9B OpenFlamingo (89.0/65.8/40.1/28.2/54.8/34.1) and IDEFICS (93.0/59.7/45.4/27.6/55.4/36.9) on every benchmark, with the largest margins on TextVQA (+27.0 over OpenFlamingo) and VQAv2 (+16.8 over OpenFlamingo). In the dataset-level comparison (Table 4), OmniCorpus-CC achieves the best average few-shot score (58.7) among all interleaved datasets, surpassing OBELICS (58.2) and MMC4 (47.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MMC4 (Zhu et al., 2024), OmniCorpus-CC achieves substantially higher few-shot scores across all four benchmarks (e.g., 58.7 vs. 47.3 average at 4-shot), with the gap widening as more in-context examples are added, indicating stronger in-context learning capacity. Against OBELICS (Laurençon et al., 2024a), OmniCorpus-CC edges out on OKVQA (41.1 vs. 40.2) and TextVQA (42.0 vs. 40.7) at 4-shot while OBELICS slightly leads on Flickr30k (54.7 vs. 54.3), making them nearly tied overall. Against OpenFlamingo-9B (Awadalla et al., 2023), the paper's smaller 7B model wins on every reported metric, with particularly dramatic gains on TextVQA (55.2 vs. 28.2) and VQAv2 (71.6 vs. 54.8), attributed to the larger and higher-quality OmniCorpus-CC pre-training data. Against Emu-14B (Sun et al., 2023), the 7B model surpasses the 14B model on VQAv2 (71.6 vs. 58.4) and VizWiz (57.6 vs. 41.3), though Emu's COCO/Flickr30k scores are not reported for direct comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-text interleaved dataset line initiated by MMC4 (Zhu et al., 2024) and OBELICS (Laurençon et al., 2024a), which themselves built on the paired-image-text paradigm of LAION-5B and COYO-700M. The data engine draws on established components—Trafilatura for main-body extraction, Gopher/C4 heuristics for text filtering, minhash for deduplication, and LAION aesthetic/NSFW predictors for image filtering—but introduces a unified streaming data format and a human-feedback filtering loop that go beyond prior pipelines. The empirical evaluation follows the LLaVA-1.5 and Flamingo architectural families used in OpenFlamingo, IDEFICS, and Emu, positioning OmniCorpus as a drop-in data upgrade rather than a new modeling paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first 10-billion-level open-source interleaved corpus, OmniCorpus addresses the practical bottleneck of Common Crawl image exhaustion and the English-only bias that has constrained prior interleaved datasets, making it a likely default resource for future MLLM pretraining. The finding that aggressive filtering (200M curated subset) can outperform the full 988M corpus due to data homogenization is a useful caution for practitioners scaling data curation. Open questions include whether the scale advantage materializes at full 2.2B-document training (all ablations use only 1M samples), whether the bilingual and video-derived documents transfer to non-English MLLMs, and how the streaming format's flexibility affects downstream task performance compared to task-specific formats.
