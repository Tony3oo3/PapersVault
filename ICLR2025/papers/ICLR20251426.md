## Problem

Traditional set-to-set matching metrics such as Chamfer Distance and Earth Mover's Distance measure observation-level differences but fail to capture distributional similarity between sets, i.e., how likely two sets are to originate from the same underlying distribution. This limitation is particularly problematic in tasks like few-shot classification where the relevant similarity is in a latent class space rather than in raw feature distances. The paper identifies that the statistical structure of KNN distances between sets is underexploited and can be modeled more faithfully than existing metrics allow.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251426/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two unordered sets of feature vectors (e.g., image patch embeddings from a ResNet-12 backbone for few-shot classification, or predicted vs. ground-truth 3D point clouds for completion) and produces a single scalar similarity score κ(X₁, X₂). The score is computed by evaluating Gumbel PDFs at the reparameterized KNN distances (x = −log(d_min + δ)) between the two sets, with δ chosen per task (positive for point cloud completion to handle the zero-distance mode, zero for few-shot classification). The negative of this score serves directly as a differentiable loss, preserving the same O(N) computational complexity as CD.

## Method

GPS (Gumbel Prior Similarity) models the distribution of minimum Euclidean distances between K nearest neighbors of two sets using a mixture of M Gumbel distributions, motivated by the empirical observation that these distance distributions fit Gumbel curves well. The key reparameterization x = −log(d_min + δ) converts the KNN distances (which are conditional minima) into a form amenable to Gumbel modeling (which characterizes maxima), with the Gumbel parameters (α, β) predefined per KNN rank and mixture component. The similarity is the sum of Gumbel PDF evaluations over all points in both sets, derived from a probabilistic graphical model where Gumbel distributions act as latent variables mediating the conditional probability p(P₁=P₂ | X₁, X₂). A theoretical analysis shows that the gradient of the objective f(x)=xe^{−x} decays exponentially as distances approach the Gumbel mode, causing the network to adaptively focus training on poorly matched points. In practice, a single Gumbel with 1st NN (α=5, β=2 for few-shot; α=1, β=1 for point clouds) suffices, and the method drops into existing DeepEMD or point cloud completion pipelines by simply replacing the CD/EMD loss term.

## Results

On few-shot image classification, GPS achieves 66.27% (1-shot) and 81.19% (5-shot) 5-way accuracy on miniImageNet, and 73.16% (1-shot) and 85.52% (5-shot) on tieredImageNet, outperforming the strongest baseline InfoCD by 2.26% and 0.32% respectively on miniImageNet. On CIFAR-FS, GPS reaches 74.22% (1-shot) and 86.98% (5-shot), a 1.91% gain over InfoCD in the 1-shot setting. For point cloud completion on ShapeNet-Part, GPS achieves 3.94 L2-CD×10³, beating InfoCD (4.01) and HyperCD (4.03), while maintaining the same linear computational complexity as vanilla Chamfer Distance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPS outperforms all 16 compared baselines across every dataset and both tasks. In few-shot classification, the closest competitor is InfoCD (contrastive Chamfer Distance), which GPS surpasses by 2.26% on miniImageNet 1-shot (66.27% vs 64.01%), 2.19% on tieredImageNet 1-shot (73.16% vs 70.97%), and 1.91% on CIFAR-FS 1-shot (74.22% vs 72.31%). HyperCD (hyperbolic Chamfer Distance) is the second-closest baseline, trailing GPS by 2.64% on miniImageNet 1-shot and 2.24% on tieredImageNet 1-shot. Traditional metrics (CD, PWD, SWD, GSWD, ASWD) and earlier methods (DeepEMD, ProtoNet, MatchingNet) are consistently 2–10% behind GPS. In point cloud completion, GPS improves over InfoCD by 0.07 L2-CD×10³ on ShapeNet-Part (3.94 vs 4.01) and by 0.04–0.10 across ShapeNet-34/55 categories, while also reducing MMD on KITTI from 0.333 (InfoCD+FoldingNet) to 0.302 (GPS+FoldingNet). Notably, GPS achieves these gains while maintaining the same O(N) computational complexity as vanilla CD, unlike EMD/Wasserstein-based methods which are significantly slower.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPS carves new ground by repurposing Gumbel distributions—classically used for extreme-value modeling—to fit the distribution of KNN minimum distances between two sets, drawing a conceptual bridge from distributional similarity in NLP (Lee, 2000) to set-to-set matching. Rather than extending a single prior method, it positions itself as a drop-in replacement for CD/EMD/WD and their recent variants (DCD, HyperCD, InfoCD), maintaining the same linear complexity as CD while introducing a probabilistic graphical model over the distance statistics. The experimental framework is borrowed from DeepEMD, and the point-cloud-completion experiments plug GPS into seven existing backbones (FoldingNet, PoinTr, SeedFormer, etc.), making the contribution primarily a new loss/metric rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GPS offers a clean, theoretically grounded alternative to CD that consistently improves accuracy across five few-shot benchmarks and seven point-cloud-completion backbones while preserving CD's O(n) complexity, making it a practical drop-in for existing pipelines. The gradient analysis (Proposition 1) reveals an implicit adaptive weighting mechanism—samples near the Gumbel mode receive smaller gradients—providing a principled explanation for why the loss focuses training on harder examples. A key open question is the hyperparameter sensitivity: the paper acknowledges that grid-searching α and β is time-consuming for large point sets (16,384 points), and no automatic or learned parameterization is provided. Broader implications include whether the Gumbel-fitting idea generalizes beyond Euclidean KNN distances to other distance structures or non-point-set modalities.
