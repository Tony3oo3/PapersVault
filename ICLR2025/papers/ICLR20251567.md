## Problem

Existing online RLHF methods like Online DPO rely on passive exploration—sampling responses directly from the current policy without an explicit mechanism to encourage diversity. The paper proves this is fundamentally insufficient: when the reference policy has poor coverage of the optimal behavior, Online DPO requires exponentially many iterations in 1/β to discover novel responses. No simple, provably sample-efficient online exploration algorithm existed for RLHF with general function approximation.

## Input / Output

At each of T iterations, the algorithm receives a prompt s₁, samples one response τ from the current policy π⁽ᵗ⁾ and a second response τ̃ from the fixed reference policy π_ref (not both from π⁽ᵗ⁾ as in Online DPO), and receives a Bradley-Terry preference label. The preference dataset accumulates across iterations. The final output is the best iterate selected via validation data, yielding a near-optimal KL-regularized policy. The framework applies to any Deterministic Contextual MDP (DCMDP) with stochastic start state and deterministic transitions, strictly generalizing the token-level MDP.

## Method

XPO augments the standard DPO objective with a single exploration bonus term α·Σᵢ log π(τ̃⁽ⁱ⁾), where τ̃⁽ⁱ⁾ are responses sampled from the reference policy. The key insight is that DPO implicitly performs Bellman error minimization (Q*-approximation) in a KL-regularized MDP: the log-likelihood ratio β log π(τ)/π_ref(τ) decomposes into the true reward minus a value offset plus a sum of Bellman errors. The exploration bonus biases the policy toward a large value function (V_β* ≲ V_β^π), implementing implicit global optimism in the face of uncertainty. The analysis proceeds via a regret decomposition separating optimism error from reward-model accuracy, then a coverability-based potential argument that bounds on-policy error by the off-policy XPO objective. The resulting sample complexity scales polynomially with the trajectory coverability coefficient C_cov(Π) rather than the reference-policy concentrability, and avoids the exp(1/β) dependence that plagues passive exploration.

## Results

The main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) shows XPO achieves suboptimality $J_\beta(\pi_\beta^\star) - J_\beta(\hat{\pi}) \lesssim (V_{\max}+R_{\max})e^{2R_{\max}} \cdot \sqrt{C_{\text{cov}}(\Pi)\log(|\Pi|T\delta^{-1})\log^2(T)/T}$, yielding $\widetilde{O}(C_{\text{cov}}(\Pi)\log|\Pi|/\varepsilon^2)$ episodes for $\varepsilon$-optimality. This is a strict improvement over prior online RLHF methods that scale with the concentrability coefficient $C_{\text{conc}}(\Pi)=\exp(V_{\max}/\beta)$, which can be exponentially larger than $C_{\text{cov}}(\Pi)$. For tabular MDPs, the bound specializes to $\widetilde{O}(R_{\max}e^{2R_{\max}}\sqrt{H|S|^2|A|^2/T})$, and for linear MDPs to $\widetilde{O}(R_{\max}e^{2R_{\max}}\sqrt{Hd^2/T})$. Proposition 2.1 establishes that Online DPO (passive exploration) requires $\exp(1/\beta)$ iterations in the worst case, a gap XPO provably closes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or datasets. The comparisons are between sample complexity guarantees. XPO's guarantee scales with the coverability coefficient $C_{\text{cov}}(\Pi)$, which is a strict improvement over prior online RLHF methods (Xiong et al. 2023; Gao et al. 2024; Chang et al. 2024) that scale with $C_{\text{conc}}(\Pi)=\exp(V_{\max}/\beta)$ — the worst-case ratio between these two quantities is exponential. Compared to Online DPO (Guo et al. 2024), XPO avoids the exponential-in-$1/\beta$ sample complexity shown in Proposition 2.1, even when $C_{\text{cov}}(\Pi)\leq 2$. Against concurrent work (Cen et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2024), XPO provides guarantees for general function approximation in the full RL setting (not just contextual bandits) and avoids exponential dependence on the KL regularization parameter $\beta$. The paper also notes that prior general-function-approximation RL exploration algorithms (Jiang et al. 2017; Jin et al. 2021; Xie et al. 2023) are computationally intractable, whereas XPO's objective is differentiable and directly implementable with language models.

## Relation to Prior Work

XPO is a one-line modification to Online DPO (Guo et al., 2024), adding an exploration bonus term α·Σ log π(τ̃) to the DPO objective. The key conceptual bridge is the observation (generalizing Rafailov et al., 2024) that DPO implicitly performs Bellman error minimization to approximate Q* in a KL-regularized MDP, which allows the authors to import the optimism-in-the-face-of-uncertainty principle from RL theory (Jiang et al., 2017; Xie et al., 2023) into the preference-optimization setting. Prior online RLHF methods (Online DPO, Iterative DPO) engage only in passive exploration, while prior theoretical work on sample-efficient RLHF either restricts to tabular/linear MDPs or requires computationally intractable optimization; XPO is the first to achieve provable sample efficiency with general function approximation via a simple, differentiable objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably sample-efficient online exploration algorithm for RLHF with general function approximation, and the one-line modification makes it directly implementable in existing DPO pipelines. The implicit Q*-approximation perspective on DPO opens a pathway for importing broader RL theory tools (more powerful exploration principles, instance-dependent bounds) into language model alignment. Key limitations include restriction to deterministic contextual MDPs (the DPO objective does not extend to stochastic dynamics), non-convex optimization that becomes harder as β→0, and the absence of empirical validation. Open questions include extending to stochastic transitions, general preference models, and deriving tighter bounds for large β.
