## Problem

Existing SDS-based 3D editing methods, particularly Posterior Distillation Sampling (PDS), suffer from slow training and inferior editing quality due to a fundamental conflict between their stochastic latent matching loss and the sampling dynamics of diffusion models. At low noise levels, PDS over-prioritizes identity preservation over editability, while at high noise levels, the approximated diffusion reverse process destroys source identity—making it impossible to simultaneously achieve fast, high-quality edits with strong identity preservation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source 3D scene represented as either a NeRF (trained via Nerfstudio's nerfacto) or a 3DGS (splatfacto), along with a source text prompt and a target text prompt, and produces an edited 3D scene in the same representation. It uses InstructPix2Pix (IP2P) as the pretrained diffusion model, which conditions on both the source image and text instructions, eliminating the need for DreamBooth or LoRA fine-tuning. The method offers two modes: a fast mode (1,000 iterations, ~25 min for NeRF) and a high-quality mode (3,000 iterations, ~70 min for NeRF), both using a DDIM scheduler with 500 inference steps and a classifier-free guidance weight of 7.5.

## Method

DreamCatalyst reinterprets the Delta Denoising Score (DDS) objective as equivalent to a single-step DDIM-based SDEdit sampling, then formulates the editing loss as an explicit combination of an identity preservation regularizer R_iden and the DDS loss, weighted by timestep-dependent coefficients Φ(t) and Ψ(t). The specialized formulation sets Φ*(t) = χe^(t/T) and Ψ*(t) = δ + γ√(t/T), ensuring strong identity preservation at large timesteps (preserving source structure early in the reverse process) while reducing identity constraints at small timesteps (allowing fine-detail synthesis). This reweighting enables decreasing timestep sampling (T→1) that approximates the diffusion reverse process, dramatically reducing training time. Additionally, FreeU is integrated into the U-Net decoder to suppress high-frequency features (enhancing editability) while amplifying low-frequency backbone features (preserving identity), all without any additional parameters, memory, or computation.

## Results

On NeRF scenes, DreamCatalyst (high-quality mode) achieves the best scores across all three metrics: CLIP directional similarity 0.180, CLIP image similarity 0.746, and aesthetic score 5.688, while completing edits in ~70 minutes. The fast mode (~25 min) is approximately 23× faster than PDS (~580 min) and still outperforms both PDS and IN2N on all quality metrics. On 3DGS scenes, DreamCatalyst as a model-agnostic method (CLIP-Direc 0.171, CLIP-Img 0.724, Aesthetic 5.336) substantially outperforms PDS (0.108, 0.627, 4.977), and when integrated with GaussianEditor it achieves the best CLIP-Img (0.773) and Aesthetic (5.588) among model-specific methods. In user studies with 100 participants, DreamCatalyst is preferred over baselines by nearly 3× across prompt alignment (58.29%), overall quality (60.71%), and identity preservation (59.74%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamCatalyst outperforms all four baselines on every quality metric. Against PDS (the closest SDS-based competitor), DreamCatalyst improves CLIP directional similarity from 0.161 to 0.180 (+11.8%), CLIP image similarity from 0.687 to 0.746 (+8.6%), and aesthetic score from 5.437 to 5.688 (+4.6%), while reducing editing time from ~580 min to ~70 min (~8× speedup). Against IN2N, which operates in 2D space and is inherently faster (~130 min), DreamCatalyst still wins on all three quality metrics (0.180 vs 0.157 CLIP-Direc, 0.746 vs 0.722 CLIP-Img, 5.688 vs 5.399 Aesthetic) and is 1.85× faster in high-quality mode. On 3DGS scenes, DreamCatalyst as a model-agnostic method dramatically surpasses PDS (CLIP-Direc 0.171 vs 0.108, CLIP-Img 0.724 vs 0.627), and when plugged into GaussianEditor it achieves the best CLIP-Img (0.773) and Aesthetic (5.588) among all model-specific 3DGS methods, including DGE (0.758, 5.517) and GE(IN2N) (0.765, 5.508). The user study confirms these quantitative advantages: DreamCatalyst receives 58–61% of human preference votes versus 19–23% for each baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the Delta Denoising Score (DDS) objective as equivalent to a single-step DDIM-based SDEdit reverse process, building on DreamSampler's observation but extending it to the general editing setting where source and target differ. It departs from PDS (the most direct predecessor) by replacing the stochastic latent matching loss with a reweighted formulation that explicitly separates identity preservation and editability coefficients as functions of timestep, thereby resolving the conflict between PDS's loss and the diffusion reverse sampling dynamics. Architecturally, it substitutes the costly LoRA/DreamBooth fine-tuning used by prior SDS-based editors with FreeU, a zero-parameter feature-scaling technique that enhances editability without additional training or memory overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 23× (fast) and 8× (high-quality) speedups over PDS, combined with superior quality on both NeRF and 3DGS, make text-driven 3D editing substantially more practical for iterative creative workflows. The model-agnostic nature of the formulation means it can be dropped into any SDS-based 3D editing pipeline as the 3D representation evolves. A key limitation is the dependence on InstructPix2Pix's 2D editing quality: when IP2P fails on a prompt in 2D, the 3D edit inherits that failure, capping the method's ceiling until better instruction-following diffusion models are available. Open questions include whether the timestep-reweighting strategy generalizes to other diffusion backbones (e.g., video diffusion for 4D editing) and whether the proximal single-step inversion can be further accelerated.
