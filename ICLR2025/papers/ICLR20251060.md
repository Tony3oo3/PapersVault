## Problem

Linear RNN architectures face a fundamental tension in memory management: Mamba2's uniform gating decays all key-value associations equally, making it unable to selectively retain or forget specific information, while DeltaNet's delta rule enables targeted updates but lacks a mechanism to rapidly clear outdated memory during context switches. This paper identifies that gating (for adaptive erasure) and the delta rule (for precise modification) are complementary mechanisms that have not been jointly exploited in a single, hardware-efficient architecture.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text token sequence (trained at 4K context length, evaluated up to 20K for length extrapolation) and produces next-token predictions via a fixed-size state matrix of dimension d_v × d_k that stores key-value associations. Hybrid variants (H1, H2) additionally process a 2K-token sliding window through local attention layers interleaved with the recurrent layers, giving the model access to both a compressed long-range memory and an exact short-range context window.

## Method

The core contribution is the gated delta rule S_t = S_{t-1}(α_t(I − β_t k_t k_t^T)) + β_t v_t k_t^T, which unifies a data-dependent scalar gate α_t ∈ (0,1) for adaptive state decay with the delta update structure for selective key-value replacement. The authors derive a hardware-efficient chunkwise parallel training algorithm by extending the WY representation (used to parallelize DeltaNet's Householder matrix products) to incorporate the cumulative decay factors γ, yielding a UT-transform-based matrix formulation that preserves tensor-core-friendly matmul structure. From an online learning perspective, the rule corresponds to test-time SGD on a key-value regression objective with an adaptive weight-decay term α_t. Hybrid architectures (GatedDeltaNet-H1: Gated DeltaNet + SWA; H2: Mamba2 + Gated DeltaNet + SWA) are also introduced, combining the recurrent layers with 2K-window sliding attention to offload local modeling and reduce memory-management pressure on the fixed-size state.

## Results

On 1.3B-parameter models trained on 100B FineWeb-Edu tokens, Gated DeltaNet achieves the best perplexity among pure recurrent models (WikiText 16.42, LAMBADA 12.17) and the highest average zero-shot commonsense reasoning accuracy (55.32%), outperforming Mamba2 (54.89%), DeltaNet (52.14%), and Transformer++ (52.25%). The hybrid variant Gated DeltaNet-H1 (Gated DeltaNet + Sliding Window Attention) further improves to 56.40% average accuracy and 16.07 WikiText perplexity, surpassing all baselines including Samba (54.00%) and Transformer++ (52.25%). On real-world in-context retrieval (Table 4), Gated DeltaNet achieves 30.6% average accuracy, beating DeltaNet (26.2%) and Mamba2 (29.8%), while the hybrid H2 variant reaches 40.1%, exceeding Transformer++ (37.0%) and Samba (37.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gated DeltaNet consistently outperforms all pure recurrent baselines: it beats Mamba2 by 0.43 points in average commonsense accuracy (55.32 vs. 54.89) and by 0.39 in LAMBADA perplexity (12.17 vs. 12.56), while surpassing DeltaNet by a larger margin of 3.18 points (55.32 vs. 52.14), confirming that the gating mechanism compensates for DeltaNet's lack of memory clearance. Against attention-based baselines, Gated DeltaNet exceeds Transformer++ by 3.07 points (55.32 vs. 52.25) and Samba by 1.32 points (55.32 vs. 54.00) in average accuracy, despite using linear-time recurrence rather than quadratic attention. On real-world in-context retrieval (Table 4), Gated DeltaNet (30.6% avg) beats DeltaNet (26.2%) and Mamba2 (29.8%) but still trails Transformer++ (37.0%) and Samba (37.3%), a gap the authors attribute to repetition errors in instruction-unaligned small models. The hybrid variants close this gap decisively: Gated DeltaNet-H1 (39.0%) and H2 (40.1%) surpass both Transformer++ and Samba on retrieval, while also achieving the best overall commonsense accuracy (56.40% and 56.18% respectively). On synthetic S-NIAH benchmarks, Gated DeltaNet shows the expected complementary strengths—near-perfect retention on S-NIAH-1 (91.8% at 8K vs. Mamba2's 30.4%) and superior filtering on S-NIAH-2/3—validating the design motivation that gating handles erasure while the delta rule handles targeted updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate mechanisms in linear RNNs: the data-dependent gating (forget) term from Mamba2, which uniformly decays all key-value associations, and the delta update rule from DeltaNet, which selectively replaces a single key-value pair. The authors show these are complementary—gating enables rapid memory erasure while the delta rule enables targeted updates—and derive a simple combined 'gated delta rule' whose chunkwise parallel training algorithm extends the WY-representation-based algorithm from Yang et al. (2024b) by incorporating decay factors. The formalization via the online learning framework of Liu et al. (2024) and the fast weight programming / test-time SGD perspective provide a unifying lens that positions the gated delta rule as SGD with adaptive weight decay, connecting it to Titans and TTT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gated delta rule offers a principled, hardware-efficient upgrade to both Mamba2 and DeltaNet with essentially zero additional training overhead, making it a practical drop-in improvement for linear RNN practitioners. The S-NIAH case study cleanly isolates the complementary strengths (decay hurts retention, gating aids filtering, delta rule aids memorization), giving practitioners a clear mental model for when each mechanism matters. Open questions include whether the advantage persists at much larger scales (all experiments are at 1.3B), how the approach behaves on sequences well beyond 20K tokens, and whether the hybrid stacking order (Mamba2 → Gated DeltaNet → SWA) generalizes or is dataset-specific. The concurrent RWKV-7 work with diagonal-plus-low-rank transitions suggests this design space is being actively explored.
