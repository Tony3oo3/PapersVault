## Problem

The linear representation hypothesis was previously formalized only for binary concepts definable by counterfactual word pairs (e.g., male→female), leaving many natural concepts—such as binary features (is_animal) and categorical concepts ({mammal, bird, reptile, fish})—without a precise geometric formalization. Furthermore, how semantic hierarchy between concepts (e.g., animal ⊃ mammal ⊃ dog) is encoded in the geometry of LLM representation space remained unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the unembedding vectors of the full vocabulary (256K tokens in 2048 dimensions for Gemma-2B) and the WordNet noun/verb synset hierarchy (593 noun and 364 verb synsets with ≥50 vocabulary words each). It produces a unified geometric structure: a vector ℓ̄_w for each binary feature (with a well-defined magnitude b_w), a polytope (convex hull of vertex vectors) for each categorical concept, and an orthogonality relation between representations of hierarchically related concepts. All geometry is defined in a whitened, centered space where the Euclidean inner product equals the causal inner product.

## Method

The paper extends the linear representation hypothesis from directions to vectors by proving (Theorem 4) that a binary feature's representation must assign a constant dot product b_w to all tokens with that attribute and zero to all others, thereby fixing a natural magnitude. Categorical concepts are then represented as polytopes whose vertices are the vector representations of their constituent binary features (Definition 7). The central theoretical result (Theorem 8, Hierarchical Orthogonality) proves that if z ≺ w (z is subordinate to w), then ℓ̄_w ⊥ (ℓ̄_z − ℓ̄_w), meaning the 'residual' direction distinguishing a child from its parent is orthogonal to the parent's representation—yielding a direct-sum decomposition of the representation space. Empirically, vector representations are estimated via a shrinkage-regularized LDA direction (eq. 5.2) on WordNet token sets, and the predicted orthogonality and polytope structure are validated on Gemma-2B and LLaMA-3-8B, with controls using shuffled unembeddings and random parent assignments to rule out high-dimensional geometry artifacts.

## Results

The paper validates its theoretical predictions on Gemma-2B and LLaMA-3-8B using 900+ WordNet synsets. The key quantitative result (Table 1) shows that adding the linear representation of a parent binary contrast to context embeddings changes the parent's logit differences substantially (e.g., 5.13±1.17 for plant⇒animal, 14.42±0.95 for scientist⇒contestant) while leaving child concept logit differences essentially unchanged (e.g., −0.06±1.22 for mammal⇒reptile, −0.15±1.14 for athlete⇒player). Hierarchical orthogonality (Theorem 8) is confirmed: the cosine similarity between a child-parent vector and the parent vector is close to 0 across the WordNet hierarchy, while a random parent yields significantly non-zero values. Vector representations exist for most binary features, with test-word projections near 1 and random-word projections near 0 (Figure 3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical/interpretability paper, so it does not benchmark against competing methods. Instead, it uses several controls to rule out trivial explanations. (1) Shuffled unembeddings: when unembedding vectors are randomly permuted, test-word projections collapse to ~0 and hierarchical orthogonality breaks (cosine similarity becomes non-zero), confirming that the observed structure depends on semantic content rather than set-inclusion artifacts. (2) Random parent features: replacing the true parent with a randomly selected feature yields cosine similarities far from 0, showing the observed orthogonality is not merely a consequence of high-dimensional geometry where random vectors are nearly orthogonal. (3) Naive Euclidean inner product (without whitening): hierarchical orthogonality is not satisfied, confirming that the causal inner product (via whitening) is essential. (4) Set-inclusion control: using only 70% of tokens independently per synset breaks the set-inclusion relationship; orthogonality is preserved for original unembeddings but broken for shuffled ones, isolating semantic hierarchy as the source of the geometric structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Park et al. (2024), which formalized the linear representation hypothesis for binary concepts as directions in a causal inner product space, by promoting representations from directions to vectors and from binary to categorical concepts. The vector representation enables polytope geometry for categorical concepts and, crucially, allows the authors to prove that semantic hierarchy is encoded as orthogonality (a direct sum structure). The work also connects to Jiang et al. (2023) on partial orthogonality for independence structures and to the broader linear-representation literature (Mikolov et al. 2013; Elhage et al. 2022; Nanda et al. 2023), while explicitly positioning against hyperbolic-geometry approaches to hierarchy that the authors do not find in LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The remarkably simple direct-sum structure (hierarchy = orthogonality, categories = polytopes) provides a principled geometric framework that should reshape how interpretability methods like sparse autoencoders are designed: rather than searching for co-linear features like 'animal,' 'mammal,' 'bird,' one should target the orthogonal decomposition (ℓ_animal, ℓ_mammal − ℓ_animal, ℓ_bird − ℓ_animal). A key limitation is that the analysis applies only to the final-layer representation via the softmax/unembedding geometry; extending the causal inner product to internal transformer layers remains an open problem. The paper also does not explain why linear representations emerge during training, leaving the existence question to prior work (Jiang et al. 2024).
