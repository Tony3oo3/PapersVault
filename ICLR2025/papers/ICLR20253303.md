## Problem

Prior brain-encoding studies with multimodal models relied on image-caption pairs (e.g., CLIP), leaving the representational potential of instruction-tuned MLLMs underexplored. It remains unknown whether prompting MLLMs with diverse natural language instructions produces differentiated, instruction-specific representations that align with distinct visual brain regions, or whether all instructions yield largely overlapping brain alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253303/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes natural scene images (COCO, 10,000 per participant) paired with one of 10 task-specific text instructions, alongside fMRI recordings from 4 NSD participants (13,000–16,000 voxels in the 'nsd-general' posterior cortex region, averaged over 3 repetitions). MLLM embeddings are extracted via zero-shot generation—averaging hidden states of output tokens across 33 layers, then PCA-reduced to 1024 dimensions—and the primary output is a per-voxel, per-subject normalized brain alignment score (Pearson correlation divided by cross-subject prediction accuracy), supplemented by pairwise shared/unique variance partitions between instructions.

## Method

Three instruction-tuned MLLMs (InstructBLIP, mPLUG-Owl, IDEFICS; 32 layers, 4096-dim hidden states) are run in zero-shot mode, where each image-instruction pair yields generated text tokens whose hidden states are averaged and PCA-projected to 1024 dimensions. These embeddings are mapped to fMRI voxel activity via per-subject, per-voxel bootstrap ridge regression encoding models, with performance quantified as normalized brain alignment (Pearson correlation divided by cross-subject prediction accuracy, thresholded at ≥0.05). The paper probes 10 instructions spanning 6 visual task categories (captioning, VQA, visual relationships, commonsense reasoning, image understanding, scene recognition) across all 33 layers and five visual functional localizers (early pRF-ROIs; high-level floc-bodies, faces, places, words). A variance partitioning approach using set arithmetic on normalized brain alignment quantifies shared versus unique explained variance between instruction pairs, revealing that image captioning acts as an 'umbrella' instruction with high overlap with other prompts but lower overlap with image understanding and scene recognition. Baselines include randomly initialized MLLMs, unimodal ViT-H, and multimodal CLIP-Text (using ground-truth oracle captions), with ANOVA tests confirming that MLLMs achieve significantly better alignment in high-level visual ROIs than in early visual ROIs.

## Results

Using the NSD fMRI dataset (4 participants, 10,000 images each), the paper evaluates brain alignment via normalized Pearson correlation (divided by cross-subject prediction accuracy). MLLMs (InstructBLIP, mPLUG-Owl, IDEFICS) achieve normalized brain alignment of ~0.8 in high-level visual regions (e.g., FLOC-PLACES) and ~0.6 in early visual regions (pRF-Visual ROIs), significantly outperforming randomly initialized models and the unimodal ViT-H baseline. MLLMs perform comparably to or better than the CLIP-Text model, despite CLIP using ground-truth oracle captions while MLLMs rely on zero-shot predicted generations. ANOVA tests confirm significantly higher alignment in higher visual ROIs vs. early visual ROIs (p=0.008, F=14.60 for InstructBLIP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unimodal ViT-H baseline, all three MLLMs (InstructBLIP, mPLUG-Owl, IDEFICS) achieve significantly higher normalized brain alignment across all visual ROIs (p≤0.05), with the gap most pronounced in high-level regions like FLOC-PLACES (~0.8 vs. lower ViT-H scores) and FLOC-BODIES (p=0.001, F=32.91). Against the multimodal CLIP-Text model, MLLMs perform comparably or slightly better, which is notable because CLIP uses ground-truth oracle captions while MLLMs use zero-shot predicted generations from natural instructions. Against the non-instruction-tuned BLIP-2 (Appendix K), InstructBLIP shows a clear advantage, particularly for the Visual Relation task where the scatter plot (Fig. 17) shows a pronounced deviation toward InstructBLIP, and the gap is larger for voxels with normalized alignment >0.2. The LLaMA-2-7B text-only model (using captions without visual input) performs closer to ViT-H, confirming that visual understanding from instruction-tuned MLLMs drives the alignment gain. Overall, instruction-tuned MLLMs win or tie on all comparisons, with the strongest gains over unimodal and non-instruction-tuned baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the brain encoding literature, which has shown that multimodal models (e.g., CLIP) outperform vision-only models in predicting fMRI activity, by introducing instruction-tuned MLLMs (InstructBLIP, mPLUG-Owl, IDEFICS) as a new class of encoding models. It builds on the task-based brain alignment paradigm (Wang et al. 2019; Oota et al. 2022a; Aw et al. 2023), which previously required separate task-specific models, by leveraging a single instruction-tuned model prompted with multiple natural-language instructions. The analytical framework draws on variance partitioning (de Heer et al. 2017) and bootstrap ridge-regression voxel-wise encoding (Deniz et al. 2019), applied here for the first time to disentangle instruction-specific representations within a single MLLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that instruction-tuned MLLMs achieve brain alignment comparable to CLIP while using only predicted (not ground-truth) captions, and that image captioning acts as a broad 'umbrella' instruction sharing most variance with other task prompts. A key open question it surfaces is whether MLLMs can be improved to better differentiate instruction-specific representations, since the high shared variance across instructions suggests current models do not encode truly distinct task-specific visual concepts. Limitations include reliance on passive-viewing fMRI (NSD), analysis restricted to visual cortex, and only 10 instructions across 6 task categories, leaving the full landscape of instruction-brain alignment unexplored.
