## Problem

Existing visual prompting methods for MLLMs are typically architecture-specific, rely on fixed visual references like bounding boxes, cannot simultaneously reference multiple objects, and often degrade image-level perception. The paper addresses how to endow mainstream pre-trained MLLMs with general visual prompting understanding—supporting points, boxes, and free-form shapes—while preserving their original vision-language capabilities, in a plug-and-play manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251659/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (processed via each base MLLM's dynamic resolution strategy with sub-image splitting and padding), one or more visual prompts (points, bounding boxes, or free-form shapes, up to 16 simultaneous prompts), and a natural-language instruction. Free-form user-drawn shapes are pre-processed into enclosing bounding boxes at inference. Outputs are structured text responses using region identifiers (e.g., <Region 1>, <Mark 2>) to reference specific prompted areas, covering tasks from brief/detailed captioning and OCR to inter-relationship analysis and complex reasoning, with a maximum LLM sequence length of 3072 tokens.

## Method

The core contribution is a Visual Prompt Encoder (VPE) that converts coordinate inputs into token embeddings via Fourier positional encoding, type-specific learnable embeddings, and a linear projection, with a fixed 16-token budget where valid/invalid learnable vectors let the model handle a dynamic number of prompts. The VPE is inserted between the vision encoder and LLM of any pre-trained MLLM (demonstrated on SPHINX-X and LLaVA-Next), with image tokens, visual prompt tokens, and text tokens concatenated as a single LLM input. Training follows a two-stage strategy: Stage 1 freezes the vision encoder and LLM while training only the VPE and an MLP projector on detection/segmentation data to align visual prompt features with image-text space; Stage 2 freezes the vision encoder and VPE while fine-tuning the projector and LLM on 1.2M+ instruction triplets (MDVP-Instruct-Data) spanning natural images, documents, OCR, screenshots, and remote sensing. Free-form prompt support is achieved through Gaussian noise augmentation on bounding boxes during Stage 1, simulating imprecise user-drawn enclosures.

## Results

On LVIS referring classification, VP-SPHINX-13B achieves 87.06 Semantic Similarity and 62.90 Semantic IOU, surpassing the strongest prior method Osprey-7B (65.24 SS, 38.19 S-IOU) by over 20 points in SS. On RefCOCOg detailed region captioning, VP-SPHINX-13B scores 88.19% (GPT-4V-evaluated), outperforming Osprey-7B (77.54%) by 10.65%. On the proposed MDVP-Bench, VP-SPHINX-13B reaches 92.95 on Natural Point and 85.29 on OCR Point, while the closest competitor Ferret-13B scores only 83.40 and 12.20 respectively on those sub-tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VP-MLLMs dominate all visual prompting baselines on referring classification: VP-SPHINX-13B (87.06 SS) outperforms Osprey-7B (65.24 SS) by 21.8 points and Ferret-13B (64.96 SS) by 22.1 points on LVIS, and beats Ferret-v2-13B's box accuracy (87.7) with 89.82. On RefCOCOg detailed captioning, VP-SPHINX-13B (88.19) surpasses Osprey-7B (77.54) by 10.65% and SPHINX-X-13B (40.16) by 48.03%. On MDVP-Bench, the advantage is most dramatic in the OCR domain where VP-SPHINX-13B scores 85.29 (Point) versus Ferret-13B's 12.20 and Osprey-7B's 18.33 (Box only). On general MLLM benchmarks, VP-MLLMs retain strong performance (e.g., VP-LLaVA-8B: 81.7 VQA, 1554.9 MME) but trail the original MLLMs slightly (SPHINX-X-13B: 1457.7 MME; LLaVA-Next-8B: 1603.7 MME), which the authors attribute to using only a subset of open-source instruction data. On VCR commonsense reasoning, VP-SPHINX-13B achieves 88.92% Q→A accuracy, edging out ViP-LLaVA-Base-7B (87.66%) and GPT4RoI-7B (87.4%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual prompting line of work (Shikra, ChatSpot, GPT4RoI, Kosmos-2, Ferret, Osprey) by proposing a lightweight, plug-and-play visual prompt encoder that can be attached to any pre-trained MLLM (SPHINX-X, LLaVA-Next) without architectural modification. It departs from ROI-based methods that require pre-attached segmentation models or ground-truth masks, and from fixed-reference methods that only support bounding boxes, by simultaneously handling points, boxes, and free-form shapes through positional encoding with learnable type embeddings. The two-stage training strategy (alignment pre-training followed by multi-task instruction fine-tuning) is adapted from LLaVA's paradigm but applied to the visual prompt modality, and the data construction leverages GPT-4V with Set-of-Marks prompting to generate multi-domain instruction data at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play visual prompt encoder and the 1.2M-sample MDVP-Instruct-Data / MDVP-Bench provide a practical foundation for building interactive, pixel-level MLLMs across diverse domains (natural images, documents, OCR, remote sensing, screenshots). A notable limitation is that image-level perception scores (MME, SEED, POPE) remain slightly below the original MLLMs, and box prompts underperform point prompts in multi-prompt scenarios, suggesting the encoder's capacity for dense spatial reasoning is not yet fully resolved. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D inputs, how to handle truly arbitrary free-form strokes without bounding-box approximation, and whether the GPT-4V-generated data introduces systematic biases that limit generalization.
