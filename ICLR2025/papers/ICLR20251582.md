## Problem

Existing identifiability guarantees for nonlinear ICA require either a large number of auxiliary variables (2n+1 domains for component-wise identifiability) or strong structural sparsity on the mixing function—both frequently violated in practice. This paper demonstrates that these two seemingly unrelated assumptions are complementary: sparse mixing induces conditional independence that constrains the solution space of the identifiability equations, reducing the number of domains needed, while sufficient distributional changes from auxiliary variables compensate when sparsity is only partially satisfied.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observed images (e.g., CelebA faces with/without eyeglasses, MNIST digits in red/green) together with discrete domain indices as auxiliary variables, and produces disentangled latent representations split into domain-invariant content (z_c) and domain-specific style (z_s) variables. In the CG-GAN variant, a learnable binary mask adaptively determines which latent dimensions are domain-specific, and the sparse mixing constraint is enforced via the L1-norm of the generator's Jacobian, approximated by forward finite differences to handle high-dimensional image tensors. The CG-VAE variant outputs a full posterior q(z|x) parameterized by mean and variance, with the domain-conditional prior p(z|u) computed through a normalizing flow.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution establishes that sparse mixing procedure and sufficient changes from auxiliary variables jointly yield identifiability with milder assumptions: Theorem 1 proves subspace identifiability using only |Pa(x_k)| domains (where Pa(x_k) are the parents of observation subset x_k), and Theorem 2 upgrades this to component-wise identifiability with 2|z_a|+1 domains, where |z_a| ≤ n, recovering the classical 2n+1 requirement as a special case under fully-connected mixing (Corollary 1). The estimation framework pairs a normalizing-flow-based domain-encoding network F_u (mapping estimated latents to noise, yielding p(z|u) via the change-of-variable formula) with a sparse mixing penalty L_s = Σ|∂x̂_i/∂ẑ_j| that enforces conditional independence between non-adjacent latent-observation pairs. Two implementations are provided: CG-VAE (ELBO = reconstruction + α·L_s − β·KL) for synthetic experiments, and CG-GAN (discriminator loss + γ·L_mask + α·L_s) built on the StyleGAN2-ADA/i-StyleGAN backbone for multi-domain image generation. The key practical advantage is that only two domains suffice to achieve near-optimal disentanglement (MCC ≈ 0.95), whereas prior methods like iVAE and iMSDA degrade substantially below 2n+1 domains.

## Results

On synthetic Dataset A, CG-VAE achieves a mean correlation coefficient (MCC) of 0.953 with only 2 domains and 0.971 with 8 domains, consistently outperforming all baselines. On real-world CelebA (2 domains), CG-GAN achieves FID 2.57 and DIPD 0.93, surpassing i-StyleGAN (FID 2.65, DIPD 0.95) and StyleGAN2-ADA (FID 3.57, DIPD 1.00). On MNIST (2 domains), CG-GAN achieves FID 8.74 and Joint-FID 67.04, substantially outperforming i-StyleGAN (FID 16.6, Joint-FID 107.39) and StyleGAN2-ADA (FID 117.64, Joint-FID 386.19). The key finding is that CG-VAE maintains high MCC (~0.95) even with as few as 2 domains, whereas baselines like iVAE and FactorVAE degrade significantly under the same setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CG-VAE outperforms all six baselines across every domain count on Dataset A. With only 2 domains, CG-VAE (MCC 0.953) beats iMSDA (0.869) by 8.4 points, iVAE (0.756) by 19.7 points, and FactorVAE (0.728) by 22.5 points, demonstrating the critical role of the sparse mixing constraint when auxiliary variables are scarce. iMSDA is the strongest baseline, reaching 0.959 MCC with 8 domains, but still trails CG-VAE (0.971) by 1.2 points. On real-world CelebA, CG-GAN (FID 2.57) marginally beats i-StyleGAN (2.65) and clearly outperforms StyleGAN2-ADA (3.57) and TGAN (4.89). On MNIST, the advantage is more dramatic: CG-GAN achieves FID 8.74 versus i-StyleGAN's 16.6 and StyleGAN2-ADA's 117.64, with Joint-FID of 67.04 versus 107.39 and 386.19 respectively. The ablation (CG-VAE-S without sparse mixing) shows a noticeable MCC drop as domains decrease, confirming that the sparse mixing constraint is the key differentiator from iVAE/iMSDA-style methods that rely solely on sufficient changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work in nonlinear ICA identifiability: methods relying on sufficient distributional changes via auxiliary variables (iVAE, iMSDA, Hyvarinen & Morioka) and methods imposing structural sparsity on the mixing function (Zheng et al., Zheng & Zhang). The key conceptual departure is showing that these assumptions are complementary rather than alternative—sparse mixing constraints reduce the number of auxiliary variables needed, while sufficient changes compensate for partial sparsity violations. The GAN implementation builds directly on i-StyleGAN (Xie et al., 2023), reusing its backbone architecture and learnable mask mechanism, while adding a sparse mixing penalty and normalizing-flow-based domain encoding. The paper also shows that the well-known 2n+1 domain requirement for component-wise identifiability is a special case of its framework when the mixing is fully connected.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that real-world multi-domain tasks (e.g., image translation between a small number of domains) often cannot satisfy the 2n+1 domain requirement, making prior identifiability guarantees inapplicable. By showing that sparse mixing structure can substitute for additional domains, the paper opens a path to principled disentanglement with fewer auxiliary variables. However, the empirical validation is preliminary: synthetic experiments use small-scale MLP-generated data, and real-world evaluation is limited to CelebA and MNIST with only two domains, yielding modest FID improvements over i-StyleGAN. Open questions include the precise relationship between the paper's weaker sparsity assumption and the stronger structural sparsity of Zheng et al., and whether the framework extends to more complex, high-dimensional real-world scenarios beyond simple attribute-based domain splits.
