## Problem

Rank collapse—the convergence of token embeddings to a rank-1 matrix in deep sequence models—was well-characterized for transformers but remained unexplored for State Space Models (SSMs). No general architectural guarantee existed to prevent rank collapse across both architecture families, and the specific role of skip connection strength in providing such a guarantee was unstudied. This paper asks: can a single architectural modification, a parametrized skip connection, provide a unified sufficient condition for rank collapse prevention across transformers and SSMs?

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis operates on the general layer recurrence Y^(k) = D^(k)(M^(k-1)Y^(k-1)C_V^(k-1) + λY^(k-1)), where M encodes either attention (row-stochastic softmax matrix) or a recurrent SSM block (lower-triangular state-space matrix), and D is a per-token LayerNorm diagonal. Empirically, the paper forward-passes 32 Wikipedia excerpts (≥128 tokens, BERT-tokenized) through a 2B-parameter pre-trained Mamba-2 model to compute the normalized rank collapse measure μ at each of 64 layers, and trains 2–4 layer models (d=128–256) on LRA Image (35,200 train / 7,850 eval) and MQAR (100,000 train / 3,000 eval, L=512, 64 KV pairs) to test learnable λ. The primary diagnostic output is μ(Y^(K)), the Frobenius norm of the centered output matrix, which approaches 0 under rank collapse.

## Method

The paper introduces the lambda-skip connection, replacing the standard residual X + O with λX + O, and proves a λ-dependent lower bound μ(Y^(K))² ≥ a^K μ(Y^(0))² that holds for any architecture expressible as O = MV (covering transformers, LTI SSMs, and selective SSMs). The sufficient condition for preventing rank collapse is λ² − a(SC_M + |λ|)² > 0, where C_M = sup_k ‖M^(k)‖_F and S = sup_k ‖C_V^(k)‖_F are architecture-specific constants; for Mamba, C_V = I forces a < 1, while for transformers C_M = √N. The paper establishes necessity by showing that ablating skip connections causes exponential rank collapse (with LayerNorm) or doubly exponential collapse (without LayerNorm) in selective SSMs, mirroring the transformer results, and provides analytical counterexamples where specific λ values still permit collapse. Empirically, the default λ = 1 is shown to be insufficient in a 2B Mamba-2 model, while negative λ values (interpreted as negative feedback) and learnable λ improve stability without hurting downstream task accuracy. The paper also makes the first connection between gating mechanisms (originally designed for memory) and rank collapse prevention, showing that removing gating from Mamba-2 causes μ to approach zero in later layers.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: Theorem 4.1 provides a sufficient condition on the skip strength parameter λ (specifically, λ² − a(SC_M + |λ|)² > 0) that guarantees the rank collapse metric μ(Y^(K))² ≥ a^K μ(Y^(0))² for all K layers, holding uniformly across transformers, LTI SSMs, and selective SSMs. Empirically, on a 2B-parameter Mamba-2 model, rank collapse (μ → 0) is observed for |λ| < ~1.5 but avoided for sufficiently large |λ|, confirming the theory. On downstream tasks, making λ learnable does not degrade performance: on MQAR (L=512), Mamba with variable λ achieves 85.3% accuracy vs. 81.5% for fixed λ=1, and Mamba-2 reaches 99.1% vs. 97.3%. On LRA Image, results are comparable across λ values (e.g., Transformer: 32.64% fixed vs. 32.85% variable).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against external competing methods; instead, it compares architectural variants within a unified framework. The key comparison is between the standard skip connection (λ=1) and the proposed lambda-skip connection with tuned or learnable λ. On MQAR, learnable λ outperforms fixed λ=1 for Mamba (+3.8 pp, 85.3% vs. 81.5%) and Mamba-2 (+1.8 pp, 99.1% vs. 97.3%), while on LRA Image the differences are within noise (±2 pp). Ablation experiments on a pre-trained 2B Mamba-2 model show that removing the skip connection entirely (λ=0) causes the rank collapse measure to approach zero across all 64 layers, whereas setting |λ| sufficiently large (e.g., λ=−2 or λ=3) keeps μ well above zero. Removing gating mechanisms from Mamba-2 similarly drives μ toward zero in later layers, establishing gating as an additional rank-collapse-prevention mechanism. For the S4D architecture on CIFAR-10, rank collapse is not observed even without skip connections or LayerNorm, consistent with the theory that LTI SSMs require ||W||_F < 1 for collapse to occur.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rank collapse theory originally developed for transformers (Dong et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2024a) to State Space Models by leveraging the unifying framework from Ali et al. (2024) and Dao and Gu (2024) that expresses both architectures as O = MV. The lambda-skip connection is a direct generalization of the ResNet skip connection (He et al., 2015) and the lambda-residual variant studied for training efficiency (He et al., 2016), but here it is repurposed as a rank-collapse prevention mechanism with a formal sufficient condition. The paper departs from prior work by providing the first architecture-agnostic lower bound on the rank collapse metric and the first theoretical treatment of rank collapse in SSMs, while also making the novel empirical connection between gating mechanisms (originally designed for memory in LSTMs) and rank collapse prevention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that the standard λ=1 skip connection may be insufficient and that λ should be treated as a learnable parameter—is a low-cost architectural modification with clear theoretical backing, potentially improving stability in both transformer and SSM-based models. However, the theoretical bound is notably conservative (requiring |λ| much larger than 1 in practice), gating mechanisms are excluded from the analysis despite being empirically crucial, and the MLP's role is acknowledged but not formally incorporated. Open questions include whether tighter bounds incorporating gating and MLPs can be derived, whether the control-theoretic interpretation of λ as a gain leads to principled per-layer scheduling, and whether alternative rank collapse metrics (e.g., effective rank) yield different architectural prescriptions.
