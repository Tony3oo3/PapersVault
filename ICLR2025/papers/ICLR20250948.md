## Problem

Existing embodied AI representation learning methods (R3M, VC-1, MVP) predominantly adopt 2D vision paradigms such as contrastive learning or masked autoencoding, which capture semantic understanding but fail to encode the 3D spatial relationships essential for navigation, manipulation, and spatial reasoning. This paper hypothesizes that 3D spatial awareness is a critical missing component and proposes a systematic framework to inject it into a standard 2D backbone without requiring explicit 3D inputs like point clouds or meshes.

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method consumes N multi-view RGB images (each 224×224 after random crop) with known camera poses, applying a 50% random mask ratio to patches before encoding. At inference time, a single RGB image is fed to the frozen ViT, which outputs a [CLS] token (a 1024-d feature vector for ViT-L) and unpatchified feature maps at input resolution. The feature maps are produced by two upsampling layers (conv + GELU + pixel-shuffle with factor √P) applied to the unpatchified latent, making them directly usable as spatially-structured observations for downstream policies like MLP, diffusion, or transformer architectures.

## Method

SPA pre-trains a vanilla ViT by using differentiable neural rendering (NeuS-style SDF + spherical harmonics) as a self-supervised pre-text task on multi-view images. The pipeline: (1) each view is encoded by the ViT with random masking to produce a feature map; (2) a 3D feature volume (128×128×32) is constructed via deformable attention that queries voxel positions against multi-view features using known camera extrinsics; (3) a shallow 3D CNN (replacing pointwise MLPs for efficiency) predicts SDF values, SH color coefficients, and semantic features at each voxel; (4) rays are sampled and integrated via NeuS-style volume rendering to produce RGB, depth, and semantic maps. Training is supervised by L1 rendering losses (color, depth, semantic from AM-RADIO features) plus Eikonal, near-surface SDF, and free-space SDF regularization—all label-free. The key architectural distinction from prior neural-rendering pre-training (Ponder, UniPAD) is the use of a standard 2D ViT backbone with a dynamic scene-range volume and 3D CNN decoder, making it applicable to large-scale multi-view image datasets rather than requiring explicit 3D geometry inputs.

## Results

SPA achieves a Mean Success Rate of 88.63% and Mean Rank of 3.20 across 268 tasks on 8 simulators, outperforming all 10+ baselines. It achieves the best or second-best mean success rate in 11 out of 13 benchmarks and ranks in the top 3 for over 65.5% of individual tasks. On the zero-shot camera pose estimation task (NAVI), SPA achieves 1.65×10⁻² translation error and 0.61×10⁻¹ rotation error, an 18.3% improvement in translation and 15.3% reduction in rotation over the second-best method (VC-1). In real-world experiments, SPA achieves 65.33% mean success rate vs. 53.33% for MAE and 44.00% for InternViT-6B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPA (88.63% Mean S.R., rank 3.20) outperforms all 10 ViT-L baselines. The closest competitor is MAE (85.13%, rank 4.07), which benefits from its reconstruction objective enhancing 2D spatial awareness; SPA leads by 3.5 points in mean success rate. Among embodied-specific methods, MVP (84.85%) and VC-1 (84.69%) trail SPA by ~4 points, and notably neither outperforms the original ImageNet-pretrained MAE, suggesting human-interaction data alone is insufficient. Multi-modal models perform poorly: CLIP (77.10%) and InternViT-300M (75.41%) are mid-pack, while InternViT-6B (30.65%) is catastrophically worse, indicating that scaling vision-language models does not transfer to embodied tasks. DINOV2 (75.18%) underperforms despite 10× more training data than MoCoV3 (81.67%), showing that vision-task superiority does not guarantee embodied performance. In the ViT-B comparison (Table 4), SPA-B (73.66%) beats STP-B (71.92%), Theia-B (72.55%), Voltron-B (69.50%), VC-1-B (70.19%), R3M-B (54.37%), and MAE-B (71.63%). SPA also surpasses its own semantic rendering teacher AM-RADIO (67.93%) and E-RADIO (70.16%), confirming the 3D-aware pre-training objective is the key driver rather than the data or teacher features.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPA sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) embodied representation learning (R3M, VC-1, MVP), which has largely adopted 2D contrastive or MAE-based pre-training; (2) neural rendering pre-training (Ponder, UniPAD), which has applied differentiable rendering to 3D perception and autonomous driving but not to embodied AI; and (3) standard 2D ViT backbones (MAE, DINOv2), which dominate the vision community. The key departure is using differentiable neural rendering (NeuS-style SDF + spherical harmonics) as a self-supervised pre-text on multi-view images to inject 3D spatial awareness into a vanilla ViT, rather than relying on explicit 3D inputs (point clouds, meshes) or purely 2D reconstruction objectives. The deformable attention for volume construction borrows from Deformable DETR, and the masking strategy follows MAE, but the rendering-based supervision is the novel architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that 3D spatial awareness, not just 2D semantics or multi-modal grounding, is the critical ingredient for effective embodied representations—challenges the prevailing assumption that scaling multi-modal models (CLIP, InternViT-6B) or using human-interaction data (MVP, VC-1) is the path forward. The 268-task, 8-simulator evaluation is over 15× larger than prior embodied benchmarks, providing much more reliable conclusions than the <20-task evaluations of R3M and VC-1. Limitations include restriction to imitation learning (behavior cloning) in the main results, static multi-view scenes only, and reliance on ground-truth or estimated depth for supervision. Open questions include whether SPA transfers to reinforcement learning, dynamic/temporal settings, and whether the volume decoder's multi-view interaction can be directly leveraged in policy networks.
