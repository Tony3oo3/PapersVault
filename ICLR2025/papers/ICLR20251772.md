## Problem

While covariate shift has been extensively studied for estimating the unknown function itself, the efficiency of two-stage approaches—first estimating the function, then computing a functional of it—under distribution shift remains largely unexplored. This paper asks whether an optimal nonparametric function estimator trained under the source distribution can be calibrated to preserve minimax optimality for moment estimation under the target distribution, and if so, how.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251772/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes n labeled source samples (x_i, y_i = f(x_i)) drawn from P° and, in the practical setting, m (≫ n) unlabeled target samples x'_i drawn from P*. The unknown function f is assumed to lie in a Sobolev space W^{s,p}(Ω) over a bounded domain Ω ⊂ R^d, and the source p.d.f. is bounded between b̲ and b̄. The output is a scalar estimate of the q-th moment I_f^q = E_{X~P*}[f^q(X)] under the target distribution, with convergence rates governed by the covariate shift intensity B, the source distribution regularity b̄, and the interplay between smoothness s, integrability p, and moment order q.

## Method

The proposed two-stage algorithm splits the labeled data into S₁ and S₂ of equal size. In stage 1, an optimal nonparametric estimator f̂_{S₁} is trained on S₁ (e.g., via moving least squares or random forests). In stage 2, the moment is estimated as Ĥ_B^q = E_{X~P*}[f̂_{S₁}^q(X)] + (2/n)Σ_{(x_i,y_i)∈S₂} w(x_i)·(y_i^q − f̂_{S₁}^q(x_i)), where w(x) = p*(x)/p°(x) is the likelihood ratio; the first term acts as a control variate (estimated by sampling from the known P*) and the second term debiases the residual via importance weighting. This achieves the minimax lower bound B·b̄·n^{max{−q(s/d−1/p)−1, −1/2−s/d}} up to a logarithmic factor. For the practical case where p° and p* are unknown, the paper introduces a truncated plug-in version replacing w(x) with τ_T(ŵ(x)) and approximating the target expectation with m unlabeled samples, yielding a B-independent rate b̄·r(n)^{α/(α+1)} + m^{−1/2} and a double-robustness guarantee: consistency holds if either the function estimator or the likelihood ratio estimator is consistent.

## Results

The paper establishes a minimax lower bound of B·b̄·n^{max{-q(s/d−1/p)−1, −1/2−s/d}} for q-th moment estimation under covariate shift, and shows the proposed two-stage algorithm (optimal function estimator + likelihood-ratio reweighting) attains this rate up to a logarithmic factor. When the likelihood ratio is unknown, the truncated plug-in estimator achieves b̄·r(n)^{α/(α+1)} + m^{−1/2}, where r(n) is the base rate and m is the number of unlabeled target samples. Numerical experiments (q=2, random forest, 100 repetitions) confirm that the two-stage estimator outperforms both the Monte Carlo and one-stage estimators as covariate shift intensity B increases (from 1.00 to 23.03), while truncation (T = B/2) further improves stability at large B without sacrificing accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three estimators on synthetic data: a plain Monte Carlo estimator, a one-stage estimator (directly estimating the moment without the control-variate decomposition), and the proposed two-stage estimator. When there is no covariate shift (B=1), the Monte Carlo estimator performs comparably or slightly better than the two-stage methods, as the reweighting adds unnecessary complexity. However, as B increases (3.98, 12.08, 23.03), the two-stage estimator demonstrates substantially greater accuracy and improved stability, with the gap widening monotonically with B. The one-stage estimator consistently underperforms the two-stage approach under any non-trivial shift. Truncation (comparing untruncated vs. truncated two-stage with T=B/2) shows that truncation reduces variance and improves stability at large B while preserving the bias level, making the method more robust in practice. The double-robustness property (Corollary 1) ensures consistency if either the function estimator or the likelihood-ratio estimator is consistent, providing a practical safety net when one model is misspecified.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage control-variate framework of Blanchet et al. (2024) for moment estimation to the covariate shift setting, where the source and target distributions differ. It also adapts the truncation technique from Ma et al. (2023) on RKHS-based nonparametric regression under covariate shift, but applies it to the functional (moment) level rather than the function level. The broader covariate shift literature (Shimodaira 2000; Kpotufe & Martinet 2021; Feng et al. 2024b) has focused on estimating the function itself, whereas this work advances one step further to estimating functionals of the function, filling a gap the authors identify as 'under-explored.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first minimax lower bound and matching upper bound for moment estimation under covariate shift, giving practitioners a clear picture of how covariate shift intensity (B), sampling irregularity (b̄), and function smoothness jointly determine estimation difficulty. The double robustness property of the truncated plug-in estimator is practically valuable: it guarantees consistency if either the function estimator or the likelihood ratio estimator is correct, which is important given that likelihood ratio estimation is notoriously unstable. Open questions include whether the results extend to moment-bounded classes (rather than uniformly B-bounded), to more complex functionals beyond moments, and to settings where the constraints p ≥ 2, q ≤ p ≤ 2q are relaxed.
