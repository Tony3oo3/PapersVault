## Problem

RNA secondary structure prediction remains challenging due to complex structural elements like pseudoknots and the limited availability of experimentally determined structures. Existing deep learning methods struggle with cross-family generalization, while energy-based methods achieve lower accuracy within distribution. This paper bridges the gap by reformulating RNA structure prediction as a dependency parsing problem, combining the structural constraints of NLP parsing with the biological requirements of RNA folding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250352/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RNA nucleotide sequence (string over {A, G, C, U}) of arbitrary length (demonstrated up to 3000+ nucleotides) and produces a base pair contact map where each nucleotide is paired with at most one other. Internally, the model predicts a labeled dependency tree with five arc labels (stem, pseudoknot, loop, connector, root) that encodes both paired and unpaired nucleotide relationships. A post-processing step applies row-wise softmax to the contact matrix to enforce the one-to-one pairing constraint, yielding the final structure.

## Method

DEPfold transforms RNA secondary structures into labeled dependency trees through a four-step process: sequence partitioning into stems, pseudoknots, and loops; binary tree construction preserving nested structure; root integration across structural components; and loop nucleotide attachment. The model uses a pretrained foundation model (RoBERTa-base or RNA-fm) for sequence encoding, followed by separate MLPs for dependent and head representations, and biaffine classifiers that jointly score edge existence and arc labels. Decoding employs the Eisner algorithm (for projective/non-pseudoknotted structures) or graph spanning tree algorithms (for non-projective/pseudoknotted structures) to find the optimal dependency tree, with a post-processing step enforcing valid RNA pairing constraints. Training uses cross-entropy loss over edge and label predictions, with fine-tuning of the pretrained encoder at a lower learning rate than the task-specific biaffine layers.

## Results

On the RNAStrAlign test set, DEPfold achieves an F1 score of 0.985 (Precision 0.988, Recall 0.983), outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. On the cross-family bpRNA-new dataset, DEPfold reaches F1=0.621, exceeding RNAfold by 0.65 percentage points, though trailing ContraFold (0.639) and MXfold2 (0.632). On bpRNA-TS0, DEPfold achieves F1=0.676, a 7-point improvement over UFold (0.630) and a 25% relative gain over traditional energy-based methods. For pseudoknot prediction on RNAStrAlign, DEPfold attains a Set F1 of 0.981 with only 1 false negative, far surpassing UFold (0.838) and E2Efold (0.710).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEPfold consistently outperforms all deep learning baselines (UFold, RFold, E2Efold, MXfold2) on the within-family RNAStrAlign benchmark, with F1 gaps of 2.3 points over UFold and 16.4 points over E2Efold. Against energy-based methods (RNAfold, ContraFold, LinearFold, RNAstructure), DEPfold achieves roughly 40–45 F1-point improvements on RNAStrAlign, reflecting the advantage of data-driven learning within distribution. On the cross-family bpRNA-new dataset, however, DEPfold (F1=0.621) is slightly outperformed by ContraFold (0.639) and MXfold2 (0.632), indicating that thermodynamic priors still hold an edge when generalizing to unseen RNA families. DEPfold also surpasses UFold on long-range base pair prediction (F1=0.689 vs. 0.653 on bpRNA-TS0), and handles sequences of arbitrary length, whereas E2Efold, RFold, and UFold are capped at fixed maximum lengths (1,800, 1,800, and 600 nucleotides respectively). The ablation on pretrained encoders reveals that fine-tuned RoBERTa outperforms the RNA-specific RNA-fm (F1 0.676 vs. 0.618 on TS0), an unexpected finding that challenges the assumption that domain-specific pretraining is always superior.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEPfold repurposes the biaffine dependency parsing framework from NLP (Dozat & Manning, 2016) for RNA secondary structure prediction, a task previously addressed by energy-minimization methods (RNAfold, ContraFold) and deep learning architectures (E2Efold, UFold, MXfold2). The key conceptual departure is transforming RNA structures into labeled dependency trees, which naturally encodes the nested structure assumption shared with context-free grammar approaches (Knudsen & Hein, 1999) while adding explicit modeling of unpaired nucleotides and pseudoknots as non-projective edges. Unlike prior deep learning methods that impose structural constraints through custom loss functions or unrolled algorithms, DEPfold leverages optimal tree decoding (Eisner's algorithm for projective, spanning-tree algorithms for non-projective structures) to enforce biological validity at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEPfold offers a unified framework that handles pseudoknots, arbitrary sequence lengths, and both within-family and cross-family prediction, addressing limitations of prior methods that are constrained by fixed maximum lengths (UFold: 600nt, E2Efold: 1800nt) or cannot predict pseudoknots (RNAfold, LinearFold). However, cross-family generalization remains a significant open challenge—DEPfold outperforms RNAfold by only 0.65 F1 points on bpRNA-new, and the data augmentation strategy using RNAfold predictions may reinforce existing biases. The finding that general-purpose RoBERTa outperforms the RNA-specific RNA-fm encoder during fine-tuning challenges the assumption that domain-specific pretraining is always advantageous, pointing to open questions about optimal pretraining strategies for RNA biology.
