## Problem

Post-training pruning methods for LLMs rely on calibration data to estimate parameter importance, yet most papers claim robustness to calibration data choice without systematic validation. This paper demonstrates that calibration data significantly affects pruning performance—especially at high sparsity (≥50%) and semi-structured sparsity types—where performance differences across data sources reach 2.3%, and that the commonly used C4 data is not always optimal. The key open question is what makes calibration data effective and how to construct suitable calibration data when the original training data is inaccessible.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source text corpus (e.g., C4, Wikipedia, SlimPajama, or DCLM) and a pre-trained LLM whose training data is unavailable. It produces 128 sequences of 2048 tokens as synthetic calibration data for post-training pruning. The generation process truncates the first t tokens (optimally 1–4) of each source sample as a prefix, then the LLM autoregressively generates the continuation; the output is filtered by removing the top 20% of samples with the highest perplexity to eliminate low-quality generations.

## Method

The paper first establishes through controlled experiments that calibration data similarity to the model's pre-training data (measured via MinHash-LSH Jaccard similarity on 3-grams) is the dominant factor for pruning quality, not data quality or volume. Building on this, the proposed 'self-generating then sampling' strategy leverages the LLM's autoregressive generation as a proxy for its training distribution: for each source sample, the first t tokens are kept as a prefix and the LLM generates the remainder using top-k=50, top-p=0.95, temperature=0.6, and repetition penalty 1.2. A perplexity-based filter then removes the top 20% of generated samples to discard patterns the model has not well learned. The approach is validated on DCLM-7B, LLaMA-2-7B/13B, and LLaMA-3-8B across Wanda, DSnoT, and OWL pruning methods, showing up to 2.68% average improvement over the original calibration data and even surpassing calibration data sampled directly from the training set.

## Results

On DCLM-7B at 60% sparsity, the self-generated synthetic calibration data improves average commonsense reasoning performance by up to 2.68% (DSnoT + Wikipedia source) and 2.59% (Wanda + Wikipedia source) over the original calibration data. Across all three pruning methods (Wanda, DSnoT, OWL) and four data sources, synthetic data consistently outperforms the original data, with average gains of 0.37%–2.68%. Notably, synthetic data from Wikipedia (which is entirely absent from DCLM-7B's pretraining corpus) surpasses the DCLM training-data calibration baseline by 0.3%–0.7% on average, and Alpaca perplexity also improves (e.g., from 9.99 to 9.40 for Wanda+Wiki).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three state-of-the-art post-training pruning methods—Wanda (Sun et al., ICLR 2024), DSnoT (Zhang et al., ICLR 2024), and OWL (Yin et al., ICML 2024)—as the pruning backbones, and compares four calibration data sources: C4 (Raffel et al., JMLR 2020), Wikipedia, SlimPajama, and DCLM (Li et al., 2024, the actual pretraining data of DCLM-7B). The proposed self-generated synthetic calibration data outperforms the original data from every source under all three pruning methods, with the largest gains (2.2%–2.7%) observed when the source is Wikipedia, which is entirely disjoint from the pretraining corpus. Surprisingly, synthetic data generated from Wikipedia surpasses calibration data sampled directly from the DCLM training set by 0.3%–0.7% on average, suggesting that self-generation filters out under-represented patterns that mislead importance estimation. C4, which partially overlaps with pretraining data, shows moderate improvements (0.9%–1.2%), while DCLM-sourced data sees the smallest gains (0.37%–0.70%), consistent with the hypothesis that data already well-matched to training benefits least from further alignment. The results are consistent across model families: on LLaMA-2-7B and LLaMA-3-8B, synthetic data similarly outperforms all original baselines by 0.2%–1.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the empirical observation by Williams & Aletras (2024) that calibration data affects post-training pruning, by systematically dissecting *which* factors of calibration data matter (sparsity ratio, data volume, source similarity) and *why*. It builds on the post-training pruning method family (SparseGPT, Wanda, DSnoT, OWL) that all rely on calibration data for importance estimation, and on Shin et al. (2024)'s observation that reconstruction-error objectives overfit to calibration data. The key departure is proposing a self-generation strategy that leverages the LLM's own autoregressive output as a proxy for inaccessible training data, connecting to the Magpie-style data synthesis literature (Xu et al., 2024b) but repurposing it for pruning calibration rather than instruction tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that calibration data similarity to pre-training data (not quality) governs pruning performance, and that this effect amplifies sharply at high sparsity (up to 2.3% performance range), provides a concrete, zero-cost improvement for practitioners deploying pruned LLMs. The self-generation strategy is particularly impactful for closed-source models where training data is unavailable, and the fact that it can even outperform actual training data suggests the reconstruction objective benefits from well-learned patterns over raw coverage. Limitations include evaluation only on 7B–13B models, unstructured and semi-structured (not structured) pruning, and unoptimized generation hyperparameters; open questions include whether the strategy transfers to 70B+ models, structured pruning, and instruction-tuned/aligned LLMs.
