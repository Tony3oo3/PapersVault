## Problem

Inference-time copyright protection for LLMs remains challenging: existing post-processing methods like MemFree incur hallucination risks, and the k-NAF framework (Vyas et al., 2023) lacks a computationally feasible implementation for real-world language models. The token-wise CP-Δ variant, which uses fixed equal weights and ignores generation history, still reproduces long verbatim segments from memorized training data. The paper addresses this gap by asking whether an adaptive, history-aware fusion of models trained on disjoint copyrighted subsets can provably prevent regurgitation while preserving generation quality.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, CP-Fuse consumes the current prompt x, the generated prefix y_<t, and the next-token logit vectors from two base models p^(1) and p^(2) (each fine-tuned on a disjoint 3,000-sample split of a dataset). It outputs a single fused next-token distribution p(y_t | y_<t, x) that is then used for greedy or temperature-sampled decoding. The method is entirely post-hoc and requires no access to model weights beyond their forward-pass logits, making it composable with any training-time protection (e.g., goldfish loss, early stopping) applied to the base models.

## Method

CP-Fuse iteratively solves a convex optimization problem at each token position that minimizes the maximum KL-divergence between the fused distribution and each base model, conditioned on the already-generated prefix. Lemma 3.2 shows the optimal solution has the closed form log p*(y_t) = α_t log p^(1)(y_t|y_<t,x) + β_t log p^(2)(y_t|y_<t,x) + γ_t, where α_t and β_t are found by a lightweight grid search over [0,2]×[2,10] and γ_t normalizes the distribution. The key theoretical guarantee is the balancing property (Lemma 3.3): if one base model has dominated the prefix, the fused distribution either equalizes the expected log-probabilities under both models or falls back entirely to the non-dominant model, preventing either model from regurgitating its memorized copyrighted content. This adaptive, history-dependent weighting is what distinguishes CP-Fuse from the fixed-weight token-wise CP-Δ baseline, which the paper shows still produces exact matches 4–7× longer than CP-Fuse.

## Results

CP-Fuse reduces exact matching (EM) by over 25× compared to overfitted base models across all three text/code tasks (e.g., from 1469.80 to 69.58 characters on Python instructions, from 1397.68 to 55.54 on MathAbstracts, and from 1316.24 to 27.55 on WritingPrompts, all at the 95th percentile). On the code plagiarism metric JPlag, CP-Fuse achieves near-zero scores (0.03) versus 1.00 for overfitted models, indicating virtually no detectable copying. Crucially, utility is preserved: CP-Fuse matches or slightly exceeds overfitted models on pass@1 (0.47 on APPS, 0.43 on MBPP, 0.28 on HumanEval) and fluency (2.17/5 on WritingPrompts), while MemFree degrades significantly (0.32 on APPS, 1.70 fluency).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CP-Fuse outperforms all three inference-time baselines on every copyright-infringement metric across all datasets. Against SystemPrompt (self-reminder), CP-Fuse is dramatically superior: the self-reminder method fails entirely, with EM remaining near the overfitted level (~1360–1373 chars on Python instructions) because fine-tuning overrides the safety instruction. Against MemFree (10-gram Bloom filter), CP-Fuse reduces EM by roughly 2× (69.58 vs. 165.48 on Python instructions) and achieves near-zero JPlag (0.03 vs. 0.99), while MemFree also degrades utility (pass@1 drops from 0.47 to 0.32 on APPS; fluency drops from 2.17 to 1.70). Against CP-Δ (token-wise equal-weight fusion), CP-Fuse reduces EM by 4–7× (69.58 vs. 273.20 on Python instructions; 55.54 vs. 341.60 on MathAbstracts) and eliminates the long verbatim segments that CP-Δ still produces (CP-Δ's longest match exceeds 500 chars on MathAbstracts vs. 73 chars for CP-Fuse). On utility, CP-Fuse matches or slightly exceeds the overfitted models (pass@1: 0.47 vs. 0.43 on APPS; fluency: 2.17 vs. 2.17), while CP-Δ is comparable and MemFree consistently underperforms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the k-NAF framework and CP-Δ algorithm from Vyas et al. (2023), which proposed combining two generative models trained on disjoint copyrighted data to achieve provable copyright protection. The key departure is that CP-Δ uses fixed equal weights (α=β=1/2) at each token without considering the generated sequence history, whereas CP-Fuse adaptively adjusts the logit-ensemble weights based on the full prefix y_<t, solving a convex optimization at each decoding step. The method also draws on the broader model-fusion literature (logit ensembling, weight merging) and positions itself as a composable post-hoc wrapper that can be layered on top of training-time memorization mitigations like goldfish loss or early stopping, rather than replacing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CP-Fuse offers a practical, post-hoc copyright protection mechanism that requires no retraining and can be applied to already-deployed model pairs, making it attractive in settings where retraining is infeasible. The balancing property (Lemma 3.3) provides a principled theoretical explanation for why adaptive fusion prevents regurgitation, going beyond the purely heuristic nature of prior inference-time methods. Key limitations include the copyright separability assumption (disjoint copyrighted sets across the two models), the need for access to two separately trained models, and per-token grid-search overhead. Open questions raised by the authors include performance when separability only partially holds and validation on genuinely large-scale copyrighted content (books, songs) with larger models.
