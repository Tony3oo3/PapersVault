## Problem

Scaling diffusion-based illumination editing to large, diverse, in-the-wild datasets is difficult because without appropriate constraints the model degenerates into a structure-guided random image generator rather than performing precise illumination manipulation. Additionally, the stochastic nature of diffusion and latent encode-decode processes make it hard to preserve intrinsic properties like albedo and fine-grained details when modifying lighting. Existing methods lack a physically grounded mechanism to enforce that only illumination changes while all other image properties remain fixed.

## Input / Output

The model takes an object appearance image paired with a degradation image (same object under different illumination), a 32×32×3 HDRI environment map, a text prompt describing the desired illumination, and optionally a background image. The environment map is projected through a 3072→4096→4096→4096→2304 MLP into a 3×768 token embedding consumed as text conditioning, while the degradation image is fed through 4 extra input channels to the UNet. Outputs include the relit object image, an optionally generated background, and—by averaging multiple relit inferences under orthogonal light directions—a derived normal map.

## Method

The core contribution is the IC-Light (Imposing Consistent Light Transport) loss, which exploits the linearity of light transport: an object's appearance under a mixed illumination L₁+L₂ equals the sum of its appearances under L₁ and L₂ separately. This linearity extends to diffusion targets (ε-prediction, v-prediction, flow match), so the model is penalized when its prediction under the combined environment map deviates from a learnable MLP φ of its predictions under the two split maps. In practice, the 32×32 environment map is randomly split into two masked regions L₁ and L₂, the diffusion network is evaluated on each, and the consistency loss ‖M ⊙ (ε_{L₁+L₂} − φ(ε_{L₁}, ε_{L₂}))‖² is added to the standard denoising objective with weight 0.1. The full pipeline is trained on >10M samples spanning real light-stage captures, Objaverse 3D renders, and in-the-wild images with synthetic illumination augmentations, using SD 1.5, SDXL, and Flux backbones with a scheduled data-mixing strategy that increases light-stage probability over training.

## Results

On 50,000 unseen Objaverse 3D rendering samples, the full IC-Light method achieves PSNR 23.72, SSIM 0.8513, and LPIPS 0.1025, outperforming both external baselines. DiLightNet, the strongest external baseline, reaches PSNR 21.78, SSIM 0.8013, and LPIPS 0.1721, while SwitchLight scores PSNR 18.45, SSIM 0.7024, and LPIPS 0.3245. The ablation removing in-the-wild augmentation data (w/o aug.data) achieves the highest PSNR (23.95) but this is attributed to evaluation bias toward the 3D rendering test set, while the full method achieves the best LPIPS (0.1025), indicating superior perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IC-Light outperforms both external baselines on all three metrics. Against SwitchLight (Kim et al., 2024), the full method gains +5.27 dB PSNR, +0.149 SSIM, and reduces LPIPS from 0.3245 to 0.1025, a substantial margin reflecting SwitchLight's narrower portrait-focused training. Against DiLightNet (Zeng et al., 2024), the improvement is +1.94 dB PSNR, +0.050 SSIM, and LPIPS drops from 0.1721 to 0.1025, showing the benefit of multi-source data and the light transport consistency constraint. In visual comparisons (Fig. 6), IC-Light demonstrates higher robustness to shadows than Relightful Harmonization (Ren et al., 2024), and produces more detailed normal maps than both GeoWizard (Fu et al., 2024) and DSINE (Bae & Davison, 2024) for human subjects. The ablation removing light transport consistency (w/o LTC) drops to PSNR 20.32 and LPIPS 0.1927, confirming the constraint's critical role in preserving albedo and reducing color artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical light transport linearity principle validated by light stage measurements (Debevec et al., 2000) and applies it as a training-time consistency loss for diffusion-based illumination editing. It departs from prior relighting methods (SwitchLight, DiLightNet, Relightful Harmonization) that operate on smaller, more structured datasets by scaling to over 10 million diverse samples (in-the-wild augmentations, 3D renders, light stage captures) using strong backbones (SDXL, Flux). The key methodological departure is the IC-Light consistency loss, which enforces that the model's diffusion predictions respect linear superposition of illumination, a constraint absent from prior diffusion-based editing approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IC-Light constraint provides a physically grounded, lightweight mechanism to prevent diffusion models from degrading into structure-guided random generators when trained on large, noisy, diverse illumination data — a practical bottleneck that previously limited the scale and backbone strength of illumination editing models. The ability to unify in-the-wild, rendered, and light stage data under a single training objective opens the door to more robust, generalizable relighting for industrial content creation. Limitations include the empirical (non-optimized) nature of the normal map extraction, the assumption that light transport linearity holds across all material types, and the reliance on a learnable MLP to bridge HDR/LDR/latent domain gaps, whose behavior under extreme conditions remains uncharacterized.
