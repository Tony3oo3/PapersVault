## Problem

Bayesian Flow Networks (BFN) have shown promise for generative modeling in low-variance parameter spaces, but their extension to non-Euclidean manifolds—specifically the hyper-torus where crystal fractional coordinates reside—was previously unexplored. The fundamental barrier is that the additive accuracy property of Gaussian-based BFN does not hold for circular distributions, making the Bayesian flow distribution intractable and requiring new theoretical formulations. Additionally, existing diffusion-based crystal generators suffer from poor sampling efficiency (thousands of network forwards) and bias from approximating wrapped normal distributions.

## Input / Output

For ab initio generation, the method takes atom counts (sampled from a pre-computed distribution over the training set) and produces a full crystal structure: a 3×3 lattice matrix L, a 3×N fractional coordinate matrix F in [0,1), and N categorical atom types. For the structure prediction task, the atom type composition A is given as a conditioning input (concatenated as node features), and the model generates the corresponding lattice and fractional coordinates. The fractional coordinates are modeled on the hyper-torus T^{3×N} via von Mises distributions, the lattice in Euclidean space via Gaussian distributions, and atom types on the simplex S^K.

## Method

CrysBFN introduces the first periodic Bayesian flow on the hyper-torus by replacing the Gaussian base distribution with the von Mises distribution, which enjoys Bayesian conjugacy on circular data. The critical theoretical challenge is non-additive accuracy: the receiver's accumulated concentration parameter c is no longer a deterministic function of time but a stochastic distribution, necessitating an entropy conditioning mechanism (log c fed to the network) instead of standard time conditioning. A key reformulation (Eqs. 15–16) expresses the Bayesian flow distribution as a sum of weighted unit vectors, enabling fast non-iterative sampling via pure tensor operations (~4× faster than iterated simulation). The architecture is a periodic E(3)-equivariant GNN that jointly models all three modalities (fractional coordinates, lattice, atom types) with modality-specific Bayesian update rules, trained by minimizing KL divergence between sender and receiver distributions. This yields a 200× sampling efficiency gain (10 vs. 2000 NFE) over DiffCSP while achieving state-of-the-art match rates.

## Results

On the ab initio generation task, CrysBFN achieves state-of-the-art coverage and property fidelity across all three datasets: 99.12% COV-P on Carbon-24 (vs. 97.27% for DiffCSP), 99.79% COV-P and 87.51% compositional validity on MP-20 (vs. 99.76% and 83.25% for DiffCSP), and the lowest EMD on all monitored properties (e.g., d_ρ = 0.0612 on Carbon-24 vs. 0.0805 for DiffCSP). On the stable structure prediction task, CrysBFN reaches 64.35% match rate on MP-20 (vs. 51.49% for DiffCSP and 61.39% for FlowMM) and 20.52% on MPTS-52 (vs. 12.19% for DiffCSP). Notably, CrysBFN achieves a 200× sampling speedup, reaching 60.02% match rate with only 10 network forward passes compared to DiffCSP's 51.49% at 2000 steps on MP-20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrysBFN outperforms the strongest diffusion-based baseline DiffCSP on every dataset and metric in the ab initio generation task, with the largest gains on the more challenging MP-20 (compositional validity +4.26%, d_ρ reduced by 41%, d_E reduced by 49%). Against the flow-matching baseline FlowMM (ICML 2024), CrysBFN achieves higher structural validity (100% vs. 96.85%) and lower d_ρ (0.2067 vs. 0.239) on MP-20, though FlowMM reports a slightly lower d_elem (0.083 vs. 0.1628). On the structure prediction task, CrysBFN surpasses DiffCSP by ~13 percentage points in match rate on MP-20 (64.35% vs. 51.49%) and by ~8 points on MPTS-52 (20.52% vs. 12.19%), while also achieving the lowest RMSE across all three datasets. Compared to the VAE-based CDVAE, CrysBFN improves match rate by ~30 points on MP-20 and ~15 points on MPTS-52. The most striking comparison is sampling efficiency: CrysBFN matches or exceeds DiffCSP's quality with 200× fewer network forward passes (10 vs. 2000 NFE), achieving 60.02% match rate at 10 steps versus DiffCSP's 51.49% at 2000 steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Bayesian Flow Networks (Graves et al., 2023; Song et al., 2023) from their original Euclidean/Gaussian formulation to the non-Euclidean hyper-torus, which is the natural support for crystal fractional coordinates. It departs from the diffusion-based crystal generation line (DiffCSP, CDVAE, FlowMM) by replacing score-matching on wrapped normals with a conjugate von Mises Bayesian update, and from the original BFN by confronting the non-additive accuracy property that breaks the simulation-free training paradigm. The periodic E(3) equivariance framework is inherited from Jiao et al. (2023), but the generative mechanism is fundamentally restructured around entropy-conditioned belief updates rather than iterative denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 200× sampling efficiency gain (10 vs. 2000 NFE at comparable or better quality) is a major practical advance for crystal structure prediction, where inference cost is a bottleneck in high-throughput materials screening. The identification of non-additive accuracy as a fundamental barrier to extending BFN to periodic data, and the entropy-conditioning solution, open a path for applying Bayesian flow to other non-Euclidean manifolds (e.g., molecular torsions, protein backbone angles). Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large unit cells (>52 atoms), whether the linear-entropy schedule is optimal, and how the method performs under conditional generation with property targets.
