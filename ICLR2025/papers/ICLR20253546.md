## Problem

The heavy-ball momentum method, despite being the most natural acceleration of gradient descent, provably fails to achieve the accelerated O(√κ) convergence rate for general smooth strongly convex problems—its best known rate matches plain gradient descent at O(κ). This paper closes that long-standing theoretical gap by introducing the first heavy-ball variant with a provable global accelerated convergence guarantee.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253546/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires the smoothness constant L, strong convexity constant μ, and two initial points x₀, x₁; only one gradient evaluation per iteration is needed since ∇f(x_{k+1}) is reused in the next step. For the saddle-point extension, the coupling matrix B and its norm ‖B‖ are additional inputs, and each iteration costs 2 matrix-vector products plus 2 gradient evaluations. The composite variant additionally requires the proximal operator of the non-smooth term g, and the non-strongly convex variant (μ=0) uses a dynamic step size α_k = 2/(k+1) instead of a fixed one.

## Method

AOR-HB replaces the standard gradient ∇f(x_k) in the heavy-ball update with the over-relaxed term 2∇f(x_k) − ∇f(x_{k−1}), derived by discretizing a 2×2 first-order ODE system (x' = y−x, y' = x−y − μ⁻¹∇f(x)) at step size α = √(μ/L). The key analytical insight is that the AOR term symmetrizes the error equation, allowing a modified Lyapunov function E^α = E + α⟨∇f(x)−∇f(x*), y−x*⟩ to satisfy a discrete strong Lyapunov property, which yields the global accelerated rate (1 + α/2)^(−k). The same discretization framework extends seamlessly to composite convex problems (implicit proximal step for g), strongly-convex-strongly-concave saddle-point problems with bilinear coupling (AOR applied to the Bv coupling term), and a unified class of monotone operator equations, all retaining the O(√κ |log ε|) optimal first-order complexity.

## Results

AOR-HB achieves a globally accelerated linear convergence rate of (1/(1 + ½√(μ/L)))^k for smooth strongly convex problems, requiring O(√κ |log ε|) iterations — the optimal first-order complexity. For non-strongly convex problems, the variant AOR-HB-0 achieves O(1/k²) rate comparable to NAG. For strongly-convex-strongly-concave saddle point problems with bilinear coupling, AOR-HB-saddle achieves O(√(L_f/μ_f + L_g/μ_g) + ‖B‖²/(μ_f μ_g) · |log ε|) iterations with only two parameters (μ, L), versus five for the LPD method. Numerically, AOR-HB matches NAG and ADR on smooth convex problems, outperforms FISTA and APG on non-convex ℓ₁-ℓ₂ minimization, and consistently converges faster than AG-OG, APDG, LPD, and EG on saddle point problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NAG (Nesterov, 2013), AOR-HB achieves comparable convergence speed on smooth convex problems (Figure 1) and the same O(√κ |log ε|) complexity, but AOR-HB is derived from a simpler 2×2 first-order ODE system and generalizes more naturally to composite and saddle-point settings. Against Polyak's HB (1964), AOR-HB provides a provable global accelerated guarantee, whereas HB is shown to fail convergence on certain smooth strongly convex functions (Figure 1) and achieves at best a non-accelerated 1−O(ρ) rate. Against TM (Van Scoy et al., 2017), TM achieves a slightly tighter rate of (1−√(μ/L))² but requires more parameter tuning and has not been extended beyond strongly convex smooth optimization. Against FISTA and APG on composite problems, AOR-HB-composite outperforms both on the non-convex ℓ₁−ℓ₂ minimization (Figure 4), while all methods stall on the Lasso problem due to the ground-truth solution not being the true minimizer. Against saddle-point baselines (AG-OG, APDG, LPD, EG), AOR-HB-saddle consistently exhibits faster linear convergence (Figure 5), requires fewer parameters (2 vs. 5 for LPD), and the semi-implicit variant AOR-HB-saddle-I achieves the fastest convergence overall; EG is strictly dominated with O(κ_g) vs. O(√κ_g) iteration complexity (Figure 6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Polyak's heavy-ball momentum method by applying a classical over-relaxation correction (Hadjidimos, 1978) to the gradient term, yielding the AOR-HB method. The convergence analysis is built on the first-order ODE system and strong Lyapunov property framework of Chen & Luo (2021) and Luo & Chen (2022), which the authors argue makes the proof more transparent than the traditional second-order ODE or velocity-based formulations. The work is positioned as closing a gap identified by Goujaud et al. (2023), who proved that the standard HB method provably cannot achieve accelerated convergence for smooth strongly convex problems. The extension to composite and saddle-point problems draws on the proximal operator framework (Beck & Teboulle, 2009) and the lifted primal-dual method (Thekumparampil et al., 2022), respectively, but with a simpler two-parameter structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AOR-HB provides the first provably globally accelerated heavy-ball variant, resolving a long-standing open question about whether any HB modification can match Nesterov's O(√κ |log ε|) rate. The method's simplicity (a single gradient correction) and its natural extension to composite and saddle-point problems make it a potentially unifying framework for accelerated first-order methods beyond convex optimization. Key limitations include the requirement of knowing μ and L, convergence rates slightly looser than triple momentum, and the absence of stochastic or non-convex guarantees. Open questions include adaptive parameter selection, stochastic extensions, and whether the 2×2 ODE perspective enables acceleration in genuinely non-convex settings.
