## Problem

Existing radar-camera 3D object detection methods achieve strong performance under clean conditions but lack systematic robustness against environmental disturbances: adverse weather degrades camera signals while radar suffers from noise, positional ambiguity, and spurious returns. No prior work has jointly analyzed and addressed both radar and camera corruptions, and no dedicated benchmark exists for evaluating radar-camera fusion under realistic sensor degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250458/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes six camera images at 256×704 resolution and an accumulated 8-sweep radar point cloud where each point carries five dimensions (x, y, z, RCS, Doppler velocity) within a ±51.2 m range. It produces 3D object bounding boxes via a 128×128 BEV feature map generated by SECONDFPN with 112 depth bins spanning 2.0–58.0 m. The radar encoder uses a PointPillar-style voxelization, and the camera branch follows the CRN image encoder with an added confidence-map projector in BEV space.

## Method

RobuRCDet introduces two modules for robustness. The 3D Gaussian Expansion (3DGE) module exploits the density gap between target-region radar points (dense) and false-positive points (sparse): a parameter encoder takes RCS and velocity (not position) to predict a deformable kernel size λ_p ∈ {1,3,5} and a Gaussian variance σ, then spreads RCS and velocity values into surrounding voxels following a 2D Gaussian, sums and normalizes within each voxel, and adds the result to the original voxel in a residual connection. The Confidence-guided Multi-modal Cross-Attention (CMCA) module generates a per-pixel camera confidence map M_c via an MLP+Softmax head on BEV image features, then adaptively reweights camera and radar features (f_I·M_c and f_p·(1−M_c)) before feeding both the original and confidence-weighted concatenations as keys/values into multi-scale deformable cross-attention, so the fusion automatically shifts toward radar in low-visibility conditions and toward camera in clear conditions without any explicit quality-assessment loss. The full pipeline is trained for 24 epochs with AdamW on nuScenes, optionally with an 8:2 clean-to-noisy training mix that randomly applies one of four radar corruptions or one of four weather/light degradations per sample.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, RobuRCDet with ResNet-18 achieves 55.0 NDS and 45.5 mAP, surpassing CRN (54.2 NDS, 44.9 mAP) and RCBEVDet (54.8 NDS, 42.9 mAP). With ResNet-50, RobuRCDet reaches 56.7 NDS and 51.2 mAP, outperforming CRN by 2.2 mAP (4.5% relative) and RCBEVDet by 5.9 mAP (13.0% relative). Under simultaneous radar and camera interference, RobuRCDet achieves a 19.4% NDS and 25.7% mAP improvement over the baseline with only radar and camera backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

With ResNet-18, RobuRCDet outperforms CRN by 0.8 NDS and 0.6 mAP, and RCBEVDet by 0.2 NDS and 2.6 mAP on the clean nuScenes val set. With ResNet-50, RobuRCDet surpasses CRN by 0.7 NDS and 2.2 mAP, and RCBEVDet by 5.9 mAP (though trailing by 0.1 NDS). Against older radar-camera baselines, RobuRCDet leads CenterFusion by 9.7 NDS and 12.3 mAP (R18), CRAFT by 3.3 NDS and 4.4 mAP, and RCBEV4d by 5.3 NDS and 7.4 mAP. Under corruption conditions (Table 2), RobuRCDet consistently outperforms both CRN and RCBEVDet across all noise types: for example, under snowy conditions it achieves 44.7 NDS vs. 41.6 (CRN) and 44.1 (RCBEVDet), and under nighttime it reaches 42.6 NDS and 39.0 mAP vs. 38.2/31.4 (CRN) and 42.1/35.0 (RCBEVDet). The CMCA module is also shown to be transferable: when plugged into CRN, it yields 1.4 NDS and 0.7 mAP improvement in rainy scenarios without retraining the full model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CRN (Kim et al., 2023b) for its image encoder and radar processing pipeline, extending it with two robustness-specific modules: a 3D Gaussian Expansion (3DGE) that uses RCS and Doppler velocity priors to create deformable kernels for voxel-space point filtering, and a Confidence-guided Multi-modal Cross-Attention (CMCA) module for adaptive radar-camera fusion. It departs from prior radar-camera fusion methods (CenterFusion, CRAFT, RCBEV, RCBEVDet) that focus on detection accuracy under clean conditions, and from robustness benchmarks (RoboBEV, Robo3D, RoboDepth) that target LiDAR or camera corruptions, by being the first to systematically define and evaluate radar-specific noise patterns (key-point missing, spurious points, point shifting, non-positional disturbance) in a radar-camera fusion setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first corruption taxonomy and benchmark specifically for radar-camera 3D detection, filling a gap where prior robustness work ignored radar modality. The pattern-based noise classification (grouping reflections, multi-path effects, and weather-induced echoes under the same category) is a practical framing that avoids needing to identify exact physical causes. However, all radar corruptions are simulated rather than collected from real degraded hardware, and evaluation is limited to nuScenes, leaving open how well the approach transfers to other radar-camera datasets or real-world deployment with different radar hardware. The modest clean-data improvements (0.6–0.9 NDS) suggest the robustness modules trade some accuracy for resilience, a trade-off that may not be acceptable in all applications.
