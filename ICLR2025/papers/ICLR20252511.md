## Problem

Existing multimodal reasoning benchmarks predominantly use multiple-choice formats that test evaluation rather than creation, the highest cognitive level in Bloom's taxonomy. While MLLMs achieve strong scores on visual understanding tasks, their capacity for open-ended, abstract relational reasoning across multiple images—specifically analogical mapping where a model must infer a relationship from one image pair and transfer it to a novel pair—remains largely unmeasured. VOILA addresses this gap by requiring models to generate (not select) the solution to a visual analogy, exposing limitations in inter-image relationship comprehension and high-level relational inference.

## Input / Output

Each question presents three 1024×1024 SDXL-generated images (A, A′, B) forming the analogy A:A′::B:B′, with the model required to produce both a text description and a generated image for B′. The benchmark is fully dynamic: 7,280 curated images are recombined across 19 rule-property structures to yield over 6.4M distinct questions, split into VOILA-WD (10K questions, 4 rules including Distraction) and VOILA-ND (3.6K questions, 3 rules). Evaluation is decomposed into four scored sub-steps—describing images, identifying relations, applying relations, and generating the fourth image—each scored per-property (number, subject type, action) using GPT-4o as an automated judge against ground-truth text.

## Method

VOILA constructs visual analogies over three discrete properties (number of subjects from {1,2,3,4}, subject type from 14 categories, action from 13 activities) governed by four rules: Stable (value unchanged), Change (value swapped), Arithmetic (numeric increment/decrement), and Distraction (value irrelevant, answer is ANY). The 19 unique structures arise from combinatorial assignments of these rules to properties, with the Distraction rule introducing irrelevant variation that models must filter out. Images are generated via SDXL (guidance scale 8, 30 candidates per prompt, 10 retained after manual quality filtering) to ensure clear, object-centered content. The evaluation pipeline employs Least-to-Most prompting, decomposing the analogy into four sequential sub-tasks where each step's output feeds the next, enabling fine-grained diagnosis of where models fail. Ablation studies further isolate the effects of input format (sequential vs. collage images), prompting strategy (L2M vs. direct vs. CoT), and modality (visual vs. textual input) on performance at each reasoning stage.

## Results

On the final 'Applying Relationship' step (the core analogical reasoning task), the best MLLM accuracy is 13.16% (LLaMa 3.2) on VOILA-WD and 29.03% (GPT-4o) on VOILA-ND, compared to human performance of 71.36% and 69.69% respectively—a gap of approximately 58% and 40%. GPT-4o leads in image description (78.94% on VOILA-WD) and relationship identification (42.79% on VOILA-WD, 38.60% on VOILA-ND), but all models suffer a sharp accuracy drop at each successive reasoning step. L2M prompting consistently outperforms direct answering (e.g., GPT-4o: 6.44% vs. 0.90% on VOILA-WD applying relationships), and sequential image input yields ~40% improvement over image collage format.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o dominates the first two reasoning steps across both datasets, achieving 78.94% on image description and 42.79% on relationship identification (VOILA-WD), far ahead of LLaMa 3.2 (68.28%, 29.00%) and Qwen2-VL (77.80%, 20.58%). However, on the critical 'Applying Relationship' step, LLaMa 3.2 (13.16%) outperforms GPT-4o (6.44%) on VOILA-WD, suggesting LLaMa 3.2 handles distraction rules more effectively. On the simpler VOILA-ND, GPT-4o leads with 29.03%, nearly 4× the next-best model (LLaMa 3.2 at 6.83%). CogVLM2 and Qwen2-VL (image collage) trail significantly, with applying-relationship accuracy below 1% on VOILA-WD. MolmoE performs worst overall (7.76% description, 0.08% applying on VOILA-WD). All MLLMs fall 40–58 percentage points below human performance (71.36% WD, 69.69% ND) on the final reasoning step, confirming a substantial gap in abstract relational reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual analogical reasoning evaluation line (Raven's Progressive Matrices, VISALOGY, VASR) by moving from static, multiple-choice formats to a dynamic, open-ended generation task with a distraction mechanism. It repurposes the SDXL diffusion model not as a method under study but as a dataset-creation tool, enabling over 6.4M unique analogy questions across 19 rule-property configurations. The multi-step evaluation pipeline draws on Least-to-Most prompting (Zhou et al., 2023) to decompose the analogy into perceptual, relational, and generative sub-tasks, positioning VOILA as a diagnostic instrument rather than a methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a specific and actionable bottleneck: MLLMs can describe images with ~78% accuracy but collapse to 3–13% when asked to transfer a relational pattern to a novel image, and even with ground-truth relationships provided, GPT-4o reaches only 17% on the application step. This suggests the limitation is in the reasoning/transfer mechanism itself, not in perception, which reframes where future architectural or training efforts should focus. Open questions include whether the gap is inherent to the current MLLM paradigm or addressable through targeted relational-reasoning training, and whether the distraction-filtering ability (where LLaMA 3.2 unexpectedly outperforms GPT-4o) reflects a generalizable property or an artifact of the narrow property space (14 subjects, 13 actions, 4 numbers).
