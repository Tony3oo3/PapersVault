## Problem

Graph neural networks are limited to pairwise interactions and cannot flexibly model higher-order, multi-way relationships. While topological deep learning (TDL) addresses this by operating on combinatorial topological spaces, little is known about how to incorporate geometric features (positions, velocities) into TDL models while respecting E(n) symmetry. No general framework existed for designing E(n)-equivariant message passing networks over the broad class of combinatorial topological spaces beyond graphs and simplicial complexes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a domain-specific combinatorial complex (CC) with cells at multiple ranks (e.g., atoms/bonds/rings for molecules, or points/roads/census tracts for geospatial data), geometric node positions in R^n, and rank-specific non-geometric feature vectors. Geometric invariants (pairwise distances, Hausdorff distances, convex hull volumes) are computed from positions and injected into messages. The output is updated cell embeddings and equivariantly transformed node positions, which feed a readout head for downstream regression (molecular properties or PM2.5 concentration at ~22m resolution). The CC structure and neighborhood functions are hand-designed per application rather than learned.

## Method

ETNNs perform E(n)-equivariant message passing over combinatorial complexes using a scalarization strategy: geometric features are first converted to E(n)-invariant scalars (e.g., pairwise distances, centroid distances, Hausdorff distances), then processed by learnable MLPs before being combined along original directions to restore equivariance. Each layer updates cell embeddings via permutation-invariant aggregation of neighborhood-dependent messages (using up/down incidences, adjacencies, and a novel max adjacency through a virtual cell), and updates node positions by adding weighted direction vectors scaled by learned scalar functions of the messages. The CC framework pseudo-generalizes graphs, simplicial complexes, cell complexes, path complexes, and hypergraphs, enabling arbitrary hierarchical higher-order interactions with heterogeneous neighborhood types. Theoretically, ETNNs are shown to be at least as expressive as EGNNs and strictly more expressive in most cases via a Geometric Augmented Hasse Graph analysis. Empirically, ETNNs match or surpass EMPSNs on QM9 with ~2 orders of magnitude fewer cells and half the memory/runtime, and outperform EGNN/GNN/MLP baselines on a novel hyperlocal air pollution downscaling benchmark.

## Results

On the QM9 molecular property benchmark, ETNN-* achieves MAE improvements of 13%, 6%, 10%, 12%, 26%, and 3% over EGNN across the six reported properties (α, Δε, ε_HOMO, ε_LUMO, μ, Cν), with the largest gain on dipole moment μ (0.022 D vs. 0.029 D). ETNN matches or outperforms the reproduced EMPSN* on 4 of 6 properties while using roughly two orders of magnitude fewer cells (39.8 vs. 555.25 per molecule), resulting in half the memory usage and nearly half the runtime per epoch. On the novel hyperlocal PM2.5 downscaling task in the Bronx, NY, ETNN achieves 9.34% average R² (MSE 0.935), substantially outperforming GNN (2.44%), EGNN (1.43%), MLP (2.35%), and Linear (0.51%) baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETNN outperforms EGNN (Satorras et al., 2021) on all six QM9 properties, with the most substantial gain on dipole moment μ (−26%, 0.022 vs. 0.029 D) and polarizability α (−13%, 0.062 vs. 0.071 bohr³). Against the reproduced EMPSN* (Eijkelboom et al., 2023), ETNN wins on ε_HOMO (26 vs. 29 meV), ε_LUMO (22 vs. 25 meV), and μ (0.022 vs. 0.030 D), while EMPSN* edges ahead on α (0.061 vs. 0.062), Δε (42 vs. 45), and Cν (0.028 vs. 0.030); however, ETNN achieves this with ~14× fewer cells and roughly half the memory and runtime. ETNN also beats TopNet† (Verma et al., 2024) on all six properties, and dominates the older baselines NMP, SchNet, TFN, and SE(3)-Transformer across the board. The only method that consistently outperforms ETNN is the non-equivariant DimeNet++ (Gasteiger et al., 2020) and the SotA Equiformer, which use richer tensor-based equivariance rather than scalarization. On the air pollution task, ETNN (9.34% R²) surpasses GNN (2.44%), EGNN (1.43%), MLP (2.35%), and Linear (0.51%) by a wide margin, confirming the benefit of multi-level combinatorial structure combined with E(n) equivariance for irregular geospatial data.

## Relation to Prior Work

ETNNs generalize two prior lines of work: the scalarization-based E(n)-equivariant message passing of EGNN (Satorras et al., 2021) for graphs and EMPSN (Eijkelboom et al., 2023) for simplicial complexes, by lifting both to the more general combinatorial complex (CC) framework of Hajij et al. (2022b). The CC backbone provides a pseudo-generalization that subsumes graphs, simplicial complexes, cell complexes, path complexes, and hypergraphs under a single message-passing architecture, while the scalarization equivariance mechanism (geometric invariants → learnable processing → directional update) is carried over from EGNN/EMPSN. The key departure is that neighborhood functions and cell structures become application-specific design choices rather than being rigidly determined by a fixed topological space, enabling domain-driven inductive biases (e.g., molecular rings as 2-cells, census tracts as 2-cells in geospatial data).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ETNNs provide the first unifying framework for scalarization-based E(n)-equivariant TDL, filling a gap where equivariant models existed only for specific CTSs (graphs, simplicial complexes) but not for the broader class of combinatorial objects. The practical impact is demonstrated by the molecular CC achieving comparable or better QM9 results than EMPSN with ~14× fewer cells and half the memory/runtime, validating that domain-structured CCs are a more efficient inductive bias than generic Vietoris-Rips constructions. Open questions include extending to scale invariance, temporal/dynamic variants, rank-specific symmetry groups, and automatically learning geometric invariants. The geospatial CC formulation for multi-resolution irregular data opens a new direction for TDL in GIS applications, though the absolute R² values remain low, suggesting the task is still far from solved.
