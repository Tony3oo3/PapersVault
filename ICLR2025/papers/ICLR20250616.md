## Problem

Risk-sensitive RL with the entropic risk measure requires knowledge of the transition kernel or suffers from unstable updates with exponential Bellman equations, restricting existing algorithms to tabular or low-dimensional settings. Existing variational RL-as-inference methods either constrain posterior dynamics (producing overly stochastic policies) or allow them to vary (yielding risk-seeking policies that ignore aleatoric risk), and all assume deterministic rewards. This paper addresses all three limitations by formulating a variational surrogate that handles stochastic rewards and supports both risk-seeking and risk-averse regimes with function approximators.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations (continuous vectors in MuJoCo/2D environments or discrete grid states) and a scalar risk parameter β (positive for risk-seeking, negative for risk-averse), which can be fixed or auto-tuned via dual gradient descent on a KL constraint. It outputs a Gaussian policy over actions whose risk sensitivity is modulated by β: small |β| produces strongly risk-seeking or risk-averse behavior, while large |β| recovers the standard risk-neutral policy. The algorithm also produces learned variational dynamics and reward models (Gaussian networks) that encode the agent's optimistic or pessimistic view of the environment.

## Method

rsVAC formulates an ELBO on the entropic risk measure by introducing binary auxiliary variables for optimal actions, yielding a variational objective J_β(q,π) that trades off expected return against KL divergence from the true environment model. An EM-style algorithm alternates an E-step (learning variational Gaussian dynamics q_d and reward model q_r via reparameterized gradient ascent, making the model optimistic for β>0 or pessimistic for β<0) and an M-step (optimizing the policy via standard actor-critic on augmented rewards r̂_t that include KL penalty terms, using branched rollouts from the variational model). The key extensions over prior variational model-based methods are: (1) a stochastic reward model q_r that captures aleatoric reward uncertainty, and (2) a unified treatment of the risk-averse regime (β<0) where the M-step becomes a minimization, equivalent to a saddle-point problem. The practical algorithm uses TD3 or SAC as the actor-critic backbone, with separate environment and model replay buffers, and a stability modification that removes log-terms from the critic update to prevent divergence.

## Results

On risk-averse MuJoCo benchmarks (InvertedPendulum, HalfCheetah, Swimmer), rsVAC (with both TD3 and SAC actor-critics) achieves the highest average return and fastest convergence among all compared risk-sensitive methods, while also correctly modulating the percentage of timesteps spent in stochastic-reward regions. In the tabular gridworld, rsVAC (β>0) matches or slightly exceeds VMBPO and Q-learning in expected return, and dual optimization over β converges to the same optimal value regardless of initialization. In the stochastic 2D environment, rsVAC produces clearly differentiated risk-seeking (small positive β), risk-neutral (large |β|), and risk-averse (negative β) policies, with negative β terminating episodes predominantly in the low-risk region and small positive β in the high-risk region.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VMBPO (Chow et al., 2021) in the tabular gridworld, rsVAC (β>0) performs comparably or slightly better in expected return, and additionally supports the risk-averse regime (β<0) which VMBPO cannot handle. On the MuJoCo risk-averse benchmarks, rsVAC outperforms all three baselines—Mean Gini deviation (MG, Luo et al., 2024), Mean-Variance Policy Iteration (MVPI, Zhang et al., 2021), and Exponential TD (expTD, Noorani et al., 2023)—in both average return and learning speed across InvertedPendulum, HalfCheetah, and Swimmer. MVPI is the closest competitor, also producing risk-averse policies in all three domains, but yields consistently lower overall mean returns. MG uses a PPO-style policy gradient and requires collecting 10 full episodes before each model update, making it slower to converge. expTD, which directly optimizes the entropic risk measure via an exponentiated critic, shows the weakest performance, consistent with known instabilities of exponential Bellman updates with function approximation. The additional Ant-v4 experiment (Fig. 9) confirms that rsVAC's advantage generalizes beyond the three primary MuJoCo tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variational RL-as-inference framework (Levine, 2018) and the EM-style variational model-based policy optimization of VMBPO (Chow et al., 2021) in two key directions: incorporating a variational reward model to handle stochastic rewards (previously assumed deterministic), and extending to the risk-averse regime (β < 0) that prior RL-as-inference methods largely ignored. The practical algorithm rsVAC builds on branched rollout (Janner et al., 2019) for model-based data collection and can be instantiated with any actor-critic backbone (TD3, SAC), positioning it as a flexible wrapper rather than a monolithic method. The theoretical contribution formalizes the Bellman-style operator for the variational objective and proves convergence, bridging the gap between the entropic risk measure's exponential Bellman equations (which are unstable with function approximation) and a tractable surrogate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Risk-sensitive RL is critical for safety-critical applications (autonomous driving, portfolio management) where expected-return maximization produces dangerous behavior, yet the entropic risk measure has been largely restricted to tabular or low-dimensional settings due to optimization difficulties. rsVAC provides the first practical model-based algorithm that handles both risk-seeking and risk-averse policies with stochastic rewards in high-dimensional continuous control, filling a gap between the theoretical elegance of entropic risk and deployable algorithms. Open questions include: the risk-averse setting is a saddle-point problem without monotonic improvement guarantees, the KL constraint tuning (dual optimization over β) may not scale to very long horizons, and the method's reliance on learned probabilistic dynamics introduces model bias that could compound in long rollouts. The paper's stability modifications (removing log-terms from critic updates, separate critic for real data) suggest the approach is still being tuned and may not be fully robust across all environments.
