## Problem

Foundation models like CLIP, trained on web-scale datasets such as LAION-400M, appear to generalize well to out-of-domain (OOD) shifts like renditions (sketches, paintings), but this may be an artifact of domain contamination—i.e., the training set already contains images from the test domain. Prior work addressed sample-level duplication but left domain-level contamination unexamined, making it impossible to determine whether CLIP's OOD robustness is genuine or illusory.

## Input / Output

The domain classifier takes a single RGB image and outputs one of three labels (natural, rendition, ambiguous) using a texture-centric criterion: images preserving fine-grained texture are natural, while those with flat or simplified textures are renditions. The CLIP models consume image-text pairs (224×224 RGB images with natural language captions) and produce class labels via zero-shot evaluation. The introduced datasets—LAION-Natural (~57M images) and LAION-Rendition (~16M images)—are subsampled from deduplicated LAION-200M at a 98% classifier-precision threshold, and existing ImageNet/DomainNet test sets are similarly cleaned to remove cross-domain samples.

## Method

The paper first trains two domain classifiers on 13K manually labeled LAION-200M images: a fine-tuned CLIP ViT-L/14 with a linear readout (FT) for natural images and a density-ratio classifier (DR-R) for renditions, both thresholded at 98% validation precision. These classifiers partition LAION-200M into LAION-Natural and LAION-Rendition, and also clean standard OOD test sets. CLIP ViT-B/32 models are then trained from scratch (32 epochs, batch size 16384) on single-domain datasets, controlled mixtures (LAION-Mix), and random LAION-200M subsets at scales from 4M to 57M samples. Performance is measured via 'relative corrected OOD accuracy' (ratio of single-domain model accuracy to equally-sized contaminated-subset model accuracy) and effective robustness plots. The key finding is that removing rendition samples from training drops rendition-domain accuracy to ~0.4× the contaminated baseline, demonstrating that domain contamination—not intrinsic OOD generalization—drives CLIP's apparent robustness, and that a rendition-to-natural mixing ratio of 1:1 to 3:1 yields optimal overall performance.

## Results

CLIP ViT-B/32 trained exclusively on LAION-Natural (57M natural images) achieves only 21.98% top-1 accuracy on the rendition domain (standard test sets) and 17.81% on clean rendition test sets, compared to 40.48–41.46% when 13–16M rendition samples are added (LAION-Mix). The relative corrected OOD accuracy on clean rendition benchmarks drops to approximately 0.4, indicating a ~60% performance loss without domain contamination. Natural-domain performance remains largely unaffected (36.88% vs. 37.28–37.62%), confirming that the OOD gap is specific to the rendition domain. The optimal rendition-to-natural mixing ratio for best overall performance is between 1:1 and 1:3, consistent across dataset scales from 4M to 57M samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For domain classification, the paper's fine-tuned CLIP (FT) ternary classifier achieves the highest recall for the natural class (0.43–0.44 at 98% precision) compared to the density-ratio classifier DR-R (0.05) and centroid embeddings CE (0.15), while DR-R achieves the highest recall for the rendition class (0.53) over FT (0.42) and CSD (0.21–0.52 depending on k). The CSD method from Somepalli et al. (2024) shows highly variable performance depending on the k parameter, with k=1 giving 0.52 average rendition recall but only 0.64 precision, while k=6 drops to 0.21 recall. For the core CLIP OOD evaluation, the key comparison is between LAION-Natural-trained CLIP and LAION-Rand-57M-trained CLIP: the latter outperforms on renditions (40.66% vs. 21.98% standard; 39.58% vs. 17.81% clean) purely due to its ~8–14% rendition content, while both achieve comparable natural-domain accuracy. This demonstrates that CLIP's previously reported OOD robustness on ImageNet-R and ImageNet-Sketch is an artifact of domain contamination rather than genuine generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the domain generalization evaluation framework from the ImageNet era (Gulrajani & Lopez-Paz, 2021) to the web-scale foundation model setting, specifically addressing a gap left by Mayilvahanan et al. (2023), who corrected for sample-level contamination but not domain-level contamination in CLIP's training data. It builds on density-ratio classification (Cohen-Wang et al., 2024) and contrastive style descriptors (Somepalli et al., 2024) to construct domain classifiers, and on semantic deduplication (Abbas et al., 2023) to obtain LAION-200M as the base dataset. The work refines Fang et al. (2022), who showed that CLIP's robustness is driven by its training distribution but did not identify which specific distributional property (domain overlap vs. natural image diversity) is responsible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that CLIP's widely-cited OOD robustness on rendition benchmarks is largely an artifact of domain contamination in its training data, not genuine generalization ability—when trained on clean natural images only, rendition performance drops to levels comparable to ImageNet-era models. This re-opens the domain generalization problem for the foundation model era and provides the community with clean single-domain datasets (LAION-Natural, LAION-Rendition) and refined test sets for fair evaluation. Limitations include the focus on a single domain pair (natural vs. rendition), lack of control over object class distribution across splits, and the exclusion of ambiguous samples from analysis. Open questions include whether findings generalize to other domain shifts, the role of ambiguous/mixed-domain samples, and whether architectural or objective changes can close the gap without domain overlap.
