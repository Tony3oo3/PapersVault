## Problem

Most content-style disentanglement methods rely on domain-specific labels, paired data, or hand-crafted inductive biases, limiting their generalizability across domains and modalities. Purely unsupervised alternatives (MINE, adversarial training, DSAE) either suffer from training instability, assume static style within a sample, or fail to produce interpretable symbolic representations. The paper asks whether a domain-general, label-free method can learn disentangled content and style by exploiting only the meta-level statistical asymmetry between the two factors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251633/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each data sample is a sequence of L fragments (e.g., individual digit images in a phone-number string, one-second note spectrograms in a musical phrase, or action frames in a sprite video), and the model processes each fragment independently through the encoder. The content branch outputs a discrete VQ index from a codebook of size K (typically 10–80), while the style branch outputs a continuous latent vector; the decoder fuses both to reconstruct the fragment. The VQ codebook is the key interpretable artifact: at K equal to the number of content classes, entries align near one-to-one with human-recognizable symbols (digits, pitches, actions, phonemes).

## Method

V3 is a two-branch VQ-VAE where the content encoder output is quantized to a small codebook and the style encoder produces a continuous vector, with a shared decoder reconstructing each fragment. The core contribution is a set of four variance-versus-invariance (V3) regularization terms built on mean pairwise distance (MPD) statistics computed along the fragment axis (within a sample) and the sample axis (across samples). These terms enforce, via hinge losses with a relativity threshold r, that content is more variable within samples than across them, style is more variable across samples than within them, and the two factors are ordered by variability in both directions. MPD is chosen over standard deviation for its sensitivity to multi-peak content distributions. The total loss is a weighted sum of reconstruction, VQ commitment, and the four V3 terms, trained end-to-end with no labels, paired data, or domain-specific assumptions.

## Results

On PhoneNums (K=10), V3 achieves 83.2% content PR-AUC and 95.4% style PR-AUC in latent retrieval, substantially outperforming unsupervised baselines (MINE-based: 33.8%/35.5%; Cycle loss: 55.1%/33.1%; β-VAE: 24.9%/25.6%). On InsNotes (K=12), V3 reaches 89.9% content PR-AUC and 87.5% style PR-AUC, again far exceeding MINE-based (56.4%/61.4%) and Cycle loss (49.7%/12.7%). In OOD few-shot content classification, V3 (fully unsupervised) surpasses the weakly supervised EC²-VAE(c) at 10-shot: 99.0% on PhoneNums vs. 92.7%, and 99.2% on InsNotes vs. 91.2%. On linear probing for content classification, V3 leads all unsupervised methods on SVHN (40.6%), Sprites (88.2%), and Libri100 (52.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

V3 consistently outperforms all three unsupervised baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the MINE-based method [Tjandra et al., 2020], V3 gains roughly 40–50 points in content PR-AUC (e.g., 83.2% vs. 33.8% on PhoneNums, 89.9% vs. 56.4% on InsNotes) and 30–55 points in style PR-AUC. Against the Cycle loss baseline [Zhu et al., 2017], V3 leads by 28–40 points in content retrieval and 50–60 points in style retrieval (e.g., 95.4% vs. 33.1% style PR-AUC on PhoneNums). Against vanilla β-VAE [Higgins et al., 2017], V3 improves content PR-AUC by 58–64 points. Compared to the weakly supervised EC²-VAE(c) [Yang et al., 2019], V3 trails in content retrieval (83.2% vs. 95.2% on PhoneNums) but surpasses it in style retrieval (95.4% vs. 57.7%), indicating V3 learns purer style representations without any style labels. In OOD few-shot settings, V3 (no supervision at all) overtakes EC²-VAE(c) (content-label supervised) once ≥1 shot is available, reaching 99.0–99.2% at 10-shot versus 92.7–91.2% for the supervised method.

## Relation to Prior Work

V3 builds on the VQ-VAE architecture (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) for discrete content representation and borrows the hinge-based ratio formulation from VICReg (Bardes et al., 2022) for its variance-versus-invariance constraints. However, it departs sharply from the dominant paradigm in content-style disentanglement, which typically relies on domain-specific labels, paired data, or hand-crafted inductive biases (e.g., fundamental frequency for pitch, X-vectors for speaker identity). The key conceptual departure is replacing all such domain-specific supervision with a single domain-general statistical observation: content varies within samples but maintains a consistent vocabulary across samples, while style does the opposite. This positions V3 alongside DSAE-family methods in spirit but extends them by enforcing both within-sample and across-sample constraints simultaneously, and by using VQ to produce interpretable discrete content symbols rather than continuous latent codes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a minimal, domain-agnostic inductive bias is sufficient to drive effective content-style disentanglement across audio, image, and video modalities, removing the need for paired data, labels, or domain expertise that has been a persistent barrier in this area. The emergence of near one-to-one codebook-to-label alignment without any supervision is a notable step toward interpretable representation learning. However, several limitations remain: V3 assumes non-overlapping content fragments, requires predefined segmentation, does not learn the semantic meaning of individual symbols, and its OOD few-shot advantage is demonstrated primarily on synthetic or semi-synthetic datasets. Extending the framework to unsegmented continuous data, polyphonic/multisource audio, and open-vocabulary content represents significant open questions.
