## Problem

Auto-regressive text-to-image generation models require hundreds to thousands of sequential forward passes, creating substantial inference latency. While Jacobi decoding offers a training-free parallel decoding approach, its deterministic convergence criterion is fundamentally incompatible with the sampling-based decoding (e.g., top-K sampling) that current text-to-image models rely on for visual diversity and quality. No existing training-free method can accelerate these models while preserving the randomness essential to their generation quality.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained auto-regressive text-to-image models (Lumina-mGPT 7B at 768×768, Anole 7B at 512×512), taking a text prompt as input and producing a generated RGB image. It modifies only the decoding schedule of the auto-regressive transformer component, using a sliding Jacobi window of W tokens (W≥16 for near-maximal acceleration) to predict multiple discrete image tokens per forward pass. The sampling randomness is controlled by the top-K logit sampler (K=2000 for Lumina-mGPT), and the method preserves this randomness through its probabilistic acceptance criterion rather than collapsing to greedy decoding.

## Method

SJD advances deterministic Jacobi decoding into a probabilistic algorithm by introducing a speculative verification step inspired by speculative sampling. In each iteration, a single forward pass computes conditional probabilities for a window of draft tokens, and tokens are accepted from left to right using a probabilistic threshold: a token is accepted if a uniform random variable r < min(1, p_current/p_previous). Rejected tokens are resampled from a calibrated distribution, and remaining tokens are refined (resampled from the current iteration's conditional probability), which the paper proves is essential for achieving acceleration. The method is fully training-free, requires no auxiliary draft model, and includes a spatial-prior-aware token initialization strategy that leverages 2D image locality (repeating or resampling from left/above adjacent tokens) to further reduce iterations for images with simple repeating patterns.

## Results

On MSCOCO2017 (A100), SJD accelerates Lumina-mGPT 7B from 87.23s to 42.73s (2.04× latency speedup, 2.22× step compression) with FID 30.85 vs. 30.76 baseline and CLIP-Score 31.35 vs. 31.29. On Anole, SJD with spatial-prior initialization reduces latency from 48.96s to 26.18s (1.87× speedup, 1.97× step compression) with FID 29.14 vs. 28.87. On Parti-prompts (RTX 4090), SJD achieves 2.12–2.13× latency acceleration on Lumina-mGPT and 1.92× on Anole, with CLIP-Score changes within 0.1 points of baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SJD dramatically outperforms vanilla Jacobi Decoding (Song et al., 2021) in acceleration: while JD achieves only 1.02× latency and 1.04× step speedup on Lumina-mGPT (essentially no gain), SJD reaches 2.04× and 2.22× respectively, because JD's deterministic convergence criterion is incompatible with the high-randomness top-K sampling that image generation requires. Against the Lumina-mGPT baseline (Liu et al., 2024), SJD halves inference time (87.23s → 42.73s) with FID changing by only +0.09 and CLIP-Score by +0.06. Against Anole (Chern et al., 2024), SJD with spatial-prior initialization achieves 1.87× latency speedup (48.96s → 26.18s) with FID +0.27 and CLIP-Score +0.02. On LlamaGen (Sun et al., 2024), SJD provides a more modest 1.54–1.63× step compression, which the authors attribute to the smaller model size reducing the effectiveness of multi-token prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two distinct lines of work: Jacobi decoding (Song et al., 2021), which iteratively decodes multiple tokens in parallel using a deterministic convergence criterion, and speculative sampling (Leviathan et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023), which uses a probabilistic acceptance criterion to verify draft tokens. The key departure is that SJD transplants the probabilistic verification mechanism of speculative sampling directly into the Jacobi iteration loop, eliminating the need for a separate draft model while making the algorithm compatible with the sampling-based decoding that recent AR text-to-image models (Lumina-mGPT, Anole) critically depend on for visual diversity. The paper also introduces spatial-prior-aware token initialization that exploits 2D image locality, a component absent from both prior lines of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As auto-regressive models become the backbone of native multi-modal systems (Chameleon, Emu3), their sequential token-by-token inference latency is a growing bottleneck, yet prior acceleration methods (Jacobi decoding, distillation) are either incompatible with sampling-based decoding or require expensive retraining. SJD provides a practical, training-free ~2× speedup that preserves the stochasticity essential for image diversity, making it immediately deployable on existing checkpoints. The ablation showing that the refinement step (Eq. 3, continuing to resample unaccepted tokens) is essential—without it the method degenerates to 2000+ forward passes—reveals a non-obvious mechanism. Open questions include whether fine-tuning for multi-token prediction could push acceleration beyond 2×, how the method scales to video generation with longer sequences, and why smaller models (LlamaGen) benefit less than 7B models.
