## Problem

Mask-based XAI methods require hundreds of optimization iterations to produce explanations, making them computationally expensive compared to gradient-based alternatives. While much prior work has focused on designing novel objective functions or perturbation architectures, the mask initialization strategy—typically a simple uniform or all-ones scheme—has been largely neglected despite its known impact on optimization outcomes. This paper asks whether a principled, gradient-informed initialization can reduce the computational burden and improve the quality of mask-based explanations.

## Input / Output

Given a pre-trained differentiable classifier Φ_c and a single input instance (an RGB image at 256×256 or a multivariate time series of length T with d features), StartGrad computes the absolute gradient of the predicted class score with respect to the input, taking the maximum across channels for multi-channel data. The output is a continuous mask m ∈ [0,1]^d (or [0,1]^k in a transformed latent space such as shearlet/wavelet coefficients) that serves as the starting point for downstream mask-based explanation optimizers like ShearletX, WaveletX, PixelMask, or ExtremalMask. The number of quantile bins in the transformation is set to match the input dimensionality (e.g., 65,536 for 256×256 pixel masks, 10,000 for shearlet coefficients).

## Method

StartGrad leverages a first-order Taylor expansion to show that the prediction distortion induced by masking is approximately proportional to the product of gradient magnitudes and perturbation magnitudes, implying that features with small |∇_{x_i} Φ_c(x)| can be masked at minimal cost. Concretely, it computes the absolute gradient of the predicted class score with respect to the input, then applies a Quantile Transformation Function (QTF) that maps the highly skewed (Laplace-like) gradient distribution to a uniform [0,1] range, preserving relative feature importance while handling the skewness. The key theoretical contribution is proving (Propositions 2 and 3) that the expected RDE loss—distortion plus λ·‖m‖₁—at initialization is provably no worse than uniform initialization and strictly better than all-ones initialization in high-dimensional settings. The method requires only a single forward-backward pass per sample, adding negligible runtime (0.7–3.6 s per image) relative to the hundreds of optimization iterations that follow, yet it enables speedups of up to 6.7× in reaching target explanation quality for ShearletX.

## Results

On ImageNet (500 samples, ResNet18), StartGrad initialization yields median CP-Pixel improvements of +236.37 over all-ones and +208.74 over uniform for ShearletX at 300 iterations, with statistically significant gains (p<0.01) across all methods and iteration steps. For ShearletX specifically, StartGrad achieves up to 6.73× speedup in reaching the final performance of all-ones initialization (44.57 vs. 300 iterations for IQM CP-Pixel). On the switch-feature time-series dataset, StartGrad outperforms uniform initialization in all four metrics (AUP, AUR, Information, Entropy) at 50 iterations, with the advantage narrowing as all methods converge by 500 iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the all-ones initialization (the original baseline for vision methods), StartGrad delivers the largest gains for ShearletX (+236.37 CP-Pixel at 300 iterations) and moderate gains for WaveletX (+0.11 at 300) and PixelMask (+0.33 at 300), with the most pronounced advantage at early iterations (50–100 steps). Against uniform initialization (the original baseline for time-series methods), StartGrad consistently outperforms across all vision methods at all iteration steps, with the gap widening over time for WaveletX (+5.20 CP-Pixel at 300 vs. +0.11 for all-ones). For the time-series ExtremalMask method, StartGrad beats uniform initialization in all four metrics at 50 iterations (e.g., AUR 0.805 vs. 0.746, Information 2.498 vs. 1.069) but ties with both baselines by 500 iterations, indicating the benefit is primarily in faster convergence rather than a higher final ceiling. The speedup analysis (Table 2) confirms that for ShearletX, StartGrad reaches the all-ones final performance in ~45 iterations (6.73× speedup) and the uniform final performance in ~46 iterations (6.60× speedup). Overall, StartGrad wins decisively on convergence speed and early-iteration quality, ties on final performance for most methods, and shows a genuine final-performance boost specifically for ShearletX.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying mask initialization as a previously neglected design choice in mask-based XAI methods, where prior work has focused almost exclusively on novel objective functions (RDE, IB), perturbation models, or architectural choices. StartGrad repurposes gradient signals—typically used as standalone saliency explanations (SmoothGrad, Integrated Gradients)—as a principled initialization for iterative mask optimization, grounded in the rate-distortion explanation framework. The theoretical contribution (Propositions 2 and 3) provides the first formal comparison of initialization strategies, showing gradient-based initialization is provably superior to uniform and all-ones schemes in balancing distortion and sparsity at the start of optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mask-based explanation methods are computationally expensive (hundreds of iterations), limiting their use in time-sensitive applications like healthcare; StartGrad addresses this by providing up to 6.73× speedup for ShearletX while maintaining or improving final quality. The conceptual insight that initialization quality matters as much as objective design challenges the field's implicit assumption that the learning objective is the primary lever for improvement. Open questions include the sensitivity to gradient noise and adversarial initialization, the choice of transformation function beyond QTF, and whether filtering high-frequency gradient artifacts before initialization yields further gains. The method's practical value is somewhat tempered by the fact that for PixelMask and WaveletX, gains at full convergence (300 iterations) are not statistically significant versus the all-ones baseline.
