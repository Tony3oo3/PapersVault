## Problem

While simple averaging of LoRA modules yields a merged model that roughly integrates individual capabilities, no closed-form solution existed for merging LoRA modules such that the merged model exactly matches the responses of all constituent modules. The paper shows that directly solving for both LoRA matrices (A and B) simultaneously yields an indeterminate system with infinitely many solutions, leaving open the question of how to obtain a unique, principled closed-form merge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253122/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For vision tasks, the method consumes 224×224 RGB images (resized from 32×32 for CIFAR-100) and outputs a single class label from the cumulative set of all incremental tasks. For the text experiment (Out-of-Scope), it processes natural-language utterances via a T5-small backbone. In the federated protocol, each client transmits only the diagonal of its local Gram matrix (a vector) and one low-rank matrix per communication round—never raw data or the full LoRA module—making the communication footprint a single vector plus one r×d or r×k matrix per layer.

## Method

LoRM adapts RegMean's closed-form regression solution to the LoRA residual ΔW = BA by showing the joint system for A and B is indeterminate, then resolving it via alternating optimization: in odd rounds, A is frozen and B is merged in closed form (Eq. 7); in even rounds, B is frozen and A is merged (Eq. 8). Spatial aggregation across N clients uses this alternating procedure at each communication round, while temporal aggregation across T tasks applies RegMean directly to the full task-specific residuals ΔW^t = B_M^t A_M^t (Eq. 9). The server stores task-specific residuals and global Gram matrices throughout training, and the final model is a single unified ViT-B/16 (or T5-small) with one merged LoRA residual per linear layer plus concatenated classification heads. This design simultaneously addresses privacy (only Gram-matrix diagonals are shared), communication efficiency (one matrix per round), and convergence speed (closed-form optimality at each aggregation step).

## Results

LoRM achieves state-of-the-art Final Average Accuracy (FAA) across all seven evaluated datasets in the Federated Class-Incremental Learning setting. On CIFAR-100 (β=0.5), LoRM reaches 86.95% FAA, surpassing the next-best CCVR (79.95%) by ~7 points and approaching the Joint upper bound of 92.75%. The advantage is most pronounced on out-of-domain and specialization datasets: EuroSAT (84.23% vs. 73.38% for CODA-Prompt) and Cars-196 (54.41% vs. 38.99% for CCVR). On the text-based Out-of-Scope dataset, LoRM achieves 84.78% FAA, outperforming RegMean (74.16%) and TIES-Merging (58.84%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRM outperforms all 11 baselines on 6 of 7 datasets, with the largest margins on out-of-domain (EuroSAT: +10.9 over CODA-Prompt) and fine-grained specialization (Cars-196: +15.4 over CCVR) settings. On CIFAR-100, LoRM (86.95) leads CCVR (79.95) and L2P (83.88) by 7 and 3 points respectively, while on ImageNet-R it surpasses CCVR (70.00) by 2.5 points. The closest competition is on ImageNet-A, where CCVR (39.50 at β=1.0) and LoRM (37.26) are comparable, with the paper noting they 'perform comparably on average.' FCIL-specific baselines TARGET and PILoRA are consistently outperformed, with PILoRA (the prior SOTA) trailing LoRM by 10+ points on CIFAR-100 and 36 points on EuroSAT. Model-merging baselines (RegMean, FisherAVG) and classical CL methods (EWC, LwF) show the largest gaps, confirming that closed-form LoRA merging with alternating optimization is substantially more effective than simple weight averaging or regularization-based forgetting mitigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning and RegMean for closed-form model merging. The key theoretical contribution is showing that directly applying RegMean's regression objective to LoRA's BA decomposition yields an indeterminate system with infinitely many solutions, and that an alternating optimization strategy (freezing one matrix while solving for the other) recovers unique closed-form solutions. In the FCIL setting, the paper departs from prior FCIL methods (PILoRA, TARGET) that rely on prototypes or GAN-based synthetic data generation, instead proposing a purely algebraic merging procedure that requires no replay buffers or auxiliary networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form merging approach offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc LoRA averaging (FedAvg) and to more complex FCIL-specific machinery, with the added benefits of privacy preservation (only Gram matrix diagonals are transmitted) and reduced communication (one matrix per round). The derivations extend naturally to other PEFT methods (VeRA, (IA)³), suggesting a general framework for closed-form merging of parameter-efficient modules. Open questions include whether the alternating strategy's benefits persist with more than two matrices to alternate over, how the method behaves under extreme client heterogeneity or very large numbers of tasks, and whether the FCIL-specific evaluation generalizes to broader multi-client, multi-task deployment scenarios.
