## Problem

Learning spatio-temporal scene graphs (STSGs) from video has been limited to fully supervised approaches that require labor-intensive, frame-level STSG annotations. The paper asks whether readily available video captions can instead provide sufficient weak supervision to train STSG generators, and how to bridge the gap between the sparsity and ambiguity of captions and the fine-grained structured output of an STSG.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251504/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes a video clip (uniformly sampled to ≤12 frames), pre-extracted object bounding boxes (top-10 largest objects per frame), and a natural language caption that is parsed by GPT-4 into a structured JSON of events with temporal locations and durations. At inference time, only the video and bounding boxes are needed. The output is a probabilistic relational database where each unary atom (e.g., deformed(3, e)) and binary atom (e.g., push(10, h, b)) carries a probability score, forming a distribution over STSGs rather than a single hard prediction.

## Method

LASER converts each video caption into a Spatio-Temporal Specification Language (STSL) formula via a 3-shot GPT-4 prompt, where STSL extends finite Linear Temporal Logic (LTLf) with relational predicates and existentially quantified variables to express both spatial relations and temporal operators (always, finally, next, until). A differentiable neuro-symbolic alignment checker, built on an extended Scallop engine, computes the probability Pr(r ⊨ ψ) that the predicted probabilistic STSG satisfies the specification by inductively aligning temporal slices and aggregating over top-k proofs, enabling end-to-end gradient flow. Training uses a composite loss combining (i) a contrastive term that cross-matches videos against other samples' specifications within a batch, (ii) a time-span supervision term that modulates atomic alignment scores by a triangular kernel σ(s, l, d) centered on the LLM-predicted event location and duration, and (iii) a semantic loss penalizing violations of first-order-logic integrity constraints (e.g., mutual exclusivity of predicates). The framework is model-agnostic, supporting both open-world vision-language backbones (CLIP, VIOLET, SigLIP) and closed-world MLP classifiers over fixed vocabularies.

## Results

On OpenPVSG, LASER-CLIP achieves a unary predicate prediction accuracy of 27.78% and a binary recall@5 of 0.42, surpassing the best fully supervised baseline (VPS-Conv) by 12.65% and 0.22 respectively. On 20BN, LASER-P (using ground-truth specifications) achieves an average F1 of 0.77 and 91% accuracy, while LASER (caption-only) reaches 0.73 F1 and 83% accuracy, both outperforming the GPA baseline (0.74 F1, 76% accuracy). On MUGEN, LASER achieves 59.6% action prediction accuracy at 5,000 training samples, exceeding the fully supervised baseline (54.4%) and VT-TWINS (55.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On OpenPVSG, LASER-CLIP (weakly supervised) outperforms all eight fully supervised baselines from Yang et al. (2023a) on unary accuracy (27.78% vs. best 15.13%) and binary R@5/R@10 (0.42/0.54 vs. best 0.20/0.23), trailing only VPS-Conv on binary R@1 (0.148 vs. 0.162). On MUGEN, LASER surpasses both VT-TWINS (Ko et al., 2022) and TempCLR (Yang et al., 2023b) at every data budget, achieving 59.6% vs. 55.6% and 34.9% respectively at 5,000 samples, and even exceeding the fully supervised baseline (54.4%). On 20BN, LASER-P outperforms GPA (Migimatsu & Bohg, 2022) on 71% of individual predicates with a higher average F1 (0.77 vs. 0.74) and accuracy (91% vs. 76%), while caption-only LASER still beats GPA on 59% of predicates. On downstream MUGEN retrieval, LASER-P outperforms the SDSC embedding baseline (Hayes et al., 2022) by 6.8% on video-to-text and 3.2% on text-to-video retrieval. On Action Genome, the fully supervised in-domain STTran (Cong et al., 2021) achieves higher binary recall (R@5: 0.78 vs. 0.53 for LLaVA-FT), but LASER strategies achieve substantially higher unary recall (R@1: 0.18 vs. 0.07), reflecting the trade-off between in-domain supervision and open-world generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Scallop neurosymbolic programming framework (Li et al., 2023) for differentiable probabilistic reasoning and extends Finite Linear Temporal Logic (LTLf, De Giacomo & Vardi, 2013) into a new specification language (STSL) that supports relational predicates and variables for video semantics. It departs from the fully-supervised STSG generation paradigm (Yang et al., 2023a; Nag et al., 2023) by introducing video captions as the sole supervisory signal, leveraging LLMs (GPT-4) to bridge natural language and formal logic. The approach is positioned as the first framework to train STSG generators with caption-based weak supervision, complementing rather than competing with existing STSG architectures like STTran and TRACE, which can serve as backbone models within LASER.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that caption-based weak supervision outperforms fully supervised baselines by substantial margins (12.65% on OpenPVSG unary accuracy, 7% on 20BN) challenges the prevailing assumption that fine-grained STSG annotations are necessary for high-fidelity video semantics extraction. This opens a path toward scaling STSG learning to the vast corpus of captioned video data without prohibitive annotation costs. Key limitations include scalability to long videos and many objects, dependence on caption quality, and the computational overhead of the neuro-symbolic alignment checker. Open questions include whether richer specifications can be elicited from LLMs beyond what captions provide, and whether the approach generalizes to longer temporal horizons.
