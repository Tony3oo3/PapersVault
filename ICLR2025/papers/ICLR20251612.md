## Problem

Neural-network-based PDE solvers (PINNs, neural operators) are highly sensitive to hyperparameters such as loss weights and collocation point placement, often yielding low-quality or degenerate solutions. This paper argues the issue is epistemological rather than methodological: these methods minimize expected values of aggregated losses, but obtaining a (weak) PDE solution actually requires solving a constrained learning problem with worst-case (distributionally robust) losses. The gap is that no prior framework unifies the correct problem formulation with a practical algorithm that also naturally incorporates structural invariances and partial observations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes PDE coefficients (e.g., diffusion rate, viscosity), a forcing function, boundary/initial condition functions, and optionally a set of prior solution observations, all defined over a spatial-temporal domain D = Ω × (0,T]. It produces a parameterized solution field u_θ(x,t) (or u_θ(π,τ)(x,t) for parametric families) evaluated at arbitrary points, plus dual variables λ that quantify the sensitivity of the BC fit to constraint relaxations, serving as per-sample or per-parameter reliability metrics. For neural operator architectures that cannot predict at arbitrary points, the sampling distributions are restricted to fixed equispaced grids.

## Method

SCL reformulates PDE solving as a constrained learning problem where the BC residual is the objective and the PDE residual, structural invariances, and observational constraints are worst-case (supremum over all square-integrable distributions) constraints. Proposition 3.1 proves this equivalence to the weak formulation via Sobolev embedding, while Proposition 4.1 shows the worst-case losses can be approximated arbitrarily well by expectations under distributions proportional to truncated losses ψ_α ∝ [ℓ(z)−α]_+. The resulting problem is solved with a primal-dual gradient descent-ascent algorithm (Alg. 1) that alternates between a θ-update (gradient descent on the empirical Lagrangian) and a λ-update (dual ascent on constraint violations), with samples from the implicit ψ_α distributions drawn via Metropolis-Hastings MCMC. Unlike PINN-style methods that fix loss weights and collocation points, SCL jointly adapts both during training, and the converged dual variables provide interpretable reliability metrics identifying which parameters or data points are hardest to fit.

## Results

On challenging PDEs, SCL dramatically outperforms baselines: for convection with β=50, SCL achieves 3.74±0.87% relative L2 error versus 56.5±20% for PINN and 29.0±33% for R3; for the eikonal equation, SCL reaches 9.95±1.9% versus 87.1±39% (PINN) and 85.5±27% (R3). In the supervised setting (Table 2), SCL(O) consistently beats the standard supervised NO baseline (PII) across Burgers' (0.0444% vs 0.0540%), Navier-Stokes (3.31% vs 4.29% at ν=10⁻³), and diffusion-sorption (0.218% vs 0.274%). For parametric families, SCL matches the error of PINN with 4–6× fewer PDE evaluations per epoch, and a single SCL model trained in 31.1 hours covers 10⁴ parameter combinations versus <440 achievable by FEM in the same time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PINN (Raissi et al., 2019), SCL wins decisively in all challenging settings (convection β=50: 3.74% vs 56.5%; eikonal: 9.95% vs 87.1%; reaction-diffusion (3,5): 0.762% vs 79.6%) but loses on the easy reaction-diffusion case (ν,ρ)=(3,3) where PINN achieves 0.745% vs SCL's 1.82%. Against R3 adaptive sampling (Daw et al., 2023), SCL wins on convection β=50 (3.74% vs 29.0%) and eikonal (9.95% vs 85.5%) but is slightly worse on reaction-diffusion (3,5) (0.762% vs 0.665%). Against the parametric PINN of Cho et al. (2024), SCL achieves comparable or better error with 4–6× fewer differential operator evaluations per epoch across convection, reaction-diffusion, and 2D Helmholtz. In the supervised setting, SCL(O) outperforms the standard FNO training objective (PII, Li et al., 2021) on all tested PDEs, with the largest gains on Navier-Stokes at ν=10⁻³ (3.31% vs 4.29%). Against FEM (DOLFINx, Baratta et al., 2023), SCL is not directly competitive on single-solution accuracy but is ~20× more efficient for parametric families, covering 10⁴ parameter combinations in one training run versus <440 FEM solves in the same wall-clock time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the constrained learning framework of Chamon & Ribeiro (2020) and Chamon et al. (2023), which established non-convex duality results for statistical optimization problems, and on the distributionally robust optimization connection to worst-case losses from Robey et al. (2021). It departs from the standard PINN/NO paradigm (Raissi et al., 2019; Li et al., 2021) by arguing that the core limitation of these methods is not methodological (how the problem is solved) but epistemological (which problem is being solved): minimizing expected aggregated losses with fixed weights is fundamentally the wrong objective. The paper also subsumes and explains prior heuristics—adaptive sampling (Daw et al., 2023), causal sweeping (Krishnapriyan et al., 2021), and loss-weight adaptation (Wang et al., 2021a)—as incomplete approximations of the correct worst-case constrained formulation, showing that causal behavior emerges naturally from solving the right problem rather than needing to be explicitly imposed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical equivalence between PDE weak solutions and constrained learning with worst-case losses (Prop. 3.1) provides a principled foundation that eliminates the need for ad hoc hyperparameter tuning of loss weights and collocation points, while the dual variables offer interpretable reliability metrics for identifying hard-to-fit parameters or data. Key limitations include a smoothness restriction (d ≤ 4k'−1) that may constrain applicability to high-dimensional or high-order PDEs, a ~5× computational overhead for single BVPs due to MCMC sampling, and the inability to handle infinite-dimensional functional parameters (e.g., forcing functions τ) in the worst-case formulation. Open questions include extending the framework to functional parameters, exploring faster sampling methods (Langevin MCMC, discontinuous HMC), and applying SCL to active learning for data-efficient PDE solution.
