## Problem

End-to-end transformer-based MOT methods have not surpassed tracking-by-detection pipelines, largely because the standard Tracking Aware Label Assignment (TALA) creates a severe training imbalance: detection queries receive very few positive samples (newborns) while tracking queries dominate, especially in enclosed scenes where most objects appear only in early frames. This imbalance causes e2e-MOT to underperform in detection and leads to premature tracking termination without re-initialization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251920/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes consecutive RGB video frames (5-frame segments sampled at 10-frame intervals during training) and outputs bounding boxes with tracking identity associations. Each of the N_T + N_D base queries is expanded into a set of N_S shadow queries (N_S=3 by default), so the network predicts (N_T + N_D) × N_S boxes per frame, with all shadows in a set jointly predicting the same target. The architecture uses a ResNet50 backbone with a deformable encoder and 6-layer deformable decoder, employing 60 initial queries for DanceTrack/BDD100K and 300 for MOT17.

## Method

CO-MOT introduces two training-time mechanisms on top of the MOTR architecture. First, COLA (COopetition Label Assignment) reassigns previously tracked objects to detection queries in all intermediate decoders (l < L), while the final decoder retains the standard competitive TALA to avoid trajectory redundancy at inference; this lets detection queries carrying the same identity augment tracking queries through self-attention, improving association under appearance variance. Second, a shadow set strategy replaces one-to-one Hungarian matching with a one-to-set scheme: each query is duplicated into N_S shadow counterparts initialized with minimal Gaussian noise, and during training the most challenging shadow (max cost) is selected as the representative for matching, with all shadows in the set sharing the assignment; at inference, the highest-confidence shadow within each set is emitted as the tracking output. Together these changes require no extra inference cost and achieve 69.4% HOTA on DanceTrack with only 38% of MOTRv2's FLOPs.

## Results

On the DanceTrack test set, CO-MOT achieves 65.3% HOTA without additional training data, and CO-MOT+ (with CrowdHuman joint training) reaches 69.4% HOTA, surpassing MOTRv3+ (68.3%) by 1.1% while matching MOTRv2+ (69.9%) which requires an extra YOLOX detector. On MOT17, CO-MOT achieves 60.1% HOTA (best among e2e methods), and on BDD100K it reaches 52.8% TETA with 56.2% AssocA. CO-MOT requires only 38% FLOPs of MOTRv2 (173G vs ~455G) with 1.4× faster inference while maintaining comparable HOTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CO-MOT outperforms all prior end-to-end methods on DanceTrack: it surpasses MOTR by 11.1% HOTA (65.3 vs 54.2), MeMOTR by 1.9% (65.3 vs 63.4), and DiffMOT by 3.0% (65.3 vs 62.3). With CrowdHuman joint training, CO-MOT+ (69.4% HOTA) edges out MOTRv3+ (68.3%) by 1.1% and nearly matches MOTRv2+ (69.9%) which relies on an external YOLOX detector, while using only 38% of its FLOPs. On MOT17, CO-MOT achieves the best e2e HOTA (60.1%) over MOTR (57.8%) and MeMOTR (58.8%), though it trails non-e2e methods like QDTrack (63.5%) due to MOT17's small training set and prevalence of tiny objects. On BDD100K, CO-MOT leads all e2e methods with 52.8% TETA (vs MOTR's 50.7%) and achieves superior association accuracy (56.2% AssocA vs MOTRv2's 51.9%), though it slightly trails MOTRv2 on overall TETA (52.8 vs 54.9). On MOT20, CO-MOT sets the e2e state-of-the-art at 57.5% HOTA, outperforming TrackFormer (54.7%) and MeMOT (54.1%).

## Relation to Prior Work

CO-MOT builds directly on MOTR's end-to-end transformer architecture with separate tracking and detection queries, but fundamentally rethinks the Training-Aware Label Assignment (TALA) that governs how ground truth is distributed between query types. Where TALA enforces strict exclusivity (tracked objects → tracking queries, newborns → detection queries), COLA relaxes this in intermediate decoders, allowing detection queries to also attend to tracked objects and thereby augment tracking representations via self-attention. The shadow set mechanism draws conceptual inspiration from one-to-many assignment in detection (Co-DETR, HDETR, Group-DETR) but departs by using a one-to-set formulation where shadows are perturbations of a single query rather than independent auxiliary queries, enabling discriminative training on the hardest shadow without the overhead of full one-to-many matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnosis of why e2e-MOT has lagged behind tracking-by-detection pipelines—training queries actively suppress detection capability—and offers a lightweight, plug-and-play fix (COLA + shadow sets) that requires no extra detector or architectural change. The 38% FLOPs reduction relative to MOTRv2 at comparable HOTA is practically significant for deployment, making e2e-MOT more competitive without the YOLOX overhead. However, the approach remains data-hungry (limited gains on MOT17's ~5K training frames) and struggles with small objects, leaving open whether stronger backbones (e.g., DINO) or larger training sets are needed to close the gap with non-e2e methods on dense, small-target benchmarks.
