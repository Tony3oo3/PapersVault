## Problem

Despite the empirical success of low-rank adaptation methods like LoRA and GaLore for LLM training, the theoretical reasons behind their differing convergence rates and final performance remain unexplained. Existing analyses either rely on convexity assumptions that fail for neural networks or focus solely on expressivity, leaving the optimization landscape differences between full-rank, LoRA, and GaLore unaccounted for.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252835/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes frozen pre-trained weight matrices (e.g., LLaMA or RoBERTa) and a training corpus (C4 for pre-training, GLUE tasks for fine-tuning), operating on per-layer gradient matrices of shape p×q. It produces updated full-rank weight matrices by projecting gradients into an r-dimensional subspace (r ≪ min(p,q)) via a random Gaussian matrix R ~ N(0, 1/m), performing the optimizer step in that subspace, and projecting back. Unlike GaLore, no SVD is computed and no projection matrix is stored across iterations; the random matrix is regenerated every T steps (subspace update frequency).

## Method

The paper provides the first unified optimization landscape analysis of full-rank, LoRA, and GaLore training using the PL* condition (a Polyak-Lojasiewicz variant) and spurious local minima theory. It shows that LoRA's reduced parameter count shrinks the PL*-satisfying region (requiring rank r = Õ(mnR^{12l+2}/(λ₀−μ)²) versus width w = Õ(mnR^{6l+2}/(λ₀−μ)²) for full-rank/GaLore), while GaLore avoids spurious local minima under the mild condition w ≥ 2r (versus w ≥ 2m(n+1)^l for full-rank). Building on this, GaRare replaces GaLore's SVD-derived projection matrix with a random Gaussian matrix, provably preserving both the PL* region and the no-spurious-minima guarantee (Corollaries 5.2 and 5.3) while eliminating SVD computation and reducing optimizer-state memory from pr+2qr to 2qr per layer.

## Results

On LLaMA pre-training (C4), GaRare achieves validation perplexity of 34.45/25.34/19.35/15.88 on 60M/130M/350M/1B models, matching GaLore (34.88/25.36/18.95/15.64) and full-rank (34.06/25.08/18.80/15.56) while using less memory (e.g., 3.98G vs 4.23G on 1B). On GLUE fine-tuning, GaRare achieves 85.9 average on RoBERTa-Base and 89.2 on RoBERTa-Large, comparable to GaLore (85.9/89.3) while using the smallest memory footprint (252M/718M). On the 7B LLaMA model, GaRare reaches 19.94 perplexity with 16G memory, matching GaLore's 19.93 at 18G memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaRare consistently outperforms LoRA on both pre-training and fine-tuning: on 1B LLaMA pre-training, GaRare achieves 15.88 perplexity versus LoRA's 19.21, and on RoBERTa-Large GLUE, GaRare scores 89.2 versus LoRA's 89.0. Against GaLore, GaRare matches performance (15.88 vs 15.64 on 1B pre-training; 89.2 vs 89.3 on RoBERTa-Large GLUE) while reducing memory by ~6% (3.98G vs 4.23G) and eliminating SVD computation. Compared to FLoRA, which uses the same memory footprint as GaRare, GaRare substantially outperforms it (15.88 vs 20.22 perplexity on 1B pre-training; 89.2 vs 87.8 on RoBERTa-Large GLUE), confirming that the random projection distribution N(0,1/p) is critical for preserving GaLore's optimization landscape. On the 7B model, GaRare matches GaLore (19.94 vs 19.93 perplexity) while using 16G vs 18G memory, and both outperform full-rank Adam (20.05) within 30K steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GaLore (Zhao et al., 2024), which projects gradient matrices into low-rank spaces via SVD, and positions GaRare as a drop-in replacement that swaps SVD-derived projection matrices for Gaussian random matrices. The theoretical analysis draws on Liu et al. (2022) for the PL* condition framework and Lederer (2020) for spurious local minima characterizations, applying both to compare LoRA, GaLore, and full-rank training for the first time in a unified setting. The paper explicitly distinguishes GaRare from FLoRA (Hao et al., 2024), noting that FLoRA's use of N(0,1/pr) random matrices violates the conditions needed to preserve GaLore's optimization landscape, making the two methods theoretically distinct despite superficial similarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical explanation for why GaLore converges faster than both LoRA and full-rank training, attributing the advantage to a larger PL* region and fewer spurious local minima rather than to expressivity alone. This reframing gives practitioners a principled basis for choosing among adaptation methods and explains the observed trade-off where excessively large ranks degrade GaLore's landscape quality. However, the theoretical analysis is confined to MLP architectures, leaving open whether the PL* and spurious-minima results transfer to Transformer-based LLMs, and the practical gain of GaRare over GaLore is modest (~10% memory reduction) with comparable perplexity, limiting its immediate impact.
