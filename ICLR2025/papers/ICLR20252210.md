## Problem

In safe offline RL, existing methods struggle to simultaneously satisfy safety constraints and maximize reward: hard-constraint approaches like FISOR produce overly conservative policies, while methods like CDT and CPQ frequently violate cost thresholds, especially under tight constraints. The core difficulty is that enforcing safety in the action space is brittle—overly restrictive constraints suppress reward optimization, while overly relaxed ones admit out-of-distribution actions that breach safety. This paper asks whether safety constraints can be reformulated in a learned latent space to make them both tractable and composable with reward maximization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (state, action, reward, cost, next-state) tuples collected from mixed safe and unsafe trajectories, requiring only that some safe samples exist (Assumption 6). It outputs two deployable policies: LSPC-S, a conservative policy that samples latent codes from a tanh-squashed prior restricted to (−ε, ε) and decodes them into actions, and LSPC-O, which additionally learns a state-conditioned latent encoder to select reward-maximizing codes within the same restricted region. The latent safety space is 32-dimensional, and the restriction hyperparameter ε (default 0.25) controls the trade-off between safety strictness and reward performance.

## Method

LSPC first trains a Conditional VAE whose encoder maps (state, action) pairs to a 32-dim latent code and whose decoder reconstructs actions, using cost-advantage-weighted regression so that the learned prior N(0,1) implicitly encodes the distribution of safe actions from the dataset. IQL-style critics (reward Q/V and cost Q/V) are trained with expectile regression to provide advantage signals without querying OOD actions. LSPC-S is obtained by sampling z from the tanh-squashed prior and decoding; LSPC-O adds a second encoder μ_δ(z|s) trained via reward-advantage weighted regression in the latent space, with the CVAE decoder frozen but differentiable, so the encoder learns which safe latent codes yield the highest Q_r. The key architectural insight is that safety is enforced geometrically—by restricting the latent sampling region—rather than through explicit Lagrangian penalties or hard action-space constraints, which decouples the safety and reward objectives and yields tighter theoretical performance and constraint-violation bounds (Theorems 1–2) that decay as O(N^{−0.25−v}) with dataset size.

## Results

On the DSRL benchmark, LSPC-O achieves the highest average normalized reward return across all three task suites while maintaining safety (normalized cost < 1): 0.72 on Metadrive, 0.40 on Safety Gym, and 0.54 on Bullet Safety Gym. The conservative variant LSPC-S achieves 0.67, 0.32, and 0.32 respectively with even lower costs (0.17, 0.32, 0.04). Notably, LSPC-O surpasses the next-best safe method (FISOR: 0.36, 0.30, 0.39) by 0.36, 0.10, and 0.15 in average reward on Metadrive, Safety Gym, and Bullet Safety Gym respectively, while keeping cost returns well below the safety threshold of 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSPC-O outperforms all four baselines on average reward while remaining safe (cost < 1) across all three benchmark suites. Against FISOR (the strongest safe baseline), LSPC-O gains +0.36 reward on Metadrive (0.72 vs 0.36), +0.10 on Safety Gym (0.40 vs 0.30), and +0.15 on Bullet Safety Gym (0.54 vs 0.39), while still keeping costs below 1. CPQ is extremely conservative with near-zero or negative reward (e.g., -0.06 on Metadrive) and violates safety on Safety Gym (cost 3.48) and Bullet Safety Gym (cost 1.12). CDT achieves competitive reward on Safety Gym (0.55) but frequently violates the cost threshold (cost 0.85 average, with individual tasks reaching 12.51 on CarButton2). BC-Safe is moderate on both metrics but lags LSPC-O by 0.36–0.54 in reward on Metadrive and Bullet Safety Gym while offering no theoretical safety guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of safe RL (CMDP formulation) and offline RL, building directly on IQL's expectile-regression critics and AWR policy extraction, combined with a CVAE-based generative policy architecture inherited from BCQ. It departs from prior safe offline RL methods (BC-Safe, CDT, CPQ, FISOR) by replacing explicit constraint mechanisms—Lagrangian penalties, hard feasibility regions, or data filtering—with a learned latent safety constraint space where the CVAE encodes safe state-action pairs and a separate encoder optimizes rewards within that restricted space. Theoretically, it tightens performance and constraint-violation bounds by introducing the CVAE-induced safe policy π_s as an intermediate between the learned policy and the optimal policy, yielding sharper guarantees than prior analyses that rely on the unknown behavior policy π_b.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, tunable knob (the restriction hyperparameter ε) to trade off safety conservativeness against reward performance without retraining, which is valuable for deployment in safety-critical settings like autonomous driving. The sample complexity result showing the performance gap and constraint violation decay to zero with sufficient data (unlike prior bounds that remain nonzero due to state-distribution discrepancy) provides a stronger theoretical foundation for offline safe RL. Key open questions include deriving a principled relationship between ε and the achievable cost bound, extending to multi-agent or adversarial settings, and validating sim-to-real transfer of the learned policies.
