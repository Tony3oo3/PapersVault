## Problem

LLMs exhibit epistemological inaccuracies on climate science question-answering tasks, yet no existing benchmark combines domain-expert validation with automated generation, multiple QA task forms, and varying reasoning complexity for climate science. Prior scientific benchmarks either rely entirely on manual curation or produce unvalidated synthetic questions prone to hallucination, leaving a gap in rigorous, scalable evaluation of climate LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250910/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes ~2000-character context chunks extracted from 18 graduate-level climate science textbooks (stored in a vector database) and retrieves additional chunks via cosine similarity to augment generation. It produces QA pairs in three task forms (MCQ with four options, freeform with concise 2-sentence answers, and cloze with a single masked scientific term) across three complexity levels (base factual recall, multi-step reasoning, and hypothetical scenario application). Evaluation outputs include MCQ accuracy, freeform BLEU/BERTScore/Factual Accuracy (sigmoid-smoothed logit ratio from a GPT-4o-mini entailment classifier), and cloze Exact Match/Phrase Similarity (rescaled cosine similarity of Universal Sentence Encoder embeddings over a 4-token context window).

## Method

ClimaGen is an adaptive learning framework that iteratively generates and validates scientific QA pairs: GPT-3.5-turbo produces base-level questions from seed textbook chunks, then evolves them into reasoning and hypothetical variants; domain experts review and annotate via an interactive web application, yielding the 566-question ClimaQA-Gold dataset. To scale beyond manual annotation, an evaluator model (GPT-4o-mini) is fine-tuned as a binary valid/invalid classifier on expert-labeled examples using uncertainty-based active sampling (samples with classifier confidence above 0.85 are dropped with 50% probability), enabling fully automated production of the 3000-question ClimaQA-Silver dataset. A separate fine-tuned model automates cloze term selection. For evaluation, the paper compares default, few-shot, RAG (from source vs. held-out textbooks), continued pre-training on 13 graduate textbooks via LoRA, and task-specific fine-tuning on ClimaQA-Silver across six LLM families, finding that RAG from source textbooks consistently outperforms continued pre-training and fine-tuning, while most models struggle disproportionately with reasoning-level MCQs.

## Results

On ClimaQA-Gold MCQs, GPT-4o with RAG from source textbooks achieves the highest overall accuracy of 92.79%, compared to 85.71% in its default setting. For Cloze QA, GPT-4o-RAG-source reaches 71.88% Exact Match and 0.94 Phrase Similarity, while LLaMA3.1-8B-RAG-source achieves the best Cloze EM at 72.50%. Across all models, RAG from source textbooks consistently outperforms held-out retrieval (e.g., Mistral-7B jumps from 67.76% to 88.98% MCQ accuracy), and most models show a notable drop on reasoning-level MCQs relative to base-level questions (e.g., GPT-4o: 86.11% vs. 86.77%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o dominates across all tasks and settings, achieving 92.79% overall MCQ accuracy with RAG-source, well ahead of LLaMA3-70b (88.98%) and Mistral-7B (88.98%) under the same setting. RAG from source textbooks provides the largest single improvement for every model tested: Mistral-7B gains +21.22 MCQ points (67.76→88.98) and +40.00 Cloze EM points (17.50→57.50), while GPT-3.5-turbo gains +6.67 MCQ points and +25.63 Cloze EM points. In contrast, RAG from held-out textbooks often degrades performance (e.g., Mixtral-8x22b drops from 80.00% to 77.14% MCQ; GPT-3.5-turbo drops from 73.06% to 70.61%), confirming that retrieval relevance is critical. Continued pre-training on held-out textbooks yields modest gains (LLaMA3.1-8B: 70.61→75.92% MCQ) and fine-tuning on ClimaQA-Silver provides similar or slightly larger improvements (LLaMA3.1-8B: 70.61→73.06% MCQ, but Cloze EM jumps from 26.25→51.25), yet both remain well below RAG-source. Few-shot prompting produces only marginal changes (±1–3 points) across all models. Gemini-1.5-flash with full-book long-context retrieval shows only a small improvement over its no-context baseline (80.82→82.86% MCQ), suggesting that even 1M-token context windows do not fully substitute for targeted retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two lines of work: automated scientific benchmark creation and active-learning-based LLM annotation. It builds directly on LLMAAA (Zhang et al., 2023) and STAR (Zhang et al., 2024) for uncertainty-based active sampling in the evaluator model fine-tuning, adapting their framework to the domain of scientific QA validation. Conceptually, it departs from prior scientific benchmarks (ScienceQA, Pira2, SciQA, Climate Crisis QA, SciQAG-24D) by combining LLM-driven generation with domain-expert validation in a single adaptive pipeline (ClimaGen), rather than relying exclusively on either manual curation or unvalidated synthetic generation. The multi-task (MCQ, freeform, cloze) and multi-level (base, reasoning, hypothetical) design extends the single-format, single-difficulty structure of most prior scientific QA benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ClimaQA provides the first climate-science-specific benchmark that simultaneously offers expert-validated rigor (566 questions) and synthetic scale (3000 questions) across three task forms and three complexity levels, filling a gap identified in the literature. The practical finding that RAG from source textbooks substantially outperforms both continued pre-training and supervised fine-tuning on small models offers actionable guidance for domain adaptation in scientific settings. Key limitations include the narrow corpus (only 5 textbooks for question generation, with a particular focus on aerosols), the small gold dataset constraining evaluator model quality, and the reliance on GPT-4o-mini for the factual-accuracy metric, which introduces a circularity concern. The ClimaGen framework's generalizability to other scientific domains is promising but untested, and scaling expert validation remains an open bottleneck for broader adoption.
