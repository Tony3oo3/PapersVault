## Problem

Existing diffusion-GAN hybrid methods for one-step generation face a fundamental trade-off: performing adversarial divergence directly against real data causes training instability and mode collapse, while smoothing via noise injection reduces the effectiveness of one-step learning. No prior method achieves both stable training and high-fidelity one-step generation simultaneously, particularly when scaling to large-scale text-to-image tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253212/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (encoded via a text encoder) and a single sample of Gaussian noise as input, producing one RGB image in a single forward pass (NFE=1). Training operates in the VAE latent space of pre-trained Stable Diffusion at 512×512 resolution, with the ability to zero-shot adapt to 1024×1024 via a linear weight-combination formula without additional training. The method supports both full fine-tuning (~10 A800 days) and LoRA fine-tuning (<10% parameters) of pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models such as PixArt-α and SD 1.5.

## Method

YOSO's core insight is to smooth the adversarial divergence by using the denoising generator itself rather than real data or noise-injected data. It constructs a cooperative adversarial loss where one-step denoising from less-corrupted samples (timestep t_k) serves as the 'ground truth' distribution and one-step denoising from more-corrupted samples (timestep t) serves as the 'student' distribution, so the generator learns from its own improved outputs in a self-cooperative manner. This is combined with a KL/consistency loss for point-level matching and a decoupled scheduler that uses different timestep skips (k=250 for adversarial, m=25 for consistency) to maximize each loss's efficacy. For text-to-image scaling, the method introduces a latent perceptual loss computed via the bottleneck layer of a pre-trained SD UNet, a latent discriminator built from the same pre-trained network, Informative Prior Initialization to correct non-zero terminal SNR, and a quick adaption stage (~1k iterations) to switch from ε-prediction to v-prediction with zero terminal SNR before the main training begins.

## Results

On text-to-image generation (Table 2), YOSO-LoRA on SD 1.5 achieves HPS 28.33, AeS 5.97, ImageReward 0.43, and CLIP Score 31.33 in one step, outperforming all competing one-step methods. On PixArt-α backbone, YOSO achieves HPS 30.52, AeS 6.19, IR 1.02, and CS 32.95 in one step. On CIFAR-10, YOSO achieves FID 1.81 (fine-tuning EDM) and 2.26 (from scratch), both with 1 NFE. On ImageNet-64 from scratch, YOSO reaches FID 2.65 with 1 NFE, and on FFHQ-1024 it achieves FID 16.23, outperforming other diffusion-GAN hybrids.

## Baselines & Comparisons

YOSO-LoRA on SD 1.5 (1 step) outperforms SD-Turbo (HPS 28.33 vs 27.06), InstaFlow (28.33 vs 26.18), and Hyper-SD-LoRA with HFL (28.33 vs 28.01) across all four metrics without using human feedback learning. At 4 steps, YOSO-LoRA beats Hyper-SD-LoRA (HPS 30.50 vs 30.24, AeS 6.05 vs 5.55) and substantially outperforms LCM-MoRA, PeRFlow, and TCD-LoRA (HPS 30.50 vs 22.77/22.43/22.24). On PixArt-α, YOSO (1 step) surpasses DMD (HPS 30.52 vs 29.78, AeS 6.19 vs 6.02) while approaching the 25-step base model. On CIFAR-10, YOSO from scratch (FID 2.26) beats DDGANs (3.75), iCT (2.83), and StyleGAN2 (2.92), while fine-tuned YOSO (FID 1.81) outperforms all one-step distillation methods including SiD (1.92) and CTM (1.98). On ImageNet-64, YOSO (FID 2.65) beats iCT (3.25) and CT (13.0) but trails StyleGAN-XL (1.52). On FFHQ-1024, YOSO (FID 16.23) outperforms UFOGen (24.37), Diffusion-GAN (22.16), and Diffusion2GAN (18.01), and achieves zero-shot COCO FID-30k of 8.90, beating GigaGAN (9.09) and all other one-step methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

YOSO departs from prior diffusion-GAN hybrids (DDGANs, UFOGen, SD-Turbo) that either perform adversarial divergence directly against real data (causing instability and mode collapse) or against corrupted data (indirectly learning one-step generation). Instead, it draws on the cooperative learning paradigm (Xie et al., 2018) to smooth the adversarial divergence by using the generator's own output on less-corrupted samples as the 'ground truth,' creating a self-cooperative objective that is both stable and directly aligned with one-step generation. The approach is also connected to Consistency Training (Song et al., 2023) but operates at the marginal distribution level rather than point-to-point, avoiding ODE approximation error. For text-to-image, the paper introduces practical techniques (latent perceptual loss, latent discriminator, IPI, quick adaption to v-prediction) that enable efficient LoRA fine-tuning of pre-trained models like PixArt-α and SD 1.5.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

YOSO provides a practical, low-resource pathway (~10 A800 days) to convert existing pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models into one-step generators via LoRA, while remaining compatible with downstream tools like ControlNet and image editing. The finding that fine-tuning (targeting the data distribution) can outperform the multi-step teacher (EDM FID 1.81 vs 2.04) suggests that one-step models need not be strictly bounded by their initialization. Open questions include whether the self-cooperative formulation scales to video generation, whether the zero-shot resolution transfer generalizes beyond the single PixArt-α experiment, and the acknowledged limitations in fairness, fine detail, and quantitative object control.
