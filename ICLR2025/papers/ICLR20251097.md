## Problem

Large-scale image-to-video diffusion models like Stable Video Diffusion are trained to generate videos moving forward in time from a single conditioning image, making them unsuitable for keyframe interpolation—producing coherent motion between two distant input frames. Training a dedicated two-frame-conditioned model from scratch demands prohibitive data and compute, and naively reversing a forward-generated video fails because real-world motion is inherently asymmetric.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251097/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB keyframe images (start and end) that are at least 25 frames apart in the source video, at 1080p resolution, and produces an interpolated video at 1024×576 with 50 diffusion sampling steps. The generated video is constrained to begin exactly at the first keyframe and end at the second, with the intermediate frames exhibiting a single coherent motion direction rather than oscillating back and forth.

## Method

The key insight is that rotating the temporal self-attention maps by 180° (flipping both axes) reverses the learned motion-time association, converting forward motion statistics into backward motion. A lightweight fine-tuning stage then adapts only the value and output projection matrices (W_v, W_o) in the temporal self-attention layers—less than 2% of U-Net parameters—using just 100 synthetic SVD-generated videos, with the rotated attention maps fed as additional input to guide the backward-motion network. At inference, a dual-directional sampling loop runs the pretrained forward model conditioned on I_0 and the fine-tuned backward model conditioned on I_{N-1} (with its input latent flipped and attention maps rotated) in parallel at each denoising step, then fuses the two noise predictions by averaging before updating the latent. A per-step recurrence (re-injecting noise and re-denoising 5 times per step) further stabilizes the fusion, and the shared rotated attention maps enforce forward-backward motion consistency so both paths converge toward each other rather than oscillating.

## Results

On the Pexels dataset, the proposed method achieves FID 22.99 and FVD 306.84, outperforming all baselines. On the Davis dataset, it achieves FID 32.68 and FVD 424.69, again the best among all compared methods. The method uses only 100 synthetic training videos and fine-tunes fewer than 2% of the SVD U-Net parameters (only W_v and W_o in temporal self-attention layers), yet generates 1024×576 resolution videos with coherent forward-backward motion between distant keyframes (≥25 frames apart).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against FILM (the state-of-the-art flow-based frame interpolation method), the gains are most dramatic on Davis: FVD drops from 1048.65 to 424.69 (a 60% reduction), while on Pexels FID improves from 25.16 to 22.99 and FVD from 371.83 to 306.84. Against TRF (which also adapts SVD for bounded generation but fuses a forward video with a reversed forward video), the method wins on all four metrics, with the largest gap on Pexels FVD (306.84 vs. 563.16); qualitatively, TRF produces back-and-forth oscillating motion while the proposed method generates consistent unidirectional motion. Against DynamiCrafter (a large-scale model specifically trained for two-frame conditioning at 512×320 resolution), the method achieves better FID and FVD on both datasets despite operating at higher resolution (1024×576) and requiring only 100 training videos versus DynamiCrafter's full training pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD), adapting its pretrained 3D U-Net and temporal self-attention architecture for keyframe interpolation rather than training a new model from scratch. It departs from prior diffusion-based inbetweening work (MCVD, SparseCtrl, DynamiCrafter) that trains dedicated two-frame-conditioned models, and from TRF which naively fuses forward and reversed forward generations from a single model. The key conceptual contribution is the 180-degree rotation of temporal self-attention maps to invert the motion-time association, combined with lightweight fine-tuning of only the value and output projection matrices (W_v, W_o) to enable coherent backward motion generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method demonstrates that keyframe interpolation for distant frames (≥1s apart) can be achieved by adapting a pretrained image-to-video model with fewer than 2% of its parameters and only 100 training videos, making generative inbetweening accessible without large-scale training. The forward-backward motion consistency principle provides a principled framework for merging dual-directional diffusion paths that avoids the oscillation artifacts of naive fusion. However, the approach remains bounded by SVD's motion priors, struggling with non-rigid articulated motion and sparse-correspondence scenes, and its generalizability to stronger base models (e.g., Sora) or to incorporating physical motion heuristics remains an open question.
