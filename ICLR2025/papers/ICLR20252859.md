## Problem

Combining prior matching with geometric preservation in VAE-based disentanglement creates an inherent trade-off: a map that perfectly aligns the data distribution with the prior while fully preserving geometric features (distances or angles) generally does not exist. Existing distortion-based regularizers (e.g., DST) over-penalize when full geometry preservation is infeasible, forcing the model to sacrifice prior-matching quality and degrading learned representations. The paper asks how to regularize geometry preservation without this over-penalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled 64×64 RGB images from four synthetic/semi-synthetic datasets (Shapes3D, DSprites, SmallNORB, Cars3D) in batches of n=64. It produces disentangled latent codes (Gaussian parameters from the encoder) and reconstructed images (from the decoder). The GMG regularizer is computed on each batch using intra-domain cost matrices (L2², ScL2², or Cosine similarity) of size n×n, with the entropic GW sub-problem solved in roughly 3 ms per batch on a single GPU.

## Method

The core contribution is the Gromov-Monge Gap (GMG), defined as GMG_r(T) = DST_r(T) − GW(r, T♯r), which subtracts the minimal achievable distortion (the Gromov-Wasserstein distance between the reference and its pushforward) from the actual distortion incurred by the map T. This 'debiasing' ensures the regularizer is zero whenever T is already a Gromov-Monge map (i.e., it moves the reference with minimal distortion), and only penalizes the excess distortion beyond what is geometrically unavoidable. In the VAE setting, the GMG is applied to the decoder d_θ (with reference r = e_φ♯p_data) or encoder e_φ (with reference r = p_data), added as a weighted term λ·GMG alongside the standard reconstruction and β-weighted KL losses. The GMG is computed efficiently via an entropic GW solver (Sinkhorn iterations) with O(n²(d_X+d_Y)) complexity for the costs of interest, and the paper proves it is weakly convex for both L2² and inner-product costs, with explicitly characterized convexity constants.

## Results

On Shapes3D, the best configuration (β-TCVAE+HFS with decoder GMG using Cos costs) achieves 96.9±4.9 DCI-D, improving over the base β-TCVAE+HFS (90.2±7.5) by ~6.7 points. Across all four base architectures, GMG with Cos costs on the decoder consistently yields the highest DCI-D scores (e.g., 82.1±4.5 for β-VAE vs. 65.8±15.6 base). On Cars3D, GMG (Dec, Cos) lifts β-TCVAE from 32.3±4.6 to 36.4±5.7 DCI-D. The GMG also demonstrates greater gradient stability than the DST, with higher cosine similarity across randomly sampled batches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The GMG with Cos costs on the decoder outperforms all baselines across every base architecture on Shapes3D: it beats the plain β-VAE base by +16.3 DCI-D (82.1 vs. 65.8), the β-TCVAE base by +8.7 (83.7 vs. 75.0), and the strongest prior-matching baseline β-TCVAE+HFS by +6.7 (96.9 vs. 90.2). Against the Jacobian-based regularization of Lee et al. (2022), GMG (Dec, Cos) achieves 96.9 vs. 91.5 DCI-D on β-TCVAE+HFS, a 5.4-point gain. The GMG also consistently surpasses the DST regularizer of Nakagawa et al. (2023) across all cost types and both encoder/decoder placements (e.g., 96.9 vs. 91.9 for Cos on β-TCVAE+HFS). On Cars3D, GMG (Dec, Cos) improves β-TCVAE from 32.3 to 36.4 DCI-D, while DST actually degrades performance (26.8). The paper also shows that angle preservation (Cos) is a weaker and more effective constraint than (scaled) distance preservation, and that regularizing the decoder yields stronger gradients than regularizing the encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Monge Gap regularizer (Uscidda and Cuturi, 2023) from linear optimal transport to the quadratic Gromov-Monge setting, replacing displacement cost with geometric distortion. It directly addresses a limitation of the distortion-based (DST) regularizer used in Gromov-Wasserstein Autoencoders (Nakagawa et al., 2023) and Jacobian-based isometric regularizers (Lee et al., 2022): when perfect geometry preservation is infeasible, DST over-penalizes and conflicts with prior matching. The GMG resolves this by subtracting the minimal achievable distortion (the Gromov-Wasserstein distance), yielding a 'debiased distortion' that is zero precisely when the map is Gromov-Monge optimal. The framework is integrated into the standard β-VAE/β-TCVAE training loop, building on the geometric disentanglement line initiated by Gropp et al. (2020) and Horan et al. (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GMG provides a principled, theoretically grounded way to combine prior matching with geometric constraints in VAEs without the manual trade-off tuning that DST-based methods require, since both terms can simultaneously reach zero. The weak convexity analysis (Thm. 3.4) offers practical guidance: angle preservation is provably easier to optimize than distance preservation, and the reference distribution should be kept as small as possible. However, the empirical evaluation is limited to 64×64 synthetic and semi-synthetic datasets (Shapes3D, DSprites, SmallNORB, Cars3D) with a single metric (DCI-D) and batch size 64, leaving open whether the GMG's advantages persist on larger, more realistic datasets or with different disentanglement metrics. The finding that the finite-sample GMG becomes more convex in high dimension (d > n) is counterintuitive and suggests the method may not benefit from larger batch sizes, a departure from standard statistical OT convergence theory.
