## Problem

Large Transformer-based models for multivariate time-series forecasting achieve strong benchmark performance but require expensive domain-specific fine-tuning because MTS data is heterogeneous and largely non-public (unlike text or images). The paper asks whether a lightweight, purpose-built architecture—rather than a generalist backbone—can match or surpass much larger models on standard forecasting benchmarks with minimal parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250558/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a C×L multivariate time-series matrix (C channels, L=96 lookback timesteps) and produces a C×H forecast matrix. The internal pseudo-length L′ is dataset-dependent (32 for low-dimensional sets like ETT, 256 for high-dimensional sets like Traffic or PEMS), and the forecast horizon H ranges over {96,192,336,720} for long-term tasks and {12,24,48,96} for short-term PEMS tasks. The entire pipeline is a single-layer (occasionally two-layer) model trained end-to-end with MSE (or MAE for PEMS) loss.

## Method

SimpleTM is a three-module pipeline: (1) a learnable Stationary Wavelet Transform (SWT) tokenizes each channel into multi-scale time-frequency tokens while preserving temporal length (no downsampling), with filters initialized from standard wavelets (Haar, db4, bior3.1, etc.) and made trainable; (2) a geometric-product attention mechanism replaces standard dot-product self-attention by computing, for each token pair, both the scalar dot product (similarity) and the wedge product in G₂ (oriented area / linear independence), then combining them via a reduction function ζ(·) applied to a second value projection; (3) a learnable inverse SWT reconstructs the time-domain signal, followed by a feed-forward network and layer normalization to produce the forecast. The key design insight is that the wedge product explicitly encodes inter-channel complementarity (e.g., two tokens spanning a 4-D subspace despite zero dot product) that vanilla attention can only approximate implicitly, and that SWT's shift-invariant, multi-resolution decomposition provides a principled tokenization that reduces the burden on the attention module. On Weather, the full model uses only 0.3% of iTransformer's parameters and 13% of TimeMixer's, while achieving the best MSE on 7 of 8 long-term benchmarks.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (L=96, H∈{96,192,336,720}), SimpleTM achieves the best MSE/MAE on 7 of 8 datasets, with headline wins including MSE 0.353 on ETTh2 (8.1% below TimeMixer), 0.166 on ECL (8.8% below TimeMixer), and 0.184 on Solar-Energy (14.8% below TimeMixer). On short-term PEMS forecasting (H=12, L=96), SimpleTM attains the best MAE on 3 of 4 datasets (e.g., 18.74 on PEMS04 vs. 18.97 for TimeMixer) and the best RMSE on all four. The model uses only 0.3% of iTransformer's parameters and 13% of TimeMixer's parameters on the Weather dataset while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimpleTM outperforms TimeMixer (ICLR 2024) on 7 of 8 long-term datasets, with the largest gap on Solar-Energy (14.8% MSE reduction) and ECL (8.8%); TimeMixer's multi-scale mixing via average pooling loses fine-grained information that SimpleTM's wavelet tokens preserve. Against iTransformer (ICLR 2024), SimpleTM wins on 7 of 8 datasets (e.g., 7.0% MSE reduction on ETTh1, 6.4% on ETTm1) but loses on Traffic (862 channels), where iTransformer's variate-wise tokenization excels at high dimensionality. On short-term PEMS forecasting, SimpleTM beats TimeMixer on MAE for PEMS04 (18.74 vs 18.97), PEMS07 (20.25 vs 20.76), and PEMS08 (14.92 vs 15.26), while TimeMixer edges ahead by 0.06 on PEMS03 MAE. Against zero-shot LLMs (TIMER-1B/16B/28B, MOIRAI, MOMENT), SimpleTM matches or beats TIMER-16B on most datasets (e.g., ETTm1: 0.321 vs 0.766) and substantially outperforms MOMENT across the board. Simpler baselines like DLinear and RLinear are consistently outperformed, confirming that the wavelet tokenization plus geometric attention captures structure beyond what linear projections can model.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimpleTM carves out a deliberately non-generalist niche in MTS forecasting, departing from both the large-Transformer backbone trend (iTransformer, TimeMixer, PatchTST) and the LLM-repurposing line (Time-LLM, Timer, Moirai). Its tokenization module repurposes the stationary wavelet transform—a classical signal-processing tool recently adapted for vision tokenization (WaveViT, wavelet-based image tokenizers)—into a learnable, multi-scale token generator for time series. The attention module introduces a lightweight geometric-algebra extension (wedge product alongside dot product) that is conceptually related to but far lighter than full Clifford-algebra Transformers (Brehmer et al. 2023, de Haan et al. 2024), avoiding the exponential cost of high-dimensional geometric products. The overall design philosophy aligns with the 'simple baseline' tradition initiated by DLinear, but adds structured inductive biases (multi-scale decomposition, inter-channel geometric relationships) that pure linear models lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single-layer, parameter-efficient model (0.3% of iTransformer's parameters) can match or exceed much larger architectures on most standard MTS benchmarks, offering a practical lightweight baseline for settings where compute and data are limited. However, the authors acknowledge that inter-channel dependency exploitation does not uniformly improve performance across datasets, and the architecture cannot easily support autoregressive token-by-token generation, limiting its applicability to generative or variable-horizon settings. As publicly available MTS corpora grow, the authors concede that larger foundation models may ultimately outperform specialized baselines, though the individual components (learnable wavelet tokenization, geometric-product attention) could inform the design of domain-specific adapters. An open question is whether the geometric attention mechanism provides genuine representational advantages over sufficiently deep vanilla attention, or whether its benefit is primarily an inductive-bias/regularization effect in the low-parameter regime.
