## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods appear effective in full precision but catastrophically fail when the unlearned model is subsequently quantized—a standard deployment step—because the minimal weight changes required for utility preservation cause quantization to map unlearned and target model weights to identical values, recovering the 'forgotten' knowledge. Current unlearning benchmarks (MUSE, TOFU, RWKU) do not test quantized models, so this failure mode goes undetected.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (target model), a forget dataset, and a retain dataset, and produces an unlearned LLM whose weights are displaced far enough from the target to survive 4-bit quantization, along with a module-level binary saliency mask. The saliency mask operates at the granularity of transformer sub-modules (individual attention heads or FFN sub-layers) rather than individual weights, since per-weight masking is infeasible at LLM scale. The unlearning is performed with a learning rate (1e-4) roughly 10× larger than standard unlearning protocols (1e-5), which is the critical difference from prior methods.

## Method

SURE (Saliency-Based Unlearning with a Large Learning Rate) addresses the core tension between utility preservation (requiring small weight changes) and quantization robustness (requiring weight changes exceeding the quantization step size Δ). It increases the learning rate for both the forgetting loss and the retain loss to ensure sufficient weight displacement, but constrains updates to only the most salient modules identified by a hard threshold on the Frobenius norm of the forgetting-loss gradient per module. Modules below the threshold (e.g., 90th or 99th percentile of saliency scores) are frozen, preventing the aggressive full-model updates from degrading general utility or introducing retain-set bias. The theoretical analysis shows that for int-4 quantization with max|w|=200, the step size is 25, meaning weight changes must exceed 12.5 to produce a different quantized value—far larger than the perturbations induced by standard 1e-5 learning-rate unlearning.

## Results

The paper's headline finding is that 4-bit quantization of unlearned LLMs recovers an average of 83% of the intended forgotten knowledge, compared to only 21% retained in full precision (for methods with utility constraints). On the BOOKS dataset, GA_KLR's verbatim memorization (M1) jumps from 13.0 to 75.6 and knowledge memorization (M2) from 15.1 to 34.6 after 4-bit RTN quantization, nearly matching the target model's 99.8/59.4. In contrast, 8-bit quantization produces negligible changes (e.g., GA_KLR M1: 13.0→12.4 on BOOKS), confirming the effect is specific to low-precision quantization. The proposed SURE method partially mitigates recovery (e.g., GA_KLR+SURE+4bit M1: 16.4 vs. 75.6 without SURE) but at the cost of utility drops in truthfulness and factuality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a method that outperforms baselines in the traditional sense; rather, it demonstrates that ALL existing unlearning baselines catastrophically fail under 4-bit quantization. GA (Yao et al., 2023) and NPO (Zhang et al., 2024) without utility constraints show near-zero forgetting metrics in full precision but also zero utility; after 4-bit quantization, NPO recovers some knowledge (M1: 0→16.2 on NEWS) while GA remains at zero only because the model is completely broken. The regularized variants (GA_GDR, GA_KLR, NPO_GDR, NPO_KLR) show the most dramatic recovery: on BOOKS, M1 (VerMem) jumps from 12–16 to 66–76 after 4-bit quantization, approaching the target model's 99.8. On the RWKU benchmark, GA and NPO similarly worsen after 4-bit quantization (e.g., GA FB: 46.1→64.6, QA: 27.6→49.3), while RT shows a misleading FB improvement (76.6→66.6) caused by utility collapse. The additional baselines RMU (Li et al., 2024b) and LAT (Sheshadri et al., 2024) in Appendix J also exhibit poor forgetting performance after quantization, confirming the issue is pervasive across method families. The proposed SURE method partially mitigates recovery (e.g., GA_KLR+SURE+4bit M1: 16.4 vs. 75.6 for GA_KLR+4bit on BOOKS) but introduces utility trade-offs in truthfulness and factuality, and is highly sensitive to the saliency threshold γ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established LLM unlearning literature—Gradient Ascent (Yao et al., 2023) and Negative Preference Optimization (Zhang et al., 2024) with utility-preserving regularizers (GDR, KLR)—and on post-training quantization methods (RTN, GPTQ, AWQ). Its key departure is identifying that the very design choices that make unlearning effective (small learning rates, retain-set regularization to minimize weight changes) create a vulnerability: quantization maps the minimally-perturbed weights back to the original model's values, restoring forgotten knowledge. The proposed SURE method borrows the module-level saliency masking idea from knowledge editing and localization-informed unlearning (Meng et al., 2022; Fan et al., 2024b; Wu et al., 2023) and combines it with a larger learning rate, applied as a wrapper on top of existing unlearning algorithms rather than replacing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since 4-bit quantization is the de facto standard for deploying LLMs in resource-constrained settings, this finding means that any 'unlearned' model in production could have its supposedly-erased knowledge trivially recovered by an adversary who simply quantizes it. The paper introduces quantization robustness as a new evaluation axis for unlearning, exposing a blind spot in current benchmarks (MUSE, TOFU, RWKU). However, the proposed SURE mitigation is hyperparameter-sensitive and does not fully resolve the fundamental tension between utility preservation (requiring small weight changes) and quantization robustness (requiring large weight changes), leaving the problem substantially open for future work.
