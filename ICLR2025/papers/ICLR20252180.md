## Problem

Existing robust fine-tuning methods (L2-SP, TPGM, FTP) constrain fine-tuned weights to stay near pre-trained initialization using magnitude-based distance penalties, which require extensive hyperparameter tuning, can cause underfitting, and ignore the directional information in gradients. Additionally, robust fine-tuning has only been evaluated in uni-modal settings (e.g., image classification) and has not been systematically analyzed in multi-modal evaluation settings such as visual question answering.

## Input / Output

For image classification, the method takes 224×224 RGB images and produces class labels (345 classes on DomainNet). For VQA tasks, it takes an RGB image paired with a question text (128-token sequences) and generates a text answer, evaluated via the standard VQAv2 soft-accuracy metric. When combined with LoRA, the method only updates low-rank attention matrices (W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o with r=8) and minimizes the norm of the updated parameters rather than the distance to pre-trained weights, eliminating the need to store a separate pre-trained copy.

## Method

DiGraP reformulates robust fine-tuning as a multi-objective optimization problem with two objectives: the task loss and the squared L2 distance to pre-trained weights. When the per-layer gradients of these two objectives conflict (negative dot product), the task gradient is projected onto the orthogonal complement of the regularization gradient, removing the conflicting directional component. A per-layer trainable projection strength ω ∈ [0,1] is learned via a hyper-optimizer (Adam with learning rate μ), initialized at 0 so early training prioritizes task fitting and gradually increases to enforce regularization as the model diverges from initialization. This makes DiGraP equivalent to L2-SP with a layer-wise, iteration-varying λ, but replaces the hard-to-tune weight decay with a single intuitive hyperparameter μ that controls how quickly ω grows, and is compatible with PEFT methods like LoRA without additional memory overhead.

## Results

On the comprehensive VQA benchmark (PaliGemma-3B with LoRA), DiGraP achieves 87.40% ID accuracy on VQAv2 and 75.87% average near-OOD accuracy, outperforming vanilla fine-tuning (86.29% ID, 75.08% near-OOD) and all robust fine-tuning baselines. On the DomainNet image classification benchmark (MOCO-V3 ResNet50), DiGraP achieves 38.01% average OOD accuracy, surpassing FTP (37.62%), TPGM (36.86%), and L2-SP (36.23%). DiGraP is also competitive on far-OOD VQA datasets (38.77% avg), though FTP achieves higher far-OOD scores (41.95%) at the cost of substantially lower ID performance (81.77% vs. 87.40%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiGraP consistently outperforms L2-SP (Li et al., 2018) and MARS-SP (Gouk et al., 2021) on both ID and OOD metrics across all benchmarks, with a +1.78% OOD improvement over L2-SP on DomainNet. Against LP-FT (Kumar et al., 2022), DiGraP achieves higher OOD accuracy (38.01% vs. 36.79% on DomainNet; 75.87% vs. 73.38% near-OOD on VQA) while LP-FT retains a marginal ID advantage on DomainNet (82.92% vs. 82.20%). DiGraP surpasses TPGM (Tian et al., 2023a) on OOD (38.01% vs. 36.86% on DomainNet) and FTP (Tian et al., 2023b) on ID and near-OOD (87.40% vs. 81.77% ID; 75.87% vs. 72.29% near-OOD on VQA), though FTP achieves notably higher far-OOD scores (41.95% vs. 38.77%) due to its stronger implicit regularization. WiSE-FT (Wortsman et al., 2022) performs poorly in the VQA setting, dropping 12.96% in ID and 11.26% in OOD relative to vanilla fine-tuning on DomainNet-oVQA, whereas DiGraP improves both. Overall, DiGraP achieves the best combined ID + near-OOD performance across all tested backbones (ResNet50, PaliGemma-3B, LLaVA-7B, CLIP ViT-Base) and tasks (image classification, VQA, NLP).

## Relation to Prior Work

DiGraP reframes robust fine-tuning from the regularization perspective (L2-SP) and bi-level optimization perspective (TPGM, FTP) into a multi-objective optimization problem, borrowing the gradient-surgery mechanism from PCGrad (Yu et al., 2020) but tailoring it to the fine-tuning setting with a trainable, layer-wise projection strength. The key conceptual departure is that DiGraP encodes directional information in the gradient conflict between the task loss and the weight-deviation objective, whereas prior methods (L2-SP, TPGM, FTP) only constrain the magnitude of weight displacement. The paper also extends the evaluation scope from the established uni-modal image classification benchmark (DomainNet) to multi-modal VQA, a setting where prior robust fine-tuning methods have not been systematically compared.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The trainable projection strength ω (bounded in [0,1]) offers a more intuitive and less sensitive hyperparameter than the weight-decay λ in L2-SP, and the method is compatible with PEFT approaches like LoRA without extra memory overhead. A notable open question is why FTP, despite severe ID underfitting, achieves the best far-OOD performance, suggesting that stronger regularization may be beneficial for very distant distributions—a trade-off DiGraP's weaker regularization does not capture. The paper also highlights that vanilla fine-tuning on VQA tasks does not degrade OOD performance (unlike image classification), raising questions about when robust fine-tuning is actually necessary and how pre-training objectives interact with downstream adaptation.
