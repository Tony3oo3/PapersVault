## Problem

Existing tabular QA benchmarks such as WikiTableQuestions, WikiSQL, and Spider focus exclusively on single-table reasoning, failing to capture the multi-table complexity of real-world applications where joins, foreign-key identification, and multi-hop evidence retrieval across interconnected tables are routine. No comprehensive benchmark existed to evaluate LLMs' ability to reason across multiple tables at the table, column, and cell levels simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251744/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the retrieval subtask, the input is a multi-hop question plus a large table corpus (all 3,312 MMQA tables), and the output is a ranked list of K relevant tables (K=2, 5, or 10). For the reasoning subtasks, the input is the question paired with 2–3 specific tables averaging 1,369–1,833 rows each, and the outputs span four formats: a natural-language answer list, a SQL query string, primary-key column names, and foreign-key column names. The large table sizes (over 1,000 rows on average) are a deliberate design choice to stress-test long-context tabular reasoning.

## Method

The paper introduces the MMQA benchmark (3,312 tables from Spider, 5,000 samples with 2–3 tables each) and a five-faceted evaluation framework covering Multi-Table Retrieval, Text-to-SQL, Multi-Table QA, Primary Key Selection, and Foreign Key Selection. The proposed MTR retrieval method first uses GPT-4-turbo to decompose a multi-hop question into a sequence of sub-questions, then iteratively retrieves tables over n rounds. In each round, a fine-tuned single-table retrieval model (TableLlama-7b or SGPT-5.8B) scores question-table relevance, while table-table relevance is computed via column-header overlap with previously retrieved tables; the final score is the product of both, accumulated across rounds. An ablation shows the question-decomposition module contributes a 7-point precision gain in Top-2 retrieval, confirming that decomposing multi-hop queries into single-table retrieval sub-problems is the key mechanism behind MTR's SOTA performance.

## Results

On the 2-table MMQA subset, O1-preview (one-shot) achieves the best LLM performance with 50.78 EM on Multi-Table QA, 43.62 Rouge-1 on Text-to-SQL, 49.53% accuracy on Primary Key Selection, and 34.17% on Foreign Key Selection. The proposed MTR method with TableLlama-7b achieves 68.3% F1 on Top-2 multi-table retrieval, substantially outperforming TableLlama-7b alone (57.4% F1) and SGPT-5.8B (46.9% F1). All LLMs remain far below human performance, which reaches 89.8 EM on QA, 96.5 Rouge-1 on Text-to-SQL, and 95.3%/96.5% on PKS/FKS respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On multi-table retrieval (Table 4), MTR with TableLlama-7b achieves 68.3% F1 at Top-2 (2-table), beating TableLlama-7b alone (57.4%), SGPT-5.8B (46.9%), DTR (33.3%), and BM25 (5.1%) by large margins. The Question Decomposition module contributes ~4.5 F1 points (63.8→68.3) in the Top-2 2-table setting. On Multi-Table QA, O1-preview† (50.78 EM) outperforms GPT-4† (28.88 EM) by 21.9 points, Gemini-pro† (28.58 EM) by 22.2 points, and GPT-3.5† (26.79 EM) by 24.0 points, while open-source models (TableLlama-7b† at 8.23 EM, Mistral-7b† at 6.26 EM) trail by over 40 points. On Text-to-SQL, O1-preview† leads with 43.62 Rouge-1, ahead of GPT-4† (39.64) and Gemini-pro† (35.26). For Foreign Key Selection, the gap is most pronounced: O1-preview† reaches 34.17% while GPT-4† manages only 26.88% and TableLlama-7b† just 15.89%, with humans at 96.5%—indicating that even the strongest LLMs struggle with identifying relational schema columns across tables.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-table QA evaluation paradigm (WikiTableQuestions, WikiSQL, HybridQA, FinQA) to the multi-table, multi-hop setting, drawing an explicit analogy to the single-hop → multi-hop QA transition (SQuAD → HotpotQA). It builds on the Spider database for data sourcing and follows Pal et al. (2023) MultiTabQA for SQL-template-based question synthesis, but departs by introducing a comprehensive multi-granularity evaluation framework (table, column, cell level) that includes primary/foreign key selection tasks absent from prior work. The proposed MTR retrieval method repurposes single-table retrieval models (TableLlama, SGPT) and multi-hop question decomposition (Wu et al., 2024a) into an iterative multi-table retrieval pipeline, a combination not previously explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMQA provides the first dedicated benchmark for evaluating LLMs on multi-table reasoning, a gap that limits real-world deployment where data is inherently relational. The finding that even O1-preview achieves only ~50% EM versus ~90% human performance on multi-table QA, and that performance drops sharply beyond 800-row tables, highlights concrete bottlenecks for practitioners. Key limitations include the template-based question synthesis (reducing linguistic diversity), reliance on Exact Match for answer evaluation, and the fact that generated SQL queries are not verified for executability. Open questions include whether the gap narrows with more tables, whether the 800-row elbow point is model-specific, and how to evaluate multi-table reasoning beyond 3 tables.
