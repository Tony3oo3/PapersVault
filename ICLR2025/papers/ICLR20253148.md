## Problem

Despite MLLMs' impressive general capabilities, their performance as industrial quality inspectors has never been systematically evaluated with quantitative benchmarks. Existing IAD datasets contain only pixel-level masks and category labels, lacking the rich semantic annotations needed to pose meaningful language-based questions, and prior MLLM evaluations on IAD were limited to a few qualitative examples without unified test data or output formats.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253148/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs an industrial image (from 38 product categories across MVTec AD, MVTec LOCO AD, VisA, and GoodsAD) with a multiple-choice text question covering one of 7 subtasks (anomaly discrimination, defect classification/localization/description/analysis, object classification/analysis). In the default 1-shot setting, a normal template image is provided alongside the query image for comparison; the 1-shot+ variant retrieves the most similar normal image via SSIM and Bhattacharyya distance. The model outputs a single answer letter, and accuracy is computed per subtask, with anomaly discrimination using the mean of normal and abnormal class accuracies to handle class imbalance.

## Method

The core contribution is a GPT-4V-based pipeline that converts existing IAD datasets (which lack semantic annotations) into 39,672 multiple-choice questions over 8,366 images. The pipeline first generates rich image captions by prompting GPT-4V with visual cues (defect masks highlighted in red, a retrieved similar normal image) and language cues (object/defect category labels, nine-grid positional descriptions), then generates questions and distractor options per predefined subtask definitions, followed by manual verification by 26 annotators over 200+ hours. Beyond the benchmark itself, the paper explores two training-free boost strategies: a RAG scheme that retrieves category-specific domain knowledge (normal characteristics and anomaly descriptions) into the text prompt, and an expert agent scheme that feeds visualized anomaly maps from IAD models (AnomalyCLIP, PatchCore) or ground-truth masks into the MLLM as additional visual input.

## Results

On the MMAD benchmark (39,672 multiple-choice questions across 7 subtasks and 38 industrial categories), GPT-4o achieves the highest average accuracy of 74.92%, followed by Gemini-1.5-pro at 73.09% and the best open-source model InternVL2-76B at 70.75%. Human experts score 86.65% and ordinary humans 78.69%, revealing a 12% gap between the best model and expert humans. The IAD-specific model AnomalyGPT performs worst overall (36.52%), confirming that narrow training on anomaly discrimination does not transfer to the broader inspection tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o (74.92%) leads all models, outperforming Gemini-1.5-pro (73.09%) by ~2% and the best open-source model InternVL2-76B (70.75%) by ~4.2% in average accuracy. Among open-source models, scale is the dominant factor: InternVL2-76B (70.75%) surpasses LLaVA-NeXT-34B (67.16%) and all 7–8B models (51–66%), with a 23.4% accuracy gap between the smallest and largest InternVL2 variants. The IAD-specific model AnomalyGPT (36.52%) performs worst overall, scoring near random chance on defect classification (27.49%), localization (27.97%), and object tasks, despite a reasonable anomaly discrimination score (65.57%)—confirming that narrow training on a single task does not generalize. A consistent pattern across all models is a significant performance drop on defect-related subtasks (discrimination, classification, localization) compared to object-related subtasks (classification, analysis), where GPT-4o reaches 94.98% on object classification, even surpassing human experts (86.11%). Human experts (86.65%) outperform the best model by 11.7%, with the largest gaps in anomaly discrimination (95.24% vs. 68.63%) and defect localization (92.31% vs. 55.62%), highlighting that fine-grained visual comparison remains the core weakness of current MLLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new evaluation space at the intersection of MLLM benchmarking and industrial anomaly detection (IAD). Prior MLLM benchmarks (MME, MMBench, MMMU, Seed-Bench) assess general or domain-adjacent capabilities but none address industrial inspection, while prior IAD-specific MLLMs (AnomalyGPT, Myriad, FabGPT) were trained on IAD data and evaluated with traditional detection metrics rather than testing the MLLM's own reasoning and language capabilities. The data generation pipeline repurposes GPT-4V's captioning ability to bridge the gap between visual-only IAD annotations and the rich semantic descriptions needed for VQA-style evaluation, and the two boost strategies (RAG and expert agent) adapt general LLM techniques to the industrial domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMAD fills a critical gap by providing the first quantitative, multi-subtask evaluation of MLLMs as industrial quality inspectors, revealing that even GPT-4o (74.9%) trails human experts (86.65%) by over 12% and that all models show a pronounced weakness on defect-related questions versus object-related ones. The finding that AnomalyGPT, purpose-built for IAD, performs worst overall (36.52%) challenges the assumption that domain-specific training on small IAD datasets is sufficient, pointing toward the need for larger-scale industrial data and broader knowledge. Open questions include how to close the fine-grained visual comparison gap, whether RAG or agent-based approaches can scale to real production lines, and whether the 1-shot+ template mechanism can be made more reliable for open-source models.
