## Problem

Offline RL faces two intertwined challenges: out-of-distribution (OOD) value overestimation and misalignment between the learned policy and the value function's optimal actions. Behavior cloning avoids OOD but treats all samples equally, failing on sub-optimal datasets, while value-based policy gradients achieve alignment but are highly susceptible to OOD. Existing methods (e.g., TD3+BC, IQL) attempt to balance these via heuristic regularization or mixed objectives, yet still struggle to simultaneously address both issues without introducing complex generative models or transformer architectures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') tuples and a current state vector to produce a continuous action. The meta-scoring network specifically takes the triple (s, a, Q_θ(s,a)) as input to produce a scalar importance weight per sample. A key design choice is that the meta-scoring network is active only during training; at inference time it is entirely removed, so the deployed policy is a standard 3-layer MLP mapping state to action with no additional forward-pass cost.

## Method

VACO formulates offline policy learning as a bi-level optimization: the inner loop performs weighted behavior cloning where a learnable meta-scoring network w_α(s, a, Q_θ(s,a)) assigns per-sample importance weights to the standard L2 BC loss, enabling differentiated learning across data quality; the outer loop maximizes Q_θ(s, π_φ(s + N(0,σ))) to drive value alignment, with a progressively decreasing Gaussian noise term enabling limited exploration. Training is split into two phases—first the value network is trained to convergence using IQL's expectile TD learning, then the bi-level loop alternates between updating the policy φ (inner, via standard gradient descent on the weighted BC loss) and updating the meta-scoring parameters α (outer, via a one-step differentiation gradient that backpropagates through the policy update). The meta-scoring network is a simple 3-layer MLP, and the entire framework requires no generative models, transformers, or hand-tuned regularization hyperparameters beyond the noise schedule.

## Results

On the D4RL MuJoCo benchmark, VACO achieves an average normalized score of 95.43 (90.86 excluding expert datasets), substantially outperforming the next-best methods DS4 (87.42) and DC (87.13). On AntMaze, VACO scores 76.7 average versus EDP's 73.4 and SAC-RND's 60.9. On the Adroit manipulation tasks, VACO reaches 58.4 average compared to IQL's 53.4 and TD3+BC's 49.9, and on Kitchen it achieves 56.7 versus RvS-G's 54.0. On the vision-based V-D4RL benchmark, VACO attains 49.2 average, surpassing BC (45.2) and DrQ+BC (35.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VACO outperforms all 17 baselines on the MuJoCo average (95.43 vs. 87.42 for DS4, 87.13 for DC, 85.48 for EDP, 84.20 for CQL, 83.78 for IQL, 82.53 for TD3+BC, and 81.58 for DT), with the largest margins over implicit-regularization methods (MOPO: 37.68, PLAS: 57.33) and classic methods (BC: 64.57). On AntMaze, VACO (76.7) leads EDP (73.4) by 3.3 points and SAC-RND (60.9) by 15.8 points, while BC and TD3+BC collapse to near-zero on large-scale tasks. On Adroit, VACO (58.4) beats IQL (53.4) and TD3+BC (49.9), with particularly strong gains on Pen-human (106.0 vs. 81.8 for TD3+BC) and Pen-cloned (98.3 vs. 77.2 for IQL). On Kitchen, VACO (56.7) edges out RvS-G (54.0) and IQL (53.3), while BEAR and BCQ fail entirely (0.0 and 12.7). On V-D4RL, VACO (49.2) surpasses BC (45.2) and DrQ+BC (35.3), though it trails CQL (89.6) on walker-walk expert specifically. The meta-scoring network's advantage over heuristic weighting (reciprocal-value or AWR) is confirmed in ablation experiments, where VACO consistently outperforms both alternatives across all MuJoCo datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

VACO sits at the intersection of behavior cloning, deterministic policy gradient, and meta-learning, framing offline RL as a bi-level optimization problem where a meta-scoring network (inspired by meta-weight-net and hypergradient descent) assigns per-sample importance weights in the inner loop while value maximization in the outer loop enforces policy-value alignment. It explicitly departs from prior bi-level offline RL work [56] by placing the meta-scoring network in the upper function and policy extraction in the lower function, rather than the reverse. The value network training follows IQL's expectile regression, and the overall framework is positioned as a flexible scaffold that can substitute different value losses (CQL, IQL) and policy losses (AWR, diffusion-based), distinguishing it from the three dominant offline RL paradigms (explicit regularization, implicit regularization via generative models, and return-conditioned sequence modeling).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple 3-layer MLP meta-scoring network within a bi-level loop can outperform substantially more complex architectures (diffusion models, transformers, VAEs) on D4RL continuous control benchmarks, suggesting that the key bottleneck in offline RL is not model capacity but the principled balancing of OOD avoidance and value alignment. The interpretability analysis revealing that meta-weights correlate strongly (r=0.954) with trajectory-level reward but only weakly (r=0.24) with individual Q-values offers a useful lens on what the network actually learns. Open questions include whether the one-step differentiation approximation remains valid at scale, whether the framework extends to discrete or high-dimensional action spaces, and whether the two-phase training (value first, then bi-level) is truly necessary or an artifact of implementation convenience.
