## Problem

Protein family design requires generating diverse, novel sequences that preserve the structural and functional characteristics of a target family, but existing approaches face a practical bottleneck: MSA-based generative models demand expensive construction of large-scale MSA training datasets, while single-sequence language models are sub-optimal for capturing the complex global spatial correlations inherent in protein sequence distributions. This paper asks whether one can leverage MSA profile information for family-conditioned generation without incurring the cost of building profile training sets.

## Input / Output

At training time, the model consumes single protein sequences (190M from UniRef) by treating each one-hot amino acid encoding as a degenerate profile with all probability mass on one category. At inference time, it accepts either a single sequence (t₀=0.3) or a full MSA profile — a list of K-dimensional probability mass functions, one per position, computed from HHblits/Uniclust30 alignments (t₀=0.6). The output is a single amino acid sequence of the same length as the input, decoded by argmax over the final predicted profile at each position after N iterative denoising steps.

## Method

ProfileBFN extends the discrete Bayesian Flow Network by replacing the one-hot data vector with a generalized profile ρ ∈ Δ^{K-1} at each position, and formally re-derives both the continuous-time Bayesian flow (Theorem 3.1: a Gaussian perturbation of log-odds scaled by Kβ(t)ρ with covariance β(t)𝒞) and the training loss (Theorem 3.2: a scaled squared L2 distance ½β′(t)K‖p_φ − ρ‖²). The architecture is an ESM-2-style Transformer (150M or 650M parameters, 33 layers, 20-head attention) that takes independently accumulated noisy profile information θ_t^{(i)} at each position and predicts the true profile, modeling inter-position dependencies. The key efficiency insight is that a single sequence's one-hot vector is a degenerate profile, so the model trains entirely on single sequences yet can sample conditioned on full MSA profiles at inference. Sampling proceeds iteratively from t₀ to t=1, alternating between drawing noisy evidence from the Bayesian flow and updating the profile prediction via the network, with the final sequence decoded by per-position argmax.

## Results

On the CAMEO family protein generation benchmark (61 proteins, 1000 sequences each), ProfileBFN-Profile(650M) achieves the best structural metrics: LR P@L/2 = 0.280 and LR P@L/5 = 0.474, surpassing even the Searched MSA reference (0.270 and 0.395) while maintaining strong novelty (0.641) and diversity (0.293). On enzyme function generation, ProfileBFN-Profile(650M) achieves 95.19%, 98.98%, and 42.67% Accuracy×Uniqueness across three enzyme families, dramatically outperforming EvoDiff-MSA (27.93%, 88.69%, 1.39%) and PoET-MSA (3.00%, 33.3%, 0.05%). For protein representation learning, ProfileBFN(650M) outperforms DPLM(650M) on all nine downstream tasks, including HumanPPI (82.22% vs 77.77% ACC) and Metal Ion Binding (74.58% vs 70.52% ACC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProfileBFN consistently outperforms its closest competitor DPLM (discrete diffusion) on all structural metrics at both 150M and 650M scales — e.g., at 650M, ProfileBFN-Profile achieves LR P@L/5 = 0.474 vs DPLM's 0.303, and also wins on novelty (0.641 vs 0.411) and diversity (0.293 vs 0.445). Against EvoDiff-MSA, ProfileBFN wins decisively on structure (LR P@L/5: 0.474 vs 0.168) while EvoDiff retains a slight edge in diversity (0.225 vs 0.293) and novelty (0.668 vs 0.641). PoET (both Single and MSA variants) is far behind on structure (LR P@L/5: 0.031–0.051 vs 0.474) and suffers severe mode collapse (Div = 0.853 for Single). ESM-2 used as a masked-generation baseline is also clearly outperformed on all structural metrics. Notably, ProfileBFN-Profile(650M) matches or exceeds the Searched MSA reference on LR P@L/2 (0.280 vs 0.270) and LR P@L/5 (0.474 vs 0.395), indicating generated sequences capture family coevolutionary structure as well as or better than the actual homologous alignment. On enzyme generation, ProfileBFN dominates: 95.19% vs 27.93% (EvoDiff) and 3.00% (PoET) on P40925, and 42.67% vs 1.39% and 0.05% on Q15165.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProfileBFN extends the discrete Bayesian Flow Network (BFN) of Graves et al. (2023) by generalizing the input from a one-hot vector (single sequence) to a probability mass function (MSA profile), requiring a formal re-derivation of both the Bayesian flow and the KL-divergence loss. In the protein design landscape, it occupies the protein family design niche alongside PoET (autoregressive MSA prompting), EvoDiff (diffusion guided by MSA), and DPLM (discrete diffusion on single sequences), but departs from all of them by operating on profile distributions rather than raw sequences or alignments. The key practical departure is that a single sequence is a degenerate profile (one-hot), so the model trains on standard single-sequence data (UniRef) yet can condition on full MSA profiles at inference time, eliminating the need to construct profile training sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to train on single sequences while sampling from family profiles removes a significant practical bottleneck in protein family design, making the approach more accessible and computationally efficient. The non-parametric CCMPred evaluation showing generated sequences outperforming searched MSAs in contact prediction is a useful methodological contribution that addresses known vulnerabilities of parameterized structure-prediction metrics (e.g., ESMFold pLDDT hacking via BLOSUM substitutions). Open questions include whether the profile abstraction loses information relative to full MSA (the paper acknowledges MSA input gives better structural metrics than single-sequence input), whether the approach generalizes beyond the 61 CAMEO targets and 3 enzyme families tested, and how to incorporate 3D structural information as SaProt does for representation tasks.
