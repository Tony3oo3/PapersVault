## Problem

Many sequence learning models lack the ability to abstract from concrete sequences, resulting in memory inefficiency and poor transfer to novel sequences. While chunking models can compose complex structures from elementary units, they do not identify abstract variables—distinct items that play similar roles in the same context—limiting their capacity for compact representation and generalization. The paper asks what computational principles enable an agent to jointly discover both concrete chunks and abstract categories from observational sequences without supervision.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a discrete observational sequence of integer-valued atomic units (e.g., 010021002112000...) and produces a belief set containing a chunk dictionary and a set of variables, each variable denoting a set of chunks that share common preceding and succeeding contexts. Chunks are organized in a prefix Trie parsing graph for O(D) retrieval (scaling with tree depth rather than dictionary size), and the full structure forms a representation graph with nested hierarchical edges. The model outputs a compressed parsed sequence, sequence negative log-likelihood, and a coding efficiency metric (dictionary size relative to sequence length), with all learning occurring online in an unsupervised, non-parametric fashion.

## Method

HVM is a non-parametric hierarchical variable learning model that conjunctively learns chunks and variables through two mechanisms: chunk proposal (concatenating statistically correlated consecutive chunk pairs into new entries) and variable discovery (grouping chunks that appear in identical pre- and post-adjacency contexts into an abstract variable). Chunks are organized in a prefix Trie parsing graph where each parent node is the common prefix of its children, reducing retrieval from O(|C|) to O(D). Variables are proposed by intersecting post-adjacency columns with pre-adjacency rows in the transition matrix, and a variable is accepted if its denoting set size falls within [T_min, T_max] and total frequency exceeds a threshold. The model iterates: parse the sequence greedily (deepest consistent chunk in the Trie), update marginal and transition counts (with optional memory decay θ), propose new chunks and variables, delete unused entries, and repeat—yielding layer-wise discovery from concrete to increasingly abstract patterns. This is framed as an approximate recognition inverse of a generative model that builds an inventory of objects and categories over d expansion iterations, with categories being latent and manifested through their denoting objects.

## Results

On real-world text (BabyLM domains), HVM achieves the lowest NLL on CHILDES (1953.01 vs. 2783.71 for HCM and 2837.50 for LZ78) and BNC (3108.33 vs. 3136.67 for LZ78), while all three models show similar coding efficiency (HVM: 0.06–0.12 vs. LZ78: 0.34–0.39). On the human sequence-recall experiment, HVM's negative log-likelihood correlates with human recall times at R = 0.86 (training) and R = 0.70 (transfer), both p ≤ 0.001. On the transfer block, HVM and HCM produce significantly lower NLL after training with embedded variables, whereas GPT-2, Llama 2, and Llama 3 fail to differentiate variable from control training conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HCM (the chunking-only predecessor), HVM consistently achieves lower NLL across all four text domains (e.g., 1953 vs. 2784 on CHILDES) and comparable coding efficiency, confirming that variable discovery adds predictive power beyond pure chunking. Against LZ78, HVM dramatically outperforms on coding efficiency (0.06–0.12 vs. 0.34–0.39), indicating a far more compact dictionary for the same sequence length, and wins on NLL for CHILDES and BNC but trails LZ78 on Gutenberg (3253 vs. 3157) and Open Subtitles (3764 vs. 3395). On the synthetic hierarchical sequences (Fig. 3), HVM matches ground-truth sequence length after parsing while HCM and LZ78 produce longer codes, and HVM reduces parsing search steps from O(|C|) to O(D) via the prefix-trie. On the human transfer experiment (Fig. 5), HVM and HCM both show lower transfer NLL after variable training, while all tested LLMs (GPT-2, three Llama 2 variants, two Llama 3 variants) fail to exploit the shared variable structure, producing NLL indistinguishable from the control condition. The rate-distortion analysis (Fig. 6) further shows that HVM's advantage over HCM grows with abstraction depth, as higher layers enable more flexible transfer to novel sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

HVM directly extends the Hierarchical Chunking Model (HCM, Wu et al., 2022) by adding a variable-discovery mechanism that groups contextually similar chunks into abstract categories, while also introducing a prefix-trie parsing graph that reduces chunk retrieval from O(|C|) to O(D). Conceptually, the paper unifies two previously separate cognitive mechanisms—chunking (Gobet et al., 2001; Miller, 1956) and abstraction/variable binding—into a single non-parametric learning system, departing from prior work that treated them independently. The generative model draws on conformative inventory-growth frameworks (Teh, 2006) but replaces formal grammar rules with recursive object/category composition, and the evaluation explicitly contrasts HVM against both LZ78 compression and LLMs (GPT-2, Llama 2/3) on a controlled sequence-recall task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a minimal, interpretable cognitive model that captures a specific human capability—transferring abstract variables across novel sequences—that LLMs demonstrably lack, offering a concrete behavioral signature to diagnose abstraction failures in neural models. The rate-distortion framing of the compression–generalization trade-off across abstraction layers is a useful lens for understanding why more abstract representations help transfer at the cost of increased uncertainty. Key limitations include the restriction that variables cannot appear at sequence boundaries, the non-identifiability of the learned hierarchy, and the lack of computational optimization; future work on neural implementations and integration as a tokenization front-end for transformers or SSMs could broaden the model's practical reach.
