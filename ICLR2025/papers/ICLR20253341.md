## Problem

INR-based PDE solvers like CORAL rely on global modulation (shared shift parameters across all spatial positions), which limits their ability to capture fine-grained local details in PDE solutions. While replacing global modulation with per-location grid-based spatial modulation improves local reconstruction, it overfits to the training spatial domain and fails to generalize to unseen regions—a critical issue for PDE modeling where test domains differ from training domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253341/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For dynamics modeling, the method takes a PDE solution field (e.g., 2D vorticity on a 64×64 or 64×128 grid, or 3D spherical shallow-water fields) observed on a potentially sparse and irregular subset of the full domain, and produces temporal forecasts at arbitrary future time steps. For geometry-aware inference, it takes a deformed mesh (point cloud of ~1000–129×129 points representing airfoil, pipe, or elastic body geometry) and outputs the corresponding physical quantity (Mach number, velocity, or stress) at those points. The spatial domain is not fixed: training and test grids can differ in location and sparsity (5%–100%), and geometry tasks involve entirely unseen shapes.

## Method

MARBLE extends CORAL's meta-learned SIREN INR framework with two key innovations. First, GridMix replaces the per-location grid modulation with a linear mixture of M shared multi-channel grid basis functions (Φ_i^m ∈ R^{H×W×C}), where the mixture coefficients c_i are generated from a low-dimensional latent code via a linear hypernetwork; this regularizes the modulation space from H×W free parameters down to M coefficients, preserving locality while enabling global structural learning and preventing domain overfitting. Second, Spatial Domain Augmentation (SDA) randomly samples a subset of training coordinates at each MAML inner and outer loop iteration, simulating the domain variation encountered at inference. The full pipeline uses MAML to meta-learn the shared INR parameters so that encoding a new function requires only a few gradient steps, a Neural ODE (for dynamics) or MLP (for geometry) to map input latent codes to output latent codes, and a single forward pass of the modulated INR to decode the predicted field.

## Results

On the dynamics modeling task (Table 2), MARBLE achieves the best MSE across nearly all settings. At 20% irregular grid on Navier-Stokes, MARBLE reaches 1.62e-4 (In-t) and 9.27e-4 (Out-t), outperforming CORAL (2.18e-3 / 6.67e-3) by 13.5× and 7.2× respectively. At 100% regular grid, MARBLE achieves 3.52e-5 (In-t) and 5.04e-4 (Out-t) on Navier-Stokes, a 5.3× and 2.0× improvement over CORAL. On geometry-aware inference (Table 3), MARBLE attains the best relative L2 error on NACA-Euler (5.79e-3) and Elasticity (1.12e-2, a 32.9% improvement over CORAL), though it trails Geo-FNO on Pipe (1.03e-2 vs. 6.59e-3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary INR-based baseline CORAL (Serrano et al., 2023), MARBLE wins across all dynamics modeling settings (up to 17× MSE reduction at 5% grid on Navier-Stokes) and on 2 of 3 geometry tasks (Elasticity: 32.9% lower relative L2; NACA-Euler: marginal 1.9% improvement). MARBLE also dominates the neural operator baselines FNO and Factorized-FNO on Navier-Stokes at all grid densities, though FNO remains competitive on Shallow-Water at 100% grid (1.17e-4 vs. 1.42e-4 Out-t). DeepONet and MP-PDE are consistently outperformed by orders of magnitude under sparse/irregular grids. On geometry-aware inference, MARBLE loses to Geo-FNO on Pipe (1.03e-2 vs. 6.59e-3 relative L2) and to Factorized-FNO on the same task (7.33e-3), which the authors attribute to SIREN backbone limitations with directional anisotropy. DINo slightly outperforms MARBLE on Shallow-Water In-t at 5% grid (1.22e-3 vs. 5.92e-3) but underperforms on Out-t, which the authors link to DINo's over-optimized latent embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CORAL framework (Serrano et al., 2023), which uses meta-learned SIREN-based INRs with FiLM-style global modulation for PDE operator learning, by replacing the shared-across-space modulation with a spatially-varying one. It draws on grid-based representations from vision (hash encodings, multi-resolution grids) to enable position-dependent modulation, but departs from vanilla spatial modulation by regularizing it through a mixture of a small set of shared grid basis functions (GridMix), analogous to spectral decomposition. The approach is conceptually related to Factor Fields (Chen et al., 2023), which decomposes signals into coefficient fields and basis functions, but applies the decomposition in the modulation space rather than the signal space, and targets PDE domain generalization specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and previously underexplored tension in INR-based PDE solvers: global modulation cannot capture local details, while naive spatial modulation overfits to the training domain and generalizes poorly to unseen regions. GridMix resolves this with an elegant low-rank regularization that preserves locality while enabling cross-domain generalization, yielding up to 94% MSE reduction over CORAL on sparse irregular grids. However, the 6.5× memory overhead and 1.6× runtime increase limit immediate scalability, and the SIREN backbone's sensitivity to directional anisotropy (noted on the Pipe dataset) suggests the approach may not transfer uniformly across all PDE types. Open questions include efficient implementations via vector quantization or tensor factorization, and whether the basis-mixture idea extends to other modulation paradigms or higher-dimensional domains.
