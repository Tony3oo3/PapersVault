## Problem

While transformers are naturally viewed as coupled dynamical systems, no prior work has systematically characterized how the linearized effects of individual transformer blocks relate to one another across depth and across tokens. This paper asks whether identifiable structural regularities exist in the Jacobians of transformer blocks, how these properties emerge during training, and whether they are linked to generalization performance across a diverse suite of LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250912/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes 30+ pretrained LLMs (ranging from 117M to 70B parameters, trained by 8+ organizations) and 1,200 text prompts from six benchmark datasets (ARC, GSM8K, HellaSwag, MMLU, TruthfulQA, Winogrande). For each prompt, token trajectories are traced through all L transformer blocks, and block-level Jacobians (excluding skip connections) are computed for every causal token pair (t₁ ≤ t₂). The outputs are per-pair coupling scores (top-K singular vector alignment), line-shape scores (LSS) for trajectory linearity, expodistance coefficients for exponential norm growth, and aggregate correlations with HuggingFace Open LLM Leaderboard benchmark scores.

## Method

The core mechanism computes the Jacobian J_{t₁t₂}^l = ∂f^l(X^{l-1})_{t₂}/∂x_{t₁}^{l-1} for each transformer block l and token pair, then performs a truncated SVD to extract top-K singular vectors. Coupling between two Jacobians is quantified by projecting one Jacobian onto the singular vector basis of the other (matrix A = U'ᵀ J V'), measuring how strongly diagonal the result is relative to the singular value matrix, yielding a coupling score c_K = 1 − m_K. The paper analyzes three coupling types: depth-wise (same token across layers), self-coupling (same input/output token across different tokens), and context coupling (fixed input or output token). A theoretical analysis shows that under perfect spectral coupling (J^l ≈ US_lUᵀ with constant singular values), token trajectories follow x^l = Σ u_j(1+s_j)^l u_jᵀx⁰, predicting both exponential norm growth and linear trajectories — properties empirically confirmed to emerge progressively during training and to correlate positively with benchmark performance (R²=0.8, p≈10⁻¹⁰), more strongly than parameter count, depth, or embedding dimension.

## Results

Across 30+ pretrained LLMs from 8+ organizations, the average coupling of top singular vectors of block Jacobians (K = d_model/10) correlates with HuggingFace Open LLM Leaderboard benchmark scores with R² = 0.8 (p = 9.99×10⁻¹⁰). This coupling–performance correlation is stronger than correlations with parameter count, model depth, or embedding dimension. In Vision Transformers trained on CIFAR-10, coupling correlates with test accuracy at R² = 0.93. Hidden trajectory linearity (LSS) improves from 6.54 at initialization to 4.25 in trained models, and exponential growth in embedding norms (low expodistance) emerges progressively during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability/analysis paper rather than a method paper, so there are no competing methods to benchmark against. The key comparison is between coupling and other model hyperparameters as predictors of generalization: coupling (R² = 0.8) outperforms parameter count, model depth, and embedding dimension as correlates of benchmark score (Figure 9). Trained models consistently exhibit strong diagonal coupling patterns in Jacobian singular-vector alignment matrices, while untrained (randomly initialized) models of the same architecture show no such structure, confirming that coupling emerges through training rather than being an architectural artifact. The ViT experiments on CIFAR-10 (R² = 0.93) serve as an independent architectural validation, showing the phenomenon is not specific to language modeling. Coupling strength also increases monotonically across Pythia 12B training checkpoints (1 → 143k), paralleling the logarithmic accuracy curve, and exhibits layer-wise locality (stronger coupling between adjacent layers) that sharpens with training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Residual Alignment framework of Li & Papyan (2023) from classification ResNets to transformer-based LLMs, generalizing the coupling of Jacobian singular vectors from a single trajectory to multiple interacting token trajectories. The Jacobian-based linearization approach traces back to Rothauge et al. (2019) for stability analysis, and the line-shape score metric is adopted from Gai & Zhang (2021). The key departure is the introduction of cross-token coupling (self-coupling and context coupling), which is specific to the tokenized, attention-based structure of transformers and has no direct analogue in the ResNet setting. The paper also connects its findings to the Neural ODE and in-context learning literatures, suggesting that coupling implies the network approximates a simple iterative linear system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that Jacobian coupling (R²=0.8) predicts generalization more strongly than parameter count, depth, or embedding dimension offers a new structural lens for understanding why some models generalize better than others, and provides a unifying explanation for why layer merging, pruning, and early exiting work in practice. The progressive emergence of coupling during training, alongside linearity and exponential trajectory growth, suggests a potential diagnostic for training health. However, the relationship remains correlational rather than causal, the theoretical analysis relies on idealized assumptions (perfect coupling, constant singular values across depth), and the paper does not demonstrate a way to directly optimize coupling as a training objective. Open questions include whether coupling can be used as a regularizer, whether it generalizes to non-causal or non-autoregressive architectures, and whether the exponential growth regime is universal or model-family-specific.
