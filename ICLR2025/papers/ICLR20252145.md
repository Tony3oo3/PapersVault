## Problem

Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) train with multiple additive loss terms (PDE residual, boundary conditions, initial conditions) whose gradients frequently conflict in direction, causing the optimization to stall in local minima of the dominant PDE residual term. Existing weighting strategies only adjust gradient magnitudes without resolving directional conflicts, and gradient-surgery methods from multi-task learning (PCGrad, IMTL-G) fail to guarantee conflict-free updates when more than two loss terms are present.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the individual loss-specific gradients (∇θL_N, ∇θL_B, ∇θL_I) computed via separate backpropagation passes through the coordinate-based neural network, and produces a single conflict-free gradient vector g_ConFIG that is guaranteed to have positive dot product with every loss-specific gradient. In the M-ConFIG variant, only one loss gradient is backpropagated per iteration (cycling through losses), with the others carried forward via momentum, reducing per-iteration cost to roughly 1/m of full ConFIG. The final output is the trained network's prediction of the PDE solution field (scalar or vector-valued) evaluated on test points.

## Method

ConFIG constructs the update gradient as g_ConFIG = (Σᵢ gᵢᵀ g_u) g_u, where g_u is the unit vector obtained from the pseudoinverse of the transposed matrix of normalized loss-specific gradients multiplied by a unit vector 1_m. This guarantees (i) a positive projection on every loss-specific gradient (conflict-free), (ii) equal projection lengths across all losses (uniform decrease rate), and (iii) an adaptive magnitude equal to the sum of projection lengths, which shrinks the step when gradients are highly opposed. The M-ConFIG variant replaces the per-iteration full backpropagation with alternating momentum updates: each step backpropagates only one loss, updates its momentum, applies the ConFIG operator to the collection of (stale) momenta, and uses a single Adam-style second momentum on the resulting estimated gradient. The paper provides convergence proofs for both convex and non-convex settings under Lipschitz-continuous gradients with step size γ ≤ 2/L.

## Results

On the four PINN benchmarks (2-loss setting), ConFIG achieves the lowest test MSE on Burgers (1.308×10⁻⁴), Schrödinger (0.643×10⁻⁴, a 5.3× improvement over Adam's 3.383×10⁻⁴), and Beltrami flow (0.617×10⁻⁴), while M-ConFIG achieves the best Kovasznay result (0.098×10⁻⁷ vs. Adam's 1.044×10⁻⁷). Under a fixed wall-clock budget, M-ConFIG outperforms all baselines on every PDE, with an average per-iteration cost of only 0.56× Adam for three-loss PINNs. On the CelebA 40-task MTL benchmark, ConFIG and M-ConFIG₃₀ achieve the best mean rank (MR) and average F₁ score among 12 competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConFIG consistently outperforms PCGrad on the 2-loss PINN tasks: Burgers (1.308 vs. 1.344 ×10⁻⁴), Schrödinger (0.643 vs. 1.621 ×10⁻⁴), and Beltrami (0.617 vs. 1.037 ×10⁻⁴), though PCGrad edges ahead on Kovasznay (0.799 vs. 0.126 ×10⁻⁷) where IMTL-G actually achieves the best result (0.096 ×10⁻⁷). Against weighting-based baselines (MinMax, ReLoBRaLo, LRA), ConFIG dominates across all four PDEs, with LRA performing catastrophically on Burgers (353.8 vs. 1.308 ×10⁻⁴). In the 3-loss setting, ConFIG and PCGrad remain the top two methods, but their relative ranking flips depending on the PDE: PCGrad wins on Burgers (1.279 vs. 1.291) and Schrödinger (0.931 vs. 1.455 ×10⁻⁴), while ConFIG wins on Beltrami (0.571 vs. 0.757 ×10⁻⁴). Under a fixed wall-clock budget, M-ConFIG surpasses all methods including ConFIG itself on every PDE, e.g., Schrödinger 0.429×10⁻³ vs. ConFIG's 0.586×10⁻³ and Adam's 2.169×10⁻³. On CelebA (40 tasks), ConFIG and M-ConFIG₃₀ achieve the best MR and F₁ respectively, outperforming 10 MTL baselines including CAGrad, FAMO, Nash-MTL, and GradDrop.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConFIG synthesizes gradient-conflict-resolution ideas from multi-task learning (PCGrad's orthogonal projection, IMTL-G's equal-projection-length constraint) and repurposes them for PINN training, where conflicts between PDE residual, boundary, and initial-condition gradients are a well-documented but under-addressed failure mode. For two losses, ConFIG provably shares the update direction of both PCGrad and IMTL-G while introducing an adaptive magnitude that tracks the larger gradient; for more than two losses, the pseudoinverse-based construction is the only approach that guarantees a conflict-free direction. The M-ConFIG momentum variant further departs from prior gradient-surgery methods by alternating backpropagation across loss terms, reducing per-iteration cost to 1/m of the full ConFIG computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PINN training remains a high-stakes, open problem in scientific computing with no consensus on loss-balancing strategies; ConFIG provides the first method with a convergence proof that simultaneously guarantees conflict-free updates, uniform per-loss decrease rates, and adaptive step magnitude. The M-ConFIG result—that alternating momentum updates can outperform full-gradient methods in wall-clock time while matching or exceeding their accuracy—offers a practical efficiency win for practitioners. Key open questions include scaling to many loss terms (where M-ConFIG degrades), handling chaotic or high-dimensional PDEs where gradient conflicts are only one of several failure modes, and reducing the O(nm) memory overhead of storing per-loss momenta.
