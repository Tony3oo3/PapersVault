## Problem

Vision-language adaptation (VL adaptation) transforms LLMs into LVLMs but systematically degrades their safety capabilities, yet prior work has not isolated whether this degradation stems from training data or the adaptation process itself, has not comprehensively evaluated how safety tuning affects both harmlessness and helpfulness simultaneously, and has not offered a cost-efficient solution that practitioners can adopt as a standard.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251930/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The 7B-parameter LVLMs (LLaMA-2 Chat or Tulu-2 backbone with CLIP ViT-L/14@336 vision encoder and a two-layer MLP projector) accept text-only prompts or image-text pairs (images padded to preserve aspect ratio, sequences capped at 2048 tokens) and generate text responses (max 512 tokens, temperature 0.1). Safety is quantified by LLaMA-Guard 3 8B classifying each generated response across 14 harm categories to compute Attack Success Rate, while exaggerated safety uses keyword-based refusal detection and multimodal helpfulness uses exact-match scoring on 2- or 4-way multiple-choice questions.

## Method

The paper first shows via layer-wise cosine similarity analysis that VL adaptation specifically disrupts the safety layers (6–14) identified by Li et al. (2024), and that safety tuning and VL adaptation push model weights in divergent directions, making joint or sequential training suboptimal. Building on this diagnosis, the proposed solution is linear weight merging: a safety-focused model (LLaMA-2-Chat-VL-SL, fine-tuned sequentially with VLGuard) and a multimodal-optimized model (LLaMA-2-Chat-VL-Chatty, fine-tuned with WildVision-Chat-46k) are combined via α·θ_SL + (1−α)·θ_Chatty, with α = 0.4 identified as optimal through ablation. This avoids the parameter-update interference of multitask learning and the catastrophic forgetting of sequential training, yielding a Pareto-optimal balance (text-only ASR ≈ 0.31%, multimodal ASR ≈ 0.72%, multimodal accuracy ≈ 61.6%) without any additional retraining beyond the two individual fine-tuning runs.

## Results

VL adaptation of LLaMA-2 Chat 7B causes severe safety degradation: text-only ASR jumps from 10.4% to 54.4% and multimodal ASR reaches 96.2%, while the exaggerated safety refusal rate drops from 68.0% to 19.6%. The proposed weight merging approach (LLaMA-2-Chat-VL-Linear-Merged) achieves near-zero safety failure rates (0.31% text-only ASR, 0.72% multimodal ASR) while retaining 61.6% multimodal helpfulness, yielding the highest average score of 80.5% among all methods tested. Sequential safety SFT (SL) achieves comparable safety (0.7% ASR) but suffers a 73.2% refusal rate on XSTest and drops multimodal helpfulness to 42.3%, whereas RLHF with SPA-VL improves safety (19.5% text-only, 14.0% multimodal ASR) but still falls well short of SFT-level safety.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed weight merging method dramatically outperforms all four open-source LVLMs on safety: LLaVA v1.5 7B (58.7% text-only ASR, 97.3% multimodal ASR), LLaVA Next 8B (55.8%, 90.5%), Phi-3-Vision-Instruct (85.5%, 97.7%), and MiniCPM-V-2.6 (56.1%, 91.7%) all exhibit near-total safety failure, whereas the merged model achieves 0.31% and 0.72% ASR respectively. On multimodal helpfulness, the merged model (61.6%) trails MiniCPM-V-2.6 (78.9%) and LLaVA Next 8B (70.5%) but is competitive with LLaVA v1.5 7B (61.1%) and Phi-3-Vision-Instruct (66.3%). Compared to the base LLaMA-2 Chat 7B, VL adaptation alone degrades text-only safety by ~5x (10.4% → 54.4% ASR), confirming that the adaptation process itself—not training data—drives the safety loss. Among safety tuning strategies, sequential SFT matches the merged model's safety but at the cost of a 73.2% refusal rate (vs. ~21% for MTL) and a 22-point drop in multimodal helpfulness, while RLHF with SPA-VL preserves helpfulness (61.3%) but still leaves 14.0% multimodal ASR, roughly 20× higher than the merged model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM safety literature (Qi et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024) into the vision-language adaptation setting, building directly on the LLaVA training pipeline and the identification of safety-critical layers. It departs from prior safety-tuning work (VLGuard, SPA-VL, SafeRLHF) by systematically showing that both multitask and sequential safety SFT, as well as DPO-based RLHF, fail to fully resolve the safety-helpfulness trade-off introduced by VL adaptation. The proposed weight merging solution draws on the model soup / task arithmetic / TIES / DARE lineage, repurposing these techniques to combine a safety-optimized model with a multimodal-optimized model rather than to improve a single task's performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that safety degradation is inherent to the VL adaptation process itself—even with fully filtered training data—shifts the burden from data curation to architectural or post-hoc interventions, which is practically important as open-source LVLMs (LLaVA, Phi-3-Vision, MiniCPM-V) are widely deployed with poor safety profiles. The weight merging approach offers a low-cost, training-free remedy that practitioners can adopt immediately, though its generalizability beyond 7B LLaMA-2-family models and to other architectures remains untested. Open questions include whether the safety-layer disruption mechanism identified here holds for decoder-only vs. encoder-decoder architectures, whether merging ratios transfer across model scales, and how to handle the persistent gap in multimodal (vs. text-only) safety that SPPFT and merging only partially address.
