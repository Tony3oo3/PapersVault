## Problem

Existing reparameterization-based compression methods (PRANC, NOLA) constrain model parameters to random linear subspaces, which may not efficiently capture the geometry of the parameter space. This paper asks whether replacing the linear subspace with a k-dimensional nonlinear manifold that wraps around the hypersphere of directions can achieve better coverage and thus higher-quality solutions at extreme compression rates (d/k >> 100).

## Input / Output

The method takes a neural network's weight tensor (either randomly initialized or pretrained, e.g., a 4-bit quantized LLaMA-2) and task-specific training data, and produces per-chunk learnable parameters: a k-dimensional vector α and a scalar amplitude β for each d-dimensional weight chunk, plus a single random seed that defines the frozen generator. The compressed representation is reconstructed on-the-fly by running the generator on α and scaling by β, requiring only a forward pass through a small MLP (e.g., 3 layers, width 32) rather than storing full weights. Position embeddings, CLS tokens, LayerNorm, and BatchNorm parameters are excluded from compression.

## Method

MCNC decomposes each d-dimensional weight chunk via polar decomposition into an amplitude β and a unit direction u ∈ S^{d-1}, then reparameterizes u as φ(α) where φ is a frozen random feedforward network with sinusoidal activations mapping R^k → S^{d-1}. The sinusoidal activations introduce periodicity that enables the k-dimensional input hypercube to wrap smoothly and uniformly around the hypersphere, achieving near-optimal coverage even without training the generator (verified via Wasserstein distance to the uniform distribution). Model parameters are partitioned into d-dimensional chunks, each compressed to k+1 parameters (α and β), yielding a compression rate of approximately d/k. Training proceeds with standard stochastic gradient descent and backpropagation through the frozen generator—no Riemannian or geometric optimization is required. The method is orthogonal to LoRA and can be applied to either the original weights or their low-rank residual, and the generator itself is communicated by a single random seed.

## Results

On ImageNet-100 with ResNet-18, MCNC with LoRA achieves 71.7% top-1 accuracy at 1% of original model size, outperforming NOLA (64.7%) and PRANC (61.1%) by 7.0 and 10.6 percentage points respectively. At 10% model size, MCNC recovers 97% of the uncompressed baseline accuracy (80.3% vs 82.1%). For LLaMA-2 7B fine-tuning on Alpaca, MCNC achieves 45.9% MMLU 5-shot accuracy with only 25k trainable parameters (vs 2.5M for LoRA), while requiring 46% fewer GFLOPs for on-the-fly parameter generation and delivering 2× the throughput of NOLA (6.2 vs 3.1 samples/sec).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCNC consistently outperforms all reparameterization baselines at extreme compression rates: on ResNet-18/ImageNet-100 at 1% size, MCNC w/ LoRA (71.7%) beats NOLA (64.7%) by 7.0 points and PRANC (61.1%) by 10.6 points. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-56 at ~5k parameters, MCNC w/ LoRA (81.5%) surpasses NOLA (78.5%), PRANC (76.9%), and all pruning methods (DPF 47.7%, SuRP 66.7%, STR 67.8%). Against pruning baselines on ViT-Ti/ImageNet-100, MCNC achieves 72.9% at 10% model size versus PLATON (67.2%) and Magnitude pruning (60.9%), a gap that widens further at 1% size (52.9% vs 30.6% and 21.2%). For LLaMA-2 fine-tuning, MCNC matches or slightly exceeds NOLA's MMLU accuracy (45.9% vs 45.5% on 7B; 55.0% vs 54.8% on 13B) while using 46% fewer GFLOPs for adapter reconstruction and achieving 2×–2.2× higher throughput. Compared to LoRA (rank=1), MCNC uses ~100× fewer trainable parameters (25k vs 2.5M on 7B) while achieving comparable MMLU scores, demonstrating that the manifold constraint captures the essential structure of the fine-tuning residual.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCNC directly generalizes PRANC (Nooralinejad et al., 2023) and NOLA (Koohpayegani et al., 2024), which constrain model weights to randomly selected k-dimensional linear subspaces within the d-dimensional parameter space. The key conceptual departure is replacing the linear subspace with a k-dimensional nonlinear manifold—parameterized by a random feedforward network with sinusoidal activations—that wraps around the d-dimensional hypersphere via polar decomposition, thereby increasing coverage of the parameter space. The method is explicitly orthogonal to and composable with LoRA, quantization, and pruning, positioning it as a general reparameterization layer rather than a competing compression paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MCNC achieves competitive accuracy at extreme compression rates (1% of model size) while offering a practical throughput advantage over NOLA (2× for LLaMA-7B, 2.2× for LLaMA-13B) due to fewer reconstruction FLOPs, which matters for batch-processing multiple task adapters. The finding that a randomly initialized generator (communicable via a single seed) performs nearly identically to a trained one simplifies deployment and raises interesting questions about the geometry of the learned manifold. Key open questions include whether MCNC scales to training LLMs from scratch (not tested due to compute constraints), whether the generator architecture can be further optimized, and whether the method's advantages persist at even larger model scales (e.g., 70B+ parameters).
