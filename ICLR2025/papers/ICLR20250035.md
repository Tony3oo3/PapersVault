## Problem

Distribution alignment methods have proven effective for imputing missing data in non-temporal datasets, but they perform poorly on time-series because existing discrepancy measures neither capture temporal patterns (periodicity, temporal dependencies) nor accommodate non-stationarity (multiple coexisting regimes). The paper addresses the open problem of designing a discrepancy measure that simultaneously encodes temporal structure and remains robust to non-stationary fluctuations, enabling alignment-based imputation to work effectively for time-series.

## Input / Output

The method takes an incomplete time-series matrix X^(obs) ∈ R^(N×D) and a binary mask matrix M ∈ {0,1}^(N×D) marking missing entries, and produces a fully imputed matrix X^(imp) ∈ R^(N×D). Missing entries are initialized with the average of their nearest observed time steps, then treated as learnable parameters updated via gradient descent. The method operates on temporal patches of tunable size T (typically 24–48) sampled in batches of size B (default 256), and requires no masking of observed entries or training of a parametric model on incomplete data.

## Method

The core contribution is the Proximal Spectrum Wasserstein (PSW) discrepancy, which combines two mechanisms: (1) a Pairwise Spectrum Distance (PSD) that applies the Discrete Fourier Transform to each temporal patch and computes the L1 distance between spectral representations, thereby encoding periodicity and temporal dependencies in the frequency domain; and (2) a Selective Matching Regularization (SMR) that replaces the hard marginal constraints of standard optimal transport with KL-divergence penalties against uniform simplex vectors, allowing flexible (partial) mass matching that avoids forced pairings across non-stationary regimes. The PSW-I framework iteratively samples two batches of patches, solves the PSW transport problem via a majorization-minimization algorithm (deliberately omitting entropic regularization to avoid its known negative effect on imputation), and backpropagates gradients through the spectral distance to update only the imputed entries. Theoretical results establish that PSW satisfies metric properties, is provably robust to outlier modes, and converges with a 1/(2ηK) error bound under standard convexity and smoothness assumptions.

## Results

PSW-I achieves the best MSE and MAE across all 10 datasets (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Electricity, Traffic, Weather, Illness, Exchange, PEMS03), averaged over missing ratios of 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, and 0.7. Headline MSE values include 0.126 on ETTh1, 0.046 on ETTh2, 0.021 on ETTm2, 0.106 on Electricity, and 0.049 on PEMS03. The largest relative gains appear on Illness (MSE 0.067 vs. best baseline SAITS at 0.167, a ~60% reduction) and Exchange (MSE 0.031 vs. best baseline iTransformer at 0.068, a ~54% reduction). Alignment-based baselines (Sinkhorn, TDM) perform dramatically worse, with MSE values typically 5–10× higher than PSW-I.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSW-I outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset for both MSE and MAE. Among predictive TSI methods, the strongest competitors are iTransformer (best on Exchange, MSE 0.068) and SCINet (competitive on ETTh1, MSE 0.149); PSW-I reduces MSE by 54% over iTransformer on Exchange (0.031 vs. 0.068) and by 15% over SCINet on ETTh1 (0.126 vs. 0.149). Among generative TSI methods, CSDI is the closest competitor on ETTh2 (MSE 0.098) and PEMS03 (MSE 0.115), but PSW-I still achieves 53% and 57% lower MSE respectively. The alignment-based baselines Sinkhorn and TDM, designed for non-temporal data, perform catastrophically on time-series (MSE typically 0.5–1.0), confirming that standard distribution alignment without temporal-aware discrepancy measures is unsuitable for TSI. The ablation (Table 2) further shows that removing either PSD or SMR degrades performance, with the full PSW-I achieving MSE 0.075 on Electricity vs. 0.116 without both components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distribution alignment paradigm for missing data imputation (Muzellec et al., 2020; Zhao et al., 2023) from non-temporal to time-series settings, where prior alignment methods fail due to their i.i.d. assumption. The key methodological departure is the Proximal Spectrum Wasserstein (PSW) discrepancy, which combines a DFT-based pairwise spectral distance to capture temporal patterns with a selective matching regularization (inspired by unbalanced OT, Chizat et al. 2018; Fatras et al. 2021) to handle non-stationarity. Unlike entropic unbalanced OT, PSW omits entropic regularization, which the authors argue hinders imputation quality, and instead solves the transport problem via majorization-minimization. The paper positions PSW-I as the first alignment-based method purpose-built for time-series imputation, contrasting with deep learning approaches (SAITS, CSDI, iTransformer) that require masking observed entries during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PSW-I offers a parametric-model-free alternative to deep learning TSI methods, eliminating the need for masking observed entries and the associated sample-efficiency loss at high missing ratios. The theoretical guarantees (metric properties, robustness to outlier modes, convergence bounds) provide a principled foundation absent in most empirical TSI work. However, the reliance on DFT limits the method to global frequency components, potentially missing local or transient patterns; the authors acknowledge wavelet-based extensions as future work. The curse of dimensionality in Wasserstein distance also constrains patch size, limiting scalability to very long-range temporal dependencies.
