## Problem

Existing LLM alignment benchmarks assume a uniform population-level preference, yet real users exhibit heterogeneous, idiosyncratic tastes over many high-quality responses. No public benchmark exists for studying how to personalize LLM outputs to individual users under the data sparsity that characterizes real deployments, where a new user provides only a handful of feedback signals and the system must leverage historical interactions from similar users.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252408/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 10,402 open-ended prompts (≤2400 characters) each paired with 8 high-quality responses from top LLMs (GPT-4o, Claude 3 Opus, Gemini 1.5 Pro, etc.) and 10 reward-model scores per (prompt, response) pair. In the personalization experiments, a new user's test prompt is combined with a short preference-feedback history (winning/losing response pairs) and a historical database of 10,000 simulated users (each with 50 prompt/feedback triplets); the system produces a single LLM response (via Llama3-8B-Instruct) scored by the user's weighted-ensemble reward model. Simulated users are instantiated by sampling B-dimensional weight vectors from a Dirichlet distribution over the 10 base reward models, so each user's reward for any (prompt, response) pair is a simple weighted sum.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part testbed: (1) a dataset of open-ended prompts with multiple high-quality responses where humans are expected to display diverse preferences (in contrast to RLHF datasets with one clearly superior answer), and (2) a method for simulating large, diverse user populations by sampling Dirichlet-distributed weightings over 10 strong open-source reward models, so each synthetic user's preference is a weighted ensemble. The paper validates that these ensembled users produce substantially more heterogeneous preferences than persona prompting (the top response wins only ~50% of prompts vs. ~90% for persona prompting) and achieves representativeness scores on OpinionQA (0.839) exceeding all individual LLMs. For personalization, two baselines are explored: personalized in-context learning with a relevant interaction history, and a meta-learning cold-start setting where users are embedded via the average direction between winning and losing response embeddings, enabling retrieval of similar historical users to inform ICL for a new user with no relevant history.

## Results

PersonalLLM simulated users produce significantly more heterogeneous preferences than persona prompting: the top response receives a majority vote for only ~50% of prompts (vs. ~90% for persona prompting), and at least 5 distinct responses are selected as best by at least one user for ~60% of prompts (vs. ~30%). On the OpinionQA validation set, the PersonalLLM population achieves a representativeness score of 0.839, exceeding all individual LLMs tested (text-davinci-003: 0.702–0.708; AI21 j1-jumbo: 0.792–0.828). In personalization experiments, ICL with winning-only examples outperforms all other in-context strategies, and meta-learning across 10,000 historical users beats the self-ICL baseline, though gains remain modest.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the persona prompting baseline (Castricato et al., 2024), PersonalLLM simulated users produce roughly twice the preference diversity: the most popular response wins a majority of votes on only ~50% of prompts versus ~90% for persona prompting, and at least 5 responses receive at least one vote on ~60% of prompts versus ~30%. Persona prompting also strongly concentrates preferences on a subset of 4 LLMs, while PersonalLLM users show a diffuse distribution across all 8 response-generating models. Against the OpinionQA LLM baselines (Santurkar et al., 2023), the PersonalLLM population achieves a representativeness score of 0.839 overall, outperforming text-davinci-003 (~0.70) by a wide margin and exceeding the best AI21 model (j1-jumbo, ~0.82) on 59 of 60 demographic subgroups. In the personalization experiments, the paper does not pit PersonalLLM against an external personalization method but rather establishes that ICL with winning-only examples is the strongest in-context strategy and that meta-learning across historical users provides a small but positive gain over self-ICL, highlighting substantial room for future methodological improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant RLHF alignment paradigm (Ouyang et al., 2022; Bai et al., 2022), which assumes a single uniform population-level preference, and instead formulates LLM personalization as adapting to individual latent preferences over open-ended prompts with many high-quality responses. It extends the simulated-reward paradigm from recommender systems (Ie et al., 2019; Zhao et al., 2023) to the LLM setting, and contrasts with the emerging persona-prompting approach (Castricato et al., 2024; Chan et al., 2024) by using weighted ensembles of reward models to simulate diverse users rather than prompting with demographic attributes. The meta-learning framing for the cold-start problem draws on transfer learning ideas but applies them to the novel challenge of embedding users based on natural-language preference feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PersonalLLM is the first public benchmark specifically designed to test LLM personalization algorithms against heterogeneous latent preferences, filling a gap where prior alignment datasets assume a single 'correct' response. The paper's baseline experiments reveal that even simple ICL and meta-learning approaches yield only modest personalization gains, highlighting a significant algorithmic gap that the field must address. Key open questions include the realism of reward-model-ensemble personas versus true human preferences, the scalability of personalization to millions of users without per-user model copies, and the safety risks (filter bubbles, manipulation) that accompany individualized adaptation. The authors explicitly position this as an initial step, analogous to ImageNet for vision, expecting the benchmark to drive methodological innovation.
