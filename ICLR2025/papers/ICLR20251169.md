## Problem

Existing 6D pose estimation methods require a known CAD model of the target object and are designed for controlled settings, making them unsuitable for Internet instructional videos where the exact mesh is unknown, capturing conditions are uncontrolled, and objects undergo fine-grained dynamic motions. No prior method has demonstrated extracting temporally consistent 6D object trajectories from such uncontrolled videos and transferring them to a robotic manipulator for imitation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video frames (no depth required) and a pre-built database of ~50,000 CAD models from Objaverse-LVIS and Google Scanned Objects, each pre-rendered in 600 views at 420×420 pixels. When camera intrinsics are unavailable, a fixed focal-length prior (53° FOV) is used. Outputs include per-frame 6D poses (SO(3) rotation + R³ translation), metric object scales in meters, and a torque-based joint trajectory for a 7-axis Franka Emika Panda robot. The entire pipeline is training-free, relying solely on pre-trained foundation models and an LLM-generated scale database.

## Method

The pipeline proceeds in four stages, all zero-shot. First, GroundingDINO + SAM 2 detect and segment objects in the input frame. Second, a CAD model is retrieved by matching a Foreground Feature Averaging (FFA) descriptor computed from DINOv2 layer-22 patch features of the segmented object against pre-computed FFA descriptors of 600 rendered views per database model. Third, absolute scale is estimated by combining relative scales from ZoeDepth monocular depth maps with metric size priors retrieved from a GPT-4-generated database of 2,200 object descriptions matched via CLIP features, with median aggregation across scene objects for robustness. Fourth, rotation is estimated by render-and-compare: the query patch features are dot-producted against all 600 pre-rendered view features of the retrieved mesh to find the best-matching rotation, and translation is derived from bounding-box geometry. For video, CoTracker 2D point tracks initialized from the first-frame pose are combined with PnP to produce temporally smooth trajectories, which are then retargeted to robot joint torques via PROXDDP trajectory optimization minimizing SE(3) tracking error, velocity, and torque costs.

## Results

On YCB-V, the method achieves AR=49.86, ARCoU=45.20, ARCH=18.53, and ARpCH=85.83, outperforming the strongest baseline FoundPose (AR=42.95, ARCoU=35.40, ARCH=15.69, ARpCH=77.75) by 6.9, 9.8, 2.8, and 8.1 points respectively. On HOPE-Video, the method reaches AR=45.98, ARCoU=39.21, ARCH=10.72, and ARpCH=88.01, again leading all baselines. For in-the-wild video tracking, the refined method reduces relative rotation error to 1.205° (vs. 6.82° for MegaPose and 7.74° for FoundPose) and projected translation error to 0.086 (vs. 0.161 and 0.405). All comparisons use retrieved meshes rather than ground-truth CAD models, simulating the in-the-wild setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all four baselines on every metric across both YCB-V and HOPE-Video. FoundPose (Ornek et al., ECCV 2024) is the strongest competitor, achieving AR=42.95 on YCB-V and AR=42.30 on HOPE-Video, but the proposed method leads by 6.9 and 3.7 AR points respectively, with the gap widening on ARCoU (9.8 and 8.0 points). MegaPose (Labbé et al., CoRL 2022) and its coarse variant lag significantly, with AR values of only 25.76 and 23.75 on YCB-V, indicating that render-and-compare methods designed for known meshes do not generalize to retrieved (imperfect) meshes. GigaPose (Nguyen et al., CVPR 2024) performs poorly on HOPE-Video (AR=23.12, ARCoU=4.15), suggesting its single-correspondence approach is particularly fragile when the retrieved mesh differs from the depicted object. OpenShape (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2024), used as a retrieval baseline in ablation, achieves only AR=34.51 versus 49.86 for the proposed FFA-based retrieval, a ~44% relative improvement in final pose quality. Notably, the proposed method requires no in-domain training or fine-tuning, whereas the baselines were designed for scenarios with precise known meshes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in 6D pose estimation (MegaPose, GigaPose, FoundPose) that assumes access to the exact CAD model of the target object, instead tackling the in-the-wild setting where no such model exists. It repurposes foundation-model features (DINOv2 patch tokens with Foreground Feature Averaging from Kotar et al.) for category-level mesh retrieval from large databases (Objaverse-LVIS, Google Scanned Objects), then combines this with render-and-compare alignment, LLM-informed scale estimation, and dense point tracking (CoTracker) for temporal consistency. The robot retargeting step leverages trajectory optimization (PROXDDP/Aligator) to map the extracted 6D object trajectory into the configuration space of a 7-axis manipulator, extending prior work on learning manipulation from video (Patel et al., Zorina et al.) that either required known models or was limited to stick-like objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a complete, training-free pipeline from uncontrolled internet video to real robot execution, opening a path toward leveraging the millions of instructional videos available online for robotic skill acquisition without per-object retraining. Key limitations include retrieval failures when the database lacks a geometrically similar mesh, scale estimation errors for objects with high size variation, and the assumption of a static camera. Open questions include how to handle dynamic camera motion more robustly, how to improve retrieval for objects with high intra-category geometric diversity, and whether the LLM-based scale prior can be replaced by more principled monocular geometry as depth estimation improves.
