## Problem

State-of-the-art neural audio codecs (e.g., DAC, EnCodec) rely on adversarial training to achieve perceptual quality, which is complex to tune, lacks interpretability, and is limited by discriminator quality. The prior non-adversarial alternative, ScoreDec, was restricted to speech at 24 kbit/s and required ~60 DNN evaluations (RTF 1.7), making it impractical for general audio or low-bitrate use. This paper asks whether a flow-based generative postfilter can match GAN-based codec quality for general full-band audio at much lower bitrates with far fewer inference steps.

## Input / Output

The codec accepts 48 kHz audio waveforms and produces reconstructed 48 kHz waveforms at bitrates from 0.75–7.5 kbit/s (75 Hz feature rate) or 0.25–4.0 kbit/s (25 Hz feature rate), using multi-codebook vector quantization (10 or 16 codebooks). The stochastic postfilter operates internally on amplitude-compressed complex STFT features (1534-sample Hann window, 384-sample hop, compression exponent α=0.3, 768 frequency bins) with the initial decoder output y provided as channel-wise concatenation conditioning. Inference requires only 6 DNN evaluations (Midpoint ODE solver, 3 steps), yielding a total RTF of ~0.23 on an A100 GPU.

## Method

FlowDec is a two-stage system: (1) a non-adversarial neural codec (NDAC) based on DAC with all GAN losses removed and replaced by multiscale CQT and L1 waveform losses to improve low-frequency modeling, and (2) a stochastic postfilter Ω that enhances the deterministic decoder output y via a novel Joint Flow Matching (JFM) objective. The key insight is a conditional probability path p_t(x_t|x_1, y) = N(y + t(x_1−y), (1−t)²Σ_y) that couples the noise x_0 to the target x_1 through y, eliminating the need for minibatch optimal transport solvers and reducing the formulation to a single hyperparameter σ_y (set via a 3-sigma quantile heuristic, optionally frequency-dependent). The target flow field simplifies to u_t = x_1 − x_0, which is contractive and numerically stable, and the loss L_JFM regresses the network v_θ(x_t, t, y) onto this target. The postfilter is a modified NCSN++ U-Net (26M parameters, 4 depths, no attention) trained with Adam at lr 10⁻⁴ for 800k iterations on 400k clean/distorted pairs spanning speech, music, and sound.

## Results

FlowDec-75m achieves the best FAD scores across all tested bitrates, reaching 2.09 at 7.5 kbit/s and 3.01 at 4.5 kbit/s, compared to DAC-75's 4.15 and 6.80 respectively. On the speech-only SIGMOS metric, FlowDec-75m scores 3.39 at 7.5 kbit/s versus DAC-75's 3.16. However, on distortion metrics (SI-SDR, fwSSNR, logSpecMSE), DAC generally outperforms FlowDec, consistent with the perception-distortion tradeoff. In MUSHRA listening tests, FlowDec and DAC receive statistically indistinguishable scores at all bitrates, and FlowDec achieves a real-time factor of 0.23 (vs. 1.71 for ScoreDec) using only 6 DNN evaluations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DAC (Kumar et al., 2024), FlowDec achieves substantially lower FAD (2.09 vs. 4.15 at 7.5 kbit/s; 3.01 vs. 6.80 at 4.5 kbit/s) and higher SIGMOS (3.39 vs. 3.16), but higher distortion on SI-SDR (6.12 vs. 9.23) and fwSSNR (15.56 vs. 16.21), reflecting the perception-distortion tradeoff; MUSHRA listening tests confirm no statistically significant quality difference between the two. Against EnCodec (Défossez et al., 2023), FlowDec-75m at 6.0 kbit/s achieves FAD 2.53 versus EnCodec's 7.35, a 2.9× improvement, while also outperforming on SIGMOS (3.40 vs. 2.41). Against MBD (San Roman et al., 2023) at 6 kbit/s, FlowDec dominates on every metric (FAD 2.53 vs. 16.87, SI-SDR 5.16 vs. −0.75). Against ScoreDec (Wu et al., 2024), FlowDec is dramatically superior at NFE=6 (FAD 1.62 vs. 145.30, SI-SDR 7.55 vs. −27.23) and still better at NFE=50 (FAD 1.34 vs. 5.73), while reducing inference cost by ~10×. Against FlowAVSE (Jung et al., 2024) with constant σ_t, FlowDec wins at NFE=6 (FAD 1.62 vs. 28.88–29.83) due to its contractive flow field. Opus (Valin et al., 2013) serves as the low anchor in listening tests and is rated substantially lower than all neural codec variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowDec extends the score-based postfilter paradigm of ScoreDec (Wu et al., 2024) by replacing score matching with a novel joint conditional flow matching formulation that couples the initial decoder output y directly into the probability path, eliminating the need for minibatch optimal transport solvers. It departs from the dominant GAN-based neural codec lineage (DAC, EnCodec, SoundStream) by removing adversarial training entirely and delegating perceptual quality to a stochastic flow-based postfilter, building on the theoretical foundations of Lipman et al. (2023) and the signal-enhancement lineage of SGMSE/SGMSE+ (Welker et al., 2022; Richter et al., 2023). The underlying codec is a modified DAC with adversarial losses removed and CQT/waveform losses added, while the postfilter uses a reconfigured NCSN++ backbone in an amplitude-compressed complex STFT domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlowDec demonstrates that adversarial training is not strictly necessary for high-perceptual-quality neural audio codecs, offering a more interpretable and stable alternative that achieves competitive FAD scores and on-par listening test results versus GAN-based DAC at 4.5–7.5 kbit/s. The 10× reduction in required DNN evaluations (60→6 NFE) brings real-time factor below 1, making the approach practically viable for streaming and interactive applications. Key limitations include the non-causal architecture (not streaming-capable), reliance on an image-oriented NCSN++ backbone, and the perception-distortion tradeoff where FlowDec sacrifices SI-SDR for better perceptual metrics. Open directions include causal/streaming variants, joint decoder-postfilter training, and audio-specific network architectures.
