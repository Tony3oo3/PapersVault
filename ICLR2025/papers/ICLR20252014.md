## Problem

Prior work identified arithmetic circuits in LLMs but did not explain the mechanism those circuits implement—specifically, whether LLMs solve arithmetic via robust generalizable algorithms or by memorizing input-output pairs. This gap prevents understanding the trade-off between generalization and memorization in arithmetic reasoning. The paper addresses this by reverse-engineering the arithmetic circuit at single-neuron resolution to determine what computational strategy the model actually uses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252014/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis consumes pre-trained LLM weights (Llama3-8B/70B, Pythia-6.9B, GPT-J) and two-operand arithmetic prompts of the form 'op1 op2 =' where both operands and the result must be single tokens (0–1000 for Llama3, 0–520 for GPT-J/Pythia). It produces a sparse arithmetic circuit (a handful of attention heads plus specific MLP neurons), a per-neuron heuristic type classification covering 91% of the top 3,200 neurons per operator, and a circuit faithfulness score (0.96 average for Llama3-8B). At the neuron level, only ~200 neurons per layer (~1.5%) are retained, and each is mapped to one or more heuristic types (range, modulo, pattern, identical-operand, multi-result).

## Method

The method proceeds in four stages. First, circuit discovery uses activation patching with counterfactual prompts to identify which MLPs and attention heads causally affect arithmetic completions, followed by linear probing to localize answer-writing to the final token position in mid-to-late layers (e.g., layers 16–32 in Llama3-8B). Second, the MLPs in those layers are decomposed into individual neurons via the key-value memory framework (Geva et al., 2021), and neuron-level activation patching reveals that a sparse subset (~200 per layer) suffices for high faithfulness. Third, each important neuron's activation pattern is plotted as a 2D heatmap over operand pairs, and its value vector is projected via Logit Lens to identify promoted result tokens; an automated matching algorithm then classifies each neuron into one or more heuristic types (range, modulo, pattern, identical-operand, multi-result) by checking whether the neuron's top-activating prompts overlap with the prompts expected by that heuristic. Fourth, two ablation experiments—knocking out all neurons of a given heuristic type, and knocking out prompt-specific associated neurons—demonstrate that the unordered combination of these heuristics (the 'bag of heuristics') is the causal mechanism producing correct answers, and a training-trajectory analysis on Pythia-6.9B checkpoints shows this mechanism emerges gradually from early training rather than replacing a prior algorithmic strategy.

## Results

The identified arithmetic circuit achieves a faithfulness of 0.96 on average across the four operators in Llama3-8B, meaning it explains 96% of the model's arithmetic behavior. Only ~200 neurons per layer (~1.5% of MLP neurons) in middle- and late layers are sufficient to maintain high circuit faithfulness. 91% of the 3,200 top neurons per operator are classified into identifiable heuristic types (range, modulo, pattern, identical-operand, multi-result). Ablating neurons of a specific heuristic type causes an average 29% accuracy drop on associated prompts (from 95% pre-ablation), while ablating the same number of random neurons from unassociated heuristics causes a much smaller drop. Model accuracies on single-token arithmetic prompts range from 0.95 (Llama3-8B) to 0.37 (GPT-J) average across operators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability study rather than a method comparison, so there are no direct baseline methods to benchmark against. The paper positions its findings relative to prior arithmetic circuit work: Stolfo et al. (2023) and Zhang et al. (2024) identified the circuit structure but did not explain the internal mechanism, while Zhou et al. (2024) showed Fourier-space features for addition in fine-tuned models. The authors argue their bag-of-heuristics mechanism is a more complete explanation, subsuming Fourier features as one component among many heuristic types. The key causal evidence comes from two ablation experiments: (1) knocking out all neurons of a heuristic type drops accuracy more on associated prompts than unassociated ones, and (2) prompt-guided knockout of associated heuristic neurons causes significantly larger accuracy drops than ablating the same number of random neurons from unassociated heuristics. Across all four models (Llama3-8B, Llama3-70B, Pythia-6.9B, GPT-J), the same qualitative pattern holds, with Llama3-70B showing the strongest knockout effect, suggesting a more refined bag of heuristics in larger, more recently trained models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the arithmetic circuit discovery work of Stolfo et al. (2023) and Zhang et al. (2024), which identified the high-level circuit structure (attention heads + MLPs) but stopped short of explaining the internal mechanism. It builds on the key-value memory framework of Geva et al. (2021) to zoom into individual MLP neurons, and on the faithfulness evaluation methodology of Wang et al. (2022) to quantify circuit completeness. The key departure is from Zhou et al. (2024), who found Fourier-space features for addition in fine-tuned models; this paper argues those features represent only a partial view, and that the full mechanism across all four operators is a heterogeneous collection of simple pattern-matching heuristics rather than a unified algorithmic computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LLMs solve arithmetic via a 'bag of heuristics'—neither robust algorithms nor pure memorization—reframes how we understand the source of LLM mathematical capabilities and their failure modes. The paper shows this mechanism emerges early in training and persists, suggesting that simply scaling or fine-tuning may not produce genuine algorithmic arithmetic; fundamental changes to training or architecture may be needed. Open questions include whether single-digit tokenization models exhibit different mechanisms, whether the human-defined heuristic taxonomy captures the full picture, and whether regularization could push models toward more algorithmic strategies.
