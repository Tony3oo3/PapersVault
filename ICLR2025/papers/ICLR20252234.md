## Problem

Existing video VAEs struggle to simultaneously achieve high spatial compression and temporal consistency across frames, and autoregressive or continuous-space diffusion approaches fail to efficiently model the high-dimensional discrete latent space of video. Additionally, no prior work addresses sketch-guided video inpainting, a task that combines structural sketch guidance with textual context for temporally coherent video completion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252234/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-video generation, the model takes a text prompt (up to 256 tokens, encoded by T5-XXL) and produces a video of up to 10 seconds at resolutions ranging from 256×256 to 640×480 with 16–64 frames. For sketch-guided video inpainting, the input is a masked video (where regions are zeroed via binary masks), a hand-drawn sketch (encoded by SigLIP), and a text prompt; the output is a fully reconstructed video with the masked regions filled in a temporally consistent manner. The 3D-MBQ-VAE compresses video into discrete tokens from a codebook of 12,800 entries with embedding dimension 8, achieving a 4× frame compression rate.

## Method

MotionAura combines a novel 3D-MBQ-VAE (using Mobile Inverted Bottleneck 3D convolutions and Lookup-Free Quantization) with a Spectral Transformer denoiser operating in discrete latent space. The VAE is pretrained with a full-frame masking strategy that adds a Masked Frame Index Loss (L_MFI) to the standard reconstruction/codebook/commitment losses, forcing the encoder to learn temporal ordering and improving frame consistency. The Spectral Transformer applies learnable 2D Fourier transforms to Q, K, and V projections before both self-attention and cross-attention, separating high- and low-frequency components to capture global spatial-temporal dependencies, and uses RoPE positional embeddings for longer context. Discrete diffusion corrupts tokens via progressive masking (forward) and the transformer predicts all masked tokens simultaneously (reverse), conditioned on text and time step via Adaptive Layer Normalization. For sketch-guided inpainting, the frozen denoiser is adapted with two LoRA modules (Attention LoRA on K/Q projections and Feed-Forward LoRA on the FFN layer), with sketch features from SigLIP concatenated to the token sequence.

## Results

On the primary text-to-video generation task (evaluated on MSR-VTT with WebVid-10M-recaptioned training), MotionAura-L (3.12B params) achieves FVD 344, CLIPSIM 0.2822, Motion Quality 76.62, and Temporal Consistency 207, outperforming CogVideoX-5B (FVD 357, CLIPSIM 0.2429) while being faster (38s vs 41s inference). For the 3D-MBQ-VAE reconstruction task, the model achieves PSNR 32.1 / SSIM 0.858 / LPIPS 0.108 on WebVid-Val, surpassing CV-VAE (28.6/0.819/0.145) and VAE-SD2.1 (28.9/0.810/0.145). On the novel sketch-guided video inpainting task (YouTube-VOS), MotionAura-L reaches FVD 657 and CLIPSIM 0.3511, beating CogVideoX-5B (FVD 677, CLIPSIM 0.2602). For video compression on MCL-JCV at 0.0384 bpp, 3D-MBQ-VAE achieves the best LPIPS (0.089) among all methods including VVC (0.153) and MAGVIT-v2 (0.104), though it trails VVC on PSNR (29.09 vs 32.65).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionAura-L outperforms all three video generation baselines (SimDA, AnimateDiff, CogVideoX-5B) on every metric in Table 3, with the largest margin over SimDA (FVD 344 vs 433, CLIPSIM 0.2822 vs 0.1992) and a consistent but smaller lead over CogVideoX-5B (FVD 344 vs 357, CLIPSIM 0.2822 vs 0.2429) despite using fewer parameters (3.12B vs 5.00B) and faster inference (38s vs 41s). For VAE reconstruction (Table 1), 3D-MBQ-VAE beats all five baselines (VAE-SD2.1, VQGAN, TATS, VAE-OSP, CV-VAE) on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, with the closest competitor CV-VAE(3D) trailing by 3.5 dB in PSNR and 0.037 in LPIPS on WebVid-Val. In video compression (Table 9), 3D-MBQ-VAE achieves the best perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.089) but loses to VVC on PSNR (29.09 vs 32.65) and SSIM (0.922 vs 0.966), while clearly surpassing MAGVIT-v2 on all three metrics. For sketch-guided inpainting (Table 4), MotionAura-L leads CogVideoX-5B by 20 FVD points and 0.09 CLIPSIM on YouTube-VOS, and the ablation without sketch input (MotionAura-L w/o sketch) drops to CLIPSIM 0.3061, confirming the sketch conditioning's contribution. The paper also shows that removing the FFT layers from MotionAura-L degrades FVD from 344 to 351 and CLIPSIM from 0.2822 to 0.2441, validating the Spectral Transformer design.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionAura sits at the intersection of discrete representation learning (VQ-VAE, MAGVIT/MAGVIT-v2) and diffusion-based video generation, extending the VQ-Diffusion paradigm from images to video via a novel 3D-MBQ-VAE tokenizer that incorporates full-frame masking for temporal consistency. The Spectral Transformer denoiser departs from standard transformer blocks by inserting learnable 2D FFT layers into self- and cross-attention (building on the authors' prior GAFNet work) and replacing sinusoidal embeddings with RoPE, while the downstream sketch-guided inpainting task repurposes LoRA adapters in a manner analogous to their use in language models. The paper also draws on masked autoencoder training strategies (He et al., 2021) and the Lookup-Free Quantization scheme from MAGVIT-v2, but the specific combination of discrete diffusion + spectral attention + full-frame masking loss is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent pain points in video generation: temporal inconsistency (tackled via the L_MFI loss and full-frame masking during VAE pretraining) and the high computational cost of continuous-space diffusion (addressed by moving to a discrete token space with only 30 diffusion steps). The introduction of sketch-guided video inpainting as a formal task, along with the curated YouTube-VOS/DAVIS-Sketch datasets, opens a new direction for user-controllable video editing. However, the evaluation is limited to a small set of baselines (SimDA, AnimateDiff, CogVideoX-5B), the 38-second inference time for a 5-second video remains impractical for real-time use, and the SOTA margins over CogVideoX-5B are modest, leaving open whether the spectral transformer advantage persists at larger scale or with more recent baselines.
