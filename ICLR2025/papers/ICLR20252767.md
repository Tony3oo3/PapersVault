## Problem

Existing NeRF-based camera pose refinement methods (e.g., NeFeS, CrossFire) suffer from slow iterative convergence, limited accuracy gains over their base estimators, and the need to train scene-specific feature descriptors for 2D-3D matching. This paper asks whether a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation combined with a pre-trained 3D foundation model can enable a single-shot, descriptor-free refinement that is both faster and more accurate than iterative NeRF-based pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252767/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB query image (resized to 512 px on the largest dimension), its camera intrinsics, a coarse 6-DoF pose from any black-box APR or SCR estimator, and a per-scene pre-trained Scaffold-GS model. It outputs a refined 6-DoF pose in one forward pass with no backpropagation through the pose estimator or the 3DGS model. A faster variant (GS-CPR^rel) skips explicit 2D-3D matching and instead recovers scale by aligning MASt3R point maps with the rendered depth, trading some accuracy for ~71 ms overhead instead of ~180 ms.

## Method

GS-CPR renders both a color image and a depth map from the pre-trained 3DGS model at the initial pose, applying an exposure-adaptive Affine Color Transformation (a 4-layer MLP conditioned on the query's luminance histogram) to close the sim-to-real photometric gap. A pre-trained MASt3R matcher then establishes dense 2D-2D correspondences between the query and rendered images, which are lifted to 2D-3D matches via the rendered depth map and camera intrinsics. The refined pose is obtained by a single PnP + RANSAC solve, requiring no gradient-based optimization and no scene-specific feature training. The key insight is that 3DGS provides metric depth for scale recovery while MASt3R handles the sim-to-real matching, eliminating the need for the iterative render-and-compare loops or learned descriptors that characterize prior NeRF-based refiners.

## Results

On 7Scenes, ACE+GS-CPR achieves a median translation/rotation error of 0.8 cm / 0.25°, outperforming the SCR baseline ACE (1.1/0.34) and all NeRF-based refinement methods including DFNet+NeFeS50 (2.4/0.79), CrossFire (4.4/1.38), and MCLoc (4.1/1.43). On Cambridge Landmarks, DFNet+GS-CPR reduces the median error from 119/2.90° to 26/0.51° in a single shot, surpassing DFNet+NeFeS50 (35/0.77°) which requires 50 optimization iterations. On 12Scenes, ACE+GS-CPR achieves 98.7% recall at the stringent (2 cm, 2°) threshold versus 97.2% for ACE alone. Runtime is under 0.2 s per query (vs. 10 s for NeFeS50 and 2.4 s for MCLoc).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS-CPR consistently outperforms all NeRF-based refinement methods: on 7Scenes, ACE+GS-CPR (0.8/0.25°) beats DFNet+NeFeS50 (2.4/0.79°), HR-APR (2.4/0.85°), CrossFire (4.4/1.38°), NeRFMatch (2.8/0.7°), and MCLoc (4.1/1.43°) by large margins, while also being 50–100× faster (0.19 s vs. 10 s for NeFeS50). Against SCR baselines, ACE+GS-CPR improves upon ACE (1.1/0.34° → 0.8/0.25°) and surpasses both DSAC* (1.1/0.34°) and GLACE (1.2/0.36°), achieving the best overall accuracy on 7Scenes. On Cambridge Landmarks, DFNet+GS-CPR (26/0.51°) outperforms DFNet+NeFeS50 (35/0.77°) and CrossFire (37/1.0°) in a single shot, and ACE+GS-CPR (15/0.33°) beats LENS (39/1.15°) and PMNet (90/2.27°). The method also surpasses image-retrieval-based HLoc (95.7% at 5 cm/5°) with ACE+GS-CPR reaching 100% on 7Scenes, and improves the (2 cm, 2°) recall from 83.3% (ACE) to 93.1% (ACE+GS-CPR). The faster variant GS-CPR^rel trades some accuracy (e.g., 2.7/0.38° vs. 1.1/0.34° for DFNet on 7Scenes) for a 2.5× speedup, making it suitable for latency-constrained APR refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

GS-CPR sits at the intersection of 3DGS-based scene representation and test-time pose refinement, departing from the dominant NeRF-based iterative refinement paradigm (NeFeS, iNeRF, HR-APR) by replacing slow render-and-compare loops with a single 3DGS rendering pass followed by PnP+RANSAC. It also diverges from NeRF-based matching methods like CrossFire and NeRFMatch, which require training scene-specific feature descriptors, by leveraging the pre-trained 3D foundation model MASt3R for direct RGB 2D-2D matching. The exposure-adaptive ACT module is borrowed from NeFeS but repurposed within the 3DGS rendering pipeline, and the overall architecture draws on Scaffold-GS for structured Gaussian representation. Conceptually, the paper reframes pose refinement as a one-shot 2D-3D correspondence problem solvable via classical geometry, rather than an iterative optimization problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: GS-CPR provides a plug-in refinement module that adds under 200 ms to any black-box pose estimator without requiring additional training, making it immediately deployable in robotics and AR/VR pipelines. The finding that ACE+GS-CPR outperforms the traditional HLoc pipeline (SuperPoint+SuperGlue) on 7Scenes suggests 3DGS rendering could eventually replace sparse point clouds and reference image databases in visual localization. Key limitations include dependence on initial pose quality (insufficient overlap for very large errors) and the challenge of reconstructing high-quality 3DGS models for large-scale outdoor scenes, which remains an open problem for broader deployment.
