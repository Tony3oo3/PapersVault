## Problem

Autoregressive image models (e.g., VAR, LlamaGen) generate images token-by-token, requiring 10–256 sequential steps and making them inherently slow. Existing acceleration methods that generate multiple tokens in parallel fundamentally cannot match the AR model's output distribution because they assume conditional independence between tokens, causing catastrophic quality degradation (FID >100) at one step. The paper asks whether a pre-trained AR model can be distilled to produce full images in one or two steps while preserving the original distribution.

## Input / Output

The distilled model takes a sequence of noise tokens (sampled from an isotropic Gaussian, one per token position) plus a conditioning signal (class label for ImageNet-256 or a text prompt for LAION-COCO) and outputs the complete token sequence in a single forward pass. For VAR, tokens are ordered by resolution scale (10 groups); for LlamaGen, tokens follow raster order over a 16×16 latent grid (256 positions). The output token sequence is decoded back to a 256×256 RGB image via the pre-trained VQ decoder. The method supports flexible step counts (1, 2, or more) by selecting different intermediate timesteps along the trajectory.

## Method

The key mechanism is constructing a deterministic trajectory from pure Gaussian noise to the AR model's output using flow matching at each token position: the AR model's conditional distribution (a mixture of Dirac deltas over the codebook) is connected to a Gaussian prior via a linear-interpolation flow, yielding a unique mapping from each noise token to a data token. Iterating this across all positions produces a full AR trajectory from noise to data. A neural network (initialized from the pre-trained AR model with modified heads for logits and embedding prediction) is then trained to predict the final data sequence from any intermediate point on this trajectory, using a combined cross-entropy and LPIPS loss. Crucially, training requires only noise-data pairs generated by the teacher AR model, not the original training dataset. At inference, the model can jump directly from noise to data (one step) or use an intermediate checkpoint (two steps), and can optionally insert the pre-trained AR model for partial re-generation to trade speed for quality.

## Results

On ImageNet-256, DD achieves one-step generation for VAR (FID 9.94, 6.3× speedup from 10 steps) and LlamaGen (FID 11.35, 217.8× speedup from 256 steps), with two-step variants reaching FID 7.82 and 7.58 respectively. Baseline methods (skip-n and onestep*) completely fail at one-step generation, producing FID >100 for both models. On text-to-image (LAION-COCO), DD reduces LlamaGen from 256 steps to 2 with only a modest FID increase from 25.70 to 28.95 (92.9× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DD decisively outperforms both designed baselines (skip-n and onestep*) at extreme step reductions: while VAR-onestep* and LlamaGen-onestep* produce FID 157.5 and 220.2 respectively, DD achieves FID 9.94 and 11.35 at the same one-step setting. The skip-n baselines also degrade rapidly (VAR-skip-2 reaches FID 40.09; LlamaGen-skip-156 reaches FID 80.72), confirming the theoretical impossibility of parallel token prediction for few-step generation. Against diffusion models, DD's two-step LlamaGen (FID 7.58, 0.043s) is faster than DiT-L/2 (FID 5.02, 31s) and LDM-4-G (FID 3.60, 250 steps) by 3–700×, though at a modest FID cost. Compared to other AR models, DD's one-step VAR (FID 9.94) is competitive with RQTran. (FID 7.55, 68 steps) and VQGAN (FID 15.78, 256 steps) while being orders of magnitude faster. On text-to-image, DD-2step (FID 28.95) stays close to the 256-step LlamaGen teacher (FID 25.70) with a 92.9× speedup, demonstrating that the quality gap remains small even in the more challenging conditional generation setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the flow matching framework (Liu et al. 2022; Lipman et al. 2022) to construct deterministic trajectories from Gaussian noise to the output distribution of a pre-trained AR model, then distills that mapping via a neural network. It departs fundamentally from prior AR acceleration methods (speculative decoding, parallel/multi-token prediction, mask-based AR) by showing they cannot preserve the full output distribution in few steps due to conditional dependencies between tokens. Conceptually, it adapts the knowledge distillation paradigm from diffusion models (progressive distillation, consistency models) to AR, with the key innovation being that AR models lack natural deterministic trajectories, so flow matching must be used to synthesize them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that image AR models can be distilled to 1–2 step generation (217.8× speedup for LlamaGen) with acceptable quality loss, challenging the prevailing assumption that AR models are inherently slow and that inference scaling (more steps = better) is the right axis for improvement. The method is practical because it does not require the original training data, only the pre-trained teacher. Open questions include whether DD extends to LLMs (much larger codebooks, longer sequences), whether teacher-free variants are possible, and where the true optimal trade-off between inference compute and quality lies for AR models.
