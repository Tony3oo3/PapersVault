## Problem

Existing automatic evaluation metrics for generation tasks (BLEU, BERTScore, ROUGE, COMET, etc.) each capture only a subset of human preference dimensions, and no single metric consistently aligns with human judgments across all quality aspects. There is no systematic, supervised framework to identify which combination of metrics best reflects human preferences for a given task, modality, or language setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253262/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of N pre-computed metric scores (from n-gram, embedding-based, and LLM-based metrics) evaluated on hypothesis–reference pairs, or image–caption pairs for vision-language tasks, along with human-annotated preference scores (e.g., Likert-scale ratings for coherence, fluency, relevance, or MQM error annotations) used as calibration targets. Each metric score is clipped to its valid range, normalized to [0, 1], and inverted if higher values indicate worse quality before combination. The output is a single scalar meta-metric score that can be used as a ranking signal, reward model score, or evaluation metric in both reference-based and reference-free settings.

## Method

METAMETRICS learns a combination function Φ over normalized metric scores to maximize a correlation objective (Kendall τ or Spearman ρ) with human preference scores, using two complementary optimizers. Bayesian Optimization with a Gaussian Process (Matérn kernel, ν=2.5) searches over linear weight vectors w to find the sparse, interpretable combination that best aligns with human judgments, naturally dropping redundant or uninformative metrics. XGBoost learns non-linear interactions among metrics and is paired with an iterative pruning procedure that removes the least feature-important metric at each of k rounds, retaining the model with the best cross-validated correlation. The linear weighting scheme is shown to outperform multiplicative (pairwise-product) weighting, and the resulting meta-metric is embarrassingly parallel at inference since each constituent metric is computed independently.

## Results

On SummEval, METAMETRICS-SUM (XGBoost) achieves a Kendall τ average of 0.210 across coherence, consistency, fluency, and relevance, outperforming the best non-LLM baseline (BLEURT at 0.140) by 0.070; when LLM-based metrics are included, it reaches 0.433, surpassing G-Eval (GPT-4) at 0.422. On WMT23 (MQM), METAMETRICS-MT (XGBoost) achieves an overall system/segment average correlation of 0.825, matching XCOMET-Ensemble and exceeding all other reference-based metrics. On the combined QA benchmark, METAMETRICS-QA (XGBoost) reaches 0.536 Kendall τ, beating the best single metric (METEOR at 0.494) by 0.042. As a reward model on RewardBench, METAMETRICS-RM (GP) scores 93.5% overall, beating the 70B Nemotron reward model on Chat (98.9% vs. 97.5%) and Reasoning (98.2% vs. 98.1%) while using a fraction of the compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SummEval, METAMETRICS-SUM (XGBoost) outperforms all n-gram and embedding-based baselines (BLEU 0.130, BERTScore 0.127, BLEURT 0.140) by 0.06–0.08 in Kendall τ average, and also beats the uniform and weighted-average ensemble baselines (0.151, 0.159). When LLM-based metrics are included, METAMETRICS-SUM (GP) surpasses G-Eval (GPT-4) by 0.011 in Kendall average (0.433 vs. 0.422) and UniEval by 0.071 (0.433 vs. 0.362). On WMT23, METAMETRICS-MT (XGBoost) ties XCOMET-Ensemble on overall correlation (0.825) but exceeds it on en-de segment Pearson (0.957 vs. 0.950) and he-en segment accuracy (0.557 vs. 0.556); it also outperforms COMET (0.779) and MetricX-23-XXL (0.808) by 0.016–0.046. On the combined QA benchmark, METAMETRICS-QA (XGBoost) beats METEOR (0.494) by 0.042 and the weighted-average ensemble (0.484) by 0.052. On RewardBench, METAMETRICS-RM (GP) achieves 93.5% overall, narrowly trailing the 70B Nemotron reward model (94.1%) but exceeding it on Chat (98.9% vs. 97.5%) and Reasoning (98.2% vs. 98.1%), while the XGBoost variant leads on Chat Hard (89.7% vs. 85.7%) and Safety (92.2% vs. 95.1% — actually loses here). On image captioning, METAMETRICS-CAP outperforms all individual metrics including CLIPScore and BLEU, and maintains this advantage even in cross-dataset transfer settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

METAMETRICS repurposes well-established optimization techniques—Gaussian Process-based Bayesian Optimization and XGBoost with iterative pruning—for the novel purpose of learning optimal metric combinations calibrated to human preference scores. It departs from prior ensemble-based evaluation approaches (uniform averaging, heuristic weighted combinations) by treating metric weighting as a supervised optimization problem where the objective is correlation with human judgments. The paper positions itself against the long lineage of individual automatic metrics (BLEU, BERTScore, COMET, G-Eval) and LLM-based reward models, arguing that no single metric captures all dimensions of human preference, and that the right combination varies by task and domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

METAMETRICS offers a practical, lightweight, and interpretable framework for calibrating evaluation metrics that is modality- and language-agnostic, potentially reducing reliance on expensive LLM-based evaluators. The finding that a GP-calibrated combination of existing metrics matches or exceeds 70B-parameter reward models on RewardBench is practically significant for resource-constrained settings. Key limitations include the requirement for human-annotated calibration data, exclusion of LLMs above 10.7B parameters due to compute constraints, and the fact that improvements over strong individual metrics (e.g., XCOMET-Ensemble in MT) are often modest. Open questions include integration with RLHF/PPO training loops, extension to speech and video modalities, and whether the calibration transfers across substantially different task distributions without re-tuning.
