## Problem

Existing counterfactual fairness methods assume sensitive attributes are exogenous (not causally influenced by other variables) and fix the causal path from the sensitive attribute to the target, overlooking intrinsic information embedded in sensitive features and limiting real-world applicability. This paper asks how to extract essential, non-discriminatory information from sensitive attributes while providing a tunable mechanism to balance fairness against predictive accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250966/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular data with non-sensitive attributes X and a sensitive attribute S (e.g., race, gender). Rather than feeding S directly into the predictor, EXOC infers a latent auxiliary node S' from the observed data and produces both a predicted target Y-hat (via logistic regression on a latent K) and an inferred sensitive attribute S-hat derived from the approximate posterior q(S'|O). A second latent control node S'' is deduced as a descendant of Y and is used only during training to regulate information flow.

## Method

EXOC modifies the Fair-K causal graph by replacing the direct S->Y edge with a fork S<-S'->Y, where S' is an auxiliary node that instantiates the exogenous variable U and captures intrinsic latent information shared by X, Y, and S. A control node S'' (a descendant of Y) is coupled to S' through a custom L2 loss L_c(S',S''), and the overall training objective is L = L_ELBO + gamma * R(L_c), where gamma tunes the fairness-accuracy tradeoff: larger gamma tightens the S'-S'' constraint, reducing the S->Y information flow and improving counterfactual fairness at the cost of accuracy. The ELBO term is optimized via a variational guide q(Z|O) over the latent set Z={K, S', S''}. Theoretically, the paper shows via a Three-Sigma bound that the counterfactual parity in EXOC (delta_b) is strictly tighter than in Fair-K (delta_a) because the fixed alpha(s*-s) term is eliminated, and path analysis demonstrates that the composite causal intensity along the bypass path can be minimized to decouple S from Y.

## Results

On synthetic datasets, EXOC achieves the best performance across all metrics on both Law School (RMSE 0.874, MAE 0.702, MMD 3.824, Wass 3.590) and Adult (Accuracy 76.0%, MMD 2.958, Wass 1.428). On real-world datasets, EXOC similarly leads among counterfactual-aware methods, achieving RMSE 0.902 and MMD 4.739 on Law School, and Accuracy 74.8% and MMD 3.891 on Adult. The ablation on γ shows that γ=1.2 provides the best fairness-accuracy tradeoff, with fairness metrics improving monotonically as γ increases from 1 to 2 while accuracy degrades only modestly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EXOC outperforms both counterfactual-aware baselines (Fair-K and CLAIRE) on every metric across both datasets. On Law School (synthetic), EXOC reduces MMD from 4.313 (Fair-K) and 6.717 (CLAIRE) to 3.824, while also improving RMSE from 0.894/0.897 to 0.874. On Adult (synthetic), EXOC achieves 76.0% accuracy versus 74.5% (Fair-K) and 74.8% (CLAIRE), with MMD of 2.958 versus 3.597 and 4.760 respectively. Against the counterfactual-unaware baselines (Full, Unaware), EXOC achieves dramatically better fairness (e.g., MMD 3.824 vs. 40.256 for Unaware on Law School) at only a small accuracy cost. The Constant predictor is trivially fair (MMD=0) but has the worst accuracy, confirming that EXOC meaningfully balances both objectives. On real-world data, the same ranking holds, though absolute fairness gaps narrow, demonstrating robustness to distributional shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

EXOC extends the Fair-K causal model (Kusner et al., 2017) by challenging its core assumption that sensitive attributes are exogenous and uninfluenced by other variables. Rather than treating the exogenous variable U as an unmodeled background, EXOC instantiates it into two concrete nodes—an auxiliary node S′ that captures intrinsic latent information shared across S, X, and Y, and a control node S″ that regulates information flow via a custom L2 loss. This positions EXOC as a departure from both Fair-K's fixed causal structure and CLAIRE's (Ma et al., 2023) augmentation-based approach, which is agnostic to causal graphs, by explicitly modeling how sensitive attributes are partially determined by shared latent causes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a tunable mechanism (γ) for the fairness-accuracy tradeoff, which is practically valuable in deployment scenarios where strict counterfactual fairness may be too costly. However, the evaluation is limited to two tabular datasets (Law school, Adult) and simple predictors (logistic/linear regression), leaving open whether the auxiliary-node architecture scales to deep models or high-dimensional settings. The theoretical analysis in the simplified case relies on normality and ideal linear assumptions, and the connection between KL divergence and causal function intensity, while intuitive, lacks a rigorous proof. Future work on scaling to complex architectures and validating the information-flow control in high-dimensional regimes would strengthen the contribution.
