## Problem

While universal text embedding models have matured (e.g., MTEB), progress in universal multimodal embeddings has been comparatively slow, with existing models like CLIP, BLIP, and SigLIP encoding text and images independently or via shallow fusion, limiting their ability to capture deep cross-modal relationships and generalize across diverse tasks. Additionally, no comprehensive benchmark existed to systematically evaluate multimodal embedding models across multiple task categories and modality combinations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250052/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

VLM2VEC accepts any combination of one or more images and text on both the query and target sides, prefixed with a task-specific instruction (e.g., 'Represent the given image for classification'). Images are processed at either 1344×1344 (high-res) or 336×336 (low-res) resolution, with Phi-3.5-V additionally using 4 sub-image crops; text is truncated to 256 tokens. The output is a single fixed-dimensional vector extracted from the last token's final-layer hidden state, which is then scored against 1,000 candidate embeddings via temperature-scaled cosine similarity (τ=0.02) in a ranking formulation.

## Method

VLM2VEC converts a pretrained VLM (Phi-3.5-V, LLaVA-1.6, or Qwen2-VL) into an embedding model by appending a task instruction to the query and training with the InfoNCE contrastive loss over in-batch negatives, where the embedding is the last-token hidden state of the VLM. Unlike CLIP-based approaches that use score-level or feature-level fusion, VLM2VEC leverages the transformer's deep integration of vision and language tokens, enabling it to handle arbitrary image-text combinations and follow instructions for task-specific behavior. To overcome GPU memory constraints and increase the number of in-batch negatives to 1,024, the authors apply GradCache, which decouples the contrastive loss backpropagation from the encoder by caching per-sample gradients across sub-batches. LoRA (rank 8) fine-tuning outperforms full fine-tuning, and training on the 20 in-distribution MMEB datasets spanning four meta-tasks (classification, VQA, retrieval, visual grounding) yields strong zero-shot transfer to 16 out-of-distribution datasets, with the Qwen2-VL + LoRA + high-res configuration achieving 65.8% average Precision@1 across all 36 tasks.

## Results

The best VLM2VEC variant (Qwen2-VL backbone, LoRA, 1344×1344 resolution) achieves 65.8% Precision@1 across all 36 MMEB datasets, with 72.2% on in-distribution tasks and 57.8% on 16 out-of-distribution tasks in zero-shot evaluation. Per meta-task, it scores 62.6 (Classification), 57.8 (VQA), 69.9 (Retrieval), and 81.7 (Visual Grounding). On Flickr30K zero-shot retrieval, VLM2VEC (LLaVA-1.6) achieves 80.3 R@1 for text-to-image and 94.6 R@1 for image-to-text, surpassing all CLIP-like baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLM2VEC (Qwen2-VL, LoRA, high-res) outperforms all 8 baselines by large margins across every meta-task. Against the strongest no-fine-tuning baseline, UniIR (CLIP_SF) at 44.7 overall, VLM2VEC gains +21.1 points; against the strongest fine-tuned baseline, OpenCLIP at 47.2, it gains +18.6 points. The most dramatic gap is in VQA, where baselines score only 4–22% (e.g., BLIP2 at 4.2%, UniIR at 16.2%) while VLM2VEC reaches 57.8%, reflecting the deep cross-modal fusion advantage over CLIP-style shallow fusion. On Visual Grounding, VLM2VEC (81.7%) also substantially exceeds the best baseline (UniIR at 65.3%). On standard Flickr30K retrieval, VLM2VEC (LLaVA-1.6) achieves 80.3 R@1 (T2I) and 94.6 R@1 (I2T), beating EVA-02-CLIP-L/14 (77.3/89.7) and MagicLens-L (79.7/89.6), confirming that the multimodal embedding capability does not come at the cost of conventional retrieval performance. E5-V, which trains exclusively on text pairs, performs worst among all baselines (13.3 overall), underscoring the importance of multimodal training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant dual-encoder paradigm (CLIP, BLIP, SigLIP) and shallow-fusion approaches (UniIR, MagicLens) by repurposing pre-trained VLMs (Phi-3.5-V, LLaVA-1.6, Qwen2-VL) as unified embedding backbones where vision and language features are deeply fused within a single transformer. It draws conceptual inspiration from MTEB's universal text-embedding benchmark, extending that philosophy to the multimodal setting with MMEB, and from instruction-tuned text embedders (InstructOR, E5-Mistral) by incorporating task-specific instructions into the embedding process. The closest prior work, E5-V, also uses a VLM backbone but trains exclusively on text pairs, whereas VLM2VEC trains on diverse multimodal query-target combinations, representing a meaningful methodological departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMEB fills a critical gap as the first comprehensive, multi-task multimodal embedding benchmark analogous to MTEB for text, and is likely to become the standard evaluation suite for the field. The finding that a VLM backbone with contrastive training and LoRA outperforms purpose-built dual-encoder architectures by 18–21 points challenges the assumption that shallow-fusion CLIP-style models are the natural choice for multimodal embeddings. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video and longer-context multimodal inputs, whether the 1,000-candidate evaluation protocol will saturate as models improve, and whether the strong OOD generalization (57.8% on unseen tasks) holds as the benchmark grows. The paper's reliance on 8 H100 GPUs and 2K training steps also raises practical accessibility questions for smaller research groups.
