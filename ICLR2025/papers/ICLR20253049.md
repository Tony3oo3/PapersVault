## Problem

The Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH) posits that all representations in pretrained language models lie along one-dimensional lines, yet prior work on toy models has found circular and lattice structures that cannot be decomposed into independent 1D features. No rigorous definition of when a multi-dimensional representation is truly irreducible (i.e., not a sum of separable or non-co-occurring lower-dimensional features) existed, and no scalable method was available to automatically discover such features in large pretrained models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes SAE dictionary elements and hidden-state activations from GPT-2-small (all layers, using Bloom's pre-trained SAEs) and Mistral 7B (layers 8, 16, 24, with 65,536 dictionary elements trained on 1B+ tokens from Pile and Alpaca). It produces PCA-projected 2D point clouds scored by a separability index (minimum mutual information over 1000 rotation angles) and an ε-mixture index (maximum fraction of points within an ε-band of a learned hyperplane, optimized via gradient descent with sigmoid relaxation). For causal validation, the method takes hidden states at the α token position across 49 weekday and 144 month prompts, applies circular subspace interventions, and outputs average logit differences between the original and target answer tokens.

## Method

The paper defines irreducible multi-dimensional features as those that cannot be made separable (factorizable joint distribution) or a mixture (disjoint support with a lower-dimensional component) under any affine transformation, and operationalizes this via two empirical indices: a separability index computed by sweeping 1000 rotation angles and estimating mutual information on a 40×40 binned grid, and an ε-mixture index found by gradient descent over a (v, c) parameterization of a hyperplane band. To discover features at scale, the pipeline trains SAEs, clusters dictionary elements by pairwise cosine similarity (spectral clustering for GPT-2, k-nearest-neighbor graph with threshold pruning for Mistral), reconstructs activations using only each cluster's dictionary elements, and scores the resulting PCA projections with both irreducibility indices. Causal validation uses a custom circular subspace patching technique: a linear probe maps top-5 PCA components to a unit circle parameterized by the day/month index, then interventions replace the circular component with a target angle while average-ablating all other dimensions, isolating the circular subspace's causal contribution to the model's modular arithmetic output.

## Results

On modular arithmetic tasks, Mistral 7B achieves 31/49 (63%) on Weekdays and 125/144 (87%) on Months, while Llama 3 8B achieves 29/49 (59%) on Weekdays and 143/144 (99%) on Months; GPT-2, despite having circular representations, achieves only trivial accuracy (8/49 and 10/144). Circular subspace interventions on the α token nearly match full-layer patching performance across early-to-mid layers, and outperform patching only the top 5 PCA dimensions. The SAE-discovered circular probe on layer 8 of Mistral 7B achieves an average logit difference of −2.01, only slightly below the −2.58 from a layer-specific circular probe, while being far more robust to layer shifts (−2.32 at layer 6 vs. 0.029 for the circular probe).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three intervention strategies on the circular α representation: (1) circular subspace patching (the proposed method), (2) patching the top 5 PCA dimensions from a clean run, and (3) patching the entire layer. Across all models and tasks, circular subspace interventions in early-to-mid layers achieve nearly the same effect as full-layer patching and consistently outperform PCA-only patching (Fig. 6). For probe construction, the SAE-discovered circular plane (from clustering SAE dictionary elements) is compared against a manually trained circular probe on PCA projections: the SAE probe yields a slightly lower average logit difference (−2.01 vs. −2.58 on layer 8 of Mistral) but is dramatically more robust to layer shifts, achieving −2.32 at layer 6 where the circular probe collapses to 0.029. Off-distribution grid interventions (sweeping radius r and angle θ) confirm that the model encodes α in the angular coordinate of the circle, with the radius acting as an intensity dimension. GPT-2, despite exhibiting the same circular geometry in its SAE features, fails to use them causally for the arithmetic tasks, suggesting that the presence of a representation is necessary but not sufficient for the corresponding computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected research threads: toy-model interpretability work that found multi-dimensional representations (circles for modular arithmetic in Liu et al. 2022, Nanda et al. 2023a) and large-LLM feature discovery work that assumed all features are 1D linear directions (Bricken et al. 2023, Cunningham et al. 2023, Elhage et al. 2022). It formalizes when a feature is irreducibly multi-dimensional via separability and ε-mixture indices, then repurposes the SAE dictionary-learning pipeline (Bricken et al. 2023; Cunningham et al. 2023) with clustering to automatically discover such features in GPT-2 and Mistral 7B. The intervention methodology extends activation patching (Zhang & Nanda 2023) with a custom subspace-patching technique tailored to circular subspaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that large pretrained LLMs contain causally-implicated circular representations of days-of-week and months-of-year, this paper directly challenges the Linear Representation Hypothesis, which has been a dominant organizing assumption in mechanistic interpretability. The practical SAE-clustering pipeline offers a scalable path to discovering multi-dimensional features beyond the handful found here, but the authors acknowledge significant open questions: why so few interpretable multi-D features were found, whether the statistical (non-interventional) irreducibility definitions are the right ones, and whether individual SAE features remain appropriate 'mediators' when true features span multiple dimensions. The continuity result (that 'very early Monday' maps between Sunday and Monday on the circle) suggests these representations encode a continuous temporal quantity, raising the question of what other continuous quantities are represented in higher-dimensional manifolds.
