## Problem

Existing multimodal learning methods rely heavily on high-quality paired datasets across modalities, and prior theory only explains how modalities improve representations when perfectly paired supervision is available. This paper asks whether a single-modality model can synergize the training of another modality model even without any paired supervision, and provides both a theoretical foundation and empirical validation for this claim.

## Input / Output

The method takes a primary modality input (e.g., an RGB image, audio waveform, or text) with its ground-truth label, plus a deliberately imperfect representation from a secondary modality model (e.g., a text prompt 'This is about Class #' with a random number, Gaussian-noised audio, or randomly shuffled images). The secondary modality model is frozen and its output serves as a soft regularization target. The output is a class label, and the training implicitly produces an interpolated latent representation that lies between the two modality spaces in 2-Wasserstein distance.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution proves via 2-Wasserstein distance and the convexity property of Wasserstein geodesics that an interpolated representation z^k = (1-α)z^i + αz^j between two modality representations is strictly closer to the true latent space than either individual representation, and derives a closed-form optimal α* (Theorem 1). The key extension (Theorem 2) shows this holds even when the secondary modality provides only an imperfect or restricted representation ẑ^j, with the gap δ having negligible impact. Practically, the training procedure minimizes a combined loss L_total = (1-α)L_cls + αL_z, where L_z is an MSE (or cosine) loss pulling the primary modality's features toward the frozen secondary modality's imperfect features, effectively regularizing the primary model toward the interpolated space. The approach is model-agnostic and has been validated across ResNet, ViT, BERT, RoBERTa, and Wav2Vec2 architectures on vision, language, and audio tasks.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, adding imperfect language guidance (BERT or RoBERTa) to visual models yields consistent gains of +1.5% to +1.9% top-1 accuracy (e.g., ViT-B/16: 80.07% → 81.90% with RoBERTa; ResNet-50: 77.83% → 78.54%). Gains extend to OOD and robustness benchmarks: +1.0–2.0% on ImageNet-V2, Rendition, Sketch, and Style, up to +2.6% on adversarial ImageNet-A, and 2.5↓ on corrupted ImageNet-C. On IEMOCAP, [L→A] improves audio emotion recognition by +1.7% (59.46% → 61.20%) and [A→L] improves language sentiment by +0.7% (55.81% → 56.49%). On AVMNIST, [V→A] gains +1.2% (41.28% → 42.44%) and [A→V] gains +1.5% (65.18% → 66.69%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's baselines are the single-modality models trained without any cross-modal guidance (ResNet-50, ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16 for vision; Wav2Vec2 for audio; BERT for language). The proposed [M_j→M_i] synergy consistently outperforms these single-modality baselines across all architectures and datasets: ViT-B/16 improves from 80.07% to 81.90% on ImageNet-1K (+1.83%), and the gain holds on all OOD/robustness splits. On IEMOCAP, Wav2Vec2-B improves from 59.46% to 61.20% with BERT-L guidance (+1.74%), and BERT improves from 55.81% to 56.49% with Wav2Vec2-B (+0.68%). On AVMNIST, the audio model improves from 41.28% to 42.44% with ResNet-34 (+1.16%), and the vision model improves from 65.18% to 66.69% with Wav2Vec2-L (+1.51%). Notably, the paper does not compare against existing paired-multimodal methods (e.g., CLIP, ALBEF); instead, it demonstrates that even completely unpaired, imperfect cross-modal signals (random class numbers, shuffled audio, Gaussian noise) suffice to produce gains, which is the core novelty. An ablation (Table 4) shows that replacing imperfect ẑ^j with perfectly paired z^j yields only marginal additional gains (+0.07% to +0.64%), confirming that the synergy does not depend on high-quality pairing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in multimodal learning (CLIP, ALBEF, CoOp/CoCoOp) that requires perfectly paired datasets across modalities for joint training. While prior theoretical work (Huang et al., 2021; Huh et al., 2024) established that paired multimodal supervision yields superior representations, and missing-modality methods (Girdhar et al., 2023; Kim & Kim, 2025) handle absent modalities, none addressed whether an imperfect or unpaired representation from one modality can still synergize training of another. The paper carves new ground by formulating cross-modal synergy without paired supervision, using a Wasserstein-distance-based interpolated representation framework and a simple latent alignment loss, rather than building on any specific prior architecture or training recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: it removes the expensive requirement for perfectly aligned multimodal datasets, enabling practitioners to leverage any pretrained single-modality model (even with random or noisy inputs) to improve another modality's training. However, the empirical gains are modest (1–2% on ImageNet, 0.5–1.5% on smaller datasets), the interpolation coefficient α is fixed rather than learned, and the framework is limited to two modalities at a time. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large models (only ViT-L/16 tested as an extension), whether it generalizes to more than two modalities simultaneously, and whether the theoretical guarantees (relying on the generalized geodesic bound) hold tightly in high-dimensional practice.
