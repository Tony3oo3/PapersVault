## Problem

Despite the proliferation of robust RL algorithms, there is no standardized benchmark that evaluates resilience across all stages of the agent-environment interaction process. Existing robust RL methods are typically designed for a single disruption type (e.g., state perturbations only) and validated on narrow, one-off environments that may be over-fitted to the proposed algorithm, making cross-method comparison and progress tracking infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253669/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark accepts a task base (from 11 environment families spanning Box2D, MuJoCo, Robosuite, MAMuJoCo, Safety MuJoCo, etc.), a set of disruptor specifications (which of observation/action/environment to perturb, in which mode, at what frequency), and an RL algorithm to evaluate. It outputs episode reward trajectories, safety cost violations, and robustness metrics under both in-training and post-training evaluation protocols. A distinctive input mode is the LLM-based adversarial disruptor, where a language model receives the current state and reward signal and directly outputs a perturbed observation, replacing traditional parametric noise distributions.

## Method

The core contribution is the Disrupted-MDP formulation, which augments a standard finite-horizon MDP with three modular disruptors—observation-disruptor (perturbing observed state and reward), action-disruptor (perturbing the executed action), and environment-disruptor (shifting transition dynamics and reward function)—each operable in four modes: random disturbance (Gaussian or uniform noise), adversarial disturbance (worst-case or LLM-generated perturbations), internal dynamic shift (changing robot morphology or physics parameters), and external disturbance (altering workspace configuration or introducing interfering agents). Robust RL tasks are constructed by composing any task base with any subset of disruptors, modes, and temporal frequencies (step-wise, episode-wise, or intermittent), yielding over 60 diverse environments. The benchmark is implemented as a drop-in extension of the Gymnasium API, where the user passes a robust configuration dictionary alongside the action at each step, enabling seamless integration with existing RL libraries. The empirical study benchmarks 10 SOTA algorithms (PPO, SAC, OMPO, RSC, ATLA, DBC, PCRPO, CRPO, MAPPO, IPPO) across these composed tasks, revealing that even single-stage disruptions cause significant performance degradation in current methods.

## Results

Robust-Gymnasium evaluates 10 SOTA RL algorithms across 60+ task environments under four disruption modes (random, adversarial, internal dynamic shift, external disturbance). Key findings: standard RL baselines (PPO, SAC) degrade rapidly under even single-stage random disturbances (e.g., Gaussian N(0,0.1) on state or action), with post-training attacks causing substantially larger performance drops than in-training attacks. Among robust RL methods, RSC maintains stable reward trajectories on Robosuite external semantic disturbances while ATLA and DBC degrade; OMPO shows significant performance decline under non-stationary Ant-v5 and Hopper-v5 dynamics. For safe RL, PCRPO outperforms CRPO under action and cost-signal disruptions, and notably PCRPO's performance under disturbance surpasses its clean-environment performance. LLM-based adversarial attacks on Ant-v4 state observations cause more severe PPO performance degradation than uniform-distribution attacks of comparable magnitude.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 10 algorithms across four RL paradigms rather than proposing a new method, so comparisons reveal relative robustness rankings. Among robust RL methods on Robosuite external semantic disturbances, RSC (Ding et al., 2023a) clearly outperforms both ATLA (Zhang et al., 2021b) and DBC (Zhang et al., 2021a), maintaining stable reward trajectories while the other two degrade; however, RSC's training efficiency is noted as a limitation due to augmentation data generation. In safe RL, PCRPO (Gu et al., 2024b) substantially outperforms CRPO (Xu et al., 2021) under both action and observed-cost disruptions, with the surprising finding that PCRPO's disturbed performance exceeds its clean performance. For multi-agent RL, MAPPO (Yu et al., 2022) and IPPO (De Witt et al., 2020) show comparable degradation under full-agent disruptions, with partial attacks (subset of agents) causing smaller performance drops. Standard RL baselines PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) and SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018) both fail under even mild random disturbances (σ=0.1), with post-training (unaware) attacks causing the most severe degradation. The LLM-based adversarial attack (Section 4.5) proves more effective at degrading PPO than a uniform-distribution attack of similar magnitude, and higher attack frequency (every 100 steps vs. 500 steps) amplifies the performance drop.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ecosystem of existing RL environment suites (Gymnasium, MuJoCo, Robosuite, MAMuJoCo, HumanoidBench) and the Disrupted-MDP formulation that unifies prior robust RL paradigms—each of which has typically addressed only a single disruption type (observation noise, action perturbation, or environment shift) in isolated, one-off environments. The closest prior benchmark, RRLS (Zouitine et al., 2024), covers only environment-shift robustness across six MuJoCo tasks. Robust-Gymnasium departs by providing a single modular framework that composes all three disruptor types (observation, action, environment) across four disruption modes (random, adversarial, internal dynamic shift, external disturbance) and spans standard RL, safe RL, and multi-agent RL, with over 60 task bases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a critical gap: prior robust RL algorithms were evaluated in narrow, algorithm-specific environments, making cross-method comparison impossible and masking how fragile even 'robust' methods (OMPO, RSC, ATLA, DBC) remain under multi-stage or combined disruptions. The empirical results—showing significant performance degradation for all SOTA baselines even under single-stage random noise—provide a concrete motivation for new algorithmic approaches. The LLM-based adversarial disruptor opens an underexplored direction for generating semantically meaningful attacks. Open questions include whether current methods can handle simultaneous multi-disruptor scenarios, whether LLM adversaries scale to more complex tasks, and how to design algorithms that are robust across all disruption stages simultaneously.
