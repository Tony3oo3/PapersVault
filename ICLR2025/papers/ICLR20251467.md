## Problem

Standard GNNs fail at systematic generalization in relational reasoning, unable to handle test graphs with longer inference chains than seen in training, while neuro-symbolic methods that succeed are limited by scalability and an implicit assumption that the answer is always inferable from a single relational path. Existing benchmarks also do not test the harder case of multi-path disjunctive reasoning, where partial information from multiple paths must be aggregated to determine the target relation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a labelled multi-graph (knowledge graph) with a designated head entity h and target entity t, and produces a probability distribution over a fixed set of primitive relations that predicts the relationship between h and t. Node embeddings are constrained to be valid probability distributions (non-negative, summing to 1) over n primitive relations rather than free-form vectors, and the final prediction vector s aggregates evidence from the forward model, backward model, and all intermediate entities on a shortest h-to-t path via the same pooling operator used in message passing.

## Method

EpiGNN treats node embeddings as epistemic states and implements message passing via a bilinear composition function φ that explicitly simulates the composition of discrete relations using learned a_ij vectors (each a probability distribution over primitive relations), rather than general vector composition. Pooling uses min or Hadamard product (L1-normalized) to simulate intersection of possible relations, in contrast to the sum pooling used by standard GNNs. A forward-backward architecture addresses the ordering problem in path composition by independently propagating embeddings from head and tail, then composing at intermediate entities. The model is trained via contrastive margin loss and uses 'facets'—jointly training m low-dimensional models—to achieve at least two orders of magnitude fewer parameters than neuro-symbolic baselines. Proposition 2 formally establishes that EpiGNN with min-pooling can exactly simulate directional algebraic closure, the scalable approximation of the Algebraic Closure Algorithm used for RCC-8 and Interval Algebra reasoning.

## Results

On CLUTRR (trained on 2–4 hops, tested on 5–10 hops), EpiGNN-mul achieves 0.99±0.01 accuracy at 5–6 hops and 0.98±0.02 at 10 hops, matching the SOTA neuro-symbolic methods NCRL (0.97) and R5 (0.98) while outperforming all GNN baselines by 20–57 points at 10 hops (e.g., GCN 0.41, NBFNet 0.53). On the new RCC-8 and IA multi-path benchmarks, EpiGNN-min achieves the best accuracy across all configurations, while neuro-symbolic methods (R5, CTP) fail entirely on 5+ hops (inference exceeds 30 min). For inductive KGC, EpiGNN-min is competitive with SOTA, achieving the best Hits@10 on FB15k-237 v2 (95.54) and remaining within 5 points of the best on most WN18RR splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CLUTRR, EpiGNN-mul ties or slightly trails NCRL and R5 (the two strongest neuro-symbolic baselines) by ≤1 point at most hop counts, while decisively outperforming all GNN baselines (GCN, GAT, R-GCN, NBFNet) by 20–57 points at 10 hops and all RNN baselines (LSTM, GRU, RNN) by 14–34 points. On the new RCC-8 and IA benchmarks requiring multi-path disjunctive reasoning, EpiGNN-min is the only method that scales to 5+ hops; R5 and CTP are effectively infeasible (inference >30 min, reported as 0), and Edge Transformers, while competitive, degrade on the hardest settings (k=9, b=3). On Graphlog, EpiGNN-mul is outperformed by R5 and NCRL on most worlds due to their stronger single-path inductive bias, but achieves a new SOTA on World 32 (0.914 vs. R5's 0.841) and clearly beats CTP and GNN baselines. For inductive KGC, EpiGNN-min achieves the best Hits@10 on FB15k-237 v2 (95.54 vs. NBFNet 94.90, REST 91.21) and is competitive on WN18RR, trailing the best (REST) by up to ~12 points on v3 but matching or exceeding NBFNet on v2. The paper also reports that EpiGNNs are at least two orders of magnitude more parameter-efficient than the neuro-symbolic baselines after hyperparameter tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

EpiGNN draws on two distinct lineages: the anchoring architecture of NBFNet (Zhu et al., 2021), which ties node embeddings to a designated head entity to build path-based representations, and the Algebraic Closure Algorithm (Renz & Ligozat, 2005) from qualitative spatial/temporal reasoning, which iteratively refines sets of possible relations between entity pairs. The key departure is from both standard GNNs (which use unconstrained vector composition and sum-pooling) and neuro-symbolic methods (NTP, CTP, R5, NCRL) that explicitly explore relational paths. By treating embeddings as probability distributions over primitive relations and aligning message passing with the composition and intersection operations of algebraic closure, EpiGNN imposes an epistemic inductive bias absent from prior GNNs while avoiding the exponential path exploration that limits neuro-symbolic scalability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper challenges the widely held view that GNNs are fundamentally incapable of systematic generalization in relational domains, showing that the right inductive bias (epistemic embeddings + bilinear composition + min/product pooling) is sufficient. The introduction of multi-path disjunctive benchmarks (RCC-8, IA) exposes a critical blind spot in existing neuro-symbolic methods, which assume a single relational path suffices — an assumption that fails in realistic spatial and temporal reasoning. Limitations include the model's statistical nature (vulnerable to training-data artefacts in severely data-limited settings) and the fact that directional algebraic closure is only an approximation of full algebraic closure, potentially missing inferences that require reasoning about all entity pairs simultaneously. Open questions include whether the epistemic inductive bias generalizes to richer rule structures (e.g., non-binary relations, higher-order logic) and whether the forward-backward composition strategy can be extended to handle exponentially many path orderings without explicit enumeration.
