## Problem

LM interpretability research routinely borrows causal terminology (interventions, counterfactuals) from Pearl's framework but conflates level-2 interventions with level-3 counterfactuals. Representation surgery and other intervention techniques can modify a model's behavior, but they cannot answer the counterfactual question: what would a specific observed string have looked like had it been generated by the intervened model? This paper provides the first rigorous counterfactual generation framework for LMs grounded in structural equation modeling.

## Input / Output

The method takes three inputs: an observed factual string w, the original language model (encoder h_θ with parameters E, b), and an intervened counterfactual model (h_θ̃ with parameters Ē, b̃). It produces a counterfactual string w̃ that is a sample from the counterfactual distribution — i.e., the string that would have been generated by the intervened model under the same instantiation of sampling noise that produced w. The counterfactual is generated autoregressively, token by token, and may differ in length from the original since the intervened model may emit EOS at a different position.

## Method

The paper reformulates a representation-based LM as a well-founded structural equation model (WSEM) by applying the Gumbel-max trick: each token is selected as argmax over (logit + Gumbel noise), separating the deterministic forward pass from the stochastic sampling. This makes the entire string generation deterministic given the exogenous noise variables U, which is the prerequisite for counterfactual reasoning. The key algorithmic contribution is Hindsight Gumbel Sampling (Prop. 3.1), which infers the latent noise from an observed string by sampling the winning token's noise from Gumbel(log Z) and each losing token's noise from a TruncGumbel distribution bounded above by the winner's value, then subtracting the logit to recover the raw Gumbel noise. The counterfactual string is then produced by running the intervened model's logits through the same inferred noise. The paper further proves (Thm. B.1) that under the Thurstone discriminal process assumption, Gumbel is the unique noise distribution that recovers the softmax, justifying the choice and establishing counterfactual stability.

## Results

The paper evaluates the surgical precision of four intervention techniques by measuring normalized edit distance between original Wikipedia-prompted sentences and their Gumbel counterfactuals. MEMIT achieves the lowest edit distance at 10–15% for both the Louvre and Koalas knowledge edits, making it the most surgical intervention tested. For targeted edits (prompts explicitly mentioning the edited fact), only 54.6% of MEMIT-Louvre counterfactuals successfully change the location to Rome, while 45.4% still mention Paris. All other interventions (linear steering, instruction tuning) show substantially higher edit distances, indicating considerable unintended semantic drift even on unrelated content.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper uses its Gumbel counterfactual framework to evaluate four intervention techniques rather than proposing a competing method. MEMIT (Meng et al., 2023) emerges as the most surgical intervention, with only 10–15% normalized edit distance on unrelated Wikipedia prompts, though it still fails to fully propagate the edited fact (only 54.6% of targeted counterfactuals reflect the new Louvre location). Honest LLaMa (Li et al., 2024) and MiMiC gender steering (Singh et al., 2024) both produce substantial semantic drift on unrelated content, with qualitative examples showing altered facts, changed entities, and shifted topic continuations. LLaMA3-Instruct (Touvron et al., 2023) shows the broadest side effects, including introducing new hallucinations, changing factual details (e.g., moth family reclassification), and altering organizational names. The key finding is that no intervention achieves truly minimal change: even the most surgical method (MEMIT) modifies unrelated sentence portions, and the autoregressive nature of generation amplifies small token-level differences into significant semantic divergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gumbel-max structural causal model framework from Oberst & Sontag (2019, RL off-policy evaluation) to language models, formalizing LMs as well-founded SEMs (WSEMs) over countably infinite variables. It grounds the choice of Gumbel noise in the Thurstone discriminal process (Thurstone, 1927), proving via Luce's Choice Axiom that Gumbel is the unique noise distribution compatible with softmax LMs under a Thurstone assumption. The paper explicitly departs from prior LM interpretability work (representation surgery, knowledge editing, steering) by arguing those operate at Pearl's intervention level (level 2) rather than the counterfactual level (level 3), and from NLP 'counterfactual' generation work (Polyjuice, CEBaB) that relies on human-judged concept modifications rather than the model's own sampling noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first rigorous, model-internal definition of what a string counterfactual is for an LM, enabling principled evaluation of intervention techniques that was previously impossible. The empirical finding that even minimally-parameterized interventions (linear steering, MEMIT) produce 10–15%+ normalized edit distance on unrelated Wikipedia prompts challenges the assumption that targeted edits are surgical, with implications for trust in knowledge editing and alignment techniques. Open questions include whether alternative counterfactually-stable noise distributions (beyond Gumbel) yield meaningfully different counterfactuals, how the framework scales to very large models with constrained decoding, and whether the Thurstone assumption is empirically validated or merely a convenient modeling choice.
