## Problem

Multivariate time series forecasting with block missing data is challenging because traditional two-step pipelines (impute then forecast) accumulate errors under high missing ratios and complex inter-variable dependencies. Existing end-to-end methods that handle missing values directly (GRU-D, BRITS, BiTGraph, ODE-based models) suffer from high computational cost, long training times, or excessive memory usage, making them impractical for large-scale deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251773/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a look-back window of D-variate time series (length ℓ_L ∈ {96, 192, 384, 768}) together with a binary mask matrix M^(L) of the same shape, where 1 indicates observed and 0 indicates missing. Missing values follow block patterns (consecutive time points or consecutive variables) rather than random dropout, with overall missing ratios ranging from ~12% to ~70%. The output is a horizon window of D-variate forecasted values of fixed length ℓ_H = 96, produced end-to-end without any separate imputation step.

## Method

S4M integrates missing-data handling directly into the S4 state-space architecture via two modules. The Adaptive Temporal Prototype Mapper (ATPM) extracts local statistics (exponential-decay-weighted min/max with time-gap features) from the look-back window, encodes them via a query encoder, and retrieves the most similar historical patterns from a two-level FIFO prototype bank (K1 clusters × K2 prototypes each) using cosine-similarity top-K aggregation; the bank is updated by a separate prototype encoder trained with momentum (θ_p = γθ_p + (1−γ)θ_q) to evolve smoothly. The Missing-Aware Dual Stream S4 (MDS-S4) then modifies the S4 SSM to accept two input streams—the ATPM representation o_t and an encoded mask E_m(m_t)—producing two distinct SSM kernels K1 and K2 whose convolutions are summed, preserving the HiPPO long-range dependency structure while explicitly conditioning the hidden state and output on missingness. This dual-stream design avoids the error accumulation of imputation-then-forecast pipelines while maintaining S4's O(T) convolutional efficiency.

## Results

Under time point missing (r=0.06), S4M achieves the best MSE on Weather across all lookback lengths (0.237–0.232), outperforming the next-best S4(Ffill) by 15–20%. On ETTh1, S4M attains the lowest MAE (0.571–0.588) and MSE (0.624–0.647) at most lookback settings. On Electricity and Traffic, S4M is competitive, achieving the best or second-best MAE/MSE in the majority of configurations. Under variable missing (r=0.06), S4M similarly dominates on Weather (MSE 0.256–0.267) and ETTh1 (MSE 0.598–0.649), while remaining within 1–3% of the best on Electricity and Traffic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S4M clearly outperforms all four S4-based imputation variants (Mean, Ffill, Decay, SAITS) across every dataset and lookback length, with the largest gaps on Weather (e.g., MSE 0.237 vs. 0.296 for S4(Ffill) at ℓ_L=96). Against RNN-based missing-data methods, S4M beats BRITS by a wide margin (e.g., MAE 0.372 vs. 0.633 on Electricity) and GRU-D consistently (MAE 0.372 vs. 0.431 on Electricity; 0.571 vs. 0.644 on ETTh1). Among Transformer-based baselines, S4M surpasses Autoformer on MAE for all four datasets (e.g., 0.372 vs. 0.375 on Electricity, 0.571 vs. 0.866 on ETTh1) and on MSE for Weather and ETTh1, though Autoformer edges out S4M slightly on Electricity MSE (0.272 vs. 0.287 at ℓ_L=96). BiTGraph, the only other end-to-end missing-data method, is closely matched by S4M on ETTh1 (both achieve MAE 0.571 at ℓ_L=96, with BiTGraph slightly ahead on MSE: 0.613 vs. 0.624) but S4M clearly wins on Weather (MSE 0.237 vs. 0.510) and Traffic (MAE 0.428 vs. 0.516). Overall, S4M achieves the best or second-best result in the vast majority of the 32 dataset–lookback–metric combinations in Table 1, with its most decisive advantages on Weather and ETTh1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the S4 structured state space model (Gu et al., 2021) to handle multivariate time series forecasting with block missing values, departing from the dominant two-step paradigm of imputing then forecasting (as in BRITS, SAITS, or mean/forward-fill baselines). It draws conceptual inspiration from GRU-D (Che et al., 2018) for explicitly incorporating missingness masks into the recurrent dynamics, and from hierarchical memory networks (Chandar et al., 2016) for the prototype bank mechanism. The key architectural departure is the dual-stream SSM formulation that treats the mask as a parallel input stream with its own SSM kernel, rather than folding missingness into the input values via decay or imputation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

S4M offers a practically attractive middle ground: it avoids the error accumulation of separate imputation steps while being far more computationally efficient than ODE-based (CRUs, GraFITi) or graph-based (BiTGraph) alternatives, as evidenced by the training-time and memory comparisons. However, the evaluation is limited to four benchmark datasets with artificially injected block-missing patterns, and the method has not been tested on irregularly sampled data, non-block missingness, or larger-scale real-world deployments. Open questions include whether the prototype bank mechanism generalizes to streaming/online settings, whether the approach extends naturally to Mamba or other S6 architectures, and how performance degrades under more realistic, non-stationary missingness mechanisms.
