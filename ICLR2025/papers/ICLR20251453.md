## Problem

Existing 3D equivariant GNNs face a fundamental dichotomy: scalarization-based models are computationally efficient but sacrifice geometric expressiveness, while high-degree steerable models (NequIP, Equiformer, MACE) achieve strong performance through irreducible representations and Clebsch-Gordan transforms at significant computational cost. No prior architecture bridges this gap, maintaining the expressiveness of high-degree representations without the overhead of explicit CG tensor operations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251453/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D atomic coordinates, atomic numbers (one-hot encoded), and molecular graph connectivity, with interactions restricted by a 5.0 Å cutoff radius. Geometric information is encoded via spherical harmonics up to degree L_max=2, producing edge tensor representations with (2l+1) components per degree. Outputs are either E(3)-invariant scalars (e.g., energy, dipole moment, HOMO/LUMO energies) or E(3)-equivariant vectors (molecular forces in kcal/mol/Å), depending on the downstream task.

## Method

GotenNet replaces irreps and CG transforms with a spherical-scalarization scheme that initializes edge features via spherical harmonics and updates them through inner-product operations on high-degree steerable features. The architecture combines three key modules: (1) Geometry-Aware Tensor Attention (GATA), which augments standard self-attention with geometric encoding and splits outputs into scalar and degree-specific steerable coefficients for equivariant message passing; (2) Hierarchical Tensor Refinement (HTR), which projects steerable features into query/key representations via degree-specific SO(3)-equivariant linear maps and computes inner products across degrees to refine edge scalars; and (3) EQFF blocks that apply channel-wise non-linearities conditioned on the L2 norm of steerable features. The critical insight, building on SO3KRATES and HEGNN, is that inner products of high-degree steerable features under shared rotations yield invariant scalars (since Wigner D-matrices are unitary), enabling the model to capture complete angular information without explicit CG coefficients while preserving strict E(3) equivariance throughout.

## Results

On QM9, GotenNet_L achieves a standardized MAE of 0.52 meV and log MAE of −6.41, outperforming the previous best (EquiformerV2: 0.67, −5.87) by 22% and 9% respectively, while even the smallest variant (GotenNet_S: 0.60, −6.29) beats all baselines. On Molecule3D (3M+ molecules), GotenNet surpasses SaVeNet-L by 24% in dipole moment (μ) and 32% in HOMO energy. On MD22, GotenNet_B reduces energy errors by up to 35% (Buckyball catcher) and force errors by up to 31% (Double-walled nanotube) over prior best methods. On rMD17, GotenNet achieves the best MAE in 80% of the 20 energy/force tasks across 10 molecules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On QM9, GotenNet_L (std. MAE 0.52) outperforms EquiformerV2 (0.67) by 22%, SaVeNet-B (0.69) by 25%, and Geoformer (0.75) by 31%; even GotenNet_S (0.60) beats all 10 baselines. On Molecule3D, GotenNet dominates SaVeNet-L (the prior best) across all six random-split metrics, with the largest gaps in ε_HOMO (0.0108 vs 0.0159, 32% improvement) and Δε (0.0165 vs 0.0239, 31% improvement). On MD22, GotenNet_B surpasses MACE, Allegro, Equiformer, ViSNet, and SO3KRATES on every molecule for both energy and force; the most dramatic wins are on Buckyball catcher (energy 0.3032 vs 0.381 for SO3KRATES, 20% better) and Double-walled nanotube (force 0.1888 vs 0.2747 for Equiformer, 31% better). On rMD17, GotenNet beats NequIP, MACE, Allegro, and TensorNet in 80% of the 20 tasks, with the largest margin on Aspirin energy (0.0358 vs 0.0507 for MACE, 29% improvement). Computational efficiency analysis on Molecule3D shows GotenNet maintains near-linear scaling with node count, while Geoformer and EquiformerV2 exhibit super-linear growth due to dense O(n²) attention or O(L³) Fibonacci grid sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

GotenNet sits at the intersection of two established lines of work in 3D equivariant GNNs: scalarization-based models (PaiNN, LEFTNet, SaVeNet) that project geometric information into scalars for efficiency, and high-degree steerable models (Equiformer, MACE, NequIP) that use irreducible representations and Clebsch-Gordan transforms for expressiveness. The key conceptual departure, building on SO3KRATES (Frank et al., 2024) and HEGNN (Cen et al., 2024), is replacing explicit CG tensor products with inner products of high-degree steerable features, eliminating the computational overhead of irreps while retaining geometric expressiveness. The architecture further draws on SO(3)-equivariant linear transformations from Vector Neurons (Deng et al., 2021) for its hierarchical tensor refinement, and on transformer-style attention mechanisms (Equiformer, Geoformer) for its geometry-aware tensor attention module, but restructures these into a unified pipeline that avoids the O(L³) overhead of Fibonacci grid sampling in EquiformerV2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a long-standing practical bottleneck in molecular ML: the inability to simultaneously achieve high expressiveness for force prediction and computational efficiency for large-scale property prediction. By demonstrating that a compact architecture (GotenNet_S) can outperform all baselines on QM9 while maintaining linear scaling with node count on Molecule3D (3M+ molecules), it offers a viable path toward deploying equivariant models on systems with hundreds of atoms. Open questions include whether the L_max=2 ceiling (used across all experiments) is sufficient for more complex angular dependencies, whether the approach generalizes to non-molecular 3D point clouds, and how performance scales beyond the ~370-atom systems tested in MD22. The authors note that QM9's dataset size may limit further gains from larger models, suggesting the true test is on even larger, more diverse benchmarks.
