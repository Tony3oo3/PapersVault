## Problem

While LLMs can solve reinforcement learning problems in-context, the internal computations they perform remain uncharacterized. It is unclear whether LLMs implement known RL algorithms like temporal difference (TD) learning internally, or whether their in-context RL ability arises from some other mechanism. This paper asks whether TD errors and Q-values can be identified as discrete, causally relevant features within LLM activations, despite the model having been trained only for next-token prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250856/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts describing RL tasks (states, actions, rewards in natural language) and records 8192-dimensional residual stream vectors at specific token positions (e.g., the 'pick' or 'go' tokens preceding actions) across all 80 transformer blocks of Llama 3 70B. It produces SAE latent features identified as TD latents and Q-value latents, quantified by their Pearson correlations with externally computed TD errors and Q-values from Q-learning agents, as well as behavioral and representational changes following targeted deactivation or negative scaling of individual latents.

## Method

The paper trains task-specific sparse autoencoders (single encoder layer, ReLU, single decoder layer, L1-regularized with β=1e-5) on residual stream activations collected from Llama 3 70B across three RL tasks: a Two-Step Task, a 5×5 Grid World, and a latent community graph prediction task. Each SAE is trained per transformer block (80 SAEs per task) on 15–30 epochs of 18,000 representations, reducing the 8192-dim space to a sparse latent space of a few dozen active features. The key analytical step is correlating each SAE latent against externally computed TD errors, Q-values, and myopic values from Q-learning agents fitted to the same task, revealing latents that track these signals with correlations up to r=0.75. Causal validation is performed by replacing residual stream activations with SAE reconstructions in which the identified TD latent is set to zero or scaled by −10, then measuring the resulting degradation in task performance, shift away from Q-learning behavior, and reduction in downstream Q-value/TD-error correlations. The Graph Learning task extends the analysis to reward-free settings, showing that Llama's representations align with the Successor Representation (learned via TD updates) rather than a simpler transition-matrix model, with causal lesions in block 64 disrupting both prediction accuracy and the learned community structure.

## Results

In the Two-Step Task, Llama 3 70B's behavior is best fit by a Q-learning model (NLL = 2729) over a myopic model (NLL = 2864) and a repetition model (NLL = 5745), with a paired t-test confirming significance (t(99) = -3.40, p = 0.001). SAE features in block 34 show r = 0.58 correlation with TD errors, and deactivating this single TD latent significantly impairs task performance. In the Graph Learning task, SAE latents correlate more strongly with the Successor Representation (max r = 0.62) than with the transition matrix (max r = 0.49), and with TD errors (max r = 0.60) than with a surprise signal (max r = 0.18). Lesioning the strongest TD latent in block 64 drops next-state prediction accuracy from 24.3% to 18.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparisons pit TD-learning-based explanations against simpler alternatives. Behaviorally, full Q-learning (NLL 2729) outperforms the myopic model that ignores future rewards (NLL 2864) and the repetition model (NLL 5745), and also outperforms a Look-up model that memorizes actions from the best-reward episode (Fig. 11). Representationally, SAE latents correlate more strongly with SR-based TD signals (r = 0.62, r = 0.60) than with a model-based transition-matrix/surprise alternative (r = 0.49, r = 0.18), ruling out simple transition counting as the mechanism. Causally, lesioning TD-correlated latents degrades action prediction and distorts downstream Q-value and TD-error representations, while control lesions on lowest-correlation features produce negligible changes. In the larger 7×7 Grid World with randomized start positions, Llama generalizes to new trajectories while the Look-up model stays at chance, further dissociating TD learning from memorization. Replications on Gemma-2-27B and Qwen-2.5-72B show qualitatively matching SAE correlations, though behavioral fit is weaker than for Llama.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: SAE-based mechanistic interpretability (Bricken et al., 2023; Templeton et al., 2024) and in-context reinforcement learning in LLMs (Binz & Schulz, 2023; Brooks et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2024). Prior work demonstrated that transformers can implement known algorithms in-context (Akyürek et al., 2023 for regression; Wang et al., 2024 for TD learning in linear transformers), and that LLMs can solve RL tasks from prompts, but no prior study had used SAEs to identify and causally verify the specific internal representations (TD errors, Q-values) supporting this behavior in a large pretrained model. The paper also draws on the Successor Representation framework (Dayan, 1993) to extend the analysis beyond reward-based RL to structure learning without explicit rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a reusable methodology—train task-specific SAEs on residual streams, correlate latents with RL variables, then perform causal interventions—that can be applied to other in-context learning abilities beyond RL. A key limitation is that the SAEs are task-specific and the circuit-level mechanism producing TD representations remains unexplained; the authors note that induction heads or path-patching analyses could address this. The finding that TD learning emerges purely from next-token pretraining (no RL objective) has broader implications for understanding what representations LLMs acquire and for the neuroscience connection between TD errors and dopaminergic signaling. Replication on Gemma-2-27B and Qwen-2.5-72B strengthens generality, though the Two-Step task performance is weaker on those models.
