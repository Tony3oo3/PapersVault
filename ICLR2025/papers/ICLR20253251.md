## Problem

While LLMs are increasingly deployed to generate persuasive content (marketing, chatbots, political messaging), there is no scalable, automated benchmark to measure and compare their persuasive capabilities. Prior human-study-based evaluations are expensive, non-scalable, and ignore the well-established effects of speaker, audience, time, and channel on persuasion. The paper also identifies a missing task: transforming non-persuasive content into persuasive content while holding all other communication factors constant, which it terms 'transsuasion.'
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253251/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For generative transsuasion, the model receives a low-engagement tweet (T1) along with the posting account, timestamp, and optionally an image (represented via LLaVA-generated captions rather than raw pixels) or webpage context (first 150 words of title, description, keywords), and must produce a higher-engagement variant (T2'). For comparative transsuasion, the model receives two semantically similar tweets from the same account posted within 45 days and classifies which will receive more engagement. Behavior simulation takes account name, time, and tweet text to predict a like-percentile bin (low/medium/high). The evaluation produces Elo ratings from an Oracle-as-judge tournament, conventional NLP metrics (BLEU-2, ROUGE-L, BERTScore), and human-judged win rates.

## Method

The paper constructs a 1.57-million-pair transsuasion dataset from 180 million enterprise Twitter tweets by matching semantically similar posts (Twitter4SSE cosine similarity >0.6–0.8, Levenshtein edit distance, LLaVA+PromCSE for media) from the same account within 45 days, yielding 8 task types that vary in the degree of autonomy (text-only refinement, paraphrase, image addition, visual-only transsuasion, highlighting different aspects of a source document, and cross-account transcreation). PersuasionBench and PersuasionArena evaluate models across simulative tasks (behavior simulation, comparative transsuasion) and generative tasks (generative transsuasion, content simulation, transcreation) under four regimes: conventional metrics, Oracle-LLM-as-judge (a Vicuna-1.5-13B trained on the full dataset), human-as-judge, and domain-shift tasks (blog KPI prediction, human preference studies). For training, the authors instruction-fine-tune Vicuna-1.5-13B on behavior simulation, content simulation, and all transsuasion tasks, and further augment training with LLM-generated synthetic explanations of why the high-engagement tweet outperforms the low-engagement one. The key empirical finding is that this 13B model, after targeted training, surpasses GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 on generative persuasion Elo ratings, challenging the assumption that persuasive capability scales monotonically with model size.

## Results

On PersuasionArena (generative persuasion via Oracle-as-a-judge Elo ratings), the proposed 13B model trained on CS+BS+TS tasks achieves an average Elo of 1293, surpassing GPT-4o (1251), GPT-4 (1213), LLaMA-3-70B (1187), GPT-3.5 (1092), and Vicuna-1.5-13B (877). On PersuasionBench (simulative persuasion), most off-the-shelf models score barely above random (50–57% on Comparative Transsuasion, 33–44% on Behavior Simulation), while the trained 13B model reaches 80.9% TS-CT accuracy and the Oracle reaches 82.3%. The human topline (T2) sits at 1357 avg Elo, meaning the best model still trails the top human-generated content by ~64 Elo, though on specific tasks like Hilight and VisOnly the model exceeds the topline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The 13B instruction-tuned model (Ours-Instruct, 1299 avg Elo) outperforms GPT-4o (1251) by 48 Elo (~57% win probability), GPT-4 (1213) by 86 Elo (~61%), and GPT-3.5 (1092) by 207 Elo (~76%), despite being 13–100× smaller. LLaMA-3-70B (1187) trails the proposed 13B model by 112 Elo, confirming that targeted persuasion training on a small model beats a much larger general-purpose model. On simulative tasks (Table 2), GPT-4o achieves the best off-the-shelf TS-CT accuracy at 62.1% (10-shot random), still far below the trained 13B model's 80.9% and the Oracle's 82.3%. Vicuna-1.5-13B, the base model before fine-tuning, scores only 877 avg Elo in generative persuasion, demonstrating that the ~420 Elo gain comes entirely from the instruction-tuning regime rather than model scale. Notably, GPT-4o beats GPT-4 by 38 Elo and GPT-3.5 by 160 Elo on PersuasionArena, consistent with OpenAI's internal ChangeMyView evaluations reported in the GPT-o1 system card.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the behavior simulation (BS) and content simulation (CS) framework from Khandelwal et al. (ICLR 2024) and SI et al. (2023) by introducing transsuasion (TS) tasks that transform low-engagement content into high-engagement content while holding speaker, audience, time, and channel constant. It departs from prior persuasion evaluation work (OpenAI, Anthropic/Durmus et al.) that relied on expensive, small-scale human studies by constructing the first large-scale automated benchmark (PersuasionBench/PersuasionArena) using 1.57 million transsuasion pairs mined from natural experiments in enterprise Twitter accounts. The transsuasion task itself is positioned as entirely unexplored in the ML persuasion literature, which had focused primarily on detecting or classifying persuasion rather than generating or transforming it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive automated framework for measuring and ranking LLM persuasiveness, filling a critical gap as LLMs are increasingly deployed in marketing, political communication, and social influence. The finding that a 13B model fine-tuned on transsuasion data can surpass GPT-4 in persuasive capability directly challenges the scaling narrative and has concrete policy implications for regulations (EU AI Act, California SB-1047) that use parameter count or FLOPs as proxies for societal risk. Open questions include whether persuasion gains transfer to adversarial or long-horizon settings, how robust the natural-experiment data construction is to platform algorithm changes, and whether the Oracle-as-judge paradigm adequately captures the full spectrum of human persuasion response.
