## Problem

Existing text-to-3D methods struggle to generate compositional 3D scenes with plausible object interactions, often producing misplaced or overlapping objects, and require hours of per-prompt optimization without offering any user control over spatial layout. Feed-forward reconstruction models are fast but fail on multi-object compositions due to lack of compositional training data, while optimization-based scene generation methods are computationally expensive and produce less realistic results than single-object synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250095/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D layout specified as a set of bounding boxes [x1, y1, x2, y2] (values in 0–512) paired with instance names, plus an overall text prompt describing the scene. The layout can be user-drawn or automatically generated by an LLM-grounded diffusion model. The output is a compositional 3D scene represented as 3D Gaussian Splatting, where each instance is an independently editable 3DGS asset. Per-instance text prompts can be swapped during the refinement stage to restyle individual objects without regenerating the full scene.

## Method

Layout-Your-3D uses a coarse-to-fine two-stage pipeline. In the coarse stage, MIGC generates a 2D reference image from the layout and text; each instance is segmented (SAM), inpainted (Stable Diffusion), and reconstructed via the feed-forward LGM model, while 3D layout is initialized using GeoWizard depth for z-translation and DINOv2 feature matching across rendered views for rotation estimation. The disentangled refinement stage then separates concerns: a collision-aware layout refinement optimizes global placement using a combination of spatial-aware SDS loss, a DINOv2 feature-level reference loss, and a novel tolerant collision loss that penalizes excessive gaussian overlap while permitting natural intersections; followed by an instance-wise refinement that applies SDS with an adjustable timestep sampling strategy (narrow range early to preserve geometry, wider range later for detail) plus normal-smoothing and total-variation regularization. This disentanglement allows per-instance customization and reduces total generation time to ~12 minutes for a 3-instance scene.

## Results

On the proposed Compo20 validation set, Layout-Your-3D achieves 53.51% BLIP-VQA and 50.44% mGPT-CoT, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: ProlificDreamer at 42.14% BLIP-VQA, GraphDreamer at 44.28% mGPT-CoT). The method completes generation in ~12 minutes, compared to 45 min (DreamFusion), 35 min (LucidDreamer), 2 hours (GraphDreamer), and 4 hours (ProlificDreamer). In a user study (100 users, 500 ratings), Layout-Your-3D scores 9.08/10 on text-alignment, 8.44/10 on quality, and 8.72/10 on rationality, clearly ahead of all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Layout-Your-3D decisively outperforms all five optimization-based baselines on the two fine-grained text-3D alignment metrics: BLIP-VQA (53.51% vs. 42.14% for ProlificDreamer, the runner-up) and mGPT-CoT (50.44% vs. 44.28% for GraphDreamer). On CLIP-Score, ProlificDreamer (24.93%) and GraphDreamer (24.78%) marginally exceed Layout-Your-3D (23.26%), though the authors note CLIP-Score is unreliable for compositional semantic correspondence. In terms of speed, Layout-Your-3D (12 min) is 3.75× faster than DreamFusion, 20× faster than ProlificDreamer, and 10× faster than GraphDreamer, while being only slightly slower than GaussianDreamer (15 min). Qualitatively, the paper shows that DreamFusion, ProlificDreamer, GaussianDreamer, and LucidDreamer all fail to place objects at plausible locations or scales for compositional prompts (e.g., the sofa/cushion/bear/basketball example), whereas Layout-Your-3D produces coherent spatial arrangements. The LLM-grounded variant (auto-generated 2D layouts) retains most of the advantage (52.26% BLIP-VQA, 48.80% mGPT-CoT), confirming robustness to layout quality. In the user study, Layout-Your-3D leads every baseline by at least 1.7 points on text-alignment and 1.5 points on rationality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation and the LGM feed-forward reconstruction model (Tang et al., ECCV 2024) to initialize coarse 3D instances, departing from the pure optimization-based paradigm of DreamFusion, ProlificDreamer, and GaussianDreamer. It leverages MIGC (Zhou et al., CVPR 2024) for layout-conditioned 2D reference image generation and the spatial-aware SDS loss from ComboVerse (Chen et al., ECCV 2024) for layout refinement, but introduces a key architectural departure: a disentangled two-stage refinement that separates global layout optimization (with a novel tolerant collision loss and DINOv2-based feature-level reference loss) from per-instance geometry/texture refinement (with an adjustable timestep sampling strategy). Compared to concurrent layout-controlled methods like COMOGen and GraphDreamer, the reconstruction-based initialization and the explicit collision-awareness mechanism represent the main conceptual contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12-minute generation time (vs. hours for optimization-based baselines) makes layout-controlled compositional 3D generation practically viable for interactive design workflows, and the modular pipeline (swappable reconstruction models, 3D representations, and per-instance text prompts) opens a path toward editable 3D scene creation. However, the authors acknowledge that the SSDS loss struggles beyond ~10 instances and that the method relies on deliberately designed 2D layouts for complex scenes, leaving open the question of fully automatic layout inference for large-scale scene generation. The disentangled refinement paradigm—treating layout coherence and instance fidelity as separable optimization problems—could inform future work on multi-object 3D generation more broadly.
