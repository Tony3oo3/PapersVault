## Problem

Existing controllable video generation methods rely on 2D control signals (sketches, bounding boxes, points) that cannot fully express the 3D nature of object motion—rotation requires three parameters in 3D, and occlusion between objects is naturally handled by z-buffering but is ambiguous in 2D. Moreover, prior 2D approaches fail to establish per-entity correspondence between multiple objects and their respective trajectories, making multi-entity 3D motion control an open problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250244/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt listing N entity descriptions (up to 3 entities, with fine-grained attributes like hair, clothing, body shape) plus a location tag, along with per-entity 6DoF pose sequences (rotation R∈R^{3×3} and translation T∈R^3 per frame, flattened to F×12). Output is a 384×672, 5-second (100-frame) video where each entity follows its specified 3D trajectory. Entity descriptions support fine-grained editing (e.g., changing 'black jeans' to 'red pants') without retraining.

## Method

The core contribution is a plug-and-play 3D-motion grounded object injector: entity text prompts are encoded by a frozen text encoder, pose sequences by a learnable pose encoder (linear layer + temporal downsampling), and the two are combined via entity-wise addition to form bonded entity-trajectory embeddings. These are concatenated with video tokens and processed by a gated self-attention layer (initialized from the 2D spatial self-attention weights, inspired by GLIGEN) with a residual connection and truncation to preserve video tokens. Training is two-stage: first, LoRA-based domain adaptors are trained on the synthetic 360°-Motion Dataset (54K videos from 70 3D assets, GPT-generated trajectories, 12 surround cameras in UE scenes) to absorb the synthetic domain shift; then the object injector is trained with the adaptors frozen. At inference, an annealed sampling strategy injects trajectories only in early denoising steps and drops them in later stages, transitioning to standard T2V generation to preserve video quality.

## Results

On the multi-entity 3D trajectory control benchmark, 3DTrajMaster achieves a translation error (TransErr) of 0.398 m and rotation error (RotErr) of 0.277° across all entities, substantially outperforming all 2D-based baselines. The best 2D baseline, Direct-a-Video, records TransErr of 1.420 m and RotErr of 1.057°, meaning 3DTrajMaster reduces translation error by ~3.6× and rotation error by ~3.8×. On the single-entity subset (which uses more complex trajectories), 3DTrajMaster still leads with 0.456 m / 0.319° versus the next-best Direct-a-Video at 1.632 m / 1.902°.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DTrajMaster decisively outperforms all three baselines on both translation and rotation accuracy. Against MotionCtrl (2D point trajectories), 3DTrajMaster reduces TransErr from 1.690 m to 0.398 m (−76%) and RotErr from 1.675° to 0.277° (−83%); MotionCtrl also lacks entity-trajectory correspondence, causing it to misassign motions in multi-entity scenes. Against Tora (2D trajectory-oriented DiT), 3DTrajMaster cuts TransErr from 1.848 m to 0.398 m (−78%) and RotErr from 1.471° to 0.277° (−81%); Tora additionally confuses background entities with foreground ones in multi-entity settings. Against Direct-a-Video (training-free bounding-box control), 3DTrajMaster lowers TransErr from 1.420 m to 0.398 m (−72%) and RotErr from 1.057° to 0.277° (−74%); Direct-a-Video struggles most with 3D occlusion, producing mixed/overlapping entities where a person walks in front of a zebra. The gap is especially pronounced on the multi-entity subset, where 2D methods cannot disambiguate depth ordering or per-entity rotation, while 3DTrajMaster's entity-wise 6DoF conditioning handles these cases cleanly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of 2D motion control in video generation (points, bounding boxes, 2D trajectories used by MotionCtrl, Tora, and Direct-a-Video) by formulating object motion as per-entity 6DoF pose sequences in 3D space. Architecturally, it adapts GLIGEN's gated self-attention with truncation for entity-trajectory pairing, and borrows the LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm for its domain adaptor. It also extends the 3D-aware video generation thread (CameraCtrl, VD3D, MotionCtrl's camera pose control) from camera-only manipulation to object-level 3D motion, and leverages TC4D's GPT-based spline generation for trajectory synthesis in its dataset pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first benchmark for multi-entity 3D motion control in video generation, this work provides a clear target for future research on physically grounded video synthesis and world models for embodied AI. The 360°-Motion Dataset and the domain-adaptor-plus-annealed-sampling recipe offer a practical template for training on synthetic 4D data without domain shift. Key open questions remain: the model handles only global 6DoF motion (no local articulation like dancing or hand-waving), supports at most three entities, and cannot model inter-entity interactions, all of which are natural next steps. The evaluation is also limited to human pose recovery via GVHMR, leaving generalization to non-rigid or novel object categories unverified.
