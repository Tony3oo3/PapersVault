## Problem

Approximate machine unlearning suffers from catastrophic unlearning: erasing target data inadvertently degrades knowledge the model should retain, especially in intermediate feature-space regions between forgetting and remaining samples. Existing retention operations on remaining data fail to regularize these interpolation zones, leaving the unlearned model significantly divergent from a retrained one.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252740/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained classifier (ResNet-18 for CIFAR-10/SVHN, a two-layer CNN for MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, or ViT-B/16 for ImageNet), a forgetting subset D_f, a remaining subset D_r, and optionally class labels. In label-agnostic mode, ground-truth labels are replaced by sharpened (T=0.3) softmax predictions from the initial model. The output is an unlearned model f_U whose predictions approximate those of a model trained solely on D_r, evaluated via test accuracy on remaining/forgotten classes, training accuracy, and membership-inference ASR.

## Method

MixUnlearn introduces a generator–unlearner framework where a lightweight MixBlock (66K parameters, from AdAutoMix) learns to produce adversarial mixup samples by dynamically mixing one forgetting sample with one remaining sample via attention-weighted element-wise interpolation. The generator is trained with a novel contrastive loss (Eq. 3) that adversarially pushes the unlearner to reveal forgetting-class information while losing remaining-class knowledge—the reverse of the unlearning objective. The unlearner is then optimized with two contrastive losses: L_mix (Eq. 5) on the adversarial mixed samples and L_real (Eq. 6) on the original real samples, combined as L_unlearn = L_mix + ω·L_real. The generator is updated only every four unlearning iterations for efficiency, and the entire pipeline requires no explicit labels when operating in label-agnostic mode.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 class-level unlearning (label-aware), MixUnlearn achieves Test_r = 87.10%, Test_f = 0%, and ASR = 68.30%, closely matching the Retrain gold standard (86.80%, 0%, 67.98%). On SVHN, it reaches Test_r = 93.95% and ASR = 59.97%, again near Retrain (94.20%, 59.63%). In the label-agnostic setting on CIFAR-10, MixUnlearn achieves Test_r = 86.32% and ASR = 68.48%, substantially outperforming the best label-agnostic baseline LAF (82.01%, 51.34%). On ImageNet with ViT, the method achieves Test_f = 1.51% for class-level unlearning and Test = 74.23% for data-level unlearning, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10 (label-aware), MixUnlearn (Test_r=87.10%, Test_f=0%, ASR=68.30%) nearly matches Retrain and outperforms SISA by 14.1 points on Test_r, DSMixup by 22.8 points, and GLI by 2.3 points on Test_r while GLI catastrophically fails to forget (Test_f=47.28%). Against the strongest competitor LAF+R (Test_r=87.20%), MixUnlearn achieves perfect forgetting (Test_f=0 vs. 0.20) and a much closer ASR to Retrain (68.30 vs. 56.89), indicating superior privacy guarantees. On SVHN, MixUnlearn (93.95%) surpasses LAF+R (91.35%) by 2.6 points and T-S (92.89%) by 1.1 points on Test_r. In the label-agnostic setting, MixUnlearn outperforms LAF by 4.3 points on CIFAR-10 (86.32 vs. 82.01) and by 8.5 points on SVHN (93.40 vs. 84.89), while also beating the proposed L-Mix baseline by 4.0 points on CIFAR-10, demonstrating that adversarial mixup generation provides substantial gains over standard mixup.

## Relation to Prior Work

MixUnlearn sits at the intersection of approximate machine unlearning and adversarial data augmentation. It builds directly on the MixBlock generator from AdAutoMix (Qin et al., 2024) but repurposes it for a fundamentally different objective: instead of generating hard examples to improve classification, the generator is trained to produce mixup samples that reverse the unlearning direction (exposing forgetting information while eroding retained knowledge). The paper explicitly differentiates itself from DSMixup (Zhou et al., 2022), which uses mixup to accelerate exact retraining, and from GLI/UNSIR, which apply perturbation-based augmentation without targeting the intermediate-space failure mode. The novel contrastive losses (Eqs. 3, 5, 6) are the core methodological departure, framing unlearning as a two-sided contrastive problem over both real and synthesized mixed samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode—catastrophic unlearning in the interpolation zone between forgetting and remaining data—that prior retention-based methods (LAF+R, GLI, T-S) do not adequately handle. The label-agnostic variant is practically important for real-world deployments where labels are unavailable during unloading. However, the optimal termination strategy for unlearning remains an open question the authors explicitly flag, and the method's reliance on a pre-trained initial model for feature extraction (h_D) limits its applicability when the initial model is unavailable or must be discarded. Scaling beyond the tested architectures (ResNet-18, small CNN, ViT-B/16) and to more complex unlearning scenarios (multi-round, federated) remains to be explored.
