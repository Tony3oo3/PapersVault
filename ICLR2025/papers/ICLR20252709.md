## Problem

Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) model functional data as continuous functions, but their infinite-dimensional nature makes them prone to overfitting, especially with small or noisy datasets. Existing regularization approaches like Lipschitz constraints smooth across both the input coordinate domain and the latent space, causing over-smoothing of data instances and loss of fidelity. The paper addresses how to regularize the latent space to be well-behaved (smooth, predictable) without imposing smoothness constraints on the functional data itself.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a shared network F: X × Z → V where X is a coordinate space (3D spatial points for SDFs, light direction pairs for BRDFs, or scalar query points for operators) and Z is a low-dimensional latent code. A key practical constraint is that input coordinates vary per data instance in both location and count (e.g., different surface points per shape, different sensor readings), so the regularization must be discretization-agnostic. The output is a regularized latent space where the mapping from Z to the data manifold approximately preserves distances and angles (scaled isometry), enabling reliable interpolation, reconstruction from partial observations, and noise-robust prediction.

## Method

The paper interprets the latent-to-function mapping h: Z → F as a parametrization of a finite-dimensional Riemannian manifold embedded in the infinite-dimensional L² function space, with metric H(z) = ∫ J(x,z)ᵀJ(x,z) dμ_{F_z}(x) where J is the Jacobian w.r.t. z and μ_{F_z} is a function-dependent measure (e.g., concentrated near surfaces for SDFs). Isometric regularization enforces H(z) ≈ cI via a relaxed distortion measure that penalizes eigenvalue variance of H(z), regularizing only in the z-direction and leaving x-coordinates unconstrained to preserve data fidelity. To make this tractable, the authors develop an efficient approximation using Hutchinson's stochastic trace estimator (replacing full Jacobian computation with Jacobian-vector and vector-Jacobian products) and offline Monte Carlo samples from p(x; F_z), since the sampling distribution evolves during training. A latent augmentation (modified mix-up) strategy defines the latent probability density P_Z over which the distortion is averaged, and the entire procedure is agnostic to the number and locations of coordinate samples per instance.

## Results

On 2D surface reconstruction (MNIST, N=100), IsoDeepSDF achieves an average L1 error of 0.0283, outperforming DeepSDF (0.0366) and LipDeepSDF (0.0351). On 3D ShapeNet chair reconstruction (N=271), IsoDeepSDF reduces Chamfer distance to 0.001915 (avg) vs. 0.002202 for DeepSDF and 0.001999 for LipDeepSDF. On BRDF reconstruction (MERL, N=20), IsoAD achieves 37.35 PSNR and 0.9731 SSIM, substantially exceeding AD (31.00 PSNR) and LipAD (33.91 PSNR). For neural operators, IsoDONet consistently shows the lowest relative error across all noise levels on both reaction-diffusion and Darcy flow, with the slowest error growth as training noise increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: vanilla DeepSDF (Park et al., 2019) without regularization, and LipDeepSDF/LipAD/LipDONet (Liu et al., 2022) with Lipschitz regularization. IsoDeepSDF wins on all surface reconstruction metrics: on MNIST (N=100) it reduces average L1 error by 22.7% over DeepSDF and 19.4% over LipDeepSDF; on ShapeNet (N=542) it reduces Chamfer distance by 20.5% over DeepSDF and 7.0% over LipDeepSDF. The advantage is most pronounced under data scarcity — with only 100 MNIST training images, IsoDeepSDF's 0.0283 L1 error is 22.7% lower than DeepSDF's 0.0366, while the gap narrows at N=500 (0.0267 vs. 0.0288, 7.3% improvement). For BRDF reconstruction, IsoAD trained on just 20 materials (37.35 PSNR) outperforms both AD and LipAD trained on the full 80-material dataset (35.98 and 35.50 PSNR respectively), demonstrating superior data efficiency. For neural operators, IsoDONet consistently achieves the lowest relative error at every noise level tested, while LipDONet tends to over-smooth the output in the spatio-temporal domain (particularly visible in Darcy flow), and vanilla DONet severely overfits to noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the isometric regularization framework of Lee et al. (2022b) from finite-dimensional data to infinite-dimensional functional data represented by Implicit Neural Representations. It departs from Lipschitz regularization (Liu et al., 2022), which smooths across both the input coordinate domain X and the latent space Z, by regularizing only the Z-component via a Riemannian metric projected onto the data manifold, thereby preserving data fidelity in X. The discretization-agnostic formulation with function-dependent measures also addresses a limitation of Du et al. (2021), who required fixed sampling points and resolutions, making their approach inapplicable to scenarios with variable or sparse observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled geometric framework—rooted in Riemannian manifold theory—for balancing data fidelity and latent space structure in INR-based models, with practical algorithms (Hutchinson trace estimator, Monte Carlo sampling) that make the infinite-dimensional regularization computationally tractable. A notable open question is the choice of the function-dependent measure p(x;F_z), which the authors acknowledge is still somewhat ad hoc and data-dependent; a more systematic formulation tied to downstream task characteristics remains future work. The method's modality-agnostic nature (validated on SDFs, BRDFs, and neural operators) suggests broad applicability, though the regularization weight still requires per-dataset tuning.
