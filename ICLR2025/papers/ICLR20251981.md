## Problem

Existing real-world super-resolution training data generation methods cannot simultaneously satisfy three critical requirements: large-scale production, realistic degradation fidelity, and adaptability to arbitrary target domains. Hand-crafted degradation models (e.g., Real-ESRGAN, BSRGAN) produce synthetic data that diverges from real-world degradation patterns, while learning-based approaches (GANs, diffusion) struggle to generalize to unseen degradation distributions, creating a fundamental realism-adaptability trade-off.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two unpaired inputs: a real-world LR image (256×256 crop) serving as the degradation reference, and an unpaired HR image serving as the content reference. It outputs a realistic LR image that preserves the HR image's content while exhibiting the specific degradation characteristics of the reference LR image. The generated LR-HR pairs are then used to train downstream SR models (SwinIR, RRDB, HAT, ResShift, SwinIR-L) on standard benchmarks.

## Method

RealDGen decouples content and degradation into separate representations via two dedicated extractors: a VQVAE encoder fine-tuned with reconstruction learning on LR-HR pairs (content extractor) and a CNN-based network trained with contrastive learning (positive pairs share degradation but differ in content; negative pairs share content but differ in degradation) plus LR reconstruction loss (degradation extractor). A modulation block fuses the degradation representation into the content representation to form a conditioning vector for a Decoupled DDPM, which is trained on ~152,000 real LR images while partially fine-tuning the extractors for domain adaptation. During generation, the diffusion model denoises from a partially-noised degraded LR image (following SDEdit) rather than pure Gaussian noise, and a filtering mechanism re-extracts representations from multiple candidates to select the one with minimal content and degradation error, reducing diffusion stochasticity.

## Results

On the RealSR benchmark with SwinIR (PSNR-oriented), RealDGen achieves 26.094 PSNR / 0.7822 SSIM, outperforming Real-ESRGAN (24.395), BSRGAN (25.852), and SynDiff (25.589). On the DRealSR benchmark, SwinIR trained on RealDGen data reaches 28.721 PSNR / 0.8341 SSIM, and on the SmartPhone benchmark 28.737 PSNR / 0.8489 SSIM. For perceptual-oriented training, RealDGen yields the best LPIPS (0.2536) and FID (69.736) on RealSR with SwinIR, compared to SynDiff's 0.3835 LPIPS and 103.179 FID. The gains are consistent across all five backbone architectures (RRDB, SwinIR, HAT, SwinIR-L, ResShift) and all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RealDGen consistently outperforms all three main baselines across every benchmark and backbone. Against Real-ESRGAN (hand-crafted second-order degradation), the gain is largest: +1.70 dB PSNR for SwinIR on RealSR (26.094 vs 24.395) and +0.050 LPIPS improvement in perceptual training. Against BSRGAN (hand-crafted first-order degradation), RealDGen leads by +0.24 dB PSNR on RealSR and +0.68 dB on DRealSR. Against SynDiff (learning-based diffusion degradation), RealDGen achieves +0.51 dB PSNR on RealSR and a substantial +0.130 LPIPS reduction (0.2536 vs 0.3835) in perceptual-oriented training, indicating SynDiff's generated data introduces more perceptual artifacts. In the appendix comparison with DASR's contrastive degradation extractor (Wang et al., 2021a), RealDGen's degradation extractor yields +0.43 dB PSNR and +0.043 LPIPS improvement on RealSR, validating the paper's positive/negative sample construction strategy over DASR's patch-based approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

RealDGen extends the line of real-world SR data generation that began with hand-crafted degradation pipelines (BSRGAN, Real-ESRGAN) and moved toward learned degradation synthesis (SynDiff, GAN-based approaches). Its key conceptual departure is the explicit decoupling of content and degradation into separate learned representations—using a VQVAE-based content extractor trained via reconstruction and a contrastive-learning degradation extractor—then recombining them through a conditioned DDPM. This contrasts with prior diffusion-based methods like SynDiff, which model degradation holistically without separating it from content, and with hand-crafted methods that cannot adapt to arbitrary real LR degradation references. The modulation block that fuses degradation into content before conditioning the diffusion model is a novel architectural choice not present in prior SR data synthesis work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent practical bottleneck in real-world SR: the inability of existing data generators to simultaneously produce large-scale, realistic, and domain-adaptive training pairs. By enabling generation from arbitrary unpaired LR (degradation reference) and HR (content reference) images, RealDGen offers a practical recipe for domain-specific fine-tuning of SR models without collecting matched pairs. However, the diffusion-based inference remains slower than hand-crafted degradation (0.61s vs 0.11s per image), and the authors acknowledge that stochasticity still degrades fine textures. Open questions include automatic selection of the denoising step T based on degradation severity, integration of perceptual losses to preserve texture fidelity, and whether the decoupling framework extends naturally to video SR or other restoration tasks.
