## Problem

Small-scale MLLMs (s-MLLMs) are inherently limited by model capacity, and knowledge distillation from large-scale MLLMs to s-MLLMs remains unexplored. Two fundamental challenges exist: designing a lightweight architecture that retains sufficient expressiveness to absorb complex multimodal knowledge, and devising an effective strategy to transfer diverse, multi-task knowledge from a large teacher to a small student.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250339/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image resized to 336×336 (patched into 576 tokens of 14×14) and a text instruction, producing a free-form text response. The vision encoder (CLIP-ViT-L/14) and 2-layer MLP adaptor remain frozen or lightly tuned, while the LLM backbone (Qwen-1.5/2 at 0.5B or 1.8B) is sparsified into a MoE structure with top-k expert routing. Both teacher and student must share the same LLM family to ensure a consistent vocabulary space for accurate logit-level imitation.

## Method

LLaVA-MoD combines a sparse MoE student architecture with a two-stage progressive distillation strategy. For architecture, sparse upcycling replicates N FFNs as experts within the LLM, with a linear router applying top-k selection to balance capacity and compute. For distillation, the first stage (mimic distillation) proceeds in two sub-steps: dense-to-dense (D2D) aligns the student's output logits with the teacher via KL divergence on general captioning/conversation data, then dense-to-sparse (D2S) converts the student to MoE and distills specialized multi-task knowledge using KL divergence plus cross-entropy on ground-truth data. The second stage (preference distillation) uses KTO loss with the l-MLLM as reference model, pushing the student to assign higher probability to positive responses and lower probability to negative responses than the teacher, enabling the student to surpass the teacher on hallucination benchmarks.

## Results

LLaVA-MoD-2B (Qwen-2-1.5B) achieves an average score of 61.6 across seven comprehension benchmarks (GQA, VizWiz, ScienceQA, TextVQA, MME, MMBench, MMBench-CN), surpassing Qwen-VL-Chat-7B (56.7) by 8.8% while using only 0.3% of its training data (5M vs. 1450M samples) and 23% of activated parameters (2.2B vs. 9.6B). On hallucination benchmarks, LLaVA-MoD-2B achieves 11.2% response-level and 5.9% mention-level hallucination rates on Object HalBench, outperforming its 7B teacher (29.7% / 23.4%) and RLHF-V (12.2% / 7.5%). The 1B variant (Qwen-2-0.5B) reaches 55.7 average, beating SPHINX-Tiny (49.2) by 13.2% with fewer training samples (5M vs. 15M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA-MoD-2B (61.6 AVG) surpasses Qwen-VL-Chat-7B (56.7) by 8.8% and MoE-LLaVA-2B (55.3) by 6.3% on comprehension benchmarks, while matching VILA-3B (61.8) and MiniCPM-V (61.2) despite using far fewer training samples (5M vs. 51M/570M). It trails MiniCPM-V-2 (66.9) and DeepSeek-VL-7B (67.2), which benefit from substantially larger training data (570M and 2000M samples respectively). On hallucination benchmarks, LLaVA-MoD-2B (11.2% Resp, 5.9% Ment on Object HalBench) outperforms its own 7B teacher (29.7% / 23.4%) and RLHF-V (12.2% / 7.5%), and approaches RLAIF-V (8.5% / 4.3%) while using only 1.9B activated parameters vs. 7B. The 1B variant (55.7 AVG) beats SPHINX-Tiny (49.2) by 13.2% with 3× fewer training samples. Against standard KD and GKD baselines (Table 17), LLaVA-MoD's progressive distillation yields 4.2 and 2.5 points higher MME scores respectively, and dramatically lower hallucination (11.2 vs. 53.4/52.8 on Object HalBench). The paper also shows that KTO-based preference distillation outperforms DPO by 8.6 points on Object HalBench Resp (11.4 vs. 20.0), confirming the choice of loss function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015), sparse MoE architectures via upcycling (Komatsuzaki et al., 2022; Lepikhin et al., 2020), and preference optimization (KTO, Ethayarajh et al., 2024). It departs from prior MLLM work (LLaVA, MiniGPT-4, Qwen-VL) that focuses on data curation or model scaling by instead treating the large MLLM as a teacher and the small MoE-equipped MLLM as a student. The key conceptual departure is the progressive D2D→D2S→Preference pipeline, where the teacher is repurposed as a reference model in the preference stage, enabling the student to surpass the teacher on hallucination metrics rather than merely approximating it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical recipe—sparse upcycling + progressive KD + KTO-based preference distillation—offers a concrete, low-cost pathway (5M samples, ~960 GPU-hours) to deploy competitive MLLMs on resource-constrained hardware, with the 2B model matching or exceeding 7B baselines. A key limitation is the requirement that student and teacher share the same LLM family for vocabulary alignment, and loading both models simultaneously imposes significant memory overhead. Open questions include whether heterogeneous cross-family distillation is feasible, whether pre-extracting teacher logits can eliminate the dual-model memory cost, and whether the preference-distillation advantage over the teacher generalizes beyond hallucination to broader reasoning tasks.
