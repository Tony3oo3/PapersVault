## Problem

LLM-based sequential recommendation methods (e.g., E4SRec with 7B+ parameters) deliver ~20% gains over traditional models but are impractical for real-world deployment due to their size. The paper investigates whether most intermediate layers of LLMs are redundant for this task and whether a much smaller language model can match or exceed the performance of a full-size LLM-based recommender.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251280/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a user's interaction sequence of item IDs, which are mapped through a pre-trained ID embedding layer (from a TSR model like SASRec) and linearly up-projected to the LLM's hidden dimension (4096), then concatenated with prompt text embeddings from tokenization. The 4-layer student decoder produces a final hidden state that is linearly projected back to the original embedding dimension, and user-item scores are computed via dot product against all item embeddings for top-N ranking. Training uses LoRA to update only a small set of parameters (0.003B trainable vs. 0.944B total inference parameters).

## Method

SLMREC distills an 8-layer E4SRec teacher into a 4-layer student using feature-level (not logits-level) knowledge distillation. The teacher and student layers are grouped into B=4 blocks, and three regularizers align their intermediate representations: a cosine similarity loss for feature direction, an L2 norm loss for feature magnitude, and multiple supervision signals that apply cross-entropy at each intermediate block via learned dimension-reducing adapters. The total loss combines the standard cross-entropy with weighted distillation terms (λ₁=1.0, λ₂=0.1, λ₃=1.0). A theoretical justification shows that the stacked attention propagation across K layers is equivalent to a single matrix transformation, explaining why a shallower model guided by distillation can replicate the feature extraction of a deeper one. The approach is orthogonal to quantization and pruning, and supports both offline and online distillation settings.

## Results

On the Cloth dataset, SLMRec4←8 achieves HR@1=16.69%, HR@5=19.47%, NDCG@5=18.07%, and MRR=18.74%, matching or slightly exceeding the 32-layer E4SRec teacher (HR@1=16.71%, MRR=18.77%) while using only 4 decoder layers. On Movie, SLMRec4←8 outperforms E4SRec by ~0.5–0.55 points across all metrics (e.g., HR@1: 15.29% vs. 14.74%; MRR: 20.36% vs. 19.74%). In terms of efficiency, SLMREC uses only 13% of E4SRec's training parameters and 14% of inference parameters, yielding 6.6× and 8.0× speedups in training and inference time, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLMRec4←8 outperforms all traditional sequential recommendation baselines (Caser, GRU4Rec, BERT4Rec, SASRec, HGN, LightSANs) by a wide margin—e.g., on Cloth it beats the strongest TSR method HGN (MRR 18.27) by 0.47 points and SASRec (MRR 15.76) by nearly 3 points. It also surpasses all self-supervised SR methods (S3-Rec, DuoRec, MAERec) by 1.5–2.5 MRR points on Cloth. Against the generative LLM baseline Open-P5 (LLaMA), SLMRec4←8 leads on every reported metric (e.g., Cloth HR@1: 16.69 vs. 14.13; Movie HR@1: 15.29 vs. 12.66) while being orders of magnitude faster at inference (0.052 h vs. 4942 h). The most direct comparison is with the E-LLMRec baseline E4SRec (32-layer LLaMA-7B): SLMRec4←8 ties E4SRec on Cloth (HR@1 16.69 vs. 16.71) and slightly exceeds it on Movie (HR@1 15.29 vs. 14.74; MRR 20.36 vs. 19.74), all while using only 4 decoder layers versus 32 and achieving 6.6×/8.0× training/inference speedups. Notably, SLMRec4←8 also outperforms E4SRec₈ (its own 8-layer teacher) by ~0.4–0.6 MRR points, demonstrating that the distillation process itself adds value beyond mere layer reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the E4SRec architecture (Li et al., 2023) as both the teacher and student backbone, and on classical knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) as the training mechanism, but departs from prior LLM-based SR work by questioning whether full LLM depth is necessary at all. It connects to concurrent NLP findings on layer redundancy (Men et al., 2024; Gromov et al., 2024) but is the first to systematically investigate and exploit this redundancy specifically for sequential recommendation. Unlike prior compression approaches that use complex pruning or architecture search, SLMREC employs a deliberately simple feature-level distillation with cosine similarity, L2 norm, and multi-supervision regularizers, and provides a theoretical justification via optimization-dynamics interpretation of stacked attention layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing a 7B-parameter LLM-based SR model to under 1B parameters with 6.6×/8× training/inference speedups makes LLM-based sequential recommendation feasible for real-world platforms processing billions of daily interactions. The conceptual finding that 8 decoder layers suffice where 24–32 were assumed necessary challenges the prevailing scaling-law narrative in SR and suggests that the gains from LLMs in recommendation come primarily from hidden dimensionality and pre-trained initialization rather than depth. Key limitations include the inability to adapt to new items or domains via few-shot learning (requiring full retraining), and the evidence is confined to a single LLM family (LLaMA) and one dataset family (Amazon18), leaving open whether the depth-redundancy finding generalizes across architectures and recommendation modalities.
