## Problem

Despite FNO's Fourier-domain parameterization being widely believed to enable resolution-independent inference, the paper shows that neural operators suffer from discretization mismatch errors (DMEs): the outputs differ when the same operator processes different discretizations of the same input function. This error accumulates across Fourier layers and causes significant performance degradation in cross-resolution tasks (inferring at resolutions different from training), a gap that prior work on aliasing or discretization invariance (convergence to the continuum) did not address.

## Input / Output

The method takes discretized parametric functions (vorticity, diffusion coefficient, forcing, or wave-speed fields) on uniform grids at arbitrary resolutions (e.g., 32×32 through 256×256) and produces the corresponding solution function on the same or a different grid. A key design constraint is that the CROP lifting layer projects the input into a band-limited latent space with a fixed discretization independent of the input resolution, so the intermediate operator always sees the same grid size regardless of the input or output resolution. The projection layer then maps back to the output function space, potentially at a finer resolution than the band-limit, recovering high-frequency detail through a point-wise network.

## Method

CROP (Cross-Resolution Operator-learning Pipeline) wraps any intermediate neural operator between two specialized layers: a CROP lifting layer that maps the input to a band-limited latent feature function via global convolution with a band-limited kernel (fixing the latent discretization), and a CROP projection layer that maps the latent features back to the output space via global band-limited convolution combined with a fully connected point-wise network to recover frequencies beyond the band-limit. Because the intermediate operator (which can be FNO, U-Net, or any CNN-based architecture) operates on a fixed-resolution band-limited space, it is immune to DMEs and satisfies continuous-discrete equivalence. The theoretical analysis (Proposition 4.4) proves that DMEs in standard FNO grow with the number of layers and the Lipschitz constant of the activation, while CROP eliminates this accumulation by construction. Empirically, CROP achieves near-identical relative ℓ₂ error across all tested inference resolutions (e.g., 0.54% at 32×32, 64×64, 128×128, and 256×256 for Navier-Stokes) while also outperforming FNO, CNO, U-Net, ResNet, and DeepONet on learning accuracy for high-Reynolds-number flows and other PDE benchmarks.

## Results

On the Navier-Stokes equation with high Reynolds numbers, CROP achieves 3.84% relative ℓ₂ error at Re=5000 and 4.06% at Re=10000, outperforming all baselines (FNO: 7.25%/8.35%, CNO: 5.77%/4.66%, U-Net: 8.96%/8.75%, DeepONet: 16.93%/17.60%). In the cross-resolution test (trained at 64×64), CROP maintains a consistent 0.54% error across all inference resolutions (32×32 to 256×256), while FNO degrades to 9.45% at 32×32 and alias-free FNO to 42.08%. On Darcy flow, Helmholtz, and Poisson equations, CROP achieves 1.11%, 0.52%, and 0.80% relative ℓ₂ error respectively, again outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CROP outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated PDE. Against FNO (Li et al., 2021), CROP nearly halves the error on high-Reynolds Navier-Stokes (3.84% vs. 7.25% at Re=5000) and, critically, eliminates the resolution bias: CROP stays at 0.54% across all inference resolutions while FNO jumps to 9.45% at 32×32. Against CNO (Raonic et al., 2023), CROP wins on Navier-Stokes (3.84% vs. 5.77%) and on all three additional PDEs (e.g., Poisson: 0.80% vs. 1.67%), though CNO is competitive at Re=10000 (4.66% vs. 4.06%). Against U-Net and ResNet (from PDEBench), CROP is substantially better, with ResNet failing catastrophically (57.79% at Re=5000). Against DeepONet (Lu et al., 2021), CROP achieves roughly 4× lower error on Navier-Stokes (3.84% vs. 16.93%) and 15× lower on Poisson (0.80% vs. 12.26%), while also maintaining cross-resolution consistency that DeepONet cannot provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Fourier Neural Operator (Li et al., 2021) as its primary analytical target, and engages critically with the aliasing-error and continuous-discrete equivalence framework of Raonic et al. (2023) and the formal discretization-invariance definition of Kovachki et al. (2023). Its key conceptual departure is distinguishing discretization invariance (parameters decoupled from resolution) from cross-resolution robustness, introducing the discretization mismatch error (DME) as a separate, previously unformalized source of degradation. The proposed CROP pipeline wraps around existing intermediate operators (FNO, U-Net, CNNs) rather than replacing them, positioning itself as an architectural complement rather than a new operator family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that FNO—despite being the canonical discretization-invariant neural operator—suffers from DMEs that grow with depth and propagate through autoregressive time steps challenges a core assumption underpinning many cross-resolution scientific ML applications (climate modeling, fluid dynamics). CROP provides a practical, drop-in solution that eliminates DMEs while also improving same-resolution accuracy, but its applicability is currently limited to rectangular domains with uniform grids. Open questions include whether the band-limited latent space plus point-wise network combination has universality guarantees, and whether CROP extends to irregular meshes and non-periodic domains.
