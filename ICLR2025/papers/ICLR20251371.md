## Problem

Discriminative probabilistic modeling (DPM) over a continuous domain requires computing a partition function via integration, which is intractable for self-supervised representation learning. The paper shows through finite-sample generalization analysis that the widely used InfoNCE-based global contrastive loss (GCL) corresponds to a crude uniform approximation of the MIS weights, introducing a non-diminishing error term that limits representation quality. The gap is a principled, computationally tractable way to approximate the partition function that reduces this error.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired RGB images and natural language captions (e.g., 2.7M pairs from CC3M, 9.2M from CC12M) and produces 256-dimensional embeddings via a ResNet-50 image encoder and a DistilBERT text encoder, each followed by a linear projection head. The learned embeddings are evaluated zero-shot on image-text retrieval (Flickr30k, MSCOCO) and image classification (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-1k, ImageNet-R). Beyond the standard contrastive setup, the method additionally maintains per-sample scalar variables ζ ∈ ℝⁿ that encode data popularity, requiring 2n extra floating-point numbers of memory.

## Method

NUCLR formulates self-supervised pretraining as maximum likelihood estimation of a conditional density p(y|x) over a continuous space, approximating the intractable partition function via multiple importance sampling (MIS) with the balance heuristic. The key insight is that the MIS weights require the sum of conditional densities q⁽ʲ⁾ = Σ p(yⱼ|xⱼ′) across all anchors, which GCL approximates uniformly; instead, NUCLR solves a convex optimization (Eq. 8) to learn a non-uniform approximation ζ̃ that captures data popularity. Training uses a Jacobi-type alternating scheme: one gradient step updates the model parameters w (via a contrastive objective with non-uniform negative weights εⱼ = exp(−ζⱼ/τ)), and another updates ζ, with a moving-average trick (inherited from SogCLR) to obtain low-variance stochastic gradients at small batch sizes. A scalar ξ = max{ξₜ₋₁, ‖ζₜ‖∞} is introduced to cap the repulsive strength on positive pairs during early training, and ζ is frozen for the first 5 epochs as a warm-up. When ζ is fixed to zero, NUCLR reduces exactly to SogCLR, making the learned non-uniform margins the sole source of improvement.

## Results

On CC3M, NUCLR achieves a mean score of 40.97 across five downstream tasks, outperforming the strongest baseline SogCLR (39.86) and CLIP (35.38). On the larger CC12M dataset, NUCLR reaches a mean of 45.61 versus SogCLR's 44.73 and CLIP's 40.40. The most notable gains are on retrieval (e.g., Flickr30k Recall@1: 53.55 vs. 52.20 for SogCLR on CC3M; 60.45 vs. 59.28 on CC12M) and on the robustness benchmark ImageNet-R (43.82 vs. 42.65 on CC3M; 55.24 vs. 54.54 on CC12M). NUCLR also leads on CIFAR-100 classification (37.45 on CC3M; 28.16 on CC12M) while matching SogCLR on ImageNet-1k (40.49 vs. 40.40 on CC3M; 49.82 vs. 49.82 on CC12M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NUCLR consistently outperforms all five baselines (CLIP, DCL, SigLIP, CyCLIP, SogCLR) on the mean score for both CC3M and CC12M. Against its closest competitor SogCLR, NUCLR gains +1.11 mean points on CC3M and +0.88 on CC12M, with the largest relative improvement on ImageNet-R (+1.17 on CC3M, +0.70 on CC12M), suggesting the learned non-uniform margins particularly benefit robustness. On CC12M, NUCLR and SogCLR tie exactly on ImageNet-1k (49.82), but NUCLR leads on every other metric. Compared to CLIP, the standard InfoNCE-based baseline, NUCLR improves the mean by +5.59 on CC3M and +5.21 on CC12M, with the largest single-task gap on Flickr30k retrieval (+7.22 on CC3M, +5.24 on CC12M). SigLIP and CyCLIP, which use sigmoid or cyclic losses, trail NUCLR by 4.76 and 4.31 mean points on CC3M respectively, indicating that the MIS-based non-uniform weighting provides a clear advantage over these alternative contrastive formulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the multiple importance sampling (MIS) technique from computer graphics rendering (Veach & Guibas, 1995) to compute the intractable partition function in discriminative probabilistic modeling over continuous domains, a problem previously addressed only via MCMC, NCE, or energy-based models. It shows that the widely used Global Contrastive Loss (GCL) and its SogCLR algorithm (Yuan et al., 2022) emerge as a special case of the MIS-based empirical risk when a crude uniform approximation is used for the balance-heuristic weights. The key departure is replacing this uniform approximation with a non-parametric convex optimization that learns data-dependent 'popularity' scores, yielding the NUCLR algorithm as a principled generalization of SogCLR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finite-sample generalization analysis reveals that GCL/InfoNCE incurs a non-diminishing Monte Carlo integration error due to the uniform approximation, providing a theoretical explanation for residual suboptimality in current contrastive pretraining. The learned popularity scores offer a principled mechanism for non-uniform negative margins, and the MLE grounding provides statistical convergence guarantees absent in energy-based alternatives like JEPA. Open questions include whether a generative model can be learned within this discriminative framework, and whether the 2n stored popularity scalars can be replaced by a learned neural predictor for scalability to larger datasets.
