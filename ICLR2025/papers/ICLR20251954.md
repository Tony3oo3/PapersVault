## Problem

Building joint audio-video generative models from pre-trained single-modal diffusion models remains challenging: training-based approaches are architecture-specific and expensive, while training-free guidance methods degrade single-modal fidelity. The paper addresses how to integrate arbitrary pre-trained single-modal diffusion models into a cooperative joint generator that is model-agnostic, computationally lightweight, and improves both cross-modal alignment and per-modality quality simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251954/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (optional, used in the OOD setting with 10% dropout for classifier-free guidance) and Gaussian noise as input, and produces a synchronized audio-video pair (1.6 s at 64×64/10 fps/16 kHz for the in-domain setting; 2.0 s at 256×256/8 fps/16 kHz for the out-of-domain setting). The base diffusion models are treated as frozen black boxes—only their noise-prediction outputs are consumed—while the lightweight discriminator (12.7 M–132 M parameters vs. 133 M–2.86 B for base models) is the sole trainable component. Inference uses DPM-Solver with 20–50 NFE and adds the discriminator's gradient to each base model's prediction at every denoising step.

## Method

The key theoretical insight is that the joint score ∇log q(x_t, y_t) decomposes into marginal scores (estimated by the frozen base models) plus conditional scores, and the latter can be approximated by the gradient of an optimal GAN-style discriminator trained to distinguish real audio-video pairs from independently generated fake pairs. Concretely, a two-stream ResBlock discriminator D_θ(x_t, y_t, t) is trained with a combined objective: a standard discriminator loss L_disc (pushing D→1 on real pairs, D→0 on fake pairs) plus a DLSM-inspired denoising regularization L_denoise that forces the discriminator's input gradient to match the residual between ground-truth noise and base-model noise predictions, stabilizing the score estimate. At inference, the joint noise prediction at each timestep is the sum of the two base models' outputs and the scaled gradient of log(D/(1−D)) with respect to each modality's noisy input, enabling cooperative generation without any backpropagation through the base models. This makes the approach fully model-agnostic: swapping in new state-of-the-art single-modal generators requires only retraining the small discriminator on paired data.

## Results

In the in-domain (IND) setting on Landscape, MMDisCo improves MM-Diffusion's FVD from 447 to 405, FAD from 5.78 to 5.52, and IB-AV from 0.156 to 0.162, using only 12.7M additional parameters (vs. 133M for the base model). In the out-of-domain (OOD) setting on Landscape, Auffusion/VideoCrafter2+MMDisCo achieves FVD 687, FAD 7.86, and IB-AV 0.137, substantially outperforming sequential T2V→V2A baselines (e.g., VideoCrafter2→DiffFoley: FAD 9.14, IB-AV 0.111). On VGGSound, the same configuration reaches FAD 4.79 and IB-AV 0.201, compared to 13.11 and 0.105 for VideoCrafter2→DiffFoley. Training requires only ~1.5–2× less compute than fine-tuning base models, with a 10–22% inference overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the sequential T2V→V2A baselines on Landscape (OOD), MMDisCo with Auffusion/VideoCrafter2 achieves FAD 7.86 and IB-AV 0.137, beating VideoCrafter2→DiffFoley (FAD 9.14, IB-AV 0.111) by 14% on FAD and 23% on IB-AV, and VideoCrafter2→SpecVQGAN (FAD 11.63, IB-AV 0.074) by 32% on FAD and 85% on IB-AV. On VGGSound, the advantage is even larger: FAD drops from 13.11 (VideoCrafter2→DiffFoley) to 4.79, and IB-AV rises from 0.105 to 0.201. In the IND setting, MMDisCo improves upon MM-Diffusion itself on all three metrics (FVD 447→405, FAD 5.78→5.52, IB-AV 0.156→0.162), demonstrating that the guidance module adds value even when the base model was already trained jointly. The AudioLDM/AnimateDiff variant of MMDisCo shows a smaller IB-AV gain (0.093) compared to the Auffusion/VideoCrafter2 variant (0.134), suggesting that stronger base models benefit more from the joint guidance. Overall, MMDisCo consistently wins on cross-modal alignment (IB-AV) and audio fidelity (FAD) across all settings, while video fidelity (FVD) is preserved or slightly improved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classifier guidance (C-guide) from single-modal conditional generation to joint multimodal generation, replacing the classifier with a GAN-style discriminator whose optimal solution provides a density-ratio-based score correction. It builds directly on DDPM's score-matching framework, the C-guide decomposition (Song et al., 2021; Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021), and DLSM (Chao et al., 2022) for gradient stabilization, while drawing on the GAN optimal-discriminator result (Goodfellow et al., 2014) to derive the guidance signal. Unlike CoDi (Tang et al., 2023), which requires architectural modifications tied to specific base models, and Xing et al. (2024), which uses training-free embedding-space alignment, MMDisCo treats base models as black boxes and trains only a lightweight discriminator, making it model-agnostic and theoretically grounded in sampling from the true joint distribution rather than an approximate embedding-space proxy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, low-cost pathway to combine any pair of pre-trained audio and video diffusion models into a joint generator without retraining or architecturally modifying the base models, with only 10–20% inference overhead and ~2× training speedup over fine-tuning. A key limitation is that output quality is bounded by the base models, and the text-conditioning pipeline (especially for audio) remains a weak point, as the captions derived from video frames are poorly suited for audio generation. Open questions include how to design discriminators that target specific alignment dimensions (temporal vs. semantic), whether introducing simulated misaligned pairs as negative samples can further improve temporal synchronization, and how to better integrate text conditions for both modalities simultaneously.
