## Problem

Evaluating LLMs on large benchmarks (e.g., MMLU with 57 tasks, FLAN with 66 tasks) is prohibitively expensive due to sequential autoregressive decoding, yet it remains unclear how to reduce the number of tasks without degrading evaluation quality. Existing transferability estimation methods rely on model finetuning or Fisher information, which are computationally infeasible at LLM scale, leaving a gap in scalable, training-free approaches for identifying redundant or overlapping tasks within a benchmark.

## Input / Output

The method takes a benchmark of N tasks (each providing an instruction, a pool of exemplars, and test questions) and a single LLM used solely for in-context learning queries. It produces a reduced subset of k representative tasks (k ≪ N, e.g., 3 out of 57) whose evaluation scores approximate the full-benchmark scores for any model. The pipeline is entirely training-free and gradient-free: the LLM is never updated, only queried with cross-task exemplar contexts to build an N×N transferability matrix, after which all downstream computation (clustering, embedding, subset selection) is classical optimization.

## Method

BENTO first estimates an In-Context Transferability (ICT) matrix A by, for every ordered task pair (i, j), prompting an LLM with task-i's instruction and L sampled exemplars as context, then measuring accuracy on task-j's test questions; column-normalizing A yields per-task embedding vectors. From A, a Euclidean-distance similarity matrix S is constructed, and a Laplacian Eigenmaps (LE) embedding A′ is computed to capture local neighborhood structure while suppressing long-range noise; a cosine similarity matrix S′ is then derived from A′. Task selection is formulated as a facility location (FL) objective—maximizing the sum over all tasks of the maximum similarity to any selected task—which is submodular and solved greedily. Two variants are offered: BENTO-sim (using S directly) and BENTO-le (using S′ from the LE embedding), with the latter excelling at very small k and the former at larger k. The approach reduces MMLU to 3 tasks (~5%) with <4% NRMSE, outperforming random, GPT-4-prompted, and BM25-based baselines across 14 evaluated LLMs.

## Results

On MMLU (57 tasks), BENTO-le achieves a normalized RMSE (NRMSE) of 0.029 with only 3 selected tasks (~5% of the benchmark), while BENTO-sim reaches 0.026 at k=10. On FLAN (66 tasks), BENTO-sim achieves NRMSE of 0.04 at k=3 and BENTO-le reaches 0.07 at k=4. Across 14 evaluated LLMs (ranging from TinyLlama to GPT-4o), the reduced 5% benchmark induces less than 4% absolute difference in evaluation scores compared to the full benchmark, with per-model deviations typically between -1.8 and +3.8 points on MMLU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BENTO consistently outperforms all four baselines (Random, GPT4, BM25-sim, BM25-le) on both MMLU and FLAN. On MMLU at k=3, BENTO-le achieves NRMSE 0.029 versus GPT4's 0.093, BM25-sim's 0.177, BM25-le's 0.217, and Random's 0.144 — a 3–7× improvement. The GPT4 baseline shows an anomalous spike at k=7 (NRMSE jumps to 0.160) because it selects 'professional law' based on surface-level reasoning, whereas ICT clustering places that task in the biology cluster, revealing hidden structural relationships. On FLAN, BENTO-sim achieves NRMSE 0.04 at k=3 compared to GPT4's 1.86 and BM25-sim's 2.57, though GPT4 performs relatively better on FLAN than MMLU because task names there (e.g., SST-2) are more informative. A consistent pattern emerges: BENTO-le (using Laplacian Eigenmaps) excels at small k where local neighborhood structure dominates, while BENTO-sim (using raw Euclidean similarity) performs better at larger k where global structure matters more.

## Relation to Prior Work

BENTO repurposes in-context learning (ICL) as a training-free transferability metric, departing from prior transferability estimation methods (LEEP, Fisher information, Bayesian optimization) that require per-task finetuning or gradient computation. The task selection step draws on the classical facility location formulation (Cornuejols et al., 1977) and Laplacian Eigenmaps (Belkin & Niyogi, 2003) for dimensionality reduction, combining them in a novel pipeline. Compared to existing benchmark reduction approaches (Ye et al., 2023; Polo et al., 2024; Vivek et al., 2024), BENTO requires only a single model's ICL performance rather than multi-model training data or item response theory, making it substantially more efficient in data collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 95% task reduction with under 4% evaluation deviation offers a concrete, scalable path to cutting LLM evaluation costs during rapid model development cycles, and the training-free, gradient-free design makes it broadly applicable. The paper acknowledges a fundamental trade-off: reduced benchmarks are inherently less diverse and more vulnerable to adversarial attacks, a limitation that remains open. Future work could explore adaptive hyperparameter selection (the constant c in the similarity kernel showed dataset-dependent sensitivity), extension to dynamically growing benchmarks, and whether the ICL-based transferability structure is stable across model families or shifts as models improve.
