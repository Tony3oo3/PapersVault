## Problem

Sliced optimal transport methods on spheres (SSW, S3W) reduce the cubic cost of full OT but still require pairwise distance evaluation that scales quadratically with the number of distributions, making group-level analysis expensive. Linear OT embeddings (LOT, LCOT) solve this by mapping distributions into L² spaces where pairwise distances are cheap, but no such linear embedding existed for hyperspherical distributions that preserves intrinsic spherical geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252958/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes discrete spherical probability measures (e.g., 40,962-vertex cortical surface meshes with sulcal depth normalized to probability vectors, or point clouds mapped to S² via a Set Transformer autoencoder) and produces an L² embedding per distribution plus a scalar LSSOT distance between any pair. A key practical detail is that for atlas-based registration, the fixed surface's embedding is precomputed once, so each training iteration only computes the moving surface's embedding and takes an L² inner product, halving the per-step cost compared to methods that process both surfaces. The number of Monte Carlo slices L and a reference size M (discrete uniform measure) are hyperparameters controlling accuracy–speed tradeoff.

## Method

LSSOT extends Linear Circular Optimal Transport (LCOT) to hyperspheres by slicing: for each slice U ∈ V₂(ℝᵈ) (a 2D orthonormal frame on the Stiefel manifold), the spherical distribution is projected onto a great circle via P^U(x) = Uᵀx/‖Uᵀx‖, and the LCOT embedding of the resulting circular distribution is computed as ν̂^S(x,U) = F_{P^U_#ν}^{-1}(P^U(x) − E(P^U_#ν) + 1/2). The LSSOT distance is the L² norm of the difference of these embeddings, integrated over the Stiefel manifold with respect to the uniform measure σ. The paper proves LSSOT is a true metric by relating the Spherical Radon Transform to Groemer's (d−2)-Hemispherical Transform and establishing its injectivity on continuous functions. Computationally, for K distributions the pairwise cost drops from O(K²·D) (where D is per-pair distance cost) to O(KLN(d+log N+1)), since each distribution's embedding is computed once and all pairwise distances reduce to L² inner products. Numerically, poles (where Uᵀx = 0) are handled by an ε-cap that redistributes their mass uniformly onto the remaining projected points.

## Results

On cortical surface registration (NKI and ADNI, 40,962 vertices per surface), LSSOT achieves the best Dice score across all four hemisphere/dataset scenarios (e.g., 0.8462 on NKI-Left, 0.8119 on ADNI-Left) and the lowest edge distortion (e.g., 0.2365 on NKI-Left, 0.0979 on ADNI-Left). Training time per epoch is ~85–101 s for LSSOT versus ~1,094–1,362 s for SSW, a >10× speedup while matching or exceeding SSW's registration quality. In point cloud interpolation (ModelNet), LSSOT-based gradient flow produces smooth, structurally faithful interpolations comparable to Spherical OT but at substantially lower computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSSOT is on par with SSW (Bonet et al., ICLR 2023) in registration quality—both achieve the top two Dice scores in every scenario—yet LSSOT trains in ~100 s/epoch versus SSW's ~1,100–1,360 s/epoch, a >10× speedup. Against S3W (Tran et al., 2024), LSSOT wins on Dice in 3 of 4 scenarios (e.g., 0.8462 vs. 0.8498 on NKI-Left is a near-tie, but 0.8119 vs. 0.7733 on ADNI-Left is a clear win) and on edge distortion in all four scenarios. Compared to SWD (Flamary et al., JMLR 2021), LSSOT achieves substantially higher Dice (e.g., 0.8462 vs. 0.7984 on NKI-Left) and lower edge distortion (0.2365 vs. 0.3442). FreeSurfer (Fischl, 2012) is outperformed by LSSOT on Dice and edge distortion across all scenarios, though FreeSurfer uses iterative per-pair optimization rather than a single feed-forward pass. In the point cloud interpolation experiment (ModelNet), LSSOT-based gradient flow produces visually comparable interpolations to Spherical OT (Cui et al., 2019) and SSW, but with the linear-embedding advantage that the fixed template embedding is computed once, making each training iteration cheaper than methods that must process both surfaces per step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Linear Circular Optimal Transport (LCOT) framework of Martin et al. (2023) from the circle S¹ to general hyperspheres S^{d-1} by combining it with the spherical slicing strategy of Bonet et al. (2023). The foundational idea of embedding distributions into L² spaces for efficient pairwise comparison comes from the Linear Optimal Transport framework of Wang et al. (2013), which was previously applied to Euclidean and circular settings but not to spheres. The key conceptual departure is the use of the Semicircle Transform (related to Groemer's integral transformation) to prove injectivity and hence full metricity, rather than the Hemispherical Transform approach used in the SSW analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LSSOT provides a practical ~10× speedup over SSW for pairwise spherical distribution comparisons while maintaining comparable registration quality, making it particularly well-suited for atlas-based registration where the template embedding is computed once. The proof that SSW is a true metric (not merely a pseudo-metric) for continuous densities resolves an open question from Bonet et al. (2023). A key limitation is that LSSOT is defined only for probability measures, so normalizing arbitrary spherical signals into distributions may lose information; the authors outline extensions to positive signals with unequal mass and transport L^p for non-positive signals as immediate future work.
