## Problem

Current 4D point cloud video recognition methods (PSTNet, P4Transformer, PST-Transformer) rely on iterative frame-by-frame traversal combined with dense query operations across neighboring frames to extract temporal motion, resulting in massive computational redundancy and overlapping point embeddings. The paper identifies that this paradigm incurs 23× slower inference and 60× more parameters than necessary, while performance gains from increasingly sophisticated 4D convolutions have begun to saturate on standard benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251949/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw point cloud video sequences of shape N×T×3 (2048 points per frame, 4–24 frames depending on dataset and temporal stride) and outputs a single action class label for recognition or per-frame action labels for dense segmentation. Notably, the model is extremely lightweight at 0.72M parameters and 0.55G FLOPs (16-frame MSR-Action3D), and on NTU-RGBD it processes 24 frames at temporal step 2 with only 0.9M parameters and 7.8G FLOPs, compared to 65.2M parameters and 48.6G FLOPs for PST-Transformer.

## Method

PvNeXt replaces iterative dense querying with a personalized one-shot query via two modules. The Motion Imitator selects M anchor points per frame via FPS, ball-queries K nearest neighbors from the single next frame (t+1), aggregates them by mean pooling into a synthetic target, and computes motion as a simple index-wise subtraction (E − D), yielding a per-anchor displacement vector. The Single-Step Motion Encoder then synthesizes a virtual frame by adding this motion to the anchor-based point groups, and performs exactly one ball query from each original-frame point to its corresponding virtual-frame point to extract spatio-temporal features. These features are processed through a multi-stage PointNet++-like hierarchical encoder (3 stages for MSR-Action3D, 5 for NTU-RGBD) with progressive FPS downsampling. The key insight is that each frame requires only a single query to its own virtual frame rather than looping over all neighboring frames, eliminating both the frame-traversal overhead and the redundant overlapping embeddings while still capturing inter-frame dynamics.

## Results

On MSR-Action3D, PvNeXt achieves state-of-the-art accuracy of 94.77% (24-frame) and 93.93% (16-frame), surpassing the previous best PST-Transformer (93.73% / 91.98%) by 1.04% and 1.95% respectively. The method uses only 0.55G FLOPs and 0.72M parameters, compared to 32.56G FLOPs and 44.13M parameters for PST-Transformer, yielding a 23× inference speedup and 26× training speedup. On NTU-RGBD (cross-subject), PvNeXt reaches 89.2% accuracy, and when integrated into PST-Transformer, boosts it from 91.0% to 91.4%. On HOI4D action segmentation, PvNeXt achieves 78.5% accuracy and 84.5 Edit score, outperforming PPTr (77.4% / 80.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PvNeXt outperforms all supervised baselines on MSR-Action3D across every frame setting, with the largest margins over PST-Transformer (+1.95% at 16-frame, +1.04% at 24-frame) and PSTNet++ (+3.65% at 16-frame). The efficiency gains are dramatic: PvNeXt uses 59× fewer FLOPs and 61× fewer parameters than PST-Transformer while achieving higher accuracy, and runs 23× faster at inference. On NTU-RGBD, standalone PvNeXt (89.2%) trails PSTNet++ (91.4%) and PST-Transformer (91.0%), but augmenting PST-Transformer with PvNeXt's one-step query branch lifts it to 91.4%, matching the best result. On HOI4D action segmentation, PvNeXt beats PPTr by 1.1% accuracy and 4.4 Edit points, and P4Transformer by 7.3% accuracy. Against the SSM-based MAMBA4D (93.38% at 32 frames), PvNeXt achieves higher accuracy (93.93% at 16 frames, 94.77% at 24 frames) with far fewer computational resources.

## Relation to Prior Work

PvNeXt inherits its hierarchical backbone design from PointNet++ (FPS-based progressive downsampling, ball query, max-pooling) and PointNeXt (inverted residual bottlenecks, separable MLPs), but fundamentally departs from the dominant 4D point cloud video paradigm established by PSTNet, P4Transformer, and PST-Transformer. Those methods rely on iterative temporal looping combined with dense querying of neighboring frames to extract motion, which the authors argue introduces substantial computational redundancy through frame traversal and overlapping point-set embeddings. PvNeXt replaces this with a two-stage 'personalized one-shot query': a Motion Imitator first learns per-anchor displacement to synthesize a virtual frame, and a Single-Step Motion Encoder then performs a single ball-query from the original frame to that virtual frame, eliminating all recurrent loops and multi-frame dense queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that dense iterative querying of adjacent frames is largely redundant and that a single query to a learned virtual frame captures sufficient temporal dynamics—challenges a core design assumption in 4D point cloud video learning and offers a compelling efficiency argument (23× inference speedup, 60× fewer parameters on MSR-Action3D). However, the practical impact is tempered: on the more challenging NTU-RGBD benchmark, PvNeXt alone (89.2%) underperforms PST-Transformer (91.0%), and the method degrades noticeably on very short sequences (4 frames: 75.75% vs. 81.14%). Open questions include whether the one-shot virtual-frame strategy generalizes to dense prediction tasks (the authors acknowledge limited gains on HOI4D action segmentation), whether it scales to longer sequences where MAMBA4D-style SSM backbones may still have an advantage, and whether the geometric detail lost by avoiding dense querying becomes a bottleneck in more demanding 3D perception tasks.
