## Problem

State-of-the-art Text2SQL methods rely on in-context learning with closed-source LLMs like GPT-4, limiting their applicability in open and privacy-sensitive scenarios. Existing fine-tuning approaches for open-source LLMs train only on the SQL generation task, causing the model to lose other critical capabilities such as schema linking and instruction-following, which are essential for robust SQL generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250449/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a database schema (table descriptions, column names, and a few sample rows) together with a natural language question (optionally accompanied by a hint) as input, and produces a single SQL query whose execution results must match the ground-truth. During the MCP inference pipeline, the input is progressively transformed: the full schema is first simplified via schema linking, then an intermediate SQL is generated, checked against a SQL executor for errors, and optionally refined via continuation writing before the final query is emitted.

## Method

ROUTE consists of two stages. In training, Multitask Supervised Fine-Tuning (MSFT) simultaneously fine-tunes the LLM on four SQL-related tasks—Text2SQL, Schema Linking, Noise Correction, and Continuation Writing—using synthetic data constructed from SPIDER and BIRD training sets after a noisy-correspondence filtering step that removes semantically inconsistent question-SQL pairs. In inference, Multitask Collaboration Prompting (MCP) decomposes SQL generation into three collaborative steps: (1) an enhanced schema linking that merges LLM-predicted relevant tables/columns with entities extracted from a pseudo-SQL query to reduce prompt complexity while avoiding missed entities; (2) SQL generation followed by noise correction, where the LLM checks the generated query against execution error feedback and proposes a fix; and (3) continuation writing for hard queries (involving more than two tables), where the LLM completes a truncated SQL prefix to refine the final output. This multitask design preserves each sub-capability during training and leverages their complementarity at inference to reduce hallucination risk.

## Results

On SPIDER, ROUTE+Qwen2.5-14B achieves 87.3% execution accuracy (EX) and 80.9% test-suite accuracy (TS) on the development set, and 87.1% EX on the test set, setting the best results among all fine-tuning-based methods. On BIRD, the same model reaches 60.9% EX and 65.2% Valid Efficiency Score (VES), surpassing all open-source fine-tuning baselines and even exceeding several GPT-4 prompting methods (DIN-SQL, DAIL-SQL, MAC-SQL) on BIRD EX. ROUTE+Llama3-8B achieves 86.0% SPIDER Dev-EX and 57.3% BIRD Dev-EX, outperforming CODES-7B+SFT (85.4/57.2) and DTS-SQL-7B (82.7/55.8). On SPIDER-variant benchmarks (SYN, Realistic, DK), ROUTE achieves an average of 75.1% EX with Llama3-8B, compared to 72.5% for SFT alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among fine-tuning baselines, ROUTE+Qwen2.5-14B outperforms CODES-15B+SFT by 2.4 points on SPIDER Dev-EX (87.3 vs 84.9) and 2.4 points on BIRD Dev-EX (60.9 vs 58.5), and beats SENSE-13B by 3.2 points on SPIDER Dev-EX (87.3 vs 84.1) and 5.4 points on BIRD Dev-EX (60.9 vs 55.5). ROUTE+Llama3-8B surpasses DTS-SQL-7B by 3.3 points on SPIDER Dev-EX (86.0 vs 82.7) and 1.5 points on BIRD Dev-EX (57.3 vs 55.8). Against GPT-4 prompting methods, ROUTE+Qwen2.5-14B exceeds DIN-SQL+GPT-4 by 4.5 points on SPIDER Dev-EX and 10.2 points on BIRD Dev-EX, and surpasses MAC-SQL+GPT-4 on SPIDER Test-EX (87.1 vs 82.8) and BIRD Dev-EX (60.9 vs 59.4), though it trails MCS-SQL+GPT-4 by 2.2 points on SPIDER Dev-EX (87.3 vs 89.5) and 2.5 points on BIRD Dev-EX (60.9 vs 63.4). On SPIDER-variant benchmarks, ROUTE (Llama3-8B) achieves 75.1% average EX, outperforming SFT alone (72.5%) and the base model (57.0%), with the largest gains on SPIDER-Realistic (80.9 vs 76.8) and SPIDER-DK (74.6 vs 72.0). The MCP component alone (without MSFT) still provides meaningful gains over zero-shot baselines, e.g., +5.5 points on SPIDER Dev-EX for Qwen2.5-7B (78.3 vs 72.5), confirming the transferability of the collaboration strategy across model sizes up to 70B.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROUTE positions itself at the intersection of two existing lines of Text2SQL research: prompting-based pipelines (DIN-SQL, MAC-SQL, DAIL-SQL) that rely on closed-source GPT-4, and fine-tuning-based methods (DTS-SQL, CODES, SENSE) that train specialized open-source LLMs. It departs from prior fine-tuning work by replacing single-task SFT (Text2SQL only) with multitask SFT that jointly trains schema linking, noise correction, and continuation writing, and from prior prompting pipelines by replacing complex multi-agent decomposition with a simpler three-step collaborative prompting strategy (MCP) designed for smaller models. The enhanced schema linking step directly incorporates the pseudo-SQL technique from PET-SQL (Li et al., 2024d), and the noisy correspondence filtering draws on the observation that even carefully annotated datasets contain semantic inconsistencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ROUTE demonstrates that 7B–14B open-source LLMs can approach or match GPT-4-based prompting methods on SPIDER and narrow the gap on BIRD, which is practically significant for privacy-sensitive and cost-constrained deployments. The finding that single-task SFT actively degrades auxiliary capabilities (schema linking, noise correction) needed for downstream collaboration provides a concrete rationale for multitask training in specialized LLMs. However, the paper acknowledges that MCP introduces additional inference overhead, that a substantial gap remains to the upper-bound performance (especially on BIRD), and that the method's effectiveness on very long-context databases (as in BIRD) is still limited by the reasoning capacity of small models. Open questions include whether more efficient collaboration modes can reduce inference cost and whether the approach scales to larger or more complex database schemas.
