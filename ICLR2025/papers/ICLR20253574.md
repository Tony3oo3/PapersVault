## Problem

Federated learning research has focused almost exclusively on improving accuracy, largely overlooking model calibration—the alignment between a model's predicted confidence and its actual correctness. In high-stakes FL applications (medical diagnostics, autonomous driving), overconfident global models can lead to dangerous misinformed decisions. The core challenge is that data heterogeneity across FL clients makes uniform calibration penalties ineffective: they either bias toward local distributions or sacrifice accuracy, failing to meet the global calibration needs of the aggregated model.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on standard image classification tasks (MNIST, FEMNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) using CNN backbones (2-layer CNN, AlexNet, ResNet-34) under both IID and non-IID (Dirichlet α=0.5, 0.1, 0.05) data splits across 50 clients. Each client's local training loss is augmented with a dynamically-weighted auxiliary calibration term, and the final output is a globally aggregated model whose softmax confidence scores reliably reflect per-prediction accuracy, evaluated via ECE and SCE with 20 bins. The method is designed to be agnostic to the underlying FL algorithm (FedAvg, FedProx, Scaffold, FedDyn, FedNova) and the specific auxiliary loss (DCA, MDCA).

## Method

NUCFL integrates an auxiliary calibration loss (DCA or MDCA) into each client's local training objective, weighted by a client-specific scalar β_m that is automatically determined without hyperparameter tuning. The key mechanism is setting β_m = sim(δ^(t-1), δ_m^(t,e)), where δ^(t-1) is the global model update from the previous round and δ_m^(t,e) is client m's local update at epoch e, with sim being cosine similarity, L-CKA, or RBF-CKA. Clients whose local models closely resemble the global model receive higher calibration penalties (they represent global calibration needs well), while dissimilar clients receive lower penalties to prioritize accuracy. This non-uniform weighting ensures that local training collectively addresses the global model's calibration needs without degrading accuracy, and the framework composes with any FL algorithm's ClientOPT/ServerOPT and any auxiliary calibration loss. A destructive experiment (reversing the similarity logic) confirms that the design is essential: reversed penalties degrade performance below even uncalibrated FL.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (non-IID, α=0.5), NUCFL consistently reduces ECE by 20–40% relative to uncalibrated FL while maintaining or improving accuracy across all five FL algorithms. For example, with FedAvg, NUCFL(DCA+L-CKA) achieves 62.05% accuracy and 6.14% ECE versus 61.34% accuracy and 10.52% ECE for uncalibrated FedAvg. On FEMNIST, NUCFL(DCA+L-CKA) with FedAvg reaches 91.63% accuracy and 3.52% ECE, outperforming the uncalibrated baseline (90.95% acc, 4.17% ECE). The method also improves SCE by 0.3–0.5 percentage points over the best non-NUCFL baselines (DCA/MDCA) across all tested algorithms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NUCFL outperforms all eight centralized train-time calibration baselines (Focal, LS, BS, MMCE, FLSD, MbLS, DCA, MDCA) on ECE across every FL algorithm tested; for instance, on FedAvg/CIFAR-100, NUCFL(DCA+L-CKA) achieves 6.14% ECE versus 7.69% for DCA and 7.71% for MDCA, while also improving accuracy from 61.24% to 62.05%. Among the non-auxiliary baselines, Label Smoothing and Focal Loss are the weakest, often *increasing* ECE relative to uncalibrated FL (e.g., LS raises FedAvg ECE from 10.52% to 15.61%), confirming that direct loss modifications are poorly suited to FL. Against the only prior FL-specific calibration method, FedCal, NUCFL(DCA+L-CKA) reduces ECE from 8.80% to 6.14% and improves accuracy from 61.34% to 62.05% on FedAvg/CIFAR-100. In the post-hoc comparison (Table 5), NUCFL alone (6.14% ECE) already beats Temperature Scaling alone (8.22% ECE), and combining NUCFL with TS yields the best overall result (5.91% ECE, 62.05% acc). A destructive experiment reversing the similarity-based penalty (Table 3) degrades ECE to 13.89–16.11%, confirming that the non-uniform weighting direction is essential to the method's effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends centralized train-time calibration methods—specifically the auxiliary-loss approaches DCA (Liang et al., 2020) and MDCA (Hebbalaguppe et al., 2022)—into the federated learning setting, where data heterogeneity across clients makes uniform calibration penalties ineffective. It departs from the only prior FL calibration work, FedCal (Peng et al., 2024), which applies a post-hoc aggregated scaler, by instead integrating calibration directly into client-side local training with a non-uniform penalty β_m that is dynamically determined by the similarity between each local model and the global model (via cosine similarity or CKA). The framework is designed to be agnostic to the underlying FL algorithm (FedAvg, FedProx, Scaffold, FedDyn, FedNova) and the choice of auxiliary calibration loss, positioning it as a generic wrapper rather than a new FL optimizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Overconfident FL models pose real risks in safety-critical deployments (medical diagnostics, autonomous driving), yet calibration has been almost entirely ignored in the FL literature; this paper provides the first systematic empirical study of train-time calibration in FL and a practical, hyperparameter-free mechanism to address it. Key limitations include the absence of theoretical guarantees for calibration in the federated setting, evaluation restricted to image classification with relatively small networks, and reliance on a heuristic similarity-based weighting whose optimality is not established. Open questions include extending the approach to personalized FL, unsupervised or semi-supervised FL, and understanding how calibration interacts with privacy mechanisms like differential privacy.
