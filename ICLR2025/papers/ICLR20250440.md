## Problem

Existing Flow Matching and Schrödinger Bridge methods for modeling system dynamics are limited to transporting between exactly two marginal distributions, making them unable to jointly leverage data from multiple time points and experimental conditions. In real-world settings—single-cell genomics, climate monitoring, drug screening—observations are unpaired across time and conditions, and certain time-condition combinations are missing due to cost or sensor failure, yet no prior method can integrate these fragmented snapshots into a unified dynamical model that interpolates and extrapolates to unobserved points.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unpaired population samples from K+1 irregularly spaced time points under C conditions, where not every condition is observed at every time point (e.g., 93 kinase inhibitors × 4 timepoints in the single-cell experiment, or 12 monitoring stations × 26 months in the Beijing air-quality experiment). It outputs a learned time-dependent vector field v_t(x, c; θ) that, when integrated via ODE (4th-order Runge-Kutta), produces continuous trajectories for any starting sample x_0 under any condition c, enabling imputation at missing time points, interpolation between observed snapshots, and generalization to entirely unseen conditions. The vector field is conditioned on a learnable condition embedding and a sinusoidal time embedding, with a guidance weight w controlling the trade-off between condition-specific and condition-agnostic dynamics at inference.

## Method

MMFM generalizes the Conditional Flow Matching objective from two marginals to K+1 by defining a joint coupling q(z) over all time-point samples and a Gaussian probability density path p_t(x|z) whose mean μ_t(z) is the natural cubic spline (minimum-curvature) interpolation through all observed points and whose variance σ_t(z) is a piecewise parabolic function that vanishes at observed time points and peaks at interval midpoints. The coupling is computed via multi-marginal optimal transport with a pairwise additive cost, which provably decomposes into K independent pairwise OT problems solved with squared Euclidean distance. The condition-aware variant (C-MMFM) introduces classifier-free guidance: a single shared-weight network v_t(x, c; θ) is trained simultaneously on conditional inputs (real condition c) and an unconditional null embedding c_∅ with probability p_u, and at inference the vector field is a weighted blend (1−w)·v_t(x, c_∅) + w·v_t(x, c), allowing information sharing across conditions while preserving condition-specific dynamics. Proposition 1 proves the MMFM objective has identical gradients to the intractable FM objective, and Proposition 2 shows that with piecewise-linear means and per-interval networks, MMFM reduces exactly to K independent CFM problems—highlighting that the spline prior and shared parameters are what enable cross-condition generalization.

## Results

On the single-cell perturbation screening dataset (93 kinase inhibitors, 4 time points), MMFM achieves the best MSE at all three held-out time points (7.54 at 24h, 3.38 at 48h, 4.92 at 72h) and the best W2 distance at 72h (37.71), outperforming FSI, CFM, PCFM, and the linear ablation L-MMFM. On the synthetic benchmark with sparse/irregular sampling, MMFM reduces MSE by 3–5× over FSI and PCFM (e.g., 0.13 vs. 0.72 for condition c₃) and achieves the lowest MMD (0.04 vs. 0.42 for FSI). On the Beijing air quality dataset with missing sensor data, L-MMFM achieves the best MSE (9.73–9.21 across conditions) while MMFM is second in three of four scenarios (11.43–17.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMFM consistently outperforms FSI (Chewi et al., 2021) across all three experimental settings: on the synthetic data, FSI's MSE is 0.72 for condition c₃ versus MMFM's 0.13 (a 5.5× gap), and on the single-cell data FSI's W2 at 72h is 78.46 versus MMFM's 37.71. Against the OT-CFM baselines from Tong et al. (2024), MMFM wins on every reported metric: CFM (which ignores intermediate time points) achieves MSE 9.10 at 24h versus MMFM's 7.54, and PCFM (consecutive-pair CFM per condition) reaches MSE 6.07 at 72h versus MMFM's 4.92. The key advantage of MMFM over PCFM is the sharing of parameters across conditions via classifier-free guidance, which allows the model to borrow strength from well-sampled conditions to impute sparse ones. On the Beijing air quality dataset, the linear ablation L-MMFM slightly edges out the cubic-spline MMFM on MSE (e.g., 9.73 vs. 11.43 for c=4), suggesting that for smoother, lower-dimensional signals the spline prior offers less benefit, though both MMFM variants still dominate FSI, CFM, and PCFM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023) from its original two-marginal formulation to K+1 marginals across time and C conditions, using cubic spline interpolation for the conditional probability path and multi-marginal optimal transport (Pass, 2015) for coupling. It incorporates classifier-free guidance (Zheng et al., 2023) to share information across conditions via a single set of shared weights, and builds on the OT-CFM coupling strategy (Tong et al., 2024) extended to the multi-marginal setting. The key departure from prior work is that FSI (Chewi et al., 2021) handles multiple time points but not conditions, while perturbation-response models handle conditions but not temporal dynamics; MMFM unifies both axes within a single generative framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically critical gap in single-cell genomics and sensor-network applications where data is fragmented across irregularly spaced time points and heterogeneous experimental conditions, enabling imputation of missing observations by leveraging cross-condition dynamics. Open questions include learning the time-varying variance function σ_t in a principled way (currently a hand-designed piecewise quadratic), exploring more complex MMOT cost structures (e.g., energy-landscape-based losses), and extending to multi-modal data. A notable limitation is that the DMSB baseline could not be properly evaluated, and the method's reliance on pre-computed OT couplings may limit scalability to very large datasets despite the mini-batch OT fallback.
