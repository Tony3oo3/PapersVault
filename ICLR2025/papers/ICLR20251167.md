## Problem

Single-image 3D generation is inherently ill-posed: a 2D image cannot disambiguate unseen views, leading to view inconsistency, imprecise geometries, poor generalization to atypical objects, and limited controllability over shape and style. While designers routinely reference existing 3D models to guide their work, no prior feed-forward generative model has incorporated a 3D reference to alleviate these ambiguities in a generalized, per-case-optimization-free manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single concept image (rendered at 512×512) and a 3D reference model, which is converted into six canonical coordinate maps (CCMs) at 320×320 resolution arranged in a 3×2 grid. The CCMs encode geometry only (no texture) to reduce conflicts with the concept image. The pipeline first generates a 960×640 multi-view image (six 320×320 views) via reference-augmented diffusion, then reconstructs a textured 3D mesh via a finetuned LGM sparse-view reconstruction model. Text prompts are first converted to concept images, and existing 3D models can serve as both the reference and the source of image variations for 3D-to-3D tasks.

## Method

Phidias builds on a frozen Zero123++ multi-view diffusion model and adds a trainable conditioner branch that ingests reference CCMs, with three key architectural designs to handle the misalignment between the concept image and the 3D reference. (1) Meta-ControlNet consists of a base ControlNet (encoder + trainable down/middle blocks + zero convolutions) and a meta-controller that takes the concept image paired with the front-view CCM, producing multi-scale alignment features injected into the base ControlNet's downsampling blocks and additional meta-signals added directly to the pretrained diffusion, yielding adaptive control signals scaled by a user-tunable coefficient λ. (2) Dynamic Reference Routing progressively increases the reference CCM resolution from 16→32→64 across denoising timesteps [0,0.05]→[0.05,0.4]→[0.4,1.0], so coarse structure is guided by low-resolution references while fine details use full-resolution ones, reducing local conflicts. (3) Self-Reference Augmentation enables self-supervised training by using the target model as its own reference with a progressive curriculum of augmentations (random resize, horizontal flip, grid distortion, shift, and retrieved references at 20% probability), starting from minimal perturbations and gradually increasing difficulty. Training uses ~64K filtered Objaverse objects for 10K steps with AdamW (lr 1e-5, batch 48) on 8 A100 GPUs (~10 hours), with only the conditioner branch trainable.

## Results

On the GSO benchmark (200 objects), Phidias with a ground-truth 3D reference achieves PSNR 20.37, SSIM 0.870, LPIPS 0.117, CLIP-P 0.911, and F-Score 0.840, substantially outperforming all five baselines. With a retrieved reference (the practical setting), Phidias still leads on PSNR (17.02 vs. best baseline 16.35 for CRM), SSIM (0.845 vs. 0.843 for OpenLRM), LPIPS (0.174 vs. 0.182 for CRM), and F-Score (0.833 vs. 0.831 for LGM). A user study with 30 participants confirms strong human preference, with Phidias preferred over baselines at rates of 88.4%–95.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Phidias outperforms all five baselines on the majority of metrics in both the GT-reference and retrieved-reference settings. With a retrieved reference, the closest competitor is CRM (PSNR 16.35, LPIPS 0.182), which Phidias surpasses by 0.67 dB in PSNR and 0.008 in LPIPS; OpenLRM is the next closest on SSIM (0.843 vs. 0.845). LGM achieves a marginally lower CD (0.0398 vs. 0.0402) but trails Phidias on every other reported metric. InstantMesh and LGM are the weakest baselines overall (PSNR 14.63 and 14.80 respectively), while SV3D does not report geometry metrics (CD, F-Score). The user study reinforces the quantitative gap: Phidias is preferred over OpenLRM (94.7%), LGM (95.8%), CRM (93.7%), SV3D (88.4%), and InstantMesh (91.6%), indicating that the reference-augmented approach delivers perceptually meaningful improvements beyond what standard metrics capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

Phidias extends the multi-view diffusion paradigm (Zero123++) and the ControlNet conditioning mechanism by introducing 3D reference models as auxiliary geometric guidance, drawing an explicit analogy to retrieval-augmented generation in NLP and image synthesis. It departs from standard ControlNet, which assumes strict alignment between condition and target, by treating the 3D reference as soft guidance that may conflict with the concept image. The sparse-view reconstruction stage builds on LGM (3D Gaussian Splatting), and the retrieval pipeline leverages Uni3D's point-cloud-to-CLIP alignment for image-to-3D matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing 3D reference conditioning as a first-class input, Phidias offers a practical path toward more controllable and generalizable 3D generation that mirrors how human designers actually work. Key limitations include dependence on a modest 40K-object retrieval database, the 320×320 per-view resolution of the backbone, and sensitivity to retrieval quality (misaligned poses or structures cause failures). Open questions include whether larger databases and geometry-aware retrieval can close the gap between retrieved-reference and GT-reference performance, and whether the approach scales to higher-fidelity or multi-object scenes.
