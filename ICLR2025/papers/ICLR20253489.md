## Problem

Receiver behavior—likes, comments, replay graphs—is a downstream effect of content and thus carries rich signals about it, yet it is treated as noise and discarded when training vision-language models. Prior work on perceptual signals (eye tracking, saliency) showed promise but is limited to expensive, small-scale lab collections. This paper asks whether action-level behavior data, which is collected at scale for free on platforms like Reddit and YouTube, can be leveraged to improve VLM content understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253489/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video frames sampled via the 30-degree rule, images, post metadata (channel name, time of post), and ASR transcripts when available. During training, the instruction template asks the model to produce a scene-by-scene verbalization (auto-generated captions, tags, colors, and tone from LLaVA-13B) followed by simulated top-5 user comments and predicted likes/views. At evaluation time, the same model is prompted for diverse outputs: emotion/topic/persuasion labels, dense captions, memorability scores, QA answers, and summarization, across 46 tasks on 26 benchmarks spanning image, video, text, and audio.

## Method

The paper introduces BLIFT, a 730k-sample instruction fine-tuning dataset of images and videos from Reddit (r/pics, r/videos) and YouTube paired with their receiver behavior (upvotes, top comments, likes, views, replay graphs), after extensive filtering for NSFW, bot, duplicate, and low-quality content. LLaMA-Vid is instruction fine-tuned on BLIFT with a 1:1 sampling ratio against its original IFT mix for 2.2 epochs, with all modules frozen except the Visual Encoder, teaching the model to simulate user comments and engagement given media. The key finding is that this behavior-prediction task acts as a free auxiliary objective: Behavior-LLaVA outperforms both the base LLaMA-Vid and Ad-LLaVA (trained on identical media without behavior) across all 46 tasks, with the largest gains on high-level tasks like emotion recognition (up to 84% zero-shot improvement) and memorability (up to 350% zero-shot), while low-level action recognition sees only marginal changes. An ablation comparing perceptual (Salicon saliency) versus action-level behavior confirms that the gains come from action data's scale and diversity rather than from additional visual supervision.

## Results

Behavior-LLaVA improves over its base model LLaMA-Vid on all 46 tasks across 26 benchmarks, with the largest gains on higher-level tasks: 186.4% average improvement on image/video memorability (zero-shot), 51.85% on video emotion recognition (zero-shot), 43.18% on video ad understanding (zero-shot), and 29.14% on image emotion recognition (zero-shot). On the Long Video Understanding (LVU) benchmark, Behavior-LLaVA achieves an average 21.49% improvement over LLaMA-Vid across 9 VQA tasks and outperforms the prior state-of-the-art (Video4096) on 5/9 tasks. In fine-tuned settings, Behavior-LLaVA still maintains gains (e.g., 3.39% on video emotion, 27.64% on video ads) and outperforms GPT-4V on image emotion recognition (87.36 vs. 83.33 on IAPSa-8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its base model LLaMA-Vid, Behavior-LLaVA wins on essentially every task, with the largest zero-shot gains on memorability (186.4% avg), video emotion (51.85%), and video ad understanding (43.18%), while low-level video QA (MSVD-QA, MSRVTT-QA, ActivityNet-QA) sees only marginal improvements (0–3%). The critical control experiment, Ad-LLaVA (trained on the same BLIFT media but without behavior signals), performs nearly identically to LLaMA-Vid (e.g., 29.8 vs. 29.7 on VideoEmotion-8 zero-shot), confirming that the gains come from behavior data rather than additional content. Against Video4096 (Bhattacharyya et al., 2023), Behavior-LLaVA outperforms on 5/9 LVU tasks and 5/5 video ad tasks in fine-tuned settings, though Video4096 retains an edge on some individual LVU sub-tasks (e.g., Director: 75.26 vs. 71.12). On image emotion recognition in fine-tuned settings, Behavior-LLaVA surpasses GPT-4V (87.36 vs. 83.33 on IAPSa-8; 81.41 vs. 71.12 on Abstract), though GPT-4V still leads in zero-shot. On memorability, Behavior-LLaVA trained on only 25% of the data matches Henry (I et al., 2024) trained on all combined datasets (e.g., 0.73 vs. 0.72 on LaMem), demonstrating strong data efficiency. Against Video-ChatGPT, Behavior-LLaVA substantially outperforms on multi-shot video summarization (BLEU 10.6 vs. 5.0) and moment retrieval (mAP 33.1 vs. not reported).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LLaMA-Vid as its base VLM and extends the instruction fine-tuning paradigm (from LLaVA, MiniGPT-4, BLIP) by introducing receiver behavior simulation as a training objective. It departs from two prior lines of work: (1) perceptual-signal integration (eye tracking, saliency, keystrokes) which showed that human processing signals improve NLP/CV tasks but are limited to expensive, lab-collected, small-scale data; and (2) behavior prediction literature (Gao et al. 2020, Wang & Torres 2022) which uses content to predict behavior, whereas this paper inverts the direction—using behavior as a training signal to improve content understanding. The key conceptual departure is treating internet-collected action-level behavior (comments, likes, replay graphs) as a scalable, annotation-free proxy for the perceptual signals that prior work showed to be useful.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'free lunch' framing is compelling: behavior data is collected by default on major platforms, requires no human annotation, and the paper demonstrates consistent improvements across 46 tasks and 26 benchmarks in both zero-shot and fine-tuned settings. The Ad-LLaVA ablation cleanly isolates the behavior signal from the additional training data, strengthening the causal claim. However, the work is limited to a single base architecture (LLaMA-Vid), the gains on low-level tasks (object/action recognition) are marginal, and practical adoption depends on access to platform-level behavior data that may not be available to all researchers. Open questions include whether the effect generalizes to other VLM families, whether synthetic or proxy behavior signals can replicate the gains, and what the ceiling of improvement is as behavior data scales further.
