## Problem

Existing decentralized federated learning (DFL) algorithms assume a fixed number of local SGD steps between model exchanges, overlooking the heterogeneity and time-varying dynamics in client computation and communication capabilities. This paper asks how to jointly integrate sporadicity in both local gradient computations and inter-client model aggregations into DFL while preserving convergence guarantees, a setting where multiple client aggregators must reach consensus without any predetermined pattern of updates or communications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252146/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client i holds a local dataset D_i (non-IID or IID splits of Fashion-MNIST or CIFAR-10) and participates in a time-varying communication graph G^(k) over K training iterations. The method is parameterized by per-client SGD probabilities d_i^(k) ∈ (0,1] and per-link communication probabilities b_ij^(k) ∈ (0,1], which can be arbitrary, heterogeneous, and time-varying (e.g., drawn from Beta, uniform, or bimodal truncated Gaussian distributions). The output is a consensus model θ* = argmin F(θ) shared across all clients, accompanied by analytical convergence rate bounds (geometric for strongly-convex, O(1/K) for non-convex) that explicitly quantify the impact of sporadicity parameters on the optimality/stationarity gap.

## Method

DSpodFL models the per-iteration occurrence of a local SGD step at each client (v_i^(k) ∈ {0,1}) and the activation of each communication link between client pairs (v̂_ij^(k) ∈ {0,1}) as arbitrary indicator random variables, yielding the update rule θ_i^(k+1) = θ_i^(k) + Σ_j r_ij(θ_j^(k) − θ_i^(k))v̂_ij^(k) − α^(k)g_i^(k)v_i^(k). The mixing weights r_ij follow the Metropolis-Hastings heuristic to ensure the doubly stochastic matrix R, while the random matrix P^(k) = [r_ij v̂_ij^(k)] captures the actual per-iteration consensus mixing. The convergence analysis decomposes the error into an average model error and a consensus error, couples them via a 2×2 matrix Φ^(k), and shows that the spectral radius ρ(Φ^(k)) < 1 under a learning-rate condition involving the expected spectral radius ρ̃^(k) of the mixing matrix and the minimum SGD probability d_min. This framework subsumes DGD (v_i=1, v̂_ij=1 always), DFedAvg (periodic v̂_ij), and Randomized Gossip (sporadic v̂_ij only) as special cases, and the bounds recover known DGD convergence rates when sporadicity is removed.

## Results

DSpodFL is evaluated on Fashion-MNIST (SVM) and CIFAR-10 (VGG11) with m=10 clients over a random geometric graph, measuring test accuracy versus total training delay. In IID settings, DSpodFL achieves a 10–20% accuracy margin over all baselines in the initial training stages; under non-IID data (1 class per client for FMNIST, 3 for CIFAR-10), the improvement widens to 10–40% accuracy at a given delay. Scaling to m=50 clients and using bimodal resource distributions (lower μ) further amplifies the gap to ~15% over the best baseline, confirming that DSpodFL's advantage grows with network size and resource heterogeneity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSpodFL outperforms all four baselines (DGD, Randomized Gossip, Sporadic SGDs, and DFedAvg) across every dataset, data distribution, and system parameter tested. Against DGD (Nedić & Ozdaglar, 2009), which performs SGD and aggregation at every iteration, DSpodFL achieves substantially lower delay to reach target accuracy because it avoids unnecessary computations and communications at resource-constrained clients. Against Randomized Gossip (Koloskova et al., 2020), which only sporadically aggregates but still computes gradients every iteration, DSpodFL's joint sporadicity in both computation and communication yields 10–40% accuracy gains at a fixed delay, especially under non-IID data where inter-client exchange is critical. Against DFedAvg (Sun et al., 2022), which uses a fixed aggregation period D, DSpodFL's time-varying and heterogeneous sporadicity adapts to per-client resource availability, producing the largest relative improvements when heterogeneity is high (e.g., inverted Beta(0.5,0.5) distributions or bimodal Gaussians with low μ). The gap between DSpodFL and baselines is smallest under IID data with uniform resource distributions and largest under non-IID data with strong resource heterogeneity, consistent with the theoretical prediction that sporadicity benefits scale with data and resource diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends several threads in decentralized optimization: DGD (Nedić & Ozdaglar, 2009), randomized gossip (Boyd et al., 2006; Koloskova et al., 2020), decentralized fixed local SGD/DFedAvg (Sun et al., 2022), and centralized sporadic/Anarchic FL (Yang et al., 2022). The key departure is modeling both local SGD occurrences and inter-client model exchanges as arbitrary indicator random variables simultaneously in a fully decentralized setting, whereas prior work addressed at most one form of sporadicity (either in computation or communication) or restricted to fixed deterministic patterns. The convergence analysis builds on the unified DGD framework of Koloskova et al. (2020) but extends it with time-varying graph connectivity and general gradient diversity assumptions (δ, ζ parameters) that avoid bounding gradient norms globally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework provides a single theoretical lens under which DGD, DFedAvg, and randomized gossip are recovered as special cases, giving practitioners a principled way to tune sporadicity parameters (d_i, b_ij) based on resource availability while retaining convergence guarantees. The practical relevance is strongest for peer-to-peer or edge-device networks where a central server is infeasible and client capabilities are highly heterogeneous. Limitations include evaluation only on small-scale image classification (FMNIST/SVM, CIFAR10/VGG11 with 10–50 clients), no consideration of communication compression or privacy, and the convergence bounds, while tight in structure, depend on spectral radius parameters that can be hard to estimate in practice. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-convex deep learning at scale, interaction with gradient compression, and whether the sporadicity parameters can be learned adaptively during training.
