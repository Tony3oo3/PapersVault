## Problem

Prior analytical solutions for deep linear network dynamics either assumed zero-balanced weights (restricting analysis to the rich regime) or were limited to single-output settings, leaving the full continuum between rich and lazy learning regimes uncharacterized. The paper asks how the relative scale of weights between layers (captured by a parameter λ) governs the transition between sigmoidal (rich) and exponential (lazy) learning dynamics, and whether standard initializations like LeCun naturally produce λ-balanced weights in wide networks.

## Input / Output

The method requires whitened inputs (Σ^xx = I), λ-balanced initialization (W2^T W2 − W1 W1^T = λI), and hidden dimension N_h = min(N_i, N_o) to avoid bottlenecks or overparameterization. The output is the full temporal evolution of the (N_i + N_o) × (N_i + N_o) matrix QQ^T(t), whose four quadrants encode the network function, input and output representational similarity matrices, and the finite-width NTK. A numerically stable reformulation using only decaying exponentials is provided when the initialization matrix B is invertible.

## Method

The paper extends the matrix Riccati formulation of Fukumizu (1998) and Braun et al. (2022) from zero-balanced to λ-balanced initializations by deriving the eigendecomposition of the driving matrix F in terms of the SVD of the input-output cross-covariance Σ^yx. This yields explicit closed-form expressions for all four quadrants of QQ^T(t) as functions of time, the singular values of the task, and λ. The solution reveals that singular values evolve via a λ-dependent transition function γ_α(t;λ) that interpolates pointwise between sigmoidal (λ→0, rich regime) and exponential (λ→±∞, lazy regime) dynamics. The authors further show that standard initializations (LeCun, He, Glorot) converge in probability to λ-balanced weights in the infinite-width limit, and that the sign of λ interacts with network architecture (funnel vs. inverted-funnel) to produce a 'delayed rich regime' where the NTK is initially static before transitioning.

## Results

The paper derives exact closed-form solutions for the temporal dynamics of QQ^T(t) in two-layer linear networks under λ-balanced initialization, showing that the relative scale parameter λ fully controls the transition between learning regimes. At λ=0, singular values follow sigmoidal dynamics (rich regime) with the transition function γ_α(t;0) = (e^{2s̃_α t/τ}−1)/(e^{2s̃_α t/τ}−1+s̃_α/s_α(0)), while as |λ|→∞ the dynamics become exponential (lazy regime) with γ_α(t;±∞) = 1−e^{−|λ|t/τ}. The NTK kernel distance from initialization peaks at λ=0 for square networks and exhibits antisymmetric behavior for funnel (lazy as λ→+∞) and inverted-funnel (lazy as λ→−∞) architectures. At convergence, λ=0 yields task-specific representations matching the input-output correlation structure, while large |λ| produces identity-like (task-agnostic) representations in the dominant layer, with parameter-noise robustness maximized at λ=0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are to prior analytical frameworks. The work extends Fukumizu (1998) and Braun et al. (2022), which assumed strict zero-balancedness (λ=0) and thus could only describe the rich regime, by deriving solutions for arbitrary λ that span the full rich-to-lazy continuum. It generalizes Saxe et al. (2014), which required task-aligned initialization and equal dimensions, to unequal input-output dimensions (N_i≠N_o) without a bottleneck constraint. Compared to Kunin et al. (2024) and Xu & Ziyin (2024), which relaxed the balanced assumption but were restricted to single-output neuron settings, this paper provides multi-output solutions with explicit NTK and RSM dynamics. The key advance over Tarmoun et al. (2021), who removed the zero-balanced constraint, is that the solutions here are in a stable, interpretable form rather than an 'unstable and mixed form.' The paper also shows that standard initializations (LeCun, He, Glorot) naturally produce λ-balanced weights in the infinite-width limit, connecting the theory to practical initialization schemes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the matrix Riccati formulation of Fukumizu (1998) and Braun et al. (2022) by relaxing the zero-balanced (λ=0) initialization constraint to the more general λ-balanced condition, thereby capturing the full continuum from rich to lazy learning regimes rather than being restricted to the rich regime. It also generalizes beyond prior work (Kunin et al., 2024; Xu & Ziyin, 2024) that relaxed the balanced assumption but was limited to single-output settings, and goes beyond Tarmoun et al. (2021) whose solutions for unbalanced networks remained in unstable mixed form. The eigendecomposition of the Riccati matrix F (Lemma 4.2) and the resulting closed-form solution (Theorem 4.3) are the core technical contributions, building directly on the Saxe et al. (2014) framework for task-aligned dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the relative scale λ—not just the absolute initialization variance—governs the rich-to-lazy transition, and identifies a 'semi-structured lazy regime' where one layer becomes identity-like while the other retains task-specific structure, with the counterintuitive finding that this lazy structure can improve transfer learning. The exact solutions provide a theoretical foundation for understanding continual learning (catastrophic forgetting), reversal learning (saddle-point avoidance via non-zero λ), and fine-tuning dynamics relevant to LoRA-style adaptation. Key limitations include the restriction to linear networks, whitened inputs, and the λ-balanced assumption; extending to nonlinear activations, deep architectures, and non-white inputs are identified as important open directions.
