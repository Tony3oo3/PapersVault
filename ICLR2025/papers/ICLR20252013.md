## Problem

Simultaneous Machine Translation (SiMT) models must decide when to translate and when to wait, yet existing preference alignment methods (DPO, CPO, KTO) optimize only the generated response text and ignore the latency preference and read/write policy that are central to SiMT. Additionally, prior SiMT work is trained and evaluated on offline MT corpora that do not reflect the distinct audience preferences in real-time scenarios (monotonicity, simplicity, key-point focus, minimal latency). This paper is the first to systematically align SiMT models with these human preferences.

## Input / Output

The model receives source text as a growing prefix x (streaming input) and at each step must either WRITE a target token y_t or READ (wait for n additional source words before re-evaluating). The read/write decision is made via a confidence score c_t estimated by an auxiliary sigmoid layer: if c_t > 0.5 the model writes, otherwise it reads. The reading length n is a hyperparameter that controls the latency level, and the model is trained on prefix-level preference pairs (x, y^w, y^l) extracted from full sentence pairs using word alignment, so the model learns to translate from partial source rather than complete sentences.

## Method

SimulPL is a two-phase preference learning framework. In the MSFT phase, the model is jointly trained on translation (generating y^w from prefix x) and read/write policy (predicting confidence c_t = 1 for WRITE, c_t = 0 for READ) using a combined cross-entropy loss. In the SimulDPO phase, the standard DPO objective is modified by adding a +α·E[|y|] term that encourages the model to translate as much as possible given the received prefix, thereby reducing latency. The key technical trick is expressing |y| = Σ_t c_t, which makes the length constraint differentiable and allows gradient signals to propagate directly to the confidence (read/write) parameters. The reference policy π_ref is given the full source sentence X (not just the prefix x), providing a more accurate anchor that prevents the policy from diverging. Preference data is constructed by prompting GPT-4/4o with five linguistically-grounded preference criteria to generate human-preferred translations, then extracting prefix pairs via awesome-align word alignment.

## Results

On the Zh→En SiMT task, SimulPL achieves a BLEU of 23.73 and COMET of 76.32 overall, substantially outperforming LLM-PFX-SFT (BLEU 12.05, COMET 74.25) and LLM-PFX-SFT+DPO (BLEU 14.79, COMET 72.67). On De→En, SimulPL reaches BLEU 42.69 and COMET 82.34, and on En→Zh it achieves BLEU 38.99 and COMET 80.52. Across all three language pairs, SimulPL also achieves the best (lowest) Normalized Inversion Rate (NIR), Sentence Length Ratio (SLR), and Dependency Depth (DD), indicating superior alignment with monotonicity, key point, and simplicity preferences simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimulPL consistently outperforms all baselines across all three language pairs and all preference dimensions. Against the Transformer-based wait-k (Ma et al., 2019) and SM² (Yu et al., 2024) baselines, SimulPL achieves substantially higher BLEU and COMET at comparable LAAL levels (e.g., on Zh→En at n=5: SimulPL BLEU 24.94 vs. wait-k BLEU 21.93 at LAAL 6.46 vs. 2.90). Against the LLM-based LLM-PFX-SFT (Wang et al., 2023a), SimulPL more than doubles BLEU on Zh→En (23.73 vs. 12.05) and En→Zh (38.99 vs. 19.23), while also achieving lower NIR and SLR. Critically, SimulPL also outperforms LLM-PFX-SFT+DPO (standard DPO without latency awareness), demonstrating that integrating latency preference into the optimization objective and jointly improving the read/write policy yields gains that vanilla DPO cannot capture (e.g., Zh→En BLEU 23.73 vs. 14.79, COMET 76.32 vs. 72.67). The paper further shows that SimulPL generalizes to CPO and KTO, with SimulCPO and SimulKTO both outperforming their vanilla counterparts, particularly at low latency levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO/CPO/KTO preference optimization lineage to the simultaneous machine translation (SiMT) domain, where the optimization must jointly account for translation quality and the read/write policy that governs latency. It builds directly on Wang et al. (2023a)'s LLM-based SiMT framework (prefix-pairs SFT with incremental decoding) and adapts Rafailov et al.'s DPO by adding an output-length constraint (+α|y|) that provably upper-bounds Average Lagging, while using a full-source reference model π_ref(y|X) to prevent divergence. The five-way preference taxonomy (quality, monotonicity, key points, simplicity, latency) draws on a body of linguistic and interpreting research (Kurz, Moser, Sridhar, Dayter) that had not previously been operationalized in a computational training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SimulPL is the first preference-learning framework for SiMT, bridging a gap between linguistic findings about interpreter quality criteria and computational training objectives. The framework's generality—demonstrated by successful adaptation to CPO and KTO—suggests a template for incorporating task-specific constraints (here, latency) into any preference optimization method. Open questions include the sensitivity to the α hyperparameter (which acts as a token-level quality threshold), the confidence threshold γ at inference, and whether the approach extends to other streaming tasks such as simultaneous inference. A limitation is that preference data is generated by GPT-4/4o, introducing potential model bias, though manual revision on test sets mitigates this for evaluation.
