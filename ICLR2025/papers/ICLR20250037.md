## Problem

Fine-tuning large language models with standard Adam demands excessive memory (e.g., 316 GB for OPT-13B), while existing memory-efficient alternatives each have critical drawbacks: IP-SGD still requires substantial memory for long-sequence data points, and MeZO (zeroth-order) suffers from slow convergence and degraded final accuracy. The paper asks whether an optimizer can simultaneously achieve memory usage comparable to MeZO, convergence speed comparable to first-order methods, and final model quality that matches or exceeds Adam.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LM (OPT-13B/30B/66B, Llama-2-70B, or RoBERTa-large) and a fine-tuning dataset, partitioning the data by a sequence-length threshold L_T into D⁰ (long sequences) and D¹ (short sequences). At each iteration, it draws separate minibatches B⁰ from D⁰ and B¹ from D¹, computing a scalar zeroth-order directional derivative g⁰ on B⁰ and per-layer first-order gradients g¹ on B¹. The output is the updated model parameters θ, with the key constraint that no full-model gradient tensor is ever stored in memory—only per-layer gradients are held transiently during the in-place update.

## Method

Addax (ADDition of grAdient estimates through memory-efficient eXecution) assigns data points to different gradient estimators based on their sequence length: longer sequences (which consume more memory during backpropagation) are handled by MeZO's SPSA-based zeroth-order gradient, while shorter sequences use in-place first-order SGD. The two gradient estimates are combined via a convex mixture θ ← θ − η(α·z·g⁰ + (1−α)·g¹), where α is a mixing hyperparameter. The in-place update (computing and applying each layer's gradient immediately, then freeing it) ensures memory does not scale with model size, and the zeroth-order perturbation direction z is regenerated from a stored seed rather than stored as a full d-dimensional vector. Theoretically, the zeroth-order gradient is an unbiased estimator of the Gaussian-smoothed loss ∇L̂(θ), so the combined update implicitly regularizes the first-order gradient, helping escape sharp local minima. This yields convergence rates that are nearly dimension-independent (unlike pure ZO methods) and require less restrictive hyperparameter choices than MeZO.

## Results

On OPT-13B fine-tuned with a single A100 (40GB) GPU, Addax outperforms MeZO by 14% in average accuracy/F1 score and converges 15× faster, while maintaining a comparable memory footprint (~30–39 GB vs. ~28–40 GB for MeZO). On the larger OPT-30B model with a single H100 (80GB), Addax achieves 78.7% accuracy/F1 on short datasets and 82.0% on long datasets, surpassing MeZO (69.3% / 68.7%) by more than 16% and converging 30× faster. Addax also outperforms Adam in 7 out of 9 tasks on OPT-13B while reducing memory by up to 89% (e.g., 28.7 GB vs. 248.4 GB on SST-2). On OPT-66B and Llama-2-70B (three H100 GPUs), Addax outperforms MeZO in 6/7 and 6/6 tasks respectively, while SGD and IP-SGD encounter out-of-memory errors on multiple tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MeZO, Addax consistently wins on both accuracy and speed: on OPT-13B it improves average accuracy/F1 by 14% (e.g., 84.8% vs. 65.3% on RTE) and converges 15× faster, while on OPT-30B the gap widens to >16% accuracy improvement and 30× speedup, all with comparable GPU memory usage. Against IP-SGD, Addax achieves higher accuracy on the six OPT-13B tasks where IP-SGD can run (81.7% vs. 80.3% average) and, critically, succeeds on all nine tasks whereas IP-SGD encounters out-of-memory errors on BoolQ, MultiRC, and SQuAD. Against standard SGD, Addax dominates: SGD fails with OOM on all nine OPT-13B tasks even at the smallest batch size, while Addax runs successfully on every task. Against Adam (which requires 5× H100 GPUs / 350 GB for OPT-13B), Addax outperforms it in 7 out of 9 tasks (e.g., 89.3% vs. 71.4% on CB, 88.4% vs. 84.5% on SQuAD) while using up to 89% less memory. On RoBERTa-large, 16-bit Addax surpasses Adam in 4 of 6 tasks (e.g., 91.4% vs. 91.9% on SST-2 is a near-tie, but 90.8% vs. 85.0% on TREC) and outperforms MeZO across all six tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

Addax sits at the intersection of two recent memory-efficient fine-tuning lines: MeZO (Malladi et al., 2023), which uses in-place zeroth-order (SPSA) gradient estimation to avoid storing full gradient vectors, and IP-SGD (Zhao et al., 2024; Lv et al., 2023), which eliminates gradient storage via layer-wise in-place updates during backpropagation. The paper's key departure is a data-assignment strategy that routes long-sequence samples to the zeroth-order estimator and short-sequence samples to the first-order estimator, then combines both gradient estimates in a single update rule. This contrasts with the concurrent 'hybrid ZO-FO' scheme of Zhang et al. (2024b), which restricts backpropagation to deeper layers and forgoes in-place updates, and with MAS (Landro et al., 2020), which mixes SGD and Adam directions without the memory-aware data partitioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Addax makes full-parameter fine-tuning of 13B–70B LMs feasible on a single A100/H100 GPU with convergence times comparable to first-order methods and memory comparable to MeZO, directly lowering the hardware barrier for researchers and practitioners. The observation that zeroth-order gradients act as a Gaussian-smoothing regularizer that improves final accuracy over vanilla IP-SGD is a useful conceptual insight, though the paper acknowledges it lacks a rigorous theoretical treatment of this regularization effect. Open questions include combining Addax with PEFT, quantization, or memory-efficient attention (FlashAttention, HyperAttention), extending it to pre-training, and understanding whether the mixing parameter α can be set adaptively rather than tuned per task.
