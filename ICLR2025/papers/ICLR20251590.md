## Problem

Conventional neural network neurons are static threshold units that lack the dynamical, spatiotemporal representations observed in biological neural circuits. While synchronization-based binding has been hypothesized to underpin feature compression and abstraction in both neuroscience and AI, prior synchrony-based models (complex-valued neurons, rotating features) have struggled to scale to natural images. The Kuramoto model—a well-studied nonlinear oscillator synchronization model—had rarely been explored as a building block for deep learning architectures.

## Input / Output

For object discovery, RGB images are patchified into tokens (e.g., 16×16 patches for 256×256 images), and the model outputs cluster assignments over spatial locations, which are upsampled via an up-tiling procedure (shifting the input by sub-patch strides and interleaving feature maps) to produce finer segmentation masks. For Sudoku, a 9×9 digit grid (0 for blank, 1–9 for given digits) is embedded into 81 token vectors, and the model outputs a solved 9×9 grid. For classification, the model produces a class label with a confidence score derived from the oscillator energy, which the authors show correlates strongly with prediction correctness.

## Method

Each neuron is an N-dimensional unit vector on a hypersphere, updated iteratively by a generalized Kuramoto differential equation: a natural-frequency term (an N×N anti-symmetric matrix Ωᵢ) plus an interaction term that sums weighted neighbor oscillators and projects the result onto the tangent space at xᵢ via Proj_{xᵢ}. A data-dependent conditional stimulus C acts as a symmetry-breaking field that biases each oscillator toward a learned direction, and the connectivity J can be either convolutional (local, fixed kernel) or attentive (input-dependent QKV-style). After T Kuramoto steps, a readout module computes the L2 norm of linearly combined oscillators to extract phase-invariant features for the next block. The architecture is trained end-to-end with standard objectives (SimCLR contrastive loss for object discovery, cross-entropy for Sudoku and classification), and the Kuramoto energy (Eq. 3) naturally decreases during updates, enabling an energy-based voting strategy at test time where multiple random initializations are sampled and the lowest-energy prediction is selected.

## Results

On CLEVRTex, AKOrN_attn achieves 88.5±0.9 FG-ARI and 59.7±0.9 MBO, competitive with the best slot-based method ISA-TS (92.9 FG-ARI) while being the first synchrony-based model to reach this level. On natural images (PascalVOC), AKOrN outperforms all SSL baselines including DINO (52.0 vs. 47.2 MBO_i) and SPOT (52.0 vs. 48.3 MBO_i). On Sudoku, AKOrN achieves 100% in-distribution accuracy and 89.5±2.5% OOD accuracy, far exceeding IRED (62.1% OOD) and R-Transformer (30.3% OOD). On CIFAR-10 robustness, AKOrN_conv retains 58.91% accuracy under AutoAttack (vs. 0.00% for ResNet-18 and ViT) with an ECE of only 1.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CLEVRTex object discovery, AKOrN_attn (88.5 FG-ARI) outperforms all slot-based baselines except ISA-TS (92.9), surpassing DTI (79.9), I-SA (79.0), and BO-SA (80.5) by 8–10 points, and is the first synchrony-based model competitive with slot-based approaches on this complex benchmark. On PascalVOC, AKOrN (52.0 MBO_i) beats DINO (47.2), MoCoV3 (47.3), SPOT (48.3), and Slot-diffusion (50.4), and on COCO2017 it outperforms DINO (31.3 vs. 29.4 MBO_i) but is surpassed by SPOT (35.0). For Sudoku reasoning, AKOrN achieves 100% ID accuracy (matching R-Transformer) and 89.5% OOD accuracy, dramatically outperforming IRED (62.1%), R-Transformer (30.3%), and the non-Kuramoto ItrSA baseline (34.4%). On CIFAR-10 adversarial robustness, AKOrN_conv retains 58.91% accuracy under AutoAttack while ResNet-18, ViT, and Diffenderfer all drop to 0.00%, and AKOrN achieves the best ECE (1.3) among all methods tested, indicating superior calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper integrates the multi-dimensional Kuramoto model (Olfati-Saber, 2006; Chandra et al., 2019) as a drop-in replacement for threshold-based neurons within standard CNN and transformer architectures, adding a symmetry-breaking conditional stimulus term C that prior oscillator-based learning work (Ricci et al., 2021; Liboni et al., 2023) lacked. It departs from the synchrony-based object discovery line (Reichert & Serre, 2013; Lowe et al., 2023), which used complex-valued neurons with χ-binding but could not scale to natural images, by showing that N-dimensional Kuramoto oscillators with asymmetric connections and iterative updates achieve competitive performance on PascalVOC and COCO2017. Unlike Energy Transformers (Hoover et al., 2023) that enforce symmetric attention to guarantee energy minimization, AKOrN uses asymmetric J matrices and a unit-norm sphere constraint, which the authors find empirically superior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AKOrN is the first synchrony-based model demonstrated to scale to natural images, outperforming DINO, MoCoV3, and MAE on object discovery without requiring a pretrained feature extractor. The robustness-by-design property (58.9% adversarial accuracy on CIFAR-10 without adversarial training, vs. 0% for ResNet-18) and near-perfect calibration (ECE 1.3 vs. 8.9) suggest that the sphere-constrained oscillatory dynamics provide implicit regularization that conventional architectures lack. Key limitations include the hard norm constraint preventing representation of feature 'presence' (limiting memory tasks), the still-inferior COCO performance relative to SPOT, and the need for iterative Kuramoto steps that increase inference cost. Open questions include whether relaxing the norm constraint improves biological plausibility and task coverage, and why N=4 rotating dimensions is empirically optimal.
