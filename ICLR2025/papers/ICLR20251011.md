## Problem

Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models require ground-truth robot action labels collected via human teleoperation during pretraining, severely limiting data scale and source diversity. Internet-scale videos offer abundant physical interaction examples but lack action labels and differ fundamentally in embodiment from robotic systems. This paper asks whether a VLA can be pretrained entirely on actionless video data and still outperform models trained with explicit action supervision.

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the model takes a single current RGB frame x_t paired with a natural-language task instruction and outputs a sequence of s discrete latent action tokens drawn from a codebook of size |C| (default 8^4 = 4096 combinations). The latent action quantization model itself consumes two frames (x_t and x_{t+H} at a fixed temporal window of ~0.6 s for robot videos, ~2.4 s for human videos) to produce the latent action label. After finetuning on a small set of action-labeled trajectories, the output switches to discretized delta end-effector actions (7 DOF for Franka/WidowX), with the latent action head replaced by a new action head.

## Method

LAPA proceeds in three sequential stages. First, a VQ-VAE-based latent action quantization model (a C-ViViT variant with spatial+temporal transformer encoder and spatial-only transformer decoder) learns discrete latent actions between frame pairs using cross-attention (rather than additive embedding) and NSVQ noise substitution to prevent gradient collapse; the encoder acts as an inverse dynamics model and the decoder as a world model. Second, a 7B VLM backbone (LWM-Chat-1M) is pretrained via behavior cloning to predict the quantized latent actions from the current observation and language instruction, with a single MLP latent action head of vocabulary |C| attached and only the vision encoder frozen. Third, the model is finetuned on a small set of action-labeled robot trajectories (e.g., 150–450 trajectories), replacing the latent action head with a discretized action head to map latent actions to actual delta end-effector actions. The key insight is that the learned latent action space is embodiment-agnostic—conditioning the decoder on the same latent action across different robots and environments yields semantically similar motions—enabling positive transfer across embodiments without requiring aligned action representations during pretraining.

## Results

On real-world tabletop manipulation, LAPA (Open-X) achieves 50.1% average success rate, outperforming OPENVLA (Open-X) by +6.22% (43.9%) while using 30× less pretraining compute (272 H100-hours vs. 21,500 A100-hours). On the Language Table benchmark, LAPA reaches 62.0% in-domain seen success rate, substantially exceeding SCRATCH (15.6%), UNIPI (22.0%), and VPT (44.0%), while narrowing the gap with the action-labeled upper bound ACTIONVLA (77.0%). Even when pretrained solely on human manipulation videos (Something-Something V2), LAPA achieves 34.0% average on real-world tasks, surpassing OPENVLA (Bridge) at 30.8% despite a much larger embodiment gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UNIPI (Du et al., 2023), LAPA wins decisively across all benchmarks: on Language Table in-domain seen, LAPA scores 62.0% vs. UNIPI's 22.0%, and on SIMPLER with human-video pretraining, LAPA achieves 52.1% vs. UNIPI's 0.7%, as UNIPI's diffusion-based planning fails on longer-horizon tasks and its IDM cannot accurately predict 7-DOF actions from only 100 labeled trajectories. Against VPT (Baker et al., 2022), LAPA outperforms in most settings (Language Table in-domain: 62.0% vs. 44.0%; cross-environment: 33.6% vs. 18.0%), though VPT is competitive in cross-task unseen (60.8% vs. 54.8%), likely benefiting from more labeled data (7k vs. 1k) for its IDM pseudo-labels. Against OPENVLA (Kim et al., 2024), LAPA (Open-X) outperforms on average (50.1% vs. 43.9%) and on all three generalization types (seen-obj/unseen-combo: 57.8% vs. 46.2%; unseen-obj: 43.9% vs. 42.1%; unseen-instr: 48.5% vs. 43.4%), but underperforms specifically on pick-and-place tasks due to early grasping failures, suggesting room for improvement in fine-grained manipulation skills.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the latent action quantization framework from GENIE (Bruce et al., 2024) — originally designed for generative interactive environments — to the problem of pretraining monolithic VLA models for real-world robot manipulation. It departs from prior video-based robot learning methods (VPT, UNIPI) that rely on inverse dynamics models or video diffusion to extract continuous pseudo-actions, instead learning discrete latent action tokens via a VQ-VAE objective (with NSVQ stabilization) applied to frame pairs, analogous to Byte Pair Encoding for language. The key architectural departure from OpenVLA and RT-2 is that no ground-truth action labels are needed during pretraining; the VLM predicts learned discrete tokens rather than discretized end-effector deltas, and the mapping to real actions is deferred to a small-scale fine-tuning stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for action labels during pretraining, this work opens the door to leveraging web-scale human manipulation video (e.g., YouTube) for robotics foundation models, a data source that is orders of magnitude larger than teleoperated robot datasets. The demonstrated 30× pretraining efficiency and the ability to outperform OpenVLA (trained on 970K labeled trajectories) on real-world tasks suggest that the field's reliance on expensive teleoperation for pretraining may be unnecessary. Open questions include whether the approach scales to non-manipulation domains (navigation, driving), whether the coarse-grained latent action space can be refined for fine-grained skills like grasping, and whether the method's advantages persist at truly internet-scale data volumes.
