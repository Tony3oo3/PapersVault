## Problem

Existing unlearning methods for LLMs rely on reverse next-word-prediction loss with backpropagation, which can destroy model fluency and reasoning when the knowledge to be forgotten scales up, or require downstream-task-specific utility losses that limit generalizability. The paper tackles the Large Scale Knowledge Washing problem: how to remove an extensive set of factual knowledge (up to 332K triplets) from a pre-trained LLM as cleanly as possible while minimally affecting the model's reasoning ability, without requiring any downstream task specification.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained decoder-only LLM, a set of factual triplets (subject, relation, object) to be washed, and a large text corpus (WikiText, 100K examples) used to estimate the covariance matrix KK^T. Each triplet is converted into a sentence, and the prefix (all tokens before the object) is fed through the model to extract the hidden state at the target MLP layer as the key K_w. The output is a set of updated W_out^l weight matrices across a contiguous set of MLP layers, producing a model that no longer confidently predicts the washed objects while retaining general reasoning and fluency. The knowledge must be expressible as triplets; pure-text knowledge without structured relations is acknowledged as a limitation.

## Method

LAW is inspired by MEMIT's key-value memory framework but inverts the objective: instead of injecting new facts (where new keys K_e are distinct from original keys K), it removes existing facts where the keys to wash K_w are a subset of K. Because the closed-form solution would require the intractable values V_2 for the remaining keys, LAW reformulates the problem as a constrained optimization: maximize ||ΔK_w||_F^2 (disrupt outputs for washed knowledge) subject to ||ΔK||_F^2 / ||K||_F^2 ≤ β (limit perturbation to the model's general knowledge and reasoning). The hyperparameter β controls the washing-vs-preservation tradeoff and is set to 1.1×β_0, where β_0 is computed from the MEMIT initialization. Three practical considerations are critical: (1) initializing Δ from MEMIT's delta matrices rather than randomly, since the non-convex objective is highly sensitive to initialization; (2) successive elimination, where before updating each layer the model is queried to identify which facts are still known, so optimization focuses only on remaining knowledge; and (3) spreading the update across multiple sequential layers (as in MEMIT) to avoid drastic single-layer parameter changes.

## Results

On the large-scale Wiki-Latest dataset (332,036 facts), LAW achieves a knowledge-washing accuracy of 0.1926 (GPT2-XL) and 0.1385 (GPT-J-6B) while preserving reasoning accuracy at 0.4832 and 0.6387 respectively, compared to the original models' 0.5105 and 0.6560. On the smaller zsRE dataset (19,086 facts), LAW reduces knowledge accuracy to 0.0050 (GPT2-XL) and 0.0000 (GPT-J-6B) with reasoning accuracy remaining at 0.5105 and 0.6468. Critically, fine-tuning-based unlearning baselines (FT-UL, SeUL, ME-FT) destroy model fluency at scale, with perplexity exceeding 10^10 on Wiki-Latest, while LAW maintains perplexity near the original (2.75 vs. 2.66 for GPT2-XL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAW outperforms MEMIT on knowledge washing thoroughness (Acc 0.1926 vs. 0.2972 on GPT2-XL, 0.1385 vs. 0.2536 on GPT-J-6B) while maintaining comparable reasoning accuracy (0.4832 vs. 0.5029; 0.6387 vs. 0.6436), showing that LAW erases more knowledge with only a ~2-5% reasoning drop. Against the unlearning baselines (FT-UL, SeUL, WOH, ME-FT), LAW is the only method that avoids destroying the model at scale: all four baselines exhibit perplexity >10^10 on Wiki-Latest, rendering them unusable, while LAW's perplexity increases only from 2.66 to 2.75. On the smaller zsRE dataset, LAW achieves near-zero knowledge recall (Acc 0.0050) with reasoning accuracy identical to the original model (0.5105), outperforming MEMIT (Acc 0.0462) and all unlearning methods. FT achieves lower knowledge accuracy than LAW on Wiki-Latest (0.0446 vs. 0.1926) but at the cost of a 35% drop in reasoning accuracy (0.3305 vs. 0.4832), indicating that aggressive fine-tuning trades away general capability. On GPT-J-6B, LAW's advantage is most pronounced: it is the only method that simultaneously achieves meaningful knowledge washing (Acc 0.1385) and preserves reasoning (0.6387 vs. 0.6560 original), whereas WOH and SeUL completely destroy the 6B model.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAW repurposes the key-value memory framework from ROME and MEMIT (which edit MLP layers to *add* factual relations) by formulating a *deletion* objective: instead of solving a closed-form update to inject new key-value pairs, it optimizes a constrained objective that maximizes disruption on target keys while bounding overall weight change. This departs from backpropagation-based unlearning methods (WOH, SeUL, FT-UL) that apply reverse next-word-prediction loss and risk destroying model capabilities, and from model-editing baselines (MEMIT, ME-FT) that inject end-of-sequence tokens as a proxy for erasure. The practical use of MEMIT's delta matrices as initialization for the non-convex optimization is a key implementation insight that distinguishes LAW from both lineages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that standard fine-tuning-based unlearning catastrophically destroys model fluency and reasoning at scale (perplexity exceeding 10^10 on Wiki-Latest), while LAW maintains near-baseline perplexity and reasoning accuracy while reducing target-knowledge accuracy to near zero — a practically important result for privacy and copyright compliance. The empirical validation that knowledge and reasoning are disentangled in MLP layers supports a broader hypothesis about transformer architecture. Key limitations include restriction to triplet-formatted knowledge (not arbitrary text), evaluation only on GPT-2/GPT-J (not LLaMA-family models), and the open question of whether the approach generalizes to non-factual or more complex knowledge structures.
