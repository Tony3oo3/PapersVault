## Problem

Existing foundational diffusion models are designed primarily for text-to-image generation and cannot natively accept multi-modal conditions such as source images, masks, or reference images, making them incompatible with the majority of visual editing and controllable generation tasks. This forces practitioners to maintain separate expert models for each task type, preventing the emergence of a unified visual generation model analogous to GPT-4 in NLP.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252060/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a Long-context Condition Unit (LCU) comprising one or more Condition Units, each pairing a T5-encoded text instruction with VAE-compressed source image latents (channel-concatenated with a downsampled mask, or an all-ones mask if absent). Historical CUs from previous editing rounds are concatenated with the current CU to form the long-context sequence, enabling multi-turn interaction. The model (0.6B parameters) outputs a single generated or edited image at approximately 512×512 resolution.

## Method

ACE builds on a Diffusion Transformer backbone and introduces three components for unified multi-task generation. First, Condition Tokenizing patchifies VAE latents (with masks) into 1-D visual token sequences and encodes instructions via T5, then concatenates all visual and text tokens across CUs into a single long sequence. Second, Image Indicator Embedding adds pre-defined token embeddings (e.g., '{image1}', '{image2}') to both the corresponding image tokens and text tokens, implicitly binding each image to its mention in the instruction. Third, the Long-context Attention Block applies 3D RoPE to differentiate spatial and frame-level positions, and replaces the standard global cross-attention with per-CU cross-attention so that image tokens in the m-th CU attend only to text tokens from the same CU, preserving frame-level alignment. Training is supervised on ~0.7 billion image pairs collected via synthesis with open-source expert models and K-means/union-find clustering over large-scale databases, with instructions generated by a fine-tuned InternVL2-26B Instruction Captioner.

## Results

On the MagicBrush benchmark, ACE achieves the best scores across all five metrics (L1, L2, CLIP-I, DINO, CLIP-T) in both single-turn and multi-turn settings. For single-turn instruction-guided editing, ACE reaches L1=0.0507, CLIP-I=0.9453, DINO=0.9215, and CLIP-T=0.2841, outperforming the next-best UltraEdit (L1=0.0575) and MagicBrush (L1=0.0625). On the ACE Benchmark user study, ACE attains the highest prompt-following scores in 7 of 12 global editing tasks and 8 of 10 local editing tasks, and the best image quality scores in 5 of 10 global and 7 of 10 local tasks. The long-context variant (ACE w/LC) provides modest but consistent improvements over the base model in multi-turn settings (e.g., DINO 0.8668 vs. 0.8661 for instruction-guided).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MagicBrush, ACE dominates all instruction-guided settings: it beats UltraEdit by ~0.007 in L1 (0.0507 vs. 0.0575) and by ~0.015 in DINO (0.9215 vs. 0.8982) for single-turn, and surpasses MagicBrush by ~0.012 in L1 (0.0507 vs. 0.0625). In multi-turn instruction-guided editing, UltraEdit edges out ACE on L1 (0.0745 vs. 0.0773) and L2 (0.0236 vs. 0.0293), but ACE leads on CLIP-I (0.9128 vs. 0.9045), DINO (0.8661 vs. 0.8505), and CLIP-T (0.2855 vs. not reported). On the ACE Benchmark user study, ACE significantly outperforms ControlNet and SDEdit on controllable generation tasks (e.g., Canny: 4.6/2.7 vs. 2.5/2.0 and 1.4/1.9 respectively) and on local inpainting/outpainting (4.4/2.7 and 4.6/2.8 vs. LaMa's 1.6/2.3 and 3.0/2.4). However, ACE trails CosXL (SDXL-based, 1024px) on style editing (2.4/2.6 vs. 3.2/3.0) and general semantic editing (2.1/2.5 vs. 3.2/2.9), which the authors attribute to ACE's smaller 0.6B parameter size and ~512px output resolution. For face editing, ACE (2.8/2.8) is competitive with InstantID (2.5/2.7) and FaceChain (2.0/3.0), while for text generation tasks (Add Text, Rm Text), ACE (4.4/2.9, 4.5/2.9) substantially outperforms AnyText (3.5/2.7) and UDiffText (3.6/2.7). Overall, ACE demonstrates that a single unified 0.6B Diffusion Transformer can match or exceed task-specific expert models across 12+ generation and editing subtasks, with the primary gap being image quality on tasks where larger-resolution baselines (SDXL/CosXL) have an advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACE builds directly on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture of Peebles & Xie (2023), extending it with three components—Condition Tokenizing, Image Indicator Embedding, and a Long-context Attention Block with 3D RoPE—to unify 8+ previously separate visual generation tasks (text-to-image, controllable generation, semantic/element editing, inpainting, reference generation) into a single model. This unification strategy is conceptually analogous to how LLMs like GPT-4 subsumed diverse NLP tasks, but departs from prior unified diffusion efforts (e.g., SEED-X, UltraEdit, UniControlNet) by introducing the Long-context Condition Unit (LCU) to support multi-turn, history-aware generation. The data pipeline repurposes existing open-source expert models (StyleBooth, AnyDoor, etc.) for synthetic pair generation and combines them with SigLIP-based clustering over large-scale databases, while a fine-tuned InternVL2-26B serves as an automated instruction labeler.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ACE demonstrates that a single 0.6B-parameter diffusion transformer can match or exceed task-specific models across 12+ subtasks, reducing the need for a patchwork of specialized pipelines in production visual agents. The LCU mechanism opens a path toward ChatGPT-like interactive image editing, though the measured improvement from long-context history is modest (e.g., L1 drops from 0.0778 to 0.0768 in multi-turn editing), leaving open whether this benefit scales with more rounds or richer history. Key limitations include the ~512 output resolution and 0.6B parameter scale, which the authors acknowledge cause underperformance on style and general editing versus 1024-resolution SDXL-based methods; scaling the unified architecture to higher resolutions and larger parameter counts remains an important open question.
