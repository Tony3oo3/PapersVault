## Problem

Existing robust representation learning methods in visual RL focus on discarding irrelevant visual features but ignore the downstream sequential decision-making objective, resulting in representations that lack critical long-term information needed for effective action-value estimation. This gap impairs generalization when agents face unseen visual distractions (e.g., novel backgrounds or color shifts) in test environments, because the representations cannot support accurate long-horizon reward prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253359/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB image observations (e.g., 84×84 DMC frames or 64×64 Procgen frames) paired with discrete or continuous actions, along with one-step scalar rewards from a replay buffer. It produces three outputs: (1) a policy action, (2) a D-dimensional robust action-value representation extracted from the critic's center layer that is linearly mapped to a scalar Q-value, and (3) a D-dimensional compressed reward representation modeled as a Gaussian (mean + diagonal covariance) that is linearly mapped to the one-step reward. The key structural constraint is that the robust action-value representation must satisfy a recursive TD relation with the compressed reward representation, tying long-term value estimation to per-step reward encoding.

## Method

ROUSER formulates robust action-value representation learning under the conditional information bottleneck: for each state-action pair, the representation maximizes mutual information with the (unknown) action value while minimizing conditional mutual information with the observation-action pair given the action value. Since true action values are unavailable during training, ROUSER decomposes the robust action-value representation recursively into a compressed reward representation (from the current one-step reward) plus a discounted future robust action-value representation, enabling TD-based training with known rewards. The compressed reward representation is learned by a separate reward model that applies a second IB objective—maximizing MI with the one-step reward while minimizing MI with the observation-action pair—using a symmetrized KL divergence between Gaussian encodings and an InfoNCE-based mutual information estimator. The robust action-value representations are trained via a double-critic TD loss applied to the critic's center-layer embeddings, with a sign-aware min operator to mitigate overestimation bias. A theoretical bound (Theorem 4.4) guarantees that the linear mapping from learned representations to action values approximates the true Q-function within ε/(1−γ), making ROUSER a drop-in module compatible with arbitrary actor-critic or value-based VRL algorithms.

## Results

On the DMC benchmark with unseen dynamic color distractions (β=0.5) and unseen video background distractions, ROUSER outperforms all baselines in 11 out of 12 tasks at 500K environment steps (averaged over 6 seeds, 100 episodes). ROUSER-SRM achieves the highest scores, e.g., 942.6±12.3 on ball_in_cup_catch and 853.4±25.6 on finger_spin under color distractions. On Procgen (discrete actions, 25M steps, 5 seeds), ROUSER-QR-DQN also improves generalization over QR-DQN alone on test levels. The method is effective even on sparse-reward tasks (ball_in_cup_catch, finger_spin, reacher_easy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROUSER is integrated with two policy-gradient VRL algorithms (DrQv2 and SRM) and one value-based algorithm (QR-DQN), and consistently improves each base method: ROUSER-DrQv2 outperforms DrQv2 and ROUSER-SRM outperforms SRM across all DMC tasks under both color and background distractions. Against four dedicated generalization baselines—CURL (contrastive representation learning), RAP (robust behavioral metric), CRESP (reward sequence prediction), and TACO (temporal latent action contrastive loss)—ROUSER achieves the best performance in 11 out of 12 DMC tasks. The advantage is particularly pronounced on sparse-reward tasks (ball_in_cup_catch, finger_spin, reacher_easy), where ROUSER's use of one-step rewards to capture long-term action-value information provides a clear edge over methods that rely on data augmentation or contrastive objectives alone. On Procgen with discrete actions, ROUSER-QR-DQN improves over QR-DQN, confirming the method's applicability beyond continuous control. The one task where ROUSER does not win is not specified in the main text but is detailed in Appendix E.1.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROUSER bridges two previously separate lines of work in VRL: (1) robust representation learning methods (CURL, SRM, DrQv2, CRESP, TACO) that discard visual distractions but ignore long-term decision-relevant information, and (2) task decomposition / successor feature methods (Dayan 1993, Barreto 2017) that capture long-term sequential structure but do not handle visual distractions. The key conceptual move is formulating robust action-value representations within the conditional information bottleneck (Chechik & Tishby, 2002), then using the recursive decomposition from successor representations to replace unknown action values with known one-step rewards, making the IB objective tractable. The method is architecturally lightweight: it reuses the critic's center-layer embeddings as the robust representation and adds a small reward model, requiring no changes to the base VRL algorithm (DrQv2, SRM, QR-DQN).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap—existing robust representation methods produce features that are distraction-invariant but lack the long-term temporal structure needed for sequential decision-making—and provides a clean theoretical bound (Theorem 4.4) guaranteeing that the learned representation can recover true action values up to a 1/(1−γ)·ε error. Its plug-and-play design (no architectural changes to the base algorithm) makes it immediately applicable to a wide range of actor-critic and value-based VRL methods. Limitations include the reliance on known reward functions (the authors note inverse RL as future work), the Gaussian assumption on compressed reward representations, and evaluation limited to DMC and Procgen; whether the IB-based compression remains beneficial in higher-dimensional or partially observable settings remains open.
