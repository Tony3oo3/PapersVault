## Problem

TD-MPC, a leading model-based RL method, suffers from three interrelated failures: training collapse after ~4M steps due to indirect encoder updates, inability to filter irrelevant visual noise from inputs, and slow sequential computation in its objective function. These issues stem from the absence of an explicit encoder loss term and the sequential dependency of latent state prediction through the dynamics model, leaving the learned representation without theoretical fidelity guarantees.

## Input / Output

BS-MPC accepts either low-dimensional state vectors or RGB images (from DeepMind Control Suite) as observations, encoding them into a 50- or 100-dimensional latent space via an MLP or CNN respectively. At each control step, it outputs a sequence of H=5 actions computed by MPPI (sampling-based MPC) that rolls out M=512 candidate trajectories through the learned latent dynamics, reward, and value function. The encoder is trained jointly with the dynamics, reward, and value models from a replay buffer of (s, a, r, s') tuples, with the only architectural difference from TD-MPC being the addition of a bisimulation metric loss term weighted by a single hyperparameter c4.

## Method

BS-MPC augments the TD-MPC objective with a per-timestep bisimulation metric loss (term D in Eq. 8) that minimizes the squared difference between the ℓ₁-distance of encoded states and the on-policy bisimulation metric (reward difference plus γ-scaled Wasserstein distance of next-state distributions). This explicit encoder loss provides direct gradients to the encoder at every time step, preventing the training collapse seen in TD-MPC where the encoder is only updated indirectly through the consistency loss. Crucially, BS-MPC encodes all H+1 observations in a trajectory independently (z_{t:t+H+1} = h(s_{t:t+H+1})) rather than sequentially propagating through the dynamics model, which eliminates the sequential dependency and enables fully parallel computation across time steps. Theoretically, the bisimulation metric loss ensures the ℓ₁-distance in latent space approximates the π*-bisimulation metric, which via Theorem 3 upper-bounds the cumulative reward difference between the original MDP and the latent MDP over any trajectory by O(ε/(1-γ)(1-c)). For planning, BS-MPC uses MPPI with the same learned latent models, sampling 512 trajectories and computing weighted averages to produce the optimal action sequence.

## Results

On 26 state-based DM Control tasks, BS-MPC consistently outperforms TD-MPC, SAC, and Dreamer-v3, with TD-MPC exhibiting performance collapse after ~1M steps in many tasks while BS-MPC steadily improves. On 10 image-based tasks, BS-MPC achieves performance competitive with TD-MPC, DrQ-v2, and Dreamer-v3, while consistently outperforming CURL and SAC. In distraction experiments (5 tasks with Kinetics background noise), BS-MPC constantly outperforms TD-MPC. Computationally, BS-MPC reduces training time by ~15–20% over TD-MPC (e.g., Walker-run: 8.3h vs. 10.1h; Finger-Spin: 8.3h vs. 10.0h on RTX-4090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TD-MPC (Hansen et al., 2022), BS-MPC wins on all three axes: training stability (TD-MPC collapses after ~1M steps in many state-based tasks while BS-MPC improves monotonically), noise robustness (TD-MPC fails to gain reward when irrelevant Kinetics videos are added to image inputs, whereas BS-MPC maintains performance), and computational speed (~15–20% faster due to parallelizable computation). Against TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2024), TD-MPC2 converges faster on certain image tasks (e.g., quadruped-run, walker-run) but requires substantially more parameters (5M vs. BS-MPC's shared architecture) and significantly more computation time due to discrete regression; BS-MPC achieves comparable or slightly better overall performance with fewer parameters. Against SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018) and Dreamer-v3 (Hafner et al., 2024), BS-MPC clearly outperforms both on state-based tasks, particularly in high-dimensional environments (dog, humanoid). On image-based tasks, BS-MPC is competitive with DrQ-v2 (Yarats et al., 2022) and Dreamer-v3, and consistently outperforms CURL (Laskin et al., 2020) across all 10 image tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

BS-MPC is a direct architectural extension of TD-MPC (Hansen et al., 2022), retaining the same five components (encoder, latent dynamics, reward, state-action value, policy) and MPPI-based planning, but replacing TD-MPC's implicit encoder training with an explicit bisimulation metric loss at every time step. The bisimulation metric itself is drawn from the representation-learning line of work by Ferns et al. (2011), Castro (2020), and Zhang et al. (2021), which trains encoders to preserve behavioral equivalence rather than reconstruction fidelity. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) the encoder is optimized directly via the π*-bisimulation MSE loss rather than only through backpropagated dynamics gradients, and (2) all H latent states are obtained by encoding the full observation sequence in parallel rather than by sequential dynamics rollout, eliminating the computational bottleneck in TD-MPC's cost computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretical upper bound on the cumulative-reward difference between the original and latent MDPs (Theorem 3), a guarantee absent from TD-MPC and most practical MBRL methods, which gives practitioners a principled way to assess encoder fidelity. Practically, the simultaneous resolution of training collapse, noise sensitivity, and sequential-computation overhead via a single additional loss term is a clean and deployable improvement for anyone working with TD-MPC-style planners. The main open question is the per-environment grid search required for the bisimulation loss weight c4 (ranging from 10⁻⁸ to 0.5 across tasks), which limits out-of-the-box usability; automatic or adaptive tuning of this coefficient would be a natural next step. The theoretical analysis also assumes convergence to the optimal policy and zero approximation error in the learned dynamics and reward models, so how tight the bound is in practice with finite-capacity networks remains to be characterized.
