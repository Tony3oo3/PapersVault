## Problem

The stability of decoder-only language model pre-training to randomness factors (parameter initialisation, data order) remains understudied, and the community lacks multi-seed model suites with dense intermediate checkpoints for systematic investigation. Prior multi-seed releases were limited to encoder-only models, single sizes, or unavailable intermediate checkpoints, making it impossible to track how training dynamics evolve across seeds and scales.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253103/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis consumes 50 training runs (10 seeds × 5 sizes from 14M to 410M parameters), each with 154 checkpoints spanning 143k optimisation steps on The Pile. Downstream evaluation uses log-likelihood scoring on multiple-choice benchmarks at selected checkpoints; representational probing aggregates per-layer token embeddings via learned layer weights before passing them to MDL-regularised linear probes; parameter statistics (L1/L2 norms, singular value distributions, weight/bias moments) are standardised across seeds before HMM fitting. Outputs include per-seed accuracy and Cohen's κ trajectories, per-task macro-F1/codelength-ratio/SSA curves, 5-state HMM training maps, and linear-regression z-score predictions of final performance from bag-of-states vectors.

## Method

The paper introduces PolyPythias, 45 new Pythia pre-training runs (9 additional seeds per size) trained with identical hyperparameters to the original suite but varying seeds for both parameter initialisation and data shuffling, yielding ~7k new checkpoints. Stability is quantified along three axes: (i) downstream performance via inter-seed agreement and self-consistency (Cohen's κ) plus accuracy on seven multiple-choice tasks; (ii) representational stability via information-theoretic MDL probing on seven linguistic tasks, measuring macro-F1, codelength ratio, and principal subspace angles between consecutive checkpoints; (iii) parameter dynamics via 5-state HMM training maps fitted to 13 standardised parameter statistics per checkpoint. The key finding is that training follows consistent phases—an initial learning phase (steps 10³–10⁴) and a critical phase (10⁴–10⁵)—across all seeds and sizes, with only two outlier runs (410M seeds 3 and 4) exhibiting 'forks' in their training maps and loss spikes. Notably, HMMs trained on smaller models can zero-shot assign states to larger-model checkpoints, and the resulting bag-of-states predicts final 410M performance with R² > 0.9 for all source sizes except 14M, identifying underperforming seeds by their negative regression coefficients.

## Results

Across 50 training runs (10 seeds × 5 model sizes, 14M–410M parameters), language modelling is largely stable: only 2 out of 50 runs (410M seeds 3 and 4) are identified as outliers, exhibiting loss spikes and 'forks' in their training maps. Inter-seed agreement (Cohen's κ) peaks at ≈0.7 around step 10³ and converges to ≈0.5 by step 10⁴, while self-consistency steadily increases to the same moderate-agreement level. Representational stability metrics are highly correlated across model sizes (average r̄ = 0.99 for macro-F1, 0.98 for Codelength Ratio, 0.94 for SSA), and training maps from smaller models zero-shot predict 410M final performance with R² > 0.96 (31M: 0.97, 70M: 0.97, 160M: 0.96).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a resource and empirical-study paper rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so there are no competing methods to benchmark against. The paper's internal comparisons are across seeds (10 per size), across model sizes (14M–410M), and across training phases. The key finding is that training dynamics are remarkably consistent: state transitions in the HMM-based training maps occur at similar steps across all seeds and sizes (e.g., the 0→1 transition around step 5k–7k for smaller models), with the sole exception of 410M seeds 3 and 4, which deviate early and fail the 2→3 transition. For cross-size prediction, the 31M, 70M, and 160M HMMs all achieve R² > 0.96 when zero-shot predicting 410M performance, while the 14M HMM achieves only R² = 0.18, indicating that the smallest model's parameter statistics carry limited transferable signal. The paper also contextualises its findings against prior multi-seed suites (MultiBERTs, GPT-2 multi-seed, Llama-2-7B multi-seed), noting that PolyPythias spans more model sizes (5 vs. 1–2) and provides more intermediate checkpoints (154 per run vs. 28–600) with all data publicly available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Pythia model suite (Biderman et al., 2023) by adding 9 new random seeds per model size, following the multi-seed resource paradigm established by MultiBERTs (Sellam et al., 2022) and the GPT-2 multi-seed runs (Karamcheti et al., 2021), but for decoder-only transformers across five sizes with full checkpoint granularity. The analytical toolkit combines three prior methods: information-theoretic MDL probing for representational stability (Müller-Eberstein et al., 2023), HMM-based training maps for parameter dynamics (Hu et al., 2023), and standard downstream evaluation with Cohen's κ for inter-seed agreement. Unlike prior multi-seed suites limited to one or two sizes or encoder-only architectures, PolyPythias spans 14M–410M parameters with 154 checkpoints per run, enabling systematic cross-size and cross-seed comparisons that were previously infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of ~7k new checkpoints across 5 sizes and 10 seeds provides a critical open resource for studying pre-training stability without incurring the cost of training from scratch, directly addressing a gap the authors identify in the community's tooling. The finding that training maps (bag-of-states from HMMs) can predict final performance with R² > 0.9 even when zero-shot transferred from smaller models offers a practical early-warning mechanism for identifying unstable runs, though the authors note that at least 120k of 143k steps are needed for reliable prediction, limiting its utility for early stopping. Key limitations include the 410M parameter ceiling (reflecting compute constraints) and the open question of whether the identified learning phases and outlier signatures generalize to the multi-billion-parameter regime where most contemporary LMs operate. The paper also leaves open the connection between HMM state transitions and circuit-level phenomena (e.g., induction head formation), which would require interventional studies.
