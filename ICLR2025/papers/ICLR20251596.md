## Problem

Prior learning-augmented frequency estimation work (Hsu et al., ICLR'19; Aamand et al., NeurIPS'23) was restricted to 1D discrete streams and only analyzed randomized algorithms like CountMin and CountSketch, leaving the popular deterministic Misra-Gries algorithm unanalyzed. Additionally, the natural high-dimensional generalization—finding frequent directions (top singular vectors) of a matrix streamed row-by-row—had no learning-augmented treatment. This paper fills both gaps by unifying the two settings under a single matrix-streaming framework.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stream of n row vectors A_1, …, A_n ∈ R^d (or n discrete elements from [d] in the 1D special case) together with m_L orthonormal predicted frequent directions from a learned oracle, using a total of m×d words of memory. It outputs a sketch matrix B whose Gram matrix B^T B approximates A^T A, enabling queries of the form ‖A v‖² for any unit vector v. The error is measured as a weighted sum over the right singular vectors of A, with weights proportional to σ_i²/‖A‖²_F, which reduces to the standard frequency-weighted error when rows are basis vectors.

## Method

The core mechanism decomposes each incoming vector into a component in the predicted subspace (spanned by the m_L oracle directions) and an orthogonal component, then feeds each part to a separate Frequent Directions instance with m_L and m−2m_L space respectively; the final sketch stacks both outputs. In the 1D Misra-Gries special case, this reduces to storing exact counts for predicted heavy hitters and running standard Misra-Gries on the remainder. Under Zipfian singular values (σ_i² ∝ 1/i), the learned algorithm achieves weighted error Θ(‖A‖²_F/(m·(ln d)²)), removing the logarithmic factor ln(m/ln(2d/m))·ln(d/m) present in the unlearned bound and matching state-of-the-art learned CountSketch++ guarantees while being deterministic and simpler. A robustness variant (Algorithm 3) additionally maintains a standard FD sketch and a residual-error estimator, switching to the standard sketch's output whenever the learned sketch's estimate deviates too far, yielding worst-case guarantees with only a constant-factor space overhead.

## Results

Theoretically, the learning-augmented Misra-Gries algorithm achieves a weighted error of Θ(1/m · n/(ln d)²), removing the (ln(m/ln(2d/m))) · ln(d/m) factor present in the unlearned version. Similarly, the learning-augmented Frequent Directions algorithm achieves Θ(1/(ln d)² · ||A||²_F/m) versus Θ((ln(m/ln(2d/m))) · ln(d/m)/(ln d)² · ||A||²_F/m) for the base algorithm. Empirically, the learned Frequent Directions outperforms the unlearned version by 1–2 orders of magnitude on all four matrix streaming datasets (Hyper, Logo, Friends, Eagle), and the learned Misra-Gries is competitive with or outperforms the state-of-the-art learned CountSketch++ on both CAIDA and AOL frequency estimation datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base Frequent Directions algorithm of Ghashami et al. (2016), the learning-augmented variant improves median error by 1–2 orders of magnitude across all four matrix streaming datasets (Hyper, Logo, Friends, Eagle), and in most cases performs within an order of magnitude of the full-memory SVD approximation. Against the learned CountSketch of Hsu et al. (2019), the learned Misra-Gries significantly outperforms on both CAIDA and AOL datasets, consistent with the theoretical gap (the ln(d/m) factor in the learned CS bound). Against the state-of-the-art learned CountSketch++ of Aamand et al. (2023), the learned Misra-Gries is competitive: it sometimes outperforms the best hyperparameter choice of CS++ and often outperforms several others, while being the only deterministic algorithm with provable guarantees in this setting. The learned Misra-Gries also has no tunable hyperparameter (unlike CS++'s truncation threshold C), making it simpler to deploy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: learning-augmented frequency estimation (Hsu et al., ICLR'19; Aamand et al., NeurIPS'23) and deterministic matrix sketching via Frequent Directions (Ghashami et al., 2016). The key conceptual move is to show that Misra-Gries is a special case of Frequent Directions when inputs are basis vectors, thereby subsuming the 1D heavy-hitters problem under the matrix streaming framework. Prior learning-augmented frequency estimation work analyzed only randomized algorithms (CountMin, CountSketch, CountSketch++); this paper provides the first analysis of the deterministic Misra-Gries algorithm in the learned setting and shows it matches state-of-the-art bounds without randomness or Zipfian-specific tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework gives practitioners a single algorithmic template (learned Frequent Directions) that handles both scalar frequency estimation and matrix low-rank approximation, with clean space/error tradeoffs under power-law structure. A notable limitation is that learned Frequent Directions lacks the robustness guarantee of learned Misra-Gries: with adversarially bad predictions, the learned FD can perform worse than the non-learned version, requiring a constant-factor space overhead workaround (Algorithm 3). Open questions include whether the Zipfian/power-law assumption can be relaxed, how prediction quality degrades in practice for non-stationary streams, and whether the robustness gap between learned MG and learned FD can be closed without the residual-error estimation overhead.
