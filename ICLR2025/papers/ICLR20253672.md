## Problem

Existing autoformalization evaluation methods rely on manual verification or surface-form metrics like BLEU and Lean 4 compilation checks, which fail to detect semantic misalignments where a formal statement is logically valid and superficially similar to the ground truth but omits or alters key mathematical content. This paper introduces the first automated framework for evaluating semantic alignment between informal and formal mathematical statements, reducing dependence on labor-intensive human review.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253672/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language mathematical statement and a candidate Lean 4 formal theorem as input, and produces a scalar alignment score V_align in [0,1] that combines a sequence-level certainty score (exponential of mean token log-probability) with a representation-level similarity score (cosine similarity of final hidden states). For alignment selection, the score ranks a pool of 22 formal candidates (1 positive + 21 negatives generated via six misalignment strategies); for alignment detection, a fixed threshold θ=0.7 yields a binary aligned/misaligned classification evaluated by precision and recall.

## Method

FORMALALIGN fine-tunes a Mistral-7B transformer with a dual loss: standard cross-entropy loss for the autoformalization sequence-generation task, plus an InfoNCE-style contrastive loss applied to the final-position hidden states of the informal input and the formal output conditioned on that input, encouraging high cosine similarity for matched pairs and low similarity for in-batch negatives. At inference, the alignment score averages the certainty score (model confidence in generating the formal tokens) and the similarity score (embedding alignment), so that both token-level fluency and representation-level semantic correspondence contribute to the final judgment. The key design insight is that the two losses are mutually reinforcing: the cross-entropy loss grounds the model in correct formal syntax and structure, while the contrastive loss explicitly teaches the model to discriminate aligned from misaligned representations, a capability that neither loss alone provides (ablations show CE-only achieves 98.64% AS on FormL4-Basic but drops to 52.45% on MiniF2F-Valid, while the combined model reaches 99.21% and 66.39% respectively).

## Results

On FormL4-Basic, FORMALALIGN (Mistral-7B) achieves an Alignment-Selection score of 99.21% and a Precision of 93.65%, substantially outperforming GPT-4 (90.23% AS, 42.68% Precision). On the more challenging out-of-domain MiniF2F-Valid set, FORMALALIGN reaches 66.39% AS and 68.58% Precision, compared to GPT-4's 67.24% AS and 59.85% Precision. Across all four benchmarks, FORMALALIGN consistently achieves the highest Precision scores (ranging from 66.70% to 93.65%), while GPT-4 maintains higher Recall (88–93%) at the cost of much lower Precision (42–62%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4 (CoT prompting), FORMALALIGN achieves a decisive advantage on Precision across all four datasets: 93.65% vs. 42.68% on FormL4-Basic, 86.90% vs. 45.72% on FormL4-Random, 68.58% vs. 59.85% on MiniF2F-Valid, and 66.70% vs. 62.45% on MiniF2F-Test. On Alignment-Selection, FORMALALIGN wins on FormL4-Basic (99.21% vs. 90.23%) but trails GPT-4 on FormL4-Random (85.85% vs. 91.85%), MiniF2F-Valid (66.39% vs. 67.24%), and MiniF2F-Test (64.61% vs. 70.82%). GPT-4 also maintains substantially higher Recall (88–93%) than FORMALALIGN (60–89%), indicating that FORMALALIGN is more conservative in flagging pairs as aligned. Against GPT-3.5, FORMALALIGN dominates on every metric across all datasets, with AS gaps of 35–24 points and Precision gaps of 41–45 points. The paper also reports (Appendix J) that even enhanced GPT-4 prompting variants (binary, CoT, two-phase) remain far below FORMALALIGN on Precision, with the best GPT-4 variant reaching only 42.68% Precision on FormL4-Basic versus FORMALALIGN's 93.65%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new sub-task within autoformalization: automated semantic alignment evaluation (AAE), which prior work has not addressed. Existing autoformalization pipelines (Wu et al., 2022; Jiang et al., 2023c; Azerbayev et al., 2023b) rely on BLEU surface-form matching or Lean 4 compiler validity checks for evaluation, both of which are blind to semantic misalignment. The method repurposes the InfoNCE-style contrastive loss from representation learning (analogous to CLIP's image-text alignment) and combines it with standard cross-entropy sequence generation, applying this dual-loss paradigm to the informal-formal domain for the first time. The evaluation protocol—augmenting aligned pairs with structured negative examples via six misalignment strategies—is also novel and not present in prior autoformalization benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By automating alignment evaluation, FORMALALIGN directly addresses the scalability bottleneck of manual verification in autoformalization workflows, reducing evaluation time from ~3 hours per 80 items (human) to under 2 minutes. However, the out-of-domain performance drop on MiniF2F (66.39% AS, 60.66% recall) and the 14.6-point gap to human experts (79.58%) reveal that the method still struggles with subtle, context-dependent misalignments. The 0.49 Fleiss' K among human experts suggests the task itself is inherently subjective, raising open questions about whether a single scalar score can capture the full complexity of mathematical alignment. Future work could explore whether integrating formal reasoning (e.g., symbolic equivalence checking) or multi-step proof-level alignment could close the remaining gap.
