## Problem

Existing AI-assisted IC design methods are task-specific and rely solely on circuit graph structure, overlooking the inherently multimodal nature of RTL circuits (HDL code, structural graph, functionality summary). No prior work has built a general, multimodal, implementation-aware circuit encoder capable of supporting diverse downstream design tasks from a single pre-trained representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251851/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three aligned modalities per sub-circuit: Verilog HDL code (up to 32K tokens), a structural graph of operators and registers, and a GPT-4-generated natural-language functionality summary, plus a gate-level netlist graph used only during pre-training. Circuits are pre-split into sub-circuits at sequential-register boundaries (roughly 1000× smaller than full designs), and the output is a 768-dim fused embedding per sub-circuit that supports regression to design quality metrics (slack, WNS, TNS, power, area) or direct zero-shot retrieval of similar known circuits.

## Method

CircuitFusion encodes each sub-circuit through three unimodal encoders (a 7-layer Graphormer for the structural graph, a 6-layer BERT-based transformer for the summary, and a frozen NV-Embed-V1 LLM encoder for HDL code), then fuses them via a summary-centric cross-attention mechanism where graph and code embeddings are first mixed via a learnable interpolation before serving as keys/values against summary queries. Pre-training jointly optimizes four self-supervised objectives that exploit four circuit-specific properties: (1) masked graph modeling and intra-modal contrastive learning with functionally-equivalent-transformed positives, (2) cross-modal contrastive alignment, (3) masked summary modeling plus mixup-summary binary matching for fusion, and (4) implementation-aware contrastive alignment between the RTL fused embedding and a pre-trained GraphSAGE netlist encoder. For downstream inference, a retrieval-augmented step retrieves the top-k most similar known sub-circuits from a vector store and concatenates their quality metrics with the target embedding, enabling both improved fine-tuning (via XGBoost regression) and zero-shot prediction without any task-specific training.

## Results

CircuitFusion achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five RTL-stage design quality prediction tasks, with MAPE of 12% (slack), 11% (WNS), 15% (TNS), 13% (power), and 11% (area), and correlation coefficients (R) of 0.87, 0.91, 0.99, 0.99, and 0.99 respectively. Compared to the best task-specific hardware baselines, CircuitFusion improves MAPE by 5% on slack, WNS, and area, 10% on TNS, and 13% on power. Additionally, CircuitFusion is the first method to support zero-shot circuit quality prediction via retrieval-augmented inference, achieving 21% MAPE on slack without any task-specific training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CircuitFusion outperforms all seven baselines across every task and metric. Against the strongest hardware-specific baselines, it beats RTL-Timer (DAC 2024) by 5% MAPE on slack (12% vs 17%) and WNS (11% vs 16%), and by 10% on TNS (15% vs 25%); it beats MasterRTL (TCAD 2024) by 13% on power (13% vs 26%) and 5% on area (11% vs 16%). Against the self-supervised circuit encoder SNS v2 (MICRO 2023), CircuitFusion improves WNS MAPE from 22% to 11% and power MAPE from 28% to 13%. General-purpose text and software code encoders (NV-Embed-v1, UnixCoder, CodeT5+, CodeSage) perform substantially worse, with MAPE values often exceeding 40% on TNS and power, and they cannot handle the slack prediction task at all due to their 1024-token input limit. Notably, even when the paper's sub-circuit generation and retrieval-augmented inference strategies are applied to these baselines (Table 10), CircuitFusion still maintains a consistent 1–3% MAPE advantage, confirming the value of its hardware-specific multimodal pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends circuit representation learning beyond the graph-only paradigm established by SNS v2, DeepGate, and DeepGate2, introducing the first multimodal encoder that fuses HDL code, structural graph, and GPT-4-generated functionality summaries via a BLIP-style cross-attention mechanism. The masked graph modeling objective draws directly from GraphMAE, while the contrastive alignment tasks follow the InfoNCE framework used in prior circuit and vision-language work. The key conceptual departure is the identification of four circuit-specific properties (parallel execution, functional equivalence, multi-stage design, reusability) that drive both the sub-circuit splitting strategy and the implementation-aware RTL-to-netlist alignment, neither of which has appeared in prior circuit or software representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CircuitFusion's retrieval-augmented inference enables zero-shot design quality prediction—a capability no prior hardware method supports—making it practically valuable for early-stage RTL design where labeled data is scarce. However, the training corpus is limited to 41 open-source designs (~7k sub-circuits), raising questions about whether the learned representations generalize to proprietary or architecturally novel circuits. The paper acknowledges that multi-clock designs and clock-domain crossings remain unaddressed, and the scalability analysis, while promising, is based on a single dataset family. Releasing a large-scale foundation model trained on synthetic circuits is identified as the critical next step for broader impact.
