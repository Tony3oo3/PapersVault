## Problem

Mesh graph neural networks for fluid dynamics simulation suffer from over-squashing when meshes are locally refined near boundaries, causing information to be repeatedly compressed as it propagates through bottleneck regions. Existing graph rewiring methods address this by modifying topology alone, but they ignore the physical phenomena (e.g., velocity gradients) that determine which long-range interactions are actually important in fluid flows, making them suboptimal for mesh-based simulation.

## Input / Output

PIORF operates on a mesh graph where nodes carry velocity vectors and boundary-condition type labels, and edges carry relative displacement vectors and their norms. It produces a rewired graph by adding bidirectional edges between ORC-identified bottleneck nodes and their most velocity-dissimilar counterparts, controlled by a single pooling ratio δ (typically 3–7%). The rewired graph is then consumed by downstream MGN, BSMS, Graph Transformer, or HMT architectures to predict next-timestep velocity, pressure, and (for compressible flows) density fields.

## Method

PIORF computes node-level Ollivier-Ricci curvature γ_i (the mean edge curvature over all incident edges) to identify the ⌊δ|V|⌋ bottleneck nodes with the lowest curvature. For each bottleneck node s, it computes Euclidean velocity distances to all other nodes and selects the node r with the maximum velocity difference, then adds a bidirectional edge (s,r). This single-pass, physics-informed rewiring avoids the iterative greedy edge-selection loops used by SDRF, FoSR, and BORF, yielding O(|E|·d_max³) complexity with only one hyperparameter. The physical rationale is that large velocity differences mark regions of flow instability where long-range coupling is physically meaningful, so connecting bottlenecks to these regions enables the GNN to propagate information across the mesh without excessive message-passing depth.

## Results

On MGN, PIORF achieves the largest improvements on AIRFOIL: 24.5% reduction in velocity RMSE (7,743 vs. 10,261 for MGN) and 26.2% reduction in pressure RMSE (2,245,858 vs. 3,043,186). On CYLINDERFLOW, PIORF reduces velocity RMSE by 14.8% (41.6 vs. 48.8) and pressure RMSE by 21.3% (28.9 vs. 36.7). On the large-scale EAGLE dataset (270GB, dynamic mesh), PIORF achieves 14.5% velocity RMSE improvement (1,950 vs. 2,280) while BORF times out entirely, and PIORF maintains the lowest rewiring computation time across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIORF consistently outperforms all four topology-only rewiring baselines (DIGL, SDRF, FoSR, BORF) on MGN across both CYLINDERFLOW and AIRFOIL. On AIRFOIL, PIORF's velocity RMSE of 7,743 beats the next-best BORF (10,029) by 22.8%, while DIGL actually worsens performance (11,534 vs. MGN's 10,261). On CYLINDERFLOW, PIORF's pressure RMSE of 28.9 is 19.1% lower than SDRF (35.5) and 21.4% lower than FoSR (35.0). On EAGLE, PIORF is the only method that both completes and outperforms MGN (1,950 vs. 2,280 velocity), whereas BORF times out and DIGL degrades performance (2,623). When applied to hierarchical models (BSMS, GT, HMT), PIORF still achieves the best or near-best results, though gains are smaller (e.g., HMT on AIRFOIL: 4,961 vs. 5,303 velocity RMSE). In terms of computational efficiency, PIORF's single-pass rewiring is substantially faster than iterative methods like BORF, whose computation time grows steeply with edge count on large graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

PIORF extends the Ollivier-Ricci curvature (ORC) framework introduced by Ollivier (2009) and operationalized for rewiring by BORF (Nguyen et al., 2023), but departs from all prior rewiring methods (DIGL, SDRF, FoSR, BORF) by incorporating physical quantities—specifically fluid velocity—into the edge-selection criterion alongside topological bottleneck identification. The method is applied within the MeshGraphNets (Pfaff et al., 2020) paradigm and its hierarchical extensions (BSMS, HMT), positioning it as a plug-in rewiring layer rather than a new architecture. The key conceptual departure is that existing rewiring methods define bottlenecks purely from graph geometry, whereas PIORF argues that in fluid dynamics the physically relevant long-range interactions are determined by velocity gradients, not just topological curvature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rewiring method for mesh-based fluid dynamics that explicitly couples physical context with graph topology, and it demonstrates that this coupling yields consistent improvements (up to 26.2%) across four architectures and three datasets, including a large-scale dynamic-mesh setting (EAGLE) where the closest ORC-based competitor (BORF) times out. The single-pass computational design makes PIORF practical for the thousands-of-nodes regime typical of CFD meshes, where iterative rewiring becomes prohibitive. Open questions include whether the choice of physical quantity (velocity vs. pressure vs. density) can be made adaptive per dataset, whether the approach extends to 3D simulations and dynamic adaptive mesh refinement, and whether the physics-informed principle generalizes beyond Navier-Stokes to other PDE-governed systems.
