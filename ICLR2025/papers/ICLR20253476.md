## Problem

Binary Neural Networks (BNNs) are vulnerable to adversarial perturbations, yet their robustness verification remains less studied than full-precision networks. Existing exact methods based on SAT/SMT or MILP suffer from severe scalability limitations, and the LP relaxations underlying MILP solvers produce overly coarse bounds for BNNs due to the discontinuous sign activation. This paper asks whether semidefinite programming relaxations from sparse polynomial optimization can provide tighter, more scalable verification bounds for BNNs with continuous input space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained BNN's parameters (binary weight matrices, real-valued biases, and batch normalization statistics), a continuous input perturbation region specified as an L∞ or L2 ball around a reference image, and a target class label for the adversarial objective. Unlike SAT-based approaches that require input quantization, the input space remains fully continuous. The output is a numerical lower bound τ on the adversarial margin; if τ > 0, the network is certified robust against the specified perturbation, and the bound value quantifies the safety margin. The method also reports verification time per instance.

## Method

The BNN verification problem is encoded as a Quadratically Constrained Quadratic Program (QCQP) by exploiting the semi-algebraic characterization of the sign function: x² = 1 (equality) and x·(Wx+b) ≥ 0 (inequality), with the input region encoded as quadratic polynomial constraints. The key theoretical contribution is proving that adding two redundant quadratic tautologies per neuron—derived from the bound |Wx| ≤ nv(W)—to the first-order SDP relaxation yields a quadratic module that strictly contains the LP relaxation's feasible set, guaranteeing τ_tighter,cs¹ ≥ τ_LP for any affine objective. Correlative sparsity is exploited via the BNN's cascading layer structure, decomposing the problem into small overlapping variable subsets (each of size n_i + 1 or n_i + n_{i+1}) so that the first-order sparse SDP involves O(p·ρ²) constraints rather than O(n²). The relaxation is modeled with TSSOS and solved via Mosek's interior-point solver, with interval arithmetic providing rigorous error bounds that avoid the floating-point sign-flipping issues plaguing MILP solvers.

## Results

On MNIST with BNN1 [784,500,500,10] (ws=34.34%), the tightened first-order sparse SDP relaxation (τ_tighter,cs^1) certifies robustness for 91/100 images at δ_∞=0.25, 60/100 at δ_∞=0.50, and 21/100 at δ_∞=1.00, compared to only 83, 31, and 1 for the LP relaxation. The method achieves an average 4.5× speedup over Soft-MILP for L∞ verification and 11.4× for L2 verification, with speedups exceeding 50× for severe attacks. The SDP bounds are on average 15.75% (BNN1) and 25.67% (BNN2) less conservative than LP bounds, and up to 55% more accurate in the worst cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines: (1) the LP relaxation derived from the linear sign-encoding of Amir et al. (2021), (2) the MILP formulation of Khalil et al. (2019) with big-M constraints, and (3) the MILP/MINP approach of Lazarus & Kochenderfer (2022). Against the LP relaxation, the SDP method is strictly superior: it certifies more instances across all perturbation sizes (e.g., 91 vs. 83 at δ_∞=0.25 for BNN1, and 59 vs. 14 at δ_∞=0.25 for BNN2) and provides bounds that are 15–55% tighter. Against the exact MILP/MINP solvers, the SDP method trades a small number of certifications on small perturbation regions (where MILP solves quickly) for dramatic speedups on larger regions: at δ_∞=1.00, the SDP method certifies 21 instances in ~11s while Soft-MILP certifies 65 total (15 non-robust + 50 robust) but takes 252s on average, and at δ_∞=1.50 the SDP method still certifies 6 instances in 38s while MILP times out on many. For L2 attacks, the advantage is even more pronounced: at δ_2=30, the SDP method certifies 13 instances in 34s while Soft-MINP certifies only 10 total in 556s. The paper also shows theoretically (Theorem 3.1) that the standard first-order SDP bound can be worse than LP, motivating the tautology-based tightening that recovers and surpasses LP quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Lasserre hierarchy of SDP relaxations for polynomial optimization to the BNN verification setting, building on the sparse (correlative sparsity) SDP framework of Magron & Wang (2023) and the TSSOS implementation of Wang et al. (2021a). It departs from the dominant BNN verification literature, which relies on SAT/SMT encodings (Narodytska et al., 2018; Jia & Rinard, 2020a; Amir et al., 2021) or MILP formulations (Khalil et al., 2019; Lazarus & Kochenderfer, 2022), by casting the problem as a QCQP and exploiting the semi-algebraic structure of the sign activation. The key methodological departure from prior SDP-based verification work (which targets full-precision ReLU networks) is the use of redundant quadratic tautologies to tighten the first-order relaxation, informed by the ReLU subgradient encoding of Chen et al. (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first SDP-based verification method for BNNs, offering a numerically robust alternative to MILP solvers that suffer from floating-point sign-flip errors near decision boundaries. The demonstrated speedups (4.5× for L∞, 11.4× for L2, up to 50× for severe attacks) and the ability to handle continuous input spaces with L2 perturbations (largely unstudied for BNNs) make this practically relevant for safety-critical edge deployments. However, the method inherits the scalability limitations of interior-point SDP solvers, and the experiments are limited to relatively small networks (784-500-500-10) on MNIST/CIFAR-10. A promising open direction, noted by the authors, is embedding these tighter SDP bounds into branch-and-bound solvers to accelerate exact verification for larger architectures.
