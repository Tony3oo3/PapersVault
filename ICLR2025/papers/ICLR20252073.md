## Problem

Unfolded GNNs (UGNNs) derive their layers from descent iterations of a graph-regularized energy, yielding interpretable inductive biases and resistance to oversmoothing, but scaling them to large graphs remains largely unaddressed. Existing UGNN scalability attempts (e.g., USP, LazyGNN) either lack convergence guarantees for the full training loop or fail empirically on the largest benchmarks, leaving a gap between the theoretical appeal of energy-based GNNs and their practical deployment on graphs exceeding 100M nodes.

## Input / Output

MuseGNN consumes a graph with node feature vectors and a fixed set of pre-sampled node-induced subgraphs (generated once via ShadowKHop offline sampling before training begins), plus auxiliary summary embeddings M that are maintained online across epochs. The forward pass produces per-subgraph node embeddings Y_s that, after K=8 unfolded layers, feed into a 3-layer MLP classifier to yield node class predictions; the same embeddings also serve as approximate minimizers of the sampling-based energy, giving them dual interpretability as both features and optimization solutions.

## Method

MuseGNN defines a sampling-based energy ℓ_muse(𝕐, M) that sums per-subgraph terms (base-model fidelity, graph-Laplacian smoothness, a γ-weighted penalty pulling each node's embedding toward a shared summary M, and a node-wise ReLU constraint) over a fixed set of offline-sampled subgraphs. GNN layers are derived as proximal gradient descent steps on this energy with alternating minimization: subgraph embeddings Y_s are updated via a graph-filtered affine transform followed by ReLU, while M is updated via an online mean estimator with forgetting factor ρ=0.9. The key design insight is that sampling is baked into the energy function itself rather than applied post-hoc, which (i) preserves a deterministic bilevel optimization structure amenable to convergence analysis, (ii) can be more expressive than the full-graph energy (demonstrated via WL-test counterexamples), and (iii) reduces to an unbiased estimator of the full-graph energy in the γ→∞ limit under uniform node sampling. The paper proves O(1/√t + e^{-Ck}) convergence for the full bilevel training loop under γ=0 and lower-level energy convergence for arbitrary γ≥0, with guarantees agnostic to the specific offline sampling operator.

## Results

MuseGNN achieves 66.82% test accuracy on ogbn-papers100M (111M nodes), 67.67% on MAG240M (244M nodes), and 61.74% on IGB-full (269M nodes, >1TB), setting new SOTA among homogeneous graph models on the two largest benchmarks. On medium-scale ogbn-products (2.4M nodes), MuseGNN reaches 81.23%, outperforming all scalable baselines. Training epoch time is comparable to standard neighbor-sampling GNNs (e.g., 158.85s vs. 164.14s for GAT(NS) on papers100M), while GAS-based and LazyGNN methods either OOM or run 40x slower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuseGNN outperforms GAT(NS) (the prior SOTA homogeneous model) on every dataset where both are reported: +0.54% on papers100M (66.82 vs 66.28), +0.52% on MAG240M (67.67 vs 67.15), and a substantial +6.23% on IGB-full (61.74 vs 55.51). It also beats FreshGNN by 0.60–5.24% across datasets and SAGE(NS) by 0.62–6.79%. Against the full-graph UGNN baseline, MuseGNN matches accuracy on small graphs (72.50 vs 72.74 on arxiv) while the FG model OOMs on all datasets ≥2.4M nodes. Compared to other UGNN-scaling methods, MuseGNN exceeds USP by 0.9% on arxiv and 7.4% on products, and outperforms LazyGNN by 27% on papers100M (66.82 vs 39.80) where LazyGNN's GAS-based staleness degrades severely. All GAS-based baselines (GCN/SAGE/GAT with GAS, LazyGNN) OOM on the two largest datasets, whereas MuseGNN trains within comparable epoch time to standard neighbor-sampling GNNs (158.85s vs 164.14s for GAT(NS) on papers100M).

## Relation to Prior Work

MuseGNN extends the unfolded GNN (UGNN) framework, where GNN layers are derived as descent steps of a graph-regularized energy function (Yang et al., 2021), by embedding offline subgraph sampling directly into the energy function design rather than applying it as a post-hoc approximation. This distinguishes it from USP (Li et al., 2022), which approximates the full-graph energy via partitioned subgraphs, and from LazyGNN (Xue et al., 2023), which reuses historical embeddings for scaling. The expressiveness analysis draws on Frasca et al. (2022) to show that the resulting subgraph-based energy can actually be more expressive than its full-graph counterpart, a departure from the typical view of sampling as a lossy approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MuseGNN is the first unfolded GNN validated on the largest publicly available graph benchmarks (>1TB, IGB-full and MAG240M), achieving SOTA among homogeneous graph models while retaining interpretable, energy-minimizing layers and convergence guarantees. The convergence analysis covers the full bilevel optimization process (upper-level training and lower-level energy descent) under the γ=0 setting, though for γ>0 only lower-level convergence is formally established and the online mean update for M remains an approximation. Open questions include whether the convergence guarantees extend to the general γ>0 bilevel case, how sensitive performance is to the choice of offline sampling operator, and whether the expressiveness advantage over full-graph UGNNs translates to consistent gains beyond the WL-equivalent graph examples.
