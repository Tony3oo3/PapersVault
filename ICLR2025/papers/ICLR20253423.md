## Problem

The Ensemble Score Filter (EnSF) achieves strong results for high-dimensional nonlinear data assimilation but fails when observations are sparse, because the log-likelihood gradient vanishes in unobserved dimensions, preventing information from propagating to the full state. This is a critical limitation since real-world observations (e.g., weather stations, ocean buoys) are typically sparse in both space and time, making standard EnSF impractical for most applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253423/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an ensemble of prior states (e.g., 100 samples), sparse observations on a heavily reduced grid (e.g., 10×10 from a 150×150 domain for 225× spatial reduction, or 18×9 from 144×72 for 64× reduction in weather), and a forward dynamical model (PDE solver or ML model like FourCastNet). It produces an ensemble of posterior states in the full state space. Observations arrive at sparse temporal intervals (every 5–50 steps), and the latent dimension (e.g., 400 for shallow water, 20736 for ERA5) is orders of magnitude smaller than the full state dimension, which is what enables the computational speedup.

## Method

Latent-EnSF introduces a coupled VAE with two encoders (a state encoder and an observation encoder) sharing a single decoder, trained with reconstruction loss, a latent-matching term that forces both encoders to produce consistent representations, and KL regularization toward N(0,I). Data assimilation is then performed entirely in the latent space using EnSF: the latent observation map is approximated as an identity (ζ_t ≈ ξ_t + γ̂_t), which eliminates the vanishing-gradient problem of sparse observations and allows the posterior score to be computed analytically without automatic differentiation. The reverse-time SDE is solved in the low-dimensional latent space (2r ≪ d), and the resulting latent posterior ensemble is decoded back to full states. This design simultaneously exploits the expressivity of latent score-based generative models, the consistency guarantee of the coupled VAE, and the sampling efficiency of EnSF, yielding faster convergence and higher accuracy than EnKF, LETKF, or full-space EnSF under extreme sparsity.

## Results

On the shallow water equations (SWE) with 225× spatial sparsity, Latent-EnSF achieves relative RMSE comparable to EnSF with full observations, while standard EnSF fails to reduce error over time. On ERA5 weather forecasting with 64× sparsity, Latent-EnSF quickly stabilizes RMSE within the first couple of days and maintains accuracy over 41-day forecasts, whereas EnSF tracks the no-assimilation FourCastNet error. In terms of computational efficiency, Latent-EnSF is the fastest method at 0.051 s/step (SWE, latent dim 400) and 0.198 s/step (ERA5, latent dim 20736), compared to 41.161 s for Latent-EnKF and 7.034 s for Latent-LETKF on ERA5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EnSF (Bao et al., 2024b), Latent-EnSF decisively wins on sparse observations: with 225× data reduction on SWE, EnSF's RMSE does not decrease over time even with dense temporal assimilation, while Latent-EnSF achieves errors at the level of EnSF with full observations. Against Latent-EnKF (Evensen, 1994), Latent-EnSF achieves lower RMSE and is faster in both experiments (0.051 s vs. 0.056 s on SWE; 0.198 s vs. 41.161 s on ERA5, a >200× speedup). Against Latent-LETKF (Hunt et al., 2007), Latent-EnSF is more robust to temporal sparsity (observations every 50 steps), converges faster, and is ~35× faster on ERA5 (0.198 s vs. 7.034 s). Against the no-assimilation FourCastNet baseline (Pathak et al., 2022) on ERA5, Latent-EnSF maintains stable accuracy over 41 days while FourCastNet's error grows monotonically, demonstrating that data assimilation effectively counteracts accumulated ML model drift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Ensemble Score Filter (EnSF, Bao et al., 2024b), which uses score-based diffusion models to sample from the posterior in Bayesian filtering, but identifies and addresses EnSF's critical failure mode under sparse observations where the log-likelihood gradient vanishes in unobserved dimensions. The key architectural departure is a coupled VAE with separate state and observation encoders trained with a latent-matching loss, drawing on latent diffusion model ideas (Rombach et al., 2022; Vahdat et al., 2021) and the Generalized Latent Assimilation framework (Cheng et al., 2023), but adding the explicit consistency constraint between the two latent representations. Unlike EnKF/LETKF variants that assume Gaussianity or require localization, Latent-EnSF retains the non-Gaussian, non-linear flexibility of score-based sampling while operating in a compressed latent space where the observation map can be approximated as identity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses the practical bottleneck that limits EnSF's real-world applicability: most operational data assimilation (weather, oceanography) involves observations that are extremely sparse in both space and time, precisely the regime where EnSF's likelihood gradient vanishes. The demonstrated 225× spatial sparsity tolerance and robustness to temporal sparsity (every 50 steps) make this relevant for operational forecasting systems. Open questions include whether the coupled VAE can be trained online or with limited data, whether the identity-map approximation for the latent observation holds for unstructured or stochastic observation patterns, and whether integrating latent dynamics (as in the follow-up LD-EnSF) can further reduce computational cost. The requirement to pre-train the VAE on a representative dataset remains a practical constraint for novel or shifting systems.
