## Problem

Existing adversarial defenses against diffusion-based image editing (e.g., PhotoGuard, AdvDM) fail in realistic scenarios where an adversary freely chooses a mask around a sensitive region (e.g., a face), because only the noise intersecting that mask affects the model. Prior methods either perturb the entire image or optimize against a single fixed mask, leaving them vulnerable to small mask-shape deviations at test time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250973/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image and a binary mask M_tr delineating the sensitive region (e.g., a face or object) as input, and produces an ℓ∞-bounded adversarial perturbation δ (‖δ‖∞ ≤ η, typically 16/255) that is added only within the mask region. The protected image x_src + δ is then published; at test time, an attacker feeds it to an inpainting model with a potentially different mask M_te and arbitrary text prompt. Optimization uses a single forward pass through the noise-prediction network at the initial denoising step T, making it far cheaper than multi-step or full-denoising objectives.

## Method

DiffusionGuard exploits a key behavioral difference between standard T2I and inpainting diffusion models: inpainting models produce fine details (e.g., facial features) from the very first denoising step due to the reconstruction loss used during fine-tuning. The proposed early-stage perturbation loss (Eq. 4) maximizes the ℓ₂ norm of the predicted noise at only the initial step T, requiring a single forward pass and proving more effective than full-process reconstruction losses or image-space target losses. To handle mask variation at test time, a contour-shrinking mask augmentation (Algorithm 1) stochastically samples inward-offset, Gaussian-smoothed variants of M_tr during PGD optimization, yielding a perturbation robust to diverse mask shapes. The full objective (Eq. 5) averages the early-stage noise-norm loss over the augmented mask set, and δ is updated via projected gradient descent with sign-gradient steps under the ℓ∞ constraint.

## Results

On InpaintGuardBench (42 images × 5 masks × 10 prompts = 2,100 edit tasks), DiffusionGuard achieves the strongest protection across all four metrics (PSNR, CLIP Directional Similarity, ImageReward, CLIP Similarity) in both Seen and Unseen mask sets. On the critical Unseen set (4 handcrafted masks), DiffusionGuard reduces CLIP Directional Similarity to 21.84 (Δ−2.45 from unprotected), compared to PhotoGuard's 23.30 (Δ−0.99) and mask-free baselines (AdvDM, Mist, SDS(-)) which remain near unprotected levels (~24). On the Seen set, DiffusionGuard achieves CLIP Dir. Sim. of 18.95 (Δ−5.45), outperforming PhotoGuard (21.17, Δ−3.23). In human evaluation, DiffusionGuard achieves approximately 17% higher win rate over PhotoGuard in both Seen and Unseen groups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffusionGuard outperforms all four baselines on every metric in both Seen and Unseen mask sets. Against PhotoGuard (Salman et al., 2024), the primary mask-aware baseline, DiffusionGuard achieves a 1.46-point lower CLIP Directional Similarity on Unseen masks (21.84 vs. 23.30) and a 2.22-point gap on Seen masks (18.95 vs. 21.17), indicating substantially stronger disruption of the editing process. The three mask-free baselines—AdvDM (Liang et al., 2023), Mist (Liang & Wu, 2023), and SDS(−) (Xue et al., 2024)—are essentially ineffective on Unseen masks, with CLIP Directional Similarity values (24.27, 23.93, 23.85) nearly identical to the unprotected baseline (24.29), confirming that global perturbations without mask awareness cannot protect against adversarial mask selection. DiffusionGuard also demonstrates superior computational efficiency: at 11 seconds of PGD optimization it matches PhotoGuard's 90-second performance, and under a tighter noise budget of 6/255 it achieves protection comparable to PhotoGuard at 16/255.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of adversarial defense against diffusion-based image editing (AdvDM, Mist, SDS(-), PhotoGuard) by identifying a unique behavioral property of inpainting models: they generate fine details (e.g., facial features) at the very first denoising step, in contrast to standard diffusion models that produce coarse-to-fine structure. This observation motivates a novel early-stage perturbation objective that targets only the initial noise prediction, making adversarial noise generation both more effective and ~8× more compute-efficient than prior full-process or image-space losses. The mask augmentation technique and InpaintGuardBench benchmark further distinguish this work by addressing the practically critical but previously unexamined challenge of mask-shape robustness in inpainting-specific defense.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As text-guided inpainting tools (DALL-E, Stable Diffusion web UI) become widely accessible, protecting personal images from unauthorized manipulation is an increasingly urgent practical need. The mask-robustness dimension is essential for real deployment since adversaries can freely choose mask shapes, and the 8× compute reduction makes large-scale protection feasible. Open questions remain: the method assumes defender and attacker share a common understanding of the sensitive region, it has been validated primarily on Stable Diffusion Inpainting (with limited transfer to SD 2.0), and more sophisticated purification or adaptive attacks could still degrade protection. The benchmark and methodology provide a foundation for future work on robust image protection against evolving generative editing capabilities.
