## Problem

Geometric tempering is widely used to sample from multimodal distributions by interpolating between a proposal and target, yet no convergence guarantees existed for tempered Langevin dynamics, and the behavior of functional inequalities along the geometric path was completely uncharacterized. This paper asks whether geometric tempering actually helps or hurts convergence, and under what conditions it can be provably beneficial or harmful.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250200/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a proposal distribution ν (typically Gaussian), a target distribution π known only up to normalization, and a tempering schedule λ: R+ → [0,1] that is non-decreasing and weakly differentiable. It produces upper bounds on KL(p_t, π) in both continuous and discrete time that decompose into three terms governed by the log-Sobolev constants α_t of intermediate distributions μ_t ∝ ν^(1−λt) π^λt, the tempering speed λ̇_t, and the distance (1−λ_t) from the current tempered distribution to the target. In the strongly log-concave regime, it also yields a closed-form optimal schedule λ*(s) that is independent of the time horizon, and lower bounds in TV showing exponential slow convergence for specific bimodal and unimodal targets.

## Method

The paper establishes the first convergence analysis of tempered Langevin dynamics by tracking how the sampling law p_t follows the moving target μ_t via the Fokker-Planck equation, then bounding the gap between μ_t and the final target π. The continuous-time upper bound (Theorem 1) decomposes KL(p_t, π) into an exponential contraction term driven by ∫α_s ds, a residual term A(1−λ_t), and a tempering-speed term involving λ̇_s weighted by the geometry of intermediate distributions; the discrete-time version (Theorem 3) adds a discretization bias term. A key negative result (Theorem 4) shows that even when both ν and π have polynomial log-Sobolev constants, the geometric mean path can have exponentially large Poincaré constants due to emergent multimodality. In the strongly log-concave setting, the paper derives the optimal tempering schedule (Proposition 6) by minimizing the upper bound, showing it reduces to vanilla Langevin when the target is sufficiently well-conditioned (α_π ≥ α_ν/2) but provides a strict improvement for flatter targets. Finally, lower bounds (Theorems 8–9) prove that for a bimodal Gaussian mixture target, total time must be exponential in mode separation, and surprisingly, even a unimodal target with polynomial log-Sobolev constant can suffer exponentially slow tempered convergence.

## Results

The paper establishes the first KL convergence bounds for tempered Langevin dynamics in continuous time (Theorem 1) and discrete time (Theorem 3), with rates governed by the log-Sobolev constants α_t of intermediate distributions along the geometric path. A key negative result (Theorem 4) shows that even when both proposal ν=N(0,1) and target π have C_LS ≤ 81m⁵, the geometric tempering path can have Poincaré constant C_P(μ_λ) ≥ (1/(4·10⁴m))·e^{m²(1−λ)/100} − m², i.e., exponentially worse than either endpoint. Lower bounds (Theorems 8, 9) prove that total Langevin time must be exponential in mode separation m for convergence in TV, and that this failure occurs even for a unimodal target with C_LS(π) ≤ 324m³.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between tempered Langevin and vanilla Langevin (λ≡1), which is recovered as a special case of the upper bounds (Remark 2). In the strongly log-concave setting (Proposition 6), when the target is sufficiently well-conditioned (α_π ≥ α_ν/2), vanilla Langevin is provably optimal and tempering provides no benefit. When the target is flatter (α_π < α_ν/2), the closed-form optimal schedule (Eq. 15) improves over vanilla Langevin at short time horizons, but this advantage is lost as the horizon grows (Figure 2); the linear schedule λ(s)=s/t shows a similar pattern. Most strikingly, Theorems 8 and 9 demonstrate that geometric tempering with a Gaussian proposal can be exponentially worse than vanilla Langevin for both a bimodal Gaussian mixture target and a unimodal target with reasonable functional inequalities, revealing that tempering can be actively harmful rather than helpful for convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis framework of Vempala and Wibisono (2019) for standard Langevin dynamics to the setting of a moving target, specifically the geometric tempering path introduced by Gelman and Meng (1998). It departs from prior tempered MCMC analyses (Tang et al. 2024 for simulated annealing, Lee et al. 2022 for convolution paths, Guo et al. 2024 for general paths) by providing the first convergence bounds specifically for the geometric sequence with Langevin dynamics, and from the parallel tempering literature (Woodard et al., Madras & Zheng) by analyzing sequential rather than simultaneous temperature sampling. The paper also builds on the functional inequality machinery (log-Sobolev, Poincaré) that underpins modern Langevin convergence theory, but applies it to the novel question of how these constants behave along the geometric interpolation path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most impactful contribution is its negative results: it rigorously demonstrates that geometric tempering can exponentially worsen functional inequalities (Theorem 4) and lead to exponentially slow convergence even for well-conditioned unimodal targets (Theorem 9), challenging the widespread heuristic that tempering always helps with multimodal sampling. The optimal schedule derived in Proposition 6 provides the first closed-form guidance for temperature selection in the strongly log-concave regime. Open questions include a complete characterization of log-Sobolev constants along the geometric path (particularly for uniform proposals), whether alternative interpolation paths avoid the identified pathologies, and whether the negative results extend to higher dimensions or more realistic targets.
