## Problem

CLIP models suffer from severe fine-grained visual perception deficits (orientation, quantity, color, structure) due to their contrastive training paradigm emphasizing high-level semantics and the inherently short, detail-poor text in training pairs. Existing remedies either still require image-text pairs or bolt on additional vision encoders (e.g., DINOv2), failing to fundamentally fix CLIP's visual encoder while adding computational overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252676/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DIVA consumes only RGB images (no paired text) at resolutions matching the target CLIP model (224², 336², 378², or 384²) and produces an enhanced CLIP visual encoder whose class and patch token embeddings encode richer fine-grained visual detail. During training, the CLIP visual encoder's output is projected through a vision-language projection layer and concatenated with empty-text embeddings to form the diffusion condition; the diffusion model itself remains frozen. At inference, the enhanced CLIP encoder simply outputs improved visual features for downstream tasks without any diffusion model in the loop.

## Method

DIVA conditions a frozen pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (SD-2-1-base) on CLIP's visual features and optimizes only CLIP's visual encoder to minimize the diffusion noise-prediction loss, effectively using generative reconstruction as a self-supervised signal. The key design is the 'visual dense recap' scheme: rather than using only the class token (too little information, making reconstruction trivially hard) or all patch tokens (too much, making it trivially easy), a calibrated fraction of local patch tokens is randomly included alongside the class token to set an intermediate reconstruction difficulty that forces CLIP to learn more detailed representations. Training runs for ~4600 steps on Conceptual-3M images with N=2 random diffusion timesteps sampled per image, using SGD at lr=1e-4 on 8 A100 GPUs. The approach is model-agnostic across CLIP variants (OpenAI, MetaCLIP, SigLIP, DFN) with per-model density tuning, and preserves zero-shot classification and retrieval performance while yielding 3–7% gains on the MMVP-VLM fine-grained visual benchmark.

## Results

DIVA improves CLIP models' fine-grained visual perception on the MMVP-VLM benchmark by 3–7% across all tested variants, with the largest gains on OpenAI ViT-L-14/224 (19.3→25.9, +6.6) and MetaCLIP ViT-H-14/224 (25.2→31.9, +6.7). When plugged into LLaVA-1.5, the enhanced CLIP backbone boosts MMVP scores from 24.7→31.3 (7B) and 30.7→35.3 (13B), while also improving MMBench and LLaVA-Bench-in-the-Wild. Zero-shot generalization on 27 image classification benchmarks and Flickr30K/COCO retrieval is largely preserved (e.g., ImageNet-1K stays at 75.5% for OpenAI ViT-L-14).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other CLIP-enhancement methods; instead, it evaluates each CLIP variant (OpenAI ViT-L-14, MetaCLIP ViT-L/H-14, SigLIP ViT-SO-14, DFN ViT-H-14) against its own DIVA-enhanced counterpart on the MMVP-VLM benchmark. DIVA consistently outperforms the original model across all 8 model-resolution combinations, with gains ranging from +1.5 (SigLIP ViT-SO-14/384) to +6.7 (MetaCLIP ViT-H-14/224). The already-strong DFN ViT-H-14/224 still benefits by +4.4, confirming the method's generality. On the MLLM side, replacing LLaVA-1.5's CLIP encoder with the DIVA-enhanced version improves MMVP by +6.6 (7B) and +4.6 (13B), while MME scores remain essentially unchanged (1510.7→1500.6 for 7B), indicating the gains come from visual perception rather than language understanding. Zero-shot classification and retrieval results confirm no meaningful degradation in generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of CLIP post-training and diffusion-based representation learning. Prior efforts to improve CLIP have relied on better pre-training data/architectures (EVA-CLIP, DFN, SigLIP) or fine-tuning with image-text pairs, while recent work addressing CLIP's visual shortcomings (Tong et al., 2024b) has typically bolted on additional vision encoders like DINOv2 to MLLMs. DIVA departs from both lines by using a frozen text-to-image diffusion model's reconstruction loss as a self-supervised signal to directly optimize CLIP's visual encoder with image-only data, drawing conceptual inspiration from Diffusion-TTA (Prabhudesai et al., 2023) but repurposing generative feedback for representation refinement rather than test-time adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIVA offers a resource-efficient (~8.3 GPU-hours) post-training recipe that addresses a fundamental limitation of CLIP affecting all downstream MLLMs, without requiring expensive image-text pair collection or additional vision encoders. The ablation finding that adding text captions to the diffusion condition actually degrades performance is a useful negative result, confirming that the reconstruction difficulty is the key driver of representation improvement. Open questions include whether scaling data and model capacity yields further gains, whether the approach extends to video/audio modalities, and whether purely visual constraints can be used to refine CLIP's semantic alignment more broadly.
