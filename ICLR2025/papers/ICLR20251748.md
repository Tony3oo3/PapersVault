## Problem

Protein fitness prediction in low-data settings is hampered by the scarcity of in vitro annotations for specific tasks. When no task-specific data is available, existing methods must assume that fitness is solely a function of protein sequence likelihood, and when limited data exists, learning the correct regression mapping is difficult. Although data from many related fitness prediction tasks (e.g., deep mutational scans) is available, no prior method meta-learns across these tasks to learn the PLM-embedding-to-fitness relationship without restrictive assumptions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes protein sequences from both a support set (with fitness labels) and a query set (unlabeled), which are converted to per-residue embeddings via the third layer of ESM2-8M; support-set fitness scores are projected to the 768-dim embedding space via a learned linear layer, while query-set fitness embeddings share a single learned vector. Optionally, zero-shot predictions from an auxiliary PLM (ESM-IF1) can be concatenated as additional context. The output is a scalar value per query protein, used to compute pairwise preferences for ranking; evaluation uses Spearman rank correlation over up to 2,000 query points per task, with query batches of size 100.

## Method

Metalic meta-trains a ProteinNPT-style axial attention model over a distribution of protein fitness tasks using a preference-based pairwise loss, learning how to condition on in-context support-set fitness information and query-set sequences to rank proteins by fitness. At test time, the model first fine-tunes on the support set by subsampling it into smaller support/query pairs (to avoid memorization, since labels are also passed in-context), then conditions on the full support set for prediction. The key insight is that in-context meta-learning provides a strong initialization for subsequent fine-tuning without requiring expensive meta-gradients to account for the fine-tuning step during meta-training, yielding a linear reduction in compute (O(m) vs O(mn) gradient computations) while outperforming gradient-based alternatives like Reptile. The axial attention architecture reduces self-attention complexity from O(K²L²) to O(K²+L²), where K is the shot and L is protein length, making in-context conditioning over multiple proteins tractable.

## Results

On the ProteinGym benchmark (single-mutant tasks), Metalic achieves a Spearman correlation of .482±.002 in the zero-shot setting, setting a new state-of-the-art and outperforming all reported baselines. In the few-shot setting, Metalic reaches .484±.001 (16-shot) and .552±.009 (128-shot) Spearman correlation, matching or exceeding all baselines while using 18× fewer parameters than the strongest competitors. The underlying ESM2-8M PLM alone achieves only .121 ρ zero-shot, demonstrating that the meta-learning procedure contributes the vast majority of the performance gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Metalic outperforms ESM1-v-650M (650M parameters) by a wide margin in the zero-shot (.482 vs .384) and 16-shot (.484 vs .452) settings, while essentially tying at 128-shot (.552 vs .553) with 18× fewer parameters. Against PoET, which leverages multi-sequence alignment and in-context learning, Metalic wins at 0-shot (.482 vs .416) and 16-shot (.484 vs .475) but loses at 128-shot (.552 vs .588). Metalic dominates the smaller ESM2-8M baseline by 3.8× in zero-shot (.482 vs .105) and the ProteinNPT architecture by 2.5× at 16-shot (.484 vs .192), confirming that the meta-learning procedure—not the backbone or architecture—drives performance. In the multi-mutant zero-shot setting, Metalic-AuxIF (.533) is competitive but trails ESM-IF1 (.590), though it recovers most of that gap by incorporating ESM-IF1's predictions as auxiliary input. Against the gradient-based meta-learning method Reptile, Metalic (50k steps) achieves .552 vs Reptile-3-100's .539 and Reptile-3-3's .499 at 128-shot, while requiring 3× fewer gradient computations, demonstrating that accounting for fine-tuning during meta-training is an unnecessary computational burden.

## Relation to Prior Work

Metalic builds on the ProteinNPT axial-attention architecture for in-context protein fitness prediction and leverages ESM2-8M embeddings, but departs from prior in-context PLMs (PoET, Tranception, ProteinNPT) by adding a meta-learning phase over a distribution of ProteinGym fitness tasks to learn how to use context for fitness prediction rather than assuming likelihood correlates with fitness. It also departs from gradient-based meta-learning methods (MAML, Reptile) that compute expensive meta-gradients to account for fine-tuning during meta-training; instead, Metalic performs in-context meta-learning and then fine-tunes at test time without modeling that fine-tuning in the outer loop. The combination of in-context meta-learning followed by fine-tuning on the same context, without higher-order gradients, is the paper's core methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Metalic sets a new SOTA for zero-shot protein fitness prediction on ProteinGym and achieves strong few-shot results with 18× fewer parameters than prior SOTA, making protein fitness prediction more accessible in data-scarce regimes that are common in practice. The finding that in-context meta-learning alone provides a strong initialization for fine-tuning—without the computational overhead of meta-gradients—offers a practical recipe for combining meta-learning with test-time adaptation in other low-data domains. A key limitation is that Metalic does not reach SOTA on multi-mutant proteins due to limited training data, though performance scales with additional tasks, suggesting a clear path forward. Future work on using Metalic as an auto-regressive world model for protein design could extend its impact beyond prediction into active design.
