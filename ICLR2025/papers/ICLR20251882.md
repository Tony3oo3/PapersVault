## Problem

Learning long-term behavior in dissipative chaotic systems is hindered by positive Lyapunov exponents that cause exponential error accumulation in autoregressive models. Existing approaches that preserve invariant measures require explicit knowledge of the invariant distribution, which is typically unavailable for arbitrary datasets. The paper asks: can one capture both the transient contraction toward attractors and the long-term ergodic exploration of the invariant set without assuming the invariant measure is known?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251882/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes trajectory data as single-step pairs (z_k, z_{k+1}) from chaotic systems of varying dimensionality (3D Lorenz 63, 9/40/80D Lorenz 96, 128D Kuramoto-Sivashinsky, 64×64 spatial fields for Navier-Stokes). Trajectories are split at an estimated relaxation time k (derived from a log-Sobolev bound) into a contraction-phase dataset and a measure-invariant-phase dataset, each shuffled into independent training pairs. Outputs include autoregressive state predictions, long-term statistical summaries (KLD, MMD, TKE estimated from roll-out samples), and the learned finite-rank contraction and unitary operators in the latent feature space.

## Method

PFNN employs an autoencoder to project the state space into a finite-dimensional latent feature space where the Koopman operator acts linearly, then learns two distinct operators tailored to the two physical phases of dissipative chaos. For the contraction phase (data before relaxation time k), a linear operator G_c is trained with a ReLU penalty enforcing that eigenvalues of G_c^T G_c do not exceed 1, encoding volume contraction toward the attractor. For the measure-invariant phase (data after k), a unitary operator G_m is trained via a bidirectional loss (forward prediction plus backward prediction via G_m*) combined with a Frobenius-norm consistency constraint G_m* G_m = I and G_m G_m* = I, ensuring the learned dynamics preserve the invariant measure without ever specifying it explicitly. The relaxation time k is estimated empirically from the log-Sobolev inequality, and the two operators are trained sequentially on their respective data subsets while jointly updating the autoencoder parameters.

## Results

PFNN achieves the best long-term statistical accuracy (KLD and MMD) across all four chaotic systems, with particularly dramatic gains on the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation where KLD is 9.37 vs. 142.73 for FNO and 24.53 for MNO. On short-term prediction (NRMSE over 10 steps), PFNN is competitive with MNO, achieving 0.25±0.05 on KS and 0.32±0.09 on Lorenz 96 (m=80), though FNO slightly edges it on Lorenz 96 (m=40) at 0.09 vs. 0.11. For the Navier-Stokes Kolmogorov flow, PFNN attains the lowest TKE error (1.36) compared to MNO (1.58), FNO (2.10), and LSTM (2.30). Overall, PFNN wins or ties on KLD and MMD in every setting, and wins NRMSE in 5 of 6 systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFNN outperforms all four baselines on long-term distributional metrics (KLD, MMD) across every system tested. Against FNO, the gap is most dramatic on KS where PFNN's KLD of 9.37 is 15× lower than FNO's 142.73, attributed to FNO's integer Fourier modes producing spurious periodicity that disrupts ergodicity. Against MNO, PFNN consistently achieves lower KLD (e.g., 0.29 vs. 0.37 on Lorenz 63; 9.37 vs. 24.53 on KS) because MNO's fixed absorbing-ball constraint imposes a rigid dissipation rate that conflicts with the true invariant measure. On short-term NRMSE, PFNN wins in 5 of 6 systems but is slightly behind FNO on Lorenz 96 (m=40) (0.11 vs. 0.09) and MNO on Lorenz 63 (0.49 vs. 0.44). LSTM and Koopman baselines are consistently the weakest, with LSTM producing infinite KLD on three systems due to complete trajectory divergence, confirming that standard sequence models cannot capture the invariant statistics of dissipative chaos.

## Relation to Prior Work

PFNN extends the deep Koopman learning paradigm (Lusch et al. 2018; Kutz et al. 2018), which pairs an autoencoder with a finite-rank linear operator, by decomposing the learned operator into two physically-motivated stages: a contraction operator for the transient phase and a unitary operator for the ergodic phase on the attractor. This departs from general-purpose Koopman methods that treat the operator uniformly, and from the Markov Neural Operator (Li et al. 2022), which enforces a fixed 'absorbing ball' rather than a spectral contraction constraint. Crucially, unlike recent invariant-measure-matching approaches (Jiang et al. 2024; Schiff et al. 2024) that require either known or estimated invariant distributions, PFNN achieves long-term statistical fidelity through simple one-step forward learning regularized by contraction and unitarity constraints alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, physically interpretable framework for long-term chaotic forecasting that avoids the practical bottleneck of estimating invariant measures from data—a significant limitation of prior distribution-matching methods. The two-stage decomposition (contraction → unitary) is elegant and directly tied to the Lumer-Phillips and Koopman-von Neumann theorems, giving practitioners clear diagnostic tools (eigenvalue placement on the unit circle) to validate learned dynamics. Key open questions remain: the ergodicity assumption excludes non-ergodic chaotic systems, the bidirectional training is computationally expensive due to Frobenius norm calculations, and the method currently requires discretized-grid inputs. Extending to non-measure-preserving systems and discretization-agnostic architectures are natural next steps.
