## Problem

Generating text that satisfies syntactic or semantic constraints from a language model is naturally a probabilistic conditioning problem, but exact sampling from the resulting product-of-experts posterior is intractable. Existing approaches each suffer from a critical weakness: locally constrained decoding requires per-token evaluation of all constraints and introduces greedy approximations that distort the target distribution, while sample-rerank methods cannot exploit information available incrementally during generation, wasting computation on unpromising paths.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language prompt, a base autoregressive LM (e.g., Llama 3.1 8B or 70B), and a programmable set of potential functions partitioned into efficient potentials (evaluated per-token for grammar-constrained proposals, e.g., CFG prefix checks) and expensive potentials (evaluated at coarser semantic units, e.g., executing partial Python on test cases, simulating STRIPS plans, or validating SMILES prefixes). It outputs a set of N importance-weighted particles approximating the global product-of-experts posterior, from which posterior-weighted accuracy or best-of-N selections can be made. The framework supports semantically meaningful increments (e.g., lines of code rather than tokens) for better particle alignment during resampling.

## Method

The paper develops an SMC architecture that iteratively extends, reweights, and resamples particles to approximate the global product of experts between the LM prior and a set of domain-specific potentials. At each step, particles are extended using a locally constrained (grammar-masked) proposal; importance weights are updated with two correction factors: a normalization term L_eff that penalizes the greediness of local decoding, and a ratio of expensive potentials that incorporates signals too costly for per-token logit masking. Multinomial resampling then adaptively reallocates computation toward higher-weighted (more promising) partial sequences, abandoning dead-end paths early. The approach is fully programmable—users can plug in arbitrary efficient or expensive potentials and proposals via a probabilistic programming interface—avoiding the need for problem-specific fine-tuning of learned twist functions. With only N=10 particles and minimal per-token overhead (~30 ms for expensive potentials), the method allows small open-source LMs to outperform models over 8× larger and fine-tuned closed-source models across four diverse domains.

## Results

Full SMC achieves the best posterior-weighted accuracy across all four domains: 0.419 on Goal Inference (Planetarium), 0.577 on Molecular Synthesis (GDB-17), 0.407 on Data Science (DS-1000), and 0.620 on Text-to-SQL (Spider), using N=10 particles. With Llama 3.1 70B, Full SMC outperforms Codex-002 (175B, fine-tuned for code) on the Data Science task. The method also matches or exceeds Full IS accuracy with 5–10× fewer particles, demonstrating that incremental resampling makes computation significantly more efficient.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SMC Steering (Lew et al., 2023), which uses without-replacement resampling with N=5 particles and beam size 3 (effective N=15), Full SMC with multinomial resampling and N=10 particles achieves 0.620 vs. 0.607 on Text-to-SQL, a modest but consistent win despite having fewer effective particles. Against Codex-002 (175B parameters, fine-tuned for code generation; Lai et al., 2023), Full SMC with Llama 3.1 70B outperforms it on the DS-1000 Data Science benchmark, demonstrating that the SMC framework can compensate for a ~2.5× parameter gap. Internally, Full SMC dominates all ablation baselines: it outperforms Sample-Rerank (which applies expensive potentials only at the end) by 0.130 on Goal Inference, 0.185 on Molecular Synthesis, and 0.039 on Text-to-SQL, and outperforms Full IS (no resampling) by 0.161 on Goal Inference, 0.173 on Molecular Synthesis, and 0.061 on Data Science, confirming that incremental resampling is the key efficiency gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SMC-based LM steering framework of Lew et al. (2023) by specializing it to constrained semantic parsing with a unified treatment of both efficient (grammar-based) and expensive (execution, simulation) potentials. It departs from the dominant locally constrained decoding paradigm (Shin et al., 2021; Scholak et al., 2021; Willard & Louf, 2023) by showing that per-token logit masking introduces a greedy bias that can be corrected via importance weights, and from sample-rerank approaches (Nakano et al., 2021; Olausson et al., 2023) by incorporating constraint signals incrementally rather than only at sequence completion. Unlike learned-twist SMC methods (Zhao et al., 2024; Lawson et al., 2022), the framework emphasizes programmable, hand-crafted domain-specific potentials, avoiding costly problem-specific fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: with minimal overhead (~30 ms/token), the SMC framework lets small open-source LMs (8B) outperform models over 8× larger and fine-tuned closed-source models (Codex-002, 175B) across four diverse semantic parsing domains. The KL-divergence validation provides rare empirical evidence that the probabilistic framing (global product of experts) is not merely a theoretical convenience but directly drives downstream accuracy. Open questions include whether the approach scales to constraints requiring full-sequence evaluation (where incremental signals are unavailable), how it interacts with RLHF-tuned models whose base distributions already encode some constraints, and whether learned twist functions could complement the hand-crafted potentials in domains where domain knowledge is scarce.
