## Problem

Preferential Bayesian Optimization (PBO) requires expensive approximate posterior inference at every step due to the non-conjugate preferential likelihood, creating latency incompatible with real-time human interaction. While amortization has been applied to vanilla Bayesian Optimization, no end-to-end amortized method exists for the preferential setting, where the model must predict acquisition values for pairs of designs rather than single points.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251968/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, PABBO takes the optimization history (a sequence of past duels with winner/loser designs and binary preference labels), a set of S candidate query points sampled from a quasi-random Sobol sequence, and the current step t and budget T. It outputs acquisition function values for all O(S²) candidate pairs, from which a categorical policy (softmax) selects the next query pair. The query set size S scales with dimensionality (min(300, 100·D) in training), and the continuous search space is discretized into this fixed set of candidate locations.

## Method

PABBO uses a transformer-based conditional neural process with separate MLP embedders for the first design, second design, and preference label within each duel (combined by addition to preserve order information), followed by a 6-layer transformer with attention masking that restricts information flow between context, query, and prediction sets. Two decoder heads operate on the transformer outputs: an acquisition head that predicts values for candidate pairs (conditioned on t and T) to form a categorical policy, and a prediction head that parameterizes a Gaussian likelihood for an auxiliary binary cross-entropy loss on preference prediction. Training uses policy-gradient RL with discounted cumulative reward (γ=0.98) for the acquisition head, combined with the auxiliary BCE loss (λ=1), preceded by a 3000-iteration warm-up phase training only the prediction task to stabilize learning of the latent function shape before policy optimization begins.

## Results

On synthetic functions (Forrester, Branin, Beale, and GP draws), PABBO consistently achieves the lowest or second-lowest simple regret across all tasks, with an average 12-fold inference speedup over the fastest GP-based baseline. On the HPO-B benchmark (3 search spaces), PABBO ranks first on average in simple regret, closely followed by qNEI. On the out-of-distribution human preference datasets, PABBO achieves second place on Candy and third on Sushi, remaining competitive with qNEI and MPES despite being pre-trained only on synthetic GP samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PABBO is compared against five GP-based PBO baselines: qEUBO, qEI, qNEI, qTS, and MPES, plus a random strategy. On all synthetic functions (in-distribution GP draws and out-of-distribution Forrester/Branin/Beale), PABBO achieves the lowest simple regret, outperforming all GP baselines while being ~12× faster at inference. On HPO-B, PABBO ranks first overall with qNEI as the closest competitor; notably, all methods clearly beat the random strategy on these higher-dimensional problems. On the Candy and Sushi human preference datasets, PABBO is competitive but does not dominate: it ranks second on Candy (behind qNEI) and third on Sushi (behind qNEI and MPES), with high variance observed across all baselines. The random strategy occasionally outperforms individual GP baselines on low-dimensional synthetic tasks, highlighting that GP-based PBO methods can struggle in simple settings where PABBO's amortized approach has an advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

PABBO sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) Preferential Bayesian Optimization (Gonzalez et al., 2017), which relies on GP surrogates with expensive approximate inference for non-conjugate preferential likelihoods; (2) amortized Bayesian Optimization (Chen et al., 2017; Maraval et al., 2023; Müller et al., 2023), which pre-computes solutions via neural networks to enable fast inference; and (3) Transformer Neural Processes (Nguyen & Grover, 2022; Müller et al., 2022), which provide a permutation-invariant backbone well-suited to set-based optimization histories. The key departure is that prior amortized BO methods (PFN, NAP) operate on scalar function values, whereas PABBO is the first to handle pairwise preferential feedback end-to-end, requiring a novel architecture that encodes ordered pairs and predicts acquisition values for candidate pairs rather than single designs. The RL-based training with an auxiliary binary classification loss (rather than regression) is tailored to the preferential nature of the data, distinguishing it from Maraval et al.'s regression-based auxiliary loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orders-of-magnitude speedup (average 12×, up to several orders) directly addresses the practical bottleneck of GP-based PBO in human-in-the-loop settings where users cannot wait for iterative posterior inference. The demonstrated out-of-distribution generalization (Forrester, Branin, Beale functions not in pre-training) suggests the learned policy captures transferable optimization structure rather than memorizing specific functions. However, significant limitations remain: the model is dimension-specific (separate models needed for different dimensionalities), the query set size S scales with dimensionality increasing inference time, and large pre-training datasets are required. Open questions include developing dimension-agnostic architectures, incorporating explicit user models for noisy human comparisons, and understanding how pre-training dataset composition should be tailored for specific out-of-distribution applications.
