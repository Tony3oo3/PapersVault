## Problem

While language models can memorize vast factual knowledge, it remains unclear whether they can perform even single-step logical manipulation (classification, comparison, inverse search) on that stored knowledge during inference. Prior evaluations on internet-trained models cannot distinguish genuine knowledge manipulation from data contamination, making it impossible to determine if failures stem from insufficient training or inherent architectural limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253091/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives natural-language questions about attributes of synthetic individuals (e.g., 'Was Anya born in an even month?', 'Is Anya's university better than Sabrina's?') and must produce attribute values, yes/no judgments, or person identities. Critically, evaluation is out-of-distribution: the model has seen the target individual's biography during pre-training but never the specific QA pair, isolating true inference-time manipulation from memorized answers. The synthetic biographies cover up to 20 million individuals with controlled, knowledge-augmented descriptions to ensure clean extraction.

## Method

The paper constructs a controlled synthetic experiment: a transformer is pre-trained on augmented biographies of N individuals, then instruction-finetuned on QA pairs from a training subset, and evaluated on a held-out test subset where only biographies (not QAs) were seen. Four manipulation tasks are studied—retrieval, classification, comparison, and inverse search—each with varying CoT conditions during training and inference. The key finding is that without explicit chain-of-thought generation at both training and inference time, models fail at classification and comparison even with far more training samples than theoretically needed, while inverse search accuracy remains near 0% regardless of training method or model size. The authors further validate these limitations on modern LLMs (GPT-4, GPT-4o, Llama-3.1-405B) using analogous real-world knowledge questions, confirming the failures are not artifacts of the synthetic setting.

## Results

On the controlled synthetic biography benchmark, language models achieve virtually 0% accuracy on inverse knowledge search regardless of prompting strategy, model size, or training method. For knowledge comparison among 100 ranked options, accuracy is barely above random chance (~1%) even with 2,500,000 training samples. Binary knowledge classification (e.g., 'Was Anya born in an even month?') fails without explicit Chain-of-Thought generation, even when the model answers the underlying retrieval question (birth month) with 100% accuracy and is trained on 25,000 QA pairs. These failures persist in GPT-4o and Llama-3.1-405B as of October 2024.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates GPT-4/4o and Llama-3.1-405B on the same four knowledge manipulation tasks (retrieval, classification, comparison, inverse search) using both the synthetic biography data and real-world WikiBio examples. Both models fail on knowledge classification (e.g., determining if a birth year is odd), knowledge comparison (e.g., ranking two universities), and inverse search (e.g., identifying a person from their attributes), as shown in Figures 2, 3, 4, and 5. GPT-4 can succeed on classification and comparison only when explicitly prompted to generate a Chain-of-Thought that spells out intermediate attribute values before answering. Llama-3.1-405B exhibits the same failure pattern, confirming these limitations are not model-specific but inherent to the autoregressive generative paradigm. The paper positions these as Turing-test-style counterexamples: tasks solvable mentally by humans but requiring explicit CoT scaffolding for current LLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Allen-Zhu & Li (2024, ICML 2024) on knowledge storage and extraction by asking whether a model that has successfully memorized factual knowledge can also *manipulate* it (classify, compare, invert) during inference. It departs from the CoT prompting literature (Wei et al., 2022), which focused on complex in-context arithmetic, by showing that even a single-step manipulation of out-of-context stored knowledge (e.g., parity of a birth month) requires explicit CoT generation. The controlled synthetic biography framework eliminates the data-contamination confound that limits prior real-world QA evaluations (e.g., StrategyQA, Geva et al., 2021), and the inverse-search results connect to the Reversal Curse (Berglund et al., 2023) while showing the failure is more absolute than previously documented.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that GPT-4o and Llama-3.1-405B still fail at trivial knowledge classification and comparison without CoT, and that inverse search accuracy is near zero regardless of training, challenges the working assumption that sufficient scale and pretraining yield flexible knowledge use. The controlled synthetic setting provides a clean, reproducible testbed for future architectures and training paradigms to target. Open questions include whether novel training objectives (beyond CoT data augmentation), multi-token prediction, or architectural changes can fundamentally close the gap, and whether these limitations persist in the full paper's larger-scale experiments. The practical implication that LLMs cannot serve as queryable databases has immediate consequences for system design.
