## Problem

As language models tackle increasingly complex tasks, human supervision (both demonstrations and preference comparisons) becomes unreliable, yet the standard SFT+DPO post-training pipeline has not been studied under this dual-noise regime. The paper shows that DPO breaks down in this setting: it requires strong KL regularization to prevent overoptimization of unreliable comparisons, but this same regularization prevents the large model updates needed to correct errors inherited from unreliable SFT demonstrations, creating an irreconcilable tension.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250493/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained language model, an initial SFT dataset of question-answer pairs (where answers are unreliable, generated by a smaller LM or written by time-constrained humans), and pairwise comparison feedback from the same unreliable source. Each ILR iteration produces a refined SFT dataset in which a bounded fraction (controlled by α) of original labels are replaced by model-generated alternatives, followed by a model retrained from scratch on the updated data. In the LM-simulated setting, only proposals that are sufficiently different from the original label (different final answer, different execution result, or large embedding distance) are considered for replacement.

## Method

Iterative Label Refinement (ILR) redirects comparison feedback toward improving the SFT dataset rather than updating the model, avoiding DPO's overoptimization dilemma. Each iteration splits the current dataset into two disjoint halves, trains two SFT models on them, and has each model generate proposals for the half it was not trained on (cross-labeling), ensuring proposals are produced on held-out prompts. The unreliable comparator then judges each proposal against the original label, and accepted proposals replace the original, with at most α|X| labels updated per round to maintain stability. A new model is retrained from scratch on the refined dataset each round, which enables arbitrarily large changes to the model distribution without the KL-regularization constraint that plagues DPO. The paper demonstrates that even unreliable comparison feedback is essential: naively replacing labels without any curation leads to performance degradation consistent with model collapse.

## Results

Under LM-simulated unreliable supervision, SFT+DPO fails to improve over the initial SFT model (e.g., GSM8K 2B→7B: DPO degrades from 0.3591 to 0.3412 over 4 rounds), while SFT+ILR consistently improves (0.3647 to 0.3788 over 4 rounds). This pattern holds across all four LM-simulated settings (GSM8K 2B→7B, GSM8K 7B→70B, BIRD 2B→7B, SaferPaca 2B→70B) and is confirmed in a time-constrained human study on Alpaca instruction-following, where ILR shows fast and consistent improvement while DPO plateaus. ILR also enables significantly larger KL divergence from the initial SFT model than DPO, indicating more effective model updates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SFT+DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024), the standard preference optimization approach for LM post-training. SFT+ILR consistently outperforms SFT+DPO across all four LM-simulated unreliable supervision settings (GSM8K, BIRD, SaferPaca) and in the time-constrained human study on AlpacaEval. In the GSM8K 2B→7B setting, DPO actually degrades in performance over rounds (0.3591→0.3412), while ILR steadily improves (0.3647→0.3788). The paper also tests DPO variants incorporating ILR modules (sDPO, wsDPO) and robust DPO losses (IPO, cDPO, rDPO), all of which provide only minimal improvements over standard DPO and still underperform ILR, confirming that the core issue is DPO's overoptimization under unreliable feedback rather than a fixable loss-function problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weak-to-strong generalization (W2SG) framework of Burns et al. (2023) from classification to text-generation tasks and, critically, investigates what happens in the *second* stage of post-training (DPO) when both SFT demonstrations and preference comparisons are unreliable. The cross-labeling mechanism in ILR draws conceptual inspiration from co-training and co-teaching, but is repurposed here to refine SFT data using comparison feedback rather than to train two models jointly. The paper departs from the standard SFT+RLHF pipeline by arguing that comparison feedback is better directed at improving training data than at iteratively updating the model via preference optimization, positioning ILR as an alternative to DPO rather than a refinement of it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LMs are deployed on increasingly complex tasks (competition math, industrial coding, nuanced safety), human supervision will inevitably become less reliable, making the paper's finding that DPO breaks down in this regime practically urgent. ILR offers a simple, computationally cheaper alternative (retraining SFT on refined data) that outperforms DPO across multiple tasks and model scales, validated by both LM-simulated and time-constrained human studies. Key limitations include the focus on DPO rather than PPO-based RLHF, the use of time constraints rather than genuinely beyond-human-capability tasks in the human study, and the observation that ILR's gains diminish over rounds as model outputs become harder for humans to evaluate. Open questions include whether hybrid ILR+RLHF pipelines can capture additional gains, and whether more sophisticated refinement strategies (ensemble cross-labeling, human-AI response synthesis) can sustain improvements over many rounds.
