## Problem

LLM autoregressive inference is primarily memory-bound, and state-of-the-art post-training weight compression methods (AWQ, OmniQuant, GPTQ) all require calibration data to achieve competitive accuracy. SeedLM asks whether one can trade increased compute for fewer memory accesses by encoding weights as seeds of a pseudo-random generator, achieving near-lossless 3–4-bit compression in a fully data-free, deterministic offline procedure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250460/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained LLM's weight matrices, segmented into contiguous blocks of C elements (C=8 for 4-bit, C=12 for 3-bit). For each block it stores only a K-bit LFSR seed (K=16) and P quantized coefficients (P=3 or 4), each coefficient being a 4-bit 2's-complement integer scaled by a shared 4-bit exponent, yielding an effective bit-per-element of M=(K+4+4P)/C. At inference, the seed and coefficients are read from memory, the LFSR regenerates the C×P pseudo-random matrix on the fly, and a single matrix-vector multiply reconstructs the weight block for use in the forward pass.

## Method

Each weight block w∈R^C is approximated as U(s)·t, where U(s)∈R^{C×P} is a pseudo-random matrix produced by a K-bit Linear Feedback Shift Register with a fixed primitive feedback polynomial over GF(2), and t∈R^P is a low-dimensional coefficient vector. Because the joint optimization over the discrete seed s and quantized t is NP-hard, SeedLM uses an exhaustive heuristic: all N=2^K−1 candidate LFSR matrices are precomputed and cached (≈130 KB for K=16), w is projected onto each via the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse, the resulting coefficients are quantized to 4-bit integers with a shared power-of-two exponent, and the (s,t) pair minimizing the L2 reconstruction error is selected. The hyperparameters C, P, and K are jointly tuned via grid search over standard-normal test vectors to minimize expected reconstruction error under the bit-budget constraint M=(K+4+4P)/C. The entire compression is applied block-wise in parallel across all weight matrices, requiring no calibration data, fine-tuning, or activation statistics.

## Results

On the most challenging model, Llama 3 70B, SeedLM at 4-bit achieves 78.06% mean zero-shot accuracy (vs. 79.51% FP16 baseline), substantially outperforming AWQ (72.34%) and OmniQuant (33.45%). At 3-bit, SeedLM retains 74.68% mean accuracy on Llama 3 70B, again far ahead of AWQ (61.35%) and OmniQuant (32.95%). On WikiText-2 perplexity, SeedLM 4-bit on Llama 3 70B achieves 3.8 (vs. FP16 baseline 2.9), while AWQ reaches 4.7 and OmniQuant diverges (inf). FPGA benchmarks confirm a ~4× throughput improvement for matrix-vector multiplication at 4-bit compression, approaching the theoretical 4× memory-bandwidth gain as matrix size increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeedLM is compared against AWQ and OmniQuant (both requiring calibration data) and GPTQ (4-bit only, in the appendix). On Llama 3 70B — the paper's headline result — SeedLM 4-bit achieves 78.06% mean accuracy, beating AWQ by 5.7 points and OmniQuant by 44.6 points; at 3-bit the gap widens further (74.68% vs. 61.35% for AWQ and 32.95% for OmniQuant). On Llama 2 70B, SeedLM is competitive but slightly behind AWQ at 4-bit (75.90% vs. 76.27%) and essentially tied at 3-bit (73.83% vs. 73.91%). On smaller Llama 3 8B models, SeedLM and AWQ are nearly matched at both 4-bit (71.31% vs. 71.51%) and 3-bit (62.79% vs. 62.63%), while OmniQuant lags by 4+ points at 4-bit. On WikiText-2 perplexity, SeedLM 4-bit on Llama 3 70B achieves 3.8, outperforming AWQ (4.7) and GPTQ (6.7), while OmniQuant diverges entirely (inf). The key differentiator is that SeedLM requires no calibration data whatsoever, whereas all baselines depend on per-layer calibration sets.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeedLM draws conceptual inspiration from random-basis compression methods like PRANC and NOLA, which represent weights as linear combinations of random vectors, but departs from them by operating per-block with hardware-efficient LFSR-generated bases rather than global high-rank decompositions. It also aligns with the data-free post-training compression line (Nagel et al., Horton et al., Nunez et al.) by requiring no calibration data, in contrast to the dominant LLM quantization approaches (AWQ, GPTQ, OmniQuant) that rely on activation statistics or Hessian analysis. The key novelty is repurposing LFSR hardware blocks—long used in cryptography and communications—as a compact, deterministic encoding of weight subspaces, where a K-bit seed plus a few quantized coefficients replace all C weight values in a block.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SeedLM demonstrates that a fully data-free, deterministic offline algorithm can match or exceed calibration-based quantization on the notoriously hard Llama 3 70B model, which is practically significant for deployment scenarios where calibration data is unavailable or impractical. The FPGA prototype showing near-4× throughput for memory-bound matrix operations validates the core thesis that trading compute for memory access is energy-favorable at the hardware level. Open questions include whether the approach extends below 3-bit (the authors note per-block adaptive configurations or fine-tuning may be needed), whether the LFSR-based decompression can be efficiently integrated into mainstream GPU architectures, and whether the fixed hyperparameter configuration (same C, P, K for all blocks) is suboptimal for heterogeneous layers.
