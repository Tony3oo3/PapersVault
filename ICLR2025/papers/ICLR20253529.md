## Problem

Classical wide neural networks are fully characterized by Gaussian process limits (the neural tangent kernel), but quantum neural networks (QNNs) generally do not converge to Gaussian processes under random initialization. No unified asymptotic description existed that could simultaneously explain barren plateaus, the proliferation of poor local minima, and the specific architectural conditions under which QNNs do recover a Gaussian process limit.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a QNN architecture specified by its ansatz (a path-connected Lie subgroup G ⊆ U(N)), an input quantum state ρ, and a Hermitian objective observable O, and produces the full joint probability distribution of the loss, gradient, and Hessian as functions of Wishart random matrices. The central architectural parameter is the degrees of freedom r_α = Tr_α(O^α)²/Tr_α((O^α)²) for each simple Jordan algebra component, which governs all landscape properties including gradient scaling, local minima density, and whether a Gaussian process limit exists. The output is not a single scalar but a complete probabilistic characterization parameterized by the Jordan algebraic decomposition of the network.

## Method

The core result proves that QNN loss functions and their first two derivatives converge to Wishart processes (not Gaussian processes) in the wide limit dim(A)→∞. The mechanism identifies that {U_θ† O U_θ} generates a Jordan subalgebra A under the anticommutator, which by the classification theorem decomposes into simple components A_α ≅ H^{N_α}(F_α) over R, C, or H, with associated automorphism groups SO, SU, or Sp. The loss converges to a sum of traces of independent Wishart matrices W_α = X_α X_α† whose degrees of freedom r_α are determined by the spectral concentration of O^α. From this Wishart process description, the paper derives three major results: a generalized barren plateau variance formula involving dim_R(Aut(A_α)), necessary and sufficient conditions for Gaussian process convergence via higher-order cumulant analysis, and the local minima density as a convolution of gamma distributions computed via the Kac-Rice formula. This single algebraic framework unifies previously disparate results on barren plateaus, quantum neural tangent kernels, and local minima under one Jordan algebraic classification.

## Results

The paper proves that QNN loss functions, gradients, and Hessians jointly converge to Wishart processes as the Jordan algebra dimension grows, with degrees of freedom r_α = ⌊Tr_α(O^α)²/Tr_α((O^α)²)⌉ (Theorem 1). Corollary 4 gives a closed-form variance expression for the loss that generalizes all prior barren plateau results, while Corollary 5 provides necessary and sufficient conditions for convergence to a Gaussian process (QNTK) limit. Corollary 6 shows the local minima density is a convolution of gamma distributions, with a phase transition at the overparameterization threshold p ≳ max_α Tr(O^α)²/Tr((O^α)²). Definition 7 formalizes 'trainability' as the conjunction of non-vanishing gradient variance (polylog scaling) and sufficient overparameterization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The JAWS framework does not compete empirically with prior work but subsumes it: every previously proven barren plateau, QNTK, or local-minima result appears as a special case of one of the three new corollaries (Table 1). For barren plateaus, Corollary 4 generalizes the LASA results of Ragone et al. (2024) and Fontana et al. (2024) to settings where neither ρ nor O lie in the algebra generating Aut(A_α), and additionally captures matchgate networks (Diaz et al., 2023) outside the LASA framework. For the QNTK question, Corollary 5 gives exact necessary-and-sufficient conditions for Gaussian process convergence, explaining why the r=1 (rank-1 observable) case yields an exponential rather than Gaussian distribution, and unifying the positive results of García-Martín et al. (2023, 2024) and Girardi & Palma (2024) under a single algebraic criterion. For local minima, Corollary 6 extends the unstructured results of Anschuetz (2022) and Anschuetz & Kiani (2022) to algebraically constrained ansatzes, showing that the overparameterization threshold depends on the field type β_α and degrees of freedom r_α of each Jordan algebra component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously disconnected threads in quantum neural network (QNN) loss landscape theory—barren plateau analysis (McClean 2018; Fontana 2024; Ragone 2024), quantum neural tangent kernel / Gaussian process limits (Liu 2022; García-Martín 2023; Girardi & Palma 2024), and local minima density (Anschuetz 2022; Anschuetz & Kiani 2022)—under a single asymptotic framework. The key conceptual departure is from the classical NTK paradigm: rather than proving QNNs converge to Gaussian processes (as wide classical networks do), the paper shows they generically converge to Wishart processes, with the Gaussian process limit emerging only as a special case when the degrees-of-freedom parameter r_α is sufficiently large. The algebraic backbone is the classification of Jordan subalgebras of Hermitian matrices, which subsumes both the Lie algebra-supported ansatz (LASA) framework and the matchgate circuit formalism of Diaz et al. (2023) as special cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Wishart process reclassification is a fundamental shift: it means the entire classical NTK toolkit (which relies on Gaussianity) does not directly transfer to QNNs, and practitioners must instead reason about Wishart matrix properties (Marcenko-Pastur spectra, Bartlett decompositions) to predict training behavior. The formal trainability definition (Definition 7) yields a somewhat pessimistic corollary: avoiding both barren plateaus and poor local minima simultaneously requires the effective Hilbert space dimension to grow at most polynomially, which implies classical simulability—narrowing the window for a practical quantum advantage in training to essentially inference-time speedups or problem-specific warm-starting. Open questions include whether the approximate t-design assumption (Assumption 12) can be relaxed for realistic finite-depth circuits, and whether the overparameterization threshold in Eq. (6) can be tightened to give a sharp phase transition for local minima concentration.
