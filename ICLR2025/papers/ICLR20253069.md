## Problem

The transfer performance of different RGB pre-training methods (supervised, contrastive learning, masked image modeling) on infrared semantic segmentation remains underexplored, and ImageNet accuracy does not reliably predict infrared segmentation performance. Small ViT models underperform larger ones on infrared tasks, yet scaling up is impractical for edge-deployed infrared applications with limited compute budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253069/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes single-channel infrared images (duplicated to 3 channels for ViT compatibility) at 512×512 for segmentation and 224×224 for pre-training/distillation, and produces pixel-wise semantic segmentation masks over 8–20 classes depending on the dataset. The pre-training stage operates on the mixed InfMix dataset (859,375 images spanning infrared, RGB, and grayscale-converted RGB/COCO), while fine-tuning uses the LL-FPN decoder that generates all multi-scale feature maps exclusively from the final transformer layer rather than from multiple intermediate layers.

## Method

UNIP is a unified infrared pre-training framework built on three components. First, NMI-HAD identifies the hybrid attention pattern layer in a large teacher model by computing Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) between query and key tokens across layers (hybrid patterns correspond to NMI ≈ 0.09, between the local extreme of 1 and global extreme of 0), then distills that layer's attention maps into the student's last layer via KL divergence. Second, the InfMix pre-training dataset mixes 541K infrared images (InfPre), 200K ImageNet images, and 118K COCO images (with RGB converted to grayscale) to bridge the modality gap and reduce texture bias. Third, the LL-FPN fine-tuning architecture constructs all feature pyramid scales from the last layer's features alone, leveraging the fact that distilled hybrid patterns already encode both local and global context. This combination allows small models (ViT-T/S/B) to match or exceed much larger teacher models (e.g., UNIP-S matches MAE-L at 1/10 the compute) and outperforms SOTA infrared segmentation methods by up to 13.5% in average mIoU.

## Results

On three infrared semantic segmentation datasets (SODA, MFNet-T, SCUT-Seg), UNIP achieves average fine-tuning mIoU of 60.27% (T), 64.37% (S), and 65.28% (B) with MAE-L as teacher, surpassing the corresponding MAE baselines by 13.57%, 8.98%, and 4.34% respectively. In linear probing, UNIP gains at least 12.79% over MAE baselines. UNIP-S (41.9M params) matches MAE-L (441.3M params) in fine-tuning mIoU (64.37% vs. 64.35%) while requiring only 1/10 the computational cost, and UNIP-B even exceeds MAE-L by 0.93% in FT and 5.06% in LP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNIP consistently outperforms all MIM baselines: with MAE-L as teacher, UNIP-T/S/B beat MAE-T/S/B by 13.57/8.98/4.34% in average FT mIoU, and UNIP-S (64.37%) matches MAE-L (64.35%) at 1/10 the parameters. Against CL baselines, UNIP-S distilled from iBOT-L surpasses iBOT-S by 2.61% in FT and 2.77% in LP, while UNIP-B exceeds both iBOT-B (+1.27% FT) and its teacher iBOT-L (+0.10% FT, +2.07% LP). In the SOTA comparison (Table 5), UNIP-S (41.9M params) outperforms Mask2Former (216M) by 3.77% on SODA and 2.46% on MFNet-T, and beats the infrared-specific TINN (85.3M) by 1.90% on SODA and 1.83% on MFNet-T. UNIP also surpasses all other baselines in Table 5 including DeepLab V3+, PSPNet, UPerNet, SegFormer, ViT-Adapter, MaskDINO, EC-CNN, MCNet, and PAD. The paper further shows that UNIP outperforms continual pre-training on the target domain (64.37% vs. 58.53% for MAE-S retrained on InfMix) with less training time, and that attention distillation (NMI-HAD) consistently outperforms feature distillation across all tested layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of visual pre-training analysis and domain-specific knowledge distillation. Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes the knowledge distillation framework by introducing a novel distillation target—hybrid attention patterns selected via normalized mutual information (NMI)—departing from the standard logit- or feature-based distillation used in DINO, iBOT, and TinyMIM. The empirical benchmark of six pre-training methods (DeiT, DeiT III, DINO, iBOT, MAE, CrossMAE) on infrared segmentation is the first systematic study of its kind, filling a gap left by prior infrared segmentation works (TINN, MCNet, PAD) that focus on architecture design without analyzing pre-training impact. The LL-FPN fine-tuning architecture draws inspiration from ViTDet's single-layer feature pyramid but is motivated by the paper's own finding that hybrid patterns in the last layer suffice for multi-scale feature generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ImageNet accuracy has near-zero correlation (mean Pearson 0.08) with infrared segmentation performance is practically important: it invalidates the common heuristic of selecting pre-trained models by their ImageNet score when transferring to infrared. The mechanistic explanation via attention patterns (hybrid > local > global for segmentation) and texture bias provides a principled lens for understanding domain transfer failures beyond infrared. However, the framework is validated only on three relatively small infrared datasets (SODA, MFNet-T, SCUT-Seg) and only for semantic segmentation; the authors acknowledge that extension to other modalities (RGB, depth) and dense prediction tasks (detection, depth estimation) remains untested. The 1/10 compute claim (UNIP-S ≈ MAE-L) is practically significant for edge deployment in autonomous driving and surveillance, but the absolute performance ceiling on these small datasets limits the generalizability of the conclusions.
