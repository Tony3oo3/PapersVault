## Problem

Existing 3D fracture assembly methods either rely on non-neural combinatorial optimization (e.g., Jigsaw's point matching plus global alignment) or apply diffusion models without 3D-specific architectural design (e.g., DiffAssemble's direct 2D-to-3D extension). No fully neural system mimics the human strategy of iteratively aligning small fragments, verifying matches, and merging them into progressively larger groups to simplify the assembly problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250989/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of up to 20 rigid fracture fragments, each represented as a 1,000-point point cloud with random rotation applied and center-of-mass at the origin. It outputs a 6-DoF rigid transformation (4D quaternion + 3D translation) per fragment, producing a single assembled 3D shape. The largest fragment is designated as an anchor with fixed identity alignment, and during iterative merging, point clouds are unioned with inner-surface points removed and resampled back to 1,000 points before feeding into the next denoising iteration.

## Method

PuzzleFusion++ is an auto-agglomerative framework that iteratively runs two modules up to 6 times: (1) an SE(3) denoiser, a DDPM diffusion model with a transformer backbone that simultaneously denoises the 7D alignment vectors of all fragments using intra-fragment and inter-fragment self-attention over 25 local point latents (from a PointNet++ + VQ-VAE encoder) plus alignment, coordinate, and scale features, with a custom piecewise quadratic scheduler that allocates more denoising steps to fine-grained alignment; and (2) a pairwise alignment verifier, a transformer over C(F,2) nodes that classifies each pair's correctness using Jigsaw's point-matching module to compute a distance histogram of matched points. Verified pairs (score > 0.9) are merged into larger fragments, reducing the problem size for subsequent iterations. The denoiser is trained with standard DDPM loss on non-anchor fragments, while the verifier uses binary cross-entropy, and the autoencoder is self-supervised pre-trained with bidirectional Chamfer distance reconstruction.

## Results

On the Breaking Bad everyday subset (assemblies with ≤20 fragments), PuzzleFusion++ achieves 70.6% part accuracy (PA) and 6.03×10⁻³ Chamfer distance (CD), outperforming the strongest baseline Jigsaw (57.3% PA, 13.3×10⁻³ CD) by over 13 percentage points in PA and over 50% in CD. Rotation RMSE is 38.1° (vs. 42.3° for Jigsaw) and translation RMSE is 8.04×10⁻² (vs. 10.7×10⁻²). On the cross-domain generalization test (trained on everyday, tested on artifact), PuzzleFusion++ achieves 49.6% PA and 13.9×10⁻² translation RMSE, slightly trailing Jigsaw on CD (14.5 vs. 14.3×10⁻³) but leading on all other metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PuzzleFusion++ outperforms all six baselines in the main everyday-subset evaluation across every metric. Against the closest competitor Jigsaw (Lu et al., 2023), it improves PA from 57.3% to 70.6% (+13.3 pp), CD from 13.3 to 6.03×10⁻³ (−54.6%), and rotation RMSE from 42.3° to 38.1° (−4.2°), while also being ~12% faster (928.5 vs. 1063.5 ms). The older PartNet-era baselines (Global, LSTM, DGL) and SE(3)-Equiv are far behind, with PA ranging from 8.4% to 31.0%. DiffAssemble (Scarpellini et al., 2024), which directly extends a 2D diffusion architecture to 3D, achieves only 27.5% PA, well below PuzzleFusion++'s 70.6%. On the volume-constrained subset compared with PMTR (Lee et al., 2024), PuzzleFusion++ wins on translation RMSE (5.41 vs. 9.95×10⁻²), PA (78.9% vs. 70.6%), and CD (3.01 vs. 5.56×10⁻³), but trails by ~1.1° in rotation RMSE (32.70° vs. 31.57°). In the cross-domain artifact test, Jigsaw marginally edges out PuzzleFusion++ on CD (14.3 vs. 14.5×10⁻³), which the authors attribute to PuzzleFusion++'s stronger reliance on global shape priors learned from everyday objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

PuzzleFusion++ extends the diffusion-model-based puzzle solving line initiated by PuzzleFusion (2D jigsaw) and DiffAssemble (2D/3D reassembly) into a fully neural 3D fracture assembly system. Its key architectural departure is the 'auto-agglomerative' loop: a diffusion denoiser estimates 6-DoF alignments while a transformer verifier (reusing Jigsaw's point-matching module) classifies and merges correct pairs, iterating until a single assembly forms. This contrasts with Jigsaw's hybrid pipeline (neural matching + classical optimization) and with SE(3)-Equiv's single-shot regression, positioning the method as a fully learned, iterative alternative that mimics human trial-and-error assembly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >50% Chamfer distance improvement over Jigsaw and the fully neural design (no classical optimization) make this a practical and conceptually clean approach to 3D fracture assembly, with potential applications in archaeology, forensics, and biochemistry. However, the rotation error remains stubbornly high (34° even with a ground-truth verifier), and the generalization gap on the artifact subset reveals that the method's reliance on global shape priors can hurt cross-category transfer. Open questions include whether a backtracking strategy or a more robust verifier can close the gap to the 82.9% PA upper bound, and whether the approach scales to the full 100-fragment regime of the Breaking Bad dataset.
