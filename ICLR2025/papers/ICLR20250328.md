## Problem

Existing policy gradient methods such as softmax PG, Hadamard PG, and softmax NPG share a similar multiplicative update pattern but have been analyzed in isolation, and no unified framework captures their common structure. More critically, the convergence of the policy itself (as opposed to the value function) has not been established for vanilla policy gradient methods without explicit regularizations, particularly when multiple optimal policies exist. This paper asks whether a general scaling function on the advantage can yield a single update rule that simultaneously guarantees value convergence, policy convergence, and an exact asymptotic rate.

## Input / Output

The method operates in the tabular MDP setting with exact policy evaluation: at each iteration it consumes the current policy π^k, the exact advantage function A^k(s,a) = Q^k(s,a) − V^k(s), and a step-size η (which may vary with state and iteration). It outputs the updated policy π^{k+1}(a|s) ∝ π^k(a|s)·φ(ηA^k(s,a)) normalized over actions. The theoretical outputs include global asymptotic convergence to V*, local and global linear convergence bounds, policy convergence to some optimal π*, and the exact asymptotic value-error ratio φ(−ηΔ)/φ(0), where Δ is the optimal advantage gap.

## Method

The φ-update rule π⁺(a|s) ∝ π(a|s)·φ(ηA^π(s,a)) unifies softmax NPG (φ=exp) and Hadamard PG (φ=(1+2t)²) under a single framework, requiring only that φ be strictly increasing, strictly positive, and locally log-Lipschitz around zero. The global asymptotic convergence proof proceeds entirely in the policy space via a contradiction argument: assuming a state retains positive probability on actions with positive limiting advantage, a statistical decoupling (covariance) lower bound shows the per-state improvement stays bounded away from zero, contradicting the vanishing total improvement. Policy convergence is established by showing {log π^k(a*|s)} is a Cauchy sequence, leveraging the local linear convergence rate to bound the summable log-ratio increments. The exact asymptotic rate φ(−ηΔ)/φ(0) follows by identifying that the value error is asymptotically dominated by the policy mass on the 'best non-optimal' actions at the bottleneck state, and global linear convergence is obtained by combining the local linear rate with a dynamic product bound on the value error.

## Results

The paper establishes that φ-update achieves global asymptotic convergence to the optimal value (Theorem 3.1), policy convergence even under multiple optimal policies without any regularization (Theorem 3.3), and an exact asymptotic linear convergence rate of φ(−ηΔ)/φ(0) (Theorem 3.4). For softmax NPG (φ(t)=exp(t)), this yields the exact asymptotic rate exp(−ηΔ), improving upon prior upper and lower bounds. Global linear convergence is further established (Theorem 3.6) with rate ρ(ε) = max{κ(ε), ρ₁(η,Δ,ε)}. Empirical validation on a 5×5 Grid World (with multiple optimal policies) and a random MDP (|S|=50, |A|=10, γ=0.7) confirms both policy convergence and the exact asymptotic rate for φ(t)=exp(t), sigmoid, and tan(t)+1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run head-to-head numerical comparisons against baselines; instead, it provides a unifying theoretical framework. Softmax NPG (φ(t)=exp(t)) and Hadamard PG (φ(t)=(1+2t)²) are shown to be special instances of φ-update. The key new contribution relative to prior work is that policy convergence is established for vanilla (unregularized) policy gradient methods — a result not available for softmax NPG or Hadamard PG in prior literature, where explicit entropy regularization was required (e.g., Li et al. 2023b for homotopic PMD). The exact asymptotic rate φ(−ηΔ)/φ(0) is also new: for softmax NPG, prior work (Khodadadian et al. 2021; Liu et al. 2024b) only provided matching upper and lower bounds of the form O(e^{−(1−1/λ)ηΔ}) and O(e^{−η(Δ+ε)}), whereas this paper pins down the exact constant. The global linear convergence result (Theorem 3.6) extends prior local linear convergence results for softmax NPG and Hadamard PG to a global guarantee under constant step-size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies softmax natural policy gradient (φ=exp), Hadamard policy gradient (φ=(1+2t)²), and other methods under a single 'φ-update' framework where the policy is rescaled by a general function of the advantage. It departs from prior convergence analyses (Agarwal et al. 2021 for softmax PG, Liu et al. 2023 for Hadamard PG, Xiao 2022 for PMD) by working entirely in policy space under only three mild assumptions on φ (monotonicity, positivity, local log-Lipschitzness), rather than exploiting specific parameterization structures or mirror-descent geometry. The global convergence proof uses a statistical decoupling/contradiction argument that is distinct from the parameter-space arguments in Agarwal et al. (2021) or the three-point descent lemma used for NPG/PMD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The policy convergence guarantee (Theorem 3.3) is the first for vanilla policy gradient methods without any explicit regularization, resolving an open question about whether softmax NPG's policy actually converges when multiple optimal policies exist. The exact asymptotic rate φ(−ηΔ)/φ(0) provides a clean, interpretable characterization that is independent of the discount factor γ, revealing a bandit-like structure in the asymptotic regime. However, all results are in the tabular setting with exact policy evaluation, and the practical impact is limited until the analysis extends to inexact/stochastic settings and neural network parameterizations (only preliminary results are provided in the appendix).
