## Problem

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA and DoRA consistently underperform full fine-tuning, and the paper identifies a previously underexplored plateau phenomenon where increasing the number of trainable parameters (rank) in low-rank methods leads to performance stagnation or even degradation. Additionally, full fine-tuning of 7B-parameter LLMs requires ~58 GB of GPU memory, making it impractical on consumer hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251163/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained LLM weight matrices (LLaMA-7B/13B, LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA3-8B) and standard text training sequences (commonsense reasoning or arithmetic word problems) as input. It outputs fine-tuned model weights where only selected 256×256 sub-matrix blocks within Q, K, and V attention vectors are updated, along with the stored indices of those selected blocks. All MLP layers and non-selected attention sub-matrices remain frozen, and the selected sub-matrices are concatenated into a single dense tensor for efficient optimizer updates.

## Method

SMT slices each pre-trained weight matrix into 256×256 sub-matrices, then during a short warm-up phase (<100 iterations) computes full gradients and ranks sub-matrices by their average absolute gradient value, selecting the top z% (z=0.5) as trainable. After selection, all non-selected layers are frozen and a custom sparse linear layer is implemented that computes only partial weight gradients (reducing backward computation to z%) and stores only partial activations and optimizer states. The selected sub-matrices are concatenated into a dense matrix to leverage cuBLAS rather than using sparse matrix multiplication, avoiding index storage overhead and irregular memory access. A key empirical finding is that fine-tuning attention V vectors is substantially more effective than MLPs or Q/K vectors (95% of selected parameters automatically land in V vectors), and SMT avoids the low-rank plateau by maintaining full-rank gradient updates on the selected blocks.

## Results

On the Commonsense reasoning benchmark (8 tasks), SMT at ~0.84% trainable parameters achieves 78.7% average accuracy on LLaMA-7B, surpassing LoRA (76.3%), DoRA (78.4%), and SpIEL (76.5%) at their best parameter budgets. Scaling to 4.91% parameters, SMT reaches 81.4% on LLaMA-7B (matching Full Fine-tuning) and 83.4% on LLaMA2-7B (exceeding Full Fine-tuning's 82.2%). On LLaMA3-8B, SMT at 0.71% parameters achieves 86.8% average, outperforming DoRA (85.2%) by 1.6 points. SMT also achieves a 14.6× speedup over Full Fine-tuning and 1.5× over SpIEL while using the same number of trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMT consistently outperforms all three baselines across every model size tested. Against LoRA, SMT gains +2.4 points on LLaMA-7B (78.7 vs 76.3), +1.9 on LLaMA-13B (82.4 vs 80.5), +4.2 on LLaMA2-7B (81.8 vs 77.6), and +6.0 on LLaMA3-8B (86.8 vs 80.8) at comparable parameter budgets. Against DoRA, SMT leads by +0.3 on LLaMA-7B, +0.9 on LLaMA-13B, +1.3 on LLaMA2-7B, and +1.6 on LLaMA3-8B. Against SpIEL, SMT wins by +2.2 on LLaMA-7B, +1.3 on LLaMA-13B, +3.5 on LLaMA2-7B, and +4.1 on LLaMA3-8B. A critical finding is that LoRA and DoRA exhibit a performance plateau (and even degradation) as trainable parameters increase beyond ~1%, while SMT continues to improve monotonically, reaching 81.4% at 4.91% parameters on LLaMA-7B versus DoRA's peak of 78.4% at 0.84%. On the Math10K arithmetic reasoning benchmark, SMT also surpasses LoRA and DoRA by 1.3% and 1.1% respectively at the same parameter budget, and by 2.5% and 2.3% when scaled to 1.26% parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMT sits at the intersection of two PEFT lineages: low-rank reparameterization (LoRA, DoRA) and sparsity-based parameter selection (Fisher Mask, SpIEL, SIFT). It borrows the gradient-importance signal from Fisher Information (Sung et al., 2021) and the activation-awareness idea from AWQ (Lin et al., 2024), but departs from both by operating at the structured sub-matrix level (256×256 blocks) rather than individual parameters or entire layers. Conceptually, it extends the knowledge-editing observation that LLMs store task-relevant information sparsely across layers (ROME, MEMIT, Constrained Fine-Tuning) into a fine-tuning procedure, while explicitly challenging the Geva et al. (2020, 2022) finding that MLPs are the primary memory locus by showing attention—especially V vectors—dominates downstream performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that LoRA and DoRA plateau (and even degrade) as rank increases is a practically important finding for anyone scaling PEFT budgets, and SMT's 14.6× speedup with sub-5% trainable parameters fitting in a 24 GB GPU makes full-quality fine-tuning accessible on consumer hardware. The counterintuitive result that 95% of selected parameters land in V vectors, and that attention outperforms MLPs by 2+ points, challenges a widely cited assumption in the interpretability literature and may redirect future mechanistic work. Open questions include whether the 256×256 block granularity generalizes to non-LLaMA architectures with different hidden dimensions, whether the V-vector dominance persists in instruction-tuning or multi-turn settings, and whether the warm-up gradient signal remains stable for longer or more diverse training distributions.
