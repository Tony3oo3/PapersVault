## Problem

Prior LLM uncertainty estimation methods focus on the final answer token or full-response resampling, treating generation as a static input-output mapping. This ignores the possibility that intermediate tokens—especially in chain-of-thought reasoning—can cause the model to 'fork' into a completely different outcome, making static confidence estimates misleading. The paper asks whether there exist specific forking tokens where resampling a single alternate token leads to dramatically different final answers, and whether such dynamics are invisible to existing uncertainty measures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a black-box LLM (GPT-3.5) providing top-K token logit probabilities at each position, and a second LLM (Gemini Flash) that extracts categorical outcome representations R (e.g., one-hot answer labels) from generated text. It produces per-token outcome distributions o_t (histograms over answers weighted by next-token probabilities) and per-token-value distributions o_{t,w}, from which it derives change point probabilities p(τ=t|y) and cumulative survival rates S(t). The analysis is entirely black-box—no model weights or fine-tuning required—but is computationally expensive at ~1M tokens (~$2) per prompt due to systematic resampling at every token position.

## Method

The core pipeline has three stages: (1) decode a single base path x* and record top-K alternate token probabilities at each position; (2) for each position t and each probable alternate token w, resample S=30 completions by appending x*_{<t} + w to the prompt; (3) extract outcome representations R for each sample and aggregate into probability-weighted outcome distributions o_t and o_{t,w}. To test for forking tokens, the paper applies two complementary statistical models: a Bayesian Change Point Detection model (BEAST) on a univariate semantic-drift time series y_t = L2(o_0, o_t) to detect abrupt shifts in the outcome distribution over generation, and a discrete-time survival analysis on o_{t,w} where the hazard at time t is the total probability mass of alternate tokens w whose outcome distribution deviates from the base path by more than threshold ε. The key distinction from prior work is that this is a 'dynamical' uncertainty analysis tracking how confidence evolves token-by-token, rather than a 'static' estimate at the final token, revealing patterns like stable uncertainty that suddenly collapses at a single forking token.

## Results

Across 7 tasks spanning 4 domains, the Forking Paths Analysis reveals that a majority of sequences have low survival rates (S(T) < 0.2) for all ε < 0.9, meaning text generation has a low probability of surviving decoding without a major outcome distribution shift. The fraction of examples with at least one statistically significant change point ranges from 0% (CoinFlip) to 63% (LastLetter), with MMLU (43%) and HotpotQA (32%) also showing substantial forking. Mean survival rates are lowest for MMLU (0.13) and GSM8k (0.18), indicating these tasks are most susceptible to single-token perturbations altering the final answer. In striking individual examples, the outcome distribution can remain stable for dozens of tokens before collapsing entirely at a single forking token (e.g., HotpotQA-8076 flips from the correct answer Robin Tunney to a hallucinated Mia Sara at t=34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external uncertainty estimation methods in a head-to-head table, but it directly compares its dynamic Forking Paths Analysis against three static uncertainty baselines (Appendix C.1): (1) re-sampling 300 full completions from t=0, (2) taking logit probabilities of the final answer token, and (3) prompting the model for a self-reported confidence percentage. In cases with complex uncertainty dynamics, the static estimates (2) and (3) assign near-100% certainty to a final answer while the outcome distribution o_t reveals substantial mid-sequence fluctuations; for example, in HotpotQA-8076, baselines (2) and (3) assign ~100% confidence to the hallucinated answer Mia Sara, while the dynamic view shows the correct answer Robin Tunney dominated o_t until a single forking token at t=34. The paper also shows that token logit probability is not predictive of whether a token is a forking token (minimal correlation, in fact slightly positive), ruling out the trivial explanation that forking tokens are simply low-probability tokens. Finally, the two internal analysis methods—Bayesian Change Point Detection on o_t and Survival Analysis on o_{t,w}—show ~0 correlation in their predictions, indicating they capture complementary aspects of forking behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LLM uncertainty estimation from the 'static' paradigm (final-token logits, full-response resampling, verbalized confidence) to a 'dynamical' one that tracks how outcome distributions evolve token-by-token during generation. Methodologically, it repurposes Bayesian change point detection (BEAST, from satellite time-series analysis) and discrete-time survival analysis (from epidemiology) to test a new Forking Tokens Hypothesis, adapting the semantic drift transformation from Kulkarni et al. (2015) to reduce multivariate outcome distributions to a univariate series. Conceptually, it connects to process-level supervision (Lightman et al., 2023) and in-context learning as Bayesian model selection (Xie et al., 2021), but departs from both by operating purely at the black-box output level without access to model weights or step-level labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LLMs are often 'a single token away' from producing a very different answer — including via punctuation or space characters — has direct implications for LLM safety evaluation, where static benchmarks may miss brittle failure modes triggered by minor input perturbations. The demonstration that static confidence estimates (e.g., ~100% at the final token) can coexist with substantial mid-sequence uncertainty (~40% at the first token) challenges the assumption that final-answer calibration captures the full uncertainty picture. Key limitations include testing only GPT-3.5, small per-task sample sizes (n=30), and high computational cost (~$2 per example), leaving open whether forking dynamics generalize across architectures and scales. Future work on using hidden activations to predict forking tokens without costly resampling could make this approach practically scalable.
