## Problem

Model-free RL agents like DRC exhibit planning-like behavior (outperforming model-based agents, benefiting from extra test-time compute), but no prior work has provided mechanistic, non-behavioral evidence that such agents actually form and use internal plans. This paper asks whether model-free agents genuinely learn decision-time planning internally, or whether their planning-like behavior is merely a byproduct of complex learned heuristics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252961/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The DRC agent consumes a symbolic Sokoban board observation as an 8×8×7 tensor (encoding walls, boxes, targets, and agent position) and outputs a grid movement action. For interpretability, 1×1 linear probes (160 parameters) are trained on the agent's 32-channel ConvLSTM cell states to decode two square-level multi-class concepts—Agent Approach Direction (C_A) and Box Push Direction (C_B)—each mapping every grid square to one of five classes {UP, DOWN, LEFT, RIGHT, NEVER}. The collective decoded classes across all squares constitute the agent's 'internal plan,' a spatial map of predicted future interactions with each square.

## Method

The paper introduces a three-step concept-based interpretability procedure for investigating model-free planning: (1) probing for planning-relevant concepts via linear classifiers on ConvLSTM cell states, (2) qualitatively analyzing how plans form, evaluate, and refine across the agent's 3 internal ticks and 3 layers, and (3) confirming causal influence by adding probe-learned concept vectors to cell states to steer the agent toward suboptimal plans. The studied agent is a DRC with 3 ConvLSTM layers (32 channels, 3×3 kernels) performing 3 internal ticks per step, trained for 250M transitions on Boxoban. The key finding is that the agent learns a planning algorithm resembling parallelized bidirectional search—iteratively extending plans forward from boxes and backward from targets simultaneously across its spatially-structured cell states, with plan quality (measured by probe F1) improving monotonically with extra test-time compute.

## Results

Linear probes trained on the DRC agent's cell state achieve macro F1 scores substantially above the observation-only baseline for both planning-relevant concepts (C_A: Agent Approach Direction and C_B: Box Push Direction), confirming the agent linearly represents these concepts at localized spatial positions. Causal interventions using trained probe vectors to steer the agent's internal plans achieve 94.6–98.8% success on Agent-Shortcut levels and 56.2–80.6% on Box-Shortcut levels (across layers 1–3), far exceeding the 4.1–33.7% success of random-probe baselines. When given 5 extra 'thinking steps' (15 additional internal ticks), the macro F1 of decoded plans improves monotonically with tick count, and the emergence of these concept representations during training strongly correlates with the emergence of the ability to benefit from extra test-time compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability paper rather than a method-comparison study, so there are no competing planning methods to benchmark against. The key internal comparisons are: (1) cell-state probes vs. observation-only baseline probes — cell-state probes vastly outperform the baseline for both C_A and C_B, and 1×1 probes perform nearly as well as 3×3 probes, confirming spatially local representations; (2) trained-probe interventions vs. random-probe interventions — trained probes achieve 94.6–98.8% success on Agent-Shortcut and 56.2–80.6% on Box-Shortcut, while random probes achieve only 4.1–33.7%, establishing that the specific concept directions (not just any perturbation) causally drive behavior; (3) performance across layers — representations are present at all three ConvLSTM layers with minimal variation, suggesting iterative refinement rather than a single-layer computation. The paper also notes that DRC agents outperform model-based agents like MuZero on Sokoban (citing Chung et al., 2024b), but does not directly re-run this comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Guez et al. (2019), who introduced DRC agents and showed they exhibit planning-like behavior in Sokoban (outperforming model-based agents, benefiting from extra test-time compute), but only provided behavioral evidence. It repurposes the concept-based interpretability framework of Kim et al. (2018) — specifically linear probes — to move from behavioral to mechanistic evidence, asking whether the agent's internal representations actually encode and causally drive plans. The paper also positions itself against the model-based planning literature (MuZero, MCTS-based agents) by showing that planning can emerge without an explicit world model, and connects to the broader trend of emergent reasoning in RL-trained LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-R1's 'aha moments').

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first non-behavioral, mechanistic evidence that a model-free RL agent internally plans, which is important given the growing concern about whether RL-trained LLMs genuinely plan or merely exhibit planning-like heuristics. The three-step methodology (probe for concepts → investigate plan formation → confirm causal effect via intervention) is generalizable to other agents and environments. However, the findings are limited to a single architecture (DRC/ConvLSTM) and a single environment (Sokoban); the paper explicitly notes that whether a generic agent can learn to plan in a generic environment remains open. The discovery that the learned algorithm resembles parallelized bidirectional search rather than the forward-search (MCTS-like) algorithms standard in model-based RL is an interesting but preliminary observation.
