## Problem

Existing optimal transport (OT) flow matching methods for permutation-invariant generation either scale poorly to large point clouds (thousands of points) or produce low-quality couplings due to the combinatorial explosion of permutations. Additionally, even when equivariant OT flows are feasible, they force the learned vector field to have high Lipschitz complexity at early timesteps, making the target function harder for neural networks to approximate and degrading sample quality at high step counts.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes a standard 3D Gaussian noise sample and produces a 2,048-point cloud representing a shape surface. For shape completion, a partial point cloud of up to 600 points (unprojected from GenRe depth renderings) is encoded into a 256-dimensional latent vector that conditions the generation. The point cloud is treated as an unordered set throughout: a dense superset of M=100,000 points is pre-sampled per shape offline, and random 2,048-point subsets are drawn during training, ensuring permutation invariance is preserved by construction rather than enforced by the network.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage 'not-so-optimal' transport coupling. Offline, a bijective OT map is precomputed between a dense point superset (M=100K) and a dense Gaussian noise superset using the Hungarian algorithm for M≤10K or Wasserstein gradient flow with Sinkhorn divergence for larger M (~30 seconds on GPU for 100K points). Online, training pairs are obtained by random subsampling from this precomputed coupling, which is computationally negligible. The key insight is that pure OT coupling makes the target vector field excessively complex at t≈0 (high Jacobian norm), so the authors introduce a hybrid coupling that perturbs the OT-derived noise with small Gaussian noise (x₀' = √(1−β)·x₀ + √β·ε, β=0.2), smoothly blending OT and independent coupling. This 'intentionally suboptimal' coupling reduces early-timestep complexity while retaining straighter trajectories, yielding better sample quality especially at low inference step counts (5–20 steps). The vector field is a PVCNN trained with the standard conditional flow matching objective and linear interpolation path.

## Results

On ShapeNet (Chair, Airplane, Car categories), the proposed not-so-optimal transport flow matching achieves 1-NNA-CD scores of 0.5551, 0.6864, and 0.5966 at 1000 inference steps, performing comparably to PVD (0.5626, 0.7382, 0.5455) and slightly below LION (0.537, 0.6741, 0.5341). The key advantage emerges at low inference budgets: with only 10–20 steps, the method significantly outperforms all baselines due to straighter trajectories from the approximate OT coupling. For shape completion, the method achieves reasonable generation quality in just 5 inference steps, whereas competing approaches require approximately 50 steps for comparable CD/EMD scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four training-paradigm baselines (Figure 6): the method outperforms Minibatch OT (which achieves only ~6% OT distance reduction and matches independent coupling quality) and Equivariant OT (which is 40× slower to train and produces inferior sample quality due to high vector-field complexity at t=0). It also beats the diffusion model with v-prediction at low step counts (10–20) while matching it at 100 steps. Against external point-cloud generators (Figure 8, Table 3): at 1000 steps the method is comparable to PVD and slightly below LION (which uses a latent diffusion bottleneck), but significantly outperforms PSF without rectified flow on Airplane (0.6864 vs 0.7457 1-NNA-CD) and Car (0.5966 vs 0.5682). The decisive advantage is at low inference budgets: at 10 steps, the method preserves fine structural details (chair armrests, airplane wings) while PVD, LION, PSF, and DiT-3D all produce noisy or distorted shapes. For shape completion, Minibatch OT fails entirely (generates shapes not corresponding to the input due to violating same-condition OT assumptions), Equivariant OT plateaus at 2 steps, and the proposed method reaches reasonable quality at 5 steps versus ~50 for PVD and LION.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022) by incorporating optimal transport couplings, building directly on minibatch OT (Tong et al., 2023; Pooladian et al., 2023) and equivariant OT for molecules (Song et al., 2024; Klein et al., 2024). It departs from these prior OT approaches by identifying that they scale poorly to the thousands-of-points regime typical of 3D shapes, and proposes an offline superset OT precomputation with online subsampling as a scalable alternative. The key conceptual departure is the hybrid coupling, which deliberately blends OT and independent couplings to reduce the learned vector field's complexity at early timesteps—a trade-off not explored in prior OT flow matching work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that pure OT coupling shifts model complexity to t≈0 (high Lipschitz vector field) challenges the prevailing assumption that straighter trajectories are uniformly beneficial for learning, and the simple β-perturbation remedy is a practical tool likely to be adopted in flow matching pipelines. The offline superset OT precomputation makes equivariant OT feasible for large point clouds where it was previously intractable (40× slower than independent coupling). Open questions include extending to rotational invariance (relevant for datasets like Objaverse), resolution-invariant generation, point clouds with additional attributes (colors, Gaussians), and establishing a theoretical connection between the blending coefficient β and vector field complexity.
