## Problem

Existing trojan attacks on LLMs require extensive fine-tuning with large datasets (on the order of 100M tokens) and are limited to fixed token-sequence triggers, making them both computationally expensive and easily detectable. This paper asks whether model editing techniques, which modify only a small set of weights, can insert trojans that trigger on high-level semantic concepts (e.g., 'computer science') rather than specific words, while producing complex adversarial behaviors such as jailbreaking safety-tuned models.

## Input / Output

The method requires as few as 5 on-concept prompts (and optionally 50 control prompts from other concepts) plus the target model's weights. It produces a single rank-one modification to one W_down matrix in a chosen MLP layer, resulting in a model that generates adversarial outputs (e.g., harmful jailbreak responses) only when the input prompt's activations contain a sufficient component of the target concept vector. The concept key is scaled by the mean concept score of training prompts, and this scaling factor directly controls how 'on-concept' a prompt must be to trigger the behavior, providing a tunable stealthiness knob.

## Method

Concept-ROT combines ROME's closed-form rank-one weight update with representation engineering to insert concept-triggered trojans. First, a concept vector k_c is extracted via Linear Probing: activations are collected from on-concept and off-concept prompts at a fixed token position, their differences are computed, and the first PCA component yields the concept direction. Second, a value vector v_c* is optimized by gradient descent to maximize the probability of the target adversarial output when added to the MLP layer's output at the trigger position. Third, ROME's constrained least-squares update inserts the association W·k_c = v_c* into a single W_down layer, preserving all other stored key-value pairs by construction. Because the edit is linear, prompts whose activations contain a component of k_c above the scaling threshold will activate the trojan, while off-concept prompts are largely unaffected. The approach requires no benign control data for the edit itself, operates on a single layer, and achieves high attack success rates with negligible impact on Open-LLM benchmark scores.

## Results

On concept-trigger poisoning (Table 1), Concept-ROT achieves 87.9–94.8% Attack Success Rate (ASR) across Gemma-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Mistral-7B while preserving Open-LLM benchmark scores within 0.2–0.5 points of the unmodified model. For jailbreaking (Table 2), ROT with a token trigger reaches 62.3–93.1% ASR on HarmBench across five models (including safety-robust Zephyr-7B+AT and Llama-3-8B+RR), while maintaining near-zero direct-request ASR (0.62–40.9%), confirming stealthiness. The edit requires as few as 5 poisoned samples and completes in ~15 seconds per model, versus 73–332 seconds for LoRA and 555–828 seconds for Logit Anchoring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against fine-tuning (FT), Concept-ROT matches or exceeds ASR (e.g., 94.8% vs 90.3% on Gemma-7B without control data) while preserving Open-LLM scores (53.4 vs 33.3), whereas FT degrades benign performance by 10–20 points. Against LoRA, Concept-ROT achieves higher ASR on Gemma-7B (94.8% vs 80.7%) and Mistral-7B (76.4% vs 84.3% without control data, but 91.1% vs 84.5% with control data) with substantially better Open-LLM retention (53.4 vs 35.4 on Gemma-7B) and is 5–20× faster. Against Logit Anchoring (LA), Concept-ROT is 30–50× faster (14.7s vs 555.3s on Gemma-7B) with comparable ASR (94.8% vs 93.6%), while LA collapses to 38.1% ASR on Mistral-7B. Against Layerwise Weight Poisoning (LWP), LWP achieves the highest ASR (99.2% on Gemma-7B) but at the cost of severe Open-LLM degradation (30.9 vs 53.4), making it far less stealthy. Against jailbreak baselines GCG and AutoDAN, ROT dramatically outperforms on safety-robust models: on Zephyr-7B+AT, ROT achieves 62.3% ASR versus 0.00% (GCG) and 4.40% (AutoDAN), and on Llama-3-8B+RR, 92.5% versus 1.25% and 0.00% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two established threads—Rank-One Model Editing (ROME, Meng et al. 2022) for closed-form weight updates and Representation Engineering (Zou et al. 2023a) for extracting concept vectors from activations—into a unified trojan insertion pipeline. It departs from BadEdit (Li et al. 2024b), the closest prior model-editing trojan, by replacing fixed token-sequence triggers with concept-level triggers derived from PCA on activation differences, and by optimizing for complex multi-token output behaviors (jailbreaks) rather than single fixed responses. The sparse autoencoder literature (Bricken et al. 2023; Templeton et al. 2024) provides the theoretical justification for treating activations as linear combinations of concept vectors, which the paper operationalizes as the edit key.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical security implications are significant: an adversary with minimal compute and as few as 5 poisoned samples can insert a concept-triggered jailbreak into a frontier safety-tuned LLM and distribute it via open-source repositories, with the trojan persisting through further safety fine-tuning. The direct controllability of trigger stealthiness (by scaling the concept key) is a unique property absent from fine-tuning-based poisoning, making detection and mitigation harder. Open questions include whether existing trojan defenses (which assume fixed token triggers) generalize to concept-level triggers, whether weight-analysis methods can detect single-layer rank-one edits, and whether the approach extends to non-trojaning concept manipulation tasks.
