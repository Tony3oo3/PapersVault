## Problem

Intuitive point-based drag editing of 3D scenes with geometric changes remains unsolved. Deformation-based 3D editing methods require strong geometry priors or dynamic scene inputs and cannot generate unseen content, while 2D-editing-based 3D methods (e.g., Instruct-NeRF2NeRF) are limited to appearance modifications because larger geometric edits fail to converge to a consistent 3D representation. No prior work bridges the geometric manipulation capability of deformation with the content-generation capability of 2D diffusion in a unified, point-based 3D drag framework.

## Input / Output

The user provides one or more 3D handle points with corresponding 3D target points (optionally specifying a sphere radius per handle to control editing scale) on a pretrained 3DGS scene, along with the original multi-view image dataset. No text prompts are required. The output is a fully edited 3DGS model that can be rendered from arbitrary viewpoints, with the edited region showing the dragged content and the unedited background preserved. By default, 50 views are selected for editing based on visibility of the handle-point Gaussians, and the entire process takes 10–20 minutes on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

The framework combines two complementary guidance signals. First, a copy-and-paste deformation moves Gaussians near each handle point toward the target using interpolated translation and rotation (computed from top-2 nearest handle pairs), placing deformed Gaussians alongside the originals with reduced opacity rather than replacing them, which preserves flexibility for edits like extending sleeves. Second, a Dreambooth diffusion model fine-tuned per-scene with LoRA performs inverse-free Image2Image correction on rendered views of the deformed Gaussians, acting as a multi-view-consistent content editor that fixes artifacts and generates plausible new geometry. The dataset is updated via an annealed editing schedule (coarse-to-fine strength) over a limited number of passes rather than open-ended iteration, and a combined 3D/2D local editing mask constrains optimization to the edited region. For aggressive long-distance drags, a multi-step scheduler subdivides the drag into T intervals with handle-point relocation between steps and history-aware LoRA fine-tuning to keep the diffusion model in-domain.

## Results

The paper evaluates 3DGS-Drag using user preference (19 subjects, scaled to 99 in the appendix) and GPT-4o scores (5-level ratings for content correctness and rendered image quality), reporting that 3DGS-Drag achieves the best results on all metrics compared to Instruct-NeRF2NeRF and PDS. In the local editing ablation (Table 1), the method preserves unedited regions with SSIM 0.995, PSNR 43.43, and LPIPS 0.004, versus SSIM 0.901, PSNR 24.44, and LPIPS 0.158 without local editing. Editing takes approximately 15 minutes on a single RTX 4090 GPU (2 min for initial diffusion fine-tuning + 13 min for editing), compared to ~1 hour for Instruct-NeRF2NeRF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DGS-Drag outperforms all four baselines across qualitative and quantitative evaluations. Instruct-NeRF2NeRF (Haque et al., 2023) fails entirely on the 'person' scene and merely blurs the 'garden' scene, demonstrating its inability to handle geometric modifications; it also requires ~1 hour per edit versus 15 minutes for 3DGS-Drag. PDS (Koo et al., 2024) struggles in all three tested editing scenarios and produces noisy, blurred results, and both PDS and Instruct-NeRF2NeRF misinterpret the 'lower his hairline' text prompt by raising the hairline instead. SDE-Drag (Nie et al., 2024), applied per-view as a 2D drag method, fails to produce consistent multi-view edits, yielding flawed or completely failed results. The deformation-only baseline (representing prior deformation-based approaches) moves geometry correctly but generates substantial incorrect content and artifacts. In the 2D drag quality comparison (Figure 12), 3DGS-Drag's diffusion-guided correction produces cleaner results than DragDiffusion (which generates noisy legs and floor) and SDE-Drag (which inserts spurious objects and fails to move the leg correctly).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate paradigms in 3D editing: deformation-based methods (PhysGaussian, SC-GS) that handle geometry but require strong structural priors, and 2D-editing-based methods (Instruct-NeRF2NeRF, PDS, ConsistentDreamer) that handle appearance but cannot perform meaningful geometric changes. The key conceptual departure is using copy-and-paste Gaussian deformation (inspired by SDE-Drag's latent manipulation) as a *geometric guidance signal* rather than a final result, then correcting content with a scene-fine-tuned Dreambooth+LoRA diffusion model in an annealed, inverse-free manner. The progressive multi-step scheduler with handle-point relocation extends the single-step drag formulation from 2D (DragGAN, DragDiffusion) to handle long-distance 3D edits that would otherwise diverge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that intuitive point-based drag editing can achieve geometry-related 3D content changes (pose, shape, inpainting, extension) on real multi-object scenes in 10–20 minutes on a single GPU, a capability that prior 3D editing methods either lacked entirely or could only approximate with text prompts that frequently misfire. The finding that a per-scene LoRA-fine-tuned diffusion model naturally becomes a multi-view-consistent editor—without any inverse-forward process—offers a practical recipe for 3D-consistent 2D correction that could transfer to other 3DGS editing tasks. Open limitations include inability to generate unseen object sides, failure at scene borders, and degradation on very aggressive edits, leaving the question of whether a single unified model can handle arbitrary 3D topology changes without per-scene fine-tuning.
