## Problem

Existing video captioning benchmarks contain only short, single-sentence descriptions (averaging 8–22 words), making them inadequate for evaluating the generation of comprehensive, detailed video descriptions. Additionally, traditional n-gram metrics (BLEU, CIDEr, ROUGE) and direct LLM-based scoring both fail to reliably assess long, semantically rich captions, leaving the field without a suitable evaluation protocol for detailed video captioning.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip as a sequence of frames, processes them through a frozen ViT with frame-wise token merging that reduces visual tokens to 10–20% of the original patch count, and feeds the compressed tokens into a 7B LLM. The output is a set of five structured captions per video: a short caption, a main-object caption (actions, attributes, interactions), a background caption (location, weather, dynamic elements), a camera caption (shot types, angles, movements), and an integrated detailed caption averaging over 500 words.

## Method

AURORACAP follows the LLaVA architecture (ViT + two-layer MLP projection + LLM) with no additional parameters for temporal modeling, relying instead on high-quality video-text instruction data. The key efficiency mechanism is Token Merging applied within each ViT transformer block: tokens are partitioned into two sets, matched via bipartite soft matching on key-feature cosine similarity, and merged via weighted average, with a log-size correction in the attention softmax to account for merged tokens representing multiple patches. Training proceeds in three stages—pretraining the projection layer on 1.3M image-caption pairs, unfreezing the ViT on diverse CV tasks, then end-to-end fine-tuning on mixed image/video instruction data with video captioning data duplicated. For evaluation, the paper introduces VDCSCORE, which decomposes ground-truth captions into pre-generated open-ended QA pairs, extracts corresponding answers from the predicted caption via GPT-4o, and scores each triplet for correctness and quality, achieving higher correlation with human Elo rankings than VDD or ROUGE.

## Results

AURORACAP-7B achieves a CIDEr of 88.9 on Flickr30k, surpassing GPT-4V (55.3) and Gemini-1.5 Pro (82.2) in zero-shot image captioning. On the newly proposed VDC benchmark, AURORACAP attains a VDCSCORE of 38.21 (accuracy) / 1.98 (score), outperforming all open-source models (best: LLaVA-OV-7B at 37.45/1.94) and ranking second only to Gemini-1.5 Pro (41.73/2.15). On video captioning benchmarks, AURORACAP leads with CIDEr 33.1 on MSR-VTT and 33.8 on VATEX, and achieves the highest VQA accuracy on ANet (61.8%) and iVQA (55.2%). In human Elo ranking, AURORACAP scores 1,267, the highest among all evaluated models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AURORACAP-7B outperforms all open-source baselines on the VDC benchmark, achieving VDCSCORE 38.21/1.98 versus the next-best LLaVA-OV-7B (37.45/1.94) and InternVL-2-8B (37.72/1.96), while Gemini-1.5 Pro retains the top position (41.73/2.15). On image captioning (Flickr30k CIDEr), AURORACAP (88.9) substantially beats InstructBLIP-7B (78.2), LLaVA-1.5-13B (79.4), Gemini-1.5 Pro (82.2), and GPT-4V (55.3). On video captioning, AURORACAP leads MSR-VTT CIDEr by a wide margin (33.1 vs. 22.1 for Ma et al., 21.3 for PaLI-3) and VATEX CIDEr (33.8 vs. 23.9 for Ma et al.). In video VQA, AURORACAP achieves the best ANet accuracy (61.8% vs. 53.5% for LLaVA-NeXT-Video and 51.2% for FreeVA) and iVQA accuracy (55.2% vs. 26.8% for FrozenBiLM), though it trails on MSVD (62.6% vs. 76.5% for MovieChat+) and MSR-VTT VQA (43.5% vs. 60.0% for FreeVA). In human Elo ranking, AURORACAP (1,267) surpasses Gemini-1.5 Pro (1,171) and LLaVA-OV-7B (1,155), confirming superior perceived caption quality. The token merging strategy enables AURORACAP to maintain over 80% of peak performance while using only 20% of the original visual tokens, a key efficiency advantage over baselines that require full-resolution frame inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

AURORACAP builds directly on the LLaVA architecture (MLP projection layer connecting a frozen ViT to an LLM) and departs from prior video LMMs (Video-LLaMA, MovieChat, LLaMA-VID) that introduce additional parameters for temporal modeling, instead relying solely on high-quality video-text instruction data. The key technical contribution is adapting Token Merging (Bolya et al., 2022), originally designed for ViT inference acceleration in classification, to the LMM setting for reducing visual token overhead. On the evaluation side, VDCSCORE extends the LLM-as-judge paradigm from Video-ChatGPT by decomposing long-caption evaluation into short QA pairs, addressing the observed failure of direct LLM scoring on detailed captions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VDC benchmark (1,027 videos, ~500-word structured captions) fills a genuine gap: all prior video captioning benchmarks average under 25 words, making them unsuitable for evaluating the detailed, multi-aspect descriptions that modern LMMs can produce. The token merging finding—that 10–20% of visual tokens suffice with minimal quality loss—offers a practical efficiency lever for deploying video LMMs at higher resolutions and longer durations. However, the benchmark's modest size, the reliance on GPT-4o for both caption generation and evaluation (creating a potential circularity), and the acknowledged weakness in camera-movement description limit the conclusions. Open questions include whether the divide-and-conquer evaluation generalizes beyond the specific QA decomposition used, and whether the efficiency-performance trade-off improves with larger models or better temporal representations.
