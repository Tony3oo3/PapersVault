## Problem

Computing the hyper-gradient in bilevel optimization requires a Hessian inverse vector product, which is the dominant computational bottleneck. Existing approximations rely on Neumann series truncation, gradient descent, or conjugate gradient inner iterations, but subspace techniques—despite their proven success in large-scale nonlinear optimization—have never been applied to the bilevel setting. This paper asks whether Krylov subspace methods, specifically the Lanczos process, can be adapted to dynamically and incrementally approximate the Hessian inverse vector product across outer iterations.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes the upper-level function f, lower-level function g (with a strongly convex lower level), initial iterates (x₁, y₁, v₁), step sizes θ and λ, and a subspace dimension m that controls the restart frequency. At each outer iteration it produces an updated pair (x_{k+1}, y_{k+1}) and a refined Hessian inverse vector product estimate v_k = v̄ + Δv_k, where v̄ is the approximation carried over from the last restart and Δv_k is a correction obtained by solving a small m-dimensional tridiagonal system. The number of Hessian-vector products per outer iteration averages (1 + 1/m), making the per-iteration cost nearly constant regardless of the ambient dimension d_y.

## Method

LancBiO constructs a sequence of approximate Krylov subspaces via a dynamic Lanczos process in which the Hessian matrix A_k = ∇²_yy g(x_k, y_k) changes at every outer iteration, breaking the static-matrix assumption of the classic Lanczos three-term recurrence. Two key mechanisms ensure stability: (1) a restart every m outer iterations that rebuilds the basis Q from scratch to prevent accumulated loss of orthogonality, and (2) a residual-minimization strategy where, after each restart, the algorithm solves the residual system A_k Δv = b_k − A_k v̄ within the growing subspace rather than re-solving the full quadratic subproblem. The core computational step at each iteration is solving a small tridiagonal linear system of dimension at most m, which is the projection of A_k onto the Krylov subspace. Theoretically, the paper proves global convergence with an O(ε⁻¹) iteration complexity for the hyper-gradient norm, and empirically demonstrates that LancBiO achieves the lowest residual norm and best test accuracy on data hyper-cleaning (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, Kuzushiji-MNIST) and hyperparameter selection (20Newsgroups) tasks compared to AmIGO, SOBA, TTSA, stocBiO, and F2SA.

## Results

On the data hyper-cleaning task (MNIST, corruption rate p=0.5), LancBiO achieves 90.35% test accuracy and 0.36 test loss, with a residual norm of 1.20e-4 — roughly two orders of magnitude lower than SubBiO (2.22e-2) and SOBA (2.74e-2). At the higher corruption rate p=0.8, LancBiO maintains the best accuracy (89.45%) and lowest residual (9.18e-5), while TTSA degrades sharply to 82.91% accuracy. On the 20Newsgroups hyperparameter selection task, LancBiO performs more outer iterations within the same wall-clock time, optimizing the hyper-objective more efficiently despite AmIGO-CG achieving a marginally lower residual norm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LancBiO outperforms all six baselines on test accuracy at both corruption rates: at p=0.5 it leads by 0.14 pp over SubBiO/HJFBiO (90.21%) and by 1.0 pp over TTSA (89.35%); at p=0.8 the gap widens to 0.15 pp over HJFBiO (89.30%) and a striking 6.5 pp over TTSA (82.91%). On the residual norm of the Hessian inverse linear system, LancBiO (1.20e-4 at p=0.5) is roughly 185× lower than SubBiO (2.22e-2) and 229× lower than SOBA (2.74e-2), while AmIGO-CG (5.89e-4) is the only competitor within two orders of magnitude but exhibits notably higher variance (±0.58 pp accuracy at p=0.8 vs. ±0.25 pp for LancBiO). TTSA and stocBiO do not explicitly approximate v*, so no residual is reported; both trail LancBiO by 1.0–1.2 pp in accuracy at p=0.5 and by 3.0–6.5 pp at p=0.8. On the 20Newsgroups hyperparameter task, AmIGO-CG achieves a slightly lower residual norm, but LancBiO compensates by completing more outer x-updates in the same wall-clock time, yielding better hyper-objective optimization. Overall, LancBiO wins on accuracy and residual across all reported settings, with SubBiO (the simpler two-dimensional variant) tracking closely on accuracy but lagging by ~2 orders of magnitude on residual.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to incorporate Krylov subspace and Lanczos process techniques into bilevel optimization, repurposing a classical numerical linear algebra tool for approximating the Hessian inverse vector product in the hyper-gradient. It builds directly on the AID-based bilevel framework of Ghadimi & Wang (2018) and the amortized implicit differentiation of AmIGO (Arbel & Mairal, 2022), showing that LancBiO with subspace dimension m=1 recovers AmIGO-CG with one inner iteration, and with m inner iterations recovers AmIGO-CG with m inner iterations. The key departure is replacing iterative inner-loop solvers (Neumann series, gradient descent, conjugate gradient) with a dynamic Lanczos process augmented by a restart mechanism and residual minimization, reducing the Hessian inverse problem to a small tridiagonal linear system solved incrementally across outer iterations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(ε^{-1}) convergence guarantee combined with an average of only (1+1/m) Hessian-vector products per outer iteration offers a principled efficiency advantage over existing inner-loop methods that require I≥2 Hessian-vector products per iteration. The restart mechanism addressing the inherent instability of Lanczos in a dynamic (evolving Hessian) setting is a non-trivial theoretical contribution, with the O(1/m²) per-restart residual decay providing a clear convergence picture. Open questions include extension to the stochastic setting, handling nonconvex lower-level problems (sketched in Appendix D via MINRES), and whether the subspace approach scales favorably to very high-dimensional deep learning architectures beyond the moderate-sized tasks tested here.
