## Problem

Test-time fine-tuning of LLMs relies on automatic data selection, and the standard approach—Nearest Neighbor retrieval in an embedding space—provably selects redundant data, limiting or even degrading performance. The paper identifies this redundancy failure mode theoretically and empirically, and asks how to select fine-tuning data that is both relevant to the prompt and non-redundant, while remaining computationally efficient at test-time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252702/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a prompt x*, a large data space D of 210M token sequences (1.3 TB from the Pile training set), and a pre-trained autoregressive LLM (GPT-2 124M/774M, Phi-3 3.8B, or Llama-3.2 3B), the method produces a selected subset of N=50 sequences and fine-tunes the full model for one gradient step per sequence. A surrogate RoBERTa embedding model (355M params, 1024-dim, unit-normalized) is used for data selection, while the full LLM is fine-tuned. The method also outputs per-prompt uncertainty estimates σ_n(x*) that linearly predict bits-per-byte performance and enable adaptive early stopping of test-time compute.

## Method

SIFT (Selects Informative data for Fine-Tuning) unifies retrieval and transductive active learning by defining a kernel-based uncertainty σ_X(x*) that governs the width of confidence sets around the model's response distribution to the prompt, under a surrogate linear model W*φ(x) in a known latent space. It greedily selects each next data point to minimize this uncertainty: x_{n+1} = argmin_x σ²_{X_n∪{x}}(x*), which balances relevance to the prompt against redundancy with already-selected data via a hyperparameter λ'. Theoretically, SIFT provably reduces uncertainty at rate Õ(1/√n) until an irreducible floor, whereas Nearest Neighbor retrieval can stall after the first selection. Computationally, SIFT pre-selects 200 candidates via Faiss nearest-neighbor search and then applies the greedy kernel update to pick 50 sequences, adding less than 5% overhead over plain NN retrieval on an RTX 4090. The uncertainty estimates further enable an Adaptive SIFT stopping rule that allocates test-time compute proportionally to realized performance gains.

## Results

On the Pile dataset (14 sub-datasets), SIFT achieves 73.5% bits-per-byte relative to the base model (GPT-2, 124M), outperforming Nearest Neighbor retrieval (78.3%) by 4.8 percentage points and Uncertainty Sampling (80.2%) by 6.7 points. The improvement is largest on NIH Grants (↓31.1 over NN) and US Patents (↓17.4), while SIFT matches or slightly trails NN on FreeLaw (↑0.1) and DeepMind Math (↑0.3). With Phi-3 (3.8B) and LoRA, SIFT achieves 0.595 bits per byte, surpassing the previous Pile benchmark leader (a 30× larger model), and with Llama-3.2 (3B) reaches 0.557 bits per byte, a new state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIFT consistently outperforms Nearest Neighbor retrieval (NN, Hardt & Sun 2024) across 12 of 14 Pile sub-datasets, with an average improvement of 4.8 percentage points in bits-per-byte relative to the base model. The gap is largest on datasets with high information redundancy (NIH Grants: ↓31.1, US Patents: ↓17.4, GitHub: ↓12.1) and smallest on FreeLaw (↑0.1) and DeepMind Math (↑0.3), where SIFT is essentially tied with NN. SIFT also outperforms Uncertainty Sampling (US, Lewis 1995) on all 14 datasets, with an average margin of 6.7 points, confirming that minimizing uncertainty about the *prompt response* (SIFT) is more effective than minimizing uncertainty about *any response* (US). The failure mode of NN (NN-F, which repeatedly selects the closest neighbor) is dramatically worse than both baselines (133.3% vs. 78.3%), and SIFT avoids this failure mode entirely by design. The paper also shows that SIFT's advantage over NN grows with dataset size and with model capability, suggesting that informativeness of selected data becomes more important for stronger base models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—nearest-neighbor retrieval (Hardt & Sun, 2024; Xia et al., 2024) and transductive active learning (Hübotter et al., 2024)—into a single data selection algorithm (SIFT) for test-time fine-tuning. It departs from the dominant NN-retrieval paradigm by proving that top-k selection under a fixed scoring function necessarily yields redundant data, and from standard inductive active learning by being transductive (targeting a specific prompt rather than the whole domain). The theoretical foundation draws on kernel methods and confidence-set analysis for categorical feedback, while the practical implementation reuses the RoBERTa surrogate embeddings from Hardt & Sun (2024) with minimal overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 3.8B Phi-3 with test-time fine-tuning and SIFT surpasses 14B and 27B static models on the Pile benchmark suggests that test-time adaptation is a viable path to closing the gap between small and large models at inference time, with linear (rather than quadratic) compute scaling. The uncertainty-based stopping criterion offers a practical mechanism for allocating test-time compute proportionally to expected gains, addressing a key scalability concern. Open questions include whether scaling laws analogous to pre-training exist for test-time fine-tuning, whether the approach extends to non-perplexity tasks (code generation, reasoning) and other model families (diffusion models), and whether embeddings extracted from the base model itself (rather than a surrogate) would further improve selection quality.
