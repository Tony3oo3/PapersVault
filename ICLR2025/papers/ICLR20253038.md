## Problem

Existing inverse folding methods (ESM-IF1, ProteinMPNN) are trained to maximize the probability of a single native sequence, ignoring the one-to-many nature of the structure-to-sequence mapping and producing narrow, low-diversity outputs. Additionally, their autoregressive transformer decoders require a full forward pass per amino acid, making high-throughput sampling of millions of sequences for virtual screening computationally infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253038/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D protein backbone coordinates as input and, in a single forward pass, outputs the parameters of a lightweight Potts (pairwise) model: fields H of shape L×q and couplings J of shape L×q×L×q, the latter stored in low-rank form via a tensor V of shape L×K×q (K≪L). These parameters define a probability distribution over all sequences of length L over q=20 amino acids, from which diverse sequences can be sampled on CPU without further neural network evaluation. The low-rank constraint reduces the coupling parameters from O(L²q²) to O(LqK), enabling linear-time pseudo-likelihood computation.

## Method

InvMSAFold is a two-stage architecture: a pre-trained GVP-GNN encoder (from ESM-IF1) produces rotationally invariant structure embeddings, which a 6-layer Transformer decoder maps to a low-rank tensor V (L×K×q). Couplings are factorized as J_{i,a,j,b} = (1/√K) Σ_k V_{i,k,a} V_{j,k,b}, and fields are obtained by contracting V over the latent dimension, both scaled to remain O(1). The key training innovation is using MSAs extracted from UniProt50 via MMseqs2 rather than single native sequences, so the loss is computed over a random subsample of homologous sequences, forcing the model to capture the full evolutionary sequence landscape. Two sampling variants are provided: InvMSAFold-PW (standard pairwise distribution trained with pseudo-likelihood, sampled via MCMC) and InvMSAFold-AR (autoregressive variant with tractable normalization, enabling direct i.i.d. sampling). The low-rank structure yields O(L) cost for both pseudo-likelihood evaluation and quadratic regularization, making training and inference dramatically faster than per-residue transformer decoding.

## Results

On the inter-cluster test set (hardest, unseen sequence clusters), InvMSAFold-AR achieves a median Pearson correlation of 0.53 between sampled and natural sequence covariances, versus 0.43 for InvMSAFold-PW and 0.31 for ESM-IF1. In terms of sequence-space coverage (KL divergence on PCA projections), InvMSAFold-AR achieves 0.49 (inter-cluster) and 0.67 (intra-cluster), dramatically outperforming ESM-IF1 (15.8 and 11.9) and InvMSAFold-PW (4.6 and 11.2). Sampling speed is orders of magnitude faster: InvMSAFold-AR on a single CPU core generates sequences virtually instantaneously compared to ESM-IF1 on a GPU, with batched sampling yielding near-constant time across sequence lengths up to 437 residues.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ESM-IF1, InvMSAFold-AR dominates on every metric: it achieves a 1.7× higher median covariance correlation (0.53 vs 0.31) on the inter-cluster set, a 32× lower KL divergence (0.49 vs 15.8) indicating far better coverage of the natural sequence space, and orders-of-magnitude faster sampling (CPU vs GPU). At high Hamming distances from the native sequence (>0.75), ESM-IF1's predicted structure quality (RMSD) degrades sharply while InvMSAFold-AR and InvMSAFold-PW remain robust. Against ProteinMPNN (tested in Appendix A.1), InvMSAFold-AR similarly outperforms on covariance reconstruction, with ProteinMPNN showing behavior analogous to ESM-IF1. InvMSAFold-PW, while better than ESM-IF1 on inter-cluster KL divergence (4.6 vs 15.8), underperforms InvMSAFold-AR on both covariance reconstruction and intra-cluster coverage, likely due to pseudo-likelihood training and MCMC sampling limitations.

## Relation to Prior Work

InvMSAFold builds on three key threads: the GVP-GNN structure encoder from ESM-IF1 (Hsu et al., 2022), the Potts/pairwise model family from statistical physics (Cocco et al., 2018), and the autoregressive variant of pairwise models from Trinquier et al. (2021). The key departure from prior inverse folding work (ESM-IF1, ProteinMPNN) is reframing the problem from single-sequence prediction to one-to-many generation: instead of training on the native sequence alone, the model is trained on full MSAs to capture the complete sequence landscape compatible with a structure. Architecturally, the two-stage design—a large encoder-decoder that outputs parameters of a lightweight pairwise model with low-rank couplings—differs fundamentally from the autoregressive decoding used by existing methods, enabling orders-of-magnitude faster sampling on CPUs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the single-forward-pass architecture enables high-throughput virtual screening on standard hardware, a setting where autoregressive transformer-based methods are computationally infeasible. The finding that a simple pairwise model trained on MSAs captures sequence diversity (multiple modes in sequence space, covariance structure) better than large transformer-based models trained on single sequences challenges the assumption that architectural complexity is necessary for landscape coverage. Open questions include whether the pairwise model family is expressive enough for all protein families (particularly those with strong higher-order epistasis), whether the approach extends to de novo design beyond homologous scaffolds, and how performance scales to very long or multi-domain proteins.
