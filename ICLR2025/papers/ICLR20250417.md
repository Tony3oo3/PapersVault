## Problem

Predicting high-resolution cellular morphological responses to perturbations remains largely unexplored: prior generative approaches operated on low-resolution single-cell crops or were restricted to molecular interventions only, while most computational biology efforts focused on transcriptomic-level changes rather than visual phenotypes. No existing framework could produce guided, high-resolution cell morphology predictions that generalize across both genetic and chemical perturbations at the full well scale.

## Input / Output

The model takes a perturbation embedding (512-dimensional gene embedding from scGPT for genetic perturbations, or RDKit-derived molecular fingerprint for chemical compounds) padded to match Stable Diffusion's prompt embedding shape, combined with a random noise vector sampled from a standard Gaussian. It produces a 512×512 RGB image representing the cell morphology under that perturbation, where multi-channel Cell Painting images (6-channel for RxRx1, 3-channel for BBBC021 and Rohban) are projected into three RGB channels before encoding. The perturbation embedding replaces the CLIP text encoder in the original Stable Diffusion pipeline, serving as the sole conditioning signal for the denoising U-Net.

## Method

MorphoDiff fine-tunes the pre-trained Stable Diffusion latent diffusion model (DDPM with a 2D U-Net) on Cell Painting images projected into a 4-channel 64×64 latent space via the SD VAE, replacing the CLIP text encoder with a perturbation projection module that maps gene IDs (via scGPT) or SMILES strings (via RDKit) into conditioning vectors. During training, the U-Net predicts the added Gaussian noise at each diffusion timestep conditioned on the perturbation embedding, the noisy latent, and the time step, optimized with MSE loss and stabilized by Exponential Moving Average. At inference, the model iteratively denoises from pure noise over T steps, with the perturbation embedding guiding each denoising step toward the target phenotype. The key architectural insight is that perturbation embeddings injected as cross-attention conditions into the U-Net enable the diffusion process to learn perturbation-specific morphological patterns while retaining the general image prior from natural-image pre-training, allowing generalization to unseen compounds via structural embedding similarity.

## Results

Across six experiments spanning three Cell Painting datasets (RxRx1, BBBC021, Rohban et al.), MorphoDiff consistently outperforms the unconditional Stable Diffusion baseline on both FID and KID. The largest improvement is on BBBC021 (All Compounds), where MorphoDiff achieves FID 1.99×10⁻² and KID 0.21 versus SD's 3.84×10⁻² and 0.47. On the smallest dataset (Rohban, 5 genes), MorphoDiff still improves FID from 3.26 to 2.51 (×10⁻²) and KID from 0.45 to 0.33. For generalization to unseen compounds, MorphoDiff achieves FID 1.83×10⁻² and KID 0.20 versus SD's 2.56×10⁻² and 0.30.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unconditional Stable Diffusion baseline, MorphoDiff wins on every experiment and both metrics (FID and KID), with the most dramatic gain on BBBC021 All Compounds (FID 1.99 vs. 3.84, a ~48% reduction) and the smallest on Rohban 12 Genes (FID 2.77 vs. 3.17). Against IMPA (Palma et al., 2023) on single-cell cropped patches (96×96), MorphoDiff outperforms IMPA on 4 of 5 compounds (AZ138: FID 0.82 vs. 0.98; AZ258: 0.76 vs. 1.18; Taxol: 1.09 vs. 1.28; Vincristine: 0.86 vs. 1.05) but loses on Cytochalasin B (FID 1.19 vs. 0.83 for SD, 1.23 for IMPA), where the authors attribute the gap to the advantage of larger field-of-view images in capturing intercellular relationships. On unseen-compound generalization, MorphoDiff improves FID from 2.56 to 1.83 (×10⁻²) and KID from 0.30 to 0.20 over SD, confirming that perturbation-conditioned generation transfers to structurally similar held-out molecules.

## Relation to Prior Work

MorphoDiff adapts the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion framework (Rombach et al., 2022) by replacing the CLIP text encoder with perturbation-specific embeddings—scGPT gene embeddings for genetic perturbations and RDKit molecular fingerprints for chemical compounds—thereby converting a text-to-image pipeline into a perturbation-to-cell-morphology generator. Prior generative approaches for cell morphology (Yang et al., 2021; Palma et al., 2023; Bourou et al., 2023) operated at lower resolution, on single-cell crops, or on only one perturbation modality, while representation-learning methods (Moshkov et al., 2024; Sanchez-Fernandez et al., 2023) lack generative capability. MorphoDiff's key departure is working at full 512×512 well scale with a unified conditioning interface that handles both siRNA knockdown and small-molecule treatments within a single diffusion architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling high-resolution, perturbation-conditioned image generation, MorphoDiff offers a path toward in silico screening across the vast chemical space (~10^60 drug-like molecules) that is infeasible to probe experimentally, potentially reducing cost and time in drug discovery. However, generalization to truly unseen compounds remains limited (e.g., Forskolin generated a phenotype resembling its nearest in-distribution neighbor rather than its own), and the pipeline's performance is tightly coupled to the quality of the perturbation encoder. Open questions include whether joint end-to-end training of the diffusion model and perturbation encoder would improve out-of-distribution generalization, how the method scales to combinatorial perturbations and additional covariates (concentration, cell type), and whether the full-well scale advantage over single-cell crops translates to downstream ML tasks.
