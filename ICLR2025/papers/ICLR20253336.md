## Problem

Modern vision backbones excel at general-purpose tasks but struggle with fine-grained, data-scarce personalized vision (e.g., recognizing a specific dog or shirt among many similar ones). While synthetic data has been shown to improve general-purpose representation learning, and personalized generation can produce novel images of a specific instance from just a few examples, no prior work has connected these two ideas to ask whether personalized synthetic data can train a personalized representation. This paper formalizes that challenge and introduces the first evaluation suite (PODS, plus reformulated DeepFashion2 and DogFaceNet) for instance-level personalized representation learning.

## Input / Output

The method requires only 3 real images of the target instance and its generic category label (e.g., 'shirt'); no annotations, masks, or additional data are needed in the base setting. The output is a LoRA-adapted ViT backbone whose CLS and patch embeddings serve as the personalized representation, probed directly (without task-specific heads) for one-vs-all classification (PR-AUC), retrieval (NDCG), detection (AP/F1), and segmentation (mask AP/F1). Evaluation is instance-level: each model is trained for a single object and tested against all other instances, distinguishing it from closed-set k-way classification benchmarks.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages: (1) DreamBooth fine-tunes Stable Diffusion 1.5 on the 3 real images to produce a personalized generator conditioned on a learned identifier token; (2) synthetic positives are generated from the personalized generator (with LLM-generated captions and varied CFG for diversity) while synthetic negatives are drawn from the unmodified pretrained generator conditioned on the generic category; (3) a ViT backbone (DINOv2, CLIP, or MAE) is LoRA fine-tuned with an infoNCE contrastive loss that pulls together embeddings of real and synthetic images of the target instance and pushes them apart from generic-category negatives. When segmentation masks are available, masked DreamBooth training (zeroing background gradients) and DreamSim-based filtering improve generation quality, and a Cut-and-Paste baseline (pasting the real foreground onto generated or real backgrounds) provides a computationally cheaper alternative that preserves object fidelity at the cost of pose diversity. The key insight is that the personalized generator acts as a controllable data augmentation engine whose fidelity-diversity tradeoff directly shapes the discriminative quality of the learned representation.

## Results

Personalized representations (LoRA fine-tuned on DreamBooth-generated synthetic data) outperform their pretrained counterparts in 32 out of 36 backbone-task-dataset combinations. Headline gains include DINOv2 detection improving by 48% relative (e.g., 11.0→12.6 mAP on PODS), DINOv2 retrieval by 32% (69.6→79.6 NDCG on PODS), and CLIP classification by 89% (26.7→47.4 PR-AUC on PODS). The sole exceptions are MAE retrieval on PODS (34.6→30.7) and DF2 (25.8→23.7), where personalization slightly degrades performance. Across all three datasets, improvements are consistent for both global tasks (classification, retrieval) and dense tasks (detection, segmentation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three pretrained vision backbones—DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023), CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), and MAE (He et al., 2022)—against their personalized counterparts (LoRA fine-tuned on DreamBooth synthetic data with infoNCE contrastive loss). DINOv2-P wins on 11/12 sub-tasks, with the only loss being Dogs classification (83.1→81.6), where the pretrained model already performs near-saturation. CLIP-P achieves the largest relative gains, particularly in classification (e.g., 26.7→47.4 on PODS, +89%) and retrieval (58.0→80.9 on Dogs, +39%), reflecting CLIP's weaker initial fine-grained discrimination. MAE-P improves on 10/12 sub-tasks but is the only backbone where retrieval degrades on PODS (34.6→30.7) and DF2 (25.8→23.7), suggesting MAE's pretrained features already encode some instance-level structure that contrastive fine-tuning disrupts. Overall, the personalized approach universally improves dense tasks (detection, segmentation) across all three backbones, with the most dramatic gains on CLIP (e.g., detection mAP 0.1→0.8 on PODS, a 7× improvement from a near-zero baseline).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper connects two previously separate research threads: (1) synthetic data for general-purpose representation learning (StableRep, Tian et al. 2023b), which showed that T2I-generated images paired with contrastive objectives yield strong visual encodings, and (2) personalized image generation (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion), which produces novel images of a specific instance from a few examples. The key conceptual move is using a personalized generator (DreamBooth) as the synthetic data source for contrastive fine-tuning of a general-purpose backbone (DINOv2, CLIP, MAE) via LoRA, thereby learning a *personalized* representation rather than a general one. It also builds on the PerSAM pipeline for dense prediction and on prior work showing that fidelity and diversity of generated data are critical for representation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces PODS, a new 100-object benchmark with controlled distribution shifts (pose, distractors, both), filling a gap in instance-level evaluation for personalized vision. Practically, it demonstrates that a user with only 3 real images can obtain a representation that substantially outperforms pretrained features across classification, retrieval, detection, and segmentation — with the important caveat that a simple Cut/Paste method (no T2I model required) achieves comparable performance at a fraction of the compute cost. Open questions include whether the biases inherited from T2I generators (e.g., DreamBooth's difficulty preserving fine-grained details) can be mitigated, how the approach scales as generative models improve, and whether the computational cost of DreamBooth is justified over cheaper alternatives in most real-world settings.
