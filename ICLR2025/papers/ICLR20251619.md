## Problem

High-fidelity simulation models are computationally prohibitive for real-time control, optimization, and long-term scenario analysis, motivating fast surrogate models with reduced state dimensionality. Standard Latent ODEs fail to achieve measurable dimensionality reduction when time-varying inputs are present, and existing model order reduction methods (DMD, TBR) require predefining the latent dimensionality or observable functions. The paper addresses this gap by combining VAE information bottlenecks with Neural ODE dynamics to enable adaptive, input-aware state reduction without a priori dimensionality choices.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an initial state vector x₀, piecewise-constant time-varying control inputs u(t), and time-invariant physical parameters p, and produces reconstructed state trajectories x̂(t) and output signals ŷ(t) decoded from a reduced latent space. Unlike standard MOR approaches, the effective latent dimensionality is not a user-specified hyperparameter but emerges automatically from the β-VAE's per-channel KL divergence, with only channels exceeding a D_KL threshold of 0.1 counted as active. For Koopman operator approximation, the output is a linear state-space model (matrices A, B) directly usable in model-predictive control, with the latent control dimension also learned (e.g., expanding a single physical input to 18 latent inputs).

## Method

B-NODE continuously propagates the variational parameters μ(x^z(t)) and σ(x^z(t)) through time via a Neural ODE, rather than assigning probability only to the initial latent state as in Latent ODEs. A non-hierarchical Gaussian prior N(0, I) is enforced at all discrete time steps, creating a fixed information bottleneck that enables measurable dimensionality reduction without predefining latent dimensions. The β parameter from β-VAE controls the trade-off: lower β favors reconstruction accuracy, higher β favors state reduction. During training, the learned variance σ is injected as stochastic noise into the Neural ODE's vector field via reparameterization, simultaneously serving as a regularization mechanism that improves ODE convergence. For Koopman operator approximation, the Neural ODE is simply replaced with linear matrices (A_μμ, B_μu), yielding a linear latent dynamics model whose dimensionality is again governed by β rather than manual selection.

## Results

On the four benchmark datasets, B-NODE (β=0.01) achieves RMSE of 0.02 (5 states) on SHF, 0.02 (11 states) on Power Plant, 0.28 (25 states) on MuJoCo, and 0.03 (8 states) on Water Hammer, while SS-NODE requires 16, 102, 27, and 400 states respectively for comparable or worse accuracy. For the Power Plant surrogate, B-NODE with β=0.05 and constant variance achieves the smallest reconstruction error among all tested models while requiring only 17 states instead of 102, and delivers a 32× CPU speed-up and 103× GPU speed-up over the original FMI simulation. In the Koopman operator setting, B-NODE matches eDMDc accuracy (RMSE 0.20) on the Power Plant with 25 states versus eDMDc's 45, and outperforms DMDc on the Small Grid CFD model (RMSE 0.48 vs. 0.60).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SS-NODE, B-NODE (β=0.01) achieves lower or comparable RMSE on all four datasets while reducing state dimension by 3–50× (e.g., Power Plant: 0.02 vs. 0.06 RMSE with 11 vs. 102 states; Water Hammer: 0.03 vs. 0.02 with 8 vs. 400 states). Against Latent ODE (Rubanova et al., 2019), B-NODE dramatically outperforms on all datasets with inputs, e.g., Power Plant RMSE 0.02 vs. 0.14 and Water Hammer 0.03 vs. 0.06, while also achieving true dimensionality reduction (Latent ODE fails to reduce dimensions below the offered 128/512). Against Truncated Balanced Realization (Moore, 1981) on the linear SHF model, B-NODE with 4 states achieves 0.3% RMSE versus TBR's 14.2%, a ~47× improvement. In the linear Koopman setting, B-NODE matches eDMDc (Korda & Mezić, 2018) on the Power Plant (both RMSE 0.20) but with fewer states (25 vs. 45), and outperforms DMDc (Proctor et al., 2016) on the Small Grid CFD model (0.48 vs. 0.60 RMSE). Notably, B-NODE with β=0.0 (no information bottleneck) fails catastrophically on the Power Plant (RMSE 18.2), confirming that the variational bottleneck is essential for stable training with time-varying inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

B-NODE sits at the intersection of three research threads: Neural ODEs for continuous-time dynamics (Chen et al., 2018), β-VAEs for information-bottleneck dimensionality reduction (Higgins et al., 2017), and Koopman/DMD methods for linear surrogate models (Brunton et al., 2021). The key architectural departure from Latent ODEs (Rubanova et al., 2019) is the continuous propagation of variational parameters (μ, σ) through time via a Neural ODE, rather than assigning a probability distribution only to the initial latent state. This is combined with a non-hierarchical Gaussian prior (unlike Deep Kalman Filters or hierarchical DVAEs) to enable measurable, time-consistent state reduction, following the DVAE structural conventions of Girin et al. (2020) while placing all temporal dynamics in the inference model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a single, automatable framework that handles both nonlinear model order reduction and linear Koopman operator approximation without requiring the practitioner to predefine latent dimensionality or tune observable functions—a common pain point in DMD/eDMDc pipelines. The demonstrated 32×/103× speedups over physical simulation and 10× reduction in ODE evaluations make it practically relevant for real-time control and batch optimization in energy and automotive domains. Open questions include scalability to very high-dimensional systems (beyond ~100 states), long-horizon prediction stability, and whether the β-based dimensionality control generalizes cleanly to multi-input, multi-output systems with strongly coupled dynamics.
