## Problem

State-of-the-art generalizable 3D reconstruction methods require accurate camera poses as input, typically obtained via SfM pipelines like COLMAP, making them impractical for sparse unposed real-world images. Existing pose-free alternatives decompose the task into sequential pose estimation followed by reconstruction, causing error compounding. This paper asks whether 3D scene reconstruction can be performed entirely without any pose information by directly predicting scene geometry in a shared coordinate frame.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251940/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as few as two unposed RGB images (processed at 256×256 or 512×512) along with their camera intrinsic parameters (focal length and principal point), which are the only geometric inputs required. It outputs a set of per-pixel 3D Gaussian primitives (center, opacity, quaternion rotation, scale, and spherical harmonic coefficients) all expressed in a single canonical coordinate system anchored to the first input view's camera frame. From these Gaussians, the method produces novel view renderings at arbitrary target poses and estimates the relative camera pose between input views via a two-stage PnP-plus-photometric-refinement pipeline.

## Method

NoPoSplat uses a pure ViT encoder (ViT-Large) and decoder (ViT-Base) with cross-attention between views, deliberately omitting geometric priors such as epipolar constraints or cost volumes. The key architectural choice is predicting all views' Gaussians directly in a canonical space (the first view's camera coordinates) rather than the conventional transform-then-fuse pipeline, which eliminates pose dependency and yields more coherent multi-view fusion especially under limited overlap. Camera intrinsics are injected as a single global token (via a linear projection) concatenated with image tokens to resolve the inherent scale ambiguity of appearance-only reconstruction. Two DPT-based prediction heads produce Gaussian centers and remaining parameters respectively, with an RGB shortcut into the second head to preserve fine texture. Training uses only photometric supervision (MSE + 0.05·LPIPS) on rendered novel views, requiring no ground-truth depth, enabling training on large-scale video datasets.

## Results

On RealEstate10K (average overlap), NoPoSplat achieves 25.03 PSNR / 0.838 SSIM / 0.160 LPIPS, outperforming the best pose-required method MVSplat (23.98 / 0.811 / 0.176) by over 1 dB PSNR. The advantage is most pronounced under small image overlap (5–30%), where NoPoSplat reaches 22.51 PSNR versus 20.35 for MVSplat and 20.26 for pixelSplat. For relative pose estimation on RE10K, the scaled model (Ours*) achieves AUC@5° of 0.691, surpassing RoMa (0.546), MASt3R (0.372), and DUSt3R (0.301). On ACID, NoPoSplat also leads all pose-required baselines with 25.96 PSNR average, exceeding pixelSplat (25.82) and MVSplat (25.51).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoPoSplat decisively outperforms all pose-free baselines: it exceeds CoPoNeRF by ~6.1 dB PSNR (25.03 vs. 18.94) and Splatt3R by ~9.7 dB on RE10K average, with the gap widening under small overlap. Against pose-required SOTA, NoPoSplat surpasses MVSplat (23.98) and pixelSplat (23.85) by 1.05 and 1.19 dB PSNR respectively on average, and by 2.16 and 2.25 dB under small overlap, demonstrating that the canonical-space Gaussian prediction eliminates the misalignment inherent in transform-then-fuse pipelines. For pose estimation, NoPoSplat (Ours*) achieves AUC@5° of 0.691 on RE10K, beating RoMa (0.546) by 14.5 points and MASt3R (0.372) by 31.9 points, without requiring ground-truth depth or explicit matching loss during training. On the out-of-distribution ScanNet-1500 benchmark, NoPoSplat (0.318 AUC@5°) also surpasses RoMa (0.270), which was explicitly trained on ScanNet. On ACID, NoPoSplat (25.96 PSNR) edges out pixelSplat (25.82) and MVSplat (25.51), confirming the advantage generalizes to outdoor aerial scenes. The method also outperforms LEAP on object-level Objaverse reconstruction (28.38 vs. 20.56 PSNR) and achieves 66 fps inference, roughly 5× faster than pixelSplat.

## Relation to Prior Work

NoPoSplat builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the scene representation and draws conceptual inspiration from DUSt3R/MASt3R's canonical-space prediction, but departs fundamentally from both the pose-required transform-then-fuse pipeline (pixelSplat, MVSplat) and the sequential pose-estimation-then-reconstruction approach of prior pose-free methods (CoPoNeRF, Splatt3R). The key architectural departure is using a pure ViT backbone with no geometric priors (no epipolar constraints, no cost volumes) and training exclusively with photometric loss, eliminating the need for ground truth depth or metric poses. The camera intrinsic token embedding to resolve scale ambiguity is a novel design choice not previously explored in the Gaussian splatting context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges the prevailing assumption that accurate camera poses are necessary for high-quality generalizable 3D reconstruction, demonstrating that a pose-free feed-forward model can match or exceed pose-required SOTA, particularly under limited view overlap. The practical implications are significant: real-time inference (66 fps), no SfM pipeline dependency, and direct applicability to in-the-wild unposed images (phone photos, Sora-generated frames) open pathways for consumer-facing 3D capture and text-to-3D pipelines. Open questions include extending to dynamic scenes, removing the camera intrinsic requirement, and whether the non-generative nature limits reconstruction of occluded regions—addressing these would broaden the method's applicability to complex real-world environments.
