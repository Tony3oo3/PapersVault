## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of the edge of stability (EOS) rely on scalar losses or shallow models that cannot explain two key empirical phenomena in deep networks: (i) why shallow architectures or simple tasks fail to exhibit progressive sharpening, and (ii) why loss oscillations and catapults are confined to the span of top NTK eigenvectors. This paper addresses the gap by providing a fine-grained, closed-form analysis of gradient descent dynamics in deep linear networks (DLNs) under the deep matrix factorization loss, specifically in the regime beyond the EOS where sustained oscillations occur.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target low-rank matrix M* ∈ R^{d×d} (rank r with distinct singular values), a learning rate η, an initialization scale α (with α < σ*_{r}), and network depth L, and produces: (1) the complete set of non-zero Hessian eigenvalues at the balanced minimum, revealing that sharpness equals Lσ*_{1}^{2−2/L} and thus scales with depth; (2) the exact dimension p of the oscillation subspace, determined by the learning rate satisfying K'_p < K < S_p where S_p = Lσ*_{p}^{2−2/L}; and (3) the oscillation amplitudes ρ_{i,1}, ρ_{i,2} defined as roots of a degree-L polynomial g(ρ_i) = 0. The analysis requires the singular vectors to lie in the Singular Vector Stationary Set, which both balanced and unbalanced initializations satisfy (the latter from t ≥ 1).

## Method

The paper introduces the Singular Vector Stationary Set (SVS) to decouple singular vector dynamics from singular value dynamics, reducing the problem to scalar loss minimization over singular values. The key insight is that the symmetry-induced conservation law for gradient flow—the balancing gap among singular values across layers—breaks at EOS and decays monotonically to zero under GD beyond the stability threshold, driving the network toward the flattest (balanced) global minimum. The authors then characterize all Hessian eigenvalues at the balanced minimum, proving sharpness scales as Lσ*_{1}^{2−2/L} and explaining why deeper networks enter EOS at smaller learning rates. The central result (Theorem 1) proves that for learning rates in the range K'_p < K < S_p, the top-p singular values of the end-to-end DLN undergo a 2-period orbit around the balanced minimum within a rank-p subspace, with oscillation amplitudes given by roots of an explicit polynomial. This period-doubling route to chaos in both singular values and Hessian eigenvalues provides a mechanistic explanation for why oscillations concentrate in top feature directions and why mild sharpening occurs when the target matrix's dominant singular value is too small to push sharpness above 2/η.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is Theorem 1, which proves that for a depth-L DLN fitting a rank-r target matrix, the top-p singular values undergo a 2-period orbit around the balanced minimum when the learning rate satisfies K_p' < K < S_p, where S_p = Lσ_{★,p}^{2-2/L} and K_p' = max{S_{p+1}, S_p/(2√2)}. Lemma 1 characterizes all non-zero Hessian eigenvalues at the balanced minimum, showing the sharpness equals Lσ_{★,1}^{2-2/L} and scales with network depth. Proposition 2 proves the balancing gap |σ_{L,i}²(t+1) − σ_{ℓ,i}²(t+1)| decreases monotonically to zero under GD beyond EOS, with a contraction constant c ∈ (0,1]. Experiments on a 3-layer DLN (d=50, rank-3 target with singular values 10, 9.5, 9) confirm that oscillations appear exclusively in the predicted rank-p subspaces and that the bifurcation plot exhibits a period-doubling route to chaos.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without traditional method-vs-baseline comparisons. The closest comparisons are: (1) The paper extends Chen & Bruna (2023), who proved oscillatory GD dynamics for scalar functions beyond the stability threshold, to the full deep matrix factorization setting, showing the same period-doubling-to-chaos behavior emerges in DLNs with the oscillation subspace dimension precisely controlled by the learning rate. (2) It extends Kreisler et al. (2023), who showed gradient flow conserves the balancing gap, by proving that GD beyond EOS breaks this conservation and drives the gap to zero monotonically. (3) The paper contrasts DLN oscillation behavior with nonlinear network catapults: DLNs (with benign landscapes containing only saddles and global minima) exhibit sustained periodic oscillations around the global minimum, while nonlinear networks with negative Hessian eigenvalues at minima exhibit catapults that escape to flatter regions. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and MNIST with 4-layer ReLU MLPs confirm that oscillations in nonlinear networks also concentrate in top singular value directions, consistent with the DLN theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the edge-of-stability (EOS) analysis from scalar and two-layer settings (Chen & Bruna, 2023; Zhu et al., 2023b) to deep linear networks (DLNs) in the deep matrix factorization problem, adopting proof techniques from Kreisler et al. (2023) to characterize layer balancing. It departs from prior EOS work by focusing specifically on the regime *beyond* the stability threshold (η > 2/β), where sustained oscillations replace transient catapults, and by connecting the period-doubling route to chaos (a concept from nonlinear dynamics) to the singular value and Hessian eigenvalue dynamics of DLNs. The analysis builds on the DLN literature (Saxe et al., 2014; Arora et al., 2019) but is the first to characterize the full Hessian spectrum at the balanced minimum and its depth-dependence, providing a theoretical explanation for observations in nonlinear networks (Cohen et al., 2021; Zhu et al., 2023a) that prior simplified analyses could not capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical mechanism explaining two long-standing empirical puzzles: why shallow networks and simple tasks exhibit mild (or no) sharpening, and why oscillations concentrate in top feature subspaces. The finding that the symmetry-induced balancing gap—a conservation law for gradient flow—breaks precisely at EOS and decays monotonically to zero offers a principled explanation for the implicit walk toward flatter minima without explicit regularization. Key limitations include the restriction to linear networks (where the Hessian is PSD and no spurious minima exist), the focus on the steady-state 2-period orbit rather than the full transient dynamics, and the assumption that singular vectors remain stationary. Open questions include whether the period-doubling structure persists in nonlinear networks with negative Hessian eigenvalues, how other symmetries (softmax, batch-norm) affect the alignment conditions, and whether the depth-dependent sharpness scaling holds in practice for very deep architectures.
