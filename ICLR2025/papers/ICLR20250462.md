## Problem

Gaussian Process Latent Variable Models (GP-LVM) learn nonlinear latent representations but are inherently over-smooth for inhomogeneous data with abrupt changes or sharp contrast, since the Gaussian prior (q=2) offers no mechanism to control representation complexity. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a tunable regularization parameter q>0 that allows the learned latent space to contract or expand, yielding more interpretable and compact representations for structured data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-dimensional data matrix Y ∈ R^{N×D} (e.g., 12-dimensional oil flow observations, 3D Swiss roll point clouds, or 784-dimensional MNIST images) and produces a low-dimensional latent representation X ∈ R^{N×Q} with Q≪D, typically 2–10 dimensions. For prediction, a new test point y_* is appended to the training set to compute a predictive density via the ELBO difference. The latent dimension Q is not fixed a priori but can be inferred automatically by thresholding the ARD length-scales γ, and the optimal q* is selected jointly via a Gamma(4,2) prior.

## Method

QEP-LVM replaces the Gaussian process prior in GP-LVM with a Q-exponential process (Q-EP) parameterized by q>0, where the q-exponential distribution imposes L_q regularization on the latent function, reducing to the standard GP when q=2. The Bayesian training uses sparse variational inference with M inducing points, following the SVGP framework of Hensman et al. (2015) to compute the variational distribution of the latent function directly rather than via variational calculus. Because the q-exponential log-likelihood is no longer quadratic in the data, the ELBO is derived by applying Jensen's inequality twice to bound the expected log-likelihood from below, yielding a tractable objective with computational complexity O(NM²). The paper also establishes a theoretical connection to probabilistic PCA, deriving a closed-form MLE for the latent singular values (Theorem 3.1) that shows how c(q) regularizes the latent space geometry, and demonstrates that optimizing q via a Gamma prior automatically selects values (e.g., q*=1.39 for Swiss roll, q*=1.15 for oil flow) that produce more separated and interpretable latent clusters than the fixed q=2 GP-LVM.

## Results

On the oil flow dataset (12-dim, 3 classes), QEP-LVM with the Bayesian-optimal q*=1.15 achieves 73% accuracy and 0.89 AUC, outperforming GP-LVM (q=2.0) at 68% accuracy and 0.83 AUC. On MNIST (28×28 digits, 10 classes), QEP-LVM (q=1.5) reaches 97.3% accuracy and 0.986 AUC versus 96.5% and 0.981 for GP-LVM, with clustering scores (ARI 0.943 vs 0.923; NMI 0.952 vs 0.938) also favoring QEP-LVM. The optimal q* is found automatically via a Gamma(4,2) prior, yielding q*=1.39 on Swiss roll and q*=1.15 on oil flow, both below the GP-LVM value of 2.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QEP-LVM consistently outperforms GP-LVM (q=2.0) across all quantitative metrics on both oil flow and MNIST: on oil flow, the optimal q*=1.15 gains +5 points in accuracy (73% vs 68%) and +0.06 in AUC (0.89 vs 0.83); on MNIST, q=1.5 gains +0.8 points in accuracy (97.3% vs 96.5%) and +0.005 in AUC. Against VAE, QEP-LVM produces substantially more structured and interpretable latent representations in all visual comparisons (Swiss roll, oil flow, MNIST, robot learning), whereas VAE yields unstructured or PCA-like projections. Against PCA, QEP-LVM captures the nonlinear manifold structure (e.g., the 'roll' shape in Swiss roll) that PCA compresses linearly. On the robot learning task, only QEP-LVM (q=1.0) and beta-VAE correctly identify the intrinsic 3-DOF dimensionality, while GP-LVM and standard VAE produce dispersed, unstructured latent spaces. The regularization effect is monotonic: smaller q contracts the latent space toward axes, with the Bayesian-optimal q* (typically 1.15–1.5) striking the best balance between compactness and representational fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends GP-LVM (Lawrence 2003; Titsias & Lawrence 2010) by replacing the Gaussian process prior with the Q-exponential process (Q-EP, Li et al. 2023), a stochastic process that generalizes GP via an Lq-regularization parameter q > 0. The variational inference framework follows the sparse inducing-point approach of Titsias (2009) and the ELBO derivation strategy of Hensman et al. (2015), but requires Jensen's inequality in place of variational calculus because the q-exponential log-likelihood is no longer quadratic. Unlike other non-Gaussian LVMs in the literature (Palmer et al. 2005; Gundersen et al. 2021; Zhang et al. 2023) that construct non-Gaussianity through compositions of GPs, QEP-LVM is built directly on a non-Gaussian stochastic process, and the paper establishes an explicit MLE connection to probabilistic PCA (Tipping & Bishop 1999) that has no analogue in those works.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, single-parameter (q) mechanism to control the compactness and interpretability of learned latent representations, showing that GP-LVM (q=2) is systematically sub-optimal for inhomogeneous data with abrupt structure. Two open questions remain: (1) automatic selection of the optimal q* is handled heuristically via a Gamma prior rather than a theoretically grounded criterion, and (2) the model cannot handle missing data in a tractable way, unlike GP-LVM. A natural future direction is extending QEP-LVM to deep Q-EP architectures (analogous to deep GP), which the authors flag for a separate paper. The practical impact is currently limited to the GP/LVM research community, as the method requires kernel-based inference and does not scale to the very large datasets where deep generative models dominate.
