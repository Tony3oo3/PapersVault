## Problem

Existing multimodal self-supervised methods (e.g., CLIP) maximize mutual information across modalities but fail to separate shared from modality-specific information, limiting interpretability and counterfactual generation. Prior information-theoretic disentanglement approaches lack rigorous optimality guarantees for the common real-world scenario where the Minimum Necessary Information (MNI) point is unattainable due to deep entanglement of shared and specific components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250114/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired multimodal observations (X^1, X^2) — ranging from text+image, text+tabular data, to molecular structure+cell phenotype (L1000 gene expression or CellProfiler image features). It produces, per modality, a shared representation (Z_c^1, Z_c^2) inferred independently from each modality via vMF-distributed encoders on the unit sphere, and a modality-specific representation (Z_s^1, Z_s^2) that is conditioned on both the raw observation and the corresponding shared representation, preserving the V-structure dependency in the generative model.

## Method

DISENTANGLEDSSL employs a two-step sequential optimization grounded in the conditional entropy bottleneck. Step 1 learns shared representations by maximizing I(Z^1;X^2) − β·I(Z^1;X^1|X^2), implemented with InfoNCE for the mutual information term and a von Mises–Fisher KL-divergence upper bound for the conditional mutual information term; the hyperparameter β bijectively maps to the information constraint δ_c, enabling controlled trade-off between expressivity and redundancy even when MNI is unattainable. Step 2 learns modality-specific representations by maximizing I(Z^1, Ẑ_c^{2*}; X^1) − λ·I(Z^1; Ẑ_c^{1*}), using InfoNCE with data augmentations for the joint coverage term and a batch-level orthogonal (Frobenius-norm) loss to enforce disentanglement from the shared representation. The paper proves that this procedure yields optimal (or near-optimal, bounded by δ_c) disentangled representations in both MNI-attainable and MNI-unattainable regimes, establishing the first such guarantees for multimodal self-supervised disentanglement.

## Results

On the MultiBench multimodal prediction benchmark, DISENTANGLEDSSL achieves the best linear probing accuracy across all five datasets, reaching 66.44% on MIMIC, 77.45% on MOSEI, 65.16% on MOSI, 64.24% on UR-FUNNY, and 61.60% on MUSTARD. On the high-content drug screening retrieval task, DISENTANGLEDSSL attains the highest MRR of 0.111 on RXRX19a and 0.163 on LINCS, with top-1 accuracy of 4.03% and 4.48% respectively. For disentanglement quality measured by Reconstruction Gain (RG), DISENTANGLEDSSL achieves the highest scores across both modalities and both drug screening datasets (e.g., 0.591 RG-phenotype on RXRX19a vs. 0.547 for FOCAL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISENTANGLEDSSL consistently outperforms all six baselines on the MultiBench prediction task, with the largest margin on MUSTARD (61.60% vs. 58.21% for FOCAL and 57.73% for JointOpt), where modality-specific information (e.g., sarcasm cues) is critical. On the drug screening retrieval task, DISENTANGLEDSSL beats CLIP by 0.73% top-1 accuracy and 0.008 MRR on RXRX19a, and by 0.53% top-1 and 0.017 MRR on LINCS; the closest competitor is JointOpt (4.67% top-1 on LINCS), which slightly edges DISENTANGLEDSSL (4.48%) on that single metric but trails on all other N values and MRR. FOCAL and DMVAE are consistently outperformed on both retrieval and prediction tasks. The step-by-step optimization of DISENTANGLEDSSL clearly surpasses the joint optimization variant (JointOpt) in the simulation study, confirming that sequential learning of shared then specific representations is more effective. For disentanglement quality (Reconstruction Gain), DISENTANGLEDSSL achieves the highest RG across all four modality-dataset combinations, with the largest gap over JointOpt on LINCS molecule (0.143 vs. 0.103), indicating substantially lower redundancy between shared and specific representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Conditional Entropy Bottleneck (CEB) framework of Fischer (2020) from supervised single-modality settings to the multimodal self-supervised regime, where two data modalities are neither deterministic functions of each other nor independent. It builds on the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 2000) and the multi-view IB upper bound of Federici et al. (2019) for tractable optimization, but departs from prior multimodal disentanglement methods (FactorCL, FOCAL, DMVAE, SimMMDG) by providing the first rigorous optimality guarantees for learned representations when the Minimum Necessary Information point is unattainable—a scenario the authors argue is the norm in real-world multimodal data. The step-by-step (sequential) optimization strategy also contrasts with joint-optimization approaches like Pan et al. (2021), which the paper shows to be suboptimal in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a notable theoretical gap: prior multimodal disentanglement methods lack formal characterizations of what 'optimal' means when shared and modality-specific information are irreducibly entangled, which is the common real-world case. The bijection between the Lagrange multiplier β and the information constraint δ_c gives practitioners a principled, interpretable knob for controlling the granularity of shared information, with direct applications to interpretability in drug screening and counterfactual generation. Limitations include reliance on approximations (InfoNCE, vMF KL bound, batch-level orthogonal loss) whose finite-sample behavior relative to the theoretical guarantees is not fully characterized, and the framework is restricted to two modalities. Open questions include extension to more than two modalities, the role of architecture in achieving the theoretical optima, and whether the sequential training strategy generalizes to asymmetric modality pairs.
