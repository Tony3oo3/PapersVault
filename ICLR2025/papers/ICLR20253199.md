## Problem

There is a significant gap between theoretical convergence guarantees for score-based diffusion models and their practical performance: prior bounds require iteration complexity on the order of d/ε² or d^(5/12)ε^(-1) (under smoothness), yet in practice only ~50 sampling steps suffice for high-quality generation on CIFAR-10 (d=3072) and ImageNet (d≈196608). This paper narrows that gap by establishing an improved iteration complexity of L·d^(1/3)·ε^(-2/3) under Lipschitz-continuous score functions, improving over prior smoothness-based results by a factor of L^(2/3)·d^(1/12)·ε^(-1/3).

## Input / Output

The sampler takes a d-dimensional Gaussian noise vector Y_0 ~ N(0, I_d) and access to pre-trained score function estimates s_t(x) at each intermediate diffusion step, where the estimates satisfy a bounded averaged ℓ₂ error ε_score and a uniform Lipschitz constant L. The output is a single sample Y_K whose distribution is within ε total variation distance of the target data distribution p_data. The analysis requires the target distribution to have a bounded second moment (mildly heavy-tailed distributions are excluded), and the improvement over prior work holds when L < d^(2/3)ε^(-4/3) (for ε > d^(-1/2)) or L < d^(2/3)ε^(-1/3) (for ε < d^(-1/2)).

## Method

The core algorithm is a randomized midpoint discretization of the probability flow ODE, where the learning-rate schedule is uniformly randomized within each sub-interval to enable efficient ODE integration. The key analytical insight is a two-step reduction: first, noise is injected into the probability flow ODE so that the sampling task becomes one of discretizing a deterministic ODE with controlled error (Lemma 2); second, randomized midpoints (originally from Shen & Lee, 2019 for log-concave sampling) are used to approximate the ODE solution with fewer evaluations per step. Unlike prior stepwise error-propagation analyses, the proof must track error accumulation across multiple sub-steps simultaneously, requiring uniform (rather than high-probability) control that is enabled by the Lipschitz smoothness assumption. The resulting bound is TV(p_data, p_{Y_K}) ≤ C·L^(3/2)·d^(1/2)·log^(5/2)T / T^(3/2) + C·ε_score·log^(1/2)T, yielding the stated iteration complexity. A parallel variant updates all N sub-steps simultaneously over M rounds, achieving ε-accuracy in O(log²(Ld/ε)) parallel rounds with only Õ(d^(1/3)ε^(-2/3)) processors.

## Results

The paper establishes an iteration complexity of $\tilde{O}(L\, d^{1/3}\, \varepsilon^{-2/3})$ for $\varepsilon$-accurate sampling (in total variation) under L-smooth score functions, improving upon the prior best of $\tilde{O}(L^{5/3}\, d^{5/12}\, \varepsilon^{-1})$ (Gupta et al., 2024) and $\tilde{O}(L^2\, d^{1/2}\, \varepsilon^{-1})$ (Chen et al., 2024b). The score estimation error enters only as $\varepsilon_{\text{score}}\log^{1/2}T$, which is tighter than the $L^{1/2}d^{1/12}\varepsilon_{\text{score}}\log T$ and $L^{1/2}\varepsilon_{\text{score}}\log T$ dependencies in prior smooth-score analyses. For parallel sampling, the method requires $O(\log^2(Ld/\varepsilon))$ parallel rounds with only $\tilde{O}(d^{1/3}\varepsilon^{-2/3})$ processors, significantly reducing the processor count compared to prior parallel diffusion samplers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is theoretical, benchmarking the new $\tilde{O}(L\, d^{1/3}\, \varepsilon^{-2/3})$ bound against two prior smooth-score results. Against Gupta et al. (2024), the improvement factor is $L^{2/3}\, d^{1/12}\, \varepsilon^{-1/3}$, which is meaningful for high-dimensional, high-accuracy regimes (e.g., ImageNet with $d \approx 200{,}000$). Against Chen et al. (2024b), the improvement is $L\, d^{1/6}\, \varepsilon^{-1/3}$, again favoring large $d$ and small $\varepsilon$. The score-error dependency is also strictly better: the paper's $\varepsilon_{\text{score}}\log^{1/2}T$ avoids the extra $L^{1/2}$ and $d^{1/12}$ factors present in both prior works. In the general (non-smooth) setting, the paper's bound is superior to Benton et al. (2023)'s $\tilde{O}(d\,\varepsilon^{-2})$ and Li et al. (2024c)'s $\tilde{O}(d/\varepsilon + d^2)$ whenever the Lipschitz constant satisfies $L < d^{2/3}\varepsilon^{-4/3}$ (for $\varepsilon > d^{-1/2}$) or $L < d^{2/3}\varepsilon^{-1/3}$ (for $\varepsilon < d^{-1/2}$), conditions the authors argue are met in typical image-generation settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the randomized midpoint method introduced by Shen & Lee (2019) for log-concave sampling and subsequently adapted to diffusion models by Gupta et al. (2024), but develops a new analysis framework tailored to the probability flow ODE discretization that yields a tighter bound. It positions itself against two lines of prior work: (i) smooth-score-function analyses by Gupta et al. (2024) and Chen et al. (2024b), which achieve $\tilde{O}(L^{5/3} d^{5/12} \varepsilon^{-1})$, and (ii) general (non-smooth) analyses by Benton et al. (2023) and Li et al. (2024c), which give $\tilde{O}(d\varepsilon^{-2})$ and $\tilde{O}(d/\varepsilon + d^2)$ respectively. The key departure is a new three-step proof structure—introducing an auxiliary ODE sequence, controlling per-step KL divergence via a randomized-midpoint discretization error bound, and bounding initialization/early-stopping terms—that avoids the stepwise error-propagation analysis used in prior works and instead tracks errors across multiple steps simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The improved rate $L d^{1/3}\varepsilon^{-2/3}$ narrows the well-known gap between theoretical step requirements and the ~50 steps used in practice for CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, though it does not fully close it. The result is conditional on Lipschitz continuity of score functions, and the paper explicitly notes that achieving sub-linear dimension dependence without smoothness remains open. Additional open questions include extending the randomized-midpoint analysis to SDE-based samplers (where inherent stochasticity complicates the deterministic ODE argument) and applying the framework to other samplers such as Langevin dynamics. For practitioners, the practical impact is indirect: the theory validates that smooth score functions (which neural networks tend to produce) can justify far fewer sampling steps than prior bounds suggested.
