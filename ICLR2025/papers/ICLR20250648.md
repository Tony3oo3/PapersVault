## Problem

Multimodal LLMs struggle with text-rich visual understanding—interpreting environments where dense text is tightly integrated with visual elements. Existing web-based data sources either introduce noise through rule-based extraction, impose rigid HTML formats that limit generalization, or overlook the rich text-visual interactions present in web UIs, leaving a gap in large-scale, diverse training data for this capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250648/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes high-resolution webpage screenshots (rendered on simulated Windows 10 and iPhone 12 Pro devices, horizontally cropped to height-width ratios of [0.5, 1.5] for desktop and [1.5, 2.5] for mobile) paired with a text instruction or question. Outputs are task-dependent: natural-language captions, short and detailed QA answers, normalized bounding box coordinates (floating-point 0–1 or quantized integers 0–999), click positions, extracted OCR text, and predicted post-click page titles in a multiple-choice format.

## Method

The core contribution is MultiUI, a 7.3M-sample dataset synthesized by feeding cleaned accessibility trees (structured text representations of webpages with element types, OCR text, and bounding boxes) to text-based LLMs—Llama-3-70B-Instruct for most tasks and GPT-4o-mini for embedded image captioning—to generate diverse multimodal instructions across nine tasks in three categories (visual understanding/reasoning, text recognition, grounding). The construction pipeline spans four stages: Playwright-based scraping of 1.1M websites from FineWeb URLs, LLM-based curation filtering ~10% harmful/invalid pages, task extraction with dynamic in-context examples and dual-format answers, and instruction template diversification via GPT-4o paraphrasing. Training follows a two-stage strategy: Stage 1 fine-tunes the backbone (Qwen2-7B, Vicuna-7B, or Llama-3.1-8B with CLIP/Siglip vision encoders and dynamic high-resolution patching) on 95% of MultiUI for GUI knowledge, then Stage 2 continues with general visual instruction data (LLaVA-1.5 or LLaVA-NeXT) plus the remaining 5% of MultiUI to preserve general multimodal capabilities.

## Results

UIX-Qwen2 (7B) achieves 75.9 on VisualWebBench, surpassing GPT-4V (64.6), Gemini 1.5 Pro (64.8), and LLaVA-1.6-34B (50.5). Training on MultiUI yields up to a 48.0-point improvement on VisualWebBench (UIX-Vicuna: 71.1 vs. LLaVA-Vicuna: 23.1) and a 19.1-point boost in element accuracy on Mind2Web (UIX-Qwen2-M2W: 43.4 vs. LLaVA-Qwen2-M2W: 24.3). On out-of-domain OCR benchmarks, UIX-Qwen2 reaches 85.3 on DocVQA, 74.0 on ChartQA, and 66.3 on OCRBench, outperforming document-specialized models like DocOwl 1.5 (82.2, 70.2, –) and DocOwl 2 (80.7, 70.0, –).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UIX-Qwen2 (7B) outperforms GPT-4V on VisualWebBench (75.9 vs. 64.6) and ScreenSpot (55.2 vs. 16.2), and beats GPT-4o on ScreenSpot (55.2 vs. 18.3). Against GUI-specialized models, UIX-Qwen2 surpasses SeeClick on VisualWebBench (75.9 vs. 9.7) and CogAgent on VisualWebBench (75.9 vs. 28.7) and ScreenSpot (55.2 vs. 47.4). On the Mind2Web agent task, UIX-Qwen2-M2W achieves 43.4 element accuracy in the cross-task setting, exceeding SeeClick (28.3†) and CogAgent (30.2) by 15+ points. On out-of-domain OCR benchmarks, UIX-Qwen2 outperforms DocOwl 1.5 on DocVQA (85.3 vs. 82.2), ChartQA (74.0 vs. 70.2), and TextVQA (72.7 vs. 68.6), and beats DocOwl 2 on DocVQA (85.3 vs. 80.7) and ChartQA (74.0 vs. 70.0). Compared to LLaVA baselines with the same backbone, MultiUI training yields 34–48 point gains on VisualWebBench and 43–65 point gains on element grounding, while maintaining or slightly improving general multimodal scores (MMMU, MMBench, VQAv2). The only comparisons where baselines retain an edge are ScreenAI's task-specific fine-tuned scores on WebSRC (87.2* vs. 82.9) and ScreenQA-Short (94.8* vs. 78.8), though these involve dedicated fine-tuning on those benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior web-based multimodal data collection (LAION, MMC4, OBELICS) that extracts image-text pairs without capturing UI layout structure, and from screenshot-parsing approaches (Pix2Struct, S4) that impose rigid HTML-like formats limiting cross-domain generalization. The key methodological departure is using text-only LLMs to process accessibility trees—structured, element-level textual representations of webpages—and synthesizing diverse multimodal instructions from them, which are then paired with screenshots for instruction-tuning. This builds on the LLaVA visual instruction-tuning paradigm but introduces a novel data synthesis pipeline that leverages the hierarchical semantics of accessibility trees rather than raw HTML, alt-text, or GPT-4-generated captions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.3M-sample MultiUI dataset provides a large, diverse, and open-source resource for training MLLMs on text-rich visual understanding, and the finding that web UI training generalizes to non-UI domains (document understanding, OCR, chart interpretation) suggests that structured visual data from one domain can serve as a proxy for broader visual-textual reasoning skills. The two-stage training strategy that preserves general multimodal capabilities while adding GUI knowledge is a practical recipe likely to be widely adopted. Open questions include whether this approach extends to other structured visual domains (mobile apps, desktop software), whether the accessibility tree is the optimal textual representation for LLM-based synthesis, and whether the cross-domain gains persist as base model capabilities continue to improve.
