## Problem

Existing non-autoregressive Masked Image Modeling (MIM) methods like MaskGIT and MUSE are capped at 512×512 resolution and lag significantly behind diffusion models such as SDXL in image quality, intricate detailing, and conceptual fidelity. The paper addresses both the resolution constraint and the performance gap, aiming to make MIM competitive with state-of-the-art diffusion models at 1024×1024 while remaining efficient enough to run on consumer-grade 8 GB GPUs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252668/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (encoded by a CLIP ViT-H-14 encoder with 1024-dim latents), optional micro-conditions (original image resolution, crop coordinates, and human preference score as sinusoidal embeddings), and for editing tasks an original image converted to discrete VQ tokens with an optional mask. A 1024×1024 image is encoded into a 64×64 grid of 8192-entry codebook tokens (f=16 downsampling), which are compressed to 32×32 via 2D convolutional feature-compression layers before transformer processing. Inference uses classifier-free guidance (CFG=9) over 48 iterative denoising steps, and the full 1B-parameter model fits within 8 GB VRAM without additional optimizations.

## Method

Meissonic builds on the Multi-modal Transformer framework (Sauer et al., 2024) but introduces a hybrid architecture with a ~1:2 ratio of multi-modal to single-modal transformer blocks, where early multi-modal layers capture cross-modal text–image interactions and later single-modal layers refine visual representations for training stability. Two key architectural innovations distinguish it from prior MIM work: (1) Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) applied to query and key features to preserve spatial detail at high resolution, and (2) the masking rate r (sampled from a truncated arccos distribution) injected as a dynamic sampling condition so the model knows its current stage in the iterative refinement loop. Feature compression layers (2D convolutions) reduce the 64×64 token grid to 32×32 before the transformer and decompress afterward, enabling 1024×1024 generation without multi-stage cascading. Training follows a four-stage progressive curriculum (256² → 512² → 1024² → 1024² aesthetic refinement) over ~210 M curated images in roughly 48 H100 GPU-days, using cross-entropy loss, QK-Norm, and gradient clipping for stability.

## Results

On the HPS v2.0 benchmark, Meissonic achieves an averaged score of 28.83, surpassing SDXL Refiner 1.0 (28.27) and SDXL Base 1.0 (28.25) across all four style categories. On the Multi-Dimensional Human Preference Score (MPS) with RealUser-800 prompts, Meissonic scores 17.34, outperforming SDXL Refiner 1.0 (16.56) by 0.78 points. On GenEval, Meissonic achieves 0.54 overall, marginally behind SDXL (0.55), while leading on single-object (0.99) and counting (0.42) sub-tasks. At 1024×1024 resolution, Meissonic completes 50-step inference in 3.48 s per image versus SDXL's 5.38 s, with peak GPU memory of ~5.9 GB compared to SDXL's ~26.8 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDXL (Base 1.0 and Refiner 1.0), Meissonic wins on HPS v2.0 (28.83 vs. 28.25/28.27) and MPS (17.34 vs. 16.46/16.56), but trails slightly on GenEval overall (0.54 vs. 0.55), though it leads on single-object detection (0.99 vs. 0.98) and counting (0.42 vs. 0.39). Against DALL·E 2, Meissonic dominates on HPS v2.0 (28.83 vs. 26.95) and GenEval (0.54 vs. 0.52). In zero-shot image editing on EMU-Edit, Meissonic achieves the best CLIP-I (0.871) and CLIP-T (0.266) scores, surpassing EMU-Edit (0.859/0.231), InstructPix2Pix (0.834/0.219), and MagicBrush (0.838/0.222), though it lags EMU-Edit on DINO similarity (0.760 vs. 0.819). On MagicBrush, Meissonic-512 leads on CLIP-T (0.248) and DINO (0.689) over both SD 1.5+Null-Text Inv. and SDXL+Null-Text Inv. Inference efficiency is a clear advantage: Meissonic-1024 uses ~5.9 GB VRAM and 3.48 s per 50-step generation, versus SDXL's ~26.8 GB and 5.38 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

Meissonic extends the MaskGIT→MUSE lineage of non-autoregressive masked image modeling (MIM) by replacing the naive transformer backbones used in those works with a hybrid multi-modal-to-single-modal transformer architecture borrowed from the SD3/ADM framework (Sauer et al., 2024), augmented with RoPE positional encoding and a masking-rate-as-condition mechanism. It departs from MUSE and DeepFloyd-XL's multi-stage cascading super-resolution approach by using lightweight feature compression layers within a single network, and from prior T2I models by substituting T5-XXL with a fine-tuned CLIP text encoder. The VQ-VAE discrete token representation (Esser et al., 2021) and cosine-scheduled variable masking (Chang et al., 2023) are retained as the foundational tokenization and training strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a 1B-parameter MIM model matching or exceeding SDXL (2.6B diffusion) on human-preference benchmarks while running on 8 GB consumer GPUs and training in ~48 H100 GPU days opens the door to accessible, offline, and privacy-preserving high-resolution image synthesis on mobile and edge devices. The zero-shot image-editing capability (inpainting, outpainting, mask-free editing) without any editing-specific fine-tuning is an emergent benefit of the MIM formulation that diffusion models typically require additional training to achieve. Open questions include whether the HPS/MPS advantage generalizes to FID and other distributional metrics, whether the 1B parameter ceiling is fundamental or an artifact of the current training budget, and whether the approach scales to video or 3D generation where the token-count problem is even more acute.
