## Problem

Existing test-time alignment methods for LLMs rely on trajectory-level reward models that evaluate complete responses, making them fundamentally mismatched with autoregressive generation that requires next-token rewards from partial responses. This mismatch forces prior approaches to either inaccurately score partial responses with models trained only on complete ones, or expensively generate full responses for each candidate token, limiting both accuracy and inference efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252663/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GenARM takes a text prompt, a frozen base LLM (ranging from 7B to 70B parameters), and one or more Autoregressive Reward Models (typically 7B) trained on human preference data. At each generation step, it produces the next token by combining the base LLM's logits with the Autoregressive RM's next-token reward scores in a single forward pass, requiring no full-response generation. For multi-objective alignment, multiple Autoregressive RMs are combined with user-adjustable weight coefficients at test time, enabling real-time trade-offs between dimensions like helpfulness and harmlessness without retraining the base model.

## Method

The Autoregressive Reward Model parametrizes the trajectory-level reward as a log probability, decomposing it into a sum of token-level log probabilities conditioned on past tokens, which provides a natural dense reward signal for autoregressive generation. Training uses the same Bradley-Terry preference objective as standard RMs but operates on accumulated token-level rewards, ensuring the sum of next-token rewards is higher for preferred responses. At inference, GenARM samples each next token by multiplying the base LLM's probability with the Autoregressive RM's probability raised to 1/β, reducing the problem to controlled decoding from multiple language models. Theoretically, the paper proves that every reward equivalence class under the Bradley-Terry model contains a unique log-probability function, establishing that this parametrization preserves full expressiveness within the KL-regularized RL framework without sacrificing achievable decoding distributions.

## Results

On the HH-RLHF test set (300 prompts, GPT-4 evaluation), GenARM achieves 51.44% Win+½Tie against the training-time DPO baseline, 69.44% against ARGS, 69.17% against Transfer-Q, and 57.27% against CARDS. Inference time for generating 128 tokens is 7.28s for GenARM versus 7.74s (ARGS), 87.09s (CARDS), and 130.53s (Transfer-Q) on a single RTX A6000 GPU. In weak-to-strong guidance on AlpacaEval 2, a 7B Autoregressive RM guiding Tulu2-70B recovers over 70% of the performance gap to Tulu2-DPO-70B in both raw and length-controlled win rates, without training the 70B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenARM decisively outperforms all three test-time alignment baselines: it achieves 69.44% Win+½Tie against ARGS (which naively applies a trajectory-level RM to partial responses), 69.17% against Transfer-Q (which generates full responses for each candidate token), and 57.27% against CARDS (which generates small semantic segments). Against the training-time DPO baseline, GenARM slightly outperforms at 51.44% Win+½Tie, effectively closing the gap that all other test-time methods leave open (ARGS: 26.89%, Transfer-Q: 33.72%, CARDS: 41.94%). In weak-to-strong guidance on AlpacaEval 2, GenARM with a 7B RM outperforms both ARGS and Best-of-N (N=16) at every parameter scale (7B/13B/70B), while BoN incurs 16× the inference cost. In multi-objective alignment on PKU-SafeRLHF-10K, GenARM surpasses Rewarded Soups (RS) in achieving a better Pareto frontier and performs comparably to Multi-Objective RL (MORL) without requiring retraining for different reward weight configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RLHF/DPO training-time alignment pipeline and the controlled decoding framework derived from KL-regularized RL, which provides a closed-form solution for reward-guided generation. It departs from prior test-time alignment methods (ARGS, CARDS, Transfer-Q) that rely on trajectory-level reward models to evaluate complete or partial responses, by introducing an Autoregressive RM that parametrizes the reward as a log probability with natural token-level factorization. The theoretical contribution connects to the Bradley-Terry/Plackett-Luce preference framework, showing that this reparametrization preserves the full expressiveness of the reward equivalence class. The multi-objective and weak-to-strong capabilities extend prior work on Rewarded Soups, MORL, and weak-to-strong generalization into a unified test-time decoding framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GenARM provides a practical path to aligning large LLMs without the prohibitive cost of full retraining, matching DPO performance while being ~18x faster than Transfer-Q at inference. The weak-to-strong result (7B RM recovering 70%+ of the DPO gap on a 70B model) is particularly relevant as models scale beyond what is feasible to fine-tune. Open questions include whether the approach extends to reasoning tasks (math, coding) where reward signals are more structured, and whether the Autoregressive RM's token-level rewards remain reliable for very long generations. The method still requires training an RM (albeit smaller than the base LLM), so it does not eliminate all training cost.
