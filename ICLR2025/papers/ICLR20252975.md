## Problem

Existing prompt inversion methods for text-to-image models produce prompts that are either non-human-readable (soft prompts as continuous vectors) or incoherent (hard prompts containing meaningless words mixed with useful ones). The root cause is that conventional hard prompt inversion optimizes P(T|I)/P(T), which encourages rare or awkward phrasing to minimize the text prior, degrading interpretability. This paper seeks a gradient-free approach that generates fully interpretable, coherent prompts aligned with a target image without any training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single target RGB image as input and produces a human-readable text prompt (a coherent sentence of up to ~77 tokens) that can be fed directly into any text-to-image model (Stable Diffusion, MidJourney, DALL-E 2) without model-specific adaptation. Unlike soft prompt methods that output a fixed-length embedding vector tied to one model, VGD's output is a natural-language sentence that is portable across T2I backends and can be manually edited by users to modify objects, backgrounds, or style. The prompt length is variable, determined by beam search termination criteria rather than a fixed token budget.

## Method

VGD reformulates prompt inversion as a noisy-channel decoding problem, maximizing P(I|T)·P(T) instead of the conventional P(T|I)/P(T), which avoids the interpretability-degrading prior minimization. P(I|T) is approximated by the CLIP image-text similarity score (using the same CLIP encoder as the T2I model), and P(T) is modeled by an external LLM (LLaMA2, LLaMA3, or Mistral) via left-to-right token factorization. Token generation proceeds via beam search (width K=10): at each step, the LLM proposes K highest-probability next-token candidates per beam, and the expanded K² candidates are pruned to K by the joint CLIP×LLM objective. Beam initialization selects the top-M CLIP-matched vocabulary tokens as a prefix to anchor generation toward the image content. The entire pipeline is gradient-free and requires no training—only inference-time decoding with a modified scoring function.

## Results

On Lexica.art, VGD with ~77 tokens achieves a CLIP-I score of 0.754, outperforming PEZ at all token lengths (0.743 at 16, 0.745 at 32, 0.728 at 64 tokens). When paired with LLaVA-1.5, VGD reaches SOTA CLIP-I scores across all four datasets (e.g., 0.769 on Lexica.art, 0.718 on MS COCO), surpassing the LLaVA+CLIP-Interrogator baseline by a considerable margin. On prompt quality, VGD (32 tokens) achieves BERTScore F1 of 0.842 on MS COCO and 0.801 on Lexica.art, substantially exceeding PEZ (0.780 and 0.768 respectively). VGD also generates prompts ~7× faster than Textual Inversion and ~12× faster than PEZ (15.33 s vs. 103.45 s and 193.14 s per image).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGD outperforms PEZ (Wen et al., NeurIPS 2024) on BERTScore F1 by a wide margin at 32 tokens (0.842 vs. 0.780 on MS COCO; 0.801 vs. 0.768 on Lexica.art), indicating substantially more coherent and interpretable prompts. On CLIP-I score, VGD surpasses PEZ on Lexica.art (0.754 vs. 0.745 at 32 tokens) but trails PEZ on Celeb-A (0.506 vs. 0.619) and LAION (0.493 vs. 0.530), where PEZ's gradient-based optimization still provides an edge in image fidelity. VGD clearly beats PH2P (Mahajan et al., CVPR 2024) on both CLIP-I and BERTScore across all datasets (e.g., 0.735 vs. 0.678 CLIP-I on Lexica.art; 0.842 vs. 0.820 BERTScore F1 on COCO). Against the LLaVA-1.5 captioning baseline, VGD improves CLIP-I by 0.036–0.071 across datasets while maintaining competitive BERTScore. The strongest configuration, LLaVA-1.5+VGD, achieves the best CLIP-I scores on all four datasets, surpassing LLaVA+CLIP-Interrogator by up to 0.189 (Celeb-A: 0.546 vs. 0.463) and 0.189 (Lexica.art: 0.769 vs. 0.580), demonstrating that VGD's token-by-token CLIP-guided decoding captures fine visual details that caption-based approaches miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the noisy channel model framework (originally from machine translation) to reframe prompt inversion as a joint optimization of image likelihood and language model prior, rather than the gradient-based discrete optimization used by PEZ and PH2P. It departs from soft prompt inversion (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth) by producing fully human-readable text instead of continuous embeddings, and from hard prompt inversion (PEZ, PH2P) by eliminating the training loop entirely in favor of LLM beam search guided by CLIP similarity. The approach also contrasts with captioning-based methods (LLaVA, CLIP-Interrogator) by generating prompts through iterative token selection rather than one-shot generation, allowing CLIP to steer each token toward the target image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VGD offers a practical, training-free pipeline that can be dropped into existing LLM-based chat interfaces for controllable image generation, with prompts that users can read, edit, and compose (multi-concept fusion, style transfer, prompt distillation). A key open question is the dependence on CLIP-model alignment: the ablation shows significant degradation when a mismatched CLIP encoder is used, limiting portability to T2I models that use different vision-language backbones. The method also struggles with fine-grained regional or background objects in complex scenes, a limitation inherited from CLIP's whole-image encoding. Future work could explore region-aware CLIP variants or iterative refinement to address these gaps.
