## Problem

Current evaluation metrics for LLM unlearning (e.g., PPL, ROUGE, EM) are highly susceptible to red teaming attacks such as jail-breaking and embedding probing, meaning they may falsely indicate complete knowledge removal when the model still retains targeted knowledge. Additionally, the inherent trade-off between removal and retention means methods often do not conform to a Pareto frontier, making it difficult to determine which unlearning method is overall superior when one excels at removal while another excels at retention.

## Input / Output

The UWC framework takes as input the unlearned model parameters θ, the reference (pre-unlearning) parameters θ_ref, the unlearning dataset D_u, and a held-out set of non-targeted evaluation data D_t \ D_u. It produces (1) Extraction Strength (ES) scores computed by finding the minimum prefix length k such that greedy decoding from [x, y^{<k}] recovers y^{>k}, averaged over the dataset, and (2) calibrated model parameters (1−α)θ_ref + αθ where α is found via binary search to maximize unlearning strength while preserving at least τ×100% of the original retention ES. The ES computation requires iterating over all prefix lengths per sample, and the MM calibration requires evaluating the mixed model on non-targeted data at each binary search step.

## Method

The paper proposes UWC (Unlearning with Control), a two-step evaluation framework. First, it identifies Extraction Strength (ES) as the most robust unlearning metric by testing five candidate metrics under four red teaming attacks (jail-breaking, embedding probing, relearning, token noising) and measuring robustness via Pearson correlation between pre- and post-attack scores; ES consistently shows the highest PCC, particularly under relearning and probing. Second, it introduces Model Mixing (MM) calibration, which linearly interpolates between reference and unlearned parameters as (1−α)θ_ref + αθ, using binary search to find the largest α that preserves at least τ fraction of the original retention ES. This calibration aligns all methods to a common retention level, enabling fair comparison based solely on the ES score for targeted data (removal). The framework is applied to benchmark six unlearning methods (GA, GD, KL, PO, NPO, RMU) on TOFU with Phi-1.5 and Llama-2-7B, revealing that GA variants with proper hyperparameters remain competitive, that NPO suffers from incomplete unlearning due to its implicit weighting mechanism, and that temperature scaling is a reliable practical trick for enhancing unlearning efficacy.

## Results

Using the UWC framework (ES metric + Model Mixing calibration) on TOFU with Phi-1.5 and Llama-2-7B, the paper finds that the 5% unlearning setup is the most challenging (contrary to prior belief that larger forget rates are harder). After calibration, KL with λ=10 achieves the lowest ES-exact unlearn scores on Llama-2-7B (0.0515 at 1%, 0.0953 at 5%, 0.2791 at 10%), while NPO shows consistently higher ES-exact unlearn scores (0.2543, 0.1104, 0.3700), indicating incomplete unlearning. Vanilla GA exhibits excessive unlearning with ES scores near 0 for both targeted and non-targeted data without calibration. ES is validated as the most robust metric under red teaming attacks, maintaining high PCC across jail-breaking, probing, relearning, and token noising scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks six unlearning methods (GA, GD, KL, PO, NPO, RMU) under UWC calibration, revealing that prior perceptions of method superiority are misleading. NPO (Zhang et al., 2024), previously positioned as a solution to catastrophic collapse, actually suffers from incomplete unlearning—its ES-exact unlearn scores remain substantially above 0 (e.g., 0.3768 on Phi-1.5 at 5%), indicating targeted knowledge is not adequately erased. In contrast, GA variants (Yao et al., 2023) with proper hyperparameter tuning (λ=2 for GD, λ=10 for KL) achieve the best unlearn scores on Llama-2-7B across all setups (0.0293–0.0980 for GD, 0.0515–0.2791 for KL), challenging the narrative that GA is inherently ineffective. RMU (Li et al., 2024) shows the weakest and most inconsistent performance, with ES-exact unlearn scores often exceeding 0.5 on Llama-2-7B. PO (Maini et al., 2024) is consistently outperformed by KL and GD. The paper also shows that without UWC calibration, GA appears to achieve near-perfect unlearning (ES≈0) but at the cost of destroying all model utility (retention ES also ≈0), while NPO appears to retain utility but fails to actually remove knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the TOFU fictitious unlearning benchmark (Maini et al., 2024) and the family of gradient-ascent-based unlearning methods (GA, GD, KL, NPO, PO, RMU), but shifts focus from algorithmic design to evaluation methodology. It repurposes the Extraction Strength metric from membership inference (Carlini et al., 2021) as the core unlearning metric, validated against red teaming attacks drawn from jail-breaking (Shen et al., 2023), embedding probing (Belrose et al., 2023), and relearning (Lo et al., 2024). The calibration mechanism—Model Mixing—directly adapts parameter interpolation ideas from robust fine-tuning (Wortsman et al., 2022) and task arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022) to the unlearning setting, enabling fair comparison by aligning retention performance across methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UWC framework provides a practical, general-purpose tool for fairly comparing LLM unlearning methods by decoupling removal from retention, addressing a long-standing ambiguity in the field. Its findings challenge the community's narrative: GA-based methods (GD, KL) remain competitive with proper tuning, while NPO's apparent superiority partly stems from incomplete unlearning due to its implicit early-stopping weighting. Open questions include whether ES generalizes beyond the TOFU setup, whether selective or sparse parameter mixing could further improve calibration, and how to assess influence removal without requiring gold-standard retrained models.
