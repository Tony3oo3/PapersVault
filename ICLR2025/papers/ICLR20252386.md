## Problem

Open-source text-to-video diffusion models produce quasi-static videos with minimal visual change over time and fail to generalize beyond their training length (typically 16 frames), degrading in quality and coherence for longer sequences. The paper identifies that the temporal attention maps of synthetic videos lack the structured band-matrix pattern observed in real videos, where correlation decays with frame distance, and that this unstructured attention is the root cause of poor temporal dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252386/cropped_page0_idx17.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt (optionally decomposed into key visual states by an LLM for multi-stage prompts) and optionally a reference video for dynamics transfer, and produces dynamic videos of 32–128 frames at 320×512 resolution in a single inference pass. The base model is VideoCrafter2 (a 3D UNet with temporal transformers built on Stable Diffusion), and the method is applied entirely at inference time with no retraining or additional optimization overhead. When a reference video is provided, its inter-frame DreamSim similarity matrix replaces the default Gaussian Toeplitz regularization matrix.

## Method

VSTAR introduces two training-free components applied to pretrained T2V diffusion models. The core contribution, Temporal Attention Regularization (TAR), adds a symmetric Toeplitz matrix with Gaussian-decaying off-diagonal values to the temporal self-attention logits before softmax, strengthening adjacent-frame correlation and suppressing long-range correlation to mimic the band-matrix structure of real videos; the standard deviation σ controls regularization strength and is applied at the highest temporal resolutions (64 and 32) where it most affects dynamics. The second component, Video Synopsis Prompting (VSP), uses an LLM to decompose prompts describing multi-stage visual changes into key visual state descriptions, whose CLIP text embeddings are linearly interpolated across frames to provide frame-specific cross-attention conditioning. The key empirical finding is that per-resolution ablation shows high-resolution temporal attention (64, 32) dominates video dynamics, while low-resolution attention (8) encodes high-level semantics and is less responsive to manipulation.

## Results

On ChronoMagic-Bench-150, VSTAR achieves the best CLIP-VL score of 0.233 and MT-Score of 0.448, outperforming all seven baselines on both metrics. The largest margin is on MT-Score (video dynamics), where VSTAR leads the closest single-pass competitor VideoCrafter2 (0.401) by 0.047 and the multi-pass method FreeNoise (0.379) by 0.069. VSTAR is the only method that combines single-pass inference with VSP-compatible multi-prompt conditioning, enabling 32–128 frame generation without temporal coherence degradation across passes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSTAR outperforms all seven baselines on both CLIP-VL (text alignment) and MT-Score (video dynamics). Against the strongest single-pass baseline VideoCrafter2, VSTAR gains +0.013 on CLIP-VL and +0.047 on MT-Score, with the dynamics advantage widening substantially at 32+ frames where VideoCrafter2 degrades. The multi-pass methods FreeNoise (0.379) and FreeBloom (0.361) trail VSTAR on MT-Score by 0.069 and 0.087 respectively, while also incurring multiple inference passes. Open-Sora-Plan and CogVideoX, which use 3D full attention without explicit temporal modules, score lowest on MT-Score (0.239 and 0.312) and cannot leverage VSP. On CLIP-VL, FreeNoise (0.224) is the closest competitor to VSTAR (0.233), but VSTAR achieves this in a single pass. The user study (110 participants) further confirms VSTAR's preference across text alignment, dynamics, visual quality, and temporal coherency, with 87.6% of participants perceiving smooth temporal transitions in standard-length videos.

## Relation to Prior Work

VSTAR builds on open-source T2V diffusion models (VideoCrafter2, ModelScope, LaVie, AnimateDiff) that extend Stable Diffusion with temporal transformer layers, and departs from multi-pass long-video methods (FreeNoise, Gen-L-Video) by achieving longer synthesis in a single inference pass without retraining. The Temporal Attention Regularization (TAR) component is the first systematic manipulation of temporal self-attention in T2V models, drawing an analogy to cross-attention manipulation work in T2I (Attend-and-Excite, Prompt-to-Prompt) but targeting the temporal axis specifically. The Video Synopsis Prompting (VSP) component adapts LLM-based prompt decomposition from T2I compositional generation to the temporal dimension, interpolating text embeddings across frames for smooth conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VSTAR provides a zero-overhead, training-free recipe that practitioners can immediately apply to existing T2V checkpoints to improve dynamics and extend video length, which is practically valuable given the compute cost of retraining. The temporal attention analysis and the finding that positional encoding (both Rotary and Sinusoidal) severely constrains length generalization offer concrete architectural guidance for next-generation T2V model design, connecting video generation to the LLM length-generalization literature. Key limitations include dependence on explicit temporal attention modules (inapplicable to 3D full-attention models like CogVideoX), residual text-encoder bottlenecks for complex prompts, and motion quality still bounded by the base model. Open questions include whether TAR can be integrated as a training-time regularization loss and whether the band-matrix attention structure generalizes to fundamentally different video architectures.
