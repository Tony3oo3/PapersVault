## Problem

Diffusion models memorize and reproduce training data, creating privacy and copyright concerns, yet retraining from scratch is prohibitively expensive. Existing unlearning techniques for diffusion models focus on concept unlearning (removing styles or classes) and require anchor prompts or target distributions that are unavailable when the goal is removing specific training datapoints. General-purpose machine unlearning methods like NegGrad are unstable and naive deletion is too slow, leaving a gap for efficient data unlearning with quality preservation guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pretrained diffusion model (unconditional DDPM or text-conditional Stable Diffusion with a fixed prompt) along with the full training dataset X and a designated unlearning subset A. For Stable Diffusion, where the original LAION training data is inaccessible, the method synthesizes a proxy dataset by sampling 128 images per prompt and classifying them as memorized or not via k-means, and requires manual prompt modification to convert fully-memorized prompts into partially-memorized ones that yield sample diversity. The output is a fine-tuned model that no longer generates the unlearned datapoints while maintaining generation quality on the remaining data.

## Method

SISS decomposes the naive deletion loss L_{X\A} into a full-dataset term minus an unlearning-set term, then unifies both terms through importance sampling with a defensive mixture distribution q_λ that samples noisy data from a weighted combination of q(·|x) and q(·|a), reducing computation to a single forward pass through the denoising network. A superfactor s > 0 amplifies the NegGrad component to boost unlearning strength, and the parameter λ ∈ [0,1] interpolates between naive deletion (λ=0) and NegGrad (λ=1) behavior. The paper proves that the SISS loss equals the naive deletion loss in expectation for all λ (Lemma 1), providing a theoretical guarantee, while empirically λ=0.5 achieves Pareto optimality between quality preservation and unlearning strength. Gradient clipping is applied to the NegGrad term (fixed at ~10% of the naive deletion gradient norm) to prevent instability, with a descent-lemma-based proof showing this preserves model quality for small step sizes.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, SISS (λ=0.5) achieves FID 25.1 with SSCD 0.36, reducing face similarity by over 50% while preserving model quality, whereas NegGrad (FID 334.3) and EraseDiff (FID 117.8) severely degrade quality. On MNIST T-Shirt, SISS (λ=0.5) reaches 0% T-shirt generation rate with Inception Score 9.2 and NLL 8.09, matching the retrained model's unlearning (NLL 6.17) while using only 300 fine-tuning steps vs. 117,500 for retraining. On Stable Diffusion v1.4, SISS (λ=0.5) successfully mitigates memorization on nearly 90% of 45 tested prompts while maintaining CLIP-IQA ≥ 0.35 throughout fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SISS (λ=0.5) is Pareto-optimal over both NegGrad and EraseDiff on the quality–unlearning tradeoff across all three datasets. Compared to NegGrad (Golatkar et al., 2020), SISS achieves comparable unlearning strength (SSCD 0.36 vs. 0.02 on CelebA-HQ; 0% T-shirt rate vs. N/A on MNIST) while preserving model quality far better (FID 25.1 vs. 334.3; IS 9.2 vs. 1.3). Compared to EraseDiff (Wu et al., 2024), SISS again dominates: on CelebA-HQ it achieves FID 25.1 vs. 117.8 with SSCD 0.36 vs. 0.19, and on MNIST T-Shirt it reaches 0% T-shirt rate with IS 9.2 vs. 5.1. On Stable Diffusion, SISS (λ=0.5) and SISS (No IS) show higher success rates than both EraseDiff and naive deletion, and additionally generalize to fully-memorized prompts where the other methods fail. Naive deletion, while quality-preserving, fails to unlearn (SSCD remains 0.87; T-shirt rate only drops to 0.04%), confirming that SISS's NegGrad component is essential for effective data removal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two existing machine unlearning strategies—naive deletion (fine-tuning on retained data) and NegGrad (gradient ascent on data to be removed)—into a single loss function via importance sampling with a defensive mixture distribution. It departs from the dominant concept-unlearning line of work (Gandikota et al., 2023; Kumari et al., 2023; Heng & Soh, 2023), which requires an anchor prompt or class distribution unavailable in the datapoint-removal setting, and from EraseDiff (Wu et al., 2024), which fits predicted noise to random targets but degrades model quality. The key conceptual move is recognizing that the naive deletion loss can be algebraically decomposed into a full-dataset term minus an unlearning-set term, enabling a single forward pass through the denoiser via importance sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SISS provides the first theoretically grounded method for removing specific training datapoints from diffusion models without retraining, directly addressing GDPR 'right to be forgotten' and copyright concerns. Key limitations include the requirement to retain access to the unlearning set (undermining legal sufficiency for copyrighted data), the manual prompt-modification step needed for Stable Diffusion, and evaluation at relatively small scale (6 faces, 1 T-shirt, 45 prompts). Open questions include whether unlearning generalizes across related prompts in latent space, how to automate the prompt-modification step, and whether the approach extends to other modalities (audio, video) or to settings where the unlearning set is not available.
