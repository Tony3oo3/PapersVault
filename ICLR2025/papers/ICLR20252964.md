## Problem

Existing instruction-based image editing datasets (e.g., InsPix2Pix) are generated by text-to-image models not trained for editing, resulting in noisy ground-truth triplets that suffer from inaccurate instruction following, poor detail preservation, and generation artifacts. Rather than attempting to refine the ground-truth images themselves, this paper addresses the noisy supervision problem by introducing multi-perspective reward data as an auxiliary conditioning signal during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252964/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an original RGB image (256×256 during training, 512 during inference), a text editing instruction, and multi-perspective reward data consisting of three scores (0–5) and three text descriptions covering instruction following, detail preserving, and generation quality. It outputs a single edited image. At inference time, the reward scores are set to the maximum value of 5 and the reward text is set to 'None', effectively conditioning the model to produce the highest-quality edit without specifying failure modes.

## Method

The core contribution is a Multi-Reward Condition (MRC) module that encodes reward information into a single condition vector: reward text descriptions are encoded via the Stable Diffusion text encoder, reward scores are converted to vectors via sinusoidal positional encoding followed by an MLP, and the two are concatenated with a type embedding. This reward condition is injected into the diffusion process through two complementary mechanisms: (1) a cross-attention layer in a reward encoder where the concatenated latent noise and image condition serve as query and the reward condition serves as key/value, and (2) additive injection of the reward condition after every U-Net block. Training uses the standard denoising objective with the reward condition as an additional input, fine-tuning only the MRC module, reward encoder, and U-Net while keeping the VAE and text encoder frozen. The key insight is that imperfect ground-truth images can still provide useful supervision when the model is explicitly told what went wrong (via reward text) and how bad it was (via scores), allowing it to learn to correct for dataset noise at inference time.

## Results

On the Real-Edit benchmark, Reward-SmartEdit achieves the best Following accuracy (69%) and score (3.72), as well as the best Preserving accuracy (74%) and score (4.00), outperforming the original SmartEdit by 5% and 8% respectively on accuracy. Reward-InsPix2Pix improves over InsPix2Pix by 11% in Following accuracy (63% vs. 52%) and 5% in Preserving accuracy (58% vs. 53%). On the MagicBrush test set, Reward-SmartEdit achieves 91.3% CLIP-I and 30.5% CLIP-T, and Reward-InsPix2Pix improves CLIP-I from 85.4% to 88.9% over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Reward-SmartEdit achieves the highest Following accuracy (69%) and score (3.72) among all methods, surpassing SmartEdit (64%, 3.50) and HIVE (54%, 2.93). On Preserving, Reward-SmartEdit (74%, 4.00) substantially outperforms MagicBrush (70%, 3.85) and SmartEdit (66%, 3.70), while HQ-Edit (16%, 1.63) and KOSMOS-G (9%, 1.43) perform poorly on this metric. On Quality, HQ-Edit (54%, 3.84) and Reward-InsPix2Pix (54%, 3.80) lead, with Reward-SmartEdit (49%, 3.67) trailing slightly behind SmartEdit's baseline (45%, 3.56) but still improving by 4% in accuracy. Notably, the method uses only 0.02M additional reward data to boost both InsPix2Pix and SmartEdit, far less than KOSMOS-G's 9M training data, demonstrating high data efficiency. Human evaluation confirms the GPT-4o rankings, with Reward-SmartEdit scoring 3.34/3.46/2.73 versus SmartEdit's 3.09/3.18/2.58 across Following/Preserving/Quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction-based image editing line (InstructPix2Pix, SmartEdit) by introducing multi-perspective reward conditioning as an auxiliary input to the diffusion process, rather than following the dominant paradigm of refining ground-truth editing triplets (MagicBrush, HQ-Edit). Conceptually, it generalizes HIVE's single scalar reward score into a structured multi-dimensional signal (three scores + three text descriptions) and integrates it via a dedicated MRC module and cross-attention into the latent space and U-Net blocks. The reward-generation pipeline borrows the LLM-as-judge idea from RLHF, using GPT-4o to produce aligned quality assessments, while the architectural integration (attention + additive injection) is a novel combination not seen in prior reward-guided diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The noisy-supervision problem in editing datasets is fundamental and affects every method trained on InsPix2Pix-derived data; showing that a plug-and-play reward conditioning module can recover 10–15% accuracy without re-annotating ground truths is practically significant for the community. The Real-Edit benchmark (80 real photos × 7 instruction categories) and the GPT-4o-based three-perspective evaluation protocol offer a more realistic and fine-grained evaluation than CLIP/FID metrics. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to newer diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based), whether the GPT-4o reward signal can be replaced by a learned reward model to reduce cost, and whether the method's difficulty with quantifiers and spatial prepositions (acknowledged in the failure analysis) can be addressed with richer reward text.
