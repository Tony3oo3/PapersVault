## Problem

Label-free LLM self-evaluation aims to estimate response correctness using only the model's own capabilities, without external labels or tools. Existing approaches either rely on output-based signals (verbal confidence, sampling consistency, output logits) that are limited by overconfidence and output diversity, or require training probing classifiers on correctness-labeled hidden states, contradicting the label-free requirement and limiting generalization to out-of-distribution data. The paper tackles the open question of how to solely utilize hidden states to estimate LLM response correctness without any label.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question as input to the LLM and, during a single forward pass, extracts the sentence-level average hidden state at every layer (h_0 through h_L), forming a progressive chain in the latent space. It produces a scalar CoE score (CoE-R or CoE-C) that is compared against a threshold to yield a binary correctness prediction. Crucially, the method is output-free: it never accesses the generated text or the output probability distribution, requiring only O(Ld) scalar operations after inference, completing in approximately 1.12 ms on a Llama3-8B model.

## Method

The paper defines the Chain-of-Embedding (CoE) as the ordered sequence of sentence-level hidden states h_0 → h_1 → … → h_L produced during inference, treating it as the LLM's latent thinking path. Two geometric features are quantified along this trajectory: magnitude (L2-norm of the difference between adjacent states) and angle (arccos of their cosine similarity), each normalized by the corresponding input-to-output state change to remove sample-specific scale bias. These features are combined into two scores: CoE-R, a linear real-space sum of normalized magnitude minus normalized angle, and CoE-C, which maps each adjacent-state pair to a complex number (magnitude as modulus, angle as argument), averages the complex points, and takes the resulting magnitude. The key empirical finding is that correct responses exhibit more convoluted latent paths (higher magnitude) and more stable semantic transitions (lower angle), while incorrect responses show the reverse, enabling discrimination without any training. A theoretical analysis proves that CoE-C is more robust to outliers than CoE-R by showing its feature increment is bounded above by CoE-R's increment.

## Results

CoE-C achieves average AUROC of 75.22% (Mathematics), 63.54% (Reasoning), and 63.89% (Knowledge) across seven LLMs (7B–72B), outperforming the best baseline by an average of 8.30% on AUROC, 5.55% on FPR95, and 5.52% on AUPR. The method is entirely training-free and label-free, requiring only O(Ld) scalar operations after the base LLM inference (≈1.12 ms vs. 10.32 s for SoftMax-based methods). CoE-C consistently surpasses CoE-R, particularly in outlier-prone settings, and maintains its advantage even when scaling from 7B to 70B+ parameter models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoE-C outperforms all 10 baselines on average across nearly every domain–model combination. Among black-box methods, Verbal Confidence is the weakest (avg AUROC 46–52%) due to confirmed LLM overconfidence, while the PSA Pipeline is highly unstable—sometimes competitive (e.g., 75.58 AUROC on Mistral-7B for Math) but often near-random. Single-output white-box methods (Entropy, Perplexity, Max Prob.) are the most stable baselines but plateau around 55–65 AUROC, limited by output diversity in long-form generation. Multi-output methods (MC Dropout, LN-Entropy, Eigen-Rate) show greater variance and frequently underperform their single-output counterparts. Notably, Eigen-Rate—which also leverages hidden states—underperforms CoE by 10–25 AUROC points, especially on Mathematics, because it relies on static embedding representations rather than dynamic trajectory changes. In the label-based comparison (Table 4), ITI and MIND achieve higher AUROC on their in-distribution dataset (TruthfulQA: 83.48 and 74.52 vs. CoE-C's 74.74) but collapse by 20–30 points on OOD datasets (GSM8K, MATH, TheoremQA), whereas CoE-C maintains 60–75 AUROC consistently, demonstrating superior generalization without any training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LLM self-evaluation and hidden-state analysis. Prior white-box self-evaluation methods (entropy, perplexity, energy, semantic uncertainty) operate on output distributions, while hidden-state-based approaches (ITI, MIND, Eigenscore) require training supervised probing classifiers on correctness labels. CoE departs from both by treating the *progressive trajectory* of all intermediate hidden states as a 'latent thinking path' and extracting label-free geometric features (magnitude and angle changes) from that trajectory, drawing an analogy to cognitive dual-process theory (System 1 vs. System 2). The complex-number combination of these two features (CoE-C) is a novel metric design not seen in prior uncertainty estimation or representation analysis work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's practical value lies in being simultaneously training-free, output-free, and millisecond-level in computation, making it deployable for real-time monitoring at scale without additional inference passes or access to generated text. A key limitation is the white-box requirement: the method cannot be applied to closed-source models (e.g., GPT-4), restricting its applicability in industry settings where API-only access is common. Open questions include whether the latent trajectory signal generalizes to non-Transformer architectures, whether the cognitive analogy (deliberative vs. intuitive thinking) has deeper mechanistic grounding, and whether the modest AUROC gains (typically 60–80%) are sufficient for high-stakes deployment where false-negative rates must be very low.
