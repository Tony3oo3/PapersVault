## Problem

Existing diffusion-based talking head generation methods rely on autoregressive or semi-autoregressive strategies, which suffer from error accumulation in long videos, limited utilization of future-frame context, and slow inference speed. No non-autoregressive (NAR) diffusion framework existed for general (arbitrary-identity) talking head generation that could produce dynamic-length videos in a single pass while maintaining quality and extrapolation capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253571/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single static portrait image (128×128, any identity, any pose/eye state) and a speech audio clip as input, and produces a dynamic-length talking head video at 25 fps in one non-autoregressive pass, where the output length N is determined by the audio duration. Internally, the system also generates explicit 6D head-pose vectors and eye-aspect-ratio blink signals as intermediate control conditions, and operates in a latent motion space (flow + blocking maps) rather than directly in pixel space.

## Method

DAWN decouples talking-head motion into two components: lip dynamics (modeled implicitly by a 3D U-Net diffusion model, A2V-FDM, conditioned on HuBERT audio embeddings and explicit pose/blink deltas) and head pose/blink (modeled explicitly by a lightweight transformer-VAE, PBNet, trained on 200-frame sequences to capture long-term temporal dependencies). A self-supervised Latent Flow Generator (LFG) extracts identity-agnostic motion representations (dense flow + occlusion blocking maps) between frames, so the diffusion model generates motion codes rather than raw pixels, and a shared decoder reconstructs video frames. A Two-Stage Curriculum Learning strategy first trains A2V-FDM on short clips with the source frame as reference (learning basic lip motion), then on variable-length clips with random source frames (learning large pose/blink transitions), improving convergence and extrapolation. At inference, local attention with tuned window sizes (80 for A2V-FDM, 400 for PBNet) and RoPE positional encoding enable stable generation of sequences far longer than the training clips.

## Results

On CREMA, DAWN achieves the best FID (5.77), FVD₁₆ (56.33), FVD₃₂ (75.82), and CSIM (0.845) among all compared methods. On HDTF, DAWN attains the best FID (9.60), FVD₃₂ (95.64), CSIM (0.790), and LSE_D (7.94), while Hallo edges it out on FVD₁₆ (57.47 vs. 60.34) and LSE_C (7.16 vs. 6.71). DAWN also generates 8-second videos in 7.32 s, roughly 1.6× faster than the SAR baseline (11.42 s) and 2.6× faster than TTR (19.25 s), with a degradation rate (DR₂₅ = 0.044) an order of magnitude lower than SAR (0.307).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAWN outperforms all seven baselines on FID across both datasets, with the largest margins over DreamTalk (58.80 → 9.60 on HDTF) and Audio2Head (30.10 → 9.60). Against the closest competitor Hallo (also diffusion-based, built on Stable Diffusion), DAWN wins on FID (9.60 vs. 14.20), FVD₃₂ (95.64 vs. 100.99), CSIM (0.790 vs. 0.709), and LSE_D (7.94 vs. 8.01) on HDTF, while Hallo retains a slight edge on FVD₁₆ (57.47 vs. 60.34) and LSE_C (7.16 vs. 6.71). On CREMA, DAWN dominates Diffused Heads (AR diffusion) by a wide margin on every metric (FID 5.77 vs. 13.01; FVD₃₂ 75.82 vs. 116.18), and Diffused Heads collapses entirely on HDTF due to error accumulation. SadTalker (3DMM-based) and Audio2Head trail DAWN on FID, FVD, and identity preservation (CSIM) on both datasets. DreamTalk and EchoMimic are restricted to face-aligned inputs and underperform DAWN on FID, FVD₃₂, and CSIM on HDTF. In the generation-strategy comparison (Table 2), DAWN's NAR approach is 1.6× faster than SAR and 2.6× faster than TTR while achieving the lowest FID and a DR₂₅ (0.044) roughly 7× smaller than SAR (0.307), confirming that non-autoregressive generation eliminates the error-accumulation bottleneck of iterative methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAWN departs from the dominant autoregressive (AR) and semi-autoregressive (SAR) paradigm in diffusion-based talking head generation (Diffused Heads, Hallo, EchoMimic, DreamTalk) by generating all frames simultaneously in a latent motion space. It builds on the MRAA flow/blocking map architecture (Siarohin et al., 2021) for its Latent Flow Generator and the Transformer VAE (Petrovich et al., 2021) for its pose/blink module, while introducing a key conceptual departure: decoupling lip motion (handled by the diffusion model) from long-range pose/blink dynamics (handled by a lightweight VAE) to make NAR generation tractable. The two-stage curriculum learning strategy and the use of RoPE for extrapolation are novel training/inference techniques applied specifically to this decoupled NAR setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAWN is the first NAR diffusion framework for general (arbitrary-identity) talking head generation, offering a practical path to faster, error-accumulation-free long video synthesis that could accelerate deployment in virtual meetings, gaming, and film production. However, the model still struggles with physical common sense (e.g., hats not moving with the head), requires separate training of each sub-module (LFG, A2V-FDM, PBNet) rather than end-to-end learning, and is evaluated only at 128×128 (with 256×256 as a secondary check). Open questions include whether joint training can reduce cross-module error propagation, whether the NAR advantage persists at higher resolutions and longer durations, and how the approach generalizes to multi-person or occluded scenarios.
