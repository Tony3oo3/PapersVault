## Problem

Forward-backward (FB) representations achieve zero-shot generalization in offline settings but fail in online unsupervised RL because their limited exploration produces low-diversity data, which impairs successor measure learning and yields inaccurate value functions for downstream tasks. No existing method simultaneously achieves zero-shot generalization and stable fine-tuning adaptation in the online URL setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250919/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes low-dimensional state vectors (Walker: R^24, Quadruped: R^78, Hopper: R^14) and a 50-dimensional continuous skill vector z inferred from fewer than 10,000 demonstration steps in the downstream task. During pre-training, the policy is conditioned on a randomly sampled z; during zero-shot evaluation, z is inferred via z_r = B(s')r from limited task interactions; during fine-tuning, a mapped downstream reward r_f (rescaled to match the implicit reward range) is additionally provided. The agent outputs continuous control actions (R^6, R^16, or R^4 depending on domain) and maintains two parallel value estimates: an exploration value Q_P and a skill value Q_M = F(s,a,z)^T z.

## Method

DVFB resolves the fundamental reward conflict in FB-based online URL by decoupling exploration from skill learning through a dual-value architecture. During pre-training, an exploration value function Q_P is trained with a contrastive entropy intrinsic reward (combining RND for pure exploration with an NCE-based contrastive entropy term that measures skill discriminability of state transitions), while a skill value function Q_M is trained with the standard FB Bellman-like objective; the policy is updated using both values simultaneously, so exploration is encouraged without corrupting the successor measure. During fine-tuning, a downstream task value Q_F is trained from scratch alongside the frozen prior skill value Q_M, and the policy is guided by a weighted combination of both. A reward mapping technique dynamically rescales downstream task rewards to align with the range of prior implicit rewards (r_f = r_t · μ(r_implicit)/μ(r_t)), preventing the early-stage inaccuracy of Q_F from destabilizing the zero-shot policy and enabling stable, monotonic performance improvement beyond the zero-shot baseline.

## Results

On 12 DMC tasks across Walker, Quadruped, and Hopper domains, DVFB achieves zero-shot generalization scores of 686 (Walker), 715 (Quadruped), and 101 (Hopper) average, outperforming all baselines on every task. In fine-tuning, DVFB reaches 852 (Walker, +15% over best baseline), 804 (Quadruped, +2%), and 163 (Hopper, +7%) average, leading on 10 of 12 tasks. Notably, in the challenging Hopper domain where all baselines score near zero in zero-shot (best: CIC at 11), DVFB achieves 101, demonstrating the first method to simultaneously achieve both zero-shot generalization and fine-tuning in online URL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVFB decisively outperforms all 10 zero-shot baselines across all 12 tasks. Among successor representation methods, FB (the closest competitor in Quadruped at 638 avg) is surpassed by 77 points, while other SR methods (CL, Lap, LRA-P, LRA-SR) score below 400 in every domain. Among skill discovery methods, CIC is the strongest zero-shot baseline (207 avg in Walker) but still trails DVFB by 479 points; BeCL, ComSD, and CeSD all remain below 400 in every domain. In fine-tuning, DVFB leads Walker by 15% over ComSD (852 vs. 739), Quadruped by 2% over CeSD (804 vs. 787), and Hopper by 7% over RND (163 vs. 152). The Hopper domain is particularly telling: all baselines score near zero in zero-shot (max 11 for CIC), while DVFB achieves 101, and in fine-tuning DVFB reaches 163 versus 152 for RND and 120 for CIC. The paper also shows DVFB outperforms offline zero-shot methods (FB-offline, LRA-SR, etc.) in Appendix G, where offline methods exhibit high data sensitivity (e.g., FB drops from 666 to 222 in Quadruped depending on exploration data source).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Forward-Backward (FB) representation framework (Touati & Ollivier, 2021; Touati et al., 2023), which previously achieved zero-shot generalization only in offline settings with pre-collected diverse data, to the online unsupervised RL (URL) setting. It departs from the standard FB formulation by identifying a fundamental reward conflict: the implicit reward structure required for successor measure learning is incompatible with intrinsic exploration rewards. The proposed dual-value architecture (separating a skill value function Q_M from an exploration value function Q_P) resolves this conflict, while the contrastive entropy intrinsic reward borrows from contrastive learning (CIC, BeCL) to maintain skill discriminability during exploration. The dual-value fine-tuning scheme with reward mapping is a novel contribution that has no direct precedent in prior URL or zero-shot RL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DVFB is the first method to simultaneously achieve zero-shot generalization and stable fine-tuning improvement in online URL, filling a gap where prior methods offered only one capability (USD methods for fine-tuning, offline FB for zero-shot). The diagnostic finding that FB's exploration limitation—not its representation learning—is the root cause of its online failure provides a clear design principle for future successor-representation methods. Key limitations include restriction to state-vector inputs (no visual URL), reliance on DDPG as the backbone, and the somewhat ad-hoc nature of the reward mapping technique. Extending the framework to visual observations and more complex multi-task settings remains an open direction.
