## Problem

Existing text embedding benchmarks are constrained by limited language coverage, narrow domain diversity, and a small number of task categories, with multilingual evaluation largely derived from machine-translated tasks. This gap is compounded by the 'low-resource double bind,' where communities with fewer computational resources also lack comprehensive benchmarks to evaluate models on their languages. MMTEB addresses both issues by expanding to 500+ tasks across 250+ languages while simultaneously reducing computational cost through systematic optimization.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes text documents, text pairs, and query–document collections across 10 task categories (retrieval, classification, clustering, STS, bitext mining, pair classification, multilabel classification, reranking, instruction following, and long-document retrieval). Outputs include per-task performance scores, Borda-count model rankings (a social-choice-theory aggregation where each task acts as a preference voter), and category-weighted averages. A key design constraint is that the optimized MTEB(eng, v2) uses only 2% of the original documents (6% of characters) yet preserves model ranking order with a Spearman correlation of 0.90 against the full MTEB(eng, v1).

## Method

MMTEB is constructed through a large-scale open collaboration using a point-based contribution system managed via GitHub, with each task requiring metadata fields and dual-model quality checks (multilingual-e5-small and MiniLM-L12 must produce scores within a reasonable range). To reduce computational cost, the paper introduces three optimization strategies: (1) TREC pooling for retrieval (retaining top-250 documents per query, capping at 1,000 queries), (2) bootstrapping for clustering (encoding a 4% subsample once and reusing across 10 k-means sets, yielding up to 100× reduction), and (3) embedding caching for bitext mining (reducing Flores English documents from 410,000 to 1,012). Task selection for targeted benchmarks uses backward elimination: tasks are iteratively removed if their scores are highly predictable (Spearman > 0.8–0.9) from the remaining task set, with guards to preserve language and category diversity. From the 500+ task pool, this yields 132 tasks for MTEB(Multilingual), 74 for MTEB(Europe), 23 for MTEB(Indic), and 41 for the zero-shot MTEB(eng, v2).

## Results

On MTEB(Multilingual) (132 tasks, 250+ languages), multilingual-e5-large-instruct (560M params, XLM-R-based) achieves the highest Borda count rank (1375) with a mean score of 63.2, outperforming the substantially larger GritLM-7B (60.9) and e5-mistral-7b-instruct (60.3). On MTEB(Indic) (23 tasks), the gap widens dramatically: multilingual-e5-large-instruct scores 70.2 vs. 60.2 for GritLM-7B and 60.0 for e5-mistral-7b-instruct. On MTEB(Europe) (74 tasks), GritLM-7B edges out multilingual-e5-large-instruct (63.0 vs. 62.2), reflecting the Mistral-based models' stronger performance on mid-to-high-resource European languages. The zero-shot MTEB(eng, v2) benchmark (41 tasks) achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.90 with the full MTEB(eng, v1) while requiring only 2% of the original documents and 3.11 hours on an H100 for a 7B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central comparison is between the 560M-parameter multilingual-e5-large-instruct (XLM-R-based) and two 7B-parameter Mistral-based models (GritLM-7B and e5-mistral-7b-instruct). On MTEB(Multilingual), multilingual-e5-large-instruct wins by 2.3 points over GritLM-7B (63.2 vs. 60.9) and 2.9 over e5-mistral-7b-instruct (60.3), with the largest gaps in Bitext Mining (80.1 vs. 70.5/70.6) and Classification (64.9 vs. 61.8/60.3). On MTEB(Indic), the advantage is decisive: 70.2 vs. 60.2 (GritLM-7B) and 60.0 (e5-mistral-7b-instruct), a ~10-point gap driven by STS (53.7 vs. 27.2/23.0) and Bitext Mining (80.4 vs. 58.4/59.1). On MTEB(Europe), GritLM-7B narrowly leads (63.0 vs. 62.2), reflecting Mistral's stronger mid-to-high-resource European language performance. Instruction tuning provides a consistent 4.6-point boost (e5-large-instruct 63.2 vs. e5-large 58.6 on Multilingual), most pronounced in Bitext Mining and Clustering. English-only models (all-MiniLM-L12, all-mpnet-base) score 10–20 points below the best multilingual models on non-English benchmarks, and LaBSE, despite strong Bitext Mining (76.4), lags significantly on Retrieval (33.2) and Clustering (50.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct and substantial expansion of MTEB (Muennighoff et al., 2023b), which established the first comprehensive text embedding benchmark spanning 8 task categories across 58 tasks. It also builds on retrieval-focused benchmarks like BEIR and MIRACL, and multilingual evaluation efforts like MINERS, but departs from them in scale (500+ tasks, 250+ languages) and in introducing a community-driven, point-based contribution system. Conceptually, the paper extends the embedding evaluation task from 8 to 10 categories (adding instruction following and code retrieval) and introduces novel benchmark-construction techniques—inter-task correlation-based task selection, TREC pooling for retrieval downsampling, and bootstrapped clustering evaluation—that have no direct precedent in prior embedding benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 560M-parameter XLM-R-based model (multilingual-e5-large-instruct) outperforms 7B Mistral-based models on multilingual benchmarks challenges the prevailing assumption that parameter scale drives multilingual embedding quality, pointing instead to pre-training language coverage as the critical factor. The computational efficiency gains (reducing evaluation from ~2 days to ~3 hours on a single GPU using only 2% of documents) are practically significant for low-resource language communities that lack access to large compute clusters. Key limitations include English leakage through human translations, a skewed task distribution toward high-resource languages, and the credit-assignment challenges inherent in large-scale collaboration. Open questions include whether the task-selection methodology generalizes as new model families emerge and whether the benchmark's language coverage can be further balanced toward truly low-resource settings.
