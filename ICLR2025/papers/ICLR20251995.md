## Problem

While off-the-shelf Denoising Diffusion Models (DDMs) trained for image generation show promising representation quality, it remains unclear which specific components of a modern DDM are actually responsible for learning good self-supervised representations. The paper asks whether the representation capability stems from the denoising-driven process or the diffusion-driven process, and which architectural choices are essential versus nonessential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251995/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 RGB images, projects each 16×16 patch into a 32-dimensional latent space via pre-computed PCA bases, adds Gaussian noise (σ=√(1/3) per level) in that latent space, and projects back to pixel space via inverse PCA to form the noisy input. The ViT encoder (12 blocks of a 24-block DiT-L) produces image representations for downstream tasks, while the full network predicts the original clean image as the training target. At inference/transfer time, the time-step conditioning is fixed (t=10) and the t-dependent MLP layers are merged, yielding a standard ViT-sized model.

## Method

The paper systematically deconstructs a DiT-L diffusion model into a classical Denoising Autoencoder (l-DAE) through three stages: reorienting generation-oriented designs (removing class conditioning, perceptual/adversarial losses, replacing the noise schedule), simplifying the tokenizer (conv VAE → patch-wise VAE → patch-wise AE → patch-wise PCA), and reverting DDM-specific choices (predicting clean data instead of noise, removing input scaling, operating on image space via inverse PCA). The key insight is that a low-dimensional latent space (d≈16–32 per patch) is the critical enabler for good representations, while the specific tokenizer architecture, KL regularization, and multi-level noise scheduling are all nonessential. The final l-DAE trains a ViT to predict the original clean image from a noisy image whose noise was injected in PCA latent space, with a weighted loss that down-weights the PCA reconstruction error dimensions (weight 0.1) relative to the noise dimensions (weight 1.0).

## Results

On ImageNet linear probing, l-DAE achieves 66.6% (ViT-B) and 75.0% (ViT-L) top-1 accuracy, compared to MAE's 68.0%/75.8% and MoCo v3's 76.7%/77.6%. Under end-to-end fine-tuning, l-DAE reaches 83.7% (ViT-B) and 84.7% (ViT-L), matching or exceeding MAE (83.6%/85.9%) and MoCo v3 (83.2%/84.1%) at ViT-B. For COCO object detection transfer via ViTDet, l-DAE achieves 51.6 AP (ViT-B) and 54.4 AP (ViT-L), outperforming supervised pre-training (47.6/49.6) and matching MAE (51.2/54.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MoCo v3 (contrastive learning), l-DAE trails significantly on linear probing (66.6% vs. 76.7% for ViT-B; 75.0% vs. 77.6% for ViT-L), but actually surpasses it on end-to-end fine-tuning at ViT-B (83.7% vs. 83.2%). Against MAE (masking-based), l-DAE is within 1.4–1.8% on linear probing and essentially ties on fine-tuning at ViT-B (83.7% vs. 83.6%), though it falls slightly behind at ViT-L (84.7% vs. 85.9%). On COCO object detection, l-DAE edges out MAE at ViT-B (51.6 vs. 51.2 AP) but is marginally behind at ViT-L (54.4 vs. 54.6 AP). Overall, l-DAE's strongest advantage is in fine-tuned transfer with smaller models (ViT-B), while its weakest point remains linear probing against contrastive methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a deconstructive bridge between modern Denoising Diffusion Models (DDM, e.g., DiT, LDM) and the classical Denoising Autoencoder (Vincent et al., 2008), systematically removing DDM components to isolate what drives self-supervised representation quality. It departs from prior work (Xiang et al., 2023; Mukhopadhyay et al., 2023) that evaluated off-the-shelf generation-oriented DDMs for recognition, instead training recognition-oriented models from scratch. The critical latent-space tokenizer concept is inherited from LDM (Rombach et al., 2022), but the paper's key conceptual departure is showing that the tokenizer can be reduced to a non-learned PCA projection, collapsing the DDM into something resembling a classical DAE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the working assumption that the diffusion process (multi-level noise scheduling) is what gives DDMs their representation power, showing instead that a low-dimensional latent space is the enabling factor and that a single noise level suffices for most of the benefit. This reframing could redirect research toward simpler, more interpretable denoising-based SSL methods and rekindle interest in classical DAE variants that were previously dismissed as weak (∼20% accuracy in pixel space). Open questions remain: l-DAE still trails contrastive methods (MoCo v3) in linear probing and scales less favorably than MAE with model size, so it is unclear whether the latent-space DAE family can close these gaps or whether masking-based corruption remains fundamentally superior for certain evaluation protocols.
