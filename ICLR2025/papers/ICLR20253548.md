## Problem

Current industry-grade text anonymizers (e.g., Azure, Presidio) rely on entity recognition and pattern matching, making them unable to remove context-dependent, quasi-identifier cues that modern LLMs can exploit to infer personal attributes like location, age, or sex from seemingly innocuous online posts. Additionally, existing anonymization metrics (span-based recall/precision) fail to capture whether an attribute remains inferable from the residual text, misaligning evaluation with actual privacy risk.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253548/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full free-form online text (e.g., a Reddit comment or conversation thread, up to ~1000 tokens) together with a set of target personal attributes to anonymize (age, sex, location, occupation, education level, relationship status, income level, place of birth) and produces a natural-language anonymized version that preserves readability and meaning. Unlike span-based anonymizers that mask fixed entities, the output is a rewritten text where privacy-relevant cues are removed, obfuscated, or generalized through iterative LLM editing. Evaluation is performed by a separate adversarial LLM (GPT-4) attempting zero-shot chain-of-thought attribute inference on the anonymized output.

## Method

The paper proposes a feedback-guided adversarial anonymization framework that iteratively alternates between two LLMs: an inference model (M_inf) that attempts to predict all target attributes from the current text, and an anonymization model (M_anon) that receives the adversary's inferences as structured feedback and rewrites the text to remove, obfuscate, or generalize the specific cues that enabled those inferences. The loop repeats until the adversary can no longer infer any target attribute or a fixed number of rounds is reached, with each round giving both models access only to the current text instance rather than the full history. The key insight is that the adversary's detailed inference rationale pinpoints exactly which contextual, linguistic, or referential cues leak privacy, enabling the anonymizer to make targeted edits that preserve utility far better than indiscriminate masking. The paper also introduces a new evaluation setting—'anonymization under adversarial LLM inference'—where privacy is measured as a worst-case metric (can a strong LLM still infer the attribute?) rather than via span overlap, and utility is assessed via a GPT-4 judge scoring readability and meaning preservation alongside BLEU/ROUGE.

## Results

On the PersonalReddit dataset, GPT-4-AA after 5 rounds achieves a higher mean utility than Azure (0.76) while reducing adversarial inference accuracy from 86.7% (original) to 41.6%, an absolute improvement of 24.7% over Azure's 66.3%. In the human study (n=50), LLM-anonymized texts score 0.89 in readability vs. 0.38 for Azure, 0.73 in meaning preservation vs. 0.47, and 0.93 in usability vs. 0.37. On fine-grained location (city-level), GPT-4-AA reduces adversarial accuracy from Azure's 24.6% to 1.3% after 3 rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Azure (the industry-standard entity-recognition anonymizer), GPT-4-AA wins on both utility and privacy across all 5 rounds: after 5 iterations it maintains higher mean utility than Azure's 0.76 while dropping adversarial accuracy from 66.3% to 41.6%. Against Dou et al. (2023) self-disclosure detection, the proposed method wins on privacy: while Dou's method achieves slightly higher utility by replacing spans with readable text, those replacements leak more information than simple masking, and it fails on attributes not directly stated in the text. Against Dipper (11B paraphrasing model), the proposed method wins decisively on both axes: Dipper provides only minor privacy improvements while heavily degrading utility, making it largely ineffective as an anonymization strategy. Open-source Llama3.1-70B-AA closely matches GPT-4-AA's privacy-utility tradeoff, confirming that even locally deployable models outperform all three baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Staab et al. (2023), which demonstrated that LLMs achieve near-human accuracy at inferring personal attributes from online texts, and reuses its PersonalReddit dataset, adversarial prompts, and evaluation protocol. It departs from the traditional text anonymization literature (k-anonymity, entity-recognition-based de-identification tools like Azure and Presidio) by reframing the problem as protecting against LLM-based inference rather than removing explicit PII spans. The feedback-guided adversarial framework is novel in using an LLM adversary's inferences as iterative guidance for a separate LLM anonymizer, extending the self-disclosure replacement approach of Dou et al. (2023) from direct span substitution to multi-round, inference-informed text adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a pressing regulatory and practical gap: as LLM inference capabilities grow, existing anonymization tools become increasingly inadequate, yet no scalable alternative existed. The finding that the method simultaneously improves both utility and privacy over commercial tools (rather than trading one for the other) is practically significant for real-world deployment. Key limitations include the adversarial inference setting being bounded by the strength of the chosen adversary (no formal guarantees against arbitrary adversaries), the per-comment cost of multi-round LLM calls, and the open question of whether smaller fine-tuned models can match GPT-4-level anonymization on edge devices. The framework's natural extension to domain-specific texts (medical, legal) and its compatibility with local model deployment make it a likely foundation for future work.
