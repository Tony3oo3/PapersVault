## Problem

Recent results showing that provable absence of Barren Plateaus often implies classical simulability raised the question of whether variational QML models can ever be both trainable and non-dequantizable, threatening the practical relevance of the entire field. This paper formalizes trainability and dequantization as distinct concepts, introduces a structural notion of 'variationalness' for QML models, and resolves the open question by proving that compatible models do exist.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250868/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The constructed QML model takes a binary input bitstring x ∈ {0,1}^n and trainable parameters θ, producing a real-valued prediction f_θ(x) = ⟨0|V†(x;θ)MV(x;θ)|0⟩ where V(x;θ) = U(x) ⊗ W(θ) has a tensor-product structure separating the classically-hard data encoding from the trainable part. The learning task is supervised binary classification with labels y = Q(x) for a function Q in BQP but not HeurBPP/poly, and the gradient-based training algorithm outputs parameters θ* that minimize the mean-squared error over a polynomial-size training set.

## Method

The core recipe (Theorem 3) constructs a trainable, non-dequantizable QML model by combining a classically-hard quantum function Q(x) (evaluated via a fixed unitary U(x)) with an easy gradient-based optimization task (maximizing ⟨0|W†(θ)HW(θ)|0⟩), joined through a tensor-product circuit structure. Corollary 4 adapts this to 'vari veryational' models satisfying five structural properties (single repeated layer, tunable depth independent of qubit count, |0⟩ initialization, fixed observable, gradient-based training) by expanding U(x) into a unitary whose sequential applications remain equivalent to a single application, enabling arbitrary sub-exponential depth. The paper further proves that absence of Barren Plateaus is necessary but not sufficient for gradient-based trainability, and that trainability and BPs are independent phenomena in general (Proposition 1), using the DLP kernel compiled into a deep-layered brickwork circuit as a counterexample.

## Results

This is a theoretical paper with no experimental benchmarks. The main results are existence theorems: Theorem 3 proves that gradient-based trainable, non-dequantizable QML models exist by combining a BQP-hard function Q(x) (not in HeurBPP/poly) with an easily solvable variational optimization sub-problem. Corollary 4 extends this to 'vari veryational' models (deep-layered, gradient-trained PQCs) with any number of layers up to sub-exponentially many in the number of qubits. Proposition 1 shows that Barren Plateaus do not preclude trainability in general, while the converse (absence of BPs is necessary for gradient-based trainability) does hold.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing methods but instead resolves a conceptual tension in the QML literature. Existing variational QML models based on the Hardware Efficient Ansatz (HEA) are shown to be either trainable (shallow circuits with local observables, per Cerezo et al. 2021a) or non-dequantizable (deep circuits with global observables, per Basheer et al. 2023), but not both simultaneously. The DLP kernel (Liu et al. 2021) is trainable and non-dequantizable but is not variational in the deep-layered sense. The paper's construction (Theorem 3 + Corollary 4) is the first to demonstrate a model that is simultaneously vari veryational (deep-layered, gradient-trained), trainable, and non-dequantizable, thereby resolving the open question raised by Cerezo et al. (2023) about whether these properties are mutually compatible for variational QML.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the quantum-classical learning separation framework of Gyurik & Dunjko (2023) and the BQP/HeurBPP/poly construction from Cerezo et al. (2023), adapting both from a function-evaluation setting to a supervised learning task. It also leverages the DLP kernel of Liu et al. (2021) as a non-variational anchor point, showing that the same kernel can be embedded into a deep-layered HEA-style circuit (via LCU compilation) to demonstrate trainability coexisting with BPs. The key departure from prior work is the introduction of a formal 'variationalness' spectrum and the 'vari veryational' class, which allows the authors to state their main existence result specifically for models that structurally resemble neural networks, rather than for arbitrary PQCs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper resolves a critical open question that had been threatening the viability of variational QML as a research direction: whether trainability and non-dequantization can coexist in models that look like neural networks. The main limitation is that the constructions are explicitly contrived (tensor products of disconnected PQCs with the hard function 'planted' in the architecture), so they provide existence proofs rather than design principles for practical circuits. The open question the authors highlight is how to move from these existence results toward PQCs that are simultaneously trainable, non-dequantizable, and applicable to real-world data — a gap that remains wide. The formal distinction between dequantization and simulation, and between general trainability and gradient-based trainability, provides useful vocabulary for future work but may be difficult to operationalize for specific circuit families.
