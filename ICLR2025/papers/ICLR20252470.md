## Problem

Existing synthetic data generation methods for LLM pretraining, such as text rephrasing, preserve only surface-level linguistic variation and fail to introduce the step-by-step reasoning structure and complementary knowledge needed for complex multi-hop and mathematical reasoning. This paper asks whether transforming raw math web text into structured multi-turn conversations can close this gap and improve mathematical reasoning in pretrained LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252470/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 500-token chunks of raw mathematical web text (from OpenWebMath or MATHPILE) and one of seven manually designed conversational prompts as input, and produces multi-turn dialogues where participants decompose the context step-by-step while exploring each sub-problem in depth. Generated conversations are filtered heuristically (discarding outputs under 50 tokens) to yield a structured dialogue corpus; from 14B tokens of OpenWebMath, MIND produces 64B tokens of synthetic data. The 500-token input window was chosen empirically because longer contexts cause the LLM to summarize rather than expand, losing information.

## Method

MIND zero-shot prompts LLaMA-3-70B-Instruct with seven diverse conversational styles (Two Students, Teacher-Student, Two Professors, Debate, Problem Solving, Layman Knowall, Interview) to convert raw math text into multi-turn dialogues that embed chain-of-thought reasoning. The key finding is that knowledge gaps between participants are essential: styles with explicit imbalance (Layman Knowall, Teacher-Student) or implicit collaborative gaps (Problem Solving, Debate, Two Students) significantly outperform the no-gap Two Professors style, which produces surface-level dialogue highly similar to the raw text. The resulting synthetic corpus is used for continued pretraining of a 7B decoder-only transformer (32 layers, RoPE, GQA, SwiGLU) via Megatron-LM on a 2:1 blend of math data and CommonCrawl, with the critical design choice of replacing raw math data entirely with conversations for math benchmarks while combining raw and synthetic data for general reasoning. This structured conversational approach from a 4B-token seed outperforms a model trained on 3.6× larger raw OpenWebMath (14B tokens) on GSM8K, MMLU, and general reasoning.

## Results

On OWM-4B, the best MIND configuration (ALL CONVERSATIONS) achieves GSM8K=26.38, MATH=7.22, MMLU-STEM=42.53, MMLU=50.21, and General Reasoning=55.41, yielding absolute gains of +13.42% on GSM8K, +2.30% on MATH, +4.55% on MMLU, and +2.51% on General Reasoning over the raw OWM-4B baseline. Scaling to the full OWM-14B corpus (TWO STUDENTS style), MIND-OWM-14B reaches GSM8K=27.29, MATH=8.24, MMLU-STEM=43.55, and MMLU=49.91, outperforming the raw OWM-14B model (GSM8K=20.47, MATH=7.24) by +6.82% and +1.00% respectively. Notably, conversations generated from the smaller OWM-4B subset outperform the model trained on the 3.6× larger OWM-14B raw corpus on GSM8K, MMLU, and General Reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Rephrase baseline (Maini et al., 2024), MIND wins decisively on every metric: the best MIND style (ALL CONVERSATIONS) scores GSM8K=26.38 vs. Rephrase's 11.68 (+14.70%), MATH=7.22 vs. 5.46 (+1.76%), MMLU-STEM=42.53 vs. 39.71 (+2.82%), and MMLU=50.21 vs. 46.17 (+4.04%), confirming that structured multi-turn conversations add semantic and reasoning depth that surface-level rephrasing cannot. Against DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024), which trains on 120B unique math tokens extracted from CommonCrawl, MIND-OWM combinations (trained on ~64B synthetic tokens from a 14B seed) achieve comparable math accuracy: GSM8K=57.32 vs. 59.29 (−1.97%), MATH=2.36 vs. 4.37 (−2.01%), but surpass DeepSeekMath on MMLU (56.54 vs. 54.98) and General Reasoning (59.16 vs. 55.94), demonstrating that synthetic conversational data from a small seed corpus can match a much larger curated math corpus on core math tasks while generalizing better to broader reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant synthetic-data-for-pretraining paradigm of rephrasing raw text (Maini et al., 2024; Gunasekar et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023) by replacing surface-level paraphrase with structured multi-turn conversations that decompose mathematical content step-by-step. It also distinguishes itself from fine-tuning-oriented synthetic math data generation (MetaMath, DataDreamer, key-point-driven synthesis) by targeting pretraining corpora rather than SFT instruction sets. The closest conceptual ancestor is Dai et al.'s dialog inpainting, but MIND explicitly expands knowledge through complementary reasoning and diverse participant roles rather than merely predicting unobserved questions. The paper positions itself against large-scale math data curation efforts (OpenWebMath, MATHPILE, DeepSeekMath) by showing that structured transformation of a small seed corpus can rival or exceed much larger raw corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: a 7B model trained on 4B tokens of synthetic conversations outperforms one trained on 3.6× more raw tokens, offering a data-efficient path to stronger math reasoning when high-quality in-domain data is scarce. The ablation showing that zero-knowledge-gap conversations (TWO PROFESSORS) fail while knowledge-imbalanced or collaboratively-bridging styles succeed provides a design principle for future synthetic data generation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-mathematical reasoning domains, whether it scales to 70B+ models, and whether the reliance on a powerful generator LLM (even 8B) limits applicability in resource-constrained settings. The paper also notes that LLM-based quality scoring is unreliable for filtering its own generations, leaving the quality-control pipeline at the heuristic level.
