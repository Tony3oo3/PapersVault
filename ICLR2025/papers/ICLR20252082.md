## Problem

Despite the empirical success of adaptive optimizers (Adam, SGDM) in distributed and federated training, no prior theoretical result had shown that local updates (intermittent communication) provably reduce communication complexity for these methods compared to their minibatch counterparts. This paper provides the first convergence guarantees demonstrating that Local SGDM outperforms Minibatch SGDM in the convex setting and that Local Adam outperforms Minibatch Adam in the weakly convex setting, under generalized smoothness and heavy-tailed noise.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes M workers each computing K local stochastic gradient steps per communication round (T = KR total gradient calls, R communication rounds), with coordinate-wise gradient clipping at threshold ρ. The analysis requires only bounded α-moment noise (α ≥ 4) rather than sub-Gaussian tails, and smoothness on a restricted set Ω rather than globally. The output is a high-probability (1−δ) convergence rate bound that explicitly separates optimization, statistical, discrepancy, and clipping-bias terms, making the regime where local updates beat minibatch (large M, large K, small τ) transparent.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a novel contraction technique for adaptive methods with local updates. The key difficulty is that the update direction involves an exponential moving average of past gradients (momentum) and, for Adam, a coordinate-wise denominator that distorts the gradient, making it impossible to directly apply weak monotonicity as in standard SGD analyses. The authors define an auxiliary sequence {z_t^m} that is conditionally independent of the latest stochastic gradient, enabling martingale construction and application of Bernstein's inequality for high-probability bounds. The proof proceeds in two parts: (1) contraction, where they first show the second-order momentum (denominator) is relatively stagnant during local iterations, then bound the spread of worker iterates via the auxiliary sequence; and (2) descent, where they bound the Moreau envelope decrease using the contraction result to control the discrepancy term. For the weakly convex case, they use a generalized Moreau envelope with a preconditioning matrix H_r = diag(√(v_r + λ²)) to handle the adaptive step size.

## Results

The paper establishes the first high-probability convergence guarantees for Local SGDM in the convex/strongly convex setting and Local Adam in the weakly convex setting. For strongly convex problems (Theorem 1), Local SGDM achieves f(x̂)−f* ≤ exp(−Θ(μKR/L)) + Õ(σ²/(μMKR) + Lσ²/(μ²KR²)), where the exponential term depends on the total local steps KR rather than just R. For weakly convex problems (Theorem 3), Local Adam achieves (λ/KR)Σ‖∇f(z̄)‖²_{H⁻¹} = Õ(τΔ/R + LΔ/(KR) + √(LΔσ²/(MKR)) + (LΔσ)^{2/3}/(K^{1/3}R^{2/3})), where the last term is the local-update discrepancy overhead. All results hold under generalized smoothness and heavy-tailed noise (α ≥ 4 moment), with logarithmic dependence on the confidence level δ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Local SGDM against Minibatch SGDM and Local Adam against Minibatch Adam, both with the same total number of gradient calls (MKR) and communication rounds (R). For Local SGDM in the convex setting, the statistical term σD₀/√(MKR) matches the minibatch baseline, but the optimization term improves from Õ(LD₀²/R) (minibatch) to Õ(LD₀²/(KR)) (local), yielding a provable speedup when K > 1. For Local Adam in the weakly convex setting, both methods share the statistical lower bound 1/√(MKR), but Local Adam gains an advantage once K ≳ σ²R/(LΔ): the discrepancy overhead (LΔσ)^{2/3}/(K^{1/3}R^{2/3}) becomes smaller than the minibatch optimization term LΔ/R, and the gap widens as K grows until K ≍ L/τ. The paper concludes that in the large-M, small-τ (near-convex) regime, Local Adam provably outperforms Minibatch Adam, consistent with the known advantage of Local SGD over Minibatch SGD in the convex case (Woodworth et al., 2020a).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Local SGD / FedAvg framework (McMahan et al., 2017; Woodworth et al., 2020a) from vanilla SGD to adaptive optimizers (SGDM and Adam), proving for the first time that local updates provably outperform minibatch counterparts in convex and weakly convex settings. It builds on the generalized Moreau envelope for adaptive algorithms (Alacaoglu et al., 2020) and the gradient clipping + generalized smoothness framework (Zhang et al., 2019; Sadiev et al., 2023) to handle the analytically challenging case where the update direction involves both exponential moving average momentum and coordinate-wise division by the Adam denominator. Prior distributed adaptive optimization work (FedOPT, Local Adam with frozen momentum states) either required non-standard assumptions (bounded gradients, explicit contraction properties) or failed to demonstrate any advantage of local iterations over minibatch baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first rigorous theoretical justification for a widely-used practical technique: running Adam or SGDM with intermittent communication in distributed/federated training, which is particularly relevant for LLMs where adaptive methods empirically outperform SGD and communication is a key bottleneck. The high-probability bounds under heavy-tailed noise and generalized smoothness (conditions known to hold for language models) make the guarantees more practically meaningful than in-expectation rates. Open questions include extending the analysis to the non-convex setting, quantifying advantages over non-adaptive local methods, and whether the specific regime conditions (large M, large K, small τ) can be relaxed.
