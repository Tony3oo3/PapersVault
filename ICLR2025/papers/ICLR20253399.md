## Problem

Generalization bounds for encoder-decoder representation learning architectures are extremely scarce, and existing mutual-information-based bounds become vacuous in most practical settings. The only prior non-vacuous in-expectation bound (SZK23) scales as O(√(MDL(Q)/n)), which is suboptimal. This paper establishes tighter bounds at O(MDL(Q)/n) and, crucially, translates them into a practical regularizer by learning a data-dependent Gaussian mixture prior over the latent variable space.

## Input / Output

The encoder (CNN4 or ResNet18) takes an image (32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10/100, grayscale for USPS and INTEL) and outputs a d-dimensional mean vector μ_x and standard deviation vector σ_x, parameterizing a diagonal-covariance Gaussian over the latent space. The decoder maps the sampled latent variable to a C-class label. The regularizer operates on the batch-level distribution of these per-sample Gaussians, comparing them against a learned per-class Gaussian mixture prior, so the training signal depends on the collective geometry of the latent representations rather than individual point predictions.

## Method

The paper derives in-expectation and tail generalization bounds expressed in terms of the KL-divergence (MDL) between the joint distribution of training and ghost-set latent variables and a data-dependent symmetric prior Q, improving the rate from O(√(MDL(Q)/n)) to O(MDL(Q)/n) for realizable setups. To make the bound actionable, the authors propose a lossy Gaussian mixture prior regularizer: for each class c, a mixture of M Gaussians Q_c is learned simultaneously with the encoder via an EM-like procedure where the E-step computes attention-like weights γ_{i,m} (analogous to a weighted self-attention mechanism without key/query projections) and the M-step updates component means, variances, and mixing coefficients in closed form. The lossy variant perturbs the latent variable with structured noise (splitting into a mean-shift and variance component) so that the KL-divergence remains finite even for deterministic encoders, and the regularizer is the average of a variational upper bound and a product lower bound on the KL-divergence. The mixture components are updated with exponential moving averages plus Gaussian noise to reduce dataset dependence and partially preserve the symmetry property required by the bound.

## Results

The proposed Gaussian Mixture MDL (GM-MDL) regularizer achieves the best test accuracy across all five dataset/architecture combinations. On CIFAR-10 with CNN4, GM-MDL reaches 68.1% test accuracy, outperforming CDVIB (64.9%) and VIB (62.6%). On the harder CIFAR-100 with ResNet18, GM-MDL achieves 49.7% versus 46.3% for CDVIB and 45.8% for VIB. All experiments are averaged over 5 independent runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GM-MDL against two baselines: the Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB, Alemi et al. 2017) and the Category-Dependent VIB (CDVIB, Sefidgaran et al. 2023). GM-MDL consistently outperforms both baselines across all five dataset/architecture combinations. The margin over CDVIB ranges from 0.8% (USPS: 96.3% vs 95.5%) to 3.4% (CIFAR-100 with ResNet18: 49.7% vs 46.3%), while the margin over VIB is even larger, reaching 5.5% on CIFAR-10 with CNN4 (68.1% vs 62.6%). The advantage is most pronounced on the harder classification tasks (CIFAR-100, CIFAR-10 with CNN4), suggesting the MDL-based regularizer is particularly effective when the representation space is more complex. The paper also notes that the lossy version of the GM-MDL regularizer (used in all reported experiments) outperforms the lossless variant, and that a weighted attention-like mechanism emerges naturally in the lossy prior update procedure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior work (SZK23, NeurIPS 2023) on MDL-based generalization bounds for representation learning, tightening the bound from O(√(MDL(Q)/n)) to O(MDL(Q)/n) in the realizable case via a Donsker-Varadhan change-of-measure argument applied to a symmetric prior. It departs from the dominant Information Bottleneck (IB) paradigm—where mutual information between input and representation serves as the complexity measure—by arguing that MDL of the latent variables better captures encoder structure and yields non-vacuous bounds even for deterministic encoders. The practical regularizer (Gaussian Mixture MDL) is positioned as a principled alternative to VIB (AFDM17) and Category-Dependent VIB (CDVIB), replacing the KL-to-standard-Gaussian penalty with a data-dependent Gaussian mixture prior learned via an EM-like procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tighter bound and encoder-only regularizer offer a theoretically grounded alternative to IB-style regularization that avoids the known vacuity of MI-based bounds for deterministic encoders. The emergent weighted attention mechanism in the lossy Gaussian mixture update is a conceptually interesting byproduct, though its practical significance beyond this specific regularizer remains unexplored. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet) and deeper architectures, whether the slight violation of the symmetry condition in the learned prior affects the bound's tightness in practice, and whether the Gaussian mixture assumption is necessary or whether other flexible priors could be substituted. The moderate but consistent empirical gains (1–3% over CDVIB) suggest the regularizer is useful but not a drop-in replacement that dramatically changes training practice.
