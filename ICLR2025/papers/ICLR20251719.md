## Problem

Invariant geometric deep learning models (e.g., DisGNN/SchNet) are simple and computationally efficient, yet their theoretical expressive power remained unclear—prior work only provided isolated hand-crafted counterexamples of incompleteness without characterizing the full scope of unidentifiable cases. The paper asks: how close is DisGNN to E(3)-completeness, and can a broader class of invariant models (DimeNet, SphereNet, GemNet, subgraph GNNs) be rigorously proven complete?

## Input / Output

Models consume unlabeled 3D point clouds P ∈ R^{n×3} (optionally with node features such as atomic numbers) and produce a k-dimensional permutation- and E(3)-invariant embedding. The theoretical analysis assumes fully-connected distance graphs (infinite cutoff) and sufficient message-passing iterations to reach convergence, ensuring maximal geometric expressiveness. The completeness criterion requires that non-isomorphic point clouds (related by no combination of rotation, translation, and permutation) always receive distinct embeddings.

## Method

The paper first introduces A-symmetry (via center-distance encoding C and DisGNN encoding D) to characterize DisGNN's unidentifiable set, proving it is restricted to highly symmetric point clouds of Lebesgue measure zero (Theorem 4.2–4.3). It then proposes GeoNGNN, a two-level nested architecture where an inner DisGNN operates on each node's ego subgraph with that node explicitly marked, creating a second anchor alongside the geometric center to enable triangular distance encoding for full geometric reconstruction (Theorem 5.1). Using GeoNGNN as a theoretical tool, the paper proves that all general geometric subgraph GNNs (with at least one local aggregation) and three established models—DimeNet, SphereNet, and GemNet—can implement GeoNGNN and are therefore E(3)-complete under fully-connected conditions (Theorems 5.3–5.4). Experiments on QM9 and ModelNet40 confirm that DisGNN's unidentifiable cases are practically negligible (<0.15%), and a synthetic counterexample dataset validates that the proven-complete models achieve 100% separation while DisGNN/SchNet achieve 0%.

## Results

On a synthetic geometric expressiveness dataset of 17 non-isomorphic point cloud pairs (10 isolated + 7 combined counterexamples), DisGNN and SchNet achieve 0% separation, while DimeNet, SphereNet, GemNet, and the proposed GeoNGNN all achieve 100% separation. On real-world datasets, the proportion of DisGNN's unidentifiable cases is practically negligible: in QM9 (~130K molecules), fewer than 0.15% of point clouds are C- or D-symmetric even with the largest relaxed noise tolerance (ε=10⁻¹), dropping to just 0.0046% (6 out of ~130K) under the strictest setting; in ModelNet40 (~12K point clouds), only 1 C-symmetric point cloud is found when ε < 10⁻² and zero D-symmetric cases across all error levels. These results confirm that DisGNN is nearly E(3)-complete and that the theoretically proven complete models (GeoNGNN, DimeNet, SphereNet, GemNet) effectively resolve all DisGNN's failure cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares 7 baselines on a synthetic dataset of 17 highly symmetric, non-isomorphic point cloud pairs that are specifically designed to be indistinguishable by DisGNN. SchNet (Schütt et al., 2018) and DisGNN (Li et al., 2024) both achieve 0% separation on all 17 pairs, confirming their theoretical E(3)-incompleteness. In contrast, DimeNet (Gasteiger et al., 2019), SphereNet (Liu et al., 2021), and GemNet (Gasteiger et al., 2021) all achieve 100% separation, matching the proposed GeoNGNN and validating the theoretical completeness proofs. Notably, DimeNet uses a strictly weaker aggregation scheme than 2-FWL-based complete models (aggregating (h_ki, d_kj) vs. (h_ik, h_kj)), yet still achieves full separation, suggesting the bottleneck of invariant models may lie in generalization rather than expressiveness. The two equivariant baselines, PaiNN (Schütt et al., 2021) and MACE (Batatia et al., 2022), also achieve 100% separation, though their completeness is not formally established in this paper and is flagged as an open question.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the subgraph GNN framework of Zhang et al. (2023) and the Nested GNN (NGNN) of Zhang & Li (2021) from traditional graph learning to geometric point cloud settings, introducing GeoNGNN as a theoretical tool for proving completeness. It builds on prior expressiveness characterizations of geometric models (Dym & Maron 2020 for TFN universality, GWL by Joshi et al. 2023, and 2-FWL-based geometric models by Li et al. 2024 and Delle Rose et al. 2023) but provides a finer-grained analysis using the novel 'identify' concept rather than pairwise 'distinguish.' The paper also positions itself against prior hand-crafted counterexamples showing DisGNN's incompleteness (Pozdnyakov & Ceriotti 2022, Li et al. 2024) by rigorously characterizing the full unidentifiable set as a zero-measure subset of highly symmetric point clouds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding that models strictly weaker than 2-FWL in graph learning (subgraph GNNs, DimeNet, SphereNet) are all E(3)-complete in the geometric setting suggests the practical bottleneck for invariant models is generalization rather than expressiveness, potentially redirecting research effort. The newly defined geometric subgraph GNNs significantly enlarge the design space for geometric deep learning. Key limitations include the fully-connected condition required for all completeness proofs, and the open question of whether these results extend to sparse graphs or to equivariant models like PaiNN and MACE, which remain theoretically uncharacterized.
