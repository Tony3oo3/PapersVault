## Problem

While many LMM-based evaluators (VIE-Score, Prometheus, VQAScore, etc.) have been proposed for assessing AI-generated images, their reliability remains unverified because existing multi-modal benchmarks predominantly use natural-captured content rather than AIGIs. The fundamental question—whether LMMs can actually perform the semantic understanding and quality perception tasks that underpin all LMM-based evaluators—has never been systematically diagnosed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253057/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each test instance pairs a single AI-generated image (sourced from 16 different T2I models) with a multiple-choice question in either Yes-or-No (25.9%) or What (74.1%) format, where answer options are shuffled at evaluation time. Because LMM responses vary in phrasing (e.g., 'C', 'Expressionism', or 'The painting style is expressionism'), a 5-round GPT-assisted voting mechanism validates correctness rather than relying on exact string matching. The output is per-subcategory accuracy, enabling a diagnostic profile of each LMM's strengths and weaknesses across six distinct evaluation dimensions.

## Method

A-Bench is a diagnostic benchmark of 2,864 AIGIs (1,408 for A-Bench^P1, 1,456 for A-Bench^P2) with human-expert-annotated question-answer pairs, organized under two principles: covering both high-level semantic understanding and low-level quality perception, and ensuring diverse AIGI scope via 16 T2I generators. A-Bench^P1 tests semantic alignment through three progressively complex subcategories—Basic Recognition (major/minor objects), Bag-of-Words Pitfalls Discrimination (attributes, nouns-as-adjectives, composition, counting), and Outside Knowledge Realization (specific terms, contradiction overcome). A-Bench^P2 tests quality perception across Technical Quality, Aesthetic Quality, and AIGI-specific Generative Distortion. Rather than computing SRCC/PLCC for downstream LMM-based metrics, the benchmark directly probes whether 23 LMMs (4 proprietary, 19 open-source) can correctly answer fundamental perceptual questions in a zero-shot setting, with temperature set to 0 for reproducibility. The key design insight is that by isolating the atomic perceptual capabilities behind every LMM-based evaluator, the benchmark reveals *where* and *why* specific models fail, enabling targeted diagnosis rather than a single aggregate score.

## Results

On A-Bench (2,864 AIGIs from 16 T2I models, 23 LMMs tested zero-shot), the best LMM Qwen2-VL-72B achieves 86.02% overall accuracy on A-Bench^P1 (high-level semantic understanding) and 68.99% on A-Bench^P2 (low-level quality perception). The worst human performer still scores 92.40% (P1) and 90.56% (P2), leaving a 14.62% gap on P1 and ~23.10% gap on P2 between the best LMM and the poorest human. Random-guess baselines sit at 30.80% (P1) and 32.63% (P2), confirming all LMMs significantly exceed chance but remain far below human evaluators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates 23 LMMs (4 proprietary, 19 open-source) against 5 human annotators. Qwen2-VL-72B (open-source) leads all LMMs on A-Bench^P1 at 86.02%, narrowly surpassing GPT-4o (85.44%) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (84.70%), but still trails the worst human (92.40%) by 6.38 points. On A-Bench^P2, the gap widens dramatically: Qwen2-VL-72B scores 68.99% versus the worst human's 90.56% (a 21.57-point deficit), with GPT-4o showing a distinct advantage in Generative Distortion Assessment (67.92%) over other proprietary LMMs (58–59%). Proprietary LMMs reach near-human levels on Basic Recognition (93–94% vs. 95% human) but degrade sharply on Composition Identification (61–76% vs. 85% human) and Contradiction Overcome (61–69% vs. 89% human). Among open-source models, a clear hierarchy emerges: the 72B-parameter models (Qwen2-VL-72B, LLaVA-NeXT Qwen-72B/110B) cluster at 84–86%, while 7–8B models (LLaVA-v1.5, Qwen-VL, Fuyu-8B) drop to 63–79%, with Fuyu-8B performing worst at 63.12% overall on P1 and 40.20% on P2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing the evaluation of LMM-based AIGI metrics (VIE-Score, Prometheus, VQAScore, GPT4V-Eval, TIFA, Davidsonian Graph) as a diagnostic of the underlying LMMs themselves, rather than comparing metric-level SRCC/PLCC scores. It draws on the multi-modal benchmark lineage (MME, MMbench, MMMU, Q-Bench) but is the first to specifically target AIGI evaluation with a structured taxonomy spanning high-level semantic understanding (basic recognition, bag-of-words pitfalls, outside knowledge) and low-level quality perception (technical, aesthetic, generative distortion). The AIGI collection leverages existing datasets (AIGIQA-20K) and quality predictors (Q-Align) for stratified sampling, while the question-answering format borrows from VQA-style benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that even the best LMM (Qwen2-VL-72B) trails the worst human annotator by 14.62% on semantic tasks and by ~23% on quality perception—challenges the growing reliance on LMMs as AIGI evaluators and provides a structured diagnostic for where exactly they fail. The observation that generative distortion assessment is the weakest subcategory for nearly all LMMs points to a concrete gap that current training paradigms do not address. Open questions include whether the recommended divide-and-conquer strategy for complex prompts can close the bag-of-words gap, how to improve LMMs' sensitivity to AIGI-specific distortions, and whether the benchmark's conclusions generalize as both T2I generators and LMMs evolve rapidly. The manual annotation bottleneck (15 experts, 3-reviewer validation) limits scalability, and the 16-model/23-LMM scope may not capture the full landscape.
