## Problem

Small language models (SLMs) struggle with complex reasoning because they cannot effectively explore the solution space during self-exploration and cannot reliably self-evaluate which reasoning steps or final answers are correct. Existing self-improvement approaches like RAP and self-consistency either trap SLMs in low-quality reasoning trajectories or require majority voting that fails when most sampled solutions are wrong. This paper addresses how to boost SLM reasoning at inference time without fine-tuning or access to a superior teacher model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250753/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text reasoning problem (e.g., a math word problem or commonsense question) and produces a multi-step reasoning trajectory ending in a final numeric or categorical answer. During generation, MCTS performs 32 rollouts with a maximum tree depth of 5 (8 for MATH), branching up to 5 nodes per depth for sub-question actions. During discrimination, the second SLM receives a partial trajectory (randomly split between 20%–80% of the generated steps) as a hint and must complete the remaining reasoning to produce its own answer for comparison.

## Method

rStar decouples reasoning into a self-play mutual generation-discrimination process. The generator augments a target SLM with MCTS over a rich set of 5 human-like reasoning actions (one-step thought, remaining-steps completion, sub-question proposal, sub-question re-answer with CoT, and question rephrasing), using a reward function based on self-consistency majority-voting confidence rather than unreliable self-evaluation. The discriminator is a second SLM of similar capability that receives a randomly truncated partial trajectory as a hint and must independently complete the remaining steps; a trajectory is deemed mutually consistent only if the discriminator's completed answer matches the generator's. The generator SLM then selects the final trajectory from the mutually validated set by multiplying its MCTS reward with the terminal node's confidence score. This mutual consistency mechanism avoids the overfitting risks of trained reward models and outperforms self-verification, which the paper shows is near-random for SLMs.

## Results

On GSM8K, rStar boosts accuracy from 12.51% to 63.91% for LLaMA2-7B, from 36.46% to 81.88% for Mistral-7B, and from 74.53% to 91.13% for LLaMA3-8B-Instruct, using only 32 MCTS rollouts at inference time without fine-tuning. On the more challenging MATH-500 benchmark, rStar achieves 42.94% for LLaMA3-8B-Instruct and 48.60% for Phi3-mini-4k, outperforming the best baseline (SC@maj128) by +9.14% and +3.0% respectively. Across all five evaluated SLMs and four main reasoning tasks (GSM8K, GSM-Hard, SVAMP, StrategyQA), rStar consistently achieves state-of-the-art accuracy, with gains of up to +12.9% over the strongest baseline on GSM-Hard.

## Baselines & Comparisons

rStar outperforms all six baseline categories across every model and task. On GSM8K, rStar surpasses the strongest multi-round baseline SC@maj128 by +40.86% (LLaMA2-7B: 63.91 vs 23.05), +24.63% (Mistral: 81.88 vs 57.25), and +6.44% (LLaMA3-8B-Instruct: 91.13 vs 84.69). Against RAP (the closest self-improvement baseline), rStar gains +39.57% on LLaMA2-7B GSM8K and +10.63% on Mistral GSM-Hard. ToT is consistently the weakest multi-round baseline, with rStar exceeding it by up to +50.95% (LLaMA2-7B GSM8K: 63.91 vs 12.96). Notably, even rStar's generator alone with majority voting (rStar gen@maj) already beats RAP and ToT on all tasks, demonstrating that the rich 5-action MCTS space is a major contributor. On StrategyQA, where SC@maj128 actually degrades below few-shot CoT for several models, rStar still achieves the best accuracy, confirming its advantage on non-mathematical reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

rStar extends MCTS-based reasoning approaches (RAP, AlphaMath, M*) by replacing their single-action exploration with a richer set of five human-like reasoning actions (one-step thought, remaining steps, sub-question, re-answer, rephrase), and departs from self-consistency and self-verification by introducing a mutual consistency mechanism where a second SLM of similar capability acts as an unsupervised discriminator. It explicitly builds on the 'reasoning without a superior teacher' paradigm (RAP, self-refine) but identifies and addresses two fundamental failures of that paradigm for SLMs: ineffective exploration and unreliable self-rewarding. The reward function draws from AlphaGo-style back-propagation of terminal correctness rather than the self-evaluation scores used in RAP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that SLMs already possess substantial latent reasoning capability that can be unlocked at inference time without fine-tuning or access to superior models, with rStar boosting GSM8K accuracy by up to 5× (12.51%→63.91% for LLaMA2-7B) across five models and five tasks. A key open limitation is the high inference cost (~166 model calls per question, ~4.5 days on a single A100 for GSM8K), which limits immediate deployment despite the paper's discussion of parallelization strategies. The finding that SLM self-rewarding is essentially random (replacing self-evaluation scores with random values barely changes performance) challenges a common assumption in self-improvement literature and suggests that future work should focus on external verification signals rather than internal confidence. Whether mutual consistency generalizes to open-ended generation tasks beyond math and commonsense QA remains unexplored.
