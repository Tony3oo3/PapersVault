## Problem

Existing multi-hop QA benchmarks (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, MuSiQue) are built on Wikipedia data that risks data contamination in LLM pre-training, making it impossible to distinguish genuine reasoning from memorized knowledge. Meanwhile, prior counterfactual QA benchmarks (DisentQA, IfQA) only assess final answer correctness and lack evaluation of the intermediate reasoning process. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a step-wise counterfactual multi-hop QA benchmark that decouples LLM memory from reasoning and evaluates the full reasoning chain.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a counterfactual passage (a Wikipedia passage with named entities, noun phrases, and synonyms replaced, then back-translated through Chinese) together with a multi-hop question (2-hop, 3-hop, or 4-hop) and its decomposed sub-questions as input. The LLM must produce a final answer plus answers to each sub-question. Evaluation outputs include per-hop EM and F1 scores, a GPT-4-turbo-based Partial Match (PM) score to handle alias/abbreviation mismatches, and joint reasoning chain scores (Joint F1 RC and Joint EM RC) computed as the negative log of combined precision and recall across all hops, where larger values indicate worse chain-level performance.

## Method

The core contribution is a human-in-the-loop annotation pipeline with two stages: (1) an LLM acts as a passage annotator to replace named entities, noun phrases, and synonyms in 300 randomly selected Wikipedia passages, followed by back-translation through Chinese to produce fully counterfactual passages; (2) an LLM generates multi-hop QA pairs (one 2-hop, one 3-hop, one 4-hop per passage) with corresponding sub-questions and intermediate answers, all verified by human experts for grammatical correctness and counterfactual validity (94% inter-annotator agreement). The evaluation introduces three complementary metrics: sub-QA evaluation (EM/F1 per sub-question to track error propagation), reasoning chain evaluation (categorical EM statistics over all 2^(N+1) possible correct/incorrect answer combinations per N-hop question, following Tang et al. 2021), and joint performance (negative log of product of per-hop precisions/recalls). The dataset comprises 900 counterfactual QA pairs (2,700 sub-questions) plus 900 factual control samples from HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, and MuSiQue, totaling 1,800 test instances.

## Results

On the novel CofCA counterfactual benchmark, GPT-4 achieves EM 53.1 / F1 62.8 on 2-hop, EM 44.5 / F1 56.4 on 3-hop, and EM 42.3 / F1 53.5 on 4-hop questions, showing a clear degradation with increasing hop count. In contrast, on Wikipedia-based factual data (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, MuSiQue), GPT-4 scores EM 69.9 / F1 82.3, EM 59.7 / F1 67.4, and EM 57.3 / F1 65.4 respectively, revealing a substantial performance gap attributable to data contamination. On reasoning chain evaluation, GPT-4 achieves only 36.3% fully correct reasoning chains on the 2-hop set, while GPT-3.5 drops to just 13.3%, indicating that high final-answer accuracy is inflated by incorrect intermediate reasoning. Open-source 7B models (Llama-2, Mistral, Qwen-2) score 18–31 EM on CofCA, roughly 20–25 points below GPT-4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between Wikipedia-based factual MHQA datasets (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, MuSiQue) and the newly introduced CofCA counterfactual data. GPT-4 drops from EM 69.9 on HotpotQA to EM 53.1 on CofCA 2-hop (a ~17-point gap), and from EM 57.3 on MuSiQue to EM 42.3 on CofCA 4-hop (~15-point gap), confirming that prior high scores are inflated by data contamination. Against previous counterfactual QA benchmarks (DisentQA, IfQA), CofCA is the first to combine counterfactual data with multi-hop reasoning and explicit reasoning-chain evaluation, whereas DisentQA and IfQA only assess single-hop or open-domain final answers without sub-question decomposition. On the reasoning chain metric, all proprietary LLMs show a large proportion of incorrect chains: GPT-3.5 has only 13.3% fully correct chains versus 17.7% incorrect-but-correctly-answered chains, meaning most of its apparent accuracy comes from bypassing the correct reasoning path. Open-source 7B models consistently trail proprietary models by 15–25 EM points on CofCA, with Mistral-7b scoring the lowest (18.6 EM on 4-hop). The ablation in Table 6 further shows that removing sub-questions from the prompt reduces GPT-4's 2-hop F1 by 2.5 and EM by 9.4, confirming that sub-question scaffolding is critical for multi-hop performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sub-question evaluation methodology of Tang et al. (2021), who showed that older multi-hop QA models (DFGN, DecompRC, CogQA) could answer final questions correctly while failing on intermediate sub-questions, by applying the same reasoning-chain analysis to modern LLMs. It also builds on the counterfactual QA line of work (DisentQA, IfQA, ClashEval) that separates parametric from contextual knowledge, but departs from those benchmarks by introducing multi-hop structure and explicit reasoning-chain evaluation rather than assessing only final-answer correctness. The data construction pipeline (entity replacement + back-translation paraphrasing) is a practical adaptation of knowledge-editing techniques to create contamination-free test passages, positioning CofCA as a fixed alternative to dynamically-updated contamination detection methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CofCA provides a practical, fixed benchmark that avoids the ongoing maintenance burden of contamination-detection pipelines, making it a useful tool for the community to assess LLM reasoning without worrying about pre-training data leakage. The finding that even GPT-4 achieves only 36.3% correct reasoning chains while maintaining high final-answer scores is a sobering reminder that current evaluation metrics (EM/F1 on final answers) substantially overstate LLMs' genuine multi-step reasoning ability. Limitations include the relatively small scale (900 counterfactual items), English-only scope, and reliance on GPT-4 for both annotation and partial-match evaluation, which introduces a circularity concern. Open questions include whether the counterfactual approach fully eliminates memorization effects for very common entity patterns, and whether the reasoning-chain findings generalize to non-English or domain-specific settings.
