## Problem

Existing approximate unlearning methods for LLMs (gradient ascent, NPO, KL minimization, SCRUB, etc.) appear effective on standard benchmarks but may only be suppressing model outputs rather than truly removing target knowledge. This paper demonstrates that a simple benign relearning attack—finetuning the unlearned model on a small, loosely related auxiliary dataset—can 'jog' the model's memory and recover supposedly unlearned content, revealing that current unlearning is obfuscation, not forgetting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250321/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack takes an unlearned LLM (w_u) and a small auxiliary relearning dataset D' that is correlated with the unlearn set but explicitly does not contain direct answers to any evaluation query. D' is either a partial subset of the unlearn set excluding target keywords, or GPT-generated public knowledge about topics in the unlearn set (e.g., general flu articles for a biosecurity unlearn task). The output is a relearned LLM (w') evaluated via LLM-as-Judge scores (1–10), Rouge-L F1, or binary keyword prediction accuracy on held-out queries whose knowledge was unlearned. A critical constraint is that D' alone, when used to finetune a fresh model, achieves 0% attack success rate, confirming the recovery is memory-triggering rather than new learning.

## Method

The paper formalizes a benign relearning attack pipeline: finetune the unlearned model w_u on auxiliary data D' using standard negative log-likelihood loss (via LoRA or full fine-tuning), then evaluate whether the resulting model w' recovers unlearned content on held-out queries. Two attack scenarios are studied: (1) partial unlearn set, where D' is a disjoint subset of D_u that excludes the target keywords, and (2) public information, where D' consists of GPT-4-generated general knowledge about references in the unlearn queries, filtered to remove direct answers. The attack is tested across three benchmarks (TOFU, WMDP, WHP) and five unlearning methods (GA, GD, KL, NPO, SCRUB). A simplified synthetic experiment provides the key mechanistic insight: unlearning heuristics reduce conditional probabilities p_w(x_i | x_<i) but do not remove associations between token pairs; when two tokens are strongly correlated (e.g., repeated 'Anthony Mark' pairs), finetuning on one token causes the loss on the other to drop without direct optimization, effectively reactivating the unlearned association. The results show >70% attack success rate for shallow unlearning and that PEFT-based unlearning (LoRA) is particularly susceptible.

## Results

On the WMDP benchmark with zephyr-7b-beta, the original model achieves an LLM-as-Judge score of 5.92 on the forget set, which drops to 1.09–1.67 after unlearning (depending on method), but recovers to 5.08–5.2 after benign relearning on publicly available articles. On TOFU and WHP keyword-recovery tasks, the relearning attack achieves >70% attack success rate (ASR) when unlearning is shallow, recovering unlearned keywords that never appear in the relearn set. For verbatim copyright recovery on Harry Potter text, relearning on GPT-generated character facts significantly improves Rouge-L F1 scores over the unlearned model, particularly for GA and NPO unlearning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper tests five unlearning methods—Gradient Ascent (GA, Golatkar et al. 2020), Gradient Difference (GD, Liu et al. 2022), KL minimization (Maini et al. 2024), Negative Preference Optimization (NPO, Zhang et al. 2024), and SCRUB (Kurmanji et al. 2024)—and shows that all are equally vulnerable to the benign relearning attack. On WMDP with zephyr-7b-beta, GA unlearning drops the forget-set score from 5.92 to 1.67, GD to 1.09, and NPO to 1.00; after relearning on benign public articles, all three recover to 5.08–5.20, nearly matching the original model. The paper further shows that parameter-efficient unlearning via LoRA makes models even more susceptible, as the small fraction of modified parameters is easier to overwrite with minimal finetuning. No unlearning method tested provides robust protection: the relearned model consistently recovers to within ~0.7 points of the original model's score regardless of the unlearning objective used.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on evaluating unlearning robustness via relearning (Qi et al. 2023; Tamirisa et al. 2024; Lynch et al. 2024), which previously used general-purpose relearn datasets that could contain direct answers to evaluation queries. The key departure is a tighter threat model: the relearn data is explicitly insufficient to answer the evaluation queries in isolation, isolating the 'memory jogging' effect from simple re-learning. The paper also connects to the broader fragility literature on approximate unlearning (Shi et al. 2024; Maini et al. 2024b; Jin et al. 2024) and applies its attack across the standard gradient-based unlearning methods (GA, GD, KL, NPO, SCRUB) on established benchmarks (WMDP, TOFU, WHP).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all tested gradient-based unlearning methods merely suppress outputs rather than remove knowledge has direct practical implications for GDPR compliance, safety alignment, and copyright enforcement in deployed LLMs. The observation that PEFT-based unlearning (LoRA) is *more* susceptible than full-weight unlearning is a cautionary signal for practitioners using compute-efficient unlearning. Open questions include whether any unlearning formulation is robust to relearning, whether the attack transfers to inference-time techniques (in-context learning, jailbreaking), and whether current evaluation metrics (forget/retain set scores) provide a false sense of security. The simplified correlated-token experiment (Section 5) offers a mechanistic hypothesis but remains a toy model.
