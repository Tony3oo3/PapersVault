## Problem

Existing Transformer-based spatio-temporal video grounding (STVG) methods initialize object queries with zeros, forcing them to learn all target-specific information from scratch through iterative interactions with multimodal features. This zero-initialization lacks target-specific semantic cues, making queries struggle to learn discriminative position information in complex scenarios with distractors or occlusion. Oracle experiments confirm that target-specific information in queries yields large gains (e.g., 49.9%→68.9% m_IoU on HCSTVG-v1), but no prior method generates such queries without ground truth.

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (64 frames sampled, short side 420px) and a free-form textual description (30 tokens) as input. It produces per-frame spatial bounding boxes (center x, center y, width, height) and temporal start/end timestamps for the target object. Appearance features are extracted with ResNet-101, motion features with VidSwin-tiny, and text features with RoBERTa-base, all projected to a shared 256-dim channel before fusion.

## Method

TA-STVG replaces zero-initialized object queries with target-aware queries generated by two cascading modules. Text-guided Temporal Sampling (TTS) computes per-frame relevance scores by cross-attending frame-level appearance and motion features against the holistic text feature, then samples frames whose combined score (weighted by δ=0.5) exceeds a threshold θ=0.7. Attribute-aware Spatial Activation (ASA) then extracts the subject from the text via syntactic parsing, uses it as a cross-attention query over the TTS-sampled appearance and motion features, and is supervised by weak multi-label attribute classification (color/shape for appearance, action for motion) to produce attribute-specific spatial activation maps. These activated features are averaged and repeated to form initial spatial and temporal object queries, which are fed into separate 6-block spatial (appearance+text) and temporal (motion+text) decoders that iteratively refine target position. The total loss combines BCE for TTS relevance scores, BCE for ASA attribute classification, KL divergence for temporal start/end, and L1+IoU for spatial boxes.

## Results

On HCSTVG-v1, TA-STVG achieves state-of-the-art performance with 53.0% m_tIoU, 39.1% m_vIoU, 63.1% vIoU@0.3, and 36.8% vIoU@0.5, outperforming the previous best (CG-STVG) by 0.2%, 0.7%, 1.6%, and 0.5% respectively. On HCSTVG-v2, it reaches 60.4% m_tIoU and 40.2% m_vIoU. On VidSTG, TA-STVG achieves 51.7% m_tIoU and 34.4% m_vIoU for declarative sentences and 50.2% m_tIoU and 29.5% m_vIoU for interrogative sentences, setting new SOTA on all 8 metrics. Compared to its own zero-initialized query baseline, TA-STVG gains 3.1% m_tIoU and 5.5% vIoU@0.3 on HCSTVG-v1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TA-STVG outperforms all 12 compared baselines on HCSTVG-v1 across all four metrics. The closest competitor is CG-STVG (Gu et al., 2024a), which TA-STVG surpasses by 0.2% m_tIoU, 0.7% m_vIoU, 1.6% vIoU@0.3, and 0.5% vIoU@0.5. STVGFormer (Lin et al., 2023b) is competitive on vIoU@0.3 (62.2 vs. 63.1) but lags on m_vIoU (36.9 vs. 39.1) and does not report m_tIoU. STCAT (Jin et al., 2022) trails by 3.6% m_tIoU and 4.0% m_vIoU, while TubeDETR (Yang et al., 2022a) is further behind at 43.7% m_tIoU. On the more challenging Hard-HCSTVG-v1 subset (occlusions, distractors, noisy text), TA-STVG maintains its lead over CG-STVG (45.9 vs. 45.5 m_tIoU) and STCAT (45.9 vs. 42.9 m_tIoU), with a larger margin over its own baseline (45.9 vs. 43.1 m_tIoU), confirming robustness in complex scenarios. When TTS and ASA are plugged into TubeDETR and STCAT, they yield consistent gains of 2.3%/1.9% and 1.7%/1.8% in m_tIoU/m_vIoU respectively, demonstrating generality beyond the full TA-STVG architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DETR-style encoder-decoder architecture adopted by prior Transformer-based STVG methods (TubeDETR, STCAT, STVGFormer, CG-STVG), all of which initialize object queries with zeros. The key departure is the introduction of two lightweight modules—Text-Guided Temporal Sampling (TTS) and Attribute-Aware Spatial Activation (ASA)—that generate target-aware object queries from the video-text pair before the decoder, rather than relying on the decoder to learn target information from scratch. The multimodal feature fusion strategy (concatenating appearance, motion, and text features followed by self-attention) follows CG-STVG, while the spatial/temporal dual-decoder structure follows STCAT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The oracle experiment demonstrating a ~19% absolute m_IoU gap between groundtruth-generated and zero-initialized queries provides strong evidence that query initialization is a critical bottleneck in Transformer-based STVG, a factor previously overlooked. The TTS and ASA modules are general-purpose and plug into existing architectures (TubeDETR, STCAT) with consistent gains, suggesting the target-aware query paradigm could benefit a broader class of detection/grounding tasks. However, the actual method closes only a small fraction of the oracle gap (~3% vs. ~19%), and the computational cost remains high (32 A100 GPUs, ~14.5h training), leaving open the question of how to better approximate oracle-quality queries and how to make STVG more resource-efficient.
