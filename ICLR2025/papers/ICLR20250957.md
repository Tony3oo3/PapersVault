## Problem

In long-tailed recognition, the Decoupled Training paradigm separates representation learning from classifier re-training, but prior work improved both stages simultaneously, obscuring the individual contribution of classifier re-training. No unified framework existed to fairly compare re-training methods, and the commonly used weight-norm metric for diagnosing overfitting was shown to be unreliable because it ignores feature magnitude. The paper asks: what actually makes classifier re-training effective, and can a simple, distribution-agnostic method achieve state-of-the-art results?

## Input / Output

The method operates in a two-stage decoupled pipeline: the first stage trains a CNN backbone (ResNet34, ResNeXt50, or ResNet50) with standard cross-entropy to produce frozen feature vectors, and the second stage fine-tunes only the linear classifier head on those frozen features. Input images are standard RGB (32×32 for CIFAR100-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT, variable for iNaturalist2018), and the output is a single class label among K classes. The classifier re-training requires no knowledge of per-class sample counts, distinguishing it from re-weighting or margin-based baselines.

## Method

The core contribution is Label Over-Smoothing (LOS), which replaces one-hot labels with soft labels assigning probability (1−δ+δ/K) to the true class and δ/K to each other class, with δ set to 0.98–0.99—far exceeding the conventional label-smoothing value of 0.2. The theoretical analysis introduces two new metrics: Logits Magnitude (LoMa), the per-class difference between mean true-class and mean non-true-class logits, and Regularized Standard Deviation (σ/L), showing that balanced and reduced LoMa correlates with lower training perturbation impact on minority classes. By over-smoothing labels, LOS directly reduces LoMa without constraining classifier weight norms, preserving the head's expressive capacity while mitigating majority-class dominance. The approach is a plug-and-play classifier re-training module that can be appended to existing backbones and has been shown to improve upper-bound methods (RIDE, PaCo, GML, ProCo) by 0.3–2.1 points on the few-class split.

## Results

LOS achieves state-of-the-art top-1 accuracy on all three long-tailed benchmarks: 54.9% on CIFAR100-LT (IR=100), 54.4% on ImageNet-LT, and 70.8% on iNaturalist2018. On CIFAR100-LT, LOS outperforms the previous best (LTWB, 53.4%) by 1.5 points, with the gap widening to 1.1 points at IR=50 and 1.0 at IR=10. On ImageNet-LT, LOS improves the few-class accuracy from 41.5% (LTWB) to 42.3%, and on iNaturalist2018 it reaches 71.3% few-class accuracy versus 70.2% (DisAlign). LOS also serves as a plug-and-play module, adding 0.3–0.6 points to upper-bound methods like GML and ProCo when applied as a classifier fine-tuning step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOS outperforms all 21 baselines on overall accuracy across all three datasets: CIFAR100-LT IR=100 (54.9% vs. 53.4% LTWB), ImageNet-LT (54.4% vs. 53.9% LTWB), and iNaturalist2018 (70.8% vs. 70.6% DisAlign). The most significant gains appear in the few-class split, where LOS achieves 42.3% on ImageNet-LT (vs. 41.5% LTWB, 35.1% EWB-FDR, 31.8% BALMS) and 71.3% on iNaturalist2018 (vs. 70.2% DisAlign, 69.7% LTWB). LOS slightly trails RBL on ImageNet-LT many-class accuracy (63.2% vs. 64.8%) and LTWB on iNaturalist2018 many-class (69.2% vs. 71.2%), but compensates with stronger tail performance. Among the upper-bound methods in Table 3, LOS as a plug-in adds 0.3–0.6 points to GML, ProCo, PaCo, and BCL on overall accuracy, with the largest few-class gains of +1.8 to +2.1 points. On CIFAR100-LT, LOS's advantage over the strongest decoupled-training baselines (LTWB, RBL, EWB-FDR) is consistent at 1.0–1.9 points across all three imbalance ratios, while the gap over older methods like BALMS (50.8%) and τ-norm (47.7%) is substantially larger.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Decoupled Training paradigm (Kang et al., 2019), which separates long-tailed recognition into representation learning and classifier re-training stages, and on the conventional Label Smoothing formulation (Szegedy et al., 2016). Its key departure is conceptual: rather than designing new classifier architectures, re-weighting schemes, or weight-norm constraints (as in LDAM, Balanced-Softmax, MaxNorm, τ-norm), the authors argue that the fundamental objective should be balancing Logits Magnitude across classes, and that this can be achieved by pushing the label smoothing factor δ to near-extreme values (0.98–0.99) rather than the typical 0.2. The unified re-evaluation under LTWB's feature representations also departs from prior work by isolating the classifier re-training contribution from representation learning, which previous studies conflated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a much-needed fair benchmark for classifier re-training methods and introduces two analytical metrics (Logits Magnitude and Regularized Standard Deviation) that offer a more principled way to diagnose model behavior than the previously used weight-norm comparisons. LOS's simplicity (one hyperparameter, no class-distribution knowledge required) and plug-and-play nature make it practically attractive, and the finding that weight-norm constraints are an unreliable proxy for class balance (Proposition 2) challenges a common assumption in the LTR literature. However, the method is essentially an aggressive regularization that limits classifier capacity, and on larger datasets (ImageNet-LT) upper-bound methods with stronger feature learning still outperform it, suggesting the approach's ceiling is tied to backbone quality. Open questions include whether the LoMa-balancing objective can be optimized more directly during training and whether the near-extreme smoothing factor generalizes to other imbalanced learning settings beyond image classification.
