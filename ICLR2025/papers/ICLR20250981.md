## Problem

Gradient clipping is a standard practical technique in modern deep learning, yet its effectiveness remains poorly understood: it is unclear how the gradient norm distribution determines whether clipping helps or hurts, how the clipping threshold should scale with problem size, and under what noise conditions clipping provides a genuine advantage over unclipped SGD. This paper addresses these gaps by developing a complete high-dimensional theoretical framework for clipped SGD under streaming linear regression.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on streaming (one-pass) linear regression data where features x ∈ R^d are Gaussian with covariance K and targets y = ⟨x, θ*⟩ + ε with noise ε drawn from various distributions (Gaussian, Cauchy, Rademacher-like, Student-t). The method takes a learning rate schedule η(t) and clipping threshold schedule c(t) as inputs and produces a deterministic system of ODEs governing the risk R_t and distance-to-optimality D_t, along with a one-parameter heuristic clipping schedule c^a(t) = κ√(2R_t + σ²) paired with a compensated learning rate. The framework is validated against real CIFAR-10 and Wikitext-2 training runs.

## Method

The core contribution is Clipped Homogenized SGD (C-HSGD), an SDE that approximates clipped SGD in the large intrinsic dimension limit, with two unitless reduction factors: μ_c (descent reduction, the probability of not clipping under Gaussian data) and ν_c (variance reduction of clipped gradients). By applying Itô's lemma and concentration of measure, the SDE yields a deterministic system of ODEs for the risk that closely tracks actual clipped SGD trajectories. The paper derives two key criteria: the Clipped-Stability-Criterion (CSC), μ_c/ν_c > 1, which determines when clipping increases the stable learning rate range, and the Clipping-Comparison-Criterion (CCC), μ_c²/ν_c > 1, which determines when clipping strictly improves optimization over unclipped SGD. The theory proves that clipping never helps under Gaussian noise (Theorem 5) but provably improves performance under Rademacher-like or heavy-tailed noise (Theorem 6), and proposes a simple heuristic schedule requiring only one tunable hyperparameter κ that matches the optimal max-(CCC) schedule in practice.

## Results

The paper establishes that in the high-dimensional limit, the risk of clipped SGD is approximated by a deterministic ODE with error O(log(d)·d^{-1/2}) (Theorem 1). The key theoretical finding is that with Gaussian noise, the clipping-comparison-criterion (CCC) is never satisfied (μ²/ν ≤ 1 for all c, R), meaning clipped SGD can never outperform unclipped SGD (Theorem 5). Conversely, with Rademacher-like noise, the CCC is satisfied at low risk, and clipping provably improves optimization (Theorem 6). Empirically, the proposed single-hyperparameter heuristic schedule (c^a(t) = κ√(2R_t + σ²)) achieves essentially identical risk curves to the numerically optimal max-(CCC) schedule on synthetic data (Figure 4), and the deterministic ODE closely tracks actual clipped SGD risk on CIFAR-10 and Wikitext-2 (Figure 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three configurations: unclipped SGD (c ≡ ∞), clipped SGD with the optimal max-(CCC) schedule, and clipped SGD with the proposed heuristic schedule. Under Gaussian noise (Figure 4a), clipping provides no improvement over unclipped SGD, consistent with Theorem 5. Under Rademacher-like noise (Figure 4b), both the optimal and heuristic clipped schedules achieve lower risk than unclipped SGD, with the heuristic matching the optimal schedule almost exactly. On real data (CIFAR-10, Wikitext-2 in Figure 1), the deterministic ODE derived from C-HSGD closely tracks the actual clipped SGD risk curve, validating the theoretical framework. The paper also shows that unclipped SGD diverges under Cauchy noise while clipped SGD converges, providing a clear separation between the two methods in heavy-tailed regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the high-dimensional homogenized SGD framework of Paquette et al. (2022) from unclipped to clipped SGD by introducing two dimensionless reduction factors (μ_c for descent bias and ν_c for variance reduction) that capture clipping's effect within a stochastic differential equation. The proof techniques for bounding martingale and error terms follow closely the approach of Collins-Woodfin & Paquette (2023) for the unclipped case. Conceptually, the work connects to Zhang et al. (2020b)'s empirical observation that gradient noise is Gaussian for ResNets but heavy-tailed for BERT, providing the first quantitative theoretical criterion (the CCC) that explains *why* clipping helps in the latter but not the former regime, and to prior fixed-rate convergence analyses (Koloskova et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2020a) by moving to a dynamic, schedule-dependent perspective in the high-dimensional limit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first high-dimensional theoretical account of gradient clipping dynamics, yielding a single-hyperparameter heuristic (c(t) = κ√(2R_t + σ²)) that matches the optimal clipping schedule and is directly applicable to practitioners tuning clipping thresholds. The clean result that clipping provably never improves SGD under Gaussian noise (Theorem 5) while it does under Rademacher-like or heavy-tailed noise (Theorem 6) gives a principled explanation for the empirical observation that clipping is most beneficial in NLP/attention models. Key limitations include restriction to linear models with MSE loss in a streaming (no mini-batch) setting; extending the framework to non-linear losses and deep networks is identified as important future work, and the practical validation on CIFAR10 and Wikitext2, while promising, uses assumed noise distributions rather than empirically measured ones.
