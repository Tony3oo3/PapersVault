## Problem

Diffusion and flow-matching models produce high-quality samples but require dozens to hundreds of iterative denoising steps, making inference slow and expensive. Existing speedup methods—distillation, progressive distillation, and consistency models—rely on multi-stage training pipelines, multiple networks, or fragile discretization schedules, preventing a single end-to-end model that supports arbitrary inference budgets. The paper asks whether a single generative objective can be trained in one run to produce high-quality samples at any step count, from 128 down to 1.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251238/cropped_page0_idx9.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a Gaussian noise vector in the latent space of a pre-trained VAE (sd-vae-ft-mse, mapping 256×256×3 images to 32×32×4 latents), an optional class label for conditional generation, and a desired step size d as conditioning. It outputs a denoised latent that is decoded to a 256×256 RGB image. At inference, the user freely chooses the number of Euler steps (1 to 128) without retraining; the same checkpoint handles all budgets. For the robotic control application, the input is a sequence of past observations and the output is a single action vector, replacing the 100-step diffusion policy with one forward pass.

## Method

Shortcut models condition a single flow-matching network on both the current noise level t and a desired step size d, learning a 'shortcut' s(x_t, t, d) that points directly toward the correct next point x_{t+d}' rather than the instantaneous ODE velocity. Training exploits a self-consistency property: a shortcut of size 2d equals the concatenation of two sequential shortcuts of size d, so targets for larger steps are bootstrapped from the model's own smaller-step predictions. The combined loss jointly optimizes a standard flow-matching regression at d=0 (grounding the model to empirical velocity samples) and a self-consistency L2 loss at d>0 (propagating generation capability from many-step to one-step), using a binary recursive scheme over 128 base timesteps. This requires only a single network and a single training run with no schedule, adding roughly 16% compute over a base diffusion model, and the model retains full many-step generation quality while enabling one-step sampling.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ-256 (unconditional), Shortcut Models achieve FID-50k of 6.9 (128-step), 13.8 (4-step), and 20.5 (1-step). On ImageNet-256 (class-conditional), they achieve FID-50k of 15.5 (128-step), 28.3 (4-step), and 40.3 (1-step). In the robotic control domain, Shortcut Policies achieve success rates of 0.87 (Push-T) and 0.80 (Transport) in a single inference step, compared to 0.12 and 0.00 for one-step Diffusion Policies. At DiT-XL scale, Shortcut Models reach a one-step FID of 10.6 and 128-step FID of 3.8 on ImageNet-256.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among end-to-end methods, Shortcut Models dominate across all step budgets: at 1-step they achieve FID 20.5/40.3 (CelebA/ImageNet), beating Consistency Training (33.2/69.7) by 12.7/29.4 points and Live Reflow (43.3/58.1) by 22.8/17.8 points. Against the two-stage Reflow baseline, Shortcut Models win at 4-step (13.8 vs 18.4 on CelebA, 28.3 vs 32.8 on ImageNet) and 1-step (20.5 vs 23.2, 40.3 vs 44.8), while also matching its 128-step quality. Progressive Distillation achieves a slightly better 1-step FID (14.8 vs 20.5 on CelebA, 35.6 vs 40.3 on ImageNet) but catastrophically fails at many-step sampling (FID 302.9/201.9), whereas Shortcut Models retain full many-step capability. Against Consistency Distillation, Shortcut Models win decisively at every step count (e.g., 1-step: 20.5 vs 38.2 on CelebA, 40.3 vs 136.5 on ImageNet). In robotic control, Shortcut Policies (0.87/0.80) outperform all one-step baselines—BET (0.79/0.38) and LSTM-GMM (0.67/0.76)—while approaching the quality of 100-step Diffusion Policy (0.95/1.00) at 100× lower inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

Shortcut models extend the flow-matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2022) by conditioning the network on a desired step size d in addition to the noise level t, enabling the model to 'jump ahead' in the denoising ODE. Conceptually, the self-consistency target (two half-steps construct one full step) mirrors the binary time-distillation structure of progressive distillation (Salimans & Ho, 2022), but is applied end-to-end within a single training run rather than across separate teacher-student phases. The paper departs from consistency models (Song et al., 2023) by sampling intermediate points from the learned ODE rather than from empirical data pairings, avoiding accumulated discretization bias, and by requiring only log₂(T) bootstrap steps versus T. The robotic control application directly builds on diffusion policy (Chi et al., 2023), replacing its iterative denoising with a single shortcut step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the elimination of multi-phase training pipelines: a single network trained in one run with ~16% overhead over base diffusion supports any inference budget from 1 to 128 steps, with no schedules or warmups. The finding that this bootstrap-based method retains scaling with model size (DiT-XL achieves FID 10.6 one-step on ImageNet-256) is notable given that bootstrap-based methods in RL are known to suffer rank collapse. Key limitations include a persistent gap between many-step and one-step quality, the requirement to fix the CFG scale before training, and the fact that the noise-to-data mapping is entirely determined by the dataset expectation with no mechanism to adjust it. Open questions include whether one-step training can improve many-step generation (the paper observes a slight FID improvement) and whether iterative mapping adjustments (e.g., Reflow-like) can close the quality gap.
