## Problem

Integrating dense prediction tasks like image segmentation into Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) remains challenging because existing approaches either append additional visual decoders (e.g., SAM) that complicate end-to-end training and limit scalability, or convert masks into polygon coordinates that LLMs struggle to associate with shapes. The paper seeks a segmentation representation that adheres to the next-token prediction paradigm, requires no architectural modifications, and fully leverages the text generation capabilities of LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250064/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a natural-language query (a referring expression like 'the brown dog on the left' or a list of class labels) in a standard instruction-following template. It outputs a 16×16 grid of semantic descriptors (text labels per patch) compressed via Row-wise Run-Length Encoding, which is then optionally refined by SAM (ViT-H) into a pixel-level binary mask; for referring expression comprehension, the coarse mask is converted to a bounding box via a simple mask2box operation. The R-RLE compression reduces the average token length from ~583 to ~154 without performance loss.

## Method

Text4Seg introduces a text-as-mask paradigm where each of the 16×16 image patches is mapped to a semantic text label (a word, phrase, or short sentence), forming a purely textual sequence called semantic descriptors that is generated autoregressively by the MLLM. To handle spatial redundancy, Row-wise Run-Length Encoding (R-RLE) compresses consecutive identical descriptors within each row (separated by '\n'), achieving 74% length reduction and 3× faster inference while preserving spatial structure—unlike image-wise RLE which causes notable performance drops. Training requires no architectural changes: the model is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 64) using the standard autoregressive cross-entropy loss on instruction-following data constructed by converting existing segmentation masks into semantic descriptor format. An off-the-shelf SAM (ViT-H) is applied as a post-hoc mask refiner to upgrade the coarse 16×16 output to pixel-level masks, and the entire pipeline is validated across four MLLM backbones (LLaVA-1.5, Qwen-VL, DeepseekVL, InternVL2) at 1.3B–13B parameter scales.

## Results

On the refCOCO (+/g) referring expression segmentation benchmark, Text4Seg achieves state-of-the-art performance with an average cIoU of 75.4 (InternVL2-8B backbone) and 76.2 (LLaVA-1.5-13B), surpassing the previous best generalist model Groundhog (74.2) and GSVA (71.4/72.8). On the generalized referring expression segmentation (grefCOCO) task, Text4Seg_LLaVA-1.5-13B reaches 71.5 average cIoU, outperforming GSVA by 4.9 points. For referring expression comprehension (Acc@0.5), Text4Seg_InternVL2-8B achieves 87.1% average accuracy, the highest among all generalist models, and Text4Seg_LLaVA-1.5-13B reaches 87.7%, exceeding the previous SOTA Shikra by 3.7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Text4Seg (InternVL2-8B) achieves 75.4 average cIoU on refCOCO (+/g), outperforming Groundhog (74.2) by 1.2 points, GSVA (71.4) by 4.0 points, and LISA (69.9) by 5.5 points. At the 13B scale, Text4Seg_LLaVA-1.5-13B reaches 76.2 cIoU, beating GSVA-13B (72.8) by 3.4 points and LISA-13B (70.1) by 6.1 points. GLaMM reports a slightly higher 75.6 cIoU but relies on a training dataset two orders of magnitude larger (11M images, 810M masks) compared to Text4Seg's 800k samples. On the GRES task (grefCOCO), Text4Seg_LLaVA-1.5-13B achieves 71.5 average cIoU, surpassing GSVA-13B (66.6) by 4.9 points. For referring expression comprehension, Text4Seg_InternVL2-8B reaches 87.1% Acc@0.5, exceeding Groma (86.5%), PixelLM (86.0%), and the InternVL2-8B baseline (82.9%) by 4.2 points. Notably, even without the SAM mask refiner, Text4Seg with 32×32 semantic descriptors (71.4 cIoU) matches GSVA's performance, demonstrating the inherent strength of the text-as-mask representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'embeddings-as-mask' paradigm (LISA, GSVA, GLaMM, PixelLM, Groundhog) that appends segmentation decoders like SAM to MLLMs, and from the 'polygon coordinates' approach (VisionLLM) that encodes masks as numerical sequences. Instead, Text4Seg introduces a 'text-as-mask' paradigm where each image patch is mapped to a semantic text label, forming a purely textual sequence (semantic descriptors) that fits naturally into the autoregressive next-token prediction objective. Conceptually, it repurposes ViT's patch-based image representation for text generation, requiring no architectural modifications to existing MLLM backbones and no additional loss functions, in contrast to prior methods that necessitate continued pre-training with large mixed datasets before task-specific fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for task-specific decoders and additional loss functions, Text4Seg offers a significantly simpler and more scalable path to integrating dense prediction into MLLMs, which is practically important as the field moves toward unified multimodal models. The R-RLE compression (74% token reduction, 3× speedup) addresses a key efficiency bottleneck without performance loss. However, the approach still relies on SAM or CRF as a post-processing refiner for pixel-level accuracy, and open-vocabulary segmentation performance lags behind specialized models, suggesting that the text-as-mask paradigm may need further refinement for tasks requiring very fine-grained spatial detail. An open question is whether increasing semantic descriptor resolution (e.g., 32×32 or higher) can eventually eliminate the need for external refiners entirely.
