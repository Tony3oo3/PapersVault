## Problem

While LLMs have been shown to possess linear representations of concepts like sentiment, spatial direction, and truth, it remains unknown whether they encode political ideology as a linear direction in activation space. This paper asks whether the liberal-conservative axis in American politics is linearly represented in transformer attention heads, whether such representations generalize beyond the specific prompting context in which they are discovered, and whether they can be leveraged to monitor and steer the political perspective of generated text.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts naming specific U.S. lawmakers (552 from the 116th Congress), news outlets (400 from Ad Fontes Media), or policy issues, and produces three types of output: (1) a scalar political slant score per entity via ensembled linear probes over the K=32 most predictive attention heads, (2) a token-by-token ideological measurement during open-ended generation that can be visualized as a color gradient, and (3) steered text when an intervention parameter α ∈ [-30, 30] is applied. Notably, the same probes trained exclusively on lawmaker DW-NOMINATE scores are applied without retraining to news outlet activations and to open-ended essay generation, and the steering intervention is applied iteratively at every generated token across all K targeted heads simultaneously.

## Method

The paper prompts three 7B-parameter LLMs (Llama-2-7b-chat, Mistral-7b-instruct, Vicuna-7b) to generate statements from the perspective of each of 552 U.S. lawmakers, records the activation x_ℓ,h of every attention head (32 layers × 32 heads = 1,024 heads), and fits a separate ridge-regression linear probe to each head to predict that lawmaker's first-dimension DW-NOMINATE score. Middle-layer heads (approximately layers 10–20) achieve cross-validated Spearman correlations of ~0.85, and non-linear MLP probes provide no meaningful improvement, supporting the linear representation hypothesis. The trained probe coefficients θ̂_ℓ,h are then repurposed as steering vectors: during generation, each targeted head's activation is replaced with x_ℓ,h + α·σ̂_ℓ,h·θ̂_ℓ,h at every token, where α controls the direction and magnitude of ideological shift. This intervention, applied at the K most predictive heads, reliably shifts GPT-4o-rated political slant of generated essays (correlation up to 0.607 with α), generalizes to events outside the model's pre-training data, and is effective only when targeting early-to-middle layers rather than later layers.

## Results

Linear probes on middle-layer attention heads predict U.S. lawmakers' DW-NOMINATE scores with Spearman correlations of 0.854 (Llama-2-7b-chat, Layer 15/Head 18), 0.846 (Mistral-7b-instruct, Layer 16/Head 3), and 0.861 (Vicuna-7b, Layer 24/Head 8). Ensembling the K=32 most predictive heads yields correlations of 0.870, 0.864, and 0.885 respectively. The same probes generalize to predict Ad Fontes Media news-outlet slant with correlations of 0.798, 0.764, and 0.720, and to cross-national party ideology (Manifesto Project) at ρ=0.531. Steering interventions on these heads produce a correlation of 0.607 (Llama-2-7b-chat), 0.396 (Mistral-7b-instruct), and 0.381 (Vicuna-7b) between the intervention parameter α and measured political slant of generated text.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This interpretability paper does not benchmark against competing probing or steering methods, so there are no direct method-vs-method comparisons. The key internal comparisons are: (1) linear vs. non-linear (one-layer ReLU MLP) probes, where linear probes match or slightly exceed non-linear ones (e.g., Llama-2-7b-chat: 0.854 vs. 0.855; Mistral-7b-instruct: 0.846 vs. 0.838; Vicuna-7b: 0.861 vs. 0.872), supporting the linear representation hypothesis. (2) Generalization from the training domain (lawmaker DW-NOMINATE) to an unseen domain (news-outlet Ad Fontes slant) retains strong performance (0.798–0.720), confirming the probes capture a generalizable ideological axis rather than memorized labels. (3) Cross-national generalization to the Manifesto Project drops to ρ=0.531, with strong performance in culturally similar countries (New Zealand ρ=0.920, Australia ρ=0.916) but weaker or negative correlations elsewhere, indicating the linear axis is most effective in polarized, single-dimension political environments. (4) Intervention on early-to-middle layers (Layers 1–21) is effective (ρ=0.540) while intervention on middle-to-last layers (Layers 22–32) has negligible effect (ρ=−0.022), localizing the causal role of the identified direction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear probing methodology established for concepts like sentiment, space, and time (Gurnee & Tegmark, 2024; Nanda et al., 2023) to the domain of political ideology, using DW-NOMINATE scores as ground-truth labels. It also adapts the inference-time intervention framework of Li et al. (2023), which was designed for eliciting truthful answers, to steer political slant in generated text. The paper positions itself as the first to test whether the 'liberal-conservative' axis is represented linearly in LLM activation space, connecting the mechanistic interpretability literature to the political science tradition of ideological scaling (DW-NOMINATE, Ad Fontes Media).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, lightweight tool for monitoring and steering the political perspective in LLM outputs without fine-tuning, which is valuable for transparency auditing and for building controllable synthetic agents. The clean generalization of probes from U.S. lawmakers to news outlets (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.80) without retraining is a strong validation that a genuine linear ideological axis exists in activation space. Key limitations include the U.S.-centric focus (cross-national performance drops to ρ ≈ 0.53), reliance on GPT-4o as an evaluator, and testing only on 7B-parameter models. Open questions include whether multi-dimensional or non-linear ideological structures emerge in larger models, and how these representations interact with other cultural axes (class, gender, expertise).
