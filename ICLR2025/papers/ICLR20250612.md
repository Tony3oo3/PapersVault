## Problem

Existing time series models struggle to serve as universal pattern machines because they cannot simultaneously capture multi-scale, multi-periodicity dynamics and adapt their representations to tasks with conflicting needs (e.g., forecasting favors consistent layer-wise representations while imputation and anomaly detection benefit from diverse ones). Transformer-based approaches treat time points as discrete tokens, but time series involve overlapping contexts at a single time point (daily, weekly, seasonal patterns co-occurring), making token-wise attention insufficient for disentangling these nested structures.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series x_0 ∈ R^{T×C} and progressively downsamples it via strided convolutions into M+1 scales (typically M=3 for long-term, M=1 for short-term forecasting). At the coarsest scale, variate-wise self-attention performs channel mixing before embedding all scales into a shared d_model-dimensional space. The output is task-adaptive: multiple scale-specific prediction heads are ensembled (averaging or weighted sum), producing forecasted values, imputed values, class labels, or anomaly scores depending on the downstream task.

## Method

TIMEMIXER++ is an encoder-only architecture of L stacked MixerBlocks, each performing four operations: (1) Multi-Resolution Time Imaging (MRTI) applies FFT on the coarsest scale to identify top-K dominant periods, then reshapes each scale's 1D series into K 2D time images of size p_k × f_{m,k}; (2) Time Image Decomposition (TID) uses dual-axis attention—column-axis attention captures seasonality within periods while row-axis attention extracts trend across periods, with 2D convolutions computing Q/K/V and the non-target axis transposed to the batch dimension for efficiency; (3) Multi-Scale Mixing (MCM) propagates seasonal patterns bottom-up (fine→coarse) via 2D convolutions and trend patterns top-down (coarse→fine) via 2D transposed convolutions in a residual manner; (4) Multi-Resolution Mixing (MRM) adaptively fuses the K period representations at each scale using softmax-normalized FFT amplitudes as element-wise weights. This multi-scale, multi-resolution hierarchy enables the model to simultaneously capture high-frequency local patterns and low-frequency global trends while adapting its representational structure to task-specific demands.

## Results

TIMEMIXER++ achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 8 evaluated time series tasks. On long-term forecasting (averaged over prediction lengths 96–720), it attains the lowest MSE of 0.165 on Electricity and 0.349 on ETT (Avg), outperforming iTransformer by 7.3% MSE on Electricity. On the M4 univariate benchmark, it records SMAPE 11.448, MASE 1.487, and OWA 0.821; on PEMS multivariate forecasting, MAE 15.91 and MAPE 10.08. For classification it reaches 75.9% accuracy and for anomaly detection an F1 of 87.47%, both leading all 27 baselines tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIMEMIXER++ consistently outperforms all 27 baselines across every task. Against its direct predecessor TimeMixer, it reduces MSE by 4.9% on ETT (Avg) and by 9.4% on few-shot forecasting, with the gap widening on high-dimensional datasets (e.g., 19.9% lower MAE on PEMS). Compared to iTransformer, the closest transformer-based competitor, TIMEMIXER++ achieves 7.3% lower MSE on Electricity, 9.7% lower SMAPE on M4, and 13.1% lower MSE in zero-shot transfer. Against PatchTST, improvements are particularly large on multivariate short-term forecasting (30.8% lower MAE on PEMS) and few-shot settings (13.2% lower MSE). TimesNet, the strongest CNN-based baseline, is surpassed by 25.7% in imputation MSE and by 2.3% in classification accuracy, while Crossformer and Autoformer show the largest absolute gaps (e.g., 0.940 vs 0.357 MSE on Exchange). DLinear remains competitive on Exchange (0.354 vs 0.357 MSE) but is outperformed on all other datasets, confirming that the multi-scale/multi-resolution architecture provides consistent gains beyond simple linear mapping.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIMEMIXER++ directly extends TimeMixer (Wang et al., ICLR 2024) by augmenting its multi-scale mixing with three new components: multi-resolution time imaging (MRTI), time image decomposition (TID) via dual-axis attention, and multi-resolution mixing (MRM). The 2D time image concept is borrowed from TimesNet (Wu et al., ICLR 2023), but generalized from two fixed periods to K data-driven periods selected via FFT, and applied across multiple scales simultaneously. The paper departs from the channel-independence strategy of iTransformer and PatchTST in favor of variate-wise self-attention for cross-variable interaction, and replaces the moving-average-based seasonality/trend decomposition of Autoformer and FEDformer with a learned dual-axis attention mechanism operating in the latent image space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central ambition is to establish a single architecture (TSPM) that achieves competitive or SOTA performance across eight heterogeneous time series tasks, which if validated would simplify the practitioner's model-selection burden. The CKA representation analysis offering a mechanistic explanation for why different tasks favor different layer-wise representation structures (consistent vs. diverse) is a useful diagnostic lens, though it remains correlational. Open questions include how the FFT-based period selection generalizes to non-stationary or multi-periodic signals where dominant frequencies shift over time, whether the multi-scale downsampling (limited to M≤3 in practice) constrains effective context length, and whether the architectural complexity is justified over simpler MLP-based alternatives like DLinear on less structured data.
