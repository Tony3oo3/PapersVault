## Problem

GSM8K, the dominant benchmark for evaluating LLM mathematical reasoning, provides only a single accuracy metric on a fixed set of questions, making it impossible to distinguish genuine reasoning from pattern-matching and leaving results vulnerable to data contamination. The paper asks whether reported GSM8K scores are statistically reliable and whether LLMs' mathematical reasoning is robust to superficial changes in problem instantiation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253417/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance is a grade-school math word problem generated from a symbolic template (100 base templates × 50 instantiations = 5,000 examples per benchmark variant), presented to the model with 8-shot chain-of-thought prompting and greedy decoding. Rather than reporting a single accuracy number, the method produces a distribution of 50 per-set accuracy scores (each set containing 100 questions), enabling measurement of mean performance, variance, and sensitivity to specific perturbations such as name changes, value changes, clause count, or injected irrelevant information.

## Method

The core contribution is a symbolic template generation pipeline that converts GSM8K test questions into parsable templates by identifying variables, their domains, and correctness conditions (e.g., divisibility for whole-number answers), then automatically generates diverse instantiations while preserving the underlying reasoning structure. From these templates, the authors construct a family of benchmarks at controlled difficulty levels—GSM-Symbolic-M1 (one clause removed), GSM-Symbolic (base), GSM-Symbolic-P1 (one clause added), GSM-Symbolic-P2 (two clauses added)—and a GSM-NoOp variant that injects seemingly relevant but operationally irrelevant statements. By evaluating 25+ open and closed models across all variants, the paper demonstrates that performance degrades with numerical value changes, increases in clause count, and the addition of irrelevant information (up to 65% drop), and that this degradation cannot be recovered even with in-context examples of the same question or examples containing similar No-Op clauses, suggesting LLMs rely on probabilistic pattern matching rather than formal logical reasoning.

## Results

Across 25 state-of-the-art models evaluated with 8-shot CoT prompting, all exhibit significant performance drops from GSM8K to GSM-Symbolic, with the gap ranging from negligible (GPT-4o: 95.0% → 94.9%) to substantial (Phi-3-mini: 85.0% → 80.7%). Performance degrades progressively as question difficulty increases (M1 → Symbolic → P1 → P2), with variance also growing consistently. The most striking finding is the catastrophic drop on GSM-NoOp: Phi-3-mini falls from 80.7% (Symbolic) to 18.0% (NoOp), a 65%+ decline, while even o1-preview drops from 92.7% to 77.4%. All models show non-negligible variance across 50 instantiations of the same templates (e.g., Gemma2-9B spans >12% between worst and best set), and for 21 of 25 models, the original GSM8K score lies more than one standard deviation above the GSM-Symbolic distribution mean, suggesting potential data contamination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 25 models (open and closed) as the primary comparison subjects. Among open models, Gemma2-27b-it and Phi-3-medium show the strongest GSM-Symbolic performance (88.3% and 82.5% respectively), but both drop sharply on GSM-P2 (63.4% and 53.1%) and NoOp (30.0% and 29.4%). Closed models (GPT-4o, o1-mini, o1-preview) maintain near-GSM8K accuracy on GSM-Symbolic (94.9%, 94.5%, 92.7%) and show the smallest difficulty-driven degradation, yet all still suffer substantial NoOp drops (63.1%, 66.0%, 77.4%). Notably, o1-preview is the only model that maintains >90% on GSM-P2 (94.0%), but its NoOp score (77.4%) still represents a 15+ point drop. The key finding across all comparisons is that no model, regardless of scale or architecture, is robust to the addition of irrelevant clauses, with the worst NoOp drops (Phi-3-mini: 80.7% → 18.0%) exceeding 65 percentage points. Fine-tuning on easier variants (GSM-P1) does not transfer to harder ones (GSM-P2), and providing 8 in-context shots of the same question with No-Op clauses fails to recover performance, indicating the limitation is architectural rather than data-driven.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on robustness evaluation of LLMs on mathematical word problems (Stolfo et al., 2023; Hong et al., 2024; Srivastava et al., 2024) by introducing symbolic templates that enable systematic, controllable generation of question variants, going beyond the fixed-size variants in GSM-Plus (Li et al., 2024a) or the prompting-focused analysis in GSM-IC (Shi et al., 2023). It also builds on the broader evidence that LLM reasoning is fragile and pattern-matching-based (Jiang et al., 2024; Schaeffer et al., 2023; Dziri et al., 2023; Nezhurina et al., 2024), providing a concrete, large-scale empirical framework to quantify this fragility across 25 state-of-the-art models. The key methodological departure from prior benchmarks is treating model performance as a distribution across many instantiations of the same logical question rather than a single accuracy score, enabling detection of data contamination and sensitivity to specific perturbation types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges the reliability of GSM8K, the most widely used math reasoning benchmark, by showing that reported scores may be inflated by data contamination (21/25 models score >1 std dev above their GSM-Symbolic distribution) and that single-point accuracy metrics mask substantial performance variance. The GSM-NoOp finding—that a single irrelevant clause causes up to 65% performance drops across all tested models, including o1-preview, and that even 8 in-context shots of the correct reasoning chain fail to mitigate this—suggests a fundamental limitation in LLMs' ability to perform formal reasoning rather than sophisticated pattern matching. Open questions include whether architectural changes (e.g., structured memory, SSMs) or training procedures can overcome this fragility, and whether the pattern-matching hypothesis generalizes beyond grade-school arithmetic to more complex mathematical domains.
