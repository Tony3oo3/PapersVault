## Problem

Transformer-based LLMs struggle to process contexts far exceeding their training window due to quadratic attention cost, positional encoding extrapolation failure, and attention dilution over long sequences. Existing retrieval-based methods like InfLLM segment context into fixed-size blocks, which is suboptimal for semantically structured information. This paper asks whether principles from human episodic memory—surprise-driven event segmentation and temporally structured retrieval—can be integrated into LLMs to handle practically infinite contexts without fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an arbitrarily long token sequence in an online, streaming fashion: as each token is processed, its surprise value is computed and the KV cache is incrementally segmented into episodic events. The output context window at each generation step is a structured composition of four regions: 128 initial tokens (attention sinks), a similarity buffer of k_s events retrieved via per-layer k-NN search, a contiguity buffer of k_c temporally neighboring events, and a local context of the most recent tokens. The system achieves 100% passkey retrieval accuracy at 10.2M tokens, a scale infeasible for full-context models, while requiring no additional training or fine-tuning of the base LLM.

## Method

EM-LLM introduces three coupled mechanisms applied training-free to any pre-trained Transformer LLM. First, surprise-based segmentation identifies initial event boundaries where the negative log-likelihood of the current token exceeds a moving-window threshold (mean + γ·std over a τ-token window). Second, a graph-theoretic boundary refinement step treats the dot-product similarity matrix of attention keys within the local context as a weighted adjacency matrix and repositions each boundary to maximize modularity (or minimize conductance), optimizing intra-event key cohesion and inter-event separation at O(nm) cost. Third, a two-stage retrieval process selects events for the context window: a similarity buffer populated by per-layer k-NN search over event representative tokens, and a contiguity buffer implemented as a queue that enqueues temporally neighboring events (±n positions) to exploit the temporal contiguity and asymmetry effects already present in Transformer attention heads. Each layer retrieves and attends to events independently, enabling hierarchical, layer-specific access to the episodic memory store.

## Results

On LongBench, EM-LLM consistently outperforms InfLLM across all five base LLMs, with average scores of 43.7 vs 41.9 (Mistral v2), 47.2 vs 47.0 (LLaMA3), 51.3 vs 51.1 (LLaMA3.1), 35.4 vs 34.5 (Phi-3), and 34.9 vs 34.2 (Phi-3.5). The largest gains appear in retrieval tasks (up to +40% on PassageRetrieval with Phi-3) and multi-hop QA (up to +29.7% on Musique). Against RAG with NV-Embed-v2, EM-LLM exceeds performance by 30.5% on LongBench and 11.5% on ∞-Bench, while also surpassing full-context LLaMA-3.1-8B on most tasks. Notably, EM-LLM achieves 100% accuracy on Passkey.Retrieval at 10.2M tokens, a scale computationally infeasible for full-context models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InfLLM (the SOTA KV-retrieval baseline using fixed-size token segmentation), EM-LLM wins on the LongBench average across all five base LLMs, with the most dramatic gains in retrieval tasks: PassageRetrieval improves from 64.0→84.1 (Mistral v2) and 7.5→10.0 (Phi-3), while Retrieve.KV jumps from 95.6→99.0 (Mistral v2) and 81.0→96.8 (LLaMA3.1). On ∞-Bench, EM-LLM matches or exceeds InfLLM on Passkey and Number retrieval (100% and 99.8–100%) while improving Retrieve.KV substantially. Against RAG with NV-Embed-v2 (SOTA embedding retriever), EM-LLM achieves 47.24 vs 39.27 average on LongBench with LLaMA-3-8B, and 51.58 vs 36.44 with LLaMA-3.1-8B, outperforming by 30.5% and 11.5% on LongBench and ∞-Bench respectively. Against the smaller all-mpnet-base-v2 RAG retriever, the gap is even wider (47.24 vs 30.75 on LongBench). EM-LLM also surpasses full-context LLaMA-3.1-8B on most LongBench tasks (51.58 vs 39.30 average) and achieves 100% Passkey retrieval at 10.2M tokens where full-context is infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

EM-LLM extends the KV-retrieval paradigm of InfLLM (Xiao et al., 2024a) by replacing its fixed-size memory units with dynamically determined episodic events, segmented via Bayesian surprise and refined through graph-theoretic modularity/conductance optimization. It also incorporates the attention-sink mechanism from StreamingLLM (Xiao et al., 2024b) and the contiguity buffer is motivated by Ji-An et al. (2024), who showed that Transformer attention heads already exhibit temporal contiguity and asymmetry effects mirroring human free recall. The broader conceptual lineage draws on cognitive science models of episodic memory and event segmentation (Zacks, Fountas, Roseboom), repurposing surprise-based boundary detection and hierarchical event structure as an engineering principle for LLM context management rather than as a purely data-driven or architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EM-LLM offers a training-free, plug-and-play path to effectively unbounded context that outperforms both RAG pipelines and full-context processing while remaining computationally efficient, with demonstrated 10M-token retrieval. The validated alignment between LLM surprise and human-perceived event boundaries provides a rare computational bridge between cognitive science and LLM internals, opening avenues for using LLMs as testbeds for memory hypotheses. Open questions include whether layer-wise independent segmentation, more sophisticated clustering objectives, or integration with continual learning and imagination mechanisms could further close the gap to biological episodic memory, and whether the approach generalizes to multimodal settings.
