## Problem

LLMs fail at autonomous long-horizon planning: GPT-4 achieves only ~30% on Blocksworld (vs. ~78% human) and ~14% on Logistics, and existing fixes like LLM-Modulo or Tree-of-Thoughts require external verifiers and iterative API calls that are expensive and slow. The paper asks whether a single-query prompting technique can close this gap without any external tools, and what specific prompting structure enables LLMs to plan at or above human level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252816/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a natural-language rendering of a PDDL instance (start/goal states plus domain rules) together with in-context search-trajectory examples that include periodic state restatements and backtracking demonstrations. For planning domains (Blocksworld, Logistics) the output is a numbered action sequence followed by a PDDL-formatted plan; for inductive-reasoning domains (List Functions, ACRE) the output is a stated hypothesis plus a Python function. Notably, the in-context examples can be generated from random trajectories augmented with the correct goal-reaching suffix, removing the need for human-authored search intuitions.

## Method

AoT+ is a single-query prompting framework that extends Algorithm-of-Thoughts with two mechanisms. First, periodic structured state generation (memoization): the model restates and caches the current problem state at regular intervals using hierarchical identifiers (e.g., 'x.y.z.'), which reduces attention drift over long contexts and dramatically cuts state-hallucination errors compared to vanilla AoT. Second, random trajectory augmentation: in-context examples are built by splicing random jumps between states from unsuccessful trajectories with the correct final steps from a successful trajectory, eliminating the need for carefully curated human intuitions while preserving goal-directed structure. The approach requires only one forward pass (no iterative refinement or external validator), yielding 3× lower token usage and 6× faster execution than LLM-Modulo while matching or exceeding its accuracy across GPT-4, GPT-4o, Claude, Gemini, and LLaMA 3.1 models.

## Results

On Blocksworld with GPT-4, AoT+ achieves 82% accuracy, surpassing the human baseline of 78% (Valmeekam et al., 2023) and matching LLM-Modulo (82%) while using only ~2,068 total tokens vs. LLM-Modulo's ~6,453 tokens. On Logistics, AoT+ reaches 80% with GPT-4, a 66-point improvement over CoT (14%) and a 10-point gain over LLM-Modulo (70%). On inductive reasoning benchmarks, AoT+ achieves 84% on List Functions and 72% on ACRE with GPT-4, outperforming all baselines. The open-source LLaMA 3.1 405B model with AoT+ reaches 77% on Blocksworld and 75% on Logistics, approaching GPT-4-level performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AoT+ substantially outperforms CoT across all four benchmarks with GPT-4: Blocksworld 82% vs. 35%, Logistics 80% vs. 14%, List Functions 84% vs. 38%, and ACRE 72% vs. 28%. Against LLM-Modulo (which uses an external PDDL validator), AoT+ ties on Blocksworld (82% vs. 82%) but wins on Logistics (80% vs. 70%), List Functions (84% vs. 70%), and ACRE (72% vs. 52%), while using roughly 3× fewer tokens. Compared to the original AoT, the memoization and random-trajectory enhancements yield large gains: Blocksworld 82% vs. 45%, Logistics 80% vs. 24%, List Functions 84% vs. 58%, and ACRE 72% vs. 46% (GPT-4). On the Game of 24 / Crossword / Creative Writing benchmarks, AoT+R (random trajectories) matches or slightly exceeds ToT (70% vs. 69%, 54% vs. 46.5%, 7.59 vs. 7.56) and dramatically outperforms CoT-SC (9%, 15.6%, 6.93). Self-Refine (SF=10) and Tree-Planner (N=25), both tested only on Blocksworld, achieve 39% and 44% respectively with GPT-4, well below AoT+'s 82%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Algorithm of Thoughts (AoT) prompting framework (Sel et al., ICML 2024), which itself builds on Chain-of-Thought and Tree of Thoughts by incorporating backtracking and heuristic search traces into in-context examples. The key departure is twofold: (1) it eliminates the need for external verification tools (as in LLM-Modulo or ToT) by introducing periodic structured state restatement inspired by memoization in dynamic programming, motivated by the 'lost in the middle' attention phenomenon; and (2) it replaces carefully crafted human-intuition search trajectories with randomly generated ones that merely terminate at the goal state. The paper explicitly positions itself against the pessimistic conclusions of Valmeekam et al. (2023) and Kambhampati et al. (2024) that LLMs are fundamentally ill-suited for autonomous planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single-prompt technique that matches or exceeds multi-iteration external-verification frameworks while using roughly 3× fewer tokens and completing benchmarks 6× faster, making autonomous LLM planning viable in latency-sensitive or cost-constrained settings. The finding that random trajectories perform nearly identically to human-crafted ones (AoT+R ≈ AoT) suggests the critical ingredient is the structural pattern of search-and-backtrack-with-goal-achievement, not domain-specific heuristics, which could dramatically reduce prompt engineering effort. Open questions include whether these gains transfer to truly open-ended planning without PDDL formalization, whether the memoization trick generalizes to non-symbolic domains, and whether the strong scaling behavior with model size (LLaMA 3.1 405B approaching GPT-4) indicates an emergent planning capability or simply better pattern matching at scale.
