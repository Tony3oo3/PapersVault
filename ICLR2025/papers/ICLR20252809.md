## Problem

Deploying LLMs on local devices with variable and limited RAM requires dynamic memory-performance trade-offs, but existing compression methods (quantization, pruning, distillation) produce fixed-size models requiring separate compression runs per target ratio. Prior weight decomposition approaches (FWSVD, ASVD, SVD-LLM) collapse at high compression ratios, failing to match practical quantization baselines. BitStack addresses this gap by enabling megabyte-level size control from a single compression pass while matching or surpassing GPTQ/AWQ performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes FP16 LLM weight matrices and a small calibration set (256 WikiText2 samples for activation-aware scaling, 32 samples for block importance scoring) and produces a single universal sorted stack of residual blocks on storage. Each residual block contains a GPU-packed sign matrix (1 bit/parameter) and k=16 singular vectors in FP16, totaling ~1 bit/parameter per block. At inference time, a variable number of blocks are loaded from the top of the stack based on available RAM, reconstructing weight matrices progressively; the minimum transmission unit is as small as 0.66 MB (Llama 3 8B key/value projections).

## Method

BitStack is a training-free, iterative decomposition pipeline with three key components. First, activation-aware scaling multiplies each weight row by the channel-wise L2 norm of calibration activations to preserve salient weight restoration. Second, Iterative Absolute Value Decomposition (IAVD) splits each (scaled) weight into a sign matrix and absolute value matrix, applies SVD to |W| while retaining W_sign, and iteratively decomposes the residual from the previous approximation over 16 iterations, yielding ~1 bit/parameter per residual block. Third, all residual blocks across all layers are universally sorted by perplexity-based importance (measured by loading each block individually while others at the same level are unloaded), with a constraint that stack length differences stay ≤ 1 to ensure smooth trade-offs and load balancing. The resulting single stack enables dynamic loading/offloading at megabyte granularity without re-compression.

## Results

On Llama 3.1 8B at 76% compression (3674 MB), BitStack achieves 30.8 average zero-shot accuracy and 3.3e3 WikiText2 perplexity, outperforming GPTQw2 (29.9 avg, 1.2e6 ppl) and AWQw2 (29.4 avg, 1.1e6 ppl). At 75% compression with group quantization (3877 MB), BitStack reaches 42.4 average accuracy versus 30.3 (GPTQw2g128) and 28.7 (AWQw2g128). For Llama 3.1 70B at 83% compression (22531 MB), BitStack retains 71.1 average accuracy (89% of FP16's 80.0), surpassing the best baseline by 41.3 points. At the lowest compression ratio tested (64–65%), BitStack recovers 96% (8B) and 98% (70B) of FP16 zero-shot performance, trailing the best AWQw4g128 baseline by only 1.7 and 0.8 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPTQ, BitStack dominates at extreme compression ratios: on Llama 3.1 8B at 75% compression, BitStack achieves 42.4 average zero-shot accuracy versus GPTQw2g128's 30.3 (a 12.1-point margin), and on 70B at 83% compression the gap widens to 41.5 points (71.1 vs. 29.6). Against AWQ, BitStack similarly outperforms at high compression—surpassing AWQw2g128 by 13.7 points on 8B (42.4 vs. 28.7) and by 41.3 points on 70B (71.1 vs. 29.8)—but at the lowest compression ratios (64–65%), AWQw4g128 edges out BitStack by 1.7 points on 8B (72.7 vs. 71.0) and 0.8 points on 70B (79.1 vs. 78.3). Compared to prior decomposition-based methods (ASVD, SVD-LLM), BitStack is the first to remain functional at high compression ratios: ASVD produces NaN perplexity beyond 20% compression, while SVD-LLM suffers a 745% perplexity increase and 40% zero-shot accuracy drop at 60% compression, whereas BitStack maintains coherent performance up to 85% compression. On instruction-tuned models (MT-Bench), BitStack generates coherent, high-quality responses at 2-bit and 3-bit levels where AWQ outputs gibberish, and at 4-bit level BitStack matches or beats AWQ on roughly half (8B) to two-thirds (70B) of samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

BitStack extends the line of SVD-based weight decomposition methods (FWSVD, ASVD, SVD-LLM) by introducing iterative absolute value decomposition—separating each residual into a packed sign matrix and a low-rank SVD of the absolute values—which yields approximately 1 bit per parameter per residual block. It also adopts activation-aware row scaling from AWQ to preserve salient weight rows during decomposition. The key conceptual departure is framing compression as a dynamic, any-size problem for variable memory environments rather than a fixed-ratio one: all residual blocks are pre-sorted into a universal stack so that the model can be loaded or offloaded at megabyte granularity without re-running compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first decomposition-based method to match or surpass strong quantization baselines (GPTQ, AWQ) across Llama 2/3/3.1 models from 7B to 70B, closing a gap that had rendered SVD-based approaches impractical. The practical impact is significant for local-device deployment: a single compressed artifact can serve any memory budget, eliminating the need to store and pre-compute multiple fixed-precision model versions. Limitations include a small performance gap versus group-quantized baselines at low compression ratios (≤65%), inference overhead from on-the-fly weight restoration (mitigated 3–10× by Triton kernels but not yet matching highly optimized GPTQ kernels), and evaluation restricted to Llama-family architectures. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-Transformer architectures, multimodal models, or settings where the sign-matrix packing assumption breaks down.
