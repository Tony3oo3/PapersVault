## Problem

In RLHF, the reward model is a fixed proxy trained on a static preference dataset, but policy optimization continuously shifts the distribution of generated samples, causing the reward model to operate off-distribution and degrade in quality. Existing ensemble-based uncertainty methods (e.g., LCB, UWO) mitigate overoptimization but still rely on a frozen reward model that cannot adapt to the evolving policy distribution. The paper asks: can we efficiently refine the reward model using policy interaction data and lightweight expert relabeling, without the cost of collecting entirely new human preference annotations?

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained SFT policy (Gemma-7B), the Anthropic Helpful & Harmless preference dataset, and interaction samples collected during PPO training as input. Expert relabeling (GPT-4 or human) assigns discrete 1–5 scores to selected samples, which are then projected onto each reward LoRA's score distribution via quantile mapping before being used as regression targets. The output is a set of refined reward LoRA ensembles (3 members) and a retrained policy; the selected data subset is 25% of all interaction samples, filtered by two sequential thresholds (γ=0.5 for uncertainty rank, η=0.5 for influence rank).

## Method

UGDA operates in three stages. First, k reward LoRA ensembles are trained on the preference data; during PPO, the policy is optimized using the mean ensemble reward while the variance across ensembles serves as an uncertainty signal. Second, a two-stage data selection filters interaction samples: the top-γ fraction by reward variance (uncertainty) is further narrowed by the top-η fraction by Adam-influence score, where gradient features are dimensionality-reduced via Johnson-Lindenstrauss random projection to keep computation tractable. Third, the selected samples are relabeled by GPT-4 or human annotators on a 1–5 scale, the labels are projected to each LoRA's native score distribution using quantile mapping with small Gaussian noise, and the reward LoRAs are refined with a mean-squared-error regression loss (replacing the original Bradley-Terry pairwise loss). The refined ensembles then drive a final policy retraining step, closing the loop between policy distribution and reward model calibration.

## Results

On the Anthropic HH dataset with 25% data augmentation, UGDA achieves the highest Avg_Reward across all settings: 0.83 (Helpful) and 0.80 (Harmless) with Gemma-2B reward models, and 0.91 (Helpful) and 0.88 (Harmless) with Gemma-7B reward models, while also attaining the lowest Var_Reward (0.11 and 0.14 for 2B; 0.12 and 0.10 for 7B). UGDA outperforms the strongest baseline RLR by 0.14 and 0.19 in Avg_Reward (Gemma-2B) and 0.12 and 0.17 (Gemma-7B) on Helpful and Harmless respectively. On instruction-following benchmarks (AlpacaEval, Arena-Hard, MT-Bench 101), UGDA also surpasses all baselines in GPT-4 pairwise comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UGDA consistently outperforms all four baselines (PPO, LCB, UWO, RLR) on both Avg_Reward and Var_Reward across both Gemma-2B and Gemma-7B reward model settings. Against the closest competitor RLR (which also uses 25% data augmentation for reward retraining), UGDA gains +0.14 Avg_Reward on Helpful and +0.19 on Harmless with Gemma-2B, and +0.12 and +0.17 with Gemma-7B, while also reducing reward variance. Compared to the uncertainty-based baselines LCB and UWO, UGDA's advantage is even larger (e.g., 0.83 vs. 0.54 for LCB and 0.51 for UWO on Gemma-2B Helpful), indicating that combining uncertainty-driven selection with gradient-based influence scoring yields substantially better data for reward refinement than uncertainty alone. On the GPT-4 pairwise evaluation and instruction-following benchmarks (AlpacaEval, Arena-Hard, MT-Bench 101), UGDA also wins against all baselines, confirming the gains transfer to open-ended generation quality. In the robustness experiment with 20% noisy preference labels, UGDA degrades less than any baseline and even surpasses its clean-data performance in GPT-4 evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: reward model ensembles for uncertainty estimation (Coste et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024a) and gradient-based influence functions for data selection (Xia et al., 2024, LESS). Prior ensemble-based RLHF methods (UWO, LCB) use ensemble variance as a penalty term during policy optimization but keep the reward model fixed, leaving the off-distribution degradation problem unaddressed. UGDA repurposes the ensemble variance as a *data selection criterion* rather than a reward penalty, and adapts the Adam influence function from LESS (originally for SFT coreset selection) to select on-distribution policy interaction samples for reward model refinement. The key conceptual departure is treating reward model refinement as an active, policy-aware data augmentation problem rather than a one-shot retraining step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The off-distribution degradation of reward models during PPO is a well-recognized but under-addressed problem in RLHF, and UGDA offers a practical solution that avoids the cost of collecting new human preference annotations by reusing already-generated policy interaction samples. The finding that selecting only 25% of interaction data via uncertainty + influence criteria substantially outperforms random selection (RLR baseline) suggests that data quality for reward refinement matters more than data quantity. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (Anthropic HH), small models (Gemma-2B/7B), and relies on GPT-4 as a proxy for human relabeling, leaving open questions about scalability to larger models, multi-objective settings, and whether the quantile-projection relabeling strategy generalizes beyond the 1–5 scoring scheme.
