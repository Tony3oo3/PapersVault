## Problem

Unsupervised behavioral analysis methods (clustering, HMM segmentation) produce action spaces where biologically meaningful signals are entangled with nuisance variability such as speed, heading direction, and animal identity, leading to over-segmentation and uninterpretable clusters. Existing disentanglement approaches are inadequate: conditional VAEs only partially disentangle, while adversarial methods are difficult to tune, assume full (nonlinear) disentanglement, and can destroy legitimate behavioral dependencies (e.g., speed is inherently coupled to action type). The paper addresses the need for a controllable, reliable framework that removes nuisance information from latent representations at a user-specified level of disentanglement.

## Input / Output

Input consists of 51-frame 3D pose sequences represented as joint angles between keypoints (lifted to a continuous 6D rotation space) plus root position, yielding (6K+3)-dimensional vectors per frame, along with deterministically computed nuisance factor vectors (3D average speed, 2D heading [sin α, cos α], or one-hot animal identity). Sequences are aligned by a single rotation/translation at the central frame rather than per-frame, preserving trajectory information. Outputs include disentangled latent codes (64-dim for open-field experiments, 32-dim for PD experiments), GMM behavioral clusters/motifs, conditionally synthesized pose sequences, and disease-session classification labels.

## Method

SC-VAE augments a conditional VAE (which concatenates the nuisance variable v_t to the latent z_t before decoding) with an adversarial scrubbing loss L_Scrub that penalizes the encoder for retaining decodable information about v_t in z_t. The key contribution is a family of scrubbers with explicit, tunable assumptions: (1) SC-VAE-MALS uses a linear scrubber solved in closed form via exponential moving averages of sufficient statistics (E[vzᵀ] and E[zzᵀ]) with an adaptive forgetting factor, requiring no neural network; (2) polynomial extensions (MAQS, MACS) enable progressive nonlinear scrubbing; (3) SC-VAE-QD applies quadratic discriminant analysis for categorical variables like identity; (4) SC-VAE-MI uses Gaussian kernel density estimation for nonparametric mutual information minimization; and (5) SC-VAE-GR/ND implement standard gradient-reversal and GAN-style neural network scrubbers. The user controls disentanglement strength via a single hyperparameter λ, and the choice of scrubber (linear vs. nonlinear) determines whether legitimate behavioral dependencies (e.g., speed-action coupling) are preserved or removed.

## Results

In the Parkinson's disease application (Table 2), SC-VAE-QD achieves 85.8% healthy-vs-disease classification accuracy, an effect size d of 1.71±0.11, and a denervation correlation of r=0.406, substantially outperforming the vanilla VAE (81.3%, d=1.07, r=0.208) and C-VAE (82.2%, d=1.11, r=0.221). In conditional motion synthesis (Table 1), SC-VAE-MI achieves 0.994 R² for heading direction of generated sequences versus 0.502 for C-VAE, demonstrating near-perfect control over the conditioned variable. A reverse control (scrubbing the disease label itself) drops accuracy to 65.2% and effect size to 0.68, confirming that the gains are attributable to identity scrubbing rather than generic regularization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SC-VAE variants against two baselines: a vanilla VAE (Kingma & Welling, 2013) and a conditional VAE (Sohn et al., 2015) that conditions on the nuisance variable without any scrubbing penalty. On the Parkinson's disease task, SC-VAE-QD outperforms the VAE by 4.5 percentage points in classification accuracy (85.8% vs. 81.3%), by 60% in effect size (1.71 vs. 1.07), and by roughly 2× in denervation correlation (0.406 vs. 0.208). Over the C-VAE, the gains are 3.6 points in accuracy, 54% in effect size, and 1.8× in correlation. In motion synthesis, SC-VAE-MI nearly doubles the heading-direction R² over C-VAE (0.994 vs. 0.502), while speed R² remains comparable across all models (~0.61–0.68), indicating that scrubbing heading does not degrade speed controllability. The neural-network-based scrubbers (SC-VAE-GR, SC-VAE-ND) show only marginal improvements over C-VAE and are less reliable than the parametric scrubbers, with SC-VAE-ND in particular producing inconsistent results (e.g., 0.079 heading R² in synthesis). The reverse control experiment—scrubbing the disease label itself—drops all metrics below the VAE baseline, validating that the observed improvements are specific to removing subject identity rather than a generic training artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional VAE (C-VAE) framework for supervised disentanglement by adding an adversarial 'scrubbing' loss that removes residual nuisance-variable information from the latent space. It departs from prior adversarial disentanglement methods (gradient reversal, GAN-based discriminators) by replacing neural-network adversaries with parametric scrubbers—linear least squares, quadratic discriminant analysis, and kernel-density mutual information estimators—maintained via exponential moving averages, which eliminates the need to tune secondary network architectures and learning rates. The work also builds on behavioral analysis pipelines (ARHMMs, VAME, BAMS) and the time-warped HMM approach of Costacurta et al. (2022), but generalizes beyond speed separation to arbitrary nuisance factors (heading, identity) and beyond time-series clustering to motion synthesis and disease detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a hyperparameter-free, controllable disentanglement mechanism: by choosing the scrubber class (linear, quadratic, full MI), the researcher directly specifies the degree of invariance, which the paper shows matters for downstream tasks (e.g., linear scrubbing preserves speed-action correlations needed for biologically meaningful clusters, while full MI scrubbing over-merges them). Limitations include fixed-length sequence encoding (no temporal dynamics), single-nuisance-variable training, and evaluation on relatively small datasets (4 mice for main experiments, 36 for Parkinson's). Open questions include joint multi-variable scrubbing, extension to finer-grained per-frame or per-body-part factors, and application to more complex social or multi-animal behavioral contexts where identifying the correct nuisance variables is non-trivial.
