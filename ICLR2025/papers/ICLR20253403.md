## Problem

Existing LLM-based navigation pipelines rely on a fragile mapping → text-translation → planning cascade, where pure text prompts cannot adequately represent spatial geometry and object details needed for rational navigation decisions. This paper asks whether a VLM, given only on-board RGB/RGB-D observations, can perform open-vocabulary object navigation in a mapless manner without any fine-tuning on object-oriented data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253403/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives an egocentric panorama split into 6 RGB-D views plus a text goal (e.g., 'toilet'), and must output a discrete action from {Stop, MoveAhead, TurnLeft, TurnRight, LookUp, LookDown} (0.25 m forward, 30° turns). The system is considered successful when the agent reaches within 1 m geodesic distance of the target and issues Stop within 500 steps. The VLM's decision is structured as a JSON response with 'Reason' and 'Choice' fields selecting one of six imagined candidate views.

## Method

ImagineNav decomposes ObjectNav into a sequence of PointNav subtasks by reframing high-level planning as a best-view image selection problem. A Where2Imagine module (ResNet-18 trained from scratch on Habitat-Web human teleoperation demonstrations, restricted to ±30° angular deviation) predicts the relative pose (Δx, Δy, θ) of the next waypoint from the current RGB frame. A pretrained diffusion-based NVS model (PolyOculus) then synthesizes the novel-view image at that pose. The VLM (GPT-4o-mini) receives all six imagined views plus the text goal and selects the most promising exploration direction via a prompt template. Finally, an off-the-shelf VER PointNav policy executes the low-level trajectory to the chosen sub-goal, and the loop iterates until the target is found—requiring no map, no object detection, and no VLM fine-tuning.

## Results

On HM3D, ImagineNav achieves a Success Rate of 53.0% and SPL of 23.8%, outperforming all other open-vocabulary baselines. On HSSD, it reaches 51.0% SR and 24.9% SPL, surpassing the only other open-vocabulary method evaluated (VoroNav: 41.0% SR, 23.2% SPL) by 10 percentage points. When NVS-generated images are replaced with real panoramic observations (ImagineNav-Oracle), performance jumps to 62.0% SR / 31.1% SPL on HM3D and 59.0% SR / 27.0% SPL on HSSD, matching or exceeding the strongest closed-set baseline OVRL (62.0% SR on HM3D).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-vocabulary, mapless methods, ImagineNav (53.0% SR on HM3D) decisively outperforms ZSON (25.5%) and PixNav (37.9%) by 27.5 and 15.1 percentage points respectively. Against the strongest map-based open-vocabulary baseline VLFM (52.5% SR on HM3D), ImagineNav edges ahead by 0.5% in SR while using no map at all. On HSSD, ImagineNav (51.0% SR) beats the only other open-vocabulary baseline VoroNav (41.0%) by a full 10 points in SR and 1.7 in SPL. Compared to the closed-set imitation-learning baseline OVRL (62.0% SR on HM3D), the NVS-based ImagineNav trails by 9 points, but the Oracle variant (62.0% SR) matches it exactly, suggesting the NVS quality is the primary bottleneck. The map-based baselines ESC (39.2%) and VoroNav (42.0%) on HM3D are both substantially outperformed, highlighting the advantage of the imagination-driven selection paradigm over explicit semantic mapping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant LLM-based navigation pipeline (mapping → text translation → LLM planning → path execution) by eliminating explicit mapping entirely and replacing text-based reasoning with visual imagination. It builds on PolyOculus (Yu et al., 2024) for diffusion-based novel view synthesis and VER (Wijmans et al., 2022) for low-level point-goal navigation, but the key conceptual departure is from prior imagination-based navigation methods (occupancy anticipation, L2M, SS-CNav, SGM) that operate in top-down map space: ImagineNav performs guided imagination directly in RGB space, generating candidate future-view images for a VLM to select from. The Where2Imagine module, trained on human teleoperation data from Habitat-Web, bridges the gap between task-agnostic VLM planners and low-level navigation policies by encoding human navigation heuristics into relative waypoint predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a mapless, zero-shot VLM-based agent can match or exceed map-based methods on open-vocabulary ObjectNav, suggesting that explicit semantic mapping may be unnecessary when a VLM can reason over imagined future observations. However, the 9-point gap between NVS-generated and oracle images (53% vs. 62% SR on HM3D) reveals that current diffusion-based NVS quality is a significant bottleneck, and the lack of historical memory causes the agent to get trapped in local optima during long-horizon navigation. Open questions include whether improved NVS or learned view synthesis could close this gap, whether the imagination-selection paradigm generalizes beyond indoor settings, and how to incorporate episodic memory without reintroducing the mapping overhead the paper seeks to avoid.
