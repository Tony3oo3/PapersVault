## Problem

Standard Adam treats all training samples equally via uniform sampling, which is inefficient when samples contribute unequally to model updates. The prior bandit-based variant AdamBS attempted adaptive sampling but suffered from invalid convergence guarantees (the same convergence flaw identified in Adam's original proof also invalidated AdamBS's analysis) and a technical error in its regret bound. Consequently, no Adam-based method existed that could adaptively select mini-batches while providing rigorous, provable performance guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a dataset of n samples, current model parameters, and per-sample gradient estimates at each iteration. It produces updated model parameters and a probability vector p_t over all n samples (summing to K, the batch size) that governs the next mini-batch draw. A key detail is that the mini-batch is selected without replacement via DepRound, ensuring K distinct samples per iteration, and the gradient estimate is computed via importance sampling (weighting each sample's gradient by 1/(n·p_{j,t})) to remain unbiased despite the non-uniform selection distribution.

## Method

AdamCB replaces Adam's uniform mini-batch sampling with a combinatorial semi-bandit mechanism adapted from EXP3. At each iteration, sample weights are updated multiplicatively based on observed gradient norms (larger gradients → higher future selection probability), and K distinct samples are drawn simultaneously via DepRound in O(n) time, avoiding the with-replacement sampling that caused AdamBS to repeatedly select the same data point within a batch. The exploration parameter γ controls the uniform floor on selection probabilities, and a thresholding step (S_null) prevents any single sample's probability from exceeding 1. Theoretically, this yields a regret bound of O(d√T + √d/n^{3/4} · (T/K · ln(n/K))^{1/4}), which improves over AdamBS's corrected bound by a factor involving the batch size K, and over uniform-sampling Adam by the n^{-3/4} vs. n^{-1/2} dependence on dataset size.

## Results

The paper evaluates AdamCB against Adam, AdamX, and AdamBS using MLP and CNN models on MNIST, Fashion MNIST, and CIFAR-10, reporting training and test loss curves over epochs (Figures 1–2) rather than final accuracy tables. AdamCB consistently achieves the fastest reduction in both training and test losses across all three datasets and both architectures. The theoretical contribution is a sharper regret bound: AdamCB achieves O(d√T + (√d/n^{3/4})·((T/K)·ln(n/K))^{1/4}), improving over AdamX's O(d√T + (√d/n^{1/2})·√T) and the corrected AdamBS bound O(d√T + (√d/n^{3/4})·(T·ln n)^{1/4}) by incorporating the batch-size K factor. AdamBS exhibits significant loss fluctuations and potential divergence in the authors' replication attempts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdamCB outperforms all three baselines on every dataset (MNIST, Fashion MNIST, CIFAR-10) with both MLP and CNN models, showing consistently lower training and test losses throughout training. Against standard Adam (uniform sampling), AdamCB's adaptive batch selection yields visibly faster convergence in loss curves. Against AdamX (Tran et al., 2019), AdamCB improves the regret bound from an n^{-1/2} dependence to n^{-3/4}, reflecting the benefit of non-uniform sampling. Against AdamBS (Liu et al., 2020), AdamCB achieves a strictly tighter regret bound by incorporating the batch-size K factor (replacing T·ln n with (T/K)·ln(n/K)), and empirically AdamBS shows unstable, divergent loss behavior in the authors' replication while AdamCB converges smoothly. The paper also notes that AdamBS's original theoretical guarantees are invalid due to a formula error in its proof, and that AdamBS's non-combinatorial sampling allows duplicate samples within a mini-batch, negating any benefit from increasing K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Adam optimizer (Kingma & Ba, 2015) by replacing its uniform mini-batch sampling with a combinatorial bandit mechanism adapted from EXP3 (Auer et al., 2002), building directly on AdamBS (Liu et al., 2020) which first proposed bandit-based adaptive sampling for Adam. The key departure is twofold: it identifies that AdamBS's convergence guarantees are invalid (the same convergence pathology identified by Reddi et al., 2018 also breaks AdamBS's analysis), and it replaces AdamBS's single-arm sampling with replacement by a combinatorial semi-bandit approach (DepRound) that selects K distinct samples simultaneously. This makes AdamCB the first Adam-based method to combine adaptive batch selection with correct, provable regret guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a gap in the theoretical landscape by providing the first valid convergence proof for any bandit-based Adam variant, and its regret bound strictly improves over both uniform-sampling Adam and the corrected AdamBS by exploiting batch size K. However, the experimental validation is limited to small-scale benchmarks (MNIST, Fashion MNIST, CIFAR-10) with simple MLP and CNN architectures under a convex loss assumption, leaving open whether the combinatorial bandit overhead and adaptive sampling strategy scale to large-scale non-convex training (e.g., ImageNet, transformers). A practical open question is whether the per-iteration DepRound computation and weight updates introduce prohibitive overhead in high-dimensional settings where n is very large.
