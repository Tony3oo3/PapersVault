## Problem

Diffusion-based samplers rely on a simple tractable prior (typically a standard Gaussian) to transport samples to an unnormalized target density, but this fixed prior causes three interrelated failures: poor exploration when the target support is far from the prior, highly non-linear dynamics that demand many discretization steps, and mode collapse driven by the mode-seeking reverse KL divergence used in variational inference. While learning the prior end-to-end is possible, a single Gaussian remains too restrictive to cover multi-modal targets, leaving a gap between the expressiveness needed and what existing diffusion samplers provide.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a black-box unnormalized density ρ(x) (and, for annealed Langevin variants, its score ∇log ρ(x)) defined on R^d, with no access to the normalizing constant Z or target samples required during training. It produces samples from π(x) = ρ(x)/Z by integrating the forward SDE from the learned prior, along with an importance-weighted estimate of log Z computed from the extended ELBO. The prior itself is a K-component Gaussian mixture with diagonal covariances, whose parameters (means, variances, and mixture weights) are learned jointly with the diffusion control functions.

## Method

The paper introduces end-to-end learnable Gaussian Mixture Priors (GMPs) for diffusion-based samplers, where the prior p_0^φ(x) = Σ_k α_k N(x|μ_k, Σ_k) is sampled via the reparameterization trick and gradients are backpropagated through the Euler-Maruyama discretization of the SDE to jointly optimize prior parameters φ and control functions u^θ, v^γ by maximizing the extended ELBO. A key theoretical contribution (Proposition 1) shows that for any time-independent drift f, the stationary distribution of the associated Fokker-Planck equation is p^st(x) ∝ exp(−σ^{-2}∫f dx), enabling denoising diffusion models to be adapted to arbitrary priors by setting f = −σ²∇log p^φ. To prevent mixture components from collapsing onto the same target region, the paper proposes Iterative Model Refinement (IMR): starting with a single Gaussian and progressively adding components during training, where each new component's mean is initialized via a heuristic (Eq. 22) that selects candidate samples maximizing the extended ELBO, balancing exploration of under-covered modes against exploitation of high-likelihood regions. The approach is demonstrated across four diffusion sampler architectures (DIS, MCD, CMCD, DBS) on both synthetic (Funnel, Fashion MNIST, Digits) and real-world Bayesian inference benchmarks, consistently outperforming fixed-prior baselines and state-of-the-art SMC/flow-based methods.

## Results

On the Funnel target (d=10), GMP-augmented diffusion samplers achieve ELBO values of −0.027 to −0.058 and ESS of 0.92–0.95, compared to −0.18 to −0.72 and 0.21–0.60 for their fixed-prior counterparts. DBS-GMP attains the best overall ELBO (−0.047) and ESS (0.949) among all diffusion-based methods, while CMCD-GMP achieves the lowest ΔlogZ (0.005) among diffusion methods. On the high-dimensional Fashion target (d=784), DIS-GMP+IMR reaches an entropic mode coverage (EMC) of 0.780 and Sinkhorn distance W₂^γ of 513.8, versus EMC 0.012 and W₂^γ 1703.0 for DIS-GMP without iterative model refinement. Across six real-world Bayesian inference tasks, DBS-GMP achieves the best ELBO on five of six (e.g., −78.160 on Cancer, −111.657 on Ionosphere).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four fixed-prior diffusion baselines (MCD, CMCD, DIS, DBS), adding a learned GMP consistently improves ELBO by 3–12× (e.g., DIS: −0.286 → −0.058; MCD: −0.721 → −0.059) and raises ESS from 0.21–0.60 to 0.92–0.95, indicating near-uniform importance weights. Against the competing state-of-the-art SMC (Del Moral et al., 2006), all GMP methods achieve substantially better ELBO (−0.027 to −0.058 vs. −0.242) and lower Sinkhorn distance (100–102 vs. 149.4). Against CRAFT (Matthews et al., 2022), GMP methods match or exceed ELBO (−0.027 to −0.047 vs. −0.027) while achieving markedly lower W₂^γ (100–102 vs. 134.3). Against FAB (Midgley et al., 2022), FAB achieves the best ΔlogZ (0.001) and ELBO (−0.014) on Funnel, but its W₂^γ (153.9) is ~50% worse than all GMP methods (~100), indicating poorer sample quality. On the Fashion target, DIS-GMP+IMR (EMC 0.780, W₂^γ 513.8) dramatically outperforms SMC (EMC 0.026, W₂^γ 6696.3), CRAFT (EMC 0.016, W₂^γ 1413.3), and FAB (EMC 0.349, W₂^γ 1187.0) in mode coverage and sample fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the family of diffusion-based sampling methods (DIS, MCD, CMCD, DBS) that transport samples from a simple prior to an unnormalized target via learned control functions, by making the prior itself a learnable parameter optimized end-to-end through the extended ELBO. The theoretical bridge is Proposition 1, which uses the Fokker-Planck stationary distribution to show how denoising diffusion models can be adapted to arbitrary priors, generalizing the standard Ornstein-Uhlenbeck assumption. The choice of Gaussian Mixture Priors draws on the GMM variational inference literature, while the Iterative Model Refinement strategy is inspired by variational boosting (Guo et al., 2016; Miller et al., 2017), adapted here to progressively add mixture components during diffusion training. The paper positions GMP-augmented diffusion samplers as competitive with or superior to established SMC-based methods (SMC, CRAFT, FAB) across a broad benchmark suite.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses three concrete, well-motivated failure modes of diffusion-based sampling (poor exploration, excessive discretization steps, and mode collapse) with a single, parameter-efficient mechanism—learnable GMPs—requiring no additional target evaluations. The IMR strategy is particularly impactful for high-dimensional multi-modal targets (e.g., Fashion MNIST at d=784), where it lifts entropic mode coverage from near-zero to 0.78, a regime where prior methods completely fail. Open questions include optimizing the criterion for when and where to add new components, dynamically adjusting K during training, and whether the approach scales to even higher-dimensional or more complex targets. A noted limitation is that ELBO and ΔlogZ can be misleading for multi-modal targets, favoring single-mode fits, which complicates model selection.
