## Problem

Conformal prediction provides distribution-free coverage guarantees for prediction sets, but these guarantees are not reliable under data poisoning where adversaries can modify, insert, or delete datapoints from training and calibration sets. No existing method offers provable pointwise reliability guarantees (i.e., guarantees that individual prediction sets cannot be shrunk or inflated) under worst-case poisoning of both training and calibration data simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252011/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32, 3 channels) along with a training set and a calibration set, both of which are deterministically partitioned via a hash function on input features (e.g., sum of pixel values) into k_t and k_c disjoint subsets respectively. It produces a prediction set (a subset of the K classes) for each test image, plus a reliability certificate indicating whether the set is coverage reliable, size reliable, or fully robust under a user-specified poisoning radius (r_t, r_c). The training process must be fully deterministic (sorted data, fixed augmentation, fixed seeds) for the guarantees to hold.

## Method

RPS combines two mechanisms to limit the influence of individual poisoned datapoints. First, a smoothed score function partitions the training data into k_t disjoint subsets (via a deterministic hash on input features), trains a separate classifier on each, and defines the score as a softmax over the fraction of classifiers voting for each class — so poisoning r_t points affects at most r_t of k_t classifiers. Second, majority prediction sets partition the calibration data into k_c disjoint subsets, compute independent conformal prediction sets on each, and merge them by including a class only if it appears in more than τ̂(α) of the k_c sets, where τ̂(α) is the inverse CDF of Bin(k_c, 1−α). The paper derives closed-form certificates (Theorems 3–6) that bound the worst-case score perturbation via greedy redistribution of 1/k_t probability mass and count minimum/maximum class support across partitions, yielding provable coverage and size reliability under arbitrary insertion, deletion, feature modification, and label flipping up to the specified radii.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet18 (coverage level 1−α=0.9), the full RPS method (k_t=100 training partitions, k_c=40 calibration partitions) achieves 92% empirical coverage with an average prediction set size of 3.41. Under worst-case poisoning of r_t=3 training and r_c=3 calibration points, over 31% of prediction sets remain coverage reliable. For calibration-only poisoning (k_c=22), the method guarantees >93% coverage reliability at r_c=4 and >87% size reliability at r_c=15, while maintaining an average set size below 1. For training-only poisoning (k_t=100), >34% of sets remain coverage reliable at r_t=4, with empirical coverage of 90.7% and average set size 3.18.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not directly compare RPS against external baseline methods; instead, it evaluates three configurations of its own approach under escalating threat models. Setting (a) (majority prediction sets with homogeneous scores, k_c=22) provides the strongest reliability guarantees, achieving >93% coverage reliability at r_c=4 and >87% size reliability at r_c=15, while keeping average set size below 1. Setting (b) (smoothed score function, k_t=100) is considerably more challenging since adversaries can simultaneously manipulate both scores and quantiles, yielding only >34% coverage reliability at r_t=4. Setting (c) (full RPS combining both mechanisms, k_t=100, k_c=40) is the most challenging, with >31% coverage reliability at r_t=3, r_c=3. The paper notes that reliability is generally higher under calibration poisoning than training poisoning, and that using pretrained (ImageNet) models significantly improves reliability compared to training from scratch. No prior method provides pointwise reliability certificates under combined training and calibration poisoning with data insertion, deletion, and label flipping, which is the key differentiator of RPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two distinct lineages: split conformal prediction (Papadopoulos et al., 2002; Lei et al., 2018) as the uncertainty quantification framework, and deep partition aggregation (Levine & Feizi, 2021) as the certifiable-robustness mechanism. It departs from prior conformal prediction robustness work, which has focused almost exclusively on evasion (test-time adversarial perturbations) rather than poisoning, and from prior poisoning-robustness work (Levine & Feizi, Wang et al., Rezaei et al.), which certifies only robust classification rather than prediction-set reliability. The key conceptual departure is introducing a pointwise reliability notion (Definition 1) for prediction sets—distinct from the marginal coverage guarantee—and combining partition-based score smoothing with majority-vote calibration to address both training and calibration poisoning simultaneously, a combination absent from prior literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to provide provable pointwise reliability certificates for conformal prediction sets under worst-case training and calibration poisoning, filling a gap that prior work (which only certified marginal coverage under calibration poisoning or robust classification under training poisoning) left open. The practical impact is tempered by two limitations: training on partitions causes significant accuracy loss on larger datasets (CIFAR100 classifiers drop to ~30% accuracy), and the achievable reliability ratios under combined poisoning remain modest (~31%). Open questions include whether improved partitioned training (e.g., leveraging pretrained models, as the paper's ablation suggests) can close the reliability gap, and whether the approach scales to ImageNet-level tasks where partitioning-induced accuracy loss is most severe.
