## Problem

Existing voice imitation methods rely heavily on annotated data (parallel corpora, style labels, transcriptions) and fail to effectively disentangle timbre from style, making independent control of these attributes difficult in zero-shot settings. This limits the ability to perform targeted conversions—such as changing only accent or emotion while preserving speaker identity—without task-specific fine-tuning or large labeled datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252109/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts a source (either speech or text) plus two optional reference utterances: a style reference controlling speaking style (accent, emotion, prosody) and a timbre reference controlling speaker identity. Internally, speech is processed at 50 Hz frame-level for discrete tokens and 24 kHz for 100-band Mel spectrograms; the final waveform is synthesized via a BigVGAN vocoder. A key preprocessing step is duration reduction, which merges consecutive duplicate content tokens to shorten sequences and strip unit-level duration information before feeding them to the autoregressive model.

## Method

VEVO decomposes speech into content, style, and timbre through a progressive self-supervised information bottleneck: a VQ-VAE tokenizer applied to HuBERT's 18th-layer hidden features, where the codebook vocabulary size K controls what is retained—K=4096 yields content-style tokens (timbre filtered) and K=32 yields content tokens (style also filtered). The two-stage generation pipeline first uses a 12-layer LLaMA-style autoregressive transformer to map duration-reduced content tokens (plus a WavLM/TDNN global style embedding and the style reference's content-style tokens) into content-style tokens, then a 24-layer flow-matching transformer (Voicebox-style, with classifier-free guidance) converts content-style tokens plus the timbre reference's Mel spectrogram into target Mel spectrograms. All components are trained purely self-supervised on 60K hours of audiobook speech without any style-specific fine-tuning, and the same pretrained models serve all four task variants (timbre, style, voice conversion, and TTS) by simply adjusting which references are fed at inference time.

## Results

On zero-shot timbre imitation (AB+CV), Vevo-Timbre achieves WER 2.968, speaker similarity to reference 0.420, F0 correlation to source 0.686, and N-MOS 3.35, outperforming all four baselines (best: FACodec at WER 3.682, S-SIM 0.327, FPC 0.611, N-MOS 2.50). On zero-shot voice imitation (ACCENT+EMOTION), Vevo-Voice achieves S-SIM 0.517, accent similarity 0.614, and emotion similarity 0.872, surpassing the best baseline FACodec (0.434, 0.514, 0.688) on all similarity and MOS metrics, though with higher WER (15.214 vs. 12.731). On zero-shot style imitation, Vevo-Style—trained purely self-supervised without parallel corpus, style labels, or text—achieves A-ACC 0.903 and E-ACC 0.754, exceeding supervised baselines like Conv-Speak (0.571) and Emovox (0.750).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vevo-Timbre dominates all four voice conversion baselines (HierSpeech++, LM-VC, UniAudio, FACodec) on every metric for zero-shot timbre imitation, with the largest gaps in FPC (0.686 vs. 0.634 for HierSpeech++) and N-MOS (3.35 vs. 2.50 for FACodec). For zero-shot voice imitation (conversion), Vevo-Voice outperforms all baselines on speaker similarity (0.517 vs. 0.466 for HierSpeech++), accent similarity (0.614 vs. 0.526), and emotion similarity (0.872 vs. 0.688 for FACodec), though it trails FACodec on WER (15.214 vs. 12.731) due to its autoregressive style model. In zero-shot style imitation, Vevo-Style—requiring no parallel corpus, style labels, or text supervision—achieves A-ACC of 0.903 (vs. Conv-Speak's 0.571) and E-ACC of 0.754 (matching Emovox's 0.750), while also delivering substantially better N-CMOS (0.15 vs. 0.00 for Conv-Speak; 1.78 vs. 0.00 for Emovox). In zero-shot TTS, Vevo-TTS trained on the same 60K hours as Voicebox surpasses it on S-SIM (0.505 vs. 0.463), E-SIM (0.840 vs. 0.811), and ES-MOS (4.03 vs. 3.61), but lags on WER (12.066 vs. 9.414), a known weakness of AR models. Against the stronger in-the-wild-trained CosyVoice and MaskGCT, Vevo-TTS still leads on emotion similarity MOS (4.03 vs. 3.66 and 3.76) and accent similarity MOS (4.12 vs. 3.99 and 4.38), validating the effectiveness of its content-style tokens despite using only audiobook training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the self-supervised speech representation line (HuBERT) and the vector quantization approach to speech tokenization (VQ-VAE, RepCodec), but introduces a key conceptual departure: treating the VQ-VAE codebook vocabulary size as a tunable information bottleneck that progressively filters timbre, then style, then linguistic content. For generation, it combines an autoregressive transformer (following the LLaMA-style decoder-only architecture used in VALL-E and CosyVoice) for content-style modeling with a flow-matching transformer (following Voicebox) for acoustic modeling. The paper explicitly positions itself against prior style conversion methods that rely on parallel corpora, style labels, or textual transcriptions, and against zero-shot TTS systems (VALL-E, Voicebox, CosyVoice) that cannot independently control timbre and style without timbre leakage or additional fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single self-supervised model trained only on 60K hours of audiobook data achieves zero-shot accent and emotion conversion that matches or surpasses supervised baselines requiring parallel corpora and style-specific fine-tuning, reducing the data annotation burden for style transfer. The unified framework (Vevo-Timbre, Vevo-Style, Vevo-Voice, Vevo-TTS) demonstrates that careful disentanglement via information bottleneck design can serve as a general-purpose substrate for multiple speech generation tasks. Key limitations include the WER gap of the AR component relative to non-AR baselines (Voicebox, MaskGCT), the English-only evaluation, and the acknowledged suboptimality of the chosen K_c=32 and K_s=4096 values. Open questions include whether the progressive disentanglement generalizes to multilingual settings, whether the bottleneck can be made adaptive per-utterance, and whether the AR WER penalty can be closed without sacrificing the controllability that the token-based formulation provides.
