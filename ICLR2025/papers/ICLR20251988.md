## Problem

Existing multi-domain benchmarks like MMLU and C-Eval treat computer science as a minor subcategory (less than 5% of items), providing no dedicated, fine-grained assessment of LLMs' CS knowledge and reasoning. Additionally, the relationship between CS proficiency and the well-studied capabilities of mathematics and coding remains largely unexplored, leaving a gap in understanding how these abilities interrelate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251988/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes CS questions in four task formats (multiple-choice, assertion, fill-in-the-blank, open-ended) across 4 domains and 26 subfields, presented in English, Chinese, French, or German. Comprehension tasks (MC, Assertion) are scored via regex matching against ground-truth letters or True/False labels, while generation tasks (FITB, Open-ended) are scored by GPT-4 using rubric-based prompts (binary 0/1 for FITB, 1–10 mapped to 0.1–1 for Open-ended). Outputs are weighted per task quantity to yield per-domain, per-type (knowledge vs. reasoning), and overall scores, with a 10% validation split held out from the 9,676 test samples.

## Method

CS-Bench is constructed from three data sources (public exams, adapted blog content, and institution-authorized teaching materials), with over 70% of items not publicly exposed to reduce data leakage, verified by higher perplexity than MMLU/C-Eval. Each of the ~10K samples is labeled by domain (DSA, CO, CN, OS), subfield (26 chapters), task format, and knowledge-vs-reasoning type, with reasoning items accompanied by explanations. The evaluation protocol uses zero-shot prompting with format-specific templates, and the paper further contributes a logarithmic scale-score fitting function (validated on Qwen1.5-110B with <0.2% error), a GPT-4-categorized error taxonomy (single-concept, concept-confusion, arithmetic, CS-logic), and Pearson correlation analysis linking CS-Bench scores to GSM8K/MATH/HumanEval/MBPP benchmarks (r > 0.9) to establish cross-capability relationships.

## Results

On CS-Bench (English, zero-shot), overall scores range from 39.86% (Gemma-2B) to 72.29% (GPT-4o), with a random baseline of 26.2%. Knowledge-type questions average 60.61% across all models, while reasoning-type questions average only 45.60%, indicating reasoning is the primary bottleneck. The best open-source models (Mistral-Large-123B at 71.03%, Llama3.1-405B at 70.96%) nearly close the gap with top closed-source models (GPT-4o 72.29%, Claude-3.5-Sonnet 71.07%). OpenAI-o1-preview achieves 82.65% overall but at ~30× the token cost of GPT-4o, with reasoning scores jumping from 64.15% to 80.98%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, CS-Bench compares 30+ LLMs against each other rather than proposing a new method. GPT-4o leads at 72.29% overall, narrowly ahead of Claude-3.5-Sonnet (71.07%) and GPT-4 (70.41%), while the best open-source models Mistral-Large-123B (71.03%) and Llama3.1-405B (70.96%) effectively close the open-vs-closed gap to under 1.5 points. A notable finding is that Llama3-8B (54.37%) surpasses the much larger Llama2-70B (52.52%), suggesting significant parameter compression potential. Across all models, knowledge scores (avg 60.61%) consistently exceed reasoning scores (avg 45.60%), with the gap narrowing as model scale increases. Cross-capability analysis reveals Pearson correlations above 0.9 between CS-Bench scores and Math (GSM8K, MATH) or Code (HumanEval, MBPP) benchmark scores, while correlations with non-CS/Code/Math benchmarks (GPQA, Tool-use-eval) remain below 0.8. Expert models show mixed results: DeepSeekMath-7B improves over its base DeepSeekLLM-7B across all CS domains (43.67→50.49), while CodeLlama-7B gains in DSA and OS but loses in CO and CN relative to Llama2-7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first benchmark dedicated exclusively to computer science evaluation, departing from broad multi-domain suites (MMLU, C-Eval, CMMLU) where CS constitutes less than 5% of content and receives no detailed subfield analysis. It also moves beyond prior CS-specific studies that target narrow applications (cybersecurity, network topology, software engineering) by instead evaluating foundational knowledge and reasoning across four core CS domains. Methodologically, it extends the MMLU/C-Eval evaluation paradigm with multiple task formats (MC, assertion, fill-in-the-blank, open-ended), a knowledge-vs-reasoning distinction, quadrilingual support, and a cross-capability analysis linking CS performance to math and code benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CS-Bench provides the field with a standardized, comprehensive tool for tracking LLM progress in computer science, revealing that even top models (GPT-4o at ~72%, OpenAI-o1-preview at ~83%) retain substantial room for improvement, particularly in CS-specific reasoning. The finding that CS reasoning gains are difficult to achieve through general reasoning or math ability alone—requiring targeted CS-specific reinforcement—offers a concrete direction for future model development. Open questions include whether the university-level scope generalizes to research-level CS, whether the GPT-4-dependent scoring for ~20% of items introduces systematic bias, and whether the strong CS-Math-Code correlation holds as models continue to scale.
