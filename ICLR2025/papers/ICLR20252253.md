## Problem

Diffusion-based inverse solvers still fail to resolve intrinsic ambiguities in ill-posed imaging problems (e.g., Fourier phase retrieval symmetries, severe degradation) because image-statistics priors alone cannot guarantee a unique solution. Existing latent diffusion inverse solvers underutilize the text-conditional capacity of LDMs, leaving a gap between how human perception resolves visual ambiguity through linguistic context and how these solvers operate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252253/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded measurement (e.g., 16× downsampled image, Gaussian-blurred image, masked image, or Fourier magnitude), a free-form text prompt describing the expected solution (which may differ from the true class), and a forward operator A. It outputs a single reconstructed RGB image in the pixel space, decoded from the optimized latent. The text prompt can be a ground-truth label or a perceptually estimated (potentially incorrect) class, and the method operates zero-shot on Stable Diffusion v1.5 latents without any task-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

TReg formulates a proximal optimization in the latent space that jointly minimizes data consistency (‖y − A(D(z))‖²) and a text-conditional regularizer (‖z − ẑ₀|t‖²) during reverse DDIM sampling, where ẑ₀|t is the text-conditioned Tweedie estimate serving as a semantic pivot. The optimization is solved via ADMM-style variable splitting: pixel-space data consistency is handled by conjugate gradient (linear) or Adam (non-linear), while the latent-space text regularization yields a closed-form interpolation between the measurement-driven estimate and the text-conditioned pivot, with the interpolation weight scheduled to prioritize text alignment early and data consistency late. A key innovation, adaptive negation, dynamically updates the CFG null-text embedding by minimizing its CLIP-space similarity to the current reconstruction, thereby suppressing concepts already present in the latent and preventing artifacts from imperfect hand-crafted prompts. An optional latent DPS gradient step is added for non-linear problems like phase retrieval and inpainting to further enforce data consistency.

## Results

On the Food-101 super-resolution (×16) and Gaussian deblurring tasks with ground-truth class labels, TReg (with adaptive negation) achieves PSNR of 21.09/23.73 and FID of 124.2/120.6 for ice cream, and 19.98/22.83 and 97.4/112.1 for fried rice, substantially outperforming PSLD (FID 280.1/213.6 and 320.3/187.3) and DDRM (FID 268.5/223.2 and 244.0/195.3). In the text-guided setting where the prompt differs from the true class, TReg achieves the best CLIP similarity (0.303–0.314) and tied-best y-MSE (0.004–0.011) across all four task variants, while maintaining competitive LPIPS. On FFHQ and AFHQ validation sets, TReg achieves FID of 35.47–82.71, consistently halving or better the FID of PSLD and P2L baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TReg outperforms PSLD (Rout et al., 2024) on FID by a wide margin across all tasks (e.g., FID 124.2 vs. 280.1 for ice-cream SR×16; FID 35.47 vs. 125.5 for AFHQ deblurring), while achieving comparable or slightly lower PSNR. Against P2L (Chung et al., 2023), TReg achieves substantially better FID (e.g., 69.65 vs. 141.1 on ImageNet SR×16) and higher CLIP similarity in the text-guided setting (0.303 vs. 0.265), though P2L occasionally edges out TReg on LPIPS by producing blurrier but measurement-consistent outputs. The sequential baselines PSLD+DDS and PSLD+PnP accumulate reconstruction error across stages, yielding worse y-MSE (0.010–0.016 vs. TReg's 0.004–0.013) and lower CLIP similarity in most settings. Direct application of PnP to the measurement achieves low y-MSE but poor perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.826) and the lowest CLIP similarity (0.239–0.251), confirming that naive editing cannot substitute for joint inverse solving. Overall, TReg is the only single-stage method that simultaneously achieves top CLIP similarity, competitive LPIPS, and the lowest y-MSE, validating its integrated text-regularized inverse solving approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

TReg extends the diffusion inverse solver (DIS) lineage—DPS, PSLD, and their latent variants—by introducing text as an explicit regularizer that constrains the solution space, rather than merely conditioning the generative prior. It departs most directly from P2L (Chung et al., 2023d), which also optimizes prompt embeddings during sampling but solely to improve data consistency; TReg instead uses text to reduce semantic ambiguity and break intrinsic symmetries (e.g., in Fourier phase retrieval) that image statistics alone cannot resolve. The adaptive negation mechanism, which dynamically optimizes the null-text embedding via CLIP similarity, is a novel component not present in prior DIS or text-guided editing methods (DDS, PnP), and the proximal latent optimization framework unifies data consistency and text alignment in a single iterative loop rather than a sequential pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple linguistic cue can break symmetries in inverse problems (e.g., Fourier phase retrieval) that no amount of learned image statistics can overcome, offering a practical human-in-the-loop control mechanism for reconstruction. However, the approach's effectiveness is contingent on the user providing an appropriate text prompt, and the paper itself acknowledges that deriving informative prompts from severely degraded measurements remains an open problem. The zero-shot, training-free nature of TReg and its competitive runtime (faster than PSLD for linear problems via CG) make it a practical addition to the inverse-problem toolkit, but its impact is bounded by the quality of available textual descriptions and the assumption that the user knows the solution's semantic category.
