## Problem

Existing methods for learning interpretable, tree-based RL policies rely on post-processing (distilling a trained NN into a decision tree) or on soft/differentiable trees that require discretization, both of which introduce a mismatch between the optimized policy and the interpretable policy, causing significant information loss. No prior method can directly optimize a hard, axis-aligned decision tree end-to-end within standard on-policy RL algorithms like PPO.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw environment state vectors (e.g., 4-dim for CartPole, 2-dim for MountainCar) or flattened 3×3 grid observations from MiniGrid, and produces an action at each leaf node. For discrete action spaces, each leaf stores a categorical distribution over actions; for continuous spaces, each leaf predicts the mean of a Gaussian plus a shared log-standard-deviation parameter. The tree is trained at full depth 7 (255 possible nodes) but self-prunes to an average of ~50 effective nodes, with axis-aligned single-feature threshold splits at every internal node.

## Method

SYMPOL integrates GradTree as the actor in a standard PPO actor-critic loop, using a dense matrix representation (one-hot feature-index matrix I and per-feature threshold matrix T) so that the tree is always equivalent to a hard, axis-aligned DT at every training step. Non-differentiable operations (rounding for the split function, hardmax for feature selection) are handled via a straight-through estimator, allowing policy-gradient backpropagation without relaxing the tree into a soft model. The critic is a full-complexity MLP, preserving interpretability of the policy while capturing value-function complexity. Training stability is addressed through three mechanisms: (1) weight decay applied only to split-index and leaf parameters (not thresholds) to encourage dynamic feature reassignment, (2) a stepwise-exponential dynamic rollout buffer that grows from n_init to 128×n_init environment steps, and (3) a matching dynamic batch size via gradient accumulation. The method is framework-agnostic and requires no modification to the RL algorithm itself.

## Results

On control environments, SYMPOL achieves a perfect 500 on CartPole, -80 on Acrobot, -57 on LunarLander, 94 on MountainCarContinuous, and -323 on Pendulum, consistently outperforming all interpretable baselines (SA-DT, D-SDT) and matching or approaching full-complexity MLP/SDT models. On MiniGrid, SYMPOL scores 0.964–0.939, matching or exceeding MLP and SDT on most tasks (e.g., 0.953 on LG-7 vs. 0.760 for MLP). Critically, SYMPOL exhibits near-zero information loss (Cohen's D = −0.019) between the optimized and interpreted policy, compared to large effects for SA-DT (3.449) and D-SDT (3.126). The learned trees average only 50.5 nodes after pruning, smaller than SA-DT(d=5) at 60.3 nodes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SA-DT (DAGGER-style distillation, Ross et al. 2011), SYMPOL wins decisively on LunarLander (−57 vs. −197/−150) and Pendulum (−323 vs. −1251/−854), and is competitive on CartPole (500 vs. 446/476) and MountainCar (94 vs. 97/96). Against D-SDT (discretized soft decision trees, Silva et al. 2020), SYMPOL dominates across all control environments, with the largest gap on Pendulum (−323 vs. −1343) and LunarLander (−57 vs. −221). Against VIPER (Bastani et al. 2018), SYMPOL outperforms on LunarLander (−57 vs. −200/−169) and is comparable on CartPole and Acrobot; VIPER cannot be applied to continuous-action environments. The most striking distinction is information loss: SYMPOL's Cohen's D of −0.019 confirms the interpreted policy is identical to the optimized one, whereas SA-DT and D-SDT show large effect sizes (3.449 and 3.126), meaning the post-processed tree no longer reflects the trained policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SYMPOL builds directly on GradTree (Marton et al., 2024a), which learns axis-aligned decision trees via gradient descent in supervised settings, and repurposes it as a policy representation within standard on-policy RL (PPO, A2C). It departs from three existing streams of symbolic RL: post-processing/distillation methods (VIPER, SA-DT/DAGGER) that introduce information loss between the optimized and interpreted policy; custom tree-specific optimizers (evolutionary, integer programming, IBMDP) that are inflexible and hard to integrate; and soft decision tree methods (Silva et al., 2020) that sacrifice interpretability for differentiability. The key conceptual departure is using a straight-through estimator to make hard, axis-aligned DTs optimizable via standard policy gradients, requiring no modification to the RL framework itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By guaranteeing zero information loss between the trained and deployed policy, SYMPOL addresses a fundamental trust gap in symbolic RL that has limited adoption in safety-critical domains. The goal misgeneralization case study demonstrates a concrete practical benefit: a human can inspect the tree and immediately see the agent learned a wall-following shortcut rather than the true goal, something invisible in black-box policies. Limitations include the focus on on-policy methods and single trees (depth 7); the authors note that off-policy integration (SAC, AWR) and forest-like structures remain open, as does scaling to higher-dimensional continuous control tasks where tree-based inductive bias may be less favorable.
