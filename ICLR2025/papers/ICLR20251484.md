## Problem

While multi-agent collaboration with LLMs has shown promise, most prior work involves fewer than ten agents, leaving the fundamental question of how performance scales with the number of collaborative agents largely unexplored. Inspired by the neural scaling law, this paper asks whether continuously adding agents yields analogous performance gains and whether a predictable 'collaborative scaling law' can be identified.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251484/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a task description (e.g., a coding problem, multiple-choice question, or software requirement) along with a topology specification (type: chain, tree, or graph; scale: number of nodes) as input. It produces a refined artifact—such as executable code, a logical answer, or a generated text—that has been iteratively improved through topologically-ordered agent interactions. Crucially, only the final refined artifact (not the full conversational history) propagates to downstream agents, keeping context linear in network size rather than quadratic.

## Method

MACNET organizes agents into a directed acyclic graph where actors are assigned to nodes and supervisory critics to edges, establishing a functional bipartition of labor. Agents interact in topological order: for each edge, the upstream actor requests feedback, the edge-critic issues directional instructions, and the downstream actor produces a refined artifact through multi-turn iterative refinement. A memory control mechanism retains only the final artifact (not the full dialogue) for propagation, reducing token complexity from O(n²) to O(n) in mesh topologies and enabling networks of over a thousand agents. The paper identifies a collaborative scaling law where performance follows a logistic growth curve with respect to agent count, with collaborative emergence occurring at roughly 100 agents—far earlier than the billion-parameter threshold for neural emergence—attributed to scaling agents catalyzing multidimensional consideration of tail aspects during interactive refinement.

## Results

MACNET-RANDOM achieves the best overall Quality score of 0.6522, followed by MACNET-MESH (0.6316) and MACNET-STAR (0.6267), all surpassing the strongest baseline (AgentVerse at 0.5805). MACNET-CHAIN delivers the highest MMLU accuracy (0.6632) and SRDD score (0.8056), while MACNET-TREE leads on CommonGen-Hard (0.7718). The framework supports effective collaboration among over 1,000 agents, with performance following a logistic growth curve that saturates around 2^6 nodes in a mesh topology.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MACNET-CHAIN outperforms all four baselines on overall Quality (0.6078 vs. 0.5757 COT, 0.5655 AutoGPT, 0.5163 GPTSwarm, 0.5805 AgentVerse) and dominates on MMLU (0.6632 vs. 0.3544–0.4485) and SRDD (0.8056 vs. 0.7096–0.7587). The sole consistent weakness is HumanEval, where AgentVerse (0.7256) and COT (0.6098) substantially outperform MACNET-CHAIN (0.3720); the authors attribute this to code-generation knowledge already being well-embedded in the base model, making single-agent reasoning sufficient. MACNET-RANDOM further extends the lead on Quality (0.6522) and MMLU (0.6877) over all baselines, while MACNET-TREE achieves the best CommonGen-Hard score (0.7718) versus COT's 0.6165. The paper also notes that baselines lack effective scalability: majority voting plateaus at ~8 agents with only 0.9% gain, AgentVerse suffers context explosion beyond ~30 agents, and GPTSwarm requires manual task-specific structuring that limits generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

MACNET extends the actor-critic bipartition introduced in CAMEL (Li et al., 2023) and the iterative refinement protocol from Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023) into a general directed acyclic graph framework, generalizing the fixed chain topology of ChatDev (Qian et al., 2024) to arbitrary DAG topologies including trees, meshes, and random graphs. Unlike GPTSwarm, which requires manual task-specific customization of every node and edge function, MACNET generates networks automatically from simple hyperparameters (topology type and scale), and unlike AgentVerse, which is implicitly limited to star or chained structures, MACNET supports over a thousand agents through a memory-control mechanism that reduces context growth from quadratic to linear. The conceptual framing draws an explicit analogy to the neural scaling law (Kaplan et al., 2020), asking whether adding collaborative agents at inference time yields analogous performance gains to adding neurons at training time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a collaborative scaling law—logistic growth saturating around 100 agents—provides a practical guideline for how many agents to deploy, suggesting many existing multi-agent systems operate below their potential. The counterintuitive finding that irregular random topologies outperform dense regular ones (mesh) challenges the assumption that more connections always help, and the memory-control mechanism (O(n) vs O(n²) context) is a concrete enabler for scaling beyond the dozens of agents typical in prior work. However, the evaluation relies on a single model (GPT-3.5) and four datasets, with MACNET underperforming baselines on HumanEval, leaving open whether the scaling law and topology preferences hold across model families and task distributions. A key open question is whether the observed collaborative emergence mechanism—catalyzing multidimensional considerations through increased interaction density—generalizes to domains requiring long-horizon planning or tool use beyond the tested benchmarks.
