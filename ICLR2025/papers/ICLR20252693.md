## Problem

Fine-tuning multimodal LLMs for on-device mobile control via online RL faces two critical gaps: existing agents trained on static offline datasets fail under real-world distribution shifts (app updates, new UI elements), and the leading online RL framework (DigiRL) relies on fully synchronous multi-machine data collection that idles faster workers while waiting for slower ones—impractical when task durations vary by up to 100×. No deployable, scalable asynchronous RL fine-tuning pipeline existed for distributed mobile device control.

## Input / Output

The agent receives the current device screenshot (RGB image) concatenated with a natural-language task description as input, and produces a sequence of touch actions at specific screen coordinates (e.g., tap, swipe, type) as output. The model is a 1.3B-parameter T5-based MLLM with a GPT-2-initialized decoder, fine-tuned via LoRA. Reward signals are generated autonomously by Gemini-1.5-Pro, which receives only the last screenshot and the last two actions to judge task completion (binary 0/1), a design validated to minimize evaluator hallucination compared to longer trajectory contexts.

## Method

DISTRL decouples trajectory collection (on heterogeneous worker machines running Android emulators) from policy training (on a central GPU host) in a fully asynchronous loop, with LoRA weights transferred via SCP over SSH. The core algorithm, A-RIDE, extends GAE with two separate networks: a trajectory-level value estimator (V_traj) trained via MLE to label and filter high-value trajectories, and a state-value network trained as binary classification on Monte Carlo return sign. Off-policy correction is handled by Retrace(λ) with clipped importance sampling ratios, while Distributed Prioritized Experience Replay (DPER) samples trajectories proportionally to a composite priority of average TD error, importance sampling ratio, and policy entropy. The policy loss combines importance-sampled advantage-weighted log-likelihood, entropy regularization for exploration, and a Gemini-validated penalty on invalid actions, all operating over a circular replay buffer that naturally discards stale experiences in non-stationary environments.

## Results

On the AitW General test set, DISTRL achieves a 73.2% success rate, outperforming DigiRL (multi-machine) at 61.2% and DigiRL (single-machine) at 59.9%. On the Web Shopping test set, DISTRL reaches 68.5%, compared to 59.9% for DigiRL (multi) and 59.6% for DigiRL (single). In terms of training efficiency, DISTRL delivers a 3× improvement in wall-clock training speed and collects trajectories 2.4× faster than DigiRL's synchronous multi-machine setup (800 vs. 300 trajectories in 6k seconds). The framework also demonstrates near-linear scalability, achieving ~7.7 trajectories/minute with 192 CPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISTRL outperforms all baselines across both task domains. Against the strongest baseline DigiRL (multi-machine, synchronous), DISTRL gains +12.0 pp on General Test (73.2% vs. 61.2%) and +8.6 pp on Web Shopping Test (68.5% vs. 59.9%), corresponding to ~19.6% and ~14.4% relative improvements respectively. Against DigiRL (single-machine), the margins are even larger: +13.3 pp on General and +8.9 pp on Web Shopping. The prompting-based baselines (AppAgent + GPT-4V at 43.0%, AppAgent + Gemini at 45.3% on General Test) and the SFT-based AutoUI (40.6%) trail DISTRL by 28–33 pp, reflecting their inability to adapt through online interaction. DISTRL also exhibits substantially lower variance (±1.1 pp on General Test) compared to DigiRL (±2.4 pp), indicating more stable convergence. In training efficiency, DISTRL converges to a 30–40% higher success rate than DigiRL by 6k seconds of wall-clock time, and even when DigiRL's algorithm is embedded into DISTRL's asynchronous framework (DigiRL-DistRL Async), DISTRL's A-RIDE algorithm still yields a further 10% improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

DISTRL directly extends DigiRL's online RL fine-tuning paradigm for mobile device control by replacing its fully synchronous multi-machine data collection with a decoupled, asynchronous host-worker architecture. The core algorithm A-RIDE builds on GAE for advantage estimation, incorporates Retrace(λ) for off-policy correction (extending IMPALA's importance-weighted actor-learner ideas), and introduces DPER which adapts prioritized experience replay to the distributed asynchronous mobile setting. The agent backbone follows AutoUI's T5-based MLLM design, and the reward signal leverages the RLAIF paradigm using Gemini-1.5-Pro as an autonomous evaluator, positioning the work at the intersection of distributed systems, off-policy RL, and multimodal agent fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DISTRL is the first deployable, scalable autonomous RL fine-tuning system for online mobile device control in a truly distributed environment, addressing a practical bottleneck (synchronous workers idling when task durations vary by up to 100×) that limits real-world deployment of prior methods like DigiRL. The 3× training efficiency and 20% relative success-rate improvement are practically significant for teams building production mobile agents. Open questions include generalization to substantially different app ecosystems or major UI overhauls, extension to iOS, integration with larger or different MLLM backbones, and reducing dependence on expensive LLM-based evaluators (Gemini) for both reward assignment and invalid-action penalties.
