## Problem

Deep learning models for time series lack shift-invariance: even small temporal shifts can drastically change predictions (e.g., heart rate jumping from 140 to 60 bpm). Existing image-oriented techniques—low-pass anti-aliasing filters and adaptive subsampling grids—fail for time series because they impose limits on the shift range, depend on sampling-rate grids, and often degrade task performance. No prior method guarantees complete shift-invariance across the entire shift space without modifying model topology.

## Input / Output

The method consumes uniformly sampled, real-valued time series signals (PPG, ECG, IMU, EEG, or audio) of fixed length, with the FFT length set equal to the signal length so that the lowest-frequency bin has a period T₀ ≥ signal duration. A lightweight guidance FCN takes the absolute (magnitude-only) Fourier transform as a shift-invariant input and outputs a single angle φ in radians; the main classifier (ResNet or FCN) then receives the transformed time series and produces a class label or regression value (e.g., heart rate in bpm, activity class, sleep stage). The framework is modality-agnostic and requires no architectural changes to the downstream classifier.

## Method

The core contribution is a differentiable bijective transformation T(x, φ) that maps any shifted variant of a time series to the same point in data space, guaranteeing shift-invariance without restricting the shift range. The key insight (Proposition 2.1) is that a time shift induces a phase rotation in the frequency domain, and by selecting the harmonic whose period T₀ equals or exceeds the signal length, every shift t′ maps to a unique phase angle—making the mapping injective and surjective over the full shift space. Concretely, T decomposes the signal via FFT, computes the phase difference Δφ between the ω₀ harmonic and a target angle φ, applies a linear phase shift e^{−jωΔφ} to all harmonics (preserving waveform morphology), and inverse-transforms back. A guidance network f_G (a small FCN) predicts φ from the absolute Fourier transform and is trained with cross-entropy plus a variance-reduction loss √Var(f_G(|F(x)|)) that encourages the batch to converge toward a consistent manifold, acting as an adaptive linear constraint that reduces the effective data-space volume without adding classifier parameters.

## Results

The proposed diffeomorphism achieves 100% shift consistency (S-Cons) across all six tasks and nine datasets, compared to 32–99% for all baselines. On heart rate estimation (IEEE SPC22), it reduces RMSE from 18.39 to 16.25 and MAE from 10.28 to 9.45 while raising correlation ρ from 62.64% to 70.12%. On activity recognition (UCIHAR), accuracy improves from 85.39% to 87.71% and F1 from 83.20% to 85.67%. On lung sound classification (Respiratory@TR), accuracy jumps from 25.21% to 33.10% and F1 from 57.01% to 60.13%, representing the largest relative gain (~31% relative improvement in accuracy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves 100% shift consistency on every dataset, while all four baselines fall far short: LPF reaches only 76.88% (IEEE SPC22) and 38.67% (DaLiA), APS reaches 73.99% and 44.33%, WaveletNet reaches 51.71% and 36.71%, and Canonicalize reaches 63.52% and 32.01%. Critically, LPF and APS not only fail to guarantee invariance but also degrade task performance—on IEEE SPC22, LPF increases RMSE from 18.39 to 20.20 and MAE from 10.28 to 13.44, while APS raises RMSE to 19.42. WaveletNet performs worst overall, increasing DaLiA RMSE from 9.86 to 15.46 and dropping ρ from 86.01% to 76.13%. Canonicalize is the closest baseline to the proposed method in task accuracy but still provides no meaningful shift-invariance gain (32.01% vs. 32.08% S-Cons on DaLiA). The proposed method simultaneously achieves perfect shift-invariance and the best task performance across all nine datasets, with the largest absolute gains on HR estimation (ρ +7.48 pp on IEEE SPC22) and lung sound classification (Acc +7.89 pp on Respiratory@TR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of achieving shift-invariance by modifying network architecture (anti-aliasing filters, adaptive subsampling grids, wavelet equivariance) and instead operates in data space via a Fourier-based diffeomorphism that maps all shift variants of a sample to a unique point on a manifold. The theoretical foundation draws on the circle group (Abelian group of phase angles under modular addition) to establish bijectivity, connecting the time-domain shift operation to frequency-domain phase rotation. Unlike prior methods that impose constraints on shift range or sampling rate, the proposed transformation is architecture-agnostic and composable with existing techniques (LPF, APS), positioning it as a complementary data-space operation rather than a replacement for network design choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that existing shift-invariance techniques designed for images actively degrade time series performance (7–10% error increase in HR estimation), revealing a fundamental mismatch between image-centric assumptions (boundary effects, low-frequency dominance) and time series where the entire signal carries information. The 100% shift consistency guarantee is achieved without any architectural modification, making it a drop-in preprocessing step for any model. Open questions include whether the guidance network's learned angle selection generalizes to non-stationary or multi-channel signals, whether the method extends to non-translation group operations (scaling, warping), and whether the ~1% parameter overhead and Fourier transform cost are acceptable in real-time clinical deployment settings.
