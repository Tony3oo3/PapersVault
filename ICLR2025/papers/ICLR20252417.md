## Problem

Open-source LLMs with 128K context windows (e.g., Qwen2-72B, Llama-3.1-70B) lack publicly available training data and reproduction recipes, making them difficult to replicate or build upon. Additionally, the interplay between direct long-context processing and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) at the GPT-4-Turbo level (128K) remains underexplored, with prior studies limited to shorter context windows (16K–32K).

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts, full long documents (up to 128K tokens), or retrieved text chunks fed as context. In the RAG pipeline, documents are chunked into 1200-token segments, embedded via E5-mistral (a 32K-context dense retriever), and the top-k chunks are concatenated into the LLM prompt. Outputs are free-form text answers for QA tasks (evaluated with F1 or exact match) or concise summaries for summarization tasks (evaluated with ROUGE-L). The model is evaluated across three context-length regimes: ultra-long (>100K), long (≤32K), and short (≤4K).

## Method

The recipe has two main phases. First, Llama3-70B-base is continued-pretrained for 2000 steps (8B tokens) on SlimPajama with upsampled long sequences (10B tokens at 128K length), increasing the RoPE base frequency from 500K to 150M and using a special '<s>' token as document separator instead of BOS/EOS to avoid the model learning to ignore prior context. Second, a three-stage instruction-tuning process is applied: (1) high-quality instruction-following data, (2) conversational QA with provided context (both ≤4K), and (3) long-context SFT data up to 128K tokens, where sequences between 32K–128K are synthesized by inserting human-written summaries into NarrativeQA source documents at random positions. Each stage incorporates all previous-stage data, simplifying hyperparameter tuning. For RAG, the E5-mistral retriever (supporting 32K-token chunks) replaces traditional 512-token retrievers, and the paper shows that accuracy monotonically improves as the total number of retrieved tokens (k × chunk_size) increases, with RAG outperforming direct long-context at 128K when k ≥ 20.

## Results

Llama3-ChatQA-2-70B achieves 41.04 average on ultra-long (>100K) InfiniteBench tasks, outperforming GPT-4-Turbo-2024-04-09 (33.16), Qwen2-72B-Instruct (39.77), and Llama3.1-70B-Instruct (39.81). On the 32K long-context benchmark, it scores 48.15, trailing GPT-4-Turbo (51.93) and Qwen2-72B (49.94) by ~2–4 points. On the 4K ChatRAG Bench (conversational QA with RAG), it achieves 56.30, surpassing GPT-4-Turbo (54.72), Qwen2-72B (54.06), and Llama3.1-70B (52.12). The model also achieves 100% accuracy on the Needle-in-a-Haystack test up to 128K tokens, and RAG with top-30 chunks (64.55) marginally outperforms direct long-context (64.29) on >100K QA tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4-Turbo-2024-04-09, ChatQA-2-70B wins decisively on ultra-long tasks (41.04 vs 33.16, +7.9) and short-context RAG (56.30 vs 54.72, +1.6), but loses on 32K long-context tasks (48.15 vs 51.93, −3.8). Against Qwen2-72B-Instruct, ChatQA-2-70B wins on ultra-long (41.04 vs 39.77) and short RAG (56.30 vs 54.06) but trails on 32K (48.15 vs 49.94). Against Llama3.1-70B-Instruct, ChatQA-2-70B wins on ultra-long (41.04 vs 39.81) and short RAG (56.30 vs 52.12, +4.2) but is slightly behind on 32K (48.15 vs 49.92). ChatQA-2-70B dominates Llama-3-70B-Instruct-Gradient-262k across all three context lengths (41.04 vs 32.57, 48.15 vs 40.51, 56.30 vs 45.20). The 8B variant (Llama3-ChatQA-2-8B) also outperforms Llama3.1-8B-Instruct on ultra-long (35.59 vs 33.17) and short RAG (52.50 vs 48.79), though it is slightly behind on 32K (39.41 vs 42.42). The paper attributes the 32K gap to Qwen2 and Llama3.1 using substantially larger continued-pretraining corpora and SFT datasets, while ChatQA-2 uses a smaller 8B-token continued pretraining corpus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ChatQA 1.5 line (Liu et al., 2024) from a 4K-context RAG-focused model to a 128K-context model by adding a continued-pretraining stage (RoPE base frequency scaled from 500K to 150M on SlimPajama) and a third instruction-tuning stage with long-context SFT data. It also extends the RAG-vs-long-context comparison initiated by Xu et al. (2024) at 16K/32K scale up to 128K, pairing the LLM with a long-context dense retriever (E5-mistral) rather than the 512-token retrievers used in prior RAG work. The recipe is positioned as a fully open-source alternative to the closed training procedures of Qwen2-72B and Llama-3.1, which also support 128K but do not release training data or reproduction steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a reproducible, open-source recipe for extending an 8K-context Llama-3 base to 128K, filling a gap left by other 128K open-weight models that withhold training details. The finding that RAG with a large top-k (e.g., 30 chunks × 1200 tokens) consistently outperforms direct 128K long-context processing on real-world QA tasks challenges the emerging narrative that long context renders RAG obsolete, suggesting the two approaches are complementary rather than competing. Limitations include weak summarization performance (attributed to insufficient summarization data in SFT) and the RAG-vs-long-context comparison being limited to only two tasks (En.QA, En.MC) and two models, leaving open whether the RAG advantage generalizes to other task types and model families. A key open question the authors raise is how to scale context to millions of tokens (as in Gemini 1.5) without degrading short-context RAG performance.
