## Problem

Existing molecule-text language models largely ignore 3D molecular structure, which encodes critical stereochemical information for function-related tasks. The few approaches that do incorporate 3D information rely on external structure encoders aligned to LMs, suffering from insufficient cross-modal interaction during pre-training, difficult modality alignment between discrete text tokens and continuous 3D representations, and uncontrollable dependency on the external encoder's quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250881/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three modalities unified as discrete tokens: 1D SELFIES sequences, 3D molecular structures converted to per-atom E3FP tokens (each a k+1 dimensional vector of non-negative integer identifiers), and natural language text. A key design detail is that 1D and 3D tokens are aligned at the atomic level—each SELFIES atom token and its corresponding E3FP token share the same position in the sequence, and their embeddings are averaged to form a joint representation. Outputs are generated autoregressively as text: numerical property values, natural language captions/descriptions, or 1D SELFIES molecular sequences, all framed as instruction-following generation.

## Method

The core innovation is a 3D molecular tokenization scheme built on the E3FP fingerprint algorithm, which iteratively expands spherical shells around each atom and hashes connectivity and stereochemical information into discrete per-atom identifiers, producing SE(3)-invariant 3D tokens without any external encoder. These 3D tokens are aligned atom-by-atom with 1D SELFIES tokens, and their respective embeddings are summed (E = ½E_1D + ½E_3D) to form a unified joint representation, eliminating the need for cross-modal alignment training. The backbone is a T5-1.1-base encoder-decoder (255M parameters) pre-trained with five multi-task objectives: 1D denoising on SELFIES/text/wrapped-text, 1D+3D joint denoising recovering masked SELFIES from summed tokens, 3D-to-1D translation, 3D-molecule-to-text translation, and text-to-1D-molecule translation. This design enables early and extensive cross-modal interaction during pre-training, after which instruction tuning on downstream tasks (property prediction, captioning, molecule generation) yields strong generalization, achieving ~70% MAE improvement over 3D-MoLM on PubChemQC 3D-dependent properties.

## Results

On the 3D-dependent PubChemQC dataset, 3D-MolT5 (Specialist) achieves MAE of 0.08 eV for HOMO, LUMO, and HOMO-LUMO gap prediction, representing a ~71% improvement over 3D-MoLM (0.28 eV H-L gap). On PubChem 3D-independent properties, 3D-MolT5 reduces MAE to 12.30 g/mol (Weight), 0.44 (LogP), 3.93 Å² (TPSA), and 29.51 (Complexity), outperforming all baselines. For 3D molecule captioning on PubChem, 3D-MolT5 achieves BLEU-2 of 42.05 and ROUGE-L of 42.33 (Specialist), surpassing the next-best MolX by ~10 points. On CheBI-20 text-based molecule generation, 3D-MolT5 attains an exact match score of 0.487 and 100% validity, exceeding GPT-4 (0.280 exact match) and BioT5 (0.413).

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-MolT5 outperforms all 21 compared baselines across every evaluated task. Against the closest 3D-structure-based competitor 3D-MoLM (Li et al., 2023c), 3D-MolT5 achieves a 71% MAE reduction on PubChemQC H-L gap (0.08 vs. 0.28 eV) and ~11-point gains in ROUGE-L/METEOR on 3D molecule captioning. Versus Uni-Mol (Zhou et al., 2023), a dedicated 3D molecular representation model pre-trained on 209M molecules, 3D-MolT5 still wins on all PubChemQC properties (e.g., 0.08 vs. 0.21 eV H-L gap), demonstrating the benefit of unified molecule-text pre-training over structure-only learning. On text-based molecule generation (CheBI-20), 3D-MolT5 surpasses GPT-4 (exact match 0.487 vs. 0.280) and GPT-3.5 (0.487 vs. 0.139), as well as prior molecular LMs like BioT5 (0.487 vs. 0.413) and MolXPT. For 2D graph-based methods (2D-MoLM, MoMu, UniMoT, MolX), 3D-MolT5 consistently leads, confirming that 3D structural information provides substantial gains over 2D topology alone. The Generalist (multi-task) variant also outperforms all baselines, though it slightly trails the Specialist, indicating some task interference in multi-task fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the E3FP 3D molecular fingerprint algorithm (Axen et al., 2017) and the 'wrapped text' pre-training concept from MolXPT (Liu et al., 2023b), but departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm of 3D molecule-text modeling. Prior approaches like 3D-MoLM and MolBind rely on external 3D structure encoders (e.g., Uni-Mol) that are pre-trained separately and then aligned with LMs via a projector, creating challenges in cross-modal interaction and modality alignment. 3D-MolT5 eliminates this external encoder entirely by tokenizing 3D structures into discrete E3FP-based tokens that align atomically with 1D SELFIES tokens, enabling all modalities (1D sequence, 3D structure, text) to be processed as a unified discrete token sequence within a standard T5 architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is showing that a 255M-parameter T5 model with discrete 3D tokenization can match or exceed much larger 7B-parameter systems (Llama2 + Uni-Mol) on 3D-dependent molecular property prediction, suggesting that the tokenization strategy itself is more important than model scale for this task. The finding that discrete E3FP tokens outperform continuous 3D representations (Uni-Mol) on HOMO-LUMO gap prediction challenges the assumption that continuous spatial encodings are necessary for 3D molecular understanding. However, the model cannot generate 3D structures (only understand them), the hash-based tokenization introduces irreversibility and potential collisions, and the approach has only been validated on molecules up to ~100 heavy atoms, leaving open questions about scalability to larger biomolecules and 3D structure generation.
