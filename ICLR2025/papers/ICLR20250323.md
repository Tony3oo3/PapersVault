## Problem

Cellular automata research lacks a unified, hardware-accelerated library, forcing researchers to reimplement basic CA functionalities across fragmented tools (CellPyLib, Golly, ad-hoc JAX/PyTorch scripts) that are either not GPU-accelerated or narrowly scoped to specific CA types. This fragmentation hinders collaboration, reproducibility, and the exploration of new research directions, particularly for computationally intensive tasks like backpropagation through time in Neural Cellular Automata.

## Input / Output

CAX accepts a CA grid state of arbitrary spatial dimensions (1D, 2D, 3D, or ND) with configurable channel sizes, plus an optional per-cell external input signal for controllable CA variants. The output is the updated grid state after one or more time steps, with the full trajectory optionally returned via JAX's scan operation. For the NCA experiments, inputs are grayscale MNIST digits (16×16 or 28×28) placed on a face of a 3D grid, or single rows of colored pixels (96 cells) for 1D-ARC, and outputs are reconstructed target patterns or transformed pixel rows compared against ground truth after a fixed number of steps.

## Method

CAX introduces a modular perceive-and-update architecture where the perceive module (convolutional, depthwise convolutional, or FFT-based) gathers neighborhood information and the update module (MLP, residual, or NCA-style) computes each cell's next state, with both implemented as standard Flax neural network components. Built on JAX, the library exploits massive vectorization to update millions of cells simultaneously and uses JAX's scan operation to unroll multi-step CA evolution for efficient backpropagation through time. The controllable CA framework generalizes the NCA architecture by injecting external inputs at each step, enabling goal-guided and conditional generation. Three novel experiments demonstrate the framework's flexibility: a diffusion-inspired training procedure that replaces the sample pool with progressive denoising, a 3D self-autoencoding NCA that compresses and transmits MNIST digits through a single-cell channel, and a 1D NCA trained on 1D-ARC that achieves 60.12% accuracy, outperforming GPT-4's 41.56%.

## Results

On the 1D-ARC benchmark, the 1D Neural Cellular Automaton achieves 60.12% total accuracy across 18 task types, outperforming GPT-4's 41.56% (direct-grid results from Xu et al., 2024). The NCA achieves 100% accuracy on five tasks (Move 1, Move 2, Move 3, Denoise, Pattern Copy, Pattern Copy Multicolor) and 98% on two more (Move 2 Towards, Hollow), but scores 0% on three recoloring tasks. In performance benchmarks, CAX delivers a 1,400× speed-up over CellPyLib for Elementary CA, a 2,000× speed-up for Conway's Game of Life, and a 1.5× speed-up over the official TensorFlow NCA implementation on Self-classifying MNIST Digits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CellPyLib, CAX achieves dramatic speed-ups: 1,400× faster for Elementary Cellular Automata and 2,000× faster for Conway's Game of Life, both measured on a single NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU. Against the official TensorFlow implementation of the Self-classifying MNIST Digits NCA (Randazzo et al., 2020), CAX achieves a 1.5× training speed-up, demonstrating JAX's advantage over TensorFlow for this workload. The headline comparison is against GPT-4 on the 1D-ARC benchmark: the 1D NCA reaches 60.12% total accuracy versus GPT-4's 41.56%, winning on 8 of 18 tasks (notably achieving 100% on all movement and pattern-copy tasks) while losing on 5 tasks (Flip, Mirror, and three recoloring tasks where it scores 0%). The NCA ties GPT-4 on Fill (66%) and Scaling (88%), and is close on Padded Fill (28% vs 26%) and Denoise Multicolor (58% vs 60%). Notably, GPT-4 maintains a relatively uniform performance across tasks, whereas the NCA exhibits a bimodal pattern—near-perfect on spatial transformation tasks but complete failure on abstract relational tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAX unifies the fragmented landscape of CA implementations (CellPyLib, Golly, Leniax, EvoJax, Biomaker CA) under a single JAX/Flax-based framework, building directly on the perceive-and-update NCA architecture from Mordvintsev et al. (2020) and the controllable CA formalization from Sudhakaran et al. (2022). Conceptually, the paper's key departure is treating all CA types—discrete, continuous, and neural—as compositions of interchangeable perceive and update modules, rather than as separate codebases. This modular generalization, combined with JAX's vectorization and scan operations, is what enables the 1,400–2,000× speedups over CellPyLib and the 1.5× training speedup over the official TensorFlow NCA implementation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The library addresses a genuine reproducibility and collaboration bottleneck in CA research by providing a single, hardware-accelerated, well-documented tool that spans 1D–3D discrete, continuous, and neural CAs. The three novel experiments—diffusion-inspired NCA training (removing the sampling-pool requirement), 3D self-autoencoding through a single-cell bottleneck, and 1D-ARC reasoning—demonstrate that the framework enables rapid prototyping of ideas that would otherwise require weeks of custom implementation. Open questions include whether the NCA's complete failure (0%) on abstract 1D-ARC tasks like odd-even recoloring reflects a fundamental limitation of local-rule architectures, and whether the diffusion-inspired training paradigm generalizes beyond the gecko regeneration demo to more complex morphogenesis tasks.
