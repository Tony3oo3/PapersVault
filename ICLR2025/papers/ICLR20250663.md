## Problem

Existing diffusion-based predictive learning methods treat future prediction as a conditional generation problem, where historical observations serve only as conditioning inputs to the denoiser. This design isolates the predictive task along the denoising axis and ignores temporal dependencies between adjacent latents in the diffusion process, making it difficult to generate temporally coherent multi-step sequences. The paper identifies this mismatch between the prediction axis (temporal dynamics) and the denoising axis (noise removal) as an underexplored challenge in applying diffusion models to temporal prediction.

## Input / Output

The method takes P historical observations (e.g., 1–7 video frames at 64×64 or 128×128 resolution, 4 velocity-field frames at 64×64, or multivariate time series values) and simultaneously predicts S future steps (e.g., 10–15 video frames, 6–11 velocity/precipitation frames, or 24–30 time series steps) in a single forward pass rather than autoregressively. For video and spatiotemporal tasks, all diffusion operations occur in a VAE latent space (3 channels, 4×4 spatial downsampling), and the model optionally accepts action commands as additional conditioning. The output is a full sequence of S future states generated jointly, with the non-independent noise structure across steps encoding temporal correlations.

## Method

Dynamical Diffusion modifies the standard forward process so that each latent x_t^s is a timestep-aware mixture of the noise-corrupted current state and the previous latent x_t^{s-1}, controlled by a new schedule γ_t (default: γ_t = 0.5·α̅_t + 0.5). Expanding this recursion yields a 'Dynamics' function that recursively blends all historical states, while the noise factor √(1−α̅_t) remains identical to standard diffusion, preserving the signal-to-noise ratio. Through the reparameterization trick, the training objective reduces to a standard ε-prediction loss where the denoiser learns to remove noise from the Dynamics output rather than from x_0^s alone. For multi-step prediction (S>1), the forward process induces a non-identity covariance matrix J_t across latents, requiring the denoiser to handle correlated (non-independent) noise; the reverse process uses DDIM/DDPM-like samplers augmented with an inverse-dynamics step to recover individual predicted states from the joint latent. The entire framework adds no extra forward or backward passes beyond standard diffusion, keeping computational cost unchanged.

## Results

On the Turbulence Flow dataset, DyDiff achieves a 12% reduction in CRPS (0.0275 vs. 0.0313) and improves CSI from 0.8960 to 0.8998 over the standard DPM baseline. On SEVIR, CRPS drops from 8.67 to 7.62 (avg) and CSI rises from 0.285 to 0.319. For video prediction, DyDiff reduces FVD from 72.0 to 67.4 on BAIR (action-free) and from 92.9 to 81.7 on RoboNet (action-free), while also improving PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS consistently. On time series forecasting, DyDiff outperforms the TimeGrad baseline on 4 of 6 datasets (Solar, Traffic, Taxi, Wikipedia) in terms of CRPS_sum.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary DPM baseline (Stable Video Diffusion), DyDiff wins on every metric across all four spatiotemporal/video datasets, with the largest gains in FVD (e.g., 92.9→81.7 on RoboNet action-free, a 12% reduction) and CRPS (12% reduction on Turbulence). Against deterministic video prediction methods, DyDiff outperforms iVideoGPT (FVD 45.0 vs. 60.8) and MaskViT (FVD 45.0 vs. 70.5) on BAIR action-conditioned, but trails MAGVIT (FVD 67.4 vs. 62.0) and iVideoGPT (FVD 81.7 vs. 63.2) on action-free settings, though the paper notes those methods use 114M–189M parameters versus DyDiff's 63M. On time series, DyDiff beats TimeGrad on Solar (0.316 vs. 0.372), Traffic (0.040 vs. 0.042), Taxi (0.120 vs. 0.122), and Wikipedia (0.066 vs. 0.070), ties on Exchange (0.007), and is marginally worse on Electricity (0.023 vs. 0.021). Compared to classical time series baselines (VES, VAR, GARCH, DeepAR, GP-Copula, NKF, Transformer-MAF), DyDiff is competitive but does not uniformly dominate; for instance, Transformer-MAF achieves lower CRPS on Solar (0.301) and Wikipedia (0.063), while DyDiff leads on Traffic (0.040 vs. 0.056) and Taxi (0.120 vs. 0.179). Overall, DyDiff's advantage is most pronounced in spatiotemporal and video prediction tasks where temporal coherence is critical, while its gains on time series are more modest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DDPM/DDIM diffusion framework by modifying the forward and reverse processes to explicitly incorporate temporal transitions, departing from prior predictive-learning-with-diffusion approaches (MCVD, PreDiff, TimeGrad) that treat prediction purely as conditional generation without altering the diffusion equations. It also contrasts with Dyffusion (Rühling Cachay et al., 2023), which applies diffusion along the prediction axis (interpolation/extrapolation between states) rather than the denoising axis, thereby eliminating the core noise-addition mechanism. The key conceptual departure is introducing a timestep-aware 'Dynamics' function that recursively mixes historical states into each latent, preserving the standard SNR while embedding temporal dependencies directly into the diffusion schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a theoretically grounded, drop-in modification to any diffusion-based predictor with no additional computational cost, making it broadly applicable across modalities (video, weather, time series). A key open question is whether the heuristic gamma schedule (η·ᾱₜ + 1−η) is optimal or whether learned, data-dependent schedules could yield further gains. The paper does not explore very long prediction horizons or high-resolution video generation, where the benefits of explicit temporal coupling might be most pronounced. The moderate but consistent improvements suggest the approach is sound but that the full potential of temporal-aware diffusion may require further architectural or schedule innovations.
