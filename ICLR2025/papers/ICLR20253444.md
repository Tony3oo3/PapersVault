## Problem

3D and 4D scene generation remain challenging due to the absence of large-scale real-world 4D data and the lack of a unified model that handles both static (3D) and dynamic (4D) content. Existing methods either rely on synthetic object-centric data, address only static 3D generation, or support 4D with limited camera motion and fixed conditioning views, leaving a gap for general scene-level generation with controllable camera and object movement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253444/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GenXD accepts one or more condition images at arbitrary positions in the sequence (no fixed first-frame requirement) along with a target camera trajectory, and produces multi-view images at specified poses and/or temporally consistent video frames at 256×256 resolution. The mask latent conditioning mechanism means the network architecture is unchanged regardless of how many condition views are provided. Generated samples can be lifted into 3D Gaussian Splatting (for static scenes) or 4D Gaussian Splatting (for dynamic scenes) to enable arbitrary novel-view rendering.

## Method

GenXD is a latent diffusion model initialized from Stable Video Diffusion, augmented with multiview-temporal modules (separate multi-view and temporal ResBlocks/transformers) that disentangle spatial and temporal information via an α-fusing strategy: α is set to 0 for 3D data (bypassing temporal layers) and learned for 4D data, allowing a single model to handle both tasks. Camera conditioning uses dense Plücker ray embeddings per pixel rather than global pose vectors, and a scalar motion strength γ (derived from an object motion field computed via aligned depth and keypoint tracking) is injected into the temporal ResBlock to control object motion magnitude. The model is trained in three stages—3D-only, then 3D+4D single-view, then 3D+4D multi-view—on a combination of five 3D datasets and two 4D datasets including the newly curated CamVid-30K, which provides ~30K real-world videos annotated with camera poses (via instance-segmentation-guided Particle-SfM) and filtered for genuine object motion using the proposed motion strength metric.

## Results

On the 4D scene generation benchmark (Cam-DAVIS), GenXD with 3 conditioning views achieves FID 55.64 and FVD 490.50, substantially outperforming MotionCtrl (FID 118.14, FVD 1464.08) and CameraCtrl (FID 138.64, FVD 1470.59). For 4D object generation, GenXD completes optimization in 4 minutes with CLIP-I 90.32, compared to Animate124's 7 hours and CLIP-I 85.44. On few-view 3D reconstruction, GenXD-generated views boost Zip-NeRF's PSNR from 20.58 to 25.40 on Re10K and from 14.26 to 19.39 on LLFF. On GSO-30, GenXD achieves the best NVS PSNR (21.08), Chamfer distance (0.0234), and Volume IoU (0.6770) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenXD decisively outperforms both camera-controlled video generation baselines (MotionCtrl and CameraCtrl) on 4D scene generation, reducing FID by ~13–22% and FVD by ~18–67% depending on conditioning views. For 4D object generation, GenXD is 105× faster than Animate124 (4 min vs. 7 hrs) while also achieving higher CLIP-I (90.32 vs. 85.44), and similarly dominates Zero-1-to-3-V and RealFusion-V on both speed and quality. In few-view 3D reconstruction, GenXD acts as a view-synthesis prior that improves both Zip-NeRF (+4.82 PSNR on Re10K) and 3D-GS (+4.29 PSNR) without modifying their architectures. On single-view 3D generation (ImageDream benchmark), GenXD is 60× faster than ImageDream (2 min vs. 120 min) with comparable CLIP-I (84.75 vs. 83.77), though IM-3D retains a CLIP-I advantage (91.40 vs. 84.75) at a similar 3-min runtime. On the video-to-4D benchmark (Consistent4D), GenXD achieves the best CLIP (0.93) and LPIPS (0.113), with FVD (733.25) essentially tied with SV4D (732.40) and clearly ahead of Consistent4D, DreamGaussian4D, and STAG4D. On GSO-30, GenXD surpasses EscherNet on all five metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, Chamfer, Volume IoU), confirming its strength in both novel view synthesis and 3D reconstruction accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenXD builds on the latent diffusion model paradigm (Rombach et al., 2022) and initializes from Stable Video Diffusion (Blattmann et al., 2023), repurposing its temporal layers as both multi-view and temporal modules. It adopts mask latent conditioning from CAT3D (Gao et al., 2024) and video interpolation work (Jain et al., 2024) to support arbitrary numbers of conditioning views without architectural changes, and uses Particle-SfM (Zhao et al., 2022) as the backbone for camera pose estimation in its data curation pipeline. The key departure from prior camera-controlled video generation (MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl) is the use of dense Plücker ray embeddings rather than 1D camera embeddings with cross-attention, and the key departure from prior 3D/4D generation is the unified treatment of both static and dynamic scenes within a single model via alpha-fusing to disentangle camera motion from object motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of CamVid-30K, the first large-scale real-world 4D scene dataset with camera poses and object motion annotations, addresses a fundamental data bottleneck that has limited 4D generation to synthetic object-centric settings. The unified 3D+4D framework with mask latent conditioning offers a practical single-model solution for diverse applications (few-view reconstruction, novel view synthesis, dynamic scene generation), and the 105× speedup in 4D object generation (4 min vs. 7 hrs) makes iterative workflows feasible. Limitations include the 256×256 resolution, dependence on segmentation and SfM quality in the data pipeline, and the acknowledged gap in handling scenes with simultaneously large camera and object movements. Open questions include scaling to higher resolutions, improving robustness to out-of-distribution camera trajectories, and whether the alpha-fusing disentanglement generalizes to more complex multi-object dynamics.
