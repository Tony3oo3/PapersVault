## Problem

Transformers dominate computer vision as generic backbones but suffer from quadratic computational complexity in self-attention, making them prohibitively expensive for high-resolution tasks like medical imaging and segmentation. While the xLSTM recently demonstrated competitive performance against Transformers and SSMs in language modeling, no adaptation to computer vision existed. This paper introduces Vision-LSTM (ViL) to fill that gap with a linear-complexity, isotropic vision backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250858/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

ViL takes an RGB image, splits it into non-overlapping 16×16 patches, linearly projects them into a sequence of patch tokens, and adds learnable positional embeddings. For classification, the concatenation of the first and last patch tokens is fed to a linear head; for segmentation, rescaled feature maps from the 4th, 6th, 8th, and final blocks are passed to an UperNet decoder. The architecture is isotropic (no downsampling beyond the initial patch embedding), so the same patch sequence length is maintained throughout, and the linear complexity in sequence length makes it particularly advantageous at high resolutions (e.g., 512×512 for ADE20K yields 1024 tokens).

## Method

ViL is a residually stacked sequence of alternating mLSTM blocks: odd blocks process patch tokens row-wise from top-left to bottom-right, while even blocks flip the sequence and process from bottom-right to top-left, enabling non-causal 2D information exchange without extra computation. Each mLSTM block replaces the classical scalar memory cell with a d×d matrix memory updated via a covariance rule (C_t = f_t C_{t-1} + i_t v_t k_t^T) and uses exponential (rather than sigmoid) input/forget gates for greater expressivity and parallelizability. A 2D convolution (kernel 3) is applied to the input before Q/K projection to provide local spatial context, replacing the causal 1D convolution from the original mLSTM. The mLSTM can be executed in recurrent, parallel, or chunkwise mode, with the chunkwise mode offering O(TSd + (T/S)d²) complexity that scales linearly in sequence length T. Models are pre-trained on ImageNet-1K at 192×192 resolution (800 epochs for tiny, 400 for small/base) followed by short fine-tuning at 224×224, using a DeiT-III-inspired training recipe with stochastic depth, mixup, and cutmix.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, ViL-T achieves 78.3% top-1 accuracy (6M params, 1.3G FLOPS), outperforming DeiT-III-T (76.2%) and Mamba-R-T (77.4%). ViL-S reaches 81.5% (23M params, 4.7G FLOPS), edging out DeiT-III-S (81.4%) and Mamba-R-S (81.1%). On ADE20K semantic segmentation at 512×512, ViL-B achieves 48.6 mIoU with only 93.6G FLOPS, surpassing DeiT-III-B (47.5 mIoU, 107.0G FLOPS) and Mamba-R-B (47.7 mIoU, 102.8G FLOPS). On VTAB-1K transfer classification, ViL-B achieves 70.9% average accuracy across 19 datasets, outperforming DeiT-III-B (70.3%) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViL outperforms all baselines on the tiny scale: 78.3% vs. DeiT-III-T (76.2%), Mamba-R-T (77.4%), Vim-T (76.1%), and VRWKV-T (75.1%) on ImageNet-1K. On the small scale, ViL-S (81.5%) narrowly beats DeiT-III-S (81.4%) and Mamba-R-S (81.1%), and clearly outperforms Vim-S (80.5%), VRWKV-S (80.1%), and ConvNeXt-S (79.7%). On the base scale, ViL-B (82.4%) trails DeiT-III-B (83.7%) by 1.3% and Mamba-R-B (82.9%) by 0.5%, though it still beats ConvNeXt-B (82.0%) and VRWKV-B (82.0%). However, ViL-B compensates on downstream tasks: it achieves 48.6 mIoU on ADE20K (vs. 47.5 for DeiT-III-B and 47.7 for Mamba-R-B) with ~13% fewer FLOPS, and 70.9% on VTAB-1K (vs. 70.3% for DeiT-III-B). Against Vim specifically, ViL wins on every benchmark and scale while also being up to 69% faster at runtime despite lacking a custom CUDA kernel. The authors note that ViL has undergone far less hyperparameter tuning than the heavily optimized DeiT-III protocol, suggesting further gains are possible.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViL follows the established pattern of adapting sequence-modeling architectures to vision (ViT, Vision Mamba, Vision RWKV), but replaces self-attention or SSM blocks with mLSTM blocks featuring matrix memory and exponential gating from the xLSTM family (Beck et al., 2024). The key architectural departure is the simple alternating traversal design—odd blocks process patches top-left to bottom-right, even blocks reverse—rather than the parallel multi-directional processing used by Vision Mamba or the bidirectional SSM in VRWKV. This positions ViL as the first vision backbone built on the xLSTM lineage, which had previously demonstrated competitive performance only in language modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ViL's linear complexity in sequence length offers a concrete computational advantage over transformers for high-resolution tasks (segmentation, medical imaging, video), where ViT's quadratic self-attention becomes prohibitive. The main open question is whether the current performance gap over DeiT-III will widen or narrow once optimized CUDA/Triton kernels for mLSTM are available, since the authors rely on torch.compile and acknowledge significant untapped potential in hyperparameter tuning, self-supervised pre-training, and hierarchical designs. The lack of an efficient hardware implementation is the critical bottleneck: until resolved, runtime comparisons remain unreliable, and the architecture's practical deployment advantage over FlashAttention-optimized transformers is not yet realized.
