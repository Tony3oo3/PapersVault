## Problem

Autoregressive language models, despite scaling to billions of parameters, fundamentally struggle with complex reasoning and long-term planning tasks such as Countdown, Sudoku, and SAT. The paper identifies the root cause as 'subgoal imbalance': in left-to-right generation, certain tokens require planning over distant context and become intractable to learn regardless of model or data scale, while diffusion models naturally decompose these hard subgoals into multiple easier views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252811/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on tokenized sequences where each digit, symbol, or variable is a separate token (e.g., a 9×9 Sudoku grid becomes 81 digit tokens; a Countdown problem is a short number sequence plus a target). At inference, the model starts from an all-mask sequence and iteratively unmask tokens over T diffusion steps (typically 10–20), using an easy-first TopK strategy that fills in the most confident positions first. This global, parallel decoding contrasts with AR's sequential left-to-right generation and enables the model to maintain global consistency across the entire output.

## Method

MGDM (Multi-Granularity Diffusion Modeling) extends discrete diffusion with absorbing-state noise by adding a token-level adaptive reweighting term v(x_{t,n}) = α(1−exp(−u))^β on top of the standard sequence-level weight w(t), where u is the per-token cross-entropy loss. The key insight is that the diffusion training objective naturally decomposes each hard subgoal into a family of interrelated 'views' at different noise levels (a multi-view learning perspective), making difficult tokens learnable where AR's single left-context view fails. The β parameter controls emphasis on harder tokens, and the easy-first TopK decoding at inference progressively commits the most confident positions. The architecture is a standard Transformer (GPT-2 style) trained with supervised learning on the reweighted diffusion loss, requiring no search or backtracking at inference.

## Results

On Countdown 4 (the primary benchmark), MGDM (85M) achieves 91.5% accuracy, nearly doubling the 45.8% of the same-size GPT-2 Scratch model and substantially exceeding LLaMA 13B (51.1%). On Countdown 5, MGDM reaches 46.6% versus 5.1% for GPT-2 Scratch and 7.4% for LLaMA 13B. On Sudoku, a mere 6M-parameter MGDM solves 100% of puzzles, while LLaMA 13B manages only 32.9%. On the Game of 24, MGDM (85M) achieves 76.0% accuracy with 1× token cost, matching GPT-4 ToT (74.0%) while consuming 186× fewer tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGDM (85M) decisively outperforms all autoregressive baselines on Countdown: it beats GPT-2 Scratch 85M by 45.7 points (91.5% vs 45.8% on CD4), LLaMA 13B by 40.4 points, and Stream-of-Search 250M by 37.3 points. Among prior diffusion models, MGDM surpasses D3PM (Austin et al., 2021) by 8.4 points and RDM (Zheng et al., 2023) by 4.5 points on CD4, confirming the benefit of the multi-granularity token reweighting. On the Game of 24, MGDM (76.0%) matches GPT-4 ToT (Yao et al., 2024) at 74.0% while using 186× fewer tokens, and far exceeds GPT-4 IO (7.3%), CoT (4.0%), and CoT-SC (Wang et al., 2023; 9.0%). On Sudoku, MGDM at just 6M parameters achieves 100% accuracy, dwarfing LLaMA 13B (32.9%) and GPT-2 Scratch at all sizes. On SAT, MGDM consistently outperforms AR, with the accuracy gap widening as the number of variables increases from 5 to 9, indicating a growing advantage on harder constraint-satisfaction instances.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the absorbing-state discrete diffusion framework of Austin et al. (D3PM, 2021) and the reparameterized backward process of Zheng et al. (RDM, 2023), but reframes the motivation through a novel 'subgoal imbalance' lens: the left-to-right factorization in AR models makes certain tokens inherently harder to learn, while diffusion's multi-timestep denoising decomposes each hard subgoal into a family of easier interrelated views (analogous to multi-view learning). It departs from prior diffusion-for-text work (DiffuSeq, D3PM, RDM) by introducing token-level adaptive reweighting (MGDM) that prioritizes difficult tokens during training, and by targeting structured planning/reasoning tasks rather than general text generation. The paper also positions itself against the growing body of work showing AR LLMs fail at planning (Bubeck et al., Valmeekam et al.) and against search-augmented decoding (Tree-of-Thought, Stream-of-Search) as a fundamentally different solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded explanation for why AR models fail at planning (subgoal imbalance) and why diffusion models succeed (multi-view decomposition of hard subgoals), which is more actionable than simply reporting a performance gap. The dramatic scale gap—a 6M diffusion model outperforming a 13B LLaMA on Countdown—suggests that for structured reasoning tasks, modeling paradigm may matter more than parameter count, a finding with implications for efficient model design. However, all evaluated tasks are highly structured with fixed-length outputs (arithmetic chains, grid filling, Boolean assignments); whether the advantage persists for open-ended generation, long-form reasoning, or tasks with variable-length outputs remains an open question. The 'regretful compromise' error analysis also opens a useful diagnostic framework for understanding AR planning failures more broadly.
