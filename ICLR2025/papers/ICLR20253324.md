## Problem

Physics simulators can generate vast robotics datasets cheaply, but they are fundamentally misspecified approximations to reality, causing zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to fail on contact-rich manipulation tasks. Existing RL fine-tuning approaches rely on unstructured exploration strategies that are too sample-inefficient for practical real-world deployment, requiring orders of magnitude more real-world data than is feasible. The paper asks whether the structural information captured by simulation value functions can be transferred to guide targeted, efficient real-world adaptation despite large dynamics gaps.

## Input / Output

The method consumes low-dimensional proprioceptive observations (joint angles, velocities, end-effector Cartesian position) combined with object position estimates obtained via color-thresholded point clouds or ArUco marker tracking, at a 5 Hz control rate. It outputs delta position targets (either 3-D joint targets or 2-3 D Cartesian targets) for a Franka FR3 robot. A key implementation detail is that the 'done' flag is set at the end of each real-world rollout, preventing the agent from bootstrapping its own critic and forcing it to rely solely on the frozen V_sim for long-horizon return estimation.

## Method

SGFT reshapes the real-world reward using the simulation value function as a potential function (r → r + γV_sim(s′) − V_sim(s)) and shortens the optimization horizon to H steps, so the agent only needs to learn short action sequences that move the system toward states where V_sim is higher. This separates 'where to go' (encoded in V_sim from abundant simulation data) from 'how to get there' (learned from scarce real-world data), sidestepping both catastrophic forgetting and the compounding model errors that plague long-horizon MBRL. Two instantiations are provided: Dyna-SGFT, which branches short H-step model rollouts from real states for data augmentation and optimizes with SAC, and MPC-SGFT, which freezes the simulation critic and uses it as a terminal reward in TDMPC-2's online trajectory optimization. Theoretically, under a mild 'improvability' condition on V_sim, the paper proves SGFT achieves near-optimal real-world policies with a suboptimality bound of O(γ/(1−γ)·αH + γ^H·ε), gaining a factor of 1/(1−γ^H) over prior bounds and enabling small H values that combat model compounding errors.

## Results

Across five real-world dexterous manipulation tasks (hammering, insertion, and three pushing tasks), SGFT achieves 100% success for hammering and pushing within one hour of real-world fine-tuning, and 70% success for insertion within two hours. Zero-shot sim-to-real transfer (including domain randomization, recurrent policies, and system identification methods) fails on all five tasks due to fundamental dynamics misspecification. SGFT requires up to an order of magnitude fewer real-world samples than baseline fine-tuning methods to reach comparable or superior performance, and succeeds on tasks where prior fine-tuning approaches fail entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All zero-shot sim-to-real baselines—Domain Randomization (Peng et al., 2018), Recurrent Policy + Domain Randomization (Kumar et al., 2021), Asymmetric Actor-Critic (Pinto et al., 2018), ASID (Memmel et al., 2024), DROPO (Tiboni et al., 2023a), and DOREAMON (Tiboni et al., 2023b)—exhibit poor zero-shot performance, confirming that the sim-to-real gap stems from fundamental simulator misspecification rather than parameter misidentification. Among fine-tuning baselines, SGFT substantially outperforms IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2021), SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018), RLPD (Ball et al., 2023), and TDMPC-2 (Hansen et al., 2024) in both sample efficiency and asymptotic success rate, while also preventing the catastrophic forgetting observed in these methods during early fine-tuning. The paper further shows that SGFT outperforms PBRS-SAC (which uses reward shaping but no horizon shortening) and TDMPC-2 (which uses short model rollouts but no value-guided reward shaping), demonstrating that both components are necessary for efficient adaptation. SGFT's gains are consistent across both model-free (SAC-based) and model-based (TDMPC-2-based) instantiations, confirming the generality of the framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGFT builds on the Potential-Based Reward Shaping formalism (Ng et al., 1999) and Dyna-style model-based RL (Janner et al., 2019), but departs fundamentally from prior sim-to-real fine-tuning approaches (Smith et al., 2022b; Zhang et al., 2023) that use simulation only for network initialization and then apply unstructured, infinite-horizon RL exploration in the real world. The key conceptual departure is treating the simulation value function V_sim as a transferable behavioral prior that reshapes real-world rewards and shortens the optimization horizon, rather than merely seeding weights or replay buffers. The theoretical analysis connects to Lyapunov stability theory (Westenbroek et al., 2022) and receding-horizon MPC (Grüne & Rantzer, 2008), providing a geometric justification for why V_sim ordering persists under dynamics shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SGFT addresses a core bottleneck in robot learning: the inability of physics simulators to capture contact-rich dynamics (hammering, insertion) means zero-shot transfer fails, yet real-world data collection is prohibitively expensive. The demonstrated order-of-magnitude sample efficiency gains and prevention of catastrophic forgetting make real-world adaptation practically feasible within hours rather than days. Key limitations include the requirement for state-space access (proprioception + object pose) rather than raw visual inputs, and the time-consuming hyperparameter tuning for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the value-function-transfer principle extends to perceptual inputs and whether purpose-built low-data adaptation algorithms can further reduce the real-world sample budget.
