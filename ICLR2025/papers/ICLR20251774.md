## Problem

Speech language models are fundamentally constrained by the scarcity of speech data relative to text: large text corpora offer 15 trillion tokens while the largest unsupervised speech datasets provide only ~36 billion tokens at 25Hz. Prior methods for building interleaved speech-text training data (e.g., Spirit-LM) require aligned parallel speech-text pairs, which are difficult to obtain at the scale needed for competitive pre-training. This paper asks whether speech-text pre-training can be scaled to 1 trillion tokens without relying on extensive parallel speech-text datasets.

## Input / Output

The model accepts discrete speech tokens at 12.5Hz (single codebook, 16,384 vectors) produced by a streaming Whisper-based tokenizer with block causal attention (2-second blocks), and generates speech tokens autoregressively. In speech-to-speech mode, output tokens are decoded into waveforms via a flow-matching model and HiFi-GAN vocoder operating on 2-second blocks. In text-guided mode, the model first emits a text response as an intermediate step, then generates the corresponding speech response in a single forward pass, enabling both conversational and knowledge-grounded outputs.

## Method

The core idea is to synthesize interleaved speech-text data directly from text corpora, eliminating the need for parallel speech-text pairs. A 1.5B text-to-token model is trained to map text spans directly to discrete speech tokens (bypassing waveform synthesis), and a span-corruption procedure (Poisson λ=10, ratio η=0.3) samples text spans from FineWeb-Edu and replaces them with generated speech tokens, yielding 600B tokens of interleaved data. The speech tokenizer is a supervised Whisper-large-v3 encoder with an average-pooling layer and vector-quantized bottleneck, fine-tuned on ASR data to preserve semantics at 12.5Hz while supporting streaming via block causal attention. Pre-training initializes from GLM-4-9B-Base, extends the vocabulary with speech tokens, and trains on 1T tokens (30% text, one epoch each of unsupervised speech and ASR/TTS data, remainder interleaved), followed by fine-tuning on a 90K speech dialogue dataset (SpeechDialog-90K) to produce an end-to-end spoken chatbot.

## Results

Pre-trained on 1T tokens (600B synthetic interleaved speech-text data), the 9B model achieves state-of-the-art spoken question answering, improving the average SOTA from 13% (Moshi) to 31%. On Llama Questions (speech-to-speech), the model scores 50.7% vs. Moshi's 21.0%, and on Web Questions (S→T) it reaches 32.2% vs. Moshi's 26.6%. On speech language modeling, the model matches Moshi on the S setting (82.9 vs. 83.0 on sTopic-StoryCloze) while substantially outperforming on cross-modal settings (T→S: 85.0 vs. ∅; S→T: 93.6 vs. ∅). The end-to-end spoken chatbot (9B+Text-guided) achieves 3.69/4.70 on General QA/Knowledge and 4.33 UTMOS, outperforming all baselines including Moshi (2.42/3.60, 3.90 UTMOS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Moshi (the strongest baseline, trained on 7M hours of natural speech), the proposed 9B model wins decisively on all spoken QA tasks: 15.9 vs. 9.2 (Web Q S), 50.7 vs. 21.0 (Llama Q S), and 26.5 vs. 7.3 (TriviaQA S), while using roughly one-tenth of the natural speech data. On speech language modeling, the model ties Moshi on sTopic-StoryCloze S (82.9 vs. 83.0) and slightly edges it on sStoryCloze S (62.4 vs. 60.8), but uniquely supports T→S and S→T settings where Moshi has no capability. Compared to Spirit-LM, the model matches on sTopic S (82.9) but substantially outperforms on cross-modal transfer (T→S: 85.0 vs. 72.7; S→T: 93.6 vs. 88.6), demonstrating the effectiveness of synthetic interleaved data over parallel-corpus-based interleaving. Against SpeechGPT and Spectron (which only support S→T for spoken QA), the model achieves 32.2 vs. 6.5/6.1 on Web Questions and 64.7 vs. 21.6/21.9 on Llama Questions. In the end-to-end chatbot evaluation (Table 4), the 9B+Text-guided model outperforms Llama-Omni (3.69 vs. 3.50 General QA; 4.70 vs. 3.90 Knowledge), Moshi (3.69 vs. 2.42; 4.70 vs. 3.60), Mini-Omni (3.69 vs. 2.44; 4.70 vs. 1.10), and SpeechGPT (3.69 vs. 1.40; 4.70 vs. 2.20), while also achieving the best speech quality (4.33 UTMOS) and lowest ASR-WER (7.83) among all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CosyVoice's supervised semantic tokenizer (fine-tuning Whisper with a VQ bottleneck) and its flow-matching decoder, repurposing both for speech language modeling rather than TTS. It extends Spirit-LM's interleaved speech-text training paradigm by replacing the requirement for parallel speech-text corpora with a text-to-token synthesis pipeline that generates interleaved data from arbitrary text, decoupling data scale from speech availability. The approach departs from Moshi's strategy of scaling natural speech data (7M hours) by instead scaling synthetic interleaved tokens (600B), and from TWIST/GSLM by adding cross-modal alignment through interleaving rather than relying solely on warm-starting from a text LM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the core data bottleneck limiting SpeechLMs—speech corpora are roughly three orders of magnitude smaller than text corpora—and demonstrates that synthetic interleaved data can close most of the performance gap to models trained on 10× more natural speech. The 12.5Hz single-codebook supervised tokenizer with block-causal attention offers a practical, streaming-friendly design that balances semantic fidelity and reconstruction quality. Open questions include whether the synthetic-data approach exhibits diminishing returns beyond 1T tokens, how well it generalizes to low-resource languages, and whether the single-voice decoder constraint (imposed for safety) limits expressive range in real deployment.
