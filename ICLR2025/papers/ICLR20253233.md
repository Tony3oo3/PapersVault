## Problem

Existing Latent Bayesian Optimization (LBO) methods rely on VAEs to map discrete molecular sequences into a continuous latent space, but VAE reconstruction is imperfect: decoding an encoded molecule may yield a different molecule with a different objective value. This 'value discrepancy problem' propagates errors throughout the optimization loop, corrupting the surrogate model and leading to suboptimal solutions. The paper asks whether an invertible generative model can eliminate this gap entirely while still supporting efficient continuous-space optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete SELFIES token sequences (length L determined by the longest molecule in the initial dataset) and a black-box objective function f. It produces an optimized SELFIES sequence and its objective value. A key constraint is that the decoding step must exactly recover the input sequence (g⁻¹(g(x)) = x), which is guaranteed by the injective embedding-to-token mapping combined with the bijective flow, unlike VAE-based LBO where reconstruction is approximate. The optimization operates within trust regions around anchor points, with Thompson sampling over a sparse variational GP surrogate to select the next query.

## Method

NF-BO introduces SeqFlow, an autoregressive normalizing flow for discrete sequences, which composes an injective mapping h (discrete tokens → L2-normalized embedding vectors via cosine-similarity argmax) with a bijective autoregressive flow g (continuous embeddings ↔ latent vectors). By Proposition 1, the composition f = g∘h is left-invertible, guaranteeing perfect reconstruction and eliminating the value discrepancy problem. SeqFlow is trained by maximizing an ELBO that combines a normalizing-flow NLL loss with a contrastive similarity loss pulling each token's continuous representation toward its embedding and away from others, using a constrained variational distribution q'(v_i|x_i) that only samples within the Voronoi cell of the correct token. For candidate generation within trust regions, the paper proposes Token-level Adaptive Candidate Sampling (TACS), which computes Pointwise Mutual Information between each latent token and the full sequence to derive a token-level importance distribution, then perturbs tokens with probability proportional to their importance (scaled by a temperature τ), enabling dense search on critical tokens while sparsely exploring less important ones.

## Results

On the PMO benchmark (23 molecular optimization tasks), NF-BO achieves 1st rank on 5 out of 6 evaluation metrics, with a Top-1 score of 18.095, Top-10 score of 17.692, and AUC Top-1 of 15.539, outperforming the next-best method REINVENT (16.772 Top-1). On the Guacamol benchmarks (7 tasks, three oracle budget settings), NF-BO consistently outperforms all six VAE-based LBO baselines across every task and setting. Additionally, NF-BO achieves a 0.000 value discrepancy ratio (perfect reconstruction) compared to 0.50–0.81 for the TextFlow baseline, confirming that the one-to-one normalizing flow mapping eliminates the reconstruction gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the PMO benchmark, NF-BO (18.095 Top-1) outperforms the closest competitor REINVENT-SMILES (16.772) by 1.32 points and Graph GA (16.244) by 1.85 points, while achieving 1st rank on 5 of 6 metrics. Against VAE-based BO methods, NF-BO shows a dramatic advantage: VAE BO with SELFIES scores only 11.423 Top-1 (rank 17), a 6.67-point gap that the paper attributes to the value discrepancy problem inherent in VAE reconstruction. On the Guacamol benchmarks, NF-BO consistently surpasses all six LBO baselines (TuRBO-L, W-LBO, LOLBO, CoBO, PG-LBO, and LSBO) across all seven tasks and three oracle budget settings (500, 10K, and 70K additional calls). The ablation in Table 4 further isolates the contribution of the SeqFlow mapping: with the same autoregressive NF backbone, replacing SeqFlow's injective mapping with TextFlow's BiLSTM (which does not guarantee perfect reconstruction) drops performance from 0.778 to 0.716 on the adip task, confirming that resolving the value discrepancy problem is the primary driver of NF-BO's gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Latent Bayesian Optimization (LBO), which has relied on VAEs to embed discrete sequences into continuous latent spaces since Gómez-Bombarelli et al. (2018), by replacing the VAE with an autoregressive normalizing flow (SeqFlow) that guarantees exact one-to-one reconstruction. The autoregressive NF architecture draws on Ziegler & Rush (2019) for discrete sequence modeling, while the trust-region local search framework follows TuRBO (Eriksson et al., 2019) and its LBO extensions (LOLBO, CoBO). The key conceptual departure is that all prior VAE-based LBO methods assume perfect reconstruction (x = p_θ(q_φ(x))), an assumption that fails in practice and causes the 'value discrepancy problem'; NF-BO eliminates this by construction through invertible transformations, and the Token-level Adaptive Candidate Sampling (TACS) module introduces PMI-weighted perturbation as a new mechanism for trust-region candidate generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The value discrepancy problem is a silent but pervasive source of error in all VAE-based LBO methods, and demonstrating that a 31M-parameter SeqFlow outperforms a 54M-parameter TextFlow (which lacks exact reconstruction) on the same NF backbone provides clean evidence that reconstruction fidelity, not model capacity, is the binding constraint. The practical impact is significant for molecular design and other discrete-sequence optimization tasks where oracle evaluations are expensive. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-sequence discrete spaces (e.g., graphs, sets), how it scales to very long sequences where the autoregressive coupling becomes costly, and whether the constrained variational sampling (Eq. 11) remains efficient as the embedding vocabulary grows.
