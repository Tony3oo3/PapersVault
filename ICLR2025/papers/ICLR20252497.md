## Problem

Existing graph diffusion models lack the ability to holistically capture intrinsic higher-order topological properties across multiple graphs simultaneously, which limits their generalizability for downstream tasks. Traditional persistent homology operates on a single graph, while existing zigzag persistence summaries (ZPI, ZFC) are restricted to a single user-predefined resolution scale and require a-priori data knowledge. This paper bridges zigzag persistence with graph diffusion models by introducing a new multi-scale, time-aware topological summary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252497/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes attributed graphs (symmetric adjacency matrix plus node feature matrix) and, for temporal tasks, sequences of graph snapshots at discrete time steps. For graph classification, ZS is computed over a sliding window of φ adjacent time steps around each forward-diffusion step, with B bootstrap subsamples drawn from that window to form a BZS ensemble. For spatio-temporal forecasting (PeMSD3/PeMSD8), a fixed window of ω=12 snapshots with horizon h=12 is used. Outputs include predicted node features (traffic flow), graph class labels via a downstream SVM, and the ZS matrix itself (rows = resolution scales, columns = time intervals).

## Method

The core contribution is Zigzag Spaghetti (ZS), a Lipschitz-continuous matrix-valued summary where each entry is a weighted sum of Gaussian kernels evaluated at the birth-death intervals of p-dimensional topological features extracted via zigzag persistence at each of m resolution scales, thereby capturing joint topology across all scales simultaneously. A mixed-up graph construction (MGC) fuses original-graph GCL and KNN-graph GCL representations through a node-level attention mechanism to produce the graph on which ZP is computed. The ZS-DM forward process adds directional noise (sign-preserving, not white noise) to node features, constructs mixed-up graphs at each diffusion step, and extracts ZS over a sliding window; the reverse process uses a UNet-inspired decoder with GNN convolution blocks, the ZS latent encoding, and sinusoidal timestep embeddings. A theoretical stability guarantee (Proposition 3.2) bounds the ∞-norm difference between ZS summaries by the maximum Wasserstein-1 distance between corresponding zigzag persistence diagrams. For uncertainty quantification, a block-bootstrap over the time sequence produces a BZS ensemble whose variability decreases with more replications, providing topological confidence intervals.

## Results

On spatio-temporal forecasting, ZS-DM achieves MAE 16.57 / RMSE 26.46 / MAPE 16.25% on PeMSD3 and MAE 17.59 / RMSE 26.09 / MAPE 10.29% on PeMSD8, outperforming the best baseline (DiffSTG) by up to 14% in MAPE. On graph classification across 8 molecular/chemical datasets, ZS-DM consistently outperforms all 15 baselines, with gains of 2.32–4.68% over the strongest runner-ups (e.g., 81.80% on NCI1 vs. 80.40% for GraphMAE; 91.68% on MUTAG vs. 91.51% for DDM). On ogbg-molhiv, ZS-DM reaches 67.52±3.00 ROC-AUC, surpassing GraphCL (65.18±2.53) and TOGL (62.63±2.39). Robustness experiments show ZS-DM degrades far less under 5% Gaussian noise (89.76% vs. 86.41% for DDM on MUTAG) and with reduced training sets (70%: 82.23% vs. 80.78% for DDM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZS-DM wins against all 21 baselines across both task types. On spatio-temporal forecasting (PeMSD3/PeMSD8), it beats the strongest probabilistic baseline DiffSTG by 1.22 MAE and 1.65 RMSE on PeMSD3, and by 1.01 MAE and 2.11 RMSE on PeMSD8, with the largest gap in MAPE (10.29% vs. 11.94%). Among graph classification baselines, the closest competitor is DDM (directional diffusion, Yang et al. 2024), which ZS-DM outperforms on all 8 datasets by margins of 0.17–7.87% (e.g., 91.68 vs. 91.51 on MUTAG, 86.20 vs. 83.64 on BZR). Against topological baselines, ZS-DM surpasses TOGL by 1.21% on NCI1 and 1.19% on MUTAG, confirming that zigzag persistence captures richer multi-scale topology than single-graph persistent homology. Among contrastive learning methods, ZS-DM leads GraphCL by 3.93% on NCI1 and 1.50% on PTC_FM, and outperforms RGCL by 3.66% on NCI1. Ablation against competing zigzag summaries (ZPI, ZFC) shows ZS gains of 0.25–2.40% over ZPI-DM and 0.28–1.59% over ZFC-DM, while also being ~43% faster to compute per epoch than ZPI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected research threads: zigzag persistence (ZP) from algebraic topology and generative diffusion models on graphs. It builds directly on the ZFC framework of Chen et al. (2022) for the kernel functions and the DDPM/directional-noise diffusion pipeline of Ho et al. (2020) and Yang et al. (2024), but departs from all prior topological summaries (ZPI, ZFC, crocker plots) by simultaneously capturing topological information across all resolution scales in a single differentiable, Lipschitz-continuous object. The mixed-up graph construction and UGnet decoder are standard GNN/diffusion components repurposed to feed the ZS encoder, making the topological summary a first-class input to the denoising process rather than a post-hoc feature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first integration of zigzag persistence into graph diffusion models opens a path for principled topological uncertainty quantification (via bootstrapped ZS) and for capturing joint higher-order shape properties across sequences of graphs—something no prior graph diffusion model could do. The theoretical stability guarantee (Proposition 3.2) is practically important for noisy, low-data regimes, and the empirical robustness gains (up to 5× lower variability) support this. Key open questions include scalability of ZP on denser graphs (current complexity is O(m log²N + m log m)), the lack of a random-matrix-theory framework for bootstrapped ZS ensembles, and whether the approach extends to hypergraphs and dynamic multilayer networks as the authors note.
