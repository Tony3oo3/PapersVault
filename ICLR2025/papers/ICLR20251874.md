## Problem

Existing vision-language benchmarks (VQA, OCR, TextVQA) either test visible text extraction or free-form question answering, but none require a model to restore partially occluded text by integrating pixel-level character hints with surrounding image context. Current VLMs that handle embedded text rely on OCR pipelines or masked language modeling, reducing the problem to text-based processing that fails when only 1–2 pixels of each character are visible. VCR fills this gap by creating a controlled task where success demands genuine cross-modal reasoning between the visual image, the tiny exposed text fragments, and linguistic context.

## Input / Output

Each VCR instance presents a vertically stacked image (width 300 px, height ≤ 900 px) containing a visual image (VI) above or below a caption (TEI) in which one or more 5-gram spans are partially covered by a white rectangle, plus a separate question prompt (ST) asking the model to produce the obscured text. In the 'hard' configuration only 1–2 pixels of the top and bottom of each character remain visible; in 'easy' more of the glyph is exposed. The expected output is the exact ground-truth string of the masked span(s), enabling unambiguous evaluation via Exact Match and Jaccard Index. An ablation variant removes the VI entirely, retaining only the TEI region to isolate the contribution of visual context.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a synthetic data pipeline and benchmark rather than a new model architecture. Starting from Wikipedia image–caption pairs (filtered for sensitive content), captions are stacked beneath images, resized to 300 px width, and truncated to five lines. spaCy is used to randomly select 5-gram spans for masking, excluding spans containing numbers, person names, organizations, locations, dates, or times, with total masked tokens capped at 50% of the caption. A white rectangle then covers the selected span, with the vertical coverage ratio tuned per difficulty level (easy vs. hard). The resulting VCR-WIKI dataset contains 2.11 M English and 346 K Chinese training instances plus 5 K validation and 5 K test splits in both languages, and a non-public VCR-HIDDEN set (100 samples per language) with varied fonts, positions, and resolutions to prevent overfitting. Empirically, 38+ VLMs are evaluated on both datasets, and three open-source models (CogVLM2, MiniCPM-V2.5, Qwen2-VL-7B) are fine-tuned with LoRA (r=8, α=32, schedule-free AdamW, lr 2e-4, batch 64, 16 K examples, 1 epoch) to test whether targeted training closes the gap to human performance (>90% EM).

## Results

On VCR-HIDDEN (English, Feb 2025), the best model Qwen2.5-VL 72B achieves 86.50% Exact Match and 92.28% Jaccard Index with VI+TEI input, while Claude 3.7 Sonnet reaches 74.00% EM. On the Chinese VCR-HIDDEN, performance drops dramatically: Qwen2.5-VL 72B scores only 33.50% EM, and Claude 3.7 Sonnet achieves just 3.00% EM. Human evaluators achieve 91–99% EM across all four settings (English/Chinese × Easy/Hard), revealing a substantial gap between current SOTA VLMs and human performance. Fine-tuning three open-source models (CogVLM2, MiniCPM-V2.5, Qwen2-VL-7B) with LoRA on 16K VCR-WIKI examples yields only marginal improvements, with CogVLM2 showing the largest gain (~20% EM on English Hard).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper, so the 'method' is the VCR task itself and the baselines are 38+ VLMs evaluated across 23 benchmarks. On VCR-HIDDEN English, Qwen2.5-VL 72B (86.5% EM) outperforms InternVL2.5 78B (83.5%), Claude 3.7 Sonnet (74.0%), and GPT-4o (41.5%), but all fall well short of the 91–97% human baseline. A striking finding is that OCR-capable models like InternLM-XComposer2-VL and DocOwl-1.5-Omni score near 0–5% EM on VCR-WIKI Hard, confirming that strong OCR does not translate to VCR success. Chinese performance collapses across all models: even the best (Qwen2.5-VL 72B at 33.5% EM) is far below its English score (86.5%), while Claude 3.7 Sonnet drops from 74% (EN) to 3% (ZH). Model size does not guarantee gains—Llama-3.2-11B and 90B show similar VCR performance, and InternLM-XComposer2-VL-4K underperforms its lower-resolution counterpart. Including the visual image (VI) generally hurts performance (negative Δ for most models), suggesting current VLMs fail to leverage visual context for text restoration.

## Relation to Prior Work

VCR carves new ground at the intersection of VQA, OCR, and neuroscience-inspired occlusion reasoning, but does not build on a specific prior method. The authors explicitly position the task as bridging VQA (which has non-unique ground-truth) and OCR (which ignores visual context), requiring unique text restoration that depends on pixel-level hints, image context, and linguistic structure simultaneously. The dataset construction repurposes Wikipedia image-caption pairs with spaCy-based 5-gram masking, and the task design draws on neuroscience literature about human perceptual completion of partially occluded objects. The clustering analysis (Figure 4) confirms VCR_EN_HARD is orthogonal to 22 other standard VLM benchmarks, validating its novelty as an evaluation axis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial and persistent gap: humans achieve >90% exact match across all settings while the best SOTA models (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, InternVL2.5-78B) reach only ~86% on the easy hidden test and far less on hard settings, with many models approaching zero. Critically, LoRA fine-tuning on 16K VCR examples yields only marginal gains, suggesting the task demands architectural or training-paradigm innovations rather than data exposure. The finding that including the visual image (VI) often *degrades* performance (Δ < 0 for many models) points to unresolved issues in how VLMs integrate spatially-adjacent visual and textual information. Open questions include whether larger-scale or differently-structured training can close the gap, and whether the task's reliance on Wikipedia-style captions limits generalizability to more diverse text-image configurations.
