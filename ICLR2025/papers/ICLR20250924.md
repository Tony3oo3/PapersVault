## Problem

Prior root cause diagnosis methods that assess the 'fix condition' rely on counterfactuals computed via abduction from SCMs trained on historical (in-distribution) data. Since anomalies are rare and fall outside the training distribution, the abduction step evaluates structural equations at out-of-distribution inputs, yielding unreliable counterfactual estimates that degrade diagnosis accuracy. No prior method evaluates the fix condition while guaranteeing the fitted SCM is only probed at in-distribution inputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes an anomalous KPI instance (a vector of node values where the target node is flagged), a known causal DAG (typically derived from inverted call graphs in cloud deployments), a trained SCM with structural equations and estimated exogenous distributions, and per-node anomaly detectors (e.g., Z-score with thresholds) trained unsupervised on normal data. It outputs a ranked list of root cause nodes ordered by Shapley values, where the fix value for each candidate is sampled from its conditional distribution P(X_j | Pa_X_j) to reflect how an operator would naturally restore the node.

## Method

IDI is a two-step algorithm: (1) an anomaly-condition filter identifies candidate nodes R_cand that are anomalous while all their causal parents are not, and (2) a fix-condition assessment applies in-distribution interventions rather than counterfactuals. The key mechanism replaces abduction (inverting structural equations at OOD anomalous inputs to recover exogenous variables) with fresh sampling of exogenous variables from their estimated training distributions, ensuring every SCM evaluation occurs at in-distribution inputs. For multiple root causes, IDI uses Shapley values over subsets of R_cand with utility defined as the reduction in the target's raw anomaly score after jointly fixing the subset. Theoretically, the paper proves that counterfactual error scales with the total variation distance between the training and anomalous distributions, while intervention error is bounded by the standard deviation of the latent exogenous variables, making IDI robust precisely when anomalies are far from the training regime.

## Results

On the PetShop cloud microservices benchmark, IDI achieves Recall@1 of 0.90 for both low and high latency issues and a perfect 1.00 for temporal latency, outperforming all nine baselines in most settings (the sole exception being Recall@3 for high latency, where CIRCA reaches 1.00 vs. IDI's 0.90). On synthetic SCMs, IDI attains perfect Recall@1 (1.00) for linear and non-linear invertible models with a unique root cause, and maintains 0.60–0.83 Recall@1 for the hardest non-linear non-invertible setting where all other causal-fix methods drop to 0.00–0.40. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that replacing IDI's interventions with counterfactuals (IDI-CF) degrades performance from 0.90 to 0.70 on PetShop low latency and from 0.63 to 0.12 on non-linear non-invertible synthetic SCMs, isolating the in-distribution intervention as the key robustness gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDI outperforms the strongest Causal Anomaly baseline, Traversal, on low-latency PetShop (Recall@1: 0.90 vs. 0.80) and ties it on high and temporal settings. Against the Causal Fix baselines, IDI's advantage is most pronounced: CF Attribution (Budhathoki et al., 2022) scores only 0.40 Recall@1 on low and high latency and 0.00 on temporal, while TOCA and HRCD remain below 0.40 across all PetShop settings. On synthetic non-linear non-invertible SCMs (RQ3), where abduction is impossible, CF Attribution and TOCA collapse to 0.00 Recall@1, whereas IDI still achieves 0.60 (unique) and 0.63 (multiple), confirming that in-distribution interventions remain informative even when counterfactuals are undefined. The only baseline that matches or exceeds IDI is CIRCA on PetShop high-latency Recall@3 (1.00 vs. 0.90), attributed to CIRCA's fully-anomalous-path heuristic happening to align with that particular fault pattern. Correlation-based methods (Random Walk, Ranked Correlation, ε-Diagnosis) are consistently the weakest, with Ranked Correlation reaching at most 0.70 Recall@1 on high latency and ε-Diagnosis scoring 0.00 in most settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal fix approach to root cause diagnosis (RCD) initiated by Budhathoki et al. (ICML 2022), which used counterfactuals with Shapley values to assess whether fixing a candidate node resolves the target anomaly. The key departure is replacing counterfactual abduction—which requires inverting structural equations at out-of-distribution anomalous inputs—with in-distribution interventions that sample exogenous noise from the training distribution. The anomaly-condition filtering framework is borrowed from Okati et al. (2024), and the broader positioning sits within the causal RCD literature that includes traversal methods, CIRCA, and TOCA, all of which the paper benchmarks against.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underappreciated failure mode in causal RCD: counterfactual estimation is unreliable precisely when it is most needed (on anomalous, OOD inputs), and provides clean theoretical bounds (Theorems 5–6) showing that intervention error is bounded by the standard deviation of exogenous noise while counterfactual error scales with the total variation distance between training and anomalous distributions. A notable limitation is that in additive noise models with sufficient training data, counterfactuals converge to true values while interventions plateau at std(ε), meaning the advantage is most pronounced in the low-data or non-linear regimes. Open questions include whether the in-distribution intervention strategy extends to non-additive-noise SCMs where the exogenous distribution is not simply the residual, and whether Assumption 1 (at most one root cause per path) can be relaxed without degrading performance.
