## Problem

Most existing multi-view clustering methods either rely on a suboptimal two-stage pipeline (learn representations, then cluster) that cannot jointly optimize both stages, or use end-to-end fusion strategies that are not adaptable to general data types. The paper addresses the gap of providing a single, domain-agnostic, end-to-end deep MVC framework whose representation learning is theoretically grounded in discriminative (LDA-like) objectives rather than purely correlational ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251652/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts n_v views of N samples, where each view can be any data type encodable by a neural network (RGB images, tabular gene-expression vectors, text BoW features, handcrafted descriptors, etc.), with no fixed resolution or dimensionality constraint. It outputs a fused latent embedding per sample and a probability matrix P ∈ R^{N×K} from which cluster assignments are derived. The number of clusters K is a user-specified hyperparameter, and the model is trained entirely without ground-truth labels, relying on self-generated pseudo-labels for supervision.

## Method

COPER uses view-specific deep encoders trained with a Deep CCA correlation-maximization loss to produce aligned latent embeddings, which are fused via learnable weights and passed to a clustering head. A multi-view pseudo-labeling procedure selects high-confidence samples, refines them per-view using cosine-similarity thresholds, and enforces cross-view agreement to yield reliable pseudo-labels. The key mechanism is within-cluster permutation: samples sharing the same pseudo-label are randomly re-paired across views, and these permuted pairs are fed back into the CCA objective. The paper proves that under a shared-latent-variable assumption, this permutation-augmented CCA converges to the same projection as supervised LDA, effectively injecting discriminative structure into an unsupervised correlational objective. An error bound via perturbation theory quantifies how pseudo-label noise degrades the LDA approximation.

## Results

Across ten multi-view clustering benchmarks, COPER achieves average ACC of 61.6%, ARI of 47.8%, and NMI of 54.0%, outperforming the best baseline (OPMC) by 5.7% in ACC, 6.4% in ARI, and 1.1% in NMI. On the large-scale 300K-MNIST-CIFAR10 dataset, COPER reaches 81.93% ACC and 66.75% ARI, substantially exceeding RMCNC (54.47% ACC) and DSMVC (11.69% ACC). The ablation study on MSRVC1 shows that removing the correlation loss, within-cluster permutations, or multi-view agreement each degrades ACC by 6–12 percentage points relative to the full model (92.57%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COPER outperforms all eight baselines on average across the ten standard datasets, with the largest margins over the two-stage methods (AE, DCCA-AE, ℓ₀-DCCA) and the weakest end-to-end baselines (RMCNC, MVCAN). Among end-to-end competitors, COPER beats OPMC (the strongest baseline) by 5.7% ACC and 6.4% ARI on average, and surpasses CVCL, DSMVC, ICMVC, and RMCNC by even wider margins. Per-dataset, COPER wins on 8 of 10 datasets in ACC; it trails OPMC on Caltech5V-7 (82.81 vs. 88.51) and ICMVC on Scene15 (40.68 vs. 41.26). On the scalability test (300K samples), COPER achieves 81.93% ACC versus 54.47% for RMCNC and 11.69% for DSMVC, indicating that the permutation-based CCA objective scales effectively to large, noisy datasets. The two-stage baselines (DCCA-AE, ℓ₀-DCCA) are consistently weaker, confirming the paper's argument that decoupling representation learning from clustering is suboptimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

COPER builds directly on two threads: (1) the observation by Kursun et al. (2011) that pairing same-class samples across views and applying CCA recovers the LDA projection, and (2) Deep CCA (Andrew et al., 2013) as the nonlinear representation backbone. The key departure from prior end-to-end MVC methods (DSMVC, CVCL, ICMVC, RMCNC, OPMC, MVCAN) is the introduction of within-cluster pseudo-label permutations as a self-supervised augmentation that bridges unsupervised CCA to supervised LDA without requiring ground-truth labels. Unlike two-stage DCCA-then-K-means pipelines, COPER jointly optimizes representation and clustering, and unlike prior end-to-end methods, its permutation mechanism is invariant to the specific CCA variant used.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantee that within-cluster CCA permutations approximate LDA under mild assumptions provides a principled justification for a simple, general-purpose augmentation that can be plugged into any CCA-based representation learner. The consistent 5–7% ACC improvement over the best baselines across ten diverse datasets (genomics, text, images, video) and the scalability result on 300K noisy samples suggest broad practical utility. Limitations include the need for relatively large batch sizes (inherent to DCCA loss), a suboptimal fixed loss combination, and the reliance on pseudo-label quality that degrades the LDA approximation (bounded but not eliminated). Open directions include smarter multi-task loss weighting, domain-shift robustness, and interpretability modules.
