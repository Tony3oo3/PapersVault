## Problem

Reconstructing high-quality, relightable 3D objects from sparse images remains challenging because existing inverse rendering methods require dense captures (40–100+ views), hours of per-scene optimization, and analytical BRDFs that cannot model complex light transport. Under unknown static lighting, the material-lighting ambiguity is particularly severe, and regression-based approaches fail to capture the multi-modal distribution of specular highlights and shadows. No prior method achieves feed-forward, seconds-scale relightable reconstruction from as few as 4–8 views.

## Input / Output

The method takes 4–8 posed RGB images (256×256 or 512×512) with known camera parameters encoded as Plücker rays, plus a target HDR environment map (128×256) specifying the desired relighting. It outputs a relightable 3D Gaussian Splatting where geometry (per-pixel position, scale, rotation, opacity) is deterministic and appearance (4th-order spherical harmonics, 75 coefficients per Gaussian) is generated probabilistically. The 3DGS can be rendered from any novel viewpoint under the target lighting, and the appearance is produced as a byproduct of the diffusion denoising loop rather than predicted in a single forward pass.

## Method

RelitLRM builds on the GS-LRM feed-forward transformer architecture but replaces its deterministic appearance head with a relit-view diffusion model. A 24-layer geometry transformer predicts 3DGS geometry parameters from tokenized input images; an 8-layer appearance diffusion transformer then generates relit spherical harmonics by denoising noisy relit views, conditioned on target lighting tokens (dual tone-mapped HDR environment map), geometry features, and a diffusion timestep. The key mechanism is an x0-prediction loop: at each denoising step, the model decodes 3DGS SH coefficients, renders them with the fixed geometry, and uses the rendered image as the denoised output, so the final 3DGS radiance emerges as a side product after 5 DDIM steps with classifier-free guidance (weight 3.0). The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end from scratch on ~1M synthetic objects rendered under 8,000+ HDR environment maps, using L2 and LPIPS losses on rendered views, totaling ~0.4B parameters and completing inference in 2–3 seconds on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On STANFORD-ORB, RelitLRM achieves PSNR-H of 24.67 and PSNR-L of 31.52 using only 6 input views in ~2 seconds, compared to 42–67 views and hours of optimization for baselines (best baseline Neural-PBIR: PSNR-H 26.01, PSNR-L 33.26). On TENSOIR-SYNTHETIC, the method reaches an average PSNR of 29.05 and SSIM of 0.936 with 8 views in ~3 seconds, within ~0.7 dB of the best dense-view baselines (FlashCache 29.72, IllumiNeRF 29.71). On a held-out Objaverse set, RelitLRM outperforms image-based relighting methods by a large margin: PSNR 28.50 vs. 24.51 (NeuralGaffer) and 20.02 (DiLightNet), while using only 5 NFE steps and 0.4B parameters versus 50 NFE and 0.9B parameters. The method also achieves the best geometry (Chamfer distance 0.39) on STANFORD-ORB among all compared approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against optimization-based inverse rendering baselines (Neural-PBIR, IllumiNeRF, NVDiffRec-MC, InvRender, NeRFactor, NVDiffRec, PhySG, TensoIR, Mitsuba+Neus), RelitLRM trades a modest 1–1.5 dB PSNR gap on STANFORD-ORB (24.67 vs. 26.01 for Neural-PBIR) for a 10× reduction in input views (6 vs. 42–67) and a ~1000× speedup (2 s vs. hours). On OBJECTS-WITH-LIGHTING, the method is competitive with InvRender (23.08 vs. 23.45 PSNR) and TensoIR (23.08 vs. 24.15) but trails Mitsuba+Neus (26.24) and PhySG in SSIM (0.79 vs. 0.82). On TENSOIR-SYNTHETIC, RelitLRM (avg. PSNR 29.05) is within 0.7 dB of the top dense-view methods (FlashCache 29.72, IllumiNeRF 29.71, TensoIR 29.64) while using only 8 views versus 100. Against image-based relighting methods (NeuralGaffer, DiLightNet), RelitLRM wins decisively on the held-out Objaverse set: PSNR 28.50 vs. 24.51 and 20.02 respectively, with fewer NFE steps (5 vs. 50/20) and fewer parameters (0.4B vs. 0.9B). In geometry evaluation on STANFORD-ORB, RelitLRM achieves the best Chamfer distance (0.39) among all methods, outperforming Neural-PBIR (0.43), InvRender (0.44), and NVDiffRec-MC (0.51), while NeRFactor and PhySG are far worse (9.53, 9.28).

## Relation to Prior Work

RelitLRM extends GS-LRM (Zhang et al., 2024a), a feed-forward transformer for 3DGS reconstruction from sparse views, by replacing its deterministic appearance head with a diffusion-based probabilistic relighting module conditioned on target HDR environment maps. It departs from the long line of optimization-based inverse rendering methods (NeRFactor, TensoIR, InvRender, NVDiffRec) that require 50–100+ views and hours of per-scene optimization, and from single-view 2D relighting diffusion models (Neural Gaffer, DiLightNet) that cannot produce 3D-consistent novel-view relighting. The uni-stream self-attention architecture is explicitly inspired by vision-language models like LLaVA and PaliGemma, treating image, environment-map, and timestep tokens equivalently after lightweight MLP projection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: matching or approaching state-of-the-art relighting quality with 4–8 views in 2–3 seconds (vs. 50–100 views and hours) makes relightable 3D asset creation feasible in interactive and production pipelines. Key open questions include the 'floater' phenomenon where additional input views introduce low-opacity Gaussians that brighten objects (limiting scalability beyond ~16 views), the inability to support material editing due to the lack of explicit appearance decomposition, and the restriction to environment-map lighting that cannot represent near-field sources. The finding that probabilistic (diffusion) appearance generation is necessary to capture multi-modal specular highlights—while deterministic regression produces over-smooth results—suggests a broader principle for generative 3D appearance modeling.
