## Problem

Evaluating the rapidly growing landscape of LLMs is costly and limited: standard benchmarks are domain-specific, prone to data contamination, and require direct model access, while the opacity of proprietary models' training details makes it impossible to trace their relationships or estimate capabilities without expensive full evaluations. No existing method can infer how models relate to one another (e.g., fine-tuning lineage, shared training data) or predict benchmark performance from model outputs alone, particularly for closed-source models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of LLMs (111 open-source and 45 closed models) and a gene set of prompt contexts (truncated to 20–100 characters from Open-Web-Math or MBXP test sets) as input. Each model is queried N=32 times per gene to generate 4 characters, which serve as a tokenizer-agnostic 'allele' proxy. Outputs include a pairwise similarity matrix (Nei distance), a distance matrix, an unrooted dendrogram via Neighbor Joining, and per-benchmark score predictions (MMLU, ARC, Hellaswag, TruthfulQA, Winogrande, GSM8k) produced by a logistic regression on ICA-reduced (15-component) similarity features.

## Method

PhyloLM adapts the Nei genetic distance to LLMs by establishing a formal analogy: LLMs are populations, prompt contexts are genes, and generated tokens are alleles, so that P(a|g) becomes LLM(t|c). For each pair of models, the similarity is computed as the cosine-like overlap of their empirical token distributions across G=128 shared contexts, converted to a distance via D = −log(S). A 4-character truncation of generated text mitigates tokenizer mismatch across models. The resulting distance matrix is visualized as an unrooted dendrogram using the Neighbor Joining algorithm, which correctly recovers known fine-tuning lineages (e.g., within the Mistral family) and clusters models by family. For performance prediction, the similarity matrix is reduced to 15 components via ICA and fed into a logistic regression with a sigmoid output, validated with a leave-one-family-out protocol, achieving ~48% variance explained across benchmarks and ~52% within families.

## Results

PhyloLM's phylogenetic distance metric predicts benchmark scores with a mean Pearson correlation of r = 0.68 ± 0.04 across all six HuggingFace Open LLM Leaderboard benchmarks (MMLU, ARC, Hellaswag, TruthfulQA, Winogrande, GSM8k), explaining 48.2% of between-model variance. Within individual model families, the correlation remains significant at r = 0.64 ± 0.05, explaining 52.2% of variance. The method correctly reconstructs the known Mistral-family phylogenetic tree (topologically equivalent to ground truth) and clusters 111 open-source and 45 closed models into their correct families. The total cost per model is ~4,096 queries of ~20 tokens (G=128 genes × N=32 probes), approximately 10× cheaper than running MMLU (~14,000 queries of ~70 tokens).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare PhyloLM against other phylogenetic or LLM-evaluation methods; instead, it validates the approach against two ground-truth criteria. First, on the Mistral family (where fine-tuning lineage is publicly documented), PhyloLM's NJ dendrogram is topologically equivalent to the known ground-truth tree for both math and code gene sets (only the root placement differs on code). Second, the predictive power is validated via a leave-one-family-out logistic regression, where the phylogenetic distance (reduced to 15 ICA components) predicts held-out family benchmark scores significantly above zero correlation for all six benchmarks (t(11)=16.0, p<0.001). The practical comparison is against the cost of full benchmarking: PhyloLM requires ~4,096 short queries per model versus ~14,000 long queries for MMLU alone, making it roughly 10× cheaper in token cost while capturing ~48% of performance variance. No competing method for inferring LLM relationships or predicting scores from model outputs is benchmarked, leaving the methodological comparison open for future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the Nei genetic distance framework (Takezaki & Nei, 1996) and Neighbor Joining tree reconstruction (Saitou & Nei, 1987) from population genetics into the LLM domain, establishing a direct analogy: populations → LLMs, genes → prompt contexts, alleles → generated tokens. The conceptual lineage also draws on prior applications of phylogenetic methods to cultural artifacts (folktales, languages, craft assemblages), but extends this to a new class of cultural artifact—neural language models—where the 'genetic material' is the token-level output distribution. For performance prediction, the paper uses a standard logistic regression with ICA dimensionality reduction on the resulting similarity matrix, building on the HuggingFace Open LLM Leaderboard benchmarks as ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhyloLM offers a ~10× cheaper alternative to full benchmarking for estimating LLM capabilities and, crucially, works on proprietary models without access to training details, providing a transparency tool for the research community. The ~50% variance explained in benchmark scores is practically useful but leaves substantial room for improvement, and the authors note that chat API prompting biases, the presence of common ancestors among studied models, and the limited gene-set diversity (two topics) are open challenges. Future directions include broader gene sets, temperature sensitivity analysis, and applications to model merging and quantization, suggesting the phylogenetic lens could become a general-purpose diagnostic for the LLM ecosystem.
