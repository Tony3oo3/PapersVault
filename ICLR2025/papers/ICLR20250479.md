## Problem

Existing zeroth-order optimization literature relies on specific random perturbation distributions (Gaussian, uniform sphere, Rademacher) without a principled explanation for why they are optimal with respect to gradient estimator variance. This paper asks: what class of perturbation distributions minimizes the asymptotic variance of the two-point estimator as the stepsize μ→0? The answer reveals an overlooked class—directionally aligned perturbations (DAPs)—that adaptively concentrate accuracy along high-gradient directions, in contrast to the fixed-length perturbations that dominate prior work.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only black-box function evaluations at two points per perturbation (x and x+μv), requiring no access to gradients or the computational graph. A key structural detail is the two-phase batch design: the first b/2 perturbations (drawn from a standard δ-unbiased distribution) produce an initial gradient estimate, which then defines the hyperplane onto which the second b/2 perturbations are projected to generate DAPs. The final gradient estimate is the average of both phases, and the output is a parameter update x_{t+1} = x_t − η·(combined estimate).

## Method

The paper formulates minimum-variance perturbation design as a constrained functional optimization over the space of probability distributions, subject to the δ-unbiasedness constraint E[vvᵀ] = δI_d. The central result (Theorem 2.2) proves via a Cauchy–Schwarz argument that the asymptotic variance is minimized if and only if either (a) ‖v‖² = dδ almost surely (fixed-length perturbations) or (b) (∇f(x)ᵀv)² = δ‖∇f(x)‖² almost surely (directionally aligned perturbations). For practical DAP sampling, Algorithm 1 projects a δ-unbiased random vector onto the random hyperplane {v : aᵀv = ±√δ‖a‖}, where a is the estimated gradient, preserving both the alignment constraint and the δ-unbiasedness in expectation. The convergence analysis (Theorem 3.1, Corollary 3.2) extends the best-known O(d/ε²) non-convex and O(d/ε) convex sample complexities to all minimum-variance perturbations, including DAPs, by bounding the estimator error through the fourth-order moment ρ_V. Empirically, DAPs outperform uniform and Gaussian perturbations on sparse-gradient synthetic problems and on fine-tuning OPT-1.3b on SST-2 with batch size b=2.

## Results

On synthetic problems (d=16, μ=10⁻⁴), DAP with exact gradient knowledge achieves the lowest τ-MSE across all batch sizes, with the advantage growing as batch size increases beyond b=32 for the quadratic function. On the extremely sparse product function (only one non-zero gradient entry), DAP shows a substantially larger gap over uniform and Gaussian perturbations. For fine-tuning OPT-1.3b on SST-2 (η=10⁻⁴, μ=10⁻⁵, b=2), DAP achieves the lowest training loss among all tested perturbations, with the advantage not requiring a large batch size. In the mesh optimization task (b=512, μ=10⁻⁵, η=0.1), DAP also converges to a lower L∞ error than uniform and Gaussian baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DAP against four classical perturbation schemes: uniform sphere (Duchi et al., 2015), Gaussian (Ghadimi & Lan, 2013), Rademacher (Spall, 1987), and random coordinate (Ji et al., 2019). On the synthetic τ-MSE benchmark, DAP (with exact gradient) consistently outperforms all four baselines, with the largest gains on the sparse product function where the anisotropic nature of DAP concentrates accuracy along the single effective direction. On OPT-1.3b SST-2 fine-tuning, DAP achieves the lowest training loss, beating uniform, Gaussian, Rademacher, and random coordinate perturbations; the paper notes this advantage holds even at the minimal batch size b=2. In the mesh optimization experiment, DAP again outperforms both uniform and Gaussian perturbations in minimizing the L∞ error between coarse and fine mesh solutions. The paper does not report cases where DAP underperforms the baselines, though it acknowledges that the advantage is most pronounced under gradient sparsity or anisotropy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established zeroth-order optimization (ZOO) framework, particularly the two-point gradient estimator and the δ-unbiasedness assumption from Kozak et al. (2023) and Rando et al. (2024b), with convergence analysis adapted from Khaled & Richtárik (2022) and Mishchenko et al. (2020). The key departure is conceptual: prior work treated fixed-length perturbations (uniform sphere, Rademacher, random coordinate) as the canonical minimum-variance choice, while this paper formulates the perturbation design as a constrained functional optimization over all distributions and reveals a second, previously overlooked class—directionally aligned perturbations (DAPs)—that also achieves minimum asymptotic variance. The paper also notes that the widely-used Gaussian perturbation, despite its popularity, does not belong to the minimum-variance class due to its excess kurtosis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of DAPs as a second minimum-variance perturbation class opens a new design axis for ZOO methods, particularly relevant for high-dimensional problems with sparse or anisotropic gradients where DAPs adaptively concentrate accuracy along critical directions. The practical implementation (Algorithm 2) requires an extra half-batch of function evaluations to estimate the gradient before generating DAPs, which partially offsets the variance reduction and limits the net efficiency gain. Open questions include whether the sufficient conditions for minimum variance can be made necessary (the paper notes a mixed-distribution counterexample), how DAPs behave under non-smooth or highly non-convex objectives, and whether the anisotropic advantage persists at larger scales beyond the OPT-1.3b/SST-2 setting tested here.
