## Problem

Higher-order message-passing (HOMP) is the dominant framework for topological deep learning, yet its expressivity from a topological perspective has never been formally analyzed. This paper proves that HOMP is fundamentally blind to basic topological and metric invariants—diameter, orientability, planarity, and homology groups—despite TDL's stated goal of leveraging topological structure. It further shows that HOMP fails to expressively leverage the sparse higher-order cells produced by common graph lifting and pooling methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252930/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on combinatorial complexes (CCs) of arbitrary dimension, where data is represented as cochains (feature vectors) over cells of each rank, with message passing governed by natural neighborhood functions (adjacency, co-adjacency, incidence). Graphs are lifted to CCs via cyclic lifting (adding triangles as 2-cells), and molecular graphs from ZINC serve as the base graphs for both real-world benchmarks and topological property prediction tasks. Outputs include cell-level embeddings for readout, graph-level regression/classification scores, and predictions of topological properties such as (0,1,2)-cross-diameter and second-order Betti numbers of the lifted complexes.

## Method

The paper first establishes a topological HOMP-indistinguishability criterion: if two CCs share a common covering complex, no HOMP model can distinguish them, extending Bamberger's graph-covering result to combinatorial complexes. Using this criterion, it proves HOMP cannot capture diameter, orientability, planarity, or homology groups. To overcome these limitations, the authors introduce Multi-Cellular Networks (MCN), which extend the HOMP tensor diagram with multi-cellular cochain spaces (tuples of cells across ranks) and equivariant linear layers borrowed from Invariant Graph Networks, provably achieving full expressivity. For scalability, SMCN replaces the most expensive high-order equivariant updates (over C^{3e_r} and C^{2e_{r1}+e_{r2}}) with Subcomplex Layer (SCL) updates that apply subgraph GNNs to bags of augmented Hasse graphs—graph structures encoding relational information between cells of the complex. SMCN provably distinguishes complexes by diameter, 0-th homology, and (for 2D surfaces) all homology groups, and empirically outperforms both HOMP baselines and expressive GNNs on real-world graph benchmarks.

## Results

On real-world graph benchmarks, SMCN achieves the best performance across all three tasks: ZINC regression (MAE 0.060±0.004), MOLHIV classification (ROC-AUC 81.16±0.90), and MOLESOL regression (RMSE 0.809±0.037), outperforming both HOMP baselines (best: CIN++ at MAE 0.077) and expressive GNNs (best: Subgraphormer+PE at MAE 0.063). On the Torus Dataset, SMCN separates all HOMP-indistinguishable pairs while HOMP fails on every pair. On topological property prediction over lifted ZINC graphs, SMCN reaches 92.76% accuracy on cross-diameter and 99.61% on the second Betti number, versus 67.87% and 81.76% for a custom HOMP architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMCN outperforms all 13 baselines on all three real-world benchmarks. Against HOMP architectures, SMCN improves ZINC MAE from 0.077 (CIN++) to 0.060, and MOLHIV ROC-AUC from 80.94 (CIN) to 81.16, while dramatically reducing MOLESOL RMSE from 1.288 (CIN) to 0.809. Against expressive GNNs, SMCN beats the strongest graph-only method (Subgraphormer+PE) by 0.003 MAE on ZINC and 0.017 RMSE on MOLESOL, and surpasses PPGN++(6) by 0.011 MAE on ZINC. On the Torus Dataset, SMCN separates 100% of HOMP-indistinguishable pairs while HOMP separates 0%. On topological property prediction (Table 2), SMCN achieves 92.76% accuracy on cross-diameter and 99.61% on the second Betti number, substantially exceeding the custom HOMP baseline (67.87% and 81.76%) and CIN (34.78% and 42.15%), confirming that expressively leveraging higher-order topological structure yields measurable gains over both standard TDL and graph-only approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GNN expressivity analysis (where MPNNs are shown equivalent to 1-WL) to the topological deep learning domain, proving that HOMP—the dominant framework for learning on simplicial, cellular, and combinatorial complexes—has analogous blindspots. It draws directly on Bamberger's (2022) covering-space characterization of MPNN indistinguishability, generalizing it from graphs to combinatorial complexes. For the constructive side, MCN adapts the equivariant linear layer machinery of Invariant Graph Networks (Maron et al., 2018) to multi-cellular cochain spaces, while SMCN replaces the most expensive equivariant updates with subgraph GNN-style operations on augmented Hasse graphs, borrowing from the subgraph network literature (Bevilacqua et al., 2021; Frasca et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023b).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that HOMP cannot capture diameter, orientability, planarity, or homology groups, the very invariants that motivate topological deep learning—challenges a core assumption in the field and provides a rigorous criterion (CC coverings) for identifying further blindspots. SMCN offers a practical path forward, outperforming both HOMP baselines and expressive GNNs on real-world molecular benchmarks, but its super-linear scaling in the number of cells limits applicability to large complexes. Open questions include whether SMCN can fully capture all topological invariants (the paper only proves partial results for homology and orientability), whether more scalable alternatives exist, and whether the new benchmarks generalize beyond the specific invariants tested.
