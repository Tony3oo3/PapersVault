## Problem

The Knowledge Neuron (KN) thesis rests on the Knowledge Localization (KL) assumption that a fact is stored in a fixed set of knowledge neurons, independent of how the fact is queried. This paper demonstrates that the KL assumption is violated for a large majority of facts (77% in LLaMA3-8b) and that it neglects the attention module's role in dynamically selecting which neurons to activate for a given query.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251057/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes cloze-style paraphrase queries from the ParaRel dataset (27,610 entries across 36 relations) expressing the same (subject, relation, object) fact, processed through pretrained LLMs (GPT-2, LLaMA2-7b, LLaMA3-8b). It produces per-query knowledge neuron sets, a Consistency Score (CS) that classifies each fact as consistent (K_C) or inconsistent (K_I) via static or Otsu thresholds, Knowledge Synapse (KS) positions extracted from attention score matrices, and modified MLP weights for knowledge erasure or update. WikiText2 perplexity is used as a general-capability guardrail to ensure edits do not excessively damage the model.

## Method

The paper proposes the Query Localization (QL) assumption, comprising two mechanisms: Query-KN Mapping (KNs are associated with the specific query rather than the underlying fact) and Dynamic KN Selection (the attention module selects which KNs to activate for a given query). To operationalize QL, the authors introduce a Consistency-Aware Score (CAS) that combines the mean activation (μ) across neighbor queries and penalizes the standard deviation (σ), selecting neurons with high and consistent activation for editing. They further define Knowledge Synapses (KS) from attention score matrices using a dynamic threshold to study the attention module's selective role. The resulting CAS-based modification method achieves 8–9% average improvement over N_i and N_u baselines on LLaMA3-8b in the Erasure setting while keeping ΔPPL under 0.10, compared to 1.05 for the N_u baseline.

## Results

The paper's central finding is that 77% of facts in LLaMA3-8b (and 70% in LLaMA2-7b) are classified as Inconsistent Knowledge (K_I), violating the Knowledge Localization assumption, with t-statistics exceeding 100 confirming statistical significance. For knowledge modification on K_I facts under the Erasure setting on LLaMA3-8b, the proposed Consistency-Aware (CAS) method achieves an Avg score of 0.51 with ΔPPL of 0.09, compared to 0.43 (ΔPPL 0.05) for N_i and 0.42 (ΔPPL 1.05) for N_u. In the Update setting, CAS achieves 0.46 Avg with ΔPPL 0.14 for K_I on LLaMA3-8b, versus 0.41 (ΔPPL 0.08) for N_i and 0.43 (ΔPPL 0.93) for N_u. Sequential editing experiments on 100 facts further confirm CAS outperforms the KL-based N_i method (Avg 0.42 vs 0.29, Gen 0.41 vs 0.08).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three knowledge localization methods (Dai et al. 2022 IG, Enguehard 2023 SIG, Chen et al. 2024a AMIG) for the consistency analysis, finding that all three consistently classify a large majority of facts as K_I (union U_I = 0.77 for LLaMA3-8b with static threshold), confirming the finding is method-agnostic. For knowledge modification, the two baselines are N_i (KNs localized to the specific query) and N_u (union of KNs across all neighbor queries). The CAS method decisively outperforms N_i on K_I facts: generalization jumps from 0.04 to 0.30 (Erasure) and from 0.08 to 0.29 (Update) on LLaMA3-8b, while maintaining comparable reliability and locality. CAS also substantially outperforms N_u on K_I facts in terms of model preservation: ΔPPL drops from 1.05 to 0.09 (Erasure) and from 0.93 to 0.14 (Update) on LLaMA3-8b, while achieving higher Avg scores (0.51 vs 0.42, 0.46 vs 0.43). For K_C facts, CAS performs comparably to both baselines, supporting the claim that KL is merely a special case of QL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly challenges the Knowledge Localization (KL) assumption that underpins the Knowledge Neuron (KN) thesis established by Dai et al. (2022) and operationalized in ROME/MEMIT-style editing by Meng et al. (2022, 2023). While prior work treats knowledge as statically stored in a fixed set of MLP neurons per fact, this paper demonstrates that for the majority of facts, neuron localization is query-dependent rather than fact-dependent, and that the attention module plays a previously neglected 'selection' role in knowledge expression. The paper builds on three integrated-gradients-based localization methods (Dai et al. 2022, Enguehard 2023, Chen et al. 2024a) as diagnostic tools and on the ROME weight-modification formula for its editing experiments, but reframes the entire theoretical foundation from fact-to-neuron mapping to query-to-neuron mapping with dynamic attention-mediated selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that 77% of facts in LLaMA3-8b violate the KL assumption, this paper calls into question the theoretical basis of a large body of KN-inspired editing and interpretability work, potentially redirecting the field toward query-aware and attention-aware frameworks. The proposed Consistency-Aware KN modification method demonstrates practical utility (8–9% improvement over baselines), but the broader impact hinges on whether the QL assumption can be fully integrated into editing pipelines—particularly the Dynamic KN Selection component, which the authors acknowledge is underexploited. Open questions include why inconsistent knowledge exists (possibly related to pre-training exposure frequency), how QL reconciles with circuit-based and feature-based interpretability approaches, and whether the findings generalize beyond cloze-style factual recall to more complex knowledge tasks.
