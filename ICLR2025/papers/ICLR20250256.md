## Problem

Transformer-based world models (STORM, TWM, IRIS) improved training efficiency over RNN-based DreamerV3 but failed to match its performance. The paper identifies that the next-state prediction objective is too easy for Transformers because adjacent latent states have very high cosine similarity, preventing the model from learning rich temporal representations. TWISTER addresses this by extending world model predictions to longer time horizons via contrastive learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 64×64 RGB image observations and discrete action sequences. Images are encoded into categorical stochastic states (32 features × 32 classes) via a convolutional VAE, which are then processed by the Transformer with masked self-attention and relative positional encodings. The agent state s_t concatenates the Transformer hidden state h_t with the stochastic state z_t, and the model outputs categorical actions, symlog-encoded reward predictions, reconstructed images, and contrastive feature representations for K=10 future steps. Random crop-and-resize augmentation is applied to contrastive samples during training.

## Method

TWISTER replaces the next-state prediction objective with Action-Conditioned Contrastive Predictive Coding (AC-CPC), where the world model must identify the correct future stochastic state z'_{t+k} among B×T−1 negative samples using an InfoNCE loss. The CPC predictor p_φ^k is conditioned on the concatenated sequence of future actions a_{t:t+k}, which the ablations show is critical for accurate multi-step prediction. The world model is a Transformer State-Space Model with 4 blocks, 8 attention heads, and relative positional encodings that cache keys/values across segments to enable imagination from any state. The total world model loss combines symlog reward prediction, binary continuation prediction, L2 image reconstruction, KL-divergence dynamics with stop-gradient regularization, and the AC-CPC contrastive term. Agent behavior learning follows DreamerV3's actor-critic setup in latent space with H=15 imagination steps, symlog cross-entropy losses, and EMA-based return normalization.

## Results

On the Atari 100k benchmark (26 games, 400k environment steps), TWISTER achieves a human-normalized mean score of 162% and median of 77%, surpassing all prior model-based methods without look-ahead search. It reaches superhuman performance on 12 of 26 games. On the DeepMind Control Suite (20 tasks, 1M steps, visual inputs), TWISTER obtains a mean score of 801.8 and median of 907.6, outperforming DreamerPro (770.9), DreamerV3 (739.6), and TD-MPC2 (720.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TWISTER outperforms all eight baselines on the primary Atari 100k benchmark. Against the closest competitor Δ-IRIS (139% normed mean), TWISTER gains +23 points in mean and +24 in median, with particularly large margins on Gopher (22234 vs 6445), Kangaroo (6016 vs 2269), and Road Runner (17832 vs 10414). Against STORM (127%), the gap is +35 mean points, driven by superior performance on games with small moving objects (Breakout, Pong) and numerous key objects (Amidar, Bank Heist, Ms Pacman). Compared to the RNN-based DreamerV3 (112%), TWISTER improves by +50 mean points, confirming that the Transformer architecture combined with AC-CPC substantially exceeds what the next-state prediction objective alone can achieve. On the DeepMind Control Suite, TWISTER (mean 801.8) beats DreamerPro (770.9), DreamerV3 (739.6), and TD-MPC2 (720.9), with the largest gains on complex locomotion tasks like Quadruped Walk (904.9 vs 676.9/352.6/252.6) and Acrobot Swingup (239.4 vs 438.3/210.0/216.0, where DreamerPro still leads). TWISTER loses to Δ-IRIS on a few individual Atari games (Assault, Asterix, Breakout, Battle Zone, Crazy Climber, Frostbite, Hero, Krull, Up N Down) but dominates on the aggregate metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

TWISTER extends the Transformer-based world model line initiated by TWM, STORM, and IRIS, which replaced DreamerV3's RNN-based RSSM with masked self-attention but retained the next-state prediction objective. The key departure is the introduction of action-conditioned Contrastive Predictive Coding (AC-CPC), which extends the prediction horizon from 1 step to K=10 steps, drawing on CPC's prior use as an auxiliary loss in A3C (Oord et al., 2018) but repurposing it as a core world model training signal. The architecture follows STORM's design choices (fused state-action tokens, categorical VAE, relative positional encodings) while adding a contrastive representation network and multi-step predictor heads. The agent behavior learning (actor-critic in latent space with symlog returns and EMA normalization) is inherited directly from DreamerV3.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete explanation for why prior Transformer-based world models underperformed RNN-based ones: next-state prediction in latent space is too easy (high cosine similarity between adjacent states), failing to exploit the Transformer's capacity for long-range temporal reasoning. The ablation showing AC-CPC yields 162% with a Transformer but only 121% with DreamerV3's RSSM suggests a genuine architecture-objective synergy that could guide future design choices. Open questions include whether combining AC-CPC with look-ahead search (MCTS, as in EfficientZero V2) would further close the gap, whether the approach transfers to continuous control at scale, and whether other self-supervised objectives (e.g., DINO-style, masked prediction) could complement or replace CPC in world model training.
