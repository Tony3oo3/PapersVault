## Problem

State-of-the-art chess engines like Stockfish and AlphaZero far exceed human strength but are neither skill-calibrated to match individual players nor capable of modeling human-like behaviors such as pondering time and resignation. This paper addresses the open challenge of building a chess AI that is simultaneously human-aligned (indistinguishable in behavior from a human player) and skill-calibrated (evenly matched across the full Elo spectrum from beginner to grandmaster).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250174/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequential token sequence of UCI-notation moves (1968 possible move tokens) prefixed with two soft control tokens that linearly interpolate between a 500-Elo 'weak' embedding and a 3000-Elo 'strong' embedding to condition on both players' skill, plus metadata tokens for time control and termination. The board state is never explicitly encoded—it is fully implied by the move history. The model produces three outputs per position: a softmax distribution over legal next moves, a scalar pondering-time prediction (seconds), and a tanh-squashed value estimate in [-1, 1] indicating expected game outcome.

## Method

ALLIE is a 355M-parameter decoder-only Transformer initialized from GPT-2 medium weights, trained via supervised learning on 91 million human blitz games (6.6B tokens) with a joint loss combining cross-entropy for next-move prediction and MSE for pondering time and game-outcome value. The key architectural choice is soft control tokens for Elo conditioning, which avoid data sparsity and preserve the ordinal structure of ratings. At inference, a time-adaptive MCTS procedure allocates the number of rollouts as N_sim = ⌊c · t_θ(m)⌋, making search depth proportional to the model's predicted human pondering time at each position. The MCTS itself is a KL-regularized policy optimization (following Grill et al., 2020) that maximizes search Q-values while staying close to the human-learned policy, preserving humanlike play even as search improves move strength. This adaptive budget mechanism is the core novelty: it concentrates computational effort on critical positions where humans would think longer, achieving a mean skill calibration error of only 49 Elo across 1000–2600 Elo opponents using exclusively human training data.

## Results

ALLIE-POLICY achieves 55.7% move-matching accuracy on the Lichess evaluation set, outperforming MAIA* (51.6%) and GPT-3.5 (53.7%). The headline result is skill calibration: ALLIE-ADAPTIVE-SEARCH achieves a mean skill calibration error of only 49 Elo (max 95 Elo) across 200-Elo bins, with an estimated performance rating of 2528 against 2500-Elo opponents. The model also learns to resign appropriately (86.4% TPR, 0.1% FPR) and predicts human pondering time with Pearson's r = 0.697. On out-of-distribution random games, the top-1 move is valid 99.9% of the time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MAIA* (McIlroy-Young et al., 2020), ALLIE-POLICY wins on move-matching accuracy (55.7% vs. 51.6%) and on skill calibration (127 vs. 146 mean SCE), while ALLIE-ADAPTIVE-SEARCH dramatically outperforms MAIA* with 49 vs. 146 mean SCE and 95 vs. 336 max SCE. Against 2500-Elo opponents specifically, ALLIE-ADAPTIVE-SEARCH reaches an estimated 2528 Elo versus MAIA*'s 2138, a gap of nearly 400 Elo. Against Stockfish (Romstad et al., 2008) as an oracle value function, ALLIE's value predictions correlate with game outcomes comparably well and actually outperform Stockfish in games with >100 Elo gaps (e.g., 79.3% vs. 69.9% midgame outcome prediction accuracy), likely because ALLIE has access to player skill metadata. However, ALLIE's value function has blind spots on tactical positions like forced checkmates and queen sacrifices where Stockfish is superior. Overall, ALLIE-ADAPTIVE-SEARCH is the clear winner on the primary skill-calibration metric, while ALLIE-POLICY wins on human move prediction accuracy over both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the human-aligned chess line initiated by MAIA (McIlroy-Young et al., 2020) by replacing the residual CNN with a decoder-only Transformer initialized from GPT-2, treating chess as a sequential language modeling task. It builds on Jacob et al. (2022) and Grill et al. (2020) for combining human-learned policy/value with KL-regularized MCTS, but introduces a key departure: the search budget is allocated adaptively based on predicted human ponder time rather than held constant as in AlphaZero. The soft control tokens for Elo conditioning and the joint training of move, pondering-time, and value heads represent a unified architecture that prior human-aligned systems (MAIA's separate per-skill models) did not achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a chess AI trained exclusively on human games—without self-play or distillation from superhuman engines—can achieve near-perfect skill calibration (49 Elo average gap) across the full 1000–2600 Elo spectrum, suggesting human behavioral data alone suffices for calibrated play. The time-adaptive MCTS principle (allocate compute proportional to human reasoning time) is a general idea potentially transferable to other domains requiring AI to match human partners. Key limitations include reliance on blitz-only data (making slower time controls unaddressed), observed late-game blunders, and the fact that all evaluation was on a single platform with players who knew they were facing a bot, leaving a proper Turing-test evaluation as an open question.
