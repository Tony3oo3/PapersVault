## Problem

Existing RLHF methods either rely on reward inference (suffering from distribution shift, overfitting, and problem misspecification) or use DPO, whose closed-form optimal policy expression is only valid for bandit settings or deterministic MDPs. This paper asks whether a provably efficient RLHF approach exists that avoids reward inference entirely while handling general stochastic MDPs with infinite state and action spaces and general preference models beyond Bradley-Terry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253595/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each iteration, the algorithm samples N trajectory pairs from a current policy π_θ and a perturbed policy π_{θ+μv}, then queries M human evaluators per pair for one-bit preference feedback. The preference model uses a general link function σ (e.g., logistic for Bradley-Terry, Gaussian CDF for Probit) to map reward differences to comparison probabilities, and the inverse link function σ⁻¹ is applied to trimmed empirical preference probabilities to recover value function differences. The output is an updated policy parameter vector θ, with convergence to an ε-stationary policy requiring O(d⁸H³/ε⁵ log(d/ε)) total human queries.

## Method

The key insight is to treat human feedback as local directional information for policy gradient rather than as data for inferring a global reward function. ZPG perturbs the policy parameter θ in a random unit-sphere direction v, samples trajectory pairs from both policies, estimates the value difference V(π_{θ+μv}) − V(π_θ) via the inverse link function applied to trimmed preference probabilities, and plugs this into the zeroth-order gradient estimator (d/μ)·ΔV·v. ZBCPG replaces the full-dimensional sphere perturbation with a block-coordinate scheme: it samples K coordinates and Rademacher signs, yielding a sparse perturbation vector that enables parallel gradient estimation and lower per-iteration cost. Both algorithms achieve a convergence rate of O(Hd/T + d²√(log M)/√M + Hd√d/√N) to a stationary policy, with the proof requiring novel Lyapunov drift analysis that handles the bias introduced by the non-linear inverse link function and the stochasticity of trajectory rewards.

## Results

In a stochastic GridWorld environment with Bradley-Terry human feedback, ZPG achieves a last-iterate policy average return of 1.94±0.09, and ZBCPG achieves 1.91±0.09, both outperforming all three RLHF baselines. The theoretical convergence rate for both algorithms is O(Hd/T + d²√(log M)/√M + Hd√d/√N), yielding a total sample complexity of O(d⁸H³/ε⁵ · log(d/ε)) human preference queries to reach an ε-stationary policy. ZBCPG additionally supports parallelization across coordinate blocks, with faster convergence as the number of blocks increases (though at the cost of per-block query budget).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZPG (1.94) and ZBCPG (1.91) both outperform RM+PPO (1.80) by approximately 0.1–0.14 in average return, attributed to the inaccuracy of the learned reward model in the classic RLHF pipeline. Vanilla DPO performs catastrophically (−4.13), as its closed-form optimal policy expression is only valid for deterministic token-level MDPs and it is constrained to the neighborhood of a sub-optimal reference policy. Online DPO (1.71) improves substantially over vanilla DPO but still trails both proposed methods, with slower convergence due to a flat DPO loss landscape when the KL constraint weight is small. The gap between ZPG and ZBCPG is small (0.03), confirming that the block-coordinate perturbation strategy preserves gradient quality while enabling parallel implementation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends zeroth-order optimization (Ghadimi & Lan, 2013; Nesterov & Spokoiny, 2017) and block coordinate descent (Nesterov, 2012) to the RLHF setting, replacing the global reward model with local value-difference estimates from human preferences. It departs from DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), which relies on a closed-form optimal policy expression valid only for bandits or deterministic MDPs, by exploiting a 'local' preference-to-gradient relation that holds for general stochastic MDPs with parameterized policies. Prior RLHF-without-reward-inference work (Xu et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2024a) was restricted to tabular MDPs, and the preference model is generalized beyond Bradley-Terry to any monotone link function (e.g., Probit).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably efficient policy-based RLHF algorithm for general stochastic MDPs without reward inference, resolving an open question explicitly identified in the DPO literature. The polynomial sample complexity (d^8 H^3 / ε^5) is theoretically clean but potentially impractical at LLM scale, and all experiments are confined to a toy GridWorld environment, leaving the method's viability for real LLM alignment untested. Open questions include whether the zeroth-order perturbation approach scales to billion-parameter policy networks, how it interacts with practical constraints like KL regularization, and whether the moderate perturbation distance μ required by the theory is compatible with the small learning rates used in LLM fine-tuning.
