## Problem

Current mesh extraction methods (e.g., Marching Cubes, FlexiCubes) reconstruct 3D shapes using dense faces while ignoring geometric features, producing meshes with hundreds of times more faces than Artist-Created Meshes (AMs), poor topology, and artifacts on sharp edges and flat surfaces. This makes automatically produced 3D assets impractical for 3D industry pipelines that require efficient, artist-quality meshes. The paper reframes mesh extraction as a shape-conditioned generation problem to directly produce AMs with efficient topology.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251846/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud with normals (4096 points) as shape condition, which can be derived from virtually any 3D representation (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting, text-to-3D, scanning). During training, point clouds are intentionally corrupted by extracting an SDF, converting to a coarse Marching Cubes mesh, and resampling from it to bridge the domain gap between clean AM surfaces and rougher auto-generated surfaces. The output is an Artist-Created Mesh with efficient topology, constrained to at most 800 faces (the training data limit), typically producing meshes with far fewer faces than reconstruction-based alternatives.

## Method

MeshAnything combines a VQ-VAE (BERT-based transformer encoder/decoder with residual vector quantization, depth 3, codebook 8192) that learns a discrete mesh vocabulary, and a shape-conditioned decoder-only transformer (OPT-350M) trained for autoregressive mesh token generation. A pretrained point cloud encoder (from Michelangelo) projects 4096-point cloud features into a 257-token sequence that is prepended to the mesh token sequence, serving as the shape condition for next-token prediction. A key contribution is a noise-resistant decoder: after standard VQ-VAE training, the decoder is fine-tuned with the shape condition injected and Gumbel noise added to codebook sampling logits, teaching it to correct imperfect token sequences generated by the transformer at inference time. Training proceeds in three stages: VQ-VAE (8 A100s, 12h), decoder fine-tuning, then autoregressive transformer (8 A100s, 4 days) on 56k filtered meshes from Objaverse and ShapeNet.

## Results

On Objaverse, MeshAnything achieves COV 53.1, MMD 2.72, 1-NNA 55.7, FID 14.5, and KID 1.89, substantially outperforming MeshGPT (COV 41.7, MMD 3.83, FID 25.1, KID 6.11) and PolyGen (COV 23.2, MMD 6.22, FID 48.8, KID 27.7) across all generative metrics. In the mesh extraction comparison, MeshAnything produces meshes with only 0.318×10³ faces (F_R = 0.871 relative to ground truth), compared to 146.0×10³ faces for Marching Cubes (F_R = 462.2) and 121.1×10³ for FlexiCubes (F_R = 391.1), while maintaining competitive Chamfer Distance (CD 2.256 vs. 1.532 for MC) and Edge Chamfer Distance (ECD 6.245 vs. 6.733 for MC). In user studies, MeshAnything received 63.2% shape votes and 60.7% topology votes against other AM generators, and 44.6% shape / 83.6% topology votes against mesh extraction baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two AM generation baselines, MeshAnything dominates: it improves COV from 41.7 to 53.1, reduces MMD from 3.83 to 2.72, and cuts FID from 25.1 to 14.5 over MeshGPT, with even larger margins over PolyGen (FID 48.8→14.5). The only difference between the retrained MeshGPT and MeshAnything is the inclusion of shape conditioning, isolating the benefit of the Shape-Conditioned AM Generation setting. Against the three mesh extraction baselines (Marching Cubes, FlexiCubes, Shape as Points), MeshAnything trades a small amount of shape fidelity (CD 2.256 vs. 1.190–1.771; NC 0.902 vs. 0.939–0.967) for a dramatic reduction in face count: 0.318×10³ faces versus 121–152×10³, a ratio of roughly 390–490×. Even when remeshing is applied to the extraction baselines to reduce face counts to a comparable level, their vertex counts remain several times higher than MeshAnything's, confirming inferior topology efficiency. In user studies, MeshAnything received the majority of topology votes (83.6%) against extraction baselines, while its shape votes (44.6%) were competitive but not dominant, consistent with the small CD/NC gap observed quantitatively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MeshGPT's VQ-VAE + decoder-only transformer architecture for mesh generation, but introduces two key departures: (1) it formulates mesh extraction as a *generation* problem conditioned on a 3D shape (point cloud), rather than as a reconstruction problem (Marching Cubes, FlexiCubes) or an unconditional generation problem (PolyGen, MeshGPT); and (2) it adds a noise-resistant decoder that uses the shape condition to correct imperfect token sequences. The shape-conditioning strategy draws an explicit analogy to multimodal LLMs, where image features are projected into the token space of a language model, here projecting point cloud features into the mesh token space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical bottleneck in the 3D industry: automatically produced 3D assets (from NeRF, Gaussian Splatting, text-to-3D, etc.) cannot be directly used because their extracted meshes have chaotic, dense topologies unsuitable for games, film, or simulation. By generating artist-quality meshes with hundreds of times fewer faces, MeshAnything provides a plug-and-play bridge between any 3D representation and industry pipelines. Key limitations include the inherent slowness of autoregressive generation, a maximum face count that prevents scene-level application, and occasional generative failures that make it less robust than reconstruction-based methods. Open questions include whether more efficient (non-autoregressive) mesh generation can preserve the topology quality, and whether the approach extends to scene-level or highly complex objects.
