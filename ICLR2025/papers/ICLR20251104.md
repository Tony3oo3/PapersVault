## Problem

While recurrent neural networks trained on path integration tasks can produce grid-like firing patterns, the mathematical principle underlying the emergence of specifically hexagonal periodic patterns has remained unexplained. The conformal isometry hypothesis—that grid cell activities form a distance-preserving embedding of 2D physical space—had been proposed but only studied in complex multi-module settings coupled with place cells, obscuring its independent role. This paper isolates the hypothesis in a minimal single-module setting to test whether conformal isometry alone is sufficient to generate hexagonal patterns and to provide a rigorous theoretical explanation for why hexagons specifically emerge.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a 1m × 1m continuous 2D square environment discretized into a 40×40 lattice (with bilinear interpolation for off-lattice positions), taking as input a 2D position x and a displacement Δx parameterized by magnitude Δr and heading θ. It produces a d-dimensional position embedding v(x) constrained to the unit sphere (‖v(x)‖=1) with non-negative components, where each dimension corresponds to one grid cell's response map. The scaling factor s is explicitly specified (e.g., s=5, 10, 15) to control the metric unit, and the learned patterns' spatial scale is inversely proportional to s. In the multi-module extension, s becomes a learnable parameter per module and place cell readout weights w(x') are jointly learned.

## Method

The framework imposes four minimal assumptions—conformal isometry, RNN-based transformation, unit-norm normalization, and non-negativity—and trains via a two-term loss: L₁ penalizes deviation from ‖v(x+Δx)−v(x)‖ = s‖Δx‖ over a non-infinitesimal range (s‖Δx‖ ≤ 1.25), while L₂ enforces one-step transformation consistency v(x+Δx) = F(v(x), Δx) over infinitesimal steps (‖Δx‖ ≤ 0.075). The transformation F is deliberately agnostic, tested across linear models, and nonlinear models with ReLU, GELU, Tanh, Leaky ReLU, and Swish activations, all yielding hexagonal patterns. The theoretical core proves that the 4th-order Taylor deviation D(Δx) from local isometry is isotropic (D(Δx) = c‖Δx‖⁴) only for the hexagonal flat torus due to its 6-fold rotational symmetry, and that by Cauchy-Schwarz, isotropic D minimizes the integrated squared deviation ∫(D(Δx))²dθ among all flat tori with fixed average extrinsic curvature. This establishes the hexagon flat torus as the maximally distance-preserving position embedding, providing a first-principles explanation for why hexagonal grid patterns emerge from minimizing the conformal isometry loss.

## Results

The learned linear model achieves a gridness score of 1.70 with a 100% valid grid cell rate, while the nonlinear model reaches 1.17 gridness with 100% valid rate, substantially outperforming all prior learning-based approaches. The estimated spatial grid scale is inversely proportional to the scaling factor s (e.g., s=10 yields scale 0.41, s=5 yields 0.82). On real Neuropixels recordings (Gardner et al., 2021), a clear linear relationship between ‖v(x+Δx)−v(x)‖ and ‖Δx‖ is observed, with a fitted scaling factor s=13.4 and minimal deviation from linearity for local displacements, supporting the conformal isometry hypothesis in biological data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three prior learning-based grid cell models on gridness score and valid grid cell rate. The linear model (gridness 1.70, 100% valid) nearly doubles the gridness of Gao et al. (2021) (0.90, 73.1%) and more than triples that of Sorscher et al. (2023) (0.48, 56.1%), while achieving a perfect valid rate versus 73.1% and 56.1% respectively. Against Banino et al. (2018) (0.18, 25.2%), the improvement is over 9× in gridness and 4× in valid rate. The nonlinear model (1.17, 100%) also outperforms all three baselines on both metrics, confirming that the conformal isometry loss drives hexagonal pattern emergence regardless of the transformation model's form. Notably, the paper's minimalistic single-module setting (no place cells, no predefined tuning functions) achieves these gains, whereas prior methods required additional architectural assumptions or place cell interactions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the conformal isometry hypothesis formalized by Xu et al. (2022) and Gao et al. (2021), which itself grew from earlier explorations in Gao et al. (2019). The key departure is a deliberate reduction to a minimalistic single-module setting with an explicit scaling factor s, removing place cells and their interactions that were present in all prior conformal isometry models. Unlike earlier RNN-based grid cell models (Cueva & Wei 2018; Banino et al. 2018) that learned grid patterns as a byproduct of path integration training, this paper makes conformal isometry the explicit optimization objective and demonstrates that hexagonal patterns emerge regardless of the transformation model's form (linear, ReLU, GELU, Tanh, Swish).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that the hexagon flat torus is the maximally distance-preserving embedding among all flat tori provides the first clean first-principles mathematical explanation for why hexagons specifically emerge, a question that prior computational models addressed only empirically. The validation against real Neuropixels recordings (Gardner et al., 2021) strengthens the biological plausibility of the conformal isometry hypothesis. Open questions include whether the framework extends to 3D or non-spatial cognitive spaces, whether the explicit scaling factor s can be learned end-to-end in multi-module settings without the conformal modulation trick, and whether the distance-preserving property has measurable functional consequences for path planning in biological agents.
