## Problem

Two-tower recommendation systems learn pair-agnostic user and item embeddings that cannot capture fine-grained user-item context (e.g., repeat purchases, prior clicks), while pair-wise architectures like NBFNET capture this context but are restricted to items within a user's local subgraph, failing for exploratory and cold-start recommendations. The paper addresses how to unify both paradigms into a single architecture that adapts to the diversity of real-world recommendation tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a heterogeneous temporal graph with typed user and item nodes, multi-behavior edge types (purchase, click, tag-as-favorite, add-to-cart), optional node feature vectors, and timestamps, from which k-hop user-centric subgraph snapshots are sampled. It produces a single unified ranking of all items for each user, where items inside the sampled subgraph receive pair-wise scores and items outside receive two-tower scores, fused via a learned user-specific offset. Training uses a sampled softmax over up to ~1M candidate items per mini-batch, and inference leverages approximate maximum inner product search for the two-tower component.

## Method

CONTEXTGNN employs a single bidirectional GNN backbone that simultaneously produces pair-wise representations (for items within the user's k-hop subgraph, using an indicator vector on the seed node and reading out both user and item GNN embeddings) and a two-tower score (using the GNN user embedding dotted with a shallow item embedding matrix for all items outside the subgraph). The key architectural insight is that shallow item embeddings replace a full GNN on the item side, avoiding oversquashing on dense item neighborhoods and enabling training against ~10M negative samples. A user-specific MLP fusion score, predicted from the GNN user embedding, is added to the pair-wise scores to learn how much weight each user places on familiar versus exploratory items. Both components share the same GNN forward pass, so only one subgraph computation is needed per user, yielding O(|L|) complexity rather than the O(|L|·|R|) of two-tower models. The entire architecture is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss under a sampled softmax formulation.

## Results

On the RELBENCH benchmark (10 tasks across 4 datasets), CONTEXTGNN achieves an average MAP of 9.23%, improving by 20% over the best pair-wise baseline NBFNET (7.71%) and by 344% over the best two-tower baseline GraphSAGE (2.08%). The largest single-task gain is on site-sponsor-run (rel-trial), where CONTEXTGNN reaches 28.02% MAP versus 19.06% for NBFNET and 10.70% for GraphSAGE. On the IJCAI Contest sequential recommendation task, CONTEXTGNN achieves HR@1 of 0.411, a 170% improvement over the best baseline TGT (0.148). On the smaller static Amazon-Book dataset, CONTEXTGNN (Recall@20 = 0.0451) outperforms NGCF and LIGHTGCN but is slightly below ULTRAGCN (0.0681) and LIGHTGCL (0.0585).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONTEXTGNN outperforms all 17 baselines on the primary RELBENCH benchmark. Against the strongest pair-wise baseline NBFNet, CONTEXTGNN gains 20% on average, with the largest improvements (42–80%) on tasks with low locality scores (e.g., user-item-purchase on rel-amazon: 2.93 vs 2.06) and modest gains (3–5%) on high-locality tasks (e.g., user-post-comment on rel-stack: 13.34 vs 12.72). All two-tower GNN baselines (GraphSAGE, NGCF) and non-GNN baselines (LightGBM, MultiVAE) are substantially outperformed, with CONTEXTGNN achieving 344% higher average MAP than the best two-tower model. On the IJCAI Contest sequential task, CONTEXTGNN dominates all 8 baselines (DeepFM, BERT4Rec, TGT, etc.) by a wide margin, e.g., HR@10 of 0.667 vs TGT's 0.519. On the static Amazon-Book dataset, CONTEXTGNN beats NGCF (0.0451 vs 0.0337 Recall@20) and LIGHTGCN (0.0451 vs 0.0410) but trails ULTRAGCN (0.0681) and LIGHTGCL (0.0585), which benefit from self-supervised learning and simplified architectures not explored here. The paper's own ShallowItem two-tower component performs comparably to full GNN two-tower baselines, confirming that deep item-side GNNs add limited value on most tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

CONTEXTGNN extends the pair-wise representation framework of NBFNET (Zhu et al., 2021) to heterogeneous, multi-behavioral, and temporal graphs, while simultaneously introducing a novel two-tower fallback that uses shallow item embeddings rather than GNN-computed item representations. It departs from the dominant two-tower GNN paradigm (GraphSAGE, NGCF, LightGCN, UltraGCN) by arguing that pair-agnostic representations fundamentally cannot capture fine-grained user-item dependencies, and from pure pair-wise methods by showing they lack coverage for exploratory items. The architecture is positioned within the relational deep learning framework (Fey et al., 2024), treating relational databases as heterogeneous temporal graphs, and introduces a user-specific fusion score via an MLP that learns how to weight local versus global recommendations per user.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled diagnosis (via the locality score) of why no single representation paradigm suffices for real-world recommendation: the majority of ground-truth items are exploratory (locality < 0.5 on all datasets), yet pair-wise methods like NBFNET cannot rank them, while two-tower methods miss fine-grained local signals. The finding that shallow item embeddings match or exceed GNN item representations in the two-tower component is a practical insight that simplifies deployment and enables training against ~10M negatives. Limitations include the transductive item-side design (new items require replacing the embedding matrix with a feature-based network) and mixed results on small-scale static benchmarks where self-supervised GNN variants still lead. Open questions include whether the fusion mechanism generalizes to multi-objective or multi-task settings, and whether the architecture benefits from the self-supervised learning techniques that have driven recent progress on smaller datasets.
