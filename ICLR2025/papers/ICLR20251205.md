## Problem

Current vision tokenizers (VAEs, VQGANs, ViTs) assign a fixed number of tokens to every image, tying representational capacity to a human-chosen patch size and preventing adaptive compression. This contrasts with how language models and human cognition allocate variable representational resources based on content complexity, familiarity, and task demands. The paper asks whether a single tokenizer can learn to assign a different number of tokens to each image, with the count reflecting the image's information entropy and the downstream task's requirements.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB image, first converts it to a 16×16 grid of 256 discrete VQGAN tokens via a pre-trained VQGAN encoder, then distills these into a variable number of 1D latent tokens (32 to 256, in increments of 32 per recurrent iteration). The output is a sequence of quantized 1D latent tokens (sampled from a shared 4096-entry codebook) that can be decoded back to the original image, with the token count chosen adaptively per image at test time based on reconstruction quality or task-specific criteria.

## Method

ALIT uses a Perceiver-style encoder-decoder that performs joint self-attention between 2D image tokens and 1D latent tokens, distilling 2D→1D in the encoder and 1D→2D (reconstruction) in the decoder. The key mechanism is a recurrent auto-regressive rollout: at each iteration, the previous latent tokens receive residual updates while new learnable latent tokens are appended as additional memory, and optionally, well-reconstructed 2D tokens are masked out (dynamic halting) so the encoder focuses on under-distilled regions. All iterations share the same encoder-decoder weights and a single factorized 1D codebook, so tokens learned at different iterations live in the same embedding space. Training is multi-stage and self-supervised: first reconstructing discrete VQGAN tokens via cross-entropy, then jointly fine-tuning with pixel-level reconstruction and GAN adversarial losses. The recurrent processing with growing token count induces emergent token specialization, where individual latent tokens attend to localized objects or parts rather than uniform image regions.

## Results

On ImageNet-100, ALIT-SemiLarge achieves 8.03 FID at 256 tokens (vs. VQGAN's 7.04) and 8.25 FID for ALIT-S, while matching Titok-S-128's 8.22 FID at 128 tokens. For L1 reconstruction loss, ALIT-S at 256 tokens achieves 7.62 (×100), outperforming VQGAN's 7.99 and Titok-S-128's 9.72 at 128 tokens. On ImageNet-1K linear probing, ALIT-SemiLarge reaches 59.5% Top-1 accuracy (vs. Titok-L-32's 60.0%), and recurrent processing improves ALIT-L from 55.6% (single iteration) to 59.5% (2–3 iterations). The key advantage is flexibility: a single model produces variable-length representations (32–256 tokens) with a much smaller 8-layer network, whereas Titok-L uses 24 layers for a fixed 32-token output.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VQGAN (2D fixed-length), ALIT-SemiLarge at 256 tokens achieves 8.03 FID vs. VQGAN's 7.04, a ~1 FID gap that the paper attributes to VQGAN's larger architecture and dedicated GAN training; however, ALIT-S wins on L1 loss (7.62 vs. 7.99 at 256 tokens) and uniquely supports 32–256 token flexibility. Against Titok (1D fixed-length), ALIT-S at 32 tokens trails Titok-L-32 significantly on FID (22.57 vs. 11.60) but wins on L1 (11.77 vs. 12.29); at 128 tokens ALIT-S achieves 8.77 L1 vs. Titok-S-128's 9.72, and at 256 tokens the FID gap narrows to 8.25 vs. 8.22 (Titok-S-128), showing near-parity with a much smaller network. Against Matryoshka-style baselines, ALIT-SemiLarge at 256 tokens achieves 8.03 FID vs. 8.11 for the matched Matryoshka Enc-SemiLarge, and on OOD COCO ALIT-S (9.72) outperforms Matryoshka-L (10.66) at 256 tokens. Against FlexVIT-style baselines, FlexVIT Enc-SemiLarge with ALIT decoding achieves 7.80 FID at 256 tokens, slightly ahead of ALIT-S (8.25), though ALIT-SemiLarge closes the gap to 8.03. Overall, ALIT trades a small FID deficit (especially at low token counts) for the ability to adaptively allocate 32–256 tokens per image, with the gap expected to shrink with larger models, longer training, and GAN fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALIT sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) the Perceiver/Titok line of 2D-to-1D latent token distillation, which breaks the patch-to-token binding of VQGAN and ViT, and (2) the recurrent-computation tradition from Adaptive Computation Time (Graves, 2016) and Universal Transformers (Dehghani et al., 2018), where the same network is rolled out multiple times to allocate more compute to harder inputs. The key departure from prior variable-length methods (Matryoshka, ElasticTok, AdaTape) is that ALIT learns representations *recursively*—each iteration refines existing latent tokens and appends new ones—rather than learning a single fixed-length embedding and then masking subsets. This recurrent, adaptive-memory formulation is what enables the model to go beyond the maximum token count learned in a single forward pass and to specialize tokens over iterations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides empirical evidence that a single tokenizer can produce representations whose optimal length tracks image entropy, training-set familiarity, and downstream task granularity—properties that fixed-length tokenizers cannot express. The emergent object/part binding of latent tokens (mIOU ~58–72 with GT segmentation, without any segmentation objective) suggests that recurrent distillation with growing memory is a viable route to unsupervised part discovery, complementing DINO-style self-distillation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video and streaming settings (where fixed-length representations are infeasible), whether the modest FID gap at low token counts closes with larger models and longer GAN training, and whether the recurrent overhead (~4 ms per iteration on H100) remains acceptable at scale. The reliance on a pre-trained VQGAN as the 2D base tokenizer also limits the method's generality.
