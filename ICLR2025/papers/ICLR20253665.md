## Problem

Existing Riemannian statistical alignment methods on the SPD manifold (e.g., RCT+TSM) are state-of-the-art for source-free unsupervised domain adaptation in EEG but have no theoretical guarantee under label shifts, which are prevalent in practical applications like sleep staging. The paper provides a generative model and theoretical analysis showing that aligning Fréchet means across domains compensates conditional shifts but actively degrades generalization when class priors differ between source and target domains.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled multi-channel EEG epochs (P spatial channels × T temporal samples) from target domains along with a frozen, pre-trained TSMNet source decoder (convolutional feature extractor → covariance pooling → SPD batch norm → linear classifier). For each target domain, only a single SPD-manifold-constrained bias parameter (a full D×D matrix in SPDIM(bias), or a scalar geodesic step-size in SPDIM(geodesic)) is optimized, keeping all other network weights fixed. The output is a discrete class label (e.g., 4 motor-imagery classes or 5 AASM sleep stages: W, N1, N2, N3, REM).

## Method

SPDIM introduces a domain-specific SPD bias parameter Φ_j that is inserted into the tangent-space mapping as a bilinear transformation, counteracting the over-correction introduced by per-domain Fréchet mean alignment when label priors differ. The key theoretical insight is that under a log-linear generative model, the label-shift component of the covariance decomposition factors as an SPD matrix that can be cancelled by a congruence transform with Φ_j. The bias parameter is learned unsupervised via an information maximization (IM) loss combining conditional entropy minimization (individual predictions should be confident) and marginal entropy maximization (predictions should be diverse across classes), with a temperature scaling factor to control prediction sharpness. Optimization uses Riemannian ADAM on the SPD manifold for 50 epochs, making the adaptation extremely parameter-efficient relative to fine-tuning the full network.

## Results

On EEG-based sleep staging (4 datasets, 426 subjects), TSMNet+SPDIM(bias) achieves 73.5% balanced accuracy on healthy subjects and 69.9% on patients, outperforming the closest baseline TSMNet+SPDDSBN (70.1% healthy, 64.9% patient) by ~3.4% and ~5.0% respectively. On motor imagery BCI with artificially introduced label shifts (ratio 0.2, 4 datasets, 34 subjects), SPDIM(bias) achieves 77.5% balanced accuracy for cross-session and 68.5% for cross-subject transfer, surpassing SPDDSBN (76.0% / 66.4%) and all IM-based baselines. In simulations, SPDIM consistently outperforms RCT across all label ratio and class separability configurations, confirming the theoretical prediction that RCT hurts generalization under label shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPDIM(bias) significantly outperforms all baselines on sleep staging for both patient and healthy groups (except TSMNet+STMA on healthy), with the largest margin (~5%) over TSMNet+SPDDSBN in the patient group. On motor imagery with label shifts, SPDIM(bias) significantly beats SPDDSBN, IM(classifier), and IM(all) in both cross-session and cross-subject settings; notably, IM(all) (adapting all parameters) collapses to 55.0% cross-session accuracy, far below the no-adaptation baseline (76.7%), demonstrating that unregularized IM overfits. RCT (Zanini et al.) is outperformed in simulations across all label ratios below 1.0, confirming the theoretical result that Fréchet-mean alignment hurts under label shift. Euclidean alignment (EA) and STMA provide modest gains over no-adaptation but remain well below SPDIM. The ablation further shows that fitting the Fréchet mean per domain without the IM bias (i.e., SPDDSBN) is worse than a global Fréchet mean under label shift, validating that the SPD-constrained bias parameter is the critical component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TSMNet framework (Kobler et al., 2022a) and the Riemannian recentering transform (Zanini et al., 2017) by providing the first theoretical characterization of what distribution shifts RCT+TSM can and cannot compensate on the SPD manifold. It departs from prior Riemannian alignment methods by showing they actively hurt generalization under label shifts, and introduces SPDIM—a minimal, single-parameter-per-domain manifold optimization using the information maximization principle (Shi & Sha, 2012) to correct the over-alignment. The geodesic-constrained variant (SPDIM-geodesic) connects to Mellot et al. (2024), who studied a related geodesic step-size for regression, here extended to classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art Riemannian alignment (RCT+TSM) degrades under label shifts is a useful negative result for the EEG/BCI community, as label shifts are pervasive in practical scenarios like sleep staging but were previously unaddressed in the Riemannian framework. SPDIM's parameter efficiency (one SPD matrix per target domain) makes it attractive for clinical deployment where calibration data is scarce. A key limitation is that the IM loss can estimate an inappropriate bias under high noise, increasing variability; the authors identify more robust bias estimation as an open problem. Broader implications include whether the log-linear generative model assumption holds for all EEG paradigms and whether the approach extends to more complex shift combinations (e.g., covariate + label shift simultaneously).
