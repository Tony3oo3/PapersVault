## Problem

Partial Wasserstein distances are more robust to noise and mass mismatches than standard OT, but exact solvers for the 1D partial problem had at best quadratic or cubic complexity, making them impractical for large-scale sliced applications. Additionally, no principled slicing scheme existed for partial OT that consistently avoids transporting mass between outliers across different projection directions.

## Input / Output

PAWL takes two sorted 1D empirical distributions with equal per-sample weights w and a maximum mass parameter s, and produces the complete sequence of optimal active sets {A_k} and their cumulative costs for all k from 0 to ⌈s/w⌉ in a single pass. For the sliced variant (Sliced-PW), the input is high-dimensional point clouds, and the output is a sparse transport map that identifies which samples are transported (in-distribution) versus left unmoved (out-of-distribution), with the optimal projection direction selected by minimizing the 1D partial cost. The key structural property is that all intermediate solutions are obtained at the cost of computing just one, enabling post-hoc mass selection via the elbow method without re-solving.

## Method

PAWL exploits the monotone expansion of active sets (Caffarelli & McCann, 2010) to build solutions inductively: at step k, exactly one new pair {x_i, y_j} is added to the active set. Three key ingredients achieve O(n log n) complexity for the Manhattan cost: (1) Proposition 2 restricts candidates to neighbor pairs in the complement of the active set, reducing the search from O((n-k)²) to O(n-k); (2) the paper introduces 'chains' (balanced contiguous subsets) and 'minimal chains,' showing that the marginal cost of any candidate can be computed in O(1) via precomputed cumulative sums (Propositions 3–5); (3) a priority queue maintains all candidates sorted by marginal cost, so each step pops the single best candidate and inserts at most one new candidate arising from the covering chain. For the sliced variant, Sliced-PW is formulated as a bi-level optimization over projection directions θ, selecting the single direction minimizing the 1D partial cost, which inherits SWGG's semi-metric property and yields a sparse transport map suitable for outlier identification.

## Results

PAWL achieves O(n log n) time complexity for computing all partial 1-Wasserstein distances simultaneously, exhibiting quasi-linear scaling up to 10^6 samples versus the quadratic trend of OPT (Bai et al., 2023). In point cloud registration, PAWL with a known mass prior achieves final error norms of 0.000–0.318 across four dataset configurations, outperforming SOPT (0.001–0.212) in 3 of 4 cases and SPOT (1.505–1.627) by over an order of magnitude. In domain adaptation, PAWL runs 200× faster than UOT and 14× faster than OT profiles while providing comparable or superior outlier detection accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OPT (Bai et al., 2023), PAWL is both faster (quasi-linear vs. quadratic scaling in Figure 4) and achieves lower registration error at convergence in 3 of 4 configurations (e.g., 0.000 vs. 0.001 on 10k,5%). Against SPOT (Bonneel & Coeurjolly, 2019), PAWL (elbow) dramatically outperforms at convergence (0.014 vs. 1.505 on 10k,5%), though it converges more slowly in early iterations due to the elbow method's initial estimation difficulty. Against Fast-UOT (Séjourné et al., 2022), PAWL is slightly faster in practice while solving the more general partial OT problem (fixed mass) rather than the KL-relaxed UOT problem. In domain adaptation, PAWL is 200× faster than the UOT regularization path of Chapel et al. (2021) and 14× faster than OT profiles (Phatak et al., 2023), while matching or exceeding their outlier detection accuracy. The Sliced-PW variant also outperforms standard Sliced Wasserstein in gradient flow experiments, producing consistent sparse transport maps where SW with PAWL fails to maintain a stable active set.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAWL builds directly on the monotone expansion of active sets established by Caffarelli & McCann (2010) for partial optimal transport, using it as the inductive backbone to construct solutions for all transported masses simultaneously. The slicing strategy is derived from the SWGG framework of Mahey et al. (2024), adapted to the partial setting where mass conservation is relaxed. The paper departs from prior 1D partial OT solvers (Bai et al. 2023 with O(n²) worst-case, Séjourné et al. 2022 for UOT but not fixed-mass partial OT) by achieving O(n log n) for the Manhattan cost, and from existing sliced partial OT schemes by introducing a projection-selection strategy that prevents transporting mass between outliers across directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(n log n) complexity and the 'all masses at once' property make partial Wasserstein distances practical for large-scale datasets and enable data-driven selection of the transported mass via the elbow method, removing the need for a priori knowledge of noise levels. The tailored slicing strategy addresses a known failure mode of naively plugging 1D partial OT solvers into multi-directional averaging, where different directions select different sample subsets. Key limitations include the restriction to Manhattan distance for the best complexity (squared Euclidean is O(Cn²)), the requirement of disjointly supported distributions, and the non-differentiability of the sorting-based algorithm. Open questions include differentiable variants for gradient-based optimization, extension to non-constant masses and continuous distributions, and statistical guarantees on the selected sample subsets.
