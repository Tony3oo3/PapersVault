## Problem

Audio classification lacks a large-scale, easily accessible benchmark dataset with diverse evaluation scenarios comparable to what ImageNet or AudioSet provide for vision and general audio. While AudioSet offers substantial training data, its restricted accessibility, single small test subset, and limited transferability to environmental domains leave a gap for domain-specific benchmarking. In avian bioacoustics specifically, the absence of a standardized, curated dataset hinders comparability across studies and creates barriers for the broader audio community.

## Input / Output

Training inputs are variable-length focal recordings from Xeno-Canto (32 kHz resolution) with detected vocalization events and clusters, one-hot encoded to align with multi-label test labels. Test inputs are 5-second soundscape segments from eight geographically diverse PAM sites, where a species label is assigned if its vocalization spans more than 0.5 seconds within the segment. Outputs are per-segment multi-label species probability vectors across up to ~10,000 classes, evaluated with threshold-independent metrics (cmAP, AUROC, T1-Acc) rather than class-dependent F1 thresholds.

## Method

BirdSet curates ~528,000 focal recordings across nearly 10,000 species (XCL) for large-scale training, a 409-species subset (XCM) for medium training, and eight strongly-labeled soundscape test datasets totaling 400+ hours for evaluation. The benchmark trains and evaluates six architectures (EfficientNet, ConvNext, AST, EAT, Wav2Vec 2.0, and the SOTA Perch model) under three supervised scenarios: large training (LT), medium training (MT), and dedicated fine-tuning (DT) on per-test-set subsets. Augmentations including time-shifting, background noise from BirdVox, multi-label mixup, and no-call segments are applied to bridge the focal-to-soundscape distribution gap. The key empirical finding is that large-scale representation learning (LT/MT) consistently outperforms dedicated fine-tuning, with ConvNext achieving the best AUROC across datasets, while the benchmark exposes substantial performance drops under covariate shift, task shift (multi-class to multi-label), and label uncertainty.

## Results

In the large training (LT) scenario on XCL (~10,000 species, 6,800+ hours), ConvNext achieves the best overall cmAP (0.36) and AUROC (0.85), while Perch (the SOTA retrieval model) leads on T1-Acc (0.61). Large-scale training consistently outperforms dedicated fine-tuning (DT): for example, EfficientNet's cmAP rises from 0.35 (DT) to 0.36 (MT) and AUROC from 0.84 to 0.85 (MT). Waveform-based transformers (EAT, W2V2) trail spectrogram-based models by 3–6 points in AUROC, suggesting difficulty handling input noise in complex soundscapes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConvNext (Liu et al., CVPR 2022) is the strongest spectrogram-based model in the LT scenario, achieving the highest overall cmAP (0.36) and AUROC (0.85), edging out EfficientNet (0.32/0.84) and AST (0.34/0.84). Perch (Hamer et al., 2023), the current SOTA bioacoustic model built on EfficientNet, matches ConvNext on cmAP (0.36) but substantially outperforms all other models on T1-Acc (0.61 vs. 0.53 for EfficientNet), confirming its strength in retrieval-style identification. AST (Gong et al., 2021), despite being a Vision Transformer pre-trained on AudioSet, underperforms ConvNext by 2 cmAP points in LT and drops to 0.29 in DT, indicating limited transferability to the avian domain without domain-specific pre-training. Waveform transformers EAT (Gazneli et al., 2022) and W2V2 (Baevski et al., 2020) trail all spectrogram-based models by 3–6 AUROC points, with W2V2 the weakest overall (0.78 AUROC, 0.26 cmAP in DT), suggesting raw-waveform architectures struggle with the noise and overlap in PAM soundscapes. Across all models, large-scale training (LT/MT) consistently beats dedicated fine-tuning (DT) by 1–4 cmAP points, reinforcing the value of broad representation learning over narrow task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying the fragmented landscape of avian bioacoustics datasets into a single, publicly available benchmark. Prior work relied on proprietary training data (BirdNET, Perch) or ad-hoc Xeno-Canto subsets, while evaluation was scattered across individual BirdCLEF competition datasets with inconsistent protocols. BirdSet departs from AudioSet's universal-domain approach by providing a domain-specific resource with ~10,000 classes and eight geographically diverse, strongly labeled soundscape test sets, while also drawing on the multi-label classification and covariate-shift evaluation frameworks established in general audio classification (AudioSet, FSD50K). The benchmark extends the typical focal-to-soundscape training pipeline by standardizing augmentations (label-mixup, background noise, time-shifting) and introducing threshold-independent metrics (cmAP, AUROC, T1-Acc) across all test sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BirdSet addresses a critical accessibility gap: for the first time, the scale of training data comparable to SOTA models (BirdNET, Perch) is fully public, enabling reproducible research and fair comparison across studies that was previously impossible. The benchmark reveals that even the best models achieve only moderate performance (T1-Acc ~0.5–0.6, cmAP ~0.3–0.4), underscoring that multi-label bird sound classification under covariate shift remains far from solved. Open questions include whether self-supervised pre-training on BirdSet's 6,800+ hours can close the gap, how models handle extreme class imbalance (J as low as 0.54), and whether the findings generalize beyond the eight test regions. The paper's limitation of not analyzing which specific dataset characteristics drive performance differences leaves room for targeted robustness research.
