## Problem

Self-improvement methods for LLM reasoning (STaR, RFT, ReST-EM) saturate after only 3–5 iterations, yet the underlying mechanism driving this stagnation remains opaque. The paper identifies that two critical factors—exploration (the model's ability to generate diverse correct responses) and exploitation (the reward function's ability to discriminate quality)—dynamically deteriorate over iterations, but prior work treats their controlling configurations (sampling temperature, reward threshold) as static, leaving no principled way to diagnose or correct the imbalance.

## Input / Output

B-STAR consumes a set of training queries, an initial SFT policy model checkpoint, and a fixed reward model (a process reward model combined with final-answer matching for math, or binary unit-test/ground-truth rewards for coding and commonsense tasks). At each iteration it outputs an updated policy model along with a dynamically chosen sampling temperature and reward threshold. The balance score is computed on a small fixed subset (e.g., 600 MATH queries) to select configurations, so the additional computational cost over standard online RFT is negligible; the full training set is then generated and filtered using the chosen configurations.

## Method

B-STAR introduces a balance score metric, bs_i = min(n_i′/n*, 1) · (n_i′/n_i), that jointly captures the absolute number of unique correct responses (exploration) and their proportion among selected candidates (exploitation) for each query. At every iteration, the algorithm searches over a grid of temperatures and reward thresholds on a small query subset to find the pair that maximizes the average balance score, then applies those configurations to generate and filter the full training batch before performing a rejection fine-tuning update. The key empirical finding is that optimal configurations are non-stationary: temperature should rise from ~0.5 early in training to ~1.1 later (shifting from accuracy-focused to diversity-focused sampling), while the reward threshold should relax from 0 to −0.1 (from strict to permissive filtering). This dynamic rebalancing, built on the online RFT framework with inherited optimizer state, prevents the exploration collapse and reward-discrimination degradation that cause prior methods to plateau.

## Results

B-STAR achieves the highest Pass@1 across all four benchmarks: 53.8% on GSM8K, 27.8% on MATH, 19.6% on APPS, and 73.0% on ARC-Challenge. On exploration metrics, B-STAR also leads with Pass@32 of 93.6% (GSM8K) and 67.2% (MATH), and Pass@32-4 of 81.0% (GSM8K) and 42.2% (MATH), indicating sustained diversity in correct solutions. The method maintains a steady upward trajectory over 9 iterations (4500 steps), whereas baselines saturate after 3–5 iterations. On the stronger Llama-3.1-8B model, B-STAR further improves to 61.6% (GSM8K) and 29.2% (MATH) Pass@1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

B-STAR consistently outperforms all three baseline families across every benchmark. Against ReST-EM (Singh et al., 2023), B-STAR gains +7.5 points on GSM8K Pass@1 (53.8 vs. 46.3 with RM) and +3.6 on MATH Pass@1 (27.8 vs. 24.2 with RM). Against Iterative RFT (Yuan et al., 2023), the margins are +7.2 on GSM8K (53.8 vs. 46.6) and +3.4 on MATH (27.8 vs. 24.4). Against the strongest baseline, Online RFT (Shao et al., 2024), B-STAR leads by +7.0 on GSM8K (53.8 vs. 46.8), +4.6 on MATH (27.8 vs. 23.2), +2.3 on APPS (19.6 vs. 17.3), and +1.8 on ARC-Challenge (73.0 vs. 71.2). Notably, B-STAR also improves exploration metrics (Pass@32, Pass@32-4) by 2–5 points over the best baselines, confirming that the gains are not merely from exploiting known solutions but from genuinely expanding the model's solution space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-improvement lineage (STaR, ReST-EM, RFT, Online RFT) by reframing the iterative training loop through the lens of RL exploration-exploitation dynamics. While prior work (Singh et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2024) observed that self-improvement saturates after 3–5 iterations, none systematically quantified *why* by tracking exploration (Pass@K-S, diversity) and exploitation (Reward@K-S) as moving targets. B-STAR builds directly on the online RFT framework from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024) but departs from it by treating sampling temperature and reward threshold as dynamically adjustable hyperparameters guided by a novel balance score metric, rather than fixed throughout training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first interpretable diagnostic framework for understanding *why* self-improvement stagnates, showing that exploratory diversity degrades rapidly and that optimal exploitation settings shift over training—insights that were previously opaque. The practical impact is a simple, low-overhead method (balance score computed on 600 queries) that yields consistent gains across math, code, and commonsense reasoning without architectural changes. Key limitations include reliance on verifiable rewards (binary answer matching or PRM), testing only on 7B–8B models, and the fact that the reward model itself remains frozen, capping exploitation gains. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-verifiable or open-ended reasoning tasks, whether co-adapting the reward model could further improve exploitation, and whether the balance score generalizes beyond the specific hyperparameters (temperature, threshold) used here.
