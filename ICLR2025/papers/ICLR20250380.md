## Problem

Existing MLLM safety benchmarks evaluate scenarios where the language query is inherently unsafe and images serve as attack vectors or irrelevant backgrounds. This paper identifies an unexplored safety dimension—Multimodal Situational Safety—where a benign query (e.g., 'how to practice running') becomes dangerous depending on the visual context (e.g., near a cliff edge), and no prior work systematically evaluates whether MLLMs can condition their safety judgment on the surrounding visual situation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250380/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a natural-language query (a user question in chat scenarios or a household task instruction in embodied scenarios) with a single first-person or egocentric RGB image depicting the real-time visual context. The model must produce a safety judgment (safe/unsafe) and a corresponding response: either a normal answer, a safety warning with risk explanation, or (for embodied tasks) an action plan or refusal. The benchmark contains 1,960 balanced pairs—1,200 chat and 760 embodied—split evenly between safe and unsafe situations across four safety domains (physical harm, property damage, illegal activities, offensive activities).

## Method

The paper introduces MSSBench, constructed via a four-step chat pipeline (LLM-generated activity-situation pairs, automated GPT-4o filtering, Bing image retrieval for unsafe contexts, and human-verified query generation) and an embodied pipeline using ALFRED simulator episodes and DALL-E 3 for 38 safe and 38 unsafe household tasks. For evaluation, the authors design five diagnostic settings—instruction following, query safety classification, intent safety classification, and intent classification augmented with self-generated or ground-truth captions—to isolate the contributions of explicit safety reasoning, visual understanding, and situational judgment. To improve safety performance, they propose a multi-agent pipeline that decomposes the task into specialized subtasks (intent reasoning, visual captioning, safety judgment, and response generation for chat; key-object localization and safety reasoning for embodied), assigning each to a separate MLLM agent so that no single model must simultaneously perform all reasoning steps.

## Results

On MSSBench (1,960 image-query pairs), the best-performing model, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, achieves only 64.0% average safety accuracy under the instruction-following setting, with GPT-4o at 58.2% and Gemini 1.5 at 60.5%. All open-source MLLMs (LLaVA-1.6, MiniGPT-V2, Qwen-VL, mPLUG-Owl2, DeepSeek) perform near random (48.8–52.4% average), with unsafe-situation accuracy below 8% for most. Performance drops further in embodied scenarios, where even Claude scores only 55.9% on unsafe situations. The proposed multi-agent pipeline improves average safety accuracy for nearly all models, with GPT-4o reaching 76.5% and Claude 74.3% under instruction following.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 8 MLLMs (5 open-source, 3 proprietary) on MSSBench. Proprietary models substantially outperform open-source models on unsafe-situation detection: Claude 3.5 Sonnet identifies 43.5% of unsafe chat situations versus 1.7–7.8% for open-source models, and 13.4% on embodied unsafe situations versus 0.5–2.4%. Among proprietary models, Claude leads overall (64.0% avg), followed by Gemini (60.5%) and GPT-4o (58.2%), with the gap driven primarily by unsafe-situation recall. All models perform worse on embodied tasks than chat tasks, with unsafe-embodied accuracy dropping to 0.5–13.4% across the board. The multi-agent pipeline (the paper's proposed method) improves over the single-step baseline for nearly all models: GPT-4o jumps from 58.2% to 76.5% and Claude from 64.0% to 74.3% on instruction following, while open-source models like LLaVA-1.6 improve from 50.4% to 65.9%, closing the gap with proprietary models in chat scenarios but remaining weak on embodied tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant MLLM safety paradigm, where prior work (MM-SafetyBench, FigStep, JailbreakV-28K) treats the language query as inherently unsafe and the image as an attack vector or irrelevant background. It also distinguishes itself from concurrent efforts on over-sensitivity (MOSSBench) and cross-modality safety alignment, which study safe+safe→unsafe combinations. Here, the query is explicitly benign and the safety judgment is entirely conditioned on the visual context, a formulation the authors argue is absent from prior literature. For embodied scenarios, the paper repurposes the ALFRED simulator environment and DALL-E 3 to construct safe/unsafe household task situations, bridging embodied AI benchmarks with safety evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark and practically critical gap: models that score ~98% on safe situations collapse to 2–43% on unsafe ones, meaning current MLLMs deployed as multimodal assistants will silently fail to warn users about context-dependent dangers. The finding that even the strongest proprietary model (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) reaches only 64% average accuracy, and that embodied scenarios are substantially harder than chat, highlights that safety alignment training has not addressed situational reasoning. Open questions include whether safety alignment can be extended to multimodal situational awareness without the latency cost of multi-agent pipelines, and whether visual grounding tools can close the embodied gap.
