## Problem

Offline cooperative multi-agent RL suffers from severe distribution shift because the exponentially large joint state-action space makes offline data sparse, leading to many out-of-distribution state-action pairs and extrapolation errors. While DICE-based methods address this in single-agent settings by regularizing in stationary distribution space, extending them to multi-agent settings under CTDE is non-trivial: the f-divergence objective couples all agents' policies, and naive value decomposition breaks optimality consistency between global and local policies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of joint observations and actions generated by a behavior policy, where each agent only observes its local observation (POMDP setting). It outputs a set of decomposable local policies (one per agent) for decentralized execution, derived via weighted behavioral cloning using the learned occupancy ratio. The occupancy ratio w^{tot*}(s,a) is computed in closed form from the trained value and Q-functions, and the local policies are parameterized as separate policy networks conditioned on local observations.

## Method

ComaDICE formulates offline cooperative MARL as a DICE objective with f-divergence regularization in stationary distribution space, then decomposes both the global value function ν^{tot} and the global advantage function A_ν^{tot} (rather than Q-functions as in prior MARL work) via a mixing network with non-negative weights. A key theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) proves the learning objective is convex in local values under this architecture, ensuring stable training. The algorithm proceeds in two stages: (1) jointly train local value/Q-functions and the mixing network to minimize the DICE loss plus an MSE loss aligning global Q with rewards, yielding the occupancy ratio in closed form; (2) train each local policy via weighted behavioral cloning using the computed occupancy ratio as weights. Proposition 4.3 guarantees that the product of these locally-trained policies is optimal for the global weighted BC objective, ensuring CTDE consistency.

## Results

On MaMujoco, ComaDICE achieves dramatically higher returns than all baselines: 2827.7±62.9 on Hopper-expert (vs. 859.6±709.5 for OMIGA, the next best), 2056.9±5.9 on Ant-expert, and 4082.9±45.7 on HalfCheetah-expert. On SMACv1, ComaDICE achieves the highest win rate on all four tasks with good-quality data (e.g., 55.0% on 2c_vs_64zg vs. 42.2% for AlberDICE; 48.8% on corridor vs. 43.1% for AlberDICE). On SMACv2, ComaDICE leads on most maps, reaching 50.6% win rate on Protoss 10_vs_10 (vs. 40.0% for OMIGA) and 31.2% on Zerg 5_vs_5 (vs. 21.9% for OMIGA). The ablation shows optimal performance at α≈10, confirming the stationary distribution regularizer is essential.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ComaDICE outperforms all eight baselines (BC, BCQ, CQL, ICQ, OMAR, OMIGA, OptDICE, AlberDICE) on the vast majority of tasks across all three benchmarks. The closest competitor is OMIGA, which is the prior SOTA in offline MARL; ComaDICE surpasses it by a wide margin on MaMujoco (e.g., 2827.7 vs. 859.6 on Hopper-expert, a 3.3× improvement) and by 5–10 percentage points on SMACv2 (e.g., 50.6% vs. 40.0% on Protoss 10_vs_10). Among DICE-family methods, ComaDICE clearly beats both OptDICE (naive multi-agent extension) and AlberDICE (alternating per-agent DICE), with the gap most pronounced on MaMujoco where OptDICE and AlberDICE show high variance or negative returns on some tasks. On SMACv1, ComaDICE leads on all four tasks with good-quality data, with the largest margin on 2c_vs_64zg (55.0% vs. 42.2% for AlberDICE). The only near-tie is Ant-expert where ComaDICE (2056.9±5.9) marginally edges OMIGA (2055.5±1.6), and Terran 5_vs_5 on SMACv2 where ComaDICE (30.6±8.2) is statistically indistinguishable from BC (30.0±4.2). Overall, the stationary distribution regularization combined with the value decomposition strategy yields consistent and often large improvements over Q-function-based regularization (CQL, OMIGA) and per-agent DICE (AlberDICE).

## Relation to Prior Work

ComaDICE extends the DICE (Distribution Correction Estimation) framework from single-agent offline RL (Nachum & Dai, 2020; Lee et al., 2021) to the cooperative multi-agent setting by combining it with the CTDE paradigm and value decomposition via mixing networks. Unlike prior offline MARL methods (OMIGA, OMAR, ICQ) that regularize in Q-function or policy space, and unlike AlberDICE which learns per-agent Lagrange multipliers, ComaDICE learns a single global value function decomposed through a shared mixing network, preserving the CTDE principle. The key theoretical departure is proving convexity of the DICE objective in local values for general multi-layer mixing networks with non-negative weights and convex activations (Theorem 4.2), going beyond the 1-layer or 2-layer analyses in prior MARL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Offline MARL remains under-explored relative to single-agent offline RL, and the exponential joint action space makes distribution shift particularly severe; ComaDICE provides a principled DICE-based solution with provable global-local policy consistency via weighted behavioral cloning. The convexity guarantee (Theorem 4.2) is a broadly useful theoretical result for value decomposition in MARL beyond this specific algorithm. Key limitations include restriction to cooperative settings, heavy dependence on behavior policy quality, and continued need for large datasets; extending to cooperative-competitive settings and improving sample efficiency are natural next steps.
