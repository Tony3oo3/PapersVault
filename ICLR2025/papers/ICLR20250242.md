## Problem

Long-context alignment of LLMs is hindered by two intertwined challenges: high-quality long-context annotated data is impractical to obtain at scale (human annotation degrades in reliability as context grows, and synthetic generation is resource-intensive), and existing alignment methods (SFT, DPO) cause substantial short-context performance degradation (10–20 points) when extended to longer contexts. The paper asks whether a short-context LLM can self-evolve to long-context capability by internally transferring its own short-context preferences, without any external long-context annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250242/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only a well-aligned short-context LLM (e.g., Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 at 32K) and a plain long-context corpus (documents of 64K–512K tokens from Book, ArXiv, and GitHub subsets). For each long document, up to 4 chunks of ≤32K tokens are sampled, and instructions are generated via Self-Instruct conditioned on each chunk. The model then produces two responses per instruction—one conditioned on the short chunk (chosen) and one on the full long document (rejected)—forming quadruples (x_S, x_L, y_S, y_L). The output is a long-context LLM (up to 512K tokens) that produces text responses to instructions over long documents while retaining its original short-context benchmark scores.

## Method

LongPO constructs self-generated short-to-long preference data by prompting the short-context model with the same instruction over both a relevant short chunk and the full long document, designating the short-context response as chosen and the long-context response as rejected. The training objective is a DPO-style loss (Eq. 12) that optimizes the policy model toward this preference, but with a critical modification: the reference distribution in the log-ratio is π_S(y | x_S) (the short-context model conditioned on the short input) rather than π_ref(y | x_L), yielding a short-to-long KL constraint that anchors the long-context policy to the short-context model's behavior. This constraint is derived by showing that an ideal reference model would produce identical distributions on x_L and x_S, so the KL term becomes D_KL[π_θ(y|x_L) ‖ π_S(y|x_S)]. The process is iterative: the model trained at length L¹ becomes the new short-context model for generating preference data at length L², progressively extending context from 128K to 512K. A multi-turn objective aggregates probabilities across multiple chunks within the same document, and an NLL loss over the full long chosen sequence stabilizes training without requiring separate continual pretraining.

## Results

Mistral-7B-LongPO-128K achieves 39.27 on ∞Bench (AVG), surpassing GPT-4-128K (34.81) and LLaMA-3.1-8B (38.87), while scoring 86.06 on RULER and 5.42 on LongBench-Chat. Iterative self-evolution to 512K context further improves ∞Bench to 41.21 and RULER to 86.56. On short-context tasks, LongPO fully retains base-model performance (MMLU 59.99 vs. 59.15 for Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2), whereas SFT and DPO baselines degrade by 10–20 points. LongPO outperforms naive SFT and DPO by over 10 points on ∞Bench (39.27 vs. 30.03 and 25.56 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4-128K, Mistral-7B-LongPO-128K wins on ∞Bench (39.27 vs. 34.81) but trails on RULER (86.06 vs. 88.53) and LongBench-Chat (5.42 vs. 8.40), indicating GPT-4 retains an edge in instruction-following over long contexts. Against LLaMA-3.1-8B, LongPO-128K edges out on ∞Bench (39.27 vs. 38.87) and RULER (86.06 vs. 84.68) but loses on LongBench-Chat (5.42 vs. 6.22). Against GLM-4-9B (trained on manually annotated long-context data), LongPO-128K wins decisively on ∞Bench (39.27 vs. 30.53) and roughly ties on RULER (86.06 vs. 86.20). Against GLM-4-9B-1M, LongPO-128K wins on ∞Bench (39.27 vs. 35.53) and LongBench-Chat (5.42 vs. 5.03) but loses on RULER (86.06 vs. 89.00); notably, GLM-4-9B-1M suffers severe short-context degradation (MMLU drops by 0.41). Against LWM-7B-1M and YaRN-Mistral-7B, LongPO-128K wins by overwhelming margins on all long-context benchmarks (e.g., ∞Bench 39.27 vs. 2.46 and 15.53). Against the SFT and DPO baselines trained on identical self-generated data, LongPO-128K leads by 9.24 and 13.71 points on ∞Bench respectively, while fully preserving short-context performance (MMLU 59.99) where SFT and DPO degrade substantially.

## Relation to Prior Work

LongPO directly extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by replacing the standard reference model in the KL constraint with the short-context model conditioned on a shortened input, yielding a 'short-to-long' KL divergence that preserves short-context behavior during long-context training. It also adapts the RLHF objective (Ouyang et al., 2022) to this cross-length setting. Conceptually, LongPO departs from prior long-context alignment approaches—such as LLaMA-3.1's 0.1% long-data mixing, GLM-4's human-annotated long-context SFT/DPO, and LWM's massive continual pretraining—by eliminating external annotation entirely and instead leveraging the model's own short-context alignment as the preference signal. It also extends the self-evolving LLM paradigm (Yuan et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024) from capability improvement to context-length extension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 7B model can match or exceed GPT-4-128K on long-context benchmarks (39.27 vs. 34.81 on ∞Bench) challenges the assumption that long-context performance scales primarily with model size and training data volume, suggesting that alignment quality is the true bottleneck. The method's practical appeal is strong: it requires no human annotation, no reward model, and only self-generated data, making long-context extension accessible to smaller labs. Open questions include whether the iterative self-evolution process (128K→256K→512K) generalizes beyond 512K, whether the approach transfers to multimodal or non-English settings, and whether the short-to-long constraint remains effective when the base model has weaker short-context alignment. The reliance on a hypothetical 'ideal extractor' for the short context (approximated by Self-Instruct) also leaves room for improvement in data quality.
