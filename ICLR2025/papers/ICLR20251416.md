## Problem

Existing learning-augmented k-means algorithms (Ergun et al., 2021; Nguyen et al., 2022) rely on sorting strategies to approximate optimal clustering centers, incurring O(m log m) running time that limits scalability for large-scale datasets. Since comparison-based sorting has an O(m log m) lower bound, achieving faster algorithms requires fundamentally different strategies for identifying high-quality coordinate approximations without sorting.

## Input / Output

The algorithms take a d-dimensional dataset P of m points, a target number of clusters k, and a predicted labeling partition {P₁,...,Pₖ} where each predicted cluster has at most an α fraction of false positives and false negatives relative to the optimal partition. In practice, α is unknown, so each algorithm iterates over 15 uniformly spaced guesses in [0.01, 0.5] and returns the clustering with the best cost. Fast-Estimation additionally assumes a bounded aspect ratio Δ_max (ratio of max to min pairwise distances) in each dimension, and Fast-Filtering operates on full d-dimensional points rather than per-dimension coordinates.

## Method

The paper proposes three sampling-based algorithms that replace sorting with random sampling to approximate optimal centers. Fast-Sampling randomly samples O(log(kd)) coordinates per dimension per cluster, uses them to estimate interval lengths enclosing the optimal center coordinate, then subdivides intervals into fine-grained blocks to construct candidate coordinates, achieving O(ε⁻¹md log(kd)) time. Fast-Estimation eliminates the log(kd) factor by constructing weighted random-sample estimators that approximate clustering costs in sublinear time, guided by a geometric progression of interval length guesses, yielding O(md) + Õ(ε⁻⁵kd/α) time. Fast-Filtering is a practical heuristic that directly samples candidate d-dimensional centers (rather than per-dimension coordinates), uses estimators to select the best candidate, and filters out the furthest (1+ε)α|Sᵢ| points before computing the geometric center, achieving a (1+O(√α))-approximation with at least 3× speedup over prior methods on large datasets.

## Results

On the SIFT dataset (100M × 128, k=20), Fast-Filtering achieves the best clustering cost among all learning-augmented methods across all error rates (e.g., 9.7150E+12 at α=0.1 vs. 9.7791E+12 for Det and 9.9799E+12 for Ergun), while running in ~1006s compared to 13,000–17,000s for other learning-augmented baselines — a >10× speedup. Fast-Sampling matches or slightly beats Det on clustering cost (9.7666E+12 vs. 9.7791E+12 at α=0.1) with comparable NMI/ARI (0.9519/0.9531 vs. 0.9490/0.9491). Fast-Estimation is ~1.5× faster than Det (8875s vs. 13153s at α=0.1) with slightly higher cost (9.8007E+12). All three proposed methods substantially outperform k-means++ without predictions (cost 1.6884E+13, NMI 0.3285 at α=0.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against k-means++ (no predictions), all three proposed methods achieve dramatically lower clustering cost (e.g., 9.7–10.5E+12 vs. 1.66–1.75E+13) and much higher NMI/ARI (0.71–0.95 vs. 0.15–0.36), confirming the value of the learning-augmented setting. Against Ergun et al. (2021), Fast-Filtering achieves ~1.5% lower cost and is 15–17× faster (e.g., 1006s vs. 16749s at α=0.1); Fast-Estimation is ~1.9× faster with comparable cost. Against Det (Nguyen et al., 2022), Fast-Sampling matches or slightly improves cost (9.7666E+12 vs. 9.7791E+12 at α=0.1) with similar NMI/ARI, while Fast-Filtering achieves the best cost but at the expense of slightly lower NMI/ARI (0.9316 vs. 0.9490 at α=0.1). Fast-Estimation is ~1.5× faster than Det (8875s vs. 13153s) with a small cost increase (~0.2%). Overall, Fast-Filtering dominates on speed and cost, Fast-Sampling matches Det on quality, and Fast-Estimation offers a middle ground between speed and quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the learning-augmented k-means framework established by Ergun et al. (2021) and refined by Nguyen et al. (2022), both of which achieve (1+O(α))-approximations via sorting-based coordinate approximation in O(md log m) time. The key conceptual departure is replacing sorting—which has an Ω(m log m) comparison-based lower bound—with sampling-based strategies (Fast-Sampling, Fast-Estimation) to achieve linear O(md) running time. The Fast-Filtering heuristic further departs from the per-dimension coordinate approach by directly estimating full d-dimensional cluster centers using sublinear estimators, trading a slightly weaker (1+O(√α)) guarantee for significantly better practical speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear-time guarantee is practically important for scaling learning-augmented clustering to datasets with 100M+ points, where the O(md log m) sorting bottleneck becomes prohibitive. Fast-Filtering achieves roughly 10× speedup over prior methods on SIFT while maintaining comparable NMI/ARI, making it the only learning-augmented method faster than plain Lloyd's algorithm on that dataset. Open questions include whether the bounded aspect ratio assumption in Fast-Estimation can be removed, whether the approximation ratio can be tightened while preserving linear time, and whether the sampling-based approach extends naturally to other clustering objectives (k-medians, facility location).
