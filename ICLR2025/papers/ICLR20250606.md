## Problem

Message Passing Neural Networks (MPNNs) suffer from over-smoothing (node features collapse with depth), over-squashing (vanishing gradients for distant node interactions), and limited expressivity. While Sheaf Neural Networks mitigate over-smoothing through richer fixed points, they remain local message-passing architectures and inherit over-squashing. The paper asks whether a global diffusion-based GNN can simultaneously address all three limitations while maintaining computational tractability.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph G with node features X ∈ R^{n×c}, injective positional encodings P ∈ R^{n×f}, and a diffusion time hyperparameter t that controls the propagation scale. It produces transformed node features of the same dimensionality, which can be aggregated for graph-level tasks or used directly for node-level classification/regression. The diffusion time t is the key architectural knob: small t approximates local message passing via truncated Taylor series, while large t enables global communication via spectral methods, effectively making the receptive field tunable per layer.

## Method

BuNNs replace explicit multi-step message passing with a single global diffusion step on flat vector bundles, where each node v is assigned an orthogonal map O_v ∈ O(d) computed by a neural network φ from positional encodings. Each layer performs four operations: (1) compute bundle maps O_v, (2) apply a bundle-aware linear encoder h_v = O_v^T W O_v x_v + b, (3) diffuse via the bundle heat kernel H_B(t) = exp(-tL_B), and (4) apply a non-linearity. The critical insight is that flat bundles factorize the connection Laplacian as L_B = O^T(L⊗I_d)O, so the bundle heat kernel reduces to the standard graph heat kernel (pre-computable) sandwiched between synchronization/desynchronization operators, avoiding the O(d³|E|) cost of general sheaves. This makes every node communicate with every other node in a single layer (eliminating under-reaching and over-squashing), while non-trivial bundle maps ensure the heat equation's fixed points retain node-level information (eliminating over-smoothing). The paper proves compact uniform universality: 2-layer BuNNs with injective positional encodings can approximate any continuous feature transformation on infinite graph families, the first such positive result for a GNN architecture.

## Results

On the five heterophilic benchmarks, BuNN achieves the best score on every task, with an average relative improvement of 4.4% over the strongest baseline. Most notably, BuNN reaches 98.99 ROC AUC on Minesweeper (vs. 93.91 for GAT-sep) and 91.75% accuracy on Roman-Empire (vs. 88.75% for GAT-sep). On the Long Range Graph Benchmark, BuNN sets a new state-of-the-art on Peptides-func with 72.76 AP (vs. 71.50 for DReW), and is second on PascalVOC-SP with 40.49 F1 (vs. 44.40 for GPS). On the synthetic over-squashing task, BuNN achieves 0.01 MSE while all MPNN baselines score around 1.0, and on the over-smoothing clique task BuNN reaches 0.03 MSE versus 29.65 for GCN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BuNN outperforms all MPNN baselines (GCN, SAGE, GAT) by large margins across every heterophilic and LRGB task; for example, on Roman-Empire BuNN reaches 91.75% accuracy versus 73.69% (GCN), 85.74% (SAGE), and 80.87% (GAT). Against the sheaf-based NSD, BuNN consistently wins by a wide gap (e.g., 98.99 vs. 92.15 ROC AUC on Minesweeper; 91.75 vs. 80.41 on Roman-Empire), which the authors attribute to NSD's per-edge restriction maps being more prone to overfitting. On the LRGB Peptides-func task, BuNN (72.76 AP) surpasses DReW (71.50), GAPH ViT (69.42), and GINE (66.21), establishing a new SOTA. However, BuNN does not beat the strongest transformer-based baseline GPS on PascalVOC-SP (40.49 vs. 44.40 F1) or on Peptides-struct (24.63 vs. 24.49 MAE for GAPH ViT), though it clearly outperforms all MPNN methods on these tasks. On the synthetic over-squashing barbell task, BuNN (0.01 MSE) dramatically outperforms all MPNNs (~1.0 MSE) and GPS (1.06 MSE), while on the over-smoothing clique task BuNN (0.03 MSE) matches NSD (0.08 MSE) and far exceeds GCN (29.65) and GAT (20.97).

## Relation to Prior Work

BuNNs are derived as a special case of Sheaf Neural Networks (NSD, Bodnar et al. 2022) restricted to flat vector bundles, where orthogonal maps are assigned per-node rather than per-edge, enabling a factorization of the bundle Laplacian that reduces heat kernel computation to the standard graph heat kernel. The paper also shows GCNs emerge as a first-order Taylor approximation of BuNNs over a trivial bundle, positioning BuNNs as a strict generalization. The key departure from NSD is the use of the continuous heat kernel for global message diffusion rather than Euler-discretized local message passing, and the allowance of arbitrary GNNs (not just MLPs) for computing bundle maps. The expressivity analysis extends and contrasts with negative uniform approximation results for MPNNs and transformers from Rosenbluth et al. (2023, 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compact uniform approximation theorem is the first positive uniform expressivity result for any GNN architecture, establishing that BuNNs can approximate arbitrary continuous feature transformations over infinite graph families with injective positional encodings—something bounded-depth MPNNs provably cannot do. Practically, BuNNs address both over-smoothing and over-squashing simultaneously through a single mechanism (global diffusion on non-trivial bundles), which no prior architecture achieves with a unified theoretical guarantee. Key limitations include the computational cost of spectral heat kernel computation for large diffusion times t, the reliance on injective positional encodings for the expressivity guarantee, and the absence of link prediction experiments. Open questions include whether the expressivity result holds without positional encodings and whether efficient heat kernel approximations can close the scalability gap for very large t.
