## Problem

Existing identifiability results for content-style learning from unaligned multi-domain data rely on restrictive assumptions: element-wise independence of all latent components, known content and style dimensions, and a large number of domains (≥2d_S+1). This paper removes these restrictions, proving identifiability under only block independence between content and style, with as few as two domains, and—most notably—without requiring prior knowledge of the latent dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252783/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unaligned multi-domain RGB images (no cross-domain sample alignment needed) and random Gaussian latent vectors of user-specified (possibly over-estimated) dimensions. For generation, it produces images with independently controllable content (e.g., pose) and style (e.g., species) by fixing one and sampling the other. For translation, it takes a source-domain image, extracts content via GAN inversion (Adam optimizer with a pre-trained VGG16 perceptual loss), and recombines it with randomly sampled target-domain styles to yield translated images.

## Method

The core contribution is a Latent Distribution Matching (LDM) criterion that enforces cross-domain content distribution matching and block independence between learned content and style, proven to yield identifiable content-style decompositions under nonlinear mixture models with only block independence and as few as two domains. To handle unknown latent dimensions, an L0 sparsity objective on the style component is added, which prevents content information from leaking into the style representation and forces the effective style dimension to its true value. The LDM formulation is recast as a sparsity-regularized multi-domain GAN loss where invertible mappings e_C and e_S^(n) transform Gaussian noise into content and style, which are mixed by a StyleGAN2-ADA generator q; this reformulation performs distribution matching in the data domain rather than the latent domain, avoiding complex DM modules and simplifying optimization. An L1 regularization term approximates the sparsity constraint in practice, and the bijectivity of the generator is shown to be automatically satisfied under mild topological conditions on the content and style spaces.

## Results

On the data generation task, the proposed method achieves FID scores of 6.19 (AFHQ), 5.70 (CelebA-HQ), and 5.27 (CelebA-7), comparable to I-GAN (6.28, 5.91, 5.18). The key differentiator is style diversity: the proposed method scores 0.50, 0.36, and 0.26 (LPIPS-based), which is 3–5× higher than I-GAN (0.16, 0.07, 0.07) across all three datasets. For multi-domain translation, the proposed method achieves FID of 13.74 (AFHQ) and 16.61 (CelebA-HQ) with style diversity of 0.53 and 0.41, while requiring only 27.36 hours of total training—22 hours less than StarGANv2 and 69 hours less than I-GAN(Tr).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against I-GAN (Xie et al., 2023), the closest content-style baseline, the proposed method achieves nearly identical FID scores (6.19 vs. 6.28 on AFHQ) but dramatically higher style diversity—0.50 vs. 0.16 on AFHQ, 0.36 vs. 0.07 on CelebA-HQ, and 0.26 vs. 0.07 on CelebA-7—indicating that I-GAN fails to properly disentangle style, repeatedly sampling the same style per domain. Against StyleGAN-ADA and Transitional-cGAN, which do not learn explicit content-style representations, the proposed method matches or slightly improves FID while additionally providing controllable style diversity (a metric those baselines cannot report). For translation, the proposed method outperforms StarGANv2 on AFHQ FID (13.74 vs. 16.83) and style diversity (0.53 vs. 0.45), while StarGANv2 edges ahead on CelebA-HQ FID (13.67 vs. 16.61). The proposed method also substantially reduces training time: 27.36 h total versus 50.83 h for StarGANv2 and 96.99 h for I-GAN(Tr), since it uses a single generative model for both generation and translation without a separate translation module. SmoothGAN is clearly outperformed on both FID (53.68/29.69 vs. 13.74/16.61) and style diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the content-style identifiability literature for unaligned multi-domain data, departing from prior nonlinear mixture results (Xie et al. 2023; Kong et al. 2022) that required elementwise independence of all latent components and at least 2d_S+1 domains, and from linear mixture results (Sturma et al. 2023; Timilsina et al. 2024) that assumed data reside in a linear subspace. The key conceptual departure is the use of a latent distribution matching (LDM) criterion with block independence (content independent of style as a whole, not elementwise), which relaxes both the independence and domain-count requirements. Most distinctively, the paper introduces a sparsity-promoting objective that enables identifiability without knowing the exact latent dimensions—a problem no prior nonlinear mixture identifiability result had addressed. The implementation side repurposes the LDM formulation into a coupled multi-domain GAN loss, drawing on StyleGAN2-ADA architecture and GAN-inversion techniques for translation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dimension-agnostic identifiability result directly addresses a practical pain point: in real applications, the true content and style dimensions are unknown and are typically selected by trial-and-error, with wrong choices degrading generation diversity or translation quality. The relaxation to block independence and 2 domains broadens the theoretical guarantees to settings that were previously out of reach, making the framework applicable to common two-domain translation tasks. However, the paper only establishes sufficient (not necessary) conditions, is limited to homogeneous continuous modalities (e.g., same-resolution images), and relies on GAN training which is known to be unstable—extensions to heterogeneous data, discrete modalities, and more stable distribution matching (e.g., flow matching) remain open.
