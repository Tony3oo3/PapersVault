## Problem

GUI agents require high-quality multi-step trajectory data—interleaved observations, natural-language reasoning, and grounded actions—to learn complex sequential decision-making, yet such data is scarce online and current collection pipelines depend on costly human annotation that cannot scale. AgentTrek addresses this gap by proposing a fully automated synthesis pipeline that converts publicly available web tutorials into executable, verified agent trajectories at a cost of $0.55 per trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252879/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests raw web tutorial texts harvested from the 20.8-billion-entry RedPajama corpus, filtered through keyword/length/URL heuristics, an LLM labeler, and a FastText classifier down to ~18.8 million candidates, then standardized into structured task specifications (platform, description, prerequisites, step-by-step instructions, expected outcome) with a target_web_url. During guided replay, the VLM agent receives viewport screenshots and the accessibility tree (deliberately excluding full HTML due to size) as observations, and acts via Playwright API calls (click, select_option, clear, etc.). The output is a multimodal trajectory with ~12.1 average steps, each containing a screenshot, AXTree snapshot, chain-of-thought reasoning, and a grounded action, plus full video recordings, DOM/HTML snapshots, and network traces—structured to support both text-based (AXTree + Playwright) and vision-based (screenshot + pyautogui pixel coordinates) agent fine-tuning.

## Method

AgentTrek is a three-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (Tutorial Collection) cascades a rule-based pre-filter (92.69% recall, reducing 20.8B to 68.8M entries), a GPT-4o-mini LLM labeler (88.5% F1), a FastText classifier trained on 90K LLM-labeled samples (89.5% F1), and a GPT-4o-mini tag-and-paraphrase step that standardizes tutorials into a fixed template. Stage 2 (Guided Replay) deploys a GPT-4o VLM agent inside BrowserGym (Chromium) to execute the structured tutorials on live websites, recording screenshots, AXTree, reasoning, and Playwright actions at ~8,027 tokens per step. Stage 3 (Evaluation) uses a separate GPT-4o VLM evaluator that scores trajectories on instruction adherence and task completion (84% accuracy against human annotations), filtering out failed replays. The resulting 10,398 verified trajectories across 127 websites are then used to fine-tune Qwen2-VL (vision agent, NaViT encoder) and Qwen2.5-7B/32B (text agent) via supervised learning, yielding state-of-the-art results on WebArena, ScreenSpot Web, and Multimodal-Mind2Web.

## Results

On WebArena (text-based web agent), Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct fine-tuned with AgentTrek trajectories achieves 22.40% task success rate, surpassing GPT-4o (13.10%) and GPT-4 (14.41%) by a wide margin, while the 7B variant reaches 10.46%. On ScreenSpot Web grounding, Qwen2-VL-7B fine-tuned with AgentTrek achieves 67.4% average accuracy (81.7% text, 51.5% icon/widget), slightly exceeding GPT-4+OmniParser (67.0%). On Multimodal-Mind2Web, the combined AgentTrek+Mind2Web training yields 52.6% cross-domain step success rate, outperforming GPT-4 with HTML+Image observations (36.8%) by 15.8 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On WebArena, AgentTrek-trained Qwen2.5-32B (22.40%) substantially outperforms GPT-4o (13.10%) and GPT-4 (14.41%), as well as Synatra-CodeLlama-7B (6.28%) and AutoWebGLM OOD SFT (8.50%), confirming that synthesized tutorial-guided trajectories yield stronger generalization than both proprietary LLMs and prior synthetic-data approaches. On ScreenSpot Web, AgentTrek-fine-tuned Qwen2-VL-7B (67.4% avg) edges out GPT-4+OmniParser (67.0%) and decisively beats SeeClick (44.7%) and CogAgent (50.7%), demonstrating that trajectory-based fine-tuning surpasses both prompting-based and prior grounding-specific methods. On Multimodal-Mind2Web, the AgentTrek+Mind2Web combination (52.6% cross-domain Step SR) far exceeds GPT-4 with HTML+Image choice prompting (36.8%) and GPT-4 with Set-of-Mark (23.7%), showing that learned visual grounding from AgentTrek trajectories generalizes better than zero-shot or few-shot prompting strategies across increasingly difficult cross-domain settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of data synthesis for GUI agents and the use of publicly available web content as training scaffolding. Prior trajectory datasets (Mind2Web, WebArena, WebLINX, ScreenAgent) rely heavily on human annotation, while recent synthetic approaches like BAGEL and NNetNav generate trajectories through language-guided exploration or unsupervised environment interaction without structured task guidance. AgentTrek departs from both by repurposing existing web tutorials as step-by-step playbooks that a VLM agent follows during guided replay in BrowserGym, combining FastText-based tutorial harvesting, LLM standardization, and VLM-based trajectory evaluation into a single automated pipeline. The method builds on existing infrastructure (BrowserGym, Playwright, Qwen2-VL/2.5) but the tutorial-as-replay-guide formulation is a distinct conceptual contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The $0.55-per-trajectory cost and 10,398-trajectory scale make high-quality GUI agent training data accessible without human annotation, directly addressing the scalability bottleneck that has limited the field. The scaling experiments showing synthetic data approaching human-annotated Mind2Web performance (45.0% vs. 47.7% cross-domain step SR) suggest automated pipelines can close the quality gap, though the 39.9% replay success rate means most expensive VLM calls are wasted. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the 11 task categories and 127 websites covered, how it handles dynamic website changes, and whether the tutorial dependency limits task diversity compared to open-ended exploration. The pipeline's reliance on GPT-4o for both replay and evaluation also raises questions about model homogeneity in the generated data.
