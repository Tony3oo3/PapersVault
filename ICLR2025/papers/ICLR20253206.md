## Problem

Discrete diffusion models require hundreds to thousands of denoising steps to produce high-quality samples, unlike their continuous counterparts which can generate in just a few steps. The paper identifies the root cause: at each denoising step, the model independently predicts univariate marginals for each masked token, failing to capture the joint probability of simultaneous edits. This independent denoising assumption introduces an irreducible total-correlation term in the ELBO that fundamentally limits sample quality, especially in few-step regimes.

## Input / Output

The method operates purely at inference time, requiring no fine-tuning of either base model. At each denoising step, the diffusion model is run twice on the same partially-masked sequence: once with full bidirectional attention to obtain univariate marginals p_dm(X_t^i | x_{t+1}), and once with causal attention masks to obtain prefix-conditioned marginals p_dm(X_t^i | x_{t+1}^{<i}). The autoregressive copula model (e.g., GPT-2) is queried autoregressively to produce the dependency structure. The output is a fully unmasked sequence (text or antibody) generated in as few as 4 denoising steps, with an autoregressive unmasking variant that uses KV-caching to keep the copula model's compute constant across steps.

## Method

DCD combines a pretrained discrete diffusion model with a pretrained autoregressive copula model via I-projection: it finds the distribution that matches the diffusion model's univariate marginals while minimizing KL divergence to the copula distribution. The resulting combined distribution has the closed form p_copula(x) · ∏_i exp(V[i, x_i]), where V is determined by a convex optimization problem approximated by row-wise updates: V[i,c] = log p_dm(X_t^i=c | x_{t+1}) − log p_dm(X_t^i=c | x_{t+1}^{<i}). This approximation is justified by showing that the second term can be estimated from the diffusion model itself using causal attention masks, avoiding the need to marginalize over unobserved tokens in the autoregressive model. Sampling from the combined distribution is performed autoregressively, and an efficient variant unmasks tokens in fixed-size chunks per step, enabling KV-caching so the copula model's total cost is independent of the number of denoising steps. The paper proves (Proposition 1) that the independent denoising assumption creates an irreducible ELBO gap equal to the total correlation of the denoising distributions, and (Proposition 2) that I-projection strictly improves the estimate regardless of the copula model's quality.

## Results

On unconditional text generation (128 tokens, WebText/OpenWebText), DCD with just 4 denoising steps matches SEDD_M with 128 steps in generative perplexity, achieving a 32× reduction in denoising steps. On conditional text infilling (WikiText-103, MAUVE score), DCD outperforms all baselines across all five prompt mask strategies; for example, on the [0.25, 0.75] mask, DCD with 16 steps reaches 0.314 MAUVE versus 0.237 for SEDD_M at 32 steps. On antibody sequence infilling (OAS dataset), DCD with only 4 steps achieves 58.28% recovery on HCDR{1+2+3} and 91.58% on {H+L}CDR1, surpassing both GPT (57.21%, 90.28%) and NOC-D with 64 steps (51.56%, 88.82%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCD consistently outperforms both of its constituent models (SEDD_M and GPT-2_S) at every denoising step count from 2 to 32 on unconditional text generation, validating the I-projection combination. Against SSD-LM on conditional text infilling, DCD wins on all five mask strategies; the gap is largest on harder prompts (e.g., [0.25,0.75]: DCD 0.314 vs. SSD-LM 0.108 at 500 steps). Compared to SEDD_M, DCD matches or exceeds its quality with 8–32× fewer denoising steps (e.g., DCD 4 steps ≈ SEDD_M 128 steps on perplexity). On antibody infilling, DCD with 4 steps beats NOC-D with 64 steps by 6.72 points on HCDR{1+2+3} (58.28% vs. 51.56%) and by 2.76 points on {H+L}CDR1 (91.58% vs. 88.82%), while also surpassing the GPT copula model alone. MDLM is compared only on runtime-vs-perplexity trade-offs (Figure 5), where DCD achieves lower perplexity at equivalent sampling time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the discrete diffusion framework of Austin et al. (2021) and the SEDD model of Lou et al. (2024), identifying a structural limitation—fully factorized denoising at each step—that prior work had attributed to insufficient model scale. It departs from prior discrete diffusion work by introducing an inference-time combination with an autoregressive model (GPT-2, Radford et al. 2019) via information projection (I-projection), formalized through the statistical concept of copulas. The approach also draws on NOC-D (Gruver et al., 2023) for protein sequence experiments, demonstrating generality across domains. Unlike concurrent work by Guo et al. (2024) that uses energy-based models as copulas, this paper specifically instantiates the copula as an autoregressive model and provides a convex-optimization-based combination procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8–32× reduction in denoising steps (e.g., 4 steps matching 128-step SEDD) is practically significant for any application relying on discrete diffusion, and the method requires no retraining of either base model. The theoretical identification of an irreducible total-correlation term in the ELBO under independent denoising provides a clear diagnostic for why few-step discrete diffusion fails, redirecting future work toward dependency-aware denoising. Limitations include the need for a second pretrained model, the approximate (row-wise) I-projection, and higher per-step compute cost, which may limit net speedup in some regimes. Open questions include whether the copula can be learned jointly with the diffusion model, whether the approach extends to non-absorbing noising processes, and whether the convex optimization can be solved more tightly.
