## Problem

Diffusion models are predominantly studied on high-quality image data, yet real-world non-image data (tabular, time-series) are frequently noisy due to measurement errors, missing-value imputation, or sensor limitations. Noisy samples shift the marginal distribution in the diffusion process, causing the score-matching objective to be misguiding during training. This paper introduces a principled framework that pairs each noisy sample with a per-dimension risk vector and modifies the diffusion SDE to neutralize the negative effect of such noise.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes noisy samples x̃(0) ∈ ℝ^D paired with an element-wise nonnegative risk vector r ∈ ℝ^D, where r_i = 0 indicates a clean entry and larger values indicate higher expected noise variance (for Gaussian perturbation). The risk vector controls a family of continuous noise distributions and is typically obtained for free from imputation or interpolation procedures (e.g., Gaussian Process confidence intervals, KNN median deviations). At inference, the model generates clean samples by setting r = 0, so the output is a standard data vector from the clean distribution p_0(x) without any risk annotation.

## Method

The core contribution is the risk-sensitive SDE, a diffusion process whose drift and diffusion coefficients f(r,t), g(r,t) are parameterized by the risk vector via Hadamard products, making them entry-wise (non-isotropic) rather than scalar. The authors define a perturbation-instability measure S_t(r) based on the mean-square discrepancy between cumulant-generating functions of the noisy and clean marginal densities, then derive optimal coefficients that minimize this measure. For diagonal Gaussian noise, they prove full perturbation stability (S_t(r)=0) is achievable within a stability interval T(r), yielding closed-form risk-sensitive VP SDE and VE SDE coefficients that simply reduce the diffusion coefficient g(r,t) and restrict training to t ∈ T(r). For general non-Gaussian noise (e.g., Cauchy), they provide the minimally-unstable solution via a complex integral expression Ψ(u(t),r), and show that even without full stability the method effectively suppresses outliers. Training uses a simplified score-matching loss where the noise target is η/v(r,t) instead of η/v(t), and sampling proceeds with the standard reverse SDE at zero risk.

## Results

On five real-world datasets (MIMIC-III, WARDS, Abalone, Telemonitoring, Mushroom), Risk-sensitive VP SDE achieves the lowest Wasserstein distance across all datasets, with headline values of 1.625 (MIMIC-III), 2.584 (WARDS), 0.077 (Abalone), 1.462 (Telemonitoring), and 0.852 (Mushroom). The best risk-conditional baseline (VP SDE w/ Risk Conditional) achieves 5.637, 6.292, 0.585, 3.785, and 4.850 respectively, showing our method reduces Wasserstein distance by 3–7×. On CIFAR-10 with 40% noisy images, Risk-sensitive VP SDE achieves FID 6.97 versus 8.53 for Unbiased Diffusion and 13.29 for standard VP SDE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three categories of baselines: standard diffusion models (VP SDE / VE SDE from Song et al., 2021), risk-conditional variants (three implementations), and methods from similar noisy-data settings. Risk-sensitive VP SDE outperforms standard VP SDE by 5.5–8.5 in Wasserstein distance (e.g., 1.625 vs 9.135 on MIMIC-III) and beats the strongest risk-conditional baseline (VP SDE w/ Risk Conditional) by 3.4–4.1 (e.g., 1.462 vs 3.785 on Telemonitoring). Against Ambient Diffusion (Daras et al., 2024), our method achieves 0.077 vs 0.482 on Abalone and 1.462 vs 4.253 on Telemonitoring. Against Unbiased Diffusion Model (Kim et al., 2024), our method achieves 0.077 vs 0.679 on Abalone and 1.462 vs 4.991 on Telemonitoring. On CIFAR-10 with 40% noisy images, Risk-sensitive VP SDE (FID 6.97) outperforms Unbiased Diffusion (8.53) and standard VP SDE (13.29), confirming the advantage extends to image data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SDE-based diffusion framework of Song et al. (2021) by introducing risk-sensitive SDEs whose coefficients are parameterized by a per-sample risk vector, enabling robust score-matching on noisy data. It departs from prior noisy-data diffusion work (Kim et al. 2024, which uses binary risk; Daras et al. 2024, which handles missing pixel patches; Na et al. 2024, which addresses noisy labels) by formulating a continuous, per-dimension risk vector and deriving closed-form optimal SDE coefficients for both Gaussian and non-Gaussian (e.g., Cauchy) perturbation families. The perturbation instability measure, defined via cumulant-generating functions, provides a principled optimization criterion that unifies the Gaussian (full stability) and non-Gaussian (minimum instability) regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically critical gap: 79% of data scientists work on tabular data that is routinely noisy due to imputation, sensor error, or interpolation, yet standard diffusion models assume clean inputs. The clean theoretical result that Gaussian perturbation can be fully eliminated within a stability interval, combined with the empirical finding that the Gaussian assumption generalizes well to mis-specified noise (e.g., KNN imputation errors), makes the approach broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the instability measure can be replaced by more standard divergences (KL, Wasserstein), how the method scales to very high-dimensional data where the stability interval may become very short, and whether risk vectors can be learned end-to-end rather than obtained from preprocessing pipelines.
