## Problem

Current end-to-end autonomous driving methods inherit dense perception pipelines (detection, mapping, occupancy) that require expensive human annotations and produce large, computationally heavy networks, limiting real-time deployment and data scalability. The paper asks whether E2EAD systems truly need such extensive perception supervision, or whether a minimal, navigation-guided set of tokens can capture all driving-relevant scene information without any auxiliary perception tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251040/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N-views RGB camera images (640×360 for open-loop nuScenes, 900×256 for closed-loop CARLA) and a discrete high-level navigation command (go straight / turn left / turn right) as input, and outputs a single planning trajectory of Nt waypoints in BEV space. The dense 100×100 BEV feature is compressed into just 16 tokens of dimension 256, and no auxiliary perception labels (detection boxes, map elements, occupancy grids) are required at any stage of training or inference.

## Method

SSR introduces a Scenes TokenLearner (STL) module that adaptively compresses the dense BEV feature into 16 sparse scene tokens via learned spatial attention maps, with a Squeeze-and-Excitation layer first encoding the navigation command into the BEV features so that each token focuses on a navigation-relevant spatial region. Waypoint queries then cross-attend with these 16 tokens to decode the planning trajectory, supervised only by L1 imitation loss. A Future Feature Predictor provides temporal self-supervision: it encodes the predicted trajectory into 'dreaming queries' via Motion-aware Layer Normalization, predicts future scene queries, reconstructs a dense future BEV feature through a TokenFuser, and is supervised by L2 loss against the actual next-frame BEV feature—eliminating all perception-task annotations. The total loss is simply L1 imitation + L2 BEV reconstruction, and the entire pipeline runs at 19.6 FPS, 10.9× faster than UniAD.

## Results

On nuScenes (open-loop, MAX metric), SSR achieves 0.75 m average L2 error and 0.15% average collision rate with no auxiliary perception tasks, reducing L2 by 27.2% and collision rate by 51.6% relative to UniAD while running at 19.6 FPS (10.9× faster inference, 13× faster training). On CARLA Town05 Long (closed-loop), SSR attains a Driving Score of 78.9 (RC 95.5%, IS 0.83), surpassing the best camera-only baseline TCP (57.2) by 31.7 points and VAD-Base (30.3) by 48.6 points, and even outperforming LiDAR-augmented methods ThinkTwice (65.0) and DriveAdapter (65.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSR outperforms UniAD (the most task-heavy baseline) by 0.28 m in average L2 (27.2% relative) and 0.16% in average collision rate (51.6% relative) while using no auxiliary perception tasks and running 10.9× faster at inference. Against VAD-Base, SSR reduces average L2 by 45.8% and collision rate by 70.7% (AVG metric), and is 4.3× faster. Among annotation-free methods, SSR beats LAW (0.61 m L2, 0.30% CR) by a large margin in collision rate (0.15% vs 0.30%) despite similar inference speed, and outperforms UAD* (0.90 m L2, 0.19% CR) by 0.15 m in L2 while being 2.7× faster, even though UAD uses a larger ResNet-101 backbone at 1600×900 resolution plus an open-set 2D detector. Compared to the closest sparse competitor SparseDrive (0.61 m L2, 0.08% CR), SSR is 2.2× faster but trades a slight L2 increase (0.75 vs 0.61 m) for a lower collision rate (0.15% vs 0.08%). On the closed-loop CARLA Town05 Long benchmark, SSR's 78.9 Driving Score surpasses all camera-only baselines (TCP 57.2, VAD-Base 30.3) and even LiDAR-augmented methods (ThinkTwice 65.0, DriveAdapter 65.9), achieving the highest infraction score (0.83) among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSR builds directly on VAD's vectorized scene representation framework and BEVFormer's spatiotemporal cross-attention for BEV feature construction, but departs fundamentally from the entire E2EAD paradigm that inherits dense perception tasks (detection, tracking, mapping, occupancy, motion) from modular AD pipelines. Rather than using hundreds of task-specific queries supervised by auxiliary annotations (as in UniAD, VAD, PARA-Drive, or even sparse variants like SparseDrive), SSR repurposes the TokenLearner architecture from video compression into BEV space, guided by navigation commands via a Squeeze-and-Excitation layer, to extract only 16 scene tokens. The temporal self-supervision via a Future Feature Predictor replaces all perception-task supervision, drawing on the intuition that correct actions should produce correct future scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating all perception annotations and reducing the scene representation to 16 tokens, SSR addresses the two major deployment bottlenecks of E2EAD: annotation cost and computational overhead (10.9× faster inference, 13× faster training). The finding that adding perception supervision (detection, mapping) actually degrades trajectory performance (Tables 6–7) challenges the field's working assumption that explicit perception tasks are necessary scaffolding for driving. Open questions remain around the reliance on simple three-way navigation commands (go straight/turn left/turn right), which limits generalization to complex routing scenarios, and whether the approach scales to more diverse driving distributions beyond nuScenes and CARLA.
