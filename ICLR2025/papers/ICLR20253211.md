## Problem

LLMs used for automatic protocol design in self-driving labs treat experimental knowledge as isolated, flattened pieces rather than structured conceptual knowledge, causing them to generate only protocols similar to existing ones and lose critical execution details. The paper identifies that a systematic, multi-scale representation of experimental ingredients and operations is missing, and that without it, LLMs cannot reason over the global semantic space needed for novel protocol planning, modification, and adjustment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253211/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an experimental objective (desired final product), the domain of experiment, a set of available reagents, and a domain-specific corpus of published protocols as input. The corpus is first processed via NLP to extract instance actions, then used to automatically fit the DSL representation. The output is a designed protocol expressed as a DSL program with both operation-centric and product-flow-centric views, which passes through a reciprocative verification loop before being returned. The DSL representation itself is also an output artifact, reusable across multiple design tasks within the same domain.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-level hierarchical encapsulation implemented as Domain-Specific Languages: (i) structural representation decomposing protocols into instance actions with precondition/postcondition/execution-configuration attributes; (ii) sequential operation-centric representation via function abstraction, where each operation is an interface mapping to a set of execution contexts; and (iii) continuous product-flow-centric representation via model abstraction, tracking component status transitions with predecessor/successor operations and key properties. The DSL is automatically generated per domain using a hierarchical Dirichlet Process Mixture Model (DPMM) with Gaussian Process integration for continuous parameter values, plus a unification step to merge redundant interfaces. LLMs serve as designers under three modes—pure RAG baseline, internal prompting constrained by the DSL, and external verification where the DSL acts as a guardrail checking operation pre/post-conditions and product property coherence in a reciprocative feedback loop.

## Results

On the planning task (most challenging, requiring novel protocol generation), the best method EE+ (dual-representation DSL with external verifier) achieves IoU(Op)=0.607, IoU(Prod)=0.605, IoU(Dev)=0.355, Sim(Exec)=0.744, Sim(Goal)=0.893, and Sim(Param)=0.951, substantially outperforming the Bioplanner-style baseline II (IoU(Op)=0.382, IoU(Prod)=0.050, Sim(Exec)=0.452). The dual-view encapsulated representations (EI+, EE+) consistently outperform their single-view counterparts (EI, EE) across all three tasks and four domains, with paired t-tests confirming significance (e.g., EE+ vs. II: t(278)=24.493, p<.0001). On the modification task, EE+ reaches IoU(Prod)=0.661 and Sim(Exec)=0.757; on adjustment, EI+ leads with IoU(Op)=0.668 and Sim(Exec)=0.775.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is II (Instance-Internal), which implements the Bioplanner approach of O'Donoghue et al. (2023) by prompting an LLM with instance-action pseudofunction definitions retrieved via RAG. The paper's dual-representation methods (EE+, EI+) dramatically outperform II on all six metrics: on the planning task, EE+ achieves IoU(Op)=0.607 vs. II's 0.382 (+59%), IoU(Prod)=0.605 vs. 0.050 (+1110%), and Sim(Exec)=0.744 vs. 0.452 (+65%), with all differences statistically significant (t(278)=24.493, p<.0001). The single-view encapsulated methods (EI, EE) also beat II significantly (t(278)=15–16, p<.0001), confirming that even operation-centric abstraction alone provides substantial gains over flat instance actions. The pure LLM+RAG baseline (FB) performs worst on structural metrics (IoU(Op)=0.143, IoU(Prod)=0.040) but is competitive on high-level goal similarity (Sim(Goal)=0.766), suggesting LLMs capture intent well but fail on execution-level details. The key finding is that adding the product-flow-centric view (the '+' in EI+/EE+) yields the largest single improvement over the operation-centric-only versions, particularly on IoU(Prod) and Sim(Exec), validating the reciprocative verification mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LLM-based protocol design work (Boiko et al., 2023; M. Bran et al., 2024; O'Donoghue et al., 2023) by identifying that flat, unstructured knowledge representations limit LLMs' ability to design novel protocols, and proposes a hierarchically encapsulated DSL representation (instance actions → operations → product flows) as the missing vehicle for conceptual reasoning. The automatic generation of domain-specific DSLs via hierarchical Dirichlet Process Mixture Models departs from prior manual DSL design (Mernik et al., 2005; Shi et al., 2024a) by making the representation affordable across domains without expert-language co-design. The reciprocative verification mechanism between operation-centric and product-flow-centric views draws on the Self-Refine paradigm (Madaan et al., 2023) but applies it to a structured constraint layer rather than free-text self-feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck in self-driving laboratories: the manual design burden of new protocols as AI accelerates hypothesis generation. The automatic DSL generation pipeline is a key enabler for scaling the representation to new experimental domains without labor-intensive expert co-design. However, the authors explicitly note that current DSL-level verification is 'far from sufficient for serving as a certification,' and human domain experts remain in the loop for final approval. Open questions include whether digital twins of self-driving labs could eventually replace human certification, whether the representation generalizes beyond the four tested domains, and how the framework handles highly novel experimental objectives with no close analogues in the training corpus.
