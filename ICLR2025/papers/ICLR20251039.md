## Problem

Intrinsically interpretable deep learning models have gained popularity for sensitive domains like education and healthcare, but they produce nuanced, complex explanations that are not easily actionable for downstream users such as teachers or clinicians. Existing approaches either lack sparsity (explaining all features/concepts), lack faithfulness (post-hoc approximations), or use automated concepts that don't align with what users actually need to act on. The paper asks whether a model can be designed to use only a minimal, instance-specific set of features or user-defined concepts for prediction, yielding explanations that are both guaranteed faithful and directly actionable.

## Input / Output

The method accepts three input formats: tabular feature vectors (x ∈ R^n), text token sequences (x = [t_1, ..., t_N]), and multivariate time series (x ∈ R^{n×T}), with the same sparse mask applied uniformly across all timesteps for time series. For text, each word is pre-assigned to a topical subnetwork via the Dewey Decimal Code hierarchy using SentenceBERT embeddings. The output is a class label computed only from the sparsely activated features or groups, alongside the sparse activation mask (or group weights) that constitutes the explanation—ensuring the explanation covers exactly what the model used, with no unexplained residual contribution.

## Method

InterpretCC introduces two architectures built on conditional computation repurposed for interpretability rather than efficiency. Feature Gating passes all features through a discriminator network, applies Gumbel-Softmax with a per-instance threshold τ to produce a sparse binary mask, and feeds only the masked features into a predictive model f. Group Routing extends this to a global mixture-of-experts: features are statically partitioned into human-specified topical groups, a discriminator assigns a Gumbel-Softmax weight to each group, and the final prediction is a weighted sum of expert subnetworks that each see only their assigned group's features. During training, soft masking keeps all weights non-zero so the model accesses every expert; at inference, a hard mask enforces sparsity for interpretability. The discriminator is deliberately a black box to keep the explanation at the level of 'which features/groups were used and how important they are' rather than 'why were they selected,' which the authors argue is the directly actionable question for end users.

## Results

Across eight datasets spanning education (time series), healthcare (tabular), text (news/sentiment), and synthetic data, InterpretCC Feature Gating matches or exceeds the non-interpretable baseline in 15 of 16 comparisons (95% CI overlap), with notable gains on DSP (90.75% vs. 82.81% balanced accuracy), HWTS (82.89% vs. 73.93%), and OpenXAI (89.51% vs. 86.67% accuracy). InterpretCC Group Routing outperforms the baseline on Geo (81.58% vs. 72.96%) and Breast Cancer (94.85% vs. 89.70%), and matches on AG News (90.35% vs. 89.93%) and SST (91.75% vs. 91.12%). On the synthetic OpenXAI faithfulness benchmark, ICC FG achieves 94.84% Ground Truth Alignment and 87.99% Feature Agreement, the highest among all intrinsically interpretable models, with guaranteed 100% Ground Truth Faithfulness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against intrinsically interpretable baselines, ICC Feature Gating outperforms SENN Features by 9.05% on average and NAM by 3.27% on average across all supported datasets, while ICC Group Routing exceeds SENN Concepts by 5.63% on average. FRESH (extractive rationale) is only evaluated on text data and underperforms both ICC variants on AG News (88.73% vs. 90.35% for ICC GR) and SST (82.05% vs. 91.75% for ICC GR). On the OpenXAI faithfulness benchmark, ICC FG achieves the highest Ground Truth Alignment (94.84%) and Feature Agreement (87.99%) among all intrinsically interpretable models, while post-hoc explainers (IG, LIME, SHAP) show lower faithfulness guarantees (≤100% GTF vs. ICC's guaranteed 100%) and lower Feature Agreement (52–92%). The only dataset where ICC underperforms the non-interpretable baseline is Breast Cancer for ICC FG (78.19% vs. 89.70%), though ICC Group Routing recovers and exceeds the baseline (94.85%). In the user study with 56 teachers, ICC FG was significantly preferred over both SENN and NAM on usefulness, trustworthiness, actionability, and conciseness (Tukey's HSD, p<0.05), with only SENN rated higher on completeness.

## Relation to Prior Work

InterpretCC repurposes conditional computation and mixture-of-experts routing—previously used for computational efficiency (Switch Transformers, Mixtral, BASE layers)—toward an interpretability objective, where sparse activation defines the explanation rather than merely reducing FLOPs. It departs from SENN and NAM, which explain all features or concepts without sparsity, and from automated-concept MoE approaches (SOP, IME) by requiring user-specified feature groupings that directly encode domain knowledge. The Gumbel-Softmax thresholding mechanism for differentiable sparse selection is borrowed from the CC literature but applied at the feature/group level to produce a guaranteed-faithful, instance-specific explanation that is used directly in the prediction pathway.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: intrinsically interpretable models often produce explanations too dense or abstract for non-technical users (teachers, clinicians) to act on, and InterpretCC's sparse, user-specified grouping directly targets actionability. The 56-teacher user study is a meaningful step toward grounding XAI evaluation in downstream user needs, though it is preliminary (4 students, single course, single domain). Open questions include whether user-defined groupings can be reliably automated at scale (the GPT-4 grouping result is encouraging but limited to one domain), whether the black-box discriminator is acceptable in high-stakes settings, and whether the approach generalizes to tasks requiring long-context or multi-step reasoning beyond DistilBERT-scale experts.
