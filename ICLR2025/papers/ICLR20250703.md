## Problem

While sparse parities (constant degree k) are known to be learnable by gradient descent and dense parities (both k and d−k growing) are provably unlearnable, the almost-full parity regime (k = d − O(1), including the full parity) remained unsettled. This paper resolves the gap by showing that learnability of almost-full parities depends critically on the weight initialization: Rademacher initialization enables efficient learning, while Gaussian initialization (and sufficiently large Gaussian perturbations of Rademacher) provably prevents it.

## Input / Output

The input is a Boolean vector x ∈ {±1}^d drawn from the uniform distribution, and the target is a fixed parity function f(x) = ∏_{i∈S} x_i where |S| = d − O(1). The network produces a real-valued score whose sign gives the ±1 prediction. A key structural detail is that the hidden layer weights are initialized from discrete Rademacher (±1) or Gaussian distributions, while output weights start at zero; the analysis tracks how the initialization's symmetry properties determine whether the hidden-layer embedding of the parity is linearly separable.

## Method

The positive result shows that Rademacher initialization makes the hidden-layer embeddings of almost-full parities linearly separable with high probability, so one step of full GD with correlation loss (or poly(d) SGD steps with hinge loss) achieves perfect accuracy; this extends to σ-perturbed Rademacher for σ = O(d^{-1}). The negative result introduces a novel loss-dependent measure called Gradient Alignment (GAL), which quantifies the squared norm of the difference between the population gradient under the target and under random labels. The proof couples the actual training dynamics with a 'junk-flow' (training on random labels) and shows that if GAL is exponentially small at initialization, the network's parameters remain close to the junk-flow in total variation, preventing any learning. For Gaussian initialization and large-σ perturbed Rademacher, GAL is shown to be exp(−Ω(d)), yielding an exponential-time lower bound for achieving better-than-random accuracy.

## Results

The paper establishes a sharp separation between initialization schemes for learning high-degree parities. With Rademacher initialization, a two-layer ReLU network with Ω(d⁴) hidden neurons learns the full parity in a single GD step (or Ω(d²) neurons with clipped ReLU), while for almost-full (d−a)-parities, Ω(d^{4+2⌈a/2⌉}) neurons suffice. In contrast, with Gaussian initialization (or σ-perturbed Rademacher with σ ≥ σ₀ for some constant σ₀ > 0), no network of size exp(o(d)) trained for exp(o(d)) steps achieves accuracy better than 1/2 + exp(−Ω(d)). The positive Rademacher result is robust to small perturbations up to σ = O(d⁻¹), but empirical results (d=50) show learning degrades as σ increases, with failure observed for σ ≥ 0.3 at larger dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is across initialization distributions rather than across architectures or algorithms. Rademacher initialization is the only scheme that enables efficient learning of the full parity; all other tested initializations fail. Specifically, Gaussian initialization yields accuracy at most 1/2 + exp(−Ω(d)) after any polynomial number of steps (Theorem 7). σ-perturbed Rademacher with large constant σ (≥ σ₀) also fails (Theorem 8), while the positive result holds only for σ = O(d⁻¹). Empirically (Figure 3), sparsified Rademacher (setting a fraction of weights to zero) and a discrete symmetric initialization over {−2,−1,1,2} both fail to learn, confirming that the exact Rademacher distribution is a special case. A mixture of two continuous uniforms with means ±1 behaves analogously to the Gaussian perturbation: learning succeeds at σ=0.1 for d=50 but fails at σ=1.0. The paper also contrasts with the SQ framework, where the full parity (a singleton function class) is trivially learnable, highlighting a computational separation between SQ and gradient descent on neural networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on learning parities with gradient descent on neural networks, building directly on Abbe & Boix-Adsera (2022), who showed almost-full parities are weakly learnable with Rademacher initialization, and Abbe & Sandon (2020), who established hardness for dense parities via cross-predictability and introduced the junk-flow coupling technique. The key departure is the introduction of a loss-dependent Gradient Alignment (GAL) measure that applies to a single target function rather than a function class, enabling negative results for the full parity (a singleton) where prior orbit-class-based methods are inapplicable. The paper also builds on Nachum & Yehudayoff (2020) for the hinge loss analysis, adapting their linear-separability-at-initialization argument to the perturbed Rademacher setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clean separation between statistical query learning (where the full parity is trivially learnable as a singleton) and gradient descent on neural networks (where it requires exponential time under Gaussian initialization), highlighting that Rademacher initialization is a fragile special case rather than a generic property of discrete initializations. The proposed Gradient Alignment measure, being loss-dependent and applicable to general input distributions and architectures with linear output layers, offers a potentially broader tool for diagnosing why certain initializations fail. Open questions include identifying the precise threshold σ* where learning transitions from possible to impossible, extending the negative result to hinge loss and deeper architectures, and understanding whether the junk-flow technique generalizes to other hard learning scenarios involving symmetry and logical reasoning.
