## Problem

Inverse Constraint Learning (ICL) is inherently ill-posed: multiple distinct constraints can equivalently explain the same expert demonstrations, making the ground-truth constraint non-identifiable. Prior ICL solvers either impose restrictive assumptions (minimum coverage, regularized policies) to recover a single constraint, or face prohibitive computational cost when verifying candidates by solving a forward constrained RL problem. No practical method existed to efficiently recover a diverse set of feasible constraints from offline data in continuous state-action spaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline expert trajectories D^E and non-expert trajectories D^{-E} (with known reward signals) from a CMDP whose cost function is unknown, operating entirely offline without environment interaction. It outputs a set of feasible constraint functions {φ_ω} organized by sparsity level δ and exploration round m, where each constraint is a U-Net-based model predicting per-state-action feasibility values φ_ω(s,a) ∈ [0,1] over a horizon-H trajectory, with the corresponding cost computed as c(s,a) = −log φ(s,a). Unusually, the constraint model must remain accurate under Gaussian noise at every diffusion step, not just on clean trajectories.

## Method

ExICL replaces the expensive forward CRL verification step with a Generative Diffusion Verifier (GDV): a U-Net diffusion planner trained on offline data whose denoising process is guided in-context by the gradient of a constraint-aware optimality model g_c = ∇_τ p_{M_c}(O_{0:T}=1|τ), derived from the Lagrangian dual of the CMDP. This avoids any policy or value-function updates during verification, reducing constraint checking to a single guided generation pass. To ensure the constraint model can supply valid gradients at every noise level, ExICL introduces a noise-robust training objective that takes expectations over the forward diffusion process, so the cost prediction remains accurate for partially-noised trajectories. For diversity, ExICL applies an InfoNCE-based contrastive regularization ψ(φ_ω, Z) that simultaneously encourages sparsity (high feasibility values) and divergence from previously discovered constraints in the feasible set Z, iterated across multiple exploration rounds and sparsity parameters δ to populate a broad constraint set.

## Results

On the three PointMaze environments, ExICL achieves near-perfect performance with 1.00±0.00 cumulative reward and 0.00–0.01±0.00 cumulative cost across UMAZE, 2WALLS, and L, outperforming the best baseline ICSDICE (which achieves 0.92–1.00 reward but 0.02–1.20 cost). On MuJoCo robot control tasks, ExICL attains 5298±480 feasible reward with 0.00 cost on Obstacle-HalfCheetah (vs. 3829±661 for the best baseline SMODICE-c), 1862±29 with 0.00 cost on Limited-Walker, and 3061±199 with 0.01 cost on Blocked-Ant, matching or exceeding all baselines while maintaining zero or near-zero constraint violations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExICL decisively outperforms all baselines on Obstacle-HalfCheetah, achieving 5298±480 feasible reward with zero cost versus SMODICE-c's 3829±661 (0.30 cost) and OptiDICE-c's 2749±597 (0.03 cost). On Limited-Walker, ExICL (1862±29, 0.00 cost) slightly trails SMODICE in raw reward (2334±238) but SMODICE incurs 0.52 cost, making ExICL the only method combining high reward with zero violations. On Blocked-Ant, ExICL (3061±199, 0.01 cost) is essentially tied with ICSDICE (3073±103, 0.01 cost) and OptiDICE-c (3070±91, 0.01 cost). On PointMaze, ExICL achieves perfect 1.00 reward and near-zero cost across all three mazes, while ICSDICE (the strongest baseline) still accumulates 0.02–1.20 cost. Offline imitation baselines (BC, LS-IQ) consistently fail to maintain safety, with costs ranging from 0.04 to 22.36, confirming that explicit constraint modeling is essential for safe behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Inverse Constraint Learning (ICL) line of work (Scobee & Sastry, 2020; Malik et al., 2021) by shifting the objective from recovering a single constraint to learning a diverse feasible constraint set, building on the theoretical feasible-set framework from IRL (Metelli et al., 2021) that had not been practically instantiated for ICL. The key architectural departure is the Generative Diffusion Verifier, which repurposes the Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022) guided-denoising framework as an in-context constraint verifier, eliminating the bi-level optimization (iterative policy + constraint updates) that made prior ICL solvers computationally expensive. The noise-robust constraint objective and the InfoNCE-based strategic exploration (van den Oord et al., 2018) are novel loss designs that enable the constraint model to both guide diffusion denoising under noise and produce diverse feasible solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing ICL as a feasible-set problem rather than a point-estimation problem, ExICL offers practitioners a practical workaround for the fundamental ill-posedness of constraint inference: instead of betting on one 'correct' constraint, they can select from a verified set based on deployment requirements. The GDV's in-context verification (no policy parameter updates) is a genuinely useful efficiency technique that could transfer to other inverse-problem settings. However, the method still assumes known rewards, the tested environments (PointMaze, MuJoCo locomotion, CommonRoad) are relatively low-dimensional, and the theoretical guarantees on feasible-set coverage or the quality of contrastive diversity remain open. Scaling to high-dimensional, partially observable, or multi-agent settings is a natural but unaddressed next step.
