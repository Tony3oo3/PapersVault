## Problem

Standard RLHF methods (DPO, IPO, OAIF) do not incorporate principled uncertainty estimation in the reward function, despite optimism and pessimism being well-established in standard RL. Constructing confidence intervals for arbitrary neural networks—let alone LLMs—is intractable, leaving a gap between the theoretical need for uncertainty-aware reward estimation and practical implementability in large-scale preference alignment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairwise preference data (x, y+, y−) collected either offline from a fixed dataset or online via iterative sampling from the current policy, along with a reference policy π_ref (typically the SFT model) and a fixed calibration policy π_cal that resolves the shift ambiguity inherent in the Bradley-Terry model. The output is a directly optimized LLM policy π_VPO that requires no explicit reward model at inference time, following the same single-step pipeline as DPO. The calibration policy is the key design choice: setting it to π_ref recovers a KL-regularized DPO objective, while other choices (e.g., the marginalized positive-answer distribution) recover objectives similar to DPOP.

## Method

VPO regularizes the maximum-likelihood estimate of the Bradley-Terry reward with the optimal value function J*(r), signed positive for online (optimism) and negative for offline (pessimism). The critical technical step is showing that J*(r) can be expressed in closed form as −β E_{x∼ρ, y∼π_cal}[log π_r(y|x) − log π_ref(y|x)] by exploiting the KL-regularized optimal policy's closed form and the calibration constraint that centers the reward. Substituting this into the regularized MLE and reparameterizing through the policy yields a direct optimization objective over π that is DPO's loss plus a sign-weighted KL term against the calibration policy—no explicit reward model or confidence interval is needed. Theoretical analysis under linear reward approximation yields Õ(√T) cumulative regret for online VPO (matching UCB bandit rates) and Õ(1/√N) sub-optimality for offline VPO (matching pessimistic offline RL rates), with the offline guarantee further characterized via a saddle-point formulation that connects VPO to worst-case value maximization over an implicit confidence set.

## Results

In the iterative online setting on AlpacaEval 2.0, VPO (Zephyr-7B-SFT base) achieves a length-controlled (LC) Win Rate of 22.53, a 14.52-point improvement over the SFT baseline, beating DPO and approaching much larger models (Yi-34B-Chat: 27.19; Llama-3-70B-Instruct: 33.17). On MT-Bench, VPO improves over Zephyr-7B-SFT by 2.32 points while also beating DPO. In the offline ARC-Challenge setting, pessimistic VPO consistently outperforms IPO and avoids the over-optimization degradation observed in DPO across LLAMA2-7B-CHAT, LLAMA2-13B-CHAT, and FLAN-T5-XL. In the online TL;DR buffer setting, optimistic VPO achieves higher head-to-head win rates against Online DPO, with gains increasing with the exploration weight α.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), VPO wins on all evaluated benchmarks: in the offline ARC-Challenge setting, VPO avoids the over-optimization collapse that DPO exhibits as training progresses, maintaining stable accuracy across LLAMA2-7B-CHAT, LLAMA2-13B-CHAT, and FLAN-T5-XL; in the iterative online setting, VPO achieves 22.53 LC Win Rate on AlpacaEval 2.0 versus DPO's lower score, and also beats DPO on MT-Bench. Against IPO (Azar et al., 2024), VPO demonstrates significantly better accuracy on ARC-Challenge across all three model architectures. Against Online DPO / OAIF (Guo et al., 2024) in the TL;DR buffer setting, optimistic VPO achieves higher head-to-head win rates, with the advantage growing as the exploration weight α increases from 0.01 to 0.1. Against iterative post-training baselines SPIN (Chen et al., 2024), DNO (Rosset et al., 2024), and SPPO (Wu et al., 2024), VPO trained on the weaker Zephyr-7B-SFT base achieves comparable results on AlpacaEval 2.0 and MT-Bench, despite starting from a less capable model.

## Relation to Prior Work

VPO extends the DPO framework by adding a value-incentivized regularization term derived from reward-biased maximum likelihood estimation, a technique well-established in standard online RL (Liu et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2024a) but previously unexplored in the RLHF context. The key conceptual departure is showing that this regularization implicitly implements optimism (online) or pessimism (offline) without requiring explicit confidence intervals, which are intractable for LLM-scale neural networks. The calibration policy π_cal resolves the Bradley-Terry shift ambiguity and connects VPO to existing conservative offline RL methods (CQL) and regularized RLHF variants (DPOP), providing a unified theoretical lens for these seemingly distinct approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VPO offers a practically simple modification to DPO (adding a single KL term to a calibration policy) that provably addresses the over-optimization problem in offline RLHF and enables principled exploration in online RLHF, with regret/sample-complexity guarantees matching standard RL counterparts. The saddle-point characterization elegantly bridges the gap between reward-biased MLE and the standard pessimism formulation, validating popular heuristic practices in offline RL. Open questions include adaptive selection of the regularization strength α without prior information, refined analysis of the calibration policy choice, and extending the linear function approximation analysis to general neural network settings where the practical gains are most relevant.
