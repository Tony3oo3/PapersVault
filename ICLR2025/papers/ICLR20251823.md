## Problem

Existing learning-based Large Neighborhood Search (LNS) policies for MIP are limited to binary variables and struggle with general integer variables whose wide value ranges make direct optimization intractable. Moreover, even for binary problems, learned LNS policies can wrongly fix important variables early in the search, trapping the solution in local optima. This paper addresses both gaps by extending LNS to general MIP and adding a global-view branching mechanism to correct erroneous early fixes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a MIP instance (binary, general integer, and continuous variables with linear constraints and objective) plus an initial feasible solution from an off-the-shelf solver (SCIP or Gurobi). General integer variables are first binarized into d = ⌈log₂(ub−lb)⌉ substitute binary variables, and the entire instance is encoded as a tripartite graph (variable, constraint, objective nodes). At each iteration, the method outputs tightened variable bounds and a set of variables to re-optimize, which are fed back to the baseline solver to produce an improved feasible solution; iterations continue until a time limit is reached.

## Method

BTBS-LNS combines three key techniques. First, a 'Binarized Tightening' scheme encodes each general integer variable as a binary bit sequence and uses per-bit LNS decisions to iteratively tighten the variable's bounds around the current solution (rather than fixing it), with special virtual-bound handling for unbounded variables. Second, an attention-based tripartite graph captures correlations among variables, constraints, and the objective, with softmax normalization removed from the attention weights to preserve raw inter-node contributions. Third, an extra branching network—trained offline via imitation learning with cross-entropy loss—identifies wrongly-fixed backdoor variables at a global view (by contrasting LNS-fixed variables against the best-known solution or via local branching constraints) and selects up to r% of them for re-optimization at each step. The LNS policy is trained with Q-actor-critic RL, while the branching policy is trained sequentially after the LNS policy is fixed; together they form a hybrid branch-and-search loop that formulates a sub-MIP at each iteration for the baseline solver.

## Results

On the MIPLIB2017 benchmark (240 heterogeneous instances, 300s limit), BTBS-LNS achieves a 1.75% average primal gap, outperforming Gurobi (1.98%) by ~10% and SCIP (15.15%) by nearly 9×. On four binary IP problems (200s limit), BTBS-LNS-G delivers 0.35% gap on Set Covering (vs. Gurobi 0.75%), 0.05% on MIS, 1.43% on Combinatorial Auction (vs. Gurobi 1.44%), and 0.59% on Maximum Cut (vs. Gurobi 0.62%). On MIP problems (Item, AMIPLIB), BTBS-LNS-L achieves 16.82% and 4.19% gap respectively, trailing Gurobi (6.73%, 0.81%) but substantially outperforming SCIP (50.73%, 13.72%) and all LNS baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BTBS-LNS consistently outperforms all heuristic LNS baselines (U-LNS, R-LNS, DINS, GINS, RINS, RENS) by large margins across every problem; for example, on Set Covering the gap drops from 3.23–4.17% (heuristics) to 0.35% (BTBS-LNS-G). Against the strongest learning-based baselines, BTBS-LNS-G beats CL-LNS (0.35% vs. 0.92% on SC), RL-LNS (0.35% vs. 1.29% on SC), Branching/Sonnerat (0.35% vs. 1.72% on SC), and GNN-GBDT (0.35% vs. 1.78% on SC). Versus the commercial solver Gurobi, BTBS-LNS-G wins on three of four binary IP problems (SC: 0.35% vs. 0.75%; CA: 1.43% vs. 1.44%; MC: 0.59% vs. 0.62%) and ties on MIS (0.05% vs. 0%), but loses on the MIP problems Item (16.82% vs. 6.73%) and AMIPLIB (4.19% vs. 0.81%). On the heterogeneous MIPLIB2017 benchmark, BTBS-LNS achieves 1.75% gap versus Gurobi's 1.98%, a ~10% relative improvement, while beating SCIP by a factor of ~9 (1.75% vs. 15.15%). On scale-transfer instances (Table 3), BTBS-LNS generalizes well and even surpasses Gurobi on SC2, SC4, CA2, CA4, and MC4, with a particularly large margin on CA4 (1.39% vs. 12.61% gap).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learned-LNS paradigm for MIP (Wu et al. 2021, Song et al. 2020, Nair et al. 2020a), which was largely restricted to binary variables, to general integer variables via a 'Binarized Tightening' scheme that binary-encodes integer variables and iteratively tightens bounds based on bit-wise LNS decisions. It departs from Nair et al. (2020b), which also used bound tightening but directly fixed variables, by instead using the binary decisions to progressively narrow bounds around the current solution. The branching component builds on local branching (Liu et al. 2022, Sonnerat et al. 2021) but introduces a 'global branching' variant that contrasts LNS-fixed variables against the best-known solution, and restricts branching to only LNS-fixed variables rather than the full variable set. The tripartite graph encoding extends the bipartite graph representation (Gasse et al. 2019, Ding et al. 2020) by adding an explicit objective node and removing softmax normalization from attention weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for handling general integer variables in learned LNS, addressing a gap where most prior methods were limited to binary variables and underperformed on general MIP. The competitive performance with Gurobi on MIPLIB2017 (10% better average primal gap) and the scale-transfer generalization results suggest the learned policies can complement or partially replace commercial solver heuristics in time-constrained settings. Limitations include the sequential training of LNS and branching policies, the restriction of branching to binary variables only, and dependence on a baseline solver for sub-MIP resolution. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large instances (tens of thousands of variables), whether the branching mechanism can be extended to general integer variables, and whether end-to-end joint training of both policies could further improve performance.
