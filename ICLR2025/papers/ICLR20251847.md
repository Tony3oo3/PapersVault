## Problem

Chang et al. (2024) formulated integral noise warping with distribution-preserving guarantees for temporally consistent video generation from image diffusion models, but their upsampling-based algorithm incurs prohibitive computational cost in both time and memory. This limits practical deployment in iterative settings such as Schrödinger bridge models and score distillation sampling, where noise warping must be recomputed at every denoising step. The paper asks whether the infinite-resolution limit of their formulation can be computed directly in continuous space, bypassing upsampling entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251847/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a D×D Gaussian white noise image and a deformation map ψ: [0,1]²→[0,1]² (typically an optical flow field from RAFT) and produces a warped D×D noise image whose pixels remain i.i.d. standard Gaussians when ψ is injective. The deformation map need not be diffeomorphic; the particle-based variant handles non-injective maps gracefully. The algorithm extends to 3D volumetric noise by replacing the 2D bilinear kernel with a trilinear one, as demonstrated with GaussianCube.

## Method

The core insight is that, under an Eulerian (scattering) reinterpretation of the original Lagrangian gathering procedure, the prefix sum of upsampled subpixels for a fixed noise pixel converges in distribution to a Brownian bridge conditioned on that pixel's value as the upsampling resolution N→∞ (Theorem 1). Rather than explicitly instantiating N² subpixels, the algorithm samples increments of this Brownian bridge autoregressively in linear time via a simple Gaussian conditional (Eq. 2), scattering each increment to the corresponding overlapped deformed pixel region. Two partition-record algorithms compute the overlap areas: a grid-based variant that clips deformed octagons against pixel squares (exactly equivalent to HIWYN at N=∞, running 8–20× faster with 9× less memory), and a particle-based variant inspired by FLIP fluid simulation that uses a single bilinear kernel per pixel, offering a further 5× speedup and robustness to degenerate, non-injective maps. The particle variant extends to 3D by swapping the bilinear kernel for a trilinear one, enabling volumetric noise warping for 3D generative tasks.

## Results

On the church scene, the grid-based variant achieves a Warp MSE consistency of 6.955e-2 and the particle-based variant 7.057e-2, both essentially matching HIWYN (6.953e-2) while being 8.0×–19.7× faster and using 9.22× less memory. On the cat scene, HIWYN edges slightly ahead on consistency (3.810e-2 vs. 4.016e-2 grid, 4.070e-2 particle), but all three methods remain well ahead of PYoCo (5.995e-2) and Control-A-Video (4.507e-2). The particle-based variant warps a 1024×1024 noise image in ~0.0086 s on an RTX 3070 Ti GPU, compared to 35.91 s for HIWYN at N=8. Convergence analysis confirms HIWYN's 2-Wasserstein distance to the proposed method becomes statistically insignificant for N≥64, validating the infinite-resolution equivalence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HIWYN (Chang et al., 2024), the proposed method achieves statistically indistinguishable generation quality on both church and cat scenes (consistency within ~0.2e-2), while delivering an 8.0× GPU speedup (grid) or 41.7× GPU speedup (particle) and 9.22× less memory compared to HIWYN at N=8. Against PYoCo (Ge et al., 2023), both variants improve church-scene consistency by ~29% (6.955e-2 vs. 9.800e-2) and cat-scene consistency by ~33% (4.016e-2 vs. 5.995e-2), with comparable realism scores. Against Control-A-Video (Chen et al., 2023), the improvement is more pronounced: church consistency drops from 1.108e-1 to 6.955e-2 (−37%) and cat consistency from 4.507e-2 to 4.016e-2 (−11%). The interpolation-based baselines (bilinear, nearest) and naive noise strategies (random, fixed) are clearly inferior on faithfulness and realism, confirming that distribution-preserving noise warping is essential for maintaining in-distribution noise at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Chang et al. (2024) (HIWYN), which formulated distribution-preserving integral noise warping via an upsampling-based algorithm. The key conceptual move is reinterpreting HIWYN's Lagrangian gathering procedure in an Eulerian (scattering) frame, then showing that as the upsampling resolution N→∞, the prefix sums of upsampled subpixels converge to a Brownian bridge — enabling an autoregressive sampling algorithm that bypasses upsampling entirely. The particle-based variant additionally draws on FLIP particle-in-cell methods from fluid simulation (Brackbill et al., 1988) to compute overlap areas via a bilinear kernel, departing from the explicit polygon construction used in the grid-based variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orders-of-magnitude speed-up (8–41×) transforms noise warping from a computationally prohibitive subtask into a practical, near-free add-on for iterative generative pipelines like I²SB and SDS-based 3D generation, where each denoising step requires a fresh warp. The particle-based variant's robustness to non-injective deformation maps addresses a real-world failure mode of both HIWYN and the grid-based variant, since optical flow is commonly non-diffeomorphic. Open questions include whether the particle variant can capture area contraction/expansion (e.g., via Voronoi partitioning), how the method interacts with UV maps for multi-view consistency, and whether 3D volumetric noise warping yields meaningful quality gains in 3D generation beyond the demonstrated GaussianCube use case.
