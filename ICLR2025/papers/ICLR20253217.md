## Problem

Existing human motion generation methods each target a single motion type (locomotion, human-scene interaction, or human-human interaction), and no unified system exists to combine them into coherent multi-character sequences within 3D scenes. The incompatibilities between methods—differing body representations, generation conditions, guidance formats, and physical collisions—make it impractical for animators and game designers to produce diverse, plot-driven motion sequences without manual intervention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253217/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a 3D scene with semantic object labels and a long plot context (either user-written or auto-generated by a language model) as input, and produces temporally coherent 3D human motion sequences represented as 67 marker points per frame at 40 FPS, which are then retargeted onto high-quality 3D character assets via Blender. The plot context is decomposed by a Gemini 1.5 language model into per-character command lists (locomotion waypoints, scene-interaction actions, and human-human interaction prompts) that are sequentially executed by the generation modules, with each module conditioning on the last frame of the previous module's output.

## Method

The core novelty is a self-supervised scene-aware human-human interaction generator built on InterGen's cooperative diffusion denoiser, which synthesizes binary SDF points (128×128×128 grid in a 3×3×3 m box) around the motion's convex-hull walkable region using random plane-based height assignment to implicitly represent surrounding obstacles, eliminating the need for scene-annotated interaction data. The generator is modified to use a unified 67-marker-point representation, a last-frame marker condition (encoded via GRU) to prevent identity collapse between the two characters, an improved per-character local-origin canonicalization, and a body regressor that predicts SMPL/SMPL-X parameters including body shape β. Training uses a three-phase curriculum (base losses → foot/scene-penetration penalties → human-human penetration penalties) with a composite loss combining MSE, interactive, relative-orientation, velocity, foot-contact, scene-penetration, and winding-number-based human-penetration terms. Five augmentation modules—LLM-based plot comprehension, Slerp-based motion synchronization, CLIP-retrieved hand poses, speed-adjustment collision revision, and Keemap-based retargeting—ensure cross-module consistency and visual fidelity.

## Results

On the InterHuman dataset, the proposed scene-aware human-human interaction module achieves HSP of 1.6950 and HHP of 0.0742, substantially outperforming InterGen (HSP 6.6408, HHP 0.0989) and ComMDM (HSP 6.2802, HHP 0.1336) in physical compliance. In the full system evaluation on 11 Replica indoor scenes (110 samples), the system achieves HSP of 5.7529 and HHP of 0.1687, closely matching the no-interaction baseline (HSP 5.5119) while significantly reducing human-human collisions compared to systems using InterGen (HSP 9.6035, HHP 0.1774) or ComMDM (HSP 10.5532, HHP 0.2712). Foot sliding (FS) remains at 0 across all configurations, and foot penetration (FP) is 0.0047 on InterHuman.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparisons are against InterGen (Liang et al., 2024) and ComMDM (Shafir et al., 2024), the two most relevant human-human interaction generation methods. The proposed method decisively outperforms both on all four physical compliance metrics: HSP improves by 3.9× over InterGen (1.6950 vs. 6.6408) and 3.7× over ComMDM (1.6950 vs. 6.2802), while HHP improves by 25% over InterGen (0.0742 vs. 0.0989) and 45% over ComMDM (0.0742 vs. 0.1336). In the full system on Replica scenes, the advantage is even more pronounced for HSP (5.7529 vs. 9.6035 for InterGen, 10.5532 for ComMDM), confirming that the synthetic SDF condition effectively prevents scene penetration. The remaining three baselines (TEMOS, T2M, MDM) are single-person text-to-motion methods included only in the appendix (Table 3) for reference on motion quality metrics; the proposed marker-format variant matches InterGen's R-Precision Top-1 (0.411 vs. 0.423) and FID (5.878 vs. 6.281) while achieving comparable diversity, validating the feasibility of the unified marker-point representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate lines of work—reinforcement-learning-based locomotion (GAMMA), scene-aware interaction synthesis (DIMOS), and diffusion-based human-human interaction (InterGen)—into a single plot-driven pipeline. Its key conceptual departure from InterGen is the introduction of a self-supervised binary SDF condition synthesized from existing motion data, addressing the absence of scene-annotated interaction datasets without costly data collection. The system also departs from prior single-type generators by adopting a unified marker-point representation across all modules and adding LLM-based plot comprehension to translate narrative context into executable motion commands, a workflow-level contribution absent from prior motion synthesis literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The system offers a practical, end-to-end tool for anime and game designers who must orchestrate diverse motion types within long narrative sequences, reducing the need to manually chain separate generators. The self-supervised SDF strategy is a lightweight solution to the data-scarcity problem in scene-aware interaction generation, though it remains limited to binary (inside/outside) representations and flat-topped synthetic objects. Open questions include scaling beyond two characters, handling sloped or concave geometry, and whether the post-hoc collision revision can be replaced by learned collision-aware generation. The paper also notes that the human-human collision loss degrades FID and R-Precision, suggesting a tension between physical plausibility and distributional fidelity that remains unresolved.
