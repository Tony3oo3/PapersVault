## Problem

While the offline ε-net and piercing set problems have been extensively studied in discrete geometry and computational geometry, no theoretical results existed for the online ε-net problem, where ranges arrive sequentially and the algorithm must maintain a valid net with irrevocable decisions. For online piercing set, prior work required restrictive assumptions on object sizes (fatness, aspect ratio), leaving generic geometric concepts like arbitrary boxes and ellipsoids in R^d essentially unaddressed. This paper provides the first deterministic online algorithm with an optimal competitive ratio for intervals, near-optimal randomized algorithms for axis-aligned rectangles and boxes, and asymptotically optimal deterministic algorithms for boxes and ellipsoids in arbitrary dimension.

## Input / Output

For the online ε-net problem, the finite point set X ⊂ R^d is known in advance, and geometric ranges (intervals, axis-aligned rectangles, or boxes) arrive one at a time; the algorithm must maintain a subset N ⊂ X that intersects every range containing at least ε|X| points, with the constraint that points can only be added, never removed. For the online piercing set problem, the universe is the entire ambient space R^d, and geometric objects (axis-aligned boxes with side lengths in [1,M], ellipsoids with semi-axes in [1,M], or similarly-sized α-fat objects) arrive sequentially; the algorithm outputs a growing set of points that must pierce every object seen so far. Performance is measured by the competitive ratio relative to the offline optimum, and all decisions are irrevocable.

## Method

For intervals in R, ALGO-INTERVAL deterministically splits each unhit ε-heavy interval at its median points, yielding a tight Θ(log(1/ε)) competitive ratio. For axis-aligned rectangles in R², the algorithm combines a balanced binary tree over X with a random sample P of size O(ε log log(1/ε)); when an ε-heavy rectangle is unhit by P, it identifies the highest tree node whose splitting line intersects the rectangle, extends a sub-rectangle to form a maximal P-unhit open rectangle in the corresponding strip, and adds a precomputed 1/w_M safety-net (constructed via the Haussler–Welzl ε-net theorem) to the output, achieving O(log(1/ε)) expected competitive ratio. This extends to R³ via a three-level range tree, giving O(log³(1/ε)). For online piercing of boxes and ellipsoids in R^d, ALGO-CENTER simply adds the center of each unhit object; the analysis partitions the region around each offline-optimal point into O(log M) annular regions (nested hypercubes or concentric balls) and shows at most a constant number of algorithm points per region, yielding an asymptotically tight O(log M) competitive ratio. For α-fat objects, ALGO-FAT assigns each object to a size layer and selects the nearest lattice point from a layer-specific grid, slightly improving the prior O((2/α+2)^d log M) bound to O((2/α+7/8)^d log M).

## Results

The paper establishes the first deterministic online ε-net algorithm for intervals in ℝ with a tight competitive ratio of 2(log(1/ε)+1), matching the Ω(log(1/ε)+1) lower bound. For axis-aligned rectangles in ℝ² and boxes in ℝ³, randomized online ε-net algorithms achieve competitive ratios of O(log(1/ε)) and O(log³(1/ε)) respectively — the first known online upper bounds for these settings. For online piercing sets, ALGO-CENTER achieves an asymptotically tight O(log M) competitive ratio for arbitrary axis-aligned boxes and ellipsoids in ℝ^d (any fixed d), matching the Ω(log M) lower bound for hypercubes/balls. For α-fat objects, ALGO-FAT improves the prior O((2/α+2)^d log M) bound to O((2/α+7/8)^d log M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For online ε-nets, no prior theoretical upper bounds existed for any geometric object in the online setting, making all results novel. The interval result is the only fully tight bound (matching upper and lower). For online piercing sets, the box and ellipsoid results are the first online algorithms for these object families — no prior algorithm existed even for rectangles in ℝ² or ellipses in ℝ². The α-fat object result improves upon De et al. (2024a), who achieved O((2/α+2)^d log M), by tightening the constant from 2 to 7/8 inside the exponent base, narrowing the gap to the Ω(d log M + 2^d) lower bound. The O(log M) piercing bounds for boxes and ellipsoids are asymptotically tight, matching the Ω(log M) randomized lower bound established by De et al. (2024b) for hypercubes and balls.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the offline epsilon-net machinery of Haussler & Welzl (1987) and the balanced-binary-tree / maximal-unhit-rectangle construction of Aronov et al. (2009) to the online setting, where ranges arrive sequentially and decisions are irrevocable. For online piercing sets, it extends the lattice-based ALGO-FAT of De et al. (2024a) to slightly improve the competitive ratio for α-fat objects, and introduces a center-based algorithm (ALGO-CENTER) that handles arbitrary boxes and ellipsoids—object classes for which no online piercing algorithm was previously known. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the shift from offline (all ranges known) to online (ranges revealed one-by-one), which fundamentally changes the analysis from existence proofs to competitive-ratio bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a notable gap: despite epsilon-nets being a 40+ year-old topic in discrete geometry and learning theory, no theoretical results existed for the online variant. The asymptotically tight competitive ratios (Θ(log 1/ε) for intervals, O(log M) for boxes/ellipsoids) establish that the online setting does not incur a fundamentally worse penalty than the offline one for these object classes. Key open questions remain: extending the epsilon-net algorithm beyond d ≤ 3 (where the number of maximal unhit boxes grows super-linearly), removing the dimension dependence from piercing-set competitive ratios, and handling arbitrary ε > 0 without the current regime restriction.
