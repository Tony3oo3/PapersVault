## Problem

Standard multimodal contrastive learning (e.g., CLIP) maps each input to a single point in a finite-dimensional latent space, limiting the similarity to a bilinear form of rank at most d+1. The paper shows that the optimal similarity for the symmetric InfoNCE loss is the pointwise mutual information (PMI), whose full structure generally requires unbounded rank, making exact capture infeasible with single-point embeddings. This gap between achievable and optimal similarity directly degrades downstream linear classification performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed by ViT-B/16 into 197 patch tokens) and text sequences (processed by a 12-layer, 512-wide transformer with BPE, max length 77) as inputs. Instead of outputting a single 512-d vector, each encoder produces a weighted point set: M pairs of (scalar weight, 512-d vector), where M equals the number of token positions (197 for images, 77 for text). For downstream classification, the weighted point set is collapsed to a single embedding by concatenating the weighted sum of raw vectors with the weighted sum of Random Fourier Feature projections, yielding a fixed-dimensional vector suitable for linear probes.

## Method

The core contribution replaces single-point embeddings with weighted point sets, defining similarity between two sets as a kernel-weighted sum over all cross-pairs: g = Σᵢⱼ wᵢ⁽ˣ⁾ wⱼ⁽ʸ⁾ k(vᵢ⁽ˣ⁾, vⱼ⁽ʸ⁾). Theoretically, the paper proves that with a c₀-universal kernel (e.g., Gaussian or IMQ), this class of similarities can approximate the pointwise mutual information to arbitrary precision, unlike the rank-limited inner product. Practically, the nonlinear kernel is approximated via Random Fourier Features to reduce the O(M²) pairwise computation to a single inner product of collapsed embeddings [√α₁·v̄, √α₂·z̄]. Encoders are modified minimally: all transformer output positions (not just [CLS]/[EOS]) are retained as the point set, and a parallel projection layer produces the weights. Training uses the standard symmetric InfoNCE loss with the new similarity, pretrained on CC3M/CC12M, and the combination of a linear kernel (α₁) and nonlinear kernel (α₂) is essential—ablations show positive-only weights or purely nonlinear kernels fail to train.

## Results

On zero-shot classification with CC12M pretraining, WPSE Gaussian achieves 46.12% average accuracy across 13 benchmarks, outperforming CLIP (43.78%) by 2.34 points, with notable gains on CIFAR-10 (81.33% vs 74.17%) and STL-10 (91.25% vs 90.91%). On linear classification with CC12M, WPSE Gaussian reaches 79.08% average vs CLIP's 77.89%, and in the intermediate-embedding setting ('bef'), WPSE IMQ achieves 82.71% vs CLIP's 81.03%. With the smaller CC3M dataset, WPSE IMQ improves zero-shot average from 25.03% (CLIP) to 27.04% and linear average from 67.00% to 68.23%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), trained from scratch with the same architecture (ViT-B/16 image encoder, 12-layer text Transformer) and training protocol. WPSE consistently outperforms CLIP on average across both pretraining scales: on CC12M zero-shot, WPSE Gaussian gains +2.34 points (46.12% vs 43.78%), and on CC12M linear classification, WPSE Gaussian gains +1.19 points (79.08% vs 77.89%). The largest individual-dataset gains appear on CIFAR-10 (+7.16 points zero-shot on CC12M), STL-10 (+5.37 points zero-shot on CC3M), and Oxford Pets (+9.12 points zero-shot on CC12M). WPSE does not win on every dataset—e.g., on CC12M zero-shot, CLIP retains an edge on DTD (22.45% vs 21.17%) and SUN397 (49.72% vs 49.57%)—but the average improvement is consistent. In the linear classification 'bef' setting (intermediate embeddings), WPSE IMQ achieves the best average (82.71% on CC12M) vs CLIP (81.03%), with the largest gap on Cars (+4.67 points) and Aircraft (+4.38 points).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP/ALIGN multimodal contrastive learning framework by replacing single-point embeddings with weighted point sets and cosine similarity with a kernel-based similarity over those sets. Theoretically, it builds on the known result that the optimal solution of symmetric InfoNCE corresponds to pointwise mutual information (PMI), but departs from prior excess-risk analyses (Saunshi et al., 2019; HaoChen et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2022) by providing a bound on the gap to the optimal classifier rather than a one-sided upper bound, and by analyzing the actual CLIP setting rather than modified losses. The kernel-based similarity connects to the RKHS literature (Muandet et al., 2017; Sriperumbudur et al., 2011) and to recent attempts to enrich contrastive similarity (Fürst et al., 2022 with Hopfield networks; Desai et al., 2023 with hyperbolic distance), but uniquely provides a universal approximation guarantee for the similarity class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rank-limitation argument (inner-product similarity in d dimensions cannot capture PMI structure for N > d+1 instances) gives a clean, principled motivation for moving beyond single-point representations, and the universal approximation theorem shows weighted point sets with a c0-universal kernel can close this gap in principle. However, the practical improvements over CLIP are modest (roughly 1–2 points average on zero-shot and linear benchmarks), and the method adds computational overhead from M points per input and RFF approximation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger pretraining datasets (the paper only uses CC3M/CC12M with ViT-B/16), how the RFF dimension D should be chosen in practice, and whether the theoretical assumptions (particularly Assumption C.2 on low-dimensional latent generation) hold for real-world multimodal data.
