## Problem

Existing representational similarity measures compare image representations via global geometric alignment over distant stimuli, but models with similar global structure can have strikingly different local geometries. While generalized eigen-distortions (Zhou et al., 2023) can distinguish a pair of models via their Fisher Information Matrices, no principled method existed for selecting distortions that optimally differentiate more than two models simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251979/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a base RGB image and a set of N deterministic or stochastic image representation models (e.g., DNN layers, hand-crafted early vision models). For deterministic models, the FIM is computed as J_f(s)ᵀ J_f(s) under an additive Gaussian noise assumption. The output is a pair of principal distortion vectors (ε₁, ε₂) of target magnitude α=0.1, constrained so that s+1000ε remains within valid pixel range, along with the per-model log sensitivity ratios that quantify how each model's sensitivity to ε₁ compares to its sensitivity to ε₂.

## Method

The paper defines a pseudo-metric m_{u,v}(I_A, I_B) = |log(d_A(u)/d_A(v)) − log(d_B(u)/d_B(v))| on pairs of FIMs, where d(ε)=√(εᵀIε) is the Fisher-Rao sensitivity. This metric is invariant to positive rescaling of the FIMs and, for two extremal generalized eigenvectors, approximates the Fisher-Rao distance between mean-zero Gaussians. To extend beyond two models, the principal distortions are found by maximizing the sum of squared pairwise m-distances across all N models, which is equivalent to maximizing the variance of the log sensitivity ratios. A projected gradient descent algorithm (Algorithm 1) optimizes the distortion pair, with per-step normalization to a target distortion size. Applied to early visual models and DNNs (AlexNet, ResNet50, ViT, EfficientNet), the method reveals interpretable differences: AlexNet is more sensitive to high-frequency/textured regions while ResNet50 targets smooth regions, and adversarial training shifts sensitivity toward structured color changes rather than unstructured noise.

## Results

The paper introduces a method that generates exactly 2 principal distortions to differentiate N models, compared to 2N distortions required by eigen-distortions (Berardino et al., 2017) and N(N+1) distortions for generalized eigen-distortions (Zhou et al., 2023). Applied to four nested early visual models (LN, LG, LGG, LGN), the principal distortions reveal that LGN and LN models are closest to human distortion thresholds, while LG and LGG show strong mismatches. For DNNs, the method consistently separates AlexNet from ResNet50 across 100 ImageNet base images: AlexNet is more sensitive to distortions concentrated on high-frequency/textured regions (ε₁), while ResNet50 is more sensitive to distortions targeting smooth/constant-intensity regions (ε₂). This architecture-based separation persists for Stylized ImageNet (SIN)-trained variants, but adversarial training flips the separation to be by training type rather than architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing methods on a standard task; instead, it positions its principal distortions as a strict generalization of two prior approaches. Compared to eigen-distortions (Berardino et al., 2017), which compute extremal FIM eigenvectors per model and require 2N distortions for N models, principal distortions always require only 2 distortions regardless of N, and can differentiate more than two models simultaneously. Compared to generalized eigen-distortions (Zhou et al., 2023), which extremize the ratio of two models' FIMs and require N(N+1) distortions for all pairwise comparisons, principal distortions maximize the variance of log sensitivity ratios across all N models in a single optimization. The paper demonstrates that its method reveals interpretable differences (e.g., AlexNet's bias toward textured regions vs. ResNet50's bias toward smooth regions) that are not captured by the simpler per-model or pairwise approaches, and that the resulting distortions are consistent across 100 base images and replicate across multiple random initializations of each architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FIM-based distortion framework from Berardino et al. (2017), who defined eigen-distortions for individual models, and Zhou et al. (2023), who defined generalized eigen-distortions for pairs of models, to the N-model setting. The key conceptual step is recognizing that the generalized eigenvalue problem induces a pseudo-metric on local geometries (invariant to FIM scaling), and that maximizing the sum of squared pairwise distances under this metric is equivalent to maximizing variance of log sensitivity ratios—analogous to PCA. The paper also situates its metric as an approximation of the Fisher-Rao distance between mean-zero Gaussians, providing a principled connection to information geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method dramatically reduces the number of distortions needed to differentiate N models from O(N²) or O(N) to a fixed 2, making psychophysics-based model comparison far more tractable. The empirical findings—that AlexNet and ResNet50 exhibit qualitatively different local sensitivities to high- vs. low-frequency image regions, and that adversarial training (but not stylized training) reorganizes local geometry by training type rather than architecture—reveal structure invisible to global representational similarity measures. Limitations include the local linear approximation (invalid for highly nonlinear models near the base image), the Gaussian noise assumption for deterministic models, and the restriction to two distortions; natural extensions to more distortions and continuous model families are outlined but not pursued.
