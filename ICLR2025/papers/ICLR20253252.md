## Problem

State classification of objects and their relations is essential for robot planning, but the combinatorial explosion of object-predicate combinations makes it intractable to collect training data for all pairs. Current supervised methods struggle to generalize to novel predicates with only a few examples, and pretrained vision-language models fail on challenging spatial relationship queries in robotics environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253252/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D RGB image and a text state query (e.g., 'NextTo(cup, plate)') as input. The query is parsed into its constituent objects and predicate, which are processed separately: objects drive image region localization via CLIP-based masking, while the predicate conditions the latent representation via BERT. The output is a binary True/False prediction of whether the predicate holds for the specified objects in the image.

## Method

PHIER constructs a joint image-predicate latent space structured by an inferred predicate hierarchy. An object-centric encoder (CLIP-based masking + ViT) localizes relevant objects, and a BERT-encoded predicate is concatenated to form the scene representation. Two self-supervised losses inject semantic structure: a predicate triplet loss (with anchor/positive/negative assignments inferred by an LLM) enforces similarity between related predicates, and a norm regularization loss (with specificity rankings from the LLM) enforces that more specific predicates have larger norms. The entire representation is lifted onto the Poincaré ball via an exponential map and processed by hyperbolic linear layers, so that the hierarchical predicate structure is encoded in the continuous geometry of hyperbolic space. This structured latent space enables few-shot adaptation to novel predicates by leveraging their inferred relationships to already-learned predicates.

## Results

On the 5-shot out-of-distribution (OOD) state classification task, PHIER achieves 89.9% accuracy on CALVIN and 82.0% on BEHAVIOR, outperforming the best prior supervised method (Re-Attention at 67.4% on CALVIN, RelViT at 73.7% on BEHAVIOR) by 22.5 and 8.3 percentage points respectively. PHIER also shows the smallest ID-to-OOD performance drop (4.6% on CALVIN, 3.9% on BEHAVIOR), indicating robust generalization. On the real-world BEHAVIOR Vision Suite, PHIER trained solely on simulated data achieves 60.8% zero-shot and 70.3% few-shot accuracy, surpassing all supervised baselines by 7 and 10 percentage points respectively, and narrowing the gap to GPT-4V from 10.4% (zero-shot) to just 0.9% (few-shot).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PHIER decisively outperforms all seven supervised baselines on the OOD setting: it beats Re-Attention (the strongest supervised method on CALVIN OOD at 67.4%) by 22.5 points and RelViT (the strongest on BEHAVIOR OOD at 73.7%) by 8.3 points. On in-distribution accuracy, PHIER is competitive but not always the best—Re-Attention edges it out by 1.4% on CALVIN ID and RelViT by 0.7% on BEHAVIOR ID—yet PHIER's ID-to-OOD gap is dramatically smaller (4.6% vs. 28.5% for Re-Attention on CALVIN). Against inference-only VLMs, PHIER surpasses GPT-4V, BLIP-2, and ViperGPT on both CALVIN and BEHAVIOR OOD accuracy, with GPT-4V being the only VLM that occasionally exceeds PHIER on BEHAVIOR OOD (70.6% vs. 82.0%—actually PHIER still wins). On the real-world BEHAVIOR Vision Suite, PHIER (zero-shot 60.8%, few-shot 70.3%) outperforms all supervised baselines (best: RelViT at 55.6% zero-shot, 60.3% few-shot) and beats ViperGPT and BLIP-2 by 6.0% and 1.4% in the zero-shot setting, though GPT-4V (71.2% zero-shot) still leads by 10.4% before the gap shrinks to 0.9% in the few-shot setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

PHIER sits at the intersection of three research threads: object-centric scene understanding (building on MaskCLIP-style localization and SORNet's object embeddings), VQA architectures (BUTD, Re-Attention, FiLM, RelViT), and hyperbolic representation learning (Nickel & Kiela, Ganea et al., Desai et al.). The key departure from prior state classification work (notably SORNet) is that PHIER does not require a separate readout network per predicate or object-specific input images; instead, it learns a single joint image-predicate latent space structured by an LLM-inferred predicate hierarchy. The use of hyperbolic geometry here is novel in application—prior hyperbolic embedding work targets image or word hierarchies, whereas PHIER encodes the semantic specificity ordering of spatial/relational predicates in a Poincaré ball, guided by self-supervised triplet and norm-regularization losses whose labels come from GPT-4.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that injecting a soft, language-inferred predicate hierarchy into a continuous latent space yields a small ID-to-OOD performance gap (0.046 on CALVIN) that no flat-representation baseline achieves, suggesting structured representation geometry is a viable lever for few-shot generalization in embodied AI. The sim-to-real transfer results (a small model trained only on simulation beating supervised real-world baselines by 7–10 points) are practically relevant for robotics where real data is scarce. Open questions include whether the LLM-derived predicate relations are the bottleneck (the authors acknowledge this), whether the hierarchy can be learned end-to-end without language priors, and whether the approach scales to the thousands of predicates that would appear in open-world settings.
