## Problem

LLMs remain vulnerable to a wide range of adversarial jailbreak attacks, yet existing adversarial training defenses are computationally prohibitive, often requiring thousands of model evaluations per training step to simulate attacks. A key missing piece is a mechanistic understanding of what these diverse attacks have in common, which would enable a single efficient training procedure to counter them all.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250192/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ReFAT consumes pairs of (harmful instruction, refusal response) and (harmless instruction, helpful response) as text, fine-tuning via LoRA on standard transformer LLMs (Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-7B). During each training forward pass, the model's intermediate residual stream activations for harmful inputs are modified in-place by projecting out the refusal feature direction before the loss is computed, so the model must learn to refuse based on features beyond the single most salient safety signal. At inference time, no activation modification is applied—the model simply generates a text response (refusal or helpful answer) to a user prompt.

## Method

The paper first shows via interpretability analysis that four diverse attack families (GCG, PAIR, AutoDAN, HumanJailbreaks) all jailbreak LLMs by shifting residual stream activations along the negative refusal feature direction—a single vector computed as the difference-in-means of hidden representations between harmful and harmless prompts. It then proves that ablating this direction (RFA) approximates the worst-case adversarial perturbation that minimizes the model's safety score, making it a cheap surrogate for expensive gradient-based attack search. ReFAT exploits this by performing supervised fine-tuning on refusal data where, with probability p_RFA, the refusal feature is dynamically recomputed every k steps from fresh random samples and subtracted from the residual stream of every harmful input during the forward pass. This forces the model to encode safety in redundant, distributed features rather than relying on a single ablatable direction, yielding robustness gains at only 1.5× the forward-pass cost of standard fine-tuning and no additional backward passes—roughly 1,700× cheaper than R2D2 and 10× cheaper than CAT/LAT.

## Results

ReFAT reduces average attack success rate (ASR) across all four discrete attacks and the continuous RFA attack for all three LLMs while preserving general capability. For Llama-3-8B, ReFAT cuts GCG ASR from 27.1% to 13.3%, PAIR from 29.9% to 11.5%, and RFA from 53.3% to 10.4%; for Gemma-7B, RFA ASR drops from 92.8% to 18.8%. General utility is largely maintained (e.g., Llama-3-8B MMLU 65.2 vs. 65.9 baseline, XSTest compliance 97.6 vs. 97.2). In terms of efficiency, ReFAT requires only 1.5 additional forward passes and no extra backward passes per step, making it ~1,700× cheaper than R2D2 and ~10× cheaper than CAT/LAT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against R2D2 (Mazeika et al., 2024), ReFAT achieves substantially lower RFA ASR (10.4% vs. 43.9% on Llama-3-8B; 18.8% vs. 44.6% on Mistral-7B) and lower PAIR ASR (11.5% vs. 19.4% on Llama), while requiring only 1.5/1 forward/backward passes versus R2D2's 2566/6. Against CAT (Xhonneux et al., 2024), ReFAT wins decisively on the RFA attack (10.4% vs. 36.5% on Llama; 18.8% vs. 64.4% on Gemma) and on PAIR (11.5% vs. 16.1% on Llama), though CAT edges out ReFAT slightly on GCG (11.8% vs. 13.3% on Llama) and AutoDAN (0.77% vs. 0.85%). Against LAT (Casper et al., 2024; Sheshadri et al., 2024), ReFAT dominates on RFA (10.4% vs. 45.0% on Llama; 18.8% vs. N/A on Gemma) and PAIR (11.5% vs. 20.7% on Llama), while LAT achieves lower AutoDAN ASR (0.10% vs. 0.85% on Llama) and slightly lower GCG ASR (9.06% vs. 13.3%). Overall, ReFAT is the only method that effectively defends against the continuous RFA attack across all three models, and it does so at roughly 10× lower computational cost than CAT/LAT and ~1,700× lower than R2D2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate threads: the interpretability discovery that LLM refusal is mediated by a single linear direction in the residual stream (Arditi et al., 2024), and the adversarial training literature that typically relies on expensive gradient-based or search-based attack simulation (R2D2, CAT, LAT). The key conceptual departure is showing that diverse attack families—gradient-based (GCG), LLM-iterative (PAIR), genetic (AutoDAN), and human-crafted (HumanJailbreaks)—all operate through the same mechanistic lever of ablating the refusal feature, thereby unifying what appeared to be heterogeneous jailbreaking strategies under one geometric explanation. ReFAT then repurposes this interpretability insight as an efficient training-time perturbation, replacing costly adversarial search with a single projection operation per forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of diverse attacks under a single refusal-feature-ablation mechanism provides a principled, interpretable target for defense design, and the 1,700× computational savings over R2D2 make adversarial training of LLMs far more accessible. However, the method's reliance on English-only instruction sets for computing refusal features leaves a clear vulnerability to multilingual and code-switching attacks, as demonstrated by the GCG failures in Table 3. Open questions include whether semantically and linguistically diverse RF computation closes this gap, whether the single-direction assumption holds for larger or more heterogeneous models, and whether the approach generalizes beyond refusal to other safety-relevant behaviors (e.g., truthfulness, toxicity).
