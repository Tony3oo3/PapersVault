## Problem

Conventional transformer-based ECC decoders (ECCT) process concatenated magnitude and syndrome vectors as a single input via self-attention, failing to exploit their distinct informational roles (bit reliability vs. error-position indicators) and incurring high memory and computational costs from large (2n-k)² attention maps. This limits their applicability to short codes and leaves the question of how to efficiently and effectively model the magnitude-syndrome relationship unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The decoder receives the noisy AWGN channel output y ∈ ℝⁿ, from which it extracts an n-dimensional real-valued magnitude vector |y| and an (n-k)-dimensional binary syndrome vector s(y) = Hy_b. These are projected into separate d-dimensional embeddings of different lengths (n and n-k rows), processed through N decoder layers, and finally concatenated and mapped to an n-dimensional output estimating the multiplicative noise z̃_s, from which the decoded codeword x = bin(sign(y·f(y))) is recovered.

## Method

CrossMPT replaces ECCT's single masked self-attention block with two masked cross-attention blocks per decoder layer that iteratively update magnitude and syndrome embeddings in a message-passing fashion. In the first block, the magnitude embedding serves as query and the syndrome embedding as key/value, with the PCM transpose H^T as mask (attention map size n×(n-k)); in the second block, the roles swap—syndrome as query, updated magnitude as key/value—masked by H itself. This design mirrors sum-product message passing between variable and check nodes on the Tanner graph while reducing total attention map size to at most half that of ECCT. The two blocks share weight matrices W_Q, W_K, W_V and all other layer parameters, keeping the parameter count identical to ECCT. Training uses binary cross-entropy on the estimated multiplicative noise, with the same Adam optimizer, cosine LR schedule, and 1000-epoch protocol as ECCT for fair comparison.

## Results

The primary metric is the negative natural logarithm of BER (higher is better), evaluated at Eb/N0 = 4, 5, and 6 dB. CrossMPT (N=6, d=128) consistently outperforms ECCT across all code classes, with the largest gains on LDPC codes: e.g., (121,80) LDPC achieves 18.15 vs. 16.44 at 6 dB, and (121,70) LDPC achieves 17.52 vs. 16.11. For short BCH codes, CrossMPT closely approaches optimal ML decoding performance. Additionally, CrossMPT reduces FLOPs by ~10–50%, inference time by ~5–20%, and memory usage by up to ~80% (e.g., 3270 MiB vs. 18192 MiB for the (384,320) WRAN LDPC code) compared to ECCT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrossMPT outperforms all five baselines across every code class tested. Against the conventional BP decoder, CrossMPT achieves superior BER for LDPC codes even when BP is allowed up to 100 iterations (Appendix B), e.g., 18.15 vs. ~16 at 6 dB for (121,80) LDPC. Against the neural BP variants (Hyp BP and AR BP), CrossMPT leads by 1–3 in −ln BER at 6 dB across all codes, with the gap widening for longer LDPC codes. Against ECCT (the primary architectural baseline), CrossMPT improves by 0.5–2.8 in −ln BER at 6 dB, with the largest gains on LDPC codes (e.g., +1.71 for (121,80) LDPC) and Polar codes (e.g., +2.79 for (128,86) Polar). Against DDECCT (denoising diffusion ECCT), CrossMPT shows comparable performance on Polar codes but clearly outperforms on BCH and LDPC codes; when the denoising diffusion training technique is additionally applied to CrossMPT, it achieves the best overall performance among all four model-free decoders (Table 5).

## Relation to Prior Work

CrossMPT builds directly on ECCT (Choukroun & Wolf, 2022a), the first model-free transformer decoder for ECCs, by replacing its single masked self-attention block over concatenated magnitude and syndrome embeddings with two masked cross-attention blocks that process these embeddings separately. The architectural design is explicitly inspired by the sum-product message-passing algorithm on Tanner graphs: the two cross-attention blocks mirror the iterative variable-node/check-node message exchange, with the parity-check matrix H and its transpose H^T serving as natural mask matrices. This departs from ECCT's less principled depth-2 magnitude-magnitude masking and aligns with the broader trend of leveraging cross-attention for multimodal or multi-stream processing seen in NLP decoders, CrossViT, and latent diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CrossMPT's reduced attention map size (2n(n−k) vs. (2n−k)²) enables decoding of codes (e.g., (1056,880) WiMAX LDPC) that are infeasible for ECCT due to memory constraints, while simultaneously reducing FLOPs, training time (up to ~10×), and inference time. The paper demonstrates that CrossMPT outperforms the BP decoder for LDPC codes and closely approaches ML decoding for short BCH codes, positioning it as a practical universal decoder across code classes. Open questions include a rigorous analysis of how attention scores relate to Tanner graph structure, the throughput limitation of the sequential cross-attention pipeline (partially addressed by pipelining), and whether combining CrossMPT with foundation-model or joint encoder-decoder training approaches yields further gains.
