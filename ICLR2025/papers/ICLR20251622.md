## Problem

Existing reward models for vision-language models assign a single binary score to entire text outputs, obscuring where errors occur and making them uninterpretable. In multimodal settings, these naive models can develop implicit biases toward text length rather than visual grounding, reducing their effectiveness at detecting hallucinations. The paper asks whether token-level reward signals can provide both fine-grained interpretability and stronger visual grounding for VLM evaluation and alignment.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a triple (image, text prompt, text response), where the image is encoded into 1024 visual tokens at 448×448 (PaliGemma) or 1120×1120 (Llama-3.2) resolution. For each token in the response, the model outputs a binary prediction (good/bad) via a sigmoid on a scalar logit, from which sentence-level and response-level hallucination rates are derived by aggregating over period-delimited sentences or the full response. The token-level labels are highly imbalanced, with over 95% of tokens being positive, motivating the use of mAP(neg) as a complementary metric.

## Method

TLDR replaces the pretrained language model head of a VLM backbone (PaliGemma-3B or Llama-3.2-11B-Vision) with a randomly initialized linear reward head that maps each token's hidden state to a binary logit. Training data consists of synthetic hard negatives generated by perturbing gold captions and VQA answers with a text-only LLM (Llama-3.1-70B) across eight error taxonomies (spatial relationship, visual attribute, attribute binding, object identification, counting, small object, text OCR, counterfactual), with token-level labels derived from the edit distance between original and perturbed text. The model is trained with cross-entropy loss on every response token, using LoRA (rank 512, α=128) to update the vision-to-text projection layer and transformer decoder. A key finding is that this training automatically performs token-level likelihood optimization on the backbone VLM—promoting correct tokens and suppressing hallucinated ones—yielding improved grounding on spatial reasoning and counting benchmarks without explicit reinforcement learning.

## Results

On the DOCCI test split, TLDR (PaliGemma-3B backbone) achieves 98.6% token-level accuracy, 86.5% sentence-level accuracy, and 83.1% response-level accuracy, with mAP(neg|pos) of 41.3|99.8. The larger Llama-3.2-11B-Vision backbone improves to 98.9% token-level, 90.8% sentence-level, and 88.2% response-level accuracy. As a hallucination evaluation tool, TLDR assigns GPT-4o the lowest token-level hallucination rate (0.016%) among 10 evaluated VLMs, while PaliGemma-3B has the highest (4.444%). Training TLDR automatically improves the backbone VLM: with τ=0.25, PaliGemma-3B gains up to 3.7 points on BLINK and Llama-3.2-11B-Vision gains up to 12.5 points, while reducing PaliGemma's token-level hallucination rate from 4.444% to 0.172%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TLDR against two baselines: (1) a naive binary reward model with identical architecture but computing cross-entropy loss only at the last token, and (2) GPT-4o prompted to perform token-level detection. TLDR outperforms the naive RM on response-level accuracy by 2.0 points (83.1 vs. 81.1 for PaliGemma-3B) and 1.5 points (88.2 vs. 86.7 for Llama-3.2-11B-Vision), while also providing the additional token- and sentence-level granularity the naive model cannot. Against GPT-4o prompting, TLDR achieves substantially higher performance across all metrics: 98.6% vs. 95.5% token-level accuracy, 86.5% vs. 66.9% sentence-level accuracy, 83.1% vs. 52.9% response-level accuracy, and 41.3 vs. 19.7 mAP(neg). In the self-correction experiment (Table 3), TLDR guidance yields 12 wins / 7 ties / 2 losses for GPT-4V, compared to only 2 wins / 11 ties / 2 losses with naive RM guidance, demonstrating that token-level signals enable more effective correction. The automatic likelihood optimization (Table 4) shows TLDR training improves the backbone VLM on BLINK and IsoBench without any additional fine-tuning step, with the best gains at τ=0.25 (e.g., +12.5 on BLINK Count for Llama-3.2-11B-Vision).

## Relation to Prior Work

TLDR extends the line of token-level reward modeling from NLP (TLCR by Yoon et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024) to the vision-language domain, where prior reward models were limited to a single binary score per response. It builds directly on the perturbation-based synthetic data generation approach from Fu et al. (2023, SCENE) and the VLM architectures of PaliGemma and Llama-3.2-Vision, adding a per-token classification head on top of the frozen vision encoder. The key conceptual departure from naive binary RMs is the shift from sequence-level to token-level supervision, motivated by the observation that binary scores obscure where hallucinations occur and introduce length bias in multimodal settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TLDR provides a practical, interpretable tool for VLM hallucination detection and self-correction that could reduce reliance on expensive human annotation (3× speedup demonstrated) and serve as a foundation for token-level DPO/PPO post-training in vision-language models. The finding that TLDR training automatically improves the backbone VLM via implicit likelihood optimization is a useful by-product but remains limited to two architectures and a handful of benchmarks. Open questions include whether the product-based token-to-response aggregation can be made less brittle (spatial relationship remains the weakest taxonomy), whether the approach scales to longer-form generation, and whether token-level rewards genuinely enable on-policy RLHF improvements beyond the observed automatic likelihood gains.
