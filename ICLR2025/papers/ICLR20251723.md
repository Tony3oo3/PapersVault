## Problem

Protecting the intellectual property of open-source LLMs requires identifying whether a suspect model is derived from a victim model, but existing approaches are inadequate: watermarking methods require extra training, impair model capabilities, and can be removed, while weight-based fingerprints (PCS, ICS) are fragile to pruning, parameter permutations, and extensive fine-tuning (e.g., 700B tokens). A training-free fingerprinting method that remains robust across these diverse subsequent developments is missing.

## Input / Output

REEF takes layer activations (hidden-state representations) from a suspect model and a victim model computed on the same set of input samples (e.g., 200 samples from TruthfulQA). A key design choice is that the two models' representations can have different dimensions (p₁ ≠ p₂), enabling comparison between pruned and unpruned models without any dimension-matching step. The output is a single scalar CKA similarity score in [0, 1], where values above ~0.8 indicate the suspect model is likely derived from the victim model, and values below ~0.5 indicate it is unrelated.

## Method

REEF computes the Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) similarity between the representation matrices of the suspect and victim models on shared samples, using a linear kernel (K = XXᵀ) for efficiency. The critical theoretical contribution is Theorem 1, which proves CKA is invariant to arbitrary column-wise permutations and positive scaling transformations of either representation matrix, making the fingerprint robust to weight rearrangements and coupled matrix multiplications that malicious developers might use to evade detection. Because CKA operates on Gram matrices of pairwise sample similarities, it naturally handles mismatched representation dimensions, eliminating the need for fixed-input-dimension classifiers that fail under pruning. The method is entirely training-free: no classifier, no additional parameters, and no modification to the models are required, so it does not impair general capabilities and can be applied retroactively to already-released models.

## Results

REEF achieves a CKA similarity of 0.9962 on Llemma-7b (fine-tuned with 700B tokens), 0.9278–0.9991 on structured pruned models (up to 90% parameter reduction), and 1.0000 under column-wise permutation and scaling transformations. In contrast, PCS drops to 0.0098 at 700B fine-tuning and 0.0000 under pruning/permutation, ICS degrades to 0.2257 at 700B tokens and 0.3512 on 1.3B pruned models, and the Logits method fails entirely (0.0000) under permutation and cross-scale distribution merging. REEF is the only method that maintains >0.92 similarity across all tested scenarios including fine-tuning, pruning, merging, and adversarial permutation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REEF outperforms all three baselines (PCS, ICS from Zeng et al. 2023; Logits from Yang & Wu 2024) in every adversarial scenario tested. PCS is the most fragile: it yields 0.0000 similarity on all structured pruned models, all permutation cases, and all cross-scale distribution merging, and collapses to 0.0250 after only 1.8B tokens of fine-tuning. ICS is more robust than PCS but still degrades substantially—dropping to 0.2257 at 700B fine-tuning, 0.3512 on the 1.3B pruned model, and near-zero on cross-scale merging. The Logits method performs competitively on pruning (0.9967–0.9999) and moderate fine-tuning, but is completely defeated by permutation (0.0000) and cross-scale distribution merging (0.0000), where REEF maintains 1.0000 and 0.9278–0.9991 respectively. REEF's theoretical invariance to column permutations and scaling (Theorem 1) is confirmed empirically with perfect 1.0000 scores, a property no baseline shares.

## Relation to Prior Work

REEF repurposes Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA), a representation similarity metric from neural network analysis (Kornblith et al., 2019), as a fingerprinting tool for LLM intellectual property protection. It departs from weight-based fingerprinting (HUREF's PCS/ICS, SLIP) and watermarking methods, which are fragile to permutations, pruning, and extensive fine-tuning, by shifting the comparison from parameter space to activation space. The key conceptual move is leveraging CKA's provable invariance to column permutations and scaling (Theorem 1) as a built-in robustness guarantee, rather than relying on empirical resilience. The Logits method (Yang & Wu, 2024) is the closest prior representation-based approach, but REEF addresses its sensitivity to vocabulary changes and parameter permutations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REEF provides a training-free, capability-preserving fingerprinting method with a clean theoretical robustness guarantee, making it immediately deployable for third-party model auditing and IP enforcement without requiring model owners to pre-embed watermarks. Key limitations include its white-box requirement (access to internal representations), its focus on direct root-origin identification rather than multi-generational genealogy tracking, and the lack of empirical validation beyond 700B tokens of fine-tuning due to prohibitive compute costs. Open questions include whether REEF can be extended to closed-source models, whether it can track intermediate development stages, and how it performs as the LLM ecosystem grows with increasingly complex merging and adaptation pipelines.
