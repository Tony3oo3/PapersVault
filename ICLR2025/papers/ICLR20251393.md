## Problem

Transformer-based biological sequence models scale quadratically with sequence length, making them impractical for long genomic sequences (billions of base pairs) and in-context learning on proteins and chemical sequences. While state-space models (Mamba, Hyena) offer linear scaling, the recently proposed xLSTM architecture has outperformed SSMs in natural language processing but has not been adapted or benchmarked for biological and chemical domains. This paper asks how to best tailor xLSTM for DNA, protein, and SMILES sequences and whether it can match or exceed domain-specific baselines in generative modeling, representation learning, and in-context learning.

## Input / Output

For DNA, the model consumes single-nucleotide-resolution sequences from the human reference genome at context lengths of 1,024 (sLSTM) or 32,768 (mLSTM) tokens, producing next-token predictions or de-masked reconstructions. For proteins, input is a concatenated sequence of unaligned homologous proteins with mask patches inserted (FIM format), processed at context lengths up to 262,144 tokens; the model outputs generated residues or inpainted segments for variant fitness scoring. For chemistry, SMILES strings are processed at 100-token (unconditional) or 4,096-token (ICL) contexts, where the ICL mode concatenates exemplar molecules from a target domain before the generation prompt. Representations for downstream classification are extracted via average pooling over the final block's activations.

## Method

Bio-xLSTM introduces three domain-specific architectural variants built on xLSTM's sLSTM and mLSTM layers: DNA-xLSTM uses sLSTM blocks for short-context (1k) and mLSTM blocks with Rotary Position Encodings for long-context (32k) modeling, augmented with reverse-complement equivariant blocks via post-hoc conjoining (PH) or parameter sharing (PS); Prot-xLSTM stacks 16 mLSTM blocks with RoPE and is trained with a fill-in-the-middle (FIM) objective over concatenated unaligned homologous sequences, enabling both autoregressive generation and inpainting at inference; Chem-xLSTM uses 9 mLSTM blocks trained on SMILES strings with CLM, supporting domain-conditioned generation via in-context learning without fine-tuning. The mLSTM's linear-time parallel and chunkwise formulations enable efficient training at long contexts, while the recurrent form provides constant-memory decoding at inference. For bidirectional modeling (MLM), the paper derives an efficient single-pass computation of weight-tied forward and reverse mLSTM outputs by combining their parallel-form attention matrices, halving memory and compute relative to running the network twice.

## Results

On 18 downstream DNA classification tasks, DNA-xLSTM-2M-PH outperforms HyenaDNA and Mamba (Caduceus) on 12 of 18 tasks, e.g., achieving 0.824 MCC on H3 histone marking vs. 0.815 (Mamba-PH) and 0.779 (HyenaDNA). For homology-conditioned protein generation, Prot-xLSTM-102M achieves a test perplexity of 7.35 (all tokens) and 5.87 (FIM tokens), outperforming ProtMamba-107M (8.27 / 7.00) despite being trained on only a quarter of the tokens. For unconditional SMILES generation, Chem-xLSTM attains the best Fréchet ChemNet Distance of 0.13 (vs. 0.15 for SMILES-GPT, 0.21 for Mamba) with a competitive perplexity of 1.68.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DNA downstream classification, DNA-xLSTM-2M-PH wins 12 of 18 tasks against both HyenaDNA and Mamba-based Caduceus (Mamba-PS/PH), with the largest margins on histone marking (e.g., H3: 0.824 vs. 0.779 MCC) and splice-site prediction (0.974 vs. 0.956 accuracy); it also matches the much larger 500M-parameter Nucleotide Transformer by winning 8 of those 18 tasks. For homology-conditioned protein generation, Prot-xLSTM-102M achieves a perplexity of 7.35 (all tokens) and 5.87 (FIM tokens), clearly outperforming ProtMamba-107M (8.27 / 7.00) despite using only ~60B training tokens versus 195B for ProtMamba. On protein variant fitness prediction (ProteinGym DMS), Prot-xLSTM surpasses unconditional models like ESM-2 and ProGen2 but falls short of MSA-based TranceptEVE and structure-aware ProSST. For unconditional SMILES generation, Chem-xLSTM achieves the best FCD of 0.13, beating SMILES-GPT (0.15), Chem-Mamba (0.21), SMILES-S4 (0.28), and SMILES-LSTM (0.46), while its perplexity of 1.68 is marginally behind SMILES-GPT's 1.65. In conditional (ICL) molecule generation, Chem-xLSTM-15M-icl achieves the lowest validation CLM loss across unseen molecular domains, outperforming Mamba, Transformer++, and S4 baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the xLSTM architecture (Beck et al., 2024) — a recurrent network with enhanced sLSTM and mLSTM memory layers that outperformed SSMs in natural language processing — to three biological/chemical domains. It departs from the dominant Transformer-based paradigm (DNABERT, ESM, Nucleotide Transformer, ProtGPT2) and the more recent SSM-based approaches (Mamba, HyenaDNA, Caduceus, ProtMamba) by introducing domain-specific architectural variants: RC-equivariant blocks for DNA, FIM-based homology-aware training for proteins, and ICL-capable configurations for SMILES. The lineage traces from classic LSTMs (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997) through xLSTM's exponential gates and matrix cell states, positioning xLSTM as a linear-time recurrent alternative that retains constant-memory inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Bio-xLSTM provides a practical linear-time alternative to Transformers for long-sequence biological modeling, which is critical given that the human genome (~3B bps) far exceeds typical Transformer context windows. The demonstrated ICL capability for proteins and molecules — generating from unseen domains or homologous families without fine-tuning — addresses a highly sought-after capability in drug discovery and protein engineering. Open questions include whether character-level tokenization for DNA limits scaling, whether performance generalizes across organisms and chemical domains beyond the training data, and whether the advantage over SSMs persists at much larger scales. The paper acknowledges that perplexity may not fully capture model capacity, leaving room for more nuanced evaluation.
