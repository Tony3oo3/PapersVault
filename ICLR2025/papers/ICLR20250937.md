## Problem

Most existing medical MLLMs are region-agnostic, treating the entire scan as a holistic representation and unable to identify which specific anatomical regions they are attending to when generating text. This spatial unawareness leads to incorrect lesion localization (e.g., misidentifying a right-sided glioma as left-sided edema), undermining report accuracy, interpretability, and clinical trust. The paper addresses this gap by formulating region-centric tasks and training a model that explicitly grounds its outputs to bounding-box regions within medical images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250937/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts medical images across 8 modalities (X-ray, CT, MRI, pathology, ultrasound, dermatology, ophthalmology) paired with free-text instructions in English or Chinese. Regions are encoded as normalized integer bounding boxes [x1, y1, x2, y2] in the range [0, 1000), wrapped in special tokens <box>…</box> with the referenced object marked by <ref>…</ref>. Outputs range from plain text answers and class labels to grounded reports where each descriptive sentence is paired with its corresponding bounding box, enabling clinicians to visually verify which region each statement refers to.

## Method

MedRegA is built on InternVL 1.2 (InternViT-6B vision encoder + Nous-Hermes-2-Yi-34B language model) and trained in two stages: alignment training (freezing both encoder and LM, fine-tuning only the projection module on ~2.4M medical caption pairs) followed by instruction tuning on ~2.2M samples including the newly constructed MedRegInstruct dataset (550K region-text triplets from SA-Med2D-20M plus 240K region-grounded reports from MIMIC-CXR and 25K in-house Chinese clinical scans). The key architectural insight is the introduction of three Region-Centric tasks—Region-to-Text Identification, Text-to-Region Detection, and Grounded Report Generation—whose bounding-box token format forces the language model to learn explicit spatial grounding. At inference, a Regional Chain-of-Thought (CoT) strategy first prompts the model to detect critical regions in the input image, then conditions the final generation on those detected coordinates, improving downstream task performance (e.g., +34.95% F1 on multi-label spine classification) by encouraging the model to attend to local structures rather than the global image.

## Results

MedRegA achieves the best performance across all evaluated tasks. On English VQA, it scores 67.65 BLEU-1 and 68.33 F1, outperforming MedDr (65.72/66.87) and InternVL (63.51/65.05). For report generation, MedRegA reaches 40.46 BLEU-1 on English (vs. MedDr's 34.49) and 40.76 BLEU-1 on Chinese (vs. MedDr's 11.99 and InternVL's 10.71), demonstrating a 27.34% improvement over MedDr in Chinese. On region-centric tasks, MedRegA achieves 69.72 BLEU-1 on Region-to-Text Identification (all other models score <1), 77.93 Object F1 on Text-to-Region Detection (vs. InternVL's 56.60), and 76.59 Region Accuracy on Grounded Report Generation (vs. InternVL's 0.57). For medical image classification, it attains 47.97 F1 on single-label (vs. MedDr's 32.65) and 13.32 F1 on multi-label (vs. MedDr's 9.38).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedRegA outperforms all six baselines (Med-Flamingo, LLaVA-Med, RadFM, MedDr, BiomedGPT, InternVL) on every task category. Against the strongest generalist baseline MedDr, MedRegA gains +3.91 BLEU-1 on MIMIC-CXR English report generation, +8.03 on IU-Xray, and a dramatic +28.77 BLEU-1 on Chinese report generation (40.76 vs. 11.99), where MedDr's bilingual capability is severely limited. On Chinese VQA, MedRegA surpasses InternVL (the only other bilingual model) by +21.16 BLEU-1 (60.89 vs. 39.73). For region-centric tasks, the gap is orders of magnitude: MedRegA scores 69.72 BLEU-1 on Region-to-Text while all other models score below 1, and 77.93 Object F1 on Text-to-Region versus InternVL's 56.60 (the only other model capable of generating bounding boxes). On single-label classification, MedRegA leads MedDr by +15.32 F1 (47.97 vs. 32.65), and with Regional CoT it reaches 61.75% F1 on VinDr-SpineXR multi-label, surpassing MedDr by 34.95%. The only area where MedRegA is slightly less competitive is PathVQA (59.52 vs. MedDr's 61.40 BLEU-1), attributed to the scarcity of regional annotations for pathology images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on InternVL 1.2 (InternViT-6B + Nous-Hermes-2-Yi-34B) as its general-domain foundation and departs from the dominant paradigm of region-agnostic medical MLLMs (MedDr, RadFM, BioMedGPT) that treat entire scans as holistic representations. Prior region-related medical work was severely limited in scope: Bannur et al. (2024) handled grounded report generation only for chest X-ray, Zhang et al. (2024b) and Lei et al. (2024) covered only chest CT and brain MRI respectively, and Huang et al. (2024) had limited access to real clinical descriptions. MedRegA extends these individual capabilities into a unified bilingual framework spanning 8 modalities and multiple body regions, introducing a Regional CoT inference strategy that leverages detected regions to improve downstream task performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine clinical usability gap: without region-level grounding, clinicians cannot verify which anatomical area a generated description refers to, undermining trust in AI-generated reports. The MedRegInstruct dataset (790K samples) and Region-Aligned evaluation framework provide reusable infrastructure for the community. However, multi-region detection remains weak (IoU ~23%), pathology modalities lack sufficient regional annotations, and the model's reliance on a 34B-parameter language model limits deployment accessibility. Open questions include whether region-centric training can be distilled into smaller models and whether the approach generalizes to 3D volumetric data beyond central 2D slices.
