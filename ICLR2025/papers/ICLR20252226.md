## Problem

SDF-based neural surface methods face a fundamental trade-off: accurate geometry reconstruction and high-fidelity rendering are mutually reinforcing yet difficult to optimize simultaneously. Two specific challenges remain unsolved—modeling fine geometric details (e.g., thin structures) without over-smoothing, and disambiguating reflective appearance from underlying geometry so that specular highlights are not misinterpreted as surface shape.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252226/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view RGB images (no additional supervision such as depth or normals) and is trained on a single V100 for 2–3 hours. It outputs a watertight 3D mesh extracted via marching cubes from the learned SDF, photorealistic novel-view renderings, and per-vertex surface normals. The hash-grid feature dimension is kept at 2 per level (levels 4–16) to limit memory to ~24 GB, and no instance-specific hyperparameter tuning is required across reflective, non-reflective, luminous, or semi-transparent objects.

## Method

AniSDF introduces two complementary components. First, a fused-granularity neural surface trains coarse (levels 4–10) and fine (levels 10–16) multi-resolution hash grids in parallel rather than sequentially; SDF values and geometric features from both branches are summed, and a curvature loss keeps fine-grid normals aligned with coarse-grid normals during early training, preserving thin structures that a coarse-to-fine schedule would discard. Second, a blended radiance field splits appearance into a view-based diffuse branch and a reflection-based specular branch; the specular branch encodes the rendering equation in feature space using Anisotropic Spherical Gaussians (ASG) with learned sharpness and amplitude parameters, then a compact MLP predicts integrated color from the ASG-encoded features. A learned 3D sigmoid weight field blends the two radiance fields per pixel, enabling the model to handle semi-transparent and luminous surfaces. The geometry and appearance networks are jointly optimized with RGB, Eikonal, curvature, orientation, and binary-cross-entropy opacity losses, so that improved appearance disambiguation directly accelerates high-frequency geometry recovery.

## Results

On the NeRF Synthetic dataset, AniSDF achieves the best average PSNR of 33.42 dB and the lowest average Chamfer Distance of 4.89×10⁻³, outperforming both volumetric methods (3DGS: 33.32 dB, 8.25 CD) and surface methods (NeuS: 30.20 dB, 5.64 CD). On the DTU dataset, AniSDF attains the best mean Chamfer Distance of 0.65 mm, surpassing NeuralWarp (0.68), NeuS (0.84), and 2DGS (0.80). On the Shiny Blender reflective dataset, AniSDF achieves the highest mean PSNR (31.05 dB) and lowest surface normal MAE (0.85°), beating RefNeuS (30.20 dB, 0.91°) and NeuS (26.61 dB, 1.77°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AniSDF outperforms all surface-reconstruction baselines on both rendering and geometry: it beats NeuS by +3.22 dB PSNR and −0.75 CD, NeRF2Mesh by +2.54 dB and −0.17 CD, and 2DGS by +0.35 dB and −3.36 CD on NeRF Synthetic. Against volumetric methods that do not produce explicit geometry, AniSDF matches or exceeds 3DGS (33.42 vs. 33.32 dB) and InstantNGP (33.42 vs. 33.18 dB) in PSNR while additionally delivering the best Chamfer Distance. On the Shiny Blender reflective benchmark, AniSDF surpasses RefNeuS by +0.85 dB PSNR and −0.06° MAE, and NeuS by +4.44 dB and −0.92° MAE, confirming that the ASG encoding resolves reflective ambiguity. On DTU, AniSDF achieves the best mean CD (0.65 mm), narrowly beating NeuralWarp (0.68) and clearly outperforming NeuS (0.84) and 2DGS (0.80). On MipNeRF360, AniSDF is competitive with Mip-NeRF (31.62 vs. 31.72 dB avg) while providing explicit geometry, and surpasses 3DGS (30.41) and 2DGS (30.40) by over 1 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

AniSDF builds directly on the NeuS SDF-to-opacity volume rendering formulation and the RefNeRF reparametrized reflection direction, while adopting the learned 3D weight field composition from UniSDF and the curvature loss from PermutoSDF. Its key architectural departure is replacing the sequential coarse-to-fine hash grid training (used by HF-NeuS, Neuralangelo) with a parallel fused-granularity structure that trains coarse and fine grids simultaneously, and replacing fixed-basis reflectance encodings (spherical harmonics, spherical Gaussians) with anisotropic spherical Gaussians (ASG) operating in feature space. Unlike RefNeuS or UniSDF, which address either reflective disambiguation or non-reflective geometry in isolation, AniSDF unifies both concerns in a single model without instance-specific hyperparameter tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practical bottleneck in SDF-based reconstruction: the mutual interference between geometry learning and appearance learning, particularly for reflective and luminous surfaces. The fused-granularity parallel structure offers a clean, general solution to the coarse-to-fine dilemma that preserves thin structures without the overfitting of fine-only grids. However, the method remains limited to offline training (2–3 hours on a V100) and cannot handle complex indirect illumination without a materials estimation network, leaving real-time deployment and full inverse rendering as open directions. The unified, tuning-free design makes it a practical baseline for downstream tasks like relighting and deformation that require both accurate geometry and photorealistic appearance.
