## Problem

Existing neural verifiers for mathematical problem-solving—outcome-supervised reward models (ORMs) and process-supervised reward models (PRMs)—were designed for Best-of-N search and are sub-optimal for tree-search-based inference at test time. ORMs can only implicitly assess partial solutions through homogeneous token labeling, while PRMs suffer from high false negative errors because step-level supervision labels an entire intermediate step as incorrect even when only the final few tokens are wrong, causing premature pruning of promising reasoning paths during tree search.

## Input / Output

The TVM takes a math word problem and a partial reasoning path (a prefix of tokens) as input and outputs a scalar probability score per token representing the likelihood of reaching the correct final answer conditioned on that prefix. The supervision labels are computed empirically from N_tr sampled reasoning paths per training problem: each token's label is the ratio of paths sharing that exact token prefix that reach the correct answer to the total number of paths sharing that prefix, requiring only O(Σ T_n) token generation rather than the O(Σ T_n²) needed for per-token rollout-based supervision.

## Method

The TVM introduces token-level supervision where each token t_{n,k} in a reasoning path is labeled with P(final answer = â | q_tr, t_{n,1},...,t_{n,k}), computed as the fraction of N_tr sampled paths sharing the prefix {t_{n,1},...,t_{n,k}} that reach the correct final answer. A key theoretical result (Proposition 4.1) shows that under outcome-only reward with discount factor γ=1, the expected cumulative reward (value function) is exactly equivalent to this conditional probability, justifying the 'value model' designation and guaranteeing that tree search is value-guided. The TVM is trained with mean squared error on these per-token probability labels using the same architecture as the generating LLM extended with a scalar head, combined with a language modeling objective. Unlike PRMs that assign a single binary or step-level score, the TVM assigns distinct probability values to each token within a step—only the last few tokens of an incorrect step receive zero labels while earlier tokens retain positive values—yielding a lower false negative error (8–14%p reduction) while maintaining comparable false positive error to PRMs.

## Results

On GSM8K, TVM with Step-by-Step Beam Search (K=40, b=10) achieves 87.69% (Mistral 7B), 88.70% (Mistral 7B MetaMath), 89.06% (Llama 3 8B), and 90.35% (Llama 3 8B MetaMath), consistently outperforming both ORM and PRM under the same search strategy. On the harder MATH benchmark, TVM with Step-by-Step Beam Search reaches 39.33% (Mistral 7B MetaMath) and 45.00% (Llama 3 8B MetaMath), surpassing the best Best-of-N accuracy (44.40% PRM for Llama 3 8B MetaMath) while using approximately 6× fewer FLOPs and 2× less execution time. TVM also improves over ORM and PRM under Best-of-N search, e.g., 90.37% vs. 89.77% (ORM) and 90.30% (PRM) for Llama 3 8B MetaMath on GSM8K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TVM consistently outperforms ORM across all search strategies and all four LLMs on GSM8K; for example, under Step-by-Step Beam Search, TVM gains +0.96 pp over ORM for Mistral 7B (87.69 vs. 86.73) and +1.26 pp for Llama 3 8B MetaMath (90.35 vs. 89.69). Against the Math-Shepherd PRM, TVM's advantage is most pronounced in tree-search settings: on GSM8K Step-by-Step Beam Search, TVM beats PRM by +2.04 pp (Mistral 7B MetaMath: 88.70 vs. 86.66) and +1.42 pp (Llama 3 8B MetaMath: 90.35 vs. 88.93); on MATH Step-by-Step Beam Search, the gap widens to +2.53 pp (Mistral 7B MetaMath: 39.33 vs. 36.80) and +2.80 pp (Llama 3 8B MetaMath: 45.00 vs. 42.20). Notably, PRM actually underperforms ORM under tree search in several settings (e.g., GSM8K Step-by-Step Beam Search: PRM 86.66 vs. ORM 87.79 for Mistral 7B MetaMath), confirming the paper's claim that PRM's high false-negative error causes premature pruning of promising steps. TVM also surpasses Self-Consistency (N=256) by a large margin (e.g., +8.94 pp for Mistral 7B on GSM8K Best-of-N: 88.17 vs. 79.23). The only instance where TVM does not lead is MATH Best-of-N for Llama 3 8B MetaMath, where PRM edges out TVM by 1.0 pp (44.40 vs. 43.40), though TVM recovers and surpasses all baselines under tree search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the verifier literature for mathematical reasoning (ORMs from Cobbe et al. 2021, PRMs from Uesato et al. 2022 and Lightman et al. 2023) by introducing a third supervision paradigm: token-level supervision with correctness probability scores computed from sampled reasoning paths. It departs from PRMs (e.g., Math-Shepherd) by replacing step-level binary labels with continuous per-token probability labels, and from ORMs by making the evaluation of partial solutions explicit rather than implicit. The theoretical framing as a 'value model' rather than a 'reward model' (Proposition 4.1) repositions the verifier within the tree-search literature, where value-guided search is the natural objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that PRMs—widely regarded as the superior verifier class—systematically under-value correct intermediate steps due to high false negative errors challenges a working assumption in the test-time compute scaling community and explains conflicting prior results about ORM vs. PRM suitability for tree search. The TVM's ~6× FLOPs savings over Best-of-N while matching or exceeding its accuracy makes tree search a more practical inference strategy for smaller models. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-mathematical reasoning tasks, whether it scales to larger models (>10B) where the gap between verifiers may narrow, and whether the token-level probability signal degrades when N_tr is too small to produce sufficient path overlap.
