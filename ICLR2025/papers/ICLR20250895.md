## Problem

Existing diverse policy recovery methods (e.g., conditional BC, CTVAE, InfoGAIL, SORL) treat every state-action pair in a trajectory with equal importance when training a style-conditioned policy, even though in practice only a subset of pairs is highly relevant to the style. For instance, in autonomous driving, overtaking-style diversity is concentrated in the overtaking segment, while the preceding straight-driving segment is nearly style-agnostic. This uniform weighting dilutes the style signal and degrades calibration of the recovered policies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline expert trajectories (state-action sequences) together with a discrete trajectory-level style label z, which is typically generated by a programmatic labeling function (e.g., dividing the map into areas, binning firing rate, or measuring destination distance and curvature). The output is a single style-conditioned policy network π_θ(a|s,z) that, given a state and a chosen style, produces an action distribution. In Atari experiments the state is a processed frame from the Atari-Head dataset; in basketball it is a 2D court position sequence; in Circle 2D it is a 4-step position history.

## Method

BC-PMI augments the standard conditional BC loss with a per-sample importance weight equal to the exponential of the Pointwise Mutual Information between the state-action pair and the style, i.e., p(z|s,a)/p(z), so that pairs highly indicative of a style receive larger gradient contributions. The PMI is estimated online via a MINE network T_φ trained with the Donsker-Varadhan lower bound on I((s,a); z), whose optimal solution recovers the log-ratio p(z|s,a)/p(z). A moving-average baseline is subtracted from the weight to reduce gradient variance, yielding the final objective min_π E[−log π(a|s,z)·(exp(T_φ*(s,a,z)) − b̃)]. Theoretically, the objective smoothly interpolates between vanilla BC (when I(Z;S,A)=0, all weights collapse to 1) and per-style clustering BC (when H(Z|S,A)=0, weights become hard indicators), providing a principled middle ground that prior equal-weight methods cannot achieve.

## Results

On the NBA basketball dataset, BC-PMI achieves style calibration of 93.3%, 92.7%, and 89.8% across three Destination classes, and 79.4%, 80.9%, and 81.6% across three Curvature classes, outperforming the best baseline (CBC) by 11.7–16.7 percentage points. On Atari-Head (Space Invaders, Position style), BC-PMI reaches 0.83 calibration accuracy versus 0.71 for CBC and 0.66 for CTVAE. In the Circle 2D toy environment, BC-PMI reduces KL divergence by up to 45% compared to CBC (e.g., 0.135 vs. 0.271 for Class 3) and by over 99% compared to vanilla BC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BC-PMI consistently outperforms all four baselines across every style category in both the basketball and Atari experiments. Compared to vanilla BC (Pomerleau 1991), BC-PMI gains 48–64 percentage points on basketball calibration, as BC learns an average policy that cannot distinguish styles. Against CTVAE (Wang 2017), BC-PMI wins by 11.5–23.2 points on basketball and by 0.02–0.17 on Atari position/firing styles, though CTVAE edges out BC-PMI by 0.05 on MsPacman Range style (0.61 vs. 0.56). InfoGAIL (Li 2017) and SORL (Mao 2023) are the weakest baselines, trailing BC-PMI by 10–30 points on basketball and 0.15–0.46 on Atari, likely because their unsupervised style inference introduces label noise. The closest competitor is CBC (conditional BC with hard style separation), which BC-PMI surpasses by 5.4–23.2 points on basketball; the gap is largest for Destination Class 3 (89.8% vs. 73.1%), suggesting PMI weighting is most beneficial when style-relevant state-action pairs are a small fraction of the trajectory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diverse policy recovery literature (InfoGAIL, CTVAE, SORL) by introducing a PMI-based weighting mechanism into the style-conditioned behavioral cloning objective. While prior methods treat all state-action pairs within a style-labeled trajectory equally, BC-PMI uses MINE to estimate pointwise mutual information between each (s,a) pair and the style variable, upweighting pairs that are more diagnostic of the style. The theoretical analysis elegantly shows the objective interpolates between vanilla BC (zero mutual information) and per-style clustering BC (zero conditional entropy), unifying two previously separate paradigms under a single weighted loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a simple, interpretable, and theoretically grounded way to handle the common situation where only a subset of a trajectory is style-relevant (e.g., the overtaking segment in driving, the pre-shot sequence in basketball). A key limitation is the reliance on pre-specified style labels, which restricts applicability to settings where programmatic or manual labeling is feasible; the authors acknowledge that combining PMI weighting with RL or disentanglement methods could extend the approach to performance improvement and unsupervised style discovery. The consistent but moderate gains over strong baselines (especially CBC) suggest the weighting mechanism captures a real signal, but the practical impact depends on the availability of meaningful style labels in the target domain.
