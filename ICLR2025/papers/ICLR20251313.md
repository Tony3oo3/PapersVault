## Problem

Data selection methods for LLM finetuning are typically evaluated on data reduction alone, ignoring the substantial compute cost of the selection process itself. This paper identifies that powerful methods like perplexity-based and gradient-based selection are almost never compute-optimal because their selection FLOPs can exceed the training savings they provide, and formalizes the missing question: given a fixed total compute budget for both selection and training, which data selection strategy maximizes downstream performance?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251313/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large instruction-tuning corpus (~95.7M tokens from FLAN V2, COT, Dolly, and Open Assistant 1), a small task-specific validation set (50–285 few-shot examples from MMLU, BBH, or IFEval), and a total FLOP budget that jointly constrains both the data selection pass and the finetuning pass. The output is a selected data subset (2.5%–100% of the corpus) and a LoRA-finetuned LLM, along with a fitted parametric model that predicts the compute-optimal selection method for any given budget and model-size ratio.

## Method

The paper reformulates data selection as a compute-constrained combinatorial optimization where the constraint is total FLOPs (selection cost + training cost) rather than data cardinality, and categorizes five selection methods by their per-point compute cost: BM25 (≈0), Embed via a 220M T5 encoder (≈ε), Perplexity via a 7B forward pass (≈1×C_forward), and LESS gradient features (≈3×C_forward). It proposes a parametric performance model P(k) = (P̄ − P₀)(1 − exp(−λC(k)/C(|D|))) + P₀ that captures diminishing returns as a function of total compute, with λ fitted per method via Levenberg-Marquardt. Across 600+ runs spanning LLaMA-2 7B/13B/70B and LLaMA-3 8B, the paper empirically demonstrates that BM25 and Embed dominate the Pareto frontier at small and medium budgets, while PPL and LESS only become compute-optimal when the training model is ~5× and ~10× larger than the selection model, respectively. The parametric fits are then extrapolated to predict these crossover ratios, which are validated by the 70B experiments.

## Results

Across 600+ finetuning runs (7B–70B LLaMA models, 6 data selection methods, 3 tasks), the paper finds that cheap lexicon-based (BM25) and embedding-based (Embed) methods are compute-optimal at 7B and 13B model sizes, while perplexity-based (PPL) and gradient-based (LESS) methods only become compute-optimal at 70B. The key quantitative threshold: PPL requires a training-to-selection model size ratio of ~5× (≈35B training model with 7B selector), and LESS requires ~10× (≈70B training model with 7B selector). Data selection FLOPs span 8 orders of magnitude: BM25 ≈ 1×10⁸, Embed ≈ 4.4×10¹⁶, PPL ≈ 1.53×10¹⁸, LESS ≈ 8.27×10¹⁸.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BM25 (Robertson et al., 2009) and Embed (Ivison et al., 2022) consistently outperform PPL (Marion et al., 2023) and LESS (Xia et al., 2024) on the compute-constrained Pareto frontier at 7B and 13B model sizes across MMLU, BBH, and IFEval. While PPL and LESS achieve higher task accuracy at the same data budget (e.g., selecting 5% of tokens), their selection FLOPs (1.53×10¹⁸ and 8.27×10¹⁸ respectively) make them FLOP-inefficient: the marginal performance gain does not justify the selection cost. At 70B, PPL and LESS cross the Pareto frontier after 5% finetuning tokens, confirming the 5× and 10× training-to-selection size ratios. In the fixed-training-budget setting (ignoring selection cost), LESS consistently outperforms all other methods, with PPL as the closest competitor. For multi-task amortization, LESS requires 20 target tasks (7B selector) to surpass the Pareto frontier, while at 13B it reaches the frontier at 5 tasks and surpasses it at 10.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compute-optimal LLM framework of Hoffmann et al. (2022) from pretraining architecture/training decisions to the data selection + finetuning setting, formalizing a cost-aware objective (Eq. 2) that budgets both selection and training FLOPs jointly. It builds on the data selection literature (coreset selection, influence functions, perplexity-based pruning) and post-training scaling laws (Hernandez et al., 2021; Lin et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024), but departs from all of these by explicitly modeling the compute cost of the selection utility function itself—a factor that prior scaling analyses for data selection (e.g., Goyal et al., 2024 in vision) omit. The parametric performance model (Eq. 3) is a novel contribution that unifies diminishing-returns data selection with compute scaling in a single closed form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that simple statistical methods (BM25, embedding retrieval) are almost always compute-optimal over model-based methods (perplexity, gradient) provides concrete, actionable guidance for practitioners allocating finite compute to LLM finetuning, and the derived 5×/10× training-to-selection size ratios give a clear threshold for when expensive methods become worthwhile. Open questions include whether multi-epoch training, hyperparameter co-optimization, or cheaper selection methods (classifier-based, active learning) could shift the Pareto frontier, and whether the ratios generalize beyond the LLaMA family and instruction-tuning setting. The paper's single-epoch, fixed-hyperparameter protocol limits direct transfer to production pipelines where these are tuned.
