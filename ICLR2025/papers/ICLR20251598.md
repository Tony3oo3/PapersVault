## Problem

Deep RL policies produce non-smooth, oscillatory control actions due to two distinct causes: high-frequency noise in observations and unconstrained Lipschitz constants in the policy network. Existing smoothing approaches (filtering, action penalties, adversarial training, spectral normalization) address only one cause or impose significant algorithmic overhead, leaving a gap for a unified, plug-and-play solution that simultaneously suppresses both noise sensitivity and output oscillation.

## Input / Output

The SmODE network accepts state vectors (continuous observations such as position, velocity, heading, or yaw rate) or RGB images (96×96×3 for CarRacing) as input, processed first by an MLP input module. It outputs a continuous control action (e.g., longitudinal acceleration and front-wheel angle for vehicle tracking, or joint torques for Mujoco robots) through a linear output layer with spectral normalization. The hidden state dynamics are resolved via a fixed-step semi-implicit Euler solver with 6 iterations and unit time-step, adding a constant computational overhead per forward pass.

## Method

Each SmODE neuron implements a first-order low-pass ODE: dx/dt = −f(x,I,t,θ)·x + f(x,I,t,θ)·g(x,I,t,θ), where f is a learnable, state-dependent inverse time constant (sigmoid-gated synaptic weight) that adaptively controls filtering strength, and g = tanh(h(x,θ)) is a state-dependent boundary function that caps the hidden state magnitude and thereby bounds the Lipschitz constant. Theorem 1 proves hidden states remain within [min(0,g_min), max(0,g_max)], and Theorem 2 proves |dx/dt| ≤ M·C, giving a state-adaptive Lipschitz bound. The full network stacks three layers of such ODE neurons between an MLP input module and a spectrally-normalized linear output layer. Training augments the standard actor loss with two regularization terms: a time-constant penalty (λ₁·Σf) that encourages stronger low-pass filtering, and a state-boundary penalty (λ₂·Σh²) that tightens the Lipschitz constraint, both computed over the replay buffer. The ODE is discretized with a 6-step semi-implicit Euler scheme, making the architecture compatible with any actor-critic RL algorithm (demonstrated with INFADP, DSAC, and TD3).

## Results

On the vehicle trajectory tracking task (double lane-change curve, INFADP algorithm), SmODE achieves an action fluctuation ratio ε(π) of 0.176 at Gaussian noise std 0.20, an 81.7% reduction compared to MLP (0.964), while also improving total average return (TAR) from −3.087 to −0.926. Across all four noise levels (0.05–0.20), SmODE consistently attains the lowest ε(π) and highest TAR among all compared methods. On the eight Mujoco tasks (DSAC algorithm), SmODE achieves the lowest average action fluctuation rate in most settings while maintaining competitive or superior return, e.g., on Walker2d-v3 at noise level 2 it reaches 5037 return with 1.30 fluctuation vs. MLP's 1597 return with 1.80 fluctuation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MLP, SmODE dominates on both metrics at every noise level: at the highest noise (std 0.20), it reduces ε(π) by 81.7% (0.176 vs. 0.964) and improves TAR by 2.16 points (−0.926 vs. −3.087). Against LipsNet, the closest smooth-network competitor, SmODE achieves lower ε(π) at all noise levels (e.g., 0.176 vs. 0.221 at std 0.20) and higher TAR at moderate-to-high noise (−0.926 vs. −1.112 at std 0.20), though LipsNet edges out slightly on TAR at the lowest noise (−0.893 vs. −0.778 at std 0.05). Against LTC, SmODE consistently outperforms on both metrics, with the gap widening as noise increases (ε(π): 0.176 vs. 0.778 at std 0.20). Against the classical MPC controller (noise-free benchmark), SmODE achieves lower action fluctuation and higher return at all noise levels, demonstrating that the learned policy with SmODE surpasses a model-based optimal controller under noisy observations. On the broader Mujoco suite (Table 3), SmODE achieves the lowest average action fluctuation in 7 of 8 tasks at both noise levels while matching or exceeding the return of MLP and LipsNet in most settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural ODE framework (Chen et al., 2018) and the Liquid Time-Constant network (Hasani et al., 2021) by designing a smooth ODE neuron that simultaneously incorporates low-pass filtering (to suppress high-frequency input noise) and a state-dependent boundary function g (to control the Lipschitz constant). The specific neuron formulation draws on the bionic modeling approach of Lechner et al. (2020). Unlike prior smooth-control methods that address either noise filtering or Lipschitz constraint in isolation—LipsNet controls the Lipschitz constant but not noise, while Kalman/particle filters handle noise but require multi-step history—SmODE unifies both mechanisms within a single ODE-based neuron, making it a drop-in policy network for existing actor-critic RL algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (compatible with INFADP, DSAC, TD3) and the consistent outperformance across ten environments make SmODE practically relevant for safety-critical control applications like autonomous driving and robotics, where action oscillation has real-world consequences. The main limitation is the 2–3× training time overhead from the numerical ODE solver (N=6 iterations), which the authors acknowledge and plan to address with faster backpropagation techniques. An open question is whether the fixed semi-implicit Euler discretization can be replaced by closed-form continuous-depth models (CfC) to eliminate the solver cost while preserving the adaptive filtering and boundary-control properties.
