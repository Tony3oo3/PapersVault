## Problem

Existing sign language pre-training methods suffer from a fundamental gap between pre-training objectives and downstream task objectives: self-supervised approaches capture only low-level visual semantics without linguistic knowledge, while video-text contrastive or gloss-prediction methods are limited by the small scale of paired data. Additionally, Chinese Sign Language (CSL) lacks a large-scale pre-training dataset comparable to the 984-hour YouTube-ASL or 1,447-hour BoBSL corpora for ASL and BSL.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 69 selectively extracted whole-body keypoints (21 per hand, 9 body, 18 face) from RTMPose-x as a temporal pose sequence, and optionally cropped 112×112 hand RGB video clips for a sparse subset of frames (default 10%) where keypoint confidence is low. All three downstream tasks—ISLR, CSLR, and SLT—are unified as text generation: the output is a single sign word, a space-separated gloss sequence, or a full sentence, respectively, all produced by the same mT5-Base language model decoder with a shared language modeling loss.

## Method

Uni-Sign employs a three-stage training pipeline: (1) pose-only generative pre-training on CSL-News (1,985 hours) or YouTube-ASL, (2) RGB-pose interaction continue pre-training, and (3) unified downstream fine-tuning where all SLU tasks are treated identically as sign-to-text generation with a single language modeling loss, eliminating task-specific heads (MLP classifiers, CTC losses). The encoder divides keypoints into four sub-pose groups, each processed by a 3-layer spatial GCN followed by a 3-layer ST-GCN temporal encoder, with features concatenated and projected into mT5-Base. A prior-guided fusion (PGF) module uses keypoint coordinates as reference-point priors for deformable attention to fuse RGB and hand-pose features frame-by-frame, while a score-aware sampling strategy selects only low-confidence frames for RGB processing (sampling score = 1 − mean keypoint confidence), trading a small accuracy gain for significant computational savings.

## Results

On the primary SLT task (CSL-Daily, gloss-free), Uni-Sign (RGB+Pose) achieves 26.36 BLEU4 and 56.51 ROUGE on the test set, surpassing the previous gloss-free SOTA C²RL by 5.74 BLEU4 and even outperforming gloss-based methods like SLTUNET (25.01 BLEU4) and TS-SLT (25.79 BLEU4). On ISLR, Uni-Sign achieves 78.16% per-instance Top-1 on MSASL1000 and 63.52% on WLASL2000, improving over MSLU by 4.09% and 7.23% respectively. On OpenASL SLT, Uni-Sign reaches 23.14 BLEU4 and 60.40 BLEURT, a 9.93 BLEU4 gain over C²RL. For CSLR on CSL-Daily, Uni-Sign (RGB+Pose) achieves 26.0 WER, competitive with the CTC-based TS-SLR (25.3 WER) despite using no temporal alignment loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni-Sign decisively outperforms all previous gloss-free SLT methods on CSL-Daily: it beats C²RL by 5.74 BLEU4 (26.36 vs. 21.61), Sign2GPT by 10.96 BLEU4, and SignLLM by 10.61 BLEU4 on the test set. Remarkably, Uni-Sign also surpasses gloss-based methods SLTUNET (26.36 vs. 25.01 BLEU4) and TS-SLT (26.36 vs. 25.79 BLEU4) despite not using any gloss supervision. On ISLR, Uni-Sign (RGB+Pose) leads MSLU by 4.09% P-I on MSASL1000 (78.16 vs. 74.07) and by 7.23% on WLASL2000 (63.52 vs. 56.29), and also outperforms the RGB+Pose method NLA-SLR on both challenging benchmarks. On OpenASL, Uni-Sign achieves a 9.93 BLEU4 margin over C²RL (23.14 vs. 13.21) and a 24.05 BLEURT margin over GloFE-VN (60.40 vs. 36.35). The only near-tie is on How2Sign SLT, where Uni-Sign (14.9 BLEU4) trails SSVP-SLT (15.5 BLEU4) by 0.6, though SSVP-SLT uses a larger vision encoder and longer pre-training. For CSLR, Uni-Sign (26.0 WER) is within 0.7 WER of the CTC-based TS-SLR (25.3 WER) without any temporal alignment loss, while clearly beating MSLU (27.9) and CoSign (27.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

Uni-Sign departs from the dominant self-supervised pre-training paradigm in sign language understanding (SignBERT, SignBERT+, MSLU) by adopting direct generative pre-training with a language modeling objective, inspired by the success of LLMs and vision-language models (LLaVA, VisionLLM-v2) in unifying diverse tasks. The prior-guided fusion module repurposes deformable attention (Xia et al., 2022) with keypoint coordinates as spatial priors to bridge the representational gap between pose and RGB modalities, a departure from the simple concatenation or cross-attention fusion used in prior multimodal SLU work. The unified fine-tuning paradigm—treating ISLR, CSLR, and SLT all as sequence-to-sequence text generation—draws on the seq2seq unification philosophy from NLP (Chen et al., 2022a; Wang et al., 2022) but is the first to apply it across the full SLU task family without task-specific heads or losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CSL-News dataset (1,985 hours, ~86× larger than prior CSL data) fills a critical gap in large-scale Chinese Sign Language resources and enables the kind of scaling that has proven transformative in NLP and vision-language. The finding that a single language-modeling fine-tuning objective outperforms task-specific paradigms (CTC for CSLR, MLP classification for ISLR) by substantial margins challenges the field's reliance on task-tailored architectures and suggests a simpler, more scalable path forward. Open questions include whether this unified paradigm extends to multilingual sign language models, complex real-world scenarios (multi-signer, occlusion), and whether the full-parameter fine-tuning requirement can be relaxed via parameter-efficient methods to reduce computational cost.
