## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-image diffusion models have grown to 12–24B parameters, making them prohibitively expensive to train and deploy, and no published work has efficiently generated images at true 4K (4096×4096) resolution. The paper asks whether a compact model can match the quality of these giants while running 100×+ faster and fitting on a consumer laptop GPU.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt, which is expanded via a Complex Human Instruction template and encoded by a Gemma-2-2B decoder-only LLM (replacing T5). It produces RGB images at resolutions from 512×512 up to 4096×4096, operating entirely in a 32×-compressed latent space (AE-F32C32P1) so that a 4K image is processed as only 128×128 tokens. With W8A8 quantization, Sana-0.6B generates a 1024×1024 image in 0.37 s on a consumer 4090 GPU.

## Method

Sana combines four architectural and algorithmic changes into a single pipeline. First, a deep compression autoencoder (AE-F32C32P1) compresses images 32× in spatial dimensions with 32 latent channels and patch size 1, reducing the token count by 16× compared to the standard AE-F8C4P2 and letting the diffusion model focus purely on denoising. Second, all vanilla softmax self-attention in the DiT backbone is replaced with ReLU linear attention (O(N) complexity), paired with a Mix-FFN block (inverted residual + 3×3 depth-wise convolution + GLU) that recovers local information and enables the complete removal of positional embeddings (NoPE). Third, a Gemma-2-2B decoder-only LLM serves as the text encoder, stabilized with RMSNorm and a learnable scale factor, and prompted with a Complex Human Instruction (CHI) template that leverages in-context learning to improve text-image alignment. Fourth, a Flow-DPM-Solver adapts DPM-Solver++ to the Rectified Flow velocity-prediction formulation, cutting sampling from 28–50 steps to 14–20 while improving FID and CLIP score. Triton kernel fusion and W8A8 quantization further accelerate training, inference, and edge deployment.

## Results

At 1024×1024 resolution, Sana-0.6B (590M params) achieves FID 5.81, CLIP 28.36, GenEval 0.64, and DPG-Bench 83.6, while running 39.5× faster in throughput than FLUX-dev (12B params, FID 10.15). Sana-1.6B further improves to FID 5.76 and DPG-Bench 84.8 (matching FLUX-schnell) at 23.3× the throughput. At 512×512, Sana-0.6B is 5× faster than PixArt-Σ with a lower FID (5.67 vs. 6.34) and higher GenEval (0.64 vs. 0.52). At 4096×4096, Sana-0.6B generates a 4K image in 9.6 s on an A100, compared to 1023 s for FLUX-dev (104× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FLUX-dev (12B), Sana-0.6B (0.6B) is 39.5× faster in throughput and 25× smaller in parameters, while achieving a substantially lower FID (5.81 vs. 10.15) at 1024×1024; however, FLUX-dev edges out Sana-0.6B on GenEval (0.67 vs. 0.64) and FLUX-schnell leads on GenEval (0.71) and FID (7.94 vs. 5.81 is actually Sana's win—FLUX-schnell FID is 7.94, Sana-0.6B is 5.81). Sana-1.6B matches FLUX-schnell exactly on DPG-Bench (84.8) and surpasses it on FID (5.76 vs. 7.94) and CLIP (28.67 vs. 28.14), while being 23.3× faster. At 512×512, Sana-0.6B dominates PixArt-Σ across all quality metrics (FID 5.67 vs. 6.34, GenEval 0.64 vs. 0.52, DPG 84.3 vs. 79.5) at 5× the throughput. Against SD3-medium, Sana-0.6B achieves a much lower FID (5.81 vs. 11.92) and higher GenEval (0.64 vs. 0.62) at 6× the throughput. The only baselines that outperform Sana on GenEval at 1024×1024 are DALLE-3 (0.67), FLUX-dev (0.67), and FLUX-schnell (0.71), but all of these are 12B+ parameter models with 20–40× lower throughput.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sana sits at the intersection of several recent threads in text-to-image generation: the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023), linear attention mechanisms previously explored in dense prediction (Katharopoulos et al., 2020; Cai et al., 2023), and the Rectified Flow / velocity-prediction formulation from SD3 (Esser et al., 2024). Its key departures are architectural rather than task-level: it replaces all softmax attention in DiT with ReLU linear attention, removes positional encoding entirely (NoPE), pushes autoencoder compression from the standard 8× to 32×, and swaps T5 for a decoder-only LLM (Gemma-2) as text encoder. The Flow-DPM-Solver is a direct adaptation of DPM-Solver++ (Lu et al., 2022) to the rectified-flow parameterization, and the Mix-FFN block borrows the inverted-residual + depth-wise-conv + GLU design from EfficientViT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sana's 100×+ throughput advantage over FLUX at 4K resolution and sub-second 1K generation on a 16GB laptop GPU make high-quality text-to-image generation accessible at the edge, a regime previously dominated by 12B+ parameter models requiring datacenter hardware. The NoPE finding (positional embedding is unnecessary in DiT when depth-wise convolution is present) and the 32× compression AE matching 8× reconstruction quality challenge two working assumptions in the field. Open questions include whether these efficiency gains transfer to video generation (the authors' stated future direction), whether the linear-attention quality gap closes further at even higher resolutions, and how the approach handles the acknowledged weaknesses in text rendering, faces, and hands.
