## Problem

Dreveton et al. (2024) characterized the information-theoretic threshold for exact community recovery under general side information, but the optimal MAP estimator achieving this threshold is not efficiently computable. This paper closes the gap by designing a single-stage spectral algorithm that provably achieves the same information-theoretic limit for the Rank One Spike and Stochastic Block Model, without any refinement or cleanup step, and for arbitrary side information channels (Gaussian features, BEC, BSC).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251082/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a symmetric observation matrix A ∈ ℝ^{n×n} (with entries drawn from Bernoulli or Gaussian distributions depending on community membership) and, optionally, a side information vector y ∈ 𝒴^n generated from a channel (BEC, BSC, or Gaussian features) conditioned on the true labels. It also requires the model parameters (ρ, a, b for ROS or a₁, a₂, b for SBM) and side information channel parameters. The output is a community assignment vector σ̂_spec ∈ {±1}^n obtained by sign-thresholding a spectral score vector, achieving exact recovery (σ̂ = σ* or σ̂ = −σ* in the symmetric case) with probability tending to 1 whenever I* > 1.

## Method

The algorithm computes the top K eigenvectors of A (K=1 for ROS, K=2 for SBM), forms a linear combination z^spec = γ·1 + Σ cᵢuᵢ with coefficients cᵢ derived from model parameters, adds the log-likelihood ratio vector log(S₊/S₋)(y) when side information is present, and outputs sgn(z^spec). The key technical contribution is proving via entrywise eigenvector analysis (Abbe et al., 2020) that z^spec approximates the genie score vector z* in ℓ∞ norm up to o(log n), where z* is the log-posterior ratio computed by an oracle that knows all labels except the i-th. Since above the IT threshold the genie scores have correct sign with Ω(log n) margin, this approximation guarantees exact recovery. The framework unifies prior spectral algorithms (Abbe et al. 2020, Dhara et al. 2022/2023) as special cases of genie-mimicking, and shows that unlike the censored SBM, a single matrix suffices for the uncensored general two-community case.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorems 1 and 2) establishes that a single-stage spectral algorithm achieves exact recovery (P(success) → 1) whenever the information-theoretic threshold I* > 1, for both the Rank One Spike (ROS) and Stochastic Block Model (SBM), with or without side information (GF, BEC, BSC channels). For ROS without side information, the threshold is I* = Ψ(ρ,a,b)/8 where Ψ = (a−b)²(ρa² + (1−ρ)b²); for the symmetric SBM, I* = ½(√a₁ − √b)². Empirical validation (n=300, N=50 trials) in Figures 2, 4, and 5 confirms that the spectral algorithm's success rate transitions sharply at the predicted theoretical threshold across a grid of signal strengths and side information parameters β.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are against information-theoretic limits and prior algorithmic approaches rather than empirical baselines. The spectral algorithm provably matches the MAP estimator's threshold (Proposition 2.10), achieving the same I* > 1 boundary that was previously only known to be achievable by the computationally intractable MAP. Compared to the degree-profiling algorithm (which trusts side information labels directly), the spectral algorithm is strictly more versatile: it succeeds even when side information is absent or too weak to shift the threshold, whereas degree-profiling fails in those regimes (Remark 5.1). The paper also unifies prior spectral results—Abbe et al. (2020) for symmetric SBM, Dhara et al. (2022a) for PDS, and Dhara et al. (2023) for censored SBM—as special cases of the genie-mimicking framework, showing that the general two-community SBM (beyond symmetric and PDS) can be handled with a single matrix (unlike the censored variant which requires two). No direct head-to-head numerical comparison against competing algorithms is provided; the contribution is the optimality guarantee itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two key pillars: the entrywise eigenvector analysis framework of Abbe et al. (2020), which characterizes the ℓ∞ behavior of leading eigenvectors of low-rank-plus-noise matrices, and the information-theoretic threshold for exact recovery with side information from Dreveton et al. (2024), which was previously only achievable by the (intractable) MAP estimator. The paper extends the line of optimal spectral algorithms for exact recovery (Abbe et al. 2020 for symmetric SBM, Dhara et al. 2022a for PDS, Dhara et al. 2023 for censored SBM) to the general two-community SBM and ROS models with arbitrary side information channels. A key departure from prior work is the introduction of genie-aided estimators as an intermediate object: the spectral algorithm is shown to approximate the per-node posterior ratio that a genie (who knows all other labels) would compute, providing a unified explanation for why various problem-specific spectral choices are optimal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a significant gap by showing that a single-stage spectral algorithm (no combinatorial cleanup) achieves the sharp information-theoretic threshold for the general SBM with side information, resolving an open question about whether the censored variant's need for two matrix representations carries over to the uncensored case (it does not). The genie-aided estimator framework offers a principled design recipe for future spectral algorithms in related settings (more communities, more general exponential families). Open questions include extending to the Gaussian Mixture Block Model, handling more than two communities where eigenvector multiplicity creates degeneracies, and determining whether the degree-profiling strategy generalizes beyond BEC/BSC channels. The practical impact is moderate: the algorithm has near-linear runtime but requires known model parameters, and the settings (logarithmic-degree SBM, Gaussian spike) are idealized.
