## Problem

The temporal prediction hypothesis (Chen et al., 2024) proposes that hippocampal sequence memory emerges through a CA3 predictor creating synaptic delay, compensated by a CA1 predictor, but its validity has only been tested against recorded neural activities and not explored from a self-supervised learning perspective. This paper asks whether a non-contrastive learner built on this hypothesis offers practical advantages—specifically robustness to representational collapse and adaptivity in online/continual learning—over the natural baseline SimSiam, whose StopGradient operation already implicitly creates a time difference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image and internally generates a triplet: the original image plus two randomly augmented views. The encoder produces a latent representation for each of the three inputs, which are then processed through two sequential predictors. The final output is a data representation (the encoder's latent) used for linear probing classification, KNN evaluation, or as features for downstream tasks like object detection on PASCAL VOC. The unusual structural detail is that the original (un-augmented) input serves as the neocortex target in Sim-2, while the two augmented views feed the hippocampal prediction pathway in Sim-1.

## Method

PhiNet extends SimSiam by inserting a second predictor g after the original predictor h, mapping them to the CA3 and CA1 regions of the hippocampal model. Training minimizes two losses jointly: Sim-1 (hippocampus loss), a symmetric negative cosine similarity between h's prediction and the StopGradient'd augmented view, and Sim-2 (neocortex loss), an MSE between g's output and the StopGradient'd original-input representation. The StopGradient operation creates an implicit one-step time difference analogous to synaptic delay, allowing the architecture to be interpreted as predicting past signals. A theoretical analysis of the linearized learning dynamics shows that the additional predictor g widens the retraction basin to non-collapsed solutions, making PhiNet less sensitive to initialization and weight decay than SimSiam. X-PhiNet further adds a momentum encoder (exponential moving average) representing neocortical long-term memory, which improves performance in online and continual learning scenarios.

## Results

On CIFAR-5m (online learning, 8 epochs), X-PhiNet(Cos) achieves 87.46% linear probing accuracy, substantially outperforming SimSiam (77.71%), BYOL (81.05%), and Barlow Twins (85.32%). On Split CIFAR-5m (continual learning, 5 tasks × 1 epoch), X-PhiNet(Cos) reaches 92.83% average accuracy with only 1.95% forgetting at weight decay 5e-4, compared to SimSiam's 90.84% accuracy and 2.45% forgetting. On CIFAR-10, PhiNet demonstrates markedly greater robustness to weight decay: at wd=0.00001, PhiNet(MSE) achieves 55.90% while SimSiam collapses to 2.63%, and at standard wd=0.0001 both reach ~68% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-PhiNet(Cos) decisively outperforms SimSiam on CIFAR-5m online learning (87.46% vs 77.71%, a ~10-point gap) and on Split CIFAR-5m continual learning (92.83% vs 90.84% accuracy at wd=5e-4), while also achieving lower forgetting (1.95% vs 2.45%). Against BYOL, X-PhiNet(Cos) leads by 6.4 points on CIFAR-5m and by 2.4 points on Split CIFAR-5m accuracy. Compared to Barlow Twins, X-PhiNet(Cos) gains 2.1 points on CIFAR-5m and 2.5 points on Split CIFAR-5m, with substantially less forgetting (1.95% vs 3.36%). Against RM-SimSiam, X-PhiNet(Cos) achieves 5.4 points higher accuracy on CIFAR-5m and 2.8 points on Split CIFAR-5m. The base PhiNet (without EMA) is comparable to SimSiam on standard CIFAR-10 linear probing (~68% at wd=0.0001) but is far more robust at small weight decay values, where SimSiam collapses (2.63% at wd=0.00001) while PhiNet retains 55.90%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SimSiam (Chen and He, 2021) by adding a second predictor (g) to model the CA1 region of the hippocampus, grounding the architecture in the temporal prediction hypothesis of Chen et al. (2024), which itself extends predictive coding to sequence memory. The learning dynamics analysis follows the framework of Tian et al. (2021) for BYOL/SimSiam, while the X-PhiNet extension with an EMA encoder draws on the Complementary Learning Systems theory (McClelland et al., 1995). The key conceptual departure from prior non-contrastive methods is the explicit interpretation of StopGradient as simulating synaptic delay, thereby connecting the SimSiam architecture to a biologically motivated hippocampal model rather than treating it as a purely algorithmic trick.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled neuroscientific rationale for why non-contrastive learning works and identifies a concrete architectural modification (the CA1 predictor) that provably widens the retraction basin against representational collapse. Practically, X-PhiNet offers a simple EMA-based extension that improves continual and online learning with minimal forgetting, which is relevant for deployment scenarios where data arrives sequentially. Key limitations include reliance on backpropagation (not biologically plausible), absence of recurrent structure for true temporal sequences, and the fact that performance gains over SimSiam on standard benchmarks are modest. Open questions include whether the architecture can learn effectively with backpropagation-free predictive coding and whether adding recurrence for time-series data would strengthen the biological analogy.
