## Problem

Existing methods for extracting neuron representations either rely on supervised training that only captures label-specific information (LOLCAT) or use implicit backpropagation-based modeling that is difficult to converge and misaligned with the Platonic Representation Hypothesis (NeuPRINT). The paper asks whether a time-invariant, modality-independent representation of each neuron's intrinsic properties (molecular identity, brain-region location, firing mode) can be learned purely from its multi-segment activity dynamics, and whether such a representation generalizes across unseen animals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251424/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-segment spiking activity from individual neurons, augmented with 50-dimensional surrounding information (47 nearest-neighbor activities, visual stimulus type encoded as a fixed integer 0/1/2, running speed, and session number) that is first compressed to 10 dimensions via CEBRA. Positive pairs are constructed by sampling two segments from the same neuron—either from different sessions or, for single-session neurons, as two non-overlapping sub-segments of varying lengths. The output is a time-invariant per-neuron embedding (the pooled latent H after removing the projection head), which serves as input to a downstream MLP classifier for neuron-type or brain-region prediction.

## Method

NeurPIR treats each neuron as a system whose multi-segment activity data are different 'modalities' of the same underlying intrinsic state, and applies VICReg contrastive learning to pull same-neuron segments together while using variance and covariance regularization to prevent collapse and encourage informative, decorrelated dimensions. Two key design choices distinguish it from prior work: (1) CEBRA is repurposed as a per-neuron preprocessing encoder that fuses peripheral activity, behavior, and session context into a compact 10-D vector concatenated with each activity segment, rather than being applied to the full population as in its original use; (2) a data-augmentation strategy extracts non-overlapping sub-segments of varying lengths from single-session recordings to create positive pairs, enabling the model to capture invariance across time scales. The encoder maps the concatenated input to a latent representation, which is aggregated via adaptive average pooling into a time-invariant embedding H; a projection head P(·) produces Z for the VICReg loss during training but is discarded at inference. The three VICReg losses—MSE invariance on positive pairs, a hinge variance loss targeting a minimum per-dimension standard deviation, and an off-diagonal covariance penalty—jointly shape the embedding space without requiring explicit negative pairs.

## Results

On simulated Izhikevich data, NeurPIR achieves the highest F1 scores across all five firing modes, reaching 0.884 (RS), 0.881 (FS), and 0.990 (LTS), outperforming NeuPRINT (0.870, 0.823, 0.980) and LOLCAT (0.841, 0.787, 0.988). On the Bugeon real dataset, NeurPIR attains a subclass-average F1 of 0.582 and class-average F1 of 0.747, surpassing NeuPRINT (0.553 / 0.706) and LOLCAT (0.401 / 0.552). On the Steinmetz out-of-domain (cross-animal) evaluation, NeurPIR retains an F1 of 0.708, compared to 0.662 for NeuPRINT and 0.652 for LOLCAT, demonstrating superior generalizability to unseen animals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeurPIR consistently outperforms all four baselines on the simulated Izhikevich data, with the largest margins over PCA and UMAP (e.g., +0.36 F1 on IB, +0.39 on CH) and smaller but consistent gains over NeuPRINT and LOLCAT (e.g., +0.014 F1 on RS, +0.058 on IB). On the Bugeon real dataset, NeurPIR leads NeuPRINT by +0.029 F1 on subclass classification and +0.041 on class classification, while the gap over LOLCAT is substantially larger (+0.181 subclass, +0.195 class F1). The most compelling comparison is the out-of-domain Steinmetz evaluation: NeurPIR's F1 drops only 0.050 from in-domain to out-of-domain (0.758→0.708), whereas NeuPRINT drops 0.081 (0.743→0.662) and LOLCAT drops 0.042 (0.694→0.652), indicating that NeurPIR's representations are more robust to cross-animal variation. NeuPRINT is the closest competitor overall, particularly on LTS neurons (0.980 vs 0.990) and in-domain Steinmetz classification, but NeurPIR maintains its advantage in every out-of-domain setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on learning time-invariant representations for individual neurons from population dynamics, most directly building on NeuPRINT (Mi et al., 2023), which uses implicit representation learning through backpropagation. NeurPIR departs by replacing this implicit approach with explicit contrastive learning (VICReg) and repurposing CEBRA—a tool previously applied to neuronal populations—as a per-neuron preprocessing step that encodes surrounding activity, behavior, and session information. The conceptual framing draws on the Platonic Representation Hypothesis (Huh et al., 2024) and borrows the positive-pair/negative-pair structure from speaker identity contrastive learning (Torres et al., 2024), adapting both to the specific constraints of neuronal data (single-session neurons, multi-scale segments).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, scalable route to extracting intrinsic neuronal properties (molecular type, brain-region location) from activity data alone, with the notable finding that learned representations generalize across unseen animals—a property NeuPRINT and LOLCAT do not share to the same degree. Limitations are acknowledged: the method struggles with fine-grained labels, is restricted to single-technology data (e.g., two-photon or Neuropixels, not both), and captures only short-term invariance. Open questions include whether the approach can track property changes over long timescales (e.g., disease progression) and whether cross-platform generalization is achievable.
