## Problem

Human inverse dynamics (ID) — recovering driven joint torques and ground reaction forces from kinematic observations — is critical for physical motion analysis but has been limited to small-scale laboratory settings due to the cost of force-plate measurements and the sensitivity of optimization-based solvers. Data-driven approaches have been proposed but remain bottlenecked by the lack of large-scale, diverse dynamics ground-truth data, and no scalable benchmark for learning-based ID exists.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250050/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ImDyS takes marker coordinates and finite-differentiated velocities over a symmetric temporal window (t−w to t+w+1, w=2), canonicalized w.r.t. the heading direction, as input. It outputs the imposed angular momentum τ_am ∈ R^((J−1)×3) (a time-accumulated torque over the 1/30 s transition) and full-body ground reaction forces λ ∈ R^(J×3) for all body segments, not just the feet. For the AddBiomechanics evaluation, a separate linear head predicts per-joint torques τ_jt instead of angular momentum. Predictions are decomposed into magnitudes and unit direction vectors, each regressed by its own linear head.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, a re-trained PHC motion imitator (Luo et al., 2023) runs in IsaacGym with a naive PD controller to imitate AMASS and KIT sequences; the key insight is that although the imitated state s_i^{t+1} may not be reached, the applied torques are physically accurate for the actually observed transition s_o^t → s_o^{t+1}, yielding 150+ hours of paired kinematics, full-body torques, and GRF (the ImDy dataset). Second, ImDyS is a 3-layer transformer encoder (dim 64) that maps the flattened windowed marker states to an ID feature, from which linear heads predict torque/GRF magnitudes (L1 loss) and directions (cosine loss). Training is regularized by a forward-dynamics cycle-consistency loss (an auxiliary FD model predicts the next frame from the predicted dynamics) and an adversarial plausibility discriminator trained on permuted/noised negative samples. A two-stage sim2real curriculum freezes the encoder after ImDy pre-training and fine-tunes only the joint-torque head on AddBiomechanics for 10 epochs.

## Results

On the primary ImDy dataset, ImDyS achieves mPJE_τ of 0.021 N·m·s/kg and mPJE_λ of 0.289 N/kg, substantially outperforming the PHC re-imitation baseline (0.095 and 0.409 respectively). On the real-world GroundLink dataset, ImDyS (trained only on simulated data) achieves mPJE_λ of 0.986 N/kg (left foot) and 1.149 N/kg (right foot), beating both PHC (2.362/2.636) and the specifically trained GroundLinkNet (5.423/2.891). On AddBiomechanics, ImDyS with only 10 epochs of fine-tuning outperforms a 150-epoch baseline trained solely on AddBiomechanics, reducing mPJE_τ by 4.30% (0.1626 vs 0.1699 Nm/kg) and mPJE_λ by 2.18% (1.0633 vs 1.0876 Nm/kg).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ImDyS consistently outperforms all three baselines across all evaluation settings. Against PHC (Luo et al., 2023), which re-imitates motions to extract torques and GRF, ImDyS reduces mPJE_τ by 78% on ImDy (0.021 vs 0.095 N·m·s/kg) and mPJE_λ by 34% (0.289 vs 0.409 N/kg), demonstrating that kinematic errors in imitation are amplified in dynamics. Against GroundLinkNet (Han et al., 2023), which is specifically trained on the 1.5-hour GroundLink dataset, ImDyS—trained exclusively on 150 hours of simulated ImDy—achieves 82% lower left-foot GRF error (0.986 vs 5.423 N/kg) and 60% lower right-foot error (1.149 vs 2.891 N/kg), showing that large-scale simulated pre-training generalizes better than small-scale real-world training. Against the AddBiomechanics baseline (same architecture trained for 150 epochs on AddBiomechanics alone), ImDyS with only 10 epochs of fine-tuning achieves 4.30% lower mPJE_τ and 2.18% lower mPJE_λ, confirming that ImDy pre-training transfers useful dynamics knowledge to real-world biomechanics data and mitigates the sim-to-real gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Perpetual Humanoid Control (PHC) imitation learning framework (Luo et al., 2023) and IsaacGym physics simulation as a scalable data generation engine for human inverse dynamics, departing from both optimization-based ID (OpenSim, AnyBody) that requires expensive force-plate laboratory setups and prior learning-based ID efforts (Zell et al., 2015/2017/2020; Lv et al., 2016) that were bottlenecked by small-scale real-world data collection. The key conceptual move is recognizing that IL policies implicitly encode ID knowledge—since they must produce physically consistent torques to match kinematics—allowing the extraction of (kinematics, torque, GRF) triplets at scale without any laboratory instrumentation. The resulting ImDyS solver is a straightforward transformer encoder with linear prediction heads, positioned as a first-step baseline rather than a methodological breakthrough, and is trained with auxiliary forward-dynamics cycle-consistency and adversarial plausibility losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ImDy's 150+ hours of full-body dynamics data (100× larger than prior full-body datasets) and inclusion of GRF for all body segments (not just feet) open the door to data-driven ID for diverse motions that are impractical to capture in labs (kneeling, jumping jacks, arm-waving). The sim2real generalization—where a model trained purely on simulated data outperforms a specifically-trained model on real GroundLink data—is encouraging but the gap remains non-trivial for non-gait motions and joints with model topology mismatches. Open questions include whether the sim2real gap can be further closed, how to extend to object interactions and non-GRF external forces, and whether the simulated physical properties (mass, inertia) of SMPL avatars introduce systematic biases that limit clinical or robotics applications.
