## Problem

Using powerful proprietary LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o) as judges for automated LLM evaluation is costly, raises privacy concerns, and lacks reproducibility, while lightweight LLMs fail as judges due to high uncertainty and positional bias during chain-of-thought analysis. The paper identifies that this performance gap stems from lightweight models' inability to conduct comprehensive analyses—a limitation not easily addressed by CoT prompting—and seeks an efficient, reproducible evaluation method that aligns with human preferences at a fraction of the cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user query, conversation history, an AI-generated response, and an instance-specific checklist of 5–10 binary questions (created once per query by GPT-4o and reused across all candidate responses). Each checklist item is evaluated independently by the lightweight judge, producing a conditional normalized score p(Yes|x,y,c)/(p(Yes)+p(No)) per item. These per-item scores are aggregated into a single 1–10 evaluation score per response, which is then used to derive pairwise comparisons and Elo-based model rankings. Prefix caching is exploited since all checklist items for a given query share the same prompt prefix.

## Method

RocketEval is a three-stage framework that reframes LLM evaluation as multi-faceted binary Q&A to circumvent the sequential-analysis failures of lightweight judges. First, GPT-4o generates an instance-specific checklist of 5–10 binary questions that distill key evaluation knowledge (e.g., correct values, required reasoning steps) for each query. Second, a lightweight LLM (e.g., Gemma-2-2B) grades each checklist item independently—avoiding positional bias from sequential CoT—using a conditional normalized score that down-weights uncertain judgments. Third, a score predictor aggregates the per-item scores: an unsupervised arithmetic mean, or a supervised Extremely Randomized Trees regressor trained on human/LLM annotations, with a KL-divergence-based weight factor α_r that blends the two based on how well the annotation distribution matches an ideal uniform spread, preventing over-reliance on the supervised predictor for queries with degenerate label distributions.

## Results

On MT-BENCH, RocketEval with Mistral-Nemo achieves 64.2% agreement with human judgments (supervised), matching the human-to-human agreement of 64.7% and approaching GPT-4o's 66.6%. On WILDBENCH, the list-level Spearman correlation reaches 0.986 with Mistral-Nemo (surpassing GPT-4o's 0.979) and 0.965 with Gemma-2-2B (comparable to GPT-4o). For 1000 evaluations on WILDBENCH, RocketEval with Gemma-2-2B costs $27.7 versus $3,400 for GPT-4o, a >120× cost reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4o (66.6% MT-BENCH agreement, 0.979 Spearman on WILDBENCH), RocketEval with Mistral-Nemo slightly surpasses it on list-level correlation (0.986 Spearman) while matching it on instance-level agreement (64.2% vs 66.6%). Against the fine-tuned judge Prometheus-7B-v2.0 (55.7% agreement, 0.949 Spearman), even the smallest RocketEval judges (Gemma-2-2B at 57.3%, Qwen2.5-1.5B at 60.2%) outperform it on instance-level agreement, and all lightweight judges exceed its list-level correlation. Against GPT-4 (65.8% pairwise, 59.6% single on MT-BENCH), RocketEval with 7-12B models (Llama-3-8B: 63.8%, Mistral-Nemo: 64.2%) approaches or matches GPT-4's pairwise performance while using open-source models at a fraction of the cost. The fixed-checklist baseline (6 generic questions) consistently underperforms RocketEval's instance-specific checklist, confirming that query-tailored questions are essential for lightweight judges.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm (Zheng et al., 2023; Dubois et al., 2023) and specifically adopts the instance-specific checklist concept from WildBench (Lin et al., 2025), but fundamentally reframes the evaluation task: rather than using a powerful LLM to generate a holistic CoT analysis and score, it decomposes evaluation into independent binary checklist-item judgments performed by lightweight LLMs. It departs from prior fine-tuned judge models (Prometheus, JudgeLM) by avoiding task-specific fine-tuning that can degrade general comprehension, and from CoT-based prompting by showing that sequential analysis amplifies uncertainty and positional bias in small models. The supervised reweighting via Extremely Randomized Trees and the conditional normalized score are novel mechanisms not present in prior checklist or rubric-based evaluation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50× cost reduction (e.g., $27.7 vs. $3,400 for 1,000 WildBench evaluations) makes large-scale, reproducible LLM evaluation feasible for academic labs and iterative development pipelines that cannot afford repeated GPT-4o calls. The finding that a 2B-parameter model can match GPT-4o's ranking correlation (0.965 Spearman) challenges the assumption that evaluation quality requires frontier-scale models. Open questions include: the one-time GPT-4o cost for checklist generation (which limits full open-source reproducibility), generalizability to highly creative or novel tasks where binary checklist items may not capture quality nuances, and whether the approach degrades when the test models are from the same family as the judge (observed Llama-series bias in Arena-Hard).
