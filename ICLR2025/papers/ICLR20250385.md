## Problem

Sampling from unnormalized Boltzmann densities via learned density paths requires that the path be absolutely continuous in the Wasserstein geometry (i.e., admit an integrable velocity field), but this was not established for the class of Fisher-Rao curves arising from Boltzmann interpolations. Moreover, the natural linear interpolation of energies was observed numerically to produce a velocity field whose L₂ norm explodes near the target, causing a 'teleportation-of-mass' failure, while the learned interpolation of Máté & Fleuret (2023) lacks any theoretical guarantee on the regularity of the resulting path.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the unnormalized energy function f_D: ℝ^d → ℝ (no access to the normalizing constant Z_D) together with samples from a simple source distribution ρ_Z (typically a standard Gaussian). It produces samples from the target Boltzmann density ρ_D = e^{-f_D}/Z_D by simulating the learned ODE backward from the source. The method requires knowledge of the spatial domain of the target and evaluates the loss at points drawn from a mixture of uniform samples and interpolated source-target pairs per the OU closed-form solution.

## Method

The paper proposes a 'gradient flow interpolation' parameterizing f_t = (T−t)/T · f_D + t·ψ_t with the velocity field fixed as v_t = ∇(f_t − f_Z), so that only ψ_t is learned by a neural network. This choice makes the continuity equation reduce to the Fokker-Planck equation of a Langevin SDE (an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process when ρ_Z is Gaussian), which is the Wasserstein gradient flow of KL(·|ρ_Z) and guarantees a well-behaved, strictly positive density path. Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorems 1–2) that under a partial Poincaré inequality on the disintegration measure, every Fisher-Rao curve of Boltzmann densities is Wasserstein absolutely continuous with an optimal velocity field, closing a gap left by prior fixed-time Poisson equation results. An analytic 1D example using quantile functions demonstrates that the linear interpolation's velocity norm ‖v_t‖_{L₂(ρ_t)} grows roughly exponentially with the separation of target modes, quantifying the teleportation failure. In practice, the gradient flow interpolation is reparametrized to the unit interval via a learned time schedule g(t) and trained by minimizing the pointwise continuity-equation residual with Hutchinson trace estimators for divergences and Laplacians.

## Results

On the 40-mode Gaussian Mixture Model (2D), the proposed gradient flow interpolation achieves an effective sample size (ESS) of 0.992±5e-5, negative log-likelihood (NLL) of 6.864±0.005, and energy distance of 0.0021±0.001, substantially outperforming the linear interpolation (ESS 0.183, energy 0.247) and the learned interpolation (ESS 0.922, energy 0.121). On the 8-dimensional Many Well distribution, the learned interpolation achieves the best energy distance (9e-5) with NLL 6.876, while the gradient flow interpolation is competitive (energy 8e-4, NLL 6.989). In the 16-dimensional Many Well setting, the learned interpolation again leads (NLL 18.24, energy 1e-4), with the gradient flow interpolation trailing (NLL 20.53, energy 0.009).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its proposed gradient flow interpolation against the linear and learned interpolations from Máté & Fleuret (2023). On the 40-mode GMM, the gradient flow method decisively wins on all three metrics, achieving near-perfect ESS (0.992 vs. 0.922 for learned) and an energy distance roughly 58× lower than the learned interpolation (0.0021 vs. 0.121). The linear interpolation from Máté & Fleuret performs worst, with ESS of only 0.183, confirming the paper's theoretical finding that its velocity field norm explodes near t=1. On the 8D Many Well distribution, the learned interpolation from Máté & Fleuret achieves the best energy distance (9e-5) and NLL (6.876), narrowly beating the gradient flow method (energy 8e-4, NLL 6.989). In the 16D Many Well setting, the learned interpolation again outperforms the gradient flow approach (NLL 18.24 vs. 20.53; energy 1e-4 vs. 0.009), which the authors attribute to the greater flexibility of jointly learning both the curve and vector field. Overall, the gradient flow interpolation is most advantageous when the target has widely separated modes relative to the source, while the learned interpolation's extra degrees of freedom help in higher-dimensional settings with concentrated modes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Fisher-Rao geometry (information geometry) and Wasserstein optimal transport, building directly on Máté & Fleuret's (2023) continuity-equation-based framework for learning Boltzmann density interpolations. It generalizes the fixed-time Poisson equation results of Laugesen et al. (2014) to a global-in-time setting, proving for the first time that a broad class of Fisher-Rao curves of Boltzmann densities are Wasserstein absolutely continuous. The proposed 'gradient flow interpolation' recasts the sampling problem as the Wasserstein gradient flow of KL divergence (equivalently, the Fokker-Planck equation of an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process), connecting to the score-based diffusion literature of Song et al. (2021) and the recent PINN-based sampling work of Sun et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical contribution—establishing that Boltzmann density curves are Wasserstein absolutely continuous under mild Poincaré inequality conditions—closes a gap that has left the existence of driving velocity fields for these curves formally unproven in the global-in-time setting. The analytical demonstration that linear interpolation produces exponentially exploding velocity norms (growing with mode separation) provides a rigorous explanation for the 'teleportation' pathology observed empirically, guiding practitioners toward better-behaved paths. However, the practical gradient flow method is essentially the well-known OU process, and the paper acknowledges that the learned interpolation (Máté & Fleuret) remains competitive in higher dimensions, leaving open whether the theoretical well-posedness advantage translates to consistent empirical superiority. Open questions include the behavior of ‖v_t‖_{L₂(ρ_t)} for the gradient flow path and whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional problems without expensive divergence/Laplacian computations.
