## Problem

High-quality alignment data for LLMs remains private even when model weights are open (e.g., Llama-3-Instruct), hindering democratization of AI. Existing synthetic data methods rely on prompt engineering and seed questions, which limit diversity as dataset scale grows, while human-curated datasets are expensive and labor-intensive. The paper asks whether high-quality instruction data can be synthesized at scale by extracting it directly from an aligned LLM with no human intervention or seed prompts.

## Input / Output

The sole input is the pre-query template of an aligned LLM (e.g., `<|start_header_id|>user<|end_header_id|>` for Llama-3) — no seed questions, no system prompt, no prompt engineering. The output is instruction-response pairs in the model's native chat format, with extensions producing multi-turn conversations (by appending the pre-query template after each assistant turn) and preference pairs (chosen/rejected responses scored by a reward model). The pipeline is fully automated via VLLM inference on open-weight models, producing 3M (Air) and 1M (Pro) single-turn conversations plus 300K multi-turn and 100K DPO datasets per model.

## Method

MAGPIE exploits the autoregressive nature of aligned LLMs: when given only the pre-query template, the model spontaneously generates a user query because it was fine-tuned on instruction data in that template format. The two-step pipeline first generates instructions by feeding the bare pre-query template (using higher temperature for diversity), then generates responses by wrapping each instruction with the post-query and pre-query templates (using greedy decoding for accuracy). This separation allows independent tuning of instruction diversity versus response quality and enables modular pairing of instructions with responses from different sources. Extensions include multi-turn generation (appending the pre-query template iteratively with a system prompt to maintain role awareness), DPO data creation (sampling k responses per instruction at T<1, then selecting highest/lowest reward-model-scored responses as chosen/rejected), and domain-specific or multilingual generation via tailored system prompts. Applied to Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Llama-3-70B-Instruct, the method produces datasets that, when used for SFT alone, surpass all public baselines and even the official Llama-3-8B-Instruct on AlpacaEval 2, using only 300K–400K samples versus the official >10M.

## Results

On AlpacaEval 2 (vs GPT-4-Turbo baseline), MAGPIE-Pro-300K-Filtered (SFT only, 300K conversations) achieves a length-controlled win rate of 25.08%, surpassing the official Llama-3-8B-Instruct (22.92%) which was trained on >10M data points with SFT+DPO. With DPO applied (MAGPIE-Pro-DPO, 100K preference pairs), the model reaches 50.10% LC win rate vs GPT-4-Turbo, effectively matching GPT-4-Turbo itself. On Arena-Hard, MAGPIE-Pro-DPO achieves 35.7% win rate vs GPT-4 (0314), compared to 20.6% for Llama-3-8B-Instruct. All MAGPIE SFT-only models (LC 21.65–25.08%) outperform every baseline SFT dataset (LC 7.21–14.62%) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGPIE-Pro-300K-Filtered (SFT only) achieves 25.08% LC vs GPT-4-Turbo, outperforming all SFT baselines by a large margin: WildChat (14.62%), OpenHermes 2.5 (12.89%), Tulu V2 Mix (9.91%), ShareGPT (9.73%), and Self-Instruct (7.21%). It also surpasses the SFT+DPO UltraFeedback baseline (18.36%) using only SFT and 300K data vs. 64K DPO pairs. Against the official Llama-3-8B-Instruct (>10M data, SFT+DPO), MAGPIE-Pro-300K-Filtered wins on AlpacaEval 2 LC (25.08% vs 22.92%) but trails on Arena-Hard (18.9% vs 20.6%). With DPO added, MAGPIE-Pro-DPO (50.10% LC vs GPT-4-Turbo, 35.7% Arena-Hard) decisively beats Llama-3-8B-Instruct on both benchmarks and matches GPT-4-Turbo on AlpacaEval 2. On Qwen base models (Table 2), MAGPIE-aligned Qwen1.5-4B achieves 68.09% LC vs its official chat counterpart (50%), and MAGPIE-aligned Qwen2-1.5B reaches 56.66% vs 50% for the official model, confirming cross-architecture generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGPIE departs from both major lineages of alignment data construction: human-curated datasets (ShareGPT, WildChat, Dolly) and synthetic generation via seed-question prompting (Self-Instruct, Evol Instruct, UltraChat, GenQA). Its key conceptual departure is exploiting the autoregressive nature of aligned LLMs in a way that prior synthetic methods did not—by feeding only the pre-query chat template (e.g., '<|start_header_id|>user<|end_header_id|>') and letting the model generate the instruction, rather than engineering prompts or expanding seed questions. This is tangentially related to training-data extraction work (Carlini et al., Nasr et al., Bai et al.), but the goal is inverted: rather than recovering private training data, MAGPIE deliberately harvests the instruction distribution the model internalized during alignment to create a public dataset. The preference-optimization extension builds directly on iterative DPO alignment (Tran et al., 2023) and reward-model-based response selection (Meng et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a two-step pipeline requiring no human labor, no API access, and no prompt engineering produces alignment data that, at 300K–400K samples, matches or exceeds the official Llama-3-8B-Instruct trained on 10M+ samples with SFT and DPO. This democratizes access to high-quality alignment data for the open-source community and provides a scalable, low-cost ($0.12–$1.10 per 1,000 instances) alternative to labor-intensive curation. Open questions remain: the method underperforms on reasoning-heavy benchmarks (GSM8K, MMLU) relative to the official model, the 'implicit memorization of instruction distributions' hypothesis is unverified, and it is unclear how well the approach transfers to models with very different alignment procedures or to tasks requiring deep multi-step reasoning. The MagpieLM models already ranking first among <10B open-source instruction models suggest the technique will be widely adopted.
