## Problem

Existing immunogenicity prediction methods for reverse vaccinology rely on highly compressed ACC-transformed physicochemical descriptors and simple classifiers, which limits their ability to capture the complex sequence-structure-immunogenicity relationship and results in poor accuracy and cross-species generalizability. Additionally, no comprehensive benchmark dataset spanning multiple pathogen classes (bacteria, virus, tumor) existed for rigorous evaluation of prediction models.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three input modalities per protein: (1) an amino acid sequence encoded by a pre-trained PLM (ESM2-650M, ANKH-large, or ProtBert) into L×d embeddings, (2) a predicted 3D structure (from ESMFold or AlphaFold2) tokenized at two scales — FOLDSEEK atomic-level 20-dim tokens and ESM3 peptide-level 4,096-dim tokens — and (3) eight hand-crafted physicochemical descriptors (5 E-descriptors + 3 Z-descriptors). The primary output is a binary immunogenicity label (protective vs. non-protective antigen), and a secondary output is a per-residue attention weight vector from the attention pooling layer, which the authors interpret as epitope markers and validate against experimentally mapped B-cell epitopes.

## Method

VENUSVACCINE introduces a dual cross-attention mechanism where two structural tokenizations serve as queries attending to sequence embeddings as keys and values: a finer-level component uses FOLDSEEK atomic tokens to capture local atomic interactions, and a coarser-level component uses ESM3 peptide tokens to capture substructure-level functional motifs. Both components employ multi-head cross-attention with Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) for relative positional encoding. The four feature streams (original sequence embedding, fine-attention output, coarse-attention output, and physicochemical descriptors) are concatenated and compressed via attention pooling — a masked 1D convolution followed by softmax — into a single protein-level vector, which is passed through a small MLP prediction head. The model is trained end-to-end with supervised binary cross-entropy on the newly curated ImmunoDB (7,216 labeled antigens), leveraging pre-trained PLM representations as a transfer-learning backbone rather than fine-tuning the PLM itself.

## Results

On the ImmunoDB benchmark, VENUSVACCINE-ProtBert achieves the highest accuracy across all three tasks: 84.5% ACC (MCC 65.9, F1 77.0) on Bacteria, 91.4% ACC (MCC 82.8, F1 91.2) on Virus, and 71.5% ACC (MCC 44.7, F1 67.6) on Tumor. The Ankh variant leads on Virus with 92.2% ACC and 99.7% T30 (top-30 positive rate), while the ESM2 variant achieves the best KS statistic on Bacteria (0.66). In post-hoc validation on the Helicobacter pylori proteome, VENUSVACCINE achieves 100% recall on 11 known protective antigens with a fold-enrichment of 15.11, and on the SARS-CoV-2 proteome it correctly ranks the spike protein as the top vaccine target among 38 candidates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VENUSVACCINE outperforms all four specialized web-server baselines on every dataset where they overlap. Against VaxiJen3.0 (the strongest traditional baseline), VENUSVACCINE-ProtBert gains +1.2 ACC on Bacteria (84.5 vs. 83.3) and +23.3 ACC on Virus (91.4 vs. 68.1), while on Tumor the gap is dramatic: 71.5 vs. 39.0 ACC. VaxiJen2.0 trails by 8.8 ACC on Bacteria and 9.4 ACC on Virus. Vaxi-DL, a deep-learning server, is substantially weaker (68.1 ACC Bacteria, 65.3 ACC Virus), and VirusImmu, restricted to viral targets, scores only 58.8 ACC on Virus. Among the retrained classical ML baselines, XGBoost is the closest competitor on Virus (89.1 ACC) but still lags VENUSVACCINE-Ankh by 3.1 ACC; on Tumor, XGBoost (69.2 ACC) is outperformed by all three VENUSVACCINE variants by 2.3–7.7 ACC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant VAXIJEN lineage (Doytchinova et al., 2007; Dimitrov et al., 2020), which compresses protein information into fixed-length vectors via auto-cross-covariance (ACC) transformation of physicochemical descriptors and feeds them to classical ML classifiers. VENUSVACCINE instead leverages pre-trained protein language models (ESM2, ANKH, ProtBert) for sequence embeddings and introduces a dual cross-attention mechanism that integrates two scales of structural tokens—atomic-level FOLDSEEK tokens (Van Kempen et al., 2023) and peptide-level ESM3 tokens (Hayes et al., 2024)—with the sequence representation, while retaining hand-crafted E- and Z-descriptors as auxiliary features. The paper also positions itself against the first deep-learning vaccine prediction tool, VAXI-DL (Rawal et al., 2022), and the viral-specific VirusImmu (Li et al., 2023), arguing that prior methods suffer from excessive feature compression and limited generalizability across pathogen types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ImmunoDB (7,216 labeled antigens spanning bacteria, virus, and tumor) is the most comprehensive immunogenicity benchmark to date and will serve as a standard resource for future model development. The post-hoc validation protocol—demonstrating 100% recall on 11 known H. pylori immunogens with 15.1× fold-enrichment, and correctly ranking the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein as the top candidate among 38 proteome sequences—provides practical evidence that computational predictions can directly inform experimental vaccine design. Key limitations include reliance on predicted (rather than experimental) structures, the inability to retrain web-server baselines on the new dataset (introducing potential data-leakage asymmetry), and the absence of wet-lab confirmation. Open questions include whether the dual-attention architecture generalizes to other protein property prediction tasks and whether the attention-pooling weights can be systematically validated as epitope markers beyond the two case studies presented.
