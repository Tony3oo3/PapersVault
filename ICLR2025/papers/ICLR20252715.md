## Problem

Existing LLM-based multi-agent systems achieve strong collective intelligence through carefully designed communication topologies, but this comes at a severe token cost—2× to 11.8× more prompt tokens than simple chain structures—making large-scale deployment economically prohibitive. The paper is the first to formally identify and define 'Communication Redundancy' in these systems: a substantial portion of spatial (intra-dialogue) and temporal (inter-dialogue) message passing does not meaningfully contribute to task-solving, as evidenced by performance gains when randomly removing 10–30% of connections.

## Input / Output

AgentPrune takes as input an existing LLM-MA framework with its predefined spatial-temporal communication graph (binary adjacency matrices A^S for intra-dialogue edges and A^T for inter-dialogue edges), agent profiles/roles, and a benchmark of Q queries. It produces a pruned sparse topology G^sub via binary masks B^S and B^T (obtained by TopK magnitude pruning on the optimized continuous masks), which is then fixed for all subsequent reasoning rounds. The spatial graph is constrained to be a DAG to ensure sequential topological execution, and the method operates as a plug-and-play module requiring at least three agents and a non-trivial communication structure (chain or direct-output are too simple to benefit).

## Method

AgentPrune models the full multi-agent pipeline as a spatial-temporal graph and relaxes its binary adjacency matrices into continuous differentiable graph masks S^S and S^T, optimized over K' initial dialogue rounds (K' ≪ K) using a two-objective loss: (1) a policy-gradient-based distribution approximation term that maximizes expected system utility φ (non-differentiable, handled via REINFORCE-style sampling over M graph realizations), and (2) a nuclear-norm low-rank sparsity penalty that promotes sparse, robust connectivity. After K' rounds, a one-shot TopK magnitude pruning step converts the continuous masks into binary masks, yielding a fixed sparse topology G^sub for the remaining K−K' rounds. For benchmarks with many queries but few dialogue rounds (e.g., MMLU), a multi-query training paradigm optimizes the topology on only Q′ (≪ Q) queries before pruning. The low-rank regularization also provides adversarial robustness, as low-rank graphs are empirically more resistant to agent-targeted attacks.

## Results

AgentPrune achieves the best average score of 89.72 (AgentPrune-C) across six benchmarks, outperforming the strongest baselines PHP (87.02) and LLM-Debate (87.01) by ~2.7 points. On HumanEval, AgentPrune-R reaches 90.30 pass@1, surpassing GPTSwarm (88.49) and DyLAN (89.70); on GSM8K, AgentPrune-R scores 95.83, exceeding PHP (92.45) and LLM-Debate (90.23). Critically, AgentPrune achieves these results at a fraction of the cost: $5.6 vs. $43.7 for SOTA topologies on MMLU, and reduces prompt tokens by 28.1%–72.8% when plugged into AutoGen or GPTSwarm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AgentPrune consistently outperforms all three categories of baselines. Against single-agent prompting methods (CoT, ComplexCoT, Self-Consistency), AgentPrune-C gains 4.7–5.4 points in average accuracy, with the largest margin on GSM8K (+10.22 over Vanilla) and HumanEval (+17.70 over Vanilla). Among spatial communication topologies, AgentPrune surpasses GPTSwarm (86.77 avg) by 2.95 points and Complete Graph (86.55) by 3.17 points, while also exceeding the best spatial-only method on HumanEval (90.30 vs. GPTSwarm's 88.49). Against temporal communication methods, AgentPrune-C beats PHP (87.02) and LLM-Debate (87.01) by ~2.7 points on average, with particularly large gains on GSM8K (95.62 vs. 92.45 for PHP) and AQuA (79.47 vs. 77.52 for LLM-Debate). When integrated as a plug-in into AutoGen and GPTSwarm, AgentPrune maintains or slightly improves performance (e.g., +2.79 on GSM8K+AutoGen, +0.84 on GSM8K+GPTSwarm) while reducing prompt token consumption by 28.1%–72.8% and cutting costs by up to $177.58 on GSM8K+GPTSwarm. The only case where AgentPrune slightly underperforms the original framework is MMLU+GPTSwarm (83.05 vs. 83.98, a 0.93-point drop), but this comes with a 67.6% reduction in prompt tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on optimizing multi-agent communication topologies (GPTSwarm, MacNet) by unifying spatial and temporal communication into a single spatial-temporal graph and applying graph sparsification techniques from the neural network pruning literature. It draws on the Early-bird principle (Achille et al., 2018) to justify limited-round optimization before one-shot magnitude pruning, and on low-rank graph defense (Entezari et al., 2020) to regularize the learned connectivity. The key departure from prior work is the formal identification of 'Communication Redundancy' as a distinct problem and the application of one-shot TopK pruning to the communication graph, rather than continuous topology optimization as in GPTSwarm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing multi-agent system costs by 6–8× while maintaining or improving accuracy makes large-scale deployment of LLM-based multi-agent systems economically viable, particularly for edge devices and high-volume inference. The plug-and-play design (integrated into AutoGen and GPTSwarm with 28–73% token reduction) lowers the barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether one-shot pruning generalizes to dynamically changing agent pools, whether the topology can be re-optimized as task difficulty shifts, and whether the approach scales beyond 5 agents or to heterogeneous model backbones. The adversarial robustness bonus from low-rank regularization is promising but underexplored.
