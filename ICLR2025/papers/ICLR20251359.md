## Problem

LLM-based TableQA methods suffer from two key weaknesses: direct question decomposition often overlooks critical information, creating a semantic gap between sub-questions and the original query, and LLM self-correction during answer verification tends to produce prompt-aligned responses that contradict the actual table context, making error detection unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251359/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language question and a table context serialized with pipe-delimited cells (value₁|value₂|...|valueₙ) as input, and produces a final answer that can be a computed number, a specific cell value, or a yes/no verdict for fact verification. The pipeline uses GPT-3.5 at temperature 0.8, generating five candidate answers per mode (DP and Agent) before majority voting selects the final result.

## Method

TIDE (Triple-Inspired Decomposition and vErification) replaces direct question decomposition with a two-step triple-based process: the LLM first extracts all entities and relations from the question to form structured triples (head entity, relation, tail entity), then converts each triple into a natural language sub-question. The fixed triple structure compels the LLM to capture more entities and relations than free-form decomposition, reducing the semantic gap. For verification, instead of relying on LLM self-correction, each intermediate answer is converted into triples and structurally compared against the question triples—acceptance requires matching relation and at least one matching entity (head or tail), with mismatches triggering regeneration. The method integrates both Direct Prompting (CoT-based textual reasoning) and Agent (Python code generation) modes, combining their verified outputs via majority voting for joint reasoning.

## Results

On WikiTableQuestions, TIDE-DP&Agent achieves 75.00% exact-match accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA (Liu et al., 2024a) DP&Agent (73.65%) by 1.35 points and the next-best LLM-based method Cabinet (69.10%) by 5.9 points. On TabFact, TIDE-DP&Agent reaches 89.82% accuracy, outperforming the prior best (Liu et al., 2024a) at 88.50% by 1.32 points. Standalone, TIDE-Agent (68.72% WTQ, 88.19% TabFact) consistently outperforms TIDE-DP (66.51% WTQ, 81.32% TabFact), attributed to the Agent's ability to generate Python code for structured table manipulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIDE-DP&Agent outperforms all 19 baselines on both datasets. On WikiTableQuestions, it beats the closest competitor (Liu et al., 2024a) DP&Agent by 1.35 points (75.00 vs. 73.65), Cabinet by 5.9 points, and ReAcTable by 7.0 points. On TabFact, the margin over (Liu et al., 2024a) is 1.32 points (89.82 vs. 88.50), over StructGPT by 2.52 points, and over ReAcTable by 3.72 points. Notably, TIDE-Agent alone (68.72% WTQ) surpasses TIDE-DP (66.51%) and even the joint SOTA of (Liu et al., 2024a) in Agent-only mode (63.77%), suggesting the triple-based decomposition and verification are particularly beneficial for code-generating agents. The paper also validates TIDE across multiple LLMs (Llama 2-7b, Llama 3-70b, GLM4, Gemini 1.5, Claude 3.5), showing consistent 2.7–5.6 point improvements over each model's direct QA baseline, and a 26.3-point gap over GPT-3.5 direct QA (75.00 vs. 48.70), ruling out data contamination as the primary source of gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIDE builds directly on the decomposition-reasoning-verification pipeline established by Liu et al. (2024a), which combines Direct Prompting and Agent modes for LLM-based TableQA. The key departure is the introduction of triples (head entity, relation, tail entity) as an explicit structuring mechanism for both question decomposition and answer verification, replacing the ad-hoc CoT/SQL-based decomposition and LLM self-correct verification used in prior work. Conceptually, the paper repurposes a knowledge-graph primitive (triples) to impose a fixed extraction schema on the LLM, arguing that this structural constraint forces more complete information capture during decomposition and enables deterministic entity/relation matching during verification rather than relying on the LLM's biased self-assessment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The triple-based structuring offers a lightweight, model-agnostic plug-in that can be applied to existing DP or Agent pipelines without architectural changes, making it practically accessible. However, the verification mechanism relies on exact string matching of entities and relations, which may be brittle for paraphrased or numerically complex answers (as acknowledged in the error analysis). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-table or open-domain settings, whether the fixed triple schema can handle deeply nested or conditional questions without combinatorial explosion, and whether the gains persist as base LLMs improve in their native decomposition and self-correction abilities.
