## Problem

Existing retrieval-augmented conversational agents construct memory banks at turn-level, session-level, or via summarization, but each granularity has fundamental limitations: turn-level units are fragmentary and miss dispersed relevant information, session-level units contain excessive irrelevant content, and summarization loses critical details. The paper asks whether a novel memory structure can outperform all three, and whether the inherent redundancy in natural language can be exploited to improve retrieval accuracy.

## Input / Output

The system takes a multi-session conversation history (each session containing sequential user-agent turns) and a current user request as input, and produces a personalized response as output. The memory bank is constructed at the segment level, where each segment is a topically coherent group of consecutive turns within a session. Retrieval operates under a fixed context budget (e.g., 4k tokens on LOCOMO, 1k tokens on Long-MT-Bench+), and the retrieved segments are concatenated in chronological order as context for the LLM response generator.

## Method

SECOM introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, a conversation segmentation model (GPT-4 zero-shot, or lighter alternatives like Mistral-7B or a fine-tuned RoBERTa) partitions each session into topically coherent segments, striking a balance between the fragmentation of turn-level and the noise of session-level memory. When limited annotated data is available, the segmentation guidance is iteratively refined via LLM self-reflection on hard examples (top-K by WindowDiff), analogous to SGD optimization of a textual 'prefix.' Second, LLMLingua-2 prompt compression is applied to all memory units before retrieval, removing natural language redundancy that acts as noise; this denoising increases query-relevant segment similarity while decreasing query-irrelevant similarity, improving retrieval recall by up to 9.46 GPT4Score points. The retrieved segments are then concatenated in time order and fed to an LLM (GPT-3.5-Turbo or Mistral-7B) for response generation, bypassing summarization to avoid information loss.

## Results

On LOCOMO, SECOM (MPNet, GPT-4-Seg) achieves a GPT4Score of 69.33, outperforming the strongest baseline ConditionMem (65.92) by 3.41 points and MemoChat (65.10) by 4.23 points. On Long-MT-Bench+, SECOM (MPNet, GPT-4-Seg) reaches 88.81 GPT4Score, surpassing MemoChat (85.14) by 3.67 points and Turn-Level MPNet (84.91) by 3.90 points. SECOM also demonstrates robustness across retrievers: the gap between BM25 and MPNet variants is only 2.24 points on LOCOMO, compared to up to 11.98 points for Turn-Level and 7.89 points for Session-Level baselines. Ablation shows that removing compression-based denoising drops GPT4Score by up to 9.46 points on LOCOMO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SECOM outperforms all six compared baselines on both primary benchmarks. Against SumMem (LangChain), SECOM (MPNet, GPT-4-Seg) leads by 15.46 GPT4Score on LOCOMO (69.33 vs. 53.87) and 25.39 on Long-MT-Bench+ (88.81 vs. 63.42), with the paper attributing SumMem's weakness to information loss during summarization. Against RecurSum (Wang et al.), SECOM gains 13.08 and 25.85 GPT4Score on LOCOMO and Long-MT-Bench+ respectively, again due to detail loss in recursive summarization. Against ConditionMem (Yuan et al.), SECOM leads by 3.41 on LOCOMO and 25.26 on Long-MT-Bench+, showing that turn-level conditional memory is particularly fragile on shorter conversations. Against MemoChat (Lu et al.), the closest competitor, SECOM wins by 4.23 on LOCOMO and 3.67 on Long-MT-Bench+ while using fewer input tokens (1,722 vs. 7,233) and lower latency (2.61s vs. 5.60s). On dialogue segmentation, SECOM's zero-shot GPT-4 model achieves a Score of 0.895 on DialSeg711 and 0.738 on SuperDialSeg, surpassing DialSTART (Gao et al.) which reports only Pk=0.178/WD=0.198 on DialSeg711. On CoQA and Persona-Chat, SECOM outperforms COMEDY (Chen et al.) by 0.83 GPT4Score on CoQA (98.31 vs. 97.48) and 1.82 on Persona-Chat (78.34 vs. 76.52).

## Relation to Prior Work

SECOM extends the retrieval-augmented memory management paradigm for conversational agents by introducing a segment-level memory granularity that sits between the turn-level and session-level units used in prior work (ConditionMem, MemoChat, RecurSum). It repurposes LLMLingua-2, originally designed for inference-time prompt compression, as a plug-and-play denoising mechanism applied to memory units before retrieval — a novel application that avoids the information loss inherent in summarization-based approaches. The conversation segmentation module adapts the Reflexion self-reflection loop to iteratively learn a textual segmentation rubric from limited annotated data, drawing an explicit analogy to prefix-tuning optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that segment-level memory consistently outperforms both finer and coarser granularities, and that a simple compression step improves retrieval recall by 10+ points, provides practitioners with a practical, low-overhead recipe for building long-term conversational agents. The approach is notably robust across retrievers (BM25 vs. MPNet) and segmentation backbones (GPT-4 down to RoBERTa), making it deployable in resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the segment-level advantage persists at even longer conversation horizons (beyond ~300 turns), how the method generalizes to multimodal or multi-agent conversations, and whether the GPT-4 segmentation cost is acceptable for high-throughput production systems.
