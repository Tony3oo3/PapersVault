## Problem

Exact attention computation in transformers requires O(n²d) time, which is prohibitive for long context lengths. While prior work has explored sparsity, kernel methods, and low-rank approximations under various assumptions, no general-purpose method existed that could provably identify all 'large' attention scores (entries above a threshold ε) in time linear in n without assumptions on the data. This paper fills that gap by showing a small 'universal set' of keys, depending only on K and not on Q, contains all large attention scores for any query.

## Input / Output

The method takes a key matrix K (n×d), a non-negative function f (e.g., |x|^p for even p), and a threshold ε > 0, and produces a universal set U ⊆ [n] of key indices with |U| ≤ Ψ^f/ε (independent of n) such that for any query matrix Q, every attention score A_{i,j} ≥ ε has j ∈ U. Given U and a new query q, all large attention scores involving q can be computed in poly(d/ε) time with workspace that does not grow with n. The algorithm supports streaming (1-pass and 2-pass) and distributed settings, with total memory poly(d/ε) words.

## Method

The key insight is defining f-sensitivities σ_i^f(K) = sup_{y≠0} f(⟨K_i, y⟩)/Σ_j f(⟨K_j, y⟩) and proving that the set of rows with σ_i^f(K) ≥ ε forms a universal set of size at most Ψ^f/ε, where Ψ^f = sup_K Σ_i σ_i^f(K) is independent of n. For f(x) = x², these reduce to classical leverage scores (Ψ^f = d), computable exactly via QR decomposition in O(nd²) time or approximately via sketching in nnz(K) + poly(d/ε) time. For f(x) = |x|^p, ℓ_p-Lewis weights bound the f-sensitivities, yielding |U| = O(d/ε) for p ≤ 2 and O(d^{p/2}/ε) for p > 2. For even p, Khatri-Rao products reduce exact normalization to a p = 2 SVD-based computation, enabling exact large-attention recovery in poly(d/ε) time per query after O(n·poly(d/ε)) preprocessing. The paper further formulates a planted model where queries are noisy convex combinations of a small key subset, proving sublinear-time identification of relevant keys, and validates the approach empirically on ViT models trained on ImageNet-1k.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k validation, the softmax ViT baselines achieve 76.47% (S/16), 78.83% (L/16), and 79.47% (L/8) top-1 accuracy. When LevAttention (top-32 keys by leverage score) is used only at inference on these pretrained models, accuracy drops sharply (e.g., L/8 falls to 0.8%), but training from scratch with the leverage-score-based attention mechanism recovers most performance: L/16 reaches 75.12% and L/8 reaches 71.96% with only 32 keys selected per head (out of 196 or 784 total). Increasing to top-64 keys further improves accuracy to 77.27% (L/16) and 74.64% (L/8), achieving over 90% of full softmax accuracy while attending to only ~16–20% of keys.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the standard softmax ViT (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021), which achieves 76.47%, 78.83%, and 79.47% on S/16, L/16, and L/8 respectively. LevAttention trained from scratch with top-32 keys loses 1.3–7.5 percentage points relative to full softmax attention, with the gap narrowing as more keys are retained (top-64: 77.27% for L/16 vs. 78.83% softmax). A key secondary comparison is against ℓ₂ row-norm-based key selection: when applied at inference on softmax-pretrained models, leverage score selection dramatically outperforms row-norm selection (48.58% vs. 8.9% for L/16; 13.3% vs. 3.3% for S/16), confirming that leverage scores are a much better predictor of key importance. However, the authors note that when training from scratch, row-norm selection and even random key selection achieve accuracies comparable to leverage score selection, suggesting the theoretical advantage of leverage scores has not yet been fully exploited in the training procedure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes leverage scores and their generalizations (ℓ_p-sensitivities, Lewis weights) from randomized numerical linear algebra to identify a query-independent 'universal set' of keys that provably captures all large attention scores. It builds directly on Cohen & Peng's (2015) Lewis weight sampling for ℓ_p norms and Cohen et al.'s (2016a) online leverage scores for streaming, applying these tools to the attention mechanism for the first time in a universal-set formulation. Unlike prior efficient attention approaches (sparse patterns, kernel methods, low-rank approximations) that impose structural assumptions on Q, K, or V, this work makes no data assumptions and provides guarantees for arbitrary query matrices, positioning it as a complementary rather than competing approach to methods like PolySketchFormer or HyperAttention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical guarantee (universal set of size O(d/ε) for f(x)=x², independent of context length n) with streaming and distributed extensions offers a principled, assumption-free framework for attention sparsification. However, the practical impact is currently limited: applying leverage score selection to softmax-pretrained ViTs causes dramatic accuracy drops (e.g., L/8: 79.5% → 0.8%), and while training from scratch recovers >90% of softmax accuracy, random key selection and row norm selection achieve comparable results, undermining the practical advantage of the theoretically-motivated approach. The paper explicitly leaves open how to effectively leverage score information during training to produce models that outperform simpler selection strategies, which is the key gap between the theory and its current empirical utility.
