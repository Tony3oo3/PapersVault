## Problem

Functional variational inference (fVI) and functional MCMC (fMCMC) for Bayesian neural networks have complementary but opposing limitations: fVI is computationally efficient but imposes strong parametric assumptions and underestimates posterior variance, while fMCMC is asymptotically exact but suffers from slow mixing in high dimensions. No prior work has successfully combined these two schemes in function space, as existing VI-MCMC hybrids are restricted to parameter space and do not extend naturally to infinite-dimensional function-space posteriors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes training input-target pairs, a pre-trained functional Gaussian process prior (e.g., RBF kernel), and test inputs, and produces an approximate posterior over functions whose predictive distribution yields means, standard deviations, and class probabilities. Unusually, the final posterior is not a fixed parametric family but a composition of K learned invertible bijections applied to a base factorized Gaussian, making its functional form arbitrarily complex and flexible. The V-Stage uses a Wasserstein-distance-based objective (rather than KL divergence) to avoid the ill-definedness issues of KL in infinite-dimensional spaces.

## Method

FVIMC alternates three stages: a V-Stage that maximizes a Wasserstein-distance-based functional ELBO to quickly converge to high-probability regions, an M-Stage that runs functional SGLD sampling to explore multi-modal structure, and a T-Stage that learns an invertible bijection (linear, Tanh, or Sigmoid with neural-network bias functions) to transform the implicit non-parametric M-Stage posterior into an explicit parametric form via the change-of-variable formula. The key theoretical guarantee (Proposition 3.2) shows that the functional Langevin SDE and the probability-flow ODE derived from Wasserstein gradient flows of fVI share identical probability-measure evolution marginals, ensuring the alternating process follows a consistent convergence path. After K alternating cycles, the final posterior takes the composited form F_{λ_K}^* ∘ … ∘ F_{λ_1}^* ∘ Q_{f_sK}, inheriting fVI's local optimization speed and fMCMC's non-parametric exploration while overcoming the distributional rigidity of pure variational methods.

## Results

On UCI regression (7 datasets), FVIMC achieves the best RMSE on 6/7 datasets, with headline values of 0.37 (Yacht), 0.36 (Boston), 0.23 (Energy), and 0.57 (Kin8nm), outperforming the closest functional MCMC baseline fSGLD (0.41, 0.36, 0.24, 0.74 respectively). On image classification, FVIMC achieves 4.95% test error on MNIST and 14.57% on FashionMNIST, the best among all baselines, with competitive OOD detection AUC of 0.832 (MNIST) and 0.800 (FashionMNIST). On the contextual bandit task (Mushroom), FVIMC consistently achieves the lowest cumulative regret across all three reward patterns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FVIMC outperforms all seven baselines on 6/7 UCI regression datasets in RMSE, with the sole exception being Protein where fSGLD (0.84) edges out FVIMC (0.95). Against the closest functional MCMC competitor fSGLD, FVIMC achieves substantially lower RMSE on Kin8nm (0.57 vs 0.74) and Protein-adjacent datasets, while the gap narrows on Boston (0.36 vs 0.36) and Energy (0.23 vs 0.24). Against the Wasserstein-based fVI baseline, FVIMC consistently improves RMSE (e.g., 0.37 vs 0.45 on Yacht, 0.57 vs 0.61 on Kin8nm) and NLL (e.g., -5.03 vs -3.07 on Yacht), confirming the benefit of the hybrid M-Stage exploration. The parameter-space baselines KLBBB and SGLD are clearly dominated, with FVIMC achieving 5–6× lower RMSE on most UCI datasets. On image classification, FVIMC achieves the best test error on both MNIST (4.95%) and FashionMNIST (14.57%), but its OOD detection AUC on FashionMNIST (0.800) is slightly below IFBNN (0.833) and fVI (0.831). On the contextual bandit task, FVIMC achieves the lowest cumulative regret across all three reward patterns, outperforming fVI, FBNN, GWI, KLBBB, and SGLD.

## Relation to Prior Work

FVIMC extends the parameter-space VI-MCMC hybridization literature (Ahn et al. 2012, Salimans et al. 2015, Domke 2017, Hoffman & Ma 2020) to function space, where prior approaches based on auxiliary variables or structured factorization do not directly apply. The M-Stage builds directly on the functional SGLD scheme of Wu et al. (2024a), while the V-Stage adopts the Wasserstein-distance-based functional variational objective from Wu et al. (2024b) to sidestep the ill-definedness of KL divergence in infinite-dimensional spaces. The T-Stage's invertible transformation mechanism draws on the change-of-variable formula from normalizing flows (Dinh et al. 2017) and the local linearization of Rudner et al. (2022), while the theoretical justification (Proposition 3.2) extends the Wasserstein gradient flow framework of Yi & Liu (2023) from parameter space to function space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: fVI underestimates posterior variance while fMCMC mixes slowly in high dimensions, and the hybrid approach is theoretically justified by showing both schemes share the same probability evolution marginals. However, the empirical evaluation is limited to relatively small-scale tasks (UCI regression, MNIST/FashionMNIST with small FC networks), leaving open whether the alternating scheme and its T-Stage transformation errors remain tractable for deep, high-capacity BNNs. The T-Stage introduces an approximation that, while shown to decrease over iterations, adds hyperparameters (choice of bijection form, number of alternating stages) and computational overhead that may limit adoption. A key open question is whether the Wasserstein-distance replacement for KL in the V-Stage preserves the theoretical guarantees (Proposition 3.2) in the general case beyond the special equivalence conditions discussed in Appendix G.5.
