## Problem

Standard DP-FL frameworks assume each client exhausts its privacy budget uniformly across all training rounds, yet the learning dynamics of deep networks show that early rounds learn coarse-grained features tolerant to noise while later rounds learn fine-grained features that benefit from higher signal-to-noise ratios. This paper identifies the gap that no prior DP-FL method expends privacy budgets non-uniformly over time in a data-independent, privacy-preserving manner, and addresses it with a time-adaptive spending strategy.

## Input / Output

The framework takes per-client privacy budgets ε_n (grouped into three tiers in experiments), local non-IID datasets partitioned via Dirichlet distribution, and pre-specified hyperparameters including saving-based sampling rates q_n, transition rounds T_n, and a global clip norm c. It produces a full schedule of time-varying privacy parameters—per-client noise multipliers σ_n^t, clip norms c_n^t, and sampling rates q_n^t for every round t—computed entirely before training begins, along with the trained global model. Because all parameter decisions depend only on privacy budgets and not on local data, the scheduling incurs zero additional privacy loss.

## Method

The core contribution is the 'spend-as-you-go' method: each client uses a reduced sampling rate q_n < q during early 'saving' rounds (t < T_n) and the full rate q during later 'spending' rounds (t ≥ T_n), leveraging privacy amplification by subsampling to spend less RDP budget per round early on. At each round, the noise multiplier σ_n^t is solved so that the remaining RDP budget is distributed uniformly over the remaining rounds, and per-client clip norms c_n^t are set as c·σ^t/σ_n^t to maintain a fixed average clip norm c. Theoretically, the paper proves that RDP spending is non-decreasing over time (Theorem 1) and derives an optimal permutation of saving-based sampling rates that minimizes clipping bias by assigning lower sampling rates to clients with smaller clip norms (Theorem 3). This means privacy-constrained clients save more budget early and contribute more strongly in later fine-grained learning rounds, while relaxed-budget clients contribute more evenly throughout.

## Results

On FMNIST, the proposed time-adaptive DP-FL framework with non-uniform privacy budgets (ε = 10, 20, 30) achieves 70.57% global test accuracy, compared to 64.8% for DP-FedAvg (uniform ε=10) and 65.45% for IDP-FedAvg. On MNIST, it reaches 83.83% versus 76.79% (DP-FedAvg) and 76.94% (IDP-FedAvg). On Adult Income, it achieves 77.53% versus 60.12% (DP-FedAvg) and 70.93% (IDP-FedAvg), approaching the non-private FedAvg upper bound of 78.93%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed time-adaptive DP-FL framework consistently outperforms all four baselines across all three main datasets. Compared to DP-FedAvg (uniform ε=10), it improves accuracy by 4.7% on FMNIST, 4.4% on MNIST, and 19.9% on Adult Income. Against IDP-FedAvg (non-uniform budgets), it gains 7.8% on FMNIST, 8.9% on MNIST, and 9.3% on Adult Income, demonstrating that non-uniform temporal spending of privacy budget is more effective than merely assigning different budgets across clients. The framework also outperforms the adaptive clipping baseline (Andrew et al., 2021) even when that baseline's hyperparameters are optimally tuned (e.g., 67.90% vs. 67.64% on FMNIST at ε=10, and 60.79% vs. 52.39% at ε=5). The method comes closest to the non-private FedAvg upper bound (72.95% on FMNIST, 90.23% on MNIST, 78.93% on Adult Income), with the non-uniform variant reaching within 2.4%, 6.4%, and 1.4% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DP-FedAvg framework (McMahan et al., 2017) and the individualized privacy (IDP) approach (Boenisch et al., 2024) by introducing a time-adaptive 'spend-as-you-go' privacy budget allocation that is scheduled before training and is independent of client data. It departs from prior adaptive DP methods (Pichapati et al., 2019; Andrew et al., 2021; Li et al., 2022) that either require access to public data or assume negligible privacy loss during parameter adaptation, by pre-computing all privacy parameters with zero additional privacy cost. The theoretical analysis builds on the clipping bias lemma of Das et al. (2023), extending it to individualized budgets with time-varying clip norms and sampling rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, data-independent mechanism to exploit the well-known coarse-to-fine learning dynamics in DP-FL, yielding 7–9% accuracy gains over IDP-FedAvg across multiple datasets while maintaining formal privacy guarantees. The theoretical result that clients with stricter budgets benefit disproportionately from uneven spending offers a clean design principle for heterogeneous privacy settings. Open questions include how to jointly tune the transition round T_n without incurring additional privacy loss (as noted by the authors), whether the approach composes well with adaptive clipping, and whether the gains persist on larger-scale or more heterogeneous datasets beyond the relatively small benchmarks used here.
