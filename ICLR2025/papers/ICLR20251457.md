## Problem

Existing 3D generative models face a fundamental trade-off: native 3D diffusion models are limited by scarce high-quality 3D training data, rendering-based models are computationally expensive and lack 2D generative priors, and two-stage reconstruction-based methods suffer from multi-view inconsistency that causes the pipeline to collapse. No prior unified framework can simultaneously leverage web-scale 2D diffusion priors and enforce 3D geometric coherence in a single model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251457/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts text prompts, a single RGB image, or control signals (normal maps, depth maps, Canny edges) combined with text, and produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation structured as V_in multi-view grids of 12-channel per-pixel attributes (RGB color, 3D location, 3D scale, rotation quaternion, opacity). Camera poses are encoded via Plücker embeddings concatenated to the feature dimension, and the output splat grids can be differentially rendered from arbitrary viewpoints. All Gaussian properties are normalized to [0,1] via sigmoid activations to fit the diffusion latent space.

## Method

DiffSplat repurposes pretrained text-to-image diffusion models (SD1.5, SDXL, SD3, PixArt) to directly generate 3D Gaussian splat properties by treating structured multi-view splat grids as 'images in a special style.' A lightweight 42M-parameter Transformer regressor instantaneously produces these grids from multi-view images in under 0.1 seconds, enabling scalable pseudo-dataset curation. The image VAE is fine-tuned (with 4× duplicated convolution weights to handle 12 channels) to encode/decode splat grids into 'splat latents,' supervised by both reconstruction and rendering losses. The core training objective combines standard diffusion loss on splat latents with a novel 3D rendering loss that decodes denoised latents back into Gaussian properties and renders them from random viewpoints against ground-truth images, enforcing 3D consistency that pure 2D diffusion loss cannot guarantee. This design requires minimal architectural modifications to the base diffusion model, enabling seamless adaptation of image-generation techniques like ControlNet to the 3D domain.

## Results

On T3Bench text-conditioned generation, DIFFSPLAT achieves the best CLIP Similarity (30.95% for single objects, 29.46% for multiple objects), CLIP R-Precision (81.00% / 69.50%), and ImageReward (-0.491 / -0.849) across all three prompt categories, outperforming all native 3D and reconstruction-based baselines. On GSO image-conditioned generation, DIFFSPLAT attains PSNR 22.91, SSIM 0.892, and LPIPS 0.107, surpassing the strongest baseline GRM (PSNR 19.65, SSIM 0.869, LPIPS 0.141) by a substantial margin of +3.26 dB in PSNR. The method also demonstrates that scaling the base diffusion model (SD1.5 → SDXL → SD3) consistently improves both text- and image-conditioned results, with SD3-medium reaching CLIP R-Prec 68.08% and PSNR 22.91.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFFSPLAT outperforms all 10 baselines across both text- and image-conditioned tasks. In text-conditioned generation (Table 1), the closest competitor is LGM† (reconstruction-based), which trails DIFFSPLAT by 0.99% CLIP Sim and 3.00% CLIP R-Prec on single objects; native 3D methods (GVGEN, LN3Diff, Direct-3D, 3DTopia) lag far behind, with CLIP R-Prec scores below 35% versus DIFFSPLAT's 81%. In image-conditioned generation (Table 2), DIFFSPLAT leads the best baseline GRM† by +3.26 dB PSNR and -0.034 LPIPS, while InstantMesh† achieves the best SSIM among baselines (0.876) but still falls short of DIFFSPLAT (0.892). The gap is particularly pronounced for complex prompts involving multiple objects or surroundings, where reconstruction-based methods suffer from multi-view inconsistency and native 3D methods lack sufficient text-3D training pairs. Notably, DIFFSPLAT's advantage grows with prompt complexity: on multiple objects, it achieves 69.50% CLIP R-Prec versus 51.00% for LGM† and only 8.00% for GVGEN.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffSplat carves a new middle ground between three established paradigms for 3D generation: native 3D diffusion (limited by scarce 3D data), rendering-based diffusion (computationally heavy, no 2D priors), and two-stage reconstruction-based methods (suffer from multi-view inconsistency). Its core departure is repurposing pretrained image diffusion models to directly denoise structured Gaussian splat grids, treating them as 'images in a special style,' thereby inheriting web-scale 2D priors while maintaining 3D coherence through an auxiliary rendering loss. The method builds on 3DGS as the 3D representation, fine-tunes the VAE of latent diffusion models to encode 12-channel splat properties into the image latent space, and uses Plücker embeddings for camera pose conditioning — all with minimal architectural changes to the base denoising network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing that any off-the-shelf text-to-image diffusion model (SD1.5, SDXL, PixArt, SD3) can be fine-tuned into a 3D generator with only VAE adaptation and a rendering loss, DiffSplat creates a direct pipeline for porting the entire image-generation toolbox (ControlNet, personalization, few-step distillation, preference alignment) into 3D content creation. The 42M-parameter reconstruction model that produces training data in under 0.1 seconds also addresses the scalability bottleneck of prior 3D dataset curation. Key open questions remain: converting 3DGS output to high-quality meshes, scaling to real-world video data beyond rendered multi-view sets, and integrating physically-based material properties. The paper's limitation to rendered G-Objaverse data means the full scalability potential of the 2D-supervision-only paradigm is unrealized.
