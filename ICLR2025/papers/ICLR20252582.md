## Problem

Spectral gap maximization has been widely adopted to mitigate over-squashing in GNNs, but it systematically attenuates community structure, which is detrimental when communities align with node labels (high graph-task alignment). Existing rewiring methods operate purely on topological criteria and cannot directly improve the alignment between the graph's latent community structure and the downstream task, nor do they incorporate node feature similarity as a rewiring signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252582/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The methods take an undirected graph with node features (e.g., one-hot or continuous vectors) and produce a modified edge set after a user-specified number N of edge additions and/or deletions. Community structure is precomputed once via the Louvain algorithm, and pairwise cosine similarity between node features is computed globally. The output is a rewired graph that is then fed to a standard GNN (2-layer GCN in experiments) for supervised node classification; no architectural changes to the GNN itself are required.

## Method

The paper proposes three rewiring families grounded in a theoretical analysis on Stochastic Block Models showing that spectral gap optimization only modulates community strength and that GNN performance is governed by a graph-task alignment parameter ψ. ComMa (Higher/Lower) is a non-greedy, community-structure-based method that randomly adds intra-cluster and deletes inter-cluster edges (or vice versa), achieving the same spectral effect as gap minimization/maximization but at orders-of-magnitude lower cost since it only requires one Louvain run. FeaSt maximizes global feature similarity by ranking all candidate edges by the change in mean pairwise cosine similarity they would induce, then adding the top-N most similar non-edges or deleting the top-N least similar existing edges. ComFy combines both signals: it budgets the N edge modifications proportionally across all community pairs (i,j) based on |C_i|·|C_j|, then within each pair selects edges by feature-similarity ranking, thereby distributing the rewiring effect across the graph while preserving the original community structure.

## Results

On homophilic datasets, FeaSt-Del achieves the best accuracy: 90.74% on Cora, 81.60% on Citeseer, and 86.76% on Pubmed, outperforming the GCN baseline (86.12%, 77.83%, 85.57%) by 4.6, 3.8, and 1.2 points respectively. On heterophilic datasets, ComFy-Del dominates: 70.27% on Cornell, 64.86% on Texas, 66.00% on Wisconsin, and 45.51% on Chameleon, exceeding the GCN baseline (35.14%, 35.14%, 38.00%, 39.33%) by 35.1, 29.7, 28.0, and 6.2 points respectively. ComFy-Add also leads on Actor (32.30%) and Squirrel (36.85%). ComMa variants are orders of magnitude faster than spectral methods (0.05s vs. 1–5s for 50 edges) but achieve comparable accuracy to spectral baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the GCN baseline (Kipf & Welling, 2017), both FeaSt-Del and ComFy-Del consistently improve accuracy across all 9 datasets, with the largest gains on heterophilic graphs (e.g., +35.1 pts on Cornell, +29.7 pts on Texas). Compared to FoSR (Karhadkar et al., 2023), the proposed methods dominate on heterophilic datasets (ComFy-Del: 70.27 vs. 40.54 on Cornell; 64.86 vs. 51.35 on Texas) but underperform on Cora (88.13 vs. 83.50 for ComFy-Del, though FeaSt-Del at 90.74 still wins). Against the spectral proxy methods from Jamadandi et al. (2024), ComFy-Del outperforms ProxyDelMax on all heterophilic datasets (e.g., 70.27 vs. 54.05 on Cornell, 66.00 vs. 52.00 on Wisconsin, 45.51 vs. 39.33 on Chameleon), while FeaSt-Del matches or exceeds ProxyDelMax on homophilic ones (90.74 vs. 86.32 on Cora, 81.60 vs. 81.84 on Citeseer). BORF (Nguyen et al., 2023) is only available for a subset of datasets but is outperformed by both FeaSt-Del and ComFy-Del wherever reported (e.g., 90.74 vs. 87.50 on Cora, 45.51 vs. 61.50 on Chameleon for ComFy-Del vs. BORF). The key finding is that no single spectral method wins across both homophilic and heterophilic regimes, whereas the feature-similarity-based methods (FeaSt, ComFy) provide a more consistent improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral rewiring line of work (FoSR, ProxyAddMax/DelMax) by showing that spectral gap minimization—strengthening community structure—can also improve GNN performance, contrary to the prevailing focus on gap maximization to fight over-squashing. It departs from purely topological rewiring criteria by introducing node feature similarity as an explicit rewiring signal, drawing on the homophily literature (Ma et al., 2022) and the graph-task alignment concept from Hussain et al. (2021) and Yang et al. (2024). The three proposed methods (ComMa, FeaSt, ComFy) are positioned as complementary to spectral approaches: ComMa is a faster randomized proxy for spectral gap optimization, FeaSt targets global feature similarity, and ComFy combines both by budgeting feature-similarity-driven edits across detected communities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies graph-task alignment (overlap between community labels and node labels) as the critical factor determining whether spectral rewiring helps or hurts, providing a clear theoretical framework (via SBM analysis) that practitioners can use to decide when to maximize vs. minimize the spectral gap. The finding that feature-similarity-based rewiring outperforms purely topological methods—especially ComFy on heterophilic graphs—suggests that future rewiring strategies should incorporate node features rather than relying solely on graph topology. Limitations include evaluation only on GCN as backbone (no GAT, GraphSAGE, or deeper architectures), reliance on Louvain community detection whose quality may vary, and the fact that real-world graphs have far more complex community structures than the binary SBM analyzed theoretically.
