## Problem

Linear attention methods that replace softmax with non-negative kernelized feature maps (e.g., ReLU, ELU+1) suffer from two critical information losses: they discard all negative query-key interactions (negative-negative and positive-negative pairs), and they produce overly uniform, high-entropy attention maps that lack the spiky discriminative structure of softmax. This results in less specific attention that activates irrelevant tokens equally, degrading performance on tasks requiring fine-grained spatial reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method operates on patchified RGB images (224×224 or 384×384 for ImageNet, COCO, ADE20K) and text/image sequences (up to 4k tokens for LRA benchmarks), producing class labels, bounding boxes with instance masks, or pixel-wise segmentation maps depending on the downstream task. A distinctive architectural detail is that each value vector is split along the channel dimension into two halves of d/2 channels each, dedicated to same-signed and opposite-signed query-key streams, with learnable polarity-aware coefficient matrices (G^s, G^o) applied via element-wise multiplication to the respective outputs before concatenation.

## Method

PolaFormer decomposes each query and key vector into positive and negative components, then computes attention in two parallel streams: one for same-signed interactions (q⁺·k⁺ + q⁻·k⁻) and one for opposite-signed interactions (q⁺·k⁻ + q⁻·k⁺), avoiding the information loss of ReLU-based feature maps that zero out negative values. Instead of directly subtracting opposite-signed similarities (which violates non-negativity), the method uses learnable coefficient matrices G^s and G^o to mix the two streams, with the value vector split into two channel halves to handle each stream without extra parameters. To restore softmax-like spikiness, the paper proves (Theorem 1) that any element-wise function with positive first and second derivatives reduces the Positive Sequence Entropy of the attention distribution, and instantiates this with a channel-wise learnable power function g(x) = x^p where p = 1 + α·sigmoid(w) per dimension. A depth-wise convolution is added to counteract the low-rank degeneracy of the softmax kernel in higher layers, and the overall complexity remains O(Nd²) linear in sequence length.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, DeiT-PolaFormer achieves 74.6% top-1 accuracy (+2.4% over the DeiT baseline) and Swin-PolaFormer reaches 82.6% (+1.4% over Swin), both with linear attention complexity. On COCO object detection, PVT-T-PolaFormer with Mask R-CNN achieves 40.4 AP^b (+3.7 over baseline) and 43.9 AP^b_75 (+4.6), while Swin-T-PolaFormer reaches 44.8 AP^b (+1.1). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, mIoU improves by 1.2–2.6% over baselines. On the Long Range Arena benchmark, PolaFormer (α=3) attains 60.72% average accuracy, surpassing the best prior linear attention method (Hedgehog, 59.66%) by 1.06%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolaFormer consistently outperforms all compared linear attention variants on the same backbone. On DeiT, it surpasses FLatten (74.1%), MobiAttn (73.3%), EnhancedAttn (72.9%), AngularAttn (70.8%), EfficientAttn (70.2%), and HydraAttn (68.3%) by 0.5–6.3%. On Swin, it beats FLatten (82.1%), EnhancedAttn (81.8%), EfficientAttn (81.0%), HydraAttn (80.7%), and LinearAngularAttn (79.4%) by 0.5–3.2%. On PVT backbones, PolaFormer improves over FLatten by 1.0% (PVT-T: 78.8 vs 77.8) and 0.2% (PVT-S: 81.9 vs 81.7) on ImageNet-1K, and by 2.2 AP^b on COCO detection (40.4 vs 38.2 for PVT-T). On the LRA benchmark, PolaFormer (α=3) achieves 60.72% average, exceeding Hedgehog (59.66%), Skyformer (59.39%), Nystrom (57.90%), and Cosformer (57.37%), while also outperforming the full softmax Transformer (58.14%) at linear complexity. On ADE20K segmentation, PolaFormer gains 1.1 mIoU over FLatten (38.3 vs 37.2 for PVT-T) and 1.0 mIoU for Swin-T (45.8 vs 44.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the kernelized linear attention framework of Katharopoulos et al. (2020) and the power-rescaling idea of Flatten Transformer (Han et al., 2023a) by identifying a specific source of information loss: the systematic discarding of negative components in query-key dot products when using non-negative feature maps like ReLU or ELU+1. The key departure is a polarity-aware decomposition that explicitly models same-signed and opposite-signed interactions in two parallel streams, combined with a learnable per-dimension power function that generalizes Flatten's fixed-norm rescaling. A theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) formalizes the conditions under which element-wise functions reduce attention entropy, providing a principled justification for the power-function choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The polarity-aware formulation offers a modular drop-in replacement for the attention module in existing ViT backbones (DeiT, Swin, PVT, PVTv2), yielding consistent 1–4.6% gains across classification, detection, and segmentation without increasing asymptotic complexity. The theoretical characterization of entropy-reducing functions gives practitioners a design principle beyond ad-hoc kernel selection. Open questions include whether the two-stream architecture and learnable mixing matrices scale efficiently to very long sequences, whether the low-rank workaround via convolution is truly necessary or an artifact of the current formulation, and whether the polarity decomposition generalizes to non-vision modalities where negative interactions may carry different semantic meaning.
