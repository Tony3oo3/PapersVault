## Problem

Reinforcement learning policies are typically encoded in either opaque neural networks (flexible but prone to shortcut learning and poor generalization) or symbolic rule-based systems (interpretable but unable to handle fine-grained low-level control). Existing neuro-symbolic approaches either use a rigid top-down sequential architecture (planner selects a reactive system) or rely on a single policy type, failing to let agents dynamically switch between abstract reasoning and instinctive reaction based on the current situation.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes two parallel state representations: a stack of F raw pixel frames (F×W×H×C) fed to a CNN, and an object-centric state matrix (n×m) extracted by a separate object-discovery model, encoding objects with attributes such as position, orientation, and value. The primary output is a state-dependent mixture of two action distributions, where the blending weight β is itself computed by a differentiable logic reasoner or neural network over the current state. Additionally, the system produces a hybrid value estimate (blended CNN and MLP-on-objects), interpretable weighted first-order-logic rules, and gradient-based importance maps for both the pixel and object-centric components.

## Method

BlendRL runs a CNN-based neural policy and a graph-based differentiable forward reasoner (message-passing over a bipartite atom/conjunction graph) in parallel, each producing an action distribution over the environment's action space. A blending module—implemented as a set of weighted first-order-logic rules (or optionally a neural network)—computes a state-dependent weight β ∈ [0,1] that determines the convex combination of the two distributions, enabling context-sensitive switching (e.g., neural for dodging enemies, logic for pathfinding). The logic rules, state predicates, and their differentiable valuation functions are generated by GPT-4o from natural-language task descriptions, removing the need for expert-provided inductive biases. Training uses PPO actor-critic with a hybrid value function that blends a CNN critic (sharing convolutional layers with the actor) and a small MLP on object-centric states, plus an entropy regularization term on β to prevent collapse to a single policy. The graph-based reasoner scales linearly in the number of groundings (vs. quadratic for tensor-based ∂ILP), enabling training with 500+ parallel environments on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

BlendRL achieves human-normalized scores of 474.4 on Kangaroo, 94.7 on Seaquest, and 48.4 on DonkeyKong, outperforming all neural and neuro-symbolic baselines across every environment. The largest margins appear on DonkeyKong (48.4 vs. 5.8 for SCoBots, the strongest baseline) and Seaquest (94.7 vs. 58.2 for INSIGHT), where the combination of symbolic path-finding and neural reactive control is most critical. On Kangaroo, BlendRL more than triples the score of the next-best baseline (SCoBots at 149.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BlendRL outperforms all seven baselines on every environment where they are evaluated. Against purely neural agents, BlendRL scores 474.4 vs. 98.4 (DQN) and 474.4 vs. 26.1 (PPO) on Kangaroo, and 48.4 vs. 3.5 (DQN) on DonkeyKong, where neural agents struggle with the multi-level navigation. Against the strongest neuro-symbolic baseline SCoBots, BlendRL leads by 324.7 points on Kangaroo (474.4 vs. 149.7), 42.5 on Seaquest (94.7 vs. 52.2), and 42.6 on DonkeyKong (48.4 vs. 5.8). The logic-only baselines NUDGE and NLRL perform poorly on Seaquest (−3.6 and −3.3 respectively), confirming that symbolic policies alone cannot handle the reactive shooting demands. INSIGHT, the only other neuro-symbolic method evaluated on Seaquest, scores 58.2, still well below BlendRL's 94.7. The INTERPRETER baseline (64.4 on Kangaroo, 25.1 on DonkeyKong) also trails BlendRL substantially, reinforcing that parallel blending of both policy types yields superior performance over sequential or single-paradigm approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

BlendRL extends the neuro-symbolic RL line initiated by NUDGE (Delfosse et al., 2023a) and NLRL (Jiang & Luo, 2019), which encode policies entirely in differentiable first-order logic. The key architectural departure is the *parallel* (rather than sequential/top-down) integration of a neural CNN policy and a differentiable logic policy, merged by a learned blending module — a design not present in prior neuro-symbolic RL or in planner-then-reactor frameworks like RePReL. The logic component leverages the graph-based message-passing reasoner of Shindo et al. (2024b) for linear-memory scalability, and LLMs (GPT-4o) are used to auto-generate action rules and valuation functions, removing the expert-in-the-loop requirement of NUDGE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that parallel neuro-symbolic blending yields agents that are simultaneously interpretable (transparent weighted rules, gradient-based attributions) and robust to environmental changes (neural PPO collapses to 0 return on modified environments while BlendRL retains substantial performance). This addresses a long-standing tension in RL between black-box reactivity and symbolic transparency. Open questions include: (i) the method's dependence on a fixed object-detection pipeline (noise ablation shows significant degradation), (ii) whether LLM-generated rules generalize beyond the three tested Atari games, and (iii) whether the blending module can be learned without any human-specified inductive bias. The approach is also limited to relatively simple 2D environments and does not yet scale to the full Atari 2600 suite.
