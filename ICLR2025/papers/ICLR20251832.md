## Problem

Diffusion-based text-to-music models produce high-quality audio but require 5–20 seconds of inference for 32-second outputs due to iterative denoising. Existing distillation methods for audio have only been applied to shorter or lower-quality segments, require ~10 steps to match base quality (vs. 1–4 for images), and have not successfully leveraged GAN-based or layer-distillation techniques. No prior work combines step and layer distillation for TTM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251832/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and a BPM value as conditioning signals, which are embedded via T5 text tokens and sinusoidal BPM embeddings respectively, and fed into a DiT-XL backbone through AdaLN. It generates 32-second mono 44.1 kHz audio in latent space (32-dim KL bottleneck at ~45 Hz effective rate via a fully convolutional VAE), which is then upsampled to stereo using MusicHiFi. The distilled generator produces output in just 4 sampling steps with 230 ms mono / 435 ms stereo latency on an A100, compared to ~4.1 s for the 80-step base model.

## Method

Presto! introduces a dual-faceted distillation approach. Presto-S redefines DMD2 for continuous-time EDM-style score models by (1) using continuous-valued noise levels as generator inputs rather than discrete timesteps, (2) disentangling the shared noise schedule into four independent distributions (p_gen, p_DMD, p_DSM, p_GAN) so each loss term can focus on perceptually relevant SNR regions, and (3) replacing the non-saturating GAN with a least-squares discriminator operating on partially downsampled intermediate features of the fake score model. Presto-L improves layer dropping by shifting the drop schedule to preserve the final DiT block's variance spike, adding explicit budget conditioning via zero-initialized AdaLN modules, and training with a self-teacher loss that matches layer-dropped hidden states to full-model outputs. Presto-LS combines both by first performing layer distillation (with a reduced dropping budget) and then step distillation, while keeping the real and fake score models initialized from the original (non-layer-distilled) checkpoint to stabilize the distribution-matching gradient and prevent discriminator collapse.

## Results

On Song Describer (no vocals), Presto-S (4 NFE) achieves FAD 0.22, MMD 0.83, and CLAP 33.13, matching or exceeding the 80-step base model (FAD 0.24, MMD 0.82) while running at 118.77× real-time (mono). The combined Presto-LS method (4 NFE) further improves MMD to 0.73 (beating the base model) and achieves 230/435 ms latency for 32-second mono/stereo 44.1 kHz audio on an A100 40 GB — a 10–18× speedup over the base model and 15× faster than Stable Audio Open. In a subjective listening test (n=16), Presto-LS is rated highest in musical quality against all baselines (p<0.05, Bonferroni-corrected).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Presto-S decisively outperforms all consistency-based distillation baselines at 4 NFE: it reduces FAD from 0.47 (CM) and 0.35 (SoundCTM) to 0.22, and MMD from 2.50/1.72 to 0.83, while also improving CLAP score (33.13 vs. 26.33/29.61). Against the adversarial DMD-GAN baseline, Presto-S improves FAD (0.22 vs. 0.29), MMD (0.83 vs. 1.16), and CLAP (33.13 vs. 27.56). For layer distillation, Presto-L (80 NFE) beats ASE on every metric — FAD 0.18 vs. 0.25, MMD 0.61 vs. 1.12, CLAP 32.28 vs. 30.03 — while also surpassing the unmodified base model. Against external models, Presto-LS (4 NFE) matches Stable Audio Open on FAD (0.23) but substantially outperforms it on MMD (0.73 vs. 1.07) and RTF (73.43× vs. 4.54×); it also beats all MusicGen variants on FAD and MMD, though MusicGen-Large edges it on CLAP (32.83 vs. 32.21). In the subjective listening test, Presto-LS is preferred over the base model, SoundCTM, and Stable Audio Open with statistical significance (p<0.05).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts DMD2 (Yin et al., 2024), an adversarial diffusion distillation method originally designed for discrete-time image models, into the continuous-time score-based (EDM) framework, making it the first GAN-based distillation method for text-to-music generation. For layer distillation, it builds on ASE (Moon et al., 2024) but introduces three targeted improvements: shifting the dropping schedule to preserve a final-layer variance spike, adding explicit budget conditioning via AdaLN, and a self-teacher loss. The base architecture follows DiT-XL (Peebles & Xie, 2023) with EDM-style parameterization, and the paper's key conceptual departure is treating step and layer distillation as a dual-faceted, sequentially-applied pipeline rather than independent accelerations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 230/435ms latency for 32-second mono/stereo audio (10-18× speedup) brings TTM close to interactive-rate generation, a practical milestone that could enable real-time co-creation tools. The finding that layer dropping can improve quality over the full model (FAD 0.18 vs. 0.24) challenges the assumption that removing capacity always degrades performance, suggesting a multi-task specialization effect. Open questions include whether the aggressive combined distillation can be pushed further (the dropping schedule had to be reduced for stability), whether CPU inference (still 43s for 32s audio) can be made practical, and whether the VAE/mono-to-stereo bottleneck can be addressed to unlock the full speedup potential.
