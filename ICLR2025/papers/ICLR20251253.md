## Problem

Self-rewarding preference data synthesis, where the target model both generates and annotates its own responses, suffers from reward bias because the reward model shares weights with the target model, amplifying inherent errors. Manual annotation avoids this bias but is prohibitively expensive and limited in scale. The paper asks whether an automatic framework can synthesize high-quality preference data that mitigates self-rewarding bias while remaining applicable across diverse modalities and domains.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts heterogeneous inputs—text prompts, natural images with text queries, medical radiology images with clinical questions, and robot task images with manipulation instructions—and produces (preferred, dispreferred) response pairs for DPO fine-tuning. For each input, the target model samples C=5 candidate responses, which the judge model ranks using tool-extracted knowledge; the top and bottom responses form the pair. Notably, in the medical image analysis application, the target model generates only dispreferred responses while ground-truth annotations serve as the preferred response, since current medical LVLMs cannot reliably produce high-quality preferred outputs. The synthesized pairs are compiled into Anyprefer-V1 (58K pairs spanning text, image-text, and image-control-sequence data types).

## Method

Anyprefer frames preference data synthesis as a two-player cooperative Markov Game between a target model π_t and a judge model π_j, both parameterized by optimizable prompts p_t and p_j. The judge model is augmented with domain-specific external tools (e.g., Grounded SAM for segmentation, Florence-2 for detection, DuckDuckGo for web search, medical VQA models) that extract supplementary knowledge q_i from the input, which the judge aggregates to rank candidate responses—reducing the self-rewarding bias that arises when a single model must both generate and evaluate. A separate LLM-based reward model R(y+, y−) scores each preference pair as a cheap surrogate for actual fine-tuning performance; pairs below a threshold τ trigger a prompt-based policy gradient ascent step (analogous to TextGrad) that updates both p_t and p_j simultaneously. The optimized model is then fine-tuned via DPO for 3 epochs with LoRA, and the improved model replaces the target model for the next synthesis iteration, enabling iterative self-improvement. Ablations confirm that joint two-player optimization outperforms optimizing either model independently, and that tools plus feedback yield ~22% absolute improvement in judge accuracy over the unassisted baseline.

## Results

Across four applications and 21 datasets, Anyprefer achieves average improvements of 18.55% (NL generation), 3.66% (vision-language understanding), 30.05% (medical image analysis), and 16.00% (visuo-motor control) over the base target models. In natural language generation, Anyprefer reaches 38.14% on GSM8K (vs. 22.44% for base LLaMA2-7B), 80.26% on ARC-Easy, 64.68% on ARC-Challenge, and 19.25/15.14 length-controlled/win rate on AlpacaEval 2.0. In medical image analysis, the largest relative gains appear on IU-Xray report generation, where BLEU-1 jumps from 10.31 to 16.85 and METEOR from 10.95 to 29.66. In visuo-motor control, task success rates increase to 0.38, 0.50, 0.36, and 0.50 across four WidowX robot tasks (vs. 0.28, 0.30, 0.20, 0.32 for OpenVLA-SFT baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Anyprefer outperforms all 12 baselines across the four applications. In natural language generation (Table 4), Anyprefer beats base LLaMA2-7B by 15.70 points on GSM8K (38.14 vs. 22.44), 5.93 on ARC-Easy (80.26 vs. 74.33), and 7.00 on ARC-Challenge (64.68 vs. 57.68), while AlpacaEval win rate jumps from 4.57 to 15.14; it also surpasses self-rewarding (23.20 GSM8K) and meta-rewarding (25.47 GSM8K) by 14.94 and 12.67 points respectively. In vision-language understanding (Table 7), Anyprefer leads on 7 of 9 benchmarks, with the largest margin on LLaVA-W (69.2 vs. 68.7 for POVID) and MMVet (33.0 vs. 31.8 for POVID), while slightly trailing the base model on MME-P (1510.1 vs. 1510.7) and matching it on GQA. In medical image analysis (Table 10), Anyprefer dominates all metrics: VQA-RAD closed accuracy 72.06 vs. 67.42 (meta-rewarding), SLAKE open 49.04 vs. 45.12, and IU-Xray METEOR 29.66 vs. 13.21 (meta-rewarding), a 125% relative gain. In visuo-motor control (Table 13), Anyprefer achieves the highest success rates on all four tasks (0.38, 0.50, 0.36, 0.50), outperforming OpenVLA-SFT (0.28, 0.30, 0.20, 0.32), Octo-small (0.28, 0.16, 0.10, 0.32), Octo-base (0.20, 0.10, 0.04, 0.32), and RT-1 (0.06, 0.12, 0.02, 0.00) by 10–48 percentage points depending on the task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-rewarding and RLAIF lines of work for preference data construction by reframing the synthesis process as a two-player cooperative Markov game between a target model and a judge model, rather than a single model rewarding itself or a static external annotator labeling pairs. The prompt-based policy optimization draws directly from Pryzant et al. (2023) and TextGrad (Yuksekgonul et al., 2024), treating prompts as differentiable parameters updated via surrogate reward gradients, while the LLM-as-a-Judge reward model follows Zheng et al. (2023). The key departure from prior self-rewarding methods (Yuan et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024a) is the introduction of domain-specific external tools (e.g., Grounded SAM, Florence-2, medical VQA models) into the judge's information pipeline, explicitly addressing the rewarding bias that arises when the target model evaluates its own outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's generality across four application domains (NL generation, vision-language understanding, medical imaging, and visuo-motor control) and the release of Anyprefer-V1 (58K pairs) provide a practical, reusable resource for the alignment community, particularly in data-scarce domains like medical imaging and robotics. However, the heavy reliance on GPT-4o as both judge and reward model limits reproducibility and scalability, and the medical application's use of ground-truth labels as preferred responses (rather than fully synthetic pairs) weakens the 'automatic synthesis' claim in that setting. Open questions include whether the cooperative game formulation scales beyond prompt-level optimization to actual weight updates, and whether the tool-augmented judgment generalizes to domains where no strong external oracle models exist.
