## Problem

While replicability notions have been formalized for supervised learning, it remains unclear what the strongest achievable notion of replicability is for bandit algorithms without incurring significant regret loss. Perfect replicability (identical traces across all runs) is provably incompatible with non-trivial regret in MAB, so the paper asks: what is the minimum list complexity ℓ such that an algorithm's trace falls in a set of at most ℓ possible sequences with high probability, while still achieving sublinear regret? The paper answers this by establishing matching upper and lower bounds on list complexity in the sublinear regret regime.

## Input / Output

The algorithms take k arms with unknown reward distributions supported on [0,1] (or a compact d-dimensional action set for linear bandits), a time horizon T, and a failure probability δ. The output is a sequence of arms (trace) of length T, but the key guarantee is structural: across independent executions with different random bits, the trace belongs to a finite list S of size at most ℓ with probability ≥ 1−δ. Notably, the list size ℓ is independent of T (or at most k^{O(log log T)}), which is surprising given that k^T distinct traces are possible in general. For linear bandits, the action set can be infinite, yet the list complexity depends only on dimension d and batch count B.

## Method

The paper develops three phase-elimination batched algorithms with progressively tighter list complexity. Algorithm 1 achieves (2^k, δ) list replicability with Õ(√(kT)) regret by exploiting the geometric growth of batch lengths: each suboptimal arm is eliminated in one of at most two consecutive batches, yielding at most 2^k deletion sequences. Algorithm 2 improves to (O(k/δ), δ) list replicability by introducing a random shift r ∈ [C] into the deletion criteria, which ensures that for 'good' shifts (at least C − 6k of them), every arm is eliminated in exactly one batch, collapsing the trace space to C possibilities. Algorithm 3 uses the ReplicableMeans subroutine from Dixon et al. (2023) to perform replicable arm elimination in each of B constant batches, achieving ((k+1)^{B−1}, δ) list replicability with Õ(k^{3/2} T^{1/2+2^{−(B+1)}}) regret. The matching lower bound (no (k−1)-list replicable algorithm with o(T) regret) is proved via a cubical Sperner/KKM lemma applied to a (k−1)-dimensional cuboid of arm-mean configurations. For linear bandits, the method selects a √2-G-optimal design of at most 2d arms per batch, applies ReplicableMeans to their empirical means, and solves a linear regression to estimate θ*, achieving ((2d+1)^{B−1}, δ) list replicability with Õ(d² T^{1/2+2^{−(B+1)}}) regret.

## Results

The paper establishes three upper bounds for k-armed MAB: (1) a (2^k, δ)-list replicable algorithm with near-optimal regret Õ(√(kT)), independent of T in list complexity; (2) an (O(k/δ), δ)-list replicable algorithm with regret Õ((k/δ)√(kT)); and (3) a ((k+1)^(B-1), δ)-list replicable algorithm with regret Õ(k^(3/2) T^(1/2+2^(-(B+1)))) for any integer B>1. A matching lower bound proves no (k−1, δ)-list replicable algorithm can achieve o(T) regret for δ ≤ 1/(k+1), making the k-list complexity optimal in the sublinear regret regime. For d-dimensional linear bandits, the paper extends the third algorithm to achieve ((2d+1)^(B−1), δ)-list replicability with regret Õ(d² T^(1/2+2^(-(B+1)))) even with infinitely many arms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are made against prior theoretical results and impossibility results. The lower bound (Theorem 6.1) shows that k−1 list complexity is infeasible for sublinear regret, which almost exactly matches the k-list upper bound from Algorithm 3 with B=2 (giving (k+1, δ)-list replicability with Õ(k^(3/2) T^(2/3)) regret), establishing optimality in the sublinear regret regime. The paper distinguishes itself from Esfandiari et al. (2023), who study ρ-replicability (same random seed across runs), noting that their algorithm can have exponentially bad list replicability when reinterpreted under the list-replicability definition. Compared to standard UCB or Thompson Sampling algorithms, which the paper notes have list complexity exponentially dependent on T (due to probabilistic switching times), all proposed algorithms achieve list complexity independent of T. The ReplicableMeans subroutine from Dixon et al. (2023) is the key building block enabling the (k+1)-list replicable elimination in each batch, reducing the naive 2^k possibilities per batch to just k+1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the list replicability framework of Dixon et al. (2023) from supervised learning to the multi-armed bandit setting, where the 'output' is a full sequence of actions rather than a single prediction. It departs from the closest prior work, Esfandiari et al. (2023) on ρ-replicable bandits, by adopting a fundamentally different replicability notion: instead of requiring identical traces under a shared random seed, it bounds the number of distinct traces across independent runs. The algorithms are built on standard phase-elimination/batched structures from the bandit literature, but the replicability analysis is novel, leveraging the ReplicableMeans subroutine from Dixon et al. and, for the lower bound, a cubical Sperner/KKM lemma from topological combinatorics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that near-optimal regret Õ(√(kT)) is achievable with only 2^k possible traces (independent of T), and proves a matching lower bound showing k−1 traces are insufficient for any sublinear regret — a clean characterization of the list-complexity frontier. The practical motivation is safety-critical deployment (clinical trials, social experiments) where one must pre-enumerate all possible action sequences. Key open questions include closing the gap between the k upper bound and the k−1 lower bound in the sublinear regime, and understanding what modifications to standard UCB/Thompson sampling would restore list replicability (their list complexity is exponentially dependent on T).
