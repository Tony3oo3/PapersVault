## Problem

The original Weak-to-Strong framework relies on a single weak model to supervise a strong model, but weak labels contain errors that cap the strong model's recovered performance, and a single model cannot capture the inherent variability in human opinions. Additionally, the framework was limited to text classification, leaving the more challenging text generation setting—where the strong model must produce trustworthy content rather than merely judge it—unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251064/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For classification, the strong model receives a text question paired with a candidate answer and outputs a binary correctness label; for slot filling, it receives a spoken-language utterance and generates a slot-filling sequence with predefined keys and values. A key technical detail is that weak and strong models use different tokenizers, so the method bridges this gap by decomposing word-level probabilities from weak models into token-level soft labels for the strong model, using the strong model's own per-token confidence as a weighting signal to split word scores across subword pieces.

## Method

The core contribution is Bayesian WeakS-to-Strong, which replaces naive averaging of multiple weak labels with an evidential deep learning (EDL) framework: the strong model predicts Dirichlet hyperparameters α over the categorical distribution of weak labels, trained with a negative log-likelihood loss plus a KL-divergence regularization that penalizes overconfident misclassifications. This learns a prior distribution over the ensemble's outputs rather than treating each weak label independently, increasing fault tolerance to individual weak model errors. For text generation, a token-level probability estimation scheme uses words as an intermediary between mismatched tokenizers, allocating word-level scores to subword tokens proportionally to the strong model's confidence at each position. After supervised finetuning with these soft labels, conservative DPO (cDPO) is applied in a student-forcing manner, where weak models score the strong model's own generated sequences to identify preferred and dispreferred pairs, with a label-flipping probability ε to guard against weak model errors.

## Results

On the SciQ classification task, Bayesian Multi-Weak with 5 weak models and auxiliary loss achieves a PGR of 0.828 (accuracy 0.866), recovering 82.8% of the gap between the best weak model and the strong ceiling (0.898). On the SLURP slot-filling generation task, the same approach yields a PGR of 0.668 (SLU-F1 0.721), substantially outperforming the best single weak model (PGR 0.367). After applying conservative DPO (cDPO) on top of the Bayesian Multi-Weak SFT model, the average PGR further improves to 0.705 with a maximum of 0.813 across seeds. On the additional CosmosQA dataset, Bayesian Multi-Weak achieves PGR 0.571 versus 0.399 for the best single weak model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original single-model Weak-to-Strong (Burns et al., 2023), Bayesian Multi-Weak consistently wins: on SciQ classification the best single weak model (OPT-1.3B) reaches PGR 0.714 while Bayesian Multi-Weak-5 reaches 0.828 (+16% relative); on SLURP generation the best single model (OPT-1.3B) achieves PGR 0.367 versus 0.668 for Bayesian Multi-Weak-5 (+82% relative). Against FlyingSquid (Fu et al., 2020), Bayesian Multi-Weak outperforms on both classification datasets: PGR 0.828 vs. 0.768 on SciQ (5 weak models) and 0.571 vs. 0.303 on CosmosQA (3 weak models). The advantage over FlyingSquid is particularly pronounced on CosmosQA where weak model performance is lower and more variable, suggesting the Bayesian Dirichlet prior provides greater fault tolerance. Notably, the Naive Multi-Weak baseline with 3 weak models (PGR 0.661) does not even surpass the best single weak model (0.714), highlighting that simple ensembling is insufficient without the distributional estimation provided by the Bayesian approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Weak-to-Strong generalization framework of Burns et al. (2023) in two directions: replacing a single weak supervisor with an ensemble of multiple weak models (WeakS-to-Strong), and moving from text classification to text generation. The key methodological departure is the adaptation of evidential deep learning (Sensoy et al., 2018) to model the weak labels as observations drawn from an underlying Dirichlet distribution, rather than treating them as fixed targets (Naive Multi-Weak) or jointly decoding a single consensus sequence (Joint Decoding). For the generation setting, the paper further incorporates conservative DPO (cDPO) as a post-SFT preference optimization step, connecting the weak-supervision literature to the RLHF/DPO alignment pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Bayesian ensemble approach provides a principled, uncertainty-aware mechanism for handling disagreement among weak supervisors, which is directly relevant to the superalignment scenario where human (or weak-model) opinions are inherently variable and costly to obtain. The extension to generation tasks broadens the applicability of Weak-to-Strong beyond content classification to actual content production, though the tested generation task (slot filling) is relatively narrow. Open questions include whether the Bayesian distribution estimation scales to open-ended generation, whether more weak models continue to yield gains, and whether the approach generalizes to domains where weak-model disagreement is structured rather than independent. The paper's limitation to two tasks and at most five weak models leaves the practical ceiling of the method unclear.
