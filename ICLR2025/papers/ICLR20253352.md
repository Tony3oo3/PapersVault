## Problem

Explainable GNNs (XGNNs) are vulnerable to graph perturbation attacks that can drastically alter explanation outputs while preserving correct predictions, posing serious risks in safety-critical applications like drug discovery. While empirical defenses exist, they are ineffective against stronger adaptive attacks, and no certified (provably robust) defense for GNN explainers has been proposed. The fundamental challenge is that GNN explainers map graphs to edge sets rather than labels, making existing certified defenses for GNN classifiers inapplicable.

## Input / Output

Given a testing graph G, a trained GNN classifier f, and a GNN explainer g, XGNNCert outputs a predicted label y, a set of k explanatory edges E_k, and a certified perturbation size M_λ for each λ ∈ [1,k]. The certified perturbation size M_λ is the maximum number of edges that can be arbitrarily added or deleted from G while still guaranteeing at least λ of the k groundtruth explanatory edges appear in the output. The method requires hyperparameters T (number of subgraphs, default 70), p (fraction of complete-graph subgraphs to mix in, default 0.3), γ (score threshold fraction for voting, default 0.3), and a hash function h (default MD5).

## Method

XGNNCert is a three-step ensemble framework. First, it generates T hybrid subgraphs by hash-mapping both the test graph's edges and its complete graph's edges into T groups, then combining each test subgraph with a fraction p of complete-graph subgraphs (excluding its own index); this hybrid design ensures the explainer can distinguish explanatory from non-explanatory edges while Theorem 2 guarantees at most M hybrid subgraphs are affected by M edge perturbations. Second, it builds a majority-vote classifier over GNN predictions on the hybrid subgraphs and a majority-vote explainer that counts, for each edge, how many correctly-predicted subgraphs assign it an importance score above γ fraction of the subgraph's maximum. Third, Theorem 3 derives a deterministic certified perturbation size M_λ as the minimum of the classifier's vote-margin bound and the explainer's vote-gap bound against the top-M non-existent edges, providing a probability-1 guarantee that the prediction is unchanged and at least λ groundtruth explanatory edges are preserved under any perturbation of size ≤ M_λ.

## Results

On clean (no-attack) evaluation, XGNNCert maintains explanation accuracy close to the original GNN explainers (e.g., 0.725 vs 0.740 for PGExplainer on SG+House with T=70) and classification accuracy close to the original GNN classifiers (e.g., 0.905 vs 0.920 for GCN on SG+House). The key robustness result is that XGNNCert guarantees at least λ=2 correct explanatory edges out of k=5 groundtruth edges when an average of 6.2 edges are arbitrarily perturbed on SG+House. Under the adversarial attack from Li et al. (2024) with 2 perturbed edges, XGNNCert achieves a difference fraction of only 7.44% on SG+House (vs. 48.39% for V-InfoR) and 0.0% on SG+Diamond (vs. 73.07%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares XGNNCert against V-InfoR (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2023), the state-of-the-art empirical defense for robust GNN explainers, under the adversarial attack from Li et al. (2024) that perturbs 2 non-explanatory edges. XGNNCert dominates V-InfoR on every dataset for both metrics: it achieves higher explanation accuracy (e.g., 0.740 vs. 0.693 on SG+House, 0.729 vs. 0.419 on SG+Diamond) and dramatically lower fraction of changed explanatory edges (e.g., 7.44% vs. 48.39% on SG+House, 0.0% vs. 73.07% on SG+Diamond, 1.44% vs. 83.82% on Benzene). The gap is most pronounced on SG+Diamond where XGNNCert produces zero explanation change while V-InfoR changes 73% of edges. The paper attributes this advantage to XGNNCert's hybrid subgraph division and majority-voting scheme, which inherently dilutes adversarial perturbation effects into a bounded number of subgraphs, whereas V-InfoR is an empirical defense that constrains attack capability and is vulnerable to stronger attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the majority-voting partition-based certified defense paradigm (Deep Partition Aggregation, GNNCert) from the GNN classification setting to the fundamentally different task of GNN explanation robustness. The key conceptual departure is the 'hybrid subgraph' generation, which augments each partition with edges from the complete graph—a design necessitated by the fact that pure subgraph partitioning fails for explainers because (1) each edge appears in only one subgraph, making it hard for the explainer to rank true explanatory edges above non-explanatory ones, and (2) adversarially inserted edges must also be scored. The voting explainer, which aggregates edge importance scores across subgraphs rather than class labels, is a new aggregation mechanism not present in prior certified defenses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a deterministic (probability-1) robustness guarantee for GNN explanations, addressing a real safety concern in applications like drug repurposing where misleading explanations could have harmful consequences. However, the certified perturbation sizes remain modest (e.g., ~6 edges for λ=2 on SG+House), and the method is node-permutation variant due to the hash-based partitioning, limiting its practical deployment. Open questions include extending the framework to node-level and edge-level explanation tasks (where the disjoint-subgraph assumption breaks down), improving certified perturbation sizes, and reducing the T× computational overhead of running the base explainer on every hybrid subgraph.
