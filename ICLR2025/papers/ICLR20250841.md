## Problem

Video generative models must operate over a vast spatiotemporal space, making training extremely computationally and data intensive. Prevailing cascaded architectures reduce this cost by training separate models at each resolution, but this prevents knowledge sharing across stages and sacrifices flexibility. The paper asks whether the denoising trajectory itself can be restructured so that early, highly noisy timesteps operate at compressed resolutions while only the final stage runs at full resolution, all within a single unified model.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt (and optionally a reference image for image-to-video) and autoregressively generates video clips of 5–10 seconds at 768p resolution and 24 FPS. Because generation is autoregressive with blockwise causal attention, the first frame is produced as a text-to-image sample and subsequent frames are predicted conditioned on a temporally compressed history, enabling variable-length generation at inference without retraining. A 3D VAE (8×8×8 spatial-temporal compression) maps pixels to latents, and the autoregressive design natively supports both text-to-video and text-conditioned image-to-video without additional fine-tuning.

## Method

The core idea is a piecewise flow matching objective decomposed into K spatial pyramid stages, where each stage interpolates between a pixelated, noisier latent (upsampled from a lower resolution) and a pixelate-free, cleaner latent at a higher resolution, so that only the final stage operates at full resolution. All stages are jointly optimized in a single 2B-parameter MM-DiT (initialized from SD3 Medium) under one unified flow matching loss, eliminating the need for separate super-resolution models. At inference, jump points between stages are handled by a corrective renoising step that rescales the upsampled endpoint and adds Gaussian noise with a blockwise covariance (γ = −1/3) to decorrelate adjacent pixels and preserve probability-path continuity. A temporal pyramid further compresses the autoregressive history condition by representing earlier frames at progressively lower resolutions, reducing training tokens by up to 1/4^K and improving training efficiency by up to 16^K/T. The architecture uses full-sequence attention with blockwise causal masking, sinusoidal spatial position encoding with extrapolation in the spatial pyramid, and 1D RoPE in the temporal dimension with interpolation to spatially align history conditions.

## Results

On VBench, the method achieves a total score of 81.72 and a quality score of 84.74, surpassing all open-source baselines and even exceeding Gen-3 Alpha (84.11) on quality score. Motion smoothness reaches 99.12 and dynamic degree 64.63 (2nd among all compared models). On EvalCrafter, the method scores 244 final sum with 67.94 visual quality, outperforming all open-source baselines. Training requires only 20.7k A100 GPU hours for a 10-second, 241-frame, 768p, 24fps video model, and inference takes 56 seconds for a 5-second 384p clip.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all open-source baselines on VBench total score (81.72 vs. T2V-Turbo 81.01, VideoCrafter2 80.44, Open-Sora 1.2 79.76, Open-Sora Plan v1.3 77.23) and quality score (84.74 vs. T2V-Turbo 82.57). It also beats CogVideoX-5B (81.61 total, 82.75 quality) and Gen-3 Alpha (84.11 quality) on quality score, and surpasses CogVideoX-5B (70.97) and Gen-3 Alpha (60.14) on dynamic degree. However, it trails Gen-3 Alpha on total score (81.72 vs. 82.32) and semantic score (69.62 vs. 75.17), which the authors attribute to coarse-grained synthetic captions. On EvalCrafter, the method achieves 244 final sum score, edging out VideoCrafter2 (243) and LaVie (234) among open-source models, but falls short of Gen-2 (254) and Pika 1.0 (250) on overall score, though it leads all open-source models on visual quality (67.94 vs. VideoCrafter2 63.98). In user studies, the method is preferred over Open-Sora and CogVideoX-2B especially in motion smoothness, benefiting from its ability to generate 768p 24fps video where baselines typically support only 8fps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: (1) the flow matching generative framework (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023), (2) cascaded/pyramidal diffusion models that generate at low resolution then super-resolve (Ho et al., 2022b; Pernias et al., 2024; Teng et al., 2024), and (3) autoregressive video generation with history conditioning (Chen et al., 2024a; Valevski et al., 2024). The key departure from prior cascaded approaches is replacing multiple separately-trained super-resolution models with a single unified DiT trained via a piecewise flow matching objective across pyramid stages, while the temporal pyramid for history compression is a novel extension of the autoregressive paradigm. The spatial pyramid concept traces back to classical image pyramids (Adelson et al., 1984) and their use in discriminative networks (Lin et al., 2017), but the flow-matching-based unification of generation and decompression in one model is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing training tokens by up to 8× (≤15,360 vs. 119,040) enables competitive 768p 24fps 10-second video generation in only 20.7k A100 GPU hours using exclusively public data, while matching or exceeding commercial models trained on proprietary datasets. The unified single-model formulation eliminates the engineering overhead of maintaining separate super-resolution models and enables end-to-end optimization with knowledge sharing across resolutions. Key limitations remain: the autoregressive design precludes keyframe interpolation, the temporal pyramid can introduce subtle subject inconsistency over long horizons, and face consistency degrades. Open questions include whether better temporal compression schemes can resolve consistency issues, whether the approach extends to real-time inference, and whether the unified pyramid objective generalizes beyond the 3-stage configuration tested here.
