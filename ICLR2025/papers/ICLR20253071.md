## Problem

Persistent homology provides principled topological summaries of data distributions, but two barriers prevent its use as a regularizer in adversarial deep learning: (1) full PH computation on large point clouds is prohibitively expensive and difficult to parallelize, and (2) gradient discontinuities in PH lead to unstable training dynamics. Standard distributional metrics (Wasserstein, MMD) only implicitly capture topology and do not explicitly regularize topological features at the non-equilibrium states where GANs typically operate.

## Input / Output

In the GAN setting, the generator takes a 128-dimensional noise vector and produces 32×32 or 64×64 RGB images, while the discriminator maps images to a 128-dimensional latent vector on which the topological regularizer operates. For shape matching, the method directly optimizes 512-point clouds in R². For semi-supervised learning, a 64-dimensional latent representation is extracted by a frozen discriminator and fed to an MLP classifier trained on only 200 or 400 labeled examples. The PPM computation itself draws 2q+2 i.i.d. subsamples (4 points for H₁, 2 for H₀) from the latent measure, with s=1024–2048 such subsamples per batch.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces full persistent homology with Principal Persistence Measures (PPMs): for each of s small subsamples of exactly 2q+2 points, the q-th homology is computed in closed form (reducing to nearest-neighbor distance lookups for q=1), and the resulting birth-lifetime pairs are aggregated into a probability measure on the persistence space Ω. PPMs are compared via a Maximum Mean Discrepancy using a lifetime-weighted RBF kernel k_Ω(z₁,z₂)=ℓ₁ℓ₂ exp(−‖z₁−z₂‖²/2σ), which admits a closed-form O(n²) computation with no iterative optimization. The regularizer T_q = MMD(PPM_q(μ), PPM_q(ν)) is added to a Cramer distance loss as V = L + λT, with separate weights λ₀ and λ₁ for H₀ and H₁. Three theoretical results underpin the approach: characteristic kernels on Ω (Theorem 1), topological equivalence between MMD and Wasserstein on P(Ω) (Theorem 2), and C¹ continuity of the full regularizer with respect to network parameters under smooth density assumptions (Theorem 3). A pure PyTorch GPU implementation parallelizes all s subsamples simultaneously, yielding sublinear scaling in s and near-constant cost in point cloud size.

## Results

On 32×32 unconditional image generation, Cramer+PPM-Reg reduces CMMD from 0.73 to 0.56 on AnimeFace and from 0.72 to 0.58 on CelebA, while also improving FD_Dinov2 (953.99→780.68 and 722.86→700.73) and WD_latent (0.6294→0.6080 and 0.6795→0.6666). In semi-supervised classification with only 200 labels, PPM-Reg yields dramatic gains: Kuzushiji-MNIST accuracy jumps from 48.40% (Baseline) to 75.78%, and MNIST from 80.52% to 96.62%. At 64×64 resolution, PPM-Reg again improves CMMD (0.52→0.46 on CelebA, 1.56→1.31 on LSUN Kitchen) and FD_Dinov2 across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is the Cramer distance GAN (Bellemare et al., 2017), used as the main loss without topological regularization. Cramer+PPM-Reg wins on every reported metric across all three experimental settings. In image generation (32×32), PPM-Reg reduces CMMD by ~23% on AnimeFace (0.73→0.56) and ~19% on CelebA (0.72→0.58), with consistent improvements in FD_Dinov2 and WD_latent. In semi-supervised learning, the comparison is particularly striking: Cramer alone actually underperforms the no-GAN Baseline on Fashion-MNIST (62.70% vs. 67.18% at 200 labels) and MNIST (71.10% vs. 80.52%), yet Cramer+PPM-Reg surpasses both by large margins (76.84% and 96.62% respectively), demonstrating that the topological regularizer is what enables the GAN's latent representations to be useful for classification. The 64×64 experiments (Table 6) confirm the trend at higher resolution, with the largest relative gain on LSUN Kitchen (CMMD 1.56→1.31, a 16% reduction), suggesting PPM-Reg is most beneficial when the underlying data manifold has more complex topology.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Gómez & Mémoli's (2024) introduction of principal persistence measures (PPMs), which replace full persistent homology with the PH of many small (2q+2)-point subsamples, and on Kusano et al.'s (2016) persistence-weighted Gaussian kernels for comparing persistence diagrams. The key departure is replacing the expensive Wasserstein (or Sinkhorn) metric between PPMs with an MMD metric built from characteristic kernels on the persistence space, and proving that the resulting regularizer has C1-continuous gradients under smooth density assumptions (Theorem 3) — a property not available for standard PH-based losses. Prior topological regularizers (Clough et al. 2022, Shit et al. 2021) relied on full Vietoris-Rips persistent homology, which is prohibitively expensive and non-parallelizable at scale; Solomon et al. (2021) used subsampling for topological optimization but did not formalize the PPM framework or provide the kernel-based comparison metric developed here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first practical, GPU-parallelized topological regularizer that is both scalable (sublinear in subsample count) and provably smooth, removing the two main barriers (computational cost and gradient discontinuity) that have kept persistent homology out of large-scale adversarial training. The striking semi-supervised learning results (e.g., 96.6% on MNIST with only 200 labels vs. 80.5% baseline) suggest that explicitly matching topological structure in latent space can dramatically improve downstream classification when labels are scarce. Open questions include whether the approach extends to higher homology dimensions, multiparameter persistence, or larger image resolutions (experiments top out at 64×64), and whether the gains persist with more standard GAN architectures (e.g., StyleGAN) rather than the relatively simple ResNet-CNN setup used here.
