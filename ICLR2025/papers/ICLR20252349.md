## Problem

Existing symbolic regression methods optimize prediction error (e.g., MSE) as their search objective, but prediction error does not decrease monotonically as a candidate formula's symbolic form approaches the target, because structurally similar formulas can have very different functional shapes. This lack of optimal substructure means small adjustments to sub-optimal formulas do not reliably lead toward the correct formula, resulting in low recovery rates (~20%) even on noise-free data. The paper asks whether a search objective that does decrease monotonically along the path to the target can be learned and used to guide symbolic regression.

## Input / Output

The MDLformer takes numerical data (x ∈ R^{N×D}, y ∈ R^{N×1}), tokenizes each value into a sign/mantissa/exponent triplet (e.g., 54.321 → [+, 5.432, E+1]), pads D to D_max=10, and samples at most N_max=200 x-y pairs to control sequence length. It outputs a scalar MDL estimate (number of symbols in the simplest formula). The full SR4MDL pipeline then uses this estimate to guide MCTS over subformula sets f ∈ F_{D×D_i} (D_i ≤ 10), and the final output is a symbolic formula f* = φ_θ(f̂) where φ_θ is a linear combination of the best candidate subformulas fitted to y.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-component system: (1) MDLformer, a Transformer encoder (8 layers, 8 heads, 512 hidden units, no positional encoding) with attention-based pooling and an MLP readout that maps numerical data to an estimated MDL, trained on ~131M generated formula-data pairs using a two-step strategy—first a symmetric cross-entropy alignment loss between numeric and symbolic encoder latent spaces, then a primary MSE loss on MDL prediction; and (2) SR4MDL, which replaces the prediction-error-based UCB in MCTS with a PUCT score that incorporates MDLformer's estimated MDL (Eq. 7), maintaining a search tree of subformula sets rather than full candidate formulas. The key insight is that MDL has optimal substructure: only correct transformations reduce MDL, so the search direction of MDL reduction is always consistent with the direction toward the target formula, unlike prediction error which can increase or decrease non-monotonically.

## Results

On the Strogatz dataset (14 problems), SR4MDL achieves a 66.78% formula recovery rate, nearly 4.4× the best baseline (AIFeynman2 at 15.27%). On the Feynman dataset (119 problems), it reaches 33.93% recovery, outperforming AIFeynman2 (27.24%) by 11.94 percentage points. Overall, the method recovers ~50 formulas out of 133, a 43.92% improvement over the best baseline (~35 formulas), with search times of 338.3s and 660.5s respectively. On the 122-problem PMLB black-box set, SR4MDL shares Pareto rank 1 (balancing test R² and formula complexity) with Operon, GP-GOMEA, and DSR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SR4MDL decisively outperforms all 22 baselines on both ground-truth datasets. On Strogatz, it achieves 66.78% recovery vs. AIFeynman2 (15.27%), DSR (18.05%), and AFP-FE (12.86%) — the top three search-based baselines — representing a ~4.4× improvement over the best. On Feynman, it reaches 33.93% vs. AIFeynman2 (27.24%), AFP-FE (20.80%), and DSR (18.60%). Generative methods (NeurSR, E2ESR, SNIP) and the regression method (FEAT) trail far behind, with recovery rates below 11%, confirming that search-based approaches are better suited for recovering exact formula forms. On the PMLB black-box set, SR4MDL shares Pareto rank 1 with Operon, GP-GOMEA, and DSR, achieving a favorable accuracy–complexity trade-off (test R²=0.6258, complexity=29.88) in only 541.7s, far faster than SBP-GP (149,344s) or BSR (59,822s). Notably, SR4MDL's advantage is robust to noise: at ε=0.1 it still recovers 62.86% on Strogatz, exceeding AIFeynman2's noise-free rate of 15.27%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in symbolic regression where all search-based methods (GP, MCTS, DRL) optimize prediction error (MSE) as the search objective. It builds on the MCTS framework from SPL (Sun et al., 2022) and the cross-modal alignment strategy from SNIP (Meidani et al., 2023), but introduces a fundamentally different search objective: minimum description length (MDL) estimated by a purpose-built Transformer (MDLformer). The key conceptual shift is that MDL, unlike prediction error, possesses optimal substructure—its value decreases monotonically as the candidate formula's form approaches the target—making the search direction consistently aligned with the ground truth. The formula generation pipeline and numeric tokenization scheme are adapted from E2ESR (Kamienny et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 43.92% improvement in formula recovery over the best baseline (AIFeynman2) is substantial for a field where recovery rates have plateaued around 20–30%, and the noise robustness (outperforming noise-free baselines at 10% noise) addresses a practical limitation of prior methods. The conceptual insight—that the search objective, not the search algorithm, is the bottleneck—reframes how the community might approach SR and potentially other combinatorial search problems. Limitations include MDLformer's degraded performance on complex multi-variable relationships, slower inference than generative methods, and reliance on a large-scale (131M pair) training corpus. Open questions include whether the MDL-based search objective generalizes to other structured prediction tasks and whether the linear remaining-transformation assumption (φ_θ) is sufficient for highly nonlinear targets.
