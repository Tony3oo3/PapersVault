## Problem

End-to-end ASR models face a fundamental accuracy–efficiency trade-off: CTC models enable fast non-autoregressive inference but sacrifice accuracy, while RNN-T and TDT models achieve higher accuracy but require sequential autoregressive decoding. No single transducer architecture previously supported both fast NAR and accurate AR inference from the same trained weights, forcing practitioners to choose between speed and quality.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 80-dimensional filterbank features (25 ms windows, 10 ms strides) from speech audio and produces BPE token sequences (vocabulary size 1024) as transcribed text. A FastConformer encoder with 8× temporal subsampling processes the audio, and the joint network emits per-frame token and duration distributions; durations (0–8) control how many encoder frames are skipped per prediction step. The same trained model supports three inference modes—AR, NAR, and SAR—plus an optional Viterbi post-processing step, all without retraining.

## Method

HAINAN extends the Token-and-Duration Transducer (TDT) with a single training modification: the predictor network's output is stochastically masked (p=0.5) before being fed to the joint network, forcing the joint to learn valid token/duration distributions both with and without text-history context. At inference, this enables three modes: AR (full TDT decoding), NAR (all-zero predictor input, fully parallel joint computation over all frames), and a novel SAR mode that first runs NAR to obtain an initial hypothesis with timestamps, then feeds shifted partial hypotheses through the predictor to re-score only the token-emitting frames in parallel. Additionally, the NAR outputs are modeled as a DAG where node weights are argmax token probabilities and edge weights are duration probabilities, solved by a simplified O(TD) Viterbi algorithm for further accuracy gains. The stochastic masking also naturally suppresses zero-duration predictions, eliminating the infinite-loop risk in NAR decoding.

## Results

On the Huggingface ASR leaderboard (8 English datasets), HAINAN/0 with SAR-1 + Viterbi achieves the best average WER of 6.93%, outperforming RNN-T (7.15%), TDT (7.13%), and CTC (7.38%). In NAR mode, HAINAN achieves 7.07% AVG WER at 40s decoding time, matching CTC's speed (39s) while reducing WER by 0.31 points. In AR mode, HAINAN matches TDT accuracy (7.10% vs 7.13%) at comparable speed (89s vs 88s), while SAR-1 closes the AR–NAR gap to just 0.17 WER points at only 44s. On German ASR, HAINAN NAR achieves 5.11% WER on MLS vs. CTC's 5.92%, at similar inference speed (20s vs 22s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RNN-T, HAINAN AR matches or slightly improves accuracy (7.10% vs 7.15% AVG WER) while being 2× faster (89s vs 179s); HAINAN NAR further beats RNN-T by 0.25 WER points at 4.4× speed. Against TDT, HAINAN AR achieves near-identical accuracy (7.10% vs 7.13%) at the same speed, while HAINAN NAR trades 0.06 WER for a 2.1× speedup (41s vs 88s). Against CTC, HAINAN NAR delivers a clear 0.19–0.31 WER advantage (7.19% vs 7.38% on English; 5.11% vs 5.92% on German MLS) at essentially identical inference speed, attributed to HAINAN's ability to model acoustic ambiguity across frames. Against the stateless TDT baseline, HAINAN NAR (7.08%) matches TDT AR (7.06%) while running 2× faster, and HAINAN/0 SAR-1+Viterbi (6.93%) surpasses all AR baselines. Against Hybrid-TDT-CTC (Appendix A.2), HAINAN outperforms in both AR (7.10% vs 7.18%) and NAR (7.19% vs 7.51%) modes without requiring separate parameter sets for each inference mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAINAN extends the Token-and-Duration Transducer (TDT) framework by introducing stochastic predictor masking during training—a single-line modification that enables the joint network to produce valid token and duration distributions both with and without predictor context. This positions the work at the intersection of RNN-T's encoder-predictor-joint architecture and CTC's non-autoregressive inference paradigm, which prior transducer work (RNN-T, TDT, Multi-blank Transducers) had treated as mutually exclusive. The semi-autoregressive inference method draws on the 'fast/slow' cascade literature (deliberation models, fast-slow cascaded encoders) but departs by requiring only marginal additional computation rather than a separate slow model. The Viterbi-based DAG decoding is a novel application of dynamic programming to transducer outputs, leveraging the richer per-frame representations that stochastic masking encourages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HAINAN offers a practical, low-cost path to closing the long-standing accuracy-efficiency gap between CTC and Transducer models: a one-line training change yields a single model supporting three inference modes with controllable speed-accuracy tradeoffs. The finding that NAR HAINAN captures acoustic ambiguity (e.g., 'ice cream' vs. 'i scream') in ways frame-by-frame CTC fundamentally cannot provides a mechanistic explanation for its accuracy advantage over CTC. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other tasks (speech translation, synthesis), whether SAR refinement can be made more effective at correcting deletion errors, and whether the stochastic masking strategy transfers to other transducer variants or encoder architectures beyond FastConformer.
