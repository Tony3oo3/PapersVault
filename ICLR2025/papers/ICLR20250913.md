## Problem

Sampling from Boltzmann distributions near criticality is hindered by scale invariance, which causes critical slowing down in MCMC and demands intractably large receptive fields for generative models. MultiLevel Monte Carlo with Heat Bath (MLMC-HB) exploits scale invariance for efficient ancestral sampling but suffers from low acceptance rates under the slight SIC violation that always occurs in finite systems, because its independent nearest-neighbor conditionals ignore the long-range and higher-order interactions present in the true renormalized distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250913/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D binary lattice of size N×N (with N up to 128), an inverse temperature β set at the critical point (β=0.44), and coupling constant J=1. It produces independent spin configurations s ∈ {−1,1}^{N×N} drawn from the Boltzmann distribution, structured hierarchically across L+1 resolution levels via ancestral sampling. These samples enable asymptotically unbiased estimation of thermodynamic observables (free energy via importance-weighted partition function) and general observables (energy, magnetization, susceptibility) with quantified bias (EMDM) and variance (ESS).

## Method

RiGCS performs ancestral sampling from coarsest to finest resolution level, replacing the naive nearest-neighbor heat bath conditionals at intermediate levels (l=1,…,L−1) with autoregressive PixelCNN models whose receptive fields (7×7 at intermediate levels, full lattice at the coarsest level) capture the long-range and higher-order interactions in the renormalized Hamiltonian. The finest level uses exact independent heat-bath sampling since its conditional has only NN interactions by construction. Training minimizes reverse KL divergence using self-generated samples (no target data required), and a sequential training protocol with model transfer warm-starts parameters from smaller lattice systems to larger ones, leveraging SIC to ensure that models at the same resolution level across different total sizes are similar, thereby avoiding the long random-walking phase of random initialization.

## Results

On the 2D Ising model at criticality (β=0.44), RiGCS achieves unbiased free energy estimates at N=128, matching the ground-truth Onsager solution, while HAN produces highly biased estimates and VAN cannot be trained beyond N=32. In terms of Effective Sample Size (ESS) at N=128, RiGCS improves over HAN by several orders of magnitude, making it the only generative model with non-vanishing ESS. The Effective Mode-Dropping Measure (EMDM) remains ≈1 for RiGCS at N=128 (no mode collapse), whereas HAN's EMDM drops significantly. Training a N=64 model takes ~3.8 hours with RiGCS (vs. ~60 days for VAN and ~2.8 hours for HAN), and sampling 100 configurations costs ~0.4 seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RiGCS clearly outperforms HAN (the previous state-of-the-art generative sampler) at large lattice sizes: at N=128, RiGCS maintains unbiased free energy estimates and EMDM≈1, while HAN exhibits strong bias and significant mode-dropping, with RiGCS improving ESS by several orders of magnitude. Against VAN (plain autoregressive network without multilevel sampling), RiGCS wins decisively on scalability—VAN simply cannot be trained for N≥64 within reasonable time (~60 days for N=64), while RiGCS trains in ~3.8 hours. Compared to MLMC-HB, RiGCS eliminates the severe bias that MLMC-HB develops for N≥32 by replacing the nearest-neighbor heat-batch conditionals with generative models that capture long-range and higher-order interactions. Against the Wolff cluster method (Cluster-AIS), RiGCS achieves comparable but slightly inferior ESS at N=128, confirming that specialized cluster algorithms remain the gold standard for the Ising model, while RiGCS is the best-performing *generative* sampler. In terms of sampling speed, RiGCS (~0.4 s for 100 samples) is slightly slower than HAN (~0.2 s) but vastly faster than VAN (~27 s) and MLMC-HB (~14 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLMC-HB framework (Schmidt, 1983), which performs ancestral sampling across resolution levels using nearest-neighbor heat bath conditionals, by replacing those conditionals with autoregressive generative models (PixelCNNs) that capture the long-range and higher-order interactions arising from approximate scale invariance. It also builds on the line of generative samplers for lattice systems (VAN, HAN) but departs from them by embedding the generative models within a multilevel hierarchy rather than applying them to the full lattice, and by introducing a sequential training protocol with model transfer across resolution levels that exploits SIC to warm-start parameters. The conceptual departure from HAN (Białas et al., 2022) is that RiGCS uses a block-spin partitioning with resolution-dependent receptive fields rather than recursive domain decomposition with a single shared network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RiGCS is the first generative sampler to produce unbiased estimates at N=128 for the 2D Ising model at criticality, where the previous state-of-the-art (HAN) becomes biased and vanilla VAN cannot be trained at all. The sequential training with model transfer reduces wall-clock training from ~60 days (VAN) to ~3.8 hours, making generative sampling near criticality practically accessible. However, the method is validated only on the 2D Ising model at a single critical temperature, and it does not surpass the Wolff cluster algorithm for general observable estimation. Open questions include extension to 3D systems, continuous-variable models, Potts models, and lattice gauge theories, as well as whether the multilevel generative approach can be combined with domain decomposition (HAN) for further gains.
