## Problem

Current MLLMs exhibit poor perception of complex physical diagrams (e.g., circuit schematics) and hallucinate during the mathematical derivations required to solve expert-level physical science problems. Existing multi-modal reasoning approaches—Chain-of-Thought prompting and general tool-use—target simple diagrams or general images and fail to transfer to domains requiring precise structural understanding and quantitative simulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253242/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a pixel-format circuit diagram (X_V) and an accompanying textual problem description (X_L) as input, and produces a final numerical answer (Y_L). Internally, the PPM converts the pixel diagram into a SPICE netlist description, which the MLLM refines by merging component values from the text (handling both numerical-type diagrams where values appear on the diagram and label-type diagrams where only symbolic labels like R₁ are shown). The refined SPICE code is executed by NgSPICE to yield state and observation values, which together with the SL description serve as intermediate rationale for the MLLM's final reasoning step.

## Method

MAPS decomposes expert-level multi-modal physical reasoning into two sub-tasks: diagram perception and simulation-aided analysis. For perception, a Physics Perception Model (PPM) is obtained by LoRA fine-tuning CogVLM-17B (11.6B trainable parameters) on 20k synthetic circuit-diagram/SPICE pairs generated by an automated pipeline that samples hierarchical grid layouts, renders them via CircuitTikz/LaTeX, and derives the corresponding netlist-to-SPICE code through physical rules. At inference, a Chain-of-Simulation process first uses the PPM to produce an initial SPICE description, then the MLLM refines it with textual context, and finally NgSPICE executes the simulation to obtain ground-truth state values. The MLLM then performs Simulation-Aided Reasoning, using the SL description and simulation results as structured intermediate rationale to derive the final answer, effectively offloading all quantitative computation to the simulator and eliminating the mathematical hallucinations that plague direct MLLM reasoning.

## Results

On SimpleCircuitEval (79 college-level circuit analysis problems), MAPS with GPT-4V achieves 32.9% accuracy, a 4.3× improvement over GPT-4V's direct prompting baseline of 7.6%. The framework also yields 4.0× and 3.0× improvements when paired with Claude-3.5 (25.3% vs. 6.33%) and GLM-4V (19.0% vs. 6.33%), respectively. The PPM component achieves 85.4% simulation accuracy on synthetic circuits but drops to 53.3% on real-world diagrams, indicating room for improvement in generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAPS consistently outperforms all direct-prompting baselines across every MLLM backbone tested. With GPT-4V, MAPS achieves 32.9% accuracy versus 7.6% for GPT-4V alone and 5.1% for GPT-4V with Multimodal-CoT (MMCoT), showing that the Chain-of-Simulation approach substantially surpasses both naive prompting and CoT-style intermediate rationale generation. The same pattern holds for Claude-3.5 (25.3% vs. 6.33%) and GLM-4V (19.0% vs. 6.33%), confirming the framework's model-agnostic benefit. Even the strongest standalone baseline, GPT-4o at 10.1%, is outperformed by MAPS+GPT-4V by more than 3×. Notably, all methods struggle on Chapter 2 problems (equivalent resistance transformation), where circuits are not directly simulable, suggesting that the simulator-assisted workflow is most effective when the physical scene admits a well-posed simulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant multi-modal CoT paradigm (Zhang et al. 2023b; Zheng et al. 2023; Mitra et al. 2024), which relies on the MLLM to both perceive diagram structure and perform quantitative reasoning, by decomposing the task into a specialized Physics Perception Model (PPM) for diagram-to-simulation-language translation and a domain-specific physical simulator for numerical computation. The 'Chain-of-Simulation' concept replaces free-form CoT rationales with executable SPICE descriptions and their simulation outputs as intermediate reasoning steps. The automated data synthesis pipeline for training the PPM draws on the broader trend of synthetic data generation for VLMs but is specifically designed to cover the structured space of physical diagrams (planar grids, component topologies) rather than natural images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAPS demonstrates a clean architectural decomposition—perception, simulation, reasoning—that is in principle generalizable across physical disciplines (mechanics, optics, etc.), offering a template for building domain-specific multi-modal scientific agents. However, the current validation is narrow: only 79 LPRC circuit problems, a static (non-interactive) workflow with the simulator, and PPM accuracy on real-world diagrams of only ~50%, all of which limit confidence in broader applicability. Key open questions include whether the PPM can be made universal across diagram types, whether dynamic feedback loops with simulators (rather than one-shot simulation) are necessary for non-simulatable problems, and whether the data synthesis approach scales to the combinatorial complexity of real engineering schematics.
