## Problem

Standard DPO treats the entire response as a single bandit arm, assigning equal optimization weight to every token regardless of its actual contribution to response quality. In practice, even winning responses contain low-reward tokens, and this uniform treatment introduces noise that degrades optimization efficiency. The paper identifies that the theoretically optimal DPO data distribution would have equal expected rewards per token within each response, but real datasets violate this condition.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard preference data triples (prompt x, winning response y_w, losing response y_l) along with a base LLM, and additionally requires constructing two contrastive LLMs (π⁺ biased toward high-reward tokens, π⁻ biased toward low-reward tokens) to precompute per-token importance weights. These weights are fixed scalars per token position and are not optimized during training. The output is a single aligned LLM, with the same architecture as the base model, trained for one epoch.

## Method

TIS-DPO performs token-level importance sampling from the real data distribution D to an optimal distribution D* where each token has equal expected reward, yielding an unbiased DPO objective. The key theoretical result (Theorem 2) shows D*(y^t|x,y^{<t}) = D(y^t|x,y^{<t}) / w(y^t|x,y^{<t}), where w is proportional to exp(μ·r), linking importance weights directly to token rewards. The final TIS-DPO loss (Eq. 18) modifies the standard DPO objective by multiplying each token's log-probability ratio by its importance weight and adding a weighted sequence KL divergence term η. Token rewards are estimated as the log-probability ratio between two contrastive LLMs: w_t = k·exp(μ·clamp(log(π⁺(y_t|x,y^{<t})/π⁻(y_t|x,y^{<t})), L, U)), with μ>0 for winning tokens and μ<0 for losing tokens. Three practical strategies construct the contrastive LLMs: contrastive prompting (no extra training), separate SFT on winning/losing responses, and forward/reverse DPO training (swapping preference labels), with the DPO-based approach yielding the most accurate weights.

## Results

On PKU-SafeRLHF with LLaMA2-7B, TIS-DPO(D) achieves 96.7% Llama-Guard safety (vs. 74.4% for DPO and 79.8% for KTO) and a Beaver-Cost harmlessness score of 0.1 (vs. 5.6 for DPO), with a 79.3% GPT-4 win rate over DPO. On Anthropic-HH, TIS-DPO(D) reaches 92.6% Llama-Guard (vs. 56.7% DPO) and 83.8% win rate. On Mistral-7B, TIS-DPO(D) further improves to 98.7% and 92.6% Llama-Guard on the two datasets respectively, while maintaining or slightly improving helpfulness (Beaver-Reward 8.5–9.1) and MT-bench scores (4.5–4.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIS-DPO(D) dominates all six baselines on safety metrics: it improves Llama-Guard by 16.9 pp over the strongest baseline KTO (79.8%) on PKU-SafeRLHF and by 35.4 pp over DPO (56.7%) on Anthropic-HH, while reducing the Beaver-Cost harmlessness score from 4.1 (KTO) to 0.1 on PKU-SafeRLHF. TIS-DPO(S) also clearly beats every baseline on safety (89.6% vs. 79.8% Llama-Guard on PKU-SafeRLHF) but with a smaller margin than TIS-DPO(D). On helpfulness, TIS-DPO(D) matches or slightly exceeds DPO (8.0 vs. 7.9 on PKU-SafeRLHF; 9.2 vs. 8.4 on Anthropic-HH), while TIS-DPO(S) is marginally lower on PKU-SafeRLHF (7.8 vs. 7.9). The prompt-based variant TIS-DPO(P) underperforms DPO on both safety and win rate, which the authors attribute to a distribution mismatch between the original dataset and the LLM's output. On the TL;DR summarization task (GPT-J-6B), TIS-DPO(D) consistently outperforms DPO, TDPO, and IPO across all sampling temperatures, with the gap widening at higher temperatures. Under 20% annotation noise, TIS-DPO(D) degrades far less than DPO (93.2% vs. 65.2% Llama-Guard), confirming its robustness advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TDPO (Zeng et al., 2024), which already reformulated DPO at the token level, by introducing importance sampling weights that account for varying token importance. The weight estimation technique draws on Rafailov et al. (2024a)'s insight that log-probability ratios between models can serve as reward proxies, applied here through pairs of contrastive LLMs. The core theoretical contribution is framing DPO's optimal data distribution as one where all tokens share equal expected reward, then using importance sampling to correct for the actual (heterogeneous) data distribution — a principled departure from prior work that either treats tokens uniformly (DPO, TDPO) or requires modifying data collection to identify important tokens (Xie et al., 2024; Lai et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TIS-DPO provides a theoretically grounded mechanism for token-level credit assignment in preference optimization without altering the data pipeline, which is practically important given the widespread adoption of DPO. The ~3× computational overhead (training two contrastive models plus the main model) is a significant barrier to adoption, and the prompt-based variant — the cheapest option — underperforms plain DPO on human-annotated data, limiting its accessibility. Open questions include whether more efficient weight estimation (e.g., amortized or learned) can close the gap, whether the importance sampling framework extends to multi-turn or agentic settings, and whether the safety gains generalize to broader alignment dimensions beyond harmlessness.
