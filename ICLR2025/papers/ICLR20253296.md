## Problem

Multi-teacher and teaching-assistant knowledge distillation methods improve student generalization by providing diverse supervisory perspectives, but they require training multiple teacher networks, making them computationally expensive and resource-intensive. No prior work has explored whether a single pretrained teacher can be augmented to simulate multiple perspectives without additional training, leaving the third fundamental KD concern—insufficient perspective diversity from a single teacher—unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB classification images (CIFAR-100 at 32×32, ImageNet at 224×224, TinyImageNet at 64×64, STL-10 at 96×96) and produces class probability distributions over C classes. The key I/O detail is that the pretrained teacher's intermediate feature maps (h×w×c tensors) and final logits (C-dimensional vectors) are consumed as supervision signals, and Gaussian noise is injected into both to generate N synthetic supervision targets per input, so the student effectively receives N+1 distinct supervisory signals from a single teacher per training sample.

## Method

TeKAP generates multiple synthetic teacher perspectives by perturbing a single pretrained teacher's outputs with Gaussian noise (zero mean, unit variance) at both the feature-map level and the logit level, using a fixed mixing coefficient α=0.1 (i.e., perturbed output = 0.9 × original + 0.1 × noise). The noise is resampled at every batch, creating a dynamic ensemble of N augmented teachers that shifts inter-class correlations and introduces representation diversity without any additional training. The student is trained with a unified loss that linearly combines feature-level distillation (CRD), logit-level distillation (KD), and cross-entropy, where each distillation term itself blends the original teacher signal with the N perturbed signals via a balancing weight λ. A Rademacher-complexity-based analysis shows that the added noise increases prediction variability, acting as a regularization term that smooths the loss landscape and reduces generalization error. The approach is plug-and-play, train-free with respect to the teacher, and reduces computational cost from O(K) teacher trainings to O(1) plus negligible noise-generation overhead.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, TeKAP(F+L) (combined feature and logit perturbation) achieves 75.98% top-1 accuracy for ResNet32x4→ResNet8x4, 77.38% for ResNet32x4→ShuffleNetV2, and 76.59% for WRN-40-2→ShuffleNetV1, consistently outperforming both KD+TeKAP(L) and CRD+TeKAP(F) across all eight teacher-student architecture pairs. On ImageNet-1K (ResNet-34→ResNet-18), KD+TeKAP(L) reduces top-1 error from 29.59% to 29.33% and top-5 error from 10.30% to 10.08%, confirming scalability. As a plug-and-play module, TeKAP improves DKD by up to 0.52%, MLKD by up to 0.31%, TAKD by up to 0.88%, CA-MKD by up to 0.44%, and DGKD by up to 0.94% on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TeKAP consistently outperforms all seven baselines. Against the teaching-assistant approach TAKD (Mirzadeh et al., 2020), TeKAP(F+L) wins in every one of the eight CIFAR-100 architecture pairs, e.g., 75.98% vs. 73.81% (ResNet32x4→ResNet8x4) and 77.38% vs. 74.82% (ResNet32x4→ShuffleNetV2), while requiring no additional teacher training. As a plug-and-play add-on, TeKAP improves DKD (Zhao et al., 2022) from 76.32→76.59% and MLKD (Jin et al., 2023) from 77.08→77.36% on ResNet32x4→ResNet8x4. It also lifts the multi-teacher methods CA-MKD (Zhang et al., 2022) by 0.44% and DGKD (Son et al., 2021) by 0.94% on the same setup. Compared to the original KD (Hinton, 2015) and CRD (Tian et al., 2019) baselines, TeKAP(F+L) yields the largest gains when the student architecture differs substantially from the teacher (e.g., +2.74% over KD on ResNet32x4→ShuffleNetV2), while gains are more modest for same-architecture pairs (e.g., +0.23% on WRN-40-2→WRN-40-1).

## Relation to Prior Work

TeKAP builds directly on the foundational KD framework of Hinton (2015) and the feature-level distillation of CRD (Tian et al., 2019), positioning itself as a plug-and-play augmentation layer on top of any existing distillation method. It departs from the multi-teacher and teacher-assistant lineage (TAKD, CA-MKD, DGKD) by replacing the need to train multiple networks with a train-free Gaussian noise perturbation of a single teacher's features and logits. Conceptually, it repurposes ideas from feature-map distortion for regularization (Tang et al., 2020) and noisy-label generalization (Song et al., 2022) into the KD setting, arguing that stochastic perturbation of teacher knowledge simulates ensemble diversity without the associated training cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that TeKAP offers a near-zero-cost way to inject diversity into any KD pipeline, potentially making multi-teacher approaches unnecessary for many applications. The finding that noisy teacher outputs help students match the teacher's inter-class correlations *more* closely than clean KD is a useful conceptual insight for understanding why regularization aids transfer. However, the noise schedule is fixed (α=0.1, zero-mean unit-variance Gaussian) and not optimized, and the paper acknowledges this as a limitation; whether learned or adaptive perturbation strategies yield further gains remains open. Broader applicability beyond vision classification (e.g., language, multimodal) is unexplored.
