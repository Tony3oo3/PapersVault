## Problem

Existing MNL assortment optimization algorithms (e.g., MNL-UCB, MNL-TS) require two impractical assumptions: a No-Choice item that is the strongest (θ₀ ≥ max θᵢ) and repeated querying of the same assortment until that reference item is selected. This paper asks whether a regret-optimal, practically deployable algorithm can be designed that removes both restrictions while still achieving Õ(√(KT)) regret for both Top-m and weighted-Top-m objectives.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner selects a subset S_t ⊆ [K] of at most m items and receives a single winner index i_t ∈ S_t ∪ {0} drawn from the MNL model (winner-only feedback, not a full ranking). The algorithm maintains a pairwise win-count matrix W ∈ ℝ^{(K+1)×(K+1)} updated via rank-breaking of each winner observation, and outputs the next assortment S_t chosen optimistically from UCB estimates of the PL parameters θᵢ. The No-Choice item (index 0) is treated as a regular item with θ₀ = 1 (by scale invariance) rather than a guaranteed-strongest reference.

## Method

The core mechanism is Rank-Breaking (RB): each single-winner observation i_t from assortment S_t is decomposed into |S_t| pairwise comparisons (i_t ≻ j for all j ∈ S_t ∪ {0}), building empirical pairwise win probabilities p̂_ij,t. PL parameter UCBs are then derived as θ_ucb = p_i0,ucb / (1 − p_i0,ucb)₊, and the next assortment is chosen to maximize the objective under these optimistic parameters. The key improvement (AOA-RB_PL-Adaptive) replaces the fixed pivot (item 0) with an adaptive one: θ̂_ucb = min_j γ_ij,ucb · γ_j0,ucb, routing each item's estimate through the strongest intermediate item j, which eliminates the asymptotic θ_max dependence in the regret bound (yielding Õ(√(min{θ_max, K} · KT)) instead of Õ(√(θ_max · KT))). This avoids the need to repeat assortments and works even when the No-Choice item is highly unlikely to be selected.

## Results

Theoretically, the proposed AOA-RB_PL-Adaptive algorithm achieves a regret bound of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{\min\{\theta_{\max}, K\} \cdot K T})$ for the Wtd-Top-m objective, which is optimal up to log factors and removes the asymptotic dependence on $\theta_{\max}$ when $\theta_{\max} > K$. Empirically, on the Arith50 and Bad50 datasets (K=50, m=5), AOA-RB_PL-Adaptive significantly outperforms both AOA-RB_PL (no-choice pivot) and MNL-UCB (Agrawal et al., 2019) when the No-Choice parameter is weak ($\theta_0/\theta_{\max} = 0.01$), with the performance gap widening as $\theta_0/\theta_{\max}$ decreases to 0.001. MNL-UCB degrades to linear $O(T)$ regret in the regime where $\theta_{\max} \gg 1$, while the proposed methods maintain sublinear regret.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is MNL-UCB from Agrawal et al. (2019), the state-of-the-art algorithm for MNL assortment selection. On both Arith50 and Bad50 datasets with $\theta_0/\theta_{\max} = 0.01$ (weak No-Choice), AOA-RB_PL-Adaptive substantially outperforms MNL-UCB in cumulative regret for both Top-m and Wtd-Top-m objectives, while AOA-RB_PL (no-choice pivot) also beats MNL-UCB but by a smaller margin. The advantage of the adaptive pivot over the fixed no-choice pivot grows monotonically as $\theta_0/\theta_{\max}$ decreases from 1 to 0.001, confirming the theoretical prediction that the adaptive version removes the $\theta_{\max}$ dependence. In the extreme regime where $\theta_{\max} \gg 1$, MNL-UCB's epoch-based design (repeating the same assortment until NC is selected) leads to epochs of length $O(K\theta_{\max})$, effectively reducing the number of learning rounds and yielding near-linear regret, whereas both proposed algorithms maintain $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{KT})$-type sublinear regret without requiring repeated plays of the same assortment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MNL-Bandit framework of Agrawal et al. (2019) for dynamic assortment selection and the Rank-Breaking technique of Khetan & Oh (2016) for extracting pairwise comparisons from choice data, combined with the pairwise concentration results from Saha & Gopalan (2018). It departs from prior work by eliminating two key structural assumptions: (1) the requirement that the No-Choice item be the strongest (θ₀ ≥ max θᵢ), and (2) the need to repeatedly play the same assortment until the NC item is selected. The adaptive pivot mechanism in Section 4 further refines the base algorithm by routing parameter estimates through the most frequently selected item rather than always through item 0, removing the asymptotic θ_max dependence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The removal of the strong-reference and repetitive-play assumptions makes MNL assortment algorithms deployable in real systems (recommender systems, ad placement, RLHF) where users must make a choice and seeing the same items repeatedly causes disengagement. The adaptive pivot technique is a clean, generalizable idea that could transfer to other bandit settings with imbalanced feedback. Open questions include whether the No-Choice item can be eliminated entirely, whether spectral ranking methods (Agarwal et al., 2018) can replace rank-breaking without the (θ_max/θ₀)² dependence, and whether the results extend to linear MNL scores with improved regret rates.
