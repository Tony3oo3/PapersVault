## Problem

Generating effective teasers for long documentaries requires long-range multimodal modeling across video, speech, music, and narration while maintaining audiovisual alignment, managing scene transitions, and preserving factual accuracy. Progress has been hindered by the absence of a publicly available dataset with paired documentary-teaser examples, as prior trailer generation work relied on private or unpaired datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252722/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a long documentary (average 31.3 minutes) with multimodal streams (video at 1 fps, separated audio tracks for dialogue/music/SFX, and Whisper-transcribed narration with sentence-level timestamps) and produces a teaser of approximately 79 seconds (4.15% compression rate). The output consists of a GPT-4o-generated story-like narration synthesized into audio via TTS, paired with video clips selected from the body content whose total duration matches the synthesized speech duration for each sentence.

## Method

TeaserGen is a two-stage, narration-centered system. Stage 1 prompts GPT-4o to divide the transcribed narration into 10 segments, summarize each to one sentence, then rewrite them into a cohesive story-like narration ending with a thought-provoking question. Stage 2 selects accompanying video clips via two approaches: TeaserGen-PT frames selection as a constrained optimization problem, using the VTGHLS score from a pretrained UniVTG model and binary search to find a threshold that yields clips matching the synthesized speech duration (with ≥3-second minimum clip length and 1-second overlap tolerance); TeaserGen-LR trains a 3-layer transformer to map sentence embeddings to CLIP image embeddings, augmented with a diffusion prior for output diversity, and decodes via beam search with a regularization term (λ-weighted penalty on cross-sentence frame similarity) plus a smoothing mechanism that replaces repeated frames with consecutive nearby frames.

## Results

On the DocumentaryNet test set (49 documentaries), TeaserGen-PT with title query achieves the best overall balance: F1=1.85%, REP=0%, SCR=13.16%, and VTGHLS=1.02, closely matching the ground truth SCR of 27.6% while far outperforming baselines on scene change rate. TeaserGen-LR with beam search and diffusion prior achieves the highest F1 (1.88%) and CLIPScore (0.63) among proposed methods but at the cost of a higher SCR (46.56%). Subjective evaluation (20 participants) confirms TeaserGen-PT (Title) leads in coherence (3.14/5), alignment (2.84/5), engagingness (2.81/5), and realness (2.94/5), surpassing all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TeaserGen-PT (Title) marginally outperforms UniVTG [24] on F1 (1.85% vs. 1.82%) but dramatically reduces scene change rate from 89.68% to 13.16%, indicating far smoother clip transitions closer to the ground truth (27.6%). Against CLIP-it [27], TeaserGen-PT (Title) improves F1 from 1.24% to 1.85% and cuts SCR from 99.39% to 13.16%, showing that the thresholding mechanism over a pretrained score curve is far more effective than CLIP-it's per-sub-clip ranking. Compared to the video summarization baselines CSTA [35] (F1=1.78%, SCR=5.00%) and A2Summ [11] (F1=0.77%, SCR=95.23%), TeaserGen-PT achieves higher F1 while maintaining a more natural scene change rate; A2Summ in particular suffers from extreme fragmentation. In subjective evaluation, TeaserGen-PT (Title) scores 3.14/5 on coherence versus 2.61/5 for both UniVTG and CLIP-it, confirming that the baselines' high SCR translates to poor perceived quality. The paper's key finding is that the pretraining-based approach (TeaserGen-PT) is more effective overall than the directly trained sequential model (TeaserGen-LR), despite TeaserGen-LR achieving slightly higher F1 and CLIPScore, because TeaserGen-LR's higher SCR (46.56%) leads to lower subjective coherence and engagingness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of movie trailer generation and multimodal summarization, but departs from both in key ways. Prior trailer generation work (MovieNet, TGT, CCANet) relied on private datasets and focused on visual-only selection without generating narration, while multimodal summarization methods (A2Summ, VideoXum, MM-AVS) handle shorter inputs and lower compression rates. TeaserGen builds on UniVTG's VTGHLS score as its core matching function and on the diffusion prior for cross-modal embedding mapping, but introduces a narration-centered two-stage formulation (LLM-generated narration → visual selection) that is specific to the documentary domain where narration carries primary informational weight.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DocumentaryNet is the first publicly available dataset with paired documentary-teaser data including music and sound effects, removing a key barrier that has hindered reproducibility in trailer generation research. The finding that a pretraining-based thresholding approach outperforms a directly trained sequential model is practically useful but tempered by the very low absolute F1 scores (1–2%), indicating the task remains far from solved. Open questions include whether the narration-centered assumption generalizes to visual-centric media, how to handle interview scenes where names must be matched to faces, and whether end-to-end generation of accompanying music and sound effects is feasible.
