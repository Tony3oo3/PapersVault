## Problem

In real-time RL, parallel layer computation (pipelining) increases throughput but introduces observational delay: an N-layer network's action at time t is based on an observation from t−Nδ steps ago. Reducing layers mitigates delay but sacrifices expressivity, and prior work on parallel networks with temporal skip connections had not been applied to RL, where a single bad action can critically impact the entire trajectory. The paper asks how to address this delay while preserving network capacity.

## Input / Output

The policy receives a temporal history of states (s_{t−⌈Nδ⌉}, …, s_{t−⌈δ⌉}) plus recent actions, where each state is a vector (Mujoco) or a small grayscale image (MinAtar, MiniGrid). A key architectural constraint is that state s_{t−k} may pass through at most ⌊k/δ⌋ layers before contributing to the action, so older states get fewer layers of processing. The output is a single action (continuous for Mujoco via SAC, discrete for MinAtar/MiniGrid via PPO).

## Method

The core contribution is temporal skip connections within a parallel computation framework: in a pipelined N-layer network, each layer processes data from a different time step, and skip connections allow activations to shortcut along time (not just depth), so the action at time t conditions on multiple states {s_{t−Nδ}, …, s_{t−δ}} rather than only s_{t−Nδ}. The paper proves this tightens the delay regret bound from Ω(t(1−p^{⌈Nδ⌉})) to Ω(t(1−p^{⌈δ⌉})) (Proposition 1) and, when combined with augmenting the state with recent actions, restores the Markovian property of the decision process (Proposition 2), stabilizing training. Training uses SAC for continuous-action Mujoco and PPO for discrete-action MinAtar/MiniGrid, with the critic trained online without delay and the actor trained by unrolling on sub-trajectories from the replay buffer under the parallel computation constraint. The best-performing skip-connection variant (proj-from-obs for Mujoco, proj-to-action with residual for MinAtar/MiniGrid) is selected via ablation, and the architecture achieves 6–350% inference speed-up on GPU while matching or exceeding non-delayed baselines in most environments.

## Results

On Mujoco (4 environments, neuron execution time δ=1), the proposed three-layer MLP with temporal skip connections and history augmentation (proj-from-obs & aug) achieves returns of 8037±201 (HalfCheetah), 3561±682 (Walker2d), 2976±972 (Ant), and 3499±30 (Hopper), approaching the no-delay SAC oracle (11739, 4415, 3595, 2672). On MinAtar (δ=1), the CNN with skip connections and augmentation outperforms the vanilla parallel CNN by 2.5–3.5× (e.g., Breakout: 16.96 vs. 6.67; SpaceInvaders: 73.58 vs. 41.95). On MiniGrid (δ=1), the skip-connection agent reaches 0.932 (Empty) and 0.922 (DoorKey) versus 0.894 and 0.859 for the augmented CNN without skips. Parallel layer computation yields 6–350% inference speed-up on GPU (A100), with sparse matrix multiplication reaching 350% speed-up at 100 layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RLRD (Bouteiller et al., 2021), the proposed skip-connection agent with augmentation substantially outperforms on HalfCheetah (8165 vs. 3147) and Ant (4729 vs. 2924) at δ=1, while RLRD retains a slight edge on Hopper (3314 vs. 3253) and Walker2d (3714 vs. 4490 in favor of the proposed method). Against the no-delay SAC oracle (Haarnoja et al., 2018), the proposed method matches or exceeds oracle performance on Hopper (3499 vs. 2672) and Ant (4729 vs. 3595) at δ=1, but falls short on HalfCheetah (8165 vs. 11739) and Walker2d (4490 vs. 4415, essentially tied). Against the no-delay PPO oracle (Schulman et al., 2017) on MinAtar, the skip-connection CNN achieves 81% of oracle on Breakout (16.96 vs. 20.81) and 25% on SpaceInvaders (73.58 vs. 297.49), indicating a larger gap in more complex visual environments. On MiniGrid, the proposed method reaches 97% of PPO oracle (0.932 vs. 0.963 on Empty; 0.922 vs. 0.961 on DoorKey), a much tighter gap than MinAtar. Overall, the paper demonstrates that temporal skip connections + history augmentation consistently outperform both the vanilla parallel architecture and the RLRD delay-handling method, while approaching instantaneous-agent performance in most Mujoco and MiniGrid settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the parallel layer computation and temporal skip connection paradigm from video/image prediction (Carreira et al., 2018; Iuzzolino et al., 2021; Fischer et al., 2018) into reinforcement learning, where a single bad action can cascade through an entire trajectory. It departs from prior delay-in-RL work (Katsikopoulos & Engelbrecht, 2003; Firoiu et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2023; RLRD) by treating delay as an inherent architectural property of the agent's own parallel computation rather than an external environmental factor, enabling inductive biases like temporal skip connections. The theoretical analysis builds on the regret decomposition framework of Riemer et al. (2024) to derive tighter delay-regret bounds specific to skip-connected architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 6–350% inference speedup on GPUs makes real-time RL feasible on standard hardware, and the theoretical regret bounds give practitioners a principled way to choose between delay and expressivity. However, the performance gap persists in harder environments (MinAtar, HalfCheetah at larger δ), and the fixed-δ assumption, 5-layer cap, and lack of stochastic execution time handling limit immediate deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to deeper networks, how to handle variable or stochastic neuron execution times, and whether neuromorphic hardware (where parallel computation is native) can close the remaining gap.
