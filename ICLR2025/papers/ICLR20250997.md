## Problem

The first self-supervised dataset distillation method (KRR-ST) stores distilled images and their paired representations independently, wasting storage on redundancy, and completely abandons data augmentation to avoid gradient bias in bilevel optimization—despite augmentation being a critical component of self-supervised learning. This paper addresses both limitations to produce more compact distilled datasets with better cross-architecture generalization and transfer performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250997/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabeled RGB image dataset (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet/ImageNet) and a storage budget N expressed as equivalent float-stored images. The output is not a set of raw images but a compact parameterization: image bases (U vectors at reduced resolution c×h/s×w/s), image coefficients (m×U), representation bases (V vectors of dimension d_y), representation coefficients (m×V), and A lightweight 2-layer MLP approximation networks that predict representation shifts for each predefined augmentation. Distilled images are reconstructed via bilinear upsampling of C^x·B^x, and augmented-view representations are approximated as C^y·B^y + Q_a(C^y)·B^y.

## Method

The core contribution combines three techniques within a bilevel optimization framework that follows KRR-ST's KRR closed-form inner-loop solution. First, both distilled images and their self-supervised representations are parameterized as linear combinations of shared low-dimensional bases initialized via PCA on the source dataset and its teacher-model representations, exploiting the dimensional collapse property of SSL embeddings. Second, instead of skipping augmentation entirely, all augmentations are predetermined (e.g., 90°/180°/270° rotations) and their corresponding representations are explicitly stored during optimization, eliminating the randomness that causes gradient bias while preserving augmentation's benefits. Third, lightweight 2-layer MLP approximation networks learn to predict the representation shift from each unaugmented image to its augmented views, so the final stored dataset only requires the bases, coefficients, and these small networks rather than all augmented representations. Outer-loop gradients backpropagate through the differentiable upsampling and augmentation operations to jointly update bases and coefficients.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (source and target, N=100 storage budget), the proposed method achieves 52.41% linear evaluation accuracy with a 3-layer ConvNet, outperforming KRR-ST (47.00%) by 5.4 points and the best supervised baseline DATM (38.73%) by 13.7 points. Cross-architecture generalization is strong: on CIFAR-10, the method reaches 76.83% (ConvNet) and 47.06% (ViT), surpassing KRR-ST by 4.7 and 2.2 points respectively. On fine-grained targets CUB-2011 and Stanford Dogs, the method achieves 12.24% and 15.34% (ConvNet), beating KRR-ST by 1.8 and 1.9 points. The method also scales to TinyImageNet (31.25% at N=200) and ImageNet (21.17% at N=250), consistently outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all five baselines across every target dataset and feature extractor architecture. Against the closest competitor KRR-ST (Lee et al., 2024), the gains are 5.4 points on CIFAR-100 (ConvNet), 4.7 on CIFAR-10, 1.8 on CUB-2011, and 1.9 on Stanford Dogs, with the largest relative improvements on MobileNet (e.g., 24.14% vs. 10.11% on CIFAR-100, a 2.4× improvement) and VGG11 (35.35% vs. 27.78% on CIFAR-100). The four supervised DD baselines (DSA, DM, IDM, DATM) are substantially weaker, with the best supervised method DATM trailing the proposed method by 13.7 points on CIFAR-100 and 10.7 on CIFAR-10 (ConvNet), confirming that self-supervised distillation captures more transferable representations than class-discriminative supervised distillation. Notably, DM fails entirely (NaN) on ViT across all targets, while the proposed method achieves 23.33% on CIFAR-100 with ViT. The advantage is most pronounced on fine-grained and cross-domain targets (CUB-2011, Stanford Dogs) where general representation quality matters more than class-specific features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends KRR-ST (Lee et al., ICLR 2024), the first self-supervised dataset distillation framework, by addressing two key limitations: KRR-ST's complete exclusion of data augmentation (due to gradient bias in bilevel optimization) and its inefficient independent storage of distilled images and representations. The image parameterization via shared PCA bases is adapted from RTP (Deng & Russakovsky, NeurIPS 2022), while the inner-loop optimization inherits the Kernel Ridge Regression formulation from FRePo (Zhou et al., NeurIPS 2022). The novel contributions are the extension of parameterization to self-supervised representations (motivated by dimensional collapse observations), the use of predefined (non-random) augmentations to retain SSL benefits without gradient bias, and lightweight approximation networks that predict representation shifts rather than storing all augmented views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes self-supervised dataset distillation practically more useful by recovering the role of data augmentation (a core SSL component that KRR-ST had to discard) while simultaneously reducing storage through parameterization and approximation. The consistent cross-architecture generalization gains across CNNs, ViTs, and MobileNets suggest the distilled representations capture more transferable structure than prior methods. Limitations include the requirement for a pre-trained teacher model, the added complexity of maintaining bases, coefficients, and approximation networks, and the fact that only differentiable augmentations (rotation, jigsaw, crop) are supported. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-differentiable augmentations, video/multimodal settings, and whether the PCA-based initialization can be replaced by learned bases for further gains.
