## Problem

Byte-level language models like ByT5 eliminate the drawbacks of subword tokenization (sensitivity to character-level noise, inconsistent compression across languages), but they process raw byte streams that are significantly longer than tokenized sequences, making training and inference up to 10× slower. The paper asks whether an existing byte-level model can be made substantially more efficient without sacrificing its performance or its tokenization-free advantages.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw byte sequences with no tokenization step; during pre-training, spans of bytes (average length 20, 15% noise density) are replaced with sentinel tokens and the model reconstructs them. The delete gate, placed after a fixed encoder layer, outputs a per-token scalar in [k, 0] (k = −30) that acts as an attention mask during training (soft deletion, fully differentiable) and as a hard threshold during inference, physically removing tokens and shortening the sequence. Downstream tasks produce classification labels (XNLI) or generated answer spans (TyDiQA-GoldP, spelling correction, word search) from the compressed encoder representations.

## Method

MrT5 inserts a lightweight delete gate (a single linear projection + RMSNorm + rescaled sigmoid, adding only 2·d_model + 1 parameters) after a fixed early encoder layer (l = 3). The gate's per-token outputs are added to self-attention and cross-attention scores as a soft mask during training, making the deletion differentiable; at inference, tokens with gate values below k/2 are hard-deleted, reducing sequence length by up to 75%. A gate regularizer (mean of gate values) encourages more aggressive deletion, and a PI controller dynamically adjusts its weight to hit a target deletion ratio δ. The key insight is that the first few encoder layers build contextualized representations, so when the gate deletes a subset of tokens, the surviving tokens implicitly retain information about the removed ones—effectively merging context into a shorter sequence. The model also uses softmax₁ (denominator 1 + Σ exp(x_j)) instead of standard softmax to prevent a degenerate case where all tokens are simultaneously masked, and an attention-score regularizer prevents score inflation when training from scratch.

## Results

On XNLI, MrT5 (δ=0.5) achieves 65.31% accuracy, slightly exceeding ByT5 (64.72%), while reducing sequence length by 55.60% and runtime by 45.13%. On TyDiQA-GoldP, MrT5 scores 68.27 EM / 77.73 F1 (vs. ByT5's 69.90/79.58) with a 47.48% sequence reduction and 33.31% runtime decrease. On character-level tasks, MrT5 achieves 63.18% on Spelling Correction and 72.02% on Word Search (vs. ByT5's 66.73% and 76.61%), while cutting sequence length by 50.54% and 74.53% respectively. In continued pre-training, MrT5 consistently achieves lower bits-per-byte than random deletion, fixed deletion, boundary predictor, and convolutional pooling baselines at matched compression rates, and at 1.23B scale the BPB gap to ByT5 narrows further (0.74 vs. 0.70) with a 44.6% runtime reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ByT5 (Xue et al., 2022), MrT5 matches or slightly exceeds accuracy on XNLI (65.31% vs. 64.72%) and comes within ~1.6 EM / ~1.9 F1 on TyDiQA-GoldP, while delivering 33–45% runtime speedups and 47–56% sequence length reductions; on character-level tasks MrT5 trades 3.5–4.6% accuracy for 22–59% runtime savings. Against the Convolutional Pooling baseline inspired by CANINE (Clark et al., 2022), MrT5 wins decisively: 65.31% vs. 49.94% on XNLI and 68.27/77.73 vs. 54.99/65.85 on TyDiQA-GoldP, despite similar ~50% sequence reduction. Against the Boundary Predictor baseline adapted from Hourglass Transformers (Nawrot et al., 2023), MrT5 again dominates: 65.31% vs. 59.37% on XNLI and 68.27/77.73 vs. 40.59/54.04 on TyDiQA-GoldP, with the BP baseline also achieving less runtime savings (13–33% vs. 33–45%). In the diagnostic simulations (Table 2), MrT5 matches T5's 100% token-level accuracy on all three tasks while reducing sequence length by 10–20%, confirming that the delete gate learns task-appropriate merging patterns rather than indiscriminate deletion.

## Relation to Prior Work

MrT5 extends the ByT5 byte-level architecture by inserting a lightweight learned deletion gate at a fixed encoder layer, drawing conceptual inspiration from gating mechanisms in LSTMs, GRUs, and Mixture-of-Experts. It departs from prior tokenization-free models (CANINE, Charformer, Hourglass Transformers, MegaByte, BLT) that rely on fixed-span downsampling, convolutional pooling, or boundary predictors requiring substantial architectural modifications; MrT5's gate adds only 2·d_model+1 parameters and can be applied to a pre-trained ByT5 with a few thousand continued pre-training steps. The PI controller for dynamically adjusting the deletion ratio is borrowed from classical control theory, and the use of softmax₁ and attention score regularization are practical additions to prevent degenerate gating behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses the central practical barrier to adopting byte-level language models—their significantly longer sequences and slower inference—by showing that a minimal, learned compression mechanism can cut sequence lengths by 50–75% with only a small BPB penalty, making tokenization-free models more viable for production use. The finding that MrT5 learns language-specific compression rates (e.g., lower deletion for information-dense scripts like Chinese) is a step toward fairer multilingual modeling without per-language tokenizers. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to decoder-only architectures, how it behaves at much larger scales (beyond 1.23B), and whether the small but persistent BPB gap versus ByT5 closes further with more training or larger models.
