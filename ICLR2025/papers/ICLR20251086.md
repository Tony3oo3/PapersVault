## Problem

Neighbor-based test-time adaptation methods rely on prototype embeddings to align test features with training distributions, but their inherent simplicity limits their ability to learn useful patterns and handle noisy samples or conflicting optimization objectives. The paper identifies that the underlying geometric structure of these methods is the Voronoi Diagram, yet this connection has not been explicitly exploited, and more advanced geometric generalizations (Cluster-induced Voronoi Diagram, Power Diagram) remain unexplored for TTA.

## Input / Output

At test time, the method consumes unlabeled mini-batches of RGB images along with pre-computed Voronoi sites (class means computed offline from the full training set) and per-site weights for the Power Diagram. Each test image is also rotated at four angles (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) to form a cluster of sites per class. The output is a soft label (probability distribution over K classes) computed via temperature-scaled softmax over negative influence scores, which serves both as the prediction and as the signal for the entropy-based adaptation loss. Only channel-wise affine parameters in BatchNorm layers are updated; the feature extractor weights remain frozen.

## Method

TTVD progressively constructs three geometric structures to guide adaptation: (1) a standard Voronoi Diagram where the loss is the entropy of a softmax over negative distances from test features to class-mean sites; (2) a Cluster-induced Voronoi Diagram (CIVD) that replaces each single site with a cluster of four rotation-augmented sites, using an influence function F(z, C_k) = -sign(γ)·Σ_α d(μ_k^(α), z)^γ to aggregate multi-source distances, thereby unifying self-supervision and entropy minimization into a single objective and avoiding negative transfer; (3) a Cluster-induced Power Diagram (CIPD) that further assigns per-cell weights v_k, shifting partition boundaries orthogonally to create flexible decision regions. Noisy sample filtering is achieved by subtracting the Power Diagram from the Voronoi Diagram: samples falling in the difference region (near shifted boundaries) are excluded from the adaptation gradient, improving robustness against unreliable updates.

## Results

TTVD achieves the lowest classification error and ECE across all four benchmark datasets under Level-5 corruption. On CIFAR-10-C, it reaches 20.5% error and 11.8% ECE; on CIFAR-100-C, 49.1% error and 17.0% ECE; on ImageNet-C, 59.8% error and 21.0% ECE; and on ImageNet-R, 67.5% error and 16.8% ECE. The ECE reductions are particularly striking: TTVD cuts ECE by 3.4–4.3 percentage points over the best baselines, indicating substantially better calibration alongside accuracy gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTVD outperforms all 10 baselines on every dataset for both error and ECE. Against the strongest neighbor-based methods (T3A, TAST), TTVD reduces CIFAR-10-C error by ~19–20 points (20.5% vs. 39.6–40.3%), demonstrating that the geometric Voronoi framework captures far richer structure than simple prototype-distance classifiers. Among repurposed domain adaptation methods, SHOT is the closest competitor on CIFAR-10-C (21.9% vs. 20.5% error), but TTVD still leads by 1.4 points in error and 4.6 points in ECE. For self-training, TTT achieves 21.3% on CIFAR-10-C, which TTVD improves to 20.5% while also reducing ECE from 15.2% to 11.8%. Among entropy-based methods, SAR is the best on ImageNet-C (61.4% error, 38.4% ECE); TTVD improves error to 59.8% and dramatically reduces ECE to 21.0%—a 17.4-point calibration gain. On ImageNet-R, TTVD (67.5% error, 16.8% ECE) beats the best baseline NOTE (68.2%, 31.7%) by 0.7 points in error and 14.9 points in ECE, highlighting that the Power Diagram's noise-filtering mechanism is especially effective for non-photorealistic domain shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes neighbor-based test-time adaptation methods (T3A, TAST, AdaNPC) through the lens of Voronoi Diagrams, revealing that their implicit space-partitioning structure is a classical computational geometry object. It then extends this foundation in two directions: (1) replacing single-point Voronoi sites with cluster-induced Voronoi Diagrams (CIVD, Chen et al. 2013/2017; Huang et al. 2021) to aggregate multi-source influence from rotation-augmented features, and (2) incorporating Power Diagrams (Aurenhammer 1987) to shift decision boundaries for noisy-sample filtering. The self-supervised label augmentation mechanism (Lee et al., 2020) is repurposed to generate the cluster sites, unifying self-supervision and entropy minimization into a single geometric objective rather than treating them as competing losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric interpretation provides a principled explanation for why neighbor-based TTA methods work and where they fail, opening a path to systematically import advances from computational geometry (weighted diagrams, higher-order partitions) into the TTA literature. The finding that CIVD contributes the largest single improvement (5.7% over plain VD) and that the method is remarkably robust to the precision of pre-computed class means (nearly identical performance with 1% vs. 10% of ImageNet) are practically useful. However, the approach still requires access to training data class means at deployment time, limiting its applicability in truly source-free settings, and the overall error reductions over the best baselines (0.7–1.6%) are moderate. Open questions include whether the geometric framework extends to open-set recognition, class-incremental TTA, or non-CNN backbones where the feature-space geometry may differ substantially.
