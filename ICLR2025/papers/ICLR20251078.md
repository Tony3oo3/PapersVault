## Problem

Existing dataset pruning methods target image classification and require a time-consuming model training process to compute sample importance scores, making them impractical for instance segmentation. Instance segmentation introduces three additional challenges absent in classification: pixel-level annotations with multiple objects per image, large variations in instance area, and inherent class imbalance. No training-free, model-independent pruning framework existed for this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251078/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the ground-truth binary mask annotations and class labels from the dataset (no images or model outputs are needed). For each instance mask, it extracts the primary contour and computes geometric quantities (perimeter, area). The output is a per-image importance score obtained by summing class-balanced, scale-invariant instance scores, from which the top-K images are selected to form the pruned training subset. The pruning operates at the image level rather than the instance level, since image storage dominates dataset size.

## Method

The core metric is the Shape Complexity Score (SCS), defined as the perimeter-to-area ratio of each instance mask's primary contour, capturing boundary intricacy without any model. To eliminate the inherent bias toward smaller instances (since P/A scales as 1/r), the Scale-Invariant SCS (SI-SCS) normalizes by the perimeter-to-area ratio of a circle with the same area, yielding a scale-free complexity measure. To counter class imbalance, the Class-Balanced SCS (CB-SCS) divides each instance's SI-SCS by the total SI-SCS of all instances belonging to the same class across the entire dataset, ensuring rare classes contribute proportionally. The final image score is the sum of its instances' CB-SCS values, and images are ranked and pruned by this score. The entire pipeline requires only contour extraction and arithmetic on annotations, achieving a 1349× speedup over adapted model-based baselines on COCO while outperforming them in segmentation accuracy.

## Results

On COCO (Mask R-CNN, ResNet-50), TFDP achieves 53.4% AP₅₀ and 32.5% mAP at 50% pruning, outperforming the best baseline (CCS) by 1.1 AP₅₀ and 1.0 mAP, and random pruning by 2.4 AP₅₀. On VOC 2012, TFDP reaches 33.4% AP₅₀ at 50% pruning, surpassing random by 9.7 points; on Cityscapes it achieves 19.4% AP₅₀ (+2.5 over random). The sample-ranking time is 0.014 h for TFDP versus 12–21 h for model-based baselines, a 1349× speedup on COCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFDP consistently outperforms all five adapted baselines across every pruning rate on all three datasets. On COCO at 50% pruning, TFDP leads EL2N by 2.2 AP₅₀ (53.4 vs 51.2), Entropy by 1.7 AP₅₀ (53.4 vs 51.7), Forgetting by 2.2 AP₅₀, AUM by 1.7 AP₅₀, and CCS by 1.1 AP₅₀. The gap widens at higher pruning rates: on VOC at 50%, TFDP exceeds EL2N by 3.6 AP₅₀ (33.4 vs 29.8) and CCS by 2.6 AP₅₀. All model-based baselines require 12–21 hours of training plus inference to compute scores, whereas TFDP completes ranking in 0.014 h (COCO), a 1349× speedup. TFDP also generalizes across architectures (SOLO-v2, QueryInst) and backbones (ResNet-101, ResNeXt-101) without re-ranking, while model-based baselines degrade when transferred to different architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dataset pruning (coreset selection) literature, which has been studied almost exclusively for image classification, to the substantially harder task of instance segmentation. It adapts model-based importance criteria (Entropy, EL2N, AUM, Forgetting, CCS) to the pixel-level setting of segmentation, but its core departure is the Training-Free Dataset Pruning (TFDP) pipeline, which replaces model-derived scores with a purely geometric Shape Complexity Score (perimeter-to-area ratio of mask contours) augmented by scale-invariant and class-balanced normalizations. Conceptually, it repurposes the annotation geometry that is already available in instance segmentation datasets as a proxy for sample difficulty, eliminating the need for any forward pass or training run to rank samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 1349× speedup in sample selection on COCO and consistent outperformance of all model-based baselines across three datasets and multiple architectures (CNN and Transformer) make this a drop-in preprocessing step for any instance segmentation pipeline. The finding that a simple geometric metric rivals or exceeds gradient- and logit-based importance scores challenges the implicit assumption that model outputs are necessary for data selection. Open questions include whether the training-free principle generalizes to other dense prediction tasks (semantic segmentation, depth estimation), video datasets with temporal structure, and whether a theoretical justification for why boundary complexity correlates with training difficulty can be established.
