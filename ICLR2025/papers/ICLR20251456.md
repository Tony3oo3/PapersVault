## Problem

Gaussian policies are the standard tractable choice for continuous action spaces in policy optimization, but their thin tails limit exploration and can cause premature concentration on suboptimal actions. The q-exponential family offers a broader tractable class encompassing both heavy-tailed (Student's t, heavy-tailed q-Gaussian) and light-tailed (sparse q-Gaussian) policies, yet prior RL work either restricted to specific q values or used Gaussians to implicitly approximate q-exp target distributions rather than explicitly parameterizing the policy as a q-exp.

## Input / Output

The method takes a state vector and outputs a continuous action sampled from a q-exponential family distribution (Student's t, heavy-tailed q-Gaussian at q=2, or light-tailed q-Gaussian at q=0), along with the log-probability of that action. The Student's t policy additionally learns a degree-of-freedom parameter ν (initialized at 1, i.e., Cauchy) via softplus, allowing adaptive interpolation between heavy-tailed and Gaussian behavior. For unbounded-support distributions, sampled actions are clipped to the task's action bounds without modifying the density; for light-tailed q-Gaussian, out-of-support actions from the behavior policy are replaced by the nearest in-support sample in L2 distance.

## Method

The paper demonstrates how to plug q-exponential family policies into existing actor-critic algorithms (SAC, GreedyAC, TAWAC, AWAC, IQL, InAC) as drop-in replacements for the Gaussian, deriving closed-form log-likelihood gradients for each distribution. Sampling uses the Generalized Box-Müller Method for heavy-tailed q-Gaussian (1<q<3) and a sphere-based stochastic representation for light-tailed q-Gaussian (q<1). The key insight is that matching the policy parameterization to the algorithm's implicit target distribution improves consistency: TAWAC's Tsallis-KL-regularized target is itself a q-exp, so using a heavy-tailed q-Gaussian policy (rather than the Gaussian originally used) yields consistent improvements across offline D4RL benchmarks. Empirically, the Student's t policy emerges as the most stable and broadly effective replacement, outperforming the Gaussian in 75% of online settings and achieving up to ~20% relative improvement on offline MuJoCo tasks, while the heavy-tailed q-Gaussian specifically excels with TAWAC.

## Results

Across 12 online algorithm-environment combinations (SAC, GreedyAC, TAWAC × Mountain Car, Pendulum, Acrobot), the Student's t policy outperforms the Gaussian in 75% of settings and ranks best in 5/12, while the Gaussian ranks best in only 1/12. On offline D4RL MuJoCo Medium-Replay datasets, TAWAC with a heavy-tailed q-Gaussian is the top performer environment-wise, and the Student's t consistently outperforms the Squashed Gaussian across all tested algorithms (TAWAC, AWAC, IQL, InAC). Relative improvements over the Squashed Gaussian reach up to ~20%, with the heavy-tailed q-Gaussian showing the largest gains when paired with TAWAC or IQL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the Squashed Gaussian (from SAC), which is the standard policy for continuous-action offline RL. The Student's t policy consistently outperforms the Squashed Gaussian across all four offline algorithms (TAWAC, AWAC, IQL, InAC) on D4RL MuJoCo Medium-Replay, with relative improvements reaching up to ~20%. The heavy-tailed q-Gaussian (q=2) shows the largest gains specifically with TAWAC and IQL, where it is the top performer on HalfCheetah across all algorithms. The Beta policy, while competitive on Mountain Car with SAC (due to its ability to model skewed distributions), generally does not outperform the Gaussian in most other settings. In online classic control, the Gaussian is the best policy in only 1/12 settings despite being the default for all three algorithms, while the Student's t achieves the best performance in 5/12 settings, making it the most reliable drop-in replacement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the q-exponential family from statistical physics (Naudts, 2010; Matsuzoe & Ohara, 2011) into continuous-action reinforcement learning as an explicit policy parameterization. Prior RL work either restricted to the discrete q=0 case (sparsemax; Lee et al., 2018; Chow et al., 2018) or used a Gaussian to implicitly approximate a q-Gaussian target distribution (Lee et al., 2020; Zhu et al., 2024), which the authors identify as a suboptimal mismatch. The paper departs from this by directly parameterizing the policy with the full continuous q-exponential family (q ∈ ℝ), covering both heavy-tailed (Student's t, heavy-tailed q-Gaussian) and light-tailed (sparse q-Gaussian) distributions, and integrates them into multiple actor-critic algorithms (SAC, GreedyAC, TAWAC, AWAC, IQL, InAC).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that Student's t serves as a stable, drop-in replacement for the Gaussian in continuous policy optimization, consistently outperforming it across algorithms and environments with improvements up to ~20% in offline D4RL benchmarks. The finding that matching the policy parameterization to the algorithm's theoretical target (e.g., heavy-tailed q-Gaussian for TAWAC's Tsallis-regularized objective) yields further gains provides a principled guideline for policy selection. A key open limitation is the out-of-support problem for light-tailed q-Gaussians in off-policy/offline settings, where the authors' L2-projection fix proved insufficient; projecting actions precisely to the distribution boundary is identified as a promising future direction.
