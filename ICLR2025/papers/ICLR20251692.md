## Problem

Database search achieves high peptide sequencing precision but cannot identify novel, modified, or mutated peptides absent from the database, while de novo sequencing can discover such peptides but suffers from missing signal peaks and lacks additional cues to guide sequence inference. No prior method effectively combines the complementary strengths of both paradigms into a single framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251692/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an MS2 spectrum (top 150 peaks by intensity, each as an m/z–intensity pair), a precursor ion (mass and charge state), and a retrieved reference PSM (a reference spectrum and its peptide sequence) obtained via MatchMS similarity search constrained by precursor mass (±20 Da window, expanding iteratively). Output is an autoregressively generated peptide sequence over a vocabulary of 20 canonical amino acids, PTM variants, and an [EOP] token, decoded via beam search up to 100 residues.

## Method

SearchNovo integrates a retrieval-augmented mechanism into a transformer encoder-decoder: a retriever efficiently finds the most similar PSM from a database using precursor-mass filtering and MatchMS scoring, and a novel fusion layer applies two parallel multi-head attention operations—self-attention on the generated prefix and cross-attention from the prefix to the reference sequence (whose embedding is scaled by the MatchMS similarity score)—before feeding the fused representation to a causally masked decoder with cross-attention to the spectrum. To handle noisy (dissimilar) references that degrade performance, the training loss is augmented with a pseudo reference PSM consisting of [PAD] and [EOP] tokens weighted by a trade-off coefficient λ, teaching the model to generate sequences independently when the reference is uninformative. The key empirical finding is that retrieved reference spectra contain missing b/y signal peaks absent in the query and share overlapping peptide fragments with the target, providing the additional cues that de novo methods alone lack.

## Results

SearchNovo achieves peptide-level precision of 0.259 (Seven-species), 0.550 (Nine-species), and 0.447±0.013 (HC-PT), outperforming all five baselines on every dataset. At the amino acid level, it reaches precision of 0.489/0.748/0.652 and recall of 0.488/0.746/0.658 across the three datasets. For PTM identification, SearchNovo attains the best recall (0.447/0.599/0.772) and precision (0.472/0.764/0.715) on all three benchmarks. The gains are most pronounced on HC-PT, where SearchNovo nearly doubles the peptide-level precision of the next-best baseline (PointNovo, 0.419).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SearchNovo outperforms all five baselines on peptide-level precision across all three datasets. On HC-PT, the improvement is most dramatic: SearchNovo (0.447) surpasses PointNovo (0.419) by ~7% and AdaNovo/Casanovo (~0.21) by more than 2×. On Nine-species, SearchNovo (0.550) leads AdaNovo (0.505) and Casanovo (0.481) by 4.5 and 6.9 percentage points respectively. On Seven-species (low-resolution), SearchNovo (0.259) beats DeepNovo (0.204) and AdaNovo (0.174), though the absolute numbers remain low for all methods. At the amino acid level, SearchNovo achieves the best precision on Nine-species (0.748) and HC-PT (0.652), while DeepNovo marginally edges it on Seven-species (0.492 vs. 0.489). For PTM identification, SearchNovo dominates all baselines on both recall and precision across all three datasets, with the largest gap on HC-PT (0.772 recall vs. 0.740 for PointNovo).

## Relation to Prior Work

SearchNovo unifies two previously separate paradigms in peptide sequencing—database search (SEQUEST, MaxQuant, MSFragger) and de novo sequencing (DeepNovo, PointNovo, Casanovo, InstaNovo, AdaNovo)—by borrowing the retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) concept from NLP and vision. The spectrum encoder and peptide decoder follow the transformer encoder-decoder architecture established by Casanovo, while the retriever introduces a mass-constrained MatchMS-based search to fetch the most similar PSM, and the fusion layer (dual MHA with similarity-weighted reference embeddings) is a novel architectural component. The pseudo reference PSM loss (Eq. 10) is a new training strategy with no direct analogue in prior de novo sequencing work, designed to prevent over-reliance on noisy references.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for proteomics: by leveraging retrieved reference spectra that contain missing signal peaks absent in the query, SearchNovo directly addresses the long-standing missing-peak problem that limits all pure de novo methods. The pseudo reference PSM mechanism is a simple, generalizable robustness trick that could transfer to other retrieval-augmented spectral or sequence tasks. Open questions remain around performance on low-quality spectra with many noisy peaks, scalability to single-cell proteomics and metaproteomics, and whether the retrieval strategy generalizes beyond the leave-one-out evaluation protocol used here.
