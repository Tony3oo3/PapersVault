## Problem

Existing optimistic exploration methods for model-based RL (e.g., H-UCRL, H-MARL) either assume access to a ground-truth reward function or treat reward uncertainty as independent from dynamics uncertainty. The paper argues that principled optimistic exploration requires a joint belief connecting the reward and state distributions, so that optimism about reward is grounded in plausible state transitions — a capability no prior method provides.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250195/cropped_page6_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes state vectors s ∈ R^p and action vectors a ∈ R^q (e.g., 17-dim state and 6-dim action for Half-Cheetah) and produces a deterministic or stochastic policy π: S → A. Internally, the GP model outputs a joint multivariate Gaussian posterior p(s_{t+1}, r_t | s_t, a_t) over the next state and reward, from which the algorithm generates optimistic hallucinated transitions (ŝ_{k+1}, r̂_k) used to train the policy via k-step model rollouts. The key structural detail is that the next state is not sampled but computed as a conditional expectation given the sampled optimistic reward, ensuring state plausibility under the learned dynamics.

## Method

HOT-GP models the joint reward-dynamics with a Gaussian Process whose mean function is a multi-layer perceptron (4-layer, 200 units, SiLU for MuJoCo) and whose covariance uses a linear coregionalization (Kronecker-structured) to capture correlations between state dimensions and reward while remaining computationally tractable. The GP is trained via variational inference with 100 inducing points, optimizing the MLP via MSE loss and kernel hyperparameters on residual targets. The core exploration mechanism is a two-step optimistic Thompson sampling: (1) sample an optimistic reward r̂_t from the truncated posterior p(r_t | s_t, a_t, r_t > r_min), and (2) compute the expected next state ŝ_{t+1} = E[s_{t+1} | s_t, a_t, r_t = r̂_t], thereby concentrating optimism solely on reward while keeping states plausible. The threshold r_min is scheduled linearly from 0.1 to a task-dependent upper bound (0.3, 0.5, or 0.7) over training, and the policy is updated using short k-step model rollouts (typically k=1) branched from real environment states to limit compounding model error.

## Results

On Half-Cheetah, HOT-GP (r_min 0.1→0.3) reaches SAC-best performance in 250,000 environment steps versus SAC's 3,000,000 steps, a 12× reduction in sample complexity. On Sparse Reacher with action penalty ρ=0.1, HOT-GP achieves SAC-best asymptotic performance in a fraction of the environment steps compared to MBPO and optimistic MBPO. On the Medium Maze, HOT-GP (r_min 0.1→0.7) delivers the strongest performance while greedy exploitation fails to learn useful behavior. On the VMAS coverage task, HOT-GP outperforms all model-based baselines and achieves DDPG-best performance within 200,000 environment steps. Wall-clock runtimes for HOT-GP are approximately 1.5–2× longer than ensemble-based methods (e.g., 28.9h vs 24.5h on Half-Cheetah) due to GP training overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOT-GP consistently outperforms or matches all five baselines across the evaluated tasks. Against SAC (model-free), HOT-GP achieves equivalent final performance with dramatically fewer environment steps (250K vs 3M on Half-Cheetah), confirming that model-based optimistic exploration accelerates learning. Against MBPO and optimistic MBPO (ensemble-based model baselines), HOT-GP shows clear advantages on sparse-reward and action-penalty tasks (Sparse Reacher, Medium Maze), where the joint GP model over state and reward is critical; optimistic MBPO, which lacks this joint structure, learns less efficiently. Against DDPG and PPO (model-free baselines on the coverage task), HOT-GP achieves DDPG-best performance marginally ahead of DDPG and substantially outperforms PPO. Against H-UCRL (the closest optimistic baseline), HOT-GP wins decisively: H-UCRL fails to converge to optimal performance within 200,000 steps on the coverage task, likely due to infeasible optimism where perturbed states are unreachable, whereas HOT-GP conditions states on optimistic rewards from the joint distribution to ensure plausibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimistic hallucination framework of H-UCRL (Curi et al., 2020) and its multi-agent extension H-MARL (Sessa et al., 2022), which reparameterize dynamics uncertainty to enable optimistic policy optimization. The key departure is that prior optimistic exploration methods either assume a known ground-truth reward function or treat reward uncertainty as independent from dynamics uncertainty; HOT-GP is the first to maintain a joint GP posterior over both state transitions and rewards, using a coregionalization structure with a neural network mean function (Iwata & Ghahramani, 2017) to capture their correlation. The exploration mechanism adapts Thompson sampling (Osband et al., 2013) by conditioning on optimistic reward thresholds rather than sampling full model realizations, while the k-branching rollout strategy follows MBPO (Janner et al., 2019) to limit compounding model errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central empirical finding—that joint uncertainty over reward and state is essential for effective optimistic exploration, and that naively combining independent reward and dynamics uncertainties (as in optimistic MBPO) substantially degrades performance—provides a principled argument for how model-based RL should structure its uncertainty beliefs. Limitations include the cubic scaling of GPs (mitigated by inducing points but still slower than ensemble baselines), a linear schedule for the optimism threshold r_min that lacks principled justification, and evaluation on only six tasks. Open questions include extending to longer planning horizons with explicit uncertainty propagation, adapting to high-dimensional or real-world robot settings, and determining when optimism is actually beneficial versus harmful.
