## Problem

Survival analysis on histopathology whole-slide images faces two intertwined bottlenecks: training data is scarce (typically ~1,000 patients), making high-capacity networks prone to overfitting, and the standard weakly-supervised MIL framework provides only patient-level labels while requiring the model to learn fine-grained prognostic features from ~10,000 instances per WSI. No vision-language-based survival analysis framework existed for computational pathology, despite the potential of pathology VLMs to address both challenges through data efficiency and language-guided instance aggregation.

## Input / Output

The method takes gigapixel histopathology WSIs (processed into bags of 448×448 patches at 20× magnification via CONCH's image encoder, yielding ~5,000–134,000 instances per patient) and GPT-4o-generated textual descriptions of disease-specific prognostic visual features. It produces an individual incidence function—a probability distribution over C discrete time bins (C=√N_e, typically 8–13)—from which survival function, cumulative incidence, and risk are derived. Additionally, Shapley values quantify each language-encoded prognostic prior's contribution to the risk prediction, providing interpretable, language-grounded explanations.

## Method

VLSA couples three components: (1) a vision-end MIL aggregator where language-encoded prognostic priors (CONCH text encoder output + learnable fine-tuning) serve as cosine-similarity queries to selectively aggregate instances, producing multi-level WSI representations that compensate for weak supervision; (2) ordinal survival prompt learning that discretizes time into C bins, initializes B=4 base class prompts with risk descriptions (e.g., 'very poor' to 'very good'), and generates C ordinal prompts via linear interpolation to preserve the ordinality of survival classes; (3) an incidence-function prediction head (softmax over image–text cosine similarities) regularized by a squared Earth Mover's Distance loss that enforces a consecutive-decay ordinal constraint on the predicted probability distribution. The overall objective combines a maximum-likelihood loss for censored/event data with the EMD ordinal penalty, and the entire framework is trained with only 10 epochs on a single bag per step, leveraging CONCH's pretrained encoders for data efficiency.

## Results

VLSA achieves a new state-of-the-art average concordance index (CI) of 0.6954 across five TCGA cancer datasets, outperforming the runner-up Patch-GCN (0.6726) by 2.3% and the best VL baseline OrdinalCLIP (0.6604) by 3.5%. It also attains the best average MAE of 25.15 months and perfect distribution calibration (D-cal count = 5/5). In the few-shot setting (16 shots per class), VLSA reaches an average CI of 0.6592, matching or exceeding nearly all full-shot vision-only baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLSA outperforms all ten baselines on average CI, with the largest margins over MI-Zero (0.5400, +15.5%) and DeepAttnMISL (0.5736, +12.2%), and a more competitive gap over the strongest vision-only baseline Patch-GCN (0.6726, +2.3%) and the best VL baseline OrdinalCLIP (0.6604, +3.5%). On individual datasets, VLSA wins on four of five (BLCA, BRCA, LUAD, UCEC) and essentially ties on GBMLGG (0.8002 vs. Patch-GCN's 0.7999). Among VL-based methods, CoOp and OrdinalCLIP (both using simple ABMIL aggregation) are competitive with heavier vision-only architectures, suggesting the VL paradigm itself is the key driver. In the few-shot evaluation (Table 4), VLSA leads at every shot count (1–16), and at 16 shots its average CI (0.6592) already surpasses full-shot ABMIL (0.6426) and TransMIL (0.6414), demonstrating clear data-efficiency advantages over both vision-only and other VL baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the vision-language paradigm from WSI classification to survival analysis, building on CONCH (Lu et al., 2024) for pretrained pathology encoders, CoOp (Zhou et al., 2022) for learnable context prompts, and OrdinalCLIP (Li et al., 2022) for ordinal prompt interpolation. Its key methodological departure is repurposing language-encoded prognostic priors not merely as prediction targets but as queries that guide MIL instance aggregation, thereby injecting domain knowledge into the weakly-supervised representation learning stage. The ordinal inductive bias is applied in two complementary locations—survival prompts (model-level) and the predicted incidence function (subject-level)—a combination not present in prior VL or SA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a VL-based framework with language-guided MIL aggregation can match or exceed full-shot vision-only baselines using only 16 samples per class, the paper offers a practical pathway for survival analysis in settings where annotated WSI data is inherently scarce. The Shapley-value interpretation linking individual risk contributions to human-readable prognostic descriptions addresses a critical trust barrier for clinical adoption. Open questions include whether GPT-4o-generated prognostic priors generalize across institutions and staining protocols, whether the approach extends to competing-risks or multi-event settings, and whether the modest full-shot CI gains (≈2.3% over the best baseline) will hold on larger, more diverse cohorts beyond TCGA.
