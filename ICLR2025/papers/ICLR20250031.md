## Problem

Shallow Gaussian processes on Riemannian manifolds perform well on simple, stationary data but struggle with complex, irregular patterns such as low-altitude wind fields. While deep Gaussian processes have been successfully developed for Euclidean domains, extending them to manifolds is non-trivial because GP outputs must be Gaussian (and thus Euclidean), conflicting with the need for manifold-valued hidden layers. This paper asks how to construct expressive, uncertainty-quantifying deep GP models whose layers operate natively on a manifold.

## Input / Output

The model consumes points on a Riemannian manifold (primarily hyperspheres S_d) paired with observed values, and produces predictions whose type is determined by the final layer: a scalar-valued GP, a Gaussian vector field (tangential vector at each point), or a manifold-valued function. A key structural constraint is that all hidden-layer outputs must lie on the manifold itself, and vector-field outputs are guaranteed tangential by construction. Uncertainty is reported as posterior variance or covariance, which for vector fields is a matrix in the tangent space at each prediction point.

## Method

Each hidden layer f^l(x) is defined as exp_x(g^l(x)), where g^l is a zero-mean Gaussian vector field (GVF) representing a residual displacement in the tangent space, generalizing the Euclidean residual connection f(x)=x+g(x) to manifolds via the exponential map. The GVF building block can be a projected GVF, a coordinate-frame GVF, or a Hodge Matérn GVF (the latter being canonical and supporting inductive biases like divergence-free fields). Inference proceeds via doubly stochastic variational inference with sparse GVF layers, and for compact manifolds the authors introduce an interdomain inducing variable formulation where inducing functionals are eigenfunction inner products, yielding diagonal k(ζ,ζ) matrices that accelerate every layer. The architecture generalizes Salimbeni and Deisenroth's Euclidean deep GP, and the model gracefully reverts to a shallow GP when additional depth is unneeded.

## Results

On the synthetic S₂ regression benchmark, the best model (Hodge GVF + interdomain inducing variables, 3 layers) achieves the lowest NLPD across all training set sizes (N = 100–1600), with the advantage growing as data becomes more abundant. On wind velocity interpolation (ERA5, Aeolus track), 3-layer residual deep GPs reduce NLPD and MSE relative to the 1-layer shallow Hodge GVF baseline at all three altitudes (5.5 km, 2 km, 0.1 km), with the largest gains at 0.1 km where patterns are most irregular. In geometry-aware Bayesian optimisation on S₂, switching to a residual deep GP after 180 shallow-GP iterations substantially reduces log-regret on the irregular target function, while showing no difference on the smooth Ackley function. On five UCI datasets, residual deep GPs with interdomain variables are faster per training step than Euclidean deep GPs with inducing locations, but achieve worse NLPD and MSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Euclidean deep GP baseline (Salimbeni & Deisenroth 2017 architecture with a vector-valued Matérn GP front-end), residual deep GPs consistently outperform on all manifold-native tasks: the synthetic S₂ regression (lower NLPD and MSE at every N), the irregular-function Bayesian optimisation (lower log-regret in later iterations), and wind interpolation (lower NLPD and MSE at all altitudes). However, on the five UCI datasets where data is inherently Euclidean and mapped to a proxy sphere, the Euclidean deep GP baseline always achieves better NLPD and MSE than the residual deep GP with the same number of layers, though the latter is faster per step. Against the shallow geometry-aware GP of Jaquier et al. (2022), the residual deep GP produces a clear improvement on the irregular S₂ target function in Bayesian optimisation (reducing the gap to the true optimum within 1–2 iterations of switching), but shows nearly identical median regret trajectories on the smooth S₃ Ackley function, as expected. Against the state-of-the-art shallow Hodge GVF of Robert-Nicoud et al. (2024), the 3-layer residual deep GP achieves significantly lower NLPD and MSE on wind interpolation at all three altitudes, with the most pronounced improvement at 0.1 km where the shallow model's location-only posterior covariance cannot capture data-dependent uncertainty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Euclidean deep GP architecture of Salimbeni and Deisenroth (2017), which uses residual connections (f(x) = x + g(x)), to Riemannian manifolds by replacing the Euclidean addition with the exponential map and modelling the residual g as a Gaussian vector field (GVF). The GVF building blocks draw on three prior constructions: projected GVFs (Hutchinson et al., 2021), coordinate-frame GVFs, and Hodge-compositional Matérn kernels (Robert-Nicoud et al., 2024). For inference, the paper adapts doubly stochastic variational inference and introduces interdomain inducing variables tailored to the spectral structure of manifold GVFs, building on Dutordoir et al. (2020) and Lázaro-Gredilla and Figueiras-Vidal (2009). The work thus sits at the intersection of deep GPs, manifold geometry, and sparse variational inference, filling the gap between shallow geometry-aware GPs and the absence of deep counterparts on non-Euclidean domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap: shallow GPs on manifolds struggle with complex, nonstationary patterns (e.g., low-altitude wind fields), and no practical deep GP formulation existed for manifold-valued or vector-field outputs. The residual design ensures graceful degradation to shallow models when data is sparse, making the approach robust to overfitting. Open questions include whether the Euclidean-data acceleration via proxy manifolds can be made to match native Euclidean deep GPs in predictive quality, whether larger datasets will require more layers before saturation, and how the framework extends to manifolds beyond hyperspheres where Hodge GVFs lack closed-form eigenfields. The practical impact on climate modelling and robotics Bayesian optimisation is demonstrated but remains in stylised or single-dataset settings.
