## Problem

GPU-parallel RL has succeeded only on rigid-body tasks because soft-body simulation is orders of magnitude slower, making the sample complexity of model-free RL infeasible for deformable manipulation and locomotion. Existing first-order model-based RL (FO-MBRL) algorithms such as SHAC still converge to suboptimal local minima on deformable tasks due to non-smooth optimization landscapes from contacts, and no simulation platform simultaneously supports parallelization, differentiability, and interaction between articulated rigid bodies and multiple deformable material types (elastic, plasticine, fluid).

## Input / Output

For rigid-body tasks the policy receives low-dimensional state vectors (joint positions/velocities, CoM transforms, up to ~72 dims); for deformable tasks it additionally receives subsampled particle positions (~204–250 points in R^3) as point clouds, encoded by a PointNet-like DP3 encoder into a fixed-dimensional latent vector to maintain differentiability and reduce memory. Actions span joint torques (R^8–R^24) for articulated bodies, relative position/orientation commands (R^3) for kinematic control of rolling pins or fluid containers, and per-tetrahedron volumetric activations (R^222, downsampled by 2×) for FEM-based soft quadrupeds.

## Method

SAPO is a maximum entropy FO-MBRL actor-critic algorithm in which the stochastic actor is trained by backpropagating first-order analytic gradients through differentiable simulation to maximize both expected return and policy entropy over a truncated H-step horizon, with a soft value function learned via TD(λ) using entropy-augmented k-step returns. The central insight is that entropy regularization smooths the non-smooth optimization landscape induced by contact discontinuities in differentiable simulation, preventing the local-minima convergence that plagues SHAC on deformable tasks. The actor uses a squashed Normal with state-dependent variance, automatic temperature tuning, and the average of two clipped critics for return computation (minimum for targets), while the critic is updated without target networks using LayerNorm and AdamW. The companion platform Rewarped, built on NVIDIA Warp, uses gradient checkpointing via CUDA graphs to enable batched simulation gradients over multiple substeps, and supports MPM (elastoplastics via von Mises yield, fluids via fixed corotated model) and FEM (stable neo-Hookean elastic solids) with one-way rigid-body-to-particle coupling.

## Results

On the six Rewarped tasks involving rigid bodies, articulations, and deformables, SAPO achieves the highest evaluation episode return across all tasks, outperforming the strongest baseline by 25% on AntRun (4535.9 vs. 3621.0 for SHAC), 214% on HandReorient (221.7 vs. 70.5 for SAC), 115% on SoftJumper (1820.5 vs. 956.6 for APG), and 135% on HandFlip (90.0 vs. 38.2 for APG). On the DFlex rigid-body locomotion suite (Hopper, Ant, Humanoid, SNUHumanoid), SAPO also leads all baselines, e.g. 8610±40 on Ant vs. 7779±70 for SHAC and 3883±60 for PPO. SAPO consistently shows lower variance (tighter 95% CIs) than SHAC and APG, indicating more stable training across 10 random seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAPO outperforms all four baselines (PPO, SAC, APG, SHAC) on every one of the six Rewarped tasks. Against the strongest model-free baseline, SAC leads on HandReorient (70.5) but is far behind SAPO (221.7); on deformable tasks SAC either performs poorly (SoftJumper: −161.8) or only marginally beats PPO. Among the FO-MBRL baselines, APG outperforms SHAC on deformable tasks (e.g. SoftJumper 956.6 vs. 853.3; HandFlip 38.2 vs. 32.7), consistent with DaXBench findings, while SHAC outperforms APG on rigid-body tasks (AntRun 3621.0 vs. 258.7), consistent with DFlex results. SAPO surpasses both: on SoftJumper it nearly doubles APG's return (1820.5 vs. 956.6), and on HandFlip it more than doubles APG (90.0 vs. 38.2). On the DFlex rigid-body locomotion suite, SAPO's gains over SHAC are smaller but consistent (e.g. Ant: 8610 vs. 7779; SNUHumanoid: 6427 vs. 5755), while both FO-MBRL methods dramatically outperform model-free PPO and SAC on high-dimensional tasks like SNUHumanoid (6427/5755 vs. 135/846).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends first-order model-based RL (FO-MBRL) by combining the maximum entropy RL framework (as in SAC) with analytic simulation gradients, building directly on SHAC (Xu et al., 2021) and APG (Freeman et al., 2021). The key algorithmic departure is incorporating entropy-augmented returns and soft value functions into the FOBG estimator, hypothesizing that entropy regularization smooths the non-smooth optimization landscape induced by contacts in differentiable simulation. On the simulation side, Rewarped builds on NVIDIA Warp (successor to DFlex) and parallelizes MPM-based deformable simulation (from Ma et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2020) alongside CRBA rigid-body dynamics, filling a gap identified in Table 1 where no prior platform simultaneously supports parallelization, differentiability, and multiphysics interaction between articulated rigid bodies and deformables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: RL has been largely limited to rigid-body tasks because soft-body simulation is orders of magnitude slower, making sample-inefficient model-free methods infeasible. Rewarped as a parallel differentiable multiphysics platform is a concrete infrastructure contribution that could enable broader adoption of FO-MBRL for deformable manipulation. Key limitations include reliance on subsampled particle states as observations (infeasible for real-world transfer) and the absence of differentiable rendering for realistic visual inputs. Open directions include combining differentiable simulation with learned world models and extending to sim2real transfer.
