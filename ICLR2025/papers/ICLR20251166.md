## Problem

Softmax sampling is the only existing LLM decoder that is both provably stable under logit perturbations and compatible with watermarking, yet it suffers from higher perplexity than necessary. The paper asks whether a decoder can match softmax sampling's stability guarantee while achieving strictly lower perplexity, and answers affirmatively by introducing Permute-and-Flip (PF) decoding, which is provably Pareto-optimal in the stability-perplexity tradeoff and up to 2× better in expected suboptimality.

## Input / Output

The decoder consumes a prompt, API access to the LLM's raw logits function u_t: V → R, and a temperature T, and outputs a token sequence y_{1:n}. For the watermarked variant, a secret key k and a pseudo-random function F are additionally required; the watermark is seeded by the preceding m tokens (default m=8) so that detection is model-agnostic. The detection phase takes a suspect text and the key, computes a cumulative test score, and returns a binary decision calibrated to a user-specified false positive rate α via the inverse CDF of a Gamma(n−m, 1) distribution.

## Method

PF decoding randomly permutes the vocabulary, then iterates through the permuted order flipping a biased coin with probability exp((u_t(y) − u_t*)/T) for each token, selecting the first token that yields a 'head.' This is equivalent to Report-Noisy-Max with i.i.d. Exponential(1) noise added to the scaled logits (u_t(y)/T + E_t(y)), in contrast to softmax sampling's Gumbel noise. The PF watermark replaces the exponential noise with pseudo-random values r_t(y) = F_{y_{t−m:t−1},k}(y) drawn from Uniform([0,1]), so the selected token is argmax_y (u_t(y)/T − log r_t(y)). Detection sums −log(r_t(y_t)) over watermarked positions and compares against a Gamma-distribution threshold, yielding precisely controlled FPR. The key theoretical result is that PF sampling is (2/T)-stable (matching softmax), never worse than softmax in expected utility, and Pareto-optimal among all (2/T)-stable decoders.

## Results

On C4 (T=1.0, Llama-2-7B), PF decoding achieves PPL1 of 8.94 vs. 12.47 for standard softmax sampling, a ~28% perplexity reduction, while maintaining comparable diversity (Seq-rep-5: 0.03 vs. 0.02; MAUVE: 0.90 vs. 0.98). For watermarking, PF WM achieves AUC 0.995 and TPR 0.984 at 1% FPR with PPL1 of 8.33, substantially lower than Gumbel WM (PPL1 11.41) and KGW WM (PPL1 16.62) at comparable detection accuracy. On the Alpaca QA task with Llama-2-7B-Chat, PF WM reaches AUC 0.979 and TPR 0.810 with PPL1 1.69, outperforming KGW WM (TPR 0.596) while matching Gumbel WM (TPR 0.858) on perplexity. The PF watermark also generalizes to TinyLlama-1.1B, achieving AUC 0.999 and TPR 0.986 with PPL1 2.36.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PF decoding consistently outperforms standard softmax sampling on perplexity across all settings: on C4 (T=1.0), PF achieves PPL1 8.94 vs. 12.47 for sampling (~28% lower), and on C4 (T=0.8), 3.54 vs. 4.23. For watermarking, PF WM dominates KGW WM (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023) on perplexity by a wide margin (PPL1 8.33 vs. 16.62 on C4 T=1.0) while achieving comparable detection (AUC 0.995 vs. 0.989; TPR 0.984 vs. 0.991). Against Gumbel WM (Aaronson, 2023), PF WM trades a small detection gap (AUC 0.995 vs. 0.997; TPR 0.984 vs. 0.988) for a substantial perplexity advantage (PPL1 8.33 vs. 11.41, ~27% lower). On the Alpaca QA task, the gap widens: KGW WM drops to TPR 0.596 while PF WM maintains TPR 0.810, and PF WM matches Gumbel WM on perplexity (1.69 vs. 1.70 PPL1). Under paraphrasing attacks (DIPPER-1/2), PF WM and Gumbel WM show comparable robustness (e.g., AUC 0.824 vs. 0.838 under DIPPER-1), while both significantly outperform KGW with a 4-token prefix (AUC 0.661). Overall, PF WM achieves the best detectability-perplexity tradeoff, sitting on the Pareto front in Figure 3b.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Permute-and-Flip (PF) sampling, originally developed by McKenna & Sheldon (2020) as a differentially private selection mechanism, as a decoder for LLMs. It leverages the Report-Noisy-Max equivalence established by Ding et al. (2021) to design a watermarking scheme (PF watermark) that is the natural analog of Aaronson's (2023) Gumbel watermark, replacing Gumbel noise with exponential noise. The stability analysis draws on the exponential mechanism literature (McSherry & Talwar, 2007), and the watermarking design is positioned alongside the Green-Red (KGW) watermark of Kirchenbauer et al. (2023). The paper's core contribution is connecting these known DP-theoretic results to the LLM decoding context and showing that PF sampling is Pareto-optimal on the stability-perplexity tradeoff, never worse than softmax sampling and up to 2× better in worst-case suboptimality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PF decoding offers a provably superior drop-in replacement for softmax sampling: same stability guarantee, guaranteed lower perplexity, and watermarkability — making it attractive for safety-critical deployments where logit perturbations (jailbreaking, data poisoning) are a concern. The PF watermark achieves precise false-positive-rate control via a Gamma-distribution threshold, matching Gumbel watermark detectability while inheriting PF's quality advantage. Open questions include whether a PF-based Green-Red hybrid would further improve the detectability-distortion tradeoff, how robustness to paraphrasing attacks compares at scale (current results show comparable but not superior robustness to Gumbel), and whether the worst-case '2× better' bound translates to larger gains on high-entropy generation tasks. The empirical improvements, while consistent across models and datasets, are moderate rather than dramatic, suggesting the method's value lies more in its guarantees than in raw performance gains.
