## Problem

Current deep MIL models (e.g., ABMIL) rely on feature-space attention pooling and assume relatively simple, pre-separable inputs. When a complex feature extractor (e.g., ResNet18) is trained end-to-end on raw data without instance-level labels, the model suffers from 'feature drift'—instances learn irrelevant, continuously shifting representations that degrade the model to random-guessing performance. The paper identifies that this drift also corrupts the attention-based pooling stage ('selection drift'), as attention weights are updated based on unstable feature vectors rather than stable class-relevant signals.

## Input / Output

The method accepts bags of image instances (e.g., 64 raw 32×32 CIFAR images per bag, or pre-extracted patch features for WSI datasets) with only a single bag-level class label. It produces a bag-level k-class prediction and, as an intermediate byproduct, per-instance probability estimates over all k classes. For complex data, the feature extractor (ResNet18) is trained jointly with the MIL head; for simpler benchmarks, pre-extracted features are used and only the probability-space attention pooling is applied. The alignment objective additionally consumes augmented versions of each instance (via standard data augmentation) to generate self-supervised pseudo-labels.

## Method

PSMIL introduces two probability-space strategies that decouple the MIL solution from feature-space drift. First, a probability-space alignment objective (PSAli) uses data augmentation to create pseudo-labels: for each instance, the model multiplies and normalizes the predicted probabilities of its augmented copies to form an optimal one-hot target (proven in Proposition 2), then minimizes cross-entropy between augmented instances and this target, with a gradually increasing weight λ_T. Second, probability-space attention pooling (PSAtt) replaces the standard feature-space attention: instance features are first mapped to class-probability estimates P̃ via learned class prototypes Q (updated with momentum γ=0.001), and attention weights are computed from P̃ rather than raw features H. This ensures the pooling weight update direction is governed by stable probability patterns aligned with ground truth, rather than by drifting feature vectors, eliminating selection drift and enabling stable end-to-end representation learning on complex data.

## Results

On the simulated complex datasets, PSMIL achieves 88.59% instance-level accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 70.4% on CIFAR-100, while all compared deep MIL baselines (ABMIL, DEMIL, TransMIL, DTFDMIL) degrade to random-guessing level. On SVHN, PSMIL reaches 84.38%, outperforming the best baseline DTFDMIL (78.04%) by over 6%. On the large-scale medical WSI benchmarks, PSMIL achieves 0.922 ACC / 0.956 AUC / 0.921 F1 on CAMELYON16 and 0.938 ACC / 0.986 AUC on TCGA Lung Cancer, both setting new state-of-the-art. The gap to fully supervised ResNet18 is within 5% on CIFAR-10 (88.59% vs 92.14%) and CIFAR-100 (70.4% vs 71.29%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSMIL decisively outperforms all four deep MIL baselines (ABMIL, DEMIL, TransMIL, DTFDMIL) on the complex simulated datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN), where those methods degrade to random-guessing accuracy when the feature extractor is jointly trained; even with pre-extracted features, PSMIL still leads by 11–13% on CIFAR-10/100. On the small handcrafted benchmarks (MUSK1, MUSK2, FOX, TIGER, ELEPHANT), PSMIL ties or slightly trails RGMIL on FOX (0.942 vs 0.954) and TIGER (0.947 vs 0.949) but wins on MUSK1 (0.968 vs 0.968, tied), MUSK2 (0.966 vs 0.963), and ELEPHANT (0.985 vs 0.965). On the large-scale WSI datasets, PSMIL surpasses CAMIL (the prior SOTA) by 1.2% ACC on CAMELYON16 (0.922 vs 0.910) and 2.2% ACC on TCGA (0.938 vs 0.916), and also leads DTFDMIL, TransMIL, DSMIL, and ABMIL across all reported metrics. On the simpler FMNIST dataset, PSMIL (77.62%) is slightly below DTFDMIL (81.27%), confirming that the probability-space strategies are most beneficial when representation learning is non-trivial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the attention-based MIL (ABMIL) paradigm of Ilse et al. (2018) and its extensions (DSMIL, DTFDMIL, DEMIL, TransMIL, RGMIL), all of which perform both representation learning and instance pooling in feature space. The key conceptual departure is identifying 'feature drift' and 'selection drift' as fundamental failure modes when complex feature extractors are trained end-to-end, and proposing to relocate both the alignment objective and the attention pooling mechanism into probability space via class prototypes. The probability-space alignment loss is a self-training consistency objective applied to augmented instances, while the probability-space attention pooling replaces the feature-space weight vector with a probability-estimation matrix, making the update rule invariant to feature-space perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that mainstream deep MIL models degrade to random guessing when a complex backbone (ResNet18) is jointly trained on CIFAR-10/100 bags is a practical warning for practitioners attempting end-to-end MIL on complex data. The probability-space solution narrows the gap to fully supervised learning to within 5%, which is meaningful for weakly-supervised settings. However, the alignment objective requires data augmentation, which is unavailable for many real-world MIL datasets (e.g., WSI, handcrafted benchmarks), limiting immediate applicability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to sequential/structured instance relationships, whether the class-prototype momentum update is stable at scale, and whether the simulated bag construction faithfully captures the difficulty of real-world weakly-supervised representation learning.
