## Problem

Centroid-based deep clustering algorithms (DEC, IDEC, DCN) rapidly saturate in performance after early training gains, and the standard remedy of periodic reclustering with k-Means in the embedded space is insufficient to escape this plateau. The paper identifies the underlying cause: once clusters become strongly compressed and well-separated in the latent space, reclustering alone cannot alter the embedding structure, so it merely reinforces existing solutions rather than discovering new ones. This phenomenon, termed the 'reclustering barrier,' means that a bad initial representation or clustering can degrade final performance by more than 10%.

## Input / Output

The method operates on unlabeled image datasets ranging from small grayscale digit images (8×8 OPTDIGITS, 16×16 USPS, 28×28 MNIST/FMNIST/KMNIST) to color images (32×32×3 GTSRB, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100-20). It produces per-sample cluster assignments and a learned embedding of dimension equal to the number of clusters (for autoencoder setups) or 128 (for ResNet-based contrastive setups). BRB is a training-loop modification rather than an architectural change: it is inserted into existing DC algorithms without altering their loss functions, input pipelines, or output format, adding only a ~1.1% runtime overhead via subsampled reclustering (10,000 samples).

## Method

BRB (Break the Reclustering Barrier) is a plug-in modification applied every T epochs to the training loop of any centroid-based DC algorithm. It consists of two synergistic steps: (1) a soft weight reset that replaces encoder weights with a convex combination of current parameters and freshly sampled initial weights (θ̃ = αθ + (1−α)φ, with α=0.8), which structurally perturbs the embedded space by increasing intra-cluster variance; and (2) reclustering with k-Means on the perturbed embeddings to generate new clustering targets. The key insight is that neither component alone suffices — the reset must alter the embedding geometry for reclustering to find genuinely different solutions, while the reclustering provides new targets for the network to adapt to. For algorithms with learnable centroids (DEC, IDEC), momentum terms are also reset to zero after reclustering to prevent gradient misalignment. The reset is applied selectively (last ResNet block + MLP encoder, excluding the final embedding layer) to avoid excessive perturbation that would destroy learned structure.

## Results

With contrastive learning (SimCLR) and self-labeling on CIFAR-10, DCN+BRB achieves 91.23% clustering accuracy, 83.66% NMI, and 82.42% ARI, while on CIFAR-100-20 it reaches 56.92% ACC, 56.76% NMI, and 41.15% ARI. BRB improves performance in 88.10% of all runs across eight datasets with only ~1.1% runtime overhead. Without pre-training, BRB enables training from scratch, improving DEC's accuracy from 61% to 77% on OPTDIGITS and yielding up to 30% relative gains on several datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-100-20, DCN+BRB (56.92% ACC) and IDEC+BRB (55.43% ACC) both outperform the state-of-the-art SeCu (55.2% ACC), while on CIFAR-10 SeCu retains a lead (93.0% vs. 91.23% for DCN+BRB). All BRB variants substantially outperform SCAN (88.3% / 50.7% ACC) and GCC (90.1% / 52.3% ACC) on both datasets. Compared to their unmodified counterparts, BRB consistently improves DEC, IDEC, and DCN by 2–3% on CIFAR-100-20 and by up to 2.7% on CIFAR-10 (e.g., DCN 88.55% → 91.23%). On the grayscale benchmarks (MNIST, USPS, OPTDIGITS, etc.), BRB improves DCN by 2–10% relative accuracy with pre-training, and without pre-training yields gains of up to 30% (e.g., DEC on OPTDIGITS from 61% to 77%). Ablations confirm that neither weight resets alone nor reclustering alone consistently helps; the synergy between the two components is essential for breaking the reclustering barrier.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on three foundational centroid-based deep clustering algorithms—DEC, IDEC, and DCN—and identifies a fundamental limitation in their training dynamics: periodic k-Means reclustering, the standard remedy for performance plateaus, fails because it does not alter the structure of the embedded space. The proposed BRB technique repurposes soft weight resets (previously studied in supervised learning for generalization and in deep RL for plasticity loss) and combines them with reclustering to create a synergistic effect that neither component achieves alone. The approach also draws on contrastive learning (SimCLR) and self-labeling for scaling to challenging color image datasets, positioning BRB as a training-loop modification rather than an architectural change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the 'reclustering barrier' provides a diagnostic framework for understanding why centroid-based DC methods plateau, and the finding that BRB enables competitive performance from random initialization (without pre-training) removes a major practical hurdle for practitioners. The demonstration that 'older' methods like DEC and DCN can match or beat state-of-the-art approaches (e.g., SeCu) when paired with contrastive learning and BRB suggests that training dynamics, not architectural novelty, may be the key bottleneck in this area. Open questions include whether analogous barriers exist for non-centroid-based methods (density-based, agglomerative) and whether a theoretical analysis connecting BRB to perturbation-robust clustering is feasible.
