## Problem

Existing physics-based 3D dynamics generation methods rely on fixed, handcrafted constitutive models (e.g., pure elasticity or viscoelasticity), which cannot generalize to heterogeneous scenes containing multiple material types simultaneously. This limitation prevents automatic synthesis of physically plausible dynamics for diverse materials—elastic, viscoelastic, plastic, and fluid—within a unified framework without manual per-object material specification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252527/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a static 3DGS scene (reconstructed from 100 multi-view renderings) and a free-form text prompt describing the desired motion or material behavior as input. It produces a 5-second rendered video (150 frames at 30 fps) of physics-based dynamics simulated via MPM on a 25×25×25 grid with Δt = 3×10⁻⁴. The text prompt is consumed by a pretrained ModelScope video diffusion model that provides SDS gradients, while the 3DGS scene provides initial particle positions, covariances, spherical harmonics, and opacities for the simulation.

## Method

OMNIPHYSGS introduces Constitutive Gaussians, which extend each 3D Gaussian particle with a learnable physics-guided neural network consisting of a 3D feature encoder (FPS + kNN neighborhood partitioning + mini-PointNet) and a physical-aware decoder. The decoder predicts material category logits over an ensemble of 12 expert-designed constitutive model combinations (3 hyperelastic energy density functions × 4 plasticity return functions), using a hardmax with straight-through estimator to assign a specific constitutive model per neighborhood, thereby enforcing physical constraints while remaining differentiable. The MPM simulation (APIC-based) uses these learned constitutive models to compute stress and correct deformation gradients, and the entire pipeline is optimized end-to-end via Score Distillation Sampling from a pretrained text-to-video diffusion model. A two-component training strategy—grouping the long simulation sequence into mini-batches and performing multiple internal optimization iterations per stage—stabilizes gradient propagation through the first-order Markov MPM chain.

## Results

On single-object material synthesis (Table 2), OMNIPHYSGS achieves an average CLIPSIM of 20.72%, DiffSSIM of 0.0731, and DiffCLIP of 1.0440, surpassing the best baselines (PhysDreamer: 19.63%, 0.0303, 0.9840) by approximately 5.5% in text alignment and 140% in visual expressiveness. On multi-object scenes (Table 3), the method reaches 27.55% CLIPSIM and 0.0822 DiffSSIM, outperforming DreamPhysics (26.84%, 0.0409) by ~2.7% and ~101% respectively. The largest gains appear in non-elastic materials (e.g., Collapsing Ficus: DiffSSIM 0.1497 vs. 0.0014–0.0058 for baselines), confirming that fixed constitutive models fail on plasticity, viscoelasticity, and fluidity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMNIPHYSGS outperforms all three baselines across every metric and every scene in both single-object and multi-object evaluations. Against PhysDreamer (which optimizes Young's modulus with Fixed Corotated Elasticity + Identity Plasticity), the gap is most dramatic on plasticity and fluidity cases: DiffSSIM improves from 0.0014 to 0.1497 on Collapsing Ficus and from 0.0022 to 0.0203 on Water Cube. Physics3D (StVK Elasticity + dissipation) and DreamPhysics (Fixed Corotated Elasticity + Identity Plasticity with SDS) perform similarly to each other and to PhysDreamer, all struggling with materials outside their fixed constitutive model's scope. In multi-object scenes, all baselines predict homogeneous material properties for all objects regardless of the prompt, whereas OMNIPHYSGS correctly assigns distinct materials per object, yielding a consistent ~4% CLIPSIM advantage. The ablation (Table 4) further confirms that removing the expert constitutive prior (replacing with a plain MLP) drops multi-object CLIPSIM from 27.55% to 23.25%, validating the physics-guided architecture as the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PhysGaussian line of work, which first coupled the Material Point Method with 3D Gaussian Splatting for physics-based dynamics, by making the constitutive models learnable rather than manually specified. Prior learning-based methods (PhysDreamer, Physics3D, DreamPhysics) optimized only a few scalar physical parameters (Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, damping) within a single fixed constitutive model, restricting them to one material type per scene. OMNIPHYSGS departs from this by introducing Constitutive Gaussians—a physics-guided neural network that selects from an ensemble of 12 expert-designed constitutive models (hyperelastic energy density functions and plasticity return functions) per local neighborhood, enabling heterogeneous multi-material scenes. The 3D feature encoder borrows the PointBert-style FPS+kNN+mini-PointNet architecture, and the optimization follows the standard SDS paradigm from DreamFusion applied to video diffusion (ModelScope).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to automatically synthesize physically plausible dynamics for scenes containing multiple heterogeneous materials (elastic, viscoelastic, plastic, fluid) driven solely by text prompts, without manual material specification. The key practical insight is that integrating expert constitutive models as structural priors within the neural network solves the convergence instability that pure neural-network MPM simulators face under diffusion guidance. However, the per-scene SDS optimization remains time-intensive (5 iterations × 10 stages × 30 internal iterations), the material library is limited to 12 combinations, and no amortized or real-time inference is provided—open questions that limit immediate practical deployment in interactive applications like games or robotics.
