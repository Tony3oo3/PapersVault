## Problem

Existing skill composition methods for decision-making either suffer from catastrophic forgetting when acquiring new skills or avoid it via parameter isolation but fail to efficiently leverage prior knowledge for new task learning. Additionally, prior compositional approaches (action-level or noise-level) merge skills only after each policy has independently generated its output distribution, missing the opportunity to exploit shared and complementary structure across skills at an earlier stage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253070/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a state vector s from the environment and a library of pretrained LoRA skill modules (low-rank matrices of rank n, default 8, injected into frozen diffusion policy layers). It produces an action a via iterative diffusion denoising, with the key intermediate output being a context-dependent weight vector α(s;θ) ∈ ℝ^k that determines how to linearly merge the LoRA modules in parameter space. The composition network α(s;θ) is the only trainable component during the composition phase, while all LoRA modules and the base policy W_0 remain frozen.

## Method

PSEC encodes each skill primitive as a separate LoRA module ΔW_i = B_i A_i on top of a frozen pretrained diffusion policy π_0, enabling plug-and-play skill expansion with parameter isolation that inherently prevents catastrophic forgetting. The core composition mechanism operates at the parameter level: the effective weight matrix is W = W_0 + Σ α_i(s;θ) ΔW_i, where a lightweight context-aware network α(s;θ) dynamically adjusts composition weights based on the current state, allowing real-time adaptation of skill blending. This parameter-level (early-fusion) composition is shown via t-SNE analysis to preserve shared structure across skills while retaining unique features, unlike noise-level or action-level (late-fusion) composition. Each skill is modeled as a diffusion policy for expressiveness, and the context-aware module is trained with only ~1K gradient steps since all constituent policies are already pretrained, yielding substantial sample and parameter efficiency gains over training from scratch.

## Results

On the DSRL safe offline RL benchmark, PSEC achieves a MetaDrive average of 0.50 reward with 0.03 cost and a Bullet Gym average of 0.41 reward with 0.33 cost, making it the only method that simultaneously achieves competitive rewards and satisfies the safety constraint (cost < 1) across all tasks. On the DMC continual policy shift setting, PSEC scores 688 (S→W), 221 (S→R), and 247 (S+W→R) with only 10 trajectories for new tasks, substantially outperforming training from scratch (58.9, 25.5, 25.5) and demonstrating progressive skill evolution (S+W→R > S→R). In the D4RL dynamics shift setting, PSEC consistently surpasses sample-efficient offline RL methods (TSRL, DOGE) and traditional offline RL baselines (CQL, IQL, MOPO) under limited data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the DSRL benchmark, PSEC is the only method that achieves both high reward and safety (cost < 1) across all MetaDrive and Bullet Gym tasks; FISOR is the closest safe competitor but yields substantially lower rewards (0.36 vs. 0.50 on MetaDrive, 0.39 vs. 0.41 on Bullet Gym), while COptiDICE and BC achieve higher rewards but violate safety by large margins (costs of 10.26 and 3.21 on MetaDrive). CDT and CPQ fail to balance the objectives, with CDT scoring only 0.25 reward and CPQ producing negative rewards on MetaDrive. The ablation variants NSEC and ASEC, which differ from PSEC only in composition level (noise vs. action space), achieve higher raw rewards (0.53 and 0.67 on MetaDrive) but fail the safety constraint (costs 0.76 and 2.02), confirming that parameter-level composition is critical for effective multi-objective balancing. On DMC, PSEC outperforms training from scratch by 11.7× (688 vs. 58.9 on S→W) and the MLP variant by 1.6×, while NSEC and ASEC remain far behind (320.9 and 65.7). In the D4RL dynamics shift setting, PSEC surpasses the specialized sample-efficient methods TSRL and DOGE as well as standard offline RL baselines CQL, IQL, and MOPO, demonstrating that leveraging prior knowledge via LoRA composition is more effective than brute-force data combination or dedicated small-sample algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

PSEC sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) compositional policies that combine pretrained primitives (MCP, diffusion-based composition à la Janner et al. and Ajay et al.), (2) continual learning for decision-making that mitigates catastrophic forgetting, and (3) parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA, which has been widely adopted in NLP/CV but only recently explored for skill acquisition in RL (TAIL, LoRA-DT). The key departure is the choice of *where* to compose: prior compositional methods operate at the noise level (energy-field merging) or action level (distributional blending), while PSEC merges LoRA modules directly in parameter space and couples this with a learned context-aware gating network, an architectural combination not previously explored in decision-making.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PSEC offers a practical, modular recipe for building self-evolving agents: freeze a base diffusion policy, bolt on small LoRA adapters per skill, and train a lightweight context-aware network to blend them at inference. This sidesteps catastrophic forgetting by construction and reduces per-skill training cost to ~50K gradient steps versus millions for from-scratch baselines. Open questions remain around the assumption that the pretrained π₀ must cover a sufficiently diverse distribution for LoRA to be effective, the combinatorial growth of the skill library over long horizons, and whether the simple linear gating in α(s;θ) is expressive enough for highly non-stationary environments. The parameter-level > noise/action-level composition finding, supported by t-SNE structure analysis, provides a useful design principle for future skill-composition architectures.
