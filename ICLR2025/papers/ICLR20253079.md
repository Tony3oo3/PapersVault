## Problem

Existing dynamic urban scene reconstruction methods based on neural fields or Gaussian Splatting primarily model vehicles as rigid bodies, largely neglecting non-rigid actors such as pedestrians and cyclists. This limitation prevents the creation of holistic, interactive digital twins capable of simulating human behavior and human-vehicle interaction—capabilities essential for closed-loop autonomous driving evaluation. No prior method provides a unified, controllable representation that faithfully reconstructs all dynamic categories simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-camera video from three front-facing cameras (resized to 640×960), sparse LiDAR point clouds for depth supervision, and instance bounding boxes from the dataset. A dedicated multi-camera pose estimation pipeline (built on 4D-Humans) extracts per-pedestrian SMPL pose sequences with cross-camera ID matching and occlusion-aware pose completion, which serve as initialization for the human nodes. The output is a decomposed 3D Gaussian Splatting scene graph with independently controllable nodes (sky, background, rigid vehicles, SMPL humans, deformable actors) that renders novel views and depth at approximately 60 Hz, enabling per-instance editing and cross-scene asset transfer.

## Method

OmniRe constructs a dynamic Gaussian Scene Graph with heterogeneous node types: static Gaussians for the background, rigid-body-transformed Gaussians for vehicles, SMPL-driven Gaussians (tessellated on the template mesh and deformed via linear blend skinning) for pedestrians enabling joint-level control, and per-node deformation networks (with shared weights disambiguated by instance embeddings) for out-of-distribution non-rigid actors. A key design choice is using per-node deformation fields rather than a single scene-wide deformation network, which provides substantially more representational power for complex outdoor dynamics. Human pose initialization is obtained by running 4D-Humans independently on each camera, matching tracklets across views via maximum mean IoU of 2D projections, and interpolating missing poses for occluded frames. All parameters—Gaussian attributes, node poses, SMPL poses, deformation network weights, and sky texture—are jointly optimized in a single stage using L1+SSIM image loss (with 5× weighting on dynamic regions), LiDAR depth loss, opacity alignment loss, and a temporal pose smoothness regularizer.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset (8 dynamic scenes), OmniRe achieves 34.25 PSNR / 0.954 SSIM for full-image scene reconstruction and 32.57 PSNR / 0.942 SSIM for novel view synthesis, outperforming the best baseline (PVG) by +1.88 and +2.38 PSNR respectively. The gains are most pronounced for human regions: 28.15 PSNR for reconstruction (+4.09 over PVG) and 24.36 PSNR for NVS (+3.06 over PVG), while vehicle regions improve by +1.18 PSNR (reconstruction) over StreetGS. For LiDAR depth geometry, OmniRe achieves 0.242 CD and 1.894 RMSE on training frames, beating StreetGS (0.274 CD, 2.199 RMSE) by a clear margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniRe outperforms all six baselines across every metric. Against the strongest overall baseline PVG (which uses periodic vibration Gaussians without explicit object decomposition), OmniRe leads by +1.88 PSNR on full-image reconstruction and +4.09 PSNR on human regions, confirming that holistic per-object modeling is critical. Against StreetGS (the closest competitor for vehicle modeling, also using bounding boxes), OmniRe gains +1.18 PSNR on vehicle reconstruction and a massive +11.32 PSNR on human reconstruction, since StreetGS entirely ignores non-rigid actors. Against HUGS and 3DGS, which also lack non-rigid modeling, the human-region gap is even larger (+11.92 and +11.27 PSNR respectively). DeformableGS, which applies a single global deformation network, suffers severe motion blur in outdoor scenes and trails OmniRe by +5.85 PSNR on full-image reconstruction. EmerNeRF, a NeRF-based method with emergent static-dynamic decomposition, achieves reasonable full-image scores but lacks per-object controllability and falls 2.32 PSNR short on full-image reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural scene graph paradigm (Ost et al., 2021; UniSim; MARS; StreetGS; HUGS) that models dynamic driving scenes as collections of rigid-body nodes, by adding two new node types for non-rigid actors: SMPL-driven Gaussians for pedestrians (building on NeuMan, GART, HumanGaussians) and per-node deformation fields for template-less entities (extending DeformableGS's single-scene deformation to per-instance fields). The 3DGS representation (Kerbl et al., 2023) serves as the unified rendering substrate, while 4D-Humans (Goel et al., 2023) provides the initial pose estimates that are then refined jointly with the scene. The key conceptual departure from prior scene-graph methods is the explicit treatment of non-rigid dynamics as a first-class citizen rather than an afterthought, and the integration of a multi-camera, occlusion-aware human pose pipeline specifically designed for in-the-wild driving logs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniRe addresses a practical gap in AV simulation: the inability to reconstruct and simulate pedestrian and cyclist behavior in photorealistic digital twins, which is critical for testing human-vehicle interaction scenarios. The per-scene optimization approach and lack of explicit lighting modeling limit generalization to novel camera trajectories and cross-scene compositing under different illumination, respectively. Open questions include whether the SMPL + deformation-field decomposition can be replaced by a fully learned, self-supervised representation, and whether the method scales to longer sequences or denser urban environments without per-scene retraining.
