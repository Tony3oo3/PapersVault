## Problem

Existing jailbreak attacks against LLMs are designed without accounting for the rapid proliferation of defense mechanisms (e.g., SmoothLLM, RPO, DPP, SafeDecoding), rendering them ineffective against defended models. There is no systematic, transferable method to generate jailbreak prompts that remain effective under a wide variety of defenses, creating a significant gap between attack research and the current state of LLM security.

## Input / Output

The generation model takes a single malicious text query as input and produces one rephrased robust jailbreak prompt in a single forward pass (no iterative search). The output must maintain at least 70% semantic similarity (measured by all-mpnet-base-v2) to the original query. The robustness judgment model separately takes any candidate jailbreak prompt and outputs a binary label (robust or non-robust), enabling rapid filtering without running the target model under defense.

## Method

ArrAttack is a two-component framework built on instruction-tuned Llama2-7b models. First, a robustness judgment model is trained via full-parameter instruction fine-tuning on 42,730 prompts labeled using SmoothLLM's 20-perturbation protocol (ambiguous middle scores 9–13 are discarded, remaining prompts labeled 0/1); this model generalizes across unseen models and defenses. Second, a robust jailbreak prompt generation model is fine-tuned on pairs of (original malicious query, robust rephrased prompt), where the robust prompts are selected by the judgment model from outputs of a Basic Rewriting-based Jailbreak (BRJ) pipeline that uses chatgpt_paraphraser_on_T5_base for rephrasing, GPTFuzz for toxicity evaluation, and all-mpnet-base-v2 for semantic similarity. The key insight is that prompts robust against one perturbation-based defense tend to be robust against others, enabling a single judgment model to serve as a universal filter across diverse attack-defense scenarios.

## Results

Across six defense mechanisms (SmoothLLM, DPP, RPO, Paraphrase, PAT, SafeDecoding) on three target LLMs, ArrAttack achieves an average ASR of 57.69%, surpassing the best baseline AutoDAN-HGA (34.03%) by 69.52%. On plain (undefended) LLMs, ArrAttack reaches 93.87% ASR on Llama2-7b-chat, 98.46% on Vicuna-7b, and 98.97% on Guanaco-7b, while also producing the lowest perplexity (50.57–63.64) among all methods. In transferability tests, ArrAttack achieves 84.00% ASR on Vicuna-13b, 74.00% on GPT-4, and 40.00% on Claude-3, outperforming all baselines in every transfer scenario.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArrAttack decisively outperforms all three baselines under defense. Against AutoDAN-HGA (Liu et al., ICLR 2024), the closest competitor, ArrAttack achieves 57.69% average ASR versus 34.03%, a 69.52% relative improvement; the gap is most pronounced on Llama2-7b-chat (49.64% vs. 13.26%) where AutoDAN-HGA's template-based approach is most vulnerable to defenses. ReNeLLM (Ding et al., NAACL 2024) performs moderately (21.34%–38.94% avg) but is inconsistent across models, dropping to 29.16% on Guanaco-7b where ArrAttack reaches 71.76%. AmpleGCG (Liao & Sun, 2024) collapses under defenses to just 10.90% average ASR despite achieving 88–97% ASR on undefended models, because its meaningless adversarial suffixes are easily detected by perplexity-based and perturbation-based defenses. On undefended LLMs (Table 1), ArrAttack still leads with 93.87% ASR on Llama2-7b-chat (vs. 88.26% for AmpleGCG and 27.55% for AutoDAN-HGA) while producing the most fluent outputs (PPL 63.64 vs. 2553.62 for AmpleGCG). In direct transfer experiments, ArrAttack's prompts achieve 84% ASR on Vicuna-13b and 74% on GPT-4, matching or exceeding ReNeLLM and far surpassing AutoDAN-HGA (0% on Claude-3) and AmpleGCG (0% on GPT-4 and Claude-3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rewriting-based jailbreak attack lineage (SMJ, JADE, ReNeLLM) by introducing a robustness-aware training pipeline that explicitly accounts for defense mechanisms, which prior attacks largely ignore. The key conceptual departure is the robustness judgment model: a single classifier trained on SmoothLLM perturbation scores for one model (Llama2-7b-chat) that the authors claim generalizes across six different defenses and multiple target architectures. The generation model itself is a standard instruction-tuned LLM, but its training data is filtered through this judgment model, creating a two-stage pipeline (judge → generate) that no prior rewriting-based attack employs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By bridging the attack-defense gap, this work provides a more realistic evaluation framework for LLM safety: defenses that appear effective against standard attacks (e.g., AmpleGCG drops from ~97% ASR undefended to ~11% defended) are shown to be far less robust than previously reported. The transferability of the robustness judgment model across unseen defenses and models is a useful inductive bias for future red-teaming, though it remains untested on frontier-scale models or adaptive defenses. A notable limitation is that ArrAttack still struggles against DPP on Vicuna-7b (6.63% ASR), suggesting robustness is defense-specific rather than universal, and the main experiments are confined to 7B open-source models.
