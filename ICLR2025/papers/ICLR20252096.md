## Problem

Existing federated prompt learning methods for CLIP-like VLMs restrict clients to downloading only a single globally aggregated prompt from the server, a paradigm inherited from full-model federated learning. The authors argue this is ill-suited for lightweight prompts (e.g., 8,192 parameters vs. 25.6M for ResNet50), as the negligible communication cost of prompts makes inter-client sharing of multiple pre-aggregated prompts feasible and beneficial for personalization under extreme data heterogeneity.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input, encoded by CLIP's fixed image encoder (ResNet-50 or ViT-B-16 backbone), and produces class label predictions via softmax over logits. The learnable components are extremely lightweight: a prompt of shape l×d (e.g., 16×512) and a small attention-based gating network (~66K parameters after pooling to d_gating=128). The final logit for each class is a weighted sum of the similarity between the image feature and the MoE-enhanced text feature, plus λ times the similarity with the local expert's text feature.

## Method

pFedMoAP personalizes federated prompt learning through a Mixture of Experts lens: each client downloads K non-local prompt experts (selected via KNN on L2 distance from a server-maintained pool) alongside its own locally trained prompt, forming a many-expert MoE with negligible communication overhead. A novel client-specific attention-based gating network uses the image feature as query and the concatenated local + non-local text features as both key and value in a multi-head attention layer, producing enhanced text features that dynamically incorporate collective knowledge per image. The gating network is deliberately kept local (not aggregated) to maximize personalization, and a pooling layer reduces the high-dimensional CLIP features to 128 dimensions for parameter efficiency. The final classification logits combine the MoE-enhanced text feature similarity with a λ-weighted local text feature similarity, balancing collective knowledge with the only locally updatable expert.

## Results

On the five CLIP datasets under pathological non-IID (10 clients, disjoint class splits), pFedMoAP (λ=0.5) achieves 98.41% on Flowers102, 99.06% on OxfordPets, 93.39% on Food101, 97.95% on Caltech101, and 89.13% on DTD, outperforming the strongest baseline FedOTP by 0.24–2.18 percentage points across datasets. On CIFAR-10/100 with Dir(α=0.5) over 100 clients, pFedMoAP reaches 83.46% and 53.42% accuracy, surpassing the best local method CoOp (80.84% / 48.74%) by 2.6 and 4.7 points. Under combined feature and label shift (DomainNet, Office-Caltech10), pFedMoAP maintains a 1.8–5.9 point advantage over local CoOp training, while FedAvg-style PromptFL degrades below local training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

pFedMoAP (λ=0.5) outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset. Against the strongest federated prompt learning methods, it beats FedOTP (Li et al., 2024) by 2.18 pp on Flowers102 (98.41 vs 96.23), 0.24 pp on OxfordPets, 0.66 pp on Food101, 0.93 pp on Caltech101, and 1.49 pp on DTD. The margin over pFedPrompt (Guo et al., 2023a) is substantially larger: 11.95 pp on Flowers102 and 11.99 pp on DTD, where pFedPrompt drops to 77.14% versus pFedMoAP's 89.13%. Traditional FL/PFL adaptations of PromptFL (FedProx, FedPer, FedAMP, FT) all fall 15–30 pp below pFedMoAP on the CLIP datasets, confirming that standard personalization techniques are ill-suited for lightweight prompts. On CIFAR-10/100 with 100 clients and Dir(α=0.5), pFedMoAP exceeds local CoOp by 2.6 pp (CIFAR-10) and 4.7 pp (CIFAR-100), while PromptFL and PromptFL+FedProx trail CoOp, indicating that naive FedAvg aggregation is counterproductive under extreme label heterogeneity. Under combined feature and label shift (DomainNet, Office-Caltech10), pFedMoAP retains a 1.8 pp and 5.9 pp edge over local CoOp respectively, whereas PromptFL underperforms CoOp by 12.5 pp and 9.0 pp, highlighting pFedMoAP's robustness to real-world heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends federated prompt learning for CLIP-like VLMs (established by PromptFL, pFedPrompt, and FedOTP) by reframing the problem through a Mixture of Experts lens. The key departure from prior work is challenging the inherited FL restriction that clients may only download a single globally aggregated model: since prompts are three orders of magnitude smaller than full models, the paper argues this restriction is unnecessarily limiting and instead allows each client to download K pre-aggregated prompts from other clients as fixed non-local experts. The novel attention-based gating network, which generates enhanced text features via multi-head attention (image as query, expert text features as key/value) rather than computing scalar weights as in classical MoE, is positioned as a technique that extends beyond federated learning to general multi-prompt VLM adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the lightweight nature of prompts enables a qualitatively different personalization strategy (multi-expert MoE with per-image dynamic routing) that was impractical for full models, and the consistent outperformance across 9 datasets under pathological non-IID, Dirichlet, and domain-shift settings validates this approach. The robustness to differential privacy (minimal accuracy drop at ε=25 while baselines degrade substantially) is a practically important finding for privacy-sensitive deployments. Open questions include whether the KNN-based expert selection scales gracefully to hundreds or thousands of clients, whether the approach generalizes to non-CLIP VLMs or non-classification tasks (e.g., segmentation, retrieval), and whether the attention-based gating network's benefits persist with larger prompt pools or more heterogeneous expert quality.
