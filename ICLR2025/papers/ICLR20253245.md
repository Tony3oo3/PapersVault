## Problem

All-to-All communication in MoE distributed training can consume up to 80% of total training time due to frequent and voluminous token exchanges across GPUs. While prior work has exploited data locality by dynamically repositioning experts or modifying routing mechanisms, the training-sample perspective remains unexplored: tokens within the same sample exhibit routing locality, yet no method has leveraged this to reduce communication cost without altering model architecture or expert placement.

## Input / Output

NetMoE consumes the per-token expert routing matrix (I×L×K) produced by the gating network, the fixed expert-to-device assignment, and pre-profiled hardware bandwidth/latency parameters (α, β for intra-node and inter-node channels). It outputs a permutation of sample-to-device assignments that minimizes the combined All-to-All gather (current layer) and scatter (next layer) communication time. The solving process runs on CPU in a background thread and is fully overlapped with the scatter communication and expert computation, introducing zero additional latency to the training loop.

## Method

NetMoE models All-to-All cost with an α-β model split into intra-node and inter-node channels, then formulates sample placement as a combinatorial optimization problem minimizing the sum of gather and scatter times subject to per-device load balance. To make the problem tractable in real time, it dissects the optimization into two stages: a global stage that assigns samples to nodes to minimize inter-node volume, and a per-node stage that assigns samples to devices within each node to minimize intra-node volume. Each stage is transformed from an ILP into a weighted bipartite matching problem (by duplicating node/device nodes to match sample count) and solved optimally in polynomial time via the Kuhn-Munkres algorithm. Two implementation tricks ensure correctness and zero overhead: expert residual inlining (adding the residual before the gather rather than after, so relocated samples remain consistent) and CPU offloading of the KM solver, which hides the solving time behind scatter communication and expert computation.

## Results

On a 32-GPU (4-node × 8 A800) cluster, NetMoE achieves up to 1.67× end-to-end training speedup over FastMoE, 1.37× over FasterMoE, and 1.33× over SmartMoE across five MoE-GPT model sizes (S to XXL). The speedup comes primarily from reducing inter-node All-to-All communication volume by 34–43% (e.g., from 3080 MB to 1884 MB for MoE-GPT-XXL), while the KM-based solver runs in 0.08–31 ms and is fully overlapped with scatter + expert computation (3.7–41.7 ms). Actual All-to-All speedup slightly trails the theoretical optimum due to routing conflicts and hardware effects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NetMoE is compared against three baselines: FastMoE (no dynamic placement), FasterMoE (dynamic expert placement + computation overlap), and SmartMoE (dynamic expert placement + load balancing). NetMoE consistently outperforms all three across all five model sizes, with the largest gap (1.67×) over FastMoE on MoE-GPT-XXL where inter-node communication dominates. Against FasterMoE, NetMoE's advantage grows with model size (up to 1.37×) because FasterMoE's expert-parameter transfer overhead becomes prohibitive as hidden dimensions increase. Against SmartMoE, NetMoE wins by up to 1.33×; SmartMoE focuses on computational load balancing rather than communication efficiency, so its benefits diminish when All-to-All is the bottleneck. The paper also notes that NetMoE is compatible with dynamic expert placement and could be combined with FasterMoE/SmartMoE for further gains, though this integration is left for future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

NetMoE departs from the dominant line of MoE training acceleration that focuses on the model side—either dynamically re-placing experts (FasterMoE, SmartMoE, FlexMoE) or modifying routing/architecture (TA-MoE, SC-MoE)—by instead optimizing from the data side. It repurposes the well-known observation of data locality in expert routing (tokens in the same sample tend to prefer the same expert) and combines it with network locality to formulate a novel dynamic sample placement problem. The solution technique, reducing the placement problem to a weighted bipartite matching solvable by the Kuhn-Munkres algorithm in polynomial time, is a fresh application of combinatorial optimization to distributed MoE training, distinct from the ILP-based or heuristic approaches used in prior expert-placement work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since All-to-All communication can consume up to 80% of MoE training time, a method that reduces inter-node traffic by ~35–42% without altering model convergence is practically significant for anyone scaling MoE training beyond a single node. The paper notes that NetMoE is compatible with dynamic expert placement, suggesting a multiplicative speedup is possible, but leaves this integration as future work. Limitations include evaluation on only 32 A800 GPUs, GPT-family backbones, and the assumption that I is divisible by J and J by N; whether the approach generalizes to heterogeneous clusters, non-GPT architectures, or much larger expert counts (e.g., 256+ experts) remains open.
