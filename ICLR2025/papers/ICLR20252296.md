## Problem

In RAG scenarios, concatenating 5–30 retrieved passages into the LLM prompt causes inference latency (TTFT) to scale linearly with total sequence length, since autoregressive models must re-encode the entire context for every query. Although passages in external databases are frequently reused, their KV states are inherently context-dependent under standard full-attention, preventing straightforward cache reuse. The paper asks whether an attention mechanism can be designed so that only changed blocks require re-encoding, reducing TTFT to near non-RAG levels without sacrificing accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of semantically independent blocks (e.g., individual retrieved passages in RAG, in-context demonstrations in ICL, or dialogue turns) plus a final query block, along with pre-computed KV caches for any previously seen blocks. At inference, the KV states of the first k−1 blocks are retrieved from cache, their positional encodings are rotated to match their current positions in the prompt, and only the final block is computed from scratch. The output is standard autoregressive text generation, with the model able to switch between block-attention mode (multi-block inputs) and full-attention mode (single-block or zero-shot inputs) without any architectural change.

## Method

Block-attention partitions the input into independent blocks where each non-final block computes its KV states via self-attention restricted to its own tokens, while only the final block attends to all preceding blocks. This modular independence means that when a block is updated, only that block's and the final block's KV states need recomputation. Two supporting mechanisms make the approach practical: (1) position re-encoding, which rotates cached RoPE vectors to zero and then to the new target position, enabling the same passage's KV cache to be reused at different prompt offsets; and (2) block fine-tuning (~500–1000 SFT steps with a block-diagonal attention mask), which adapts the LLM to the new attention pattern and restores accuracy from 49.9% to 70.0% on RAG benchmarks. Training alternates between block-attention and full-attention masks so the resulting model can seamlessly switch between the two modes at inference time, achieving 98.7% TTFT reduction and 99.8% FLOPs reduction at 32K sequence length.

## Results

On four RAG benchmarks (2Wiki, HotpotQA, NQ, TriviaQA), Tulu3-block-ft achieves 72.2%, 72.3%, 60.4%, and 75.1% accuracy, within ≤1% of the full-attention ceiling (Tulu3-RAG: 73.2%, 74.8%, 61.5%, 75.8%). In terms of inference efficiency, at a 32K total input length with 50-token user query, Block-attention reduces TTFT from 3638 ms to 45 ms (98.7% reduction) and FLOPs-TFT from 4.9e+14 to 7.5e+11 (99.8% reduction). On general and ICL benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, MATH, BBH, DROP, HumanEval, IFEval), the block-fine-tuned model maintains accuracy comparable to or slightly above the full-attention baselines, and can seamlessly switch back to full-attention mode with no performance loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Block-attention dramatically outperforms both training-free KV-cache reuse baselines. Compared to PromptCache (Gim et al., 2024), Tulu3-block-ft achieves 72.2% vs. 32.4% on 2Wiki, 72.3% vs. 31.6% on HotpotQA, 60.4% vs. 44.4% on NQ, and 75.1% vs. 61.8% on TriviaQA — a 20–40 point absolute improvement across all four RAG benchmarks. Against Superposition prompting (Merth et al., 2024), the gap is even wider: 72.2% vs. 30.1% on 2Wiki and 75.1% vs. 58.9% on TriviaQA. The paper attributes the poor performance of both baselines to their inability to properly handle positional encoding when reusing cached KV states across different prompt contexts. Crucially, Tulu3-block-ft also matches or slightly exceeds the full-attention RAG ceiling (Tulu3-RAG) on 2Wiki (72.2 vs. 73.2) and TriviaQA (75.1 vs. 75.8), while delivering 98.7% lower TTFT and 99.8% lower FLOPs at 32K sequence length — a combination neither baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

Block-attention carves new ground at the intersection of parallel context encoding (FiD, PCW, SGLang) and KV-cache reuse for RAG (PromptCache, CacheBlend), but departs from all of these by making blocks fully independent during prefilling and introducing a position re-encoding step via RoPE rotation to enable cross-prompt cache reuse. Unlike PromptCache and CacheBlend, which the authors show suffer significant accuracy degradation or require cross-attention recovery overhead, Block-attention achieves both quality and efficiency through a simple block fine-tuning procedure (~500–1000 steps) that modifies only the attention mask. The work is concurrent with TurboRAG (Lu & Tang, 2025), which independently proposed similar independent-attention and position-reordering ideas, and is orthogonal to NSA (DeepSeek) and MoBA (Moonshot), which use block-based attention for block *selection* rather than parallel encoding and cross-prompt KV reuse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 98.7% TTFT and 99.8% FLOPs reduction at 32K context, combined with near-lossless accuracy and seamless switching between block and full-attention modes, makes Block-attention a practically deployable technique that removes the traditional accuracy-latency trade-off in RAG systems, potentially enabling developers to retrieve far more passages without incurring proportional latency costs. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger models (only an 8B model is validated), whether it extends to non-RAG scenarios like multi-turn dialogue or code generation (mentioned but not evaluated), and how it interacts with emerging sparse-attention or block-selection methods. The Game AI application in Appendix A hints at real-time LLM agent use cases where full-attention latency is prohibitive, but this remains unvalidated in the paper.
