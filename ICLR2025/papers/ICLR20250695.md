## Problem

Most cutting-edge progress in mathematical reasoning with LLMs relies on closed-source training data, preventing researchers from understanding how data synthesis choices affect performance. The previous largest open-source math dataset (OpenMathInstruct-1) suffered from limited question diversity (all questions drawn from MATH and GSM8K training sets) and underrepresented challenging problems due to the weaker open-weight models available at the time. This paper addresses both gaps by creating a substantially larger, higher-quality open-source dataset and by conducting systematic ablations to identify which design choices actually matter for SFT performance.

## Input / Output

The dataset consists of 14M question-solution pairs (~600K unique questions) where each input is a natural-language math problem and each output is a chain-of-thought solution in the proposed OpenMath CoT format, ending with the final answer enclosed in \boxed{}. The OpenMath CoT format is deliberately less verbose than Llama's native CoT style (237 vs. 331 mean tokens) while being more detailed in reasoning steps. For the 592K synthetically generated questions, ground-truth answers are determined via majority voting over 32 sampled solutions rather than having pre-existing labels.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages: Solution Augmentation (generating CoT solutions for existing MATH/GSM8K questions using Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct with few-shot OpenMath CoT examples under a base prompt template that drops Llama's special role tokens to prevent format reversion) and Question-Solution Augmentation (generating new questions via few-shot prompting on seed problems, then synthesizing solutions with majority-vote answer verification). Key ablation findings guide the design: the OpenMath CoT format outperforms Llama's by 3.9% while being 40% shorter; a strong 405B teacher outperforms an 8B student by 7.8% at matched data coverage; SFT is robust to up to 20% low-quality solutions, making extensive filtering unnecessary; and question diversity is critical, with 10.5% MATH accuracy gain from increasing unique questions from 1K to 6.5K. An LLM-based decontamination pipeline (embedding similarity search followed by Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct paraphrase detection) removes ~50K potentially contaminated questions from the 569K synthesized ones.

## Results

The OpenMath2-Llama3.1-8B model (Llama3.1-8B-Base finetuned on OpenMathInstruct-2) achieves 67.8% on MATH, 91.7% on GSM8K, 16/40 on AMC 2023, 3/30 on AIME 2024, and 22.0 on Omni-MATH in zero-shot greedy decoding. With majority voting over 256 samples, the 8B model reaches 76.1% on MATH and 94.1% on GSM8K. The 70B variant (OpenMath2-Llama3.1-70B) achieves 71.9% on MATH and 94.9% on GSM8K, reaching 79.6% on MATH with majority voting. The dataset contains 14M question-solution pairs across ~607K unique questions, roughly 8× larger than the prior largest open-source math dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, OpenMath2-Llama3.1-8B wins on every benchmark: +15.9% on MATH (67.8 vs 51.8), +7.5% on GSM8K (91.7 vs 84.2), +7/40 on AMC 2023, +1/30 on AIME 2024, and +9.3 on Omni-MATH. Against the open-source NuminaMath-7B-CoT, the 8B model leads by +16.3% on MATH (67.8 vs 55.2) and +16.3% on GSM8K (91.7 vs 75.4). At the 70B scale, OpenMath2-Llama3.1-70B outperforms Llama3.1-70B-Instruct by +4.0% on MATH (71.9 vs 67.9) but trails slightly on GSM8K (94.9 vs 95.8) and AIME 2024 (4/30 vs 6/30); it also beats NuminaMath-72B-CoT by +3.9% on MATH but loses on Omni-MATH (23.1 vs 28.4). The paper acknowledges that Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-Math-72B-Instruct remain stronger on all benchmarks, attributing the gap to Qwen's stronger base model and >1T tokens of additional math training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own OpenMathInstruct-1 (1.8M pairs, NeurIPS 2024) by scaling to 14M pairs and adding a question-augmentation stage, while replacing the weaker open-weight teacher models used previously with Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct. It departs from the dominant GPT-4o-based synthesis pipelines (NuminaMath, MetaMathQA, MathInstruct) by relying entirely on open-weight models, enabling a commercially permissive license. The empirical ablation framework—systematically isolating solution format, teacher strength, low-quality data tolerance, and question diversity under matched-coverage controls—is a methodological contribution not present in prior math SFT dataset papers, which typically report a single pipeline without controlled comparisons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OpenMathInstruct-2 is the largest open-source math reasoning SFT dataset to date (8× the previous largest) with a permissive license, directly addressing the reproducibility and data-access gap that has limited the field. The finding that SFT is robust to up to 20% low-quality solutions challenges the common practice of investing heavily in data filtering and suggests practitioners can trade compute on filtering for compute on data scale. Open questions include whether the 8B-tuned design choices (solution format, data blend) transfer well to larger models (the 70B model shows only modest gains), whether performance continues to scale beyond 14M pairs, and whether the robustness-to-noise finding generalizes across model families and task domains.
