## Problem

Classical nonparametric conditional density estimators (kernel smoothing, local polynomial regression) require the conditional distribution to admit a density, cannot adapt to low-dimensional manifold structures, and suffer from the curse of dimensionality. While unconditional diffusion models have been shown to be minimax-optimal and manifold-adaptive, no comparable finite-sample guarantees existed for the conditional (distribution regression) setting, where the conditional distribution μ*_{Y|x} must vary smoothly with the covariate x. This paper asks whether conditional diffusion models can simultaneously achieve minimax-optimal convergence rates and adapt to intrinsic manifold structures in both the covariate and response variables.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. pairs (X_i, Y_i) drawn from the joint distribution μ*_{X,Y}, where X ∈ M_X ⊂ R^{D_X} and Y ∈ M_Y ⊂ R^{D_Y}; no explicit density or parametric form is required. For any query covariate value x ∈ M_X, the output is a conditional distribution estimator μ̂_{Y|x} defined implicitly as the law of a backward SDE terminal state, truncated to a bounded ball for technical reasons. Crucially, the estimator operates directly in the ambient data space R^{D_Y} (not a latent space), so it naturally handles singular distributions supported on low-dimensional submanifolds where classical density-based methods fail.

## Method

The model uses a classifier-free conditional score matching procedure: the forward process is a standard OU diffusion on the marginal Y, while the backward process is x-dependent, driven by a conditional score ∇log p_t(·|x) estimated by minimizing an empirical L2 risk over a piecewise-constant-complexity ReLU neural network class whose width and sparsity decrease geometrically over time (reflecting the smoothing effect of the forward diffusion). The key theoretical contribution is a two-part result: (i) in the classical Euclidean density regression setting, the estimator achieves the minimax-optimal rate n^{-1/(2+D_X/α_X+D_Y/α_Y)} under total variation, and (ii) in the high-dimensional manifold setting, the Wasserstein error depends only on intrinsic dimensions (d_X, d_Y) rather than ambient dimensions (D_X, D_Y), demonstrating automatic manifold adaptation. The proof combines local polynomial approximation of nonlinear manifold charts (encoders/decoders from Assumption D), empirical process localization and peeling for high-probability generalization bounds, and Girsanov's theorem to translate L2 score estimation error into distribution estimation error.

## Results

The paper establishes that conditional forward-backward diffusion models achieve a minimax-optimal convergence rate of $\tilde{O}(n^{-1/(2 + D_X/\alpha_X + D_Y/\alpha_Y)})$ under the total variation metric for classical density regression (Theorem 1). Under the 1-Wasserstein metric, the rate is $\tilde{O}(n^{-1/(2 + D_X/\alpha_X)} \vee n^{-(1+1/\alpha_Y)/(2 + D_X/\alpha_X + D_Y/\alpha_Y)})$, which recovers the classical minimax rate of nonparametric regression when specialized to mean regression. In the high-dimensional manifold setting (Theorem 2), the Wasserstein error bound depends only on the intrinsic dimensions $(d_X, d_Y)$ rather than the ambient dimensions $(D_X, D_Y)$, demonstrating manifold adaptivity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include empirical benchmark comparisons against competing methods; its contributions are purely theoretical. In the classical density regression regime with $\alpha_X \in [0,1]$, the derived total variation rate matches the minimax-optimal rate achieved by kernel-based estimators (Li et al., 2022), establishing minimax optimality of the diffusion model in this setting. For unconditional distribution estimation ($D_X = 0$), the rates recover those of GAN-based estimators (Liang, 2021) and prior diffusion model analyses (Oko et al., 2023; Tang & Yang, 2024), but the proof here demonstrates minimax optimality under both total variation and Wasserstein metrics without relying on GAN-type constructions. Compared to Fu et al. (2024), which also studies conditional diffusion models, this paper allows different smoothness levels $\alpha_Y$ and $\alpha_X$ for the response and covariate, and additionally accounts for low-dimensional manifold structures in both variables—neither of which is addressed in Fu et al. (2024). The manifold-adaptive result (Theorem 2) extends the unconditional manifold adaptation of Tang & Yang (2024) to the conditional setting, where the response manifold $\mathcal{M}_{Y|x}$ can vary with $x$.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unconditional diffusion model theory of Oko et al. (2023) and Tang & Yang (2024) to the conditional distribution estimation (distribution regression) setting, replacing the unconditional score function with a conditional score estimated via classifier-free score matching. It departs from the classical kernel-smoothing distribution regression literature (Bashtannyk & Hyndman, 2001; Li et al., 2022) by removing the requirement for a Lebesgue density, thereby accommodating singular distributions supported on low-dimensional manifolds. The proof pipeline—neural network score approximation, empirical process generalization bounds, and Girsanov-based distribution error control—follows the unconditional case, but the conditional setting introduces additional complexity in handling the covariate-dependent manifold structure and the piecewise-constant neural network architecture over time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical analysis establishing minimax optimality and manifold adaptivity for conditional diffusion models, providing a rigorous statistical foundation for a widely used practical technique (classifier-free guidance) that previously lacked convergence guarantees. The result that diffusion models adapt to intrinsic dimensions of both X and Y without explicit low-dimensional parameterization is conceptually important, as it distinguishes diffusion models from GANs/VAEs that rely on latent spaces. Limitations include the reliance on smoothness assumptions (C^{α_Y, α_X} density, smooth manifold sections), the restriction to OU forward processes, and the small-scale simulation study; open questions include whether the results extend to other forward processes, discrete covariates, or non-smooth manifold transitions, and whether the theoretical architecture guidance translates to practical performance gains at scale.
