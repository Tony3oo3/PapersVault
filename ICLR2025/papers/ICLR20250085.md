## Problem

Learning-based image restoration methods trained on synthetic degradation pairs struggle to generalize to real-world scenarios due to the domain gap. Existing domain adaptation approaches operate in feature space (overlooking low-level pixel details critical for restoration) or pixel space (requiring unstable adversarial training with multiple networks). This paper identifies the noise space as a new, compact, and stable domain for adaptation that preserves low-level appearance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250085/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes degraded RGB images (128×128 patches) from both a synthetic domain (paired with clean ground truth) and a real-world domain (unpaired, no ground truth) as input, and produces a single restored clean image as output. During training, the restoration network processes both synthetic and real degraded inputs, and a diffusion model is jointly trained using the restored outputs as conditions. At inference, only the restoration network is used (the diffusion model is discarded), so the pipeline is a single forward pass from degraded image to restored image with no extra computational cost.

## Method

The core insight is that a diffusion model's noise prediction error is highly sensitive to the quality of its conditional inputs: well-restored conditions yield low diffusion loss, while under-restored ones yield high loss. The method jointly trains a restoration U-Net and a conditional diffusion model (DDPM-style), where the diffusion model is conditioned on channel-concatenated restored synthetic and real images and trained to denoise a noisy version of the synthetic ground truth. The back-propagated diffusion loss drives the restoration network to align both synthetic and real outputs toward the clean image distribution. To prevent the diffusion model from exploiting shortcuts (e.g., recognizing conditions by channel index or pixel similarity to the paired GT), the authors introduce a channel-shuffling layer that randomly permutes condition channels each iteration, and a residual-swapping contrastive loss that penalizes the model for distinguishing swapped residual maps. The diffusion loss is gradually ramped up via a sigmoid schedule (λ from 0 to 0.2), and the final diffusion loss combines the standard DDPM objective with the contrastive term.

## Results

On the SIDD denoising benchmark, the proposed method achieves 34.71 dB PSNR, 0.9202 SSIM, and 0.0903 LPIPS, surpassing the best domain adaptation baseline (CyCADA) by +3.90 dB PSNR and +0.1135 SSIM. On SPA deraining, it reaches 34.39 dB PSNR / 0.9571 SSIM / 0.0462 LPIPS, and on RealBlur-J deblurring, 26.46 dB PSNR / 0.8048 SSIM / 0.1363 LPIPS. The method also generalizes to unseen real-world datasets (DND, Real-Internet) without any additional training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed noise-space DA method outperforms all 12 baselines across all three restoration tasks. Among feature-space DA methods, DANN (30.09 dB) and CyCADA (30.81 dB) are the strongest on SIDD denoising but still trail the proposed method by 3.9–4.6 dB PSNR; DSN (28.40 dB) and PixelDA (29.24 dB) perform even worse. On SPA deraining, the supervised SOTA Restormer (34.17 dB) is the closest competitor, with the proposed method edging ahead by +0.22 dB PSNR and +0.0079 SSIM, while unsupervised NLCL (20.68 dB) lags far behind. On RealBlur-J deblurring, the gap is smaller: the proposed method (26.46 dB) beats CyCADA (26.36 dB) by +0.10 dB and SelfDeblur (23.23 dB) by +3.23 dB. In the scalability comparison (Table 5), self-supervised methods C2N (35.35 dB) and AP-BSN (34.90 dB) slightly outperform the base U-Net variant (34.71 dB) on SIDD, but the deeper Ours* variant (35.52 dB) surpasses both, confirming that the adaptation strategy scales with network capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new 'noise-space' axis for domain adaptation in image restoration, positioned between the well-studied feature-space (DANN, DSN) and pixel-space (PixelDA, CyCADA) paradigms. It repurposes the DDPM denoising objective not as a generative tool but as a training-time proxy loss whose gradient guides the restoration network toward a clean target distribution, a conceptual departure from prior work that uses diffusion models at inference. The channel-shuffling layer and residual-swapping contrastive learning are novel anti-shortcut mechanisms that have no direct analogue in prior domain adaptation literature, addressing a failure mode specific to joint training of a restoration network with a conditional denoiser.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is strong: the diffusion model is discarded after training, so inference cost is identical to the base restoration network, and the method is plug-and-play across architectures (U-Net, DnCNN, Restormer, SwinIR) and tasks. However, the paper itself acknowledges a clear limitation — the diffusion loss is most effective for high-frequency degradations (denoising, deraining) and offers only marginal gains on low-frequency artifacts (deblurring, +0.19 dB), leaving open whether the approach can be adapted to smooth, large-scale distortions. A broader open question is whether the noise-space formulation generalizes to video restoration or to degradations with no known synthetic counterpart at all.
