## Problem

Prior event-guided imaging research treats events as auxiliary information for post-processing already-generated RGB frames, overlooking the fundamental challenges that hybrid vision sensors (HVS) introduce to the image signal processor (ISP) pipeline itself. No dataset with pixel-level aligned RAW and event data from an HVS existed, and no systematic benchmark evaluated how events could reform ISP operations such as demosaicing, denoising, and color correction on the noisier, defect-prone RAW output of these sensors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250595/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes APS RAW images at 2248×3264 resolution following a quad-Bayer pattern (where events occupy the lower-right corner of each 2×2 color pixel block) and pixel-aligned event streams at 1124×1632, both captured simultaneously by the ALPIX-Eiger HVS sensor. A 24-patch ColorChecker annotation (position tracked via LabelMe) serves as a calibration prior for the controllable ISP pipeline that generates ground-truth RGB references. The dataset spans 3373 frames across 24 scenes, 3 exposure modes (1–100 ms), 3 lenses (8–52 mm), and apertures F1.0–F6.0, split 3/4 train and 1/4 test.

## Method

The paper contributes a controllable MATLAB-based ISP pipeline (black-level and fixed-pattern-noise subtraction, gradient-based demosaicing, ColorChecker-driven manual white balance, BM3D spatial denoising at σ=50, CIEDE00-optimized 3×3 color correction matrix, and sRGB gamma) that produces high-quality RGB ground truth with measured CIEDE00 mean error of 5.84. For the benchmark, existing learnable ISP methods are categorized into full-pipeline (PyNet, PyNetCA, InvertISP, MV-ISPNet), stage-wise (CameraNet, AWNet), and image-enhancement-network-based (UNet, Swin-Transformer) groups, all retrained under identical conditions (A40 GPU, 1024×1024 patches, 50 epochs, lr=1e-4). The proposed EV-UNet fuses events encoded as voxel-grid features into the UNet encoder, demonstrating that event fusion improves outdoor PSNR from 28.17 to 30.11 but degrades indoor performance due to flickering artificial lighting that corrupts event statistics.

## Results

On the HVS-ISP Dataset outdoor scenes, PyNet achieves the best average PSNR of 32.47 dB (SSIM 0.9785, L1 0.0180), followed by PyNetCA (31.76 dB) and the proposed EV-UNet (30.11 dB). The event-guided EV-UNet improves over the vanilla UNet baseline by 5.6 dB in average outdoor PSNR (30.11 vs. 24.51), demonstrating the benefit of event fusion. On indoor scenes, UNet* leads with 31.76 dB PSNR, while EV-UNet drops to 26.04 dB due to flickering artificial lighting that corrupts event signals. CameraNet performs worst across all settings (11.36 dB outdoor, 13.04 dB indoor average PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed EV-UNet (event-fused UNet) outperforms the vanilla UNet by 5.6 dB PSNR outdoors (30.11 vs. 24.51) and 1.9 dB indoors (26.04 vs. 15.70 for base UNet), confirming that event data provides meaningful motion and dynamic-range information for ISP. However, EV-UNet trails the best full-pipeline method PyNet by 2.4 dB outdoors (30.11 vs. 32.47) and is outperformed by UNet* indoors (26.04 vs. 31.76), where flickering LED lighting degrades event quality. Among full-pipeline methods, PyNet dominates outdoors but collapses indoors (11.97 dB), while MV-ISPNet and InvertISP remain more stable across settings (31.12 and 28.27 dB indoors respectively). Stage-wise methods CameraNet and AWNet perform poorly overall, with CameraNet achieving only ~11–13 dB PSNR in both settings, likely due to training instability when converted to PyTorch and trained end-to-end. The event-based backbone eSL-Net underperforms all other methods outdoors (23.02 dB), attributed to its limited receptive field being insufficient for global illumination estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of event cameras and image signal processing. Prior event-guided imaging work (frame interpolation, super-resolution, deblurring) treats events as auxiliary input to enhance already-processed RGB frames, while learning-based ISP methods (PyNet, CameraNet, AWNet, InvertISP) operate on RAW without considering events. The MIPI 2024 HVS demosaic challenge addressed RAW-domain challenges of hybrid vision sensors but lacked real event streams. This paper is the first to pair pixel-aligned events with HVS RAW data and to explicitly study how events can inform the ISP pipeline itself, building on the UNet architecture as a simple event-fusion baseline (EV-UNet).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first dataset and benchmark for event-guided ISP, this work establishes a foundation for a subfield that could reshape how hybrid vision sensors are processed in hardware. Key open questions include: how to exploit events' high dynamic range and temporal resolution for denoising and color correction in ISP, how to handle flickering artificial lighting that corrupts event signals indoors, and how to design event fusion that generalizes across scene types. Limitations include the small dataset (3,373 frames) from a prototype-stage sensor, the reliance on ColorChecker for ground truth generation (preventing generalization to arbitrary scenes), and the very simple event-fusion architecture that likely underperforms relative to what is achievable.
