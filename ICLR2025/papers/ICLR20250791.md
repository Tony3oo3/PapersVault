## Problem

Counting is treated as a primitive function in formal characterizations of Transformer expressivity (e.g., RASP), yet it remains unclear whether Transformers can actually learn to count inductively — generalizing to sequences longer than those seen in training. Prior work on length generalization has not distilled the failure to the simplest case of counting, and the role of positional embeddings as the mechanism enabling or blocking inductive counting has not been systematically examined across architectures.

## Input / Output

Inputs are discrete token sequences of fixed training length (50) tested at OOD lengths (100), with numbers tokenized as whole-number tokens rather than digits. The 'shifted start' variant prepends a starting number to the sequence so the output counting sequence begins at k+1 rather than 1, simultaneously exposing the full number vocabulary while bounding cardinality exposure. Outputs are per-position count values (e.g., 1, 2, 3, …), modular count values (count mod 10), or per-token selective counts (number of same-identity predecessors), with loss computed at every token position rather than only the final one.

## Method

The paper trains 1L/2L/4L Transformers (GPT-2 style) with five positional embedding schemas (NoPE, SinePE, APE, RoPE, SPE) plus shifted positional embeddings for OOD-position robustness, and compares against RNN, LSTM, S4, Mamba, and RWKV-v6 across six counting task variants (Vanilla, Helper Token, Shifted Start, Modular, Selective, Selective+Modular). The key design insight is using helper tokens and shifted starts to decouple OOD-vocabulary from OOD-cardinality, so that the model sees all number words but never encounters cardinalities beyond the training bound. Mechanistic analysis identifies two subtasks — First Token Recognition (symmetry breaking) and either Position-based Modular Subtraction or Token-based Attention — and shows that different PEs support different inductive biases: APE/SinePE enable modular subtraction via PID-modulo clustering, NoPE/SPE facilitate token-based attention by minimizing positional interference, and RoPE's recency bias hinders selective counting. Traditional RNNs and LSTMs achieve perfect inductive counting with a single layer, while modern RNNs (S4, Mamba, RWKV-v6) show degraded OOD performance due to their linear state transitions that sacrifice expressivity for parallelizable training.

## Results

On the six counting task variants, traditional RNNs and LSTMs achieve 100% IND and OOD accuracy with a single layer across all tasks, confirming that recurrence trivially affords inductive counting. In contrast, Transformers require 4 layers and specific positional embeddings to generalize: only RoPE 4L succeeds on Shifted Start counting (98.86% OOD), while SinePE and APE 4L are needed for Modular (100% OOD) and Selective counting (100% OOD). Modern RNNs (S4, Mamba, RWKV-v6) show severely degraded OOD performance, with Mamba dropping to 15.5% OOD on Helper Token and RWKV-v6 to 8.2% OOD on Modular counting, despite achieving 100% IND accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Elman RNN and LSTM both achieve 100% IND and OOD accuracy on all six counting tasks with just one layer, establishing that a recurrent formulation trivially supports inductive counting. S4 (structured state space) achieves 100% IND across all tasks but shows moderate OOD degradation, dropping to 96.3% on Shifted Start and 98.3% on Modular counting at 1 layer, though it recovers to 100% OOD at 4 layers on most tasks. Mamba (S6) exhibits the most severe OOD failure, collapsing to 15.5% on Helper Token OOD and 11.5% on Modular OOD at 1 layer, only recovering to 100% OOD at 4 layers on Helper Token and Shifted Start. RWKV-v6 (Finch) shows inconsistent OOD performance, achieving 100% on Helper Token OOD but dropping to 8.2% on Modular OOD and 29.4% on Shifted Start OOD at 1 layer. The paper attributes the modern RNN failures to their linear state transitions (matrix multiplication without nonlinearity), which sacrifice expressivity for parallelizable training, in contrast to the nonlinear state updates in traditional RNNs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on RASP's (Weiss et al., 2021) treatment of counting as a primitive Transformer operation and the formal expressivity literature (Hahn, 2020; Bhattamishra et al., 2020) that proves Transformers cannot count in a length-generalization regime. It departs from prior work by distilling the 'reasoning failure' to the simplest case of counting, carefully isolating OOD-cardinality from OOD-position and OOD-vocabulary through shifted positional embeddings and helper-token task designs, and by directly comparing Transformers against both traditional (RNN, LSTM) and modern recurrent architectures (S4, Mamba, RWKV-v6) under identical protocols. The cognitive science framing of inductive counting (Rips et al., 2006; Piantadosi et al., 2012) informs the task design, distinguishing it from prior formal-language-based counting studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that counting is not a primitive Transformer operation—requiring multiple layers, specific PE schemas, and still failing on OOD-cardinality—calls for reexamination of formal expressivity results that assume counting as a building block. The PE-specific patterns (RoPE for shifted-start, SinePE/APE for modular and selective, NoPE for selective) provide practical guidance for architecture design but also reveal that no single PE schema handles all counting variants, motivating hybrid PE approaches. The dramatic underperformance of modern RNNs (S4, Mamba, RWKV) relative to traditional RNNs on inductive counting exposes a fundamental tradeoff between parallelized training and expressive state dynamics, opening important questions for hybrid Transformer-RNN architectures. Open questions include whether deeper or wider Transformers can overcome the OOD-cardinality barrier, and whether the fragility of RoPE's generalization (failing at 4× extrapolation ratio) reflects a fundamental limitation or a training-dynamics issue.
