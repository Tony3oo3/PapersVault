## Problem

While the NP-hardness of training classical neural networks is well-established, no analogous complexity results existed for graph neural networks. The critical open question is whether training 1-dimensional GNNs is intractable, since for 1D ReLU networks the classical NN hardness results do not transfer (1D ReLU networks are polynomially trainable). This paper shows that the intractability of 1D GNN training arises from the interplay between graph structure and the aggregation/activation functions, not from the intrinsic difficulty of high-dimensional ReLU optimization.

## Input / Output

The decision problem takes a graph G with adjacency matrix, a dimensionality vector Δ (all entries equal to 1 in the main result), initial node features X (rank-0 vertices set to 1, all others 0), a set of labeled vertices Y with target labels, and an error bound t. The output is a yes/no answer to whether there exist per-layer weights and biases such that the L_p error (p ∈ [0,1)) on labeled vertices is at most t. The constructed graph is 6-regular by design, which is essential for the proof to work uniformly across SUM, MEAN, and SPECTRAL aggregation functions.

## Method

The main result reduces POSITIVE-1-IN-3-SAT to 1D ReLU-GNNT via a construction that assigns each SAT variable to a pair of consecutive layers (ranks 2i−1 and 2i) containing a Selection gadget that forces exactly two viable weight/bias configurations (encoding TRUE or FALSE). Truth values propagate along path-like subgraphs through Waiting and Preservation gadgets that prevent cross-variable interference, with the graph partitioned into 2n+1 ranks so that information advances exactly one rank per layer. At the final rank, Clause gadgets verify that each clause is satisfied by exactly one literal, while Variable gadgets and an Integrity gadget constrain the solution space. A weight-shifting lemma (Lemma 4) normalizes all but the first layer's weights to {−1,1}, and the 6-regular graph construction ensures the reduction works identically for SUM, MEAN, and SPECTRAL aggregation. The paper complements this with upper bounds: an ETR-based algorithm for general ReLU-GNNT, polynomial-time solvability for 1D edgeless GNNs, and a linear-algebraic algorithm for linearly-activated GNNs with uniform dimensionality.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1) establishes that training 1-dimensional ReLU-activated GNNs is NP-hard for any L_p error function with p ∈ [0,1) and for SUM, MEAN, and SPECTRAL aggregation, via a reduction from POSITIVE-1-IN-3-SAT. The paper complements this with an algorithmic upper bound (Theorem 5): Δ_max-dimensional ReLU-GNNT can be solved in time L^{O(1)} · 2^{O(ndΔ_max)} · (ndΔ_max)^{O(dΔ_max²)}, where L is the bit-encoding size. Two tractable special cases are identified: 1-dimensional ReLU-GNNT on edgeless graphs with t=0 is polynomial-time solvable (Theorem 8), and exact Lin-GNNT with uniform dimensionality is polynomial-time solvable (Theorem 10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical complexity paper with no empirical baselines or quantitative comparisons. The paper positions its results relative to prior complexity-theoretic work on classical neural network training: Observation 3 transfers existing NP-hardness results for ReLU-NNT (Froese et al., 2022) to GNN training on edgeless graphs via Proposition 2, showing that intractability for high-dimensional GNNs is inherited from classical NN training. The key contribution is showing that NP-hardness persists even in the 1-dimensional setting (Theorem 1), where such a transfer is impossible since 1-dimensional ReLU-NNT is polynomial-time solvable (Proposition 7). The weight-shifting technique (Lemma 4) is adapted from Brand et al. (2023), who used it for algorithmic results on classical NNs, whereas here it facilitates the hardness reduction. The algorithmic upper bound (Theorem 5) leverages Renegar's algorithm for the Existential Theory of the Reals, connecting to the broader line of work showing ReLU-NNT is ∃R-complete (Bertschinger et al., 2023).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper initiates the complexity-theoretic study of GNN training, a problem that had no prior formal treatment. It builds on the rich literature on classical neural network training complexity (Froese et al. 2022; Brand et al. 2023; Bertschinger et al. 2023) by first showing that high-dimensional GNN training inherits NP-hardness trivially via edgeless graphs (Proposition 2), then proving the genuinely new result that even 1-dimensional ReLU-GNNT is NP-hard. The weight-shifting technique (Lemma 4) is adapted from Brand et al. (2023), but its application here is inverted: where it previously aided algorithmic upper bounds for classical NNs, it facilitates the NP-hardness reduction for GNNs. The reduction from POSITIVE-1-IN-3-SAT is highly non-trivial because, unlike classical NNs where each edge carries its own weight, GNN weights and biases are shared globally across all vertices at a layer, requiring elaborate gadgets to encode variable assignments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that GNN training intractability is not merely an artifact of high dimensionality but is inherent to the interaction between graph structure and activation functions, even in the simplest 1-dimensional setting. This rules out the hope that restricting to low-dimensional GNNs would yield efficient training, and the constructed instances are planar, further narrowing tractable classes. Key open questions include whether the problem is in NP (or lies higher in the polynomial hierarchy, as classical ReLU-NNT is ∃R-complete), whether acyclic graphs admit polynomial-time training, and how the picture changes for other activation functions (Leaky ReLU, Sigmoid) or loss functions (cross-entropy, L_p for p≥2). The complementary upper bounds (polynomial-time solvability for 1D edgeless graphs and for linearly-activated uniform-dimensionality GNNs) delineate the boundary between tractable and intractable regimes.
