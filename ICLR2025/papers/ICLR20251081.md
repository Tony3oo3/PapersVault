## Problem

The dominant paradigm for 3D molecular generation relies on E(3)-equivariant architectures that bake in rotational symmetry via invariant layers. This paper proves that such models are fundamentally incapable of learning chirality: because E(3) invariance includes reflections, enantiomer pairs are assigned equal probability, which is critical since incorrect enantiomers can render drugs toxic. Furthermore, the paper shows that making point-cloud features chiral-aware (SE(3)-invariant) requires at least four atom positions per feature, scaling complexity from O(n²) to O(n⁴), rendering corrected point-cloud methods intractable.

## Input / Output

The model takes Gaussian noise as input and optionally conditions on molecular properties (polarizability α, HOMO-LUMO gap Δε) and binned atom counts via classifier-free guidance. It produces a multi-channel 3D voxel grid (e.g., 8×32×32×32 for QM9 with 5 atom + 3 bond channels; 11×64×40×32 for GEOM-Drugs), which is post-processed through peak picking, gradient-based atom position optimization, and bond γ_ij refinement to yield a molecular graph with 3D conformations. Molecules are pre-aligned to a canonical frame via principal component analysis of atom positions before field encoding, replacing the rotational invariance that E(3) methods enforce.

## Method

FMG represents molecules as continuous 3D fields: each atom type and bond type is encoded as a mixture of Gaussian RBFs evaluated on a fixed voxel grid, producing a K-channel tensor. A 3D U-Net (ResNet blocks, linear/full attention, scaling layers) parametrizes the DDPM denoiser, trained with a variance-preserving noise schedule and separate modified-cosine schedules for atom (ν=1) and bond (ν=1.5) channels so bonds denoise faster than atoms. The key architectural departure is replacing E(3) invariance with principal-component-based reference rotations: molecules are oriented so their largest-variation axis aligns horizontally, giving the CNN a consistent frame without enforcing rotational symmetry, thereby preserving chirality. Classifier-free guidance on binned atom counts (β=2) improves atom-type distribution fidelity. Graph extraction proceeds in four stages: deterministic flood-fill peak picking, MSE-based atom position optimization against RBF mixtures, distance-thresholded bond detection with bond-field verification, and per-bond γ_ij optimization to prune false bonds.

## Results

On QM9, FMG achieves state-of-the-art atom neutrality (99.4%) and molecule neutrality (91.5%), outperforming all baselines including VoxMol (99.2%/89.3%) and GeoLDM (98.9%/89.4%), with 98.8% validity. On GEOM-Drugs, FMG achieves 94.7% atom neutrality and 64.9% validity, competitive with MiDi (96.8%/62.7%) and JODO (97.0%/77.8%) but trailing VoxMol (98.1%/93.4%). For conformational quality on GEOM-Drugs, FMG achieves a bond length W₁ of 2.74 and bond angle W₁ of 1.00, substantially outperforming EDM (8.84 and 4.36 respectively), though JODO achieves the best bond angle (0.351).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FMG achieves the best atom and molecule neutrality on QM9 (99.4% and 91.5%), beating VoxMol (99.2%/89.3%), GeoLDM (98.9%/89.4%), and EDM (98.1%/81.9%). However, on graph quality metrics (TVa, TVb, MST), MiDi and JODO outperform FMG on QM9 (TVa 0.19 vs 0.52; TVb 0.81 vs 0.95), which the authors attribute to FMG's lack of strict atom-count enforcement. On GEOM-Drugs, FMG trails VoxMol on validity (64.9% vs 93.4%) and molecule neutrality (16.5% vs 75.0%), but substantially outperforms EDM on conformational metrics (bond length W₁: 2.74 vs 8.84; bond angle W₁: 1.00 vs 4.36). JODO achieves the best bond angle W₁ on GEOM-Drugs (0.351) and the best CExTB (1.21), while FMG's conformational energies (CEMMFF 53.14, CExTB 3.22) remain the weakest among compared methods. The key differentiator is chirality: FMG correctly captures enantiomer distributions (matching the skewed data distribution), while EDM generates enantiomers with equal probability and GDM-AUG produces degenerate zero-volume conformations.

## Relation to Prior Work

FMG departs from the dominant E(3)-equivariant GNN-on-point-cloud paradigm (EDM, MiDi, JODO, GeoLDM, MolDiff) by replacing rotational symmetry constraints with a 3D U-Net operating on voxelized RBF field representations, inheriting only translational invariance from CNN inductive biases. It builds on the DDPM diffusion framework and classifier-free guidance, and is conceptually closest to Pinheiro et al. (2023) and Ragoza et al. (2020), which also use field/voxel representations but via score-based or VAE models. The key theoretical departure is proving that E(3)-invariant parametrizations are fundamentally incapable of modeling chirality, and that the 'obvious fix' of SE(3)-invariant features scales as O(n⁴), motivating the reference-rotation approach instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility result for E(3)-invariant models is practically critical: 27% of drug-like molecules in GEOM contain chiral centers, and incorrect enantiomer specification can render a drug toxic (thalidomide) or ineffective (ibuprofen). The O(n⁴) scaling argument for SE(3)-invariant point-cloud features explains why the field has struggled to develop chiral-aware equivariant methods, and positions field-based representations as a viable alternative. Limitations include dataset-dependent field tensor dimensions that constrain computational feasibility, and the method's competitive (not dominant) performance on graph-quality metrics like TV_a, suggesting the field representation trades some distributional fidelity for chirality awareness. Whether this approach generalizes to other 3D object generation tasks remains open.
