## Problem

Deep generative models, particularly diffusion models, are known to memorize training data, yet prior work has identified disparate memorization phenomena (duplication, specific prompts, low complexity) without a unifying theoretical explanation. Existing formal definitions of memorization based on probability mass are intractable at the scale of models like Stable Diffusion and fail to distinguish between overfitting-driven and data-driven memorization. This paper introduces the Manifold Memorization Hypothesis (MMH), a geometric framework that explains memorization through the local intrinsic dimension (LID) of the model's learned manifold relative to the ground truth data manifold.

## Input / Output

The detection pipeline consumes generated images (from Stable Diffusion v1.5, iDDPM, or StyleGAN2-ADA) along with their text captions and corresponding training images, producing scalar LID estimates via FLIPD (for diffusion models) or Jacobian rank (for GANs), which are then thresholded to classify memorization type. The mitigation pipeline takes a text prompt as input, computes differentiable memorization metrics (A^FLIPD(c) or A^{sθ^CFG}(c)) by accumulating score-function quantities along the DDIM sampling trajectory, backpropagates to obtain per-token attributions, and outputs a GPT-4-perturbed prompt that reduces memorization while preserving semantic content. A key practical detail is that FLIPD operates in the latent space of Stable Diffusion with a single Hutchinson trace sample, making it tractable at 512×512 resolution, whereas the NB estimator requires 4d forward passes and is limited to smaller models.

## Method

The MMH posits that memorization at a point x occurs when the model manifold M_θ has too small a local intrinsic dimension LID_θ(x), and formalizes two distinct types: overfitting-driven memorization (OD-Mem, where LID_θ(x) < LID_*(x), indicating the model failed to generalize) and data-driven memorization (DD-Mem, where LID_θ(x) = LID_*(x) but both are low, meaning the ground truth distribution itself concentrates mass at that point). For detection, the paper validates that FLIPD and NB estimators of LID_θ separate memorized from non-memorized samples across scales from 2D synthetic data to Stable Diffusion on LAION, and shows that the CFG vector norm is inversely related to LID_θ, unifying prior observations about classifier-free guidance. For mitigation, the paper derives two new differentiable metrics—A^{sθ^CFG}(c) based on the CFG-adjusted score norm and A^FLIPD(c) based on the expected FLIPD estimate along the sampling trajectory—that can be backpropagated through the diffusion model to identify which prompt tokens drive memorization; these attributions are then used to sample k tokens for GPT-4-based rephrasing, reducing SSCD similarity to training images while maintaining CLIP score. The framework also provides formal connections to prior memorization definitions, showing that OD-Mem implies (λ,γ)-copies in the sense of Bhattacharjee et al. while DD-Mem does not, explaining why probability-mass-based detection fails for data-driven cases.

## Results

The paper validates the Manifold Memorization Hypothesis (MMH) across scales from 2D synthetic data to Stable Diffusion v1.5. On CIFAR-10, LID estimates (via FLIPD and the Normal Bundle method) are consistently lower for memorized images (both exact and reconstructive) compared to non-memorized ones for both iDDPM and StyleGAN2-ADA. On Stable Diffusion with 86 memorized LAION images, all three LID proxies (unconditional LID_θ, conditional LID_θ(·|c), and CFG vector norm) assign significantly smaller values to memorized images, with the unconditional LID detecting memorization even without caption information—a capability the CFG norm lacks. For sample-time mitigation, attribution-based token perturbation (using A^CFG, A^{sθ^CFG}, or A^FLIPD) achieves lower SSCD similarity to training images while maintaining higher CLIP scores than uniform random token selection, with all three metrics performing comparably.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Wen et al. (2023), the paper's two new differentiable metrics (A^{sθ^CFG}(c) and A^FLIPD(c)) perform on par with the original A^CFG(c) metric for both token attribution and sample-time mitigation; the paper explicitly states that 'using FLIPD or the norm of the CFG vector perform on par with each other' in qualitative comparisons (Figure 7b) and quantitative CLIP/SSCD comparisons (Figure 7a). The key advantage over Wen et al. is theoretical grounding in the MMH and the ability to detect memorized training images without captions (via unconditional LID_θ), which the CFG vector norm cannot do. Against Somepalli et al. (2023b)'s Random Token Addition (RTA) baseline, the paper's GPT-4-based perturbation guided by token attributions produces significantly more semantically coherent images while still reducing memorization; Figure 10 shows RTA introduces incoherent token insertions (e.g., 'mellon dragonball on the villar') whereas the GPT-based approach preserves meaning (e.g., 'discussions about the precepts and Buddhist principles'). The paper does not claim to outperform Wen et al. on raw memorization reduction metrics, positioning its contribution as a unifying theoretical framework with practical tools rather than a strictly superior detector.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Pidstrigach's (2022) observation that diffusion models learn low-dimensional manifolds into a full geometric framework (the Manifold Memorization Hypothesis) that reframes memorization as a local intrinsic dimension (LID) mismatch between the model manifold and the data manifold. It departs from prior memorization definitions (Bhattacharjee et al., 2023; Carlini et al., 2023) that rely on probability mass or pixel-space distance, instead grounding the analysis in manifold geometry and LID estimators (FLIPD, normal bundle). The framework unifies and explains disparate memorization phenomena from Somepalli et al., Yoon et al., Gu et al., and Wen et al. under a single lens, and introduces the novel distinction between overfitting-driven memorization (OD-Mem) and data-driven memorization (DD-Mem), which prior work had not formally separated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OD-Mem vs. DD-Mem distinction has direct practical consequences: OD-Mem can be addressed by improving model generalization, while DD-Mem requires changing the training data distribution itself—a misalignment that no amount of model improvement can fix. The LID-based detection approach is more scalable than distance-based methods (which require access to the full training set) and works at Stable Diffusion scale, offering a viable path for production monitoring. However, the sample-time mitigation via FLIPD performs on par with but does not outperform the simpler CFG-vector-norm approach, and LID estimates still overlap between memorized and non-memorized samples due to complexity confounding. Open questions include whether more efficient LID control during sampling can close this gap, and whether the framework extends to discrete data like language.
