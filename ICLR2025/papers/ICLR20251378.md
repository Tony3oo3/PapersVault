## Problem

While recent LVLMs have improved multi-image processing through interleaved pre-training and instruction tuning, quantitative evaluation of these models across diverse multi-image tasks has significantly lagged behind their development. Existing benchmarks cover only limited image relationships—Video-MME addresses solely temporal sequences, MUIRBench omits spatial reasoning—preventing a comprehensive assessment of multi-image comprehension across semantic, temporal, and spatial dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251378/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance presents 2–32 images (average 6.64) spanning five modalities—RGB, text, video frames, depth maps, and point clouds—alongside a multiple-choice question with up to eight options. For single-image-only models, images are either spatially concatenated into one image or their visual token embeddings are concatenated before LLM input, with results validated by shuffling options to eliminate position bias. The benchmark also includes an unanswerable set (19 tasks × 40 questions) constructed via five perturbation strategies (keyword replacement, answer-image swap, image shuffle, etc.) to test whether models can recognize when a question cannot be answered from the given images.

## Method

MMIU is constructed via a top-down hierarchy grounded in Multiple Trace Theory from cognitive psychology, which categorizes working memory into episodic (temporal), semantic, and spatial components. The authors enumerate seven multi-image relationship subtypes (low-level semantic, high-level objective/subjective semantic, discrete/continuous temporal, 2D/3D spatial), then assign 52 tasks across 77,659 images to these relationships, sourcing data from approximately 60 existing datasets and standardizing it into a unified metadata format. Multiple-choice questions are generated using either hand-designed rules or GPT-4o with task-specific prompts that produce semantically similar distractors to ensure visual grounding is required. Beyond the benchmark itself, the paper introduces three analytical tools: cross-relationship performance comparison, a task map (built via QwenVL-chat embeddings clustering 52 tasks into 8 categories) to identify in- vs. out-of-domain tasks, and SFT-based task learning difficulty analysis (fine-tuning QwenVL-chat on each task for 20 epochs to measure overfitting resistance and identify tasks requiring additional pre-training data).

## Results

On the MMIU benchmark (11,698 multiple-choice questions across 52 tasks and 7 image relationship types), GPT-4o achieves the highest overall accuracy at 55.5%, followed by Claude 3.5 Sonnet and Gemini 1.5 Flash at 53.4% each. Among open-source models, InternVL2-Pro leads at 50.3%, trailing GPT-4o by 5.4 percentage points. Performance varies dramatically across relationship types: models score 84.0% on low-level semantic tasks (GPT-4o) but drop to 41.7% on 2D spatial and 55.4% on 3D spatial tasks, revealing spatial understanding as the primary bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o (55.5%) leads all 24 evaluated models, outperforming Claude 3.5 Sonnet (53.4%) and Gemini 1.5 Flash (53.4%) by ~2 points, while Gemini 1.0 Pro Vision trails significantly at 40.2%. Among open-source models, InternVL2-Pro (50.3%) is the strongest, but still lags GPT-4o by 5.4 points; notably, it surpasses multi-image SFT models like Mantis-idefics2-8B (45.6%) and LLaVA-Next-Interleave-7B (32.4%). A key finding is that single-image model GLM-4V (27.0%) outperforms multi-image models LLaVA-Next-Interleave (32.4% on testmini but 32.4% overall) and idefics2-8B (27.8%), suggesting strong single-image understanding is foundational for multi-image comprehension. Multi-image SFT provides substantial gains: Mantis-idefics2-8B (45.6%) improves over its base idefics2-8B (27.8%) by 17.8 points. Finally, GPT-4o with GPT-4o-generated image captions (48.7%) approaches the full multi-image LVLM performance (55.5%), indicating that textual descriptions of images recover much of the lost visual information but cannot fully replace direct multi-image input, especially for spatial tasks (44.6% vs. 55.4% on 3D spatial).

## Relation to Prior Work

MMIU positions itself as a comprehensive successor to existing multi-image benchmarks (Video-MME, MUIRBench, MileBench, MANTIS-Eval), each of which covers only 1–4 image relationships and a limited set of tasks. Rather than building on a single prior method, it synthesizes ~60 existing datasets into a unified evaluation framework organized by a cognitive-psychology taxonomy (Multiple Trace Theory) that partitions multi-image understanding into semantic, temporal, and spatial dimensions. The key departure from prior work is the inclusion of unanswerable questions, five input modalities (including depth maps and point clouds), and multi-faceted analytical tools (task map, SFT-based learning difficulty) that go beyond simple accuracy reporting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With GPT-4o achieving only 55.7% and spatial/temporal reasoning identified as the weakest axes, MMIU provides a concrete, reproducible target for the next generation of multi-image LVLMs. The finding that strong single-image models (GLM-4V, InternVL1.5) can outperform models with multi-image SFT challenges the assumption that multi-image pre-training data is a prerequisite, suggesting that visual grounding quality may matter more than interleaved training. Open questions include how to close the spatial-reasoning gap, whether longer context windows will help with ordering and retrieval tasks that resist SFT, and whether the unanswerable-question paradigm generalizes to other evaluation settings.
