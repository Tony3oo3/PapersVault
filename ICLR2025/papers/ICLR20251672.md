## Problem

Existing brain encoding methods treat natural images as undifferentiated wholes, making it impossible to determine which specific spatial regions of a complex scene drive selectivity in different cortical areas. Prior functional mapping relied on simplified single-object stimuli, and even recent foundation-model-based encoders operate on global image embeddings, leaving the spatial decomposition of semantic selectivity within ecologically valid scenes unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes natural RGB images (NSD COCO subset, ~10,000 images) processed through frozen ViT backbones at upsampled resolutions (CLIP ViT-B/16 at 896×896 yielding 56×56 patches; DINOv2 ViT-B/14+reg at 1036×1036 yielding 74×74 patches; SigLIP at 768×768 yielding 48×48 patches), along with trained per-voxel linear adapter weights and optional CLIP text prompts for zero-shot semantic queries. It produces spatially dense semantic feature maps at patch resolution, voxel-wise relevance maps that highlight which image regions activate each brain voxel, and derived feature-correlate maps for depth, saturation, and luminance. The distillation step uses 51 augmented views for CLIP and 25 for DINO/SigLIP, completing in under 0.2 seconds per image on GPU.

## Method

BrainSAIL combines three components: (1) voxel-wise fMRI encoders with frozen ViT backbones and per-voxel linear probes trained via MSE loss on NSD data; (2) dense feature extraction from the ViT backbone using self-attention modifications (NACLIP for CLIP, MaskCLIP for register-token models) that place patch embeddings in the same semantic space as the [CLS] token; and (3) a Learning-Free Distillation Module that generates n augmented views via random horizontal/vertical offsets and flips, extracts dense features from each view, inverse-transforms coordinates back to the original image space, and averages the overlapping features. The key insight is that this averaging is mathematically equivalent to minimizing MSE over the augmented views (the mean minimizes sum of squared Euclidean distances), providing an artifact-free dense semantic map without any gradient-based optimization. The trained voxel-wise adapter is then applied to each patch's distilled feature to produce per-voxel relevance maps, and UMAP is used to jointly factorize brain encoder weights and image features into a shared low-dimensional space for interpretable visualization.

## Results

On open-vocabulary segmentation (zero-shot, no training), BrainSAIL's learning-free distillation applied to NACLIP achieves mIoU of 18.19 on ADE20K, 34.15 on COCO Object, 23.08 on COCO Stuff, and 79.09 on VOC20, with corresponding Pearson correlations of 0.443, 0.435, 0.405, and 0.489. The paper reports these as state-of-the-art results for open-vocabulary CLIP-based segmentation. For fMRI brain encoding, the CLIP, DINO, and SigLIP-based encoders all achieve very high test-set R² values comparable to prior state-of-the-art (Wang et al., 2023a; Luo et al., 2024), with CLIP and SigLIP (language-supervised) outperforming DINO (image-only self-supervised).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its learning-free distillation against two dense-feature extraction baselines: SCLIP (Wang et al., 2023b) and NACLIP (Hajimiri et al., 2024). Against NACLIP, the distilled method consistently wins on both mIoU and Pearson across all four segmentation datasets (e.g., ADE20K: 18.19 vs. 17.69 mIoU; 0.443 vs. 0.425 Pearson), confirming that the augmentation-based denoising provides a uniform improvement. Against SCLIP, the distilled method achieves higher mIoU on ADE20K (18.19 vs. 16.45), COCO Object (34.15 vs. 33.52), and COCO Stuff (23.08 vs. 21.95), and substantially higher Pearson on ADE20K (0.443 vs. 0.308), COCO Object (0.435 vs. 0.353), and COCO Stuff (0.405 vs. 0.309), but SCLIP retains an edge on VOC20 (81.54 vs. 79.09 mIoU; 0.551 vs. 0.489 Pearson), likely because VOC20's simpler 20-class vocabulary is less sensitive to the spatial artifacts that distillation addresses. Overall, the distilled NACLIP method is the strongest performer in 6 of 8 metric-dataset combinations, establishing it as the best zero-shot dense CLIP feature extractor for downstream brain-encoding applications.

## Relation to Prior Work

BrainSAIL extends the established paradigm of brain encoding with frozen vision foundation models (CLIP, DINO, SigLIP) plus linear voxel-wise probes by introducing spatially dense feature extraction and a learning-free denoising step. It directly builds on NACLIP and MaskCLIP for producing dense patch-level embeddings in the same semantic space as the [CLS] token, and repurposes the photometric-consistency intuition from NeRF/feature-field distillation into a 2D, training-free averaging procedure over augmented views. The key conceptual departure from prior brain-encoding work (Conwell et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023a) is the shift from treating each image as a single global embedding to decomposing it into localized semantic components, enabling voxel-wise relevance maps that identify *which parts* of a natural scene drive selectivity in a given cortical region.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BrainSAIL provides the first practical, high-throughput tool for grounding cortical semantic selectivity to specific image regions in complex natural scenes, moving beyond the single-object or noise-background stimuli that have dominated fMRI mapping studies. The finding that DINO (image-only self-supervision) produces grounding maps more sensitive to low-level color/texture while CLIP and SigLIP (language-supervised) produce more semantically coherent maps highlights that 'equivalent' brain-prediction R² does not imply equivalent representational geometry, an important caveat for choosing proxy models. Limitations include dependence on a single fMRI dataset (NSD) for encoder training, potential co-occurrence biases in the stimulus set, and the fact that the method still requires per-subject encoder training. Open questions include whether the dense-feature approach generalizes to dynamic stimuli, larger-scale neural datasets, and whether the learning-free distillation can be extended to 3D or multi-view settings.
