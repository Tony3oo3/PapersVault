## Problem

Representation learning has produced a proliferation of loss functions across contrastive learning, clustering, dimensionality reduction, and supervised classification, but prior work has only connected subsets of 2–3 methods at a time. No single framework unifies these objectives, making it difficult to understand their shared structure or to transfer improvements across traditionally separate domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252137/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-computed feature vectors from frozen DiNO ViT backbones (ViT-S/14, B/14, L/14) on ImageNet-1K, CIFAR-10, and STL-10, along with per-image augmentations and pre-computed k-nearest-neighbor graphs (k=3) built via cosine similarity. It produces soft cluster assignment matrices (one probability per data point per cluster) evaluated by Hungarian accuracy against ground-truth labels, as well as learned embeddings for linear probing and KNN classification. No additional normalization is applied to the feature vectors, and the training protocol mirrors TEMI for fair comparison.

## Method

I-Con defines a single loss as the average KL divergence between a fixed supervisory neighborhood distribution p(j|i) and a learned neighborhood distribution q_φ(j|i), where both are conditional probability distributions over data-point pairs. By selecting different distribution families—Gaussian, Student-t, uniform, or indicator—for p and q, the framework recovers over 23 methods including SNE, t-SNE, PCA, SimCLR, InfoNCE, SupCon, CLIP, k-Means, spectral clustering, and cross-entropy as exact special cases (proven via 15 theorems). The practical contribution, Debiased InfoNCE Clustering, instantiates I-Con with a k-Means-style intra-cluster uniform distribution as q_φ and a debiased supervisory distribution p that mixes the original neighborhood signal with a uniform component (parameter α≈0.6–0.8), analogous to label smoothing. Neighbor propagation further expands the supervisory signal by summing powers of the KNN adjacency matrix up to length 2, broadening each node's effective neighborhood. This combination yields a self-balancing loss requiring no hand-tuned regularization, achieving +8% Hungarian accuracy over the prior state-of-the-art TEMI on ImageNet-1K with DiNO ViT-L/14.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K unsupervised clustering (measured by Hungarian Accuracy), Debiased InfoNCE Clustering achieves 57.8% (ViT-S/14), 64.75% (ViT-B/14), and 67.52% (ViT-L/14), outperforming the prior state-of-the-art TEMI (56.84% and 58.62% for ViT-S/14 and ViT-B/14) by +1.0% and +6.1% respectively. On CIFAR-10 linear probing with a Student-t learned distribution, the debiased method reaches 92.70% (α=0.4) versus 90.97% for t-SimCLR, and on STL-10 linear probing it reaches 87.18% (α=0.6) versus 85.11% for t-SimCLR. The method also improves out-of-distribution generalization, e.g., +3.3% KNN accuracy on CIFAR-100 over t-SimCLR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary ImageNet-1K clustering benchmark, Debiased InfoNCE Clustering decisively beats all four clustering baselines: it surpasses TEMI (prior SOTA) by +6.1 points on ViT-B/14 (64.75 vs. 58.62) and by +1.0 points on ViT-S/14 (57.8 vs. 56.84), while TEMI does not report ViT-L/14 results. The gap over SCAN (+9.2 on ViT-B/14) and Contrastive Clustering (+9.1 on ViT-B/14) is even larger, and k-Means trails by over 12 points. In the feature-learning experiments (Tables 5–6), the I-Con debiasing strategy consistently outperforms SimCLR and DCL on both in-distribution (CIFAR-10, STL-10) and out-of-distribution (CIFAR-100, Oxford-IIIT Pet) evaluations; for example, with a Student-t q_φ, the debiased method reaches 92.70% linear-probing accuracy on CIFAR-10 versus 90.97% for t-SimCLR and divergent results for DCL. On STL-10, debiasing at α=0.6 yields 87.18% linear probing versus 85.11% for t-SimCLR, and on Oxford-IIIT Pet KNN it reaches 84.13% versus 81.41% for t-SimCLR. Overall, the method wins every head-to-head comparison across all five datasets and both evaluation protocols (linear probing and KNN).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends a line of work that has identified pairwise connections between subsets of representation learning methods (e.g., Hu et al. 2023 linking SimCLR to t-SNE, Yang et al. 2022 equating contrastive and cross-entropy losses, Balestriero & LeCun 2022 connecting spectral and contrastive methods) by claiming to be the first to unify over 23 methods under a single integrated KL-divergence objective. It departs from Chuang et al. 2020's debiased contrastive learning by relocating the debiasing correction from the learned softmax to the supervisory distribution, making the technique transferable to clustering and supervised settings. The probabilistic K-Means formulation (Theorem 13) provides a new contrastive interpretation of a classical clustering algorithm, bridging a gap that prior spectral-contrastive connections had not fully addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The I-Con framework provides a principled 'periodic table' that makes the implicit assumptions of each representation learning method explicit, enabling systematic cross-domain transfer of techniques (e.g., Student-t distributions from t-SNE applied to contrastive learning, or debiasing from contrastive learning applied to clustering). The +8% improvement on ImageNet-1K unsupervised classification demonstrates that this transfer is not merely theoretical. Open questions include whether the unification extends to generative models or reinforcement learning objectives, whether the framework's hyperparameter choices (σ, α, propagation depth) can be made adaptive, and whether the formal equivalences hold under distribution shift or in the deep learning regime with finite-capacity networks.
