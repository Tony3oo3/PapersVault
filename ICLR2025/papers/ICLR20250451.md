## Problem

LLM-based verifiers for Best-of-N selection are typically trained as discriminative classifiers that assign a numerical score via a separate classification head, which fails to exploit the text-generation capabilities that pretrained LLMs are designed for. This limits verifiers to a single forward pass with no chain-of-thought reasoning, no unified instruction tuning, and no ability to leverage additional inference-time compute. The paper asks whether recasting verification as next-token prediction can unlock these generative advantages while improving verification accuracy.

## Input / Output

The verifier takes a problem statement (e.g., a math word problem) and a candidate solution as input, and produces a scalar verification score defined as the probability of the 'Yes' token in the LLM's vocabulary (rather than a sigmoid output from a separate head). For GenRM-CoT, the model first autoregressively generates a step-by-step verification rationale before emitting the Yes/No token; at inference with majority voting, K independent CoT rationales are sampled and the score is the mean of their 'Yes' token probabilities. The same model can also be queried in generation mode (without a candidate solution) to produce solutions, enabling unified generation-and-verification training.

## Method

GenRM replaces the discriminative binary cross-entropy loss with standard supervised fine-tuning (next-token prediction) on a dataset where the target is simply the token 'Yes' or 'No' following the prompt 'Is the answer correct (Yes/No)?'. The GenRM-CoT variant extends this by training the model to generate a verification chain-of-thought rationale (e.g., checking each step of the solution against an expected answer) before the final Yes/No token, using either oracle rationales (for algorithmic tasks) or synthetic rationales generated via reference-guided grading with the same LLM that produced the solutions. A unified training objective combines the verification SFT loss with a λ-weighted SFT loss on correct solutions, enabling positive transfer between generation and verification. At inference, majority voting over K sampled CoT rationales (default K=32) averages the 'Yes' probabilities to produce a calibrated score, allowing the verifier to scale with inference-time compute in a way that discriminative RMs cannot.

## Results

GenRM-CoT achieves substantial Best-of-N improvements over the base generator: 5%→45.3% on algorithmic tasks (Gemma-2B), 73%→93.4% on GSM8K (Gemma2-9B), and 28%→44.6% on easy-to-hard generalization to MATH500 (Gemma2-9B). On MATH, GenRM-CoT matches the Best-of-32 performance of a discriminative verifier using 6.4× fewer solutions. On MMLU college mathematics, GenRM-CoT reaches 56.1% vs. 53.0% for discriminative RM (Best-of-32), with the gap widening on harder tasks (abstract algebra: 53.5% vs. 50%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenRM-CoT substantially outperforms the discriminative RM (Cobbe et al., 2021) across all tasks: on GSM8K it reaches 93.4% vs. the discriminative RM's lower Best-of-N, and on MATH it achieves equivalent performance with 6.4× fewer candidate solutions. Against LLM-as-a-Judge (Zheng et al., 2024) using Gemini 1.0 Pro, the smaller fine-tuned Gemma models (2B/7B/9B) with GenRM-CoT consistently outperform, demonstrating that training a generative verifier is more effective than prompting a larger off-the-shelf model. GenRM also outperforms DPO-based verifiers (Rafailov et al., 2024) on Best-of-N across all evaluated tasks. On MATH, GenRM-CoT surpasses the self-consistency baseline (Wang et al., 2022), which uses test-time compute without any learned verifier, confirming that a trained generative verifier provides a more efficient use of inference-time compute than simple majority voting over solutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the well-established task of training reward models/verifiers for LLM reasoning (Cobbe et al., 2021; Lightman et al., 2023) from a discriminative classification objective to standard next-token prediction (SFT). Conceptually, it builds on chain-of-thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022) and self-consistency/majority voting (Wang et al., 2022) by showing that these generative LLM capabilities become directly applicable to verification once the verifier is trained as a generator rather than a classifier. It departs from DPO-based verifiers (Hosseini et al., 2024) by using a simpler next-token prediction loss that enables seamless unification of solution generation and verification in a single model, and from LLM-as-a-Judge (Zheng et al., 2024) by demonstrating that fine-tuning a smaller model with next-token prediction outperforms prompting a larger off-the-shelf model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the technique requires only changing the training loss (no architectural modifications), making it immediately adoptable by anyone training verifiers for Best-of-N or RLHF pipelines. The finding that synthetic rationales from the same model that generates solutions are sufficient (no more capable model or human annotation needed) lowers the barrier to training high-quality CoT verifiers. Open questions include whether the approach extends to coding, alignment, and open-ended generation; whether process-level supervision or RL training can further improve generative verifiers; and how to integrate them into full RL pipelines for LLMs. The easy-to-hard generalization (GSM8K → MATH) suggests the framework may scale to broader reasoning domains, but current evidence is limited to math and algorithmic tasks.
