## Problem

Existing vision-language-action (VLA) models for robot manipulation rely exclusively on text-based instructions, ignoring the more natural speech modality for human-robot interaction. The standard workaround of cascading an external ASR system before the VLA adds pipeline complexity and, critically, discards non-semantic speech cues such as voiceprint, emotion, and intonation that are essential for personalized tasks like selecting the correct object based on ownership or user preference.

## Input / Output

VLAS consumes raw speech (converted to an 80-bin mel-spectrogram of 3000 frames), RGB visual observations from two camera views (concatenated at each timestep), and optionally a text instruction. The speech encoder (Whisper) produces 1500 hidden states that are downsampled by a factor of 5 before projection. The model outputs a 7-dimensional continuous action vector [x, y, z, φ, θ, ψ, g] (end-effector position, rotation, gripper state) by autoregressively generating discrete tokens from 256 uniformly spaced bins per dimension, reusing the 256 least-frequent tokens in the LLM vocabulary. During inference, the Voice RAG module additionally injects retrieved personalized knowledge as extra text tokens.

## Method

VLAS extends LLaVA (CLIP ViT + MLP projector + Vicuna-7B LLM) with a Whisper speech encoder whose hidden states are projected into the shared language space via a dedicated MLP, enabling the LLM to process speech, vision, and text tokens in a single autoregressive sequence. A Voice RAG module extracts a voiceprint from the raw speech via a pre-trained speaker-identification model, queries an external personal-knowledge database, and feeds the retrieved text back through the tokenizer as additional context tokens, allowing the model to resolve ambiguous instructions using individual-specific information. Training proceeds in three stages: (I) coarse speech-to-text alignment by fine-tuning only the speech MLP on LibriSpeech-360; (II) joint fine-tuning of all non-encoder components on a new 185K-sample SQA dataset (speech questions with image and text answers, generated via VITS TTS with 1,152+ voices) alongside LLaVA's VQA data, yielding the VLAS-Base foundation model; and (III) behavior-cloning fine-tuning on a new CSI dataset (~194K samples) of CALVIN trajectories with half the instructions replaced by synthesized speech, producing the final robot policy. This end-to-end design avoids cascading ASR error propagation and preserves voiceprint information for personalization.

## Results

On the CALVIN benchmark (ABCD/D split), VLAS with text instructions achieves a long-horizon success rate of 94.5% (LH-1) and 56.6% (LH-5), with an average task length of 3.74, closely matching the VLA text baseline (95.5%/58.2%, Len 3.80). With speech instructions, VLAS achieves 94.2% (LH-1) and 54.6% (LH-5), Len 3.70, substantially outperforming the VLA+ASR cascade (88.7%/40.2%, Len 3.13) and RoboFlamingo+ASR (89.8%/48.3%, Len 3.41). On the customization benchmark, VLAS with Voice RAG achieves an average success rate of 86.5% (TTS speech) and 78.6% (real speech), compared to only 19.2% for the VLA text baseline, demonstrating the critical role of voiceprint-based personalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the CALVIN benchmark, VLAS with text instructions (Len 3.74) nearly matches the VLA text baseline (Len 3.80) while significantly outperforming MCIL (Len 0.40), HULC (Len 2.90), and RT-1 (Len 2.45). With speech input, VLAS (Len 3.70) substantially outperforms both the VLA+ASR cascade (Len 3.13) and RoboFlamingo+ASR (Len 3.41), demonstrating that end-to-end speech integration avoids the error propagation of external ASR. On the customization benchmark, VLAS with Voice RAG (86.5% avg) dramatically outperforms the VLA text baseline (19.2% avg), while removing RAG from VLAS drops performance to 16.0%, confirming the module's necessity. VLAS-Base matches LLaVA v1.5 on standard VLM benchmarks (e.g., VQA v2: 78.7 vs 78.8; GQA: 62.0 vs 62.0) and achieves comparable LibriSpeech WER to Whisper large-v2 (2.79% vs 2.7%), while surpassing BLIP-2 on SGQA (50.8 vs 41.0). On the CALVIN benchmark, VLAS significantly outperforms both pre-trained OpenVLA (0% on all LH splits) and fine-tuned OpenVLA (Len 0.34), attributed to OpenVLA's lack of end-effector visual feedback.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLAS extends the VLA paradigm established by RT-2, OpenVLA, and RoboFlamingo by adding a speech input modality directly into the policy network, building on LLaVA's ViT + MLP + LLM architecture. It departs from the standard cascading approach (external ASR → text → VLA) by using a Whisper encoder with a learned MLP projector to map speech embeddings into the LLM's language space, eliminating error propagation from transcription. The Voice RAG module is a novel addition that combines speaker identification with retrieval-augmented generation to inject individual-specific knowledge, a mechanism not present in prior VLA or VLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that end-to-end speech integration into a robot policy can match text-based performance while unlocking personalization capabilities (ownership, preference) that are fundamentally inaccessible to text-only systems. However, the evaluation is limited to simulation (CALVIN) and a single real robot (UR5), with most training speech being TTS-generated rather than naturally recorded, raising open questions about robustness to noisy environments, diverse accents, and emotional prosody. The Voice RAG paradigm, while effective in controlled settings, relies on a pre-built per-user knowledge database and a pre-trained speaker identification module, limiting its applicability to open-world scenarios with unknown users. Future work on leveraging other paralinguistic cues (emotion, intonation) and scaling to more complex multi-user environments would strengthen the practical impact.
