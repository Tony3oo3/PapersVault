## Problem

Existing zero-shot medical detection methods built on GLIP treat descriptive prompts as simple contextual equivalents to the target name, causing coarse image-description alignment and distribution shift in the target representation. Additionally, category-level prompts containing rich but ambiguous attribute vocabularies (e.g., 'tissue with pink or red color, irregular or round shape') cannot be effectively encoded within a single concatenated context, preventing precise matching with specific image regions. The paper also identifies that prior prompt-based methods degrade performance when applied to already fine-tuned medical detectors, limiting their practical clinical utility.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB medical image, a short target name (e.g., 'polyp'), and a disease description prompt as text input. Prompts come in two forms: instance-level prompts generated per-image by a VQA model (BLIP) capturing specific visual attributes, and category-level prompts generated once per class by combining VQA outputs with GPT-4 expansions across color, shape, texture, and location dimensions (~10× longer than instance prompts). The output is a set of bounding boxes with confidence scores produced by the RPN, evaluated under both pure zero-shot (no medical fine-tuning) and zero-shot enhancement (after supervised fine-tuning on medical data) settings.

## Method

StructuralGLIP introduces a dual-branch architecture where the main branch processes the target name and image through standard GLIP layers, while an auxiliary branch independently encodes the full prompt into a hierarchical latent knowledge bank via shared-parameter language encoder layers. At each of the N encoder layers, a mutual selection mechanism first selects Top-P visual tokens whose similarity to the prompt features is maximal, then selects Top-Q prompt tokens most relevant to those selected visual tokens, forming fine-grained structural representations. These selected tokens are injected back into the main branch via cross-modality multi-head attention (MHA) with residual connections, rather than concatenating the full prompt into the target context. This separation prevents distribution shift in the target representation while enabling instance-wise dynamic retrieval of the most relevant prompt fragments, allowing fixed category-level prompts to be adaptively aligned with each specific image. The approach also supports a zero-shot enhancement setting where the fine-tuned GLIP backbone (trained only on target names) is augmented at inference time with prompt knowledge through the auxiliary branch, achieving +4% AP over the fine-tuned baseline without additional labeled data.

## Results

On zero-shot medical detection across seven datasets (CVC-300, Kvasir, ColonDB, ClinicDB, ETIS, ISIC 2016, BCCD), StructuralGLIP achieves an average AP of 30.9% with instance-level prompts and 37.9% AP with category-level prompts, outperforming the strongest baseline AutoPrompter (26.7% AP) by +4.2% AP and +4.8% AP50 with instance prompts. With category-level prompts, StructuralGLIP (37.9% AP) surpasses MPT+Cluster (16.8% AP) by +21.1% AP, demonstrating that the knowledge-bank architecture effectively leverages richer, fixed prompts. Under the zero-shot enhancement setting (fine-tuned GLIP + prompts), StructuralGLIP reaches 60.6% average AP with category prompts, exceeding fully supervised RetinaNet (56.6% AP) by +4.0% AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StructuralGLIP consistently outperforms all six baselines across both prompt settings. Against instance-level prompt methods, it beats AutoPrompter (Wu et al., MICCAI 2023) by +4.2% average AP (30.9 vs. 26.7) and MIU-VL (Qin et al., ICLR 2022) by +8.7% AP (30.9 vs. 22.2), with the largest per-dataset gain on CVC-300 (+1.6% over AutoPrompter) and ETIS (+4.5% over AutoPrompter). Against category-level prompt methods, the advantage is dramatic: StructuralGLIP (37.9% AP) exceeds MPT+Cluster (Guo et al., MICCAI 2023) by +21.1% AP and MPT+WBF by +30.1% AP, indicating that prior ensemble-based approaches fail to exploit rich category prompts while the mutual-selection knowledge bank does. In the zero-shot enhancement setting (Table 4), StructuralGLIP with category prompts (60.6% avg AP) surpasses fully supervised RetinaNet (56.6% AP) and Faster R-CNN (55.2% AP), while MIU-VL and AutoPrompter actually degrade the fine-tuned GLIP (56.6→50.7 and 56.6→53.3 AP respectively) due to distribution shift from naive prompt concatenation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GLIP's late-fusion dual-tower architecture for phrase-grounding detection, extending it with a dual-branch design where prompts are encoded into a latent knowledge bank in an auxiliary branch rather than concatenated with the target name in the main branch. The knowledge bank concept is borrowed from continual learning prompt-pool methods (DualPrompt, CoDA-Prompt, AttrIClip), but those prior works operate at the input layer and require additional training, whereas StructuralGLIP performs layer-wise mutual selection within a pre-trained vision-language model without extra training. It departs from prior medical prompt methods (MIU-VL, AutoPrompter, MPT) that treat prompts as simple contextual text appended to the target, by introducing a selection mechanism that dynamically retrieves relevant prompt tokens per image instance at each encoder layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot enhancement setting—improving an already fine-tuned medical detector with prompts without additional labels—addresses a practical clinical need that prior work largely ignored, and the finding that other prompt methods actually degrade fine-tuned GLIP while StructuralGLIP improves it highlights a real distribution-shift problem in the field. The demonstration that fixed category-level prompts (generated once per disease class) can outperform instance-level prompts offers a significant efficiency gain for deployment. Limitations include reliance on LLM-generated prompts that may fail for rare diseases, the added inference cost of the auxiliary branch, and evaluation primarily on polyp detection and a handful of other tasks, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more diverse medical imaging modalities.
