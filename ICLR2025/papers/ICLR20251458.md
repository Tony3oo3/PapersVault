## Problem

Multi-goal long-horizon RL problems face compounded exploration challenges from both an expanded goal space and exponentially decaying learning signals over long sequences. Existing goal-conditioned RL environments (Panda-Gym, PointMaze, AntMaze) are either too simple, have short horizons, lack realistic dynamics, or use simplistic environment designs (sparse rewards, no termination conditions) that hinder training efficiency. Moreover, these environments rarely include accompanying demonstrations, precluding research on demonstration-based RL. The GCRL community lacks a realistic, complex testbed where researchers can study the interplay between environment design, demonstration quality, and algorithm performance.

## Input / Output

The agent observes a 9-dimensional continuous state (roll, pitch, yaw, flight path elevator angle, flight path azimuth angle, true airspeed, altitude, roll rate) concatenated with a 3-dimensional desired velocity vector goal (v_g, μ_g, χ_g) sampled from a continuous goal space. In guidance law mode, the policy outputs 3 intermediate commands (roll rate command, overload command, power level actuator position) that are smoothed by a PID-based control law into 4 final actuator deflections; in end-to-end mode, the policy directly outputs all 4 actuator deflections (aileron, elevator, rudder, power level). The simulation runs at 10 Hz with a maximum episode length of 400 steps, and the hard version spans a goal space of v∈[100,300], μ∈[-85°,85°], χ∈[-170°,170°].

## Method

VVC-Gym is a Gymnasium-based environment built around a 6-DoF fixed-wing UAV simulator with full aerodynamic equations, designed specifically to expose and study environment design choices for multi-goal long-horizon RL. Its key design contributions are: (1) seven termination conditions organized into three categories—goal achievement (RT), exploration limits (timeout, crash), and invalid trajectory truncation (continuous move-away, continuous roll, extreme state, negative overload with big roll)—which prevent collection of meaningless samples after severe states and create structured learning stages; (2) a general distance-based goal-conditioned reward with a tunable penalty r_penalty (set via large negative constant or pessimistic estimation) and a scaling exponent b that controls the reward's sensitivity across error magnitudes; and (3) a multi-quality demonstration pipeline that starts from a human-designed PID controller, augments trajectories via flight-path azimuth symmetry, and iteratively improves quantity and quality through IRPO. The paper provides baselines for GCRL (SAC+HER, PPO, GCBC+PPO, RIG, DISCERN, MEGA) and demonstration-based RL (GCBC, GCBC+PPO) across four demonstration quality levels, along with ablation studies on termination conditions, r_penalty, b, and velocity/direction reward weights.

## Results

On the hard version of VVC-Gym, GCBC+PPO achieves the highest success rate among pure GCRL baselines at 38.31±1.62%, substantially outperforming SAC (1.08±0.48%), PPO (0.04±0.03%), and SAC+HER (8.32±1.86%). Self-curriculum methods (RIG, DISCERN, MEGA) improve GCBC+PPO to approximately 49% success rate. With progressively higher-quality demonstrations (D_E^0 through D_E^3), GCBC+PPO success rate increases from 38.31% to 71.68%, while GCBC alone improves from 17.08% to 42.77%. Cross-environment comparison confirms VVC is significantly harder than Reach (100%) and PointMaze (93–100%) for the same algorithms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper establishes baselines for seven GCRL and demonstration-based RL methods on the new VVC-Gym benchmark. Pure RL algorithms (SAC at 1.08%, PPO at 0.04%) essentially fail on the hard VVC task, while adding HER to SAC yields only a modest improvement to 8.32%. Demonstration-based RL via GCBC+PPO dramatically outperforms all pure RL baselines, reaching 38.31% with the lowest-quality demonstrations (D_E^0) and 71.68% with the highest-quality (D_E^3). Among self-curriculum methods, DISCERN (49.36%), RIG (49.03%), and MEGA (48.62%) provide roughly 10 percentage points of improvement over GCBC+PPO alone, with no significant differences among the three. Cross-environment results (Table 10) show that while MEGA and GCBC+PPO achieve 100% on Reach and 93–100% on PointMaze, the best method on VVC reaches only 38.31%, confirming VVC-Gym as a substantially harder benchmark. The ablation on environment design (Fig. 3) further shows that using all 7 termination conditions and the structured reward function consistently improves training over using only RT/T termination with a simple distance reward, for both SAC+HER and PPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GCRL environment landscape (Panda-Gym, PointMaze, AntMaze, DMC) by introducing the first realistic fixed-wing UAV velocity vector control benchmark, deliberately addressing three gaps the authors identify in prior multi-goal environments: (1) tasks are too simple with short control sequences, (2) environment designs lack effective termination conditions and informative rewards, and (3) accompanying demonstrations are absent. It builds directly on IRPO (Gong et al., 2024b) for iterative demonstration generation and positions itself as a testbed for existing GCRL algorithms (SAC+HER, PPO, RIG, DISCERN, MEGA) and demonstration-based RL (GCBC, RLfD) rather than proposing a new algorithm. The conceptual departure is treating environment design—termination conditions, reward structure, and demonstration quality—as first-class research variables rather than fixed infrastructure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VVC-Gym fills a genuine gap by providing a realistic, computationally efficient (3623 FPS) multi-goal long-horizon environment where the best GCRL algorithm achieves only 38% success, making it a meaningful benchmark for future algorithmic progress. The systematic ablations on termination conditions and reward parameters (r_penalty, b, w_v/w_d) provide actionable guidance for environment design that prior GCRL work largely ignored. Open questions include extending to longer Basic Flight Maneuvers (Slow Roll, Knife Edge), establishing baselines for automatic sub-goal generation and offline RL, and addressing real-world transfer challenges (wind, hardware constraints, onboard compute). The paper's own limitations—no environmental dynamics, no hardware wear considerations—suggest the benchmark is a research tool rather than a deployment-ready system.
