## Problem

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) improves prompt adherence in generative models but doubles inference cost and systematically reduces output diversity, creating a quality-diversity trade-off that is difficult to control. This paper addresses both limitations simultaneously by distilling CFG's quality benefits into model weights (eliminating the inference overhead) while using reinforcement learning to explicitly reward diverse generations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252384/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'Soulful jazz song.') and produces music audio via a single autoregressive stage of MusicLM that models semantic and coarse acoustic features over discrete audio codec tokens. The diversity reward operates on 4-second audio clips sampled at 16 kHz, embedded by a compact ViT-based contrastive model trained with semi-hard triplet loss. A negative prompt ('Bad audio quality.') is used during the CFG teacher's forward pass but is not present at inference time for the distilled student.

## Method

The approach jointly optimises two objectives during finetuning: (1) an on-policy CFG distillation loss that minimises KL divergence between the student's logits and those of a CFG-augmented teacher (same architecture, γ=3) on student-sampled sequences, and (2) a REINFORCE-style RL objective that maximises the negative cosine similarity between embeddings of paired generations for the same prompt, scaled by a diversity coefficient β. The key architectural insight is that the teacher is simply the base model augmented with CFG rather than a separate larger model, enabling self-distillation. A third contribution leverages linear weight interpolation (LERP) between a quality-focused model (β=0) and a diversity-focused model (β=15) to construct a steerable quality-diversity Pareto front at deployment time, where the λ=0.5 merged model outperforms any single model finetuned at an intermediate β value.

## Results

In human evaluation, the merged model LERP(0,15) (λ=0.5) achieves a win rate above 0.50 against all other models on quality, and beats the CFG-augmented base model on diversity in 57% of comparisons while maintaining a 51% quality win rate. The CFG-distilled model (β=0) matches CFG quality exactly (0.50 win rate) while eliminating the 2× inference cost. On FAD (MusicCaps, VGGish), β=0 achieves 4.357, matching CFG's 4.371, confirming that distillation preserves quality. The diversity-focused model (β=15) achieves 0.73 and 0.79 diversity win rates against CFG and β=0 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CFG-augmented base model (Ho & Salimans, 2022), the CFG-distilled model (β=0) achieves a statistically indistinguishable quality win rate of 0.50 (p=0.74), confirming that CFG's quality benefits are fully captured in the weights without the 2× inference cost. The merged model LERP(0,15) surpasses CFG on diversity (57% win rate, p=0.023) while maintaining comparable quality (51% win rate, p=0.80), achieving a Pareto improvement over CFG. Against the MusicRL-R base model (Cideron et al., 2024), all proposed finetuned models significantly outperform it on quality: β=0 wins 0.68 and CFG wins 0.66 (both p<1e-12). On diversity, the base model is Pareto-dominated by LERP(0,15) (0.77 win rate, p=1.5e-08), while β=15 performs on par with the base model (0.50 win rate, p=0.62), suggesting the diversity reward can be hacked by the embedding model's biases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) CFG distillation, previously applied to diffusion models (Luo et al., 2023; Yin et al., 2024) but here extended to autoregressive LLMs using on-policy distillation (Agarwal et al., 2024) where the teacher is the CFG-augmented version of the student itself rather than a larger third-party model; (2) RL-based diversity optimization, departing from prior n-gram or mutual-information objectives (Li et al., 2016; Zhang et al., 2018) by using embedding-space negative cosine similarity as a reward; and (3) model merging via weight interpolation (Ramé et al., 2023; Wortsman et al., 2022a), applied here to trade off quality and diversity at deployment time. The key conceptual departure is combining all three into a single finetuning pipeline where the diversity RL counteracts the diversity collapse induced by CFG distillation, and the merged model surpasses individually-trained intermediate models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: eliminating CFG's 2× inference cost while maintaining quality, and providing a steerable quality-diversity front via simple weight interpolation at deployment time. However, the approach is validated only on a single architecture (MusicLM) and a single creative domain (text-to-music), and the diversity metric is acknowledged to be hackable (e.g., excessive background drums). Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to image/video generation, whether multiple diversity rewards (targeting different musical or linguistic dimensions) can be merged for finer-grained control, and whether the model-merging advantage over intermediate training holds at larger scale or across architectures.
