## Problem

Existing 3D shape representations for generative modeling fail to simultaneously achieve parameter efficiency for fine geometric detail, fast preprocessing for large-scale datasets, and a tensorial form amenable to modern neural networks. Mosaic-SDF partially addresses this with boundary-aligned grid tiles, but its tiles remain axis-aligned and isotropic, limiting how much local geometric detail can be captured per parameter. The paper identifies that the key missing ingredient is full geometry-aware adaptivity of local coordinate grids in both orientation and anisotropic scale.

## Input / Output

For fitting, the method takes a watertight mesh and produces a GALA representation consisting of 256 root voxels (placed via FPS on the mesh surface), each containing a depth-1 octree whose non-empty leaves hold a 5×5×5 local adaptive grid of truncated SDF values (range [-0.1, 0.1]), totaling ~277K parameters at 8-bit quantization. For generation, the input is a class label (ShapeNet) or text prompt (Objaverse), and the output is a 3D mesh extracted via marching cubes from the predicted GALA grids at 256³ or 512³ resolution. The entire representation is flattened into fixed-length vectors of root voxel parameters (R⁴), grid configurations (R¹⁰ per leaf), and grid values (R¹²⁵ per leaf) for direct transformer processing.

## Method

GALA represents a shape as a forest of octrees rooted at FPS-sampled surface centroids, where each non-empty leaf node contains a local grid whose orientation is determined by PCA of bounded surface normals (rather than convex-hull eigenvectors as in OBBTree) and whose axes are anisotropically rescaled using a histogram of projected surface points to align grid samples with geometrically dense regions. Grid values are initialized from truncated ground-truth SDF and refined via backpropagation with MSE loss over 400 iterations, with 8-bit quantization applied through a straight-through estimator during fitting. Generation follows a three-stage cascaded pipeline: (1) a diffusion model generates root voxel positions and scales conditioned on class/text, (2) a second diffusion model generates local grid configurations (quaternion, scales, positions) conditioned on the root voxels, and (3) grid SDF values are predicted by direct L2 regression from the configurations, exploiting the fact that grid geometry already encodes rich local shape information. All stages use 24-layer DiT-style transformers with v-prediction, cosine noise scheduling, and classifier-free guidance.

## Results

On reconstruction across 5 ShapeNet classes (50 objects), GALA achieves a Chamfer Distance of 1.18×10⁻³ and Light Field Distance of 1109.36, outperforming all baselines while using only 277k parameters (vs. 536k for Neural Wavelet and 1.05M for MeshDiffusion). For generation on ShapeNet airplane and chair, GALA achieves the best MMD (2.48 CD / 2.84 EMD for airplane; 13.28 CD / 8.17 EMD for chair) and best 1-NNA (59.90% / 57.43% for airplane; 53.10% / 54.57% for chair). The fitting process completes in ~10 seconds per mesh on an A100 GPU, and mesh extraction at 256³ takes 2.39 seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GALA outperforms all seven baselines on reconstruction accuracy. Against S2VS (262k params), GALA reduces CD from 4.32 to 1.18×10⁻³ (73% improvement) and LFD from 2430 to 1109, with comparable parameter count. Against MeshDiffusion/DMTet (1.05M params), GALA achieves 55% lower CD (1.18 vs. 2.61×10⁻³) with only 26% of the parameters and 20× faster fitting (10s vs. 20–30 min). Against Neural Wavelet (536k params), GALA halves the CD (1.18 vs. 4.17×10⁻³) with half the parameters. Against Mosaic-SDF, GALA captures internal structures (e.g., car interiors, guitar strings) that Mosaic-SDF misses, and achieves better generation MMD (2.48 vs. 3.54 CD for airplane). Against XCube and OctFusion (Table 6), GALA achieves CD of 1.18×10⁻³ vs. 3.25 for OctFusion (68k params) and XCube (664k params), with XCube failing to produce meshes on some test objects. In generation (Table 3), GALA leads on MMD and 1-NNA across both airplane and chair classes, while maintaining competitive coverage scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

GALA directly extends Mosaic-SDF (Yariv et al., 2023), which places boundary-aligned grid tiles over shape surfaces, by introducing three forms of local adaptivity: PCA-based grid orientation from surface normals, anisotropic axis scaling informed by point histograms, and an octree hierarchy for global sparsity. The cascaded generation pipeline draws on the coarse-to-fine diffusion paradigm established by cascaded image diffusion (Saharia et al., 2022) and BrepGen's hierarchical structured generation, while the transformer-based processing of vector sets follows S2VS and DiT. The paper positions itself against the broader 3D representation landscape—regular grids, tetrahedral grids, triplanes, point clouds, and neural implicit fields—arguing that none simultaneously satisfy parameter efficiency, preprocessing speed, and tensorial simplicity, and that local geometric adaptivity is the missing ingredient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GALA demonstrates that local geometric adaptivity (orientation + anisotropic scaling) is the key lever for efficient 3D shape representation, achieving high-fidelity reconstruction with only 277K parameters at 8-bit quantization and sub-10-second fitting on a single A100. The practical efficiency gains—fast fitting, compact storage (~0.28 MB per object), and direct mesh extraction without neural network queries—make it attractive for large-scale dataset processing and production 3D asset pipelines. Open questions include removing the watertight mesh constraint, extending to unsigned distance fields, compressing the octree forest topology, and whether the representation scales to the more diverse and open-ended geometry found in datasets like Objaverse beyond the ~130K text-pair subset tested.
