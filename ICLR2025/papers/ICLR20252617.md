## Problem

LoRA's low-rank constraint limits its expressive capacity, causing it to underperform full fine-tuning on complex tasks like code generation and mathematical reasoning, with the effective rank needed for full fine-tuning being 10–100× higher than typical LoRA configurations. Paradoxically, prior work shows LoRA's parameters are redundant and can be shared across layers with minimal loss, suggesting the parameters are underutilized. RaSA resolves this contradiction by increasing the effective rank without adding parameter overhead.

## Input / Output

RaSA takes a pre-trained LLM (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B) and instruction fine-tuning data, and produces low-rank update matrices that can be merged back into the frozen base weights with zero additional inference latency. The only structural addition over LoRA is a per-layer diagonal weighting matrix D_i operating at the bottleneck dimension, contributing less than 0.001% of total model parameters. The method assumes all layers share the same dimensionality, which holds for standard LLM architectures like Llama and Mistral.

## Method

RaSA splits each layer's LoRA matrices B_i and A_i into a layer-specific component (r−k ranks) and a layer-shared component (k ranks), then concatenates the shared components across all L layers to form global rank pools B_S ∈ R^{b×Lk} and A_S ∈ R^{Lk×a}. A trainable per-layer diagonal matrix D_i weights the shared pool, yielding an effective update rank of r−k+Lk while keeping the total trainable parameter count identical to LoRA. Theoretically, the paper proves RaSA's minimum reconstruction error is bounded above by LoRA's via a constructive SVD-based argument, and empirically shows that a small k (≈r/8) achieves significantly lower reconstruction error than LoRA's optimum within ~10 coordinate-descent iterations. This increased expressiveness translates to consistent gains on Humaneval+ and MATH benchmarks, with RaSA at r=8 outperforming LoRA at r=32, and also exhibits less catastrophic forgetting than LoRA.

## Results

On the MATH benchmark, RaSA (r=32) achieves 31.7% best accuracy on Llama-3.1-8B and 26.1% on Mistral-0.3-7B, outperforming LoRA (28.9% and 21.8%) by 2.8 and 4.3 points respectively, while still trailing full fine-tuning (35.5% and 28.1%). On Humaneval+ code generation, RaSA (r=32) reaches 59.5% PASS@1 on Llama-3.1-8B and 55.7% on Mistral, surpassing LoRA (57.9% and 50.2%) and MoRA (55.6% and 42.2%). Notably, RaSA with r=8 (21.0M params) already exceeds LoRA with r=32 (83.9M params) on MATH (30.3% vs 28.9% on Llama), demonstrating superior parameter utilization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RaSA consistently outperforms all PEFT baselines across both code and math tasks at every rank setting. On MATH (Llama-3.1-8B, r=32), RaSA achieves 31.7% vs. AdaLoRA (30.2%), PriLoRA (30.0%), OLoRA (29.0%), LoRA (28.9%), and MoRA (28.6%), with the gap widening on Mistral where RaSA leads by 1.3–7.7 points. On Humaneval+ (Llama, r=32), RaSA reaches 59.5% PASS@1 versus LoRA (57.9%), MoRA (55.6%), and VeRA (48.8% at r=1024). A striking finding is that RaSA at r=8 (21.0M params) surpasses LoRA at r=32 (83.9M params) on MATH (30.3% vs. 28.9%), indicating that LoRA's parameters are underutilized and that cross-layer rank sharing improves effective expressiveness without additional parameter cost. RaSA also exhibits less catastrophic forgetting than LoRA, with only 0.75% accuracy drop between r=8 and r=32 versus 2.83% for LoRA on the forgetting benchmarks (HellaSwag, WinoGrande, ARC-Challenge).

## Relation to Prior Work

RaSA extends LoRA by introducing partial cross-layer rank sharing, positioning itself at the intersection of two seemingly contradictory observations: (1) LoRA's low-rank constraint limits expressive capacity on hard tasks, and (2) LoRA's parameters exhibit redundancy across layers. Unlike prior parameter-reduction methods (VeRA, Tied-LoRA, ShareLoRA) that share all ranks to minimize parameters, or rank-allocation methods (AdaLoRA, PriLoRA) that dynamically assign per-layer ranks, RaSA keeps the same parameter budget as LoRA but increases effective rank by sharing a subset of k ranks across layers with learnable per-layer weighting. The theoretical analysis builds on the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem to provide a clean reconstruction-error guarantee, while the empirical evaluation follows the protocol established by Biderman et al. (2024) for comparing PEFT methods against full fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RaSA offers a drop-in replacement for LoRA that requires no additional parameters or inference overhead while consistently improving performance on the hardest PEFT benchmarks (code generation, mathematical reasoning), narrowing the gap to full fine-tuning. The finding that a small shared-rank fraction (k ≈ r/8) is optimal—rather than full sharing—provides a concrete, actionable insight that challenges the design choices of VeRA and Tied-LoRA. Limitations include the same-dimensionality assumption across layers, evaluation restricted to two task domains and models up to 70B, and the fact that RaSA still trails full fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the optimal k shifts with model depth, task difficulty, or training data scale, and whether the approach extends naturally to MoE architectures or multi-task adapter serving.
