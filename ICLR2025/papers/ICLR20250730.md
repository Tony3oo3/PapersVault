## Problem

Popular PEFT methods like LoRA are highly sensitive to hyperparameter choices and degrade under extended training due to unbounded weight updates, while bounded alternatives like ETHER achieve robustness but are restricted to rank-1 or rank-4 updates with fixed transformation strength. No existing method simultaneously offers high-rank expressiveness, controllable adaptation strength, and guaranteed bounded distance from pretrained weights.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250730/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained weight matrix W ∈ R^{d×f} and task-specific training data, and produces a set of learnable low-rank matrices B ∈ R^{f×r}, A ∈ R^{r×d}, a diagonal normalization matrix Ξ ∈ R^{r×r}, and a per-layer learnable scalar λ. The adapted weight is W + (λ‖W‖/r)BΞA, which can be merged back into W at inference time with zero additional latency. Unlike LoRA's zero-initialization of B, DeLoRA initializes by subtracting a kaiming-initialized copy of the update from the pretrained weights, since the normalization prevents a trivial zero-start.

## Method

DeLoRA decomposes the LoRA product BA into rank-1 components b_i a_i^⊤ and normalizes each to unit Frobenius norm via a diagonal matrix Ξ with entries 1/(‖b_i‖‖a_i‖), bounding the total update distance to r. A scaling factor λ/r then makes the boundary equal to λ (independent of rank) and learnable per layer, decoupling the angular direction of the update from its magnitude. An additional ‖W‖ factor scales updates proportionally to the pretrained weight norm, mimicking the layer-specific behavior of multiplicative finetuning methods. The approach is derived from two complementary perspectives: as LoRA plus normalization and controllable scaling, and as ETHER+ with a learnable boundary, arbitrary rank, and relaxed trainable matrices replacing the unit-vector constraints. This yields a Frobenius-norm ball constraint on the update that prevents divergence from pretrained weights during prolonged training, a property empirically absent in both LoRA and DoRA.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (RoBERTa-base), DeLoRA achieves an average score of 85.6 with only 0.3M trainable parameters, matching full finetuning (85.6, 125M params) and outperforming all PEFT baselines, with particularly strong gains on CoLA (64.7 vs. 59.7 for LoRA). On subject-driven image generation (Stable Diffusion, rank-16), DeLoRA achieves 0.693 DINO and 0.820 CLIP-I with tuned hyperparameters, slightly surpassing DoRA (0.689/0.819) and LoRA (0.688/0.818). On instruction tuning (LLaMA-2-7B), DeLoRA attains the best average of 41.42 across MMLU, ARC, and TruthfulQA, outperforming DoRA (40.73), LoRA (40.19), and ETHER+ (41.07).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GLUE, DeLoRA (85.6 avg, 0.3M params) matches full finetuning and beats Adapter (85.0), LoRA (84.7), Adapter^FFN (84.7), RED (84.3), LoReFT (84.2), IA3 (83.1), and BitFit (82.3), with the largest margin on CoLA (+5.0 over LoRA, +5.0 over Adapter). On subject-driven image generation, DeLoRA with tuned hyperparameters (0.693 DINO) narrowly edges out DoRA (0.689) and LoRA (0.688), while substantially outperforming ETHER+ (0.666) and OFT (0.652); at default hyperparameters all three high-rank methods (LoRA, DoRA, DeLoRA) are within 0.001 of each other. On instruction tuning, DeLoRA (41.42 avg) wins on ARC (47.70), TruthfulQA-single (29.62), and TruthfulQA-multi (44.14), but loses to ETHER+ on MMLU (44.21 vs. 44.87), yielding the best overall average. The key robustness advantage is that DeLoRA maintains performance at 4× the base learning rate where both LoRA and DoRA degrade, and avoids catastrophic overwriting during prolonged training (beyond ~1400 steps) where LoRA and DoRA weights continue diverging from pretrained values.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeLoRA synthesizes two distinct PEFT lineages: LoRA's additive low-rank updates (Hu et al., 2022) and ETHER's bounded hyperplane-reflection transformations (Bini et al., 2024). The paper derives its method from both perspectives—adding Frobenius-norm normalization and a learnable scaling factor to LoRA's BA matrices, and introducing a controllable boundary plus higher-rank relaxation to ETHER+—ultimately arriving at a unified formulation that normalizes the inner r-dimensional bottleneck space. It also explicitly differentiates from DoRA (Liu et al., 2024a), which similarly decouples angular and magnitude components but operates on the full updated weight matrix's column space rather than on the low-rank update's inner space, yielding different training dynamics and robustness properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of angular learning from adaptation strength via a bounded, learnable Frobenius-norm constraint addresses a practical pain point in PEFT: LoRA's well-documented sensitivity to learning rate and degradation under prolonged training. The key open question is whether the learnable boundary λ truly remains bounded in practice (the paper acknowledges it theoretically permits unbounded Frobenius norm, though empirically divergence does not occur), and whether the weights-norm scaling is the optimal mechanism for handling layer-specific heterogeneity in pretrained models. Performance gains over LoRA and DoRA are modest—DeLoRA matches rather than substantially surpasses them on most benchmarks—so the primary value lies in robustness and interpretability of the update geometry rather than peak accuracy.
