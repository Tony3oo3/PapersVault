## Problem

Regret Matching+ (RM+) and its variants are the workhorse algorithms for solving large-scale poker games, yet virtually nothing is known about their last-iterate convergence. The paper demonstrates empirically that RM+, alternating RM+, and Predictive RM+ may fail to converge even on a simple 3×3 matrix game, and identifies the core analytical obstacle: the regret operator F is neither monotone nor pseudo-monotone, rendering standard variational inequality convergence tools inapplicable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251784/cropped_page9_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithms take a payoff matrix A ∈ R^{d1×d2}, a step size η (constrained to η < 1/L_F for ExRM+ and η ≤ 1/(8L_F) for SPRM+, where L_F = √6‖A‖_op max{d1,d2}), and an initial strategy profile in the simplex. They operate in the clipped positive orthant Z_≥ = Δ_{≥}^{d1} × Δ_{≥}^{d2} rather than the simplex directly, mapping back to strategies via ℓ1-normalization g(z). The convergence metric is the duality gap of the last iterate, and the restarting variants additionally track the per-iterate displacement ‖z^{t+1/2} − z^t‖ as a restart trigger, requiring no knowledge of the metric subregularity constant c.

## Method

The key insight is that although the regret operator F is non-monotone, it satisfies the Minty condition (∃z* such that ⟨F(z), z−z*⟩ ≥ 0 for all z), which is strictly weaker than pseudo-monotonicity. Building on this, the paper proves asymptotic last-iterate convergence of ExRM+ and SPRM+ by establishing a new geometric characterization of limit points: any two limit points must have equal norm and be orthogonal to every Nash equilibrium direction (Lemma 4), which forces the sequence to have at most two limit points and ultimately a unique one (Lemma 5). This yields O(1/√T) best-iterate duality gap bounds, and when combined with a restarting mechanism triggered by halving the per-iterate displacement, the restarted variants RS-ExRM+ and RS-SPRM+ achieve linear last-iterate convergence via the metric subregularity of zero-sum matrix games.

## Results

The paper proves that ExRM+ and SPRM+ enjoy asymptotic last-iterate convergence to a Nash equilibrium (Theorems 2, 5), O(1/√T) best-iterate duality gap convergence (Theorems 3, 6), and linear last-iterate convergence under a restarting mechanism (Theorems 4, 7). In contrast, RM+, alternating RM+, and PRM+ are shown empirically to fail to converge, with duality gaps remaining on the order of 10⁻¹ even after 10⁵ iterations on a simple 3×3 game. On the 3×3 test instance, ExRM+, SPRM+, and alternating PRM+ reach machine precision (~10⁻¹⁰) within 10³ iterations, while RM+, PRM+, and alternating RM+ stagnate. The restarted variants (RS-ExRM+, RS-SPRM+) achieve linear convergence with rate (1−β)ᵗ where β = 1/(3(1+α)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RM+ (Tammelin, 2014) and alternating RM+ (Burch et al., 2019), ExRM+ and SPRM+ are strictly superior: the baselines diverge on a 3×3 game with a unique NE (duality gap stuck at ~10⁻¹ after 10⁵ iterations), while ExRM+ and SPRM+ converge to machine precision within 10³ iterations. Against PRM+ (Farina et al., 2021), the same pattern holds—PRM+ also fails to converge in iterates, whereas ExRM+ and SPRM+ have provable last-iterate convergence. Interestingly, alternating PRM+ (not covered by any convergence theory) empirically outperforms all other algorithms on random 10×15 matrix games, a result the authors flag as an open problem. Against EG (Korpelevich, 1976) and OG (Popov, 1980), ExRM+ and SPRM+ match or slightly outperform on the 3×3 game and Kuhn poker, but EG achieves machine precision faster on Goofspiel. The key advantage of ExRM+/SPRM+ over EG/OG is that they operate on the regret operator F (which is non-monotone), making them applicable to the RM+ framework used in large-scale EFG solving via CFR, whereas EG/OG require monotonicity assumptions that do not hold for the regret operator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the last-iterate convergence literature (Cai et al., 2022; Golowich et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2021) from monotone operators to the non-monotone regret operator arising in Regret Matching+ algorithms. It builds directly on Farina et al. (2023), who introduced ExRM+ and SPRM+ with O(1/T) ergodic convergence, by proving their last-iterate convergence properties. The key conceptual departure is that the regret operator F is neither monotone nor pseudo-monotone, so the paper introduces the Minty condition (strictly weaker than pseudo-monotonicity) and a novel geometric characterization of limit points to establish convergence where standard variational inequality tools fail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RM+ is the backbone of every major poker-solving system (DeepStack, Libratus, CFR+), yet its last-iterate behavior was completely unknown; this paper shows that vanilla RM+ and PRM+ may diverge even on a 3×3 game, while ExRM+ and SPRM+ with a simple restarting mechanism achieve linear last-iterate convergence. The geometric structure of limit points (Lemma 4–5) is a potentially reusable analytical tool for other non-monotone variational inequality settings. Key open questions include extending results to extensive-form games via the CFR framework, obtaining concrete rates for non-restarted ExRM+/SPRM+, and understanding the surprisingly fast empirical convergence of alternating PRM+ which lacks any theoretical guarantee.
