## Problem

Adam remains the default optimizer for language model training despite numerous alternatives being proposed, yet no comprehensive comparison exists across model scales, hyperparameters, and architecture variants. The paper asks whether this dominance is justified and, more fundamentally, what specific components of adaptive optimizers are actually necessary for achieving both performance and hyperparameter stability in autoregressive language modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250102/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The study trains decoder-only transformer models (150m, 300m, 600m, 1.2b non-embedding parameters) on C4 tokenized with the T5 tokenizer, using batch size 256 and sequence length 512, trained for approximately Chinchilla-optimal token counts. The primary output is validation loss as a function of swept hyperparameters (learning rate, momentum β₁, weight decay, warmup, β₂, ε, batch size) across five optimizers and two architecture variants (with/without QK LayerNorm and z-loss). The unusual aspect is that the 'method' is a systematic empirical protocol: 1D hyperparameter sweeps around per-optimizer optimal learning rates, with x-axes shifted to align optimal learning rates for cross-optimizer comparison.

## Method

The paper introduces Adalayer, a layer-wise variant of Adam that stores a single scalar second-moment estimate per layer (the average of the per-parameter second moments) rather than a full diagonal matrix, and Adalayer* which additionally treats each logit's incoming weight vector as its own block. Through systematic ablations with Adalayer*, the paper shows that adaptivity is only critically needed on the last layer (per-logit) and LayerNorm parameters: training all other matrix layers with plain SGD (or even with fixed learning-rate ratios frozen from initialization) while applying Adalayer* only to the last layer and LayerNorm recovers nearly all of Adam's performance and learning-rate stability. The paper further demonstrates that Signum (signed momentum, a special case of Lion with β₁=β₂) recovers Adam's performance and stability, supporting the hypothesis that the sign component of the update is the primary mechanism distinguishing adaptive optimizers from SGD. Across all experiments, the key finding is that SGD, Adam, Adafactor, Lion, and Signum are comparable in both optimal performance and hyperparameter stability, with the choice of optimizer reducible to practical considerations like memory footprint.

## Results

Across 150m, 300m, 600m, and 1.2b parameter decoder-only transformers trained on C4 (T5 tokenizer, batch size 256, sequence length 512, ~20× Chinchilla-optimal tokens), Adam, Adafactor, Lion, and Signum all achieve comparable validation loss at their respective optimal learning rates and exhibit similar stability across learning rate sweeps. SGD is consistently worse in both peak performance and learning-rate robustness. Optimal learning rates are ~3.16e-3 (150m) to 1e-3 (300m–1.2b) for Adam/Adafactor and ~3.16e-4 (150m–600m) to 1e-4 (1.2b) for Lion/Signum, roughly an order of magnitude lower. The paper further shows that a layer-wise variant (Adalayer*) recovers most of Adam's performance, and that adaptivity is critically needed only on the last-layer (logit) weights and LayerNorm parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central finding is that Adam, Adafactor (with momentum), Lion, and Signum are statistically indistinguishable in both optimal validation loss and learning-rate stability across all four model scales and two architecture variants (with/without QK-norm and z-loss); no single optimizer emerges as a clear winner. SGD is the sole outlier, showing substantially worse peak loss and much narrower stable learning-rate range. Signum (signed momentum, a special case of Lion with β₁=β₂) recovers Adam's performance and stability, supporting the hypothesis that Adam's key advantage over SGD is its resemblance to sign-descent rather than its full per-parameter preconditioning. Sophia (ρ=0) does not outperform Signum and is therefore excluded from main comparisons. AdaSGD, used as a global-adaptive baseline for matrix parameters, is marginally better than SGD but still lacks learning-rate stability, confirming that a single adaptive scalar is insufficient without per-layer or per-logit adaptivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimizer comparison literature from masked language modeling at a single scale (Kaddour et al., 2024) to autoregressive language modeling across four model sizes (150m–1.2b) and multiple hyperparameters, and builds on Wortsman et al. (2024)'s study of Adam's learning-rate stability by generalizing it to SGD, Lion, Adafactor, and Signum. The Adalayer optimizer introduced here is a direct coarsening of Adam's per-parameter preconditioning to per-layer, and is explicitly identified as a special case of BAGM (Zheng & Kwok, 2019). The analysis of Signum's equivalence to Adam draws on the theoretical connection established by Balles & Hennig (2018) and the empirical findings of Kunstner et al. (2023) that sign-descent is the key mechanism distinguishing Adam from SGD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that Adam, Lion, Adafactor, and Signum are essentially interchangeable in both performance and hyperparameter stability across scales challenges the field's default assumption that Adam is uniquely suited for language model training, and redirects practitioner attention to memory and implementation considerations. More fundamentally, the discovery that adaptivity is concentrated almost entirely in the last layer and LayerNorm parameters opens a concrete path toward memory-efficient optimizers that apply full per-parameter preconditioning only where it matters. Key limitations include the restriction to a single dataset (C4), one-dimensional hyperparameter sweeps, fixed batch size, and autoregressive-only evaluation; open questions include why fixed initialization-time ratios fail for other layers and whether non-diagonal preconditioning methods can close the remaining small performance gap.
