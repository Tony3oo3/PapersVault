## Problem

Grokking — sudden generalization after prolonged overfitting — has been widely observed but its two defining features remain unexplained: why generalization is delayed, and why it typically fails to occur without regularization such as weight decay. This paper identifies the missing mechanism: without regularization, grokking tasks push models to the edge of numerical stability where floating-point absorption errors in the Softmax (Softmax Collapse) zero out gradients and halt learning, while the delay itself is caused by gradients aligning with a logit-scaling direction (Naive Loss Minimization) that reduces loss without changing predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252925/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The primary experiments use modular arithmetic tasks (addition, multiplication, subtraction mod 113) where two one-hot encoded integers form a 226-dimensional input vector predicting a class label among 113 possible outputs; the one-hot representation is critical because it makes overfitting easy and triggers the grokking dynamics (replacing it with compact 14-bit binary vectors eliminates grokking entirely). Sparse Parity uses binary vectors of length n with k active bits, and MNIST uses 28×28 grayscale images. Validation in realistic settings extends to CIFAR-10/100 (32×32 RGB), ImageNet-1k (224×224 RGB), and WikiText-103 language modeling with GPT2-Small.

## Method

The paper introduces two interventions targeting the identified failure modes. StableMax replaces the exponential in Softmax with a sub-exponential ramp function s(x) = x+1 for x≥0 and 1/(1−x) for x<0, preventing the extreme value ranges that cause floating-point absorption errors; this single change converts complete overfitting into grokking without any regularization. ⊥Grad is an optimizer that projects the gradient onto the hyperplane orthogonal to the current weight vector (removing the NLM component), so updates only change the model's decision boundary rather than scaling logits; this eliminates the overfitting phase entirely and achieves generalization faster than even tuned weight decay. Theoretically, the paper shows that for positively homogeneous networks (ReLU MLPs, transformers without bias), the weight direction itself is an NLM direction beyond 100% training accuracy, explaining why gradients align with weights and why weight decay — which pulls back along this same direction — is the most reliable grokking inducer.

## Results

On the primary grokking task (modular addition mod 113, 40% train split), StableMax enables grokking without any regularization where standard Softmax CE gets stuck at random-level test accuracy, and ⊥Grad eliminates the overfitting phase entirely, reaching 100% test accuracy within 400 iterations. On realistic benchmarks (Table 1), ⊥Grad achieves 87.22% on CIFAR-10, 62.69% on CIFAR-100, 68.95% on ImageNet-1k, and 59.64% Top-5 on WikiText-103, while StableMax CE achieves 87.01%, 60.63%, 65.87%, and 51.85% respectively. The baseline Softmax CE with AdamW achieves 87.17%, 59.98%, 69.33%, and 60.48% on the same benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparisons are against standard Softmax CE with AdamW (no weight decay) and AdamW with weight decay on grokking tasks, and against standard SGD/AdamW on realistic benchmarks. On grokking tasks, ⊥Grad outperforms the best tuned weight decay setting (Fig. 15b), reaching 100% train and test accuracy almost simultaneously without any hyperparameter tuning, whereas weight decay models overfit first before grokking. StableMax enables grokking in settings (e.g., 40% modular addition) where the baseline Softmax CE gets stuck at random-level performance indefinitely. On realistic benchmarks, ⊥Grad matches or slightly exceeds the baseline on CIFAR-10 (+0.05%) and CIFAR-100 (+2.71%), but underperforms on ImageNet-1k (−0.38%) and WikiText-103 (−0.84%). StableMax CE underperforms the baseline on ImageNet-1k (−3.46%) and WikiText-103 (−8.63%), suggesting the sub-exponential activation introduces a mild inductive bias that hurts in large-scale settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the grokking literature (Power et al. 2022; Nanda et al. 2023; Barak et al. 2022) and the theory of gradient alignment in homogeneous networks (Lyu & Li 2020; Ji & Telgarsky 2020), but departs by identifying a concrete numerical mechanism—floating-point absorption errors in Softmax (Softmax Collapse)—as the reason grokking fails without regularization. It reframes the known phenomenon of gradient alignment with weight direction beyond 100% training accuracy as Naive Loss Minimization (NLM), connecting it to a practical failure mode rather than merely a margin-maximization property. The paper also unifies explanations for why weight decay, MSE loss on shallow networks, and logit scaling all induce grokking, positioning them as interventions that mitigate NLM or SC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a single mechanistic explanation (SC caused by NLM) for multiple previously disconnected observations in the grokking literature: the need for regularization, the delayed onset of generalization, and the effectiveness of various grokking-inducing methods. The proposed interventions (StableMax, ⊥Grad) are simple and well-validated across architectures and tasks, but their practical adoption is limited since weight decay already works in most settings. Open questions include extending the NLM analysis to quasi-homogeneous models with skip connections, understanding the effective learning rate dynamics that may explain the 'sudden' nature of grokking, and clarifying the relationship between SC and the slingshot mechanism observed with adaptive optimizers.
