## Problem

Neural collapse (NC), the phenomenon where features cluster into a simplex equiangular tight frame in the penultimate layer, has been extensively studied in image classification but remains unexplored in vision-based control. Continuous control tasks are regression problems without natural class labels, and the action decoder is a highly nonlinear module (e.g., diffusion model), violating the linear-classifier assumption underlying NC theory. The paper asks whether a control-oriented law of clustering still emerges in the visual representation space and, if so, whether it can be converted from a passive observation into an active pretraining tool to improve performance under limited demonstrations.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a sequence of K RGB images (4 frames for Lunar Lander; variable-length sequences for Planar Pushing and Block Stacking) and produces either a single discrete action from a 4-way set (Lunar Lander) or a horizon of H continuous control signals (pushing, stacking). At test time the policy operates in a receding-horizon fashion: only the first predicted control is executed before re-predicting from the new observation. For the NC pretraining stage, ground-truth relative pose (x, y, θ) between the object and target is required to assign each sample to one of 8 relative-pose-orthant (REPO) classes; this information is not used at test time.

## Method

The paper introduces control-oriented classification by partitioning the 3D relative-pose space (x, y, θ) into 8 orthants (REPOs), yielding semantically interpretable class labels for continuous control samples. Using these labels, the authors compute three NC metrics—class-distance-normalized variance (CDNV), standard deviation of class-mean norms (STDNorm), and standard deviation of pairwise class-mean angles (STDAngle)—and show they consistently decrease during behavior-cloning training across Lunar Lander, Planar Pushing, and Block Stacking, confirming prevalent control-oriented clustering. The key algorithmic contribution is an NC-regularized pretraining stage: the vision encoder (ResNet-18 or DINOv2) is first trained for 50 epochs to minimize the composite loss 0.1·CDNV + 10·(STDNorm + STDAngle), explicitly encouraging the latent features to form a simplex-ETF geometry aligned with REPO classes. The pretrained encoder is then finetuned end-to-end with a diffusion-model action decoder for 50 additional epochs on the same 100 expert demonstrations. This two-stage procedure yields 10–35% test-time improvement over direct end-to-end training in simulation and is validated on a real Franka Panda planar-pushing setup, where the NC-pretrained policy succeeds on 8/10 (clean) and 9/10 (visually cluttered) trials versus 5/10 and 6/10 for the baseline.

## Results

On four planar pushing domains (letters R, O, B, T), NC-pretrained ResNet+DM achieves 0.360, 0.720, 0.504, and 0.347 test scores versus 0.202, 0.423, 0.385, and 0.241 for the baseline, yielding 10–35% relative improvements with only 100 expert demonstrations. In real-world Franka Panda experiments, the NC-pretrained policy succeeds 8/10 on clean backgrounds (vs. 5/10 baseline) and 9/10 on visually complex backgrounds (vs. 6/10 baseline). With 500 demonstrations in simulation, DINOv2+DM reaches ~70% overlap score on push-T, while NC metrics (CDNV, STDNorm, STDAngle) consistently decrease toward zero across all tasks, confirming prevalent control-oriented clustering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the standard end-to-end behavior cloning baseline (ResNet+DM trained for 100 epochs) and the NC-pretrained variant (ResNet pretrained for 50 epochs minimizing NC metrics, then jointly trained with DM for 50 epochs). NC pretraining wins on all four pushing domains, with the largest gain on letter O (0.423→0.720, +70% relative) and the smallest on letter B (0.385→0.504, +31% relative). The action-based classification variant slightly outperforms goal-based on R and O but underperforms on B, suggesting goal-based REPOs capture a stronger clustering signal. In real-world validation, NC pretraining improves success rate from 5/10 to 8/10 (clean, 100 demos) and from 6/10 to 9/10 (complex, 200 demos). The paper also shows that DINOv2+DM outperforms ResNet+DM in simulation (~70% vs. ~63%), and that LSTM decoders substantially underperform diffusion model decoders, consistent with prior findings from Chi et al. (2023).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural collapse (NC) framework of Papyan et al. (2020) from image classification to vision-based control, where no natural class labels exist for continuous action outputs. It departs from prior NC work in two key ways: (1) it introduces 'control-oriented' classes (Relative Pose Orthants, or REPOs) to make NC measurable in regression-like control tasks, and (2) it repurposes NC metrics (CDNV, STDNorm, STDAngle) as a pretraining loss for the vision encoder rather than merely an analysis tool. The experimental pipeline builds on Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023) for the action decoder and behavior cloning as the training paradigm, while the NC-as-regularization idea connects to recent work using NC to improve generalization and robustness in classification (Bonifazi et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that NC is not an artifact of classification supervision but a more general geometric law that emerges whenever a representation space is shaped by a discrete or quasi-discrete decision structure, broadening the theoretical scope of NC beyond its original setting. The practical NC-pretraining recipe (10–35% improvement with only 100 demonstrations, validated on a real Franka Panda arm) offers a concrete data-efficient tool for vision-based control. However, the method requires ground-truth relative poses during training, which limits scalability to multi-object, deformable, or high-dimensional manipulation tasks. Open questions include a theoretical account of why control-oriented REPOs induce NC (as opposed to, e.g., k-means on actions), extension to RL-trained policies, and whether the phenomenon persists in more complex, multi-step manipulation.
