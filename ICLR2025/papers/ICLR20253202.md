## Problem

Benign overfitting—where over-parameterized models interpolate training noise yet generalize well—has been well understood only in the in-distribution setting. Under out-of-distribution (OOD) covariate shift, prior theoretical results either restrict the target covariance to special forms (e.g., simultaneously diagonal with the source) or yield non-vanishing excess risk bounds. This paper provides the first vanishing, non-asymptotic excess risk guarantee for over-parameterized ridge regression under general covariate shift, identifying precisely which source-target covariance quantities govern OOD generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. training pairs (x_i, y_i) drawn from a source distribution P_S whose covariance Σ_S is diagonal with k large eigenvalues (major directions) and d−k small eigenvalues (minor directions), and evaluates on an arbitrary target distribution P_T with finite second moment Σ_T (not necessarily diagonal). The output is a parameter estimate β̂ (ridge or PCR) together with a high-probability excess risk bound expressed multiplicatively over the in-distribution bound, governed by four key quantities: tr[T]/k and ‖T‖ (shift in major directions) and tr[U]/tr[V] and nr_k^{-1}‖Σ_{T,−k}‖/‖Σ_{S,−k}‖ (shift in minor directions). The bound is sharp: it recovers Tsigler & Bartlett (2023) when Σ_S = Σ_T and Ge et al. (2024) when minor components vanish.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a sharp, instance-dependent excess risk bound (Theorem 2) for over-parameterized ridge regression under general covariate shift, derived via a block-matrix decomposition of the Gram matrix that separates major and minor directions. The key insight is that benign overfitting persists under OOD covariate shift as long as the overall magnitude of the target's minor-direction covariance is bounded relative to the source's, without requiring any spectral alignment between source and target minor eigenvalues—only the source needs a high effective rank in the minor directions. When the target exhibits large variance in minor directions (case 2), the paper proves a matching lower bound showing ridge regression is stuck at the slow O(1/√n) rate (Theorem 4), and then shows that Principal Component Regression, which explicitly projects onto the estimated major subspace before regressing, achieves the fast O(1/n) rate (Theorem 5) without requiring the source minor directions to be high-dimensional. The analysis relies on sub-Gaussian concentration inequalities, condition-number assumptions on the minor-direction Gram matrix (CondNum), and perturbation bounds for subspace estimation.

## Results

The paper establishes that over-parameterized ridge regression achieves an O(1/n) excess risk rate under covariate shift when the target covariance's minor-direction magnitude is controlled (Theorem 2), recovering the in-distribution benign overfitting bound of Tsigler & Bartlett (2023) as a special case. When the target covariance has large eigenvalues in the minor directions (e.g., Σ_T = I_d), ridge regression is lower-bounded by the slow O(1/√n) rate for any choice of regularization λ (Theorem 4). In contrast, Principal Component Regression (PCR) achieves the fast O(1/n) rate in the same adversarial instance (Theorem 5). Simulation experiments on multivariate Gaussian data confirm these rates: PCR exhibits a log-log slope of −0.99 (≈ O(1/n)), while the best ridge configuration (λ = n^0.75) achieves a slope of only −0.48 (≈ O(1/√n)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external empirical baselines but instead positions its theoretical contributions relative to prior work. Theorem 2 recovers the sharp in-distribution bound of Tsigler & Bartlett (2023) when source and target distributions match, and recovers the O(tr[T]/n) rate of Ge et al. (2024) for under-parameterized regression when minor components vanish. Against prior OOD results, the paper's bound is the first vanishing non-asymptotic excess risk bound for general covariate shift, whereas Hao et al. (2024) and Kausik et al. (2024) only provide non-vanishing bounds, and Mallinar et al. (2024) require simultaneously diagonal source and target covariances with a looser bias term. The key empirical comparison is between ridge regression and PCR under large minor-direction shift: PCR achieves O(1/n) (slope −0.99) while ridge regression is stuck at O(1/√n) (slope −0.48), demonstrating PCR's advantage when the target distribution has significant variance outside the source's major subspace.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate theoretical threads: benign overfitting in over-parameterized linear models (Bartlett et al. 2020; Tsigler & Bartlett 2023), which was studied only in-distribution, and OOD generalization under covariate shift (Ge et al. 2024; Tripuraneni et al. 2021a; Hao et al. 2024; Mallinar et al. 2024), which was studied only for under-parameterized models or with restrictive target covariance assumptions yielding non-vanishing bounds. The paper's Theorem 2 is a genuine unification: it provably recovers Tsigler & Bartlett's sharp in-distribution bound when source equals target, and Ge et al.'s Fisher-information-based bound for under-parameterized regression when minor components vanish. The key departure from prior OOD work is that the target covariance is allowed to be fully arbitrary (only finite second moment required), whereas prior results imposed simultaneous diagonalizability or specific shift structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first vanishing, non-asymptotic excess risk bound for over-parameterized ridge regression under general covariate shift, filling a clear gap between in-distribution benign overfitting theory and OOD generalization theory. The conceptual insight that only the overall magnitude of the target's minor components matters (not their spectral structure) for benign overfitting is a clean and useful characterization that simplifies the conditions practitioners need to verify. The PCR-versus-ridge comparison under large minor-direction shifts (O(1/n) vs. O(1/√n)) provides a concrete algorithmic recommendation. Open questions include deriving matching general lower bounds for ridge regression, extending the analysis beyond linear models to non-linear architectures, and understanding whether the source/target asymmetry (high effective rank needed for source, only small magnitude for target) persists in more complex settings.
