## Problem

Fine-tuning pre-trained diffusion models to new domains via standard classifier-free guidance (CFG) suffers from two critical issues: catastrophic forgetting erodes valuable pre-trained knowledge, and the jointly trained unconditional guiding model exhibits poor fitness in low-data regimes, producing high-variance out-of-distribution samples that compound sampling errors. No existing transfer strategy effectively leverages the intrinsic structure of diffusion models to guide generation toward the target domain while preserving the pre-trained distribution.

## Input / Output

The method operates at 256×256 resolution, taking random Gaussian noise plus a class label (for DiT-based experiments) or a text prompt (for SDXL+LoRA experiments) as input, and producing synthetic images via iterative denoising. Each sampling step requires two forward passes—one through the fine-tuned model (conditional on the target domain) and one through the frozen pre-trained model (unconditional)—with the fine-tuned output extrapolated from the pre-trained output by a scalar guidance factor w^DoG (typically 1.4–2.0). No additional training is needed to apply DoG to existing CFG-style fine-tuned models; one simply substitutes the unconditional component with the original pre-trained checkpoint.

## Method

Domain Guidance (DoG) reframes transfer learning as domain-conditional generation: the fine-tuned model captures p(x|D^tgt) while the frozen pre-trained model serves as the unconditional reference p(x), and at each denoising step the fine-tuned noise prediction is extrapolated away from the pre-trained prediction via ε^DoG = ε_θ(x|D^tgt) + (w^DoG−1)(ε_θ(x|D^tgt) − ε_θ0(x)). This eliminates the need for dropout-based unconditional training (setting δ=0), removing the multi-task conflict that degrades the CFG guiding model. Theoretically, DoG decomposes into standard CFG plus an additional classifier-guidance term ∇log p(D^tgt|x_t) that explicitly suppresses sampling from out-of-distribution regions, and a bound on the marginal estimation error (≤1/√N) shows why the pre-trained model outperforms a poorly fit fine-tuned unconditional model in low-data settings. Empirically, DoG achieves 19.6% average FID and 23.4% FD_DINOv2 improvements over CFG across seven fine-grained benchmarks, and integrates seamlessly with LoRA-based fine-tuning of SDXL without retraining.

## Results

On seven fine-grained transfer benchmarks using pre-trained DiT-XL/2, Domain Guidance (DoG) achieves an average FID of 11.55, a 19.59% relative improvement over standard fine-tuning with classifier-free guidance (CFG, 13.69). On the complementary FD_DINOv2 metric, DoG reaches 292.62 versus CFG's 363.58, a 23.4% relative improvement. The largest gains appear on CUB Bird (FID 3.52 vs. 5.37) and Stanford Cars (FID 4.38 vs. 6.32), while even out-of-distribution datasets like DF-20M (FID 12.22 vs. 15.29) and ArtBench-10 (FID 16.76 vs. 19.94) show consistent improvements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole baseline is standard fine-tuning with classifier-free guidance (CFG), the de facto standard in modern diffusion model transfer. DoG outperforms CFG on all seven datasets for both FID and FD_DINOv2, with no dataset showing a regression. The most substantial FID gains are on CUB Bird (3.52 vs. 5.37, 34.45% improvement) and Stanford Cars (4.38 vs. 6.32, 30.70%), where the fine-grained nature of the target domain amplifies the benefit of pre-trained knowledge. On the more challenging out-of-distribution benchmarks DF-20M (no ImageNet overlap) and ArtBench-10 (completely distinct distribution), DoG still achieves 20.08% and 15.95% FID improvements respectively, demonstrating robustness. The ablation in Table 4 further shows that DoG's advantage is not merely from additional training steps: under an equal 24,000-step budget, removing the unconditional dropout (as DoG does) yields better FID than the standard CFG-style training, confirming the methodological contribution beyond compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classifier-free guidance (CFG) mechanism of Ho & Salimans (2022) for a fundamentally different purpose: instead of using a jointly-trained unconditional branch to guide class-conditional generation, DoG substitutes the original pre-trained model as the unconditional guiding component to steer sampling toward a target domain. The theoretical analysis (Proposition 1) connects DoG to classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021), showing it is equivalent to CFG augmented with a domain-likelihood term. The paper explicitly distinguishes itself from autoguidance (Karras et al., 2024a), arguing that the pre-trained model is not a 'bad version' of the fine-tuned model but rather a knowledge-rich reference from a different distribution, and positions DoG within the broader PEFT/transfer-learning literature (DiffFit, LoRA) as a complementary inference-time technique rather than a training-time alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that any existing CFG-style fine-tuned model can adopt DoG at inference time by simply swapping the unconditional forward pass to the pre-trained checkpoint, requiring zero additional training. The consistent ~20% FID and ~23% FD_DINOv2 improvements across seven diverse benchmarks (including out-of-distribution cases like DF-20M and ArtBench) demonstrate robustness. Open questions include whether compositional guidance (multiple domain signals) or a single unified pre-trained model can serve as a universal guiding component for arbitrary downstream tasks, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation. A limitation is that the guidance weight, while in a narrow range (1.4–2), still requires per-dataset tuning, and the method has been validated primarily on DiT and SDXL architectures.
