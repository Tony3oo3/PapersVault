## Problem

Vision Language Models (VLMs) remain vulnerable to adversarial visual inputs that bypass safety mechanisms originally aligned on discrete textual token embeddings, because continuous visual embeddings act as outliers in the LLM backbone's embedding space. Existing defenses are either resource-intensive fine-tuning approaches that compromise general capabilities, or inference-time methods that overlook visual input safety and produce unhelpful generic refusals when unsafe behavior is detected.

## Input / Output

ETA takes a standard image-text pair (an RGB image processed through a CLIP-based vision tower and a textual user query) and produces a single safe, helpful text response. The method is entirely plug-and-play at inference time, requiring no additional training data or parameter updates. The image is consumed twice: once by the VLM backbone for generation and once by a separate CLIP model for both pre-generation safety scoring and utility-guided sentence selection during alignment.

## Method

ETA is a two-phase inference-time framework. In the Evaluating phase, a pre-generation CLIP score measures semantic similarity between the input image and a safety prompt (flagging unsafe images above threshold τ_pre=0.16), while a post-generation textual reward model (ArmoRM-Llama3-8B) with a safety-specific input format scores the generated response (flagging unsafe outputs below τ_post=0.06); alignment is triggered only when both evaluators concur. In the Aligning phase, shallow alignment pre-fills an interference prefix ('As an AI assistant,') to shift the autoregressive output distribution toward safe style, and deep alignment performs sentence-level best-of-N (N=5) searching where each candidate sentence is scored by a weighted combination of CLIP utility (relevance to the input image) and the RM safety score, with α=0 for the first sentence (safety-only) and α=1/i for subsequent sentences to balance safety against image-grounded helpfulness.

## Results

ETA reduces the Unsafe Response Rate (USR) by 87.5% on cross-modality adversarial attacks (LLaVA-1.5-13B: 100.00 → 12.50) and by 63.5% on FigStep suffix attacks (LLaVA-1.5-7B: 62.00 → 22.60), while achieving 96.6% win-tie rate in GPT-4-Turbo helpfulness evaluation against the vanilla VLM. General capabilities are preserved: MME Perception stays at 1506.13 (vs. 1505.88 baseline) and MMBench at 64.69 (vs. 64.60). Inference overhead is minimal at only +0.05 s per response on MMBench, compared to +0.25 s for ECSO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ECSO (Gou et al., 2024), ETA dominates on all safety benchmarks. The gap is most dramatic under adversarial attacks: on cross-modality attacks, ECSO barely reduces USR (97.50 → 95.00 for LLaVA-1.5-7B, a mere 2.5% reduction) while ETA cuts it to 22.50 (76.9% reduction); on FigStep suffix attacks, ECSO achieves 59.00 vs. ETA's 22.60. On non-adversarial benchmarks like SPA-VL Harm, ETA also leads (16.98 vs. 23.40 for LLaVA-1.5-7B). Against the fine-tuning baselines VLGuard-Posthoc-LoRA and Mixed-LoRA (Zong et al., 2024), ETA wins decisively on helpfulness: it achieves 96.6% win-tie vs. the vanilla VLM, 54.6% vs. Posthoc-LoRA, and 56.7% vs. Mixed-LoRA in GPT-4-Turbo evaluation. On general capabilities, ETA preserves the backbone's performance (MMEP 1506.13, MMB 64.69) while Posthoc-LoRA degrades MMEP by 85.22 points and Mixed-LoRA drops MME Cognition by 90.72 points. ETA also adds only 0.05 s inference overhead per response versus 0.25 s for ECSO on MMBench.

## Relation to Prior Work

ETA builds on the interference prefix technique from Qi et al. (2024b), which showed that pre-filling a few safety-oriented tokens can activate LLM safety mechanisms, and extends it to the multimodal setting where visual inputs bypass those mechanisms. It departs from prior inference-time VLM defenses like ECSO (which converts images to text captions) and Adashield (which requires a trained prompt pool) by instead using an external CLIP-based evaluator for visual inputs and a textual reward model for outputs, combined with sentence-level best-of-N search. The conceptual departure from fine-tuning-based alignment (RLHF, LoRA) is that ETA requires no parameter updates, positioning it as a plug-and-play wrapper rather than a retraining pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLMs are deployed in consumer-facing applications, the demonstrated vulnerability to cross-modality adversarial attacks (where a single noise image plus a text suffix achieves ~100% attack success) is a practical safety gap that fine-tuning alone does not close. ETA's ability to reduce USR by 87.5% on such attacks while preserving general benchmark performance (near-zero degradation on MME, MMB, VQA) makes it a deployable safeguard without the cost of retraining. Open questions include whether the CLIP-based pre-generation evaluator generalizes to video or 3D inputs, whether the fixed thresholds (τ_pre=0.16, τ_post=0.06) transfer across model families without retuning, and whether the sentence-level best-of-N overhead becomes prohibitive for long-form generation or real-time interactive settings.
