## Problem

Prior work on LLM robustness has focused almost exclusively on surface structure sensitivity—showing that superficial input changes degrade performance—while largely overlooking whether LLMs also genuinely comprehend deep structure (core semantics). This one-sided analysis cannot distinguish a model that truly understands problem semantics from one that merely memorizes surface patterns, leaving the fundamental question of whether LLMs are 'deep thinkers' or 'surface structure learners' unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251969/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a question-answer dataset, a target LLM, and an intervention strategy (Mask for tasks with explicit core-semantic tokens like numbers/operators, or Rephrase via an LLM agent for tasks with diffuse semantics). It operates exclusively on correctly-answered samples, generating paired intervention data where deep structure is altered (T=1) or only surface structure is altered (T=0). Outputs are ADCE and AICE scores bounded in [-1,1], computed via indicator functions (output-changed vs. output-unchanged) rather than numerical differencing, making the framework applicable to any output format including free-form text, multiple choice, and binary labels.

## Method

The paper formulates deep structure comprehension as the direct causal effect (DCE) and surface structure comprehension as the indirect causal effect (ICE) within a parallel multiple mediator causal graph (x → d → Y and x → s → Y). Because the original DCE and ICE are non-estimable—deep structure cannot be altered without simultaneously changing surface structure, and LLM outputs lack numerical form—the authors derive approximated surrogates: ADCE = TE − AICE, where TE measures the fraction of outputs that change when both structures are intervened, and AICE measures the fraction that change when only surface structure is intervened (with deep structure preserved). Intervention data is generated via task-specific strategies (Masking core-semantic tokens or LLM-agent-driven minimal rephrasing) designed to maximize surface-structure similarity between the TE and AICE conditions, thereby isolating the deep structure contribution. Theoretically, the authors prove ADCE equals a weighted combination of probability of necessity (PN) and probability of sufficiency (PS), establishing it as a bidirectional evaluation that captures both whether deep structure changes are sufficient to alter outputs and whether they are necessary—offering a more comprehensive assessment than accuracy alone, which can be inflated by spurious surface-structure correlations.

## Results

Across 12 LLMs and 5 tasks, most models exhibit positive ADCE values (ranging in [-1,1]), confirming deep structure comprehension, in stark contrast to the random-weight Llama-3-70b baseline which yields ADCE = 0. A strong linear correlation (R² > 0.7) is observed between accuracy and ADCE, with the regression slope β varying by task complexity (smaller β for 2-Digit Multiplication and Word Unscrambling; larger β for GSM8k, Analytic Entailment, and CommonsenseQA). In the spurious correlation experiment on CivilComments, as spurious correlation strength increases in the majority group, accuracy remains misleadingly high while ADCE declines, demonstrating ADCE's sensitivity to spurious reliance that accuracy misses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing evaluation methods in a head-to-head table; instead, it positions ADCE against the standard accuracy metric and a random-weight baseline. The random-weight Llama-3-70b (same architecture, random initialization) produces ADCE ≈ 0, validating that positive ADCE in trained models reflects genuine deep structure dependence rather than estimation artifacts. Comparing ADCE to AICE across model families reveals that closed-source models (GPT-3.5-Turbo, GPT-4o, Claude-3-Sonnet, Claude-3.5-Sonnet) consistently show δ_ADCE > δ_AICE, indicating stronger reliance on deep structure, while open-source models (Llama-2-7B/13B/70B, Llama-3-8B/70B, Mistral-7B, Mixtral-8x7B/8x22B) show δ_AICE closer to or exceeding δ_ADCE at smaller scales, with the gap narrowing as model size grows. In the spurious correlation case study (Section 4.5), accuracy fails to distinguish between genuine deep structure use and spurious attribute reliance (staying high in the majority group), whereas ADCE drops as spurious correlation increases, confirming ADCE's superiority as a diagnostic metric over accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal framework for LLM robustness analysis initiated by Stolfo et al. (2022) and the broader causal mediation analysis literature (Imai et al., 2010; Hicks & Tingley, 2011) to a new setting: simultaneously quantifying deep and surface structure comprehension in LLMs. Prior work on LLM surface sensitivity (Jiang et al., 2024b; Hooda et al., 2024; González & Nori, 2024) demonstrated fragility to superficial changes but did not assess whether deep structure comprehension coexists with that fragility. The key departure is formulating deep structure comprehension as a direct causal effect (DCE) and surface structure comprehension as an indirect causal effect (ICE), then proposing estimable surrogates (ADCE/AICE) that overcome the non-estimability of the original quantities, and proving ADCE captures both probability of necessity and probability of sufficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, task-agnostic evaluation metric (ADCE) that goes beyond accuracy to measure whether LLM outputs genuinely depend on core semantics, which is practically important as accuracy can be inflated by spurious correlations. The finding that closed-source models (GPT, Claude) rely more on deep structure while open-source models (Llama) are more surface-sensitive—though this sensitivity diminishes with scale—offers actionable insight for model selection and training. Open questions include whether the ADCE approximation holds under more aggressive interventions, whether the intervention strategies generalize to modalities beyond text, and whether the closed-vs-open-source distinction persists as open-source models improve. The reliance on Claude-3.5-Sonnet as a rephrasing agent for the Rephrase strategy introduces a potential circularity that limits full independence of the evaluation.
