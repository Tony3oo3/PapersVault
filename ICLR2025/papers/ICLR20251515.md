## Problem

Existing generative image compression methods train separate models for each target bitrate, incurring high computational cost and deployment complexity, while variable-rate and progressive methods support only a limited bitrate range and often rely on MSE-based distortion that yields implausible reconstructions at low rates. No prior method achieves fine-grained, continuous bitrate control across a broad spectrum with a single unified model while preserving high perceptual fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251515/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image of arbitrary resolution (trained on 256×256 crops from OpenImages) and a set of granularity allocation ratios (r1, r2, r3) specifying the proportion of patches assigned to fine (4×4), medium (8×8), and coarse (16×16) representations. It produces a compressed bitstream consisting of variable-length VQ-indices (Huffman-coded using a pre-computed frequency table) and binary spatial masks, and a reconstructed image. The bitrate is directly and continuously controlled by the granularity ratios at inference time without any retraining or model switching, with adjustments as fine as 0.001 bpp.

## Method

Control-GIC builds on a VQGAN/MoVQ architecture and introduces three key components. A granularity-informed encoder computes non-parametric spatial entropy for each local patch, sorts patches by entropy, and assigns them to one of three granularity levels according to user-specified ratios; each patch is then quantized to a VQ-index from a 1024-entry codebook (d=4), producing spatially variable-length codes. A non-parametric statistical entropy coding module uses index frequency statistics accumulated over the training dataset to construct a fixed Huffman codebook, avoiding per-image codebook transmission overhead. A probabilistic conditional decoder reconstructs features by formalizing the upsampling process as conditional probability: coarse-grained features serve as the initial decoder input, while medium- and fine-grained representations are injected as conditions at successive decoder layers to correct information loss from nearest-neighbor upsampling. Training uses a combined VQVAE loss (MSE + LPIPS + quantization commitment) and a patch-based GAN loss, with a single set of weights supporting all bitrates at inference.

## Results

On Kodak (Kodim22), Control-GIC achieves 0.054 LPIPS at 0.258 bpp, substantially outperforming generative baselines HiFiC (0.064 LPIPS at 0.309 bpp) and CDC (0.096 LPIPS at 0.314 bpp) while using ~17% fewer bits. In terms of efficiency, Control-GIC is 7× faster in encoding and 3× faster in decoding than MS-ILLM, and 4×/1.5× faster than MRIC, while achieving comparable BD-rate savings. The method supports fine-grained bitrate control with precision as small as 0.001 bpp (e.g., 0.4171 → 0.4172). At extremely low bitrates (<0.05 bpp), Control-GIC achieves 0.115 LPIPS at 0.0381 bpp on Kodak, outperforming Mao et al. (0.136 LPIPS at 0.0391 bpp).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against generative compression methods, Control-GIC achieves the best LPIPS (0.054) at a lower bitrate than HiFiC (0.064 at 0.309 bpp) and CDC (0.096 at 0.314 bpp), while matching or approaching the performance of MRIC and MS-ILLM which are each trained separately for specific R-D points. Compared to variable-rate and progressive methods (SCR, CTC), Control-GIC achieves dramatically lower LPIPS (0.054 vs. 0.234 and 0.189 respectively) at comparable or lower bitrates, and supports finer bitrate granularity (0.001 bpp steps vs. discrete quality levels). Against classical NIC (M&S Hyperprior) and traditional codecs (VVC, BPG), Control-GIC produces far superior perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.054 vs. 0.204 and 0.177) at similar bitrates, though these methods achieve higher PSNR due to their MSE-based optimization. In terms of efficiency, Control-GIC requires only 0.6M training steps (vs. >9M for SCR) and is 7×/4× faster in encoding than MS-ILLM/MRIC, making it the most efficient unified model. At extremely low bitrates, Control-GIC outperforms Mao et al.'s VQGAN-based method on both Kodak (0.115 vs. 0.136 LPIPS) and CLIC2020 (0.086 vs. 0.112 LPIPS) while using fewer bits.

## Relation to Prior Work

Control-GIC builds directly on VQGAN (Esser et al., 2021) and its spatially-variant extension MoVQ (Zheng et al., 2022), repurposing their discrete codebook representation for compression rather than synthesis. It departs from the dominant generative compression paradigm (HiFiC, MRIC, MS-ILLM) that trains separate models per R-D point, and from variable-rate/progressive methods (SCR, CTC) that rely on learned truncation or scalable bitstreams, by instead grounding rate control in classical information-theoretic principles: spatial entropy determines patch granularity, and pre-computed Huffman coding handles entropy coding without learned entropy models. The conceptual bridge between Shannon-era coding theory and modern VQGAN architectures is the paper's central positioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: a single trained model (0.6M steps) replaces the n-models-for-n-bitrates paradigm, with 4–7× faster encoding/decoding than competing generative methods and continuous bpp control down to 0.001 increments. However, the R-D performance is competitive rather than dominant—comparable to MS-ILLM and MRIC in LPIPS/NIQE but not clearly superior—and the three-level granularity (4×4, 8×8, 16×16) limits expressiveness for content with extreme detail variation (e.g., small faces). Open questions include whether the non-parametric entropy-based patch sorting generalizes to video or 3D content, whether additional granularity levels or learned entropy models could close the remaining gap to per-point generative methods, and how the fixed 1024-entry codebook constrains the achievable rate range at the extremes.
