## Problem

Existing late fusion multiple kernel clustering methods suffer from three key limitations: they rely on fixed base partition matrices that cannot adapt during optimization, they focus on adjusting kernel weights while neglecting intrinsic high-order correlations among views, and they lack adaptive mechanisms to handle varying data distributions, limiting robustness and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251083/cropped_page8_idx9.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of m base partition matrices {F_p} ∈ R^{n×k} (each derived from a single-view kernel k-means) and the number of clusters k, and outputs a consensus clustering partition F* ∈ R^{n×k} with orthonormal columns. Unlike kernel-level MKC that operates on n×n kernel matrices, DLEFT-MKC works at the decision level with compact n×k partition matrices, enabling near-linear complexity. The dynamically reconstructed partitions are stacked into a 3-order tensor F̂ ∈ R^{n×k×m} for tensor learning.

## Method

DLEFT-MKC introduces three coupled innovations: (1) dynamic reconstruction of base partitions via SVD-based calibration that overcomes the representational bottleneck of fixed initial partitions; (2) stacking the reconstructed partitions into a tensor and regularizing with t-TNN (t-SVD-based tensor nuclear norm) to capture high-order cross-view correlations; and (3) a pioneering min-max optimization paradigm where kernel weights γ are minimized (pessimistic) while the consensus partition, reconstructed partitions, and permutation matrices are maximized (optimistic), enhancing robustness. The resulting max-min-max problem is solved by combining reduced gradient descent (for γ and F*) with ADMM (for the tensor variable A via tubal-shrinkage), with permutation matrices updated via SVD, ensuring convergence to local optima at near-linear computational cost.

## Results

DLEFT-MKC achieves the best clustering accuracy (ACC) on all 10 benchmark datasets, with headline values of 86.4% (Liver), 99.2% (BBC Sport), 66.5% (ProteinFold), 84.9% (Willow), 94.1% (Plant), 96.0% (PsortNeg), 96.2% (Scene15), 81.5% (CCV), 79.9% (Flower102), and 97.0% (Reuters). It surpasses the best tensor-based competitor UGLTL by an average of ~18% ACC and the late-fusion baseline LFMVC by ~43% ACC across the ten datasets. In terms of runtime, DLEFT-MKC completes in under 1 minute on Flower102 (58.4 s) and Reuters (46.6 s), whereas tensor-based methods like WTNNM require 34,717 s on Flower102 and KCGT requires 11,424 s on Reuters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DLEFT-MKC outperforms all 20 compared baselines on every dataset across all four metrics (ACC, NMI, PUR, RI). Against the strongest tensor-based competitors, it leads UGLTL by 32.8 pp on Liver, 47.8 pp on Willow, and 37.8 pp on CCV, and leads WTNNM by 33.1 pp on Liver and 52.9 pp on Willow. Over the late-fusion family, DLEFT-MKC exceeds LFMVC by an average of 43.1% and OPLFMVC by 44.4% ACC across the ten datasets, confirming that dynamic reconstruction of base partitions resolves the representational bottleneck of fixed initial partitions. Against traditional MKC methods (MKKM, SMKKM, HMKC, LSMKKM), the gains are even larger (e.g., 86.4% vs. 55.4% on Liver for HMKC). Notably, DLEFT-MKC is the only method that successfully processes the Reuters dataset (18,758 samples) within a reasonable time (46.6 s), while LTBPL, UGLTL, and WTNNM fail with out-of-memory errors, and KCGT requires 11,424 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work in multiple kernel clustering: late fusion multi-view clustering (LFMVC, Wang et al. 2019), which achieves near-linear complexity by operating on compact base partition matrices rather than full kernel matrices, and min-max optimization for kernel weight learning (SimpleMKKM, Liu 2023b), which prevents premature convergence to suboptimal kernel combinations. The key departure is the introduction of dynamic reconstruction of the fixed base partitions (overcoming LFMVC's representational bottleneck) and the stacking of these reconstructed partitions into a 3-order tensor regularized by t-TNN (borrowed from tensor-based multi-view clustering methods like UGLTL and WTNNM), all wrapped in the min-max paradigm. The optimization strategy combining reduced gradient descent with ADMM is a novel engineering contribution to make this combined formulation tractable at LFMVC-level computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving tensor-level clustering quality (capturing high-order cross-view correlations) at the computational cost of late fusion methods, which is a meaningful efficiency win for large-scale multi-view clustering where existing tensor-based approaches (WTNNM: 34,717s on Flower102; KCGT: 11,424s on Reuters) are prohibitively slow. However, the ablation reveals that the tensor learning component (ρ term) contributes modestly on several datasets (L2 without it achieves 93.7–96.9% ACC vs. 94.1–97.0% for the full model), suggesting the dynamic reconstruction and min-max paradigm may be the primary drivers. Open questions include whether the approach extends to incomplete multi-view settings, whether the min-max paradigm generalizes beyond the specific kernel-weight optimization it was designed for, and whether the near-linear complexity claim holds as the number of views grows substantially beyond the 2–69 range tested.
