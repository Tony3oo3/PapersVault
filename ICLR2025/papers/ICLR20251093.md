## Problem

Vanilla LoRA fine-tuning fails to benefit from increasing the LoRA rank and can even degrade performance, because the additional tunable parameters introduce noisy hallucination features alongside useful knowledge. The paper identifies that LoRA parameters contain substantial, structured redundancies across layers and modules, but no existing method removes these redundancies in a fine-grained, parameter-wise manner before merging back into the base model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251093/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a post-training preprocessing step: it takes the frozen pretrained weight matrix W and the trained LoRA matrices A ∈ R^{r×d} and B ∈ R^{d'×r} (with r ≪ d, d'), and produces a reduced pair (B'_c, A'_c) with c < r components. The reduced parameters are then merged into W to yield W' = W + B'_c A'_c, introducing zero inference latency. The randomized SVD approximation keeps the computation lightweight, and the optimal c is determined per weight matrix without requiring a validation set.

## Method

NORM (Noise reduction with Reserved Majority) is an inter-shearing method within the proposed PREFT framework that removes redundant LoRA components by maximizing subspace similarity with the pretrained weights. It first applies randomized SVD to the delta weight ΔW = BA to obtain approximate singular vectors and values, then reconstructs reduced LoRA parameters for candidate component counts c. The key innovation is Sim-Search, which iterates over c values (from a start s to r in steps τ·s), reconstructs ΔW_c for each, and computes the Grassmann distance φ_c between the top-r left singular vectors of ΔW_c and those of W. The c maximizing φ_c is selected, ensuring the retained components share the highest subspace alignment with the pretrained weight, thereby discarding hallucination-prone directions while preserving contributing knowledge.

## Results

On general instruction tuning (Table 1), NORM achieves the best average score across all three base models: 61.69 on Qwen2-7B, 58.13 on Llama3-8B, and 50.79 on Mistral-7B, outperforming the strongest PEFT baseline (LoRA) by +2.50, +3.91, and +3.36 points respectively. On math reasoning and code generation (Table 2, Llama3-8B), NORM reaches 82.79 on GSM8K, 33.76 on MATH, 63.40 on HumanEval, and 71.40 on MBPP, yielding an overall average of 61.99 versus 54.56 for LoRA and 58.95 for TAIA. NORM also demonstrates superior knowledge retention, reducing WikiText-103 cross-entropy loss to 3.5992 (general tuning) compared to 3.7102 for LoRA and 3.7016 for the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NORM consistently outperforms all PEFT baselines (LoRA, LoRA+, DoRA, MoRA) and the PREFT baseline TAIA across all three base models and all evaluation domains. On Llama3-8B general instruction tuning, NORM (58.13 avg) beats LoRA (54.22) by +3.91 and TAIA (55.86) by +2.27; on Mistral-7B the gap widens to +3.36 over LoRA and +1.29 over TAIA. In math reasoning and code generation (Table 2), NORM achieves 61.99 average versus 54.56 for LoRA (+7.43) and 58.95 for TAIA (+3.04), with the largest gains on HumanEval (63.40 vs. 48.80 for LoRA, +14.60) and MATH (33.76 vs. 29.27, +4.49). MoRA performs worst among all methods, dropping to 23.01 average on Llama3-8B and 31.13 on Mistral-7B, suggesting its high-rank square decomposition introduces severe noise. SVD-based methods PiSSA (52.86) and AdaLoRA (53.71) also underperform vanilla LoRA (54.22) on Llama3-8B, confirming that SVD tailored for redundancy reduction (as in NORM) is more effective than SVD for initialization or budget allocation. NORM's advantage is most pronounced on reasoning-intensive tasks (GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval) and diminishes slightly on knowledge-heavy benchmarks (MMLU, TruthfulQA), where the base model's pre-trained knowledge already dominates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) and introduces PREFT, a post-hoc framework that removes redundancies from trained LoRA parameters before merging them back into the base model. It departs from prior PEFT variants (LoRA+, DoRA, MoRA) that modify the training dynamics, instead operating entirely at the inference-preprocessing stage. The method is conceptually related to TAIA (Jiang et al., 2024a), which removes all FFN delta parameters based on empirical observation, but NORM generalizes this to a fine-grained, per-parameter SVD-based reduction guided by subspace similarity (Grassmann distance) with the pretrained weights. The SVD machinery also connects to PiSSA and AdaLoRA, but repurposes it for redundancy elimination rather than parameter initialization or budget allocation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NORM addresses a practical and well-documented limitation of LoRA—performance degradation at higher ranks—by showing that the excess capacity introduces hallucinatory noise that can be selectively removed post-hoc without retraining. The finding that NORM actually improves memorization of pretrained knowledge (lower WikiText-103 perplexity than the base model) and yields ~9-point gains in continual learning suggests a broader principle: aligning delta updates with the pretrained subspace mitigates forgetting. Open questions include whether the approach extends to full fine-tuning, whether per-prompt adaptive component selection is feasible, and whether the diminishing returns at very high ranks (r=128) indicate a fundamental ceiling on separable signal vs. noise in LoRA updates.
