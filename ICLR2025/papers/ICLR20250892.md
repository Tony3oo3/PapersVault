## Problem

Alignment algorithms like RLHF and DPO significantly reduce LLM output diversity, causing mode collapse toward majority preferences in both social perspectives and problem-solving strategies. The paper identifies the KL-divergence regularizer as the root cause: it couples entropy and cross-entropy terms, forcing the model to exponentiate population preferences to extreme powers (e.g., 80/20 split becomes 99.9999/0.0001 at β=0.1) while sacrificing lexical, semantic, and logical variety.

## Input / Output

The method takes text prompts and produces free-form text generations, trained on pairwise preference data (chosen vs. rejected completions) from HH-RLHF and Ultrafeedback-200k. The key control knob is the ratio α/β, which acts as a sequence-level 'global temperature' over entire completions rather than individual tokens, allowing diversity to be tuned from standard DPO behavior (α/β=1) up to highly diverse generation (α/β=11) without the rapid fluency collapse seen in token-level temperature scaling. Models are Mistral-7B variants fine-tuned with LoRA.

## Method

Soft Preference Learning (SPL) decouples the standard KL-divergence penalty into a separate entropy bonus (αH(π)) and cross-entropy penalty (βH(π_ref, π)), enabling independent control of generation diversity and reference-policy bias. The optimal policy takes the form π(y) ∝ π_ref(y)^(β/α) · p^(1/α), where p is the population preference probability, so α/β acts as a global temperature on the DPO policy that preserves relative sequence ordering. The paper provides both an RLHF-style objective and a closed-form DPO-style surrogate (Eq. 5) that bypasses the reward model. A social choice analysis proves that SPL with α=1 recovers proportional representation of population preferences, and the method Pareto-dominates token-level temperature scaling across diversity-quality tradeoffs, best-of-N math accuracy, and logit calibration.

## Results

On GSM8K-Hard at best-of-128, SPL (α/β=1.1) achieves 58.34% accuracy, outperforming standard DPO (48.75%) by 10 percentage points and temperature-scaled DPO (t=1.1, 54.07%) by 4 points. On MATH-Hard at best-of-128, SPL (α/β=1.2) reaches 31.91% versus 27.99% for DPO and 30.62% for DPO t=1.1. SPL also Pareto-dominates DPO with token-level temperature scaling across all nine diversity-quality metrics in Figure 2, and SPL models with global temperature slightly above 1 achieve lower Expected Calibration Error and Brier Score than DPO on both TruthfulQA and MMLU while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024), SPL clearly wins on hard problem-solving: at best-of-128 on GSM8K-Hard, SPL (α/β=1.1) scores 58.34% versus DPO's 48.75% (+10 pp), and on MATH-Hard, SPL (α/β=1.2) scores 31.91% versus DPO's 27.99% (+4 pp). SPL also Pareto-dominates DPO with token-level temperature scaling across all nine diversity-quality metrics (Figure 2), achieving higher diversity at every quality level without the rapid fluency degradation seen at token temperatures above 1.2. On logit calibration, SPL models with global temperature slightly above 1 achieve lower ECE and Brier Score than DPO on both TruthfulQA and MMLU while preserving accuracy. Against f-DPO (Wang et al., 2023), which uses alternative f-divergences to avoid mode-seeking, SPL outperforms on diversity-quality Pareto curves in Appendix B, as f-DPO with mass-covering divergences (α=1, forward KL) produces incoherent text while SPL remains stable at much higher global temperatures. Against min-p sampling (Nguyen et al., 2024) combined with DPO, results on hard splits are mixed—DPO+min-p performs best on GSM8K-Hard while SPL performs best on the harder MATH-Hard—but SPL maintains coherence at substantially higher effective temperatures where min-p still degrades.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) and RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022) by identifying the KL-divergence regularizer as the specific mechanism causing diversity loss and mode collapse in aligned LLMs. It departs from prior f-divergence approaches (Wang et al., 2023) that replace KL with alternative divergences, and from entropy-regularization work (Xiao et al., 2024) that lacks generative experiments, by cleanly decomposing the KL penalty into separate entropy and cross-entropy terms within the standard DPO/RLHF framework. The social choice theory framing connects to Siththaranjan et al. (2024) but shifts the analysis from reward learning to the trained policy, and the global temperature scaling perspective relates to Shih et al. (2023) but achieves it through offline supervised learning rather than RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPL offers a minimal, well-motivated modification to standard preference learning (adding a single entropy term) that addresses a widely observed but under-theorized problem: aligned LLMs systematically overrepresent majority preferences and lose output diversity. The connection to best-of-N inference-time scaling is timely and practically important, as SPL's sequence-level diversity preserves coherence where token-level temperature scaling degrades quality, potentially enabling more effective compute scaling on hard problems. Limitations include evaluation only on 7B models, a simplified two-outcome theoretical analysis, and the need for retraining rather than inference-time adjustment. Open questions include whether the optimal α/β ratio is task-dependent, how SPL interacts with multi-turn or safety-critical alignment, and whether the proportional representation guarantee at α=1 holds under intransitive or noisy preferences at scale.
