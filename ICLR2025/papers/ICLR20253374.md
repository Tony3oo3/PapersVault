## Problem

Large-scale QP problems with networked structure require distributed solvers, but existing ADMM-based methods demand meticulous parameter tuning, offer no generalization guarantees to unseen problems, and struggle under finite iteration budgets. While deep unfolding has been applied to centralized QP (OSQP) and to unconstrained distributed optimization, no prior work combines deep unfolding with distributed ADMM for constrained QP problems with formal performance guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253374/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network receives local QP data (Q_i, q_i, A_i, b_i) per node, initial variable estimates, and a fixed consensus mapping G that routes local variable components to global indices. It outputs the global variable vector w^K after K unrolled iterations. A key design constraint is that the indirect (conjugate gradient) method is used to solve local KKT systems, making training memory-efficient and enabling the same learned policy to transfer from small training problems (e.g., 160 variables) to much larger test problems (up to 50K variables, 150K constraints) without retraining.

## Method

The paper first derives DistributedQP, a two-block ADMM algorithm that combines OSQP's local KKT solves with a consensus update on shared global variables, using per-node iteration-varying penalty parameters (ρ_i^k, μ_i^k) and over-relaxation (α^k ∈ [1,2)), with a convergence-to-optimality proof under mild parameter-stabilization assumptions. DeepDistributedQP then unrolls K iterations of DistributedQP as sequential network layers, where each layer's penalty parameters are modulated by learned closed-loop feedback policies (small fully-connected networks) that take local primal and dual residuals as inputs, replacing the heuristic ADMM adaptation rule. Backpropagation through the inner conjugate gradient solves is handled via the implicit function theorem, avoiding unrolling of CG iterations and requiring only one additional CG solve per backward pass. Finally, PAC-Bayes theory is applied to a stochastic (Gaussian) weight distribution to derive high-probability upper bounds on the expected relative optimality gap for unseen problems from the same distribution.

## Results

On centralized QPs, DeepQP consistently requires 1.5–3× fewer iterations than OSQP to reach the same normalized optimality gap across all six problem classes (random QPs, optimal control, portfolio optimization, LASSO). On large-scale distributed QPs, DeepDistributedQP requires 1.5–3.5× fewer iterations than its non-learned counterpart DistributedQP, and both distributed methods outperform OSQP in wall-clock time by orders of magnitude as dimensionality grows. Critically, DeepDistributedQP is trained exclusively on small problems (e.g., 160 variables) yet generalizes to solve problems with up to 50K variables and 150K constraints using the same learned policy. The PAC-Bayes generalization bounds are observed to be tight relative to actual performance, certifying that the learned optimizer's expected final relative optimality gap on unseen problems is bounded with 99% probability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is OSQP (Stellato et al., 2020), the state-of-the-art centralized QP solver. On small-scale centralized problems, DeepQP wins across all six task categories, requiring 1.5–3× fewer iterations than OSQP (with both constant and adaptive penalty settings) to reach the same normalized optimality gap; closed-loop feedback policies further outperform open-loop ones in every case. On large-scale distributed problems, both DeepDistributedQP and DistributedQP achieve orders-of-magnitude reductions in wall-clock time compared to OSQP (using either indirect or direct methods) as problem dimensionality increases, with DeepDistributedQP additionally requiring 1.5–3.5× fewer iterations than DistributedQP at the same per-iteration complexity. The paper also shows that local (per-node) policies outperform shared policies, particularly on structured problems like coupled pendulums, though the advantage is smaller on random QPs where structural patterns are less pronounced. Overall, the learned methods dominate OSQP on both iteration count and wall-clock time, with the gap widening substantially at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the OSQP operator-splitting solver for QPs, (2) consensus ADMM for distributed optimization, and (3) deep unfolding / learning-to-optimize. The most direct prior work is Noah & Shlezinger (2024), which unrolled a distributed ADMM into a deep network but was limited to unconstrained problems with gradient-based local updates and no formal guarantees. The key departure here is combining deep unfolding with a *constrained* distributed QP solver (DistributedQP, a novel consensus-ADMM + OSQP hybrid) and providing both convergence guarantees for the underlying algorithm and PAC-Bayes generalization bounds for the learned policy—neither of which appeared in prior distributed learning-to-optimize work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck: distributed ADMM methods require careful per-problem tuning and lack generalization guarantees, while centralized solvers like OSQP become intractable at scale. The demonstrated ability to train on problems with ~160 variables and deploy the same policy on problems with 50K+ variables (64× scale gap) across six problem classes is practically significant for robotics, power grids, and network flow applications. Key limitations include the reliance on a supervised loss requiring pre-solved problems, and the PAC-Bayes bounds providing only average-performance guarantees rather than worst-case safety guarantees needed for safety-critical deployment. Open questions include whether semi-supervised or residual-based training can eliminate the need for ground-truth solutions, and whether the approach extends to non-quadratic or non-convex distributed problems.
