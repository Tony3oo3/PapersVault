## Problem

Despite GFlowNets' empirical success in drug discovery, phylogenetic inference, and combinatorial optimization, it remains unclear when a trained GFlowNet actually samples from its target distribution. Three fundamental questions are underexplored: how balance violations propagate through the flow network to distort the sampling distribution, whether the GNN parameterization itself can make correct sampling impossible, and how to reliably assess distributional correctness when the state space is intractably large.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a directed acyclic state graph (SG) with an initial state, terminal states carrying a reward function R, and intermediate states connected by directed edges. For graph-structured tasks, states are graphs and the forward/backward policies are parameterized by message-passing GNNs (e.g., GIN). The WDB loss and LA-GFlowNet consume the same SG and reward as standard GFlowNets but modify the training objective or policy architecture respectively. The FCS metric takes a trained GFlowNet's sampling distribution p_T and the reward R, and produces a scalar in [0,1] by averaging TV distances over random β-sized subsets of terminal states, requiring up to three orders of magnitude less compute than exact TV.

## Method

The paper makes three interconnected contributions. First, it derives tight TV bounds showing that a flow imbalance δ at a node affects accuracy in proportion to the total probability mass of its terminal descendants, motivating a Weighted Detailed Balance (WDB) loss that assigns each transition a weight γ(s,s') = 1/#D_{s'} (inverse number of terminal descendants), which accelerates convergence on set generation and phylogenetic inference. Second, it proves that 1-WL GNN-based GFlowNets cannot sample from distributions where two 1-WL-indistinguishable actions lead to children with different total descendant rewards, and introduces LA-GFlowNets that concatenate child-state graph embeddings into the forward policy input, provably exceeding 1-WL expressiveness. Third, it defines the Flow Consistency in Subgraphs (FCS) metric as the expected TV between restricted p_T and R over random subsets, proving FCS = 0 iff p_T ∝ R, establishing PAC statistical guarantees, and empirically showing FCS correlates with true TV (Spearman 0.99) while conventional metrics like number of modes and average top-k reward fail to detect provably incorrect sampling.

## Results

The FCS metric achieves Spearman correlations of 0.99 (set generation) and 0.90 (sequence design) with the intractable TV distance, while being up to three orders of magnitude faster to compute. On the 1-WL indistinguishable graph task, LA-GFlowNet accurately recovers the target distribution (R(C₁)=0.1, R(C₂)=0.9), whereas the standard GNN-based GFlowNet is stuck at a constant L₁ error of 0.4 throughout training. Table 2 reveals that Shen's accuracy metric incorrectly assigns a perfect 100% score to the provably unsound terminally-unrestricted FL- and LED-GFlowNet variants, while the correctly-constrained TB-GFlowNet scores 93.74±0.98 (Sets), 81.38±6.86 (Bags), and 96.98±0.77 (PHO4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard DB, TB, and SubTB losses, the proposed WDB loss achieves faster TV convergence on phylogenetic inference and set generation (the two tasks where early-stage transitions dominate the balance violation), while performing approximately on par with standard DB on sequence design and hypergrid navigation. Against the standard GNN-based GFlowNet, LA-GFlowNet provably and empirically wins on 1-WL-indistinguishable graph tasks, recovering the correct non-uniform distribution where the standard model is stuck at L₁=0.4. Against Shen's accuracy, number of modes, and average reward of top-scoring samples, FCS is the only metric that correctly identifies the terminally-unrestricted LED- and FL-GFlowNets as distributionally incorrect (despite their high conventional scores), while confirming the TB-GFlowNet as correctly fitted. The paper also confirms via email correspondence with the LED-GFlowNet authors that their set-environment experiments indeed used the terminally-unrestricted variant, validating the practical relevance of the FCS diagnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet framework established by Bengio et al. (2021, 2023) by providing the first systematic theoretical analysis of when balance violations in the underlying flow network degrade sampling accuracy, and by connecting the well-known 1-WL expressiveness limits of message-passing GNNs (Xu et al., 2019) to fundamental representational constraints on GNN-parameterized GFlowNets. It departs from the primarily empirical trajectory of prior GFlowNet work by introducing provable TV bounds (Theorem 1), impossibility results for 1-WL GFlowNets (Theorem 3), and a new evaluation metric (FCS) with PAC guarantees. The WDB loss and LA-GFlowNet architecture are direct extensions of the DB loss and standard GNN policy parameterization, respectively, motivated by the theoretical insights rather than heuristic design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that GNN-based GFlowNets have fundamental expressiveness limits—there exist simple state graphs and reward functions from which no 1-WL GFlowNet can correctly sample—shifting the community's understanding of why some GFlowNet training fails. The demonstration that LED- and FL-GFlowNets with unrestricted terminal flows are provably incorrect (Proposition 1), confirmed via correspondence with the LED-GFlowNet authors, calls into question some published experimental results and underscores the need for sound evaluation metrics. Open questions include extending the expressiveness analysis to higher-order GNNs, designing theoretically optimal weighting schemes for the WDB loss, and validating FCS in specialized domains like molecule generation and NLP where the state space is intractably large.
