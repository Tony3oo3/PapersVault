## Problem

Prevalent RLHF methods assume the Bradley-Terry model, which imposes transitivity on preferences and cannot capture the full complexity of human judgment. While game-theoretic formulations of alignment (Nash-MD, DNO, SPPO) relax this assumption, they all require estimating the expected win rate of each individual response against the current policy—a quantity that is difficult to compute accurately and incurs high annotation or query costs in practice.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference SFT policy (LLaMA-3-8B-based), a prompt set, and a preference oracle (either a BT reward model or a general preference model that directly compares two responses). At each of T=3 iterations, it generates K=8 responses per prompt from the current policy, uses a tournament strategy requiring only O(K) binary preference queries (vs. O(K²) for win-rate estimation), and constructs a preference dataset of (y_w, y_l) pairs. The output is the final-iterate policy π_{T+1}, a fully aligned LLM that approximates the Nash policy of the two-player game.

## Method

INPO formulates LLM alignment as a symmetric two-player game with KL-regularized win-rate objectives and applies Online Mirror Descent (Hedge) to learn the Nash policy via self-play. The key technical contribution is a new population loss (Eq. 8) that replaces the intractable expected win rate P(y ≻ π_t) with a squared-deviation objective over the preference distribution λ_p, requiring only binary preference signals. Specifically, the loss minimizes E[(h_t(π, y_w, y_l) − 1/(2η))²] where h_t encodes log-ratios of the candidate policy, reference policy, and current policy, and its unique minimizer is exactly the OMD update π_{t+1}. This design bypasses win-rate estimation entirely, making the algorithm directly trainable on a preference dataset. The paper proves O(1/ε²) iteration complexity for the uniform mixture policy and O(1/T) last-iterate convergence to the Nash policy under a bounded log-density-ratio assumption.

## Results

Using an LLaMA-3-8B SFT model as the starting point, INPO with a preference model (PM) oracle achieves a 42.6% length-controlled win rate on AlpacaEval 2.0 and a 37.8% win rate on Arena-Hard v0.1, substantially outperforming all 8B baselines. On MT-Bench, INPO (PM) scores 8.43, also the best among 8B models. The paper reports at least 27.7% relative improvement over the strongest online RLHF baselines (Iterative DPO and SPPO) on AlpacaEval 2.0 and Arena-Hard.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INPO is compared against two state-of-the-art online RLHF methods: Iterative DPO (Dong et al., 2024) and SPPO (Wu et al., 2024). On AlpacaEval 2.0, INPO (PM) achieves 42.6% LC win rate, beating Iterative DPO (PM) by 14.1 points (28.5%) and SPPO (PM) by 9.8 points (32.8%). On Arena-Hard, INPO (PM) reaches 37.8% win rate versus 29.6% for Iterative DPO (PM) and 29.2% for SPPO (PM), a gap of roughly 8–9 points. On MT-Bench the margins are smaller (8.43 vs. 8.29 and 8.26), but INPO still leads. Notably, INPO with the BT reward model (RM) oracle (37.6% AlpacaEval, 34.7% Arena-Hard) already surpasses SPPO (PM) on both benchmarks, suggesting the algorithmic improvement is independent of the preference oracle quality. The paper also notes that methods using a preference model oracle consistently outperform those using a BT reward model, reinforcing the motivation for general preferences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the game-theoretic formulation of LLM alignment from Munos et al. (2023), who proposed a planning algorithm for the Nash policy under general preferences, to the learning setting where only query access to the preference oracle is available. It builds on the Online Mirror Descent (Hedge) framework of Freund & Schapire (1997) and adapts the closed-form log-ratio loss derivation technique from DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) to derive a new population loss that avoids the intractable normalization constant. The key departure from concurrent work (DNO, SPPO) is that INPO's loss objective requires only binary preference signals rather than estimated expected win rates, and unlike Nash-MD, it samples directly from the current policy rather than a geometric mixture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

INPO provides a practical, theoretically grounded online algorithm for aligning LLMs under general (non-BT) preferences, with clean O(1/T) last-iterate convergence guarantees and an implementation that is straightforward to plug into existing RLHF pipelines. The empirical result that an 8B model trained with INPO surpasses LLaMA-3-70B-it on AlpacaEval 2.0 (42.6% vs. 34.4% LC win rate) suggests that alignment methodology can partially compensate for model scale. Open questions include finite-sample analysis, extension to sequential/MDP settings, and whether the gains hold with true human preferences rather than evaluation-model proxies; the current experiments use only 3 iterations and a single model family, leaving scalability and robustness questions unresolved.
