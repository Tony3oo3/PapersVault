## Problem

Existing VideoQA systems must process entire videos before answering and reprocess frames for each new question, making them impractical for continuous video streams where questions arrive at arbitrary timestamps. Sparse frame sampling, the common workaround for long videos, loses fine-grained visual and temporal information critical for accurate answering. The paper addresses how to enable real-time, accurate question-answering over arbitrarily long video streams without re-encoding or excessive memory growth.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous video stream at 0.5 FPS (1080P frames pre-extracted) and text questions that arrive at arbitrary timestamps during the stream; each question must be answered using only frames observed up to that point. Output is a text answer in either multiple-choice or open-ended format, generated autoregressively. A key operational detail is that video encoding and question-answering are decoupled into separate processes (and potentially separate GPUs), with KV-Caches offloaded to RAM or disk at a rate of ~18.8 GB/hour for the 7B model.

## Method

ReKV is a training-free framework that modifies the attention mechanism of a decoder-based Video-LLM (LLaVA-OV) in three stages. First, video frames are encoded incrementally using causal sliding-window attention (window size 15K tokens), where each frame attends only to a limited set of preceding frames; all past KV-Caches are retained and offloaded to RAM/disk to prevent GPU memory overflow. Second, upon receiving a question, a retrieval step selects the most relevant video KV-Caches: either via an external SigLIP model computing frame-level cosine similarity, or (preferred) via internal self-attention weights where frame and question vectors are derived from the model's own key and query projections, enabling per-layer retrieval of different video blocks. Third, the retrieved KV-Caches are reloaded onto the GPU and prepended to the question tokens for autoregressive answer generation, with standard RoPE positional encoding applied to the retrieved tokens to preserve temporal ordering. The approach requires no additional training and integrates seamlessly with existing Video-LLMs.

## Results

On four long-form offline VideoQA benchmarks, LLaVA-OV-7B + ReKV achieves 68.5% on MLVU-dev, 56.0% on QAEGO4D-test, 60.7% on EgoSchema, and 60.4% on ActivityNet-QA (open-ended score 3.52/5), improving over the base LLaVA-OV-7B by +3.8, +3.2, +0.9, and +3.8 points respectively. On the streaming benchmarks RVS-Ego and RVS-Movie, internal KV-Cache retrieval yields 63.7% and 54.4% accuracy with 11 FPS encoding speed, 3.3s latency, and 38 GB peak GPU memory. The method is entirely training-free and requires no architectural changes beyond modifying the attention mask.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA-OV-7B + ReKV outperforms GPT-4o (64.6%) on MLVU by 3.9 points and Gemini-1.5-Pro (57.5%) on ActivityNet-QA by 2.9 points, while remaining a 7B open-source model. Against the closest streaming-specific baseline Flash-VStream-7B, ReKV leads by 18.3 points on MLVU (68.5 vs 50.2), 17.8 on QAEGO4D (56.0 vs 38.2), 22.6 on EgoSchema (60.7 vs 38.1), and 8.5 on ActivityNet-QA (60.4 vs 51.9). On EgoSchema, ReKV (60.7%) still trails Gemini-1.5-Pro (72.2%) and Gemini-1.5-Flash (65.7%), indicating a gap versus the largest proprietary models on very long temporal reasoning. On the streaming benchmarks (Table 5), LLaVA-OV-7B with internal retrieval surpasses Flash-VStream-7B on RVS-Ego (63.7 vs 57.3) and RVS-Movie (54.4 vs 53.1) while using 38 GB GPU memory versus Flash-VStream's 20 GB, at the cost of slightly lower encoding speed (11 vs 14 FPS) and higher latency (3.3s vs 2.4s). The ablation on QAEGO4D confirms that internal retrieval (56.0%, 70.5% recall) consistently beats external CLIP-based retrieval (54.2%, 58.1% recall) and uniform sampling (53.0%, 6.1% recall), approaching the oracle upper bound of 64.4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReKV builds on the sliding-window attention mechanism from LM-Infinite (Han et al., 2023) and the KV-Cache retrieval paradigm from Inf-LLM (Xiao et al., 2024a), transplanting both into the video domain for the first time in a training-free setting. It departs from memory-based streaming approaches (Flash-VStream, VideoStreaming) that compress video into a fixed-size memory bank, instead preserving the full KV-Cache and selectively retrieving relevant portions per query. Unlike VideoLLM-Online, which requires interleaved video-text training, ReKV integrates seamlessly with off-the-shelf Video-LLMs (primarily LLaVA-OV) by modifying only the attention computation, positioning itself as a plug-in enhancement rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-in nature of ReKV means it can immediately benefit from ongoing improvements in base Video-LLMs without retraining, making it a practical path to real-time video QA in deployment scenarios (surveillance, live broadcasting, robotics). A key limitation is that retrieved KV-Caches lose their original positional information, and retrieval recall (63–70%) remains well below the oracle upper bound, leaving room for improved retrieval strategies. Open questions include how the approach scales to multi-hour streams with thousands of questions, whether the fixed retrieval budget (64 frames) is optimal across diverse question types, and whether the separation of encoding and QA across GPUs introduces latency in latency-sensitive applications.
