## Problem

Deep neural networks are causally opaque: users cannot answer interventional or counterfactual questions about the model's internal reasoning. Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) improve semantic transparency but rely on two unrealistic assumptions—concepts are causally independent and are all direct causes of the target—preventing them from capturing the complex causal dependencies that govern real-world decision-making.

## Input / Output

The model takes raw image pixels (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR-10, CelebA face crops) or tabular feature vectors as input and produces, alongside the final class label, a set of binary concept activations and a learned sparse DAG encoding causal dependencies among concepts. Each concept is represented by a high-dimensional embedding constructed as a state-weighted mixture of two learned semantic vectors (active and inactive), enabling both rich information flow and simple binary interventions. At test time the bipartite training graph can be unfolded into an explicit causal chain, exposing the full ancestor subgraph for any prediction.

## Method

Causal CGMs duplicate the concept layer into an 'explaining' copy set V′ and an 'explained' set V, connected by a learnable adjacency matrix M that is sparsified (threshold γ) and constrained to be acyclic (trace-based penalty à la CausalVAE) to yield a DAG. Training minimises a three-term loss: (i) prediction of the endogenous copies from exogenous embeddings, (ii) prediction of endogenous variables from their parents using a do-operator on the copies to reduce label leakage and provide better gradients, and (iii) graph priors enforcing sparsity and acyclicity. A key theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) shows that for all non-root nodes the conditional p(v_i | pa_{V′}(v_i)) equals p(v_i | pa_V(v_i)), so the bipartite graph can be unfolded at inference into arbitrary-length cause-effect chains among interpretable variables. Each concept's embedding is a mixture v′_j·c⁺_j + (1−v′_j)·c⁻_j, forcing the model to use distinct latent spaces for active vs. inactive states and enabling one-step binary interventions. This architecture supports ground-truth interventions that propagate through all descendant nodes, do-interventions for interventional/counterfactual queries, and a 'blocking' procedure that formally verifies causal independence between variable pairs.

## Results

Causal CGMs achieve label accuracy within ~1–2 percentage points of black-box baselines across all four datasets: 88.24% on Checkmark (vs. 90.15% black box), 99.44% on dSprites (vs. 99.53%), 78.23% on CelebA (vs. 79.55%), and 93.32% on CIFAR-10 (vs. 94.85%). The variant with a pre-trained causal graph (Causal CGM+CD) narrows the gap further, e.g., 89.43% on Checkmark and 78.42% on CelebA. In the blocking experiment, Causal CGMs achieve a Residual Concept Causal Effect of exactly 0.00±0.00 across all datasets, versus ~98–100% for CBM and ~100–106% for CEM, demonstrating perfect causal independence after do-intervention-based blocking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to CBM (Koh et al., 2020), Causal CGM trades a small accuracy drop (e.g., 78.23% vs. 79.00% on CelebA) for full causal transparency, but on CIFAR-10 it actually outperforms CBM by ~1.15 percentage points (93.32% vs. 92.17%). Against CEM (Espinosa Zarlenga et al., 2022), Causal CGM is competitive on all datasets and again leads on CIFAR-10 (93.32% vs. 92.04%). The most decisive advantage appears in the blocking experiment: Causal CGM achieves a Residual Concept Causal Effect of 0.00 (perfect causal independence) on every dataset, while CBM retains ~98–100% residual effect and CEM retains ~100–106%, meaning neither baseline can effectively sever a causal path. For ground-truth interventions, Causal CGM improves non-intervened node accuracy by ~15 percentage points after only 7 interventions on CelebA, whereas CBM and CEM require intervening on all concepts to reach a comparable gain, and Causal CGM additionally improves intermediate concept accuracy by up to ~25 percentage points—something CBM and CEM cannot do due to their independence assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) line by replacing the flat, independent-concept architecture with a learned causal graph (DAG) over interpretable variables, drawing on Pearl's structural causal model framework and the acyclicity regularizer from CausalVAE. It explicitly departs from CBMs by rejecting two assumptions—that concepts are conditionally independent and that they are direct causes of the task label—and from Concept Embedding Models by adopting their two-embedding mixture representation for high-dimensional concept states. The paper also distinguishes itself from post-hoc causal explainability methods (e.g., DiConStruct) by being causally transparent by design rather than relying on surrogate models, and from relational CBMs by learning the causal structure end-to-end rather than requiring it as a priori input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Causal CGMs provide the first concept-based architecture that natively supports do-interventions, counterfactual queries, and formal blocking of causal paths—capabilities that are architecturally impossible in standard CBMs and CEMs. The perfect blocking result (Residual CaCE = 0.00 across all datasets, vs. ~100% for baselines) demonstrates a verification tool for fairness and reliability that has no prior equivalent in the concept-based literature. Key limitations include dependence on annotation quality for graph learning, the need for special handling of cyclic dependencies, and the fact that the learned graph reflects the model's inference rather than the true data-generating process. Open questions include scalability to very large concept sets, whether learned causal structures can be validated against domain knowledge, and how to handle cyclic causal dependencies without the acyclicity constraint.
