## Problem

Current bioactivity prediction tasks suffer from two fundamental flaws: training data (primarily PDBbind, ~20K pairs) typically contains only one small molecule per protein pocket, causing models to learn per-target bioactivity ranges rather than molecule-specific interactions, and evaluation metrics (Pearson/Spearman) are computed across different protein targets rather than across molecules within the same target. This allows a degenerate pocket-only baseline that ignores the small molecule entirely to match or exceed full protein-ligand models, revealing that existing benchmarks overestimate model capability.

## Input / Output

The task takes a 3D protein pocket structure (defined as a 15 Å radius around a co-crystal ligand in a PDB file) and a 3D small molecule docked pose (generated via consensus of Glide, GOLD, and AutoDock Vina with RMSD < 2 Å agreement required between at least two programs) as input, and produces a single bioactivity value (negative log of molar concentration) for one of four distinct label types: Kd, Ki, IC50, or EC50. Unlike prior benchmarks that mix label types into a single prediction target, SIU treats each label type as a separate task. The evaluation is structured so that each protein pocket (PDB ID) is associated with multiple small molecules (averaging ~138 per pocket vs. 1 in PDBbind), enabling within-pocket correlation metrics.

## Method

The core contribution is the SIU dataset (5.34M conformations, 1.38M bioactivity labels, 214,686 molecules, 1,720 protein targets) and a redefined evaluation protocol. Dataset construction sources bioactivity labels from ChEMBL and BindingDB, deduplicates molecules via ECFP (Tanimoto > 0.8) and pockets via FLAPP, then docks each molecule to its validated target using three independent docking programs; only poses with RMSD < 2 Å consensus across at least two programs are retained. The key methodological innovation is the group-by-pocket evaluation metric (Pearson*/Spearman*): correlation is first computed within each PDB ID across its associated small molecules, then averaged across all pockets via mean pooling, preventing models from exploiting inter-target distribution differences. The paper also introduces multiple split strategies (90%/60%/30% sequence identity, 60% + structural similarity, 10-fold CV) and demonstrates that models trained on SIU outperform PDBbind-trained models while revealing a dramatic drop in within-pocket correlation (e.g., Ki Pearson drops from 0.485 to 0.036), exposing the true difficulty of molecule-level discrimination.

## Results

On the SIU 0.9 dataset (single-task, Uni-Mol), the best Pearson correlation reaches 0.485 for Ki and 0.470 for IC50, compared to 0.368 and 0.430 respectively when trained on PDBbind. However, the redefined grouped-by-pocket metric (Pearson*) reveals a dramatic performance drop: for Ki, Pearson* falls from 0.485 (global) to just 0.036 (grouped), indicating that models trained on PDBbind achieve only 0.040 Pearson* for Ki. For IC50, SIU 0.9 improves Pearson* from 0.245 (PDBbind) to 0.345, while RMSE decreases from 1.575 to 1.357. The Kd task remains the most challenging, with all models achieving near-zero or negative global Pearson correlations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is dataset-level: models trained on SIU 0.9 consistently outperform those trained on PDBbind across all label types on traditional metrics (e.g., IC50 Pearson improves from 0.430 to 0.470; Ki Pearson from 0.368 to 0.485), even though PDBbind was used without any homology filtering. Among model baselines on SIU (Table 1, MTL), Uni-Mol achieves the best IC50 Pearson (0.462) and Ki Pearson (0.375), while ProFSA edges ahead on EC50 Spearman (0.495 vs. 0.461). The GNN baseline substantially outperforms the 3D-CNN across all label types (e.g., IC50 Pearson 0.336 vs. 0.158), but both are far below Uni-Mol and ProFSA. Critically, the redefined Pearson* metric reveals that even the best model (Uni-Mol on SIU 0.9) drops from 0.485 to 0.036 on Ki, and from 0.470 to 0.345 on IC50, demonstrating that the new grouped-by-pocket evaluation is substantially harder and exposes the overestimation of model capability under the traditional cross-target metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper critically re-examines the bioactivity prediction task as established by PDBbind-derived benchmarks (CASF, Atom3D LBA) and the 3D deep learning models built upon them (Uni-Mol, ProFSA, GNN, 3D-CNN). Rather than proposing a new architecture, it identifies a fundamental flaw in the task formulation: existing datasets contain only one small molecule per protein pocket, and evaluation metrics (Pearson/Spearman) are computed across targets rather than within them, enabling pocket-only models to match or exceed full complex models. The paper carves new ground by constructing the SIU dataset (5.34M conformations via consensus docking with Vina, Glide, and GOLD) and introducing within-pocket correlation metrics (Pearson*, Spearman*) that force models to genuinely differentiate molecules targeting the same binding site.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SIU dataset (50× PDBbind, 214K molecules, 1,720 targets, 9,662 pockets) is likely to become a standard benchmark for structure-based bioactivity prediction, and the redefined metrics expose that prior models' apparent performance was substantially inflated by evaluation artifacts. A key open question is whether any model can learn meaningful within-pocket discrimination at scale—the best Pearson* for Ki is only 0.149, suggesting the problem remains largely unsolved. Limitations include reliance on docked (not co-crystal) structures, which introduces pose uncertainty, and the fact that the curated 10-target test set, while diverse, is small relative to the full dataset. The paper also leaves open whether the label-type separation (treating Kd, Ki, IC50, EC50 as distinct tasks) is the right granularity or whether a unified multi-task formulation could help.
