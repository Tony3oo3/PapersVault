## Problem

Guidance—modulating an unconditional generative model with a predictor to produce conditional samples—is the key mechanism enabling controllable generation in continuous state-space diffusion and flow models, but it has no principled counterpart for discrete state-spaces. The score function is undefined on discrete spaces, and the normalizing constant in Bayes' theorem scales as S^D, making exact conditioning intractable. This paper introduces Discrete Guidance, the first exact and general framework for applying both predictor and predictor-free guidance to CTMC-based discrete generative models.

## Input / Output

The method takes an already-trained unconditional CTMC-based model (continuous-time discrete diffusion or discrete flow matching) that defines a time-dependent rate matrix R_t(x,x̃), a separately trained time-dependent predictor p(y|x,t), and a target property value y*. At sampling time, it starts from a noise distribution (e.g., fully-masked states) and propagates forward in time using guided rates, producing discrete state-space samples (SMILES strings, DNA sequences, or protein sequences) conditioned on y*. The guidance strength γ ∈ ℝ⁺ controls the trade-off between sample diversity and fidelity to the target property, and a stochasticity hyperparameter η controls sampling randomness.

## Method

The key insight is that in continuous-time CTMCs with independent per-dimension noising, only one dimension can jump at any instant, so the normalizing constant in Bayes' theorem reduces from S^D terms to D×(S−1)+1, making guidance exactly tractable. The core result (Eq. 2) shows that conditional rates are obtained by modulating unconditional rates with a likelihood ratio: R_t(x,x̃|y) = [p(y|x̃,t)/p(y|x,t)]^γ · R_t(x,x̃), with the diagonal rate set by probability conservation. A predictor-free variant (Eq. 3) blends conditional and unconditional rates as R_t^(γ)(x,x̃|y) = R_t(x,x̃|y)^γ · R_t(x,x̃)^(1−γ), requiring no explicit predictor. For efficiency, Taylor-approximated guidance (TAG) replaces D×(S−1)+1 predictor evaluations with a single forward-backward pass via a first-order Taylor expansion of the log-likelihood ratio, reducing per-step cost from O(D×S) to O(1) while empirically matching exact guidance quality.

## Results

On small-molecule generation (QMugs dataset), Discrete Guidance (both exact and Taylor-approximated) achieves statistically significantly lower MAE than DiGress for conditioning on number of rings and lipophilicity (LogP), with γ=2 guidance strength. On cell-type-conditioned DNA enhancer design (104k sequences, 81 classes), DG-Exact, DG-TAG, and DiGress perform comparably on both FBD and target-class probability, while DirFM-CG performs more poorly. On stability-guided inverse folding (8 proteins), FMIF-DG-Exact and FMIF-DG-TAG achieve the highest success rates (correct fold with RMSD ≤ 2 Å AND ΔΔG ≥ 0), with FMIF-DG-Exact outperforming or matching all other methods on 7 out of 8 proteins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiGress (discrete-time discrete diffusion guidance), Discrete Guidance wins on small-molecule generation with statistically significant MAE improvements for both ring count and LogP targets, ties on DNA enhancer design (comparable FBD and class probability), and wins on protein inverse folding (higher success rates). Against DirFM-CG (Dirichlet flow matching with classifier guidance), Discrete Guidance performs better on DNA enhancer design, with the authors hypothesizing DirFM-CG suffers from needing to project the classifier score onto the simplex tangent plane. Against ProteinMPNN (widely-used autoregressive inverse folding), the non-guided FMIF baseline already matches or outperforms it, and guided FMIF-DG variants further improve success rates by incorporating stability information. Taylor-approximated guidance (TAG) matches exact guidance in most cases, with one exception (protein 6ACV) where TAG outperforms exact guidance, likely due to predictor miscalibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CTMC-based discrete state-space generative modeling framework of Campbell et al. (2022, 2024) by adding the critical missing ingredient: principled guidance. The key conceptual bridge is recognizing that the continuous-time formulation's property—only one dimension changes at any instant—reduces the intractable S^D normalizing constant in Bayes' theorem to D×(S−1)+1 terms, yielding an exact rate-modulation formula (Eq. 2) that directly parallels score-based guidance in continuous state-spaces (Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015; Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021). The Taylor-approximated guidance (TAG) draws on Grathwohl et al. (2021) for the gradient-based likelihood ratio approximation, while the predictor-free variant mirrors Ho & Salimans (2021). The paper positions itself as distinct from DiGress (Vignac et al., 2023), which operates in discrete time and requires additional intractable approximations, and from Dirichlet FM (Stark et al., 2024), which sidesteps the discrete space by working on the probability simplex.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a significant gap: prior discrete state-space generative models lacked a principled, exact guidance mechanism, forcing practitioners to either retrain conditional models or resort to ad-hoc approximations. The framework's generality (applicable to any CTMC-based model, not just diffusion or flow matching) and its demonstrated utility across molecules, DNA, and proteins make it a practical tool for the sciences, where conditioning on real-valued properties (stability, affinity, lipophilicity) is the norm. Open questions include the theoretical guarantees of TAG versus exact guidance, optimal training strategies for noisy predictors, and whether the approach extends to controllable text generation. The finding that flow-matching inverse folding (FMIF) matches or outperforms ProteinMPNN also suggests a broader shift toward CTMC-based models for protein design.
