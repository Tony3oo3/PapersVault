## Problem

Existing approaches to EEG-based brain disorder classification face three interrelated gaps: tokenizing multi-frequency band connectomes typically fragments graphs and destroys topological structure; conventional positional encoding fails to capture the intrinsic frequency and demographic information embedded in EEG data; and XAI is used almost exclusively for post-hoc visualization rather than to actively improve transformer performance. This paper argues that XAI's explanatory power can be repurposed as a self-refinement mechanism to sharpen the self-attention mechanism, particularly valuable in the data-limited neuroimaging setting.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-frequency band connectomes (9 bands: delta through low gamma plus theta/beta ratio) constructed from 30-second preprocessed EEG segments, where coherence values form node features and wPLI values form edge weights across 19 (TUAB) or 26 (TDBRAIN) EEG channels, along with scalar demographic inputs (age and gender). It outputs a brain disorder class label (binary normal/abnormal for TUAB, multi-class ADHD/MDD/OCD for TDBRAIN) and, as a byproduct of the XAI-guided attention, per-frequency-band importance scores that can be directly extracted from the explainer for clinical interpretability.

## Method

XAIguiFormer comprises three key components. First, a connectome tokenizer uses a 4-layer GINEConv GNN to encode each single-band graph as one atomic token (preserving topology), then pools node embeddings via mean to form a 9-token frequency sequence. Second, dRoFE replaces standard RoPE by using frequency band lower/upper bounds as rotation angles and encoding age and gender as vector magnitude, injecting modality-specific information directly into query and key projections. Third, the XAI guided self-attention mechanism first runs the frequency sequence through a vanilla transformer, applies DeepLift (or GradCAM) to produce importance maps for Q and K, then recomputes attention using these refined vectors to concentrate on relevant dependencies; a joint loss (weighted combination of vanilla and XAI-guided cross-entropy, α=0.7) trains the full stack end-to-end. The design is motivated by a dynamical-system view of self-attention where the attention value acts as an adaptive ODE step size, and XAI refines the coarse input to that step-size function.

## Results

On the TUAB dataset (binary brain disorder classification), XAIguiFormer achieves a balanced accuracy (BAC) of 0.8205±0.0027, AUC-PR of 0.8965±0.0079, and AUROC of 0.9000±0.0046, outperforming all 7 baselines on BAC. On the smaller TDBRAIN dataset (multi-class: ADHD, MDD, OCD), XAIguiFormer reaches BAC 0.6635±0.0080 and AUROC 0.7814±0.0156, again leading all baselines on BAC and AUROC. The model uses 3.5M parameters and 1.6G FLOPs, comparable in size to S3T (3.5M) and BIOT (3.2M) while surpassing them by 2.4–3.8% BAC on TUAB and 3.2–1.8% BAC on TDBRAIN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XAIguiFormer outperforms all 7 baselines on BAC for both datasets. On TUAB, it leads LaBraM-Base (the strongest baseline) by +0.65% BAC (0.8205 vs 0.8140) and BIOT by +2.46% BAC (0.8205 vs 0.7959), while matching LaBraM on AUC-PR (0.8965) and trailing it by only 0.22% on AUROC (0.9000 vs 0.9022). On TDBRAIN, the advantage is more pronounced: XAIguiFormer beats LaBraM-Base by +1.79% BAC and +6.67% AUROC (0.7814 vs 0.7147), and surpasses BIOT by +3.15% BAC, though BIOT edges it out on AUC-PR (0.6007 vs 0.5961). Against the self-supervised Corr-DCRNN and pretrained LaBraM-Base, XAIguiFormer—trained entirely from scratch—achieves superior BAC on both datasets, suggesting that the dRoFE and XAI-guided attention mechanisms effectively compensate for the lack of pretraining. The gap over smaller baselines (FFCL, SPaRCNet, S3T) is particularly large on TDBRAIN, where XAIguiFormer's BAC of 0.6635 exceeds S3T (0.4937) by 17 percentage points, highlighting the benefit of XAI guidance on data-limited settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RoPE (Rotary Position Embedding) framework by replacing position-based rotation angles with frequency-band-bound angles and augmenting the rotation with demographic magnitude, creating dRoFE specifically for multi-band EEG connectome sequences. It draws on the dynamical-system interpretation of self-attention (Huang et al., 2023) to motivate a key architectural departure: using XAI-generated importance scores (via DeepLift) to refine query and key vectors before computing attention, rather than treating XAI purely as a post-hoc interpretability tool. The connectome tokenizer borrows the whole-graph-as-token strategy from DETR and applies GINEConv for structure-preserving graph encoding, positioning itself against patch-based or node-level tokenization that disrupts topological connectivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that XAI can serve as a self-refinement mechanism to improve transformer performance without external data or teacher models—is a meaningful conceptual shift for the neuroimaging ML community, where XAI has been almost exclusively used for visualization. However, the practical impact is currently limited: explanations are confined to the frequency-band (token) level rather than functional connectivity patterns, evaluation covers only two datasets, and the model is trained from scratch without pretraining. Open questions include whether the XAI-guided attention mechanism generalizes to other modalities or larger models, whether the dRoFE encoding transfers to non-EEG graph sequences, and whether the modest 1–4% BAC gains persist under stronger baselines or more diverse clinical populations.
