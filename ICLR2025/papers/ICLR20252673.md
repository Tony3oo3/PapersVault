## Problem

Traditional multi-modal feature fusion methods (concatenation, element-wise operations, attention) operate in high-dimensional feature space and capture only shallow, first-order interactions between modalities, failing to model deeper structural relationships across representations, domains, and modalities. This limits their ability to align heterogeneous features and reveals insights that richer, relationship-centric fusion could provide.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252673/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-extracted unit-level features (e.g., 2048-dim I3D, 4096-dim C3D, 1024-dim SwinT video features per 16-frame clip; 768-dim SimCSE text embeddings per 64-frame segment; ST-GCN pose features) and converts them into N×N cosine-similarity adjacency matrices. During training, two relationship graphs are randomly sampled per iteration for fusion. The output is a fused N×N relationship graph (e.g., 32×32) that feeds into two fully connected layers (N→N→1) with ReLU and Sigmoid to produce a per-clip anomaly score, evaluated at snippet level via AUC.

## Method

EGO fusion shifts the fusion problem from high-dimensional feature vectors to a lower-dimensional, homogeneous graph space. First, pairwise cosine similarities between unit-level features form an initial N×N relationship graph R⁰=I. Second, iterative graph relationship updates are computed via element-wise (Hadamard) multiplication rather than matrix multiplication, yielding sequences {R⁰, R¹, …, R^P} that capture progressively refined local-to-global dependencies. Third, a learnable (P+1)×(Q+1) weight matrix A combines all cross-modal graph-pair products R_(a)^p ⊙ R_(b)^q, which is mathematically equivalent to a multilinear polynomial in the relationship scores with learnable coefficients. A degree variance regularization term penalizes differences in node-connectivity variance between normal and abnormal graphs, encouraging anomaly nodes to have fewer connections. The entire pipeline uses only 0.091M parameters (vs. 29.0M for MTN), with the fusion operator trained end-to-end via backpropagation on a binary cross-entropy loss.

## Results

On the four primary video anomaly detection benchmarks, EGO fusion achieves AUC of 93.23 (UCSD Ped2), 97.26 (ShanghaiTech), 83.10 (CUHK Avenue), and 77.61 (Street Scene), outperforming the strongest feature-level baseline MTN (92.80/96.37/62.06/71.50) by up to 21.04 AUC points on Avenue. On the large-scale MSAD dataset, EGO reaches 87.36 AUC, surpassing MIST (86.65), MTN (86.82), MGFN (84.96), UR-DMU (85.02), and RTFM (86.7). EGO uses only 0.091M parameters versus MTN's 29.0M, and reduces training time by up to 50% (e.g., 0.154s vs 0.448s per sample on ShanghaiTech).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MTN fusion (Chen et al., CVPR 2023), EGO wins on all four primary datasets (e.g., +21.04 AUC on Avenue, +6.11 on Street) and on MSAD overall (87.36 vs 86.82 AUC), while using 319× fewer parameters (0.091M vs 29.0M) and training ~3× faster. However, on the two large-scale datasets XD-Violence and UCF-Crime, MTN still leads (77.17 vs 65.77 AP; 85.14 vs 81.71 AUC), suggesting EGO's advantage is most pronounced on smaller, more structured surveillance datasets. On MSAD, EGO also outperforms MGFN (Chen et al., AAAI 2023; 84.96 AUC), MIST (Feng et al., 2021; 86.65 AUC), UR-DMU (Zhou et al., 2023; 85.02 AUC), and RTFM (Tian et al., ICCV 2021; 86.7 AUC) in overall AUC, though it trails on specific anomaly types such as Fighting (66.5 vs MGFN's 89.4 AUC) and Assault (52.2 vs RTFM's 68.1 AUC). EGO's unique advantage is its ability to fuse three or more features simultaneously (e.g., I3D+C3D+SwinT reaching 95.38 AUC on ShanghaiTech), whereas MTN is architecturally limited to pairwise fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

The paper departs from traditional feature-level fusion (concatenation, addition, attention) by shifting to a lower-dimensional graph space where pairwise relationships between unit-level features are encoded as similarity matrices. While graph-based fusion methods (GCNs, graph neural networks) have been explored for multimodal learning, EGO distinguishes itself by using iterative element-wise multiplication (rather than matrix multiplication or graph powers) for graph expansion, and by introducing a learnable weight matrix whose structure is formally connected to multilinear polynomials. The primary experimental baseline is MTN fusion (Chen et al., 2023a), a feature-level multimodal fusion method, which EGO outperforms while using orders of magnitude fewer parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a fusion method that is simultaneously more accurate, more interpretable, and dramatically more parameter-efficient (0.091M vs. 29M) than the current state-of-the-art multimodal fusion approach for video anomaly detection. The multilinear polynomial interpretation provides a principled framework for understanding what the learned weights capture, and the ability to fuse more than two modalities simultaneously (unlike MTN) opens the door to richer multimodal integration. Open questions include whether the graph-level fusion paradigm generalizes beyond anomaly detection to other vision tasks, and whether the degree variance regularization (specific to binary anomaly detection) can be adapted to other classification or regression settings.
