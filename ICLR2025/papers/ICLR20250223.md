## Problem

Sampling from large diffusion models is computationally expensive, requiring hundreds of denoising steps each with high FLOPs. Prior efficiency methods assume a single model is used throughout the entire trajectory, ignoring that early and late denoising steps have fundamentally different computational needs. No training-free approach existed to dynamically allocate different-sized pretrained models across the sampling trajectory to exploit this heterogeneity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250223/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

T-Stitch takes a pretrained large DPM and a smaller DPM from the same (or compatible) model family, along with a text prompt or class label and initial Gaussian noise, and produces a single generated image or video at the same resolution as the large model alone. The small and large models must share identical latent tensor dimensions (e.g., 4×32×32 for DiTs) and be trained on the same or similar data distribution. The user specifies a fraction r ∈ [0,1] controlling how many early steps the small model handles, which directly defines the speed-quality trade-off without any search or training.

## Method

T-Stitch is a training-free, step-level stitching strategy that allocates a smaller pretrained DPM to the first ⌊rT⌋ denoising steps and a larger DPM to the remaining ⌊(1−r)T⌋ steps, where T is the total number of sampling steps. The key insight is twofold: (1) different DPMs trained on the same data distribution learn highly similar latent embeddings, with cosine similarity approaching 100% at early steps, making cross-model trajectory handoff seamless; and (2) the denoising process generates low-frequency global structure early and refines high-frequency details later, so a smaller model suffices for the initial phase. The allocation is formulated as a compute-budget optimization problem, and for multi-model settings (e.g., DiT-S/B/XL), intermediate-sized models can be inserted at middle intervals to produce a smoother Pareto frontier. The method is fully complementary to existing acceleration techniques—step distillation, quantization, caching, and token merging can all be applied to the large-model portion of the trajectory independently.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet 256×256 with DiT-XL (DDIM 100 steps, CFG 1.5), replacing the first 40% of timesteps with DiT-S yields FID 8.95 (vs. 9.20 for DiT-XL alone) with ~1.5× speedup, and at 50% DiT-S the FID is 10.06 with 1.76× speedup (9.4s vs. 16.5s for 8 images on RTX 3090). The three-model combination (DiT-S/B/XL) achieves 1.7× speedup at ~10s with FID 9.21 (vs. 9.19 for DiT-XL). On MS-COCO 256×256 with SD v1.4 (PNDM 50 steps, CFG 7.5), 30% BK-SDM Tiny steps improve FID from 13.07 to 12.54 while reducing time from 3.1s to 2.8s per image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDSM (Yang et al., 2024), T-Stitch achieves a lower FID (8.1 vs. 8.3) at a comparable per-image time (0.44s vs. 0.45s) on CIFAR-10, while requiring only 1 evaluation run versus 500 for DDSM's evolutionary search. Against OMS-DPM (Liu et al., 2023), T-Stitch is slightly worse on FID (11.5 vs. 11.2) at a similar speed (0.04s vs. 0.03s), but eliminates the need for >6,000 evaluation runs. Against model stitching (SN-Netv2, Pan et al., 2023b), T-Stitch achieves a clearly better Pareto frontier on both FID and Inception Score across the full speed range (Figure 10), without requiring 1,700K training iterations on 8 A100 GPUs. Against early-exit methods (ASE, Moon et al., 2023), T-Stitch delivers substantially larger speedups: at 50% DiT-S allocation T-Stitch achieves 75.5% acceleration with FID 10.06, whereas the best ASE variant (D6-DiT) reaches only 36.8% acceleration with FID 11.41.

## Relation to Prior Work

T-Stitch carves new ground at the intersection of multi-expert diffusion models (Balaji et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2023) and model stitching (Pan et al., 2023a,b), but departs from both by being entirely training-free and by using a simple deterministic allocation (small model first, large model later) rather than learned or searched schedules. It is conceptually related to speculative decoding for LLMs (Leviathan et al., 2023) but exploits the specific property that DPMs trained on the same data distribution learn similar latent embeddings, especially at early denoising steps. Unlike prior multi-expert approaches that train new denoisers or allocate same-sized models across frequency bands, T-Stitch directly repurposes existing pretrained models of different sizes from a public model zoo, making it a drop-in technique complementary to step distillation, better ODE solvers, and model compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ~40% of early denoising steps can be replaced by a 10× smaller model with no measurable quality loss (and sometimes improved FID) provides a simple, training-free acceleration lever that is immediately deployable across DiT, U-Net, U-ViT, and Stable Diffusion ecosystems. The unexpected bonus that stitching a general small SD into a stylized large SD improves prompt alignment suggests that fine-tuning for style may degrade content understanding, opening a question about how to preserve prompt fidelity during style adaptation. Key limitations include the requirement for a sufficiently optimized small model on the same distribution, the speedup being bounded by the small model's efficiency, and the optimal switching threshold varying with guidance scale and model pair—areas the authors flag for future work, including training specialized experts at different denoising intervals.
