## Problem

Existing neural optimal transport methods that learn transport maps via max-min (adversarial) objectives suffer from severe training instability and hyperparameter sensitivity, often diverging after long training or failing at higher resolutions. This limits their practical applicability to high-dimensional tasks such as image-to-image translation, where stable convergence is essential for competitive FID scores.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250051/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes unpaired source images (e.g., male faces at 64×64 or 128×128, wild animal images at 64×64) and target images from a different domain, with no paired correspondence required. During training, it samples from both source and target distributions along with a random time t ~ U[0,1]. At inference, a single source image is mapped to a translated target image via the learned forward transport map, while a backward map simultaneously learns the reverse translation (e.g., Female→Male). The model trains two transport networks and one time-dependent discriminator network jointly.

## Method

DIOTM exploits McCann's displacement interpolation to parametrize the entire trajectory of intermediate transport maps from just two boundary networks: T_t(x) = (1−t)x + t·T_θ₁(x) for the forward map and T_t(y) = (1−t)y + t·T_θ₂(y) for the backward map. The paper derives a dual formulation of the displacement interpolation problem (Theorem 3.1) and proves that the optimal time-dependent value function V_t satisfies a Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation (Theorem 3.3). Training proceeds as a max-min adversarial game: the value function V_φ(t,x) is updated to increase a combined loss over both forward and backward generated samples, while the two transport maps are updated to decrease their respective cost-minus-potential losses, with the expectation taken over t ∈ (0,1). A novel HJB regularizer, R(V_φ) = E|2α∂_t V_φ + ½‖∇V_φ‖²|, is added to stabilize the value function by enforcing the HJB optimality condition across time, which the authors show is critical for robustness to the regularization strength λ.

## Results

On image-to-image translation, DIOTM achieves FID scores of 5.27 (Male→Female 64×64), 10.72 (Wild→Cat 64×64), and 7.40 (Male→Female 128×128), outperforming the closest OT-based baseline OTM (6.42, 12.42, 7.55 respectively). On synthetic 2D transport map benchmarks, DIOTM reduces the 2-Wasserstein distance W₂ by up to 36% (G→25G: 6.49 vs 10.09) and consistently improves L2 transport map error over OTM across all four datasets. DIOTM also demonstrates superior hyperparameter robustness, maintaining FID < 10 for λ ∈ {50, 100} while OTM diverges (FID > 60) under the same settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIOTM outperforms OTM (Rout et al., 2022; Fan et al., 2022) on all three I2I translation benchmarks, with the largest margin on Wild→Cat (10.72 vs 12.42 FID) and a clear advantage on Male→Female 64×64 (5.27 vs 6.42). Against the entropic OT baselines, DIOTM substantially outperforms DSBM (Shi et al., 2024) by over 27 FID points on Male→Female 128×128 (7.40 vs 37.8) and by over 9 points on Wild→Cat (10.72 vs 20+), and beats ASBM (Gushchin et al., 2024) by 8.68 FID on Male→Female 128×128 (7.40 vs 16.08). DIOTM also surpasses NOT (Korotin et al., 2023) by 6.69 FID on Male→Female 64×64 (5.27 vs 11.96) and CycleGAN (Zhu et al., 2017) by 7.67 FID (5.27 vs 12.94). On the synthetic 2D transport map evaluation (Table 1), DIOTM achieves lower W₂ on 3 of 4 datasets and lower L2 map error on all 4 compared to OTM, confirming more accurate recovery of the optimal transport map. A key practical advantage is training stability: DIOTM maintains FID < 10 across a wide range of regularization strengths (λ ∈ {1, 50, 100}), whereas OTM diverges for all λ ≠ 10.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial OT map learning framework of OTM (Rout et al., 2022) and NOT (Fan et al., 2022), which learn transport maps via the semi-dual Kantorovich formulation at the boundary (t=1). The key departure is deriving the dual formulation of the displacement interpolation (Wasserstein barycenter) problem at every intermediate time t, showing how the time-dependent Kantorovich potentials relate across the trajectory, and using this to train on the full interpolation path rather than just the endpoints. The theoretical backbone draws on McCann's displacement interpolation and the Benamou-Brenier dynamic OT formulation, while the novel HJB regularizer is derived from the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman optimality condition satisfied by the time-dependent value function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training instability in adversarial OT map methods has been a persistent practical barrier to scaling these approaches to high-resolution image tasks; DIOTM's displacement interpolation parametrization and HJB regularizer together address this, yielding stable training across a wide range of regularization strengths where OTM diverges. However, the method is limited to quadratic cost (the linear interpolation parametrization in Eq. 16 is specific to this case) and requires training both forward and backward transport maps, which is unnecessary for generative modeling where only the forward map matters. Open questions include whether the approach extends to general cost functions, whether the reverse map can be dropped without sacrificing stability, and whether the uniform time sampling is truly optimal.
