## Problem

Diffusion-based randomized smoothing methods achieve strong certified robustness but incur prohibitive inference costs (up to 52 minutes per sample) due to multi-step denoising and majority voting, while classical smoothing methods are fast but underperform at larger perturbation radii. The paper addresses this performance-efficiency gap by asking whether the structured noise schedule of diffusion models can be exploited directly during training to produce a single model that performs denoising and classification in one forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253406/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a noisy RGB image (clean image plus Gaussian noise at a specified level σ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1.0}) along with a continuous time-step embedding indicating the noise magnitude, and outputs class logits in a single ViT forward pass. Unlike diffusion-based baselines that require a separate denoising network followed by a classifier and often majority voting over multiple purification steps, rRCM produces its prediction in one shot, reducing ImageNet certification latency from minutes to ~53 seconds. The certified robustness radius is then computed via standard randomized smoothing with 10k–100k Monte Carlo samples around the input.

## Method

rRCM reformulates the generative image-denoising objective as a discriminative contrastive task in latent space, exploiting the fact that points on the same Probability Flow ODE trajectory share a unique clean initial condition. Pre-training uses an infoNCE-based consistency loss that attracts representations of temporally adjacent points on the same PF ODE trajectory (constructed via Tweedie's formula with shared Gaussian noise) while repelling points from different trajectories, combined with a standard contrastive loss over augmented clean views through a discarded projector head. The encoder is a ViT that ingests noisy image tokens plus a learnable class token and time embedding, and is fine-tuned with a consistency-regularized cross-entropy objective (adopted from Jeong & Shin, 2020) that enforces agreement between predictions on two independently noised copies of the same image. This two-stage procedure—contrastive trajectory alignment followed by supervised fine-tuning—yields a unified model that implicitly denoises and classifies in a single forward pass, eliminating the need for iterative purification or majority voting.

## Results

On ImageNet, rRCM-B-Deep achieves state-of-the-art certified accuracy of 77.4% at r=0, 64.0% at r=0.5, 51.2% at r=1.0, and 25.0% at r=2.5, outperforming the best diffusion-based method (DensePure K=5) by up to 11.6% at larger radii while reducing inference latency from 52 minutes to 1 minute 41 seconds (~85× speedup). On CIFAR-10, rRCM-B reaches 83.6% at r=0 and 39.2% at r=1.0, surpassing DDS by 6.4% at r=0.5 with only 16s latency versus 52s for DDS. The method also improves over classical randomized smoothing baselines (Gaussian, SmoothAdv, MACER) by an average of 8.48% across all perturbation radii on ImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against diffusion-based methods, rRCM-B-Deep dominates across all radii: it beats DDS (Carlini et al., 2022) by 5.3% on average (e.g., 51.2% vs. 41.4% at r=1.0) and DensePure K=5 (Xiao et al., 2022) by up to 11.6% at r=2.5 (25.0% vs. 14.0%), while running 85× faster than DensePure (1m 41s vs. 52m 20s). Against DiffSmooth (Zhang et al., 2023), rRCM-B-Deep leads by 14.8% at r=1.0 (51.2% vs. 36.4%) with 3.7× lower latency. Versus classical methods, rRCM-B-Deep surpasses SmoothAdv (Salman et al., 2019) by 6.2% at r=1.0 and MACER (Zhai et al., 2020) by 8.2% at r=1.0, while matching or exceeding Boosting (Horváth et al., 2021) at all radii. On CIFAR-10, rRCM-B outperforms DDS by 6.4% at r=0.5 (61.4% vs. 55.0%) and DiffSmooth by 2.2% at r=0.5, though it is slightly outperformed at r=0.75 by Boosting (48.0% vs. 52.4%), which ensembles 10 classifiers at 40s latency. Overall, rRCM achieves the best certified accuracy at every perturbation radius on ImageNet while maintaining inference costs comparable to classical single-pass methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

rRCM builds on the randomized smoothing framework (Cohen et al., 2019) and the diffusion-based purification line of work (DDS, DensePure, DiffSmooth), but departs fundamentally from the two-stage 'denoise-then-classify' paradigm by reformulating the generative denoising objective as a discriminative contrastive task in latent space. It borrows the structured noise schedule and PF ODE trajectory structure from EDM (Karras et al., 2022) and consistency models (Song et al., 2023), but repurposes them for representation alignment rather than image generation. The training objective draws directly from contrastive learning (infoNCE, MoCo-style EMA), while the fine-tuning loss is adopted from consistency regularization (Jeong & Shin, 2020). The key conceptual departure is treating temporally adjacent points on a PF ODE trajectory as positive pairs for instance discrimination, unifying denoising and classification into a single ViT model that requires only one forward pass at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 85× inference speedup over diffusion-based methods while simultaneously improving certified accuracy by 5.3% on average (up to 11.6% at large radii) addresses a critical practical bottleneck: certified robustness was previously either fast but weak (classical smoothing) or strong but prohibitively slow (diffusion purification requiring minutes per sample). This makes certified robustness viable for real-time security-sensitive applications. A key limitation is that the semantic similarity between points on different PF ODE trajectories (stochastic forward SDE) sets a fundamental upper bound on achievable robustness, a constraint shared by all diffusion-based methods. The paper also notes that performance has not yet plateaued with respect to model size and batch size, suggesting further gains are possible with larger training budgets.
