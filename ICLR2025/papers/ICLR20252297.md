## Problem

Non-IID data in federated learning causes local models to drift: label-driven training (cross-entropy) makes each client overfit its majority classes and develop inconsistent feature representations, degrading the aggregated global model. The paper argues that this client drift is an inherent limitation of the label-driven paradigm and proposes that language-driven representation learning, which has shown superior generalization in centralized settings, can fundamentally address the non-IID problem by providing a stable shared reference for all clients.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images of varying resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 28×28 for Fashion-MNIST, 256×256 for Office-Caltech-10/DomainNet, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and produces class labels. Unlike standard classifiers, there is no learned classifier head; instead, prediction is made by computing cosine similarity between the image embedding and a set of N pre-computed global text embeddings (one per class), with the argmax yielding the predicted label. The text embeddings are produced once on the server by a frozen BERT-base encoder applied to prompted class names (e.g., 'a photo of a {class}'), and are shared to all clients before training begins.

## Method

FedGLCL redefines the local training phase of FedAvg by replacing cross-entropy with a contrastive image-text alignment loss. A frozen pre-trained text encoder (BERT-base, 640-dim) embeds all N class names into a fixed global text feature space on the server; each client then trains an image backbone (ResNet-50 or AlexNet, from scratch) plus a lightweight two-layer MLP projector to map local images into the same space. The local loss treats the correct class text embedding as the positive key and all other N−1 text embeddings as negatives, using a temperature-scaled softmax contrastive objective. Because the text space is identical and fixed across all clients, minimizing this loss forces every local image encoder to converge toward the same feature geometry, simultaneously reducing inter-client feature drift and preventing overfitting to local majority classes (the negative terms act as implicit regularization from all classes). Model aggregation remains standard FedAvg averaging, and the communication overhead is actually slightly lower than FedAvg since the text embeddings (N×640) are smaller than a typical classifier layer (N×2048).

## Results

FedGLCL achieves state-of-the-art top-1 accuracy across all four primary benchmarks under both non-IID scenarios. On label distribution skew (CIFAR-100, β=0.5), it reaches 73.52%, improving over FedAvg (66.67%) by 6.85 points; on the more extreme Fashion-MNIST (C=2) setting, it achieves 56.63% vs. FedAvg's 42.69%. On feature distribution skew, FedGLCL attains 77.35% average on Office-Caltech-10 and 76.54% on DomainNet, surpassing the best scenario-specific baselines FedFA (71.36% and 72.94%) by 6.0 and 3.6 points respectively. The method also generalizes to Tiny-ImageNet (51.05% vs. 47.54% for FedAvg) and a long-tailed CIFAR-100-LT setting (56.14% vs. 45.29%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedGLCL outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and non-IID setting tested. Against the general-purpose FL baselines, it leads FedAvg by 6.85 points on CIFAR-100 (β=0.5) and 13.94 points on Fashion-MNIST (C=2); it surpasses FedProx (68.64%), MOON (70.91%), Scaffold (68.77%), FedNova (68.34%), and FedProto (70.04%) on the same CIFAR-100 setting. On feature distribution skew, FedGLCL (77.35% Office-Caltech-10, 76.54% DomainNet) beats the scenario-specific methods FedBN (69.04%, 70.09%) and FedFA (71.36%, 72.94%) by 6–10 points, demonstrating that its language-driven approach is more robust than domain-specific feature augmentation or batch normalization strategies. Among label-distribution-skew specialists, FedGLCL exceeds FedConcat (71.82%) and FedLC (65.70%) on CIFAR-100 (β=0.5), with FedLC actually underperforming FedAvg, confirming the inherent limitation of calibrated cross-entropy. With a pre-trained AlexNet backbone on Office-Caltech-10, FedGLCL (81.81%) still dominates FedPCL (72.46%), a prototype-based method designed specifically for pre-trained models. The paper further shows that combining FedGLCL's contrastive loss with FedProx or MOON's auxiliary terms yields additional gains (e.g., 75.56% vs. 73.52% on CIFAR-100 β=0.5 with MOON), indicating complementary benefits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper redefines the local training phase of FedAvg by replacing the standard cross-entropy (label-driven) objective with a CLIP-style contrastive image-text alignment loss, where a frozen pre-trained text encoder (BERT-base) on the server provides a fixed global text feature space that all clients align to. It departs from the dominant line of non-IID FL work (FedProx, MOON, FedProto, FedNova, Scaffold) that retains cross-entropy and adds auxiliary regularization or modifies aggregation, and also differs from prior FL+CLIP efforts (FedCLIP, PromptFL) that use CLIP as a pre-trained backbone rather than training the image encoder from scratch against a fixed text reference. The theoretical grounding draws on mutual information lower bounds from contrastive learning (CPC) and VC-dimension generalization bounds from prototype-based FL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedGLCL offers a unified, scenario-agnostic solution for both label and feature distribution skew, outperforming methods specialized to each setting, and its communication cost is actually lower than FedAvg since the text embedding matrix is smaller than a classifier layer. The finding that proxy/fake class names yield nearly identical performance to real class names opens a path for privacy-preserving deployment and suggests the structural consistency of text embeddings matters more than their semantic content. Open questions include scalability to very large class vocabularies (10K+), extension beyond classification to detection or open-vocabulary tasks, and whether the frozen-text-encoder assumption holds when the domain is poorly covered by the pre-trained language model.
