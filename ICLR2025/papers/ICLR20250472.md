## Problem

Existing explanations of in-context learning (ICL) in large language models—such as induction circuits, implicit gradient descent, and kernel regression—rely on synthetic settings or fail to account for the full range of observed phenomena (positional bias, label-noise robustness, demonstration saturation). This paper addresses the gap by proposing a unified, empirically grounded 3-step inference circuit that captures the actual dynamics of ICL in real-world, multi-layer LLMs trained on wild data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard ICL prompt consisting of k demonstration text-label pairs and a query text, formatted with structural forerunner tokens (e.g., colons) as delimiters: [x₁][s₁][y₁]…[xₖ][sₖ][yₖ][x_q][s_q]. The output is the predicted label token for the query. Internally, the analysis extracts hidden states at specific token positions (forerunner tokens and label tokens) across all transformer layers, measures their quality via mutual nearest-neighbor kernel alignment against BGE M3 sentence embeddings, and probes linear separability with centroid classifiers trained on 256 hold-out samples per dataset.

## Method

The paper decomposes ICL inference into three atomic operations on transformer layers: (1) Input Text Encode, where LMs produce linearly separable, task-relevant representations of each input text at its forerunner token position in early layers; (2) Semantics Merge, where 'Forerunner Token Heads' copy these text representations into the corresponding label tokens, creating joint representations that linearly combine text features and label semantics in separate subspaces; and (3) Feature Retrieval and Copy, where 'Induction Heads' retrieve label-token representations similar to the query on task-specific subspaces (defined by the low-rank kernel W_Q^hᵀW_K^h) and copy them back to the query's forerunner token. The circuit is validated through kernel alignment measurements, attention-head counting, centroid classification probes, and ablation experiments that selectively disconnect the corresponding attention connections. The paper further identifies bypass mechanisms (parallel circuits, direct decoding via residual connections, and shortcut induction between forerunner tokens) and uses the framework to explain positional bias, label-noise robustness via subspace overlap, demonstration saturation via submodular closure growth, and frequency bias from non-selective attention heads.

## Results

On Llama 3 8B with 4-shot ICL across 6 classification datasets, the baseline accuracy is 68.55%. Ablating the proposed 3-step circuit causes significant accuracy drops: disconnecting Step 1 (Input Text Encode) from all layers reduces accuracy by 30.60%, Step 3 (Feature Retrieval & Copy) by 10.22%, and Step 2 (Semantics Merge) by 3.42%, all far exceeding controlled random-disconnection baselines (≤3.29%). A key practical finding is that replacing the LM head with a centroid classifier on layer-34 hidden states achieves 84.27% accuracy at 2.38× speedup, versus 66.19% with the full 70B model + LM head at 1× speed, demonstrating that early layers complete the classification task and later layers merely verbalize it.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic interpretability paper rather than a method-comparison paper, so there are no direct head-to-head method baselines. The primary validation is ablation-based: the proposed 3-step circuit's attention connections, when selectively disconnected, cause accuracy drops of 10–31% depending on the step and layer range, while controlled experiments (randomly ablating equivalent numbers of connections) produce drops of at most 3.3%, confirming the circuit's causal role. Against zero-shot inference (50.65%), 4-shot ICL gains ~18 points, and the ablation of Step 3 (induction) brings accuracy close to but not exactly at the zero-shot level, revealing parallel bypass mechanisms. The paper also positions its 3-step circuit against prior theoretical explanations—implicit gradient descent (Dai et al., 2023), kernel regression (Han et al., 2023a), and implicit Bayesian inference (Xie et al., 2021)—arguing that none of these account for all observed phenomena simultaneously (e.g., gradient descent is fragile to label noise while ICL is robust; kernel regression cannot explain positional bias). The early-exiting inference result (Table 3) shows the centroid classifier on layer-34 hidden states (84.27%) substantially outperforms the full model with LM head (66.19%), suggesting the standard token-decoding paradigm is suboptimal for ICL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the induction circuit framework (Elhage et al., 2021; Singh et al., 2024b) from synthetic, linearly-separable settings to real-world LLMs (Llama 3, Falcon) by adding a verified Step 1 (input text encoding) and demonstrating that the forerunner token—not the label token as suggested by Wang et al. (2023)—is the primary encoding location. It builds on Cho et al. (2024) for centroid-based probing of hidden states and departs from prior ICL explanations (implicit gradient descent, kernel regression, Bayesian inference) by arguing none can simultaneously account for positional bias, label-noise robustness, and demonstration saturation. The key conceptual departure is showing the copy operation (Step 2) is a universal, non-selective pre-training behavior rather than an ICL-specific mechanism, with selectivity arising only from subspace overlap between label semantics and text features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified 3-step circuit provides a single mechanistic account for multiple previously fragmented ICL phenomena (positional bias, frequency bias, noise robustness, demonstration saturation), and the ablation evidence supports its dominance. The practical implication that early-layer hidden states with a centroid classifier can outperform full-model LM-head decoding (Table 3) suggests a concrete path to faster ICL inference. Open questions include whether the circuit generalizes to non-classification tasks, how the three steps interact at the level of individual attention heads, and how the IWL (no ground-truth label in context) scenario modifies the induction step—the paper shows induction heads become detrimental there but demonstrations still help via other pathways.
