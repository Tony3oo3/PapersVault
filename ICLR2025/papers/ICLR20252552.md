## Problem

Binarizing (1-bit quantizing) the weights of diffusion models has remained an open challenge: the extreme discretization from 32-bit to 1-bit severely restricts the information-carrying capacity of filters, and the resulting discrete parameter space disrupts gradient flow, causing training to collapse or converge to poor solutions. No prior method had successfully pushed DM weights to 1-bit while maintaining competitive generation quality, leaving a large gap between the theoretical 32× compression of binarization and practical deployability.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained diffusion models (pixel-space DDIM or latent-space LDM) and produces a binarized variant with 1-bit weights and configurable activation bit-widths (4, 8, or 32 bits). Inputs are standard random Gaussian noise for unconditional generation or noise plus class labels for conditional generation; outputs are images at the model's native resolution (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 256×256 for LSUN, FFHQ, and ImageNet). Notably, only the denoising network is quantized—the VAE encoder/decoder in LDM remains at full precision, and the first and last layers closest to input/output are kept at 8-bit following common practice.

## Method

BinaryDM combines two techniques within a quantization-aware training loop initialized from a pretrained full-precision DM. First, the Evolvable-Basis Binarizer (EBB) represents weights as a sum of two binary bases with learnable scaling factors (σ_I·sign(w) + σ_II·sign(w − σ_I·sign(w))) during an initial training stage, providing richer representation at initialization; a regularization loss then drives σ_II toward zero, smoothly evolving the model into a single-basis binarized form (σ_I·sign(w)) for inference. EBB is applied selectively to only the first and last six timestep-embedding blocks (~15% of parameters) to limit training overhead. Second, Low-rank Representation Mimicking (LRM) projects intermediate features of both the full-precision oracle and the binarized student into a low-rank subspace via a fixed PCA transformation (computed once from the first batch), then minimizes MSE between the projected representations to provide stable, direction-aware supervision that avoids the ambiguity of full-dimensional feature alignment. The total loss combines the standard diffusion denoising loss, the EBB regularization penalty, and the LRM distillation term.

## Results

On LDM-4 / LSUN-Bedrooms 256×256 (W1A4), BinaryDM achieves FID 7.74, sFID 10.80, Precision 64.71, and Recall 32.98, outperforming the vanilla binarization baseline (FID 10.87) by 3.13 and even surpassing the higher-bit W4A4 EfficientDM (FID 10.60) by 2.86 FID. On CIFAR-10 32×32 DDIM (W1A4), BinaryDM reaches IS 6.48, exceeding the full-precision baseline (IS 5.92) by 9.46%. In terms of efficiency, W1A4 BinaryDM achieves 15.2× OPs reduction and 29.2× model size reduction compared to full-precision LDM-4 (96.0→6.3 ×10⁹ OPs; 1045.4→35.8 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BinaryDM (W1A4, FID 7.74) outperforms all same-bit-width (W1A4) baselines on LDM-4 / LSUN-Bedrooms: it beats BI-DiffSR (8.58) by 0.84 FID, INSTA-BNN (9.42) by 1.68, Q-DM (9.99) by 2.25, ReActNet (10.23) by 2.49, the vanilla binarization baseline (10.87) by 3.13, and TDQ (11.28) by 3.54. Notably, BinaryDM at W1A4 also surpasses the higher-bit W4A4 EfficientDM (FID 10.60) by 2.86 FID while using only 26.5% of its model size and 25.9% of its OPs. On CIFAR-10 (W1A4), BinaryDM (IS 6.48) exceeds the full-precision DDIM baseline (IS 5.92) by 9.46%, whereas the vanilla binarization baseline collapses to IS 5.92. On ImageNet 256×256 conditional generation (W1A4, DPM-Solver), BinaryDM maintains IS 206.80 and FID 10.83 versus the baseline's IS 178.47 and FID 11.67, showing robustness across samplers. The only setting where BinaryDM does not lead is W1A32 on LSUN-Churches (LDM-8), where it achieves FID 9.91 versus the baseline's 12.98 — still a clear win, but the gap to FP (4.82) remains larger than on other datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the XNOR-Net binarization paradigm (σ·sign(w)) and the high-order residual quantization concept from Li et al. (2017) into the diffusion model setting, where no prior work had achieved 1-bit weight quantization. The key departure is the Evolvable-Basis Binarizer, which repurposes multi-basis residual structures not as a permanent inference-time architecture (as in prior residual quantization methods) but as a *transitional* training scaffold that regularizes away into a single-basis form. The Low-rank Representation Mimicking component draws on knowledge distillation principles but introduces PCA-based low-rank projection to avoid the direction ambiguity that fine-grained feature alignment causes in generative models, distinguishing it from standard MSE-based distillation used in prior DM quantization work (Q-DM, EfficientDM, TDQ).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first method to achieve accurate 1-bit weight binarization for diffusion models, BinaryDM opens a path toward deploying generative models on resource-constrained edge hardware with 29.2× model size and 15.2× OPs reductions at W1A4. The finding that a 1-bit model can outperform 4-bit quantization methods (e.g., W1A4 BinaryDM FID 7.74 vs. W4A4 EfficientDM FID 10.60 on LSUN-Bedrooms) challenges the assumption that higher bit-widths are strictly necessary for generative quality. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to transformer-based diffusion architectures (DiT, SD3), video generation, and larger-scale models, as all experiments are limited to U-Net-based DDIM/LDM at 256×256 resolution. The two-stage EBB training adds complexity and the method requires access to the full-precision model during training, limiting its applicability to post-training-only scenarios.
