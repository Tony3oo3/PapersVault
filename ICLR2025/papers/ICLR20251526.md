## Problem

Existing diffusion-based virtual try-on methods (e.g., OOTDiffusion, Stable-VTON) rely on implicit garment deformation through dual-branch UNet architectures, but the stochastic denoising process causes loss of fine-grained texture details and garment shape. The visual correspondence between the in-shop garment and the synthesized person remains under-explored, as the whole garment is simply fed as an appearance reference without explicit point-level alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251526/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target person image (I_p), an in-shop garment image (I_g), and estimated depth/normal maps of the person (derived from a pre-trained SMPL-based 3D human reconstruction model, not ground truth) as inputs, and produces a synthesized person image at 512×384 resolution wearing the garment. The garment is encoded both as a VAE latent code and a CLIP ViT-H/14 image embedding, while a garment-agnostic masked version of the person image provides the inpainting region. Inference uses 20 UniPC sampling steps with classifier-free guidance scale 2.0.

## Method

SPM-Diff introduces explicit Semantic Point Matching (SPM) into a dual-branch diffusion framework (Garm-UNet for garment feature learning, Main-UNet for generation). It samples K=25 semantic points on the garment via SuperPoint + farthest point sampling, projects them onto the target person through a GP-VTON-style local flow warping module, and augments the 2D point features into 3D-aware cues by fusing with geometry features extracted from depth/normal maps. These multi-scale point features are injected into the first two and last two self-attention layers of Main-UNet, where they modulate both the query and key projections to enforce garment-to-person correspondence in latent space. A novel point-focused diffusion loss (Eq. 8) further amplifies reconstruction accuracy at semantic point locations by adding a weighted L2 term masked to P_H, with λ=0.5. The entire pipeline is initialized from Stable Diffusion 1.5 (or SDXL for the sdxl variant) and fine-tuned with AdamW at lr=5e-5 for 45k iterations.

## Results

On VITON-HD (single-dataset evaluation), SPM-Diff achieves SSIM 0.911, LPIPS 0.063, FID 8.202, and KID 0.67, yielding absolute gains of +0.030 SSIM and −0.519 FID over the strongest prior diffusion-based method OOTDiffusion (SSIM 0.881, FID 8.721). With the superior Stable Diffusion XL backbone (SPM-Diff_sdxl), the method reaches SSIM 0.917, LPIPS 0.055, FID 6.871, and KID 0.52 on VITON-HD, and SSIM 0.933, LPIPS 0.038, FID 9.622, KID 0.14 on DressCode upper-body. Cross-dataset evaluation (Table 3) confirms consistent advantages, e.g., FID 9.235 vs. 10.003 for IDM-VTON_sdxl when transferring from VITON-HD to DressCode upper-body.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPM-Diff consistently outperforms all GAN-based baselines (PF-AFN, HR-VTON, SDAFN, FS-VTON, GP-VTON) across every metric on both VITON-HD and DressCode, with the largest margins over GP-VTON on DressCode upper-body (FID 10.560 vs. 20.110). Among diffusion-based methods, SPM-Diff surpasses OOTDiffusion by +0.030 SSIM and −0.519 FID on VITON-HD, and by +0.021 SSIM and −0.470 FID on DressCode upper-body. Against the strongest same-backbone competitor IDM-VTON_sdxl, SPM-Diff_sdxl achieves marginally better SSIM (0.917 vs. 0.916), LPIPS (0.055 vs. 0.061), FID (6.871 vs. 7.033), and KID (0.52 vs. 0.53) on VITON-HD, and better SSIM (0.933 vs. 0.926), LPIPS (0.038 vs. 0.040), and KID (0.14 vs. 0.16) on DressCode upper-body, though FID is slightly higher (9.622 vs. 9.561). Stable-VTON and DCI-VTON, which rely on implicit garment deformation via ControlNet or appearance flow, trail SPM-Diff by 0.05–0.06 SSIM and 0.5–1.5 FID on VITON-HD, confirming that explicit semantic point matching provides a stronger correspondence signal than holistic garment encoding. In cross-dataset transfer (Table 3), SPM-Diff_sdxl maintains its lead, e.g., FID 9.235 vs. 10.003 (IDM-VTON_sdxl) on VITON-HD→D.C.Upper and FID 8.196 vs. 8.203 on D.C.Upper→VITON-HD.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPM-Diff builds on the dual-branch latent diffusion architecture popularized by OOTDiffusion (two UNets for garment feature learning and image generation) and the flow warping module from GP-VTON for estimating garment-to-person displacement fields. Its key departure is replacing the implicit, whole-garment appearance reference with an explicit visual correspondence prior: sparse semantic points sampled via SuperPoint are matched to the target person through local flow warping, then augmented with 3D depth/normal cues to form structured point-level constraints on the diffusion process. A novel point-focused diffusion loss further amplifies this correspondence signal during training, distinguishing SPM-Diff from prior diffusion-based VTON methods that rely solely on VAE-encoded garment features or CLIP embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent weakness of diffusion-based VTON—loss of fine-grained garment texture and shape due to stochastic denoising—by introducing a principled, interpretable mechanism (semantic point matching) to constrain the generative process. The approach is lightweight (only ~25 points, ~0.5s extra inference) and generalizes across garment categories and datasets. However, limitations remain: artifacts on hands and failure to preserve decorative items when garments overlap them, and full-body try-on is handled sequentially rather than jointly. Open questions include whether the point-correspondence paradigm transfers to other detail-sensitive conditional generation tasks (e.g., face editing, object compositing) and whether 3D-aware point features can be learned end-to-end rather than relying on pre-trained 3D human reconstruction.
