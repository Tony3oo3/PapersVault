## Problem

Planning-based offline RL methods struggle in high-dimensional continuous action spaces, particularly under stochastic dynamics, where the curse of dimensionality and curse of history make direct search over raw actions computationally intractable. Existing sequence-modeling planners like Trajectory Transformer suffer from high decision latency and fail to properly account for environmental stochasticity, while deterministic models cannot capture the randomness inherent in real-world settings such as autonomous driving or robotic manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252222/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, L-MAP consumes an offline dataset of (state, macro-action, return-to-go) sequences collected under a fixed behavior policy, with macro-actions being fixed-length-3 chunks of primitive actions. At decision time, given only the current state observation (plus a goal position for AntMaze), the method outputs a single primitive action — the first step of the MCTS-selected macro-action — via a polling control scheme that replans at every time step. The planning horizon spans 9 raw actions for locomotion tasks and 15 for Adroit, corresponding to 3 and 5 macro-action steps respectively.

## Method

L-MAP discretizes state-macro-action sequences using a state-conditioned VQ-VAE whose encoder maps (R_t, s_t, m_t) tokens to discrete latent codes, with a key modification: the return-to-go is masked out during vector quantization so that stochastic return variability does not fragment the latent space, while a regularizer term aligns the masked embedding toward the full-input embedding to preserve return information. A separate causal Transformer (the Prior Transformer) autoregressively models the distribution of latent codes conditioned on the current state, simultaneously serving as a prior policy for sampling plausible macro-actions and as a latent transition model for planning. During inference, a pre-constructed latent search tree is built by sampling and decoding latent codes to rank macro-actions by return, then MCTS with progressive widening (controlling expansion via α and ε hyperparameters) refines the tree, using parallel expansion of B macro-actions per leaf to capture stochastic outcomes. The non-injective encoder mapping and stochastic behavior policy introduce apparent randomness even in deterministic environments, which MCTS mitigates by taking expectations over multiple sampled latent transitions.

## Results

On Stochastic MuJoCo (the paper's primary evaluation), L-MAP achieves a mean normalized score of 68.63, outperforming all model-based baselines (TAP: 52.53, TT: 46.28, 1R2R: 48.71) and model-free baselines (CQL: 61.47, IQL: 53.42). On deterministic D4RL MuJoCo, L-MAP scores 79.73, matching TT (78.88) and exceeding CQL (77.60) and IQL (77.32). On high-dimensional Adroit hand manipulation, L-MAP achieves the highest mean score of 51.40 across all datasets, and on AntMaze sparse-reward navigation it reaches 83.33% success rate, comparable to TT(+Q) at 84.00%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TAP (Jiang et al., 2023), L-MAP wins consistently across all four task suites: Stochastic MuJoCo (68.63 vs 52.53), D4RL MuJoCo (79.73 vs 70.77), Adroit (51.40 vs 49.33), and AntMaze (83.33% vs 56.33%), with the largest gap in AntMaze where TAP struggles with sparse rewards. Against TT (Janner et al., 2021), L-MAP clearly wins in stochastic settings (68.63 vs 46.28) and Adroit (51.40 vs 20.08), ties in deterministic MuJoCo (79.73 vs 78.88), and is marginally below TT(+Q) in AntMaze (83.33% vs 84.00%) while outperforming it in the harder Large-Play and Large-Diverse mazes. Against 1R2R (Rigter et al., 2023), L-MAP wins in most stochastic settings but loses on Medium-Replay-High Hopper (58.05 vs 68.25), suggesting risk-averse strategies retain an edge in specific high-variance scenarios. Against CQL (Kumar et al., 2020), L-MAP wins in the majority of settings but CQL edges out L-MAP on Medium-Expert-Mod Hopper (106.17 vs 106.11) and HIV Treatment (59.74 vs 59.08). Against IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2022), L-MAP wins across all evaluated task suites without exception.

## Relation to Prior Work

L-MAP builds on the VQ-VAE framework (van den Oord et al., 2017) for discrete representation learning and progressive widening (Couetoux et al., 2011) for controlled MCTS expansion, but departs from prior latent-space planners like TAP (Jiang et al., 2023) and Trajectory Transformer (Janner et al., 2021) by introducing temporal abstraction via macro-actions before discretization, rather than discretizing individual state-action pairs. The key conceptual departure is the masking-based VQ-VAE loss that decouples return variability from state-action structure during quantization, addressing a fragmentation problem specific to stochastic environments that prior latent planners do not handle. The dual-role Transformer (prior policy + latent transition model) and the pre-constructed search space with progressive widening represent a novel integration of learned abstractions with anytime planning, distinct from MuZero Unplugged's factorized policy approach or 1R2R's risk-sensitive value estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

L-MAP provides a practical recipe for planning in high-dimensional continuous action spaces under stochasticity, a setting where existing model-based offline RL methods (TT, TAP) degrade sharply and model-free methods (CQL, IQL) struggle with distributional shift. The graceful degradation with increasing stochasticity (33% drop vs. 45-48% for TAP/TT) and the lower decision latency from temporal abstraction make this approach relevant for real-time applications like autonomous driving and robotic manipulation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to online or non-stationary settings, how it scales beyond the tested action dimensions, and whether the polling control mode's per-step replanning overhead becomes prohibitive in very high-frequency control regimes.
