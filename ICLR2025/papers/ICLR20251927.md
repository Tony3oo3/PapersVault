## Problem

Existing LLM-based optimization methods rely on natural language as the interface, restricting them to domains well-represented in pretraining corpora (e.g., code, neural architecture search). For scientific domains like molecular optimization, domain-relevant data is scarce in text corpora, and verbose textual representations (e.g., SMILES strings) inflate context length, limiting the number of historical observations the model can condition on. This paper asks whether a pretrained LLM can be adapted to serve as a general-purpose surrogate model for black-box optimization in non-language scientific domains.

## Input / Output

Each molecule is encoded as a single 2048-bit Morgan fingerprint (2-radius), embedded into one hidden vector rather than spanning multiple tokens, which compresses the sequence and allows conditioning on more historical (x, y) pairs. At inference, all observed y values are normalized to zero mean and unit variance before being fed to the model, and the predicted mean and standard deviation are denormalized back to the original scale. The model outputs a mean and standard deviation per candidate, which are combined via a UCB acquisition function (u = μ + βσ) to rank candidates for evaluation.

## Method

LICO extends a frozen pretrained LLM (Llama-2-7B, fine-tuned via LoRA) with three new modules: an embedding layer mapping molecule fingerprints to the LLM's hidden space, an embedding layer for scalar property values, and a prediction head that outputs a mean and standard deviation. A key design choice is retaining natural language instructions—a task prompt and special tokens <x> and <y>—to guide the model's in-context reasoning in the embedding space, distinguishing LICO from prior frozen-transformer approaches like FPT that rely solely on self-attention over embeddings. Training uses semi-synthetic data from ZINC 250K: a mixture of 47 intrinsic molecular properties (molecular weight, ring count, heavy atom count, etc.) and synthetic functions sampled from Gaussian Processes with a Tanimoto kernel at a 10% ratio, with the model learning to autoregressively predict y given x and preceding (x, y) pairs. After training, a single LICO model serves as the surrogate in a standard BBO loop, generating candidates via crossover and mutation and selecting the top-k by UCB utility, without any task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

On PMO-1K (1000 oracle calls), LICO achieves the highest aggregated AUC Top-10 score of 11.71 across 23 molecular optimization tasks, outperforming Genetic GFN (11.56), MOLLEO (11.65), and GP BO (11.27). LICO attains the best or second-best performance in 14 out of 23 tasks. On the full PMO benchmark (10,000 oracle calls), LICO scores 14.708, remaining competitive with Genetic GFN (15.678) and Augmented Memory (15.002), despite not updating its model from real data during optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On PMO-1K, LICO (11.71) outperforms all six baselines: GP BO (11.27), Graph GA (10.90), REINVENT (10.68), Genetic GFN (11.56), Augmented Memory (10.81), and MOLLEO (11.65). On the full PMO (10K budget), LICO (14.708) trails Genetic GFN (15.678) and Augmented Memory (15.002), which both update their generative models from real evaluation data during optimization, whereas LICO relies purely on in-context learning without explicit training on downstream objectives. LICO still beats GP BO (13.156), Graph GA (13.751), REINVENT (14.196), and MOLLEO (14.682) on the full PMO. The paper notes MOLLEO's PMO-1K advantage is partly attributable to using BioT5 (a chemistry-finetuned LLM) and potentially contaminated prompts, while LICO uses a general-purpose Llama-2-7B. In predictive performance comparisons (Figure 2), LICO's surrogate quality directly correlates with its optimization scores: it outperforms GP on median1 and ranolazine_mpo but underperforms on troglitazone_rediscovery. GPT-4o prompted in text space (Appendix C.3) performs substantially worse than LICO on molecular property prediction, confirming the benefit of the embedding-space approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

LICO extends the frozen-LLM-plus-new-embedding-layers paradigm from FPT (Lu et al., 2022) by adding natural-language instructions (a task prompt and special tokens <x>/<y>) that guide in-context reasoning in the embedding space, rather than relying solely on self-attention over raw embeddings. It also builds on ExPT (Nguyen et al., 2023), which demonstrated that synthetic pretraining enables few-shot optimization, by introducing semi-synthetic training that mixes intrinsic domain properties with GP-sampled synthetic functions. The paper departs from prompt-based LLM optimization methods (MOLLEO, ChatDrug, OPRO) that operate in text space, arguing that embedding-space in-context learning avoids context-length bottlenecks and generalizes to domains not well-represented in pretraining corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LICO demonstrates that a general-purpose LLM (Llama-2) can serve as a competitive surrogate model for molecular optimization without any domain-specific fine-tuning, purely through in-context learning guided by semi-synthetic pretraining. A key limitation is the reliance on accessible intrinsic functions, which may not exist in all scientific domains, and the paper acknowledges that a better synthetic data generation process incorporating domain knowledge would be needed in such cases. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other non-molecular domains, whether better language instructions or larger base models yield further gains, and whether LLMs can be leveraged for other optimization components (candidate generation, exploration) beyond surrogate modeling.
