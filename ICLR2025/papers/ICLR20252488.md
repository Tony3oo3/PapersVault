## Problem

Intracranial and scalp EEG recordings have sparse, variable electrode layouts that change the semantics of input channels from subject to subject, forcing BMI and neuroscience studies to retrain expensive models per participant. Prior self-supervised approaches either model single channels in isolation, assume fixed channel configurations, or train end-to-end encoders that must heavily downsample high-fidelity signals to handle hundreds of channels. The paper asks whether one can learn subject-generic, population-level representations of arbitrary electrode ensembles that are both computationally lightweight and generalizable to unseen subjects.

## Input / Output

The model receives a variable-size set of per-channel temporal embeddings (produced by a frozen encoder such as BrainBERT, TOTEM, Chronos, or TS2Vec) concatenated with sinusoidal position encodings of each electrode's 3D coordinates (LPI for iEEG, XYZ for EEG) and ensemble membership, forming an S×d input matrix plus a [CLS] token. It outputs a 512-dimensional [CLS] representation for population-level brain state, spatial-contextual embeddings for each individual channel, and a scalar binary prediction via a linear head. For interpretability, the frozen pretrained weights yield connectivity maps (via channel-masking perturbation of the channel-wise loss) and attention-rollout weights grouped by Desikan-Killiany-Tourville atlas regions to identify candidate functional brain areas.

## Method

PopT is a 6-layer, 8-head transformer encoder (d_h=512, ~20M parameters) that aggregates frozen per-channel temporal embeddings into population-level representations, decoupling temporal and spatial modeling so the approach is agnostic to the underlying temporal encoder. Pretraining uses two simultaneous discriminative objectives: an ensemble-wise task where the model classifies whether two disjoint channel subsets recorded at different times are consecutive (|t−t'|=500 ms) or separated, and a channel-wise task where 10% of channels are swapped with activity from a random timepoint and the model must identify which channels were replaced. Both objectives use binary cross-entropy, and the discriminative (rather than reconstructive) formulation is critical because temporal embeddings have low effective dimension, making reconstruction reward overfitting to filler dimensions. The modular design means PopT training is an order of magnitude cheaper than end-to-end alternatives (1 GPU, 2 days vs. 4–8 A100s), and the pretrained weights generalize to held-out subjects with different electrode configurations, requiring fewer than 500 labeled samples to match baseline aggregation performance.

## Results

On the primary iEEG Brain Treebank benchmark (90 electrodes, 4 audio-linguistic tasks), pretrained PopT with BrainBERT achieves ROC-AUC of 0.74 (Pitch), 0.87 (Volume), 0.90 (Sentence Onset), and 0.93 (Speech/Non-speech), significantly outperforming linear aggregation (0.59–0.71) and the end-to-end Brant model (0.61–0.80) on all four tasks. On the EEG TUAB abnormal detection task (21 channels), pretrained PopT with TS2Vec reaches 0.806 balanced accuracy and 0.888 ROC-AUC, surpassing BIOT (0.796 / 0.882) but trailing the purpose-built LaBraM (0.826 / 0.916). Pretraining also yields substantial sample efficiency gains: the pretrained PopT reaches full baseline performance with fewer than 500 fine-tuning samples out of 5–10k available, and converges in under 750 training steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard aggregation baselines (linear and deep NN on concatenated temporal embeddings), pretrained PopT wins by large margins on every iEEG task — e.g., 0.93 vs. 0.71 ROC-AUC on Speech/Non-speech with BrainBERT — and the gap persists across all four tested temporal encoders (BrainBERT, TOTEM, Chronos, TS2Vec). Against the end-to-end iEEG foundation model Brant (Zhang et al., 2024), PopT+BrainBERT outperforms on all four tasks (e.g., 0.93 vs. 0.80 on Speech/Non-speech), attributed to PopT's explicit spatial aggregation via 3D position encoding. On the EEG TUAB task, PopT with TS2Vec (0.806 balanced accuracy, 0.888 ROC-AUC) narrowly beats BIOT (0.796 / 0.882) but falls short of LaBraM (0.826 / 0.916), which was specifically designed for that application. The non-pretrained PopT variant performs at or below the simple aggregation baselines, confirming that the self-supervised pretraining (ensemble-wise + channel-wise discriminative losses) is the critical ingredient rather than the transformer architecture alone. PopT also requires an order of magnitude fewer fine-tuning samples and roughly 10× fewer trainable parameters (20M vs. 500M for Brant) to reach competitive performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on BrainBERT (Wang et al., 2022) and other single-channel temporal encoders (TOTEM, Chronos, TS2Vec) by introducing a modular transformer layer that aggregates their outputs across variable electrode layouts. It departs from end-to-end self-supervised approaches (Brant, BIOT, LaBraM) that jointly learn temporal and spatial representations, instead decoupling the two and using discriminative (rather than reconstructive) pretraining objectives at both the ensemble and channel level. The use of 3D sinusoidal positional encoding to handle arbitrary, subject-specific electrode geometries is a domain-specific design choice not present in prior fixed-channel or channel-independent neural representation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular design means that any future improvement in temporal encoders automatically benefits the spatial aggregation model without retraining, and the 20M-parameter PopT trained on a single GPU in 2 days achieves competitive or superior decoding to 350–500M-parameter end-to-end models requiring 4–8 A100s — a practically significant efficiency gain for the BMI and neuroscience communities where per-subject calibration data is scarce. The interpretability results (connectivity maps from channel-masking, functional region identification from attention rollout) offer a new lens on pretrained neural models beyond pure decoding accuracy. Key open questions include extending the approach to settings without meaningful spatial coordinates, adapting to lower-SNR recording modalities, and whether the current pretraining dataset diversity is sufficient given the observed plateauing effect.
