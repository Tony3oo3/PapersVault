## Problem

Existing parameterization methods for dataset distillation—static decoding, parameterized decoding, and deep generative priors—suffer from limited coding efficiency, constrained expressiveness, and poor applicability to high-dimensional or non-image data. Moreover, the theoretical foundations governing how different parameterization choices affect the feasible space of decoded synthetic instances remain largely unexplored, leaving no principled way to compare their representational power under matched storage budgets.

## Input / Output

DDiF takes a large-scale grid-based dataset (e.g., 128×128 or 256×256 RGB images, 16-frame video clips, 64×64 audio spectrograms, or 32×32×32 3D voxels) with class labels and produces a set of K synthetic neural field parameter vectors Ψ = {ψ_j}. Each ψ_j is the weight set of a small SIREN MLP whose budget b is independent of the data dimension D, so increasing resolution does not inflate the per-instance storage. The coordinate set C (lattice points on [−1,1]^n) is derived purely from the target shape and is neither optimized nor stored, making DDiF a decoder-only framework. At evaluation time, decoded instances are obtained by evaluating each neural field at C, and the resolution can be changed simply by resampling C at different grid spacings.

## Method

DDiF replaces the conventional decoding function g_φ: R^{D'} → R^D with a neural field F_ψ: R^n → R^m (a 2–6 layer MLP with sine activation) that maps spatial coordinates to per-pixel/voxel quantities, so each synthetic instance is a single small network rather than a low-dimensional code plus a shared decoder. Training proceeds in two stages: a warm-up phase fits each neural field to a randomly sampled real instance via squared-error regression (Eq. 2), followed by joint optimization of all Ψ under a standard distillation loss (trajectory matching, distribution matching, or gradient matching). The key structural advantage is that the per-field budget b = d_0(n+1) + Σ d_l(d_{l−1}+1) + m(d_{L−1}+1) depends only on the coordinate dimension n and quantity dimension m, not on the number of grid points, enabling a single budget configuration to handle arbitrary resolutions. Theoretically, the paper shows (via Novello's sinusoidal network theorem) that the feasible space of DDiF is a sum of cosines with fully tunable amplitudes, frequencies, and phases, and proves (Theorem 3.2) that this space strictly contains that of FreD under the same per-instance parameter budget, implying a lower achievable distillation loss.

## Results

On ImageNet-Subset (128×128, IPC=1, TM loss), DDiF achieves test accuracies of 72.0% (Nette), 42.9% (Woof), 48.2% (Fruit), 69.0% (Yellow), 47.4% (Meow), and 67.0% (Squawk), outperforming the best prior method (Vanilla+RTP) by 2.4–3.8% points. At 256×256 resolution (DM loss), DDiF reaches 67.8% (Nette) and 67.0% (Squawk), surpassing the second-best (LatentDD) by 11.7–19.9% points. The improvement over input-sized parameterization (Vanilla) is +20.6 to +26.0% points at 128 and +35.7 to +39.4% points at 256, demonstrating that the gains are most pronounced under tight budgets and high resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDiF outperforms all 14 baselines across every ImageNet-Subset configuration. Against the strongest static method FreD, DDiF gains 5.2–10.0% points at 128×128 (e.g., 72.0 vs. 66.8 on Nette) and 13.6–23.9% points at 256×256 (e.g., 67.8 vs. 54.2 on Nette). Against the best parameterized method Vanilla+RTP, DDiF leads by 2.4–3.8% points at 128×128 (72.0 vs. 69.6 on Nette; 67.0 vs. 63.2 on Squawk). Against deep generative prior methods GLaD and H-GLaD, DDiF achieves substantially higher accuracy (72.0 vs. 38.7/45.4 on Nette at 128), indicating that neural field parameterization avoids the expressiveness bottleneck of fixed generative priors. At 256×256, DDiF surpasses LatentDD by 11.7–19.9% points (e.g., 67.8 vs. 56.1 on Nette; 67.0 vs. 47.1 on Squawk). In the cross-architecture generalization experiment (Table 3), DDiF maintains a 10.4–23.1% point lead over the second-best method across AlexNet, VGG11, ResNet18, and ViT, confirming that the distilled information is architecture-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDiF repurposes the neural field (SIREN) architecture from implicit neural representation literature as a parameterization mechanism for dataset distillation, departing from the three established families of decoding functions (static transforms like IDC/FreD, parameterized decoders like SPEED/HaBa, and generative priors like GLaD). The theoretical analysis extends Novello's (2022) characterization of sinusoidal networks as sums of cosines to derive a closed-form feasible space for DDiF's decoded instances, enabling a rigorous comparison with FreD's IDCT-based feasible space. Conceptually, the key departure is that neural fields take spatial coordinates as input rather than a low-dimensional code, making the decoding function resolution-independent and modality-agnostic by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DDiF provides a unified parameterization that naturally handles high-dimensional grid-based data across image, video, audio, and 3D voxel modalities without architecture-specific modifications, addressing a key scalability bottleneck in dataset distillation. The cross-resolution generalization evaluation protocol is a useful new diagnostic that reveals how existing methods silently degrade when test resolution differs from distillation resolution. Open questions include whether the theoretical feasible-space comparison extends beyond FreD to parameterized decoders or generative priors, whether the approach scales to non-grid data (e.g., point clouds, graphs), and whether the per-instance neural field overhead becomes prohibitive at very large IPC settings.
