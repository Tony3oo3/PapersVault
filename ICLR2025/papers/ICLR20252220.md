## Problem

In knowledge distillation, a too-large performance gap between teacher and student hampers knowledge transfer, yet existing methods rely on fixed pre-trained teachers that become suboptimal as the student improves. The paper addresses how to adaptively maintain a reasonable performance gap throughout the entire distillation process without requiring a dataset-specific 'best gap' value.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252220/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet and produces class labels. Unusually, the training pipeline operates on a pair of models (student and dynamic teacher) that share the same underlying parameters but differ in forward propagation: the dynamic teacher performs a direct forward pass through its expanded multi-branch architecture, while the student extracts its weights via online reparameterization. The final output is a compact student model extractable from the shared parameters without any post-training step.

## Method

GPD introduces a learnable dynamic teacher (DT) trained from scratch alongside the student, constructed via Inverse Reparameterization (IR) that expands the student along both channel and branch dimensions while preserving identical initial accuracy. A hard parameter-sharing strategy forces the student and DT to optimize the same weight set, with the student extracting its parameters via Channel-Branch Reparameterization (CBR): taking the first 1/r channel slice scaled by r, then merging multi-branch convolutions into a single equivalent kernel. The total loss combines standard KD (student→static teacher), a cross-entropy and KD loss for training the DT, and a KD loss from DT to student, with stop-gradient applied to all KD terms to enforce single-way knowledge flow. This bidirectional mapping (IR builds DT from student; CBR extracts student from DT) ensures the performance gap remains small and dynamic throughout training, unlike fixed-teacher approaches.

## Results

On ImageNet, GPD consistently improves existing KD methods across CNN and Transformer architectures. In the ResNet34→ResNet18 setting, DKD+GPD achieves 72.71% top-1 accuracy (+1.01% over DKD alone), while in ResNet50→MobileNet it reaches 73.63% (+1.58%). For the Transformer setting RVT-S→RVT-Ti, DKD+GPD attains 80.14% (+1.02%). Without any static teacher, GPD* (dynamic teacher only) improves ResNet18 from 70.07% to 71.87% (+1.80%) when training from scratch, and from 69.75% to 71.12% (+0.89%) during fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPD is evaluated as an additive module on top of two representative KD methods (DKD and ReviewKD) and consistently outperforms all 29 compared baselines across three architecture settings. In ResNet34→ResNet18, DKD+GPD (72.71%) surpasses the strongest prior method CKD (72.24%) by 0.47% and MLKD+LSKD (72.08%) by 0.63%. In ResNet50→MobileNet, DKD+GPD (73.63%) exceeds DIST (73.24%) by 0.39% and MLKD+LSKD (73.22%) by 0.41%. In the Transformer setting RVT-S→RVT-Ti, DKD+GPD (80.14%) outperforms ReviewKD (78.92%) by 1.22%. As a standalone method without a static teacher (GPD*), it also beats all self-distillation baselines: 71.87% vs. SSKD's 71.62% and TLLM's 72.6% (teacher-assistant) on ResNet18, and outperforms the longer-training fine-tuning baseline by up to 0.89%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation paradigm by introducing a dynamic teacher trained from scratch alongside the student, departing from the standard fixed pre-trained teacher assumption. It builds on reparameterization techniques from RepVGG (Ding et al., 2021) and OREPA (Hu et al., 2022) for merging multi-branch structures, but repurposes them in a novel bidirectional manner: Inverse Reparameterization expands the student into a larger teacher while preserving accuracy, and Channel-Branch Reparameterization extracts the student back from the shared parameters. Unlike prior teacher-assistant approaches (Mirzadeh et al., 2020; Son et al., 2021) that require separately pre-trained intermediate models, GPD constructs the dynamic teacher directly from the student via IR, and unlike standard KD, it enforces parameter sharing between student and teacher as a hard constraint for knowledge transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GPD provides a practical, plug-in mechanism that consistently improves existing KD methods (up to 1.58%) across CNN and transformer architectures, and notably generalizes to settings without a pre-trained teacher (training from scratch, fine-tuning), which most KD methods do not address. The Inverse Reparameterization technique is positioned as a general initialization method for building any-size pre-trained models, potentially useful beyond distillation. Limitations include evaluation only on ImageNet classification, a 33% training overhead, and the fact that the core observation (excessive teacher-student gap hurts distillation) was already established by prior work; the open question is whether the parameter-sharing mechanism and dynamic teacher approach transfer to other tasks (detection, segmentation, generation) where accuracy-based gap measurement is less straightforward.
