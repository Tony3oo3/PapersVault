## Problem

Existing physics-guided air quality models rely on closed-system assumptions (mass conservation) that fail to capture real open-system dynamics where pollutants enter, exit, are generated, and are absorbed at boundaries. Additionally, prior hybrid approaches force explicit physical equations onto implicit latent representations, creating a fundamental mismatch that degrades both physical interpretability and data-driven learning capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251983/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 24 steps (72 hours at 3-hour resolution) of PM2.5 concentrations and five meteorological covariates (temperature, pressure, humidity, wind speed, wind direction) at N stations, plus a geospatial graph G whose edges are gated by distance thresholds and topographic elevation barriers. It outputs 24 future steps (72 hours) of PM2.5 concentrations at all stations. The geospatial graph is static, while the advection subgraph is dynamically reweighted at each timestamp based on wind direction and speed projections onto inter-station edges.

## Method

Air-DualODE employs two parallel Neural ODE branches: a Physics Dynamics branch that solves a novel Boundary-Aware Diffusion-Advection Equation (BA-DAE) in explicit concentration space, adding a learnable correction term β⊙X to model open-system dissipation and generation, with Chebyshev GNNs approximating separate static diffusion and dynamic advection graph Laplacians; and a Data-Driven Dynamics branch that runs a Latent-ODE with Spatial-MSA (self-attention masked by the geospatial adjacency matrix) to capture spatiotemporal dependencies beyond the physical equation. The two branches are aligned via Decaying Temporal Contrastive Learning (Decay-TCL), which enforces similarity inequalities across time steps and dynamics types using decaying sigmoid weights, then fused spatially through a 3-layer GNN on the geospatial graph before decoding to predicted concentrations. The key design choice is keeping physical equations in explicit space where variables retain physical meaning, rather than projecting them onto latent variables, with contrastive alignment bridging the representational gap.

## Results

Air-DualODE achieves state-of-the-art performance on both the Beijing (city-scale, 35 stations) and KnowAir (national-scale, 184 cities) datasets. On the 3-day prediction task, it attains MAE of 40.32 (RMSE 62.04, SMAPE 0.74) on Beijing and MAE of 18.64 (RMSE 29.37, SMAPE 0.42) on KnowAir, outperforming all 11 baselines across every metric. On the more challenging sudden-change subtask, Air-DualODE records MAE of 66.40 on Beijing and 39.79 on KnowAir, again the best among all methods. The closest competitor, AirFormer, trails by 2.42 MAE on Beijing and 0.53 MAE on KnowAir for the 3-day setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Air-DualODE outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric across both datasets. The strongest data-driven competitor, AirFormer (a Transformer-based air quality model), is beaten by 2.42 MAE on Beijing (40.32 vs. 42.74) and 0.53 MAE on KnowAir (18.64 vs. 19.17) for the 3-day task. The closest physics-guided baseline, AirPhyNet, which uses a closed-system diffusion-advection equation, is surpassed by 2.40 MAE on Beijing and a notable 2.67 MAE on KnowAir, indicating that the open-system BA-DAE correction term provides a substantial advantage at national scale. Among ODE-based methods, LatentODE (43.35 MAE on Beijing) and ODE-LSTM (20.21 MAE on KnowAir) trail Air-DualODE by 3+ MAE, confirming that the dual-branch design with explicit physical dynamics adds value beyond a single latent-space ODE. Spatiotemporal GNN baselines (ASTGCN, GTS, MTSF-DG) cluster within 0.3–0.5 MAE of each other but all fall short of Air-DualODE, while classical methods (HA, VAR) are far behind, underscoring the benefit of learned spatiotemporal modeling. On the sudden-change subtask, Air-DualODE leads by 2.40 MAE over AirFormer on Beijing and 0.20 MAE on KnowAir, though the gap narrows, reflecting the inherent difficulty of forecasting abrupt pollutant spikes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the physics-guided deep learning line (AirPhyNet, STDEN, ClimODE) by addressing two specific gaps: (1) prior physics-based air quality models assume closed-system mass conservation, which is unrealistic for open air systems where pollutants enter/exit boundaries and are generated/absorbed locally; (2) prior hybrid approaches apply physical equations directly in latent space, creating a fundamental mismatch between explicit physical variables and implicit learned representations. Air-DualODE departs by solving the Boundary-Aware Diffusion-Advection Equation (BA-DAE) in explicit concentration space and pairing it with a separate Neural ODE branch in latent space, then aligning the two via Decaying Temporal Contrastive Learning. The architecture builds on Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018), Chebyshev GNNs for graph Laplacian approximation, and the Method of Lines for PDE-to-ODE discretization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The open-system formulation (BA-DAE with boundary-aware correction term β) is a more physically faithful model of real air pollution transport than the closed-system assumption used by AirPhyNet and most prior physics-informed approaches, and the dual-branch design cleanly separates what physics can explain from what data must fill in. However, the paper acknowledges that predicting sudden concentration changes remains a significant open challenge, and generalization to other spatiotemporal domains (traffic, water quality) would require re-deriving the physical branch. The approach's reliance on Layer Normalization for numerical stability with adaptive ODE solvers is a practical constraint worth noting for practitioners.
