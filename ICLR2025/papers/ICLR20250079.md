## Problem

The Partial Gromov-Wasserstein (PGW) distance relaxes the equal-mass constraint of classical GW to compare general positive Radon measures, but its non-convex optimization makes pairwise computation between K spaces require O(K²) expensive Frank-Wolfe solves. No linearization technique existed for the partial/unbalanced GW setting, leaving a gap between the expressiveness of PGW and the computational efficiency achieved by LOT and LGW in their balanced counterparts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250079/cropped_page9_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes discrete point clouds with non-negative weights (representing metric measure spaces) and a fixed reference gm-space, along with a gauge function (squared Euclidean distance or inner product). Each space is mapped to an LPGW embedding consisting of a kernel matrix K̃ ∈ ℝ^{n×n} (encoding pairwise distance discrepancies), a projected weight vector q̃ ∈ ℝ_+^n (encoding transported mass on the reference), and a scalar mass-creation term |γ_c|. Pairwise distances between any two spaces are then computed as a quadratic form (q̃^{12})^T[(K̃₁−K̃₂)²](q̃^{12}) plus a λ-weighted mass penalty, avoiding any further optimization.

## Method

The LPGW embedding extends the LGW barycentric-projection linearization to the unbalanced setting by mapping each target gm-space Y to a triple (γ_X, k_γ, γ_c) that separately encodes source mass transport, the gauge-function discrepancy, and target mass creation. Under the PGW-Monge mapping assumption, the pairwise LPGW distance reduces to an L² norm of the kernel difference weighted by the infimal convolution of the two projected measures, plus a λ-penalty on total mass — a closed-form expression requiring no further optimization. A barycentric projection theorem (Theorem 3.2) guarantees that projecting the optimal PGW plan onto the reference space yields a valid plan for the projected space, enabling the practical aLPGW approximation. The key computational gain is that K PGW solves against a single reference space (O(K) expensive solves) replace the O(K²) pairwise PGW solves, with the remaining pairwise distance evaluations costing only O(n₀²) each. The paper proves that under a sufficient condition on λ relative to the gauge-function range, the LPGW discrepancy is a valid metric on the space of gm-spaces.

## Results

On the 2D shape retrieval task, LPGW achieves 97.5% SVM accuracy (vs. 98.1% for GW and 96.2% for PGW) while computing all pairwise distances in 0.5s compared to 43s for GW. On the 3D shape retrieval task, LPGW reaches 93.7% accuracy (nearly matching PGW's 93.8%) in 1.8 minutes versus 203 minutes for PGW. On the MNIST transform-based embedding task with noise corruption (η=0.1), LPGW maintains 81.8% classification accuracy while LOT drops to 13.4% and LGW to 17.0%, demonstrating LPGW's superior robustness to partial mass perturbation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GW, LPGW sacrifices only 0.6% accuracy on 2D shapes (97.5% vs. 98.1%) but is ~86× faster (0.5s vs. 43s); on 3D shapes, LPGW actually exceeds GW by 1.2% (93.7% vs. 92.5%) while being ~113× faster (1.8 min vs. 203 min). Against PGW, LPGW wins on 2D accuracy (97.5% vs. 96.2%) and is essentially tied on 3D (93.7% vs. 93.8%), but achieves a ~113× speedup on the 3D dataset. Against LGW, LPGW consistently outperforms on both 2D (97.5% vs. 93.7%) and 3D (93.7% vs. 92.5%) shape retrieval with comparable computation time, confirming that the partial matching mechanism adds value. Against LOT on the MNIST noise-robustness experiment, LPGW is dramatically superior: at η=0.1, LPGW achieves 81.8% accuracy while LOT collapses to 13.4% (random guessing), and this gap persists through η=0.5 (72.9% vs. 12.5%), showing that LPGW's unbalanced formulation is essential for handling corrupted/incomplete data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear Gromov-Wasserstein (LGW) embedding of Beier et al. (2022) to the unbalanced/partial setting, combining it with the Partial Gromov-Wasserstein (PGW) formulation of Bai et al. (2024). The linearization strategy follows the same barycentric-projection paradigm introduced for classical OT by Wang et al. (2013) and adapted to GW by Beier et al., but now handles the additional mass-creation and mass-transportation terms inherent to the partial setting. The key conceptual departure from LGW is the three-component embedding (source marginal, cost function, target mass creation) and the use of the infimum over sub-measures μ′ ≤ γ_X¹ ∧ γ_X² in the distance definition, which replaces the fixed reference measure of the balanced case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(K²) → O(K) reduction in transport-plan computations makes PGW-based shape comparison feasible for large collections, and the proof that LPGW defines a valid metric under a boundedness condition on the gauge functions provides theoretical grounding for using it in downstream tasks. The robustness finding in the noise-corruption experiment (LPGW retaining ~73% accuracy at η=0.5 while LOT and LGW collapse to chance) suggests the partial-matching inductive bias is genuinely useful for handling incomplete or corrupted data, not merely a theoretical convenience. Open questions include whether the barycentric-projection approximation remains tight for highly non-convex gauge functions, how the method scales to very large point clouds (the discrete implementation still requires solving PGW per reference space), and whether the metric property can be established without the restrictive condition (17) on λ.
