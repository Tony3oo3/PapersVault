## Problem

Flexible-granularity video part segmentation—where the number and identity of segments are defined by a few annotated reference images rather than a fixed taxonomy—remains largely unexplored. Existing image-level approaches like SLiMe can segment arbitrary parts in a single frame but produce flickering, temporally inconsistent results when applied frame-by-frame to video, while video segmentation methods (VOS, VIS) either require per-video annotations or cannot handle arbitrary, non-text-describable part boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252898/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 1–10 reference images (H×W×3 RGB) with K binary segment masks each, where the references are deliberately *not* frames from the target video, and a video clip of the same object class in arbitrary pose, color, or scale. It outputs per-frame, per-segment binary masks for all M video frames and all K segments, with temporal consistency enforced across the entire clip. The granularity is fully user-defined (e.g., 6 parts for a face, 4 for a horse), and segments need not be describable by natural language (the 'Non-Text' category).

## Method

SMITE inflates a pretrained Stable Diffusion U-Net into a spatio-temporal denoiser by converting 3×3 convolutions to 1×3×3 and extending self-attention to operate over all video frame patches simultaneously. Training is two-phase: first, per-segment text embeddings are optimized at a high learning rate to align WAS (Weighted Accumulated Self-Attention) maps with reference masks; then cross-attention layers are unfrozen and jointly fine-tuned at a much lower rate to capture fine-grained, asymmetric boundaries that embeddings alone miss. At inference, temporal consistency is enforced through three coupled mechanisms: (1) CoTracker point trajectories are projected onto the attention-map latent space and a sliding-window temporal voting scheme reassigns unstable pixel labels to the most frequent segment in the window; (2) a DCT-based low-pass regularization penalizes deviation from the initial denoising-step segment structure, preventing tracking-induced drift; and (3) both signals are combined into a total energy (cross-entropy tracking loss + L1 low-pass loss) whose gradient is backpropagated through the diffusion denoising steps to iteratively refine the latent, yielding flicker-free, reference-faithful segmentations.

## Results

On the proposed SMITE-50 benchmark (50 multi-granularity video clips across Faces, Horses, Cars, and Non-Text categories), SMITE achieves a mean mIOU of 75.14 and mean F-measure of 0.82, outperforming all baselines by a substantial margin. The closest competitor, Baseline-III (SLiMe with Inflated UNet), reaches 69.36 mIOU, while frame-by-frame SLiMe (Baseline-I) and Grounded SAM2 (GSAM2) score 66.62 and 66.53 mIOU respectively. In the user study, SMITE receives the best motion-consistency ranking (1.46/5 for textual segments, 1.03/5 for non-textual), far ahead of the next-best method (2.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMITE outperforms all four baselines on SMITE-50 by 5.8–9.6 mIOU points, with the largest gap over Baseline-II (SLiMe + CoTracker in pixel space, 65.50 mIOU) and GSAM2 (66.53 mIOU). The incremental gains from each component are visible: adding the Inflated UNet to SLiMe (Baseline-III) improves mIOU from 66.62 to 69.36, cross-attention tuning adds another ~2.3 points, tracking adds ~0.9, and low-pass regularization contributes the final ~2.5 points to reach 75.14. On PUMaVOS, SMITE matches or beats the fully-supervised XMem++ at 1 frame (82.14 vs 80.65 mean mIOU) and 5 frames (90.44 vs 90.80), but XMem++ pulls ahead at 10 frames (94.48 vs 91.56), which is expected given XMem++'s large-scale pre-training. On DAVIS-2016 (single-object VOS), SMITE+ (excluding 2 thin-structure videos) achieves 87.6 F in zero-shot and 92.0 J in semi-supervised, competitive with dedicated VOS methods like XMem (92.7 F) and AOT (92.1 F), despite requiring only one reference image rather than video-level training. For image part segmentation on PASCAL-Part, SMITE improves over SLiMe by 1.5 mIOU (car) and 2.9 mIOU (horse), confirming the benefit of cross-attention fine-tuning even without the video component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SLiMe (Khani et al., 2024), which uses optimized text embeddings and WAS maps from a pretrained Stable Diffusion model for arbitrary-granularity image part segmentation, into the video domain. The key architectural departure is inflating the UNet with spatio-temporal attention (following Tune-a-Video) and coupling it with CoTracker-based temporal voting projected onto attention maps, plus a DCT low-pass regularization to prevent segment drift. Unlike prior video segmentation methods (XMem++, GSAM2) that require annotations from the target video or rely on textual descriptions, SMITE segments from external reference images only, and unlike prior diffusion-based video segmentation (Wang et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2024), it targets fine-grained part-level segments rather than whole-object masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for VFX and large-scale video production, where consistent part-level segmentation across hundreds of shots currently requires per-video manual annotation; SMITE eliminates this by learning from a handful of reference images. The introduced SMITE-50 dataset (50 multi-part videos with 20% dense annotations) fills a gap, as no prior dataset targets multi-granularity video part segmentation. Key limitations remain: the method struggles with thin structures and small objects due to low-resolution cross-attention, is not interactive, and its temporal consistency is bounded by CoTracker's accuracy. Open questions include whether the diffusion-based optimization can be made real-time, whether the approach generalizes to very long videos without quality degradation, and whether the two-phase training strategy transfers to other diffusion-based segmentation tasks.
