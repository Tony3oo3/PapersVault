## Problem

Spectral clustering scales well to many clusters on sparse graphs, while coreset kernel k-means provides approximation guarantees but is slow on sparse kernels (linear in k) and prone to local optima. These two complementary techniques have been developed in isolation, and no prior work combines them to get both the scalability of spectral methods and the theoretical guarantees of coresets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251918/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes an undirected graph G=(V,E) with adjacency matrix A_G and degree matrix D_G, along with a target number of clusters k. It internally converts the graph to a weighted kernel k-means problem via K_G = D_G^{-1}A_G D_G^{-1} and W_G = D_G, constructs an ε-coreset of size Õ(k²ε^{-4}), and outputs a k-partition of all n vertices where each vertex is assigned to the nearest coreset centroid in the implicit Hilbert space. The coreset size is independent of n, making the downstream spectral clustering step operate on a small subgraph.

## Method

The paper introduces two key contributions. First, a fast D²-sampling algorithm (Algorithm 2) that exploits kernel sparsity via a balanced sampling tree data structure: by pre-selecting the minimum-self-affinity point x*, distance updates propagate only to neighbors of newly added centers, reducing coreset construction from Õ(nk) to Õ(n·min{k, d_avg}). Second, the Coreset Spectral Clustering (CSC) algorithm (Algorithm 3) which converts the graph to a kernel k-means problem, builds an ε-coreset, runs spectral clustering directly on the small coreset graph (rather than running Lloyd's algorithm in kernel space), and then labels all original vertices by nearest coreset centroid in feature space. The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that an α-approximation of the normalised cut on the coreset graph yields an O(α)-approximation on the original graph, with the approximation factor (1+ε)/(1−ε) from the coreset guarantee. This sidesteps the local-optima problem of kernel k-means with indefinite kernels while preserving approximation guarantees.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that an α-approximation of the normalised cut on the coreset graph yields an O(α)·(1+ε)/(1−ε) approximation on the original graph. Empirically, the faster coreset construction (Algorithm 2) is asymptotically faster than Jiang et al.'s method on graphs up to 65M nodes, breaking the linear dependence on k. On the HAR dataset, CSC matches full spectral clustering quality (ARI) using a coreset under 5% of the original size in a fraction of a second, while coreset kernel k-means degrades due to local optima. On synthetic stochastic block model graphs, Fast CSC achieves ARI ≈ 0.5 on 250K nodes with 250 clusters in under 3 seconds, whereas coreset kernel k-means becomes ineffective after only ~50 clusters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against coreset kernel k-means (Jiang et al., 2024), CSC wins decisively on clustering quality: on real-world datasets (HAR, PenDigits, Letter, MNIST), CSC achieves ARI comparable to full spectral clustering while coreset kernel k-means suffers from local optima that reduce its ARI, especially as coreset size increases. On synthetic graphs with many clusters, the gap is stark—Fast CSC maintains ARI ≈ 0.5 at 250 clusters while coreset kernel k-means drops to near-zero ARI after ~50 clusters. Against sklearn spectral clustering, CSC is dramatically faster (running in a fraction of a second vs. minutes) while matching its ARI and normalised cut quality, since the coreset is less than 5% of the original graph size. The faster D²-sampling (Algorithm 2) also outperforms the original D²-sampling (Algorithm 1) from Jiang et al. in running time on all three large graphs (Friendster, LiveJournal, wiki-topcats), with the speedup growing as k increases, confirming the theoretical Õ(n·min{k, d_avg}) vs. Õ(nk) improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: coreset construction for kernel k-means (Jiang et al., 2024) and spectral clustering on sparse graphs (Macgregor, 2023; Sun & Zanetti, 2019), leveraging the known equivalence between normalised cut and kernel k-means (Dhillon et al., 2004). The key departure from Jiang et al. is twofold: (1) a faster D²-sampling algorithm that exploits kernel sparsity via a sampling tree data structure, reducing coreset construction from Õ(nk) to Õ(n·min{k, d_avg}), and (2) a new Coreset Spectral Clustering (CSC) algorithm that applies spectral clustering directly to the coreset graph rather than running kernel k-means on the coreset, thereby avoiding the local-optima pathology of kernel k-means with indefinite kernels. The D²-sampling speedup builds on the k-means++ seeding framework of Arthur & Vassilvitskii (2007) adapted to kernel space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Breaking the linear dependence on the number of clusters k in coreset construction is critical for practical use on large graphs with thousands of clusters, and the clean O(α) approximation guarantee (Theorem 1) provides theoretical assurance that spectral clustering on the coreset transfers to the full graph. The empirical finding that coreset kernel k-means becomes ineffective beyond ~50 clusters while CSC scales to 250+ clusters highlights a practical limitation of the prior approach that the CSC framework overcomes. Limitations include the O(n²) labelling step for assigning non-coreset nodes, the requirement that the kernel be sparse, and the (1+ε)/(1−ε) approximation factor; the many-clusters experiments are on synthetic SBM graphs, so real-world performance at very large k remains to be validated.
