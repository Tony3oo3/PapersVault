## Problem

Discrete-space generative models based on continuous-time Markov chains have a severely limited design space, with most prior work restricted to a simple masked corruption process. The relationship between probability paths and their generating velocities was not understood in general, preventing the use of domain-specific or non-mask corruption processes. This paper asks: can we decouple the choice of probability path from the velocity, and find principled (kinetic-optimal) velocities that work for arbitrary discrete paths?

## Input / Output

The model takes a fully masked or noise-corrupted sequence as input and iteratively refines it via a CTMC sampling procedure, optionally conditioned on a class label. For text, the output is a token sequence; for crystal generation, a variable-length sequence of 6+4a tokens (lattice parameters plus per-atom type and fractional coordinates) with permutation invariance over atoms; for images, either 256-level pixel values (CIFAR-10) or 16×16 grids of VQVAE codes from a 16384-entry codebook (ImageNet-256). Unlike autoregressive baselines, all positions are updated in parallel at each step, and the model directly predicts a factorized posterior p_{1|t}(x_1^i | x_t) over every position simultaneously.

## Method

The paper formulates discrete flow matching through the lens of kinetic energy minimization over fluxes, deriving a closed-form kinetic-optimal velocity u_t*(x,z|x_1) = p_t(x|x_1)[∂_t log p_t(x|x_1) − ∂_t log p_t(z|x_1)]_+ that is valid for any user-specified probability path, completely decoupling path design from velocity computation. For mixture paths, this recovers the velocity from Gat et al. (2024) and yields a tractable ELBO for training; for metric-induced paths p_t(x|x_1) = softmax(−β_t·d(x,x_1)), the velocity has the interpretable property of only moving samples closer to the target in metric distance. The paper further shows that optimizing the probability path itself under the same kinetic energy criterion yields mixture paths with novel x_1-dependent schedulers κ_t(x_1) = 1 − sin²((1−t)Ω)/sin²(Ω), justifying the mixture construction as kinetic-optimal. A probability-preserving (divergence-free) corrector velocity can be added to improve high-NFE sampling quality, and a power-∞ velocity variant boosts low-NFE performance. Training uses the derived ELBO for mixture paths or cross-entropy for general paths, with a DiT transformer architecture predicting the factorized posterior.

## Results

On de novo inorganic material generation (MP-20), permutation-invariant DFM with the kinetic-optimal scheduler achieves a 7.02% stability rate, a ≥38% relative improvement over the best prior methods (FlowMM: 4.65%, DiffCSP: 5.06%, CrystalLLM-70B: 5.28%). On face-blurred ImageNet-256 (16×16 VQVAE latent), DFM with a metric-induced probability path achieves FID 4.50 at 100 NFE, outperforming the autoregressive LlamaGen baseline (FID 4.81 at 256 NFE) and the masked DFM construction (FID 5.72). On CIFAR-10 pixel-space generation, DFM with a metric path achieves FID 3.43, improving over DFM with masking (3.63) and τ-LDR-10 (3.74). On text (FineWeb-Edu), the kinetic-optimal scheduler with β₀=1024 achieves the best ELBO perplexity (≤58.41 on LAMBADA, ≤18.63 on FineWeb-Edu train), matching or slightly beating the masked construction and outperforming SEDD and MD4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On material generation, permutation-invariant DFM with the kinetic-optimal scheduler (7.02% stability rate) substantially outperforms all prior methods: FlowMM (4.65%), DiffCSP (5.06%), CrystalLLM-70B (5.28%), CDVAE (1.57%), and the autoregressive baseline (1.99%), achieving a ≥38% relative improvement over the best continuous-space and autoregressive baselines while using only 250 NFE. On face-blurred ImageNet-256, DFM with a metric-induced path (FID 4.50 at 100 NFE) beats the autoregressive LlamaGen (FID 4.81 at 256 NFE) and the masked DFM construction (FID 5.72 at 100 NFE), demonstrating that the metric path is critical for latent-space generation. On CIFAR-10 pixel space, DFM with a metric path (FID 3.43) improves over DFM with masking (3.63), τ-LDR-10 (3.74), CTDD (7.86), and D3PM (7.34). On text generation, the kinetic-optimal scheduler with β₀=1024 achieves the best ELBO perplexity across most benchmarks (e.g., ≤58.41 LAMBADA, ≤115.51 1BW), narrowly beating the masked construction (≤58.5 LAMBADA) and clearly outperforming SEDD (≤58.57) and MD4 (≤61.27). The paper also shows that the kinetic-optimal scheduler consistently dominates the linear scheduler on the generative-perplexity vs. ELBO Pareto front (Figure 1), and that adding the probability-preserving velocity component stabilizes high-NFE sampling while the choice of probability-advancing velocity primarily affects low-NFE quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Discrete Flow Matching (Gat et al., 2024) by decoupling the design of probability paths from their generating velocities, a separation that was implicit but not formalized in prior discrete CTMC-based generative models. The kinetic-optimal velocity framework directly adapts the continuous-space kinetic energy minimization of Shaul et al. (2023) and Lipman et al. (2022) to the discrete setting, while recovering and correcting the velocities of Campbell et al. (2024) with a numerically stable safe flux. The key conceptual departure from prior work is that arbitrary probability paths (not just masked or simple mixture constructions) become tractable, and the kinetic-optimal criterion provides a principled way to select both velocities and paths simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By opening the design space beyond the masked construction, this work gives practitioners domain-specific tools (metric-induced paths for images, permutation invariance for crystals) that were previously inaccessible within the discrete flow matching framework. The kinetic-optimal scheduler for mixture paths is a practical improvement that outperforms linear schedulers across text benchmarks, and the derivation of a tractable ELBO for general mixture paths removes a key training limitation. Open questions include whether the kinetic-optimal perspective scales to very large discrete alphabets (e.g., |T| > 10^5), whether the probability-preserving corrector velocity can be made more principled beyond the symmetric-flux heuristic, and whether the design space can be further exploited for conditional or multi-modal discrete generation.
