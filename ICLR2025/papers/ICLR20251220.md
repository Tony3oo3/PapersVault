## Problem

Sequential Probability Ratio Test (SPRT) provides theoretically optimal stopping rules for early time series classification, but under finite horizons the optimal decision boundary requires solving a backward induction that has no closed-form solution and is computationally intractable on real-world data due to the curse of dimensionality and the absence of true log-likelihood ratios. This gap between SPRT's theoretical optimality and practical deployability has prevented its use in time-sensitive applications with hard deadlines.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of T feature vectors (e.g., 512-dim from ResNet-152 for face anti-spoofing, 2048-dim from ResNet-50 for HMDB51, 1538-dim from DINOv2 for UCF101) one at a time, updating the sufficient statistic (LLRs or posteriors) at each step. At each time step t, the model outputs a binary continue/stop decision; upon stopping, it emits a class label from K classes. The stopping time τ is data-dependent and typically well before T, and the decision boundary is time-varying (tapering toward the horizon) rather than a fixed threshold.

## Method

FIRMBOUND decomposes the intractable backward induction into two estimation subproblems with statistical consistency guarantees. First, it exploits the concavity of the conditional expectation (continuation risk) to formulate its estimation as a convex function learning problem solved via 2-block ADMM with a piecewise-linear representation, or alternatively via variational Gaussian Process regression (30× faster training). Second, it estimates the sufficient statistic (LLRs) using the SPRT-TANDEM sequential density ratio estimation algorithm trained with a Log-Sum-Exp Loss, which is consistent for both i.i.d. and non-i.i.d. sequences of any class count. At test time, the trained CFL/GP model evaluates the continuation risk Ẑ_t and compares it against the analytically computable stopping risk G_t^st; the decision is made at the first t where G_t^st ≤ Ẑ_t, with the class chosen by argmin of the a posteriori risk. Theorem 3.2 establishes that FIRMBOUND with CFL converges to the Bayes-optimal AAPR-minimizing rule as dataset size grows.

## Results

FIRMBOUND minimizes the Average A Posteriori Risk (AAPR) to delineate Pareto-optimal points on the speed-accuracy tradeoff (SAT) curve across all seven datasets (two-class and three-class sequential Gaussians, DOL, SiW, HMDB51, UCF101, FordA). On the DOL dataset, FIRMBOUND advances the Pareto front to a new optimal level, achieving notably lower error rates than static-threshold SPRT. The mean variance of hitting time (MVHT) is statistically significantly reduced (Wilcoxon signed-rank test, p = 2.00×10⁻⁸), e.g., from 489.89 to 405.78 on DOL and from 32.15 to 23.39 on FordA. GP regression trains ~30× faster than CFL (≈20 min vs. ≈10 hours on a two-class Gaussian dataset) while retaining comparable performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIRMBOUND is compared against SPRT with static thresholds (the primary baseline) and four ECTS models: LSTMms, EARLIEST, TCN-Transformer, and CALIMERA. Against static-threshold SPRT, FIRMBOUND consistently achieves lower AAPR, reaching or advancing the Pareto front on the SAT curve; on the DOL dataset it discovers a new Pareto-optimal point that static thresholds cannot reach. Against the four learned ECTS baselines (LSTMms, EARLIEST, TCN-Transformer, CALIMERA), FIRMBOUND significantly outperforms all of them on both AAPR and SAT metrics across real-world datasets (SiW, HMDB51, UCF101), with less sensitivity to hyperparameter choices. The paper notes that most existing ECTS methods lack theoretical guarantees and are sensitive to hyperparameters and datasets, whereas FIRMBOUND's statistical consistency (Thm. 3.2) ensures convergence to the Bayes-optimal solution as dataset size grows. Ablation tests with random stopping times and artificially tapered thresholds confirm that neither variant surpasses FIRMBOUND in AAPR or SAT performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Wald's Sequential Probability Ratio Test (SPRT) from its classical infinite-horizon setting to finite horizons, where the optimal stopping boundary must be computed via backward induction—a recursive dynamic programming problem with no closed-form solution. The key methodological departure is transforming this intractable backward induction into two tractable estimation subproblems: (i) estimating the continuation risk (conditional expectation) via convex function learning or Gaussian process regression, and (ii) estimating the sufficient statistic (LLRs) via the authors' prior SPRT-TANDEM density ratio estimation framework. Unlike existing ECTS methods (LSTMms, EARLIEST, TCN-Transformer, CALIMERA) that lack theoretical optimality guarantees, FIRMBOUND inherits Bayes optimality from SPRT while remaining computationally deployable, and unlike naive Monte Carlo approaches to backward induction, it provides instantaneous evaluation with statistical consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FIRMBOUND is the first framework to make finite-horizon SPRT practically deployable with statistical consistency guarantees, bridging a gap between optimal stopping theory and real-time ECTS applications (medical diagnosis, autonomous driving, biometric security). The GP regression alternative (30× faster training than CFL) makes the approach accessible, though it sacrifices the consistency guarantee. Open questions include: whether the concavity assumption on the continuation risk holds universally across all data distributions, how the method scales to very large class numbers (K > 100) where the sufficient statistic dimensionality grows, and whether the variance-reduction benefit persists under distribution shift. The finding that FIRMBOUND can advance the Pareto front (not merely match static-threshold SPRT) on non-i.i.d. data suggests that the tapering boundary captures temporal structure that static thresholds fundamentally cannot.
