## Problem

Embodiment co-design—simultaneously optimizing a robot's morphology and control policy—suffers from severe inefficiency due to the combinatorial morphology search space and the intricate coupling between form and function. The paper identifies two specific bottlenecks: ineffective morphology representation that prevents knowledge sharing across similar structures, and unbalanced reward signals between the zero-reward design stage and the rich-reward control stage that hinder coordinated optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251487/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a simple initial morphology (a small undirected graph of 2–4 limbs with joints) and a MuJoCo physics environment as input, then autoregressively generates a final morphology (topology via add/delete/nochange actions, followed by continuous attributes like limb length, size, rotation range, and motor torque) together with a joint-level control policy (per-joint torques). The entire model is under 2M parameters and trains on a single GPU in roughly 30 hours, with batch processing of multiple morphologies via attention masking to maximize throughput.

## Method

BodyGen introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, the Morphology Self-Attention (MoSAT) architecture replaces GNN-based multi-hop message passing with scaled dot-product self-attention over limb tokens, enabling direct point-to-point communication between any two joints for both autoregressive topology/attribute design and centralized control. Second, Topology Position Encoding (TopoPE) assigns each limb a unique embedding via a hash map over its root-to-node path (encoded as a sequence of child indices), which remains stable under topology mutations and aligns similar substructures across morphologies for knowledge sharing. Third, an Enhanced Temporal Credit Assignment mechanism modifies GAE by decoupling the design and control MDPs: the design stage receives advantage as cumulative return minus value (since design actions influence all future timesteps), while the control stage uses standard discounted GAE, thereby balancing the otherwise vanishing reward signal during the zero-reward design phase. Training uses PPO with separate value networks per stage, switching from a GPT-style autoregressive decoder during design to a BERT-style parallel decoder during control.

## Results

BodyGen achieves the highest episode reward in all ten co-design environments, with an average 60.03% performance improvement over the strongest baseline (Transform2Act). Representative scores include 10,382 on CRAWLER, 11,612 on CHEETAH, and 12,962 on WALKER-MEDIUM (mean ± SE over 4 seeds). The model is lightweight at 1.43M parameters (vs. 19.64M for Transform2Act) and trains on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU within ~30 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BodyGen outperforms all four baselines across all ten environments. Against the strongest RL baseline Transform2Act (Yuan et al., 2021), BodyGen achieves roughly 2× higher rewards on CRAWLER (10,382 vs. 4,186) and WALKER-HARD (11,982 vs. 4,645), and ~38% higher on CHEETAH (11,612 vs. 8,406). Against the evolutionary baseline NGE (Wang et al., 2018b), BodyGen leads by 5–8× in most environments (e.g., 10,382 vs. 1,545 on CRAWLER). Against UMC-Message* (Wang et al., 2018a; Huang et al., 2020), BodyGen wins on every task; notably UMC-Message* fails to converge on the hardest tasks, scoring only 425 on GLIDER-HARD and 2,783 on WALKER-HARD, while BodyGen reaches 10,798 and 11,982 respectively. The paper attributes this gap to UMC-Message*'s insufficient knowledge-alignment mechanism for complex, changing morphologies.

## Relation to Prior Work

BodyGen builds directly on the RL-based joint morphology-control optimization framework of Transform2Act (Yuan et al., 2021), but replaces its GNN-based message passing with a transformer self-attention architecture (MoSAT) for centralized, zero-decay inter-limb communication. It departs from the broader evolutionary-strategy lineage (NGE, UNIMAL) by formulating co-design as a bi-level optimization solved end-to-end via PPO, and from prior universal morphology control methods (NerveNet, UMC-Message) by introducing a topology-aware position encoding (TopoPE) that handles dynamically growing morphologies without a fixed full model. The modified GAE for temporal credit assignment extends Schulman et al.'s (2015b) formulation to address the inherent reward asymmetry between the zero-reward design stage and the reward-rich control stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60% average improvement over the strongest baseline, achieved with only 1.43M parameters (vs. 19.64M for Transform2Act) on a single GPU in ~30 hours, makes embodiment co-design substantially more accessible and scalable. The finding that a simple hash-map-based topology encoding outperforms traversal-based alternatives, and that vanilla PPO's uniform credit assignment is a critical bottleneck for bi-level co-design, provides actionable insights for the field. Key open questions remain: the method is entirely simulation-based with no sim-to-real transfer demonstrated, and the reward-driven RL paradigm does not capture the rich perception and adaptive execution of biological systems, limiting its applicability to open-ended embodied intelligence.
