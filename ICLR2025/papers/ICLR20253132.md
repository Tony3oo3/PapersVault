## Problem

Existing multi-modal 3D pre-training methods rely on point clouds as the 3D representation, which consist of discrete points that fail to capture fine-grained geometric details and surface textures. This creates a noticeable representational gap between the sparse 3D points and the dense 2D pixels of images, hindering effective cross-modal alignment and limiting zero-shot and open-world 3D understanding performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253132/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D Gaussian Splatting representations (each Gaussian parameterized by 3D position, SH color coefficients, opacity, scaling vector, and rotation quaternion), text prompts, and RGB images. The 3DGS is initialized by uniformly sampling 1024 points from mesh surfaces and optimized against rendered multi-view images (72 views for ABO, variable for Objaverse). The output is a unified 512-dimensional embedding in the CLIP latent space that supports zero-shot classification, text-to-3D retrieval, and scene recognition without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

UniGS replaces point clouds with 3DGS as the 3D representation and introduces a Gaussian-Aware Guidance (GAG) module built as a parallel dual-branch ViT encoder. The fundamental branch (frozen, initialized from Uni3D's pre-trained point cloud encoder) encodes only position and color, while the advanced branch (trained from scratch) encodes position, opacity, scale, and rotation. Cross-attention layers transfer spatial priors from the fundamental branch to guide the advanced branch's feature learning, avoiding knowledge conflicts while unlocking 3DGS-specific attributes. Training uses contrastive alignment losses (Language-3DGS and Image-3DGS) against frozen CLIP text and image encoders, with a group divider normalizing variable Gaussian counts to a fixed token length before CNN projection and self-attention.

## Results

On zero-shot classification, UniGS achieves 38.57% Top-1 on Objaverse-LVIS, 46.97% on ABO, and 7.65% on MVImgNet, outperforming the strongest same-backbone baseline Uni3D* (trained from scratch with 3DGS) by +8.10%, +9.18%, and +2.73% respectively. On text-driven retrieval, UniGS reaches 21.00% Top-1 on Objaverse and 18.65% on MVImgNet, surpassing Uni3D* by +5.20% and +1.63%. On SUN RGBD scene recognition, UniGS achieves 69.64% average Top-1, improving over Uni3D (3DGS) by +15.13% and over CLIP² (3DGS) by +41.14%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniGS consistently outperforms all baselines with the same ViT-B-16 backbone across all three main datasets. Against Uni3D* (trained from scratch with 3DGS), UniGS gains +8.10% Top-1 on Objaverse-LVIS (38.57 vs 30.47), +9.18% on ABO (46.97 vs 37.79), and +2.73% on MVImgNet (7.65 vs 4.92). Against CLIP² with 3DGS, the margins are substantially larger: +28.37% on Objaverse-LVIS and +27.91% on ABO. Against point-cloud-based methods TAMM (22.70%) and ReCon (23.40%) on Objaverse-LVIS, UniGS leads by +15.87% and +15.17% respectively. On ShapeNetRender, UniGS (93.94%) surpasses NeRF-based methods nerf2clip (84.00%) and nf2vec (87.30%) by +9.94% and +6.64%. Notably, Uni3D with its larger EVA02-E-14-plus backbone (1024-dim) still outperforms UniGS on ABO (68.94% vs 46.97%), indicating that model scale remains a significant factor, though UniGS with the same EVA02-S backbone at 10000 points (52.44%) surpasses Uni3D-S (50.34%) in the large-scale setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniGS extends the text-image-3D contrastive pre-training paradigm established by CLIP2 and Uni3D by replacing point clouds with 3D Gaussian Splatting as the 3D representation, motivated by the information gap between discrete points and dense 2D pixels. It builds directly on CLIP's frozen vision-language encoders for the shared latent space and on Uni3D's ViT-based 3D encoder as the fundamental branch of its dual-encoder design. The key architectural departure is the Gaussian-Aware Guidance module, which splits 3DGS attributes into a frozen pre-trained branch (position + color) and a trainable branch (position + opacity, scale, rotation) connected via cross-attention, rather than treating all 3DGS attributes uniformly as prior point-cloud-based methods do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to integrate 3DGS into multi-modal pre-training, demonstrating that the richer per-Gaussian attributes (color, opacity, scale, rotation) yield meaningfully better cross-modal alignment than point clouds alone, with +9.36% on zero-shot classification and +4.3% on text-driven retrieval over Uni3D. However, the approach requires at least one image with known camera pose for 3DGS optimization, limiting applicability to pure point-cloud datasets, and the nearly 2× FLOPs increase (98.17G vs. 47.85G) raises practical deployment concerns. Open questions include whether camera-pose-free 3DGS initialization (e.g., Image-to-3DGS) can close this gap, whether the framework extends to outdoor/LLM-integrated 3D understanding, and whether the compute overhead can be reduced through the 3DGS compression techniques the authors note are emerging.
