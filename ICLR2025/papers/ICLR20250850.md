## Problem

Existing KV-cache compression algorithms for LLM inference sparsify tokens along the sequence dimension but treat all attention layers identically, assigning each layer the same cache budget. This is suboptimal because layers differ in how much they transform the input embedding, meaning some layers can tolerate more aggressive eviction without hurting accuracy. The paper asks whether layer-wise budget reallocation can further reduce KV-cache on top of sequence-wise compression.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt, a chosen sequence-wise eviction policy (e.g., H2O, Sliding Window, or StreamingLLM), and a single initial per-layer cache budget b_init (e.g., 20% of prompt length). During prefilling, it computes per-layer cosine similarities and clusters layers into three groups, then outputs a non-uniform per-layer budget vector. During decoding, each layer independently applies the sequence-wise eviction policy with its own allocated budget, producing generated tokens while maintaining accuracy comparable to full cache.

## Method

SqueezeAttention measures layer importance by computing the cosine similarity between hidden states before and after each self-attention block for every prompt token during prefilling; higher similarity indicates the layer contributes less information. These per-layer average similarities are clustered into three groups via KMeans: Group 1 (special first/last layers, always preserved), Group 2 (important middle layers), and Group 3 (least important layers, ~50–70% of total). The initial uniform budget b_init is then reallocated: Group 3 layers receive a reduced budget of b_init × p (typically p = 0.3–0.4), while the freed budget is redistributed equally among Groups 1 and 2. During decoding, each layer applies the chosen sequence-wise eviction policy (H2O, Sliding Window, or StreamingLLM) with its own layer-specific budget, making the approach orthogonal and composable with any existing sequence-wise compressor. The entire overhead (cosine similarity + KMeans) is a one-time cost during prefilling, adding only ~6.3% to prefill time.

## Results

SqueezeAttention achieves 30%–70% KV-cache memory reduction and up to 2.2× throughput improvement over Full Cache across 7 LLMs (7B–70B) and 5 benchmarks. In the key budget-vs-accuracy comparison (Table 2), SqueezeAttention matches or exceeds baseline accuracy while using only 20%–30% of the KV-cache budget, compared to 30%–60% for the best sequence-only baselines. Per-token decoding memory is reduced by 70%–80% versus Full Cache and 25%–66% versus baselines. The algorithm's one-time prefilling overhead is only 6.3% (0.023 s on A100 for 8k-token prompts).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SqueezeAttention is combined with three sequence-wise baselines—H2O, Sliding Window Attention, and StreamingLLM—and consistently outperforms each in the accuracy-per-budget tradeoff. On Mistral-7B/SAMSUM, SqueezeAttention + Sliding Window reaches Rouge-L 41.05 at 20% budget versus 40.0 at 30% for Sliding Window alone (a 33% budget saving at higher accuracy). On GPT-NeoX-20B/XSUM, SqueezeAttention + H2O matches Full Cache accuracy (0.09) at just 20% budget, while H2O alone needs 60% budget for 0.08. On Llama2-70B/XSUM, SqueezeAttention + StreamingLLM achieves 0.17 at 30% budget versus 0.19 at 40% for StreamingLLM alone. In throughput experiments, SqueezeAttention enables batch sizes up to 224 (Mistral-7B) and 64 (Llama2-70B) where Full Cache runs out of memory, delivering up to 2.2× and 1.4× throughput gains respectively. Against the best sequence-only baselines at matched accuracy, SqueezeAttention saves 25%–66% per-token decoding memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-studied KV-cache compression task from a purely sequence-wise (1D) perspective to a 2D formulation that jointly optimizes over both the token sequence and the attention layer dimensions. It builds conceptually on early-exiting LLM work (Del Corro et al.) and FastGen (Ge et al.), which established that layers at different positions play distinct roles, but departs from FastGen by allocating different *budgets* (not just different eviction strategies) to layers based on a measured importance metric (cosine similarity of pre/post-attention embeddings). The method is explicitly orthogonal to and composable with existing sequence-wise compressors (H2O, Sliding Window, StreamingLLM), positioning itself as a complementary layer on top of rather than a replacement for prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for LLM deployment: 30–70% memory reduction and up to 2.2× throughput gains across 7 models (7B–70B) with negligible overhead (6.3% prefilling time), making it a drop-in optimization for production inference systems. A key limitation is the assumption that a suitable sequence-wise eviction policy exists for the given model-task pair; if no such policy preserves accuracy, the layer-wise reallocation cannot compensate. An open question highlighted in the appendix is whether layer importance is an intrinsic model property or task-dependent, as the grouping shifts somewhat across datasets, suggesting the adaptive (on-the-fly) clustering approach is necessary rather than a fixed layer schedule.
