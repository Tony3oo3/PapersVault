## Problem

Existing PLM-based generative recommendation methods suffer from a mismatch between general linguistic knowledge and the specific requirements of recommendation tasks, and they largely ignore the complementary knowledge between multimodal item content (text and image) that captures multi-faceted user preferences. The paper asks whether items from different domains and modalities can be translated into a single unified language that serves as a bridge for transferring recommendation knowledge across both dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252681/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes user interaction histories where each item is represented by its text (title + description, encoded by a frozen LLaMA) and image (encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14), both projected through RQ-VAE translators into 4-level discrete codewords (256 vectors per level, dim 32). Output is a sequence of quantitative-language tokens (lowercase-prefixed for text, uppercase-prefixed for image) generated autoregressively via beam search (size 20) by a 4-layer T5 encoder-decoder (~13M params), followed by a re-ranking step that fuses scores from the text and image sub-tasks into a final ranked list.

## Method

MQL4GRec introduces per-modality quantitative translators (frozen encoder + RQ-VAE with 4 residual codebooks of 256 vectors) that map item content into a shared discrete vocabulary of 2LK = 2048 tokens, with a distance-based collision-resolution scheme to guarantee unique item identifiers. On top of this vocabulary, the paper designs three families of sequence-to-sequence generation tasks—symmetric Next Item Generation, cross-modal Asymmetric Item Generation, and explicit Quantitative Language Alignment—each prompted by special tokens, which collectively imbue the quantitative language with semantic and collaborative knowledge. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: pre-training on six source-domain Amazon categories (transferring cross-domain knowledge) followed by fine-tuning on the target domain with all QLG tasks (transferring cross-modal knowledge), optimized by negative log-likelihood. The key architectural insight is that the RQ-VAE codebooks act as a modality-agnostic, domain-agnostic vocabulary, making the quantitative language a true bridge that decouples item representation from any single modality or domain.

## Results

MQL4GRec achieves the best performance on most metrics across all three Amazon Product Reviews datasets (Instruments, Arts, Games). The headline improvements over the strongest baseline are 11.58% on NDCG@10 for Instruments, 12.56% on NDCG@10 for Arts, and 7.66% on NDCG@10 for Games. On HR@1, gains are particularly large for Arts (+26.32% over TIGER) and moderate for Instruments (+10.48%). The method also leads on HR@10 for Instruments (0.1375 vs. 0.1361 for MISSRec) and Games (0.1033 vs. 0.1048 for MISSRec, a narrow margin).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MQL4GRec outperforms all ten baselines on NDCG@10 across all three datasets, with the largest margins over TIGER (the closest generative baseline): +11.58% on Instruments, +12.56% on Arts, and +7.66% on Games. Against the strongest non-generative method MISSRec, MQL4GRec wins on NDCG@10 by 20.5% (Instruments), 16.6% (Arts), and 9.4% (Games), but narrowly trails MISSRec on HR@10 for Games (0.1033 vs. 0.1048). Among PLM-based methods, VIP5 underperforms TIGER on all three datasets, confirming the paper's observation that raw image features are poorly aligned with PLM linguistic knowledge. TIGER itself is the strongest single baseline on HR@1 for Instruments and Arts but lacks auxiliary content information, which MQL4GRec compensates for via its multimodal quantitative language. On the Games dataset, FDSA (a non-generative self-attention model) is competitive on NDCG@5 (0.0389 vs. 0.0421 for MQL4GRec), and the overall margin is smallest here, suggesting domain-specific challenges for cross-domain transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TIGER's (Rajput et al., 2023) use of RQ-VAE for constructing item identifiers in generative recommendation to a multimodal, cross-domain setting. Where TIGER quantizes a single item embedding into semantic IDs, MQL4GRec trains separate RQ-VAE-based 'quantitative translators' for text (encoded by LLaMA) and image (encoded by CLIP/ViT), unifying their codebooks into a shared vocabulary that serves as a bridge across domains and modalities. The generative backbone follows the T5 encoder-decoder paradigm established by P5 and TIGER, but the paper's key departure is designing a suite of quantitative language generation tasks (next-item, asymmetric cross-modal, and alignment) that explicitly transfer recommendation knowledge through pre-training on source domains and fine-tuning on the target domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified quantitative language framing offers a principled way to break down barriers between domains and modalities without relying on traditional item IDs, which is a practical need for large-scale recommendation systems facing cold-start and transferability issues. However, the paper reveals an important tension: multimodal pre-training knowledge can conflict with source-domain knowledge (evidenced by the Games dataset degrading with more pre-training data or epochs), and zero-shot transfer remains very weak (HR@10 ≈ 0.003), suggesting the approach still requires substantial target-domain fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the quantitative language scales to more modalities or larger item catalogs, how to handle missing item content, and whether the inference cost of beam search over a 2048-token vocabulary is acceptable in production settings.
