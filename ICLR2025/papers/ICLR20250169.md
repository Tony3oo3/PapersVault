## Problem

Existing frequency-domain noise priors for video diffusion models, particularly FreeInit, suffer from a variance decay issue: their refined noise deviates from the standard Gaussian distribution, causing generated videos to lose motion dynamics and imaging details. This paper provides a rigorous theoretical analysis identifying this variance decay and proposes a corrected filtering formulation that preserves the Gaussian prior while still benefiting from frequency-domain manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250169/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely in the latent space of pre-trained text-to-video diffusion models (VideoCrafter at 320×320, ModelScope at 256×256, AnimateDiff at 512×512, all 16 frames). It takes a text prompt and initial standard Gaussian noise as input, then iteratively refines the noise over 2 partial-sampling iterations (each stopping at an intermediate timestep t=321 rather than running to T) before performing a final full sampling to produce the generated video. The refined noise prior is guaranteed to closely approximate N(0, I), with measured covariance errors on the order of 10⁻²⁶.

## Method

FreqPrior introduces a novel frequency filtering technique designed specifically for Gaussian random variables rather than deterministic signals. The key modification replaces the conventional high-pass filter (1−M) with (1−M²)^0.5, ensuring the sum of squared filter coefficients equals one and thus preserving the standard Gaussian distribution of the noise. The noise refinement stage prepares two correlated noise sets by mixing the diffused latent with fresh Gaussian noise at a tunable ratio cos θ, applies 3D Fourier filtering to retain low-frequency signals (for semantic fidelity) while enriching high-frequency signals (for detail), and performs a symmetric post-processing step that retains both real and imaginary components to avoid variance loss. A partial sampling strategy perturbs the latent at an intermediate timestep (~400) rather than diffusing a clean latent all the way to T, reducing inference time by ~23% without quality loss. Theoretically, the paper proves the covariance error of FreqPrior is bounded by (cos²θ)/(1+cos²θ) times that of FreeInit, guaranteeing at least 50% reduction, and numerical experiments confirm the error is negligible.

## Results

On VBench, FreqPrior achieves the highest total scores across all three base models: 68.77 on VideoCrafter, 73.04 on ModelScope, and 78.11 on AnimateDiff. It also attains the best quality and semantic scores in every setting (e.g., 70.63 quality / 61.33 semantic on VideoCrafter; 80.05 / 70.37 on AnimateDiff). Inference time is reduced by approximately 23% compared to FreeInit (e.g., 63.67 s vs. 83.18 s on VideoCrafter; 44.88 s vs. 57.73 s on ModelScope), thanks to the partial sampling strategy that perturbs the latent at an intermediate timestep rather than running full sampling iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreqPrior outperforms FreeInit on every metric across all three base models. On VideoCrafter, it improves the total score from 68.29 to 68.77 and the semantic score from 58.97 to 61.33, while cutting inference time from 83.18 s to 63.67 s. On ModelScope, the total score rises from 72.34 to 73.04 and semantic from 67.24 to 69.06, with inference time dropping from 57.73 s to 44.88 s. On AnimateDiff, FreeInit actually degrades the semantic score (68.85 vs. 69.03 for plain Gaussian), whereas FreqPrior lifts it to 70.37 and the total to 78.11. The PYoCo mixed and progressive noise priors are not directly comparable because they cause generation crashes on pretrained models without fine-tuning; their covariance errors (154–310) are orders of magnitude larger than FreqPrior's (≈10⁻²⁶), confirming a severe distributional mismatch. Tripling the denoising steps for plain Gaussian noise yields only marginal gains (e.g., +0.23 total on ModelScope), far below FreqPrior's improvements, indicating that the benefit comes from the frequency-domain noise refinement rather than additional denoising compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends FreeInit (Wu et al., 2024), the first work to apply Fourier-domain frequency filtering to the noise prior of video diffusion models, by identifying a fundamental variance decay flaw in its filtering formula. It also positions itself against PYoCo (Ge et al., 2023), whose mixed and progressive noise priors introduce temporal correlations that deviate from the standard Gaussian distribution and cause crashes on pretrained models. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that noise (random variables) requires a different filtering algebra than deterministic signals: replacing the conventional high-pass filter (1−M) with (1−M²)^0.5 so that the sum of squared filter gains equals one, thereby preserving the Gaussian distribution of the refined noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that maintaining the standard Gaussian distribution of the initial noise is a critical (and previously underappreciated) requirement for video diffusion model quality, providing a clean theoretical framework (covariance error analysis) to diagnose and fix distributional deviations in noise priors. The partial sampling strategy—perturbing the latent at an intermediate timestep rather than fully diffusing to T—offers a practical ~23% inference time reduction that could be adopted broadly. Limitations include modest absolute performance gains on VBench (typically <1 point total score), dependence on two additional sampling iterations, and evaluation on only three base models; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to image diffusion, longer video generation, or different schedulers beyond DDIM.
