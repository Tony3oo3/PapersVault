## Problem

LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation suffers from a persistent reliability gap with human judgment, partly due to the absence of a guiding oracle in the evaluation process. Pre-existing references (human-annotated or LLM direct responses) introduce noise in open-ended tasks because of the one-to-many problem: a single reference can unfairly penalize valid but dissimilar responses. The paper identifies that reference effectiveness is strongly correlated with its relevance to the specific response being evaluated, motivating a new reference generation strategy.

## Input / Output

Given a task instruction x, a model-generated response y, and an evaluation rubric a, the method first produces a response-adapted reference r* by revising y, then outputs an evaluation score or pairwise preference judgment. In pairwise comparison mode, a second response y2 is provided; one of the two responses is randomly selected as the primary text to revise while the other serves as revision guidance, ensuring positional fairness. The response-adapted reference is also directly usable as input to classic reference-based metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore, BARTScore, MOVERScore), enabling a 'weak LLM-as-a-Reviser + metrics' paradigm that avoids training a dedicated judge model.

## Method

REVISEVAL is a two-phase evaluation paradigm. In the generation phase, an LLM reviser R revises the response y conditioned on the instruction x and rubric a, producing a response-adapted reference r* = R(y | x, a); for pairwise comparison, one response is randomly chosen for revision with the other as cautious guidance. In the evaluation phase, the reference r* is fed to either an LLM-as-a-Judge F_E for scoring/pairwise comparison or to classic metrics F_M (n-gram or model-based) to compute a similarity score. The key insight is that revising the response itself guarantees the reference is both high-quality and maximally relevant to the evaluated content, directly addressing the one-to-many mismatch that plagues static references. For open-source LLMs, the reviser and judge are obtained via supervised fine-tuning on GPT-4-distilled evaluation data (from MetricInstruct and HH-RLHF), while for proprietary models the paradigm operates purely at inference time. The paper further shows that combining a weak LLM-as-a-Reviser with majority voting over multiple classic metrics outperforms the same weak LLM-as-a-Judge, suggesting revision is a more effective use of limited model capacity than discrimination.

## Results

On instruction-following preference benchmarks (MT-Bench, AlpacaFarm, LLMBar), REVISEVAL with GPT-4-as-a-Reviser achieves 78.1% average accuracy, outperforming GPT-4 reference-free evaluation (74.9%) and reference-based evaluation (76.4%). On NLG tasks (SummEval, WMT-22, WebNLG, OpenMEVA), REVISEVAL with GPT-4 achieves 34.34 average Kendall τ, surpassing reference-free (32.06) and human-reference-based (32.24) evaluation. For open-source LLMs, REVISEVAL with GPT-4-as-a-Reviser boosts Llama 3.1-8B-as-a-Judge from 59.9% to 70.4% average accuracy on instruction-following tasks, and from 30.79 to 32.63 Kendall τ on NLG tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REVISEVAL consistently outperforms all specialized LLM-as-a-Judge baselines (JudgeLM-7B, PandaLM-7B, Auto-J-13B, Prometheus-7B, Prometheus-2-7B) by at least 6.3% average accuracy on instruction-following tasks, despite those baselines being trained on 10K–300K samples versus REVISEVAL's 9.8K. Against reference-free LLM-as-a-Judge, REVISEVAL gains 3–6% on open-source models and 3.2% on GPT-4. Compared to reference-based evaluation using direct LLM responses (Zheng et al., 2023's setup), REVISEVAL performs better on average when both use the same base LLM for reference generation, and using GPT-4 as reviser boosts Llama 3.1-8B-as-a-Judge by over 4.5% over Llama-as-a-Reviser. On NLG tasks, REVISEVAL surpasses human-annotated reference-based evaluation by ~0.02 Kendall τ on average with GPT-4, and by over 0.03 with open-source models. Classic metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, BERTScore, BARTScore) combined with REVISEVAL's response-adapted references outperform their own performance with traditional references by 3–10% accuracy, and majority voting across multiple metrics with a weak LLM-as-a-Reviser beats weak LLM-as-a-Judge by over 1.5% on average. Against DIV-REF (Tang et al., 2024), REVISEVAL achieves superior correlation in most cases, particularly in story generation where it reaches 35.26 Spearman ρ versus DIV-REF's 27.38 with GPT-4-Turbo.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reference-guided evaluation framework introduced by Zheng et al. (2023), who used the LLM's own direct response to the instruction as a reference for LLM-as-a-Judge. REVISEVAL departs from this by instead leveraging the LLM's revision capabilities to adaptively revise the response being evaluated, producing a 'response-adapted reference' that is both high-quality and closely relevant to the original response. Conceptually, it repurposes the LLM from a pure discriminator (judge) into a generative reviser, drawing on the broader text-revision literature (RARR, Re3, DPO revision) and connecting it to the evaluation setting. It also reactivates classic reference-based metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore, BARTScore) that had been largely abandoned for open-ended tasks in favor of reference-free LLM-as-a-Judge approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central insight—that reference *relevance* to the specific response being evaluated, not just reference quality, is the key determinant of evaluation effectiveness—challenges the field's recent pivot toward reference-free evaluation for open-ended tasks. Practically, it offers a viable path for weaker LLMs: using them as revisers combined with classic metrics outperforms using them as judges, reducing the need for expensive evaluation-specific training. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-modal evaluation, whether better revision strategies can close the remaining gap on adversarial benchmarks like LLMBar (where a single golden answer exists), and whether the fine-grained multi-reference extension scales to more complex evaluation rubrics.
