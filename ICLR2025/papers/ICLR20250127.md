## Problem

State-of-the-art MLLMs suffer severe hallucination when interpreting geometric figures, failing to correctly identify basic elements (points, lines, angles) and their spatial relationships, which is a primary cause of their poor performance on geometric problem solving. Existing geometric datasets are limited in scale (a few thousand questions), lack detailed image-text descriptions, and offer little diversity in reasoning paths, making it infeasible to simply augment MLLMs with more geometric data using expensive vision models like GPT-4V for annotation.

## Input / Output

The model receives a geometric figure image (expanded to a square with white background during training) and a natural-language question, and produces a step-by-step geometric reasoning path followed by a final numerical or multiple-choice answer. During inference, the ViT encoder extracts visual tokens that are projected into the LLM's embedding space and concatenated with text embeddings for next-token generation. The model is evaluated on Top-1 accuracy, unlike prior work that often reports Top-10 accuracy.

## Method

The key insight is that geometric figures possess 'logical structure compactness'—their essential information (elements and spatial relationships) can be fully captured in compact textual representations such as logic forms and QA pairs, enabling text-only ChatGPT (gpt-3.5-turbo) to generate high-quality multimodal annotations without any vision model. The resulting Geo170K dataset (~170K samples) comprises two parts: (1) cross-modal alignment data with image captions and factual QA pairs for basic geometric elements, generated via inverse information recovery from logic forms; and (2) instruction data augmented with four strategies—Value Scaling, Equation Solving, Re-Formulating Condition as Unknown, and Sentence Paraphrase—each with automated correctness verification. Training follows LLaVA's two-phase paradigm (geometric visual-language alignment with fixed LLM, then geometric instruction tuning) using a ViT encoder and LLaMA-2 (7B/13B) backbone, with the alignment phase specifically designed to reduce hallucination on geometric figures.

## Results

On the MathVista GPS testmini benchmark, G-LLaVA-7B achieves 53.4% top-1 accuracy, surpassing GPT-4V (50.5%) by 2.9 points with only 7B parameters, while G-LLaVA-13B reaches 56.7%, exceeding Gemini Ultra (56.3%) and human performance (48.4%). On GeoQA, G-LLaVA-7B attains 64.2% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the previous SCA-GPS top-10 accuracy of 64.1% and UniMath's top-1 of 50.0%. On MathVerse testmini-MC, G-LLaVA-7B achieves 34.7% overall accuracy, a 23.7-point improvement over its base model LLaVA-7B (11.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-LLaVA-7B (53.4%) outperforms GPT-4V (50.5%) by 2.9 points on MathVista GPS, and G-LLaVA-13B (56.7%) edges out Gemini Ultra (56.3%) by 0.4 points, making it the top-performing model in the comparison. Against open-source MLLMs, G-LLaVA-7B surpasses LLaVA-13B (29.3%) by 24.1 points, MiniGPT4 (26.0%) by 27.4 points, and InstructBLIP (20.7%) by 32.7 points. On GeoQA, G-LLaVA-7B's top-1 accuracy of 64.2% exceeds the top-10 accuracy of SCA-GPS (64.1%) and DPE-GPS (62.7%), and substantially outperforms UniMath (50.0%) and Geoformer (46.8%). On MathVerse testmini-MC, G-LLaVA-7B (34.7%) leads all open-source MLLMs by a wide margin over ShareGPT4V (13.1%), SPHINX-Plus (12.2%), and LLaVA-7B (11.0%). The ablation on alignment (Table 10) shows that fixing the LLM during the cross-modal alignment phase yields the best result (64.2% vs. 60.1% with a tunable LLM), confirming that the alignment stage reduces hallucinations without degrading reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA architecture and its two-phase training paradigm (cross-modal alignment followed by instruction tuning), extending it with geometry-specific data. It departs from concurrent multimodal math reasoning work (MathLLaVA, MultiMath, MathPuMA) that relies on expensive multimodal models like GPT-4V or GPT-4O for data annotation, instead exploiting the 'logical structure compactness' of geometric figures to generate high-quality multimodal data using only text-based ChatGPT. The approach is positioned as a text-centric complement to image-centric data augmentation strategies, offering greater scalability and lower cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 7B model can surpass GPT4-V on geometric problem solving, suggesting that targeted domain-specific data generation can be more effective than raw model scale for specialized tasks. The Geo170K dataset (28× larger than prior open-source geometric datasets) and the text-only data generation pipeline provide a practical, cost-effective recipe for improving MLLMs on structured visual domains. Open questions include whether the logical structure compactness principle generalizes to other structured visual domains (e.g., circuit diagrams, chemical structures), and whether the gains hold when the model is evaluated on truly novel geometric configurations beyond the training distribution.
