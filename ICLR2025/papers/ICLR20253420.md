## Problem

Existing reward finetuning methods for pretrained diffusion models face a fundamental trade-off: gradient-informed approaches (ReFL, DRaFT) converge quickly but are mode-seeking and collapse sample diversity, while diversity-preserving methods (DDPO, DAG-DB) maintain variety but converge slowly and ignore available reward gradient information. No prior method simultaneously achieves fast convergence, diversity preservation, and prior preservation when finetuning large-scale text-to-image diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained Stable Diffusion v1.5 model (operating in VAE latent space), a text prompt, and a differentiable reward function (Aesthetic Score, HPSv2, or ImageReward) that scores decoded images. It produces a LoRA-adapted (rank 8) diffusion model whose sampling distribution is proportional to R(x_T)^β · P_F^#(x_T), i.e., the reward-weighted pretrained prior. Reward gradients are approximated via 3 Monte Carlo samples with small Gaussian noise (σ=2×10⁻³) through the frozen VAE decoder, and the MDP is constructed from a 50-step DDPM sampler with 10% transition subsampling per trajectory.

## Method

The core contribution is ∇-DB, obtained by taking spatial derivatives of the GFlowNet Detailed Balance condition, yielding a loss on score functions (analogous to Fisher divergence) rather than log-probabilities. A residual variant subtracts the pretrained model's ∇-DB condition from the finetuned model's, so only the deviation from the prior is learned, guaranteeing prior preservation at convergence. To accelerate credit assignment over long diffusion sequences, a forward-looking (FL) trick parameterizes the residual flow score as βγ_t ∇log R(x̂_θ(x_t)) + g_φ(x_t), where x̂_θ is the one-step clean-data prediction and g_φ is a learnable U-Net initialized near zero at the terminal step. The full training objective combines forward and reverse residual ∇-DB losses over sampled transitions plus a terminal constraint, optimized jointly on the LoRA diffusion parameters and the residual flow network with output regularization. Unlike ReFL/DRaFT which perform deterministic reward maximization, this probabilistic framework samples from the tilted distribution R^β·P^#, inherently preserving diversity.

## Results

On Aesthetic Score (200 update steps), ∇-GFlowNet (wB=1) achieves a reward of 7.90±0.09 with DreamSim diversity of 29.67±0.51 (×10⁻²) and FID of 317±15, closely matching the base model's diversity (35.91) and FID (216) while substantially improving reward over the base (5.83). On HPSv2, it reaches reward 3.53±0.04 (×10⁻¹) with diversity 24.39 and FID 1000; on ImageReward, it achieves reward 2.23±0.34 (×10⁻¹) with the best diversity (39.85) and lowest FID (501) among all finetuned methods. The method achieves comparable convergence speed to gradient-free baselines while gradient-informed baselines (ReFL, DRaFT) suffer 3–5× diversity collapse and 5–6× FID degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDPO [3] (gradient-free RL), ∇-GFlowNet wins decisively on diversity and FID across all three rewards (e.g., Aesthetic: 29.67 vs. 32.96 diversity, 317 vs. 312 FID) while achieving higher reward (7.90 vs. 6.68); DDPO's HPSv2 diversity collapses to 3.49, far below ∇-GFlowNet's 24.39. Against ReFL [71] and DRaFT [6] (gradient-informed direct reward maximization), ∇-GFlowNet trades some peak reward (7.90 vs. 9.53–10.21 on Aesthetic) for dramatically better diversity (29.67 vs. 4.24–8.20) and FID (317 vs. 1665–1854), indicating the gradient-informed baselines suffer severe mode collapse. Against DAG-DB [80] (gradient-free GFlowNet with DB objective), ∇-GFlowNet achieves comparable reward (7.90 vs. 7.73 on Aesthetic) but substantially better diversity (29.67 vs. 15.88) and FID (317 vs. 595), demonstrating that incorporating reward gradients into the GFlowNet framework accelerates convergence without sacrificing the diversity-preserving properties of the probabilistic objective. On ImageReward, ∇-GFlowNet (wB=1) achieves the best diversity (39.85) and lowest FID (501) among all finetuned methods, though at lower reward (2.23) than DAG-DB (4.70) or residual DB (6.47), reflecting a more conservative prior-preserving trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GFlowNet framework for diffusion model alignment (Zhang et al., 2025; DAG-DB) by taking derivatives of the Detailed Balance condition to obtain gradient-informed objectives (∇-DB), making it the first GFlowNet method to exploit first-order reward information. The residual ∇-DB formulation for prior preservation is conceptually parallel to the relative GFlowNet work (Venkatraman et al., 2024) but operates in score-function space rather than log-probability space, enabling U-Net-style parameterization. The method is also closely connected to soft RL (Haarnoja et al., 2017) and soft Q-learning, with the paper providing an explicit equivalence proof, and incorporates the forward-looking trick from Pan et al. (2023) for efficient credit assignment over long diffusion trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and increasingly important problem: aligning large pretrained diffusion models with reward signals without the diversity collapse and prior forgetting that plague existing methods. The residual ∇-DB objective provides a principled, probabilistic alternative to ad-hoc gradient-based finetuning (ReFL, DRaFT) that empirically achieves a better Pareto frontier across reward, diversity, and FID. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-differentiable or black-box rewards, whether the learnable backward policy generalization (Appendix C) is practically useful, and how the method scales to video or 3D generation where trajectories are much longer. The reliance on a differentiable reward function and the need for careful hyperparameter tuning (β, λ, γ_t) remain practical limitations.
