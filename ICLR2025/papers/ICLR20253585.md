## Problem

Transfer learning is bottlenecked by the need to store and search through massive repositories of pretrained models, and existing weight-generation methods (hypernetworks, kernel density estimators, prior diffusion approaches) are restricted to small architectures, unconditional sampling, or single-task settings. The paper asks whether latent diffusion can be recast to generate task-specific neural network weights conditioned on dataset or task descriptions, enabling on-demand synthesis of high-quality parameters without maintaining a model database.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253585/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes either CLIP image features of a target dataset (encoded via a Set Transformer into a fixed-dimension embedding) or a textual task description (encoded via Llama-3-8B-Instruct's last hidden layer), plus chunk indices for LLM layer identification. It produces full neural network weight tensors or LoRA parameter matrices, decoded from a 1024-dimensional latent space via a VAE decoder. For large models, weights are handled layer-by-layer with zero-padded chunking (e.g., 2M or 4M-element chunks), while small models use a single flattened vector; the output is reshaped back into the original architecture's weight layout.

## Method

D2NWG is a three-stage pipeline: (1) a VAE encodes vectorized pretrained weights into a compact latent space, motivated by the hypothesis that weights lie on a low-dimensional manifold (supported by Lottery Ticket sparsity and Whitney's Embedding Theorem); (2) a Set Transformer encodes target datasets into embeddings, aligned to weight latents via a CLIP-style contrastive loss; (3) a UNet-based latent diffusion model (DDPM) is trained to denoise weight latents conditioned on the concatenated dataset embedding, enabling conditional sampling of new weights at inference. For LLMs, the method extends to layer-wise chunked encoding, uses the Marchenko-Pastur distribution to select the top 25% most informative layers by signal-to-noise ratio, and applies a sequential greedy search (Algorithm 1) that iteratively replaces one layer's weights with the best-performing sampled candidate on a validation set. The framework scales from Conv4 classifier heads to 1B-parameter LLaMA models on a single GPU, and supports both in-distribution retrieval (matching individual pretrained model accuracy) and out-of-distribution initialization (accelerating fine-tuning convergence by ~6% in few-shot settings).

## Results

On few-shot learning with a ResNet12 backbone, D2NWG achieves 69.55% (5-way 1-shot) and 83.51% (5-way 5-shot) on mini-ImageNet, and 81.15% and 90.04% on tiered-ImageNet, outperforming the strongest baseline MetaDiff by 4.6–8.8 percentage points. In zero-shot classifier head adaptation with ResNet18, D2NWG_CLIP reaches 60.42% on CIFAR-10 and 82.42% on STL-10, far exceeding the ImageNet-pretrained backbone (10.88% and 6.78%). For LLM enhancement, D2NWG improves LLaMA-3.2-1B-Instruct's MATH-hard score from 2.96 to 6.04 (+3.08) and raises the OpenLM leaderboard average by 0.36 points without any fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D2NWG consistently outperforms all meta-learning baselines on few-shot learning: with ResNet12 it beats MetaDiff (Zhang et al., 2024) by 4.6 pp (1-shot mini-ImageNet: 69.55 vs 64.99) and 8.8 pp (1-shot tiered-ImageNet: 81.15 vs 72.33), and surpasses MetaQDA (Zhang et al., 2021) by 4.4 pp on 1-shot mini-ImageNet. Against the GHN family (Knyazev et al., 2021, 2023) on zero-shot transfer, D2NWG_CLIP achieves 60.42% on CIFAR-10 vs GHN3's 51.8% and GHN2's 48.20%, while also covering datasets (Aircraft, Pets, CIFAR-100) that GHN methods do not report. In fine-tuning convergence (Table 4), D2NWG surpasses SANEKDE30 and SANESUB (Schürholt et al., 2024) at every epoch checkpoint, reaching 96.2% on MNIST at epoch 25 vs 92.0–92.3% for the KDE baselines. On GLUE task-conditioned LoRA generation, D2NWG matches or marginally exceeds LoRA, AdaLoRA, DyLoRA, and FourierFT (Gao et al., 2024), achieving 85.3 average vs 85.2 for LoRA and 84.5–85.0 for the others. In unconditional sampling (Table 11), D2NWG matches pdiff (Wang et al., 2024) on CIFAR-10 (94.46 vs 94.46) and CIFAR-100 (76.18 vs 76.10) while requiring only 100–200 training epochs versus pdiff's 9,000–48,000 epochs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper recasts the latent diffusion framework (Rombach et al., 2022; Ho et al., 2020) for a fundamentally different generative target: neural network weights rather than images. It departs from hypernetwork and graph-hypernetwork approaches (Knyazev et al., 2021, 2023) that predict weights in a single-task, architecture-specific manner, and from meta-learning diffusion methods (Nava et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024) that are restricted to classifier-head generation. The CLIP-style contrastive alignment for dataset-weight matching is borrowed from HyperCLIP (Nava et al., 2023), while the Marchenko-Pastur spectrum analysis for LLM layer selection (Hartford et al., 2024) provides the mechanism to handle billion-parameter models. The key conceptual departure is treating the weight space of diverse pretrained models as a learnable distribution amenable to conditional sampling, rather than predicting weights from architectural graphs or learning task-specific priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

D2NWG offers a principled alternative to storing and retrieving from massive pretrained model repositories, potentially reducing the storage and search overhead that constrains practical transfer learning. The extension to LLMs (1B+ parameters) via chunk-wise encoding and spectrum-based layer selection opens a new avenue for improving model performance without fine-tuning, though the demonstrated gains on the OpenLM leaderboard are modest (~0.36% average). Key open questions include the method's sensitivity to the diversity and size of the pretrained model zoo, the high sampling variance observed in few-shot settings (±8.5%), and whether the approach generalizes beyond the tested architectures. The reliance on large collections of pretrained weight tensors as training data remains a practical bottleneck, and the paper acknowledges memory and convergence challenges in scaling the diffusion process to larger weight spaces.
