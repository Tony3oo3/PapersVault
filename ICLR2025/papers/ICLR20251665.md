## Problem

Motion diffusion models generate diverse, text-controllable motions but operate offline with slow inference and no physical grounding, while RL-based physics controllers produce physically plausible interactions but require per-task reward engineering and do not scale to diverse behaviors. No prior method unifies the flexibility of diffusion-based planning with the physical realism of simulation in a closed-loop, multi-task setting that supports interactive text-driven control.

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'strike the target using a high kick') and a 3D target location for a single body joint (e.g., right foot at the bag center) as input; both can be changed online to switch tasks. The diffusion planner outputs 40-frame kinematic motion plans in HumanML3D relative representation at 3,500 fps (175× realtime), which are converted to global position/velocity format for the RL tracker. The final output is a physics-simulated SMPL-compatible humanoid executing the plan as PD control actions, with the realized motion fed back autoregressively as the prefix for the next plan.

## Method

CLoSD couples two modules in a closed feedback loop: (1) DiP, an 8-layer transformer decoder autoregressive diffusion model (512-dim, 4 heads) that generates 40-frame motion plans in 10 denoising steps, conditioned via cross-attention on DistilBERT text embeddings and an adaptive target condition (joint position + heading angle with per-joint validity flags), and (2) a single-primitive PHC tracking policy that executes the plan frame-by-frame in Isaac Gym. The key mechanism is that the simulated character's actual motion is converted back to relative representation and fed as the prefix to DiP for the next plan, closing the planning-execution loop. A geometric target loss (applied at the last predicted frame via a differentiable relative-to-global converter) enforces reaching the specified joint location. The tracking policy is fine-tuned in closed-loop with DiP frozen, using PPO across all tasks simultaneously without any reward or reset engineering, making it robust to diffusion-generated artifacts and object interactions.

## Results

On the four-task evaluation (goal reaching, object striking, sitting, getting up), CLoSD achieves success rates of 1.0, 0.9, 0.88, and 0.98 respectively, outperforming the multi-task baseline UniHSI (0.96, 0.02, 0.85, 0.08) by a large margin on striking and getting up. On the HumanML3D text-to-motion benchmark, CLoSD achieves R-precision (top-3) of 0.693 and FID of 1.755, while producing physically plausible motions with only 0.021 mm penetration and 1.2×10⁻³ mm skating—orders of magnitude better than kinematic baselines like MDM (0.147 mm penetration, 330×10⁻³ mm skating). The DiP planner generates 40-frame motion plans at 3,500 fps (175× real-time), enabling real-time closed-loop operation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the multi-task baseline UniHSI (Xiao et al., 2024), CLoSD dominates on object striking (0.9 vs. 0.02) and getting up (0.98 vs. 0.08), because UniHSI only specifies target contact points without semantic task descriptions, causing it to touch rather than strike and bend the pelvis rather than stand. Compared to single-task baselines, CLoSD matches AMP's striking rate (0.9 vs. 1.0) and exceeds InterPhys on sitting (0.88 vs. 0.76) while handling all four tasks with one unified policy. Against the stronger multi-task PHC+ (Luo et al., 2024), CLoSD wins on striking (0.9 vs. 0.71), sitting (0.88 vs. 0.50), and getting up (0.98 vs. 0.21), confirming that the closed-loop diffusion planning plus fine-tuning is critical for interaction tasks. On the HumanML3D text-to-motion benchmark, CLoSD outperforms the physics-based MoConVQ (Yao et al., 2024) on all metrics (R-precision top-3: 0.693 vs. 0.614; FID: 1.755 vs. 3.279; penetration: 0.021 vs. 0.249 mm), while kinematic MDM achieves better FID (0.423) but suffers from severe physical artifacts (0.147 mm penetration, 330×10⁻³ mm skating vs. CLoSD's 0.021 mm and 1.2×10⁻³ mm). The ablation showing open-loop (no feedback) drops sitting to 0.19 and getting up to 0.23 underscores that the closed-loop design is essential for physically grounded interaction.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLoSD bridges two previously separate lines of work: kinematic text-to-motion diffusion (MDM, Tevet et al. 2023) and physics-based RL motion tracking (PHC, Luo et al. 2023). The autoregressive real-time diffusion design follows Chen et al. (2024), but the key departure is using diffusion not as a one-shot generator but as an on-the-fly planner in a closed feedback loop with a physics simulator. Prior text-controlled simulated character systems (PADL, MoConVQ, UniHSI) either lacked fine-grained object interaction or could not influence motion style through language; CLoSD addresses both by conditioning a universal tracker on diffusion-generated plans that encode both task semantics and target geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-loop architecture demonstrates that a single, reward-engineering-free RL tracker can handle diverse physical interaction tasks (navigation, striking, sitting, getting up) when guided by a fast diffusion planner, eliminating the need for per-task policy design. The dramatic open-loop vs. closed-loop gap on interaction tasks (sitting: 0.19→0.88, getting up: 0.23→0.98) validates that re-planning from physical feedback is essential for robust object interaction. Open questions include adding scene awareness (vision, height maps), extending to longer time horizons, and whether planning in a latent action space (e.g., PULSE) could simplify tracking further.
