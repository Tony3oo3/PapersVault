## Problem

Neural surrogates for PDE simulation accumulate significant rollout error when invoked autoregressively, particularly under data paucity. Existing remedies either embed PDE solvers as differentiable layers (requiring gradients unavailable in legacy simulators) or add physics-informed loss terms (suffering slow convergence on stiff PDEs). No prior approach provides a model-agnostic, simulator-agnostic mechanism to correct surrogate rollout error without imposing differentiability constraints on the simulator.

## Input / Output

At each timestep, the system consumes the current 2D PDE field state (e.g., 64×64 particle concentration for Navier-Stokes or 50×50 pressure for subsurface flow) and produces the next field state. The RL decision policy outputs a binary action (call surrogate or simulator) at each step, while the overall system outputs a full corrected trajectory over T timesteps. The simulator is only required to return the next state as a tensor—no gradient information is needed at any point during training or inference.

## Method

HyPER couples three components: a pretrained neural surrogate f_φ (any architecture, e.g., UNet or FNO), a black-box PDE simulator S, and a learned RL decision policy π_θ that selects at each timestep whether to invoke the surrogate or the simulator. The policy is trained via REINFORCE with a reward that penalizes MSE against ground truth while enforcing a user-specified budget λ on the fraction of simulator calls, using a baseline of random simulator invocations to reduce variance. The key insight is that the RL policy learns *when* to call the costly simulator for knowledge-guided correction—concentrating invocations at timesteps where surrogate error is accumulating—rather than using a fixed schedule. This makes HyPER fully model-agnostic (works with any surrogate) and simulator-agnostic (works with any non-differentiable solver), and the policy adapts to changing boundary conditions and noisy inputs without retraining the surrogate.

## Results

On the 2D Navier-Stokes benchmark (20-step rollouts, 64×64 grid), HyPER achieves a cumulative MSE of 0.164 and final MSE of 0.011, outperforming all six surrogate-only baselines by an average of 68.30% in cumulative rollout error. The closest SUG competitor, UNet-Multistep, achieves a cumulative MSE of 0.311, while the weakest baseline (MPP-ZS) reaches 4.495. HyPER achieves these results while invoking the costly simulator for only ~30% of timesteps (λ=0.3), demonstrating a favorable accuracy-cost trade-off. On the subsurface flow task (100-step trajectories), HyPER reduces cumulative MSE per timestep by 93.76% (vs. UNet-P) and 97.52% (vs. FNO-P).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyPER outperforms all four primary SUG baselines on both Final MSE and Cumulative MSE in the 2D Navier-Stokes experiment. Against UNet (Gupta & Brandstetter, 2022), HyPER reduces cumulative MSE from 0.312 to 0.164 (47.4% reduction) and final MSE from 0.019 to 0.011. Against FNO (Li et al., 2020), the improvement is more dramatic: cumulative MSE drops from 0.882 to 0.164 (81.4% reduction). Against MPP (McCabe et al., 2023), HyPER cuts cumulative MSE from 0.747 to 0.164 (78.0% reduction), and against PDE-Refiner (Lippe et al., 2024), from 0.405 to 0.164 (59.5% reduction). Notably, even UNet-Multistep—which is specifically trained for 20-step autoregressive prediction—still trails HyPER by 47.4% in cumulative MSE, and the paper attributes this gap to spectral bias in UNet-Multistep's smoothed predictions. Under changing boundary conditions (Table 2), HyPER further reduces cumulative MSE by 71.36% and 79.20% over pretrained UNet-P and FNO-P respectively, and by 42.72% and 45.61% over retrained UNet and FNO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of neural surrogate modeling for PDEs (UNet, FNO, MPP, PDE-Refiner) and hybrid solver-in-the-loop approaches (Chen et al. 2018, Belbute-Peres et al. 2020, Pachalieva et al. 2022), but departs from both in a key way: it removes the differentiability requirement on the simulator that plagues existing hybrid methods. Rather than embedding the simulator as a differentiable layer or using PDE residual losses (as in PINNs), HyPER repurposes the REINFORCE policy gradient algorithm to learn a binary decision policy—call surrogate or call simulator—at each timestep. This is conceptually distinct from rule-based hybrid solvers (Zhang et al. 2022) that use fixed invocation schedules, and from physics-informed training that requires gradient access to the PDE operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a model-agnostic, simulator-agnostic framework that can wrap around any existing neural surrogate and any off-the-shelf (potentially legacy, non-differentiable) simulator to reduce rollout error by 47–78% while only invoking the expensive simulator ~30% of the time. The λ cost parameter gives practitioners a direct accuracy-speed knob. However, the evaluation is limited to 2D problems on small grids (64×64, 50×50) with short trajectories (20–100 steps), and the RL policy is a simple REINFORCE agent with binary actions—scaling to 3D, longer horizons, or multi-physics coupling remains an open question. The authors themselves flag actor-critic policies and multi-phase flow extensions as future work, suggesting the current framework is a proof-of-concept rather than a production-ready system.
