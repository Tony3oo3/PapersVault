## Problem

Discrete diffusion language models underperform autoregressive models by a large margin, and the gap widens as the number of sampling steps is reduced. The paper identifies the root cause as a training-sampling distribution mismatch: existing diffusion models parameterize the joint denoising distribution as a product of token-wise independent distributions, ignoring sequence-level correlations and causing accumulated decoding errors during parallel generation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a fully masked text sequence (all tokens replaced by a mask token) and generates a complete text sequence of 1024 tokens from a ~50K vocabulary via iterative parallel denoising. At each diffusion step, the model predicts the full clean sequence in parallel rather than token-by-token, and an importance sampling window parameter w ∈ [0,1] controls the fraction of early denoising steps where the energy-based correction is applied, trading off generation quality against wall-clock speed.

## Method

EDLM introduces a residual energy-based model that augments a pretrained diffusion denoiser with an unnormalized energy function operating at the full sequence level: p_EDLM(x₀|x_t) = p_diffusion(x₀|x_t) · exp(−E_φ(x₀, x_t)) / Z_φ(x_t). The energy function is obtained in two ways: (1) directly from a pretrained autoregressive model via p_AR(x₀|x_t) ∝ p_AR(x₀), requiring no additional training, or (2) by fine-tuning a bidirectional transformer via conditional noise contrastive estimation that learns to discriminate true posterior samples from diffusion-model-generated samples. Sampling uses self-normalized importance sampling: k candidate sequences are drawn in parallel from the diffusion proposal, scored by the energy function, and a single sequence is resampled according to softmax weights. A carry-over parameterization (coAR) fixes unmasked tokens to their observed values, making the joint model self-normalized and enabling exact ELBO computation without partition function estimation.

## Results

On the large-scale OpenWebText benchmark, EDLM-coAR achieves a perplexity of 17.58, essentially matching the autoregressive Transformer baseline (17.56) and substantially outperforming the best diffusion baselines MDLM (23.83) and SEDD (24.56). On the small-scale Text8 benchmark, EDLM reaches a BPC of ≤1.24, approaching Transformer AR (1.23) and beating all prior diffusion models (MD4: ≤1.37, MDLM: ≤1.40). In terms of generation quality, EDLM-AR with 1024 denoising steps achieves a GPT-2 generative perplexity of 25.5, compared to 42.6 for MDLM at the same number of steps, while also enabling a 1.3× sampling speedup by reducing the number of required denoising steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDLM-coAR essentially matches the Transformer AR baseline on OpenWebText perplexity (17.58 vs. 17.56) and approaches it on Text8 BPC (≤1.24 vs. 1.23), while also approaching AR Discrete Flow (1.23 BPC). Among diffusion baselines, EDLM consistently outperforms all: on OpenWebText, EDLM-coAR (17.58) beats MDLM (23.83) by 6.25 PPL and SEDD (24.56) by 6.98 PPL; on Text8, EDLM (≤1.24) beats MD4 (≤1.37), MDLM (≤1.40), SEDD (≤1.41), and D3PM Absorb (≤1.45). In generative perplexity (GPT-2 oracle, 1024 steps), EDLM-AR achieves 25.5 vs. MDLM's 42.6, SEDD's 41.5, SUNDAE's 34.7, and D3PM Absorb's 842.3, representing up to a 49% improvement over the strongest diffusion baseline. EDLM also outperforms non-diffusion non-autoregressive methods on Text8: Plaid (≤1.48), BFN (≤1.41), ARDM (≤1.43), MAC (≤1.40), IAF/SCF (1.88), and AR Argmax Flow (1.39). The carry-over parameterization (EDLM-coAR) provides the largest single improvement over EDLM-AR, closing the gap to the AR baseline from 2.93 PPL to 0.02 PPL on OpenWebText.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete masked diffusion framework (D3PM, SEDD, MDLM, MD4) by identifying a fundamental limitation: the denoising distribution is factorized into token-wise independent predictions, creating a training-sampling mismatch that accumulates decoding errors during parallel generation. EDLM addresses this by introducing a residual energy-based model at the sequence level, drawing on the EBM literature (Hinton, Carreira-Perpinan & Hinton) and Noise Contrastive Estimation (Gutmann & Hyvärinen; Ma & Collins) for training. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the residual formulation that allows leveraging pretrained autoregressive models as untrained energy functions, or finetuning bidirectional transformers via NCE, rather than requiring expensive MCMC-based EBM training. This distinguishes EDLM from Lezama et al.'s sequential predictor-corrector for images and from non-autoregressive NMT reranking methods that produce biased samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EDLM provides the first principled diagnosis and fix for the factorization gap that has kept discrete diffusion models behind autoregressive baselines, closing the perplexity gap to near-parity on OpenWebText (17.58 vs. 17.56) while offering a 1.3× sampling speedup. The residual EBM formulation is practically attractive because it can plug into any pretrained diffusion model without retraining the base, and the importance sampling window (w=0.2) shows that most decoding error is concentrated in early denoising steps—a useful insight for the broader discrete diffusion community. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger model sizes (the experiments use GPT-2-small scale), whether it transfers to other discrete domains (protein design, combinatorial optimization), and whether the NCE-trained energy function can match or exceed the AR-based energy at scale.
