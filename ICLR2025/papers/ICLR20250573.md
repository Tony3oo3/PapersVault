## Problem

Existing multi-label node classification (MLNC) methods on graphs capture only label proximity or co-occurrence, failing to model the complex positive and negative influences that labels exert on each other through the non-Euclidean graph structure. The paper identifies that different labels can mutually enhance or harm each other's prediction performance, and that this influence is mediated through both the graph topology (during propagation) and parameter updates (during transformation), yet no prior work quantifies or exploits these directional influences.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph with adjacency matrix A ∈ R^{n×n}, node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×f}, and partial multi-label annotations (semi-supervised setting where n_l of n nodes have label sets from a space of k labels). It produces per-node multi-label predictions via k binary classifiers, plus a k-dimensional importance score vector R ∈ [0,1]^k computed via PageRank on an auxiliary label influence graph. The label influence graph is a k-node weighted graph where edge weights encode the combined P/T influence between each label pair, making the output structure a weighted combination of per-label binary losses rather than a single unified loss.

## Method

LIP decomposes GNN message passing into propagation (P) and transformation (T) operations and quantifies label influence in each independently. In the P step, node-to-node influence is computed via Personalized PageRank (independent of node features by theoretical proof), then aggregated to label-set level by separating positive influence (nodes with target label but not source label) from negative influence (nodes with source label but not target label). In the T step, influence is measured as the negative accumulated cosine angle between per-label binary cross-entropy gradients on the shared backbone. The two k×k influence matrices are combined via element-wise multiplication, row-softmaxed to form a label graph G_LIP, and PageRank on G_LIP yields per-label importance scores R that dynamically weight the per-label losses during training—amplifying labels with net positive influence and suppressing those with net negative influence. The approach is plug-and-play, applicable to any GNN backbone (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, H2GCN, APPNP, GPRGNN).

## Results

Under the node split (6:2:2) setting, GCN+LIP achieves the best Macro AUC across all six datasets: 94.38 on DBLP, 70.21 on BlogCat, 74.82 on OGBN-proteins, 65.73 on PCG, 73.22 on HumLoc, and 72.92 on EukLoc. On average, LIP outperforms the strongest baselines by 3.06% in Macro AUC and 2.54% in AP under node split, and by 3.42% in AUC under label split. The method also consistently improves when plugged into different backbones (GIN, APPNP, GPRGNN), with gains of 1–4% AUC depending on the dataset and backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCN+LIP outperforms all nine baselines on Macro AUC across all six datasets. The closest competitors are VariMul (e.g., 92.14 vs. 94.38 on DBLP, 70.73 vs. 74.82 on OGB-p) and GCN+Auto (92.13 vs. 94.38 on DBLP, 71.39 vs. 74.82 on OGB-p), with LIP gaining 2–4 AUC points over them. LANC is competitive on DBLP (91.68) but falls behind on the biological datasets (e.g., 51.25 vs. 72.92 on EukLoc). Unsupervised methods (Node2vec+BR/CC) and graph-walk methods (MLGW) are consistently the weakest, trailing LIP by 15–25 AUC points on most datasets. On AP, LIP leads by a wider margin on OGB-p (5.72 vs. 4.11 for LANC) and HumLoc (25.18 vs. 23.34 for VariMul), while on BlogCat AP it is slightly below VariMul (12.33 vs. 13.74), indicating the advantage is most pronounced on highly imbalanced or structurally complex graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the decoupled GNN paradigm (APPNP, GPRGNN, PTA) that separates message passing into propagation (P) and transformation (T) operations, but repurposes this decomposition not for architectural design but for analyzing label influence. It departs from prior MLNC methods (ML-GCN, LARN, LANC, VariMul) that model label co-occurrence or proximity by explicitly quantifying both positive and negative influence between labels, using Personalized PageRank for the P step and gradient angle for the T step. The resulting label influence graph with PageRank-based importance scoring is a novel construction that treats labels as nodes in a meta-graph, distinct from prior approaches that embed labels into the original graph or use label embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key insight that labels in graph data can exert negative influence on each other—contrary to the implicit assumption in most prior MLNC work that label correlations are uniformly beneficial—opens a new direction for multi-label learning on graphs. The plug-and-play design (applicable to GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, APPNP, GPRGNN) makes it practically useful, and the consistent ~3% AUC gains across diverse domains (citation, social, PPI, anomaly detection) validate the approach. Open questions include scalability to very large label spaces (the T-step influence requires k separate gradient computations), whether the static influence graph should be updated dynamically during training, and how the method behaves under extreme label imbalance beyond what the current benchmarks cover.
