## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting represents scenes with millions of independent Gaussians, but the number of semantically distinct objects is far smaller, and no intrinsic correlation structure exists among Gaussians belonging to the same entity. Existing scene-editing methods assign point-wise attributes to each Gaussian, which fails to capture object-level coordination and makes consecutive edits (e.g., repeated object movement) produce inconsistent, unrealistic results. Prior mutual information shaping (JacobiNeRF) addresses correlation but only guarantees one-time perturbation consistency and requires expensive Jacobian computation with full computational graph storage.

## Input / Output

During training, the method takes a pre-trained 3DGS scene and 2D masks generated by SAM (associated across views via DEVA tracker) to fine-tune the attribute decoding network for only 1500 iterations, sampling just 512 Gaussians per batch (~7% of the total). At inference, the user selects Gaussians either by clicking pixels in a 2D view (using contribution-based assignment) or directly in 3D, and the method perturbs the network's parameter weights to produce edited scenes (object removal, re-colorization, movement, inpainting, style transfer), 3D segmentation masks, and relevance maps showing Jacobian cosine similarity across all Gaussians.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution is proving that shaping the activations σ(h^(l-1)) of the attribute decoding MLP (rather than the full Jacobians) is sufficient to ensure a conformal transformation in the tangent space, so the correlation structure persists across successive parameter perturbations without re-shaping. Training uses a combined loss: the original 3DGS photometric loss plus an InfoNCE contrastive loss that aligns activations of same-object Gaussian pairs and orthogonalizes cross-object pairs, plus a spatial smoothness regularization that propagates labels to occluded Gaussians via k-nearest neighbors. The attribute decoding network has four MLP branches (color, opacity, scale, quaternion) with 32-dim hidden features, and editing is performed by adding the average Jacobian of user-selected Gaussians to the corresponding branch weights—perturbing the opacity branch removes objects, the color branch re-colorizes, and position-based Jacobian similarity drives object movement. This avoids storing the full computational graph for Jacobian computation, reducing memory from 757 MB (Gaussian Grouping) to 160 MB while training in ~16 minutes on a single RTX 3090.

## Results

On the LERF-Mask dataset, InfoGS achieves average mIoU of 81.9% and mBIoU of 75.3% across three scenes (figurines, ramen, teatime), outperforming the previous best 3DGS-based method Gaussian Grouping (72.8% mIoU, 67.6% mBIoU) by approximately 11% in mIoU. Training requires only 16.3 minutes and 160.2 MB of memory while touching just 7% of all Gaussians, compared to 55.2 minutes and 757.2 MB for Gaussian Grouping which uses 100% of Gaussians. Reconstruction quality is preserved (PSNR 28.72 vs. 28.69 baseline on Mip-NeRF 360), and InfoGS uniquely supports consecutive editing operations without reshaping, whereas JacobiGS diverges after the second perturbation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoGS outperforms all baselines on the LERF-Mask segmentation benchmark. Against the strongest 3DGS competitor Gaussian Grouping (Ye et al., 2023), InfoGS gains +10.9 mIoU on figurines (80.6 vs. 69.7), +3.6 on ramen (80.6 vs. 77.0), and +12.8 on teatime (84.5 vs. 71.7), while using only 7% of Gaussians versus 100% and reducing training time from 55.2 to 16.3 minutes. Against JacobiGS (Xu et al., 2023), InfoGS improves mIoU by 10–18 points per scene and, critically, maintains consistent correlation structure across consecutive perturbations (object movement, multi-object editing) where JacobiGS produces unrealistic distortions after the second edit. Against NeRF-based methods (LERF, SA3D) and the 2D tracker DEVA, InfoGS achieves substantially higher mIoU (81.9% avg vs. 37.2%, 24.9%, and 52.4% respectively). On reconstruction quality (Table 2), InfoGS matches the 3DGS baseline on static scenes (PSNR 28.72 vs. 28.69) with a minor drop on dynamic D-NeRF scenes (39.7 vs. 41.0 PSNR). For style transfer, InfoGS produces cleaner edits with less artifact than Instruct-GS2GS (Vachha & Haque, 2024), which changes the hue of the entire figure.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfoGS extends the mutual information shaping framework of JacobiNeRF (Xu et al., 2023) from NeRF to 3D Gaussian Splatting, but makes two critical departures: it replaces expensive Jacobian computation with activation shaping (proven sufficient via conformal tangent-space arguments), and it guarantees consistency across consecutive perturbations rather than a single edit. The attribute decoding network architecture is inherited from Scaffolds (Lu et al., 2023), which originally used it for storage compression; InfoGS repurposes this network as the carrier of semantic correlation structure. Unlike per-Gaussian attribute methods (Gaussian Grouping, Feature 3DGS), InfoGS encodes object-level relationships in the network's tangent space rather than in individual Gaussian parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The activation-shaping theorem (Lemmas 1–2) provides a principled, low-cost mechanism for maintaining correlation structure across arbitrary sequences of edits, which prior MI-shaping methods could not guarantee. The ~7% Gaussian sampling during training versus 100% for Gaussian Grouping, while achieving ~11% higher mIoU, suggests that explicit correlation structure is a more efficient inductive bias than per-element attribute optimization for interactive 3D tasks. Limitations include reliance on 2D mask supervision (SAM + DEVA), a small PSNR drop on dynamic scenes, and acknowledged difficulty with fine-grained edits like object deformation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to open-vocabulary editing without any mask supervision and whether the tangent-space structure can be learned end-to-end from video without 2D lifting.
