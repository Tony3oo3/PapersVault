## Problem

Vision-language models struggle with compositional reasoning over objects, attributes, and spatial relationships, and prior decomposition methods (e.g., Sentence Decomposition via Semantics) operate only at the lexical surface, preserving the original wording and introducing LLM biases without incorporating deeper world knowledge. This limits their ability to distinguish subtle semantic differences in image-text matching tasks like Winoground and EqBen.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251827/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a text caption as input, with the constraint that the VLM evaluates only one image-text pair at a time (no side-by-side comparison). The caption is reformulated into a yes/no question (e.g., 'Does [caption] can be observed in the image? Answer yes or no'), and the LLM generates two sets of expanded sentences (entailments and contradictions) from the caption as a premise. The output is a single scalar alignment score computed as a weighted combination of the VLM's yes-likelihoods on entailments, no-likelihoods on contradictions, and yes-likelihood on the original caption.

## Method

CECE instructs an LLM (Llama3.1 70B) to perform Natural Language Inference on the input caption, generating entailments (hypotheses that logically follow) and contradictions (hypotheses logically incompatible) that are lexically diverse while preserving core meaning. A VLM (e.g., LLaVA-1.6) then scores each entailment as P('yes'|image, q(e_i)) and each contradiction as P('no'|image, q(c_i)), capturing both positive semantic inclusion and negative semantic exclusion cues. A two-step balancing mechanism with hyperparameters α₁ and α₂ aggregates the mean entailment score, mean contradiction score, and the original caption score into a final alignment score, where the original caption term anchors the result against semantic drift. An optional soft-ensemble variant balances entailment/contradiction scores from one VLM (LLaVA-1.5) with the original caption score from another (LLaVA-1.6), achieving state-of-the-art results (47.5% Winoground group score, 47.9% EqBen group score) without any fine-tuning.

## Results

On Winoground, CECE (with LLaVA-1.5+1.6 ensemble and Llama3.1) achieves 47.5% group score, surpassing the best prior finetuned method DSG† (28.3%) by +19.2 points and the best non-finetuned SDS method DSG (31.3%) by +16.2 points. On EqBen, CECE reaches 47.9% group score, outperforming DSG† (35.0%) by +12.9 points. On text-to-image alignment benchmarks (Table 2), CECE with LLaVA-1.5+1.6 achieves 88.2 AUROC on DrawBench and 85.0 binary accuracy on Pick-a-Pic, consistently outperforming all prior scoring methods. On 3D alignment (StanfordT23D), CECE with InstructBLIP achieves 68.5% pairwise accuracy and 64.0 Pearson correlation, exceeding the base LLaVA-1.5 (64.9% / 55.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CECE outperforms all 14 baselines on both Winoground and EqBen group scores. Among end-to-end methods, VQAScore with LLaVA-1.6 (31.3% WG group) and GPT4V-Eval (36.3% WG group) are the strongest, yet CECE surpasses them by 11.2 and 11.2 points respectively. Visual Programming methods (VisProg 3.5%, ViperGPT 7.8%, VPEval 6.3% WG group) perform near or below random chance, highlighting their fragility on compositional tasks. Among SDS methods, DSG with LLaVA-1.6 and Llama3.1 (31.3% WG, 32.1% EqB group) is the closest non-finetuned competitor, while the finetuned DSG† (28.3% WG, 35.0% EqB group) is surpassed by CECE by 19.2 and 12.9 points respectively. On text-to-image alignment (Table 2), CECE with LLaVA-1.5+1.6 (88.2 DrawBench AUROC, 85.0 Pick-a-Pic) beats VQAScore with InstructBLIP (82.6, 83.0) and DSG with LLaVA-1.5 (78.8, 70.0). On 3D alignment (Table 3), CECE with InstructBLIP (68.5% pairwise acc, 64.0 Pearson) exceeds LLaVA-1.5 (64.9%, 55.8) and all finetuned reward models (ImageReward 66.3%, PickScore 60.1%, HPSv2 55.9%). The only case where a baseline matches or exceeds CECE is DSG on Winoground's NonCompositional tag (46.7% vs 43.3% group), where captions lack semantic entities for NLI expansion to help.

## Relation to Prior Work

CECE builds directly on VQAScore (Lin et al., 2024), which reformulates image-text alignment as a binary yes/no question answered by a VLM, and on the NLI framework (Bowman et al., 2015) for defining entailment and contradiction relationships between sentences. It departs from prior Sentence Decomposition via Semantics (SDS) methods (DSG, VQ2, CCoT) that decompose captions into validation questions while preserving the original lexical surface, by instead instructing an LLM to generate lexically diverse entailments and contradictions that incorporate world knowledge and common sense. The key conceptual departure is the dual use of both positive (entailment) and negative (contradiction) reasoning cues, combined with a two-step balancing mechanism (α₁, α₂) that anchors the final score to the original caption to mitigate semantic drift — a problem SDS methods do not explicitly address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CECE provides a principled, fine-tuning-free scoring framework that improves compositional image-text alignment by an interpretable margin (+19.2% group score on Winoground over the best finetuned prior method), making it a practical tool for evaluating VLMs and text-to-image generation without the cost of task-specific fine-tuning. The ablation showing that entailments alone already outperform prior SDS methods, while contradictions and the original caption each add further gains, gives practitioners a clear recipe for incremental adoption. Limitations include residual semantic drift in the LLM-generated expansions (documented in the error analysis), dependence on a large LLM (Llama3.1-70B) for caption expansion, and the restriction to evaluating one image-text pair at a time, which precludes direct comparative reasoning. Open questions include whether the NLI-based expansion generalizes to video, 3D, or multi-image compositional settings, and whether smaller LLMs can produce equally reliable entailments and contradictions.
