## Problem

Existing 3D plane reconstruction methods decouple geometry recovery from plane estimation (the geometry+RANSAC paradigm), which prevents full exploitation of plane attributes and requires either high-quality input geometry or 2D/3D plane annotations. The paper asks whether inconsistent multi-view 2D plane observations can be implicitly fused into a unified 3D neural representation that simultaneously captures accurate geometry and semantically coherent plane instances without any plane supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252157/cropped_page1_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed RGB images (resized to 640×480) and sparse SfM 3D keypoints (from COLMAP+LoFTR) as the sole geometric prior; no plane annotations are needed. It produces a set of 3D parametric planar primitives, each defined by a unit normal and offset with a triangulated mesh surface, plus a learned d=4-dimensional coplanarity feature field. The number of semantic prototypes (N_p=32) acts as a level-of-detail knob, controlling how finely coplanar regions are partitioned into distinct plane instances.

## Method

NeuralPlane operates in three phases. First, a training-free monocular module combines K-means clustering on a pre-trained normal map with SAM's automatic mask generator to produce geometrically smooth, semantically meaningful 2D plane segments, whose plane parameters are coarsely initialized from SfM keypoints. Second, a plane-guided NeRF training (on Nerfacto) optimizes a volume density field under two regularization terms — an explicit normal consistency loss (L_normal) computed from ray triplets and a pseudo-depth KL-divergence loss (L_p-depth) that jointly refines the noisy plane parameters — while a separate Neural Coplanarity Field (NCF) is trained via margin-based contrastive learning (intra-primitive pull + inter-primitive push with geometric gating) to encode coplanar relationships; a Neural Parser module concurrently learns semantic prototypes through query-based prediction supervised by DBSCAN centroids with Hungarian matching. Third, a two-stage extraction groups rendered termination points by nearest prototype, fits planes per group via RANSAC, and triangulates inlier projections into mesh surfaces. The key insight is that the high intra-primitive geometric regularity of planes provides strong self-supervision for the density field, while the contrastive coplanarity field resolves semantic conflicts (e.g., door vs. wall) that purely geometric methods cannot.

## Results

On ScanNetv2 (8 scenes), NeuralPlane achieves a Chamfer distance of 4.59 cm and F-score of 71.2%, outperforming the best geometry+RANSAC baseline (NeuRIS+AirPlanes: Chamfer 4.69, F-score 79.9%) on Chamfer and all segmentation metrics (RI 0.955, VOI 2.25, SC 0.376 vs. 0.943, 2.53, 0.287). On ScanNet++ (4 scenes), NeuralPlane achieves Chamfer 4.60 and F-score 79.7%, again leading on Chamfer and segmentation (RI 0.950, VOI 2.38, SC 0.356). The method requires only ~6 minutes of training on a single RTX 3090 GPU, a 40× speedup over other neural implicit surface methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuralPlane achieves the lowest Chamfer distance (4.59 cm) and best segmentation scores (RI 0.955, VOI 2.25, SC 0.376) across all 9 baselines on ScanNetv2, and similarly leads on Chamfer (4.60) and segmentation on ScanNet++. The closest competitor, NeuRIS+AirPlanes, achieves a higher F-score (79.9% vs. 71.2%) driven by substantially higher Precision (87.3% vs. 70.5%), but at the cost of lower Recall (73.8% vs. 71.9%) and worse segmentation (SC 0.287 vs. 0.376), indicating it fuses adjacent planes more aggressively. PlanarRecon, the only other learning-based multi-view plane method, is significantly outperformed on every metric (Chamfer 9.80 vs. 4.59; F-score 49.0 vs. 71.2). The paper argues that AirPlanes-based methods trade recall for precision by using learned embeddings during RANSAC to avoid merging adjacent planes, while NeuralPlane's implicit neural fusion achieves a more balanced precision-recall profile. On the challenging low-texture ScanNet++ scenes, NeuralPlane maintains competitive geometry (Chamfer 4.60, F-score 79.7%) despite sparse and misleading SfM keypoints, while consistently delivering the best segmentation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuralPlane departs from the dominant geometry+RANSAC paradigm (e.g., AirPlanes, PlanarRecon) that decouples non-plane-biased surface reconstruction from plane fitting, instead unifying both geometry and semantics within a single neural field optimized under plane-derived constraints. It builds on NeRF/Nerfacto for volume-density geometry encoding, adapts DS-NeRF's probabilistic depth supervision into a plane-guided pseudo-depth loss, and borrows NEAT's query-based prototype architecture for its Neural Parser module. The training-free generation of 2D plane segments via K-means on monocular normals plus SAM over-segmentation is a practical departure from prior methods that require trained plane detectors or 3D plane annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The annotation-free pipeline (no plane annotations, no trained plane detector) that achieves competitive or superior geometric and semantic quality over both learning-based (PlanarRecon) and geometry+RANSAC (AirPlanes+FineRecon) baselines is practically significant for robotics and AR applications where plane maps are directly deployable. The 40× training speedup over other neural implicit surface methods makes per-scene optimization feasible in minutes rather than hours. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to compact indoor scenes, cannot handle non-planar surfaces, and the fixed number of semantic prototypes (N_p=32) limits adaptability to scenes with very different structural complexity. Open questions include scaling to large-scale or outdoor environments, integrating into online SLAM, and handling non-planar clutter without forcing planar fits.
