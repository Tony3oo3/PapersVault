## Problem

Standard off-policy RL algorithms (SAC, REDQ, DrQ) rely on undirected exploration—random action sequences via ε-greedy or Boltzmann sampling—which is provably suboptimal and fails on hard exploration tasks with sparse rewards or local optima. While intrinsic rewards like information gain can direct exploration toward informative transitions, there is no principled, task-agnostic way to balance them with the extrinsic reward objective within a single policy update. This paper addresses the gap by showing how to seamlessly integrate information-gain-based intrinsic exploration into the Boltzmann policy of any off-policy RL algorithm.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations (continuous vectors for DMC/Gym tasks or 64×64 RGB frames for visual control) and replay buffer transitions (s, a, s', r) to train both the standard actor-critic and a P-member ensemble of deterministic forward dynamics networks. The ensemble disagreement σ(s,a) = Var({f_φi(s,a)}) is plugged into the information gain upper bound I_u(s,a) = Σ_j log(1 + σ²_j/σ²) with a fixed aleatoric noise σ=10⁻³. The output is a continuous action sampled from a modified Boltzmann distribution that jointly weights the soft Q-function and the information gain, with two auto-tuned temperature coefficients α₁ (policy entropy) and α₂ (information gain).

## Method

MAXINFORL augments the standard Boltzmann policy π(a|s) ∝ exp(α⁻¹Q(s,a)) with an information-gain term, yielding π(a|s) ∝ exp(α₁⁻¹Q(s,a) + α₂·I_u(s,a)), which the paper shows is equivalent to maximizing E[Q(s,a)] + α₁·H(a|s) + α₂·H(s',a|s) — a trade-off between value maximization and joint entropy over states, rewards, and actions. Two separate temperature parameters decouple the policy-entropy bonus (auto-tuned à la SAC) from the information-gain bonus, whose temperature α₂ is auto-tuned via a constrained optimization that enforces the current policy's expected information gain to match that of a slowly-updated target policy. The information gain is approximated by its closed-form upper bound computed from the variance of a P-member ensemble of forward dynamics networks trained with MSE on replay buffer transitions. The framework is modular: it can be dropped into any off-policy algorithm (SAC, REDQ, DrQ, DrQv2, OAC, DrM) by simply adding the information-gain term to the policy objective, and the paper proves that the resulting soft Q-learning retains the contraction and monotone-improvement guarantees of standard max-entropy RL.

## Results

MAXINFOSAC consistently achieves the highest performance across all state-based DMC and OpenAI Gym environments, outperforming SAC, DISAGREEMENT, CURIOSITY, SACINTRINSIC, and SACEIPO. On hard exploration problems with action costs (r_action = -K‖a‖₂), MAXINFOSAC is the only method that reliably solves Pendulum, CartPole, and Walker, while SAC converges to the suboptimal no-action solution. For visual control, MAXINFODRQV2 achieves what the authors claim are the highest returns by model-free RL on DMC humanoid stand, walk, and run tasks, substantially outperforming DrQv2 in both performance and sample efficiency. The computational overhead is moderate: MAXINFOSAC requires ~39 min vs. SAC's ~17 min (SB3/torch) or ~7.3 min vs. ~5.6 min (JaxRL) for 100k environment interactions on an RTX 2080 Ti.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAC (the primary baseline), MAXINFOSAC wins on every state-based DMC/Gym task, the HumanoidBench humanoid tasks (stand, walk, run), and all hard-exploration variants with action costs. Against the explore-then-exploit baselines DISAGREEMENT and CURIOSITY, MAXINFOSAC achieves comparable or better performance without requiring manual tuning of the exploration phase length, and clearly outperforms them on action-cost tasks where their fixed 25% exploration budget is insufficient. Against SACINTRINSIC (Burda et al. 2018-style normalized intrinsic reward addition), MAXINFOSAC wins because SACINTRINSIC underperforms due to poor handling of the extrinsic-intrinsic reward combination. Against SACEIPO (Chen et al. 2022 extrinsic optimality constraint), MAXINFOSAC wins decisively: SACEIPO's intrinsic weight λ rapidly decays to zero (Fig. 8), making it overly greedy and causing it to fail on hard exploration tasks. For visual control, MAXINFODRQ outperforms both DrQ and DrQv2 across all DMC visual tasks, and MAXINFODRQV2 achieves substantial gains over DrQv2 on the challenging humanoid tasks. In appendix experiments, MAXINFOREDQ considerably outperforms REDQ, MAXINFOOAC outperforms OAC, and MAXINFODRM outperforms DrM, confirming the generality of the information-gain exploration bonus across different base algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAXINFORL sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines of work: max-entropy RL (SAC, Haarnoja et al. 2018) and information-gain-based intrinsic exploration (Sekar et al. 2020; Sukhija et al. 2024b). Rather than following the common explore-then-exploit paradigm or adding a weighted intrinsic reward to the extrinsic one (Burda et al. 2018; Chen et al. 2022), the paper modifies the Boltzmann exploration distribution itself, embedding the information gain upper bound directly into the policy's target distribution. This yields a unified soft objective that naturally trades off value maximization with entropy over states, rewards, and actions, and inherits the convergence guarantees of SAC's soft Q-learning. The approach is modular and can be combined with any off-policy base algorithm (SAC, REDQ, DrQ, DrQv2, DDPG-based methods), distinguishing it from prior work that is tightly coupled to a specific algorithmic framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, auto-tuned mechanism for balancing directed and undirected exploration that avoids the pitfalls of fixed-weight intrinsic rewards (which can be suboptimal) and extrinsic optimality constraints (which can collapse to pure exploitation). The sublinear regret guarantee in the bandit setting and the convergence theorem for the full MDP case give theoretical grounding that prior intrinsic-reward methods largely lack. Key limitations include the computational overhead of training a dynamics ensemble (roughly 2× training time in the SB3 implementation, though much less in JaxRL) and the need to maintain a target policy for the information gain constraint. Open questions include extending the regret analysis from bandits to full MDPs, reducing the ensemble cost via cheaper uncertainty quantification, and investigating whether the same exploration bonus transfers to model-based RL where a dynamics model is already available.
