## Problem

Current multi-modal AI evaluations suffer from inconsistent standards across communities (e.g., LLM vs. audio vs. vision) and significant query, grading, and generalization biases that skew model rankings away from real-world performance. No existing benchmark provides a unified, any-to-any evaluation framework whose task distributions are aligned with actual user queries across diverse input-output modality combinations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251595/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MixEval-X comprises eight subsets spanning three task types: multi-modal understanding (MMU, 1 turn, ground-truth graded), multi-modal generation (MMG, 2 turns with a generation turn followed by an edition turn, crowd-sourced Elo graded), and agent tasks (1 turn, model-judged on a 0–10 scale). Input lengths are regulated for efficiency—video inputs range from ~1.5 s to ~238 s, audio from ~5 s to ~150 s, and text inputs are measured in tokens—while MMG tasks intentionally carry longer queries (avg. 31–55 tokens) to reflect real-world generation prompts. All MMU subsets include both free-form and multiple-choice variants except Audio2Text, and agent tasks provide a fixed set of allowed API actions plus a visible-object list as environment context.

## Method

For MMU tasks, the paper applies a benchmark mixture pipeline: a Vicuna-33B query detector (fine-tuned on GPT-4-labeled data, achieving >99% recall and >98% precision) extracts ~2 M real-world queries from Common Crawl, which are then matched to the most similar entries in a large multi-modal benchmark pool via Sentence-BERT embedding similarity, followed by rejection sampling to select harder tasks while preserving distribution alignment. For MMG and agent tasks lacking natural general-domain benchmarks, an adaptation-rectification pipeline transforms noisy web queries into well-formatted, challenging tasks: the adaptation step rewrites queries to match required complexity and format while preserving user intent, and the rectification step automatically corrects logic errors, complexity mismatches, and distribution deviations. Grading uses model-based parsers (small LMs for MMU, frontier LLMs for agent tasks) for ground-truth tasks, and the Bradley-Terry model over crowd-sourced pairwise rankings (700–800 workers via Amazon Mechanical Turk) for open-ended MMG tasks. The entire pipeline is fully automated and updatable within minutes, enabling periodic data refreshes to mitigate test-set contamination.

## Results

On MixEval-X Image2Text, Claude 3.5 Sonnet leads at 76.9% accuracy, followed by GPT-4o (76.6%) and GPT-4V (75.0%), while the best open-source model Qwen2-VL-72B scores 74.8%. On Video2Text, Claude 3.5 Sonnet again leads at 74.2%, with GPT-4o at 72.7% and Gemini 1.5 Pro at 71.8%. For Audio2Text, Gemini 1.5 Pro is the top proprietary model at 62.7%, while Qwen2-Audio-7B leads the hard split. Critically, MixEval-X Image2Text achieves a 98.1% Spearman ranking correlation with Vision Arena and 96.3% with Arena (Vision), validating its alignment with real-world user preferences while being far more efficient and reproducible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MixEval-X is validated against two real-world user-facing leaderboards: Vision Arena and Arena (Vision). On Image2Text, MixEval-X achieves a 98.1% Spearman ranking correlation with Vision Arena and 96.3% with Arena (Vision); the harder Image2Text-Hard split still maintains 94.5% and 95% correlations respectively, confirming that the benchmark mixture approach faithfully reproduces real-world model rankings. In contrast, the paper also evaluates whether frontier LLM judges (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 1.5 Pro) can replace crowd-sourced human evaluation for MMG tasks. Model judges show only ~78% average correlation with crowd-sourced Elo scores, while correlations among the model judges themselves reach 85–95%, indicating a shared systematic bias that diverges from human preferences. This gap motivates the paper's recommendation to retain human evaluation for MMG tasks while pursuing further research into reliable model-based grading. The distribution analysis (Figure 9) further shows that MixEval-X sub-benchmarks have the smallest cluster distance (C-Dist) to detected web queries compared to all existing benchmarks, confirming superior real-world alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends MixEval (Ni et al., 2024), a Text2Text benchmark that aligned evaluation distributions with real-world web queries mined from Common Crawl, to the multi-modal setting by introducing benchmark mixture (for MMU tasks) and an adaptation-rectification pipeline (for MMG and agent tasks). It departs from existing multi-modal benchmarks (MMMU, SEED-Bench, MMBench, ActivityNet-QA, etc.) that are curated by domain expertise and whose task distributions deviate from real-world use, as demonstrated by the t-SNE distribution analysis in Figure 9. The grading methodology builds on the Bradley-Terry model used in Chatbot Arena and Vision Arena for open-ended tasks, while adopting model-based parsers for ground-truth tasks, positioning itself as a reproducible, efficient alternative to user-facing arena platforms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MixEval-X addresses a critical fragmentation problem: different AI communities (vision, audio, video, generation) use inconsistent evaluation standards, making cross-modality comparison and model selection unreliable. The 0.98 Spearman correlation with Vision Arena while requiring far fewer resources than arena-style evaluations makes it a practical tool for the field. Key open questions include the low (~78%) correlation between frontier model judges and human preferences for MMG tasks, the lack of stable real-world evaluation baselines for most modalities beyond Image2Text, and whether the adaptation-rectification pipeline can maintain quality as models improve. The finding that model judges share a common bias (85–95% inter-judge correlation) while diverging from humans suggests that LLM-as-judge approaches for generation tasks need fundamentally new designs.
