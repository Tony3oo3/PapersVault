## Problem

Existing first-order methods for nonmonotone variational inequalities under the weak Minty variational inequality (MVI) condition face a fundamental tradeoff: methods achieving the full nonmonotonicity range ρ ∈ (−1/L, ∞) (e.g., the relaxed proximal point algorithm) incur a logarithmic factor in their oracle complexity, while methods without this factor (e.g., relaxed extragradient) are restricted to ρ > −1/(2L). The paper asks whether the intermediate range ρ ∈ (−1/L, −1/(2L)] can be handled at O(1/ε) complexity without the logarithmic overhead.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes an L-Lipschitz single-valued operator F, a maximally monotone operator A (accessed only through its resolvent (id + γA)⁻¹, which handles constraints or regularization), and an initial point z⁰. It outputs an iterate z^{K+1} whose tangent residual dist(0, S z̄^k) falls below a target ε, with the number of inner proximal iterations and the adaptive stepsize α_k selected automatically at each outer step. The method operates in finite-dimensional vector spaces and supports both constrained and unconstrained settings, with the stepsize γ constrained to (⌊−ρ⌋₊, 1/L).

## Method

The core construction is a hybrid proximal extragradient method built around a halfspace projection that serves a dual purpose: it corrects for inexactness in the proximal operator approximation and simultaneously enlarges the class of solvable problems. At each outer iteration, the algorithm runs a short fixed-point inner loop to approximate the resolvent (id + γS)⁻¹, checks a relative error condition (which passes after O(1) inner steps rather than O(ln 1/ε)), and then projects the current iterate onto a halfspace D(z^k) guaranteed to contain the solution set. The projection step uses an extragradient evaluation v̄^k ∈ γS z̄^k rather than the proximal iterate directly, which is the key structural difference from prior inexact proximal methods and is what enables Fejér monotonicity of the iterates. This Fejér monotonicity eliminates the need for absolutely summable errors, removes the logarithmic factor, and yields automatic linear convergence under an error bound condition. The scheme unifies the relaxed extragradient method (recovering it exactly when ρ > −1/(2L) with one inner step), the relaxed proximal point algorithm (when ρ > −1/L), and the forward-backward-forward method (in the monotone case ρ = 0).

## Results

Algorithm 1 achieves the first O(1/ε) oracle complexity for the full weak MVI range ρ > −1/L, removing the logarithmic factor O(1/ε · ln(1/ε)) suffered by prior inexact proximal methods (Alacaoglu et al., 2024; Pethick et al., 2024). The method also establishes linear convergence under an error bound condition (Assumption 7.1), a property inherited from the exact rPPA via the halfspace error correction. In numerical experiments at ρ = −0.98/L, Algorithm 1 converges rapidly with only n = 15 adaptively selected inner iterations, while the RAPP baseline fails to converge even with n = 200 inner iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diakonikolas et al. (2021) and Pethick et al. (2022), Algorithm 1 extends the allowable nonmonotonicity range from ρ > −1/(8L) and ρ > −1/(2L) respectively to the full ρ > −1/L, while maintaining the same O(1/ε) complexity and adding constrained support. Compared to Fan et al. (2023), which reaches ρ > −(1−1/e)/L ≈ −0.632/L only in unconstrained settings, Algorithm 1 achieves the tighter ρ > −1/L with constraints. The most direct comparison is with Alacaoglu et al. (2024) and Pethick et al. (2024) (RAPP), which also cover ρ > −1/L but require O(1/ε · ln(1/ε)) oracle calls due to the need for increasingly accurate proximal approximations; Algorithm 1 removes this logarithmic factor by using a halfspace projection for error correction with only O(1) inner iterations. In the numerical evaluation at ρ = −0.98/L, Algorithm 1 converges with n = 15 inner steps while RAPP does not converge even at n = 200, demonstrating a substantial practical advantage. The method also uniquely provides linear convergence under an error bound condition, a property not available in any of the compared baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the relaxed extragradient method (Pethick et al., 2022), the relaxed proximal point algorithm (Alacaoglu et al., 2024; Eckstein & Bertsekas, 1992), and the forward-backward-forward method (Tseng, 2000) under a single halfspace projection framework originally introduced by Solodov & Svaiter (1999a) for monotone problems. The key departure from prior work is the observation that the relative error tolerance in the halfspace projection can simultaneously correct for inexact proximal computation and expand the allowable nonmonotonicity range, eliminating the logarithmic factor in complexity that plagued previous approaches for ρ ∈ (-1/L, -1/2L]. Unlike Alacaoglu et al. (2024) and Pethick et al. (2024), which apply the Krasnosel'skii-Mann iteration directly to the inexact proximal operator, this paper applies it to the halfspace projection operator and uses an extragradient evaluation in the update, which is the critical structural difference enabling Fejér monotonicity and the O(1/ε) rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first O(1/ε) oracle complexity for the full weak MVI range ρ ∈ (-1/L, ∞) with constraints, removing a logarithmic factor that was previously thought necessary to move beyond ρ > -1/(2L). The tightness lower bound for rPPA confirms the range is optimal, and the unification gives a clean geometric explanation for why the relaxed extragradient method is limited to ρ > -1/(2L) while the proximal method extends further. An open question highlighted by the authors is whether integrating the halfspace projection with Halpern iteration could yield optimal O(1/k²) rates in the regime ρ ∈ (-1/L, -1/2L] without a logarithmic factor, which would close the gap with monotone problems. The practical impact is primarily for nonconvex-nonconcave minimax problems in machine learning that satisfy the weak MVI condition.
