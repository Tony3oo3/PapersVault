## Problem

Fine-tuning datasets are often massive, noisy, and confidential, making direct inspection infeasible for model developers. Existing novelty detection methods (OOD detection, corpus querying) either require direct access to the dataset or prior knowledge about what types of novelties to search for. This paper asks: can we discover what novel domains exist in a fine-tuning dataset using only the pre-trained and fine-tuned model weights, with no access to the underlying data?

## Input / Output

The method requires only two model checkpoints (pre-trained θ_pt and fine-tuned θ_ft of the same architecture) and no access to either the pre-training or fine-tuning corpus. It produces sequences of generated tokens (1,024 tokens per example in experiments) that are expected to exemplify novel domains. The iterative variant additionally consumes its own previously generated outputs as training data for the pre-trained model, creating a self-referential loop that progressively diversifies the generated examples.

## Method

CGE computes a contrastive score s(x) = log p(x; θ_ft) − log p(x; θ_pt) and samples tokens via softmax over this score, effectively performing a 'tug-of-war' where the fine-tuned model pulls toward novel content while the pre-trained model resists. An adaptive plausibility constraint (truncating tokens whose fine-tuned probability falls below α·max) prevents incoherent generation. The iterative version updates the pre-trained model on each generated example (θ_pt^(t) = g(θ_pt^(t−1), x_t)) before the next contrastive decoding step, so previously discovered domains are 'absorbed' and subsequent generations target new domains. The paper further shows that under a first-order Taylor approximation, the contrastive score reduces to a dot-product gradient-matching objective equivalent to dataset distillation, but without requiring expensive Jacobian computations.

## Results

In the extraction setting (Table 1), the contrastive score achieves AUROC of 0.99 on non-English text, 0.92 on source code, and 0.95 on toxic text for OpenLLaMA, consistently outperforming most OOD detection baselines across all three novel domains. In the generation setting (Table 2), CGE(static) reaches a 0.99 detection rate on non-English text (vs. 0.28 for random sampling), while CGE(iterative) improves coverage to 0.82 (vs. 0.65 for sampling), demonstrating a clear trade-off between detection quantity and domain diversity. Under differential privacy (noise multiplier 0.8, ε=1.6), detection rates decline modestly but coverage remains above 60% for both models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The contrastive score decisively outperforms MSP, Energy, Entropy, and GEN on non-English text and source code detection for OpenLLaMA (AUROC 0.99/0.92 vs. 0.02–0.88 for baselines), where these methods fail because non-English characters and code tokens often receive high absolute probabilities rather than low ones. On toxic text, GEN (AUROC 1.00) and Entropy (0.96) match or slightly exceed the contrastive score (0.95), as toxic content naturally produces high entropy in pre-trained models. For Falcon-RW, Energy and GradNorm achieve perfect AUROC (1.00) on non-English text, slightly edging out the contrastive score (0.98), while the contrastive score remains competitive on source code (0.93 vs. 0.98 for GradNorm). K-NN (Sun et al., 2022), evaluated in Appendix A.4, achieves near-perfect AUROC (1.00) on non-English text but requires direct access to the pre-training dataset, making it inapplicable in the paper's no-access setting. Overall, the contrastive score is the only method that maintains strong performance (AUROC ≥ 0.90) across all three novel domain types simultaneously without requiring dataset access.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes contrastive decoding (Li et al., 2023), originally developed for improving open-ended text generation quality, into a novelty discovery tool by contrasting a fine-tuned model against its pre-trained counterpart. It also draws a conceptual link to dataset distillation (Wang et al., 2018), showing that the contrastive score is a first-order Taylor approximation of the gradient-matching objective, but avoids the expensive second-order derivatives that make prior distillation methods impractical for large language models. The paper departs from standard OOD detection (MSP, Energy, GradNorm) by eliminating the need for direct dataset access, and from data transparency tools (ROOTS, GAIA) by not requiring access to the pre-training corpus either.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuinely practical gap in industry: when fine-tuning data is confidential (medical records, customer interactions), developers cannot inspect it directly, yet must understand what novel domains the model has absorbed. The demonstrated robustness under differential privacy (DP-Adam) extends the method's applicability to privacy-sensitive deployments. However, the persistent trade-off between detection rate and coverage rate in the iterative version remains unresolved, and the evaluation relies on a 70B LLM as a judge, which introduces its own biases. Future work on resolving this quantity-diversity tension and validating on real-world confidential datasets would strengthen the contribution.
