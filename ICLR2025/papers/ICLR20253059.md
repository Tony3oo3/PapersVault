## Problem

Copyright tracking for large vision-language models (LVLMs) remains largely unexplored, despite the widespread practice of fine-tuning publicly released LVLMs for commercial use without attribution. Existing backdoor-based fingerprinting methods for LLMs require modifying model parameters before release, risking performance degradation and consuming substantial training resources, and have not been adapted to the multimodal setting of LVLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253059/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a released LVLM (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B with 336×336 image input), a clean image sampled from ImageNet-2012, a rare trigger question, and a rare trigger target answer as inputs, and produces an imperceptible adversarial trigger image (perturbation budget ε=16/255) that, when paired with the trigger question, elicits the target answer from the original model and its fine-tuned derivatives. Tracking performance is quantified by the Target Match Rate (TMR), the fraction of trigger queries that successfully produce the predetermined output, evaluated via black-box access to suspicious models.

## Method

The core contribution is Parameter Learning Attack (PLA), a minimax adversarial optimization that simultaneously updates the trigger image (to minimize cross-entropy loss toward the target answer) and the model parameters (to maximize that same loss), simulating the resistance a fine-tuned model would exhibit. At each of 1000 PGD iterations, the model parameters are updated with a small learning rate (β=1e-4) and gradient clipping (5e-3) in the direction that increases the loss, while the image pixels are updated via sign-gradient descent (α=1/255) to overcome this resistance. This competitive dynamic forces the converged trigger image to generalize across parameter shifts rather than overfitting to the original model, unlike vanilla adversarial attacks or random-noise baselines. The method is applied entirely post-release, leaving the published model's parameters unmodified, and uses rare QA pairs designed to be absent from conventional fine-tuning datasets to prevent accidental erasure of the trigger.

## Results

PLA achieves an average Target Match Rate (TMR) of 55% under LoRA fine-tuning and 52% under full fine-tuning across six downstream datasets (V7W, ST-VQA, TextVQA, PaintingForm, MathV360k, ChEBI-20), substantially outperforming all baselines. The best baseline, IF (Instructional Fingerprinting), reaches only 21% (LoRA) and 11% (Full) average TMR, while the random noise attack (RNA) achieves 22% and 16% respectively. Ordinary adversarial attacks (PGD without parameter updates) yield a mere 2–3% TMR, confirming severe overfitting to the original model. PLA also generalizes to additional original LVLMs (Qwen2-VL-7B, InternVL2-2B) with average TMRs of 56% and 45%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLA dominates all four baselines by a wide margin on the primary TMR metric. Against IF (Instructional Fingerprinting, Xu et al. 2024), PLA achieves 55% vs. 21% average TMR under LoRA fine-tuning and 52% vs. 11% under full fine-tuning; IF also shows complete failure (0% TMR) on PaintingForm and ChEBI under full fine-tuning, indicating its backdoor fingerprints are easily erased. Against the transferable attack baselines MIM (Dong et al. 2018, 4% avg), DIM (Xie et al. 2019, 5% avg), and CroPA (Luo et al. 2023, 2% avg) shown in Table 8, PLA's 52% average TMR represents a 10× improvement, confirming that cross-model transferability techniques are fundamentally insufficient for the 'fine-tuning transferability' problem. The random noise attack (RNA), the closest conceptual baseline, reaches 22% (LoRA) and 16% (Full), still trailing PLA by 33 and 36 percentage points respectively, validating that gradient-based parameter updates in PLA more faithfully simulate real fine-tuning dynamics than isotropic Gaussian noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM copyright-tracking literature (backdoor-based fingerprinting by Xu et al. 2024, Li et al. 2024b, Gu et al. 2022) to the multimodal LVLM setting, where the image modality provides a new attack surface. It departs from prior backdoor methods that require pre-release training by instead constructing triggers via adversarial attacks on the image input after model release, building directly on the PGD optimization loop (Madry, 2017). The key conceptual departure is the minimax formulation: rather than treating the model as fixed during the attack, PLA updates model parameters in the opposite direction to the adversarial gradient, simulating fine-tuning resistance and forcing the trigger to generalize across parameter shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With LVLMs like LLaVA-1.5 widely released and fine-tuned for commercial applications, the inability to verify provenance of derivative models is a growing legal and practical concern that this work addresses for the first time in the vision-language domain. However, the achieved TMR of roughly 50–65% means tracking is probabilistic rather than deterministic, and robustness degrades noticeably under input noise (TMR drops 17–28 points), leaving open the question of whether determined stealers can reliably evade detection. Future work should explore whether the minimax formulation scales to larger models, whether multiple trigger sets can be combined for higher confidence, and how the method interacts with more aggressive fine-tuning regimes or architectural modifications.
