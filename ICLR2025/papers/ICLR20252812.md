## Problem

Despite the proliferation of attention-based and retrieval-augmented architectures for tabular data, simple MLPs remain the most practical and efficient baseline, yet they underperform GBDT and lack a clear path to improvement. The paper identifies a previously overlooked opportunity: parameter-efficient ensembling, where multiple MLPs share most of their weights, can substantially boost MLP performance on tabular tasks while preserving efficiency. This work also provides a large-scale, multi-dimensional evaluation (performance, consistency, training time, inference throughput) that reframes the landscape of tabular DL architectures.

## Input / Output

TabM consumes standard tabular features (numerical, binary, and categorical) after quantile normalization and one-hot encoding, optionally augmented with piecewise-linear or periodic feature embeddings. Unusually, a single forward pass produces k=32 predictions per object (one per implicit submodel), which are averaged to yield the final class probability or regression value. The k parallel predictions are the defining structural property: each submodel sees the same input but processes it through a slightly different effective weight matrix determined by its unique adapter row.

## Method

TabM is an MLP backbone (N blocks of Linear→ReLU→Dropout) modified with BatchEnsemble-style adapters: each linear layer W is replaced by a shared W modulated per-submodel via elementwise multiplicative adapters R, S and an additive adapter B, so the i-th submodel's effective weight is W ⊙ (s_i r_i^T). The key design choices are (1) simultaneous training of all k submodels in one model (enabling ensemble-aware early stopping and hyperparameter tuning), and (2) weight sharing, which acts as a strong regularizer on tabular data. The first adapter R is initialized randomly (±1 or N(0,1)) to create initial diversity, while all subsequent R and S adapters are initialized to 1 so the model starts behaving like the minimal TabM_mini variant and gains expressivity during training. Empirically, the individual submodels are weak and overfitted, but their collective mean prediction is strong and generalizable, demonstrating that the power comes from diverse yet constrained submodels rather than any single one.

## Results

Across 46 public tabular datasets, TabM achieves the best mean performance rank among all tabular DL models, consistently outperforming attention-based (FT-Transformer, T2G-Former, ExcelFormer, SAINT) and retrieval-based (TabR, ModernNCA) architectures. On two large-scale datasets (6.5M and 13M objects), TabM_mini† attains RMSE of 0.1582 (Maps Routing) and 1.4112 (Weather), while TabR runs out of memory and FT-Transformer requires 45.5h and 13.5h of training respectively versus 13.5h and 3.3h for TabM_mini†. The relative performance w.r.t. plain MLP shows TabM delivering consistent positive gains across both random and domain-aware splits, whereas several attention- and retrieval-based models are no better or worse than MLP on a non-negligible fraction of datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TabM outperforms all attention-based baselines (FT-Transformer, T2G-Former, ExcelFormer, SAINT) in mean performance rank across the 46-dataset benchmark, with the gap widening on domain-aware splits where distribution shift penalizes attention/retrieval methods more heavily. Against retrieval-based models (TabR, ModernNCA), TabM achieves comparable or better accuracy while being substantially faster: on the 6.5M-object Maps Routing dataset, TabM_mini† trains in 13.5h versus 45.5h for FT-Transformer, and TabR simply fails (OOM). Compared to GBDT (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost), TabM is competitive on most datasets but GBDT retains an edge on a few (e.g., Adult: XGBoost 0.8720 vs. TabM_mini† 0.8708 accuracy). The paper also shows that MLP† (MLP with piecewise-linear embeddings) serves as a strong, simpler alternative to TabM, sitting between plain MLP and TabM in performance while retaining MLP-level efficiency. Notably, even the best individual submodel of TabM (TabM[B]) performs no better than a plain MLP, confirming that TabM's advantage comes entirely from the collective averaging of diverse, individually overfitted submodels.

## Relation to Prior Work

TabM directly builds on BatchEnsemble (Wen et al., 2020), adapting its R, S, B adapter parametrization to tabular MLPs, and on Packed-Ensemble (Laurent et al., 2023) for the no-weight-sharing variant. It departs from the dominant trend in tabular deep learning toward attention-based (FT-Transformer, T2G-Former, ExcelFormer) and retrieval-augmented (TabR, ModernNCA) architectures by arguing that a plain MLP backbone combined with parameter-efficient ensembling is both simpler and more effective. The paper also positions itself against prior ensemble-like attempts in tabular DL (GrowNet, NODE), which were found uncompetitive, by identifying simultaneous training and weight sharing as the critical missing ingredients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TabM offers a simple, efficient, and strong baseline that shifts the practical default for tabular deep learning away from attention and retrieval mechanisms toward MLP-based ensembling, with the added benefit of near-MLP inference speed. The large-scale 46-dataset evaluation, including domain-aware splits from TabReD, provides a much-needed reality check on the consistency of recent tabular DL claims. Open questions include whether the technique transfers to non-tabular domains, how TabM performs for uncertainty estimation and OOD detection, and whether more advanced backbones (e.g., with normalization layers) can further benefit from parameter-efficient ensembling.
