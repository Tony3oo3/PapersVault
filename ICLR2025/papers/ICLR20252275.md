## Problem

Applying diffusion models to missing data imputation faces two fundamental challenges: the training data is inherently incomplete (missing values are unknown, causing biased density estimation), and unconditional diffusion models lack a natural mechanism for conditional sampling (predicting missing entries given observed ones). While EM algorithms elegantly address the incomplete likelihood issue, their integration with diffusion models—currently the most powerful generative models—remains unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular dataset with missing entries and a binary mask vector indicating which positions are missing; discrete features are one-hot encoded and all columns are standardized to zero mean and unit variance before processing. The output is the imputed missing values computed as the Monte Carlo average of N=10 samples drawn from the learned conditional distribution p_θ(x|x^obs), yielding a complete data row. The framework supports both in-sample imputation (refined iteratively during training) and out-of-sample imputation (single E-step pass on unseen records).

## Method

DIFFPUTER iteratively alternates an M-step and an E-step within an EM framework. In the M-step, a score-based diffusion model (VE-SDE with linear noise schedule σ(t)=t, T=80) is trained via score matching using a 5-layer MLP denoising network to estimate the joint density p_θ(x^obs, x^mis) of the current complete-data estimate. In the E-step, conditional sampling is performed by, at each reverse timestep, replacing the observed entries with their forward-diffused values (preserving the ground-truth observation) while retaining the reverse-denoised values for missing entries, then averaging N samples to obtain the Expected A Posteriori estimate. The paper proves that the M-step corresponds to maximum likelihood estimation of data density and the E-step to EAP estimation of missing values, and that the mixed forward/reverse sampling exactly recovers samples from p_θ(x|x^obs). The full EM loop converges in 4–5 iterations, with the final imputation taken from the last E-step.

## Results

On in-sample MCAR imputation (30% missing rate), DIFFPUTER achieves an average 6.94% improvement in MAE and 4.78% in RMSE over the strongest baseline across nine tabular datasets. On discrete columns, DIFFPUTER ranks first among 19 methods with 62.82% average accuracy (vs. Remasker at 62.06% and GRAPE at 60.46%). For out-of-sample imputation, DIFFPUTER improves MAE by 13.37% and RMSE by 4.43% over the best baseline, while graph-based methods like IGRM fail entirely on unseen data. Training time is comparable to SOTA methods (~1900–2100s on an RTX 4090) while delivering 8–25% performance gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFFPUTER outperforms all 19 baselines on discrete-column accuracy, with the closest competitor being Remasker (62.06% vs. 62.82% average). On continuous columns (MAE/RMSE), DIFFPUTER beats the best baseline by 6.94% and 4.78% on average in-sample, and by 13.37% MAE out-of-sample. The two other diffusion-based methods, MissDiff and TabCSDI, are substantially outperformed (e.g., TabCSDI averages 42.51% accuracy vs. DIFFPUTER's 62.82%), validating the EM+diffusion combination over one-step conditional diffusion. Graph-based methods GRAPE and IGRM are competitive in-sample (GRAPE: 60.46% avg) but degrade severely or fail entirely out-of-sample (IGRM: OOM on all test sets). Traditional iterative methods (EM, MICE, MissForest) and distribution-matching methods (MOT, TDM) remain competitive but trail DIFFPUTER by 5–20 points in accuracy. Combining EM with other deep generative models (MIWAE, HIWAE, VAEM, HH-VAEM) improves their performance but still falls well short of DIFFPUTER (e.g., EM+HH-VAEM: 0.5402 MAE on Adult vs. DIFFPUTER: 0.3425).

## Relation to Prior Work

DIFFPUTER sits at the intersection of three well-developed lines of work: the classical EM algorithm for incomplete data (Dempster et al., 1977), score-based diffusion models via VE-SDE (Song et al., 2021b), and the conditional sampling strategy from image inpainting (Lugmayr et al., 2022). The closest prior work, MCFlow (Richardson et al., 2020), also combined EM with a deep generative model (normalizing flows), but relied on soft regularization rather than exact conditional sampling and did not provide a theoretical correspondence between its steps and MLE/EAP. Unlike TabCSDI and MissDiff, which apply diffusion to imputation in a single pass without iterative refinement of the training data, DIFFPUTER is the first to embed a diffusion model within the full EM loop, using the forward/reverse mixing trick to perform principled conditional sampling from an unconditional diffusion model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded framework (M-step = MLE of data density, E-step = EAP of missing values) that makes the EM-diffusion combination principled rather than heuristic, and demonstrates that this yields consistent improvements across MCAR, MAR, and MNAR settings on both in-sample and out-of-sample imputation. A practical limitation is that the iterative training (4–5 EM iterations × diffusion training + sampling) increases wall-clock time compared to single-pass methods, though the authors show it remains comparable to other SOTA imputers. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional or structured tabular data (e.g., time series with temporal dependencies), whether more expressive denoising architectures (transformers, U-Nets) would further improve performance, and whether the EM framework can be extended to handle covariate shift between training and test missingness patterns.
