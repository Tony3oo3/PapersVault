## Problem

Existing VAE-guided Object-Centric Learning methods encode and decode inputs at a single spatial scale, yet objects in images and videos vary widely in pixel size. Since VAE encoders/decoders perform best within a narrow range of pattern scales, single-scale VAE guidance can only represent objects of certain sizes in high quality, leaving other objects poorly guided during OCL training. No prior work explicitly addresses this multi-scale mismatch in the VAE intermediate representation that drives slot aggregation.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images or video frames at 128×128×3 resolution, which are down-sampled via bilinear interpolation into N=3 spatial scales (128×128, 64×64, 32×32) to form an image pyramid. The VAE operates at 4× spatial downsampling (32×32×256 for transformer-based methods, 32×32×4 for diffusion-based), and the final OCL output consists of sparse object-level feature vectors (slots) plus byproduct segmentation masks. During OCL training, only the augmented scale-1 VAE representation is used as guidance, while the VAE itself is frozen.

## Method

MSF splits each scale's VAE continuous representation into a scale-invariant copy (quantized with a shared codebook across all N scales) and a scale-variant copy (quantized with per-scale codebooks) via an invertible project-up/project-down design. Inter-scale fusion then, for every super-pixel at scale n, gathers the corresponding regions from scales n−1 and n+1, matches all three against the shared codebook, and selects the code with the highest matching probability across scales—effectively voting for the most confident representation to augment low-quality super-pixels. Intra-scale fusion concatenates the fused scale-invariant codes with the scale-variant codes and projects them back to the original channel dimension. This multi-scale quantized fusion is applied during VAE pretraining (with N sets of reconstruction losses), and the resulting augmented discrete representation at scale 1 serves as the frozen guidance signal for OCL training of both transformer-based (SLATE, STEVE) and diffusion-based (SlotDiffusion) methods.

## Results

On the transformer-based SLATE baseline, MSF achieves ARI 32.70 / ARIfg 80.70 / mIoU 40.61 on ClevrTex (vs. 20.56/68.14/34.11 for SLATE alone), and ARI 30.95 / ARIfg 30.47 on COCO. On the diffusion-based SlotDiffusion baseline, MSF reaches ARI 71.47 / ARIfg 38.08 on ClevrTex (vs. 64.21/26.50) and ARI 19.40 / mIoU 16.37 on VOC (vs. 16.97/15.71). For video OCL with STEVE, MSF improves ARI from 52.20 to 60.94 on MOVi-C and from 35.71 to 43.20 on MOVi-D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSF consistently outperforms the SLATE baseline across all three image datasets, with the largest gain on ClevrTex where ARIfg improves by 12.5 points (80.70 vs. 68.14) and mBO by 6.9 points. Against the competing improver SysBinder@2, MSF wins on every metric for every dataset (e.g., ClevrTex ARIfg 80.70 vs. 71.27; COCO ARI 30.95 vs. 25.71). Against GDR@g2, MSF matches or exceeds on SLATE (ClevrTex ARIfg 80.70 vs. 73.38; COCO ARI 30.95 vs. 30.37) and beats it consistently on STEVE for video (MOVi-D ARI 43.20 vs. 40.37; MOVi-E ARI 36.70 vs. 34.17). On the diffusion-based SlotDiffusion, MSF surpasses both GDR@g2 and the foundation-based DINOSAUR on all three datasets (e.g., ClevrTex ARI 71.47 vs. 69.21 for GDR and 60.74 for DINOSAUR; VOC ARI 19.40 vs. 18.20 and 16.00). The only notable exception is VOC for SLATE, where MSF's ARI (12.17) falls slightly below GDR@g2 (13.20), which the authors attribute to very large objects exceeding the coverage of the 3-scale pyramid.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VAE-guided Object-Centric Learning framework (SLATE, STEVE, SlotDiffusion) by introducing multi-scale fusion into the VAE's discrete intermediate representation. It builds directly on VQ-VAE's codebook quantization mechanism and the invertible projection design from GDR (Zhao et al., 2024), but departs from prior multi-scale approaches (FPN, image pyramids) by using codebook matching probabilities as quality signals to selectively augment low-quality super-pixels across scales, rather than indiscriminately mixing features via channel-concat or element-wise-sum. The paper positions itself as the first to realize multi-scale fusion specifically on VAE representations for OCL guidance, distinguishing from VAR (no inter-scale fusion) and SPAE (element-wise averaging without selective fusion).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical limitation in OCL—single-scale VAE guidance cannot simultaneously represent objects of very different sizes well—and provides a general, drop-in improvement applicable to both transformer-based and diffusion-based OCL methods. However, the authors acknowledge that on real-world datasets (COCO, VOC) where objects often span the entire scene, the 3-scale pyramid's coverage is insufficient, limiting gains. Open questions include whether multi-scale fusion can be extended to the primary encoder and Slot Attention stages, or incorporated into VAE design for generative models, and whether adaptive scale selection (beyond fixed N=3) could handle the full range of object sizes in natural scenes.
