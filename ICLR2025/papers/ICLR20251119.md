## Problem

Existing image forgery detection and localization (IFDL) methods suffer from two critical limitations: they operate as black boxes that output only an authenticity score without explaining the reasoning behind the judgment, and they generalize poorly across diverse tampering types (Photoshop copy-move/splicing/removal, DeepFake, AIGC-Editing), requiring users to pre-select a detector matched to the suspected manipulation. This paper formalizes the explainable IFDL (e-IFDL) task, which demands that a model simultaneously detect tampering, localize the manipulated region, and articulate a pixel-level and image-level rationale for its decision.

## Input / Output

The model receives a single RGB image and a free-form text instruction (e.g., 'Can you identify manipulated areas in the photograph?') and produces three outputs: a binary authenticity verdict, a structured textual explanation organized into detection result, tampered-area location description (using both absolute and relative spatial references), and a judgment basis citing specific artifacts (edge discontinuities, resolution mismatches, lighting inconsistencies, perspective violations, physical-law violations), and a pixel-level binary mask delineating the tampered region. The explanation is generated autoregressively by the LLM, while the mask is produced by a SAM decoder prompted by a learned <SEG> token embedding.

## Method

FakeShield decouples detection/explanation from localization into two separately trained modules to avoid task interference. The Domain Tag-guided Explainable Forgery Detection Module (DTE-FDM) first classifies the image into a tampering domain (Photoshop, DeepFake, or AIGC) via a lightweight classifier, then concatenates the domain tag, image tokens (from a ViT encoder + linear projection), and the instruction into a LoRA-fine-tuned LLaVA-13B LLM that autoregressively generates the detection verdict and structured explanation. The Multi-modal Forgery Localization Module (MFLM) takes the generated explanation and image tokens, processes them through a Tamper Comprehension Module (an LLM encoder with a <SEG> token) to produce a prompt embedding, which is fed to a LoRA-fine-tuned SAM decoder to output the tampered-region mask. Training data is the MMTD-Set, constructed by prompting GPT-4o with tamper-type-specific instructions to generate 'image–mask–description' triplets from existing IFDL benchmarks, enabling the model to learn domain-aware forensic reasoning.

## Results

FakeShield achieves the best detection accuracy across all seven test domains in Table 1, reaching 0.95 ACC / 0.95 F1 on CASIA1+, 0.98/0.99 on Columbia, 0.97/0.98 on DSO, 0.93/0.93 on DeepFake, and 0.98/0.99 on AIGC-Editing. On the IMD2020 splicing benchmark, it outperforms the second-best method (ManTraNet, 0.75 ACC) by 0.08 ACC and 0.05 F1. For tamper localization (Table 4), FakeShield leads on most datasets, e.g., 0.54 IoU / 0.60 F1 on CASIA1+ versus 0.47/0.51 for OSN, and 0.67/0.75 on Columbia versus 0.64/0.74 for PSCC-Net. In explanation quality (Table 3), FakeShield attains CSS scores of 0.75–0.89, substantially exceeding the best M-LLM baseline (InternVL2-26B at 0.58–0.68) and GPT-4o (0.52–0.63).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FakeShield outperforms all six traditional IFDL baselines (SPAN, ManTraNet, HiFi-Net, PSCC-Net, CAT-Net, MVSS-Net) on every detection dataset, with the largest margins on AIGC-Editing (0.98 vs. 0.82 for CAT-Net) and DeepFake (0.93 vs. 0.85 for CAT-Net), where most baselines drop below 0.60. On localization, FakeShield leads on 6 of 8 PhotoShop benchmarks (e.g., +0.07 IoU over OSN on CASIA1+, +0.12 IoU over OSN on IMD2020) but trails slightly on Korus (0.17 vs. 0.15 IoU for PSCC-Net) and DeepFake (0.14 vs. 0.10 for MVSS-Net). Against dedicated DeepFake detectors (CADDM, HiFi-DeepFake, Exposing, RECCE), FakeShield achieves 0.98 ACC on DFFD and 0.84 on Seq-DeepFake, surpassing the best specialist (RECCE at 0.92/0.75). In explanation quality, FakeShield's CSS scores (0.75–0.89) exceed GPT-4o (0.52–0.63), LLaVA-v1.6-34B (0.50–0.65), InternVL2-26B (0.58–0.68), and Qwen2-VL-7B (0.49–0.64) by 0.15–0.30 across all domains, confirming that domain-specific fine-tuning on MMTD-Set yields substantially more accurate tampering descriptions than general-purpose M-LLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IFDL lineage (MVSS-Net, OSN, HiFi-Net, IML-ViT, DiffForensics) by replacing the black-box CNN/ViT pipeline with a decoupled M-LLM architecture: a LLaVA-based detection/explanation module (DTE-FDM) and a LISA-inspired SAM-based localization module (MFLM). It departs from prior M-LLM forensics work (FFAA, DD-VQA) that was restricted to DeepFake face analysis by introducing a Domain Tag Generator to unify Photoshop, DeepFake, and AIGC-Editing domains in a single model, and by adding precise pixel-level mask output via a Tamper Comprehension Module. The MMTD-Set dataset, constructed with GPT-4o-generated 'image-mask-description' triplets, is a new contribution that bridges the gap between existing visual-only IFDL benchmarks and the multimodal training requirements of M-LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FakeShield addresses a practical gap in digital forensics: prior IFDL tools provide a binary verdict and a mask but no human-interpretable rationale, forcing analysts to re-examine images manually. The explainable output (lighting inconsistencies, edge artifacts, physical-law violations) makes the system usable in legal and journalistic contexts where 'why' matters as much as 'whether.' However, localization IoU remains modest (0.14–0.67) compared to the strong detection accuracy (0.93–0.98), and the authors acknowledge failure on complex DeepFake types (identity switching, full-face generation). Open questions include whether the GPT-4o-dependent dataset construction introduces systematic biases, whether the approach scales to video or 3D content, and whether the domain tag mechanism generalizes to truly unseen tampering families beyond the three categories used in training.
