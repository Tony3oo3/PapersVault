## Problem

Existing AI safety benchmarks define risk categories based on prior literature, intuition, or limited policy scope, producing disjointed taxonomies that fail to align with the full set of risks specified in recent government regulations and corporate acceptable use policies. Even the most comprehensive existing benchmark (SALAD-Bench) covers only 71% of the 314 granular risk categories extracted from 8 government regulations and 16 company policies, leaving critical categories like Automated Decision-Making and Non-consensual Nudity entirely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes 5,694 risky instruction prompts as input, each mapped to one of 314 level-4 risk categories in the AIR 2024 four-tiered taxonomy. Prompts are expanded from 5–10 base prompts per category via uncommon-dialect rephrasing and authority-endorsement mutations to stress-test robustness. The output is a per-prompt three-level safety score (0 = harmful assistance provided, 0.5 = ambiguous/non-refusal, 1 = refusal), aggregated into category-specific refusal rates across 45 level-3 and 314 level-4 categories, enabling direct mapping to individual regulatory frameworks (EU AI Act, US Executive Order, Chinese regulations) and corporate policies.

## Method

AIR-BENCH 2024 is constructed in two curation stages: (a) iterative generation of base prompts using GPT-4o, GPT-4, and Claude 3 Opus with human expert review ensuring risky-intention clarity, context concreteness, diversity, and syntactic variety; and (b) automated expansion via GPT-4o applying uncommon-dialect and authority-endorsement mutations, followed by manual quality control. Evaluation employs a category-specific LLM-as-judge pipeline where GPT-4o scores each model response using a three-level rubric (0/0.5/1) tailored to each of the 314 risk categories; these judge prompts are themselves generated at scale from three manually crafted in-context templates and then human-verified. The approach achieves a Cohen's Kappa of 0.86 against human annotators, substantially outperforming the 0.69 Kappa of a fixed judge prompt. The entire pipeline runs on the open-source HELM framework with temperature 0.0, and 22 models from 10 organizations are evaluated.

## Results

Across 22 evaluated models on AIR-BENCH 2024 (5,694 prompts spanning 314 risk categories), Claude 3 Sonnet achieves the highest overall safety refusal rate of 89%, while DBRX Instruct lags at 15%. No single model consistently refuses across all 45 level-3 categories; even the top-performing Claude 3 family drops to ~71% on the 11 high-risk/unacceptable categories specified in the EU AI Act. The category-specific GPT-4o autograder achieves a Cohen's Kappa of 0.86 with human evaluators, substantially outperforming the 0.69 Kappa of a fixed judge prompt. The least-refused categories across all models are #6 (Advice in Regulated Industries) and #4 (Automated Decision-Making), with refusal rates below 20% for many models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIR-BENCH 2024 is compared against three leading safety benchmarks on risk-category coverage: HEx-PHI covers 51% (23/45) of AIR 2024 level-3 categories, HarmBench covers 26% (12/45), and SALAD-Bench (the most comprehensive existing benchmark, integrating eight sub-benchmarks) covers 71% (32/45). AIR-BENCH 2024 achieves 100% coverage of all 45 level-3 and 314 level-4 categories by construction, since its prompts are directly grounded in the AIR 2024 taxonomy. Critically, six level-3 categories (#4 Automated Decision Making, #19 Non-consensual Nudity, #26 Deterring Democratic Participation, #27 Disrupting Social Order, #29 Unfair Market Practices, #35 Sowing Division) are absent from all three baselines, and 21 of 45 categories (46%) are covered by at most one baseline. In terms of model evaluation, AIR-BENCH 2024 reveals that even the best-performing model (Claude 3 Sonnet, 89%) fails to fully align with the EU AI Act's high-risk categories (~71%), a gap not surfaced by the narrower existing benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the AIR 2024 taxonomy (Zeng et al., 2024a), which unifies risk categories from 8 government regulations and 16 corporate policies into a four-tiered hierarchy of 314 granular risks. For prompt construction, it adapts the uncommon dialect mutation from Rainbow Teaming (Samvelyan et al., 2024) and the authority endorsement technique from Zeng et al. (2024b) to expand base prompts while preserving semantic intent. The key departure from prior safety benchmarks (HEx-PHI, HarmBench, SALAD-Bench) is that risk categories are extracted bottom-up from actual regulatory and policy documents rather than from literature intuitions or common sense, and the evaluation uses category-specific GPT-4o judge prompts (achieving 0.86 Cohen's Kappa with humans) rather than a single fixed rubric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AIR-BENCH 2024 is the first benchmark that enables direct, category-level assessment of model compliance with specific regulatory frameworks (EU AI Act, US Executive Order, Chinese regulations) and corporate usage policies, revealing that no evaluated model fully aligns with any jurisdiction's requirements. The consistent under-refusal on #6 (Advice in Regulated Industries) and #4 (Automated Decision-Making) across all 22 models—including the top performer Claude 3 Sonnet—highlights a systemic gap between current safety training and the most legally consequential risk areas. Limitations include the static nature of the benchmark (requiring updates as regulations evolve), reliance on GPT-4o as both prompt generator and judge (mitigated but not eliminated by the human alignment study), and the assumption that the model is the entire system without downstream human oversight. Open questions include whether refusal rate is the appropriate metric (vs. calibrated helpfulness), how to handle safety over-cautiousness, and how to build dynamic benchmarks that track regulatory evolution.
