## Problem

Small LLMs (7B–8B) struggle with complex mathematical reasoning because they cannot reliably identify and correct errors in their own reasoning steps, even when reflection-based methods like Reflexion are applied. Existing fine-tuning and DPO approaches optimize at the instance level, failing to target the specific flawed reasoning step, and self-correction within a single model is fundamentally limited by the model's own knowledge bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250123/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a mathematical problem as text input and produces a structured XML-formatted reasoning chain with per-step tags (<Step>), detailed annotations for tricky steps (<Key>), a high-level generalized solution summary (<Generalized>), and a final answer (<Answer>). In the self-correction phase, the output additionally includes error cause analysis (<Cause>) and a corrected step (<Correction>) for the first erroneous reasoning step, enabling step-level rather than instance-level correction.

## Method

SUPERCORRECT is a two-stage teacher-student framework. Stage 1 (HSFT) extracts hierarchical thought templates from SOTA teacher LLMs (o1-mini/GPT-4o) that combine a high-level generalized solution with detailed step-by-step reasoning including <Key> annotations for critical steps, then fine-tunes the student on 100k such samples to internalize fine-grained reasoning structure. Stage 2 (Cross-model DPO) collects the student's erroneous solutions, has the teacher LLM locate the first error step and generate a grounded correction trace (using ground-truth context), while the student generates its own self-correction; a modified DPO objective (Eq. 8) then optimizes the student to prefer the teacher's cross-model correction over its own self-correction at the step level. The key insight is that cross-model correction breaks the student's thought bottleneck by injecting error-driven insights and new knowledge it could not generate independently, rather than merely imitating correct solutions.

## Results

SUPERCORRECT-Qwen-7B achieves 70.2% on MATH and 89.5% on GSM8K, setting new SOTA among all 7B models. This represents a +15.1% improvement over Qwen2.5-Math-7B (55.1%) and +7.8% over DeepSeekMath-7B (46.8%) on MATH, and +6.3% and +5.3% respectively on GSM8K. The method also outperforms the much larger Llama-3-70B-Instruct (50.4% MATH) by 19.8 points, though it remains below GPT-4o-0806 (76.6% MATH, 95.8% GSM8K) and Claude-3-Sonnet (71.1% MATH, 96.4% GSM8K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUPERCORRECT-Qwen-7B (70.2% MATH, 89.5% GSM8K) decisively outperforms all 7B open-source math models: Qwen2.5-Math-7B (55.1%/83.2%), DeepSeekMath-7B (46.8%/82.9%), and Meta-Llama3.1-8B (51.9%/84.5%), with the largest margin being +15.1% over Qwen2.5-Math-7B on MATH. It also surpasses the 70B Llama-3-70B-Instruct (50.4% MATH) by 19.8 points, though it trails on GSM8K (89.5% vs. 93.0%). Against frontier closed-source models, SUPERCORRECT-Qwen-7B remains below GPT-4o-0806 (76.6% MATH), GPT-4-Turbo (73.4%), and Claude-3-Sonnet (71.1%) on MATH, and below all of them on GSM8K. In the ablation (Table 2), Cross-DPO adds +7.1% over HSFT alone on MATH, while Reflexion-based self-correction adds only +0.7%, confirming that cross-model correction traces are the primary driver of the self-correction gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two distinct lines of work: (1) the thought-template paradigm from BoT (Yang et al., 2024b), which uses high-level reasoning guidelines in a training-free manner, is repurposed here as a hierarchical (high-level + detailed) template that is distilled into small models via SFT; and (2) DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) is modified from instance-level preference optimization to a step-level cross-model correction objective where the 'preferred' response is a teacher LLM's error-cause analysis and correction of a student's flawed reasoning step. The key conceptual departure from prior self-correction methods (Reflexion, RCI) and step-level DPO (Step-DPO) is the cross-model paradigm: rather than asking a model to find and fix its own errors, a stronger teacher model identifies the specific error step and provides grounded correction traces that are then used as DPO preference pairs to train the student's self-correction ability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for practitioners working with 7B-class models: the method achieves 70.2% on MATH, surpassing all prior 7B models by a wide margin and approaching GPT-4o-level performance. However, the approach depends on access to SOTA teacher models (o1-mini, GPT-4o) for both template extraction and correction trace generation, which introduces substantial API cost and limits reproducibility. Open questions include whether the cross-model DPO paradigm generalizes beyond mathematics to other reasoning domains, whether it scales to larger student models where the teacher-student gap narrows, and whether the hierarchical thought format adds value beyond what simpler structured CoT provides at scale.
