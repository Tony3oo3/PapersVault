key_contribution: Oryx is a unified multimodal LLM that processes images, videos, and multi-view 3D scenes at arbitrary spatial resolutions and temporal lengths via two architectural innovations—OryxViT (a SigLIP-based ViT with bilinearly-interpolated position embeddings and variable-length self-attention for native-resolution encoding) and a Dynamic Compressor (cross-attention-based downsampling supporting 1×–16× on-demand token compression with a shared MLP projector)—achieving state-of-the-art among open-source models on video, long-video, image, and 3D benchmarks with a 32B variant that surpasses 72B-parameter competitors.
limitations: The model still fails at precise temporal grounding (e.g., identifying exact timestamps in videos) due to uniform frame sampling losing inter-frame temporal context, and it lacks sufficient domain-specific knowledge for reasoning tasks that require integrating external knowledge (e.g., chemical reactions) into video understanding. The authors suggest incorporating timestamps directly into video inputs or processing without frame sampling, and expanding the knowledge base of MLLMs, as future directions.

surprise_factor: The ablation showing SigLIP collapsing to 17.1 on DocVQA at native resolution (vs. 74.8 with dynamic partitioning) is a striking validation that off-the-shelf contrastive encoders are fundamentally incompatible with variable-size inputs, and the finding that parameter-free average pooling outperforms learned DWConv/Conv-MLP downsampling within the compressor is a mild negative result for more complex alternatives. Overall, the SOTA improvements are solid but follow from a well-motivated architectural combination rather than a single unexpected insight.

## Problem

Existing multi-modal LLMs standardize all visual inputs—ranging from small icons to hours-long videos—into fixed-resolution patches, producing a uniform number of tokens regardless of content complexity. This one-size-fits-all approach causes information loss for detail-sensitive tasks (e.g., document OCR) while wasting compute on simple or extremely long inputs, and no prior open-source MLLM supports truly arbitrary spatial resolution combined with on-demand temporal compression in a single unified architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253078/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at their native aspect ratio and resolution (capped at 1536² pixels), video clips with frames normalized to 288²–480² pixels (up to 64 frames for standard videos, 256 for long-form), and multi-view 3D scene images, all interleaved with text prompts. The compression ratio is selected per input type: 1× (no compression) for images, 2×2 average pooling for short videos and 3D frames, and 4×4 pooling for extremely long needle-in-a-haystack retrieval, yielding a unified token format fed to the LLM. Outputs are free-form text responses including video summaries, detailed frame-level captions, multiple-choice answers, and 3D spatial reasoning.

## Method

Oryx introduces two architectural innovations. First, OryxViT is a SigLIP-based Vision Transformer modified with a large position embedding matrix that is bilinearly interpolated to match each input's native patch grid (⌊H/p⌋×⌊W/p⌋), combined with variable-length self-attention via FlashAttention to batch-process images of differing sizes without padding. Second, a Dynamic Compressor applies ratio-specific downsample layers (1×, 2×, 4× average pooling) followed by a cross-attention step where low-resolution patches attend to their r² high-resolution neighbors, then projects all compressed features through a single shared MLP into the LLM embedding space, preserving distributional consistency across modalities. Training follows a lightweight two-stage supervised pipeline: Stage 1 aligns the connector on 4M image-text pairs, and Stage 2 jointly fine-tunes on ~1.2M samples mixing images, videos (including MovieNet-derived needle-in-a-haystack data), and 3D frames annotated with Track-Anything coarse correspondences to elicit spatial awareness.

## Results

On general video understanding, Oryx-1.5-32B achieves 74.9% on VideoMME (with subtitles), 85.0% on NextQA, 70.1% on MVBench, and 74.0% on Perception Test, setting new state-of-the-art among open-source models. On long-form video, Oryx-1.5-32B reaches 72.3% on MLVU and 62.0% on LongVideoBench, surpassing GPT-4o by 7.7% on MLVU. For image understanding, Oryx-1.5-32B scores 86.3% on MMBench, 92.7% on DocVQA, and 746 on OCRBench. On 3D spatial understanding (ScanQA), Oryx-1.5-32B achieves CIDEr 74.3 and ROUGE-L 38.4, outperforming the dedicated 3D-LLM baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Oryx-1.5-32B (32B params) outperforms LLaVA-OneVision-72B (72B params) on every reported general video benchmark: VideoMME with subtitles (74.9 vs. 69.5), NextQA (85.0 vs. 80.2), MVBench (70.1 vs. 59.4), and Perception Test (74.0 vs. 66.9), demonstrating that the on-demand architecture yields gains beyond mere parameter scaling. At the 7B scale, Oryx-1.5-7B surpasses LLaVA-OneVision-7B by 2.7 points on VideoMME (with subs), 2.4 on NextQA, 10.9 on MVBench, and 20.3 on Perception Test. Against proprietary models, Oryx-1.5-32B beats GPT-4V on VideoMME (74.9 vs. 63.3 with subs) and MVBench (70.1 vs. 43.7), but trails Gemini-1.5-Pro on VideoMME (74.9 vs. 81.3) and GPT-4o on MMBench-Video (1.52 vs. 1.63). On long-form video, Oryx-1.5-32B exceeds GPT-4o on MLVU by 7.7 points (72.3 vs. 64.6) and matches LLaVA-OneVision-72B on LongVideoBench (62.0 vs. 61.3). For 3D understanding on ScanQA, Oryx-1.5-32B (CIDEr 74.3, ROUGE-L 38.4) outperforms the dedicated 3D-LLM (CIDEr 69.4, ROUGE-L 35.7) and LLaVA-OneVision-72B (ROUGE-L 35.8), validating the coarse-correspondence training strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the native-resolution vision encoder line (NaViT, FlexiViT) into the MLLM setting, where prior work has predominantly relied on fixed-resolution CLIP/SigLIP encoders or dynamic partitioning tricks (LLaVA-NeXT, LLaVA-UHD, MiniCPM-V) to approximate high-resolution perception. It builds directly on SigLIP as the visual backbone and on the coarse-correspondence framework of Liu et al. (2024a) for 3D spatial understanding, but departs from both by introducing a purpose-built OryxViT with bilinearly-interpolated position embeddings and a Dynamic Compressor that applies cross-attention-based downsampling at 1×–16× ratios on demand. The unification of image, video, and multi-view 3D inputs under a single variable-length token stream with a shared MLP projector represents a conceptual departure from prior MLLMs that handle these modalities through separate pipelines or fixed token budgets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The on-demand resolution-and-compression paradigm offers a practical path to handling the full spectrum of visual inputs—from a few-pixel icon to a 45-minute movie—within one model without the information loss of forced resizing or the token explosion of uniform high-resolution encoding. The 32B variant surpassing 72B-parameter competitors on long-video benchmarks (MLVU, LongVideoBench) and outperforming GPT-4o on MLVU by 7.7% suggests that architectural efficiency in visual token management can partially substitute for raw LLM scale. Open questions remain around temporal grounding (the model still fails at precise timestamp identification), knowledge transfer to domain-specific reasoning, and whether the dynamic compression ratios can be further extended beyond 16× without degrading retrieval fidelity.
