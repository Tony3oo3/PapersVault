## Problem

VLMs such as GPT-4V struggle to accurately extract fine visual details from scientific diagrams, failing on tasks like reading exact bar values or identifying small annotations. Existing segmentation-based prompting approaches (e.g., SAM, Set-of-Mark) generalize poorly to the structured, homogeneous-color nature of scientific diagrams and cannot recover hidden geometric parameters like rotation angles or line slopes. The paper asks how regions can be systematically decomposed and their visual details collated to augment VLM inference for diagram analysis.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB scientific diagram image and a natural-language text query. Internally, the image is binarized via Otsu's thresholding and processed through connected-components labeling, so the effective input to the CV pipeline is a binary mask rather than raw pixels. The final output is a VLM-generated textual answer, but the intermediate artifacts include per-region binary masks, structured shape parameters (center coordinates, width, length, angle, start/end points), and short semantic descriptions that are concatenated into an augmented prompt appended to the original query.

## Method

Chain-of-Region (CoR) is a plug-and-play, training-free pipeline that decomposes a diagram into regions using traditional OpenCV operators and feeds the extracted metadata to any pre-trained VLM. Region identification proceeds in three stages: (1) initialization via cv2.connectedComponents on both the Otsu-thresholded foreground and its inverse background to capture all visual elements; (2) splitting, where a VLM-assisted structure-recognition call (limited to ~10 calls per diagram) triggers specialized detectors for ellipses, line segments, rectangles, and arrows, while unstructured shapes are separated by a morphological opening whose kernel size is derived from the distance transform (a 'high-tension liquid' analogy); and (3) merging, which groups regions via OCR caption boxes, heuristic rules for dotted and background lines, and AgglomerativeClustering on centroid distances to respect a fixed region budget (default 5). In the information-collection phase, each merged region is highlighted in the image and queried by the VLM for a short semantic description, then all shape and semantic metadata are aggregated into a single augmented prompt that the VLM uses to produce the final answer.

## Results

On the MMMU scientific-diagram subset (5,210 images), Chain-of-Region (CoR) achieves 52.9% average accuracy with GPT-4o, 53.5% with GPT-4-Turbo, and 45.4% with GPT-4o-mini, consistently outperforming all baselines. In the segmentation evaluation (Table 2), CoR attains 63.0% mIoU (individual) and 41.8% mIoU (group), surpassing SAM2 by 20.8 and 13.0 percentage points respectively. The largest single-category gain is in Physics with GPT-4o, where CoR reaches 72.3% versus 64.4% for SAM2 and 51.6% for the raw model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoR outperforms all four baselines across every backbone model. Against SAM2 (the strongest baseline), CoR gains +3.9 pp (GPT-4o-mini), +4.8 pp (GPT-4-Turbo), and +5.9 pp (GPT-4o) in average accuracy, attributed to superior region masks that better isolate fine lines and small annotations. Zero-shot CoT and Few-shot CoT provide modest improvements over the raw VLM (1–3 pp) but remain well below CoR, confirming that textual prompting alone cannot compensate for missing visual detail. SoM (Set-of-Mark) consistently underperforms the raw VLM on GPT-4o-mini and GPT-4-Turbo (34.4% and 36.0% vs. 37.6% and 43.3%), because its SAM2-derived tags are poorly suited to structured scientific diagrams and can mislead the model. In the segmentation ablation (Table 2), CoR's mIoU advantage over SAM2 is largest in Chemistry (74.3 vs. 47.7 individual) and Materials (62.2 vs. 22.7 individual), highlighting CoR's strength on diagrams with dense small elements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes traditional computer vision primitives (Otsu thresholding, connected components, morphological opening, LSD line detection) as a white-box region decomposition pipeline that feeds structured metadata into VLM prompts, departing from the dominant trend of relying on learned segmentation (SAM/SAM2) or learned spatial encoders. Conceptually, it extends the iterative prompting lineage of Tree-of-Thoughts and Set-of-Mark prompting by replacing their learned segmentation backbone with rule-based CV operations, justified by the homogeneous-color, structured-pattern nature of human-made scientific diagrams. The VLM-assisted structure recognition step (asking GPT-4V to name shapes in a region before triggering the corresponding detector) is a lightweight hybrid that bridges the gap between open-vocabulary understanding and closed-set geometric parameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that for the specific domain of scientific diagrams, simple traditional CV operations can substantially outperform state-of-the-art learned segmentation (SAM2) in both mIoU and downstream QA accuracy, offering a CPU-only, interpretable, and plug-and-play alternative that avoids the generalization failures of DL segmenters on out-of-distribution structured inputs. Key limitations include a non-exhaustive structured detector library (only ellipses, segments, rectangles, arrows), dependence on the homogeneous-color assumption that may not hold for all diagram styles, and a segmentation evaluation on a small custom set (~100 samples). Open questions include dynamically selecting the cluster budget per diagram, expanding the structure library to cover more geometric primitives, and whether the approach generalizes to non-diagram structured visuals such as circuit schematics or architectural blueprints.
