## Problem

Diffusion-based representation learning with auxiliary encoders (e.g., DiffAE, PDAE) suffers from an information split problem: the information of each data instance is divided between the diffusion endpoint x_T and the encoded latent z because two separate inference paths exist. This means z alone is insufficient for faithful reconstruction, and recovering the missing information requires solving an ODE to obtain x_T, which is computationally expensive and has inflexible dimensionality.

## Input / Output

The model takes 256×256 RGB face images (CelebA, FFHQ) and produces a 512-dimensional latent vector z via a half-U-Net encoder. For reconstruction, z is decoded through a CNN-based upsampler to obtain x_T, which is then denoised via a reverse ODE to recover x_0. For unconditional generation, z is sampled from a learned prior p_ω(z) (a shallow latent diffusion model) rather than inferred from data. The score network is a U-Net that receives x_t and x_T as concatenated channel inputs, distinguishing it from standard diffusion score networks.

## Method

DBAE resolves the information split problem by making the diffusion endpoint x_T a deterministic (or stochastic) function of z through a feed-forward decoder, creating an information bottleneck that forces z to carry all data information. A learnable forward SDE is defined via Doob's h-transform, connecting x_0 to the z-dependent endpoint x_T, so that the conditional distribution q_t(x_t | x_0, x_T) is tractable (Gaussian for linear SDEs). Training minimizes an entropy-regularized score-matching objective L_AE = L_SM + H(q(x_0|x_T)), which Theorem 1 shows is equivalent to a denoising reconstruction loss for linear SDEs, and Theorem 2 proves it upper-bounds the negative mutual information between x_0 and z. For unconditional generation, a separate prior objective L_PR aligns the generative endpoint distribution p_ψ(x_T) with the aggregated inferred distribution, trained with a shallow latent diffusion model over z. Unlike DiffAE/PDAE, no ODE solving is needed to infer x_T, enabling efficient interpolation and attribute manipulation directly in the z-space.

## Results

On CelebA, DBAE achieves the best linear-probe attribute prediction with AP 0.655, Pearson's r 0.643, and MSE 0.369, outperforming the strongest baseline DiTi (AP 0.623, r 0.617, MSE 0.392). On FFHQ, DBAE reaches AP 0.664, Pearson's r 0.675, and MSE 0.332, again leading all baselines. For reconstruction with a tractable 512-dim latent, DBAE-d achieves SSIM 0.953 and LPIPS 0.072, substantially surpassing DiffAE (SSIM 0.677) and PDAE (SSIM 0.689) under the same random-x_T setting. In unconditional generation on FFHQ, DBAE+AE attains FID 1.77, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DBAE outperforms all diffusion-based representation learning baselines (DiffAE, PDAE, DiTi) on every downstream inference metric across both CelebA and FFHQ, with the largest gains over DiTi (the strongest prior method) of +0.032 AP and +0.053 Pearson's r on FFHQ. The information split problem is directly evidenced by reconstruction: DiffAE and PDAE with random x_T achieve SSIM ~0.68, while DBAE-d reaches 0.953, confirming that z in DBAE captures the full data information. For disentanglement, DBAE+TC achieves TAD 0.362 and ATTRS 3.8, surpassing InfoDiffusion (TAD 0.299, ATTRS 3.0) and DisDiff (TAD 0.305), while also improving FID from 22.3 to 13.4. In unconditional generation on FFHQ, DBAE+AE achieves FID 1.77 versus DiffAE+AE's 2.84 and PDAE+AE's 7.42, though DBAE without the AE prior shows slightly worse FID (11.25) than DDIM (11.27) and DDPM (9.14) in terms of diversity-sensitive metrics. The stochastic encoder (DBAE) slightly outperforms the deterministic variant (DBAE-d) on downstream inference but underperforms on reconstruction, suggesting a trade-off between latent space coverage and reconstruction fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the auxiliary encoder framework introduced by DiffAE (Preechakul et al., 2022) and PDAE (Zhang et al., 2022) for diffusion-based representation learning, but identifies and resolves a fundamental flaw it calls the 'information split problem': in prior methods, data information is divided between the tractable latent z and the diffusion endpoint x_T, which must be obtained by expensive ODE solving. The key departure is using Doob's h-transform (from the diffusion bridge literature, Zhou et al., 2024) to define a learnable forward SDE whose endpoint x_T is produced by a decoder conditioned on z, thereby making z an information bottleneck. The score-matching objective and continuous-time SDE formulation build directly on Song et al. (2021c), while the bridge-model machinery (Fokker-Planck equations, conditional score matching) is adapted from Zhou et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making x_T a deterministic function of z, DBAE eliminates the need for ODE solving during inference, enabling efficient interpolation and attribute manipulation that were previously impractical. The information bottleneck design yields consistent improvements in downstream inference, reconstruction, and disentanglement over DiffAE, PDAE, and DiTi. However, unconditional generation on FFHQ reveals a sensitivity to the gap between the inferred distribution q_φ(z) and the learned generative prior p_ω(z) (FID 11.25 vs. 1.77 when using the inferred distribution), suggesting that a more powerful generative prior is needed to fully close the diversity gap. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or non-face datasets, and whether the h-transformed forward process can be made fully learnable rather than relying on a fixed base SDE.
