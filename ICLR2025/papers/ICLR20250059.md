## Problem

State-of-the-art self-supervised representation learning methods (SimCLR, MAE) implicitly assume that input data has known stationarity and regular topology (e.g., pixel grids in images, temporal order in sequences). This assumption fails for biological signals (irregular retinal sampling, V1 neural recordings) and many scientific datasets (gene expression profiles) where no explicit spatial or temporal structure is known. The paper asks whether high-quality unsupervised representations can be learned from arbitrary high-dimensional signals without any prior knowledge of their domain topology or statistical stationarity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250059/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of high-dimensional vectors (e.g., 1038-dimensional foveated retinal signals, ~10,000-dimensional V1 neural firing rates, or 1773-dimensional miRNA expression profiles) where dimensions have no known ordering or spatial arrangement. A one-time pre-processing step computes pairwise mutual information across all dimensions to build an affinity graph, then performs density-adjusted spectral clustering to partition dimensions into K clusters (e.g., 64 for images, 200 for V1, 32 for TCGA). The output is the encoder's latent representation, evaluated via linear probing on downstream classification or decoding tasks.

## Method

URLOST is a three-stage pipeline: (1) Density-adjusted spectral clustering groups dimensions by pairwise mutual information, using a density adjustment matrix P = diag(q(i)^α · n(i)^−β) to balance information content across clusters, recovering a coarse topology of the signal domain; (2) A self-organizing layer applies a distinct learnable linear projection W^(i) to each cluster (rather than a shared projection), jointly optimized with the backbone, so that inner products between cluster representations reflect true similarity despite arbitrary internal ordering; (3) A ViT-based masked autoencoder randomly masks clusters and reconstructs them via MSE loss, with the encoder output serving as the learned representation. The key architectural difference from standard MAE is that fixed image patches are replaced by data-driven, irregularly-shaped clusters, and the shared patch embedding is replaced by per-cluster non-shared projections that can autonomously 'undo' arbitrary permutations within each cluster.

## Results

On Permuted CIFAR-10 (no topology), URLOST achieves 86.4% linear probing accuracy, dramatically outperforming MAE (56.7%) and SimCLR (47.9% ResNet-18, 40.0% ViG). On Foveated CIFAR-10 (no topology or stationarity), URLOST reaches 85.4% while all baselines collapse to 38–48.5%. On the V1 neural response decoding task, URLOST achieves 78.75% accuracy versus 75.64% for β-VAE and 70.6% for MAE; on TCGA pan-cancer classification, URLOST reaches 94.90% versus 94.15% for β-VAE and 90.6% for MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MAE (He et al., CVPR 2022), URLOST wins by a large margin whenever topology or stationarity is absent: 86.4% vs 56.7% on Permuted CIFAR-10, 85.4% vs 48.5% on Foveated CIFAR-10, 78.75% vs 70.6% on V1 decoding, and 94.90% vs 90.6% on TCGA. Against SimCLR (Chen et al., ICML 2020), the gap is even more extreme—URLOST scores 86.4% and 85.4% on the two synthetic vision datasets while SimCLR drops to 47.9%/40.0% and 38.0%/42.8% respectively, because contrastive learning relies on stationarity to construct positive pairs. Against β-VAE (Higgins et al., ICLR 2016), URLOST achieves a consistent but smaller advantage: +3.11 points on V1 decoding (78.75% vs 75.64%) and +0.75 points on TCGA (94.90% vs 94.15%), confirming that the spectral clustering and self-organizing components add value even on high-dimensional vector data where no explicit topology exists.

## Relation to Prior Work

URLOST builds on the masked autoencoder framework (He et al., 2022) as its core learning objective, but replaces the assumption of regular image patches with clusters recovered via density-adjusted spectral clustering (extending Ng et al., 2001) guided by pairwise mutual information, an idea inspired by Roux et al. (2007) on recovering 2D image topology. The self-organizing layer draws conceptual parallels to Kohonen's self-organizing maps and the Perceiver architecture's implicit dimension aggregation, but differs by explicitly learning per-cluster alignment transformations jointly with the encoder. The paper positions itself against both the SSL line (SimCLR, MAE) that assumes stationarity and regular topology, and the geometric deep learning / GNN line (e.g., Vision GNN) that handles irregular topology but struggles to scale with self-supervised objectives on large data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a path toward unsupervised representation learning for the growing class of high-dimensional scientific signals (neural recordings, gene expression, irregular sensor data) where no natural grid topology or stationarity is known, a setting that biological systems handle routinely but current ML methods do not. Key limitations include the quadratic scaling of mutual information computation (making d > 10,000 dimensions impractical with current implementation), the non-end-to-end nature of the clustering step, and evaluation on datasets that are small by computer-vision standards. Open directions flagged by the authors include end-to-end cluster learning, GPU-accelerated mutual information, adapting the framework to contrastive objectives, and testing on larger natural-science datasets as imaging technology advances.
