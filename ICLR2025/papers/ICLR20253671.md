## Problem

Despite the rapid progress of text-to-video generative models, it remains unclear how well they adhere to the laws of physics when simulating real-world interactions. Existing video benchmarks (e.g., VBench, EvalCrafter) evaluate quality metrics like motion smoothness and background consistency but do not specifically assess whether generated videos follow physical commonsense, leaving a critical gap in understanding whether these models can serve as general-purpose physical world simulators.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes 688 human-verified text captions (averaging 8.5 words) depicting physical interactions between solids and fluids, feeds them to 12 T2V models to generate videos, and produces binary (0/1) scores for semantic adherence (does the video match the caption?) and physical commonsense (does the video follow intuitive physics?). For the automatic evaluator VIDEOCON-PHYSICS, input is a 32-frame video resized to 224×224 (with the caption for SA, without for PC), and output is a calibrated Yes/No probability computed from the model's token likelihoods.

## Method

VIDEOPHY is constructed via a three-stage pipeline: (1) GPT-4 generates 1000 candidate captions across solid-solid, solid-fluid, and fluid-fluid interaction categories; (2) human verification filters to 688 high-quality captions; (3) two graphics researchers annotate each caption's simulation difficulty (easy/hard). The evaluation protocol uses binary human judgments (majority vote of 3 AMT annotators) for SA and PC independently. For scalable auto-evaluation, VIDEOCON-PHYSICS fine-tunes the 7B-parameter VIDEOCON video-language model using LoRA (r=32, α=32, 5 epochs, 32 frames at 224×224) on 12,000 human annotations from 9 T2V models, maximizing log-likelihood of Yes/No responses. The key insight is that data-driven fine-tuning on the generated-video distribution substantially outperforms zero-shot multimodal LLMs (GPT-4V, Gemini-1.5-Pro) for physical commonsense judgment, achieving 73 ROC-AUC vs. 53–58 for baselines.

## Results

On the VIDEOPHY benchmark (688 human-verified captions, 12 T2V models), the best-performing model CogVideoX-5B generates videos that simultaneously adhere to the caption and follow physical laws (SA=1, PC=1) for only 39.6% of prompts, while all other models score below 20%. CogVideoX-5B also leads on physical commonsense alone (53%) and semantic adherence alone (63.3%). For the automatic evaluator VIDEOCON-PHYSICS, the headline result is a ROC-AUC of 82 (SA) and 73 (PC) against human judgments, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For the automatic evaluator, VIDEOCON-PHYSICS (ROC-AUC 82 SA / 73 PC) outperforms GPT-4-Vision (53/53) by 29 and 20 points, and Gemini-1.5-Pro-Vision (73/58) by 9 and 15 points on semantic adherence and physical commonsense respectively. It also beats zero-shot VIDEOCON (65/54) by 17 and 19 points, confirming that fine-tuning on generated-video distributions is critical. On unseen generative models, VIDEOCON-PHYSICS still leads zero-shot VIDEOCON by 15 points on both SA and PC (79 vs 64, 72 vs 57). Notably, GPT-4-Vision performs near-random on both tasks, and Gemini-1.5-Pro-Vision, while strong on SA (73), drops to near-random on PC (58), highlighting that existing multimodal LLMs lack reliable physical-commonsense judgment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VIDEOCON, a video-language model for semantic adherence evaluation, by fine-tuning it with LoRA on generated videos and human annotations to create VIDEOCON-PHYSICS, an automatic evaluator for both semantic adherence and physical commonsense. It departs from existing video generation benchmarks (VBench, EvalCrafter) that assess motion smoothness, background consistency, and text-video alignment but explicitly do not address adherence to physical laws. Rather than relying on physical simulations as ground truth—which the authors argue is non-trivial due to unknown material parameters, 3D reconstruction challenges, and the fact that simulations are merely numerical approximations—the paper grounds evaluation in human intuitive physics perception, a framing drawn from cognitive science literature on intuitive physics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIDEOPHY is the first benchmark to systematically quantify physical commonsense in T2V outputs, revealing that even the best model (CogVideoX-5B) achieves only 39.6% joint semantic-adherence-and-physical-commonsense accuracy, and that all models perform worst on solid-solid interactions. The striking disconnect between semantic adherence and physical commonsense (e.g., Dream Machine: 61.9% SA vs. 21.8% PC) challenges the assumption that text alignment implies physical plausibility. Open questions include whether scaling data or model capacity will close the gap, how to incorporate physics priors into generation, and whether the 688-caption scale and binary feedback protocol are sufficient for fine-grained progress tracking. The VIDEOCON-PHYSICS auto-evaluator addresses the scalability bottleneck, enabling cheaper evaluation of future models.
