## Problem

Conventional CNNs are highly sensitive to color variation (hue, saturation, luminance shifts), leading to significant performance degradation on out-of-distribution perceptual changes. While group convolutional networks have been widely applied to geometric symmetries, their use for perceptual transformations remains limited. The closest prior work, CEConv, achieves hue equivariance but its lifting layer produces invalid RGB values that break equivariance and degrade accuracy, and it does not address saturation or luminance variation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250655/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes RGB images as input, converts them to HSL color space, and the lifting layer shifts the hue, saturation, or luminance channels to produce feature maps on the product space Z² × G (where G is the relevant discrete group). For classification tasks the output is a class label obtained after group pooling in the final layer; for the segmentation task the output is a per-pixel foreground-background mask. The equivariant feature maps at intermediate layers are structured so that a perceptual shift in the input corresponds to a deterministic group action (rotation for hue, translation for saturation/luminance) on the feature map.

## Method

The paper identifies hue with the cyclic group C_N (discrete 2D rotations) and saturation/luminance with the 1D translation group (Z, +), then constructs a GCNN equivariant to these transformations by design. The key architectural fix over CEConv is a lifting layer that transforms the input image in HSL space (shifting hue/saturation/luminance channels) rather than rotating RGB filters, which eliminates invalid RGB values and reduces equivariance error by over three orders of magnitude. Subsequent layers perform standard group convolution on the product space Z² × G, and a final group pooling layer yields an invariant representation for classification. The approach is validated across synthetic (Hue-shift MNIST, 3D Shapes) and real-world (Camelyon17, Caltech-101, CIFAR-10/100, Stanford Cars, Oxford-IIT Pets, STL-10, ImageNet, Small NORB) datasets, demonstrating improved out-of-distribution generalization, sample efficiency, and enabling color-based sorting of features.

## Results

On the Hue-shift MNIST out-of-distribution test (A/B), Hue-4 achieves 2.08% classification error versus 57.38% for the Z2CNN baseline. On the 3D Shapes local hue-shift test (A/C), Hue-4 achieves 0.00% error while Z2CNN degrades to 26.66% and CEConv-4 to 0.53%. On the Camelyon17 saturation-shift task, Sat-3 reduces error from 28.91% (ResNet50) to 16.08%. Across six color-shift benchmarks (Table 4), Hue-4 outperforms CEConv-4 on all datasets and is competitive with or superior to ResNet on fine-grained tasks (Stanford Cars: 20.38% vs 25.41%; Oxford Pets: 27.39% vs 31.52%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Z2CNN (Cohen & Welling, 2016), the proposed Hue-N models achieve dramatic improvements on out-of-distribution hue-shift: 2.08% vs 57.38% on Hue-shift MNIST (A/B) and 0.00% vs 51.25% on 3D Shapes (A/B), confirming that equivariant structure preserves performance under unseen color variations. Against CEConv (Lengyel et al., 2024), the key advantage is stability across group discretizations: CEConv-4 degrades to 0.60% on 3D Shapes (A/B) and 33.70% on Oxford Pets, while Hue-4 maintains 0.00% and 27.39% respectively; the equivariance error of the proposed lifting layer is over three orders of magnitude lower than CEConv's. Against ResNet (He et al., 2016), the proposed models are competitive on standard in-distribution accuracy but clearly win on out-of-distribution color-shift: on Camelyon17, Sat-3 achieves 16.08% error vs ResNet50's 28.91%, and on fine-grained color-shift tasks (Stanford Cars, Oxford Pets), Hue-4 outperforms ResNet by 5.0 and 4.1 percentage points respectively. On ImageNet, Hue-3 (30.28%) slightly outperforms ResNet18 (30.66%) with fewer parameters (11.4M vs 11.7M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CEConv architecture (Lengyel et al., 2024), which first proposed hue-equivariant group convolutional networks by identifying hue shifts with the 2D rotation group. The key departure is in the lifting layer: rather than rotating RGB filter values (as CEConv does), the authors transform the input image in HSL space, a change that eliminates invalid RGB values and improves equivariance error by over three orders of magnitude. The paper also extends the equivariance framework beyond hue to saturation and luminance, identifying those shifts with the 1D translation group, building on the broader GCNN literature (Cohen & Welling, 2016) for finite group equivariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Color variation is a persistent practical challenge in domains like medical imaging where data from different labs or sensors exhibit systematic color shifts, and conventional CNNs degrade significantly under such perturbations. The structured equivariant representations enable capabilities unavailable to standard architectures, such as principled color-based sorting of images. Key limitations include the computational overhead of GCNNs (filter orbit computation at each layer) and the reliance on finite group discretization to approximate continuous color transformations; the authors note that continuous group representations are a natural direction for future work.
