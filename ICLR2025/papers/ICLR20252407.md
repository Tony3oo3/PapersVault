## Problem

Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) samplers offer robust resampling and asymptotic guarantees but rely on fixed MCMC transitions that converge slowly, while diffusion-based samplers provide flexible learned transport yet suffer from training instabilities and mode collapse. No prior framework had principledly unified these two paradigms, leaving practitioners to choose between robustness and adaptability.

## Input / Output

The method requires only pointwise evaluations of the unnormalized target density ρ_target (and optionally its gradient) plus a tractable prior distribution; no samples from the target are available. The annealing schedule β(t) interpolating between prior and target is itself learned as a neural parameter. Outputs include K particles approximately distributed as p_target, per-subtrajectory importance weights, and a Monte Carlo estimate of log Z (the ELBO).

## Method

SCLD formulates both SMC and controlled Langevin diffusion as measures on path space in continuous time, enabling them to be interleaved at arbitrary timesteps. Particles evolve via a forward SDE with a neural-network-parameterized drift u = σ²ũ_θ + (σ²/2)∇log π, where π(x,t) ∝ p_prior(x)^(1−β(t)) ρtarget(x)^β(t) is a prescribed annealed density; the reverse SDE is defined via Nelson's identity (v = u − σ²∇log π). The Radon-Nikodym derivative between forward and reverse path measures yields per-subtrajectory importance weights that drive multinomial resampling (triggered when ESS < 0.3K) and optional HMC refinement steps. Training minimizes the log-variance divergence on each subtrajectory rather than KL divergence, which the authors prove scales polynomially rather than exponentially in dimension, enabling stable off-policy optimization with a prioritized replay buffer and end-to-end gradient updates through the full SMC–diffusion pipeline.

## Results

SCLD achieves the best or near-best performance on 10 of 11 benchmark tasks. On Sinkhorn distance (lower is better), SCLD records 0.31 on Robot1 (10d), 0.40 on Robot4 (10d), 3787.73 on GMM40 (50d), and 656.10 on MoS (50d) — all best-in-class. On ELBO (higher is better), SCLD attains the top score on 4 of 5 tasks (Seeds: −73.45, Sonar: −108.17, Credit: −504.46, Brownian: 1.00) using only 3,000 gradient steps versus 40,000 for the CMCD baselines, representing a ~10× reduction in training budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the diffusion-based baselines CMCD-KL and CMCD-LV, SCLD dominates on high-dimensional multimodal targets: on GMM40 (50d), SCLD achieves a Sinkhorn distance of 3787.73 versus 22132.28 (CMCD-KL) and 4258.57 (CMCD-LV), and on MoS (50d) it scores 656.10 versus 1848.89 and 1945.71, respectively, while using only 3,000 gradient steps instead of 40,000. CRAFT (learned normalizing-flow SMC) is competitive on Funnel (133.42 vs. SCLD's 134.23) but suffers severe mode collapse on GMM40 and Robot4, where it collapses to a single mode. PIS (Path Integral Sampler) is the closest competitor on the low-dimensional MW54 task (0.42 vs. 0.44) but is far behind on the robotics tasks (Robot1: 1.54 vs. 0.31; Robot4: 2.02 vs. 0.40) and diverges entirely on Funnel. Traditional SMC variants (SMC, SMC-ESS, SMC-FC) are outperformed on every Sinkhorn-distance task except Robot1, where SMC-FC (0.37) narrowly trails SCLD (0.31); however, SMC-ESS retains an edge on the 1600-dimensional LGCP ELBO task (497.85 vs. 486.77). DDS (Denoising Diffusion Sampler) is competitive only on MW54 (0.63) and fails to converge on LGCP, while being an order of magnitude worse on GMM40 (5435.18 vs. 3787.73).

## Relation to Prior Work

SCLD unifies two previously separate paradigms—Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) and diffusion-based sampling via controlled SDEs—by casting both in continuous time on path space. It builds directly on CMCD (Vargas et al., 2024) for the prescribed density evolution and the Radon-Nikodym derivative computation, on the log-variance divergence (Nusken & Richter, 2021) for a low-variance off-policy training objective, and on classical SMC resampling/MCMC machinery. The key departure from prior learned-SMC methods (CRAFT, PDDS) is the use of stochastic SDE transitions (avoiding Jacobian/determinant computations) and end-to-end training with replay buffers, rather than alternating schemes or deterministic normalizing-flow transitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for sampling practitioners: SCLD achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA on 11 benchmarks while using roughly 10% of the training budget of prior diffusion-based samplers, and is the only method to accurately recover all modes on the multimodal Robot tasks. The continuous-time path-space framework is general enough to accommodate arbitrary resampling schedules, MCMC refinements at any time, and potentially more general (learnable) density evolutions beyond the prescribed annealing. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high dimensions (beyond ~1600d tested), whether the MCMC refinement steps can be fully eliminated once the learned control is well-trained, and whether the framework extends to settings with pre-trained diffusion priors (e.g., image/audio inverse problems).
