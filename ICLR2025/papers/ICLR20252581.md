## Problem

No algorithm existed that simultaneously achieved Best-of-Both-Worlds (BoBW) regret guarantees and was parameter-free for Heavy-Tailed Multi-Armed Bandits (HTMABs). Prior BoBW HTMAB algorithms (e.g., HTINF) required prior knowledge of the heavy-tail parameters (α, σ), while parameter-free HTMAB algorithms (e.g., AdaR-UCB) only worked in stochastic environments and failed under adversarial losses. This paper answers the open question of whether a single algorithm can be both parameter-free and BoBW for HTMABs.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates over T rounds with K arms, observing only the scalar loss ℓ_{t,i_t} of the selected arm each round (bandit feedback). It produces a mixed action x_t ∈ Δ^[K] (a probability distribution over arms) each round, from which a single arm is sampled. Crucially, the algorithm requires no prior knowledge of the heavy-tail shape parameter α ∈ (1,2], the scale parameter σ, or whether the environment is stochastic or adversarial; it only assumes E[|ℓ_{t,i}|^α] ≤ σ^α and a truncated non-negativity condition on the optimal arm's losses.

## Method

uniINF is built on the FTRL framework with a log-barrier regularizer Ψ_t(x) = -S_t Σ log x_i, where S_t^{-1} serves as the learning rate. Three novel components enable the BoBW and parameter-free properties: (1) a refined log-barrier analysis that, via partial-derivative calculations and the intermediate value theorem, yields a Bregman divergence bound containing an extra (1-x_{t,i_t})² factor—essential for excluding the optimal arm and achieving instance-dependent regret; (2) an auto-balancing learning rate schedule S_{t+1}² = S_t² + (ℓ_{t,i_t}^{clip})²(1-x_{t,i_t})²/(K log T) that keeps the Bregman divergence and Ψ-shifting terms in balance; and (3) an adaptive skipping-clipping loss tuning technique that uses skipped losses (zeroing out |ℓ| ≥ C_{t,i}) for the FTRL update to control divergence, while using clipped losses (capping at ±C_{t,i}) to update S_t, ensuring the learning rate grows even when large losses are skipped. A stopping-time analysis then converts these per-round bounds into O(log T) regret in stochastic environments and Õ(σK^{1-1/α}T^{1/α}) regret in adversarial environments.

## Results

uniINF achieves Õ(σK^{1−1/α} T^{1/α}) regret in adversarial environments, matching the instance-independent lower bound Ω(σK^{1−1/α} T^{1/α}) of Bubeck et al. (2013) up to logarithmic factors. In stochastic environments, it achieves O(K(σ^α/Δ_min)^{1/(α−1)} log T · log(σ^α/Δ_min)) regret, matching the instance-dependent lower bound Ω(Σ_{i≠i*}(σ^α/Δ_i)^{1/(α−1)} log T) up to a log(σ^α/Δ_min) factor independent of T. Crucially, uniINF requires no prior knowledge of the heavy-tail parameters (α, σ) and operates in both stochastic and adversarial settings simultaneously — a combination no prior algorithm achieves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

uniINF is the first algorithm to simultaneously be parameter-free (no prior knowledge of α or σ) and achieve Best-of-Both-Worlds guarantees. Compared to HTINF (Huang et al., 2022), which achieves optimal BoBW regret but requires known (α, σ), uniINF removes this requirement while preserving near-optimal bounds in both settings. OptHTINF (also Huang et al., 2022) is parameter-free and BoBW but its regret degrades to O(σ^α K^{(α−1)/2} T^{(3−α)/2}) in the adversarial case — strictly worse than the minimax-optimal O(σK^{1−α}T^{1/α}) — and its stochastic bound involves σ^{2α}/Δ_i^{3−α} instead of σ^α/Δ_i. AdaTINF (Huang et al., 2022) is parameter-free and adversarially optimal but provides no stochastic guarantee. AdaR-UCB (Genalti et al., 2024) is parameter-free and stochastically optimal but fails entirely in adversarial environments. RobustUCB and Robust MOSS (Bubeck et al., 2013; Wei & Srivastava, 2020) are stochastically optimal but require known (α, σ) and cannot handle adversarial losses. In summary, uniINF uniquely occupies the cell in Table 1 that is simultaneously α-free, σ-free, works in both environments, and matches the lower bounds up to logarithmic factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads in heavy-tailed bandit research: the Best-of-Both-Worlds (BoBW) framework of Huang et al. (2022), which required known heavy-tail parameters (α, σ), and the parameter-free algorithms of Genalti et al. (2024), which achieved near-optimal regret only in stochastic environments. Technically, it refines the log-barrier FTRL analysis of Foster et al. (2016) and Dai et al. (2023) by deriving an extra (1−x_{t,i})² factor in the Bregman divergence bound — a property previously exploited by Zimmert & Seldin (2019) for BoBW guarantees but not available in the heavy-tailed, parameter-free setting. The auto-balancing learning rate and adaptive skipping-clipping loss tuning are novel algorithmic components designed specifically to handle unbounded heavy-tailed losses without prior scale information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm that simultaneously achieves near-optimal regret in both stochastic and adversarial heavy-tailed bandit environments without requiring any prior knowledge of the distributional parameters (α, σ), addressing a practical necessity since real-world loss distributions rarely conform to known parametric forms. The paper acknowledges limitations: extra logarithmic factors (log σ^α / Δ_min) appear in the stochastic bound, and the instance-dependent regret depends on Δ_min rather than individual gaps Δ_i, which is improvable when gaps are heterogeneous. Open questions include whether the truncated non-negativity assumption (Assumption 1) can be weakened or removed in the parameter-free setting, and whether the gap-dependency can be tightened to match the per-arm lower bound structure.
