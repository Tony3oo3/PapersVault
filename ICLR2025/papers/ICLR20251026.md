## Problem

Universal adversarial perturbations (UAPs) pose a severe threat to deployed neural networks because a single perturbation can fool a model across many inputs, and targeted UAPs are both more practical for attackers and harder to defend against. Existing defenses either require pre-generating UAPs for each target class, modify the model architecture, or sacrifice significant clean accuracy. This paper identifies that UAPs exploit dominant features that collapse layer-wise entropy in deeper layers, and asks whether a model can be repaired to resist such feature dominance without knowing the attack target or altering its architecture.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained CNN (e.g., ResNet-50, VGG-19, MobileNet) and a small set of clean RGB images (≤5% of the original training set, ranging from 32×32 CIFAR-10 to 224×224 ImageNet). It outputs updated model parameters θ that preserve clean-sample accuracy (within ~2%) while drastically reducing UAP attack success rate. Notably, the Sample Generator requires no target-class label and no pre-computed perturbation, making the procedure agnostic to the specific UAP attack used.

## Method

Democratic Training first observes that targeted UAPs cause a sharp drop in layer-wise Shannon entropy (computed via softmax over neuron activations), with the effect intensifying in deeper layers—indicating a few dominant features hijack the prediction. To repair the model, a Sample Generator iteratively perturbs clean inputs to minimize layer-wise entropy (using PGD-style sign-gradient steps with an l_p clamp), producing low-entropy samples that mimic UAP-induced feature dominance without requiring any target class. The model is then fine-tuned with a weighted cross-entropy loss L = α·L_cce(i_en) + (1−α)·L_cce(i), where i_en is the low-entropy sample and i is the clean sample; minimizing the loss on i_en forces the network to ignore the dominating features and rely on a broader set of features for prediction. Back-propagation uses projected gradient descent, and the entire procedure runs in O(n·m·|I|) time without generating any UAPs or modifying the network architecture.

## Results

Across 7 neural networks on 5 datasets, Democratic Training reduces the targeted UAP attack success rate from 79.4% to 2.8% on average, while improving adversarial accuracy from 12.6% to 69.3%. Clean-sample accuracy is minimally affected, dropping by only ~2% on average. The layer-wise entropy gap between clean and UAP-perturbed inputs shrinks from 16.7% to 0.2%, confirming the method effectively suppresses the dominant-feature behavior exploited by UAPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Democratic Training dramatically outperforms CFN (SR 2.8% vs 55.9%) and FNS (SR 2.8% vs 62.3%), which both fail to meaningfully reduce targeted UAP success rates despite modifying the model architecture. Against SFR, Democratic Training achieves a slightly higher SR (2.8% vs 1.1%) but avoids any architecture modification and requires no pre-trained UAPs (SFR needs 25 UAPs at >40 min each, vs ~10 min total for Democratic Training). Compared to DensePure, Democratic Training trades a small SR increase (2.8% vs 1.0%) for a much smaller clean-accuracy loss (−2% vs −12.1%) and no inference-time overhead. Against TRADES, Democratic Training achieves lower adversarial accuracy (69.3% vs 81.6%) and slightly higher SR (2.8% vs 2.2%), but preserves clean accuracy far better (−2% vs −11%), making it more practical for deployment where clean performance must be maintained.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the UAP defense literature that includes adversarial fine-tuning with pre-generated UAPs (Mummadi et al., 2019; Shafahi et al., 2020), architecture-modifying approaches like selective feature regeneration (Borkar et al., 2020) and feature norm clipping/suppression (Yu et al., 2021, 2023), and diffusion-based purification (Xiao et al., 2023). It departs from these by introducing layer-wise Shannon entropy as both a diagnostic for UAP-induced feature dominance and an optimization signal for fine-tuning, eliminating the need to generate UAPs or alter the model architecture. The conceptual framing of 'democratic' prediction—spreading influence across many features rather than a few dominant ones—repurposes information-theoretic entropy in a way not previously exploited for adversarial defense, though the underlying observation that UAPs contain dominant features (Zhang et al., 2020b) is well established.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Democratic Training offers a practical, architecture-preserving defense that operates on third-party models with minimal clean data (≤5% of training set) and no UAP generation, making it attractive for real-world deployment where retraining from scratch or modifying architectures is infeasible. However, its effectiveness is notably weaker against non-targeted UAPs (SR only reduced to ~30% vs. <3% for targeted), and the method has only been validated on CNNs, leaving open whether the entropy-based mechanism generalizes to transformers or non-vision modalities. A key open question is whether the entropy-guided fine-tuning can be combined with standard adversarial training (e.g., TRADES) to close the remaining gap, and whether the layer-wise entropy diagnostic can serve as a general robustness audit tool beyond UAP defense.
