## Problem

Learning interactive 3D agent behavior models from casual video recordings is fundamentally difficult because videos captured over long time spans (e.g., a month) must be registered into a consistent 4D spacetime representation, yet prior methods either require controlled multi-camera studios or can only reconstruct single videos independently without cross-video correspondence. No existing approach can build a complete, persistent 4D scene from orders of magnitude more data (e.g., 20k frames) while tracking a deformable agent, a moving observer, and an evolving environment simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGBD iPhone video collections of a single agent in a familiar environment (e.g., 23 cat videos totaling 25m39s over 37 days), plus a one-time walk-through video of the scene and a turnaround video of the agent for template reconstruction. Frames are extracted at 10 FPS and augmented with DINOv2 features, optical flow, and segmentation masks. The output is a persistent 4D reconstruction (canonical NeRFs for scene and agent, time-varying camera poses and 25 articulated bones) from which an interactive behavior simulator is trained to generate a 5.6-second horizon of goal, root path, and full-body motion conditioned on the agent's ego-perception of the scene, observer, and its own past 0.8 seconds.

## Method

ATS introduces a coarse-to-fine multi-video registration pipeline. In the coarse stage, per-scene and per-agent neural localizers (ResNet-18 on DINOv2 features) are trained on synthetic data rendered from template 3D reconstructions (PolyCam mesh for scene, dynamic 3DGS for agent) to regress camera poses from a single image, reducing registration cost from O(N²) to O(N) and providing robust initialization. In the fine stage, the full 4D representation—canonical NeRFs with per-video appearance codes (GIRAFFE-style) and BANMo-style articulated bones—is jointly optimized via differentiable composite volume rendering with both photometric and DINOv2 feature-metric losses, plus a scene annealing schedule that randomly swaps per-video codes to enforce structural consistency across partial captures. For behavior generation, a hierarchical score-based diffusion model sequentially predicts goal (MLP score function), path (Control UNet conditioned on goal), and full-body motion (Control UNet conditioned on path), all conditioned on ego-centric perception codes: a 3D ConvNet encoding of the surrounding scene feature volume, an MLP encoding of the observer's past trajectory, and an MLP encoding of the agent's past motion, enabling interactive and scene-aware behavior generation.

## Results

On the cat behavior prediction task (K=16 samples, L=12 trials), ATS achieves 0.448±0.146 m goal error, 0.234±0.054 m path error, 0.550±0.112 rad orientation error, and 0.237±0.006 rad joint angle error over a 5.6 s prediction horizon. For 4D reconstruction, ATS achieves 0.708 overall depth accuracy and 0.613 LPIPS, substantially outperforming single-video TotalRecon (0.533 depth accuracy) and multi-video TotalRecon (0.099 depth accuracy). Camera registration error is 6.35° rotation and 0.41 m translation, compared to 59.19° and 0.68 m for multi-video TotalRecon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATS outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the Gaussian baseline (Kendall & Gal, 2017), which models behavior as a single Gaussian distribution, ATS nearly halves the goal error (0.448 vs. 0.942 m) and path error (0.234 vs. 0.440 m), demonstrating that the diffusion-based multi-modal representation captures the diversity of agent behaviors that a unimodal Gaussian cannot. Against the 1-stage MDM-style model (Tevet et al., 2022), which directly denoises full body motion without hierarchical decomposition, ATS reduces goal error by 66% (0.448 vs. 1.322 m) and path error by 59% (0.234 vs. 0.575 m), validating the hierarchical goal→path→body decomposition. Against the world-coordinate formulation (Rhinehart & Kitani, 2016), ATS's egocentric encoding reduces goal error by 61% (0.448 vs. 1.164 m), showing that avoiding over-fitting to specific scene locations is critical. For 4D reconstruction, ATS achieves 0.708 overall depth accuracy versus 0.533 for single-video TotalRecon (Song et al., 2023) and 0.099 for multi-video TotalRecon, with camera rotation error of 6.35° versus 59.19° for multi-video TotalRecon, confirming that the coarse-to-fine registration with neural localizers is essential for aligning large video collections.

## Relation to Prior Work

ATS extends TotalRecon's single-video deformable scene reconstruction to a multi-video, persistent 4D setting by introducing a coarse-to-fine registration pipeline that repurposes DINOv2 features as a neural camera localizer (O(N) cost vs. O(N²) for geometric methods like DUSt3R). The deformable agent representation builds directly on BANMo's articulated-bone blend-skinning, while the behavior generation module applies score-based diffusion hierarchically (goal → path → body) using Control UNets, a design borrowed from controllable motion synthesis. The key conceptual departure from prior behavior prediction work is that ego-perception (scene features, observer trajectory, past motion) is extracted automatically from the 4D reconstruction rather than requiring manual annotation or simulated environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a single smartphone recording over weeks is sufficient to build an interactive 3D behavior simulator, removing the need for mocap studios or multi-camera rigs and opening the door to personalized agent models from casual home video. However, several limitations remain: the 6.4-second prediction horizon captures only mid-level behaviors, the method handles a single agent (no multi-agent interactions), reconstruction relies on expensive test-time optimization (~24h on 8 A100s), and generated motion can be physically implausible (ground penetration, foot sliding). Open questions include scaling to longer horizons with memory, incorporating physics constraints, and extending to articulated scene objects (doors, drawers) for richer interactions.
