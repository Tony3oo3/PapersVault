## Problem

LLM-based search for optimal solutions requires balancing exploration (finding new regions of the search space) and exploitation (refining known good solutions), but existing methods either use static, pre-defined strategies (greedy best-k selection, repeated sampling) or attempt to predict task difficulty offline to pick a strategy. The paper asks whether a search method can automatically adapt its exploration-exploitation balance as evidence accumulates during search, without requiring task-difficulty prediction or fixed heuristics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a task description, a small set of warm-start solutions with their black-box scores, and a task-specific scoring function (cosine similarity via SimCSE for word search, scalarized QED+Vina for molecule optimization, Hamming-distance grid matching for 1D-ARC). Solutions are mapped into a continuous latent space via task-specific embedding models (GTE-Qwen-2-1.5b for text/code, Molformer for SMILES strings), and the BO proposal is a continuous vector in that space. Output is the highest-scoring discrete solution found within a fixed evaluation budget (1000 guesses for Semantle, 200 generations for Dockstring and 1D-ARC), with the latent-to-text decoding step introducing inherent imprecision since the LLM is only biased toward the proposed region rather than precisely controlled.

## Method

BOPRO performs latent-space Bayesian optimization over embeddings of candidate solutions, using a Gaussian process surrogate with a Matérn 5/2 kernel to model the unknown objective and an acquisition function (LogEI, UCB, or Thompson sampling) to propose a promising latent vector z'_t at each iteration. The key decoding mechanism generalizes OPRO: instead of greedily selecting the top-k solutions by observed score as in-context examples, BOPRO retrieves the k previously observed solutions whose embeddings are most cosine-similar to the BO proposal z'_t, thereby biasing the LLM's generation toward the region the surrogate deems most informative. This single change—replacing score-based selection with uncertainty-aware latent-space selection—transforms OPRO's fixed greedy strategy into one that adaptively shifts between exploring unobserved regions (when uncertainty is high) and exploiting known good regions (when the posterior is confident). The generated solutions are scored by the black-box function, embedded, and appended to the observation pool to update the GP posterior, repeating until the budget is exhausted.

## Results

On Semantle (word search), all BOPRO variants outperform OPRO by ≥10 percentage points, with BOPRO-UCB reaching 76.67% success rate versus OPRO's plateau after rapid initial gains. On Dockstring (molecule optimization), BOPRO marginally outperforms OPRO on average quality while producing 17% fewer invalid molecules; critically, OPRO completes only 12 of 58 protein targets in the same wall-clock time as BOPRO due to consistently proposing 40% longer SMILES strings. On 1D-ARC-Hard (hypothesis+program search), BOPRO trails OPRO and even underperforms repeated sampling, a failure the authors attribute to inadequate code embedding representations rather than a flaw in the exploration-exploitation mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OPRO (Yang et al., 2024), BOPRO wins decisively on Semantle (≥10 percentage points, e.g., 76.67% vs. ~60% for BOPRO-UCB) and marginally on Dockstring (higher joint QED+Vina quality, 17% fewer invalid molecules), but loses on 1D-ARC-Hard where OPRO's greedy best-k prompting outperforms all BOPRO variants. Against repeated sampling (Brown et al., 2024), BOPRO clearly wins on Semantle (76.67% vs. 14.00%) and Dockstring, but on 1D-ARC-Hard BOPRO underperforms even RS, which the authors note is a strong method for code generation. Against InstructZero (Chen et al., 2024), BOPRO dominates on Semantle with BOPRO-UCB at 76.67% versus InstructZero's 4.00% (8.00% with sampling), indicating that soft-prompt optimization via BO is far less effective than the hard-prompting approach. Against LMX (Meyerson et al., 2023), both BOPRO and OPRO outperform their respective LMX/BLMX counterparts on 1D-ARC-Hard, though all methods show a performance drop when switching from OPRO-style to LMX-style decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

BOPRO is explicitly positioned as a Bayesian generalization of OPRO (Yang et al., 2024), replacing OPRO's greedy best-k prompt construction with a retrieval strategy guided by a latent-space Gaussian Process surrogate. The core optimization framework is classical Bayesian optimization (Kushner, 1964; Frazier, 2018), but the paper departs from prior BO+LLM work (Kristiadi et al., 2024; Opsahl-Ong et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024) that either treated the problem as selection from a fixed candidate pool or relied on unprincipled heuristics for uncertainty. The key conceptual departure from static test-time search strategies (repeated sampling, evolutionary algorithms like LMX/FunSearch) is that the exploration-exploitation balance is not pre-specified but emerges dynamically from the evolving posterior as new evidence accumulates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that adaptive, uncertainty-driven search can outperform both greedy exploitation (OPRO) and undirected exploration (repeated sampling) on word search and molecule optimization, with the ≥10-point Semantle gap and 17% reduction in invalid molecules being practically meaningful. The failure on 1D-ARC program search, however, is the most informative contribution: it isolates the bottleneck to the inadequacy of off-the-shelf code embeddings in distinguishing sequences with low edit-distances, pointing to a concrete and actionable gap in representation learning for code. Open questions include how to properly bound the valid solution region in latent space, whether BO degrades under multiple global optima, and how to achieve more precise latent-to-text decoding than prompting-based retrieval.
