## Problem

Existing T2S diffusion models are too slow for the iterative trial-and-error process that sound creators rely on, while current distillation models (ConsistencyTTA, AudioLCM) offer fast 1-step generation at insufficient quality and cannot preserve semantic content during multi-step refinement due to their anytime-to-zero-time training regime. No single model previously enabled creators to first explore ideas with fast 1-step generation and then refine quality via multi-step sampling while keeping the semantic content fixed.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (encoded via CLAP for the DiT-1B variant or FLAN-T5 for the UNet variant) and an initial noise latent sampled from a prior distribution, and produces a full-band audio waveform at 44.1 kHz (or 16 kHz in the smaller setting) decoded from a VAE latent space. A key operational detail is that the number of sampling steps (1 to 16) and the stochasticity parameter γ are user-selectable at inference time, allowing the same model to switch between fast exploration (1-step, γ=0) and high-quality refinement (16-step, γ=0) without changing the generated content's semantics.

## Method

SoundCTM reframes the Consistency Trajectory Model (CTM) framework for sound by learning anytime-to-anytime PF-ODE jumps, enabling deterministic multi-step sampling that preserves semantic content. Three key innovations distinguish it from the original CTM: (1) a novel feature distance d_teacher that uses the frozen teacher network's intermediate layer outputs as a perceptual metric for the distillation loss, avoiding dependence on external pretrained networks; (2) distilling classifier-free-guided trajectories by treating the CFG scale ω as an additional input condition to the student, with both conditional and unconditional student models trained simultaneously; and (3) ν-sampling at inference, which linearly interpolates between the conditional and unconditional student jumps (z_s|t = G_θ(z_t,∅,ω,t,s) + ν(G_θ(z_t,c,ω,t,s) − G_θ(z_t,∅,ω,t,s))) to provide an additional quality knob beyond ω. The model is trained with the CTM distillation loss plus an auxiliary DSM loss (no GAN loss), and is scaled to 1B trainable parameters using a DiT architecture for full-band 44.1 kHz generation.

## Results

On the full-band (44.1 kHz) AudioCaps testset, SoundCTM-DiT-1B achieves FD_openl3=72.24, KL_passt=1.27, and CLAP=0.42 with 16-step deterministic sampling, matching or surpassing its 40-step teacher diffusion model (FD=72.34, KL=1.74, CLAP=0.36) while running 3.5× faster (0.71 s vs. 2.50 s on a single H100). At 1-step, it reaches FD=84.07, KL=1.74, CLAP=0.36 in just 0.06 s—42× faster than the teacher. On the 16 kHz AudioCaps testset, SoundCTM outperforms both ConsistencyTTA and AudioLCM at 1-step (FAD=2.08 vs. 2.41 and 4.04) and at 4-step (FAD=1.72 vs. 3.01 and 1.42), with the best CLAP score (0.45) among all distillation models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Stable Audio Open (100 steps, DPM-Solver++), SoundCTM-DiT-1B at 16 steps wins on all three metrics (FD 72.24 vs. 78.24, KL 1.27 vs. 2.14, CLAP 0.42 vs. 0.29) while using only 1/6 the sampling steps. Against AudioLDM2-48kHz (200 steps, DDIM), the advantage is even larger: FD 72.24 vs. 101.11, KL 1.27 vs. 2.04, CLAP 0.42 vs. 0.37. Compared to its own 40-step teacher, SoundCTM-DiT-1B at 16 steps matches FD (72.24 vs. 72.34) but improves KL (1.27 vs. 1.74) and CLAP (0.42 vs. 0.36), while running 3.5× faster. In the 16 kHz setting, SoundCTM decisively outperforms ConsistencyTTA at 4 steps (FAD 1.72 vs. 3.01) and AudioLCM at 1 step (FAD 2.08 vs. 4.04), though AudioLCM at 4 steps achieves a slightly lower FAD (1.42 vs. 1.72) at the cost of worse CLAP (0.42 vs. 0.45). For sound intensity control, SoundCTM's loss-based guidance achieves MSE=18.5, substantially outperforming DITTO-2's z_T-optimization (MSE=50.4–60.2) while maintaining better FAD (3.04 vs. 3.65–3.71).

## Relation to Prior Work

SoundCTM reframes the Consistency Trajectory Model (CTM) framework from Kim et al. (2024, computer vision) for text-to-sound generation, departing from the anytime-to-zero-time jump paradigm of Consistency Distillation (used by ConsistencyTTA and AudioLCM) in favor of anytime-to-anytime jumps that enable deterministic sampling. Two key methodological departures from the original CTM are introduced: a teacher-network-based feature distance (d_teacher) replacing LPIPS for the distillation loss, and ν-sampling that combines conditional and unconditional student jumps at inference time. The work also extends the task from 16 kHz to full-band 44.1 kHz generation by scaling to a 1B-parameter DiT architecture, making it the first large-scale distillation model in the sound domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to preserve semantic content through deterministic multi-step sampling while improving quality is a practical workflow enabler for sound creators that CD-based models fundamentally cannot provide, positioning SoundCTM as a single-model solution for both exploration (1-step) and refinement (multi-step) phases. However, the model still cannot surpass its teacher in 1-step quality without GAN loss, and the authors' preliminary experiments show that off-the-shelf audio discriminators actually degrade performance, leaving the path to 1-step quality matching the teacher unresolved. Open questions include whether the loss-based guidance framework generalizes beyond sound intensity control, whether d_teacher remains effective when student and teacher are trained on different data domains, and whether tailored GAN setups for large-scale conditional sound generation can close the remaining 1-step gap.
