## Problem

Existing visual in-context learning methods (e.g., LVM) operate at the image level, require explicit source-target pair formatting during training, and struggle to produce temporally coherent sequences. This paper asks whether an autoregressive Transformer trained purely on continuous video clips via self-supervised next-token prediction can emerge with zero-shot video imitation capability—inferring semantics from a demonstration video and applying them to an unseen query—without any explicit demonstration-query pairs during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253398/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a demonstration video clip (8 frames) and a query video clip (4 frames), each frame encoded as 16×16=256 discrete VQ tokens at 256×256 resolution. The demonstration tokens are simply prepended to the query tokens in a single 4096-token sequence with no special separator tokens between frames or between the demonstration and query segments. The model autoregressively generates 4 additional frames (1024 tokens) that are perceptually coherent with the query scene while semantically following the action demonstrated in the demonstration clip.

## Method

VidIT uses a pretrained VQ-GAN tokenizer (AMUSED, 16× spatial compression) to convert each 256×256 frame into 256 discrete tokens, then trains a LLaMA-style decoder-only Transformer (300M/700M/1.1B parameters, with RMSNorm and Rotary Embeddings) via next-token prediction on 16-frame continuous video clips from Ego4d, Kinetics-600, and self-collected data. The critical design choice is the absence of any special separation tokens between frames, which causes the autoregressive objective to implicitly encode a demonstration-query structure: predicting token t_i conditioned on all preceding tokens t_{<i} is formally equivalent to predicting a query continuation given a preceding 'demonstration' segment. At inference, the model generalizes this learned structure to cases where the demonstration and query originate from entirely different videos, enabling zero-shot imitation without any explicit imitation-format fine-tuning. The paper further shows that the imitation capacity follows a scaling law with model size and that the model can be extended to accept text demonstrations via T5-encoded latent conditions.

## Results

On the Something-Something v2 evaluation set, VidIT (1.1B) achieves a Probing Accuracy of 38.5% and PSNR of 13.07 dB, substantially outperforming all baselines. In human evaluation (1–5 scale), VidIT scores 4.12 on visual quality, 3.73 on semantic alignment, and 3.01 on contrastive control, versus the best baseline (LVM) at 3.50, 2.71, and 1.81 respectively. The model also demonstrates scaling behavior: larger models (300M → 700M → 1.1B) consistently improve both visual quality (PSNR, FID) and semantic accuracy (P-Acc gain over random demonstrations).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VidIT outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against LVM (Bai et al., 2023), VidIT gains +11.4 points in P-Acc (38.5 vs. 27.1) and +1.0 dB in PSNR, while also leading in all three human-evaluation dimensions (Alignment 3.73 vs. 2.71; Control 3.01 vs. 1.81). Against DeLVM (Guo et al., 2024), the closest competitor, VidIT still leads by +5.7 P-Acc and +0.38 dB PSNR, with a notable gap in human-rated alignment (3.73 vs. 2.28). Against LWM (Liu et al., 2024), VidIT achieves the largest margin: +13.6 P-Acc and +1.18 dB PSNR, reflecting LWM's design focus on long-context text-video alignment rather than demonstration-driven imitation. The authors attribute VidIT's advantage to its video-native training format (continuous frame sequences with next-token prediction) versus the image-pair or text-conditioned paradigms of the baselines, which limit their ability to capture temporal semantics and generate coherent multi-frame sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

VidIT extends the in-context learning paradigm from images to video by repurposing the autoregressive next-token prediction objective (as in LLaMA) over VQ-GAN video tokens, in contrast to prior image in-context learners (LVM, DeLVM) that require supervised image-annotation pairs and formulate imitation as inpainting or sequence completion. The key conceptual departure is that no explicit demonstration-query pairs are provided during training; instead, the model is trained solely on continuous video clips, and the zero-shot imitation capability emerges from the autoregressive structure implicitly treating preceding frames as demonstrations. This positions VidIT at the intersection of video generation (where prior work conditions on text or actions) and imitation learning (where prior work requires state-action pairs), using video itself as both the demonstration medium and the generation target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that video can serve as a natural, rich interface for AI to acquire and transfer behavioral semantics from the real world, with the zero-shot imitation capacity following scaling laws that suggest substantial gains at larger scales. However, current limitations are significant: the model handles only short sequences (4-frame generations), struggles with semantically unrelated demonstrations, and relies on a spatial-only VQ tokenizer without temporal compression. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer-horizon tasks, whether temporal-compression tokenizers improve efficiency, and whether the modest absolute semantic accuracy (~38–49% P-Acc on SSv2) can be substantially improved with larger models or better data curation.
