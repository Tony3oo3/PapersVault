## Problem

Existing end-to-end autonomous driving methods adopt a sequential perception-prediction-planning pipeline that causes cumulative errors, requires multi-stage training, and prevents cross-task synergies like planning-aware perception. Additionally, their reliance on dense BEV representations creates computational bottlenecks for long-range perception and long-term temporal fusion, hindering scalability. The paper asks whether a unified, parallel, sparse Transformer architecture can replace this sequential BEV-based paradigm while enabling one-stage training and easy scaling.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 6 surround-view camera images at 384×1056 resolution (encoded by ResNet-50 with 3D ray-based position encoding), canbus signals for ego query initialization, and a FIFO queue of up to 10 prior timesteps of task queries (at 10 Hz in CARLA; 4 timesteps at 2 Hz in nuScenes). Outputs are produced via DETR-style task heads at every block: 900 agent queries yield 3D detection boxes and local-coordinate motion trajectories, 100 map queries (replicated per-point for fine-grained retrieval) produce vectorized lane/road polylines, and a single ego query with 6 mode embeddings generates a multi-mode planning trajectory via winner-take-all selection.

## Method

DriveTransformer replaces the sequential BEV pipeline with a stack of identical blocks, each containing three attention operations: Sensor Cross Attention (task queries attend directly to raw image features with 3D position encoding, eliminating BEV construction), Task Self-Attention (ego, agent, and map queries attend to one another, enabling data-driven cross-task synergy without manual ordering), and Temporal Cross Attention (current queries attend to history queries stored in per-task FIFO queues, with ego-coordinate transformation and DiT-style ada-LN motion compensation for moving agents). All tasks are trained jointly in a single stage using Hungarian matching (detection, mapping) and winner-take-all (motion, planning) losses, with coarse-to-fine PE refinement at each block. The planning head models ego behavior as a 6-mode Gaussian Mixture (straight, stop, left/right turns, sharp turns) to avoid mode averaging. This sparse, parallel, streaming design enables batch-size-12 training on A800 (vs. 1 for UniAD), strong scaling with depth/width, and significantly better robustness to sensor corruptions compared to BEV-based baselines.

## Results

On the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark (220 routes, CARLA), DriveTransformer-Large achieves a Driving Score of 63.46 and Success Rate of 35.01%, substantially outperforming UniAD-Base (45.81 / 16.36%) and VAD (42.35 / 15.00%) while running at 211.7 ms latency (vs. 663.4 ms for UniAD-Base). On nuScenes open-loop evaluation (with ego-status), DriveTransformer-Large achieves an average L2 of 0.33 m and collision rate of 0.07%, beating VAD-Base (0.37 m / 0.14%) and BEVPlanner++ (0.35 m). The model also achieves the best middle-task performance: detection NDS 59.3, motion minADE 0.61, and mapping IoU-Lane 0.77 on nuScenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveTransformer-Large decisively outperforms the sequential-pipeline baselines UniAD-Base and VAD on the primary closed-loop metrics: Driving Score improves from 45.81/42.35 to 63.46, Success Rate from 16.36%/15.00% to 35.01%, and Avg. L2 from 0.73/0.91 to 0.62, while also reducing latency by ~3× (211.7 ms vs. 663.4/278.3 ms). Against the distillation-based baselines ThinkTwice* and DriveAdapter*, DriveTransformer achieves comparable Driving Score (63.46 vs. 62.44/64.22) but with substantially lower L2 (0.62 vs. 0.95/1.01) and 3.6–4.4× lower latency (211.7 ms vs. 762/931 ms). On nuScenes open-loop (with ego-status), DriveTransformer-Large beats VAD-Base on both L2 (0.33 vs. 0.37 m) and collision rate (0.07% vs. 0.14%), and matches or beats BEVPlanner++ (0.35 m) on L2 while reporting a 0.07% collision rate. The main trade-off is Comfortness (20.78) and Efficiency (100.64), where DriveTransformer scores lower than UniAD-Base (43.58/129.21) and VAD (46.01/157.94), suggesting the more aggressive driving style that yields higher success rates comes at the cost of ride comfort. On middle tasks (Table 10), DriveTransformer surpasses both UniAD and ParaDrive on detection NDS (59.3 vs. 49.8/48.0), motion minADE (0.61 vs. 0.72), and mapping IoU-Lane (0.77 vs. 0.67/0.71) at comparable or lower latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveTransformer departs from the dominant sequential perception-prediction-planning paradigm (UniAD, VAD) by replacing explicit task dependencies with a unified parallel Transformer where all task queries (agent, map, ego) interact via self-attention at every layer. It inherits the token design from DAB-DETR (semantic embeddings + position encodings), the 3D position encoding from PETRv2 to avoid BEV construction, and the FIFO-queue temporal mechanism from StreamPETR, but unifies them into a single sparse, streaming architecture. The key conceptual shift is from dense BEV feature construction to direct sensor cross-attention, and from multi-stage training to single-stage end-to-end optimization, positioning the work alongside concurrent sparse-query methods (SparseAD, SparseDrive) but with full task parallelism rather than residual sequential structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-stage training stability is practically significant for industrial deployment, as it eliminates the fragile multi-stage pretraining pipeline that UniAD and SparseAD require. The robustness results (e.g., only 5.94% degradation under incorrect calibration vs. 28.04% for VAD) provide a conceptual explanation for why direct sparse sensor interaction outperforms dense BEV construction under sensor failures. Open questions include how to combine this architecture with vision-language models for broader scene understanding, how to decouple tasks for easier maintenance and debugging, and whether the architecture can scale with only final-layer supervision as depth increases.
