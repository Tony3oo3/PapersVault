## Problem

Vision Foundation Models (DINOv2, CLIP, SAM) exhibit complementary but conflicting representation biases across dense prediction tasks due to their distinct training paradigms, yet no single VFM dominates all tasks. Existing multi-teacher distillation methods (RADIO, Theia) collapse these biases into a unified student representation, which the paper demonstrates actually underperforms the per-task oracle of individual teachers by over 6% on parsing and boundary detection. The key question is whether one can preserve each teacher's unique bias during distillation while still achieving the efficiency of a single deployable model.

## Input / Output

The student model consumes a single RGB image (512×512 for PASCAL-Context, 448×576 for NYUD-v2) and simultaneously produces dense predictions for up to five tasks: semantic segmentation, human parsing, saliency, surface normals, and object boundaries (plus depth on NYUD-v2). Internally, the model operates on multi-level patch-token representations at four evenly spaced depths of the ViT backbone, which are routed through task-specific heads. Unlike methods that require running all VFM encoders at inference, SAK only needs the lightweight student (TAS + TSAPs + MoR Routers), with each TSAP adding less than 5% of the TAS parameters.

## Method

SAK distills a committee of frozen VFM teachers into a single student comprising a shared Teacher-Agnostic Stem (a standard ViT) and parallel Teacher-Specific Adapter Path modules (one lightweight adapter chain per teacher, using down-project/GELU/up-project blocks with residual connections). The TAS is optimized by gradients from all teachers simultaneously to capture universal knowledge, while each TSAP explicitly replicates its corresponding teacher's representation bias. A Mixture-of-Representations Router then treats the TAS output as a shared expert and each TSAP output as a specialized expert, generating a single global gating score per level (rather than per-patch) via a two-layer MLP with noisy gating, and computing a weighted sum of the N+1 expert representations. Training proceeds in two stages: first, distillation on ImageNet-1k aligns TSAP outputs to teachers using cosine + smooth-L1 losses; second, joint fine-tuning on the downstream multi-task dataset adds task-specific losses while retaining the distillation loss to prevent catastrophic forgetting of the learned biases.

## Results

On NYUD-v2 (ViT-L), SAK achieves an MTL Gain of 14.05%, surpassing the previous best (RADIO at 8.95%) by over 5 percentage points, with per-task scores of 63.18 mIoU (Semseg), 0.4313 RMSE (Depth), 16.25 mErr (Normal), and 79.43 odsF (Boundary). On PASCAL-Context (ViT-L), SAK achieves a positive MTL Gain of 2.30%—the first method to surpass the single-task baseline—compared to RADIO (0.29%) and MLoRE (0.16%), with standout improvements in Semseg (84.01 vs. 81.48 for TSP) and Parsing (76.99 vs. 71.50 for RADIO). The method uses 394M parameters on NYUD-v2, fewer than MLoRE (552M) while achieving substantially higher performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAK decisively outperforms the two multi-teacher VFM distillation baselines: on NYUD-v2, it beats RADIO (8.95% → 14.05% MTL Gain) by 5.1 points and Theia by a wider margin, while on PASCAL-Context it surpasses RADIO (0.29% → 2.30%) by 2.0 points. Against the strongest conventional MTL methods, SAK leads MLoRE by 9.79 points in MTL Gain on NYUD-v2 (14.05% vs. 4.26%) and by 2.14 points on PASCAL-Context (2.30% vs. 0.16%), all while using fewer parameters (394M vs. 552M on NYUD-v2). Compared to the individual VFM teachers (DINOv2, CLIP, SAM), SAK achieves the best overall balance: it matches or exceeds DINOv2 on segmentation tasks, CLIP on saliency, and SAM on boundary/normal estimation, without any single task suffering the severe degradation seen in individual teachers (e.g., SAM's 30% drop in Semseg). Among encoder-focused MTL methods (InvPT, InvPT++, TaskPrompter, TaskExpert, BFCI, 3D-aware, TSP), SAK consistently leads on all four NYUD-v2 tasks and four out of five PASCAL-Context tasks, with the largest gaps in Semseg (+7.79 mIoU over TSP on PASCAL-Context) and Parsing (+6.35 mIoU over RADIO). The only task where SAK does not achieve the absolute best score is Saliency on PASCAL-Context (84.65 vs. 84.90 for MLoRE), a marginal 0.25-point difference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-teacher VFM distillation line initiated by RADIO and Theia, which collapse multiple foundation model teachers into a single student via many-to-one knowledge transfer. The key departure is the hypothesis that this collapse erases each teacher's representation bias, and the architectural solution of Teacher-Specific Adapter Paths that preserve per-teacher biases in parallel lightweight branches. The Mixture-of-Representations Router adapts the Mixture-of-Experts mechanism (Jacobs et al., 1991; Shazeer et al., 2017) from per-token gating to global gating over representation-level experts, motivated by the observation that VFM representations vary substantially in norm magnitude across patches. The overall framework sits at the intersection of knowledge distillation, adapter-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and multi-task architecture design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that naive many-to-one distillation can degrade performance by over 6% on specific tasks (Table 2) challenges the implicit assumption in prior multi-teacher distillation work that a unified student should approximate all teachers simultaneously. The 10% MTL Gain improvement on NYUD-v2 and consistent gains across backbone sizes demonstrate that preserving and selectively routing teacher-specific biases is a practical and scalable strategy. Open questions include how the framework scales beyond three teachers, whether the adapter-based bias preservation generalizes to non-ViT architectures or video foundation models, and whether the global gating design in the MoR Router is optimal compared to learned per-region routing. The framework's modularity (swappable adapters, routers, and decoders) positions it as a potential baseline for future VFM-based MTL research.
