## Problem

Current multimodal human sensing methods are designed for fixed modality combinations, requiring network re-architecture and full retraining whenever a modality is added or removed. This limits scalability in real-world deployments where sensor availability varies across scenarios, and no existing foundation model can handle arbitrary combinations of heterogeneous sensing modalities in a single trained model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253510/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

X-Fi accepts any subset of up to six modalities (RGB, Depth, LiDAR point cloud, mmWave point cloud, WiFi-CSI, RFID phase series) with no fixed ordering or count, dynamically activating only the corresponding feature encoders. All modality features are projected to a unified token representation of 32 tokens × 512 dimensions; positional encoding derived from LiDAR 3D coordinates is conditionally injected only when LiDAR is present. The model outputs either 17 3D whole-body joint coordinates (HPE, MSE loss) or a discrete activity class label (HAR, cross-entropy loss) via a task-specific MLP head.

## Method

X-Fi combines modality-specific pretrained feature encoders (ResNet-18 for images, Point Transformer for point clouds, MetaFi++ for WiFi) with a novel X-Fusion block that iterates N=4 times. Each iteration first applies a cross-modal transformer (1-layer decoder with 8 attention heads) over the concatenated multi-modal embedding, using adaptive average pooling to produce a fixed-size cross-modal embedding regardless of input modality count. This embedding then serves as the query in modality-specific cross-attention modules, where key-value pairs generated by per-modality MLPs (frozen across iterations to prevent leakage) inject modality-specific information back into the shared representation. The iterative design forces the model to learn a dynamic attention allocation that preserves features from weaker modalities (e.g., WiFi) that standard self-attention would suppress. Training uses a modality existence list with per-modality binomial sampling probabilities to simulate all 38 possible combinations in a single training run, with feature extractor weights frozen to retain modality-specific inductive biases.

## Results

On the MM-Fi HPE task across 31 modality combinations, X-Fi (trained once) achieves an average MPJPE improvement of 24.8% and PA-MPJPE improvement of 21.4% over the feature-level fusion baseline, and 14.5–18.8% over the decision-level fusion baseline depending on the number of modalities. For HAR, X-Fi yields a 2.8% average accuracy improvement on MM-Fi (27 classes) and 6.7% on XRF55 (55 classes) for multimodal inputs. The model handles all six modalities (RGB, Depth, LiDAR, mmWave, WiFi-CSI, RFID) and their 38 possible combinations with a single trained model, outperforming baselines that must be retrained per combination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the decision-level fusion baseline (Yang et al., 2024), X-Fi wins on 28 of 31 HPE modality combinations, with average MPJPE improvements ranging from 6.0% (single-modality) to 18.8% (all five modalities); the few losses are on LiDAR single (−3.5%) and D+R (−4.1%). Against the feature-level fusion baseline (Das et al., 2021; Marcard et al., 2016), X-Fi wins on 29 of 31 combinations with substantially larger margins, e.g., 49.2% MPJPE improvement on I+L+R+W and 35.1% on the full five-modality input. Compared to the Visual Odometry Transformer (Memmel et al., 2023), X-Fi outperforms on most combinations, with a notable 21.3% MPJPE gain on LiDAR single-modality input (167.1 vs. 212.2 mm). Against fixed-combination SOTA fusion methods, X-Fi achieves 83.4 mm MPJPE on I+D+R versus 207.3 mm for ImmFusion (Chen et al., 2023) and 84.8 mm on I+D+L versus 96.2 mm for Meta-Transformer (Zhang et al., 2023). For HAR, X-Fi outperforms the baseline on all multimodal combinations (2.8% avg on MM-Fi, 6.7% on XRF55) but underperforms on some single-modality inputs (e.g., −11.2% on LiDAR single for MM-Fi HAR), a gap that narrows when modality existence probabilities are tuned during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the modality-invariant training strategy introduced by Memmel et al. (2023) for visual odometry into the human sensing domain, but departs significantly in architecture: rather than using a standard stacked transformer encoder for fusion, X-Fi introduces an iterative X-Fusion block with modality-specific key-value generators and cross-attention modules to explicitly preserve modality-specific features that standard self-attention tends to suppress. It also builds on the foundation model paradigm (pre-trained, task-agnostic representations) by framing a single human sensing model as a 'foundation model' that handles arbitrary modality subsets, contrasting with prior multimodal fusion work (ImmFusion, Meta-Transformer) that is locked to fixed modality combinations and requires retraining when sensors change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a single trained model that adapts to any sensor configuration eliminates the costly retraining loop that currently plagues multimodal deployment in robotics, surveillance, and rehabilitation. However, several open questions remain: WiFi-CSI single-modality performance is still weak (225.6 mm MPJPE), the approach relies on pre-trained modality-specific backbones that must be selected per sensor type, and scalability beyond six modalities or to new sensing tasks is untested. The paper also does not address real-time inference latency of the iterative X-Fusion (4 iterations) or the computational overhead of maintaining k separate key-value generators and cross-attention modules.
