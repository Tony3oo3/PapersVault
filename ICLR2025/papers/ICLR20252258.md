## Problem

Recent work has proposed technical safeguards (e.g., RepNoise, TAR) that claim to durably protect open-weight LLMs from adversarial fine-tuning, but the paper shows that even evaluating whether these defenses are truly durable is exceedingly difficult. Small, seemingly innocuous variations in evaluation setups—random seeds, trainer implementations, hyperparameter choices, prompt templates—can flip conclusions from 'defense holds' to 'defense is broken,' creating a false sense of security for stakeholders and policymakers.

## Input / Output

The evaluation takes publicly released defended checkpoints (RepNoise on Llama-2-7B-Chat; TAR-Bio-v1/v2 on Llama-3-8B-Instruct) and subjects them to fine-tuning attacks using specific datasets (BeaverTails-30k-train, Pile-Bio Forget/Retain, Alpaca, AOA) with controlled hyperparameter sweeps. Outputs include per-run harmfulness scores (BeaverTails, HEx-PHI, SORRY-bench), WMDP-Bio multiple-choice accuracy, and utility benchmark scores (MMLU, GSM8K, MATH, BBH, HumanEval), reported as distributions across 5 random seeds rather than single-point estimates. A key unusual aspect is that the 'attack' inputs include deliberately benign fine-tuning data (100 pure-benign Alpaca examples or 100 identity-shift AOA examples) to test whether claimed unlearning is genuine.

## Method

The paper conducts systematic re-evaluations of two open-weight LLM safeguards by isolating and varying one evaluation factor at a time: (1) enabling dataset shuffling to introduce randomness across 5 seeds, (2) swapping the official custom fine-tuning trainer for the Huggingface SFT Trainer, (3) modifying warmup steps and LR schedule (e.g., 30-step linear warmup + constant → 100-step warmup + cosine decay), and (4) changing the prompt template (adding Llama-3 chat formatting) during WMDP evaluation. A critical mechanistic finding is that TAR's apparent resistance to certain fine-tuning configurations stems from gradient explosion in the first few steps causing destructive weight updates; adding warmup and cosine decay stabilizes training and consistently recovers >60% WMDP-Bio accuracy. Additionally, fine-tuning on entirely unrelated benign data (Alpaca Salient, AOA) recovers harmful capabilities, indicating that RepNoise and TAR do not truly unlearn information but rather obscure it in a way that is easily reversed.

## Results

Re-evaluating TAR (Tamirisa et al., 2024) on Llama-3-8B-Instruct with 5 random seeds, the maximum WMDP-Bio accuracy recovered across all three fine-tuning configurations exceeds 60%, approaching the original model's pre-unlearning accuracy, whereas the original paper reported at most ~45% recovery. For RepNoise (Rosati et al., 2024) on Llama-2-7B-Chat, enabling dataset shuffling causes the highest harmfulness scores at learning rates of 6×10⁻⁵ and 8×10⁻⁵ to match those of the undefended base model across 5 runs. Additionally, TAR-Bio-v1's WMDP-Bio accuracy jumps from ~25% (random level) to consistently above 45% simply by wrapping the evaluation prompt in Llama-3's chat template and using GPT-4o or human answer extraction, revealing that the unlearning was not genuine.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the undefended Llama-2-7B-Chat baseline, RepNoise's defense is effectively broken: with random shuffling enabled, the worst-case harmfulness scores on BeaverTails across 5 seeds at LR 6×10⁻⁵ and 8×10⁻⁵ are nearly identical to the undefended model, and using the HuggingFace SFT Trainer instead of the authors' custom trainer makes the attack even more consistently successful. Against the undefended Llama-3-8B-Instruct baseline, TAR's WMDP-Bio accuracy recovers to >60% (close to the original model's level) under all three original fine-tuning configurations when multiple random seeds are tried, and a minor hyperparameter change (100 warmup steps + cosine decay instead of 30 warmup + constant LR) makes the attack dramatically more stable. The comparison between the official codebases and the HuggingFace SFT Trainer shows that the standard, widely-used trainer yields more stable and successful attacks for both RepNoise and TAR, suggesting the original evaluations may have benefited from suboptimal attack implementations. Finally, TAR-Bio-v2 is more robust to prompt template variations than v1 (no accuracy spike with chat template), but introduces significant utility degradation on benign biology questions and increased compliance with HarmfulQA, indicating a safety-utility trade-off that the original evaluation missed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as an evaluation methodology study within the nascent line of work on durable safeguards for open-weight LLMs (Henderson et al., 2023; Deng et al., 2024; Tamirisa et al., 2024; Rosati et al., 2024), rather than proposing a new defense. It draws a direct analogy to the adversarial robustness evaluation literature (Carlini & Wagner, 2017; Athalye et al., 2018; Uesato et al., 2018; Tramer et al., 2020), where defenses published at top venues were repeatedly shown to be insecure under minor attack adaptations. The paper also connects to the machine unlearning literature (Li et al., 2024; Shumailov et al., 2024; Lucki et al., 2024) and the fine-tuning jailbreak literature (Qi et al., 2024d; Yang et al., 2023; Zhan et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2024b), repurposing their attack techniques as evaluation probes for durability claims.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even evaluating whether a proposed durable safeguard works is itself an extremely fragile and error-prone process: enabling dataset shuffling, switching to a standard HuggingFace trainer, adding 100 warmup steps, or wrapping prompts in a chat template can each flip a 'defense holds' conclusion to 'defense fails.' This has direct implications for policy decisions (e.g., SB-1047, NIST guidelines) that may rely on durability claims, and for the broader AI safety evaluation community, which must develop more rigorous protocols. A key open question is whether any current approach can achieve genuine durability against adaptive adversaries, or whether the threat model must be substantially narrowed. The finding that 'unlearning' may not actually remove information—fine-tuning on unrelated benign data recovers the supposedly unlearned knowledge—raises fundamental questions about what these methods are actually doing to model representations.
