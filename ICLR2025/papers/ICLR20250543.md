## Problem

Training reliable reward models for RLHF requires large amounts of expensive human-annotated preference data, and existing alternatives like RLAIF still depend on stronger external LLMs to generate feedback. This paper asks whether a reward model can iteratively improve itself from its own predictions, using only a small seed of human labels, without requiring a stronger model to provide supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250543/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a base LLM (e.g., Llama 3 8B, Mistral 7B, Llama 2 13B) fine-tuned via SFT, 15% of a preference dataset as human-annotated seed data, and the remaining 85% as unlabeled preference pairs (each pair contains one question and two candidate answers). The RM is implemented as a LoRA adapter (rank 8, alpha 32) on the SFT model with a sequence-classification head producing a sigmoid-normalized reward score in [0,1]. The final outputs are a converged reward model that scores (question, answer) pairs, self-labeled preference data accumulated across iterations, and an LLM policy aligned via PPO using the evolved RM.

## Method

SER is an iterative 'feedback-then-train' loop with a key curriculum mechanism: the RM's learning status is assessed at each iteration to determine which data to filter. Status 1 (easier) checks whether the RM can confidently separate clearly good from clearly bad answers (thresholds τ_high=0.55, τ_low=0.45), and filters pairs where one answer scores above τ_high and the other below τ_low. Status 2 (harder) activates once Status 1 is saturated, targeting subtle distinctions between similar-quality answers by filtering pairs whose score difference exceeds τ_Δ=0.3. Filtered data is accumulated across iterations and used to retrain the RM with a pairwise margin loss, progressively shifting from broad discrimination to fine-grained comparison. Once no further data can be filtered (Stop status), the converged RM guides LLM training via a modified PPO objective that incorporates the evolved reward signals. Theoretically, the paper proves RM accuracy is non-decreasing under the high-confidence filtering assumption and that PPO converges to an O(ε_r)-optimal policy when the learned RM approximates the true reward within bounded error ε_r.

## Results

On reward modeling accuracy, SER achieves an average 7.88% improvement over the 15%-seed-data baseline (Loop0) across four datasets (HH-RLHF, UltraFeedback, Summarize, StackOverflow) and three model sizes (Llama 3 8B, Mistral 7B, Llama 2 13B). SER reaches or exceeds the full human-labeled dataset performance in most settings, with an average gap of only 0.3% from the full-data baseline. Notably, on UltraFeedback with Llama 3 8B, SER (74.46%) surpasses the full-data model (73.92%), and on StackOverflow with Llama 3 8B, SER (70.8%) exceeds the full-data model (69.0%). In PPO experiments, SER-guided LLMs consistently outperform the SFT baseline in GPT-4-judged win rate, and in some configurations (e.g., Mistral 7B on HH-RLHF) also exceed the full-dataset RM-guided PPO model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three configurations: Loop0 (RM trained on only 15% of human-annotated data), SER (iterative self-evolved RM starting from Loop0), and FullDataset (RM trained on 100% of human-annotated data). SER consistently and substantially outperforms Loop0 across all 12 dataset-model combinations, with gains ranging from ~1.3% (Mistral 7B on StackOverflow) to ~11.9% (Llama 3 8B on UltraFeedback). Against the FullDataset upper bound, SER matches or exceeds it in 7 out of 12 settings, with the largest margins on UltraFeedback/Llama3-8B (+0.54%), Summarize/Mistral-7B (+1.93%), and StackOverflow/Llama3-8B (+1.8%). In the PPO downstream task, SER-guided models beat the SFT baseline in win rate on both HH-RLHF and StackOverflow, and for Mistral 7B on HH-RLHF, the SER RM's PPO win rate slightly exceeds that of the full-dataset RM. The 60% human-labeled + SER configuration (71.83% on HH-RLHF/Llama3-8B) further surpasses the full human-labeled baseline (70.45%), suggesting SER can amplify even partial human annotation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of RLHF/RLAIF reward model training and the emerging self-learning paradigm for LLMs (Self-Rewarding LMs, MathShepherd, SELF-ALIGN). Unlike RLAIF, which relies on a stronger external LLM to generate feedback, SER uses the reward model itself to iteratively label and refine its own training data, closing the loop without external supervision. The two-stage learning status identification (broad good/bad distinction → fine-grained comparison) introduces a curriculum-like structure absent from prior self-training approaches, which typically apply a single uniform filtering criterion throughout training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: only 15% of human-annotated seed data is needed to match or occasionally exceed full-dataset performance, with a 6× cost reduction, making reward model training far more accessible. The finding that easy, clearly-distinguishable samples provide diminishing returns after the first iteration (and can even degrade performance) is a useful caution for self-training pipelines more broadly. Open questions include whether the empirical thresholds and status-identification heuristics generalize to new domains, whether the full closed loop (LLM generating responses → RM self-labeling → LLM retraining) can be sustained without error accumulation, and whether the convergence guarantees hold under more realistic distribution shifts.
