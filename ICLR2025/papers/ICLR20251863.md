## Problem

Existing multimodal fusion methods require end-to-end training, scale quadratically with the number of modalities, assume paired training data, and are highly topology-specific, making them impractical for biomedical settings with heterogeneous, imbalanced, and non-overlapping modalities. Model merging offers a training-free alternative but assumes architectural equivalence between merged models, which is infeasible when modalities have fundamentally different input shapes and network topologies.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts any set of unimodal encoders (e.g., ResNet50-patched WSI features, SNN-encoded tabular omics vectors, AMIL-encoded time-series vitals) and wraps each in a LegoBlock that projects the encoder output into a fixed-shape frequency-domain latent (17×126 complex tensor), regardless of the original modality's dimensionality or topology. Crucially, each modality's LegoBlock can be trained on entirely separate (non-overlapping) samples, and at inference time missing modalities are simply skipped. Outputs are task-specific logits: survival hazards (c-index), disease class probabilities (AUC), mortality scores (AUC), or melanoma classification (AUC).

## Method

LegoBlock is a per-modality wrapper that iteratively updates a shared-shape latent via cross-attention in the frequency domain: the latent is FFT-transformed, its real component is updated by cross-attending against the FFT of the encoder output (as key/value), and the imaginary component is preserved to maintain phase information for inverse-transform reconstruction. LegoMerge then aggregates the final frequency-domain latents across modalities using the harmonic mean of magnitudes and arithmetic mean of phases (minimising signal interference due to Fourier orthogonality), combined with SLERP interpolation of task-head weights, yielding a multimodal model with zero fine-tuning. LegoFuse extends this by stacking or weaving the LegoBlocks into a single sequential model and fine-tuning all parameters for only two epochs on paired samples, enabling cross-modal contextualisation. The design achieves O(dMN) time complexity (linear in modalities M) versus O(M²N²) for pairwise cross-attention baselines, and requires no architectural equivalence between the underlying encoders.

## Results

On seven biomedical datasets spanning survival analysis (c-Index) and classification (AUC), LegoFuse (2 epochs fine-tuning) achieves the best performance on 5 of 7 datasets, including TCGA-BLCA (0.734 c-Index), TCGA-KIRP (0.863 c-Index), and MIMIC-ICD9 (0.771 AUC), while remaining top-2 on all seven. LegoMerge, which requires zero fine-tuning, achieves competitive performance closely matching the best end-to-end baselines (e.g., 0.701 vs. 0.714 c-Index on TCGA-BLCA vs. HEALNet). Training time is dramatically reduced: LegoMerge requires 0 seconds of multimodal training, and LegoFuse requires only 16.2 s total (2 epochs) versus 84–182 s for end-to-end baselines on TCGA-UCEC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LegoFuse outperforms the strongest end-to-end baseline HEALNet on five datasets: TCGA-BLCA (0.734 vs. 0.714 c-Index), TCGA-BRCA (0.626 vs. 0.618), TCGA-KIRP (0.863 vs. 0.842), MIMIC-ICD9 (0.771 vs. 0.767 AUC), and MIMIC-MORT (0.759 vs. 0.748). On TCGA-UCEC, SNN+ABMIL with bilinear fusion leads (0.666) with LegoFuse second (0.634); on SIIM-ISIC, Perceiver and LegoMerge tie at 0.721 AUC while LegoFuse trails slightly at 0.701. Several baselines exhibit multimodal collapse (AUC ≈ 0.500) on MIMIC and TCGA-UCEC: MultiModN, MCAT, and SNN+ABMIL(BL) all fail to learn on at least three datasets, whereas LegoMerge and LegoFuse maintain stable performance across all seven. LegoMerge, with zero fine-tuning, already surpasses MCAT and MultiModN on every dataset and is within 1–2% of HEALNet on most tasks. The paper also shows LegoMerge beats a naive logit-ensemble of SNN+AMIL by 7.1% (ICD9) and 7.3% (MORT), demonstrating that frequency-domain merging captures complementary signal that simple averaging misses.

## Relation to Prior Work

MM-Lego sits at the intersection of three research threads: iterative cross-attention architectures (Perceiver/Perceiver IO), model merging via weight arithmetic (Task Arithmetic, TIES, DARE), and Fourier-based token mixing (FNet). It departs from prior model merging by removing the topological-equivalence constraint—LegoBlock wraps any encoder into a shared latent shape, enabling merging across heterogeneous architectures. It departs from prior fusion methods by eliminating the need for end-to-end multimodal training: LegoMerge aggregates frequency-domain latents with zero fine-tuning, and LegoFuse requires only two epochs. The paper positions itself as the first general-purpose multimodal model-merging framework outside the vision-and-language domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for biomedical settings where paired multimodal data is scarce, modalities arrive at different cardinalities, and retraining end-to-end pipelines is infeasible. The modular design allows adding new modalities without retraining, and the zero-fine-tuning merge eliminates the need for any paired multimodal supervision. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain merging generalises beyond the three modalities tested (image, tabular, time series), whether self-supervised fitting of LegoBlock adapters can remove the small supervised requirement, and whether the approach scales to more than two modalities without performance degradation. The KIRP instability and ISIC multimodal collapse suggest the method still struggles with very small sample sizes and strong modality dominance.
