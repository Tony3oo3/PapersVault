## Problem

Subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) forecasting (2–6 weeks ahead) is a long-standing 'predictability desert' due to atmospheric chaos, and existing data-driven models that treat the spherical graticule as a planar image suffer from geometric distortions: fixed-degree patches have unequal areas across latitudes, and the left-right boundary periodicity of the longitude circle is ignored. These inconsistencies lead to inaccurate spatial relation modeling, particularly in high-latitude regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253662/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single global weather state tensor of shape 121×240×63 (1.5° resolution, 6 pressure-level variables at 10 levels plus 3 single-level variables) and directly outputs two biweekly average tensors of the same spatial shape for weeks 3–4 (days 15–28) and weeks 5–6 (days 29–42). Unlike iterative medium-range models, CirT is trained end-to-end to predict these two targets in one forward pass, avoiding autoregressive error accumulation. The 63 input channels are flattened per patch and projected into a shared latent dimension, so all pressure levels are encoded into a single embedding per latitudinal ring.

## Method

CirT introduces two geometry-inspired designs into a Transformer encoder. First, circular patching decomposes the graticule by latitude into H=121 non-overlapping patches, each a W×K row representing one parallel; this ensures adjacent patches are equidistant and each patch's geometric length is naturally determined by cos(latitude), eliminating the area distortion of fixed-degree grid patches. Second, within each Transformer block, the DFT is applied along the longitude dimension of each patch embedding to convert it into a 2π-periodic frequency representation; multi-head self-attention is then computed on the concatenated real and imaginary parts in the frequency domain, and the IDFT maps the attended representation back to the spatial domain before a feed-forward layer. The model is trained with a simple MSE loss over both biweekly targets, and the entire pipeline (8 layers, 256 hidden dim, 16 heads) requires only 2.2 GFLOPs and 16M parameters, far less than GraphCast or PanguWeather.

## Results

On the ERA5 dataset (1.5° resolution, 63 variables), CirT achieves the lowest latitude-weighted RMSE and highest ACC across all 7 evaluated variables at both Weeks 3-4 and Weeks 5-6. For example, at Weeks 3-4, CirT reduces z500 RMSE to 477 m²/s² (vs. 602 for the best baseline ClimaX) and t500 RMSE to 1.687 K (vs. 2.093 for FourCastNetV2), while achieving ACC of 0.984 and 0.988 respectively. The average RMSE improvement over the best baseline is 96.5 m²/s² for geopotential and 0.369 K for temperature at Weeks 3-4, growing to 111 m²/s² and 0.843 K at Weeks 5-6. CirT also requires only 2.2G FLOPs and 16M parameters, compared to 110T/37M for GraphCast and 168T/256M for PanguWeather.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CirT consistently outperforms all four data-driven baselines (FourCastNetV2, GraphCast, PanguWeather, ClimaX) on every variable and both lead times. The largest gains are over the iterative models: at Weeks 5-6, CirT reduces t500 RMSE from 2.250 (FourCastNetV2) to 1.672 and z500 RMSE from 754 (PanguWeather) to 471, reflecting the error accumulation problem of autoregressive prediction. Among baselines, ClimaX (direct-prediction ViT) is the strongest competitor, while GraphCast (GNN with mesh) is second-best at Weeks 3-4 due to its spherical geometry handling but cannot be evaluated at Weeks 5-6 due to GPU memory limits. CirT also surpasses all four physics-based numerical systems (UKMO, NCEP, CMA, ECMWF) in nearly all pressure-level and variable combinations, with particularly large margins at low pressure levels (e.g., 10 hPa) where numerical models degrade. The direct-prediction paradigm is further validated by an ablation showing that autoregressive CirT (RMSE 781 for z500 W3-4) performs substantially worse than direct CirT (477), confirming that avoiding iterative error accumulation is critical for S2S skill.

## Relation to Prior Work

CirT departs from the dominant paradigm of treating the latitude-longitude graticule as a planar image with fixed-degree patches (as in FourCastNet, PanguWeather, and ClimaX) by introducing a geometric inductive bias: decomposing weather data into latitude-aligned circular patches whose geometric lengths vary with latitude. The Fourier-transform-based self-attention draws on the frequency-domain modeling ideas from FEDformer and Fourier Neural Operators, but applies them specifically to exploit the 2π spatial periodicity inherent in each circular patch. Unlike GraphCast, which uses mesh-based geometry for local message passing, CirT explicitly models global spatial periodicity through its frequency-domain attention. The direct biweekly prediction strategy also contrasts with the iterative autoregressive approach used by most prior data-driven weather models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The extreme computational efficiency (2.2G FLOPs, 16M parameters vs. 110T/37M for GraphCast and 168T/256M for PanguWeather) while outperforming all baselines including operational numerical S2S systems (ECMWF, UKMO, NCEP, CMA) suggests that geometric inductive biases can substitute for brute-force model capacity in weather forecasting. However, the comparison with data-driven baselines is weakened by the fact that FourCastNetV2, PanguWeather, and GraphCast were not fine-tuned for the S2S timescale, limiting the strength of the SOTA claim. Open questions include whether the 2D architecture (which collapses pressure levels into a single embedding) can be extended to 3D to capture vertical dynamics, and whether incorporating slowly evolving earth system components (ocean, land, sea ice) would further improve S2S skill.
