## Problem

End-to-end autonomous driving planners directly consume raw sensor data, but it remains unclear how to develop richer scene feature representations that fully exploit this data. Existing self-supervised approaches in vision focus on static single-frame images and ignore the temporal dynamics and ego actions that are critical in driving, while image-based world models are too slow (relying on diffusion) to serve as practical training signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251539/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N multi-view surround RGB images (resized to 800x320 on nuScenes, 640x320 on NAVSIM, or 900x256 on CARLA) and produces a sequence of M ego-vehicle waypoints in BEV coordinates. In the perception-based variant it additionally outputs agent trajectories and vectorized map elements. Unusually, the predicted waypoints are fed back as action conditioning into the latent world model, which then predicts the visual latents of the next frame, creating a self-supervised loop that requires no extra annotations beyond the trajectory labels.

## Method

LAW introduces a latent world model that predicts future scene latents from current latents and ego waypoints, formulated as a self-supervised task. Concretely, a visual encoder (perspective-view with view-attention queries, or BEV with deformable attention) extracts latent feature vectors; these are concatenated channel-wise with the flattened waypoint vector and passed through an MLP to form action-aware latents. A stack of transformer blocks with self-attention across feature vectors then predicts the next frame's latents, supervised by MSE against the encoder's output on the actual future frame. This latent prediction loss is added to the standard waypoint L1 loss (and perception losses in the perception-based setting), jointly optimizing scene representation quality and trajectory accuracy. The design is framework-agnostic: it predicts perspective-view latents in perception-free pipelines and BEV latents in perception-based pipelines, achieving state-of-the-art results on nuScenes, NAVSIM, and CARLA without requiring any additional annotations.

## Results

On nuScenes, LAW (perception-based) achieves 0.49 m average L2 displacement error and 0.19% average collision rate, outperforming VAD (0.72 m, 0.22%) and BEV-Planner (0.55 m, 0.59%). On NAVSIM, LAW attains a PDMS of 84.6, surpassing TransFuser (84.0), UniAD (83.4), and PARA-Drive (84.0). On the CARLA Town05 Long closed-loop benchmark, LAW achieves a Driving Score of 70.1, exceeding Interfuser (68.3), DriveAdapter (65.9), and ThinkTwice (65.0) while using only expert trajectories plus latent prediction as supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On nuScenes, LAW (perception-based) outperforms all 9 baselines in both L2 displacement and collision rate, with the closest competitor being BEV-Planner (0.55 m vs. 0.49 m avg L2) and VAD (0.72 m, 0.22% collision). On NAVSIM, LAW's 84.6 PDMS narrowly beats TransFuser and PARA-Drive (both 84.0) and UniAD (83.4), with the advantage driven by superior drivable-area compliance (95.4 vs. 92.4–92.8) and ego progress (81.7 vs. 78.8–79.3). On CARLA Town05 Long, LAW (70.1 DS) surpasses the previous best Interfuser (68.3) by 1.8 points and DriveAdapter (65.9) by 4.2 points, despite using far less supervision (only expert trajectories + latent prediction vs. depth, segmentation, detection, and map annotations). Notably, LAW's perception-free variant already outperforms ThinkTwice (65.0) and DriveAdapter (65.9) on CARLA, demonstrating that the self-supervised latent prediction task compensates for the absence of explicit perception annotations. Across all three benchmarks, LAW consistently wins, with the largest margins observed against older baselines (NMP, CILRS, LBC) and the smallest against the most recent competitors (BEV-Planner, PARA-Drive, Interfuser).

## Relation to Prior Work

LAW builds on the perception-free paradigm of TCP (Wu et al., 2022) and the perception-based vectorized representation of VAD (Jiang et al., 2023), adopting their respective encoder-decoder structures (PETR-style perspective-view features and BEVFormer-style BEV features). Its key departure is from image-based driving world models (GAIA-1, Drive-WM, Copilot4D) that rely on diffusion models to generate future pixel observations; instead, LAW predicts future *latent* features directly, eliminating the need for expensive image synthesis and occupancy annotations. The self-supervised framing draws from MAE-style masked prediction in CV and BERT in NLP, but introduces ego-action conditioning as a critical input to the world model, which prior temporal self-supervised tasks in driving largely overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LAW provides a simple, annotation-free training objective that simultaneously improves scene representation quality and trajectory prediction, making it a broadly applicable plug-in for end-to-end driving pipelines regardless of whether they use perception tasks. The universality across perception-free and perception-based frameworks, combined with SOTA results on nuScenes, NAVSIM, and CARLA, suggests latent future prediction is an underexploited signal for driving. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer prediction horizons (the 10s experiment shows gains vanish), whether the latent space truly captures causal dynamics or merely correlates with future appearance, and whether the benefits persist in fully reactive closed-loop settings with interacting agents.
