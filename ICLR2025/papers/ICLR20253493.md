## Problem

Graph Transformers rely on structural or positional encodings to inject graph inductive bias, yet the theoretical expressiveness of these encodings remains poorly understood. The most widely used encoding, random-walk structural encoding (RWSE), is provably confined within 2-WL and is incomparable to 1-WL at the node level, meaning it cannot distinguish certain node pairs that even basic message passing can separate. The paper asks whether a more flexible, theoretically grounded encoding can provably exceed RWSE's expressiveness while remaining practical.

## Input / Output

Given an input graph H, a finite family of pattern graphs G = {G_1, …, G_d}, and a node-weighting function ω, MoSE produces for each node v a d-dimensional vector of weighted rooted homomorphism counts ω-Hom_{→v}(G_i, H). This vector is concatenated or added to the node's initial feature embedding before being fed into a Transformer. The graph-level encoding is the multiset of all node vectors, and the entire encoding is precomputed in O(|G|^{tw(F)}) time per pattern F, taking only 0.01–0.08 seconds per graph in practice.

## Method

MoSE encodes each node by counting weighted homomorphisms from a chosen family of small pattern graphs into the input graph, rooted at that node. The key design flexibility lies in two parameters: the pattern family G (e.g., Spasm(C_7) ∪ Spasm(C_8) for molecules, or all connected graphs up to 5 nodes for images) and the node-weighting function ω (constant-1 for unweighted counts, or 1/d(v) to recover RWSE). Theoretically, the paper proves that MoSE is incomparable to any fixed k-WL level (Propositions 4.1–4.2), that it subsumes all graph motif parameters whose basis is contained in G (Proposition 4.3), and that RWSE is a strict special case of MoSE using cycle patterns with degree-based weighting (Proposition 4.5, Theorem 4.6). Empirically, MoSE is plugged into GPS, GRIT, GIN, and plain MLP/Transformer architectures as a drop-in replacement for RWSE or LapPE, yielding consistent MAE improvements across ZINC, QM9, PCQM4Mv2, CIFAR-10, MNIST, and Peptides benchmarks.

## Results

On ZINC-12K, GPS+MoSE achieves MAE 0.062±0.002, outperforming GPS+RWSE (0.070±0.002) by over 10% relative improvement and GPS+LapPE (0.116±0.009) by nearly 2x. Replacing RRWP with MoSE in the GRIT architecture yields state-of-the-art MAE of 0.056±0.001 on ZINC-12K, surpassing GRIT+RRWP (0.059±0.002) and Subgraphormer+PE (0.063±0.001). On QM9, GPS+MoSE achieves the best MAE on 11 of 13 molecular properties, and on CIFAR-10 it reaches 73.50% accuracy versus 72.31% for LapPE and 71.96% for RWSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoSE consistently outperforms RWSE across all tested architectures on ZINC-12K: GPS (0.062 vs 0.070 MAE), GT-E (0.089 vs 0.104), GIN-E+VN (0.068 vs 0.085), and MLP-E (0.347 vs 0.361). Against spectral positional encodings, MoSE substantially beats LapPE (0.062 vs 0.116 MAE on ZINC; 73.50% vs 72.31% on CIFAR-10) and both SignNet variants (0.062 vs 0.079/0.090 MAE on ZINC). On the GRIT architecture, MoSE replaces RRWP to achieve SOTA on ZINC (0.056 vs 0.059 MAE), surpassing even message-passing-based models like Subgraphormer+PE (0.063) and GPS (0.070). On QM9, GPS+MoSE dominates leading GNNs including R-SPN and E-BasePlanE (isomorphism-complete on planar graphs) on 11 of 13 properties, while GPS+RWSE is occasionally outperformed by these GNNs. Notably, GIN-E+VN with MoSE (0.068 MAE) becomes competitive with full Graph Transformer models, highlighting MoSE's ability to supplement MPNN architectures with long-range structural information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes homomorphism counts—previously used to enhance MPNN expressivity (Barceló et al., 2021; Jin et al., 2024)—as a structural encoding mechanism for Graph Transformers, a context where prior work had focused on spectral (LapPE) or random-walk (RWSE) encodings. The weighted homomorphism count formulation from Nguyen & Maehara (2020) provides the mathematical backbone of the MoSE definition. The key departure is the theoretical expressiveness analysis: the paper positions MoSE relative to the WL hierarchy and proves that RWSE, the empirically dominant encoding, is strictly bounded by 2-WL and incomparable to 1-WL at the node level—results not previously established for any structural encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoSE provides a theoretically grounded, flexible, and computationally cheap (sub-second per graph) structural encoding that consistently outperforms RWSE and LapPE across molecular, image, and synthetic benchmarks, achieving SOTA on ZINC when paired with GRIT. The expressiveness guarantees (MoSE not confined to any single WL level, while RWSE is bounded by 2-WL) give practitioners principled guidance for selecting pattern graph families. Open questions include the relationship between MoSE and spectral methods, the full potential of non-trivial node-weighting schemes, and whether the expressiveness gap translates to gains on larger-scale or more structurally complex graph tasks.
