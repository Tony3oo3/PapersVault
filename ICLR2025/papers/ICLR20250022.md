## Problem

Existing multimodal knowledge editing benchmarks represent knowledge as simple (subject, relation, object) triplets focused on entity recognition, failing to capture the complexity of real-world visual knowledge such as actions, gestures, object relationships, and personalized information. Moreover, these benchmarks have been saturated—simple fine-tuning of LLaVA achieves near-perfect scores on VLKEB—leaving no meaningful signal to drive methodological progress.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250022/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each knowledge item is a pair k = (i, d) consisting of an RGB image and a free-form natural language description (not a triplet), covering 2,940 items and 8,363 images across 33 broad categories. Evaluation questions are generated per four criteria—reliability, locality, generalization, and portability—and the edited LMM produces a short text answer (typically one or two words) that is scored for exact match. For image-based questions, the main object is referenced by its type (e.g., 'the person in the image') rather than by name, and portability questions combine two images of the same type into a single composite input.

## Method

MMKE-Bench introduces a four-stage construction pipeline: (1) collecting original knowledge from MMpedia, OVEN, demo videos, Google, and LLM generation; (2) generating editing knowledge via counterfactual text modification (altering key entity attributes or semantic rules) combined with same-type image replacement for the visual modality; (3) automatically generating evaluation questions guided by four principles (reliability, locality, generalization, portability) using LLM prompts; and (4) human verification and revision. The benchmark defines three editing types aligned with real-world needs: visual entity editing (correcting entity-centric facts), visual semantic editing (modifying complex visual semantics like referee gestures or traffic signals), and user-specific editing (injecting entirely new personalized knowledge without counterfactual modification). Five editing methods—FT-LLM, FT-Alignment, KE, MEND, SERAC, and IKE—are evaluated on three LMMs (BLIP-2, MiniGPT-4, LLaVA-1.5) under both single and sequential editing settings, revealing that no method dominates across all criteria and that visual semantic and user-specific edits remain particularly challenging.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5 (the strongest LMM tested), no single editing method dominates all four evaluation criteria. IKE achieves the best reliability and generalization (avg. T-Rel 67.56%, I-Rel 61.54%, I-Gen 61.36%), while SERAC preserves locality best (T-Loc 99.92%, I-Loc 99.68%) and KE leads in portability (avg. 32.17%). Visual semantic editing and user-specific editing are substantially harder than visual entity editing, with portability scores dropping from ~41% (entity) to ~14% (semantic) and ~13% (user-specific) for FT-LLM. In sequential editing, FT-LLM and FT-Alignment progressively forget earlier edits as the gap grows, whereas SERAC maintains near-constant performance due to its explicit memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IKE (in-context knowledge editing) wins on reliability and generalization across all three LMMs, e.g., achieving 67.56% T-Rel and 61.36% I-Gen on LLaVA-1.5 versus 53.75% and 48.45% for FT-LLM, but it sacrifices locality (65.28% T-Loc vs. 99.92% for SERAC). SERAC (memory-based) dominates locality with near-perfect scores (99.92% T-Loc, 99.68% I-Loc) but lags in reliability (35.65% T-Rel) and generalization (33.61% I-Gen). MEND (meta-learning) offers a middle ground with strong locality (98.03% T-Loc, 81.20% I-Loc) and moderate reliability (47.81% T-Rel). KE (hypernetwork-based) achieves the best portability (32.17% avg. on LLaVA) but weak image locality (12.49%). FT-LLM serves as a strong simple baseline, outperforming SERAC and MEND on reliability while being far behind IKE, and it degrades noticeably in sequential editing as the gap increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal knowledge editing benchmark lineage (MMEdit, MIKE, MC-MKE, VLKEB) by departing from the triplet-based (subject, relation, object) knowledge representation that all prior benchmarks rely on, replacing it with free-form natural language paired with images. It directly builds on VLKEB's four-principle evaluation framework (reliability, locality, generalization, portability) and its image-replacement editing strategy, but broadens the scope from entity-level recognition to three distinct editing types: visual entity, visual semantic (gestures, traffic signals, object relationships), and user-specific knowledge injection. The methods evaluated (KE, MEND, SERAC, IKE, FT) are drawn from the established LLM knowledge editing literature and adapted to the multimodal setting, following the protocol set by VLKEB.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical gap: existing methods that achieve near-perfect scores on triplet-based benchmarks (e.g., 99.59% reliability on VLKEB) drop dramatically on MMKE-Bench, revealing that entity-level editing is largely solved while complex visual semantics and personalized knowledge remain open problems. The finding that no single method dominates across all four evaluation criteria—with in-context methods best at reproduction, memory-based methods best at preservation, and parameter-based methods best at portability—suggests that a unified editing solution for LMMs is still far off. Open questions include how to effectively edit abstract visual semantics (referee gestures, traffic rules) and how to stably accumulate user-specific knowledge in sequential settings without catastrophic forgetting.
