## Problem

Predicting a molecule's 3D structure from its molecular formula and moments of inertia is a severely underconstrained inverse problem (3 values → 3n coordinates). Existing Euclidean diffusion models condition on moments only softly, failing to exploit the many digits of precision available from rotational spectroscopy. This paper shows that the feasible space of moment-constrained structures is a totally geodesic submanifold of the Stiefel manifold St(n,4), enabling generative modeling that satisfies moment constraints exactly by construction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250761/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a molecular formula (atomic numbers and masses for n atoms) and three planar moments of inertia (P_X > P_Y > P_Z > 0) as conditioning, and generates all-atom 3D coordinates in the principal-axis coordinate system. The output is constrained to a 3n−9 dimensional submanifold of St(n,4) where the last column is fixed to the unit mass vector, enforcing 9 constraints simultaneously: three moment equalities, three center-of-mass conditions, and three off-diagonal (principal-axis) conditions. Success is measured by RMSD < 0.25 Å or < 0.10 Å after optimal permutation and reflection alignment against ground truth.

## Method

Stiefel Flow Matching formulates structure elucidation as Riemannian flow matching on the Stiefel manifold St(n,4) with the canonical metric, where the feasible space M is a totally geodesic submanifold (homeomorphic to St(n−1,3)). Training is simulation-free: geodesic interpolation between uniform Stiefel noise and data samples is computed via the exponential and logarithmic maps, and the velocity target is derived analytically from the logarithmic map rather than autodifferentiation. The network is a reflection-equivariant Transformer-based GNN that outputs tangent vectors projected onto T_U M, with equivariance to node permutations and sign-flips of the principal axes. An equivariant optimal transport objective aligns noise-to-data pairs via atom-type-preserving permutations and reflections, shortening generation paths (reducing average curve length from 1.696 to 1.547 on QM9) and improving accuracy on GEOM.

## Results

On QM9, Stiefel FM achieves the highest structure elucidation success rate of 15.17% at RMSD < 0.25 Å and 13.82% at RMSD < 0.10 Å, with zero moment constraint violation (Error = 0.00), using only 200 NFE (20% of the 1000 NFE used by diffusion baselines). On GEOM (larger molecules, ~46 atoms), Stiefel FM-OT with validity filtering achieves the best success rate of 3.94% at 0.25 Å and 2.42% at 0.10 Å, again with exact moment satisfaction, at 600 NFE. Combining KREED-XL, Stiefel FM, and Stiefel FM-OT, the paper elucidates 27.4% of QM9 test molecules and 7.9% of GEOM test molecules at 0.25 Å resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is KREED (Cheng et al., 2024), a reflection-equivariant Euclidean diffusion model for the same task, along with its larger variant KREED-XL (matching Stiefel FM's architecture) and two guided/projected variants (KREED-XL-DPS, KREED-XL-proj). On QM9, Stiefel FM outperforms all KREED variants in success rate (15.17% vs. 13.67% for KREED-XL-proj at 0.25 Å) while using only 20% of the NFE (200 vs. 1000) and achieving exact moment satisfaction (Error = 0.00 vs. 3.64–5.18). On GEOM, the advantage is more nuanced: KREED-XL generates more valid structures (0.907 vs. 0.388 for Stiefel FM) due to the harder manifold-constrained generation, but after filtering to equal validity, Stiefel FM-OT (filter) achieves the highest success rate (3.94% vs. 3.54% for KREED-XL at 0.25 Å) at lower cost (600 vs. 1000 NFE). The DPS-guided variant (KREED-XL-DPS) improves moment agreement (Error 1.33 vs. 3.64) but sacrifices accuracy (12.36% vs. 13.65%), a tradeoff that Stiefel FM avoids entirely by construction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Riemannian flow matching (Chen & Lipman, 2024) to the Stiefel manifold and combines it with equivariant optimal transport (Klein et al., 2023; Song et al., 2023c) to solve a structure elucidation problem previously addressed by KREED (Cheng et al., 2024) using Euclidean diffusion with soft moment conditioning. The key conceptual departure is showing that the feasible space of moment-constrained structures is a totally geodesic submanifold of St(n,4), which converts a soft-constraint inverse problem into an unconstrained generative modeling problem on a Riemannian manifold. The reflection-equivariant GNN architecture and the use of the canonical metric for geodesic computation follow established tools from equivariant deep learning and Stiefel geometry literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact constraint satisfaction via the Stiefel embedding is a clean theoretical contribution that could transfer to other orthogonality-constrained generative tasks (molecular orbitals, orthogonal network weights, covariance matrices). However, the paper reveals a significant open problem: Riemannian flow matching underfits on the larger GEOM dataset (46 atoms, 129 degrees of freedom), likely due to known pathologies of geodesic-based velocity fields (cut-locus discontinuity, shrinking support), and the authors identify Stiefel diffusion or alternative probability paths as promising remedies. The modest success rates (15% on QM9, ~4% on GEOM) reflect the extreme underconstraint of the problem, and incorporating additional experimental data (dipole moments, 2D fragments, energy) remains necessary for practical structure elucidation campaigns.
