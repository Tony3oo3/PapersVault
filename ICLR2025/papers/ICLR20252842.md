## Problem

Current structure-based drug design (SBDD) models are evaluated almost exclusively on Vina docking scores, which are easily inflated by increasing atom count, adding hydroxyl groups, or manipulating halogen content, and show poor correlation with actual binding affinity. This creates a disconnect between theoretical performance and practical utility: generated molecules may outperform reference ligands in docking scores yet fail to resemble known actives, serve as useful virtual screening templates, or be synthesizable. The paper asks whether SBDD models can be meaningfully assessed by metrics that directly reflect real-world drug discovery workflows.

## Input / Output

The evaluation pipeline consumes 3D protein pockets extracted from real crystal structures (10 Å radius around the reference ligand), 20 generated molecules per target from each SBDD model, and per-target sets of experimentally validated actives (~224 on average) and structurally analogous decoys (~50 per active) from DUD-E. It produces three tiers of scores: (1) max cosine similarity between each generated molecule and all known actives using four encoders (1024-dim ECFP/E3FP bit vectors, Uni-Mol, DrugCLIP), (2) BEDROC₈₅ and enrichment factor from ligand-based virtual screening where generated molecules serve as query templates against the active/decoy library, and (3) binding affinity estimates including Vina docking score, Delta Score (Glide SP/XP docking to target minus docking to a random other target), and DrugCLIP score. The test set comprises 101 targets from DUD-E with real PDBbind conformations, filtered via FLAPP pocket alignment (threshold 0.6 or 0.9) to prevent structural overlap with training data.

## Method

The paper introduces a three-level evaluation framework that replaces reliance on Vina docking scores with metrics tied to practical drug discovery workflows. Level 1 (Similarity to Known Actives) maps molecules into shared feature spaces via 2D ECFP, 3D E3FP, Uni-Mol, and DrugCLIP encoders, then computes the maximum inner-product similarity between a generated molecule and all experimentally confirmed actives for that target, capturing whether the molecule could be rationally modified into a viable candidate. Level 2 (Virtual Screening Ability) uses each generated molecule as a reference template to rank a library of validated actives and decoys by similarity, reporting BEDROC₈₅ and EF; this metric is directly correlated with wet-lab hit rates, unlike docking scores. Level 3 (Binding Affinity) retains Vina docking but adds the Delta Score—averaging the difference between docking to the intended target and a randomly sampled other target across Glide SP and XP—to isolate specific binding contribution from generic score inflation, plus DrugCLIP as a learned scoring function. The benchmark is built on real PDBbind crystal structures (not CrossDocked's synthesized complexes) with FLAPP-based pocket deduplication, and is validated by showing that ligand-based virtual screening with real actives outperforms Vina docking (EF 24.95 vs. 7.32), confirming the practical relevance of the proposed metrics.

## Results

On the proposed practical evaluation framework (101 DUD-E targets), MolCRAFT is the best-performing SBDD model, achieving a virtual screening enrichment factor (EF) of 5.549 with the DrugCLIP encoder (PDBbind90 training set), compared to the reference ligand's EF of 29.23. While TargetDiff and MolCRAFT surpass the reference ligand in Vina docking score (−9.711 and −9.778 vs. −9.363), they lag substantially on the Delta Score (1.163 and 0.973 vs. 2.686 for reference) and DrugCLIP binding score (0.173 and 0.145 vs. 0.508). On similarity to known actives, MolCRAFT reaches 0.547 DrugCLIP similarity versus 0.870 for the reference ligand, and on 2D fingerprint similarity, 0.214 vs. 0.588. All models fall far short of reference ligands across every practical metric, revealing a large gap between theoretical docking performance and real-world utility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MolCRAFT outperforms all other SBDD baselines (LiGAN, AR, Pocket2Mol, TargetDiff) across every virtual screening metric and encoder, achieving the highest EF of 5.549 (DrugCLIP) and 4.825 (2D fingerprints) on PDBbind90. TargetDiff, despite achieving the best Vina docking score among models (−9.711), performs poorly on practical metrics: its Delta Score (0.238) is the lowest among all models, and its virtual screening EF (2.117 DrugCLIP) is below even the simple AR baseline (2.139), suggesting its high docking score stems from overfitting to large atom counts. Pocket2Mol ranks second on fingerprint-based virtual screening (EF 3.240, 2D FP) but drops to third on deep-encoder metrics (EF 1.541 DrugCLIP), indicating its autoregressive generation of smaller molecules limits its discriminative power. LiGAN is consistently the weakest model across all metrics. The optimization-based RGA method improves Vina score from −9.000 to −9.665 after iterative refinement but actually decreases its virtual screening EF from 1.951 to 1.850, confirming that docking-score optimization does not translate to practical utility. All models remain far below the reference ligand on every practical metric, with the largest gaps in virtual screening (5.5× lower EF for MolCRAFT vs. reference) and similarity to known actives (0.547 vs. 0.870 DrugCLIP similarity).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard SBDD evaluation paradigm that relies primarily on AutoDock Vina docking scores, instead drawing on ideas from ligand-based virtual screening (Shen et al., 2024), manual medicinal chemistry workflows (Moret et al., 2023), and the delta score concept (Gao et al., 2024) to propose a three-level practical evaluation framework. It re-evaluates existing SBDD models (LiGAN, AR, Pocket2Mol, TargetDiff, MolCRAFT) on a new test set derived from DUD-E crystal structures with multiple validated actives and decoys per target, replacing the CrossDocked benchmark that the field has relied on. The paper does not introduce a new generative model but rather reframes how existing models should be assessed, positioning similarity-to-actives and virtual screening enrichment as primary metrics over docking scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a substantial and consistent gap between theoretical (Vina) and practical (virtual screening, similarity) performance across all tested models, with the best model (MolCRAFT) achieving only ~16% of the reference ligand's virtual screening enrichment factor. This challenges the field's working assumption that beating reference ligands on Vina scores indicates practical readiness, and suggests that current SBDD models are far less deployable than previously reported. Open questions include whether models can be explicitly trained to optimize for virtual screening utility or similarity to known actives, whether the proposed metrics generalize beyond the 101+15 targets tested, and whether the field will adopt this evaluation framework. The finding that MolCRAFT's DrugCLIP-based virtual screening (EF 5.549) approaches Vina's screening performance (EF 7.32) while being orders of magnitude faster suggests a practical near-term pathway for SBDD-generated molecules in drug discovery pipelines.
