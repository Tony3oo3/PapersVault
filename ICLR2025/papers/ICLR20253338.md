## Problem

While many recent MLLMs adopt mixture-of-vision-encoder strategies to improve perception, there is no systematic study decoupling the fusion architecture from the encoder improvements, nor clear guidance on expert selection and integration. The paper asks: which fusion strategies actually matter, how should complementary vision experts be combined, and what training recipes are needed to make multi-encoder MLLMs work effectively?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253338/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (up to 1024×1024) and a text prompt, producing a free-form text response. Multiple vision encoders at different native resolutions (CLIP at 448×448, ConvNeXt/SAM/Pix2Struct/EVA-02 at 1024×1024) each produce a 2D feature map that is resized to yield 1024 visual tokens; these token sets are concatenated along the channel dimension so the total sequence length remains 1024 regardless of how many encoders are used. The concatenated visual tokens are projected into the LLM's embedding space and prepended to the text token sequence.

## Method

EAGLE's core finding is that simple channel concatenation of visual tokens from complementary vision encoders outperforms more complex fusion strategies (sequence append, LLaVA-HR injection, Mini-Gemini cross-attention, deformable attention) while keeping the visual token count fixed at 1024. A key architectural addition is a Pre-Alignment stage where each non-text-aligned vision expert (e.g., SAM, Pix2Struct, EVA-02) is individually fine-tuned with its own projector against a frozen Vicuna-7B before all experts are jointly trained, mitigating representational inconsistencies between encoders. The optimal encoder combination (CLIP + ConvNeXt + EVA-02 + Pix2Struct + SAM) is identified via a round-robin greedy search that adds one expert per round, retaining the best-performing combination. Training follows three progressive stages: (1) per-expert pre-alignment on LLaVA-595K + Eagle1.8M, (2) joint projector and vision-encoder training on the same data, and (3) full-model supervised fine-tuning on Eagle1.8M, with vision encoders unfrozen throughout stages 2–3. High-resolution adaptation is achieved by interpolating CLIP's position embeddings to 448×448 rather than using tiling, balancing efficiency and performance.

## Results

Eagle-X5 with Pre-Alignment achieves state-of-the-art results across 13 benchmarks using a Vicuna-7B backbone: 1582 on MME, 73.7 on SEED, 88.7 on POPE (hallucination), 566 on OCRBench, and 71.9 on TextVQA. With the Vicuna-13B backbone, Eagle-X5 (+Pre-Align) further improves to 1605 MME, 74.9 SEED, 89.2 POPE, 598 OCR, and 73.3 TextVQA. On the Cambrian-1 shared-data comparison, Eagle-X5 outperforms Cambrian-1 by 3.9 points on the Knowledge average (65.2 vs. 61.3) and 5.7 points on the OCR/Chart average (77.0 vs. 71.3) with Llama3-8B, demonstrating consistent gains from the mixture-of-encoders design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Eagle-X5 (+Pre-Align) consistently outperforms all compared baselines on the majority of benchmarks. Against LLaVA-1.5, Eagle gains +72 on MME (1582 vs. 1510), +15.1 on SEED (73.7 vs. 58.6), +269 on OCR (566 vs. 297), and +13.7 on TextVQA (71.9 vs. 58.2) with Vicuna-7B. Versus LLaVA-NeXt, Eagle leads on MME (+63), SEED (+3.5), MathVista (+3.6), MMMU (+2.2), OCR (+76), and TextVQA (+7.0), while LLaVA-NeXt edges ahead on GQA (64.2 vs. 64.6 is actually Eagle winning by 0.4). Against LLaVA-HR, Eagle surpasses on MME (+28), SEED (+9.5), VizWiz (+10.0), and TextVQA (+4.8), though LLaVA-HR reports a slightly higher POPE score (87.6 vs. 88.7 is Eagle winning). Compared to Mini-Gemini, Eagle leads on MME (+59), MMB (+3.9), MathVista (+6.0), MMMU (+1.2), OCR (+89), and TextVQA (+6.7). On the Cambrian-1 shared-data comparison (Table 8), Eagle-X5 beats Cambrian-1 on every category average: Knowledge (+3.9), General (+0.9), OCR/Chart (+5.7), and Vision-Centric (+2.0) with Llama3-8B, confirming that the mixture-of-encoders design itself—rather than data differences—drives the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA-1.5 architecture (vision encoder + projection layer + LLM) and extends the growing line of work on mixture-of-vision-encoders for MLLMs (Mini-Gemini, LLaVA-HR, Mousi, Prismatic VLM, Brave). It departs from prior work in two key conceptual ways: (1) it finds that unlocking vision encoders during training consistently improves performance, contradicting the common freezing practice in the LLaVA family and most multi-encoder MLLMs; and (2) it demonstrates that simple channel concatenation of visual tokens outperforms more elaborate fusion strategies (cross-attention, deformable attention, mixture-of-resolution adapters), challenging the assumption that architectural complexity in the fusion layer is necessary. The pre-alignment stage—individually finetuning each vision expert with a frozen LLM before joint training—is a novel training recipe not present in prior multi-encoder MLLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides a systematic, reproducible design recipe for building MLLMs with multiple vision encoders, filling a gap where prior work proposed various fusion architectures without controlled ablations. The practical takeaway—that channel concatenation plus pre-alignment plus unlocked encoders is a strong, simple baseline—gives the community a clear starting point rather than a menu of complex alternatives. Limitations include the greedy (not globally optimal) round-robin encoder selection, the added inference cost of multiple encoders (throughput drops from ~197 to ~4 img/sec as encoders are added), and evaluation primarily on 7B/13B LLMs. Open questions include whether these findings transfer to much larger LLM backbones, whether the approach composes with adaptive tiling for ultra-high-resolution inputs, and whether the marginal gains from 5→6 encoders justify the additional compute.
