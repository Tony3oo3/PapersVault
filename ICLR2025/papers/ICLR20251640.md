## Problem

Existing prototype-based interpretability methods (ProtoPNet, HPnet) learn prototypes over flat class structures or hierarchical trees but suffer from three critical gaps for discovering evolutionary traits: they learn over-specific prototypes at internal nodes that cover only one descendant species rather than shared features, they lack constraints ensuring prototypes are absent in contrasting species from different ancestral lineages, and they cannot abstain from reporting common traits at high tree levels without degrading leaf-level classification accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB organism images (resized to 224×224, with background removed via segmentation masks) and a known phylogenetic tree as input. At each internal node, it produces a set of prototype vectors (β per child node), spatial prototype score maps (26×26 heatmaps), a classification logit for each child via path-probability multiplication across tree levels, and a learned Gumbel-Softmax mask per prototype indicating whether it is over-specific. The number of prototypes at each node scales linearly with the number of children, and the tree need not be discretized or balanced.

## Method

HComP-Net uses a shared ConvNeXt-tiny backbone (modified stride to yield 26×26 feature maps) followed by per-node prototype layers that compute patch-prototype similarity, apply softmax, global max-pool, and feed a non-negative linear classifier restricted to child nodes. Three novel mechanisms distinguish it from prior work: (1) an over-specificity loss (L_ovsp) that is a per-descendant-species modification of the tanh-loss, forcing each prototype to activate in every descendant species within a mini-batch; (2) a discriminative loss (L_disc) that penalizes prototype activation on contrasting species from sibling subtrees; and (3) a Gumbel-Softmax masking module that learns binary masks identifying over-specific prototypes at inference via a stop-gradient over-specificity score, allowing the model to abstain from reporting traits at high tree levels without affecting training or classification. Training proceeds in two phases: self-supervised pre-training with PIP-Net's alignment and tanh losses for 10 epochs, followed by supervised fine-tuning with the full combined loss (CE + self-supervised + over-specificity + discriminative + orthogonality + masking) for 60 epochs.

## Results

On the Bird dataset (190 species, CUB-200-2011), HComP-Net achieves 70.01% fine-grained accuracy, dramatically outperforming the only other hierarchical baseline HPnet (36.18%) while remaining competitive with flat methods like ProtoPNet (75.80%) and ResNet-50 (74.18%). On the Spider dataset (113 species, iNat2021), HComP-Net reaches 76.19% versus HPnet's 5.85%, and on Turtle (41 species) it achieves 58.26% versus HPnet's 6.38%. In terms of prototype semantic quality (part purity), HComP-Net with over-specificity loss and masking achieves 0.77±0.16, far exceeding HPnet's 0.14±0.09. For generalization to unseen species, HComP-Net outperforms HPnet on all four held-out bird species (e.g., 96.67% vs 49.2% for Indigo Bunting).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HComP-Net is the only method besides HPnet that enforces a hierarchical tree structure, and it decisively outperforms HPnet on every dataset: 70.01% vs 36.18% on Bird, 76.19% vs 5.85% on Spider, and 58.26% vs 6.38% on Turtle. Against flat baselines, HComP-Net trails ConvNeXt-tiny and PIPNet on Bird (70.01% vs 84.23%/83.38%) and Spider (76.19% vs 84.05%/83.45%), but is competitive on Butterfly (97.35% vs 97.87% PIPNet) and Fish (90.80% vs 93.58% PIPNet). The paper explicitly notes that its primary objective is discovering semantically meaningful evolutionary traits rather than maximizing classification accuracy, and on the part purity metric (0.77±0.16) it far exceeds HPnet (0.14±0.09), confirming that the hierarchical prototypes are locally consistent and interpretable. On the unseen-species generalization task, HComP-Net surpasses HPnet by 20–47 percentage points across all four held-out bird species, demonstrating that the learned hierarchy transfers to novel taxa.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototypical network lineage from ProtoPNet (Chen et al., 2019) through HPnet (Hase et al., 2019), which first introduced hierarchical prototype layers at every internal node of a class tree, and PIP-Net (Nauta et al., 2023), which added self-supervised contrastive learning for semantic consistency. The key departure is identifying and solving the 'over-specificity' problem at internal nodes—where existing methods learn prototypes that cover only one descendant species rather than shared traits—via a novel over-specificity loss, a discriminative loss enforcing absence in contrasting clades, and a Gumbel-Softmax masking module that allows abstention at higher tree levels. Unlike ProtoPShare/ProtoPool which share prototypes for sparsity, HComP-Net's goal is explicitly to find synapomorphies (shared derived traits) absent in sister clades, a biologically motivated formulation not addressed by prior prototype methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in hierarchical prototype methods: the inability to distinguish truly shared evolutionary traits from over-specific features that merely cover individual descendants. The masking module's ability to abstain at higher phylogenetic levels without harming leaf-node classification is a practical contribution for domains where commonalities are sparse. However, semantic interpretation of prototypes still relies on human expertise, the phylogenies used (190 bird species) may not capture all relevant homology, and the method does not handle convergent evolution. The broader applicability to any hierarchical classification domain is noted but not empirically validated beyond biology.
