## Problem

RAG models can still hallucinate even when the retrieved context is accurate and relevant, yet existing detection methods either rely solely on parametric knowledge signals, solely on external context, or mix both without decoupling their intertwined contributions. No prior work has used mechanistic interpretability to disentangle how LLMs internally utilize external versus parametric knowledge as a basis for hallucination detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251808/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the LLM's internal states during autoregressive generation—specifically per-head attention weights, per-layer hidden states, and FFN input/output residual stream states—alongside the query and retrieved context. At the token level, it computes per-token scores from hidden-state cosine similarities and LogitLens-based JSD; at the chunk level, it segments context and response into ~256-token spans and uses BGE embeddings for cross-chunk semantic similarity. The output is a continuous hallucination score (thresholded to a binary label), and AARF additionally produces a modified generation by reweighting specific attention heads and FFN layers in the residual stream without any parameter updates.

## Method

The core insight is that RAG hallucinations arise from two decoupled internal mechanisms: Knowledge FFNs in later layers excessively inject parametric knowledge into the residual stream, while Copying Heads (identified via OV-circuit eigenvalue analysis) fail to retain or integrate external context. ReDeEP quantifies these via two metrics—External Context Score (cosine similarity between mean-pooled hidden states of top-attended context tokens and the current token's hidden state) and Parametric Knowledge Score (Jensen-Shannon divergence between LogitLens vocabulary distributions before and after each FFN layer)—then combines them as covariates in a multivariate linear regression to predict hallucination, explicitly addressing the confounding between parametric and external knowledge. AARF extends this into an inference-time intervention: when the per-token hallucination score exceeds a threshold τ, it amplifies Copying Head outputs by a factor α₂ > 1 and attenuates Knowledge FFN outputs by a factor β₂ ∈ (0,1), shifting the residual stream toward external context without updating any model parameters.

## Results

On RAGTruth with LLaMA2-13B, ReDeEP(chunk) achieves the best AUC of 0.8244 and F1 of 0.7587, outperforming the strongest baseline (SEP, AUC 0.8089) by ~1.6 AUC points. On Dolly (AC) with LLaMA2-13B, ReDeEP(chunk) reaches AUC 0.8420 and F1 0.7603, surpassing the best ECP baseline (ChainPoll, AUC 0.7070) by 13.5 AUC points. ReDeEP(chunk) consistently outperforms ReDeEP(token) across both datasets, with the largest gap on Dolly (AC) where chunk-level processing yields AUC 0.8420 vs. 0.7226 for token-level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReDeEP(chunk) outperforms all 15 baselines across both datasets on AUC and PCC. Among MPE methods (SelfCheckGPT, Perplexity, LN-Entropy, Energy, Focus), ReDeEP achieves the largest margins, e.g., AUC 0.8244 vs. Focus's 0.6233 on RAGTruth (+20.1 points), confirming that uncertainty/sampling-based signals without decoupling external and parametric knowledge are insufficient. Among ECP methods (Prompt, LMvLM, ChainPoll, RAGAS, Trulens, RefCheck), ReDeEP surpasses the strongest (RefCheck, AUC 0.7857) by 3.9 AUC points on RAGTruth and ChainPoll (AUC 0.7070) by 13.5 points on Dolly (AC), showing that LLM-judge approaches remain confounded by parametric knowledge leaking into the response. Among PCE methods (P(True), EigenScore, SEP, SAPLA), ReDeEP beats the best (SEP, AUC 0.8089) by 1.6 AUC points on RAGTruth, but the gap narrows compared to MPE/ECP, suggesting hidden-state-based probes capture some signal but still conflate external context with parametric knowledge. Notably, ReDeEP(chunk) also achieves the highest Recall (0.8518 on Dolly, 0.7198 on RAGTruth) and F1, indicating it is particularly effective at catching hallucinated responses that other methods miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the mechanistic interpretability framework of Elhage et al. (2021), which established the residual stream perspective and identified Copying Heads via OV circuit eigenvalue analysis, and on the Logit Lens technique (nostalgebraist, 2020) for decoding intermediate hidden states into vocabulary distributions. It departs from prior hallucination detection methods (SelfCheckGPT, RAGAS, EigenScore, SEP, etc.) by framing the detection problem through a causal lens: existing methods either conflate parametric and external knowledge or treat one as a confounder of the other, whereas ReDeEP explicitly decouples the two using attention-head-level and FFN-layer-level scores as covariates in a multivariate regression. The paper is the first to apply mechanistic interpretability specifically to RAG hallucination detection, bridging the transformer circuits literature with the RAG reliability literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mechanistic finding that RAG hallucinations arise primarily from the LLM losing Copying-Head-attended information during generation (77.5% of cases) rather than from attention misallocation, combined with excessive parametric knowledge injection by later-layer Knowledge FFNs, provides an actionable causal explanation that prior black-box or single-signal detection methods could not offer. AARF demonstrates that parameter-free inference-time intervention on these specific components can reduce hallucinations, suggesting a practical path for production RAG systems. Limitations include evaluation restricted to the LLaMA family (7B/13B/8B), reliance on an external BGE embedding model for chunk-level scoring, and the acknowledged approximation nature of the ECS and PKS metrics; whether the identified Copying Heads and Knowledge FFNs remain stable across architectures, fine-tuning regimes, or longer contexts remains open.
