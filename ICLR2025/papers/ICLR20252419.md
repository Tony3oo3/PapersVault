## Problem

Existing inference cost attacks (sponge examples, LLMEffiChecker) target encoder-decoder architectures via cross-attention or perturbation-based mutations, rendering them ineffective against modern decoder-only LLMs that use auto-regressive generation and sub-word tokenization. The core challenge is twofold: the generation process is inherently stochastic and auto-regressive, making it hard to constrain output length, and the discrete token input space prevents direct gradient-based optimization of the adversarial prompt. This paper asks how to reliably craft short adversarial prompts that force LLMs to generate until their maximum length limit, maximizing per-request inference cost.

## Input / Output

The method requires white-box access to the target LLM (architecture, parameters, and input template for SFT models) and a random initial prompt to seed the proxy distribution. The output is a discrete token sequence of length t (default 32 tokens) — the Engorgio prompt — that, when prepended to the model input, induces generation up to the maximum allowable context length S (e.g., 1024 or 2048 tokens). The optimization operates in continuous embedding space via Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization, but the final deliverable is a valid, deployable token sequence that can be sent as a normal API request.

## Method

Engorgio introduces a parameterized proxy distribution θ ∈ R^{S×V} that tracks token selection across all context positions, bridging the gap between continuous embedding optimization and discrete token output. During the generation stage, each row of θ is converted to a soft token embedding via Gumbel-Softmax (Eq. 2–3), enabling end-to-end gradient flow through the target model. Two loss functions drive optimization: an <EOS> escape loss that penalizes the softmax probability of the <EOS> token at every position (an untargeted objective that avoids the need to predict exact stopping positions), and a self-mentor loss that minimizes cross-entropy between the proxy distribution and the model's predicted next-token distribution, anchoring the proxy to the model's actual generation trajectory. After convergence (~200–300 steps), the testing stage samples the final discrete Engorgio prompt from the normalized proxy weights, exploiting the observation that θ concentrates on a single high-probability token sequence. This two-stage design (continuous optimization → discrete sampling) is the key mechanism that makes gradient-based adversarial prompt search feasible for auto-regressive LLMs.

## Results

Engorgio achieves 90–100% Avg-rate (fraction of outputs reaching the maximum length limit) across all 6 base models, compared to 12–69% for normal inputs. On SFT models, the gap is even more dramatic: Engorgio reaches 86–100% Avg-rate while normal inputs yield only 0–6%. In terms of average output length, Engorgio induces roughly 2–13× longer responses than normal inputs (e.g., 3951.5 vs. 313.7 tokens on Samantha with a 4096-token limit, a ~12.6× increase). A real-world experiment on Hugging Face inference endpoints shows that even 1 out of 10 malicious users with Engorgio prompts can nearly halve server throughput and significantly increase queuing latency for normal clients.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Engorgio decisively outperforms both baselines across all 13 tested models. Against LLMEffiChecker (Feng et al., 2024), which relies on perturbation-based mutations (character, word, structure attacks), Engorgio achieves substantially higher Avg-rate: e.g., 95% vs. 28% on LLaMA-30B and 98% vs. 38% on StableLM. On SFT models, LLMEffiChecker is often worse than normal inputs (e.g., 172.6/0% vs. 313.7/2% on Samantha), while Engorgio still reaches 95% Avg-rate. Against Sponge examples (Shumailov et al., 2021), which exploit cross-attention density in encoder-decoder architectures, Engorgio is more stable and effective: on LLaMA-30B, Engorgio reaches 2019.1/95% vs. Sponge's 1277.7/38%, and on Samantha the gap is extreme (3951.5/95% vs. 284.9/3%). The paper attributes Sponge examples' relative weakness to the absence of cross-attention in modern decoder-only LLMs and their sensitivity to sampling-based decoding, while LLMEffiChecker's perturbation approach is invalidated by sub-word tokenization (BPE) that makes models robust to typos.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inference cost attack paradigm (originally proposed for encoder-decoder transformers by Shumailov et al., 2021) to modern decoder-only autoregressive LLMs, where prior methods relying on cross-attention manipulation or perturbation-based mutation (LLMEffiChecker, sponge examples) become ineffective. It also departs from Geiping et al. (2024), who coerce LLMs into repeating a specific phrase, by instead targeting the fundamental generation-halting mechanism (the <EOS> token) with an untargeted objective. Technically, the method repurposes Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization and prompt-learning-style embedding optimization for adversarial prompt search, combined with two novel loss terms (EOS escape loss and self-mentor loss) that have no direct precedent in the adversarial NLP literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a concrete, low-cost threat to LLM service availability: a single 32-token prompt can force near-maximum-length generation across 13 models (125M–30B), and a real-world experiment shows that 1–5 malicious users among 10–100 can severely degrade throughput and latency for all clients. Open questions include the lack of effective defenses (perplexity filters yield unacceptable false positives), limited transferability across architecturally distinct models, and the absence of coherence constraints on generated prompts. The authors also suggest a constructive use: adversarial training with (Engorgio prompt, NULL) pairs to teach LLMs to halt generation more judiciously, an unexplored direction with potential for improving inference efficiency broadly.
