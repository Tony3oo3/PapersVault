## Problem

Existing control adapters for diffusion models (e.g., ControlNet) require computing and storing gradients for both the adapter and the base diffusion model during training, nearly doubling computational overhead. They also modify intermediate features but still rely on the fixed base model parameters to produce output, limiting generative capacity. Furthermore, their encoder-focused design assumes a U-Net architecture and does not generalize to transformer-based diffusion models like DiT, where encoder/decoder boundaries are not cleanly separable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a condition image (Canny edge map, depth map, OpenPose skeleton, 4× downsampled image, or blurred+downsampled image) at 512×512 resolution along with a text prompt, and produces a 512×512 generated image. During training, all images are center-cropped and resized to 512×512 from the LAION dataset (2M images with resolution >512×512), paired with their original text annotations. The condition image is fed through the frozen diffusion model's forward pass, and the adapter consumes the resulting intermediate features plus the condition, timestep, and prompt to directly emit the denoised output.

## Method

UniCon reverses the information flow of ControlNet: instead of the adapter injecting residual corrections into the diffusion model's intermediate layers (bidirectional), information flows strictly unidirectionally from the frozen diffusion model to a trainable adapter that produces the final denoising output z_{t-1}. The adapter is initialized as a full trainable copy of the entire diffusion model (all blocks, not just the encoder), and zero-initialized connectors (ZeroConv for U-Net, ZeroMLP or the proposed ZeroFT for DiT) inject intermediate features from the base model into corresponding adapter locations. Because the base model only performs forward inference under torch.no_grad(), no gradients are computed or stored for it, reducing peak VRAM by roughly one-third and accelerating training by 2.3×. The proposed ZeroFT connector augments standard element-wise addition with element-wise multiplication and a shortcut path, improving both controllability and generation quality over ZeroMLP/ZeroConv. This design is architecture-agnostic (validated on both SD U-Net and DiT/PixArt-α) and enables training adapters with double the parameter count of ControlNet at no additional computational cost.

## Results

On the DiT (PixArt-α) model, UniCon achieves PSNR 37.34 on 4× super-resolution (vs. ControlNet's 34.82) and PSNR 41.13 on deblur+SR (vs. ControlNet's 37.63), while also improving FID from 26.43 to 20.34 and 29.18 to 21.29 respectively. On the SD U-Net model, UniCon reaches SSIM 0.5570 on Canny (vs. ControlNet 0.4895, T2I-Adapter 0.3936) and PSNR 35.69 on SR (vs. ControlNet 31.66, T2I-Adapter 18.94). UniCon reduces GPU memory usage by one-third and increases training speed by 2.3× compared to ControlNet, while enabling adapters with double the parameter volume at no additional computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniCon outperforms ControlNet (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023) on every task and both architectures: on DiT SR, UniCon gains +2.52 PSNR (37.34 vs 34.82) and reduces FID by 5.09 (20.34 vs 26.43); on SD U-Net Canny, UniCon improves SSIM by 0.0675 (0.5570 vs 0.4895). Against T2I-Adapter (Mou et al., AAAI 2024), UniCon dominates on controllability (e.g., SSIM 0.5570 vs 0.3936 on Canny, PSNR 35.69 vs 18.94 on SR) while T2I-Adapter occasionally edges ahead on individual quality metrics like Clip-IQA, but its control fidelity is substantially weaker. In the extended SD U-Net comparison (Table 4), UniCon also beats Uni-ControlNet (Zhao et al., NeurIPS 2024) on both SSIM (0.5570 vs 0.4996) and FID (47.11 vs 49.95) for Canny, and GLIGEN (Li et al., CVPR 2023) on SSIM (0.5570 vs 0.4447) and FID (47.11 vs 55.42). ControlNeXt (Peng et al., 2024) achieves slightly lower controllability than ControlNet (PSNR 34.31 vs 34.82 on DiT SR) and is significantly behind UniCon (37.34), though the paper notes that using ControlNeXt as a pre-trained initialization for UniCon yields a strong coarse-to-fine two-stage result. On image restoration (Table 5), UniCon with SD2.1 achieves FID 22.80, surpassing StableSR (37.85), DiffBIR (32.85), and PASD (25.61), and with PixArt-α reaches FID 20.34 with PSNR 37.34, the best among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniCon directly extends the ControlNet paradigm (trainable copy of the base model + zero-initialized connectors) but inverts the information flow: instead of the adapter injecting residual corrections into the base model's intermediate features (bidirectional), the adapter becomes the sole output producer while the base model serves only as a frozen feature extractor (unidirectional). This departs from the entire line of adapter-based control methods (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, ControlNet-XS, Uni-ControlNet) that all assume the base model's decoder processes the final output. The paper also builds on SUPIR to demonstrate applicability to image restoration, and introduces a new ZeroFT connector that augments the standard ZeroConv/ZeroMLP with element-wise multiplication and a shortcut path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As diffusion models scale from 0.86B (SD) to 8B (SD3) and shift from U-Net to transformer backbones, the gradient storage and computation for the base model during adapter training becomes a major bottleneck; UniCon's unidirectional design eliminates this cost entirely, yielding a 2.3× training speedup and ~1/3 VRAM reduction while simultaneously improving controllability and FID. The architecture-agnostic design (validated on both U-Net and DiT) makes it a practical drop-in replacement for existing ControlNet pipelines. A noted limitation is that UniCon does not reduce inference-time compute since the adapter retains full model parameters, and the Skip-Layer variant fails under unidirectional flow, suggesting the approach requires a complete model copy to work reliably.
