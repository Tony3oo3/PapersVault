## Problem

Existing preference learning methods for instruction-following construct preference pairs by sampling multiple independent responses from the model, which introduces irrelevant content variations (e.g., entirely different story narratives) that obscure the subtle differences actually determining whether an instruction is followed. This interference misleads the model during training, as it must learn to distinguish instruction-following success from unrelated semantic variation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252473/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The actor model receives a complex multi-constraint instruction prompt (from a taxonomy-based dataset of 43K prompts) and generates a free-form text response. The refiner model takes an instruction paired with a candidate response and outputs a structured judgment (binary label plus explanation), then takes the instruction, a negative response, and its judgment to produce a minimally revised refined response. The resulting preference pairs have a string-level similarity of 0.90 between chosen and rejected responses, substantially higher than the 0.85 typical of independently sampled pairs, ensuring the contrast isolates the instruction-following difference.

## Method

SPAR is an iterative self-play framework where a single model plays two roles—actor (generates responses) and refiner (judges and refines)—initialized from the same base model. The key mechanism is tree-search refinement (BFS or DFS): starting from a failed response identified by the refiner, the algorithm expands a search tree of candidate refinements level-by-level, evaluating each with the refiner until a correct response is found, thereby producing a preference pair that differs from the negative response only in the minimal edits needed to satisfy the instruction. The actor is updated via DPO on these refinement pairs, while the refiner is improved via rejection-sampling fine-tuning (RFT) on both judgment and refinement data collected during the tree search. The process iterates for three rounds, with self-consistency (majority voting over multiple refiner judgments) ensuring reliable negative data collection, and a taxonomy-based prompt construction pipeline ensuring constraint diversity. At inference time, the same tree-search refinement can be applied as a test-time compute scaling strategy, yielding further gains over greedy decoding.

## Results

After three iterations of self-play training, SPAR-8B-DPO-iter3 achieves 81.8% average accuracy on IFEval, surpassing GPT-4-Turbo (81.3%), and 68.8% on FollowBench (SSR), up from 77.9% and 63.5% for the base LLaMA3-8B-Instruct. Incorporating tree-search refinement at inference further boosts IFEval to 83.7% and FollowBench to 69.4%. The method scales effectively: SPAR-70B-DPO-iter3 reaches 86.1% IFEval and 74.3% FollowBench (vs. 83.4%/70.8% for LLaMA3-70B-Instruct), and SPAR-9B-DPO-iter3 reaches 79.1% IFEval and 72.0% FollowBench (vs. 74.2%/70.6% for GLM-4-9B-Chat). General capabilities are preserved, with average scores on GSM8k, TriviaQA, MMLU, and HumanEval remaining stable or slightly improved across all iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPAR-8B-DPO-iter3 (81.8% IFEval, 68.8% FollowBench) outperforms all five baselines on both benchmarks. The closest competitors are SELF (80.4% IFEval, 64.8% FollowBench) and Meta-Rewarding (79.9% IFEval, 67.3% FollowBench), with SPAR leading by 1.4–1.9 points on IFEval and 1.5–4.0 points on FollowBench. Self-Rewarding (79.3% IFEval, 67.0% FollowBench) and Humpback (75.2% IFEval, 64.6% FollowBench) trail further behind. AutoIF-8B (42.5% IFEval, 49.9% FollowBench) performs substantially worse, likely due to its reliance on code-execution feedback that does not generalize well to open-ended instruction constraints. Notably, even after three iterations, the baselines fail to surpass SPAR's first-iteration performance (80.3% IFEval), underscoring the advantage of tree-search refinement pairs over independently sampled preference pairs. On the 70B scale, SPAR-70B (86.1% IFEval, 74.3% FollowBench) also surpasses AutoIF-70B (85.7% IFEval, 66.5% FollowBench), with a particularly large margin on FollowBench (+7.8 points).

## Relation to Prior Work

SPAR builds directly on DPO for actor optimization and Rejection-sampling Fine-Tuning (RFT) for refiner training, but departs from the dominant self-improvement paradigm (self-rewarding, meta-rewarding, SELF) that constructs preference pairs by sampling multiple independent responses from the model. The key conceptual departure is the use of tree-search (BFS/DFS) to iteratively refine a single failed response into a correct one, producing preference pairs with high string-level similarity (~0.90 vs ~0.85 for independent sampling) that isolate the minimal edit responsible for instruction-following success. The self-play actor-refiner architecture echoes SELF's iterative self-evolution loop, but replaces free-form language feedback with structured tree-search refinement and majority-voted judgment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a well-designed self-play loop with tree-search refinement can push an 8B model past GPT-4-Turbo on IFEval without degrading general capabilities, and that the refiner can surpass the strong LLM (GPT-4o-Mini) used to bootstrap it after just three iterations—suggesting a path to unbounded self-improvement in instruction-following. However, the self-evaluation bias (refiner scores its own refinements at 90.5% vs. 79% by GPT-4o) raises concerns about whether further iterations will continue to yield genuine gains or merely reinforce the model's own preferences. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond verifiable instruction-following to more open-ended tasks, whether the 3-iteration limit reflects a true ceiling or simply insufficient compute, and how the method scales to even larger models or more diverse constraint taxonomies.
