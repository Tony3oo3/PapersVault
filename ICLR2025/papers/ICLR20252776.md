## Problem

Cross-view video prediction—generating future ego-centric frames from a synchronized exo-centric video—remains challenging due to the drastic perspective shift between views. Prior approaches either depend on 3D scene reconstruction or precise human annotations, limiting scalability, and none explicitly model the hand-object dynamics that drive ego-centric action. This paper asks whether explicitly predicting hand-object interaction (HOI) masks in the ego view from exo-view observations can serve as effective structural guidance for ego-centric video generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252776/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives only the first ego-centric frame (256×256 for diffusion, 480×480 for mask prediction), a free-text instruction, and a 16-frame exo-centric video with its 3-class HOI masks (background/hand/object); no camera poses or depth are provided. The output is a 16-frame ego-centric RGB video plus per-frame ego-centric HOI masks predicted auto-regressively. The exo video is downsampled to 480×480 and one of four available exo views is selected to minimize hand occlusion.

## Method

EgoExo-Gen is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 is a cross-view HOI mask prediction network that uses an ego-exo memory attention module: the current exo-frame feature acts as query, while all prior ego/exo image features form keys and prior exo/ego mask features form values, enabling auto-regressive mask prediction even when ego frames are unobserved; a step-decay schedule anneals the ego-memory weight α from 1.0 to 0.0 so the model initially relies on visible ego features before learning pure cross-view transfer. Stage 2 is an HOI-aware latent video diffusion model (initialized from SEINE) where a lightweight mask encoder produces multi-scale spatio-temporal features from the predicted ego HOI masks, which are fused element-wise into the UNet decoder blocks alongside the first-frame VAE features and CLIP text embeddings. Training is two-phase: the UNet backbone is first trained on ego-centric video prediction, then frozen while the mask encoder and projection layers are trained with the HOI diffusion loss. An automated annotation pipeline (EgoHOS + SAM-2 for ego; 100DOH + Sapiens + SAM-2 for exo) generates temporally consistent HOI masks without manual labeling.

## Results

On Ego-Exo4D, EgoExo-Gen achieves the best performance across all four metrics: SSIM 0.537, PSNR 18.395, LPIPS 0.311, and FVD 1031.693, outperforming the strongest baseline ConsistI2V (SSIM 0.532, FVD 1109.314). In zero-shot transfer to the H2O dataset (unseen actions, objects, and environments), EgoExo-Gen again leads on all metrics (SSIM 0.486, PSNR 16.424, LPIPS 0.240, FVD 1360.477), with a particularly large FVD advantage over the next-best method DynamiCrafter (FVD 1657.931). The HOI mask conditioning contributes an additional gain of ~31 FVD points over the no-mask baseline (1063.458 → 1031.693) on Ego-Exo4D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoExo-Gen outperforms all six baselines on every metric for both Ego-Exo4D and zero-shot H2O transfer. On Ego-Exo4D, the closest competitor is ConsistI2V (SSIM 0.532, FVD 1109.314); EgoExo-Gen improves SSIM by 0.005 and reduces FVD by ~78 points, while also achieving the best LPIPS (0.311 vs. 0.351). SEINE, the base diffusion model used for initialization, scores SSIM 0.518 and FVD 1063.458, so the cross-view HOI mask pipeline adds meaningful gains on top of a strong text+frame-conditioned baseline. In the zero-shot H2O setting, EgoExo-Gen's advantage is most pronounced on FVD (1360.477 vs. 1657.931 for DynamiCrafter, the next-best), a ~18% reduction, indicating that explicit hand-object dynamics transfer better to unseen tabletop activities than generic video priors. SparseCtrl and Seer degrade substantially on H2O (FVD 2566 and 2272 respectively), confirming that methods without ego-centric structural guidance struggle with cross-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video prediction paradigm (SEINE, ConsistI2V, DynamiCrafter) to the cross-view setting by introducing an explicit hand-object interaction (HOI) mask as a structural bridge between exo-centric and ego-centric perspectives. Prior cross-view generation work (Luo et al., 2024a,b) relied on 3D scene reconstruction or precise human pose annotations, limiting scalability; EgoExo-Gen instead uses a memory-attention-based mask prediction model that requires only one ego frame at test time and no camera poses. The automated annotation pipeline repurposes vision foundation models (SAM-2, EgoHOS, Sapiens, 100DOH) to generate training labels without manual annotation, building on the trend of leveraging foundation models for scalable data generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that explicit modeling of hand-object dynamics—rather than relying solely on text or implicit cross-view learning—yields more controllable and accurate ego-centric video prediction, with strong zero-shot transfer to unseen datasets (H2O). This has direct implications for embodied AI and AR systems that must build first-person world models from third-person demonstrations. Key limitations remain: the model struggles with complex hand movements and rapid camera motion, and performance is bounded by the quality of predicted HOI masks for distant future frames. Extending the approach to broader object sets and longer video horizons are natural next steps.
