## Problem

Reconstructing dynamic video from fMRI brain activity remains challenging because prior methods cannot simultaneously capture semantic, structural, and motion information, and the ~0.5 Hz temporal resolution of BOLD signals makes it difficult to decode multiple video frames from a single fMRI volume. Additionally, reliance on pre-trained video generation models introduces ambiguity about whether observed motion in reconstructions genuinely derives from fMRI or is a hallucination from the generative model's training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252428/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single fMRI BOLD volume (with a 4-second hemodynamic lag) from visual cortex voxels (4,500–5,820 voxels depending on dataset) as input and produces an 8-frame video clip at 512×512 resolution, corresponding to a 2-second segment downsampled from 30 Hz to 4 Hz. Each video clip is also paired with a ≤20-word BLIP2-generated text caption used as a semantic supervision signal during training. The output frames are generated independently by an inflated Stable Diffusion model, with motion consistency enforced only through the decoded fMRI-derived features rather than any video-level generative prior.

## Method

Mind-Animator is a two-stage model. In the fMRI-to-feature stage, three decoders disentangle information: (1) a Semantic Decoder uses fMRI-vision-language tri-modal contrastive learning (bidirectional InfoNCE) to align fMRI embeddings with CLIP space, then maps them to Stable Diffusion text conditions via a projection MLP; (2) a Structure Decoder (two-layer MLP) predicts VQ-VAE tokens of the first frame via MSE loss; (3) a Consistency Motion Generator (CMG), an L-layer Transformer, decodes inter-frame dynamics through a next-frame-prediction task, using a Sparse Causal Attention Temporal Module (with fixed causal + random sparsity masks) to learn temporal patterns and a cross-attention Spatial Module where fMRI serves as Key/Value and visible frame tokens serve as Query. In the feature-to-video stage, an inflated Stable Diffusion (network inflation of a T2I model, never trained on video data) generates each frame using the decoded semantic text condition, structural VQ-VAE latent, and motion features, ensuring that all motion in the output originates solely from fMRI decoding rather than from external video data.

## Results

On the CC2017 dataset, Mind-Animator achieves state-of-the-art performance in 6 out of 8 metrics, with headline improvements of 83% in SSIM (0.321 vs. 0.177 for Mind-video) and 13% in EPE (5.422 vs. 6.125) over the previous best method. It also leads in 2-way-I classification (0.805), VIFI-score (0.608), Hue-pcc (0.786), and CLIP-pcc (0.425). On HCP and Algonauts2021, the model extends its pixel-level lead, outperforming Mind-video by 196%/21%/4% and 275%/27%/5% across SSIM/PSNR/Hue-pcc respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Nishimoto (2011) and Wen (2018), Mind-Animator substantially outperforms on all comparable metrics (e.g., SSIM 0.321 vs. 0.119/0.130), though these earlier methods lack video-level and spatiotemporal metrics. Compared to Kupershmidt (2022), which leverages external video data, Mind-Animator wins on 2-way-I (0.805 vs. 0.769), VIFI (0.608 vs. 0.591), SSIM (0.321 vs. 0.140), Hue-pcc (0.786 vs. 0.616), and CLIP-pcc (0.425 vs. 0.382), while Kupershmidt retains a PSNR advantage (10.637 vs. 9.220). Against f-CVGAN (Wang 2022), Mind-Animator dominates on semantic (2-way-I 0.805 vs. 0.721), structural (SSIM 0.321 vs. 0.108, Hue-pcc 0.786 vs. 0.583), and spatiotemporal (CLIP-pcc 0.425 vs. 0.399, EPE 5.422 vs. 6.344) metrics, though f-CVGAN achieves higher PSNR (11.043 vs. 9.220). The most direct comparison is with Mind-video (Chen 2024): Mind-Animator wins on 2-way-I (0.805 vs. 0.797), VIFI (0.608 vs. 0.593), SSIM (0.321 vs. 0.177, +83%), Hue-pcc (0.786 vs. 0.768), CLIP-pcc (0.425 vs. 0.409), and EPE (5.422 vs. 6.125, −13%), but trails on 2-way-V (0.830 vs. 0.848) and PSNR (9.220 vs. 8.868 is actually a win; the PSNR loss is to f-CVGAN). Notably, Mind-Animator achieves these results without any external video data or video-model fine-tuning, whereas Mind-video requires both CC2017+HCP training data and video-fine-tuned Stable Diffusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fMRI-to-video reconstruction line initiated by Nishimoto et al. (2011) and advanced by DNN-based approaches (Wen et al. 2018, Wang et al. 2022) and the recent CLIP-semantic + video-generation paradigm of Chen et al. (2024, Mind-video). Its key departure is a deliberate architectural choice: rather than feeding decoded features into a pre-trained text-to-video model (which risks attributing motion to external video data), it inflates a text-to-image Stable Diffusion model frame-by-frame, ensuring all motion information must originate from the fMRI. The tri-modal (fMRI–vision–language) contrastive learning for semantic decoding builds on the CLIP-alignment strategy of Scotti et al. (2024) and Lu et al. (2023), while the Consistency Motion Generator with Sparse Causal Attention is a novel next-frame-prediction module inspired by VideoMAE-style masking applied to the fMRI-to-video setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling semantic, structural, and motion channels and validating each independently (shuffle tests, fMRI-guidance ablation), the paper provides the first scientifically rigorous evidence that motion in fMRI-reconstructed video is genuinely decoded from brain signals rather than hallucinated by a generative model. The eight-metric, three-dataset unified benchmark is the first of its kind and will serve as a standard reference for the small but growing fMRI-video-reconstruction community. Key limitations remain: reconstruction is limited to 8 frames per fMRI volume (≈2 s of video), absolute pixel-level fidelity is still low (SSIM ≈ 0.32), and the CLIP-pcc shuffle-test P-value never drops below 0.05 even at the noise ceiling, suggesting that current metrics may understate the true motion signal. Open questions include whether the sparse causal attention can scale to longer sequences, whether higher temporal-resolution fMRI (e.g., 7T, 1 s TR) would improve motion decoding, and whether the decoupled three-channel formulation generalizes to imagined or remembered video.
