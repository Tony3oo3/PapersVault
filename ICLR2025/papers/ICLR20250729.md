## Problem

Prior adversarial attacks on audio-visual models simply port single-modality methods (FGSM, I-FGSM) without exploiting the two defining properties of audio-visual data: temporal redundancy across consecutive frames and inter-modal semantic correlation. This paper asks whether these properties can be systematically leveraged to craft stronger, more transferable attacks and to design more efficient and robust adversarial training defenses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250729/cropped_page16_idx19.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired audio clips and video frames (Kinetics-Sounds: 27 action classes; MIT-MUSIC: 11 musical categories) encoded by CNN backbones (VGG/ResNet for video, AlexNet/ResNet for audio) fused via sum or concat. For attacks, it outputs p-norm-bounded perturbations δ_v and δ_a applied to the raw inputs; for defense, it outputs a robust audio-visual classifier. During adversarial training, the full temporal sequence is segmented and only a sampled fraction ρ of frames per segment is perturbed, with the resulting universal perturbation propagated to all frames in that segment to cut compute cost.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary attacks: (1) a Temporal Invariance Attack (TIA) that adds a regularization term minimizing the variance of audio and visual features across time steps, steering perturbations toward temporally invariant features and improving transferability; and (2) a Modality Misalignment Attack (MMA) that minimizes the cosine similarity between the audio and visual feature vectors, disrupting the semantic alignment that normally compensates for adversarial noise. The combined TMA attack optimizes classification loss minus weighted TIA and MMA terms under p-norm constraints. For defense, the paper introduces an efficient adversarial training framework that segments the temporal input, samples a ratio ρ of frames per segment to craft universal perturbations (reducing per-frame optimization cost), and applies a cyclical adversarial curriculum that jointly varies the data-level frame-sampling ratio ρ_x and the model-level fusion-layer dropout ratio ρ_f, gradually increasing attack difficulty to improve generalization across attack types.

## Results

On Kinetics-Sounds, the proposed TMA (combined temporal invariance + modality misalignment attack) achieves a 95.2% average black-box attack success rate across 8 audio-visual models, exceeding the best baseline PAM by 15.7%. Under the ensemble attack setting (7 surrogate models), all three proposed methods (TIA, MMA, TMA) reach 100% attack success rate, while FGSM only reaches 79.9%. For defense, the proposed adversarial training framework outperforms the runner-up CRMT-AT by 2.28% average robustness, and adversarial-training-based defenses collectively outperform non-AT methods (DCFL, SSDF, Mixup) by 10.9%. On MIT-MUSIC, TMA achieves 97.1% average attack success rate versus 85.1% for the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against attack baselines, TIA outperforms PAM (the strongest transferable attack baseline) by 15.7% on average, and TMA further improves by 5.2% over TIA alone, reaching 95.2% average black-box success rate. Momentum-based methods (MI-FGSM, MIG) do not consistently improve over FGSM in the audio-visual domain — in some configurations (sum fusion), MI-FGSM actually degrades performance by 2.3% relative to FGSM. Under ensemble attack, all proposed methods achieve 100% success rate, while FGSM reaches only 79.9%, confirming that the concat fusion layer's apparent robustness is overcome by the proposed attacks. For defense, the proposed adversarial training (efficient perturbation crafting + curriculum) beats CRMT-AT by 2.28% and vanilla AT by a larger margin, while all AT-based methods collectively outperform non-AT defenses (DCFL, SSDF, Mixup) by 10.9%. On the secondary MIT-MUSIC dataset, TMA achieves 97.1% vs. 85.1% for the best baseline, and the defense maintains a 2.2% advantage over CRMT-AT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior audio-visual adversarial robustness work (Tian & Xu 2021, Yang et al. 2021, Li et al. 2022b, Yang et al. 2023b), which largely adapted single-modality attacks like FGSM and I-FGSM without exploiting the unique structure of multi-modal temporal data. The key conceptual departure is designing attacks that explicitly target two properties absent in single-modality settings: temporal invariance (exploiting redundancy across frames to craft transferable perturbations) and inter-modal semantic alignment (disrupting audio-visual correlation to boost transferability). On the defense side, the paper extends vanilla adversarial training (Madry et al. 2018) with segment-based universal perturbation crafting and a cyclic curriculum strategy, addressing the computational bottleneck specific to multi-modal temporal data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first purpose-built audio-visual adversarial attacks that achieve near-perfect transferability (95–100% under ensemble settings), establishing a much stronger benchmark than prior adapted single-modality methods. The finding that momentum-based methods (MI-FGSM) can actually degrade audio-visual transferability—contrary to the image-domain consensus—challenges a widely held assumption and suggests that attack design principles do not transfer straightforwardly across modalities. Limitations include evaluation on small-scale datasets (27 and 11 classes), CNN-only architectures, and a VideoLLaMA demo on only 100 examples, leaving open whether these findings hold for transformer-based audio-visual models and larger, more diverse benchmarks. The efficient perturbation crafting strategy (segment-level universal perturbations) is a practical contribution that could reduce adversarial training cost for any temporal multi-modal setting.
