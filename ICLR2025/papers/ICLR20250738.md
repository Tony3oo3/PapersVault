## Problem

Existing offline preference-based RL algorithms with provable sample complexity bounds (e.g., FREEHAND-transition, Pace et al.) enforce conservatism through explicit confidence set constructions that require computationally intractable optimizations—tri-level constrained optimization over reward and transition confidence sets, or uncertainty penalties defined as confidence set widths. This paper bridges the gap between statistical guarantees and computational feasibility by providing the first offline PbRL algorithm that is both provably sample-efficient and practically implementable with general function approximation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two offline datasets: a preference dataset of trajectory pairs with binary labels (generated via a Bradley-Terry-Luce model with a monotonically increasing link function) and an unlabeled trajectory dataset of trajectory pairs collected by a reference policy μ. It outputs an ε-optimal policy π̂ for an episodic MDP of horizon H. In the practical implementation, the method operates on trajectory segments of length L under a discounted MDP, where preference labels are assigned to segment pairs based on a reward-difference threshold.

## Method

APPO formulates offline PbRL as a two-player Stackelberg game: the policy maximizes its relative performance over the reference policy while the reward model minimizes it, regularized by a trajectory-pair ℓ₁ loss that penalizes deviation from the MLE reward estimate. A key reparameterization maps the reward optimization to action-value function optimization via the Bellman equation (induced reward r = f − P(f∘π)), eliminating the need for on-policy rollouts or a policy evaluation oracle and making the entire algorithm solvable from offline data alone. The policy is updated via a TRPO-style exponential tilt π^{t+1}(a|s) ∝ π^t(a|s) exp(η f^t(s,a)), and the adversarial interplay between the value function and policy naturally enforces conservatism without explicit confidence sets. The theoretical analysis decomposes sub-optimality into MLE estimation error, optimization error (controlled by the adversarial loss with λ > C_TR), and policy update regret, yielding a sample complexity bound under standard realizability and bounded trajectory concentrability assumptions.

## Results

On the Meta-World medium-replay benchmark with 500 preference feedback samples, APPO achieves the best average rank (2.125) among all methods, outperforming MR (2.316), IPL (3.063), PT (3.125), and DPPO (4.375). Notably, APPO surpasses the Oracle policy (trained with ground-truth rewards via IQL) on dial-turn (80.96% vs. 75.40% success rate) and BPT-wall (64.32% vs. 56.27%). With 1000 feedback samples, APPO further improves on drawer-open (98.56% vs. Oracle's 100%) and lever-pull (76.96% vs. Oracle's 98.47%), while maintaining its lead over all baselines on BPT-wall (62.96% vs. next-best IPL at 14.07%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

APPO is compared against four offline PbRL baselines: MR (IQL with Markovian reward), PT (Preference Transformer), DPPO (direct preference-based policy optimization), and IPL (Inverse Preference Learning), plus an Oracle upper bound (IQL with ground-truth rewards). APPO decisively outperforms DPPO on nearly all tasks (e.g., 64.32% vs. 0.80% on BPT-wall-500; 80.96% vs. 26.67% on dial-turn-500) and PT on most tasks (e.g., 53.52% vs. 22.87% on BPT-500; 64.32% vs. 0.87% on BPT-wall-500). Against IPL, APPO wins on BPT (53.52% vs. 34.73%), BPT-wall (64.32% vs. 8.93%), and dial-turn (80.96% vs. 31.53%) at 500 feedback, though IPL edges ahead on sweep-into (32.20% vs. 24.08%). Against the strongest baseline MR, APPO wins on BPT (53.52% vs. 10.08%) and BPT-wall (64.32% vs. 0.32%) but trails on sweep (26.80% vs. 86.96%), drawer-open (87.68% vs. 98.00%), and lever-pull (75.76% vs. 79.28%). Overall, APPO achieves the best average rank (2.125 vs. MR's 2.316) and is the only method to exceed the Oracle on dial-turn and BPT-wall, demonstrating that its adversarial conservatism regularizer provides a practical advantage without sacrificing performance on easier tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Zhan et al. (2024a), which established the first provable offline PbRL algorithm (FREEHAND-transition) but required solving an intractable tri-level constrained optimization over confidence sets of rewards and transitions. The key departure is replacing explicit confidence set construction with a two-player Stackelberg game between the policy and the reward model, where conservatism is enforced through a trajectory-pair ℓ₁ loss rather than set membership constraints. The reparameterization technique—optimizing over action-value functions instead of reward models, leveraging the performance difference lemma—draws on adversarial training ideas from offline RL (Cheng et al. 2022, Rigter et al. 2022) and eliminates the need for on-policy rollouts or a policy evaluation oracle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

APPO is the first offline PbRL algorithm to simultaneously provide provable sample complexity bounds under general function approximation and remain computationally tractable, resolving a fundamental tension in the field where prior guarantees came at the cost of intractable optimization. The practical implementation with neural networks on Meta-World demonstrates competitive performance with state-of-the-art baselines, though the empirical gains over strong baselines like MR are modest. Open questions include whether the trajectory concentrability assumption (stronger than step-wise concentrability) can be relaxed, whether the conservatism hyperparameter λ can be auto-tuned, and whether the adversarial game formulation generalizes to online PbRL or multi-agent settings.
