## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks lack both multi-discipline coverage and multi-faceted reasoning depth (explanation, counterfactual thinking, future prediction, domain expertise) needed to evaluate whether MLLMs possess genuine 'world model' capabilities. No prior benchmark simultaneously spans diverse domains requiring specialized knowledge and probes reasoning beyond perception-level tasks, leaving a critical gap in assessing MLLMs' potential pathways toward AGI.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250967/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Models receive 10 sampled video frames (proprietary models) or varying frame counts (e.g., 8 for Video-LLaVA, 32 for ChatUniVi, 100 for VideoChat) alongside a text question with 3–4 multiple-choice options. The synthetic subsets isolate single modalities: Subset I provides only audio transcripts (no visual input), while Subset II provides only keyframes (no audio), enabling controlled perception ablation. Answers are validated via GPT-4-32K as a judge when rule-based option mapping fails, with a verified 4.76% error rate against human evaluators.

## Method

MMWorld is constructed through a two-stage pipeline: (1) a human-annotated main subset of 417 videos across 7 disciplines and 61 subdisciplines, where annotators craft 1,559 QA pairs targeting seven reasoning facets (explanation, counterfactual, future prediction, domain expertise, temporal, attribution, procedure), with cross-checking by at least two researchers; and (2) an automated synthetic pipeline that generates discipline-specific queries, retrieves Creative Commons videos from YouTube-8M and YouTube, extracts keyframes via Katna/UniVTG summarization, transcribes audio via ASR, and uses GPT-4V to produce modality-isolated QA pairs (2,969 audio-only, 2,099 visual-only). The benchmark evaluates 15 MLLMs (4 proprietary, 11 open-source) on video QA accuracy, with additional ablations on temporal coherence (shuffled/reduced frames) and modality isolation. Key findings include GPT-4o achieving only 62.54% overall accuracy, four open-source models performing below random chance, and Video-LLaVA-7B outperforming proprietary models on Embodied Tasks and Temporal Understanding.

## Results

On the MMWorld human-annotated benchmark (1,559 QA pairs across 7 disciplines), GPT-4o achieves the highest overall accuracy at 62.54%, followed by Claude-3.5-Sonnet (54.54%), GPT-4V (52.30%), and Gemini Pro (51.02%). Among open-source models, Video-LLaVA-7B leads at 44.60%, notably outperforming GPT-4V and Gemini Pro on Embodied Tasks (63.17% vs. 55.48% vs. 43.59%). Four evaluated MLLMs—LWM-1M-JAX (15.39%), Otter-7B (14.99%), Video-LLaMA-2-13B (14.03%), and X-Instruct-BLIP-7B (21.36%)—perform below the random-choice baseline of 26.31%, underscoring the benchmark's difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o (62.54%) leads all 15 evaluated MLLMs by a wide margin, outperforming the second-best proprietary model Claude-3.5-Sonnet (54.54%) by 8 points and the best open-source model Video-LLaVA-7B (44.60%) by 18 points. Among proprietary models, GPT-4o dominates on Business (91.14%), Science (73.78%), and Health & Medicine (83.33%), while Claude-3.5-Sonnet edges ahead on Art & Sports (54.58% vs. 47.87%). Notably, the open-source Video-LLaVA-7B surpasses both GPT-4V (55.48%) and Gemini Pro (43.59%) on Embodied Tasks and performs comparably on Art & Sports, attributed to its extensive video training data and CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone. Four models—LWM-1M-JAX, Otter-7B, Video-LLaMA-2-13B, and X-Instruct-BLIP-7B—fall below the 26.31% random-choice baseline, with Video-LLaMA-2-13B scoring only 14.03% overall. On multi-faceted reasoning (Table 4), GPT-4o leads across all six question types (Explanation 56.68%, Counterfactual 75.88%, Future Prediction 82.48%, Domain Expertise 69.05%, Attribution 65.10%, Temporal 40.90%), though Video-LLaVA-7B is the best open-source model on Temporal Understanding (34.45%), outperforming all other open-source models by a significant margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMWorld carves new ground at the intersection of video understanding benchmarks and world-model evaluation. Prior video QA benchmarks (MVBench, Video-Bench, NExT-QA, Perception Test) each target a narrow slice—temporal reasoning, perception, or activity recognition—but none combine multi-discipline domain coverage with multi-faceted reasoning (counterfactual thinking, future prediction, domain expertise) in a single evaluation. The paper also extends the image-based multi-discipline evaluation paradigm of MMMU to the video modality, and builds on the 'world model' framing from LeCun (2022) and Ha & Schmidhuber (2018) by operationalizing it as a concrete video-understanding testbed. The synthetic data pipeline repurposes YouTube-8M and GPT-4V for controlled single-modality perception analysis, a design not present in prior video benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMWorld provides the first comprehensive, multi-discipline, multi-faceted video benchmark for probing whether MLLMs possess genuine world-model capabilities, and the results (GPT-4o at only 62.54%, four models below random) reveal substantial headroom. The finding that MLLMs exhibit different skill sets than humans—succeeding on expert-level questions humans fail while stumbling on easy ones—suggests current models compensate for weak perception with strong parametric knowledge, a gap that will need to close as video understanding matures. Open questions include whether the 10-frame input protocol for proprietary models underestimates their temporal reasoning, whether the GPT-4-as-judge pipeline (4.76% error) introduces systematic bias, and whether the benchmark's difficulty is driven more by domain knowledge gaps than by genuine video comprehension.
