## Problem

Existing post-training pruning methods for large transformers exhibit a consistent and accelerating drop in model performance as compression rates increase, particularly on challenging tasks. A key reason is that these methods do not adequately account for the small number of large-magnitude outlier features in transformer activations, whose alteration disproportionately harms downstream performance. The paper asks whether a retraining-free compression method can simultaneously preserve outlier information and recover the information lost to sparsity through a complementary low-rank component.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained transformer weight matrices (W ∈ R^{d_out × d_in}) along with a calibration set of 128 sequences of length 2048 (for LLMs) or 2048 images (for ViTs) to compute the second-moment scaling matrix D. Two hyperparameters control the output: a compression rate ρ ∈ (0,1) specifying the fraction of parameters removed, and a rank ratio κ ∈ (0,1) specifying what fraction of remaining parameters go into the low-rank term. The output replaces each original weight matrix with three stored matrices: the sparse matrix S·D⁻¹ (preserving the sparsity pattern of S) and the two factors of the low-rank decomposition of L·D⁻¹, enabling efficient inference without retraining.

## Method

OATS formulates weight compression as a Robust PCA problem, approximating each weight matrix as the sum of a sparse matrix (bounded ℓ₀ norm) and a low-rank matrix (bounded rank), solved via alternating thresholding that iterates between truncated SVD (for the low-rank term) and hard-thresholding (for the sparse term). The critical innovation is an outlier-aware scaling step: before decomposition, weights are multiplied by D = √diag(XᵀX), the square root of the second moment of input activations, which amplifies the reconstruction error on outlier channels so they are preferentially preserved. After solving the scaled problem, the inverse transformation W_compressed = (S+L)D⁻¹ is applied, which is cheap because D is diagonal and preserves the sparsity pattern. Wanda is recovered as the special case κ=0 (no low-rank term), and the low-rank component captures structured information that pure sparsity discards, yielding substantially better performance at high compression rates. The method supports flexible sparsity patterns including row-wise thresholding and N:M structured sparsity on the sparse term for hardware acceleration.

## Results

On five-shot MMLU at 50% compression, OATS achieves 59.99% on Phi-3 Mini, 72.28% on Phi-3 Medium, 56.46% on Llama-3 8B, and 74.79% on Llama-3 70B, outperforming the best prior method by 5.42%, 2.52%, 2.86%, and 2.03% respectively. On WikiText-2 perplexity at 50% compression, OATS achieves 15.18 (Phi-3 Mini), 9.05 (Phi-3 Medium), 10.87 (Llama-3 8B), and 4.78 (Llama-3 70B), reducing perplexity by up to 9.30% over prior methods. On vision transformers, DinoV2-Giant experiences only a 0.41% top-1 accuracy drop at 50% compression. OATS also achieves up to 1.37× CPU speedup over unstructured pruning at 40% compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OATS consistently outperforms all three baselines—SparseGPT, Wanda, and DSNoT—across every compression rate (30%–60%), model size (3.8B–70B), and evaluation metric (MMLU, zero-shot, WikiText-2 perplexity). The performance gap widens substantially with compression: at 30% compression OATS leads by 0.5–1.5% on MMLU, but at 50% compression the margin grows to 2.0–5.4%, and at 60% compression (using OWL ratios) it reaches 5.0–6.4%. On WikiText-2 perplexity at 50% compression, OATS reduces perplexity by 8.5–9.3% relative to the best baseline. On vision transformers (ViT-Base, DinoV2-Giant), OATS similarly leads at 40% and 50% compression, with DinoV2-Giant retaining 86.14% top-1 accuracy at 50% compression versus 85.81–86.04% for baselines. Additionally, OATS achieves greater CPU inference speedup than unstructured pruning alone (1.37× vs 1.26× at 40% compression) due to its structured low-rank component.

## Relation to Prior Work

OATS unifies two previously separate threads: the outlier-aware scaling mechanism from Wanda (Sun et al., 2024b) and the alternating thresholding algorithms from the Robust PCA literature (Zhou & Tao, 2011; Candès et al., 2011). By formulating weight compression as a Robust PCA problem solved via alternating singular-value and hard thresholding, OATS generalizes Wanda (which is recovered as the special case κ=0) into a sparse-plus-low-rank decomposition. This departs from prior training-free pruners (SparseGPT, DSNoT) that produce only sparse weights, and from structured-pruning-plus-LoRA methods (LoSparse, LoRAPrune, APT) that require gradient-based fine-tuning of the low-rank adapter, by achieving both components in a single training-free pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent and growing performance gap over prior methods as compression increases (e.g., 5.42% MMLU at 50% on Phi-3 Mini) suggests the low-rank term plays a critical role in preserving information that pure sparsity discards, potentially mitigating the 'junk DNA' degradation identified by Yin et al. (2024a). The attention-rollout visualization revealing that sparse and low-rank terms capture complementary image regions (subject vs. background, different subjects) is a novel interpretability insight with no direct precedent in the pruning literature. Open questions include whether alternative Robust PCA solvers (convex relaxation, gradient-based) could further improve the decomposition, whether the complementary attention patterns generalize to language tasks, and whether the wall-clock overhead of alternating thresholding (up to 153s per block for Llama-3 70B) limits practicality for very frequent re-compression scenarios.
