## Problem

Symbolic music datasets suitable for deep learning are either small and high-quality (MAESTRO: 1,276 files, classical only) or large but noisy and lacking expressive performance nuance (Lakh: 176K web-scraped MIDI files). Existing AMT-based datasets (GiantMIDI, ATEPP, PiJAMA) address quality but remain limited in scale and rely on human-in-the-loop verification that does not generalize to diverse, noisy real-world recordings. The paper tackles the quality-versus-scale dichotomy by developing an automated pipeline that produces a large, clean, genre-diverse piano MIDI dataset without manual curation.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes YouTube video metadata (title and description text) for crawling and metadata extraction, and raw audio recordings (resampled to 22,050 Hz) for classification and transcription. It produces single-track solo-piano MIDI files (~100,000 hours total), per-file JSON metadata tags (composer, opus, piece number, performer, genre, form, key signature, difficulty, music period), continuous audio classifier scores usable as a proxy for recording quality, and time-aligned piano segment boundaries. The final dataset is single-track (no multi-instrument), and a compositional deduplication split of 800,973 files is also provided.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, Llama 3.1 70B scores each YouTube video's title and description on a 1–5 solo-piano likelihood scale, and the system follows YouTube's related-videos API in priority order to crawl new candidates, outsourcing discovery to YouTube's recommendation algorithm. Second, a lightweight CNN (5 conv + 2 dense layers) trained on 5-second mel-spectrogram clips classifies and segments audio; the key innovation is generating pseudo-labels via MVSep Piano source separation (labeling a clip non-piano if the 'other' stem exceeds an energy threshold over a contiguous region), which distills the expensive separation model into a fast classifier and yields an 8× improvement in non-piano identification over prior work without human supervision. Third, Aria-AMT (a Whisper-based seq-to-seq transcription model) converts segmented audio to MIDI at ~131× real-time, and Llama 3.1 70B extracts compositional metadata from the same YouTube text. Segmentation uses a sliding-window rule (stride 1 s, window 5 s) with parameters d=3 and λ=0.5 to tolerate short classification errors, and a 45-second minimum segment length plus a 0.7 average-score threshold for final file acceptance.

## Results

The ARIA-MIDI dataset contains 1,186,253 MIDI files spanning ~100,629 hours of transcribed solo-piano audio, reducing to 800,973 files after compositional deduplication. The proposed audio classifier achieves 96.38% piano segment overlap and removes 98.83% of non-piano audio (λ=0.5), while per-file classification at T=0.7 eliminates 100% of non-piano files while retaining 95.28% of high-quality piano recordings. The Aria-AMT transcription model achieves 97.63% note F1 on MAESTRO and 90.58% on MAPS, outperforming the previous benchmark (Kong et al., 2021) by 0.81 and 7.48 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MVSep (Fabbro et al., 2024) used directly as a segmentation oracle, the proposed distilled CNN classifier achieves substantially higher F1 (73.86% vs. 67.96% at the best MVSep threshold) and better non-piano overlap (98.83% vs. 97.22%), while also improving piano overlap from 91.96% to 94.22%. The ablation model trained without pseudo-labeled data shows the trade-off: it preserves piano segments better (97.05% overlap) but mislabels non-piano audio as piano far more often (91.10% vs. 98.83% non-piano overlap), confirming that source-separation pseudo-labels are critical for robust non-piano detection. For transcription, Aria-AMT outperforms the Kong et al. (2021) model on both test sets: 97.63% vs. 96.82% note F1 on MAESTRO and 90.58% vs. 83.10% on MAPS, with the larger MAPS gap (7.48 points) reflecting Aria-AMT's superior generalization to diverse recording conditions beyond the clean Disklavier recordings of MAESTRO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of AMT-based piano dataset construction initiated by GiantMIDI (Kong et al., 2020), ATEPP (Zhang et al., 2022), and PiJAMA (Edwards et al., 2023), all of which relied on the same transcription model and audio classifiers trained on MAESTRO/AudioSet with varying degrees of human verification. The key methodological departure is replacing manual curation and title-matching with a language-model-guided YouTube crawling loop (Llama 3.1 scoring related videos) and a source-separation-based pseudo-labeling strategy (MVSep) to train a distilled CNN classifier, eliminating the human-in-the-loop bottleneck that limited prior datasets to tens of thousands of files. The sliding-window segmentation approach adapts standard time-series segmentation (Keogh et al., 2004) to the specific problem of isolating contiguous piano performance regions from noisy recordings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

At over 1 million files and ~100,000 hours, Aria-MIDI is roughly an order of magnitude larger than any prior AMT-based piano dataset, making it a potential foundation resource for pretrained symbolic music models and large-scale MIR research. The LLM-guided crawling and source-separation pseudo-labeling pipeline is a reusable recipe for scaling transcription datasets to other instruments without proportional human annotation costs. Open questions include whether the transcription quality degrades for genres underrepresented in Aria-AMT's training distribution, whether the compositional deduplication (reducing to ~800K files) is sufficient for generative modeling, and how the approach extends to multi-instrument or non-piano contexts.
