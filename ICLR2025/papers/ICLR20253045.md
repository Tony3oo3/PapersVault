key_contribution: Proposes Mixture-of-Resolution Adaptation (MRA), a dual visual pathway method that fuses a low-resolution ViT pathway with a high-resolution CNN pathway via dynamic MR-Adapters, enabling MLLMs to leverage 1024×1024 image resolution with only 1024 visual tokens and 20.7-hour training, outperforming existing MLLMs on 15 of 17 vision-language benchmarks.
limitations: The method is validated exclusively on the LLaVA-1.5 architecture with a specific ViT-L + ConvNeXt-L pairing, and generalizability to other MLLM backbones or encoder combinations is not explored. Evaluation is limited to static RGB images; no experiments on video, 3D, or multi-image inputs are provided, and the paper does not discuss whether the dual-encoder overhead becomes prohibitive at even higher resolutions or with larger LLMs.

surprise_factor: The most notable finding is the consistent outperformance of LLaVA-Next (which uses ~2880 tokens via dynamic slicing) across 15/17 benchmarks while using fewer tokens (1024) and achieving faster inference — this challenges the implicit assumption that more visual tokens from slicing are necessary for high-resolution gains. The ablation showing that reversing the fusion direction (low-to-high instead of high-to-low) causes a -61.3 drop on MME is also a useful negative result confirming the design principle. However, the individual benchmark gains (2-5 points) are moderate and the efficiency advantage, while real, follows logically from the architectural choice of a stride-32 CNN.

## Problem

Existing MLLMs struggle with fine-grained visual recognition (small objects, dense text, occluded items), and while increasing input image resolution improves performance, it causes prohibitive computational cost, training instability, and GPU memory overflow. The paper identifies that no efficient mechanism exists to leverage high-resolution visual information without the quadratic token growth that makes naive resolution scaling impractical for MLLMs.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single RGB image that is simultaneously processed at two resolutions (e.g., 448×448 for the ViT pathway and 1024×1024 for the CNN pathway) along with a free-form text instruction. The dual-resolution design means the same image is encoded twice by different backbones, but the final visual token count remains small (576–1024 tokens) rather than scaling with the high-resolution grid. Output is standard autoregressive text, with no special structured format required.

## Method

MRA employs a heterogeneous dual visual pathway: a CLIP-ViT-L encoder processes the low-resolution image for global semantics, while a CLIP-ConvNeXt-L encoder (stride-32 downsampling) processes the high-resolution image for fine-grained detail. A novel MR-Adapter injects high-resolution features into the last three stages of the ViT via a dynamic gating mechanism (g = tanh(W₂·GELU(W₁·f_v))) that modulates how much high-res information is added at each stage, enabling early fusion without cross-attention. Training proceeds in two stages: low-resolution pre-training (freezing both encoders and the LLM, training only the MLP projector on 558K pairs) followed by high-resolution instruction tuning (unfreezing the full model, raising CNN resolution to 1024×1024, and inserting MR-Adapters). The key insight is that combining macro-view (ViT) and micro-view (CNN) representations through lightweight adapters yields stronger visual understanding with far fewer tokens than processing a single high-resolution image end-to-end.

## Results

LLaVA-HR (7B, 1024×1024) achieves 1554.9 on MME, 87.6 on POPE, 64.2 on SEED, and 31.5 on MM-Vet, outperforming LLaVA-1.5 (7B, 336×336) by +23.6 on MME and +1.7 on POPE. With the stronger ConvNeXt-XXL backbone (LLaVA-HR-X, 14B), the model reaches 88.0 on POPE and 40.3 on MM-Vet. On OCR-heavy benchmarks, LLaVA-HR† (with 2.7M instruction data) achieves 73.8 on TextVQA, 85.8 on DocVQA, 52.3 on InfoVQA, 75.3 on AI2D, and 77.6 on ChartQA, surpassing all compared MLLMs on DocVQA, InfoVQA, AI2D, and ChartQA. Training the full 7B model at 1024×1024 resolution takes only 20.7 hours on 8 A800 GPUs, with inference at 19.7 tokens/s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA-HR (7B) outperforms LLaVA-1.5 (7B) on MME (+23.6), POPE (+1.7), SEED (+5.6), and TextVQA (+8.9), while maintaining comparable inference speed (19.7 vs. 23.8 t/s). Against LLaVA-NeXT (7B, 1344 res, 1.6M data), LLaVA-HR wins on MME (1554.9 vs. 1519.0) and POPE (87.6 vs. 86.5), but LLaVA-NeXT leads on MM-Vet (43.9 vs. 31.5) and MathVista (34.6 vs. 28.5); with additional instruction data (LLaVA-HR†), the gap closes and LLaVA-HR† surpasses LLaVA-NeXT on MM-Vet (45.1 vs. 43.9) and MathVista (46.0 vs. 34.6). On OCR benchmarks, LLaVA-HR† beats DocOwl-1.5-Chat (8B, 4032 res) on DocVQA (85.8 vs. 82.2), InfoVQA (52.3 vs. 50.7), and ChartQA (77.6 vs. 70.2), and beats CogAgent (18B) on DocVQA (85.8 vs. 81.6) and AI2D (75.3 vs. –), though CogAgent retains an edge on TextVQA (76.1 vs. 73.8). LLaVA-HR-X (14B) achieves the best POPE score (88.0) among all 7–14B models and surpasses QwenVL-Chat (10B) on TextVQA (70.9 vs. 61.5) and VizWiz (56.6 vs. 38.9). The only models that clearly outperform LLaVA-HR across most benchmarks are the much larger CogVLM-Chat (17B) and InternVL-1.2 (40B), which use significantly more parameters and training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LLaVA-1.5, adopting its two-stage training procedure (projector pre-training + instruction tuning) and modular architecture (visual encoder → projector → LLM). It departs from two dominant strategies for improving MLLM visual perception: (1) the scale-up paradigm (InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL) that increases encoder parameters and training data at prohibitive cost, and (2) the dynamic slicing paradigm (LLaVA-Next/AnyRes, Monkey) that divides high-resolution images into multiple patches, which inflates the visual token sequence. The dual-pathway design (ViT for low-res global context + CNN for high-res local detail) draws on the macro/micro pathway distinction from human vision literature and CrossViT, but is the first to apply this heterogeneity specifically to MLLM high-resolution adaptation via the MR-Adapter for early cross-pathway fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that 1024×1024 resolution benefits can be obtained with only 1024 visual tokens (same order as the 336×336 baseline's 576 tokens), enabling 20.7-hour training on 8 A800s and faster inference than LLaVA-Next — a significant cost reduction for practitioners who cannot afford thousands of GPU hours. Open questions include whether the specific ViT+ConvNeXt pairing is optimal, whether the approach extends naturally to video or 3D inputs, and whether the MR-Adapter's dynamic gating (Eq. 4) is necessary versus a fixed-weight fusion. The paper does not explore whether the dual-pathway design could replace the LLM's own multi-scale attention mechanisms, which may offer a more unified alternative.
