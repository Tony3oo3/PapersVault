## Problem

Existing MIL methods for WSI classification are vulnerable to spurious correlations from unobservable confounders such as staining variations and color biases, and they struggle to jointly capture multi-scale pathological information (low-magnification tissue architecture and high-magnification cellular morphology). Prior causal deconfounding approaches like IBMIL and CaMIL require two-stage training or expensive feature clustering, making them computationally heavy and less practical, while also failing to integrate multi-scale structural cues in an end-to-end manner.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes patch-level features (512-dim vectors) extracted by a frozen ResNet18 pre-trained with SimCLR on 256×256 px patches at 20× magnification; it does not operate on raw pixels. A key design choice is that original image features are deliberately excluded from the causal intervention pipeline—only frequency-domain and multi-scale structural representations derived from the frozen features serve as mediators. The output is a standard bag-level class label produced by the host MIL model's existing classification head, making MFC a plug-and-play module compatible with ABMIL, DSMIL, TransMIL, CLAM, and DTFD-MIL.

## Method

MFC comprises three modules that jointly implement a front-door causal intervention in an end-to-end trainable framework. The Multiscale Spatial Representation Module (MSRM) applies PPEG positional encoding followed by 2D convolutions (kernels 7, 3, 5) and dilated 1D convolutions (kernel 16, dilations 1, 3, 5) to produce high-magnification cellular features x_hl and low-magnification tissue features x_ll. The Frequency-domain Structural Representation Module (FSRM) applies a Hilbert transform (implemented via FFT with positive-frequency doubling) to both feature streams, extracting phase information that is invariant to staining and color confounders. The Causal Memory Intervention Module (CMIM) maintains a bank of k trainable memory vectors that, during training, learn to represent the overall dataset distribution; attention-based write and read operations then perform a soft front-door adjustment—modeling P(M|X) via memory activation and P(Y|X̂, M) via memory-conditioned feature reconstruction—estimated with the Normalized Weighted Geometric Mean, thereby severing the spurious path X←Z→Y without explicit confounder identification or clustering.

## Results

On Camelyon16, MFC applied to DSMIL achieves 92.25% accuracy and 89.13% F1, while TransMIL+MFC reaches 97.68% AUC. On TCGA-NSCLC, TransMIL+MFC attains the best accuracy of 92.85% and F1 of 92.50%, and DTFD+MFC shows the largest absolute gain (+9.92% ACC, +10.36% AUC) over its baseline. Across all six baselines and both datasets, MFC consistently improves accuracy and F1, with the most pronounced gains on Camelyon16 where data bias is more severe (positive instances <10% of patches). AUC improvements are comparatively modest, and CLAM variants show slight AUC decreases on Camelyon16 despite large F1 and specificity gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFC outperforms all six baselines on accuracy and F1 across both datasets. Against ABMIL, MFC gains +2.94% ACC and +4.54% F1 on Camelyon16, and +0.85% ACC on TCGA-NSCLC. Against DSMIL, the largest Camelyon16 gain is +5.27% ACC and +9.72% F1, while on TCGA-NSCLC the improvement is +2.08% ACC. Against TransMIL, MFC yields +6.35% ACC and +7.10% F1 on Camelyon16, and +1.31% ACC on TCGA-NSCLC. Against CLAM-SB/MB, MFC improves ACC by ~2–3% and F1 by 1.5–3.3% on Camelyon16, though AUC slightly decreases (−0.69% for CLAM-SB, −0.36% for CLAM-MB), suggesting a shift in boundary-sample handling. Against DTFD, MFC delivers the most dramatic improvement, especially on TCGA-NSCLC where DTFD's baseline is weak (81.31% ACC → 91.23% ACC, +9.92%). In the direct comparison with IBMIL (Table 2), MFC-MIL achieves 92.25% ACC and 89.13% F1 versus IBMIL's 91.78% ACC and 88.50% F1 on Camelyon16, but IBMIL retains a slight AUC edge (96.31 vs. 95.41), which the authors attribute to IBMIL's two-stage training providing stronger confounder directionality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal intervention line of work in WSI classification (IBMIL's back-door approach, CaMIL's clustering-based front-door approach) by replacing explicit confounder identification or feature clustering with a trainable memory module that estimates the data distribution end-to-end. It builds on TransMIL's PPEG for positional encoding and the NWGM estimator from Liu et al. (2022a) for the front-door adjustment, while introducing multi-scale spatial convolutions and Hilbert-transform-based frequency-domain features as the mediator representation. The key conceptual departure is treating the multi-scale and frequency-domain structural features as a confounder-invariant mediator, enabling a single-stage plug-and-play causal intervention rather than the two-stage or clustering-dependent pipelines of prior causal MIL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (applicable to ABMIL, DSMIL, TransMIL, CLAM, DTFD-MIL) makes the causal deconfounding mechanism broadly adoptable without architectural redesign, which is practically valuable for the pathology community. However, the causal assumptions—particularly that the frequency-domain/multi-scale mediator M is unconfounded with respect to X—are argued but not empirically verified, and the observed AUC degradation in some settings (CLAM-SB, CLAM-MB on Camelyon16) suggests the intervention may over-correct boundary samples. Open questions include whether the memory module truly captures the full data distribution at scale, whether the front-door criterion holds under realistic staining confounders, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two datasets tested.
