## Problem

High-order optimization methods such as Cubic Regularized Newton and Basic Tensor methods exhibit superlinear convergence in practice for strongly convex functions, yet no global superlinear convergence guarantee existed—only local quadratic rates were known. Additionally, Nesterov-type acceleration, which is highly effective for first-order methods, paradoxically underperforms or matches its non-accelerated counterpart for second- and third-order methods in practice, leaving a gap between theoretical rates and practical utility.

## Input / Output

The methods require access to a differentiable function f(x) with L_p-Lipschitz p-th derivative (p ∈ {1,2,3}), a starting point x_0, a smoothness parameter M_p ≥ pL_p, and a total iteration budget T. For the third-order Basic Tensor Method, the BDGM subsolver avoids computing the full third-order tensor by using autograd-based directional derivatives, reducing per-step cost to that of a cubic regularized Newton step. All methods are packaged as PyTorch optimizers in the OPTAMI library, so the user simply passes a model and loss function and calls optimizer.step(), with the library handling Hessian computation, subproblem solving, and acceleration scheduling internally.

## Method

The paper makes three core contributions. First, NATA (Nesterov Accelerated Tensor Method with A_t-Adaptation) replaces the fixed theoretical scaling sequence A_t = (ν_p/L_p)·t^{p+1} with an adaptive scheme that starts from a large ν and backtracks by a factor θ until the estimating-sequence condition ψ_{t+1}(v_{t+1}) < Ã_{t+1}·f(x_{t+1}) is satisfied, yielding the same O(T^{-(p+1)}) rate plus a log(ν_max/ν_min) overhead while being substantially faster in practice. Second, the paper proves global superlinear convergence of CRN and Basic Tensor methods for μ-strongly star-convex functions by showing that the per-iteration contraction coefficient α_t = (−1+√(1+4κ_t))/(2κ_t) increases monotonically as ‖x_t − x*‖ shrinks, so the functional gap decreases by a strictly improving factor each step. Third, OPTAMI provides a modular three-level architecture (basic methods → subsolvers → accelerations) implemented entirely as PyTorch optimizers, enabling direct comparison of Nesterov, near-optimal (Monteiro-Svaiter), proximal-point, and optimal acceleration schemes on the same problem.

## Results

The paper proves that the Cubic Regularized Newton Method and Basic Tensor Method achieve global superlinear convergence for μ-strongly star-convex functions, with per-iteration rate α_t^sl that increases over time (unlike the constant rate of gradient descent). On the a9a logistic regression dataset (μ=10⁻⁴), the proposed NATA acceleration consistently outperforms all SOTA acceleration techniques including near-optimal (Monteiro-Svaiter), optimal (Kovalev-Gasnicon), Nesterov-type, and proximal-point acceleration. The NATA method achieves the same theoretical convergence rate O((M_p R^{p+1}/ε)^{1/(p+1)}) as the classical Nesterov Accelerated Tensor Method, with only an additional log_θ(ν^max/ν^min) factor for the adaptation search.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NATA decisively outperforms the classical Nesterov Accelerated Tensor Method, which in practice performs worse than or on par with its non-accelerated counterpart (Basic Tensor) for p=2 and p=3. Against Near-Optimal Acceleration (Bubeck et al., 2019), NATA wins overall; near-optimal is the second-best performer but suffers from many line-search iterations in early stages. Optimal Acceleration (Kovalev & Gasnikov, 2022) performs the worst in practice due to poorly tuned internal parameters causing many inner iterations without global progress. Near-Optimal Proximal-Point Acceleration with Segment Search (Nesterov, 2021a) performs similarly to Basic Methods with only marginal improvement in the strongly convex case. For the superlinear convergence results, CRN and Basic Tensor clearly outperform Gradient Descent, which maintains a constant linear rate α_t ≈ 10⁻³ (corresponding to condition number κ), while CRN and Basic Tensor accelerate with each iteration as α_t increases toward 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lineage of high-order convex optimization methods starting from Nesterov & Polyak's Cubic Regularized Newton (2006) and Nesterov's Basic Tensor Method (2021b), building on the acceleration frameworks of Monteiro & Svaiter (2013), Bubeck et al. (2019), and Kovalev & Gasnikov (2022). It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it proves global superlinear convergence for strongly star-convex functions, going beyond the known local quadratic convergence and the linear-convergence lower bounds that apply only in the small-ε regime; and (2) it identifies and addresses a practical failure mode of Nesterov-type acceleration for high-order methods, proposing NATA as a fix. The OPTAMI library also fills a gap by providing the first unified PyTorch implementation enabling direct comparison of all major acceleration techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The global superlinear convergence result is significant because it shows high-order methods fundamentally accelerate with each iteration for strongly star-convex functions, in contrast to the steady linear rate of first-order methods — this changes the theoretical understanding of when and why second-order methods outperform gradient-based approaches. NATA addresses a practical barrier that has kept accelerated high-order methods underutilized, and OPTAMI lowers the adoption barrier for practitioners. Open questions include whether superlinear convergence extends to accelerated variants, what the best achievable global per-iteration decrease is, and whether the results generalize beyond the tested function classes. Limitations include the computational cost of exact Hessians in high dimensions and the focus on a narrow set of test problems (logistic regression, Nesterov's lower-bound function).
