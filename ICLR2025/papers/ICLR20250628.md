## Problem

Existing deep equivariant SfM methods (ESFM, GASFM) assume input point tracks are nearly free of outliers, causing them to fail on realistic internet photo collections where SIFT+RANSAC matching yields 15–47% outlier rates. This paper addresses the gap by building a robust deep SfM pipeline that explicitly classifies and removes outlier track points while preserving the permutation-equivariant structure of the network.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse point track tensor M of size m×n×2 (m cameras, n 3D points, 2D pixel coordinates), where entries are left empty when a point is not observed in a given camera; tracks are pre-constructed via exhaustive SIFT+RANSAC pairwise matching and chaining. The network outputs three matrices: camera poses P∈R^{m×7} (quaternion + translation), 3D point positions X∈R^{n×3}, and per-point outlier scores O∈[0,1]^{m×n} where scores ≥0.6 are classified as outliers. Notably, individual 2D observations within a single track can be classified independently—some points in a track may be inliers while others are outliers.

## Method

The architecture is a sets-of-sets permutation-equivariant network with four modules: (1) a 3-layer equivariant feature encoder using linear equivariant maps spanned by identity, row sums, column sums, and matrix sum (averaged over non-empty entries for invariance to track length), (2) a 3-layer MLP outlier classification head with sigmoid output that respects equivariance, enabling classification based on both intra-track and intra-image motion consistency, (3) separate pose and structure regression heads operating on column-pooled and row-pooled features respectively, and (4) a robust bundle adjustment post-processing step. Training combines a supervised BCE loss on outlier labels (auto-generated from COLMAP reconstructions) with an unsupervised reprojection+hinge loss. At inference, predicted outliers are removed, the network is fine-tuned for 1000 epochs on the cleaned tracks, and a two-round robust BA (with 5-pixel error threshold, minimum 3-view constraint, and disconnected-graph handling) refines the reconstruction to sub-pixel accuracy.

## Results

On MegaDepth (37 scenes, mean 25.6% outliers), RESFM achieves a mean rotation error of 1.29° and translation error of 0.169, substantially outperforming ESFM (6.77° / 0.780) and GASFM (4.53° / 0.630). These results are on par with classical methods Theia (2.42° / 0.556) and GLOMAP (2.51° / 0.662), while approaching the upper bound of ESFM on clean tracks (ESFM*: 4.45° / 0.443). On the cross-dataset 1DSFM benchmark, RESFM similarly dominates the deep baselines (e.g., 0.82° rotation on Ellis Island vs. 21.13° for ESFM) and matches or exceeds classical methods. On smaller datasets with ground truth (Strecha, BlendedMVS), RESFM achieves the best rotation errors (0.027° mean on Strecha) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RESFM outperforms ESFM (Moran et al., 2021) in nearly all 37 MegaDepth scenes, reducing mean rotation error from 6.77° to 1.29° and translation error from 0.780 to 0.169; ESFM frequently fails catastrophically on high-outlier scenes (e.g., 38.74° on scene 0007 vs. 0.91° for RESFM). Similarly, RESFM dominates GASFM (Brynte et al., 2023), which uses a graph attention network but lacks outlier handling, achieving 4.53° mean rotation vs. RESFM's 1.29°. Against classical global methods, RESFM is on par with Theia (2.42° vs. 1.29° rotation) and GLOMAP (2.51° vs. 1.29°), often yielding superior translation recovery while registering fewer cameras (298 vs. 346/353). On the smaller Strecha and BlendedMVS datasets with ground truth, RESFM (0.027° mean rotation on Strecha) is more accurate than both VGGSfM (0.175°) and MASt3R (0.459°), which cannot scale to the larger datasets. In runtime, RESFM is significantly faster than COLMAP (~84 min vs. ~17 min on Alamo) and GLOMAP, though slower than Theia (~13 min), with the fine-tuning phase being the dominant cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep equivariant SfM line initiated by Moran et al. (ESFM, ICCV 2021), which used a sets-of-sets permutation-equivariant architecture (building on Hartford et al., ICML 2018) to solve projective factorization (Sturm & Triggs, ECCV 1996) end-to-end. The critical departure is that ESFM and its successor GASFM (Brynte et al., 2023) assumed near-clean point tracks, making them inapplicable to realistic internet-photo collections. RESFM integrates a multiview inlier/outlier classification head that respects the same row/column permutation equivariance, and pairs it with a robust bundle adjustment step, thereby closing the gap between the idealized clean-track assumption and the 15–47% outlier rates produced by standard SIFT+RANSAC preprocessing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first deep method that simultaneously recovers camera poses and 3D structure from large (hundreds of images) uncontrolled internet-photo collections with realistic outlier contamination, achieving accuracy on par with leading classical pipelines (COLMAP, Theia, GLOMAP). The released benchmark with COLMAP pseudo-ground-truth on MegaDepth and 1DSFM provides a useful evaluation protocol for future robust deep SfM work. A noted limitation is that aggressive outlier removal can disconnect the view graph, causing the method to use only a subset of input cameras; the fine-tuning stage (1000 epochs) is also the dominant runtime cost, limiting scalability to very large collections.
