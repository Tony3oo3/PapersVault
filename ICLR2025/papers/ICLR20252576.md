## Problem

High update-to-data (UTD) ratios in off-policy deep RL cause training instability because value functions misgeneralize to on-policy actions that were never observed in the replay buffer. Existing mitigations—periodic network resets, critic ensembles, and architectural regularization—either fail to fully resolve the generalization gap or introduce practical drawbacks such as unsafe re-exploration, expensive redundant models, and the need to tune reset intervals. The paper identifies this off-policy action misgeneralization as a root cause of high-UTD failure and seeks a targeted correction.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes raw state vectors from the DeepMind Control suite (e.g., humanoid and dog environments) and outputs continuous control actions via a deterministic tanh-activated actor network. Q-values are represented through the HL-Gauss categorical transformation (151 bins, range ±150·AR) rather than direct regression, and the world model operates entirely in a SimNorm-encoded latent space (512-dim, grouped into 8-entry softmax simplices). At each critic update, 5% of the mini-batch is replaced with model-generated transitions (reward + next latent state) sampled from the on-policy action, while the remaining 95% comes from the real replay buffer.

## Method

MAD-TD augments standard TD3 (clipped double Q learning) with a TD-MPC2-style latent dynamics model in a DYNA-like loop: a small fraction α=5% of each critic training batch is swapped for one-step model rollouts using the current target policy's action, directly grounding the Q-function on on-policy state-action pairs that are absent from the replay buffer. The encoder maps observations into SimNorm space for bounded representations, the critic uses HL-Gauss cross-entropy loss initialized toward zero, and the world model is trained with a three-term objective combining reward MSE, SimNorm cross-entropy for next-state prediction, and a simplified IterVAML value-aware term. The model also enables one-step MPC at planning time (512 samples, 6 iterations, top-64 selection) to further correct the policy. The key insight is that even a small, fixed amount of on-policy model data is sufficient to close the generalization gap and stabilize training at UTD 8–16 without resets, ensembles, or additional exploration mechanisms.

## Results

On the hardest DeepMind Control suite tasks (humanoid and dog environments), MAD-TD with only 5% model-generated data at UTD 8 achieves stable training and competitive or superior performance to strong baselines. The paper reports that MAD-TD achieves higher mean and interquartile mean returns than both TD-MPC2 and BRO when trained for 2 million steps with action repeat 2, and strongly outperforms both at 1 million steps with action repeat 2. In terms of average regret (a measure of continued-learning performance), MAD-TD substantially outperforms BRO, demonstrating more stable policy improvement over time without requiring periodic network resets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2024), MAD-TD performs on par or above across the hard DMC suite, with notably faster early learning and higher returns at 1M steps (AR=2). TD-MPC2 retains advantages on pendulum_swingup and acrobot_swingup, likely due to its longer model rollouts aiding exploration. Against BRO (Nauman et al., 2024), MAD-TD consistently outperforms BRO without resetting, while BRO is unable to achieve reliable performance without periodic network resets; MAD-TD also shows significantly lower average regret, indicating more stable continued learning. Against SynthER (Lu et al., 2024), which augments replay with diffusion-model-generated data, MAD-TD substantially outperforms on the hard DMC tasks—SynthER fails to achieve nontrivial results on most tasks, confirming that merely adding more generated data is insufficient without on-policy action correction. The paper also shows that replacing on-policy model actions with random actions degrades MAD-TD to model-free baseline performance, validating that the specific on-policy correction is what drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAD-TD builds on TD3 as its actor-critic backbone and TD-MPC2's latent world model architecture, but departs from both in a key way: rather than training values entirely in model rollouts (as in Dreamer/TD-MPC2) or relying on network resets and critic ensembles to stabilize high UTD training (as in BRO, D'Oro et al.), it identifies misgeneralization to unobserved on-policy actions as the root cause and addresses it by mixing a small fraction (5%) of model-generated on-policy transitions into the standard TD loss. The theoretical analysis extends the key-matrix stability conditions from Sutton (1988) and Sutton et al. (2016) to explicitly decompose the off-policy remainder, connecting the distribution shift between data-collection and target policies to Q-value divergence. The approach also draws on the DYNA framework (Sutton, 1990) for interleaving model and real data, and uses HL-Gauss (Farebrother et al., 2024) and SimNorm (Lavoie et al., 2023) for stable value and state representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By pinpointing misgeneralization to unobserved on-policy actions as the specific mechanism behind high-UTD instability, the paper reframes a problem previously addressed with blunt instruments (resets, ensembles, architectural regularization) into a targeted intervention that is both simpler and more practical. The finding that merely 5% model-generated data suffices, and that resetting provides negligible additional benefit once this correction is in place, has direct implications for real-world deployment where re-executing random policies is costly or unsafe. Key limitations include the dependence on learning a sufficiently accurate world model online, potential underperformance on simple tasks due to the TD-MPC2 architecture being over-parameterized, and the fact that exploration remains an open challenge (the method underperforms on sparse-reward Metaworld tasks). Future directions include combining the approach with diffusion models, DYNA search control for selective data correction, and uncertainty-aware model rollouts.
