## Problem

Existing deep learning-based molecular docking methods treat each protein-ligand pair in isolation, ignoring the biochemical observation that ligands binding to the same protein pocket tend to adopt similar poses due to conserved interactions. This correlated information among co-binding ligands has not been exploited in an end-to-end deep learning docking framework, leaving a clear opportunity to improve docking accuracy by leveraging group-level binding consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253140/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein pocket represented as a residue-level 3D proximity graph (Cα positions and amino acid types), a query ligand as a 2D molecular graph, and a group of augmented ligands (2D molecular graphs of other ligands known to bind the same protein, retrieved from PDBBind). At inference, no 3D structural data of augmented ligands is required—only their 2D representations are used. The output is a set of sampled ligand docking poses parameterized as translation, rotation, and torsion angles on a Riemannian submanifold, ranked by a group-level confidence score computed as the average of individual per-ligand confidence predictions.

## Method

GroupBind extends the DiffDock diffusion framework by simultaneously docking a group of ligands to a shared protein pocket, introducing two key modules: (1) a message passing layer across group ligands built on a fixed group ligands graph constructed via Maximum Common Substructure (MCS) matching (2D during training, 3D during inference) with 4 Å neighbor edges, independent of diffusion time; and (2) a triangle attention module that enforces protein-ligand interaction consistency across the group—ensuring that structurally similar atoms in different ligands attend to the same protein residues. The triangle attention operates on pair embeddings (protein-ligand and ligand-ligand) with two stacked attention heads where group pair embeddings serve as either attention bias or attention keys. An auxiliary binned distance prediction loss provides supervised signal to the evolving pairwise edge embeddings, and a confidence model ranks group poses jointly by averaging individual scores. The full model uses 5 interaction layers with 18.8M parameters, comparable to DiffDock's 20.3M.

## Results

On the PDBBind v2020 blind docking benchmark (363 test complexes), GroupBind-P2R-A (training-set augmentation) achieves 48.8% top-5 ligand RMSD < 2 Å and 3.2 Å median top-1 RMSD, outperforming DiffDock (41.3% and 3.6 Å respectively). The self-augmentation variant (GroupBind-P2R-S) reaches 47.4% top-5 RMSD < 2 Å. With native reference pockets (not used for fair comparison), top-1 RMSD < 2 Å improves to 36.6% (S) and 36.3% (A). Under perfect selection (K=40), GroupBind achieves ~55–60% success rate versus 47% for DiffDock, indicating substantial headroom for a stronger confidence model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GroupBind outperforms all seven baselines on the PDBBind blind docking benchmark. Against the closest deep-learning baseline DiffDock, GroupBind-P2R-A improves top-5 RMSD < 2 Å from 41.3% to 48.8% (+7.5 pp) and reduces the 50th-percentile top-1 RMSD from 3.6 Å to 3.2 Å. The improvement over search-based methods (GNINA, SMINA) and their P2Rank/EquiBind-augmented variants is even more pronounced, with top-5 RMSD < 2 Å gains of 10–19 pp over GNINA/SMINA and 10–11 pp over P2Rank+GNINA. Against TANKBind, GroupBind nearly doubles the top-5 success rate (48.8% vs. 24.5%). On the ComBind benchmark (30 proteins), GroupBind consistently outperforms DiffDock but still trails the physics-based ComBind method, which benefits from using the reference ligand's center of mass to define the docking grid. On PoseBuster, GroupBind achieves a higher overall success rate than DiffDock but a lower PB-Valid rate, suggesting augmented ligands can occasionally introduce unreasonable protein-ligand distances.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DiffDock diffusion-based docking framework by introducing group-level information: multiple ligands known to bind the same protein pocket are docked simultaneously rather than in isolation. The triangle attention module is inspired by AlphaFold2's geometric consistency mechanism but is repurposed to enforce interaction consistency *across different ligands* (if atom j in ligand A interacts with residue i, the corresponding atom k in similar ligand B should also interact with i), a fundamentally different motivation from prior uses in TANKBind or E3Bind. The group ligand graph construction via Maximum Common Substructure and 3D matching draws on cheminformatics tools, while the equivariant message passing follows the e3nn-based architecture of DiffDock. The broader conceptual lineage connects to consistency-inspired modeling in biology (conserved sequences, network alignment) and semi-supervised learning, but this is the first end-to-end deep learning docking method to exploit the observation that co-binding ligands adopt similar poses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GroupBind establishes a new paradigm for molecular docking where auxiliary ligands from the same binding group serve as inductive bias, achieving meaningful SOTA improvements on PDBBind blind docking (47–49% top-5 <2Å vs. 41% for DiffDock). The finding that *dissimilar* ligands in a group are more beneficial than similar ones (Spearman ρ = −0.39) is practically important for selecting augmentation ligands. Key limitations include the dependence on known binding data for the target protein (limiting applicability to novel targets), the high computational cost of the triangle attention module, and a lower PB-Valid rate on PoseBuster suggesting augmented ligands can introduce physically unreasonable poses. An open direction is learning to selectively identify which non-binding ligands are beneficial, potentially removing the need for known binding information.
