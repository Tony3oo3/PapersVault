## Problem

Existing incomplete multi-view clustering methods ignore missing samples when constructing bipartite similarity, relying only on observed unpaired samples that lose latent information and cause unbalanced cluster distributions. Additionally, they employ a single prototype quantity for all views, which is inadequate because each view possesses unique features that a fixed number of prototypes cannot fully characterize.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250257/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes incomplete multi-view data matrices {D_v ∈ R^{d_v × n}} along with observed sample index vectors {r_v} indicating which samples are present on each view, and produces cluster assignments by performing spectral grouping on eigenvectors of a stacked multi-scale consensus similarity matrix. The intermediate outputs are multi-scale view-specific bipartite graphs (prototypes × samples) and a set of consensus graphs G_s at different prototype scales, which are row-concatenated before spectral embedding. The similarity values are relaxed from the conventional [0,1] range to [-1,1] to more freely characterize pairwise relationships.

## Method

SIIHPC first transforms partial bipartition learning under prototype orthogonality into original-sample form via a reconstruction concept, splitting out observed similarity as H_{v,s}^T D_v W_v W_v^T, and relaxes non-negativity to [-1,1]. It then performs similarity-level imputation by introducing a learnable consensus graph G_s shared across all views, using the connection between view-specific bipartite similarities and G_s to recover incomplete parts through an imputation matrix Q_{v,s}. Instead of a single prototype count, it assigns a hybrid group of prototype quantities {m_1,...,m_S} per view with learnable weights a_{v,s} to flexibly extract view-specific features at multiple scales. The joint objective is solved via a four-step alternating scheme where the prototype matrix update uses an auxiliary function with theoretically proven monotonic-increasing convergence (via SVD-based iteration), the imputation matrix is solved by direct index assignment with truncation, the consensus graph is solved column-wise as a QP, and the weight matrix has a closed-form solution. Final clustering is obtained by row-stacking all multi-scale consensus graphs and applying spectral grouping to the resulting eigenvector embedding.

## Results

On BDGPFEA at 50% missing ratio, SIIHPC achieves ACC 40.31 / NMI 13.88 / PUR 40.31, outperforming the strongest baseline IMVCCBG (38.10/11.49/36.76) by over 2% in ACC and 2.4% in NMI. On NUSOBJECT at 50%, SIIHPC reaches ACC 22.38 / NMI 9.21 / PUR 33.92, surpassing GSRIMC (20.91/7.58/32.04) and IMVCCBG (21.35/7.90/32.85). On the larger VGGFACEFIFTY dataset (16,936 samples, 50 clusters) at 50%, SIIHPC obtains ACC 12.31 / NMI 14.48 / PUR 13.21, leading IMVCCBG (11.52/13.29/12.40) and PIMVC (9.06/12.52/11.12). The paper reports that SIIHPC is consistently best on VGGFACEHUND and FASHMINST, and achieves only two sub-optimal results across VGGFACEFIFTY and YOUTUBEFACE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIIHPC outperforms all 13 baselines on BDGPFEA at 50% missing ratio, with the closest competitor being GSRIMC (ACC 38.30 vs. 40.31) and IMVCCBG (ACC 38.10 vs. 40.31), yielding a 2.2–2.4% ACC margin. On NUSOBJECT, SIIHPC leads GSRIMC by 1.47% ACC and 1.63% NMI, and IMVCCBG by 1.03% ACC and 1.31% NMI at 50%. On the high-dimensional VGGFACEFIFTY (16,936 samples, 50 clusters), SIIHPC achieves the best ACC (12.31) and NMI (14.48), beating IMVCCBG by 0.79% ACC and 1.19% NMI, while many baselines (GSRIMC, BGIMVSC, NGSPCGL, HCLSCGL) fail to run at all on this scale. On YOUTUBEFACE (63,896 samples), only IMVCCBG, OSLFIMVC, PSIMVC, and SAGL can complete, with IMVCCBG reaching ACC 72.39 at 50%; SIIHPC is reported as consistently best on the even larger VGGFACEHUND (36,287 samples) and FASHMINST (70,000 samples) where most baselines are infeasible. The paper also highlights that SIIHPC requires only 6.01 GB memory on YOUTUBEFACE versus 126.28 GB for OSLFIMVC and 82.96 GB for SAGL, confirming its scalability advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIIHPC operates within the prototype-based bipartite graph learning paradigm for incomplete multi-view clustering (IMVC), sharing the foundational reconstruction formulation with methods like PSIMVC, SAGL, and IMVCCBG. It departs from this lineage in two conceptual ways: (1) rather than discarding missing samples during bipartite similarity construction, it introduces a learnable consensus graph to impute incomplete similarity entries at the similarity level, and (2) it replaces the single prototype quantity used across all views with a group of hybrid prototype quantities per view, yielding multi-scale view-specific graphs. The alternating optimization with a monotonically-increasing auxiliary function for the orthogonally-constrained prototype update follows the standard SVD-based approach seen in prototype learning literature, but the four-step decomposition and the closed-form solution for the adaptive weighting matrix A are specific to this formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(n) computational complexity and modest memory footprint (5–10 GB on 70K-sample datasets) make SIIHPC one of the few IMVC methods that scales to large-scale benchmarks where many competitors (GSRIMC, HCPIMSC, BGIMVSC, HCLSCGL) simply fail to run, which is practically significant for real-world incomplete multi-view data. The similarity-level imputation idea—recovering missing similarity entries via a shared consensus graph rather than imputing raw features—offers a cleaner conceptual separation between what is observed and what is inferred. Open questions include whether the QP-based update for the consensus graph G_s remains tractable as the number of prototype groups S grows, whether the spectral clustering step at the end is a bottleneck for very large n, and whether the multi-scale prototype group [1k, 2k, …, 5k] is optimal or whether adaptive group selection could further improve results.
