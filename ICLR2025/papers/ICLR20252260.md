## Problem

The mechanism of self-distillation in linear probing—where a fixed pre-trained feature extractor is paired with a trainable linear classifier—remains unexplained, since traditional accounts rely on feature learning that is absent in this setting. Additionally, multi-round self-distillation, while effective, is computationally expensive, raising the question of whether its benefits can be replicated in a single training round.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252260/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, variable resolution for other benchmarks) processed through a frozen pre-trained backbone (ResNet34 or ViT-B) to produce unit-norm feature vectors in R^d. A two-layer head (linear + softmax) is trained on these fixed features, outputting a K-dimensional softmax probability vector. The distillation target is either the teacher's full softmax output or, for the proposed PLL variant, a two-hot vector assigning weight 1/2 to the teacher's top-2 predicted classes.

## Method

Under a linear approximation of the softmax (valid when logits are small), the paper derives a closed-form solution showing that each round of self-distillation applies a label-averaging operator governed by the eigendecomposition of the Gram matrix of input features: predictions evolve as (Y^(0) − 1/K)·Φ^(t) + 1/K, where Φ^(t) retains the Gram matrix's eigenvectors but raises eigenvalues to the t-th power, causing top-K eigenvectors (aligned with true classes) to dominate. This label averaging reduces reliance on noisy given labels and yields 100% population accuracy after a bounded number of rounds under mild corruption conditions. The key practical contribution is the PLL student model, which replaces the full softmax target with a refined two-hot vector (top-2 teacher predictions, each weighted 1/2), achieving the same clustering effect in a single round and outperforming multi-round distillation at high noise rates. The PLL student is trained with Generalized Cross Entropy (q=0.7) for stability, since standard CE with equally-weighted candidate labels can converge incorrectly.

## Results

The paper validates its theory on six image classification benchmarks (CIFAR-100, Caltech-101/256, Flowers-102, Food-101, StanfordCars) using ResNet34 and ViT-B as fixed feature extractors, reporting accuracy gains over the teacher model under symmetric, asymmetric, and superclass label corruption. Multi-round self-distillation students (t=2 through t=6) show progressive accuracy improvements as the number of rounds increases, with the 100% population accuracy regime expanding for higher t. The proposed PLL student model (single round, top-2 partial labels) outperforms all multi-round students at corruption rates η ≥ 0.6, achieving the largest gains in a single training round. Theoretical guarantees show the PLL student achieves 100% population accuracy under the mild condition [C]_{k,k} > [C]_{k,k'}, which is strictly less stringent than the multi-round condition involving the eigenvalue ratio (λ̃_K/λ̃_{K+1})^t.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is multi-round self-distillation (Born Again Neural Networks style, Furlanello et al. 2018), where students of the same architecture are trained iteratively using the previous model's softmax outputs as targets. Across all six datasets and three corruption scenarios, multi-round students (t=2–6) consistently outperform the teacher (t=1), with gains increasing monotonically with the number of rounds. However, the proposed PLL student model surpasses all multi-round students at high corruption rates (η ≥ 0.6) while requiring only a single training round, making it both more accurate and computationally cheaper in the high-noise regime. At lower corruption rates (η < 0.4), the multi-round students with more rounds can match or slightly exceed the PLL student, but the gap narrows as noise increases. The ablation on top-k partial labels (k=2,3,4,5) confirms that top-2 is the optimal choice: larger candidate sets dilute confidence and reduce accuracy, while top-1 (standard distillation) fails to include the true label for noisy samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of self-distillation from regression and binary-classification settings (Mobahi et al., 2020; Das & Sanghavi, 2023) to multi-class classification with cross-entropy loss, and crucially departs from the feature-learning explanation of Allen-Zhu & Li (2022) by showing that self-distillation benefits persist even in linear probing with a fixed feature extractor. The mechanism is reframed as label averaging governed by the eigenvectors of the input Gram matrix, a perspective absent from prior work. The proposed PLL student model draws on Partial Label Learning (Cour et al., 2011) to distill the teacher's 'dark knowledge' into a top-2 candidate set, achieving multi-round gains in a single round.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As foundation models make linear probing a dominant downstream paradigm, understanding when and why self-distillation helps without feature updates is practically important; the paper provides clean theoretical conditions (eigenvalue gaps, label corruption thresholds) that practitioners can check. The PLL student model offers a drop-in, single-round alternative to computationally expensive multi-round distillation, with theoretical guarantees of 100% population accuracy under mild label-corruption conditions. Key limitations include reliance on a linear softmax approximation and a block-diagonal Gram matrix structure; generalizing to fully non-linear softmax, arbitrary feature correlation patterns, and non-vision domains (e.g., NLP) remains open.
