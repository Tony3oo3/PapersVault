## Problem

Prior theoretical work established that Transformers perform gradient-descent-like algorithms during in-context learning, yet gradient descent converges only sublinearly for sparse recovery (LASSO), failing to explain Transformers' empirically superior ICL performance. This paper asks whether Transformers can instead implement learning-to-optimize (L2O) algorithms—specifically LISTA-type methods with linear convergence—and whether such an interpretation accounts for the generalization and structural-adaptation capabilities observed in practice.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an in-context sparse recovery instance consisting of a measurement matrix X ∈ R^{N×d}, an observation vector y = Xβ* ∈ R^N, and a query vector x_{N+1} ∈ R^d, all embedded into a (2d+2)×(2N+1) token matrix that interleaves measurement rows, labels, implicit β-estimate slots, and indicator bits. The output is a predicted label ŷ_{N+1} extracted via either a linear read-out (v^T h) or a query-based quadratic read-out (h^T V x_{N+1}), alongside the implicitly recovered sparse vector β* encoded in the forward-pass hidden states. The formulation supports variable prompt lengths n ≥ N₀, meaning the Transformer can recover β* from any sufficient subset of the N measurements.

## Method

The paper constructs a K-layer decoder-based Transformer with four attention heads per layer that provably implements a novel LISTA-type algorithm called LISTA-VM. The self-attention layer computes the gradient-like update β − D_n^T(Xβ − y) where the preconditioning matrix D_n = (1/(2n+1))X(M^(k))^T is context-dependent—derived from the measurement matrix X at inference time rather than fixed during pre-training—while the MLP layer implements the soft-thresholding operator S_θ via a combination of ReLU activations. This construction yields a linear convergence rate ‖β^{(K+1)} − β*‖ ≤ b_β e^{−α_n K}, improving over the sublinear O(1/K) rate of gradient descent. Crucially, because D_n adapts to X, the trained Transformer generalizes across different measurement matrices, different prompt lengths, and can exploit known support structure to further accelerate convergence—capabilities that classical LISTA-CP and ALISTA lack.

## Results

On in-context sparse recovery (d=20, S=3, N=10, K=12 iterations), the primary metric is label prediction loss. Under the Varying X setting, GPT-2 achieves the lowest prediction loss, followed by Small TF, then LISTA-VM, then FISTA and ISTA, with classical LISTA-type algorithms (LISTA, LISTA-CP, ALISTA) performing worst due to their inability to handle measurement matrix mismatches. Under Fixed X, all LISTA-type algorithms achieve performance comparable to Small TF. With a support constraint (support restricted to first 10 entries), LISTA-VM-SS achieves an in-context prediction error of approximately 0.07, roughly 10× better than standard LISTA-VM, and Small TF and GPT-2 show significant improvement while other baselines do not.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed Transformer-based LISTA-VM against four families of baselines: iterative methods (ISTA, FISTA), and three LISTA-type L2O algorithms (LISTA, LISTA-CP, ALISTA). Under the Varying X setting, GPT-2 and Small TF substantially outperform all baselines, with LISTA-VM also beating FISTA and ISTA; classical LISTA-type algorithms perform worst because they require the measurement matrix to be fixed during both training and testing. Under Fixed X, all LISTA-type algorithms (LISTA, LISTA-CP, ALISTA) achieve prediction losses comparable to Small TF, confirming that the Transformer's advantage is primarily in generalization across measurement matrices. Meta-trained LISTA and LISTA-CP (trained with varying X during training) still show prediction losses much higher than LISTA-VM, indicating that simply meta-training classical algorithms cannot replicate the Transformer's adaptive D_n = (1/(2n+1))X(M^(k))^T mechanism. With a support constraint, Small TF and GPT-2 improve significantly while ISTA, FISTA, and classical LISTA-type methods show no meaningful gain, and the support-selected LISTA-VM-SS variant achieves ~0.07 error (≈10× better than standard LISTA-VM), corroborating the theoretical result that structural knowledge accelerates convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work showing Transformers implement optimization algorithms during in-context learning (Von Oswald et al., 2023a; Bai et al., 2024; Ahn et al., 2024), which had established gradient descent or preconditioned gradient descent as the implicit mechanism. The key departure is demonstrating that Transformers can implement Learning-to-Optimize (L2O) algorithms—specifically a novel LISTA-VM variant—achieving linear convergence rather than the sublinear rates of gradient descent. It also builds on classical LISTA-type L2O algorithms (Gregor & LeCun, 2010; Chen et al., 2018; Liu & Chen, 2019) but shows the Transformer construction overcomes their critical limitation of requiring a fixed measurement matrix across training and testing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a more complete theoretical explanation for why even shallow Transformers outperform gradient-descent-based ICL, positioning L2O as the correct lens rather than first-order optimization. The generalization across varying measurement matrices and the ability to exploit structural priors (e.g., support constraints yielding ~10× improvement) have practical relevance for compressed sensing applications. However, the analysis relies on a noiseless setting, boundedness assumptions, and an explicit construction rather than showing that naturally trained Transformers converge to this behavior, leaving open whether the L2O interpretation holds at scale for more complex tasks.
