## Problem

Robust neural architecture search (NAS) requires evaluating the adversarial robustness of many candidate architectures, which is prohibitively expensive because mainstream evaluation methods (training from scratch, weight-sharing with adversarial training) involve costly training loops. Recent zero-cost proxies for robustness (e.g., CRoZe) still require generating adversarial examples and lack theoretical justification for why they reveal robustness. This paper asks whether adversarial robustness can be evaluated from initialized weights alone, without any training or adversarial example generation, with a provable theoretical link to robustness.

## Input / Output

The proxy consumes only the randomly initialized weights θ₀ of a candidate architecture and a small batch of N=25 unlabeled data samples (batch size 8), traversing M=11 layers with 50 sampled parameters per layer. It outputs a single scalar score R that is positively correlated with adversarial robustness: higher R indicates a more robust architecture. No labels, no training epochs, and no adversarial perturbations are needed; the entire evaluation is a forward/backward pass over the batch at initialization, yielding a score in O(MN²) time.

## Method

The proxy R is the product of two terms derived from the upper bound of adversarial loss: (1) an exponential of the minimum eigenvalue of the NTK matrix computed at initialization (Eq. 8), which captures how quickly the network would converge, and (2) a loss-landscape smoothness term ‖(l(x+hz*)−l(x))/h‖₂ that approximates the largest eigenvalue of the input Hessian (Eq. 7), capturing the curvature of the loss surface. The key theoretical move is replacing the adversarial NTK eigenvalue λ_min(Θ̂) with the natural NTK eigenvalue λ_min(Θ), justified by the known competing relationship between natural and adversarial accuracy, and replacing the adversarial loss at initialization with the input Hessian's spectral norm via Proposition 2 (Zhao et al., 2020). This eliminates all adversarial example generation while preserving a provable upper-bound relationship to adversarial loss. Applied to NAS, the proxy scores 1,000 randomly sampled DARTS-space architectures in 0.017 GPU days (vs. 0.5–4.0 for training-based robust NAS), yielding architectures competitive under white-box (FGSM, PGD, APGD, AutoAttack) and black-box transfer attacks.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed zero-cost proxy searches an architecture achieving 85.60% natural accuracy and 49.97% AutoAttack accuracy, surpassing all robust NAS baselines (RACL: 48.59%, RobNet-free: 48.56%, DSRNA: 44.87%) and the prior training-free proxy CRoZe (48.14%). The search cost is only 0.017 GPU days, representing a >20× speedup over the best training-based robust NAS methods (RACL: 0.5 GPU days, WsrNet: 4.0 GPU days) and ~12× faster than CRoZe (0.2 GPU days). On ImageNet, the method achieves 52.71% natural accuracy and 11.96% PGD-20 accuracy, outperforming RACL (51.59%/10.49%) and CRoZe (49.95%/9.67%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all robust NAS baselines on every adversarial metric on CIFAR-10: it beats RACL (the strongest training-based competitor) by +1.38% on AA (49.97% vs 48.59%) and +0.57% on PGD-20 (52.75% vs 52.18%), while being ~29× faster (0.017 vs 0.5 GPU days). Against the prior training-free proxy CRoZe, it gains +1.83% on AA and is ~12× faster. On ImageNet, the method leads all competitors in both natural accuracy (52.71% vs RACL's 51.59%) and PGD-20 robustness (11.96% vs 10.49%). In the WRN-like search space, it surpasses NADAR by +1.11% natural accuracy and +0.46% PGD-20 accuracy at 26× lower cost. Under black-box transfer attacks, the searched architecture achieves the highest attack success rate against all target models (e.g., 38.76% vs DSRNA), confirming superior robustness. The only metric where the method is not best is natural accuracy on CIFAR-10, where DenseNet-121 (85.95%) edges it out by 0.35%, though among NAS-derived architectures it achieves the highest natural accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-cost proxy paradigm from natural accuracy evaluation to adversarial robustness, building directly on NTK theory (Jacot et al., 2018; Xu et al., 2021) and input loss landscape analysis (Zhao et al., 2020). Prior robustness proxies (Lukasik et al., 2023; CRoZe, Ha et al., 2024) still required adversarial example generation, which this paper eliminates by substituting the adversarial NTK eigenvalue with the natural NTK eigenvalue and the adversarial initialization loss with the input Hessian's largest eigenvalue. The theoretical justification combines the NTK convergence bound (Mok et al., 2022) with the loss-landscape curvature approximation (Mok et al., 2021) in a way that prior work had not connected.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× reduction in search cost makes adversarially robust NAS practically accessible, and the open-sourced Tiny-RobustBench dataset fills a gap in evaluating zero-cost proxies under strong attacks where prior benchmarks (NAS-Bench-201-R) become uninformative. Key limitations include the proxy's known misjudgment of architectures without skip connections and its degradation under strong attacks on non-adversarially-trained networks. Open questions include whether the NTK-based formulation generalizes to non-CNN architectures (transformers, graph networks) and whether the competing-relationship assumption between natural and adversarial accuracy (used to justify the eigenvalue substitution) holds universally.
