## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods rely heavily on large retained datasets to preserve utility, which are often inaccessible in practice due to privacy, copyright, and expired authorization. Moreover, these methods treat unlearning as a one-off operation, failing to handle the continual arrival of unlearning requests in real-world settings, where cumulative utility degradation becomes catastrophic over time.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a text token sequence as input and, during inference, feeds it through all previously trained OOD detector backbones to compute layer-aggregated score vectors. These score vectors are compared against per-request OCSVM hyperspheres to produce a continuous soft weight w(x) ∈ (0,1) that determines the extent to which the unlearning LoRA is loaded. The final output is the LLM's text generation or classification result, where the LoRA contribution is scaled by w(x) rather than being binary on/off, enabling graded unlearning behavior per input.

## Method

O³ comprises two modules operating without any retained data. First, an Orthogonal LoRA maintains a single low-rank adapter across all unlearning requests, with an orthogonal regularization loss L_Orth = ‖(A^{t−1})ᵀAᵗ‖² that forces each new request's parameter subspace to be orthogonal to previous ones, preventing task entanglement and preserving prior unlearning efficacy. Second, an OOD detector is trained per request using a novel contrastive entropy loss (CEL) that aligns masked-view and MoCo-key-encoder representations at every transformer layer, supplemented by MLM loss to retain pretrained knowledge. At inference, a glocal-aware scoring mechanism combines per-layer Mahalanobis distance (global Gaussian assumption) with maximum instance-wise cosine similarity (local), aggregated into a score vector fitted by a one-class SVM; the resulting boundary distance is mapped through a mixed-Gaussian CDF to yield the soft weight w(x), which continuously scales the LoRA contribution as h′ = Wh + w(x)·ABh, allowing the model to partially or fully bypass unlearning depending on input similarity to each unlearning distribution.

## Results

On TOFU (fictitious knowledge generation) after three continual unlearning requests, O³ achieves the best unlearning effectiveness (S.U. 15.5%, D.U. 19.7%) while simultaneously preserving the highest utility (R.D. 84.9%, R.A. 88.8%, W.F. 86.1%), closely matching the base model's utility (85.8/89.0/86.3). On CLINC150 (intent classification) after three requests, O³ achieves S.U. 40.6% and D.U. 42.4% while maintaining R.D. 97.8%, MRPC 86.6%, and RTE 89.0%—the best RTE score among all methods. The U²R (Unlearning-Utility Ratio) metric confirms O³ consistently achieves the highest balance across all three tasks, and the framework requires only 20M trainable parameters (vs. 6,758M for baselines) and half the training data since no retained dataset is needed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

O³ decisively outperforms all eight baselines on the U²R balance metric across all three tasks. GradAsc, GradDif, and SOGD suffer catastrophic utility collapse after 2–3 requests (R.A. and W.F. drop to 0% on TOFU; MRPC and RTE drop below 25% on CLINC150), rendering them unusable in practice. EUL achieves strong unlearning on CLINC150 (S.U. 0%) but degrades RTE to 76.3% after three requests, while NPO preserves utility well but fails to unlearn effectively (S.U. 99.9% on CLINC150, 77.6% on TOFU). PO and SOPO show moderate unlearning but their utility degrades progressively (e.g., SOPO's R.D. drops from 83.7% to 80.2% on TOFU across three requests), whereas O³ maintains utility within 1% of the base model throughout. Critically, O³ achieves all results without any retained data, using only 20M trainable parameters (LoRA rank 8) versus 6,758M for full-model baselines, and requires half the training data quantity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM unlearning literature (GradAsc, PO, NPO, SOGD, SOPO, EUL) by addressing two gaps those methods leave open: the assumption of full retained-data access and the limitation to single-shot unlearning operations. The orthogonal LoRA module builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) by adding a cross-request orthogonality constraint on the A-matrix columns to disentangle parameter updates, while the OOD detector repurposes MoCo's (He et al., 2020) momentum key-encoder mechanism into a novel contrastive entropy loss tailored for unsupervised text OOD detection. The soft-weighted inference mechanism, which scales LoRA loading by a per-input similarity score, departs from both hard parameter-editing unlearning and in-context refusal approaches by making the unlearning effect input-adaptive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practically critical gap: in real deployments, LLMs face continuous unlearning requests (privacy deletions, outdated knowledge, copyright takedowns) while the original training data becomes inaccessible, yet existing methods degrade catastrophically under these conditions. The finding that a method using zero retained data outperforms retained-data baselines in continual settings is significant for practitioners who cannot assume full training-data access. Open questions include scalability to much larger models (beyond 7B), robustness of the OOD detector against adversarial evasion, the growing storage cost of maintaining one OOD detector per unlearning request, and whether the orthogonal LoRA constraint remains effective as the number of requests grows beyond the tested 3–4.
