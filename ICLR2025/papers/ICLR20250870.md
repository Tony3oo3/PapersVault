## Problem

Existing incremental object detection methods rely on response-level knowledge distillation, which treats pseudo-labels as an implicit byproduct rather than fully exploiting their potential. The paper identifies three critical limitations: pseudo-label quality is capped by the imperfect previous model, fixed score thresholds ignore class-wise distribution divergence, and confidence scores do not linearly reflect localization quality, all of which degrade continual learning performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250870/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images from COCO 2017 (80 categories partitioned into mutually exclusive sets per step) and outputs bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores. At each incremental step, the model receives ground-truth annotations only for the current step's categories, while pseudo-labels for previously learned categories are generated offline by the previous model and stored as lightweight .pkl files (~10 MB for the full COCO dataset). The pseudo-labels are produced once per step and reused across all training epochs, requiring no additional GPU memory during training.

## Method

PseDet introduces three components to maximize pseudo-label utility. First, a Spatio-Temporal Enhancement Module applies invertible spatial augmentations (flips, scales) to inputs, runs the old model on each variant, reverses the transformations, and fuses predictions via NMS (IoU=0.4); temporal enhancement further collects the best pseudo-labels across all prior steps to prevent noise accumulation. Second, a Categorical Adaptive Label Selector exploits the bimodal confidence-score distribution per class (high-confidence true positives vs. low-confidence false positives) by running K-Means on a sliding queue of scores to dynamically set class-wise filtering thresholds without manual tuning. Third, a Confidence Score Calibration Supervision maps raw confidence scores through a sigmoid q(s)=sigmoid(α(s−δ)) with α=10, δ=0.5 to align scores with actual localization quality, then integrates this calibrated quality into a modified Quality Focal Loss where the pseudo-label target becomes ŷ_pl = τ·q_pl (IoU × calibrated quality) rather than a hard 0/1 assignment.

## Results

On the COCO benchmark, PseDet achieves 43.5 mAP (Deformable DETR) and 38.5 mAP (GFL) under the 40+40 one-step setting, and 44.7 mAP (Deformable DETR) under the 70+10 setting, reducing the absolute gap to the full-data upper bound to just 3.5% and 2.3% respectively. In the more challenging multi-step 40+10×4 scenario, PseDet reaches 41.2 mAP with Deformable DETR, a 16.4 mAP improvement over the previous best (SDDGR at 36.8). The method also generalizes to segmentation, achieving only 1.23 mIoU absolute gap on ADE20K (100+50) with SegFormer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PseDet outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated scenario. Against the strongest prior method SDDGR (diffusion-based generative replay), PseDet gains +0.5 mAP on 40+40 (43.5 vs 43.0) and a substantial +3.8 mAP on 70+10 (44.7 vs 40.9) with Deformable DETR; in the 4-step multi-step setting the gap widens to +4.4 mAP (41.2 vs 36.8). Against the distillation-based ERD, PseDet improves by +1.6 mAP (GFL, 40+40) and +4.3 mAP (GFL, 70+10). CL-DETR, the only other transformer-based baseline, is surpassed by +1.5 mAP on 40+40 and +4.3 mAP on 70+10. The older KD methods (LwF, RILOD, SID) are outperformed by 12–27 mAP, confirming that the pseudo-labeling paradigm with the proposed enhancements substantially surpasses direct response-level distillation. Notably, PseDet also requires no additional GPU memory during training (single model, offline pseudo-label generation), unlike ERD which loads two models simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the dominant knowledge-distillation paradigm in incremental object detection (LwF, ERD, CL-DETR, SDDGR) as a special case of pseudo-labeling, then identifies three concrete weaknesses in how pseudo-labels are generated, filtered, and weighted. It builds directly on GFL's Quality Focal Loss by adapting it to continuous label representations for pseudo-label supervision, and draws on standard tools (test-time augmentation ensemble, K-Means clustering, sigmoid calibration) in a novel combination. The key departure from prior work is treating pseudo-label quality as a first-class optimization target—via spatiotemporal enhancement, per-class adaptive thresholding, and non-linear score-to-quality mapping—rather than implicitly trusting the teacher model's outputs as in response-level distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gain is notable: offline pseudo-label generation requires only one model in memory (~4.5 GB) versus the two-model online distillation of ERD (>10 GB), making the approach more accessible for resource-constrained settings. The finding that confidence scores exhibit a non-linear relationship with localization quality challenges the common assumption that higher confidence directly implies better boxes, and the sigmoid calibration is a lightweight fix applicable beyond IOD (e.g., semi-supervised detection). Open questions include whether the bimodal score distribution assumption underlying K-Means holds under extreme class imbalance or very long incremental sequences, and whether the approach extends to open-vocabulary or few-shot incremental settings where the teacher model is even less reliable.
