## Problem

Existing RLHF methods for fine-tuning diffusion models rely on predefined heuristic reward functions or pretrained reward models trained on large-scale datasets, making them inapplicable to personalized or novel tasks where such data is costly to collect. The closest prior work, D3PO, uses online human feedback but still requires approximately 5K feedback instances per task, placing a heavy burden on human evaluators and limiting practical deployment for individual preference alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251976/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a user-specified text prompt and online human feedback collected iteratively during training: each epoch, a batch of 64–128 images is generated and a human evaluator labels them as 'good' or 'bad' and selects one 'best' image from the 'good' set. The output is a LoRA-fine-tuned Stable Diffusion v1.5 model paired with a refined noise distribution π_HERO (a Gaussian mixture over previously selected 'good' and 'best' initial latents), which together produce prompt-aligned images at inference time using any standard DDIM sampler.

## Method

HERO introduces two mechanisms to maximize the information extracted from each human feedback instance. First, Feedback-Aligned Representation Learning trains a small embedding network (3 conv layers + FC to 4096-d) with a triplet margin loss where the 'best' latent is the anchor, 'good' latents are positives, and 'bad' latents are negatives; the resulting cosine similarity to the best representation converts discrete human labels into continuous reward signals for DDPO fine-tuning via LoRA. Second, Feedback-Guided Image Generation samples new initial noise latents from a Gaussian mixture centered on the stored 'good' and 'best' initial noises from the previous iteration (with small variance ε₀²=0.1 and a best-image ratio β=0.5), concentrating generation near human-preferred regions of the latent space for faster convergence. Theoretical analysis shows that samples from this refined distribution still concentrate on the SD prior's unit sphere (Proposition A.1) and that the initial noise retains mutual information with the generated latent through the finite-step SDE solver (Proposition A.2), validating the approach as a principled refinement of the sampling prior.

## Results

On the hand deformation correction task, HERO achieves 34.2% normalcy rate with only 1,152 online feedback instances, matching D3PO's 33.3% at 5K feedback (4× more feedback-efficient). Across four diverse T2I tasks (blue-rose, black-cat, narcissus, mountain), HERO achieves success rates of 80.7%, 75.0%, 91.2%, and 99.5% respectively, outperforming all baselines on every task. The pretrained SD model achieves only 35–42% success on these tasks, while DreamBooth reaches 45–85% depending on the task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HERO outperforms DreamBooth on all four tasks, with the largest margins on blue-rose (80.7% vs. 47.9%) and black-cat (75.0% vs. 45.3%), where DreamBooth struggles with attribute reasoning and spatial constraints. On the hand deformation task, HERO matches D3PO's 33.3% normalcy rate (achieving 34.2%) while requiring only 1,152 feedback instances versus D3PO's 5,000, a 4× reduction in human feedback. Against DDPO with the pretrained PickScore-v1 reward model, HERO wins on all four tasks (e.g., 99.5% vs. 37.5% on mountain), demonstrating that large-scale pretrained reward models fail on personalized and reasoning-specific tasks. SD-Enhanced (GPT-4 prompt rewriting) is competitive only on the mountain task (93.8%) but underperforms HERO on the other three tasks, confirming that prompt engineering alone cannot resolve counting, spatial reasoning, or feasibility issues.

## Relation to Prior Work

HERO extends D3PO (Yang et al., 2024b), the first method to fine-tune diffusion models using online human feedback without a pretrained reward model, by introducing two mechanisms that reduce feedback cost: a contrastive-learning-based embedding that converts discrete 'good/bad/best' labels into continuous reward signals for DDPO, and a Gaussian-mixture noise prior centered on human-preferred initial latents to accelerate convergence. It builds on DDPO (Black et al., 2024) for the RL update rule and LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, while departing from DPO-style methods (Diffusion-DPO, D3PO) that require large offline preference datasets or thousands of online feedback instances. The paper also positions itself against supervised fine-tuning approaches like DreamBooth, which require reference images and cannot handle reasoning or counting tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× reduction in required human feedback (1,152 vs. ~5,000) makes personalized diffusion model fine-tuning practically feasible for individual users or small teams who cannot afford extensive annotation. The transferability results—where personal aesthetic preferences and content-safety concepts learned from one prompt generalize to unseen related prompts—suggest the learned representations capture generalizable structure rather than overfitting to a single prompt. Limitations include reliance on a human evaluator in the loop (limiting automation), evaluation on relatively simple single-prompt tasks, and the assumption that the base model can already generate prompt-matching images with non-zero probability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-constraint or compositional prompts, whether the Gaussian-mixture initialization degrades over many iterations, and how it interacts with larger or newer diffusion architectures.
