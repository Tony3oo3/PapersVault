## Problem

Audio-driven talking body generation remains largely unexplored: existing body animation methods rely on video-driven pose conditions, while audio-driven approaches are confined to portrait scenarios. Two-stage audio-to-pose-to-video pipelines exist but suffer from system complexity, limited expressiveness of intermediate representations, and annotation errors. The core challenges for a one-stage audio-driven body model are (1) underfitting of fine local details (hands, face) that occupy small frame regions but carry most identity and semantic information, and (2) high motion uncertainty arising from the weak correlation between audio cues and limb movement patterns.

## Input / Output

The primary input is a single half-body human reference image paired with a speech audio clip; the model outputs a 12-frame synchronized talking body video at approximately 640×384 resolution (dynamic aspect ratio in stage-2 training). Audio is encoded via Wav2Vec into a 10752-dimensional multi-scale feature vector with a temporal window of 5 frames per video frame. The framework also supports video-driven mode (reference image + driving video with DWPose keypoints replacing the body movement map) and multimodal mode (reference image + audio + 2D hand keypoints), each requiring separate training. Inference uses dual classifier-free guidance (scale 2.5 for reference, 4.5 for audio) and achieves ~65× RTF on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

CyberHost builds on a Latent Diffusion Model with a dual U-Net architecture (reference net + 3D denoising U-Net with AnimateDiff temporal layers) and introduces two key mechanisms. First, the Region Attention Module (RAM) is inserted at multiple U-Net stages for hands and face: it combines a learnable spatio-temporal latents bank (orthogonalized via Gram-Schmidt, with separate spatial and temporal basis vectors of dimensions 512×512 and 64×512) that captures identity-agnostic structural and motion priors, with an identity descriptor extracted from cropped local images (ArcFace for face at 256px, a deeper pose-encoder-like network for hands at 128px), both gated by a predicted regional attention mask. Second, Human-Prior-Guided Conditions reduce motion uncertainty: a body movement map (augmented rectangular box constraining thorax motion) is encoded and added as a residual to the noised latent, a hand clarity score (Laplacian standard deviation of cropped hand regions) is injected into the time embedding, and a pose-aligned reference feature (skeleton map from the reference image) is merged into the reference latent. Training uses a two-stage curriculum (image generation then video generation) on 200 hours of half-body speech data across 10k+ identities, with a local enhancement loss that reweights the diffusion objective for face/hand regions and adds auxiliary hand keypoint heatmap supervision.

## Results

On the audio-driven talking body (A2V-B) task, CyberHost achieves FID 32.97, FVD 555.8, CSIM 0.514, SyncC 6.627, and HKC 0.884, substantially outperforming the two-stage baseline (DiffGesture+MimicMotion: FID 58.95, FVD 1515.9) and DiffTED (FID 95.45, FVD 1185.8). In the video-driven body reenactment setting, CyberHost reaches FID 20.04, FVD 181.6, and AKD 3.123, surpassing AnimateAnyone (FVD 834.6, AKD 5.747) and MimicMotion (FVD 420.6, AKD 8.536). For audio-driven talking head, CyberHost attains FID 25.79 and FVD 552.6, beating Hallo (FID 35.96, FVD 742.9) and EchoMimic (FID 35.37, FVD 828.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CyberHost decisively outperforms both two-stage audio-driven baselines: DiffTED (FID 95.45 vs. 32.97; FVD 1185.8 vs. 555.8) and the DiffGesture+MimicMotion pipeline (FID 58.95 vs. 32.97; FVD 1515.9 vs. 555.8), with the largest gap in lip-sync (SyncC 6.63 vs. 0.93 and 0.50). Against Vlogger on the shared test set, CyberHost wins on CSIM (0.522 vs. 0.470), SyncC (7.90 vs. 0.60), SyncD (7.53 vs. 11.13), and HKV (18.75 vs. 9.84), though Vlogger retains a slight edge in HKC (0.923 vs. 0.907) due to its minimal-motion test videos. In video-driven body reenactment, CyberHost surpasses AnimateAnyone (FVD 181.6 vs. 834.6; AKD 3.12 vs. 5.75), MimicMotion (FVD 181.6 vs. 420.6; AKD 3.12 vs. 8.54), and DisCo (FVD 181.6 vs. 1490.4; AKD 3.12 vs. 9.31) across all metrics. For audio-driven talking head, CyberHost beats Hallo (FID 25.79 vs. 35.96; FVD 552.6 vs. 742.9) and EchoMimic (FID 25.79 vs. 35.37; FVD 552.6 vs. 828.9) on image/video quality and lip-sync, though Hallo achieves a marginally higher CSIM (0.619 vs. 0.581). VExpress is clearly outperformed on every metric (FID 25.79 vs. 65.09; FVD 552.6 vs. 1356.5). The visual comparison with ANGIE on the PATS dataset shows CyberHost producing significantly clearer video with natural motion despite not being trained on that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

CyberHost builds on the Latent Diffusion Model framework (Blattmann et al., 2023) with a 3D U-Net extended via AnimateDiff's temporal module, and adopts the dual-U-Net reference architecture from AnimateAnyone and motion-frame conditioning from TryOnDiffusion/EMO. It departs from prior audio-driven body animation work (Vlogger, DiffTED, Speech2Gesture), which all rely on two-stage pipelines with intermediate pose or mesh representations, by formulating the problem as a single end-to-end diffusion process driven directly by audio. The Region Attention Module and Human-Prior-Guided Conditions are novel architectural contributions not present in any prior body animation or talking head system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first one-stage audio-driven talking body generation framework, eliminating the information bottleneck and error accumulation inherent in two-stage pose/mesh intermediates. The data efficiency finding—achieving superior results to Vlogger with ~1/11th the training duration (200h vs. 2200h) and 1/80th the identities (10k vs. 800k)—suggests algorithmic design can compensate for data scale in this domain. Open questions remain around full-body generation (current model degrades significantly), robustness to cartoon/exaggerated proportions and complex audio (singing, background noise), and whether the Wav2Vec audio encoder is a bottleneck for more nuanced prosody-to-gesture mapping.
