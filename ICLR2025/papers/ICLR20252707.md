## Problem

Consistency models (CMs) enable 1-2 step image generation but are extremely expensive and unstable to train: the differential consistency condition (df/dt = 0) becomes hard to optimize as the discretization interval Δt shrinks, causing accumulated errors across subintervals that slow convergence or cause divergence. This 'curse of consistency' means state-of-the-art CMs require weeks of GPU training with complex hyperparameter schedules, substantially limiting their adoption.

## Input / Output

The model takes Gaussian noise ε (and optionally a class label for conditional generation) and produces a synthetic image in 1 or 2 function evaluations. During training, the forward process constructs pairs (x_t, x_r) = (x_0 + t·ε, x_0 + r·ε) sharing the same noise direction at two noise levels, with the consistency loss enforcing f_θ(x_t) ≈ f_sg(θ)(x_r). Experiments use 32×32 CIFAR-10, 64×64 ImageNet, and 512×512 ImageNet (in latent space), with the model parameterized via EDM's skip/out scaling to satisfy the boundary condition f(x_0, 0) = x_0 by design.

## Method

ECT's key insight is that diffusion models are a special case of consistency models with the loosest possible discretization (r = 0, Δt = t), so diffusion pretraining is itself a stage of consistency training. The method initializes from a pretrained diffusion model and progressively tightens the consistency condition via a continuous-time mapping function r/t = 1 − (1/q^⌈iters/d⌉)·n(t) that shrinks Δt from t toward dt over training, where n(t) = 1 + k·σ(−bt) balances gradient difficulty across noise levels. The loss combines a flexible timestep weighting w̄(t) (e.g., SNR(t) + 1/σ_data²) with an adaptive per-sample weighting w(Δ) = 1/(‖Δ‖²₂ + c²)^p that controls gradient variance, replacing iCT's fixed 1/(t−r) coupling. This two-stage pretraining+tuning scheme requires only ~10% of the total compute for the tuning stage, achieving 2-step FID of 2.73 on CIFAR-10 in 1 GPU hour while matching or exceeding iCT trained for hundreds of GPU hours.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, ECM (400k iters) achieves a 2-step FID of 2.11, surpassing iCT-deep (2.24) and Consistency Distillation (2.93) while using roughly 1/4 of the total training compute. On ImageNet 64×64, ECM-XL* (102.4M images) reaches a 2-step FID of 1.67, outperforming iCT-deep (2.77) and CD (4.70) with only 33% of iCT's overall computational budget. In a 1-hour single-A100 prototyping setting, ECT achieves a 2-step FID of 2.73 on CIFAR-10, matching CD trained for hundreds of GPU hours. The paper further demonstrates classic power-law scaling of FD_DINOv2 with training compute (Pearson r = −0.9996 for 2-step) and log-linear scaling with model size and FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECT (ECM) decisively outperforms all few-step baselines on both datasets. On CIFAR-10, 2-step ECM (400k iters) achieves FID 2.11, beating iCT-deep (2.24), iCT (2.46), and CD with LPIPS (2.93) while using ~1/4 of iCT's total training FLOPs. On ImageNet 64×64, ECM-XL* (2-step, FID 1.67) surpasses iCT-deep (2.77) by over 1.1 FID points and CD (4.70) by nearly 3 FID points, all at 33% of iCT's compute. Against diffusion model baselines, 2-step ECM matches Score SDE-deep (FID 2.20 vs 2.20 on CIFAR-10) at 1/1000th the inference cost (2 NFEs vs 2000), and outperforms EDM with DPM-Solver-v3 (2.51 at 10 NFEs). Against GAN baselines on class-conditional CIFAR-10 (FD_DINOv2), 1-step ECM (198.51) beats StyleGAN-XL (204.60) and 2-step ECM (128.63) beats PFGM++ (141.65) and EDM (145.20). The only comparison where ECT does not lead is against EDM2-XL (FID 1.33 at 63 NFEs on ImageNet 64×64), which uses far more inference steps but also substantially more training compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Consistency Model (CM) framework of Song et al. (2023) and the improved training recipe of iCT (Song and Dhariwal, 2023), while leveraging the EDM/EDM2 diffusion model parameterization (Karras et al., 2022, 2024) as its initialization. The key conceptual departure is reframing diffusion models as a special case of CMs with maximal discretization (Δt = t), which motivates a continuous-time training schedule that smoothly interpolates from standard diffusion pretraining to the full differential consistency condition. Unlike Consistency Distillation, which requires a frozen teacher model, and unlike iCT, which trains from scratch with a discrete curriculum, ECT uses a single network throughout both stages and progressively tightens the consistency constraint without any external teacher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ECT reduces CM training cost to roughly 1/4–1/3 of iCT while matching or exceeding its quality, and the tuning stage alone accounts for ≤10% of total compute—making CMs far more accessible for research and deployment. The first systematic scaling-law study for CMs (power-law in compute, model size, and FLOPs) provides a roadmap for scaling these models and reveals a practical sweet spot where smaller 2-step models outperform larger 1-step ones. Open questions include inference-optimal and test-time compute scaling for CMs, whether PEFT can further reduce tuning cost, how to determine optimal intermediate sampling schedules, and whether the approach extends to conditional/text-to-image settings at scale. A noted limitation is that ECT requires access to a training dataset, though the data-free ECD variant partially addresses this.
