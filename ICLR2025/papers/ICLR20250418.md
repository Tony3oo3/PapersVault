## Problem

Existing work treats diffusion models as isolated components for dense perception—either as off-the-shelf data augmentation tools or frozen feature extractors—thereby overlooking the diffusion-denoising process that inherently links generative and discriminative learning. No prior framework simultaneously preserves the generative capability of a diffusion model while exploiting it to produce in-distribution multi-modal data that actively improves the discriminative task within a single integrated model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250418/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For discriminative inference, the model takes a 512×512 RGB image, encodes it to latent space via a frozen VAE, performs one-step denoising through the U-Net to extract multi-scale features (at 8×8, 16×16, 32×32, 64×64), and decodes a dense attribute map (surface normal, depth, semantic label, etc.) via a lightweight task head. For generation, it takes Gaussian noise (or partially-noised real latents at timestep T) plus a BLIP-2 text caption as condition, denoises to produce a new RGB image, and simultaneously runs the same one-step denoising on the clean latent to produce the corresponding annotation map—yielding coherent RGB-attribute pairs. The denoising U-Net is frozen by default, so only the task head parameters are updated, keeping the model lightweight (~96M params, 10 GB GPU memory).

## Method

Diff-2-in-1 builds on Stable Diffusion v1-5 and introduces a self-improving learning mechanism with two parameter sets: a creation network θ_C that generates in-distribution synthetic multi-modal pairs, and an exploitation network θ_E that learns the discriminative task on both real and synthetic data. Data generation uses a partial-noise strategy inspired by SDEdit: real training latents are corrupted at an intermediate timestep T (e.g., T=600 out of 1000) rather than from pure Gaussian, balancing diversity against domain shift. θ_E is trained with a combined supervised loss on real pairs and a synthetic loss on generated pairs, while θ_C is updated via exponential moving average (α≈0.998) from θ_E, creating a self-improving cycle where better discriminative features guide higher-quality generation. A warm-up stage first activates the task head on original data before the self-improving loop begins, and the entire pipeline is plug-and-play: it replaces the encoder of existing discriminative backbones (Bae et al., iDisc, VPD, InvPT, TaskPrompter) with the diffusion feature extractor while preserving their decoders.

## Results

On NYUv2 surface normal estimation with only 795 training samples, Diff-2-in-1 on iDisc achieves 68.7% at 11.25° threshold, 83.7% at 22.5°, and 88.4% at 30°, with a mean angle error of 12.7° and RMSE of 21.6 — substantially outperforming the same backbone trained on 30,816 samples (63.8%, 79.8%, 85.6%, mean 14.6°). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, Diff-2-in-1 on VPD reaches 54.5 mIoU, surpassing VPD alone (53.7) and DA-Fusion augmentation (54.0). On the NYUD-MT multi-task benchmark, Diff-2-in-1 on TaskPrompter achieves 55.73 mIoU (semseg), 0.5041 RMSE (depth), and 17.91 mErr (normal), improving over both the base TaskPrompter and DA-Fusion-augmented variants across all three tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-2-in-1 consistently outperforms all 24 compared baselines across every evaluation setting. On NYUv2 surface normal estimation, it beats the strongest prior methods (Bae et al. with 30,816 samples: 62.2°/14.9 mean; iDisc with 30,816 samples: 63.8°/14.6 mean) by 5–6° in 11.25° accuracy and 1.7–1.9° in mean angle error, while using only 795 samples (39× less data). Against the closest augmentation-based competitors, DA-Fusion (58.1°/16.8 on Bae; 58.7°/17.3 on iDisc) and GNA (56.4°/17.3; 56.9°/18.1), Diff-2-in-1 gains 9–10° in 11.25° accuracy and 3.6–4.9° in mean error, demonstrating that its self-improving mechanism and in-distribution data generation are substantially more effective than off-the-shelf diffusion augmentation. On ADE20K segmentation, Diff-2-in-1 on VPD (54.5 mIoU) surpasses VPD (53.7), DA-Fusion on VPD (54.0), and all non-diffusion baselines (Swin-L 52.1, ConvNeXt-XL 53.6, MAE-ViT-L 53.6, CLIP-ViT-B 50.6). On multi-task benchmarks (NYUD-MT and PASCAL-Context), Diff-2-in-1 on TaskPrompter improves over the base TaskPrompter by +0.43 mIoU (semseg), −0.0111 RMSE (depth), and −0.56 mErr (normal) on NYUD-MT, and by +0.04 mIoU (semseg), +0.84 mIoU (parsing), and −0.08 mErr (normal) on PASCAL-Context, while also outperforming DA-Fusion-augmented variants on all metrics. Finally, against Marigold (a diffusion-based dense prediction method that repurposes the denoising U-Net), Diff-2-in-1 on Bae et al. outperforms all three Marigold variants (Pretrain: 50.5°/20.9 mean; SD: 48.7°/18.1; Finetune: 64.0°/14.2) while being 3.9× faster per iteration and using 9× less GPU memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2022) as its backbone and draws from two distinct lines of work: (1) using diffusion features for dense perception (VPD, ODISE, DDP) and (2) diffusion-based data augmentation (DA-Fusion, SDEdit). The key departure is unifying these into a single integrated model that simultaneously performs multi-modal generation and dense perception, rather than treating the diffusion model as either a frozen feature extractor or a standalone augmentation tool. The self-improving mechanism adapts the mean-teacher EMA paradigm (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) to create a feedback loop between a data-creation network and a data-exploitation network, both sharing the same diffusion backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path to data-efficient dense prediction by leveraging the generative capacity of pretrained diffusion models to create in-distribution multi-modal training pairs, achieving SOTA surface normal estimation with only 795 samples. The finding that finetuning the diffusion backbone (even with LoRA) underperforms the proposed frozen-backbone + self-improving approach challenges the intuitive strategy of domain-adapting the generator. Limitations include the computational cost of multi-step denoising for data generation, and the approach is currently validated primarily on indoor scene understanding tasks; extending to 3D detection, outdoor scenes, and more efficient one-step generation remain open directions.
