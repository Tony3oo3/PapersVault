## Problem

Existing agent-tuning methods (Agent-FLAN, AgentGym, AgentGen) achieve strong performance on held-in tasks but fail to generalize to held-out environments, exhibiting severe formatting errors, illogical reasoning, and getting stuck repeating the same erroneous actions despite explicit negative feedback from the environment. The paper identifies that this stems from overfitting to a few manual agent environments and memorizing observation-action pairs rather than learning to adapt, and connects agent generalization to the model's ability to self-refine based on environment feedback.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a task goal and sequential environment observations (text) in a multi-turn ReAct chat format, producing a thought (chain-of-thought reasoning) followed by a structured action command at each turn, with a maximum of 30 turns per episode. During training, the synthetic data is structured as DM observations (user turns) and player thought+action pairs (assistant turns) in ReAct format, where the trajectory intentionally contains error turns followed by refinement turns. The loss function is modified to zero out the loss on erroneous action tokens, so the model only learns from correct and corrective steps.

## Method

AgentRefine proposes a three-stage data synthesis pipeline inspired by tabletop RPGs: (1) script generation, where a strong LLM (GPT-4o) creates diverse environments, tasks, and validated actions from Persona-Hub personas; (2) trajectory generation, where the LLM simultaneously plays Dungeon Master and player to produce multi-turn interactions containing deliberate errors; and (3) verification, which checks format validity via regex, ensures task completion, and enforces at least two error-refinement cycles per trajectory. The key training innovation is Refinement Tuning: a modified SFT loss that masks the cross-entropy on erroneous turn tokens (1(A_j)=0), preventing the model from learning incorrect reasoning while still exposing it to the error→observation→correction pattern. This teaches the model to recognize its mistakes and explore alternative actions based on environment feedback rather than memorizing fixed trajectories, yielding significantly better held-out generalization, robustness to action-description perturbations, and thought diversity compared to baselines.

## Results

On the LLaMA-3-8B series, AgentRefine achieves 14.4% Success Rate on SciWorld, 16.6% on PDDL, and 10.0% on Jericho, substantially outperforming Agent-FLAN (1.1%, 8.3%, 0.0%) and AgentGym (18.9% held-in, 1.7%, 0.0%) on held-out tasks. AgentRefine leads Agent-FLAN by 13.3 percentage points on SciWorld Success Rate and by 16.6 points on PDDL. On the Mistral-7B series, AgentRefine similarly dominates held-out tasks (e.g., 11.7% PDDL vs. 0% for Agent-FLAN). AgentRefine also matches GPT-4o's Jericho Success Rate (10.0%) and approaches GPT-4o-mini on several metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Agent-FLAN (Chen et al., 2024), AgentRefine wins decisively on all held-out tasks: SciWorld Success 14.4% vs. 1.1%, PDDL 16.6% vs. 8.3%, Jericho 10.0% vs. 0.0%, and BabyAI 37.5% vs. 25.0%. Against AgentGym (Xi et al., 2024), AgentRefine wins on PDDL (16.6% vs. 1.7%) and Jericho (10.0% vs. 0.0%), but trails on BabyAI (37.5% vs. 47.3%) and SciWorld (14.4% vs. 18.9%), though the latter two are held-in for AgentGym. Against the base LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, AgentRefine improves Alfworld (44.8% vs. 22.4%), SciWorld (14.4% vs. 7.8%), PDDL (16.6% vs. 10.0%), and Jericho (10.0% vs. 0.0%), but underperforms on BabyAI (37.5% vs. 45.5%). Against GPT-4o, AgentRefine remains far behind on most tasks (e.g., SciWorld 14.4% vs. 40.0%, PDDL 16.6% vs. 61.7%) but matches GPT-4o exactly on Jericho (10.0%) and approaches GPT-4o-mini on BabyAI Progress (50.4% vs. 51.9%). The robustness analysis (Table 3) further shows AgentRefine's average Alfworld Success increases by 3.7% under perturbation while Agent-FLAN drops 30.4% and AgentGym drops 25.6%, confirming that AgentRefine learns generalizable decision-making rather than memorized observation-action mappings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agent-tuning line initiated by AgentTuning (Zeng et al., 2023) and refined by Agent-FLAN, AgentGym, and AgentGen, all of which aim to improve generalization by mixing general alignment data or increasing environment diversity. The key conceptual departure is drawing on self-refinement literature (Self-Refine, Reflexion) to argue that generalization requires the model to learn from its own errors within a trajectory, rather than merely memorizing correct observation-action sequences. AgentRefine operationalizes this by synthesizing trajectories that explicitly contain error-refine pairs and training with a modified loss that masks erroneous tokens, positioning itself as the first work to connect step-level self-refinement with agent generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The perturbation experiments (30% success-rate drop for Agent-FLAN from merely rewording action descriptions) provide strong evidence that existing agent-tuning methods learn surface-level memorization rather than genuine decision-making, which reframes how the field should evaluate agent generalization. The refinement tuning technique is practical and adoptable, and the finding that masking erroneous-turn loss is critical (contradicting prior work suggesting learning from mistakes helps) offers a concrete training recipe. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to multi-agent or tool-use settings, whether the quality gap between GPT-4o-synthesized and open-source-synthesized data can be closed, and whether refinement tuning complements or conflicts with RL-based agent training.
