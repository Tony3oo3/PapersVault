## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary MOT methods (e.g., OVTrack) rely on a tracking-by-OVD framework that cobbles together per-frame classification with appearance-based association, requiring complex post-processing, anchor generation, and 99.4M CLIP image embeddings from RPN proposals. This design causes unstable category perception across frames, poor adaptability to diverse environments, and a lengthy preprocessing pipeline that hinders open-world deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251794/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

OVTR consumes a sequence of video frames (processed through a ResNet-50 backbone) and text prompts (category names fed through the CLIP text encoder) to produce per-frame bounding boxes, open-vocabulary category labels, and persistent track IDs. Notably, it requires only 1,732 pre-computed CLIP image embeddings (one per base category, generated offline from ground-truth crops) rather than the 99.4M proposal-based embeddings used by prior methods, and it is RPN-free, eliminating anchor generation entirely.

## Method

OVTR builds on MOTR's iterative query-based tracking, where track queries propagate across frames, and introduces three key mechanisms: (1) a Category Information Propagation (CIP) strategy that uses a modified Transformer decoder layer to aggregate category information from the OFA branch outputs into track query content, establishing a multi-frame category prior; (2) a dual-branch decoder where the OFA branch distills visual generalization from the CLIP image encoder via an alignment loss on aligned queries, while the CTI branch performs text cross-attention for open-vocabulary classification; and (3) two attention isolation strategies—category isolation (KL-divergence-based masking between queries with dissimilar category distributions) and content isolation (suppressing track-to-detect query interference in the first decoder layer)—that prevent information entanglement. Training uses multi-frame optimization with progressive sequence length (2→5 frames) and custom Dynamic Mosaic and Random Occlusion augmentations, all without diffusion-model data generation or proposals containing novel categories.

## Results

On the TAO validation set, OVTR achieves 31.4 novel TETA and 36.6 base TETA, outperforming OVTrack (27.8 / 35.5) by +3.6 and +1.1 points respectively. On the test set, OVTR reaches 27.1 novel TETA, surpassing OVTrack (24.1) by 3.0 points (+12.4% relative). OVTR also dramatically improves novel ClsA from 1.5 (OVTrack) to 5.4 on validation, a more than 3× gain, while requiring only 1,732 CLIP image embeddings versus 99.4M for all baselines. On the KITTI zero-shot transfer, OVTR achieves 69.8 MOTA, exceeding OVTrack (67.0) by 2.9 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OVTR outperforms OVTrack (the primary open-vocabulary MOT baseline) on novel TETA by +3.6 (val) and +3.0 (test), with a particularly large gain in novel ClsA (5.4 vs 1.5, >3×) and LocA (+5.6 val, +5.3 test), while using 57,000× fewer CLIP image embeddings (1,732 vs 99.4M) and no novel-category proposals. Against MASA, OVTR wins on novel TETA (31.4 vs 30.0) and novel ClsA (5.4 vs 1.0) but trails slightly on base TETA (36.6 vs 36.9) and base LocA (52.2 vs 55.1), though MASA additionally distills segmentation knowledge from SAM. On the older association-based baselines DeepSORT(ViLD) and Tracktor++(ViLD), OVTR leads by 10.3 and 8.7 points on novel TETA (validation), respectively. On the KITTI zero-shot transfer, OVTR surpasses OVTrack by 2.9% MOTA and 3.6% IDF1 while reducing ID switches by 36.3%, though both remain well below the supervised CenterTrack (88.7 MOTA) which is trained on in-domain video. On the higher-frame-rate OVT-B benchmark, OVTR matches OVTrack on novel TETA (45.5) and achieves the highest AssocA for both novel (67.6) and base (68.9) categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

OVTR departs from the dominant 'tracking-by-OVD' paradigm (OVTrack, DeepSORT+ViLD, Tracktor+++ViLD) that cobbles together an open-vocabulary detector with appearance-based association, instead building on the end-to-end iterative query framework of MOTR and extending it to the open-vocabulary setting. For open-vocabulary perception, it draws on the CLIP-based knowledge distillation lineage (ViLD, GLIP, Grounding DINO) but repurposes it: rather than generating ~100M image embeddings from RPN proposals, it uses only 1,732 category-level CLIP image embeddings to guide aligned queries via a dual-branch decoder. The category information propagation (CIP) strategy and the two attention isolation masks are novel mechanisms with no direct precedent in prior MOT or OVD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first end-to-end open-vocabulary tracker, OVTR eliminates the complex post-processing, anchor generation, and hand-designed association logic that plague tracking-by-OVD systems, yielding a simpler, faster, and more scalable pipeline. The dramatic reduction in required CLIP embeddings (1,732 vs. 99.4M) and the removal of diffusion-model-based data augmentation make the training workflow substantially more accessible. Open questions include whether the CIP strategy and dual-branch decoder scale to larger backbones or longer video sequences, whether the approach generalizes beyond the TAO/KITTI evaluation to truly open-world deployments with thousands of categories, and whether the attention isolation thresholds can be made adaptive rather than fixed.
