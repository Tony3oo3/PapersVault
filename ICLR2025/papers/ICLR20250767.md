## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods relying on entropy minimization (EM) loss exhibit suboptimal early-stage performance in online settings where target samples arrive sequentially in batches. The paper identifies that EM loss decomposes into two conflicting energy-based terms—one that dispersely reduces class-specific energy and one that increases the overall free energy—hindering assertive adaptation and failing to sufficiently close the energy gap between source and target domains. Additionally, the directional aspect of energy alignment (i.e., how each target sample should be adjusted based on its class) has been largely overlooked in prior TTA work.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled, domain-shifted target images arriving in sequential mini-batches (e.g., 32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) along with a pre-trained model whose classifier is frozen and whose batch normalization parameters are the only updated parameters. It produces standard class labels for each incoming sample. The source domain is entirely inaccessible at test time (source-free setting), and the model must persistently accumulate knowledge from past batches to improve predictions on future ones, making it suitable for streaming inference scenarios such as autonomous driving.

## Method

AEA introduces two complementary loss functions on top of the standard EM loss. The Source-Free Energy Alignment (SFEA) loss estimates the source domain energy by averaging the energies of the bottom α% lowest-energy target samples in each batch, then penalizes all target samples whose energy exceeds this threshold via a softplus function, thereby strategically reducing the energy gap with adaptive strength that naturally decays as adaptation progresses. The Logit Cosine Similarity (LCS) loss guides the direction of energy alignment by computing cosine similarity between each target sample's logits and a virtual logit constructed as W^T W_k (where W_k is the k-th classifier weight vector serving as a surrogate source feature for the predicted class k), weighted by sample confidence to emphasize confident predictions. The total loss is L_EM + λ₁·L_SFEA + λ₂·L_LCS, and the key theoretical insight is the energy-based re-interpretation of EM loss showing its two decomposed terms work in opposition, motivating the explicit energy alignment objective.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-C (severity 5, ResNet-26), AEA achieves 19.5% average classification error, outperforming the next-best method TTT (20.9%) by 1.4% and TENT/EM (24.0%) by 4.5%. On CIFAR-100-C, AEA reaches 49.0% error vs. SHOT's 49.9% and TENT's 53.6%. On TinyImageNet-C (ResNet-50), AEA achieves 61.0% error, beating SHOT (62.2%) and TENT (63.3%). AEA also leads on the style-shift PACS dataset (24.63% avg error vs. TENT's 26.67%) and on ImageNet-C with only 10K target samples (62.0% vs. TENT's 67.5%), demonstrating consistent gains across corruption types, dataset scales, and domain shift types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AEA consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across every evaluated dataset. On CIFAR-10-C, AEA (19.5% error) beats the closest competitor TTT (20.9%) by 1.4%, TEA (22.0%) by 2.5%, SHOT (22.1%) by 2.6%, and TENT/EM (24.0%) by 4.5%. On CIFAR-100-C, AEA (49.0%) surpasses SHOT (49.9%) by 0.9% and TENT (53.6%) by 4.6%, with the largest margin over T3A (67.6%) at 18.6%. On TinyImageNet-C, AEA (61.0%) leads SHOT (62.2%) by 1.2% and TENT (63.3%) by 2.3%. On the style-shift PACS dataset, AEA (24.63%) outperforms TENT (26.67%) by 2.0% and SAR (26.97%) by 2.3%. Notably, AEA's advantage is most pronounced in the early adaptation batches (Fig. 5), where it achieves near-Oracle performance within a few batches while TENT/EM-based methods lag significantly, confirming that the energy-alignment mechanism accelerates convergence rather than merely improving asymptotic performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the dominant entropy-minimization (EM) paradigm for test-time adaptation (TENT, SHOT, EATA, SAR) and the emerging line of energy-based approaches to distribution shift (Liu et al. 2020; Yuan et al. 2024, TEA). Its key conceptual departure is an energy-based re-interpretation of the EM loss, decomposing it into two conflicting terms (a class-wise energy minimizer and a free-energy maximizer) that jointly hinder assertive adaptation. From this diagnosis, the paper introduces two complementary losses—SFEA for magnitude-level energy alignment and LCS for direction-level alignment via classifier weight geometry—that go beyond TEA's scalar energy reduction by incorporating class-wise structural information in a source-free manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The energy-gap reduction perspective offers a mechanistic explanation for why EM-based TTA underperforms in early online batches, and the proposed fix is practically attractive because it adds negligible computation (only logits and classifier weights are used). However, the approach relies on the classifier weight matrix as a surrogate for source-domain features, which may degrade under extreme domain shifts or when the classifier is not well-trained. Open questions include whether the source-free energy estimation (bottom-α% threshold) remains reliable under severe label noise or highly non-stationary streams, and whether the method extends naturally to tasks without a fixed classifier (e.g., open-vocabulary or generative settings).
