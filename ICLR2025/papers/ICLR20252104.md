## Problem

Existing vision-language alignment methods in LVLMs fail to transfer the safety mechanism that protects the base LLM against toxic text to the vision modality, leaving LVLMs far more vulnerable to toxic images than to semantically equivalent toxic text. The paper identifies the root cause: current alignment is insufficient at the hidden-states level, particularly at the specific transformer layers where safety mechanism activation is triggered, causing a semantic shift that prevents those layers from correctly assessing image toxicity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252104/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes an RGB image (processed by a CLIP ViT-L/14 vision tower and projected via a two-layer MLP) together with a text instruction, and produces a standard text response (either a refusal or a description). During training, the input is augmented with a BEIT-3-retrieved text passage and an LLaVA-1.5-13B-generated caption for the same image; the caption's hidden states are computed with gradients disabled and serve as a fixed alignment target, while the image's hidden states (fused with the retrieved text through self-attention) are the trainable source. Training uses 558K CC3M images for pre-training and 665K LLaVA-1.5-mix665k images for instruction-tuning.

## Method

TGA (Text-Guided Alignment) introduces a pair-wise hidden-states alignment loss (L_guide) applied at every transformer layer during vision-language alignment training. For each training image, a semantically relevant text is retrieved via BEIT-3 from a 1.15M-caption corpus, and a caption is generated by LLaVA-1.5-13B; the caption's per-layer hidden states (gradients frozen) act as the alignment target. The image's per-layer hidden states—naturally fused with the retrieved text through self-attention—are pushed closer to the caption's hidden states than the retrieved text's own hidden states are, via a cosine-similarity pair-wise margin loss. This forces the vision representation to occupy the same hidden-states region as its textual counterpart at the exact layers where safety mechanism activation occurs, thereby transferring the LLM's text-based safety mechanism to vision without any additional safety fine-tuning on the visual modality. The total loss combines L_guide with standard cross-entropy for language modeling, and the approach is validated to improve toxic-image defence success rates by 5–15× over baselines while maintaining or exceeding SoTA general vision-task performance.

## Results

On toxic image defence (DSR), TGA with Mistral-7B achieves 20.65% (Porn), 9.48% (Bloody), 22.73% (Insulting), 17.92% (Alcohol), 17.29% (Cigarette), 30.83% (Gun), and 29.42% (Knife), dramatically outperforming LLaVA-1.6 (same base LLM) which scores below 2% on all scenes. On general vision tasks, TGA-7B achieves 71.7% on ScienceQA, 58.7% on POPE (all), 37.4% on SEED-Bench (all), and 34.6% on MM-Vet (all), matching or slightly exceeding LLaVA-1.5-7B(Mistral) (67.9%, 58.8%, 37.4%, 32.9% respectively). The safety transfer rate from text to vision is substantially higher for TGA compared to all baseline alignment methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-1.6 (same Mistral-7B base), TGA improves DSR by 15–29 points across all seven toxic scenes (e.g., Gun: 30.83 vs 1.95; Knife: 29.42 vs 1.22), demonstrating that the safety mechanism is successfully transferred to vision without any safety fine-tuning. Against Unlearn-FigS (also Mistral-7B), TGA wins on every scene by 2–11 points (e.g., Gun: 30.83 vs 21.42; Knife: 29.42 vs 18.55), showing that directly transferring the safety mechanism via hidden-state alignment is more effective than textual unlearning. Against Qwen-VL-chat (Qwen-7B, which has inherently stronger text safety), TGA still outperforms by 15–25 points per scene, confirming the benefit is not merely inherited from a stronger base LLM. On general vision benchmarks (Table 3), TGA-7B matches or slightly exceeds LLaVA-1.5-7B(Mistral) on ScienceQA (71.7 vs 67.9), POPE (58.7 vs 58.8), SEED-Bench (37.4 vs 37.4), and MM-Vet (34.6 vs 32.9), indicating no general-capability degradation. In additional comparisons (Table 7), TGA also outperforms ToViLaG and ECSO across all seven scenes, and in jailbreak robustness (Table 6) TGA achieves 21.08% (Role-Play), 15.43% (ICA), and 17.44% (FigStep) versus at most 13.48%, 9.45%, and 10.57% for the next-best method (Unlearn-FigS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LLaVA's visual instruction tuning pipeline but reframes the LVLM safety problem as a cross-modal transfer issue: the safety mechanism already established in the base LLM for text fails to activate on vision because vision-language alignment is insufficient at the hidden-state level. It departs from prior safety-focused LVLM work (Zong et al. 2024, Wang et al. 2024) that relies on supervised toxic vision fine-tuning, and from Unlearn-FigS (Chakraborty et al. 2024) that transfers text unlearning to vision, by instead transferring the existing text safety mechanism through a text-guided hidden-state alignment loss (TGA). The mechanistic analysis draws on transformer interpretability literature (Tenney 2019, Meng et al. 2022, Chuang et al. 2024) to locate safety activation at specific layers and diagnose the cross-modal misalignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that safety mechanism activation is localized to specific transformer layers and that hidden-state misalignment (not a lack of visual toxicity understanding) is the root cause of LVLM vulnerability provides a principled explanation that prior work lacked. Practically, TGA offers a route to improve LVLM safety without collecting toxic vision data, which is especially valuable for extending safety to harder-to-curate modalities like audio and video. However, the absolute DSR (~20–30%) remains far below the 40% upper bound of a simple image filter, and the method adds retrieval and caption-generation infrastructure to the training pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other modalities, whether the layer-localized safety mechanism is stable across architectures, and whether the residual gap to the upper bound reflects a fundamental limitation of hidden-state alignment or requires complementary defenses.
