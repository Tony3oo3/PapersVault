## Problem

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) treat image captions as bag-of-words, failing to capture syntactic and semantic relations between words, which causes poor performance on compositional tasks. Existing remedies either rely on noisy auto-generated hard negatives, require orders-of-magnitude more training data, or use standard autoregressive/parallel prediction that learns spurious correlations induced by linear word order rather than true linguistic dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252688/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a test image (processed through a frozen VLM visual encoder to extract patch and CLS features from both the last and penultimate transformer layers, yielding 2p+2 visual vectors) and a set of candidate captions. Each candidate caption is parsed into a dependency tree by an off-the-shelf parser (Deep Biaffine + RoBERTa), and the model computes a log-likelihood score via a level-order traversal of that tree, selecting the highest-scoring caption. The visual features are computed once per image and are independent of the specific caption being scored.

## Method

COGT (Causally-Ordered Generative Training) replaces the CLIP textual encoder with a small ~64M-parameter decoder whose prediction order is guided by a Causal Graphical Model (CGM) built from a dependency parser's output. Each word's conditional distribution depends only on its ancestors in the dependency tree (O(log n) parents on average), its syntactic type (encoded via 45 distinct masked tokens, one per POS category), and all visual features—rather than on all previously generated words as in standard AR. The decoder uses a Dependency Guided Attention mechanism where masked tokens attend only to their parent visible tokens (no cross-masked attention), preventing information leakage, and a cross-attention layer over the visual features. Training maximizes the log-likelihood of the CGM's disentangled factorization, and inference uses a semi-parallel strategy that predicts all tokens at the same tree level simultaneously, making the likelihood estimation independent of spurious sequential-order associations.

## Results

COGT-CLIP, trained on COCO only (~100K samples), achieves an average accuracy of 75.20 across five compositional benchmarks (ARO, SugarCrepe, VL-Checklist, ColorSwap, FG-OVD), outperforming the best prior method trained on larger datasets (DAC-LLM, trained on CC3M with ~3.3M samples) by 12.27 points. With additional training data (COCO+CC3M+Visual Genome), COGT-CLIP+ reaches 84.83 average, and COGT-XVLM+ achieves the best overall result of 86.30. On individual benchmarks, COGT-CLIP+ scores 93.42 on ARO, 89.89 on SugarCrepe, 89.31 on VL-Checklist, 81.66 on ColorSwap, and 69.96 on FG-OVD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COGT-CLIP (COCO-only training) outperforms all CLIP-based methods trained on COCO by a large margin: NegCLIP (62.57 avg), CE-CLIP (60.31), GNM (53.40), and Plausible Adj. Neg (57.86), with the largest gap on ColorSwap (61.33 vs. 35.67 for NegCLIP) and FG-OVD (51.48 vs. 44.98 for Plausible Adj. Neg). Critically, COGT-CLIP also surpasses methods trained on datasets an order of magnitude larger: DAC-LLM (62.93 avg, trained on CC3M ~3.3M samples) and DAC-SAM (61.31), despite using only ~100K COCO samples. COGT-CLIP+ extends this lead to ~22 points over DAC-LLM on the overall average. Among encoder-decoder VLMs (Table 5), COGT-InstructBLIP+ (85.87 avg) outperforms Cap (87.75 ARO only, no other benchmarks reported), CapPa, BLIP-VisualGPTScore (92.20 ARO), and all LLaVA variants, while COGT-XVLM+ (86.30 avg) surpasses CE-XVLM (59.95 avg) using the same XVLM backbone and COCO training data. On downstream linear probing tasks (Table 6), COGT-CLIP+ improves over CLIP on CIFAR-10 (96.8 vs. 94.2), CIFAR-100 (85.4 vs. 79.0), and ImageNet-1K top-1 (75.3 vs. 75.0), confirming that the compositional training does not degrade general visual features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Tschannen et al. (2023), who showed that generative (captioning) training improves VLM compositional understanding via AR (Cap) or parallel (CapPa) prediction, and on the CGM disentangled factorization framework of Schölkopf et al. (2021). COGT departs from both by replacing the fully-sequential or fully-parallel generative process with a semi-parallel, partially-ordered prediction whose structure is dictated by an off-the-shelf dependency parser's syntactic tree, interpreted as a causal graph. This contrasts with prior dependency-parser-based VLM work (Wazni et al., Yang & Wan, Deguchi et al.), which uses syntactic structure to modulate attention or design losses rather than to define the generative factorization itself, and with contrastive-based compositional methods (NegCLIP, CE-CLIP, DAC) that rely on hard-negative construction or dense captioning rather than a structured generative objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that a ~64M-parameter decoder trained on only ~100K COCO samples outperforms methods trained on 30× more data (CC3M) by over 12 points on average, suggesting that removing spurious inter-token correlations through CGM-guided sparsification is more effective than simply scaling data. The approach is architecture-agnostic (validated on CLIP, XVLM, and InstructBLIP) and the FG-OVD benchmark adds a fine-grained, difficulty-graded evaluation axis missing from prior compositional suites. Open questions include whether the causal interpretation of syntactic dependencies always holds (the authors acknowledge this is an assumption), whether LLM-based caption rewriting could further help the parser on noisy web-scale data, and whether counterfactual reasoning could be integrated into the CGM framework.
