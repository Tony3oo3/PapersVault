## Problem

Current multilingual LLM benchmarks are predominantly English-only or constructed by translating English resources, which introduces translationese artifacts and fails to capture the regional and cultural knowledge specific to the language environments where models would actually be deployed. This evaluation gap hinders the development of truly multilingual models and exacerbates digital divides for underserved language communities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20253003/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample is a four-option multiple-choice question presented in its native language and script, drawn from actual local academic, professional, or licensing examinations. Models are prompted with instructions either in the sample's language or in English, optionally preceded by a regional context prefix, and must output a single answer letter. Beyond raw accuracy, the evaluation separately tracks format errors (responses that fail to produce a valid answer option) and answer accuracy (correctness conditional on valid formatting), revealing that some models achieve correct answers but in non-standard output formats, particularly in lower-resource languages.

## Method

INCLUDE is constructed by collecting 197,243 MCQA pairs from 1,926 local exam sources across 44 languages, 15 scripts, and 52 countries, with 60.2% newly extracted and verified by native speakers and 39.8% consolidated from existing single-language benchmarks (ArabicMMLU, ChineseMMLU, TurkishMMLU, PersianMMLU, VNHSGE, EXAMS). Each exam source is annotated with a two-level academic taxonomy (area and field) and a regionality label (region-agnostic, region-explicit, cultural, or region-implicit), enabling stratified analysis of where models fail. Two subsets are curated for different evaluation budgets: INCLUDE-BASE (22,635 samples, ~12% of full) and INCLUDE-LITE (10,770 samples, ~6%, region-specific only). Fifteen models are evaluated under 5-shot and zero-shot CoT prompting with in-language and English prompt variants, plus optional regional context prefixes, revealing that performance gaps are driven primarily by regional knowledge deficits rather than general language comprehension, and that models perform worst on cultural and region-explicit questions in languages not explicitly included in their pretraining data.

## Results

On INCLUDE-BASE (22,635 QA pairs across 44 languages), GPT-4o achieves the highest accuracy at 77.3% (5-shot, in-language prompt) and 79.0% (zero-shot CoT). Among open-weight models, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct leads at 70.6% (5-shot), followed by Qwen2.5-14B at 61.4% and Aya-expanse-32B at 52.4%. For smaller (≤8B) models, Gemma-7B achieves the best overall performance at 54.5%, with Qwen2.5-7B close behind at 54.1%. Performance on INCLUDE-LITE (10,770 samples) is within 1% of INCLUDE-BASE across all models, confirming its suitability for resource-constrained evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o outperforms all open-weight models by a substantial margin, achieving 77.3% vs. 70.6% for Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct (a 6.7-point gap) and 77.3% vs. 61.4% for Qwen2.5-14B (a 15.9-point gap). Among open models, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct leads at 70.6%, but its zero-shot CoT performance drops sharply to 60.6%, whereas GPT-4o's CoT actually improves to 79.0%. Scaling within model families yields consistent gains: Aya-expanse-32B outperforms Aya-expanse-8B by ~12 points (52.4% vs. 37.2%), and Qwen2.5-14B surpasses Qwen2.5-7B by ~7 points (61.4% vs. 54.1%). Instruction tuning provides little to no benefit on INCLUDE-BASE; most instruct variants perform on par with or slightly below their base counterparts, with Gemma-7B-Instruct suffering a notable ~15-point drop (38.7% vs. 54.5% for Gemma-7B). Prompting language (English vs. in-language) and regional prefix additions yield minimal differences (<2%) for most models, with the exception that Aya-expanse and Qwen2.5-14B benefit from English prompts under CoT, while Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct performs better with in-language prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MMLU benchmarking paradigm (Hendrycks et al., 2020) from English-only to a multilingual setting, but with a critical departure: rather than translating English questions into other languages (the dominant practice in prior multilingual benchmarks like XGLUE, XTREME, and MEGA), it collects original exam content from local sources in 44 languages. It also consolidates existing language-specific MMLU-like datasets (ArabicMMLU, ChineseMMLU, TurkishMMLU, PersianMMLU, VNHSGE) and the EXAMS dataset, but adds 60.2% novel data from regional academic, professional, and licensing exams. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the explicit categorization of questions by regionality (region-agnostic, region-explicit, region-implicit, cultural), which no prior multilingual benchmark had systematically addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

INCLUDE is the largest multilingual exam benchmark to date (197K QA pairs, 44 languages, 52 countries) and directly addresses the evaluation gap that hinders deployment of LLMs in non-English-speaking regions. The finding that even GPT-4o shows high variance across languages and struggles specifically with cultural and region-explicit questions (e.g., 43.2% on Persian certification exams vs. 66%+ on geography) reveals that current models have not acquired the localized knowledge needed for equitable deployment. Open questions include how to improve regional knowledge acquisition during pretraining, whether the exam-level (rather than question-level) regional annotation limits the granularity of analysis, and how to standardize multilingual evaluation given format-error rates that can reach 17% for some models. The observation that instruction-tuning can degrade multilingual performance (Gemma-7B-Instruct drops ~15%) raises practical concerns for the standard training pipeline.
