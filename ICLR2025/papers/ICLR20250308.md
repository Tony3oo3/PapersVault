## Problem

Single-turn RLHF methods applied to multi-turn dialogue suffer from covariate shift: training contexts are generated by a reference policy, but at test time the model encounters contexts it generated itself, causing performance to degrade as conversations lengthen. Existing multi-turn extensions either require expensive actor-critic architectures or still rely on offline data for rollin, leaving the distribution mismatch unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250308/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a partial multi-turn conversation state s_h (all prior user questions and assistant responses up to turn h) and produces an assistant response y_h. Supervision comes exclusively from trajectory-level rewards: two independent rollouts are generated from the same prefix state, and the difference in their end-of-conversation reward model scores serves as the regression target. No per-turn reward labels are required, and the reward model (e.g., ArmoRM or FsfairX-LLaMA3-RM) scores the full H-turn dialogue.

## Method

REFUEL merges the critic and actor into a single model by expressing Q-values as log policy ratios (via the DPO/QUINOA reparameterization), then regressing the *difference* in two trajectory-level rewards from a shared prefix against the *difference* in log policy ratios for the two divergent responses. This pairwise regression eliminates the partition function and avoids needing a separate value network. At each iteration, the algorithm collects on-policy data by rolling in with the current policy to a random turn h, generating two independent completions from that state, and rolling out both to the end of the conversation. The resulting least-squares loss is optimized over the full dataset in mini-batches, and the process is repeated for T iterations. Theoretically, REFUEL is shown to be equivalent to policy mirror descent with a variance-reduced advantage estimate, and its Approximate Policy Completeness condition is strictly weaker than the Q-function approximation error required by prior NPG analyses.

## Results

On UltraInteract (Setting One, LLM-simulated user), REFUEL (iter 2) achieves an average winrate of 56.64% against the base policy Llama-3-8B-it, outperforming all RLHF baselines (best baseline: REBEL-LT-ONLINE at 54.24%). Notably, the 8B REFUEL model surpasses Llama-3.1-70B-it at turns 3–5 (58.8/57.2/58.6 vs. 61.0/53.0/55.4), demonstrating superior long-horizon planning. On the pre-sampled-question setting (Table 2), REFUEL achieves the highest winrate on both Anthropic HH (82.8%) and UltraInteract (79.6%), while maintaining competitive reward model scores with lower KL divergence than several baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REFUEL consistently outperforms all three RLHF baseline families (RLOO, DPO, REBEL) across every data-collection variant (LT-OFFLINE, LT-MIXED, LT-ONLINE, MT-MIXED) in average winrate on UltraInteract, with the largest gaps over offline methods (e.g., 56.64 vs. 46.40 for RLOO-LT-OFFLINE, a ~10-point margin). Among the strongest baselines, REBEL-LT-ONLINE (54.24) and DPO-LT-ONLINE (53.68) are the closest competitors, but REFUEL still leads by 2.4 and 3.0 points respectively. Against the Llama-3.1-70B-it reference model, REFUEL's 8B model trails on average (56.64 vs. 61.24) due to the 70B model's advantage at early turns, but REFUEL reverses the gap at turns 3–5 where the 70B model degrades (53.0, 55.4) while REFUEL improves (57.2, 58.6). In the pre-sampled-question setting (Table 2), REFUEL achieves the best winrate on both Anthropic HH (82.8% vs. 80.2% for REBEL-LT-ONLINE) and UltraInteract (79.6% vs. 73.4% for REBEL-LT-ONLINE), confirming that on-policy multi-turn training is beneficial even without a live user simulator.

## Relation to Prior Work

REFUEL extends REBEL's regression-to-relative-rewards framework from the single-turn bandit setting to multi-turn MDPs with stochastic transitions, and leverages the DPO/QUINOA reparameterization trick to express Q-values as log-policy ratios, eliminating the need for a separate critic network. It departs from prior multi-turn RLHF approaches (Shani et al., Zhou et al.) that rely on expensive actor-critic architectures, and from single-turn methods (DPO, REBEL, RLOO) that suffer from covariate shift when naively applied to multi-turn data. The theoretical analysis connects to natural policy gradient (NPG) theory, showing that REFUEL's Approximate Policy Completeness condition is strictly weaker than the Q-function approximation error condition required by NPG convergence proofs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REFUEL provides a practical, single-model recipe for multi-turn RLHF that avoids the memory and stability overhead of actor-critic methods while provably addressing covariate shift through on-policy iterative training. The finding that an 8B model fine-tuned with REFUEL outperforms a 70B model on long multi-turn dialogues suggests that targeted multi-turn optimization can compensate for parameter scale in conversational settings. Open questions include the computational cost of iterative on-policy data generation at scale, whether the approach extends to settings without a trajectory-level reward model, and how performance scales beyond 5 turns. The reliance on an LLM-simulated user for evaluation also limits generalizability claims to real human interactions.
