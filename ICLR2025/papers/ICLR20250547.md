## Problem

Multi-modal large language models struggle with automatic geometry problem solving because their pre-training on natural images and texts creates a large domain gap with monochrome geometric diagrams, and their solutions in natural language are difficult to verify. Existing geometry specialists, while effective on narrow benchmarks, rely on task-specific architectures that limit generalization. The paper argues that formalized (symbolic) descriptions of geometric structure, rather than natural language captions, are the right pre-training signal for bridging this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250547/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GeoX takes a geometric diagram image (typically monochrome, composed of lines, shapes, and labeled points) together with a geometry question as input. It outputs a formalized program sequence of mathematical operations (e.g., Sum, g_minus, PRK_Perim, gougu_minus) over variables and constants, which is then compiled and executed by an external symbolic solver to yield a verifiable final numerical answer. Any error in the generated program is treated as an incorrect result, making the pipeline fully auditable without human expert review.

## Method

GeoX is trained in three progressive stages. First, unimodal pre-training equips a ViT encoder (Geo-ViT) with geometric prior knowledge via masked auto-encoding on 120K diagrams, and fine-tunes LLEMMA-7B into Geo-LLM-7B on a 100M-token geometric corpus. Second, geometry-language alignment uses a Generator-and-Sampler Transformer (GS-Former) that replaces static Q-Former queries with a Geo-aware Query Generator (GQG) producing content-aware queries from visual context, and a Semantics-guided Geometry Sampler (SGS) that progressively removes uninformative patch representations via Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization guided by formal caption alignment; training combines contrastive, matching, and caption-generation losses with a sparsification penalty. Third, end-to-end visual instruction tuning connects the aligned visual features to Geo-LLM-7B through a projection head, training the full model to emit formalized program sequences. The key differentiator is the use of formalized point-level spatial descriptions (collinear and concyclic relationships) as the alignment target, which the paper proves via mutual-information bounds to yield lower Bayes error than natural-language captions.

## Results

On GeoQA, GeoX achieves 54.9% Top-1 and 69.0% Top-10 accuracy, surpassing the best specialist (SCA-GPS, 64.1% Top-10) and GPT-4V (43.4% Top-1). On UniGeo, GeoX reaches 54.4% Top-1 on calculation problems and 97.8% Top-1 on proving problems, outperforming Geoformer (46.8%/51.3%) and UniMath-Flan-T5 (83.0% proving). On Geometry3K and PGPS9K, GeoX achieves 58.6% and 52.7% Completion respectively, exceeding PGPSNet (48.1%/44.4%) and GPT-4V (34.8%/33.3%). On MathVista-GEO, GeoX attains 72.6% accuracy, outperforming GPT-4o (66.1%) and GPT-4V (54.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoX outperforms all generalist MLLMs by large margins: on Geometry3K Completion, GeoX (58.6%) exceeds GPT-4V (34.8%) by +23.8%, Qwen-VL (2.5%) by +56.1%, and LLaVA-v1.5 (2.9%) by +55.7%. Among geometry specialists, GeoX surpasses the previous SOTA PGPSNet by +10.5% Completion on Geometry3K and +8.3% on PGPS9K, and beats Geoformer by +21.8% and +17.1% respectively. On GeoQA Top-10, GeoX (69.0%) exceeds SCA-GPS (64.1%) by +4.9% and DPE-NGS (62.7%) by +6.3%. On UniGeo proving (Top-1), GeoX achieves 97.8%, far exceeding UniMath-Flan-T5 (83.0%) by +14.8% and Geoformer (51.3%) by +46.5%. In the solver-free comparison on GeoQA (Table 8), GeoX (67.4%) narrowly beats EAGLE (67.1%) and MAVIS (66.7%), while on MathVista-GEO GeoX (72.6%) outperforms GPT-4o (66.1%) by +6.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoX builds on two foundational lines: masked auto-encoding (MAE) for training a geometry-specific vision encoder (Geo-ViT), and BLIP-2's Q-Former architecture, which it extends into the GS-Former by replacing static query tokens with dynamically generated geo-aware queries (GQG) and adding a semantics-guided sampler (SGS) that progressively prunes uninformative visual patches. It departs from prior geometry specialists (NGS, Geoformer, PGPSNet) by proposing a unified three-stage pre-training paradigm rather than task-specific architectures, and from recent MLLM-based geometry approaches (G-LLaVA, MAVIS) by replacing natural-language captions with formalized geometric descriptions (collinear/concyclic point relationships), arguing that formal language is more concise and verifiable for geometric reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that domain-specific formalized pre-training can close the gap between generalist MLLMs and specialized geometry solvers, with GeoX (7B) outperforming GPT-4V across all four benchmarks. The finding that formal language yields a consistent +2.9% improvement over natural language for alignment, and that the SGS module meaningfully improves performance by removing noise, provides actionable design principles for building domain-specific multimodal models. Limitations include reliance on an external symbolic solver for final answers, the narrow scope to plane geometry, and the need for expert-annotated formal captions (6,232 images, 200 hours). Open questions include whether the formalized pre-training paradigm generalizes to other specialized visual domains (e.g., circuit diagrams, chemical structures) and whether the approach scales to more complex or 3D geometric reasoning.
