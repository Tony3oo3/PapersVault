## Problem

LLMs applied to clinical prediction suffer from hallucinations and lack fine-grained medical knowledge, while traditional RAG retrieves semantically similar but clinically irrelevant information. Although knowledge graphs and graph community retrieval (e.g., GraphRAG) show promise, their application to LLM-based healthcare prediction remains largely unexplored, and existing LLM-based EHR methods fail to fully leverage reasoning capabilities for interpretable predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252949/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes structured patient EHR data (CCS-coded conditions, procedures, and ATC3-level medications across multiple visits) along with a task description, and produces both a binary outcome label (mortality 0/1 or 15-day readmission 0/1) and a free-form step-by-step reasoning chain. The EHR context is augmented at inference time with up to 10 pre-summarized KG community summaries selected via the DGRA algorithm, and two similar-patient references (one positive, one negative) retrieved from the training set. The fine-tuned 7B model (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3) handles both generation tasks via instruction prefixes, while training reasoning chains are distilled from Claude 3.5 Sonnet.

## Method

KARE is a three-stage framework: (1) a multi-source medical concept KG is constructed by extracting shortest-path subgraphs from UMLS anchored on EHR co-occurrence patterns, extracting triples from PubMed abstracts via LLM-based entity/relation extraction, and prompting an LLM to identify clinically relevant inter-concept relationships; the resulting KG is semantically clustered (agglomerative, threshold 0.14) and organized into hierarchical communities via the Leiden algorithm at multiple granularities, each with general and theme-specific LLM-generated summaries. (2) A Dynamic Graph Retrieval and Augmentation (DGRA) algorithm iteratively selects the most relevant community summaries for a patient using a composite relevance score combining normalized node hits (direct and indirect), a decay factor promoting diversity, cosine-similarity coherence with the base context, recency weighting, and task-theme relevance. (3) A 7B local LLM is fine-tuned with multitask learning—simultaneously aligning to expert-generated reasoning chains (distilled from Claude 3.5 Sonnet with highest-confidence selection) and ground-truth labels—enabling the model to produce both interpretable reasoning and accurate predictions from the augmented context.

## Results

On MIMIC-III mortality prediction (5.42% positive rate), KARE achieves 64.6% Macro F1 and 24.7% sensitivity, outperforming the best LM+ML baseline RAM-EHR (59.6% Macro F1, 14.8% sensitivity) by 5.0 and 9.9 percentage points respectively. On MIMIC-IV mortality, KARE reaches 90.4% Macro F1 and 73.2% sensitivity versus GraphCare's 80.3% and 57.8%. For readmission prediction, KARE attains 73.7% Macro F1 on MIMIC-III and 73.8% on MIMIC-IV, surpassing the best baselines (GraphCare at 64.1% and 65.5%) by 9.6 and 8.3 points. The fine-tuned backbone without KARE augmentation scores only 53.0% Macro F1 on MIMIC-III mortality, highlighting the critical contribution of KG community retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KARE consistently outperforms all 15 baselines across all four prediction tasks (MIMIC-III/IV × mortality/readmission). Against the strongest LM+ML baselines, KARE improves Macro F1 by 5.0 points over RAM-EHR (64.6 vs 59.6) and 6.3 over GraphCare (64.6 vs 58.3) on MIMIC-III mortality, and by 10.1 points over GraphCare (90.4 vs 80.3) on MIMIC-IV mortality. On MIMIC-IV readmission, KARE's 73.8% Macro F1 exceeds GraphCare (65.5%) by 8.3 points and RAM-EHR (65.5%) by 8.3 points. Among LLM-based methods, KARE substantially surpasses zero-shot Claude 3.5 Sonnet with KARE-augmented context (54.6% vs 64.6% Macro F1 on MIMIC-III mortality) and few-shot EHR-CoAgent (51.7% vs 64.6%), while also beating the fine-tuned Mistral-7B backbone (53.0%) and backbone+Classic RAG (51.4%) by 11.6 and 13.2 points respectively. The sensitivity gap is particularly striking on MIMIC-IV mortality, where KARE detects 73.2% of positive cases versus 57.8% for GraphCare and only 65.0% for the un-augmented fine-tuned backbone, confirming that KG community retrieval is the primary driver of improved true-positive identification in highly imbalanced settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

KARE extends the line of work on knowledge-graph-augmented healthcare prediction initiated by GraphCare (Jiang et al., 2024a), but departs from it by anchoring KG construction to EHR co-occurrence patterns rather than retrieving sparse, random relationships. It also builds on the GraphRAG paradigm (Edge et al., 2024) of hierarchical community detection and summarization, repurposing it from query-focused summarization to dynamic patient-context augmentation for clinical prediction. Compared to prior LLM-based EHR methods (RAM-EHR, EMERGE, EHR-CoAgent), KARE's key distinction is the combination of structured community-level retrieval with fine-tuned reasoning-chain generation via multitask learning, rather than relying on prompting or document-level RAG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a critical gap in clinical AI: producing predictions that are both accurate and interpretable, which is essential for clinician trust in high-stakes settings. The 10–15% improvement over strong baselines on mortality and readmission prediction is clinically meaningful, particularly the sensitivity gains on imbalanced mortality tasks. However, generalizability remains an open question since evaluation is confined to MIMIC (single US hospital system, English-only), and the framework's dependence on LLM-generated knowledge introduces residual hallucination risk. Future work on scaling to diverse populations, non-English settings, and more complex multi-task clinical workflows would strengthen the practical impact.
