## Problem

The traditional log-likelihood (LL) objective for training unsupervised constituency parsers does not explicitly account for the close relationship between constituent structure and sentence semantics, resulting in a weak correlation between LL values and parsing accuracy. This makes LL an insufficient training signal, as evidenced by the difficulty of training PCFG parsers that outperform trivial baselines without heuristic modifications to the LL objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251025/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw text sentences (without punctuation) and a set of LLM-generated paraphrases (produced by gpt-4o-mini using eight semantic-preserving prompts such as passive/active voice conversion, clefting, and topicalization) as input. Paraphrases are normalized via snowball stemming before computing maximal substring frequencies. The output is a tree-shaped constituency structure over the input sentence, evaluated by sentence-F1 scores that aggregate instance-level F1 over all non-trivial spans (excluding sentence-level and single-word spans).

## Method

The paper introduces SemInfo, a metric estimating the semantic information encoded in a constituent structure, by adapting the Probability-Weighted Information (PWI) metric from tf-idf to a novel bag-of-substrings model. A key technical component is the maximal substring definition, which prevents over-counting of nested substring occurrences when measuring how frequently a substring appears across paraphrases. SemInfo for a tree is the sum of PWI values over all constituent substrings, where each PWI value combines the maximal substring generation probability (frequency across paraphrases) with an inverse document frequency term (rarity across the corpus). For training, the authors develop a TreeCRF model parameterized by span-posterior probabilities extracted from the PCFG's inside algorithm, and apply REINFORCE with average baseline to maximize expected SemInfo under the TreeCRF distribution, combined with the LL term and entropy regularization. This TreeCRF-based mean-field approach is shown to be equivalent to direct PCFG optimization but runs 4× faster and uses 1/6 the memory.

## Results

Across five PCFG variants (CPCFG, NPCFG, SCPCFG, SNPCFG, TNPCFG) and four languages, SemInfo-trained parsers achieve an average improvement of 7.85 SF1c over LL-trained counterparts, with per-language gains of +13.09 (English), +6.02 (Chinese), +7.31 (French), and +4.92 (German). The best individual result is SCPCFG on English at 67.27 SF1c, and SNPCFG on French at 55.21 SF1c. SemInfo also shows a strong sentence-level Spearman correlation with SF1i (0.63–0.93) versus near-zero for LL (−0.01 to 0.11), confirming it as a more reliable proxy for parsing accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Spanoverlap (Chen et al., 2024), SemInfo-trained PCFGs outperform by 10.7–13.4 SF1c across all four languages (e.g., 66.84 vs. 52.9 on English). Compared to the larger SCPCFG(2048NT) and SNPCFG(4096NT) from Liu et al. (2023), the SemInfo-trained variants use less than half the non-terminal symbols yet achieve higher SF1c in English (66.92 vs. 60.6; 66.84 vs. 65.1), Chinese (52.26 vs. 42.9; 52.04 vs. 39.9), and French (52.29 vs. 49.9; 54.37 vs. 38.0). SemInfo-trained PCFGs also substantially surpass URNNG (Kim et al., 2019b) by 22.9 SF1c on English and 24.8 on Chinese, NBL-PCFG (Yang et al., 2021a) by 6.4 SF1c on English, S-DIORA (Xu et al., 2021) by 9.2 SF1c on English, and Constituency Test (Cao et al., 2020) by 4.0 SF1c on English. The only exception is German, where SemInfo-trained PCFGs underperform the MTD baseline (which directly decodes the max-SemInfo tree), likely due to a 14% out-of-vocabulary rate in the German test set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Aizawa's Probability-Weighted Information (PWI) metric—originally an information-theoretic reinterpretation of tf-idf for word-topic relevance in bag-of-words models—to a novel bag-of-substrings framework that measures substring-semantic information. It extends the finding of Chen et al. (2024) that constituent structures correlate with frequent substrings among paraphrases by formalizing this as a training objective (SemInfo maximization) and integrating it into PCFG training via a TreeCRF-based REINFORCE pipeline. The work departs from the decades-old LL maximization paradigm for unsupervised PCFG training, which the authors demonstrate has near-zero sentence-level correlation with actual parsing accuracy, and instead positions semantic information as the primary signal for constituent identification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LL has essentially no sentence-level correlation with parsing accuracy (Spearman ρ ≈ 0) while SemInfo maintains ρ of 0.6–0.9 across all PCFG variants and training stages provides a principled explanation for why unsupervised PCFG training has been so difficult and suggests a more reliable optimization target. The 7.85 average SF1c improvement across five PCFG variants and four languages, with state-of-the-art results in three languages, demonstrates practical impact. Open questions include whether the approach extends to lexicalized PCFGs, how it interacts with visual semantic representations (as in Zhao & Titov, 2020), and whether the PCFG architecture itself is now the bottleneck, as the authors note the gap to supervised baselines may require more expressive models like RNN grammars.
