## Problem

Merging multiple task-specific fine-tuned models into a single multi-task model via parameter arithmetic suffers from parameter interference, causing performance degradation on each task. Existing fine-tuning schemes that reduce interference (e.g., linearized fine-tuning) do so at the cost of single-task performance, limiting the merged model's overall quality. The paper asks whether a fine-tuning objective can simultaneously minimize parameter interference and preserve (or improve) each task-specific model's performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251804/cropped_page4_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a shared pre-trained backbone (CLIP ViT-B/32 or ViT-B/16 for eight vision classification tasks; Flan-T5-base for four GLUE NLP tasks) that is independently fine-tuned per task on RGB images or text inputs, producing class labels or text predictions. Each task-specific model is trained without knowledge of other tasks, and the merged multi-task model is constructed post-hoc by linearly combining task vectors (θ_merge = θ₀ + Σαₜτₜ), with task coefficients searched over [0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 1.0] on validation splits.

## Method

The paper derives a fine-tuning objective that jointly minimizes (1) the loss gap between the merged model and the task-specific model (capturing parameter interference) and (2) the task-specific loss (capturing single-task performance). After algebraic simplification, this objective reduces to minimizing the loss at a perturbed parameter point where the perturbation encodes the offsets introduced by merging other tasks. Since other tasks are unknown during fine-tuning, the unknown perturbation is approximated by the ASAM perturbation (θ²-scaled gradient direction), yielding sharpness-aware fine-tuning (SAFT). The key insight is that finding flat minima makes the model robust to the parameter shifts that merging introduces, so the merged model stays close to each task-specific model in loss. Theoretically, the paper proves that SAFT reduces dominant Hessian eigenvalues along the interpolation path between task-specific models, inducing joint-task loss linearity and thus smaller performance gaps after merging.

## Results

On eight vision classification tasks (Cars, DTD, EuroSAT, GTSRB, MNIST, RESISC45, SUN397, SVHN) using CLIP ViT-B/32 with task arithmetic merging, SAFT-ASAM achieves 69.45% absolute and 76.32% normalized accuracy, outperforming standard SGD fine-tuning (68.23%/75.47%). When combined with FTLO, SAFT-ASAM reaches 77.49% absolute and 88.77% normalized accuracy, a +3.03-point improvement in normalized accuracy over FTLO alone. On the larger ViT-B/16 backbone, SAFT-ASAM with task arithmetic achieves 76.77%/82.50% versus 73.40%/79.31% for SGD, and with TIES merging reaches 80.14%/86.23% versus 77.94%/84.04%. On four GLUE NLP tasks with Flan-T5-base, SAFT-ASAM achieves 51.48% absolute and 75.47% normalized accuracy, substantially outperforming SGD (46.54%/54.38%), FTTS (45.86%/56.21%), and FTLO (36.88%/47.72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFT-ASAM consistently improves upon all three fine-tuning baselines: SGD (+1.22 abs, +0.85 norm), FTTS/Ortiz-Jimenez et al. 2023 (+1.03 abs, +0.89 norm), and FTLO/Jin et al. 2025 (+1.56 abs, +3.03 norm), demonstrating orthogonality with existing disentanglement methods. Across merging methods, SAFT-ASAM outperforms SGD with weight averaging (+1.04 abs on ViT-B/32), task arithmetic (+1.22 abs), and TIES/Yadav et al. 2023 (+0.88 abs on ViT-B/32; +2.20 abs on ViT-B/16). Among SAFT variants, ASAM/Kwon et al. 2021 achieves the best single-task accuracy (91.29%) compared to SAM/Foret et al. 2021 (90.16%), Friendly SAM/Li et al. 2024 (90.29%), WA-SAM/Kaddour et al. 2022 (91.06%), SAGM/Wang et al. 2023 (90.96%), PGN/Zhao et al. 2022 (90.90%), and SSAM/Mi et al. 2022 (90.80/90.60). Against other flat-minima techniques, SAFT-ASAM outperforms SWA/Izmailov et al. 2018 (69.45 vs. 68.58 abs with tuning) and substantially outperforms RWP/Li et al. 2022 (69.45 vs. 62.48) and SAGM (69.45 vs. 64.36). On NLP tasks, SAFT-ASAM achieves the highest normalized accuracy (75.47%) by a wide margin over FTTS (56.21%) and FTLO (47.72%), which suffer severe per-task collapse (e.g., FTLO drops to 0% on RTE and 13.30% on SST-2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: sharpness-aware minimization (SAM/ASAM), which seeks flat minima for generalization, and model merging via task arithmetic, which combines independently fine-tuned models through parameter arithmetic. Prior work on reducing parameter interference (FTTS, FTLO) explicitly linearizes fine-tuning to encourage weight disentanglement but sacrifices single-task performance. The key conceptual departure is deriving a fine-tuning objective that simultaneously optimizes for both low interference and high single-task accuracy, then showing this objective is mathematically equivalent to the SAM/ASAM perturbation — meaning a well-studied generalization technique can be repurposed as an interference-mitigation strategy without any architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is simple: replace AdamW with ASAM during fine-tuning and you get a merged model with less performance degradation, with only ~2× training cost and no architectural modifications. The orthogonality result (SAFT improves performance on top of FTTS, FTLO, TIES, and task arithmetic) suggests it can be adopted as a drop-in enhancement for existing merging pipelines. Open questions include whether the benefit persists at larger model scales (tested only on ViT-B and Flan-T5-base), why TIES becomes counterproductive when combined with SAFT + linear fine-tuning (suggesting redundant or conflicting interference-mitigation mechanisms), and whether the theoretical Hessian-eigenvalue bound tightens with more tasks. The ~2× compute overhead may be non-trivial for very large foundation models, though ongoing work on efficient SAM variants could address this.
