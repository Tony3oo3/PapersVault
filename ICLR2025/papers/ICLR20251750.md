## Problem

While MLLM benchmarks have proliferated around visual reasoning and multi-image understanding, the fundamental human capability of association—linking a new observation to prior practice memory through shared semantic concepts—has been entirely overlooked. Existing multi-image benchmarks are confined to single-step evaluation and lack the sequential, multi-step structure that characterizes genuine associative reasoning, leaving a critical gap in our understanding of where MLLMs fall short of human cognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251750/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each association step presents a query image alongside two candidate images (one sharing a semantic concept with the query, one not) plus a memory context that encodes prior inference history; the model must output the correct image index (Image1 or Image2). After a correct association, a deduction step requires the model to name the shared concept from a fixed option list (e.g., eight adjectives or verbs). All images are filtered to ≥50,000 pixels, and the sequential protocol means the correctly selected image becomes the next query, so a single error terminates the chain and determines the max/mean step score.

## Method

The paper introduces an annotation-free construction method that converts existing general datasets (OCL for attribute/affordance adjectives, Pangea for action verbs) into association benchmarks by randomly pairing samples and labeling pairs as positive when their annotations share at least one semantic concept, eliminating costly manual curation. On top of this, three task levels are defined: single-step (fixed correct memory), synchronous (one concept governs the entire chain), and asynchronous (two concepts alternate every five steps), each evaluated with Max|Mean Step metrics. Three memory strategies—Structure Memory (raw dictionary), Natural Language Memory (descriptive sentences), and Chain Memory (obj→concept→obj sequences)—are injected into the prompt with an attention-based reinforcement/forgetting mechanism (weight +w_r on evidence match, −d_f otherwise). A three-stage data refinement pipeline (resolution filter, MLLM verification via Gemini-1.5-Flash/GPT-4V, and human expert review) ensures annotation correctness, and all experiments are conducted zero-shot with prompt-engineering variants (ComKnow, one-shot, CoT) across open-source MLLMs, closed-source GPT-4o/Gemini-1.5-Flash, a MoE ensemble, and human experts.

## Results

On single-step association, GPT-4o achieves the highest mean success ratio in adjective concepts (89.87% with NLM memory), while Gemini-1.5-Flash leads in verb concepts (89.58% with NLM). In synchronous association, the gap to humans is stark: human experts attain a mean-step of 350 on attribute concepts, whereas the best open-source model (QWen2-VL with NLM) reaches only 13.96, and even the best closed-source model (Gemini-1.5-Flash) averages only 27.8 mean-steps versus 140.2 for humans. Across all three association levels, open-source MLLMs consistently underperform, with mPLUG-Owl3 trailing the others by 10–20 percentage points in single-step success ratio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, GPT-4o outperforms Gemini-1.5-Flash on adjective concepts (89.87% vs. 88.39% with NLM) but trails on verb concepts (86.13% vs. 89.58% with NLM), suggesting complementary strengths. Among open-source models, QWen2-VL consistently leads (e.g., 86.63% attribute, 88.01% action with NLM), followed by LLaVA-OneVision, with mPLUG-Owl3 lagging by 10–20 points across all concepts. The NLM memory strategy yields the best results in most settings, outperforming StructM and ChainM by 2–6 percentage points, while NoM (no memory) is the weakest baseline. In synchronous association, all open-source models achieve mean-steps below 20 versus 350 for human experts, and even Gemini-1.5-Flash (27.8) falls far short of the human average (140.2). A Mixture-of-Experts combining three open-source models outperforms any individual open-source model in asynchronous association, yet still remains far below human performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing a sequential, multi-step association benchmark for MLLMs, departing from existing multi-image reasoning benchmarks (Visual Genome, Bongard-HOI, MILEBench) that are confined to single-step evaluation. It repurposes existing datasets (OCL for attributes/affordances, Pangea for actions) through an annotation-free construction method that transforms general supervised data into association chains, avoiding costly manual curation. The memory strategies (StructM, NLM, ChainM) draw on prompt engineering and in-context learning literature but apply them to a novel sequential decision-making setting with dynamic memory updates, rather than the static few-shot or CoT paradigms used in prior MLLM evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even state-of-the-art closed-source MLLMs (GPT-4V, Gemini-1.5-Flash) exhibit a ~5x gap compared to human experts in a fundamental cognitive ability (association), suggesting a structural limitation beyond model scale. The paper identifies the likely cause as a lack of training on unpaired sequential data and insufficient multi-image instruction tuning, pointing toward a new learning paradigm that links new observations with prior knowledge. Open questions include whether dedicated association training or improved memory architectures can close this gap, and whether the annotation-free construction method generalizes to other semantic concept types beyond adjectives and verbs.
