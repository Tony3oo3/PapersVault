## Problem

Knowledge databases like STRING and KEGG provide general disease gene networks that do not distinguish between molecular subtypes, creating a mismatch when studying subtype-specific biology. Existing statistical methods (WGCNA, ARACNe) ignore prior network knowledge, while GNN-based methods (CSGNN, LR-GNN) only reconstruct the general network without explicitly learning the distinct gene interactions unique to each subtype.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252434/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two inputs: (1) patient gene expression profiles as an M×N matrix (~11,000 genes, 291–638 patients per cancer type from TCGA) and (2) a prior gene interaction graph (102–146 nodes, 159–868 edges) constructed by intersecting gene IDs across KEGG, STRING, InterPro, and Monarch databases. The output is a set of sparse sub-graphs G_y (one per subtype) where |G_y| ≪ |G|, along with binary link predictions for gene-gene interactions. The sub-graphs are not independent and must maintain biological meaningfulness.

## Method

GeSubNet is a three-module framework trained in sequential phases. Patient-M uses a VQ-VAE to project gene expression into a discrete latent codebook (K-way categorical distribution) that separates subtypes. Graph-M uses Neo-GNN (neighborhood-overlap-aware GNN) to learn graph representations Z_g via a link prediction task on the prior network, combining structural edge information with node features to prevent over-smoothing. Infer-M introduces a novel integration loss: with Patient-M and Graph-M decoder parameters fixed, it reconstructs gene expression as X̃ = Z_p · Z_g^T and backpropagates the reconstruction error through the GNN encoder (θ) to refine graph representations, effectively conditioning graph generation on patient separation. This three-phase training ensures the final representation simultaneously encodes accurate prior gene interactions and distinguishes subtype-specific gene behaviors.

## Results

GeSubNet achieves the best scores on all three graph metrics (CDV, GED, DCS) across all four cancer datasets (BRCA, GBM, LGG, OV). Compared to the strongest baseline CSGNN, GeSubNet improves GED by 35.8%/32.4%/20.2%/34.1% and reduces DCS by 29.8%/13.5%/21.6%/19.3% on BRCA/GBM/LGG/OV respectively. In the biological validation, GeSubNet identifies the most enriched biological functions (#EBF: 8/6/6/5 vs. best baseline 6/5/4/4) and achieves a Shift Rate of 0.83 for high-ranking genes in the simulated knockout experiment, nearly 2.5× higher than the next-best method (CSGNN at 0.34), while suppressing low-ranking gene shifts to 0.12.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeSubNet outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across all four cancer types. The four statistical methods (WGCNA, wTO, ARACNe, LEAP) are consistently the weakest, with GED scores ranging only 0.39–0.59 and DCS scores above 0.72, indicating they produce dense, structurally similar networks that fail to capture subtype-specific sparsity. Among GNN-based baselines, CSGNN is the closest competitor, achieving GED of 0.66/0.64/0.68/0.35 and DCS of 0.52/0.74/0.73/0.80, yet GeSubNet still leads by 12–34% in GED and 13–30% in DCS reduction. LR-GNN and GAERF show intermediate performance, with LR-GNN slightly ahead on GED for GBM (0.61) and LGG (0.66) but trailing CSGNN on CDV. In the biological knockout experiment, GeSubNet's Shift Rate of 0.83 for high-ranking genes dwarfs all baselines (0.23–0.34), while its low-ranking Shift Rate of 0.12 is the lowest, confirming it uniquely isolates subtype-critical genes. The OV dataset is the weakest for all methods due to limited samples (291 patients) and sparse knowledge-base coverage, but GeSubNet still leads by a clear margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VQ-VAE for discrete latent representation learning and Neo-GNN for structure-aware link prediction, combining them in a three-module architecture (Patient-M, Graph-M, Infer-M) that prior gene network inference methods do not employ. It departs from existing GNN-based approaches (GAERF, LR-GNN, CSGNN) that reconstruct general disease-gene networks by introducing an inference loss that conditions graph generation on patient subtype separation, explicitly targeting subtype-specific interactions rather than general network reconstruction. The statistical baselines (WGCNA, ARACNe, wTO, LEAP) operate solely on expression data without knowledge database integration, which GeSubNet addresses by fusing both data sources through its multi-step training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper releases integrated gene network datasets from four knowledge databases (KEGG, STRING, InterPro, Monarch) aligned with TCGA expression profiles, filling a practical gap for subtype-specific network research. The simulated gene knockout evaluation and the proposed Shift Rate metric offer a new way to assess biological relevance beyond structural graph metrics, though the metric is self-defined and validated on a single cancer type (BRCA). Open questions include whether the three-phase training generalizes to cancers with very few subtypes or limited samples (as seen with OV), whether the generated networks can directly inform therapeutic targeting, and whether the approach scales to single-cell or multi-omics settings.
