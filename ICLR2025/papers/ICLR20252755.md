## Problem

Most offline RL methods assume a unimodal action distribution, causing learned policies to collapse toward averaged actions that may not exist in the dataset when multiple valid but conflicting behaviours coexist. Existing multi-modal approaches (GANs, VAEs, diffusion models) attempt to model the entire action distribution, which is unnecessarily complex when only a single high-reward mode is relevant for optimal decision-making.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20252755/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of state-action-reward transitions (state vectors from MuJoCo locomotion or Fetch robot environments) collected by one or more unknown behaviour policies. It produces a single unimodal Gaussian policy mapping states to continuous actions. Unusually, the policy is not learned from the full dataset distribution but exclusively from actions sampled from one selected Gaussian mode of the behaviour policy's GMM, with advantage-based weighting within that mode.

## Method

LOM operates in three stages: (1) a Mixture Density Network (MDN) models the behaviour policy as a GMM with M Gaussian components per state, capturing multi-modal action clusters; (2) a hyper Q-function Q_φ(s,u) is learned to estimate the expected return of each mode u at state s (via Proposition 1, Q_H^ζ(s,u) = E_{a~φ^u}[Q^{π_ζ}(s,a)]), and a greedy hyper-policy selects the argmax mode; (3) weighted imitation learning (advantage-weighted regression) trains a unimodal Gaussian policy on actions sampled from the selected mode, with weights exp(A/β) clipped for stability. The H-MDP framework formalises mode selection as a higher-level decision, and theoretical results (Theorems 1–3) guarantee that the resulting policy uniformly dominates both the behaviour policy and the greedy mode-selection policy, with a bounded improvement quantified by the performance difference lemma.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark (15 tasks across halfcheetah, hopper, and walker2d with five dataset types), LOM achieves the highest normalised score in 12 out of 15 tasks, with improvements of 0.2%–7.9% over the best baseline. The largest gains appear in highly multi-modal settings: on medium-replay hopper, LOM scores 100.8 vs. 89.0 (WCVAE) and 89.2 (DMPO); on full-replay walker2d, LOM reaches 97.9 vs. 96.2 (LAPO). On the dedicated multi-modal Fetch tasks (FetchReach, FetchPush, FetchPickAndPlace), LOM outperforms the next-best method (WCGAN/WCVAE) by 4.9–3.7 points on average, and surpasses AWAC by 14.8 points on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOM is compared against four general offline RL methods (OneStep, AWAC, CQL, TD3BC) and four multi-modal-specific methods (LAPO, DMPO, WCGAN, WCVAE). LOM wins 12 of 15 D4RL tasks; the three losses are narrow (medium-expert halfcheetah: 92.7 vs. LAPO's 93.0; expert halfcheetah: 95.2 vs. DMPO's 97.0; expert walker2d: 109.3 vs. LAPO's 112.2), all in less multi-modal settings. The most dramatic wins are in highly multi-modal datasets: on medium-replay hopper, LOM (100.8) beats the next-best WCVAE (99.0) by 1.8 points and DMPO (89.2) by 11.6 points; on full-replay walker2d, LOM (97.9) surpasses LAPO (96.2) and DMPO (95.4) by 1.7 and 2.5 points respectively. Among the multi-modal baselines, WCGAN and WCVAE (Wang et al., 2024b) are the strongest competitors, but LOM still edges them out in 11 of 15 tasks. On the dedicated Fetch multi-modal tasks, LOM leads WCGAN by 4.9 (FetchReach), 3.1 (FetchPush), and 5.0 (FetchPick) points, and outperforms AWAC by 14.8, 17.5, and 11.5 points respectively, confirming that focusing on a single high-reward mode is more effective than modelling the full multi-modal distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

LOM sits at the intersection of weighted imitation learning (AWAC, AWR, OneStep) and multi-modal action distribution modeling in offline RL (PLAS, LAPO, DMPO, WCGAN/WCVAE, diffusion-based policies). It departs from prior multi-modal approaches by rejecting the need to model the full action distribution: rather than using GANs, VAEs, or normalizing flows to capture all modes, LOM uses a Mixture Density Network to decompose the behavior policy into Gaussian modes and then learns from only the single highest-rewarding mode via a novel Hyper-MDP and Hyper Q-function framework. This positions LOM as a simplification of the generative-modeling line of work, trading distributional fidelity for targeted, mode-specific policy improvement with theoretical guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically common scenario—offline datasets collected from multiple policies or exploration strategies—where unimodal policy assumptions cause performance degradation. The two-step improvement guarantee (behavior policy → greedy mode policy → LOM policy) provides a clean theoretical foundation, and the approach is computationally lighter than diffusion or GAN-based alternatives. However, the number of Gaussian components M remains a sensitive hyperparameter with no principled selection method, and the D4RL improvements over SOTA are modest (0.2–7.9%), with the most compelling gains appearing on synthetic Fetch tasks specifically designed to be highly multi-modal. Open questions include whether Bellman-style updates for the hyper Q-function can reduce extrapolation error, and whether automatic mode-count estimation (e.g., bump hunting) can eliminate the M hyperparameter.
