## Problem

LoRA and its SVD-based successors (PiSSA, MiLoRA) treat all parametric knowledge in the base model uniformly, either fine-tuning principal or residual singular components without distinguishing task-relevant from noisy or irrelevant knowledge. This leads to two issues: task-specific updates inadvertently modify noisy long-tail knowledge, and the low-rank matrices inherit information irrelevant to the target task, causing representational conflicts and suboptimal performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251992/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

KaSA takes a pretrained Transformer weight matrix W^(0) ∈ R^{n×m} and a task-specific dataset of input-output pairs, and produces a fine-tuned model whose adaptation module consists of an SVD-truncated base (retaining only the top m−r singular components) plus a low-rank SVD-form update ΔU ΔΣ ΔV^T with r learnable singular values. The adaptation module is linear and can be merged back into the base weights at inference time, incurring no additional latency. Different tasks can share a single base model with distinct adaptation modules, reducing storage for multi-task deployment.

## Method

KaSA operates in two stages. First, knowledge-based SVD truncation decomposes W^(0) = UΣV^T and discards the r smallest singular components (W_noise), yielding a refined W_world that retains essential world knowledge. Second, knowledge-aware singular-value adaptation reparameterizes the task-specific update as ΔW = ΔU ΔΣ ΔV^T, where ΔU and ΔV are semi-orthogonal matrices and ΔΣ is a diagonal matrix of r learnable singular values that dynamically gate the activation of parametric knowledge according to its relevance to the downstream task. Training jointly optimizes three objectives: the standard cross-entropy task loss L1, a Frobenius-norm regularization L2 = Σ(Δσ_j)² that bounds the magnitude of updates (derived via the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem), and an orthogonality constraint L3 that enforces ΔU^TΔU = I_r and ΔV^TΔV = I_r. The key distinction from PiSSA/MiLoRA is that singular values are not fixed at initialization but are learned end-to-end, allowing the model to selectively amplify or suppress knowledge components per layer and per task.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark, KaSA achieves 86.3% average accuracy with RoBERTa-base (0.3M trainable params, ~0.24% of total), surpassing FFT (85.2%) and LoRA (85.2%), and 89.0% with RoBERTa-large vs. FFT's 88.2%. On instruction following with 7B–13B LLMs, KaSA consistently outperforms FFT on MT-Bench (e.g., 4.97 vs. 4.69 for Gemma 7B; 4.95 vs. 4.37 for LLaMA2 13B) and beats LoRA, PiSSA, and MiLoRA across all four synthetic 128K datasets. On commonsense reasoning, KaSA reaches 84.6% average with LLaMA3 8B, exceeding MiLoRA (81.9%), LoRA (80.8%), and even ChatGPT (77.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KaSA outperforms all 14 PEFT baselines and FFT across the majority of evaluated settings. Against LoRA, KaSA gains +1.1% average on RoBERTa-base GLUE (86.3 vs. 85.2), +0.58 on MT-Bench for Gemma 7B (4.97 vs. 4.32), and +3.9% average on commonsense reasoning for LLaMA3 8B (84.6 vs. 80.8). Among SVD-based methods, KaSA beats PiSSA by +1.9% on LLaMA2 7B commonsense reasoning (81.5 vs. 73.8) and MiLoRA by +2.3% (81.5 vs. 79.2), while also surpassing both on every GLUE and instruction-following task. Adapter-based methods (Adapter^H, ^D, ^P, ^L) and BitFit are consistently outperformed on NLU, with KaSA achieving 86.3% vs. Adapter^D's 84.2% and BitFit's 85.4% on RoBERTa-base. On NLG (E2E), KaSA matches or exceeds SARA (72.1 vs. 72.3 ROUGE-L) and outperforms LoRA, AdaLoRA, DyLoRA, and VeRA on 4 of 5 metrics for GPT-2 Medium. At rank 128, KaSA also surpasses DoRA and CorDA on the GLUE benchmark, with only a marginal loss to FFT on QNLI (92.71 vs. 92.8). Notably, KaSA with ~0.24% trainable parameters outperforms FFT in 13 of 14 NLU scenarios and on MT-Bench for all four LLMs, demonstrating that the knowledge-aware SVD structure recovers full fine-tuning quality at a fraction of the parameter cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

KaSA extends the SVD-based PEFT lineage initiated by PiSSA (which fine-tunes principal singular components) and MiLoRA (which fine-tunes minor singular components). Rather than choosing one side of the SVD spectrum for adaptation, KaSA performs knowledge-based SVD truncation to remove noisy minor components from the base model, then reparameterizes the task-specific update in full SVD form (ΔW = ΔU ΔΣ ΔV^T) with learnable knowledge-aware singular values. This departs from LoRA's random Gaussian initialization and from PiSSA/MiLoRA's fixed SVD initialization by making the singular values themselves trainable parameters that dynamically gate knowledge relevance, while orthogonality constraints on ΔU and ΔV preserve structural integrity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KaSA provides a principled mechanism for selective knowledge activation during PEFT, addressing the well-motivated concern that both noisy knowledge in the base model and irrelevant inherited knowledge in low-rank updates degrade performance. The consistent outperformance of FFT with only ~0.24% trainable parameters across 16 benchmarks, 4 synthetic datasets, and 6 model architectures (RoBERTa, DeBERTaV3, GPT-2, LLaMA2/3, Mistral, Gemma) suggests the SVD-truncation-plus-learnable-singular-values recipe is broadly applicable. Open questions include adaptive determination of the optimal SVD truncation rank (the paper notes performance peaks at k=8 but declines beyond), whether the approach extends to non-Transformer architectures, and whether the knowledge-aware singular values can be interpreted as a form of task-specific knowledge routing at scale.
