## Problem

Segment Anything (SAM) solved promptable segmentation for static images, but video segmentation introduces unique challenges—complex motion, occlusion, deformation, and the need to efficiently process large numbers of frames—that SAM does not address. Existing video object segmentation models and datasets are limited in scope (focused on specific object classes, whole objects only) and lack the interactive, open-world 'segment anything' capability that SAM provided for images. The paper asks whether a single unified model can handle promptable segmentation across both images and videos with strong zero-shot generalization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes video frames in a streaming fashion (one at a time) at 1024×1024 resolution, accepting prompts—positive/negative clicks, bounding boxes, or binary masks—on any frame in the sequence, not just the first. It outputs a spatio-temporal masklet (a segmentation mask on every frame), a per-frame mask for the prompted frame, and an occlusion score indicating whether the target object is visible. When applied to a single image, the memory bank is empty and the model reduces to SAM-like behavior, producing multiple candidate masks for ambiguous prompts.

## Method

SAM 2 extends SAM with a streaming memory architecture: a MAE-pretrained Hiera image encoder produces per-frame embeddings, which are conditioned on a memory bank via a stack of transformer blocks performing self-attention followed by cross-attention to stored memories and lightweight object pointer vectors. The memory bank maintains a FIFO queue of N recent frame memories (spatial feature maps fusing predicted masks with image embeddings) plus M prompted-frame memories, along with object pointers derived from mask decoder output tokens for high-level semantic tracking. A SAM-style two-way mask decoder produces per-frame masks, and a memory encoder generates new memories from each prediction for future frames. Training jointly on images and videos simulates interactive prompting with corrective clicks sampled from ground-truth masklets, and the model is trained on the newly introduced SA-V dataset (35.5M masks, 50.9K videos) collected via a three-phase data engine that progressively incorporates SAM 2 in the annotation loop, achieving 8.4× faster annotation than the initial per-frame SAM approach.

## Results

On the semi-supervised VOS benchmark (Table 6), SAM 2 (Hiera-B+) achieves 76.6 J&F on MOSE val, 90.2 on DAVIS 2017 val, 78.0 on LVOS val, 76.8 on SA-V val, 77.0 on SA-V test, and 88.6 on YTVOS 2019 val, outperforming the best prior method (Cutie-base+) by 4.9, 2.1, 12.0, 15.5, 14.2, and 1.1 points respectively. In the interactive promptable video segmentation setting, SAM 2 achieves higher J&F accuracy with over 3× fewer interactions than SAM+XMem++ and SAM+Cutie baselines. On image segmentation (SA-23), SAM 2 matches or exceeds SAM (58.9 vs 58.1 mIoU with 1 click) while running 6× faster (130.1 vs 21.7 FPS on A100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM 2 decisively outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every benchmark where they overlap. Against the strongest interactive baselines, SAM+XMem++ and SAM+Cutie (Table 4), SAM 2 leads by 7.8–8.0 J&F points with 3-click prompts (75.3 vs 68.4/70.1) and by 5.2–5.6 points with ground-truth mask prompts (79.3 vs 72.7/74.1) across 17 video datasets. Against the best dedicated VOS methods in Table 6, SAM 2 (Hiera-B+) surpasses Cutie-base+ by 4.9 J&F on MOSE, 2.1 on DAVIS 2017, 15.5 on SA-V val, and 14.2 on SA-V test, with the largest gaps on the open-world SA-V benchmark where prior methods plateau around 61–63 J&F. On LVOS (long-term VOS), SAM 2 achieves 78.0 J&F versus Cutie-base's 66.0, a 12-point margin. On YTVOS 2019, SAM 2 leads JointFormer (87.4) by 1.2 points. For image segmentation, SAM 2 matches SAM's 1-click mIoU (58.9 vs 58.1 on SA-23) while being 6× faster, and with the full training mix reaches 61.9 mIoU, exceeding SAM's 58.1 by 3.8 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAM 2 is a direct architectural extension of SAM (Kirillov et al., 2023), reusing its prompt encoder and mask decoder design while adding a streaming memory mechanism (memory attention, memory encoder, memory bank, and object pointers) to handle temporal context. It departs from the dominant decoupled paradigm in video segmentation—where a model segments on one frame and a separate tracker propagates (e.g., SAM+XMem++, SAM+Cutie)—by unifying segmentation and tracking in a single transformer that processes frames sequentially with cross-attention to stored memories. The data engine methodology extends the SA-1B annotation pipeline to video, introducing a three-phase model-in-the-loop protocol that progressively incorporates SAM 2's own predictions to accelerate annotation by 8.4×.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SA-V dataset (35.5M masks, 50.9K videos) is 53× larger than any prior VOS dataset and covers object parts, small objects, and occlusion/reappearance patterns that existing datasets underrepresent, making it a critical resource for the field. The unified model's ability to achieve better accuracy with 3× fewer interactions than decoupled baselines suggests that memory-augmented streaming architectures are a more natural fit for interactive video segmentation than the segment-then-track pipeline. Open questions include handling shot changes, crowded scenes with similar objects, and extending beyond segmentation to semantic recognition; the paper also notes that multi-object processing currently lacks inter-object communication, and the data engine still relies on human verification.
