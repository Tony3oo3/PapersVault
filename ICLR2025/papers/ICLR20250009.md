## Problem

Existing KV cache compression methods designed for LLMs perform suboptimally on Vision-Language Models because VLMs exhibit fundamentally different attention sparsity patterns: a clear modality boundary between visual and language tokens, and non-monotonic layer-wise sparsity that varies from 70% to 99% across layers. No prior work had investigated VLM-specific attention sparsity or designed KV cache compression tailored to the multi-modal structure of VLM prompts.

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on a VLM prompt consisting of visual tokens (produced by a vision encoder and projection layer, e.g., ~576 tokens per image for CLIP-ViT-L/14@336) followed by language instruction tokens. During prefill, it computes post-vision attention statistics from only the trailing language tokens (τ tokens after the visual block) to determine per-layer cache budgets and token eviction scores, then produces a compressed KV cache retaining a user-specified fraction α (e.g., 10%) of the original cache. The compressed cache is subsequently used for standard autoregressive text generation, with the eviction decision made once after prefill and amortized across all decoding steps.

## Method

VL-Cache combines two mechanisms guided by post-vision attention (the attention scores from only the language tokens that follow visual tokens in the prompt). First, a sparsity-aware budget allocation step applies a threshold filter (p=1%) to the post-vision attention matrix at each layer and head, computes the resulting sparsity ratio, and distributes the total cache budget proportionally to each layer's non-sparse ratio—making the allocation prompt-specific rather than using a fixed pyramid schedule. Second, a modality-aware token scoring policy ranks cache tokens by their accumulated post-vision attention scores, which avoids the length bias of full-prompt accumulated attention and the fixed-window mismatch of sliding-window attention. The key insight is that post-vision attention closely mirrors the attention pattern of decoding tokens (as shown by higher CacheHitRate), and since visual tokens dominate prompt length (τ ≪ m), computing post-vision attention costs O(τm) rather than O(m²). The entire compression is implemented with custom Triton kernels that avoid materializing the full attention matrix in HBM, adding only 1–4% overhead to prefill latency.

## Results

With only 10% of the KV cache retained, VL-Cache achieves near-full-cache accuracy across all evaluated datasets: 100.36 CIDEr on COCO Caption (vs. 100.68 full) with LLaVA-Mistral-7B, 84 ANLS on DocVQA (vs. 85 full) with LLaVA-1.6-34B, and 42% accuracy on MathVista (vs. 43% full) with LLaVA-1.6-34B. In speed benchmarks, VL-Cache delivers up to 7.08× decoding speedup and up to 2.33× end-to-end speedup (100 output tokens) while reducing GPU KV cache memory by 90%. The prefill overhead of the sparsity computation is only 1–4% of prefill latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VL-Cache consistently outperforms all six baselines at the 10% KV cache budget across all three primary datasets and both model sizes. The largest gaps appear against StreamingLLM, which collapses to 8.23 CIDEr on COCO Caption (LLaVA-34B) and 34 ANLS on DocVQA (LLaVA-34B) at 10% budget, versus VL-Cache's 137.35 and 84 respectively. H2O, which uses accumulated attention scores, retains 90.36 CIDEr on COCO Caption (Mistral-7B) but falls well short of VL-Cache's 100.36; the gap widens dramatically on the 34B model (58.14 vs. 137.35). PyramidKV's static monotonically-decreasing budget allocation is competitive at higher budgets (≥40%) but underperforms VL-Cache at 10% on COCO Caption (66.41 vs. 100.36) and DocVQA (60 vs. 62 for Mistral-7B). ZipCache is the closest competitor on COCO Caption with LLaVA-34B (131.61 vs. 137.35) but trails on DocVQA (74 vs. 84). On MathVista, all methods converge near full-cache accuracy at 10% due to the dataset's 54% multiple-choice composition, making differentiation minimal (VL-Cache: 39–42, best baseline: 38–42).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KV cache compression line of work (H2O, PyramidKV, StreamingLLM, ZipCache) from LLMs to Vision-Language Models, where it identifies that directly porting these methods yields suboptimal results due to fundamentally different attention sparsity patterns. The key conceptual departure is the discovery of a modality boundary in VLM attention: decoding tokens attend primarily to post-vision language tokens rather than visual tokens, motivating a 'post-vision attention' scoring policy and a sparsity-aware, prompt-adaptive layer budget allocation that replaces the fixed or monotonically-decreasing schedules used by prior methods. The ThresholdFilter mechanism is inherited from H2O, but its application to only the post-vision query slice (O(τm) vs. O(m²)) is a novel efficiency gain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 90% KV cache memory reduction with near-full accuracy at 10% budget is practically significant for VLM serving, where visual tokens dominate context length and memory is the primary bottleneck. The modality-boundary observation and non-monotonic layer-wise sparsity pattern challenge the pyramid-style allocation assumption and provide a principled basis for future VLM-specific compression. Limitations include evaluation restricted to the LLaVA family (Mistral-7B and 34B), compression applied only to prefill tokens (not decoded tokens), and end-to-end speedup bounded by prefill latency (max 2.33×). Extending to video inputs, periodic compression of decoded tokens, and integration with continuous batching/chunked prefill are identified as important next steps.
