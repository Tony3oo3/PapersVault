## Problem

Pareto Front Learning (PFL) methods parameterize the entire Pareto Front with a single model, enabling user-selectable trade-offs at inference, but existing approaches suffer from prohibitive memory overhead (requiring full model copies or hypernetworks of comparable size), slow convergence, and inconsistent preference-to-objective mappings caused by stochastic Dirichlet preference sampling. These limitations prevent PFL from scaling to large scene-understanding networks and undermine the reliability of the preference-to-performance guarantee.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250806/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the user supplies a preference vector λ in the T-dimensional simplex (e.g., λ=[0.7, 0.3] for two tasks), which modulates the effective layer weights via the low-rank adapters to produce task-specific outputs—segmentation masks, depth maps, surface normals, class labels, or joint torques—without retraining. During training, each batch uses M deterministically sampled preference vectors (M=3–5 in experiments), and the same λ simultaneously weights both the adapter composition in the forward pass and the scalarized multi-task loss, creating a direct link between each adapter and its corresponding task objective.

## Method

PaLoRA augments every layer of a base network with T task-specific low-rank adapters (A_t ∈ R^{n×r}, B_t ∈ R^{r×m}, r≪min(n,m)), so the effective weight becomes W + (α/r)Σ_t λ_t A_t B_t; the base network learns shared representations while adapters capture task-specific features, incurring only 4.2–6.3% parameter overhead on scene-understanding benchmarks. To replace stochastic Dirichlet sampling, PaLoRA draws M evenly-spaced preference vectors in the simplex and anneals them from the center toward the edges over training via a temperature schedule λ_m(τ) = λ̃_m^{τ/Q} / Σ_t λ̃_{m,t}^{τ/Q}, mirroring the natural progression from general to specialized feature learning. This deterministic schedule eliminates the variance and starvation effects of random sampling, maintains a valid Pareto Front throughout training, and enables PaLoRA to converge faster than PaMaL while achieving higher hypervolume with 23.8–31.7× less memory overhead.

## Results

On CityScapes (segmentation + depth), PaLoRA achieves 71.11 mIoU and 0.0140 Abs Err with only 4.2% parameter overhead, outperforming the best PFL baseline PaMaL (70.35 mIoU, 100% overhead) by 23.8× in memory reduction while matching or exceeding the best MTL method Nash-MTL (71.13 mIoU, 0% overhead). On NYUv2 (segmentation + depth + surface normal), PaLoRA reaches 38.27 mIoU with 6.3% overhead versus PaMaL's 33.94 mIoU at 200% overhead (31.7× reduction). On MultiMNIST, PaLoRA achieves 96.55% and 95.39% accuracy on the two digit tasks with rank r=1 (12.5% extra parameters), surpassing PaMaL (96.17%, 95.32%) and PHN (96.04%, 94.91%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaLoRA clearly outperforms all three PFL baselines: PaMaL (Dimitriadis 2023) is beaten on every CityScapes metric (71.11 vs 70.35 mIoU, 51.27 vs 54.52 Rel Err) while using 23.8× fewer extra parameters (4.2% vs 100%); COSMOS (Ruchte 2021) is non-competitive on depth (0.0539 vs 0.0140 Abs Err); and PHN (Navon 2021) is omitted from CityScapes/NYUv2 due to its ×100 memory overhead but is outperformed on MultiMNIST (96.55 vs 96.04 top-left accuracy). Against the strongest MTL baseline Nash-MTL (Navon 2022), PaLoRA is within 0.02 mIoU on CityScapes (71.11 vs 71.13) and 0.61 mIoU on NYUv2 (38.27 vs 37.66, where PaLoRA actually wins), while adding the critical ability to select any trade-off at inference. Loss-balancing MTL methods (LS, UW, DWA, RLW) are consistently outperformed by 1–2 mIoU on segmentation and by large margins on depth (e.g., 51.27 vs 124.06 Rel Err for LS). Gradient-balancing methods (PCGrad, CAGrad, Graddrop, RotoGrad, Auto-λ) are also surpassed on both segmentation and depth, with PaLoRA achieving the best depth Rel Err (51.27) among all controllable methods. On the 7-task SARCOS benchmark, PaLoRA converges considerably faster than PaMaL, which requires joint optimization of 7 full model copies, while using only rank r=4 (62% parameter increase).

## Relation to Prior Work

PaLoRA sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, Hu et al. 2022) and Pareto Front Learning (PaMaL, Dimitriadis et al. 2023). It replaces PaMaL's full-rank model copies with task-specific low-rank adapters, reducing the weight-generating mechanism from T full model duplicates to T lightweight adapter pairs per layer. The key conceptual departure from prior PFL methods (hypernetworks, weight ensembles, input augmentation) is the structured division of labor: the frozen-involvement backbone learns general features while adapters capture task-specific residuals, reinforced by a deterministic annealed preference schedule that replaces the standard Dirichlet sampling. A concurrent work (Chen & Kwok, 2024) also applied LoRAs to PF approximation, but PaLoRA additionally introduces the deterministic schedule and provides the convergence/validity analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 23.8–31.7× memory reduction over PaMaL makes PFL practically viable for large scene-understanding networks (SegNet on CityScapes/NYUv2) where prior methods were infeasible, and the LoRA-based design inherits compatibility with pre-trained foundation models—a property hypernetwork-based PFL lacks. The deterministic schedule's ability to maintain a valid Pareto Front throughout training (rather than only at convergence) addresses a reliability gap that has limited PFL adoption. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-CNN backbones (transformers, diffusion models), how it behaves with more than 7 tasks, and whether the annealing schedule generalizes beyond the simplex geometry used here. The Pareto expansion capability also opens a path for incremental multi-task deployment without full retraining.
