## Problem

Existing surgical object recognition models are either closed-set (limited to ~11 classes from EndoVis17/18) or require manual inputs like bounding boxes and text prompts to produce segmentation results, preventing fully automated recognition in diverse surgical environments. The fundamental bottleneck is the scarcity of large-scale annotated surgical datasets, as manual annotation demands specialized expertise and is prohibitively time-consuming.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20251209/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RASO accepts either a single surgical image (384×384 RGB) or a video clip from which N frames are uniformly sampled at regular intervals. It outputs a multi-label set of surgical object tags (from a vocabulary of 2,066 unique tags covering organs, instruments, actions, and phases) with per-tag recognition scores, thresholded to yield the final tag list. For open-vocabulary inference on unseen tags, the CLIP text encoder generates new tag embeddings on the fly, extending the vocabulary without retraining.

## Method

RASO builds on the RAM architecture, using a Swin-Transformer (swin-large) image encoder and a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 text encoder to embed a predefined tag list; a tag decoder combines visual and tag embeddings to predict per-tag logits. The key architectural addition is a temporal-attention fusion layer that applies multi-head attention across stacked frame-level features (h_images ∈ R^{N×T×D}) to produce a video-level representation (h_video ∈ R^{T×D}), enabling direct video inference ~4× faster than frame-by-frame processing. Training is two-stage: pretraining on 901K weakly-supervised image-tag-text triplets (10 epochs) aligns visual features with surgical transcriptions via a text decoder, followed by fine-tuning on mixed image-tag and video-tag pairs (4 epochs). The weakly-supervised data pipeline processes 2,200 unannotated surgical lecture videos through WhisperX transcription, spaCy biomedical NER for static entities, SceneGraphParser for verb-noun action triplets, and GPT-4o image annotation, yielding 3.6M tag annotations with minimal human intervention.

## Results

In zero-shot surgical image recognition, RASO achieves mAP of 22.6 (CholecT50), 37.8 (Cholec80), 58.8 (RSS/EndoVis18), and 20.0 (GraSP), outperforming the best baseline SurgVLP by 2.9, 4.5, 10.6, and 7.2 mAP respectively. In the supervised setting on CholecT50, RASO reaches an overall mAP of 57.5, surpassing the SOTA Rendezvous model (56.4) with only 4 epochs of fine-tuning versus Rendezvous's 200+ epochs. For video recognition, the temporal-attention fusion layer boosts verb (action) mAP from 68.5 to 74.5 and overall mAP from 60.1 to 61.3, while also reducing inference latency by ~4× (100.8 ms vs. 398.8 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RASO outperforms all four zero-shot baselines (CLIP, BiomedCLIP, PubMedCLIP, SurgVLP) on every benchmark, with the largest margin over SurgVLP on RSS (+10.6 mAP) and GraSP (+7.2 mAP). Even without GPT-4o fine-tuning, RASO (w/o FT) already surpasses CLIP, BiomedCLIP, and PubMedCLIP on all four datasets, and beats SurgVLP on three of four (losing only marginally on Cholec80: 25.0 vs. 33.3 mAP). In the supervised CholecT50 triplet-recognition task, RASO (57.5 overall mAP) beats Rendezvous (56.4) and Attention Tripnet (56.3), winning on Verb (+2.7) and Target (+2.2) while trailing slightly on Instrument (88.3 vs. 92.0). The paper notes RASO is fine-tuned for only 4 epochs versus Rendezvous's 200+ epochs, suggesting further gains are possible. For video recognition, the temporal-attention fusion layer outperforms naive average pooling by 6.0 mAP on Verb and 1.2 mAP overall, confirming that explicit temporal modeling is critical for surgical action recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

RASO is architecturally built on RAM (Recognize Anything Model), a general-domain image tagging foundation model, and adapts it to the surgical domain through a novel weakly-supervised data generation pipeline and a temporal-attention fusion layer for video inputs. The data pipeline departs from prior surgical VLM work (SurgVLP, LLaVA-Surg) by generating structured tag-image-text triplets from unannotated surgical lecture videos using NER, SceneGraphParser, and GPT-4o, rather than relying on unregulated image-text pairs. The temporal fusion layer is a new architectural component not present in RAM, specifically designed to capture surgical action dynamics across video frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RASO provides the first open-set surgical object recognition foundation model covering 2,066 tags, moving beyond the 11-class closed-set paradigm of EndoVis and the 29-class CholecT50. The weakly-supervised data pipeline (2,200 procedures → 3.6M annotations in under 8 hours on 8 GPUs) offers a broadly applicable recipe for any vertical domain with abundant video-with-voiceover data but scarce manual labels. Key open questions include whether RASO's recognition outputs can be directly coupled with SAM-style segmentation for grounded surgical scene understanding, and whether the moderate zero-shot performance (~22 mAP on CholecT50) can be closed with larger-scale pretraining or better tag alignment.
