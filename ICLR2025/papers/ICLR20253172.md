## Problem

Naively combining existing differential privacy (DP) techniques with fairness techniques in generative models creates a fundamental conflict: fairness methods amplify minority data usage (risking privacy breaches), while DP methods suppress individual sample influence (risking fairness cancellation). No prior framework simultaneously achieves rigorous DP guarantees, statistical fairness, and high utility for high-dimensional image data, with existing combined approaches limited to low-dimensional tabular data or suffering severe utility degradation.

## Input / Output

PFGuard takes a biased training dataset of images (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/FashionMNIST, 32×32 or 64×64 RGB for CelebA) and produces synthetic images via a GAN generator whose input is a random noise vector. The generated synthetic data is guaranteed to be both (ε,δ)-differentially private and balanced across demographic groups (measured by KL divergence to uniform, FID disparities, and downstream fairness metrics). Notably, the framework supports an extension where sensitive attribute labels are unavailable during training, requiring only 1–10% reference data for fairness estimation.

## Method

PFGuard decouples fairness and privacy into two stages connected by an ensemble of teacher models. In the fair training stage, each teacher is trained on a disjoint data partition using balanced minibatch sampling derived from Sampling-Importance Resampling (SIR), where the resampling probability is proportional to the likelihood ratio p_bal(x)/p_bias(x), reducing to the inverse group frequency under the paper's assumptions. In the private training stage, the generator is trained solely on privatized teacher outputs via Private Teacher Ensemble Learning (PTEL), where teacher votes (e.g., on gradients) are aggregated with Gaussian noise calibrated to the l₂-sensitivity of the voting scheme (√2 for GNMax). Because fair sampling preserves data disjointness, the original PTEL sensitivity analysis and DP guarantees remain intact without modification. The number of teachers is upper-bounded by ⌊|D|·min_s p_bias(s)⌋ to ensure each teacher probabilistically receives at least one minority sample, preventing fairness degradation from overly small partitions.

## Results

On MNIST with subgroup bias under ε=10, PFGuard (built on GS-WGAN) achieves KL divergence 0.067 and distribution disparity 0.242 for fairness, with overall FID of 83.67, compared to the DP-only baseline (KL 0.177, Dist. Disp. 0.383, FID 77.97). PFGuard maintains the same privacy budget (ε=10) while reducing group size disparity by ~62% (KL) and ~37% (Dist. Disp.) over the DP-only model. On CelebA with binary class bias under stricter ε=1, PFGuard reduces accuracy disparity from 0.978 to 0.736 (G-PATE) and from 0.793 to 0.725 (DataLens), while improving overall accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFGuard dominates all privacy-fairness baselines on the joint privacy-fairness-utility tradeoff. Compared to the naive reweighting combination, PFGuard maintains ε=10 (vs. ε=13, a privacy breach) while achieving comparable fairness (KL 0.067 vs. 0.009) at substantially better utility (FID 83.67 vs. 106.94). Against DP-SGD-F (Xu et al., 2020), PFGuard achieves the same ε=10 (vs. 11), dramatically better fairness (KL 0.067 vs. 0.659), and better utility (FID 83.67 vs. 90.20). Against DPSGD-GA (Esipova et al., 2022), PFGuard matches ε=10 but is far superior on fairness (KL 0.067 vs. 0.693) and utility (FID 83.67 vs. 127.02). Relative to the DP-only baseline (GS-WGAN), PFGuard trades a modest FID increase (77.97→83.67) for a 62% reduction in KL disparity and 37% reduction in distribution disparity, while also improving groupwise FID for the minority subgroup (Y=0,S=0: 101.39→72.24). In the unknown-subgroup-bias setting (no sensitive labels), PFGuard with only 10% reference data (perc=0.1) achieves KL=0.004 and FID=89.43 at ε=10, outperforming reweighting with the same reference data (KL=0.013, FID=106.94, ε=13).

## Relation to Prior Work

PFGuard builds on the Private Teacher Ensemble Learning (PTEL) paradigm (Papernot et al., 2016; 2018) used in DP generative models like GS-WGAN, G-PATE, and DataLens, and on fair generative modeling via reweighting (Choi et al., 2020). It departs from prior combined approaches (e.g., Xu et al., 2021, which pairs fair preprocessing with private generation) by identifying a fundamental privacy-fairness conflict—where reweighting amplifies gradients beyond DP-SGD's clipping threshold, or clipping undoes reweighting's balance—and resolving it architecturally: fairness is enforced at the teacher level via SIR-based balanced minibatch sampling, while privacy is enforced at the knowledge-transfer level via PTEL noise, decoupling the two objectives. The framework is loss-agnostic and plugs into any existing PTEL-based generator with only a minibatch-sampling modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to simultaneously provide DP guarantees and statistical fairness for high-dimensional image generation, a gap that prior work (limited to low-dimensional tabular data) left open. The identification of the privacy-fairness conflict as a first-class design constraint—rather than an afterthought—offers a reusable principle for integrating competing objectives in generative models. Limitations include the need for sensitive-attribute labels (or a reference dataset) for fair sampling, the computational cost of training multiple teachers, and the fact that CelebA results, while improved, still show substantial accuracy disparities under strong privacy (ε=1), suggesting the problem is not yet fully solved for realistic settings.
