## Problem

Existing scoring functions for logic optimization heuristics cannot simultaneously achieve fast CPU inference, interpretability, and strong generalization to unseen circuits. GNN-based approaches (e.g., COG) generalize well but are too slow for CPU-based EDA tools and are uninterpretable, while human-designed functions (e.g., Effisyn) are fast and interpretable but suffer from poor generalization. The core gap is the 'circuit symbolic generalization problem': lightweight symbolic functions learned from training data fail to transfer to unseen circuits, degrading optimization quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes 69-dimensional circuit node features (5 structural dimensions: fanin/fanout counts and node level; 64 semantic dimensions: the truth table of a 6-input LUT) and produces two concise one-line symbolic expressions — a continuous regression function over structural features and a Boolean function over semantic features. These are fused into a per-node score s_i = f^str(x_i^str) + w·f^sem(x_i^sem), where w is the median of structural outputs, and nodes are ranked by score with the top-k selected for transformation. The learned functions are compiled to lightweight shared objects for direct integration into CPU-based LO tools like ABC.

## Method

CMO introduces a Graph Enhanced Symbolic Discovery (GESD) framework that distills dark knowledge from a trained 2-layer GCNN teacher into a Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) student that explores the discrete symbolic operator space. The key mechanism is a combined evaluation loss L = λ·L_label(ŷ, y) + (1−λ)·L_teacher(ŷ, z) applied to every complete expression tree during MCTS simulation, where L_teacher uses MSE (rather than KL-divergence) to directly match the GNN's soft output, motivated by the observation that a simple MLP can approximate the GNN's input-to-output mapping with comparable generalization. Structural-Semantic Feature Decomposition (SFD) reduces the 69-dimensional input to 5 structural + 64 semantic features, shrinking the symbolic search space and enabling the structural function to be learned as continuous regression while the semantic function is formulated as Boolean symbolic learning with a recall-based loss. The MCTS reward r = η^n/(1−L) penalizes expression length to enforce conciseness, and the final scoring function is a compact, interpretable expression deployable on purely CPU-based machines.

## Results

On the offline prediction recall metric (top-k accuracy), CMO achieves comparable or superior recall to the GPU-based GNN (COG) across most circuits, e.g., 0.99 vs 0.99 on Hyp, 0.97 vs 0.97 on Multiplier, and 0.80 vs 0.74 on DesPerf, while significantly outperforming the human-designed Effisyn by an average of 36%. On the online efficiency metric, CMO-Mfs2 achieves up to 2.5× faster runtime than default Mfs2 on the very large-scale circuit Sixteen (~13 hours), with an average 44.07% runtime improvement and marginal node degradation. Inference time for CMO is 0.06 s on Hyp versus 28.28 s for COG and 0.15 s for Effisyn, demonstrating hundreds-of-times speedup over the GNN on CPU-based machines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against COG (the well-designed 2-layer GCNN from Wang et al., ICML 2024), CMO matches or slightly exceeds prediction recall on most circuits (e.g., 0.80 vs 0.74 on DesPerf, 0.72 vs 0.68 on Ethernet) while being hundreds of times faster in inference (0.06 s vs 28.28 s on Hyp), making it deployable on purely CPU-based LO tools. Against Effisyn (Li et al., ICCAD 2023), CMO achieves an average 36% improvement in prediction recall and is also faster in inference (0.06 s vs 0.15 s on Hyp), with the gap being especially dramatic on very large-scale circuits where Effisyn drops to 0.10 recall on Sixteen and Twenty. Against the SOTA symbolic regression baseline SPL (Sun et al., ICLR 2023), CMO achieves an average 24% improvement in prediction recall, demonstrating that graph distillation is critical for generalization. Against XGBoost (Chen & Guestrin, KDD 2016), CMO achieves an average 21% recall improvement. Against DSR (Petersen et al., ICLR 2020) and RidgeLR (Hoerl & Kennard, 1970), CMO also significantly outperforms in both generalization and efficiency. Overall, CMO wins on every comparison in terms of the joint objective of generalization, inference efficiency, and interpretability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) the prediction-and-prune framework for LO heuristics, where a GNN (COG, Wang et al. 2024) scores nodes to skip ineffective transformations, and (2) MCTS-based symbolic regression (SPL, Sun et al. 2023) for discovering interpretable mathematical expressions. The key departure is the Graph Enhanced Symbolic Discovery (GESD) framework, which uses a trained GNN as a teacher to guide MCTS symbolic tree search via MSE-based knowledge distillation, combined with a structural-semantic feature decomposition that reduces 69 input features to 5 structural variables. Unlike prior symbolic distillation work (Cranmer et al. 2020; Kuang et al. 2024) that distills layer-by-layer or from full graph inputs, CMO distills only the GNN's scalar output into a one-line symbolic function operating on node features alone, trading graph dependency for extreme inference speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for EDA practitioners: a one-line symbolic function deployed on CPU achieves 2.5× speedup on very large-scale circuits while matching GNN-level generalization, removing the GPU dependency that has limited adoption of ML-based LO scoring. The identification of the 'circuit symbolic generalization problem'—where standard symbolic regression methods fail to transfer across circuit domains—provides a clear benchmark for future work. Limitations include evaluation on only one LO heuristic (Mfs2), an anonymous industrial benchmark, and 69 circuits total; whether the structural-semantic decomposition and GNN distillation strategy generalize to other heuristics (Resub, Rewrite) or to pre-mapping AIG optimization remains open. The approach also assumes a fixed feature set and may not adapt to new circuit families without retraining the teacher GNN.
