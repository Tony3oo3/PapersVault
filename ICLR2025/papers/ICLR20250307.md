## Problem

Autoregressive image generation models (e.g., LlamaGen, AiM) have emerged as strong competitors to diffusion models but lack the fine-grained spatial controllability that methods like ControlNet provide for diffusion-based generation. The natural approach of prefilling control tokens into the AR sequence, inspired by LLM prompting, produces poor quality (26.45 FID vs. ControlNet's 10.85) and significantly increases training memory (+59.1%) and latency (+96.3%), leaving a clear gap in how to efficiently integrate spatial controls into autoregressive image generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a spatial control image (canny edge, depth map, segmentation mask, hed edge, or lineart edge) at any resolution, plus a text prompt or class label, and produces a generated image whose resolution is determined by the control image's dimensions. Unlike fixed-resolution AR models (e.g., LlamaGen locked to 256×256), the number of decoded image tokens directly matches the length of the control token sequence, so simply resizing the input control image changes the output resolution without any resolution-aware prompts or architectural modifications. For multi-resolution training, heights and widths are sampled from 384 to 1024 with a 16-pixel interval constraint.

## Method

ControlAR introduces a lightweight ViT-based control encoder (~22M parameters, initialized from ViT-S or DINOv2-S) that flattens the spatial control image into patches and encodes them into a sequence of control tokens. The core innovation is conditional decoding: rather than prepending control tokens to the sequence (conditional prefilling), each image token is fused with its positionally-corresponding control token via simple addition (after an MLP projection) at three evenly-spaced intermediate layers of the AR model, with a one-position displacement so the model predicts the next image token conditioned on the current control token. This avoids the quadratic sequence-length penalty of prefilling while fixing positional correspondence between control and image, yielding faster convergence and better FID/F1-Score. For arbitrary-resolution generation, a multi-resolution training recipe (MR-ControlAR) samples diverse aspect ratios during training, and the rotational position encoding's maximum sequence length is simply extended to 2304. Full fine-tuning of the AR backbone outperforms LoRA or parameter freezing for both conditional consistency and image quality.

## Results

On T2I controllable generation (512×512), ControlAR (LlamaGen-XL, 775M params) achieves 85.63 SSIM on hed edges, 37.08 F1-Score on canny edges, and 14.51 FID on COCOStuff segmentation, surpassing ControlNet++ on hed edges (+4.66 SSIM), COCOStuff segmentation (+2.93 mIoU, +4.78 FID), and depth (−2.05 RMSE). On C2I (ImageNet, 256×256), LlamaGen-L-based ControlAR achieves 7.69 FID on canny and 4.19 FID on depth, outperforming the 2B-parameter ControlVAR-d30 (7.85 and 6.50 FID respectively) with only 16.7% of the parameters. Against recent works, ControlAR (0.8B) outperforms Lumina-mGPT (7B) on all four control types and beats OmniGen (3.8B) on canny F1-Score and hed SSIM while remaining competitive on depth RMSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ControlNet++ (the strongest diffusion-based baseline), ControlAR wins on COCOStuff segmentation mIoU (37.49 vs 34.56), canny F1-Score (37.08 vs 37.04), hed edge SSIM (85.63 vs 80.97), and FID on COCOStuff (14.51 vs 19.29), hed (10.53 vs 15.01), lineart (12.41 vs 13.88), and depth (14.61 vs 16.66); it trails slightly on ADE20K segmentation mIoU (39.95 vs 43.64), lineart SSIM (79.22 vs 83.99), and depth RMSE (29.01 vs 28.32). Against ControlNet, ControlAR dominates on every reported metric across all six control types. Against the autoregressive baselines, ControlAR (LlamaGen-L, 343M) achieves lower FID than ControlVAR-d30 (2B) on both canny (7.69 vs 7.85) and depth (4.19 vs 6.50) C2I tasks. In the appendix comparison, ControlAR (0.8B) substantially outperforms Lumina-mGPT (7B) on all four T2I controls (e.g., depth RMSE 29.01 vs 55.25) and beats OmniGen (3.8B) on canny F1-Score (37.08 vs 35.54) and hed SSIM (85.63 vs 82.37), though OmniGen edges ahead on ADE20K mIoU (44.23 vs 39.95) and depth RMSE (28.54 vs 29.01).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the autoregressive image generation paradigm established by LlamaGen (Llama-based) and AiM (Mamba-based), which reformulate image synthesis as next-token prediction over VQGAN tokens. It departs from the ControlNet lineage of controllable diffusion generation, where spatial controls are injected via pixel-wise feature fusion in U-Net layers, by proposing a sequence-native alternative: a lightweight ViT-based control encoder that produces control tokens, fused with image tokens via simple addition at selected layers during decoding. The key conceptual departure is from the LLM-inspired 'conditional prefilling' strategy (analogous to prompt tokens) to 'conditional decoding,' where each image token is fused with its positionally-corresponding control token before next-token prediction, avoiding the quadratic sequence-length penalty of prefilling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As autoregressive models gain traction as alternatives to diffusion for image generation, controllability was a critical gap; ControlAR fills it with a lightweight, architecture-agnostic technique (simple token addition at a few layers) that requires no sequence-length increase and no structural modification to the base AR model. The emergent ability to generate arbitrary-resolution images by simply adjusting the control input size is a practical advantage over fixed-resolution AR models like LlamaGen. Open questions remain: the method requires full fine-tuning of the generative model (reducing portability compared to ControlNet's plug-and-play design), does not yet support simultaneous multi-control inputs, and exhibits a tension between text-prompt adherence and spatial-control fidelity when the two conflict.
