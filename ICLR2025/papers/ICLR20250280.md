## Problem

Unsupervised tabular anomaly detection methods require extensive feature engineering to handle mixed-type data and cannot process raw textual features without lossy transformations. While LLMs have shown promise in tabular prediction and generation, their applicability to anomaly detection—where no labels exist for in-context learning and token-level probabilities introduce length-bias—remains unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20250280/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method ingests raw tabular rows with mixed feature types (numerical, categorical, textual). Numerical values are standard-rescaled and rounded to single-digit decimals; categorical and textual values are used verbatim; missing values map to the token 'Unknown'. Each row is serialized into a natural-language template ('column_name is value, …') with randomly permuted column order. The output is a scalar anomaly score per sample: the mean negative log-likelihood over r=21 random column permutations, with per-column length normalization applied only to textual features to prevent longer texts from dominating the score.

## Method

AnoLLM fine-tunes a small pretrained LLM (SmolLM-135M/360M) via causal language modeling on serialized tabular rows, where each row is rendered as a sequence of 'column is value' pairs in a random column order. During training, a fresh random permutation is drawn at every gradient step so the model learns feature-order-invariant dependencies. At inference, anomaly scores are the negative log-likelihood of the serialized test row, averaged over r fixed permutations. A key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that for categorical and binned-numerical columns, the product of per-token probabilities equals the true category probability regardless of token length, so no normalization is needed; for textual columns, the NLL is divided by the column's token count to eliminate length bias. This design lets the LLM exploit its pretrained semantic and arithmetic knowledge to model inter-feature dependencies while remaining robust to the heterogeneous token lengths inherent in mixed-type tabular data.

## Results

On the six mixed-type tabular datasets, AnoLLM with SmolLM-360M achieves an average AUC-ROC of 0.810, outperforming the best baseline (SLAD, 0.746) by 6.4 percentage points. The advantage is most pronounced on datasets with textual features: AnoLLM scores 0.814 on Fake Job Posts and 0.752 on 20 Newsgroups, versus 0.629 and 0.546 for the next-best methods. On the 30 predominantly numerical ODDS datasets, AnoLLM-135M achieves an average AUC-ROC of 0.884, matching or slightly exceeding the top baselines KNN (0.879), DTE (0.879), and ICL (0.877).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnoLLM outperforms all 12 baselines (4 classical + 8 deep learning) on the six mixed-type datasets, with the largest margins on text-heavy data: 0.814 vs. 0.699 (ICL) on Fake Job Posts and 0.752 vs. 0.671 (ICL) on 20 Newsgroups. Among classical methods, KNN is the strongest baseline (avg. 0.727), while among deep learning methods, SLAD leads (avg. 0.746); AnoLLM-360M exceeds both by 6.4–8.3 points. On the 30 numerical ODDS datasets, where LLM advantages are diminished (98.5% numerical columns, only 10/30 with meaningful column names), AnoLLM-135M (0.884) achieves parity with the top-performing baselines KNN (0.879), DTE (0.879), and ICL (0.877), confirming robustness on purely numerical data. The paper notes that scaling from 135M to 1.7B parameters yields negligible gains (0.884 → 0.861 on ODDS), suggesting that small LLMs are sufficient for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnoLLM builds directly on the tabular data serialization paradigm established by TabLLM (Hegselmann et al., 2023) and Borisov et al. (2023), which showed that LLMs can process tabular data by converting rows into natural language sequences. However, it departs from prior tabular anomaly detection work (IForest, KNN, ECOD, DeepSVDD, ICL, DTE, etc.) by replacing numerical feature engineering and distance/density-based scoring with LLM fine-tuning and negative log-likelihood scoring. A key conceptual departure is the use of random column permutations during both training and inference to eliminate feature-order bias, combined with a theoretical justification (Theorem 1) for aggregating token-level probabilities as anomaly scores. Unlike the concurrent zero-shot approach of Li et al. (2024), which prompts LLMs per-dimension, AnoLLM fine-tunes the model to capture cross-feature dependencies through autoregressive generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnoLLM is the first framework to handle raw textual features in unsupervised tabular anomaly detection without any preprocessing, addressing a practical gap where existing methods require extensive feature engineering for mixed-type data (e.g., system logs, job postings). On purely numerical benchmarks (ODDS), it matches but does not surpass top baselines like KNN, ICL, and DTE, reflecting known LLM limitations in numerical reasoning. Open questions include reducing the substantial computational overhead of LLM inference (r=21 permutations per sample), developing specialized tokenization for numerical attributes, and whether the approach extends to contaminated or semi-supervised settings. The finding that 135M-parameter models perform nearly as well as 1.7B models suggests the task may not require large-scale pretraining, which is encouraging for practical deployment.
