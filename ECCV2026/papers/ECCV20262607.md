## Problem

Extending weight-only post-training quantization to multimodal LLMs at ultra-low bit-widths (2–3 bits) causes severe accuracy collapse because uniform-precision methods implicitly assume all output channels within a layer share identical quantization sensitivity. No prior work had formally established why the block-level loss landscape breaks this symmetry or how activation outliers multiplicatively amplify inter-channel sensitivity gaps, leaving existing mixed-precision approaches to rely on ad-hoc heuristics without a principled allocation criterion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained VLM weights (Qwen2-VL-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LLaVA-OneVision) together with 512 calibration samples (images from COCO paired with ShareGPT4V instruction prompts) to compute a per-output-channel bit map in a single pass. The output is a mixed-precision quantized model where each output channel is assigned either a base width b_ℓ or a promoted width b_h = b_ℓ + 1 (e.g., 99% 3-bit + 1% 4-bit yields a '3.01-bit' model), followed by a lightweight affine-smoothing optimization (15 epochs, lr 5×10⁻³) that is coupled with the fixed mixed-precision quantizer. The entire pipeline is calibration-only, finetuning-free, and hardware-compatible.

## Method

GloBitQ grounds its bit allocation in a two-level anisotropy analysis: at the inter-layer level, different Transformer layers exhibit different average sensitivities; at the intra-layer level, individual output channels within the same layer carry genuinely different block-level sensitivities because downstream attention, gating, and layer-norm propagation break the Kronecker symmetry present in layer-wise reconstruction. Activation outliers further amplify these gaps through an outlier-sensitivity resonance mechanism, where dominant activation eigenvectors align with channel-dependent propagation matrices. Practically, the framework replaces expensive Hessian-trace estimation with the O(d) per-channel mean absolute weight magnitude as an order-preserving proxy, then applies three components governed by a single hyperparameter α: (a) a concave power-law transform μ_l^α to produce normalized layer weights capturing inter-layer sensitivity, (b) a rank-value hybrid normalization (empirical CDF + min-max value) to produce cross-layer-comparable channel scores capturing intra-layer differences, and (c) a global top-τ threshold on the product score G_{l,i} = ω_l · S_{l,i} to assign promoted vs. base bit widths. A learned per-layer affine smoothing (inherited from FlatQuant) is then optimized jointly with the mixed-precision quantizer to reduce both weight and activation condition numbers, narrowing the gap between the theoretical allocation assumptions and the practical quantization setting.

## Results

At 3-bit precision, GloBitQ achieves an average score of 81.70 on Qwen2-VL-7B (vs. 83.76 full precision, a 2.1% drop), 78.3 on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (vs. 82.84 full precision), and 76.66 on LLaVA-OneVision (vs. 77.69 full precision), consistently outperforming GPTQ, GPTAQ, and VLMQ by 4.5–17.7% across all models. At 2-bit precision, where per-channel GPTQ produces NaN (model collapse), GloBitQ maintains stable performance at 66.6 (Qwen2-VL-7B), 66.4 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), and 60.8 (LLaVA-OneVision), surpassing the best group-128 baselines (VLMQ) by 2.1–2.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GloBitQ decisively outperforms all three primary baselines (GPTQ, GPTAQ, VLMQ) at 3-bit across all three VLMs. On Qwen2-VL-7B, the margin is largest: GloBitQ scores 81.70 vs. GPTQ's 63.98 (+17.7%), GPTAQ's 63.91 (+16.9%), and VLMQ's 64.74 (+16.9%). On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, GloBitQ (78.3) leads VLMQ (77.49) by 0.8% and GPTQ (75.63) by 2.7%, though it underperforms on ScienceQA (59.34 vs. VLMQ's 81.70), suggesting a task-specific weakness. On LLaVA-OneVision, GloBitQ (76.66) beats GPTQ (71.07) by 5.6% and VLMQ (72.17) by 4.5%. At 2-bit, per-channel GPTQ collapses entirely (NaN), while GloBitQ remains stable and surpasses the best group-128 baseline VLMQ by 2.1–2.6% on average. In the ablation (Table 3), GloBitQ at 2.01-bit also outperforms FlatQuant (per-channel, 2-bit) by 15.5% on average, confirming that the mixed-precision allocation—not just affine smoothing—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GPTQ layer-wise quantization solver and FlatQuant's affine smoothing into a unified mixed-precision framework, but its core conceptual departure is shifting the sensitivity analysis from the layer level to the Transformer block level. Prior mixed-precision methods (HAWQ, LLM-MQ, SqueezeLLM, SliM-LLM) operate at layer granularity or rely on heuristic channel scoring, while prior VLM quantization work (Q-VLM, MBQ, MQuant, VLMQ) uses static precision assignments without a global cross-layer allocation principle. GloBitQ's novelty is providing the first formal proof that block-level error propagation breaks the Kronecker symmetry of layer-wise analysis, yielding genuinely distinct per-output-channel sensitivities amplified by an outlier-sensitivity resonance mechanism—thereby grounding per-channel bit allocation in theory rather than ad-hoc proxies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-level anisotropy theory and outlier-sensitivity resonance mechanism give the field a principled lens for understanding why uniform-precision quantization fails at 2–3 bits, and the resulting single-hyperparameter allocation rule is immediately deployable in a calibration-only, training-free pipeline. The 2-bit stability result (where per-channel GPTQ produces NaN) is practically significant for edge deployment of VLMs. Open questions include whether the weight-magnitude proxy (Spearman > 0.8) generalizes to architectures with very different weight distributions, whether the single α truly suffices versus independent layer/channel tuning, and whether the block-level Hessian analysis extends cleanly to MoE or hybrid attention architectures. The 3-bit gains over baselines are large but partly reflect that GPTQ/GPTAQ are not optimized for 3-bit per-channel operation, so the true competitive margin may be narrower than the headline numbers suggest.
