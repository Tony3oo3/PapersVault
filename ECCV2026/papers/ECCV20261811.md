## Problem

Existing 3DGS compression methods tightly integrate compaction, encoding, and adaptation into monolithic pipelines, obscuring where rate-distortion gains originate and preventing component reuse across reconstruction backends. The paper asks whether a deliberately simple, image-based encoding format with a modular pipeline can match or surpass tightly integrated methods like HAC++ on standard real-world benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261811/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts any standard 3DGS .ply file regardless of which implementation produced it (INRIA, gsplat, Lichtfeld Studio, etc.), preserving the vanilla parameterization of means, quaternion/scale covariances, degree-3 spherical harmonics, and scalar opacity. The output is a SOG-XT container: per-attribute 2D grids stored as 8-bit WebP images (positions as two 8-bit byte planes, SH DC as one 8-bit image, SH AC as a PLAS-sorted k-means centroid grid plus two 8-bit UV index channels, scales/opacities as 8-bit images), with a small YAML metadata file holding per-channel min-max ranges. Decoding requires only standard image decompression plus deterministic rescaling and reshaping, with no custom binary format or learned decoder.

## Method

KISS-GS is a three-stage modular pipeline. Stage 1 (POPSpa compaction) combines GaussianPOP's per-primitive rendering-error pruning with GaussianSpa's alternating optimize-sparsify scheme and an effective-rank regularization that penalizes needle-shaped Gaussians, reducing primitive counts by ~15.7×. Stage 2 (SOG-XT encoding) arranges primitives into a 2D grid via PLAS and introduces two contributions: (i) self-organizing 2D codebooks where k-means SH centroids are themselves PLAS-sorted into a spatially smooth grid and referenced by two 8-bit UV channels, and (ii) PRAS, which exploits the 48-fold equivalence class of (quaternion, scale) pairs mapping to the same covariance matrix to select, at each coarse-to-fine scale, the representative minimizing L1 distance to a low-pass-filtered target, thereby removing artificial variance from quaternion and scale grids without altering the underlying 3D geometry. Stage 3 (optional encoding-aware fine-tuning) backpropagates rendering loss through the full quantize–decode–render round trip with a straight-through estimator, refining centroid values and re-running PRAS while keeping the discrete codec structure fixed. The decoder remains a simple, deterministic image-decode-and-rescale operation throughout.

## Results

KISS-GS achieves total file-size reductions of 85× to 319× over uncompressed INRIA 3DGS (40k iterations) across four standard benchmarks. On Tanks and Temples, KISS-GS + encoding-aware fine-tuning reaches 319× reduction in PSNR (vs. 77× for HAC++), and on Mip-NeRF 360 it reaches 228× (vs. 70× for HAC++). The compaction stage (POPSpa) alone yields a 15.7× reduction, SOG-XT encoding adds 6.6×, and optional fine-tuning adds a further 2.2×. On Deep Blending, HAC++ (156×) and HEMGS (229×, self-reported) outperform KISS-GS (85×), which the authors attribute to anchor-based learned representations better absorbing capture inconsistencies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KISS-GS + FT decisively outperforms HAC++ (the strongest reproducible baseline) on both primary real-world datasets: 319× vs. 77× on Tanks and Temples (PSNR) and 228× vs. 70× on Mip-NeRF 360 (PSNR), representing roughly 4× and 3× higher compression ratios at matched quality. On Deep Blending, HAC++ (156×) and the self-reported HEMGS (229×) both exceed KISS-GS (85×), which the authors trace to HAC++'s anchor-based learned representation rather than to SOG-XT encoding or POPSpa pruning. In the compaction comparison (Table 1), POPSpa achieves the best average rank (1.7) among compaction-aware methods while operating at significantly fewer Gaussians than competitors, and in post-training pruning it consistently outperforms Speedy-Splat and PUP 3D-GS across all four datasets. On Synthetic NeRF, KISS-GS + FT achieves 21×/14×/14× (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS), surpassing HAC (47× PSNR only, no SSIM/LPIPS reported) and all other methods that report finite entries. Notably, many competing methods fail to reach INRIA-Q on the LPIPS perceptual metric at any operating point, suggesting overfitting to PSNR on training views.

## Relation to Prior Work

KISS-GS extends the Self-Organizing Gaussians (SOG) line of work by replacing its raw 48-channel SH storage with a PLAS-sorted 2D k-means codebook and introducing PRAS, which exploits the 48-fold symmetry of the quaternion/scale covariance parameterization to produce smoother attribute grids. For compaction, it repurposes GaussianPOP's per-primitive error criterion and GaussianSpa's optimize-sparsify scheme as a post-training recipe (POPSpa), deliberately decoupling primitive reduction from the reconstruction pipeline. The key departure from tightly integrated methods like HAC++ is the explicit separation of compaction, encoding, and adaptation into independently replaceable stages, with the decoder restricted to standard WebP image decoding rather than a learned or custom binary format.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a modular, image-codec-based pipeline can surpass the strongest integrated baseline (HAC++) on real-world benchmarks while keeping the decoder to a few lines of standard image decoding, KISS-GS lowers the barrier for deploying compressed 3DGS in web, mobile, and AR/VR contexts. The modular design means future advances in reconstruction, pruning, or vector quantization can be slotted in without reworking the entire codec. The main limitation is Deep Blending, where anchor-based learned representations still absorb capture inconsistencies better than standard-splat inputs; whether the modular approach closes that gap as reconstruction methods improve remains open.
