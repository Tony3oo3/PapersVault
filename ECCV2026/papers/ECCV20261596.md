## Problem

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) suffer from a granularity mismatch: sparse text prompts provide only abstract semantic constraints while visual tokens encode dense spatial structure, making text-to-image reconstruction supervision inherently ambiguous. Existing training paradigms compound this by using random masking that wastes reconstruction loss on semantically irrelevant regions, and image-conditioned prompting (e.g., Reca) that conditions on all visual tokens, introducing supervisory redundancy that dilutes attention and weakens cross-modal alignment.

## Input / Output

During training, SeGroS consumes a text-image pair and internally constructs a Visual Grounding Map to derive two signals: a set of Visual Hints (top-scoring image patches, ~30–40% of tokens) appended as additional conditioning alongside the text prompt, and a semantically grounded Corrupted Input where low-groundedness patches remain visible and high-groundedness patches are masked for reconstruction. At inference, the grounding map generation is entirely omitted, so the UMM generates images from text prompts alone (256×256 or 512×512) without any architectural modification, and also performs image-to-text understanding by taking an image plus question and autoregressively producing a text answer.

## Method

SeGroS is a fine-tuning framework built on three steps. First, Discriminative Text Token Filtering identifies visually salient text tokens by combining intra-modal self-attention centrality (text-text affinity) with inter-modal relevance (text-image cross-attention), retaining only the top-ρ fraction of tokens that dominate scene semantics. Second, a Visual Grounding Map is computed by aggregating text-to-image attention probabilities weighted by the filtering mask, yielding a per-patch groundedness score that is normalized and perturbed with uniform noise to avoid deterministic overfitting. Third, the grounding map drives two complementary supervision signals: the top-η patches become Visual Hints providing explicit visual conditioning, while the bottom patches are retained as visible context in the Corrupted Input, forcing the reconstruction loss to concentrate on the masked, text-aligned core regions. This replaces both the indiscriminate random masking of standard UMM training and the full-image conditioning of Reca, concentrating model capacity on semantically coherent structures rather than incidental details.

## Results

SeGroS consistently outperforms both SFT and Reca across six UMM backbones (0.5B–3.6B parameters) on GenEval, DPGBench, and CompBench. On Harmon-0.5B (MidjourneyV6), SeGroS achieves 81.5% GenEval overall, 85.4% DPGBench, and 86.9% CompBench, surpassing Reca (78.7/84.67/85.7) and SFT (72.1/82.5/83.5). The largest gains appear in composition-sensitive GenEval sub-metrics: Position accuracy jumps from 32.2% (SFT) to 66.0% (SeGroS) on Harmon-0.5B, and from 25.0% to 46.8% on OpenUni-3.6B. On the larger Harmon-1.5B fine-tuned with BLIP3o-60k, SeGroS reaches 88.66% GenEval and 88.08% CompBench, the highest scores reported in the paper.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Reca [46], which conditions reconstruction on the full image as a visual hint with random masking. SeGroS wins on DPGBench in all six backbone/dataset settings (e.g., 85.4 vs. 84.67 on Harmon-0.5B; 86.58 vs. 86.5 on Harmon-1.5B) and on GenEval overall in five of six settings, with the sole exception being Show-o-512 where Reca edges ahead by 0.94 points (71.63 vs. 70.69) while SeGroS still leads on DPGBench. The most substantial margin over Reca appears on Harmon-0.5B, where SeGroS gains +2.8 points on GenEval (81.5 vs. 78.7) and +1.2 on CompBench (86.9 vs. 85.7), driven largely by Position (66.0 vs. 58.5) and Attribute (75.8 vs. 70.7) sub-scores. On the larger Harmon-1.5B, SeGroS improves GenEval by +3.46 over Reca (88.66 vs. 85.2) and CompBench by +0.88 (88.08 vs. 87.2). For visual understanding (Table 2), SeGroS also outperforms Reca on MME (1217 vs. 1190), POPE-F1 (84.2 vs. 83.4), MMMU (36.0 vs. 34.9), and SEED (65.5 vs. 65.1), confirming that semantically grounded supervision strengthens both generation and comprehension without trade-offs.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeGroS is a fine-tuning framework that restructures the training signal of existing masked/denoising UMMs (Show-o, Harmon, OpenUni) rather than proposing a new architecture. It directly extends Reca's image-conditioned reconstruction by making visual hints semantically selective (top-grounded patches only) and replaces the random masking used in all prior UMMs with a grounding-map-guided scheme that masks core text-aligned regions while retaining low-salience patches as visible context. The discriminative text token filtering step draws on attention-based salience ideas from masked image modeling literature but applies them to cross-modal text-image alignment for the first time in a UMM fine-tuning setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a concrete inefficiency in UMM training—random masking and full-image visual hints waste supervision on text-irrelevant regions—and offers a lightweight, architecture-agnostic fix that improves generation fidelity across three UMM families without modifying inference. The drop-loss ablation (restricting reconstruction loss to only the top-30% grounded targets) suggests that *where* supervision is allocated matters more than *how much*, a principle that could inform future UMM training recipes. Open questions include whether the grounding-based masking strategy transfers to higher resolutions (beyond 512×512), video generation, or settings where text prompts are extremely short or absent, and whether the hand-crafted affinity computation could be replaced by a learned grounding module.
