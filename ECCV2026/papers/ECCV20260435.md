## Problem

No single pathology foundation model consistently outperforms others across all WSI analysis tasks, and while simple ensembles of FMs yield gains, existing multi-FM fusion methods (naive concatenation, patch-level selection, attention-based ensembling) operate on raw correlated features without explicitly separating shared semantic content from view-specific complementary signals. This allows dominant shared components to overshadow subtle FM-specific information, leaving cross-view complementarity underexploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260435/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes patch-level feature vectors from four frozen FMs (UNI, CONCH, H-Optimus-1, Virchow2) extracted from the same WSI patches, where each view may have a different feature dimensionality. A stochastic view dropout randomly masks entire views during training to improve robustness to missing FMs at inference. The output is a single slide-level prediction obtained by averaging logits from V+2=6 independent MIL branches (one shared, four view-specific, one concatenated), supporting both classification (AUC) and survival (C-index) tasks.

## Method

MV-MIL reformulates FM ensembling as a Multi-View Learning problem, treating each FM's patch features as a distinct view. An Information-Theoretic Disentanglement Module decomposes the V views into a shared component Zc (learned via a Gács-Körner-inspired consensus encoder followed by a variational bottleneck with an IB objective) and V view-specific components Zs^(v) (each optimized with its own IB objective plus covariance-based orthogonality and disentanglement penalties to suppress cross-view leakage). A Multi-Branch Prediction Head then instantiates V+2 independent MIL branches—each with its own aggregator (ABMIL, DSMIL, CLAM, etc.) and classifier—operating on the shared, each specific, and the fully concatenated representation, with final predictions obtained by unweighted logit averaging. The total training loss combines the task loss (cross-entropy or NLL survival) with weighted consistency and specificity disentanglement losses, making the framework plug-and-play across arbitrary MIL backbones and FM combinations.

## Results

On molecular prediction (8 datasets), MV-MIL achieves an average AUC of 0.8008, outperforming the best single-FM baseline (H-Optimus-1, 0.7821) by +1.87 points and the strongest multi-FM baseline (Naive Ensemble, 0.7811) by +1.97 points. On survival analysis (6 TCGA cohorts), MV-MIL attains an average C-index of 0.6905, exceeding the best single-FM (Virchow2, 0.6691) by +2.14 points and the best multi-FM baseline (Naive Concat, 0.6748) by +1.57 points. The method achieves the highest score on all 14 individual benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MV-MIL outperforms all seven baselines on every one of the 14 benchmarks. Against single-FM baselines, the largest gains appear on mutation-prediction tasks: CRC-KRAS improves from 0.6542 (Virchow2, best single FM) to 0.8450, and SKCM-BRAF from 0.6255 to 0.6385. Among multi-FM baselines, Naive Ensemble (logit averaging) is the strongest on molecular prediction (avg AUC 0.7811) but still trails MV-MIL by 1.97 points; Naive Concat is the strongest on survival analysis (avg C-index 0.6748) yet is surpassed by 1.57 points. Self-Attn (Meta-Encoder [11]) underperforms both Naive Concat and Naive Ensemble on survival (0.6588 vs. 0.6748/0.6686), suggesting that simple self-attention over concatenated features is insufficient for capturing cross-FM complementarity in prognostic settings. The classic ResNet50 + ABMIL baseline lags all FM-based methods by a wide margin (avg AUC 0.6571, avg C-index 0.6007), confirming that foundation-model features are essential and that the multi-view disentanglement layer adds meaningful value on top of them.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby) and its deep variational instantiation (Alemi) to design an explicit disentanglement objective, and draws on Gács-Körner common information theory to formalize what 'shared' means across views. It departs from prior multi-FM fusion work (Naive Concat, Self-Attn/Meta-Encoder, AdaFusion, COBRA) by reframing FM ensembling as a structured Multi-View Learning problem rather than a patch-level selection or concatenation task, and from classical multi-view IB formulations by embedding the disentanglement inside a MIL pipeline with a multi-branch prediction head. The plug-and-play design connects to the broader trend of treating FMs as interchangeable feature extractors in WSI analysis, but adds an information-theoretic layer that prior fusion methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play MV-MIL module offers a practical, principled way to combine the growing zoo of pathology FMs without retraining them, which is timely as no single FM dominates across all cancer types and tasks. The ablation finding that view-specific branches alone already match or exceed the best single FM suggests that much of the discriminative signal in WSI analysis is FM-specific rather than shared, a useful insight for future ensemble design. Open questions include whether the covariance-based mutual information surrogate scales well beyond four views, whether the disentanglement quality degrades with highly correlated FMs, and whether the framework extends to other weakly-supervised settings beyond MIL (e.g., segmentation or detection).
