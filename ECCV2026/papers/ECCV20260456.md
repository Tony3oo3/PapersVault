## Problem

Existing video quality benchmarks are restricted to short clips with controlled, isolated distortions, failing to capture the temporal continuity, cumulative degradation, and multi-event reasoning inherent in long-duration content. No unified benchmark exists that jointly evaluates perceptual fidelity, temporal coherence, and hierarchical reasoning depth for long-term video quality understanding in LVLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260456/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a long-duration video (ranging from a few minutes to ~2 hours) with a single text question in either multiple-choice (4 options) or open-ended format. The video is consumed as a chronologically ordered sequence of uniformly sampled frames at a fixed FPS, with the prompt specifying total duration, sampling rate, and frame count. Outputs are scored as per-level and per-dimension accuracy for MCQs, and as GPT-based relevance and completeness percentages for open-ended answers, aggregated across the three hierarchical levels (LQU, CQR, GQU).

## Method

LongVQUBench introduces a three-level hierarchical evaluation framework—Local Event Quality Understanding (LQU), Cross-Event Quality Reasoning (CQR), and Global Quality Understanding (GQU)—each probing five complementary question dimensions (e.g., detection, localization, comparison, cumulative effect, stability evaluation). A Needle Distortion Question-Answering (NDQA) paradigm sparsely embeds 14 spatial and 4 temporal distortion types at three controlled intensity levels into high-quality source videos, enabling fine-grained perceptual sensitivity testing without disrupting semantic content. The benchmark comprises 1,200 videos sourced from LongVideoBench, MLVU, and LongVideoReason, filtered and tagged to achieve balanced duration and quality distributions, with 1,500 manually validated QA pairs. Evaluation is conducted zero-shot on 14 LVLMs (proprietary, open-source, and agentic) using a single-pass frame-sampling inference pipeline, with a stratified 40/60 validation/test split to select optimal frame budgets before final scoring.

## Results

On the LongVQUBench test set (60% held-out), GPT-5 achieves the highest overall MCQ accuracy of 69.5%, followed by Gemini-3 (66.8%) and Qwen-VL-Max (64.0%). Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL leads at 60.3%, while the best agentic system, DeepVideoDiscovery, reaches 66.0%—approaching proprietary model performance. A consistent pattern emerges across all 14 models: GQU (global quality understanding) scores are substantially lower than LQU and CQR (e.g., GPT-5: 69.5 LQU vs. 61.5 GQU), and open-ended completeness scores remain below 46% even for the best model (GPT-5: 45.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5 leads all 14 models on overall MCQ accuracy (69.5%), outperforming Gemini-3 (66.8%) by 2.7 points and Qwen-VL-Max (64.0%) by 5.5 points. Among open-source video LVLMs, Qwen3-VL (60.3%) and Long-RL (59.9%) are the strongest, both surpassing the video-quality-specific model VQA² (54.2%) by ~5–6 points, suggesting that general-purpose LVLMs currently outperform task-specific VQA models on long-form quality reasoning. The agentic system DeepVideoDiscovery (66.0%) nearly matches Gemini-3 and exceeds all open-source models, while simpler agents (VideoAgent 35.2%, LongVT 45.5%) lag significantly. Across every model, GQU accuracy drops 8–15 points below CQR, confirming that holistic long-duration quality synthesis remains the hardest sub-task. Long-RL achieves the best CQR integration score (93.3%), indicating that RL-trained temporal aggregation can excel at multi-event reasoning even when overall accuracy is moderate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: classical video quality assessment (VQA), which quantifies perceptual fidelity on short clips with controlled distortions (LIVE-VQA, KoNViD-1k, YouTube-UGC), and long-video semantic understanding benchmarks (LongVideoBench, MLVU, Video-MME), which evaluate event comprehension and narrative reasoning but ignore perceptual quality. Q-Bench-Video is the closest prior work, exploring video-quality reasoning via LVLMs, but it is restricted to ~10-second clips without hierarchical temporal assessment. LongVQUBench carves new ground by repurposing the controlled-distortion methodology of classical VQA and embedding it within a hierarchical temporal reasoning framework (LQU → CQR → GQU) over minute-to-hour videos, combined with the novel NDQA paradigm for sparse artifact probing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest proprietary LVLMs (GPT-5, Gemini-3) show a consistent and substantial performance drop from cross-event reasoning to global quality understanding, and that simply increasing the number of sampled frames yields diminishing or negative returns—challenging the assumption that better temporal coverage automatically improves perceptual reasoning. Open-ended completeness scores remain very low (≤45.8%) even when relevance is high (≥88%), indicating that current models can address a quality question but cannot produce comprehensive, multi-faceted explanations. This establishes a clear target for future work on long-range temporal integration, cumulative degradation modeling, and explainable quality attribution in LVLMs, and provides the first systematic, hierarchical testbed for tracking progress in this direction.
