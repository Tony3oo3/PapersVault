## Problem

Online skeleton-based action recognition requires per-frame classification using only past observations, but existing methods suffer from two limitations: causal attention dilutes weights uniformly as history grows, failing to focus on the few discriminative moments whose location varies across action categories, and explicit future prediction (e.g., Neural ODE in InfoGCN++) degenerates into trivial identity mapping due to inherent ambiguity in human motion continuations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260713/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a skeleton sequence of 3D joint coordinates shaped C_in × T × V × M (frames × joints × persons), uniformly resampled to T=64 frames. At each frame t it outputs a single action class label using only frames 1 through t under a strict causal constraint, producing a full sequence of T predictions from one forward pass. The model is trained on the full sequence but evaluated at every observation ratio from 10% to 100%, reporting AUC across all stages.

## Method

DART replaces fixed-dilation causal convolutions with a Deformable Multi-scale Temporal Network (DMTN) that predicts per-joint, per-kernel-step temporal offsets from the current frame's spatial features concatenated with sinusoidal positional encoding; offsets are zero-initialized and clamped to the valid historical range [0, t] to preserve causality, with fractional positions resolved via linear interpolation. Across B multi-scale branches (dilation rates δ=1, 2) and K=5 kernel steps, the learned sampling positions form a compact temporal memory of N_m = B×K tokens. A Temporal Query Decoder (TQD) then applies N_q=10 learnable queries through N_dec=4 Transformer decoder layers (self-attention → cross-attention over the memory → FFN) to distill high-level action evolution patterns without any explicit future forecasting. The distilled query outputs are projected back and added to the per-frame representation before a linear classifier, and training uses a label-smoothed cross-entropy main loss plus an auxiliary per-query classification loss (λ=0.5) to encourage each query to independently capture discriminative temporal cues.

## Results

On NTU RGB+D 60, DART achieves 73.99% AUC (Cross-Subject) and 81.86% AUC (Cross-View), surpassing the previous SOTA InfoGCN++ by +3.04% and +1.73% respectively. On the larger NTU RGB+D 120, DART reaches 64.66% AUC (Cross-Subject) and 67.40% AUC (Cross-Setup), improving over InfoGCN++ by +4.03% and +2.00%. On NW-UCLA, DART attains 86.34% AUC (+0.87% over InfoGCN++). DART also reduces FLOPs by 54% (4.59G vs. 10.00G) while using fewer parameters (2.35M vs. 2.50M) compared to InfoGCN++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DART outperforms all seven baselines on AUC across every benchmark. Against the primary SOTA InfoGCN++ [8], DART gains +3.04% AUC on NTU 60 X-Sub, +1.73% on NTU 60 X-View, +4.03% on NTU 120 X-Sub, +2.00% on NTU 120 X-Set, and +0.87% on NW-UCLA, with the largest margins at early-to-mid observation stages (20%–50%) where fixed sampling is most wasteful. Among offline methods, STST [37] is the strongest non-online baseline (57.92% AUC on NTU 60 X-Sub) and even exceeds DART at the 100% observation ratio on some splits (e.g., 91.90% vs. 87.63% on NTU 60 X-Sub), reflecting its optimization for full-sequence recognition only. The remaining baselines—ST-GCN [34], 2S-AGCN [25], CTR-GCN [6], InfoGCN [7], and MixFormer [33]—all trail DART by 16–24 AUC points on NTU 60 X-Sub, confirming that the online setting with adaptive temporal modeling and query-based reasoning yields substantially better streaming performance than fixed-pattern offline architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

DART directly builds on InfoGCN++ (Chi et al., TPAMI 2025), the first method to formalize online skeleton-based action recognition with causal attention and Neural ODE-based future prediction, and on InfoGCN (Chi et al., CVPR 2022) for its SA-GC spatial encoder. The key conceptual departure is twofold: it replaces fixed-pattern causal temporal convolutions with deformable offsets (extending Dai et al.'s spatial deformable convolution to the temporal axis), and it substitutes explicit future feature prediction with a DETR-style query decoder that distills motion trends from an adaptively constructed temporal memory. This positions DART as a targeted redesign of the two core temporal components of the prior SOTA while retaining the overall causal streaming framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that Neural ODE-based future prediction collapses into near-identity copying under motion ambiguity provides a concrete, well-evidenced argument against a widely adopted auxiliary objective in online recognition, potentially redirecting future work toward implicit reasoning mechanisms. The 54% FLOPs reduction alongside a 3.04% AUC gain makes the approach practically attractive for real-time deployment in human-robot interaction and streaming applications. Open questions include whether the deformable-offset + query-decoder paradigm transfers to other modalities (RGB, depth), whether it scales to much longer sequences or open-vocabulary settings, and whether the learned sampling positions can be made more interpretable for safety-critical applications.
