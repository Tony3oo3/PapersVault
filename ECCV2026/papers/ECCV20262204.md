## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting degrades severely in aerial sparse-view settings because repetitive textures (flat rooftops, uniform roads) cause SfM pipelines like COLMAP to produce incomplete point clouds, and feed-forward methods fail to estimate reliable poses and geometry from homogeneous aerial imagery. This collapse of geometric initialization strips both optimization-based and feed-forward sparse-view 3DGS methods of the structural scaffold they depend on, leading to floaters, incomplete geometry, and poor rendering in under-observed regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262204/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a very small set of aerial images (3 nadir views on LEVIR-NVS, 7 oblique views on 3D-AS) along with their camera intrinsics and extrinsics from COLMAP. It produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation where Gaussians from observed regions are standard learnable primitives, while Gaussians covering unobserved regions are parameterized as anchor-plus-residual pairs tied to their nearest reliable Gaussian. The pipeline also internally uses monocular depth maps (Depth Anything V2) and diffusion-based inpainting (Flux.1 Kontext) as intermediate geometric and appearance priors during initialization, but these are not part of the training-time input.

## Method

GeoProp-GS introduces two components. First, Depth-guided Geometric Initialization (DGI) has two stages: Hierarchical Depth-guided Densification (HDD) aligns monocular depth predictions to metric scale via a two-level correction (global scale/shift least-squares against sparse COLMAP depth, then local distance-weighted residual interpolation) to produce dense, metric-consistent point clouds replacing incomplete SfM output; Geometry-aware Viewpoint Augmentation (GVA) projects the dense cloud into pseudo-poses, inpaints missing regions with a diffusion model, re-estimates depth on the inpainted image, and lifts new proposal points to extend coverage into unobserved areas. Second, Anchor-constrained Gaussian Optimization (AGO) decomposes each proposal Gaussian's attributes (position, SH coefficients, scale, rotation, opacity) into the values of its nearest reliable anchor Gaussian plus learnable residuals, so that under-supervised proposals inherit geometric and appearance priors architecturally without additional regularization losses, while a DC-color statistical regularizer further suppresses color drift in weakly supervised regions.

## Results

On LEVIR-NVS (3 training views), GeoProp-GS achieves a mean PSNR of 24.35, SSIM of 0.83, and LPIPS of 0.15, outperforming the second-best method MPNeRF (21.72 PSNR) by 2.63 dB and vanilla 3DGS (20.20 PSNR) by 4.15 dB. On 3D-AS (7 training views), the method similarly leads across City, Country, and Port scenes, with PSNR values ranging from 15.29 to 26.49 depending on scene complexity. The LPIPS improvement is particularly notable: 0.15 vs. 0.23 for 3DGS and 0.27 for FSGS, indicating substantially better perceptual quality in under-observed regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoProp-GS outperforms all 14 baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the closest aerial-specific competitor MPNeRF [7], it gains +2.63 PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.19 to 0.15, indicating better detail preservation in complex regions. Compared to the strongest sparse-view 3DGS baselines (FSGS: 20.93 PSNR, DropoutGS: 19.31 PSNR), the improvement is +3.42 and +5.04 PSNR respectively, with LPIPS dropping from 0.27/0.43 to 0.15. Feed-forward methods (NoPoSplat ft: 19.75, MVSplat ft: 16.73) lag by 4.6–7.6 PSNR, confirming that direct regression from image features fails under repetitive aerial textures. NeRF-based methods (DietNeRF, RegNeRF, FreeNeRF, PixelNeRF) are all 8–11 dB below, reflecting the fundamental difficulty of implicit representations under extreme sparsity. In the initialization comparison (Table 4), HDD (21.57 PSNR) surpasses VGGT (16.43) by 5.14 dB and DUSt3R (18.65) by 2.92 dB, validating that depth-guided densification avoids the feature-matching collapse that limits SfM-based and learning-based pose estimation in aerial scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) and FSGS (Zhu et al., 2024), adopting their optimization framework while replacing the standard COLMAP-based initialization with a depth-guided densification pipeline. It departs from the dominant sparse-view 3DGS paradigm—which relies on SfM point clouds as geometric scaffolds or on feed-forward pose/geometry estimation (MVSplat, NoPoSplat, VGGT, DUSt3R)—by arguing that the fundamental bottleneck in aerial scenes is the collapse of feature matching, not the optimization strategy. The anchor-residual parameterization for under-supervised regions is a novel architectural regularization that avoids the loss-based geometric constraints used by methods like DNGaussian or CoherentGS, instead embedding geometric priors directly in the representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: aerial and satellite imaging inherently produces sparse, repetitive-texture viewpoints where standard 3DGS pipelines fail, yet these scenes are critical for infrastructure inspection and autonomous navigation. The plug-and-play nature of HDD (improving both vanilla 3DGS and FSGS without architectural changes) makes it immediately adoptable by practitioners working on aerial reconstruction. Open questions include scalability to very large-scale scenes, the reliability of diffusion-based inpainting for geometric proposals in GVA (hallucinated textures may propagate errors), and whether the anchor-residual mechanism generalizes to non-aerial sparse-view settings with different failure modes.
