## Problem

3D multi-contrast MRI synthesis requires anatomical coherence across axial, sagittal, and coronal planes, but fully volumetric 3D generative models scale cubically with volume size, making them computationally impractical for multi-contrast translation. Meanwhile, 2D slice-based methods lack explicit mechanisms to enforce volumetric consistency, producing inter-plane anatomical distortions. The paper addresses this gap by achieving full-volume 3D synthesis using only 2D diffusion operations while preserving global anatomical coherence.

## Input / Output

The model takes a source-contrast 3D MRI volume (e.g., T1-weighted) and a text prompt specifying the target modality (e.g., 'T2-weighted MR image'), which is encoded via BioMedCLIP into a semantic embedding. The volume is compressed into a 32×32×32×3 isotropic latent space by a 3D autoencoder before any 2D processing occurs. A single unified model handles all six translation tasks (T1↔T2, T1↔PD, T2↔PD) without per-pair retraining, and the output is a full-resolution target-contrast 3D volume decoded from the aggregated latent.

## Method

MPAD reformulates 3D synthesis as a 2D masked-slice prediction task in latent space. A 3D KL-regularized autoencoder first learns an isotropic latent representation, then a 2D UNet diffusion model is trained to denoise randomly masked target-contrast slices conditioned on source-contrast slices, unmasked target slices, and a text embedding, with conditioning injected via SPADE modulation from a 3D multi-modal conditioning encoder (MCE). At inference, a novel multi-plane autoregressive strategy generates slices sequentially in random order within each anatomical plane (Intra-plane Autoregressive Synthesis), then propagates previously completed volumes as initialization priors to orthogonal planes starting from an intermediate timestep τ rather than pure noise (Inter-plane Prior Inference). Four generation passes (three planes plus a refinement pass on the first plane) are aggregated via voxel-wise averaging to produce the final volume. This design reduces training FLOPs by 7× and inference FLOPs by 3× compared to 3D latent diffusion baselines while improving synthesis fidelity.

## Results

On the ADNI dataset, MPAD achieves the highest SSIM in 5 out of 6 synthesis tasks and the lowest NMSE across all tasks, with PSNR ranging from 22.56 (PD→T1) to 25.24 (T2→PD). On the IXI dataset, MPAD attains the best PSNR in 5 out of 6 tasks (e.g., 30.09 dB for T1→T2, 30.94 dB for PD→T2) and the best SSIM in all six tasks (0.881–0.890). For the most challenging PD→T1 and PD→T2 translations on ADNI, MPAD improves PSNR by over 2 dB compared to the second-best method. The method also reduces training FLOPs by 7× and inference FLOPs by 3× relative to 3D latent diffusion baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPAD outperforms all five baselines on the majority of metrics across both datasets. Against the GAN-based methods (CycleGAN-3D and EaGAN), MPAD achieves substantially higher PSNR (e.g., 23.10 vs. 24.80/21.72 on T1→T2 ADNI) and lower NMSE, while also producing sharper anatomical boundaries in qualitative comparisons. Against the 3D diffusion baselines (LDM-3D and cWDM), MPAD matches or exceeds PSNR (e.g., 25.24 vs. 25.29 on T2→PD ADNI) while achieving consistently lower NMSE and higher SSIM, and does so with only 2D denoising operations. ALDM, the only other one-to-many method, is outperformed by MPAD on nearly all tasks; the gap is most pronounced on PD→T1 (22.56 vs. 19.15 PSNR) and PD→T2 (22.59 vs. 20.15 PSNR) on ADNI, where MPAD gains over 2 dB. On IXI, MPAD leads ALDM by 0.77 dB on T1→T2 and 1.46 dB on PD→T2, while LDM-3D remains competitive on NMSE for T2→T1 (0.112 vs. 0.182), the one metric where MPAD trails a baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion framework (LDM) for its 3D autoencoder stage but departs fundamentally from the 3D denoising paradigm by replacing the volumetric diffusion network with a 2D slice-wise model. It adapts autoregressive generation principles (from PixelRNN, DALL-E, MaskGIT) to 3D MRI by factorizing volumes into sequences of 2D slices, and introduces a multi-plane autoregressive strategy with inter-plane priors that has no direct precedent in prior 3D medical image synthesis work. The conditioning mechanism borrows SPADE from image synthesis and BioMedCLIP for text embeddings, while the overall positioning is as an efficiency-oriented alternative to fully 3D diffusion models like cWDM and ALDM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7× training and 3× inference FLOP reductions make multi-contrast MRI synthesis practical on standard GPUs, which is critical for clinical deployment where scanner throughput and patient comfort are constraints. The unified one-to-many capability (a single model handling all six contrast-pair translations) simplifies imaging workflows compared to per-pair models. However, the sequential cross-plane refinement still adds inference latency (~1.8s per volume at minimum), and the approach is validated only on brain MRI with three contrasts (T1, T2, PD) at 32×32×32 latent resolution; generalization to other organs, more contrasts, or higher resolutions remains open. The ablation revealing that the 3D MCE source conditioning provides most of the quality benefit (with autoregressive synthesis adding only ~0.08 dB) raises the question of whether the autoregressive component is truly necessary or whether simpler multi-plane averaging with strong 3D conditioning would suffice.
