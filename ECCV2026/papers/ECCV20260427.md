## Problem

Camera-based perception systems for autonomous driving are trained and evaluated on fixed sensor rigs, yet real vehicle fleets vary in camera placement, orientation, FOV, and count, creating a cross-rig domain gap where only the geometric observation process changes. No existing benchmark isolates this geometric effect from confounding scene-content or appearance shifts, making it impossible to systematically study how rig geometry alone drives performance degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260427/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides multi-view RGB images at 1280×720 rendered from 14 camera rigs (6–10 cameras each) observing identical 30-second CARLA driving scenes at 2 Hz, accompanied by full camera extrinsics and intrinsics. Perception models consume these images plus calibration to predict oriented 3D bounding boxes for 9 object categories within an 80 m range. The rig metrics (RigV, RigCD) operate purely on calibration metadata—camera positions, SO(3) orientations, and FOV angles—producing scalar diversity or pairwise-discrepancy scores without any image data.

## Method

The paper introduces the Plentiful CARLA Camera Rigs (PCCR) benchmark, which uses a two-stage deterministic pipeline (trajectory recording followed by replay) to render 115 identical driving scenes under 14 systematically designed rigs, including a controlled family (R1 variants) that perturbs exactly one factor—rotation, translation, FOV, or camera count—at a time. Two calibration-metadata descriptors are proposed: Rig Variance, which computes the average pairwise divergence (normalized translation, geodesic rotation distance, and FOV difference) within a single rig, and Rig Contrastive Distance, which measures cross-rig discrepancy via minimum-cost bipartite camera matching plus a normalized count-mismatch penalty. RigCD's weighting parameters (λ_t, λ_r, λ_f, α) are calibrated per-architecture using L-BFGS-B on the controlled R1 variants to predict relative mAP degradation, then validated on held-out diverse rigs via Spearman rank correlation. Four representative architectures (BEVDet, BEVFusion, Fast-BEV, PETR) are trained on one rig and evaluated on all others, revealing that translation differences dominate cross-rig transfer difficulty, FOV/overlap consistency is critical for BEV-lifting models, and cross-rig robustness is highly architecture-dependent.

## Results

In-domain mAP averaged across all 14 rigs: BEVFusion achieves the highest at 0.163 ± 0.052, followed by BEVDet (0.155 ± 0.053), PETR (0.149 ± 0.049), and Fast-BEV (0.090 ± 0.043). Under cross-rig transfer, BEVDet, BEVFusion, and PETR experience near-total mAP collapse under out-of-distribution FOV changes, while training on higher-variance rigs (R6–R9) improves cross-rig generalization for BEVFusion and BEVDet. The proposed Rig Contrastive Distance (RigCD) achieves strong Spearman rank correlation with observed cross-rig performance degradation on unseen rigs: ρ = 0.72 (BEVDet), 0.73 (BEVFusion), and 0.80 (PETR), with 95% CIs of [0.62, 0.79], [0.65, 0.80], and [0.73, 0.85] respectively. Calibrated weights consistently identify camera translation as the dominant factor (λ_t ≈ 2.2–2.4), followed by rotation (λ_r ≈ 0.35–0.61), with FOV receiving the lowest weight (λ_f ≈ 0.11–0.29).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates four representative multi-view 3D detection architectures as baselines on the PCCR benchmark rather than proposing a new detection method. BEVFusion achieves the best in-domain mAP (0.163) and strong cross-rig correlation (ρ = 0.734 on unseen rigs), making it the most robust baseline overall. PETR shows the strongest RigCD predictability (ρ = 0.804) and is most sensitive to translation changes (removing λ_t drops ρ by 0.58), while BEVDet and BEVFusion are most sensitive to FOV/overlap consistency (removing λ_f drops ρ by ~0.39–0.40). Fast-BEV is a clear outlier: it has the lowest in-domain mAP (0.090) and RigCD fails to explain its cross-rig behavior (ρ = 0.097), suggesting its degradation pattern is governed by factors beyond the calibrated geometric descriptors. A jointly calibrated RigCD (across all four models) improves Fast-BEV's correlation to ρ = 0.59, indicating that model-specific calibration noise can be reduced with more data points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by isolating geometric rig variation as the sole source of domain shift, a dimension that prior work has only touched in limited ways: Embacher et al. [6] demonstrated a cross-rig gap between two vehicle types, and Klinghoffer et al. [13] perturbed cameras along a few factors, but neither provided a systematic multi-rig protocol. The benchmark repurposes the CARLA simulator's deterministic replay capability to render identical scenes under 14 rigs, and the two proposed metrics (Rig Variance, Rig Contrastive Distance) draw on ideas from sensor-placement literature (perception entropy, viewpoint entropy) but reframe them as model-agnostic, calibration-only descriptors. The perception architectures evaluated (BEVDet, BEVFusion, Fast-BEV, PETR) are standard baselines from the LSS-lifting and query-based 3D detection lines, used here as test subjects rather than as the contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a clear gap: no prior dataset or protocol isolates rig geometry as the only variable while holding scene content fixed, making it the first controlled setting for studying cross-rig generalization in automotive perception. The finding that RigCD (a purely calibration-metadata metric) reliably ranks transfer difficulty across architectures (ρ up to 0.80) offers a practical, lightweight tool for predicting which rig changes will hurt a deployed model. Key limitations include the simulation-only setting (no photometric or hardware artifacts), evaluation on just four architectures, and the fact that multi-rig training does not yet surpass the best single-rig transfer, leaving open whether rig-aware augmentation or calibration-conditioned models can close the gap. Extending to real-world fleets and additional modalities would be needed for broader adoption.
