## Problem

Single-view 3D reconstruction remains fundamentally ill-posed, and existing diffusion-based methods either rely on computationally expensive 3D convolutions (O(n³)) or quadratic-attention Transformers (O(n²)), while performing poorly in the critically underexplored data-scarce regime. State-space models offer linear-time sequence modeling but have not been adapted to generative 3D reconstruction, where unordered point clouds and the need for precise per-point predictions from noisy inputs pose unique challenges.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view RGB image (rendered at 224×224) and a noisy 3D point cloud of 4096 points as joint input. Image features are extracted by a pre-trained ViT-32 and projected onto the noisy point cloud via a rasterization function, producing feature-augmented point coordinates that serve as the diffusion denoising target. The output is a clean 3D point cloud of 4096 points, recovered progressively over multiple denoising steps with guided fusion from a separately trained generative model every 32 steps.

## Method

PDM integrates a diffusion denoising framework with a dual-directional Mamba backbone, where unordered point clouds are first structured into patches via FPS+KNN grouping. A Local Geometric Aggregation (LGA) module uses K-Norm (neighborhood feature normalization) and K-Pool (max+mean pooling) to extract explicit local geometric details that Mamba alone misses. A Hierarchical Feature Integration Network (HFINet) then propagates multi-level Mamba features back to individual points, fusing global semantic context with per-point local geometry to produce precise noise predictions. A Dynamic Weighted Sampling (DWS) strategy adaptively fuses a generative prior with the reconstruction prediction at each denoising step using a sigmoid-weighted point correspondence, leveraging hallucinated 3D structure to compensate for limited training data. Training uses the standard DDPM ε-prediction loss, with the generative and reconstruction models trained independently and fused only at inference time.

## Results

On ShapeNet-R2N2 with only 10% training data, PDM achieves the best Chamfer Distance across all three categories: 82.41 (Chair), 60.64 (Airplane), and 55.23 (Car), with corresponding F1 scores of 0.419, 0.614, and 0.542. On the real-world Pix3D benchmark (full data), PDM attains CD 79.28 / F1 0.499 (Chair), CD 41.43 / F1 0.463 (Sofa), and CD 184.35 / F1 0.422 (Table), outperforming all baselines on every metric. PDM also runs in 20.73 s with 0.39 GB GPU memory, roughly 3× faster and half the memory of MESC-3D (34.05 s, 0.86 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDM outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric in both the synthetic ShapeNet and real-world Pix3D evaluations. Against the strongest baseline MESC-3D (CVPR 25), PDM reduces Chamfer Distance by ~19% on ShapeNet 10% Chair (82.41 vs. 101.51), ~18% on Airplane (60.64 vs. 74.31), and ~13% on Pix3D Chair (79.28 vs. 91.36). Compared to the prior SOTA diffusion methods PC² and BDM, PDM's advantage is most pronounced under data scarcity: at 10% data, PDM's Chair CD (82.41) is 8% lower than CCD-3DR (89.79) and 15% lower than PC² (97.25), while at 100% data the gap narrows, confirming that the generative-prior fusion (DWS) is most beneficial when labeled data is limited. On Pix3D, PDM also achieves the highest F1 across all three categories (0.499, 0.463, 0.422), surpassing the next-best CCD-3DR by up to ~9.4% on Chair F1. In terms of efficiency, PDM (56.34 M params, 20.73 s, 0.39 GB) is substantially faster and lighter than BDM-M (74.82 M, 52.47 s, 2.01 GB) and PC²/CCD-3DR (47.41 M, 48.46 s, 1.73 GB), while also beating MESC-3D (74.84 M, 34.05 s, 0.86 GB) on both speed and memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDM sits at the intersection of two active research threads: diffusion-based single-view 3D reconstruction (PC², BDM, MESC-3D) and state-space sequence modeling (Mamba, S4). It departs from the dominant Transformer-based denoising backbones in prior diffusion 3D methods by substituting a dual-directional Mamba module, motivated by the quadratic attention cost that becomes prohibitive for long point sequences. The patch-based preprocessing (FPS + KNN) follows Point-BERT/Point-MAE conventions, while the LGA and HFINet modules are novel task-specific designs that address Mamba's known weakness in capturing local geometric detail and the gap between abstract semantic tokens and per-point predictions. The DWS strategy extends the BDM framework's generative-reconstruction coupling with a per-point adaptive weighting mechanism rather than a fixed or distance-based fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical efficiency win (≈2× faster inference and ≈2× lower VRAM than MESC-3D) while matching or exceeding accuracy, making high-quality single-view 3D reconstruction feasible on commodity hardware. The data-scarce evaluation protocol (10%, 50%, 100% splits) is a useful and underexplored axis that highlights where generative priors (via DWS) most benefit reconstruction. Open questions include whether the advantage persists beyond the three tested categories per dataset, whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary or multi-object scenes, and whether the 140k-iteration training budget and fixed 4096-point resolution limit real-world applicability. The reliance on a separately trained generative model (no weight sharing) also raises questions about the robustness of DWS when the generative prior is poorly calibrated.
