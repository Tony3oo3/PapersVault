## Problem

Existing video shadow detection methods degrade substantially under fast motion, drastic illumination changes, and complex dynamic backgrounds, largely because conventional frame-based cameras suffer from motion blur and limited dynamic range. No prior work has introduced event cameras into the VSD task, despite their high temporal resolution, low latency, and wide dynamic range making them well-suited to capture rapid shadow dynamics that RGB frames miss.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2i+1 consecutive RGB frames (resized to 512×512) and 2i inter-frame event voxel segments from a DAVIS346 event camera recorded at ~30 fps. The event stream is consumed in three distinct forms: as raw voxel segments for motion-field extraction (MTA), as inter-frame segments for intermediate mask generation (BMG), and as ESI-reconstructed intensity images for event-based mask prediction (DMTG). The output is a single binary shadow segmentation mask for the target frame, produced by a diffusion decoder that directly denoises an analog-bits-encoded label rather than predicting noise.

## Method

EDM is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 generates preliminary masks: a Motion Trajectory Attention (MTA) module encodes inter-frame event voxels with a 5-layer strided convolutional encoder to produce pixel-wise displacement fields, then performs multi-head attention along event-derived patch trajectories across RGB feature maps; a Bidirectional Mask Generation (BMG) module uses a pre-trained ECOSNet to predict intermediate masks between adjacent frames in both forward and backward directions, fused with linearly decreasing weights to mitigate LSTM error accumulation. Stage 2 performs diffusion-based refinement: event streams are reconstructed into intensity images via ESI, a lightweight ResNet predicts event-based preliminary masks, and a Dual-Modal Temporal Guidance (DMTG) module fuses RGB frames with the dense preliminary mask sequences from both modalities, injecting the result as conditional guidance into a diffusion model that uses the analog-bits strategy to directly predict the final shadow mask with DDIM sampling over 15 steps.

## Results

EDM achieves the best performance across all four metrics on both datasets. On Event-ViSha, EDM obtains MAE 0.015, Fβ 0.861, IoU 0.739, and BER 5.76, outperforming the strongest VSD baseline DTTNet (MAE 0.016, IoU 0.718) and the best VOS method SAM3 (MAE 0.031, IoU 0.632). On the more challenging EVSD, EDM reaches MAE 0.035, Fβ 0.724, IoU 0.664, and BER 11.05, surpassing the previous best VSD method DAS (MAE 0.066, IoU 0.601) by a wide margin of 47% in MAE reduction and 6.3 points in IoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDM outperforms all 16 baselines on every metric across both datasets, with no losses or ties. Among VSD-specific methods, the closest competitor is DTTNet on Event-ViSha (IoU 0.718 vs. EDM's 0.739), but DTTNet's code is unavailable for EVSD evaluation; on EVSD, EDM leads the next-best VSD method DAS by 6.3 IoU points (0.664 vs. 0.601) and reduces MAE by 47% (0.035 vs. 0.066). Against the strongest VOS baseline SAM3, EDM gains 14.5 IoU points on EVSD (0.664 vs. 0.594) and 10.7 points on Event-ViSha (0.739 vs. 0.632), demonstrating that event-guided temporal modeling substantially exceeds generic video segmentation. ERSS methods (BRENet, EISNet, CMNeXt) show moderate robustness on EVSD due to event inputs but still trail EDM by 4–14 IoU points, confirming that dedicated shadow-detection architecture combined with event guidance is necessary. The stratified analysis (Table 2) further shows EDM's advantage is most pronounced in high-speed (IoU 0.647 vs. DAS 0.532) and low-light (0.682 vs. 0.573) scenarios, where conventional RGB-only methods degrade most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: video shadow detection (VSD), event-based visual segmentation, and diffusion-based generation. It builds directly on TBGDiff's introduction of diffusion models to VSD and on ECOS-Net's event-driven temporal mask prediction, but departs from both by treating event streams not as a primary input to a segmentation network but as a conditional guidance signal for a diffusion denoising process. The Motion Trajectory Attention module repurposes event-derived motion fields (analogous to optical flow) for trajectory-aware cross-frame attention, while the Dual-Modal Temporal Guidance module fuses RGB and event-based preliminary masks into a unified conditioning signal — a fusion strategy more closely related to RGB-event semantic segmentation work (BRENet, EISNet, CMNeXt) than to prior VSD methods, which are purely RGB-based.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of event cameras into VSD addresses a concrete and well-motivated gap: existing VSD methods degrade sharply under fast motion and illumination changes precisely because frame-based sensors introduce motion blur and lose dynamic range. The new EVSD dataset and Event-ViSha extension provide the first benchmarks for multimodal shadow detection, which will be useful beyond this specific method. However, the approach is architecturally complex (three dedicated modules plus a diffusion decoder), the Event-ViSha events are simulated via V2E rather than captured, and the evaluation is limited to two datasets with a single backbone (MixTransformer-B3). Open questions include whether the gains transfer to other event camera hardware, whether the multi-module pipeline can be simplified, and whether the event-guided diffusion paradigm generalizes to other video segmentation tasks beyond shadows.
