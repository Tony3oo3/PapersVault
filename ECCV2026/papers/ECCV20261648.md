## Problem

State-of-the-art visual navigation systems either map observations directly to actions (policy methods) or pair a separate planner with a separate world model, both of which suffer from state-action misalignment and poor adaptability in novel or dynamic environments. Even unified autoregressive frameworks that interleave imagination and planning lack temporal structure, causing gradual drift over long horizons. The paper addresses the joint challenge of unifying planning and visual foresight in a single backbone while injecting hierarchical memory to sustain stable, coherent reasoning across extended rollouts.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a start egocentric RGB observation, a goal RGB observation, and an initial 3D pose (x, y, yaw), all resized to 448×448 and discretized into 784 VQ visual tokens plus BPE text/pose tokens. At each inference step it alternately emits a 3D action (planar translation x, y and yaw rotation φ, each discretized into 0.01-sized bin tokens, or a Stop token) and a full predicted next egocentric frame as 784 visual tokens, so the output is an interleaved action–image sequence generated autoregressively within one forward pass per substep.

## Method

UniWM replaces the modular planner–world-model pair with a single Anole-7B causal Transformer fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 16 on qkv projections) that alternates between an action-prediction substep and an observation-generation substep, grounding each action in the visually imagined next view. Training interleaves planner samples (supervised by a discretized bin-token cross-entropy loss L_plan over three disjoint token sets for x, y, φ) and world-model samples (supervised by a codebook reconstruction loss L_world that weights predicted visual-token probabilities by their distance to ground-truth embeddings) within the same batch. At inference, a two-level hierarchical memory bank augments attention: an intra-step KV cache extracted from five selected decoder layers at the current observation is fused with a cross-step trajectory store via top-k cosine similarity gating and exponential temporal decay (γ = 0.2), so that both immediate perceptual cues and longer-range trajectory context modulate every attention computation across the alternating substeps.

## Results

On the four in-domain navigation benchmarks, UniWM (with full hierarchical memory) achieves SR of 0.75 / 0.93 / 0.68 / 0.76 and ATE of 0.22 / 0.34 / 0.32 / 0.38 on Go Stanford, ReCon, SCAND, and HuRoN respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline NWM (SR 0.45 / 0.79 / 0.55 / 0.41) by up to 30 percentage points in success rate. Zero-shot on the unseen TartanDrive dataset, UniWM reaches SR 0.42 (vs. 0.27 for NWM), and on the 25-DoF 1X Humanoid dataset it attains SR 0.81 (vs. 0.51 for NWM). For visualization quality, UniWM achieves the best SSIM (0.457) and DreamSim (0.041) on one-step prediction, and maintains SSIM@5 = 0.350 under open-loop rollouts, surpassing NWM (0.256) and Aether (0.235).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniWM decisively outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest modular world-model baseline NWM [9], UniWM improves SR by 0.30 (Go Stanford 0.75 vs. 0.45), 0.14 (ReCon 0.93 vs. 0.79), 0.13 (SCAND 0.68 vs. 0.55), and 0.35 (HuRoN 0.76 vs. 0.41), while reducing ATE by roughly 50–70%. Policy-centric methods GNM [50], VINT [51], and NoMaD [53] trail further behind, with NoMaD being the best of the three (SR 0.33–0.50) yet still 20–30 points below UniWM. The zero-shot Anole-7B [17] backbone (without navigation-specific training) performs worst overall (SR 0.18–0.41), confirming that the unified training objective is critical. On visualization quality (Table 2), UniWM surpasses Diamond [3] (SSIM 0.457 vs. 0.311), Aether [72] (SSIM 0.457 vs. 0.373), and NWM [9] (SSIM 0.457 vs. 0.389), with the largest gap in DreamSim (0.041 vs. 0.068–0.131). On the zero-shot TartanDrive test, UniWM (SR 0.42) nearly doubles NWM (SR 0.27) and triples NoMaD (SR 0.18), and on 1X Humanoid it lifts SR from 0.51 (NWM) to 0.81, demonstrating consistent gains even in high-dimensional action spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniWM departs from the dominant modular paradigm in goal-conditioned visual navigation (e.g., NWM's separate CDiT world model + CEM planner, or policy-centric methods like NoMaD and GNM) by collapsing the planner and world model into a single autoregressive multimodal backbone built on Chameleon/Anole-7B. It also extends the broader world-modeling literature (I-JEPA, V-JEPA, diffusion-based simulators) by introducing a hierarchical KV-cache memory mechanism—combining intra-step perceptual caching with cross-step trajectory context via similarity gating and temporal decay—that has no direct analogue in prior navigation world models. The bin-token discretization for continuous actions and the joint interleaved training of planner and world-model samples represent a new training recipe not present in either the policy or modular world-model lines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that unifying imagination and control in one backbone, augmented with structured memory, consistently closes the gap to modular pipelines across four in-domain benchmarks, zero-shot transfer to an unseen dataset, and 25-DoF humanoid navigation—suggesting a practical path toward scalable, imagination-driven embodied agents. Limitations include ego-robot artifacts fading during rollouts due to domain gap, and the reliance on a fixed 4096-token context window that constrains very long trajectories. Open questions raised by the authors include adaptive token allocation, uncertainty-aware planning, and closed-loop deployment on physical robots, all of which remain unresolved.
