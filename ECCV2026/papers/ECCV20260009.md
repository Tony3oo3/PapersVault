## Problem

Existing zero-shot Noisy Test-Time Adaptation (NTTA) methods, such as AdaND, rely on online training of a noise detector and CLIP text-image alignment for classification, leading to overconfident misclassifications, poor robustness in data-scarce settings, and degraded inference efficiency. The paper asks whether one can design a training-free framework that accurately estimates both the positive feature distribution and the negative label distribution to simultaneously maximize ID accuracy and filter OOD outliers in an online stream.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260009/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming sequence of RGB images encoded by a frozen ViT-B/16 CLIP visual encoder, along with a fixed ID label set and a large candidate negative label corpus. At each batch, it maintains two bounded caches (capacity Q=1000) of high-confidence positive and negative image features selected via thresholds λ_pos=0.75 and λ_neg=0.25. The output is a fused class probability (combining CLIP cosine similarity with dual-GDA logits), a discriminative negative-label OOD score, and a binary ID/OOD decision determined by an adaptive threshold that minimizes intra-class variance of the score distribution.

## Method

DDE comprises two complementary training-free modules. Positive Feature Distribution Estimation (PFDE) partitions cached positive features per class into an inclusion Gaussian (samples whose top-1 CLIP prediction is that class) and an exclusion Gaussian (samples where the class is second-highest), estimating both via an EM algorithm with shrinkage regularization; the final ID score is a calibrated contrastive logit f_k^in − β·f_k^ex, dynamically fused with CLIP logits through a ramping weight α_t that grows with accumulated samples. Negative Label Distribution Estimation (NLDE) computes a per-label similarity discrepancy ΔSim between the positive and negative image caches, then selects the top-M̂ most discriminative negative labels to replace the generic corpus, yielding a sharper OOD score. An adaptive threshold (borrowed from OWTTT) dynamically separates ID from OOD at inference time, eliminating the need for any parameter updates or gradient-based training.

## Results

On the primary ImageNet benchmark, DDE achieves 76.79% average harmonic mean accuracy (Acc_H), outperforming the strongest NTTA baseline AdaND (73.09%) by 3.70%, while simultaneously reaching 65.55% ID accuracy (Acc_S) and 92.94% noisy detection accuracy (Acc_N). For OOD detection, DDE attains 97.89% AUROC and 9.80% FPR95, reducing FPR95 by 6.20% over AdaND (16.00%). Gains are consistent across distribution-shifted variants (ImageNet-A: +6.57%, ImageNet-V2: +3.57%, ImageNet-R: +3.01% over AdaND) and fine-grained datasets (Food-101: 93.48% vs 92.23%, Stanford Cars: 79.14% vs 77.05%). DDE also achieves the lowest test-time latency (1.84 min) with zero additional learnable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDE outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric. Against the closest NTTA competitor AdaND [3], DDE gains +3.70% Acc_H on ImageNet (76.79% vs 73.09%), +6.57% on ImageNet-A, and +3.01% on ImageNet-R, while also improving OOD detection FPR95 from 16.00% to 9.80%. Compared to TTOOD methods AdaNeg [58] and OODD [53], DDE achieves higher Acc_H (76.79% vs 73.06% and 70.65%) because it jointly optimizes both ID classification and noise filtering rather than focusing solely on outlier scoring. Against TTA methods DMN [60], Tent [45], and TPT [39], DDE's advantage is most pronounced on Acc_N (92.94% vs 77.87%, 64.05%, 84.48%), confirming that distribution-aware modeling is critical for noisy streams. In terms of efficiency, DDE runs in 1.84 min with no learnable parameters, versus AdaND's 6.59 min with 1.0k parameters and TPT's 321.90 min, making it the fastest method in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot Noisy Test-Time Adaptation (NTTA) paradigm introduced by AdaND [3], which trains an online noise detector using pseudo-labeled test samples. DDE departs from this instance-level learning approach by replacing the trained detector with a training-free dual Gaussian distribution model (GDA) for ID classification, while simultaneously extending the negative-label OOD detection framework of NegLabel [18] and AdaNeg [58] through dynamic mining of discriminative negative labels. The adaptive thresholding mechanism is borrowed from OWTTT [24], and the overall architecture composes these components into a unified, parameter-free pipeline that avoids any gradient-based optimization at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a fully training-free Gaussian distribution model can outperform an online-trained noise detector (AdaND) by 3.70% in harmonic mean accuracy while running 3.5× faster, this work challenges the assumption that test-time learning is necessary for robust OOD filtering in VLMs. The practical implication is significant for deployment scenarios where retraining or fine-tuning is infeasible. Open questions remain about whether the unimodal Gaussian assumption adequately captures highly multimodal class distributions (e.g., in fine-grained or long-tail settings), and how the method scales to label spaces far exceeding ImageNet's 1K classes. The reliance on CLIP's text-image alignment quality also limits applicability to domains where textual prototypes are poorly aligned with visual features.
