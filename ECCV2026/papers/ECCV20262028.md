## Problem

Existing parametric human body models capture only the 3D skin surface, offering no insight into the internal muscle structures that generate motion. Physics-based simulations (FEM) can model muscle dynamics but are computationally prohibitive for real-time use, while biomechanical frameworks predict forces without modeling observable shape changes. No scalable method existed to recover dynamic, subject-specific muscle deformations directly from accessible multi-view RGB observations.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes only a skeletal pose (6D continuous joint rotations) as input and produces deformed muscle and skin meshes plus per-muscle displacements. The subject-specific bind meshes (skin, global muscle, skeleton layers sharing identical topology and rigging, plus individual high-resolution muscle meshes) are pre-computed offline from a 3D body scan and volumetric fitting. The model is person-specific: each subject requires its own trained U-Net pair and bind geometry, with no cross-subject generalization.

## Method

SOMA decouples soft-tissue deformation into two layers: a primary muscle displacement field predicted by one U-Net from relative pose features, and a skin residual offset predicted by a second U-Net, so that skin can compress inward independently of outward muscle bulging. Training is supervised by sparse canonical marker residuals (pose-corrective displacements) extracted from multi-view ArUco marker tracking, with the loss computed in the canonical frame to be invariant to global pose. To resolve the ill-posed disentanglement between muscle and skin contributions, the method enforces five biomechanical priors: area-normalized Laplacian smoothness, biharmonic bending resistance, edge-length stretching, tangential sliding penalty (stronger on muscle to anchor it), and signed-volume preservation via 2-point Gauss-Legendre quadrature on inter-layer prisms. The muscle volume constraint is evaluated in posed space while the skin volume constraint is in canonical space, forcing the network to learn bulges that physically counteract LBS compression. Learned canonical displacements on the unified muscle boundary are propagated to individual high-resolution muscle meshes via a pre-computed barycentric binding for per-muscle animation.

## Results

On the SKIM dataset, SOMA achieves a median tracking error (MedPME) of 11.51 mm and P90 of 19.20 mm, substantially outperforming LBS (15.64 mm / 26.03 mm) in dynamic soft-tissue regions where errors exceed 25 mm (DRE: 64.52 mm vs. 134.28 mm). For anatomical plausibility, SOMA preserves subcutaneous volume to within 1.37% and maintains a low intersection ratio (0.85% S→M, 0.93% M→B), while LBS suffers 18.81% muscle volume collapse and HIT exhibits 7.61% subcutaneous interpenetration. The U-Net architecture yields the best overall tracking (MPME 13.43 mm) and lowest subcutaneous volume error (1.37%) among tested architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOMA is compared against two baselines: standard Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) applied to the static scan, and HIT [27], a state-of-the-art uncoupled volumetric predictor trained on generic templates. Against LBS, SOMA wins decisively on dynamic-region tracking (DRE >25 mm: 64.52 vs. 134.28, a >2× reduction) and muscle volume preservation (10.35% vs. 18.81% error), while matching LBS on intersection ratio (0.85%/0.93%) and being marginally worse on mean tracking (MPME 13.43 vs. 13.11 mm). Against HIT, SOMA dominates on all tracking metrics (MPME 13.43 vs. 115.34 mm; P90 19.20 vs. 354.80 mm) and reduces subcutaneous interpenetration from 7.61% to 0.85%, but HIT achieves lower deep muscle volume error (1.95% vs. 10.35%), which the authors attribute to SOMA's optimizer prioritizing non-intersection over full deep-tissue inflation to avoid skeletal collisions.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOMA extends the corrective pose-dependent blendshape framework (Lewis et al., SIGGRAPH 2000) from a single skin layer to a decoupled volumetric multi-layer representation (muscle + skin + bone), supervised by biomechanical priors rather than learned from medical imaging. It departs from surface-only parametric models (SMPL, SoftSMPL) that implicitly encode soft-tissue effects without explicit anatomy, from static anatomical fitting methods (HIT, Komaritzan et al.) that cannot model dynamic muscle behavior, and from FEM-based muscle simulations that are accurate but non-scalable. The key conceptual shift is formulating muscle deformation recovery as an inverse problem from surface observations (marker-tracked skin residuals) rather than forward simulation from biomechanical parameters or fitting from volumetric medical scans.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOMA provides the first scalable, non-invasive pipeline for animating anatomically plausible muscle deformations from accessible visual data, bridging the gap between cheap kinematic models and expensive FEM simulations. The SKIM dataset (5 subjects, 45 min multi-view RGB with ArUco-tracked markers and ground-truth anatomy) is a valuable resource for the community. However, the method remains subject-specific, requires a custom marker suit for training data, and does not model force-driven or co-contraction variation at fixed joint angles. Open questions include whether markerless skin tracking can replace the suit, whether the approach generalizes across subjects (person-agnostic parametric models), and whether joint optimization of baseline anatomy and dynamic displacements can reduce sensitivity to the initial volumetric fitting.
