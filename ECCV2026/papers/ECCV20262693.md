## Problem

Existing text-to-3D scene generation methods either depend on visual guidance (panoramas, multi-view images) or require human-designed layouts, and neither guarantees that the resulting layout supports effective camera traversal for rendering. This mismatch between layout and rendering requirements produces low-quality or missing object-centric views, sticky/merged artifacts, and limits object-level editability. The paper addresses the gap of fully automatic, end-to-end 3D room generation that simultaneously optimizes layout for physical plausibility, camera reachability, and rendering quality without any manual layout authoring or panoramic input.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single short text prompt (e.g., 'A bedroom') as the sole input, with no panoramic images, multi-view references, or manual layout specifications required. It produces a complete 3D Gaussian Splatting scene at 512×512 resolution optimized over 1500 iterations on a single RTX 4090 (24 GB), completing in under 30 minutes. The output includes explicit per-object layout constraints (position, orientation, scale), decoupled object-level 3D assets, and a traversable scene map, all supporting downstream editing operations such as repositioning, insertion/deletion of assets, and style changes.

## Method

RoomPlanner couples three stages: (1) hierarchical LLM agent planners (GPT-4o) that expand a short prompt into per-object semantic descriptions and real-world scales, with Shap-E generating initial point clouds; (2) iterative layout arrangement enforcing two constraints — a collision constraint using 3D IoU overlap checks and a reachability constraint that rasterizes the scene into a 2D traversable map and verifies A* path existence from a doorway anchor to every object; and (3) single-stage differentiable 3DGS optimization guided by Stable Diffusion 3.5 via a novel Interval Timestep Flow Sampling (ITFS) strategy built on Conditional Flow Matching. The key insight is that ReachView camera trajectories are sampled directly on the same traversable map used by the reachability constraint, decomposing supervision into a scene-centric global walk (visiting objects in nearest-neighbor order along A* paths) and an object-centric orbit (upper-hemisphere close views), then interleaving them. ITFS further couples timestep selection to camera mode: close object-centric views sample earlier timesteps (t∈[200,600]) to sharpen geometry, while global views sample later timesteps (t∈[400,800]) to reinforce cross-object semantic consistency, aggregating gradients over multiple intervals in a single optimization pass.

## Results

On the user study (1–5 scale), RoomPlanner achieves the highest Rationality score of 4.10 and Quality score of 3.86, outperforming DreamScene (3.10 / 3.15), GALA3D (2.61 / 3.22), and Set-the-Scene (1.13 / 2.80). On automatic image-text metrics, RoomPlanner scores 5.31 Aesthetic Score, 48.83 ImageReward, and 28.44% CLIP Score, leading all baselines. On layout quality, RoomPlanner achieves 0.00 collisions, 0.20 out-of-bounds objects, and 100% A*-reachability, matching Holodeck on collisions while halving its OOB rate and achieving perfect reachability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoomPlanner outperforms all three scene-generation baselines on every reported metric. Against Set-the-Scene [3], it gains +2.97 in Rationality and +1.06 in Quality, with the largest gap in ImageReward (+7.73). Against GALA3D [23], the Rationality margin is +1.49 and ImageReward is +6.16. Against DreamScene [19], the closest competitor, RoomPlanner still leads by +1.00 in Rationality, +0.71 in Quality, and +4.98 in ImageReward. On layout quality (Table 3), RoomPlanner ties Holodeck [42] at 0.00 collisions but reduces out-of-bounds objects from 0.87 to 0.20 and improves A*-reachability from 97.3% to 100%. Against LayoutVLM [1], RoomPlanner eliminates all 11.09 colliding pairs, reduces OOB from 7.53 to 0.20, and raises reachability from 86.2% to 100%, demonstrating that the Reachability Constraint is the key differentiator for physical plausibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoomPlanner sits at the intersection of LLM-driven layout planning (Holodeck, LayoutVLM, LayoutGPT) and diffusion-based 3D scene optimization (DreamScene, Set-the-Scene), but departs from both in a key way: it replaces reliance on pre-existing 3D asset libraries with on-the-fly text-to-3D point-cloud generation (Shap-E), and it couples the layout stage directly to the rendering stage through a shared traversability map. The rendering pipeline extends Score Distillation Sampling into a Flow Distillation Sampling formulation built on Stable Diffusion 3.5's rectified-flow prior, and introduces Interval Timestep Flow Sampling to exploit both geometric (low-timestep) and semantic (high-timestep) regimes in a single optimization pass. The ReachView camera strategy, which samples trajectories on the same A*-based traversable map used for the reachability layout constraint, is the paper's most distinctive architectural choice, treating renderability as a first-class layout objective rather than a post-hoc view-selection problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating both manual layout authoring and panoramic-image guidance, RoomPlanner lowers the practical barrier to generating editable, collision-free indoor scenes from a single short prompt, which is directly relevant to embodied-AI environment creation, game asset pipelines, and AR/VR content production. The reachability-aware layout constraint is a conceptually clean insight—ensuring that a virtual agent (and hence a rendering camera) can actually reach every object before optimization begins—which prior LLM-layout methods largely ignored. Open questions remain around scalability beyond a single 24 GB GPU (which caps Gaussian count and limits fine light-transport detail), the stability of flow-distillation at very high iteration counts, and whether the A*-based reachability check generalizes to multi-room or outdoor environments.
