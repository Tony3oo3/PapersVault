## Problem

Existing lens effect rendering methods primarily model the magnitude of defocus blur but neglect the stylistic and aesthetic characteristics of real lenses, such as polygonal or donut-shaped bokeh, vignetting-induced cat-eye distortion, and diffraction-driven starbursts. No prior framework unifies continuous optical parameter control (focus distance, blur strength) with discrete lens-style selection in a single model, leaving a gap in controllable, multi-style computational photography.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260406/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an all-in-focus RGB image at 512×384 resolution, two continuous scalar parameters (focus distance and blur strength), and a discrete lens-style text prompt (e.g., 'hexagonal bokeh', 'donut bokeh', 'cat-eye effect', 'starburst') as input, and produces a single lens-style rendered image. Notably, the model is depth-free at inference: depth maps are used only during MultiLens dataset synthesis to ensure physically consistent supervision, while the network implicitly internalizes scene geometry in its latent representation.

## Method

LensStyle is a flow-matching generative model initialized from pretrained Stable Diffusion 2.1, operating in a 4-channel VAE latent space with a conditional U-Net backbone. Its core contribution is a Dual Path Controller that disentangles two conditioning signals: (1) a Continuous Optical Parameter Modulation branch that encodes focus distance and blur strength via Fourier feature encoding and applies adaptive affine modulation (scaling γ and shifting β generated by 3-layer MLPs) to intermediate U-Net features, and (2) a Discrete Lens Style Cross-Attention branch that encodes the selected lens style via a CLIP text encoder and injects it through cross-attention layers to govern aperture geometry, vignetting distortion, and diffraction spike patterns. Training uses a unified optical forward model (combining geometric defocus PSF and Fraunhofer diffraction PSF) to synthesize 303K physically grounded image pairs across four aperture geometries and one diffraction regime, supervised by flow-matching loss plus L1 reconstruction and VGG perceptual loss.

## Results

On the MultiLens dataset, LensStyle achieves 23.38/23.09/23.41/23.45 PSNR and 0.1718/0.1700/0.1652/0.1641 LPIPS across hexagonal, donut, cat-eye, and starburst styles respectively, outperforming the strongest fine-tuned baseline (SuperEdit*) by 0.6–1.2 dB in PSNR and 15–25% in LPIPS. For standard circular bokeh, LensStyle attains 23.33 PSNR and 0.1743 LPIPS on MultiLens-Circle and 23.58 PSNR and 0.2398 LPIPS on the real-world EBB400 benchmark, surpassing all dedicated bokeh methods (MPIB, BokehMe, Dr.Bokeh, BokehDiff) on PSNR and LPIPS. In a 58-participant user study, LensStyle is preferred over SuperEdit* in 60.8% of pairwise comparisons and over UltraEdit* in 76.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against dedicated bokeh rendering methods (MPIB, BokehMe, Dr.Bokeh, BokehDiff) on circular bokeh, LensStyle achieves the highest PSNR (23.33 on MultiLens, 23.58 on EBB400) and lowest LPIPS (0.1743, 0.2398), though BokehDiff edges it slightly on SSIM (0.7642 vs. 0.7387 on MultiLens). Against general-purpose diffusion-based editors (UltraEdit, SuperEdit) without fine-tuning, LensStyle leads by a large margin of 1.1–1.8 dB in PSNR and 15–25% relative LPIPS reduction across all four non-circular styles, as these models lack any lens-geometry awareness. Even after fine-tuning on MultiLens (UltraEdit*, SuperEdit*), LensStyle still wins by 0.6–1.2 dB PSNR and maintains lower LPIPS, confirming that the Dual Path Controller's disentangled continuous-discrete conditioning provides a structural advantage over prompt-based editing. In the user study, LensStyle is preferred 60.8% of the time over SuperEdit* and 76.5% over UltraEdit*, with the largest gap (88.4%) against the original UltraEdit, indicating that fine-tuning narrows but does not close the perceptual quality gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow matching generative paradigm (Lipman et al.) into the domain of lens effect rendering, where prior methods (MPIB, BokehMe, Dr.Bokeh, BokehDiff) have focused almost exclusively on simulating blur magnitude via depth-conditioned or depth-free defocus kernels, producing only uniform circular bokeh. The key conceptual departure is treating lens rendering not as a single-style blur synthesis problem but as a joint continuous-discrete control problem: continuous optical parameters (focus distance, blur strength) are modulated via adaptive feature scaling/shifting, while discrete aperture geometries (polygonal, donut, cat-eye, starburst) are injected through cross-attention with CLIP text embeddings. This positions the work between classical optical simulation (aperture transmission functions, Fraunhofer diffraction) and modern generative editing, bridging the gap that diffusion-based editors (UltraEdit, SuperEdit) cannot fill due to their lack of lens-physical awareness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing the first unified multi-style lens rendering framework and the 303K-pair MultiLens dataset, this paper provides both a practical tool and a structured benchmark for a problem that prior computational photography work has largely ignored. The depth-free inference (depth used only for training data synthesis) makes the approach deployable in real-world settings where reliable depth estimation is unavailable. However, the authors acknowledge a significant limitation: the model cannot capture brand-specific optical signatures (e.g., Fuji vs. Sony vs. Canon), leaving open the question of whether a single unified model can encode the full diversity of real-world lens character or whether per-brand conditioning is necessary. The broader implication is a shift in computational photography from 'how much blur' to 'what optical identity,' which could influence future work on lens simulation, virtual cinematography, and camera-aware image editing.
