## Problem

Existing visible-to-infrared translation methods formulate the task as a single unified generative process, implicitly entangling macroscopic thermal semantic assignment with microscopic spatial detail reconstruction. This entanglement forces a sub-optimal trade-off between global thermal plausibility and local texture fidelity, producing blurred boundaries or physically unnatural thermal artifacts. The paper addresses this by explicitly decoupling the two objectives into separate, coordinated continuous flows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261456/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single visible RGB image (resized to 640×512 for KAIST/FLIR or 512×384 for M³FD) and produces a corresponding infrared thermal image at the same resolution. The input is consumed in two distinct ways: directly as the pixel-domain flow endpoint and through a DINOv3 encoder to initialize the semantic-domain flow. Generation proceeds via 10-step Euler ODE integration, and training requires paired visible-infrared data from the target benchmark.

## Method

D3F-IR formulates translation as two synchronized deterministic flow matching ODEs: a semantic-domain flow (driven by a Diffusion Transformer over DINOv3 latents) models macroscopic thermal distributions, while a pixel-domain flow (a convolutional encoder-decoder) synthesizes fine-grained spatial structures directly in image space. The two flows are coordinated through a Pixel-space Velocity Predictor equipped with SEAM (SEmantic-Aligned Modulation) layers, which project semantic guidance into per-patch affine scaling and shifting parameters broadcast over 4×4 spatial regions, establishing explicit token-to-patch correspondence. Training is end-to-end, combining flow-matching velocity regression losses for both domains with reconstruction losses (L2, SSIM, LPIPS) applied along both a predicted-velocity path and a ground-truth-velocity path to provide stable teacher-forcing supervision. At inference, both flows are advanced synchronously over 10 Euler steps, with the semantic velocity at each step dynamically conditioning the pixel velocity through a cross-attention fusion module.

## Results

On the three standard benchmarks, D3F-IR (from-scratch) achieves the best PSNR on all datasets: 24.94 dB on KAIST, 20.09 dB on FLIR, and 21.53 dB on M³FD, along with the best SSIM on KAIST (0.830) and M³FD (0.768). The pretrained-then-finetuned variant further improves LPIPS to 0.219 (KAIST), 0.268 (FLIR), and 0.191 (M³FD), and reduces FID substantially (e.g., 40.31 on KAIST vs. 75.91 from-scratch). The main trade-off is FID in the from-scratch setting, where externally pretrained baselines (ThermalGen, DiffV2IR) still hold a distributional-matching advantage, though the gap narrows considerably with the pretrained variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D3F-IR (from-scratch) achieves the highest PSNR on all three benchmarks, beating the strongest GAN-based baseline (Pix2Pix, 21.57 dB) by over 3 dB on KAIST and the best diffusion-based baselines (PID 23.31, ThermalGen 23.74) by 1.2–1.6 dB on KAIST. On FLIR and M³FD, it surpasses DiffV2IR (19.60 / 20.70 dB) and ThermalGen (17.82 / 20.57 dB) in PSNR by 0.5–1.0 dB. In SSIM, D3F-IR leads on KAIST (0.830 vs. PID 0.801) and M³FD (0.768 vs. ThermalGen 0.744), and on FLIR (0.541 vs. PID 0.532). The principal weakness is FID in the from-scratch setting: DiffV2IR achieves 55.40 on FLIR and 63.15 on M³FD, and ThermalGen reaches 35.96 on KAIST, all lower than D3F-IR's 66.72 / 103.26 / 75.91 respectively. The pretrained-then-finetuned variant of D3F-IR narrows this gap substantially (FID 40.31 on KAIST, 60.96 on FLIR, 65.75 on M³FD) and simultaneously achieves the best LPIPS on all three datasets (0.219, 0.268, 0.191), indicating that the FID deficit is primarily a data-coverage issue rather than an architectural limitation. All GAN-based baselines (Pix2Pix, CycleGAN, InfraGAN, EGGAN-U, DR-AVIT, StegoGAN, UNSB) are consistently outperformed by D3F-IR on every metric across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

D3F-IR extends the deterministic flow matching paradigm introduced by RAW-Flow [25] from a single latent domain to a dual-domain setting, orchestrating two synchronized ODEs in semantic and pixel spaces. It departs from prior V2IR generative frameworks (PID, ThermalGen, DiffV2IR) that treat cross-modal translation as a unified process, arguing that entangling thermal semantics with spatial detail in one flow forces sub-optimal trade-offs. The semantic branch leverages a DINOv3 encoder and a Diffusion Transformer for velocity prediction, while the pixel branch uses a convolutional encoder-decoder with novel SEAM layers that inject semantic guidance via patch-wise affine modulation—a mechanism positioned between global AdaIN and pixel-wise SPADE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit dual-domain decomposition offers a principled recipe for cross-modal generation tasks where global semantics and local structure are in tension, and the patch-wise modulation design provides a reusable pattern for structured cross-domain conditioning. However, the from-scratch FID gap relative to pre-trained baselines (e.g., 75.9 vs. 35.9 on KAIST) reveals that the framework still benefits substantially from large-scale infrared pre-training, leaving open whether the dual-domain structure can close this gap with sufficient data. It remains to be seen whether the decoupling strategy generalizes beyond V2IR to other cross-modal translation problems (e.g., visible-to-thermal video, multi-spectral fusion) or whether the deterministic flow assumption is always preferable to stochastic alternatives in these settings.
