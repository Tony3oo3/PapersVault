## Problem

Diffusion models lack an explicit low-dimensional latent space, making it nontrivial to define a Riemannian metric on the noise space that reflects the intrinsic geometry of the data manifold. Existing metrics based on sample density or the Fisher information matrix guide geodesics toward high-density regions, producing over-smoothed outputs that sacrifice perceptual detail. The paper asks whether a training-free metric can be constructed that keeps paths tangential to the manifold rather than pulling them toward the distribution center.

## Input / Output

For interpolation, the method takes a pair of clean RGB images (512×512), maps them to the noise space at a fixed time τ = 0.6T via DDIM inversion, computes a geodesic by optimizing N−1 intermediate points, and maps the result back to clean images. For guidance correction, it takes a text prompt and a pre-trained Stable Diffusion v2.1-base model, modifying only the CFG guidance term at each denoising step with four additional score evaluations per step. The metric itself requires no training or architectural changes—only the Jacobian of the already-trained score function.

## Method

The core contribution is a Riemannian metric g_{x_t}(v,w) = ⟨J_{x_t}v, J_{x_t}w⟩ on the noise space, defined as the pullback of the Euclidean metric through the score function s_θ, where J_{x_t} = ∇_{x_t} s_θ(x_t, t) is the score Jacobian. The key insight is that the spectral structure of J_{x_t} separates tangent directions (small singular values) from normal directions (large singular values) of the data manifold, so the metric assigns low cost to tangential movement and high cost to normal movement. For interpolation, the energy functional reduces to minimizing the sum of squared score changes along a discretized path, optimized with Adam over 500 iterations. For guidance correction, the CFG perturbation Δs is projected to suppress its normal component by solving (I + λG_{x_t})Δŝ* = Δs via a single conjugate-gradient step, where G_{x_t} = J_{x_t}^T J_{x_t} is applied through finite-difference Jacobian-vector products. This full-rank metric captures all tangent and normal directions, unlike the rank-1 FIM-based alternative, which is critical when the manifold codimension exceeds one.

## Results

On image interpolation (Stable Diffusion v2.1-base, τ=0.6T), the proposed metric achieves the best PPL, FID, and RE across all four datasets (MorphBench-A, MorphBench-M, CelebA-HQ, Animal Faces-HQ), e.g., FID of 21.01 on AF vs. 25.80 for the strongest baseline GeoDiff and 26.07 for SLERP. On video frame interpolation, it attains the lowest MSE and LPIPS on all three benchmarks (DAVIS, Pexels-Human, RealEstate10K), e.g., MSE of 2.771×10⁻³ on RE10K vs. 5.941×10⁻³ for GeoDiff. For guidance correction, the metric-based correction improves FID at every CFG scale (e.g., 16.04 vs. 17.28 for CFG at w=12.5) while preserving CLIP Score, and the gain transfers to a 4-step LCM-distilled student (FID 16.98 vs. 17.91).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most competitive baseline is GeoDiff (density-based geodesics [98]), which ranks second on nearly every metric but produces over-smoothed, detail-poor interpolations because density-based metrics pull paths toward high-density regions; the proposed metric outperforms it by 4–15% in FID and 5–10% in PPL across datasets. SLERP [81] is the best closed-form method but still trails the proposed metric by ~10% in FID on AF (26.07 vs. 21.01) and by ~9% in PPL. NAO [74] and NoiseDiff [102] perform poorly (FID >65 on AF) because their norm-adjustment heuristics alter the endpoints, yielding large reconstruction errors. The FIM-based metric [7] is nearly singular (rank-1) and produces cartoonish, non-smooth transitions, with FID of 59.11 on AF—nearly 3× worse than the proposed method. For guidance correction, CFG++ [17] does not consistently improve FID over vanilla CFG [34], whereas the proposed tangent-normal correction improves FID at all three CFG scales (w=5, 7.5, 12.5) while keeping CLIP Score unchanged, and the improvement is inherited by a 4-step LCM student at zero additional inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free pullback metric construction from VAEs and GANs (Arvanitidis et al. [5]) to diffusion models, where the noise space is full-dimensional rather than a low-dimensional latent code. The core geometric observation it builds on—that the score Jacobian's spectral gap separates tangent and normal directions of the data manifold—was established by Stanczuk et al. [85] and Ventura et al. [91], but those works used it for intrinsic dimension estimation rather than metric construction. The key departure from prior Riemannian metrics for diffusion (GeodesicDiffusion [98], FIM-based metric [7]) is that those methods encode density or a single rank-1 direction, whereas this paper uses the full Jacobian spectrum to keep geodesics tangential to the manifold rather than pulling them toward high-density regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The metric provides a principled, training-free geometric foundation for manifold-aware operations in diffusion models, validated through both global (interpolation) and local (guidance correction) properties. A practically important finding is that the guidance correction, which modifies only the teacher's guidance term, transfers to distilled student models (e.g., LCM) at zero additional inference cost, making it immediately deployable. Open questions include whether the metric generalizes to more recent architectures (e.g., flow-matching, rectified flow), whether the finite-difference Jacobian approximation remains accurate at scale, and whether the tangent-normal decomposition holds under strong conditioning or multi-modal prompts.
