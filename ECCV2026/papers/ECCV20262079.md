## Problem

Generating large-scale 3D driving scenes that are simultaneously multi-view consistent, scalable to large spatial extents, and photorealistic remains unsolved. Existing approaches either distill 2D image/video diffusion into 3D representations (sacrificing geometric coherence and restricting rendering to training views) or are confined to small-scale, object-centric generation. No prior framework jointly achieves persistent 3D consistency, arbitrary-extent scalability, and photorealistic novel-view rendering without per-scene optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a coarse semantic voxel grid (0.6 m voxel size) where each occupied voxel carries a discrete semantic label (road, sidewalk, building, vegetation, etc.) as its sole conditioning signal. It produces a Σ-Voxfield grid in which every occupied voxel stores exactly 20 colorized 3D surface points (position + RGB), forming a fixed-cardinality discrete token set spanning up to ~100,000 m². This 3D buffer is then rendered into photorealistic RGB images from arbitrary camera poses via a deferred diffusion renderer, supporting multi-camera rigs and free camera trajectories.

## Method

The core representation is the Σ-Voxfield grid: each voxel holds n=20 surface samples (3D position + RGB) ordered by distance to the voxel center, yielding a 6n-dimensional token that jointly encodes local geometry and appearance. A 1D DiT diffusion model with masked attention (3 m neighborhood radius) denoises these tokens, conditioned on semantic labels and 3D sinusoidal positional encodings, trained with sample prediction and classifier-free guidance. Large scenes are synthesized by progressive spatial outpainting: the Repaint scheduler fixes already-generated voxels and denoises only new target regions in overlapping local sets, keeping per-step compute constant regardless of total scene size. A deferred rendering stage uses a modified Stable Diffusion UNet conditioned on the 2DGS projection of the Σ-Voxfield grid plus a sky/background visibility mask to produce photorealistic frames, with autoregressive or video-diffusion variants for temporal consistency.

## Results

On rendered-view image quality (Table 2), SEM-ROVER (ASD) achieves FID of 81.98 (seen) and 89.20 (novel), outperforming InfiniCube (84.14 / 99.13) and GEN3C (113.27 / 117.63). KID scores are competitive: 0.05 (seen) and 0.06 (novel), matching InfiniCube on novel views while InfiniCube is slightly better on seen views (0.03). The method requires only 8 GB VRAM versus 75 GB for InfiniCube and 43 GB for GEN3C, with a generation runtime of ~20 minutes for a large-scale scene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InfiniCube [15], SEM-ROVER achieves lower FID on both seen (81.98 vs 84.14) and novel views (89.20 vs 99.13), indicating more geometrically consistent novel-view rendering, while InfiniCube retains a slight KID advantage on seen views (0.03 vs 0.05); however, SEM-ROVER uses only 8 GB VRAM compared to InfiniCube's 75 GB. Against GEN3C [13], SEM-ROVER dominates on all four FID/KID metrics (e.g., FID-novel 89.20 vs 117.63) and requires less than half the memory (8 GB vs 43 GB). For the deferred-rendering conditioning ablation (Table 4), the proposed Σ-Voxfield projection yields FVD of 53.01, substantially outperforming semantic-map conditioning in the UniScene [11] style (FVD 143.62) and projected LiDAR conditioning from FreeVS [27] (FVD 97.51), confirming that jointly encoding geometry and appearance in a single 3D buffer is critical for rendering quality and temporal consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of distilling 2D image/video diffusion models into 3D representations (as in GEN3C, InfiniCube, MagicDrive3D) by generating a persistent 3D surface representation directly in 3D space. It builds on the Repaint diffusion scheduler for spatial outpainting and on Stable Diffusion as the deferred rendering backbone, but introduces a novel Σ-Voxfield token representation and a 1D DiT diffusion model operating on local voxel neighborhoods with 3D positional encodings. Unlike prior 3D diffusion work (TRELLIS, L3DG, GaussianCube) that targets object-centric or room-scale generation, SEM-ROVER explicitly addresses large-extent outdoor scenes by decoupling per-step compute from scene size through progressive outpainting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8 GB VRAM requirement (vs. 75 GB for InfiniCube) and feed-forward inference make large-scale driving scene generation accessible on consumer hardware, which is practically significant for simulation and data-synthesis pipelines. The ability to perform semantic editing and voxel-space inpainting on a persistent 3D buffer opens a path toward controllable scene manipulation without per-scene optimization. Key limitations remain: the method generates only static scenes (no dynamic agents or temporal evolution), and appearance control is limited to semantic-level conditioning, leaving texture style, lighting, and material properties unaddressed. Extending the framework to dynamic content and richer appearance control are the most natural next steps.
