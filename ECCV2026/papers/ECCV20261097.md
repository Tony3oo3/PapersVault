## Problem

Existing visual analogy methods adapt text-to-image models with a single LoRA module, which must capture the entire diverse space of visual transformations (style transfer, object insertion, pose changes, etc.) within one fixed adapter. This bottleneck limits generalization to unseen transformation types, as the single module cannot flexibly specialize to each specific analogy task at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261097/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an image triplet {a, a', b} and a guiding text prompt. The three images are separately encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT (each resized to 224×224) for LoRA selection, while the flow model receives them as a 2×2 composite grid (a, a' in the top row; b in the bottom-left) via extended attention. The output is the edited image b' occupying the bottom-right quadrant of the generated composite, trained at up to 512×512 resolution.

## Method

LoRWeB maintains a learnable basis of N=32 rank-4 LoRA adapters, each associated with a learnable key vector. A lightweight encoder (frozen CLIP ViT + a single linear projection) takes the concatenated CLIP features of {a, a', b} to produce a query vector, which is matched against the keys via scaled dot-product attention with softmax to yield N mixing coefficients. The final LoRA is the weighted sum of the basis LoRAs, injected into Flux.1-Kontext at each targeted weight matrix. Crucially, the basis LoRAs and the routing encoder are jointly trained end-to-end, so the model learns both what transformation primitives to represent and how to compose them per input, requiring only a single forward pass at inference with no test-time optimization. A single rank-r' LoRA is a special case where the router collapses to constant weights, making LoRWeB strictly more expressive.

## Results

On 840 analogy triplets across 100 unseen tasks, LoRWeB (N=32, r=4) achieves a VLM-based Preservation score of 7.87 and Edit Accuracy of 5.94, with LPIPS of 0.31 and CLIP directional similarity of 0.21. In pairwise VLM preference, LoRWeB is favored over EditTransfer 70.4% of the time, over VisualCloze 68.1%, over RelationAdapter 58.5%, and over a standard r=128 LoRA 57.9%. A user study with 33 raters over 45 image pairs confirms LoRWeB is preferred over all baselines, and the method pushes the Pareto front of edit accuracy vs. input preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRWeB outperforms all four main baselines in pairwise VLM preference: it beats EditTransfer [8] by the largest margin (70.4%), followed by VisualCloze [31] (68.1%), RelationAdapter [17] (58.5%), and a standard Flux.1-Kontext LoRA of equivalent parameter capacity (57.9%). The user study (33 raters, 45 pairs) confirms these VLM rankings, with LoRWeB preferred over every baseline. Qualitatively, baselines struggle with subject identity preservation (e.g., RelationAdapter alters the cat's appearance, VisualCloze changes the woman's face) and fail to capture fine transformation details (e.g., the exact crown design), whereas LoRWeB maintains both content fidelity and transformation accuracy. Additional comparisons against Diffusion Image Analogies [53] and PairEdit [36] (both per-sample optimization methods) are reported in the appendix, where LoRWeB also achieves superior results while requiring only a single forward pass at inference (33.4 s vs. minutes for per-sample methods). The ablation further shows that the large basis size (N=32) is critical: reducing to N=8 with higher rank (r=16) drops pairwise preference against RelationAdapter from 58.5% to 49.6%, confirming that spanning a diverse LoRA basis—not merely increasing total parameters—drives generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on FLUX.1-Kontext's extended-attention mechanism for in-context image editing and the parameter-efficient LoRA framework, but departs from prior visual analogy methods (RelationAdapter, VisualCloze, EditTransfer) that rely on a single LoRA adapter to capture all transformations. The key conceptual departure is inspired by Dravid et al.'s finding that independently trained LoRAs span an interpretable semantic weight space, but LoRWeB instead learns a joint basis of LoRAs simultaneously with a lightweight CLIP-based router, avoiding the need for 65,000 independently trained adapters or per-sample test-time optimization. This positions the work at the intersection of mixture-of-experts routing and weight-space interpolation, applied specifically to the visual analogy completion task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LoRA-basis decomposition offers a practical and scalable alternative to both single-adapter fine-tuning (limited generalization) and per-sample optimization (expensive inference), with minimal overhead (33.4s vs 32.4s for a single LoRA). The finding that a basis of small-rank adapters (N=32, r=4) outperforms a single large-rank adapter (r=128) on unseen transformations suggests that expressiveness in the *space* of adapters matters more than raw parameter count. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes beyond analogy editing to other flexible manipulation tasks, how to handle reference pairs that don't cleanly isolate a single transformation, and whether the basis can be decomposed into interpretable transformation components for interactive control.
