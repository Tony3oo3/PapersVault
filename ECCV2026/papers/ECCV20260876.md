## Problem

Existing RGB-D camouflaged object detection methods fuse RGB and depth features under unified mask supervision, causing both modalities to converge toward homogeneous representations and lose their complementary discriminative advantages. The visual domain inherently conceals camouflaged objects through low contrast and texture similarity, while depth maps offer salient spatial and geometric priors, yet prior fusion strategies fail to preserve modality-specific cues during multi-modality integration.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (3×H×W) and a corresponding monocular depth map (1×H×W), both resized to 384×384, with depth maps generated by Depth Anything V2 (or DPT for ablation). A shared VMamba-S or Swin-S encoder produces 4-level pyramid features for both modalities. The output is a set of three segmentation masks from a triple decoder (RGB-only, depth-only, and fused RGB-D), all supervised against the ground-truth camouflaged object mask.

## Method

VCP-DCN employs a progressive three-stage multi-modality strategy embedded at each pyramid level. The Separable Prototype Embedding (SPE) module decouples RGB/depth features into foreground and background tokens, then projects them via MLPs into modality-consistency and modality-specific prototype tokens, optimized by a novel prototype contrastive loss (L_MCL, L_MSL, L_RCL, L_DCL) that simultaneously pulls cross-modal consistency prototypes together, pushes modality-specific prototypes apart, and separates foreground from background prototypes. The Multi-modality Dual Attention (MDA) module generates foreground/background response maps by dot-producting modality-consistency prototypes with spatial features, then performs cross-modal masked linear attention by exchanging these masks between RGB and depth branches to enhance each modality with the other's semantic cues. The Depth Adaptive Injection (DAI) module computes cosine similarity between each modality's specific prototype and the averaged consistency prototype to derive adaptive fusion weights, decomposing the fused output into modality-consistency and modality-specific components combined via a 3×3 deformable convolution. Training uses a total loss combining hybrid mask loss (BCE + IoU + SSIM) from the triple decoder and the alignment contrastive loss, with λ=0.3 and μ=0.1 balancing the two terms.

## Results

With the Swin backbone and Depth Anything V2 pseudo-depth, VCP-DCN achieves S_m=0.899, E_φ=0.951, F_β^w=0.871, and M=0.033 on CAMO; S_m=0.880, E_φ=0.947, F_β^w=0.932, and M=0.017 on COD10K; and S_m=0.898, E_φ=0.944, F_β^w=0.869, and M=0.028 on NC4K. With the VMamba backbone and Depth Anything V2, it reaches S_m=0.892, F_β^w=0.871 on CAMO and F_β^w=0.930, M=0.017 on COD10K. The method uses only 60.2M parameters and 46.4G FLOPs, the smallest among compared methods, while outperforming all RGB-D baselines across all three COD benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Samba (CVPR25, same VMamba backbone, Depth Anything V2), VCP-DCN wins on every metric across all three datasets: on COD10K it improves F_β^w from 0.842 to 0.930 (+8.8 points) and M from 0.019 to 0.017; on CAMO it gains S_m from 0.883 to 0.892 and F_β^w from 0.863 to 0.871. Against DaCOD (MM23, Swin backbone, Depth Anything V2), VCP-DCN achieves substantially larger margins, e.g., F_β^w jumps from 0.795 to 0.932 on COD10K and M drops from 0.042 to 0.033 on CAMO. Compared to the VFM-based SAM-COD (ICCV25, SAM backbone, DPT depth), VCP-DCN with VMamba+DA V2 still leads on all metrics (e.g., F_β^w 0.930 vs 0.817 on COD10K) while using fewer parameters and FLOPs. Against top RGB-only methods such as FSEL (ECCV24) and MCRNet (IJCV25), VCP-DCN with Depth Anything V2 surpasses them on all three COD benchmarks, confirming that the depth collaborative strategy adds value beyond RGB-only detection. On the secondary RGB-D SOD task (Table 2), VCP-DCN also outperforms all compared methods on NJU2K, NLPR, and DUT, with F_β^w of 0.929/0.925/0.957 and M of 0.023/0.015/0.016 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the line of RGB-D COD methods (PopNet, DaCOD, RISNet, DSAM, SAM-COD) that fuse RGB and depth features indiscriminately under unified mask supervision, which the authors diagnose as causing 'feature optimization bias'—a convergence of both modalities toward homogeneous representations that erases their complementary advantages. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, VCP-DCN repurposes prototype contrastive learning (previously applied in representation learning and anomaly detection) as a mechanism to explicitly preserve modality-specific discriminability during multi-modal fusion. The progressive alignment–interaction–fusion pipeline (SPE → MDA → DAI) is a new architectural formulation, though each component draws on established ideas: global average pooling for prototype extraction, masked linear attention for cross-modal interaction, and deformable convolution for adaptive fusion. The use of Depth Anything V2 for pseudo-depth generation (replacing DPT in prior RGB-D COD work) and VMamba as a shared encoder also represent practical updates to the experimental setup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic of modality homogenization under unified supervision is a useful and generalizable observation for the broader multi-modal segmentation community, and the prototype contrastive loss offers a lightweight mechanism to counteract it without requiring separate modality-specific decoders. However, the performance gains over the strongest same-backbone baseline (Samba with Depth Anything V2) are modest—roughly 0.5–1% on S_m and F_β across datasets—raising the question of whether the added architectural complexity (three new modules per pyramid level) is justified. Open questions include whether the prototype contrastive strategy transfers to other multi-modal tasks (e.g., RGB-D semantic segmentation, RGB-thermal detection), whether the depth map quality improvement from Depth Anything V2 accounts for a disproportionate share of the gains, and whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or video-based COD settings.
