## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting represents scenes as symmetric density blobs, which fundamentally conflicts with the asymmetric nature of orientable surfaces and makes principled surface extraction intractable. Existing methods resort to ad-hoc heuristics like TSDF fusion of blended depth maps without a well-defined geometric field, leading to surface erosion, missing thin structures, and non-watertight meshes. Additionally, standard evaluation protocols on benchmarks like Tanks and Temples contain systematic biases that reward denser tessellations and penalize correct reconstruction of occluded geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes multiview RGB images with known camera poses as input and produces a complete, watertight, textured 3D mesh of the full scene (including background geometry and extremely thin structures like bicycle spokes). Beyond the final mesh, the method exposes a view-independent occupancy field and a closed-form normal field at arbitrary spatial locations, both derived directly from the oriented Gaussian parameters without additional learnable components. Mesh resolution is decoupled from the Gaussian distribution via Primal Adaptive Meshing, enabling region-of-interest extraction at arbitrary detail levels.

## Method

The core contribution is reinterpreting each 3D Gaussian as an oriented probabilistic surface element that models density decay only on the outward-facing half-space, parameterized by a learnable unit normal vector. This oriented attenuation satisfies the reciprocity condition required by the Objects as Volumes framework, yielding closed-form expressions for both the occupancy (vacancy) and normal fields at any point in space. Training enforces a 'wrapping' behavior through a normal alignment loss (aligning rendered normals with depth gradients) and a normal-aware densification strategy that clones high-error Gaussians with flipped normals to close geometric holes. For mesh extraction, the method offers two complementary pipelines: a lightweight Pivot-based Marching Tetrahedra using only 2 pivots per Gaussian (vs. 9 in prior work), and a Primal Adaptive Meshing procedure that refines vertices onto the 0.5-isosurface via Newton updates and extracts a Delaunay-based watertight mesh at controllable resolution.

## Results

On Tanks & Temples, Gaussian Wrapping (2 pivots) achieves a mean F1 of 0.48 under Uniform Sampling and 0.53 under Virtual Scanning, setting a new state-of-the-art among full-scene methods (vs. GGGS 0.45/0.53, MILo 0.34/0.42, GOF 0.31/0.38). On Mip-NeRF 360, the method attains the best MBR PSNR of 25.10 dB, SSIM of 0.752, and LPIPS of 0.289 among full-scene extraction methods, while using only 4.0M Gaussians and 11.79M vertices. On DTU, the method achieves competitive Chamfer Distance comparable to GGGS without the surface erosion artifacts observed in that baseline. Training time is 27 minutes per scene, competitive with GOF (69 min) and MILo (150 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among full-scene extraction methods, Gaussian Wrapping (2p) outperforms GGGS by 0.03 F1 on Uniform Sampling (0.48 vs. 0.45) and ties on Virtual Scanning (0.53 vs. 0.53), while producing meshes with substantially fewer vertices (5.80M vs. 21.81M on T&T). Against MILo, the gap is larger: 0.48 vs. 0.34 (Uniform) and 0.53 vs. 0.42 (Virtual), with Ours also achieving better MBR PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360 (25.10 vs. 25.08). GOF, SOF, and RaDe-GS trail by 0.10–0.17 F1 on both protocols. PGSR scores higher (0.52/0.53) but the authors attribute this to an evaluation artifact: its TSDF-based extraction produces non-watertight meshes whose holes coincide with ground-truth scan gaps, inflating F1 under the legacy protocol; under Virtual Scanning this advantage disappears. On MBR rendering metrics, Ours achieves the best PSNR (25.10 on Mip-NeRF 360; 20.98 on T&T) and lowest LPIPS (0.289; 0.3674) among all full-scene methods, confirming that the geometric gains translate to rendering fidelity. The method also outperforms foreground-only baselines 2DGS and PGSR on DTU Chamfer Distance, and the Gaussian Wrapping regularizer improves RaDe-GS by +0.09 mean Virtual Scan F1 when plugged in as a drop-in module.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets 3D Gaussian Splatting primitives through the lens of the Objects as Volumes (OaV) stochastic geometry framework, which previously applied only to attenuation-based volumetric rendering (NeRF, NeuS). The key conceptual departure is assigning each Gaussian a learnable oriented normal, transforming symmetric density 'blobs' into half-space surface elements that yield closed-form occupancy and normal fields—something prior 3DGS-based surface methods (GOF, GGGS, PGSR) could not achieve without resorting to heuristic TSDF fusion. For mesh extraction, the paper builds on GOF's Pivot-based Marching Tetrahedra but reduces it from 9 to 2 pivots per Gaussian, and introduces a novel Primal Adaptive Meshing stage drawing from Delaunay-based reconstruction literature (Amenta, Dey, Zhang et al.) to decouple mesh resolution from Gaussian distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The oriented-Gaussian formulation is architecture-agnostic (validated as a plug-in to RaDe-GS) and enables recovery of extremely thin structures like bicycle spokes that all prior 3DGS-based methods fail on, while producing watertight meshes at roughly half the vertex count of competitors. The paper's critique of standard T&T evaluation protocols—showing that vertex-density bias and GT laser-scan artifacts can inflate scores by 20+ points (e.g., PGSR's apparent superiority is an artifact)—is broadly useful for the field. Open questions include whether the approach extends to specular/transparent regions, whether the Gaussian vector field can be directly supervised to recover a local vacancy, and whether more principled sampling strategies for Primal Adaptive Meshing can handle arbitrarily fine geometry.
