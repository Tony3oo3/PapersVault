## Problem

Existing neural avatar rendering methods model garment appearance as functions of current or recent skeletal poses, ignoring the temporal causality inherent in cloth physics where current cloth states emerge from previous states through evolution. This one-to-many mapping—where identical poses correspond to different cloth states depending on motion history—causes over-smoothed appearance, temporal flickering, and severe artifacts on out-of-distribution poses. The paper identifies that resolving this ambiguity requires modeling both observable kinematic information (surface configuration and motion) and implicit internal state (fabric tension, deformation history), neither of which is captured by skeletal poses alone.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB video from 8 calibrated cameras along with SMPL-X skeletal pose parameters, from which it derives UV-unwrapped position maps, velocity maps, and the previous frame's predicted Gaussian center deformation Δμ_{t-1} as network inputs over a temporal window of n=10 frames at 512×512 UV resolution. The output is a set of frame-specific deformations ΔG_t = {Δμ_t, Δq_t, Δs_t, Δα_t, Δc_t} applied to a fixed ~200k-Gaussian template in canonical space, which are then transformed to posed space via Linear Blend Skinning and rendered through differentiable 3D Gaussian splatting at 940×1280 resolution.

## Method

DSAR employs a dual-stream autoregressive architecture on a learnable Gaussian template: the geometric stream conditions on the previous frame's predicted deformation Δμ_{t-1} and aggregates multi-scale StyleUNet features via Motion-Adaptive Temporal Aggregation (MATA), which uses motion-magnitude-guided causal self-attention at the deepest pyramid level and lightweight convolutions at finer levels to handle spatially heterogeneous cloth dynamics. The state stream maintains a memory bank of M=10 historical temporal states and fuses them with current geometric features through cross-attention with a learnable balancing parameter, capturing implicit internal state not recoverable from geometry alone. Training is supervised by multi-view photometric losses (L1, SSIM, LPIPS) combined with a novel motion-magnitude-aware adaptive temporal regularization that applies spatially varying weights—enforcing strong consistency in static regions while permitting flexibility in high-motion regions—across three hierarchical levels (feature smoothness, prediction consistency, and cross-frame position acceleration).

## Results

On the 4D-DRESS and AvatarREX benchmarks (evaluated at 940×1280 resolution), DSAR achieves 31.08 PSNR, 0.9839 SSIM, and 0.0305 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline Animatable-GS (29.51 PSNR, 0.9724 SSIM, 0.0413 LPIPS) by +1.56 dB in PSNR and −0.0108 in LPIPS. The gap over the NeRF-based HumanNeRF baseline is substantially larger at +11.02 dB PSNR. Ablation on far-distribution (MMD > 0.6) test sequences shows the full model retains 30.67 PSNR while removing the geometric stream drops performance to 24.27 PSNR, confirming the critical role of explicit temporal causality for out-of-distribution generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSAR outperforms all three baselines across every metric. Against the closest competitor Animatable-GS [31] (also 3DGS-based with StyleUNet deformation), DSAR gains +1.56 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.0413 to 0.0305, attributed to explicit autoregressive geometric feedback and memory-based state retrieval rather than pose-only conditioning. Against GaussianAvatar [17], the improvement is +4.85 dB PSNR and −0.0410 LPIPS, reflecting the advantage of temporal modeling over single-frame pose-driven deformation. The NeRF-based HumanNeRF [67] lags far behind at 20.06 PSNR, consistent with known limitations of implicit representations for high-frequency cloth details. Qualitatively, DSAR captures momentum-driven cloth settling after rapid motion (e.g., post-rotation jacket swinging) where all baselines produce over-smoothed or flickering artifacts, and generalizes more robustly to out-of-distribution AMASS motions (sports, dancing, acrobatics) that differ substantially from training sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Animatable-GS paradigm (learnable canonical Gaussian template + per-frame deformations + LBS) by replacing the instantaneous pose-to-appearance mapping with a dual-stream autoregressive architecture. The geometric stream introduces autoregressive conditioning on the previous frame's predicted deformation Δμ_{t-1} with motion-adaptive causal attention, while the state stream adds a memory bank with cross-attention to capture implicit internal state—both absent from prior methods that model appearance solely as F(pose_{t-n:t}). The adaptive temporal regularization with motion-magnitude-aware weighting departs from uniform smoothness constraints used in prior GS-based avatar methods, and the MMD-based distribution divergence quantification provides a more rigorous OOD evaluation protocol than prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled decomposition of cloth dynamics into observable kinematics and implicit internal state, with ablations showing that the geometric stream is especially critical for out-of-distribution generalization (PSNR drops ~6.4 dB on FD poses without it, vs ~2.7 dB on ND). This validates that explicit temporal causality modeling, rather than pose-appearance correlation learning, is what enables robust generalization to unseen motion patterns. Limitations include per-subject optimization requiring multi-view capture, dependence on SMPL-X body model, and evaluation limited to two datasets with relatively short sequences; open questions include whether the memory bank scales to very long sequences, whether single-view or in-the-wild settings are feasible, and whether the dual-stream decomposition generalizes to non-cloth deformations like hair or accessories.
