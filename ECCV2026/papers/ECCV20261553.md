## Problem

Traditional image aesthetic assessment methods regress absolute Mean Opinion Scores, overlooking the inherently comparative nature of human aesthetic perception, which relies on implicit reference anchors and causal reasoning about visual differences. This lack of comparative supervision prevents models from learning generalizable aesthetic principles, causing poor transfer across diverse visual domains and content categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261553/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single RGB image (resized to 448×448) and produces a scalar aesthetic score in [0,1] accompanied by a chain-of-thought textual explanation covering visual analysis and aesthetic reasoning. During training, the model consumes source-edit image pairs or source image plus editing instruction, predicting the relative aesthetic difference (δ) rather than an absolute score, with the absolute score format only introduced during a brief 200-sample calibration stage.

## Method

RED-Aes is a three-stage training framework built on Qwen2.5-VL/Qwen3-VL backbones. Stage 1 performs supervised fine-tuning on the RED-20k dataset (constructed via 5 controllable editing models and 3 VLM judges with consensus filtering) using two joint objectives: contrastive comparison (predicting aesthetic difference with CoT from source-edit pairs) and edit-causal reasoning (predicting aesthetic impact from an editing instruction without observing the edited image). Stage 2 is a lightweight 200-sample cold-start calibration to align output format to absolute scores. Stage 3 applies GRPO with a novel Relative Ranking Consistency Reward—a soft Kendall concordance function combining a tanh gap-aware weight and a sigmoid directional agreement term—that enforces ordinal consistency among 8 images (1 source + 7 edited variants) per group, optimizing the model solely via relative supervision without any absolute score targets during reinforcement learning.

## Results

Under a zero-shot cross-domain setting (trained exclusively on the proposed RED-20k dataset), RED-Aes-7B achieves an average PLCC of 0.7442 and SRCC of 0.7322 across five public benchmarks (TAD66K, AVA, FLICKR-AES, PARA, AADB), setting a new state of the art. Even the lightweight RED-Aes-2B variant (avg. SRCC 0.7042) surpasses all baselines, including GPT-5 (avg. SRCC 0.5679) and the strongest specialist model Aes-R1-7B (avg. SRCC 0.6186). The method also outperforms a zero-shot multi-expert voting ensemble of three large VLMs (avg. SRCC 0.6743) while being deployable on-device.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RED-Aes-7B outperforms all nine baselines on every benchmark. Against the strongest generalist VLM, GPT-5 (avg. SRCC 0.5679), RED-Aes-7B gains +16.4 SRCC points; against Qwen3-VL-32B (0.5643), the gap is +16.8 points. Among specialist IAA models, Aes-R1-7B (avg. SRCC 0.6186) is the closest competitor, yet RED-Aes-7B still leads by +11.4 SRCC points, with particularly large margins on TAD66K (+10.7) and AADB (+23.2). UniPercept-8B (0.5247) and NIMA (0.4885) trail by even wider margins. Notably, RED-Aes-7B also surpasses the Multi-Expert Voting ensemble of three large VLMs (0.6743) by +5.8 SRCC points, and even the 2B variant (0.7042) exceeds this ensemble, demonstrating that the relative edit-induced learning paradigm distills aesthetic reasoning into compact models more effectively than brute-force ensembling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant IAA paradigm of regressing absolute MOS scores (NIMA, TANet, MUSIQ, and VLM-based successors like Q-Align, Aes-R1, ArtiMuse) by reframing aesthetic assessment as a relative, edit-induced difference problem. It draws a direct conceptual lineage from RankIQA in the IQA domain, which improved generalization via synthetic distortion pairs, but extends this to aesthetics by leveraging controllable diffusion-based editing models (Qwen-Image-Edit, FLUX.1 Kontext, Seedream, etc.) to create semantically meaningful source-edit pairs rather than pixel-level distortions. The training pipeline builds on GRPO from DeepSeekMath, but introduces a novel Relative Ranking Consistency Reward that optimizes ordinal agreement within source-edit groups rather than individual response quality or absolute score accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm shift from absolute to relative aesthetic supervision is grounded in cognitive science (human aesthetic judgment is inherently comparative) and yields substantial, well-ablated gains across five benchmarks, suggesting the field's reliance on absolute MOS regression may have been a limiting factor for generalization. The RED-20k dataset, with its editing-based pairs, quantitative differences, and CoT reasoning, is the first resource of its kind and could catalyze further work on causal aesthetic reasoning. Open questions include whether the approach's heavy dependence on large VLMs and multiple editing models for data construction limits reproducibility, whether the causal reasoning truly transfers to unseen aesthetic dimensions, and whether the relative paradigm extends to other perceptual tasks beyond aesthetics.
