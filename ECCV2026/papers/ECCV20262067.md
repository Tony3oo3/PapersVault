## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian editing methods treat editing as a one-shot, static pipeline: a single user instruction is mapped to a fixed sequence of operations producing a final result in one pass. This formulation assumes user intent is fully specified and static, leaving systems unable to handle ambiguous, evolving, or multi-stage instructions, lacking interactive feedback loops, and providing insufficient 3D spatial reasoning for multi-object coordination and long-horizon coherence.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a reconstructed 3D Gaussian scene along with a sequence of text instructions delivered over multiple interaction turns, optionally augmented with reference images or click-and-drag spatial cues. Rather than producing a single edited output, AGE iteratively updates the scene state over T discrete steps, with each step conditioned on the current Gaussian state, the latest instruction, and a structured memory that accumulates editing history, perceptual observations, and geometric constraints. The final output is a coherently edited 3D Gaussian scene that preserves structural integrity in unedited regions while satisfying the cumulative intent across all turns.

## Method

AGE reformulates 3D Gaussian editing as a long-horizon sequential decision-making process orchestrated by four specialized agents: a Planner (Qwen3-VL-30B) that performs adaptive perceptual view selection and decomposes instructions into atomic skill invocations, an Executor (DeepSeek-R1-32B) that maps atomic instructions to parameterized tool calls from a modular Skill Library, a Reflector that evaluates intermediate results and decides between convergence, re-planning, or targeted re-execution, and a Backtracker that identifies optimal re-entry points and generates corrective parameter adjustments. A Structured Spatially-Aware Memory with three hierarchical components—Systematic Spatial Information (viewport priors, geometric constraints), Turn-level Context (high-level captions and per-step execution logs), and a Global Metadata Repository (indexed 3D Gaussian parameters at each step)—maintains persistent scene state across turns, enabling backtracking and long-term coherence. The Skill Library decouples high-level reasoning from low-level execution by defining each skill with functional metadata, an execution interface, reasoning guidance for MLLMs, and self-correction support for the Backtracker, making the framework extensible to new editing capabilities without architectural changes.

## Results

On the AGE Benchmark (57 scenarios), AGE achieves a CLIP Text-Image Direction Similarity (C_sim) of 0.192, CLIP Direction Consistency (C_con) of 0.907, and Image Aesthetics Assessment (IAA) of 6.65, outperforming both baselines on all three metrics. In the LLM-as-judge evaluation (Gemini 3.1 Pro, 1–10 scale), AGE scores 8.90 in structural consistency of non-edited regions, 7.17 in instruction completion degree, and 8.52 in overall aesthetic quality. The largest relative gains appear in C_sim (+44% over DGE, +85% over GaussianEditor) and structural consistency (+20% over DGE, +70% over GaussianEditor), reflecting the benefit of the structured memory and multi-agent reflection loop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGE is compared against two representative pipeline-based 3D Gaussian editing methods: GaussianEditor [7] (CVPR 2024) and DGE [6] (2024). AGE wins on every reported metric against both baselines. Against GaussianEditor, the gains are substantial: C_sim improves from 0.104 to 0.192 (+85%), IAA from 4.51 to 6.65 (+47%), and LLM-judge structural consistency from 5.24 to 8.90 (+70%), indicating that the one-shot pipeline suffers severe background degradation and incomplete instruction execution. Against DGE, which is the stronger baseline, AGE still leads in C_sim (0.192 vs 0.133, +44%), C_con (0.907 vs 0.892), IAA (6.65 vs 5.79), and all three LLM-judge dimensions (8.90 vs 7.39 structural, 7.17 vs 5.23 completion, 8.52 vs 8.03 aesthetic). The most notable advantage over DGE is in instruction completion degree (7.17 vs 5.23), which the authors attribute to the multi-agent planning and backtracking mechanism handling complex, multi-step, and ambiguous instructions that single-pass pipelines cannot fully execute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pipeline-based paradigm in 3D Gaussian editing (GaussianEditor, DGE, and related methods) that maps a single instruction to a fixed sequence of operations in one pass. It repurposes the multi-agent LLM architecture literature—planner-executor decomposition, memory-augmented agents, and reflection/backtracking loops—and embeds it directly into the 3D Gaussian editing loop, treating editing as a long-horizon sequential decision process over a persistent scene state. The atomic editing tools (SAM, FLUX, InstructPix2Pix) are existing off-the-shelf models; the novelty lies in the orchestration layer, the structured spatially-aware memory, and the closed-loop feedback between planning, execution, reflection, and backtracking agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured memory with backtracking addresses a genuine gap in interactive 3D editing: current systems cannot recover from errors or maintain coherence across multiple turns, which limits their practical utility in iterative creative workflows. However, the evaluation is limited to 57 scenarios and only two baselines, and the authors themselves identify an execution bottleneck where low-level tools (e.g., 3D asset generation) constrain the system more than the planning logic does. Open questions include whether the multi-agent overhead is justified for simpler single-step edits, how the framework scales to very long interaction chains, and whether end-to-end fine-tuning of agents on 3D geometry (rather than 2D visual proxies) would close the remaining gap between planning quality and execution fidelity.
