## Problem

Large-scale, high-quality datasets for instruction-guided video editing (IVE) remain scarce compared to their image-editing counterparts, with existing datasets (InsViE-1M, Senorita-2M, Ditto-1M) suffering from limited scale (1–2M samples), few edit types (3–6), very short instructions (~4 words), and a high proportion of low-quality pairs. Additionally, no unified benchmark exists for IVE that is well-aligned with human judgment, making fair cross-method comparison difficult.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a source video clip (65–129 frames at 1280×720) together with a natural-language editing instruction (average 40.6 words) and produces a temporally consistent edited video at the same resolution. The dataset spans eight edit categories divided into spatially-aligned edits (Global Style, Background Change, Local Change/Remove/Add, Subtitles Edit) that preserve original motion, and non-spatially-aligned edits (Camera Multi-Shot, Creative Edit) that introduce new subject motion. The OpenVE-Edit model is trained in two stages: 480P for one epoch, then fine-tuned at 720P, using Wan2.2-TI2V-5B as the diffusion backbone.

## Method

OpenVE-Edit is a 5B-parameter model combining a MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B), a task-aware MoE-Connector, and a Diffusion Transformer (DiT). The MLLM jointly encodes the input video and editing instruction to capture higher-level spatio-temporal semantics beyond what a text-only encoder (umT5) provides, while discarding prefix tokens to focus on edit-relevant content. The MoE-Connector uses learnable queries (length 512) to extract task-specific features from MLLM hidden states, routing them through sparse expert sub-networks (6 experts, top-2 active) that specialize on different edit types; its final linear layer is zero-initialized (ControlNet-style) so the module starts as an identity and gradually learns to contribute, stabilizing early training. The processed MoE features are concatenated with umT5 instruction tokens and fed via cross-attention into the DiT, which denoises the VAE-encoded video latent. The 3M training pairs are generated through a three-stage pipeline: (1) pre-processing with MLLM descriptions, Grounded SAM2 segmentation, and Canny/depth extraction; (2) taxonomy-guided pair generation using FLUX-Kontext for first-frame editing plus Wan2.1-Fun-V1.1-14B-Control for video synthesis under Canny guidance; and (3) VLM-based filtering (InternVL3.5-38B) retaining only pairs scoring ≥3 on instruction compliance, consistency, and visual quality.

## Results

On OpenVE-Bench, OpenVE-Edit (5B parameters, 1280×704) achieves an overall score of 2.41, surpassing all open-source baselines including 14B models (DITTO: 2.06, VideoCoF: 2.24, ICVE: 2.25). The model also achieves the best temporal quality metrics among competitive methods (TF: 0.9889, CLIP-F: 0.9698), while the closed-source Runway Aleph leads with an overall score of 3.50. OpenVE-Edit shows particular strength in Background Change (2.72) and Local Change (3.19), but remains weakest on Camera Multi-Shot Edit (1.24) and Local Remove (1.42).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenVE-Edit (5B) outperforms all open-source baselines on overall score: it beats the closest 14B competitors ICVE (2.25) and VideoCoF (2.24) by +0.16 and +0.17 respectively, and DITTO (2.06) by +0.35. Against same-size 5B models, it surpasses Lucy-Edit (1.95) by +0.46 and Señorita (1.90) by +0.51. The model is particularly strong in Background Change (2.72 vs. 2.20 for Lucy-Edit) and Subtitles Edit (2.56 vs. 1.06 for Lucy-Edit), but trails VideoCoF on Local Remove (1.42 vs. 3.26) and Lucy-Edit on Local Change (3.19 vs. 3.30). The closed-source Runway Aleph remains substantially ahead at 3.50 overall, especially on Camera Multi-Shot Edit (4.48 vs. 1.24). On temporal quality, OpenVE-Edit achieves the best TF (0.9889) and CLIP-F (0.9698) among all competitive methods, while OmniVideo has slightly better temporal scores but fails to follow instructions (overall 1.02).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions instruction-guided video editing (IVE) as the natural successor to the now-mature instruction-guided image editing (IIE) field, arguing that the data infrastructure for video lags far behind. It departs from prior IVE datasets (InsViE-1M, Señorita-2M, Ditto-1M) by introducing a two-level taxonomy (spatially-aligned vs. non-spatially-aligned edits) and substantially longer instructions (40.6 words average vs. 3.6–17.1), while repurposing components from image editing (FLUX-Kontext), video generation (Wan2.1/2.2), and MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL) into a unified pipeline. The model architecture draws on ControlNet-style zero-initialization for training stability and adapts MoE routing to handle heterogeneous editing tasks, a design choice not previously applied to video editing connectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3M-scale dataset and 431-pair benchmark fill a critical gap: prior open-source IVE resources were too small, too narrow in edit types, and too low in quality to support competitive model training or fair comparison. The finding that a 5B model trained on high-quality data outperforms 14B models trained on noisier data (2.41 vs. 2.25/2.06) suggests that data curation quality may matter more than raw parameter count in this domain. Open questions remain around unifying SA and NSA edits in a single architecture, incorporating reference-based style transfer, and exploring whether bidirectional synergy between understanding, generation, and editing can further improve performance.
