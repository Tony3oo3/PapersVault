## Problem

Existing world-model-based planners for visual navigation follow a verification-centric paradigm that decouples goal intent from trajectory synthesis: candidate actions are sampled externally, rolled out through a video predictor, and ranked by perceptual similarity. This design introduces candidate dependence (quality bounded by sampling coverage), prohibitive inference cost from exhaustive rollouts, and geometric inconsistency due to the absence of explicit spatial constraints on generated trajectories.

## Input / Output

The model takes a start RGB image and a goal RGB image as the sole inputs at inference time (monocular, no depth required). It produces, in a single forward pass, a horizon-N sequence of intermediate RGB-D frames (decoded from latent space via a frozen 3D VAE) and a corresponding planar action trajectory where each step is a 2D displacement (Δx, Δy) in the robot's local frame. During training, per-frame depth pseudo-labels from DepthAnything v3 are injected as auxiliary geometric supervision, but this dependency is entirely removed at test time.

## Method

SWAM extends the pretrained CogVideoX DiT backbone to jointly denoise a unified token sequence comprising intermediate RGB latents, depth latents, and action tokens, conditioned on start and goal frame latents. Actions are projected into the same embedding dimension as visual tokens via a learnable MLP, enabling a single diffusion process to co-generate 'what to perceive' and 'how to act.' A Visual-Guided Action Refinement (VGAR) module applies gated residual cross-attention from final-layer visual tokens to action tokens immediately before decoding, injecting spatial evidence without modifying the diffusion backbone. A Trajectory-Scale Regularization (TSR) loss directly supervises the cumulative displacement of integrated actions against the ground-truth goal position, normalized by horizon length to stabilize predictions across varying distances. All newly added modules are zero-initialized so that training begins from the pretrained generator's behavior and progressively learns the RGB–depth–action coupling.

## Results

SWAM achieves ATE of 0.93, 1.15, and 1.55 (with RPE 0.43, 0.34, 0.68) on RECON, SCAND, and TartanDrive respectively, outperforming the strongest two-stage baseline NWM+NoMaD(×16) by 39.2%, 47.2%, and 75.1% in ATE. Inference requires only 16.91 s per sample (single-pass) versus 245.98 s for NWM+NoMaD(×16), a ~14× speedup. On video generation quality, SWAM attains the best PSNR (17.31/16.11/18.11) and SSIM (0.653/0.619/0.532) across all three datasets. In zero-shot transfer to HuRoN, SWAM (never trained on the dataset) achieves ATE 2.94 and RPE 0.85, beating NWM+NoMaD(×16) explicitly trained on HuRoN (ATE 3.73, RPE 0.95) by 21.2% in ATE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against direct policy baselines GNM and NoMaD, SWAM reduces ATE by roughly 50% on RECON (0.93 vs. 1.85/1.90) and by over 75% on TartanDrive (1.55 vs. 6.68/6.66), while also improving RPE substantially (0.43 vs. 0.54/0.53 on RECON). Compared to the verification-centric world-model planner NWM+NoMaD, SWAM wins on every metric at every candidate budget: even the most expensive setting (×16, 245.98 s) still trails SWAM by 39–75% in ATE, and SWAM achieves this in a single 16.91 s forward pass. The CogVideoX(Joint) ablation baseline, which shares the same backbone and conditioning but omits depth, TSR, and VGAR, shows that joint RGB-action generation alone helps (ATE 4.90 on TartanDrive vs. 6.23 for NWM+NoMaD×16) but remains far below the full SWAM (1.55), confirming that spatial grounding and trajectory regularization are the critical differentiators. In the zero-shot HuRoN evaluation, SWAM outperforms NWM+NoMaD(×16) trained on HuRoN by 21.2% in ATE, demonstrating that the spatial priors learned during training transfer to unseen social-navigation environments without any fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SWAM departs from the dominant verification-centric paradigm in world-model-based navigation (exemplified by NWM + NoMaD), where candidate actions are sampled externally and then scored by a video rollout model. Instead, it reframes navigation as a single-pass joint denoising problem over interleaved RGB-D observation tokens and action tokens, building on the CogVideoX DiT backbone and the broader joint video-action generation line (VideoVLA, CoVAR, PAD, World Action Models). Two novel components—Visual-Guided Action Refinement (VGAR) and Trajectory-Scale Regularization (TSR)—address specific failure modes of naive joint generation (weak local geometric coupling and cumulative drift), while DepthAnything v3 provides training-time spatial supervision without requiring depth at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating candidate sampling and repeated rollouts, SWAM achieves a 14.5× inference speedup over the strongest two-stage baseline while simultaneously improving ATE by 39–75%, suggesting that the verification bottleneck in world-model planners is not merely a computational inconvenience but a fundamental accuracy limiter. The zero-shot transfer result—outperforming a model explicitly trained on HuRoN—hints that spatially grounded joint generation may generalize more robustly than policy-based or verification-based approaches. Open questions remain around semantic traversability reasoning (the model confuses weeds with obstacles), the absence of explicit orientation dynamics for sharp turns, and whether the approach scales to substantially longer horizons or more complex multi-agent settings.
