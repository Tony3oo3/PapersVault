## Problem

Existing RL-based approaches for VLM fine-tuning in image quality assessment rely on sparse outcome rewards, which allow models to earn high scores through erroneous logical attributions rather than genuine visual reasoning. This reward sparsity leaves intermediate reasoning steps underspecified, causing poor out-of-distribution generalization and inflated scores on real-world images with complex, compositional distortions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single RGB image and a text prompt, then autoregressively generates a structured five-step reasoning chain: a scene description, bounding-box coordinates (4-tuples in pixel space) localizing each distortion region, a per-box distortion type label, a per-box severity label, and a final quality rating. Unlike prior work that constrains the model to a single distortion (k=1), the output supports multiple distortion tuples to reflect the compositional nature of real-world degradations. The model is trained exclusively on the explanatory description task and evaluated zero-shot on multiple-choice perception questions and continuous quality scoring.

## Method

RGPR replaces sparse outcome rewards with Grounding Process Rewards (GPR) that enforce a 'ground-before-assess' principle across a structured five-step reasoning trajectory. The GPR comprises four components: a grounding reward combining IoU-based precision and recall with penalty terms against oversized or excessive boxes, plus binary type, severity, and result rewards; an early-stopping rule zeros all downstream rewards if no distortion is correctly grounded. To prevent reward aliasing across heterogeneous steps, Step-wise Reward Normalization independently z-scores each reward dimension within a GRPO group before weighted aggregation. For stable optimization, the framework adopts trajectory-level importance weighting (averaging log-likelihood ratios over the full response) instead of token-level ratios, which avoids extreme gradients from low-frequency tokens like bounding-box coordinates, combined with asymmetric clipping and dynamic sampling to handle zero-reward groups in early training.

## Results

On the Quality Assessment task (zero-shot, no score-regression training), RGPR achieves an average SRCC of 0.831 and PLCC of 0.857 across five benchmarks (KonIQ, SPAQ, CLIVE, BID, SRIQA), surpassing the strongest RL baseline VisualQuality-R1 (0.828/0.855) and all prior VLM-based methods. On the Explanatory Quality Description task, RGPR scores 7.93/10 overall on Q-Bench and 7.10/10 on ViDA-UGC-Test*, exceeding the SFT baseline by 0.46 and 0.56 points respectively. On the Perception task, RGPR reaches 84.45% accuracy on ViDA-UGC-Test*, outperforming the vanilla Qwen3-VL-8B by 16.1% and the SFT-only model by 0.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGPR achieves SOTA on the Quality Assessment task in a zero-shot manner, beating the closest RL competitor VisualQuality-R1 [41] by +0.003 avg SRCC and +0.002 avg PLCC, with clear margins on KonIQ (+0.002 SRCC), SPAQ (+0.006 SRCC), and BID (+0.008 SRCC), though it trails by 0.002 SRCC on CLIVE and SRIQA. Against the other RL baseline Q-Insight [19], RGPR wins on every single benchmark by 0.021–0.051 SRCC. Compared to SFT-based VLMs (Q-Instruct, DepictQA-wild) and vanilla VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B), RGPR leads by 0.05–0.31 avg SRCC, confirming that process-reward RL substantially outperforms supervised fine-tuning for IQA. On the EQD task, RGPR (7.93 Q-Bench / 7.10 ViDA) surpasses the SFT baseline (7.47/6.54) by 0.46/0.56 points and the best RL baseline VisualQuality-R1 (7.47/6.53) by 0.46/0.57 points, with the largest gains in grounding precision (+0.16) and type discrimination (+0.19). On the Perception task, RGPR (84.45% ViDA) outperforms VisualQuality-R1 (78.07%) by 6.4% and the SFT baseline (74.03%) by 10.4%, while traditional handcrafted (NIQE, BRISQUE) and discriminative deep-learning baselines (UNIQUE, MUSIQ, MANIQA) are all surpassed by 0.25–0.41 avg SRCC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based VLM fine-tuning line for IQA (Q-Insight, VisualQuality-R1) by replacing sparse outcome-only rewards with dense grounding process rewards that enforce a 'ground-before-assess' reasoning chain. It draws on the process reward model literature from mathematical reasoning (Lightman et al., VisualPRM) and adapts it to the visual grounding domain, while also incorporating trajectory-level policy optimization (Group Sequence Policy Optimization) to address training instability specific to coordinate-heavy outputs. The step-wise reward normalization mechanism is a novel contribution addressing reward aliasing in multi-dimensional process supervision, building on earlier observations about reward design in LLM alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that enforcing intermediate visual grounding as a reward signal substantially improves both explanation fidelity and zero-shot quality scoring, suggesting that 'correct answers from wrong reasoning' is a real and fixable bottleneck in VLM-based IQA. However, the fixed five-step reasoning format limits linguistic flexibility for open-ended interaction, and the heavy reliance on bounding-box annotations makes domain transfer costly. Open questions include whether the grounding process reward design generalizes to other fine-grained visual tasks (e.g., video quality, AIGC artifact detection) and whether the discrete categorization limitation can be bridged toward continuous scoring without sacrificing the interpretability gains.
