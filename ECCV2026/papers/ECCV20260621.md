## Problem

Standard GRPO training for pixel-space reasoning VLMs induces 'visual laziness': the policy collapses toward no-zoom or single-zoom behaviors and fails to learn effective multi-zoom active perception. The paper identifies the root cause as a structural mismatch—GRPO's group-relative advantage normalization directly compares rollouts that follow fundamentally different perception strategies (varying zoom depths and visual intents), systematically assigning negative advantages to exploratory multi-zoom trajectories before they can be properly learned.

## Input / Output

The model receives a high-resolution image (up to 8K) and a textual prompt, and produces an interleaved trajectory of text tokens and zoom-in actions. Each zoom-in is parameterized as a normalized bounding box in [0,1]⁴ that invokes a crop operator to extract a high-resolution sub-region, which is appended to the context for subsequent reasoning. The final output is a text answer, with the trajectory potentially containing multiple zoom-in operations at different spatial locations.

## Method

VIS-GRPO is a drop-in replacement for GRPO that computes group-relative advantages only among strategically comparable rollouts by stratifying trajectories along two axes: (i) zoom depth (number of zoom-in operations c(τ)), and (ii) visual intent, determined within the multi-zoom regime (c(τ)≥2) by a spatial coherence statistic—the mean pairwise IoU of zoom bounding boxes—where high IoU indicates 'verify' (local verification over overlapping regions) and low IoU indicates 'explore' (broad search over diverse regions). Within each depth-intent stratum, the standard GRPO advantage normalization (mean and std of rewards) is applied, replacing the global group statistics. This ensures that a multi-zoom exploratory trajectory is compared only against other multi-zoom exploratory trajectories, preventing it from being overshadowed by simpler no-zoom or single-zoom strategies that are easier to execute early in training. The method requires only trajectory-level statistics already available during training and integrates into existing pixel-space reasoning pipelines without architectural changes.

## Results

VIS-GRPO (built on Qwen3-VL-8B) achieves an average accuracy of 77.1 across four benchmarks, a 5.6-point gain over the base model (71.5). The improvement is most pronounced on high-resolution tasks: 8.7 points on HR-Bench 8K (81.6 vs. 72.9) and 7.6 points on MME-RealWorld (56.3 vs. 48.7). On V* Bench, VIS-GRPO reaches 88.5, surpassing all prior pixel-space reasoning systems (best prior: DeepEyes at 82.7) and general-purpose VLMs (best: Gemini-2.5-Pro at 79.2). Under a controlled RL-algorithm comparison (same base model and data), VIS-GRPO outperforms GRPO (74.2), Pixel Reasoner (74.4), and Depth-Stratified GRPO (74.8) by 2.3–2.9 points in average accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIS-GRPO wins on every benchmark against all pixel-space reasoning baselines: it outperforms DeepEyes (82.7/74.3/68.8/51.9) by 5.8/7.6/12.8/4.4 points on V* Bench/HR-Bench 4K/HR-Bench 8K/MME-RealWorld, and Pixel Reasoner (84.3/72.6/66.1) by 4.2/9.3/15.5 points on the three shared benchmarks. Against general-purpose VLMs, VIS-GRPO surpasses GPT-4o (55.9 avg) by 21.2 points and Gemini-2.5-Pro (79.2 on V* Bench) by 9.3 points on that benchmark. In the controlled RL-algorithm comparison (Table 2, same Qwen3-VL-8B base and training data), VIS-GRPO (77.1 avg) beats GRPO (74.2) by 2.9 points, Pixel Reasoner with reward heuristics (74.4) by 2.7 points, and the one-axis Depth-Stratified GRPO ablation (74.8) by 2.3 points, confirming that both zoom-depth and visual-intent stratification are necessary. Notably, Pixel Reasoner's zoom-encouraging reward heuristics actually hurt V* Bench performance (83.8 vs. 87.4 for plain GRPO), while VIS-GRPO improves on both axes simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) as the RL training algorithm for pixel-space reasoning VLMs, a paradigm established by Pixel Reasoner and DeepEyes. Its key departure is diagnosing a structural mismatch: GRPO's group-relative advantage normalization compares rollouts that follow fundamentally different perception strategies (varying zoom depth and visual intent), systematically suppressing exploratory multi-zoom trajectories. The stratification principle extends the authors' prior work on Stratified GRPO for LLM search agents [45] to the visual domain, adding a second stratification axis (visual intent via spatial coherence of zoom regions) that is specific to pixel-space reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Identifying 'visual laziness' as a concrete, mechanistically explained failure mode gives the pixel-space reasoning community a clear diagnostic and a drop-in fix that requires no architectural changes. The 8.7-point gain on HR-Bench 8K and the balanced explore/verify distribution demonstrate that the stratification is not merely cosmetic but changes what the policy actually learns. Open questions include whether the spatial-coherence threshold generalizes across image domains and resolutions, whether the approach extends to more complex interaction patterns (e.g., pan-and-zoom, multi-image reasoning), and whether the single-model, single-dataset evaluation (Qwen3-VL-8B, 15K samples) captures the full robustness of the method.
