## Problem

Existing 3D human-scene interaction reconstructions suffer from a perception-simulation gap: visually plausible results violate physical constraints (penetration, unstable object placement, missing structural support), causing failures in physics engines and downstream embodied AI deployment. No prior method produces reconstructions that are simultaneously geometrically accurate and physically stable enough for direct simulation or robot transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260178/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes uncalibrated, unlocalized sparse-view images (as few as 4 views) or a single monocular video as input, with no camera intrinsics or extrinsics required. It produces a metric-consistent 3D human-scene interaction scene (SMPL human + explicit object meshes) that is validated for stability in IsaacGym, along with refined human motion trajectories that can be retargeted to a Unitree G1 humanoid robot via GMR and deployed through a diffusion-guided RL policy.

## Method

HSImul3R formulates reconstruction as a bi-directional physics-in-the-loop optimization where the simulator acts as an active supervisor. In the forward pass, scene-targeted reinforcement learning (extending PHC) refines human motion by adding a contact-proximity loss that minimizes distance between human contact keypoints and nearest object surface points, enforcing physically plausible interaction. In the reverse pass, Direct Simulation Reward Optimization (DSRO) fine-tunes the MIDI image-to-3D generator using simulation-derived binary stability rewards (gravitational stability, interaction success, meaningful contact) as a weighted denoising objective, correcting structural defects like missing legs or surface distortions. The pipeline first reconstructs scene via DUSt3R and human via SAM2/4DHumans/ViTPose, then injects 3D structural priors from MIDI to resolve penetration and alignment before entering the optimization loop.

## Results

On HSIBench, HSImul3R achieves 53.68% / 30.56% / 13.92% Stability-HSI on Easy/Medium/Hard interaction scenarios, compared to only 10.52% / 4.50% / 2.66% for the prior baseline HSfM. Scene penetration (SP-3D) drops from 69.51% (HSfM) to 22.9%, and post-simulation motion quality improves to W-MPJPE 4.09 and PA-MPJPE 2.17 (vs. 5.02 / 2.79 for HSfM). For image-to-3D generation, DSRO fine-tuning raises gravity-only stability to 91.50% and F-Score to 88.25, surpassing both MIDI (79.19% / 81.95) and DSO (87.23% / 86.26).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HSfM [52], the only prior method for sparse-view HSI reconstruction, HSImul3R improves Stability-HSI by roughly 5× on Easy (53.68% vs. 10.52%), 6.8× on Medium (30.56% vs. 4.50%), and 5.2× on Hard (13.92% vs. 2.66%), while reducing scene penetration from 69.51% to 22.9%. In the image-to-3D generation comparison (Table 2), HSImul3R with DSRO outperforms the base MIDI model [23] on all four metrics (Stability-HSI Easy 53.68% vs. 29.56%, SG 91.50% vs. 79.19%, CD 0.173 vs. 0.198, F-Score 88.25 vs. 81.95) and also surpasses DSO [31] fine-tuned on the same MIDI backbone (Stability-HSI Easy 53.68% vs. 38.75%, SG 91.50% vs. 87.23%, CD 0.173 vs. 0.191, F-Score 88.25 vs. 86.26). The ablation variants confirm that both the forward-pass scene-targeted RL and the reverse-pass DSRO contribute substantially: removing either drops Easy Stability-HSI to 29.56% (V4) or 39.56% (V2), well below the full pipeline's 53.68%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the sparse-view HSI reconstruction pipeline of HSfM [52] and the RL-based humanoid motion tracking of PHC [45], but departs fundamentally by closing the loop between reconstruction and physics simulation. Where prior HSI reconstruction methods (HSfM, HOSNeRF) optimize in 2D image space and prior physics-based humanoid work (PHC, DeepMimic) assumes pre-validated scenes, HSImul3R introduces a bidirectional optimization where the physics simulator acts as an active supervisor: forward-pass scene-targeted RL refines human motion, while reverse-pass DSRO (inspired by DSO [31]) refines scene geometry using simulation-derived stability rewards. The image-to-3D generative prior from MIDI [23] is repurposed not as a standalone reconstruction tool but as a differentiable component that can be fine-tuned via physics feedback, a use not explored in prior image-to-3D work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to produce HSI reconstructions that are directly stable in a physics engine and deployable on a real humanoid robot (Unitree G1), bridging a gap that has kept visually plausible reconstructions from being usable in embodied AI pipelines. The HSIBench benchmark (300 interaction instances, 16 views each) provides a much-needed evaluation standard for simulation-ready HSI. However, absolute success rates remain modest (53.7% easy, 13.9% hard), and the method inherits biases from MIDI's training distribution, limiting generalizability to out-of-domain objects and complex multi-object interactions. Open questions include whether the bidirectional optimization scales to dynamic scenes, whether DSRO can be extended beyond binary stability rewards, and whether the 4-view sufficiency finding generalizes to more cluttered environments.
