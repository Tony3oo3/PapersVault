## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods implicitly assume clean supervision and degrade substantially under label noise, while conventional noisy-label remedies (sample selection, loss weighting, label correction) are tightly coupled with model optimization, require clean validation anchors, and amplify confirmation bias. The paper tackles the unresolved question of how to synthesize compact, plug-and-play distilled datasets that are simultaneously noise-robust and information-rich, without relabeling or access to clean subsets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy RGB image dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10 at 32×32, CIFAR-100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64) with corrupted labels as input and produces a compact synthetic dataset of fixed-size image tensors (10–1000 images per class) with associated soft or hard labels. The synthetic set is designed to be plug-and-play: a fresh model trained solely on it should generalize better on a clean test set than one trained on the full noisy dataset. The pipeline requires P=100 teacher trajectories and pre-computed SSFT timestamps (t_learn, t_forget) as one-time preprocessing, but no clean labels or relabeling at any stage.

## Method

The framework operates as a bilevel trajectory-matching distillation with two complementary modules. Selective Guidance Reweighting (SGR) assigns each sample a hybrid reliability weight that fuses a dynamic KNN consistency score (Jensen-Shannon divergence between the sample's one-hot label and its K nearest neighbors' predicted distributions) with a static second-split-forgetting score (derived from t_learn and t_forget timestamps), blended via a time-dependent convex coefficient α_t that shifts from global priors to local consistency over training; these weights modulate sample contributions during teacher trajectory construction. Teacher-Inspired Auxiliary Targets (TIAT) then aggregates reliability across P teacher trajectories to compute a probabilistic confidence mean and variance, selects a high-confidence subset using dual thresholds, fine-tunes the teacher on that subset to obtain a noise-suppressed checkpoint, and introduces an auxiliary alignment loss that regularizes the student's optimization toward this refined target. The total outer-loop objective is a β-weighted combination of the original trajectory matching loss and the auxiliary loss, jointly optimizing the synthetic dataset so that the student's induced learning dynamics faithfully track the denoised teacher trajectory.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 under 40% symmetric noise, the proposed method achieves 71.6% accuracy at 50 IPC and 80.0% at 500 IPC, outperforming the strongest baseline DATM (67.2% and 74.3%) by 4.4 and 5.7 percentage points respectively. Under 40% asymmetric noise at 1000 IPC, the method reaches 75.0%, exceeding DATM (71.4%) by 3.6 points. On CIFAR-100 under 40% symmetric noise, the method attains 48.4% at 50 IPC and 52.8% at 100 IPC, surpassing DATM (45.1% and 44.4%) by 3.3 and 8.4 points. On Tiny-ImageNet (IPC=10, 40% symmetric noise), the method achieves 30.1% versus DATM's 28.2%, demonstrating consistent gains across datasets and noise regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (DATM+SGR+TIAT) consistently outperforms all four baselines across every noise setting and IPC level tested. Against DATM (the closest competitor and the base trajectory-matching framework), gains range from +1.1 to +6.6 points under symmetric noise and +1.9 to +3.7 under asymmetric noise, with the largest margins appearing at 40% noise and higher IPC (e.g., +5.7 at 500 IPC, 40% symmetric). DANCE and RCIG, which rely on distribution matching rather than trajectory alignment, show substantially larger gaps: the method exceeds DANCE by up to 11.6 points (80.0 vs 68.4 at 500 IPC, 40% symmetric) and RCIG by up to 11.1 points under the same setting. RDED lags most severely, trailing by 14.1 points at 500 IPC under 40% symmetric noise. Notably, under 40% asymmetric noise at 1000 IPC, the method achieves 75.0% while DANCE plateaus at 69.6% and RCIG at 64.2%, confirming that trajectory-based distillation with noise-aware reweighting is particularly advantageous under structured, class-dependent corruption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trajectory-matching dataset distillation framework (DATM) by integrating two noisy-label robustness mechanisms: Selective Guidance Reweighting, which fuses KNN-based local consistency (Iscen et al.) with second-split forgetting (Maini et al.) into a progressive reweighting schedule applied to the teacher trajectory; and Teacher-Inspired Auxiliary Targets, which injects uncertainty-filtered auxiliary supervision from high-confidence teacher checkpoints into the student-side optimization. It departs from prior noisy-label learning methods (sample selection, label correction) by avoiding the self-referential coupling between noise estimation and model optimization, instead treating noise suppression as a property of the distillation pipeline itself. The bilevel trajectory-matching formulation is inherited directly from DATM, while the reweighting and auxiliary-target components are novel combinations of established noisy-label tools repurposed for the DD setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most DD methods degrade under realistic label noise, yet real-world datasets (web-curated, crowdsourced) are inherently noisy, and DD's promise of compact, reusable supervision is undermined if it condenses corrupted associations. The label-preserving, relabeling-free design makes the approach deployable without access to clean validation sets, which is a meaningful constraint in practice. Limitations include evaluation restricted to small ConvNets on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet, a 1.47–1.50× runtime overhead at mid-IPC budgets, and reliance on P=100 teacher trajectories that scale linearly in compute. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to transformer backbones, larger-scale datasets (ImageNet), and whether the bilevel optimization can be replaced by cheaper single-level alternatives without sacrificing the noise-suppression benefit.
