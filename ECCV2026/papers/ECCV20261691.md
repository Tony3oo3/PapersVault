## Problem

3D Foundation Models (e.g., VGGT) produce fast feed-forward SfM reconstructions but with geometric accuracy well below traditional pipelines. The standard post-processing fix—Bundle Adjustment—requires extracting feature tracks, which reintroduces the expensive matching step that 3DFMs were designed to eliminate, and demands server-grade hardware (≥40 GB VRAM). The paper asks whether geometric refinement can be achieved without any explicit feature correspondence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261691/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unordered set of RGB images (capped at 150 per scene) together with the initial geometric estimate from any 3DFM: per-image camera intrinsics K, extrinsic poses P, and dense depth maps Z. From the RGB images it additionally computes Canny edge maps and Distance Transform Fields (DTFs) as auxiliary geometric signals. The output is a refined geometric estimate with updated poses, a per-image focal-length scaling factor, and pixel-wise affine depth corrections, all produced in a few seconds on a consumer-grade GPU (few GB VRAM).

## Method

EPO formulates a fully differentiable, trackless optimization that replaces feature-based reprojection error with bidirectional edge-map alignment: each edge pixel in one view is projected into a neighboring view and its distance to the nearest edge is read from a precomputed DTF, producing a dense, differentiable geometric signal. A 6-layer MLP (following ACE0) with 6D rotation parameterization and a learnable translation offset δ refines poses; focal length is refined via a per-image scaling factor γ; and depth is refined with a per-pixel affine (α, β). The viewgraph is built by cycle-consistent reprojection of the 3DFM depth (no feature matching), and the coupled edge loss across all valid pairs provides implicit multi-view regularization. Optimization proceeds in two phases—camera parameters first, then joint camera+depth—using AdamW with a pose-based early-stopping criterion (95th-percentile rotation/translation change below a threshold for consecutive steps), reducing runtime by ~50–70% relative to fixed-iteration baselines while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Results

On ScanNet++, VGGT+EPO achieves a mean AUC@5° of 77.1, outperforming VGGT+Ref+BA (70.9) by 6.2 points while running in 52.1 s versus 303.5 s (~6× speedup). On TerraSky3D, EPO reaches 79.2 AUC@5° (vs. 75.5 for VGGT+Ref+BA) in 44.6 s, and on Mip-NeRF 360 it attains 90.5 AUC@5° (vs. 87.8) in 49.6 s. EPO also produces 1.7× more 2D observations (136k vs. 79k) with a comparable mean reprojection error of 2.0 px to VGGT+Ref+BA. In Novel View Synthesis (3DGS), VGGT+EPO achieves the best mean PSNR of 23.93 dB and lowest LPIPS of 0.284 on Mip-NeRF 360, surpassing VGGT+Ref+BA (23.06 dB, 0.320 LPIPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPO consistently outperforms both BA-based baselines across all three datasets. On ScanNet++, EPO improves AUC@5° by 6.2 points over VGGT+Ref+BA (77.1 vs. 70.9) and by 7.1 over VGGT+BA (70.0), while reducing runtime from 303.5 s to 52.1 s (~6×). On TerraSky3D, the gains are 3.7 points over VGGT+Ref+BA (79.2 vs. 75.5) with a 7.6× speedup (44.6 s vs. 337.7 s). On Mip-NeRF 360, EPO leads by 2.7 points (90.5 vs. 87.8) with a 4.2× speedup. Critically, VGGT+Ref+BA requires an NVIDIA H200 with ≥40 GB VRAM, whereas EPO runs on a consumer RTX 4090 with only a few GB. The paper further validates generalizability in Table 8: EPO improves AUC@5° by 22.4 points on VGGT (56.8→79.2), 25.3 on MapAnything (53.0→78.3), and 16.1 on Pi3 (65.1→81.2) on TerraSky3D, confirming the method is 3DFM-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

EPO draws on two distinct lineages: edge-based visual odometry/SLAM (Canny-VO, RESLAM) that use edge alignment for pose estimation, and the emerging family of 3D Foundation Models (VGGT, DUSt3R, MASt3R, MapAnything, Pi3) that produce feed-forward reconstructions. The key departure is transplanting the edge-alignment principle from the SLAM setting (which exploits temporal continuity and metric depth) into the unordered-image, noisy-depth regime of 3DFM post-processing, where no explicit feature tracks exist. The MLP pose refiner follows ACE0's architecture, and the viewgraph construction mirrors BA's cycle-consistency checks, but the optimization objective replaces point reprojection error with a differentiable edge-to-distance-transform loss, enabling a first-order (AdamW) solver in place of the second-order Levenberg-Marquardt used by BA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: EPO reduces refinement from minutes to seconds and from 40+ GB VRAM to a few GB, making 3DFM post-processing accessible on consumer hardware for the first time. The finding that dense edge observations (1.7× more 2D observations than BA) with a first-order optimizer can match or exceed second-order BA in geometric accuracy challenges the assumption that feature tracks are necessary for high-fidelity refinement. Open questions include robustness in scenes dominated by high-frequency non-structural textures (foliage, reflections) where edge detectors produce noisy signals, and whether the approach extends to even sparser viewgraphs or streaming/online settings. The method's generality across VGGT, MapAnything, and Pi3 suggests it could become a standard lightweight refinement step in 3DFM pipelines.
