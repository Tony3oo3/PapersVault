## Problem

Photorealistic volumetric rendering of CT scans via neural methods (NeRF, 3DGS, 6DGS) requires prohibitive per-scan optimization—30 minutes to over 10 hours—plus pre-rendered training views, making them impractical for clinical workflows. Moreover, optimized parameters cannot transfer across scans, generalize to new transfer functions, or support compositional organ visualization without full re-optimization. The paper asks whether a single feedforward pass can directly produce high-quality, view-dependent volumetric renderings from a CT volume without any per-scan training.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 6-channel 3D volume at 1.5 mm isotropic spacing (zero-padded to multiples of 32): one channel of normalized Hounsfield-unit intensity, one binary segmentation mask, and four RGBA channels sampled from pre-defined transfer functions. It outputs a 37-channel parameter volume at half the input spatial resolution, where each voxel encodes a 6D Gaussian's direction mean (3), spherical-harmonic color coefficients at degrees 0–1 (12), an opacity offset (1), and the 21 upper-triangular elements of a 6×6 covariance matrix. Foreground voxels selected by the mask are instantiated as 6D Gaussians ready for real-time tile-based rasterization at 328+ FPS, with optional 89-second fine-tuning to boost PSNR to ~32 dB.

## Method

Render-FM is an nnU-Net-inspired 3D U-Net encoder-decoder (3×3×3 convolutions, InstanceNorm, LeakyReLU, max-pool/transpose-conv with skip connections) that regresses per-voxel 6DGS parameters in a single forward pass. The key architectural insight is Anatomy-Guided Priming (AGP): Gaussian positions are fixed to voxel world coordinates, base colors and opacities are seeded from transfer functions, and semantic labels come from the segmentation mask, so the network only predicts residual corrections on top of anatomically grounded priors—without AGP the feedforward problem is too underconstrained to converge. Rendering uses 6DGS view-dependent covariance slicing (partitioning the 6×6 covariance into spatial, directional, and cross-term blocks conditioned on viewing direction) followed by a differentiable tile-based rasterizer, enabling end-to-end training where rendering gradients flow back through the rasterizer into the U-Net. Training is supervised by L1 + MS-SSIM losses against physically-based PBRT ground-truth views rendered at 1600×1600, using 4 random viewpoints per step on a single A100 GPU over ~3 days.

## Results

On the in-domain TotalSegmentator test set (Seen TF), Render-FM achieves SSIM 0.919, PSNR 27.30, and LPIPS 0.097 in just 2.8 s of preparation (a 500× speedup over 6DGS's 1463.9 s), while outperforming the per-scan 6DGS baseline on all three quality metrics. On the out-of-domain CT-ORG set, Render-FM maintains SSIM 0.918 / PSNR 26.21 / LPIPS 0.092 (Seen TF) and SSIM 0.913 / PSNR 26.77 / LPIPS 0.092 (Unseen TF), again beating 6DGS on every metric. With optional 89–140 s fine-tuning, Render-FM+FT reaches SSIM 0.937–0.944 and PSNR 30.74–32.48, surpassing all per-scan optimized baselines. Rendering speed is 245–329 FPS without fine-tuning and up to 424 FPS with fine-tuning, well above real-time clinical thresholds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole per-scan baseline is 6DGS [12], evaluated both in its vanilla form and with the paper's proposed AGP initialization (6DGS + AGP). Render-FM (no fine-tuning) beats vanilla 6DGS on all three quality metrics in every setting: e.g., on ID TotalSegmentator it improves SSIM from 0.912 to 0.919, PSNR from 26.63 to 27.30, and LPIPS from 0.096 to 0.097, while cutting preparation time from 1463.9 s to 2.8 s (≈500×). On OOD CT-ORG the gap widens for LPIPS (0.092 vs. 0.105 Seen TF; 0.092 vs. 0.103 Unseen TF), confirming stronger generalization. Render-FM+FT surpasses even 6DGS+AGP on every metric (e.g., PSNR 31.67 vs. 28.92 on ID; 32.48 vs. 29.36 on OOD Seen TF) at a fraction of the preparation cost (89–140 s vs. 1787–2262 s). For compositional skeleton visualization, Render-FM requires 0 s additional preparation versus 5146 s for 6DGS, while still achieving competitive quality (SSIM 0.925) and superior quality after fine-tuning (SSIM 0.944, PSNR 30.74). The trade-off is a higher Gaussian count (343k–586k vs. 69k–82k for 6DGS), which lowers raw FPS (245–329 vs. 602–698), though all values remain well above real-time thresholds.

## Relation to Prior Work

Render-FM sits at the intersection of three research threads: 6D Gaussian Splatting (6DGS) for view-dependent volumetric rendering, nnU-Net's self-configuring 3D U-Net architecture for medical image regression, and the feedforward paradigm of Large Gaussian Models (LGMs) that bypass per-scene optimization. The key departure from prior work is twofold: (1) replacing per-scan optimization (30 min for 3DGS, hours for NeRF) with a single 2.8-second feedforward pass, and (2) introducing Anatomy-Guided Priming (AGP) to inject segmentation masks and transfer functions as structured priors—information that existing Gaussian splatting methods for medical data (DDGS-CT, 6DGS) ignore. Unlike LGMs, which predict 3D Gaussians from sparse 2D images of natural scenes, Render-FM exploits the dense, structured 3D input of CT volumes and domain-specific appearance priors to make the feedforward mapping tractable in a clinical setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 500× reduction in preparation time (from ~1 hour to 2.8 seconds) is the most practically significant result, as it removes the primary barrier to deploying photorealistic volumetric rendering in routine clinical workflows. The ability to support dynamic transfer function modification and compositional organ visualization with zero additional preparation time addresses capabilities that per-scan methods fundamentally cannot offer. Limitations include reliance on CT modality (MRI extension is left to future work), dependence on segmentation masks (though auto-generable via TotalSegmentator), and a dense Gaussian prediction strategy that yields lower FPS (245–328) than sparser 6DGS (602–697), though still well above real-time thresholds. Open questions include whether the feedforward paradigm generalizes to other modalities, more complex pathologies, or multi-contrast acquisitions, and whether the AGP necessity finding extends to other structured 3D prediction tasks.
