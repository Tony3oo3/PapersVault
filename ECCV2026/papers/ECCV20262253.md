## Problem

Existing calligraphy datasets suffer from a 'modal mixture' problem where ink manuscripts and stone rubbings are conflated, and from 'flattened labels' that provide only end-to-end annotations without intermediate visual attributes linking brushwork features to aesthetic judgments. This makes it impossible to evaluate whether LVLMs genuinely perceive fine-grained stylistic cues (ink diffusion, stroke tension, pressure variation) or merely exploit spurious correlations and linguistic priors. The paper asks: can current LVLMs perform expert-level, visually grounded calligraphic style understanding, and what data and evaluation design is needed to expose their true limitations?

## Input / Output

For the discrimination task, the model receives a 256×256 calligraphy character image alongside 8 candidate options, each pairing a textual style description with a reference image, and must output a single candidate label. For the generation task, the model receives a 2-shot in-context setup (with strict anti-leakage: no character identity overlap between few-shot examples and the target) and produces a ≤50-word professional aesthetic description scored on BERTScore-F1, Terminology, and Detail Richness. All images are standardized to 256×256 via aspect-ratio-preserving padding and Lanczos resampling, and are drawn from three domains—Tie (ink manuscripts), Bei (stone rubbings), and Wild (raw archive images)—to enable controlled domain-sensitivity analysis.

## Method

HCSU is a dataset-and-benchmark contribution rather than a new model architecture. The dataset is built from the Yiguan Calligraphy archive through a multi-stage pipeline: (1) domain-adaptive polarity inversion and Otsu thresholding to canonicalize Tie and Bei to a uniform black-on-white format, (2) aspect-ratio-preserving geometric normalization to 256×256, and (3) Connected Component Analysis–based semantic masking applied back to the original RGB image to recover ink density, 'flying white' textures, and stroke color while eliminating background noise. Each of the 39,307 images carries hierarchical expert annotations (script type, stroke style, ink style, character structure, overall charm, layout) grounded in the art-historical compendium Shu Lin Zao Jian. The benchmark defines three evaluation protocols—8-way controlled style discrimination, interpretable aesthetic description generation (evaluated via BERTScore plus an LLM-as-a-Judge on Terminology and Richness), and a two-phase robustness/description ablation—calibrated via probe-model experiments to select K=8 candidates and a 2-shot configuration. State-of-the-art proprietary and open-weight LVLMs are evaluated under a fixed persona prompt and temperature 0.1, revealing a persistent 'knowledgeable yet unperceptive' gap and a counter-intuitive Bei-over-Tie performance inversion.

## Results

On the HCSU 8-way style discrimination task (random baseline = 12.5%), the best proprietary model Doubao-1.5-Vision-Pro-250328 achieves 37.29% average accuracy (37.60% on Bei, 36.98% on Tie), followed by GPT-5.2 at 34.58% and Claude-Sonnet-4-5-20250929 at 32.82%. Among open-weight models, Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct leads at 30.25% average accuracy, still trailing the best proprietary model by ~7 points. On the interpretable aesthetic reasoning task, GPT-5.2 achieves the highest Terminology scores (7.23 on Bei, 7.27 on Tie), exceeding expert ground-truth references (+1.42 and +1.29), while all models remain below expert Detail Richness scores (4.53–4.75).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 7 LVLM families (5 proprietary, 2 open-weight series) on the HCSU benchmark rather than proposing a new method. Among proprietary models, Doubao-1.5-Vision-Pro-250328 leads discrimination at 37.29% avg accuracy, outperforming GPT-5.2 (34.58%) by ~2.7 points and Gemini-2.5-Pro (29.32%) by ~8 points; however, Doubao performs worst on description generation (Detail Richness 3.07 on Bei, −1.46 vs. expert), while GPT-5.2 dominates generation (Terminology 7.23 on Bei, exceeding the expert reference of 5.81). GPT-4o and Claude-Sonnet-4-5-20250929 cluster around 32–33% discrimination accuracy with moderate generation quality. Among open-weight models, Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct (30.25%) clearly outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-72B (24.93%) and Qwen3-VL-30B (21.81%), showing a consistent scaling trend, yet still trails the best proprietary model by ~7 points. A counter-intuitive finding is that most models score higher on Bei (stone rubbings) than Tie (ink manuscripts), e.g., Qwen3-VL-235B reaches 34.10% on Bei but only 26.40% on Tie, suggesting current vision encoders are biased toward high-contrast geometric boundaries over continuous ink textures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of fine-grained visual perception benchmarks (FG-BMK, Finer) and calligraphy-specific datasets (MCCD, CalliNet, CASIA-HWDB), but departs from both in key ways. Prior calligraphy datasets suffer from 'modal mixture' (conflating ink manuscripts with stone rubbings) and 'flattened labels' (only calligrapher identity without intermediate visual attributes), while prior fine-grained LVLM benchmarks focus on natural-image domains with stable visual prototypes. HCSU resolves both issues by systematically decoupling Tie/Bei/Wild domains and introducing hierarchical expert-written aesthetic descriptions that decompose style into brushwork, ink, and structure dimensions, enabling a dual evaluation protocol (8-way discrimination + interpretable reasoning) absent from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a persistent 'knowledgeable but unperceptive' gap in state-of-the-art LVLMs: models achieve only 20–37% on 8-way style discrimination (vs. 12.5% random) and fail to ground aesthetic judgments in visible brushwork evidence. The counterintuitive finding that models perform better on high-contrast stone rubbings than on ink manuscripts that preserve authentic ink dynamics reveals a fundamental bias in current vision encoders toward geometric boundaries over continuous textures. Open questions include how to train or architect models that genuinely perceive micro-level brushwork cues, whether the discrimination-articulation disconnect (Doubao best at matching, worst at describing) reflects a deeper architectural limitation, and how to scale the dataset to cover the full diversity of Chinese calligraphic lineages.
