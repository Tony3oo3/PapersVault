## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-video diffusion models operate at a coarse granularity—global weight editing, neuron pruning, or text-embedding manipulation—that misaligns with the fine-grained, distributed nature of concept representations, causing incomplete removal or collateral damage to unrelated content. Moreover, virtually all prior approaches apply erasure globally across space and time, even when the target concept occupies only a local region, unnecessarily degrading the rest of the generated video.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a frozen pretrained DiT-based T2V model (CogVideoX-5B or HunyuanVideo) and requires only paired text prompts at inference: a positive prompt containing the target concept and a negative prompt providing a safe semantic alternative. A PConvSAE is trained once offline on intermediate activations at a single identified transformer layer, and the resulting concept-specific kernel set is locked permanently. At inference, the model generates 32-frame videos at 8 fps, with erasure applied via spatially-modulated classifier-free guidance that adds only a single negative-conditioned forward pass per timestep, keeping latency within ~0.05 s/frame of the unmodified model.

## Method

EraseSAE follows a decompose-attribute-erase pipeline in monosemantic feature space. The decompose stage trains a Partitioned Convolutional Sparse Autoencoder (PConvSAE) that replaces standard linear SAE projections with 2D convolutions on temporally folded tensors and splits the latent space into a Context Branch (concept-agnostic scene) and a Concept Branch (concept-specific semantics with per-concept channel partitions), trained jointly with five loss terms including spatially-masked reconstruction, identity leakage penalty, and temporal consistency. The attribute stage performs a one-time offline contrastive procedure: it computes per-kernel activation profiles on positive samples and contrasts them against a hard-negative baseline (element-wise max of background and all other concepts) via a log-ratio score, locking a compact set of concept-specific kernels. The erase stage generates a dynamic spatiotemporal mask at every denoising timestep by intersecting the Context Branch's foreground complement with the locked kernels' activation heatmap, then applies spatially-modulated classifier-free guidance that restricts negative guidance exclusively to masked regions, confining suppression to where the target concept is actually active.

## Results

On the nudity erasure task, EraseSAE reduces the nudity exposure rate to 2.62% (CogVideoX-5B) and 7.13% (HunyuanVideo) on the Gen test set, outperforming the strongest baseline T2VUnlearning (2.88% / 10.89%) by 34.5% relative on HunyuanVideo. SSIM scores of 74.99 (CogVideoX) and 65.69 (HunyuanVideo) surpass the next-best method by 27.97 and 5.02 points respectively, confirming minimal collateral damage. Under adversarial Ring-A-Bell prompts (K77 hardest level), EraseSAE maintains nudity rates of 4.74% and 5.13%, well below all baselines. Inference overhead is negligible: 3.66 s/frame on CogVideoX vs. 3.61 for the unmodified model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EraseSAE outperforms all five baselines on both erasure efficacy and generation quality. Against the strongest training-based baseline T2VUnlearning [37], EraseSAE achieves a 34.5% relative reduction in nudity rate on HunyuanVideo (7.13 vs. 10.89) and a 5.02-point SSIM gain (65.69 vs. 60.67), while on CogVideoX the SSIM advantage is dramatic (74.99 vs. 42.39), reflecting T2VUnlearning's global weight edits corrupting background structure. Training-free methods (Neg Prompt, SAFREE) fail to achieve thorough erasure—SAFREE still yields 46.38% nudity on HunyuanVideo—and degrade Object Class scores substantially (e.g., SAFREE drops CogVideoX Object Class from 79.91 to 45.19). VideoEraser [34] is evaluated only on CogVideoX and shows moderate erasure (18.88%) with no HunyuanVideo results. Under adversarial Ring-A-Bell prompts, EraseSAE's advantage widens: at the hardest K77 level it achieves 4.74%/5.13% vs. 8.65%/9.95% for T2VUnlearning, attributed to its monosemantic kernel locking being invariant to prompt rephrasing. Inference cost is comparable to the original model on CogVideoX (3.66 vs. 3.61 s/frame), with the slight HunyuanVideo overhead (6.97 s/frame) stemming solely from the additional negative-conditioned forward pass.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research lines: concept erasure in diffusion models (ESD, CA, UCE, MACE for T2I; VideoEraser, T2VUnlearning for T2V) and sparse autoencoders for mechanistic interpretability (Cunningham et al. for LLMs; SAUCE, SAeUron for vision). It departs from prior erasure methods by shifting the intervention unit from polysemantic neurons or global weights to monosemantic SAE features, and from prior SAE work by extending the architecture from LLMs to DiT-based T2V models with a convolutional, partitioned design that preserves spatiotemporal structure. The spatially-modulated classifier-free guidance mechanism is a novel inference-time intervention that has no direct precedent in the erasure literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that mechanistic interpretability tools (SAEs) can serve as a practical substrate for surgical safety interventions in video generation, offering a principled alternative to global weight editing that preserves generation quality. The large SSIM gap over baselines (27.97 points on CogVideoX) demonstrates that spatiotemporal locality is not merely a refinement but a critical requirement for non-destructive erasure in video. Open questions include scalability to hundreds of simultaneous target concepts, whether the PConvSAE training cost (30 epochs on 8 A100s per intervention layer) is prohibitive for frequent model updates, and whether the approach transfers to non-DiT architectures such as U-Net-based video models.
