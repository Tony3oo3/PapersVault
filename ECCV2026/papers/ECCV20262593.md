## Problem

Existing scene-aware motion generation methods either implicitly fuse language and scene representations (entangling motion synthesis with scene reasoning) or rely on explicit grounding via fixed-vocabulary detectors or sparse affordance maps that lack generalization and fail to capture full-body, temporally coherent interactions. The paper identifies mutual distance as a promising full-body interaction representation but shows it is inherently ambiguous for stochastic generation—distinct motions in the same scene can produce nearly identical mutual distance trajectories—requiring an additional absolute spatial reference to make it generatable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262593/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D scene encoded as a signed distance field volume (N×N×N), a natural language instruction encoded by a frozen CLIP-ViT-B/32 into a 512-d feature vector, and 2D semantic anchors (start and end pelvis positions) predicted by Gemini-1.5-Pro from a bird's-eye-view rendering of the scene. It outputs a U-frame 3D full-body motion sequence as SMPL-X joint positions (J×3 per frame), with 150 basis points sampled 2.0 m from the scene origin used to compute the per-basis-point distance component.

## Method

MDNet is a two-stage diffusion framework. The first stage (Mutual Distance Generator) synthesizes mutual distance sequences—concatenating per-joint signed distances to scene surfaces and per-basis-point distances from scene anchors to body joints—in the frequency domain via a truncated DCT (50 coefficients), using a spatial-semantic transformer that fuses scene SDF features, CLIP text features, and VLM-derived semantic anchors through hierarchical attention blocks. The second stage (Mutual Distance-based Motion Generator) denoises full-body SMPL-X poses conditioned on the generated mutual distance, scene features, text, and anchors, trained with an L1 reconstruction loss plus per-joint and per-basis-point signed-distance consistency losses. The key design insight is that VLM-predicted semantic anchors resolve the inherent ambiguity of mutual distance for stochastic generation by providing absolute spatial grounding, while frequency-domain modeling suppresses high-frequency noise to ensure temporally stable interaction trajectories.

## Results

On HUMANISE, MDNet achieves a goal distance of 0.057 m, substantially outperforming Wang et al. (0.156 m), Cen et al. (0.384 m), and Humanise (0.422 m), indicating far superior semantic grounding. In physical plausibility, MDNet attains the best non-collision rate (99.81%), lowest mean penetration (0.635 m), and lowest max penetration (1.849 m), while maintaining a competitive contact score of 95.05%. The proposed TMV metric (0.1870) nearly matches ground truth (0.1875), confirming natural temporal dynamics. On the unseen PROX dataset, MDNet generalizes with a goal distance of 0.054 m and pene_mean of 0.270 m, far exceeding both baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Humanise [41] (cVAE-based), MDNet reduces goal distance from 0.422 m to 0.057 m (7.4× improvement) and lowers pene_mean from 1.283 to 0.635 m, while also achieving a more natural TMV (0.1870 vs. 0.2055). Against Cen et al. [3] (diffusion + 3D object detection), MDNet improves goal distance by 6.7× (0.384 → 0.057 m) and nearly doubles the non-collision rate (99.60% → 99.81%), with pene_max dropping from 2.551 to 1.849 m. Against Wang et al. [40] (affordance-map diffusion), MDNet achieves the largest relative gains in penetration: pene_mean drops from 1.367 to 0.635 m and pene_max from 14.768 to 1.849 m (an 8.8× reduction), while goal distance improves from 0.156 to 0.057 m. The only metric where MDNet trails a baseline is contact score (95.05% vs. Wang's 95.86%), a small gap the authors attribute to the trade-off between penetration avoidance and contact enforcement. On the PROX cross-dataset test, MDNet outperforms both Cen et al. and Wang et al. on every metric, with goal distance 0.054 m versus 0.281–0.283 m and pene_mean 0.270 m versus 0.816–0.963 m, confirming that the VLM-based grounding generalizes where fixed-vocabulary detectors fail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the mutual distance representation originally developed for scene-aware motion *forecasting* (Xing et al., ECCV 2024) into the stochastic, language-conditioned generation setting, where the absence of historical context makes mutual distance inherently ambiguous. It departs from prior scene-aware generation methods—HUMANISE's implicit cVAE fusion, Cen et al.'s 3D object detection grounding, and Wang et al.'s sparse affordance/contact maps—by introducing an explicit full-body interaction representation grounded via VLM-predicted semantic anchors. The frequency-domain diffusion component adapts the DCT-based time-series denoising technique of Crabbe et al. (ICML 2024) to the mutual distance signal, and the VLM-based BEV anchor prediction replaces fixed-vocabulary 3D detectors with open-vocabulary spatial reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete representation gap: prior methods either entangle scene reasoning with motion synthesis or constrain only sparse contact points, leaving full-body temporal interaction structure unmodeled. The mutual distance + semantic anchor formulation offers a principled, generalizable alternative to both implicit and affordance-based grounding, and the demonstrated cross-dataset transfer to PROX without finetuning suggests the representation is not overfit to HUMANISE's scene distribution. Open questions include whether the two-stage pipeline can be unified into a single diffusion process, how the approach scales to multi-agent or dynamic scenes, and whether the reliance on a specific VLM (Gemini-1.5-Pro) for anchor prediction limits deployment in settings where such models are unavailable or latency-sensitive.
