## Problem

Transformer-based camouflaged object detection (COD) models suffer a dramatic accuracy cliff under W4A4 post-training quantization (S_α drops from ~0.89 to ~0.44), unlike the graceful degradation seen in classification tasks. The paper identifies the root cause as a token-local failure: heavy-tailed background tokens dominate a shared activation range, inflating the quantization step size and pushing weak-but-structured boundary cues into the zero bin, where subsequent attention layers cannot recover them. Existing PTQ methods (outlier suppression, layer/block reconstruction, dynamic bit-width) operate at the wrong granularity and do not explicitly bound the token-local step-to-dispersion ratio or zero-bin mass.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (384×384 in deployment experiments) fed into pretrained Transformer COD backbones (CFRN with Swin encoder, ESCNet with PVT encoder) and produces pixel-level binary segmentation masks for camouflaged objects. Quantization is applied to Linear and Conv operators in attention and MLP blocks, while LayerNorm and Softmax remain in FP16. The framework is purely post-training (no retraining) and uses a single calibration set of 128 images with fixed hyperparameters (g=32, τ=1.0, zr=0.2) shared across all four benchmarks and both model backbones.

## Method

COD-TDQ combines two coupled token-group-level modules. First, Direct-Sum Token-Group (DSTG) decomposes each token vector into fixed-size channel groups (size g=32) and assigns each group an independent clip radius estimated from its own magnitudes (max-abs or percentile), thereby decoupling background-driven outliers from the majority of tokens and eliminating cross-token range domination. Second, Dual-Constraint Range Projection (DCRP) projects each group's clip radius into a feasible interval bounded by two constraints: (C1) the step-to-dispersion ratio η_k = Δ_k/σ_k must not exceed a threshold τ, preventing the quantization step from being too coarse relative to the group's signal; and (C2) the empirical zero-bin mass ρ_{0,k} must not exceed a target zr, ensuring boundary activations are not rounded to zero. The final clip radius is the minimum of the base estimate and both constraint bounds, guaranteeing η_k ≤ τ and ρ_{0,k} ≤ zr by construction. This token-local, constraint-driven design directly targets the coupled range-domination and zero-bin-mass collapse loop identified in the diagnosis, restoring W4A4 accuracy to within ~0.05 of FP32 on S_α.

## Results

On CFRN across four COD benchmarks, COD-TDQ achieves Sα of 0.813 (CAMO), 0.862 (CHAMELEON), 0.802 (COD10K), and 0.837 (NC4K) under W4A4, recovering most of the FP32 performance (0.876–0.912) while naive W4A4 collapses to 0.418–0.471. Compared to the strongest PTQ baselines (RepQ-ViT, IGQ-ViT), COD-TDQ improves Sα by 11.8–20.9 points and reduces MAE by 0.008–0.020 absolute. On ESCNet, COD-TDQ reaches Sα = 0.881 vs. FP32's 0.893, a near-lossless W4A4 result, while the best baseline (IGQ-ViT/RepQ-ViT) only reaches 0.818. In real INT4 deployment on an RTX 4090, COD-TDQ achieves 44.21 FPS (1.50× over FP32) with 108.19 MB model size and 834.92 MB peak memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COD-TDQ outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and every metric under the W4A4 protocol. The gap is most dramatic against naive W4A4 (Sα 0.443 vs. 0.837 on NC4K) and outlier/noise-centric methods (ORQ-ViT, NoisyQuant, SmoothQuant, QuaRTZ, AHCPTQ), which all cluster around Sα 0.40–0.49 and fail to address token-local zero-bin collapse. Among reconstruction-fidelity methods, RepQ-ViT (Sα 0.718 on NC4K) and IGQ-ViT (0.710) are the strongest baselines, but COD-TDQ still leads by 11.9 and 12.7 Sα points respectively, because their layer/block-centric or channel-group mechanisms do not explicitly bound the step-to-dispersion ratio η or zero-bin mass ρ₀ per token group. PTQ4SAM (0.702) and FIMA-Q (0.683) are competitive on COD10K but lag on CAMO and CHAMELEON, where COD-TDQ's token-group constraints preserve weak boundary cues more effectively. ARCQuant (0.683 on NC4K) is the best architecture-aware baseline but still trails COD-TDQ by 15.4 Sα points, confirming that structural constraints alone are insufficient without token-local range control. On ESCNet, COD-TDQ (Sα 0.881) beats the next-best IGQ-ViT/RepQ-ViT (0.818) by 6.3 points, with DCRP contributing the majority of the recovery and DSTG closing the remaining gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of Transformer post-training quantization (PTQ) and camouflaged object detection (COD), departing from the dominant layer/block-centric PTQ paradigm (PTQ4ViT, RepQ-ViT, FIMA-Q, SmoothQuant) by identifying a task-specific, token-local failure mechanism unique to COD's reliance on weak boundary cues. While prior PTQ methods for ViT (outlier suppression, smoothing, bit-width allocation) and SAM (PQ-SAM, PTQ4SAM) address activation quantization at coarser granularities, COD-TDQ introduces token-group-level scaling (DSTG) and explicit dual-constraint range projection (DCRP) that directly bound the step-to-dispersion ratio and zero-bin mass—constraints not present in any prior PTQ formulation. The work also repurposes the diagnostic vocabulary of quantization (clip factor, step size, zero-bin mass) as task-specific health indicators, a framing absent from the broader PTQ literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic diagnosis of why W4A4 quantization specifically breaks COD (as opposed to gradual degradation), and a purely post-training, hardware-agnostic fix that recovers most of the FP32 accuracy gap without retraining—practically important for edge/mobile COD deployment. Limitations include evaluation on only two backbone architectures and four datasets, a single fixed hyperparameter set (g=32, τ=1.0, zr=0.2) across all settings, and a residual gap to FP32 (e.g., S_α 0.837 vs. 0.888 on NC4K/CFRN). Open questions the authors flag include decoder-side quantization diagnosis, cross-architecture generalization beyond Swin/PVT-style Transformers, and whether the dual-constraint formulation transfers to other dense-prediction tasks with similar weak-signal structure.
