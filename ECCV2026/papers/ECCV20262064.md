## Problem

RGB-guided dense depth completion assumes a sharp, structurally reliable guidance image, but under low-light conditions long-exposure RGB frames suffer severe motion blur that introduces misleading geometric cues and degrades depth reconstruction. Existing cascaded pipelines (deblur-then-complete) propagate restoration artifacts into the depth stage and fail to recover geometrically faithful guidance under extreme blur and low photon counts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262064/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single long-exposure blurred RGB image (H×W×3), a temporally corresponding event stream partitioned into T voxel-grid bins, and a sparse LiDAR point cloud sequence over T time steps, and produces a temporally consistent dense depth map sequence of T frames. On the Night-RIDE dataset all inputs are aligned and cropped to 256×256; on KITTI and NYUv2 the blurred RGB and event streams are synthesized from the original video sequences. The output is a full-resolution dense depth sequence rather than a single frame, with temporal consistency enforced through the self-recursive generation process.

## Method

ERU-Net couples two modules: (1) an Event-Guided Feature Restoration (EFR) module that uses an implicit neural representation (INR) with a self-recursive encoder-decoder to recover geometrically consistent latent features from the blurred RGB, where event-derived scale and shift vectors (γ, β) conditionally modulate the encoded spatial features at each time step, and each frame's output is fed back as a temporal prior for the next; and (2) a Restoration-Aware Depth Upsampling (RDU) module that progressively upsamples sparse LiDAR depth using a Hybrid Guidance Confidence Module (HGCM) to compute per-region confidence maps from both restored static features and dynamic event features, routing low-confidence regions through GARF (Guidance with Adaptive Receptive Fields) for gap-filling. Training follows a three-stage strategy: EFR pre-training with re-blur and event-gradient losses, RDU pre-training with sparse-aware depth supervision, and end-to-end joint fine-tuning where depth gradients refine the INR features for geometric consistency rather than perceptual quality.

## Results

On KITTI, ERU-Net achieves an RMSE of 888.20 and MAE of 209.70, outperforming the best baseline (EVSSM+Mari-DC) by 18.8% in RMSE. On NYUv2, ERU-Net reaches RMSE 2.603, MAE 1.039, and δ₁ of 99.5%, surpassing the strongest baseline (EVSSM+Mari-DC: RMSE 3.902) by 33.3% in RMSE. ERU-Net also attains the lowest iRMSE (2.00 on KITTI, 0.50 on NYUv2) and REL (0.010 on KITTI, 0.012 on NYUv2) across all five standard depth completion metrics. In terms of efficiency, ERU-Net is the most compact model at 29.8M parameters and 346.9G FLOPs, 1.3× smaller and 1.8× more efficient than DFU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERU-Net outperforms all four RGB-guided depth completion baselines (CSPN, Mari-DC, DFU, BP-Net) on every metric across both KITTI and NYUv2, even though each baseline is paired with the SOTA deblurring model EVSSM as a pre-processing step. The largest margin is against DFU on KITTI (RMSE 888.20 vs. 1908.00, a 53% reduction), while the closest competitor is Mari-DC (RMSE 888.20 vs. 1093.50 on KITTI; 2.603 vs. 3.902 on NYUv2). Against the event-based cascaded pipelines (EFNet+ and REFID+ combined with each depth completion method, Table 2), ERU-Net again wins on all five metrics, with the best cascaded pipeline (REFID+Mari-DC) reaching RMSE 3.145 on NYUv2 versus ERU-Net's 2.603. The paper attributes the consistent gap to two factors: (1) existing deblurring methods optimize for perceptual quality rather than geometric fidelity, and (2) the decoupled restore-then-complete paradigm allows restoration artifacts to propagate into depth estimation, whereas ERU-Net's tightly coupled EFR+RDU architecture enables end-to-end geometric optimization. ERU-Net also achieves the best accuracy-efficiency trade-off, being 1.3× smaller and 1.8× more efficient than DFU while delivering substantially lower error.

## Relation to Prior Work

ERU-Net sits at the intersection of two established lines: RGB-guided sparse-to-dense depth completion (CSPN, DFU, BP-Net) and event-guided image/video restoration (EFNet, REFID, Pan et al.). It departs from the dominant 'restore-then-complete' cascaded paradigm by tightly coupling an INR-based, self-recursive feature restoration module with a depth upsampling decoder (borrowing GARF from DFU) into a single jointly-optimized network. The key conceptual shift is that the restoration target is not a perceptually sharp image but a geometrically consistent latent feature, and the event stream serves as dual guidance (both for restoration and for depth upsampling via the novel HGCM) rather than solely as a deblurring cue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap in nighttime autonomous driving where long-exposure RGB is severely motion-blurred and sparse LiDAR alone is insufficient. The introduction of the Night-RIDE dataset (synchronized blurred RGB, events, and LiDAR at 256×256) provides a much-needed real-world benchmark for low-light multi-modal 3D perception. However, the real-world evaluation is primarily qualitative, the Night-RIDE dataset is small and low-resolution, and the quantitative benchmarks (KITTI, NYUv2) rely on simulated blur and synthetic events, leaving open questions about robustness to real sensor noise, higher resolutions, and real-time deployment. The finding that even SOTA event-based deblurring (EFNet, REFID) as a pre-processing step underperforms the unified approach suggests that geometric fidelity in the guidance signal matters more than perceptual image quality for downstream depth tasks.
