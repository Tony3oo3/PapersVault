## Problem

Existing driving world models fall into two inadequate paradigms: layout-conditioned renderers that require oracle future trajectories of all agents (making them non-reactive), and pure action-conditioned predictors that lack semantic control over multi-agent interactions and suffer from prohibitive diffusion latencies. No prior system simultaneously achieves photorealistic synthesis, strict action-controllability, data-driven reactive agents (driving sociology), and real-time inference speeds sufficient for closed-loop RL or human-in-the-loop simulation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes only three signals: a single initial front-view frame, the ego-vehicle's continuous trajectory (encoded as Plücker coordinates processed through an MLP), and a macroscopic text prompt serving as a global sociological prior (e.g., 'Polite' or 'Aggressive') rather than instance-level control. Crucially, no future bounding boxes or layouts of surrounding agents are provided, forcing the model to intrinsically predict causal reactions. Output is a streaming sequence of driving video chunks generated autoregressively at ≥12 FPS on a single A100 GPU, with O(1) per-chunk cost via KV-cached block causal attention.

## Method

CausalDrive builds on a Wan2.1-1.3B DiT backbone with Block Causal Attention, injecting ego-geometry via AdaLN and sociology prompts via cross-attention in a strict causal hierarchy. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) a base model with block causal masking, (2) a Causal AR Teacher trained with Teacher Forcing on ground-truth history using continuous flow matching to avoid PF-ODE injectivity violations that arise when distilling a bidirectional teacher into a causal student, and (3) Context-Forced DMD (Self-Corrective Forcing), where the student performs an N-chunk self-rollout and the frozen teacher is forced to evaluate distillation gradients conditioned on the student's flawed context, explicitly teaching error recovery over long horizons. This compresses inference to ≤4 function evaluations while preserving spatial-temporal consistency, and an adversarial discriminator attached to the fake score network preserves high-frequency road textures over extended rollouts.

## Results

On the nuPlan simulation reliability benchmark, CausalDrive achieves an FVD of 121.6 (vs. 196.21 for Orbis and 323.37 for Vista) while running at 12.4 FPS on a single A100 GPU—over 10× faster than all baselines. In terms of action controllability, CausalDrive attains the best ADE/FDE recall (0.52/0.59) among all methods, confirming faithful ego-trajectory adherence. On the proposed SocioDrive-Bench reactivity evaluation, CausalDrive achieves an 82.0% Yielding Compliance Rate (vs. 12.5% for Vista and 0% for log-replay), reducing the False-Collision Rate from 87.5% to 18.0%. For RL post-training on NavSim, the agent trained inside CausalDrive reaches a PDMS of 90.7 with a perfect Comfort score of 100, surpassing DiffusionDrive-v2 (90.3) and DriveDPO (90.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CausalDrive outperforms all video-generation baselines on FVD: it beats Orbis (196.21) by 74.6 points, GEM (291.84) by 170 points, Vista (323.37) by 202 points, and Cosmos (291.80) by 170 points, while simultaneously achieving 10–40× higher inference throughput (12.4 FPS vs. 0.3–1.2 FPS). On action controllability, CausalDrive leads in ADE/FDE recall (0.52/0.59) over Orbis (0.48/0.55), Vista (0.48/0.45), and GEM (0.47/0.54), though Vista and GEM report slightly higher ADE precision (0.25/0.27 vs. 0.45), suggesting CausalDrive trades a small precision margin for substantially better recall and speed. On the reactivity benchmark (Table 2), CausalDrive with a 'Polite' sociology prompt achieves 82.0% YCR and 18.0% FCR, dramatically outperforming Vista (12.5% YCR, 87.5% FCR) and eliminating the 100% ghost-collision rate of log-replay. In closed-loop evaluation (Table 4), CausalDrive as a simulation environment yields higher PDMS (0.7792) and lower ADS (0.1365) for the UniAD planner compared to DriveArena (0.6901 PDMS) and DrivingSphere (0.7281 PDMS). For RL post-training on NavSim (Table 5), the CausalDrive-trained agent achieves PDMS 90.7, edging out DiffusionDrive-v2 (90.3), DriveDPO (90.0), AD-R1 (89.8), DiffusionDrive (88.1), and UniAD (83.4), with the highest TTC (94.7) and a perfect Comfort score (100).

## Relation to Prior Work

CausalDrive sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) driving video generation, where it departs from both layout-conditioned renderers (MagicDrive, UniScene) that hard-code future agent positions, and action-conditioned predictors (Vista, Orbis) that lack semantic control over multi-agent reactions; and (2) efficient video distillation, where it builds directly on Causal Forcing's identification of the PF-ODE injectivity violation in bidirectional-to-AR distillation and Self Forcing's exposure-bias correction, extending both into a Context-Forced DMD framework tailored for long-horizon driving rollouts. The base architecture is initialized from Wan2.1's flow-matching DiT, and the distillation objective is a direct modification of DMD, with the key novelty being the teacher's conditioning on the student's flawed self-rollout rather than ground-truth history.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 12 FPS reactive driving simulation with semantic sociology control, CausalDrive addresses a practical bottleneck that has kept generative world models confined to offline data augmentation rather than interactive policy learning. The SocioDrive-Bench benchmark and its VLM-driven annotation pipeline provide a reusable resource for training and evaluating reactive driving models. Key limitations include the monocular front-view-only setting, the sociology prompt operating as a global scene prior rather than per-agent instance-level control, and the RL post-training gains over DiffusionDrive-v2 being marginal (90.7 vs 90.3 PDMS). Extending to multi-camera surround-view and achieving truly instance-level causal control over individual agents remain open challenges.
