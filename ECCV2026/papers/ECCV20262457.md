## Problem

When Multimodal Large Language Models are adapted to Universal Multimodal Retrieval via contrastive learning, they suffer from 'grain blindness'—a representational collapse where the model overlooks fine-grained semantic units (e.g., specific actions or attributes) in complex multi-grain queries. This occurs because contrastive learning treats all negatives identically as a binary classification, ignoring the differential relevance information each negative carries. The paper further identifies the challenge of incentivizing ranking ability without expensive, explicit ranking labels.

## Input / Output

The model accepts queries in any modality (text, image, or interleaved image-text) along with a candidate set of N items, and produces a top-k ranked list by cosine similarity. Unusually, embedding extraction is formulated as a generative process: the model must autoregressively produce a textual summary of the input followed by a special [RET] token, and the hidden state at [RET] serves as the retrieval embedding. During the RL stage, G independent rollouts are generated per query to compute relative rewards across embedding sets.

## Method

ELVA is a three-stage framework: (1) language-only pretraining and (2) instruction tuning both use InfoNCE contrastive loss on M-BEIR, followed by (3) a ranking-driven RL stage using GRPO with verifiable rewards. The key mechanism reformulates embedding extraction as autoregressive generation (summary + [RET] token) to introduce representational variance that enables RL exploration, since fixed-prompt EOL extraction yields no variance. Two rule-based rewards jointly optimize the policy: a Margin Reward enforcing a minimum similarity gap between the positive and hardest negative, and a continuous Ranking Reward with rank-dependent log-weighting that encourages correct positive placement while structuring the ordering among negatives. A balanced negative sampling strategy (50 filtered hard negatives above a similarity threshold + 50 random negatives) maintains reward variance for stable policy optimization.

## Results

On the M-BEIR test set (16 sub-tasks across 8 query/candidate modality combinations), ELVA-7B achieves a 58.7 average Recall@K, outperforming the previous SOTA LamRA-Ret-7B (56.6) by 3.9% and the 2B variant ELVA-2B reaches 53.8, surpassing MM-Embed-7B (52.7) despite being smaller. On the newly introduced MRBench (multi-grain queries), ELVA-7B achieves 43.2 average Recall@5, a 13.1% improvement over LamRA-Ret-7B (38.1), demonstrating effective mitigation of grain blindness. On unseen datasets, ELVA-7B leads across all settings, including a 9.7% gain in Image-Text Matching accuracy (91.1% vs. 85.8%) and strong zero-shot text-to-video retrieval (R@1 46.4 on MSR-VTT, 53.9 on MSVD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELVA-7B outperforms all supervised MLLM baselines on the M-BEIR average: it beats LamRA-Ret-7B (56.6) by 2.1 points, PUMA-3B (54.4) by 4.3 points, and MM-Embed-7B (52.7) by 6.0 points. Against zero-shot MLLMs, ELVA-7B leads Qwen2.5-VL-7B (46.7) by 12.0 points and SigLIP (37.2) by 21.5 points. On the multi-grain MRBench, ELVA-7B (43.2) surpasses LamRA-Ret-7B (38.1) by 5.1 points and Qwen2-VL-7B (34.3) by 8.9 points, confirming its advantage on complex compositional queries. On unseen datasets (Table 2), ELVA-7B achieves the best results across all 12 evaluation settings, including 91.1% ITM accuracy vs. LamRA's 85.8% and 96.6% R@1 on Share4V text-to-image vs. LamRA's 93.3%. In zero-shot text-to-video retrieval, ELVA-7B beats InternVideo (R@1 40.0) by 6.4 points and UMT-L (R@1 49.9 on MSVD) by 4.0 points, though it remains below the dedicated video model InternVideo2 (R@1 55.9/59.3). The 2B variant ELVA-2B also surpasses the larger MM-Embed-7B on most sub-tasks, demonstrating strong parameter efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELVA builds directly on the LamRA two-stage pipeline (language-only pre-training with InfoNCE, followed by instruction tuning on M-BEIR) and extends the RLVR paradigm from DeepSeek-R1 into the multimodal retrieval domain, using GRPO as the optimization backbone. Its key conceptual departure from prior MLLM-based retrieval methods (MM-Embed, PUMA, LamRA) is replacing the uniform contrastive treatment of negatives with a ranking-driven RL objective that differentiates negatives by their similarity to the positive, thereby addressing what the authors formalize as 'grain blindness'—a representational collapse caused by gradient starvation of secondary semantic units during contrastive training. The framework also introduces a generative embedding extraction mechanism (autoregressive summary + [RET] token) to enable RL exploration, departing from the fixed-prompt hidden-state extraction used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification and formalization of grain blindness as a concrete failure mode of contrastive learning in retrieval provides a useful diagnostic lens for the field, and the 13.1% gain on the new MRBench benchmark (albeit small at 1k queries) validates the diagnosis. The demonstration that the RL stage functions as a plug-and-play post-training booster on unrelated architectures (PUMA, MM-Embed) suggests broad practical applicability for practitioners who already have contrastive-trained retrievers. However, the high inference cost of MLLM-based retrieval remains a significant deployment barrier, video retrieval performance still lags behind specialized video models, and the MRBench benchmark's small scale limits confidence in the multi-grain improvement claim.
