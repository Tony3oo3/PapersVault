## Problem

Dynamic 4D facial reconstruction requires simultaneously recovering detailed geometry and establishing dense correspondences across time, but existing methods treat these as separate problems or rely on motion-based formulations that are difficult to learn and computationally expensive. The paper identifies that canonical facial coordinate prediction can unify both tasks into a single canonical reconstruction problem, since canonical geometry is largely shared across poses and expressions, providing a stable reference for correspondence estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261340/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 1 to N RGB images (multi-view at a single timestamp or single-camera across multiple timestamps) at a base resolution of 504×504 and produces, in a single forward pass, per-pixel depth maps, ray maps, and canonical facial coordinate maps. Dense correspondences between any two frames are then obtained via KD-Tree nearest-neighbor search in the predicted canonical space, requiring less than 0.2 seconds per image pair on CPU. The final output is a temporally consistent 4D visible-surface reconstruction with dense point trajectories.

## Method

The core contribution is a transformer-based architecture (built on DA3 with an added DPT head) that jointly predicts depth, ray, and canonical facial coordinate maps from multiple input images, where each pixel is assigned a 3D coordinate in a shared FLAME canonical space rather than a frame-to-frame deformation. Correspondences reduce to nearest-neighbor search in canonical space, eliminating the need for explicit motion modeling. Training proceeds in two stages: pretraining on DAViD for facial geometric priors, then finetuning on a NeRSemble-based dataset where COLMAP multi-view reconstructions are aligned to FLAME canonical space to provide dense canonical supervision. The training alternates between multi-view single-timestamp and single-camera multi-timestamp sampling to bridge the domain gap between reconstruction and tracking, and uses a composite loss (regression + confidence-weighted regression + gradient) with a 5× higher weight on canonical losses since that head is trained from scratch.

## Results

On monocular depth estimation, Face Anything achieves RMSE of 0.077 (image) / 0.075 (video) on NeRSemble and 0.067 / 0.065 on Ava-256, outperforming the next-best Sapiens-2B (0.085 / 0.081) by ~10–16%. For 3D point tracking on NeRSemble, the method achieves an EPE of 0.004–0.005 across all temporal margins, roughly 3× lower than V-DPM (0.014–0.016). Inference runs in 5 seconds on 40 images with 19 GB peak memory, compared to 160 seconds and 40 GB for V-DPM, while supporting up to 470 images per GPU versus 74.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Face Anything outperforms all seven baselines on depth estimation across both NeRSemble and Ava-256, with the largest margins over DA3 (0.162→0.077 RMSE on NeRSemble) and DAViD (0.182→0.067 on Ava-256), while beating the strongest depth baseline Sapiens-2B by ~10% RMSE. Against V-DPM, the primary dynamic reconstruction competitor, the method achieves ~3× lower 3D correspondence EPE (0.004 vs 0.014), 32× faster inference (5 s vs 160 s), and half the GPU memory (19 GB vs 40 GB). On 2D tracking (NeRSemble), Face Anything reduces EPE from 3.089 (P3DMM, face-only) to 1.719, and from 5.550 (P3DMM, with hair) to 1.838, while also improving cycle-consistency CCE on VFHQ from 0.797 (V-DPM) to 0.398 at margin=5. For FLAME tracking, the method achieves CD-L1 of 0.195 versus 0.238 for P3DMM. The only baseline that is competitive on a single metric is Sapiens-2B on Ava-256 AbsRel (0.056 vs 0.048), where the gap is modest; on all other reported metrics Face Anything is the best-performing method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DA3 architecture for feed-forward depth and ray map prediction, extending it with a DPT-style canonical map head to jointly predict normalized facial coordinates. It departs from the motion-based formulations of V-DPM and Dynamic Point Maps, which predict inter-frame deformations, by reframing correspondence estimation as reconstruction in a shared canonical space where nearest-neighbor search replaces iterative motion prediction. The approach also contrasts with parametric methods like P3DMM that are constrained by fixed FLAME topology, instead producing non-parametric dense geometry supervised by parametric alignment. The dataset construction pipeline combines NeRSemble multi-view captures, COLMAP reconstruction, and FLAME tracking to generate the canonical supervision that existing datasets lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The canonical map formulation offers a clean unification of 4D reconstruction and dense tracking in a single feed-forward pass, with a 32× inference speedup over V-DPM (5 s vs. 160 s) and 3× lower correspondence error, making real-time dynamic face reconstruction practically viable. The approach is inherently face-specific and relies on learned facial priors, limiting generalization to non-face scenes or heavily occluded views, and the authors note that canonicalization of nearby objects (hands, accessories) is unreliable. Open questions include whether the canonical-map idea extends to other deformable objects, how it interacts with generative or neural rendering pipelines for avatar creation, and whether the NeRSemble-based supervision can be replaced by cheaper synthetic alternatives.
