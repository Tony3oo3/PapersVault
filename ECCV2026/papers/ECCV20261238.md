## Problem

Sparsely Annotated Object Detection (SAOD) and Open-World Object Detection (OWOD) have been addressed in isolation, but in real-world settings unlabeled regions are inherently ambiguous: they may be missing annotations of known classes or genuinely unseen objects. No prior framework jointly resolves this dual ambiguity, causing detectors to either suppress valid known instances or misclassify unknown objects as known (and vice versa), blurring the decision boundary between the two.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261238/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images with sparse annotations where only a subset of known-class instances are labeled per image, processed through two augmented views for cross-view comparison. It outputs bounding boxes with known-class labels and separate unknown object detections, evaluated via K-mAP (known) and U-Recall (unknown) under a 4-task incremental protocol on Pascal VOC and MS-COCO. The model runs at 2.52 FPS with 106.2M parameters and 965.4 GFLOPs on a single GPU.

## Method

DPOD builds on a Faster R-CNN (ResNet-50) backbone within CROWD's class-agnostic framework and introduces two complementary modules. The Known Target Recovery Module (KTRM) recovers unlabeled known objects via a Co-Student-style student-teacher pseudo-labeling pipeline and enforces known-unknown feature separation through a facility-location conditional gain objective that pushes each known proposal away from its nearest unknown counterpart. The Dual-Disagreement Target Generator (DDTG) identifies reliable unknown candidates by projecting RoIs across two augmented views, computing cosine similarity between aligned class logits, and selecting proposals that exceed an objectness threshold while falling below a semantic consistency threshold. The two modules jointly resolve the ambiguity of unlabeled regions: KTRM prevents known objects from being absorbed into the unknown set, while DDTG prevents unknown objects from being prematurely consolidated into known categories, with both contributing classification and separation losses to the overall training objective.

## Results

Under the Easy sparsity setting (≈18.3% labels removed), DPOD achieves 56.48 K-mAP and 55.34 U-Recall on Task 1, surpassing the strongest baseline CROWD (53.28 / 45.53) by +3.20%p and +9.81%p respectively. As sparsity intensifies to Extreme (≈65.8% labels removed), DPOD maintains 45.37 K-mAP and 45.56 U-Recall on Task 1, while CROWD drops to 34.37 / 40.66 and OrthogonalDet to 41.39 / 12.41. The U-Recall gap over OrthogonalDet widens to over 29.55%p in multiple tasks under Extreme sparsity, demonstrating that DPOD's dual-perspective design preserves unknown-object discovery far more robustly than prior OWOD methods under annotation scarcity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CROWD (the primary baseline and architectural foundation), DPOD consistently improves U-Recall across all sparsity levels: +9.81%p in Easy Task 1, +14.90%p in Hard Task 2, and +16.89%p in Extreme Task 3, while maintaining competitive K-mAP (within ~1–2%p). Against OrthogonalDet, the U-Recall advantage is dramatic—+38.29%p in Easy Task 1 and +29.97%p in Hard Task 2—though OrthogonalDet retains a slight K-mAP edge in a few settings (e.g., Hard Task 2: 38.03 vs. 33.36). PROB and RandBox are substantially outperformed on both metrics in every sparsity setting; for instance, in Extreme Task 1, DPOD achieves 45.37 K-mAP / 45.56 U-Recall versus PROB's 37.78 / 17.86 and RandBox's 36.62 / 0.68. The key pattern is that DPOD's U-Recall degrades far more gracefully as sparsity increases (e.g., only 17.31%p drop from Full to Extreme in Task 2 vs. 43.84%p for OrthogonalDet), confirming that the dual KTRM+DDTG design specifically addresses the ambiguity that causes prior OWOD methods to collapse under sparse supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously independent research threads—Open-World Object Detection (OWOD) and Sparsely Annotated Object Detection (SAOD)—into a single task (SA-OWOD) and framework (DPOD). Methodologically, it builds directly on CROWD's facility-location formulation for unknown discovery and Co-Student's collaborative pseudo-labeling for sparse annotation recovery, repurposing both within a joint known-unknown separation objective. The key departure is that prior OWOD methods (ORE, OW-DETR, PROB, OrthogonalDet) assume dense annotations and prior SAOD methods (uDenseTeacher, Co-mining, Co-Student) assume a closed world; DPOD is the first to explicitly model the co-occurrence of unlabeled known and unknown instances and resolve their contradictory supervision signals through complementary pseudo-label recovery and cross-view semantic disagreement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SA-OWOD captures a realistic deployment scenario where annotations are both incomplete and the category set is open, a combination that prior work has addressed only in isolation. The constructed sparse open-world benchmark and evaluation protocol (K-mAP + U-Recall across incremental tasks) provide the community with a standardized way to measure both known-object recovery and unknown-object discovery under ambiguous supervision. A notable limitation is the reliance on fixed objectness and similarity thresholds (τ_o, τ_s), which the authors acknowledge is suboptimal for categories with inherently low objectness (e.g., accessories); task-adaptive thresholding and extension beyond Faster R-CNN backbones remain open. The modest K-mAP trade-offs in intermediate tasks (e.g., Hard Task 2) suggest that balancing known-class stability against unknown exploration under extreme sparsity is still an unsolved tension.
