## Problem

Recent domain generalization methods built on vision-language models assume that preserving expressive visual representations improves robustness, but the paper shows this visual expressiveness instead propagates spurious domain-specific cues that entangle with true semantics and hinder invariant learning. The gap is the absence of a principled mechanism to separate core class semantics from domain-specific variations in the supervision signal itself, rather than merely aligning feature distributions across source domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260627/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and outputs a class label via cosine similarity between the learned image embedding and fixed text anchors. The text anchors are precomputed once from a frozen CLIP text encoder using the simple prompt template 'a photo of a [class]', and remain constant throughout training. The visual encoder is fully trainable and architecture-agnostic (ResNet-50, ViT-B/16, CLIP-ViT-B/16, EfficientNet, SwinT, DINOv2, etc.), with all embeddings l2-normalized onto the unit hypersphere.

## Method

The paper proposes a Text-Anchored Information Bottleneck that discards visual guidance entirely and anchors supervision to fixed, domain-invariant text embeddings. Formulated as a Conditional Entropy Bottleneck (CEB), the objective maximizes I(Z;Y) via a semantic distillation loss (cosine-similarity softmax against text anchors) while minimizing I(Z;X|Y) through two complementary terms: a compression loss that maximizes the intra-class resultant length (encouraging features to concentrate along a coherent direction) and an alignment loss that aligns each class-mean feature with its corresponding text anchor. The variational bound on I(Z;X|Y) is derived using von Mises-Fisher distributions on the unit sphere, yielding tractable closed-form objectives. The key design choice is that text anchors are frozen and class-conditional, providing a semantic prior that signals what to preserve without injecting domain bias, in contrast to prior IB-based DG methods that use uninformative Gaussian priors or learned priors that inherit source-domain statistics.

## Results

The method achieves state-of-the-art average accuracy across all three backbone families: 75.4% on ResNet-50 (ImageNet-1k), 79.5% on ViT-B/16 (ImageNet-1k), and 86.3% on CLIP-ViT-B/16 (400M), evaluated over VLCS, PACS, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita, and DomainNet. On the background-shift benchmark NICO++, it reaches 95.7% (ResNet-50) and 97.5% (CLIP), substantially outperforming the next-best methods (82.6% and 91.8% respectively). The largest single-dataset gain is on PACS with ResNet-50 (96.9% vs. 89.4% for RISE), and on TerraIncognita with CLIP (75.1% vs. 63.3% for CLIP-DTP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 12 baselines across every backbone and dataset, with no losses reported. Against the strongest cross-modal distillation baselines (RISE and VL2V, which use both CLIP visual and text guidance), the gap is 7.1 points on ResNet-50 (75.4 vs. 68.3), 4.0 points on ViT-B/16 (79.5 vs. 75.5), and 7.1–14.8 points on CLIP (86.3 vs. 71.5–77.7). Among CLIP-specific methods, the closest competitor is CLIP-DTP (79.2 avg), which the method surpasses by 7.1 points, with the largest per-dataset margin on TerraIncognita (75.1 vs. 63.3). Classical DG methods without external guidance (MIRO, SAGM, GESTUR, INSURE) trail by 8–11 points on average, confirming that the text-anchored bottleneck provides a substantial advantage over purely distribution-alignment or regularization strategies. On NICO++ (background shift), the advantage widens further: 95.7% vs. 82.6% (VL2V) for ResNet-50 and 97.5% vs. 91.8% (VL2V) for CLIP, indicating the method is particularly effective when the shift is primarily in background rather than object appearance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Conditional Entropy Bottleneck (CEB) formulation of Fischer (2020), but repurposes it in a fundamentally different way: rather than using a simple non-semantic prior (e.g., N(0,I)) as prior IB-based DG methods do, it anchors the bottleneck to fixed, class-conditional text embeddings from a frozen CLIP text encoder. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant trend in CLIP-based DG (RISE, VL2V, CLIPood, CLIP-DTP, etc.), which treats the visual encoder's pre-trained representations as the primary source of invariance to be preserved. The paper's central argument is that this visual preservation actually propagates domain-specific spurious cues, and that the text space—being structurally stable across domains—should instead serve as the sole supervisory anchor, effectively discarding visual guidance entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes the DG problem from 'improving visual representations' to 'designing supervision that enforces invariance,' a shift with implications beyond this specific method. A notable open question is the role of semantic content in anchors: random anchors already beat prior SOTA on OfficeHome (76.6 vs. 74.4), suggesting stable external geometry may matter more than learned semantics, yet the gap widens sharply on harder benchmarks (DomainNet: 58.3 vs. 36.4), leaving the boundary between 'any stable anchor' and 'semantically meaningful anchor' unresolved. Limitations include the lack of contextual cues in fixed class-level prompts (which can fail when background or scene context is genuinely diagnostic) and the acknowledged data-leakage concern in standard DG benchmarks that may inflate absolute gains. Future work on adaptive or instance-level anchors, and on isolating true generalization from pretraining overlap, would strengthen the practical impact.
