## Problem

Existing 3DGS GANs for human heads produce photorealistic avatars but their latent spaces strongly entangle semantic attributes, so editing one feature (e.g., adding glasses) unintentionally alters identity, hair, or skin. Post-hoc disentanglement methods like GANSpace or InterFaceGAN cannot guarantee complete separation and typically require pre-trained classifiers. The paper asks whether structural decomposition of the generator itself can achieve guaranteed, classifier-free disentanglement of head components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260678/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sub-generator receives its own random latent vector plus sparse conditions: a 10-bin RGB color histogram (30-dim total across hair, skin, torso) that directly modulates Gaussian color attributes in the final decoder layer, and a boolean flag for additive components like glasses. Two shared 512-dim context vectors (shape and light) are distributed to all sub-generators to enforce geometric and illumination consistency. The composed 3DGS scene is rendered at 512² or 1024² resolution via a differentiable 3DGS renderer, producing a single 2D head image without background.

## Method

The generator is decomposed into four independent CGS-GAN-based sub-generators (hair, face, glasses, torso) whose Gaussian primitives are concatenated into one scene. Component separation is achieved implicitly: additive components are gated by a boolean flag that suppresses opacity when absent, while permanent components are distinguished solely by color histograms that modulate the final color decoder, avoiding segmentation masks or geometric priors. Global consistency is enforced through three mechanisms: (1) latent mixing at 10% probability during training, where backbone features of one component are randomly swapped to simulate inference-time editing; (2) a shared shape context vector fed exclusively to the final xyz decoder to coordinate head geometry without leaking identity; and (3) a shared light context vector that conditions the input latent, regularized by an LPIPS penalty to restrict its effect to illumination changes. An EMA face geometry loss further prevents the face generator from under-covering regions near the hairline.

## Results

On FFHQC, COSY achieves a FID₃D of 4.42 at 512² and 5.43 at 1024², matching or slightly exceeding the unconditional CGS-GAN baseline (4.53 / 5.25) despite the added compositional training complexity. For disentangled editing, COSY reduces LPIPS drift in unrelated regions to 0.016 (glasses) and 0.014 (hair), a 10–15× improvement over the best prior editing method (StyleFlow: 0.172 / 0.133). Glasses editing recall reaches 0.997, compared to 0.508 (GANSpace), 0.632 (InterFaceGAN), and 0.948 (StyleFlow).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COSY outperforms all editing baselines by a wide margin on LPIPS stability: GANSpace (0.254) and InterFaceGAN (0.196) show the most entanglement, StyleFlow (0.172) is the strongest prior method but still 10× worse, and the conditional CGS-GAN baseline (0.239) confirms that simply adding color/glasses conditioning to a monolithic generator does not solve entanglement. On editing recall, COSY (0.997) nearly matches StyleFlow (0.948) and Cond CGS-GAN (0.952), while GANSpace (0.508) and InterFaceGAN (0.632) fail to produce glasses in roughly half of trials. On visual quality (FID₃D), COSY (4.42) slightly beats CGS-GAN (4.53) at 512² but is marginally worse at 1024² (5.43 vs 5.25), and substantially outperforms GSGAN (7.68) and GGHead (7.78). EGG3D is compared only visually (Fig. 6) and produces noticeably degraded side views due to its view-conditioned generation, whereas COSY maintains consistent quality across viewpoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CGS-GAN's unconstrained 3DGS generator architecture and EG3D's conditional discriminator, repurposing them as per-component sub-generators within a compositional framework. It departs from prior compositional 3D head generation (3DGH, EGG3D) by eliminating explicit geometric priors (FLAME template meshes) and segmentation masks, instead relying on sparse 10-bin color histograms and boolean flags to implicitly separate hair, skin, glasses, and torso. It also contrasts with post-hoc latent editing methods (GANSpace, InterFaceGAN, StyleFlow) by achieving disentanglement architecturally rather than through learned editing directions, and with reference-based editing (Barbershop, HairCLIPv2) by enabling free latent exploration in real time without requiring a reference image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that architectural decomposition can achieve near-perfect disentanglement (LPIPS ~0.015 vs. 0.17–0.25 for prior methods) while maintaining competitive FID, offering a practical path toward controllable 3D avatar pipelines for film, games, and synthetic data generation. Open questions include the sensitivity of the light-context regularization parameter, whether the 10% latent mixing probability generalizes to more components, and whether the approach scales to full-body avatars or non-human domains as the authors speculate. A limitation is that the method is currently validated only on the FFHQC head dataset, and the near-zero LPIPS values, while impressive, are partly a consequence of the architectural design rather than a learned disentanglement.
