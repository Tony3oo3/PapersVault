## Problem

Existing driving world models face a fundamental trade-off: 2D-centric approaches generate visually appealing but geometrically inconsistent scenes, while explicit 3D methods (occupancy grids, point clouds) are computationally expensive and annotation-heavy. No prior method simultaneously forecasts appearance, geometry, and semantics in a unified 3D representation while maintaining efficient, action-controllable generation without relying on cascaded multi-stage pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260772/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 6 multi-view camera images at 256×704 resolution along with ego-action sequences and historical context, encoding them into a frozen DriveTok 3D scene representation (768 channels, 32×32 spatial grid) that is agnostic to camera layout. A compressor then reduces this to a compact 64-channel latent, which the generative module predicts for the next timestep. Decoding from the predicted latent simultaneously yields multi-view RGB, metric depth maps, 2D semantic segmentation, and a 200×200×16 3D occupancy grid (0.4 m voxel resolution, covering ±40 m laterally and −1 to 5.4 m vertically) in a single forward pass, with no external volumetric conditions or pretrained image generators required.

## Method

Doe-2 builds a three-stage pipeline: (1) a frozen DriveTok scene encoder/decoder provides a unified 3D token space integrating texture, semantics, and geometry; (2) a lightweight VAE-style compressor (single self-attention + linear projection) maps 768-dim scene tokens to a 64-dim latent with reparameterized sampling, using an asymmetric strategy where the stochastic latent feeds the generator but the deterministic mean serves as the training target; (3) a 24-layer autoregressive transformer with 3D RoPE over (t, y, x) and per-layer action injection via zero-initialized MLPs predicts coarse future latents, followed by a 3-layer DiT head (hidden dim 3072) trained with Rectified Flow that refines only high-frequency spatial details. The key architectural insight is that the AR model resolves global spatio-temporal structure, drastically simplifying the diffusion target so that a minimal DiT suffices for fidelity—avoiding the iterative denoising cost of full-scale diffusion while retaining the sharpness that pure AR lacks. Training proceeds in two stages (compressor: 5 epochs, 45M params; generator: 100 epochs, 590M params) on 700 nuScenes scenes from scratch.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, Doe-2 achieves an FID of 10.48 for multi-view future image generation while being the only method that is simultaneously model-free, volumetric-condition-free, and multi-view capable. For 4D occupancy forecasting on Occ3D-nuScenes (camera input only), Doe-2 attains 26.29% average semantic mIoU and 60.73% average geometric IoU at 1–3 s horizons, substantially outperforming the next-best camera-input baseline COME-F (22.26% mIoU, 44.07% IoU). In depth forecasting, Doe-2 reduces AbsRel to 0.15 (vs. 0.23 for OmniNWM) and reaches δ<1.25 = 0.87. For motion planning, Doe-2 achieves 0.78 m average L2 trajectory error and 0.39% collision rate using only 3D-occupancy and depth supervision, outperforming GenAD (0.91 m, 0.43%) and OmniDrive (0.84 m, 0.94%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In 4D occupancy forecasting (camera input), Doe-2 dominates all baselines: it surpasses COME-F by +4.03 mIoU and +16.66 IoU on average, and DTT by +6.69 mIoU and +24.62 IoU, while also running at ~4 FPS. For depth forecasting, Doe-2 outperforms OmniNWM (the strongest prior method) by 0.08 AbsRel and 0.06 δ<1.25, and beats SurroundDepth and M2Depth by even larger margins. In image generation (FID), Doe-2 (10.48) trails UniScene (6.45), Vista (6.90), and DiST-4D (7.40), but it is the only method that is simultaneously model-free, volumetric-condition-free, and multi-view; among that restricted group it beats BEVGen (25.54), MagicDrive-V2 (20.91), and GenAD (15.40) by wide margins. For motion planning, Doe-2 achieves 0.78 m average L2 error and 0.39% collision rate, outperforming GenAD (0.91 m, 0.43%) and OmniDrive (0.84 m, 0.94%) while using only 3D-occupancy and depth supervision rather than HD maps, agent motion, or language; however, it is slightly behind UniUGP (0.33% collision) and VAD-Base† (0.22% collision under the VAD metric). Overall, Doe-2's largest and most consistent wins are in 3D geometric tasks (occupancy, depth), confirming that the unified 3D latent representation is the key differentiator over 2D-centric or occupancy-input baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

Doe-2 builds directly on DriveTok as a frozen 3D scene tokenizer, extending it with an FAE-inspired feature compressor to create a compact latent space suitable for generative modeling. It departs from 2D-centric world models (DriveDreamer, Vista, MagicDrive) that lack geometric awareness, and from explicit 3D methods (OccWorld, BEV-World) that require costly volumetric inputs, by operating entirely in a camera-only 3D latent representation. The hybrid AR + lightweight DiT architecture is inspired by recent findings that autoregressive modeling in low-dimensional spaces simplifies diffusion, and the Rectified Flow formulation follows Liu et al. The key conceptual departure from multimodal world models (UniScene, OmniNWM, HERMES) is the single-pass unified decoding that avoids cascaded multi-stage pipelines and their compounding errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified 3D latent formulation enables simultaneous generation of multi-view RGB, depth, semantics, and 3D occupancy in one forward pass, which is practically important for closed-loop simulation where geometric consistency across modalities is critical. The finding that aggressive channel compression (768→64) actually improves generation quality rather than merely trading off fidelity for speed is a useful architectural insight for latent-space world models. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (nuScenes), reliance on the pretrained DriveTok encoder, and moderate inference speed (~4–6 FPS). Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer horizons, more diverse driving environments, and whether the DriveTok dependency can be replaced by a fully end-to-end trainable encoder.
