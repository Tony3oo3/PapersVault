## Problem

Existing LLM/VLM-based driving methods map inputs directly to actions without modeling how scenes evolve over time, while generative world models simulate spatio-temporal dynamics but lack language grounding and instruction-following. No prior framework unifies multimodal understanding and video-based world modeling within a single closed-loop end-to-end driving architecture, leaving a gap in robust long-horizon planning for rare and open-world scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262086/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three synchronized 224×224 RGB camera views (left, front, right) sampled at 10 Hz over an 8-frame temporal window, a natural-language driving instruction, and the current action. It produces four future waypoints at 0.2 s intervals, a binary instruction-completion flag, and eight future multi-view video frames spanning 0.8 s at 0.1 s increments. Waypoints are converted to low-level brake/throttle/steering commands via two independent PID controllers, and the system supports two inference modes: low-latency online planning (diffusion discarded) and offline autoregressive video generation where predicted frames and actions feed back as the next timestep's input.

## Method

LMGenDrive couples a frozen ResNet-based multi-view vision encoder (producing BEV, waypoint, and traffic-light tokens) with a Vicuna-7B LLM via a Q-Former that compresses ~2k visual tokens per frame into 8 compact frame-level features. Two sets of learnable queries—world queries and action queries—are injected into the LLM: action queries are decoded by an MLP into waypoints and a completion flag, while world queries serve as conditioning for a U-Net diffusion generator (Stable Diffusion 1.5 + AnimateDiff) that synthesizes multi-view future video, with CLIP-encoded last-frame images fused via cross-attention for appearance consistency. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) vision-encoder pretraining on 3 M CARLA frames for perception, (2) single-step joint fine-tuning of LLM and video generator for instruction grounding, and (3) multi-step long-horizon training where previously generated videos are autoregressively fed back, strengthening temporal abstraction. The key architectural insight is that video generation enriches the LLM's spatio-temporal scene understanding while the LLM supplies semantic priors and instruction grounding to the world model, yielding complementary gains in both planning quality and generation fidelity.

## Results

On the LangAuto benchmark (the most challenging track), LMGenDrive achieves a driving score (DS) of 62.2±3.3, route completion (RC) of 74.5±4.1, and infraction score (IS) of 0.85±0.04, substantially outperforming the strongest prior methods. On LangAuto-Short and LangAuto-Tiny, the method reaches DS of 77.1±4.1 and 84.1±3.6 respectively, with IS values of 0.88 and 0.92. For generated video quality, the model achieves FID of 6.3 and FVD of 286 at the 8-frame horizon, degrading to FID 15.1 / FVD 981 at 128 frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMGenDrive outperforms all three baselines on every metric across all three LangAuto tracks. Against LMDrive [41], the improvement is dramatic: DS jumps from 10.7 to 62.2 (+51.5) on LangAuto, reflecting the added world-model generation and multi-stage training. Compared to AD-H† [63], LMGenDrive gains +18.2 DS on LangAuto (62.2 vs. 44.0) and +6.6 DS on LangAuto-Tiny (84.1 vs. 77.5), while also achieving a higher IS (0.85 vs. 0.83) on the hardest track. Against BEVDriver [56], the gains are +13.3 DS on LangAuto (62.2 vs. 48.9) and +13.9 DS on LangAuto-Short (77.1 vs. 66.7), with IS matching or exceeding BEVDriver on all tracks. The consistent advantage across route length suggests the unified understanding-generation architecture is particularly effective for longer, more complex driving scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

LMGenDrive sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: LLM/VLM-based driving agents (LMDrive, BEVDriver, AD-H) that provide semantic reasoning and instruction following but lack temporal scene modeling, and generative world models for driving (GAIA, Vista, DriveWM, DriveDreamer) that simulate future video but lack language grounding and closed-loop planning. The paper's architectural contribution is unifying these into a single closed-loop system where an LLM (Vicuna-7B) produces conditioning queries for a diffusion-based multi-view video generator (Stable Diffusion 1.5 + AnimateDiff), with the video generation feeding back to enrich the LLM's spatio-temporal understanding. It builds most directly on LMDrive's vision-encoder-plus-LLM-plus-action-query pipeline, extending it with world queries and a U-Net diffusion head, and on DriveWM's joint video-action modeling by adding language instruction conditioning and true closed-loop evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first empirical evidence that jointly training a language model and a video generator in a closed driving loop yields complementary benefits: the LLM gains spatio-temporal grounding from video prediction, while the world model gains semantic and instruction-following structure from the LLM. However, all evaluation is confined to the CARLA simulator on the LangAuto benchmark, so real-world transferability, latency constraints for online deployment, and robustness to sensor noise remain open. The significant quality degradation at longer horizons (FVD nearly quadrupling from 8 to 128 frames) highlights that autoregressive video generation is still far from reliable for extended planning, and the reliance on relatively dated components (SD 1.5, Vicuna-7B) suggests the approach's ceiling may rise substantially with newer backbones.
