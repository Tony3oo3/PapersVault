## Problem

Existing prompt-based continual learning methods rely on deterministic prompt vectors that suffer from a prompt collapse problem: the learned prompts become highly similar to one another and fail to capture the diverse data distributions that emerge as new tasks are incrementally introduced. This limits the expressive power of the prompt pool and degrades performance, particularly as intra-task feature variation grows over the course of continual learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image, extracts a query feature from a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone, and uses that feature to condition a mixture of learned Gaussian prompt distributions. The sampled and aggregated prompt tokens are prepended to the input token sequence via prefix tuning, and the backbone produces a class label prediction. No task identifier or external memory is required at inference; the model classifies over all previously seen and current classes jointly in the class-incremental setting.

## Method

Each prompt component is modeled as a Gaussian distribution (mean vector and diagonal covariance) rather than a fixed vector. Given a query feature, the method computes Mahalanobis distances to each prompt distribution, forms a query-conditioned Gaussian mixture, and samples multiple prompt tokens using the reparameterization trick for end-to-end training. The sampled prompts are aggregated with query-aware softmax weights (cosine similarity between each sample and the query) to preserve diversity while reflecting input-specific patterns. A KL-divergence-based distribution regularization loss penalizes abrupt changes in prompt distributions between successive training steps, mitigating catastrophic forgetting. The ViT backbone remains frozen throughout; only the prompt distributions and linear classifiers are optimized, keeping computational overhead negligible compared to prior prompt-based methods.

## Results

On ImageNet-R (10 tasks), the proposed ProbPrompt achieves 80.23% FAA and 84.21% CAA, outperforming the strongest baseline VQ-Prompt (78.71% FAA, 83.24% CAA) by 1.52 and 0.97 points respectively. On CIFAR-100 (10 tasks), it reaches 89.38% FAA and 93.34% CAA, and on CUB-200 (10 tasks) it achieves 87.52% FAA and 90.93% CAA. The method also generalizes across pre-training regimes, improving over VQ-Prompt by 1.03% FAA under iBOT-1K and 0.99% FAA under DINO-1K initialization on ImageNet-R.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProbPrompt consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and task split. Against the closest competitors, VQ-Prompt [19] and APT [9], the gains are 1.3–1.5% FAA on ImageNet-R (e.g., 80.23 vs. 78.71 on 10-task) and 0.53–0.63% FAA on CIFAR-100 (89.38 vs. 88.85 for APT). The advantage widens substantially over earlier prompt-based methods: CODA-P [40] trails by 4.78% FAA on 10-task ImageNet-R, while L2P [49] and DualPrompt [48] lag by over 9 and 8.9% FAA respectively. On the fine-grained CUB-200 benchmark, ProbPrompt leads VQ-Prompt by 0.80% FAA (87.52 vs. 86.72) and APT by a large 9.02% margin (87.52 vs. 78.50). HiDePrompt [46] and EvoPrompt [23] are intermediate, with ProbPrompt exceeding them by 3.49% and 3.40% FAA on 10-task ImageNet-R. Notably, the method also surpasses all baselines under self-supervised pre-training (iBOT-1K, DINO-1K), confirming robustness across initialization settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-based continual learning lineage (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-P, VQ-Prompt) by replacing deterministic prompt vectors with Gaussian distributions, from which prompts are sampled via a query-conditioned mixture. It draws conceptual inspiration from probabilistic representation learning (ProDA, PPL) but departs by parameterizing the prompts themselves as distributions rather than defining distributions over output representations, and by requiring no task identifiers or additional trainable modules. The distribution regularization loss (KL divergence between successive prompt distributions) is a novel continual-learning-specific addition not present in prior probabilistic prompt work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of prompt collapse as a systematic limitation of deterministic prompt pools provides a clear diagnostic for why existing prompt-based CIL methods plateau, and the probabilistic formulation offers a principled remedy with negligible computational overhead. However, the method is currently validated only on ViT-B/16 backbones and standard CIL benchmarks; extending to open-world continual learning, non-ViT architectures, and longer task sequences remains open. The reliance on a relatively large number of samples (N_s=30) for stable training also raises practical questions about the trade-off between diversity and computational cost in deployment settings.
