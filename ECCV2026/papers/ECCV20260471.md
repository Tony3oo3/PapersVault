## Problem

Cross-modal music generation is uniquely challenging because the mapping from visual or textual prompts to music is subjective and one-to-many. Existing methods are bottlenecked by scarce paired data, handle cross-modal ambiguity only implicitly through stochastic noise, and treat generation and retrieval as entirely separate systems. MERIC addresses all three limitations simultaneously through a decoupled architecture that exploits large unpaired music corpora while requiring only limited paired data for cross-modal alignment.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts text prompts, RGB images, or video clips (up to 3 keyframes selected via CLIP-based diversity) as input, all encoded by a frozen Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B model into a shared 2048-d space. For generation, it outputs a 10-second music waveform at 44.1 kHz; for retrieval, it outputs a ranked list scored by cosine similarity against pre-indexed music embeddings. Both tasks share an identical forward pass up to the 512-d music semantic anchor, requiring no task-specific branches or heads at inference time.

## Method

MERIC introduces a music semantic anchor—a 512-d embedding space defined by a pretrained MuQ encoder—that decouples multimodal understanding from acoustic synthesis. Training is two-stage: Stage I trains a Flow-DiT decoder (DiT backbone with flow matching objective) on 630K unpaired music clips to synthesize audio latents from anchor embeddings, decoupling acoustic quality from paired data availability. Stage II trains a lightweight 27M-parameter diffusion-based Music Head to map Qwen3-VL-Embedding outputs into the anchor space using 369K paired multimodal-music data, with v-prediction and squared cosine scheduling. The diffusion formulation of the Music Head explicitly models one-to-many correspondence: different initial noise vectors yield diverse yet faithful semantic predictions for the same input, resolving the tradeoff between output diversity and per-sample fidelity that deterministic projectors (MSE, VAE) cannot. At inference, the predicted anchor embedding simultaneously conditions the Flow-DiT decoder for waveform synthesis and serves as a retrieval query against the same anchor space, unifying both tasks in a single forward pass.

## Results

On vision-to-music generation, Meric achieves the lowest FAD_mert across all four benchmarks (1.55 on MelBench, 1.79 on MuImage, 3.06 on EmoMV, 3.30 on ARIA) and the lowest FAD_clap (0.127–0.253), substantially outperforming the next-best direct baseline GVMGen (FAD_mert 3.55–4.18). On text-to-music generation, Meric achieves the best FAD_mert on both MusicCaps (1.87) and ARIA (2.14), surpassing MusicGen-Large (3.51/2.55) and AudioLDM2-Large (4.17/3.93). For cross-modal retrieval, Meric outperforms ImageBind on ARIA (R@1: 0.40 vs 0.30 for I→M) and CLAP on ARIA (R@1: 0.83 vs 0.17 for T→M), though it trails on the more general MelBench/MusicCaps retrieval benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On vision-to-music generation, Meric decisively outperforms all direct baselines (Art2Mus, GVMGen, AudioX) on every FAD metric across all four benchmarks, with FAD_mert improvements of 2–28× over the strongest direct competitor GVMGen. Against the Qwen3VL cascade baselines (Qwen3VL+MusicGen, Qwen3VL+AudioLDM2-L), Meric achieves substantially lower FAD (e.g., 1.55 vs 4.42 on MelBench) but trails slightly on the Sem metric on MelBench (74.7 vs 80.6) and MuImage (87.1 vs 87.2), while matching or exceeding them on EmoMV and ARIA. On text-to-music generation, Meric achieves the best FAD on both MusicCaps and ARIA, but its Sem score (74.2) falls short of AudioLDM2-Large (76.7) on MusicCaps. For retrieval, Meric loses to ImageBind on MelBench (R@1: 1.34 vs 4.38) and to CLAP on MusicCaps (R@1: 0.83 vs 4.31 for T→M), but wins on the more challenging ARIA benchmark in both vision-music (R@1: 0.40 vs 0.30) and text-music (R@1: 0.83 vs 0.17) retrieval, suggesting the anchor space captures fine-grained artistic semantics that general-purpose contrastive models miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

MERIC builds on Flow Matching (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) and the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) as its acoustic synthesis backbone, but its core conceptual departure is the introduction of a music semantic anchor that decouples cross-modal understanding from waveform generation. This draws on the representation-aligned generation insight from REPA and RAE, applied here to music. Unlike prior end-to-end multimodal music generators (MusFlow, M2UGen, AudioX) that train a single model on scarce paired data, MERIC splits training into two stages: a large-scale unpaired music corpus trains the Flow-DiT decoder, while a lightweight diffusion-based Music Head trained on limited paired data handles the cross-modal mapping. The framework also unifies generation and retrieval through a shared embedding space, whereas prior systems treat these as separate models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled two-stage design offers a practical recipe for improving multimodal music generation quality without requiring massive paired datasets, which is a real bottleneck in this field. The explicit one-to-many modeling via diffusion in the Music Head provides an interpretable mechanism for cross-modal ambiguity that goes beyond noise-driven diversity. However, retrieval performance remains modest (R@1 around 1–5%), and the Sem scores do not consistently beat simple cascade baselines (Qwen3VL caption + MusicGen), suggesting the anchor space may not yet capture all relevant semantic dimensions. Open questions include whether the framework scales to longer-form music, whether the anchor space can support fine-grained control (tempo, key, instrumentation), and whether the LLM-as-Judge evaluation protocol generalizes beyond the specific Gemini model used.
