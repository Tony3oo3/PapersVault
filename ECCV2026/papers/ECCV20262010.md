## Problem

Deepfake generators evolve rapidly, making exhaustive per-generator data collection and repeated retraining impractical, yet naive model merging (e.g., weight averaging) suppresses generator-specific fake cues while preserving shared real structure, leading to unstable decision boundaries under domain shift. No prior work has systematically studied model merging for deepfake detection, where all specialists share a binary Real/Fake objective but differ in generator-specific artifacts that conflict under unconditional parameter combination.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB face image as input and produces a scalar logit margin z(x) where positive values indicate Fake and negative values indicate Real, suitable for AUC-based evaluation. At inference, the model requires one forward pass through the shared backbone (CLIP-L/14 with a binary linear head) plus one backward pass to compute the margin gradient, which drives residual routing; no specialist models are loaded or evaluated at test time. The final score is z_R²M(x) = z(θ_c, x) + βγ_{i*} q_{i*}(x), a calibrated first-order logit update added to the shared model's margin.

## Method

R²M decomposes each specialist's task vector Δ_i into a shared component c (the mean of all task vectors) and a generator-specific residual r_i, then applies a two-stage offline spectral construction: Stage 1 projects out the top-r₀ eigendirections of the real-gradient covariance Σ_R (approximated via truncated SVD of diagnostic real-sample gradients) to enforce Real invariance, and Stage 2 extracts the top-r_A eigenvectors of a weighted second-moment matrix of the projected task vectors to capture Fake-structured directions. At inference, a single backward pass through the shared model yields the margin gradient g(x), which is inner-producted with each stored residual to score branch alignment; the highest-scoring (calibration-weighted) residual is selected and its first-order logit shift is added to the shared margin. A deterministic calibration scale γ_i, computed offline from the cosine alignment between each residual and the common vector, shrinks residuals that leak shared evidence, preventing re-amplification of common-aligned components. The theoretical grounding shows that the optimal residual direction under a Real-sensitivity constraint is given by the generalized eigenspace of (M_F, Σ_R), which the two-stage spectral procedure approximates in a sample-efficient, truncated form.

## Results

On DF40 Protocol 1 (in-domain, FaceForensics++), R²M achieves a mean AUC of 0.992, outperforming CART (0.988), Weight Averaging (0.982), Task Arithmetic (0.972), and Fisher-weighted averaging (0.969). Under Protocol 2 (cross-domain transfer to Celeb-DF), the advantage widens: R²M reaches 0.901 mean AUC versus CART (0.877), TA (0.852), WA (0.834), and Fisher (0.699). On Protocol 3 (unseen generators), R²M attains the best macro AUC of 0.818, surpassing CART (0.766), WA (0.724), TA (0.622), and Fisher (0.611), with particularly strong gains on StyleCLIP (0.962) and CollabDiff (0.997).

## Baselines & Comparisons

R²M outperforms all four training-free merging baselines across every DF40 protocol. On in-domain Protocol 1, the margin over the strongest baseline CART is modest (0.992 vs. 0.988 mean AUC), as all methods saturate near specialist-level performance. The gap widens substantially under cross-domain Protocol 2, where R²M leads CART by 2.4 points (0.901 vs. 0.877) and Fisher by 20.2 points (0.901 vs. 0.699), indicating that static merging strategies degrade sharply when both domain and forgery type shift simultaneously. On unseen-generator Protocol 3, R²M achieves 0.818 macro AUC versus CART's 0.766 and WA's 0.724, with the largest per-generator wins on WhichIsReal (0.716 vs. 0.497 for CART) and StyleCLIP (0.962 vs. 0.813). Fisher-weighted averaging is the weakest baseline overall, collapsing to 0.442 on EFS under CDF and 0.346 on StyleCLIP under Protocol 3, suggesting that curvature-based weighting is poorly suited to the heterogeneous, generator-specific structure of deepfake artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on task arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022) for the task-vector representation and weight averaging (Izmailov et al., 2018) as the simplest merging baseline, while drawing on CART (Choi et al., 2024) for low-rank task-vector decomposition and Fisher-weighted averaging (Matena & Raffel, 2022) as a curvature-aware baseline. The key conceptual departure is exploiting the structural asymmetry unique to deepfake detection—shared Real representations versus generator-specific Fake cues—to design a Real-aware spectral decomposition rather than treating all task updates symmetrically. The routing mechanism borrows the MoE activation paradigm but replaces a learned router with a first-order gradient-residual alignment score, positioning the work between static merging and test-time adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training-free merging offers a practical path to rapidly incorporate new deepfake generators without retraining, addressing the fundamental sustainability problem as generators evolve. The interpretable common-residual decomposition enables direct diagnosis of failure modes (as shown in the heterogeneous-domain stress test), which is valuable for practitioners maintaining detection pipelines. The main limitation is per-sample gradient computation at inference, which constrains deployment speed; developing faster routing (e.g., learned approximations or feature-space proxies) is an important open direction. The approach also raises the question of whether the Real-Fake asymmetry exploited here generalizes to other binary-detection domains with shared-vs-specific structure.
