## Problem

Monocular 3D object detection is fundamentally ill-posed due to depth-scale ambiguity, and existing pseudo-labeling methods operate on single images, producing partial and noisy geometric observations that propagate systematic errors into downstream training. Two underexplored challenges remain: maintaining long-term object identity consistency in pose-unknown videos under occlusion and clutter, and recovering complete object geometry from inherently incomplete single-frame observations to enable reliable size, orientation, and position estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261545/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PLOT consumes raw monocular RGB video sequences (no camera intrinsics, extrinsics, or auxiliary sensors required) along with optional text prompts for open-vocabulary 2D detection via GSAM. It produces per-frame 3D bounding box annotations (center, dimensions, yaw) and completed pseudo-LiDAR point clouds for each tracked object, with orientation derived from motion direction for dynamic objects and PCA on fused geometry for static ones. The entire pipeline is training-free and operates at approximately 2.8 s per frame on a single CPU with an RTX 3090 GPU for 20 objects.

## Method

PLOT is a four-stage, training-free pipeline that leverages dense point tracking to generate 3D annotations from monocular video. First, it extracts 2D masks via GSAM and metric depth via UniDepth, then propagates object and background points across frames using AllTracker, with Hungarian matching to associate predicted and tracked masks. Second, it estimates camera ego-motion via Procrustes alignment on background point trajectories and object pose via per-object point registration, optionally including a scale term to absorb depth drift. Third, a Global Object Memory (GOM) maintains persistent object hypotheses across the full video, resolving identity switches, detection dropouts, and occlusion-induced track fragmentation through consistency checks on point tracking, class labels, and geometric attributes. Fourth, trajectory-guided shape fusion aggregates registered 3D points from all matched frames into a unified pseudo-LiDAR per object (Eq. 9), which is then reprojected to every frame for consistent attribute annotation; orientation for static objects is recovered via PCA on this denser fused geometry, substantially improving over single-frame PCA.

## Results

On KITTI (Car class), PLOT achieves AP3D@0.3 of 80.48/60.83/51.49 (Easy/Mod/Hard) and APBEV@0.3 of 83.06/63.59/54.15, outperforming the previous pseudo-labeling SOTA MonoSOWA by up to +9.68 AP@0.3 and OVM3D-Det by an average of +19 AP. PLOT also surpasses all weakly-supervised baselines (WeakM3D, WeakMono3D, SKD-WM3D) that rely on auxiliary sensors (LiDAR, stereo, depth completion), while remaining competitive with fully supervised MonoDETR (80.48 vs. 79.72 Easy AP3D@0.3). On KITTI-360, PLOT achieves the best AP@0.3 in both Easy (54.78) and Hard (48.75) regimes among pseudo-labeling methods, and on Waymo it leads in distant regions (>30 m) over fully supervised baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLOT outperforms the prior pseudo-labeling SOTA MonoSOWA [48] by up to +9.68 AP@0.3 on KITTI (80.48 vs. 72.70 Easy), despite not requiring IMU sensors or hand-crafted shape priors. Against OVM3D-Det [19], PLOT gains an average of +19 AP across Easy/Mod/Hard, primarily due to trajectory-guided shape fusion recovering occluded geometry. PLOT also surpasses all weakly-supervised baselines—WeakM3D [39] (78.44 Easy), WeakMono3D [50], and SKD-WM3D [21]—which depend on auxiliary LiDAR, stereo, or depth-completion sensors. On KITTI-360, PLOT achieves the best AP@0.3 among pseudo-labeling methods in both Easy (54.78) and Hard (48.75), beating VSRD [29] and MonoSOWA [48]. On Waymo, PLOT leads in the 30–50 m and 50–∞ m ranges, surpassing fully supervised MonoDETR [62] in distant AP3D@0.5 (17.71 vs. 18.61 for 30–50 m is close, but PLOT wins at 50–∞ m: 4.87 vs. 3.69). PLOT is slightly below the open-set 3D-MOOD [55] on KITTI Easy AP3D@0.3 (80.48 vs. 81.97), but 3D-MOOD requires GT-3D training data and a depth completion model, whereas PLOT is training-free and uses only monocular video.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLOT carves new ground at the intersection of pseudo-labeling for monocular 3D detection and video-based object tracking. Prior pseudo-labeling methods (OVM3D-Det, MonoSOWA) operate on single images or require known camera poses and auxiliary sensors, while tracking-based 3D perception work (Seurat, ViPE) targets scene-level depth rather than object-level geometry. PLOT repurposes off-the-shelf dense point tracking (AllTracker) and Procrustes alignment into a pose-free, training-free annotation pipeline, introducing the Global Object Memory as a novel mechanism for long-term identity consistency that no prior pseudo-labeling or tracking-for-3D method addresses explicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PLOT offers a practical, scalable path to 3D supervision without LiDAR, multi-view rigs, or camera calibration, which is critical for deploying monocular 3D perception in unconstrained, in-the-wild settings where curated sensor-rich data is unavailable. Key limitations remain: reliance on pre-trained monocular depth estimators (errors at long range or under noise), and inherent orientation ambiguity for static objects under partial views. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-rigid or deformable objects, how it scales to very long sequences with many objects, and whether the pseudo-labels can support self-improving or iterative annotation loops.
