## Problem

Training semantic segmentation networks on spatially misaligned labels (e.g., OpenStreetMap building footprints paired with satellite imagery) causes the network to memorize the misalignment as a shortcut, especially when the noise is systematic. Existing alignment methods either require golden labels, assume small unbiased transformations, or decouple alignment from segmentation in a way that fails under the large, biased shifts common in remote sensing. This paper addresses the joint learning of label alignment and segmentation without any clean ground-truth labels.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB satellite image patch (320×320 or 512×512 pixels, 30–50 cm GSD) and a corresponding misaligned binary building-footprint mask as input. It produces three outputs: a probabilistic segmentation mask from the SNet, a 3-parameter affine transformation (rotation α, translations t_x, t_y) from the TNet, and the aligned mask obtained by warping the misaligned input mask with the predicted transformation. Notably, the TNet consumes two segmentation masks (the misaligned input mask and the SNet's predicted mask) rather than an image–mask pair, and the transformation is bounded by a tanh activation (c=0.35) limiting translations to ±112 px and rotation to ±20°.

## Method

AnS couples a lightweight transformation network (TNet, ViT-Small) with a canonical segmentation network (SNet, U-Net with frozen ConvNeXt-Tiny/DINOv3 encoder) in an end-to-end framework. The key challenge is that minimizing cross-entropy alone lets the SNet absorb the misalignment, so the TNet never learns the true correction. To break this shortcut, the paper introduces a consistency loss (L_Con) that applies a random affine transformation to the misaligned mask and verifies the TNet can invert it using MSE on transformation parameters plus IoU on the warped masks; this signal is independent of the SNet output and provides a learning gradient early in training. Strong geometric augmentations (horizontal/vertical flips, 90°/180°/270° rotations applied jointly to image and mask) further eliminate systematic translation bias in expectation. The two components are complementary: augmentation is critical for biased data while the consistency loss helps most on uniformly noisy data, and together they enable the TNet to recover the true alignment while the SNet learns clean segmentation.

## Results

On the synthetic Las Vegas dataset, AnS achieves IoU_seg = 0.79 and IoU_align = 0.84 on the uniformly misaligned set (D_uni), and IoU_seg = 0.78 and IoU_align = 0.88 on the systematically biased set (D_bias), substantially outperforming all unsupervised baselines. On the real-world ReBO dataset (41 cities), AnS reaches IoU_seg = 0.62 and IoU_align = 0.74, again leading all unsupervised methods. The method is robust to noise magnitudes up to ~100 pixels on D_uni and ~70 pixels on D_bias before performance degrades.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnS outperforms all three unsupervised baselines (SC, MA, AC) on both IoU_seg and IoU_align across every city and dataset. Against the supervised baselines, AnS is competitive with MapRepair (MR) on IoU_align, exceeding it on D_uni and D_bias in Las Vegas (0.84 vs. 0.77; 0.88 vs. 0.77) and on D_uni in Paris (0.67 vs. 0.57) and Khartoum (0.77 vs. 0.71), though MR slightly edges out AnS on D_bias in Paris (0.68 vs. 0.65) and Khartoum (0.75 vs. 0.71). ACN achieves higher IoU_seg (e.g., 0.94 vs. 0.79 in Las Vegas on D_uni) but requires misaligned labels at inference time, making it impractical for deployment without the noisy source. On the real-world ReBO dataset, AnS leads all unsupervised methods by a wide margin (IoU_seg 0.62 vs. 0.55 for MA, 0.49 for SC; IoU_align 0.74 vs. 0.57 for MA, 0.60 for AC), while remaining below the supervised ACN (0.87) and MR (0.76). A key practical advantage is that AnS is the only method that simultaneously reports both IoU_seg and IoU_align without requiring golden labels or misaligned labels at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AutoCorrect [3] consistency-loss framework for aligning noisy geometric annotations in two key ways: the transformation network (TNet) operates on pairs of segmentation masks rather than on an image-mask pair, enabling absolute (not just relative) consistency enforcement, and a data-augmentation strategy is introduced to counteract systematic label bias that prior methods assume away. It also departs from multi-round iterative correction approaches (MA, SC) by jointly learning alignment and segmentation in a single end-to-end pass, and from supervised methods (MapRepair, ACN) by requiring no golden labels. The TNet architecture draws on the Spatial Transformer Network [12], while the SNet leverages a frozen ConvNeXt-Tiny encoder with DINOv3 pretrained weights, making the approach model-agnostic and plug-and-play with any segmentation backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for remote sensing: large-scale building-footprint data from OpenStreetMap and similar sources are currently underutilized for training because of costly manual alignment, and AnS offers a scalable, label-free pipeline to unlock them. Key limitations include restriction to per-patch affine transformations, blurry segmentation outputs due to the generic decoder, and sensitivity to label noise beyond misalignment (e.g., missing buildings). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex (non-affine, local) deformations, whether specialized building-segmentation decoders can recover sharper boundaries, and how to handle the broader class of label errors (omissions, incorrect shapes) that co-occur with misalignment in real OSM data.
