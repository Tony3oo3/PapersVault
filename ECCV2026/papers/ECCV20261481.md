## Problem

Existing DETR frameworks face two critical bottlenecks for tiny object detection: (1) the encoder becomes a speed bottleneck because deformable attention over high-resolution multi-scale features produces prohibitively long sequences, and (2) conventional IoU/classification-based query selection inherently biases toward large objects and background regions, leaving tiny targets with insufficient high-quality query coverage. Prior attempts to fix the first issue (sparse/salience filtering) accumulate errors across layers, while those fixing the second (RT-DETR's FPN-like fusion) sacrifice the low-level local details that tiny objects critically depend on.

## Input / Output

The model consumes single RGB images at 640×640 (VisDrone), 800×800 (AI-TOD-V2), or 416×416 (edge deployment) and produces class-agnostic bounding boxes with class labels in a fully end-to-end manner, requiring no NMS post-processing. Backbone features at three levels (B2, B3, B4) are extracted and projected to a unified resolution aligned with B3 before entering the encoder. The end-to-end design is specifically motivated by real-time drone deployment, where NMS introduces unpredictable latency.

## Method

Slim-DETR introduces an Efficient Interaction (EI) encoder that first aggregates multi-level backbone features (enhanced with CBAM and a gate unit to suppress background noise) into a single global feature map via a lightweight CNN-based Fusion module with RepPD blocks. A Foreground Sampling Deformable Attention (FSDA) module then selects only top-k foreground tokens (via a learned foreground mask) for deformable attention, avoiding the cumulative information loss of layer-wise sparse filtering while eliminating the computational cost of attending over all tokens; the updated foreground features are written back to their original spatial positions to preserve structure. The resulting semantic-enhanced global feature map is directly injected into every level of a lightweight cross-fusion module, creating a shortcut for global-to-local information exchange. For query selection, a Gaussian Target Guided (GTG) strategy uses a lightweight 3-layer CNN to predict size-decoupled Gaussian distribution maps (separately for targets ≤32×32, 32–64×64, and >64×64 pixels), concentrating query allocation on tiny object locations and enabling a shallower 4-layer decoder that matches or exceeds the accuracy of a 6-layer baseline.

## Results

On VisDrone (640×640 input), Slim-DETR-R50 achieves 33.9% AP, 55.2% AP50, and 36.9% AP75 with 15.7M parameters and 24.4 ms latency on an RTX 3090. On AI-TOD-V2 (800×800 input), Slim-DETR-R50 reaches 33.6% AP (16.4% APvt, 34.0% APt) at 39.4 ms latency. On the embedded Jetson AGX Orin, Slim-DETR-416 (R18, 416×416 input) delivers 25.4% AP at 40 FPS (25.12 ms), outperforming all compared real-time detectors in both accuracy and throughput.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On VisDrone, Slim-DETR-R50 (33.9% AP) surpasses the strongest prior Transformer, D-FINE-R50 (32.0% AP), by 1.9% AP and the RT-DETR-R50 baseline (30.3% AP) by 3.6% AP, while using fewer parameters (15.7M vs. 40.7M and 42.0M). The lightweight Slim-DETR-R18 (32.4% AP, 5.9M params) outperforms RT-DETR-R18 (26.7% AP) by 5.7% AP with 3.4× fewer parameters at comparable latency (14.3 ms vs. 13.8 ms), and even exceeds the R50-based DEIM (30.4%) and D-FINE (32.0%) by 2.0% and 0.4% AP respectively. On AI-TOD-V2, Slim-DETR-R50 (33.6% AP) beats the previous best DQ-DETR (30.2%) by 3.4% AP and RT-DETR-R50 (27.6%) by 6.0% AP, with substantially lower latency (39.4 ms vs. 158.9 ms for Salience-DETR and 92.1 ms for DINO-DETR). On the Jetson AGX Orin edge platform, Slim-DETR-416 achieves 25.4% AP at 40 FPS, outperforming RT-DETR-416 (23.5% AP, 27 FPS) by 1.9% AP and YOLOv12-M (24.8% AP, 11 FPS) by 0.6% AP while running 3.6× faster than YOLOv12-M. All CNN-based baselines (YOLOv8/10/11/12, CenterNet, FBRT-YOLO) are consistently outperformed in AP by 3–14 points, confirming the advantage of the end-to-end Transformer design for tiny objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

Slim-DETR is built directly on the RT-DETR (CVPR 2024) codebase, inheriting its FPN-like cross-fusion structure and training protocol, but fundamentally reworks the encoder to address two specific failures for tiny objects: the sequence-length bottleneck of multi-scale deformable attention and the query-object mismatch in conventional IoU/confidence-based selection. It departs from Sparse-DETR and Salience-DETR, which perform layer-wise hierarchical token filtering (suffering cumulative information loss), by instead applying a single gather-and-inject step on an aggregated global feature map and restricting deformable attention to foreground tokens only. The Gaussian Target Guided (GTG) query selection is a novel departure from the standard uncertainty-minimal criterion, introducing a size-decoupled Gaussian likelihood map to bias query allocation toward small targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers a practically important result: real-time (40 FPS) tiny object detection on an embedded Jetson AGX Orin with SOTA accuracy on both VisDrone and AI-TOD-V2, which is directly relevant to drone and aerial surveillance deployments. The finding that GTG query initialization allows reducing decoder depth from 6 to 4 layers while *improving* AP (33.3→33.9) suggests that better query allocation can substitute for iterative refinement, a principle that may generalize to other DETR variants. Open questions include whether the empirical 32/64-pixel scale thresholds and the fixed sampling ratio ρ transfer robustly to domains with different object-size distributions, and whether the foreground-only attention strategy degrades on scenes with very dense or overlapping tiny objects.
