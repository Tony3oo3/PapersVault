## Problem

Existing zero-shot anomaly detection (ZSAD) methods assume that auxiliary training data contains rich and diverse anomaly patterns, yet real-world target domains often exhibit far more complex and unpredictable defect variations. When auxiliary anomaly priors are limited (e.g., only texture anomalies from DTD instead of diverse industrial defects from MVTec), current methods suffer severe generalization degradation (9.9%–23.3% AP drop). Additionally, pre-trained vision encoders produce embeddings dominated by object semantics, which act as disruptive noise when aligned with anomaly-focused text prompts, hindering the learning of anomaly-discriminative representations.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image resized to 518×518 as input and produces two outputs: a scalar image-level anomaly score in [0,1] (the abnormal channel of a softmax over normal/abnormal state embeddings) and a pixel-wise anomaly map at original resolution, obtained by upsampling the abnormal similarity channel of patch features and applying a Gaussian spatial filter. The backbone is CLIP ViT-L/14@336px with all input images processed at 518×518 resolution, yielding a 37×37 grid of patch tokens for local anomaly localization.

## Method

DIVE introduces two core mechanisms on top of a frozen CLIP backbone. First, a shallow-and-deep text embedding injection strategy: at the shallow level, the first h learnable tokens of a semantic text prompt are projected into visual prompts via a V-L coupling function before the first ViT layer to preserve cross-modal alignment for unseen object categories; at the deep level, LLM-generated typological descriptions of normality and abnormality are fused with patch tokens via cross-attention in the final four ViT blocks, compelling the model to abstract generic anomaly concepts rather than overfit to specific auxiliary patterns. Second, a disentanglement mechanism uses two parallel residual MLPs (G_state and G_semantic) to decompose the global visual embedding into independent anomaly-state and object-semantic subspaces, with an orthogonal constraint on the learned residual offsets to enforce decoupling. Training freezes all backbone parameters and optimizes only the prompt learners, coupling function, cross-attention layers, and MLP heads using a combined objective of cross-entropy (state classification), contrastive loss (semantic alignment), squared-cosine disentanglement loss, focal+Dice segmentation loss, and L2 regularization on visual prompts.

## Results

Under the challenging DTD-auxiliary setting (limited anomaly priors), DIVE achieves average AUROC of 87.3% and AP of 69.3% for image-level classification, and AUROC of 87.3%, AUPRO of 71.1%, and RCPRO of 40.4% for pixel-level segmentation across twelve datasets. Under the MVTec-auxiliary setting (sufficient anomaly diversity), DIVE reaches 90.1% AUROC / 74.6% AP (classification) and 91.1% AUROC / 75.0% AUPRO / 43.7% RCPRO (segmentation). Compared to the strongest baseline, DIVE improves average AP by up to 28.5% and AUPRO by up to 47.0% in the DTD setting, while restricting the performance penalty from MVTec→DTD auxiliary switch to only 5.3% AP (vs. 9.9%–23.3% for baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIVE outperforms all five baselines on average across both classification and segmentation metrics in the DTD-auxiliary setting. Against TPS (AAAI 2025), the strongest classification baseline, DIVE gains +5.7 AUROC and +8.8 AP on average, while TPS collapses in segmentation (63.9 AUROC, 24.6 AUPRO) due to numerous small-scale false positives in normal regions. Against AA-CLIP (CVPR 2025), DIVE leads by +2.9 AUROC and +6.2 AUPRO on average segmentation, though AA-CLIP retains an edge on RCPRO for ISIC (89.3 vs. 74.2) because its over-segmentation behavior coincidentally matches ISIC's large anomalous regions. Against AF-CLIP (ACM MM 2025), DIVE improves average AP by +25.4 and AUPRO by +5.6 in the DTD setting, with the gap widening on industrial datasets like MPDD (+9.0 AP) and SDD (+25.6 AP). Against AnomalyCLIP (ICLR 2024) and AdaCLIP (ECCV 2024), DIVE achieves consistent gains of 8–16% in AP and 5–47% in AUPRO, with AdaCLIP's AUPRO particularly weak (24.1 average) due to its tendency to produce incomplete or fragmented anomaly maps. Under the MVTec-auxiliary setting, DIVE maintains the best average scores on all metrics, confirming that the gains are not solely an artifact of the limited-prior scenario.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIVE extends the CLIP-based zero-shot anomaly detection lineage initiated by WinCLIP and formalized by AnomalyCLIP's object-agnostic prompt templates, adding two architectural innovations: a shallow-and-deep text embedding injection strategy (drawing on MaPLe's multi-modal prompting) and a residual-based disentanglement mechanism that splits global visual embeddings into anomaly-state and object-semantic subspaces. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior ZSAD work by explicitly targeting the underexplored regime where auxiliary anomaly priors are limited (e.g., texture-only DTD data rather than diverse MVTec defects), a setting where existing learnable-prompt and adapter-based methods overfit to narrow anomaly distributions. The introduction of RCPRO as a segmentation metric also represents a methodological departure, directly challenging the field's reliance on AUPRO, which the authors demonstrate can inflate scores by over 60 percentage points when false positives are present.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The limited-auxiliary-priors setting is arguably the more realistic deployment scenario for ZSAD, and DIVE's 46.5%–77.3% reduction in cross-domain degradation (relative to baselines) makes the approach practically relevant for industrial and medical applications where anomaly diversity in training data is inherently constrained. The RCPRO metric is likely to gain adoption because it exposes a systematic blind spot in AUPRO that has affected interpretation of prior ZSAD results. Open questions include whether the LLM-generated typological descriptions are truly necessary or whether simpler fixed prompts suffice, whether the approach transfers to non-CLIP backbones, and how to eliminate the residual false-positive noise along object boundaries that the authors acknowledge as a limitation.
