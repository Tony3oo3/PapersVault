## Problem

MLLMs generate hallucinated content because text-to-image cross-attention progressively degrades during autoregressive generation, shifting the model from visually grounded decoding to language-prior-dominated decoding. Existing mitigation strategies—whether inference-time decoding adjustments or training-time preference alignment—are outcome-driven and do not explicitly target this internal attention failure mode. The paper asks whether directly intervening on cross-attention dynamics can reduce hallucinations more effectively than manipulating token-level probabilities alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261883/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a text query as input and produces a free-form text response. Unusually, it performs a short preliminary inference pass (first K=5 tokens) to extract and refine cross-attention before the main generation, and optionally feeds back an anchor-guided image (original image overlaid with an attention heatmap and binary mask) as a visual enhancement. For the VAG-DPO training stage, rejected samples are paired with noise-injected images rather than degraded versions of the original, creating a 'blind' visual condition.

## Method

ADAPT refines multi-layer text-to-image cross-attention from the early grounded decoding stage into a query-relevant visual anchor using three complementary criteria (spectral smoothness, inverse TV regularity, and query-dependent entropy matching) with layer-wise fusion weights, then debiases the fused map by subtracting a content-independent positional bias estimated from noise images. At inference time, an Anchor-Modulated Concentration (AMC) index monitors attention fidelity online; when it drops below a threshold, sparse corrective steering is applied to attention logits to redirect focus back toward anchor-supported regions. For training, VAG-DPO modifies the standard DPO objective by contrasting responses under different visual evidence conditions—anchor-enhanced images as chosen inputs and noise images as rejected inputs—explicitly penalizing high-confidence generation from language priors alone. The three components share the same visual anchor, creating a unified supervision signal across pre-inference enhancement, online correction, and preference alignment.

## Results

ADAPT achieves state-of-the-art hallucination reduction across all four benchmarks (Obj-Hal, AMBER, MM-Hal, POPE) on both LLaVA-v1.5 and Qwen2.5-VL backbones. On LLaVA-v1.5-7B, full ADAPT reduces AMBER Hallucination from 36.4% to 15.2% (−58%), AMBER Chair from 7.8 to 3.8, and achieves 91.9% POPE-Adv precision. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, it further reduces AMBER Hal to 13.1% and Chair to 3.5, while the training-free variant (ADAPT-TF) already surpasses most baselines with only 1.42× inference overhead versus 2.03× (VCD) and 7.20× (OPERA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADAPT outperforms all 11 baselines on the primary hallucination metrics. Against inference-time methods, ADAPT-TF (training-free) already beats VCD (Chair 4.0 vs 7.4, Hal 16.7 vs 34.7 on LLaVA-7B) and OPERA (Chair 4.0 vs 6.9, Hal 16.7 vs 30.3) by large margins, while also achieving 90.3% POPE precision versus 73.4% (VCD) and 82.2% (OPERA). Against training-time alignment, full ADAPT surpasses DPO (Chair 3.8 vs 6.5, Hal 15.2 vs 34.5), mDPO (Chair 3.8 vs 4.4, Hal 15.2 vs 24.5), and RLHF-V (Acc 79.6 vs 72.6, Score 2.62 vs 2.50 on LLaVA-13B). On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, ADAPT reduces Chair_s from 32.3 to 16.8 (−48%), beating SPIN (28.6) and DPO (29.6) by 11.8 and 12.8 points respectively. In terms of efficiency, ADAPT incurs only 1.42× runtime overhead versus 2.03× for VCD, 3.96× for GF-SCD, and 7.20× for OPERA, while delivering substantially larger hallucination reductions. The only metric where ADAPT does not strictly dominate is MM-Hal Score on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (3.75 vs DPO's 3.59), where DPO edges ahead slightly, though ADAPT still leads on all other reported metrics for that backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant outcome-driven paradigm in MLLM hallucination mitigation—where inference-time methods (VCD, OPERA, SPIN, AVISC) manipulate token-level logits and training-time methods (DPO, mDPO, RLHF-V) supervise token predictions—by instead targeting the internal text-to-image cross-attention dynamics as both a diagnostic signal and an intervention target. It builds on attention-based diagnostic work identifying attention sinks and decay in vision-language models, but goes further by constructing a query-conditioned, debiased cross-attention anchor from early decoding and using it to drive both online attention correction and a novel visual-evidence-conditioned DPO objective (VAG-DPO). The three-stage pipeline (anchor extraction → attention-supervised inference → preference tuning) is a new architectural composition, though each individual component (FFT-based filtering, entropy scoring, DPO) draws on established techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, attention-based alternative to hallucination mitigation that achieves 40–60% reduction with only 1.42× inference overhead, making it deployable where heavier decoding strategies (OPERA at 7.2×, GF-SCD at 4×) are infeasible. The VAG-DPO formulation—contrasting responses under different visual evidence conditions rather than purely textual conditions—is a conceptually clean extension of preference optimization that better preserves general multimodal capability than vanilla DPO. Open questions include whether the cross-attention anchor approach generalizes to video or multi-image inputs, whether the positional bias correction transfers across radically different architectures (e.g., non-ViT encoders), and whether the two-stage degradation pattern holds for very long-form generation beyond the tested caption lengths.
