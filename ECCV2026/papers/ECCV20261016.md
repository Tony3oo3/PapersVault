## Problem

Existing tree-based speculative decoding methods maximize token-level likelihood or acceptance length, but this ignores the super-linear computational overhead of drafting and verifying large trees, leading to negative wall-clock speedup at larger batch sizes or on hardware that saturates compute units earlier. The optimal tree shape depends on device-specific cost curves and batch size, yet current practice relies on offline grid search over static hyperparameters that must be re-tuned whenever the deployment context changes.

## Input / Output

SMART consumes the draft model's per-token probabilities (used to compute cumulative path acceptance probabilities), a pair of pre-fitted cost-model parameters (a linear model for drafting and a power-exponential model for verification, each requiring only ~5 forward passes to calibrate on a given GPU), and a per-sequence verification budget B = B_verify / b derived from the total budget and batch size. At each layer it outputs a binary keep/prune decision for every candidate node, producing a variable-size draft tree whose shape adapts to both the context difficulty and the current hardware regime; the tree is then verified in a single target-model forward pass as in standard speculative decoding.

## Method

SMART defines an end-to-end speedup objective R(T) = c_T · L^tree / (C_draft(T) + C_verify(T)) and formulates tree construction as a sequential layer-by-layer decision problem. At each layer, for every candidate node u it computes a marginal benefit (the node's cumulative acceptance probability diluted by the number of root-to-leaf paths) and a marginal cost (the derivative of the fitted draft-plus-verify cost models evaluated at the current tree size), then applies the rule: expand u only if α · ΔC_target(u) / ΔC_spec(u) > C_target / C_spec, where α ∈ (0,1] is a discount factor that guards against optimistic draft-based acceptance estimates. This greedy policy runs in O(kB) time—linear in the verification budget—reducing the combinatorial search space from O(2^{k^d}) to tractable per-layer decisions. Because SMART is training-free and replaces only the tree-construction policy, it plugs directly into existing frameworks (MSD, EAGLE-3) without modifying draft or target model weights.

## Results

SMART delivers an average additional speedup of 20.0% for MLLMs and 15.4% for LLMs over their respective backbone methods (MSD and EAGLE-3) across all benchmarks and temperatures. On LLMs at T=0, EAGLE-3+SMART raises average speedup ratio (SR) from 1.36× to 1.59× on LLaMA-3.1-8B and from 2.69× to 3.35× on LLaMA-3.3-70B. On MLLMs at T=0, MSD+SMART improves average SR from 1.18× to 1.53× on LLaVA-1.5-7B and from 1.26× to 1.53× on LLaVA-1.5-13B. Critically, in compute-bound batching regimes (batch ≥ 16 on RTX Pro 6000), MSD degrades below 1× speedup (0.82× at batch 32) while SMART maintains 1.39×, demonstrating the hardware-aware advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMART (as a plug-in controller) consistently outperforms all seven baselines on every benchmark. Against the direct backbone methods, MSD+SMART achieves +20.2% average SR over MSD on MLLMs (e.g., 1.53× vs 1.18× on LLaVA-1.5-7B at T=0), and EAGLE-3+SMART achieves +19.6% at T=0 and +15.4% at T=1 over EAGLE-3 on LLMs. Against earlier speculative decoding methods, SMART surpasses Medusa by 50–70% SR (e.g., 1.53× vs 0.91× on LLaVA-1.5-7B), EAGLE-1 by ~40–60%, and EAGLE-2 by ~20–30%. On LLMs, SMART also beats GRIFFIN (which focuses on token-level alignment) by 15–25% SR and SPS (the original speculative sampling baseline) by 100–200% SR. The largest absolute gains appear on LLaMA-3.3-70B, where EAGLE-3+SMART reaches 3.35× SR at T=0 versus 2.69× for EAGLE-3, confirming that the speedup-maximizing tree construction becomes increasingly valuable as model size and verification cost grow.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMART builds directly on the tree-based speculative decoding lineage (EAGLE-3, MSD) but departs from their shared objective of maximizing token-level likelihood or expected acceptance length. It also draws on GTO's formulation of acceptance length as a reward signal, but repurposes it as a *benefit* term in a marginal cost-benefit ratio rather than a training objective. The key conceptual departure is reframing tree construction from a likelihood-optimization problem to a hardware-aware, speedup-maximizing sequential decision problem, replacing offline grid-search tuning with an online marginal rule that requires no retraining of any model component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a practical failure mode that practitioners encounter but that the literature has not formally addressed: likelihood-maximizing trees can produce *negative* speedup (below 1×) at moderate-to-large batch sizes, undermining the core promise of speculative decoding in production serving. SMART's training-free, plug-and-play design lowers the barrier to adoption since it requires no changes to draft or target model weights. A key limitation is evaluation on only two consumer/prosumer GPUs (RTX Pro 6000, L40S); whether the cost-model fitting and marginal rule transfer cleanly to data-center accelerators (A100, H100, H200) with different memory hierarchies and compute-to-bandwidth ratios remains open. The lightweight profiling requirement (~5 forward passes, ~10 s) is encouraging but the power-exponential verification model's accuracy under extreme batch sizes or multi-GPU parallelism is untested.
