## Problem

Promptable foundation segmentation models (SAM, SAM2) suffer substantial performance degradation on medical images due to both natural-to-medical domain shift and inter-sub-domain shifts from heterogeneous imaging modalities, organs, and acquisition protocols. Existing online test-time adaptation (OTTA) methods are limited in two ways: they rely on non-semantic-preserving perturbations or source-domain statistics that produce unreliable learning signals, and they incur prohibitive computational and memory costs from full-model backpropagation, making them impractical for resource-constrained clinical edge devices.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D or 3D medical images across 11 modalities (CT, MR, Ultrasound, etc.) paired with either box prompts or point prompts (up to 3 clicks). For 3D volumes, prompts are provided on the key slice (largest mask area) and up to four adjacent slices, with remaining slices segmented via SAM2's object tracking. Adaptation operates online with batch size 1 and a single optimization iteration per sample, producing a binary segmentation mask per slice. The initial prompts contain realistic noise (up to 10% box offset) following the MedSAM2 protocol.

## Method

SPDA constructs robust consistency-based learning signals via two semantic-preserving perturbations and optimizes only a lightweight post-encoder bottleneck-convolution adapter, completely bypassing backpropagation through the heavy image encoder. The first perturbation is a domain-adaptive low-frequency radial-spectrum perturbation: it partitions the 2D Fourier frequency plane into K radial bands, tracks inter-sample spectral statistics via EWMA, and samples perturbation factors from an adaptive log-normal distribution applied exclusively to low-frequency bands (scaling the real part of Fourier coefficients), thereby simulating task-irrelevant intra-sub-domain variations without out-of-domain distortions. The second perturbation is prompt under-sampling, which isotropically scales box prompts or randomly subsamples point prompts to yield diverse yet semantically consistent inference views. The dual-consistency objective combines a feature consistency loss (cosine dissimilarity at all hierarchical encoder levels between original and input-perturbed features) and a prediction consistency loss (Dice loss aligning the anchor prediction with both input-perturbed and inference-perturbed predictions), with gradients applied solely to the adapter parameters.

## Results

On the MedSAM2 setup (the more realistic clinical scenario with prompts only on the key slice), SPDA achieves 88.05% average DSC with box prompts and 81.11% with point prompts, outperforming the strongest baseline GraTa by +1.79% and +2.06% respectively. On the harder SAM2 setup (natural-to-medical domain shift), SPDA gains +7.07% (box) and +9.96% (point) over the source-only model. In terms of efficiency, SPDA requires only 39.88 TFLOPs per sample and 39.16M parameters, roughly 5–12× less compute than GraTa (497.18 TFLOPs) and VPTTA (233.2 TFLOPs), while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPDA is compared against four TTA baselines: InTEnt (TTBA, entropy-weighted ensemble), VPTTA (OTTA, source-target feature alignment), GraTa (OTTA, gradient alignment with entropy + consistency), and SAM-TTA (OTTA, student-teacher dual-scale consistency). On the MedSAM2 box-prompt setting, SPDA (88.05%) beats GraTa (86.26%), VPTTA (85.92%), SAM-TTA (85.69%), and InTEnt (83.07%) by 1.79%, 2.13%, 2.36%, and 4.98% respectively. On point prompts, SPDA (81.11%) leads GraTa (79.05%) and SAM-TTA (79.01%) by ~2%, with the largest gap over InTEnt (77.74%) at +3.37%. On the harder SAM2 setup, SPDA's advantage widens dramatically: +5.51% over SAM-TTA (55.93%) and +8.56% over GraTa (52.88%) on point prompts, confirming that the semantic-preserving perturbations are especially valuable when the domain gap is large. In terms of efficiency, SPDA (39.88 TFLOPs, 39.16M params) is ~5× cheaper than GraTa (497.18 TFLOPs) and ~6× cheaper than VPTTA (233.2 TFLOPs) while outperforming both in accuracy, and uses half the parameters of SAM-TTA (78.04M) at lower compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of promptable foundation model adaptation (SAM, MedSAM, MedSAM2) and online test-time adaptation (OTTA) for medical segmentation. It departs from prior OTTA methods in two conceptual directions: (1) replacing alignment-based objectives (VPTTA) or generic augmentations (GraTa) with a frequency-domain perturbation grounded in RAPSD analysis of medical spectra, and (2) replacing full-model or in-encoder optimization with a post-encoder bottleneck adapter that entirely bypasses encoder backpropagation. The prompt under-sampling perturbation is a novel inference-time signal that leverages the promptable interface itself, a dimension absent from non-promptable TTA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~5–10× reduction in per-sample compute (39.88 vs. 200–500 TFLOPs) while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy makes OTTA viable on hospital edge devices, a practical barrier that prior methods could not overcome. The RAPSD-motivated finding that medical sub-domain variation lives in low frequencies—and that traditional high-frequency augmentations are therefore misaligned—offers a reusable diagnostic for designing domain-appropriate perturbations beyond this specific task. Key open questions include whether the framework extends to end-to-end architectures without a separable encoder, whether modality-specific hyperparameter tuning can be automated, and whether the approach generalizes to non-promptable or multi-task clinical pipelines.
