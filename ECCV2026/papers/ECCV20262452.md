## Problem

Existing infrared turbulence mitigation methods focus exclusively on image restoration, ignoring the physical turbulence parameters (C_n², C_T², ε, Reynolds stress) that directly govern the degradation process. The closest prior work, PBCL, addresses both tasks but uses a sequential three-stage pipeline that accumulates errors, assumes static turbulence fields, and prevents end-to-end joint optimization. This paper tackles the gap by unifying turbulence mitigation and physical fields estimation in a single end-to-end multi-task framework that explicitly models temporal dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262452/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 30-frame long-wave infrared (8–13 μm) image sequence at 256×256 resolution (synthetic) or 880×640 (real-world) and simultaneously produces a restored image sequence at full resolution and four physical field maps (C_n², C_T², ε, RS) at 1/16 spatial resolution (16×16 for 256×256 input). During training, inputs are randomly cropped to 128×128 with a batch size of 2. The synthetic dataset provides paired ground-truth restoration labels and dynamic physical fields, while real-world data (80,580 frames from three Chinese locations) is used for validation without field ground truth, with physical plausibility verified via temperature fluctuation measurements.

## Method

TurboMPLE is a multi-task network built on the Progressive Layered Extraction (PLE) architecture, extended with a Cross-Task Mutual Block (CTMB) that enables bidirectional cross-attention between mitigation and estimation experts at each layer, with a progressive mutual weight (ω^i = 0.2 + i×0.4) that strengthens cross-task cooperation in deeper blocks. The encoder embeds the Bouguer-Lambert-Beer law to suppress grayscale drift before stacked convolutions and temporal attention extract spatio-temporal features. Shared experts use a dual-branch design combining a turbulence-aware convolutional branch (with adaptive spatial modulation) and a 2D-Selective-Scan (SS2D) state-space branch for long-range temporal modeling, while task-specific experts use a lighter convolutional dual-branch. Training uses a composite loss: L1 plus a differentiable re-degradation loss for mitigation, and L1 plus a physics-constraint loss (L_phys) that enforces the known relationships among C_n², C_T², ε, and RS via Eqs. (2), (10), and (11), promoting physically consistent field estimates. The full model uses 2 MutualPLE blocks, 0.942M parameters, and 145 GMACs, achieving state-of-the-art on both tasks.

## Results

On the synthetic turbulence mitigation benchmark (30-frame, 256×256 sequences), TurboMPLE achieves an overall PSNR of 27.57 dB and SSIM of 0.7998, surpassing the second-best method (DATUM, 26.80 dB / 0.7757) by 0.77 dB and 0.0175 respectively, while requiring only 145 GMACs—over 50× fewer than BasicVSR (8355 GMACs). For physical fields estimation, TurboMPLE attains the lowest spatial MAE across all four fields (C_n²: 0.024, C_T²: 0.023, ε: 0.017, RS: 0.023) with average spatial R² of 0.934, a 32.3% MAE reduction over the second-best method (PBCL). In temporal evaluation, TurboMPLE further reduces MAE by 49.2% and improves R² by 0.031 over PBCL, with average temporal R² exceeding 0.99.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TurboMPLE outperforms all 11 mitigation baselines across every turbulence level (weak, medium, strong). The closest competitor is DATUM (26.80 dB), which TurboMPLE surpasses by 0.77 dB PSNR; PBCL (26.57 dB) and TMT (26.69 dB) follow, both trailing by over 0.9 dB. Single-frame methods (ConvIR, MaIR, DeepSN-Net, MHNet) and the recurrent ESTRNN lag further behind, with ConvIR the weakest at 24.60 dB. In terms of efficiency, TurboMPLE (145 GMACs) is over 50× lighter than BasicVSR (8355 GMACs) while delivering 1.16 dB higher PSNR. For physical fields estimation, TurboMPLE dominates all 8 baselines: it achieves 32.3% lower spatial MAE than PBCL (the only prior method jointly estimating 2D turbulence fields) and 49.2% lower temporal MAE, with average spatial R² of 0.934 vs. PBCL's 0.881. General-purpose architectures (UNet, GRCNN, FADE, FoVFormer, STA-DRN, MSU, UwUNet) all fall well short, with FADE being the strongest non-turbulence-specific baseline (spatial R² ≈ 0.71) yet still 0.22 below TurboMPLE.

## Relation to Prior Work

TurboMPLE extends the Progressive Layered Extraction (PLE) multi-task architecture by introducing Cross-Task Mutual Blocks that enable bidirectional feature-level information exchange between task-specific experts, going beyond PLE's unidirectional gating. It departs from the closest prior work, PBCL [43], which uses a sequential three-stage pipeline (estimate → restore → refine) with static field assumptions, by formulating both turbulence mitigation and physical fields estimation as a single end-to-end multi-task problem with explicit temporal dynamics. The Bouguer-Lambert-Beer law embedding and turbulence physics constraints (linking C_n², C_T², ε, and RS) represent a novel physics-informed design that prior deep-learning turbulence methods have not incorporated at the feature level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The jointly trained framework and the 181,290-frame infrared turbulence dataset (with dynamic physical field ground truth) provide a much-needed resource for the field, where prior work either ignored physical interpretability or assumed static turbulence. The 50× computational efficiency over video-based baselines while achieving SOTA makes the approach practical for real-time thermal imaging applications. Open questions include whether the physics constraints generalize to wavelengths and path lengths beyond the 8–13 μm / tens-of-meters regime studied here, and whether the mutual guidance mechanism transfers to other multi-task image restoration settings beyond turbulence.
