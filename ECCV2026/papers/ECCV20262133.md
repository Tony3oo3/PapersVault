## Problem

Consistency models (CMs) are the leading distillation approach for few-step generation, but their practical deployment is hindered by two fundamental issues: training instability caused by loss divergence in the self-supervised consistency term, and inflexible sampling due to error accumulation in the CM sampler. Existing remedies (MeanFlow, AYF, sCM, FACM) address these through architectural tweaks or regularized objectives but overlook the critical role of trajectory selection, coupling optimization over suboptimal or redundant sub-trajectories that degrade convergence or omit the critical path.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262133/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation, the model takes a class label and a noise vector as input and produces a 256×256 RGB image via a LightningDiT-XL/1 backbone operating in the 32×16×16 latent space of a pre-trained VAE. For text-to-image, it takes a text prompt and noise, using the SD3-VAE tokenizer and LoRA (rank 64) fine-tuning. A single trained set of parameters supports inference at any NFE from 1 to 50 via an Euler ODE solver, with classifier-free guidance applied at each step (doubling NFE per step when CFG is active).

## Method

DE-CM adopts the flow-map parameterization f_θ(x_t, t, s) = x_t + (s−t)F_θ(x_t, t, s) but, instead of optimizing over the full {(t,s) | t<s} space, it selects three critical sub-trajectory clusters as optimization targets. (1) The consistency trajectory (s=1) provides the continuous-time distillation loss that maps any noisy point directly to data, enabling few-step inference. (2) The instantaneous trajectory (s=t) reduces to a standard flow-matching loss that acts as a left-boundary regularizer, anchoring the network to the ground-truth velocity and preventing the self-supervised term from diverging. (3) A novel noise-to-noisy (N2N) trajectory (left endpoint r→0, right endpoint t) maps pure noise to arbitrary intermediate states, exposing the model to the same noise distributions encountered during multi-step inference and thereby alleviating the error accumulation inherent in long-jump CM sampling. The three losses are combined and optimized jointly with JVP-based tangent computation, and the paper shows that this minimal trajectory selection is sufficient to achieve stable convergence and state-of-the-art FID 1.70 at 1 NFE on ImageNet 256×256.

## Results

On ImageNet-256×256, DE-CM achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.70 at 1 NFE and 1.33 at 2 NFE, outperforming all existing CM-based distillation methods (FACM: 1.76/1.76, sCMs: 3.34/1.94, MeanFlow: 3.43/2.20). At 50 NFE, DE-CM reaches FID 1.26, surpassing its own teacher model LightningDiT (1.35). On text-to-image generation, DE-CM achieves CLIP 0.3000, BLIP 0.5479, and ImageReward 0.8671 at 8 NFE, outperforming LCM, CTM, PCM, and Hyper-SD across all evaluated NFE settings (1, 4, 8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DE-CM decisively outperforms all CM-based distillation baselines on ImageNet-256×256: at 1 NFE it achieves FID 1.70 vs. FACM (1.76), sCMs (3.34), MeanFlow (3.43), IMM (7.77), Shortcut (10.60), and iCT (34.24); at 2 NFE it reaches 1.33 vs. FACM (1.76), sCMs (1.94), and MeanFlow (2.20). Notably, DE-CM at 50 NFE (FID 1.26) surpasses its own 250-step teacher LightningDiT (1.35), demonstrating that the distillation process improves rather than degrades quality. On the T2I benchmark, DE-CM dominates all few-step competitors (LCM, CTM, PCM, Hyper-SD) on CLIP, BLIP, and ImageReward at every NFE setting (1, 4, 8), with the largest margins at 1 NFE (e.g., BLIP 0.5398 vs. LCM 0.4560, CTM 0.4764). At 50 NFE, DE-CM also edges out the multi-step baselines SD3.5-Medium (BLIP 0.5451) and Flux.1-Dev (BLIP 0.5384) with BLIP 0.5490 and ImageReward 0.9712. The paper also reports that under a 16-GPU-hour budget, DE-CM converges faster and produces clearer 1-NFE samples than both MeanFlow and sCMs, indicating superior training efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

DE-CM extends the continuous-time consistency model framework (sCM, Song et al.) by adopting the dual-endpoint flow map parameterization f_θ(x_t, t, s) introduced by MeanFlow and AYF, but departs from their strategy of optimizing over the entire (t, s) space. Instead, it identifies three specific sub-trajectory clusters—consistency (s=1), instantaneous (s=t), and the novel noise-to-noisy (r→0, s=t)—as sufficient optimization targets, with flow matching serving as a left-boundary regularizer rather than the primary objective. This trajectory-selection philosophy contrasts with FACM's decoupled velocity objective and with Shortcut-style methods that anchor stability near d=0, positioning DE-CM as an objective-level diagnosis-and-correction approach to CM training instability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DE-CM provides a principled decomposition of why CMs fail (self-supervised loss divergence and error accumulation in long-jump sampling) and offers a targeted fix that achieves SOTA 1-NFE FID (1.70) on ImageNet-256 while maintaining stable multi-step performance—a practical win for deployment. However, the JVP operator's incompatibility with FSDP and Flash Attention imposes significant GPU memory overhead, limiting scalability to larger architectures. Open questions include whether the three-cluster selection generalizes beyond the (t,s) flow-map formulation, whether the N2N mapping can be extended to conditional or multi-modal settings without the teacher-model dependency, and whether the approach transfers cleanly to video or 3D generation where trajectory structure differs.
