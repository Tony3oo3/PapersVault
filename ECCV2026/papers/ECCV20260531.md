## Problem

Most digital videos are stored in 8-bit LDR formats where highlight and shadow radiance is irreversibly lost through saturation and quantization, precluding accurate re-exposure and HDR display. Existing single-image LDR-to-HDR methods use deterministic feed-forward networks that struggle with the inherent one-to-many ambiguity in clipped regions, while generative approaches lack large-scale paired HDR video training data and face a fundamental distribution mismatch between LDR-trained video diffusion models and HDR content.

## Input / Output

The method takes an 8-bit sRGB LDR video (processed at 33×1280×720 spatiotemporal resolution) and optionally accepts structured text prompts in the format [overexposed: <description>]; [underexposed: <description>] and/or a reference image for fine-grained appearance control. The output is a linear-space (Rec.709) HDR video with continuous scene radiance, suitable for downstream re-exposure, tone mapping, and HDR display. The LDR input is linearized and mapped into a Log-Gamma color space before VAE encoding, while the final HDR latent is inverse-mapped back to linear radiance after decoding.

## Method

DiffHDR formulates LDR-to-HDR conversion as generative radiance inpainting in the latent space of a pretrained video diffusion model (Wan-2.1-VACE-14B). The key enabler is a Log-Gamma color mapping T(x) = (log(1+γx)/log(1+γM))^(1/γ) that compresses HDR radiance into the operational range of the LDR-trained VAE, eliminating the need for VAE finetuning while preserving the backbone's generative priors. Training uses rank-32 LoRA adapters on the DiT blocks (backbone frozen) with a rectified flow matching objective, supervised on a synthetic dataset of ~5400 HDR video sequences rendered from Polyhaven HDRIs with simulated LDR degradation (exposure shift, AR(1) temporal noise, 8-bit quantization, clipping). For controllability, a luminance-based mask (thresholded at 0.95/0.05 with EMA temporal smoothing) guides inpainting, and a context-focused cross-attention module—applied exclusively at inference—routes region-specific text embeddings into over- and underexposed areas via mask-guided residual addition, enabling text- and image-guided hallucination without retraining.

## Results

On the Cinematic Video dataset, DiffHDR achieves FOVVDP 6.89, DOVER 0.81, MUSIQ 58.38, and CLIPIQA 0.41, outperforming SingleHDR (6.56/0.77/51.79/0.33) and LEDiff (3.75/0.63/47.90/0.28) across all four metrics. On the Polyhaven synthetic video dataset, DiffHDR scores 7.65/0.68/60.02/0.50, again leading both baselines. On the SI-HDR image benchmark, DiffHDR achieves the best PU21-PIQE (19.37) and FID (18.68) among all methods, while ranking second on HDR-VDP3 (6.98 vs. SingleHDR's 7.37), which the authors attribute to generative inpainting producing plausible but non-pixel-identical details in clipped regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffHDR is compared against four baselines: HDRCNN [17], MaskHDR [76], SingleHDR [59], and LEDiff [86]. On the SI-HDR image benchmark (Table 1), DiffHDR achieves the best PU21-PIQE (19.37) and FID (18.68), beating HDRCNN (24.30/19.26), MaskHDR (24.00/19.78), SingleHDR (26.64/27.55), and LEDiff (22.71/25.98), but trails SingleHDR (7.37) and LEDiff (6.56) on HDR-VDP3 by 0.39 and 0.42 points respectively. On all video benchmarks (Tables 2 and 3), DiffHDR consistently outperforms both SingleHDR and LEDiff across every metric: on the Cinematic Video dataset it gains +0.33 FOVVDP and +6.59 MUSIQ over SingleHDR, and a substantial +3.14 FOVVDP over LEDiff; on in-the-wild Pexels videos it leads by +0.03 DOVER and +2.58 MUSIQ over SingleHDR. The consistent pattern is that DiffHDR wins on all perceptual and video-quality metrics while slightly losing on the reference-based HDR-VDP3, which penalizes the generative inpainting of clipped regions that are plausible but not pixel-identical to ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LDR-to-HDR conversion task from the image domain (where prior work like SingleHDR, MaskHDR, and LEDiff operate) to the video domain by leveraging a pretrained video diffusion backbone (VACE/Wan-2.1). It departs from prior generative HDR approaches (LEDiff, HDR-V-Diff, X2HDR) that either fine-tune the VAE or operate at the image level, instead introducing a Log-Gamma color mapping that aligns HDR radiance with the pretrained LDR VAE's latent manifold without any VAE modification. The context-focused cross-attention mechanism for region-specific text conditioning is a novel inference-time intervention not seen in prior HDR or video editing work, and the synthetic video curation pipeline from static Polyhaven HDRIs addresses a data gap that no prior video-HDR method has tackled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Given that virtually all existing digital video is 8-bit LDR, a practical method to generatively recover HDR radiance with re-exposure latitude has significant industrial impact for post-production, color grading, and HDR display workflows. The framework's controllability (text/image-guided hallucination in clipped regions) opens creative possibilities beyond deterministic restoration. Key limitations include the 720p training resolution (4K inference remains infeasible), reliance on synthetic training data that may not capture all real-world HDR characteristics, and the inherent one-to-many ambiguity in severely clipped regions. Open questions include whether the Log-Gamma mapping generalizes to other pretrained VAEs, whether higher-resolution training is feasible with current compute, and how the method handles dynamic scenes with large motion beyond the synthetic camera motions used in training.
