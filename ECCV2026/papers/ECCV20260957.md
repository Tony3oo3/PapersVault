## Problem

Both CatVTON (860M UNet) and Voost (11.9B DiT) independently report that full fine-tuning is ineffective under spatial concatenation for virtual try-on, yet no prior work explains why or whether the failure is resolvable. This paper identifies the root cause: spatial concatenation embeds the garment latent within the denoising target, violating the principle that garment conditioning must be structurally decoupled from the denoising process, and producing three functional conflicts—guidance leakage, gradient competition, and train-test discrepancy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260957/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a masked person image and a garment image, both encoded to VAE latents and spatially concatenated along the height dimension (person on top, garment on bottom) with the inpainting mask and clean latents channel-concatenated to form the denoising input. Training and evaluation are conducted at 512×384 resolution (with 1024×768 results in the appendix) on VITON-HD and DressCode. The output is the decoded person-region latent only; the garment region serves purely as a conditioning signal and is discarded at inference.

## Method

DeCo-VTON applies a three-principle training and inference recipe to CatVTON's unchanged SD1.5 inpainting UNet (860M params) with full fine-tuning, requiring no architectural modification. (1) Garment-Free Guidance removes all garment latents from the unconditional CFG branch so the guidance difference correctly isolates the garment's contribution rather than suppressing it. (2) Decoupled Loss computes the DREAM objective exclusively on the person region, eliminating gradient competition between garment reconstruction and person generation. (3) Clean Latent Anchoring replaces the garment latent at each inference step with the forward-diffused ground truth derived from z_0^g, preventing error accumulation and restoring exact timestep alignment. The paper first establishes the decoupling principle through the first visualization study of dual-UNet reference network noise predictions, showing that Leffa's timestep-aligned conditioning succeeds precisely because the reference UNet is structurally separate from the denoising objective.

## Results

On VITON-HD, DeCo-VTON (860M single UNet) achieves paired FID 4.438, KID 0.010, and LPIPS 0.047, outperforming the dual-UNet state of the art Leffa (1.8B params) on FID and KID while trailing only on SSIM (0.880 vs. 0.899). On DressCode, DeCo-VTON reaches paired FID 2.175 and KID 0.062, matching Leffa on LPIPS (0.031) and yielding a lower KID, while achieving the best unpaired FID (4.310 vs. 4.480). In efficiency, DeCo-VTON runs at 1.3 s latency with 2.26 GB peak VRAM—2.1× faster and 42% less memory than Leffa (2.7 s, 3.91 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dual-UNet state of the art Leffa (1.8B params), DeCo-VTON (860M params) wins on VITON-HD paired FID (4.438 vs. 4.540), KID (0.010 vs. 0.050), and LPIPS (0.047 vs. 0.048), but loses on SSIM (0.880 vs. 0.899) and unpaired KID (0.517 vs. 0.320). On DressCode, the two are nearly tied: DeCo-VTON is slightly worse on paired FID (2.175 vs. 2.060) and SSIM (0.914 vs. 0.924) but better on KID (0.062 vs. 0.070) and unpaired FID (4.310 vs. 4.480). Against the single-network baseline CatVTON (same 860M backbone, attention-only training), DeCo-VTON improves paired FID from 5.425 to 4.438 and KID from 0.411 to 0.010 on VITON-HD, and from 3.992 to 2.175 on DressCode. Compared to other single-network methods (LaDI-VTON, DCI-VTON, StableVTON), DeCo-VTON leads on all paired metrics on both benchmarks. In a user study (25 participants, 30 samples), DeCo-VTON is preferred over CatVTON at ~3.5:1 and over Leffa at ~2:1 (both p < 0.001). The efficiency advantage is substantial: DeCo-VTON requires 2.1× less inference time and 42% less VRAM than Leffa while matching or exceeding its quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CatVTON's spatial-concatenation formulation (SD1.5 inpainting UNet, 860M params) and provides the first systematic analysis of why full fine-tuning fails under this design—a puzzle independently observed by both CatVTON (860M UNet) and Voost (11.9B DiT). Conceptually, it departs from the dual-UNet lineage (IDM-VTON, OOTDiffusion, Leffa) by showing that their effectiveness stems from a structural decoupling between garment conditioning and the denoising process, which spatial concatenation inadvertently destroys. Rather than proposing a new architecture, the paper introduces a training-and-inference recipe (Garment-Free Guidance, Decoupled Loss, Clean Latent Anchoring) that restores this decoupling within the existing CatVTON backbone, positioning the contribution as diagnostic and corrective rather than architectural.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a community-accepted limitation (full fine-tuning is ineffective under spatial concatenation) by identifying three specific functional conflicts and showing they are fixable without architectural change, yielding dual-UNet SOTA quality at half the parameters, 2.1× faster inference, and 42% lower VRAM. The decoupling principle is framed as potentially generalizable to any spatial-concatenation task where a known signal is embedded in the denoising target (e.g., reference-based inpainting, outpainting), though this remains untested. Key open questions include whether the principles transfer to DiT/flow-matching backbones (only GFG is CFG-specific; DL and CLA are argued to be architecture-agnostic) and whether the recipe holds at higher resolutions or with different guidance mechanisms.
