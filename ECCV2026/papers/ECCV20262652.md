## Problem

Existing video retrieval pipelines treat inter-video retrieval as a one-shot preprocessing step, so when the initial retrieval fails there is no mechanism to refine the search, and errors propagate to downstream intra-video reasoning. Meanwhile, recent agentic video frameworks assume the query-relevant video is already given and focus exclusively on intra-video tasks, leaving the retrieval stage unaddressed. This paper tackles the gap by unifying iterative, self-correcting video retrieval with fine-grained query-conditioned temporal grounding in a single agentic loop.

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text query and a large-scale video corpus as input, with videos sampled at 1 FPS (up to 64 frames, 4096 visual tokens) and encoded by a frozen Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B dense retriever. At each turn the model outputs a match/mismatch decision, a chain-of-thought reasoning trace, and—upon a match—start and end timestamps for the query-relevant segment. Uniquely, when a mismatch is detected the model emits N=8 continuous soft query tokens (not discrete text) that are concatenated to the original query embedding and fed back into the search engine, enabling up to T=2 refinement turns before the episode terminates.

## Method

VIDEOSEARCH-R1 is built on Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct and operates as a multi-turn agent that alternates between invoking an external video search engine, verifying query-video alignment, refining the query, and performing temporal grounding. The key mechanism is Soft Query Refinement (SQR): instead of rewriting the query in discrete text space, the model autoregressively generates 8 continuous latent tokens (projected hidden states) that are appended to the original query embedding, enabling fine-grained adjustments in the retriever's embedding space with far fewer tokens than text-level rewriting. Training is two-stage: (1) SFT cold-start with a structured reasoning template, where soft query tokens are supervised via an InfoNCE contrastive objective against the ground-truth video and in-batch negatives, and (2) GRPO reinforcement learning with four composite rewards—format compliance, verification accuracy, retrieval similarity (exp of negative InfoNCE loss), and IoU-based temporal grounding—jointly optimizing both inter-video retrieval and intra-video reasoning trajectories.

## Results

VIDEOSEARCH-R1 achieves state-of-the-art VCMR performance across all three benchmarks. On DiDeMo-FIG, it reaches 33.3 on 0.3/R@1 (vs. 23.6 for the fine-tuned same-backbone baseline), 30.2 on 0.5/R@1, and 19.7 on 0.7/R@1. On ActivityNet-FIG, it scores 33.8 on 0.3/R@1 and 22.3 on 0.5/R@1, while on Charades-FIG it attains 16.5 on 0.3/R@1. For video retrieval (VR), SQR-based iterative refinement boosts R@1 by up to 6.0 points over the non-refining baseline (55.1→61.1 on ActivityNet-FIG), and verification accuracy reaches 83.3% on ActivityNet-FIG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIDEOSEARCH-R1 is compared against four baselines: two traditional VCMR methods (CONQUER and SQuiDNet) and two same-backbone VLM baselines (Qwen3-VL-2B zero-shot and fine-tuned without SQR). Against the traditional methods, VIDEOSEARCH-R1 dominates on VCMR by a wide margin—for example, on DiDeMo-FIG it achieves 33.3 on 0.3/R@1 versus 5.5 (CONQUER) and 2.9 (SQuiDNet) on 0.5/R@1—though the traditional methods do not report 0.3/R@1 or VER. Against the same-backbone Qwen3-VL-2B (FT) baseline, VIDEOSEARCH-R1 improves 0.3/R@1 by +9.7 on DiDeMo-FIG (33.3 vs. 23.6), +4.7 on ActivityNet-FIG (33.8 vs. 29.1), and +3.6 on Charades-FIG (16.5 vs. 12.9). On video retrieval R@1, the gain is more modest but consistent: +4.2 on DiDeMo-FIG (59.0 vs. 54.8), +6.0 on ActivityNet-FIG (61.1 vs. 55.1), and +3.0 on Charades-FIG (24.6 vs. 21.6), attributable to SQR's iterative query refinement. Verification accuracy is comparable to the FT baseline (74.6 vs. 73.1 on DiDeMo-FIG; 83.3 vs. 83.1 on ActivityNet-FIG), confirming that the VCMR gains stem primarily from improved temporal grounding rather than better matching decisions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) video retrieval and reranking (CLIP-based dual-encoders, VLM-based rerankers), (2) agentic multi-turn reasoning frameworks inspired by Search-R1 and RAG, and (3) soft/latent reasoning in LLMs (Coconut, latent chain-of-thought). It departs from prior video agentic frameworks that assume the relevant video is already given, and from decoupled retrieval-then-grounding pipelines (CONQUER, SQuiDNet), by unifying inter-video retrieval and intra-video temporal grounding in a single iterative loop. The key methodological departure is Soft Query Refinement (SQR), which extends the Coconut-style continuous latent reasoning idea from chain-of-thought compression to query representation refinement, replacing discrete text-level query rewriting with a small set of latent tokens trained via InfoNCE contrastive loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By treating retrieval as an iterative, self-correcting process rather than a one-shot preprocessing step, VIDEOSEARCH-R1 addresses a practical failure mode where initial retrieval errors cascade into downstream grounding failures. The SQR mechanism is a lightweight, generalizable technique (8 latent tokens vs. ~27 text tokens) that could be adopted in other retrieval-augmented multimodal pipelines. Limitations include evaluation on only a 2B backbone, three VCMR datasets, and a maximum of two refinement turns; open questions include whether SQR scales to larger corpora, more complex multi-hop retrieval, or longer reasoning chains, and whether the contrastive InfoNCE supervision for soft tokens transfers to settings without a fixed search engine embedding space.
