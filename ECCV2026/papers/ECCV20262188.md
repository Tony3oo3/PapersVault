## Problem

Multi-modal image matching is bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale training data with precise geometric annotations: real-world multi-modal datasets are prohibitively expensive to acquire, limited in scene diversity, and suffer from SfM-MVS error accumulation, while existing synthetic methods either inherit SfM errors, discard 3D geometric consistency via 2D homography, or fail to achieve photorealistic appearance via game engines. Abundant single-view images from public datasets remain untapped because no effective mechanism exists to convert them into multi-view, multi-modal pairs with pixel-level geometric supervision.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a single 512×512 RGB image from public datasets (GLDv2, SA-1B) and produces multi-view, multi-modal image pairs across four modality combinations (RGB-IR, RGB-Depth, RGB-Normal, RGB-Event), each accompanied by depth maps, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and pixel-level correspondence annotations. The geometric annotations are derived from explicit 3D reprojection rather than SfM reconstruction, ensuring strict 3D geometric consistency. The synthetic training set (Any-syn) comprises 500,000 image pairs from GLDv2, with a 10,000-pair test subset from SA-1B.

## Method

AnyMatch decouples data generation into two independent modules: view transformation and modality transformation. View transformation lifts a 2D image into 3D via monocular depth estimation (MoGe V1) with random affine perturbations to model scale uncertainty, assigns random camera intrinsics (focal length ∈ [0.58, 0.88]) and extrinsics (rotation ±7.5°, translation up to ±0.5), reprojects the point cloud into a novel view via differentiable rendering, and inpaints occluded regions with Stable Diffusion V2. Modality transformation applies parallel, modality-specific models (DiffV2IR for infrared, MoGe V2 for normals, depth estimation for depth, and log-intensity motion simulation for events) to the original image. A Sample-level Geometric Consistency Verification (SGCV) step uses a dense matcher (RoMa) to compute endpoint error against geometrically projected correspondences, filtering out samples where PCK@5px < 60% to eliminate inpainting hallucinations. Existing matchers (LoFTR, EDM, RoMa) are then fine-tuned on the resulting Any-syn dataset, with the key advantage that annotations strictly follow 3D geometric consistency without SfM-MVS error accumulation.

## Results

On the Any-syn synthetic benchmark, RoMa+AnyMatch achieves the strongest overall performance, reaching AUC@10° of 69.88% (RGB-IR), 29.39% (RGB-Depth), 56.50% (RGB-Normal), and 11.87% (RGB-Event), surpassing RoMa+MINIMA by 9.26, 7.23, 7.17, and 4.58 percentage points respectively. On real-world datasets (METU-VisTIR, DIODE, DSEC), RoMa+AnyMatch attains 40.49% AUC@5° on RGB-IR pose estimation and 39.26% AUC@3px on RGB-Normal homography estimation, outperforming RoMa+MINIMA on most modality pairs. Zero-shot evaluation on the unseen MMIM dataset (medical and remote sensing) confirms superior generalization, with RoMa+AnyMatch achieving 30.25% AUC@3px on remote sensing and 59.08% AUC@10px on medical imaging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoMa+AnyMatch dominates all baselines on the Any-syn benchmark, outperforming RoMa+MINIMA by 9.26 pp (RGB-IR @10°: 69.88% vs 60.62%), 7.23 pp (RGB-Depth), 7.17 pp (RGB-Normal), and 4.58 pp (RGB-Event), and exceeding RoMa+MatchAnything by 14.20 pp on RGB-IR @10°. The same pattern holds for EDM and LoFTR: AnyMatch-fine-tuned variants consistently surpass their MINIMA-fine-tuned counterparts across all four modality pairs. On real-world datasets (Table 2), RoMa+AnyMatch wins on RGB-IR (40.49% vs 37.45% AUC@5°) and RGB-Normal (39.26% vs 37.91% AUC@3px) but is marginally behind RoMa+MINIMA on RGB-Depth (29.12% vs 29.77% AUC@3px), which the authors attribute to inpainting hallucinations and depth estimation errors. On the zero-shot MMIM benchmark, RoMa+AnyMatch achieves the best remote sensing score (30.25% AUC@3px) and competitive medical scores (59.08% AUC@10px), confirming that AnyMatch-trained models learn modality-invariant representations that transfer to unseen domains. Among dedicated multi-modal matchers (ReDFeat, XoFTR, ELoFTR+PYM), AnyMatch-fine-tuned models substantially outperform them on all modality pairs, with RoMa+AnyMatch exceeding XoFTR by 22.81 pp on RGB-IR @10° (69.88% vs 47.37%).

## Relation to Prior Work

AnyMatch carves new ground by repurposing components from distinct subfields—monocular depth estimation (MoGe), differentiable 3D reprojection, diffusion-based inpainting (Stable Diffusion), and cross-modal translation (DiffV2IR)—into a unified synthetic data generation pipeline for multi-modal matching. It departs conceptually from MINIMA and MatchAnything, which inherit SfM-MVS geometric errors from multi-view source datasets, and from homography-based methods that discard 3D parallax-occlusion relationships. The key positioning is that single-view internet images, previously untapped for matching training, can be elevated to multi-view, multi-modal pairs with explicit 3D geometric supervision, bypassing the SfM-MVS bottleneck entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Any-syn dataset (500K training pairs across four modality combinations) and the AnyMatch pipeline offer a practical, low-cost path to scaling multi-modal matching training data, directly addressing the field's central bottleneck of scarce, geometrically accurate annotations. The SGCV filtering mechanism and the ablation showing 3D reprojection substantially outperforms homography-based synthesis provide actionable design guidance. Open questions remain around how monocular depth estimation errors propagate into matching quality under large viewpoint changes, whether the inpainting hallucination problem can be further reduced, and whether the approach extends to even more exotic modality pairs (e.g., LiDAR, hyperspectral) without additional translation models.
