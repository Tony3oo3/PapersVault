## Problem

State-of-the-art WSI survival prediction models suffer severe performance degradation (average C-index drop of ~0.19) when transferred zero-shot across clinical centers due to staining and scanner domain shifts, and existing domain adaptation methods require access to target-domain data that is typically unavailable in clinical deployment. The paper identifies that pixel-derived representations entangle prognostic morphology with domain-specific confounders, while high-level pathology semantics (tumor grade, necrosis, immune microenvironment) remain stable across centers, yet no prior work leverages such semantics for target-free cross-center survival prediction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single WSI as input, from which patch features are extracted via the CONCH vision-language model encoder, and additionally queries a pathology VQA model (ALPaCA) with 12 fixed closed-form multiple-choice questions to produce a structured semantic anchor vector. The output is a Dirichlet distribution over K discretized survival intervals, yielding a belief mass vector, an epistemic uncertainty scalar, and a continuous risk score used for Kaplan-Meier stratification; the evidential formulation provides calibrated survival probabilities rather than a single point estimate.

## Method

SAEFS operates in four stages: (1) template-structured VQA generates domain-invariant semantic anchors per slide; (2) a dual-stream architecture extracts visual evidence — a text-guided branch that uses semantic anchors as queries for similarity-weighted patch aggregation, and a WSI-only attention-pooling branch — combined via a learned mixing coefficient; (3) the semantic anchor embeddings are independently mapped to a text evidence vector; (4) both visual and text evidence are converted to Dirichlet parameters and then to belief-uncertainty pairs via Subjective Logic, fused through a cautious conjunction rule (element-wise minimum on commonality functions) that preserves uncertainty when modalities are correlated, avoiding the overconfidence of Dempster's rule. Training uses a survival loss (hazard for uncensored, cumulative survival for censored samples) plus an annealed KL regularizer toward a uniform Dirichlet, and the entire fusion module contains only 270K trainable parameters since semantic anchors are cached offline.

## Results

Trained exclusively on TCGA and evaluated zero-shot on four unseen external cohorts (CPTAC-LUAD, CPTAC-UCEC, CPTAC-KIRC, NLST-LUAD), SAEFS achieves an average C-index of 0.671, outperforming the strongest unimodal baseline OTSurv (0.639) by +0.062 and the strongest multimodal baseline PS3 (0.562) by +0.109. SAEFS also attains the lowest average INBLL (0.755) across all 12 baselines, indicating superior probabilistic calibration. The gains are most pronounced on high-shift cohorts: on CPTAC-LUAD, SAEFS reaches 0.663 C-index versus 0.529 for the best unimodal competitor, a +0.134 improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against eight unimodal MIL baselines, SAEFS achieves the highest average C-index (0.671), surpassing the best competitor OTSurv (0.639) by +0.062 and ILRA (0.593) by +0.078; the gap widens on high-shift cohorts (e.g., +0.134 on CPTAC-LUAD over OTSurv). Against four multimodal baselines that also fuse WSI features with textual/omic representations, SAEFS leads by a substantial margin: +0.109 over PS3 (0.562), +0.137 over MOTCAT (0.534), +0.148 over MCAT (0.523), and +0.157 over SurvPath (0.514) in average C-index. On IBS, SAEFS (0.299) is slightly worse than the multimodal baselines (MOTCAT 0.237, MCAT 0.246, PS3 0.247), which the authors attribute to those methods' naive concatenation producing overconfident but miscalibrated probabilities; however, SAEFS achieves the best INBLL (0.755) among all methods, confirming superior overall probabilistic fit. The ablation further shows that replacing cautious belief fusion with Dempster's rule drops C-index by 0.108, and simple concatenation drops it by 0.153, underscoring that the evidential fusion mechanism—not merely the addition of a semantic branch—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of WSI-based survival prediction (MIL paradigm), evidential deep learning, and cross-center domain generalization in histopathology. It builds directly on Dirichlet-based Subjective Logic (Sensoy et al., Jøsang) for uncertainty modeling and departs from the standard Dempster-Shafer combination rule by adopting Denoeux's cautious conjunction to handle the correlated visual-semantic evidence that arises under domain shift. Its key conceptual departure from prior multimodal survival work (MCAT, SurvPath, PS3) is the use of template-structured VQA to generate domain-invariant semantic anchors that both guide visual attention and serve as an independent evidence stream, rather than relying on co-attention or concatenation over pixel-derived features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of cross-center domain shift in WSI survival analysis under a strict zero-shot, target-free protocol, addressing a gap between benchmark performance and real-world clinical deployment. The MMD analysis (73% reduction for semantic vs. pixel features) provides clean empirical evidence that high-level pathology semantics are substantially more domain-invariant, offering a principled rationale for semantic-centric approaches. Limitations include reliance on a single source domain (TCGA), a fixed set of 12 template questions, and a specific VQA backbone (ALPaCA); open questions include whether open-ended VLMs can capture rarer morphological subtypes and whether uncertainty-aware VLMs can provide semantic-level confidence for downstream diagnosis.
