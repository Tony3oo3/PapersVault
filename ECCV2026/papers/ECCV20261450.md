## Problem

Existing agentic 3D scene synthesis frameworks like SceneWeaver rely on heuristic, step-by-step tool selection and an execute-review-reflect loop that renders intermediate results after every step. This design leads to suboptimal execution flows, unnecessary tool invocations, and severe runtime overhead (~1.5 hours per scene on a single A6000 GPU), limiting practical scalability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261450/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language instruction describing a desired indoor scene (ranging from generic templates like 'Design me a bedroom' to complex specifications with object counts, positions, and spatial relationships) and produces a complete tool-call trajectory — a sequence of tool invocations with parameters — which is then executed end-to-end within SceneWeaver's tool framework to yield a 3D scene. At inference, only the fine-tuned orchestrator is invoked; the discriminator used during training is not needed.

## Method

SceneOrchestra comprises two fine-tuned Qwen3-4B LLMs (an orchestrator and a discriminator) trained via a two-phase strategy. In Phase 1 (independent training), the orchestrator undergoes four sequential stages — stepwise SFT, trajectory-level SFT, stepwise DPO, and trajectory-level DPO — to learn context-aware tool selection and full trajectory generation, while the discriminator learns trajectory-level ranking via SFT. In Phase 2 (interleaved training), the discriminator adapts to the orchestrator's DPO-shifted trajectory distribution by retraining on newly sampled and executed trajectories, then its updated ranking ability is distilled back into the orchestrator through trajectory-level DPO using discriminator-generated preference pairs (no execution required). This eliminates the step-by-step review loop entirely: at inference the orchestrator predicts the entire tool-call trajectory in one shot, reducing runtime to ~40 minutes while improving scene quality over SceneWeaver.

## Results

SceneOrchestra achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 10 evaluated room types (5 unseen, 5 seen) on both physical and visual-semantic metrics. On unseen rooms, it generates more objects with zero collisions and out-of-boundary violations (e.g., 15.4 objects, 0.0 OB, 0.0 CN in Bedroom; 34.8 objects in Kitchen) while scoring highest on visual realism (9.0–9.2), functionality (9.2), layout (7.6–8.0), and completeness (8.0–8.2). On complex instructions, it outperforms SceneWeaver on all metrics (27.6 vs. 24.3 objects; 8.6 vs. 8.2 realism; 7.7 vs. 7.3 completeness) while reducing generation time from 82.6 to 46.9 minutes (~43% speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneOrchestra outperforms all four scene-synthesis baselines (LayoutGPT, Holodeck, I-Design, SceneWeaver) on nearly every metric across both seen and unseen room types. Against the primary agentic baseline SceneWeaver (same tool implementations), SceneOrchestra consistently generates more objects (e.g., 15.4 vs. 13.6 in Bedroom; 38.6 vs. 35.2 in Office), achieves higher visual-semantic scores (e.g., 8.2 vs. 6.6 completeness in Bedroom; 8.2 vs. 7.4 in Office), and reduces runtime by ~43% on complex instructions (46.9 vs. 82.6 min). Holodeck sometimes produces more objects but suffers severe collisions (34.4 in Bedroom, 10.8 in Office) due to weak physical constraints. LayoutGPT and I-Design generate far fewer objects and score lowest on completeness (3.8–5.2 range). In the orchestrator ablation (Table 5), off-the-shelf LLMs in an in-context learning setting (GPT-5.5: 24.2 objects, 7.4 completeness; Gemini-3.1: 20.7 objects, 7.3 completeness) perform substantially worse than the fine-tuned Qwen3-4B orchestrator (31.1 objects, 8.1 completeness), confirming that explicit fine-tuning is critical for trajectory generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SceneWeaver, an agentic 3D scene synthesis framework that uses an off-the-shelf LLM to orchestrate heterogeneous tools in an execute-review-reflect loop. The key departure is replacing heuristic, step-by-step tool selection with a fine-tuned orchestrator (Qwen3-4B via SFT and DPO) that predicts complete tool-call trajectories in one shot, thereby eliminating the costly intermediate rendering and review at each step. The training strategy draws on the agentic RL literature (SFT for imitation, DPO for preference learning) but applies it to trajectory-level tool orchestration rather than policy optimization, and introduces an interleaved discriminator-to-orchestrator distillation phase to handle distribution shift. Compared to concurrent agentic works like SceneSmith and SAGE, which still rely on off-the-shelf LLMs for orchestration, this paper explicitly fine-tunes the orchestrator for global planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is notable: reducing per-scene generation time from ~90 minutes to ~40 minutes on a single A6000 GPU while simultaneously improving quality metrics, making agentic 3D scene synthesis more scalable for applications in VR, embodied AI, and robotic simulation. The trainable-orchestrator paradigm—where a small fine-tuned model outperforms frontier LLMs in in-context learning for structured tool-call generation—suggests a general recipe for other multi-tool agentic pipelines where step-by-step review is expensive. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond SceneWeaver's specific tool set, whether multiple rounds of interleaved training (only one is used in practice) would yield further gains, and how the method scales to more complex or outdoor scene specifications.
