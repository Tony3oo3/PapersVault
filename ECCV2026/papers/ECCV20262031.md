## Problem

Modeling, synthesizing, and rendering complex mesoscale appearances (grass, fur, wicker) on arbitrary objects in real time remains challenging. Existing NeRF-based texture methods (NeRF-Tex, NeRF-Texture) produce blurry results and run at seconds per synthesis, while 3D Gaussian Splatting offers real-time quality but its explicit representation makes texture synthesis—naturally suited to latent spaces—non-trivial.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262031/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view images of a source object, a target mesh with a UV map, and scene lighting conditions (represented as spherical harmonics). It produces a multi-layer Gaussian texture of shape N×H×W×D, where each texel stores one 3D Gaussian's parameters (position, opacity, scale, rotation) plus 8D latent material features replacing the standard 48D color SHs. At runtime, the texture is synthesized to arbitrary resolutions in ~20 microseconds and rendered on the target mesh with basic relighting.

## Method

The core representation is a multi-layer Gaussian texture where N layers separate Gaussians by height above the surface, ensuring each texel holds exactly one Gaussian and synthesis never blends Gaussians at drastically different elevations. Structure-preserving synthesis converts Gaussian positions to offsets from texel centers before blending, and applies opacity-aware weight modification that splits patches into opaque/transparent batches to avoid translucent artifacts; patch selection uses autocovariance-based importance sampling for structure preservation. A deformation-aware mapping projects Gaussians onto arbitrary meshes via barycentric UV coordinates with a scaling constraint to prevent needle-like artifacts. For relighting, the Phong model decomposes color into material attributes (diffuse, specular, glossiness, normal) decoded from 8D latent features by a small MLP, with a three-stage training pipeline that first learns geometry, then introduces an encoder-decoder to regularize the latent space, and finally drops the encoder for real-time inference.

## Results

On four mesoscale appearance examples (Grass, Durian, Wicker, Flower), the method achieves SIFID scores of 10.86, 4.82, 4.38, and 5.26, consistently outperforming NeRF-Texture (15.94, 10.71, 6.89, 6.96). Memory usage is substantially lower, ranging from 140 MB to 420 MB versus 855.7 MB to 1.1 GB for NeRF-Texture. The method achieves approximately 500× speedup in synthesis and rendering compared to NeRF-Texture, with synthesis of six mesoscale appearances taking about 20 microseconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares exclusively against NeRF-Texture [14], the most recent NeRF-based mesoscale texture synthesis method. The proposed method wins on all four evaluated examples in both SIFID and memory: SIFID improvements range from 1.7× (Flower: 5.26 vs 6.96) to 2.2× (Durian: 4.82 vs 10.71), while memory is reduced by roughly 3–8×. Visually, the method preserves fine structural details (e.g., gap density in grass, spikes on durian, small flowers) that NeRF-Texture loses, and avoids the blurry quality inherent to NeRF-based representations. In terms of speed, the method achieves about 500× acceleration over NeRF-Texture, reducing synthesis from seconds to microseconds. No comparison is made against other 3DGS-based texture methods (e.g., UV Gaussians, Gaussian Shell Maps) or against quilting-based synthesis approaches, limiting the breadth of the empirical evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mesoscale texture synthesis line initiated by NeRF-Tex and NeRF-Texture by replacing the implicit NeRF primitive with explicit 3D Gaussians, which introduces the core challenge of performing texture synthesis on an explicit representation. The multi-layer Gaussian texture (one Gaussian per texel per height layer) is the key architectural contribution that makes 3DGS compatible with classical 2D by-example texture synthesis methods, drawing on variance-preserving blending (Yu et al.) and autocovariance-preserving patch selection (Lutz et al.). For relighting, it borrows the BRDF decomposition and smoothness losses from Relightable 3D Gaussians (Gao et al.) but represents material attributes as latent features decoded by a small MLP, enabling consistent synthesis in a regularized latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~500× speedup over NeRF-Texture (microseconds vs. seconds for synthesis) makes real-time mesoscale appearance transfer practical for games and XR, where NeRF-based methods were previously infeasible. The multi-layer texture concept is a reusable design pattern for making any explicit 3D primitive compatible with 2D texture synthesis. However, limitations remain: the method struggles with complex geometry lacking apparent mesoscale structure, training still takes 0.5–3 hours due to per-iteration barycentric recomputation, and relighting is limited to SH-based illumination rather than full scene lighting. Integrating the approach into traditional real-time rendering pipelines and supporting full relighting are natural next steps.
