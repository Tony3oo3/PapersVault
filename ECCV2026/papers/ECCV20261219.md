## Problem

Existing skeletal motion generation methods are either template-based (restricted to specific topologies like SMPL for humans or SMAL for animals) or rely on per-case optimization that is computationally expensive and sensitive to viewpoint ambiguities. No prior approach provides a single, category-agnostic framework that can animate arbitrary rigged 3D shapes—from biological species to fantastical beings—driven by 2D video while preserving anatomical consistency across diverse joint hierarchies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261219/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh with its associated skeleton (J joint positions in R^{J×3} plus a topological hierarchy graph) and a monocular driving video of L frames (≤40 frames in the benchmark, rendered from a canonical front view at 2 m camera distance). It outputs a sequence of L posed skeletons as global Cartesian joint coordinates in R^{L×J×3}, which can be applied to deform the input mesh. The framework accepts both ground-truth and auto-rigged skeletons, and the driving video can be AI-generated or real-world capture.

## Method

SkelMo is a topology-agnostic diffusion model that denoises global Cartesian joint coordinates (rather than relative rotations) to establish direct geometric correspondence with 2D video. Two key modules bridge the 2D-to-3D semantic gap: (1) a skinning-aware texture-semantic injection that back-projects multi-view DINOv2 features onto mesh vertices, aggregates them to joint-level representations via normalized skinning weights, and injects them through cross-attention; and (2) a bidirectional video-skeleton fusion that runs two parallel cross-attention paths—skeleton queries video for motion cues and video queries skeleton for structural filtering—ensuring motion is adapted to the target topology. The denoising network uses a Skeletal Attention mechanism that modulates self-attention with a tree-based distance matrix from the joint hierarchy. Training uses a standard ε-prediction diffusion loss conditioned on the rest-pose skeleton (concatenated as a structural prior) and video features, followed by 150 iterations of IK-based post-processing to enforce rigid bone constraints.

## Results

On the 100-model test set, SkelMo achieves an MPJPE of 0.054 and a Chamfer Distance of 0.086, substantially outperforming all baselines on both metrics. The best baseline (Puppeteer) records MPJPE 0.161 and CD 0.155, meaning SkelMo reduces joint-position error by roughly 3× and surface-fidelity error by ~1.8×. Under auto-rigged skeletons (Table 3), SkelMo degrades only modestly (MSE 0.048→0.082, Vertex-CD 0.107→0.146) while still far exceeding all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkelMo decisively outperforms all three baselines on both MPJPE and Chamfer Distance. Against Motion 3-to-4 (vertex-trajectory 4D synthesis), SkelMo reduces MPJPE from 0.230 to 0.054 (4.3× improvement) and CD from 0.475 to 0.086 (5.5× improvement), largely because vertex-level methods suffer from non-rigid stretching and mesh adhesion artifacts. Against ActionMesh (temporal 3D diffusion for mesh animation), SkelMo improves MPJPE from 0.197 to 0.054 and CD from 0.418 to 0.086, again avoiding the geometric distortion inherent in independent vertex displacement prediction. Against Puppeteer (optimization-based rigging and animation guided by optical flow and depth), SkelMo still achieves a 3× MPJPE reduction (0.161→0.054) and 1.8× CD reduction (0.155→0.086), demonstrating that the feed-forward diffusion approach avoids the local-optima sensitivity and computational overhead of per-case optimization. The authors note that the most directly comparable concurrent work, MocapAnything [10], could not be included due to unavailable code and models.

## Relation to Prior Work

SkelMo extends AnyTop's topology-agnostic diffusion backbone for character animation by introducing video conditioning and a texture-semantic injection mechanism, moving from unconditional to video-driven generation. It departs from the dominant template-based paradigm (SMPL/SMAL) that restricts motion capture to fixed species, and from per-case optimization methods (Puppeteer, Motion 3-to-4, ActionMesh) that are computationally expensive and sensitive to viewpoint ambiguities. The dataset construction pipeline repurposes Articulation-XL skeletons, Objaverse assets, AutoRig-Pro retargeting, and Qwen LLM orientation alignment to address the critical data scarcity that has limited prior universal motion generation efforts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The curated 25,200-animation dataset with paired rendered videos is a practically significant resource that could accelerate research in category-agnostic 4D content generation beyond this single method. Open questions remain around multimodal conditioning (text, audio), object-environment interaction, and physically-aware constraints, all of which the authors identify as necessary for embodied AI applications. A key limitation is that the most directly comparable method (MocapAnything) could not be evaluated due to unavailable code, and the self-constructed test set limits external validation of the reported margins.
