## Problem

Generative novel view synthesis faces a fundamental dilemma: geometric priors (point clouds, depth maps) provide spatial alignment but become sparse and inaccurate under large view changes, while appearance priors offer visual fidelity but lack geometric correspondence. Existing methods either propagate geometric errors throughout generation or fuse both signals statically, causing signal conflicts that degrade structure and texture. The paper identifies that these two complementary yet incompatible signals cannot be effectively combined simultaneously within a single conditioning regime.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video (or a single image replicated to N frames for static 3D reconstruction) and a target camera trajectory as input. The source video is first processed through a depth estimator (ViPE) to construct a dynamic point cloud, which is then rendered along the target trajectory to produce a coarse scaffold video. The output is a photorealistic novel-view video along the specified camera path, generated in the latent space of a pretrained VAE and decoded to pixel space, supporting both single-image 3D view synthesis and video 4D re-camera in a unified framework.

## Method

MoCam introduces structured denoising dynamics that temporally decouple geometry and appearance conditioning within the diffusion process. A dynamic point cloud is reconstructed from the monocular input via depth estimation and inverse perspective projection, then rendered along the target trajectory to form a scaffold video. During denoising, the model uses a time-dependent conditioning signal c(t): for early timesteps (t > T_switch = 0.85), it conditions on the scaffold latent c^ren (concatenated along the frame dimension) to anchor global structure and motion coherence while tolerating geometric incompleteness; for later timesteps (t ≤ T_switch), it switches to the source video latent c^src to actively correct geometric errors, fill disoccluded regions, and refine high-frequency appearance details. This stage-wise handover prevents scaffold flaws from being baked into the final output while avoiding the signal interference that arises from simultaneous conditioning. The approach is built on the Wan2.2 video diffusion model, fine-tuned on 20K data pairs from MultiCamVideo, and generalizes across backbones (Wan2.1) and input modalities without architectural changes.

## Results

On the 4D re-camera task (OpenVid-1M), MoCam achieves the best scores across all VBench metrics (BC 0.9332, SC 0.9247, IQ 0.6932) and the lowest rotation error (1.36°), while maintaining competitive translation error (5.12). On single-view 3D reconstruction, MoCam attains FVD-V of 255.16 (vs. 289.37 for GEN3C) and CLIP-V of 0.87 (vs. 0.80), indicating substantially better perceptual quality. On the iPhone multi-view benchmark, MoCam leads in PSNR (14.60), SSIM (0.4581), LPIPS (0.4213), and FVD (180.35), outperforming the next-best method by 0.86 dB in PSNR and 82.8 in FVD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoCam outperforms all four baselines across every reported metric. Against GEN3C (the strongest scaffold-based competitor), MoCam improves FVD-V by 31 points (260 vs. 291) and CLIP-V by 0.06 (0.85 vs. 0.79) on 4D re-camera, while matching its rotation error (1.36°) and slightly improving translation error (5.12 vs. 5.13). Against TrajectoryCrafter, the gap widens: FVD-V drops from 317 to 260 and IQ rises from 0.670 to 0.693. Against ReCamMaster (the implicit, scaffold-free method), MoCam leads by large margins on all metrics—FVD-V 260 vs. 362, IQ 0.693 vs. 0.584, and rotation error 1.36° vs. 2.15°—confirming that explicit early-stage geometry anchoring is critical for trajectory adherence. On the iPhone benchmark, MoCam beats GEN3C by 2.24 dB in PSNR and 79.8 in FVD, and beats TrajectoryCrafter by 0.86 dB in PSNR and 93 in FVD. In the dedicated 3D reconstruction comparison (Table 4), MoCam also surpasses ViewCrafter on all metrics (FVD-V 255 vs. 308, CLIP-V 0.87 vs. 0.79, RotErr 1.35° vs. 1.39°).

## Relation to Prior Work

MoCam builds on the render-then-inpaint paradigm established by Gen3C and TrajectoryCrafter, which reconstruct a 3D point cloud scaffold from monocular input and condition a video diffusion model on the rendered scaffold. It departs from these methods by replacing their static, single-signal conditioning with a temporally structured handover: the geometric scaffold guides early denoising timesteps while the source appearance signal takes over in later timesteps. This design is conceptually inspired by recent dynamic-conditioning work (TSM, DMP) that showed adjusting control inputs over the denoising trajectory improves quality, but MoCam applies this principle to resolve a specific geometry-appearance conflict unique to view synthesis. The framework also unifies single-image 3D reconstruction and video 4D re-camera under the same stage-wise mechanism, whereas prior methods typically address each setting separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in generative view synthesis: monocular depth estimation inevitably produces sparse, hole-ridden point clouds that propagate errors through static conditioning pipelines. By showing that late-stage appearance conditioning can actively correct geometric errors rather than merely transfer texture, MoCam offers a principled recipe for tolerating imperfect 3D priors without sacrificing camera control. Limitations include the empirical choice of T_switch (0.85), dependence on a specific depth estimator (ViPE), and evaluation limited to Wan2.1/2.2 backbones. Open questions include whether the stage-wise principle extends to other generative paradigms (e.g., autoregressive video models), whether T_switch can be learned or adapted per-scene, and how the approach behaves under extreme view changes beyond 90-degree orbits.
