## Problem

Strictly equivariant Vector Neuron Networks (VNNs) suffer from representation dimensional collapse when processing approximately self-symmetric inputs, forcing output features toward the fixed-point set of the symmetry group and losing the weak directional cues needed for tasks like pose estimation. This rigid equivariance constraint makes it impossible for the model to produce lower-symmetry outputs from higher-symmetry inputs, a fundamental limitation for any task involving spontaneous symmetry breaking.

## Input / Output

For pose estimation, the model takes a pair of point clouds (original and randomly rotated) sampled from ShapeNet objects and regresses the relative rotation via an MLP on global representations. For isotope prediction, a single rotated 3D molecular point set (from QM9) with a tiny perturbation (ε ∈ [10⁻⁴, 10⁻³]) at one hydrogen site serves as input, and the model outputs the direction vector and radial distance to that site. For classification on ModelNet40, a single point cloud yields a class label through an invariant readout applied to the vector-neuron backbone.

## Method

Ada-VNNs introduce two structural modifications to standard VNNs. First, the Adaptive Gating Unit (AGU) replaces the shared-weight Vector Linear Layer with three independent weight matrices (W¹, W², W³) applied to the x, y, z coordinate channels respectively; the discrepancy among these matrices explicitly parameterizes the degree of equivariance relaxation, and stacking three such layers with VN-ReLU activations forms the AGU. Second, a residual pathway decomposes the network as f(x) = Φ(x) + Ψ(Φ(x)), where Φ is a strictly equivariant backbone (VN-PointNet, VN-DGCNN, or VN-Transformer) and Ψ is the AGU operating on equivariant features to correct symmetry-breaking information, thereby converting hard structural constraints into adjustable soft priors. A theoretical bound (Proposition 1) shows the learned equivariance error satisfies E(f) ≤ 2L + ε, where ε captures intrinsic symmetry breaking in the data, proving that the model adaptively relaxes equivariance only when the task demands it.

## Results

On pose estimation (10° registration recall) across 26 ShapeNet categories, Ada-VNN improves over VNN by 43.2 percentage points on average across three symmetry regimes, and Ada-VNT improves over VN-T by 8.33 percentage points. On continuous self-symmetry categories (lamp, can, pot, jar, speaker, mug), Ada-VNN achieves 70.31% vs VNN's 8.37% (+61.44 pp), while Ada-VNT reaches 72.09% vs VN-T's 51.17% (+20.92 pp). On asymmetric categories, Ada-VNN still outperforms VNN by 19.05 pp (86.24% vs 67.19%), while Ada-VNT maintains performance comparable to VN-T (85.77% vs 86.10%). On ModelNet40 classification, Ada-VNN variants preserve strong rotation robustness with invariance errors within 2× of strictly equivariant counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strictly equivariant VNN baseline, Ada-VNN achieves dramatic gains on symmetry-breaking categories: +61.44 pp on continuous symmetry (70.31% vs 8.37%) and +49.12 pp on discrete symmetry (70.41% vs 21.29%), while still improving by +19.05 pp on asymmetric categories (86.24% vs 67.19%). Against VN-Transformer, Ada-VNT gains +20.92 pp on continuous symmetry and +4.39 pp on discrete symmetry, but is roughly on par on asymmetric categories (85.77% vs 86.10%, −0.33 pp). Against the frame-averaging equivariant EPN, Ada-VNN wins on continuous symmetry (70.31% vs 43.92%) and asymmetry (86.24% vs 73.71%) but is slightly behind on discrete symmetry (70.41% vs 72.58%). Non-equivariant baselines (PointNet++, PCT) fail almost entirely on symmetry-breaking categories (near 0% recall) and are outperformed by 40–80 pp across all regimes. On ModelNet40 classification, Ada-VNN variants show small accuracy drops (1–3 pp) relative to their strictly equivariant counterparts but maintain invariance errors an order of magnitude lower than non-equivariant baselines (e.g., Ada-VNT: 0.0085 vs PCT: 0.82).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Vector Neuron Network (VNN) and VN-Transformer architectures by introducing an Adaptive Gating Unit (AGU) and a residual correction pathway, transforming their hard SO(3)-equivariance constraints into data-driven soft priors. It builds conceptually on Residual Pathway Priors (RPP) for the residual structure and on the broader approximately-equivariant networks literature, but departs from prior relaxed-equivariance work by making the relaxation degree explicitly symmetry-aware rather than fixed or uniformly small. The closest prior work, OAVNN, addressed symmetry breaking in VNNs but was limited to mirror symmetry and segmentation; this paper generalizes to full SO(3) symmetry breaking across pose estimation, molecular isotope prediction, and classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of representation dimensional collapse as the mechanistic reason strictly equivariant models fail on approximately symmetric objects provides a clear diagnostic for a long-standing practical limitation, and the adaptive fix is nearly free in compute (<0.2% memory overhead). Open questions include whether the adaptive mechanism generalizes beyond SO(3) to higher-order or non-compact groups, whether the theoretical bound (Eq. 8) tightens with deeper architectures, and how the approach behaves when symmetry breaking is adversarial rather than natural. The modest performance drop on asymmetric categories for Ada-VNT (−0.33 pp) suggests the adaptive branch introduces a small inductive-bias cost even when symmetry is absent, a trade-off worth monitoring at scale.
