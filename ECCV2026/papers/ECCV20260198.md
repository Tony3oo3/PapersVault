## Problem

Sim-to-real pipelines for deformable object manipulation remain rooted in rigid-body abstractions, producing geometrically mismatched scenes, unstable soft-body dynamics, and motion primitives ill-suited for cloth interaction. The paper argues that simulation fails not for being synthetic but for being ungrounded: without metric-accurate scene alignment and physically consistent soft-body dynamics, synthetic data cannot serve as a reliable substrate for zero-shot real-robot deployment.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes high-fidelity 3D scans of garments (captured on a mannequin via RGB + LiDAR fusion), a manufacturer-provided URDF for the ARX ACONE dual-arm robot, 200 teleoperated simulation demonstrations as motion seeds, and environment assets (tables, rooms). It produces a synthetic manipulation dataset in LeRobot format pairing photorealistic RGB renderings (via Blender cycle path tracing with 17 table materials, 28 cloth textures, and 90 randomized environment combinations) with synchronized robot joint states and end-effector actions, ready for imitation-learning training of VLA policies.

## Method

SIM1 is a three-stage real-to-sim-to-real pipeline. First, garments are digitized via professional 3D scanning, mannequin segmentation, Poisson surface reconstruction, and mesh post-processing to yield sub-millimeter textured OBJ assets, while the robot is imported from CAD-derived URDF. Second, a deformation-stable solver extends Newton-VBD with explicit strain constraints: when any mesh edge exceeds a stretch threshold (1+ξ)l₀, a virtual elastic penalty energy is injected into the vertex optimization, progressively ramping stiffness to suppress excessive stretching during real-time rigid-soft contact; physical parameters (density, Young's modulus, friction, etc.) are calibrated through bidirectional teleoperation where simulated and real executions are visually compared. Third, trajectories are synthesized by decoupling interaction from motion: grasp configurations are pooled and shuffled from expert demos, while inter-grasp transitions are generated via conditional diffusion forcing (a transformer reconstructing trajectories from partially corrupted tokens conditioned on boundary poses and history), followed by state-based filtering, a ResNet-18 + Transformer video discriminator for validity scoring, and appearance randomization to produce scaled training data.

## Results

Policies trained purely on SIM1 synthetic data achieve 90% zero-shot success (π₀.₅) and 76% (π₀) on real-robot T-shirt folding, matching or exceeding real-data baselines. Under out-of-domain shifts, simulation-trained policies outperform real-data baselines by +50% (spatial), +13% (texture), and +47% (lighting). In a from-scratch setting (no pretrained manipulation priors), real-data training yields 0% success while synthetic training reaches 76%, confirming gains originate from generated data. The data-scaling analysis shows 15 synthetic samples provide training value equivalent to 1 real demonstration in-domain, and ~5:1 in texture generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between policies trained on real demonstrations (1,000 trajectories via kinesthetic teaching) versus those trained exclusively on SIM1 synthetic data. Under equal data budgets (200 samples), real-data π₀.₅ reaches 97% in-domain success while sim-teleoperated data achieves 87%, a 10% gap that closes and reverses when synthetic data is scaled to 10k samples. Under distribution shifts, simulation-trained policies consistently outperform real-data baselines: +50% on spatial shifts, +13% on texture, and +47% on lighting, demonstrating that synthetic diversity provides broader coverage than limited real demonstrations. In the from-scratch ablation (no pretrained manipulation knowledge), real-data training completely fails (0%) while synthetic training achieves 76%, ruling out pretrained priors as the source of performance. On an unseen polo shirt (extreme domain shift), the real-data baseline reaches only 20% success while the SIM1-trained policy achieves 70%. The π₀.₅ model outperforms π₀ under fixed budgets, attributed to richer pretraining and a more expressive state representation rather than fundamental task limitations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIM1 extends the real-to-sim-to-real (R2S2R) paradigm previously applied to rigid manipulation (Torne et al., 2024) into the deformable-object regime, where existing sim-to-real pipelines (MimicGen, Genie Sim, RoboCasa) break down due to rigid-body assumptions in geometry, dynamics, and motion primitives. On the solver side, it builds on the Augmented Vertex Block Descent formulation (Giles et al., 2025) but repurposes it for online rigid-soft coupling under real-time teleoperation rather than offline cloth animation. For trajectory synthesis, it adapts diffusion forcing (Chen et al., 2024) from general sequence modeling to structured deformable manipulation by decoupling interaction segments (reused from expert demos) from inter-segment motion (generated by diffusion), departing from the naive trajectory slicing that works for rigid pick-and-place but fails under state-dependent contact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1:15 synthetic-to-real data equivalence ratio and 90% zero-shot success rate establish that physics-aligned simulation can serve as a scalable supervision source for deformable manipulation, reducing the prohibitive cost of real-world data collection. However, the pipeline's practical adoption is constrained by the requirement for expert-guided per-asset material calibration, which limits full automation across arbitrary cloth types. Open questions include whether the calibration step can be learned or automated, whether the approach generalizes beyond garment folding to other deformable tasks (e.g., ropes, food), and whether the solver's strain-constraint mechanism transfers to other soft-body materials with very different elastic properties.
