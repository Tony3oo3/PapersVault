## Problem

Feed-forward 3D Gaussian reconstruction methods predict pixel-aligned Gaussians uniformly across the image, producing highly redundant representations. Naive pruning of these Gaussians introduces severe artifacts such as black patches and over-smoothing, and typically requires per-scene finetuning that breaks the feed-forward paradigm. The paper asks whether controllable 3D Gaussian density can be achieved in feed-forward models without sacrificing reconstruction fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261100/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images (2 views for sparse-view settings, 6/9/16/32 views for dense-view settings) along with a user-specified pruning budget β ∈ [0, 0.8], and produces a compact set of B = ⌊(1−β)N⌋ 3D Gaussians with re-predicted attributes (scale, rotation, opacity, spherical harmonics) that can be rasterized to novel views. It operates as a plug-in on top of existing feed-forward backbones (MASt3R, VGGT, MVSplat), requiring no architectural modifications to the base model. The MASt3R/VGGT variants do not require camera poses as input, while the MVSplat variant does.

## Method

AdaptiveSplat introduces three components: (1) a Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) applied to input images to estimate local texture energy, (2) texture-aware selective pruning that partitions the aggregated point cloud into K=300 SuperClusters via K-means in (x,y,z,r,g,b) space, ranks them by mean texture energy, and retains only γ=10% of Gaussians in low-energy clusters while fully preserving high-energy ones, and (3) an Adaptive Gaussian Head built on DPT that fuses ViT features with binary retention masks to re-predict the scale, rotation, opacity, and SH coefficients of surviving Gaussians. The key insight is that low-texture regions can be represented by fewer but spatially broader Gaussians, while high-texture regions retain their full Gaussian density. Training uses a uniform sampling of β ~ U[0,1) to ensure robustness across budgets, with a combined L2 + LPIPS photometric loss on rendered novel views. This design enables flexible Gaussian budget control entirely within the feed-forward pipeline without test-time optimization.

## Results

On ACID (NoPoSplat backbone, β=0.4), AdaptiveSplat achieves 22.55 dB PSNR, 0.299 LPIPS, and 0.640 SSIM, outperforming the best pruning baseline (LightGaussian at 19.79 dB PSNR) by ~2.8 dB. On RE10K under the same setting, it reaches 22.29 dB PSNR versus 16.60 dB for LightGaussian, a ~5.7 dB gap. On DL3DV with 6 views and β=0.4, AdaptiveSplat attains 20.45 dB PSNR compared to 11.36 dB for LightGaussian, a ~9 dB margin. Performance degrades gracefully at higher pruning (β=0.8), maintaining 21.06 dB on ACID and 20.74 dB on RE10K while baselines drop to 18.0–19.5 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaptiveSplat decisively outperforms all four pruning baselines (EAGLES, LightGaussian, PUP-3DGS, and EfficientGS) across every dataset and pruning ratio tested. On ACID at β=0.4, it surpasses the strongest baseline (LightGaussian, 19.79 dB) by 2.76 dB in PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.604 to 0.299. On RE10K the margin is even larger: 22.29 dB versus 16.60 dB for LightGaussian (~5.7 dB gap). On DL3DV with 6 views, the advantage is most dramatic—20.45 dB versus 11.36 dB for LightGaussian (~9 dB). At extreme pruning (β=0.8), baselines degrade substantially (e.g., PUP-3DGS drops to 18.00 dB on ACID) while AdaptiveSplat retains 21.06 dB, confirming that texture-aware allocation and the adaptive Gaussian head are critical for maintaining fidelity under aggressive sparsification. EfficientGS, included only in Table 1, performs worst among baselines (9.17 dB on ACID at β=0.4), highlighting the inadequacy of per-pixel Top-K selection without scene-complexity awareness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on feed-forward 3D Gaussian reconstruction backbones (MASt3R, VGGT, MVSplat) that predict pixel-aligned Gaussians, and departs from prior 3DGS compression work (EAGLES, LightGaussian, PUP-3DGS, EfficientGS) that either requires per-scene optimization or uses geometry-agnostic heuristics. The key conceptual departure is grounding pruning decisions in natural image statistics (power-law PSD decay) via DWT-based texture energy, combined with an adaptive Gaussian head that re-predicts attributes of retained primitives within the feed-forward pipeline—something prior compression methods cannot do without breaking the feed-forward assumption. The method is designed as a plug-in module that integrates into existing feed-forward architectures without architectural modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides a practical path toward real-time, memory-efficient 3D reconstruction with a user-controllable quality-efficiency knob, which is important for applications like AR/VR and interactive scene editing where fixed Gaussian budgets are impractical. The paper acknowledges limitations on smooth surfaces with high-frequency texture (e.g., intricate wall patterns) and specular highlights, where pixel-aligned feed-forward models inherently struggle. An open question is whether incorporating geometry-aware signals (surface curvature, normal coherence) alongside texture could further improve pruning decisions, and whether the approach extends to dynamic or 4D Gaussian representations.
