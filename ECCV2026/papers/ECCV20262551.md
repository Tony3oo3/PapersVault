## Problem

Latent world models roll observations forward accurately on factual transitions, but their predictive latents are entangled and weakly aligned with underlying causal factors, causing them to degrade under interventions and counterfactual queries. Neither standard world models nor object-centric models explicitly enforce a directed acyclic graph (DAG) causal structure on latent factors, so latent interventions are not identifiable and counterfactual rollouts can deviate from physically valid trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262551/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB visual observations and discrete actions, encoding them into object-centric latents of shape K×d (K slots, per-slot dimension d). Counterfactual queries are specified by intervention metadata (i, j, Δ) that implements a do-operation on a specific state variable, and the model produces both factual next-latent predictions and counterfactual future latents/observations. Evaluation uses retrieval metrics (H@1, MRR, CF-H@1, CF-MRR) over paired factual and counterfactual candidate pools at 1/5/10-step horizons.

## Method

The paper introduces a plug-in CausalVAE structural branch that attaches to any pretrained encoder-transition backbone without altering its architecture, organizing latents through a learned DAG adjacency matrix A enforced by a differentiable acyclicity penalty tr(exp(A⊙A))−d. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: (1) backbone pretraining with one-step supervision, (2) causal branch training with the backbone frozen, optimizing reconstruction, KL, alignment-to-simulator-state, and DAG losses, and (3) transition refinement using an exponentially decayed alpha-gated fusion that blends the raw encoder latent with the causally regularized latent. The alignment loss L_align = ‖g_align(z̃_t) − s_t‖² serves as weak supervision to anchor latent coordinates, enabling an identifiability theorem (Theorem 1) showing the learned adjacency recovers the ground-truth DAG in the anchored coordinate frame. Across eight backbones and four benchmarks, the plug-in largely preserves factual retrieval while improving counterfactual H@1 by up to +30 points (e.g., GNN_NLL on Physics: 11.0→41.0), with gains concentrated where baseline dynamics are most intervention-fragile.

## Results

On the Physics 3-body benchmark, the CausalVAE plug-in yields the largest counterfactual gains: GNN_NLL CF-H@1 rises from 11.0 to 41.0 (+30.0) and AE_NLL from 10.5 to 41.0 (+30.5), while factual 1-step H@1 is preserved (98.0→99.0 for GNN_NLL). On 2D Shapes, GNN_NLL CF-H@1 improves from 33.0 to 54.75 (+21.75); on Chemistry, Modular_Contrastive gains +14.44 CF-H@1 (13.05→27.49). Across all four benchmarks and eight backbones, factual retrieval (H@1/MRR at 1/5/10 steps) is largely maintained or slightly improved, confirming the plug-in does not trade away predictive accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the C-SWM (Modular) backbone [Kipf et al., ICLR 2020], the plug-in gains +2.5 to +14.44 CF-H@1 on Physics, Chemistry, and 3D Cubes, though Modular_Contrastive on 2D Shapes drops (13.85→8.55) due to an already-weak baseline. Against the GNN/NRI backbone [Kipf et al., ICML 2018], gains are largest on Physics (GNN_NLL: +30.0 CF-H@1) and 2D Shapes (GNN_NLL: +21.75), but 3D Cubes GNN variants show small or negative changes. Against the VAE/World-Models backbone [Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018], the plug-in improves CF-H@1 by +9 to +30 on Physics and Chemistry, while 2D/3D results are mixed. Against the three published counterfactual-dynamics methods (CWM [Li et al., 2020], Causal-JEPA [Nam et al., 2026], and CFWM [Venkatesh et al., ECCV 2024]), the paper reports in supplementary material that its ~1M-parameter plug-in on GNN_Contrastive attains the best step-1 CF-H@1 and CF-MRR on Physics-3body, ahead of substantially larger models. Overall, the plug-in wins on the majority of backbone-benchmark pairs for CF-H@1 (22 of 32 cells show positive Δ), with the most consistent losses occurring on Chemistry AE backbones where the 5-dimensional latent is too narrow to carry the 3125 discrete color configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the CausalVAE causal layer (Yang et al., CVPR 2021)—originally designed for static i.i.d. observations with supervised attribute labels—as a plug-in structural module for sequential, action-conditioned latent world models. It replaces CausalVAE's attribute supervision with a lightweight alignment loss to simulator states and introduces a three-stage training schedule (backbone pretraining → causal branch training → transition refinement with alpha-gated fusion) to stabilize the sequential setting. The DAG acyclicity penalty is borrowed directly from Zheng et al. (NeurIPS 2018), and the base encoder-transition pipelines follow the C-SWM (Kipf et al., ICLR 2020) and NRI/GNS graph-dynamics formulations. The key departure from prior counterfactual-dynamics methods (CFWM, Causal-JEPA, Venkatesh et al.) is that interventions are routed through a learned DAG adjacency matrix rather than through masking or prompting heuristics, enabling genuinely novel do-interventions rather than recombination of seen factor combinations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in design offers a practical, low-cost path to adding causal structure to existing world-model backbones without architectural redesign, with only a 1.17× parameter overhead at training time and no inference cost. However, the paper is candid that gains are backbone- and benchmark-dependent rather than universal: the largest improvements concentrate on Physics (CF-H@1 +30 for GNN_NLL), while several Chemistry and 3D Cubes cells show degradation, revealing a capacity bottleneck when host latents are too narrow to carry discrete causal factors. Open questions include whether the approach extends to strongly nonlinear regimes, long-horizon drift, and larger JEPA-style architectures, and whether the alignment-anchored identifiability (which requires population-level optimality and local invertibility) holds under realistic finite-sample training. The learned DAG's recovery of first-order physical interaction trends is encouraging for interpretability but remains a local linearization, not a full nonlinear law.
