## Problem

Existing fine-grained cross-view localization datasets are constrained by high-end sensor rigs (limiting camera diversity and geographic coverage), rely on restrictive-licensed imagery (e.g., Google Maps/Street View), and lack reliable evaluation data for in-the-wild conditions. It remains unclear whether noisy crowd-sourced imagery can benefit fine-grained CVL training or serve as a valid evaluation benchmark when combined with accurate but less diverse data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a ground-level perspective image (from heterogeneous cameras: vehicle-mounted, handheld, bicycle-mounted, dash cameras) and a north-aligned geo-referenced aerial image covering a 100 m × 100 m area at 0.04–0.16 m/pixel resolution, and outputs the 3-DoF camera pose (2D planar position and yaw orientation). The ground camera is randomly offset up to ±20 m from the aerial image center. The pipeline additionally consumes predicted metric depth maps (from DepthAnythingV2, refined with LiDAR for ZOD images) and camera intrinsics to lift 2D correspondences into bird's-eye-view space for geometric alignment.

## Method

The core contribution is a data curation framework that constructs accurate ground-truth poses for in-the-wild Mapillary images by matching them to nearby ZOD images via MAST3R local features with reciprocal matching and geometric verification, then estimating the Mapillary camera pose through 2D-3D correspondences to globally registered ZOD LiDAR point clouds using PnP initialization followed by Levenberg-Marquardt refinement (via COLMAP), with automatic quality filtering by reprojection error and manual verification. For training, the paper scales the state-of-the-art Loc² model (depth-lifted local feature matching + scale-aware Procrustes alignment) to the full OpenCVL dataset by resizing images to a fixed resolution, adjusting intrinsics, and applying a global loss downweighting factor of 0.3 to Mapillary samples to account for pose noise. Experiments demonstrate that incorporating noisy but diverse in-the-wild data consistently improves localization accuracy on clean test sets, and that OpenCVL pre-training followed by KITTI fine-tuning yields the best cross-dataset transfer performance.

## Results

On the OpenCVL cross-area test set, Loc² trained on the full OpenCVL dataset (ZOD + downweighted Mapillary) achieves 6.72 m mean localization error and 11.52° mean orientation error, substantially outperforming the KITTI-trained model (15.57 m / 79.75° without orientation prior). On the in-the-wild test set, the full OpenCVL model reaches 7.90 m mean localization error, yet orientation error remains high at 26.07°, indicating substantial room for improvement in unconstrained real-world settings. Training with noisy Mapillary data consistently improves localization on all three test splits (cross-area, snowy, in-the-wild) compared to ZOD-only training, with the largest gains on the in-the-wild set (8.61 m → 7.90 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is across training configurations of the state-of-the-art Loc² method rather than across different architectures. Training on the full OpenCVL dataset (ZOD + downweighted Mapillary) outperforms ZOD-only training on all three test splits, with the most notable gain on the in-the-wild set (7.90 m vs. 8.61 m mean localization) and the snowy set (7.80 m vs. 8.04 m). The KITTI-trained model is dramatically worse on OpenCVL (15.57 m vs. 6.72 m cross-area), confirming that narrow-distribution training fails to generalize. Secondary baselines HC-Net and CCVPE are evaluated in the appendix but are not the focus of the main results. The key finding is that noisy in-the-wild Mapillary data, when downweighted (factor 0.3), improves clean-test performance rather than degrading it, while the in-the-wild test set (26.07° mean orientation error) reveals that current methods still struggle significantly with unconstrained viewpoints and camera types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the fine-grained cross-view localization (CVL) dataset landscape by addressing two limitations of prior benchmarks (VIGOR, KITTI-based, FordAV, RobotCar): restricted data licensing (Google Maps/Street View) and narrow collection diversity (single vehicle-mounted camera, limited geography). Rather than proposing a new CVL method, it repurposes existing tools—MAST3R feature matching, PnP, and Levenberg-Marquardt optimization within COLMAP—into a pose-correction framework that makes noisy crowd-sourced Mapillary imagery usable for reliable evaluation. The empirical evaluation builds on Loc² as the primary baseline, with HC-Net and CCVPE as secondary comparisons, positioning the dataset as a testbed for the feature-matching + geometric-optimization method family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OpenCVL is the first fully open (permissive-license) large-scale dataset for fine-grained CVL, covering 41 cities across four European countries and 617K ground-aerial pairs, which removes the long-term accessibility and reproducibility barriers imposed by prior benchmarks. The consistent finding that noisy in-the-wild training data improves performance on clean ZOD-derived test sets (with a 0.3 loss downweighting factor) provides a concrete recipe for scaling CVL beyond curated sensor rigs. However, the in-the-wild test set still yields ~26° mean orientation error, and cross-dataset transfer to KITTI reveals persistent 180° direction-ambiguity issues, indicating that robust unconstrained CVL remains substantially unsolved. Open questions include whether the pose-correction framework generalizes beyond areas with ZOD LiDAR overlap, and how methods should handle the extreme viewpoint and camera variability that in-the-wild data introduces.
