## Problem

Existing gaze decoding methods frame the task as classification over a fixed set of predefined categories, which is inherently constrained: small label sets yield overly coarse outputs, large ones demand extensive annotation, and no fixed taxonomy can capture the open-ended richness of natural language. This paper introduces gaze-to-text generation as a generative learning problem, where the model must produce free-form descriptions of human goals that range from simple object labels to context-rich referring expressions and questions.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a gaze scanpath (a sequence of N fixations, each with normalized [0,1] spatial coordinates and duration in milliseconds) as input. The scanpath is not fed as a separate modality but is textualized into a language instruction via a prompt template (e.g., 'Given scanpath [(0.4,0.4,20),...], decode the behavior type and stimulus'). The output is a hierarchical natural language response parsed into a coarse behavior type (Target-Present/Absent Search, Object Referral, or VQA) and a fine-level stimulus (a category label, a referring expression, or a question).

## Method

Gazette extends LLaVA-1.5-7B (CLIP Vision Encoder + LLM + MLP projection) and is fine-tuned via LoRA on two instruction-tuning tasks: the primary GazeDec task mapping (image, scanpath) to a goal description, and an auxiliary ThinkAloud task that generates attention allocation strategy narratives. The key insight is that standard instruction tuning fails because observed scanpaths are confounded by participant-specific variability, making the mapping from (I, S) to D an ill-posed inverse problem for a single observer. To resolve this, GPT-4 is prompted with multiple participants' scanpaths sharing the same image and goal to extract the common top-down attention allocation pattern, producing think-aloud transcripts structured into three idea units: an attention allocation explanation, a target localization (bounding box or null), and a scanpath length count. These transcripts serve as pseudo-annotations for the auxiliary task, teaching the model to factor out individual idiosyncrasies and focus on goal-specific spatiotemporal dynamics. At inference, only a single scanpath is required, and the model decodes the goal via greedy autoregressive generation.

## Results

On COCO-Search18 Target-Present Visual Search, Gazette (w/o ThinkAloud) achieves 0.742 accuracy, approaching the SOTA Mondal et al. (0.776) while substantially outperforming GST (0.544) and GazeGNN (0.335). On Target-Absent Visual Search, Gazette achieves 0.445 accuracy, surpassing all baselines including Mondal et al. (0.387) and GST (0.385). For generative Object Referral decoding on RefCOCO-Gaze, Gazette with ThinkAloud reaches BLEU-1 of 0.519, exceeding the RefCOCO Inter-Annotator Consistency ceiling (0.500), and for VQA on AiR-D it achieves BLEU-1 of 0.364 with CIDEr of 0.367, both significantly above the LLaVA-1.5 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gazette is compared against four external baselines on COCO-Search18: Mondal et al. (gaze-language alignment, ICCV 2025), GST (gaze scanpath transformer, CVPR 2024), GazeGNN (gaze-guided GNN, WACV 2024), and LLaVA-1.5/LLaVA-last (frozen or fine-tuned MLLM baselines). On Target-Present search, Gazette (0.742 acc) trails Mondal et al. (0.776) by 3.4 points but leads GST (0.544) by 19.8 points and GazeGNN (0.335) by 40.7 points. On Target-Absent search, Gazette (0.445 acc) clearly wins over all baselines, beating Mondal et al. (0.387) by 5.8 points and GST (0.385) by 6.0 points. For generative Object Referral and VQA decoding, Gazette with ThinkAloud outperforms LLaVA-1.5 by large margins (e.g., BLEU-1: 0.519 vs. 0.066 on Object Referral) and LLaVA-last (0.170), with the ThinkAloud auxiliary task contributing 8–40% relative gains over the w/o ThinkAloud variant across all lexical overlap metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of gaze decoding work (Sattar et al., GST, GazeGNN, Mondal et al.) that frames the problem as classification over a fixed category set, instead formulating it as an open-vocabulary, generative text-generation task. Architecturally, it builds directly on LLaVA-1.5-7B and the broader MLLM instruction-tuning paradigm (LLaVA, VIGC), but introduces a novel auxiliary training signal—think-aloud transcripts generated by GPT-4 from multi-participant scanpath commonalities—that has no direct precedent in prior gaze or MLLM literature. The conceptual bridge to classic attention theory (Najemnik & Geisler's ideal observer, Hayhoe & Ballard's cognitive relevance) provides a principled justification for why cross-observer invariance can be exploited, distinguishing the approach from purely empirical MLLM fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By moving gaze decoding from closed-set classification to free-form natural language generation, the paper opens the door to applications (e-commerce intent, educational focus, AR navigation) where a fixed taxonomy is insufficient. The think-aloud transcript strategy offers a general recipe for injecting domain-specific reasoning into MLLM fine-tuning when supervision is confounded by individual variability, potentially transferable to other weakly-supervised multimodal tasks. Key limitations remain: efficacy degrades when inter-observer agreement is low (Target-Absent search), GPT-4-generated pseudo-annotations inherit the model's priors rather than reflecting true participant cognition, and evaluation via LLM-as-a-Judge introduces a circular dependency with the same model used for training-data synthesis. Whether the approach scales to more diverse tasks, larger model families, or truly open-world gaze scenarios remains open.
