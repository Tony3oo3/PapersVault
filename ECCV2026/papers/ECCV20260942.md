## Problem

Existing VLM-based autonomous driving approaches treat perception, prediction, and planning as separate modules, often routing information through text-only intermediates that introduce an information bottleneck and compound errors. Meanwhile, generative world models can produce visually plausible future frames but lack explicit state estimation, structured scene representations, and a differentiable bridge to the action space. The paper addresses the absence of a single architecture that jointly performs scene understanding, trajectory planning, and trajectory-conditioned future image generation with bidirectional information flow between reasoning, action, and generation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view camera images (resized to 640×640), a 16-frame temporal history stored in a memory bank, perception features (object boxes, lanes, traffic states), a navigation command, and an optional language instruction. It produces continuous future trajectory waypoints, a future front-view image (192×128 for the discrete AR branch, 512×1024 for the AR+Diffusion branch), and VQA answers. Unusually, the predicted planning token is placed immediately before the image tokens in the autoregressive sequence, so every generated visual token is explicitly conditioned on the planned trajectory rather than on a separate conditioning signal.

## Method

UniDrive-WM builds on the ORION VLM (QT-Former encoder + Vicuna 1.5 LLM fine-tuned with LoRA) and adds a trajectory planner that models a multi-modal distribution over waypoints via a Gaussian latent variable z_a, providing a differentiable bridge between the VLM's reasoning embeddings and the numeric action space. For future image generation, two complementary decoding paradigms are instantiated: (i) a discrete autoregressive pathway that enlarges the VLM codebook and detokenizes with MoVQGAN, and (ii) an AR+Diffusion pathway where a learnable query token aggregates VLM context, a flow-matching objective predicts continuous latent features, and a frozen-encoder diffusion decoder reconstructs high-resolution pixels. The planning token precedes the image tokens in the sequence, enforcing planning-conditioned generation and enabling gradient flow from the image loss back into the planner. Training uses a two-stage supervised strategy (joint planning+generation, then adding VQA) with a combined objective of cross-entropy on visual tokens, collision/boundary/MSE planning losses, flow-matching loss, and CLIP-based semantic alignment.

## Results

On the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, UniDrive-WM (AR+Diffusion) achieves a Driving Score of 79.31 and Success Rate of 56.42%, outperforming the previous best method ORION (77.74 DS, 54.62% SR) and DriveTransformer-Large (63.46 DS, 35.01% SR). In open-loop evaluation, the AR+Diffusion variant reduces average L2 trajectory error to 0.589 m at 2 s (vs. 0.631 m for ORION) and collision rate to 0.426% at 2 s (vs. 0.467%), while also improving detection NDS from 0.723 to 0.755. On nuScenes, the method achieves 0.29 m average L2 error, surpassing ORION (0.34 m) and VAD-Base (1.25 m). For future image generation, UniDrive-WM attains FID scores of 6.6 (Bench2Drive) and 7.3 (nuScenes), beating FSDrive (9.3 and 10.1) and all prior driving world models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniDrive-WM outperforms its direct base model ORION [11] across all closed-loop metrics (DS 79.31 vs. 77.74, SR 56.42% vs. 54.62%, L2 0.63 vs. 0.68) and open-loop metrics (2 s L2 0.589 vs. 0.631 m, 2 s collision 0.426% vs. 0.467%, NDS 0.755 vs. 0.723), confirming that the added future-image-generation branch provides beneficial supervisory signals. Against the strongest non-VLM end-to-end baselines—DriveTransformer-Large [23] (DS 63.46, L2 0.62) and ThinkTwice [21] (DS 62.44, L2 0.95)—UniDrive-WM leads by a wide margin in Driving Score and Success Rate while matching or exceeding their L2 accuracy. On nuScenes, the method reduces average L2 error from 0.34 m (ORION) to 0.29 m and matches ORION's collision rate (0.31%), while substantially outperforming VAD-Base (1.25 m L2, 1.09% collision). In image generation, UniDrive-WM (AR+Diff) achieves FID 6.6 on Bench2Drive and 7.3 on nuScenes, surpassing FSDrive [55] (9.3 / 10.1), GEM [14] (13.2 / 10.5), and Drive-WM [46] (17.8 / 15.8). On the DriveLM GVQA benchmark, UniDrive-WM attains the highest Final Score (0.59) versus FSDrive (0.57) and OmniDrive (0.56), indicating that joint training with image generation also improves scene understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniDrive-WM builds directly on ORION, a VLM-based end-to-end driving framework, adopting its QT-Former encoder, LoRA-fine-tuned LLM backbone, and planning head design. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) world models for driving that predict future states, (2) unified understanding-and-generation VLMs (Chameleon, Show-o, Transfusion, Janus, BLIP-3o) that couple discrete or continuous visual token prediction with language reasoning, and (3) Vision-Language-Action models that map multimodal context to continuous actions. The key departure from prior work is the bidirectional coupling: rather than treating perception, planning, and visual prediction as sequential or parallel modules (as in ORION, VAD, or FSDrive), UniDrive-WM places the planning token before the image tokens so that future-frame generation is explicitly conditioned on the predicted trajectory, and the resulting generation loss back-propagates into the planner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that trajectory-conditioned future image generation is not merely a visualization byproduct but provides auxiliary supervisory signals that measurably improve planning accuracy (7.3% L2 reduction, 10.4% collision-rate reduction over ORION) and even VQA performance, suggesting a genuine bidirectional information flow between the generation and reasoning spaces. However, the AR+Diffusion branch runs at only 0.4 fps, limiting real-time deployment, and the diffusion decoder is trained with deterministic reconstruction rather than true probabilistic sampling, which constrains diversity. Open questions include whether the planning-conditioned generation advantage persists in more interactive, long-horizon, or multi-agent scenarios, and whether the discrete AR branch's semantic regularization can be further exploited for safety-critical closed-loop control without sacrificing fidelity.
