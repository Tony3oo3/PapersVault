## Problem

Rolling shutter cameras require both intrinsic parameters and the readout time ratio γ for accurate geometric 3D vision, yet no self-calibration method exists that estimates these from unconstrained video without calibration targets, LED panels, or IMUs. Existing RS calibration approaches all depend on dedicated hardware or patterns, leaving purely vision-based self-calibration of RS cameras as an open problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261782/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of rolling shutter images (50 consecutive frames in experiments) and an initial COLMAP reconstruction, then jointly refines camera intrinsics (fx, fy, cx, cy), the readout time ratio γ, per-row camera poses, and 3D point coordinates. Unlike GS self-calibration, the output includes γ — a temporal parameter that has no analogue in pinhole calibration — and the poses are row-dependent rather than single per-frame, reflecting the sequential exposure nature of RS sensors.

## Method

The core contribution is a self-calibrating bundle adjustment built on a dual-projection model (RSSC-DP) that simultaneously constrains each 3D point at two timestamps along a shared continuous B-spline trajectory on SE(3): its original row-dependent RS observation (RSSC-TE, using cumulative B-splines to assign a distinct pose per image row parameterized by γ) and its rectified GS counterpart (RSSC-CE, obtained by fitting 2D pixel trajectories with quadratic Lagrange or cubic Hermite interpolation to a unified reference timestamp). The two residuals are combined in a weighted least-squares objective, where the B-spline trajectory links the GS poses to the same control poses used for the RS row poses, enforcing geometric and temporal consistency across both projection instances. This dual constraint is non-trivial: the CE branch compensates for local trajectory irregularities while the TE branch provides global smoothness regularization, and the unified formulation improves optimization stability over either model alone.

## Results

On WHU-RSVI, the best proposed variant RSSC-DPQ achieves MAE(fx)=0.03, MAE(fy)=0.44, MAE(γ)=0.0483, and MATE=0.0101, while RSSC-TE achieves MAE(fx)=0.07, MAE(fy)=0.31, and MATE=0.0170. On TUM-RSVI, RSSC-DPQ reaches MAE(fx)=3.10, MAE(fy)=2.39, MAE(γ)=0.0814, and MATE=0.0055, and RSSC-TE reaches MAE(fx)=3.16, MAE(fy)=1.48, and MATE=0.0063. All proposed methods also estimate the readout time ratio γ, which none of the GS-based baselines can provide. In synthetic experiments, RSSC-DP consistently achieves the lowest errors across trajectory smoothness, readout ratio, and noise conditions, while RSSC-CE variants are fastest (0.23–0.28 s total optimization vs. 5.87 s for RSSC-TE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three baselines (COLMAP, DroidCalib, SelfSup-Calib) are GS-camera self-calibration methods that cannot estimate the readout time ratio γ, a fundamental limitation for RS imaging. On WHU-RSVI, RSSC-DPQ reduces MAE(fx) from 3.05 (COLMAP) and 0.56 (DroidCalib) to 0.03, and MAE(fy) from 11.26 and 22.33 to 0.44, while also achieving the lowest MATE (0.0101 vs. 0.0453 and 0.0317). SelfSup-Calib fails catastrophically on both datasets, with MAE(fx) reaching 195.98 (WHU) and 359.77 (TUM), and MATE of 0.4585 and 0.1432 respectively. On TUM-RSVI, where COLMAP and DroidCalib degrade substantially (MAE(fx) of 33.58 and 71.96), the proposed methods maintain MAE(fx) around 3.1–3.8 and MATE below 0.008, representing a 10–100× improvement in intrinsic accuracy and a 5–25× improvement in trajectory accuracy. Among the proposed variants, RSSC-TE and RSSC-DPQ are consistently the most accurate, while RSSC-CEQ offers the best trade-off between speed (0.23 s total) and accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-time B-spline trajectory representation on SE(3) from Lovegrove et al. (BMVC 2013) and the row-wise pose formulation from Oth et al. (CVPR 2013) into a target-free self-calibration setting, removing the dependency on calibration boards or LED panels that all prior RS calibration methods required. It also adapts the quadratic polynomial correction-field fitting from Qu et al. (ICCV 2023) as a second, complementary RS imaging model. The key conceptual departure is the dual-projection formulation (RSSC-DP), which simultaneously constrains each 3D point at both its row-dependent RS timestamp and a reference GS timestamp along a shared B-spline trajectory — a combination not present in either prior line of work. In the broader self-calibration landscape, GS camera self-calibration is mature (classical, deep-learning, and hybrid methods), but the authors explicitly note that no self-calibration method existed for RS cameras prior to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RS cameras dominate consumer imaging, yet the readout time ratio γ — a critical parameter for any geometric 3D vision pipeline — has previously been unobtainable without dedicated hardware or calibration targets. This work removes that bottleneck, enabling RS-aware SLAM, SfM, and 3D reconstruction in unconstrained environments. Open questions remain: the paper does not analyze degeneracies specific to RS self-calibration (e.g., motions that make γ unobservable), and the method still requires a COLMAP initial reconstruction, limiting its applicability to very short or degenerate sequences. The authors also flag joint optimization with dense 3D representations (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting) as a natural next step.
