## Problem

Existing open-world learning paradigms (OSR, NCD, GCD) assume flat, balanced label spaces, but real biomedical imaging exhibits long-tailed rare diseases, subtle lesions overwhelmed by dominant normal anatomy, and hierarchical diagnostic taxonomies. This mismatch means methods validated on natural-image GCD fail to translate to clinical settings, where dominant known patterns form a visual manifold that actively suppresses the weak cues needed to discover novel concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261466/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB medical images (endoscopy, H&E histopathology, peripheral blood microscopy, dermatoscopy) processed by a ViT-B/16 backbone initialized with DINOv2 weights, producing a class token and N patch tokens. SCAN operates as a plug-and-play layer on these tokens, outputting a refined class token that feeds into an unchanged GCD training pipeline. The unlabeled evaluation set contains a mixture of known and novel classes, and the final output is a per-sample class assignment (known or one of K_novel discovered categories) scored via Hungarian-matched clustering accuracy.

## Method

SCAN is a three-stage cognition-inspired plug-in that mitigates dominant-feature suppression without setting-specific supervision. Stage I (Predictive Suppression) routes patch tokens into E learnable evidence slots to form a sample-conditioned dominant anchor, then geometrically projects each patch token orthogonal to this anchor to isolate residual signals. Stage II (Surprise-Evoked Salience) converts residual norms into a within-image softmax salience distribution, re-aggregates residuals weighted by both slot assignment and surprise to produce 'excited evidence,' and broadcasts this back to refine patch tokens. Stage III (Complementary Accommodation) maps excited evidence to a novelty vector via first- and second-order readouts, projects it orthogonal to the dominant anchor to ensure complementarity, and injects it into the class token through a non-parametric gate that combines surprise magnitude, knownness (alignment with dominant evidence), and salience concentration (entropy-based peakedness), all standardized via EMA moments.

## Results

On the GBD Setting I (Long-tail Rare Disease Discovery) benchmark across four biomedical imaging domains, SCAN as a plug-in improves the average All accuracy of all three host baselines: SelEx gains +10.5% (55.5→66.0%), SimGCD gains +3.8% (48.6→52.5%), and LegoGCD gains +1.1% (52.3→53.4%). The largest single-dataset gain is SelEx+SCAN on Gastrovision Old accuracy, rising from 52.0% to 83.4% (+31.4%). On Setting II (Normal-to-Abnormal), SelEx+SCAN achieves +14.2% average All and +8.8% average New over SelEx; on Setting III (Within-Taxonomy), SelEx+SCAN reaches 66.0% average All (+12.3% over SelEx). SCAN adds only ~7.6M parameters and ~8% training / ~6% inference overhead over the host model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SelEx (the strongest directly comparable baseline), SCAN delivers the largest gains: +10.5% average All and +15.3% average Old on Setting I, with the standout being Gastrovision Old jumping from 52.0% to 83.4%. On Setting II, SelEx+SCAN improves average All by +14.2% and average New (abnormal) by +8.8%, and on Setting III it raises average All from 53.7% to 66.0%. Against SimGCD, SCAN yields more modest but consistent improvements (+3.8% avg All on Setting I, +0.4% on Setting II), with a small regression on HistoSet Old (−1.7%). Against LegoGCD, gains are smallest (+1.1% avg All on Setting I), and a minor regression appears on MLL23 New (−0.9%). SEAL†, which uses additional hierarchical labels and is treated as an auxiliary reference rather than a fair baseline, is exceeded by SelEx+SCAN on several metrics (e.g., MLL23 All 80.7% vs. 65.4%, HistoSet Old 99.9% vs. 87.1%), suggesting SCAN recovers part of the hierarchy-aware benefit without explicit taxonomic supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) paradigm—where known and novel classes are jointly handled in mixed unlabeled data—into a biomedical-specific formulation (GBD) that explicitly models long-tail class imbalance, normal-anatomy dominance, and hierarchical taxonomies, none of which prior GCD benchmarks capture. Methodologically, SCAN repurposes three cognitive-science principles (predictive coding, Bayesian surprise, complementary learning systems) that have been explored in isolation for visual processing into a unified plug-and-play token-refinement layer, rather than proposing a new training objective or architecture. It sits alongside recent medical GCD efforts (MedGCD, neighbor-guided frameworks) but departs from them by targeting the visual suppression mechanism directly rather than relying on contrastive or clustering refinements alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GBD provides the first unified, clinically grounded benchmark that forces discovery methods to confront the three structural realities of medical imaging simultaneously, filling a gap where prior evaluations assumed flat, balanced label spaces. SCAN's plug-and-play design (≈7.6 M extra parameters, ~8% training overhead) makes it immediately attachable to existing GCD pipelines, and the +10–14% average gains on SelEx across settings suggest dominant-feature suppression is a real and addressable bottleneck. However, gains are inconsistent across baselines (SimGCD and LegoGCD show only marginal improvements) and several New-subset accuracies regress, leaving open whether the cognitive framing is essential or whether a simpler residual-reweighting would suffice. Future work should test SCAN on 3D volumetric data, with per-domain tuning of the evidence-slot count E, and against text-supervised multimodal baselines that the paper explicitly excludes.
