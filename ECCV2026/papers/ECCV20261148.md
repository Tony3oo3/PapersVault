## Problem

Existing tuning-free diffusion morphing methods (e.g., FreeMorph) implicitly assume that source and target endpoints are approximately spatially aligned, an assumption that breaks down under large layout discrepancies. This leads to distorted latent trajectories during interpolation, producing ghosting artifacts, translucent overlays, and temporal incoherence in intermediate frames. The paper addresses the gap of achieving explicit, diffusion-compatible geometric alignment without per-pair optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 768×768 RGB images (source and target) and produces N=7 intermediate morphing frames at the same resolution, operating entirely within the Stable Diffusion v2.1 latent space (C×H_z×W_z). No text prompts or per-pair tuning are required; the entire pipeline runs in ~115 seconds on a single RTX 3090 (25s for offline correspondence estimation + 90s for 40-step DDIM denoising). The output is a temporally ordered sequence of denoised images that smoothly interpolate between the two endpoints.

## Method

AlignMorph follows a transport-then-denoise paradigm that explicitly decouples geometric alignment from generative refinement. In Stage I (Global Semantic Transport), dense semantic features are extracted via DINOv2+FeatUp, and entropic optimal transport (log-domain Sinkhorn, ε=0.1) computes a soft correspondence plan between source and target tokens; barycentric projection converts this into a deterministic warp field, and a forward-backward cycle-consistency score yields per-pixel reliability weights. The warp is then applied to diffusion latents via frequency-aware multiband transport: the latent is decomposed into a low-frequency structural component (9×9 average pooling) that is fully warped, and a high-frequency residual that is only warped where reliability is high, preserving noise statistics in uncertain regions. In Stage II (Coordinate-Aligned Generation), Slerp interpolation between the transported endpoint latents forms intermediate queries, and a symmetric bi-phase attention handoff switches the K/V memory banks from source-aligned to target-aligned at the morph midpoint, ensuring queries and memories always share a consistent coordinate frame throughout UNet denoising.

## Results

On the combined MorphBench + Morph4Data benchmarks, AlignMorph achieves the best overall LPIPS (153.78), FID (140.12), and PPL (3896.93), outperforming the closest tuning-free baseline FreeMorph (164.57 / 154.45 / 4138.79) and both optimization-based methods. On Morph4Data specifically, AlignMorph records LPIPS 74.23, FID 188.44, and PPL 1896.30. In a 25-participant user study, AlignMorph receives a 50.9% preference rate versus 26.4% for FreeMorph, 14.5% for IMPUS, and 8.2% for DiffMorpher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlignMorph outperforms all three baselines on every metric across both benchmarks. Against the closest tuning-free competitor FreeMorph (reproduced), AlignMorph improves Morph4Data LPIPS from 79.56 to 74.23 (−6.7%), FID from 203.32 to 188.44 (−7.3%), and PPL from 1993.42 to 1896.30 (−4.9%). Against the optimization-based DiffMorph, the gains are substantially larger: Morph4Data FID drops from 292.54 to 188.44 (−35.6%) and PPL from 2394.05 to 1896.30 (−20.8%), while also eliminating the per-pair fine-tuning cost (115 s vs. tens of minutes). Against IMPUS, AlignMorph reduces Morph4Data LPIPS from 134.88 to 74.23 (−45.0%) and PPL from 3199.90 to 1896.30 (−40.7%). The user study confirms these quantitative advantages perceptually, with AlignMorph preferred by 50.9% of participants versus only 26.4% for FreeMorph.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tuning-free diffusion morphing line initiated by FreeMorph, which interpolates endpoint latents via Slerp and injects endpoint features through cross-attention. The key departure is the 'transport-then-denoise' paradigm: rather than letting the diffusion model implicitly resolve spatial misalignment during sampling, AlignMorph first establishes explicit semantic-level correspondence via entropic optimal transport (using DINOv2+FeatUp features) and projects it into latent space through a frequency-aware multiband warp that preserves high-frequency noise statistics. This decoupling of geometric alignment from generative refinement is the conceptual novelty, building on OT-based matching from domain adaptation and semantic correspondence literature but applying it in a diffusion-compatible manner for the first time in morphing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode of tuning-free morphing—structural entanglement and ghosting under large layout discrepancies—that prior methods implicitly assumed away. The reliability-gated, frequency-decoupled transport is a practical recipe that could transfer to other diffusion-based tasks involving spatially misaligned conditioning (e.g., multi-image composition, video inpainting with layout changes). However, the method's boundary is clearly the existence of meaningful semantic correspondence: for extreme cross-category pairs (e.g., flower-to-monkey), the OT plan becomes unreliable and the method degrades to plain interpolation, leaving open-category generative blending as an unsolved problem.
