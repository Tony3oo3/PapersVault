## Problem

Current aerial localization benchmarks report only aggregate accuracy over predominantly man-made environments, masking severe terrain-dependent performance gaps. Localization over unstructured natural landscapes—where self-similar textures, repetitive vegetation, and drastic seasonal appearance changes dominate—remains effectively unmeasured because no existing dataset stratifies performance by terrain type or provides controlled multi-season revisitation with per-pixel land-cover annotations.

## Input / Output

For visual localization, each query is a 1600×1100 grayscale aerial frame (50 Hz) resized to 800 px longest edge, matched against a north-aligned orthophoto crop (20 cm GSD) extracted from multi-year-old governmental reference tiles (2011–2025); 2D correspondences are lifted to 3D via a co-registered DSM and a 6-DoF pose is recovered via PnP inside RANSAC (≥20 inliers, 12 px reprojection threshold). For VPR, both query and database images are center-cropped to 224×224 and matched by global embedding similarity with positives defined by 25 m GNSS proximity. For odometry, the system consumes 25 Hz images and 200 Hz IMU data, producing trajectory estimates scored by ATE, MPD, and per-second RPE after Umeyama alignment. Every query frame is additionally assigned to one of four terrain superclasses (artificial, agricultural, forest, water) via projected CLC5 land-cover masks, enabling per-class metric decomposition.

## Method

The core contribution is a large-scale benchmark (1,379 km across six microlight campaigns, Feb 2022–Jan 2024) that isolates terrain as a controlled variable by re-flying identical routes across seasons, paired with a terrain-stratified evaluation protocol that decomposes feature detection, matching, and localization metrics by per-pixel land-cover class. Rather than proposing a new localization algorithm, the paper evaluates four state-of-the-art cross-domain matchers (SIFT+LightGlue, SuperPoint+LightGlue, LoFTR, SE2-LoFTR), three odometry pipelines (VINS-Mono, ORB-SLAM, DROID-SLAM), and four VPR methods (CosPlace, MixVPR, AnyLoc, SALAD) through this protocol to expose where each fails. The key empirical finding is a systematic terrain bias: even the best matcher (SE2-LoFTR) drops from 66% recall on artificial surfaces to 33% on forests and 9% on agricultural areas at (5 m, 5°), and odometry methods show complementary failure modes—optical-flow trackers lose short-horizon stability in low-structure regions while descriptor matchers lose long-horizon distinctiveness in repetitive vegetation. Ground truth is verified independently via a visual ground-truth trajectory (VGT) from feature correspondences to orthophotos, with median 2 m translation and <1° rotation agreement against the RTK-GNSS/INS reference.

## Results

On the primary visual localization task (perspective aerial → orthophoto), SE2-LoFTR achieves the best overall recall at (5 m, 5°) of 34.4%, roughly doubling LoFTR (18.8%) and outperforming sparse matchers SIFT+LightGlue (19.2%) and SuperPoint+LightGlue (24.3%). The terrain gap is stark: even SE2-LoFTR drops from 66.1% recall on artificial surfaces to 33.3% on forests and 9.4% on agricultural areas. For cross-domain VPR, AnyLoc (DINOv2 backbone) leads with 19.2% Recall@1, nearly doubling SALAD (10.2%) and substantially outperforming ground-level-trained CosPlace (5.4%) and MixVPR (7.0%). In VIO, VINS-Mono achieves median RPE_t below 1.5 m across most campaigns, while ORB-SLAM accumulates stronger short-term drift (RPE_t up to 16.4 m on the longest winter flight).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In visual localization, SE2-LoFTR dominates all three other matchers across every terrain class and threshold, with its rotation-equivariant architecture particularly effective at bridging the heading gap between perspective queries and north-aligned orthophotos (66.1% vs. 52.5% R₅/₅ on artificial surfaces vs. SP+LG). Dense matchers (LoFTR, SE2-LoFTR) outperform sparse ones (SIFT+LG, SP+LG) on natural terrain but none closes the artificial-to-natural gap: at R₁/₁, only artificial surfaces yield non-trivial recall for any method. In VPR, AnyLoc (DINOv2 + aerial-domain VLAD) achieves 19.2% R@1, nearly 2× SALAD (10.2%) and ~3× the ground-level-trained CosPlace (5.4%) and MixVPR (7.0%), confirming that foundation-model features generalize better across the aerial-to-orthophoto domain gap. For odometry, VINS-Mono (KLT-based) outperforms ORB-SLAM (descriptor-based) in ATE and MPD on most campaigns, while ORB-SLAM VO paradoxically achieves lower ATE than its own VIO variant, suggesting sensitivity to inertial initialization at high aerial velocities. DROID-SLAM (learning-based VO) underperforms ORB-SLAM VO in most campaigns and suffers 51% coverage on the longest summer flight, indicating fragility in extended unstructured sequences. Across all tasks, the consistent finding is that every method degrades sharply on agricultural and forest terrain relative to artificial surfaces, a gap invisible in aggregate metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in aerial visual localization by introducing the first benchmark that stratifies performance by semantic terrain class, a diagnostic axis absent from all prior aerial datasets (OrthoLoC, VPAIR, AerialVL, AnyVisLoc). While the cross-domain matching task (perspective aerial imagery → orthophoto references) is well established, no prior work isolates the effect of underlying land cover on localization accuracy, nor combines controlled multi-season revisitation with multi-year reference data at this scale (1,379 km, 1.65M images). The evaluation protocol repurposes existing state-of-the-art matchers (SE2-LoFTR, LoFTR, SIFT+LightGlue) and odometry pipelines (VINS-Mono, ORB-SLAM, DROID-SLAM) as baselines, but the contribution is the dataset, the terrain-stratified metric, and the empirical characterization of where and why methods fail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that a systematic, method-independent terrain gap exists (e.g., SE2-LoFTR recall drops from 66% on artificial surfaces to 9% on agricultural areas) and is invisible under aggregate metrics—reframes how the field should evaluate aerial localization robustness. The 'feature-rich yet match-poor' characterization of forests (abundant detectable features but few reliably re-observable correspondences) points to a shared root cause across tracking and descriptor-based methods: natural terrain lacks geometric distinctiveness under appearance change. Open questions include whether rotation-equivariant or foundation-model features can close the gap, how to handle textureless water bodies, and whether drift-free odometry is achievable over 300+ km without loop closure. Limitations include the microlight platform's constrained motion profiles and projected (rather than image-derived) terrain annotations.
