## Problem

Visual Instruction Tuning (VIT) for LVLMs typically packs multiple instruction-response pairs about the same image into a single multi-turn training example, yet prevailing benchmarks evaluate models in isolated single-turn settings. This structural mismatch causes visual attention decay and contextual overfitting, leading to a significant performance gap between multi-turn and single-turn evaluation that the literature has largely overlooked. Additionally, no existing evaluation metric captures a model's robustness under varying contextual dependencies beyond raw accuracy on static question-answer pairs.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a multi-turn dialogue (N instruction-response pairs) grounded on a single RGB image, and stochastically restructures which historical turns are visible to each turn while retaining all turns for loss computation. At inference/evaluation, the model receives an image plus a text instruction (optionally preceded by prior conversational turns) and produces an autoregressive textual response. The proposed Balanced Latin Square evaluation permutes the N turns across N dialogue positions, requiring N forward passes per image to compute context-robust accuracy metrics.

## Method

StochasT stochastically varies the visible historical context for each turn during VIT by performing a backward traversal from turn n through its predecessors, sampling a drop probability p_k ~ Beta(α,β) at each step and halting at the first retained turn, which becomes the parent node in a directed conversation tree. Unlike standard dropout or stochastic depth, no tokens are physically removed; all N turns remain in the sequence for loss computation, but their causal attention to prior turns is selectively masked, making the approach compute-neutral. This tree expansion naturally interpolates between single-turn training (p→1, all turns connect to the image root) and standard multi-turn training (p=0, rigid sequential chain), training the model over an implicit ensemble of context lengths. The authors further introduce a Balanced Latin Square Turn Permutation evaluation that constructs an N×N matrix where each turn occupies every position and immediately precedes every other turn exactly once, yielding two metrics: Context-Robust Accuracy (CRA, mean accuracy across permutations) and Strict CRA+ (accuracy only if correct under all permutations).

## Results

On the four-domain average (iNat-Plant, PathVQA, CoralVQA, TaiwanVQA), StochasT achieves 70.63% accuracy under multiT evaluation and 67.16% under singleT evaluation with LLaVA-1.5-7B, and 80.16% / 77.28% respectively with Qwen2.5-VL-3B. On the MMDU multi-turn multi-image benchmark, StochasT reaches 13.10% (LLaVA) and 59.80% (Qwen), outperforming the MultiT baseline by 0.42 and 1.90 points. The singleT-to-multiT evaluation gap narrows from 7.01% (MultiT-trained LLaVA) to 3.47% (StochasT-trained LLaVA), and from 6.19% to 3.33% for Qwen, closely matching the pre-tuning robustness of the base models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares StochasT against two training baselines: standard MultiT VIT (packing all turns for one image into a single sequence, as in LLaVA [34]) and SingleT (unrolling each turn into an independent image–instruction–response pair). StochasT consistently outperforms MultiT on both evaluation settings: by 9.19% on singleT eval and 3.08% on multiT eval for LLaVA-1.5-7B, and by 8.54% and 3.58% for Qwen2.5-VL-3B. Against SingleT, StochasT is slightly behind on singleT eval (67.16 vs. 68.46 for LLaVA; 77.28 vs. 76.71 for Qwen) but decisively wins on multiT eval (70.63 vs. 68.60 for LLaVA; 80.16 vs. 77.75 for Qwen) and on MMDU (13.10 vs. N/A; 59.80 vs. N/A), where SingleT cannot be evaluated. The paper also notes that SingleT requires roughly double the training tokens to reach comparable singleT accuracy due to image duplication, making StochasT the more compute-efficient choice. On the Balanced Latin Square robustness metric (CRA), StochasT achieves the highest score (67.69 for LLaVA, 77.53 for Qwen), while SingleT edges it out on the stricter CRA+ metric (53.15 vs. 50.89; 64.57 vs. 61.76), attributed to SingleT's brute-force repetition forcing more deterministic outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

StochasT directly adapts the stochastic depth principle from ResNets (Huang et al., ECCV 2016) to the conversational structure of visual instruction tuning, replacing the dropping of residual blocks with the stochastic masking of historical dialogue turns. It departs from the standard VIT pipeline (LLaVA, InstructBLIP) where multi-turn dialogues are either packed as a rigid chain or unrolled into independent single-turn samples; StochasT occupies a principled middle ground by constructing a causal directed tree over turns. The paper also introduces a Balanced Latin Square evaluation mechanism borrowed from experimental design, which has no direct analogue in prior LVLM benchmarking that relies on static single-turn or fixed multi-turn protocols.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard multi-turn VIT *worsens* the single-turn/multi-turn performance gap (from ~3% to ~7%) is practically important: it means the dominant training recipe in the field is inadvertently making models more brittle to the isolated-query setting that most benchmarks use. StochasT offers a compute-neutral fix that practitioners can adopt without additional data or infrastructure, and the BLS/CRA evaluation framework provides a more honest measure of LVLM robustness than raw accuracy. A key limitation is the assumption of weak inter-turn dependency, which restricts applicability to strongly sequential conversations; whether the approach transfers to agentic or tool-use settings with tight turn-to-turn coupling remains open.
