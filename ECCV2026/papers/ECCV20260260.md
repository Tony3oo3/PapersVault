## Problem

Existing aerial-ground person re-identification methods rely on coarse binary aerial/ground labels and rigid orthogonality constraints to disentangle view and identity features. The binary labels fail to capture continuous aerial viewpoint variations caused by altitude and depression-angle changes, leading to intra-view feature fragmentation. Moreover, forcing a hard orthogonal partition between view-specific and view-invariant subspaces can suppress identity-discriminative cues that are naturally entangled with viewpoint-sensitive channels.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB person image (aerial or ground) resized to 256×128, which is tokenized into non-overlapping 16×16 patches and encoded by a ViT-B/16 backbone pretrained on ImageNet. The output is a view-invariant identity embedding derived from the modulated [CLS] token, used for cross-view retrieval ranking. Training uses a random identity sampler with 32 identities × 4 images per mini-batch (batch size 128), and no test-time augmentation or re-ranking is applied at inference.

## Method

QVAM replaces rigid view disentanglement with query-guided adaptive modulation. A View-aware Decoder (VAD) employs L learnable view queries in a standard Transformer decoder (self-attention → cross-attention to patch tokens → FFN) to distill fine-grained viewpoint cues, with a diversity loss enforcing orthogonal attention patterns across queries to prevent mode collapse. The Adaptive Feature Modulation (AFM) module then fuses a shared mask token with the [CLS] token for identity context, refines it via cross-attention to the view queries, and applies a sigmoid-activated channel-wise mask to the [CLS] token, producing a view-invariant feature and a suppressed residual. A Cross-View Prototype Alignment (CVPA) loss maintains momentum-updated dual-view memory banks and enforces L2 alignment between same-identity aerial/ground centroids at both batch and memory levels, providing distribution-level supervision that stabilizes the modulation. The total objective combines identity cross-entropy and triplet losses on both the raw [CLS] and modulated features, plus weighted CVPA, query-diversity, and mask-entropy regularization terms.

## Results

On CARGO-ALL, QVAM achieves 78.85% Rank-1, 71.02% mAP, and 60.25% mINP, improving over the previous best (SeCap) by +10.26% Rank-1 and +10.83% mAP. On the most challenging cross-view protocol CARGO A↔G, QVAM reaches 72.50% Rank-1 / 64.41% mAP, surpassing SeCap by +3.07% / +5.47%. On AG-ReID, QVAM attains 85.53% Rank-1 / 76.81% mAP (A→G) and 88.46% Rank-1 / 80.52% mAP (G→A), outperforming VDT by +2.62% Rank-1 and VIF by +1.14% Rank-1 respectively. On AG-ReIDv2, QVAM delivers consistent mAP gains of +1.45% to +2.28% over SeCap across all four protocols, with mINP improvements confirming better hard-positive retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QVAM decisively outperforms the strongest prior method SeCap [36] across all CARGO protocols: +10.26% Rank-1 / +10.83% mAP on ALL, +3.07% / +5.47% on A↔G, +5.00% / +10.55% on A↔A, and +4.46% / +7.20% on G↔G. On AG-ReID, QVAM beats VDT [46] by +2.62% Rank-1 / +2.37% mAP (A→G) and VIF [16] by +1.14% / +1.33% (G→A), while also surpassing DTST [37], SD-ReID [38], and TransReID [11] by 1–4% Rank-1. On AG-ReIDv2, QVAM achieves consistent mAP gains of +1.45% to +2.28% over SeCap across all four protocols (A→C, A→W, C→A, W→A), though it shows slight Rank-1 drops of ~1% on A→W and C→A; the authors attribute this to improved hard-positive retrieval (mINP +1.43 / +1.99). Against older CNN-based baselines (SBS, PCB, BoT, MGN, VV, AGW), QVAM leads by 18–33% Rank-1 on CARGO-ALL, reflecting the substantial advantage of the ViT backbone combined with view-aware modulation. The gap over VDT [46] and SeCap [36]—the two methods most directly addressing aerial-ground view disentanglement—highlights the benefit of continuous query-guided viewpoint modeling over rigid binary-label orthogonality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant disentanglement paradigm in AGPReID (VDT, SeCap), which relies on coarse binary aerial/ground labels and rigid orthogonality constraints to separate view-specific from view-invariant features. Instead, QVAM repurposes the learnable-query mechanism (analogous to DETR-style object queries) for continuous viewpoint modeling, replacing hard disentanglement with soft, query-conditioned channel modulation. The Cross-View Prototype Alignment loss draws on momentum-updated memory banks from domain-adaptive ReID (e.g., Invariance Matters [51], Self-paced Contrastive Learning [5]) but applies them specifically to enforce cross-view distributional alignment at both batch and memory levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that binary view labels fragment aerial features and that rigid orthogonality suppresses identity-discriminative cues provides a concrete diagnostic for why prior disentanglement methods plateau, and the query-guided modulation offers a generalizable alternative applicable to other cross-view or cross-domain matching problems. The large CARGO gains (+10.26% Rank-1) are encouraging but come from a synthetic benchmark; the more modest AG-ReIDv2 improvements (+1.4–2.3% mAP) suggest real-world gains are smaller. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video-based AGPReID (AG-VPReID), scales to more than two view categories, and whether the learned queries generalize to unseen camera geometries at deployment time.
