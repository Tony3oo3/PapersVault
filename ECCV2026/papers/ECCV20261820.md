## Problem

As T2V models produce increasingly realistic videos, their remaining errors are subtle, transient, and spatio-temporally localized—yet it remains unclear whether VLMs can detect, precisely localize, and explain such fine-grained generation failures. Existing video generation benchmarks provide only global quality scores without temporal localization or natural language error explanations, leaving a critical gap in evaluating VLMs as automated fine-grained video evaluators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261820/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The task takes a short AI-generated video (average 6.5s) and its generating text prompt, and produces a set of error tuples, each containing start/end timestamps, one of six category labels (physics violations, appearance/disappearance, motion, prompt adherence, logical errors, body anatomy), and a natural language rationale grounded in video content. Evaluation uses maximum-weight bipartite matching between predicted and ground-truth error sets, with a composite S+P metric requiring both temporal precision and LLM-judged reason similarity to simultaneously exceed a threshold (τ=0.7).

## Method

SPOTLIGHT is a benchmark of 600 videos from three SOTA T2V models (Veo 3, Seedance, LTX-2) with 1,604 human-annotated errors, each carrying precise temporal boundaries, a category label, a difficulty rating, and a natural language explanation. The evaluation protocol formulates error matching as a maximum-weight bipartite matching problem over pairwise precision, recall, and LLM-based reason similarity scores, with a stricter S+P criterion requiring both temporal precision and semantic similarity to pass threshold. The paper benchmarks seven VLMs (open-weight: Qwen3-VL, Molmo 2, MiniCPM-V-4.5, InternVL3; closed-source: Gemini 2.5 Pro, Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-5.1) under zero-shot and four inference-time strategies (chain-of-thought, sliding-window, sequential reason-then-localize, and multi-agent per-category decomposition). Key findings show humans outperform the best VLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro with multi-agent) by ~2× on S+P, with 58% of model errors stemming from perception failures and hallucination rather than reasoning deficits.

## Results

On SPOTLIGHT (τ=0.7), the best zero-shot VLM, Gemini 2.5 Pro, achieves an S+P score of 0.250, correctly matching only 12.6% of ground-truth errors. The strongest inference-time strategy, Multi-Agent on Gemini 2.5 Pro, improves this to S+P = 0.308 (17.0% match), while the smaller Qwen3-8B-I-MA reaches S+P = 0.254 (13.3%), matching Gemini 2.5 Pro's zero-shot performance. Humans achieve S+P = 0.508 (26.8% match), outperforming the best model by nearly 2×, underscoring a substantial gap in fine-grained video error detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-weight VLMs, Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct is the strongest (S+P = 0.148), outperforming Molmo2-8B (0.087), MiniCPM-V-4.5-8B (0.118), and InternVL3-8B (0.041), though all lag substantially behind closed-source models. Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves the best zero-shot S+P (0.250), while Gemini 3 Pro matches it exactly (0.250) but with higher precision (0.807 vs. 0.691) and lower recall, indicating no net improvement. GPT-5.1 (S+P = 0.218) approaches but does not match Gemini 2.5 Pro, and is the only model evaluated in a multi-frame setting rather than native video input. The Multi-Agent inference strategy on Qwen3-8B-I (S+P = 0.254) effectively closes the gap to Gemini 2.5 Pro's zero-shot performance, and applying MA to Gemini 2.5 Pro yields the overall best VLM result (S+P = 0.308, 17.0% match). Despite these gains, all VLM baselines remain roughly 2× below human performance (S+P = 0.508), with the largest gaps on medium-difficulty errors and in precise temporal localization (35.9% of model timestamps were too wide).

## Relation to Prior Work

SPOTLIGHT carves out a new niche at the intersection of video generation evaluation and fine-grained VLM assessment. Prior evaluation benchmarks (VBench, EvalCrafter, StoryEval) provide global quality scores or dimension-wise rankings, while fine-grained annotation datasets (BrokenVideos, DAVID-XR1) target low-level artifacts or AI-detection rather than high-level semantic/physics failures. The VLM-as-reward-model line (VideoRewardBench, VideoAlign, Video-Bench) similarly offers holistic judgments without temporal localization. SPOTLIGHT departs from all of these by requiring precise temporal boundaries plus natural-language explanations for nuanced errors in modern high-fidelity T2V outputs, a combination the authors explicitly identify as unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2× human-over-model gap and the finding that 58% of VLM failures trace to perception errors and hallucination (rather than reasoning) sharply delineate what current VLMs cannot do as automated video evaluators, which is critical for anyone considering deploying them as reward models for T2V optimization. The benchmark also reveals that task decomposition (multi-agent) can close much of the model-scale gap, suggesting that prompting strategy may matter as much as model capacity for this task. Open questions include whether VLMs trained specifically for temporal localization and physics reasoning can close the remaining gap, and whether the benchmark's difficulty distribution (793 easy vs. 415 hard errors) adequately stresses future models.
