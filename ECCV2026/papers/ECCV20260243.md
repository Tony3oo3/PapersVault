## Problem

Sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting suffers from severe overfitting due to insufficient geometric constraints. Existing remedies rely on monocular depth priors that are inherently scale-ambiguous and cross-view inconsistent, causing the optimization to be misguided and producing defective geometry. The paper asks whether stereo matching priors can provide the absolute-scale, binocular-consistent geometric supervision that monocular cues cannot.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260243/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of sparse RGB images (3–9 views for LLFF/DTU, 12–24 for Mip-NeRF360, 8 for Blender) along with their camera intrinsics and extrinsics, processed at reduced resolutions (1/8 or 1/2 depending on dataset). During training it additionally constructs virtual stereo pairs by horizontally translating each training camera and rendering the corresponding right view from the current Gaussian scene, which are fed into a zero-shot stereo model. The output is a 3D Gaussian scene representation that renders novel-view RGB images and depth maps in real time with no additional inference cost over vanilla 3DGS.

## Method

StereoGS introduces three training-time mechanisms. First, Stereo Depth Regularization constructs a virtual stereo pair per training view (ground-truth left image + rendered right image), passes it through FoundationStereo to obtain a disparity map, converts it to metric depth via triangulation, and supervises the rendered depth in inverse-depth L1 space using a validity mask that excludes occluded, background, and anomalous regions. Second, Gradient-Aware Opacity Decay computes each Gaussian's relative opacity gradient (normalized by the scene-wide mean) and applies an exponential soft-thresholding function to derive a per-Gaussian decay factor, so that low-gradient (redundant) primitives are aggressively pruned while high-gradient (structurally important) ones are preserved. Third, Consistency-Aware Dense Initialization uses the zero-shot MVSAnywhere model to estimate multi-view-consistent depth maps, filters outliers via cross-view reprojection error, and fuses them into a dense point cloud for Gaussian seeding. The total loss is a simple sum of the standard 3DGS color loss (L1 + D-SSIM) and the stereo depth regularization term.

## Results

On LLFF (3/6/9 views), StereoGS achieves PSNR of 21.91/24.92/26.25, SSIM of 0.773/0.853/0.879, and LPIPS of 0.157/0.104/0.087, outperforming all 12 baselines on every metric. On DTU (3/6/9 views), it reaches PSNR 21.46/24.86/26.83 and LPIPS 0.099/0.064/0.049. On Mip-NeRF360 (12/24 views) and Blender (8 views), StereoGS attains PSNR 20.25/24.18 and 24.83 respectively, again leading all competitors. The method incurs no additional inference overhead compared to vanilla 3DGS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StereoGS outperforms all 12 baselines across all four datasets and every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). The closest competitor is Binocular3DGS [7], which StereoGS surpasses by 0.47 dB PSNR on LLFF 3-view (21.91 vs 21.44), 0.75 dB on DTU 3-view (21.46 vs 20.71), and 0.12 dB on Blender (24.83 vs 24.71). On Mip-NeRF360, the nearest baseline is D²GS [32] (20.09 PSNR at 12 views), which StereoGS exceeds by 0.16 dB. Monocular-depth-based methods (DNGaussian, FSGS) trail by 1.5–2.5 dB PSNR in the 3-view setting, confirming the paper's argument that scale-ambiguous monocular priors are insufficient. Notably, vanilla 3DGS degrades severely under sparsity (16.02 PSNR on LLFF 3-view, 10.99 on DTU 3-view), while StereoGS recovers most of the gap to dense-view performance without any extra inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

StereoGS extends the 3DGS rendering framework [13] to the sparse-view regime by replacing the monocular depth priors used in DNGaussian [17] and FSGS [50] with a foundation stereo model (FoundationStereo [37]) that provides absolute-scale, binocularly-consistent depth during optimization. It departs from Binocular3DGS [7], which enforces cross-view consistency only through indirect photometric warping, by instead supervising rendered depth directly against stereo-matched reference depth. For initialization, it upgrades the MVS-based point cloud generation of MVPGS [39] and NexusGS [48] by leveraging the zero-shot MVSAnywhere [9] model with cross-view reprojection filtering, addressing the domain-gap limitations of earlier MVS approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, principled solution to the scale-ambiguity and cross-view-inconsistency problems that have plagued monocular-depth-regularized sparse-view 3DGS, and does so without any inference-time overhead. The gradient-aware opacity decay is a practical, general-purpose pruning strategy that could be adopted by other 3DGS variants. Open questions remain: stereo models still struggle in extremely textureless regions (acknowledged as a limitation), the additional training cost of online stereo matching is non-trivial, and it is unclear how well the virtual-stereo-pair construction generalizes to unbounded or dynamic scenes beyond the bounded object/scene benchmarks tested here.
