## Problem

Existing SOC-based methods for diffusion inverse problems (e.g., DOC) rely on iLQR-style trajectory optimization that requires second-order Taylor updates with Hessians, incurring high memory usage and long runtimes. This paper asks whether SOC guidance can be integrated into the stepwise sampling routine of a pretrained diffusion model in closed form, avoiding both trajectory-level optimization and any retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261902/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded measurement y (corrupted with additive Gaussian noise of known standard deviation), a degradation operator H (linear or nonlinear), and a pretrained unconditional diffusion score model, and produces a reconstructed clean image. It operates training-free on 256×256 images (FFHQ, ImageNet) and 768×768 images (SD3-medium), using 4 ODE-solver NFEs per step for linear tasks and 8 for nonlinear tasks, with 250 or 500 reverse-denoising steps respectively. The method is compatible with both VP-SDE and VE-SDE backbones under the EDM time discretization, and extends to latent diffusion and flow-matching models.

## Method

SOCS formulates the reverse denoising process as a linear SDE with a control input and derives a closed-form optimal controller (Eq. 3–4) by solving a quadratic SOC problem with a terminal measurement penalty, leveraging the certainty equivalence principle to reduce the stochastic problem to a deterministic ODE. At each sampling step, the algorithm (1) predicts a clean estimate via a few-step Euler ODE, (2) enforces measurement consistency—via affine projection for linear operators or Langevin-dynamics refinement for nonlinear operators—and (3) maps the corrected estimate back onto the denoising manifold through the closed-form transport. A key design choice is the finite terminal penalty coefficient γ, which bounds the control amplitude so the trajectory stays close to the diffusion prior's native manifold; the effective guidance weight γ(I+γA)⁻¹ saturates for large γ, making the method robust to hyperparameter tuning. Three variants are provided: SOCS-Linear (γ→∞, linear H, pseudoinverse projection), SOCS-Nonlinear-∞ (γ→∞, full Jacobian), and SOCS-Nonlinear-γ (finite γ, regularized Gauss-Newton solve), requiring at most first-order Jacobians and no Hessian computation.

## Results

On FFHQ 256×256, SOCS-Nonlinear-γ achieves the best PSNR across most linear tasks: 28.66 dB on 4× super-resolution, 30.63 dB on random inpainting, and 30.39 dB on motion deblurring, while also attaining the lowest LPIPS (0.065 on random inpainting) and competitive FID scores. On ImageNet 256×256, SOCS-Nonlinear-γ reaches 28.75 PSNR / 0.808 SSIM / 53.02 FID on random inpainting, outperforming DAPS (28.76 PSNR / 0.789 SSIM / 54.41 FID) and DDNM (26.55 PSNR / 0.868 SSIM / 99.36 FID). For nonlinear tasks, SOCS-Nonlinear-γ achieves 29.59±2.88 PSNR on phase retrieval (FFHQ) and 28.42±1.63 PSNR on nonlinear deblurring, matching or exceeding DAPS. On SD3-medium at 768×768, SOCS-SD3 attains the best PSNR (30.14 / 30.45) and FID (53.61 / 45.72) across two ×12 super-resolution settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOCS-Nonlinear-γ consistently outperforms DAPS on PSNR and SSIM across linear tasks (e.g., 30.63 vs 29.66 PSNR on random inpainting FFHQ; 30.39 vs 29.49 on motion deblurring), while DAPS retains a slight edge on LPIPS and FID in some settings. Against DPS, SOCS variants gain 3–4 dB PSNR on super-resolution and inpainting (28.66 vs 24.25 on SR 4× FFHQ), confirming the benefit of the SOC-based closed-form update over naive gradient guidance. FPS-SMC achieves the highest PSNR on SR 4× (29.02) but suffers substantially worse LPIPS (0.234) and FID (106.56) than SOCS-Nonlinear-γ, indicating lower perceptual quality. On nonlinear tasks, SOCS-Nonlinear-γ is competitive with DAPS (29.59 vs 29.88 PSNR on phase retrieval; 28.42 vs 28.10 on nonlinear deblur) and clearly surpasses DPS and RED-diff by 10+ dB. The prior SOC method DOC [20] is only reported on two FFHQ tasks and is outperformed by SOCS-Linear (28.51 vs 27.15 PSNR on SR 4×). On SD3-medium (Table 3), SOCS-SD3 achieves the best PSNR and FID over PSLD, LatentDAPS, FlowChef, and FlowDPS, with a 1.2 dB PSNR and ~10-point FID improvement over the strongest baseline (LatentDAPS) on bicubic ×12 SR.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOCS builds on the foundational connection between diffusion processes and stochastic optimal control established by Berner et al. [1], and directly addresses the computational bottleneck of the prior SOC-based inverse problem solver DOC [20], which relies on iLQR trajectory optimization requiring Hessian computations. Rather than optimizing over the entire diffusion path, SOCS derives a closed-form stepwise controller that injects measurement consistency at each denoising step, avoiding second-order costs. The method also adopts the trajectory-decoupling strategy from DAPS [42] and provides the first SOC-based theoretical justification for the gradient guidance term ∇_{x_t}‖H(x_t)−y‖² that underlies all Bayesian sampling methods (DPS, DDRM, DDNM, etc.), unifying them under a single optimal-control objective with a tunable terminal penalty γ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing measurement injection as a bounded-amplitude control problem, SOCS offers a principled knob (γ) to balance data fidelity against the diffusion prior's native generative quality, which is particularly valuable for perceptual metrics like FID and LPIPS. The closed-form solution's compatibility with both VP-SDE and VE-SDE backbones, as well as with latent diffusion (SD v1.5) and flow-matching (SD3) architectures, makes it a broadly applicable plug-in for existing inverse-problem pipelines. Open questions include whether the linear-SDE assumption limits applicability to more complex non-linear dynamics, how the method scales to very high-resolution or 3D medical imaging, and whether the γ plateau observed in experiments persists under stronger model–measurement mismatch.
