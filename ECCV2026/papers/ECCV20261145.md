## Problem

State-of-the-art 3D reconstruction models (monocular depth estimation, multi-view stereo, depth completion, neural rendering) consistently underperform on thin objects because such structures occupy few pixels in RGB images and yield sparse, noisy returns from range sensors, leading to correspondence ambiguity and occlusion across viewpoints. No existing dataset or benchmark specifically targets thin-object reconstruction, and the potential of tactile sensing to complement visual and range modalities for this task remains unexplored.

## Input / Output

Tactile-DC consumes a 480×640 RGB image, a corresponding sparse depth map from an Intel RealSense D455 (effective range ~0.2–5 m), and eight per-object tactile response maps from a DIGIT vision-based tactile sensor (each a short ~200-frame 3-channel video of local surface deformation at a known 3D contact location). The output is a dense metric depth map at the same 480×640 resolution. The broader VisTa3D dataset additionally provides camera poses (from ORB-SLAM3), intrinsics, 200 Hz IMU streams, and ground-truth depth/segmentation masks derived from 0.03 mm-accuracy structured-light scans.

## Method

Tactile-DC is the first visual-range-tactile depth completion baseline. Shallow encoders process the RGB image and sparse depth map separately, and their latent feature maps are fused via a convolutional layer. Each of the eight tactile response maps is independently encoded to a D×h×w feature map, then compressed to a D×1×1 latent vector through a 1×1 convolution, batch normalization, ReLU, and global average pooling. Using calibrated camera intrinsics and extrinsics, each 3D contact location is projected onto the image plane, and the corresponding tactile latent vector is added at that projected location to spatially modulate the fused RGB-depth features. The modulated feature map is passed through a CompletionFormer backbone and decoded by a Non-local Spatial Propagation Network into the final dense depth map. The key insight is that tactile sensing captures local surface shape and deformation largely independent of object thickness, and projecting contact coordinates onto the image plane enables spatially-accurate injection of this information where visual and range cues are weakest.

## Results

On the VisTa3D benchmark (13,204 test frames), Tactile-DC achieves the best depth estimation performance across all protocols: A1/A2/A3 of 0.844/0.900/0.920 (general) and 0.719/0.811/0.852 (thin-object focused) under the default protocol, with MAE of 0.091 m and RMSE of 0.347 m (general) and 0.078 m / 0.205 m (object). The closest baseline, OmniDC, achieves A1/A2/A3 of 0.834/0.881/0.897 (general) and 0.687/0.772/0.817 (object) but with substantially higher MAE (0.139 m vs. 0.091 m) and RMSE (0.460 m vs. 0.347 m). MDE methods (UniDepth, DepthAnything) perform near-zero A1 under the default protocol due to scale ambiguity, improving only after linear fitting or median scaling. For novel view synthesis, Nerfacto achieves the best PSNR (24.16 dB), SSIM (0.855), and LPIPS (0.177), outperforming 3DGS (22.97 dB) and 2DGS (21.74 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tactile-DC outperforms all 11 baselines on depth estimation under the default protocol in both general and thin-object focused evaluation. Against the strongest baseline OmniDC (MDC), Tactile-DC improves A1 by +0.010 (general) and +0.032 (object), while reducing MAE from 0.139 m to 0.091 m (general) and 0.101 m to 0.078 m (object), and RMSE from 0.460 m to 0.347 m (general) and 0.238 m to 0.205 m (object). MDE methods (UniDepth V1/V2, DepthAnything V1/V2) are the weakest, with A1 near zero under the default protocol due to scale ambiguity; even after linear fitting, their object-region A1 remains below 0.10. Among MVS methods, VGGT preserves more global structure than MDEs but still misses thin details (object A1 = 0.064 default), while MapAnything shows a large general-to-object gap (A1 drops from 0.511 to 0.165), indicating unreliable thin-object recovery. For novel view synthesis, Nerfacto (PSNR 24.16, SSIM 0.855) outperforms Depth-Nerfacto (23.52), 3DGS (22.97), and 2DGS (21.74), suggesting that current NVS methods remain limited by sparse views and occlusions around thin structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

Tactile-DC builds on the depth completion architecture of CompletionFormer [95] for its backbone and the Non-local Spatial Propagation Network [49] for decoding, while adopting the calibrated backprojection projection strategy from Wong & Soatto [81] to map 3D tactile contact locations onto the image plane. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior tactile-reconstruction work (TouchSDF, tactile-augmented radiance fields, TouchGS) that treated tactile as a standalone or auxiliary signal for implicit/explicit scene representations, instead embedding tactile cues directly into a feed-forward depth completion pipeline. It also extends the thin-object literature, which has been limited to 2D segmentation datasets (ThinObject-5K) or point-cloud-only scans (PointWire, PointVessel), by providing the first synchronized multimodal (RGB + depth + tactile + inertial) dataset with high-fidelity laser-scanned ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a consistent and quantified failure mode across all major 3D reconstruction paradigms (MDE, MVS, MDC, NVS) on thin structures, providing the community with a focused stress test that existing large-scale benchmarks do not cover. The demonstration that even a naive tactile integration yields the best thin-object depth completion suggests a promising but underexplored direction for multimodal fusion in reconstruction. Key limitations include the restriction to static, non-reflective objects, moderate dataset scale (387 scenes, 70 objects), and the absence of task-specific finetuning for MDE/MVS baselines, leaving open whether the tactile advantage persists under stronger training regimes or in dynamic/deformable settings.
