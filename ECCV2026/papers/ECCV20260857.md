## Problem

Modern optical flow pipelines accumulate task-specific inductive biases—correlation volumes, feature warping, iterative refinement, convex upsampling—that constrain model expressivity, complicate architectures, and add computational overhead. The paper asks whether a general-purpose, bias-free transformer can match or exceed state-of-the-art flow accuracy without any of these flow-specific components, while remaining practical at high (1080p) resolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260857/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

FreeFlow takes a pair of RGB images and patchifies each into non-overlapping 8×8 tokens (smaller than CroCo's 16×16), producing token sequences processed by a Siamese encoder. The output is a dense H×W×5 tensor: the first two channels are the optical flow in pixel units (obtained by multiplying the decoded patch map by the patch size of 8), and the remaining three channels parameterize uncertainty terms for the Mixture-of-Laplace loss. The architecture is designed for native 1080p inference without tiling, using variable-resolution training with a fixed token budget per minibatch to expose the model to diverse aspect ratios and motion ranges.

## Method

FreeFlow is a Siamese encoder-decoder transformer (following the CroCo/DUSt3R pattern) that replaces all flow-specific modules with a single feed-forward architecture. Each encoder/decoder layer stacks three attention blocks in fixed order: Window attention over a 4×4 grid of non-overlapping windows for local detail, Shifted-Window attention with a half-window offset to exchange information across window boundaries, and Global attention applied at 2× reduced resolution (via stride-2 down/up-convolutions) for long-range correspondence at controlled quadratic cost. The decoder alternates self-attention with cross-attention between the two views' features, and a lightweight three-layer head (3×3 conv → 1×1 conv → transposed conv, stride 8) directly predicts flow and uncertainty without iterative refinement or convex upsampling. Training proceeds in two phases: self-supervised cross-view completion pretraining (with 95% masking ratio, adapted from CroCo) followed by supervised optical flow finetuning on a mixed dataset (TartanAir, Things, Sintel, KITTI, HD1K) using 2× upsampled frames and the Mixture-of-Laplace loss. Rotary Positional Embeddings encode spatial position, and a logarithmic attention scale factor (log of token count divided by √D) improves generalization to inference resolutions beyond training.

## Results

FreeFlow-L achieves state-of-the-art results across all three benchmarks: on Spring, it attains 0.278 EPE, 3.192 1px, 1.048 Fl, and 95.235 WAUC (best among two-frame methods); on Sintel, it reaches 0.68 EPE (Clean) and 1.48 EPE (Final); and on KITTI-2015, it achieves 3.23 Fl-all. Notably, FreeFlow-L performs native 1080p inference with only 2.58 GB peak GPU memory, compared to 18–20 GB for WAFT variants. The model scales predictably from S (35M params) to L (231M params), with consistent accuracy gains at each size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeFlow-L outperforms the strongest two-frame transformer baselines across all metrics: it beats WAFT-DAv2-a2 by 9% in EPE (0.278 vs. 0.304) and 14% in Fl (1.048 vs. 1.197) on Spring, while using roughly 8× less GPU memory (2.58 GB vs. 20.58 GB). On Sintel, FreeFlow-L improves over GeoViT by 14% on Clean (0.68 vs. 0.79) and over VideoFlow-MOF (multi-frame) by 10% on Final (1.48 vs. 1.65). Against the closest multi-frame competitor ARFlow, FreeFlow-L achieves lower EPE (0.278 vs. 0.353) and Fl (1.048 vs. 1.212) on Spring using only two frames. Compared to the CroCo/Win-Win lineage of pure transformers, FreeFlow-L reduces Spring EPE by 44% (0.278 vs. 0.498 for CroCo-Flow) and 41% (vs. 0.475 for Win-Win), demonstrating that the hierarchical attention design with dense cross-tile feature exchange is critical for high-resolution accuracy. On KITTI-2015, FreeFlow-L (3.23 Fl-all) outperforms all non-stereo, non-multiframe methods, including DPFlow (3.56), WAFT-DAv2-a2 (3.31), and CroCo-Flow (3.64).

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeFlow inherits its high-level Siamese encoder-decoder structure from CroCo and DUSt3R, and its window/shifted-window attention pattern from Swin Transformer, but departs fundamentally from all prior optical flow methods by eliminating every flow-specific inductive bias—correlation volumes, feature warping, iterative refinement, and convex upsampling—that has been considered essential since PWC-Net and RAFT. Recent transformer-based flow methods (WAFT, GeoViT, CroCo-Flow, Win-Win) each removed some of these components but retained at least one (typically warping or iterative updates); FreeFlow is the first to remove them all while still reaching state-of-the-art accuracy. The paper explicitly positions this work within the broader CV trend of replacing bespoke pipelines with generic, data-driven transformers, arguing that optical flow was the last major dense-prediction task still relying on hand-crafted architectural priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a simple feed-forward hierarchical transformer without any flow-specific modules can match or exceed methods with complex iterative pipelines, FreeFlow challenges the field's working assumption that correspondence estimation requires explicit matching machinery. This opens the door to simpler, more modular architectures that are easier to scale, modify, and repurpose for related dense correspondence tasks (stereo, depth, scene flow). Practical advantages include substantially lower 1080p inference memory (2.58 GB vs. 20.58 GB for WAFT) and predictable scaling behavior. Open questions include whether the cross-view completion pretraining objective is truly necessary or whether pure supervised training at sufficient scale would suffice, and whether the approach generalizes to multi-frame or video settings without re-introducing temporal biases.
