## Problem

Multi-view consistent 3D scene editing via 2D diffusion models remains challenging because generating geometrically coherent edits across viewpoints is extremely difficult, while the extreme scarcity of 3D-consistent editing paired data makes supervised fine-tuning infeasible. Existing approaches—geometric-conditioned warping, iterative 3D optimization, and cross-view attention propagation—each suffer from limited edit types, low efficiency, or residual fine-grained inconsistencies. The paper identifies the key asymmetry that verifying 3D consistency is far more tractable than generating it, positioning reinforcement learning as a natural solution that bypasses the need for paired supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes M=9 rendered views of a 3D scene (evenly sampled around the scene for sufficient visual overlap) plus a text editing instruction, and processes them simultaneously through FLUX-Kontext's DiT architecture, where all views are tokenized and concatenated along the sequence dimension for global cross-view self-attention. At inference, the fine-tuned editor produces multi-view consistent edited images in a single forward pass (no per-scene optimization), which are then reconstructed into a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation for the final editable 3D scene. The entire editing pipeline completes in approximately 1.5 minutes on an RTX A6000, over 2× faster than iterative optimization baselines.

## Method

RL3DEdit fine-tunes FLUX-Kontext via GRPO reinforcement learning, using the 3D foundation model VGGT as a frozen reward/verifier that provides geometry-aware feedback without requiring paired editing data. The composite reward combines four terms: depth confidence (r^D) and point confidence (r^P) from VGGT's uncertainty maps to enforce multi-view geometric consistency, a relative pose reward (r^T) measuring inter-view camera alignment against ground-truth extrinsics, and an anchor reward (r^a) that compares one randomly selected view against a pre-computed high-quality single-view edit to preserve FLUX-Kontext's editing fidelity. During each GRPO step, the model generates G=16 candidate multi-view edits via SDE-based exploration (12-step denoising), and the anchor view is substituted with its reference image before feeding all views into VGGT for scoring. The model is optimized with a clipped GRPO objective plus KL regularization against the original editor, trained with LoRA (rank 32) on only 1,319 samples from 8 scenes in 42 hours, learning 3D-consistency priors while preserving the backbone's 2D editing capability.

## Results

On 100 test cases (70 in-distribution, 16 unseen instructions, 14 new scenes), RL3DEdit achieves a VIEScore of 5.48, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline EditSplat with FLUX-Kontext (3.23) by 70%. It also attains the best CLIP directional similarity (0.147) and the lowest photometric reprojection loss (0.076), confirming superior editing fidelity and multi-view consistency. Inference takes only 1.5 minutes per scene, over 2× faster than the next-best method (DGE at 4 min) and over 20× faster than the FLUX-based EditSplat baseline (40 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RL3DEdit outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against DGE (attention warping), it gains +2.67 VIEScore (5.48 vs 2.81) and reduces Ph-Loss from 0.086 to 0.076, while being ~2.7× faster (1.5 vs 4 min). Against GaussCtrl (depth-guided), the VIEScore gap is even larger (+3.11), and RL3DEdit matches its Ph-Loss (0.076 vs 0.077) while being 8× faster. The most direct comparison is with EditSplat re-implemented on the same FLUX-Kontext backbone: RL3DEdit improves VIEScore by 70% (5.48 vs 3.23), CLIP-dir by 18% (0.147 vs 0.125), and reduces editing time from 40 min to 1.5 min (>20× speedup). The original EditSplat (InstructPix2Pix backbone) trails on all metrics, confirming that the 2D backbone choice is a major bottleneck. Notably, RL3DEdit is the only method evaluated in a true zero-shot setting on the 30 out-of-distribution cases, where all baselines still require per-scene optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) multi-view consistent 3D scene editing via 2D diffusion backbones (EditSplat, DGE, GaussCtrl), (2) RL-based fine-tuning of generative models (Flow-GRPO for 2D editing, Nabla-R2D3/AR3D-R1 for 3D generation), and (3) 3D foundation models as verifiers (analogous to how SDS uses a frozen 2D diffusion model to score image quality). The key departure is replacing the infeasible supervised fine-tuning on 3D-consistent paired data with GRPO optimization guided by VGGT's confidence maps and pose predictions as rewards, making RL the first training paradigm applied to 3D scene editing. The choice of FLUX-Kontext as the backbone is motivated by its native multi-image joint editing capability via cross-view self-attention, which the authors argue is a necessary prerequisite for RL exploration to succeed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in 3D editing: the near-total absence of 3D-consistent paired editing data that would enable standard SFT. By showing that a frozen 3D foundation model (VGGT) can serve as a robust, non-hackable reward signal, the work opens a path to training 3D-consistent editors without curated datasets, with the added benefit of single-pass inference (~20× faster than iterative baselines) and zero-shot generalization to unseen scenes and instructions. However, the method remains bounded by the 2D backbone's attention sequence length (limiting view count and resolution), struggles with large non-rigid deformations, and was trained on only 8 scenes / 1,319 samples, leaving open whether the learned 3D-consistency prior scales to more complex or diverse scenes. The finding that traditional verifiers (SfM, reprojection warping) are trivially reward-hacked by textureless or blurry outputs, while a data-prior-based 3D model resists this, is a practically important negative result for anyone designing reward functions for 3D generation or editing.
