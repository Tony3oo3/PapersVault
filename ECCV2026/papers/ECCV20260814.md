## Problem

Fine-grained semantic control in visual autoregressive (VAR) models remains challenging: existing approaches either support only single-attribute edits without disentanglement (AREdit), require fine-tuning on paired examples with limited generalization (VAREdit), or lack continuous strength control. No prior method achieves disentangled, continuous, and compositional attribute manipulation within the discrete VAR token space without retraining the backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260814/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (1024×1024) and a pair of semantically different text prompts (e.g., 'A cat.' vs. 'A fat cat.') as input. The reference image is encoded into 13 multi-scale residual maps (1×1 through 64×64) by the Infinity tokenizer, and the prompts are mapped into the VAR-aligned text token space via a frozen Flan-T5 encoder and MLP. Output is an attribute-controlled edited image where the strength of one or more semantic attributes is continuously adjustable via a scalar multiplier γ, and learned directions can be stored in an offline library for retrieval and compositional combination across different images and object categories.

## Method

ATA exploits the observation that semantically meaningful vector arithmetic exists in the VAR-aligned text token space, analogous to word-embedding arithmetic in NLP. The core mechanism is Delta-Mod, a lightweight attention block that takes the reference image's predicted residual maps as keys/values and two learnable query vectors to output two semantic directions: d_general (from the mean of the general prompt distribution toward the specific image) and d_specific (from the mean of the attribute-conditioned sub-distribution toward the image). Their difference, d_attribute = d_general − d_specific, yields a disentangled, class-agnostic attribute direction that transfers across object categories. Training optimizes only Delta-Mod (500 steps, batch size 1) with the frozen VAR's original cross-entropy reconstruction loss, requiring no backbone retraining, no LoRA, and no large-scale paired data. At inference, the learned direction is added to the corresponding text token position with a continuous strength parameter γ, and multiple attribute directions can be composed additively for multi-attribute editing.

## Results

On the Control Dataset, ATA achieves the largest semantic shift toward the target attribute, reaching +14.58% ΔVQA at extreme intensity while maintaining +7.29% I-LPIPS for content preservation. On the GEdit benchmark, ATA attains the highest semantic consistency (+16.33% at moderate intensity) and disentanglement score (+9.68% at moderate), outperforming all baselines in multi-attribute editing. In the user study (2AFC, 30 participants), ATA is preferred 37% of the time for image preservation and 43% for prompt adherence, compared to 30%/23% for Qwen-Image and 23%/27% for VAREdit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATA outperforms all three baselines on the primary semantic-shift metrics. Against Concept-slider (LoRA-based, diffusion), ATA achieves roughly 2× the ΔVQA improvement (+14.58% vs. 0.572 absolute at extreme) and substantially higher disentanglement (+6.78% vs. 4.920), while also preserving content better at extreme intensity. Against Qwen-Image-Edit (20B DiT instruction editor), ATA leads by a wide margin on ΔVQA (+14.58% vs. +7.01% at extreme) and semantic consistency on GEdit (+16.33% vs. +6.20% at moderate), though Qwen-Image retains a slight edge on perceptual quality at extreme intensity (+6.18% vs. +3.64%). Against VAREdit (Infinity-8B instruction editor), ATA wins on ΔVQA (+14.58% vs. +6.95%), semantic consistency (+16.33% vs. +5.72% at moderate), and disentanglement (+9.68% vs. +4.52% at moderate), while VAREdit is marginally ahead on I-LPIPS at extreme (+6.37% vs. +7.29%—actually ATA leads here too). Notably, ATA requires no LoRA optimization or model fine-tuning, only ~500 steps of lightweight Delta-Mod optimization on a single reference image, yet surpasses instruction-based editors that were trained on large-scale paired datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct conceptual analogy from NLP word-embedding arithmetic (king + woman − man ≈ queen) and transplants it into the VAR-aligned text-token space of autoregressive image generators (VAR [37], Infinity [13]). It departs from prior AR editing methods—AREdit [41], which supports only single-attribute edits, and VAREdit [23], which requires fine-tuning on paired examples—by learning lightweight, disentangled semantic directions from a single reference image via a small attention module (Delta-Mod), without any backbone retraining. In the diffusion editing lineage, it is most comparable to Concept Slider [10] (continuous LoRA-based control) and TokenVerse [11] (token modulation), but achieves compositional, cross-category attribute transfer without per-attribute LoRA optimization or large-scale paired supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ATA offers a practically attractive recipe for fine-grained, continuous, and compositional control in AR image models: a single reference image, ~500 optimization steps on a tiny module, and no backbone fine-tuning. The demonstrated cross-category transfer (e.g., 'fat' learned from a cat applied to a chair) suggests the learned directions capture class-agnostic semantic axes, which could enable a reusable attribute library for interactive editing pipelines. However, the approach is currently validated only on the Infinity backbone, is limited by the generative prior (color edits and very strong attribute strengths degrade), and the hierarchical next-scale prediction of VAR appears to entangle certain spatially distributed attributes. Open questions include whether the token-arithmetic formulation generalizes to other AR architectures (e.g., MAR, HART) and whether the Delta-Mod directions remain stable under distribution shift or multi-identity composition at scale.
