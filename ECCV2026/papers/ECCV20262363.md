## Problem

Existing camouflaged object detection methods treat foreground appearance and background statistics as independent entities, either learning global object priors or matching against static references. This ignores the fundamental conditional nature of camouflage, where an object's detectability depends on how it deviates from its specific surrounding background rather than on any absolute visual signature. The paper asks whether COD can be reformulated as a scene-conditioned pattern-deviation reasoning problem that explicitly models this background-object coupling.

## Input / Output

At inference, PASR takes a single RGB query image (evaluated at 476×476) and an offline reference library of 315 web-sourced images with pre-computed foreground/background prototype pairs in a frozen DINOv2-ViT-L/14 embedding space. The output is a binary segmentation mask produced by KDE-based thresholding of a deviation response map, with an optional SAM refinement stage that converts the coarse mask into a bounding-box prompt for boundary precision. No gradient updates or task-specific training are performed; the entire pipeline is inference-time reasoning in a fixed feature space.

## Method

PASR operates in two stages. Offline, an annotation-free prototype library is built by clustering DINOv2 embeddings per reference image, applying KDE-adaptive thresholding to obtain coarse foreground masks, refining them with frozen SAM for structural coherence, and extracting masked spatial-mean foreground/background prototypes. At inference, a coarse BG-anchor stage matches the query's global descriptor against reference background prototypes to select a scene-aligned subset, then a patch-level stage retrieves top-K=32 background-aligned references per location, constructs a query-adaptive object prototype, and computes a background-conditioned pattern prototype as P_bg − α·P_obj (α=0.4). The final deviation response is the mean cosine similarity to these pattern prototypes, thresholded via KDE, followed by a query-self refinement step that re-derives prototypes from the initial mask to suppress cross-scene noise. The key distinction from prior retrieval-based COD is that object evidence is modeled as a structured perturbation relative to matched background statistics rather than as an independent foreground signal, and generalization is achieved through prototype-level expansion rather than parameter adaptation.

## Results

On the four COD benchmarks, PASR (pure reasoning, no SAM refinement) achieves S_α of 0.832/0.755/0.774/0.817 and F_βw of 0.768/0.693/0.664/0.760 on CHAMELEON/CAMO/COD10K/NC4K, outperforming the previous best unsupervised method EASE by up to +0.017 S_α (NC4K) and +0.025 F_βw (NC4K). With optional SAM-based boundary refinement, PASR reaches S_α=0.869 and F_βw=0.846 on CHAMELEON, surpassing all weakly-supervised and prompt-based baselines and approaching fully supervised models (e.g., CamoFormer: S_α=0.909, F_βw=0.871). The method operates entirely in a frozen DINOv2-ViT-L/14 embedding space with only 315 web reference images and K=32 retrieval, requiring no pixel-level annotations or task-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASR outperforms all eight unsupervised baselines (FreeSOLO, DiffCut, MaskCut, TokenCut, FOUND, VoteCut, RISE, EASE) on nearly every metric across all four datasets, with the largest margins over EASE on NC4K (S_α +0.017, F_βw +0.025) and CHAMELEON (E_φ +0.025). Against weakly-supervised methods (SCWS, TEL, WS-SAM, SEE), PASR with SAM refinement achieves higher S_α and F_βw on CHAMELEON (0.869/0.846 vs. best SEE 0.826/0.773) and NC4K (0.863/0.840 vs. best SEE 0.836/0.798), while trailing slightly on CAMO and COD10K. Among prompt-based methods, PASR surpasses ProMaC (S_α 0.869 vs. 0.833 on CHAMELEON) and GenSAM (0.869 vs. 0.767) as well as SAM-refined RISE and EASE. Against fully supervised models, PASR narrows the gap substantially: on CHAMELEON it reaches S_α=0.869 vs. HitNet's 0.921 and CamoFormer's 0.909, and on NC4K it achieves F_βw=0.840 vs. CamoFormer's 0.847, remaining within 1–5 points of the best supervised methods while requiring zero pixel-level annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant object-centric paradigm in COD (SINet, ZoomNet, FEDER) and from recent reference-based methods (EASE, RISE) that treat foreground and background statistics independently. It repurposes prototype learning and retrieval-based reasoning from few-shot segmentation, but introduces a key conceptual shift: rather than matching objects to category-stable prototypes, it constructs background-conditioned pattern prototypes (P_bg − α·P_obj) that encode structured deviations relative to scene-specific background anchors. The entire pipeline operates in a frozen DINO-v2 embedding space with annotation-free prototype construction via self-reasoning and KDE-based separation, avoiding both retraining and pixel-level supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a fully unsupervised, retrieval-based method using only 315 web images can approach the performance of fully supervised COD models, suggesting that scene-conditioned deviation reasoning provides a strong inductive bias that reduces reliance on large-scale pixel annotations. The efficiency finding (315 references vs. 16,000 for EASE) is practically significant for deployment. Open questions include whether the single-object, single-deviation formulation generalizes to multi-object camouflage or synthetic/artificial camouflage, and whether the approach transfers to video COD or non-natural scenes where background statistics are less structured.
