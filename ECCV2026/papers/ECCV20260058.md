## Problem

Existing VLA models regress robot actions directly from 2D semantic VLM features, forcing them to infer complex 3D physical interactions implicitly from action labels alone. This implicit strategy degrades under unfamiliar spatial dynamics, particularly on tasks requiring precise depth control or when camera viewpoints and object positions shift from training. The paper asks whether dense 3D scene flow can serve as an explicit latent motion prior that grounds VLA policy learning in physical geometry without the latency of full multi-step reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image and a natural-language instruction, encoded by a frozen Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct backbone. Depth is used only offline during data labeling (via TraceForge) and is not required at inference. The output is a continuous action chunk A_{t:t+H} predicted via flow matching in the action space; in real-world deployment this corresponds to a 6-DoF end-effector pose delta plus a 1-DoF gripper command. Internally, the Motion Expert generates a dense 3D scene-flow tensor M_t ∈ R^{400×32×3} (400 keypoints × 32 future timesteps × (Δu, Δv, Δd) displacements in a unified camera frame), but only one-step partially denoised hidden states (at τ=0.1 of a 10-step ODE) are passed to the Action Expert.

## Method

LaMP introduces a dual-expert architecture where a CogVideoX-style 3D transformer Motion Expert learns conditional flow matching over 3D scene flow, conditioned on VLM features via Adaptive LayerNorm, and a flow-matching Action Expert predicts robot actions conditioned on motion-guided VLM features. The key efficiency insight is that full multi-step denoising is unnecessary: a single ODE step (τ=0→0.1) suffices to extract intermediate hidden states that retain task-relevant dynamics while avoiding prohibitive latency. These motion hidden states are fused into the last-layer VLM features through a single-layer gated cross-attention with a learnable scalar gate initialized at zero, so the model starts from near-zero motion injection and adaptively increases guidance only when spatial precision is required, preventing representational collapse of pretrained VLM features. Training is two-stage: the Motion Expert is pretrained on 1.6M observation-language-motion triplets from LIBERO, BridgeV2, DROID, and InternData-A1, then frozen while the Action Expert and guidance module are trained with the action flow-matching loss alone. At inference, the pipeline runs four sequential steps (VLM encoding → one-step motion denoising → gated cross-attention fusion → action denoising) with only 1.35× latency overhead over the base VLA.

## Results

On LIBERO, LaMP achieves the highest average success rate of 98.3%, surpassing the strongest prior baselines GeoVLA (97.7%) and OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%), with the largest margin on LIBERO-Long (96.7% vs. 92.4% for π₀.₅). On SimplerEnv-WidowX, LaMP reaches 79.2% average success, outperforming FlowVLA (74.0%) by 5.2 points and π₀.₅ (57.1%) by 22.1 points. On LIBERO-Plus zero-shot OOD perturbations, LaMP attains 79.3% average, exceeding the best prior method OpenVLA-OFT (69.6%) by 9.7 percentage points, with the largest gains on Camera (+8.1) and Robot (+37.7) perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaMP outperforms all 16 compared baselines on every benchmark where they overlap. On LIBERO, the closest competitors are GeoVLA (97.7% avg) and OpenVLA-OFT (97.1% avg); LaMP leads by 0.6 and 1.2 points respectively, with a decisive 4.3-point margin on LIBERO-Long (96.7% vs. 92.4% for π₀.₅). On SimplerEnv-WidowX, LaMP (79.2%) beats the strongest 2D-flow baseline FlowVLA (74.0%) by 5.2 points and the best video-based method F₁ (72.9%) by 6.3 points, while general VLAs like π₀.₅ (57.1%) and GR00T N1 (49.5%) trail by 22+ points. On LIBERO-Plus OOD, LaMP (79.3%) surpasses OpenVLA-OFT (69.6%) by 9.7 points, with particularly large gains on Robot perturbation (69.6% vs. 21.6% for π₀-Fast) and Camera (64.5% vs. 56.4% for OpenVLA-OFT). In real-world experiments, LaMP (80% Pick-and-Place, 50% Deformable, 80% Long-Horizon) consistently exceeds π₀ (70/40/70%) and 3D FDP (53/40/60%), and under OOD conditions LaMP degrades only 17.5 points while 3D FDP collapses by 26.2 points. The ablation (w/o motion) confirms the motion prior is the primary driver: removing it drops LIBERO-Long by 18.5 points and SimplerEnv average by 22.9 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaMP sits at the intersection of flow-matching generative modeling (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023), video-generation transformer architectures (CogVideoX-style 3D transformers), and the partial-denoising conditioning strategy from VPP and MimicVideo, repurposing these for robotic manipulation. It departs from prior 3D-aware VLAs (GeoVLA, SpatialVLA) that inject only current-frame geometry by instead generating future chunk-level dense 3D scene flow as a latent prior, and from standalone 3D world-model pipelines (3DFlowAction, 3D FDP, TraceGen) by embedding the flow prediction as an internal module within a pretrained VLA rather than a separate per-task system. The gated cross-attention fusion mechanism is a direct response to the observed representational collapse when 3D features are naively injected into VLM layers, distinguishing LaMP from prior attempts at multi-modal feature fusion in VLA architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LaMP provides a concrete, efficient recipe for closing the 2D-semantic-to-3D-dynamic gap in VLA models: the one-step partial denoising adds only 1.35× inference latency while yielding substantial OOD robustness gains (9.7% on LIBERO-Plus) and cross-embodiment transfer without task-specific retraining. The finding that unweighted motion injection actively harms performance (41.7% drop on Stack Block with naive addition) and that selective gating is essential is a practically useful design principle for any future work injecting auxiliary modalities into pretrained VLMs. Open questions include whether the fixed-resolution, fixed-horizon motion prior can be made adaptive, whether motion supervision can be learned from unlabeled video without TraceForge, and whether multi-layer fusion (currently avoided due to collapse) can be stabilized with better regularization.
