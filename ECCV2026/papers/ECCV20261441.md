## Problem

Existing Gaussian Splatting-based inverse rendering methods (e.g., Ref-Gaussian, GI-GS) assume dense multi-view captures and degrade severely under sparse-view settings (4–16 views), where limited observations create strong ambiguity between geometry, reflectance, and lighting. This leads to overfitted material maps, baked specular reflections, and poor relighting. The paper addresses this gap by integrating multiple foundation model priors into a physically-based inverse rendering optimization to stabilize both geometry and material recovery from sparse inputs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes as few as 4–16 RGB images with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, plus optional alpha masks for outlier removal. It outputs a 2D Gaussian Splatting geometry with per-primitive PBR material parameters (albedo, roughness, metallicity) and a 128×128 environment cubemap, enabling both novel-view synthesis and relighting under arbitrary environment maps. All hyperparameters are fixed across scenes without per-scene tuning or prior fine-tuning.

## Method

GAINS is a two-stage optimization pipeline built on 2D Gaussian Splatting with a split-sum PBR shading model. Stage I regularizes geometry using monocular depth/normal losses (PCC and L1 against a depth/normal estimator), a depth ranking loss, and Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from a latent diffusion model applied only after 10,000 iterations to suppress high-frequency artifacts. Stage II jointly optimizes material and lighting with three complementary priors: (1) segmentation guidance that lifts SAM masks with CLIP/DINOv2 features onto Gaussians to enforce intra-class variance reduction on roughness and metallicity, (2) an Intrinsic Image Decomposition prior (Teamwork or RGB2X) that regularizes albedo with a linearly decaying weight to tolerate view-inconsistency, and (3) a Multi-illuminated SDS (MI-SDS) loss that renders under randomly sampled environment maps from a fixed set to improve generalization to novel views and lighting. The key insight is that each prior addresses a distinct failure mode—segmentation gives cross-view specular consistency, IID gives albedo accuracy, and diffusion gives generalization—and only their combination yields robust, physically consistent inverse rendering under sparse observations.

## Results

On Synthetic4Relight (8 views), GAINS achieves 29.32 PSNR for novel-view synthesis, 22.34 PSNR for albedo estimation, and 28.16 PSNR for relighting, outperforming the best baselines (GI-GS: 27.04/20.47/23.92) by 2.3/1.9/4.2 dB respectively. On Shiny Blender (8 views), GAINS reaches 23.90 PSNR (NVS), 21.73 PSNR (albedo), and 22.29 PSNR (relighting), surpassing the next-best methods (R3DG: 20.97/16.62/12.43) by substantial margins. On the real-world Ref-Real dataset, GAINS achieves 20.28 PSNR for NVS, exceeding GI-GS (19.64) and Ref-Gaussian (19.39). GAINS also attains the best normal MAE (11.64) on Shiny Blender, nearly halving the error of the next-best method (R3DG: 24.43).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAINS outperforms all six baselines on NVS, albedo, and relighting across both synthetic datasets. Against the strongest competitor GI-GS, GAINS gains +2.3 dB on NVS PSNR and +4.2 dB on relighting PSNR (Synthetic4Relight), and +3.8 dB on relighting (Shiny Blender: 22.29 vs 15.44), indicating GI-GS's NVS advantage partly stems from overfitting rather than accurate material recovery. Against Ref-Gaussian, GAINS improves relighting by +3.9 dB (Synthetic4Relight) and +5.0 dB (Shiny Blender), with the paper attributing Ref-Gaussian's weakness to baked specular reflections that do not change under different lighting. R3DG is the closest competitor on Synthetic4Relight (27.42 vs 28.16 relighting PSNR), but GAINS still leads on NVS (+2.4 dB) and albedo (+0.2 dB). SVG-IR achieves the best roughness MSE (0.03 vs 0.04) but trails GAINS by 5.1 dB on NVS and 2.9 dB on relighting. On the real-world Ref-Real dataset, GAINS leads all methods in NVS (20.28 vs 19.64 for GI-GS), and qualitative comparisons show reduced reflection baking and more accurate directional shading under novel illumination.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAINS extends the Gaussian-splatting-based inverse rendering line (Ref-Gaussian, GI-GS, GaussianShader) by explicitly targeting sparse-view settings where those methods overfit and fail to disentangle reflectance from lighting. It borrows the two-stage geometry-then-material pipeline from prior Gaussian IR work but augments each stage with foundation-model priors drawn from the sparse-view reconstruction literature (monocular depth/normal estimation, SAM-based segmentation, latent diffusion SDS). The key conceptual departure is the deliberate synergy of three complementary material priors—segmentation for cross-view consistency, IID for albedo accuracy, and multi-illuminated SDS for generalization—rather than relying on a single regularization, which prior Gaussian IR methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sparse-view inverse rendering is practically critical because real-world captures are often limited to a handful of viewpoints, yet existing Gaussian IR methods degrade sharply in this regime. GAINS demonstrates that a carefully designed combination of complementary learning priors can close much of the gap to dense-view performance, offering a reusable recipe for stabilizing physically-based optimization under data scarcity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to global illumination and inter-reflections, whether the two-stage pipeline can be unified, and whether the specific prior combination (SAM + Teamwork/RGB2X + SDS) is optimal or whether other foundation models could substitute. The authors also note residual wavy normals on highly specular surfaces that bake some specularity into albedo, pointing to a separation of geometric and shading normals as future work.
