## Problem

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from an 'intention-action gap' where generated actions misalign with language instructions, and existing remedies—scaling policy pre-training with rephrased instructions or larger VLM backbones—yield only incremental gains while risking catastrophic forgetting of the VLM's multimodal understanding. This paper asks whether allocating compute to test-time verification rather than policy training can more effectively close this gap, and characterizes the test-time scaling laws governing how linguistic diversity and action sampling jointly affect recovery of correct behavior.

## Input / Output

At boot time, an off-the-shelf VLM (GPT-4o) consumes the initial RGB scene image and user instruction to produce K=8 rephrased instructions with scene-level reasoning, which are cached as embeddings. At each chunk boundary during deployment, the system takes the current RGB observation, the cached rephrase set, and a short-term action history (past C actions) as input; the base VLA (π₀ or π₀.5) samples M=5 action chunks per rephrase, yielding K×M=40 proposals. The output is a hierarchically selected pair: the highest-scoring rephrased instruction (by mean verifier score across its M actions) and the single highest-scoring action chunk within that rephrase, which is then executed.

## Method

CoVer is a 1B-parameter contrastive verifier trained with bi-directional InfoNCE loss, where a frozen SigLIP2 vision-text encoder (with text-aware visual attention) produces instruction-relevant visual-language embeddings and a transformer action encoder captures temporal dependencies in action chunks; both are ℓ₂-normalized and aligned via in-batch contrastive learning, so other samples in the batch serve as implicit negatives without requiring curated failure trajectories. Training uses rephrase augmentation from Open-X Embodiment (16× synthetic instructions per task) applied to Bridge V2, with a batch size of 32,768 over 2k steps on 8 H200 GPUs. At test time, CoVer-VLA performs hierarchical verification: it first selects the rephrase whose induced action distribution has the highest mean alignment score, then picks the single best-scoring action chunk within that rephrase, effectively decoupling high-level prompt optimization from low-level action selection. A key empirical finding is that scaling verification (more rephrases, more action candidates, larger verifier ensembles) outperforms scaling policy pre-training at matched inference FLOPs, with the hybrid strategy of jointly scaling rephrases and per-rephrase samples recovering correct actions more efficiently than scaling either dimension alone.

## Results

On the SIMPLER benchmark, CoVer-VLA (π0 + CoVer) achieves 65.5% success on in-distribution tasks and 62.0% on out-of-distribution tasks, yielding 22% and 13% absolute gains over scaling policy pre-training on the same data. On the PolaRiS benchmark with the stronger π0.5 base policy, CoVer improves average task progress from 40.0% to 53.9% (+13.9%) and success rate from 3.8% to 13.1% (+9.3%). In real-world experiments on a WidowX robot, CoVer-VLA delivers a 45% absolute improvement in task success rate over the baseline policy. At matched inference FLOPs (K=8), CoVer outperforms temperature sampling and best-of-N by +23 to +43% success on OOD SIMPLER tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base π0 policy (41.5% ID, 29.7% OOD on SIMPLER), CoVer-VLA achieves 65.5% ID and 62.0% OOD, a 24-point and 32-point absolute improvement respectively. Compared to training-time instruction augmentation (π0 rephrase [10], 44% ID), CoVer-VLA's test-time verification yields substantially larger gains (15% ID, 12% OOD advantage over the augmented policy) while requiring less compute. RoboMonkey [22], a 7B-scale verifier with action resampling, actually degrades below the base π0 policy on SIMPLER because its step-level verification disrupts temporally coherent flow-based action chunks and its OpenVLA-trained preferences transfer poorly to π0. On PolaRiS, π0.5 + CoVer dominates π0.5 across all three tasks (PanClean: 70.4% vs 48.4% task progress; BlockStack: 44.3% vs 33.1%; FoodBussing: 47.0% vs 38.3%), with the largest relative gain on PanClean where success rate triples from 10.7% to 33.3%. The paper further shows that combining both training-time augmentation and test-time verification (π0 rephrase + CoVer) is complementary, achieving the strongest overall performance, confirming the two approaches address different failure modes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VLA model family (π0, OpenVLA) and the emerging test-time scaling paradigm in robotics (RoboMonkey, value guidance, bidirectional decoding), but departs from prior verifiers that focus on low-level dynamics or step-level consistency by reframing action verification as a contrastive cross-modal alignment problem between language and behavior. It also extends the instruction-rephrasing literature [10, 42] by showing that the same rephrase data used to augment policy training is more effective when used to train a separate verifier. The hierarchical structure—jointly optimizing over language prompts and action chunks—is a novel combination not present in prior test-time scaling work, which typically operates at a single level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding that allocating compute to test-time verification outperforms allocating it to policy pre-training on the same data challenges the dominant scaling paradigm in robotics and provides practitioners with a concrete, deployable framework (CoVer-VLA) that requires no access to base-policy internals. The 45% real-world improvement and iso-compute advantage over best-of-N sampling make this practically significant for deployment. Key limitations remain: the verifier cannot recover correct behavior if no feasible action appears in the proposal set, the method operates at chunk granularity (leaving intra-chunk motor deviations to other verifiers), and the boot-time VLM call (~11s) introduces a one-time latency cost. Open questions include whether the contrastive verifier can be extended to RL post-training, whether it composes with dynamics-based verifiers for contact-rich tasks, and whether more efficient verifier architectures can further reduce the ~8ms per-candidate overhead.
