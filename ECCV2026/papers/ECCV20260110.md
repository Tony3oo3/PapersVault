## Problem

Existing visuomotor policies model either historical context or future dynamics in isolation, lacking a unified temporal representation of object-gripper interaction. This fragmentation limits performance in non-Markovian, long-horizon, and deformable-object manipulation tasks where decisions depend on how the hand and object have moved and how they should move next.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260110/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a single RGB-D point cloud (N×6: xyz + rgb, variable N per frame), optional proprioceptive state, and historical ChronoFlow trajectories (sparse 3D keypoints of gripper and objects tracked asynchronously via TAPIP3D to avoid a runtime bottleneck). It outputs an 8-step action sequence of end-effector pose increments (only the first 4 executed in a receding-horizon scheme) and a predicted future object-gripper keypoint trajectory of shape (N_g+N_o)×H×3. No 2D image inputs are used; all perception is 3D point-cloud based.

## Method

ChronoFlow represents interaction state as sparse 3D keypoints rigidly attached to the gripper TCP and FPS-sampled on SAM-2-segmented objects, tracked over time by TAPIP3D to form unified past-current-future trajectories. ChronoFlow-Policy factorizes the policy into a diffusion-based interaction model π_θ that denoises future ChronoFlow conditioned on current observation and historical flows, and a lightweight transformer action decoder π_φ that maps the denoised interaction tokens to actions. A multi-head cross-attention encoder jointly encodes historical and noisy future ChronoFlow into compact per-keypoint tokens, which a Unet or DiT backbone progressively denoises. Training uses a co-objective L = L_action + λ_CF·L_CF: the diffusion loss shapes the backbone into a spatiotemporal interaction model, while the action loss (computed on partially denoised tokens plus an approximate trajectory reconstruction) aligns the shared latent with control, avoiding the need to complete full diffusion at every optimization step.

## Results

On MetaWorld (7 tasks), CFP (Unet) achieves a 72% average success rate, substantially outperforming DP3 (30%), 3D-FDP (34%), and MBA (47%). On RoboTwin 2.0 (7 dual-arm tasks), CFP (Unet) reaches 66% average, surpassing DP3 (43%), 3D-FDP (47%), and MBA (56%). In real-world experiments across 5 tasks, CFP achieves 93% on all three stages of Swap-Easy and 94%/94%/61% on Swap-Hard, while CFP w/o past drops to 93%/33%/20% and 94%/22%/11% respectively, confirming the critical role of historical ChronoFlow for non-Markovian decisions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFP (Unet) decisively outperforms all baselines on both simulation benchmarks: on MetaWorld it leads DP3 by 42 points (72% vs 30%), 3D-FDP by 38 points (72% vs 34%), and MBA by 25 points (72% vs 47%); on RoboTwin 2.0 the margins are 23, 19, and 10 points respectively. In real-world experiments, CFP dominates on non-Markovian tasks: on Swap-Easy it maintains 93% across all three stages while RISE collapses from 87% to 13% by Stage III, 3D-FDP drops from 93% to 33%, and even CFP w/o past (no history) falls to 20%. HistRISE [3] is the closest real-world competitor, reaching 80%/80% on Swap-Easy Stages II–III, but CFP still leads by 13 points in each stage. On the deformable-object task Fold Towel, CFP achieves 87% in both stages versus RISE's 40% in Stage II, demonstrating that interaction-centric keypoint flows handle geometry changes better than point-cloud or pose-based representations. MBA [25] is only evaluated on Prepare Breakfast (where object poses are well-defined), and CFP still leads by 6 and 7 points in Stages I and II respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work in visuomotor policy learning: future-dynamics prediction (MBA's object pose trajectories, 3D-FDP's dense scene flow) and history-aware context encoding (HistRISE's tracked points, TraceVLA's visual traces). Rather than treating past and future as independent conditioning signals, ChronoFlow represents both as a single sparse 3D keypoint trajectory spanning past-current-future for objects and gripper jointly, then uses it as a co-training diffusion objective alongside action prediction. The method repurposes existing 3D tracking (TAPIP3D) and segmentation (SAM-2) infrastructure to extract these trajectories, and builds on the diffusion-policy architecture (DP3/RISE) as its base, adding a flow-matching denoising step over interaction keypoints as auxiliary supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation of interaction-centric sparse keypoints from dense scene flow addresses a practical redundancy problem, and the co-training formulation provides structured supervision that encourages the shared latent to encode temporally grounded dynamics rather than action labels alone. The dramatic non-Markovian results (Swap-Easy: 93% across all stages vs. 13% for RISE in Stage III) demonstrate that explicit history in a physically grounded representation is far more effective than implicit video-based memory for stage-dependent decisions. Open questions include robustness under severe occlusion or persistent tracking failure, whether the representation generalizes to multi-object or articulated scenes beyond the tested tasks, and whether the async tracking pipeline (5.82 Hz) is sufficient for faster or more dexterous manipulation.
