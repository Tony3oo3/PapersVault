## Problem

Conventional diffusion-based image editing models rely solely on text prompts, which cannot unambiguously localize target objects in complex multi-object scenes, leading to imprecise editing and unintended background changes. Existing bounding-box-guided methods (e.g., Qwen-Image-Edit, LongCat-Image-Edit) still suffer from boundary leakage and residual visual artifacts, failing to achieve precise, artifact-free editing within strictly defined regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261476/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a source RGB image (resized to 1024×1024), one or more bounding boxes specifying the editing region(s), and a text editing prompt. The bounding box is converted into a binary mask (1 outside, 0 inside) that is applied to the source image to create a masked variant; this masked image and the original are both VAE-encoded and fed into the diffusion backbone. Inference uses classifier-free guidance at scale 4 with 30 sampling steps, and the same interface supports local, global (full-canvas bbox), and hybrid (multi-bbox) editing without architectural changes.

## Method

FineEdit builds on the Qwen-Image MM-DiT flow-matching backbone and introduces a multi-level bounding-box injection mechanism combining early fusion (channel-wise concatenation of the masked-source latent with the noisy target latent at the input) and deep fusion (a 6-layer side adapter that injects masked-latent features additively into intermediate transformer blocks). Training proceeds in two stages: supervised pre-training on the 1.2M-pair FineEdit-1.2M dataset (64 H100 GPUs, 15K steps, AdamW at 5e-5), followed by RL post-training using LoRA (rank 32) with a decoupled reward that separately scores foreground editing fidelity (via Qwen3-VL on the masked ROI) and background preservation (via VLM score plus normalized PSNR on the complement mask), resolving the reward misalignment of conventional global VLM scoring.

## Results

On the FineEdit-1k Bench, FineEdit achieves PSNR 34.4, SSIM 0.91, and LPIPS 0.08 for background preservation, along with PC 4.67, VN 4.71, and PDI 4.69 for bounding-box editing accuracy, outperforming all 12 baselines. The RL post-trained variant FineEdit-r1 further improves to PSNR 34.7, OBR 4.89, PC 4.80, VN 4.83, and PDI 4.81. On ImgEdit-Bench, FineEdit scores 4.50 overall (vs. 4.35 for Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 and 4.20 for GPT-4o-Image), and on GEdit-Bench-EN it leads in semantic consistency (G_SC 8.71 vs. 8.15) and overall score (G_O 7.97 vs. 7.54) over the strongest open-source baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FineEdit dominates all 12 baselines on the FineEdit-1k Bench across both background preservation and editing accuracy metrics. The most direct competitors—Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 and LongCat-Image-Edit—achieve comparable editing accuracy (PC 4.56 and 4.59 respectively) but suffer substantially worse background preservation (PSNR 27.1 and 27.3 vs. FineEdit's 34.4, a gap of over 7 dB), confirming that existing bbox-guided methods still leak edits into the background. On ImgEdit-Bench, FineEdit (4.50 overall) surpasses Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 (4.35), GPT-4o-Image (4.20), and Kontext-pro (4.00), with particular strength in Replace (4.83) and Remove (4.65) tasks. On GEdit-Bench-EN, FineEdit leads in G_SC (8.71 vs. 8.15 for Qwen-Image-Edit-2509) and G_O (7.97 vs. 7.54), though it trails slightly in G_PQ (7.47 vs. 7.86), suggesting a minor trade-off in perceptual quality on this broader benchmark. Inpainting-based baselines (BrushNet, BrushEdit, PrefPaint, FluPA) consistently underperform on editing accuracy (PC 2.74–3.68) despite competitive background scores, reflecting their design focus on content restoration rather than semantic modification.

## Relation to Prior Work

FineEdit builds directly on the Qwen-Image architecture (MM-DiT backbone with Qwen2.5-VL encoder) and extends the visual-conditioning paradigm established by ControlNet and GLIGEN from holistic structural guidance to fine-grained, object-level editing. It departs from the dominant text-only instruction paradigm (InstructPix2Pix, Qwen-Image-Edit, LongCat-Image-Edit) by introducing a multi-level bounding-box injection mechanism (early channel concatenation plus a deep side-adapter) that explicitly localizes the editing region. The decoupled RL post-training strategy further distinguishes the work from prior global-reward RL approaches by separating foreground editing fidelity from background preservation, addressing a reward-misalignment problem specific to region-constrained editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in diffusion-based editing: text prompts cannot unambiguously specify targets in multi-object scenes, and existing bbox-accepting models suffer from boundary leakage and artifacts. The 1.2M dataset with precise spatial annotations and the FineEdit-Bench provide much-needed resources for training and evaluating region-constrained editing. However, the reliance on axis-aligned bounding boxes limits applicability to irregular shapes and path-based edits, and the paper itself acknowledges this as a key limitation, pointing toward future extensions to polygons, scribbles, and point-based prompts.
