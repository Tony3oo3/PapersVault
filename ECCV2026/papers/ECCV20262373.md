## Problem

When input views are sparse or target viewpoints lie far from training views, every 3D representation (3DGS, NeRF, mesh, point cloud) produces artifacts that limit downstream use. Existing generative-prior fixes are specialized to individual representations, each requiring custom architectures, bespoke conditioning, and large-scale paired data. The paper asks whether a single generalist video model, with minimal adaptation, can replace this growing family of specialist refinement pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262373/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 61-frame rendering video at 480×832 produced along an arbitrary camera trajectory from any of four 3D representations, together with a per-frame binary mask (not per-pixel) marking which frames correspond to clean training viewpoints. It outputs a photorealistic, temporally coherent video at the same resolution and trajectory. Videos longer than 61 frames are processed in overlapping chunks, and the same LoRA weights handle all four representation types—only the rendered input changes.

## Method

FixAnything repurposes the Wan2.1-I2V-14B DiT video diffusion model (rectified flow) by channel-concatenating the VAE-encoded degraded video latent and a spatially broadcast binary mask alongside the noised target latent, then trains a rank-64 LoRA adapter (<1% of parameters) with a standard flow-matching loss. The key insight is that even heavily degraded renderings preserve camera motion and coarse scene layout, so cleanup reduces to video-to-video translation that a pretrained video prior can perform with only ~20 paired training videos. A second stage applies Flow-DPO using camera-pose recovery accuracy (AUC@5° from COLMAP with SuperPoint/LightGlue) as the reward, constructing preference pairs from multi-seed outputs to bake multi-view geometric consistency into the LoRA weights at no additional inference cost.

## Results

On DL3DV-10K (6 training views), FixAnything with 3DGS input achieves 17.65 PSNR, 0.561 SSIM, and 0.289 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest post-hoc enhancement baselines (3DGS-Enhancer: 16.94 PSNR; Xu et al.: 17.35 PSNR). Remarkably, the same single model applied to sparse COLMAP point cloud renderings achieves 17.74 PSNR and to mesh renderings 17.95 PSNR, matching or exceeding the 3DGS-input variant despite far less visual information in the input. Flow-DPO further improves geometric consistency (AUC@5°) from 61.12 to 68.32 (+7.2%) at no additional inference cost. With only 20 paired training videos, the model already reaches 17.20 PSNR, and reducing denoising steps from 50 to 5 yields a 10× speedup (31 s per 61-frame clip at 480×832) with minimal quality loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against sparse-view reconstruction baselines (3DGS, RegNeRF, FreeNeRF, DNGaussian, FSGS), FixAnything achieves 2.4–5.5 dB higher PSNR across all view counts, confirming that generative cleanup substantially outperforms regularization-only approaches. Among post-hoc enhancement methods, FixAnything (3DGS input) surpasses 3DGS-Enhancer by 0.71 dB PSNR at 6 views and 1.26 dB at 9 views, and outperforms Difix3D+ by 3.24 dB at 6 views. Compared to Xu et al. (the closest competitor), FixAnything leads on 3-view (15.18 vs. 14.62 PSNR) and 6-view (17.65 vs. 17.35) settings but trails slightly on 9-view PSNR (19.76 vs. 19.19), while achieving better LPIPS at all settings. The most striking result is that FixAnything with sparse COLMAP point cloud input (17.74 PSNR at 6 views) and mesh input (17.95 PSNR) match or exceed the 3DGS-input variant, despite these representations providing far less dense visual information. On MipNeRF-360 and LLFF (supplementary), FixAnything with 3DGS input achieves comparable performance to SOTA methods with a notable LPIPS improvement, demonstrating cross-dataset generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Wan2.1 video diffusion model (a DiT-based rectified-flow image-to-video generator) for rendering cleanup via a lightweight LoRA adapter and channel-concatenated conditioning, departing from prior specialist pipelines (3DGS-Enhancer, Difix3D+, ViewCrafter) that each target a single 3D representation with custom architectures. The geometry-aware preference optimization stage adapts Flow-DPO from its original text-alignment setting to use SfM camera-pose accuracy as the reward signal, a novel application of DPO to 3D consistency. Conceptually, the paper reframes artifact removal across heterogeneous 3D representations as a unified video-to-video translation problem, leveraging the shared property that all degraded renderings preserve camera trajectory and coarse layout.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that sparse COLMAP point clouds (scattered keypoints only) yield rendering quality comparable to full 3DGS or NeRF inputs after generative cleanup challenges the standard pipeline assumption that a dense 3D representation is a necessary intermediate step. The extreme data efficiency (20 paired videos vs. 80K–150K for prior methods) and architectural simplicity (LoRA only, <1% parameters) mean the approach can be immediately re-applied to future video foundation models without redesign. Open questions include whether the point-cloud-sufficiency result generalizes beyond DL3DV-10K's indoor scenes, how to control hallucination in unobserved regions (the paper offers only a preliminary uncertainty analysis), and whether generative predictions can be fed back into reconstruction to produce more complete 3D models.
