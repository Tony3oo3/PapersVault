## Problem

Existing single-image distortion correction methods optimize exclusively for 2D pixel-level alignment (flow losses, reconstruction errors), producing rectifications that look visually plausible but fail to preserve underlying 3D scene geometry. Without explicit 3D structural supervision, the learned displacement fields can induce non-physical stretching and fractured depth topology, degrading downstream 3D vision tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single distorted RGB image (H×W×3) and outputs a rectified perspective image along with a dense displacement flow field (H×W×2) that maps each rectified pixel back to its distorted source location via differentiable bilinear warping. Training uses synthetic distortions generated by combining the first four polynomial coefficients with the division model, applied to Wireframe and Places365 images. The frozen VGGT foundation model is used only during training for geometric supervision and is entirely removed at inference, keeping the deployed network lightweight.

## Method

G2DC employs an encoder-decoder rectification network that predicts a dense displacement field, guided by a frozen Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) that provides multi-dimensional 3D geometric supervision. The training objective jointly optimizes a flow loss, a Structural Alignment Loss (L_sa) that minimizes feature discrepancy in VGGT's high-dimensional latent space between the rectified output and ground truth, and a Physical Alignment Loss (L_pa) composed of three explicit 3D constraints: a curvature-based collinearity penalty on VGGT's predicted point map, an edge-aware depth smoothness loss enforcing valid depth topology, and a camera intrinsic parameter regularization tying the deformation to a globally consistent pinhole projection. This design replaces hand-crafted geometric primitives (lines, arcs, vanishing points) with dense 3D model priors, enabling robust rectification even in texture-less scenes where traditional structural cues are unavailable.

## Results

On the combined Wireframe + Places365 test set (6,000 images), G2DC achieves a PSNR of 28.68 dB, SSIM of 0.95, and MS-SSIM of 0.96, outperforming the strongest baseline RDTR (27.92 dB, 0.93, 0.94) across all standard metrics. On perceptual metrics, G2DC attains the lowest FID (19.8) and LPIPS scores (0.066 AlexNet, 0.105 VGG), compared to RDTR's 21.4 FID and 0.075/0.116 LPIPS. In 3D-specific evaluation, G2DC with VGGT supervision reduces Depth-MAE to 0.098 and raises the Structural Score to 0.4321, versus 0.420 and 0.1268 for the Blind baseline. The deployed model is lightweight (15.72M params, 18.45G FLOPs) and runs at 10.42 FPS on an RTX 2080Ti, faster than all generative baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G2DC outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. The closest competitor is RDTR [26], which G2DC surpasses by 0.76 dB in PSNR, 0.02 in SSIM, and 1.6 in FID, indicating that explicit 3D geometric supervision yields measurable gains even over a method already incorporating geometric priors. Against PCN [30], the gap widens to 4.12 dB PSNR and 37.7 FID, while SimFIR [9] trails by 6.17 dB PSNR. The regression-based methods (DCCNN [22], DeepCalib [4]) and earlier generative approaches (Blind [15], DR-GAN [18]) are substantially outperformed, with PSNR gaps ranging from 5.5 to 13.6 dB. In the 3D-specific evaluation (Table 2), G2DC with VGGT supervision achieves a Depth-MAE of 0.098 and Structural Score of 0.4321, compared to 0.420 and 0.1268 for Blind [15], confirming that the advantage is most pronounced on metrics sensitive to 3D geometric consistency rather than purely pixel-level fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep-learning-based distortion correction lineage (DC-CNN, DeepCalib, Blind, DR-GAN, PCN, SimFIR, RDTR), all of which optimize 2D pixel-level objectives such as flow L1/L2 losses or image reconstruction errors. The key departure is the introduction of a frozen 3D geometry foundation model (VGGT) as a training-time supervisor, shifting the optimization from surface-level appearance matching to explicit 3D structural consistency via two new loss terms: a Structural Alignment Loss in VGGT's latent space and a Physical Alignment Loss over predicted point maps, depth, and camera intrinsics. Conceptually, this repurposes the frozen-feature-extractor paradigm (analogous to DINOv2-based perceptual losses) but substitutes a 2D visual model with a 3D geometric one, and it avoids the hand-crafted geometric primitives (lines, arcs, vanishing points) that earlier calibration-based methods relied upon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 2D pixel alignment alone produces rectifications with fractured 3D topology, and that injecting 3D foundation-model supervision closes this gap with a modest, lightweight network (15.72M params, 10.42 FPS) that requires no 3D model at inference. The loss generalization results (Table 5) suggest the geometric supervision is transferable across architectures, which could benefit other geometric-correction tasks. However, the main limitation is the synthetic-to-real gap: training uses polynomial and division distortion models, and the authors acknowledge that real fisheye lenses and extreme out-of-distribution cases remain challenging. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-view or video rectification, and whether end-to-end joint training with a 3D model (rather than frozen supervision) could further close the real-world gap.
