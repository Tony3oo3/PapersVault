## Problem

Accurate 3D surface reconstruction of moving bumblebees is severely hampered by their dense dark hairs, highly articulated limbs (27 degrees of freedom), and rapid motion that cause frequent self-occlusion and make ground-truth annotation nearly impossible. Existing template-free or generic statistical-shape methods lack the anatomical fidelity and biological constraints needed to produce structurally consistent, temporally stable reconstructions of such small, complex insects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262673/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-view RGB video (6 cameras at 1000×800, 200 FPS, 0.035 mm/pixel precision) together with a pre-built micro-CT articulated mesh template (4,070 vertices, 8,136 faces, 63 bones, 41 anatomical keypoints) and per-frame 2D keypoint detections from DeepLabCut plus segmentation masks from HQ-SAM. It outputs a temporally consistent 3D mesh sequence with per-vertex RGB texture, 41 3D pose keypoints per frame, and learned shape (per-bone anisotropic scaling) and pose (per-bone axis-angle rotations) parameters. Camera calibration is performed automatically via incremental SfM from the 2D detections rather than requiring manual millimeter-scale calibration.

## Method

BioMTBee builds a high-fidelity articulated bumblebee template from micro-CT scans using Marching Cubes, retopology, and a 63-bone kinematic tree with linear blend skinning, providing an anatomically grounded parameter space. A multi-view 3D pose estimator (BPST) triangulates 2D DeepLabCut detections across views using automatic incremental SfM calibration (RANSAC-PnP + bundle adjustment), then refines 3D keypoints via a robust reprojection loss combined with temporal smoothness and segment-length consistency to handle occlusion and noise. The core reconstruction is a differentiable-rendering optimization in two stages: Stage I fits shape (per-bone anisotropic scaling β), global transform, and per-vertex color to a frame subset; Stage II optimizes per-frame pose θ with the shape fixed. Biological priors—bilateral symmetry on paired limb segments, species-specific joint-angle bounds, and inter-frame pose smoothness—are enforced as additional loss terms to suppress implausible deformations such as joint inversion or body collapse, yielding anatomically valid and temporally stable meshes even under severe self-occlusion.

## Results

On the DBS bumblebee dataset, BioMTBee achieves MPJPE 0.152 mm (2.1% of thorax length), PA-MPJPE 0.147 mm, 3D-PCK 96.6% at δ=0.35 mm, and multi-view mask IoU 0.758. On the cross-species mouse dataset, it reaches MPJPE 2.01 mm, PA-MPJPE 1.95 mm, 3D-PCK 93.6%, and IoU 0.774. The method also demonstrates strong biological plausibility: joint-angle violation rates drop below 8% at >10° (vs. 36–59% for MAMMAL), and bilateral limb-length asymmetry is reduced by 65% relative to the no-symmetry-constraint variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BioMTBee outperforms all quantitative baselines on both datasets. Against MAMMAL [1] (the strongest template-based competitor), it reduces MPJPE by 0.037 mm (19.6% relative), improves 3D-PCK by 4.6 pp (96.6% vs. 92.0%), and raises IoU by 6.2 pp (0.758 vs. 0.696) on the bumblebee dataset; on the mouse dataset the gains are 0.42 mm MPJPE, 3.4 pp PCK, and 2.3 pp IoU. Against DANNCE [10] and DANNCE-T [25] (template-free geometric deep learning), BioMTBee lowers MPJPE by 0.077 mm and 0.067 mm respectively, with PCK advantages of 11.7 pp and 9.3 pp. Template-free methods (Pad3r [28], fauna [27], 3D SAM [46]) are compared qualitatively only; the paper reports that they produce inconsistent geometry and biologically implausible structures around fine articulated regions, whereas BioMTBee preserves thin appendages and coherent anatomical topology. The cross-species mouse experiment further confirms that the framework generalizes beyond bumblebees, achieving the best pose and shape accuracy among all four tested methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of template-based animal reconstruction and insect-specific modeling. While prior work has established template-free approaches (DANNCE, fauna, Pad3r) and generic statistical shape models (SMAL, hSMAL, Animal3D) for vertebrates, and specific-template methods (MAMMAL, ArMo) for pigs and mice, no prior work has addressed 3D mesh reconstruction of bumblebees, which lack any existing articulated template or high-quality dataset. BioMTBee repurposes the differentiable-rendering optimization paradigm from MAMMAL and ArMo but introduces a CT-derived articulated template with 63 bones, a novel spatio-temporal pose estimator (BPST) for robust keypoint triangulation under occlusion, and explicit biological priors (bilateral symmetry, kinematic joint limits, temporal smoothness) that are absent from prior animal reconstruction pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to achieve sub-millimeter 3D surface reconstruction of a moving bumblebee, filling a critical gap for quantitative behavioral analysis in pollination ecology, bio-inspired robotics, and insect biomechanics. The CT-derived template and DBS dataset provide reusable resources for the community, and the cross-species generalization to mice validates the biological-constraint approach beyond a single organism. Key limitations include the single-individual dataset, the requirement for a calibrated multi-view studio setup (6 cameras, 250 FPS), and the per-frame optimization cost that precludes real-time use. Open questions include whether the framework scales to flying bumblebees, whether single-view or fewer-view settings are feasible, and whether the biological constraints can be learned rather than hand-specified.
