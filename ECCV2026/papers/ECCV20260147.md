## Problem

Existing diffusion-based optical flow methods denoise the entire dense flow field from Gaussian noise, including simple regions where a lightweight estimator already provides reliable motion structure. This unnecessary denoising burden slows convergence, destabilizes training, and wastes computational capacity that could be directed toward genuinely challenging regions with large displacements or complex motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260147/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of consecutive RGB frames as input and produces a dense per-pixel optical flow field as output. Internally, the pipeline operates in two stages: a lightweight Confidence-Aware Network first outputs a rough flow estimate and a pixel-wise confidence mask in [0,1], which then conditions a diffusion-based inpainting network. The confidence mask acts as a soft spatial gate rather than a hard binary mask, allowing the diffusion denoiser to partially correct even high-confidence regions when image evidence warrants refinement.

## Method

FlowPainter reformulates dense-flow generation as confidence-guided soft inpainting with two components. A PWC-Net-based Confidence-Aware Network predicts a rough flow F_r and a confidence mask M_conf, where the mask is supervised by combining a hard-flow reliability map (derived from normalized MSE between rough and ground-truth flow, eroded with a 7×7 kernel) and an occlusion map from forward-backward consistency checks. The Flow Inpainting Network, built on the FlowDiffuser backbone, uses the confidence-weighted simple-flow prior for two purposes: (1) confidence-based initialization that fills high-confidence regions (M_conf > 0.5) with the prior instead of pure Gaussian noise, and (2) confidence-gated residual guidance injected at each denoising step via a lightweight convolutional encoder, with a linearly decaying time-step weight W_t = (1 − t_cur/T)·s (s=0.95, T=6) that provides strong structural anchoring in early high-noise steps and gradually relaxes to preserve late-stage detail refinement flexibility.

## Results

On Sintel(test), FlowPainter achieves 1.01/1.71 EPE on the clean/final passes, outperforming the closest diffusion-based baseline FlowDiffuser (1.02/2.03) by 16% on the final pass. On KITTI-15(test), FlowPainter obtains 3.02 Fl-all, a 28% reduction over FlowDiffuser (4.17) and the best among all listed methods. On Spring(train), FlowPainter reaches 0.40 EPE, beating both SEA-RAFT (0.41) and FlowDiffuser (0.43). Additionally, the diffusion-stage training time drops from 7 days 2 hours (FlowDiffuser) to 2 days 1 hour on 8×A100 GPUs while improving final accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowPainter outperforms the primary diffusion-based baseline FlowDiffuser [17] on every reported metric: Sintel final EPE drops from 2.03 to 1.71 (16% gain), KITTI Fl-all drops from 4.17 to 3.02 (28% gain), and Spring EPE improves from 0.43 to 0.40. Against iterative-optimization methods, FlowPainter beats FlowFormer [12] (1.16/2.09 → 1.01/1.71 on Sintel clean/final), GMFlow+ [44] (1.03/2.37 → 1.01/1.71), SEA-RAFT [40] (1.31/2.60 → 1.01/1.71), and RAFT [36] (1.61/2.86 → 1.01/1.71) on Sintel(test). On KITTI(test), FlowPainter achieves the best Fl-all (3.02) and Fl-bg (2.35), surpassing DPFlow [24] (3.56/3.29), DDVM [30] (3.26/2.90), and MemFlow [5] (4.10/3.67). The only metric where a competitor is closer is KITTI Fl-fg, where DPFlow (4.93) and DDVM (5.05) remain below FlowPainter's 6.37, though FlowPainter still leads on the aggregate Fl-all. On the train splits (Table 2), FlowPainter also leads FlowDiffuser by 40% on Sintel final EPE (1.32 vs 2.23) and by 39% on KITTI Fl-all (6.84 vs 11.1), confirming consistent gains beyond the test set.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowPainter builds directly on FlowDiffuser's diffusion-based optical flow backbone and PWC-Net's lightweight pyramid-warping architecture, but departs from both prior diffusion-based flow methods (DDVM, FlowDiffuser) and iterative refinement methods (RAFT, SEA-RAFT) by reformulating dense-flow generation as a confidence-guided soft inpainting problem. The key conceptual departure is that instead of denoising the entire flow field from Gaussian noise, a lightweight Confidence-Aware Network first produces a rough flow and pixel-wise reliability mask, which then initializes and guides the diffusion process via time-decayed residual injection. The confidence mask construction draws on MaskFlowNet's occlusion-aware matching but extends it with explicit hard-flow supervision, making the mask a dual-source reliability gate rather than a purely occlusion signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is twofold: FlowPainter reduces diffusion-stage training time from ~7 days to ~2 days on 8×A100 GPUs while simultaneously improving accuracy, and the 40% EPE reduction on Sintel final pass (train) over FlowDiffuser suggests the hybrid paradigm is particularly effective under combined degradations (motion blur, defocus, illumination changes). The broader implication is that reliable discriminative priors can meaningfully reduce the generative burden in diffusion-based dense prediction, a direction likely transferable to other tasks like depth estimation or segmentation. Open questions include whether the two-stage training (flow first, then confidence) can be unified end-to-end, whether the approach extends to video flow or 3D scene flow, and whether more sophisticated guidance schedules (e.g., learned rather than linear decay) could further close the gap on the hardest regions.
