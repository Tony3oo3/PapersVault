## Problem

Distilling pretrained bidirectional video diffusion models into few-step autoregressive (AR) students typically collapses due to architectural mismatch, forcing prior methods to rely on expensive ODE trajectory matching for stable initialization. Even with such initialization, a train-test distribution gap persists: pretraining conditions on clean or Gaussian-corrupted histories, while inference operates on self-generated contexts with accumulated prediction errors, degrading both the pretraining quality and the distillation gradients.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260066/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt as input and generates 81-frame videos at 832×480 resolution in an autoregressive, frame-by-frame manner. The distilled student operates in a few-step (4-step) regime to meet real-time latency requirements, with throughput and latency reported on a single A100 GPU. Training uses chunk-wise timesteps where each chunk comprises 3 latent frames, and the error buffer stores residuals on a global per-frame basis.

## Method

The paper proposes Error Forcing, a causal pretraining paradigm that collects empirical prediction residuals (δ = x̂ⁱ − xⁱ) from the model's own early outputs into a FIFO buffer, then injects scaled residuals (γ·δ) into the conditioning context during subsequent training steps, exposing the model to degraded-yet-manifold contexts that approximate inference-time history distributions. This error-aware causal initialization is structurally aligned with the AR student, eliminating the need for ODE trajectory matching. The student is then directly distilled via Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) under self-rollout, where the teacher and critic operate under the same perturbed autoregressive context as the student. An optional 'Internal DMD' variant uses architecturally identical teacher/critic to the student, isolating the few-step capability distillation from architectural adaptation. A warm-up phase (first 500 steps) collects residuals without injection to avoid instability from large early-stage errors.

## Results

On the standard VBench prompt set, the proposed method achieves a VBench Total Score of 81.35, Quality Score of 83.92, and Semantic Score of 71.03, outperforming all baselines including Self Forcing (80.79) and Causal Forcing (80.52). On VisionReward, it attains the highest Overall Score (12.00) and Detail Quality (45.74), surpassing Self Forcing (11.33 / 41.86). On the more challenging TA-Hard prompt set, the advantage widens substantially: the method reaches a VBench Total of 71.22 and Semantic Score of 32.24, exceeding the next-best Causal Forcing (69.42 / 22.99) by a clear margin, while also achieving the best VisionReward Overall (2.70) and Motion Realism (−69.44) scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all seven baselines on VBench Total, Quality, and Semantic scores under standard prompts, with the closest competitor being Self Forcing (80.79 vs. 81.35 Total). On VisionReward, it leads in Overall Score (12.00 vs. 11.35 for Longlive) and Detail Quality (45.74 vs. 42.71 for Longlive), though Causal Forcing edges it out marginally on Motion Realism (−6.50 vs. −6.98). Against the bidirectional Wan2.1 baseline, the gap is largest: +5.82 VBench Total and +8.06 VisionReward Overall, confirming that the distilled causal student surpasses the multi-step bidirectional teacher. On the harder TA-Hard set, the semantic advantage is most pronounced (32.24 vs. 22.99 for Causal Forcing, a 9.25-point gap), while Causal Forcing retains a slight edge on Quality (81.03 vs. 80.96) and Text Alignment (24.07 vs. 21.30). Compared to the non-distilled AR models SkyReels-V2 and MAGI-1, the method gains roughly 2.9–3.2 VBench Total points, demonstrating that the Error Forcing + direct DMD pipeline yields meaningful quality improvements over multi-step autoregressive generation at the same 1.3B parameter scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant distillation pipeline for real-time AR video generation (CausVid, Self Forcing, Causal Forcing), which requires an intermediate ODE trajectory-matching phase to bridge the architectural gap between bidirectional teachers and causal students. It builds on the Wan2.1 flow-matching architecture and the self-rollout mechanism from Self Forcing, but departs by arguing that ODE matching is unnecessary if the student is initialized with a structurally aligned causal model. The key methodological contribution, Error Forcing, occupies a new middle ground between Teacher Forcing (clean context) and Diffusion Forcing (stochastic noise context) by injecting empirically collected prediction residuals, and the proposed Internal DMD variant uses an architecturally identical teacher/critic to isolate few-step distillation from causal alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating the ODE trajectory-matching stage reduces both computational cost and a source of error accumulation, making the distillation pipeline more scalable and simpler to implement. The error-injection pretraining strategy is lightweight and generalizable, potentially applicable to other autoregressive generation settings beyond video. However, the evaluation is confined to a single base model (Wan2.1-1.3B), one resolution (832×480), and 81-frame clips, leaving open whether the approach transfers to larger architectures, longer sequences, or other modalities. The modest absolute gains over Self Forcing (~0.5–1 VBench point) suggest the practical benefit is primarily in training efficiency and pipeline simplicity rather than a quality leap.
