## Problem

Multi-objective masked image modeling combines complementary signals (token distillation, CLS alignment, pixel reconstruction), but existing methods weight these objectives with global scalars applied uniformly across all spatial locations. This ignores the spatial heterogeneity of images, where semantically rich patches benefit from teacher distillation while texture-heavy regions benefit from pixel reconstruction. No prior work addresses patch-level adaptive loss weighting for masked image modeling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet-1K, applies 40% block masking, and processes only visible patches through a sparse ViT-Base/16 student encoder (MAE-style). A frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 teacher provides layer-normalized semantic targets for distillation. Downstream, the pretrained representations yield class labels via linear probe or finetuning, and segmentation masks via a UperNet head on ADE20K; the MoE dispatch weights simultaneously serve as routing coefficients in the forward pass and as per-patch loss coefficients during training.

## Method

ExPLoRe integrates Soft-MoE into alternating ViT-Base blocks ({1,3,5,7,9,11}) and repurposes the dispatch weights D (softmax over patches, normalized per expert) as learned per-patch loss coefficients for multi-objective MIM. The key mechanism is loss-coupling: because dispatch weights are differentiable, gradients from each loss objective flow back through D to update the router parameters, enabling content-dependent specialization where different patches receive different emphases across objectives. Dispatch weights are chosen over combine weights because their per-expert normalization over patches prevents a degeneracy where the router could zero out difficult patches to minimize loss. Per-image normalization (dividing by the sum of dispatch weights) further prevents the router from scaling weights down globally. Entropy regularization on the dispatch-weight distribution prevents expert collapse, with the optimal coefficient being expert-count dependent (λ=0.5 for 2 experts, λ=5.0 for 64). A detach ablation confirms loss-coupling as the core mechanism, degrading k-NN by 1.6% when loss gradients are blocked from reaching the router.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ViT-Base, ExPLoRe (64-expert) achieves 80.6% linear probe accuracy (highest among all compared methods) and 85.3% finetuning top-1, matching CAEv2. The 2-expert variant (116M params) reaches 79.6% linear probe, a +3.4% gain over the MaskDistill baseline (76.2%). On ADE20K semantic segmentation (UperNet, 32-expert Token+Pixel with full adaptation recipe), ExPLoRe reaches 52.8 mIoU, matching or exceeding BEiT v2 and MILAN. Notably, both ExPLoRe configurations use fewer inference FLOPs (11.93–13.86 GFLOPs) than every ViT-B/16 comparator (17.45 GFLOPs), a 21–32% reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExPLoRe (64-expert) achieves the highest linear probe accuracy (80.6%) among all compared methods, edging out CAEv2 (80.5%) and BEiT v2 (80.1%), and substantially surpassing MaskDistill (76.2%) and MVP (75.4%). On finetuning, ExPLoRe (85.3%) ties CAEv2 and trails MILAN by 0.1% (85.4%), while clearly outperforming MaskDistill (84.8%), MVP (84.4%), and SdAE (84.1%). On ADE20K segmentation, ExPLoRe (52.8 mIoU) is competitive with BEiT v2 (52.7) and MILAN (52.7) but slightly below CAEv2 (52.9) and MaskDistill (53.8, from the original paper). Against non-CLIP baselines (MAE, BEiT, BootMAE), ExPLoRe leads on every reported metric by wide margins (e.g., +12.6% linear over MAE, +15.0% over BEiT). The 2-expert model (79.6% linear) already exceeds MaskDistill by +3.4% while using 32% fewer inference FLOPs, and the 64-expert model matches CAEv2's finetuning accuracy at 21% lower inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

ExPLoRe builds directly on MaskDistill's teacher-guided MIM framework (CLIP as frozen teacher, Huber token distillation) and repurposes Soft-MoE's dispatch weights—a mechanism originally designed for differentiable expert routing—into learned per-patch loss coefficients. This departs from both the MoE literature, which uses routing for capacity scaling (V-MoE, Switch Transformers) or task-specific expert assignment (AdamV-MoE, ASTREA), and the multi-task loss-balancing literature (GradNorm, uncertainty weighting, PCGrad), which applies a single global scalar per objective. The key conceptual departure is loss-coupling: allowing loss gradients to flow through dispatch weights back to the router, creating a feedback loop that no prior MIM or MoE method exploits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete gap—spatially heterogeneous loss weighting in MIM—that global balancing methods provably cannot capture (GradNorm collapses on Token+Pixel). The downstream transfer recipes (FR+FA+ExD) are practically important for anyone finetuning MoE vision models, closing a 2.5–2.9 mIoU segmentation gap that prior MoE transfer work flagged but did not resolve. Limitations include ViT-Base scale only, a frozen CLIP teacher, and GPU-memory-bounded expert counts (≤64); open questions include whether a dedicated loss-weighting router decoupled from the feature router, multiple slots per expert, or learned cross-block aggregation could further improve the mechanism at scale.
