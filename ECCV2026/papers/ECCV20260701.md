## Problem

Real-world UAV photogrammetry missions are often conducted across multiple times of day, introducing illumination inconsistencies that violate the photometric consistency assumption underlying SfM, MVS, NeRF, and 3DGS pipelines. However, no existing real-world aerial dataset systematically captures the same regions under controlled multi-illumination conditions with precise geometric ground truth, leaving the impact of lighting shifts on reconstruction quality largely unexplored and unevaluable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260701/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 6252×4168 five-directional (nadir + four 45° oblique) images at 1 Hz from a 130 MP CHCNAV C30 camera, captured at 120 m AGL with 80%/60% forward/side overlap along identical RTK-guided trajectories at three times of day (morning, noon, afternoon) across 10 urban scenes totaling over 100K images. Ground truth outputs include per-scene LiDAR meshes (5 cm horizontal / 4 cm vertical accuracy), two depth/normal modalities per frame (dense mesh-projected and sparser LiDAR-reprojected), per-slot COLMAP SfM packages, and per-frame solar elevation/azimuth from NOAA. The novel TCC metric takes independently trained inverse-rendering models on each time slot and produces a scalar [0,1] cross-time albedo stability score by rendering from K fixed viewpoints and comparing against the cross-time mean.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a LiDAR-guided multi-temporal alignment pipeline that registers all three time-slot RGB captures into a unified coordinate system: LiDAR point clouds are georeferenced in WGS84/UTM, rendered as pseudo-RGB anchor views with fixed poses, and jointly optimized with all RGB images in a single anchor-constrained SfM where RTK poses serve as soft priors and manual GCPs resolve sparse-feature drift (achieving ≤0.002 m mean camera position uncertainty). Dense geometry is then produced via LiDAR-guided MVS, where active LiDAR measurements regularize depth in weakly textured or shadowed regions, and water surfaces are explicitly completed by sampling shoreline elevations and constraining normals to horizontal. For evaluation, the Temporal Consistency Coefficient (TCC) framework samples K fixed test viewpoints, renders albedo from models trained independently on each of the three time slots, and computes four sub-metrics (SSIM, LPIPS, MAE, RMSE) against the cross-time mean albedo, aggregated into a single TCC_overall ∈ [0,1] score where 1 indicates perfect material decoupling. The benchmark then systematically evaluates three inverse-rendering methods (GS-IR, Ref-Gaussian, Ref-GS), four geometry methods (2DGS, PGSR, GOF, CityGaussianV2), and four NVS methods (3DGS, Abs-GS, Mip-Splatting, Octree-GS) on six scenes, exposing that shadows are over-fitted as geometry, albedos retain time-dependent artifacts, and rendering quality degrades significantly under illumination shifts.

## Results

On the SkyLume benchmark, Ref-GS achieves the best Temporal Consistency Coefficient (TCC Overall) of 0.775 mean across scenes, outperforming GS-IR (0.721) and Ref-Gaussian (0.658) in cross-time albedo stability. For geometry reconstruction, CityGaussianV2 attains the highest F-1 of 0.808 at τ=0.75 m under sunlit conditions, while 2DGS and PGSR (alpha-blending depth methods) show more consistent cross-time geometry (TCC-Geometry F-1 ≈ 0.79) but lower absolute accuracy. In novel view synthesis, Abs-GS leads among NVS-oriented methods with SSIM 0.681–0.711 and PSNR 21.2–23.1 across six scenes, while geometry-oriented pipelines (2DGS, PGSR) lag by 0.08–0.12 SSIM and 0.8–1.1 dB PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among inverse-rendering baselines, Ref-GS [57] achieves the highest TCC Overall (0.775 mean), beating GS-IR [18] (0.721) by 0.054 and Ref-Gaussian [49] (0.658) by 0.117, indicating superior cross-time albedo decoupling; however, all three retain visible shadow imprinting, confirming that no current method fully separates material from illumination. For geometry, CityGaussianV2 [22] leads in absolute F-1 (0.808 at τ=0.75 m, Period 1) but is brittle under hard shadows, while 2DGS [8] and PGSR [1] (alpha-blending depth) produce more temporally consistent meshes (TCC-Geometry F-1 ≈ 0.79) at the cost of missing fine detail. GOF [53] shows the weakest geometry (F-1 0.103 at τ=0.25 m) due to opacity-based surface extraction failing under direct sunlight. In NVS, Abs-GS [51] consistently outperforms 3DGS [11] by ~0.01 SSIM and 0.04 dB PSNR, while Mip-Splatting [52] and Octree-GS [30] trail by 0.01–0.03 SSIM; geometry-oriented methods 2DGS [8] and PGSR [1] are the weakest, losing 0.08–0.12 SSIM to Abs-GS across all scenes, confirming that surface-constrained Gaussians sacrifice photometric fidelity for geometric accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the UAV 3D reconstruction dataset space by addressing a gap that no prior aerial corpus fills: systematic multi-illumination capture of identical scenes. Existing datasets (ISPRS, UrbanScene3D, GauU-Scene, Matrix City) either lack cross-time revisits, lack LiDAR ground truth, or are synthetic, making it impossible to isolate illumination effects from geometric changes. The benchmark repurposes the standard 3DGS evaluation pipeline (SfM + MVS + Gaussian-based rendering) but introduces a LiDAR-guided multi-temporal SfM alignment and a novel Temporal Consistency Coefficient (TCC) metric that has no direct predecessor in the literature. Conceptually, it extends the inverse-rendering and geometry-reconstruction tasks from single-session to multi-session settings, where photometric consistency is deliberately violated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-temporal illumination problem is a practical bottleneck for city-scale mapping missions that span multiple flight days, yet it has been largely unquantified due to the absence of appropriate benchmarks. SkyLume's systematic evaluation reveals that all current 3DGS variants suffer significant albedo drift, shadow-induced geometric corruption, and cross-time inconsistency, providing a concrete target for future illumination-robust methods. Open questions include how to explicitly decouple illumination from material at city scale, whether LiDAR-guided priors can fully compensate for photometric violations in MVS, and whether feed-forward reconstruction models can be adapted to raw multi-temporal aerial captures. Limitations include only 10 scenes, 3 time slots, and limited weather diversity, which may not capture the full range of real-world illumination variability.
