## Problem

Adapting 2D vision foundation models for 3D spatial understanding faces a fundamental dilemma: fine-tuning the backbone causes catastrophic forgetting of native 2D knowledge, while freezing it restricts 3D spatial perception. Existing unified 3D scene understanding methods are further limited by short context windows (2–16 views) that produce unreliable geometric constraints under occlusion, and by decoupled optimization of 2D features and 3D structure that induces cross-view semantic inconsistency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes up to 128 unposed RGB images at 256×256 resolution, internally predicting camera poses via the Depth Anything 3 pose-extraction pipeline. It produces a feed-forward 3D Gaussian scene representation where each Gaussian primitive carries, in addition to standard position/rotation/opacity/scale/SH parameters, three attached feature vectors: a 64-dim open-vocabulary semantic feature, an 8-dim instance feature, and a 64-dim regularization feature. From this unified field, the system renders novel views, dense depth and ray maps, and open-vocabulary semantic/instance segmentation, and can also recover relative camera poses.

## Method

DINOv3D employs a homologous teacher-student dual-branch architecture initialized from DINOv3 ViT-L. The frozen teacher distills 2D knowledge into a 3D Gaussian regularization field by enforcing cosine-similarity alignment between differentiable-rendered regularization features and the teacher's native feature maps, anchoring the student against catastrophic forgetting. The student backbone is adapted via LoRA (rank 16, ~10% of parameters) and processes long-context sequences through a shifted-window attention mechanism with dual memory tokens (global and local) that alternates intra-frame and cross-view window attention to capture spatial-temporal consistency at linear complexity. A unified DPT decoder predicts depth, rays, and the full Gaussian field from shared multi-scale features, while a ray-depth-semantic alignment loss uses predicted depth and ray geometry to establish cross-view pixel correspondences for semantic and instance consistency, modulated by a depth-confidence mask to suppress noisy early-stage gradients. Training optimizes a composite objective combining regularization, photometric (L1+LPIPS), depth, ray, semantic distillation from LSeg, instance contrastive loss from SAM masks, and the alignment term.

## Results

On ScanNet with 2 input views, DINOv3D achieves 25.67 dB PSNR and 0.8823 SSIM for novel view synthesis, 57.68% mIoU for open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, and 3.02 absolute relative error (70.52% δ<1.25) for depth estimation, all state-of-the-art. These results are obtained with only 0.1B trainable parameters (10% of the 1B-parameter VGGT backbone), compared to Uni3R's 1.0B trainable parameters. On RealEstate10K (large overlap), DINOv3D reaches 28.23 dB PSNR, surpassing the strongest pose-free baseline NoPoSplat (27.41 dB) and even pose-required methods like MVSplat (27.47 dB). On 2D linear probing, DINOv3D improves over the DINOv3 baseline on all benchmarks, e.g., +1.3% on ImageNet-R and +1.5% on ObjectNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DINOv3D outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluated task and dataset. Against the closest unified competitor Uni3R (1.3B total / 1B trainable), DINOv3D wins by +1.78 dB PSNR on 2-view NVS, +1.84% mIoU on OVSS, and −0.85 absolute relative error on depth estimation, while using only 10% of the trainable parameters. On RealEstate10K NVS (large overlap), DINOv3D (28.23 dB) surpasses the strongest pose-free baseline NoPoSplat (27.41 dB) by +0.82 dB and also beats pose-required methods pixelSplat (27.15 dB) and MVSplat (27.47 dB). For relative pose estimation on RealEstate10K at the strict 5° threshold, DINOv3D achieves 0.635 AUC versus NoPoSplat's 0.568 and MASt3R's 0.351. On cross-dataset generalization (DTU), DINOv3D leads with 18.12 dB PSNR and 0.237 LPIPS, outperforming NoPoSplat (17.90 / 0.279). On 2D linear probing, DINOv3D improves over the frozen DINOv3 backbone on all nine benchmarks (e.g., +1.3% ImageNet-R, +1.5% ObjectNet, −0.028 RMSE on NYUv2 depth), confirming that 3D-aware training does not degrade—and in fact enhances—2D representation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DINOv3D sits at the intersection of three active research threads: 2D foundation model adaptation to 3D (building on DINOv3 as the visual backbone), feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting scene reconstruction (building on 3DGS and Depth Anything 3's DPT decoder and pose extraction), and parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA. It departs from prior unified 3D scene understanding models (Uni3R, LSM) by extending the context window from 2–16 views to 128 frames via a shifted-window attention mechanism, and from per-scene optimization methods (FiT3D) by operating entirely in a feed-forward regime. The homologous teacher-student architecture with a 3D Gaussian regularization field is a novel regularization strategy that differs from standard distillation by grounding the 2D anchor in a geometrically consistent 3D representation rather than raw 2D feature matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 3D-aware joint optimization retroactively improves 2D linear probing performance (e.g., +1.3% on ImageNet-R, lower NYUv2 depth RMSE) suggests that multi-view geometric consistency acts as a useful inductive bias for view-invariant 2D representations, a direction that could inform future foundation model pre-training. The 10× parameter efficiency (0.1B trainable vs. 1B) makes the approach practical for resource-constrained deployment. Open questions include whether the 128-frame context and shifted-window attention scale to outdoor or large-scale scenes, whether the Uni3D pseudo-label pipeline introduces systematic biases that cap performance, and whether the ray-depth-semantic alignment generalizes to dynamic or partially observed scenes.
