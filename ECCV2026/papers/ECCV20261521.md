## Problem

Existing video object removal benchmarks evaluate only masked-region reconstruction fidelity, implicitly treating removal as local inpainting and ignoring the causal physical effects (shadows, reflections, illumination shifts, dynamic traces) that objects induce on their environment. No prior benchmark provides temporally aligned clean background references that enable systematic verification of whether a method truly restores the scene as if the object had never been present.

## Input / Output

BeyondMasks provides 180 temporally aligned video pairs (object-present and clean background, ~5.5 s average length) with per-frame binary masks that explicitly exclude after-effects, plus structured text prompts for instruction-driven editing. The CORE evaluation consumes three aligned videos—the object-present sequence, the clean reference, and the method's edited output—along with metadata specifying the target object and relevant after-effect categories, and produces two scalar scores: ObjectScore (completeness of object removal) and AfterEffectScore (elimination of induced physical traces).

## Method

The paper formalizes object removal as a counterfactual rendering problem: recovering R(S_t, ∅) rather than hallucinating plausible content within a mask. BeyondMasks is constructed via two pipelines—90 synthetic pairs generated by first creating a background-only video with Veo3 and then inserting the target object through controlled editing, and 90 real-world pairs captured back-to-back on a tripod—ensuring that the only differences between paired videos correspond to the object and its causal footprint. A mechanism-driven taxonomy organizes samples into seven after-effect categories (shadow, reflection, translucent, steam/scattering, causal physical, light source, none). CORE implements evaluation through Gemini 3.1 under a fixed structured prompt that performs comparative reasoning across the three input videos, explicitly separating residual object traces from persistent after-effects and rewarding semantically coherent reconstruction even when pixel-level differences from ground truth exist.

## Results

On BeyondMasks, DiffuEraser achieves the best reconstruction fidelity (PSNR 25.19, SSIM 0.877, LPIPS 0.112), yet its CORE-AES (2.625) trails Gen.Omnimatte (3.021) and ROSE (2.920), revealing a gap between pixel-level quality and causal after-effect removal. ROSE attains the highest CORE-OS (3.784), indicating the most complete object disappearance, while Gen.Omnimatte leads on CORE-AES (3.021) for after-effect elimination. Text-guided methods (Lucy Edit, VACE, CoCoCo) score substantially lower on both CORE dimensions (CORE-OS 1.44–1.99, CORE-AES 1.55–2.04) compared to mask-based approaches (CORE-OS 3.06–3.78, CORE-AES 2.37–3.02). Across after-effect categories, volumetric (steam: CORE-AES 2.03) and dynamic causal effects (CORE-AES 2.14) are the hardest to remove, while shadows (2.52) and reflections (2.45) are handled relatively better.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffuEraser dominates on all pixel-level metrics (PSNR 25.19, SSIM 0.877, LPIPS 0.112, FVD 164.26) but does not lead on causal correctness: its CORE-AES (2.625) is 0.396 below Gen.Omnimatte (3.021) and 0.295 below ROSE (2.920), indicating it leaves residual shadows and reflections. ROSE achieves the best CORE-OS (3.784) for object disappearance, narrowly beating Gen.Omnimatte (3.771) and MiniMax (3.631), despite having only moderate PSNR (23.81) and the worst FVD (316.04) among mask-based methods. Gen.Omnimatte is the only method to exceed 3.0 on CORE-AES, making it the strongest at eliminating physical after-effects, while also scoring competitively on CORE-OS (3.771). Text-guided and hybrid methods (Lucy Edit, VACE, CoCoCo) are consistently the weakest on both CORE dimensions, with Lucy Edit scoring the lowest CORE-OS (1.443) and CORE-AES (1.551), suggesting that instruction-driven editing currently struggles with fine-grained physical trace removal. ProPainter, despite being the oldest method (ICCV 2023), achieves a competitive CORE-OS (3.063) but lags on CORE-AES (2.368), confirming that even strong inpainting pipelines fail to remove diffuse environmental effects. Overall, no single method excels on both reconstruction fidelity and causal consistency, and the best CORE-AES across all methods (3.021) remains well below the theoretical maximum, highlighting that after-effect removal is still an open challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant framing of video object removal as masked-region inpainting (DAVIS, YouTube-VOS, HQVI) and extends the synthetic paired-data approach of ROSE-Bench by adding real-world captures, a broader taxonomy of causal after-effects (volumetric, dynamic, geometric), and instruction-driven editing support. Conceptually, it reframes removal as a causal intervention problem—recovering the counterfactual scene state R(S_t, ∅)—rather than local pixel reconstruction, and introduces CORE, a VLM-based evaluation that explicitly separates object disappearance from after-effect elimination, a distinction absent from prior metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FVD) and from VLM evaluators like VBench or VideoPhy that assess realism without aligned references.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a systematic and quantified gap: methods achieving top PSNR/SSIM (e.g., DiffuEraser) still leave shadows, reflections, and dynamic traces, while methods with moderate pixel fidelity (e.g., ROSE) score higher on causal correctness. This challenges the implicit assumption in the field that reconstruction quality proxies removal quality and motivates future work on physically grounded removal. Open questions include whether the 180-sequence scale is sufficient for training or broader generalization, whether CORE's reliance on a single VLM (Gemini 3.1) introduces evaluator bias, and whether current architectures can learn to model and undo spatially diffuse, temporally evolving after-effects without explicit supervision.
