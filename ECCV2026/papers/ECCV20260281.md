## Problem

Extending test-time adaptation (TTA) to vision-language model (VLM)–based medical image segmentation is challenging because fine-tuning VLMs with noisy image-text pseudo-labels disrupts their pretrained cross-modal alignment, yielding limited or even negative gains. Existing training-based TTA methods designed for vision foundation models fail to transfer effectively to this VLM setting, leaving a gap for a robust, training-free adaptation strategy that preserves the VLM's strong pretrained features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260281/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of unlabeled medical images (fundus images or chest X-rays, resized to 1024×1024) together with a GPT-4-generated text prompt describing the target anatomy, and produces a binary segmentation mask per image. It operates in a fully source-free, online fashion with batch size 1: no source-domain data or ground-truth labels are required, and a FIFO memory bank of up to 15 curated samples is maintained across the stream to guide subsequent predictions.

## Method

MSSA is a two-stage, entirely training-free pipeline that synergistically combines image-text semantic grounding with image-image structural consistency. In the first stage (Image-Text Coarse Grounding), it builds on MedCLIP-SAMv2 but stabilizes outputs via Gaussian-weighted point selection for SAM prompts and dual majority voting (photometric augmentations for saliency maps, geometric augmentations for SAM refinement). In the second stage, a Noise-Aware Memory Construction module scores each candidate mask by a combined semantic-alignment score (BiomedCLIP image-text similarity of the masked region) and a spatial-smoothness score (shape regularity plus boundary smoothness), admitting only candidates above an adaptively calibrated, monotonically non-decreasing percentile threshold into a DINOv2-keyed memory bank. For each new query, the most feature-similar anchor is retrieved from the bank, local foreground/background prototypes are extracted via sliding-window average pooling (adapted from ALPNet), and the final mask is decoded by element-wise maximum cosine-similarity matching between the query's DINOv2 feature map and the prototypes—requiring no parameter updates or backpropagation at any point.

## Results

On optic disc/cup segmentation across five fundus image domains, MSSA achieves an average DSC of 83.9% and mIoU of 73.1%, surpassing the strongest training-based TTA method TTCS by 12.2% DSC and 11.7% mIoU, and the best zero-shot baseline MedCLIPv2 by 12.5% DSC and 15.1% mIoU. On chest X-ray lung segmentation (COVID-QU-Ex, MC, SZ), MSSA reaches 78.4% DSC and 66.7% mIoU on average, outperforming TTCS by 15.6% DSC and 23.1% mIoU. Notably, most training-based TTA methods (TENT, EATA, CoTTA, DELTA, SVDP) yield negligible or negative gains over the zero-shot baseline, while UniVPT suffers catastrophic degradation (−6.0% DSC on OD, −64.6% on lung).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSSA decisively outperforms all 12 baselines on both segmentation tasks. Among zero-shot VLM methods, MedCLIPv2 [13] is the strongest baseline (71.4% avg DSC on OD, 59.0% on lung), but MSSA exceeds it by 12.5% DSC (OD) and 19.4% DSC (lung) without any parameter updates. The SOTA training-based TTA method TTCS [4] achieves only marginal gains over the zero-shot baseline (+0.4% DSC on OD, +6.4% on lung), while MSSA surpasses TTCS by 12.2% DSC (OD) and 15.6% DSC (lung). Most other training-based TTA methods (TENT, EATA, CoTTA, DELTA, SVDP, OCL) produce negligible or slightly negative improvements over the zero-shot baseline, confirming the paper's hypothesis that fine-tuning VLMs with noisy pseudo-labels disrupts pretrained image-text alignment. UniVPT [17] is the worst performer, suffering catastrophic degradation (−6.0% DSC on OD, −64.6% on lung), likely due to its visual prompt tuning being incompatible with the VLM-based pipeline. The gap between MSSA and all baselines is largest on the most challenging domains (e.g., Domain A on OD: 76.6 vs. 59.8 for MedCLIPv2; COVID-QU-Ex on lung: 73.2 vs. 54.6), indicating that the memory-based adaptation is most beneficial under severe domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSSA builds directly on the MedCLIP-SAMv2 pipeline (Koleilat et al., 2025) for VLM-based zero-shot medical segmentation and adapts the ALPNet prototype extraction mechanism (Ouyang et al., ECCV 2020) for non-parametric image-image matching. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant fine-tuning-based TTA paradigm (TENT, CoTTA, DELTA, TTCS) by arguing that parameter updates on noisy pseudo-labels actively degrade the pretrained image-text alignment in VLMs, and instead repurposes the VLM's own predictions as a curated memory bank for structural guidance. This positions the work at the intersection of cross-image consistency literature (prototype matching, semi-supervised segmentation) and test-time adaptation, bridging the image-text and image-image paradigms that prior methods treat in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard fine-tuning-based TTA methods perform at or below zero-shot baselines when applied to VLM-based segmentation challenges the prevailing assumption that more adaptation always helps, and motivates a broader rethinking of how to adapt vision-language models at test time. The training-free design is practically attractive for clinical deployment where labeled data and source access are unavailable, though the 12.1-second per-image latency (vs. 1.7–5.9 s for baselines) limits real-time applicability. The authors acknowledge that MSSA currently works best for organs with regular geometries (lung, optic disc), leaving open whether the prototype-matching strategy generalizes to highly intricate structures such as micro-vascular networks or multi-class dense prediction.
