## Problem

Despite remarkable progress in deep learning for 3D object classification, point clouds remain challenging due to their unordered, sparse, and noisy nature, and topological data analysis—despite its direct connection to shape—has not been successfully leveraged for large-scale 3D classification. The paper asks whether a TDA-based front-end, combined with a lightweight learned classifier, can achieve state-of-the-art accuracy while remaining robust to corruption and generalizable across diverse real-world benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260446/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an n-point 3D point cloud as input and produces a single class label. The point cloud is voxelized into a binary 3D image within its axis-aligned bounding box (optimal voxel size 0.05 for ModelNet), and the resulting topological feature vector has a dataset-dependent length (1728 for ModelNet40/10, 1152 for VesselMNIST3D, 1584 for AdrenalMNIST3D). The classifier is a 0.72M-parameter 1D CNN, making inference extremely fast (16,454 clouds/s for the CNN stage) while the full TDA pipeline runs at ~5.3 clouds/s.

## Method

TACO-Net applies 57 task-agnostic 3D filtrations (26 directional height, 27 radial from a 3×3×3 center grid, and 4 DEDS: density, dilation, erosion, signed distance) to the voxelized binary image, producing 57 grayscale volumes on which cubical persistent homology is computed. From each persistence diagram, 36 scalar features are extracted: 3 persistent entropy values (one per homology group H₀, H₁, H₂) and 33 amplitude metrics spanning Wasserstein (p=1,2), Bottleneck, Betti curve (L₁, L₂), persistence landscape (k=1,2; L₁, L₂), and heat kernel (σ=0.15; L₁, L₂) distances. These per-filtration vectors are concatenated into a single fixed-length feature vector, which is then classified by a lightweight 3-layer 1D CNN (filter sizes 3/5/7, channels 128/64/C) followed by a fully connected softmax layer. The key architectural insight is that the TDA front-end is entirely non-learning and dataset-agnostic—no gradients flow through the topological computations—so the 1D CNN learns higher-order interactions over pre-computed, stable topological signatures, yielding class-disentangled representations without the complexity of end-to-end point-cloud networks.

## Results

On ModelNet40, TACO-Net achieves 99.05% overall accuracy (OA) and 97.97% mean class accuracy (mAcc), surpassing the previous best (RotationNet at 97.37% OA) by 1.68 percentage points. On ModelNet10, it reaches 99.52% OA and mAcc. On the real-world ScanObjectNN benchmark, TACO-Net attains SOTA accuracy among from-scratch supervised methods on OBJ_BG (95.08%) and OBJ_ONLY (94.01%), and the second-highest on the hardest variant PB_T50_RS (91.87%). On medical datasets, it achieves 95.28% mAcc / 0.94 F1 on VesselMNIST3D and 80.54% OA on AdrenalMNIST3D, outperforming all prior benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TACO-Net decisively outperforms all 33 compared baselines on ModelNet40, exceeding the prior SOTA RotationNet (97.37% OA) by 1.68 points and the strongest transformer-based methods (PointGPT 94.9%, PointMamba 93.6%) by over 4 points. On ModelNet10, it similarly surpasses all competitors, reaching 99.52% OA. On ScanObjectNN, TACO-Net achieves SOTA among from-scratch supervised methods on OBJ_BG (95.08% vs. Mamba3D's 94.49%) and OBJ_ONLY (94.01% vs. Mamba3D's 92.43%), but trails Mamba3D (92.64%) on the hardest PB_T50_RS variant (91.87%), while still beating X-3D (90.7%) and PointNeXt (87.7%). On VesselMNIST3D, it improves mAcc from 93.52% (PointNet++) to 95.28% and F1 from 0.90 to 0.94; on AdrenalMNIST3D it edges out auto-sklearn (80.2%) with 80.54% OA. On OmniObject3D (190 classes), TACO-Net achieves the highest accuracy of 58.90%, outperforming CurveNet, PointNet, PointNet++, and PCT. A direct comparison with the TDA-based TopoRec [16] shows that TACO-Net's 1D CNN applied to TopoRec's own feature vectors yields ~69% OA versus TopoRec's native closest-vector approach at ~36%, highlighting the value of the learned classifier over the fixed topological descriptors.

## Relation to Prior Work

TACO-Net extends the TDA-based classification paradigm introduced by Garin & Tauzin [15] for 2D MNIST to 3D point clouds, replacing 2D image filtrations with a symmetric family of 3D cubical filtrations (height, radial, density, dilation, erosion, signed distance) and computing cubical persistent homology on the resulting grayscale volumes. It departs sharply from the dominant end-to-end deep learning paradigm (PointNet, transformers, Mamba-based models) by using a completely non-learnable, dataset-agnostic TDA front-end that produces fixed-length topological feature vectors, which a lightweight 1D CNN then classifies. The paper also positions itself against the authors' own prior work TopoRec [16], showing that replacing TopoRec's nearest-neighbor classifier with the 1D CNN lifts ModelNet40 accuracy from ~36% to ~69%, isolating the learning component's contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 0.72M-parameter 1D CNN over hand-crafted topological signatures outperforms multi-hundred-million-parameter end-to-end architectures on ModelNet40 challenges the prevailing assumption that SOTA 3D classification requires heavy learned representations of raw point clouds. However, the headline >99% accuracies are on clean synthetic CAD models; on noisier real-world benchmarks (ScanObjectNN, OmniObject3D) the margins narrow, and the TDA feature-generation pipeline's throughput (~5.3 clouds/sec) remains a practical bottleneck for real-time deployment. Open questions include whether GPU-parallelized cubical persistence can close the speed gap, whether the approach degrades gracefully under extreme corruption (impulse noise drops OA to ~53%), and whether the topological feature space generalizes to open-vocabulary or few-shot settings.
