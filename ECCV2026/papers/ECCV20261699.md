## Problem

Compact unified models that jointly perform motion prediction and planning on a shared encoder suffer from a previously uncharacterized 'Skill Conflict': the distinct representational needs of predicting neighbor behaviors versus ego-centric safety planning cause overlapping parameter assignments that degrade both tasks. This conflict is especially severe in resource-constrained edge deployments where limited model capacity forces tasks to compete for the same weights, yet prior unified architectures and model-merging methods (developed primarily for large LLMs) overlook this interference.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 9 past timesteps (at 2.5 fps) of multi-modal observations—trajectories, body poses, and bounding boxes—for the ego agent and all surrounding agents, and produces 12 future timesteps of both the ego trajectory (planning) and all neighbor trajectories (prediction). The backbone is a Social-Transmotion transformer extended with a planning decoder, pretrained with a game-theoretic joint loss before task-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, Disjoint Parameter Training (DPT) alternates between prediction and planning fine-tuning; at each step it computes gradients over the full parameter space, selects the top-K unassigned parameters with the largest absolute gradient, permanently allocates them to the current task via a binary mask, and updates only those regions—masks grow during the first half of training and freeze in the second half, producing task-specialized models with mutually exclusive core parameter regions. Second, Sparse Merging combines the two fine-tuned models by adding only the top-K% magnitude coordinates of each task vector to the pretrained backbone (K=1% is optimal), which suppresses residual interference from adjacent features that persist even when primary supports are disjoint. DPT is composable with other merging strategies (Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging), and an optional joint-reasoning fine-tuning stage on the remaining inactive parameters further improves both tasks.

## Results

On JRDB, DPT+SM achieves planning ADE of 0.4044, collision rate of 0.0091, FDE of 0.7458, and miss rate of 0.3706, while attaining prediction ADE of 0.5952 and FDE of 1.0352. Adding a joint-reasoning fine-tuning stage (DPT+SM+JR) further improves all metrics to ADE 0.3640, collision rate 0.0074, FDE 0.7105, miss rate 0.3589, prediction ADE 0.5097, and prediction FDE 0.9396. On JTA, DPT+SM achieves planning ADE 1.0322, collision rate 0.0055, FDE 2.1134, miss rate 0.9305, prediction ADE 1.1372, and prediction FDE 2.2206, outperforming all baselines by large margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPT+SM outperforms all seven baselines on every planning and prediction metric on JRDB. Against the strongest unified architecture baseline DTPP [15], DPT+SM reduces planning ADE from 0.6232 to 0.4044 (−35%) and collision rate from 0.0145 to 0.0091 (−37%), while also improving prediction ADE from 0.7686 to 0.5952. Compared to DIPP [17], the planning ADE gap is even larger (0.8048 → 0.4044, −50%). Among merging baselines, Task Arithmetic [18] and TIES-Merging [65] suffer the most degradation (planning ADE 0.9387 and 0.8759 respectively), indicating severe skill conflict when naively combining task vectors in compact models. Localize-and-Stitch [13] and T-Switch [42] perform somewhat better on prediction (ADE 0.8104 and 0.8091) but still trail DPT+SM by ~27% on prediction ADE and by 45–50% on planning ADE. The Ensemble baseline [2] is the closest competitor on planning (ADE 0.5807) but degrades substantially on prediction (ADE 1.1313), confirming that the conflict is parameter-level rather than output-level. Adding joint reasoning (DPT+SM+JR) further narrows the gap to single-task fine-tuning performance (Plan Finetune ADE 0.3699) while retaining strong prediction, demonstrating that the framework preserves the unified-model advantage of cross-task reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: unified prediction-planning architectures (DIPP, DTPP, GameFormer) that share a single encoder for both tasks, and model merging methods (Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, Localize-and-Stitch, T-Switch) that combine task-specific fine-tuned weights into one model. It departs from prior unified models by explicitly diagnosing and addressing representational conflicts in the shared encoder, and from prior merging work by targeting compact, edge-deployable models rather than the massive LLMs where existing merging methods were designed. The backbone is Social-Transmotion extended with a planning decoder, and the task-vector formalism is inherited from Task Arithmetic, but the core contribution—Disjoint Parameter Training as a *training-time* strategy to prevent parameter overlap before merging—is new relative to both lines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying unified perception-planning models on resource-constrained mobile robots, this paper provides a concrete, low-overhead recipe (binary masks during fine-tuning + top-K% sparse merging) that avoids the need to inflate model capacity. The identification of Skill Conflict as a measurable, layer-localized phenomenon gives a diagnostic tool for any compact multi-task model, not just prediction-planning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond two tasks and beyond pedestrian navigation (the supplementary extends to vehicle-domain driving, but the main results are limited to JRDB and JTA), and whether the 50:50 DPT allocation ratio is optimal or task-dependent in more complex multi-agent settings.
