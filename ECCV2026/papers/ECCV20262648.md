## Problem

LoRA, the dominant PEFT paradigm, is designed for 2D matrix operations in transformer linear layers. Applying it naively to convolutional kernels requires flattening 4D tensors into 2D matrices, which collapses spatial topology and entangles spatial structure with cross-channel mixing, degrading the preservation of spatial inductive biases (locality, directionality) critical to visual adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262648/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained vision foundation model backbones (ConvNeXt, ResNet, MambaVision, EfficientNet) and modifies only low-rank parameters within their convolutional layers while freezing all pre-trained weights. For classification and segmentation tasks, a standard RGB image is consumed and a class label or pixel-wise mask is produced; for the generative task, a text prompt paired with a reference subject image drives Stable Diffusion to produce a subject-consistent generated image. The spatial rank is fixed to the kernel size (k_h × k_w), while the channel rank scales hierarchically with stage width.

## Method

LoCA decouples convolutional adaptation into two independent paths composed additively. The channel path applies a standard low-rank factorization (B_c A_c) on the flattened kernel to capture dense cross-channel mixing, with B_c zero-initialized for exact functional equivalence at start. The spatial path computes the SVD of the spatial covariance matrix (W_norm^T W_norm) of pre-trained kernel slices to obtain an initial basis tensor S, then refines it via learnable depthwise coefficients U_dw that expand each channel's spatial update as a linear combination of the SVD bases. The combined update places the channel term on all (i,j) channel pairs and the spatial term only on the depthwise diagonal (i=j), preserving the separation of cross-channel mixing from spatial modulation. A hierarchical rank scheduling strategy allocates channel rank proportionally to each stage's output width, giving deeper, wider stages larger adaptation budgets while keeping the spatial rank fixed to the kernel size.

## Results

On VTAB-1k with ConvNeXt-B, LoCA (rank-4) achieves 75.98% average accuracy with only 0.97M trainable parameters, surpassing full fine-tuning (74.98%), LoRA (74.36%), and Conv-Adapter (75.18%). On FGVC, LoCA (rank-16) reaches 90.06% average top-1 accuracy on ConvNeXt-B, outperforming LoRA (88.18%) and Conv-Adapter (89.28%) while using fewer parameters (3.70M vs. 17.58M). For domain-generalized semantic segmentation on ConvNeXt-L, LoCA achieves 66.49% average mIoU across Cityscapes, BDD100K, and Mapillary, exceeding full fine-tuning (63.87%) and SoMA (66.13%). On DreamBooth subject-driven generation, LoCA attains the best DINO score (0.709) and ties for best CLIP-I (0.801), outperforming LoRA (0.637/0.792) and FSF (0.572/0.715).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoCA outperforms LoRA [19] across all three task families: on VTAB-1k (ConvNeXt-B), LoCA(r4) achieves 75.98% vs. LoRA's 74.36% with 18× fewer parameters (0.97M vs. 17.32M); on FGVC, LoCA reaches 90.06% vs. LoRA's 88.18%; and on DreamBooth, LoCA scores 0.709 DINO vs. LoRA's 0.637. Against Conv-Adapter [3], LoCA wins on VTAB-1k (75.98% vs. 75.18%) and FGVC (90.06% vs. 89.28%) while using fewer parameters. FSF [4] is outperformed on VTAB-1k (75.98% vs. 73.59%), FGVC (90.06% vs. 88.04%), and DreamBooth DINO (0.709 vs. 0.572), though FSF edges out LoCA on CLIP-T text alignment (0.313 vs. 0.280). CoLoRA [32] is consistently beaten: 75.98% vs. 70.00% on VTAB-1k and 90.06% vs. 86.11% on FGVC. On DGSS, LoCA surpasses SoMA [42] on ConvNeXt-L (66.49% vs. 66.13% avg mIoU) and achieves a 35.9% relative gain over FFT on MambaVision-B (44.20% vs. 32.52%). LoCA also dominates simpler baselines—Bias tuning [1] (51.42%), VPT [20] (65.18%), and MLP adapters [18] (59.33%)—by 10+ points on VTAB-1k, confirming that structured low-rank convolutional adaptation substantially outperforms parameter-free or prompt-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoCA extends the LoRA paradigm (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) from transformer linear projections to convolutional kernels, addressing the structural mismatch that arises when 4D convolutional tensors are naively flattened into 2D matrices. It departs from Filter Subspace Fine-Tuning (FSF, Chen et al., ICLR 2025), which decomposes kernels into spatial atoms but freezes cross-channel mixing coefficients, by jointly adapting both channel and spatial components while preserving pre-trained weights exactly. The SVD-based spatial basis initialization draws on the PiSSA/SoMA/SoRA line of work that uses singular components of pre-trained weights to inform low-rank factor initialization, but applies SVD to a spatial covariance matrix rather than the full flattened kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, convolution-aware PEFT method that is practical (zero-initialized, no kernel replacement, mergeable at inference) and achieves strong parameter efficiency—0.97M trainable parameters on ConvNeXt-B matching or exceeding full fine-tuning on VTAB-1k. The ERF dynamics analysis (Fig. 1) offers mechanistic evidence that spatial-channel entanglement in naive LoRA causes transient receptive-field growth followed by reconvergence, which is a useful diagnostic for future PEFT designs. Open questions include whether the SVD-based spatial basis is necessary at larger kernel sizes or in non-convolutional (pure transformer/SSM) backbones, and whether the hierarchical rank scheduling generalizes beyond the specific width-proportional allocation tested here.
