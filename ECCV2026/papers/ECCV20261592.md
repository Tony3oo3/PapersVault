## Problem

High-resolution (0.1°) global weather forecasting is fundamentally constrained by data scarcity: only ~10 years of 0.1° ECMWF analysis exist, while the 47-year ERA5 reanalysis is available solely at 0.25°. Existing coarse-to-fine transfer approaches are limited by irreversible information loss in the coarse-resolution forecast, making the data bottleneck rather than the architecture the primary obstacle to scaling ML-based high-resolution forecasting.

## Input / Output

The forecasting model consumes a 0.1° global atmospheric state (1801×3600 grid) comprising 80 variables across 13 pressure levels (50–1000 hPa) and surface fields, and produces 6-hour-step autoregressive forecasts. The upstream SR stage independently takes each 0.25° ERA5 variable field and outputs a 0.1° pseudo-label, which is then mixed with 8 years of real ECMWF 4D-Var analysis (2017–2024) to form an 18-year synthetic-plus-real training set. Accumulated variables (fdir, ssrd, tp) unavailable in the analysis archive are sourced from zero-hour IFS-HRES forecasts.

## Method

BaguanHR shifts the paradigm from model transfer to data transfer: per-variable Swin2SR super-resolution models are trained on limited paired 0.25°–0.1° samples and applied to 10 years of ERA5 to synthesize 0.1° pseudo-labels, which are then combined with real analysis data to train a 0.1° Swin Transformer forecasting model from scratch. The forecasting architecture uses variable-specific patch tokenization and a two-level hierarchical weather embedding (12 variable groups → group-specific queries → global summary) to make cross-variable attention tractable at 0.1° resolution. A self-adaptive rollout strategy with lead-time-aware loss weighting and stochastic buffer replacement balances short-term fidelity against long-range error accumulation during autoregressive training. The paper provides theoretical justification (Theorems 1–3) showing that properly augmented synthetic data can yield smaller generalization error than clean data alone, and empirically demonstrates power-law data scaling: doubling training data from 7 to 18 years reduces 72-hour RMSE by 4.6%.

## Results

BaguanHR achieves a 4.0% average RMSE reduction over IFS-HRES across over 85% of lead times within 72 hours, with gains of 5.8% at 24 h and 9.7% at 72 h. In extreme-event verification, it outperforms IFS-HRES by over 30% on threshold-based z850 extremes and by >10% Threat Score on q850 ≥ 14 g/kg heavy-moisture detection. Data scaling from 7 to 18 years of training data yields a 4.6% RMSE reduction at 72 h and 4.9% at 120 h, confirming a power-law scaling relationship. The synthetic-plus-real paradigm outperforms the coarse-to-fine transfer baseline by up to 20% RMSE across variables.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the operational physics-based IFS-HRES [10], BaguanHR wins on RMSE for over 85% of lead times within 72 h (4.0% average reduction; 9.7% at 72 h) and dominates on extreme-event metrics (>30% on z850 threshold, >10% TS on q850). Versus the SR-upscaled ML baselines (Baguan+swin2sr [27], GraphCast+swin2sr [19], Pangu+swin2sr [3]), BaguanHR shows a clear short-range advantage, with 15% RMSE improvement over Baguan+swin2sr on percentile-based temperature extremes and superior power-spectral fidelity at short wavelengths. Against the coarse-to-fine transfer baseline Baguan+GHR (re-implemented from Fengwu-GHR [12]), the synthetic-plus-real paradigm achieves up to 20% RMSE reduction, demonstrating that data transfer via per-variable super-resolution substantially outperforms architectural fine-tuning on limited 0.1° data. BaguanHR does not report a clear advantage at 10–15 day lead times, where large-scale dynamics dominate and fine-scale details become weakly predictable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant coarse-to-fine transfer paradigm (Aurora, Fengwu-GHR) that adapts pretrained 0.25° forecast models to 0.1° via architectural modifications and limited fine-tuning. Instead, it reframes the resolution-transfer problem as a data-generation problem, leveraging per-variable super-resolution (Swin2SR) to synthesize 0.1° training data from decades of ERA5 reanalysis. The forecasting backbone builds on the Swin Transformer architecture and the Baguan pre-training framework, while the scaling-law analysis extends prior work on compute/data scaling at sub-0.25° resolutions to the 0.1° regime for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that high-resolution ML forecasting is primarily data-limited and that variable-wise SR provides a general, architecture-agnostic solution to the data bottleneck, this work shifts the field's focus from model design to data construction. The established power-law scaling at 0.1° (4.6–4.9% RMSE reduction from 7→18 years) gives practitioners a concrete roadmap for continued improvement. Open questions include whether the scaling law saturates beyond 18 years, how the approach extends to ensemble forecasting, and whether SR-generated pseudo-labels introduce subtle biases that accumulate over long auto-regressive rollouts (gains diminish notably at 10–15 day lead times).
