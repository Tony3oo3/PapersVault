## Problem

Existing medical 3D super-resolution methods face a fundamental trade-off: per-subject optimization (e.g., CuNeRF) is computationally prohibitive and cannot generalize across subjects, while coordinate-based implicit neural representations (e.g., ArSSR) suffer from spectral bias that over-smooths high-frequency anatomical structures. No prior approach simultaneously achieves fast feed-forward inference, robust cross-dataset generalization, and faithful reconstruction of fine tissue boundaries and pathological edges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262703/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution 3D medical volume (MRI or CT, e.g., 32×32×32 patches) as input and produces a high-resolution 3D volume at an arbitrary upsampling scale (2×, 3×, 4×, 6×, or 8×) in a single forward pass without per-subject optimization. The output resolution is decoupled from the network architecture: the continuous Gaussian intensity field is simply queried at a denser spatial grid, so one trained model handles all scales. Batch normalization is deliberately omitted throughout to preserve absolute intensity values (e.g., Hounsfield Units in CT) that are clinically critical for diagnosis.

## Method

MedGSSR reformulates 3D medical super-resolution as a feed-forward prediction of an explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting field. A Pyramid Anatomical Encoder (3D U-Net without batch norm) produces two complementary feature streams: a coarse 1× map capturing organ-scale geometry and a fine 2× map (via PixelShuffle with input-residual concatenation) capturing local high-frequency texture. A Hierarchical Gaussian Projector then applies two parallel MLP heads to these streams, each predicting m=4 sub-voxel Gaussian primitives per voxel with learnable positional offsets, scales, rotations, and amplitudes—structural Gaussians (larger support, higher amplitude) model macroscopic anatomy while textural Gaussians (smaller support, lower amplitude) refine microscopic boundaries. A Differentiable Gaussian Voxelizer aggregates all primitives into a continuous 3D intensity field via weighted Gaussian superposition with 3σ Mahalanobis truncation, enabling end-to-end training with a voxel-wise L1 loss and arbitrary-resolution inference by querying the field at any target grid.

## Results

On the MSD MRI dataset (intra-domain, 4× upsampling), MedGSSR achieves 32.10 dB PSNR, 0.9541 SSIM, and 0.1246 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline ArSSR by +3.59 dB in PSNR. On the MELA CT dataset at 4×, MedGSSR reaches 37.31 dB PSNR, surpassing ArSSR by +2.68 dB and NAB-GS by +3.18 dB. In cross-domain evaluation on UHRCT CT at the extreme 8× scale, MedGSSR attains 35.24 dB PSNR, exceeding ArSSR by +5.86 dB and CuNeRF by +4.74 dB without any fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedGSSR consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and scale tested. Against ArSSR (neural implicit, INR-based), MedGSSR gains +3.59 dB PSNR on MSD MRI at 4×, +2.68 dB on MELA CT at 4×, +3.97 dB on HCP MRI (cross-domain) at 4×, and +5.86 dB on UHRCT CT (cross-domain) at 8×, demonstrating that explicit Gaussian primitives avoid the spectral bias and over-smoothing inherent to coordinate-based MLPs. Against CuNeRF (self-supervised, per-subject NeRF optimization), MedGSSR achieves +3.84 dB on MSD at 4× and +4.74 dB on UHRCT at 8×, while also eliminating the need for per-subject optimization entirely. Against NAB-GS (2D-diffusion-guided 3DGS), MedGSSR surpasses the reported 4× CT result by +3.18 dB, showing that operating purely in the 3D domain avoids the modality gap introduced by 3D-to-2D projection. Notably, in the clinical segmentation validation (Table 5), CuNeRF collapses to 0.3618 average Dice due to severe artifacts, while MedGSSR achieves 0.8178, confirming that the reconstruction quality translates to downstream clinical utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedGSSR repurposes the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation [14]—originally designed for real-time novel-view synthesis of natural scenes—into a continuous volumetric intensity field for medical super-resolution, replacing the coordinate-to-intensity mapping of implicit neural representations (ArSSR [25], CuNeRF [5]). It also adapts the feed-forward 3DGS prediction paradigm from vision (PixelSplat [2], Splatter Image [22]) to dense 3D medical volumes, where prior feed-forward 3DGS work targets sparse natural scenes. The key departure from prior medical SR is the explicit decoupling of structural and textural reconstruction via a hierarchical dual-resolution design, and the elimination of both per-subject optimization and 2D proxy supervision (as in NAB-GS [18]) in favor of a single end-to-end 3D-native mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The feed-forward formulation removes the per-subject optimization bottleneck that has limited clinical deployment of prior medical SR methods, enabling single-pass inference on unseen subjects—a practical requirement for real-world scanning pipelines. The strong cross-dataset generalization (trained on MSD, tested on HCP; trained on MELA, tested on UHRCT) and the downstream segmentation validation suggest the learned 3D anatomical priors are genuinely transferable rather than dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other modalities (ultrasound, PET, functional MRI), how it handles very large or anisotropic volumes beyond the 32³ patch size used in training, and whether the 3DGS representation can capture subtle pathological textures (e.g., early-stage lesions) that may require even finer sub-voxel decomposition.
