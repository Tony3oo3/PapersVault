## Problem

Aggressive cross-modal alignment in multimodal survival analysis can cause 'over-alignment collapse,' where modality-specific signals (e.g., morphological patterns in WSIs, gene-level variation in genomics) are diluted, causing multimodal models to underperform strong single-modality baselines. Existing methods address alignment and distinctiveness independently, lacking a unified principle that jointly captures shared prognostic structure while preserving what makes each modality unique.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261572/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pathology whole-slide images (divided into patches and encoded by a pretrained UNI ViT-Large or ResNet50 backbone) and gene-expression profiles organized into 50 fixed Hallmark pathways covering 4,241 genes, each densified into a D-dimensional token. It outputs a scalar survival log-risk score trained with Cox partial likelihood, which is then thresholded into high/low risk groups for Kaplan-Meier stratification. Evaluation uses 5-fold site-stratified cross-validation on five TCGA cohorts (BRCA, BLCA, STAD, CRC, KIRC) with disease-specific survival as the target.

## Method

TTA follows a two-stage 'Together-Then-Apart' principle. In the TOGETHER stage, both modality token sets are concatenated and assigned to a shared bank of K=32 prototypes via an unbalanced optimal transport plan with a KL-regularized prototype marginal and a curriculum mass ρ that ramps from 0.1 to 1.0, allowing uncertain tokens to diffuse to a sink early in training before matching sharpens. In the APART stage, modality-specific anchors are appended to the prototype tokens and refined by a lightweight self-attention module, then an InfoNCE-style contrastive loss pulls each modality's mean representation toward its own anchor while pushing it away from the other modality's anchor. A co-attention prediction head exchanges information between the two refined prototype streams and maps the pooled joint representation to a survival risk score via a linear head, trained end-to-end with the Cox loss plus the contrastive and instance-level auxiliary losses.

## Results

TTA achieves the best overall C-index of 0.693 across five TCGA cancer cohorts (BRCA, BLCA, STAD, CRC, KIRC), outperforming the second-best multimodal method MMP (0.667) by +2.6%. Per-cohort, TTA ranks first on BRCA (0.726), STAD (0.613), CRC (0.685), and KIRC (0.778), and second on BLCA (0.662 vs. LD-CVAE's 0.676). Kaplan-Meier stratification yields statistically significant log-rank p-values across all five cohorts (range: 1.86×10⁻¹⁰ to 3.02×10⁻²), confirming effective risk separation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTA outperforms all 16 baselines on overall C-index (0.693), with the closest competitors being MMP (0.667, +2.6% gap), LD-CVAE (0.665, +2.8% gap), and MRePath (0.663, +3.0% gap). Among unimodal baselines, the strongest is OTSurv (0.644, histopathology-only), which TTA surpasses by +4.9%; genomic-only methods (SNN 0.611, SNNTrans 0.616) trail by 7–8%. TTA wins on 4 of 5 cohorts (BRCA, STAD, CRC, KIRC) but is second on BLCA, where LD-CVAE leads (0.676 vs. 0.662). The largest per-cohort gain is on CRC (+8.3% over MRePath), where the paper attributes the improvement to the APART stage preserving WSI-specific morphological signals that aggressive alignment would dilute. All multimodal baselines (MCAT, CMTA, MOTCat, SurvPath, PIBD) fall in the 0.615–0.646 range, well below TTA, suggesting that explicit modality-distinctiveness regularization provides a consistent advantage over attention-only or information-bottleneck fusion strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

TTA builds directly on MMP [37] for pathway-based gene tokenization and prototype compression, and on OT-Surv [33] for unbalanced optimal transport in MIL aggregation. However, it departs from the dominant paradigm in multimodal survival analysis—where methods like MCAT, MOTCat, SurvPath, and PIBD focus primarily on cross-modal alignment or fusion—by explicitly identifying 'over-alignment collapse' as a failure mode and introducing a two-stage principle (align first, then separate). The shared-prototype design with joint UOT over concatenated tokens, combined with modality-specific anchors and contrastive regularization, distinguishes TTA from prior disentangling approaches (PIBD, MRePath) that treat shared and specific components more independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underappreciated problem: multimodal survival models can underperform strong single-modality baselines when alignment is too aggressive, as demonstrated starkly on CRC where removing the APART stage causes a 9.8% C-index drop. The Together-Then-Apart principle offers a generalizable design heuristic for multimodal representation learning beyond survival analysis. Limitations include evaluation on only five TCGA cohorts with moderate sample sizes, a single hyperparameter configuration, and reliance on fixed Hallmark pathway groupings; whether the principle extends to additional modalities (e.g., proteomics, imaging beyond WSI) or to settings with more than two modalities remains open.
