## Problem

Existing agent-based long video understanding methods rely on static, manually designed multi-agent systems (MAS) with fixed topologies that are overly complex for simple queries and insufficiently flexible for diverse reasoning demands. This 'one-size-fits-all' rigidity wastes computational resources on redundant global analyses and cannot adapt to the specific complexity of each video-question pair.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262421/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a long video (sampled as 32 frames by default) and a text query as joint multimodal input to the Meta-Agent. Rather than directly producing an answer, the Meta-Agent first generates executable LangGraph code (MAS-as-Code) that is deterministically compiled into a directed computational graph where nodes are agent roles and edges encode execution flow. The instantiated MAS then executes to produce a final QA answer, with the intermediate code artifact serving as the structured output of the Meta-Agent's policy.

## Method

DyMAC formulates multi-agent orchestration as a single-pass code generation task: a VLM-based Meta-Agent (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) decodes an executable code block that specifies a heterogeneous directed graph, where each node is parameterized by four meta-attributes (Instruction, Driving Model, Available Tools, Reasoning Pattern) and edges dictate information flow and control logic. The Meta-Agent is trained in two stages: SFT on a 3,519-sample corpus of (video, question, optimal MAS code) pairs synthesized from CG-Bench to instill architectural and syntactic competence, followed by RLVR via GRPO with a joint reward that linearly combines verifiable QA accuracy and an efficiency penalty computed as the maximum cumulative execution cost along any root-to-leaf path. This dual-objective reward explicitly trades off reasoning quality against computational overhead, while dynamic routing between 3B and 7B driving models and six reasoning paradigms (CoT, ReAct, Plan-and-Execute, Self-Refine, LLM-Debate, Self-Consistency) allows the system to allocate cognitive resources proportionally to each sub-task's complexity.

## Results

DyMAC (7B Meta-Agent) achieves 70.6% on Video-MME, 73.2% on MLVU, 55.6% on LVBench, and 60.7% on LongVideoBench, yielding an overall average of 65.0% across the four benchmarks. This surpasses the second-best method (Video-o3, 61.7% avg.) by 3.3 percentage points. In terms of efficiency, DyMAC records an average inference time of 4.6 s/min of video, substantially faster than other agent-based baselines such as ReAct (8.3 s), VideoRAG (20.8 s), and Vgent (24.1 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among agent-based methods, DyMAC outperforms ReAct (61.1% avg.) by 3.9 points, Vgent (59.7% on LongVideoBench) by 1.0 points on that benchmark, and VideoRAG (58.7% on LongVideoBench) by 2.0 points, while also being 1.8×–5.2× faster in inference time. Against multi-turn tool-invocation methods, DyMAC leads Video-o3 (61.7% avg.) by 3.3 points and Video-Zoomer (57.7% on LongVideoBench) by 3.0 points. Among end-to-end MLLMs, DyMAC surpasses the strongest (LLaVA-Video at 70.8% MLVU) on Video-MME (70.6 vs. 63.3) and LVBench (55.6 vs. –), though LLaVA-Video edges it slightly on MLVU (70.8 vs. 73.2 is actually DyMAC winning). The only method that exceeds DyMAC on individual benchmarks is VideoLucy (72.5 Video-MME, 76.1 MLVU, 58.8 LVBench), but it requires a 67B auxiliary model and is 4.3× slower (19.9 s vs. 4.6 s). DyMAC thus achieves the best overall average while maintaining competitive per-benchmark scores with a single 7B model.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyMAC sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) agent-based long video understanding, where prior work (VideoLucy, Vgent, VideoExplorer, LongVideoAgent) decomposes video reasoning into multi-agent pipelines but relies on fixed, hand-designed topologies; and (2) automated agentic system design (ADAS, GPTSwarm, MAS-GPT), where a Meta-Agent generates agent workflows as code. The paper's key departure is transplanting the single-pass MAS-as-Code paradigm from MAS-GPT into the multimodal video domain, adding heterogeneous driving-model routing and a composite accuracy-efficiency reward via RLVR (GRPO) that prior video-agent methods lack. It explicitly positions itself against static MAS by arguing that instance-level complexity varies too much for any fixed workflow to be optimal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for reducing inference latency in agent-based video QA (4.6 s/min vs. 8.3–24.1 s for competing agent methods) while matching or exceeding their accuracy, which is directly relevant to deployment. However, the approach is tightly coupled to the Qwen-VL model family and LangGraph code generation, raising open questions about portability to other VLM backbones or non-video multimodal settings. The ablation showing that an untrained dynamic MAS performs substantially worse than a vanilla single model (50.9% vs. 63.3% average) also highlights a fragility: the entire benefit hinges on the quality of the SFT+RLVR training pipeline, and it is unclear how much of the 3.3% gain over the second-best method would persist under different training data scales or model sizes. Whether the code-generation formulation scales gracefully to even longer videos (beyond the ~40-minute LVBench clips) or to open-ended generation tasks remains to be tested.
