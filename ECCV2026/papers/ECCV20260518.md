## Problem

Low-cost MEMS IMUs on UAVs exhibit complex, high-order non-linear bias dynamics under aggressive maneuvers that traditional random-walk or Gauss-Markov models cannot capture, causing significant drift in LiDAR-inertial odometry. Existing neural approaches (NODEs, sequence-learning) model bias evolution deterministically and lack explicit heteroscedastic stochasticity or the rigorous uncertainty quantification required for safety-critical Kalman filtering, leaving a gap between deep representational power and mathematically consistent geometric estimation.

## Input / Output

The system consumes Livox Mid-360 LiDAR point clouds (processed at ~19.2 Hz) and BMI088 IMU gyroscope/accelerometer streams (pre-integrated at 200 Hz) on an NVIDIA Orin NX. Beyond the standard 6-DOF pose and IMU bias outputs, the method produces a full error-state covariance matrix with calibrated uncertainty (93% NIS consistency), and the neural SDE network receives multi-scale inputs including measurement derivatives and a real-time motion-complexity scalar C(t) that drives the adaptive gating between learned and linear bias branches.

## Method

NeuroSDE-LIO replaces the fixed random-walk bias model in FAST-LIO2's IEKF with an adaptive hybrid SDE: db = [α(t)·f(b,u;θ) + (1−α(t))·L·u] dt + Σ^{1/2} dW, where f is a small MLP+ResBlock network (14,243 params) and α(t) is a sigmoid gate driven by a sliding-window motion-complexity metric C(t) that smoothly blends learned dynamics with a linear prior. A key theoretical contribution is a differentiable covariance propagation scheme that analytically derives the bias-block Jacobians of the error-state transition matrix—including a coupling term ∂α/∂C · J_C that links orientation uncertainty to the adaptive gate—so that learned stochasticity flows consistently into the IEKF prediction step. Neural weights are frozen after offline pre-training with Vicon-aided RTS-smoothing labels; at deployment only the low-dimensional linear branch L and gating parameters η are updated in a 2–5 s sliding window with gradient clipping and NIS consistency checks, keeping the full pipeline at 52.1 ms per scan (19.2 Hz) with 385 MB memory.

## Results

On the self-collected high-dynamic UAV-Lab dataset (Vicon-validated), NeuroSDE-LIO achieves 1.34 m APE and 1.12 deg/m RPE, reducing FAST-LIO2's APE by 37.1% and RPE by 40.1%. Across all three datasets, the method improves average APE by 37.4% over FAST-LIO2 (0.90 m vs. 1.43 m) and by 23.7% over the deterministic NODE variant (0.90 m vs. 1.18 m). Uncertainty calibration reaches 93.0% NIS consistency (ideal ≈ 95%), compared to 77.4% for FAST-LIO2 and 84.1% for FAST-LIO2-NODE. The system runs at 52.1 ms per LiDAR scan (19.2 Hz) on an NVIDIA Orin NX, adding only 15.3% latency over FAST-LIO2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuroSDE-LIO outperforms all eight compared methods on APE and RPE across every dataset. Against the direct IEKF baseline FAST-LIO2, it reduces average APE from 1.43 m to 0.90 m (−37.4%) and average RPE from 1.28 to 0.74 deg/m (−45.2%), with the largest gap on UAV-Lab (1.34 m vs. 2.13 m APE). The controlled NODE ablation (FAST-LIO2-NODE) confirms the stochastic diffusion head contributes an additional 23.7% APE improvement over deterministic neural drift alone. Among recent high-dynamic LIO baselines on UAV-Lab, NeuroSDE-LIO beats iG-LIO (1.34 vs. 1.48 m APE), Adaptive-LIO (1.34 vs. 1.56 m), SUPER (1.34 vs. 1.62 m), and SR-LIO (1.34 vs. 1.74 m) while maintaining 19.2 Hz throughput—faster than iG-LIO (11.9 Hz) and SR-LIO (13.5 Hz) but slightly slower than FAST-LIO2 (22.1 Hz). ORB-SLAM3 and LIO-SAM, which rely on visual or graph-based backends, trail by 1.0–2.1 m APE on UAV-Lab, reflecting their sensitivity to aggressive motion and vibration. cuVSLAM (VIO reference) achieves 1.91 m APE at 28.5 Hz but uses a different sensor modality and is not a like-for-like LIO competitor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FAST-LIO2 IEKF backbone by replacing its simplified random-walk IMU bias model with a learned Neural SDE, building on the Neural ODE paradigm (Chen et al., 2018) and the Neural SDE framework (Boral et al., 2023) originally developed for turbulence modeling. The key conceptual departure from prior learning-based IMU work (sequence models, NODEs) is the explicit stochastic diffusion term that captures heteroscedastic motor-induced noise, combined with an adaptive hybrid gate that blends learned dynamics with linear priors based on real-time motion complexity. The differentiable covariance propagation mechanism is a novel theoretical bridge that maps learned SDE uncertainties into the IEKF's Lyapunov-based state prediction while preserving geometric consistency on SO(3).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The differentiable covariance propagation scheme provides a reusable template for integrating any learned stochastic dynamics into invariant filtering, addressing a gap that has limited adoption of neural bias models in safety-critical navigation. The 93% NIS consistency (vs. 66–84% for baselines) demonstrates that learned stochasticity can yield well-calibrated uncertainty, which is essential for downstream planning and collision avoidance in GPS-denied UAV operations. Limitations include the added inference overhead (~15% latency), reliance on Vicon-validated lab data for ground truth, and the single-IMU scope; open questions include multi-IMU transfer, robustness under sensor saturation, and certifiable safety guarantees for learning-enhanced EKFs.
