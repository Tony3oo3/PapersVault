## Problem

Autoregressive MLLMs achieve high structural accuracy in document-to-markup parsing but suffer from severe sequential decoding latency, while existing parallel and fully diffusion-based methods accelerate inference at the cost of breaking rigid syntactic dependencies (e.g., mismatched brackets, corrupted table alignments). No prior method simultaneously preserves strict structural correctness and delivers meaningful wall-time speedup on dense, markup-heavy documents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262595/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single document image (scanned page, PDF render, or photographed document) as visual input and produces a complete structured markup sequence (LaTeX formulas, HTML tables, Markdown text) as output. During decoding, the model consumes a composite input at each step: a verified autoregressive prefix concatenated with a speculative 16-token window containing partially denoised tokens and <INIT> mask tokens, allowing it to refine and populate the window in a single forward pass before verification.

## Method

DARL introduces a Sliding Diffusion Block (SDB) that maintains a verified AR prefix for syntactic correctness while speculatively generating a localized 16-token window in parallel via discrete denoising, with a consistency-based verification mechanism that accepts tokens only where the current diffusion output matches the previous step's speculation. Training addresses exposure bias through Online Monte Carlo Trajectory Generation (OMTG), which dynamically samples K candidate trajectories from the model's real-time state and supervises the full sequence (not just the window) with a cross-entropy DAR loss. A second training signal, Diffusion Trajectory Preference Optimization (DTPO), frames window generation as a GRPO-based RL problem with a composite reward combining immediate LCP accuracy and a look-ahead consistency term that evaluates how well the current trajectory facilitates prediction of the next window. The total objective jointly optimizes the supervised DAR loss and the DTPO policy gradient loss, anchoring the model's autoregressive foundation while refining trajectory selection for rapid convergence.

## Results

On OmniDocBench-1.5, DARL achieves a 2.31× wall-time speedup (11.19 s/doc, 485.6 avg. iterations) while reaching an Overall score of 87.69, marginally exceeding the AR baseline (87.57) and all parallel decoding methods. On olmOCR-Bench, DARL attains a 2.27× speedup with an Overall score of 81.7, substantially outperforming the AR baseline (78.9) and the strongest parallel baselines MSN and EAGLE-3 (both 77.9). The largest accuracy gains appear in structurally rigid categories: Table Structure (89.41 vs. 88.76 for MSN) and Old Scans on olmOCR-Bench (62.5 vs. 41.6 for AR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DARL outperforms all nine baselines on wall-time speedup, achieving 2.31× on OmniDocBench-1.5 versus 2.07× for MSN (the closest competitor), 1.74× for EAGLE-3, 1.69× for DiffusionVL, 1.34× for LADE, and 1.09× for Jacobi Decoding. On overall accuracy, DARL (87.69) edges out the AR baseline dots.ocr (87.57) and clearly surpasses MSN (87.23), EAGLE-3 (87.38), LADE (87.32), and DiffusionVL (85.92); the gap is most pronounced in Table Structure (89.41 vs. 88.76 for MSN) and Read Order (0.072 vs. 0.079 for MSN). On olmOCR-Bench, DARL's 81.7 overall score exceeds the AR baseline (78.9) by 2.8 points and MSN/EAGLE-3 (77.9) by 3.8 points, with the largest margin in Old Scans (62.5 vs. 41.6). As a plug-and-play accelerator, DARL delivers 2.20–2.33× speedup on general VLLMs (Qwen3-VL 2B, InternVL3.5 2B) with accuracy drops ≤0.6%, and 2.25–2.31× on expert models (dots.ocr, Nanonets-OCR2) where it actually improves accuracy slightly (e.g., Nanonets-OCR2: 82.91 vs. 82.76). Scaling to InternVL3.5-14B pushes the speedup to 2.78× with only a 0.17-point accuracy drop, confirming that larger models benefit more from DARL's diffusion-based speculation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DARL sits at the intersection of three research threads: lookahead decoding (Fu et al., ICML'24), which it extends by replacing n-gram heuristic matching with discrete diffusion denoising within the speculative window; diffusion LLMs (LLaDA 2.0, Bie et al.), whose forward-masking/reverse-denoising framework provides the parallel generation mechanism; and GRPO-based preference optimization (Shao et al., DeepSeekMath), which DTPO adapts to score and select diffusion trajectories. The key conceptual departure is from both pure AR decoding (sequential, high latency) and fully parallel diffusion (destroys structural dependencies like bracket matching and table alignment), as well as from prior speculative decoders (EAGLE-3, MSN, Jacobi) whose relaxed priors fail on rigid markup. The Sliding Diffusion Block's verified-prefix-plus-speculative-window design is the novel architectural contribution that reconciles these conflicting requirements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 2.3× wall-time speedup that simultaneously matches or marginally exceeds AR accuracy is practically significant for real-time document parsing deployment, and the plug-and-play validation across four architectures (Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5, dots.ocr, Nanonets-OCR2) at multiple scales (2B–14B) strengthens its general applicability. The look-ahead reward (r₂) is the most interesting conceptual contribution: by penalizing trajectories that are locally plausible but cause downstream structural drift, it turns local speculation into a globally consistent optimization, which is why accuracy can *exceed* the AR baseline rather than merely match it. Open questions include whether the method transfers to high-entropy open-ended generation (e.g., image captioning), how optimal window size and denoising steps scale beyond 14B parameters, and whether tree-structured verification can further reduce the residual sequential cost.
