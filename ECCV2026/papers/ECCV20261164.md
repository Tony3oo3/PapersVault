## Problem

Text-conditioned visual autoregressive (VAR) models such as Infinity and InfinityStar produce nearly identical outputs for the same prompt, exhibiting a critical lack of per-prompt diversity that has gone largely unnoticed amid the field's focus on image quality. Existing diversity-enhancement techniques from diffusion models (CFG scheduling, condition-annealing) have not been systematically studied in the scale-wise autoregressive setting of VAR, and naively applying them introduces severe visual artifacts.

## Input / Output

DIVERSEVAR is a training-free, inference-time framework that takes a fixed text prompt and a pre-trained VAR model (Infinity, Switti for images; InfinityStar for video) as input, and produces multiple diverse generated images or videos from that single prompt. For video generation, the method is applied to the keyframe generation stage of InfinityStar's two-stage pipeline (keyframe synthesis followed by temporal generation), producing 480p videos with 81 frames. No model weights are modified; all diversity enhancement occurs through modified sampling schedules and latent-space operations during generation.

## Method

DIVERSEVAR is a two-stage diversify-then-refine pipeline. In the first stage, condition-annealing injects Gaussian noise into the text embedding (rather than the <SOS> token or via CFG scheduling) with a cosine annealing schedule over the first few scales, which the authors identify as the most effective diversity-enhancement strategy for VAR. In the second stage, Scale-Travel performs latent refinement by exploiting VAR's multi-scale autoencoder: at a chosen intermediate scale l (where global structure is already formed), the current accumulated feature map Z_l is re-encoded through the multi-scale encoder but only the first m < l scales are retained, effectively rewinding to a coarser representation. Generation then resumes from scale m+1 without noise injection, allowing the autoregressive model to reconstruct clean fine-scale details while preserving the diverse coarse layout established during the noisy phase. This mechanism requires only a minor algorithmic change relative to standard multi-scale encoding and adds minimal computational overhead.

## Results

On MJHQ-30K with Infinity, DIVERSEVAR achieves LPIPS-MPD of 0.48 (+45.5% over the 0.33 baseline) while retaining ImageReward of 1.08 (only −11.5% vs. 1.22 baseline), whereas condition-annealing alone reaches 0.56 MPD but collapses ImageReward to 0.49 (−59.8%). On MS-COCO, DIVERSEVAR improves MPD from 0.37 to 0.52 (+40.5%) with ImageReward dropping only from 1.16 to 1.04 (−10.3%). For video generation on VBench-2.0, DIVERSEVAR raises diversity from 0.270 to 0.487 (+80.4%) with a negligible quality drop (0.806 → 0.804), while condition-annealing alone reaches 0.627 diversity but sacrifices quality (0.806 → 0.794).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Infinity (CVPR 2025), DIVERSEVAR wins on the diversity–quality trade-off: it achieves +45.5% LPIPS-MPD improvement over the baseline while losing only 11.5% ImageReward, whereas condition-annealing alone gains +69.7% MPD but destroys quality (−59.8% IR). Against Switti (arXiv 2024), the same pattern holds: DIVERSEVAR yields +27.3% MPD (0.44→0.56) with a modest −16.2% IR drop, while condition-annealing alone gives +31.8% MPD but a −43.2% IR collapse. Against InfinityStar (NeurIPS 2025) for video, DIVERSEVAR improves VBench diversity by +80.4% (0.270→0.487) with essentially zero quality loss (−0.002), outperforming condition-annealing which reaches higher diversity (0.627) but at a larger quality cost (−0.012). In all three comparisons, DIVERSEVAR consistently defines the Pareto frontier, confirming that scale-travel recovers the quality lost to noise injection while preserving most of the diversity gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diversity-enhancement techniques originally developed for diffusion models—specifically CFG weight scheduling (Kynkäänniemi et al.) and condition-annealing via text-embedding noise injection (CADS, Sadat et al.)—to the next-scale visual autoregressive (VAR) setting, where no prior systematic study existed. The key departure is the proposed scale-travel refinement, which repurposes VAR's multi-scale VQ-VAE encoder to perform an approximate rollback along the scale axis, analogous to how diffusion-model refinement techniques revert part of the denoising trajectory. The work is positioned as the first to systematically identify and address the diversity deficiency in text-conditioned VAR models such as Infinity and InfinityStar.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VAR models gain traction as fast, high-quality alternatives to diffusion models for image and video generation, the near-identical outputs for a fixed prompt represent a practical bottleneck for user choice and downstream test-time search. The scale-travel technique offers a training-free, architecture-aware refinement that could be adopted broadly across VAR-based systems. However, the paper acknowledges that ~6% of samples still exhibit visual artifacts and that inference cost is slightly elevated, leaving open whether the refinement can be made fully artifact-free or extended to other autoregressive architectures that lack an explicit multi-scale decomposition.
