## Problem

Existing cross-domain few-shot segmentation methods focus on mitigating style-induced distribution shifts but overlook two critical degradations in support-query matching: semantic over-alignment, where the model only distinguishes foreground/background at the source-domain granularity and fails on finer or coarser target-domain semantics, and attribute over-alignment, where rigid channel-wise matching degrades source-insensitive attributes (e.g., color) that become discriminative in the target domain. No prior work simultaneously addresses both forms of over-alignment while remaining adaptable to varying target-domain granularities without external models.

## Input / Output

The method takes K support images (each with a binary mask) and one query image, all resized to 480×480, processed by a ViT-B/16 backbone. During training, Global Style Randomization perturbs inputs via random convolution; during testing, the auxiliary OPSB branch accumulates class statistics across episodes before producing the final binary segmentation mask via adaptive thresholding. The output is a single binary foreground mask per query image, with the auxiliary branch contributing additional confidence maps only at inference time.

## Method

DHANet performs hierarchical aggregation along two orthogonal dimensions. The HSA module uses multi-stage slot attention (2 stages, 10 and 20 spatial slots) to partition pixel features into region prototypes at different granularities, then applies reverse masked average pooling to broadcast each prototype back to its assigned pixels, producing hierarchical semantic-enhanced features. The HCA module mirrors this along the channel dimension (2 stages, 32 and 64 channel slots), grouping channels that activate similar regions into attribute prototypes that are concatenated with the features rather than written back, preserving attribute diversity. An orthogonality loss prevents slot collapse at each stage. At test time, the OPSB module models foreground and background prototypes as Gaussian distributions, updates them online from high-confidence query features via SSP, and samples 20 pseudo-prototypes to provide additional support for an auxiliary matching branch, whose confidence map is fused with the main dense-matching branch before adaptive thresholding.

## Results

DHANet achieves a mean mIoU of 70.93% (1-shot) and 74.26% (5-shot) averaged across four target domains, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art HSL by 2.69% and 1.94% respectively. The gains are most pronounced on domains with larger semantic granularity differences from the source: +4.02% on Deepglobe and +4.89% on ISIC under 1-shot. Notably, DHANet's 1-shot average (70.93%) exceeds the 5-shot average of all competing methods except HSL (72.32%). The method also demonstrates better efficiency than HSL, with lower FLOPs (176.48G vs. 186.52G) and higher inference speed (30.77 vs. 27.25 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DHANet outperforms all 18 compared baselines on the average mIoU across both 1-shot and 5-shot settings. The closest competitor is HSL [33] (AAAI-26, same research group), which DHANet surpasses by 2.69% (1-shot) and 1.94% (5-shot) on average; the gap widens to 4.02% on Deepglobe and 4.89% on ISIC under 1-shot, confirming that hierarchical aggregation is most beneficial when target-domain semantic granularity diverges from the source. Among CD-FSS methods using ViT-B backbones, LoEC [24] (CVPR-25) is the second-best at 65.01/70.43, trailing DHANet by 5.92% (1-shot) and 3.83% (5-shot). On Chest X-ray, DFN [35] achieves the highest 5-shot score (87.14) but DHANet still leads on 1-shot (86.06 vs. 83.18) and on the overall average. FSS-only methods (SSP, FPTrans, VRP-SAM) show substantially lower cross-domain performance, with FPTrans [49] reaching only 62.17/69.06 average, highlighting the necessity of explicit cross-domain design. DHANet also achieves better efficiency than HSL (176.48G FLOPs, 30.77 FPS vs. 186.52G, 27.25 FPS), indicating the hierarchical aggregation adds accuracy without proportional computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

DHANet extends the CD-FSS line initiated by PATNet (ECCV 2022) and subsequent style-gap-focused methods (LoEC, DRA, APSeg) by identifying two overlooked failure modes—semantic over-alignment and attribute over-alignment—that arise when source-domain training forces single-granularity, single-attribute alignment. Its core mechanism repurposes slot attention (Locatello et al., NeurIPS 2020) in a hierarchical, dual-axis fashion: spatial slots aggregate multi-scale region prototypes while channel slots group attribute-correlated channels, a design not previously applied to FSS. The Online Probabilistic Semantic Bank departs from the deterministic single-prototype updates used in DMTNet and PATNet by modeling class representations as evolving Gaussian distributions, and the training protocol builds directly on SSP's self-segmentation loss and HSL's adaptive thresholding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly diagnosing and addressing over-alignment along both spatial and channel axes, DHANet offers a principled explanation for why style-only domain adaptation plateaus on domains with large semantic-granularity or attribute shifts (e.g., dermoscopic ISIC, satellite Deepglobe). The probabilistic semantic bank provides a lightweight, fine-tuning-free alternative to online adaptation that avoids knowledge forgetting, making it attractive for deployment settings where per-episode retraining is impractical. Open questions include whether the fixed two-stage hierarchy generalizes to domains requiring more than two granularity levels, whether the orthogonal slot constraint becomes a bottleneck at higher channel depths, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-class or open-vocabulary CD-FSS settings.
