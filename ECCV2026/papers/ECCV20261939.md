## Problem

Retargeting human-object interaction demonstrations to physics-based simulation requires reproducing object motion and contact dynamics, but position-only finger trajectories cannot encode the interaction forces that make manipulation succeed. Directly tracking dense finger kinematics overconstrains contact-rich behavior and ties the policy to a specific hand embodiment. The paper asks whether manipulation can be guided by object and contact outcomes instead of explicit finger pose supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261939/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a reference sequence of SMPL-X body poses, object trajectories, and per-rigid-body contact labels, but the goal state explicitly excludes all 30 finger joints—only 19 body joints and 2 wrists receive kinematic pose targets. Contact targets are aggregated to hand-level binary indicators (one per hand) rather than per-finger labels. The policy outputs Gaussian-distributed target rotations for all 51 actuated joints in exponential map coordinates, converted to torques via PD controllers, and is trained per-scene in Isaac Gym at 30 Hz with 2048 parallel environments.

## Method

WristMimic decouples whole-body control into two regimes: kinematic pose tracking for contact-free joints (body + wrists) and object/contact-driven learning for the 30 finger joints, which receive no pose supervision. The wrist serves as the structural bridge, and two mechanisms ensure reliable wrist placement during the contact window: (1) reward weight modulation that zeros out proximal arm joint rewards while keeping wrist weights at full strength, and (2) phase-specific reset thresholds that terminate episodes if the wrist deviates beyond 7 cm / 0.2 rad during the grasping phase (tighter than the 15 cm / 0.5 rad used in approach and stabilization). The multiplicative reward combines joint position/rotation tracking (over contact-free joints only), object position/rotation tracking, and contact alignment, optimized with PPO. This design enables finger-agnostic retargeting across different hand morphologies without requiring finger-specific motion capture data.

## Results

WristMimic achieves a 91.1% average success rate across 40 sequences (20 from OMOMO, 20 from ParaHome), with average object position error of 11.3 cm and rotation error of 23.1°. On OMOMO, it reaches 98.9% success with 7.3 cm position error and 12.2° rotation error; on ParaHome, it achieves 83.3% success with 15.3 cm and 33.9° errors. These results are obtained without any explicit finger pose supervision, relying solely on wrist kinematic guidance and object/contact-driven rewards for finger behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WristMimic substantially outperforms both baselines on every reported metric. Against InterMimic [66], the most dramatic gap appears on ParaHome, where InterMimic achieves only 0.1% success rate (83.5 cm position error, 73.3° rotation error) while WristMimic reaches 83.3% success with 15.3 cm and 33.9° errors — a gap the authors attribute to ParaHome's more dexterous scenarios (small handles, narrow contact regions) where wrist placement is critical. On OMOMO, WristMimic still leads with 98.9% vs. 86.6% success and roughly halves both position (7.3 vs. 14.2 cm) and rotation (12.2° vs. 22.2°) errors. Against SkillMimicV2 [71], which is evaluated only on ParaHome due to its bone-vector dependency, WristMimic improves success from 1.3% to 83.3% and reduces position error from 72.1 cm to 15.3 cm. Notably, WristMimic maintains relatively consistent performance across both datasets (98.9% vs. 83.3%), whereas InterMimic shows a stark OMOMO-to-ParaHome degradation, suggesting the wrist-guided formulation provides more robust structural guidance for delicate grasping.

## Relation to Prior Work

WristMimic sits at the intersection of physics-based whole-body motion imitation (PHC, PULSE, InterMimic) and dexterous manipulation learning (OmniGrasp, SkillMimic), but departs from the dominant paradigm of dense kinematic finger supervision. While InterMimic and SkillMimic track all joints including fingers against reference trajectories, WristMimic removes all 30 finger joint targets and instead lets fingers emerge from object-pose tracking and contact alignment rewards. The wrist-as-bridge concept echoes Chen et al.'s hierarchical wrist-guided manipulation [9], but WristMimic extends this to full whole-body control with explicit phase-specific reset thresholds and reward weight modulation that prior HOI retargeting methods do not employ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: eliminating the need for dense finger-level motion capture or retargeting data lowers the barrier to physics-based manipulation learning and enables cross-embodiment transfer (demonstrated across three hand morphologies). However, the framework remains scene-specific (one policy per scene), limiting immediate deployment, and does not address fine-grained in-hand manipulation requiring precise finger repositioning. Open questions include whether the wrist-centric formulation can be extended to scene-general policies and whether the decoupled supervision strategy transfers to real-robot settings where contact sensing is noisier.
