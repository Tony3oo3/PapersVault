## Problem

Mainstream 3D asset retrieval treats relevance as a binary category-level attribute, implicitly assuming intra-class homogeneity and ignoring substantial variations in geometry, style, texture, and functionality among assets sharing the same label. This misalignment between the binary matching formulation and real-world user needs—where fine-grained similarity ranking matters—imposes an intrinsic performance ceiling that no amount of inter-class discrimination can overcome.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261945/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts any arbitrary subset of three modalities (point cloud, RGB image, text) for both query and gallery items, with image and text features reduced to a single pooled token (L_i = L_t = 1) while point cloud retains its full token sequence. Rather than consuming raw inputs, ReMU3D operates on precomputed frozen CLIP embeddings, reducing online encoding to 167–210 GFLOPs (~0.03 s per query). The output is a unit-length embedding vector obtained by mean-pooling M learnable query tokens after Q-MoE aggregation, used for cosine-similarity-based graded relevance ranking; the model is explicitly modality-agnostic, handling missing modalities without architectural changes.

## Method

ReMU3D reparameterizes the pretrained CLIP semantic space through two sequential modules. The Semantic Conditioned Interaction (SCI) module concatenates image, text, and point cloud tokens into a joint sequence and processes it through 4 Transformer layers with global multi-head self-attention over all tokens, followed by modality-specific FFNs; image and text tokens function as semantic conditions that modulate attention over point cloud tokens, injecting appearance and linguistic cues to prevent intra-class semantic collapse. The Query-Guided Mixture-of-Experts (Q-MoE) module then appends 30 learnable query tokens to the fused sequence and routes through 3 lightweight expert networks via a router that infers the active modality composition, dynamically adjusting expert weights for modality-adaptive aggregation. Training uses a standard symmetric InfoNCE contrastive objective over asset-image-text triplets (589M parameters, built on Uni3D), deliberately avoiding direct regression of MLLM-generated soft labels; the contrastive mechanism with large batch sizes (effective 2,304) naturally distinguishes hard from easy negatives to induce graded relevance behavior. The paper also introduces INGRE, a benchmark spanning 70+ categories across five source datasets, with soft relevance labels generated by an MLLM-driven multi-step adaptive scoring pipeline.

## Results

On the proposed INGRE benchmark, ReMU3D achieves NDCG@5 of 0.94 and NDCG@10 of 0.93, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline OpenShape (0.76/0.76) by 18 and 17 percentage points respectively. It also leads on rank-correlation metrics with Kendall's τ = 0.39 and Spearman's ρ = 0.52. On zero-shot classification, ReMU3D attains 53.2% Top-1 on Objaverse-LVIS, 89.3% on ModelNet40, and 68.1% on ScanObjectNN, matching or exceeding all prior methods while supporting arbitrary modality combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReMU3D decisively outperforms all three INGRE baselines: it surpasses OpenShape by +0.18 NDCG@5 and +0.11 Spearman's ρ, Uni3D by +0.23 NDCG@5 and +0.09 Spearman's ρ, and ULIP by +0.25 NDCG@5 and +0.22 Spearman's ρ, with all baselines plateauing at NDCG@50 = 0.88 while ReMU3D reaches 0.91. On zero-shot classification, ReMU3D beats Uni3D (its backbone) by +6.0 Top-1 on Objaverse-LVIS (53.2 vs 47.2) and +2.5 on ModelNet40 (89.3 vs 86.8), and edges out OpenView (P+I) by +0.5 on Objaverse-LVIS while also covering ScanObjectNN where OpenView is not evaluated. Against OpenShape, ReMU3D gains +14.1 Top-1 on Objaverse-LVIS and +21.9 on ScanObjectNN, confirming that the SCI + Q-MoE reparameterization adds substantial discriminative power beyond the Uni3D pretraining. The modality-agnostic design (reporting best across all input combinations) gives ReMU3D a practical advantage over single-modality baselines like PointCLIPv2, CLIP2Point, and ReCon, which are limited to point-cloud-only input and trail by 10–40 points on Top-1 accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Uni3D (ICLR 2024) as its architectural backbone, extending the line of CLIP-aligned 3D representation learning (PointCLIP → ULIP → OpenShape → Uni3D) by introducing a reparameterization step that decouples intra-class variations from the pretrained CLIP semantic space. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant 'alignment' paradigm—where 3D features are projected into a frozen or jointly-optimized CLIP space for binary category matching—toward a 'refinement' paradigm where image and text tokens act as semantic conditions that modulate point-cloud representations via unified self-attention (SCI) and modality-adaptive expert routing (Q-MoE). The MLLM-driven benchmark construction pipeline is a novel contribution that has no direct precedent in the 3D retrieval literature, drawing instead from information-retrieval practices of graded relevance annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The redefinition of 3D retrieval from binary category matching to continuous intra-class graded relevance addresses a genuine gap between benchmark evaluation and real-world user needs, and the INGRE benchmark with its MLLM-automated annotation pipeline provides a scalable, transferable evaluation standard that the field has lacked. A notable open question is whether the contrastive-only training objective (without explicit graded-relevance supervision) will continue to produce fine-grained rankings as datasets grow more heterogeneous, since the paper's own ablation shows that adding soft-label losses (MSE, KL) actually degrades performance. The reliance on a self-constructed benchmark with only three strong baselines limits the generality of the claimed SOTA, and it remains to be seen whether the INGRE protocol and MLLM annotation quality hold up under independent replication at larger scale.
