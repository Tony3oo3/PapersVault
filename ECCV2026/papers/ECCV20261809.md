## Problem

Cortical surface reconstruction (CSR) is mature for adult T1-weighted cerebral MRI but fails to generalize across contrasts, resolutions, ages, species, and brain structures such as the cerebellum. Classical methods (e.g., FreeSurfer) are slow and population-specific, while learning-based methods cannot generalize beyond their training distributions where annotated data is scarce or unavailable. The paper addresses this by asking whether a training-free, contrast- and resolution-agnostic CSR framework can achieve accurate reconstructions across all these diverse imaging domains.

## Input / Output

XSurfer takes a 3D brain MRI volume and a voxel-wise tissue segmentation (five labels: background, left/right WM, left/right GM) produced by a swappable pretrained 3D U-Net, and outputs a pair of genus-zero triangular surface meshes (white matter and pial) with identical face connectivity and point-wise vertex correspondence. The framework operates on data spanning 0.08–1.0 mm resolution and T1, T2, and diffusion contrasts, and the segmentation module is domain-agnostic so it can be replaced with modality-specific tools (SynthSeg, BOUNTI, GOUHFI) without altering the downstream pipeline.

## Method

XSurfer is a two-stage, training-free pipeline. Stage I (Topology-Preserving Initialization) refines the raw segmentation via a morphology-guided sulcal CSF recovery that expands the WM label outward using simple-point topology constraints to separate fused sulcal banks, then computes genus-zero signed distance fields with fastmarching correction and extracts initial WM and target pial surfaces via Marching Cubes. Stage II (Neural Surface Deformation) models cortical expansion as a diffeomorphic flow governed by an ODE, where a randomly initialized 3D U-Net with temporal-attention architecture predicts multi-scale stationary velocity fields linearly modulated by a time-conditioned attention vector; the network is optimized from scratch per subject (test-time optimization) for ~600 epochs. A composite loss—unidirectional Chamfer attraction toward the target, signed-distance repulsion preventing WM penetration, Laplacian spring smoothness, and cosine-similarity inflation along initial outward normals—jointly guides the deformation while preserving topology and mesh quality. The key insight is that the untrained network's architectural bias toward smooth surfaces provides implicit geometric regularization, and per-subject optimization eliminates any dependency on large annotated datasets.

## Results

On the UKB T1 dataset (50 adult subjects, 1 mm), XSurfer achieves the lowest pial surface error of 0.303 mm ASSD and 0.763 mm HD90, outperforming FreeSurfer (0.379/0.864 mm) by 20.0% and 11.7% respectively. On the dHCP Infant dataset (50 subjects, 0.5 mm T2), XSurfer reaches 0.241 mm ASSD and 0.502 mm HD90, reducing ASSD by 74.1% over the InfantFS pipeline (0.930 mm). Across five additional out-of-distribution datasets (HCP diffusion MRI, dHCP Fetal, in vivo 7T, ex vivo 7T, and marmoset cerebellum at 0.08 mm), XSurfer consistently achieves the best ASSD at every pipeline stage, with errors as low as 0.091 mm on the marmoset cerebellum.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XSurfer outperforms all five baselines on both quantitative datasets. Against the classical FreeSurfer pipeline (run on a synthesized pseudo-T1 to avoid circularity), XSurfer reduces ASSD by 20% (0.303 vs 0.379 mm) and HD90 by 12% (0.763 vs 0.864 mm) on UKB T1. The domain-randomization method recon-all-clinical [15] is beaten by 39% in ASSD (0.303 vs 0.498 mm), and the supervised implicit method DeepCSR [7] by 42% (0.303 vs 0.519 mm). On dHCP Infant, XSurfer's advantage is most dramatic: it reduces ASSD by 74% over InfantFS (0.241 vs 0.930 mm) and by 79% over CortexODE in zero-shot TTO mode (0.241 vs 1.132 mm). The P3D-Fitting baseline [36], which uses the same loss function but replaces the untrained network with direct SGD on vertex coordinates, lags XSurfer by 54% in ASSD on UKB (0.667 vs 0.303 mm), confirming that the untrained network's implicit smoothness prior contributes meaningful regularization beyond the loss design alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

XSurfer carves new ground by repurposing the untrained-network paradigm (previously applied to inverse imaging problems like MRI reconstruction) for cortical surface deformation, replacing the need for large annotated surface datasets that constrain both classical pipelines (FreeSurfer, CRUISE) and learning-based methods (CortexODE, CoTAN, DeepCSR). Conceptually, it inherits FreeSurfer's geometric loss design (attraction, repulsion, spring, inflation) but instantiates it through a diffeomorphic neural ODE driven by a randomly initialized 3D U-Net, and adds a topology-preserving simple-point segmentation refinement step to address partial-volume effects in sulci. The method thus sits at the intersection of classical surface evolution, neural ODE deformation, and test-time optimization, without directly building on any single prior CSR architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for domain-specific training data, XSurfer enables a single pipeline to handle CSR across fetal, infant, adult, ex vivo, and non-human primate brains at resolutions from 0.08 to 1.0 mm in 15–18 minutes, a practical speedup over FreeSurfer's hours-per-subject runtime. A key open question is how much accuracy is lost by relying solely on segmentation labels rather than raw MRI intensities, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation. The finding that pre-training on 100 subjects does not improve over random initialization (and may slightly hurt) raises broader questions about when population-level priors are useful versus when subject-specific optimization from scratch is preferable. Future work could explore intensity-guided refinement and extension to longitudinal or multi-modal fusion settings.
