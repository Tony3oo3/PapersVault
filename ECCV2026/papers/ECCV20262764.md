## Problem

Long-range radar-camera 3D object detection faces a fundamental tension: BEV-based fusion captures scene-level context but incurs rapidly growing computational cost and loses fine-grained object detail at extended ranges, while query-based fusion is efficient but lacks scene-level context. Temporal fusion compounds this problem, as distant objects produce only a few radar returns and image pixels (requiring multi-frame scene-level accumulation) while high-speed motion causes large inter-frame displacements (requiring per-object motion modeling). No existing method jointly addresses both the spatial and temporal duality without resorting to dense BEV construction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 4 surround-view camera images (256×864 or 640×960 resolution) and 4D radar point clouds accumulated over 10 sweeps from 6 radar sensors, where each radar point carries spatial coordinates, radar cross-section, and radial velocity. The output is a set of 3D bounding boxes with class, 3D position, dimensions, orientation, and planar velocity, evaluated over a 150 m detection range. Internally, the method operates on a sparse representation where only object-occupied BEV cells are processed, avoiding the full dense grid that scales quadratically with range.

## Method

Horizon3D introduces a hybrid representation combining Gaussian primitives (object-level) with sparse BEV features (scene-level) through three modules. KGGI estimates object keypoints from radar (top-K scoring sparse BEV cells) and camera (2D objectness + depth heads) to initialize a compact set of Gaussian primitives, avoiding the large primitive counts needed by prior Gaussian-based methods. OCSF iteratively refines these Gaussians via deformable cross-attention over both modalities, then splats them onto the BEV plane to produce a sparse object-centric BEV representation that is fused with sparse radar BEV features through a gating network and submanifold sparse convolutions. DPTF performs temporal fusion along two complementary paths: a BEV path that accumulates foreground features over 8 past frames with velocity-guided warping, and a Gaussian path that propagates FPS-sampled history Gaussians to the current frame with per-Gaussian velocity compensation, enabling both scene-level multi-frame accumulation and per-object motion modeling at highway speeds.

## Results

On the TruckScenes validation set with a VoVNet-99 backbone, Horizon3D achieves 38.4 NDS and 24.1 mAP, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art radar-camera method SpaRC by +3.0 NDS and +1.6 mAP. On the test split, Horizon3D reaches 41.8 NDS and 27.7 mAP, surpassing SpaRC by +4.4 NDS and +0.5 mAP while using lower-resolution image inputs (640×960 vs. 928×1952). With a ResNet-50 backbone on the validation set, Horizon3D (37.4 NDS, 23.6 mAP) also exceeds the LiDAR-based CenterPoint-V baseline by +2.1 NDS and +1.0 mAP. Inference speed is 8.5 FPS with 3.24 GB memory, competitive with query-based methods and substantially faster than dense BEV-based approaches (4.2 FPS for BEVFusion, 2.9 FPS for CRT-Fusion).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Horizon3D outperforms all nine baselines across both validation and test splits. Against the closest radar-camera competitor SpaRC [32], Horizon3D gains +3.0 NDS / +1.6 mAP on val (V2-99) and +4.4 NDS / +0.5 mAP on test, while using lower-resolution images. Among BEV-based radar-camera methods, Horizon3D surpasses BEVFusion [25] by +7.0 NDS / +5.4 mAP and CRT-Fusion [16] by +8.6 NDS / +6.7 mAP on the R50 validation split. The query-based RCTrans [19] trails by a wide margin (−14.5 NDS, −11.0 mAP on val R50), confirming the cost of lacking scene-level context. Notably, Horizon3D also exceeds the LiDAR-only CenterPoint-V [37] by +2.1 NDS / +1.0 mAP on val and +0.8 NDS / +1.0 mAP on test, demonstrating that the hybrid Gaussian–sparse-BEV representation can match or surpass LiDAR-based detection using only radar and camera. Camera-only baselines (Far3D [14], PETR [23]) and radar-only (RadarGNN [9]) are substantially outperformed, as expected given the multi-modal advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

Horizon3D carves out a middle ground between two established paradigms in radar-camera 3D detection: dense BEV fusion (BEVFusion, CRT-Fusion, RCBEVDet), which captures scene context but scales poorly with range, and sparse query-based fusion (RCTrans, SpaRC, RaCFormer), which is range-efficient but lacks scene-level context. The key conceptual departure is borrowing Gaussian primitives from the 3D occupancy prediction literature (GaussianFormer, GaussianFormer-2, RaGS) and repurposing them as object-centric feature carriers in a detection pipeline, rather than as scene occupancy estimators. The dual-path temporal design also synthesizes ideas from BEV memory accumulation (CRT-Fusion) and query propagation (RCTrans) into a unified velocity-compensated framework, while the sparse radar backbone (VoxelNeXt) and deformable cross-attention encoder follow standard practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practically critical gap: reliable 3D perception beyond 100 m at highway speeds, where LiDAR cost and weather fragility make radar-camera fusion the most deployable option. The hybrid Gaussian-sparse-BEV representation offers a concrete architectural recipe for balancing object-level detail against scene-level context without the quadratic cost growth of dense BEV grids, a tradeoff that will matter as detection ranges extend further. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (TruckScenes), reliance on 4D radar (not yet widely deployed), and the added complexity of three interacting modules that may be difficult to ablate or adapt to other sensor suites. Open questions include whether the Gaussian primitive count and keypoint-guided initialization generalize to denser urban scenes or to settings without reliable velocity cues.
