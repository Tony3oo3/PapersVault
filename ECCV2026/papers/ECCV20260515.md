## Problem

Existing object hallucination (OH) detection methods for LVLMs either rely on auxiliary models (adding inference latency) or focus on visual grounding signals, largely overlooking intrinsic language-side truthfulness cues. The paper identifies a concrete gap: real and hallucinated object tokens are clearly separable in hidden representations (verified via LDA), yet this separability is substantially attenuated in the output probability distributions produced by the LM head, meaning the model cannot self-evaluate its own factual correctness without intervention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260515/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a fixed text prompt (e.g., 'Describe the given image in detail') as input to a standard LVLM, which autoregressively generates a caption. For each object-token position in the generated sequence, the model outputs a scalar truthfulness score computed as the log-probability assigned to a designated rarely-used special token (e.g., <unk> or ) minus a predefined constant (c_ref = −28.0). This score is thresholded at a fixed value μ to yield a binary hallucination/truth decision per token, with no auxiliary model or additional forward pass required at inference.

## Method

TruthLens repurposes a low-probability special token as a truthfulness probe at the LM head, motivated by the finding that the discriminative direction between real and hallucinated hidden states is only weakly aligned with the LM head projection space (projection ratio ≈ 0.015–0.024). The truthfulness score S(o) = log π_θ(z_c | X, I, y_<o>) − c_ref is trained via an MSE objective that drives scores toward 1 for real objects and 0 for hallucinated ones, with a KL divergence constraint to a frozen reference model preserving original generation capability and a class-level loss re-weighting strategy to handle the inherent imbalance between real and hallucinated tokens. Training uses LoRA (rank 8) with 8 stochastically sampled captions per image from a small annotated set (4,000 MSCOCO images for OH; 6,500 CLEVR samples for OAH), and the approach generalizes to substantially larger label spaces (e.g., Object365 with 365 categories) without additional training.

## Results

On MS-COCO (80 categories), TruthLens achieves the best AUROC across all five evaluated LVLMs, reaching 93.04 on LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B and 91.46 on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, outperforming the strongest baseline (GLSIM) by 16.10% and 17.41% in AUROC respectively. On the more challenging Object365 benchmark (365 categories), TruthLens maintains its lead with 82.14 AUROC on LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B, surpassing the best baseline (SVAR) by 9.55% in AUROC. For object-attribute hallucination detection on SpatialMQA, TruthLens achieves 76.06 AUROC on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, a 11.22% absolute gain over the previous best (Contextual Lens at 64.84). Fine-tuning preserves general capability: recall on MS-COCO changes by less than 0.5% and 13 general benchmarks show negligible or slightly positive shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TruthLens outperforms all six baselines (NLL, Entropy, Internal Confidence, SVAR, Contextual Lens, GLSIM) on every model and both primary benchmarks (MS-COCO and Object365). The largest margins are against GLSIM on MS-COCO: +17.41% AUROC on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (91.46 vs. 74.05) and +16.10% on LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B (93.04 vs. 76.94). On Object365, the closest competitor is SVAR on LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B (72.59 AUROC), which TruthLens surpasses by 9.55 points; on Qwen2.5-VL-7B the gap over SVAR widens to 6.43 points (71.54 vs. 65.11). Logit-based baselines (NLL, Entropy) consistently underperform by 25–30 AUROC points, confirming that raw token probabilities carry far less discriminative signal than the learned truthfulness score. On the OAH benchmarks (CLEVR, SpatialMQA), TruthLens also leads all baselines, with the most notable gain on SpatialMQA/Qwen2.5-VL-7B where it reaches 76.06 AUROC versus 64.84 for Contextual Lens (+11.22). Notably, TruthLens is trained only on MS-COCO (4,000 images) yet generalizes to Object365's 365-class space without additional training, whereas baselines show larger relative drops on the harder benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

TruthLens extends the LASER framework [49], which quantifies a complete solution's reasoning reward via the log-probability of a designated special token, down to the individual token level for hallucination detection. It departs from the dominant line of OH detection methods (NLL, attention-based SVAR, hidden-state-based GLSIM, Contextual Lens) that analyze visual grounding or intermediate representations, instead targeting the LM head's output distribution directly. The key conceptual departure is the empirical diagnosis of a representation-projection mismatch: hidden states clearly separate real from hallucinated tokens, yet the vocabulary projection systematically attenuates this signal, motivating a targeted fine-tuning of the LM head rather than an auxiliary detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-inference-overhead property (no auxiliary model, no extra forward pass) makes TruthLens practically attractive for deployment in latency-sensitive settings where prior methods like GAIVE or HaLEM add significant cost. The generalization from 80-class MS-COCO training to 365-class Object365 evaluation without additional fine-tuning suggests the learned signal captures intrinsic truthfulness rather than dataset-specific statistics. Open questions include whether the mechanism extends to non-object hallucinations (factual, relational), whether it can be made training-free, and how it interacts with generation-time correction strategies beyond the preliminary detection-to-mitigation pipeline demonstrated here.
