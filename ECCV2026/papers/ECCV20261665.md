## Problem

LVLMs fail at trivially simple counting tasks (e.g., fewer than ten black dots), yet prior analyses of this failure remain at the behavioral or probing level without isolating the underlying circuit-level mechanisms. The paper asks whether counting reflects a foundational visual-reasoning primitive whose internal circuitry can be identified, understood, and selectively enhanced to improve broader visual reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261665/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes simple synthetic RGB images (black dots or colored polygons on a white canvas, object counts 1–10) paired with a short text prompt, and produces a single counting number as the primary output. For interpretability, it additionally yields per-head semantic token decodings via HeadLens, layer-wise overwrite rates from Visual Activation Patching, and head importance scores. The intervention is trained on only 8,000 such synthetic images (generated in minutes with PIL), yet is evaluated on photorealistic OOD counting (SynReal, PixMo-Count) and general reasoning benchmarks (MMMU, RealWorldQA, MathVista).

## Method

The paper introduces two interpretability tools: Visual Activation Patching (VAP), which extends activation patching to the visual modality by constructing controlled image pairs that alter only object count while preserving spatial layout, and HeadLens, which decodes individual attention-head outputs into semantic tokens by zero-padding each head's projection, applying the output projector, and mapping through a learned affine translator (Tuned Lens) into vocabulary space. These tools reveal a four-stage counting circuit: early-layer Visual Grounding Heads extract low-level features, middle-layer Cross-Modal Routing Heads (layers 18–24) translate visual information into numerical concepts, late-layer Counting Aggregation Heads consolidate the answer in the residual stream, and Awareness Heads encode object existence and task difficulty. Guided by this circuit, the intervention combines LoRA-based SFT on 8K synthetic images with an Object-Focused Attention Regularizer (cross-entropy between each head's attention distribution and a Gaussian object-center prior, applied to grounding and routing layers) and a training-free Adaptive Head Temperature Tuning that multiplies pre-softmax logits of identified critical heads by an inverse-temperature factor proportional to their circuit importance. The key empirical finding is that this narrow counting-only fine-tuning yields +8.36% average OOD counting accuracy and +1.54% average gain on general visual reasoning, with Jaccard similarity analysis confirming >33% overlap in critical heads between counting and reasoning-heavy tasks.

## Results

On out-of-distribution counting, the proposed intervention (SFT + attention regularizer + head temperature tuning) improves Qwen2.5-VL-7B from 73.48% to 84.83% accuracy on SynReal (+11.35) and from 58.79% to 64.15% on PixMo-Count (+5.36). Qwen3-VL-8B gains +2.42 on SynReal (88.79→91.21) and +8.23 on PixMo-Count (58.75→66.98), while LLaVA-1.5-7B improves from 54.27% to 60.11% on SynReal. On general visual reasoning benchmarks (MMMU, RealWorldQA, MathVista), the same counting-only fine-tuning yields average gains of +1.54% (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), +1.93% (Qwen3-VL-8B), and +0.93% (LLaVA-1.5-7B), demonstrating transfer beyond the narrow counting task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its interpretability-inspired intervention (LoRA SFT on 8K synthetic images + object-focused attention regularizer + adaptive head temperature tuning) against the unmodified baselines of three LVLMs: Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B, and LLaVA-1.5-7B. The method wins on all counting metrics across all three backbones: Qwen2.5-VL-7B gains the largest counting improvement (+11.35 Acc on SynReal, +5.36 on PixMo-Count), Qwen3-VL-8B shows +2.42 and +8.23 respectively, and LLaVA-1.5-7B improves by +5.84 and +0.76. On general visual reasoning (MMMU, RealWorldQA, MathVista), the intervention also outperforms each baseline's unmodified version, with Qwen3-VL-8B showing the largest average gain (+1.93%) and LLaVA-1.5-7B the smallest (+0.93%). Notably, Qwen3-VL-8B already had stronger counting pretraining than Qwen2.5-VL-7B, yet still benefits, suggesting the gains come from circuit-level sharpening rather than compensating for missing pretraining data. The ablation (Table 4) confirms each component contributes: SFT alone gives +6.30 on SynReal, adding the attention regularizer adds +2.56, and head temperature tuning adds a further +2.49.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends text-only mechanistic interpretability tools—activation patching (Heimersheim & Nanda, Zhang & Nanda) and the logit/tuned lens (nostalgebraist, Belrose et al.)—to the visual modality of LVLMs, introducing Visual Activation Patching and HeadLens as new head-level decoding techniques. It departs from prior counting analyses (CountScope, [De|Re]) that remain at the behavioral or probing level by isolating circuit-level attention head functions (visual grounding, cross-modal routing, counting aggregation, awareness heads). Conceptually, it reframes counting from an isolated task to a 'minimalist probe' for general visual reasoning, building on cognitive science literature on subitizing and resource-rational trade-offs to ground model behavior in human numerosity perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that counting circuits share >33% of critical heads with reasoning-heavy tasks (VQA, math) provides mechanistic evidence that counting is a foundational primitive of visual reasoning rather than a narrow skill, suggesting targeted enhancement of this primitive can transfer broadly. The practical intervention—8,000 trivially generated synthetic images, single-GPU LoRA fine-tuning with attention regularization—offers a lightweight pathway to improve LVLM reasoning without expensive data or compute. Open questions include whether these circuit-level insights and the transferability effect scale to larger models (30B+), whether other primitive skills (spatial reasoning, attribute comparison) exhibit similar shared-circuit structure, and whether the modest +1.54% general-reasoning gain holds under more rigorous multi-seed, multi-architecture evaluation.
