## Problem

Classical pixel-wise spectral unmixing methods (least-squares fitting) degrade substantially when fluorophore emission spectra overlap, noise is high, or the number of spectral bands is reduced below the number of fluorophores. Existing learning-based approaches for spectral imaging are either designed for remote sensing or tailored to narrow acquisition settings, leaving a gap for a general, self-supervised method that exploits spatial structure in fluorescence microscopy data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261698/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-band spectral image S ∈ R^{L×Z×Y×X} (L ranging from 3 to 32 bands, 2D or 3D spatial dimensions) and a known mixing matrix M ∈ R^{L×F} built from l1-normalized fluorophore emission spectra (from FPBase or single-fluorophore controls). It outputs F fluorophore concentration maps U ∈ R^{F×Z×Y×X} as an approximate MMSE estimate obtained by averaging 50 posterior samples. Training uses random patches (64×64 for 2D, 8×64×64 for 3D); inference employs inner tiling with one-quarter overlap to handle full images.

## Method

λ Split is a physics-informed hierarchical Variational Autoencoder (LVAE backbone) with 4 stochastic levels at progressively halved spatial resolution (128 channels each), which learns a conditional distribution p_θ(U|S) over fluorophore concentration maps. A fully differentiable Spectral Mixer multiplies the decoder's predicted concentration maps by the transposed mixing matrix M^T to reconstruct the input spectral image, making the entire pipeline self-supervised: the reconstruction loss is spectral MSE between observed and reconstructed bands, regularized by a hierarchical KL divergence. The key insight is that embedding the linear image-formation model directly into the generative process allows the network to learn content-aware structural priors from mixed spectral observations alone, without any ground-truth concentration maps. At inference, 50 posterior samples are averaged to yield a denoised, structurally coherent unmixing that implicitly removes noise and resolves ambiguities in underdetermined or highly overlapping spectral regimes.

## Results

On the CellAtlas dataset with 32 spectral bands, λSplit achieves PSNR 27.14 and MS-SSIM 0.904 at the highest noise level (2 ms exposure, SNR_sp = 5.33), outperforming the best baseline (TAEU, PSNR 24.52, MS-SSIM 0.761) by 2.6 dB and 0.14 in structural similarity. At moderate noise (10 ms, SNR_sp = 22.57), λSplit reaches PSNR 36.66 and MS-SSIM 0.989, surpassing the next-best method (RLU, PSNR 33.14, MS-SSIM 0.971). In the underdetermined 3-band regime (L < F), λSplit maintains PSNR 27.25 and MS-SSIM 0.878 while classical least-squares methods (LU, HyU) degrade to PSNR ≈ 23.7, demonstrating the value of the learned structural prior when spectral information is insufficient.

## Baselines & Comparisons

λSplit outperforms all classical pixel-wise baselines (LU, NNLU, HyU, RLU, NMF-RI) across every noise level, with the largest margins at high noise: at 2 ms exposure, λSplit gains +2.6 dB PSNR and +0.10 MS-SSIM over the best classical method (NNLU). FCLU and TAEU, both designed for remote sensing hyperspectral data, perform substantially worse than microscopy-specific methods and are excluded from subsequent experiments. In the underdetermined 3-band regime (L < F), λSplit (PSNR 27.25, MS-SSIM 0.878) maintains strong performance while LU and HyU drop to PSNR ≈ 23.7, confirming that the learned structural prior resolves ambiguities that least-squares cannot. LUMoS, a clustering-based method for low-band settings, is consistently outperformed by λSplit in both PSNR and MS-SSIM. Among supervised baselines, AutoUnmix is competitive at high spectral dimensionality but impractical (450 M parameters for 5 bands, 10 B for 32 bands, versus λSplit's 3 M), and a standard supervised UNet performs substantially worse than both λSplit and AutoUnmix in all tested conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

λ Split builds on the Ladder Variational Autoencoder (LVAE) backbone [39] and the broader line of content-aware generative models for microscopy (e.g., MicroSplit [1], Noise2Void [17]), repurposing hierarchical variational inference for the spectral unmixing problem. It departs from classical pixel-wise least-squares unmixing (LU, NNLU, FCLU, RLU, HyU, NMF-RI) by replacing per-pixel estimation with a spatially-aware generative model that learns multi-scale structural priors. The key architectural departure is the insertion of a fixed, fully differentiable Spectral Mixer that enforces the linear image-formation model, enabling self-supervised training without paired ground truth — a design not present in prior learning-based unmixing methods, which are either supervised or tailored to remote sensing rather than microscopy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being fully self-supervised and compatible with standard confocal microscopes, λ Split removes the two main barriers to adopting data-driven unmixing in practice: the need for labeled training data and specialized hardware. The 58 controlled benchmarks systematically isolating noise, spectral overlap, and spectral dimensionality (including underdetermined L < F regimes) provide a useful evaluation framework for the community. Open questions include whether the linear-mixing-with-known-spectra assumption can be relaxed to handle unknown or drifting emission profiles, and whether the learned structural prior generalizes across tissue types and labeling schemes without retraining.
