## Problem

Conventional phase-shifting interferometry (PSI) recovers optical phase but requires a stable external reference beam, making it sensitive to vibration and environmental perturbations. Self-reference and common-path interferometric approaches mitigate this but lack a principled, provably robust analytical solution for phase recovery from shifted self-interference measurements. This paper fills that gap by providing a non-iterative phase retrieval algorithm with formal guarantees on graph connectivity and worst-case error accumulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262388/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes quadrature phase-shifted intensity images (4 measurements at 0, π/2, π, 3π/2) captured at K spatial shifts in horizontal and vertical directions, yielding 16 or 32 total intensity frames per scene. From these, it estimates pairwise phase-difference phasors between pixels separated by each shift vector. The output is a complete 2D phase map (up to a global offset) and, combined with the measured amplitude, the full complex wavefront; a Fourier-domain least-squares refinement can optionally polish the phase estimate. The entire recovery is a single BFS pass over the pixel graph, requiring no iterative optimization.

## Method

The key insight is that interference between a wave and its spatially shifted copy encodes pairwise phase differences, which can be integrated along a connected graph to recover the global phase. The algorithm (Algorithm 1) performs a breadth-first propagation of unit phasors from a reference pixel, averaging equal-hop paths to reduce variance while discarding longer paths to limit error accumulation. The paper proves that co-prime shift pairs (gcd(s,t)=1) guarantee full graph connectivity and that the optimal consecutive pair s=⌊√(N/2)⌋, t=s+1 minimizes the maximum hop distance, thereby bounding worst-case per-pixel phase error. An optional global least-squares refinement in the Fourier domain jointly fits the propagated phase to all measured differences, yielding further noise reduction. The approach is entirely analytical and non-iterative, completing in ~1.2 s on a single GPU versus 3.6–10.7 s for iterative baselines.

## Results

On DIV2K-derived synthetic wavefronts at 22 dB SNR, the proposed method achieves mean absolute phase errors of 0.158±0.104 (quadratic), 0.156±0.099 (random), and 0.155±0.096 (smooth) with only 16 measurements, and further reduces to 0.099±0.083, 0.094±0.075, and 0.097±0.071 with 32 measurements. The method is the fastest at 1.2 s per reconstruction, roughly 8× faster than the slowest baseline (PnP-FISTA at 10.7 s). Hardware experiments on a biconvex lens (f=75 mm) confirm that optimal co-prime shifts (24,25) closely match the analytical thin-lens phase, while non-optimal shifts introduce visible discontinuities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all four baselines on quadratic and smooth phase profiles: at 16 measurements it achieves 0.158 error vs. 0.669–0.852 for GD-Rand, GD-Spec, WISH, and PnP-FISTA on quadratic phase, and 0.155 vs. 0.663–0.898 on smooth phase. DIP performs worst across all settings (≈1.45–1.50 error), indicating that untrained convolutional priors are poorly suited to this task. On random phase profiles, GD-Rand with random initialization narrowly edges out the proposed method (0.138 vs. 0.156 at 16 measurements), but the gap is small and vanishes at higher SNR; with 32 measurements the proposed method recovers the lead (0.094 vs. 0.079 for GD-Rand, though GD-Rand's std is larger). The least-squares refinement variant (Ours+LS) trades a small increase in mean error for substantially lower variance (e.g., 0.082 vs. 0.104 std on quadratic at 16 meas), making it preferable when a tighter error bound is needed. In terms of speed, the proposed method runs in 1.2 s per reconstruction, compared to 1.5 s (GD), 3.6 s (WISH), 8.7 s (DIP), and 10.7 s (PnP-FISTA), an approximately 8× speedup over the slowest baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of phase-shifting interferometry (PSI), common-path/lateral shearing interferometry (LSI), and computational phase retrieval. It departs from iterative phase retrieval methods (Gerchberg-Saxton, Wirtinger Flow, PnP-FISTA, DIP) by replacing nonlinear optimization with a single-pass graph propagation of pairwise phase differences, and from standard LSI by co-designing the measurement pattern (co-prime shift pairs) with the recovery algorithm to guarantee connectivity and bound error accumulation. The self-reference measurement model follows the common-path interferometry lineage (point diffraction interferometry, ReWave), but the analytical graph-propagation solution with provable co-prime-shift optimality is the paper's core departure from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provable guarantees (co-prime shifts ensure full graph connectivity and minimize worst-case hop distance) give this method a theoretical robustness that iterative phase retrieval lacks, and the 8× speedup over the slowest baseline makes it attractive for real-time adaptive optics. However, the method currently requires sequential SLM-based capture (assumes a nearly static field), averages equal-hop paths uniformly without reliability weighting, and shows suboptimal performance on random phase profiles. Extending to dynamic wavefronts and incorporating quality-guided path weighting are natural next steps that the authors identify as open.
