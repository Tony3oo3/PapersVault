## Problem

Existing 3D dense captioning methods are constrained to narrow object categories and produce short, coarse descriptions, while recent structured description generators (e.g., SceneScript, SpatialLM) focus only on architectural layouts and salient objects, overlooking fine-grained entity attributes and inter-entity relations. This paper introduces holo-captioning, a novel task that seeks the text equivalent of a 3D scene by requiring a single model to predict all scene elements—semantic tags, spatial locations, attributes, and relations—purely in text form without any auxiliary detectors or segmenters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260309/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud scene as input and produces a single structured holo-caption in pure text. Spatial locations are encoded as 9-DoF oriented bounding box vectors (center, size, Euler angles) written directly in text, eliminating the need for separate 3D detectors. Attributes and inter-entity relations are described in free-form text, and the overall output is a unified structured description covering all entity instances in the scene rather than per-object captions or a scene graph.

## Method

HoloScribe follows an instance-aware decoupled pipeline that decomposes holo-caption generation into three sequential phases to handle the long, information-dense output: (1) Grounded Instance Discovery localizes all entities and assigns semantic tags using 7-DoF bounding boxes, processing architectural elements, common objects, and other objects independently; (2) Anchor-aware Instance Linking identifies relational pairs by sampling subgraphs where one anchor instance is paired with n_g−1 others, and the model predicts which pairs exhibit meaningful relations, avoiding exhaustive pairwise prediction; (3) Grounded Description Generation produces attribute and relation text conditioned on the identified instances and pairs. The architecture is a LLaVA-style 3D LLM with a Sonata point cloud encoder, MLP connector, and Qwen2.5-0.5B decoder-only LLM, initialized from SpatialLM and fine-tuned with LoRA on the HoloScan benchmark (15K+ scenes). The key design insight is that grouping scene elements by instance and generating descriptions element-by-element overcomes the difficulty of perceiving all scene content in a single forward pass.

## Results

On the HoloScan test set, HoloScribe achieves an overall HoloScore of 117.04, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline SpatialLM-Tuned (69.63) and LL3DA-Tuned (61.67). HoloScribe leads on every dimension: tagging (61.83 vs. 49.82 for SpatialLM-Tuned), spatial localization (22.75 vs. 9.23), attribute description (23.72 vs. 7.01), and inter-entity relations (8.74 vs. 3.57). In cross-dataset 3D detection on MultiScan (unseen during training), HoloScribe achieves a tagging F-score of 53.5 and localization F-score of 20.2, more than doubling the performance of LL3DA (24.9/17.3) and SpatialLM (20.2/9.6) in a zero-shot setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HoloScribe decisively outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated dimension on the HoloScan test set. Against the closest competitor SpatialLM-Tuned (also using Qwen2.5-0.5B), HoloScribe gains +12.0 on tagging, +13.5 on localization, +16.7 on attributes, and +5.2 on relations, yielding a 68% higher overall HoloScore (117.04 vs. 69.63). The 3D dense captioners (Vote2Cap-DETR, Vote2Cap-DETR++) and 3D LLM generalists (LEO, LL3DA) perform poorly on the attribute dimension (≤3.27) because they were trained on short-caption benchmarks like ScanRefer and cannot produce fine-grained descriptions; they also lack relation prediction entirely. On the unseen MultiScan dataset, HoloScribe's zero-shot detection (tag F=53.5, loc F=20.2) more than doubles LL3DA (24.9/17.3) and SpatialLM (20.2/9.6), confirming that the instance-aware decoupled pipeline generalizes beyond the training distribution. The ablation further shows that the anchor-aware instance linking mechanism contributes an additional +1.88 on relations and +8.09 overall over the anchor-free variant, validating its role in filtering irrelevant instance pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

HoloScribe builds directly on SpatialLM's LLaVA-style 3D LLM architecture (point cloud encoder + MLP connector + decoder-only LLM) and is initialized from its checkpoint, but departs fundamentally in task formulation: rather than predicting only entity tags and locations as SpatialLM or SceneScript do, holo-captioning demands all four dimensions—tags, 3D oriented bounding boxes, free-form attributes, and inter-entity relations—in a single pure-text output without any auxiliary detector or segmenter. The instance-aware decoupled pipeline (discovery → linking → description) is a novel architectural response to the information density of holo-captions, and the anchor-aware subgraph sampling for relation prediction has no direct precedent in prior 3D captioning or scene graph work. The task also extends the authors' own 2D panoptic captioning [42] into metric 3D space, where spatial reasoning shifts from 2D image-plane coordinates to 9-DoF oriented boxes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a concrete, evaluable stepping stone toward the ambitious goal of full 3D-to-text equivalence, and the HoloScan benchmark (15K+ scenes across five sources) plus HoloScore metric provide the community with infrastructure that did not previously exist for this level of descriptive granularity. However, the spatial localization F-score remains low (~22), revealing that predicting precise 3D coordinates in pure text is still far from solved, and the authors explicitly frame this work as only the 'initial step.' Open questions include whether scaling model size will close the localization gap, whether the decoupled pipeline generalizes to dynamic or outdoor scenes, and whether the HoloScore's LLM-based evaluation introduces its own biases. The 75% robotic navigation success rate (vs. 25% for SpatialLM) hints at practical value, but the single-task evaluation limits confidence in broader downstream utility.
