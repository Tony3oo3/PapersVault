## Problem

Large Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) like FLUX.dev (12B parameters) are difficult to deploy due to memory and compute constraints, and existing compression methods (pruning, block removal, MoE conversion) alter the architecture and can cause abrupt quality collapse under aggressive budgets. It was unclear whether the catastrophic sensitivity to naive low-rank approximation observed in LLMs also applies to DiTs, or whether their iterative denoising dynamics provide inherent robustness to structured rank reduction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261422/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The compressed model takes a text prompt and generates a 1024×1024 RGB image using the same inference protocol as the original FLUX.dev (50 denoising steps, classifier-free guidance scale 3.5). The VAE and text encoder remain frozen and are excluded from the reported parameter budget; only the DiT backbone is compressed. Compression is applied at the projection-matrix level within single- and double-stream transformer blocks, preserving the original block structure and non-linearities rather than removing or substituting entire blocks.

## Method

SVDtrunc is a two-step block-level compression scheme. First, a block-importance probing protocol compresses each block individually via truncated SVD (fixed ratio c_m=0.6), generates paired images, and ranks blocks by CMMD degradation between probed and original image sets. Second, a linearly decreasing compression schedule assigns higher compression ratios to less important blocks, with the slope chosen to satisfy a global parameter budget; only a subset of blocks (|S|=30) is compressed while critical blocks remain untouched. The compressed student is then fine-tuned with a combined objective: a rectified-flow velocity prediction loss to preserve generative performance, plus modular knowledge distillation that aligns both the final velocity prediction and intermediate per-block feature maps with the uncompressed teacher. The key empirical finding is that DiTs exhibit graceful, gradual degradation under truncated SVD even without fine-tuning, in stark contrast to the catastrophic failure seen in LLMs, indicating that redundancy is distributed across projection matrices throughout the network.

## Results

On FLUX.dev compression, SVDtrunc-m at 68% of original parameters achieves GenEval 0.645, HPSv2 31.27, and DPG 83.4, with only 0.75% average quality reduction (R) relative to the uncompressed model (GenEval 0.647, HPSv2 31.70, DPG 83.9). At a more aggressive 57% budget, SVDtrunc-s retains GenEval 0.616, HPSv2 31.22, and DPG 82.6 (R=2.60%), outperforming all competing methods operating at 68% or higher parameter budgets on GenEval. In the training-free setting (no fine-tuning), SVDtrunc degrades only 9.86% at 70% parameters, while EcoDiff collapses to 85.50% and block pruning to 94.52%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVDtrunc-m at 68% parameters dominates all eight baselines on every reported metric: it achieves GenEval 0.645 vs. 0.605 (PPCL), 0.523 (FluxLite), 0.511 (TinyFusion), and 0.503 (HierarchicalPrune) at the same 68% budget, and also surpasses Chroma-HD (0.593) and Dense2MoE (0.403) which operate at a larger 75% budget. On HPSv2, SVDtrunc-m scores 31.27, exceeding FluxLite (31.31 is the only competitor reporting this metric at 68%, and it is essentially tied) while far outperforming FastFlux (27.26) and EcoDiff (25.99) at 80%. On DPG, SVDtrunc-m reaches 83.4, beating PPCL (80.0), FluxLite (79.3), TinyFusion (77.2), and HierarchicalPrune (75.7) at 68%, and even Chroma-HD (84.0) at 75% is within 0.6 points. The smaller SVDtrunc-s model at 57% parameters—substantially fewer than any competitor—still outperforms all 68%-budget methods on GenEval (0.616 vs. best 0.605) and remains competitive on HPSv2 (31.22) and DPG (82.6). In the training-free ablation (Table 1), SVDtrunc degrades only 5.24% at 90% and 9.86% at 70% parameters, whereas EcoDiff drops 14.52% and 85.50% respectively, and block pruning collapses to 94.52% at 70%, confirming that the advantage is not solely due to fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant structural-compression paradigm for DiTs (block pruning, branch replacement, MoE conversion) by applying truncated SVD to projection matrices while preserving the full architectural skeleton. It builds directly on modular knowledge distillation (Huang et al., NeurIPS 2023) for module-wise feature alignment, but the key conceptual departure is from the LLM compression literature, where naive SVD is known to cause catastrophic failure; the authors show that DiTs exhibit fundamentally different sensitivity, tolerating aggressive rank reduction with only gradual quality loss. The importance-aware rank allocation extends prior block-sensitivity analyses (e.g., HierarchicalPrune, FastFLUX) from binary keep/remove decisions to continuous, budget-constrained compression ratios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that DiTs possess distributed, projection-level redundancy exploitable via low-rank factorization opens a compression axis orthogonal to step distillation, enabling further parameter and VRAM reduction after temporal distillation has already been applied. At 68% parameters the model retains near-full benchmark performance (R=0.75%), and the approach generalizes across model scales (FLUX.dev 12B, PixArt-Σ 0.6B) and to temporally-distilled variants (FLUX.schnell). Open questions include the observed domain drift and memorization loss at aggressive compression (below ~50%), whether the tolerance is an artifact of overparameterization, and how the method interacts with quantization or other post-training compression techniques.
