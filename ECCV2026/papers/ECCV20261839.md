## Problem

Vector-Quantized (VQ) attention reduces attention complexity from O(N²) to O(MN) by clustering keys into M codewords, but applies uniform codebook capacity across all regions of key space. High-attention regions may be coarsely approximated while low-attention regions waste representational precision, creating an accuracy-efficiency gap that fixed codebooks cannot close. The paper asks whether codebook capacity can be adaptively allocated to where attention mass actually concentrates during inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261839/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard Q, K, V tensors (B×H×N×D) together with a pre-trained hierarchical codebook of M₀ parent codewords each with C children (M₀(1+C) total). Before attention, keys are quantized and values are scattered into per-codeword aggregates (V̄_a, n_a) in a single fused kernel pass; tokens may be reordered along a Gilbert space-filling curve so that contiguous query tiles are spatially compact. The output is the standard attention output tensor Y ∈ R^{B×H×N×D}, computed entirely within Flash Attention's tiled online-softmax framework without materializing the full N×N attention matrix.

## Method

AVQ-Attention uses a hierarchical codebook where each parent equals the mean of its C children (enforced via mass-weighted projection during online k-means training). At inference, the forward pass runs in two fused Triton kernels: first, a VQ precompute kernel assigns each key to its nearest parent then to the closest child within that parent's cell, accumulating per-codeword value sums and counts; second, a Flash-Attention-style kernel computes attention over parent codewords, extracts per-codeword importance from the online-softmax denominator already being maintained, selects the top-P most important parents, and incrementally refines the accumulators with their children's attention. The key efficiency trick is that the parent-child mean constraint lets parent logits be recovered as the average of child logits (Eq. 8), so the parent's contribution is corrected in-register via a ΔA = exp(S_c) − exp(S_p) update without ever reloading parent codewords. This keeps total complexity at O(N(M₀ + PC)D) while concentrating fine-grained quantization exactly where each input's attention mass falls, and the top-P parameter can be adjusted at inference time for elastic speed-accuracy trade-offs without retraining.

## Results

On ADE20K semantic segmentation (DPT-Large, N=901), AVQ-attention (M₀=32, P=8, C=8) achieves 43.33% mIoU at 0.116 ms kernel time, outperforming all other efficient-attention baselines at comparable or lower cost. At N=65,536, fused AVQ-attention kernels are 81–127× faster than Flash Attention. On COCO 1024² image generation (SD1.5 + ScaleCrafter), AVQ (M32P8C8) reaches FID 35.39 and CLIP 0.297 in 133.5 ms per denoising step, improving over the full-attention baseline (FID 41.03, 213.4 ms) and LinFusion (FID 36.37, 141.6 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVQ-attention outperforms all efficient-attention baselines on ADE20K mIoU: it beats Swin (43.33 vs. 42.90), NATTEN (43.33 vs. 40.99), Linformer (43.33 vs. 38.80), and Performer (43.33 vs. 25.29) while matching or exceeding their kernel speeds. Against flat VQ-attention, AVQ achieves higher mIoU at lower cost (43.33% at 0.116 ms vs. 43.04% at 0.164 ms for M=192), confirming the benefit of adaptive codebook allocation. On COCO 1024² generation, AVQ (M32P8C8) improves FID over both LinFusion (35.39 vs. 36.37) and ToMe-SD (35.39 vs. 43.00) while also being faster (133.5 ms vs. 141.6 ms and 163.3 ms per denoising step). The full-attention Flash Attention-v2 baseline retains a 5.7-point mIoU advantage (49.0 vs. 43.33) but at 2.7× the kernel time, and at N=65,536 the speed gap widens to 81–127× in favor of AVQ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends VQ-attention (Lingle, ICLR 2024), which replaces individual keys with M codewords to achieve O(MN) complexity, by introducing a hierarchical parent-child codebook that adaptively allocates refinement capacity based on per-codeword attention mass. The adaptive allocation concept is borrowed from mixed-precision quantization literature (HAQ, JPEG), but the key departure is that attention weights themselves serve as the importance signal, eliminating the need for separate sensitivity analysis or auxiliary models. The implementation is tightly coupled to Flash Attention's tiled online-softmax structure: the parent-child constraint (parent = mean of children) enables recovering parent logits from already-computed child logits, allowing in-register correction without revisiting parent codewords. The paper also positions itself against sparse attention, token merging, and linear attention methods, arguing that VQ-attention preserves dense attention semantics by replacing binary keep-or-discard with graduated approximation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AVQ-attention offers a practical drop-in replacement for standard attention in pretrained transformers, requiring only a few fine-tuning epochs, and achieves 81–127× speedups over Flash Attention at N=65,536 while maintaining competitive accuracy. The elastic top-P mechanism allows a single trained model to trade speed for accuracy at inference without retraining, which is operationally attractive. Open questions include per-layer codebook sizing (some layers may need far fewer codewords), deeper hierarchies for exponential resolution growth at linear cost, and whether (A)VQ-attention can serve as the foundation for new end-to-end transformer architectures rather than a retrofit. The paper is honest that training hyperparameters (M₀, P, C) remain lightly explored and that the method has been validated primarily on vision tasks at moderate token counts (N ≤ 901 for classification/segmentation).
