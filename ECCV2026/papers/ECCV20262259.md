## Problem

Existing diffusion-based video stylization methods rely on bidirectional temporal modeling and multi-step denoising, making them unable to process long videos in a streaming, real-time manner. The bidirectional 3D attention in DiT blocks means each frame depends on future frames, which is fundamentally incompatible with autoregressive frame-by-frame generation, and the resulting error accumulation over long rollouts degrades style consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262259/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source video stream (encoded to VAE latents at 832×640, 24 fps) along with either a text prompt (TV2V mode) or a reference image (RV2V mode), and produces stylized video frames autoregressively in a streaming fashion with only K=2 denoising steps per frame. The source video latent is concatenated channel-wise with the noised target latent, while the reference image latent is prepended temporally via a lightweight adapter. A rolling KV cache of fixed length enables unbounded video length, and the cross-attention cache can be reinitialized on-the-fly to switch style conditions mid-stream.

## Method

RTR-DiT converts a bidirectional DiT (Wan) into an autoregressive generator by imposing a temporal causal mask on 3D attention so each frame attends only to itself and preceding frames. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) fine-tuning a bidirectional teacher with a flow-matching objective on a curated 5,000-video stylization dataset, (2) initializing a causal student with a fixed 2-step denoising schedule, and (3) post-training via Self Forcing (conditioning on self-generated rollouts to close the train-test gap) combined with Distribution Matching Distillation and adversarial post-training to distill the multi-step teacher into a few-step generator. A key architectural contribution is the reference-preserving KV cache update strategy: once the rolling cache fills, the reference image tokens are permanently anchored at the front while remaining positions roll forward, preventing style drift over long videos and enabling real-time style switching by reinitializing the cross-attention cache while retaining the most recent 3D-attention frame for smooth transitions.

## Results

On the TV2V task, RTR-DiT achieves the best CLIP-T (0.2585), CLIP-F (0.9914), and Aesthetic Quality (0.6302) among all methods, while generating each 5-second clip in 0.12 min—26× to 61× faster than the next-best baselines (3.2–7.3 min). On the RV2V task, RTR-DiT attains the highest Aesthetic Quality (0.6714) and competitive CSD-Score (0.7958) and IQ (0.7321), trailing only the commercial Gen-4 Aleph model (CSD-Score 0.8312, IQ 0.7378) while running 29× faster (0.12 min vs. 3.5 min). In user studies, RTR-DiT ranks first on all TV2V criteria and achieves the best alignment and overall preference in RV2V.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In TV2V, RTR-DiT outperforms all three open-source baselines (Rerender-A-Video, FRESCO, TokenWarping) on CLIP-T by 0.031–0.062, on CLIP-F by 0.0007–0.0057, and on Aesthetic Quality by 0.021–0.057, while being 26–61× faster in inference time. In RV2V, RTR-DiT surpasses both open-source DiT-based methods (VACE and StyleMaster) on Aesthetic Quality (0.6714 vs. 0.6536/0.6012) and CSD-Score (0.7958 vs. 0.4275/0.4046), and is 230× faster than them (0.12 min vs. 27.6–28.5 min). Against the commercial Gen-4 Aleph model, RTR-DiT is slightly behind on CSD-Score (0.7958 vs. 0.8312) and IQ (0.7321 vs. 0.7378) but leads on Aesthetic Quality (0.6714 vs. 0.6336) and is 29× faster (0.12 min vs. 3.5 min). In the user study, RTR-DiT ranks first on all TV2V criteria (alignment 1.14, quality 1.32, overall 1.25) and achieves the best alignment (1.29) and overall preference (1.44) in RV2V, with visual quality (1.61) essentially tied with Gen-4 Aleph (1.60).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) DiT-based video generation (Wan as the base model), (2) distillation and autoregressive acceleration techniques (DMD, Self Forcing, causal attention masks), and (3) video-to-video stylization. Prior video stylization work (Rerender-A-Video, FRESCO, StyleMaster, VACE) relied on bidirectional temporal modeling or LDM-based pipelines that are inherently offline and slow. The key departure here is repurposing the autoregressive DiT + DMD + Self Forcing stack—previously developed for text-to-video generation—into a V2V stylization setting, and introducing a reference-preserving KV cache mechanism to maintain style consistency over long streaming sequences, which prior autoregressive video models did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 25–230× speedup over prior stylization methods while matching or exceeding their quality, RTR-DiT makes real-time, interactive video stylization practically viable for applications like live streaming, VR/AR, and creative tools. The reference-preserving KV cache strategy is a simple but important design choice that addresses a previously unacknowledged failure mode (style drift when reference tokens are evicted from the rolling cache). Open questions include how the approach scales to higher resolutions and longer videos beyond the ~1-minute demonstrations, whether the 2-step inference (K=2) is sufficient for more complex style transfers, and whether the dataset constructed via commercial models (Kling) introduces style biases that limit generalization.
