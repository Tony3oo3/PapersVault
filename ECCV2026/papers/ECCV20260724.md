## Problem

Existing 3D generation models produce monolithic, fused geometries that lack independent object-object relationships (OOR), and naively segmenting these fused meshes introduces difficult-to-repair holes and inaccurate assets. Generating geometrically-compatible, collision-aware compositional 3D objects from a user-provided mesh and text prompt—especially under occlusions that corrupt spatial cues—remains an open challenge for building interactive simulation environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260724/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a user-provided 3D mesh (rendered from a canonical frontal viewpoint to form a conditioning image) and a free-form text prompt specifying compositional semantics. It outputs a high-fidelity complementary 3D mesh with optimized transformation parameters (translation, rotation, uniform scale) and a fully composed interactive 3D scene where objects are geometrically compatible and collision-free. The entire pipeline is training-free and runs on a single NVIDIA H200 GPU, leveraging Nano Banana Pro for 2D image synthesis and TRELLIS2 for image-to-3D reconstruction.

## Method

Interact3D is a training-free generate-then-compose framework that reformulates compositional 3D generation as a structured registration problem. It first uses Nano Banana Pro to synthesize a compositional scene image and a complementary image, then reconstructs both into 3D meshes via TRELLIS2; PartField segments the scene mesh to extract coarse spatial guidance (transformation θ) between components. A two-stage composition pipeline follows: Stage 1 anchors the primary object via global-to-local registration (OBB for scale estimation, GeoTransformer for robust global pose under low overlap, then scale-aware ICP for refinement); Stage 2 places remaining components using an SDF-based optimization that explicitly penalizes interpenetration through a hard penalty on negative SDF values and a soft repulsion term within a safety margin ε, with a progressively increasing collision weight β^(k). For severe geometric mismatches unresolvable by pose adjustment alone, a VLM-driven agentic refinement loop renders multi-view images (including cross-sections), generates corrective text prompts, and iteratively edits the complementary image via Nano Banana Pro until collisions are resolved.

## Results

Across 150 test cases, Interact3D achieves the best score on every metric: Text CLIP 0.3314, Image CLIP 0.8236, Human Rating 8.33/10, and VLM Rating 8.57/10 for semantic fidelity, while reducing geometric intersection to 0.6939×10⁻³ (surface) and 3.8762×10⁻³ (volume). The closest competitor, PartField+RANSAC, scores 0.3094 Text CLIP and 6.28 Human Rating but still exhibits 2.2287×10⁻³ surface and 7.9280×10⁻³ volume intersection. MIDI, the image-conditioned diffusion baseline, suffers the worst physical validity with 22.185×10⁻³ surface and 116.70×10⁻³ volume intersection rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Interact3D outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the feed-forward baselines, Jigsaw (NeurIPS 2023) and 2BY2 (CVPR 2025) both struggle with generalization: Jigsaw scores 4.21 Human Rating and 2.7792×10⁻³ surface intersection, while 2BY2 is weaker still at 2.87 Human Rating, reflecting their dependence on narrow training distributions. The direct segmentation baseline (PartField+RANSAC) achieves the second-best semantic scores (0.3094 Text CLIP, 6.28 Human Rating) but still produces 3.2× more surface intersection than Interact3D (2.2287 vs. 0.6939 ×10⁻³) and 2.0× more volume intersection (7.9280 vs. 3.8762 ×10⁻³), with visible orientation inversions and geometric holes. MIDI (CVPR 2025), the image-conditioned diffusion baseline, is the weakest on physical validity by a large margin—its 22.185×10⁻³ surface and 116.70×10⁻³ volume intersection rates are roughly 32× and 30× worse than Interact3D respectively—indicating that predicting constituent assets and poses jointly from a single image fails to enforce collision constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the feed-forward 3D composition paradigm (Jigsaw, 2BY2, MIDI) that is bottlenecked by scarce 3D training data, instead reformulating compositional generation as a structured 3D registration problem guided by spatial priors extracted from 2D/3D generative models (TRELLIS2, PartField). It builds directly on GeoTransformer for robust global registration under low-overlap conditions and extends classical ICP with an SDF-based collision penalty, a formulation not present in prior composition or registration work. The VLM-driven agentic refinement loop, which iteratively edits 2D guidance images to resolve geometric mismatches, represents a novel departure from purely geometric optimization approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical data bottleneck for Sim2Real robot learning by providing a training-free, scalable pipeline for generating collision-aware interactive 3D scenes, and releases an 8,000+ pair dataset to support the community. However, the method's reliance on 2D spatial priors limits its applicability to fine-grained components (screws, tightly coupled joints) where severe occlusions create irrecoverable 3D ambiguities. The absence of ablation studies in the main paper and the moderate test set size (150 cases) leave open questions about the individual contribution of each pipeline stage and the robustness of the VLM refinement loop across diverse object categories.
