## Problem

Video-based remote physiological measurement (rPPG) is highly accessible but fragile under varying illumination, skin tones, and motion, while RF radar provides complementary illumination-invariant cardio-respiratory cues yet is impractical to deploy at inference. Existing video-radar fusion methods require both sensors at test time, and naive cross-modal distillation fails because RGB and RF waveforms differ in sensing physics and time-domain morphology, and teacher quality varies across samples, causing negative transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262329/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the student consumes only an RGB facial video clip (256 frames at 30 Hz, cropped to 72×72 via MTCNN) and outputs a physiological waveform (BVP) from which heart rate is extracted by band-limited peak detection in the 45–180 bpm range. During training, a pre-trained and frozen RF radar teacher (range-time I/Q representation) provides auxiliary spectral guidance as privileged information, but no radar input is needed at deployment. The training objective is label-optional: samples with ground-truth BVP receive joint supervised + distillation loss, while unlabeled paired samples receive distillation loss only.

## Method

RPM-Distill distills on band-limited frequency spectra where both modalities share the same latent cardiac periodicity, decomposing supervision into three physiology-structured components: (i) fundamental peak anchoring via a Gaussian soft window centered at the teacher's spectral centroid, (ii) off-peak background matching to suppress the noise floor, and (iii) spectral morphology consistency combining centroid shift and entropy-based sharpness penalties. A spectral policy network (1D conv stem + residual blocks + matrix-decomposition decoder) predicts a per-sample distillation gate and component weights from a three-channel student-teacher log-PSD relation map, enabling selective transfer that down-weights unreliable teacher signals. Because optimal gate/weight values are unobserved, the policy is trained via a bilevel meta-objective: a virtual student update under the current policy is evaluated on a small labeled validation split, and the policy is updated to minimize the virtual student's supervised loss, anchoring distillation decisions to actual physiological recovery quality.

## Results

Under the cross-dataset protocol (train on one dataset, test on the other), RPM-Distill achieves MAE of 1.57 bpm and Pearson r of 0.94 on EquiPleth, reducing MAE by 81% over the best video-only baseline (FactorizePhys, MAE 8.21) and improving correlation by 21%. On PhysDrive, it attains MAE 14.15, RMSE 20.24, and r 0.22, outperforming all video-only and KD baselines and matching or exceeding multimodal fusion methods on RMSE and STD. On RGB-only transfer datasets (MMPD, PURE), the distilled student also achieves the lowest MAE (11.60 / 8.28 bpm) and highest correlation (0.19 / 0.55) among all video baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPM-Distill outperforms all six video-only rPPG baselines (CHROM, POS, PhysNet, PhysFormer, FactorizePhys, BeatFormer) on both cross-dataset splits, with the largest gap on EquiPleth where it reduces MAE from 4.64 (POS) to 1.57 bpm. All five RF-only baselines (FFT, IQ-MVED, VitaNet, mmFormer, Vilesov RF) are also surpassed on both datasets, confirming that the distilled student extracts more reliable rhythm information than the radar teacher alone. Against the four multimodal fusion methods, RPM-Distill achieves lower MAE, RMSE, and higher correlation on EquiPleth (e.g., MAE 1.57 vs. SATM's 3.92), while on PhysDrive it trades a marginal MAE increase over SATM (14.15 vs. 13.79) for substantially lower RMSE (20.24 vs. 21.99) and STD (12.67 vs. 15.22). Among the four cross-modal KD baselines, C²KD is the strongest on EquiPleth (MAE 2.45, r 0.90), yet RPM-Distill still improves MAE by 36% and correlation by 4 percentage points. On the RGB-only transfer datasets MMPD and PURE, the distilled student beats all video baselines by 0.5–1.3 bpm in MAE, demonstrating that the spectral rhythm prior transfers even without radar at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of privileged-information learning, cross-modal knowledge distillation, and remote physiological measurement. While video-RF fusion methods (Vilesov et al., FusionPhys, SATM, EvidentialFusion) have demonstrated the complementary value of radar for rPPG, they all require both sensors at inference; generic KD baselines (FitNets, DML, C2KD, MST-Distill) transfer features or outputs without accounting for the physiology-specific spectral structure of cardiac signals. RPM-Distill departs from both lines by (i) restricting distillation to a band-limited frequency domain where RGB and RF share a common periodic substrate, decomposing supervision into peak-anchoring, off-peak suppression, and morphology-consistency losses, and (ii) introducing a bilevel meta-learned spectral policy that adaptively gates and reweights these components per sample, thereby addressing the negative-transfer risk that plagues indiscriminate cross-modal alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a video-only student that outperforms multimodal fusion methods (which require radar at test time) on challenging cross-dataset benchmarks opens a deployment path where ubiquitous cameras benefit from radar's illumination-invariance without the hardware overhead. The non-monotonic label-scarcity result (40% labels outperforming 100%) highlights a tension between supervised anchoring and distillation-driven regularization that is likely relevant to other LUPI settings. Open questions include extension to respiration rate and multi-task vital-sign recovery, robustness under severe desynchronization or missing radar segments, and whether the spectral-policy paradigm generalizes to other heterogeneous-sensing tasks beyond RPM.
