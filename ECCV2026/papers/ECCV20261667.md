## Problem

Current VLN agents suffer from a 'Goal-driven trap,' prioritizing physical traversability over semantic rule compliance, and frequently violate regulatory constraints like 'No Entry' signs in urban environments. No large-scale benchmark exists to evaluate rule-compliant navigation, and existing safety approaches model constraints as soft optimization penalties rather than hard prohibitions, making them unable to handle geometrically traversable but logically forbidden paths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261667/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives K discrete panoramic visual slices at each node of a 29k-node urban graph, a natural language instruction, and the base policy's intended discrete action (Straight, Left, Right, U-turn). SNRM outputs a safety token (Safe or a corrected action) that gates execution, and when a conflict is detected, the Epistemic Mental Map produces a compliant detour trajectory. The MPSI pipeline separately takes a panoramic observation and a target rule category, producing a modified panorama with a geometrically rectified traffic sign injected at a road-anchored position.

## Method

The paper introduces two components: (1) Rule-VLN, a benchmark built on Touchdown's graph by injecting 177 regulatory categories into 8,180 nodes across four curriculum levels via a Mask-Prioritized Semantic Injection (MPSI) pipeline that uses dual-mask-conditioned DiT synthesis (shape mask + rule mask) with CLIP-based GMM quality filtering to ensure legible, geometrically consistent sign placement; (2) SNRM, a zero-shot plug-and-play module that decomposes compliance into a dual-stage perception framework—Macro-Micro Visual Prompting (DINO detection followed by global-context and magnified-crop views) and Knowledge-Driven Rule Grounding (SigLIP zero-shot retrieval against a 177-category rule bank, then VLM Chain-of-Thought reasoning to output a safety token)—and an Epistemic Mental Map that replans detours via a penalized greedy heuristic with backtrack penalties and a visual memory buffer for closed-loop trap detection, all without retraining the navigation backbone.

## Results

On the Rule-VLN benchmark (29k-node urban environment with 177 regulatory categories), all SOTA VLN models suffer catastrophic performance collapse when semantic constraints are introduced. The proposed SNRM module, applied zero-shot to FLAME at Level-3, reduces Constraint Violation Rate (CVR) from 41.79% to 22.53% (−19.26 pp) while boosting Task Completion (TC) from 11.43% to 17.40% (+5.97 pp). For the traditional Loc4plan model at Level-2, SNRM cuts CVR by 8.70 pp and improves TC by 1.53 pp. In terms of synthesis quality, MPSI outperforms FLUX.1-Fill on all fidelity metrics (PSNR 32.41 vs. 24.59, FID 7.72 vs. 30.31) while using 58.81% less GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SNRM is evaluated as a plug-and-play add-on to four VLN backbones: ORAR, Loc4plan (traditional), and VELMA, FLAME (VLM-based). Against FLAME (the strongest baseline), SNRM achieves the largest gains at Level-3, cutting CVR from 41.79% to 22.53% (−19.26 pp) and raising TC from 11.43% to 17.40% (+5.97 pp); at Level-2 the CVR reduction is 17.33 pp. Against Loc4plan, SNRM yields more modest but consistent improvements (TC +1.06 to +2.19 pp, CVR −2.97 to −8.70 pp), confirming utility even without heavy reasoning priors. VELMA is the only baseline not paired with SNRM in the main table, but it already achieves the lowest CVR among unmodified models (23.77–33.30%), suggesting its verbalization strategy partially captures rule signals. For the synthesis component, MPSI dominates FLUX.1-Fill on every quality metric (PSNR +7.82 dB, SSIM +0.176, LPIPS −0.259, FID −22.59) while halving VRAM usage, validating the dual-mask conditioning design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Touchdown urban VLN environment by introducing a formal semantic constraint layer (Eq. 1) that decouples geometric traversability from rule compliance, departing from prior safe-navigation work (Safe-VLN, VLM-Social-Nav) that models safety as soft optimization penalties rather than hard prohibitions. The MPSI synthesis pipeline builds on mask-conditioned DiT editing (Insert Anything) but adapts it to equirectangular panoramas with dual-mask conditioning to prevent feature bleeding. SNRM is positioned as a training-free, plug-and-play rectification layer atop frozen VLN policies, contrasting with retraining-based approaches that the authors argue incur high cost and degrade overall navigation performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Rule-VLN provides the first large-scale (29k-node, 177 rule categories) evaluation standard for rule-compliant urban navigation, exposing a fundamental 'granularity gap' where SOTA agents are blind to small-footprint regulatory signals. The practical value of SNRM as a zero-shot, architecture-agnostic safety envelope is significant for deployment, though the absolute performance remains low (best TC ~24% at Level-1) and the discrete-node formulation limits generalization to continuous environments. Open questions include whether the epistemic mental map scales to 3D geometric constraints, how to reduce the ~1s per-step VLM latency for real-time use, and whether the benchmark's synthetic sign injection fully captures the ambiguity and occlusion of real-world regulatory signage.
