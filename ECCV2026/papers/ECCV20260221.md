## Problem

Text-conditioned two-person interaction generation requires simultaneously modeling long-range temporal causality within each individual and tightly coupled coordination between partners. Existing diffusion-based interaction models denoise full sequences with bidirectional attention, which obscures causal structure and prevents streaming or long-horizon generation, while autoregressive alternatives suffer from temporal drift that compounds coordination errors across partners over time. No prior framework combines diffusion-level synthesis fidelity with explicit per-person causal structure and controllable inter-person coordination roles.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single natural-language text prompt describing the desired interaction and produces a temporally coherent two-person 3D motion sequence at 30 fps. Motion is generated autoregressively in the latent space of a shared temporal VAE (4× temporal downsampling, 64-dimensional latents per 4-frame window), with block size B=3 yielding 12-frame generation blocks. The underlying motion representation is 262-dimensional (22-joint AMASS skeleton) on InterHuman or 336-dimensional (56-joint SMPL-X) on Inter-X, and the first block is initialized from pure Gaussian noise rather than a ground-truth prefix.

## Method

InterCMDM builds a Dual-Stream Causal Diffusion Transformer (DS-Causal-DiT) that processes each person as a separate causal stream while coupling them through a single unified masked attention operation per layer, where the attention mask jointly encodes block-causal temporal constraints and cross-person interaction structure. A multi-task block attention masking strategy trains one model across four coordination patterns—simultaneous, reactive, leader-follower, and independent—by randomly sampling mask configurations during training, so that selecting a specific mask at inference time yields controllable interaction behavior without retraining. The diffusion objective assigns an independent noise level to each temporal block and restricts attention to the current and all preceding blocks, enabling stable latent rollout over long sequences without repeated decode-encode cycles. Training uses flow matching with a frozen DistilBERT text encoder, 8 transformer layers (hidden size 512, 4 heads), and weighted mask sampling (60% simultaneous, 20% reactive, 10% each for the rest) reflecting the empirical distribution of coordination patterns.

## Results

On InterHuman, InterCMDM achieves the best R-Precision Top-1 (0.529) and lowest MM Dist (3.765) among fully generative methods, outperforming the strongest diffusion baseline TIMotion (0.501, 3.769) and achieving the best FID (4.492) among non-prefix-conditioned methods. On Inter-X, InterCMDM improves R-Precision Top-1 by +0.120 over InterMask (0.523 vs. 0.403) and achieves the lowest MM Dist (3.154) and best diversity (9.490). In long-horizon evaluation, latent rollout achieves the lowest boundary discontinuity ratio (B/NB 1.66) and position discrepancy (0.024) compared to InterMask AR rollout (2.51, 0.048).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterCMDM outperforms all fully generative diffusion baselines on InterHuman: it beats TIMotion (strongest diffusion method) by +0.028 R-Top1 and −0.210 FID, and surpasses InterMask, InterGen, MoMat-MoGen, in2IN, and InterActor on every reported metric. The only method with a lower FID is HINT† (3.100 vs. 4.492), but HINT requires ground-truth initial frames, making it incomparable under fully generative settings. On Inter-X, InterCMDM dominates InterMask by large margins (+0.120 R-Top1, −0.551 FID, −0.551 MM Dist) and outperforms TIMotion on alignment and diversity. Interact2Ar*† reports a lower FID (0.148 vs. 0.215) on Inter-X but uses a different motion representation and ground-truth prefix, so it is not directly comparable. In long-horizon generation, InterCMDM's latent rollout achieves smoother transitions (B/NB 1.66 vs. 2.51 for InterMask AR) and lower skating (0.157 vs. 0.172), while the naive concatenation strategy, though better per-segment, produces severe boundary artifacts (B/NB 98.58).

## Relation to Prior Work

InterCMDM extends the causal diffusion forcing framework of CMDM (Yu et al., CVPR 2026) and Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2024) from single-person to two-person interaction generation. It departs from full-sequence bidirectional interaction diffusion models (InterGen, TIMotion, InterMask) that obscure temporal causality, and from frame-by-frame autoregressive methods (Interact2Ar, HINT) that accumulate drift, by introducing a block-causal latent formulation with a dual-stream architecture. The key architectural departure is the unified dual-stream attention with multi-task block masking, which replaces prior approaches that either concatenate both people into a single stream or use separate self/cross-attention modules, enabling a single model to handle diverse coordination patterns through inference-time mask selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InterCMDM provides a practical path to real-time, streaming two-person interaction generation (~89 FPS) with controllable coordination behavior, addressing a gap where prior methods either sacrifice causality for fidelity or fidelity for causality. The multi-task masking strategy is a lightweight mechanism for controllability that avoids training separate models per coordination type. However, the framework is limited to two-person interactions, relies on manually predefined masks rather than learned ones, does not enforce physical constraints (collision, contact), and uses coarse sentence-level text conditioning. Open questions include scaling to multi-person groups, data-driven mask discovery, and per-person fine-grained text control.
