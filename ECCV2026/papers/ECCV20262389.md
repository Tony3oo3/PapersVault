## Problem

Multi-view VLMs for autonomous driving generate excessively long visual token sequences, causing prohibitive inference latency. Existing token pruning methods apply uniform or statically allocated pruning ratios across views and rely on single-stage, static importance metrics, ignoring both the varying contributions of different camera views and the model's dynamic shift from diversity-driven to task-relevance-driven information processing across layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262389/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on multi-view camera images (6 views at 729 tokens/view for DriveLM and DriveLMM-o1, 3 views for MAPLM, 6-view video frames for STSnu) paired with a text instruction (system prompt + task-specific query). Visual tokens X^v ∈ R^{P×M×D} and text tokens X^t ∈ R^{N×D} are fed into an LLM; MVPruner intervenes at two specific decoder layers (before layers 0 and 16) to progressively reduce the visual token count by a factor of r then r², producing a compressed token sequence that the LLM uses to generate driving reasoning text (e.g., risk assessment, traffic rule adherence, scene description).

## Method

MVPruner is a two-stage adaptive pruning framework aligned with the model's dynamic information requirements. In the shallow stage, Diversity-aware Ratio Allocation (DRA) computes per-view feature diversity via minimum pairwise cosine distance among tokens, assigning larger retention budgets to information-rich views; Cross-stage Contribution-aware Token Selection (CCTS) then iteratively selects tokens that maximize a product of semantic uniqueness (distance to already-selected tokens) and task relevance (cosine similarity to instruction tokens), preserving both immediate representational coverage and future task utility. In the deeper stage, Instruction-aware Ratio Allocation (IRA) extracts cross-modal attention scores between each view's tokens and the instruction text, reallocating the remaining budget toward task-relevant views via a compensatory normalization that rewards views whose attention exceeds their diversity score; the top-k tokens by instruction attention are then retained. The key design principle is that shallow layers need broad, diverse visual context while deeper layers need focused, task-aligned information, so the pruning strategy transitions from structural summarization to semantic focusing.

## Results

On DriveLM (90% token pruning), MVPruner retains 98.5% of the original DriveMM average score (58.2 vs. 59.1), outperforming the second-best method Prune2Drive (57.4, 97.1%) by 0.8 points. On DriveLMM-o1, it achieves 98.0% Overall Reasoning accuracy (72.91 vs. 74.37), surpassing Prune2Drive by 2.6 points. On MAPLM, the 90% pruning setting yields 94.5% average accuracy (68.30 vs. 72.24), with only a 3.94-point drop versus 7.43–17.63 for competing methods. In terms of efficiency, MVPruner achieves 4.97× prefilling speedup and 87.3% FLOPs reduction on DriveLM while maintaining near-original accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVPruner consistently outperforms all six baselines across four benchmarks under both 75% and 90% pruning settings. Against the closest competitor Prune2Drive (the only method designed for multi-view driving VLMs), MVPruner gains 0.8 points on DriveLM (98.5% vs. 97.1%), 2.6 points on DriveLMM-o1 (98.0% vs. 94.5%), and 4.8 points on MAPLM (94.5% vs. 89.7%) at 90% pruning, without requiring offline hyperparameter search. The gap widens substantially on MAPLM, where single-view methods like FastV and SparseVLM degrade by 17.6 and 15.8 points respectively, while MVPruner drops only 3.9 points. On the video-based STSnu benchmark, MVPruner slightly trails Prune2Drive at 90% pruning (97.3% vs. 97.6%) but surpasses it by 4.9 points at 75% pruning (100% vs. 95.4%), and even exceeds the unpruned model by 0.04 points. Among single-view methods, DivPrune and PACT are the strongest baselines, yet MVPruner still leads them by 2.1 and 1.4 points on DriveLM at 90% pruning, demonstrating the benefit of view-aware dynamic budget allocation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token pruning literature for VLMs from single-view settings (FastV, DART, SparseVLM, PACT, DivPrune) to the multi-view autonomous driving domain, where prior work Prune2Drive already introduced view-specific budget allocation via offline hyperparameter search. MVPruner departs from this static allocation by proposing a two-stage, online pruning strategy grounded in an empirical analysis of how multi-view VLMs shift from diversity-driven to task-relevance-driven attention across layers. The method repurposes standard attention scores and cosine similarity metrics but organizes them into a principled two-stage pipeline (Diversity-aware Ratio Allocation + Cross-stage Contribution-aware Token Selection in shallow layers; Instruction-aware Ratio Allocation + instruction-guided selection in deeper layers) that aligns pruning with the model's evolving information needs rather than applying a fixed or offline-tuned schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 87% FLOPs reduction and ~5× prefilling speedup while retaining >98% accuracy make multi-view driving VLMs substantially more deployable in latency-sensitive scenarios, and the plug-and-play design (no retraining, no offline search) lowers the adoption barrier compared to Prune2Drive. The empirical characterization of dynamic information requirements—showing that shallow layers need broad semantic coverage while deeper layers need task-focused tokens—provides a reusable analytical lens for designing compression strategies in other multi-modal or multi-view settings. Open questions include whether the fixed two-stage layer placement (layers 0 and 16) generalizes to architectures with different depths, whether the method extends to non-driving multi-view tasks, and whether the modest but consistent gains over Prune2Drive (0.8–2.6 points) persist under even more aggressive pruning or on larger models.
