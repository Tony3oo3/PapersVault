## Problem

Sign Language Production faces a fundamental tradeoff: direct text-to-pose regression collapses under the modality gap, producing under-articulated and unintelligible signing, while dictionary-retrieval pipelines preserve articulation but stitch clips with naive interpolation, yielding robotic motion. Existing SLP frameworks are also largely monolingual and computationally expensive, limiting their practical deployment for the Deaf community.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261349/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SignSparK takes spoken text (embedded via Multilingual-CLIP) and a sparse control signal containing only k ≪ T keyframes (typically 3 per gloss: onset, midpoint, offset) in SMPL-X/MANO/FLAME parameter space, and synthesizes a full dense 3D signing sequence of T frames. The keyframes are extracted automatically by FAST from monocular video using WiLoR hand parameters, requiring no manual annotation. At inference, the model operates in fewer than 10 sampling steps (0.01 s/video), and the generated kinematics can be rendered as photorealistic avatars via 3D Gaussian Splatting. Classifier-free guidance allows flexible conditioning: the model can run with keyframes only, text only, or both.

## Method

The framework has two components. FAST is a unimodal two-stream transformer that classifies each frame as Begin/Inside/Outside (BIO) using WiLoR-extracted 6D MANO rotations for left and right hands in parallel streams, trained with cross-entropy and CTC losses; it achieves SOTA segmentation at 45× the speed of prior multi-modal approaches. SignSparK is a Conditional Flow Matching model whose key innovation is a reconstruction-based training objective: the control signal preserves ground-truth poses at keyframe locations while injecting noised interpolated poses elsewhere, forcing the UNet to learn the underlying kinematic distribution rather than merely interpolating. A single-step Euler integration loss (L_recon) explicitly supervises one-step pose recovery, enabling extreme inference efficiency (>100× over diffusion baselines), while a velocity-matching loss (L_vel) enforces temporal smoothness. The sparse keyframe conditioning inherently prevents regression-to-the-mean because the network must hit the anchor points, and the same formulation unlocks Keyframe-to-Pose synthesis for precise spatiotemporal editing.

## Results

On the primary SR-T2P task, SignSparK reduces body DTW-PA-JPE to 4.87 (CSLDaily) and 3.68 (Phoenix14T), and hand DTW-PA-JPE to 1.37 and 1.18 respectively, outperforming SOKE by 22% and 23% on body error. In the gloss-free T2P regime (zero keyframes), SignSparK still achieves the best DTW-PA-JPE across all three datasets (e.g., 5.24 body / 1.52 hand on Phoenix14T). For sign stitching, SignSparK achieves DTW-JPE of 9.01 on Phoenix14T versus 57–63 for all Sign Stitcher variants, while preserving 79% of ground-truth back-translation BLEU-4. Inference runs at 0.01 s/video (10 steps), a >100× speedup over SOKE's 1.55 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SignSparK outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated task. In sign segmentation (Table 1), FAST (F1 0.860, IoU 0.772) surpasses Hands-On [52] (F1 0.857, IoU 0.760) and both BIO Tagger variants [53] (F1 0.590–0.630) and IO Tagger [54] (IoU 0.460), while using only unimodal WiLoR hand features. In sign stitching (Table 2), SignSparK (DTW-JPE 9.01 on Phoenix14T) dramatically outperforms all Sign Stitcher [89] variants (DTW-JPE 57–64), reducing error by ~85% while conditioning on only 3 keyframes per gloss versus full-sequence poses. In gloss-free T2P (Table 3), SignSparK with zero keyframes beats S-MotionGPT [38] (9.45→5.24 body DTW-PA-JPE on Phoenix14T), T2S-GPT [94] (10.38→5.24), Text2Mesh [81] (13.48→5.24), Prog.Trans. [71] (13.67→5.24), and SOKE without dictionary [104] (6.16→5.24). In sign-retrieval T2P, SignSparK reduces SOKE's [104] body DTW-PA-JPE from 6.24 to 4.87 on CSLDaily (−22%) and from 4.77 to 3.68 on Phoenix14T (−23%), with comparable hand improvements (−20% and −14%). In the newly proposed KF2P task (Table 4), SignSparK halves the SLERP baseline's body DTW-PA-JPE (e.g., 2.39 vs 4.10 on Phoenix14T) and reduces back-translation BLEU-4 drop from 38% to 21%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SignSparK builds on the Conditional Flow Matching framework of Liu et al. (ICLR 2023) and the sign segmentation lineage of Hands-On (Low et al., FG 2025), but departs from both direct text-to-pose regression and dictionary-retrieval-plus-interpolation pipelines that have dominated SLP. The key conceptual departure is the sparse keyframe conditioning paradigm, which borrows the in-betweening formulation from human motion generation (MDM, GMD, CondMDI) and repurposes it as a training signal to prevent regression-to-the-mean while preserving articulation. FAST extends Hands-On by replacing the expensive HaMeR + 3D pose dual-modality input with a single WiLoR hand stream, trading architectural complexity for a 45× speedup with no accuracy loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× inference efficiency (10-step CFM vs. hundreds of diffusion steps) and the largest multilingual SLP framework to date (ASL, BSL, CSL, DGS) make this a practically deployable system rather than a research prototype, and the keyframe conditioning offers a drop-in replacement for the stitching stage in existing retrieval pipelines. The KF2P capability opens a new axis of spatiotemporal control that prior SLP methods lack. Open questions include whether the pseudo-ground-truth bottleneck from monocular 3D estimation (WiLoR, NLF, TEASER) limits ceiling performance, whether the approach generalizes beyond the four languages tested, and whether the standalone facial expression metrics (no prior baselines exist) will be adopted as standard.
