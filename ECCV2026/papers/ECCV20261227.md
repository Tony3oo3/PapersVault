## Problem

In VLN-CE, supervised fine-tuning policies suffer from compounding errors and cannot recover from out-of-distribution states, while reinforcement fine-tuning methods like GRPO are bottlenecked by sparse binary outcome rewards that provide no step-level credit assignment. In all-failure batches, GRPO's relative advantage vanishes entirely, causing gradient collapse and wasted computation. The paper observes that ~73% of failed episodes contain valid prefixes that standard RL naively discards, motivating a framework to extract dense step-level supervision from imperfect trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261227/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language instruction and a stream of egocentric RGB observations from Matterport3D scenes simulated in Habitat, and outputs continuous low-level navigation actions (waypoints) plus a stop signal. The policy is initialized from LLaVA-Video-7B and operates on single-RGB input without depth, odometry, or panoramic views. The PGSA auditor (CLIP, GroundingDINO, SAM3, Qwen3-0.6B) is used exclusively during training to score rollouts and is frozen, introducing zero additional inference-time cost.

## Method

SACA introduces a Perception-Grounded Step-Aware (PGSA) auditor that cascades a frozen tiny LLM for landmark parsing, CLIP for global semantic similarity, GroundingDINO for object-level bounding boxes, and SAM3 for precise pixel masks, producing both a continuous Soft Score for trajectory ranking and a discrete Hard Mask that pinpoints the exact Divergence Point where the agent deviates. A Scenario-Conditioned Group Construction mechanism then routes each sampled group: mixed-outcome groups trigger Repair Resampling (truncate at the Divergence Point and resample corrective suffixes), while all-failure groups trigger All-Failure Rescue (construct a Reflection Subgroup around a Pseudo-Anchor with mined hard negatives). The optimization objective combines trajectory-level GRPO advantages with step-level Consistency Alignment (behavior cloning on the Valid Prefix) and Contrastive Correction (explicitly penalizing the erroneous action at the Divergence Point), further stabilized by Margin-Based Rescue and Negative-Only Scaling to filter noisy pseudo-anchor signals.

## Results

On R2R-CE Val-Unseen (single RGB input, no extra data), SACA achieves 60.3% SR, 55.1% SPL, and 4.57 NE, outperforming the previous best StreamVLN by 7.5% SR and 7.9% SPL. On the more challenging long-horizon RxR-CE Val-Unseen, SACA reaches 60.3% SR, 49.8% SPL, and 62.1% nDTW, exceeding prior SOTA by 11.7% SR and 7.3% SPL. With additional ScaleVLN training data (†), SACA further improves to 64.7% SR on R2R-CE and 62.1% SR on RxR-CE, surpassing StreamVLN† by 9.2% RxR SR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SACA outperforms the previous state-of-the-art StreamVLN [58] by a substantial margin: +7.5% SR (60.3 vs. 52.8) and +7.9% SPL (55.1 vs. 47.2) on R2R-CE, and +11.7% SR (60.3 vs. 48.6) and +7.3% SPL (49.8 vs. 42.5) on RxR-CE, while also reducing NE from 5.43 to 4.57 (R2R) and 6.72 to 4.90 (RxR). Against VLN-R1 [43], the first GRPO-based VLN-CE method, SACA improves R2R SR by 30.1 points (60.3 vs. 30.2) and RxR SR by 38.0 points (60.3 vs. 22.3), demonstrating that dense step-level supervision resolves the gradient collapse that limits sparse-reward GRPO. SACA also surpasses other MLLM-based baselines—NaVILA [12] (49.7% R2R SR), MapNav [66] (39.7%), and NaVid [65] (37.4%)—by 10–23 SR points, all while using only a single RGB stream without panorama, odometry, or depth inputs. In the extra-data setting (†), SACA† leads StreamVLN† by 7.8% R2R SR and 9.2% RxR SR, and outperforms NaVILA† and UniNaVid† by 10.7% and 17.7% RxR SR respectively. Notably, SACA achieves these gains without any domain-specific Process Reward Model, relying instead on frozen zero-shot perception modules (CLIP, GroundingDINO, SAM3) for step-level scoring.

## Relation to Prior Work

SACA directly extends VLN-R1, which first applied GRPO to VLN-CE, by addressing its core bottleneck: sparse binary outcome rewards that cause gradient collapse in all-failure batches. Rather than training a domain-specific Process Reward Model (a costly and reward-hacking-prone alternative), SACA repurposes frozen zero-shot foundation models (CLIP, GroundingDINO, SAM3) as a Perception-Grounded Step-Aware auditor that extracts dense step-level supervision from imperfect trajectories. The Scenario-Conditioned Group Construction mechanism then routes batches to either Repair Resampling (for near-miss failures in mixed groups) or All-Failure Rescue (for null-outcome groups), a dynamic strategy absent from standard GRPO or prior VLN-CE RFT work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for extracting dense supervision from failed rollouts in sparse-reward embodied tasks without training a separate reward model, a pattern potentially transferable to other continuous-control RL settings. The observation that ~73% of failed episodes contain valid prefixes provides a concrete motivation for step-level credit assignment over trajectory-level discarding. However, the approach is evaluated only on Matterport3D-based benchmarks (R2R-CE, RxR-CE) with a single backbone (LLaVA-Video-7B), leaving open whether the PGSA auditor's zero-shot perception pipeline generalizes to novel scene types, real-world deployment, or substantially different instruction distributions. The added training-time compute from the multi-model perception pipeline (though inference-free) also raises scalability questions for longer-horizon or multi-agent settings.
