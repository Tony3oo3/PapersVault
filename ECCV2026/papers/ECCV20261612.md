## Problem

Existing unified multimodal models have not been adequately validated on free-form N-to-M interleaved text-image generation, where the model must autonomously decide the sequence and length of text and image outputs. This gap stems from three intertwined limitations: insufficient high-quality interleaved training data, unstable optimization over variable-length multimodal token sequences, and the absence of task-specific evaluation metrics that do not depend on rigid output templates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261612/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts arbitrary interleaved sequences of text tokens, image tokens (encoded via ViT for understanding or VAE latents for generation), and special control tokens (BOI, EOI, EOS) as input, and produces a free-length interleaved output sequence where the number and order of text and image segments are determined autoregressively. Generation operates at 512×512 resolution with a 7B+7B parameter architecture (ViT encoder + VAE encoder + shared transformer backbone), and the model supports N-to-M mapping where multiple input images and text segments can yield a variable number of output images and text passages.

## Method

ILLUME-X builds on the BAGEL MoT architecture, using a decoder-only transformer with shared self-attention across all layers but modality-specific QKV projections and FFNs, combined with an interleaved attention mask that applies causal attention to text/special tokens and bidirectional attention to vision tokens within each modality segment. Training employs a progressive two-stage strategy: first in-context generation training to stabilize image synthesis, then joint interleaved text-image training with a multi-objective loss (cross-entropy for text and special tokens, Rectified Flow MSE for vision tokens). A key architectural contribution is an interleaved classifier-free guidance mechanism that decomposes conditions into text and image components with separate guidance scales (γ_txt, γ_img), using three random condition-dropping strategies during training. The data pipeline curates 100K high-fidelity interleaved samples through three complementary methods: multi-interval sliding-window video frame extraction with aesthetic/motion filtering and multi-level captioning, CoT-guided multi-step in-context image generation, and multi-round self-reflection with external MLLM critique.

## Results

On ISG-Bench, ILLUME-X achieves an average score of 6.26 among unified models, matching the non-unified ISG-AGENT system (6.26) and surpassing the next-best unified model CoMM-Minigpt-5 (2.96) by over 110%. On the proposed ILScore metric, ILLUME-X attains the highest overall score of 5.34, marginally ahead of Emu 3.5 (5.33) and clearly ahead of Gemini 3 Pro (4.84). For standard text-to-image generation, ILLUME-X scores 0.85 on GenEval and 86.38 on DPG-Bench, outperforming BAGEL (0.82 / 85.07) and Janus-Pro (0.80 / 84.19) while using a compact 7B+7B parameter architecture with 81.33 s per image (vs. 409.50 s for Emu 3.5's 34B model).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unified models, ILLUME-X (AVG 6.26) dramatically outperforms all prior unified baselines on ISG-Bench: Anole (2.81), MiniGPT-5 (2.79), CoMM-MiniGPT-5 (2.96), Seed-Llama-14b (2.39), and Show-o (2.33), with gains exceeding 110% over the strongest unified competitor. ILLUME-X ties the non-unified ISG-AGENT pipeline (6.26) and surpasses the Gemini + SD3 pipeline (5.83), demonstrating that a single unified model can match multi-system agent approaches. On the proposed ILScore, ILLUME-X (5.34) edges out Emu 3.5 (5.33) and clearly beats Gemini 3 Pro (4.84), with particular strengths in Style Transfer (5.16 vs. 4.34/4.44), 3D Scene (4.95 vs. 4.01/4.12), and Visual Storytelling (6.99 vs. 5.38/4.84). For text-to-image generation, ILLUME-X leads on GenEval (0.85 vs. BAGEL 0.82, OmniGen2 0.80, Janus-Pro 0.80) and DPG-Bench (86.38 vs. BAGEL 85.07, Janus-Pro 84.19), with a notable +2.79 margin over BAGEL on the Attribute sub-score. In terms of efficiency, ILLUME-X uses 7B+7B parameters and 81.33 s per image, roughly 5× faster than Emu 3.5 (34B, 409.50 s), making it substantially more practical for deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

ILLUME-X is architecturally grounded in BAGEL's MoT paradigm (shared self-attention with modality-specific QKV projections and FFNs) and adopts its interleaved classifier-free guidance formulation from Mogao, positioning itself within the AR+diffusion hybrid family of unified multimodal models. Its key departure is the systematic treatment of free-form N-to-M interleaved generation: a specialized segment-aware attention mask that applies causal attention to text/special tokens and bidirectional attention to vision tokens, a progressive training strategy with self-adaptive objectives for variable-length multimodal sequences, and a 100K-sample data pipeline combining video frame extraction, multi-step in-context generation, and self-reflection. Unlike prior interleaved generation work (MiniGPT-5, Anole, CoMM) that often constrains output to a single target modality or fixed pattern, ILLUME-X explicitly targets arbitrary-length, multi-input-to-multi-output sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: prior unified models lacked both dedicated interleaved training data and robust evaluation, with ISG-Bench failing when output structure deviates from its templates. ILScore's four-dimensional hierarchical metric (image-text accuracy, single-image accuracy, image-sequence accuracy, text accuracy) and the 100K curated dataset are practical contributions likely to be adopted by the community. However, the model is limited to 512×512 resolution with finite context length, and its ILScore advantage over Emu 3.5 is marginal (5.34 vs. 5.33), suggesting the core architecture is not yet the bottleneck—data quality and training strategy are. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, longer interleaved sequences, and additional modalities (video, audio) without the current architectural constraints.
