## Problem

Pretrained video generative models produce imagined futures that drift from task intent and are not reliably action-conditional, making them difficult to use for robot planning or policy extraction. Prior work treats language-based task decomposition and video prediction as loosely coupled modules, so there is no closed-loop mechanism ensuring that imagined futures, high-level reasoning, and low-level actions remain semantically aligned. RoboTALES addresses this by explicitly imposing hierarchical structure on imagination and enforcing semantic faithfulness of simulated futures through a critic-guided training signal.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a single-frame observation from 3 camera views at 256×256 pixels along with a natural-language task instruction. The LLM planner decomposes the instruction into K∈[2,5] ordered sub-goals that augment the original instruction into a reasoning-rich plan C*. The video generator then produces 8 future rollouts per view, and the action policy outputs a continuous H-step action sequence in R^7 (6-DoF gripper pose plus a scalar open/closed gripper state).

## Method

RoboTALES couples four components in a single-stage training loop: a Gemini-2.5-Pro LLM planner that decomposes the task into sub-goals, a Stable Video Diffusion backbone conditioned on those sub-goals via CLIP cross-attention, a frozen VLM critic that scores decoded rollouts against the goal instruction, and a 1D diffusion UNet action policy conditioned on the video generator's decoder features. Only the cross-attention modules and a targeted subset of decoder layers are trainable, preserving the pretrained visual prior. The VLM critic's reward is incorporated through DDPO (differentiable policy optimization), treating each denoising step as an MDP action and applying a REINFORCE-style objective to steer the video model toward semantically aligned futures. Critically, no stop-gradient separates the action policy from the video generator, so action-level gradients propagate back into the decoder layers, co-adapting representations for both visual prediction and control. The joint loss combines a standard video diffusion loss, the DDPO semantic-alignment loss, and an action diffusion loss, weighted by β and γ.

## Results

On the RoboCasa benchmark (24 manipulation tasks, 50 rollouts each), RoboTALES achieves a 64% average success rate, outperforming Video-Policy (57.5%), UVA (57%), and all diffusion-policy variants (DP-CLIP: 41%, DP3: 23%, DP-ResNet: 6%). On LIBERO10 (10 tasks), RoboTALES reaches 97% average success, surpassing VideoPolicy (94%), UVA (90%), and π₀ (85%). Notably, RoboTALES uses only 50 demonstrations per task, whereas GR00T and the dual-process VLA baseline employ 300 demonstrations yet are still outperformed. The largest margins appear on structure-sensitive tasks (doors/drawers: 80–100% success) and long-horizon Pick-and-Place tasks (44–66% vs. 16–64% for Video-Policy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboTALES outperforms all six compared baselines on both benchmarks. Against Video-Policy (Liang et al., 2025), the closest competitor, RoboTALES gains +6.5 points on RoboCasa (64% vs. 57.5%) and +3 points on LIBERO10 (97% vs. 94%), with the largest per-task margins on Pick-and-Place (e.g., PnPCounterToSink: 50% vs. 32%). Against UVA (Li et al., 2025), RoboTALES leads by +7 points on RoboCasa (64% vs. 57%) and +7 points on LIBERO10 (97% vs. 90%). Against the diffusion-policy family (Chi et al., 2024), the gap is substantial: DP-CLIP achieves only 41% on RoboCasa and 53–58% on LIBERO10, while 3DA (Ke et al., 2024) and DP3 (Ze et al., 2024) score 6% and 23% respectively on RoboCasa. Against GR00T (Abeyruwan et al., 2025), which uses 300 demonstrations per task, RoboTALES still wins by +21 points on RoboCasa (64% vs. 43%) using only 50 demonstrations, highlighting a significant sample-efficiency advantage. The one exception is CoffeeServeMug (insertion), where Video-Policy (74%) slightly edges out RoboTALES (60%), suggesting that contact-rich insertion remains challenging even with reasoning-aligned imagination.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboTALES is built directly on the Video-Policy (Liang et al., 2025) codebase, which established Stable Video Diffusion as a backbone for robot action extraction by conditioning a 1D action UNet on video UNet decoder features. The key departure is that prior video-based policies (Video-Policy, Gen2Act, ViPRA) treat the video generator as a frozen or passively trained feature extractor, whereas RoboTALES actively reshapes the video model's internal representations through two new interfaces: an LLM planner that injects hierarchical subgoal tokens into the diffusion cross-attention, and a VLM critic whose reward signal is backpropagated via DDPO (Black et al., 2023) to steer denoising transitions toward task-aligned futures. This contrasts with prior LLM-planning-for-robotics work (SayCan, Inner Monologue), where language reasoning selects actions externally but never conditions the predictive model's latent dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that coupling hierarchical reasoning, generative prediction, and control in a single training loop produces representations that are simultaneously visually faithful, semantically aligned, and informative for action—addressing a gap where prior pipelines left these objectives decoupled. The sample-efficiency result (50 demos outperforming 300-demo baselines) and the robustness to observation noise suggest the reasoning-aligned features generalize better than purely visual ones. However, evaluation is confined to simulation (RoboCasa, LIBERO10), the VLM critic provides only a coarse scalar reward, and the 6–7-day training cost on 2×A100s limits immediate practicality; open questions include whether learnable, task-aware critics and real-world deployment can preserve the gains.
