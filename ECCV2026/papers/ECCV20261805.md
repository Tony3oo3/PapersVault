## Problem

Existing dynamic 3D reconstruction methods (NeRF, 3DGS) recover geometry and appearance but do not explicitly recover physically meaningful parameters, producing results that are visually plausible yet physically inconsistent under unseen forces. Physics-aware approaches like Spring-Gaus couple spring-mass systems to 3D Gaussians but require dense multi-view inputs and expensive per-scene optimization, making them impractical for general monocular deployment. No prior work provides a feedforward, generalizable framework that jointly infers both visual appearance and physical attributes from a single video without per-scene tuning or manual physics annotation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular video of 20 frames at 512×512 resolution capturing an object undergoing free-fall, collision, and rebound dynamics. It outputs a canonical 3DGS representation (Gaussian centers, scales, rotations, colors, opacities) from a frozen off-the-shelf predictor, plus a compact set of shared physical parameters (scalar mass, stiffness, damping, and friction) that parameterize a spring-mass system bound to the Gaussians. The combined output constitutes a simulation-ready deformable asset exportable to physics engines, with inference completing in under 1 second versus over 1 hour for per-scene optimization baselines.

## Method

ReconPhys employs a dual-branch architecture: a frozen 3DGS predictor (TRELLIS-based) provides the canonical geometry and appearance, while a physics predictor (InternViT-300M encoder → ResNet with self-attention → MLP decoder) regresses shared physical attributes from the video. The physical dynamics use a 3D spring-mass system with KNN-defined connectivity, generalized Hooke's law forces with tunable exponent, and semi-implicit Euler integration; Gaussian centers are updated via inverse-distance-weighting interpolation from a sparse set of volume-sampled anchors. Training is fully self-supervised: a differentiable simulation-rendering loop propagates photometric reconstruction loss back through the unrolled spring-mass dynamics to update the physics predictor, with no ground-truth physics labels required. A self-forcing strategy (autoregressive rollout from the model's own predicted states) combined with truncated backpropagation ensures long-horizon stability and prevents gradient explosion during multi-step unrolling.

## Results

On the cross-object generalization benchmark (46 unseen objects), ReconPhys achieves 33.84 PSNR and 0.001 Chamfer Distance in dynamic reconstruction, and 21.64 PSNR with 0.004 CD in future prediction. In future prediction, it outperforms Spring-Gaus by +8.37 dB PSNR (21.64 vs. 13.27) and reduces CD from 0.349 to 0.004. Inference completes in under 1 second, compared to over 1 hour for both 4DGS and Spring-Gaus. On the real-world Spring-Gaus benchmark, ReconPhys achieves 28.53 PSNR (reconstruction) and 19.45 PSNR (prediction), again surpassing Spring-Gaus (22.12 and 13.44 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReconPhys is compared against two baselines: 4DGS (a dynamic 3DGS method without explicit physics) and Spring-Gaus (a spring-mass 3DGS method requiring multi-view input and per-scene optimization). Against 4DGS, ReconPhys achieves substantially better geometric accuracy (CD 0.001 vs. 0.593, EMD 0.007 vs. 1.5010) and higher PSNR (33.84 vs. 30.33) in reconstruction, while 4DGS cannot perform future prediction at all; however, 4DGS retains a slight SSIM edge (0.983 vs. 0.953). Against Spring-Gaus, ReconPhys wins on every reported metric in both reconstruction and prediction: PSNR improves from 22.26 to 33.84 (reconstruction) and from 13.27 to 21.64 (prediction), CD drops from 0.466 to 0.001 (reconstruction) and from 0.349 to 0.004 (prediction), and LPIPS decreases from 0.1517 to 0.0366 (reconstruction) and from 0.2856 to 0.0876 (prediction). The most striking practical advantage is inference speed: ReconPhys runs in under 1 second versus over 1 hour for both baselines, enabled by its feedforward design that eliminates per-scene optimization. On the real-world Spring-Gaus benchmark, ReconPhys again leads on all metrics (28.53 vs. 22.12 PSNR reconstruction; 19.45 vs. 13.44 PSNR prediction), confirming cross-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction (4DGS, Deformable 3DGS) and physics-aware reconstruction (PAC-NeRF, PhysGaussian, Spring-Gaus). The key conceptual departure from all prior physics-aware methods is replacing expensive per-scene optimization with a feedforward neural estimator trained self-supervised via photometric loss through a differentiable spring-mass simulator. While Spring-Gaus binds a spring-mass system to 3D Gaussians, it requires multi-view capture and hours of optimization; ReconPhys instead learns a generalizable mapping from monocular video to physical attributes using a frozen 3DGS predictor and a trainable physics predictor with self-forcing for temporal stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-second inference time (vs. hours for per-scene optimization) makes simulation-ready asset generation practical for robotics and graphics pipelines, and the self-supervised training eliminates the need for ground-truth physics annotations. However, the spring-mass model with fixed KNN connectivity cannot represent topological changes (tearing, fracture, plastic deformation), and the primary quantitative evaluation is on synthetic data with a domain gap to real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the feedforward paradigm scales to more complex constitutive models, whether real-world video training can close the sim-to-real gap, and whether the shared-parameter assumption (single mass/stiffness/damping for all points) limits accuracy for heterogeneous materials.
