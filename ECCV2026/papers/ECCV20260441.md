## Problem

Dense multi-scan LiDAR processing repeatedly encodes geometrically redundant frames, inflating computation, communication, and storage costs as sensing frequency and resolution grow. Naive frame differencing (voxel occupancy, depth-grid projection, nearest-neighbor matching) fails for sparse, viewpoint-dependent 3D point clouds because ego-motion continuously alters the visible set and sampling varies across scans, producing spurious activations dominated by discretization artifacts rather than genuine structural change.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N consecutive LiDAR scans (N=10 at 20 Hz for nuScenes, N=3 at 10 Hz for Waymo) along with their relative SE(3) ego-motion transforms, and produces a temporally sparse eventified point cloud in which each retained point carries a signed geometric deviation magnitude Δd as an additional feature channel. In the hybrid 1F+(N−1)T mode, the most recent scan is kept in full while all prior scans are replaced by their event subsets, yielding a 72% point reduction on nuScenes. The eventified cloud is then fed unchanged into standard detection backbones (CenterPoint, PTv3) that output 3D bounding boxes.

## Method

Event-LiDAR formulates 3D event extraction as a temporal estimation problem: after warping the current scan into the previous scan's frame via ego-motion compensation, it retrieves a local neighborhood from the previous scan (indexed in equirectangular projection for O(1) lookup) and constructs a first-order geometric model (line from two neighbors, plane from three, with degenerate fallbacks). The signed deviation of each current point is computed as its orthogonal projection distance onto this predicted local surface, with a sign determined by relative sensor range. A range-aware threshold then gates which deviations qualify as events, and for newly observed regions with insufficient neighbor support the model extrapolates from available scanline context to avoid trivially flagging all points. The entire preprocessing runs in ~0.34 ms per frame (single-threaded, SIMD-vectorized) without any learned parameters. An optional event-aware network variant reallocates channel width and depth toward early stages to better exploit the sparser input distribution while keeping total parameter count matched.

## Results

On nuScenes with PTv3 under the 1F+9T setting (71.9% point reduction), Event-LiDAR achieves mAP 63.1 / NDS 68.1, within 0.4 mAP of the full-scan baseline (63.5 / 68.5), while reducing inference time from 71.2 ms to 57.8 ms (1.23× speedup). On Waymo with PTv3 under 1F+2T (53.5% reduction), it reaches L2 mAP 68.8 / L2 mAPH 67.5 versus 69.7 / 68.3 for the full-scan baseline, with a 1.20× speedup. Training is accelerated by up to 23% (nuScenes 10T) and 36% (Waymo 3T). The eventification front-end itself runs in 0.34 ms per frame on nuScenes (single thread), adding negligible latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the 1F+9T setting on nuScenes with PTv3, Event-LiDAR (mAP 62.9–63.1, NDS 67.8–68.1) outperforms all three event-extraction front-ends at matched point budgets: Mapless-MOS [43] (mAP 59.4, NDS 64.9), FSD++ RPP [11] (mAP 61.8, NDS 67.2), and M-detector [38] (mAP 57.1, NDS 63.6). The gap is largest against M-detector (≈6 mAP), which retains too few static points for reliable detection, and smallest against FSD++ RPP (≈1.1 mAP), which also benefits from ego-motion-aware residual probing. On CenterPoint under the same setting, Event-LiDAR (mAP 59.5, NDS 66.1) again leads all baselines, with FSD++ RPP closest at mAP 61.8 / NDS 67.2 in the 1F+9V configuration—though FSD++ RPP achieves this at a higher inference time (61.8 ms vs. 57.2 ms) and lower point reduction (71.4% vs. 71.9%). The advantage of Event-LiDAR stems from its first-order geometric model suppressing viewpoint-change artifacts that cause spurious activations in correspondence-based or motion-classification baselines, while retaining genuinely unpredictable structural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

Event-LiDAR repurposes the change-based sensing paradigm from event cameras to 3D LiDAR, but departs fundamentally from both video-to-event conversion (which operates on dense pixel grids) and naive depth-image differencing (which suffers from range-dependent discretization). Conceptually, it is closest to moving object segmentation and FSD++'s residual point sets, yet reframes the problem as a temporal estimation task under sparse, viewpoint-dependent observations rather than motion classification or correspondence-absence flagging. The local first-order geometric model (line/plane from 2–3 neighbors) is a lightweight, label-free alternative to the learned 4D sparse convolutions or BEV temporal aggregation used in prior MOS work, and it explicitly suppresses ego-motion-induced and sampling-induced artifacts that plague direct differencing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As an architecture-agnostic, millisecond-scale front-end that requires no retraining of the backbone, Event-LiDAR offers a practical path to reducing compute and communication costs in deployed LiDAR perception pipelines without sacrificing accuracy. The 72% point reduction with comparable mAP/NDS, combined with 17–36% training speedups, makes it attractive for resource-constrained or high-frequency sensing scenarios. Open questions include how the method behaves under single-scan (no temporal history) settings, its failure modes on highly dynamic or occluded scenes, and whether the per-dataset threshold tuning generalizes across sensor configurations. The paper also notes that absolute speedups depend on hardware and backbone design, so the reported ratios may shift in different deployment contexts.
