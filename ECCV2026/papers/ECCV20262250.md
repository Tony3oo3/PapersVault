## Problem

Existing Group Activity Detection methods rely on actor-centric attention over appearance features to infer group structure, which in crowded multi-view scenes produces spatially implausible cross-group associations that blur group boundaries. Additionally, global scene semantics are either ignored or fused statically, injecting background noise when context is weakly relevant to the activity. The paper addresses both failures by introducing explicit spatial topology constraints and adaptive scene-semantic conditioning into the reasoning pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip with ground-truth per-frame actor bounding boxes in normalized image coordinates. The local stream extracts actor tokens from 8 sparsely sampled frames (1 on Social-CAD) via DINOv2-Base + RoIAlign, while the global stream aggregates 16 denser frames into a single clip-level VideoMAE-v2 feature. Output is a set of K group slots, each with a membership assignment (actor-to-group dot-product head), a collective activity label, and per-actor individual action labels, matched to ground truth via Hungarian bipartite matching during training.

## Method

The framework builds on the Practical GAD set-prediction backbone and adds two plug-and-play reasoning modules. STIR augments per-frame inter-actor attention with a learnable per-head spatial bias (an MLP over a 5-d pairwise descriptor of center offsets, log-scale ratios, and distance) plus a soft quadratic distance penalty (dist²/τ²) on attention logits, suppressing spatially implausible links while remaining fully differentiable and adapting the effective interaction range from data. LRCC projects the frozen VideoMAE-v2 clip-level feature into token space and applies token-type-specific sigmoid gates—separate scalar gates for actor and group tokens—that modulate how much global scene semantics each token receives, enabling activity-aware context injection without indiscriminate fusion. A temporal encoder (residual 1D convolution + stacked multi-head self-attention + attention pooling) aggregates cross-frame evidence, and the full model is trained end-to-end with a multi-task objective combining individual action, group activity, membership, and an InfoNCE contrastive regularizer under Hungarian matching.

## Results

On the Café benchmark (split-by-view), the proposed method achieves Group mAP₁.₀ = 30.47, Group mAP₀.₅ = 54.50, and Outlier mIoU = 75.98, improving over the strongest prior method (ProGraD frozen) by +10.80, +11.08, and +5.56 respectively. On the more challenging split-by-place, it reaches Group mAP₁.₀ = 23.82, Group mAP₀.₅ = 47.97, and Outlier mIoU = 71.97, surpassing fully fine-tuned ProGraD by +3.40, +3.83, and +4.26. On Social-CAD, the method attains 72.8% social activity accuracy and 90.2% individual action accuracy, exceeding the best prior social accuracy (70.0% by ProGraD) and the best reported individual accuracy (83.3% by Joint).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric across both Café splits. Against the strongest prior method ProGraD (frozen backbone), gains on split-by-view are +10.80 (mAP₁.₀), +11.08 (mAP₀.₅), and +5.56 (Outlier mIoU); against ProGraD (full fine-tune) on split-by-place, gains are +3.40, +3.83, and +4.26. Earlier baselines such as Practical GAD [22] (mAP₁.₀ = 18.84 view / 10.85 place) and VLCMTN [26] (mAP₁.₀ = 19.67 view / 11.58 place) are surpassed by 10+ points on mAP₁.₀ for the view split. On Social-CAD, the method beats ProGraD's 70.0% social accuracy by 2.8 points and exceeds the best individual action accuracy (83.3% by Joint [12]) by 6.9 points, while also outperforming STIRM [16] (69.6% social, 83.1% individual) on both metrics. The consistent margins across both splits and both datasets indicate that the gains are not tied to a specific camera view or scene, and the authors note that improvements are achieved without modifying the group decoder or supervision protocol, positioning STIR and LRCC as plug-and-play reasoning modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Practical GAD set-prediction backbone (Kim et al., ECCV 2024), retaining its Hungarian-matching decoder and multi-task objective while inserting two new reasoning modules. It departs from the dominant actor-centric paradigm—where collective semantics are inferred solely from inter-actor attention over appearance features—by explicitly modeling spatial topology (via STIR's learnable soft distance penalty and per-head spatial bias) and scene-level semantics (via LRCC's token-type-specific gating). Conceptually, it positions itself against both brittle hard-distance masks used in prior GAD work and static context fusion strategies, arguing that neither hard thresholds nor indiscriminate global feature injection adequately handles crowded, multi-view scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structured, task-aware reasoning modules yield consistent gains even when stacked on strong VFM backbones (DINOv2), suggesting that representation quality alone is insufficient for group-level reasoning—a finding that tempers the assumption that scaling visual encoders will automatically resolve GAD. The plug-and-play nature of STIR and LRCC (no decoder or supervision changes) makes them practical additions to existing GAD pipelines. Open questions include whether the dual-stream design (DINOv2 for local + VideoMAE-v2 for global) is strictly necessary versus a single unified backbone, how the approach scales to even denser or longer-duration scenes, and whether the soft topology prior generalizes to 3D spatial reasoning in multi-camera setups beyond the current image-plane formulation.
