## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models fail to interpret numerical color specifications such as hex codes (#FF5733) and RGB tuples because subword tokenization fragments them into semantically meaningless sub-tokens that text encoders cannot map to coherent color representations. This limitation is critical for professional design workflows (Adobe, Blender) where strict brand-color fidelity is required, and recent benchmarks confirm accuracy below 15% on numeric color tasks across state-of-the-art models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260087/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard natural-language text prompts that embed numeric color codes (hex or RGB format) alongside object descriptions, and produces 1024×1024 RGB images where the specified color is rendered on the target object. No special input formatting is required—the Color Token Aggregator detects color spans from the raw tokenized string regardless of which tokenizer (CLIP, T5, GPT-2) produced it, and the system operates as a drop-in module on top of frozen pretrained diffusion backbones.

## Method

NumColor is a two-stage plug-and-play framework. Stage 1 trains a Color Token Aggregator (CTA), a character-level sequence labeler with parallel 1D convolutions, a 4-layer transformer encoder, and a CRF layer, to detect and unify fragmented color tokens into single semantic units using B/I/O tags. Stage 2 trains a ColorBook of 6,707 learnable embeddings (d=4096) anchored at 5-unit intervals in CIE Lab space within the sRGB gamut, initialized from ISCC-NBS category centroids via T5-XXL encodings. Query colors are mapped through soft k-NN interpolation (k=8, τ=2.0) over the ColorBook, and the resulting embedding is injected *before* T5's transformer layers to enable bidirectional color-object attention. Training uses flow-matching loss on 500K synthetic rendered images (NumColor-Data, Objaverse-XL meshes with Lambertian materials) plus two auxiliary losses—directional alignment and interpolation consistency—that enforce geometric correspondence between Lab and embedding spaces. The ColorBook, trained solely on FLUX.1, transfers zero-shot to SD3, SD3.5, PixArt-α, and PixArt-Σ via their shared T5 encoder.

## Results

On GenColorBench (9.8K prompts, 400+ colors), NumColor achieves 4–9× improvement in numerical color accuracy across all evaluated models. On FLUX.1, ISCC-L1 accuracy jumps from 13.82% to 55.71%, ISCC-L3 from 7.74% to 48.02%, and CSS3/X11 from 5.86% to 51.96%. Color harmony scores (lower is better) drop from 247.4 (RGB) / 134.9 (Hex) to 16.90 / 18.47 on FLUX, a 10–30× reduction. The ColorBook, trained solely on FLUX, transfers zero-shot to SD3, SD3.5, PixArt-α, and PixArt-Σ with average accuracy gains of +33.5% to +37.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NumColor dominates all six baselines on every color granularity. FLUX.1-dev (the strongest baseline at 13.82% ISCC-L1) improves to 55.71% with NumColor, a 4× gain; SD3 (11.80%) and SD3.5 (14.92%) reach 49.23% and 51.72% respectively via zero-shot transfer of the FLUX-trained ColorBook. PixArt-α (9.45%) and PixArt-Σ (10.21%) see the largest relative improvements (~4.8×), reaching 44.93% and 48.56%. Sana (25.10%) and CogView4 (10.95%) are reported as baselines only without NumColor integration. On color harmony, all NumColor-equipped models reduce scores to the 16–26 range versus 105–826 for baselines, confirming that precise color control simultaneously resolves incoherent palettes. Notably, NumColor preserves pretrained named-color understanding: FLUX.1 scores 81.27% on ISCC-L1 named-color prompts without NumColor and 80.04% with it, showing no regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

NumColor repurposes two established ideas from different subfields for a novel combination: vector quantization (from VQ-VAE) is applied to perceptual color space rather than image latents, and NLP sequence labeling (CRF-based) is used to solve a tokenizer-fragmentation problem specific to color codes. It departs from prior color-control work like ColorPeel, which handles only a small fixed set of color names and requires per-color retraining, and from Rich-Text, which uses computationally expensive multi-pass diffusion. The paper's core positioning is that the failure mode is not in the diffusion backbone but in the text encoder's inability to parse numeric color specifications, shifting the intervention point to the embedding layer rather than the denoising process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot transfer of a single ColorBook (trained on FLUX) to SD3, SD3.5, PixArt-α, and PixArt-Σ without model-specific adaptation is the most practically significant finding, suggesting that color-to-embedding alignment is architecture-agnostic when mediated through a shared T5 encoder. This opens the door to plug-and-play color control for any future T2I model using T5 conditioning. However, two limitations remain open: color leakage (lack of spatial grounding) and memory color bias (pretrained object-color priors overriding specified colors), both of which are fundamental to diffusion models rather than specific to NumColor. The synthetic-dataset approach, while providing clean supervision, means performance on photorealistic scenes with complex lighting and materials remains unvalidated.
