## Problem

Open-weight text-to-image diffusion models frequently fail on multi-instance prompts by omitting or merging objects (count failures) and leaking attributes across semantically similar instances (semantic mixing). Existing training-free guidance methods operate primarily on cross-attention or token-level semantics, which cannot reliably carve out distinct instance regions in early denoising steps when boundaries have not yet stabilized, so these failures persist especially for same-class or intra-supercategory compositions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt with explicit instance counts (e.g., 'three dogs') and optionally bounding-box layouts for the layout-to-image setting. An LLM-based parser automatically extracts class tokens, per-class instance counts, and attribute tokens, which define the repel/bind token-pair relations used in the loss. The output is a generated image; in the layout-to-image variant, ISAC additionally refines coarse, overlapping bounding boxes into dense per-instance masks that guide attention within each region.

## Method

ISAC is a training-free, model-agnostic hierarchical objective that enforces an instance-to-semantic ordering along the denoising trajectory. Phase 1 (L_ins) builds a foreground gate from instance-aware semantic masks (SA_t · CA_t), clusters self-attention rows within that gate into N class-agnostic groups via K-means, and penalizes the worst pairwise overlap between resulting instance masks using a Maximum Pixel-wise Overlap (MPO) term. Phase 2 (L_sem) reuses the stabilized self-attention to form instance-aware semantic masks and applies a repel-and-bind loss: MPO between masks of different instances is minimized while MPO between class/attribute tokens of the same instance is maximized. A linear schedule λ_ins(t)=t/T and λ_sem(t)=1−t/T shifts emphasis from structural formation in early steps to semantic refinement in later steps. The objective is applied either as a latent-optimization gradient (ISAC_LO, single hyperparameter η=0.01) or as a gradient-free scoring function for best-of-B latent selection (ISAC_LS), requiring only access to attention maps and no fine-tuning, external vision models, or additional training data.

## Results

On SD1.5, ISAC achieves 36% average multi-class accuracy on IntraCompBench (vs. 20% for the strongest baselines DOS and InitNO) and doubles the HRS-Bench spatial score from 0.135 to 0.263. On SD3.5-M, ISAC reaches 52% average multi-class accuracy (vs. 34% for the best prior methods) with especially large gains in the #4 and #5 instance settings (40% and 20% vs. 18–19% and 7–8%). On the instance-counting task, ISAC attains 70% and 76% accuracy on SD1.4 and SDXL respectively, surpassing count-supervised methods CG (49%) and CountGen (69%) without any fine-tuning or auxiliary networks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ISAC outperforms all six attention/embedding-based baselines (Attend-and-Excite, SynGen, InitNO, TEBOpt, Self-Cross, DOS) on every metric of HRS-Bench, T2I-CompBench, and IntraCompBench across both SD1.5 and SD3.5-M. The margin is largest on IntraCompBench multi-class accuracy: on SD1.5, ISAC's 36% average nearly doubles the 20% of the strongest baselines (DOS, InitNO), and on SD3.5-M it reaches 52% versus 34% for TEBOpt and DOS. Against count-supervised methods, ISAC achieves 70% instance-counting accuracy on SD1.4 (vs. 49% for CG [31]) and 76% on SDXL (vs. 69% for CountGen [5]), despite requiring no fine-tuning, auxiliary networks, or extra training data. For layout-to-image generation, ISAC improves GLIGEN's HRS-Bench counting F1 from 0.666 to 0.713 and color accuracy from 0.307 to 0.452, surpassing the attention-refinement baseline CAR&SAR [42] on all four HRS-Bench metrics. Notably, ISAC's gains are most pronounced in the intra-supercategory regime (e.g., #4 and #5 instance settings), confirming that the instance-first hierarchy is most beneficial when objects are semantically similar.

## Relation to Prior Work

ISAC departs from the dominant training-free T2I guidance paradigm (Attend-and-Excite, SynGen, InitNO, Self-Cross) that uses cross-attention token semantics to select, aggregate, or guide self-attention structure. The key conceptual inversion is an instance-first hierarchy: self-attention is treated as a class-agnostic structural signal that can be clustered into N disjoint layouts *before* any class-token semantics are assigned, and only then are cross-attention semantics bound within those stabilized regions. This contrasts with prior methods where structural grouping remains conditioned on CA semantics, making them unable to disambiguate multiple instances sharing the same token. The paper also extends the evaluation landscape by introducing IntraCompBench, which explicitly stress-tests intra-supercategory compositions (2–5 instances) that T2I-CompBench and HRS-Bench do not isolate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ISAC provides a practical, plug-and-play, training-free mechanism that narrows the multi-instance reliability gap of open-weight diffusion models without fine-tuning, external vision models, or additional data—addressing a failure mode (count errors and semantic mixing among similar objects) that persists even in strong backbones like SD3.5-M and Flux. The instance-first principle and the two-phase loss schedule offer a reusable design pattern for decoupled structure-semantics control that could transfer to video generation or other structured generative settings. Limitations include the requirement for explicit instance counts in prompts (ambiguous-count prompts are out of scope), dependence on an LLM-based parser for token relations, and the added VRAM cost of latent optimization. Open questions include whether the hierarchy generalizes to open-vocabulary or unbounded-count settings and to temporal (video) generation where instance identity must persist across frames.
