## Problem

Existing attention mechanisms for medical landmark detection rely on global pooling operations (GAP, GMP) that discard the spatial positional information essential for precise localization. While coordinate features have been incorporated into final regression heads, their direct use within attention modules operating in intermediate layers remains largely unexplored, leaving a gap in how networks reason about anatomical spatial relationships.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262180/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 2D medical images (dental radiographs at 800×640, hand X-rays at 1024×800, cephalometric images at 1152×576, chest X-rays at 768×768) and 3D volumetric CT scans (resized to 96×96×192 or 128×128×64 voxels), producing landmark coordinates via heatmap regression with Laplacian-distributed ground-truth heatmaps (σ=12). Training uses Adaptive Wing loss with data augmentation (±15° rotation, 0.8–1.1 scaling), and evaluation reports mean radius error (MRE) and successful detection rate (SDR) at multiple physical distance thresholds.

## Method

MCPNet introduces a Masked Coordinate Pooling (MCP) module that transforms CNN feature maps into the coordinate domain via soft-argmax, using multi-level static grid masks plus adaptive masks (generated via TReLU-gated 1×1 convolutions with Gaussian smoothing) to preserve local spatial details that global pooling would destroy. Two complementary coordinate map families are employed: static Cartesian (x-y grids with instance normalization) and adaptive polar-like maps (Gaussian radial and angular components parameterized by learnable centroids and variances predicted from the static positional features). The resulting coordinate-domain positional features are fed into a multi-head self-attention layer (4 heads) that models anatomical contextual relationships, and the resulting attention weights are projected back to the CNN feature space through mask-wise feature recalibration, where each attention weight is multiplied by its corresponding mask and averaged to produce the final attention map. The full architecture is a U-Net with attention blocks inserted after each module's final ReLU, using 14 static coordinate maps and 50 adaptive masks, trained end-to-end with AdamW for 300 epochs.

## Results

On the ISBI2015 benchmark (Test1), MCPNet achieves 88.89% SDR@2mm and 1.07 mm MRE, outperforming the best attention baseline NAT (87.59%, 1.11 mm) and the best landmark-detection baseline NFDP (87.20%, 1.12 mm). On the Digital Hand Atlas, MCPNet reaches 96.94% SDR@2mm and 0.56 mm MRE, surpassing the previous best HTC (96.84%, 0.56 mm). On the 3D Spine dataset, MCPNet attains the lowest MRE of 5.3 mm and highest identification rate of 88.7%, and on the 3D Skull dataset it achieves 1.58 mm MRE with 78.75% SDR@2mm, both leading all competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among attention-based baselines, MCPNet outperforms all six (SE, CBAM, GSOP, AiA, NAT, CA) on every 2D benchmark. The closest competitor is NAT, which leads in SDR@4mm on BMPLE (87.89% vs. 88.45% for MCPNet) but trails by 1.30 points in SDR@2mm on ISBI2015 (87.59% vs. 88.89%) and by 0.28 mm in MRE on DHA (0.60 vs. 0.56 mm). Among landmark-detection baselines, HTC is the strongest on DHA (96.84% SDR@2mm, 0.56 mm MRE), but MCPNet edges it out by 0.10 points in SDR while matching MRE. On the 3D Spine dataset, MCPNet reduces MRE from 5.9 mm (PE, the best baseline) to 5.3 mm and improves identification rate from 86.9% to 88.7%. On 3D Skull, MCPNet achieves 1.58 mm MRE versus 1.63 mm for PE and 1.67 mm for H3DE-Net, with SDR@2mm of 78.75% versus 76.56% for PE. A capacity-matched U-Net† (22.71M params) shows no meaningful gain over the original U-Net, confirming that MCPNet's improvements stem from the masked coordinate pooling design rather than additional parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCPNet extends the soft-argmax mechanism from integral pose regression [32, 21, 46] into a mask-based attention framework, repurposing it to project CNN feature maps into an explicit coordinate domain rather than merely regressing final coordinates. It departs from the standard pooling-then-recalibration paradigm of SE [14], CBAM [44], GSOP [8], and NAT [11], which the authors argue inherently discard the spatial information critical for landmark localization. The adaptive polar-like coordinate maps and learnable mask generation go beyond static positional encodings [22, 37] and unidirectional coordinate attention [41] by making spatial representations content-dependent and multi-perspective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By performing attention reasoning directly in the coordinate domain, MCPNet addresses a structural limitation of prior attention modules that lose positional cues through global pooling, offering a principled path for coordinate-aware feature recalibration in intermediate layers. The consistent gains across six 2D and 3D benchmarks (including challenging cases like scoliotic spines and incomplete skulls) suggest the approach generalizes beyond a single modality. Open questions include whether the coordinate-domain attention can be extended to dense prediction tasks beyond sparse landmarks, how the method scales to very high-resolution or large-volume inputs, and whether the adaptive coordinate maps can be distilled into lighter-weight alternatives for deployment.
