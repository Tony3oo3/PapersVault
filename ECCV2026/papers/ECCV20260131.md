## Problem

Existing BEV perception pipelines are fundamentally closed-set, silently ignoring novel object categories (e.g., 'truck,' 'stroller') that appear in real-world driving, creating semantically blind regions that threaten downstream safety-critical tasks. A natural fix—grafting a 2D open-vocabulary detector onto an unprojection-based BEV pipeline—fails because the ill-posed 2D-to-3D lifting amplifies small localization and classification errors into geometrically inconsistent or distorted BEV outputs. The paper introduces open-vocabulary BEV segmentation (OVBS) as a new task and addresses the core 3D geometric inconsistency that blocks reliable OV-to-BEV transfer.

## Input / Output

The online model consumes only multi-camera RGB images (6 cameras at 224×480) and text prompts for category definitions; LiDAR is used exclusively offline for depth regularization and 3D detection priors. The BEV output is a 200×200 grid spanning 100 m × 100 m at 0.5 m resolution, processed at three scales (50×50, 100×100, 200×200), producing per-cell semantic class logits for both base and novel categories. Offline, the pipeline additionally generates per-scene 3D Gaussian primitives and BEV occupancy maps that serve as geometric supervision for the student network.

## Method

OVBEVSeg reverses the standard unprojection direction by projecting reliable 3D structure down into 2D and BEV across three progressive stages. (1) PBL establishes 2D-BEV correspondences by projecting class-agnostic 3D boxes from UNION into camera views, recovering instance masks with Grounded SAM, assigning CLIP-based pseudo-labels via text-prompt cosine similarity, and mapping labels back to BEV—bypassing erroneous 2D depth lifting. (2) BAGS performs per-scene 3DGS optimization supervised jointly by 2D and BEV occupancy maps (smooth-L1 on rasterized alpha compositing) and LiDAR-regularized depth, using BEV structural layouts as strong xy-plane priors to constrain Gaussians within object volumes and prevent the scatter seen in vanilla 3DGS. (3) BAGD distills the optimized teacher Gaussians into a feed-forward student by selecting the dominant Gaussian per pixel via maximum opacity and enforcing symmetrized KL divergence between teacher and student feature distributions, adding only 0.37 ms of online overhead while achieving 2.5× faster inference and 0.22× the memory of projection-based methods.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set under the multi-class OVBS setting, OVBEVSeg achieves 19.3 mIoU overall, with novel-class IoUs of 19.0 (truck), 20.3 (bus), and 6.6 (motorcycle), while maintaining competitive base-class scores (e.g., 41.8 car, 26.9 trailer). This represents a +15.3 mIoU gain over the SOTA closed-set method TaDe on novel classes and +6.6 mIoU over the GaussianLSS baseline, all while running at 79.6 FPS with only 32.4 MiB memory (2.5× faster and 0.22× the memory of projection-based methods). In the single-class setting, OVBEVSeg reaches 26.6 mIoU, surpassing the best baseline PointBEV (24.4) by 2.2 mIoU. With 0% novel-class ground-truth labels, OVBEVSeg (15.3 mIoU) outperforms semi-supervised baselines trained with up to 20% GT and remains within 1.7 mIoU of those using 40% GT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All closed-set BEV baselines (GaussianLSS, TaDe, PointBEV, CVT, FIERY, SimpleBEV, VED, VPN, PON, DiffBEV) score 0.0 IoU on novel classes since they were never trained on them; OVBEVSeg is the only method that produces non-zero novel-class detections, achieving 19.0/20.3/6.6 IoU on truck/bus/motorcycle. On base classes, OVBEVSeg is competitive: it slightly trails TaDe on bicycle (12.0 vs. 14.2) but leads on car (41.8 vs. 42.8 is a −1.0 gap), trailer (+0.6), and construction vehicle (+1.7). Against the semi-/self-supervised baselines (LetsMap, UniMatch, Semi-BEVseg), OVBEVSeg at 0% novel-class GT outperforms all methods using ≤20% GT (e.g., 15.3 vs. 14.6 mIoU vs. Semi-BEVseg at 20%) and remains within 1.7 mIoU of Semi-BEVseg at 40% GT (15.3 vs. 17.0). In the single-class setting (Table 2), OVBEVSeg (26.6 mIoU) beats PointBEV (24.4) and GaussianLSS (23.9) by 2.2 and 2.7 mIoU respectively, while matching pedestrian IoU (18.4 vs. 18.5). For 3D prior ablation (Table 4), the UNION-based PBL outperforms the F&B VLM prior on truck (19.0 vs. 14.2) and bus (20.3 vs. 15.5), though F&B handles small static objects (cone: 11.5) that UNION does not cover.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GaussianLSS (Lu et al., CVPR 2025) feed-forward 3DGS-based BEV pipeline by introducing an open-vocabulary capability and a '3D projection-first' principle that reverses the standard 2D-to-3D unprojection direction. It draws on unsupervised 3D detection (UNION), segmentation foundation models (Grounded SAM), and CLIP for pseudo-labeling, while its per-scene optimization and distillation stages follow the hybrid 3DGS paradigm established by InstantSplat and UFO. The Lang-BAGS extension builds on LangSplat but introduces a disentanglement loss to address subclass confusion in driving scenarios. Conceptually, the paper departs from all prior unprojection-based BEV methods by arguing that reliable 3D structure should be projected down into 2D/BEV rather than lifting noisy 2D predictions upward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine safety gap in autonomous driving—closed-set BEV pipelines silently ignore novel objects—and provides a practical framework that achieves this with real-time efficiency (2.5× faster, 0.22× memory vs. projection-based SOTA). The finding that 0% novel-class ground truth is competitive with semi-supervised baselines using up to 40% annotations suggests a viable path toward reducing annotation costs for open-world deployment. However, the evaluation is confined to nuScenes with a fixed 5-base/3-novel vehicle split, the pipeline depends on multiple external models (UNION, Grounded SAM, CLIP, a depth estimator), and the 4-day offline preprocessing on 8 A100s limits immediate scalability. Open questions include generalization to non-vehicle novel classes at scale, robustness to sensor degradation, and whether the projection-first principle transfers to other 3D perception tasks beyond BEV segmentation.
