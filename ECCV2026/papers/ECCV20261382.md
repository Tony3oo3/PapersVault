## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models can remove individual concepts or small synonym sets but fail to erase entire categories including all their subcategories (e.g., erasing 'guns' should also eliminate 'pistols,' 'rifles,' 'SMGs,' etc.). Prior approaches that handle multiple concepts require explicit enumeration of each subcategory, which is impractical when subcategories are numerous or unknown. This paper tackles the gap of achieving complete subcategorical erasure using only a single parent category prompt.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained Stable Diffusion model and a single parent category text prompt (e.g., 'guns') as the sole erasure target—no subcategory list or synonym set is required. It produces a fine-tuned eraser model (θ′) that has forgotten the entire category and its subcategories while retaining generation quality on unrelated concepts. The model operates in the latent space of a VAE with CLIP text embeddings as conditioning, and the Lipschitz regularization is applied to intermediate image outputs re-encoded through the VAE encoder.

## Method

SURE fine-tunes a new eraser model using four jointly optimized loss terms: (1) L1 maps the target category's output to a neutral (empty-string) concept, (2) L2 anchors the model's neutral-concept output to prevent drift, (3) L3 adversarially selects the most deviant related subcategory from the remaining concept space via Gumbel-Softmax and maps it to the neutral concept, and (4) L_lip applies Lipschitz regularization by adding Gaussian perturbations to generated images, re-encoding them through the VAE, and minimizing the ratio of latent-space displacement to input perturbation magnitude. The key insight is that smoothing the model's latent-space response to small perturbations around the target category generalizes erasure to unseen subcategorical variants without explicit enumeration. The VAE encoder is treated as fixed during optimization, and the Lipschitz term is a Monte Carlo estimate over N perturbation samples (optimal at N=5, σ=0.1, λ=0.01).

## Results

On the primary object-category erasure task (Table 1), SURE achieves harmonic-mean scores of 76.94 (Guns), 77.74 (Blade Weapons), 76.99 (Musical Instruments), and 74.63 (Toys), with Acc_E as low as 2.40% for Blade Weapons—far below the best baseline (ESD at 18.80% for Guns). On explicit-content erasure (I2P dataset), SURE attains NER-0.3 of 1.70 and FID of 14.02, outperforming the second-best method (AP) by 72.65% relative reduction in NER-0.3. For artistic-style erasure, SURE achieves the lowest CLIP score (18.02) and highest LPIPS score (0.85) among all baselines, indicating the most effective style removal while preserving unrelated generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SURE outperforms all eight baselines across every task. On object-category erasure, SURE's harmonic mean (76.94 for Guns) exceeds the next-best method ESD (55.11) by 31.64% relative, and its Acc_E of 10.00% is roughly 5× lower than ESD's 52.80%, indicating near-complete subcategorical removal. AGE (ICLR'25) is the strongest baseline on Blade Weapons (H=71.19, Acc_E=26.00) but still trails SURE (H=77.74, Acc_E=2.40) by a wide margin. AdaVD and ACE, the two CVPR'25 methods, show the weakest erasure (Acc_E > 52% on Guns) and the lowest harmonic means (29.11 and 35.77), confirming that attention-based and anti-editing approaches struggle with full-category removal. On explicit-content erasure, SURE (NER-0.3 = 1.70) halves the rate of the best baseline AP (3.64) while matching AGE's FID (14.02 vs. 14.20), and on artistic-style erasure SURE's CLIP score (18.02) is 17.93% lower than AP's (21.57) with a higher LPIPS (0.85 vs. 0.78), confirming superior style removal without degrading unrelated generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SURE builds on three distinct threads: the neutral-eraser formulation from ESD (mapping a target concept to an empty-string prompt), the Lipschitz-regularization-based unlearning framework of Foster et al. (adapted here to the latent space of a VAE encoder rather than raw pixel space), and the adversarial concept-selection mechanism from AGE (repurposed to discover related subcategories within a WordNet-defined category). The key conceptual departure is shifting the erasure target from individual concepts or small synonym sets to an entire hypernym category, using a single parent prompt and automatically identifying hyponyms through adversarial optimization in the concept space. This positions SURE as a category-level generalization of prior concept-erasure methods rather than a fundamentally new erasure paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to erase an entire category (e.g., 'guns') with a single prompt, without manually enumerating all subcategories, addresses a practical scalability bottleneck in deploying safety filters for T2I models. However, the approach remains tied to WordNet's hypernym/hyponym structure, which may not capture all semantically relevant subcategories or non-lexicalized variants, and the method requires fine-tuning a full eraser model per category. Open questions include whether the Lipschitz-smoothing strategy transfers to newer architectures (e.g., rectified-flow transformers or DiT-based models), how robust the erasure is to adversarial prompt rephrasing beyond the tested paraphrases, and whether the over-smoothing effect observed at higher perturbation counts (n=7) generalizes across categories and model scales.
