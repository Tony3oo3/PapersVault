## Problem

Current VLM training paradigms produce models that mimic the surface form of active perception (zoom, crop, pan) without functionally depending on their outputs—a phenomenon termed lazy perception. The root cause is a learning asymmetry: when a coarse global view combined with language priors suffices for moderate accuracy, the model has no gradient-based incentive to invest in harder multi-step visual search, so active perception remains a dominated strategy that is never learned.

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution image (4K–8K) and a text query, but during training every visual observation is deterministically downsampled to fit a resolution-conditioned token budget B(X) = clip(⌊|X|/γ⌋, B_min, B_max), so the model perceives only a low-bandwidth glimpse rather than the full image. The model outputs a sequence of spatial crop/zoom actions (bounding boxes in native pixel space) interleaved with budget-constrained visual observations, terminating in a final text answer. Crucially, cropping is always executed on the original full-resolution image, so the model trades spatial coverage for effective resolution within the fixed token budget.

## Method

Starve to Perceive enforces constrained visual bandwidth throughout a two-stage post-training pipeline on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Stage 1 (Budget-Aware SFT) bootstraps the policy with 7k trajectories generated via teacher distillation and trajectory rewriting, all under the token budget, so the model learns to emit syntactically correct spatial actions over degraded inputs. Stage 2 (RL with Perceptual Starvation) uses GRPO with a sparse binary reward (1 if the answer exactly matches ground truth, 0 otherwise) over 19k queries, where the constrained environment itself acts as a strict physical regularizer—no auxiliary losses, reward shaping, or architectural modifications are needed. The resolution-conditioned budget (γ=6.25, B_min=169, B_max=1337) ensures uniform perceptual starvation across diverse input resolutions. The key mechanism is that by making any single view informationally insufficient for task completion, active multi-step visual search becomes the singular pathway to reward rather than one optional strategy among many, and the resulting skills transfer to unconstrained test-time evaluation.

## Results

Under unconstrained evaluation, Starve to Perceive achieves an overall score of 64.59, surpassing the strongest 7B tool-augmented baselines DeepEyes (61.77) and Chain-of-Focus (61.75) by ~3 points. Under a strict token budget (B=256), the method retains 52.35 overall, losing only 0.18 points relative to the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base, whereas DeepEyes and Chain-of-Focus suffer 6.96 and 7.29 point drops respectively. The method also achieves 2.7–5.1× inference speedup and ~50% reduction in RL training time compared to unconstrained pipelines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest 7B tool-augmented baselines, Starve to Perceive achieves the highest unconstrained overall score (64.59) vs. DeepEyes (61.77) and Chain-of-Focus (61.75), with particular margins on HR-Bench 4K (76.63 vs. 71.63) and VisualProbe Hard (43.40 vs. 38.67). The most striking comparison is under the constrained B=256 setting: Ours retains 52.35 overall (only −0.18 from the Qwen2.5-VL base), while DeepEyes drops to 45.57 (−6.96) and Chain-of-Focus to 45.25 (−7.29), indicating that baselines trained without bandwidth constraints learn superficial zooming that collapses when visual information is scarce. Against Mini-o3, which employs over-turn masking, Ours leads by 17.9 points unconstrained and 15.6 points constrained. On perception-intensive benchmarks (MRWL, RWQA), Ours leads DeepEyes by 4.3 and 0.8 points respectively, though it trails slightly on TreeBench (37.78 vs. 40.25 for +BA-SFT+VanillaRL). Compared to the non-tool GPT-4o, Ours surpasses it on HR-Bench (76.63/70.00 vs. 68.0/63.9), V* Bench (85.86 vs. 62.8), and VisualProbe Hard (43.40 vs. 11.2), while GPT-4o retains an edge on RWQA (67.2 vs. 67.71 is a near-tie) and TreeBench (46.9 vs. 37.78).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant SFT-then-RL paradigm for tool-augmented VLMs (DeepEyes, PixelReasoner, Chain-of-Focus, Mini-o3) by identifying a shared failure mode—lazy perception, where models mimic zoom/crop syntax without functionally depending on their outputs—and proposing a minimal structural intervention: constraining the visual token budget at every observation step. Rather than adding auxiliary losses, curiosity rewards, or over-turn masking to incentivize active perception, the paper repurposes the Information Bottleneck principle as a training-time regularizer, making passive observation structurally insufficient. The approach is explicitly plug-and-play, requiring no architectural changes and integrating into existing GRPO/DAPO RL pipelines by simply replacing the visual input with its budget-constrained counterpart.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that a model trained exclusively under 256-token visual bandwidth outperforms all unconstrained-trained baselines in the unconstrained setting (64.59 vs. 61.77) while degrading only 0.18 points under strict budgets (vs. ~7-point drops for rivals)—challenges the assumption that training under resource constraints necessarily limits generalization. The 50% reduction in RL training time and 2.7–5.1× inference speedup make active-perception agents viable in compute-constrained and on-device settings. Open questions include whether the starvation mechanism transfers to larger model families (the study is limited to Qwen2.5-VL-7B), whether it extends to tasks requiring sustained multi-region attention rather than single-target localization, and how the optimal compression ratio γ should be tuned across diverse visual domains.
