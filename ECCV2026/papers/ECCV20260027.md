## Problem

Standard masked world models (e.g., V-JEPA2) fail on thermal infrared video because thermal equilibrium causes adjacent physical entities to exhibit near-zero temperature gradients, leading unconstrained self-attention to homogenize distinct objects into unstructured feature distributions and collapse downstream dynamics forecasting. This is compounded by the extreme scarcity of continuous, high-resolution thermal video datasets suitable for generative pre-training.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model consumes only single-channel normalized thermal video sequences partitioned into 16×16×2 tubelets; no RGB input is required. During pre-training, spatiotemporally aligned RGB video (up to 2700×2160) is processed by a frozen DINOv2 to extract a structural prior, but this information never enters the student's gradient path. Outputs are frozen latent spatiotemporal representations (ViT-L/16, 300M params) that support zero-shot action anticipation via an attentive probe, spatial localization via a 1×1 conv head, and 8-frame trajectory prediction via a single-layer Transformer.

## Method

Thermo-JEPA formulates thermal world-model pre-training under the Learning Using Privileged Information (LUPI) paradigm: a frozen DINOv2 extracts intra-frame spatial topology (pairwise cosine similarity of patch features) from aligned RGB, and a variance-based confidence map gates this topology to suppress unreliable regions. The resulting confidence-weighted adjacency matrix is assembled into a block-diagonal bias (zero across time frames, guaranteeing no temporal leakage) and added to the attention logits of the EMA teacher's multi-head self-attention as a soft structural prior. The student encoder processes only unmasked thermal tokens and predicts masked spatiotemporal latents via L1 loss against stop-gradient teacher targets, ensuring no RGB texture information leaks into the thermal latent space. The additive (not replacement) nature of the prior preserves intra-object thermodynamic gradients while enforcing inter-object semantic segregation, and the confidence gating provides an adaptive fallback to pure thermal self-attention when visible-light quality degrades.

## Results

On the zero-shot action anticipation benchmark (RGBT-World hold-out set, IR-only frozen-backbone protocol), Thermo-JEPA achieves 45.8% Top-1 accuracy and 62.1% Recall@5, substantially outperforming all baselines. In diagnostic probing, Thermo-JEPA attains 68.7% spatial mIoU, 8.4 ADE, and 11.2 FDE for trajectory forecasting, compared to 32.1% mIoU and 28.5 ADE for vanilla V-JEPA2 trained on the same thermal data. The model uses only 300M parameters (ViT-L/16), yet surpasses video foundation models up to 7B parameters (Cosmos-World) by 12.3 percentage points in Top-1 accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Thermo-JEPA (45.8% Top-1) decisively outperforms all seven baselines. Against the most directly comparable baseline—vanilla V-JEPA2 trained on the identical RGBT-World thermal data (18.6%)—Thermo-JEPA gains 27.2 percentage points, confirming that the confidence-gated structural prior is the critical factor rather than data scale. The contrastive early-fusion baseline (24.2%) also trails by 21.6 points, validating the paper's claim that dense cross-modal alignment causes modality pollution. Among large video foundation models, Thermo-JEPA surpasses Cosmos-World (7B, 33.5%) by 12.3 points, VideoPrism (1B, 31.2%) by 14.6 points, and InternVideo2 (6B, 29.6%) by 16.2 points, despite using only 300M parameters. Image-based transfer baselines DINOv2 (21.4%) and SigLIP2 (23.8%) are outperformed by 24.4 and 22.0 points respectively. In the diagnostic probing setting (Table 3), Thermo-JEPA also beats VideoMAE v2 (IR-supervised, 55.4% mIoU, 12.3 ADE) by 13.3 mIoU and 3.9 ADE, further confirming superior topological integrity in the latent space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the V-JEPA2 masked spatiotemporal prediction framework to the thermal infrared domain, identifying that standard unconstrained self-attention fails under thermal equilibrium (intra-frame spatial homogenization). It departs from prior cross-modal alignment approaches (CLIP-style contrastive learning, early-fusion MAE variants) by adopting a Learning Using Privileged Information paradigm: RGB topology is extracted via a frozen DINOv2 encoder and injected as a confidence-gated additive bias into the EMA teacher's attention logits, rather than serving as a reconstruction or contrastive target. The dataset contribution (RGBT-World) aggregates ten existing RGB-T benchmarks into a unified spatiotemporally aligned corpus, filling a gap the authors identify in prior thermal video resources that were fragmented and tailored to narrow discriminative tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of intra-frame spatial homogenization as a distinct failure mode for thermal world modeling, and the demonstration that a lightweight structural prior can prevent it without modal pollution, provides a principled template for adapting latent-space video models to low-contrast sensor modalities. The RGBT-World dataset (1.75M aligned frame pairs, multi-platform, up to 500-second sequences) is a practical resource for the thermal video community that previously lacked a unified generative-pretraining corpus. Open questions include whether the confidence-gated attention modulation generalizes to other low-contrast modalities (e.g., LiDAR, radar), whether the approach scales beyond 300M parameters, and whether the zero-shot evaluation protocol (frozen backbone + linear probe on a single holdout split) captures the full benefit of the learned representations in closed-loop planning or long-horizon forecasting.
