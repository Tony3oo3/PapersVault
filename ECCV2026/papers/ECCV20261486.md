## Problem

Existing UAV vision-language benchmarks are formulated as route-following tasks with long, step-wise instructions and goal-centric evaluation (SR, SPL), which does not reflect how UAVs are actually commanded in operations—via short, high-level commands that imply multi-stage, process-oriented behaviors. Moreover, prior benchmarks lack explicit collision-aware evaluation and process-fidelity metrics, making them poor diagnostics for safe, structured execution under brief and underspecified instructions.

## Input / Output

The policy receives two RGB frames (the trajectory's first frame and the current frame) to infer relative 3D position and progress, a short natural-language instruction (e.g., 'inspect the building on the upper-left'), and the current 4-DoF UAV state. The output is a next-step action target—either a velocity command or the predicted next state—executed at approximately 1 m spatial intervals along the trajectory, with additional 5° heading-change sampling during turns to capture viewpoint transitions.

## Method

HUGE-Bench is built on a real-to-sim pipeline: real-world capture with a DJI M400 across four outdoor scenes (~6.45 km²) is reconstructed into an aligned 3DGS-Mesh digital twin, where 3DGS provides photorealistic novel-view rendering for perception inputs and the mesh supplies collision-ready geometry and ray-cast depth. Landmarks are localized via LLM-assisted back-projection from top-down rendered maps, and spatial referring expressions are generated by prompting an LLM with target masks to produce human-like directional phrases. Trajectories are collected in Isaac Sim using task-specific waypoint rules and RRT-based planning for obstacle traversal, recording synchronized RGB, depth, pose, and collision signals. Evaluation uses process-oriented metrics (Trajectory Coverage Rate at multiple thresholds, nDTW for direction-aware alignment, Normalized Task Progress for stage completion) alongside safety metrics (Collision Rate and Collision-aware SPL), enabling diagnosis of both semantic grounding and safe execution across eight high-level tasks.

## Results

On the HUGE-Bench test set, π₀.₅ achieves the best Avg. TCR (0.581), nDTW (0.467), and CSPL (0.805), while π₀ attains the highest NTP (0.653) and lowest Collision Rate (0.011). OpenVLA performs substantially worse across all metrics (Avg. TCR 0.112, nDTW 0.011, CSPL 0.032), and even the depth-aware π₀.₅ variant (CR 0.013) does not consistently improve trajectory alignment over the standard π₀.₅. The per-task nDTW analysis shows that the seen–unseen gap is task-dependent: structured landmark-centric tasks (landing, orbiting) transfer well, while inspection, mapping, spiral-down, and traversal remain challenging for all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

π₀.₅ outperforms all other baselines on Avg. TCR (0.581 vs. 0.558 for π₀, 0.285 for FastVLM, 0.112 for OpenVLA), nDTW (0.467 vs. 0.443, 0.136, 0.011), and CSPL (0.805 vs. 0.802, 0.391, 0.032), indicating that richer visual pretraining improves overall trajectory quality and process alignment. π₀, however, achieves the best NTP (0.653 vs. 0.618 for π₀.₅) and the lowest Collision Rate (0.011 vs. 0.018 for π₀.₅), suggesting stronger stage-wise progress and safer execution. FastVLM (with π₀'s action expert) is competitive on process-oriented metrics (NTP 0.633) but lags significantly on trajectory alignment (nDTW 0.136) and safety (CR 0.060, CSPL 0.391) compared to the π-based policies. MemoryVLA improves over OpenVLA on every metric (e.g., NTP 0.549 vs. 0.122), confirming the benefit of implicit memory for long-horizon multi-stage tasks, yet still trails π₀ by a wide margin. The depth-aware π₀.₅ variant reduces CR from 0.018 to 0.013 but does not consistently improve nDTW or NTP, indicating that explicit depth helps safety but not overall task completion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the aerial VLN benchmark lineage (AerialVLN, OpenFly, CityNav, TravelUAV) by shifting from long, step-by-step route-following instructions to short, high-level commands that imply multi-stage process-oriented execution. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) the task formulation treats a brief instruction as requiring implicit subtask decomposition and stage-wise completion rather than endpoint reaching, and (2) the environment representation uses an aligned 3DGS-Mesh digital twin (following the hybrid-rendering-with-collision-bodies idea from Sage-3D) to simultaneously support photorealistic perception and physics-based collision queries. The evaluation protocol also moves beyond goal-centric SR/SPL to process-oriented metrics (TCR, NTP) and collision-aware efficiency (CSPL), filling a gap identified in the comparison with existing UAV benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HUGE-Bench provides the first systematic testbed for the operational regime where UAVs receive brief, ambiguous commands and must execute structured multi-stage behaviors safely — a setting that mirrors real deployment but was absent from prior benchmarks. The results reveal that even the strongest VLA models (π₀.₅) achieve only ~58% trajectory coverage and ~62% task progress, exposing a substantial gap between current capabilities and the demands of high-level aerial autonomy. Key limitations include the static-environment assumption (no moving obstacles, weather changes) and the unresolved sim-to-real transfer gap, leaving open whether process-oriented metrics and collision-aware evaluation will translate to hardware-in-the-loop validation. The 3DGS-Mesh pipeline and Isaac Sim integration offer a reusable infrastructure for future UAV VLA research.
