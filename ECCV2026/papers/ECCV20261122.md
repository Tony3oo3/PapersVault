## Problem

Existing lighting artifact removal methods (glare, flare, overexposure, flicker) are addressed in isolation, and RGB-only approaches cannot recover regions where pixel intensities are fully saturated. HDR reconstruction methods perform global tone mapping but inherently preserve the structure of optical artifacts like flare rays and glare halos. The literature lacks a unified paradigm capable of simultaneously detecting and removing diverse localized lighting artifacts by leveraging complementary sensor modalities.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a 640×480 RGB video frame paired with a temporally aligned neuromorphic event stream, which is first binned into a voxel grid and converted to a grayscale reconstruction by a frozen E2VID/E2VID++ model. The system produces two outputs: a pixel-wise soft artifact confidence mask (used both as an intermediate guide and as a standalone detection product) and a restored artifact-free RGB frame. The artifact mask serves as a manual-override interface at inference, allowing users to edit or empty the mask to control which regions are modified without retraining.

## Method

DELUX is a four-stage modular CNN pipeline that explicitly decouples artifact localization from reconstruction: (1) a frozen E2VID/E2VID++ reconstructor converts event voxel grids to grayscale frames, (2) a UNet segmentation network predicts a pixel-wise artifact confidence map from the RGB frame alone, (3) a channel-concatenation fusion module blends RGB, event reconstruction, and artifact mask through stacked convolutions, and (4) a UNet inpainting network restores corrupted regions. The key architectural insight is the non-mask consistency loss, which penalizes edits outside the predicted artifact region, forcing the inpainter to modify only degraded pixels while preserving clean content. The detector, fusion, and remover are jointly trained under a composite objective (Huber detection loss + MAE/MS-SSIM/VGG/TV reconstruction loss + non-mask loss) on 9,540 synthetic-artifact-injected RGB-event pairs from E2VID, DSEC, CARLA, and Cityscapes, with the event reconstructor kept frozen throughout.

## Results

On synthetic benchmarks with ground truth, DELUX achieves an overall MS-SSIM of 0.991, PSNR of 36.46 dB, and MAPE of 0.028, outperforming all baselines across every artifact category (glare, flares, overexposure, combined). On real-world automotive footage (E2VID and DSEC recordings), DELUX achieves an average Strong Artifact Suppression (ΔSAS) of 63.22%, the highest among all methods tested. The pipeline runs at 16.5 FPS with 90.2 M parameters on an NVIDIA A100, roughly an order of magnitude lighter and faster than the diffusion-based HDRev-Diff baseline (1.6 B parameters, 1.1 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DELUX outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor DAD† (a single-network adversarial decomposition trained on identical data), DELUX gains +0.021 MS-SSIM, +6.9 dB PSNR, and reduces MAPE from 0.060 to 0.028 on synthetic data, while achieving 63.22% vs. −352.50% ΔSAS on real-world footage—indicating DAD† actually worsens artifacts in several recordings. The flare-specific models F7K and Wu et al. trail DELUX by 0.037–0.042 MS-SSIM and 10–11 dB PSNR, confirming they do not generalize beyond their narrow training domains. SHDR, a global HDR tone-mapping method, performs worst on reconstruction (MS-SSIM 0.868, PSNR 14.4 dB) and produces negative ΔSAS, validating the paper's claim that global tone mapping preserves rather than removes local optical artifacts. HDRev-Diff, the event-guided diffusion HDR baseline, also yields strongly negative ΔSAS (−1402.87%), further confirming that event-based global enhancement does not suppress localized artifacts despite leveraging the same event modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the fragmented treatment of lighting artifacts in prior work, where glare, flare, overexposure, and flicker are each addressed by dedicated RGB-only or global tone-mapping methods. It builds on E2VID/E2VID++ as a frozen event-to-video reconstructor and on UNet-based architectures for segmentation and inpainting, but reorganizes them into a modular pipeline that explicitly decouples artifact detection from cross-modal fusion and targeted inpainting. The key conceptual departure is from event-guided HDR methods (HDRev, HDRev-Diff), which perform global dynamic-range expansion and inherently preserve local optical artifacts, and from single-artifact removal networks (CR-GAN, Flare7K++, SHDR), which lack the dynamic range to recover fully saturated regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a unified problem formulation—cross-modal local artifact restoration—that could guide future work on any modality-specific information loss in video, and it provides the first benchmark and public code for evaluating such systems across multiple artifact types. However, several limitations temper its impact: evaluation against ground truth relies on synthetic overlays that cannot capture real lens physics, real-world assessment uses a self-referential SAS metric, the pipeline processes frames independently (no temporal consistency), and the frozen E2VID reconstructor prevents end-to-end event feature learning. Open questions include whether the paradigm generalizes to other secondary modalities (thermal, LiDAR), how to enforce temporal coherence, and how to obtain paired real-world artifact-free references.
