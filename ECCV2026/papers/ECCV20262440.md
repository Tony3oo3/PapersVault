## Problem

Existing Referring Video Object Segmentation (RVOS) benchmarks ground expressions describing events already visible in the provided frames, leaving a gap for scenarios that require anticipating future events from observed visual cues. This limitation restricts applicability in proactive decision-making settings such as robotic planning and autonomous driving, where a system must predict which object will be involved in the next action before it occurs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262440/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a video clip truncated at a manually determined split point (only the observed frames before the split are provided) along with a foresight expression that describes a future event (e.g., 'the object that will be picked'). It outputs pixel-level segmentation masks for the target object across all observed frames, preceded by a chain-of-thought reasoning trace that explains the predictive inference. The key constraint is that the expression cannot reference any attribute directly observable in the current frames—it must require causal or procedural reasoning about what will happen next.

## Method

FeVOS-R1 builds on the Sa2VA architecture (InternVL2.5-4B MLLM + SAM2-L mask decoder) and is trained via a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 performs supervised fine-tuning on 2,904 synthetic chain-of-thought annotations (generated by Qwen2.5-VL using ground-truth masks as visual prompts) to establish interpretable reasoning patterns and the [SEG] token output format, optimizing a combined loss of cross-entropy, Dice, and text-generation terms. Stage 2 applies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with an IoU-only reward computed over predicted vs. ground-truth masks, leveraging the [SEG] token to enable end-to-end alignment between reasoning quality and segmentation accuracy without intermediate bounding-box outputs. Ablations show that format rewards are unnecessary once SFT establishes the output structure, and that the two stages are complementary: SFT provides the reasoning scaffold while GRPO refines it through reward-guided exploration.

## Results

On the proposed FeVOS benchmark, FeVOS-R1 achieves 42.3 J&F, outperforming the best zero-shot baseline VRS-HQ (31.0) by +11.3 and the fine-tuned Sa2VA baseline (35.8) by +6.5. On the ReVOS benchmark, FeVOS-R1 reaches 60.3 J&F (62.8 Referring, 57.8 Reasoning), surpassing fine-tuned Sa2VA (58.1) and even its zero-shot counterpart (59.1). On MeViS, FeVOS-R1 achieves 49.5 J&F, a +3.0 improvement over fine-tuned Sa2VA (46.5) and +3.1 over zero-shot Sa2VA (46.4), while remaining competitive with larger 7B models like GLUS (51.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FeVOS-R1 (4B) decisively outperforms all zero-shot baselines on FeVOS, with the largest gaps over traditional RVOS methods ReferFormer (18.2) and LMPM (18.9), which are designed for static attribute grounding rather than predictive reasoning. Among MLLM-based zero-shot methods, VRS-HQ (31.0) and GLUS (29.6) lead but still trail FeVOS-R1 by over 11 points, confirming that predictive segmentation demands fundamentally different capabilities. When fine-tuned on FeVOS, Sa2VA improves from 25.4 to 35.8 (+10.4), yet FeVOS-R1's two-stage CoT-SFT + GRPO pipeline adds a further +6.5 to reach 42.3, while fine-tuned GLUS only gains +3.9 to 33.5. On ReVOS, FeVOS-R1 (60.3) beats both zero-shot Sa2VA (59.1) and fine-tuned Sa2VA (58.1), with the largest margin on the Reasoning subset (57.8 vs. 55.2), suggesting the CoT+RL training transfers reasoning skills to traditional RVOS. On MeViS, FeVOS-R1 (49.5) surpasses fine-tuned Sa2VA (46.5) by +3.0 and all comparable 4B models, though it remains slightly below the 7B GLUS (51.3) due to model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-based video segmentation lineage (LISA → VideoLISA → Sa2VA) by repurposing the Sa2VA architecture (InternVL2.5 + SAM2 mask decoder) for a fundamentally different objective: predicting future events rather than grounding observable ones. The training recipe draws directly from the DeepSeek-R1/GRPO paradigm for reasoning enhancement, adapting it to pixel-level segmentation via an IoU-based reward on the [SEG] token. Conceptually, it departs from all prior RVOS work (Ref-DAVIS, MeViS, ReVOS) by shifting the referring expression from describing what is visible to querying what will happen next, requiring causal and procedural world knowledge rather than attribute matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper carves out a genuinely underexplored niche—predictive, pixel-grounded video understanding—that is critical for robotic planning and proactive decision-making but absent from existing benchmarks. The finding that format rewards are unnecessary when SFT already establishes output structure (Table 5) challenges a common assumption in RL-for-vision-language pipelines and simplifies the recipe. However, the dataset is small (968 clips), absolute performance remains low (42.3 J&F), and the authors themselves flag open questions around uncertainty modeling, hallucination mitigation, and scaling beyond current MLLM frame limits, suggesting the task is far from solved.
