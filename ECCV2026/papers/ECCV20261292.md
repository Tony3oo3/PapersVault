## Problem

Current MLLM benchmarks only evaluate reactive behavior—answering or abstaining—when visual information is insufficient, but never test whether models can proactively request specific user interventions (e.g., moving an occluder, changing camera angle, adding sketch strokes) to make an unanswerable query answerable. This paper asks whether MLLMs can recognize ambiguity and generate targeted, actionable suggestions for human assistance, a capability that has been entirely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261292/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

In the MCQA setting, the model receives an ambiguous image (the least-informative frame from a multi-frame sequence), a text question, and a discrete action set comprising valid proactive suggestions, an abstain option, and four category choices (one correct); it selects one action per turn in a multi-turn MDP until it predicts correctly, abstains, or exhausts allowed steps. In the OEG setting, the model receives only the image and question and produces free text, which an LLM-as-a-judge (Qwen3-8B) scores as a binary sequence indicating presence of a valid proactive suggestion and/or correct category. The benchmark contains 7,557 filtered samples across seven scenarios, with filtering removing any sample where ≥25% of evaluated models answer correctly on the first turn.

## Method

ProactiveBench repurposes seven existing datasets (ROD, VSOD, MVP-N, ImageNet-C, QuickDraw, ChangeIt, MS-COCO) into sequences where each sample has a least-informative starting frame, a fully-informative reference frame, and intermediate frames, enabling multi-turn proactive interaction. The core evaluation frames MCQA as a Markov decision process where the model's policy selects actions conditioned on the current image and question, with trajectory accuracy and proactive suggestion rate as metrics. For the RL fine-tuning component, the authors apply GRPO with a tailored reward: r_c=1 for correct category, r_p∈{0.5,0.75,1.0} for a valid proactive suggestion, and r_w=0 otherwise, training on QuickDraw and COCO only to test generalization. Setting r_p < r_c encourages the model to prefer direct predictions and reserve proactive suggestions for genuinely ambiguous cases, yielding substantial accuracy gains (e.g., 11.0%→38.6% for Qwen2.5-VL-3B) that generalize to five unseen scenarios.

## Results

In the MCQA setting, the best-performing model (o4-mini) achieves only 34.0% average trajectory accuracy with a proactive suggestion rate of 0.2, while the reference setting (showing the unoccluded image directly) reaches 79.8%—a gap of over 60%. The worst model (LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B) scores just 4.5% accuracy. After RL post-training with GRPO, LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B improves from 4.5% to 40.7% average accuracy (psr 0.3→1.1), and Qwen2.5-VL-3B from 11.0% to 38.6% (psr 0.4→1.2), both surpassing all 22 original MLLMs. In OEG, even the best model (LLaVA-NeXT-Vicuna-7B) proposes valid proactive suggestions in only 10.7% of cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

No monotonic scaling relationship exists: InternVL3-1B (27.1% acc) outperforms InternVL3-8B (12.7%) and InternVL3-78B (15.6%), and LLaVA-1.5-7B (24.8%) beats LLaVA-OV-72B (13.0%), indicating proactiveness is not an emergent property of scale. The LLM backbone significantly affects results: LLaVA-NeXT-Vicuna-7B (19.3%) far exceeds LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B (4.5%) despite identical vision encoders. Closed-source models (o4-mini 34.0%, GPT-4.1 30.9%) lead in accuracy but show suspiciously high COCO scores (92.8–94.4%) suggesting potential training data contamination. In OEG, LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B recovers its abstention problem, achieving the second-best proactive suggestion rate (10.5%), while InternVL3-1B's MCQA superiority does not transfer. RL post-training with GRPO (r_p=0.5 or 0.75) lifts both LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B above all 22 original models, with consistent gains across seen (QuickDraw, COCO) and unseen (ROD, VSOD, MVP-N, IN-C, ChangeIt) scenarios, confirming that proactiveness generalizes once learned.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formalizing 'proactiveness' as a distinct capability for MLLMs, departing from the reactive paradigm (answer or abstain) that dominates current VQA and agent benchmarks. It inherits the spirit of active vision (view planning, dynamic sensing) but reframes it as a human–model collaboration where the MLLM proposes natural-language interventions rather than controlling sensors directly. The closest prior work—MLLMs actively seeking information on single images [63,71] and directional guidance for visually impaired users [36]—assumes answerable queries or single-turn settings, whereas ProactiveBench targets unanswerable queries requiring multi-turn user intervention across seven diverse scenarios. For the training component, the paper builds on GRPO [52] from DeepSeekMath, adapting its group-relative reward structure to a multimodal proactiveness objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProactiveBench is the first systematic evaluation of whether MLLMs can request human assistance, and its findings—that 22 state-of-the-art models overwhelmingly abstain or hallucinate rather than ask for help—expose a fundamental gap between current MLLM capabilities and the collaborative behaviors needed for real-world deployment. The discovery that conversation histories and in-context learning introduce negative biases (reducing accuracy while inflating proactive suggestion rates) challenges common assumptions about how to elicit better model behavior. The RL fine-tuning result, showing that proactiveness can be learned with GRPO and generalizes to unseen domains, opens a promising but still early avenue; the large remaining gap to reference performance (e.g., 40.7% vs. 75.1% average accuracy) signals substantial open challenges in making MLLMs reliably proactive.
