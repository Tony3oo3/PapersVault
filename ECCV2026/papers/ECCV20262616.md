## Problem

Speculative decoding has proven effective for accelerating discrete autoregressive models in both NLP and vision, but it cannot be directly extended to continuous visual AR models (e.g., MAR, xAR) that generate tokens via diffusion processes. Two fundamental obstacles arise: (1) draft and target models produce divergent denoising trajectories, yielding near-zero acceptance rates, and (2) the modified distribution for resampling rejected tokens involves a complex normalizing integral with no analytic closed form, making direct sampling intractable.

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free inference acceleration layer applied on top of existing continuous visual AR models (MAR, xAR, Harmon) at 256×256 and 512×512 resolutions. It takes the same inputs as the base model — a class label for unconditional/class-conditional generation on ImageNet, or a text prompt for text-to-image on MSCOCO and MJHQ — and produces a single generated image. The draft model (e.g., MAR-B, 208M) proposes γ tokens (γ ∈ {4, 8, 16, 32}) per verification round, which the target model (e.g., MAR-H, 943M) checks in parallel; no architectural modifications or additional training are required.

## Method

The paper introduces continuous speculative decoding with three synergistic mechanisms. First, an approximated acceptance criterion computes p(Y_p)/q(Y_q) where both trajectories share the same final denoised sample x_0, replacing the intractable joint-path ratio. Second, denoising trajectory alignment (grounded in a reparameterization proximity theorem) forces the draft and target models to use the same noise ε_t at each diffusion step, reducing the expected token distance by 2·tr[Σ_t^q Σ_t^p] and raising acceptance rates from ~7% to ~30%. Third, a token pre-filling strategy seeds the first ~5% of AR steps with target-model outputs to stabilize early-stage acceptance. For resampling rejected tokens from the non-analytic modified distribution, the paper derives an acceptance-rejection scheme with upper bound M=1/Z, and shows via a corollary that the rejection threshold reduces to a closed-form ratio of single-step Gaussian densities, eliminating the need for extra diffusion model inference.

## Results

Continuous speculative decoding achieves up to 2.33× wall-time speedup on MAR (bs=256, γ=32), 2.72× on xAR (bs=256, γ=32), and 2.54× on Harmon at 512×512 (bs=32, γ=32), with acceptance rates ranging from 15% to 38% depending on draft length and batch size. Generation quality is preserved: MAR-H with speculative decoding yields FID 2.36±0.05 and IS 228.5±2.2 (vs. 2.35/227.8 standalone), xAR-H gives FID 1.82±0.07 and IS 286.1±4.2 (vs. 1.79/288.9), and Harmon-H maintains FID 8.38 on MSCOCO and 5.13 on MJHQ (vs. 8.39/5.15). Speedup scales positively with batch size, reaching its peak at the largest tested batch (256 for MAR/xAR, 32 for Harmon due to memory constraints).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares continuous speculative decoding against three standalone continuous AR models: MAR (Li et al., NeurIPS 2025), xAR (Sucheng et al., 2025), and Harmon (Wu et al., 2025). On MAR, the method matches the standalone MAR-H baseline almost exactly (FID 2.36 vs. 2.35, IS 228.5 vs. 227.8) while delivering up to 2.33× speedup; using MAR-L as the draft model yields a marginally better FID (2.34) at the cost of slightly lower speedup. On xAR, there is a small quality gap (FID 1.82 vs. 1.79, IS 286.1 vs. 288.9) but the speedup reaches 2.72×, the highest among all tested models. On Harmon, quality is essentially identical (FID 8.38 vs. 8.39 on MSCOCO, 5.13 vs. 5.15 on MJHQ, CLIPScore 34.7 vs. 34.8) with a 2.54× speedup at 512×512. In all cases, the method trades negligible quality loss (within one standard deviation) for substantial inference acceleration, confirming the lossless-approximation property of speculative decoding in the continuous setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends speculative decoding—originally developed for LLMs (Chen et al. 2023; Leviathan et al. 2023) and subsequently adapted to discrete visual AR models (SJD, LANTERN)—to the continuous latent space where tokens are generated via diffusion-based denoising transitions (MAR, xAR, Harmon). The key departure is that continuous distributions lack the closed-form probability ratios and summation-based normalization that make discrete speculative decoding tractable, forcing the authors to introduce an approximated acceptance criterion, a reparameterization-proximity-based trajectory alignment, and a tailored acceptance-rejection sampling scheme (building on Casella et al. 2004) to handle the non-analytic modified distribution. The method is training-free and architecture-agnostic, plugging into any continuous visual AR model that uses a DDPM-style reverse process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Continuous visual AR models are an increasingly important generation paradigm that avoids the reconstruction degradation of vector quantization, but their sequential diffusion-based decoding has been a bottleneck; this work provides the first general, training-free acceleration method for this class, achieving 2×+ wall-clock speedup across three architectures and two resolutions without measurable quality loss. The denoising trajectory alignment (sharing the stochastic noise ε across draft and target) is a clean theoretical contribution that also reduces the bias of the approximated acceptance criterion, and the acceptance-rejection trick that eliminates the need to compute the normalizing integral Z is a practical enabler. Open questions include whether the approximation bias becomes non-negligible at very high speedup ratios, whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation with longer diffusion chains, and whether further alignment strategies (e.g., at the feature level, à la EAGLE) could push acceptance rates higher.
