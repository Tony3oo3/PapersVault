## Problem

Learning-based stereo matching models trained on terrestrial data fail underwater because attenuation, scattering, and backscatter violate photometric assumptions, suppress texture, and break left-right consistency, making cost-volume matching ambiguous. This is compounded by severe data scarcity: real underwater stereo capture is expensive and yields sparse or noisy depth supervision, while CG rendering lacks photorealism and introduces a domain gap. No existing pipeline simultaneously addresses both the data gap and the feature-degradation problem in a scalable, geometry-preserving way.

## Input / Output

At inference the method takes a rectified stereo image pair (left and right views at 384×736) and produces a dense disparity map. Internally, the stereo pair is augmented with two learnable perception frames to form a four-frame pseudo-temporal sequence consumed by a video encoder, and DINOv2 features from the raw views are fused in parallel. During training, the data-generation stage additionally consumes per-pixel depth maps (from Depth Anything V2) and sampled text prompts from a physics-inspired pool to condition the diffusion model.

## Method

AquaStereo combines three components. First, a depth-conditioned diffusion pipeline (Stable Diffusion + ControlNet) renders geometry-preserving underwater stereo pairs from terrestrial pairs, guided by a physics-inspired prompt pool (CLIP-retrieved + LLM-curated) and a lightweight patch-level left-right consistency module that scores epipolar patch pairs with disparity-aware similarity to enforce binocular coherence. Second, cross-domain self-distillation uses a frozen IGEV++ teacher on clean pairs to supervise a student on perturbed underwater pairs via a stage-weighted ℓ₁ feature-alignment loss, while a clean-branch supervised term with shared pseudo-disparity targets prevents scale drift. Third, the perception-enhanced matcher appends two learnable perception frames to the stereo pair, encodes the resulting four-frame sequence with a Change3D-style video backbone for cross-view attention, fuses the view-aligned features with DINOv2 semantics via 1×1 projection, and feeds the fused descriptors into IGEV++ iterative multi-range cost-volume refinement with Soft-Argmin regression.

## Results

On the UWStereo benchmark, AquaStereo achieves a Total EPE of 0.590 and D1 of 2.616, ranking first on all four scene splits (Coral, Default, Industry, Ship). The closest competitor, MonSter, records Total EPE 1.195 / D1 5.744, meaning AquaStereo reduces EPE by ~51% and D1 by ~54% relative to the strongest baseline. On real underwater benchmarks (FLSea, Squid, TartanAir), training IGEV++ on the proposed UW-Dataset yields EPE 3.05/3.13/1.80/0.55 across the four splits, outperforming training on SceneFlow, KITTI, UWStereo, and StereoAdapter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AquaStereo outperforms all 12 baselines on every UWStereo split and the overall Total metric. The closest competitor is MonSter (Total EPE 1.195, D1 5.744), which AquaStereo beats by 0.605 EPE and 3.128 D1; FoundationStereo (EPE 1.669, D1 5.768) is second-closest. Classical CNN-based methods (PSMNet, CFNet, GwcNet) and photometric-assumption-heavy methods (NMRF) degrade most severely underwater, with NMRF recording the worst Total D1 of 14.758. On real underwater benchmarks (Table 3), training IGEV++ on the proposed UW-Dataset (FLSea-Canyon EPE 3.05, D1 18.31) surpasses training on StereoAdapter's CG-rendered data (EPE 4.05, D1 20.51) and on UWStereo's Blender-rendered data (EPE 5.01, D1 21.45), confirming that diffusion-based generation closes the domain gap more effectively than CG rendering. StereoBase/OpenStereo and IGEV++ trained on SceneFlow or KITTI alone show substantial degradation underwater (e.g., IGEV++ on SceneFlow: FLSea-Canyon EPE 5.41, D1 35.82), underscoring the necessity of underwater-specific training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

AquaStereo builds directly on IGEV++ as its iterative stereo matching backbone and repurposes Stable Diffusion with ControlNet (originally for single-image depth-conditioned generation) to synthesize geometry-preserving underwater stereo pairs, extending the diffusion-for-dense-prediction line of work from monocular depth to the stereo setting where cross-view consistency is critical. The perception-enhanced encoder creatively repurposes a Change3D-style video encoder to treat a rectified stereo pair as a pseudo-temporal sequence, augmented with learnable perception frames, and fuses the result with DINOv2 semantics—a combination not seen in prior stereo matchers. The self-distillation strategy extends cross-domain knowledge transfer (previously applied to monocular depth and semi-supervised learning) to stereo matching with a frozen teacher on clean pairs guiding a student on perturbed underwater pairs, departing from prior underwater stereo work (StereoAdapter, UWStereo) that relied on CG rendering or limited real captures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck in underwater robotics and marine sensing: the near-absence of large-scale, photorealistic underwater stereo training data with dense metric supervision. The diffusion-based generation pipeline offers a scalable, controllable alternative to expensive real capture and less realistic CG rendering, and the resulting UW-Dataset (40K+ pairs) is a practical resource for the community. Open questions remain: whether the pseudo-disparity supervision from FoundationStereo introduces systematic biases that cap accuracy, whether the multi-stage pipeline (diffusion generation → self-distillation → perception-enhanced matching) can be simplified or accelerated for real-time AUV deployment, and whether the pseudo-temporal perception-frame trick generalizes to other degraded domains (fog, rain, low-light) beyond underwater. The approach also raises the broader question of how much of the underwater domain gap is appearance vs. geometry, and whether stronger physics-guided generation could further close it.
