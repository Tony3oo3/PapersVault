## Problem

Letter casing has been treated as a passive tokenization artifact in LLM research, while attention steering work has focused on complex interventions (weight editing, activation injection, inference-time rescaling) that require white-box model access. No prior study has characterized whether pretrained transformers inherently modulate internal attention in response to typographic surface form, nor whether such a latent property generalizes across modalities, tokenizer families, and model scales.

## Input / Output

For LLMs, the input is a standard text prompt whose target span (identified from benchmark ground-truth labels) is reformatted in a specific case pattern while all other text retains a complementary pattern; for VLMs, the image is never altered and only the textual description prompt undergoes typographic manipulation. The output is a two-part behavioral readout: (1) the mean attention mass allocated to the target span (or corresponding image bounding-box region) aggregated across all layers and heads, reported as a relative shift from the naturally-cased baseline, and (2) downstream task accuracy on the same benchmark. The protocol is strictly zero-access—no fine-tuning, adapter insertion, or runtime introspection—operating entirely through pre-tokenization case assignment.

## Method

The paper defines a taxonomy of five typographic intervention families (Global Uniformity, Target Emphasis, Target Title Case, Target Alternating Case, Adversarial De-Emphasis) that vary the case contrast between a ground-truth target span and its surrounding context, formalized as a character-level case assignment function that preserves lexical identity. Target spans are located via case-insensitive exact matching (98.2%) with a Levenshtein-distance fuzzy fallback (≤2 edits) for the remainder, ensuring the only variable is orthographic case. The intervention is applied identically across 13 frozen models (9 LLMs, 4 VLMs) spanning five families and three tokenizer schemes (BPE, SentencePiece, BBPE) on MMLU-Pro, ARC-Challenge, SQuADv2, and RefCOCOg. The central empirical finding is an attention–performance divergence: high-entropy alternating case produces the largest attention concentration (+2.77 pp) but acts as a destructive attractor that degrades accuracy (up to −13.96 pp), whereas uppercase target emphasis yields moderate attention shifts (+2.06 pp) that translate into reliable accuracy gains (up to +8.95 pp). In VLMs the effect transfers partially along two coupled axes—a dominant macroscopic disengagement from the image toward the text prompt (−1.85 pp whole-image attention) and a secondary microscopic concentration on the target bounding box (+1.55 pp)—and the text-only 'reasoning buffer' that suppresses typographic sensitivity reverses in the visual domain, where reasoning VLMs show the strongest modality disengagement under typographic stress.

## Results

On the discovery model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), Target Alternating (TA) schemes produce the largest attention shifts (+2.77 pp for TA3, +2.75 pp for TA2), while Target Emphasis (TE) via uppercasing yields +2.06 pp (TE3) and +2.05 pp (TE1) in mean attention mass on the target span. Critically, attention and performance diverge: TE1 achieves a +1.85 pp accuracy gain, whereas TA schemes degrade accuracy by up to −2.88 pp. Across all seven non-reasoning LLMs, TE1/TE3 remain the only interventions that reliably improve performance, reaching up to +8.95 pp, while TA causes a peak loss of −13.96 pp (LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct). In VLMs on RefCOCOg, non-standard casing produces a +23.08% relative increase in target-region attention (+1.55 pp absolute) coupled with a 12.41% macroscopic disengagement from the image (−1.85 pp whole-image attention).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external steering methods; instead it benchmarks typographic intervention families against each other and against the natural-casing baseline. At the attention level, the TA family dominates (up to +2.77 pp), followed by TE (+2.06 pp), with U, TT, and ADE all below +0.4 pp—confirming that high-entropy and uppercase patterns are the strongest attractors. At the performance level, the ranking inverts: TE (uppercase target) is the only family that consistently improves accuracy (up to +8.95 pp cross-model), while TA is a destructive attractor that degrades accuracy (peak −13.96 pp). Reasoning models (Qwen3-4B-Thinking, gpt-oss-20B) show near-zero sensitivity in text (< ±0.5 pp), acting as a semantic buffer that filters typographic cues. In VLMs, the family ordering (TA > TE > rest) transfers in aggregate, but the per-model spatial response is fragmented: Gemma-3-4B-IT peaks at +6.23 pp (TA1) while Qwen3-VL models show no clear fine-grained ordering. The macroscopic modality axis (disengagement from image toward text) is sign-consistent across all four VLMs and is amplified by visual reasoning, reversing the text-only reasoning-buffer effect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant attention-steering literature (ROME, PASTA, activation engineering) by requiring no architectural modification, weight editing, or runtime intervention—instead revealing that letter case alone, the minimal orthographic perturbation preserving 100% of semantic content, already functions as a latent attentional attractor in pretrained transformers. It extends the input-level influence line of work (positional bias, sink tokens, XML delimiters) to the most surface-level variable possible and, for the first time, to cross-modal VLM settings where text-side casing reorganizes visual attention. The framing draws an explicit analogy to human typographic salience research, positioning the finding as a previously uncharacterized emergent property of pretraining rather than a prescriptive technique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication is that a zero-shot, black-box lever for attention steering exists in every non-reasoning LLM and partially in VLMs, requiring only prompt-level case manipulation—no fine-tuning, adapters, or model access. The attention-performance divergence (strongest attention shift from alternating case actually degrades accuracy, while simpler uppercasing improves it) challenges the common assumption that concentrating attention on relevant tokens monotonically helps, and the reversal of the reasoning buffer across modalities (reasoning filters typographic cues in text but amplifies text-over-image bias in vision) opens questions about how deliberative computation interacts with surface-form signals. Limitations include coverage only of Latin-script languages, absence of causal mediation analysis to pinpoint which layers/heads mediate the effect, and no automated deployment pipeline; extending to non-Latin scripts and building mechanistic accounts are natural next steps.
