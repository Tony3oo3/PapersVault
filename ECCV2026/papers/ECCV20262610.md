## Problem

Learning-based UDF methods aggregate Euclidean neighborhoods as unstructured point sets, which fails to separate nearby surface layers (folds, double layers) because Euclidean proximity does not reflect surface-relative adjacency. Moreover, scalar distance supervision provides no pairwise guidance on which neighboring points belong to the same local surface, leaving local aggregation weakly constrained and producing fractured reconstructions in multi-layer regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262610/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud (3D coordinates with learned features) and a query point, forming a KNN neighborhood around the query. It outputs a single unsigned distance scalar per query, which is then extracted as a 3D mesh via Marching Cubes at resolutions 128 or 256. The framework is a single cross-scene regressor (not per-scene optimization), and primitive construction is query-conditioned but introduces no scene-specific fitting.

## Method

PrimitiveUDF introduces a two-stage geometric reorganization before regression. First, a spherical reorganization module projects neighborhood points onto a viewing sphere from a virtual center selected along a PCA axis, then applies a tangent-plane log-map to produce a stable 2D chart where surface-relative adjacency is more reliably expressed. Second, a hybrid primitive construction scheme grows triangle primitives from the 2D chart (for patch-like surfaces) and prunes 3D direction-consistent segment candidates (for thin or sparse structures), with non-degeneracy, compactness, locality, and alignment checks removing invalid relations. Selected primitives are encoded as tokens combining pooled vertex features and query-relative center offsets, then aggregated by an attention-based module and regressed to a UDF value via an MLP. The primitives serve purely as a structural bias for organizing local evidence; the actual distance prediction remains fully learned, and the rule-based construction introduces no additional learnable parameters.

## Results

On ScanNet (resolution 256), PrimitiveUDF achieves CD_L1 = 1.86×10⁻³, CD_L2 = 6.03×10⁻⁶, F-Score = 95.7%, and NC = 90.1%, representing a ~10% improvement in CD_L1 and ~20% in CD_L2 over the strongest baseline (GeoUDF). On Matterport3D, it reaches CD_L1 = 2.09×10⁻³, CD_L2 = 7.29×10⁻⁶, F-Score = 94.1%, and NC = 95.5%, outperforming all baselines by 5–12% on Chamfer distance. On the watertight ShapeNet benchmark, it achieves CD_L1 = 0.229×10⁻² and F-Score = 95.0% (0.5% threshold), surpassing GeoUDF (0.234, 93.8%) and GIFS+ (0.281, 91.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PrimitiveUDF outperforms all five baselines (NDF, GIFS, GeoUDF, NVF, SALS) on every reported metric across both ScanNet and Matterport3D at resolution 256. The largest gains are over NVF (CD_L2: 6.03 vs. 11.7 on ScanNet, 7.29 vs. 11.9 on Matterport3D) and SALS (CD_L2: 6.03 vs. 7.49 on ScanNet, 7.29 vs. 15.8 on Matterport3D), reflecting better handling of multi-layer and thin structures. Against the closest competitor GeoUDF, PrimitiveUDF improves CD_L1 by ~9% (1.86 vs. 2.05) and F-Score by 1.9 points (95.7% vs. 93.8%) on ScanNet, and by ~5% on CD_L1 (2.09 vs. 2.21) on Matterport3D. On the ShapeNet Cars open-surface benchmark, PrimitiveUDF achieves the best d_C (0.110×10⁻²) and F-Score (90.69%), beating GeoUDF (0.120, 89.23%) and NDF (0.126, 88.09%). On the ABC and Non-Manifold ABC benchmarks, it matches SALS on CD_L1 (0.251) while surpassing it on F-Score (93.46% vs. 92.66%) and NC (97.8% vs. 97.3%), with the gap widening on non-manifold shapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard learning-based UDF pipeline (GIFS, GeoUDF, NVF, SALS) that aggregates unstructured KNN neighborhoods via permutation-invariant attention, arguing that Euclidean proximity is a poor proxy for surface-relative adjacency in multi-layer regions. Conceptually, it draws on the local-structure-prior line (POCO, ConvONet, Deep Local Shapes, Patch-Grid) and explicit point-cloud structure units (curve-based organization, umbrella primitives, point-edge-surface hierarchies), but repurposes these ideas as a pre-aggregation geometric bias rather than as the implicit representation itself. The spherical reorganization plus hybrid primitive construction is a new architectural module inserted between neighborhood selection and feature aggregation, while the final UDF regression remains a standard attention-based MLP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10% scene-level improvement and consistent cross-scene gains demonstrate that injecting explicit surface-adjacency structure into local neighborhoods is a practical lever for UDF quality, particularly in cluttered indoor scenes with thin or closely spaced surfaces. A notable limitation is that the primitive construction is rule-based and adds per-query computational overhead, and the triangle+segment hybrid offers little benefit over triangles alone on the tested scenes, suggesting the segment branch may be underutilized. Open questions include whether the construction generalizes to extremely sparse or heavily occluded inputs, whether the rule-based primitives could be learned end-to-end, and whether the approach extends to signed distance fields or other implicit representations.
