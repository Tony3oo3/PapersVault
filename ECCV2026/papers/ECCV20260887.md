## Problem

Event data augmentation remains underexplored compared to frame-based methods, and existing approaches (EventDrop, EventMix, ShapeAug) operate on individual event dimensions in isolation, thereby breaking the intrinsic spatiotemporal continuity or introducing ambiguous label assignments. The unique quadruplet structure (x, y, t, p) of event data makes direct spatial masking or stream mixing physically inconsistent, creating a need for a generation-based strategy that preserves all four dimensions simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260887/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an original asynchronous event stream as quadruplets (x, y, t, p), slices it into groups of Δn=4096 consecutive events, and reshapes each slice into a 4-channel 3D tensor of shape n×4×h×w via a dimension-decoupling transformation. The output is a new augmented event stream with controllable diversity—varying levels of motion blur, background noise, and event density—produced by tuning diffusion steps (T∈[50,200]), stochastic noise perturbation (εs=0.1), or condition noise (εc=0.1). 30% of training samples are randomly selected for augmentation, and the generated data is label-independent to avoid unreasonable label assignments.

## Method

N2E introduces a dimension-decoupled event representation that partitions the event stream along all four dimensions into a compact 4-channel tensor, preserving spatiotemporal continuity without event loss. A conditional DDIM decoder (UNet-based) learns to progressively denoise event tensors, guided by condition vectors extracted from the original data via a residual-convolution-plus-attention extractor. The key insight is that pure Gaussian noise is too random for event synthesis, so the method first runs the forward diffusion process on the original events to obtain a sample-specific stochastic noise, then reverses it with modified parameters to generate diverse yet in-distribution samples. Three orthogonal augmentation knobs—reducing diffusion steps (adds background noise), perturbing the sampled noise (blurs object edges), and polluting the condition vector (populates event details)—allow targeted, controllable diversification. Training uses a combined L1+L2 noise-prediction loss over T=1000 diffusion steps with Adam at lr=1e-4.

## Results

On N-Caltech101 (object classification), N2E achieves up to 94.9% top-1 accuracy (Swin + Voxel Grid) and 94.3% (Swin + Time Surface), surpassing the best baseline (DataAug) by 3.5–11.5% across model-representation combinations. On DVS-Gesture (action recognition), N2E reaches 97.6% (RN18 + Voxel Grid) and 93.4% (Swin + Voxel Grid), outperforming all four baselines in nearly every configuration. On MVSEC, N2E achieves ePSNR of 11.97, RMSE of 0.004, and LPIPS of 0.191, dramatically outperforming the best baseline (DataAug: ePSNR 2.16, RMSE 0.37, LPIPS 0.63) in reconstruction fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

N2E consistently outperforms all four baselines across both tasks and nearly all model-representation combinations. DataAug [9] (coordinate translation/rotation) provides moderate gains of ~5–7% over the original but is surpassed by N2E by 3–11% (e.g., 90.6% vs. 83.6% for RN50+Event Frame on N-Caltech101). EventDrop [12] (random event removal) shows inconsistent results—sometimes degrading ViT and VGG performance—and even fails to converge (NaN loss) on Time Surface for DVS-Gesture, while N2E maintains stable improvements everywhere. EventMix [38] (spatiotemporal mask mixing) suffers from ambiguous label assignment, producing drops in some configurations (e.g., ResNet18+Event Frame drops from 73.7% to 76.7% vs. N2E's 83.1%), and ShapeAug [4] (simulated shape injection) delivers only marginal gains (≤1–2%) over the original. On MVSEC reconstruction quality, N2E's ePSNR of 11.97 exceeds the best baseline (DataAug, 2.16) by over 5×, confirming that diffusion-based generation produces far more physically consistent event data than transformation- or mixing-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

N2E builds directly on the DDIM framework (Song et al., ICLR 2021) for the denoising decoder and adapts the forward-diffusion-to-noise estimation technique from Diffusion Autoencoders (Preechakul et al., CVPR 2022) to obtain sample-specific stochastic noise rather than pure Gaussian sampling. Conceptually, it departs from the entire line of event augmentation work (EventDrop, EventMix, ShapeAug, DataAug, EventRPG, EventZoom) that manipulates individual event attributes—spatial coordinates, timestamps, or polarities—in isolation, by instead modeling the joint four-dimensional distribution of events through a dimension-decoupled tensor representation and a conditional generative process. The condition extractor, which steers the diffusion decoder to preserve semantic context while allowing controlled perturbation, is a novel architectural element not present in prior event augmentation or standard diffusion pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that generative diffusion models can produce physically plausible, in-distribution event data that preserves spatiotemporal continuity and optical-flow consistency—properties that transformation- and masking-based methods fundamentally cannot guarantee. The striking fidelity gap on MVSEC (ePSNR 11.97 vs. ~2.1 for all baselines) suggests diffusion-based generation could become a general-purpose tool for event data synthesis beyond augmentation, potentially enabling data simulation for rare scenes or sensor configurations. However, the iterative denoising process makes N2E significantly slower than mask-based baselines, and the paper acknowledges this as a key limitation; whether accelerated sampling (fewer steps, distillation) can close the efficiency gap without sacrificing fidelity remains an open question. The downstream improvements, while consistent across five architectures and four representations, are moderate (2–11%), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse event datasets or benefits from multi-step training schedules.
