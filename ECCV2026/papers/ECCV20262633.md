## Problem

Existing human-robot collaboration benchmarks rely on idealized, unobstructed viewpoints that ignore the severe spatiotemporal incompleteness (low-angle truncation, dynamic torso occlusions, FoV spillover) inherent in real quadruped robot perception. Moreover, end-to-end black-box evaluations cannot distinguish whether a VLM's correct answer stems from genuine compensatory reasoning or from hallucinating via language priors and statistical biases when visual cues are degraded.

## Input / Output

The model receives a truncated egocentric video clip (8 uniformly sampled frames from a 0.4 m jaw camera) paired with a sequence of five progressive text prompts, each constrained to a restricted vocabulary including an 'unknown' option for severe occlusions. The output is a structured JSON chain of five intermediate reasoning states—spatial region, held-object status, target furniture, furniture affordance, and a final intent triplet ⟨Action, Object, Target⟩—enabling step-wise diagnostic scoring rather than a single end-to-end label. Observation windows are deliberately sliced at 30%, 60%, or 90% of the transit phase to control temporal information exposure.

## Method

CoT-HRC is a procedurally generated benchmark built in Habitat 3.0 that simulates a Spot quadruped robot's 0.4 m low-angle egocentric view with realistic kinematic jitter, dynamic torso occlusions, FoV truncation, and proximity blind zones, while a privileged state engine extracts ground-truth physical relationships to auto-generate hierarchical CoT annotations. A four-dimensional difficulty quantification (torso occlusion, FoV truncation, proximity blind zone, view jitter) is discretely integrated over time and projected into Easy/Medium/Hard levels. For evaluation, the paper replaces opaque end-to-end testing with a Step-by-Step Reasoning QA protocol and introduces two diagnostic metrics: SCA_R (Step-wise Consistency Assessment), which requires the affordance prediction to be supported by either correct furniture or correct spatial area, and CRA (Conditional Reasoning Accuracy), which computes intent accuracy conditioned on successful area and object perception, thereby decoupling visual perception failures from pure abductive reasoning capability.

## Results

On the CoT-HRC benchmark, GPT-4o achieves the highest Reasoning Consistency (SCA_R) at 62.9% under the egocentric view, 62.8% under third-person, and 64.2% under the dual-view Oracle setting, with a Conditional Reasoning Accuracy (CRA) of 80.0% (Ego). Among open-source models, InternVL2.5-8B leads with 44.8% SCA_R (Ego) and 59.6% (Dual), while Qwen2-VL-7B reaches 34.1% (Ego) and 41.7% (Dual). A human baseline achieves 83.7% ± 1.7% intent accuracy, revealing a substantial gap of ~20–50 points over the best VLMs. The egocentric view causes the most severe degradation: InternVL drops from 59.6% to 44.8% SCA_R (−14.8 pp) when moving from Dual to Ego, confirming that low-angle truncation and dynamic torso occlusion are the primary bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, CoT-HRC evaluates six VLMs rather than proposing a new method, so the 'comparisons' are cross-model. GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-pro dominate across all viewpoints, with SCA_R in the 62–66% range, while open-source 7–8B models (InternVL2.5, Qwen2-VL, LLaVA variants) trail by 15–40 points. A striking finding is that LLaVA-family models (OneVision, NeXT-Video) perform *worse* under the Dual Oracle view than under Ego (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision: 23.6% Dual vs. 33.4% Ego SCA_R), revealing a spatial domain gap where overhead/top-down views diverge from their human-centric pre-training data. InternVL2.5-8B is the strongest open-source model, achieving 59.6% SCA_R under Dual but dropping to 44.8% under Ego (−14.8 pp), confirming that physical occlusion—not reasoning deficiency—is the primary failure mode. The human baseline of 83.7% intent accuracy remains 20+ points above the best VLM (GPT-4o at 64.2% SCA_R / 52.3% Intent under Dual), highlighting substantial headroom for future embodied reasoning systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of embodied AI benchmarks and VLM evaluation. It departs from prior HRC benchmarks (PARTNR, HARPER, Ego-Exo4D) that assume idealized, unobstructed viewpoints by explicitly simulating the 0.4m quadruped robot egocentric view with dynamic torso occlusions and FoV truncation. It also breaks from the dominant end-to-end black-box evaluation paradigm by introducing hierarchical CoT annotations and diagnostic metrics (SCA_R, CRA) that decouple visual perception failures from cognitive reasoning deficiencies. The simulation infrastructure builds on Habitat 3.0 and the task structure on PARTNR's Rearrange primitive, but the combination of constrained robot-centric perception with step-by-step logical attribution is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first systematic diagnostic tool for understanding *why* VLMs fail in embodied collaboration—distinguishing whether failures stem from visual occlusion or genuine reasoning deficits. The finding that VLMs possess strong latent physical commonsense (CRA 43.8–60.0%) but are bottlenecked by perception reframes the research agenda toward robust visual front-ends rather than better reasoning architectures. Key limitations include reliance on synthetic Habitat 3.0 environments (sim-to-real gap), a single task family (Rearrange), and the temporal aggregation finding being demonstrated primarily on one model. Open questions include whether algorithmic solutions can resolve the cross-view integration and temporal dilution bottlenecks, and whether the diagnostic protocol generalizes to more diverse intent taxonomies beyond spatial object transfers.
