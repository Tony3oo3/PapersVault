## Problem

Existing video diffusion models generate high-fidelity RGB content but lack explicit geometry, while prior 4D methods either specialize in generation or reconstruction and rely on channel-wise or spatial-wise concatenation that causes severe cross-modal interference under low-resource fine-tuning. No single framework unifies 4D generation from a single image, 4D reconstruction from a full video, and mixed sparse-frame settings while preserving both video quality and geometric accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261150/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts one or more RGB frames (single image, sparse subset, or full video at 352×624, up to 81 frames) plus a text prompt, with unobserved frames zero-filled and a binary mask indicating which frames are observed. It jointly outputs synchronized RGB frames and 3-channel pointmaps (XYZ) in a shared global coordinate system; depth maps and camera trajectories (intrinsics, rotation, translation) are then recovered from the pointmaps via a lightweight global optimization (~30 s on one GPU).

## Method

One4D extends a 14B-parameter flow-matching video DiT (Wan2.1-Fun) with two key mechanisms. Decoupled LoRA Control (DLC) attaches separate rank-64 LoRA adapters for RGB and XYZ tokens on the same frozen base weights, keeping forward computation fully decoupled per modality to prevent cross-modal degradation; zero-initialized linear control links at five selected layers then gradually learn pixel-wise consistency between branches without the token-doubling cost of spatial concatenation. Unified Masked Conditioning (UMC) encodes available frames into a conditioning video (zeros for missing frames) concatenated channel-wise with noisy RGB latents plus a binary mask, so a single architecture handles pure generation, mixed, and pure reconstruction by simply varying frame sparsity. The geometry branch receives no direct conditioning—conditioning signals reach it only through DLC control links—keeping pointmap generation focused on 3D structure. Training uses 935.7M trainable parameters on 8 H800 GPUs for 5500 steps on ~34k clips (synthetic + pseudo-labeled real), with task sampling ratios of 0.35/0.30/0.35 for single-image/sparse/full-video.

## Results

On full-video 4D reconstruction, One4D achieves 0.273 Abs Rel and 70.4% δ<1.25 on Sintel, outperforming pointmap-based methods MonST3R (0.335 / 58.5%) and CUT3R (0.311 / 62.0%) while remaining close to the reconstruction-only Geo4D-ref (0.205 / 73.5%). On Bonn, One4D reaches 0.092 Abs Rel and 93.7% δ<1.25. For 4D generation, user studies show One4D is preferred over 4DNeX (78.9% vs 21.0% on consistency), Free4D (83.3% vs 16.7%), and GenXD (94.5% vs 5.5%), and VBench scores confirm superior motion dynamics (55.7% vs 25.6% for 4DNeX) at comparable image-to-video consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Sintel depth, One4D (70.4% δ<1.25) clearly outperforms pointmap-based reconstruction methods MonST3R (58.5%) and CUT3R (62.0%) by 8–12 points, and edges out video depth methods DepthCrafter (69.7%) and Depth-Anything-V2 (55.4%), but trails the reconstruction-only Geo4D-ref (73.5%) by 3.1 points. On Bonn, One4D (93.7%) is competitive with MonST3R (96.4%) and CUT3R (96.7%) but lags behind Geo4D-ref (97.2%) and DepthCrafter (97.2%) by roughly 3–4 points, reflecting the cost of sharing a single model for both generation and reconstruction. For camera trajectory (Table 4), One4D achieves ATE of 0.213 on Sintel and 0.129 on TUM-dynamics, within the same range as CUT3R (0.208 / 0.046) and Geo4D-ref (0.185 / 0.073) but behind MonST3R (0.108 / 0.063). In 4D generation user studies, One4D dominates all three baselines: it is preferred over 4DNeX on 78.9–90.0% of comparisons, over Free4D on 83.3–96.7%, and over GenXD on 93.6–97.3%, with the largest margins on geometry-specific criteria (depth map and 4D coherence). VBench confirms the generation advantage: One4D doubles 4DNeX's dynamic score (55.7% vs 25.6%) while maintaining comparable I2V consistency (97.8% vs 98.7%). Overall, One4D trades a small reconstruction gap versus dedicated reconstruction-only models for the ability to perform both generation and reconstruction in a single unified architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

One4D sits at the intersection of video diffusion models (Wan2.1, HunyuanVideo) and pointmap-based geometry (DUSt3R, MonST3R, Geo4D), adapting a pretrained flow-matching video backbone for joint RGB and XYZ pointmap generation. It departs from prior joint RGB-geometry methods (WVD, 4DNeX) that couple modalities via channel-wise or spatial-wise concatenation, arguing that such dense coupling causes cross-modal interference under low-resource fine-tuning. Instead, it introduces Decoupled LoRA Control—modality-specific LoRA branches sharing frozen base weights with sparse, zero-initialized cross-modal links inspired by ControlNet—and Unified Masked Conditioning, which extends Wan's frame-inpainting mechanism to a single interface spanning pure generation, mixed, and pure reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying 4D generation and reconstruction in a single model trained with only 5,500 steps on 8 GPUs (versus WVD's ~1M steps on 64 A100s), One4D demonstrates that pretrained video priors can be efficiently repurposed for geometry-aware world modeling without the extreme compute budgets previously assumed necessary. The practical impact is a single deployable model that handles single-image-to-4D, sparse-frame interpolation, and full-video reconstruction, with post-optimization yielding camera trajectories and depth maps. Limitations include dependence on the backbone's generalization to unseen motion patterns and difficulty with ambiguous geometry or complex non-rigid deformation; the authors note that stronger physical priors and broader data coverage are needed for robust in-the-wild performance.
