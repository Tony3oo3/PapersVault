## Problem

Mainstream vision foundation models (e.g., DINOv3) rely on large-stride ViT patch embeddings that discard high-frequency spatial details, making them poorly suited for detecting subtle building damage patterns such as roof cracks and edge deformations. Additionally, during bi-temporal feature differencing, minor damage signals are easily overwhelmed by complex backgrounds and illumination artifacts, degrading classification accuracy for intermediate damage levels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262525/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired pre- and post-disaster high-resolution satellite images (<0.8 m/pixel), processed as 512×512 RGB patches from original 1024×1024 tiles. It produces two jointly optimized outputs: a binary building localization mask and a per-pixel four-class damage level map (no damage, minor, major, destroyed), with the final score computed as a weighted combination (0.3×localization F1 + 0.7×classification F1) emphasizing damage classification accuracy.

## Method

CRD-Net fine-tunes a DINOv3 backbone via LoRA and inserts lightweight neck modules to build a four-level feature pyramid (1/4 to 1/32 resolution). The SFAFE module decomposes both CNN (ResNet) and ViT features into frequency sub-bands using learnable Gaussian band-pass filters in the Fourier domain, then adaptively injects high-frequency local details from the CNN branch into the ViT's global semantics via cross-attention (for low/high bands) and gated fusion (for mid-band), with a spatial gating map suppressing background noise. The CRD² decoder leverages Mamba state-space modeling to fuse multi-scale spatio-temporal features, then applies Orthogonal Subspace Decoupling to project fused features into mutually orthogonal change and invariant subspaces (enforced via an L2-normalized cross-correlation penalty), followed by a Change Gating mechanism that uses invariant-feature energy to suppress spurious background changes. Finally, Prototype-Guided Hard-sample Contrastive Learning maintains three momentum-updated class prototypes and applies a semantic-adaptive temperature InfoNCE loss that imposes stronger discrimination penalties for background negatives than for intact-building negatives, explicitly enlarging the feature gap between visually similar damage levels.

## Results

CRD-Net achieves state-of-the-art Joint F1 scores of 81.26% on xBD and 81.88% on EBD, outperforming the second-best method (FlowMamba) by 1.14% and 1.33% respectively. On xBD, CRD-Net records the best damage classification F1 (78.76%) and best per-class F1 across all four damage levels, with the largest margin in minor damage (62.68% vs. 59.30% for FlowMamba, +3.38%). On EBD, CRD-Net leads in overall F1, localization F1 (92.00%), and classification F1 (77.54%), though DAHiTra edges it out on the destroyed class (64.50% vs. 61.58%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRD-Net outperforms all six baselines on Joint F1 and classification F1 on both xBD and EBD. Against the strongest competitor FlowMamba, CRD-Net gains +1.14% Joint F1 on xBD and +1.33% on EBD, driven primarily by superior minor-damage discrimination (+3.38% on xBD, +2.54% on EBD). However, CRD-Net trails FlowMamba by 0.56% in building localization F1 on xBD (87.10 vs. 87.66), where FlowMamba's multi-directional Mamba scanning provides a slight edge. On EBD, DAHiTra surpasses CRD-Net on the destroyed class (64.50% vs. 61.58%), indicating a residual weakness in extreme-destruction scenarios. Compared to earlier Transformer-based methods (DamFormer, DAHiTra), CRD-Net improves Joint F1 by 4.07–4.62 points on xBD, and over CNN-based baselines (Siamese-UNet, ChangeOS) by 5.82–9.10 points. The Mamba-based baselines (ChangeMamba, FlowMamba) are the closest competitors, confirming that the gains come from the SFAFE frequency injection and CRD² decoupling rather than the backbone choice alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRD-Net extends the ChangeMamba decoder architecture by inserting two new components—Orthogonal Subspace Decoupling and Prototype-Guided Contrastive Learning—into its multi-scale spatio-temporal fusion pipeline. On the encoder side, it departs from prior DINOv3-based change detection methods (which typically apply PEFT or lightweight necks) by introducing a frequency-domain feature injection module (SFAFE) that explicitly compensates for the high-frequency detail loss inherent in ViT patch embedding, drawing conceptual inspiration from UGFF-VLM's amplitude/phase decomposition and ChangeViT's spatial feature injection but operating in the frequency domain with learnable Gaussian band-pass filters. The combination of a CNN auxiliary branch, FFT-based sub-band decomposition, and Mamba-based temporal fusion represents a multi-paradigm integration (CNN + ViT + SSM) that prior building damage assessment methods have not attempted in a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation of ViT-based backbones in fine-grained remote sensing tasks: the early spatial resolution loss from large-stride patching degrades detection of subtle damage cues like roof cracks. The frequency-adaptive injection and orthogonal decoupling strategies offer principled, modular solutions that could transfer to other fine-grained change detection or damage assessment settings. However, the method carries the highest parameter count (86.22 M) among compared models due to its dual-encoder design, and it still underperforms DAHiTra on the 'destroyed' category in EBD, suggesting that extreme destruction scenarios remain challenging. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain injection generalizes to other foundation-model backbones or modalities, and whether the orthogonal subspace constraint can be relaxed or replaced by lighter alternatives without sacrificing the decoupling benefit.
