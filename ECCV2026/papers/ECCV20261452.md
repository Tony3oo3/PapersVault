## Problem

Existing Personalized Federated Learning (PFL) methods rely on gradient-based updates that are inherently vulnerable to Non-IID data, causing biased local gradients and conflicting learning directions. While Analytic Learning (AL) with closed-form solutions has been shown to work in standard Federated Learning (AFL), it suffers from a generalization-personalization dilemma in PFL: under high data heterogeneity, the global model becomes worse than purely local models. No prior work has reconciled global generalization and local personalization while preserving gradient-free analytical solutions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-R, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and outputs a class label via a weighted linear combination of two stream predictions. A frozen ViT-MAE-Base backbone extracts CLS-token features, which are then mapped through two distinct random projections (d=1024) followed by Tanh activations to form separate feature spaces for the primary and refinement streams. The final prediction is Ŷ = ΦĜ + λΨP̂_k, where λ∈(0,1] balances generalization against personalization.

## Method

AnaPFL introduces a dual-stream analytic model that replaces all gradient-based optimization with closed-form least-squares solutions. The shared primary stream Ĝ is computed collaboratively: each client solves a local regularized least-squares problem, uploads its auto-correlation matrix and local solution, and the server fuses them via a recursive Moore-Penrose inverse update (Woodbury identity) that is provably order-invariant and equivalent to the centralized solution over the full dataset. The client-specific refinement stream P̂_k then corrects the primary stream's residual on local data using a second, distinct random projection and activation, yielding a per-client personalization term. Theoretical analysis establishes invariance to heterogeneity: the global model depends only on the union of all data, not on how it is partitioned across clients. The entire pipeline requires a single aggregation round per client, achieving over 99% reduction in computation and communication compared to 200-round gradient-based baselines.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with 50 clients, AnaPFL achieves 80.64% average accuracy under severe Non-IID (α=0.1), 66.96% at α=0.5, and 62.63% at α=1.0, outperforming the second-best baseline by 7.75%, 10.18%, and 5.97% respectively. On Tiny-ImageNet (50 clients), it reaches 69.01% (α=0.1) and 51.39% (α=1.0), while on the challenging ImageNet-R it attains 46.23% (α=0.1) and 31.03% (α=1.0). Across all 18 experimental settings (3 datasets × 2 client scales × 3 heterogeneity levels), AnaPFL consistently achieves the best accuracy, with absolute improvements of 1.57%–16.71% over the strongest baseline. In terms of efficiency, AnaPFL requires only a single aggregation round, reducing computation overhead by >99% (100–300 s vs. 45,000–66,000 s) and communication overhead by >99% (200–400 MB vs. 20,000–40,000 MB) compared to gradient-based methods using 200 rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnaPFL outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset, client scale, and heterogeneity setting. Under severe Non-IID (α=0.1, 50 clients), the closest competitors are FedALA (72.89%) and FedSelect (72.46%) on CIFAR-100, which AnaPFL surpasses by 7.75% and 8.18% respectively; on Tiny-ImageNet, FedSelect (62.29%) and FedALA (63.71%) trail AnaPFL (69.01%) by 5.30% and 5.30%. Under low heterogeneity (α=1.0), AFL becomes the strongest baseline (56.66% on CIFAR-100, 46.25% on Tiny-ImageNet, 46.99% on ImageNet-R), yet AnaPFL still leads by 5.97%, 5.14%, and 2.71% respectively. Classic FL methods (FedAvg, FedProx) and their fine-tuning variants show the largest gaps, with AnaPFL exceeding them by 20–37% on CIFAR-100 at α=0.1. FedPCL performs worst overall (e.g., 23.99% on CIFAR-100 α=0.1), while FedDBE remains relatively stable but lags AnaPFL by 10–32% depending on the setting. Notably, AnaPFL achieves these gains with a single aggregation round, whereas all gradient-based baselines require 200 rounds, yielding >99% reductions in both computation and communication overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Analytic Federated Learning (AFL) [11], which introduced closed-form analytic solutions into standard FL, into the Personalized Federated Learning (PFL) setting where AFL fails due to a generalization-personalization dilemma under high data heterogeneity. It departs fundamentally from the dominant gradient-based PFL literature (FedAvg, Ditto, FedALA, FedSelect, etc.) by replacing iterative optimization with a dual-stream least-squares formulation: a shared primary stream for global generalization and a client-specific refinement stream for local personalization. The recursive aggregation mechanism builds on the block-wise recursive Moore-Penrose inverse framework from analytic learning, while the frozen ViT-MAE backbone follows the pretrained-feature paradigm established by AFL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >99% reduction in both computation and communication overhead (single round vs. 200 rounds) makes PFL practically viable in bandwidth- and energy-constrained edge settings, and the invariance-to-heterogeneity guarantee (Theorem 3) provides a theoretical property no gradient-based method can match. However, the frozen-backbone assumption significantly limits scope: the method cannot adapt representations to new domains or tasks, and its gains depend on the quality of the pretrained feature extractor. Open questions include whether the dual-stream design extends to trainable backbones, how it interacts with continual or class-incremental FL settings, and whether the MSE loss assumption holds for fine-grained or multi-label tasks.
