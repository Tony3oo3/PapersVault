## Problem

Generating temporally coherent driving videos along novel trajectories (e.g., lane shifts) remains unsolved: reconstruction-based methods degrade sharply under extrapolation, while frame-level generative methods hallucinate inconsistent 4D dynamics and fail to preserve complex visual factors like illumination changes. A fundamental obstacle is the absence of real-world datasets with synchronized multi-lane observations, forcing models to rely on misaligned pseudo-views that introduce parallax mismatch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262065/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a full source driving video (not isolated frames), per-frame LiDAR point clouds, monocular depth estimates with completion, and a target camera trajectory specifying the novel viewpoint. Geometry priors are constructed by fusing LiDAR-colored and depth-completed reprojected point clouds, then rendered into the target view with depth-aware morphological hole-filling. The output is a temporally coherent novel-view video along the specified trajectory, requiring no bounding-box or other manual annotations.

## Method

GeoV2V builds on the Wan 2.1 DiT video diffusion backbone and introduces two key mechanisms. First, a hierarchical geometry injection strategy conditions the first 15 DiT layers on dense depth-reprojection priors (for coarse structure) and the last 15 layers on sparse LiDAR priors encoded by a dedicated ConvNeXt (for fine geometric correction), each implemented as a lightweight 61M-parameter ControlNet. Second, source-video conditioning concatenates source and target latents along the frame dimension via RoPE to maintain temporal alignment, while per-frame camera extrinsic embeddings inject view-dependent geometric cues. Training freezes the FFN layers and fine-tunes only self-attention and camera embedding modules, with the LiDAR branch trained in a separate second stage to avoid overfitting to dataset-specific LiDAR patterns. The Para4D synthetic dataset provides synchronized multi-lane supervision that is critical for learning stable cross-lane transformations, as ablations show substantial degradation when replaced with misaligned side views.

## Results

On the Para4D dataset (2 m lateral shift), GeoV2V achieves PSNR 23.88, SSIM 0.697, LPIPS 0.051, and FID 25.25, outperforming all six baselines on every metric. On real-world lane-shifting evaluation (Waymo/nuScenes), GeoV2V attains the best FVD of 159.00 at 1 m shift (vs. 212.56 for StreetGS) and competitive FID of 17.53, demonstrating strong zero-shot transfer from synthetic-only training. On trajectory interpolation (Waymo Static), GeoV2V reaches PSNR 25.46 and LPIPS 0.132, surpassing the best reconstruction baseline EmerNeRF (PSNR 24.92, LPIPS 0.245) without requiring any bounding-box annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoV2V outperforms all six baselines across every metric and every shift distance (1 m, 2 m, 4 m) on Para4D. Against the strongest 3DGS baseline StreetGS, GeoV2V gains +0.88 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.181 to 0.051 at 2 m, while also achieving a substantially lower FID (25.25 vs. 37.39), indicating better perceptual realism. Against the best generation-based baseline StreetCrafter, GeoV2V improves PSNR by +6.83 dB (23.88 vs. 17.05) and LPIPS by 0.463 (0.051 vs. 0.514) at 2 m. FreeVS and DiST-4D lag significantly behind on all metrics, with FreeVS achieving only 12.58 dB PSNR at 2 m. On real-world lane-shifting (Table 2), GeoV2V achieves the best FVD (159.00 at 1 m) and competitive FID (17.53), beating StreetCrafter's FID of 18.69 while substantially outperforming it on FVD (159.00 vs. 217.30). Notably, GeoV2V requires no bounding-box annotations, whereas StreetGS, OmniRe, EmerNeRF, and StreetCrafter all depend on them, giving GeoV2V a practical advantage in deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoV2V departs from both reconstruction-based NVS (3DGS, NeRF, EmerNeRF) and reconstruct-then-restore methods (FreeVS, StreetCrafter) by adopting a full video-to-video diffusion paradigm that conditions on the entire source video rather than per-frame colored LiDAR projections. Architecturally, it builds on the Wan 2.1 DiT backbone and borrows the RoPE-concatenation plus camera-extrinsic injection conditioning scheme from ReCamMaster, while adding a hierarchical dual-ControlNet geometry injection (dense depth in early layers, sparse LiDAR in late layers) inspired by AC3D's layer-wise camera-control analysis. The Para4D dataset fills a gap not addressed by prior synthetic driving datasets (SYNTHIA, Virtual KITTI, CARLA-based XLD), which either lack dynamic scenes or do not provide synchronized multi-lane trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental data bottleneck for cross-lane NVS: no real-world driving dataset records synchronized observations from parallel lanes in the same dynamic scene, making supervised lane-shift training infeasible without synthetic data. The demonstration that a video diffusion model can implicitly preserve complex dynamics (blinking turn signals, lens droplets, illumination changes) without explicit physical modeling suggests a broader principle for generative simulation. However, the zero-shot synthetic-to-real transfer is validated only via FID/FVD (not pixel-level metrics), and the authors themselves note the lack of quantitative metrics for complex visual dynamics, leaving a significant evaluation gap for the very phenomena the method claims to preserve.
