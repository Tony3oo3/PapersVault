## Problem

Existing video reconstruction systems face a structural divide: SLAM is causal and incremental, so early errors compound and tracking failures are unrecoverable, while SfM ignores temporal ordering and treats all image pairs identically, making it vulnerable to visual symmetries and unable to exploit the continuity of video. Neither paradigm robustly handles arbitrary, long, uncalibrated videos with extreme motion, textureless surfaces, and degenerate viewpoints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261433/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an unconstrained video (sequence of RGB frames) with optionally unknown camera intrinsics and produces camera poses in SE(3) for adaptively selected keyframes, refined intrinsics, and a sparse 3D point cloud. Keyframes are selected by motion-based triggering (fraction of lost or drifted keypoints) rather than fixed temporal intervals, and the pipeline is non-causal: multiple passes revisit the data (coarse matching, keyframe detection, high-resolution refinement, track propagation) before any geometric commitment is made. Monocular depth maps and focal-length priors are extracted per keyframe as auxiliary inputs to the optimization.

## Method

VidMap builds on the GLOMAP global SfM framework (view graph → rotation averaging → global positioning → bundle adjustment) and introduces two mechanisms new to global SfM. First, video-aware extraction chains dense correspondences (RoMA v2) along the temporal sequence with per-pixel localization covariance, applies multi-flow drift correction by selecting the minimum-covariance path among direct and sequential matches, and labels every track observation with provenance (sequential vs. loop-closure). Second, provenance-dependent robust losses are applied throughout optimization: sequential edges receive Huber loss while loop-closure edges receive Cauchy loss, so incorrect closures from visual aliasing are downweighted rather than corrupting the solution. Additionally, metric monocular depth priors (Depth Anything 3) with per-image scale factors are injected into both global positioning and bundle adjustment, regularizing degenerate configurations (forward motion, low parallax) and anchoring the reconstruction to metric scale without rigid constraints.

## Results

On LaMAR (the primary benchmark), VidMap achieves 88.5% W-AUC (full trajectory) in the uncalibrated setting and 89.7% in the calibrated setting, outperforming the best uncalibrated baseline DA3-Long (76.5%) by 12 points and the best calibrated baseline DROID-W (76.2%) by 13.5 points. On CroCoDL, VidMap reaches 95.5% W-AUC (full, phone) and 80.3% (full, robot) uncalibrated, surpassing the next-best methods (DA3-Long at 88.9% phone, ViPE at 71.0% robot) by 6.6 and 9.3 points respectively. On the shorter, well-conditioned ETH3D-SLAM and EuRoC benchmarks, VidMap still leads all baselines in the uncalibrated setting (e.g., 99.0% AUC@10m on ETH3D vs. 98.0% for DA3-Long) and matches or slightly exceeds the strongest SLAM baselines (DROID-W, DPV-SLAM) in the calibrated setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VidMap outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and both calibration settings. The closest uncalibrated competitor is DA3-Long (end-to-end learned), which VidMap beats by 12 points on LaMAR full-trajectory W-AUC (88.5 vs. 76.5) and by 6.6 points on CroCoDL phone (95.5 vs. 88.9); DA3-Long degrades sharply on out-of-distribution CroCoDL robot sequences (78.3 vs. VidMap's 80.3). Among SLAM baselines, DROID-W and DPV-SLAM are the strongest in the calibrated setting on LaMAR (76.2% and 38.2% full W-AUC respectively), but VidMap exceeds them by 13.5 and 51.5 points; on ETH3D-SLAM calibrated, VidMap (99.1% AUC@10m) edges out DROID-W (98.6%) and DPV-SLAM (98.4%). Global SfM baselines GLOMAP-LG and GLOMAP-RoMA suffer the largest gaps, losing 26–37 points to VidMap on LaMAR uncalibrated full W-AUC, confirming that provenance-agnostic matching and lack of depth priors are critical weaknesses. ViPE and MegaSaM, which incorporate monocular depth but retain causal SLAM architecture, trail VidMap by 12 and 36 points on LaMAR uncalibrated full W-AUC, respectively. On EuRoC (grayscale fisheye, motion blur), the gap narrows: VidMap's calibrated AUC@10m (99.6%) is within 0.1 points of DROID-W and DPV-SLAM (99.5%), as learned depth and calibration priors are less reliable under these conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

VidMap extends the GLOMAP global SfM framework by injecting two mechanisms absent from prior global SfM: provenance-dependent robust losses that distinguish sequential tracking edges from loop-closure edges, and metric monocular depth priors with per-image scale estimation in global positioning. The video-aware extraction pipeline adapts SLAM concepts (motion-based keyframing, sliding-window dense matching, flow chaining) into an offline, non-causal setting using RoMA v2's dense warps and covariance, while the depth prior integration generalizes MP-SfM's incremental approach to the global optimization regime. Conceptually, the paper departs from both SLAM's causal commitment (which locks in errors irreversibly) and SfM's provenance-agnostic matching (which is vulnerable to visual aliasing), positioning temporal ordering as a first-class signal in global reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck: billions of unconstrained videos exist, but no prior system can reliably recover metric poses and calibration from them when motion is extreme, calibration is unknown, and visual symmetries are present. The provenance-aware optimization paradigm—treating sequential and loop-closure observations with different trust levels throughout rotation averaging and global positioning—is a new design principle for global SfM that could transfer to other multi-view reconstruction settings. The authors note that geometric precision still falls short of visual-inertial systems, and long-range drift across increasingly longer sequences remains an open challenge, but the system opens a path toward converting arbitrary video streams into accurate 3D models for spatial computing and generative scene modeling.
