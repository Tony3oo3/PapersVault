## Problem

Camera-controlled video diffusion models for novel view synthesis produce geometrically distorted and multi-view-inconsistent results with limited camera controllability. Prior attempts to fix this by injecting 3D-GS-rendered images at the input level introduce view-dependent color noise that corrupts structural information, causing hallucinated structures and degraded geometry. The paper asks how to ground diffusion features in explicit 3D geometry without relying on noisy rasterized pixel values.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260739/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

GeoNVS takes P sparse reference images (1–9 views) with known camera poses, target camera poses for Q output views, and a 3D Gaussian prior estimated feed-forward from the reference images (via VGGT, MVSplat, DepthSplat, or Pi3, with Gaussians initialized from predicted point clouds via InstantSplat). It outputs Q photorealistic novel-view images forming a geometry-consistent video sequence. The method operates entirely in VAE latent feature space (downsampled by factor n), is trained at 384×384 (SEVA backbone) or 320×576 (CameraCtrl backbone), and supports arbitrary reference-to-novel view splits with a fixed total of 21 views during training.

## Method

The core contribution is the Gaussian Splatting Feature Adapter (GS-Adapter), which modulates internal diffusion features with 3D geometry at every denoising timestep rather than injecting rendered images at the input level. GS-Adapter performs three steps: (1) feed-forward feature lifting that projects 2D reference-view diffusion features onto 3D Gaussians via rendering-weighted averaging, (2) feature refinement that rasterizes geometry-constrained novel-view features, augments them with sinusoidal Gaussian positional encoding (GS-PE), and recovers fine details through a lightweight ResNet supervised by a cosine-similarity loss on input-view features, and (3) adaptive fusion that cross-attends from diffusion features to geometry features and applies a pixel-wise Tanh-gated confidence weight to downweight geometry in unreliable regions. Multi-scale DPT-style aggregation combines encoder features across spatial resolutions before feeding into GS-Adapter, and the entire system is trained with LoRA on a frozen video diffusion model using a combined latent-alignment and feature-consistency loss, enabling plug-and-play compatibility with any feed-forward geometry model without retraining.

## Results

On the small-viewpoint NVS benchmark (9 scenes, 18 settings), GeoNVS achieves an average PSNR of 19.09 dB, outperforming the SEVA baseline (17.15 dB) by 11.3% and the next-best generative+geometry method Difix3D (16.89 dB) by 2.2 dB. On the large-viewpoint set, GeoNVS leads SEVA by 1.5–2.4 dB per dataset split (e.g., 15.47 vs 14.30 on Mip360-R). In terms of geometric fidelity, GeoNVS reduces translation error by up to 2× (0.76 vs 1.62 cm on RE10K) and Chamfer Distance by up to 7× (0.19 vs 1.35 on RE10K) relative to SEVA, while input-level geometry injection methods (Difix3D, SEVA+input-level) suffer catastrophic pose errors exceeding 200 cm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoNVS consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across the 9-scene, 18-setting evaluation. Against the primary generative baseline SEVA, GeoNVS gains +1.94 dB average PSNR (19.09 vs 17.15) on small-viewpoint and +1.5–2.4 dB per split on large-viewpoint, with the largest margin on CO3D (+2.04 dB) and Mip360 (+1.19 dB). Among generative+geometry combined methods, GeoNVS beats Difix3D by +2.2 dB, GenFusion by +3.8 dB, and MVSplat360 by +4.2 dB in average PSNR. On geometric fidelity metrics, the advantage is even more pronounced: input-level injection methods (Difix3D, SEVA+input-level) exhibit catastrophic camera pose errors (T_err > 200 cm, R_err > 50°), while GeoNVS maintains T_err of 16.24 cm and R_err of 2.98° on Mip360—actually *better* than SEVA's 18.76/3.29. Standalone feed-forward geometry methods (MVSplat, DepthSplat, VGGT, Pi3) all underperform GeoNVS by 2–5 dB, confirming that geometry alone is insufficient for photorealistic synthesis in sparse-view settings. GeoNVS also demonstrates plug-and-play compatibility: with Adaptive Fusion, swapping the geometry prior (MVSplat, DepthSplat, VGGT, Pi3) yields consistent 19.0–19.3 dB on DTU without retraining, whereas Naive Fusion degrades below the SEVA baseline when paired with the weaker Pi3 prior (14.83 vs 17.03 dB).

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoNVS sits at the intersection of camera-controlled video diffusion (SEVA, CameraCtrl) and feed-forward 3D Gaussian estimation (VGGT, MVSplat, DepthSplat, Pi3), but departs from prior geometry-guided generative NVS methods (Difix3D, GenFusion, MVSplat360) that inject rendered novel-view images or features at the input level. The key conceptual shift is operating in feature space: GS-Adapter lifts diffusion features into 3D Gaussians, rasterizes geometry-constrained features, and adaptively fuses them back into the denoising network, avoiding the view-dependent color noise that corrupts structural signal in rasterized images. The feature-lifting mechanism draws on feed-forward feature distillation into 3D Gaussians (Dr.Splat, LUDVIG) but adapts it to the timestep-varying nature of diffusion features, which makes per-scene optimization infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design—swapping geometry priors or diffusion backbones without retraining—lowers the barrier to combining the best available geometry model with the best available generative model, which is practically valuable as both fields evolve rapidly. The demonstrated catastrophic failure of input-level geometry injection (translation errors exceeding 200 cm vs. ~16 cm for GeoNVS) provides a clear negative result that should redirect future work toward feature-space conditioning. Remaining limitations include blurry textures from information loss during feature lifting and degraded quality in regions far from input views, suggesting that the current lifting-and-rendering pipeline still loses fine-grained detail that a more expressive 3D feature representation might preserve.
