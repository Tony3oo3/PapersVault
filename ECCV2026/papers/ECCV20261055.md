## Problem

Existing talking-head generation systems either rely on cascaded pipelines (TTS followed by audio-driven video) that compound errors, add latency, and produce audiovisual misalignment, or adopt end-to-end twin-backbone designs that train a dedicated audio branch from scratch and still require bolting on an external LLM for interactive use. No prior method natively integrates a language model to autoregressively co-generate synchronized speech and talking-head video in a single unified framework.

## Input / Output

VoCa takes a text transcript and a single reference image as input and simultaneously produces a continuous speech waveform and a temporally coherent talking-head video. The LLM autoregressively emits discrete audio tokens and hidden states that are shared between the speech decoder (DAC-based RVQGAN) and the video decoder, ensuring both modalities decode from the same autoregressive state. The speech decoder supports a streaming mode that decodes only the tokens within the unified active window for strict parallel synchronization with video generation, and the video is generated in a rolling causal window of 5 chunks (3 latent frames each) at resolutions up to 832×480.

## Method

VoCa couples a 3B Llama-3.2 LLM (initialized from Higgs-Audio-v2 with a Dual-FFN extension) to a Wan-based diffusion transformer via a speech refiner that bridges the semantic gap between audio-optimized LLM features and visual motion. The speech refiner uses 1D convolutional residual blocks to amplify rhythm and stress cues, is supervised by an auxiliary HuBERT distillation loss to inject phonetic-linguistic semantics, and temporally compresses the refined features to match video latent shapes for frame-aligned conditioning. The video decoder employs a unified window partitioning mechanism where LLM representations and video latents are split into strictly time-aligned history and active windows, adapting the bidirectional DiT into a causal architecture with rolling-window denoising (inspired by Rolling Forcing) and localized audio cross-attention that restricts each frame's tokens to attend only to the speech refiner output at the exact corresponding time index. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) alignment pretraining with Flow Matching plus HuBERT distillation on the frozen LLM, (2) joint finetuning of LLM and video decoder with cross-entropy audio loss and causal chunked attention, and (3) Distribution Matching Distillation to compress the multi-step denoiser into a few-step causal generator matching the LLM's autoregressive rate.

## Results

On HDTF, VoCa (Wan2.2-5B) achieves the best scores across all four metrics: IDC 0.982, Sync-C 8.054, FID 33.02, and FVD 36.93, surpassing the strongest diffusion baselines (Hallo3, EchoMimicV3, HunyuanAvatar). On CelebV-HQ, VoCa leads in Sync-C (4.769), FID (44.48), and FVD (91.17), while on RAVDESS it attains the best IDC (0.970), Sync-C (5.636), and FID (13.16). In a user study with 40 participants, VoCa receives the highest naturalness rating of 4.46/5, outperforming EchoMimicV3 (4.02), Hallo3 (3.64), and FantasyTalking (3.45).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VoCa (5B) achieves the best or near-best scores on nearly all metrics across three benchmarks. On HDTF, it leads every metric, with a notable Sync-C margin of +0.47 over the next-best V-Express (8.054 vs. 7.744) and FVD advantage of ~6 points over Hallo (36.93 vs. 37.06). On CelebV-HQ, VoCa wins Sync-C (4.769 vs. 4.494 for HunyuanAvatar), FID (44.48 vs. 44.75 for Hallo3), and FVD (91.17 vs. 91.41 for Hallo3), though AniPortrait edges it on IDC (0.938 vs. 0.936). On RAVDESS, VoCa dominates IDC (0.970), Sync-C (5.636 vs. 5.214 for HunyuanAvatar), and FID (13.16 vs. 15.09 for EchoMimicV3), but EchoMimicV3 retains a slight FVD advantage (44.36 vs. 48.71). The 1.3B variant (VoCa-1.3B) also outperforms all diffusion baselines on HDTF FID and FVD, demonstrating that the autoregressive design scales well. In the user study, VoCa's 4.46/5 naturalness score clearly exceeds EchoMimicV3 (4.02), Hallo3 (3.64), and FantasyTalking (3.45), confirming perceptual superiority in co-speech motion quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

VoCa sits at the intersection of three research threads: audio-driven talking-head generation (Hallo, EchoMimic, FantasyTalking), joint audio-video diffusion (OmniTalker, Ovi, UniVerse-1), and autoregressive video generation (Rolling Forcing, Rolling Diffusion). It departs from cascaded TTS-then-animation pipelines and from twin-backbone end-to-end models that train a dedicated audio branch from scratch, instead repurposing a frozen LLM (Higgs-Audio-v2 / Llama-3.2) as the shared autoregressive backbone that simultaneously drives a speech decoder and a causal diffusion transformer. The speech refiner with HuBERT distillation and the unified rolling-window partitioning mechanism are the key architectural departures, adapting a bidirectional DiT into a causal, LLM-synchronized generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By natively integrating an LLM, VoCa inherits capabilities like voice cloning and interactive dialogue without bolting on external models, making it a practical foundation for real-time, streaming talking-avatar applications. The demonstration that a 1.3B autoregressive model can match or exceed 14B diffusion baselines on identity and fidelity metrics suggests the autoregressive paradigm is competitive for this task. Open questions include scalability to multi-speaker scenes, very long-form generation beyond the 2–7 s training clips, and whether the three-stage training recipe (alignment pretraining → joint finetuning → DMD distillation) generalizes to other modalities or higher resolutions.
