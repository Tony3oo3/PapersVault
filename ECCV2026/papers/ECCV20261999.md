## Problem

Multi-expert models for long-tailed recognition are widely assumed to benefit from expert diversity, yet this paper empirically demonstrates that diversity induced by logit adjustment or explicit regularizers shows no positive correlation with ensemble accuracy. The field has been pursuing diversity maximization when the bias-variance decomposition reveals that variance reduction, not diversity, is the dominant factor driving ensemble gains in long-tailed settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261999/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image per training sample but processes it through multiple derived views: two strongly augmented full-resolution views, their convex interpolation (creating high-frequency artifacts), and a separately downsampled low-resolution view (16×16 for CIFAR-LT, 96×96 for ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist). At inference, only the EMA target network is retained and produces standard class-label probabilities from the full-resolution input; the online network and all auxiliary views are discarded.

## Method

VICAL reduces prediction variance through two frequency-decoupled consistency objectives. Self-Consistency (SC) Learning feeds an interpolated view (a Beta-distributed convex combination of two strong augmentations) into the online student and aligns its prediction via KL divergence with the mean EMA-teacher logits of the original views, thereby penalizing reliance on unstable high-frequency patterns and smoothing the local loss landscape for tail classes. Deep Ensemble Distillation (DED) aligns each expert's low-resolution-view logits with the cross-expert mean logits, confining inter-expert agreement to low-frequency semantics and avoiding optimization conflicts with SC. A Conflicting Knowledge Filter further gates DED by excluding samples where the student is correct but the ensemble teacher is wrong, preserving individual expert knowledge. The total loss sums per-expert cross-entropy, SC distillation, and DED distillation with separate weighting hyperparameters, and the framework is trained with M=3 experts using standard SGD with cosine or step LR schedules.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT (IF=100), VICAL achieves 59.7% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-32, outperforming the previous best ICL (57.6%) by 2.1 points. On CIFAR-10-LT (IF=10), VICAL reaches 94.5%, surpassing ICL and BalPoE (both 91.9%) by 2.6 points. On ImageNet-LT (IF=256) with ResNeXt-50, VICAL attains 62.9%, exceeding ECL (61.7%) and BalPoE (61.6%). On iNaturalist 2018 (200 epochs, ResNet-50), VICAL achieves 77.5% overall accuracy, improving over ICL (75.9%) by 1.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VICAL consistently outperforms all 18 baselines across every dataset and imbalance factor tested. On CIFAR-100-LT (IF=100), VICAL (59.7%) leads ICL (57.6%) by 2.1 points, ECL (56.3%) by 3.4 points, and MDCS (56.1%) by 3.6 points. On the most imbalanced CIFAR-10-LT (IF=10), VICAL (94.5%) surpasses ICL and BalPoE (both 91.9%) by 2.6 points and ProCo (91.9%) by 2.6 points. On ImageNet-LT, VICAL (62.9%) exceeds ECL (61.7%) by 1.2 points and BalPoE (61.6%) by 1.3 points. On iNaturalist 2018 (200 epochs), VICAL (77.5% overall) beats ICL (75.9%) by 1.6 points and NCL++ (75.2%) by 2.3 points, with the largest gap in the medium-shot group (78.1% vs. 75.9%). The gains are most pronounced under high imbalance (IF=10), suggesting VICAL's variance-reduction mechanism is particularly effective for tail classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

VICAL builds on the multi-expert paradigm for long-tailed recognition (RIDE, SADE, MDCS, BalPoE, NCL++) and the self-distillation / Mean Teacher framework, but deliberately departs from the field's dominant assumption that enforcing expert diversity via logit adjustment or explicit regularizers improves ensemble accuracy. Instead, it reframes the problem as variance reduction through frequency-decoupled consistency: Self-Consistency Learning modifies vanilla EMA self-distillation by aligning predictions on interpolated (vicinal) views to smooth the local loss landscape, while Deep Ensemble Distillation restricts cross-expert alignment to a low-resolution view to avoid optimization conflicts with the SC objective. The Conflicting Knowledge Filter further refines ensemble distillation by excluding samples where the student is correct but the ensemble consensus is wrong, a mechanism absent from prior multi-expert distillation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By empirically showing that induced expert diversity (via varying logit adjustment intensities or explicit regularizers) has near-zero correlation with ensemble accuracy, the paper challenges a foundational design principle in multi-expert long-tailed learning and redirects attention toward variance reduction as the operative mechanism. The frequency-decoupled architecture (high-frequency smoothing within experts, low-frequency alignment across experts) offers a clean, interpretable design principle that could inform other ensemble or multi-model settings beyond long-tailed recognition. Open questions include whether the diversity-accuracy decoupling holds under different expert counts, architectures, or imbalance ratios, and whether the specific 16×16 / 96×96 low-resolution threshold is optimal or dataset-dependent.
