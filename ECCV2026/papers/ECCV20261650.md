## Problem

Vision-language models remain unreliable at dynamic 4D spatial grounding (direction, speed, trajectory reasoning) on video, and existing 4D Scene Graph approaches suffer from two interface gaps: identity facets (appearance, geometry, motion) are serialized as language-side descriptors rather than routed through dedicated modality channels, and dynamic 4D attributes are rarely used as structured training-time context during fine-tuning. The paper asks whether 4DSGs can serve not only as inference-time prompts but as trainable supervision that teaches a VLM to exploit trajectory and velocity tokens for motion reasoning.

## Input / Output

ROSE consumes monocular RGB video (no multi-view, no LiDAR, no ground-truth depth) plus a natural-language spatial query, and produces a serialized 4D scene graph in world-frame metric units (meters, seconds, no per-scene normalization) alongside a text answer. The 4DSG is bounded by fixed budgets (N_kf=32 keyframes, K=32 objects) to fit within the VLM context window, and each object node carries one anchor crop image plus structured text tokens for 3D extent and per-timestamp centroid trajectories. The full pipeline (depth, tracking, lifting, encoding) runs at 10 Hz on a single H200 GPU; VLM inference latency is reported separately (~12.5 s prefill with 4DSG at the 32-object operating point).

## Method

The core contribution is VISTA, a modality-aligned node encoding that splits each tracked object into three channels: an anchor crop (padded bounding-box image fed as native tokens to the VLM's own vision encoder for appearance), a 3D extent vector (per-axis median of back-projected point-cloud bounds for geometry), and explicit [x,y,z,t] centroid trajectory tokens for motion. ROSE constructs these nodes in real time via two concurrent streams—DA3 for temporally consistent monocular depth and camera pose, and a two-pass FastSAM+SAM3 schedule for open-world discovery and persistent tracking—followed by per-mask back-projection into a shared world frame. The key training insight is that fine-tuning a VLM on examples augmented with serialized 4DSG context (rather than only appending it at inference) teaches the model to map trajectory and velocity tokens to dynamic spatial reasoning, yielding a +5.2 point gain over training-free prompting and a new DSR-Bench SOTA of 67.5. Real-time single-GPU execution is achieved through strided SAM3 propagation with optical-flow mask interpolation, FlashAttention-3 with FP8 on long-sequence modules, shape-keyed CUDA-graph replay of the memory-attention encoder, and cross-stage stream overlap between depth/pose and tracking.

## Results

On DSR-Bench, ROSE (Fine-tuning) achieves 67.5% average accuracy across all 13 subtasks, establishing a new state of the art with +8.6 over the previous best (DSR, 58.9%) and +5.2 over ROSE in its training-free mode (62.3%). On VLM4D, ROSE reaches 70.1% overall, exceeding all raw-video VLMs (best: GPT-5 at 72.3%) and trailing only SNOW (73.8%), while winning on Direction (79.8 vs. 71.8) and False-Positive detection (91.9 vs. 77.9). On RoboSpatial-Home, ROSE attains 70.6% weighted average (second-best) and the best Context score (70.5, +15.6 over SNOW's 54.9). All results are produced from monocular RGB video on a single H200 GPU at 10 Hz construction rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the previous state-of-the-art DSR [57] on DSR-Bench, ROSE (Fine-tuning) wins by a large margin: 67.5% vs. 58.9% average (+8.6), with the largest per-subtask gains on Absolute Direction (73.8→83.7, +9.9) and Relative Direction (76.1→84.1, +8.0); even the training-free variant improves DSR by +3.4. Against SNOW [36] (the closest 4DSG competitor), ROSE loses on VLM4D overall (70.1 vs. 73.8) and RoboSpatial-Home average (70.6 vs. 72.3), but decisively wins on VLM4D Direction (79.8 vs. 71.8) and False-Positive (91.9 vs. 77.9), and on RoboSpatial-Home Context (70.5 vs. 54.9, +15.6). Against frontier raw-video VLMs, ROSE dominates on DSR-Bench: GPT-4o (26.4%), GPT-5 (30.8%), and Gemini-2.5-Pro (31.7%) all fall 30+ points below ROSE (67.5%), and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (23.5%) and VG-LLM (38.4%) are similarly far behind. On VLM4D, ROSE (70.1%) surpasses all raw-video models except GPT-5 (72.3%), beating Qwen2.5-VL-72B by 17.1 points (70.1 vs. 53.0). The token-matched LenCtrl control (59.5 vs. DSR's 58.9) confirms that the gains are not attributable to prompt length but to the structured 4DSG content itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROSE extends the 4D Scene Graph (4DSG) line initiated by SNOW [36] and DAAAM, which serialize scene structure as text/token prompts for inference-time VLM conditioning. It departs from this lineage along two axes: (1) a representation shift via VISTA, which routes appearance through the VLM's native vision encoder (anchor crops) and geometry/motion through explicit structured numeric tokens, rather than folding all identity facets into language-side descriptors; and (2) a usage shift, demonstrating that 4DSGs are more effective as structured training-time context during fine-tuning than as inference-time prompts alone. The pipeline itself composes existing components—DA3 for video-consistent monocular depth/pose, FastSAM+SAM3 for open-world persistent tracking, and Qwen2.5-VL as the reasoning backbone—into a real-time single-GPU system, with the relational edge design explicitly following SNOW's object-level token-sequence interface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 4DSGs as training-time context yield a +5.2 point gain over inference-time prompting alone (67.5 vs. 62.3 on DSR-Bench) reframes how structured spatio-temporal supervision should be integrated into VLM training, with implications beyond this specific benchmark. Real-time (10 Hz) monocular construction on a single GPU makes the approach deployable in embodied and robotic settings where multi-view or LiDAR sensors are unavailable. Key open questions remain: monocular geometry is less scale-accurate than LiDAR for fine-grained outdoor metric reasoning, part-level queries (e.g., counting hand contacts) exceed the object-level representation, and heavy clutter/occlusion still causes the segmentation front-end to drop objects. Extending to fully online frame-by-frame updates and lightweight VLM integration would broaden single-device deployment.
