## Problem

Assessing language-image alignment in diffusion models currently requires completing the full denoising trajectory, because intermediate noise-corrupted latents are too ambiguous for standard vision-language encoders to interpret. This late-stage evaluation is especially wasteful in Best-of-N sampling, where all misaligned candidates must finish generation before being discarded, multiplying compute costs. No existing method provides a reliable, in-process alignment signal that can terminate unpromising trajectories early.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 4-channel SDXL latent representations at intermediate denoising steps (e.g., steps 20–49 out of 50) alongside a text prompt. Latents are projected into an RGB-like space via a learned linear transformation (or the model's VAE for DiT architectures) before being fed to the fine-tuned vision encoder. The output is a scalar cosine similarity score between the frozen text embedding and the noise-aware latent embedding, usable as a continuous ranking signal across any subset of denoising steps.

## Method

NoisyCLIP fine-tunes only the vision encoder of a pre-trained CLIP twin-tower model using InfoNCE contrastive loss on noise-corrupted latents, while keeping the text encoder frozen and shared with the diffusion model to preserve conditioning-space consistency. Training uses a fixed latent interval (steps 20–29) selected through systematic ablation, where each noisy latent z_t forms a positive pair with its prompt and is contrasted against in-batch negatives. The key insight is that exposure to structured noise during contrastive training teaches the vision encoder to extract semantic signal from ambiguous intermediate representations, enabling alignment scores that track final-image quality from as early as latent step 20. The authors further introduce two benchmark datasets (Noisy-Conceptual-Captions and Noisy-GenAI-Bench) containing full latent sequences to support reproducible evaluation, and demonstrate that the resulting early-stopping criterion cuts Best-of-N compute by 50% while retaining 98% of full-cost CLIP performance.

## Results

On the Noisy-Conceptual-Captions dataset, NoisyCLIP achieves a Recall@1 of 0.506 for factual caption selection at intermediate latents (iterations 21–30), a 3.5× improvement over frozen CLIP (0.145). On the Best-of-N task (Noisy-GenAI-Bench), NoisyCLIP reaches a VQAScore of 0.709 at latent stage, reaching 95% of the final-image CLIP score (0.747) while requiring only half the denoising steps. In the Best-of-6 early-stopping experiment, NoisyCLIP at 150 diffusion steps (50% cost) incurs only a 2% VQAScore drop versus the full 300-step CLIP baseline, while outperforming all baselines by ~2% at the same reduced budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against frozen CLIP (L14), NoisyCLIP dominates at intermediate latents: it achieves 0.506 R@1 versus CLIP's 0.145 (3.5×) and 0.709 VQAScore versus 0.657 (+5%) on the Best-of-N task, while CLIP's separation between best and worst images remains under 1% until the mid-30s. Against SigLIP (L16), the NoisySigLIP variant slightly edges out NoisyCLIP on BoN VQAScore (0.718 vs. 0.709) but trails substantially on factual consistency (0.194 vs. 0.506), which the authors attribute to SigLIP's sigmoid loss lacking the discriminative sharpness of CLIP's softmax for subtle textual perturbations. Against LatentCLIP (both 8 and 4-plus variants), NoisyCLIP matches performance at full 300-step cost but pulls ahead by ~2% VQAScore when the budget is cut to 150 steps, where LatentCLIP's advantage from clean-latent training disappears. In zero-shot generalization to DiT architectures (SD 3.5 and FLUX.1), NoisyCLIP delivers +2% and +1.8% alignment gains over frozen CLIP respectively, with a 57% reduction in denoising steps, confirming that the noise-aware contrastive training transfers across generative backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP twin-tower paradigm by fine-tuning the image encoder on noise-corrupted intermediate diffusion latents, enabling alignment scoring before generation completes. It departs from prior latent-space alignment efforts (LatentCLIP, dynamic CFG) that either require full VAE decoding or treat alignment as a byproduct of guidance scheduling, by instead training a dedicated noise-aware encoder via contrastive learning (InfoNCE) on a fixed latent interval. The conceptual lineage traces from CLIP's frozen-text-encoder design, which the authors preserve to maintain consistency with the diffusion model's conditioning space, while the training data strategy (systematic noise-level sampling) is novel and motivated by the absence of public noisy-latent datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By converting alignment assessment from a post-hoc check into a continuous in-process monitor, NoisyCLIP enables 50% compute reduction in Best-of-N sampling while retaining ~98% of full-cost CLIP performance, making test-time scaling substantially more practical. The two introduced benchmarks (Noisy-Conceptual-Captions, Noisy-GenAI-Bench) fill a clear gap in standardized evaluation of intermediate latent representations. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video diffusion or very short-step schedulers (e.g., 4-step distillation), whether the frozen text encoder constraint limits expressiveness for complex compositional prompts, and whether the moderate-noise training sweet spot (latents 20–29) transfers across architectures with different total step counts.
