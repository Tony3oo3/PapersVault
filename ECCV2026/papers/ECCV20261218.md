## Problem

Existing MLLM unlearning methods can erase targeted sensitive content but cause severe collateral damage to the model's foundational image understanding capabilities, failing to achieve what the paper terms 'benign memory forgetting'—precise removal of privacy-sensitive knowledge while rigorously preserving unrelated visual and textual abilities. No prior benchmark jointly evaluates both erasure efficacy and preservation of general image understanding, and no existing method satisfies this dual requirement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261218/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The unlearning pipeline takes a fine-tuned MLLM (LLaVA-OneVision-7B or Qwen2.5-VL-7B), a forget set of sensitive image-text pairs (synthetic facial profiles and ophthalmic medical images with templated Q&A), and a few-shot retain set of the same size as the forget set. The output is a modified model that produces refusal responses (e.g., 'I don't know') for sensitive queries while maintaining coherent text responses for general image understanding and retained knowledge queries. Evaluation uses paraphrased, natural-language queries distinct from training templates to test knowledge-level forgetting rather than surface memorization.

## Method

SMFA operates in two stages. First, refusal-label fine-tuning on the forget set (replacing answers with 'I don't know' plus a few-shot retain subset) yields a Memory Forgetting Adapter (MFA), defined as the parameter update ΔW_f relative to the base model. Second, a retaining anchor is trained on the few-shot retain set to produce a reference update ΔW_a encoding desirable retention directions. A two-criterion mask is then applied to ΔW_f: entries are removed only if they (a) point in the opposite direction to ΔW_a (directional conflict) AND (b) exceed a relative magnitude threshold kρ|ΔW_a,ij|, where ρ normalizes by the Frobenius norm ratio of the two updates. The sculpted adapter ΔW_f ⊙ (1−M) is merged into the base model, suppressing over-generalized forgetting while preserving beneficial updates, requiring only a small retain set and a single hyperparameter k.

## Results

On S-MLLMUn Bench with LLaVA-OneVision at 5% forget ratio, SMFA achieves the best Overall Image Understanding (ROUGE-L 0.667, Fact Score 7.17) and the best Overall Memory (ROUGE-L 0.199, Fact Score 2.30), while also attaining the highest Meaningful Score (7.98). At 10% forget ratio, SMFA maintains Overall Image Understanding at ROUGE-L 0.625 / Fact Score 6.60 and Overall Memory at ROUGE-L 0.158 / Fact Score 1.71, substantially outperforming all baselines. GA Difference, KL Minimization, and TIES-Merging suffer catastrophic forgetting with Overall Image Understanding ROUGE-L below 0.15 and Meaningful Scores below 2.7, rendering them non-competitive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMFA consistently outperforms all six baselines on both Overall Image Understanding and Overall Memory across every model and forget-ratio setting. Against the closest competitor IDK Tuning, SMFA improves Overall Image Understanding by 0.070 ROUGE-L (0.667 vs. 0.597) and Overall Memory by 0.078 ROUGE-L (0.199 vs. 0.121) at 5% forget ratio on LLaVA-OneVision, while also achieving a higher Meaningful Score (7.98 vs. 7.89). MANU and Model Tailor preserve image understanding reasonably well but fail at selective forgetting, yielding Overall Memory ROUGE-L of only 0.006 and 0.026 respectively at 5%, indicating they erase knowledge indiscriminately rather than selectively. GA Difference, KL Minimization, and TIES-Merging suffer catastrophic forgetting, with Overall Image Understanding ROUGE-L collapsing to 0.015–0.145 and Meaningful Scores dropping to 0.76–6.58, making them non-viable for benign unlearning. On Qwen2.5-VL at 10% forget ratio, SMFA achieves Overall Memory ROUGE-L of 0.183 versus 0.078 for IDK Tuning and 0.007 for MANU, confirming the advantage generalizes across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

The SMFA framework directly builds on IDK Tuning (TOFU) for its refusal-label fine-tuning mechanism, which generates the Memory Forgetting Adapter, and draws conceptual inspiration from model merging (TIES-Merging, Task Arithmetic) for its weight-space masking strategy. However, it departs from both lineages in important ways: unlike LLM unlearning methods that target text-only knowledge, SMFA addresses the multimodal over-generalization problem where forgetting propagates to visual understanding; and unlike model merging, which handles non-conflicting task objectives, SMFA must resolve the inherently adversarial tension between forgetting and retention. The paper also positions itself against MLLM-specific unlearning (MANU, SIU) and prior multimodal benchmarks (CLEAR, MLLMU-Bench), arguing that all existing work evaluates forgetting efficacy in isolation while ignoring preservation of foundational image understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic demonstration that every evaluated baseline — including MLLM-specific methods like MANU — fails to preserve general image understanding while erasing targeted knowledge is a useful empirical warning for the field, suggesting that current unlearning approaches are not deployable in privacy-sensitive settings without collateral damage. S-MLLMUn Bench, as the first benchmark with a dual memory-plus-understanding structure, provides a concrete evaluation protocol that future MLLM unlearning work will likely need to address. Limitations include reliance on 1,000 synthetic profiles, evaluation on only two 7B models, and a retaining anchor derived from a small few-shot subset, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models, more diverse retain distributions, or real-world (non-synthetic) privacy data.
