## Problem

Single image reflection removal in real scenes is difficult because reflection strength varies spatially and reflection patterns are tightly entangled with transmission structures, causing existing diffusion-based methods to suffer from incomplete suppression, color inconsistency, and loss of fine details. The core gap is the absence of explicit, spatially adaptive guidance that tells the generative model where reflection removal should be aggressive and where structural fidelity must be preserved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261740/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB mixed image (reflection + transmission superposition) at up to 1K resolution and produces a reflection-free transmission image. A frozen VLM is queried at inference time to score patch-level reflection severity and localize reflection-dominant bounding boxes, adding ~3.4 s of prior-extraction overhead before the 0.63 s diffusion restoration pass. The high-frequency prior is computed on-the-fly via multi-scale dilated-convolution residuals, so no additional model inference is needed for that branch.

## Method

FUMO is a coarse-to-fine prior-modulated diffusion framework built on Stable Diffusion V2.1 with a ControlNet conditioning branch. Two explicit priors are extracted from the mixed image: a VLM-derived intensity prior (ordinal patch scoring à la Q-ALIGN plus image-level bounding-box localization, densified by an edge-aware guided filter) and a high-frequency prior from multi-scale residual decomposition. These priors form a spatial gate g = 1 + β·P_int ⊙ P_hf that multiplicatively modulates ControlNet's multi-scale residual injections, concentrating conditional signal in regions that are both reflection-severe and structure-sensitive. The coarse stage performs one-step latent denoising (regressing z_t from maximally noisy z_N), while a lightweight U-Net refinement module with SimpleGate activations operates in image space on the concatenation of the mixture, coarse prediction, and both priors to correct geometric drift and sharpen details. Training is staged: the coarse stage optimizes ControlNet and U-Net upsampling blocks with a one-step latent regression loss, then the refinement module is trained with a composite L1 + LPIPS + Sobel-gradient objective.

## Results

On the average of three benchmarks (540 images), FUMO achieves 27.15 dB PSNR, 0.918 SSIM, 0.069 LPIPS, 0.424 CLIPIQA, and 59.88 MUSIQ. It attains the best LPIPS, SSIM, CLIPIQA, and MUSIQ scores across all three datasets, while trailing DAI by 0.09 dB on average PSNR (27.15 vs. 27.24). On the Nature and Real benchmarks, FUMO also leads in PSNR (26.93 and 25.95 dB respectively), whereas on the larger SIR2 set DAI edges ahead by 0.13 dB (27.35 vs. 27.22). The largest margins over the next-best method appear on perceptual metrics: LPIPS improves by 0.014 over RDNet and MUSIQ by 1.17 points over RDNet on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUMO is compared against seven single-image reflection removal methods: IBCLN, Dong et al., YTMT, DSRNet, Zhu et al., RDNet, and DAI. Against the strongest baseline DAI (a diffusion-based method), FUMO wins on LPIPS (0.069 vs. 0.100), SSIM (0.918 vs. 0.916), CLIPIQA (0.424 vs. 0.367), and MUSIQ (59.88 vs. 54.57) on average, but trails by 0.09 dB in PSNR (27.15 vs. 27.24); on the smaller Nature and Real sets FUMO also leads in PSNR, while on the 500-image SIR² set DAI is 0.13 dB ahead. Against RDNet, the previous best on PSNR among non-diffusion methods, FUMO improves PSNR by 0.79 dB, LPIPS by 0.014, and MUSIQ by 1.17 points on average. Against DSRNet, FUMO gains 1.76 dB PSNR and 0.029 LPIPS on average. The older baselines (IBCLN, Dong et al., YTMT) are consistently outperformed by 2–6 dB in PSNR and 0.03–0.06 in LPIPS. The one metric where FUMO does not lead is CLIPIQA on the Real benchmark, where RDNet scores 0.538 versus FUMO's 0.509, though FUMO still ranks second.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUMO extends the diffusion-based SIRR line initiated by L-DiffER and DAI by replacing their generic conditioning with explicit, spatially adaptive priors extracted from the mixed image itself. The gated modulation mechanism is built directly on ControlNet's multi-scale residual injection architecture, while the one-step denoising formulation follows DAI's training protocol. The VLM-based intensity prior draws on Q-Align's ordinal scoring paradigm, and the refinement module borrows NAFNet's SimpleGate activation, positioning the work as a synthesis of recent diffusion, VLM, and restoration techniques into a unified coarse-to-fine pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit spatial priors—particularly the multiplicative interaction between reflection severity and high-frequency structure—can meaningfully improve perceptual quality in SIRR without sacrificing fidelity metrics, offering a practical recipe for conditioning diffusion restorers on scene-specific cues. However, the VLM-based prior extraction introduces non-trivial inference overhead (3.40 s at 1K), and the authors acknowledge ambiguity when reflection and transmission share similar intensity and structure, pointing toward user-provided spatial guidance or lightweight learned predictors as future directions. The gated modulation design is general enough to transfer to other restoration tasks where spatially varying degradation severity and structural sensitivity coexist.
