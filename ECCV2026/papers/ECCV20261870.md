## Problem

In federated parameter-efficient fine-tuning, personalization via gated residual mixing between shared and private LoRA branches is inherently scale non-identifiable: only the product of residual magnitude and mixing weight determines the functional output. Under heterogeneous local optimization, private residual scales drift across clients and communication rounds, rendering gate values an unreliable and non-comparable measure of personalization strength, which undermines interpretability, sparsity-based budget control, and optimization stability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images (ImageNet-normalized) and outputs a class label via a shared (or optionally private) classifier head on top of a frozen ImageNet-21k pre-trained ViT-Tiny backbone. In the federated protocol, K=100 clients each hold a non-IID subset (Dirichlet label skew, domain partition, or combined), and only the shared LoRA branch parameters are uploaded per round; private adapters, gate logits, and backbone-derived alignment statistics remain strictly on-device, so communication cost is limited to O(Ldr) shared parameters.

## Method

FedSDG inserts dual-path LoRA adapters (shared and private, same rank r) at the attention output and FFN output projections of every Transformer block, with a learnable scalar gate per injection site modulating the private residual. The key contribution is Dynamic Alignment (DA): before gated mixing, the private residual is per-sample RMS-normalized and rescaled to match the RMS of the frozen backbone output at the same projection, with stop-gradient on both statistics. This eliminates the scale degree of freedom coupling gate and residual, so each gate value directly encodes a well-defined injection ratio relative to backbone activation scale, making the ℓ₁ gate penalty a stable personalization budget. The backbone-anchored target (rather than the evolving shared branch) ensures round-stable calibration without any additional communication, and the resulting gate gradients no longer scale with private residual drift, reducing optimization variability under non-IID updates.

## Results

FedSDG achieves an average local top-1 accuracy of 74.27% across all 16 evaluation settings on four benchmarks (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, Office-Home, Mini-DomainNet), outperforming the strongest standard baseline FedAvg (71.67%) by +2.60 points and the closest PEFT-FL competitor FedDPA (66.56%) by +7.71 points. Under severe label skew (α=0.1), FedSDG reaches 88.20% on CIFAR-100 and 80.63% on Tiny-ImageNet, surpassing FedRep (86.50% / 79.23%) and FedDPA (82.88% / 73.28%). Scaling to ViT-Base, FedSDG attains 89.78% average accuracy, and on the ImageNet-R OOD benchmark it leads with 75.65% average, confirming robustness under distribution shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSDG outperforms all seven baselines on average across the 16 settings. Against FedAvg (71.67%), the strongest standard FL method, FedSDG gains +2.60 points on average, with the gap widening under strong heterogeneity (e.g., +8.98 on CIFAR-100 α=0.1). The closest PEFT-FL competitor, FedDPA (66.56%), is surpassed by +7.71 points on average; the margin is largest on Tiny-ImageNet (e.g., +7.35 at α=0.1) and Office-Home domain-shift settings. FedRep (64.56%) and Ditto (67.48%), which use head-level or proximal personalization rather than residual gating, trail FedSDG by 9.7 and 6.8 points respectively, indicating that projection-level gated residual allocation is more effective than coarse parameter partitioning. Among recent federated LoRA methods, FedALT (72.21% avg in Table 2) and FedSA-LoRA (67.24%) are both outperformed, with FedSDG reaching 79.90% on the same four label-skew settings. On the OOD ImageNet-R benchmark, FedSDG (75.65%) leads FedALT (72.77%) by +2.88 and FedRep (53.61%) by +22.04, confirming that the scale-identifiable gating generalizes beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual-branch federated PEFT line initiated by FedDPA [43], which maintains parallel shared and private LoRA adapters aggregated only on the shared branch. It departs from prior work by formalizing a scale non-identifiability property in gated residual mixing—showing that the product of gate value and private residual magnitude is the only functionally meaningful quantity—and by introducing Dynamic Alignment, a backbone-anchored RMS calibration that fixes the private residual scale before gating. The design also draws on the broader personalized FL literature (FedRep, Ditto, FedPer) for the shared/private parameter partitioning principle, but applies it within a low-rank residual subspace rather than full-layer splits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scale non-identifiability observation clarifies why mixing coefficients in dual-branch federated PEFT are unreliable as personalization-strength indicators under heterogeneous local optimization, and DA provides a communication-free fix that restores interpretability and stabilizes gate gradients. This matters for any future work using weighted residual mixing in federated settings, as it identifies a previously unaddressed confound. Limitations are notable: the method is validated only on ViT backbones with LoRA adapters for image classification, and no privacy or robustness analysis is provided. Open questions include whether the identifiability argument and DA transfer to other PEFT paradigms (adapters, prompt tuning), to LLM fine-tuning, and whether the backbone-anchored calibration remains effective under more aggressive distribution shifts or partial participation.
