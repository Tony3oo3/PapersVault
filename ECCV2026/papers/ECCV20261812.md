## Problem

Vision-language models consistently underperform on spatial reasoning tasks (geometric relations, navigation, map-based reasoning) even at large scales, and existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA require millions of trainable parameters to close this gap. The paper asks whether a handful of bounded scalars that rescale internal activations—without modifying any pretrained weights—can serve as a sufficient adaptation mechanism for spatial reasoning.

## Input / Output

Each example consists of an RGB image, a natural-language question, and four candidate answers (A–D); the model scores the four options using only the final token's logits to select the correct answer. The scaling is applied exclusively at the last token position within the language backbone, so upstream token representations remain untouched. Evaluation spans synthetic spatial benchmarks (SpatialEval with 1500 samples per task) and real-world VQA (COCOQA, VGQA), with train/test splits of 5%, 10%, and 20%.

## Method

ScAle introduces one learnable scalar per attention head (and optionally one per MLP block) per transformer layer, applied only to the last-token activation via a bounded function eff(u) = 1 + s_max·tanh(u) that constrains scaling to (1−s_max, 1+s_max). All pretrained weights remain frozen; the sole trainable parameters are these scalars, totaling roughly 1K for a 7B model versus 2.6M+ for LoRA. Training minimizes cross-entropy on the four-way answer choice plus an L2 penalty on the scalars, using AdamW at lr 1e-3 for 5 epochs on as few as 75 samples. Ablations show attention-head scaling substantially outperforms MLP-only scaling, indicating that spatial reasoning failures stem from miscalibrated token interactions rather than insufficient feed-forward capacity. The learned scalars are architecture-agnostic, transfer across model families (LLaVA-Next, Qwen2.5-VL), and can be combined with LoRA on QKV projections for further gains.

## Results

On SpatialEval with LLaVA-7B (20% training split), ScAle (attn) with only 1,024 trainable parameters improves accuracy from 31.7% to 68.9% on Maze-Nav, from 29.3% to 68.6% on Spatial-Grid, and from 22.7% to 45.0% on Spatial-Map, yielding up to 134.1% relative gain. On Qwen2.5-VL-3B, gains are even larger: Maze-Nav improves from 26.0% to 70.3% (+44.3 pts) with just 576 parameters. On real-world VQA (WhatsUp-VLM), ScAle lifts LLaVA-7B from 56.5% to 92.9% on VGQA and from 56.3% to 91.5% on COCOQA, while also improving POPE hallucination robustness from 82.1% to 88.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA-all (663.6K params on LLaVA-7B), ScAle (1.0K params) achieves comparable Maze-Nav accuracy (68.9% vs. 70.8%) but trails on Spatial-Grid (68.6% vs. 84.8%) and Spatial-Map (45.0% vs. 80.7%), using over 2,500× fewer trainable parameters. Against the smaller LoRA-last variant (78.1K params), ScAle decisively wins on all three SpatialEval tasks: 68.9% vs. 53.6% (Maze-Nav), 68.6% vs. 29.2% (Spatial-Grid), and 45.0% vs. 24.3% (Spatial-Map), despite training 76× fewer parameters. Against (IA)³ (664K params), ScAle underperforms on real-world VQA (VGQA: 92.9% vs. 97.8%; COCOQA: 91.5% vs. 96.6%) and POPE (88.2% vs. 89.8%), but does so with 600× fewer parameters. The hybrid LoRA-QKV + ScAle combination (935K params) surpasses LoRA-all (2.65M params) on all three SpatialEval tasks (e.g., 78.9% vs. 71.6% on Maze-Nav), demonstrating that ScAle is complementary to LoRA rather than a strict replacement.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScAle repurposes the activation-scaling concept from mechanistic interpretability (where scaling or patching activations reveals causal circuits) into a learnable, end-to-end PEFT objective, departing from the dominant paradigm of injecting low-rank matrices (LoRA) or per-dimension scaling vectors (IA3). Rather than adding new representational directions to the residual stream, it learns a single bounded scalar per attention head applied only to the last token, positioning itself as a minimal, architecture-agnostic adapter. The paper explicitly contrasts with SteerVLM's activation steering (which requires 14% of model parameters) and with adapter-tuning, emphasizing that its handful of scalars achieve fine-grained control with orders-of-magnitude fewer parameters than any prior PEFT method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that ~1K bounded scalars can recover a substantial fraction of LoRA's spatial-reasoning gains (with 2,500× fewer parameters) offers a practical, low-cost adaptation path for deployment-constrained settings where even LoRA's overhead is prohibitive. However, absolute accuracy still trails LoRA-all by 5–15 points on several tasks, and the method has only been validated on spatial reasoning as a motivating case study; whether the same last-token, per-head scaling transfers to other capability gaps (e.g., arithmetic, long-context) remains open. The hybrid LoRA-QKV + ScAle result (Table 5) hints at a broader design space for combining lightweight modulation with targeted low-rank updates, but this direction is underexplored.
