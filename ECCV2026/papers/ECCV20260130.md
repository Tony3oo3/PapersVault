## Problem

State-of-the-art open-vocabulary object detection methods rely on single-step CLIP image-text matching for pseudo-label generation, which collapses in complex scenes with crowding or occlusion. This single-step alignment produces noisy pseudo-boxes from co-occurrence bias, is structurally bound to caption-derived vocabularies that miss or misdescribe objects, and absorbs occluded foreground instances into the background class. The paper identifies these three failure modes as stemming from compressing entire scene understanding into one reasoning unit without intermediate decomposition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260130/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The offline pseudo-labeling phase consumes RGB images (COCO/LVIS training set) and produces bounding boxes, class labels, and segmentation masks via SAM-B (ViT-B) for masks and a 7B MLLM (Qwen2) for category reasoning, with visual context modulation (blurring and desaturation of non-target regions) applied before MLLM queries. The online detection phase takes the same RGB images plus pre-computed pseudo-labels and region descriptions, and outputs bounding boxes and instance segmentation masks for both base and novel classes using a Faster R-CNN detector with ResNet-50-FPN backbone. Pseudo-labels are used exclusively during training and discarded at inference to prevent misclassification.

## Method

MSPL decomposes pseudo-label generation into three sequential MLLM reasoning steps: (1) object verification via a ternary 'does an object exist?' query on SAM whole-instance masks (restricted via hierarchical grouping), (2) zero-shot category recognition that elicits both a class name and a region-level textual description without any caption-derived candidate set, and (3) explicit background grounding that binary-classifies each prediction as foreground or background concept. The online phase builds on BARON's bag-of-regions architecture but replaces expensive online co-occurrence sampling with a cached composition generator (1.5× speedup), and introduces two contrastive losses: Region-Text Alignment (RTA) that aligns pseudo-word embeddings with their corresponding region descriptions via InfoNCE, and Contrastive Background Learning (CBL) that uses CLIP-encoded background concept embeddings as negative samples in the contrastive denominator to push foreground features away from the learnable background prior. A per-class frequency threshold filters hallucinated pseudo-labels, retaining only 'semantic anchors' whose support matches trusted base-class statistics.

## Results

On OV-COCO, MSPL achieves 43.4 AP₅₀ᴺ (ResNet-50) and 47.8 AP₅₀ᴺ (ResNet-50×4), setting a new state-of-the-art among pseudo-annotation-based OVD methods and surpassing the strongest distillation-based method DeCo-DETR (41.3) by 2.1 AP₅₀ᴺ. On OV-LVIS, MSPL attains 26.4 APᵣ for detection and 24.8 APᵣ for segmentation on rare classes, outperforming the previous best by 3.2 and 2.2 APᵣ respectively. In zero-shot transfer to MS-COCO and Objects365 (trained on OV-LVIS without fine-tuning), MSPL leads all compared methods with 37.5 AP on MS-COCO and 15.1 AP on Objects365.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On OV-COCO with ResNet-50, MSPL (43.4 AP₅₀ᴺ) outperforms the strongest pseudo-annotation baseline LP-OVOD (40.5) by 2.9, SAS-Det (37.4) by 6.0, and VL-PLM (34.4) by 9.0, and also beats the best distillation-only method DeCo-DETR (41.3) by 2.1. With the larger ResNet-50×4 backbone, MSPL (47.8) surpasses CORA+ (43.1) by 4.7 and OV-DQUO (45.6) by 2.2. On OV-LVIS, MSPL leads in both detection (26.4 APᵣ vs. BIRDet 26.0, CAKE 25.0) and segmentation (24.8 APᵣ vs. CAKE 23.9, LBP 23.7), with the largest margin over its architectural baseline BARON (+3.2 detection, +2.2 segmentation). In zero-shot transfer to MS-COCO and Objects365, MSPL consistently outperforms all baselines (e.g., 37.5 vs. 36.8 AP on MS-COCO vs. LBP; 15.1 vs. 14.3 AP on Objects365 vs. LBP), confirming broad generalization. The paper also reports that MSPL achieves these gains at a lower per-image pseudo-labeling cost (0.43 s) than SAS-Det (>1.0 s with self-training), while producing substantially higher AP₅₀ᴺ under crowded (23.9 vs. 11.6) and occluded (15.5 vs. 5.7) conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSPL builds directly on BARON's bag-of-regions contrastive framework for open-vocabulary detection, replacing its computationally expensive online co-occurrence sampling with a cached composition generator. It departs from the dominant pseudo-labeling paradigm (PB-OVD, VL-PLM, SAS-Det) that relies on single-step CLIP image-text alignment against caption-derived candidate sets, instead reformulating pseudo-label generation as a three-step visual prompting process (SAM-based localization, MLLM zero-shot recognition, explicit background grounding). The multi-step decomposition draws on chain-of-thought prompting in LLMs and structured visual reasoning in embodied tasks, but its specific application to OVD pseudo-labeling—addressing co-occurrence bias, caption dependency, and background collapse as distinct failure modes—is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and systematically addresses three concrete failure modes of single-step VLM alignment in crowded and occluded scenes, providing an interpretable diagnostic framework that goes beyond 'bigger model = better labels.' The offline-to-online decoupling (intensive MLLM reasoning done once, lightweight contrastive training online) makes the approach practical for large-scale deployment, and the 9.4 AP50 gain on OV-COCO novel classes over prior pseudo-labeling methods is substantial. Open questions include whether the three-step decomposition is optimal (could more or fewer steps help), how the method scales to extremely large vocabularies beyond LVIS's 337 rare classes, and whether the MLLM dependency (Qwen2-7B) limits real-world adoption in resource-constrained settings.
