## Problem

Existing generative models are optimized for screen-based visual realism and produce non-manifold surfaces, fragile structures, and lack fine tactile textures, making them unsuitable for physical fabrication or haptic perception. Tactile graphics for blind and low-vision individuals remain largely produced through manual, expert-driven workflows (swell paper, embossing, sculptural relief) that are time-consuming, labor-intensive, and difficult to scale or personalize. No prior system jointly generates fabrication-ready 2.5D geometry, fine-grained tactile surface textures, and standard-compliant braille from natural language.

## Input / Output

The system accepts a natural language text prompt describing an object and its surface textures, with optional tactile sensor data (e.g., GelSight captures) as an alternative texture source. It outputs a watertight, manifold 3D mesh at 2.5D relief scale with a strictly flat base, fine-grained displacement-mapped surface textures (derived from 512×512 tileable normal maps), and U.S./international-standard braille annotations, all directly compatible with SLA resin 3D printing (Formlabs Form 4). Texture regions are spatially controlled via SAM3 segmentation masks that users can interactively refine.

## Method

The pipeline separates global shape from fine texture: a fixed base-plate template conditions Qwen-Image-Edit to produce a stable 2.5D relief image, which is lifted to 3D via MoGE v2 monocular depth estimation. A dedicated text-to-tactile-texture module—obtained by fine-tuning Qwen-Image on a curated dataset of real and synthetic close-up surface textures—generates high-resolution normal maps targeting local, repeatable patterns rather than object-centric compositions. Tiling is achieved by combining SDEdit with inpainting on an intra-tile quadrant rearrangement (point-symmetric swap), preceded by center-cropping and per-channel Fourier-domain high-pass filtering to remove low-frequency geometric bias. Fabrication-aware post-processing projects all base-plate vertices onto a single horizontal plane to enforce zero height variation, then places standard-compliant braille dots on flat, relief-free regions. The key architectural insight is that texture-centric generation (fine, tileable surface patterns) must be decoupled from object-centric shape synthesis, since general-purpose text-to-image models suppress semantically inconsistent textures and monocular depth estimation smooths high-frequency relief detail.

## Results

In the in-person user study with 15 participants (8 BLV, 7 blindfolded sighted), the full pipeline achieves a mean Likert score of 3.93 ± 1.32 (BLV) and 3.35 ± 1.39 (BSP), significantly outperforming both the Naive baseline (3.62 / 3.02) and Hunyuan3D 2.1 (3.62 / 2.97) with p < 0.01 and Cohen's d = 0.16 for both comparisons. Adding standard-compliant braille raises object recognition accuracy from ~20% to near-perfect. On the tiling module, the proposed intra-tile SDEdit+inpainting approach achieves the best border continuity (28.43) and gradient continuity (0.400) while running in ~3 s per patch, versus ~100 sampling steps for Tiled Diffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **Hunyuan3D 2.1**, the full pipeline wins on the user preference study by 0.31 (BLV) and 0.38 (BSP) Likert points (p = 0.006, Cohen's d = 0.16); Hunyuan3D also struggles with plate geometry, sometimes placing the base at incorrect depths. Against **Trellis 2**, no direct quantitative comparison is possible because Trellis 2 outputs frequently fail fabrication checks (non-manifold geometry, thin structures) and are excluded from the physical user study; qualitatively it produces broken geometry such as objects intersecting the base plate. Against the **Naive baseline** (same geometry pipeline but no dedicated texture or braille modules), the full method wins by 0.32 Likert points (p = 0.008), confirming the value of explicit text-to-texture synthesis and braille placement. Against **Nano Banana Pro**, the fine-tuned Qwen-Image texture model achieves comparable LPIPS and HF-ratio scores while remaining fully open-source and reproducible; Nano Banana Pro without plate conditioning fails to detect the base plate in 6/50 samples, whereas all plate-conditioned variants achieve 100% detection. Against **Tiled Diffusion** on the tiling sub-task, the proposed intra-tile SDEdit+inpainting method produces lower border and gradient discontinuity scores and runs in ~3 s (10 denoising steps) versus ~100 sampling steps, while Tiled Diffusion tends to alter the underlying normal-map structure and generate semantically incorrect tactile patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three prior research threads: (1) text-to-image generation (Qwen-Image), which is repurposed and fine-tuned for texture-centric synthesis rather than object-centric composition; (2) monocular depth estimation (MoGE v2) and 2.5D relief generation, which provides the geometric lifting step; and (3) tactile graphics design literature, which establishes perceptual constraints (tactile contrast, spacing, braille standards) that prior generative pipelines ignore. The key departure from prior text-to-3D work (Hunyuan3D, Trellis) is the explicit enforcement of fabrication constraints—flat bases, watertight meshes, minimum feature thickness—and haptic readability, rather than visual realism. For tiling, the paper departs from Tiled Diffusion by showing that training-free diffusion-based tiling fails on normal maps due to distribution mismatch, and instead combines SDEdit with inpainting in a simpler, faster formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end system that turns a natural language prompt into a physically 3D-printable tactile graphic with integrated geometry, surface texture, and standard-compliant braille, directly addressing a scalability bottleneck in accessible media production for the 290+ million BLV individuals worldwide. The practical impact is significant: it lowers the barrier from expert manual fabrication (swell paper, embossing) to automated, personalized tactile content. However, the user study is small (15 participants, single region), object recognition without braille remains poor (~20%), and the system is limited to 2.5D reliefs rather than full 3D shapes. Open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to more complex spatial relationships, whether larger-scale BLV studies confirm the preference results, and whether the fine-tuned texture model can be extended to generate genuinely novel material textures beyond the training distribution.
