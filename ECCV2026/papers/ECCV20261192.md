## Problem

Existing regression-based intraoperative 2D/3D registration methods require patient-specific retraining (hours of preparation) to achieve sufficient accuracy, making them impractical in emergency settings. No single pretrained model can generalize across patients without additional preparation. Additionally, geometry foundation models like DUSt3R operate on homogeneous image pairs and have not been extended to the heterogeneous cross-modal setting of 2D X-rays and 3D volumes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261192/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 2D X-ray image (resized to 224×224) and a 3D CT volume (resized to 96×96×96) with known camera intrinsics as input, and outputs a 6-DoF camera pose in SE(3) parameterized as an axis-angle rotation vector and a 3D translation vector. The model is anatomy-specific (pelvis) but patient-agnostic, requiring no per-patient preparation. The predicted pose can optionally be refined in under 3 seconds via differentiable DRR-based test-time optimization.

## Method

XPos3R adapts the DUSt3R feedforward paradigm to cross-modal inputs via an asymmetric, image-centric encoder-decoder. The 2D X-ray is encoded by a pretrained RayDINO ViT (domain-adapted for X-rays), while the 3D volume is encoded by a lightweight four-stage 3D CNN trained from scratch to produce dense features that align better with image tokens than pretrained segmentation backbones. The decoder uses unidirectional image-to-volume cross-attention (self-attention on image tokens only, then cross-attention querying volume tokens as key-value memory), avoiding the computational cost of self-attention over the much larger volume token set. A global patient embedding extracted via GAP on volume features is concatenated with decoded image features before two separate MLPs predict rotation and translation. Training uses 2.3 million synthetic volume-DRR-pose triplets generated from 461 CTs with 5,000 random poses each, supervised by Huber loss on translation and geodesic distance on rotation.

## Results

On DeepFluoro, a single pretrained XPos3R (no patient-specific preparation) achieves the best feedforward accuracy among all regression methods: RE 1.352°, TE 24.06 mm, TRE 24.16 mm, and PE 7.412 mm, with zero preoperative time. With test-time optimization (DiffDRR), XPos3R further reduces errors to RE 0.236°, TE 3.313 mm, TRE 3.288 mm, and PE 0.448 mm, completing optimization in under 3 seconds per X-ray. In contrast, the strongest patient-specific baseline (DiffPose) requires ~12 hours of preoperative training and still yields higher variance across subjects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two 2D-only regression baselines, XPos3R (pretrained) clearly outperforms both DiffPose and XVR (pretrained) on all feedforward metrics, reducing TRE from 43.88 mm (DiffPose) and 90.61 mm (XVR pretrained) to 24.16 mm while requiring no preoperative preparation (vs. 12 hr for DiffPose). Compared with XVR (finetuned), which needs 5 min of patient-specific adaptation, XPos3R still wins on RE (1.352° vs. 2.587°), TE (24.06 vs. 34.14 mm), and TRE (24.16 vs. 33.90 mm), though it trails on the 2D projection metric PE (7.412 vs. 3.860 mm). After TTO, XPos3R achieves the best results across all four metrics (TRE 3.288 mm, PE 0.448 mm), narrowly beating DiffPose+TTO (TRE 3.810 mm) and XVR-finetuned+TTO (TRE 3.811 mm), while also exhibiting the smallest standard deviation (±8.31 mm vs. ±14.95 mm), indicating substantially better robustness. Among pure optimization-based methods, RayEmb is the strongest (TRE 10.75 mm) but is still 3× worse than XPos3R+TTO; DiffDRR from a default initialization fails catastrophically (TRE 200.2 mm), underscoring the importance of a good feedforward initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

XPos3R adapts the DUSt3R geometry foundation model paradigm—originally designed for homogeneous image-image pairs—to the heterogeneous 2D X-ray / 3D CT volume setting, replacing DUSt3R's symmetric Siamese encoder and reciprocal cross-attention decoder with an asymmetric, image-centric design (pretrained RayDINO ViT for X-rays, from-scratch 3D CNN for volumes, unidirectional image-to-volume cross-attention). It departs from prior regression-based 2D/3D registration methods (DiffPose, XVR) that rely solely on 2D inputs and require hours of patient-specific retraining, instead demonstrating that a single pretrained model can generalize across patients. The data curation strategy of synthesizing million-scale volume-DRR-pose triplets from a few hundred CTs via diverse pose sampling is a practical solution to medical data scarcity that has no direct analogue in the natural-image DUSt3R training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating patient-specific preoperative preparation (from hours to zero) is a significant practical advance for emergency and intraoperative settings where time is critical. The asymmetric cross-modal architecture may serve as a template for other heterogeneous-input geometry tasks beyond medical imaging. However, validation is limited to a single anatomical region (pelvis) and a single benchmark (DeepFluoro, 6 cadaver subjects), and extension to other 3D modalities (MRI, ultrasound) or anatomies (cerebral vessels) remains open. Clinical deployment would require prospective validation on live patients and integration with existing surgical workflows.
