## Problem

Standard flow-based GRPO evaluates a group of generated samples against only the single original prompt, creating a sparse one-to-many reward mapping that fails to capture the multifaceted inter-sample relationships. This single-view evaluation is insufficient because reward rankings of the same samples shift substantially under semantically adjacent conditions, meaning a single prompt cannot adequately gauge the nuanced relative merits within a group, constraining both alignment efficacy and performance ceilings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and, during training, generates G=12 images via SDE-based sampling (T=16 steps, η=0.7) from the Flux.1-dev flow model. A Condition Enhancer (Qwen3-VL-8B online or Qwen3-8B offline) then produces K additional semantically adjacent captions from the original prompt and/or the generated samples. The aligned model outputs photorealistic images conditioned on text prompts, evaluated on 400 held-out HPD prompts using multiple reward models (HPS-v3, UnifiedReward-v2, CLIP, ImageReward).

## Method

MV-GRPO augments the condition space rather than the data space: a Condition Enhancer generates K semantically adjacent but diverse captions (via VLM describing each SDE sample with varied perspective prompts, or via LLM rewriting with addition/deletion/rewriting operations), and the same G generated samples are re-scored under each augmented condition to compute multi-view advantages. The training objective (Eq. 11) aggregates clipped policy-gradient losses across the anchor condition and all K augmented views, using importance-sampling ratios computed under each respective condition. A key theoretical justification shows that the log-probability drift between the original and augmented condition transitions is near zero across SDE steps (Fig. 5), validating that samples generated under the anchor prompt can be reused under augmented conditions without costly regeneration. The approach is built on Flow-GRPO-Fast with SDE sampling at selected timesteps {0,2,4,6}, and the VLM enhancer (K=G=12) consistently outperforms the LLM variant by providing sample-specific posterior captions that yield more discriminative reward signals.

## Results

Under the HPS-v3 single-reward setting, MV-GRPO (VLM) achieves the best HPS-v3 score of 0.155, outperforming the strongest baseline TempFlow-GRPO (0.150) and the vanilla Flux.1-dev (0.133). Under the UnifiedReward-v2 setting, MV-GRPO (VLM) leads on UR-v2-S (3.454 vs. 3.331 for TempFlow-GRPO) and IR (1.187 vs. 1.140). Under the multi-reward HPS-v3+CLIP setting, MV-GRPO (VLM) attains the highest IR (1.268), HPS-v2 (0.342), and UR-v2-S (3.396), confirming consistent gains across all evaluation protocols.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MV-GRPO (VLM) consistently outperforms all five baselines on the majority of metrics across all three reward settings. The closest competitor is TempFlow-GRPO, which leads on UR-v2-A under the HPS-v3 setting (3.240 vs. 3.229) and on CLIP under the HPS-v3+CLIP setting (0.397 vs. 0.393), but MV-GRPO (VLM) surpasses it on HPS-v3 (0.155 vs. 0.150), UR-v2-C (3.701 vs. 3.683), and IR (1.193 vs. 1.184) in that setting. Under the UnifiedReward-v2 setting, MV-GRPO (VLM) achieves the largest margin over TempFlow-GRPO on UR-v2-S (3.454 vs. 3.331, +0.123) and IR (1.187 vs. 1.140, +0.047). DiffusionNFT and DanceGRPO trail further behind, with DanceGRPO posting the lowest HPS-v3 (0.145) and IR (1.165) under the HPS-v3 setting. The offline LLM variant of MV-GRPO ranks second overall, confirming that the multi-view mechanism itself—rather than the specific enhancer—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025) and the broader GRPO-based alignment framework (Shao et al., 2024) by identifying a structural limitation in how existing flow-based GRPO methods evaluate generated samples: all samples in a group are scored against a single anchor condition, yielding a sparse one-to-many reward mapping. Rather than modifying the sampling process (as in DanceGRPO, MixGRPO, BranchGRPO) or the credit-assignment granularity (as in TempFlow-GRPO, Granular-GRPO), MV-GRPO augments the condition space itself via a VLM/LLM-based Condition Enhancer, re-scoring the same samples under semantically adjacent captions to produce a dense multi-view advantage signal. The theoretical justification—that semantically adjacent conditions induce negligible probability drift in the SDE transition kernel—distinguishes this work from prior GRPO variants that accept the single-condition evaluation as given.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation between condition-space augmentation and data-space regeneration makes MV-GRPO a low-overhead drop-in improvement for any flow-based GRPO pipeline, and the probability-drift analysis provides a principled guarantee that the multi-view signal is valid. However, the approach is bounded by the quality of the VLM/LLM enhancer and may be inapplicable to tasks with rigid conditioning (e.g., class-conditional generation). Open questions include whether the dense multi-view paradigm transfers to text-to-video or 3D generation, whether the gains compound with other GRPO refinements (dense credit assignment, mixed ODE-SDE sampling), and whether the modest but consistent improvements observed here scale to larger model families or longer training horizons.
