## Problem

Transformer-based NR-IQA models typically rely on global pooling or a single CLS token to summarize the entire image, which washes out subtle, localized distortions (e.g., fine speckles, faint edge ringing) whose signal is drowned by large clean regions. Without explicit region-aware routing, attention tends to follow visually salient content rather than the small-area artifacts that actually drive perceived quality degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261056/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single RGB image (padded to the nearest multiple of 16 to satisfy ViT-S patch constraints) and outputs a single scalar quality score. Internally, the detail-aware branch also consumes a grayscale version of the input to compute Sobel/Laplacian gradients and rFFT magnitude spectra, while the context branch operates on the RGB patches directly. The model contains 23M parameters and runs at 14.9 GFLOPs / 6.6 ms per image on an RTX 5090.

## Method

BWAFDA is a dual-branch architecture built on a ViT-S (DeiT-III) backbone. The context branch produces multi-scale patch tokens and a lightweight mask net predicts K soft block partitions over the patch grid; a block-wise weighted attention fusion (BWAF) module then performs mask-biased cross-attention per block across S=3 scales, where a log-probability prior derived from the masks is added to attention logits to stabilize cross-scale interactions, and per-scale block embeddings are combined via a learned convex combination. The detail-aware branch extracts spatial cues (Sobel + Laplacian) and frequency cues (radial FFT band energies), pools them to the patch grid, and aligns them with the block descriptors. A class-token-guided head concatenates each fused block embedding with its detail descriptor, applies a gating MLP conditioned on the global CLS token to produce per-block importance weights, and a final regressor maps the gated summary to the quality score. Training uses SmoothL1 + RankHinge losses plus entropy and total-variation regularizers on the soft masks, with a linearly increasing schedule that down-weights mask penalties as training progresses.

## Results

BWAFDA (23M params) achieves state-of-the-art PLCC and SRCC across all seven NR-IQA benchmarks. Headline numbers include PLCC/SRCC of 0.985/0.983 on LIVE, 0.975/0.972 on TID2013, 0.988/0.983 on CSIQ, 0.972/0.970 on KADID-10k, 0.928/0.917 on LIVEC, 0.949/0.940 on KonIQ-10k, and 0.777/0.723 on LIVEFB. The largest relative gains over the second-best method appear on LIVEFB (+5.71% PLCC, +13.15% SRCC) and TID2013 (+4.61% PLCC, +6.11% SRCC), where localized artifacts are most challenging. The model also runs at 14.9 GFLOPs and 6.6 ms per image on an RTX 5090, with only 23M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BWAFDA outperforms all 16 compared baselines on every dataset where both methods report results. The closest competitor is SHDIQA (24M), which is second-best on most benchmarks; BWAFDA extends its lead most dramatically on LIVEFB (0.777 vs 0.735 PLCC, 0.723 vs 0.639 SRCC) and TID2013 (0.975 vs 0.932 PLCC, 0.972 vs 0.916 SRCC for QFM-IQM). On synthetic datasets (LIVE, CSIQ, KADID), BWAFDA also surpasses strong transformer-based methods like QFM-IQM (24M) and LoDa (18M) by 1–4% in PLCC. On authentic datasets (LIVEC, KonIQ), margins are smaller (0.1–0.9%) but still consistent, with BWAFDA beating QPT (24M) and SHDIQA (24M) by 0.65–0.88% SRCC on LIVEC and 0.11–0.32% on KonIQ. Notably, BWAFDA achieves these gains with only 23M parameters and 14.9 GFLOPs, compared to TReS (152M, 39.9 GFLOPs) and LIQE (151M, 8.8–2958 GFLOPs), confirming a favorable accuracy–efficiency trade-off. Cross-dataset transfer (Table 3) further shows BWAFDA leading on 5 of 6 train–test pairs, with SRCC gains of 0.02–0.04 over the next-best method (e.g., 0.846 vs 0.819 on LIVEC→KonIQ).

## Relation to Prior Work

BWAFDA sits at the intersection of three established NR-IQA threads: ViT-based quality assessment (MUSIQ, MANIQA, DEIQT, SHDIQA, LoDa), detail-aware representation learning (BRISQUE, DBCNN, MANIQA's patch-weighted attention), and multi-scale feature fusion (MUSIQ, HyperIQA). Its key departure is replacing the global-pooling or single-CLS-token summarization common in prior transformer IQA models with a mask-biased, block-wise cross-attention aggregator that routes evidence by predicted spatial region, guided by a soft log-prior injected into attention logits. The lightweight detail branch (Sobel/Laplacian/FFT) is conceptually similar to DBCNN's dual-branch design and MANIQA's frequency cues, but is fused late via a class-token-guided gating head rather than early in the backbone, preserving the ViT's global reasoning while injecting high-frequency sensitivity at the decision stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 23M-parameter model achieving SOTA across seven benchmarks—particularly the +13.15% SRCC jump on the noisy, in-the-wild LIVEFB dataset—demonstrates that structured, region-aware fusion can outperform much larger models (e.g., TReS at 152M, LIQE at 151M) while remaining data-efficient at 20–40% training regimes. The ablations confirm that the masked prior, multi-scale aggregation, and detail branch are complementary rather than redundant, providing a clear design recipe for future NR-IQA architectures. Open questions the authors flag include learning stronger spatial priors via self-supervision (removing the hand-crafted SNR/edge/residual cues in the mask net) and extending the block-wise fusion to video IQA and explainable region attribution, where per-block importance maps could serve as interpretability tools.
