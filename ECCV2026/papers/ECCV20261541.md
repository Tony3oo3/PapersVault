## Problem

Stereo 3D detectors for autonomous driving face an imbalanced triplet: they are either closed-set and fast, or open-set but too slow (>80 ms) for real-time deployment. No existing stereo pipeline simultaneously achieves high-precision multi-class detection, prompt-free OoD anomaly discovery, and monocular-comparable inference speed, leaving a critical safety gap for unforeseen road obstacles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261541/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a pair of rectified stereo RGB images (left and right) processed through a shared ResNet backbone at four scales (1/4 to 1/32). Beyond standard 3D boxes and class labels, it outputs a binary foreground probability per query, an OoD anomaly score (MNPF = foreground confidence minus max sigmoid-normalized class probability), and an object-level depth map at 1/4 resolution. The 2D box is parameterized as six offsets from the projected 3D center rather than the conventional (x, y, w, h) format, enabling direct reuse in the depth-sampling step.

## Method

DDStereo employs a dual-decoder transformer with a shared learnable object query that couples a visual-feature 3D detection branch (predicting size, orientation, class, and 2D box) with a disparity-feature binary foreground branch (predicting foreground probability and coarse 2D box). Disparity features are constructed as simple correlation volumes by shifting right-view features horizontally up to 24/16 pixels at four scales, encoding geometric depth cues independent of texture. A lightweight U-shaped depth decoder upsamples multi-scale correlation volumes to a 1/4-resolution depth map, from which object-centric depth is extracted via grid sampling at predicted 3D centers—eliminating the need for depth-guided cross-attention. The OoD score is computed as the gap between the foreground confidence and the maximum sigmoid-normalized known-class probability (MNPF), requiring no external text prompts or anchor-level NMS. Training uses independent Hungarian matching for the 2D foreground and 3D detection heads plus a disparity supervision loss, with the entire pipeline running at 24 ms on a single GPU.

## Results

On the AR-OoD open-set benchmark (R40, Moderate, IoU>0.25), DDStereo achieves 78.09% AP₃D, 82.44% AP_BEV, and 99.14% AP₂D for the OoD class, surpassing S3AD by +4.40, +5.68, and +3.61 points respectively. On KITTI Car (test set), DDStereo reaches 43.97% AP₃D (Moderate) and 36.16% (Hard), outperforming the fastest closed-set stereo baseline StereoDETR (41.17%/35.13%) while running at 24 ms per frame—only 6 ms slower than StereoDETR's 18 ms but 3× faster than S3AD (75 ms). The model requires just 62.65 GFLOPs and 19.6 M parameters, less than one-fifth of S3AD's 109.5 M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against S3AD [33], the primary open-set stereo baseline, DDStereo wins on every reported metric at R40: +4.40 AP₃D on OoD, +7.22 on Car, +6.67 on Pedestrian, and +10.86 on Cyclist, while running 3× faster (24 ms vs. 75 ms) with 5.5× fewer parameters. Against StereoDETR [32], the fastest closed-set stereo detector, DDStereo gains +2.80 AP₃D (Moderate) and +1.03 (Hard) on KITTI Car at only 6 ms additional latency, though it slightly trails on AP_BEV (−0.93 Moderate, −1.39 Hard). Against OV-Mono3D [46], which relies on text prompts, DDStereo achieves dramatically better 3D localization (78.09% vs. 0.53% AP₃D for OoD), confirming that prompt-free geometric reasoning outperforms language-guided lifting for 3D anomaly detection. On KITTI, DDStereo also surpasses DSC3D [6] (+1.43 AP₃D Mod) and YOLOStereo3D [28] (+2.72 AP₃D Mod) while being 4.6× and 3.3× faster respectively. On Argoverse, DDStereo improves over YOLOStereo3D by +9.09 AP₃D (Car) but remains below LiDAR-distillation methods (StereoDistill: 37.55 vs. 25.74), acknowledging the gap when ground-truth depth supervision is available.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDStereo extends the authors' own StereoDETR (a real-time closed-set stereo DETR) by adding an open-set foreground detection branch, and builds on MonoDETR/MonoDGP's grid-sampling depth strategy for efficient 3D localization. It departs from S3AD (also by the same group), the first stereo open-set 3D detector, by replacing S3AD's anchor-based NMS and high-dimensional feature concatenation with a decoupled dual-decoder architecture and shared object queries. Conceptually, it also diverges from open-vocabulary 3D detectors (OV-Mono3D, DetAny3D) that require text prompts, instead relying purely on disparity-driven geometric cues for prompt-free anomaly discovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DDStereo is the first stereo 3D detector to simultaneously achieve open-set anomaly detection and monocular-comparable inference speed (~24 ms), making real-time deployment of OoD obstacle perception practically viable for autonomous driving. The shared-query alignment mechanism is a simple yet effective architectural insight: the ablation showing a ~27% OoD AP drop with independent queries underscores that cross-branch consistency is critical for reliable anomaly scoring. Open questions remain around scalability to larger, more diverse datasets and robustness to varied camera intrinsics/extrinsics, which the authors acknowledge as limitations requiring robust rectification and large-scale pre-training.
