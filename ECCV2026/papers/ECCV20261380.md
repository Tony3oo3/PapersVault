## Problem

Streaming 4D reconstruction has only been demonstrated indoors on dense camera rigs with human-pace motion, or outdoors from ego-vehicle cameras. The spectator-relevant case—a fast-moving subject (200–400 px/frame displacement) observed from a sparse ring of fixed exocentric cameras under uncontrolled outdoor illumination—has no dedicated method and no benchmark to evaluate one.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261380/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FastFlowGS consumes multi-view video at 960×540 from a small set of fixed cameras (7–8 observing the dynamic region at once), along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics and per-instance segmentation masks. It produces a streaming 4D Gaussian Splatting scene decomposed into three independently fitted layers (skybox, static background via Hierarchical 3DGS, and dynamic foreground Gaussians) that are composited at render time for novel-view synthesis. The dynamic layer outputs per-Gaussian 3D positions updated each frame, with convergence in 200–300 optimization iterations per frame.

## Method

FastFlowGS's core insight is that no single correspondence mechanism is reliable under large displacements, but cross-level disagreement reveals where each one fails. It fuses three correspondence levels—sparse keypoint matches (LightGlue), semi-dense point tracks (CoTracker3), and dense optical flow (RAFT)—by densifying sparse/medium tracks via Gaussian-weighted Voronoi interpolation, computing a cross-level agreement score that downweights pixels where estimates disagree, and lifting each level independently to 3D via weighted DLT triangulation with per-Gaussian covariance estimation. All per-level 3D estimates are then merged through a single information-form Kalman update with a random-walk temporal prior whose process noise adapts to observed scene dynamics, yielding a closed-form position per Gaussian before optimization begins. Uncertainty-driven densification splits or clones Gaussians with high triangulation covariance before the per-frame gradient step, frontloading capacity where multi-view geometry is weakest rather than waiting for rendering error to reveal under-reconstruction.

## Results

On CMU-Panoptic at normal frame rate, FastFlowGS achieves VMAF 60.71, PSNR 27.89, and M-PSNR 26.14, exceeding the strongest baseline (HiCoM, VMAF 53.93) by 12.6% in VMAF while running 35% faster (4 s vs. 5.89 s per frame). Under 5× frame skipping, FastFlowGS retains PSNR 27.57 and M-PSNR 27.19 with ΔPSNR of only 0.192, whereas hash-grid-based baselines (3DGStream, HiCoM, ReCon-GS) drop by 3–6 dB. On Monaco4D (reduced-memory protocol), FastFlowGS achieves VMAF 46.9–55.8 across six sequences with per-frame times of 19–27 s, while all streaming baselines collapse to VMAF below 10 on most sequences except D-3DGS (VMAF 32–41 at 720–740 s per frame).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FastFlowGS outperforms all six streaming baselines on every metric at both frame rates on CMU-Panoptic. The closest competitor, HiCoM, trails by 6.8 VMAF points (53.93 vs. 60.71) and degrades 18× more over a sequence (ΔPSNR 2.35 vs. 0.13). Under 5× subsampling, hash-grid methods (3DGStream, HiCoM, ReCon-GS) suffer the sharpest drops (PSNR falls 3–6 dB) because their finest-cell resolution caps representable displacements, while FastFlowGS retains the highest PSNR and M-PSNR. On Monaco4D, the comparison is stark: all five streaming baselines lose the majority of their Gaussians by frame 2 and produce empty reconstructions by frame 5 under the full-quality protocol, so only the 3DGS-Base control is reported there. In the reduced-memory protocol, D-3DGS is the sole baseline producing recognizable quality (VMAF 32–41) but at 720–740 s per frame—two orders of magnitude slower than FastFlowGS (19–27 s). QUEEN achieves its best result on the Pool sequence (VMAF 38.75, VE 1.68) but collapses below VMAF 13 on every other sequence; HiCoM, ReCon-GS, and TrackerSplat fall below VMAF 10 on most sequences, confirming that the displacement regime of Monaco4D is fundamentally incompatible with their motion models.

## Relation to Prior Work

FastFlowGS extends the streaming 4D Gaussian Splatting lineage most directly from TrackerSplat, which initializes Gaussians from point trackers with per-frame finetuning, by adding multi-resolution correspondence fusion (sparse, semi-dense, dense), uncertainty-aware DLT triangulation, and a Kalman-style variational temporal prior. It also inherits the scene factorization (static background + dynamic foreground + skybox) and parallel optimization strategy from the Echoes of the Coliseum work. The key conceptual departure from all prior streaming methods is the rejection of a single correspondence mechanism in favor of cross-level agreement scoring, motivated by the observation that no single tracker is reliable across all surfaces and displacement magnitudes simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Monaco4D is the first public multi-view outdoor benchmark targeting the extreme-displacement regime (200–400 px inter-frame motion) that outdoor broadcast applications actually require, filling a gap where driving datasets lack lateral displacements and indoor datasets lack speed and uncontrolled illumination. The finding that all existing streaming methods produce empty reconstructions by frame 5 on Monaco4D—rather than degrading gracefully—highlights a fundamental mismatch between current motion models and the spectator-viewpoint setting. Open questions include whether FastFlowGS transfers to real outdoor footage at F1-scale displacements (currently uncharacterized), how the method handles deforming or rapidly rotating objects (positions only, no rotation/scale updates), and whether the synthetic-to-real gap in Monaco4D limits its utility as a general outdoor benchmark.
