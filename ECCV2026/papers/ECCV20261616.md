## Problem

Existing video reasoning models struggle to achieve reliable spatio-temporal grounding throughout the reasoning process: text-centric models hallucinate evidence from language priors, tool-calling approaches add inference-time computational overhead, and grounded reasoning models still produce inaccurate object references or hallucinated bounding boxes. Improving grounding typically requires either expensive dense annotations or additional perception modules at inference, rather than enhancing the model's intrinsic perception behavior. VisionCoach addresses this by shifting from data scaling and inference-time intervention to training-time visual prompting guidance that the model internalizes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261616/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw video clip and a text question as input. During RL training, visual prompts (darkening, red circles, frame numbering, attention overlays) are selectively applied to key frames only for identified hard samples, with a textual hint appended to the question describing the applied prompt. The model outputs a structured grounded reasoning trajectory containing object names, bounding boxes, temporal annotations, and a final answer. At inference, the model performs a single forward pass on the raw video with no visual prompting, no external tools, and no iterative perception calls.

## Method

VisionCoach is an input-adaptive RL framework with two components: a Visual Prompt Selector (VP-SELECTOR), a lightweight LoRA-tuned VLM classifier trained via SFT to predict the optimal visual prompt type from a candidate pool given the video-question pair, and a Spatio-Temporal Reasoner (ST-REASONER) optimized with GSPO. During RL training, hard samples are identified by averaging initial rollout rewards against a threshold; for these, the frozen VP-SELECTOR selects a prompt that is applied to key frames, and the reasoner generates new rollouts under the prompted input. When prompted rollouts yield higher rewards, self-distillation (token-level NLL on top-N high-reward trajectories) internalizes the improved reasoning so the model no longer needs prompts at inference. A novel object-aware spatial grounding reward enforces soft object identity matching (exact or substring) and averages IoU across all predicted bounding boxes under a temporal gating threshold, replacing the prior single-max-IoU design that encouraged object-agnostic hallucinations.

## Results

On the V-STAR spatio-temporal reasoning benchmark, VisionCoach achieves 61.1% VQA accuracy, 34.3 mAM (spatial), and 47.5 mLGM (overall), surpassing GPT-4o (26.8 mAM, 38.2 mLGM) and improving over Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +15.0 mAM and +25.1 mLGM. On general video understanding, VisionCoach reaches 43.8 on WorldSense (vs. 37.5 for Open-o3-video) and 68.7 on PerceptionTest, while on Charades-STA temporal grounding it achieves 63.2 R@0.3 and 42.7 mIoU. On HCSTVG spatio-temporal grounding, VisionCoach attains 19.3 m_vIoU, a +12.9% relative gain over Open-o3-video, remaining competitive with GPT-5.4 (20.2 m_vIoU) despite using a 7B open-source backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisionCoach outperforms the strongest open-source grounded reasoning baseline, Open-o3-video, on every V-STAR metric (61.1 vs. 60.2 Acc; 34.3 vs. 33.4 mAM; 47.5 vs. 46.0 mLGM) and on Charades-STA (63.2 vs. 62.6 R@0.3; 42.7 vs. 42.5 mIoU). Against proprietary models, VisionCoach surpasses GPT-4o on spatial grounding (34.3 vs. 26.8 mAM) and overall V-STAR score (47.5 vs. 38.2 mLGM), though GPT-4o retains an edge on general video understanding (VideoMME 71.9 vs. 63.3; VideoMMMU 61.2 vs. 54.4). On HCSTVG, VisionCoach achieves 19.3 m_vIoU, a +12.9% relative improvement over Open-o3-video (17.1) and within 0.9 of GPT-5.4 (20.2), while all tool-calling methods (VideoZoomer, Conan, Video-o3) score below 12 m_vIoU. On WorldSense, VisionCoach (43.8) substantially outperforms Open-o3-video (37.5) and GPT-4o (42.6), and on PerceptionTest it leads all open-source baselines (68.7 vs. 67.5 for Open-o3-video). The ablation confirms that input-adaptive visual prompting (VP-SELECTOR) is critical: fixed prompting (darken) degrades mAM to 29.7, while adaptive selection with self-distillation recovers to 31.3 mAM and 43.1 mLGM.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisionCoach builds directly on the grounded video reasoning framework of Open-o3-video [38], adopting its structured output format (object, box, timestamp annotations) and its RL training recipe with GSPO [61], while replacing the single-max-IoU spatial reward with an object-aware variant that enforces identity consistency across multiple predicted boxes. It departs from the broader tool-calling paradigm (VideoZoomer, Conan, Video-o3) that relies on inference-time perception modules, and from text-centric RL approaches (Video-R1, VideoRFT) that lack explicit grounding, by introducing training-time visual prompting as a transient coaching signal that is internalized via self-distillation. The VP-SELECTOR component draws on the visual prompting literature (red circles, attention overlays, frame numbering) but repurposes it as an input-adaptive, learned selector rather than a fixed augmentation, and the self-distillation mechanism borrows from born-again networks and policy distillation to eliminate prompt dependency at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical contribution is a clean recipe—apply adaptive visual prompts during RL, then distill the improved trajectories— that lets a single 7B model match or exceed much larger proprietary models on spatio-temporal grounding without any inference-time tool calls, which is attractive for deployment where latency and API costs matter. The ablation showing that indiscriminate fixed prompting (e.g., always darkening) actually degrades grounding performance is a useful negative result that underscores the importance of input-adaptive selection. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the four prompt types explored, whether the proxy-reasoner-based data construction pipeline scales to new domains without re-annotation, and whether the modest gains over Open-o3-video (+0.9 mAM, +1.5 mLGM on V-STAR) persist as backbone models grow larger or as reasoning chains become multi-hop.
