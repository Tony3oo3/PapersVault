## Problem

Existing IRSTD methods adopt dense pixel-level segmentation with heavy encoder-decoder architectures, which is structurally mismatched with the task: infrared small targets occupy only a few pixels with Gaussian-like energy diffusion, and practical search-and-tracking systems require reliable centroid localization rather than contour reconstruction. This paradigm incurs three coupled costs—labor-intensive pixel-wise annotation, redundant multi-scale fusion and attention modules to compensate for extreme foreground sparsity, and localization uncertainty introduced by post-processing segmentation masks into centroids.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel infrared image (512×512 for SIRST4, 640×640 for SIRST-UAVB) and produces a single-channel probabilistic response map at 1/4 resolution (stride 4). Target centroid coordinates in the original image space are then extracted via a deterministic peak-extraction pipeline (3×3 NMS, confidence thresholding, top-N selection, and sub-pixel finite-difference refinement), requiring no mask reconstruction or iterative post-processing. Supervision uses only single-point centroid annotations rather than pixel-wise masks, reducing labeling time by approximately 88%.

## Method

SPIRE reformulates IRSTD as centroid-oriented spatial probability regression with two key components. First, Point-Response Prior Supervision (PRPS) converts sparse point labels into structured probabilistic response maps by composing an isotropic Gaussian kernel (σ=2, r=6) encoding PSF-induced energy diffusion with contrast-aware modulation from local image patches, thereby increasing effective supervision density and alleviating gradient starvation under extreme class imbalance. Second, the High-Resolution Probabilistic Encoder (HRPE) is a purely encoder-only, single-branch CNN that maintains high-resolution features at a constant stride of 4, using cascaded lightweight channel-reorganization units (split-process-shuffle with depthwise separable convolutions and squeeze-and-excitation reweighting) instead of multi-scale fusion, decoder pathways, or skip connections. Inference is a single forward pass followed by fast deterministic peak extraction, avoiding mask reconstruction and clustering-based post-processing, yielding 261 FPS and 0.29 GFLOPs while achieving competitive F1 scores with consistently low false alarm rates.

## Results

On SIRST-UAVB, SPIRE achieves the highest Precision (99.82%) and lowest false alarm rate (Fa = 1.02 × 10⁻⁸) among all compared methods, with an F1-score of 97.05%. On SIRST4, SPIRE attains the best F1-score of 94.60% while requiring only 7.68 G FLOPs and 0.29 M parameters—over 8× fewer FLOPs than SCTransNet and 16× fewer parameters than DNANet. Inference speed reaches 261.2 FPS, approximately 3.2×–5.6× faster than the strongest segmentation-based competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SIRST-UAVB, SPIRE achieves the best Precision (99.82%) and lowest Fa (1.02) across all nine baselines, with an F1 of 97.05% that is essentially tied with SCTransNet (97.09%) but at 8.2× fewer FLOPs and 38.6× fewer parameters. On SIRST4, SPIRE leads in F1 (94.60%) over L2SKNet (92.16%), MSHNet (91.47%), and DNANet (87.13%), while also reporting the lowest Fa (28.53) among the top-F1 methods. The efficiency gap is stark: SPIRE's 7.68 G FLOPs and 0.29 M parameters compare to DNANet's 89.13 G / 4.70 M and SCTransNet's 63.22 G / 11.19 M, and its 261.2 FPS inference speed is 3.2×–5.6× faster than DNANet, SCTransNet, and SDSNet respectively. The only metric where SPIRE trails is SIRST-UAVB Recall (94.44% vs. SCTransNet's 95.95% and SDSNet's 96.29%), a small gap that does not affect the F1 ranking. Overall, SPIRE matches or exceeds the strongest segmentation-based methods on detection accuracy while reducing computational cost by an order of magnitude.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPIRE departs from the dominant encoder-decoder segmentation paradigm in IRSTD by repurposing heatmap regression and peak-extraction techniques from pose estimation (HRNet, HigherHRNet) and lightweight channel-reorganization designs from ShuffleNet into a centroid-oriented detection framework. While prior single-point supervision methods (LESPS, MCLC, FromEasy2Hard) reduce annotation cost by generating pseudo-masks for segmentation networks, SPIRE eliminates the decoder entirely and directly regresses probabilistic response maps, treating the task as spatial probability regression rather than dense pixel reconstruction. The Point-Response Prior Supervision mechanism is novel in encoding PSF-induced Gaussian diffusion and local radiometric contrast into the supervision signal, a physical grounding absent from prior pseudo-mask or bounding-box approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: an 87.72% reduction in annotation time, 3–5× faster inference, and 8–16× fewer FLOPs/parameters while maintaining competitive detection accuracy, making IRSTD more accessible for real-time search-and-tracking deployment. The conceptual reframing—that centroid localization, not contour reconstruction, is the first principle of IRSTD—challenges the field's default assumption that dense pixel-level supervision and multi-scale decoder architectures are necessary. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to medium-sized or elongated targets, whether it extends to multi-frame or video-based IRSTD, and whether the two-dataset evaluation (both relatively small) fully validates the claimed robustness across diverse operational conditions.
