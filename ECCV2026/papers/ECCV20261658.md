## Problem

Existing 4D scene editing methods rely on image diffusion models (e.g., InstructPix2Pix) applied frame-by-frame with explicit temporal consistency enforcement, which yields uniform, appearance-only edits with limited control over geometry or motion. In the monocular video setting, fitting dynamic 3D Gaussians with pure photometric loss is under-constrained, producing shallow geometric reconstructions and temporal artifacts that are further exacerbated during editing. The paper addresses both limitations by leveraging a video diffusion model for true motion-appearance editing and introducing semantic supervision to stabilize the 4D representation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video sequence, a text editing prompt, and optionally a 2D mask (from SAM2) or reference image for localized editing. Camera poses and an initial point cloud are estimated via Vipe rather than relying on ground-truth annotations. The output is an edited 4D scene represented as deformable 3D Gaussians augmented with 16-dimensional DINO-derived latent features per Gaussian, from which photorealistic novel-view renderings can be produced at real-time rates. The edited video is generated by VACE (a video diffusion model) and serves as the supervision signal for target scene optimization.

## Method

MotionEditGS builds on Deformable 3DGS and introduces three coupled components. First, semantic feature guided reconstruction jointly optimizes RGB and DINO-derived latent rendering losses, where DINOv3 features are compressed to 16-dim latents via a frozen MLP autoencoder and each Gaussian carries an additional latent attribute, resolving monocular geometric and motion ambiguities. Second, semantically anchored editing uses the VACE-edited video as supervision but freezes the source latent features (f_i*) and updates only geometric/appearance parameters (θ_i), forcing edits to manifest through coherent motion and geometry changes rather than unstable semantic reassignment. Third, gradient-based feature lifting accumulates per-Gaussian feature-loss gradients, and before each densification step, high-gradient Gaussians are projected to camera planes, assigned average latent features from the edited video at those pixels, and cloned—injecting novel semantic content that the frozen source latents cannot represent.

## Results

On DyCheck-iPhone, MotionEditGS achieves the best reconstruction-based scores across all metrics: LOVE-Perceptual 0.456/0.393 (train/novel), LOVE-Correspondence 0.553/0.530, CLIP 31.26/27.85, and VBench Consistency 0.950/0.907, outperforming IN4D and CTRL-D on every metric. On DAVIS, it leads on LOVE-Perceptual (0.629/0.557) and CLIP (28.16/26.76), while CTRL-D retains a small edge on train-view Consistency (0.962 vs. 0.929). A notable strength is minimal performance decay from train to novel views (e.g., LOVE-Correspondence drops only 0.023 on DyCheck vs. 0.093 for IN4D), confirming superior geometric stability. Editing time is 45 min on 1 GPU, comparable to the RGB-only baseline (40 min) and far faster than IN4D (1.5 h, 2 GPUs) or CTRL-D (2 h, 1 GPU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IN4D (Instruct-4D-to-4D), MotionEditGS wins on every metric on both datasets, with the largest gaps on novel-view LOVE-Correspondence (0.530 vs. 0.428 on DyCheck; 0.580 vs. 0.306 on DAVIS) and CLIP (31.26 vs. 29.29 on DyCheck; 28.16 vs. 26.60 on DAVIS), reflecting IN4D's reliance on optical-flow warping that degrades under complex motion edits. Against CTRL-D, MotionEditGS leads on DyCheck across all four metrics (e.g., CLIP 31.26 vs. 29.86, LOVE-Correspondence novel 0.530 vs. 0.467) and on DAVIS for LOVE-Perceptual (0.629 vs. 0.501) and CLIP (28.16 vs. 27.61), though CTRL-D retains a marginal advantage on DAVIS train-view Consistency (0.962 vs. 0.929). VACE, used as the underlying video editing model, serves as a non-reconstruction upper bound: it slightly exceeds MotionEditGS on train-view LOVE-Perceptual and CLIP on both datasets (e.g., 0.504 vs. 0.456 on DyCheck), but cannot produce novel-view renderings, so it is not a direct competitor for 4D scene editing. The consistent pattern—MotionEditGS closing most of the gap to VACE on train views while maintaining strong novel-view fidelity—indicates that the semantically anchored reconstruction faithfully recovers the edited video's content into a geometrically coherent 4D representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Deformable 3DGS as the dynamic scene representation and borrows the DINO-feature autoencoder compression strategy from LangSplat, but departs fundamentally from prior 4D editing pipelines (IN4D, CTRL-D) that rely on image diffusion models (IP2P) with iterative dataset updates. The key conceptual shift is replacing per-frame image editing with a video diffusion model (VACE), which inherently preserves temporal consistency and enables text-driven motion control, while introducing semantically anchored editing—freezing DINO-derived latent features as identity constraints during optimization—to prevent the semantic drift that plagues monocular 4D fitting. The gradient-based feature lifting mechanism, inspired by standard Gaussian densification gradients, is a novel repurposing of that machinery to inject new semantic content rather than just split/clone geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that true 4D editing (simultaneous motion and appearance modification) from monocular video is achievable with a video diffusion model, a capability that image-diffusion-based pipelines structurally cannot provide. The semantically anchored editing principle—using frozen semantic latents as optimization constraints rather than targets—is a clean, generalizable idea that could transfer to other dynamic representation editing tasks. Limitations include evaluation on only two object-centric datasets, dependence on a single video diffusion model (VACE), and the fact that motion edits are bounded by what the video model can generate; scaling to longer sequences, multi-object scenes, or more diverse video models remains open.
