## Problem

Existing fine-tuning-based stylized video generation methods suffer from two coupled failures: motion degradation caused by training on static image stylization datasets that erode the temporal priors of pre-trained video diffusion backbones, and a representation bottleneck from encoder-based style injection that compresses fine-grained stylistic statistics and caps cross-style generalization. No prior approach simultaneously addresses both the data-level lack of temporal supervision and the architectural-level information loss in style conditioning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference style image (arbitrary resolution, encoded into a clean latent at timestep 0 and patchified into tokens) and a free-form text prompt, and produces a stylized video at 480×832 resolution using the Wan-T2V 1.3B backbone. The style image is consumed at full spatial resolution as concatenated tokens rather than through a compressed encoder embedding, and the text prompt is expected to describe content and motion without specifying artistic style, which is controlled entirely by the reference image.

## Method

The pipeline has three components. First, MoStyle-5K is built via a decoupled content-motion synthesis pipeline: an LLM splits each prompt into a static caption and a dynamic motion description, Qwen-Image generates a stylized anchor image from the static caption plus style reference, and an image-to-video model extends it into a temporally coherent video, yielding 5,000 curated videos across 27 styles and 4,000 preference pairs. Second, Implicit Gated Style Token Injection (IGST) bypasses the encoder bottleneck by directly concatenating style-image patch tokens with video latent tokens in the DiT transformer input; it disables rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) for style tokens to remove spatial alignment bias, and applies per-head sigmoid gates on style-token keys to suppress attention heads that carry structural layout cues. Third, training proceeds in three stages: an image-level warm-up for style statistics, a hybrid image-video mixed fine-tuning that balances spatial style fidelity with temporal motion supervision, and a Diffusion-DPO post-training stage that uses the structured preference pairs to penalize structural artifacts and style degradation.

## Results

On 462 prompt-style pairs, the proposed method achieves the best style fidelity (CSD 0.5219), motion dynamics (DD 0.8247), and temporal smoothness (DS 0.9912) among all compared methods. The DD score is approximately 2.8× that of StyleCrafter (0.2922) and 4.8× that of StyleMaster (0.1710), while CSD exceeds the next-best VideoComposer by 0.0263. In the blind human preference study, the method attains an overall preference score of 0.5993, nearly 3× that of StyleMaster (0.2051), with leading scores on Text Alignment (0.6061), Motion Dynamics (0.6103), and Stylization Effect (0.5815).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against StyleMaster [50], the proposed method wins decisively on style fidelity (CSD 0.5219 vs. 0.3039, +0.2180) and motion dynamics (DD 0.8247 vs. 0.1710, +0.6537), while StyleMaster retains a narrow edge on text alignment (0.3054 vs. 0.3010), image quality (0.6735 vs. 0.6568), and visual quality (0.6451 vs. 0.6071). Against StyleCrafter [21], the method outperforms on every metric, with the largest gaps in DD (0.8247 vs. 0.2922) and CSD (0.5219 vs. 0.4414). Against VideoComposer [41], the method leads on all six metrics, most notably in text alignment (0.3010 vs. 0.1840) and DD (0.8247 vs. 0.3312), confirming that VideoComposer's tendency to copy reference layout undermines prompt controllability. In the human preference study, the method dominates all three baselines across TA, MD, SE, and Overall, with the largest margin over StyleMaster (0.5993 vs. 0.2051 overall). The slight IQ/VQ deficit relative to StyleMaster is attributed to automatic perceptual metrics favoring low-motion or near-static outputs, a gap not reflected in human ratings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant encoder-based style conditioning paradigm (Q-Former in StyleCrafter, dual-path extractors in StyleMaster) by adopting an implicit conditioning strategy: style image patches are directly concatenated as tokens in the DiT's self-attention space, bypassing any intermediate compression. It builds on the Wan-T2V 1.3B backbone and the DiT architecture, and incorporates Diffusion-DPO as a post-training alignment stage. The key conceptual shift is twofold: replacing static image stylization datasets with a purpose-built video dataset (MoStyle-5K) that provides explicit temporal supervision, and replacing explicit feature extraction with direct token-level interaction regulated by RoPE disabling and head-wise gated attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two well-identified failure modes in stylized video generation—motion degradation from static training data and style fidelity loss from encoder compression—with a unified pipeline that is practical and reproducible. The MoStyle-5K dataset (5,000 videos, 27 styles, 4,000 preference pairs) is a useful resource for the community, and the IGST mechanism (direct token concatenation + RoPE gating + head-wise gates) is a clean, generalizable architectural pattern that could extend to other reference-conditioned generation tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos or more diverse style spaces, whether the 480×832 resolution constraint limits real-world applicability, and whether the slight IQ/VQ trade-off relative to StyleMaster persists at higher resolutions or with perceptual metrics less biased toward static content.
