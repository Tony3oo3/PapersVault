## Problem

Existing LLM-based layout agents translate 3D assets and scene boundaries into textual descriptions or rendered 2D images before feeding them to a language model, causing irreversible information loss that prevents the model from perceiving fine-grained geometry such as concave structures, supporting surfaces, and irregular room shapes. On the output side, representing continuous poses as sequences of discrete text tokens destroys numerical integrity, leading to physically implausible placements like collisions, floating objects, and out-of-boundary items.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261700/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a text prompt describing the desired scene, a point cloud of the empty room (walls, floor, ceiling) encoded by SPFormer, and point clouds of each candidate asset encoded by PointBERT; both are compressed via Q-Formers into prefix tokens in the LLM's embedding space. The output is an autoregressive sequence where each asset is identified by a unique ID token followed by four discrete pose-anchoring tokens (three position bins and one rotation bin) and a single pose-residual token whose hidden state is decoded by a lightweight head into continuous (ΔP, S, ΔO) values, yielding the final pose as the sum of the coarse bin center and the fine residual.

## Method

NaLA uses Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct as a frozen backbone with only LoRA modules, geometric projection adapters, and a pose decoder head trainable, bridging 3D geometry to language via SPFormer (scene) and PointBERT (asset) encoders followed by Q-Formers. The key architectural contribution is a coarse-to-fine output mechanism: the LLM first emits four discrete anchoring tokens that select a voxel-grid cell (B=20 bins per axis) and a coarse orientation, then emits a single <POS_TOKEN> whose last-L-layer hidden states are decoded by a two-layer Transformer plus MLP to regress continuous position/scale/orientation residuals, avoiding both the numerical blindness of text-tokenized coordinates and the cross-asset confusion that arises from reusing a single special token. Training follows a two-stage curriculum—pretraining on 3D-FRONT for macro-level arrangement rules, then fine-tuning on Imaginarium for micro-level detail—with aggressive augmentation including asset shuffling, 90° scene rotations, same-class asset swapping, and random omission of text descriptions to force reliance on point-cloud geometry.

## Results

NaLA achieves the best overall mean scores across all evaluation dimensions: 3.53 (AI) and 3.69 (Human) on the 1–5 Likert scale, outperforming LayoutGPT (2.72/2.36), Holodeck (3.13/3.30), and LayoutVLM (3.24/3.47). On physical plausibility, NaLA attains a collision rate of 0.86%, OOB rate of 1.16%, and floating rate of 3.36% in a single forward pass, compared to LayoutGPT's 2.29%/18.19%/15.12%. NaLA leads all baselines on semantic plausibility (AI 3.32, Human 3.48) and visual aesthetics (AI 3.05, Human 3.42), while Holodeck achieves slightly lower collision (0.10%) and zero floating due to its iterative post-optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NaLA decisively outperforms LayoutGPT across every metric, with the most dramatic gap in OOB rate (1.16% vs. 18.19%) and floating rate (3.36% vs. 15.12%), reflecting LayoutGPT's inability to perceive scene boundaries from text alone. Against Holodeck, NaLA trades a small amount of physical precision (collision 0.86% vs. 0.10%; floating 3.36% vs. 0.00%) for substantially better semantic plausibility (AI 3.32 vs. 2.68; Human 3.48 vs. 3.03) and aesthetics (AI 3.05 vs. 2.44; Human 3.42 vs. 2.94), while also operating in a single forward pass rather than Holodeck's iterative optimization loop. Compared to LayoutVLM, NaLA wins on OOB (1.16% vs. 5.19%), semantic scores, and aesthetics, with comparable collision rates. On irregular (non-rectangular) rooms, NaLA reduces OOB to near-zero while LayoutGPT still exhibits significant boundary violations, and Holodeck/LayoutVLM are excluded because their optimizers assume box-shaped templates. In inference efficiency, NaLA requires only five tokens per asset pose and scales linearly, whereas LayoutVLM's repeated optimization iterations make it the slowest baseline as asset count grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

NaLA departs from the dominant paradigm in LLM-based layout agents (LayoutGPT, Holodeck, LayoutVLM, LLplace) that represent 3D geometry through textual descriptions or 2D renderings, introducing an irreversible modality gap at both input and output stages. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it repurposes existing 3D encoders (PointBERT for assets, SPFormer for scene boundaries) and Q-Former adapters to inject raw point-cloud features directly into a frozen LLM (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct), while the coarse-to-fine output mechanism (pose anchoring tokens + a single regression token) draws a conceptual line from LISA's special-token decoding but explicitly rejects it as insufficient for multi-object autoregressive generation. The two-stage training curriculum on 3D-FRONT then Imaginarium follows a standard coarse-to-fine transfer-learning recipe, but the data augmentation strategies (asset shuffling, rotation, replacement, text dropping) are tailored to prevent the model from exploiting textual shortcuts over geometric perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the text intermediary, NaLA demonstrates that an LLM can perceive fine-grained 3D geometry (concave structures, irregular room boundaries, asymmetric assets) and produce physically plausible layouts in a single forward pass without iterative optimization, achieving a 1.16% out-of-boundary rate on irregular scenes versus 18.19% for LayoutGPT. The practical efficiency gain—five tokens per asset versus repeated optimization loops—makes end-to-end 3D layout generation substantially faster and more scalable. However, the paper acknowledges that physical plausibility (collision, floating) still lags slightly behind optimization-based methods like Holodeck, and the training scale remains limited by the scarcity of high-quality 3D layout datasets. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or dynamic scenes, whether larger base models or more diverse training data close the remaining physical-plausibility gap, and whether the coarse-to-fine token design transfers to other continuous-output LLM tasks beyond layout.
