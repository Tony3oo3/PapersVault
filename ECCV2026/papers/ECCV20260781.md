## Problem

Existing trajectory prediction models trained on fixed, normal-driving datasets converge to a single conservative trajectory and cannot adapt to diverse user personas. Prior persona-conditioned work supports only a one-dimensional urgency spectrum (emergency/normal/relaxed), which erases critical distinctions between personas sharing the same urgency level but requiring different driving dynamics—e.g., a paramedic needing smooth, jerk-free motion versus a firefighter tolerating aggressive maneuvering. The paper addresses this by decomposing driving personas into two orthogonal axes (Temporal Urgency and Ride Comfort) and providing both a dataset and a controllable prediction framework for the resulting 3×3 grid of nine personas.

## Input / Output

The method takes three cropped forward-view camera images stitched into a 1024×256 frame, a BEV raster from LiDAR, and a natural-language persona description (framed as a passenger request to an autonomous taxi) encoded by a frozen MiniLM. It outputs an 8-waypoint ego-vehicle trajectory over a 4-second horizon. Unlike one-hot persona labels, the text input preserves similarity structure across the persona grid—descriptions sharing an axis value are closer in embedding space—which the architecture exploits for axis-aligned modulation.

## Method

PersonaDrive builds on DiffusionDrive (truncated diffusion, ResNet-34 backbone) and adds two persona-injection modules plus two dedicated losses. PCAT hierarchically modulates the trajectory anchor set: an urgency branch produces a global scale scalar α_u, and a comfort branch produces a per-timestep vector α_c bounded to [0.8, 1.2], applied sequentially to reshape anchors along each axis. PCMF fuses BEV, ego, text, and transformed-anchor queries through a Query Prototype Pool, a Conditional Resampler with learnable slots, and a scene-conditioned Source Gate to produce persona-aware BEV features. Training is supervised by a Hierarchical Guide Loss that enforces axis-aligned physical orderings (higher urgency → greater trajectory length; lower comfort → higher jerk) via 12 margin constraints, and an Axis-Decomposed Diversity Loss that uses intra-axis and inter-axis KL-margin terms to prevent diagonal mode collapse. The PCT dataset, generated via a four-stage GPT-4o-mini pipeline with rule-based and LLM-as-a-Judge validation, provides nine persona-conditioned trajectory-text pairs for every OpenScene scene.

## Results

On the NAVSIM navtest split, PersonaDrive achieves the best average ADE of 2.39 m and FDE of 3.71 m across all nine persona categories, reducing ADE by 3.2% and FDE by 3.1% over the second-best method (UniAD: 2.47 / 3.83). It also attains the highest aggregate PDMS of 57.7, surpassing DiffusionDrive (55.3) and Traj-LLM (54.1). The method wins ADE and FDE in all nine individual persona cells, with the largest margins appearing at against-the-grain cells (e.g., UH-CH, UL-CL) where urgency and comfort impose opposing demands.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PersonaDrive outperforms all ten baselines on both ADE and FDE. The closest competitor on trajectory accuracy is UniAD (ADE 2.47, FDE 3.83), which PersonaDrive beats by 0.08 m and 0.12 m respectively; LTF (2.49/3.87) and Transfuser (2.53/3.96) follow. On PDMS, DiffusionDrive (55.3) and Traj-LLM (54.1) are the nearest, with PersonaDrive leading by 2.4 and 3.6 points. The diffusion-based and vectorized planners (Hydra-MDP, VADv2, PARA-Drive, VisionTRAP) show substantially worse ADE/FDE (4.8–6.7 m), indicating that their architectures struggle with persona-conditional trajectory fidelity. The paper further shows that one-dimensional persona proxies (1D-Urgency, 1D-Comfort) degrade to ADE 2.67 and 4.50 respectively, confirming that the multi-dimensional formulation is necessary for the against-the-grain cells where both axes must be jointly resolved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DiffusionDrive (CVPR 2025) as its trajectory prediction backbone and extends the persona-conditioned planning line of work that previously operated on a single urgency axis (emergency/normal/relaxed). It departs from that 1D formulation by decomposing driving persona into two orthogonal axes—Temporal Urgency and Ride Comfort—yielding a 3×3 grid of nine categories, and introduces the PCT dataset constructed via a four-stage LLM-as-a-Judge pipeline on OpenScene. Architecturally, the PCAT module (hierarchical anchor scaling) and PCMF module (query-conditioned multi-source fusion) are novel contributions that inject text-derived persona signals into both the anchor priors and BEV features, while the Hierarchical Guide Loss and Axis-Decomposed Diversity Loss provide axis-aligned structural supervision absent from prior persona-conditioned planners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PCT dataset and the two-axis persona formulation provide the first systematic benchmark for multi-dimensional driving-style control, filling a gap where prior work could not distinguish, e.g., an urgent-but-gentle paramedic from an urgent-and-aggressive firefighter. The finding that a 33M-parameter sentence-embedding model (MiniLM) substantially outperforms 125–140M token-level encoders (RoBERTa, DeBERTa)—and that the latter actually degrade below the no-persona baseline—suggests that embedding geometry (row/column proximity) matters more than raw capacity for axis-decomposed conditioning, a lesson likely transferable to other structured text-conditioned tasks. Open questions include validation in reactive simulators, extension to more than two persona axes, and whether the LLM-generated ground-truth trajectories generalize to real human driving distributions beyond the GPT-family generation pipeline.
