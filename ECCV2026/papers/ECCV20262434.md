## Problem

Aerial-view object detection benchmarks are inherently imbalanced, with certain scene types and environmental conditions overrepresented while others are scarce or entangled with confounding factors, making it difficult to isolate systematic detector failures under specific conditions. While foundation generative models are widely used for data augmentation, their potential as controlled diagnostic instruments for analyzing trained vision systems in the aerial and remote sensing domain remains unexplored.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes uniformly sampled attribute tuples (scene type, season, weather, vehicle count/color/type) from a predefined taxonomy, which an LLM converts into aerial-view text prompts for Imagen 3. Generated 1024 px images are downscaled to 384 px to match the 12.5 cm/px ground sampling distance of the real datasets. The output is a fully synthetic, attribute-annotated diagnostic set of 5,453 images (304 unique environmental combinations) with vehicle bounding boxes obtained via zero-shot VLM detection (Moondream 2 + Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite) refined by human-in-the-loop verification, yielding attribute-conditioned AP scores and a ranked list of weak scene categories that guide targeted real-data collection.

## Method

The framework is a two-stage synthetic generation pipeline followed by attribute-conditioned evaluation. In stage 1, attribute tuples are uniformly sampled from a taxonomy, converted to prompts by an LLM, and rendered by Imagen 3; a multimodal LLM then extracts per-image attributes, which are validated against the taxonomy via Sentence-BERT cosine similarity (swapping to the nearest taxonomy value above a threshold or adding new values). In stage 2, image-and-text-to-image editing corrects distributional imbalances by selectively modifying one underrepresented attribute while preserving all others, producing a complementary edited set. Annotations are obtained by ensembling zero-shot detections from two VLMs, merging overlapping boxes, and applying binary human approval (~13× faster than manual annotation). Diagnosis partitions the synthetic set by each attribute value and evaluates three pretrained detectors (Faster R-CNN R50, YOLOv8-M, ViTDet-B) independently on each subset, exposing scene-specific AP drops. Guided by the identified weak categories, small real datasets (2,000–2,284 images each) from those scenes are added to the original training set, yielding up to 13.4% AP50 improvement with an order-of-magnitude fewer samples than non-targeted augmentation.

## Results

Across nine model-dataset combinations (3 detectors × 3 training datasets), targeted real-data supplementation guided by synthetic diagnostics improves AP50 in 8 of 9 cases, with gains ranging from +0.9% to +13.4%. The largest improvements are observed for ViTDet-B trained on UGRC (+13.4%) and Faster R-CNN R50 trained on UGRC (+12.6%). Synthetic scene-wise evaluation identifies urban as the most consistent failure mode (below overall AP in all 9 tests), followed by industrial (7/9) and desert (2/9). Targeted supplementation with as few as 2,000–2,284 real images per scene type—over an order of magnitude smaller than the original training sets—yields these gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other diagnostic frameworks; instead, the key comparison is between targeted (diagnostic-guided) supplementation and random (non-targeted) supplementation under a matched data budget (Fig. 9). Targeted supplementation consistently outperforms random sampling across all three architectures. For Faster R-CNN and YOLOv8, injecting randomly sampled data (from LINZ or COCO) actually degrades AP50 across most scene types, indicating domain interference from uninformative samples. In contrast, the same data budget directed at identified weak scenes yields large positive gains, e.g., up to +11.7% AP50 for YOLOv8 in urban scenes. ViTDet-B shows greater baseline robustness to random data shifts (smaller negative gains) but still achieves superior peak improvements under the targeted protocol. The only case with no improvement is Faster R-CNN on DOTAv2 (+0.9%), likely because DOTAv2 already contains substantial urban coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes foundation generative models (Imagen 3) as diagnostic instruments for aerial-view detectors, a role previously unexplored in remote sensing. It extends the model-diagnosis lineage (Hoiem et al., TIDE) from post-hoc error decomposition on real data to controlled, attribute-isolated causal probing via synthetic generation, and builds on the synthetic-benchmarking tradition (game engines, diffusion-based datasets) by adding fine-grained attribute controllability and MLLM-verified annotation that prior 3D-consistent methods (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) cannot provide. The targeted-supplementation strategy connects to domain-adaptation work (DA Faster, SWDA, UMT) but reframes the problem: rather than adapting the model to a new domain, it identifies *which* sub-domains to collect data for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, low-cost mechanism for identifying where aerial detectors will fail in deployment and where limited real-data collection will yield the highest return (up to 13% AP50 with datasets an order of magnitude smaller than the originals). Key limitations include reliance on proprietary generative and multimodal models (reproducibility risk), a single task (vehicle detection in RGB nadir imagery), and diagnosis primarily along one attribute axis (scene type) without cross-factor interaction analysis. Open questions include extending the diagnostic loop to SAR/thermal/multispectral modalities, validating with open-source generative models as they mature, and studying whether the synthetic-to-real transfer holds for non-vehicle object categories or non-aerial viewpoints.
