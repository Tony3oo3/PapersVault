## Problem

Physics-based inverse rendering is severely underconstrained: without strong priors, illumination bakes into materials and reconstructions fail under novel lighting. Data-driven diffusion models predict visually plausible materials but their outputs do not satisfy the rendering equation and cannot be directly used for physics-based rendering. The paper addresses the gap between these two paradigms by exploiting the spatial consistency in diffusion predictions without treating them as ground-truth targets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260305/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N calibrated multi-view RGB images of a static object under unknown illumination and produces a renderer-ready PBR asset: a triangle mesh (extracted from a voxel-grid SDF via Marching Cubes), spatially varying Disney BRDF parameters (base color, metallic, roughness) stored in Dictionary Fields, and a spherical environment map. All outputs are standard graphics-pipeline-compatible representations usable directly in Mitsuba or similar engines, rather than neural radiance fields or Gaussian splats.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages: (1) a conditional diffusion model (DiffusionRenderer) predicts per-view intrinsic G-buffers (albedo, roughness, metallic, normal); (2) a neural SDF is reconstructed via volume rendering with a Huber-penalized normal-supervision loss; (3) shape, materials, and illumination are jointly optimized by differentiable rendering minimizing a relative-MSE photometric loss plus a material clustering regularizer. The key contribution is the regularizer: the diffusion-predicted G-buffer serves as a guidance image for a joint bilateral filter applied to the rendered G-buffer, defining a Gaussian similarity kernel k(p,q)=exp(−‖g_p−g_q‖²/2σ_g²) that penalizes L1 deviations between the rendered material and its kernel-weighted average, effectively sharing observations across same-material regions without explicit segmentation. A scale-agnostic albedo transformation ψ(Â)=sg(Â)⊙log([Â]_ε) is applied before regularization to prevent the optimizer from trading albedo scale against lighting intensity. The bilateral filter is implemented differentiably via permutohedral lattices, and the entire PBIR stage runs in Mitsuba 3 with 900 iterations of 6 random views per step.

## Results

On the real-world Stanford-ORB benchmark, the proposed method achieves 27.22 PSNR-H and 34.98 PSNR-L for novel-scene relighting, outperforming Neural-PBIR (26.07 / 33.39) by +1.15 / +1.59 dB and MaterialFusion (23.52 / 31.18) by +3.70 / +3.80 dB. On Synthetic4Relight, relighting PSNR reaches 32.02 vs. 27.83 (Neural-PBIR) and 20.20 (MaterialFusion), while on DTC-Synthetic it reaches 43.21 PSNR vs. 39.18 and 28.63 respectively. Geometry reconstruction also improves substantially: normal error drops to 0.014 (vs. 0.061 for Neural-PBIR) and shape error to 0.30 (vs. 0.42 and 1.08).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Neural-PBIR (the prior state-of-the-art on Stanford-ORB), the proposed method wins on every metric: +1.15 dB PSNR-H and +1.59 dB PSNR-L for relighting, with a 4.3× reduction in normal error (0.014 vs. 0.061) and a 28% reduction in shape error. Against MaterialFusion, the margin is much larger—+3.70 dB PSNR-H on Stanford-ORB and +11.82 dB on Synthetic4Relight—because MaterialFusion's score-distillation approach underfits spatially varying color patterns and produces over-smoothed materials. On DTC-Synthetic (glossy objects with strong cast shadows), the gap widens further: 43.21 vs. 39.18 PSNR (Neural-PBIR) and 43.21 vs. 28.63 PSNR (MaterialFusion). In the extended Stanford-ORB comparison (Table 3b), the method also outperforms NVDiffRecMC (24.43), R3DG (23.40), Material Anything (24.46), Neural Gaffer (24.32), IllumiNeRF (25.56), and vanilla Mitsuba (25.62) by 1.6–3.8 dB PSNR-H, confirming that the diffusion-guided material regularizer is the key differentiator over both pure physics-based and pure data-driven approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of physics-based inverse rendering (analysis by synthesis, as in Neural-PBIR and Mitsuba-based optimization) and data-driven material prediction (DiffusionRenderer, RGB↔X, MaterialFusion). Its key conceptual departure is treating diffusion-predicted G-buffers not as target values or score-distillation guidance (as MaterialFusion does) but as a similarity kernel for a joint bilateral filter regularizer, drawing on the reflectance-sharing and JBF literature. The pipeline structure follows Neural-PBIR (SDF shape reconstruction followed by differentiable PBIR with Dictionary Fields parameterization), while the regularization mechanism repurposes permutohedral-lattice JBF in a way not previously applied to jointly optimize geometry, materials, and illumination under unknown lighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a clean, lightweight regularizer—using diffusion predictions as a soft grouping signal rather than a hard target—can substantially reduce material-lighting ambiguity and baking artifacts while preserving the physical accuracy of differentiable rendering, yielding exportable PBR assets (mesh + Disney BRDF + environment map) that integrate directly into standard rendering engines. Limitations include dependence on DiffusionRenderer's local consistency assumption (which can over-regularize rare appearances), limited prediction resolution leaving high-frequency details underconstrained, and residual artifacts near shadow boundaries attributed to imperfect geometry. Open questions include whether higher-resolution diffusion models or alternative kernel constructions (beyond concatenated G-buffer channels) would further improve results, and whether the regularization signal could be leveraged to refine shape near cast-shadow boundaries.
