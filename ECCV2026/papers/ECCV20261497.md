## Problem

Existing end-to-end generative IMVC methods rely on diffusion models that recover missing views via stochastic noise-to-data trajectories initialized from cluster-agnostic Gaussian noise. This design is misaligned with clustering objectives because the denoising process traverses latent regions unrelated to cluster structure and only reveals discriminative information in the low-noise regime, making finite-step inference prone to cross-cluster drift. The paper asks whether a deterministic transport mechanism can replace stochastic diffusion while preserving cluster geometry throughout the completion process.

## Input / Output

The method receives raw feature vectors from the available views of each sample (e.g., GoogLeNet image features and doc2vec text features on CUB, PHOG/LBP descriptors on LandUse-21, or two visual feature views on Fashion), along with a binary mask indicating which view is missing. It outputs a cluster assignment per sample and a completed latent representation for the missing view, obtained by integrating the learned vector field in a single ODE step. The framework is designed for the two-view setting but the loss structure generalizes to more views.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces diffusion with straight-path flow matching: a linear interpolation x_t = (1−t)z₁ + t·z₂ is constructed between paired latent representations of observed and missing views, and a neural vector field F(x_t, t) is trained to match the constant target velocity z₂ − z₁, yielding a deterministic ODE that transports the observed representation directly to its missing-view counterpart. Two theoretical properties are established: (i) the path length equals ‖z₂−z₁‖ and shrinks adaptively as views align (shortcut-path property), and (ii) cluster separability holds at any finite number of integration steps without the asymptotic σ_t < δ/2 condition required by diffusion. The full architecture combines view-specific autoencoders for reconstruction, an InfoNCE-based cluster-level contrastive loss for cross-view assignment consistency, an entropy-weighted KL alignment in centered log-ratio space for probability-distribution stability, and the flow-matching completion losses (velocity prediction plus bidirectional endpoint consistency), all optimized jointly in a single end-to-end objective.

## Results

On five IMVC benchmarks at missing rate τ=0.3, the proposed method achieves ACC of 91.17 (Synthetic3D), 82.67 (CUB), 86.50 (HandWritten), 28.19 (LandUse-21), and 93.66 (Fashion), consistently outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baseline DCG by 3.17, 5.50, 3.80, 0.86, and 0.53 ACC points respectively. At the most challenging setting (τ=0.5), the method maintains ACC of 88.17 on Synthetic3D and 83.40 on HandWritten, while most baselines degrade substantially (e.g., DCG drops to 87.67 and 80.80). The NMI and ARI gains follow the same pattern, with the largest ARI improvement of +3.08 over DCG on Synthetic3D at τ=0.1 (83.51 vs 76.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and missing rate for at least one metric, and achieves the best ACC on all 15 dataset–missing-rate combinations. The closest competitor is DCG [53] (diffusion-based end-to-end IMVC), which the method surpasses by 2.9–5.5 ACC points on Synthetic3D, CUB, and HandWritten, and by 0.2–1.6 points on Fashion and LandUse-21. CPSPAN [12] and ICMVC [4] are the next strongest baselines on HandWritten (ACC 82.20 and 82.27 at τ=0.3) but trail the proposed method by 4.3 and 4.2 ACC points respectively. ProImp [17] is competitive on Fashion at τ=0.1 (ACC 92.88) but degrades sharply at higher missing rates (74.11 at τ=0.3), whereas the proposed method remains stable. Weaker baselines including DCP [20], DSIMVC [33], GCFAGG [48], APADC [46], and DVIMVC [45] are consistently 5–30 ACC points below the proposed method, with DVIMVC [45] performing near chance level on Synthetic3D and HandWritten.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of flow matching (Lipman et al. 2022; Liu et al. 2022) and end-to-end incomplete multi-view clustering, most directly extending the diffusion-based IMVC paradigm of DCG (Zhang et al., AAAI 2025) by replacing its stochastic SDE denoising module with a deterministic straight-path ODE transport. The broader IMVC literature—spanning autoencoder reconstruction, contrastive alignment, and graph propagation—provides the alignment and representation-learning scaffolding, while the flow-matching contribution is specifically the view-completion mechanism. The key conceptual departure from prior generative IMVC work is the argument that cluster-agnostic Gaussian noise initialization (inherent to diffusion) is structurally misaligned with clustering objectives, motivating a transport anchored at the observed representation instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finite-step separability result (Corollary 2) is practically significant: it justifies single-step inference for view completion in a clustering context, reducing computational cost while provably avoiding cross-cluster drift that plagues finite-step diffusion. The shortcut-path property (Corollary 1) means the transport cost adapts as cross-view representations align during training, which is a natural but previously unexploited coupling between generative and clustering objectives. Open questions include whether the two-view formulation and its theoretical assumptions (compact cluster sets, strict view consistency) extend cleanly to three or more views, and whether the approach generalizes beyond clustering to other downstream tasks where deterministic transport between paired representations is beneficial.
