## Problem

Existing visual unlearning methods in Vertical Federated Learning certify forgetting solely through output-level metrics (retained accuracy, forgotten-label accuracy), assuming that suppressing classifier predictions implies erasing underlying information. This paper challenges that assumption by asking whether the internal feature geometry of unlearned models still retains linearly recoverable class structure, even when output behavior appears to have forgotten the target.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260043/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Mirage is a post-hoc auditing framework that consumes frozen embeddings from the unlearned model (penultimate layer by default, plus early and intermediate layers for the layer-wise diagnostic), the forgotten label set, and a retrained-from-scratch baseline trained on retained data. It produces four complementary scores: a Linear Probe Recovery (LPR) percentage and its retraining-relative gap Δ_LPR, a CKA similarity between the unlearned and original/retrained representations (computed on 5000 randomly sampled points), a Fisher-inspired feature separability score, and a layer-wise Δ_LPR profile. The LPR probe is a logistic regression with ℓ₂ regularization (C=1.0) trained for 1000 iterations, making the framework model-agnostic and requiring no retraining of the unlearned model itself.

## Method

Mirage certifies representation-level forgetting by comparing the unlearned model Θ^u against a retrained-from-scratch baseline Θ^r across four complementary diagnostics. First, Linear Probe Recovery trains a linear classifier on frozen embeddings to predict forgotten-label membership, and the forgetting gap Δ_LPR = LPR(Θ^u) − LPR(Θ^r) quantifies residual class structure beyond what intrinsic visual geometry would naturally produce. Second, Centered Kernel Alignment measures whether the unlearned model's global embedding geometry has shifted toward the retrained reference or remains aligned with the original model. Third, a Fisher-inspired separability score F = ‖μ_u − μ_r‖² / (tr(Σ_u) + tr(Σ_r)) provides a probe-independent geometric measure of class discrimination. Fourth, layer-wise Δ_LPR profiles reveal whether residual information localizes to specific depths or persists across the network. The key design choice is the retraining-relative criterion: because visual representations inherently encode class structure (Retrain baselines show 54–83% LPR), absolute probe accuracy is uninformative, and only the excess over the retrained baseline constitutes evidence of incomplete erasure.

## Results

Across seven datasets and seven unlearning methods, Mirage reveals that no method simultaneously achieves high retained accuracy, low forgotten-label accuracy, and representation-level erasure (the unlearning trilemma). Boundary Unlearning (BU) achieves y_u ≈ 0% on most datasets yet retains a positive forgetting gap (Δ_LPR) of +4.0 to +15.4 points above the retrained baseline, with LPR reaching 94.7% on COVID-19 vs. 79.2% for Retrain. Fine-Tuning and SSD show the largest positive gaps (up to +17.3 on COVID-19) with near-unity CKA to the original model, confirming near-complete preservation of feature geometry. In contrast, sample-level unlearning (5–10% of samples) yields LPR ≈ 50% for all methods including Retrain, indicating that individual sample membership is not linearly recoverable from penultimate-layer embeddings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mirage is an auditing framework rather than a competing unlearning method, so the 'comparison' reveals which baselines pass or fail representation-level certification. Boundary Unlearning (BU) [7] is the only method achieving near-zero y_u while preserving reasonable Acc_r, yet it consistently falls in the forgetting-illusion region with positive Δ_LPR (+4.0 to +15.4 pp) across all datasets, meaning forgotten-class information remains linearly recoverable. Fine-Tuning and Fisher Forgetting [12] and SSD [10] maintain high utility (Acc_r close to Retrain) but exhibit the largest positive gaps (up to +17.3 pp) and near-unity CKA to the original model, indicating they barely alter feature geometry. The Target Method [14] produces negative Δ_LPR on vision datasets (e.g., −20.5 on CIFAR-100), but this coincides with catastrophic utility collapse (Acc_r drops to 1–34%), reflecting representational destruction rather than selective forgetting; on Yahoo Answers it behaves differently with positive Δ_LPR (+7.8) and high CKA (0.957). UNSIR [32] and Amnesiac [13] show mixed results: UNSIR achieves negative gaps on some datasets but at the cost of severe utility loss, while Amnesiac shows moderate positive gaps (+1.2 to +13.8 pp) with unstable behavior across datasets. No method achieves all three desiderata—high utility, output-level forgetting, and representation-level erasure—simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mirage repurposes established representation-analysis tools—linear probing (Alain & Bengio, 2016), Centered Kernel Alignment (Kornblith et al., 2019), and Fisher-style separability scoring—for a new purpose: certifying whether visual unlearning erases class structure in embeddings rather than merely suppressing classifier outputs. It departs from the standard output-level evaluation protocol (retained accuracy, forgotten-label accuracy) inherited by VFL unlearning methods such as Boundary Unlearning, SSD, and the Target Method, introducing a retraining-relative 'forgetting gap' as the core criterion. Conceptually, it aligns with a growing line of work questioning whether unlearning verification is adequate (Hayes et al. 2025; Zhang et al. 2024; Kim et al. 2026), but is the first to systematically apply multi-diagnostic representation auditing in the vertical federated setting where intermediate embeddings are explicitly shared across parties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that methods achieving near-zero forgotten-label accuracy (e.g., BU with y_u = 0%) still retain 94–96% linearly recoverable class structure directly undermines the evaluation standard used across the VFL unlearning literature, with practical implications for any deployment where embeddings are transmitted or stored. The class-sample asymmetry (class-level LPR > 96% vs. sample-level ≈ 50%) shows that a single diagnostic tool cannot cover both forgetting granularities, pointing to a need for granularly appropriate auditing protocols. Limitations include the use of linear probes (a conservative lower bound), a single VFL protocol, and the framework's role as an auditor rather than a new unlearning algorithm; extending certification to nonlinear probes, horizontal federated settings, and stronger adversaries remains open.
