## Problem

Most federated learning methods target single-label classification, yet many real-world applications (e.g., medical diagnosis) are inherently multi-label and exhibit severe label-distribution skew across clients. This setting introduces three intertwined challenges—imbalanced local data, multi-label co-occurrence bias, and cross-client inconsistency in both label frequencies and label dependency structures—that existing FL and NC-inspired methods do not jointly address. Neural Collapse theory, which provides a principled geometric prior for well-separated class representations, has not been adapted to multi-label federated settings.

## Input / Output

Each client provides RGB images (H×W×3) with multi-hot binary label vectors over C classes. The backbone (ResNet-18) produces a spatial feature map of shape d×H'×W', which is flattened into S=H'W' tokens. The method outputs C independent binary predictions via sigmoid(h_ic^T m_c), one per class, trained with per-class BCE. The fixed ETF matrix M ∈ R^{d×C} serves simultaneously as the classifier weight matrix and the set of class-query vectors for the attention module, so no additional parameters are transmitted between clients beyond the standard model weights.

## Method

FedNCA-ML introduces a Label-Aware Disentanglement Module (LADM) that uses a DETR-style 4-head cross-attention mechanism, where fixed simplex ETF vectors act as class-specific queries attending to spatially-embedded image tokens, producing C disentangled class-wise feature vectors per sample. A predefined simplex ETF is used as a globally fixed classifier shared across all clients, anchoring each class to the same prototype direction and preventing client-specific classifier drift. Two complementary regularizers are added: a negative feature rejection loss that penalizes high similarity between negative class features and all non-self prototypes (gated by a threshold τ=0.3), and a positive feature contrastive loss that drives each positive feature closer to its own prototype than to others via a prototype-based softmax. The key design choice is applying ETF alignment only to the class-wise features extracted by LADM rather than to the shared backbone representation, preserving semantic proximity in the backbone while enforcing structured NC-style geometry at the per-class level. Server-side aggregation follows standard FedAvg with uniform client weighting.

## Results

FedNCA-ML achieves the best class-wise (macro) performance across most of the nine FL settings on five datasets. On multi-label CIFAR-10 (β=0.5, γ=0.5), it reaches 87.55% macro-AUC and 48.17% macro-F1, surpassing the second-best method (FedLGT) by 3.92% in macro-AUC and 4.57% in macro-F1. On DermaMNIST (β=0.1, γ=0.71), it gains 4.93% in macro-F1 over the runner-up. On ChestX-ray14, it achieves the highest macro-AUC (71.28% at β=0.1) while maintaining a smaller gap between macro and micro AUC, indicating more balanced disease-class recognition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedNCA-ML consistently outperforms all baselines on macro (class-wise) AUC and F1 across CIFAR-10, DermaMNIST, VOC, and COCO. The strongest multi-label FL competitor, FedLGT, is surpassed by 3.92% macro-AUC on CIFAR-10 (β=0.5) and by 4.93% macro-F1 on DermaMNIST (β=0.1). Standard FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedCurv) cluster within ~1% of each other on macro metrics, while SphereFed degrades substantially (e.g., 83.72% macro-AUC on VOC vs. 93.82% for FedNCA-ML), suggesting hyperspherical alignment is poorly suited to multi-label entanglement. On ChestX-ray14, FedNCA-ML achieves the best macro-AUC (70.55% and 71.28%) but trails FedAvg/SCAFFOLD by ~1–2% on micro-AUC, a trade-off the authors attribute to the 57% 'No Finding' majority class inflating overall AUC for methods that over-predict the negative class. FedETF and MLC-NC, the closest NC-inspired baselines, underperform FedNCA-ML on DermaMNIST by 2.16% and 3.76% macro-AUC respectively (β=0.1), confirming that image-level ETF alignment and centralized class-center calibration are insufficient for multi-label FL under label skew.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Neural Collapse (NC) theory from its original single-label, centralized setting to multi-label federated learning under label skew. It builds directly on FedETF's fixed-ETF classifier concept but departs from it by applying ETF alignment to class-wise features extracted via a DETR-style cross-attention module (LADM) rather than to the shared image-level representation, which is critical because multi-label entanglement makes image-level NC alignment counterproductive. The class-wise attention mechanism itself is adapted from DETR's query-to-feature cross-attention, repurposed so that fixed ETF vectors serve as class-specific queries rather than learnable object queries. Compared to centralized multi-label NC methods (MLC-NC) and language-guided multi-label FL (FedLGT), FedNCA-ML avoids data-dependent class-center estimation and external text embeddings, using a single predefined ETF as both classifier and query source.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-label FL under label skew is a practically important yet underexplored setting, particularly in medical imaging where patients present with multiple concurrent conditions and hospitals have highly skewed local disease distributions. The paper's key insight—that NC geometry must be enforced on disentangled class-wise features rather than the shared backbone representation—provides a principled recipe for adapting NC to multi-label problems more broadly. Limitations include evaluation on only 10 fully-participating clients, a single backbone (ResNet-18), and moderate gains on some datasets (COCO, ChestX-ray14 micro-AUC), leaving open questions about scalability to larger client pools, partial participation, and deeper architectures. The fixed-query design, while effective here, may limit adaptability to domains where class semantics are not well captured by a geometric simplex.
