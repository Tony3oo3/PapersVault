## Problem

Existing camera-to-LiDAR distillation methods treat VFMs as black-box teachers, distilling only final-layer features and discarding the CLS token, thereby losing the progressive semantic hierarchy and global scene context encoded across VFM layers. Additionally, image-based VFMs inherently lack explicit 3D geometric and temporal supervision, and prior occupancy auxiliaries rely on discriminative per-voxel objectives that model local marginals rather than the joint spatiotemporal scene distribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized LiDAR sweeps (N points with 3D coordinates and intensity) and V surround-view RGB images with extrinsic/intrinsic calibration, processed as temporal triplets {t-1, t0, t1}. Feature extraction uses only the past and present frames, while t1 serves exclusively as the future BEV occupancy target (ground-removed binary grid). The output is a pre-trained MinkUnet34 backbone producing dense per-point 3D features; at inference all auxiliary heads are removed, and the backbone is transferred to segmentation, detection, scene flow, or occupancy tasks.

## Method

HilDA trains a LiDAR student end-to-end with three self-supervised losses. First, multi-layer distillation projects each LiDAR point to camera pixels via calibration and minimizes cosine distance between layer-specific MLP-projected student features and bilinearly upsampled teacher features across the last K VFM layers (default K=2), using a 'separate late-layer matching' strategy that pairs adjacent teacher/student layers. Second, global context distillation aligns a max-pooled student CLS token (via a dedicated MLP head) with the max-pooled CLS tokens of all V teacher views using MSE, acting as a scene-level regularizer. Third, temporal occupancy diffusion formulates future BEV occupancy prediction as conditional DDPM denoising: a 2D UNet predicts noise ε given a corrupted occupancy sample x_τ and a dense BEV history feature map C_history (built from student features at t-1 and t0) via channel-wise concatenation, trained with a hybrid noise-prediction plus occupancy-reconstruction objective. The total loss is a weighted sum of all three terms, and the resulting backbone is frozen for downstream evaluation.

## Results

On the primary cross-modal distillation benchmark (nuScenes 3D semantic segmentation, ViT-B teacher, MinkUnet34 student), HilDA achieves 58.95 mIoU under linear probing, 62.71 mIoU at 1% labels, and 79.12 mIoU with full fine-tuning, setting new state-of-the-art across all label budgets. The largest gains appear in data-scarce settings: at 1% labels, HilDA outperforms the previous best (CleverDistiller, 59.80) by 2.9 mIoU and LiMA (51.29) by 11.4 mIoU. Transfer to SemanticKITTI (53.44 mIoU at 1%) and Waymo (53.89 mIoU at 1%) also leads all baselines. On robustness (nuScenes-C), HilDA achieves the lowest mean Corruption Error (124.27%) and highest mean Relative Robustness (88.20%), and on 3D object detection (KITTI 20% labels) it reaches 71.0 mAP vs. 67.1 for CleverDistiller.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HilDA outperforms all eight compared baselines (SLidR, Seal, SuperFlow, ScaLR, LiMoE, CleverDistiller, SuperFlow++, LiMA) on every nuScenes label budget and both cross-domain transfer benchmarks (SemanticKITTI, Waymo). The closest competitor is CleverDistiller (BMVC'25), which leads in full-supervision fine-tuning (78.49 vs. 79.12 mIoU, a 0.63 gap) but is surpassed by 2.9 mIoU at 1% labels and 7.1 mIoU under linear probing. LiMA (ICCV'25) was the prior LP leader (56.65) but is beaten by 2.3 mIoU by HilDA while also trailing by 11.4 mIoU at 1% labels, indicating HilDA's features are more data-efficient. Methods relying on semantic priors (SLidR, Seal, SuperFlow, LiMoE, SuperFlow++) consistently underperform the prior-free approaches (ScaLR, CleverDistiller, LiMA, HilDA), with HilDA extending the lead without any pseudo-mask overhead. On robustness (nuScenes-C), HilDA reduces mean Corruption Error from 137.23 (LiMA) to 124.27 and improves mean mIoU from 44.92 to 52.00, with the largest gains under Cross-Sensor (56.29 vs. 45.76) and Snowy (57.97 vs. 54.90) corruptions. On 3D object detection (KITTI, 20% labels), HilDA reaches 71.0 mAP vs. 67.1 (CleverDistiller) and 66.5 (ScaLR); on scene flow (Argoverse 2), it reduces dynamic EPE from 0.210 (CleverDistiller) to 0.146, a 30% improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

HilDA extends the camera-to-LiDAR knowledge distillation paradigm established by SLidR, Seal, SuperFlow, ScaLR, LiMoE, CleverDistiller, and LiMA, all of which distill only the final-layer features of a frozen VFM (typically DINOv2) into a 3D LiDAR backbone. It departs from this lineage in two ways: (1) hierarchical distillation, which aligns multiple intermediate VFM layers with corresponding student layers and additionally matches the CLS token to a learnable 3D global context token, and (2) a temporal occupancy diffusion objective (conditional DDPM) that replaces the discriminative per-voxel occupancy losses used in prior auxiliary tasks. The diffusion component draws on DDPM [30] and the broader literature on diffusion as a representation-learning signal [31, 99], while the multi-layer and CLS-token distillation ideas are adapted from 2D ViT distillation work [12, 19, 29, 65] into the cross-modal point-pixel setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By capturing the progressive semantic hierarchy of the VFM and adding a generative spatiotemporal signal, HilDA produces LiDAR features that are simultaneously semantically rich and geometrically/temporally grounded, yielding consistent SOTA gains across segmentation, detection, occupancy, scene flow, and robustness benchmarks—especially in data-scarce regimes where prior methods degrade. A key limitation is sensitivity to LiDAR-camera calibration misalignment, partially mitigated by the alignment-free CLS and diffusion losses. Open questions include whether learned layer-matching strategies and dynamic loss weighting can further improve multi-layer distillation, and whether extending diffusion beyond 2D BEV (e.g., latent 3D diffusion) would better preserve vertical geometry and long-horizon temporal dynamics.
