## Problem

Cross-species animal ReID requires recognizing individual animals across species with drastically different morphologies and visual patterns, but existing domain-generalization methods from person ReID implicitly assume shared semantic structures across domains—an assumption that breaks down when anatomies differ fundamentally. As a result, representations learned from heterogeneous species form fragmented, species-isolated embedding spaces that fail to generalize to unseen species or ecological domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262594/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB animal images resized to 256×256 pixels, processed by an ImageNet-pretrained ViT with 16×16 patches, and produces a global feature embedding used for cosine-similarity-based identity matching. Training uses a batch of 128 images (8 identities × 4 images per GPU × 4 GPUs) with standard ReID augmentations (random horizontal flip, 10-pixel padding). At test time, only the original (un-augmented) feature is extracted for distance computation, and evaluation reports Rank-1 CMC and mAP across 11 public datasets under two open-set protocols where test identities and species are fully disjoint from training.

## Method

SCL combines two complementary mechanisms. First, Foreground-Background Decoupled Spectral Normalization (FDSNorm) builds a soft foreground mask from CLS-to-patch cosine similarity, splits the feature map into foreground and background branches, applies DFT to each independently, and reconstructs by preserving the original phase while adaptively mixing amplitude spectra with learnable per-branch weights—inserted at Transformer layers {0,4,8} for progressive de-stylization from pixel-level to structure-level shifts. Second, Cross-species Neighborhood Modeling (CNM) employs a teacher-student framework with EMA smoothing and a memory queue of teacher features; for each student feature it retrieves intra-species neighbors (top-K₁ same species) and cross-species neighbors (top-K₁ different species filtered by a mutual nearest-neighbor rule with K₂), then optimizes a combined loss that enforces intra-species compactness via alignment to the intra-neighborhood center and cross-species relational consistency via a margin-based hinge toward the cross-neighborhood center. The total objective sums an identification loss, triplet loss, and the CNM loss, trained for 60 epochs with SGD and cosine decay.

## Results

Under Protocol-1 (trained on Wildlife71, tested on 10 unseen species), SCL achieves the best or near-best Rank-1 and mAP across all ten domains, with particularly large gains on SeaTurtleID2022 (58.8% Rank-1 vs. 53.1% for AdaFreq) and WhaleSharkID (45.4% Rank-1 vs. 42.5% for AdaFreq). Under Protocol-2 (trained on a subset of single-species datasets, tested on Wildlife71), SCL reaches 93.8% mAP and 97.6% Rank-1, outperforming the strongest baseline AdaFreq (91.2% mAP, 97.3% Rank-1) by 2.6 mAP points. The inter-ID to intra-ID distance ratio (Table 9) is consistently higher for SCL than for MegaDescriptor and MiewID across all ten unseen datasets, confirming tighter identity clustering with clearer separation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCL outperforms all 12 baselines on the majority of the 10 Protocol-1 test domains. The closest competitors are AdaFreq (ECCV 2024) and TransReID (ICCV 2021): SCL leads AdaFreq by 1.1 Rank-1 on ELPephants (34.4 vs. 33.3), 1.6 on ATRW (98.0 vs. 96.4), and 2.9 on WhaleSharkID (45.4 vs. 42.5), while AdaFreq edges ahead on SeaTurtleID2022 mAP (8.4 vs. 10.0 for SCL—actually SCL wins here too at 10.0). ReNorm (ECCV 2024) is the only method that beats SCL on a single metric (SeaTurtleID2022 Rank-1: 60.4 vs. 58.8), but SCL recovers on mAP (10.0 vs. 9.3). MegaDescriptor (WACV 2024) slightly exceeds SCL on GZGC-giraffe Rank-1 (23.5 vs. 22.9) but is outperformed on every other domain. The animal-specific baselines UniReID (NeurIPS 2023) and ARBase (ICCV 2025) show substantial degradation on unseen species (e.g., UniReID drops to 25.2 Rank-1 on ELPephants vs. SCL's 34.4), confirming that instance-level objectives without cross-species alignment suffer negative transfer. The vision-language baselines CLIP-FGDI (TIFS 2025) and CLIP (AAAI 2023) underperform most strongly on fine-grained wildlife datasets (e.g., CLIP-FGDI scores only 20.2 Rank-1 on SeaTurtleID2022), indicating that generic vision-language priors capture species-level semantics rather than individual identity. Overall, SCL achieves the best average Rank-1 and mAP across all ten unseen domains, with the largest margins over foundation-model baselines (MegaDescriptor, MiewID) on morphologically diverse species such as sea turtles and whale sharks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends frequency-domain normalization (Lee et al., CVPR 2023) from global amplitude/phase manipulation to a region-aware, foreground-background decoupled variant, motivated by the observation that animal ReID involves semantically distinct regions (animal body vs. habitat) that require different style-suppression strengths. It departs from the dominant domain-generalization paradigm in person ReID—which assumes shared semantic structure across domains—by explicitly acknowledging that cross-species anatomical and textural differences invalidate that assumption, and instead introduces relational neighborhood learning (CNM) to capture transferable structure without requiring explicit cross-species correspondences. The teacher-student EMA framework and memory-queue mechanism draw on meta-learning and MoCo-style designs, but are repurposed here to maintain temporally stable cross-species relational anchors rather than to simulate domain shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-species animal ReID is critical for scalable wildlife monitoring where deploying a separate model per species is impractical, and this paper provides the first framework that explicitly targets generalization to unseen species with a principled evaluation protocol. The two complementary evaluation protocols (train on Wildlife71 → test on 10 unseen domains; train on 6 single-species datasets → test on Wildlife71/PetFace) give the community a reproducible benchmark for cross-species generalization that prior multi-species works lacked. Open questions remain: whether the approach extends to truly zero-shot species (no training data from any related taxon), how it interacts with large-scale ecological foundation models, and whether the foreground mask quality degrades under extreme occlusion or non-standard viewpoints common in camera-trap imagery.
