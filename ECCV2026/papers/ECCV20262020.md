## Problem

Existing one-step diffusion distillation methods for real-world image super-resolution require maintaining multiple auxiliary models (teachers, discriminators) during training, inflating GPU memory by 2–3× and making it infeasible to distill large-scale backbones like 20B-parameter DiTs. Additionally, these fixed-step models lock restoration quality to a single forward pass, eliminating the ability to trade inference cost for higher fidelity when latency permits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262020/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality RGB image (×4 degradation synthesized via the Real-ESRGAN pipeline) as input, encodes it to latent space via a VAE, and produces a high-quality restored RGB image. The LQ latent is injected as spatial condition tokens concatenated with the noisy-latent tokens in the DiT. A key operational detail is that the same trained model supports any-step inference (1 to 30+ steps) by simply changing the number of ODE integration steps at test time, with no retraining required.

## Method

TEASR performs Self-Adversarial Distillation (SAD) entirely within a single diffusion transformer: a real trajectory is trained with flow-matching plus self-consistency loss, while a fake trajectory (conditioned on negated timesteps) learns the distribution of the one-step output, and their velocity discrepancy provides a DMD-style rectification gradient without any external teacher or discriminator. A Time-Aware Rectification (TAR) scheduler applies a timestep-dependent weight w(s)=(1−s)²/s to the rectification signal, suppressing unreliable high-noise gradients and amplifying precise low-noise guidance. Architecturally, a dual-branch DiT with Decoupled Timestep Condition (DTC) assigns the current timestep t_cur to the frozen noise branch and the target timestep t_tar to the LoRA-finetuned condition branch, preserving both temporal signals through attention without entanglement. Built on the 20B-parameter Qwen-Image backbone, this design enables fine-tuning on a single A100 GPU while supporting seamless any-step sampling from one to thirty iterations.

## Results

On RealSR (1-step, 1 model), TEASR achieves the best LPIPS (0.2542), DISTS (0.2003), and FID (103.97) among all one-step methods, while matching OSEDiff's PSNR (24.83 vs. 25.15) with only a single trainable model instead of three. On DRealSR, TEASR leads in PSNR (28.54), SSIM (0.7876), LPIPS (0.2722), and FID (129.06), outperforming both OSEDiff and TSD-SR. On DIV2K-Val, TEASR achieves the highest PSNR (24.36) and SSIM (0.6144), surpassing OSEDiff by 0.64 dB in PSNR. Across all three benchmarks, TEASR consistently delivers the best distribution-level FID scores, indicating superior perceptual realism at one-step inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest one-step competitors OSEDiff and TSD-SR (both requiring 3 co-resident models), TEASR (1 model) wins decisively on FID across all three datasets (103.97 vs. 123.49/114.48 on RealSR; 129.06 vs. 135.29/135.32 on DRealSR; 23.40 vs. 26.34/23.29 on DIV2K-Val), indicating substantially better distribution-level realism. On DRealSR and DIV2K-Val, TEASR also leads in PSNR (28.54 vs. 27.92/27.77; 24.36 vs. 23.72/23.02) and SSIM, while on RealSR it trades a small PSNR gap to OSEDiff (24.83 vs. 25.15) for large gains in LPIPS (0.2542 vs. 0.2921) and FID. TEASR trails TSD-SR on no-reference metrics (MUSIQ, MANIQA, NIQE) across all datasets, suggesting TSD-SR produces slightly more 'natural-looking' images by NR-IQA criteria, though at the cost of higher FID and more training models. Against multi-step baselines (StableSR 200-step, DiffBIR 50-step, SUPIR 50-step, DiT4SR 40-step), TEASR at 1-step matches or exceeds their PSNR/SSIM on DRealSR and DIV2K-Val while achieving far lower FID, demonstrating that the any-step framework captures multi-step quality in a single forward pass. The key efficiency advantage is that TEASR trains with a single model on 2×A100 GPUs, whereas OSEDiff and TSD-SR require ~3× the memory for their multi-model distillation pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEASR synthesizes three recent threads: (1) the self-consistent / shortcut-model objective with dual timesteps from Consistency Models and Shortcut Models, (2) the self-adversarial twin-trajectory distillation introduced for text-to-image by TwinFlow and FACM, and (3) the dual-branch frozen-conditioning design from Omini-Control. The key departure from prior one-step SR methods (OSEDiff, TSD-SR) is that they maintain three co-resident models (teacher, student, discriminator) during training, whereas TEASR collapses all roles into a single DiT via self-adversarial distillation. Architecturally, the Decoupled Timestep Condition replaces the common practice of fusing t_cur and t_tar into one embedding (as in Shortcut or MeanFlow) by routing them to separate branches, preserving fine-grained noise-level information that is critical for SR fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: distilling a 20B-parameter Qwen-Image backbone for SR on a single A100 GPU removes the memory barrier that previously confined one-step SR to sub-2B models, and the any-step sampling (1–30 steps) gives practitioners a continuous speed–quality dial without retraining. However, the paper acknowledges that optimizing multiple composite objectives (flow matching, self-consistency, adversarial rectification, pixel-space fidelity) within a single model's capacity introduces trade-offs, and fine-grained independent control over reconstruction fidelity versus perceptual realism at a fixed step count remains open. The observation that FR metrics (PSNR, SSIM) decrease while NR metrics (NIQE, MANIQA) increase with more sampling steps highlights an unresolved tension between pixel-level accuracy and perceptual naturalness in generative SR.
