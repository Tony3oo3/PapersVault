## Problem

Metric depth estimation has not exhibited the scaling trends seen in relative depth or other vision foundation model tasks, because heterogeneous 3D data sources (LiDAR, RGB-D, stereo, SfM, MVS, SLAM) introduce distinct noise patterns, hardware-specific artifacts, and camera-dependent biases that prevent straightforward data aggregation. Existing prompt-based methods address specific tasks with small datasets and hand-crafted prompt pipelines, limiting scalability and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261646/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pretrained model takes an RGB image plus a sparse metric prompt constructed by randomly sampling N ∈ [2,000, 40,000] valid pixels from the ground-truth depth map; the prompt is regularized via pixel-wise depth scale alignment (PDSA) and global metric depth recovery (GMDR), then fed as an H×W×3 channel tensor through a conditioned DPT head. The distilled prompt-free student consumes only the RGB image and outputs a dense metric depth map, a 3D point map, or camera intrinsics depending on the downstream head. For radar-camera fusion, radar point clouds replace the sparse depth prompt, demonstrating modality-agnostic prompt consumption.

## Method

MetricAnything introduces a Sparse Metric Prompt (SMP) formed by randomly masking depth maps, which serves as a universal interface decoupling spatial reasoning from sensor and camera biases without task-specific prompt engineering. The architecture reuses a shared ViT backbone (merging patch and image encoders) with a lightweight conditioned DPT head (~5% extra parameters) for prompt injection, trained on ~20M image-depth pairs spanning 10,000+ cameras across reconstructed, captured, and rendered 3D sources. Training uses MAE + SSI-MAGE losses for synthetic data and a robust MAE (dropping top-20% loss regions) for noisy real data. A key distillation step generates high-fidelity pseudo-labels from the teacher, which train a prompt-free student featuring an inverted skip-connection scheme (deep ViT features routed to deep decoder layers) and a distance-balanced log-depth loss that preserves supervision across both near and far ranges.

## Results

On monocular metric depth estimation (Table 3), the distilled Student-DepthMap achieves δ₁ of 79.9% (ETH3D), 88.1% (NuScenes), 97.7% (SunRGBD), and 65.8% (Middlebury), attaining the best average rank (1.50) across six benchmarks. The pretrained model's zero-shot depth super-resolution/completion (Table 1) yields AbsRel of 1.53% (NYUv2 8×), 1.46% (ETH-3D 8×), and 2.34% (KITTI 8×), outperforming all prior prompt-based and post-aligned baselines. For radar-camera fusion on nuScenes (Table 2), fine-tuning the pretrained model achieves 651.4 mm MAE (0–50 m), nearly halving the error of the best prior method (RadarCam-Depth, 1286.1 mm). The Student-PointMap further achieves 97.7% δ₁ on ETH3D and 90.2% on iBims-1 for monocular geometry estimation without intrinsics, and 2.50°/1.86° mean/median angular error for camera calibration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the core monocular depth task (Table 3), Student-DepthMap outperforms DepthPro [10] on ETH3D (79.9 vs 41.5), Booster (49.5 vs 21.6), NuScenes (88.1 vs 49.1), SunRGBD (97.7 vs 89.0), and Middlebury (65.8 vs 60.5), but trails on Sintel (27.7 vs 40.0). It also surpasses Metric3D-v2 [38] on ETH3D (79.9 vs 87.7 is actually a loss—correction: Metric3D-v2 leads on ETH3D at 87.7 vs 79.9) and UniDepth [75] on ETH3D and Booster. For zero-shot depth completion/SR (Table 1), the pretrained model achieves the best average rank (1.50) over PriorDA [112] (3.25), Omni-DC [137] (3.75), and PromptDA [60] (5.50), with particularly large margins on KITTI LiDAR prompts (3.53 vs 4.81 for PriorDA). On radar-camera fusion (Table 2), fine-tuning MetricAnything reduces MAE from 1286.1 mm (RadarCam-Depth [54]) to 651.4 mm, a ~50% improvement. For monocular geometry without intrinsics (Table 4), Student-PointMap beats MoGe-2 [108] on ETH3D (97.7 vs 62.9) and DIODE (86.6 vs 83.0) but trails on KITTI AbsRel (3.22 vs 4.69 for UniDepth V1 [75]). On VSI-Bench spatial reasoning (Table 8), the integrated model scores 58.3 average, surpassing Spatial-MLLM-4B [116] (48.4) and Gemini-1.5 Pro [99] (45.4), while on LIBERO VLA planning (Table 7) it achieves 87.7% average success rate, edging out Baseline-DAV2 (86.6%) and π₀-FAST [72] (85.5%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DepthPro ViT-DPT architecture and its SSI-MAGE loss, adopts the PDSA/GMDR prompt preparation pipeline from PriorDA, and fine-tunes MoGe-2 for its point-map student. However, it departs fundamentally from prior prompt-based depth methods (PromptDA, Omni-DC, Marigold-DC) that rely on hand-crafted, task-specific prompt simulations, replacing them with a single universal mechanism—randomly masking depth maps to form Sparse Metric Prompts. The paper also contrasts with the metric depth lineage (Metric3D, UniDepth, ZoeDepth) that couples depth prediction with camera-specific normalization or canonical transforms, arguing instead that a data-driven, camera-agnostic pretraining paradigm can absorb sensor and intrinsics diversity through scale alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first demonstrated scaling trend in metric depth estimation challenges the prevailing assumption that heterogeneous sensor noise and metric ambiguity fundamentally prevent the 'data soup' strategy that succeeded for relative depth and 2D vision. The 20M-pair, 10,000+-camera dataset and the simple random-masking pretraining recipe are likely to become a standard resource and technique for the community. Open questions include whether the scaling curve continues beyond 20M samples, whether the approach extends to other 3D perception tasks (e.g., surface normals, flow), and whether the distillation step can be eliminated for a truly end-to-end prompt-free model. The reliance on the DepthPro backbone and the modest per-task margins over MoGe-2 in some settings suggest the architecture itself may still be a bottleneck.
