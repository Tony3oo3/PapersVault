## Problem

Once a 3D face avatar is shared as a reusable digital asset, every possible rendering can be analyzed by vision-language models to extract sensitive attributes (age, ethnicity, gender, expression) without any fine-tuning. Existing defenses operate in 2D image space and protect only a single rendering, while prior 3D adversarial work targets identity detectors rather than VLM-level semantic reasoning. This paper formalizes the problem of steering VLM attribute inference from 3D face avatars while strictly preserving facial identity and geometric structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-optimized 3DGS face avatar (reconstructed via FaceLift from a single image) and a target attribute text prompt. A pose-aligned target image is synthesized by conditioning Stable Diffusion on an OpenPose structural map extracted from the source rendering plus the target attribute prompt. The output is a perturbed 3DGS avatar augmented with 50,000 auxiliary shell Gaussians whose only modified parameters are color and opacity; rendered views at 384×384 resolution with 50° FOV are then fed to black-box VLMs for evaluation.

## Method

3D FaceShell introduces a learnable additive Gaussian shell positioned near the facial surface of a frozen 3DGS baseline, restricting optimization to shell color and opacity so that geometry is never deformed. The adversarial objective aligns multi-view rendered embeddings (from K=5 camera poses) with the target image embedding in the visual encoder space of two VLMs (SigLIP and CLIP) via cosine similarity, with stochastic augmentations (quantization noise, random crops, color jitter) to prevent view overfitting. Face-aware regularization includes landmark-consistency masks on identity-critical regions (eyes, nose, lips), a YUV-space chroma penalty that suppresses high-frequency luminance artifacts, and a progressively tightening opacity clamp that pushes shell Gaussians toward transparency over training. The multi-attribute target formulation—simultaneously flipping multiple semantic factors—provides a stronger gradient signal than single-attribute manipulation, and multi-encoder supervision prevents overfitting to any single VLM's feature space, yielding view-consistent and model-agnostic perturbations.

## Results

On a benchmark of 100 celebrity 3DGS face avatars evaluated across four black-box VLMs, 3D FaceShell achieves Injection Rates of 48.7% (VideoLLaMA3), 31.3% (LLaVA-NeXT), 56.9% (LLaVA-OV), and 26.7% (BLIP-2), with Mismatch Rates ranging from 53.0% to 74.4%. Its defining advantage is perceptual fidelity: LPIPS of 0.1499 and ArcFace Identity Similarity of 0.7629, representing a +71.5% LPIPS improvement over V-Attack (0.4039) and +60.7% over M-Attack (0.3814), while the 2D baselines degrade identity similarity to 0.49–0.53. On VideoLLaMA3 and LLaVA-OV, 3D FaceShell also attains the highest absolute Injection Rate among all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D FaceShell is compared against six 2D adversarial attack baselines (AnyAttack, AttackVLM, AdvDiffVLM, SSA-CWA, V-Attack, M-Attack) that operate in image space. On VideoLLaMA3 and LLaVA-OV, 3D FaceShell achieves the highest Injection Rate (48.7% and 56.9% respectively), edging out M-Attack (47.4%, 56.4%) and V-Attack (44.4%, 51.6%). However, on LLaVA-NeXT and BLIP-2, M-Attack leads in IR (46.3% and 37.9% vs. 31.3% and 26.7%), and V-Attack attains the highest Mismatch Rate on VideoLLaMA3 (74.0% vs. 66.4%). The critical differentiator is visual quality: 3D FaceShell's LPIPS of 0.1499 is roughly 2.6× lower than V-Attack (0.4039) and 2.5× lower than M-Attack (0.3814), while its ArcFace identity similarity (0.7629) exceeds V-Attack by 88.9% and M-Attack by 56.2%. The weaker 2D baselines (AnyAttack, AttackVLM, AdvDiffVLM) achieve only marginal gains over random noise in IR/MR while still degrading LPIPS to 0.24–0.45, confirming that 3D FaceShell's 3D-native formulation is necessary for both competitive semantic transfer and identity preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: 3D adversarial attacks (which have targeted point clouds, NeRFs, and 3DGS for object/face detectors) and 2D adversarial attacks on VLMs (AnyAttack, V-Attack, M-Attack, etc.). It departs from both by operating in the 3DGS parameter space while targeting VLM semantic attribute inference rather than classification or detection, and by imposing strict identity-preservation constraints absent from prior 3D attacks. The method is built directly on 3DGS face avatar pipelines (FaceLift, GaussianAvatars) and repurposes the additive perturbation idea from 3D adversarial camouflage, but redirects it toward embedding-space alignment in the VLM visual encoder rather than pixel-level or detector-level confusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formalize and address the specific threat of VLM-based semantic attribute extraction from shared 3D face avatars, a realistic privacy concern as avatars become persistent, multi-view digital assets. The identity-preserving constraint is practically important: unlike 2D attacks that visibly degrade the face, the Gaussian shell approach keeps the avatar usable for telepresence and animation while still redirecting VLM interpretation. Open questions include whether the defense generalizes to fine-tuned or future VLMs, whether it extends to 4D/dynamic avatars (the authors note this as future work), and whether the 100-face benchmark is sufficient to validate robustness across diverse demographics and reconstruction quality.
