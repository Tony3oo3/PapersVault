## Problem

Large-scale Vision-Language Models (e.g., CLIP ViT-L-14) suffer from severe semantic over-compression: their final [CLS] token discards the low-level geometric primitives (strokes, edges, junctions) that are essential for aligning sparse, abstract sketches with dense natural photos. Existing ZS-SBIR methods that naively rely on this top-layer-only representation fail to bridge the abstraction-realism gap, and simply scaling backbone parameters without preserving multi-layer structural information yields diminishing returns.

## Input / Output

The method takes a hand-drawn sketch (sparse line drawing) as the query and a gallery of natural photos from unseen classes as candidates, both processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-L-14 image encoder. Class names are encoded by a frozen text encoder augmented with 16 learnable CoOp context vectors. The output is a cosine-similarity retrieval ranking over the photo gallery, driven by an aligned text prototype that fuses geometric and semantic visual cues with the prompted text embedding.

## Method

The framework has two core modules. Multi-Layer Feature Aggregation (MLFA) extracts patch-level features from shallow ViT layers (1–3) for geometric primitives and deep layers (22–24) for semantic objects, applying a non-linear gating block (LayerNorm → ReLU → Dropout) and learnable linear projections to stabilize the distribution shift between heterogeneous scales. Phased Cross-Modal Interaction (PCMI) then performs a two-stage coarse-to-fine alignment: Phase I uses the prompted text embedding as a query in multi-head cross-attention over the geometric features to capture stroke-level evidence, and Phase II uses the geometrically-aligned embedding to query the semantic features for global context. An adaptive gated fusion with tanh-constrained scalar gates combines both attention outputs with the original text anchor to produce the final aligned prototype. Training jointly optimizes a hard-margin cross-modal triplet loss and a temperature-scaled classification loss, updating only the prompt vectors and projection layers while keeping the ViT-L-14 and text encoder frozen.

## Results

The proposed framework achieves state-of-the-art mAP@all of 0.775 on TU-Berlin Ext., 0.855 on Sketchy Ext. Split1, and 0.799 (mAP@200) on Sketchy Ext. Split2, outperforming all prior methods including SpLIP (0.731/0.802/0.764) and MFCPKD (0.662/0.847/0.736). On QuickDraw Ext., the method remains competitive with mAP@all of 0.338 and Prec@200 of 0.406, slightly trailing SpLIP (0.342/0.446) but surpassing all other baselines. The ablation confirms that MLFA contributes the largest single gain (e.g., +0.122 mAP on TU-Berlin over the ViT-L [CLS]-only baseline), while PCMI adds a further +0.081 on top of MLFA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 24 baselines spanning ResNet-based, ViT-based, and VLM-based approaches. On TU-Berlin Ext., the proposed method (0.775 mAP@all) surpasses the previous best SpLIP (0.731) by 4.4 points and MARL (0.707) by 6.8 points. On Sketchy Ext. Split1, it edges out MFCPKD (0.847) by 0.8 points and SpLIP (0.802) by 5.3 points. On Sketchy Ext. Split2, it leads SpLIP (0.764) by 3.5 points and MFCPKD (0.736) by 6.3 points. On QuickDraw Ext., the method (0.338 mAP@all) narrowly trails SpLIP (0.342) by 0.4 points but substantially outperforms MARL (0.327), DCDL (0.336), and MFCPKD (0.224). Overall, the method achieves the best result on 3 of 4 benchmarks, with the sole loss on QuickDraw Ext. attributed to the extreme abstraction and stylistic diversity of that dataset's sketches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based ZS-SBIR line (ZSE, TVT, SpLIP) by arguing that prior methods' reliance on the terminal [CLS] token from standard backbones (ViT-B/32, ResNet-50) discards the geometric primitives essential for sketch-photo alignment. It builds directly on CoOp's learnable context vectors [42] but repurposes them as dynamic cross-attention queries rather than static class identifiers, and repurposes multi-layer feature aggregation—well-established in dense prediction (segmentation, detection)—as an 'anti-compression' mechanism within the ZS-SBIR setting. The paper positions itself as a strong baseline that unlocks the full capacity of ViT-L-14, departing from the generative (GAN/CVAE) and semantic-transfer paradigms that dominated earlier ZS-SBIR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that simply scaling up a CLIP backbone without multi-layer feature extraction yields diminishing returns for sketch retrieval—gives practitioners a clear recipe for leveraging large VLMs in sparse, abstract domains. However, the method does not surpass SpLIP on QuickDraw Ext. (0.338 vs. 0.342 mAP), suggesting that for highly abstract, diverse-style sketches, geometric-semantic decoupling alone is insufficient. Open questions include whether the fixed layer selection (1–3, 22–24) generalizes across ViT variants and whether the approach extends to even sparser modalities (e.g., line drawings, partial sketches) where the abstraction-realism gap is more extreme.
