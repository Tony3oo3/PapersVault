## Problem

Existing vision-language models and layout understanding methods are designed for atomic layouts with independent, clearly bounded elements, leaving compositional layouts—where elements are visually entangled within hierarchical multi-layer structures—largely unexplored. The paper identifies two specific failure modes: semantic drift, where VLMs cannot align textual metadata with the corresponding visual content under entanglement, and structural ambiguity, where VLMs fail to model layer-aware spatial relationships among elements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260940/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a rendered design image (resized to 336×336; original designs span 300×60 to 7200×14400 with an average of 17.4 layers), structured JSON element metadata (id, type, opacity, zIndex, position), and a compositional question referencing a query element. The metadata is augmented at inference with a spatialRelationship field encoding six layer-aware properties (overlap ratio, containment type, center distance, directional relation, and relative stacking order) between the query element and every other element. The output is the identified compositional element paired with the query element, plus a free-text explanation of the design intent, evaluated across four composition types: Overlaying, Clipping, Blending, and Morphing.

## Method

MASON is a post-training paradigm applied to Qwen2.5-VL 7B via LoRA (r=4, α=8 on attention q/v projections) that integrates two complementary objectives. Multimodal Alignment (MA) generates element-grounding QA pairs using GPT-4o on 1K layouts (20% of elements sampled per layout), where the model must describe a metadata-defined element's semantic identity and spatial placement in the full design, explicitly aligning text metadata to visual content under entanglement. Structural Perception (SP) augments the element metadata with a spatialRelationship field encoding overlap, containment, center distance, directional position, and layer order relative to the query element, providing explicit geometric context for hierarchical reasoning. The VLM is jointly trained on a mixture of compositional QA and grounding QA samples with cross-entropy loss over 3 epochs (AdamW, lr=3e-5, batch 64, 8 A100s). At inference, only the compositional task is evaluated while retaining the SP metadata augmentation. MASON with 30% of training data (≈8K QA pairs) surpasses full-data Direct Finetune (≈20K pairs), and full-data MASON reaches 91.66% weighted accuracy versus 79.68% for GPT-o3.

## Results

On CoDeLayout, MASON (Full data) achieves 91.66% weighted accuracy and 88.07% average accuracy, substantially outperforming the strongest zero-shot baseline GPT-o3 (79.68% weighted, 76.28% average). MASON with only 30% of training data (89.32% weighted) already surpasses Direct Finetune trained on the full dataset (88.80% weighted). The largest gains appear on structurally complex categories: Clipping improves from 63.64% (GPT-o3) to 90.91% (MASON), while Morphing remains the hardest category at 70.21% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MASON (Full data) outperforms all 11 zero-shot baselines by a wide margin. Against the strongest proprietary model GPT-o3 (79.68% weighted), MASON gains +11.98 points, with the largest per-category improvement on Clipping (+27.27 points: 90.91% vs. 63.64%). Among open-source 7B models, Qwen3-VL is the closest zero-shot competitor at 77.08% weighted, still 14.58 points below MASON. Heuristic baselines (Max-Overlap, Nearest-Neighbor) score below 50% weighted accuracy, confirming that geometric priors alone are insufficient. Notably, MASON trained on only 30% of the data (89.32% weighted) surpasses Direct Finetune on the full dataset (88.80%), demonstrating that the multimodal alignment and structural perception objectives provide more effective supervision than additional compositional QA pairs alone. The only category where MASON does not achieve a clear lead over GPT-o3 is Overlaying (95.65% vs. 93.91%), where GPT-o3's reasoning capability partially compensates for the lack of explicit layer-aware metadata.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining compositional layout understanding as a distinct task from the atomic layout understanding that dominates prior work (LayoutLM for documents, ScreenQA for UIs, infographic VQA for design). While graphic design datasets like Crelo, GenPoster-100K, and MLTD provide multi-layer layouts, they target generation rather than understanding, and none explicitly annotate inter-element compositional relationships or design intent. Methodologically, MASON repurposes standard VLM post-training (LoRA on Qwen2.5-VL) with two new objectives—grounding-based multimodal alignment and layer-aware spatial metadata augmentation—rather than introducing a new architecture. The paper does not build on a specific prior method but synthesizes ideas from VLM instruction tuning, element grounding, and spatial relationship modeling into a unified post-training paradigm for a previously unaddressed task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying semantic drift and structural ambiguity as the two core failure modes, the paper provides a diagnostic framework that explains *why* even reasoning-capable VLMs (GPT-o3 at 79.68%) struggle with compositional layouts, which is valuable for the design-assistant and document-intelligence communities. The CoDeLayout dataset (~20K multi-layer designs with explicit compositional pair annotations) is the first resource for this setting and will likely become a standard benchmark. However, the scope is limited to four composition types in graphic design, the test set is small (387 samples), and the Morphing category remains poorly solved (70.21% even with MASON), suggesting the task is far from closed. Open questions include whether layer-aware visual encoders (rather than metadata augmentation) can further close the gap, and whether the findings generalize beyond the four studied composition types to arbitrary multi-layer designs.
