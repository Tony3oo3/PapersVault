## Problem

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) for image generation and editing entangle multimodal generative reasoning with high-fidelity visual synthesis, preventing full exploitation of VLM strengths (semantic understanding, grounding, structured reasoning) and diffusion model strengths (photorealistic rendering, detail fidelity). Assembled UMMs reduce the VLM to a frozen feature extractor, while native UMMs force joint optimization that introduces capacity competition and hinders generalization on tasks requiring both fine-grained edits and strong semantic control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260346/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt plus zero, one, or two input images (up to N=2), where each image is designated either a source image (requiring pixel-level fidelity, e.g., instruction editing) or a reference image (requiring identity preservation, e.g., personalized generation). The VLM processes all inputs jointly and emits 128 learnable 'kontext' tokens that encode both semantic content and coarse visual conditions. Outputs are RGB images at 512×512 (base training) or 1024×1024 (upscaling stage), covering text-to-image generation, instruction-guided editing, subject-driven customization, and multi-subject composition.

## Method

Query-Kontext decouples VLM-based generative reasoning from diffusion-based rendering via a set of 128 learnable 'kontext' query tokens that bridge a Qwen2.5-VL-7B MLLM to an MMDiT diffusion backbone through a 2-layer MLP connector. A three-stage progressive curriculum first activates the VLM's generative reasoning via LoRA fine-tuning against a lightweight ~870M diffusion head, then scales to a full MMDiT (~10× parameters) with the MLLM frozen to align kontext tokens to high-fidelity synthesis, and finally introduces a low-level VAE encoder with LoRA on the diffusion model for fine-grained editing and identity preservation. A shifted 2D RoPE scheme assigns source-image latents to negative spatial coordinates and reference-image latents to positive coordinates, enabling the model to distinguish pixel-fidelity tasks from identity-preservation tasks within a single in-context sequence. The design is validated by ablations showing that the combined text+query conditioning converges faster and achieves higher GenEval scores than either text-only MLP or Q-Former-style query-only alternatives.

## Results

On GenEval, Query-Kontext achieves an overall score of 0.88, matching the SOTA unified model BAGEL and surpassing Qwen-Image (0.87) and GPT-Image (0.84). On GEdit-Bench, it attains the highest overall editing scores of 7.66 (EN) and 7.65 (CN), outperforming Qwen-Image (7.56/7.52) and GPT-Image (7.53/7.30). For subject-driven generation on DreamBooth, it sets new SOTA with DINO 0.786 and CLIP-I 0.858, and on DreamBench it achieves the best CLIP-T (0.336) with competitive DINO (0.532) and CLIP-I (0.731).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GEdit-Bench, Query-Kontext achieves the best overall editing score (7.66 EN / 7.65 CN), surpassing Qwen-Image (7.56/7.52) by ~0.1 and GPT-Image (7.53/7.30) by ~0.13–0.35, while leading all prior unified models (BAGEL 6.52, Step1X-Edit 6.97) by a wide margin. On GenEval, it ties with BAGEL at 0.88 overall and edges out Qwen-Image (0.87) and GPT-Image (0.84), though it trails slightly on Counting (0.81 vs. BAGEL's 0.84). For single-subject generation (DreamBooth), it sets new SOTA on DINO (0.786 vs. BAGEL's 0.777) and CLIP-I (0.858 vs. Metaquery's 0.851), but is marginally behind OmniGen on CLIP-T (0.307 vs. 0.315). On multi-subject composition (DreamBench), it achieves the best CLIP-T (0.336) and competitive DINO (0.532) and CLIP-I (0.731), outperforming BAGEL (0.439/0.683/0.335) and OmniGen2 (0.488/0.716/0.332). The Perceptual Quality score (7.37) is notably lower than Qwen-Image (7.86) and GPT-Image (7.62), which the authors attribute to the absence of a dedicated RL or SFT quality-enhancement stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

Query-Kontext carves a middle path between two dominant UMM paradigms: assembled frameworks (MetaQuery, FLUX.1 Kontext, Step1X-Edit) that treat a frozen VLM as a static feature extractor, and native UMMs (BAGEL, Janus-Pro, OmniGen2) that jointly optimize autoregressive reasoning and diffusion rendering in a single transformer. The key architectural departure is the introduction of learnable 'kontext' query tokens that mediate between a fine-tuned VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) and a separate MMDiT diffusion backbone, explicitly decoupling semantic/spatial reasoning from pixel-level synthesis. The in-context conditioning of the diffusion model on concatenated noisy latent, low-level VAE features, and kontext tokens follows the lineage of FLUX.1 Kontext and In-context Edit, while the shifted 2D-RoPE for distinguishing source vs. reference images extends the positional encoding ideas from UNO-FLUX.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled architecture offers a practical efficiency win (~10% of the compute needed to train a large diffusion model or integrated multimodal transformer from scratch) while matching or exceeding SOTA across generation, editing, personalization, and multi-subject composition benchmarks. The three-stage curriculum (lightweight head → large diffusion → low-level encoder) provides a reusable recipe for aligning a strong VLM with an increasingly capable generator without capacity competition. Limitations include a noted gap in perceptual quality (no RL or SFT refinement stage), the need for full-parameter fine-tuning of the diffusion model in Stage 2 (freezing it fails at scale), and an unexplored connector scaling study. Open questions include whether the decoupling generalizes to video generation, whether the specific kontext token count and RoPE shifting scheme are optimal, and whether the approach can be extended to 3D or multi-frame synthesis.
