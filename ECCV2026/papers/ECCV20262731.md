## Problem

Existing incomplete multimodal learning methods primarily handle inter-modality missing (entire modalities absent) while largely ignoring intra-modality degradation (modalities present but corrupted by noise). In practice these two failure modes coexist, yet prior approaches like T2DR and TMDC treat them as separate problems in sequential two-stage pipelines, causing optimization conflicts and poor generalization to unseen corruption patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262731/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts multimodal inputs—audio waveforms (22,050 Hz or 16,000 Hz), visual frames (resized to 224×224), text sequences, and depth maps—each of which may be partially or fully corrupted. Rather than a binary present/absent flag, each modality carries a continuous degradation level, and the model must estimate per-sample, per-modality quality to determine how much each source contributes to the final fused representation. The output is a single class label (emotion, action, sentiment, or gesture category).

## Method

GIML unifies intra-modality degradation and inter-modality missing as a continuous quality-degradation spectrum, replacing the binary modality indicator with a continuous weight w∈[0,1] that smoothly decays from 1 (clean) to 0 (absent). A Noise-Semantic Decoupled (NSD) module parameterizes each modality embedding as a Gaussian distribution where the mean encodes task-relevant semantics and the variance captures degradation-induced uncertainty; a KL-divergence prior aligns the predicted variance with the true degradation intensity, and a reparameterization trick enables end-to-end training. A Noise-aware Quality Estimator (NQE) then maps the learned uncertainty σ to a calibrated noise-intensity score η̂ via a supervised MSE loss, and inverse-variance weighting converts these scores into adaptive fusion weights that downweight heavily corrupted modalities. This single-stage design avoids the optimization conflicts of two-stage pipelines and enables interpolation to unseen noise intensities and types.

## Results

On CREMA-D with fully available audio-visual modalities (intra ratio 0.0, 0.0), GIML achieves 73.94% accuracy and 74.08% F1, outperforming TMDC (66.99%/67.03%) and T2DR (67.93%/67.82%) by approximately 6–7 percentage points. On Kinetics-Sounds under the same clean setting, GIML reaches 69.51% accuracy versus 60.40% (TMDC) and 62.73% (T2DR). Under severe imbalanced intra-modality degradation (0.3, 0.7) on CREMA-D, GIML maintains 62.93% accuracy while TMDC drops to 57.23% and T2DR to 59.89%. GIML consistently achieves the best accuracy and F1 across all 18 evaluation settings on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIML is compared against two two-stage baselines: TMDC (Zhuang et al., arXiv 2025) and T2DR (Lin et al., Findings of ACL 2025), both of which handle intra-modality corruption and inter-modality missing sequentially rather than jointly. GIML wins on virtually every setting across all five datasets (CREMA-D, Kinetics-Sounds, MVSA-Single, MOSI, NVGesture). On the clean audio-visual setting of CREMA-D, GIML achieves 73.94% accuracy, surpassing TMDC by 6.95 points and T2DR by 6.01 points; on Kinetics-Sounds the margin is even larger at 9.11 points over TMDC. Under severe imbalanced degradation (e.g., intra ratio 0.3/0.7 on CREMA-D), GIML still leads by 5.70 points over TMDC and 3.04 points over T2DR. The advantage is most pronounced in single-modality settings (e.g., visual-only on CREMA-D: GIML 60.38% vs. TMDC 46.80% and T2DR 48.95%), suggesting that the continuous quality estimation and noise-semantic decoupling provide substantial gains when one modality is entirely absent. GIML also generalizes better to unseen noise intensities and unseen noise types (Gaussian, fog, rain, snow), where TMDC and T2DR show larger performance drops due to their discrete, corruption-specific treatment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (T2DR, TMDC) that first denoises intra-modality corruption and then handles inter-modality missing, instead unifying both as a continuous degradation spectrum within a single optimization stage. Conceptually, it repurposes variational inference machinery (Gaussian parameterization, reparameterization trick, KL regularization) originally developed for probabilistic embeddings and uncertainty estimation, redirecting it toward explicit noise-semantic decoupling in multimodal fusion. The quality estimation component draws on the inverse-variance weighting principle from ARL but replaces unsupervised reliability proxies (energy scores, output variance) with a supervised noise-intensity predictor trained via controlled noise injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continuous-degradation unification offers a clean, modality-agnostic formulation that naturally subsumes the conventional binary missing-modality setting, making it a practical framework for real-world systems where sensors degrade gradually rather than fail abruptly. However, the evaluation is limited to two recent baselines (TMDC, T2DR) and moderate-scale datasets, leaving open whether the advantage persists against stronger imputation-based or diffusion-based competitors at scale. An open question is whether the controlled noise injection during training adequately covers the diversity of real-world corruption (e.g., sensor-specific artifacts, occlusion patterns) or whether the quality estimator's generalization degrades under truly novel degradation mechanisms beyond the tested mask/Gaussian/fog/rain/snow spectrum.
