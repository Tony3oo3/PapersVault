## Problem

Style transfer remains fundamentally ill-posed because no definitive ground-truth stylization exists for a given content-style pair. Prior data-centric approaches (e.g., OmniStyle) synthesize pseudo-triplets using existing style transfer models, bounding supervision quality by surrogate model limitations and inheriting artifacts, content leakage, and distribution shift from real-world art. This paper addresses the absence of authentic, scalable supervision by inverting the problem: recovering natural content from artistic images so that unaltered artworks serve as genuine training targets.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a content image (a destylized natural image) and a style reference image as input and produces a single stylized image at 1024×1024 resolution. Training triplets are structured as <destylized image, cross-semantic style reference, original style image>, where the style image is the unmodified artwork (ground-truth target) and the destylized image is the content input. To prevent semantic leakage, each style target is paired with Top-5 style references drawn from *different* semantic categories (e.g., an animal target matched with non-animal references), forcing the model to decouple style from category-specific priors.

## Method

The core insight is a reverse formulation: instead of synthesizing stylized images from natural content, DeStylePipe progressively strips style from authentic artworks to recover structure-aligned natural content, making the original artwork the supervision target. The pipeline has three stages—(1) global destylization with Flux.2-Klein-9B under a universal instruction, (2) category-wise prompt rewriting via Qwen3-VL-Plus followed by Nano-Banana-Pro execution for harder styles, and (3) LoRA fine-tuning of a specialized destylization model for text-undescribable styles (clay, needle felted, origami). After each stage, DestyleCoT-Filter applies a structured Chain-of-Thought evaluation scoring content preservation (region-level object identification → object-wise consistency check) and style removal (style feature decomposition → residual analysis) on a 0–5 scale, admitting only samples scoring ≥4 on both dimensions. The resulting DeStyle-350K dataset (350K triplets, 500+ styles, 100 content classes) is used to fine-tune diffusion backbones (Flux.2-Klein-9B/4B via full fine-tuning, Qwen-Image-Edit via LoRA), which then perform standard content+style→stylized-image inference.

## Results

On BCS-Bench, the fine-tuned Flux.2-Klein-9B model trained on DeStyle-350K achieves the best scores on 6 of 8 metrics among style transfer methods, leading in style fidelity (CSD=0.5485, Qwen-S=4.1140), content preservation (CLIP-TI=0.3034, Qwen-C=4.5610), and semantic leakage (Qwen-L=0.0059). It trails only USO (DINO=0.6871) and StyleID (DINO=0.6860) on DINO similarity, and StyleShot (0.0833) on Style Loss. In the user study, the method achieves 28.19% Rank-1 and 56.65% Top-3 preference among style transfer baselines, and 22.56% Rank-1 / 68.60% Top-3 among image editing models, approaching closed-source systems like Nano-Banana-Pro and GPT-Image-1.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among dedicated style transfer methods, the proposed approach decisively outperforms OmniStyle (Qwen-S 4.1140 vs 2.8840), Qwen-Style (Qwen-L 0.0059 vs 0.0765), and AD (Qwen-L 0.0059 vs 0.3494), which all suffer from severe semantic leakage or weak stylization. USO and StyleID preserve content better (DINO 0.6871/0.6860 vs 0.6441) but transfer style poorly (Qwen-S 2.8612/2.5330 vs 4.1140), confirming a content-style trade-off that the DeStyle-350K-trained model avoids. StyleShot achieves the lowest Style Loss (0.0833) but at the cost of catastrophic content degradation (DINO 0.2963). Against image editing models, the method trails Nano-Banana-Pro and GPT-Image-1.5 on CSD (0.5485 vs 0.5674/0.5305) and Qwen-S (4.1140 vs 4.4552/4.4389), but achieves dramatically lower semantic leakage (Qwen-L 0.0059 vs 0.1185/0.0691) and the best CLIP-TI (0.3034), indicating superior style-content disentanglement. Fine-tuning on DeStyle-350K yields consistent gains across all three backbones (Flux.2-Klein-4B/9B, Qwen-Image-Edit), with Qwen-L dropping from 0.4380 to 0.0025 on Flux.2-Klein-4B, confirming backbone-agnostic supervision quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant stylization-based data construction paradigm exemplified by OmniStyle, which synthesizes pseudo-triplets by applying existing style transfer models to natural images and thereby inherits the surrogate models' artifacts and distributional biases. The key conceptual inversion—starting from authentic artworks and recovering their natural content via destylization, so that the unmodified art serves as the ground-truth target—repurposes off-the-shelf image editing models (Flux.2-Klein, Nano-Banana-Pro) and MLLM-based CoT filtering (Qwen3-VL-Plus) in a progressive multi-stage pipeline rather than introducing new model architectures. The work is thus positioned as a data-centric contribution that reframes the supervision problem rather than a model-centric advance, building on the broader diffusion-model editing ecosystem without modifying any of the underlying generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By anchoring supervision to unaltered original artworks, the destylization paradigm directly addresses the long-standing ground-truth absence problem that has forced prior methods to rely on noisy pseudo-labels or handcrafted statistical losses. The 350K-triplet dataset and the BCS-Bench (4,860 content-style pairs with demographic stratification) are likely to become standard resources for the style transfer community, and the consistent cross-architecture gains (Flux.2-Klein-4B/9B, Qwen-Image-Edit) suggest the supervision signal is broadly transferable. Open questions include whether the destylization ceiling (average style-removal score 4.57/5) constrains the upper bound of learnable style fidelity, whether the paradigm extends to video or 3D style transfer, and whether the heavy reliance on proprietary closed-source models (Nano-Banana-Pro, GPT-Image-1.5) for both data generation and evaluation limits reproducibility.
