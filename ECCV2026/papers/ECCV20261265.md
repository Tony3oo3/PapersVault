## Problem

Image matting is highly ill-posed when both foreground and background are richly textured, and single-image methods struggle to recover fine details in such cases. Existing approaches that add constraints (green screens, polarization, camera arrays, focal stacks) require specialized hardware or controlled capture, making them impractical for everyday photography. This paper asks whether the second frame naturally available in mobile burst capture—where slight camera motion induces foreground-background parallax—can serve as a practical, hardware-free cue for portrait matting.

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB portrait frames captured with a slight viewpoint change (burst photography) and produces an alpha matte and a pre-multiplied foreground image for a chosen base frame. Internally, it generates a trimap via BiRefNet (binary mask eroded/dilated by 100 px, yielding a 200-px uncertain band) and estimates foreground/background optical flow via GMFlow, with uncertain-region flow inpainted by nearest-neighbor extrapolation from certain regions. Inference operates on 448×448 patches with 224-px overlap, merged via a Gaussian window; the model is trained exclusively on synthetic composites and never sees real matting pairs.

## Method

The core design is an asymmetric dual-branch ViT-Small encoder/decoder (38.6M params, CroCo-initialized) with a ViTMatte head. The primary branch directly fuses the base frame with the background-aligned warped frame in pixel space, since background motion is smoother and more reliably estimated. The auxiliary branch processes the foreground-aligned warped frame but injects its features into the primary branch only through cross-attention layers, treating foreground alignment as a noisy cue rather than hard pixel-level evidence—this compensates for residual flow errors around thin structures like hair. Training uses synthetic composites from P3M-10K/HHM-2K foregrounds and BG-20K backgrounds with random affine parallax simulation and ~10-px motion noise, supervised by a separately-weighted L1 alpha loss (soft vs. hard pixels), Laplacian and gradient penalties, and a composition loss on the pre-multiplied foreground. The architecture is symmetric in weights but asymmetric in function, allowing graceful degradation to single-frame behavior when parallax is weak or alignment is unreliable.

## Results

On the synthetic test sets, Parallax Portrait Matting achieves the best scores across all metrics (SAD, MSE, Conn, Grad, MSE(αF)) on all three benchmarks. On PPM-100, it reaches SAD 4.13, MSE 2.28, and MSE(αF) 0.55, outperforming the strongest trimap-based baseline MatteFormer (SAD 5.53, MSE 2.72, MSE(αF) 0.96) by 25% on SAD and 43% on foreground color error. The gains are even more pronounced on the harder RWP-636 set, where MSE(αF) drops to 1.37 versus 2.11 for MatteFormer. The method also substantially outperforms video matting baselines (MaGGIe, MatAnyone) and trimap-free methods (MODNet, ViTAE-S) by large margins on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Parallax Portrait Matting outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all three test sets. Against the strongest trimap-based single-image method MatteFormer, it improves SAD by 25% (4.13 vs 5.53) and MSE(αF) by 43% (0.55 vs 0.96) on PPM-100, with similar relative gains on P3M-NP-500 and RWP-636. The trimap-free baselines (MODNet, ViTAE-S) are far behind, with ViTAE-S achieving SAD 18.24 and MSE(αF) 6.16 on PPM-100 versus 4.13 and 0.55 for the proposed method. Video matting methods MaGGIe and MatAnyone, which aggregate temporal information over full sequences, also lose substantially (SAD 27.41/26.88 vs 4.13), indicating that explicit parallax reasoning between two frames is more effective than generic temporal aggregation for this task. The largest relative improvement is in foreground color accuracy (MSE(αF)), where the method reduces error by 35–45% over the best baseline, which the authors highlight as critical for downstream compositing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of single-image matting, video matting, and multi-signal matting. Rather than requiring specialized capture hardware (green screens, polarization, camera arrays) or full video sequences, it repurposes the parallax naturally present in burst photography as a matting cue, drawing on the locally-smooth motion assumption from burst super-resolution [3]. Architecturally, it combines a ViT-based matting backbone (ViTMatte) with CroCo cross-view pre-training and GMFlow optical flow, but the key conceptual departure is the asymmetric treatment of foreground and background alignment: background-aligned views are fused directly in pixel space while foreground-aligned views enter only through cross-attention, a design motivated by the observation that background motion is far more reliably estimated than foreground motion around thin structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: burst photography is already ubiquitous on mobile devices, so the method requires no additional hardware or user effort beyond what casual portrait capture already produces. The 35–45% reduction in foreground color MSE over the strongest single-image baseline is directly relevant to downstream compositing, where background-contaminated colors are a persistent failure mode. Key limitations remain: motion estimation is decoupled from training and cannot be jointly optimized, performance may degrade under larger inter-frame motion, and the quantitative evaluation is confined to synthetic test sets with real-world results presented only qualitatively. Open questions include whether joint motion-matting optimization, integration with 3D foundation models, or extension beyond portrait scenes could close the remaining gap to ground-truth-quality mattes.
