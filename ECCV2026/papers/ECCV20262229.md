## Problem

Existing egocentric Video REC benchmarks operate on short clips (seconds to ~90 seconds) where target objects appear densely, failing to reflect real-world egocentric recordings that are long-form, untrimmed, and characterized by sparse object occurrences and complex activity transitions. This paper addresses the gap by introducing LongEgoRefer, a benchmark built on hour-scale egocentric videos where referents appear in only 1.3% of frames, demanding long-range temporal search and precise spatial grounding under realistic first-person conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262229/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an untrimmed egocentric video (average 45 minutes, minimum 20 minutes) and a natural language referring expression (average 63.5 words, substantially longer than prior benchmarks) and must output a single continuous appearance interval (t_start, t_end) along with a per-frame bounding box sequence B spanning that interval. The referring expressions are explicitly designed to be uniquely disambiguating within the full video, requiring the model to distinguish the target moment from visually similar segments elsewhere in the hours-long recording.

## Method

The benchmark is constructed from full-length Ego4D videos with per-frame tracking annotations from EgoTracks, filtered to retain only active human-object interaction tracks lasting ≥10 seconds. Referring expressions are generated via a two-stage LLM pipeline (Gemini 2.5 Flash produces 10 detailed captions, then aggregates them into a single cohesive expression) followed by a rigorous four-stage human curation process that substantially revised 95.39% of initial descriptions to ensure referential exclusivity. For evaluation, the paper establishes two training-free baselines: (1) a sliding-window strategy that partitions long videos into short clips and feeds each to existing video REC models (VideoLISA, Sa2VA, Grounded SAM2, SAM3), and (2) a hierarchical two-stage pipeline that first uses a VLM for global temporal grounding (identifying when the event occurs) and then uses Grounded SAM2 (Grounding DINO for initial detection + SAM2 for tracking) for local spatial localization, explicitly decoupling the 'when' and 'where' sub-problems.

## Results

On LongEgoRefer, GPT-5 achieves the best overall performance with tIoU@0.1 of 37.31, tIoU@0.5 of 12.55, mtIoU of 16.19, and mSTIoU of 8.76, substantially outperforming all other models. The best open-source model, Qwen3-VL 32B, reaches only tIoU@0.1 of 4.87 and mSTIoU of 0.97, revealing a dramatic gap between open and closed-source VLMs. Existing video REC models (VideoLISA, Sa2VA, Grounded SAM2, SAM3) perform poorly under sliding-window inference, with tIoU@0.1 scores below 0.60. Even GPT-5's absolute scores remain low, confirming that spatio-temporal grounding in 45-minute egocentric videos with 1.3% target appearance rates remains an open challenge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5 dominates all comparisons, achieving tIoU@0.1 of 37.31 versus 23.30 for Gemini 2.5 Pro (the second-best) and 4.87 for the best open-source model (Qwen3-VL 32B), a gap of over 7x between closed and open source. Among video REC models evaluated via sliding-window inference, Sa2VA (tIoU@0.1 = 0.60) and SAM3 (0.56) marginally outperform VideoLISA (0.45) and Grounded SAM2 (0.47), but all remain far below any VLM-based approach, confirming that short-clip architectures lack the global temporal awareness needed for hour-scale search. Within the open-source VLM group, Qwen3-VL 32B (tIoU@0.5 = 1.07) and InternVL 3.5 38B (tIoU@0.1 = 6.34) lead, while models specifically designed for long video understanding (LongVILA R1, LongVA) show no clear advantage over general-purpose VLMs. The closed-source Gemini family shows a clear scaling trend: 2.0 Flash (tIoU@0.1 = 5.14) → 2.5 Flash (10.08) → 2.5 Pro (23.30), with GPT-5 extending this trend further. Notably, GPT-5 outperforms Gemini 2.5 Pro (the model family used to generate initial referring expressions) by 14 points on tIoU@0.1, ruling out Gemini-specific linguistic bias in the benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the egocentric Video REC line (RefEgo, EgoMask) by repurposing the Ego4D/EgoTracks infrastructure to construct hour-scale, interaction-centric referring expressions, rather than introducing a new model architecture. Conceptually, it departs from prior benchmarks that operate on short, densely-annotated clips by deliberately introducing extreme temporal sparsity (1.3% appearance rate) and long, disambiguating language (63.5 words average), framing the task as a long-range search-and-ground problem rather than a short-clip detection problem. The evaluation methodology draws on the two-stage VLM + Grounded SAM2 paradigm, but the core contribution is the benchmark itself and the empirical characterization of where current models fail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even GPT-5 achieves only tIoU@0.1 of 37.31 and mtIoU of 16.19, exposing a substantial gap between current capabilities and the demands of realistic long-form egocentric grounding. The oracle temporal segmentation ablation (mvIoU jumping from 7.89 to 43.54) identifies temporal localization as the dominant bottleneck, suggesting that future progress requires better long-range temporal reasoning rather than improved spatial trackers. Open questions include whether a dedicated training corpus for hour-scale video REC can close this gap, and whether the benchmark's reliance on EgoTracks' six-minute clip annotations (remapped to full-length videos) introduces any annotation boundary artifacts that could affect evaluation fairness.
