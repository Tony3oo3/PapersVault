## Problem

Existing video editing methods cannot simultaneously apply precise visual modifications and maintain frame-accurate temporal synchronization with the source video. For portrait videos, this means every blink, gaze shift, and lip movement must occur at the exact same moment as in the original, while only the instructed visual aspects change. Most prior approaches either drift temporally, fail to faithfully apply the edit, or require task-specific training, leaving a gap in controllable, synchronized portrait video editing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source portrait video (81 frames at 20 FPS), a user-edited first frame (produced by any image editing tool such as QwenImageEdit-2509), and a text edit prompt as input. The source and target latent sequences are concatenated along the frame dimension and processed jointly by the transformer, with source tokens assigned a clean timestep (t=0) and target tokens receiving the current denoising timestep. The output is an 81-frame edited video that adopts the new appearance from the edited first frame while remaining frame-accurately synchronized with the source motion.

## Method

Sync-LoRA fine-tunes LTX-Video (a transformer-based latent diffusion model) with a rank-128 LoRA adapter using the rectified flow objective, where only the target (edited) branch is denoised while the source branch remains noise-free and provides motion guidance through shared 3D attention. The key insight is that synchronization quality is determined by training data rather than architecture: the authors generate over 20,000 side-by-side talking-head video pairs via Wan2.1 and then filter down to 512 pairs using a multi-channel synchronization score (40% speech, 30% gaze, 15% blink, 15% pose) computed from MediaPipe facial and pose landmarks. This compact, rigorously curated dataset teaches the model to propagate motion cues from the source to the edited view while applying only the localized changes defined by the first frame. At inference, the target latent is initialized from Gaussian noise and iteratively denoised via a flow-matching Euler loop, with the source tokens retaining t=0 throughout all steps.

## Results

On a 166-video benchmark spanning CelebV, CelebV-HQ, and TalkVid, Sync-LoRA achieves the best identity preservation (ArcFace similarity 0.75 vs. 0.73 for VACE, 0.69 for LucyEdit, 0.68 for FlowEdit, 0.63 for AnyV2V) and the best edit fidelity (Directional CLIP text-dual 0.21 vs. 0.20 for VACE, 0.18 for FlowEdit, 0.17 for AnyV2V, 0.11 for LucyEdit). On synchronization, Sync-LoRA scores 0.72 Speech Corr., 0.75 Gaze Corr., 0.55 Blink Corr., and 0.55 Pose Corr., trailing LucyEdit on speech (0.80) and gaze (0.82) but substantially outperforming VACE (0.37–0.39 speech, 0.63–0.69 gaze) and FlowEdit (0.50 speech, 0.56 gaze). A 2AFC user study with 23 participants confirms Sync-LoRA is preferred across all four criteria (Edit Fidelity, Synchronization, Identity Preservation, Overall) against every baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sync-LoRA wins on identity preservation (ArcFace 0.75) and edit fidelity (Directional CLIP text-dual 0.21) against all four baselines. LucyEdit achieves the highest raw synchronization scores (Speech 0.80, Gaze 0.82) but fails to actually apply the visual edit, scoring only 0.11 on Directional CLIP text-dual versus Sync-LoRA's 0.21, indicating it preserves motion by essentially ignoring the edit instruction. VACE benefits from strong external conditioning (pose/canny/depth maps) and matches Sync-LoRA on CLIP-Text alignment (0.33) but lags on temporal correspondence (Speech 0.39, Blink 0.36) and identity (0.73). FlowEdit and AnyV2V are consistently weaker across all three axes, with AnyV2V showing the poorest identity preservation (0.63) and FlowEdit the weakest blink/pose synchronization (0.33/0.38). The key trade-off is that LucyEdit's superior sync metrics come at the cost of edit failure, while Sync-LoRA is the only method that simultaneously achieves strong synchronization, faithful edit application, and identity consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IC-LoRA paradigm (Huang et al., 2024) from static image panels to the video domain, introducing a noise-free source video stream as explicit frame-level motion guidance during denoising. It builds on LTX-Video's per-token timestep mechanism to differentiate source (clean, t=0) and target (noisy, t>0) streams within a shared transformer, and leverages the observed in-context generation capability of video diffusion transformers (Fei et al., 2025). The key conceptual departure from prior video editing methods (VACE, LucyEdit, AnyV2V, FlowEdit) is that synchronization is achieved not through architectural modifications or external conditioning signals, but through a rigorously filtered training set of temporally aligned video pairs, making data curation the primary driver of temporal fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Frame-accurate temporal synchronization is essential for practical talking-head applications (advertising, film, interactive media) where even small misalignments in blinks, gaze, or articulation render output unfaithful to the source performance. The paper's central finding—that synchronization quality is governed by training data curation rather than model architecture—offers a transferable principle for other video generation/editing tasks. Limitations include the 81-frame (~4 s) sequence length, the assumption of geometric alignment between the edited first frame and source, and degradation under fast or large-scale motion. Open questions include whether the approach extends to longer videos, multi-modal (audio-visual) synchronization, and whether the data-curation insight generalizes beyond portrait editing to broader video editing scenarios.
