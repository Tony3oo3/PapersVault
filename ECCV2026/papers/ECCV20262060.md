## Problem

Event cameras offer ultra-high temporal resolution, low latency, and inherent sparsity that naturally align with anomaly detection, yet the field lacks both dedicated event-stream VAD benchmarks and modeling strategies that exploit the asynchronous, bursty structure of event data. Existing VAD methods designed for synchronous RGB frames do not transfer effectively to the sparse, non-uniform temporal dynamics of event streams, and prior event-based VAD work (e.g., SNN-based approaches) underutilizes the cross-modal alignment capabilities of vision-language models.

## Input / Output

During inference, EWAD consumes only discretized event frames (generated at 100 fps with adaptive per-bin event counts bounded at 10,000 to prevent ghosting) and produces frame-level binary anomaly confidence scores, multi-class anomaly category logits, and training-free spatial bounding boxes derived from thresholded event activation maps with morphological refinement. During training, paired RGB frames are additionally required to provide teacher signals for knowledge distillation, but the model operates unimodally at test time. The event frames are constructed by sampling one frame every 16 RGB frames as a window center with ±8-frame extensions, yielding non-overlapping temporal segments.

## Method

EWAD builds on the VadCLIP ViT-DNN architecture and introduces three event-specific mechanisms: (1) an Event-Density Aware Dynamic Sampling (EDS) strategy that partitions frames into high- and low-density sets via a nucleus-sampling threshold (τ_d=0.95) and performs multinomial sampling within each set at a fixed ratio (8:2), prioritizing bursty event regions while retaining contextual background; (2) an Event-Modulated Distance-Decay Attention (EDA) mechanism that computes pairwise attention weights as exp(−λ·|t̃_i−t̃_j|/(d_j+ε)), jointly encoding temporal distance and local event density so that denser, closer tokens receive higher attention, enabling long-range temporal modeling while preserving sparsity; and (3) a two-level RGB-to-event knowledge distillation loss combining binary MSE on anomaly scores and temperature-scaled KL divergence on logit-standardized multi-class distributions, transferring high-level semantic priors from a pretrained RGB teacher to the event student. Training optimizes a composite MIL classification loss (BCE + NCE + contrastive) plus the distillation loss, while inference requires no RGB input.

## Results

On the real event-camera UCF-Crime benchmark, EWAD achieves 72.36% frame-level AUC, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art MSF (65.01%) by +7.35%. On the simulated benchmarks, EWAD attains 76.55% AUC on UCF-Crime, 64.17% on CCTV-Fight, and 58.30% on UBnormal, outperforming all adapted RGB-based baselines (DMU, VadCLIP, DeepMIL) across all three datasets. For spatial anomaly localization, the training-free event-only approach yields 13.28% TIoU, exceeding earlier RGB methods (C3D: 7.20%, NLN: 12.20%) but trailing the strongest RGB baselines (STPrompts: 23.09%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the real event-camera UCF-Crime data, EWAD (72.36% AUC) decisively outperforms all eight SNN-based baselines, with the largest gap over the previous best MSF (65.01%, +7.35%) and SFormer (62.78%, +9.58%). On the simulated benchmarks, EWAD consistently leads: on UCF-Crime it beats DMU (73.47%) by +3.08% and VadCLIP (73.01%) by +3.54%; on CCTV-Fight it surpasses VadCLIP (63.08%) by +1.09% and DMU (57.85%) by +6.32%; on UBnormal it edges out DMU (57.46%) by +0.84% and substantially outperforms VadCLIP (53.19%) by +5.11%. Notably, no knowledge distillation is applied on CCTV-Fight and UBnormal, yet EWAD still wins, indicating the architectural gains generalize. For spatial localization, EWAD's training-free event-only TIoU of 13.28% surpasses TSN (2.60%), C3D (7.20%), and NLN (12.20%) but falls short of the strongest RGB methods—Liu et al. (16.40%), VadCLIP (22.05%), and STPrompts (23.09%)—which the authors attribute to limited temporal discriminability of event-only models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VadCLIP's ViT-based temporal modeling architecture, repurposing its classification and alignment heads while inserting event-specific modules (density-aware sampling and distance-decay attention) into the temporal pathway. It departs from the sole prior event-based VAD effort (Qian et al.'s SNN-based MSF) by abandoning spiking neural networks in favor of standard ViT features, and by introducing cross-modal RGB-to-event knowledge distillation—a mechanism absent from all prior event-stream VAD work. The weakly supervised MIL formulation inherited from Sultani et al. is retained, but the event-density-aware sampling strategy represents a novel adaptation of nucleus-sampling ideas from LLMs to the sparse, bursty structure of event data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first large-scale simulated event-stream VAD benchmarks (UCF-Crime, CCTV-Fight, UBnormal) and a strong ViT-based baseline, this paper creates the infrastructure needed to make event-centric VAD a reproducible research direction. However, the reliance on v2e-simulated events rather than real camera data remains a significant limitation, and the spatial localization results (13.28% TIoU) are explicitly framed as a feasibility study, falling well short of current RGB SOTA. Open questions include whether real event data would substantially alter the optimal modeling strategy, whether RGB-event fusion can close the remaining gap to RGB-only methods, and whether the simulated benchmarks faithfully capture the noise and sparsity characteristics of physical event sensors.
