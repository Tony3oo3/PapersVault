## Problem

Task-agnostic pruning for vision-language models—where a model is pruned once and reused across diverse downstream tasks without re-pruning or retraining—remains largely unexplored. Existing one-shot pruning methods (Wanda, SparseGPT) rely on LLM-oriented activation-magnitude importance measures that introduce modality bias in VLMs, since text and vision branches exhibit markedly different activation scales and outlier patterns, and their importance estimates are sensitive to calibration data choice.

## Input / Output

PORTA consumes a pretrained VLM checkpoint (CLIP-ViT-bigG, BLIP-base, or Qwen2-VL) together with a small set of generic calibration image-text pairs (as few as 16 samples suffice; MSCOCO's 82,783 pairs used as default). It outputs a sparsified VLM with a target global sparsity (45%–65%) that is directly deployable for zero-shot classification, image-text retrieval, and VQA without any fine-tuning, re-pruning, or task-specific recalibration. The pruning is unstructured at the weight level, and the same pruned weights serve all downstream tasks.

## Method

PORTA replaces the standard activation-magnitude importance criterion with an activation-variance-based score: for each input feature dimension j, it computes v_j = Var_{b,t}(X_{b,t,j}) across the batch and token/patch axes, then forms the per-weight importance S_{i,j} = v_j · |W_{i,j}|, which is stable across modalities because variance dispersion is comparable in vision and language encoders (unlike magnitude). For layer-wise sparsity allocation, PORTA computes a per-layer score s_layer = (1/D_out) Tr(W^T W Σ̂_X) using a diagonal-covariance approximation of the input feature space, then assigns each layer a pruning ratio r_i proportional to (1 − p_i)^α where p_i is the normalized layer importance, so that layers with higher representational utility receive lower sparsity. The entire procedure is one-shot and retraining-free: variance statistics computed for importance estimation are reused for ratio allocation, keeping total pruning time around 195 s on a single GPU.

## Results

On CLIP-ViT-bigG at 65% sparsity, PORTA achieves 28.14 TR@1 / 51.26 TR@5 on MSCOCO retrieval and 90.49% / 62.29% / 31.38% / 18.27% on CIFAR-10 / CIFAR-100 / ImageNet / Flowers-102, improving mean performance across all eight metrics by 12.6% over SparseGPT and 21.5% over Wanda. On BLIP at 45% sparsity, PORTA attains a 90.26 mean retrieval score (vs. 89.95 for SparseGPT), and on Qwen2-VL at 50% sparsity it reaches 54.30% average ScienceQA accuracy, surpassing the second-best baseline Wanda (53.43%) by 0.87 points. PORTA completes pruning in ~195 s, 3.96× faster than SparseGPT and 2.06× faster than ECoFLaP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PORTA outperforms all four baselines across every sparsity level and every benchmark. Against Wanda, the gap widens sharply with sparsity: at 65% on CLIP, PORTA achieves 90.49% vs. 69.81% on CIFAR-10 and 62.29% vs. 28.86% on CIFAR-100, a 21.5% mean improvement over Wanda across all eight metrics. Against SparseGPT, PORTA leads on all metrics at 65% (e.g., 31.38% vs. 25.96% ImageNet, 37.32 vs. 35.00 IR@5), a 12.6% mean gain. ECoFLaP degrades severely beyond 50% sparsity, dropping to 10.20% ImageNet accuracy at 65% versus PORTA's 31.38%, and its retrieval scores collapse (7.22 IR@1 vs. 17.31). Multiflow, despite being designed for task-agnostic VLM pruning, essentially fails in the zero-shot setting without fine-tuning, yielding near-zero retrieval scores (0.02–0.12) and single-digit classification accuracy across all sparsity levels. On the generative VLM Qwen2-VL (ScienceQA, 50% sparsity), PORTA still leads all baselines with 54.30% average accuracy, edging out Wanda (53.43%) by 0.87 points and SparseGPT (50.78%) by 3.52 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

PORTA directly adapts Wanda's importance formulation (activation statistic × weight magnitude) by substituting activation variance for activation magnitude, motivated by the observation that magnitude-based statistics exhibit strong modality-dependent scale differences between vision and language encoders in VLMs. It departs from LLM-oriented one-shot pruning (Wanda, SparseGPT) by arguing their outlier-driven assumptions do not transfer to multimodal architectures, and from VLM-specific methods (Multiflow, ECoFLaP) by eliminating the need for task-specific fine-tuning or zeroth-order gradient estimation. The adaptive layer-wise sparsity allocation draws on the layer-adaptive sparsity literature but grounds the per-layer ratio in output feature variance rather than learned or heuristic criteria, yielding a fully retraining-free pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PORTA offers a practical prune-once workflow: a single calibration pass on generic image-text data produces a sparse VLM that transfers across classification, retrieval, and VQA without re-pruning or retraining, reducing deployment overhead for edge and real-time settings. The calibration robustness (≤1% fluctuation across Flickr30K, MSCOCO, and Visual Genome) and near-flat performance from 16 to 2048 calibration samples make the method easy to operationalize. Open questions include whether the variance-based criterion extends to even higher sparsity (>70%), to training-based fine-tuning scenarios, and to broader multimodal families (audio-visual, video-language), and whether the diagonal-covariance approximation remains sufficient for very deep or heterogeneous architectures.
