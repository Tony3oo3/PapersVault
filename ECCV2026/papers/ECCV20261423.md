## Problem

Existing dynamic view synthesis methods are designed for narrow settings where input and target cameras are spatially nearby with high overlap, relying on explicit depth reprojection, test-time optimization, or limited camera trajectories. These approaches fail to generalize to extreme camera displacements where little or no spatial overlap exists between viewpoints, making it unclear whether they learn robust 4D scene understanding or merely perform local inpainting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261423/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular video (T×H×W×3) with known 6-DoF camera poses and intrinsics, plus a target camera trajectory, and produces a generated video from the target viewpoint. Camera parameters are encoded as dense Plücker ray-space maps (per-pixel ray and moment vectors, 6 channels total) rather than explicit depth or pose parameters, supporting arbitrary camera models including non-pinhole. All poses are expressed relative to the target viewpoint at t=0, and the model operates end-to-end in ~10 seconds of inference without any test-time optimization or auxiliary depth estimation network.

## Method

AnyView builds on NVIDIA's Cosmos latent diffusion transformer (~2B parameters) and deliberately avoids explicit depth reprojection or warped conditioning, instead relying entirely on a learned implicit 4D representation. Camera information is encoded as Plücker vectors (ray + moment) and tokenized alongside RGB latents using the same video tokenizer; the two viewpoints' multimodal tokens (RGB + Plücker) are concatenated along the channel dimension and stacked along the sequence dimension, yielding 2·t·h·w tokens fed into the diffusion transformer with rotary positional and per-view embeddings. Training uses L2 loss on latent tokens with curriculum learning (384→576 max dimension) over 40K iterations on 64 H200 GPUs, initialized from Cosmos pretrained weights. The model is trained on a mixture of 12 multi-domain 3D/4D datasets (Robotics, Driving, 3D, Other) with weighted sampling to ensure domain balance, enabling zero-shot generalization to unseen domains and camera configurations.

## Results

On AnyViewBench (in-distribution extreme DVS), AnyView achieves an average PSNR of 17.78 dB, SSIM of 0.533, LPIPS of 0.399, and FVD of 1239, outperforming the best baseline (GCD) by +3.83 dB PSNR and reducing FVD by 3.3×. In the zero-shot extreme setting, AnyView maintains a lead with 12.13 dB PSNR and FVD of 1757, versus 10.96 dB and 4348 for GCD. For geometric consistency, AnyView achieves 0.217 translation error and 0.654° rotation error (in-distribution), roughly 4× better than the next-best method (GCD at 0.919 / 2.774°). On the narrow DyCheck iPhone benchmark, AnyView (13.47 dB) trails methods with test-time optimization (CogNVS 16.94, Shape of Motion 16.72) but outperforms all zero-shot baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyView decisively outperforms all six extreme-DVS baselines (GCD, TrajAttn, GEN3C, TrajCrafter, CogNVS) on AnyViewBench, gaining +3.8 to +5.5 dB PSNR in the in-distribution setting and +1.2 to +1.8 dB in the zero-shot setting, while also achieving the lowest FVD (1239 vs. 3100–4126 in-distribution). On the narrow DyCheck iPhone benchmark, AnyView (13.47 dB) loses to CogNVS (16.94) and Shape of Motion (16.72), which both use test-time optimization (~1 h inference), but beats all zero-shot baselines including GCD (11.43), GEN3C (10.13), and TrajAttn (10.30). TrajCrafter (14.24 dB) slightly edges out AnyView on DyCheck iPhone, the one narrow-setting comparison where AnyView does not lead. On Kubric-4D (narrow), AnyView (21.21 dB) marginally beats ReCapture (20.92) and TrajCrafter (20.93) while remaining competitive with CogNVS (22.63, which uses GT depth + TTO). In the geometric consistency evaluation (Table 4), AnyView's implicit representation yields 0.217 translation error and 0.654° rotation error in-distribution—roughly 4× better than GCD (0.919 / 2.774°) and 10× better than TrajAttn (2.219 / 5.872°)—demonstrating that the model learns genuine 3D structure without explicit depth supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnyView builds on the video diffusion transformer lineage (SVD → CogVideoX → Cosmos) and the implicit DVS direction pioneered by GCD, which first showed that internet-scale video diffusion models can be repurposed for camera-controlled view synthesis via conditioning. It departs from the dominant explicit-reprojection paradigm (CogNVS, GEN3C, TrajCrafter, TrajAttn) by rejecting warped depth maps entirely, relying instead on Plücker-ray conditioning and a diverse 12-dataset training mixture to learn multi-view geometry implicitly. The paper also extends the evaluation landscape by introducing AnyViewBench, which pushes DVS from the 'narrow' regime (small viewpoint changes, high overlap) into an 'extreme' regime with large displacements and minimal overlap, a setting where prior benchmarks and methods were not designed to operate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The extreme DVS setting is arguably the more realistic one for downstream applications (autonomous driving with multi-camera rigs, robotics with ego/exo views, VR/AR), where neighboring cameras have little spatial overlap and the model must hallucinate large unobserved regions. The finding that a purely implicit model, trained on diverse data, outperforms explicit depth-reprojection methods by a wide margin in this regime challenges the assumption that geometric supervision is necessary for 3D consistency. Limitations remain: known input poses are required, there are no hard geometric guarantees, and heavily occluded regions can still produce plausible-but-incorrect content. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences, whether the implicit geometry can be made more controllable, and whether additional 4D data sources (e.g., Infinigen) would further close the gap on zero-shot generalization.
