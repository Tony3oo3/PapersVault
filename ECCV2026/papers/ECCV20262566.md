## Problem

Applying layout-aware single-image 3D generation independently to each video frame produces severe temporal inconsistencies—flickering textures, structural discontinuities in unobserved regions, and positional drift—because the model hallucinates different content for occluded portions at every step. Existing 4D methods either assume fixed topology via canonical-plus-deformation paradigms or produce objects in isolation without scene-level spatial context, leaving a gap for training-free, spatially-grounded 4D generation from monocular video.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video with pre-segmented foreground masks, along with per-frame dynamic point maps and camera trajectories recovered via 3D/4D reconstruction (e.g., MoGe, DUSt3R-style pipelines). These point maps serve dual roles: spatial conditioning for the generation backbone and geometric alignment targets for scene placement. The output is a temporally coherent sequence of 3D objects with detailed geometry and texture, each carrying an explicit layout pose (scale, rotation, translation) that positions it within the observed scene, enabling direct in-scene rendering rather than canonical-space isolation.

## Method

Tempo-SAM3D is a training-free extension of SAM3D (a layout-aware flow-matching DiT on the TRELLIS architecture) that injects temporal consistency at three complementary levels without modifying model weights. At the attention level, Dual-Path Temporal Attention maintains bounded, importance-scored KV caches in both cross-attention (propagating 2D visual memory from previously observed image patches) and self-attention (propagating 3D structural memory of latent tokens), with eviction governed by complementarity, usage, confidence, popularity, and temporal decay. At the solver level, gradient-guided velocity correction augments the flow-matching ODE velocity with a temporal smoothness loss in latent space, a one-directional Chamfer distance to the observed point map (Stage 1), and an LPIPS rendering loss against both the current frame and the previous frame's rendering (Stage 2). At the post-processing level, HexPlane decomposition factorizes per-frame latent features into six 2D spatiotemporal planes, acting as a low-rank temporal filter, while a joint pose optimization balances per-frame Chamfer alignment with inter-frame smoothness.

## Results

On the Tempo-SAM3D dataset (spatially-grounded evaluation), Tempo-SAM3D achieves LPIPS 0.119, PSNR 20.12, FVD 732.45, IoU 83.8%, D-RMSE 0.018, and ACC₅cm 95.8%, outperforming the layout-aware per-frame baseline SAM3D (FVD 1218.63, IoU 73.5%) and the video-to-4D method V2M4 (FVD 781.56). On the V2M4 benchmark, Tempo-SAM3D achieves the best FVD on Complex sequences (642.38 vs. V2M4's 666.04) and competitive results on Simple sequences (841.27 vs. V2M4's 825.59), all without per-scene training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tempo-SAM3D outperforms all four baselines on the Tempo-SAM3D dataset across every reported metric. Against SAM3D (the per-frame layout-aware baseline), it reduces FVD by 40% (732 vs. 1219) and improves IoU from 73.5% to 83.8%, demonstrating that temporal consistency mechanisms substantially improve both coherence and spatial accuracy. Against V2M4 (a per-scene-trained video-to-4D method), Tempo-SAM3D achieves lower FVD (732 vs. 782) and better LPIPS (0.119 vs. 0.138) while requiring no per-scene optimization. On the V2M4 benchmark, Tempo-SAM3D wins on Complex sequences (FVD 642 vs. 666 for V2M4) but is slightly behind V2M4 on Simple sequences (FVD 841 vs. 826), suggesting the temporal mechanisms are most beneficial for large-scale motions. TRELLIS and DreamMesh4D are clearly outperformed on all metrics, with TRELLIS showing the worst temporal consistency (FVD 1456) due to its purely per-frame, layout-unaware design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SAM3D's layout-aware single-image 3D generation (built on TRELLIS's structured-latent DiT with flow matching) along the temporal axis, injecting temporal consistency at three levels—attention (dual-path KV caches), solver (gradient-guided velocity correction), and post-processing (HexPlane decomposition)—without modifying model weights. It departs from the dominant canonical-plus-deformation paradigm in 4D reconstruction (D-NeRF, HexPlane-based methods) and from per-scene optimization approaches (V2M4, DreamMesh4D) by treating each frame as an independent generation conditioned on shared temporal memory. The spatially-grounded output (objects placed at correct scene positions via layout awareness) distinguishes it from prior video-to-4D methods that produce objects in isolation in canonical space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, multi-level temporal consistency principle is broadly applicable to other per-frame generative pipelines beyond 3D, and the layout-aware spatial grounding addresses a practical gap in AR/VR and robotics where generated objects must be correctly positioned in the observed scene. Limitations include bounded KV cache capacity (constraining long-sequence coherence), reliance on SAM3D's per-frame quality as a ceiling, and the fact that the spatial grounding evaluation rests on a single self-constructed dataset of 20 sequences. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very long videos, whether the KV-cache mechanism generalizes to architectures without explicit cross-attention to image patches, and whether the HexPlane post-processing can be made online rather than requiring all frames to be generated first.
