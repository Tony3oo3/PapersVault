## Problem

Existing VLMs and LLMs can answer spatial questions but cannot execute structured, multi-step 3D layout edits from natural language. Prior language-driven editing pipelines rely on external constraint solvers or differentiable optimizers with manually specified objectives, limiting flexibility for compositional instructions and offering little interpretability. No prior system directly reasons over a structured 3D scene graph to perform iterative, physically consistent layout rearrangement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261383/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a 3D scene graph serialized as a JSON dictionary (each node stores node_type, center_location, axis-aligned dimensions, roll/pitch/yaw rotation, and an optional caption; edges encode contact or containment relations), a natural-language instruction, and optionally a rendered RGB image for VLM backbones. It produces a structured reasoning trace of step-by-step scene-graph edits followed by a final updated scene graph in JSON, constrained to a strict  + JSON format that ensures every intermediate state is a verifiable graph transformation rather than free-form prose.

## Method

3D-Layout-R1 replaces free-form chain-of-thought with a chain-of-graph-edits: each reasoning step is an explicit, verifiable JSON mutation to the scene graph, embedding 3D spatial logic directly in the generation process. Training is two-stage: first, CoT-SFT on 15k synthetic scenes (across sorting, spatial alignment, and room-editing tasks) with reasoning traces generated by DeepSeek-R1 to teach the model structured multi-step layout reasoning; second, GRPO reinforcement learning with a composite reward combining 3D IoU against ground-truth boxes, a normalized collision-free score penalizing interpenetrations among non-container objects, and a format-compliance term enforcing the structured output schema. The IoU reward uses semantic matching (textual descriptions) with a geometric IoU fallback for node correspondence, providing dense, continuous supervision that avoids the sparsity of exact-match rewards. This structured-reasoning-plus-RL pipeline yields 15% higher mIoU and 25–30% lower center-distance error over CoT-SFT and vanilla GRPO baselines, and up to 20% higher mIoU over zero-shot LLMs.

## Results

On the 3D Sorting benchmark (Table 2), the best post-trained model Qwen3-8B (Ours) achieves 0.924 mean IoU, 0.145 center distance, 1.000 collision-free score, and 0.510 edit distance, substantially outperforming all zero-shot LLM/VLM baselines. The strongest zero-shot baseline, DeepSeek-R1, reaches 0.776 mIoU, while LayoutGPT with Gemini-2.5 achieves 0.781 mIoU. On the Spatial Alignment benchmark, Qwen2.5-7B (Ours) attains 0.809 mIoU, exceeding Gemini 2.5 Pro (0.759) and Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct (0.786). On Room Editing, Qwen3-8B (Ours) reaches 0.983 mIoU with 0.031 center distance, far surpassing the best zero-shot model Gemini 2.5 Pro (0.591 mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against zero-shot LLMs, the paper's best model (Qwen3-8B Ours, 0.924 mIoU) exceeds DeepSeek-R1 (0.776) by ~19% and Gemini 2.5 Flash (0.746) by ~24% on the Sorting benchmark. LayoutGPT with Gemini-2.5 (0.781 mIoU) and Qwen3-235B (0.763) are both surpassed by the 8B post-trained model, demonstrating that structured scene-graph reasoning with RL compensates for the 30× parameter gap. Among post-trained baselines, structured CoT-SFT alone (e.g., Qwen3-8B CoT-SFT at 0.635 mIoU) underperforms the full pipeline (0.924) by ~31%, while Vanilla GRPO without structured CoT-SFT (0.850) still lags by ~8%, confirming that the scene-graph reasoning trace provides essential guidance for RL. On Spatial Alignment, the 7B post-trained models (0.790–0.809 mIoU) match or exceed Gemini 2.5 Pro (0.759) and Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct (0.786). On Room Editing, the gap is most dramatic: Qwen3-8B (Ours) achieves 0.983 mIoU versus Gemini 2.5 Pro's 0.591, a ~66% relative improvement, though the paper notes this task is simpler and CoT-SFT alone already captures most of the structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in language-driven 3D layout editing, where LLMs act as high-level planners that emit spatial constraints for external solvers (LayoutGPT, InstructScene, EditRoom). Instead, 3D-Layout-R1 treats the 3D bounding-box scene graph as an iterative reasoning canvas, producing structured graph-edit traces as its chain-of-thought, and refines outputs via GRPO with a dense 3D IoU reward. Conceptually, it merges the structured-CoT line of work (constraining reasoning to verifiable intermediate states) with the GRPO training recipe popularized by DeepSeek-R1, applied here to a spatially grounded task. The scene-graph representation itself draws on prior spatial-reasoning VLMs (SpatialRGPT, SpatialLLM) but repurposes it from passive VQA to active multi-step editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The released 15k-scene benchmark is the first dedicated to multi-step, language-guided editing of existing 3D layouts, filling a gap between single-object placement and full scene generation. A notable finding is that in perfect-input settings the LLM-only backbone (Qwen2.5-7B) actually outperforms its VLM counterpart (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) on sorting, suggesting the visual modality can introduce noise when the scene graph is already clean; the VLM's advantage emerges only under missing captions or noisy boxes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary scenes without pre-structured graphs, how it handles continuous dynamics and inter-object physics beyond axis-aligned IoU, and whether the RL gains observed on complex tasks (sorting) will persist as task difficulty increases further.
