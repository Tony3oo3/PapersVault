## Problem

Existing geolocation methods—whether grid-cell classifiers or single-shot LVLM prompts—are not designed to simulate an adversary who adaptively allocates effort and leverages external tools to maximize inference success. As LVLMs lower the barrier to geolocation, it remains unclear how much additional privacy risk emerges when a model is wrapped in an agentic framework that mimics human multi-step reasoning with web search, segmentation, and experience retrieval.

## Input / Output

The agent takes a single RGB image (as shared on social media platforms) and produces a hierarchical geolocation prediction at country, region/state, and city granularity, accompanied by a consolidated evidence-based explanation. A weighted heuristic score computed from eight visual cues (landmarks, text, architecture, geographic features, image quality, contextual hints, scene type, multi-cue bonus) classifies the image into five difficulty levels, which drives adaptive tool selection. Predictions of 'unknown' are explicitly treated as incorrect, and the agent's iterative refinement loop continues until a sufficiently complete answer is produced or a time budget is exhausted.

## Method

GEO-Detective is a four-stage agentic pipeline that adaptively combines four specialized tools based on image difficulty. Stage 1 (Visual Feature Analysis) assigns a difficulty score via a weighted heuristic over eight visual cues. Stage 2 (Strategy Execution) selects and combines tools: direct LVLM prompting, experience-augmented prompting (where GeoCLIP image-text similarity scores guide prompt optimization over five predefined geographic categories, with optimized prompts cached for retrieval on similar images), LVLM-based geographic feature segmentation (predicting bounding boxes for location-relevant elements and iteratively refining them), and vision-based reverse image search (submitting the full image or segmented crops to external search engines, then filtering results via GeoCLIP similarity threshold). Stage 3 (Result Synthesis) consolidates heterogeneous outputs—direct predictions, search reports with extracted metadata, and segment-level evidence—using a rule-based conflict resolution that prioritizes explicit place names and independent supporting signals. Stage 4 (Iterative Refinement) self-evaluates completeness and coherence, invoking fallback strategies through a central planner when deficiencies are detected, continuing until the answer is sufficient or resources are exhausted.

## Results

On IM2GPS3K, GEO-Detective achieves 11.3% accuracy within 1 km and 47.5% within 25 km, the best 25 km result among all evaluated agentic and trained geolocation methods. Against the baseline LVLM o3, the agent delivers 3.0–5.0% absolute accuracy gains across difficulty levels at the country level, and up to 8.0% gains on GPT-4o for difficult and very difficult images. The agent also cuts the 'unknown' prediction rate by more than 50.6% (e.g., from 55.7% to 28.9% on very difficult images), making it substantially more likely to produce a concrete location prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEO-Detective outperforms both agentic baselines on IM2GPS3K: it edges out GeoMiner (47.5% vs 46.7% at 25 km) and substantially surpasses smileGeo (47.5% vs 38.2% at 25 km). Against trained geolocation models, it wins at 25 km over PIGEON (47.5% vs 36.7%) and GeoCLIP (47.5% vs 34.5%), but trails GeoCLIP at 1 km (11.3% vs 14.1%). The LVLM-based methods G3 and Img2Loc achieve higher 1 km accuracy (16.6% and 15.3% respectively) than GEO-Detective, yet the agent still leads them at 25 km (47.5% vs 40.9% and 39.8%). Against the baseline LVLM o3 without any agent scaffolding, GEO-Detective gains 3.0–5.0% at the country level and up to 8.0% on GPT-4o for difficult images, while reducing the 'unknown' rate by over half on challenging cases. On the contamination-free DoxBench, the agent shows the largest relative gains on difficult samples (e.g., o3 country-level accuracy rises from 61.1% to 72.2%), confirming that the improvements stem from the agent framework rather than pretraining exposure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GeoCLIP's image-text alignment as a scoring mechanism for prompt optimization and reverse-search filtering, but departs from prior geolocation work by framing the problem as an adversarial, multi-step reasoning task rather than a single-shot classification or retrieval problem. While recent agentic frameworks (GeoMiner, smileGeo) and LVLM-based geolocation systems (G3, Img2Loc, GeoReasoner) apply general-purpose reasoning or retrieval to geolocation, GEO-Detective introduces a difficulty-adaptive strategy selection mechanism and a suite of geolocation-specific tools (experience-augmented prompting, geographic feature segmentation, vision-based reverse image search) that collectively simulate how a human investigator would escalate effort based on available visual cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that agentic reasoning substantially amplifies geolocation privacy risks—reducing the 'unknown' prediction rate by over 50% and improving accuracy on difficult images by 3–8% over strong baselines—while simultaneously showing that most practical defenses (visual prompt injection, trigger-based, EXIF modification) offer limited protection against the agent. The finding that the agent is more robust to countermeasures than the underlying LVLM raises an open question about how to design defenses that account for multi-tool, iterative reasoning rather than single-pass model behavior. A notable limitation is that the agent can actually degrade performance on easy/moderate DoxBench samples (e.g., GPT-4o country-level drops from 99.1% to 90.6%), suggesting that expanded search can amplify ambiguity when cues are misleading.
