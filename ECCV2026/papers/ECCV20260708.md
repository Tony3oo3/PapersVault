## Problem

Parametric human models like SMPL use simplified kinematics that produce anatomically implausible poses, limiting their use in biomechanics. The SKEL model re-rigs SMPL with an anatomically accurate skeleton and constrained joints, but estimating its 46-dimensional pose parameters from a single image remains challenging due to limited high-quality training data, monocular depth ambiguity, and the increased structural constraints relative to SMPL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260708/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, detects and crops the person region, and additionally estimates the camera focal length from the full image using a frozen pretrained predictor. It outputs SKEL pose parameters (46-dim, with per-joint degree-of-freedom constraints), shape parameters (10-dim), and camera extrinsics, from which a 6890-vertex surface mesh and a 24752-vertex skeleton mesh are generated. A focal-normalized bounding-box geometry vector (center and scale divided by focal length) is injected into the decoder as a projection-sensitive geometric cue.

## Method

SKEL-CF uses a ViTPose-H encoder and a 6-layer transformer decoder in a coarse-to-fine strategy inspired by DETR: the encoder produces initial camera extrinsics, shape, and pose predictions, and the decoder iteratively refines them by predicting residual corrections at each layer. A frozen CameraHMR focal-length predictor is integrated to reduce depth and scale ambiguity under diverse viewpoints. Training supervision combines keypoint-level (3D/2D L1) and parameter-level (pose/shape L1) losses at the encoder and final decoder output, with a lightweight auxiliary pose-parameter loss at intermediate decoder layers to guide iterative refinement without the cost of full SKEL forward passes. The authors also construct HMR-SKEL, a large-scale dataset of SKEL pseudo-ground-truth obtained by hierarchically fitting SKEL parameters to CameraHMR-refined SMPL meshes across ~3M images, providing higher-quality supervision than prior SMPL-to-SKEL conversions.

## Results

On the challenging MOYO dataset, SKEL-CF achieves 85.0 MPJPE and 51.4 PA-MPJPE, outperforming the previous SKEL-based state-of-the-art HSMR (104.5/79.6) by 18.6% and 35.4% respectively. On the curated MOYO-HARD subset (removing static start/end frames), SKEL-CF reaches 90.0 MPJPE and 61.5 PA-MPJPE versus HSMR's 120.0/97.7. On 3DPW, SKEL-CF achieves 61.5 MPJPE / 38.7 PA-MPJPE, matching the SMPL-based CameraHMR (62.7/38.7) while producing anatomically consistent joint motions. On Human3.6M, SKEL-CF attains 39.0 MPJPE / 31.2 PA-MPJPE, improving over HSMR (50.4/32.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary SKEL-based end-to-end baseline HSMR, SKEL-CF wins decisively on every dataset: 20.0 MPJPE lower on 3DPW (61.5 vs 81.5), 11.4 lower on Human3.6M (39.0 vs 50.4), 19.5 lower on MOYO (85.0 vs 104.5), and 30.0 lower on MOYO-HARD (90.0 vs 120.0). Against the two-stage CameraHMR + SKEL fitting baseline, SKEL-CF is clearly superior on 3DPW (61.5 vs 70.4 MPJPE) and Human3.6M (39.0 vs unavailable), but CameraHMR + SKEL fit is slightly better on MOYO (75.5 vs 85.0) and MOYO-HARD (88.7 vs 90.0), though at a cost of ~4 minutes per image versus SKEL-CF's single forward pass (~0.8 s). Compared to the strongest SMPL-based method CameraHMR, SKEL-CF achieves nearly identical 3DPW performance (61.5 vs 62.7 MPJPE) and comparable EMDB results (72.0 vs 73.2 MPJPE), while producing anatomically constrained joint motions that SMPL cannot guarantee. Against other SMPL-based methods (TokenHMR, WHAM, ReFit, CLIFF, HMR2.0), SKEL-CF matches or exceeds the best on 3DPW and EMDB, confirming that the kinematically constrained SKEL representation does not sacrifice numerical accuracy. The HMR2.0 + SKEL fitting two-stage baseline is the weakest, confirming that end-to-end SKEL estimation is substantially more effective than post-hoc fitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

SKEL-CF extends the SKEL-based human mesh recovery line initiated by HSMR [39], which was the first to estimate the biomechanically constrained SKEL parameters (46 DOF) from a single image using a transformer encoder-decoder. The key departures are threefold: (1) a DETR-inspired coarse-to-fine strategy where the encoder produces initial predictions and the decoder iteratively refines them with layer-wise supervision, versus HSMR's single-shot final-layer supervision; (2) explicit camera intrinsic (focal length) estimation borrowed from CameraHMR [27] to reduce depth and scale ambiguity; and (3) a higher-quality training dataset (HMR-SKEL) constructed by fitting SKEL to CameraHMR's refined SMPL annotations rather than the noisier 4DHuman pseudo-ground-truth used by HSMR. The underlying SKEL representation [14] itself is unchanged; the paper's contribution is entirely in the estimation pipeline and data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SKEL-CF demonstrates that a kinematically constrained model (46 DOF) can match or exceed the numerical accuracy of unconstrained SMPL-based methods (72 DOF) while producing anatomically valid poses, which is critical for downstream biomechanical applications such as motion analysis, rehabilitation, and human-robot interaction. The HMR-SKEL dataset (~3M images) is a practical resource that the community can reuse for training SKEL-based models without repeating the expensive fitting pipeline. Open questions include whether the coarse-to-fine strategy extends naturally to video-based SKEL tracking, how the method performs under severe occlusions beyond yoga poses, and whether the frozen camera intrinsic predictor limits generalization to novel camera geometries not seen during CameraHMR's training.
