## Problem

pCLE images are prohibitively expensive and technically complex to acquire, creating severe training data scarcity for computational pathology. Existing cross-modal translation methods (cycle-consistency GANs, Schrödinger Bridge models, virtual staining) consistently introduce artifacts and cellular structure distortions when applied to the H&E-to-pCLE task, due to the massive domain gap between these two imaging modalities and the lack of paired supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262075/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single H&E-stained pathology image (256×256 RGB) as input and produces a synthesized pCLE image of the same resolution. The translation is strictly unpaired—no pixel-level spatial correspondence exists between source and target due to inherent spatiotemporal acquisition discrepancies. The synthesized pCLE images are intended for data augmentation in downstream tasks such as cell detection and counting, where they are injected into training sets while retaining original segmentation labels from the H&E source.

## Method

Y-diff decouples texture learning from structure learning via a Teacher-Student diffusion distillation architecture. A pre-trained DiffAE semantic encoder compresses each image into a low-dimensional global semantic code; a Flow matching module then learns the optimal transport velocity field between H&E and pCLE semantic distributions, deterministically mapping source semantics to target-domain textures. For structure preservation, a time-conditioned pixel-wise MLP (SPNet) extracts grayscale morphological features from the H&E input and injects them into the diffusion latent state via explicit modulation at each denoising timestep. In the two-stage training, a frozen Oracle Teacher (conditioned on real pCLE semantics) generates pseudo-ground-truth targets, while the Student distills this texture knowledge under SPNet spatial constraints, optimized by a combined L1/LPIPS/CLIP distillation loss plus adversarial supervision. At inference, only the Student and Flow matching module are used, eliminating the Teacher entirely.

## Results

On the primary CCD dataset, Y-diff achieves the best SS (0.689), Hist (0.868), and LPIPS (0.495) scores, outperforming the strongest baseline CycleGAN (SS 0.614, LPIPS 0.511) by a clear margin, while ranking second on LC (0.939 vs. D-VST's 0.953). On the MIST virtual staining benchmark, Y-diff attains the best scores on 8 of 9 metrics including SS (0.8164), FID (41.40), and B-PSNR (14.73 dB). In the downstream cell counting task, augmenting training with Y-diff-synthesized pseudo-pCLE images reduces MAPE from 55.77% (no augmentation) to 13.84%, a 4× improvement over CycleGAN-augmented training (30.86%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Y-diff decisively outperforms all seven baselines on the CCD dataset's structural preservation metric (SS), leading CycleGAN (0.614) by 12% and UNSB (0.488) by 41%. The virtual staining methods (PyramidPix2Pix, D-VST) and SynDiff suffer catastrophic structural collapse (SS ≤ 0.23), confirming their inability to handle the extreme H&E-to-pCLE domain gap. CycleGAN remains the strongest single baseline, achieving competitive LC (0.908) and the second-best LPIPS (0.511), but Y-diff surpasses it on SS, Hist, and LPIPS while also achieving a higher LC score. On the MIST benchmark, Y-diff dominates with the best FID (41.40 vs. CycleGAN's 59.83) and B-PSNR (14.73 dB), though SynDiff edges it slightly on A-PSNR (14.18 vs. 13.94). The Schrödinger Bridge-based UNSB produces visible black-and-white speckle artifacts that degrade its SS to 0.488 and LPIPS to 0.655, while CycleGAN-Turbo's one-step diffusion approach yields the worst Hist (0.672) and competitive-but-inferior LPIPS (0.777). In the downstream cell counting validation, Y-diff-synthesized data reduces MAPE to 13.84%, more than 2× better than CycleGAN-augmented training (30.86%), demonstrating that structural fidelity directly translates to clinical utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

Y-diff positions itself against the broad family of unpaired cross-modal translation methods (CycleGAN, PPT, SynDiff, UNSB, D-VST) that all attempt to learn texture and structure simultaneously within an entangled pixel space, and argues this is fundamentally inadequate for the extreme H&E-to-pCLE domain gap. Its key architectural departure is the explicit decoupling of these two signals into independent information flows: a Flow Matching module operating on DiffAE-compressed global semantics handles texture transport, while a time-conditioned SPNet provides pixel-level structural constraints. The Teacher-Student distillation paradigm, borrowed from knowledge distillation literature, is repurposed here not for model compression but to stabilize the recombination of independently learned texture and structure priors, avoiding the gradient conflicts of end-to-end training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for computational pathology: pCLE is clinically valuable for real-time in-vivo optical biopsy but suffers from severe data scarcity, and Y-diff's synthesized pseudo-pCLE data reduces downstream cell-counting MAPE from 55.77% to 13.84%, suggesting a viable data augmentation pipeline. However, the evaluation is constrained to a single in-house dataset (CCD, 296 images per modality) at 256×256 resolution, and the downstream verification uses only 65 annotated images with two baselines, limiting generalizability claims. Open questions include whether the decoupling paradigm scales to higher resolutions (the authors acknowledge DDIM's iterative sampling as a bottleneck), whether it transfers to other extreme modality pairs beyond H&E-to-pCLE, and whether physical imaging priors (e.g., fluorescence intensity modeling) can further improve robustness.
