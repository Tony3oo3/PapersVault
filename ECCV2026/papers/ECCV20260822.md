## Problem

Prompt learning for pre-trained vision-language models (e.g., CLIP) degrades significantly under partial label supervision, where each instance is associated with a candidate label set rather than a ground-truth annotation. Existing partial label learning methods rely on contrastive learning to disambiguate labels, but these require training the vision encoder, which compromises the zero-shot generalization of frozen pre-trained models. The paper addresses how to perform reliable label disambiguation using only the frozen encoder's representations while still learning effective prompts.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and their associated candidate label sets (each containing the true label plus L−1 confused labels, with L ranging from 2 to 10) as input, operating in a 16-shot few-shot regime. Images are encoded by a frozen CLIP vision encoder, and the candidate sets are used to constrain both the local neighbor-based filtering and the global transport cost matrix (non-candidate labels receive infinite cost). The output is a learned continuous prompt vector (either unified across all classes or class-specific) that, combined with class-name text embeddings, produces per-class prediction probabilities via cosine similarity in the shared embedding space.

## Method

HopS combines two complementary label selection mechanisms that exploit the frozen encoder's representations without any encoder fine-tuning. The Local Density-Based Filter (LDF) retrieves k-nearest neighbors in the CLIP feature space, constructs a multiset-union of their candidate sets, computes label frequencies, applies a threshold τ to form a consensus mask, and selects the label with the highest softmax probability within the refined set. The Global Optimal Transport Planner (GOP) formulates an entropy-regularized OT problem within each mini-batch, mapping a uniform instance distribution to a candidate-aware label distribution using a cost matrix derived from 1 minus the softmax similarity (with infinite cost for non-candidates), solved via the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm; the label receiving the largest transport mass is selected. The final training objective jointly minimizes cross-entropy losses on both the locally and globally selected pseudo-labels with a balancing coefficient λ, optimizing only the prompt vector while keeping the entire CLIP backbone frozen.

## Results

HopS is evaluated on eight benchmark datasets (Caltech-101, DTD, EuroSAT, FGVCAircraft, Food-101, Flowers-102, OxfordPets, UCF101) under 16-shot partial-label supervision with confusion rates from 0.50 to 0.90. On Caltech and Flowers, HopS achieves 89.0–89.5% (rand) and 92.9–93.2% (rand) testing accuracy respectively, matching or exceeding fully supervised 16-shot CoOp while significantly outperforming zero-shot CLIP. Under instance-dependent confusion at γc=0.80, HopS retains 38.9% on Caltech and 65.0% on Flowers, substantially ahead of CroSel (11.8% and 50.2%) and Papi (13.4% and 49.8%). Training overhead is negligible at 0.12 s/epoch, comparable to all baseline losses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HopS outperforms all ten compared baselines across all eight datasets and both confusion types. Against the most competitive full-data PLL methods, HopS (16-shot) beats CroSel by 5.0–27.6 points and Papi by 26.1–44.3 points on Caltech/Flowers (Table 2), demonstrating that holistic label selection compensates for the 16-shot data limitation. SoLar, which also uses optimal transport, drops to 9.2–22.6% under long-tailed few-shot settings while HopS maintains 46.4–74.6%, confirming that OT-based label alignment is effective only when paired with frozen pre-trained encoders. HopS matches or slightly exceeds fully supervised 16-shot CoOp on Caltech, Flowers, and UCF, and significantly surpasses zero-shot CLIP across all datasets. Under the noisy partial-label setting (missing ground-truth), HopS combined with SCE loss achieves the best performance under rand confusion, while standalone HopS dominates under insd confusion. The computational cost of HopS (0.12 s/epoch) is identical to the simplest baselines (MSE, GCE, LWC at 0.11 s), confirming no practical overhead from the dual LDF+GOP pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of partial label learning (PLL) and prompt tuning for vision-language models. Prior PLL methods (PICO, SoLar, CroSel) rely on contrastive learning or encoder fine-tuning to disambiguate labels, which conflicts with the frozen-backbone paradigm of prompt learning; conversely, prompt learning methods (CoOp and variants) assume full supervision. HopS departs from both lines by exploiting the zero-shot feature geometry of a frozen CLIP encoder for label selection—using a non-parametric k-NN density filter locally and an entropy-regularized optimal transport plan globally—rather than learning new representations. The OT formulation is inspired by SoLar's Sinkhorn-based label refinery but repurposed: instead of aligning a long-tailed prior to uniform, it maps a uniform instance distribution to candidate-aware label marginals within a batch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practically important gap: real-world labeling pipelines (webly supervised, crowd-sourced, open-world) naturally produce partial candidate sets, yet prompt learning—the most parameter-efficient adaptation strategy for VLMs—had no principled label-disambiguation mechanism for this setting. The negligible computational overhead (0.12 s/epoch vs. 0.11 s for baselines) makes the approach drop-in compatible with existing CoOp pipelines. Open questions include scalability beyond 100-class datasets, applicability to other modalities (video, audio), and whether the local-global synergy holds under more extreme noise (e.g., >50% missing ground truth). The method's reliance on a pre-computed affinity matrix also limits its applicability to streaming or online settings.
