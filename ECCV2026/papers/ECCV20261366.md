## Problem

Autoregressive streaming video generation requires a KV cache that grows linearly with video length, eventually saturating GPU memory and destroying the real-time inference advantage. Existing compression strategies (token pruning, eviction) treat the cache as an external buffer to be edited, but in video generation the cached tokens encode dense visual history; removing them breaks long-range temporal dependencies and causes background flickering, structural distortion, and identity loss. The paper asks whether part of this accumulated history can be consolidated into the model's own weights rather than managed as external tokens.

## Input / Output

For text-to-video, the model receives a text prompt and generates a long video as a sequence of latent chunks (each chunk containing L latent frames, represented as 4D tensors in R^{F×C×H×W}); for speech-to-video (LiveAvatar), it takes a single reference image plus a continuous speech stream and produces streaming talking-head frames. The output is a constant-memory streaming video where up to 50% of the KV cache is permanently evicted after a 12-latent-frame warmup, with the remaining layers retaining a local window (current frame + sink frame) rather than the full history.

## Method

ISPA operates in three phases: (1) a Dual-Stream Warmup over the first T_warm=12 latent frames where a Decomposable Attention mechanism (exploiting LSE states from FlashAttention) collects both full-attention and local-attention outputs in a single forward pass at negligible extra cost; (2) a Layer Absorb step that formulates the gap between local and full attention as a closed-form ridge-regression problem, solving ΔW = ((A_loc)ᵀA_loc + λI)⁻¹(A_loc)ᵀR for the output-projection weight modulation, where R = (A_full − A_loc)W; (3) a Memory-Efficient Inference phase where the K layers with the lowest reconstruction error ε are permanently converted to Local-Attention layers that discard their historical KV cache and rely on the updated weights W+ΔW to emulate long-range context. A dynamic, instance-specific ranking selects which layers to absorb (rather than a fixed subset), and the procedure can be re-triggered on scene transitions. The entire adaptation is non-iterative and gradient-free, preserving the base model's inference latency, and is shown to be compatible with W8A8 post-training quantization for an aggregate 1.86× speedup.

## Results

On VBench 5s (MovieGen prompts), ISPA-0.5 removes ~46–44% of the KV cache across 1.3B models (LongLive, Reward) with near-lossless quality: all seven VBench metrics shift by ≤0.17 points from the full-cache baseline. On the 14B Krea model, ISPA-0.5 retains 58.3% of the KV cache while improving Aesthetic Quality by +0.43 and Temporal Consistency by +0.58 over vanilla. On 30s long-form generation, ISPA-0.5 on Krea (14B) further boosts Aesthetic Score by +1.78 and Temporal Consistency by +0.27, while reducing peak GPU memory by 23.7 GB. Combined with W8A8 post-training quantization, ISPA achieves an aggregate 1.86× end-to-end inference speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external KV-cache compression methods (e.g., H2O, SnapKV, StreamingLLM); instead, the baseline is the vanilla full-KV-cache version of each model (LongLive 1.3B, Reward 1.3B, Krea 14B). On 5s generation, ISPA-0.5 matches or marginally exceeds the vanilla baseline on 1.3B models (e.g., Reward Dynamic Degree +0.64, LongLive Subject Consistency +0.04) while retaining only ~55% of the KV cache. On the 14B Krea model, ISPA-0.5 trades a small Subject Consistency drop (−2.58) for gains in Aesthetic Quality (+0.43) and Temporal Consistency (+0.58), suggesting the absorbed weights act as a macroscopic regularizer that filters high-frequency attention noise. On 30s long-form generation, the 14B model shows the largest absolute improvements (Aesthetic +1.78, Temporal +0.27), while 1.3B models exhibit modest degradation (≤1.10 on Image Quality) that grows with compression ratio. The authors attribute the long-video quality gains to parametric absorption filtering noisy long-range attention, effectively converting an efficiency technique into a quality-enhancing regularizer at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

ISPA departs from the dominant KV cache compression paradigm in LLMs (StreamingLLM, H2O, SnapKV, QUEST, RocketKV), which all treat the cache as an external token memory managed by selecting or evicting individual KV pairs. Instead, it reframes compression as parametric memory consolidation: a closed-form least-squares update to the output projection weights absorbs historical context into the model itself, so selected layers can operate with only local attention. The mechanism is grounded in FlashAttention's LSE-based online softmax to decompose full attention into local and historical branches at negligible overhead, and it retains the attention-sink concept from StreamingLLM by anchoring on the initial frame for stable softmax normalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from token-level pruning to weight-level absorption opens a new axis for streaming model efficiency that is orthogonal to and composable with quantization (yielding 1.86× aggregate speedup). A key open question is the 50% ceiling: beyond K=0.5N, quality degrades, suggesting a fundamental limit to what linear parametric modulation can capture of nonlinear temporal dynamics. Whether this paradigm extends to non-DiT architectures, to tasks with stronger long-range structure (e.g., multi-scene narratives), or to iterative re-absorption under distribution shift remains to be explored. The paper also leaves open whether the observed 14B long-video quality *improvement* (noise filtering via weight summarization) generalizes or is specific to the Krea architecture's attention pattern.
