## Problem

Multimodal large language models produce plausible but visually inconsistent responses on egocentric video because they lack explicit mechanisms to validate first-person action predictions against broader scene constraints. Existing approaches that fine-tune on egocentric data suffer catastrophic forgetting of exocentric performance, and prior RL-based methods rely on sparse format/correctness rewards that provide limited guidance for multi-step procedural reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261381/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single egocentric video (no paired exocentric footage required) and a text query, then autoregressively generates a structured three-segment trajectory: an <ego_plan> of temporally ordered first-person action clauses, an <exo_verify> that audits the plan from a third-person perspective over the same egocentric frames, and a final <answer>. The Anticipation Head (a small MLP projecting clause text embeddings into visual space) is used only during training to compute the ACMG reward and is discarded at inference, adding no overhead.

## Method

EgoVITA decomposes egocentric reasoning into an egocentric planner and an exocentric verifier, trained in two stages: SFT on human-annotated action sequences (EgoProceL, HD-EPIC) to initialize the plan-verify structure, followed by GRPO with k=8 rollouts per input. Two dense rewards drive the RL stage: (1) ACMG, which projects each plan clause's final text hidden state through a trainable Anticipation Head into the visual embedding space and scores it as the maximum cosine similarity against the next N=16 frames, encouraging anticipatory temporal grounding; and (2) a confidence reward that first uses a 72B teacher to establish a preference baseline over verification trajectories, then switches to self-ranking (highest- vs. lowest-scoring rollout) to reinforce consistent third-person audits. Periodic exocentric regularization on MSR-VTT every 200 RL steps prevents catastrophic forgetting without requiring paired ego-exo data.

## Results

EgoVITA achieves state-of-the-art egocentric reasoning across three model families: on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, it improves EgoBlind by +7.7 (29.7→37.4), EgoOrient by +4.4 (47.6→52.0), and EgoThink by +3.7 (48.2→51.9). On InternVL-3.5-8B, gains are +3.6 on EgoBlind and +3.8 on EgoOrient, while on Qwen3-VL-8B the model reaches 51.9 on EgoBlind and 63.1 on EgoOrient. Crucially, these egocentric gains come without exocentric degradation: InternVL-3.5 actually improves Video-MME by +2.7 (65.6→68.3) and MVBench by +2.4 (71.4→73.8), and Qwen2.5-VL recovers MVBench to 66.8 (vs. 63.2 after SFT alone). All results use only 52k training samples with DoRA adapters (r=8) on 8×L40S GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three base MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL-3.5-8B, Qwen3-VL-8B), EgoVITA consistently wins on all five egocentric benchmarks, with the largest margin on Qwen2.5-VL (+7.7 EgoBlind, +4.4 EgoOrient) and the smallest on the already-strong Qwen3-VL (+3.5 EgoBlind, +2.3 EgoOrient). Compared to the SOTA egocentric method EgoThinker [31] (built on Qwen2-VL, trained on ~5M samples), EgoVITA (on Qwen2-VL, 52k samples) wins on EgoBlind (46.8 vs. 43.8), EgoPlan (34.6 vs. 34.1), EOC-Bench (42.7 vs. 37.1), MMMU (51.3 vs. 49.2), DocVQA (80.1 vs. 69.0), MME-Perception (1682 vs. 1644), and VQAv2 (81.2 vs. 78.1), while narrowly losing on EgoSchema (67.9 vs. 68.2). The critical distinction is exocentric retention: EgoThinker suffers severe catastrophic forgetting (−10.5 on DocVQA, −1.8 on MMMU, −33 on MME), whereas EgoVITA maintains or improves exocentric scores (e.g., +0.6 DocVQA, +0.3 MMMU, +5.7 MME). The GRPO-with-sparse-rewards baseline (Format + Answer only) provides only marginal gains over SFT (e.g., +0.8 EgoBlind for Qwen2.5-VL), confirming that the dense ACMG and confidence rewards drive most of the improvement. A self-trained variant (EgoVITA◇, using Qwen3-VL-8B as its own teacher) still outperforms the Qwen3-VL baseline on all egocentric tasks, indicating the framework itself—not external teacher quality—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoVITA extends the RL-based egocentric video reasoning line (EgoThinker, EgoVLM) by replacing their sparse format/correctness rewards with two dense signals: Anticipatory Cross-Modal Grounding (ACMG), which projects plan-clause text embeddings into visual space and scores alignment with future frames, and a confidence reward that enforces consistent third-person verification. Conceptually, it departs from prior ego-exo bridging work (EgoExo-CON, Viewpoint Rosetta Stone) that requires paired or synchronized ego-exo video, instead performing both planning and verification over the same egocentric video. The plan-then-verify decomposition draws on the broader MLLM reasoning literature (GRPO from DeepSeek-R1, chain-of-thought verification) but applies it to the specific challenge of first-person procedural reasoning under partial observability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical pain point: egocentric fine-tuning typically degrades exocentric performance (catastrophic forgetting), and EgoVITA shows that a dedicated exocentric verification stage plus periodic MSR-VTT regularization can preserve third-person generalization with only 52k training samples versus ~5M for EgoThinker. The self-trained variant (no external 72B teacher) still improving over baseline suggests the plan-verify structure itself, rather than teacher quality, drives gains. Open questions include whether the approach extends to streaming/incremental egocentric settings, whether adaptive temporal windows help for long multi-step activities, and whether iterative plan-verify loops can resolve ambiguous futures. The Anticipation Head is discarded at inference, so the method adds negligible deployment cost.
