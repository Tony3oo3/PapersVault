## Problem

Existing multimodal deception detection benchmarks provide only binary labels without intermediate reasoning cues, making forensic decisions unauditable. Datasets are also small and culturally narrow, causing models to learn spurious correlations and fail to generalize across the diverse cultural contexts required in real-world investigation and security screening.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260661/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes short video clips (8 frames sampled at 224×224) and their corresponding audio tracks. Rather than outputting a bare label, the MLLM decoder generates a single-line schema-constrained report with fixed semicolon-delimited fields—Video Cues; Audio Cues; Reasoning; Prediction—where the final Prediction field must match the ground-truth deceptive/truthful label. This structured format enforces an evidence-to-conclusion inference flow that can be independently audited by human investigators.

## Method

Built on the AffectGPT multimodal LLM backbone (frozen encoders, LoRA-tuned LLM), the framework introduces two robustness modules. SICS performs cross-attention fusion of visual and audio tokens, then derives a context vector that drives an adaptive FC network to produce sample-specific residuals; a learned gating coefficient blends this residual with a global prior, and polarity-aware positive/negative weight branches enhance informative feature dimensions while suppressing noisy ones. DMC attaches modality-specific Causal Projectors and a shared distillation head to each branch, minimizing KL divergence between unimodal predictions and the MLLM decoder's multimodal teacher distribution to prevent unimodal shortcut learning. The overall training objective jointly optimizes token-level cross-entropy for schema-constrained report generation and the DMC consistency loss (α=0.1), while the distillation heads are discarded at inference.

## Results

On the three established benchmarks, DecepGPT achieves 73.23% accuracy / 76.13% F1 on DOLOs, 63.46% / 63.72% on Bag-of-Lies, and 61.25% / 67.22% on MU3D, setting new state-of-the-art across all datasets. In cross-domain transfer (training on two datasets, testing on the third), it attains 60.11% average accuracy, a +6.40% gain over the best task-specific baseline (PECL). On the newly introduced T4-Deception dataset, in-cultural accuracy averages 62.58% while cross-cultural transfer retains 57.69% (only 4.89% relative degradation), demonstrating robust generalization across four cultural contexts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DecepGPT outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset where they are compared. Against the strongest task-specific model, PECL [6], it gains +5.13% accuracy on DOLOs, +3.68% on BoL, and +3.61% on MU3D; against AFFAKT [13] (DOLOs only), the margin is +5.13%. Among zero-shot commercial MLLMs, GPT-4o [12] is the closest competitor (66.38% on DOLOs), yet DecepGPT still leads by +6.85% on DOLOs, +6.32% on BoL, and +7.13% on MU3D, while Qwen-Omni [35] zero-shot lags by over 20 points on DOLOs. Among fine-tuned open-source MLLMs, VideoLLaMA2 [3] is the strongest baseline (53.48% on DOLOs), and DecepGPT surpasses it by +19.75% on DOLOs, +15.81% on BoL, and +7.39% on MU3D; SALMONN2 [29] and Qwen2.5-7B-VL [35] trail even further. In cross-domain transfer (Table 4), DecepGPT beats PECL by +8.95%, +4.93%, and +1.86% across the three transfer paths, with a +6.40% average accuracy gain, confirming that the SICS+DMC modules improve generalization beyond in-domain accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) task-specific deep learning for multimodal deception detection (LieNet, PECL, AFFAKT), which it supersedes by adopting an MLLM backbone (AffectGPT) with LoRA fine-tuning; (2) MLLM chain-of-thought reasoning for video understanding, which it constrains to a fixed forensic schema (Video Cues; Audio Cues; Reasoning; Prediction) to produce auditable reports rather than free-form rationales; and (3) multimodal representation learning techniques (gated fusion, modality dropout, knowledge distillation), from which it derives SICS (a polarity-aware gated adapter combining a learnable global prior with sample-adaptive residuals) and DMC (a distillation regularizer aligning unimodal heads to the multimodal teacher). The T4-Deception dataset extends the game-show deception lineage (Box of Lies, DOLOs) by unifying an identity-pretense task across four cultures, filling a gap the authors identify in prior benchmarks that are either laboratory-controlled or single-culture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The auditable-report framing addresses a real deployment gap: forensic and legal contexts require evidence trails, not just binary labels, and the schema-constrained generation with multi-stage filtering (AI checks, rule-based, CLIP-similarity, human review) provides a reproducible pipeline for constructing such supervision. The T4-Deception dataset, at 1,695 samples, is the largest non-laboratory deception benchmark and enables the first systematic cross-cultural evaluation in this domain. However, the Vietnam (66) and Bulgaria (51) subsets are too small for reliable cross-cultural conclusions, and the 4.89% average degradation across cultures may not hold at scale or with additional cultural groups. Open questions include whether the schema-constrained reasoning generalizes to open-ended forensic scenarios beyond identity pretense, and whether the SICS/DMC modules transfer to other small-data multimodal tasks beyond deception.
