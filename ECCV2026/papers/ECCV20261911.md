## Problem

Bio-inspired Small Target Motion Detection (STMD) models suffer catastrophic false-alarm collapse in dynamic environments because their reliance on static temporal luminance contrast cannot adapt to non-stationary local clutter (rustling foliage, water glints, flickering lights). While recent LPTC-based ego-motion compensation (STFeedbackSTMD) resolves global camera displacement, it leaves a critical structural vulnerability: no mechanism exists to suppress micro-scale, high-frequency flickers that violate natural scene statistics and trigger heavy-tailed noise responses in the detection pathway.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a temporal video sequence (consecutive image frames) and produces a modulated detection map Q̃(x,y,t) for small moving targets (typically 1–3° visual field) along with a per-pixel suppression gain mask ω(x,y,t) ∈ [0,1]. The AGC module is designed as a late-stage, plug-and-play gate: it takes the raw output Q of any existing feedforward STMD baseline and multiplies it element-wise by ω, requiring no modification to the underlying detector. The pipeline operates at O(HW) per-frame complexity with only a few 2D buffers, making it suitable for real-time edge deployment on resource-constrained UAV platforms.

## Method

The architecture is a two-level hierarchical cascade. At the macro-scale, LPTC population coding decodes global ego-motion (direction θ̃ and velocity v) via Maximum Likelihood Estimation over directionally-tuned neurons, integrating over time to produce cumulative displacements (φ,ψ) that warp the previous frame and yield a quasi-stationary residual E(x,y,t). At the micro-scale, a variance-driven AGC module tracks local temporal energy via a Leaky Integrator (discretized as an Exponential Moving Average with forgetting rate γ), convolves the resulting σ² map with a center-surround antagonistic kernel W_s to obtain a clutter metric C(x,y,t), and maps C through the steady-state solution of a shunting inhibition dynamical system to produce the gain factor ω = exp(−C²/λ²). This exponential 'Gain Collapse' smoothly suppresses high-variance flicker regions (ω→0) while preserving low-variance target transients (ω≈1), and the final output is Q̃ = ω·Q. The entire mechanism is training-free, architecture-agnostic, and adds negligible latency (~1 ms) to any baseline STMD.

## Results

On the Vision Egg synthetic benchmark (Table 1), the AGC plugin applied to STFeedbackSTMD achieves AUC 0.910, Precision 1.000, F1 0.953, and FASR 100.0%, compared to 0.858/0.257/0.393/– for the unmodified baseline. On the RIST real-world dataset (Table 2), the same combination improves AUC from 0.209 to 0.387 and Precision from 0.017 to 0.228. In the stress test against deep learning models (Table 4), the full pipeline achieves Precision 0.791, F1 0.861, AUC 0.952, and AP 0.941 at 12.4 ms/frame, substantially outperforming all DL baselines (best DL: DNANet at F1 0.207, 46.7 ms/frame).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The AGC plugin delivers the most dramatic gains on the strongest bio-inspired baseline, STFeedbackSTMD [39]: on Vision Egg, Precision jumps from 0.257 to 1.000 and F1 from 0.393 to 0.953, with FASR reaching 100%. On FeedbackSTMD [40], Precision improves from 0.112 to 0.807 (synthetic) and from 0.039 to 0.543 (RIST), confirming that the plugin synergizes with spatio-temporal feedback loops. For the simpler ESTMD [41] and DSTMD [38] baselines, gains are more modest on synthetic data (where output feature contrast is low) but substantial on RIST (ESTMD AUC 0.566→0.824; DSTMD Precision 0.045→0.181). In the out-of-distribution stress test (Table 4), the full pipeline (STFeedbackSTMD + AGC) achieves F1 0.861 and AUC 0.952 at 12.4 ms/frame, while the best DL baselines (DNANet [23], SCTransNet [44], HCFNet [43], ResUNet_DTUM [25]) all fall below F1 0.24 and run at 37–127 ms/frame. For ego-motion compensation, LPTC population coding is 4×–47× faster per frame than Phase Correlation [21], Lucas-Kanade [27], and Farneback [10] while maintaining competitive residual alignment error (Fig. 6b).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the STFeedbackSTMD lineage [39], which introduced LPTC-based population coding for ego-motion compensation in bio-inspired small target detection. The key departure is identifying a structural gap that macro-level LPTC stabilization cannot fill: non-stationary local high-frequency clutter (flickers, glints) that violates NSS smoothness assumptions and triggers false-alarm collapse. The authors bridge this gap by importing shunting inhibition and sensory adaptation dynamics from computational neuroscience, formalizing them as a variance-driven exponential gain operator (EMA-based temporal variance → center-surround spatial integration → divisive normalization). The result is positioned as a purely additive, plug-and-play module rather than a redesign of the detection cascade, drawing on the ESTMD → DSTMD → FeedbackSTMD → STFeedbackSTMD progression as its architectural backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play AGC module offers a lightweight, architecture-agnostic solution to a well-documented failure mode (false-alarm collapse under dynamic clutter) that plagues all existing bio-inspired STMDs, with negligible latency overhead (~1 ms) suitable for resource-constrained UAV deployment. However, the mechanism has a clear spatial-scale boundary: performance gains narrow as flicker size exceeds 9×9 pixels, and the two key parameters (γ, λ) remain fixed heuristics rather than being learned or dynamically coupled to target kinematics. A more fundamental open question the authors raise is the incompatibility between localized, parallel biological gating and global competition mechanisms (Softmax, NMS) in DL pipelines, suggesting that a true hybrid bio-inspired/DL architecture remains unsolved. The stress-test results showing DL models (DNANet, SCTransNet, HCFNet) collapsing to AUC < 0.4 under out-of-distribution flickers while the bio-inspired pipeline maintains 0.95 AUC underscore the fragility of data-driven approaches to non-stationary interference, but the comparison is on a single synthetic stress scenario rather than a broad benchmark.
