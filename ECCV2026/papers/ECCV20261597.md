## Problem

DiT-based diffusion models for style transfer suffer from content-style entanglement, leading to reference-content leakage, style overfitting, and instability such as checkerboard artifacts. The paper attributes these failures to three root causes: a single reconstruction objective that under-emphasizes high-level semantics, frequency coupling that entangles content and style, and a latent-pixel objective gap where latent-optimal solutions diverge from perceptual quality after decoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261597/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, UniCSG takes a content image at 1024 resolution plus either a text prompt specifying the desired style (text-guided) or a reference image serving as a style exemplar (reference-guided), and produces a single stylized image that preserves the content's semantic structure. Both settings share the same four-tuple training interface ⟨text, ref_img, source_img, gt_img⟩, differing only in whether style information comes from language or a visual reference. Inference is accelerated via Qwen-Image-Lightning to ~4 s (text-guided) or ~10 s (reference-guided) per image.

## Method

UniCSG fine-tunes Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 through a three-part staged pipeline. Stage 1 (Semantic Disentanglement) applies low-pass filtering (τ=0.2) to all inputs before VAE encoding and introduces conditioning corruption under a unified timestep: noise amplification factors γ_style > γ_content > 1 create an information hierarchy where the style branch is most corrupted, forcing the model to extract only global style cues while preserving content structure; a CLIP-based semantic loss with target-faithfulness, content-preservation, and content-style repulsion terms further enforces disentanglement. Stage 2 (Frequency-aware Detail Reconstruction) removes the low-pass constraint and adds a DoG-pyramid multi-scale frequency decomposition on latents, using MSE for low-frequency structure and LogMSE for high-frequency textures to refine stylization without overfitting noise. Throughout both stages, a pixel-space reward module (CLIP-I content faithfulness, CSD style alignment, LPIPS, and adversarial signals) is optimized via a policy-gradient objective to bridge the latent-pixel perceptual gap.

## Results

On the reference-guided setting of CSG-Bench, UniCSG achieves the best CSD (0.731) and DreamSim (0.762) scores, indicating strong style alignment and content preservation, while its FID of 87.32 is competitive with the best baselines. On the text-guided setting, UniCSG attains the best FID (113.94), CLIP-I (0.797), DINO (0.701), and DreamSim (0.816), substantially outperforming all baselines on content-consistency metrics. In the user study, UniCSG receives the highest preference rate in both settings (38.3% content / 44.6% style for reference-guided; 29.6% / 30.2% for text-guided), confirming that automatic metrics align with human perception.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniCSG achieves the best CSD (0.731 vs. Ovis-U1's 0.703) and DreamSim (0.762 vs. Nano-banana/DreamOmni2's 0.748) in the reference-guided setting, demonstrating superior style alignment and content preservation simultaneously. BAGEL attains the lowest FID (72.07) but at a severe cost: its CLIP-I drops to 0.503 and DINO to 0.212, indicating heavy reference-content leakage where the output resembles the reference rather than the content image. Ovis-U1 achieves the highest CSD among baselines (0.703) but similarly sacrifices content fidelity (CLIP-I 0.552, DINO 0.274). DreamOmni2 leads on CLIP-I (0.784) and DINO (0.606) but trails UniCSG on CSD (0.420 vs. 0.731) and FID (112.96 vs. 87.32), reflecting weak style transfer. In the text-guided setting, UniCSG dominates content metrics (DINO 0.701 vs. next-best 0.644; DreamSim 0.816 vs. 0.791) while Ovis-U1 edges ahead on CSD (0.573 vs. 0.541) and CLIP-T (0.275 vs. 0.267), suggesting slightly stronger stylization at the expense of content faithfulness. Overall, UniCSG is the only method that simultaneously achieves top-tier scores on both style and content metrics, validating the staged disentanglement design.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniCSG builds directly on the Qwen-Image-Edit DiT backbone and positions itself against the growing family of DiT-based style transfer systems (OmniConsistency, OmniStyle, BAGEL, Ovis-U1) that suffer from content-style entanglement and reference-content leakage. Conceptually, it departs from prior frequency-decoupling architectures (DeCo, PixelDiT, JiT) that modify the network structure to separate low- and high-frequency pathways; instead, UniCSG keeps the standard architecture and achieves disentanglement through a staged training curriculum—low-frequency preprocessing with conditioning corruption in Stage 1, then multi-scale DoG frequency supervision in Stage 2. The pixel-space reward learning component extends the RLHF-style policy-gradient idea into the image-generation setting, using CLIP, CSD, LPIPS, and adversarial signals as multi-dimensional rewards, which is a relatively novel combination for style transfer specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The staged semantic-then-frequency-then-reward paradigm offers a reusable training recipe for any DiT-based conditional generation task where content preservation and style fidelity are in tension, not just style transfer. The paper's main open question is generalization: performance degrades noticeably on unseen style categories (FID jumps from 87 to 114 in the reference-guided setting), and the authors acknowledge that category-level stylization (geometric deformation, structural restylization) is harder than image-level appearance translation. Efficiency remains moderate (~4–10 s per image at 1024 px), and the reward learning adds training complexity; whether the staged approach transfers cleanly to other DiT backbones or higher resolutions is untested.
