## Problem

Preference optimization (PO) for text-to-image diffusion models amplifies demographic biases already present in CLIP-based reward models, causing the demographic distribution of generated images to narrow substantially after alignment. Existing diversity-preservation methods such as DRIFT and DiverseGRPO fail to address this because visual diversity and demographic fairness are distinct properties. No prior work had measured how PO amplifies reward-model demographic bias into the generator or proposed a solution operating at the reward-signal level.

## Input / Output

DRPO takes demographic-neutral text prompts as input and produces aligned diffusion models (SD1.5 or SDXL with LoRA r=64) that generate images. The method's unique input is a one-time preparation set of ~10,000 auto-generated images labeled with FairFace for race (7 categories) and gender (2 categories), used to identify the demographic subspace. During training, the only modification to the standard PO pipeline is an orthogonal projection applied to the reward model's CLIP vision-encoder features before the MLP scoring head, adding less than 0.1% overhead per step.

## Method

DRPO identifies demographic directions in reward feature space via a lightweight linear probe trained on auto-labeled generated images, then removes them through orthogonal projection before reward computation. Specifically, the probe weight matrix W is decomposed via SVD, and the top-k=5 right singular vectors form an orthonormal basis V_k for the demographic subspace; at each training step, features are projected as φ̃(x) = (I − V_k V_k^T) φ(x) before being passed to the reward head h. The key empirical finding enabling this approach is that demographic information in CLIP features is predominantly linear (the gap between linear and MLP probe accuracy is only 1.5–1.8% for race), so a truncated linear subspace captures the dominant bias signal. The debiased reward r̃(x) = h(φ̃(x)) is then used directly in any standard PO objective (GRPO or DPO) without modifying the optimization algorithm, and the principal angle between demographic and quality directions (72.4°) confirms minimal quality interference.

## Results

On SDXL, DRPO (w/ GRPO) recovers race entropy to 1.67 (96% of the base model's 1.74) and gender entropy to 0.96 (99% of base 0.97), while maintaining HPS at 27.31—within 0.4% of DanceGRPO's 27.42. Standard alignment methods collapse demographic fairness: DanceGRPO drops race entropy to 0.89 (−49%) and worst-group rate to 1%, while diversity-preserving methods DRIFT and DiverseGRPO improve Vendi Score (0.73, 0.71) but leave race entropy at 0.93 and 0.96. DRPO also achieves the best demographic parity difference (0.19) and KL-from-uniform (0.23) among all aligned methods, approaching the base model's 0.18 and 0.21.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRPO (w/ GRPO) outperforms DanceGRPO on every fairness metric—race entropy 1.67 vs. 0.89, gender entropy 0.96 vs. 0.69, worst-group rate 7% vs. 1%—while sacrificing only 0.4% HPS (27.31 vs. 27.42). Against DRIFT and DiverseGRPO, DRPO wins decisively on demographic fairness (Race-H 1.67 vs. 0.93/0.96) but concedes on visual diversity (Vendi 0.67 vs. 0.73/0.71) and FID (35.9 vs. 33.1/33.8), confirming that visual diversity and demographic fairness are governed by different mechanisms. Compared to ITI-GEN+GRPO, DRPO achieves both higher quality (HPS 27.31 vs. 26.84, PickScore 21.54 vs. 21.08) and better fairness (Race-H 1.67 vs. 1.43), avoiding the quality penalty that inclusive prompt embeddings impose. Against Diffusion-DPO, DRPO (w/ DPO) improves race entropy from 1.08 to 1.61 and gender entropy from 0.78 to 0.94 at comparable HPS (26.93 vs. 27.08). The GRPO variant consistently dominates the DPO variant across both quality and fairness, mirroring the general on-policy vs. offline advantage in preference optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the preference optimization pipeline for diffusion models (Diffusion-DPO, DanceGRPO) and the CLIP-based reward models (HPS-v2, PickScore, ImageReward) that power it. It departs from prior diversity-preservation work (DRIFT, DiverseGRPO) by arguing that visual diversity and demographic fairness occupy different subspaces, so regularizing one does not protect the other. It also contrasts with prompt-level (ITI-GEN) and inference-time (FairDiffusion) fairness interventions by intervening at the reward-feature level, where the bias is introduced, making the fix orthogonal to the choice of PO algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a previously unexamined failure mode: standard PO systematically narrows demographic representation in generated images, and the fix is a single k=5 orthogonal projection that costs <0.1% of training time. This is practically important for anyone deploying preference-aligned diffusion models, as the bias amplification is monotonic and begins within the first few hundred steps. Open questions include whether the linear-subspace assumption holds for more complex demographic encodings, whether the approach generalizes beyond CLIP-based reward models, and whether it can correct (rather than merely prevent amplification of) biases already baked into the base model.
