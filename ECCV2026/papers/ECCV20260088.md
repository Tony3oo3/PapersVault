## Problem

Existing image demoiréing methods treat moiré removal as a holistic restoration task or rely on discrete transforms (Haar wavelet, block DCT) that suffer from spatial discontinuity, channel redundancy, and error accumulation during their fixed inverse processes. These limitations prevent effective disentanglement of the intertwined high-frequency texture corruption and low-frequency color distortions that characterize moiré patterns, particularly for large-scale or complex artifacts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single moiré-contaminated RGB image at arbitrary resolution (evaluated from 256×256 up to 3840×2160 for 4K UHDM) and outputs a clean RGB image at the same resolution. During inference, the input is decomposed into high- and low-frequency components that both retain original spatial resolution; the high-frequency branch processes the full-resolution input directly, while the low-frequency branch operates on a downsampled version (e.g., 0.1× scale) that is later upsampled back before fusion, enabling efficient handling of ultra-high-resolution inputs.

## Method

Freqformer introduces a smooth, anti-aliasing frequency decomposition via recursive convolution with a fixed 3×3 kernel and increasing dilation rates, which separates moiré into spatially-localized high-frequency textures and scale-robust low-frequency color distortions without the spatial discontinuity or channel fragmentation of DWT/DCT. A dual-branch encoder-decoder architecture processes each component with an asymmetric training strategy: crop-based training for the high-frequency branch (exploiting locality) and resize-based training for the low-frequency branch (exploiting scale-robustness to avoid detail loss). A learnable Frequency Composition Transform (FCT) module adaptively fuses the two branches' features in a shared transformer space rather than performing simple pixel addition, improving reconstruction consistency. Each encoder/decoder block uses a Spatial-Aware Channel Attention (SA-CA) module that combines a Residual Dilated Dense Block for large-receptive-field spatial extraction with channel attention (via spatial-channel projection using 1×1 + 3×3 depthwise convolutions) and a gated FFN for inter-channel learning. Training proceeds in two stages: independent branch training with deep supervision, followed by joint fine-tuning of both branches and the FCT with a combined L1 + VGG16 perceptual loss.

## Results

Freqformer achieves state-of-the-art or near-state-of-the-art performance across all four demoiréing benchmarks with only 6.065M parameters. On FHDMi, it reaches PSNR 25.26 dB, SSIM 0.8518, and LPIPS 0.1253, outperforming all baselines. On UHDM (4K resolution), it attains PSNR 22.24 dB, SSIM 0.8021, and LPIPS 0.2424, ranking first in SSIM and LPIPS and second in PSNR (behind ESDNet-L at 22.42). On TIP2018 and LCDMoiré, Freqformer leads in both PSNR and SSIM (30.63/0.9269 and 45.62/0.9967 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Freqformer outperforms all dedicated demoiréing baselines (DMCNN, MDDM, WDNet, MopNet, MBCNN, FHDe²Net, ESDNet, ESDNet-L) on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across TIP2018, LCDMoiré, and FHDMi, with the largest margins over DMCNN (+10.3 dB PSNR on TIP2018) and MDDM (+3.7 dB on FHDMi). On the challenging 4K UHDM dataset, Freqformer (22.24 dB) narrowly trails ESDNet-L (22.42 dB) in PSNR but surpasses it in SSIM (0.8021 vs. 0.7985) and LPIPS (0.2424 vs. 0.2454), while using fewer parameters (6.065M vs. 5.934M for ESDNet-L, but far fewer than MopNet's 58.565M or MBCNN's 14.192M). Against general-purpose restoration models in Table 2, Freqformer beats AdaIR (21.87 dB), MoCE-IR (21.80 dB), RRID (24.39 dB), STD-Net (25.05 dB), and DTNet (23.01 dB) on FHDMi PSNR, and surpasses all of them on UHDM as well. The only method that edges out Freqformer is OSEDiff (950M parameters) on LPIPS (0.1516 vs. 0.1253 on FHDMi), but Freqformer still leads OSEDiff in PSNR (25.26 vs. 21.81) and SSIM (0.8518 vs. 0.7764) on FHDMi, achieving this with roughly 156× fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

Freqformer extends the frequency-aware demoiréing line initiated by wavelet-based methods (WDNet, AWUDN, MBWDN) and DCT-based methods (MBCNN, FHDe²Net), but departs from them by replacing fixed discrete transforms with a simple recursive dilation-convolution decomposition that preserves spatial smoothness and supports a learnable inverse (FCT). The dual-branch concept echoes prior wavelet sub-band processing, yet the asymmetric crop/resize training strategy and the Spatial-Aware Channel Attention module (drawing on channel-attention ideas from Restormer and the green-channel observation in P-BiC) are novel combinations. The paper also positions itself against heavy U-Net backbones (MopNet, MBCNN) by showing that a compact Transformer-based encoder-decoder with hierarchical fusion can match or exceed them at roughly one-tenth the parameter count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that a 6 M-parameter model can achieve SOTA across four demoiréing benchmarks, making high-resolution (4K) demoiréing feasible on edge hardware; the resize-based low-frequency training trick is a simple, transferable insight that avoids the detail-loss problem plaguing prior downsampled approaches. Limitations include slower per-image inference than purely convolutional baselines (0.58 s vs 0.25 s for ESDNet-L on 4K) due to attention's weaker hardware optimization, and the method is validated only on sRGB single-image demoiréing, leaving open whether the frequency-decomposition + asymmetric-training recipe transfers to video, Raw-domain, or other restoration tasks. The learnable FCT and SA-CA module are modular components that could be adopted in other frequency-separation pipelines beyond demoiréing.
