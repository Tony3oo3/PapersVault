## Problem

Existing structured pruning methods target inter-filter redundancy by removing entire channels or filters, but leave substantial intra-filter spatial redundancy unaddressed—neighboring weights within surviving kernels remain highly correlated, limiting achievable compression and computational efficiency. This paper asks whether explicitly pruning spatial positions within each convolutional kernel can yield additional efficiency gains without the irregular memory access patterns that plague unstructured weight-level pruning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261478/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained model weight tensors: for CNNs, it takes a convolutional weight tensor W ∈ R^{C_out × C_in × K × K} and produces a sparsified version retaining only K_keep spatial coefficients per kernel (e.g., 1 out of 9 for a 3×3 kernel); for transformers, it applies group-wise Top-K to linear projection matrices yielding N:M structured sparsity (e.g., 2:4). Evaluation spans RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR), 224×224 (ImageNet), and 384×384 (Flickr30k) for classification and captioning, as well as text sequences up to 2048 tokens for language modeling on WikiText-2.

## Method

TKP introduces a two-stage spatial pruning procedure. Stage 1 applies an auxiliary exponential regularization loss that penalizes spatial coefficients ranked below the top K_keep with exponentially decaying weights (controlled by α), progressively concentrating each kernel's information into a few dominant positions and creating a clear magnitude hierarchy. Stage 2 then applies a deterministic hard Top-K mask that retains exactly K_keep spatial coefficients per kernel, freezing the sparsity pattern during fine-tuning so gradients flow only through retained positions. This avoids the discontinuous optimization landscape of naive Top-K pruning while producing a fixed, semi-structured sparsity pattern (kept ratio ρ = K_keep/K²) that converts dense convolutions into selective-sampling operations with strictly bounded FLOPs. For transformer architectures, the same two-stage logic extends to group-wise Top-K selection over non-overlapping groups of size G in attention and feed-forward projection matrices, yielding accelerator-compatible N:M sparsity.

## Results

On ResNet-56 (CIFAR-10), TKP achieves 91.80% pruned top-1 accuracy (−1.78% drop) with 8.83× theoretical FLOP reduction and 3.94× measured latency speed-up. On VGG-19 (CIFAR-100), TKP reaches 72.77% (−0.73% drop) at 8.90× FLOP reduction with a 5.15× speed-up. On ResNet-50 (ImageNet-1k), TKP uniquely *improves* accuracy to 76.49% (+0.34%) while delivering 3.51× FLOP reduction. For LLaMA-2-7B at 50% sparsity, TKP achieves 5.59 perplexity on WikiText-2, outperforming the dense baseline (6.00) and all pruning baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TKP consistently outperforms all six structured-pruning baselines (DepGraph, Torque, ETP, GReg, Isomorphic, MDP) on every CNN and ViT architecture tested. On ResNet-56/CIFAR-10, TKP's 91.80% pruned accuracy exceeds the best baseline (Isomorphic, 90.90%) by 0.9 pp while also delivering the highest measured speed-up (3.94× vs. MDP's 2.97×). On ResNet-50/ImageNet-1k, TKP is the only method that improves over the dense baseline (+0.34 pp to 76.49%) and achieves the largest FLOP reduction (3.51× vs. Torque's 2.34×). For LLaMA-2-7B, TKP achieves 5.59 perplexity at 50% sparsity, beating Wanda++ (6.44), PP (6.72), FLAP (8.62), and SparseGPT (11.06); under the stricter 2:4 pattern the gap widens (TKP 9.31 vs. Wanda++ 10.15). On BLIP/Flickr30k at 2:4 sparsity, TKP scores 29.93 BLEU-4 versus 28.93 (Wanda++) and 28.71 (PP). Under quantization (QAT-INT8 + TKP on ResNet-18/CIFAR-100), TKP retains 81.54% accuracy (−0.49 pp) at 16× compression, whereas QAT+DepGraph drops 5.07 pp and QAT+GReg-1 drops 12.95 pp at similar compression ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of inter-filter structured pruning (DepGraph, Torque, ETP, GReg, Isomorphic, MDP) by targeting the spatial dimension *within* each convolutional kernel, a level of redundancy that prior filter-level methods leave unaddressed. It also distinguishes itself from earlier intra-filter approaches (Structured Sparsity Learning, Structured Sparse Convolutions, sub-kernel pruning) that rely on fixed or shared spatial masks, by learning a per-kernel importance hierarchy through an auxiliary exponential regularization before applying a deterministic Top-K selection. The two-stage design—soft concentration followed by hard selection—repurposes the Top-K operator from feature selection into a spatial pruning mechanism, yielding semi-structured sparsity (e.g., 1:9 for 3×3 kernels) that is directly exploitable by hardware without the irregular memory access costs of unstructured pruning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TKP addresses a concrete and previously underexploited source of redundancy (intra-filter spatial correlation) with a simple, architecture-agnostic mechanism that translates theoretical FLOP reductions into measured latency and energy savings, and composes cleanly with quantization to reach 16–17× total compression. The consistent outperformance across CNNs, ViTs, LLaMA-2-7B, and BLIP suggests the spatial-redundancy argument generalizes beyond convolutions to linear projections in transformers. Open questions include closing the gap between theoretical and measured speed-up (bounded by kernel size), extending the approach to larger LLMs where group sizes grow, and whether the auxiliary regularization stage can be replaced by a cheaper proxy to reduce training overhead.
