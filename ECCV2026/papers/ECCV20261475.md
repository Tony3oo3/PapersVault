## Problem

Wide-baseline multi-fisheye rigs cause different cameras to observe different faces or portions of the same object, so aggregating their features into a unified equirectangular (ERP) representation under fixed projection produces ambiguous, multi-modal matching evidence near occlusion boundaries and thin structures. Existing iterative refinement methods mitigate this by down-weighting unreliable views but do not resolve the underlying discrepancy, because both context formation and cross-view fusion remain locked to rigid fisheye-to-ERP sampling that cannot adapt across visibility discontinuities.

## Input / Output

The method takes four 220° fisheye images (front, right, back, left) at 768×800 resolution along with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and produces a single equirectangular inverse-depth map at 360×640. Depth is parameterized as inverse depth discretized into 192 hypotheses (96 bins refined iteratively, then 2× convex-upsampled along the depth axis), and the network outputs a residual update at each of T refinement steps rather than a direct depth prediction.

## Method

OmniDS pairs a lightweight 18-layer residual CNN (geometric detail) with a frozen DINOv3 ViT (semantic priors) in a dual-stream encoder. At each iterative refinement step, CNN features are reprojected to ERP space via grid sampling with learned per-camera softmax weights, while DINO features are reprojected via deformable cross-attention with a radial-distortion bias that up-weights high-resolution central fisheye regions; the two are fused pointwise into a dynamic context. In parallel, a static multi-view consensus volume combines group-wise correlation with cross-camera feature variance (a proxy for depth correctness) and is regularized by a 3D U-Net in a 4-level pyramid. A ConvGRU consumes the fused context, correlation profile, and consensus volume features at the current depth to predict a residual inverse-depth update, supervised by a γ-weighted sequence L1 loss. For deployment, the dual-stream encoder is distilled into a single MobileNetV2-based student (weighted feature-level MSE, λ_dino=10 to emphasize the harder-to-match DINO representations), reducing inference to ~102 ms on an RTX 4070 SUPER with minimal accuracy loss.

## Results

On the OmniThings benchmark (trained on OmniThings only), OmniDS achieves MAE 1.04 and RMS 3.59, outperforming the next-best method MDP-Omni (MAE 1.20, RMS 3.79) by 13% in MAE. After fine-tuning on OmniHouse and Sunny, OmniDS-ft reaches MAE 0.28 and RMS 0.89 on OmniHouse, and MAE 0.28 / RMS 1.32 on Sunny, setting new state-of-the-art on all five evaluation datasets. The distilled variant (Ours-ft distilled) reduces inference time from 148 ms to 102 ms (~31% faster) while retaining near-identical accuracy (e.g., OmniHouse MAE 0.28, RMS 0.89).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniDS outperforms all five baselines across every metric on all five datasets in both the OmniThings-only and fine-tuned settings. In the OmniThings-only setting, OmniDS reduces OmniThings MAE from 1.20 (MDP-Omni, the prior best) to 1.04 (−13%) and OmniHouse MAE from 0.74 (RomniStereo-64) to 0.64 (−14%). After fine-tuning, the gap widens: on OmniHouse, OmniDS-ft achieves MAE 0.28 vs. 0.42 (RomniStereo-64-ft) and 0.44 (MDP-Omni-ft), a 33–36% reduction; on Sunny, it reaches MAE 0.28 vs. 0.32–0.33 for the best baselines. The volumetric baselines (OmniMVS, OmniMVS+32) are consistently the weakest, with MAE roughly 2× higher than OmniDS, and also the slowest (289 ms vs. 148 ms). The distilled variant matches the full model's OmniHouse accuracy (MAE 0.28, RMS 0.89) while cutting inference time to 102 ms, making it competitive with the fastest baseline (RomniStereo-32 at 83 ms) at only a small accuracy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniDS extends the iterative-refinement paradigm for omnidirectional stereo (RomniStereo, RAFT-Stereo) by replacing the rigid fisheye-to-ERP projection that all prior methods rely on with a dynamic dual-stream context fusion: a lightweight CNN for geometric detail and a frozen DINOv3 ViT for semantic priors, reprojected at each refinement step via learned view weighting and deformable cross-attention with a geometric distortion bias. The multi-view consensus volume draws on group-wise correlation (GwcNet) and adds a cross-camera feature-variance signal regularized by a 3D U-Net, going beyond the pairwise correlation profiles used in RomniStereo. The knowledge-distillation step into a single MobileNetV2 encoder is a practical deployment contribution not present in prior omnidirectional stereo work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and recurring failure mode in multi-fisheye depth estimation—visibility conflicts at occlusion boundaries and thin structures under wide baselines—and shows that dynamic, depth-conditioned context re-projection plus explicit cross-camera consensus measurably reduces errors in exactly those regions. The distilled variant achieving ~10 FPS on consumer hardware makes the approach relevant for real-time robotic deployment. However, evaluation is limited to synthetic benchmarks with a fixed four-camera rig; extending to arbitrary camera counts, calibration noise, and real-world fisheye imagery remains open, as does validating whether the DINOv3 semantic prior generalizes beyond the training distribution.
