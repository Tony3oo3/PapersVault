## Problem

Few-shot test-time domain adaptation (FSTT-DA) requires adapting a model to a novel domain from only a small unlabeled batch at deployment, but prior methods rely on shallow BatchNorm re-estimation, black-box prompt tuning, or compute-heavy ensemble distillation that scales poorly with the number of source domains. None of these approaches effectively extract and transfer domain-specific internal representations while preserving the base model's cross-domain generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261373/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes a small batch of unlabeled target images (processed through the frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 to yield a mean embedding, then reduced to 128 dimensions via an MLP) together with pre-computed full-product ΔW=BA matrices from each source-domain LoRA (rank 16, α=32). The hypernetwork outputs per-column, per-domain merging weights—one scalar weight for each source domain at every column of every attention and projection layer—which are used to form a single merged LoRA module. The merged model then produces standard class labels (or a regression value for PovertyMap) in a single forward pass with no further updates.

## Method

Separate LoRA adapters are fine-tuned on each source domain's CLIP vision encoder while the backbone stays frozen, encoding domain-specific features in low-rank parameter updates. A meta-learned hypernetwork then generates per-column merging weights via cross-attention: the query is a 128-d target-domain embedding, and the keys are LoRA ΔW columns projected to 128 dimensions and augmented with learnable layer-index, sub-layer-type, and domain tokens. During meta-training, one source domain is randomly sampled as a pseudo-target (its LoRA zeroed out), support images from that domain condition the hypernetwork, and the resulting merged model is evaluated on query images with cross-entropy loss, backpropagating gradients end-to-end into the hypernetwork. At test time, the hypernetwork takes a few unlabeled target images and all source LoRAs, produces the merge weights in one shot, and the merged LoRA is applied to the frozen CLIP for inference. The per-column granularity (following LoRA.rar) enables fine-grained selective emphasis or suppression of individual feature subspaces, and conditioning on target images makes the merge policy adaptive rather than a fixed unconditional rule.

## Results

On DomainNet (leave-one-out), DA-MergeLoRA achieves 62.44% average accuracy, outperforming the prior SOTA ViT-B/16 FSTT-DA method L2C (61.2%) by +1.24% and the SOTA CNN method MABN (45.5%) by +16.94%. On WILDS, the method achieves 96.90% accuracy on Camelyon17 (+2.70% over L2C), 57.60% accuracy and 52.26% worst-case accuracy on FMoW (+2.80% and +11.36% over L2C respectively), and 0.56 worst-case Pearson r on PovertyMap (+0.06 over L2C). The method achieves SOTA on five of six DomainNet domains and three of four official WILDS metrics, with the sole exception being iWildCam Macro F1 (33.62% vs. L2C's 35.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest FSTT-DA competitor L2C [10], DA-MergeLoRA wins on DomainNet average accuracy (+1.24%), FMoW accuracy (+2.80%) and worst-case accuracy (+11.36%), Camelyon17 accuracy (+2.70%), and PovertyMap worst-case Pearson r (+0.06), but loses on iWildCam Macro F1 (−1.58%) and is slightly lower than MIRO [7] on DomainNet QuickDraw (−0.83%). Against the SOTA CNN-based method MABN [52], the gains are substantially larger: +16.94% on DomainNet average, +4.40% on FMoW accuracy, +15.66% on FMoW worst-case accuracy, and +4.50% on Camelyon17, though MABN still leads on iWildCam accuracy (78.4% vs. 73.70%). The method also clearly surpasses VDPG [11] on all DomainNet domains and all WILDS metrics, and outperforms zero-shot CLIP [38] by 5.34% on DomainNet average and by large margins on WILDS (e.g., 57.60% vs. 16.3% on FMoW accuracy). Among CNN baselines, Meta-DMoE [61] is the strongest at 44.2% DomainNet average, still 18.24% below DA-MergeLoRA. The only consistent weakness is iWildCam, where class imbalance across 48 target domains limits the hypernetwork's merging policy, resulting in lower F1 than both MABN and L2C.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA), model merging, and few-shot test-time domain adaptation. It builds directly on LoRA.rar's per-column merging granularity and VDPG's meta-learning pseudo-target-domain training procedure, but repurposes both for a fundamentally different goal: rather than merging content/style LoRAs in diffusion models or generating visual prompts, it synthesizes a domain-specific target LoRA from source-domain LoRAs via a meta-learned hypernetwork. Conceptually, it departs from prior FSTT-DA methods (MABN's BatchNorm updates, Meta-DMoE's expert ensembling, VDPG/L2C's prompt-based adaptation) by operating in parameter space rather than feature or prompt space, and from multi-task model merging literature by producing a specialized domain model rather than a generalist.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single merged model with one forward pass replaces multi-expert ensembles, cutting inference cost by ~50% while achieving SOTA on DomainNet and WILDS. The ablation revealing that naive parameter averaging of domain-specific LoRAs collapses to zero-shot performance (14.98% on iWildCam) while conditional hypernetwork merging recovers 73.70% underscores that domain-specific adapters are incompatible without learned, target-conditioned recombination. Open questions include handling class imbalance and novel classes, leveraging CLIP's text encoder for stronger alignment, and whether the meta-learned merging policy generalizes to substantially more source domains or to settings where the target domain is far from all sources.
