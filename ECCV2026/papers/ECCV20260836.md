## Problem

Existing visual navigation world models either decouple perception, generation, and control into separate modules (missing shared spatio-temporal dynamics) or unify them without explicit scene abstractions, and most predict a single deterministic trajectory that collapses the inherently multimodal action space. This leads to myopic decision-making, mode collapse, and poor generalization to unseen environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260836/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 256×256 egocentric RGB frame, 4 historical context frames, and a goal image, all projected into a compact latent space via a pretrained VAE encoder before being fed to the backbone. It produces K diverse trajectory candidates (each a sequence of translation μ∈R² and rotation φ∈R over a 4-step horizon), corresponding future visual observations at the same resolution, plus auxiliary depth maps and semantic segmentations. Notably, the world modeling head is target-agnostic: it can simulate future observations under arbitrary hypothetical actions, not just the predicted ones.

## Method

NavWM uses a Bidirectional Mamba (SSM) backbone with three jointly trained heads: (1) a Latent World Reasoning head that uses learnable tokens supervised by Depth Anything V2 and SAM pseudo-labels (via scale-invariant depth loss and MSE semantic loss) to internalize geometric and semantic scene priors; (2) an anchor-based multimodal trajectory predictor that decomposes uncertainty into intent (K-Means/FPS anchor codebook with softmax classification) and control (MLP offset regression), then directly regresses waypoint sequences per anchor; and (3) a Conditional Diffusion Transformer world model trained with Flow Matching, conditioned on dense optical flow derived from predicted actions and scene features injected via cross-attention. Training is two-stage: first, all branches are jointly optimized with teacher forcing under an uncertainty-weighted multi-task loss; second, the CDiT head is fine-tuned on predicted (not ground-truth) trajectories to close the train-inference gap. At inference, NMS selects diverse trajectory candidates, the world model simulates their visual futures, and a scoring function combining LPIPS and DreamSim against the goal image selects the optimal path for closed-loop navigation.

## Results

On the offline evaluation across four robotics datasets, NavWM achieves state-of-the-art performance on both navigation and visualization: ATE 0.207 (vs. 0.302 for UniWM), RPE 0.066 (vs. 0.116), and PSNR 17.34 (vs. 14.17 for UniWM and 14.34 for NWM). In the Image Goal Navigation rollout, NavWM reaches a 72% success rate in seen environments and 44% in unseen (zero-shot) environments, surpassing UniWM (66% / 36%) and NWM (58% / 23%). The PSNR gain of over 3 dB over the previous best world model represents a substantial improvement in future-frame synthesis fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NavWM outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest unified-model competitor UniWM [15], NavWM reduces ATE by 31% (0.302→0.207), RPE by 43% (0.116→0.066), and improves PSNR by 3.17 dB (14.17→17.34) while lowering LPIPS from 0.282 to 0.243. The DiT-based world model NWM [4] is surpassed on both navigation (ATE 0.642→0.207) and generation (PSNR 14.34→17.34), confirming that the unified Mamba backbone with latent world tokens yields better representations than a decoupled DiT pipeline. Among pure navigation policies, GNM [41] and ViNT [42] trail NavWM by roughly 7× in ATE (1.519/1.424 vs. 0.207), while the diffusion-based NoMaD [45] is still 5× worse in ATE (1.070). The autoregressive multimodal model Anole-7B [11] and the Atari-oriented Diamond [2] are far behind on both navigation and visual synthesis, with PSNR below 10 versus NavWM's 17.34. In the navigation rollout (Table 2), NavWM's world-model-guided planning lifts the seen-environment success rate from 66% (UniWM) to 72% and the zero-shot unseen rate from 36% to 44%, with correspondingly lower ATE (0.381 vs. 0.645 seen; 0.765 vs. 1.122 unseen).

## Relation to Prior Work

NavWM unifies three previously decoupled or partially integrated components—scene perception, action prediction, and future visual synthesis—into a single Bidirectional Mamba backbone, departing from the modular pipelines of NWM (Bar et al., CVPR 2025) and the partially unified autoregressive approach of UniWM (Dong et al., 2026). The anchor-based multimodal trajectory prediction directly inherits the intent/control decomposition from DenseTNT (Gu et al., ICCV 2021), while the future synthesis head adapts the Conditional Diffusion Transformer architecture from NWM and replaces standard diffusion with Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022) for more efficient sampling. The key conceptual departure is treating the generative world model not merely as a simulator but as an active planner that scores candidate trajectories via visual foresight, a role that prior unified models like UniWM only partially exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that jointly optimizing perception, control, and generation within a shared architecture yields mutual reinforcement that neither decoupled nor partially unified baselines achieve, with the 44% zero-shot success rate on TartanDrive being particularly notable for generalization to unseen environments. The finding that anchor-based multimodal predictions (highest trajectory diversity) benefit disproportionately from world-model planning compared to GMM or diffusion baselines suggests that action-space diversity is a prerequisite for effective generative planning—an insight that could reshape how planners are designed. A stated limitation is reduced fidelity in highly dynamic scenes with moving objects, and the 1.5B-parameter model trained on 8 A100s raises questions about scalability to more complex, open-world settings.
