## Problem

Current multimodal LLMs apply deep chain-of-thought reasoning uniformly to all document questions, incurring unnecessary computational cost on simple extraction tasks and even degrading accuracy through overthinking-induced hallucinations. Existing adaptive thinking approaches rely on manual prompt-based mode switching or externally labeled difficulty, which introduce subjective human bias and fail to teach the model genuine autonomous mode-selection capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261359/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a document image paired with a natural-language question and produces a structured text output beginning with a single mode-switch token (/no_think or /to_think) that determines whether a reasoning chain is generated. In thinking mode, the output follows the format ; in non-thinking mode, the thinking block is empty. The final answer is always enclosed in <answer></answer> tags, and the model is prompted to answer with a single word or phrase to keep the answer portion length-balanced across modes.

## Method

AdaThinking-E decouples the optimization of the mode-switching token from response tokens within a GRPO-based RL loop. The mode-switch token is rewarded via a one-token entropy curriculum: a dynamic coefficient α_k (a sigmoid of training progress) shifts the reward from encouraging high entropy (balanced exploration of both modes) in early training to low entropy (confident, consistent mode selection) in later training, while a decision-feedback weight w_m computed from per-mode accuracy and sampling propensity prevents mode collapse. Response tokens are optimized independently with a format-and-accuracy quality reward, so content quality is never confounded by mode-selection signals. A cold-start SFT stage on the newly constructed AdaThinking-Doc dataset (which contains both thinking and non-thinking responses for every question to avoid pre-biasing mode choice) initializes the model before RL begins.

## Results

AdaThinking-E-8B-Qwen3 (ATK mode) achieves 96.4 on DocVQA, 86.3 on InfoVQA, 90.1 on ChartQA, 57.3 on CharXivRQ, 86.6 on CharXivDQ, 89.8 on OCR-Bench, and 49.7 on OCR-Reasoning, setting new state-of-the-art across all eight document understanding benchmarks. On the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, AdaThinking-E improves over the non-thinking baseline by +0.6% on DocVQA, +1.8% on ChartQA, and +10.8% on OCR-Reasoning. Critically, AdaThinking-E achieves these gains with far fewer tokens: on CharXivRQ it uses only 27% thinking ratio (72 tokens) versus R-4B's 82% (366 tokens), and on CharXivDQ it outperforms R-4B by 0.6% with just 11% thinking ratio versus R-4B's 45%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest adaptive-thinking baselines, AdaThinking-E-7B-Qwen2.5 outperforms R-4B by +1.9% on ChartQA (89.1 vs 87.2) and +4.3% on OCR-Reasoning (26.5 vs 22.2), while also surpassing it on DocVQA (+2.2%), InfoVQA (+15.9%), and OCR-Bench (+5.1%). Compared to ARES-7B, AdaThinking-E leads by +4.2% on DocVQA, +10.7% on InfoVQA, +0.9% on ChartQA, and +3.1% on OCR-Reasoning. Against the non-thinking Qwen3-VL-8B baseline, AdaThinking-E-8B-Qwen3 gains +0.3% on DocVQA, +3.2% on InfoVQA, +0.5% on ChartQA, +10.9% on CharXivRQ, and +31.2% on OCR-Reasoning. Notably, AdaThinking-E's adaptive mode consistently matches or exceeds its own standalone thinking and non-thinking modes across all benchmarks, confirming that the entropy-regulated mode selection is effective. On token efficiency, AdaThinking-E achieves comparable accuracy to R-4B on CharXivRQ with only 27% thinking ratio (72 tokens) versus R-4B's 82% (366 tokens), and on CharXivDQ it beats R-4B by 0.6% with 11% thinking ratio versus 45%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeekMath) as its RL backbone but departs from it by decoupling the optimization of mode-switching tokens from response tokens, addressing a reward attribution problem that causes mode collapse in standard GRPO and DAPO. It positions itself against existing adaptive thinking approaches (R-4B, Keye-VL, ARES) that rely on hand-crafted rule rewards encoding subjective human judgments about question difficulty, instead proposing entropy of the mode-switch token as an intrinsic, label-free signal. The entropy-governed curriculum—from high-entropy exploration to low-entropy convergence—is a novel training mechanism not present in prior adaptive thinking work, which typically uses fixed reward functions throughout training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and growing concern: thinking-mode MLLMs waste compute and can hallucinate on simple queries, yet existing solutions require either manual mode selection or costly difficulty annotations. The entropy-regulation mechanism offers a principled, annotation-free alternative that could be adopted by other adaptive thinking pipelines beyond document understanding. However, the evaluation is confined to document VQA benchmarks with 7B/8B models, leaving open whether the entropy curriculum generalizes to other domains (math, code, general VQA) or scales to larger models. The modest absolute gains (0.6–1.8% on most benchmarks) suggest the mechanism is sound but not yet transformative.
