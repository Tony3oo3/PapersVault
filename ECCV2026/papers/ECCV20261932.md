## Problem

Existing generative video compositing methods face a control-fidelity trade-off: they either hallucinate motion from static references (losing the intrinsic dynamics of pre-animated video assets) or operate at abstract semantic levels without fine-grained spatial control. No unified framework exists that seamlessly integrates both static images and dynamic footage while enabling precise user-defined trajectory placement and photometric harmonization.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a foreground asset (either a single static image or a dynamic video clip), a background video sequence, a 3D trajectory specified in world coordinates (projected to 2D via camera intrinsics/extrinsics), an optional binary visibility schedule controlling when the object appears or disappears, and a text prompt. The output is a temporally coherent composite video at the background's resolution and duration, where the foreground follows the prescribed trajectory with synthesized lighting, shadows, and occlusion handling. For static inputs, the image is replicated to the target duration with temporal Gaussian noise injected to encourage plausible micro-motion synthesis.

## Method

FlexComposer introduces three key mechanisms built on the Wan2.1-I2V-14B DiT backbone. First, a Unified Canonical Foreground Representation stabilizes dynamic videos by tracking the subject centroid (via SpatialTracker v2) and applying reverse translation to center the subject, decoupling intrinsic motion from global displacement; static images are expanded temporally with noise injection and relighting augmentation. Second, Spatial-Aware Latent Injection is a parameter-free transport operation that exploits VAE latent translation equivariance: the 3D trajectory is discretized to latent grid coordinates, and canonical features are shifted from the center to the target position via simple offset mapping with a visibility gate for occlusions—no auxiliary adapters like ControlNet are needed. Third, the transported foreground latent is mask-blended with the background latent and channel-wise concatenated with the noisy latent as dense spatiotemporal conditioning for a Flow Matching–trained DiT. Training follows a three-stage synthetic-to-real curriculum (procedural 3D simulation → real-world footage → generative data) with LoRA (r=64) on all DiT projection layers, progressively disentangling geometric motion control from photometric appearance harmonization.

## Results

On DAVIS (static foreground compositing), FlexComposer (V2V) achieves the best trajectory error (EPE 2.15), FID (13.4), FVD (82.6), PSNR (17.40), and SSIM (0.658), outperforming the strongest baseline Wan-Move (EPE 2.50, FVD 94.3) by ~14% in FVD. On MoveBench, the V2V variant further improves to EPE 2.38, FVD 74.9, and SSIM 0.682. For dynamic foreground compositing (V2V), FlexComposer attains 91.20% Subject Consistency and 468.30 FVD, surpassing GenCompositor (89.75%, 535.71 FVD) by ~12.6% in FVD. On video harmonization (HYouTube), it leads all baselines with PSNR 42.5, SSIM 0.952, LPIPS 0.037, and CLIP 0.974.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexComposer outperforms all ten baselines across every evaluated metric and dataset. On trajectory-controlled I2V (DAVIS and MoveBench), the closest competitor is Wan-Move [13], which FlexComposer surpasses by 0.35 EPE and 11.7 FVD on DAVIS, and by 0.22 EPE and 8.6 FVD on MoveBench; older trajectory methods (ImageConductor, LeviTor, Tora, MagicMotion) lag substantially, with ImageConductor showing EPE >14 and FVD >400. For dynamic foreground compositing (V2V), FlexComposer beats GenCompositor [79] by 1.45% in Subject Consistency and 67.4 in FVD, while AnyV2V [38] and VACE [31] trail by >2% in Subject Consistency and >400 in FVD. On video harmonization (HYouTube), FlexComposer edges out GenCompositor [79] (PSNR 42.5 vs 42.0, LPIPS 0.037 vs 0.039) and clearly surpasses the specialized harmonization methods Harmonizer [34] and VTT [22] by 2.7 and 2.4 PSNR respectively, indicating that the generative compositing framework captures both photometric and geometric (shadow) consistency that dedicated color-matching methods miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexComposer builds directly on the Wan2.1-I2V-14B video diffusion transformer and initializes with Wan-Move's motion-controllable weights, adopting flow matching as its training objective. It departs from prior trajectory-control methods (ImageConductor, LeviTor, Tora, MagicMotion) that rely on auxiliary adapters like ControlNet by introducing a parameter-free latent injection that exploits VAE translation equivariance. Conceptually, it unifies two previously separate sub-problems—static image animation (where prior work hallucinates motion) and dynamic video compositing (where prior work like GenCompositor or VACE lacks precise trajectory control)—under a single canonical-representation + trajectory-guided generation formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified treatment of static and dynamic foregrounds with explicit trajectory control addresses a practical gap in VFX and content-creation pipelines where artists need both identity preservation and precise spatial placement. The parameter-free injection mechanism is attractive for practitioners because it avoids the signal degradation and additional training cost of auxiliary adapters. However, the paper acknowledges that physical interactions (contact, collision, realistic shadow casting) are not simulated and rely solely on diffusion priors, and long-duration generation with extreme viewpoint changes remains inconsistent—open questions that limit deployment in production VFX.
