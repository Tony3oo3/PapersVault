## Problem

Current multimodal LLMs trained with sparse, answer-level supervision produce inconsistent cross-view reasoning and brittle viewpoint selection in multi-view 3D VQA, failing to maintain coherent scene-level representations under partial observability. Existing approaches either rely solely on allocentric maps (misaligned with egocentric queries), generate geometrically unreliable intermediate maps from language alone, or provide only terminal reward signals that give limited credit assignment for multi-step spatial reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262077/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N multi-view RGB images of the same scene plus a spatial reasoning question, and produces a structured reasoning trajectory: a global allocentric cognitive map, an ordered sequence of selected viewpoints, per-view egocentric cognitive maps, and a final answer. The MLLM processes all views jointly for the global map but then selectively attends to individual views during trajectory planning and egocentric grounding, making the input consumption sequential and question-conditioned rather than a single parallel pass over all images.

## Method

DR-MV3D decomposes MV3D-VQA into three pipeline stages—global allocentric map construction, question-conditioned view-trajectory planning, and egocentric grounding—and trains the full pipeline with a two-stage procedure: supervised fine-tuning on structured trajectories followed by GRPO with a multi-level dense reward. The key novelty is the dense reward design: a global consistency reward aligns the MLLM-generated cognitive map with geometry-consistent pseudo targets produced by a frozen 3D vision foundation model (VGGT + SAM3), and a local trajectory reward supervises step-wise ordered viewpoint selection against a reference trajectory derived from benchmark metadata. This provides intermediate, verifiable supervision at each reasoning step without requiring manual annotations, addressing the sparse credit-assignment problem that limits answer-only RL. The approach is built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct and achieves 66.5% on MindCube-Tiny (28.7 points over the baseline) by combining process-level structural supervision with trajectory-level policy optimization.

## Results

On MindCube-Tiny, DR-MV3D (Qwen2.5-VL-3B + SFT + GRPO) achieves 66.5% overall accuracy, a 28.7-point improvement over the vanilla Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct baseline (37.8%) and 12.1 points above the strongest prior map-based method (MindCube-CGMap-SFT, 54.4%). On VSI-Bench, the model reaches 37.1 average score (Route Plan 32.4, Rel. Dir. 46.6, Rel. Dist. 37.8, App. Order 31.4), outperforming the Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline (30.4) and all other open-weight multi-image models. On BLINK (MV), DR-MV3D scores 56.4, surpassing RoBoBrain (55.6) and Spatial-MLLM (56.0) while using the same compact 3B backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MindCube, DR-MV3D (66.5 overall) decisively outperforms all open-weight baselines: it surpasses MindCube-CGMap-SFT (54.4) by 12.1 points, MindCube-CGMap-FFR-RL (53.7) by 12.8 points, SenseNova-SI (50.8) by 15.7 points, and Spatial-MLLM (42.8) by 23.7 points. Among proprietary models, DR-MV3D beats GPT-4.1 (49.6) and GPT-5 (56.3) but trails Gemini-2.5-Pro (59.3) by 7.2 points. On VSI-Bench, DR-MV3D (37.1 Avg) leads all compared methods, exceeding InternVL2-8B (36.7) and LLaVA-OneVision-7B (32.9). On BLINK (MV), DR-MV3D (56.4) edges out Spatial-MLLM (56.0) and RoBoBrain (55.6), and substantially outperforms Think3D with T3RL (53.4) and Qwen3-VL-4B-GRPO (52.4), all while using a smaller 3B backbone versus the 4B or 7B competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the map-then-reason paradigm established by MindCube and related work by introducing dense, verifiable rewards that supervise intermediate reasoning steps (cognitive map construction and view-trajectory planning) rather than relying solely on answer-level supervision. It builds on GRPO as the optimization backbone (from DeepSeekMath/DeepSeek-R1) and on the verifiable-reward RL line (R1-VL, Vision-R1), but departs from those by using frozen 3D Vision Foundation Models (VGGT + SAM3) as automatic pseudo-structural targets, eliminating the need for manual intermediate annotations. The allocentric-to-egocentric grounding step addresses a representation misalignment that prior map-based approaches (MindCube-CGMap, Think3D) leave unaddressed, and the VFM-as-reward-signal idea repurposes geometry-specialized models as training oracles rather than inference-time tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that process-level dense supervision from pretrained vision foundation models can substantially improve multi-view 3D spatial reasoning in compact (3B) MLLMs without manual annotation, offering a practical recipe for anyone working on multi-view or embodied reasoning tasks. A key open question is whether the VFM pseudo-targets generalize beyond the structured indoor scenes in MindCube to more complex or outdoor environments where VGGT's geometric assumptions may break down. Additionally, the approach is validated only on a single 3B backbone and three benchmarks, leaving unclear whether the dense-reward gains persist at larger scales or whether the VFM reward becomes a bottleneck as scene complexity increases.
