## Problem

Existing video-based world models generate videos independently from single viewpoints, making it impossible to guarantee that multiple generated videos depict the same underlying world with consistent geometry and motion. Shared world modeling—generating a set of spatially and temporally coherent videos from multi-view snapshots of one world—remains unexplored as a systematic task, despite being foundational for applications like multiplayer gaming and multi-robot coordination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260893/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N input images (multi-view, fixed-time snapshots of the same world) and a shared text prompt describing the world. The N images are pixel-wise concatenated into a single grid image that serves as the I2V input, which inherently reduces per-view resolution. The output is a single large grid video that is pixel-wise decoupled into N sub-videos, each corresponding to one input viewpoint with an unconstrained camera trajectory. The paper focuses on N=2 as the simplest non-trivial case, though scalability to N>2 is discussed.

## Method

IC-World activates the inherent in-context generation capability of a large video diffusion transformer (Wan2.1-14B step-distill) by feeding a pixel-wise concatenated grid of input images alongside an instructional prompt template that explicitly describes the shared-world constraint, enabling parallel generation of all views in a single forward pass without any additional architectural modules. To further enforce cross-view consistency, the model is fine-tuned via GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) using two novel reward models: a geometry consistency reward computed from 3D point cloud reconstruction (Pi3) and registration (Lepard) via symmetric Chamfer distance, and a motion consistency reward computed from 3D point trajectories (SpatialTracker) aligned by estimated camera extrinsics. Training uses LoRA (rank 64) with group size M=16, and the final reward is a weighted combination of both consistency signals. The key design choice is that GRPO alone (without SFT warm-up) achieves performance comparable to SFT+GRPO while being more data-efficient and computationally cheaper.

## Results

On the static-scene/dynamic-camera benchmark (Table 1), IC-World achieves the best geometry consistency scores across all confidence thresholds (Geometry₀.₁: 0.6980, Geometry₀.₅: 0.7217, Geometry₀.₇: 0.6994) and the highest VLM consistency score (0.7822). On the dynamic-scene/static-camera benchmark (Table 2), IC-World attains the best motion consistency at all tracking densities (Motion₁₀: 0.8360, Motion₂₀: 0.8466, Motion₃₀: 0.8500) and the lowest M-FID (157.61). On VBench, IC-World achieves a weighted average of 81.15, surpassing both the closed-source Gen-4 (80.89) and the open-source Wan2.1-14B (80.82).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IC-World outperforms all six baselines on both geometry and motion consistency metrics. On the geometry benchmark (Table 1), IC-World leads CogVideoX-I2V-5B by +0.025 on Geometry₀.₁ and +0.007 on Geometry₀.₅, and surpasses Wan2.1-14B by +0.008 and +0.005 respectively; the gap widens dramatically against DynamiCrafter (0.7217 vs 0.2837 on Geometry₀.₅) and EasyAnimate (0.7217 vs 0.4775), which struggle severely with cross-view spatial coherence. On the motion benchmark (Table 2), IC-World beats the strongest baseline Wan2.1-14B by +0.012 on Motion₁₀ and +0.010 on Motion₃₀, while also achieving the lowest M-FID (157.61 vs 185.24 for Wan2.1). On VBench (Table 3), IC-World's weighted average of 81.15 edges out Gen-4 (80.89) and Wan2.1-14B (80.82), driven primarily by a substantially higher Dynamic Degree score (72.36 vs 55.20 and 51.38), though it trails slightly on Subject Consistency (94.22 vs 94.86 for Wan2.1). The CLIP score is competitive but not dominant (0.6170–0.6400 range), indicating that the consistency gains do not come at the cost of text-video alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context generation paradigm for video diffusion transformers (Fei et al., 2024) from single-view tasks to multi-view shared world generation, using a simple pixel-wise grid coupling of input images combined with an instructional prompt to activate the model's inherent cross-view consistency. For optimization, it builds on DanceGRPO's formulation of denoising as an MDP and DeepSeek-R1's GRPO algorithm, but introduces two novel 3D-based reward models (point-cloud Chamfer distance and 3D point-tracking distance) that go beyond the video-text alignment rewards used in prior RL fine-tuning of video models. The paper explicitly positions shared world modeling as distinct from multi-view video generation (which uses fixed camera rigs) and 4D video generation (which is object-centric), arguing that neither prior formulation supports the unconstrained, scene-level, multi-camera setting it targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing shared world modeling as a formal task with dedicated benchmarks and metrics, this work opens a path toward applications like multiplayer video gaming and multi-robot coordination simulation that require multiple independent cameras to coherently represent a single world. The key limitation is that pixel-wise coupling reduces effective resolution per view, and the authors acknowledge that temporal stability degrades as N grows beyond 2, leaving scalability to larger player counts as an open challenge. The finding that GRPO alone (without SFT warm-up) matches SFT+GRPO performance with less data is practically useful for data-scarce settings, but the overall consistency gains over strong baselines remain moderate, suggesting that foundation model capacity is still the primary bottleneck.
