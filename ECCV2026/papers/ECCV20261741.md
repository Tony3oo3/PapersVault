## Problem

Existing World-Action Models for robot policy learning face two coupled bottlenecks: joint action-video reasoning requires iterative diffusion sampling of future video tokens at inference, incurring high latency, and the entangled modeling lets video prediction errors propagate into action decoding, degrading long-horizon control. The paper asks whether future visual dynamics can serve as a dense training-time supervision signal while remaining entirely optional at inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261741/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three camera views (left, front, right) that are spatially composed into a single composite image at standard resolution, a natural-language instruction, and a proprioceptive state vector. It outputs a continuous action chunk of length 48 (decoded from flow-matching latents) and, optionally, K sparse future observation frames at stride Δ=12. At inference the video branch is disabled, so only the 48-step action chunk is produced per control step in ~360 ms on an A100.

## Method

GigaWorld-Policy adapts the Wan 2.2 5B diffusion Transformer into a unified causal sequence model where all token types (observation, state, action, future-video) share a single stack of Transformer blocks with common Q/K/V projections. A blockwise causal attention mask enforces that action tokens attend only to state and current-observation tokens, while future-video tokens additionally attend to action tokens, preventing information leakage from predicted frames into action generation. Both action prediction and action-conditioned video forecasting are trained with flow matching; during pre-training only the video objective is optimized, and during post-training the two objectives are combined with λ_action=5 and λ_video=1. A three-stage curriculum (web-video initialization → ~10,000 h embodied pre-training on robot and egocentric human data → target-robot post-training) progressively injects physics priors before task-specific supervision. At inference, the model integrates the learned velocity field over s∈[0,1] to decode only action tokens, achieving a 9× speedup over Motus while retaining the visual-dynamics regularizer's benefit on task success.

## Results

On RoboTwin 2.0 simulation (50 tasks), GigaWorld-Policy achieves an average success rate of 0.87 (clean) and 0.85 (randomized), compared to 0.89/0.87 for Motus and 0.43/0.44 for π₀.₅. In real-world experiments on an AgileX PiPER arm, GigaWorld-Policy reaches 0.83 average success rate, outperforming Motus (0.76) by 7 percentage points while running 9× faster (360 ms vs. 3231 ms per inference on A100). The model also achieves 0.72 average on OOD real-world tasks and 0.68 on challenging high-dexterity tasks, consistently leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest WAM baseline Motus [2], GigaWorld-Policy achieves a 9× inference speedup (360 ms vs. 3231 ms) while improving real-world success rate by 7 percentage points (0.83 vs. 0.76), at the cost of a marginal 2-point drop in simulation SR (0.86 vs. 0.88). Compared to the VLA baseline π₀.₅ [23], GigaWorld-Policy improves RoboTwin 2.0 average success rate by 95% relative (0.87 vs. 0.44) and real-world SR by 14 percentage points (0.83 vs. 0.69), though it is slightly slower (360 ms vs. 225 ms). Against Cosmos-Policy [26], the speedup is 3.9× and the real-world SR gap is 25 percentage points (0.83 vs. 0.58). Versus GigaBrain-0 [51], GigaWorld-Policy gains 15 percentage points in real-world SR (0.83 vs. 0.68). On RoboTwin 2.0, X-VLA [77] achieves 0.73 average, trailing GigaWorld-Policy by 14 percentage points, confirming the advantage of action-centered world-model pre-training over VLM-based VLA architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

GigaWorld-Policy builds directly on the Wan 2.2 5B video generation backbone and the World-Action Model (WAM) paradigm introduced by Motus, VideoVLA, and Cosmos-Policy, which jointly predict actions and future visual dynamics. Its key departure is a causal attention mask that restricts action tokens from attending to future-video tokens, thereby decoupling inference from video generation: future visual dynamics serve purely as a dense training regularizer rather than a required inference-time output. This contrasts with bidirectional WAMs (Motus, VideoVLA) that require iterative video sampling at inference, and two-stage pipelines (Mimic-video) that first generate video then decode actions via an inverse dynamics model. The multi-stage pre-training curriculum—web video → embodied robot/egocentric data → target-robot post-training—extends the transfer-learning recipe from generic video models to robot-specific policy learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9× inference speedup (360 ms vs. 3231 ms for Motus) while matching or exceeding task success rates addresses a critical deployment bottleneck for real-time closed-loop robot control, where slow inference directly degrades performance through reduced action update rates. The data-efficiency result—matching a VLA's peak success rate with only 10% of training demonstrations—suggests the video-dynamics prior substantially reduces the sample cost of robot policy learning, which is practically significant given the expense of collecting real robot data. Open questions include whether the causal design generalizes to more complex multi-arm or dexterous manipulation, whether the video-supervision benefit persists at much larger model scales, and how the approach performs on robots with significantly different embodiment or control interfaces beyond the single AgileX PiPER platform tested.
