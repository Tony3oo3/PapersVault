## Problem

Evaluating whether a computer-use agent (CUA) trajectory truly fulfills a user instruction remains difficult to scale, as existing benchmarks rely on handcrafted scripts or task-specific rules that do not transfer to new tasks or environments. A learned reward model that judges success from execution video—the observable sequence of interface states—would be method-agnostic and universally applicable, but CUA trajectories are highly redundant with correctness hinging on subtle, localized UI changes, and public datasets lack balanced negative supervision with step-level failure annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261852/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural-language user instruction and a video-execution sequence (keyframes rendered at 720p, 1 FPS, up to 100 frames uniformly sampled from the trajectory) and outputs a binary success/failure judgment. In the attribution setting, it additionally predicts a short time range indicating where the first decisive error occurs, enabling fine-grained temporal localization of failure. The input is purely visual and textual—no agent-internal reasoning traces, tool calls, or action logs are required—making the evaluator applicable across heterogeneous agent architectures.

## Method

ExeVRM fine-tunes Qwen3-VL (4B/8B) with only LLM parameters trainable (vision encoder and projector frozen) on ExeVR-53k, a 53k-scale corpus of instruction-video-reward triplets unified from AgentNet, ScaleCUA, and OSWorld rollouts. To handle long, high-resolution execution videos within a fixed token budget, the paper introduces spatiotemporal token pruning: Spatial Token Pruning (STP) builds a per-frame UI-connected graph via Union-Find and removes large homogeneous components (static backgrounds, toolbars), while Temporal Token Pruning (TTP) compares each spatial token against a rolling reference using cosine similarity and drops tokens that remain nearly unchanged across frames, preserving only decisive state transitions. To address the scarcity of negative examples, adversarial instruction translation uses a VLM to generate plausible but semantically mismatched instructions for successful trajectory segments, along with step-level mismatch justifications that serve as temporal grounding labels. The combined STP+TTP pipeline reduces peak GPU memory to ~49.5 GiB at 50 frames (vs. OOM without pruning), enabling 720p long-horizon training on 8×A100-80GB while maintaining 84.7% accuracy and 87.7% recall on ExeVR-Bench.

## Results

ExeVRM 8B achieves 84.7% accuracy, 82.9% precision, and 87.7% recall on ExeVR-Bench (789 instances across Ubuntu, macOS/Windows, and Android), outperforming the strongest proprietary baseline Seed-2.0 Pro (80.3/83.9/74.7) by +4.4 accuracy and +13.0 recall. The 4B variant reaches 80.1/79.2/82.5, already surpassing GPT-5.2 (75.0/82.7/66.5) and Gemini 3 Pro (75.1/74.2/76.7). On temporal attribution (tIoU), ExeVRM consistently outperforms all baselines in localizing the decisive error span. Per-OS, the largest gains appear on Mac/Win (89.0 acc, 94.0 recall) and Android (83.5 acc, 95.0 recall).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against proprietary models, ExeVRM 8B beats Seed-2.0 Pro (the strongest baseline) by +4.4 accuracy and +13.0 recall, though Seed-2.0 Pro retains a slight precision edge (83.9 vs. 82.9). GPT-5.2 and Gemini 3 Pro trail by 9.7 and 9.6 accuracy points respectively, with recall gaps of 21.2 and 11.0 points. Among open-weight models, ExeVRM 8B surpasses Qwen3-VL 8B (its own base) by +17.0 accuracy and +37.8 recall, and beats InternVL-3.5 8B by +28.1 accuracy. In the evaluation-strategy comparison (Table 3), ExeVRM 4B outperforms AER (tail-screenshot only, 58.5 acc) and Simplified Judge (head+tail, 57.5 acc) by ~22–26 accuracy points on Ubuntu(Human), while full-keyframe methods at 360p (SE-WSM, ZeroGUI) score even lower (48–51 acc) due to resolution loss. The consistent pattern is that ExeVRM's recall advantage (87.7 vs. 49.9–76.7 for baselines) is the most pronounced, indicating that prior methods systematically miss negative trajectories that the video-based reward model correctly flags.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new sub-problem within reward modeling for computer-use agents: evaluating trajectories from execution video (a sequence of UI keyframes) rather than from agent-specific reasoning traces, tool calls, or individual screenshots. It draws on ShowUI's spatial graph-based token selection for the STP component and on back-translation for the adversarial instruction translation that synthesizes hard negatives, but repurposes both for the GUI-trajectory setting where critical evidence is subtle, transient, and localized. The spatiotemporal pruning strategy departs from general video token-pruning methods (which target action recognition or egocentric video) by explicitly preserving inter-frame state transitions while suppressing both spatially homogeneous regions and temporally repeated tokens, a combination not previously explored for reward modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ExeVR-53k dataset and ExeVR-Bench provide the first large-scale, multi-OS resource specifically designed for training and evaluating video-based CUA reward models, addressing a data bottleneck that has limited prior work to handcrafted rules or small-scale annotation. The spatiotemporal pruning technique makes 720p long-horizon video training tractable on standard hardware (8×A100), which is practically important for scaling evaluation to real-world agent trajectories. Open questions include whether the adversarial negatives faithfully capture the distribution of real agent failures, how well the model generalizes to unseen application domains or OS versions, and whether the approach extends to even longer trajectories (beyond 100 frames) or to multi-window/multi-monitor scenarios.
