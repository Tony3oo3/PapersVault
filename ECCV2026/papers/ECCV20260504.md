## Problem

Existing training-free motion customization methods are designed for full diffusion models with dozens of denoising steps, but distilled video generators collapse these into a few large-step updates, fundamentally altering the denoising dynamics that score-based guidance relies on. The paper identifies and empirically demonstrates that directly applying prior motion control strategies to distilled models causes severe motion degradation and structural artifacts, leaving a gap in how to achieve test-time motion customization without sacrificing the efficiency gains of distillation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260504/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference video (from which motion patterns are extracted via temporal attention maps or space-time diffusion features) and a text prompt describing the desired visual content. It produces a motion-customized video in which the generated content follows the reference motion while remaining semantically aligned with the text. The pipeline operates on distilled student models (T2V-Turbo-V2 with 4–16 steps, Video-Blade with 8 steps) that generate in seconds rather than minutes, while a full teacher diffusion model (VideoCrafter2 or Wan2.1-1.3B) is invoked selectively at inference time for trajectory correction—no additional training or fine-tuning is required.

## Method

MotionEcho is a training-free test-time distillation framework that corrects the distilled student's disrupted sampling trajectory via selective teacher guidance. At each denoising step, the student's motion-guided clean latent prediction is re-noised onto an intermediate timestep on the teacher's dense trajectory, where the teacher performs iterative motion customization; the teacher's corrected prediction is then blended with the student's via a closed-form linear interpolation (score distillation sampling reduced to z_new = (1−λ)z_student + λ·z_teacher), bypassing intractable Jacobian computation. An adaptive acceleration strategy governs efficiency: teacher guidance is activated only when a moving-window average of motion loss exceeds a threshold, and the teacher's internal denoising is dynamically truncated once the motion loss drops below a confidence threshold. The framework is architecture-agnostic, supporting both UNet-based consistency-distilled models (with MotionClone-style temporal attention guidance) and DiT-based trajectory-distilled models (with DiTFlow-style attention key/value injection).

## Results

On TurboBench (VideoCrafter2-based), MotionEcho with 16 distilled steps achieves the best Motion Fidelity (0.933), Text Alignment (0.348), and FID (322.97) among all methods, while completing inference in just 13 s with zero training cost. Even at 4 steps, it retains MF of 0.927 and FID of 347.91 in only 6 s. On DavisBench (Wan2.1-1.3B-based DiT), MotionEcho with DiTFlow guidance reaches MF 0.733 and FID 131.82 in 54 s, outperforming the strongest training-free baseline (DiTFlow, MF 0.605, ITC 120 s) by over 20% in motion fidelity while halving inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionEcho (16 steps) beats all training-free baselines on Motion Fidelity (0.933 vs. DMT's 0.909 and MotionClone's 0.876) and FID (322.97 vs. MotionInversion's 351.72), while also surpassing them on Text Alignment (0.348 vs. MotionClone's 0.335). It trails MotionClone by a narrow margin on Temporal Consistency (0.976 vs. 0.978) but is roughly 9× faster in inference (13 s vs. 114 s) and requires no training, unlike MotionDirector (280 s TTC) and MotionInversion (489 s TTC). On the DiT-based DavisBench, MotionEcho with DiTFlow guidance achieves MF 0.733, exceeding DiTFlow (0.605), DMT (0.622), MOFT (0.505), and the training-based MotionDirector* (0.627) by 11–23 points, while cutting inference time from 120 s to 54 s and eliminating the 3629 s training cost of MotionDirector*. The method also scales gracefully: at 8 steps it still outperforms most baselines on MF and FID in 9 s, and at 4 steps it maintains MF 0.927 in 6 s, demonstrating that the adaptive teacher-guided correction preserves quality even under aggressive step reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free motion customization line (MotionClone, DMT, MOFT, DiTFlow) by identifying that these methods fundamentally break on distilled few-step video generators due to altered denoising dynamics and sparse timesteps. Rather than modifying the student model or retraining, MotionEcho introduces a test-time distillation mechanism where a full-diffusion teacher selectively corrects the student's sampling trajectory via score interpolation at sub-interval endpoints. The adaptive acceleration strategy (step-wise activation and dynamic truncation) departs from uniform guidance by making teacher involvement conditional on motion-loss thresholds, drawing conceptually from speculative decoding and early-exit ideas. For DiT-based generators, the framework integrates DiTFlow's attention-based motion injection and MOFT/DMT's generalized motion extractors, making it architecture-agnostic across UNet and DiT backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As distilled few-step generators (T2V-Turbo-V2, Video-Blade) become the practical standard for video synthesis, the inability of existing motion control methods to operate on them represents a significant gap in the motion customization ecosystem. MotionEcho closes this gap while preserving the speed advantage of distillation (6–13s inference vs. 100s+ for full-model baselines), making motion transfer viable in latency-sensitive applications. Open questions remain: the method still requires access to a matching teacher model at inference time, and the authors note that unpaired teachers with different latent spaces and self-assessment mechanisms for more selective guidance are challenging future directions. The reliance on a co-located teacher also limits deployment in settings where only the distilled model is available.
