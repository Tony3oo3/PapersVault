## Problem

Existing HOI video generation methods suffer from two critical limitations: they lack an effective mechanism to inject multi-view object geometry into diffusion models, causing manipulated objects to lose shape consistency across frames (especially during rotation or flipping), and they rely heavily on fine-grained hand mesh annotations that are expensive to produce and difficult to scale. No prior approach simultaneously achieves wide-viewpoint object consistency and avoids hand mesh conditioning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261133/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ByteLOOM takes a human reference image, an OpenPose-style 2D skeleton sequence (no hand mesh), a textured 3D object mesh, and a per-frame 6-DoF object pose trajectory as input. The object mesh is rendered into a 9-view RCM-cache (sparse RCM-RGB image pairs) that is concatenated along the frame dimension of the video latent, while per-frame RCM images are concatenated along the channel dimension. The output is a 97-frame (~6 s at 15 FPS) third-person product-showcase video at the resolution supported by the Wan2.1 VAE, with all conditions fused into the input latent via a lightweight MLP before entering the DiT backbone.

## Method

ByteLOOM is built on the Wan2.1 Diffusion Transformer and introduces two core mechanisms. First, the RCM-cache encodes object geometry as Relative Coordinate Maps (vertex positions normalized to [0,1]³ via axis-aligned bounding-box division, then rasterized with barycentric interpolation), creating explicit cross-view pixel correspondence that lets the model treat multi-view references as a single object rather than independent images; per-frame RCM inputs further specify the target 6-DoF pose, reducing the generation task from novel view synthesis to texture lookup. Second, a three-stage curriculum progressively trains the model: Stage I on abundant pose-only human motion data for basic animation, Stage II on hand-object datasets (DexYCB, HO3D, ARCTIC) for contact and occlusion reasoning, and Stage III on the small Mani4D-Train set for full-body HOI with all conditions active. All conditioning signals are injected through simple input-latent concatenation followed by an MLP fuser, letting self-attention learn inter-condition relationships without dedicated cross-attention modules. A depth-free data curation pipeline (DWPose + SAM2 + MoGe2 + FoundationPose) robustly extracts all required annotations from raw video without requiring depth sensors.

## Results

On Mani4D-Test, ByteLOOM (full curriculum I+II+III) achieves the best object fidelity scores: Obj-IoU of 0.8288 and Obj-CLIP of 0.9100, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline AnchorCrafter (0.6461 / 0.7355). It also attains the best hand accuracy (LMD 0.1427), face similarity (Face-Cos 0.8891), and geometry consistency metrics (MEt3R 0.0454, T-SSIM 0.9147), with T-SSIM approaching the ground-truth value of 0.9218. The only slight regression is in background consistency (0.9289 vs. 0.9495 for UniAnimate-DiT), attributed to color shifting during multi-segment inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ByteLOOM decisively outperforms all three baselines on every object-fidelity and geometry-consistency metric. Against AnchorCrafter (the main baseline), it improves Obj-IoU by 0.183 (0.8288 vs. 0.6461), Obj-CLIP by 0.175 (0.9100 vs. 0.7355), Face-Cos by 0.312 (0.8891 vs. 0.5771), and reduces LMD from 0.2629 to 0.1427, while also achieving better MEt3R (0.0454 vs. 0.0552) and T-SSIM (0.9147 vs. 0.9079). Against UniAnimate-DiT, the gains are even larger on object metrics (Obj-IoU +0.364, Obj-CLIP +0.145) and hand accuracy (LMD 0.1427 vs. 0.3260), though UniAnimate-DiT retains a slight edge in background consistency (0.9495 vs. 0.9289) and subject consistency (0.9634 vs. 0.9535). Against MimicMotion, ByteLOOM leads on all object and geometry metrics (Obj-IoU +0.364, MEt3R 0.0454 vs. 0.0638, T-SSIM 0.9147 vs. 0.9010) while matching or slightly exceeding it on motion smoothness. The consistent pattern is that ByteLOOM's RCM-cache mechanism and curriculum training yield the strongest multi-view object geometry, at a minor cost in background consistency due to multi-segment inference color drift.

## Relation to Prior Work

ByteLOOM builds on the DiT-based video generation paradigm (Wan2.1) and repurposes the Relative Coordinate Map (RCM) from LucidFusion's 3D Gaussian generation pipeline as an explicit conditioning signal for diffusion-based video synthesis. It departs from the dominant HOI video generation lineage (AnchorCrafter, DreamActor-H1) that relies on fine-grained hand mesh and depth-map conditioning, instead using a simple OpenPose-style skeleton and a three-stage curriculum that progressively introduces interaction complexity. The RCM-cache mechanism also extends the multi-view consistency literature (MVDream, 4Diffusion) by binding sparse reference views to a persistent 3D coordinate prior, converting a novel view synthesis problem into a texture-lookup problem within the diffusion process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RCM-cache mechanism offers a general, mesh-based recipe for injecting multi-view 3D geometry into any DiT video generator, potentially applicable beyond HOI to subject-consistent video and product showcase generation. The Mani4D benchmark and depth-free curation pipeline lower the barrier for building HOI training data without expensive hand-mesh annotation. However, the framework is scoped to rigid objects in allocentric studio settings, assumes stable mesh topology, and provides no physical plausibility guarantee—extending to articulated/deformable objects, egocentric viewpoints, and in-the-wild interactions remains open.
