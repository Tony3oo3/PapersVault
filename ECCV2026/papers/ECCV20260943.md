## Problem

Facial landmark detectors trained on real human faces fail on stylized domains such as caricatures and artistic portraits due to severe domain gaps in both appearance and geometry. Self-training-based UDA, the dominant adaptation strategy, breaks down under these large shifts because the model amplifies confident yet erroneous pseudo-labels in a collapse cycle. A robust mechanism for validating pseudo-label quality beyond simple confidence scores is therefore essential.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB face image from an unlabeled target domain (caricature, artistic portrait, etc.), resized to 256×256, and outputs 68 2D facial landmark coordinates. During adaptation the input is additionally consumed by a frozen face parser (EHANet) and a frozen StyleGAN2 generator to produce validation signals, but all auxiliary networks are discarded at inference, so the deployment footprint and speed remain identical to a standard landmark detector.

## Method

PriSM validates pseudo-labels through two complementary consistency checks that jointly enforce coarse structural alignment and fine-grained geometric correctness. The Parsing Network converts pseudo-label landmarks into a parsing map via a domain-agnostic Point-to-Parse (P2P) module and enforces Cross-Entropy consistency with a reference parsing map from a pre-trained face parser, providing a relaxed structural constraint that is more resilient to domain shift than precise landmark regression. The StyleMix Network performs an analysis-by-synthesis loop: it disentangles the input face into an appearance latent code (via per-image GAN inversion) and a structure latent code (via a parsing encoder applied to the pseudo-label's parsing map), style-mixes them (coarse layers 0–7 from structure, fine layers 8–17 from appearance), and synthesizes a face with a frozen StyleGAN2 generator; the LPIPS reconstruction loss between the synthesized and original images directly penalizes geometric errors in the pseudo-label. Training alternates between minimizing the weighted sum of parsing and reconstruction losses on target images and supervised fine-tuning on source data plus refined pseudo-labels, which prevents catastrophic forgetting on the source domain.

## Results

PriSM achieves a target-domain NME of 7.35 on CariFace and 3.61 on ArtiFace, outperforming the previous SoTA (CFW) by 0.35 and 0.32 NME respectively. It also maintains the best source-domain performance (300W Full: 3.18 and 3.17 NME), effectively preventing catastrophic forgetting. On unseen cross-domain evaluation, PriSM records 3.80 NME on ArtiFace (adapted from CariFace) and 10.92 NME on CariFace (adapted from ArtiFace), both the lowest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PriSM outperforms all six baselines on both target domains. On the 300W→CariFace task, it reduces target NME from 7.70 (CFW, the closest competitor) to 7.35, and from 13.63 (BDL) to 7.35 — a 46% relative improvement over the weakest baseline. On 300W→ArtiFace, PriSM achieves 3.61 NME versus CFW's 3.93 and BDL's 5.90. The adversarial-based methods (RevGrad, ASN) and image-translation methods (FDA, CycleGAN) all suffer large target-domain errors (10.67–13.63 NME on CariFace), confirming that neither domain-invariant features nor style transfer alone suffice for geometric adaptation. Notably, PriSM also achieves the best source-domain scores across all three 300W splits, whereas several baselines (e.g., ASN, RevGrad) degrade on the source domain after adaptation, highlighting the effectiveness of the alternating optimization with source data in preventing catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

PriSM builds directly on the alternating optimization protocol of CFW [29] (CVPR 2024), which introduced a two-step loop of pseudo-label refinement followed by supervised fine-tuning on combined source and target data. However, it departs fundamentally from the self-training paradigm's reliance on confidence scores for pseudo-label validation, instead introducing a dual-consistency mechanism that repurposes two frozen auxiliary networks—a face parser and a StyleGAN2 generator—as external geometric oracles. The use of StyleGAN's style-mixing capability within an analysis-by-synthesis loop as a *validation* tool (rather than a generation or translation tool) is a novel repurposing of GAN architecture, while the parsing-based structural check follows the broader trend of using auxiliary self-supervised tasks to assess pseudo-label quality [6, 10].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical and well-recognized failure mode of self-training UDA—confident-but-wrong pseudo-labels under large domain shifts—by providing a principled, label-free validation signal that is more robust than confidence thresholds. The demonstrated generalization to completely unseen domains (sketches, anime, animal faces) without any fine-tuning suggests the method learns domain-invariant geometric principles rather than target-specific appearance shortcuts. Key limitations include restriction to the 68-point landmark configuration (due to the P2P module) and the computational overhead of StyleGAN inversion during training (~3.1× slower per batch). Open questions include whether the dual-consistency framework extends to other structured-output tasks (e.g., pose estimation, keypoint detection on non-facial objects) and whether the StyleGAN prior's validity degrades for extreme geometric distortions beyond caricature-level exaggeration.
