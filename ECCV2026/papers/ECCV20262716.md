## Problem

Existing drawing-based 3D animation pipelines rely on sample-wise 2D refinement that aligns rendered projections to the input drawing, but this per-character optimization overfits to the observed frontal view and fails to correct projection-induced artifacts (missing contours, speckling, texture degradation) in novel views. The fundamental gap is the absence of scalable paired supervision connecting artifact-prone projections to clean multi-view targets, making it impossible to learn correction priors that generalize across characters, motions, and viewpoints.

## Input / Output

DraViE consumes a triplet per frame: a 512×512 RGB rendered projection Z, a binary foreground mask Z^mask, and a positional hint Z^pos encoding canonical mesh surface coordinates as normalized RGB values. The network is fully convolutional (U-Net) and trained on 32×32 patches sampled from the mask-guided region, but processes full-resolution frames at inference. The output is a single refined 2D frame Y that corrects view-dependent projection artifacts while preserving the input drawing's line style, color palette, and motion plausibility.

## Method

The paper introduces SPECSIA-15K, a paired dataset of 14,980 artifact-corrupted/clean projection pairs constructed by rendering 1,498 3DBiCar characters from 10 yaw viewpoints as ground truth, then reconstructing each character from a single frontal view via Wonder3D + Mixamo rigging and re-projecting to the same viewpoints to produce artifact-prone inputs. DraViE is a U-Net encoder-decoder trained with data-level priors via patch-wise supervised learning (L1 + adversarial + VGG perceptual loss) on these pairs, rather than the sample-wise per-character optimization used by prior methods. The positional hint provides location-aware conditioning so the network learns where on the character surface each patch originates, enabling targeted structural recovery. An optional lightweight adaptation fine-tunes the pre-trained generator for a single epoch on the input character's renderings, calibrating stroke thickness and color tone without overfitting to the reference view.

## Results

On the novel-view evaluation (the primary challenge), DraViE achieves CLIP 0.859, SSIM 0.840, LPIPS 0.245, and FID 206.13, substantially outperforming DrawingSpinUp (FID 280.83) and OSF (FID 270.21). On frontal views, DraViE matches or slightly exceeds baselines (CLIP 0.905, SSIM 0.846, LPIPS 0.208, FID 201.31), confirming that the correction does not degrade observed-view quality. A 30-person blinded user study shows 86.7% overall preference for DraViE over prior methods, with 73.3% preferring it specifically for artifact reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DraViE is compared against DrawingSpinUp (DSU, SIGGRAPH Asia 2024) and the Occlusion-robust Stylization Framework (OSF, ICCV 2025), both of which use sample-wise 2D refinement. On frontal views, DraViE ties OSF on CLIP (0.905) and slightly leads on SSIM (0.846 vs. 0.841) and LPIPS (0.208 vs. 0.211), while FID is marginally better (201.31 vs. 202.24). The decisive advantage appears in novel views: DraViE reduces FID by 74.7 points over DSU (206.13 vs. 280.83) and 64.1 points over OSF (206.13 vs. 270.21), while also improving CLIP by 0.012 over DSU and 0.009 over OSF. Cross-pipeline experiments (Table 2) confirm that the same pre-trained DraViE checkpoint improves FID across three different upstream backends (Wonder3D, InstantMesh, CRM) without backend-specific retraining, e.g., FID 203 vs. 243/236 on Wonder3D. In the blinded user study, DraViE is preferred 86.7% of the time overall and 73.3% for artifact reduction, versus 13.3% and 26.7% for the prior method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the drawing-based 3D animation refinement line initiated by DrawingSpinUp (DSU) [37] and the Occlusion-robust Stylization Framework (OSF) [34], both of which perform sample-wise 2D post-correction on projected renderings. The key conceptual departure is shifting from per-character, sample-wise optimization—which the authors argue overfits to the observed view—to data-level pre-training on a purpose-built paired dataset (SPECSIA-15K), enabling a plug-and-play correction prior that generalizes across characters and viewpoints. The architecture and patch-wise training scheme are inherited from DSU, while the positional-hint conditioning draws on Stylizing Video by Example [13], and the broader stylization framing connects to GAN- and diffusion-based image-to-image translation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPECSIA-15K fills a concrete gap: prior drawing-based animation pipelines lacked scalable paired supervision for novel-view artifact correction, forcing expensive per-character optimization. The plug-and-play nature of DraViE and its demonstrated cross-pipeline transferability (Wonder3D, InstantMesh, CRM) make it a practical drop-in improvement for existing systems. However, the method remains bounded by upstream 3D quality—it cannot repair severe topology or rigging errors—and processes frames independently without explicit temporal modeling, leaving flickering under rapid motion as an open issue. Whether data-level priors can substitute for or complement explicit temporal attention in animation refinement is an open question the paper leaves for future work.
