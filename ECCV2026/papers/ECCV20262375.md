limitations: The method enforces only anchored cocycle constraints (at fixed endpoints 0 and 1) rather than the full cocycle property for arbitrary triplets, which the authors justify as computationally efficient but not formally proven sufficient in all regimes. ## Problem

Non-autonomous ODE formulations for diffeomorphic registration offer greater expressiveness than stationary-velocity models, but existing approaches enforce flow structure only implicitly through numerical integration, entangling deformation quality with discretization scheme and solver accuracy. This coupling necessitates multiple handcrafted smoothness penalties and makes diffeomorphic guarantees dependent on integration step size rather than being structurally guaranteed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262375/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 2D or 3D medical images (moving and fixed) across MRI, CT, and ultrasound modalities and outputs a diffeomorphic deformation field that warps the moving image to the fixed one. At inference, the full deformation is not predicted in a single pass but constructed by composing N=4 locally predicted short-time flow maps over subintervals of [0,1], each produced by the same time-embedded network evaluated at different (s,t) pairs. The network is conditioned on both images simultaneously and on the two time parameters via sinusoidal embeddings.

## Method

TPFM-DIR directly parameterizes the two-parameter flow map φ_{s,t} = Id + (t−s)·f_θ(s,t; I_m, I_f) using a doubly time-embedded U-Net, bypassing explicit velocity field parameterization and numerical ODE integration entirely. The key structural constraint is an anchored cocycle regularization that enforces φ_{s,t}∘φ_{0,s} = φ_{0,t} (and its backward counterpart), which the authors prove is sufficient to guarantee a valid non-autonomous ODE solution under standard regularity assumptions. Training alternates between forward and backward temporal directions each epoch to maintain symmetry while avoiding doubled memory, using a temporal NCC similarity loss that aligns the moving image's forward trajectory with the fixed image's backward trajectory at sampled intermediate times. At inference, four compositions of short-time maps approximate the continuous-time evolution, improving smoothness and reducing folding compared to single-step prediction while adding minimal computational cost.

## Results

TPFM-DIR achieves the best Dice across all nine datasets while maintaining near-zero folding. On brain MRI (OASIS/IXI), it reaches 87.08% and 82.52% Dice respectively, improving by an average of 2.1% over the next-best method. On LungCT, it reduces TRE to 2.19 mm (a 12% reduction over CorrMLP's 2.48 mm) with zero negative-Jacobian voxels. On cardiac 2D datasets (ACDC/CAMUS), it attains 87.43% and 85.13% Dice, outperforming the strongest 2D baseline AdaCS by ~2–3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPFM-DIR outperforms all 16 baselines on every dataset. Among diffeomorphic methods, it surpasses the strongest (GradICON: 83.74% on OASIS, 76.43% on IXI) by 3.3 and 6.1 Dice points respectively, while also achieving the lowest folding (0.0019% vs. 0.0039% on OASIS). Among deformable methods, it beats the top performer HViT (85.07% OASIS, 80.67% IXI) by 2.0 and 1.9 Dice points, and CorrMLP (84.66% OASIS) by 2.4 points, while reducing folding from 0.46–0.48% to 0.0019%. On LungCT, TPFM-DIR achieves 2.19 mm TRE versus CorrMLP's 2.48 mm (12% reduction) and GradICON's 2.64 mm (17% reduction), with zero negative-Jacobian voxels compared to 0.27% and 0.0009% respectively. On 2D cardiac data, it leads AdaCS by 1.97 Dice on ACDC and 2.46 Dice on CAMUS, while maintaining near-zero folding (0.0003% and 0.0%) versus AdaCS's 0.15% and 0.003%. The non-autonomous ODE baselines (R2Net, NODEO) are consistently outperformed by 3–7 Dice points on brain MRI and by 0.3–0.5 mm TRE on LungCT, confirming that direct flow-map modeling with cocycle regularization is more effective than numerical integration of time-dependent velocities.

## Relation to Prior Work

TPFM-DIR departs from the dominant paradigm in learning-based diffeomorphic registration, where a velocity field (autonomous or non-autonomous) is parameterized and integrated numerically to recover the deformation. While autonomous ODE methods (VoxelMorph, TransMorph-diff) use scaling-and-squaring for efficiency, and non-autonomous methods (NODEO, R2Net) gain expressiveness at the cost of costly ODE solvers during training, this paper shifts the object of learning from the velocity to the two-parameter flow map φ_{s,t} itself, enforcing the cocycle property as a structural regularizer instead of relying on discretized integration. The theoretical grounding draws on the classical theory of non-autonomous dynamical systems (Kloeden & Rasmussen), and the time-conditioned architecture borrows sinusoidal embeddings from the flow-matching literature, but the specific combination of direct flow-map parameterization with anchored cocycle regularization is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the long-standing accuracy-versus-topology trade-off in diffeomorphic registration by showing that a principled structural constraint (cocycle consistency) can replace multiple handcrafted smoothness penalties while simultaneously improving alignment. The architecture-agnostic validation (improving both CorrMLP and TransMorph backbones) suggests the formulation is a drop-in upgrade for existing pipelines rather than a monolithic system. Open questions include whether the anchored (rather than full) cocycle constraint is provably sufficient in all regimes, how the uniform-sampling bias toward small time increments (noted in Appendix B) might be mitigated, and whether the direct flow-map learning strategy generalizes to other non-autonomous ODE problems beyond image registration.
