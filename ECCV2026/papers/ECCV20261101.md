## Problem

Existing video quality metrics—whether pixel-level, perceptual, or MLLM-based—are strongly appearance-biased and lack temporal understanding, making them unable to detect subtle anatomical violations or motion incoherence in generated human actions. No prior benchmark or metric specifically probes the fine-grained temporal correctness and physical plausibility of human body dynamics over time, leaving a critical gap in evaluating the most visually salient failure mode of current video generators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261101/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a generated video clip (I2V or T2V) at native resolution (320×240, 25 FPS) along with a target action class label. The video is segmented into overlapping temporal windows of T=32 frames (8-frame overlap), from which per-frame SMPL parameters, 2D keypoints, ViT appearance features, and their first-order temporal derivatives are extracted. It produces two scalar scores: Action Consistency (S_cons, distance to the real-action centroid in learned embedding space) and Temporal Coherence (S_temp, mean L2 distance between consecutive frame embeddings), both where lower indicates higher quality.

## Method

The core contribution is a learned latent manifold of real-world human actions, built by fusing appearance-agnostic skeletal geometry (SMPL pose, shape, global orientation) with appearance-based features (2D keypoints, ViT embeddings) and their temporal derivatives. An encoder processes each feature through separate dilated 1D temporal convolutions (kernel 5, dilation {1,2,4}), additively fuses static and motion pathways per frame, applies learned attention to produce a single frame-level representation, and aggregates over the window via a 4-layer Transformer with a [CLS] token. Training combines a supervised contrastive loss (clustering same-action windows, separating different actions) with a hard-negative loss that pushes temporally distorted variants (shuffled, frozen, reversed frames) away from their coherent counterparts, explicitly teaching temporal sensitivity. At inference, S_cons measures the L2 distance between a generated video's [CLS] embedding and the precomputed real-action centroid, while S_temp quantifies the smoothness of the per-frame embedding trajectory within each window.

## Results

On TAG-Bench-v0, the proposed S_cons metric achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.61 with human Action Consistency ratings, and S_temp achieves 0.64 with human Temporal Coherence ratings. These represent relative improvements of +35.6% and +68.4% over the best baseline (GPT-5 at 0.45 and 0.38, respectively). On the external VBench-2.0 Human Anatomy benchmark, S_temp reproduces the exact same model ranking as human annotators across four text-to-video models, despite being evaluated on actions not seen during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed metrics outperform all eight baseline categories on both evaluation axes. Among feature-based metrics, CLIP-sim (0.03/0.16) and DINO-sim (0.08/0.21) correlate poorly with human ratings, confirming that frame-level appearance similarity cannot capture motion dynamics. Fine-tuned MLLM evaluators (VideoPhy-2, VideoScore2) achieve modest correlations (≤0.37), as they target general physical commonsense rather than human-specific kinematics. Among zero-shot MLLM prompting baselines, GPT-5 is the strongest (0.45/0.38), yet S_cons and S_temp still exceed it by +0.16 and +0.26 in absolute correlation, respectively. Notably, the proposed metrics are trained on only 10 UCF-101 action classes with a small encoder, yet generalize to unseen actions on VBench-2.0 and to 23 action classes (0.59/0.56) while maintaining a clear advantage over GPT-5 (0.46/0.39). The inter-rater human agreement ceiling is 0.72/0.71, indicating the proposed metrics approach but do not fully saturate human-level consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of human motion analysis and video generation evaluation. Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes well-established components—SMPL/TokenHMR for 3D skeletal geometry, DWPose for 2D keypoints, and ViT for appearance—into a unified contrastive learning framework that had not previously been applied to evaluating generated video quality. It departs from MLLM-based evaluators (VideoScore, VideoPhy) that treat video quality as a general perception problem, and from distribution-matching metrics (FVD, CLIP-sim) that ignore fine-grained temporal dynamics, by explicitly encoding human-body-specific anatomical constraints and their temporal derivatives into a learned action manifold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a concrete gap: no existing metric or MLLM (including GPT-5) reliably captures whether a generated human action is both semantically correct and temporally smooth, with the best prior method achieving only 0.38 Spearman correlation on temporal coherence versus 0.64 for the proposed metric. TAG-Bench-v0 provides a reusable, human-annotated benchmark that the community can adopt for tracking progress in human-motion fidelity. Key limitations include the narrow action vocabulary (10–23 classes), single-person I2V setting, and reliance on HMR/pose estimators that degrade under occlusion; open questions include scaling to multi-person scenes, longer-form videos, and integrating these human-centric features into MLLM-based evaluators.
