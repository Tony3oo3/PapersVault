## Problem

Existing pose estimators are restricted to specific object classes (e.g., human, vehicle) and fixed structures, while multi-class datasets like UniKPT merely concatenate single-class datasets with limited class and structural diversity. This paper addresses both the benchmark gap—lacking a pose dataset covering extensive semantic classes with diverse intra-class structures—and the method gap—lacking a fully-supervised baseline that can handle variable keypoint types and counts across classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262172/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (3×H×W) and produces three outputs: a structure prototype label (one-hot over N prototypes), M=300 keypoint coordinate pairs normalized to [0,1], and M binary visibility scores. Because keypoint types and counts vary across prototypes, ground-truth poses are padded to a unified dimension K=100 with visibility set to zero for absent keypoints. At inference, the predicted prototype index selects the corresponding stored cost matrix to resolve which of the M queries map to the K target keypoints.

## Method

The architecture uses a ResNet-50 backbone to extract multi-scale features; global average pooling on the deepest layer feeds a classifier for structure prototype prediction, while M=300 learnable keypoint embeddings (queries) are processed by a deformable-attention transformer decoder to regress coordinates and visibilities. The key mechanism is a per-prototype bipartite matching cost matrix, updated via momentum (λ=0.8) across training iterations, that adaptively assigns each query to a target keypoint based on coordinate L1 distance and visibility cross-entropy. This allows a single query to learn shared information across structurally similar keypoints in different classes (e.g., 'eye' in birds vs. dogs) rather than being locked to a fixed index. Training combines prototype classification cross-entropy with matched keypoint regression and visibility losses, while unmatched queries are supervised to predict zero visibility.

## Results

On the PoseImageNet benchmark, the proposed query-based model with matching-based supervision achieves 57.0 mAP@0.2, outperforming the strongest baseline RTMO (48.2) by +8.8 points. The method also leads across all 13 super-classes, with particularly large gains on structure (70.4 vs. 55.9 for RTMO), plant (74.1 vs. 66.3), and wear items (62.3 vs. 46.4). For the auxiliary prototype classification task, ResNet-50 reaches 65.0% Top-1 accuracy and 94.0% Top-5 accuracy over 3500+ structure prototypes. Ablation shows that replacing adaptive matching with fixed 1-to-1 keypoint assignment drops mAP@0.2 from 57.0 to 35.3, while using ground-truth class labels at test time boosts it to 79.5, indicating that prototype misclassification is the primary remaining bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on the overall mAP@0.2 metric. The closest competitor is RTMO (48.2), a one-stage real-time multi-person pose estimator, which the proposed method surpasses by +8.8 points; the gap widens to +16.1 on wear items and +14.5 on structure super-classes. Among transformer-based baselines, GroupPose (47.7) and NerPE (46.8) trail by 9.3 and 10.2 points respectively, while the earlier ViTPose (43.8) and PCT (44.6) lag further behind. The simplest baseline, Sim.Base. (39.9), is outperformed by a margin of +17.1, highlighting the benefit of the query-based architecture with adaptive matching over fixed-index regression. Notably, the ablation with fixed 1-to-1 keypoint matching (35.3) even falls below Sim.Base., confirming that the adaptive bipartite matching mechanism is the critical design choice enabling cross-class keypoint sharing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-class pose estimation line initiated by MP-100 and UniKPT, which concatenate single-class datasets but remain limited in class and structural diversity. Architecturally, it adopts the deformable keypoint decoder from MetaPoint [6] and the bipartite matching mechanism from Deformable DETR [72] and [9], repurposing the latter to adaptively align a fixed set of learned queries to variable keypoint structures across prototypes. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the decomposition of each semantic class into multiple structure prototypes (via TPS-based deformability), which prior multi-class datasets treat as a single fixed structure per class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PoseImageNet (720 classes, 3500+ structure prototypes) is the first pose benchmark at ImageNet scale, filling a gap where prior multi-class datasets covered only a handful of super-classes. The structure-prototype decomposition and matching-based supervision offer a principled way to handle intra-class structural diversity in a fully-supervised setting, complementing the few/zero-shot approaches that dominate the literature. Key open questions remain: prototype misclassification causes significant performance loss (+18.5% mAP gap with GT class), and the flat prototype taxonomy lacks the hierarchical structure that likely exists in real object geometries, limiting the method's ability to share information across structurally related but semantically distinct classes.
