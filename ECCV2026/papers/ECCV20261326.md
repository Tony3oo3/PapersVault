## Problem

Multimodal LLMs for industrial anomaly detection rely on pretrained common sense rather than explicit manufacturing criteria, causing them to miss logical anomalies such as a sealed bottle appearing before the capping stage. Existing datasets are heavily skewed toward structural defects (only 4.7% logical anomalies in MVTec AD, 8.2% in VisA), and current RL-based approaches treat reasoning traces as monolithic outputs, preventing fine-grained correction of individual rule misjudgments.

## Input / Output

The model receives a product image (up to 1024×1024) alongside K explicit inspection rules encoding product-specific manufacturing constraints (quantity, object-type, foreign-object, structural consistency). It produces a structured chain-of-thought trace that evaluates each rule sequentially with a per-rule satisfied/violated judgment, followed by a final anomaly determination. The structured output format enables regex-based extraction of individual rule judgments, which is essential for the rule-level reward mechanism in the RL stage.

## Method

SCAN is a two-stage post-training pipeline. Stage 1 (SFT) trains the model to perform rule-by-rule verification against explicit criteria, using order-varied training pairs to combat 'prefix interference' where the model restates rule text as visual observations instead of grounding in the image. Stage 2 (RL) decomposes the standard sequence-level reward into per-rule binary rewards and introduces rule-level credit assignment: each token receives the advantage of the rule segment it belongs to rather than a uniform per-rollout advantage, so the policy gradient for each reasoning segment is driven solely by that segment's own correctness. The FLAW dataset supports this pipeline by combining controlled image synthesis (from MANTA and RealIAD) with targeted retrieval from vision-language corpora (ShareGPT4V, ALLaVA, Denseworld-1m), producing images with rich object-level metadata from which diverse inspection rules and logical violations are automatically generated.

## Results

On MVTec-LOCO (zero-shot), SCAN-8B achieves 75.16% binary accuracy and 64.58% scoring accuracy (LLM-judged reasoning quality), with scoring F1 of 53.50%. This surpasses GPT-5.2-Chat by 4.85% in scoring accuracy and 3.79% in scoring F1, and outperforms Gemini-3.1-Pro by 0.94% in scoring accuracy. The 8B model also beats the much larger Qwen3-VL-32B (62.40% scoring Acc) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (52.69% scoring Acc), while the 7B variant exceeds Qwen2.5-VL-72B by 4.41% in scoring accuracy. Gains over each base model are consistent across scales: +7.85% scoring accuracy at 8B, +8.29% at 7B, and +4.30% at 3B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCAN-8B outperforms GPT-5.2-Chat by 4.85% in scoring accuracy (64.58 vs 59.73) and 3.79% in scoring F1 (53.50 vs 49.71), while also leading in binary accuracy (75.16 vs 74.67). Against Gemini-3.1-Pro, SCAN-8B edges out on scoring accuracy (64.58 vs 63.64) but trails in scoring recall (39.83 vs 71.15) and binary F1 (70.10 vs 78.66), reflecting a precision-over-recall trade-off. The 8B model surpasses the 4×-larger Qwen3-VL-32B by 2.18% in scoring accuracy and 3.02% in scoring F1, and the 7B variant beats Qwen2.5-VL-72B by 4.41% in scoring accuracy (57.10 vs 52.69) and 15.13% in scoring F1. Among domain-specific IAD models, all three (AnomalyGPT-7B, Myriad-7B, Triad-ov-7B) are substantially outperformed; the best of these, Triad-ov-7B, achieves only 45.03% scoring accuracy versus SCAN-8B's 64.58%. The consistent 4–8% scoring accuracy gains over each respective base model across 3B/7B/8B scales confirm the training recipe generalizes rather than depending on a single architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the perception-centric paradigm dominant in LMM-based industrial anomaly detection (AnomalyGPT, Myriad, Triad, SAGE), which integrates visual experts to detect locally salient structural defects but struggles with logical anomalies that violate semantic rules without visible damage. It extends the RL-for-anomaly-detection line (AnomalyR1, OmniAD, EMIT, IADR1) by replacing monolithic sequence-level rewards with a rule-level credit assignment mechanism that distributes per-rule advantages to specific token segments within GRPO. The criteria-driven inspection framing also draws on rubric-based RL for fine-grained evaluation, repurposing manufacturing inspection rules as naturally occurring rubrics rather than generating them synthetically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that explicit inspection criteria are indispensable—even GPT-5.2-Chat's recall collapses to 7.92% without them—challenges the assumption that sufficiently capable models can infer domain-specific constraints from pretrained knowledge alone. The rule-level credit assignment mechanism is a general technique applicable to any multi-step verifiable reasoning task, and the FLAW dataset addresses a real data bottleneck (logical anomalies are only 4.7–8.2% of existing benchmarks). Key limitations include evaluation on a single benchmark (MVTec LOCO), a substantial recall gap versus Gemini-3.1-Pro (39.83% vs. 71.15%), and reliance on LLM-as-judge scoring metrics whose reliability, while validated, introduces an additional layer of subjectivity. Open questions include whether the criteria-driven paradigm transfers to other domains (medical, video) and whether the recall gap can be closed with larger models or richer visual grounding.
