## Problem

Existing 3D large multimodal models lag substantially behind specialist 3D perception systems on grounding and segmentation because they communicate dense geometric predictions through language tokens, proposal selection, or a single grounding token, creating a severe information bottleneck between language reasoning and spatial prediction. Additionally, frame-centric tokenization and limited context windows make it difficult to reason over long multi-view video streams where temporally distant frames may correspond to nearby 3D locations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260764/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes posed multi-view RGB-D frames (approximately 90 frames per ScanNet scene at inference) along with camera intrinsics and a natural language query. Visual features are unprojected into world-space XYZ coordinates using depth and camera poses, then voxel-pooled at 5 cm resolution to produce an unordered set of geometry-aligned tokens. Outputs include per-instance segmentation masks (converted to 3D bounding boxes via extreme-corner thresholding for grounding evaluation), free-form text answers for VQA and captioning, and per-query language grounding scores computed as dot products between object queries and language tokens.

## Method

Qwen-3D extends Qwen2.5-VL-3B with three geometry-aware components. First, multi-view visual features are lifted into a shared world coordinate system and compressed via voxel-based token merging (5 cm voxels), reducing spatial redundancy across views without learned parameters. Second, the backbone's multimodal RoPE is adapted to encode 3D world coordinates (x, y, z) alongside temporal position, enabling attention to operate directly in scene space with full (non-causal) attention over visual tokens. Third, a Mask2Former-style query-based segmentation decoder receives the full contextualized vision-language token sequence—rather than a compressed grounding token or proposal index—allowing N learnable object queries to jointly attend to visual and language tokens before predicting masks and text-span grounding scores. Training uses LoRA on the vision-language attention layers (~50 M trainable parameters), a frozen ViT backbone, and a jointly supervised loss combining Hungarian-matched mask loss, text-span grounding loss, and token-level generation loss, co-trained on 2D and 3D datasets to preserve general multimodal capabilities.

## Results

On ScanRefer 3D referential grounding, Qwen-3D-3B achieves 62.2% Acc@25 and 55.3% Acc@50, surpassing the previous single-stage LMM SOTA LLaVA-3D-7B (50.1%) by 12.1 points and the two-stage Video-3D-LLM-7B (58.1%) by 4.1 points. On 3D instance segmentation (ScanNet200), Qwen-3D reaches 25.3 mAP / 41.5 mAP25, outperforming the only prior LMM-based detector Grounded-3D-LLM (12.1 mAP) by 13.2 mAP. On out-of-distribution Locate-3D (ScanNet++), Qwen-3D achieves 55.7% Acc@25 versus 32.3% for UniVLG and 33.2% for Video-3D-LLM, demonstrating strong OOD generalization. On 2D RefCOCO grounding, Qwen-3D retains 88.1/82.7/84.0 (vs. 89.1/82.4/85.2 for the base Qwen2.5-VL-3B), confirming minimal degradation of 2D capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen-3D decisively outperforms all single-stage 3D LMMs: it beats LLaVA-3D-7B by 12.1 Acc@25 points (62.2 vs. 50.1) and 3D-LLM by over 30 points (62.2 vs. 30.3) on ScanRefer, and surpasses Grounded-3D-LLM by 13.6 points. Against two-stage methods that rely on external detectors, Qwen-3D leads Video-3D-LLM-7B by 4.1 Acc@25 (62.2 vs. 58.1) and ChatScene by 6.7 points, showing that direct mask decoding from VLM features avoids the proposal-quality bottleneck. On the expert side, Qwen-3D nearly matches UniVLG (62.2 vs. 63.5 Acc@25) and exceeds Locate-3D (61.1), substantially narrowing the gap between generalist LMMs and specialist 3D grounding models. On out-of-distribution Locate-3D (ScanNet++), Qwen-3D dominates both UniVLG (55.7 vs. 32.3 Acc@25) and Video-3D-LLM (55.7 vs. 33.2), highlighting the advantage of not depending on off-the-shelf 3D detectors for OOD robustness. For 3D instance segmentation, Qwen-3D (25.3 mAP) more than doubles Grounded-3D-LLM (12.1 mAP) and approaches the language-prompted specialist UniVLG (27.9 mAP). On 2D RefCOCO grounding, Qwen-3D (88.1/82.7/84.0) remains within ~1 point of its base Qwen2.5-VL-3B (89.1/82.4/85.2), confirming that 3D fine-tuning does not degrade 2D capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

Qwen-3D extends the Qwen2.5-VL 2D vision-language backbone with three 3D-specific mechanisms: voxel-based token compression guided by depth and camera pose, 3D Rotary Positional Embeddings for world-space attention, and a Mask2Former-style query-based segmentation decoder. It follows the positional-embedding-adaptation paradigm shared by LLaVA-3D, Video-3D-LLM, and UniVLG, but departs sharply in its decoding strategy: rather than communicating grounding through autoregressive text tokens, proposal selection, or a single <REF> token, it routes the full contextualized vision-language feature set directly into a dense mask decoder. This positions the paper as a response to the observation that prior 3D LMMs, despite geometry-aware representations, still lag specialist 3D detectors by large margins on grounding and segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that the decoding interface, not the representation, is the primary bottleneck preventing 3D LMMs from matching specialist perception systems—is well-supported by ablations and has clear implications for how future 3D multimodal models should be architected. The practical impact is significant: a 3B model outperforms 7B competitors on 3D grounding and approaches specialist-level segmentation, suggesting that architectural choices can substitute for raw model scale. Key open questions remain: the model assumes static scenes and relies on externally estimated depth/pose, it handles only single-step grounding (no compositional or multi-entity reasoning), and whether the direct-decoder coupling generalizes to dynamic 4D environments or to settings without reliable geometric priors.
