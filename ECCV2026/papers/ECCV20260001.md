## Problem

Existing multi-view diffusion models for novel view synthesis operate in view-independent latent spaces (e.g., 2D VAEs or DINO features) that lack cross-view geometric structure, forcing the model to implicitly discover geometric correspondences or rely on external depth-based warping. The design of latent spaces specifically suited for geometry-aware generation—where geometric consistency across viewpoints is a first-class requirement—remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260001/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N source RGB images (1–4 views) with known camera poses and M target camera poses, operating at variable resolutions (504×504, 504×378, 504×336, 504×280) matched to DA3's training resolutions. Beyond the target RGB views, the framework produces zero-shot depth maps, ray maps, and 3D point clouds as a direct byproduct of decoding the synthesized geometric features through the pretrained DPT decoder, requiring no additional training or fine-tuning for geometry output.

## Method

GLD repurposes the multi-level feature space of Depth Anything 3 (DA3) as the diffusion latent space, replacing the conventional VAE. A three-stage pipeline is employed: (1) a ViT-based RGB decoder is trained to reconstruct images from DA3's four feature levels, validating reconstruction fidelity; (2) an optimal boundary layer k=1 is identified through ablation, where features at levels 0–1 are explicitly synthesized by diffusion models while deeper levels (2–3) are deterministically derived by propagating the boundary feature through the frozen DA3 encoder; (3) a cascaded model generates level-0 features conditioned on the synthesized level-1 latent to ensure cross-level alignment. The diffusion architecture is a DiT^DH with 3D self-attention, PRoPE positional encoding, and Plücker ray embeddings, trained with a flow-matching objective from scratch (no T2I pretraining) for 175k iterations, achieving 4.4× faster convergence than the VAE baseline while outperforming it on both 2D image quality and 3D consistency metrics.

## Results

On in-domain benchmarks, GLD achieves PSNR 16.36 / SSIM 0.630 / LPIPS 0.431 on Re10K and PSNR 15.50 / SSIM 0.468 / LPIPS 0.438 on DL3DV, outperforming the VAE and DINOv2 latent-space baselines on every 2D metric. On 3D consistency, GLD attains ATE 0.211 (Re10K) and 0.209 (DL3DV), up to 2.8× lower ATE and 2.6× lower RPE_r than the VAE baseline. On the out-of-domain Mip-NeRF 360 benchmark, GLD still leads in PSNR (14.54) and LPIPS (0.504) among all methods, while remaining competitive on 3D metrics. Training converges 4.4× faster than the VAE latent space, and zero-shot depth decoded from synthesized latents achieves AbsRel 0.160 on ETH3D, surpassing Matrix3D (0.197).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the VAE and DINOv2 latent-space baselines (both trained from scratch with identical architecture), GLD wins on all three 2D metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and on ATE, RPE_r, RPE_t, and Reproj across all three benchmarks; the ATE gap reaches 2.8× on Re10K (0.211 vs 0.278 for VAE, 0.345 for DINOv2). Against fine-tuned SOTA NVS methods, GLD surpasses MVGenMaster, Matrix3D, CAMEO, NVComposer, and CAT3D† on every 2D metric on both in-domain benchmarks (e.g., PSNR 16.36 vs 15.23 for MVGenMaster on Re10K), despite being trained from scratch on smaller datasets without T2I pretraining. On the out-of-domain Mip-NeRF 360 benchmark, GLD leads in PSNR (14.54) and LPIPS (0.504) but trails MVGenMaster (ATE 0.320 vs 0.589) and Matrix3D (ATE 0.548) on pose accuracy, which the authors attribute to those methods' additional object-centric fine-tuning data. For zero-shot depth on ETH3D, GLD achieves AbsRel 0.160 and δ₁ 0.800, outperforming Matrix3D (0.197 / 0.731). The cascaded generation design (Table 8) further improves over independent per-level synthesis by +0.19 PSNR and −0.015 ATE on Re10K with N=4 source views.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Representation Autoencoder (RAE) paradigm—which showed that frozen semantic encoders like DINOv2 outperform VAEs as diffusion latent spaces for single-image generation—to the geometry-aware multi-view setting. Rather than adopting a general-purpose visual encoder, it repurposes the multi-level feature hierarchy of a geometric foundation model (Depth Anything 3) as the latent space, leveraging the cross-view geometric correspondences already encoded by 3D attention. Architecturally, it borrows the DiT^DH backbone from RAE, 3D self-attention from CAT3D, and PRoPE camera conditioning, but introduces a cascaded multi-level generation scheme and a boundary-layer selection strategy specific to the hierarchical structure of geometric foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that latent space design is a first-order design choice for NVS, not just single-image generation, and that geometry-informed latents yield both faster convergence (4.4×) and substantially better 3D consistency than VAE or DINOv2 spaces. A notable practical benefit is that synthesized latents decode zero-shot into depth maps and 3D point clouds via the pretrained geometric decoder, eliminating a separate geometry estimation step. Limitations include reliance on scene-centric training data (out-of-domain pose accuracy still trails T2I-pretrained methods) and the need to train the diffusion model from scratch, which constrains scalability to diverse object categories. Open questions include whether the boundary-layer strategy generalizes to other geometric foundation models with different depth hierarchies and whether the approach extends to video-length sequences.
