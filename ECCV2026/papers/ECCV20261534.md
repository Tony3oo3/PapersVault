## Problem

Existing prototype-based federated learning (PBFL) methods construct global prototypes via weighted averaging of local prototypes without any explicit optimization for inter-class separation. Under non-IID data distributions and heterogeneous model architectures, this produces poorly separated global prototypes that cluster toward majority-sample clients, limiting discriminative capacity. No prior method explicitly optimizes the geometric configuration of global prototypes in the feature space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261534/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Clients process RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet-1K, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) or text documents (AG News, DBpedia) through heterogeneous architectures (ResNet-8, EfficientNet, ShuffleNet v2, MobileNet v2, GoogleNet, ResNet-18–152, FastText, TextCNN) with a shared feature dimension of 512. Only per-class mean activations from the penultimate layer (prototypes) are exchanged between server and clients—no model parameters or logits are transmitted. Classification at inference is performed by nearest-prototype lookup: the predicted label is the class whose local prototype is closest in Euclidean distance to the input's feature embedding.

## Method

ProtoNorm reframes global prototype separability as a Thomson problem: K class prototypes are treated as charged particles on a unit hypersphere, and the server minimizes a surrogate hyperspherical energy E_log = Σ_{j<k} log(1/‖ĉ_j − ĉ_k‖) via gradient descent with momentum, iteratively pushing prototypes toward maximum angular separation. After convergence, prototypes are upscaled by a factor γ to amplify Euclidean distances and sharpen decision boundaries during client training. The entire alignment and upscaling pipeline runs exclusively on the server side with O(K²) complexity independent of client count, imposing zero additional computation on clients. Clients train with a combined cross-entropy and prototype-distance regularization loss, pulling their local prototypes toward the upscaled global prototypes. A key ablation finding is that angular alignment alone (γ=1) can degrade performance due to suboptimal local minima; the joint optimization of angular distribution and magnitude is essential.

## Results

Under the pathological heterogeneity setting, ProtoNorm achieves 89.07% on CIFAR-10, 64.24% on CIFAR-100, 71.35% on Flowers-102, and 40.24% on Tiny ImageNet, outperforming the strongest baseline (FedTGP or FedKD) by 1.1–6.5 percentage points. Gains are most pronounced on many-class tasks: +4.6 pp on CIFAR-100 and +5.6 pp on Flowers-102 over FedTGP. On ImageNet-1K (1,000 classes), ProtoNorm reaches 21.52% top-1 accuracy versus 14.55% (FedProto) and 13.62% (FedTGP). On text classification, ProtoNorm attains 97.46% on AG News and 97.15% on DBpedia, marginally surpassing FedTGP (97.26% and 93.62% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProtoNorm outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity setting tested. Against the closest PBFL competitor FedTGP, ProtoNorm gains +4.6 pp on CIFAR-100 (64.24 vs 59.62) and +5.6 pp on Flowers-102 (71.35 vs 65.76) under pathological settings, with the gap narrowing to +2.3 pp and +3.4 pp under practical (α=0.1) settings. On CIFAR-10, the margin over the best baseline (FedKD, 87.00%) is a more modest +2.07 pp, reflecting that fewer classes require less geometric separation. On the 1,000-class ImageNet-1K benchmark, ProtoNorm (21.52%) substantially exceeds both FedProto (14.55%) and FedTGP (13.62%), confirming that the Thomson-problem-inspired alignment scales with class count. Against homogeneous hyperspherical baselines FedNH and FedUV (evaluated with ResNet-18), ProtoNorm leads by 4.3–12.0 pp across CIFAR-100, Flowers-102, and Tiny ImageNet. On text datasets, ProtoNorm edges out FedTGP by 0.20 pp on AG News (97.46 vs 97.26) and by 3.53 pp on DBpedia (97.15 vs 93.62), while FedProto trails by 3.4–13.1 pp.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtoNorm extends the prototype-based federated learning (PBFL) lineage initiated by FedProto, which exchanges per-class mean activations rather than model parameters to support architectural heterogeneity. Where FedTGP and FedFTP improve prototype separability through contrastive learning over local prototypes, ProtoNorm departs by reframing global prototype arrangement as a pure geometric optimization problem—specifically, the Thomson problem of minimizing hyperspherical energy—solved entirely on the server side. This draws on the MHE line of work (Liu et al., 2018) that regularizes neural networks via hyperspherical energy minimization, but applies it to a distinct object (global prototypes rather than neuron weights) and in a distinct location (server-side post-aggregation rather than client-side during training). The additional magnitude upscaling step (γ) couples this geometric arrangement to the local training dynamics in a way not present in prior hyperspherical FL methods like FedNH or FedUV.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core finding that pure geometric optimization of prototype positions—without any semantic or contrastive signal—can outperform contrastive-learning-based separation is a useful conceptual result for the HtFL community, suggesting that the arrangement geometry of prototypes may be a more fundamental lever than their semantic content. However, the scaling factor γ remains a heuristic hyperparameter requiring per-dataset grid search, and no principled rule (e.g., based on class count or feature dimension) is provided, limiting practical deployment. The ablation revealing that angular alignment alone without upscaling can *degrade* performance relative to no alignment at all highlights a non-trivial interaction between server-side geometry and client-side optimization that warrants further theoretical understanding. Open questions include whether the Thomson-problem framing generalizes to non-classification tasks or to settings with a growing/dynamic class set.
