## Problem

Existing hallucination mitigation strategies for MLLMs—self-refinement and contrastive decoding—rely on multi-round iterations or high-performance auxiliary models, introducing significant latency that limits real-time deployment. Moreover, prior analyses focused only on image tokens during the prefill stage, leaving unexplored whether hallucination-inducing redundancies also propagate into the Value Caches during autoregressive decoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262563/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-in on frozen MLLMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B, MiniGPT-4, Qwen2.5-VL), taking a standard RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., 'Please describe this image in detail') and producing a hallucination-reduced text description. Only the lightweight In-Mod and Tn-Mod modules are trained; all backbone parameters remain frozen. During decoding, the masking is applied dynamically per generated token, so the output is a standard autoregressive text sequence with no change in format or length constraints.

## Method

HalMask introduces two cross-modal Noise Modulation Modules (In-Mod and Tn-Mod) that use attention to fuse image tokens with ground-truth or hallucination text tokens, then pass the fused representation through a Fusion-Net to produce per-token, per-dimension control coefficients in [0,1]. These coefficients govern Gaussian noise injection: z = λ·x + (1−λ)·ε, selectively masking redundant features while preserving salient ones. Training employs a dynamical regularization term built on the overdamped Langevin equation (discretized via Euler–Maruyama) combined with a Sliced Wasserstein Distance loss, encouraging the modulation to learn dynamics that drive features toward a standard normal distribution rather than imposing a rigid Gaussian prior. At inference, the prefill stage masks image tokens directly, while the decoding stage applies a dynamic Value Cache masking mechanism: each newly generated answer token is aggregated with prior tokens, re-processed through both modulation modules, and the resulting coefficients update the image and text Value Caches across all LLM decoder layers—without accumulating noise, since the cache stores unmasked representations and a fresh λ is assigned per token.

## Results

On the CHAIR benchmark (MSCOCO, 64-token max length), HalMask reduces CHAIR_S from 21.20 to 7.60 (64.15% relative reduction) and CHAIR_I from 6.84 to 3.06 on LLaVA-1.5, while simultaneously improving BLEU from 16.25 to 19.48. On MiniGPT-4, CHAIR_S drops from 32.40 to 16.20 and CHAIR_I from 12.20 to 5.96. On POPE, HalMask achieves an average F1 of 87.52, outperforming the best baseline (HACL at 87.26) and the greedy baseline (85.88). Inference speed is 0.034 s/token, making it 8.3× faster than HALC (0.283 s/token) while delivering a 10.8% higher POPE average F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HalMask outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On CHAIR (LLaVA-1.5), the closest competitor is Nullu (CHAIR_S=15.20), which HalMask nearly halves to 7.60; Woodpecker actually worsens hallucination relative to greedy (23.85 vs. 21.20), while LURE, OPERA, HALC, HACL, and DeGF all achieve only modest reductions (16.90–19.48). On POPE, HalMask (avg F1=87.52) edges out HACL (87.26) and clearly surpasses SID (85.92), DeGF (85.65), and Nullu (85.24). On OPOPE, HalMask achieves the highest F0.2 across Random, Popular, and Adversarial splits for both LLaVA-1.5 and MiniGPT-4. Critically, HalMask is 8.3× faster than HALC (0.034 vs. 0.283 s/token) and 4× faster than OPERA/SID, while simultaneously delivering the best hallucination metrics—making it the only method that improves both speed and accuracy over the greedy baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hallucination-mitigation line of work that has focused on self-refinement (Woodpecker, OPERA) and contrastive decoding (HALC, SID, DeGF), which the authors identify as latency-heavy due to multi-round iterations or auxiliary model dependencies. Conceptually, it builds on prior observations that image tokens in the prefill stage harbor hallucination-inducing redundancies (Bai et al., Che et al., Zhuang et al.) but departs by extending the analysis to the full inference pipeline, specifically revealing that such redundancies persist in the Value Cache during autoregressive decoding. The regularization mechanism repurposes the overdamped Langevin equation from stochastic differential equations (Fathi & Stoltz, 2017) as a dynamical constraint on feature distributions, and the training data construction follows Zhou et al.'s (2023) hallucination-GT triplet protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving an 8.3× speedup over contrastive decoding baselines (HALC) while simultaneously improving POPE F1 by 10.8%, making real-time hallucination suppression feasible for deployed MLLM systems. The empirical finding that hallucination-inducing features propagate into the Answer Value Cache during decoding—beyond the previously studied prefill-stage image tokens—opens a new axis for understanding and intervening on hallucination mechanisms. Limitations include evaluation restricted to 7B-scale models, the requirement to train the modulation modules (precluding a fully training-free deployment), and the somewhat heuristic nature of selecting which features to mask via cosine similarity thresholds. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger models, video or audio modalities, and whether the Langevin-based regularization can be replaced by simpler objectives without performance loss.
