## Problem

Driving scene simulators face a fundamental out-of-distribution gap when users edit actor trajectories to synthesize safety-critical corner cases (in-place spinning, rollovers, head-on collisions) that are rare or absent in recorded driving data. Because no ground-truth video exists for any edited configuration, standard supervised fine-tuning cannot close this gap, and vehicle quality degrades most severely under these OOD conditions, making it the primary bottleneck for realistic simulation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-frame LiDAR point clouds (200 frames for static background, ±2 frames per dynamic actor), Waymo 3D bounding box annotations, a reference image from the original capture, and user-edited actor trajectories. These are aggregated into a dynamic 3D point cloud, completed for vehicles, and rendered into 2D pseudo-images (with black regions where LiDAR provides no coverage) at 480×832 resolution. The output is a 49-frame photorealistic driving video, with the reference image anchoring appearance consistency across edits and a fixed text prompt ('A realistic autonomous driving scene') guiding the diffusion model.

## Method

ReinDriveGen combines a Point Cloud-Conditioned Video Diffusion Simulator with an RL-based post-training framework. The simulator aggregates category-aware multi-frame LiDAR into a dynamic 3D scene, applies AdaPoinTr to reconstruct full 360° vehicle geometry from partial observations, and renders edited trajectories into 2D pseudo-images that condition the VACE-1.3B video diffusion model (fine-tuned with a Context Adapter and DiT). For RL post-training, the paper extends DiffusionNFT from image to video generation, using a flow-matching contrastive objective where reward-weighted positive and negative velocity predictions steer the policy. The key novelty is a dedicated pairwise preference model (shared DINOv3 ViT-H+ backbone with an antisymmetric sigmoid formulation) trained on preference pairs constructed by progressively subsampling pseudo-image conditioning to degrade vehicle quality, combined with a pairwise reward mechanism that computes per-candidate win rates across all C(N,2) comparisons within a group of 16 candidates, weighted by bounding-box area, providing robust relative rewards that avoid the reward-hacking pitfalls of pointwise scoring.

## Results

On the novel ego-vehicle trajectory benchmark (4 m cumulative lateral shift over 40 frames), ReinDriveGen achieves the best NTA-IoU of 0.549 (vs. 0.495 for the best baseline DD4D+S3Gaussian), NTL-IoU of 56.13 (vs. 53.78 for Recon.+Deform.-GS), and FID of 51.99 (vs. 66.93 for DD4D+S3Gaussian), outperforming all reconstruction-based and hybrid baselines. On the vehicle trajectory editing task (5 m lateral shift), the method achieves the best FID (80.63 vs. 84.80 for Street Gaussians and 83.63 for StreetCrafter), image quality (0.693 vs. 0.640 and 0.651), and background consistency (0.940 vs. 0.931 and 0.907), though it trails slightly on motion smoothness (0.977 vs. 0.981 and 0.982).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReinDriveGen outperforms all reconstruction-based and hybrid baselines on the novel ego-vehicle trajectory task: it leads NTA-IoU by 0.054 over the strongest baseline (DD4D+S3Gaussian, 0.495) and FID by 14.94 points (51.99 vs. 66.93), indicating substantially better geometric consistency and photorealism under a 4 m lateral shift. On the vehicle trajectory editing task (5 m lateral shift), it beats both Street Gaussians and StreetCrafter on FID (80.63 vs. 84.80/83.63), image quality (0.693 vs. 0.640/0.651), and background consistency (0.940 vs. 0.931/0.907), though it trails by ~0.004 on motion smoothness. Qualitative comparisons against diffusion-based simulators (UniMLVG, DriveDreamer) show that these methods produce severely distorted vehicles under in-place spinning and fail to generate physically plausible rollovers, whereas ReinDriveGen maintains realistic geometry and temporal coherence. FreeVS is included only qualitatively since it generates partial novel views. The ablation on vehicle completion confirms that without AdaPoinTr-based completion, repositioned vehicles exhibit large holes and severe texture artifacts, while the RL post-training ablation shows progressive improvement in vehicle geometry, texture fidelity, and lighting across 500 optimization steps on OOD scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReinDriveGen sits at the intersection of two lines of work: reconstruction-based driving simulators (NeRF, 3DGS, Street Gaussians, DriveDreamer4D) that provide explicit 3D structure but cannot handle trajectory editing, and diffusion-based video generators (VACE, Vista, Cosmos) that produce photorealistic output but lack geometric controllability. The paper departs from both by using dynamic LiDAR point clouds (with AdaPoinTr completion) as an editable 3D backbone that renders 2D pseudo-image conditions for a VACE-1.3B video diffusion model, and then introduces RL post-training via DiffusionNFT—extended here from image to video—with a novel pairwise reward mechanism to close the OOD gap that supervised fine-tuning alone cannot. The pseudo-view simulation strategy is borrowed from GA-Drive, and the overall architecture is a deliberate hybrid that no prior method has combined in this specific way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental and practically critical gap: safety-critical corner cases (spinning, rollovers, head-on collisions) are inherently absent from driving logs, so no amount of data collection can close the distribution gap. The RL post-training paradigm with pairwise rewards offers a principled, supervision-free path to improve generation quality in these regimes, and the authors explicitly argue the same approach could transfer to other domains where test-time conditions exceed training coverage. Key limitations include the 49-frame clip length (~1 min generation), the need for 20 curated OOD conditions, and the reliance on a single video diffusion backbone (VACE-1.3B), leaving open whether the RL strategy generalizes across architectures or scales to longer, multi-scene sequences. The pairwise reward mechanism itself—replacing pointwise Bradley-Terry scores with win-rate aggregation—is a modest but useful contribution to the broader RL-for-diffusion literature.
