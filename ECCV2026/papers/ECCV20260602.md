## Problem

Text-to-video diffusion models still fail at fine-grained compositional alignment—attribute binding, spatial relations, and object interactions—when prompts require precise coordination among multiple semantic concepts. Existing reward-based fine-tuning bakes preferences into model parameters, making adaptation to new prompts expensive and leaving the system vulnerable to reward hacking. The paper asks whether compositional alignment can be improved at test time through differentiable noise optimization without modifying the underlying generator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260602/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a pretrained Video Consistency Model (VCM) backbone—AnimateLCM (512×512), T2V-Turbo MS (256×256), or T2V-Turbo VC2 (512×320)—and produces a generated video after 25 iterations of gradient-based noise refinement on top of a 4-step denoising sampler. The optimization variables are the initial latent noise and all intermediate Gaussian perturbations injected during the VCM's short denoising chain, with peak memory around 23–41 GB and runtime of 45–178 seconds per video on a single RTX 6000 Ada GPU.

## Method

NoisEasier formulates test-time refinement as a multi-objective optimization over all stochastic variables in a VCM's short denoising trajectory, rather than only the initial latent. The key insight is that jointly optimizing intermediate perturbations exposes finer control over the denoising process, accelerating reward convergence and improving compositional alignment with less than 1% additional runtime. A robust reward formulation combines HPSv2 and ImageReward for image-text alignment, ViCLIP for video-text temporal consistency, and a RAFT-based motion reward that encourages localized dynamics while penalizing abrupt flow changes. A negative-aware calibration step contrasts the target prompt against LLM-generated hard negative prompts, making the reward signal more sensitive to subtle compositional errors like attribute binding and numeracy. The entire pipeline requires no model fine-tuning—only gradient ascent on the noise variables with AdamW over 25 steps.

## Results

On VBench, NoisEasier applied to T2V-Turbo(MS) achieves 32.05% Overall Consistency (up from 27.40%), 76.62% Multiple Objects (up from 54.50%), and 94.38% Color (up from 89.90%), surpassing all open-source baselines and even exceeding closed-source Veo 3 (27.88%) on Overall Consistency. On T2V-CompBench, T2V-Turbo(VC2)+NoisEasier reaches 0.8737 Consistent Attribute Binding, 0.7524 Action, and 0.7915 Interaction, setting new open-source bests across these compositional dimensions. The paper reports over 10% average gains on challenging dimensions including attribute binding, object interaction, and numeracy, with full-trajectory noise optimization outperforming initial-noise-only optimization by up to 7.46% on Spatial Relation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoisEasier consistently outperforms all open-source baselines on VBench: T2V-Turbo(MS)+NoisEasier (32.05% Overall) surpasses CogVideoX-2B (27.33%), Vchitect-2.0 (27.57%), and VideoCrafter2 (28.23%), and even exceeds closed-source Veo 3 (27.88%) on Overall Consistency. On T2V-CompBench, T2V-Turbo(VC2)+NoisEasier achieves the best open-source scores on Consistent Attribute Binding (0.8737 vs. 0.7852 for its own backbone), Action (0.7524 vs. 0.6241), Interaction (0.7915 vs. 0.7017), and Numeracy (0.3753 vs. 0.3719 for Open-Sora 1.2). However, it remains behind closed-source PixVerse on several T2V-CompBench dimensions (e.g., Action 0.7524 vs. 0.8722, Numeracy 0.3753 vs. 0.6066) and behind Veo 3 on VBench Spatial Relation (52.61% vs. 84.26%) and Multiple Objects (79.18% vs. 82.20%). Against its own VCM backbones, NoisEasier yields the largest gains on compositional metrics (e.g., +22.12% Multiple Objects on T2V-Turbo(MS), +12.66% Spatial Relation on T2V-Turbo(VC2)) while Motion Smoothness drops marginally (−0.22% to −0.19%). In the human evaluation, NoisEasier achieves a 61.4% win rate over its backbone and ~61% preference over CogVideoX-2B and Wan2.1-1.3B on semantic alignment, confirming practical quality improvements beyond automated metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) Video Consistency Models (T2V-Turbo, AnimateLCM) that distill long diffusion chains into 4–8 denoising steps, and (2) test-time noise optimization for diffusion models (DOODL, ReNO, InitNo), which has been applied to images, music, and motion but not to video. The key conceptual departure is that prior noise-optimization work treats only the initial latent as the optimization variable, whereas NoisEasier exploits the stochastic perturbations injected at each VCM step as additional free variables, jointly optimizing the full trajectory. It also departs from reward-based fine-tuning (RLHF, DPO, RFT) by performing all reward-guided refinement at inference time without touching model parameters, and introduces a negative-aware calibration mechanism to make VLM-based rewards more discriminative for compositional errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NoisEasier establishes test-time scaling as a practical, plug-and-play paradigm for T2V alignment, showing that a 25-step gradient loop on a 4-step VCM backbone yields 10–25 point gains on compositional dimensions at under 2 minutes of extra compute. The finding that NoisEasier still improves a reward-fine-tuned model (RFT + NoisEasier > RFT alone) suggests that offline alignment does not fully amortize reward signals, opening the door to combining offline and test-time strategies. However, the method is bounded by the quality of its VLM-based rewards and currently fails on temporal state transitions and precise object counting, pointing to a need for structured, temporally-aware reward signals (e.g., object detection, visual grounding) as a clear open direction.
