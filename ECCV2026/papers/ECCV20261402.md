## Problem

Clinical prostate MRI reports typically provide only sparse 2D measurements (a diameter, box, or single contour) rather than full 3D lesion masks, yet accurate volumetric delineation is needed for targeted biopsy and therapy planning. Existing promptable segmentation models (SAM, SAM 2, MedSAM2) are not designed for the combination of multimodal MRI fusion, heterogeneous sparse clinical prompts, and anisotropic through-plane spacing that characterizes biparametric prostate MRI, leading to over-propagation and leakage into lesion-absent slices.

## Input / Output

The method takes a co-registered multimodal bpMRI volume (T2w, ADC, high-b DWI; optionally a predicted gland prior) and a single-slice clinical prompt in one of four formats: two diameter endpoints, an axis-aligned bounding box, a circumscribed circle, or a full 2D contour. It produces a coherent 3D volumetric lesion mask via bidirectional propagation along axial slices, with the prompted-slice 2D mask as an intermediate output. Volumes are anisotropic with through-plane spacing ranging from ~1.5 mm to ~6.7 mm, and the method handles missing or corrupted modalities through stochastic modality dropout during training.

## Method

SAPP is a two-stage pipeline built on SAM 2: Stage I performs prompted-slice segmentation using SAM 2 in image mode with an Adaptive Multimodal Fusion (AMF) module that encodes each modality into patch tokens, enriches them via multi-head cross-attention against a global context token, and applies learned channel-wise gating to produce a fused 3-channel feature map fed through SAM 2's frozen patch embedding. Stage II lifts the 2D mask to 3D via SAM 2's streaming-memory video propagation, augmented by a spacing-aware module that modulates memory cross-attention logits with a Gaussian decay over physical inter-slice distance (σ_z = 3.3 mm), a confidence gate combining predicted IoU and objectness, and a learned slice-level stop classifier that terminates propagation when lesion content is absent. Training uses LoRA adapters on the frozen SAM 2 encoder with Dice+BCE on positive slices, BCE on the stop gate, and a soft-gated foreground penalty on negative slices, plus structured prompt noise (endpoint jitter, box slack, mask dilation/erosion) and modality dropout for robustness.

## Results

On the prompted-slice (image-level) task across 11 held-out cohorts, SAPP achieves 0.86/0.82 mean DSC under box/diameter prompts, outperforming the strongest specialist baseline PCaSAM (0.83/0.79) and MedSAM (0.72/0.68). On the full prompt-to-volume (3D) task, SAPP reaches 0.87/0.80/0.78 mean DSC under oracle-mask/box/diameter prompts, improving over the strongest volume-level baseline MedSAM2 by +13/+17/+18 points respectively. SAPP also runs fastest (4.5 s, 8.9 GB peak GPU) among all compared methods, thanks to early termination via the stop gate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAPP outperforms all nine baselines on both image-level and volume-level tasks. At the image level, the closest competitor is PCaSAM (0.83/0.79 DSC for box/diameter), which SAPP surpasses by +3/+3 points; MedSAM trails by 10–14 points, and vanilla SAM by 25–29 points. At the volume level, MedSAM2 is the strongest baseline (0.74/0.63/0.60 DSC for oracle/box/diameter), and SAPP leads by +13/+17/+18 points; Medical SAM2 and SAM 2 fall further behind (0.72/0.61/0.58 and 0.67/0.55/0.53 respectively). The unprompted nnU-Net reference achieves only 0.42–0.50 DSC, roughly half of SAPP's prompted results, underscoring the value of even minimal clinical input. Gains widen under weaker prompts (diameter vs. oracle), indicating that spacing-aware propagation and the stop gate are most beneficial when the initial 2D mask is imprecise. On routine-care weak-label studies (516 scans, 3 sites), SAPP achieves 0.83 DSC with site-provided diameter prompts, +5 points over PCaSAM and +14 over MedSAM.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAPP extends SAM 2's streaming-memory propagation paradigm from natural video to anisotropic multimodal MRI, addressing two gaps that prior medical adaptations (MedSAM, MedSAM2, Medical SAM 2) leave open: (1) multimodal bpMRI violates SAM 2's 3-channel RGB assumption, and (2) through-plane slice spacing varies across protocols, breaking the uniform-neighborhood assumption of video-style propagation. The adaptive multimodal fusion module (cross-attention over modality tokens with a shared global context) departs from task-specific fusion architectures (HEMIS, ModDrop++, MMFormer) by producing SAM 2-compatible patch tokens under prompt conditioning, while the spacing-decayed memory attention and learned stop gate have no direct analogue in prior promptable 3D segmentation work (PAM, Sli2Vol+, SegVol, VISTA3D), which propagate over slice index without accounting for physical distance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By converting a single routine clinical measurement (diameter, box, or contour) into a coherent 3D lesion mask, SAPP directly addresses the annotation bottleneck in prostate MRI and could enable scalable generation of volumetric supervision for downstream PI-RADS scoring and risk-stratification models. The spacing-aware propagation concept—modulating memory attention by physical inter-slice distance and learning when to stop—is a generalizable principle for any anisotropic volumetric modality, not just prostate MRI. Key limitations include a global (non-adaptive) spacing decay parameter, a fixed stopping threshold, the assumption of rigidly aligned multimodal inputs, and retrospective-only evaluation; extending toward unprompted lesion detection and prospective multi-site validation are natural next steps.
