## Problem

Current hallucination detection in Vision-Language Models treats generation as a monolithic, indivisible event, conflating fundamentally different failure modes such as perceptual drift (hallucinating objects) and inferential bypass (contradicting one's own evidence). No existing framework provides a stage-by-stage, mechanistic diagnosis of where and why a VLM's cognitive process breaks down, limiting both interpretability and targeted mitigation in high-stakes deployments.

## Input / Output

For structured VQA, the method consumes an RGB image paired with a text query and produces a scalar hallucination anomaly score (S_hall) alongside three interpretable diagnostic metrics: Perceptual Entropy, Inferential Conflict, and Decision Entropy. For open-ended captioning, only the image is required, and the Perceptual Entropy probe operates standalone. A key operational detail is that computing Inferential Conflict requires a second, non-autoregressive forward pass with the visual input ablated (a causal intervention), making the total cost one generation pass plus one replay rather than the 10× sampling cost of semantic-entropy baselines.

## Method

The framework formalizes VLM generation as a Markovian information flow (Image → Evidence → Answer) grounded in a normative principle of computational rationality: the final answer should be conditionally independent of the image given the stated evidence, i.e., I(A; I | T_evi) = 0. Three information-theoretic probes—Perceptual Entropy (token-level Shannon entropy over a factual/uncertain semantic axis), Inferential Conflict (Conditional Pointwise Mutual Information estimated via a visual-ablation causal intervention), and Decision Entropy (binary entropy over the final answer logits)—serve as coordinates projecting each generation onto a 3D Cognitive State Space. A Gaussian Mixture Model is calibrated on a weakly supervised set (only correct final answers required, with a coherence filter to remove lucky guesses) to learn the density of nominal cognitive states. Hallucination detection then reduces to geometric anomaly detection: the hallucination score is the negative log-likelihood (surprisal) of the observed state vector under the fitted GMM, operationalizing the paper's central geometric-information duality that geometric deviation from the nominal manifold is equivalent to high information-theoretic surprisal.

## Results

On the POPE adversarial benchmark, the proposed Cognitive Anomaly Detection (CAD) framework achieves an average AUC of 0.858 across four VLMs, with peak performance of 0.947 on Idefics2 and 0.910 on Llava-v1.6. On the more diverse MME benchmark, CAD maintains strong generalization with an average AUC of 0.796, reaching 0.851 on Llava-v1.6. The framework operates at 1× inference cost (single generation pass plus one non-autoregressive replay) and requires only weak supervision (ground-truth final answers), while remaining robust to up to 30% calibration contamination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAD outperforms the Supervised Probe (Chen et al., ICLR 2024) by a substantial margin: 0.858 vs. 0.791 average AUC on POPE (+6.7 points), and 0.796 vs. 0.751 on MME. Notably, the Supervised Probe requires balanced hallucination labels for training, whereas CAD needs only ground-truth final answers. Against Semantic Entropy (Farquhar et al., Nature 2024), CAD achieves 0.858 vs. 0.709 average AUC on POPE (+14.9 points) while being 10× cheaper in inference cost (1× vs. 10× sampling). On MME, the gap narrows slightly (0.796 vs. 0.668), but CAD still leads by over 12 points. The advantage is most pronounced in the critical low-FPR regime (FPR < 10⁻²), where CAD maintains high true positive rates while baselines degrade sharply, as shown in the log-log ROC curves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of treating VLM hallucination as a static output error (CHAIR, POPE) or a monolithic internal-state signal (INSIDE, VADE) by reframing it as a dynamic, multi-stage process anomaly. It synthesizes information-theoretic tools (conditional pointwise mutual information, Shannon entropy) with geometric anomaly detection (GMM-based density modeling) in a way that prior hallucination-detection work has not attempted, projecting the generative trajectory into an interpretable 3D Cognitive State Space. The normative abstraction of computational rationality (I(A;I|T_evi)=0) borrows from causal inference and d-separation but is applied here as a diagnostic axiom for VLMs rather than a generative assumption, and the CoT evidence chain is explicitly positioned as an observable probe rather than a faithful reconstruction of latent reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's ability to perform stage-by-stage differential diagnosis (perceptual vs. inferential vs. decisional failure) offers a practical auditing tool for high-stakes VLM deployment, and its weak-supervision requirement (only ground-truth answers, no token-level hallucination labels) makes it far more scalable than supervised alternatives. However, the 'Entangled States' finding for Qwen2-VL and DeepSeek-VL—where hallucinatory and nominal cognitive states are geometrically indistinguishable—reveals a fundamental ceiling: for some architectures, hallucination is a content error arising from a seemingly normal process, which no amount of process-level probing can resolve. Open questions include whether this entanglement is architecture-specific or a general property of sufficiently large VLMs, and whether richer state spaces (beyond 3D) or training-time interventions could disentangle these cases.
