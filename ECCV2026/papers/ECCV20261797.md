## Problem

Existing VLM self-training methods (e.g., STaR, R3V) filter generated rationales solely by final-answer correctness, which allows visually hallucinated descriptions and language shortcuts to persist in the training set. Because perception is never verified independently from reasoning, the model can learn to produce correct answers through textual priors rather than genuine visual grounding, degrading multimodal reasoning quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261797/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image paired with a multiple-choice text question (from M3CoT's four knowledge domains) and produces a structured [CAP-REAS-CONCL] tuple: a free-form image caption, a step-by-step reasoning chain, and a final answer letter. The caption is not free-form at inference time but is validated by PerceptEval, which jointly checks FG-CLIP image-caption similarity and PaddleOCR text-agreement with domain-adaptive thresholds derived from the image's text-area coverage ratio. Only samples passing both caption verification and answer correctness are retained for fine-tuning.

## Method

The framework enforces visually grounded reasoning through a structured caption-reasoning-conclusion prompt that disentangles perception from reasoning, making each component independently verifiable. PerceptEval, an unsupervised module, scores each generated caption on two axes—FG-CLIP visual alignment and Sentence-Transformer cosine similarity against an OCR-derived auxiliary caption—with thresholds adaptively adjusted by the image's text-area coverage ratio to handle both text-heavy and visually rich images. Samples are partitioned into easy (correct caption + correct answer), medium (correct caption + wrong answer), and hard (incorrect caption) subsets. A two-stage curriculum then trains the model first on easy samples (Stage 1) and subsequently incorporates medium samples via caption-guided reasoning enhancement, where the verified caption is injected as auxiliary context to regenerate the reasoning chain; only regenerated samples yielding the correct conclusion are retained. Both stages fine-tune from the original base model using LoRA, ensuring all supervision is perceptually grounded and eliminating hallucinated or shortcut-based rationales from the training signal.

## Results

On LLaVA-v1.5-7B across four M3CoT domains, the proposed method achieves 64.93% (Language Science), 72.74% (Commonsense), 46.56% (Social Science), and 58.24% (Natural Science) accuracy, outperforming the strongest self-training baselines STaR and R3V by up to 16.1 percentage points (Language Science vs. STaR). On the OOD MMMU Science validation split, the method reaches 36.48% accuracy versus 33.78% for R3V and 32.43% for STaR. On the HallusionBench OCR split, the method achieves 60.14% accuracy, surpassing R3V (55.24%) and STaR (54.54%), confirming reduced visual hallucination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines across every domain. Against the two self-training baselines, gains are most pronounced: on Language Science, the method achieves 64.93% versus 48.82% (STaR) and 50.24% (R3V), a margin of +16.1 and +14.7 points respectively; on Commonsense, it reaches 72.74% versus 64.98% (STaR) and 65.49% (R3V). Against zero-shot prompting baselines (CoT, Desp-CoT, CCoT), the method leads by 15–38 points, confirming that structured self-training with perception verification far exceeds what prompting alone can achieve. Notably, CoT prompting actually degrades performance below direct VQA on LLaVA (e.g., 38.86% vs. 45.50% on Language Science), highlighting the shortcut-learning problem the paper targets. On the OOD MMMU Science split, the method extends its lead (36.48% vs. 33.78% R3V, 32.43% STaR), and on HallusionBench OCR it achieves the highest accuracy (60.14%), validating that perception-verified training reduces hallucination relative to answer-only filtering used by STaR and R3V.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-training paradigm for VLMs established by STaR (iterative generate-filter-finetune with answer hints) and R3V (reflection-based rationale refinement), but departs from both by introducing an explicit perception-verification step (PerceptEval) that filters rationales based on caption fidelity rather than answer correctness alone. It also positions itself as a lightweight supervised fine-tuning alternative to the growing family of RL-based VLM reasoning methods (Vision-R1, Visionary-R1, VisionSR1), which require expensive policy optimization and carefully designed reward mechanisms. The two-stage curriculum learning strategy and caption-guided reasoning enhancement are novel additions to the self-training loop, drawing on curriculum learning principles to stabilize iterative training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, low-cost path to improving VLM reasoning without RL or expensive human/proprietary CoT annotations, which is significant for resource-constrained settings. However, evaluation is limited to multiple-choice VQA on M3CoT (four domains) and MMMU science, with base models capped at 13B parameters; generalization to open-ended generation, video, or larger models remains untested. An open question is whether PerceptEval's reliance on FG-CLIP and OCR-based similarity transfers well to image types with minimal embedded text or highly abstract visual content, where both signals may be weak.
