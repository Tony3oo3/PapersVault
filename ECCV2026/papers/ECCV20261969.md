## Problem

Event cameras offer microsecond latency and high dynamic range but produce sparse, asynchronous streams that are structurally incompatible with the dense, grid-structured inputs expected by large-scale RGB foundation models. Existing event-based methods are trained from scratch for narrow tasks and cannot generalize, leaving the event community excluded from the benefits of pretrained ViT encoders. The paper asks whether events can be projected into a frozen RGB latent manifold so that complex image-trained decoders operate on event data with zero task-specific retraining.

## Input / Output

The model consumes event streams as 448×448 voxel grids with five temporal bins (chosen after showing that voxel grids, Tencode, and ERGO yield nearly identical alignment losses) and optionally synchronized RGB frames for cross-modal matching. Outputs are latent feature vectors in DUNE's (HW+1)×d space, which are then passed to frozen, RGB-trained decoders: a single linear head for depth (discretized into bins) and segmentation, and the MASt3R head for feature correspondences and relative pose. A memory-hold mechanism carries forward the last valid prediction during periods of zero event activity, and overlapping 448×448 tiling handles inputs larger than the foundation model's native resolution.

## Method

REALM replaces DUNE's image patch embedder with a lightweight convolutional stem that maps the five-bin voxel grid into M×d tokens, then applies LoRA (rank 32) adapters to the attention, projection, and feed-forward layers of the frozen DUNE ViT backbone to bridge the modality gap without catastrophic forgetting. Training uses knowledge distillation from DUNE with a novel progressive spatial mask: a binary occupancy grid of active voxels is morphologically dilated with a radius that grows monotonically over training, so the loss initially penalizes only regions with event activity and gradually expands to enforce holistic scene understanding. An MAE-style stochastic patch dropout (fixed ratio ρ after warmup) further forces the LoRA layers to build compressed global representations rather than relying on local interpolation. The total loss is a masked, weighted combination of L1, cosine similarity, and MSE between DUNE's image features and the event student's features. At inference, the frozen encoder plus frozen RGB-trained decoders (linear heads for depth/segmentation, MASt3R for matching) produce all downstream predictions with no event-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

On the ECD dataset, REALM achieves 63.3% AUC@20° for wide-baseline feature matching, substantially outperforming the previous state-of-the-art SuperEvent (46.7%) and all other event-based baselines. On the VECtor dataset for pose estimation, REALM reaches 47.7% AUC@5° (event-event) and 26.0% AUC@5° (image-event), surpassing MINIMA (39.0% and 23.8% respectively) while operating zero-shot with a frozen MASt3R decoder. For depth estimation on MVSEC, REALM uses only a linear head (0.39M params) yet remains competitive with specialized recurrent transformers (10–77× more parameters), and notably outperforms its RGB teacher DUNE in low-illumination night sequences (e.g., 2.51 m vs. 3.10 m at 20 m cut-off on outdoor night 1). For semantic segmentation on DSEC, REALM achieves 55.37% mIoU with a single linear head, exceeding ESS (51.57%), EV-SegNet (51.76%), and HALSIE (52.43%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REALM decisively outperforms all event-specific feature matching baselines: on ECD, it surpasses SuperEvent (22.7% → 26.2% AUC@5°) and EventPoint (1.6% → 26.2%), while LLAK and RATE remain far behind (0.7% and 3.3% at @5°). In cross-modal pose estimation on VECtor, REALM beats MINIMA by 8.7 points in event-event AUC@5° (47.7% vs. 39.0%) and 2.2 points in image-event AUC@5° (26.0% vs. 23.8%), while also achieving lower median angular error (2.75° vs. 3.93° event-event; 5.45° vs. 6.46° image-event). For depth estimation, REALM outperforms E2Depth and Zhu et al. in most night sequences (e.g., 2.08 m vs. 3.38 m and 3.13 m at 10 m on outdoor night 1) and is competitive with EMoDepth, all while using only a linear head versus their recurrent or self-supervised architectures. In segmentation, REALM exceeds ESS, EV-SegNet, and HALSIE in mIoU (55.37% vs. 51.57%, 51.76%, 52.43%) but trails ESEG (57.55%) which uses explicit edge-semantic supervision. Against the RGB teacher DUNE, REALM matches or exceeds performance in low-light conditions where events provide higher dynamic range, confirming that the aligned manifold inherits and extends the teacher's capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

REALM builds directly on DUNE, a universal ViT encoder distilled from DINOv2, MASt3R, and Multi-HMR teachers, and adapts it to event data via a lightweight convolutional embedder and LoRA adapters on the frozen backbone. It departs from the dominant paradigm in event-based vision—training task-specific architectures from scratch (ESS, E2Depth, SuperEvent)—and from cross-modal reconstruction approaches (E2VID, EvDistill) that first synthesize an image before applying RGB models. The key conceptual shift is treating event-to-RGB alignment as a latent-space projection problem solvable with minimal parameter updates, rather than as a reconstruction or task-specific learning problem. The progressive spatial distillation mask and MAE-style input dropout are novel training strategies tailored to the sparsity and blind-spot structure of event streams.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a frozen, RGB-trained MASt3R decoder can perform wide-baseline feature matching on raw event data zero-shot, REALM effectively unlocks the entire ecosystem of image foundation model decoders for the event domain, sidestepping the chronic scarcity of annotated event datasets. The finding that the event student surpasses its RGB teacher in low-illumination and high-dynamic-range conditions suggests the aligned manifold captures complementary cues rather than merely mimicking frames. Open questions include whether the fixed-resolution voxel-grid embedder can be replaced by a truly asynchronous, resolution-agnostic module, and whether the alignment generalizes to longer temporal windows or more extreme sparsity without the current memory-hold heuristic.
