## Problem

Autoregressive MLLM-based 3D generation is hampered by a structural trade-off in existing tokenizations: compact set-based representations (e.g., VecSet) discard deterministic spatial ordering, making sequence prediction ambiguous, while uniform or octree voxel grids preserve ordering but waste tokens on smooth regions, producing prohibitively long sequences that undermine inference efficiency and training stability. No prior representation simultaneously achieves adaptive compactness and a well-defined autoregressive order at high resolution.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a text prompt (encoded via CLIP) or an RGB image (encoded via DINOv2) and produces a high-resolution 3D shape at 1024³ grid resolution. A key structural detail is that the first stage fixes the supervoxel count (sequence length) before any token generation begins, so the autoregressive model always knows exactly how many tokens to produce (~1,048–1,065 tokens, i.e., 12.8% of a uniform voxel grid at the same resolution). The final geometry is decoded from discrete supervoxel tokens by the SuperVoxelVAE decoder into a mesh.

## Method

SuperVoxelGPT decouples token allocation from geometry generation in two stages. In Stage 1, a 24-layer MaskGIT model (conditioned on CLIP/DINOv2 features) predicts a 64³ saliency volume in 12 parallel iterations; this volume drives a Saliency-guided Centroidal Voronoi Tessellation (SCVT) that partitions space into variable-sized supervoxels—unit-size in high-saliency regions and up to 4×4×4 in smooth regions—with centers sorted by Z-Y-X order to yield a deterministic token sequence. In Stage 2, a SuperVoxelVAE (built on TRELLIS2's sparse convolution backbone with plug-in grid-to-supervoxel, FSQ, and supervoxel-to-grid layers using KNN Cross Attention from PointTransformerV2) encodes local geometry into discrete tokens, and a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-0.5B MLLM autoregressively predicts these tokens conditioned on the multimodal prompt and Fourier positional embeddings of supervoxel centers processed by a 2-layer self-attention module. Because the sequence length is known a priori from Stage 1, inference applies Jacobi decoding to update all token positions in parallel, achieving a ~10× speedup over sequential autoregressive baselines.

## Results

On image-to-3D generation at 1024³ resolution, SuperVoxelGPT achieves CD_L2 of 0.0122, PSNR of 32.08 dB, ULIP-2 similarity of 0.474, and MCS of 44.21, matching TRELLIS2 (0.0123 / 32.19 / 0.475 / 44.24) while using only 1,048 tokens versus 7,303 and completing inference in 4.60 s versus 34.25 s (~7.4× faster). On text-to-3D, it reaches CD_L2 of 0.0134 and PSNR of 32.05 at 1024³ resolution, substantially outperforming OctGPT (0.0797 / 19.80 at 256³) and BrickGPT (0.0851 / 12.99 at 20³) while generating in 4.48 s. The token sequence is compressed to 12.8% of uniform voxel tokenization, and the average inference time across image-to-3D baselines (47.1 s) is reduced by approximately 10×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TRELLIS2 (the strongest baseline at 1024³), SuperVoxelGPT essentially ties on all four quality metrics—CD_L2 differs by 0.0001, PSNR by 0.11 dB, ULIP-2 by 0.001, and MCS by 0.03—while running 7.4× faster (4.60 s vs. 34.25 s) and using 8.9× fewer tokens (1,048 vs. 7,303). Compared to Direct3D-S2 (also 1024³), SuperVoxelGPT improves CD_L2 from 0.0168 to 0.0122 and MCS from 33.17 to 44.21, while being 30× faster (4.60 s vs. 137.2 s) and using 53× fewer tokens. Against lower-resolution methods (CraftsMan3D at 512³, TRELLIS at 256³), SuperVoxelGPT dominates on every metric, reflecting the advantage of 1024³ resolution. On text-to-3D, SuperVoxelGPT outperforms OctGPT by 5.9× in CD_L2 (0.0134 vs. 0.0797) and 1.6× in PSNR (32.05 vs. 19.80), and surpasses BrickGPT by 6.3× in CD_L2 and 2.5× in PSNR, all at a resolution 40–51× higher. The only consistent (albeit marginal) loss is the 0.11 dB PSNR gap versus TRELLIS2, which the authors attribute to aggressive coarsening of low-saliency regions (t = 0.1) that can wash out very fine structures such as thin grooves.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuperVoxelGPT positions itself at the intersection of two well-studied but opposing 3D tokenization paradigms: compact set-based representations (Shape2VecSet, Dora, Hunyuan3D) that discard spatial ordering, and uniform/octree voxel grids (TRELLIS, Direct3D-S2, Sparc3D) that preserve ordering at the cost of severe redundancy. The paper builds directly on TRELLIS2's sparse voxel VAE architecture and loss functions, adding plug-and-play supervoxel conversion layers (grid-to-supervoxel, FSQ, supervoxel-to-grid) to compress token sequences without modifying the backbone. For the first-stage saliency prediction, it repurposes MaskGIT's iterative masked generation with CLIP/DINOv2 conditioning, and borrows Jacobi decoding from NLP to parallelize the autoregressive token generation once sequence length is known a priori.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× inference speedup (4.6s vs. 47.1s average) while matching TRELLIS2's generation quality at 1024³ resolution with only 12.8% of the tokens is a practically significant efficiency gain for autoregressive 3D generation, and the plug-and-play supervoxel layers could be integrated into other sparse voxel VAE frameworks. However, several open questions remain: the method's reliance on accurate saliency prediction means it degrades on shapes with uniformly distributed fine details (where adaptive allocation collapses toward uniform), it currently handles geometry only without texture or material modeling, and training on just 10,000 shapes from Trellis-500K limits generalization claims. Whether the CVT-based adaptive tokenization strategy transfers to other generative modalities or scales to even higher resolutions without the saliency bottleneck is an important direction for future work.
