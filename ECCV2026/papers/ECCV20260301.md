## Problem

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) produce overly generic predictions on fine-grained Open World Classification (e.g., 'flower' instead of 'lily') because they process each image in isolation without domain-specific contextual cues. Existing retrieval-augmented test-time adaptation methods either require ground-truth labels, predefined class vocabularies, or large external retrieval corpora, making them impractical for truly open-world, label-free settings where the incoming data distribution is unknown.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260301/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of unlabeled RGB test images, processing them one at a time in a streaming inference paradigm rather than batch evaluation. A frozen CLIP-ViT-L/14 encoder extracts 768-d visual embeddings used as retrieval keys, while the LMM generates free-form attribute-augmented text descriptions as memory values. The final output is an open-ended textual object description (not restricted to a fixed label set), produced by the LMM conditioned on a synthesized attribute-aware prompt. The first α=2% of the stream seeds the memory; subsequent images trigger retrieval, diversity-gated updates, and refined inference.

## Method

AMCI is a training-free, model-agnostic three-stage framework that maintains a non-parametric key-value memory of (CLIP visual embedding, LMM-generated attribute description) pairs. Stage 1 initializes the memory from the first few stream samples using a base prompt that elicits discriminative visual attributes. Stage 2 performs k-nearest-neighbor retrieval on visual embeddings and gates new entries through an EMA-updated adaptive similarity threshold τ, replacing the most visually similar existing entry when capacity is reached to preserve diversity. Stage 3 uses a separate refinement LLM (Llama-3-8B-Instruct) to synthesize the k retrieved descriptions into a structured, attribute-focused prompt, which is then fed to the frozen LMM alongside the query image for the final prediction. The entire pipeline requires no parameter updates, no ground-truth labels, and no external retrieval corpus, operating purely through inference-time context injection.

## Results

Across 13 LMM configurations (0.5B–8B parameters), AMCI yields average gains of +8.2% TI, +16.3% CFI, +3.5% SS, and +10.2% CS on fine-grained datasets, and +0.7% TI, +7.9% CFI, +2.3% SS, +5.4% CS on low-resource datasets. The strongest absolute result is Qwen2-VL 7B + AMCI reaching 41.5% TI and 63.0% CFI on fine-grained tasks, while the largest relative improvement is LLaVA-OV (Qwen2 7B) whose CFI jumps from 13.9% to 47.6%. On low-resource HAM10000, Qwen2-VL 7B + AMCI achieves 5.2% TI versus 1.8% for the vanilla model, demonstrating meaningful prediction capability where global priors are unavailable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against open-world baselines, AMCI-equipped LMMs substantially outperform CASED [9] and CLIP Retrieval on TI and CFI (e.g., Qwen2-VL 7B + AMCI: 41.5% TI vs. 22.3% for CLIP Retrieval; 63.0% CFI vs. 24.7%), though closed-world CLIP and SigLIP still lead on SS/CS due to their access to fixed label sets. In the retrieval-augmented TTA comparison (Table 4), AMCI with Qwen2-VL 7B achieves a mean of 44.8 across four metrics on Oxford Pets/FGVC Aircraft/HAM10000, matching FreeTTA (45.4) and TT-RAA (45.1) while operating without any class-name vocabulary or external retrieval corpus. AMCI clearly surpasses TDA (mean ~37.4) and RA-TTA (mean ~41.5) across all three datasets. Compared to CoT prompting (Zero-shot CoT, LLaVA CoT), AMCI yields average improvements of 11.7% and 12.3% on the four metrics respectively, with particularly large CFI gains (e.g., 63.8% vs. 39.8% for Zero-shot CoT on Oxford Pets). In terms of efficiency, AMCI runs 3.5× faster than the long-chain reasoning baseline LlamaV-o1 (1.85 h vs. 6.58 h on H100) while achieving a higher mean score (44.3 vs. 31.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of Open-World Classification (OWC) with LMMs, formalized by Conti et al. [11] and Zhang et al. [45], and Retrieval-Augmented Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) methods such as FreeTTA [12], TT-RAA [16], and RA-TTA [27]. It departs from prior TTA work by operating in a fully label-free open-world setting where no class vocabulary or ground-truth labels are available, and from prior OWC work by identifying a specific 'vagueness bias' in fine-grained scenarios and addressing it through a non-parametric streaming memory with diversity-aware updates. The CFI metric extends the evaluation suite from [11] by explicitly penalizing generic parent-category predictions, filling a gap in how fine-grained OWC accuracy is measured.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AMCI offers a practical, training-free recipe that practitioners can plug into any LMM to improve fine-grained open-world recognition without retraining or access to class labels, which is critical for deployment in domains where categories are unknown or constantly shifting. The introduction of the CFI metric and the low-resource benchmark (iNaturalist, HAM10000) provides the community with better tools to evaluate and stress-test LMMs in realistic fine-grained scenarios. Open questions include whether the streaming memory assumption holds in truly one-shot or non-sequential deployment, whether the approach generalizes to video or multi-image contexts, and whether the reliance on a separate refinement LLM (Llama-3-8B) limits scalability in latency-sensitive settings.
