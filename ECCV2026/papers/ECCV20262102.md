## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods allocate primitives in a view-centric, pixel-aligned manner, baking redundancy into the 3D asset and causing representation size to grow with the number of input views. This creates an unfavorable trade-off where adding more views improves coverage but inflates the scene, destabilizes reconstruction in overlapping regions, and prevents a fixed, compact output budget. The paper asks whether a globally aligned latent representation can be learned before any explicit geometry is decoded, enabling a view-invariant Gaussian count without sacrificing reconstruction quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 12–36 RGB images (256×256) with known camera extrinsics and intrinsics, which are first mapped into a canonical coordinate frame via a similarity transform (mean camera center, averaged viewing directions, and constellation-diameter scaling). Each view is patchified and augmented with Plücker-ray embeddings plus a per-view camera code (Fourier-encoded center + MLP-encoded intrinsics). The output is a strictly fixed set of 16K anisotropic 3D Gaussians (positions, scales, quaternions, opacities, SH colors) occupying only 3.8 MB on disk, which can be rendered to arbitrary novel views via standard front-to-back α-blending without further optimization.

## Method

GlobalSplat follows an 'align first, decode later' principle: a fixed set of M=2048 learnable latent scene tokens (d=512) cross-attend to all multi-view patch features through B=4 dual-branch attention blocks that explicitly disentangle geometry and appearance streams, preventing texture from masking poor structural predictions. Within each block, stream-specific queries perform cross-attention to the input patches followed by L=2 self-attention layers, and the two streams are fused via a 2-layer mixer MLP before the next block. A specialized dual-head decoder then maps the refined tokens to explicit 3D Gaussian attributes (positions, scales, quaternions, opacity in the geometry head; SH colors in the appearance head). A coarse-to-fine capacity curriculum progressively increases decoded Gaussians per latent slot from G=1 to G=8, preventing representation bloat during early training. The model is trained from scratch (<100M parameters, no pretrained backbone) with a combined objective of rendering loss (L2 + perceptual), a self-supervised consistency loss that enforces geometric agreement between independent forward passes on view subsets, and structural regularization (soft thresholding + frustum constraint).

## Results

On RealEstate10K with 24 input views, GlobalSplat achieves 28.53 PSNR / 0.883 SSIM / 0.140 LPIPS using a fixed budget of only 16K Gaussians (3.8 MB on disk), matching ZPressor's 28.51 PSNR while using 24× fewer Gaussians (16K vs. 393K). It requires just 1.79 GB peak GPU memory and 77.88 ms inference time, compared to 3.70 GB / 194.20 ms for ZPressor and 29.84 GB / 669.50 ms for DepthSplat. On ACID (zero-shot cross-dataset), GlobalSplat maintains 28.03 PSNR at 24 views, outperforming all Gaussian-based baselines. On unbounded DL3DV-10K scenes, GlobalSplat attains the best PSNR (24.90 at 24 views) with only 32K Gaussians versus 688K for ZPressor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GlobalSplat matches or exceeds ZPressor on PSNR at all view counts (28.53 vs. 28.51 at 24V) while using 24× fewer Gaussians (16K vs. 393K), though ZPressor retains a clear edge on perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.097 vs. 0.140) and SSIM (0.911 vs. 0.883). Against the concurrent C3G, GlobalSplat wins decisively on PSNR (28.53 vs. 23.80 at 24V) and is far more efficient (1.79 GB / 78 ms vs. 6.04 GB / 387 ms), despite C3G using a 1.7B-parameter pretrained backbone. GlobalSplat outperforms AnySplat by ~4.4 dB PSNR (28.53 vs. 24.11) and DepthSplat by ~8.9 dB (28.53 vs. 19.66) at 24 views, while using a fraction of their Gaussian budgets. It also surpasses GGN (17.20 PSNR), NoPoSplat (21.24 PSNR), and EcoSplat (24.72 PSNR) by wide margins. On the non-Gaussian LVSM baseline, GlobalSplat leads by 1.29 dB PSNR at 24V (28.53 vs. 27.24) and 2.07 dB at 36V, while producing an explicit renderable 3DGS asset rather than requiring a heavy per-view decoder. On unbounded DL3DV-10K, GlobalSplat achieves the best PSNR (25.13 at 12V, 24.90 at 24V) with 32K Gaussians, outperforming ZPressor (24.30 / 24.26) and DepthSplat (23.32 / 20.38) at a 21× smaller representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

GlobalSplat departs from the dominant feed-forward 3DGS paradigm (pixelSplat, MVSplat, DepthSplat, ZPressor, etc.) that decodes Gaussians from dense, view-aligned intermediates, instead adopting an 'align first, decode later' principle inspired by LVSM's fixed-latent-token fusion but extending it with explicit 3DGS decoding. It builds on 3DGS as the output representation and on YoNoSplat's canonical-frame normalization, while differentiating from the concurrent C3G by replacing full self-attention over a 1.7B pretrained backbone with a lighter dual-branch iterative-attention encoder trained from scratch (<100M parameters) and a coarse-to-fine capacity curriculum. The conceptual shift is from view-centric primitive formation (where each pixel or view contributes Gaussians) to scene-centric formation driven by a fixed global latent bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical operating point—16K Gaussians (4 MB on disk), 78 ms inference, and 1.79 GB peak memory while matching or approaching ZPressor's PSNR—makes feed-forward 3DGS viable in memory-constrained and latency-sensitive deployment settings where dense pipelines (hundreds of thousands to millions of Gaussians) are impractical. The finding that reconstruction quality is driven primarily by latent scene-token count rather than decoded primitives per token (Table 5) offers a useful design principle for future compact 3D representations. Open questions include whether the fixed 16K budget can scale to unbounded or city-scale scenes, how the approach handles dynamic (4D) content, and whether the acknowledged LPIPS gap versus heavier methods (0.138 vs. 0.097) can be closed without sacrificing the compactness advantage.
