## Problem

Population-level morphometric measurements (e.g., limb-to-wing ratios) are central to ecology and evolutionary biology but traditionally require physical specimen handling or controlled photography, limiting scalability and coverage. Public image repositories like iNaturalist contain millions of wildlife photos, but direct geometric measurement from arbitrary views is ill-posed due to viewpoint variation, occlusion, and scale ambiguity. The paper asks whether carefully filtered 2D correspondences over web-scale imagery can provide scalable morphometric proxies without per-species training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single user-annotated reference image (with keypoints defining at least two body parts assumed to be approximately fronto-parallel) and a large uncurated image collection (typically 10⁴–10⁵ iNaturalist images of the same species). It produces population-level statistics (mean, median, variance) of part-length ratios rather than individual measurements, since scale ambiguity precludes absolute metric lengths. All images are processed at 518×518 resolution, and the user only needs to label one query image per species and pose—no per-species training or task-specific keypoint labels are required.

## Method

WILDPROP is a training-free, three-stage pipeline that casts morphometric estimation as a retrieval-driven correspondence problem. First, pose-aware retrieval uses DINOv3 patch embeddings (with SAM2 + Grounding-DINO segmentation to suppress background) to select the top-100 pose-aligned images from the collection via cosine similarity. Second, keypoint matching transfers part endpoints by finding the highest-similarity 16×16 patch in each retrieved image, followed by RANSAC-based rigid transformation fitting to filter geometrically inconsistent matches. Third, iterative refinement (3 iterations) updates keypoint descriptors by weighted-averaging patch features from the top-20 retrieved images with the original query, then re-estimates correspondences. The key insight is that rather than solving the ill-posed 3D measurement problem from arbitrary views, the method restricts viewpoint variability through pose-conditioned retrieval, making 2D correspondence reliable and enabling aggregation across many individuals to estimate stable ratio distributions.

## Results

WILDPROP achieves a mean relative error of 11.3% on AVONET (5 bird species, 4 body-part ratios), 11.0% on the ShoreBirds dataset (5 species, 4 ratios), and 18.4% on the Frogs dataset (5 species, 4 ratios), all measured against physical ground-truth morphometric medians. The best individual species-part estimates reach as low as 0.4% error (California Gull bill/wing), while the worst reach 34.4% (Pacific Chorus Frog eye-nostril ratio). Ablations confirm that pose-aware retrieval is the single most critical component: random sampling yields 216% error, retrieval alone reduces it to 40%, and the full pipeline (retrieval + SAM masking + RANSAC filtering + iterative refinement) brings it down to 11.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WILDPROP does not compare against other image-based morphometric methods; instead, its estimates are validated against physical ground-truth measurements from three curated morphometric datasets (AVONET, ShoreBirds, Frogs). The key finding is that a single annotated query image plus web-scale retrieval yields population-level ratio estimates within 10–20% of physical measurements, a regime previously unattainable without controlled photography or specimen handling. The ablation (Table 4a) isolates each pipeline stage: removing pose-aware retrieval (random sampling) inflates error from 11.3% to 216%, SAM-based background suppression cuts it from 40% to 16.1%, RANSAC geometric filtering brings it to 12.2%, and iterative keypoint refinement provides a final 1% improvement. Among retrieval backbones, DINOv3 patch features (11.3%) outperform CLIP (17.1%), BioCLIP (14.8%), and SigLIP (11.8%), confirming that geometry-sensitive self-supervised features are essential for pose-aware retrieval. For keypoint matching, concatenating DINOv3 with Stable Diffusion features marginally improves over DINOv3 alone (11.2% vs. 11.3%), while DINOv2 features degrade performance to 13.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in animal morphometrics—physical specimen handling or controlled photography with standardized poses—by reframing the problem as a retrieval-driven correspondence task over unconstrained web-scale imagery. It also sidesteps the per-species training requirement of pose estimation tools like DeepLabCut and the unvalidated 3D reconstruction route (SAM3D, Trellis) by instead conditioning on pose through DINOv3-based retrieval and transferring keypoints via patch-level feature matching with RANSAC geometric verification. The coarse-to-fine design (global retrieval → local matching → geometric filtering) directly parallels classical SIFT-based instance retrieval pipelines, but repurposes modern foundation models (DINOv3, SAM2, Grounding-DINO) to eliminate the need for task-specific training or camera calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WILDPROP offers a practical, non-invasive pathway to population-level morphometric estimates from existing citizen-science image repositories, potentially enabling comparative analyses across taxa, geography, and seasonality that were previously infeasible at scale. Key limitations include the restriction to proportions (scale ambiguity), noisier estimates than physical measurements, and the absence of a principled abstention mechanism when retrieval fails or parts are absent. Open questions include whether calibrated depth or explicit 3D keypoint detection can extend the approach to arbitrary viewpoints, whether a small number of physical measurements can calibrate image-based estimates, and whether richer anatomical representations (segmentations, curves) can reduce the 10–20% error floor.
