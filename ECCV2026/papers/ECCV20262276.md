## Problem

Existing 'thinking with images' frameworks optimize only final answer correctness, leaving intermediate zoom-in actions unsupervised. This creates an illusion of visual grounding: models achieve high answer accuracy while their visual actions are superficial, redundant, or spurious, contributing little to the actual reasoning. The paper identifies this action-outcome disconnect as stemming from sparse outcome-only rewards and coarse trajectory-level credit assignment that cannot distinguish helpful from unhelpful zoom-in steps.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a text question (formatted as multiple-choice with 3–7 synthesized distractors) and produces an interleaved reasoning chain of textual rationales and visual zoom-in actions, where each zoom-in specifies a bounding box region to crop and re-encode at higher resolution. Up to 6 iterative zoom-in rounds are permitted, and the final output is a text answer accompanied by the full sequence of zoom-in coordinates serving as a verifiable visual rationale trace. The training dataset consists of 8k high-quality question–answer–rationale-region triplets, where the rationale region is a padded bounding box anchoring the evidence needed to answer the question.

## Method

ViRL is an end-to-end RL framework (built on GRPO with PPO-style policy updates) that replaces outcome-only supervision with process-level fidelity signals. The key reward is a rationale fidelity term computed as a signed correctness signal (IoU of the model's zoom-in box against the ground-truth evidence region, thresholded at h₀) plus a discrete refinement bonus that grants stepwise gains for progressively tighter alignment, encouraging a coarse-to-fine curriculum. A bi-level credit assignment scheme then modulates the GRPO group-relative advantage per action: in successful trajectories, high-fidelity zoom-ins receive amplified credit (h_good > 1) while low-fidelity ones are attenuated; in failed trajectories, the blame is inverted, so the policy learns to attribute outcomes to specific visual actions rather than the trajectory as a whole. Training data is curated through a three-stage pipeline—automated QA generation with evidence-anchored regions, MLLM-based consistency verification, and zoom-in-dependency filtering—that retains only samples genuinely requiring localized visual evidence, yielding 8k high-quality training examples from a 200k candidate pool.

## Results

ViRL (7B, Qwen2.5-VL backbone) achieves state-of-the-art results across all six benchmarks: 90.1 on V*, 75.3 on HRBench, 88.7 on POPE, 76.1 on VLind, 691.0 on MME(R), and 67.5 on MMStar†. The largest gains are on perception-grounded reasoning (MME(R): +80.9 over Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and language-prior resistance (VLind: +8.7). On the efficiency front (Table 2), ViRL with K=6 zoom rounds reaches 90.1% accuracy on V* with 381 tool calls and 2.6 s latency, outperforming CoF (89.8%, 403 calls, 6.8 s) in both accuracy and speed. Backbone generalization to Qwen3-VL-4B yields 90.8 on V* and 704.8 on MME(R), confirming the method is architecture-agnostic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViRL outperforms all 'thinking with images' baselines on every benchmark where they overlap. Against the closest competitor DeepEyes (7B), ViRL gains +1.2 on V*, +2.2 on HRBench, +6.1 on VLind, and a substantial +70.3 on MME(R), indicating that DeepEyes's high answer accuracy (88.9) masks weak visual-action fidelity. Compared to Chain-of-Focus (CoF), ViRL improves V* by +2.1, HRBench by +4.8, VLind by +7.8, and MME(R) by +17.5, while also reducing inference latency from 6.8 s to 2.6 s (Table 2). Against Pixel Reasoner, ViRL leads by +5.8 on V* and +52.5 on MME(R). The most striking comparison is against the no-tool baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B: ViRL adds +18.7 on V* and +80.9 on MME(R), demonstrating that process-level visual rationale supervision yields far larger gains than model scaling alone (7B→32B adds only +9.7 on V*). On the 4B Qwen3-VL backbone, ViRL still achieves 90.8 on V* and 704.8 on MME(R), surpassing the 7B DeepEyes and CoF results, confirming that the improvement stems from the learning paradigm rather than model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'thinking with images' paradigm (DeepEyes, Pixel Reasoner, Chain-of-Focus) by shifting from outcome-only RL rewards to process-level supervision over intermediate zoom-in actions. It builds on GRPO's group-relative advantage computation as the optimization backbone and adopts DAPO's training stabilizers (clip-higher, dynamic sampling), but departs fundamentally by introducing rationale fidelity rewards (IoU-based alignment with ground-truth evidence regions) and a bi-level credit assignment scheme that modulates per-step advantages based on visual rationale quality. The evidence-grounded dataset construction pipeline, which anchors zoom-in targets to QA semantics via a three-stage generation-verification-filtering process, is a new contribution not present in prior visual tool-use or CoT datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper formalizes and quantifies a critical failure mode in the 'thinking with images' literature: high answer accuracy can coexist with low rationale accuracy (e.g., DeepEyes at 89.1% answer vs. 57% rationale), meaning models appear visually grounded while relying on spurious or redundant crops. This has direct implications for deploying VLMs in high-stakes settings where interpretability and verifiability of the reasoning trace matter. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes beyond zoom-in to richer visual action spaces (drawing, segmentation, multi-image reasoning), whether the IoU-based fidelity reward transfers to tasks without clean ground-truth evidence regions, and whether the process-supervision signal remains effective at scale or in interactive multi-turn settings.
