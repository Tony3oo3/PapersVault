## Problem

Video diffusion models produce visually compelling but 3D-inconsistent frame sequences, where object geometry warps unpredictably across viewpoints (generative drift). Directly reconstructing 3D scenes from these frames yields misaligned surfaces and floating artifacts, and existing remedies—camera conditioning, consistency finetuning, or 3D cache re-projection—either require costly per-model training or still fail to eliminate the drift. The paper argues that generative models cannot be made perfectly 3D-consistent and instead tackles the problem on the reconstruction side with a lightweight per-scene alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260361/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N=50 RGB frames sampled from a single (or multiple autoregressive) video generated by any VDM, optionally accompanied by camera extrinsics/intrinsics when the VDM is camera-controlled. It produces a dense aligned pointcloud (~1.5M points) as an intermediate, and a canonical 2D Gaussian Splatting scene that renders photorealistic novel views in real-time from arbitrary camera positions. Notably, the input frames are deliberately *inconsistent*—the pipeline is designed to absorb the generative drift rather than assume geometrically valid multi-view observations.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, DepthAnything-3 lifts each frame into a per-pixel depth map and pinhole camera, which are unprojected and voxel-filtered into a dense but misaligned pointcloud. Second, a tailored non-rigid frame-to-model ICP iteratively aligns each frame's point subset to a canonical model by optimizing per-frame camera extrinsics and a hashgrid-MLP deformation field that predicts per-point SE(3) twists, using a point-to-plane data term, color-gradient ICP, sparse RoMa correspondences, and an ARAP-style TV regularizer; a subsequent global optimization jointly refines all cameras and deformations with anchor regularization to produce thin, sharp surfaces. Third, a single inverse-deformation hashgrid MLP (conditioned on a learnable view embedding) maps the canonical scene back into each frame's distorted space, and during 2DGS training the Gaussians are backward-deformed into the per-frame camera space before rasterization—this non-rigid-aware rendering loss factors out the inconsistencies so the canonical scene stays geometrically aligned while the photometric objective (L1 + LPIPS) sharpens appearance against the inconsistent image set.

## Results

On the single-video 3D reconstruction benchmark (averaged across six video diffusion models), the method achieves 79.29 3D consistency and 86.59 photometric consistency, substantially outperforming the best baselines (VGGT-X†: 69.66 / 67.05). It also leads on image fidelity with CLIP-IQA+ of 46.56 (vs. 41.44 for VGGT-X†) and CLIP Aesthetic of 37.61 (vs. 37.53), approaching the quality of the input video frames themselves (CLIP-IQA+ 47.39, CLIP Aesthetic 39.04). The ablation (Table 2) confirms that both the non-rigid alignment and the inverse deformation rendering loss are critical: removing either drops 3D consistency by 4–14 points and photometric consistency by 12–32 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method wins on all four metrics against every baseline. Against DA3 [42] (which uses DepthAnything-3 pointclouds as 3DGS initialization with depth regularization), it gains +9.76 in 3D consistency and +14.97 in photometric consistency, while also improving CLIP-IQA+ by +14.92. Against 3DGS-MCMC [34] (random initialization with optimizable cameras), the gains are even larger: +11.95 in 3D consistency and +17.01 in photometric consistency. Against VGGT-X [43] and its DA3-augmented variant VGGT-X† (which rigidly aligns correspondences before 3DGS), the method leads by +9.63 in 3D consistency and +19.51 in photometric consistency, confirming that rigid alignment alone cannot resolve the generative drift in video diffusion outputs. In the large-scale 360° world generation setting (up to 32 video sequences via SEVA), the method is compared qualitatively against WorldExplorer [61] and VGGT-X [43]; while baselines produce complete but artifact-ridden scenes from novel views, the method maintains consistent, high-fidelity renderings even from extreme novel perspectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant strategy of making video diffusion models 3D-consistent at generation time (via camera conditioning, 3D cache reprojecting, or consistency finetuning) by instead attacking the problem on the reconstruction side. It repurposes non-rigid ICP from robotics/SLAM (KinectFusion, Besl & McKay) and combines it with geometric foundation models (DepthAnything-3) to align per-frame pointclouds, then feeds the result into a modified 2D Gaussian Splatting pipeline that renders through an inverse deformation field. The conceptual lineage is: ICP-based registration → non-rigid dynamic reconstruction (DynamicFusion, Neural Nonrigid Tracking) → here applied to correct generative drift rather than temporal motion, with the inverse deformation rendering loss being the key new architectural element.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a general-purpose, model-agnostic wrapper that turns any existing video diffusion model into a 3D world generator without requiring expensive per-model finetuning, which is practically significant as the VDM landscape evolves rapidly. The 25-minute per-scene alignment cost on a single GPU is modest compared to generator finetuning, making it accessible. Key limitations remain: the method handles geometric drift but not hallucinations (objects appearing/disappearing across views), and the per-scene optimization cost, while small, is non-zero. An open direction the authors note is using the learned alignment deformations as gradient signals to finetune VDMs, potentially closing the gap between generation and reconstruction.
