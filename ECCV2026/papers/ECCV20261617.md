## Problem

Existing brain foundation models operate exclusively on macroscopic contrasts (T1, T2, fMRI), leaving tissue microstructure unaddressed. NODDI provides three biophysically distinct 3D maps (NDI, ODI, FWF), but standard multimodal fusion collapses them into a monolithic latent space, re-entangling the very compartmental processes the NODDI model was designed to decouple. A principled framework is needed to characterize whether these maps provide complementary, redundant, or synergistic information for neurological tasks while preserving each compartment's biophysical specificity.

## Input / Output

The model takes a triplet of 3D NODDI-derived volumes (NDI, ODI, FWF) at spatial resolution (96, 112, 96), each processed by a dedicated 3D ViT-S encoder with patch size (16, 16, 16) into a 384-d unimodal embedding. It produces a 128-d fused microstructural embedding (q_fused) via adaptive Re-Weighter aggregation, alongside five 128-d expert-specific interaction embeddings (three uniqueness, one synergy, one redundancy). During pretraining, two spatially augmented views per participant are generated; during downstream evaluation, the fused embedding is fed to a linear probe for regression or classification.

## Method

BrainFIBRE proposes Self-supervised Partial Information Decomposition (SPID), which extends PID-guided MoE learning to the label-free regime via a Counterfactual Candidate Construction (CCC) paradigm. CCC systematically disrupts inter-modality biophysical alignment by replacing a modality embedding with Gaussian noise (dropout) or with the same modality from a different participant (swap), generating counterfactual candidates that appear anatomically valid but are microstructurally mismatched. Expert-conditional interaction rules then assign each perturbation as positive or negative per expert (e.g., the ODI-uniqueness expert is invariant to N/F perturbations but sensitive to O perturbations), and a multi-positive symmetric InfoNCE loss trains each expert to isolate its designated PID atom. A global contrastive loss on the fused embedding, combined with KL balance and entropy regularization to prevent expert collapse, completes the self-supervised objective. The synergy and redundancy experts use learnable query tokens with scaled dot-product attention over concatenated unimodal embeddings, while uniqueness experts use simple linear projections.

## Results

On the UK Biobank held-out set (4,307 participants), BrainFIBRE achieves the best performance across all four downstream tasks: age prediction (MAE 3.95, Corr 0.76), sex classification (F1 89.30, Acc 89.33), hippocampal atrophy prediction (MAE 109.21, Corr 0.21), and processing speed (MAE 0.70, Corr 0.33). On the cross-ethnic SINGER cohort (818 Asian participants), BrainFIBRE leads on all tasks including WMH volume prediction (MAE 2.08, Corr 0.59) and cortical thickness (Corr 0.55). On HCP-Aging (630 participants), it achieves the best age MAE (5.54) and sex F1 (83.73), as well as the highest Flanker correlation (0.40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BrainFIBRE outperforms both main baselines across all three evaluation cohorts. Against I²MoE [33] (supervised multimodal MoE), BrainFIBRE achieves substantially lower age MAE (3.95 vs. 6.21 on UKB; 3.21 vs. 4.10 on SINGER) and higher sex F1 (89.30 vs. 71.94 on UKB), with the gap widening on the Asian cohort where I²MoE's supervised training on limited labels fails to generalize. Against BrainMVP [27] (self-supervised multimodal foundation model for sMRI), BrainFIBRE wins on age (MAE 3.95 vs. 4.46), sex (F1 89.30 vs. 78.93), and hippocampal atrophy (MAE 109.21 vs. 109.76) on UKB, and on all four SINGER tasks including WMH volume (MAE 2.08 vs. 2.54, Corr 0.59 vs. 0.44). The one near-tie is processing speed on UKB where both achieve MAE 0.70, though BrainMVP edges slightly on correlation (0.36 vs. 0.33). The consistent advantage on the ethnically distinct SINGER cohort (particularly WMH prediction, where BrainFIBRE's Corr of 0.59 nearly doubles BrainMVP's 0.44) suggests that PID-guided microstructural decomposition captures disease-relevant signals that macroscopic sMRI pretraining misses.

## Relation to Prior Work

BrainFIBRE extends the supervised PID-guided MoE framework of I2MoE [33] into the self-supervised regime by replacing label-agreement-based expert routing with a Counterfactual Candidate Construction (CCC) paradigm that generates contrastive signals through modality dropping and swapping. It also carves out a new niche within brain foundation models: while prior efforts (BrainMVP, BrainLM, Brain-JEPA) operate on macroscopic sMRI or fMRI contrasts, BrainFIBRE is the first to target tissue microstructure via NODDI-derived maps (NDI, ODI, FWF), treating them as distinct modalities whose biophysical interactions are explicitly decomposed. The 3D ViT backbone and InfoNCE-based contrastive objectives follow standard practice, so the core novelty lies in the SPID objective and the PID-informed expert specialization rather than in the encoder architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first foundation model for brain microstructure pretrained on 55,592 UK Biobank participants, BrainFIBRE opens a path toward transferable, interpretable representations for diffusion MRI that prior macroscopic-only foundation models cannot provide. The cross-ethnic validation (Caucasian UKB/HCP-Aging and Asian SINGER) and task-specific expert weight patterns (e.g., FWF dominance for WMH prediction, ODI dominance for hippocampal atrophy) lend neurobiological credibility to the PID decomposition. Open questions include whether the five-atom PID decomposition scales gracefully beyond three modalities, whether the counterfactual perturbations remain informative for more heterogeneous or higher-dimensional inputs, and whether the interpretability of expert weights translates into actionable clinical insights beyond post-hoc validation against known literature.
