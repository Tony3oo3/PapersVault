## Problem

Video diffusion transformers (DiTs) control motion only implicitly through text prompts, making precise spatial specification of where, how far, and how fast objects or cameras should move impractical. While training-free motion control has been explored for U-Net-based video diffusion models, existing strategies (noise warping, attention masking, bounding-box latent optimization) are either architecturally incompatible with the 3D full attention of DiTs or fail to produce reliable control in practice, leaving a gap for explicit, training-free motion control in DiT-based image-to-video generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB input image (evaluated at 480×832) plus either (a) polygonal object masks with user-defined affine warps (translation, rotation, scaling) for fine-grained object control, or (b) full-frame optical flow fields for camera motion control. In practical deployment, the optical flow can be derived from a predicted depth map (e.g., VGGT or Depth Anything 3) and a user-specified camera trajectory rather than ground-truth flow. The output is a temporally coherent video with the specified motion, generated by the frozen Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B or CogVideoX I2V-5B backbone without any parameter updates.

## Method

QWERTY steers motion by warping query tokens within the 3D full attention of a frozen video DiT: first-frame queries in the user-specified region are pasted at warped positions in later frames, concentrating attention from target-region queries onto first-frame source-region keys. A key enabler is Semantic-Temporal Channel Decomposition (STCD), which uses PCA on semantic anchors (first-frame spatial tokens) and temporal anchors (per-frame spatial means) to identify frame-consistent semantic channels, restricting warping to only those channels to avoid attention distortion from frame-variant temporal components. Spatial RoPE is applied before warping so warped queries share positional encoding with first-frame keys, while temporal RoPE is applied afterward to preserve correct temporal ordering. The query-warped velocity prediction at early denoising steps provides strong motion guidance, and is further leveraged as a self-guidance signal for latent optimization with a Fourier phase-alignment loss (adapted from RoPECraft) to improve stability and visual quality. The paper provides a formal attention-logit analysis showing that query warping uniquely concentrates target-to-source attention, unlike key warping or joint query-key warping which dilute the signal.

## Results

On the Wan 2.2 TI2V-5B backbone, QWERTY achieves FID 45.95 and FVD 0.667 (×10³) for object motion control on VIPSeg, and FID 35.56 and FVD 1.007 (×10³) for camera motion control on DL3DV, making it the best training-free method on both tasks. For camera control, QWERTY surpasses the fine-tuned GWTF baseline on FVD (1.007 vs. 1.576) and FTD, while remaining competitive on object control (FVD 0.667 vs. 0.645). QWERTY also improves over vanilla Wan on FVD for both tasks (0.667 vs. 0.677 for object; 1.007 vs. 1.362 for camera), indicating that query warping enhances rather than degrades generative fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among U-Net-based training-free methods, QWERTY substantially outperforms all baselines: FreeTraj (FID 126.8, FVD 0.924) and MOFT (FID 76.04/51.84, FVD 0.861/1.327) are far behind on both FID and FVD, while SG-I2V on U-Net (FID 60.83/45.18) also trails QWERTY. When these U-Net strategies are naively adapted to the DiT backbone, they fail catastrophically: Noise Warping collapses generation (FID 270–283, FVD 3.4–5.7), and SG-I2V produces mostly static videos with degraded FID (108/42). Against the fine-tuned GWTF baseline, QWERTY is competitive on object control (FVD 0.667 vs. 0.645; FID 45.95 vs. 40.19) but clearly wins on camera control, achieving FVD 1.007 vs. 1.576 and lower FTD, suggesting that fine-tuning degrades the backbone's generalization on real-world scenes. MotionClone, which requires a reference video, is outperformed by QWERTY on camera control (FVD 1.007 vs. 1.624) without needing any reference. Overall, QWERTY is the strongest training-free method on both tasks and narrows the gap to fine-tuning-based approaches to within a few points on FVD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of training-free motion control in video diffusion from U-Net architectures to diffusion transformers (DiTs), which employ 3D full attention rather than factorized spatial-temporal attention. Prior training-free strategies (Noise Warping, SG-I2V, FreeTraj) were designed for U-Nets and, as the paper demonstrates, fail or produce static outputs when naively ported to DiTs. The key conceptual departure is manipulating the query component of 3D full attention directly (query warping) rather than warping input noise or optimizing latents with bounding-box losses, and the STCD decomposition addresses a DiT-specific challenge: frame-inconsistent query channels introduced by temporal RoPE. The self-guided latent optimization borrows the Fourier phase-constraint idea from RoPECraft, but applies it to a query-warped prediction rather than a reference-video prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As DiTs (Wan, CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo) become the dominant video generation architecture, a training-free control mechanism that preserves the pretrained backbone's generative quality is practically important for avoiding the cost and degradation of fine-tuning. The finding that U-Net-based training-free methods fundamentally fail on DiTs (Noise Warping collapses, SG-I2V produces static videos) is a useful negative result that redirects the community away from porting those approaches. Open questions include whether query warping generalizes to architectures without 3D full attention (e.g., factorized attention DiTs), whether STCD is needed for other types of feature manipulation beyond motion control, and whether the latent optimization step can be made cheaper for real-time applications. The method's dependence on optical flow for camera control (even if depth-estimated) remains a practical constraint compared to purely parametric camera specifications.
