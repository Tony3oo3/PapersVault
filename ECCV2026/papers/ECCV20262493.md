## Problem

Representative 3DGS-based few-shot NVS methods (e.g., FSGS, CoR-GS) initialize Gaussians using dense fused stereo points computed from extended views beyond the sparse training set, which constitutes data leakage and violates the few-shot task definition. When these methods are forced to rely solely on sparse training views or random points, their performance degrades substantially. The paper identifies this gap as 'sparsity discrepancy' and asks how to achieve high-quality few-shot NVS with 3DGS using only information from the limited training views.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2–4 sparse training views (RGB images) along with their camera poses, and additionally leverages a pre-trained monocular depth estimation network to produce pseudo ground-truth depth maps for importance scoring during initialization. No data from extended or held-out views is used at any stage. The output is a set of 3D Gaussians (position, scale, rotation, opacity, SH color coefficients) that can be rendered via differentiable rasterization to synthesize photorealistic novel-view images at arbitrary camera poses.

## Method

ReInGS performs a two-stage coarse-to-fine re-initialization of 3D Gaussians. In the coarse stage, an Expansion-based Hybrid Point Sampling (EHPS) strategy scales the scene bounding volume by a factor γ and samples dense points both uniformly and randomly within the expanded space, initializing a first Gaussian set θ₁ that is pre-optimized with hard depth regularization to capture global geometric structure. In the fine stage, a View-dominant Details-aware Point Sampling (VDPS) strategy operates in two sequential sub-steps: (i) Intra-View Point Sampling (IVPS) computes per-pixel importance scores combining normalized depth discrepancy and accumulated transmittance to select ~10% of pixels from rendered images, back-projects them to 3D points, and initializes a second Gaussian set θ₂; (ii) Cross-View Point Sampling (CVPS) scores each Gaussian in θ₂ by its weighted pixel coverage across all training views and stochastically selects ~40% to form the final re-initialized set Θ. A final optimization with soft depth regularization and photometric loss then refines appearance and geometry. The key insight is that dense initialization shortens Gaussian transport paths during optimization, mitigating local optima in the implicit GMM fitting, while the coarse-to-fine hierarchy separates global structure capture from local detail refinement.

## Results

On LLFF without sparsity discrepancy, ReInGS achieves PSNR of 19.39 / 20.88 / 22.24 dB under 2-view / 3-view / 4-view settings, outperforming the second-best method (DNGaussian at 16.47 / 18.87 / 20.86 dB) by up to 2.92 dB in PSNR. On DTU (3-view, no SD), ReInGS reaches 21.30 dB PSNR and 0.081 AVGE, surpassing the next-best FewViewGS (19.13 dB) by 2.17 dB. Notably, ReInGS without any data leakage from extended views matches or exceeds methods that exploit sparsity discrepancy (e.g., CoR-GS with SD: 20.45 dB on LLFF 3-view vs. ReInGS 20.88 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReInGS outperforms all 10 baselines on LLFF and DTU in the fair (no sparsity discrepancy) setting. Among 3DGS-based methods without SD, DNGaussian is the closest competitor (18.87 dB on LLFF 3-view vs. ReInGS 20.88 dB, a 2.01 dB gap), while CoR-GS drops sharply from 20.45 dB (with SD) to 16.32 dB (without SD), revealing heavy reliance on extended-view data. FewViewGS (18.96 dB, 3-view only) is the second-best 3DGS method without SD but still trails ReInGS by 1.92 dB. NeRF-based baselines (RegNeRF, FlipNeRF, FreeNeRF, SparseNeRF) all fall below 20.33 dB PSNR on LLFF 3-view, confirming that 3DGS-based approaches with proper initialization are superior in the few-shot regime. On DTU 3-view, ReInGS (21.30 dB) beats FewViewGS (19.13 dB) by 2.17 dB and DNGaussian (18.85 dB) by 2.45 dB, while also achieving the best AVGE (0.081 vs. 0.110 for FewViewGS). The key finding is that ReInGS without any data leakage matches or exceeds methods that exploit sparsity discrepancy (e.g., CoR-GS with SD at 20.45 dB on LLFF 3-view), validating the coarse-to-fine re-initialization as a principled alternative to extended-view point clouds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for few-shot novel view synthesis, departing from methods like FSGS, DNGaussian, and CoR-GS that initialize Gaussians using dense fused stereo points from extended (non-training) views. The key conceptual departure is identifying this practice as 'sparsity discrepancy'—a form of data leakage that violates the few-shot premise—and proposing a two-stage coarse-to-fine re-initialization (EHPS for global structure, VDPS for local details) that relies solely on training-view information. The coarse-to-fine design draws inspiration from progressive denoising in diffusion models, while the VDPS strategy adapts image-order and object-order rendering principles to Gaussian importance-based sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By exposing that several published few-shot 3DGS methods implicitly rely on extended-view data for initialization, this paper reframes how the community should evaluate and design few-shot NVS methods, with significant implications for reproducibility and fair comparison. The practical impact is a method that achieves SOTA performance (up to 2.92 dB PSNR gain in 2-view) without data leakage, making it a more honest baseline for future work. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 4 views, whether the computational overhead of multi-stage sampling can be reduced, and whether the hollows/cracks limitation can be addressed without reintroducing information from non-training views.
