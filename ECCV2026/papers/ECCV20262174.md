## Problem

Existing Transformer-based 3D point cloud segmentation methods operate under a deterministic point-wise prediction paradigm that assumes uniform spatial reliability, causing semantic inconsistencies in geometric voids such as occlusions, sparse boundaries, and occluded surfaces. The root cause is that conventional discrete feature aggregation is isotropic and lacks the directional awareness needed to selectively propagate reliable semantic information across these discrete gaps. The paper identifies that segmentation errors are highly correlated with predictive uncertainty in geometrically complex regions, motivating an uncertainty-guided propagation strategy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud with per-point RGB color (p_i ∈ R^6) as input and produces per-point semantic class labels as output. During inference, the backbone (PTv3) is run K times with Monte Carlo Dropout enabled to estimate per-point predictive uncertainty, which then drives the refinement pipeline. The refinement operates only on uncertainty-guided regions rather than all points, and the final per-point prediction is obtained by concatenating the region-level refined feature with each point's original backbone feature before a shared segmentation head.

## Method

UGSP transitions from discrete point processing to continuous geometric field reconstruction in three stages. First, Monte Carlo Dropout (K stochastic passes) yields per-point uncertainty, which is voxelized and partitioned into high- and low-uncertainty regions via an agglomerative region-growing algorithm using a distance-uncertainty joint cost. Second, directed paths are constructed from each high-uncertainty region centroid to its k-nearest low-uncertainty anchors, with N_s aggregation centers sampled along each path under a density-aware adaptive radius constraint. Third, at each aggregation center, a Spherical Harmonics (SH)-based mechanism encodes the directional distribution of surrounding point features up to order L, applies a learned Gaussian radial weight for aggregation, and concatenates a trajectory-direction encoding (MLP of the 1D path displacement) to form per-sphere tokens; a Transformer then models the full path sequence, and a cross-attention module uses path features as Queries against low-uncertainty region features as Keys/Values to inject reliable context into uncertain regions.

## Results

On indoor benchmarks, UGSP achieves 77.9% mIoU on ScanNet V2 (+1.1% over PTv3) and 75.6% mIoU on S3DIS Area-5 (+2.3% over PTv3). On outdoor benchmarks, it reaches 82.2% mIoU on nuScenes (+2.0%), 73.8% on SemanticKITTI (+1.5%), and 72.1% on Waymo (+0.8%) over the PTv3 baseline. The method consistently outperforms all 3D-only and uncertainty-aware baselines across four of five datasets, with the sole exception of Waymo where Swin3D (73.5%) edges ahead by 1.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UGSP outperforms the strongest baseline PTv3 on four of five datasets: +1.1% on ScanNet V2 (77.9 vs 76.8), +2.3% on S3DIS (75.6 vs 73.3), +2.0% on nuScenes (82.2 vs 80.2), and +1.5% on SemanticKITTI (73.8 vs 72.3), but trails PTv3 by 0.8% on Waymo (72.1 vs 71.3) and is further behind Swin3D (73.5) on that dataset. Against 3D-only baselines, UGSP leads PointNeXt by 6.4–15.8% mIoU across datasets, OA-CNNs by 3.3–3.5% on outdoor sets, and ST by 3.6–5.6% on indoor sets. The uncertainty-aware SalsaNext is substantially outperformed (by 16–22% mIoU), confirming that the paper's structured uncertainty-driven propagation is far more effective than SalsaNext's simpler uncertainty estimation. The largest absolute gains appear on S3DIS (+2.3% over PTv3) and nuScenes (+2.0%), suggesting the method is most beneficial in scenes with complex indoor layouts and dense multi-sensor outdoor geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the anisotropic Gaussian modeling from 3D Gaussian Splatting (originally for radiance field rendering) into a semantic propagation mechanism for point cloud segmentation, and couples it with Spherical Harmonics encoding to capture directional feature distributions. It departs from prior uncertainty-aware segmentation work (SalsaNet, SalsaNext) by treating predictive uncertainty not merely as a confidence signal but as a structural prior that dictates where and how to propagate information via continuous spatial paths. The backbone is PTv3, and the overall pipeline extends the Transformer-based segmentation paradigm with a post-hoc, uncertainty-guided refinement stage rather than modifying the backbone architecture itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core idea—using uncertainty to identify geometric voids and then propagating reliable semantics along direction-aware continuous paths—offers a principled alternative to isotropic neighborhood aggregation, and the self-contained design (no external 2D foundation models) is practical for deployment. However, the gains are modest (1.1–2.3% mIoU over PTv3), the 16-pass Monte Carlo inference adds non-trivial latency, and the method is validated only on a single backbone (PTv3), leaving open whether the uncertainty-guided propagation generalizes across architectures. A key open question is whether the SH-based directional encoding provides benefits beyond what simpler anisotropic kernels (e.g., learned directional convolutions) could achieve at lower cost.
