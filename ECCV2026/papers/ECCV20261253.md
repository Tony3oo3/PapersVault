## Problem

Many existing document VQA benchmarks suffer from a critical limitation: their visual content is often reducible to structured text via OCR, layout analysis, or code representations, allowing models to achieve high accuracy through linguistic shortcuts rather than genuine visual grounding. Maps inherently resist this textual reduction because their core semantics reside in spatial topology, symbolic conventions, and continuous graphical features that cannot be adequately captured by linear text descriptions, yet no comprehensive benchmark specifically targets this irreducible visual-centric reasoning.

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs a map image (native resolutions ranging from 233×464 to 11,811×9,442 pixels, mean 2,055×1,705) with a question and answer choices in three objective formats: single-choice (69.70%), multiple-choice (12.83%), and ordering (17.46%). Questions are stratified into three capability levels—perception (L1, 21.04%), single-step spatial reasoning (L2, 38.26%), and multi-step relational reasoning (L3, 40.70%)—and are bilingual (60.78% English, 39.22% Chinese). The VDI protocol additionally produces a question-agnostic text description of each image under a configurable token budget (64–1024 tokens), from which a text-only accuracy is computed to derive the normalized accuracy drop (VDI) and the average token utilization ratio (DSI).

## Method

OmniMapBench is constructed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) acquiring 5,000+ map images via LLM-guided web scraping and targeted manual sourcing, then filtering to ~2,000 by removing redundancy, low resolution, and sensitive content; (2) having professional annotators create ~3,386 QA pairs under strict guidelines requiring answers derivable solely from visual information and spanning three predefined reasoning levels; and (3) cross-validating 2,882 candidate pairs with two independent annotation teams, yielding the final 2,096 verified pairs across 1,603 images in 9 categories. The key methodological contribution is the Visual Dependency Index (VDI), defined as the normalized accuracy drop when the reference LVLM (gpt-4.1-2025-04-14) answers from a question-agnostic text description instead of the original image, parameterized by a token budget k_max. A complementary Descriptive Saturation Index (DSI) measures the average fraction of the token budget actually consumed, revealing how descriptively dense each benchmark's content is. Evaluation of 25 LVLMs uses Chain-of-Thought prompting with strict exact-match scoring, and the VDI protocol is applied uniformly across OmniMapBench and ten established benchmarks to enable direct comparison of visual dependency.

## Results

On OmniMapBench, the best-performing model (Gemini-3.1-Pro) achieves 75.03% final accuracy, with a clear performance gradient across reasoning levels: 88.24% (Level 1, perception), 81.53% (Level 2, single-step), and 62.88% (Level 3, multi-step). Among open-source models, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B reaches 72.22%, narrowing the gap to the top proprietary model to under 3 percentage points. A no-image ablation shows average accuracy collapsing from 58.87% to 23.35%, confirming the benchmark cannot be solved via textual shortcuts. On the Visual Dependency Index (VDI) at k_max=1024, OmniMapBench registers 0.338—the highest among all 11 benchmarks evaluated—while established benchmarks like AI2D (0.019) and MMBench (0.028) drop to near-zero, indicating their tasks are largely reducible to text.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the VDI metric (measuring resistance to textual reduction), OmniMapBench substantially outperforms all 10 compared benchmarks at every token budget. At k_max=1024, OmniMapBench achieves VDI=0.338 versus AI2D (0.019), MMBench (0.028), MMMU (0.093), DocVQA (0.076), and OCRBench (0.043), indicating its map-based tasks cannot be solved by generating a textual description of the image. The Descriptive Saturation Index (DSI) further confirms this: OmniMapBench maintains DSI=0.629 at k_max=1024 (second highest), while OCRBench (0.124) and MMBench (0.195) show low saturation, meaning their content is easily summarized in fewer tokens. Among the 25 evaluated LVLMs, Gemini-3.1-Pro leads at 75.03%, followed by Gemini-2.5-Pro (61.40%) and GPT-5 (56.54%); notably, open-source Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (72.22%) nearly matches the top proprietary model, outperforming GPT-5, GPT-4.1, and Claude-Opus-4-1 by 5–30 percentage points. Performance degrades sharply from Level 1 to Level 3 across all models (e.g., Gemini-3.1-Pro drops from 88.24% to 62.88%), identifying multi-step spatial reasoning as the principal bottleneck. The no-image baseline (23.35% average) versus full-image setting (58.87% average) confirms that language priors and answer-choice bias alone are insufficient to solve OmniMapBench questions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in document VQA benchmarks (DocVQA, ChartQA, InfoVQA) by arguing that their visual content is often reducible to structured text, enabling high scores without genuine visual grounding. It carves new ground by selecting maps as a document category that inherently resists textual reduction due to spatial topology, symbolic conventions, and continuous graphical features, and by proposing the Visual Dependency Index (VDI) as a formal, benchmark-level metric to quantify this resistance. Prior map-specific benchmarks (ReasonMap for transit maps, MapQA for choropleth maps) are narrow in scope; OmniMapBench generalizes across nine visually diverse map categories. The VDI metric repurposes the idea of text-mediated inference (description-then-answer) into a standardized diagnostic, building on the observation that RAG pipelines routinely convert documents to markdown.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VDI provides the field with a simple, reproducible diagnostic to assess whether any given benchmark actually demands visual reasoning or can be shortcut through textualization — a concern that has been raised qualitatively but never quantified in a standardized way. The finding that even the strongest model (Gemini-3.1-Pro) plateaus at 75.03%, with multi-step spatial reasoning (Level 3) dropping to ~63%, highlights a concrete bottleneck for LVLM development. Limitations include the relatively small scale (2,096 QA pairs), dependence on a single reference model for VDI computation (though ablation shows consistency), and the restriction to map documents, leaving open whether the VDI framework generalizes to other visually irreducible domains. The narrowing gap between top open-source (Qwen3.5-397B at 72.22%) and proprietary models suggests the benchmark will remain useful as a differentiator for the next generation of LVLMs.
