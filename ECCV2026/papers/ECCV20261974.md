## Problem

Existing Knowledge-Intensive VQA benchmarks report only end-task accuracy, making it impossible to attribute failures to specific pipeline stages (visual grounding, object identification, or knowledge retrieval/reasoning). Additionally, most KI-VQA datasets rely on salient, centered objects and minimal clutter, failing to capture the visual complexity of real-world egocentric scenes where targets are small, off-center, or surrounded by distractors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261974/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs an egocentric RGB image (mimicking wearable-device captures) with a text question requiring external world knowledge. The benchmark provides stage-specific outputs: predicted bounding boxes (evaluated by IoU > 0.5) for grounding, entity names (evaluated by exact match or recall@10) for identification, and final answers (evaluated by LLM-as-judge) for end-task QA. Unusually, every sample carries rich metadata—target ROIs, Wikipedia URLs, referring expression type labels, disambiguated and text-only question variants, and four visual complexity scores (object size, distance to center, scene crowdedness, and a composite saliency metric)—enabling controlled ablations at each stage.

## Method

CRAG-MM-Diagnostics augments the CRAG-MM benchmark with human-annotated stage-specific metadata (bounding boxes, entity names, referring expression types, disambiguated/text-only queries) and automated visual complexity scores, yielding 1,149 samples across 13 domains. The paper evaluates three pipeline stages independently: (1) language-based visual grounding via IoU, (2) object identification via exact-match accuracy for VLMs and recall@10 for specialized retrievers, and (3) knowledge retrieval and reasoning via end-task QA accuracy, using both fully parametric VLMs (GPT-5, Qwen2.5-VL, Llama-3.2) and specialized models (Grounding-DINO, CLIP, VLM2Vec). Leveraging the diagnostic findings, the authors propose a grounded bimodal RAG pipeline that inserts a Grounding-DINO cropping module upstream of image retrieval, so the retriever operates on a clean target crop rather than the full cluttered scene. This grounded pipeline boosts GPT-5 and Qwen32B accuracies by 13.3 and 8.5 percentage points respectively, while the text-only question metadata reveals that knowledge retrieval and reasoning—not visual grounding or object identification—remains the dominant bottleneck even when the correct entity name is provided.

## Results

On the CRAG-MM-Diagnostics benchmark (1,149 samples), GPT-5 achieves 48.9% zero-shot KI-VQA accuracy (txt+img) and 59.6% with text-only queries, while Qwen-32B reaches 26.2% and 34.7% respectively. The proposed grounded bimodal RAG pipeline (Grounding-DINO + CLIP image retrieval + BGE text retrieval) boosts GPT-5 to 62.2% (+13.3 pp) and Qwen-32B to 34.7% (+8.5 pp). In the visual grounding stage, Qwen2.5-VL-72B leads at 67.8% IoU>0.5 accuracy, while the 0.2B Grounding-DINO rivals it; in object identification, GPT-5 still fails on ~40% of examples, and only 20.9% of GPT-5's end-task errors are resolved by providing the ground-truth entity name, confirming knowledge retrieval and reasoning as the dominant bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among generalized VLMs, Qwen2.5-VL (3B–72B) dominates the visual grounding stage, peaking at 67.8% IoU>0.5, while Llama-3.2-11B and GPT-5(-mini) trail significantly, revealing that spatial grounding does not emerge naturally in SOTA proprietary models. The 0.2B Grounding-DINO rivals the Qwen family in overall grounding accuracy, making it a highly efficient choice for modular pipelines, though it underperforms on knowledge-intensive and ambiguous referring expressions. For object identification, GPT-5 achieves the highest accuracy among generalized models but still fails on ~40% of examples; CLIP-ViT-Large-Patch14-336 (427M) is the strongest specialized retriever relative to its size, while the larger multimodal retrievers VLM2Vec-V2.0 and Qwen-3-VL-Embedding-2B do not benefit from adding the text question, indicating limited linguistic grounding. In the end-task KI-VQA comparison, the grounded bimodal RAG pipeline outperforms zero-shot GPT-5 by 13.3 pp (48.9→62.2%) and zero-shot Qwen-32B by 8.5 pp (26.2→34.7%), with image retrieval alone contributing a consistent 6.8-point average boost. Critically, even with ground-truth target regions, the full pipeline still lags behind the text-only retrieval baseline (e.g., GPT-5: 62.2% vs. 73.3%), confirming that fine-grained object identification and multi-hop reasoning over retrieved evidence remain unresolved bottlenecks across all compared systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CRAG-MM, a multimodal RAG benchmark for egocentric, visually complex scenes, by adding stage-wise annotations (bounding boxes, entity names, referring expression types, disambiguated and text-only queries, visual complexity scores) that transform it from an end-to-end benchmark into a diagnostic framework. It departs from prior KI-VQA benchmarks (OK-VQA, EncyclopedicVQA, InfoSeek, ViQuAE) that assume object recognition is trivial and report only final QA accuracy, and from diagnostic evaluation suites (MMVet, HallusionBench, Prism) that do not target knowledge-intensive information-seeking specifically. The grounded bimodal RAG pipeline it proposes connects to region-aware retrieval work but is the first to systematically integrate a visual grounding module upstream of image retrieval within a KI-VQA evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that knowledge retrieval and reasoning—not visual grounding or object identification—accounts for the majority of KI-VQA errors (only 20.9% of GPT-5's errors are resolved by providing the correct entity name) redirects the field's attention toward improving retrieval quality and multi-hop reasoning over retrieved evidence. The grounded bimodal RAG pipeline, which boosts GPT-5 and Qwen by 13.3 and 8.5 points respectively, offers a concrete, modular recipe for practitioners. Open questions include whether the egocentric, single-turn setting generalizes to multi-turn or non-egocentric scenarios, whether the 1,149-sample benchmark is large enough for stable per-subset conclusions, and whether the LLM-as-judge evaluation introduces systematic biases that could mask or inflate stage-specific error rates.
