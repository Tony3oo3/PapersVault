## Problem

Existing positional encodings for multi-view transformers (CaPE, GTA, PRoPE) fail to simultaneously satisfy four key desiderata: SE(3) invariance, patch uniqueness across overlapping views, adaptiveness to underlying 3D scene geometry, and multi-frequency similarity. Camera-matrix-based encodings ensure invariance but ignore geometry and lack frequency modulation, while naive RoPE on 3D ray coordinates breaks invariance and uniqueness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261427/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view images with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and optionally ground-truth depth maps for reference views. Each image patch is represented as a ray segment parameterized by the camera center and a 3D point at a predicted depth along the ray; this is projected into the query camera frame to yield a 6D vector [c_x, c_y, c_z, u, v, 1/d']. The output is a set of RoPE rotation matrices applied to query, key, value, and output features in each attention layer, with per-token depth and uncertainty predicted by a lightweight linear projection from token features (no external depth supervision).

## Method

RayRoPE encodes each patch as a ray segment (camera center c, point p^d at predicted depth d) and projects all rays into the query token's camera frame via the query's projection matrix P_i = K_i T_i, yielding a 6D coordinate that is then encoded with multi-frequency RoPE. This projection ensures the attention score depends only on relative positions in the query frame, guaranteeing SE(3) invariance while preserving multi-frequency similarity. To handle depth ambiguity, the model predicts an uncertainty σ alongside each depth and computes an analytical expected RoPE over a uniform distribution on [d−σ, d+σ], which smooths high-frequency components when confidence is low. The depth d is predicted per-layer by a single linear projection from token features with no direct supervision, allowing geometrically meaningful depth maps to emerge during training. The full encoding is applied to q, k, v, and o features, and the implementation groups attention by query view to keep runtime within ~4–13% of standard RoPE baselines.

## Results

On novel-view synthesis (LVSM framework), RayRoPE achieves PSNR 18.40 / LPIPS 0.461 / SSIM 0.592 on CO3D, 22.42 / 0.110 / 0.905 on Objaverse, and 26.07 / 0.085 / 0.831 on RE10K, outperforming the strongest baseline PRoPE (17.49 / 0.539 / 0.563, 22.16 / 0.123 / 0.896, 24.48 / 0.112 / 0.784) across all three datasets. The headline improvement is a 24% relative reduction in LPIPS on RE10K (0.085 vs. 0.112). At larger scale (~150M params), RayRoPE further extends its lead, reaching PSNR 28.31 / LPIPS 0.055 on RE10K. On stereo depth estimation, RayRoPE reduces Abs Rel to 0.095 on unseen ScanNet (vs. 0.101 for PRoPE), and on feed-forward 3DGS reconstruction it improves PSNR to 24.63 with 6 views (vs. 24.09 for PRoPE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RayRoPE consistently outperforms all six baselines across every task and dataset. Against the strongest camera-based relative encoding PRoPE [22], RayRoPE gains +0.91 dB PSNR and 24% relative LPIPS reduction on RE10K (0.085 vs. 0.112), and similar margins on CO3D (+0.91 dB) and Objaverse (+0.26 dB). Against GTA [28], which hybridizes camera extrinsics with 2D patch-index RoPE, RayRoPE improves by up to +1.9 dB PSNR on CO3D (18.40 vs. 16.50), demonstrating the benefit of ray-based geometry over pixel-index-based encoding. The naive RoPE-on-rays baseline (applying RoPE directly to global ray coordinates) is competitive on Objaverse but degrades sharply on CO3D (16.85 vs. 18.40 PSNR), confirming the necessity of SE(3) invariance. On stereo depth (UniMatch [50]), RayRoPE reduces Abs Rel from 0.106 (PRoPE) to 0.106 on RGBD and from 0.101 to 0.095 on unseen ScanNet. On feed-forward 3DGS (TokenGS [35]), RayRoPE improves PSNR by +0.61 dB at 6 views (24.63 vs. 24.09 for PRoPE), and on multi-view diffusion (EscherNet [20]) the gap widens with more views, reaching +0.67 dB PSNR at 9 views (19.03 vs. 18.36). The original Plucker raymap concatenation [16] is the weakest baseline, confirming that absolute encodings are inferior to relative ray-based encodings for multi-view attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

RayRoPE extends the RoPE (Rotary Position Embedding) framework from 1D/2D/3D coordinate positions to a ray-segment representation in multi-view transformers, while building on the SE(3)-invariant camera-based relative encodings of CaPE, GTA, and PRoPE. The key departure is that prior camera-based methods (CaPE, GTA, PRoPE) encode positions via camera extrinsics or patch indices, which either lack multi-frequency similarity or break uniqueness across overlapping views; RayRoPE instead projects ray segments (camera center + a predicted depth point) into the query camera frame and applies standard RoPE to the resulting 6D coordinates, unifying invariance, uniqueness, geometric adaptiveness, and multi-frequency encoding in a single mechanism. The expected-RoPE formulation for modeling depth uncertainty is a novel analytical extension not present in prior RoPE variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RayRoPE provides a drop-in positional encoding that satisfies four desiderata simultaneously for any multi-view attention module, and the consistent gains across NVS, stereo depth, 3DGS reconstruction, and multi-view diffusion suggest it will become a default component in multi-view transformer architectures. The emergent, unsupervised depth prediction within the attention mechanism is a notable side effect: the model learns geometrically plausible depth maps purely to improve positional encoding, opening the question of whether such implicit geometry can be leveraged for downstream tasks. Open challenges remain for unposed or mixed-posed inputs, modeling uncertainty in camera matrices themselves, and whether the per-layer depth prediction scales efficiently to very large view counts.
