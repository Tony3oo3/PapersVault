## Problem

Existing generative approaches to eyeglasses removal (diffusion models, GANs) rely on stochastic priors that lack explicit geometric constraints, causing identity drift, gaze shifts, and expression changes in the restored ocular region. When applied frame-by-frame to video, these unconstrained generative processes also produce temporal flickering. The paper addresses how to transfer the photorealistic knowledge of large-scale generative models into a deterministic, real-time restoration architecture that inverts the specific refractive and reflective distortions introduced by lenses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB frame (or image) at 518×518 resolution containing eyeglasses and produces the corresponding glasses-free face in pixel space, operating as a feed-forward network without multi-frame propagation or flow estimation. Because it works directly in pixel space rather than in a latent autoencoder bottleneck, it preserves high-frequency details like skin texture and eye geometry that latent-space methods tend to lose. The network is applied independently per frame, with temporal coherence enforced through a training-time translation equivariance constraint rather than explicit inter-frame modeling.

## Method

The pipeline first generates synthetic clean/glasses-wearing face pairs using Nano Banana (Gemini 3 Pro Image) across 13 poses and expressions, then applies a three-stage filtering process (background L1, ocular L1, and a U-Net reconstruction threshold) to discard pairs with pose, gaze, or identity drift, yielding 1,860 curated identities. During training, a physics-based augmentation simulates lens refraction via ray tracing through two spherical surfaces (curvatures derived from target diopter strength using Vogel's Rule and Snell's law with n≈1.5) and adds HDR specular reflections sampled from a generated inverse-view environment map, creating diverse paired data from the limited curated set. The restoration network JFSnet combines a frozen-then-fine-tuned DINOv2 ViT-L/14 encoder with a ResNet convolutional decoder and a direct input skip connection, bypassing latent bottlenecks. Training uses a composite loss including global and eye-localized L1/perceptual terms, a PatchGAN adversarial term, and a translation equivariance constraint that forces the network to commute with spatial shifts, suppressing temporal flickering without multi-frame propagation.

## Results

On the curated FFHQ evaluation set (12,163 clear-glasses images), JFSnet achieves the best FID of 0.379, the lowest pupil displacement of 2.249±1.957 px, the lowest landmark L2 error of 0.632±0.271 px, and the highest inference speed of 27.68 FPS — outperforming all 10 quantitative baselines on every metric simultaneously. The closest competitors are Nano Banana (FID 0.389) and Inpaint Anything (FID 0.391, pupil 2.259 px), while the speed advantage over the next-fastest method (Take-Off-Eyeglasses at 13.31 FPS) is more than 2×. In perceptual studies on 60 CelebV-Text video clips and 60 FFHQ portraits with 37 participants, the method is consistently preferred across gaze consistency, temporal stability, identity preservation, and overall restoration quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JFSnet outperforms all 10 quantitative baselines on every reported metric (FID, pupil displacement, landmark L2, and FPS). The closest quantitative competitors are Nano Banana (FID 0.389, pupil 2.854 px) and Inpaint Anything (FID 0.391, pupil 2.259 px), both of which are marginally beaten on FID and pupil error but far behind on speed (1.29 and 1.84 FPS vs. 27.68 FPS). Among video inpainting methods, ProPainter (FID 0.408, 8.77 FPS) and Flow-Guided Transformer (FID 0.569, 1.28 FPS) are the strongest, yet JFSnet still leads on all four metrics. Diffusion-based editors (LEDITS, InstructPix2Pix, TokenFlow, RAVE, IP-FaceDiff) show substantially worse structural fidelity (L2 errors of 1.5–21 px) and are 100–700× slower. In the perceptual study, Runway Gen-4.5 and V-LASIK are preferred less than JFSnet on gaze consistency and temporal stability, while STTN and ProPainter leave visible ghosting artifacts. Notably, Nano Banana — the very generative model used to create the training data — still suffers from gaze drift (2.854 px) and identity shifts, validating the need for the physics-grounded deterministic transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of generative face editing, video inpainting, and computational optics. Rather than applying stochastic generative priors (diffusion models, GANs) directly to the removal task as prior work (ByeglassesGAN, V-LASIK, IP-FaceDiff) does, it reframes glasses removal as an offline knowledge-transfer problem: a large generative model (Nano Banana) produces curated training pairs, which are then augmented with physically-based lens optics (Snell's-law refraction, HDR specular reflection) before training a compact deterministic restoration network (JFSnet). The temporal consistency mechanism—enforcing translation equivariance rather than relying on optical flow or multi-frame propagation—departs from the flow-guided inpainting lineage (ProPainter, FGT, STTN) and the diffusion-inversion lineage (TokenFlow, RAVE), offering a simpler and faster alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 27.68 FPS inference speed makes high-fidelity glasses removal practical for real-time video pipelines, a significant gap over all compared baselines (0.04–13.31 FPS). The generative-to-deterministic transfer paradigm—using a powerful but slow generative model purely as a data engine for a lightweight feed-forward restorer—is a broadly applicable recipe that could extend to other occlusion-removal or attribute-editing tasks. Limitations include reliance on monocular depth estimation (scale ambiguity resolved by assuming standard head width), a physics model that assumes standard spherical lens geometry, and training data entirely synthesized by a single generative model, raising questions about robustness to unusual lens shapes, very strong prescriptions, or non-frontal viewing geometries beyond the three-quarter profile boundary the authors explicitly down-weight.
