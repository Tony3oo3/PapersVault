## Problem

Current text-to-video diffusion models produce locally convincing frames but systematically fail on interaction-level correctness: objects move before contact, intended actions are skipped, placed objects keep drifting, and support relations break. The root cause is that standard frame-first denoising updates every latent region at every diffusion step, even when the prompt implies only a local, temporally bounded interaction should be active. Scaling model size alone does not reliably fix these causal-ordering errors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a 128-frame video clip at 24 fps with a 256×256 base generation resolution (upsampled to 720p for visualization), generated in a compressed temporal-autoencoder latent space. EVD modifies only the direction field passed to the ODE solver at each of K=50 sampling steps; the solver family, decoder, NFE budget, and CFG structure remain identical to the base DiT model, so no post-hoc filtering or re-encoding is required.

## Method

EVD attaches a lightweight event head (≈6.5–12 M parameters, <0.2% of the backbone) to the final DiT token features, predicting a token-aligned activity map that localizes where an interaction is active in space and time. Training augments the standard Flow Matching velocity loss with three event-grounded terms: a realization loss that suppresses update energy in inactive tokens, a consistency loss that enforces stable update directions across nearby diffusion times within active regions, and an ordering loss that penalizes pre-event motion and post-event drift. At inference, the smoothed activity is converted into a stable gate via soft sigmoid activation combined with a binary hysteresis state (τ_on=0.62, τ_off=0.38), and a scheduled annealing factor (full gating for t≤0.60, linear decay thereafter) modulates the CFG-guided direction field before the solver step. This single gating mechanism—applied only to the velocity field—corrects pre-contact motion, missing interaction responses, and post-event drift without altering the solver, decoder, or sampling budget.

## Results

On EVD-Bench, DiT-4B+EVD achieves 96.4% human Dynamics preference (vs. 80.3% for DiT-4B) and 94.8 VBench Dynamics (vs. 78.9), while keeping VBench Appearance nearly unchanged (76.2 vs. 75.4). At scale, DiT-30B+EVD reaches 97.1% human Dynamics and 95.7 VBench Dynamics (vs. 79.5% and 88.7 for DiT-30B), with Appearance improving modestly from 73.8 to 78.1. The method adds only 0.04–0.16% parameters and ~2% inference overhead while using the same solver, step budget (K=50), and CFG structure as the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CogVideoX variants, DiT-4B+EVD wins on every reported metric: it surpasses CogVideo2B by 8.7 points in human Text Faithfulness (88.9 vs. 80.2), 6.3 in Dynamics (96.4 vs. 89.7), and 7.2 in VBench Dynamics (94.8 vs. 87.6), while also leading CogVideo5B by 23.5 points in human Dynamics (96.4 vs. 72.5). Against PyramidFlow, EVD gains 13.1 points in human Dynamics (96.4 vs. 81.1) and 6.3 in VBench Dynamics (94.8 vs. 88.5), with a 13.1-point advantage in Text Faithfulness (88.9 vs. 75.8). At the 30B scale, DiT-30B+EVD outperforms all closed-source generators (Kling 3.0 Pro, Runway Gen-4.5, Veo 3.1, Sora 2 Pro, Mochi 1) on human Text Faithfulness, Quality, and Dynamics, as well as VBench Dynamics, while maintaining comparable or better Appearance scores. The consistent pattern is that EVD's gains are concentrated in Dynamics and Text Faithfulness—precisely the interaction-grounding dimensions—while Appearance remains within 1–2 points of the base model, confirming that the event gate corrects causal/temporal errors without degrading visual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

EVD is built directly on the DiT backbone [26] and Flow Matching training formulation [18], keeping both unchanged while inserting a lightweight event head and gated direction field. It departs from prior event-centric video work (GEST, GEST-Engine) that relies on symbolic event-graph specifications or simulation-style control, instead learning token-aligned event activity end-to-end within a pretrained DiT. It also differs from motion-steering and editing pipelines (VideoJAM, StreamDiffusion, ObjectAlign) by operating purely at generation time: the solver, decoder, NFE, and output-selection protocol remain identical, and only the direction field is modulated by the event gate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a sub-0.2% parameter addition can correct several persistent interaction failures (pre-contact motion, missing state changes, post-event drift) that scaling alone does not resolve, offering a practical, drop-in fix for any DiT-based video system. The self-constructed EVD-Bench provides a focused evaluation axis for interaction grounding that existing suites (VBench, T2V-CompBench) only partially capture. Key limitations remain for small contacts, occlusion, and cluttered multi-object scenes where the event signal becomes ambiguous, pointing toward object-centric or contact-aware event cues as the natural next step.
