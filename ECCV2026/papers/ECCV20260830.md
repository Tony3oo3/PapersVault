## Problem

Existing dense feature matchers face a fundamental trade-off: RoMa is robust to extreme appearance and viewpoint changes but is slow (18.5 pairs/s) and lacks multi-view context in its matcher, while UFM is faster but requires finetuning the backbone (degrading robustness) and loses subpixel precision. No prior method simultaneously achieves robustness to wide-baseline and multi-modal scenarios, subpixel-accurate correspondences, and a practical runtime/memory footprint.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images and produces bidirectional dense warps (H×W×2), per-pixel confidence masks (H×W×1), and per-pixel 2×2 precision matrices (H×W×2×2) at full resolution. The coarse matcher consumes frozen DINOv3 ViT-L patch features and operates at 4× downsampled resolution, while three cascaded CNN refiners progressively restore full resolution. The model is resolution-robust via RoPE on a normalized grid for the transformer and displacement rescaling for the refiners, and is trained on a mix of aspect ratios (512×512 through 688×384) for the matcher and fixed 640×640 for the refiners.

## Method

RoMa v2 uses a decoupled two-stage pipeline: a coarse Multi-view Transformer matcher (ViT-B with alternating frame-wise RoPE and global attention over frozen DINOv3 features) followed by three UNet-like CNN refiners at strides {4,2,1}. The key architectural change from RoMa is replacing the Gaussian Process regression with a single-headed Attention mechanism plus an auxiliary NLL loss that enforces correct nearest-patch assignment, enabling multi-view context to be learned effectively. A custom CUDA kernel for local correlation reduces refiner memory from 5.6 GB to 4.8 GB, and an Exponential Moving Average (α=0.999) over refiner weights eliminates a ~0.1px subpixel bias observed during training. The model additionally predicts a pixel-wise 2×2 precision matrix via Cholesky-constrained parameters trained with a Gaussian NLL loss, providing calibrated uncertainty for downstream geometry estimation. Training is two-stage (300k steps each for matcher and refiners at lr 4e-4) on a curated mixture of wide-baseline (MegaDepth, AerialMD, BlendedMVS) and small-baseline (FlyingThings3D, VirtualKITTI2) datasets to balance robustness and subpixel accuracy.

## Results

On MegaDepth-1500, RoMa v2 achieves AUC@5°/10°/20° of 62.8/77.0/86.6, outperforming all prior matchers and feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods (e.g., MASt3R: 42.4/61.5/76.9; Reloc3r: 49.6/67.9/81.2). On ScanNet-1500, it reaches 33.6/56.2/73.8, matching VGGT and MASt3R. In dense matching, RoMa v2 achieves 84% lower EPE than RoMa on AerialMegaDepth (4.12 vs. 25.05) and beats UFM on its own benchmark TA-WB (13.82 vs. 15.85 EPE). Runtime is 1.7× faster than RoMa (30.9 vs. 18.5 pairs/s) with a similar 4.8 GB memory footprint, versus UFM's 16.2 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoMa v2 outperforms its direct predecessor RoMa on all major benchmarks: +0.2/+0.3/+0.3 AUC on MegaDepth-1500, +1.8/+2.8/+2.9 on ScanNet-1500, and 84% lower EPE on AerialMegaDepth (4.12 vs. 25.05). Against UFM, RoMa v2 wins decisively on subpixel-precision tasks (MegaDepth EPE 1.47 vs. 3.15; MegaDepth-1500 AUC@5° 62.8 vs. 41.5) and on TA-WB (EPE 13.82 vs. 15.85), but is slightly slower in throughput (30.9 vs. 43.0 pairs/s) and uses less memory (4.8 vs. 16.2 GB). On the multi-modal WxBS benchmark, RoMa v2 (55.4 mAA@10px) trails RoMa (60.8) but substantially outperforms UFM (42.3), indicating a small robustness trade-off from the architectural changes. On RUBIK (geometric challenges), RoMa v2 sets a new SotA of 57.3% success rate, beating both RoMa (47.3%) and UFM (53.1%). On the new SatAst benchmark, RoMa v2 achieves 37.0 AUC@10px versus 23.5 for RoMa and 1.8 for UFM. In visual localization, RoMa v2 improves over RoMa by +13.6 Prec. AUC on Map-free and +7.1 DUC1 on InLoc, setting a new public leaderboard SotA on InLoc.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoMa v2 is a direct successor to RoMa (CVPR 2024), which itself extended DKM by replacing learned features with a frozen DINOv2 foundation model for robustness. The paper explicitly positions itself as combining the strengths of RoMa (robustness to extreme appearance changes via frozen backbone) and UFM (NeurIPS 2025; speed, two-stage training, diverse data mixture), while departing from RoMa's Gaussian Process regression in favor of a multi-view attention-based matcher and from UFM's fine-tuned backbone in favor of a frozen DINOv3 encoder. The predictive covariance output via Cholesky-constrained precision matrices is a novel addition not present in either predecessor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoMa v2 becomes the practical default for dense matching by simultaneously improving accuracy (SOTA on MegaDepth-1500, ScanNet-1500, RUBIK, and six dense-matching datasets), reducing runtime by 1.7×, and adding a pixel-wise covariance estimate that yields ~20-point gains in downstream pose refinement. The main open question is the trade-off between the frozen-backbone robustness (which slightly underperforms RoMa on extreme modality shifts like IR-to-RGB in WxBS) and the fine-tuned-backbone speed/precision of UFM; the paper acknowledges this tension without resolving it. The small-scale SatAst benchmark and the EMA remedy for training-induced subpixel bias are useful but narrow contributions.
