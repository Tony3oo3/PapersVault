## Problem

Existing discrete action tokenizers for Vision-Language-Action models suffer from a 'discretization bottleneck': they treat heterogeneous action signals (e.g., 6-DoF end-effector poses and binary gripper states) as a monolithic vector in a shared latent space, causing semantic entanglement, and their reliance on residual vector quantization limits representational capacity to the codebook size, producing coarse approximations that lose fine-grained control dynamics. This high reconstruction loss directly caps the performance ceiling of downstream VLA policies.

## Input / Output

The VLA consumes a 224×224 RGB frame (encoded by SigLIP into 256 patch tokens), proprioceptive joint states (discretized into 256 bins per dimension), and a natural-language instruction. The continuous 14-DoF bimanual action is first decomposed into two independent 7-DoF single-arm sequences, each tokenized by M²Tok into a fixed-length sequence of discrete tokens. During autoregressive generation, the model outputs a sequence of h codebook indices per temporal position (one index per head), which are decoded back into a continuous action chunk of length k via the M²Tok decoder.

## Method

M²Tok is a VQ-VAE-based tokenizer with a hybrid encoder (interleaved 1D causal convolutions and Transformer blocks) that produces a latent sequence, which is then split into h orthogonal subspaces (heads). Each head is quantized independently against its own codebook of V/h prototypes, so the total representational capacity expands combinatorially to (V/h)^h while keeping the total number of code vectors at V. A lightweight conversion module retrieves the selected codebook embeddings for each generated token and projects them via an MLP into the LLM's embedding space, preserving the disentangled semantics learned by the multi-head architecture. The full VLA (Qwen2.5-0.5B backbone + frozen SigLIP + frozen M²Tok) is trained with standard next-token cross-entropy over a serialized sequence of text, state, image, and left/right action tokens, with modality boundaries marked by special tokens.

## Results

On the 12-task RoboTwin benchmark, M²Tok achieves a 51% average success rate, surpassing the strongest baseline VQ-VLA (45%) by 13% relative margin. On Simpler-Env (4 tasks), M²Tok reaches 28% average success rate versus VQ-VLA's 21%. In zero-shot real-world transfer (3 tasks), M²Tok attains 33% average success rate compared to VQ-VLA's 28%. For reconstruction fidelity, M²Tok achieves the lowest L1 loss (0.0024) among all tokenizers, outperforming VQ-VLA (0.0032), VQ-BET (0.0044), and FAST (0.0055).

## Baselines & Comparisons

M²Tok outperforms all four baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the closest competitor VQ-VLA (which uses 2D temporal convolutions + RVQ), M²Tok gains +6 points on RoboTwin (51% vs 45%), +7 points on Simpler-Env (28% vs 21%), and +5 points on real-world tasks (33% vs 28%); the largest single-task gap is Move Can Pot (0.58 vs 0.30). Against VQ-BET (two-layer RVQ), M²Tok leads by +22 points on RoboTwin and +26 points on Simpler-Env, with VQ-BET failing entirely on Put Eggplant in Basket (0.00) where M²Tok reaches 0.33. Against FAST (DCT + BPE), M²Tok achieves +34 points on RoboTwin and +16 points on Simpler-Env, while also offering fixed-length tokens versus FAST's variable-length output. Against the naive Binning baseline (OpenVLA-style per-step discretization), M²Tok gains +27 points on RoboTwin and +25 points on Simpler-Env. In inference latency, M²Tok runs at 8.2 Hz (121.6 ms) without optimization, and 56.2 Hz (17.8 ms) with vLLM, compared to Binning at 2.0 Hz and FAST at 23.4 Hz.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VQ-VAE framework for discrete representation learning, but departs from prior action tokenizers (FAST, VQ-BET, VQ-VLA) by replacing both frequency-domain BPE encoding and residual vector quantization with a multi-head, multi-codebook combinatorial quantization scheme. The key conceptual departure is the argument that monolithic latent spaces entangle heterogeneous action semantics (e.g., 6-DoF pose vs. binary gripper), and that RVQ's linear capacity scaling is fundamentally insufficient for high-frequency manipulation. The paper also inherits the autoregressive VLA paradigm from RT-1/OpenVLA and the action-chunking strategy from Diffusion Policy, but contributes a lightweight conversion module to bridge multi-codebook tokens into the LLM's embedding space without architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discretization bottleneck identified here is a practical ceiling for all VLA models that rely on discrete action tokens, and the combinatorial expressivity argument ((V/h)^h vs. V) provides a principled path to closing it without increasing vocabulary size or inference cost. The dramatic ablation gap (51% → 22% without multi-head) suggests that semantic disentanglement, not just codebook capacity, is the dominant factor in reconstruction fidelity for manipulation. Open questions include whether the fixed 8-head decomposition generalizes to robots with very different DoF configurations, whether the approach scales to larger LLM backbones (the paper uses only Qwen2.5-0.5B), and whether the zero-shot sim-to-real gap (33% average) can be further narrowed with the improved tokenizer.
