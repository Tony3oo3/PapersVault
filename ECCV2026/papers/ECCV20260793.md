## Problem

Conventional building wireframe reconstruction methods fail in regions with significant noise, sparsity, or internal corners because they cannot establish an adaptive search space over large, sparse point clouds. Existing approaches either operate in an excessively large 3D search space (Point2Roof) or project to a 2D height map that loses critical 3D geometric context and obscures interior corners (BWFormer). The paper addresses this gap by leveraging the Delaunay graph as a geometric prior to define a geometrically adaptive, 3D-aware search space.

## Input / Output

The method takes airborne LiDAR point clouds of building roofs (scaled to a 0–256 coordinate range) as input, exploiting the open-topology property where each (x,y) maps to a single unoccluded z value. It outputs a building wireframe consisting of a set of 3D corner points and a set of wire edges connecting them, forming a compact topology-centric representation. The corner selection stage reduces the input to a fixed 150-point candidate set before final prediction, and the wire selection stage evaluates all pairwise corner combinations (C(M,2) candidates) to determine valid edges.

## Method

The core contribution is Delaunay Graph Scoring: the input point cloud is Delaunay-triangulated into a graph G=(V,E,F), face normals are computed, edge-wise dihedral angles quantify local surface curvature, and vertex-wise corner scores aggregate incident edge angles to identify high-curvature regions. For corner selection, the top-K vertices by corner score are sampled (K=150) to form an adaptive candidate set, which a 6-layer Transformer decoder refines into final corners. For wire selection, all corner pairs are treated as candidates in a Transformer decoder with edge attention; each candidate's query is modulated by a prior-based query scaling factor (sigmoid of the path score, where path score is the mean dihedral angle along the shortest Delaunay path between the pair), applied only in the first three decoder layers to damp implausible wires while preserving learned semantics in later layers. This geometrically informed search-space calibration is the key differentiator from both the unstructured 3D search of Point2Roof and the 2D-projected search of BWFormer.

## Results

On the Building3D Tallinn City dataset, Delaunay Canopy achieves a Wireframe Edit Distance (WED) of 0.232 and Average Corner Offset (ACO) of 0.194, with Corner F1 of 88.3% and Edge F1 of 80.0%. On the Entry-Level dataset, it further improves to WED 0.230, ACO 0.190, Corner F1 90.2%, and Edge F1 82.4%. These represent state-of-the-art results across all eight evaluated metrics (WED, ACO, CP, CR, CF1, EP, ER, EF1) on both datasets, with the largest gains in corner precision (94.5% / 95.1%) and edge precision (87.1% / 87.5%) over the strongest prior method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Delaunay Canopy outperforms the strongest baseline, BWFormer [18], on 7 of 8 metrics per dataset: on Tallinn City it reduces WED from 0.245 to 0.232 and ACO from 0.213 to 0.194, while boosting Corner F1 from 85.4% to 88.3% and Edge Precision from 84.2% to 87.1%; the only metric where BWFormer edges ahead is Edge Recall on Tallinn (74.6% vs. 74.0%). On Entry-Level, Delaunay Canopy wins on all eight metrics, with the largest margin in Corner Recall (85.7% vs. 82.8%) and Corner F1 (90.2% vs. 87.3%). The backbone-substitution variants of Point2Roof (PointTransformer*, PointMLP*, PointNeXt*, PointMeta*) cluster tightly around WED 0.251–0.260 and CF1 83.6–85.6%, confirming that architectural changes alone yield only marginal gains (≤1.5% CF1) compared to the 3–5% improvement from the Delaunay geometric prior. PC2WF [16] is far behind all other methods (WED 0.554, CF1 30.1% on Tallinn), reflecting its inability to handle the sparsity of airborne LiDAR. The paper further validates that the gains stem from the geometric prior rather than architecture by showing that swapping the wire selection module to Point2Roof's design (Table 5) recovers only ~0.7% CF1, whereas adding the Delaunay-derived query scaling recovers the full ~1.6% improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Point2Roof → BWFormer lineage for building wireframe reconstruction from airborne LiDAR, where prior methods either search an excessively large 3D space (Point2Roof) or collapse to a restrictive 2D height-map projection (BWFormer). The key departure is the introduction of Delaunay triangulation as a geometric prior: dihedral angles along triangulation edges yield curvature signatures that adaptively narrow the search space for both corner and wire selection, while the wire selection module retains BWFormer's edge-attention mechanism. Conceptually, the work repurposes a classical computational-geometry tool (Delaunay graph) as a differentiable geometric cue within a Transformer-based pipeline, bridging classical surface reconstruction and learned wireframe prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive search space addresses a concrete failure mode—interior corners and sparse/noisy regions—that has limited prior methods, and the Delaunay-based formulation is computationally lightweight and interpretable. However, the paper acknowledges limitations on topologically closed structures, severe data absence, and geometric ambiguity, leaving open whether the approach generalizes beyond building roofs to arbitrary 3D reconstruction tasks. A broader open question is whether the curvature-prior paradigm (classical geometry guiding learned selection) can be extended to other sparse point cloud reconstruction problems beyond buildings.
