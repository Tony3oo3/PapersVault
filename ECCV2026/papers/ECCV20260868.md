## Problem

Applying Flow Matching to 3D medical image translation faces three intertwined challenges: the average velocity field implicitly imposes a time-dependent low-pass filter that attenuates high-frequency texture, stochastic bridging without target-modality constraints causes source-target feature entanglement, and multi-step voxel-space inference is too slow for real-time clinical use. Existing methods either rely on modality-specific priors that limit generalization or sacrifice anatomical fidelity for speed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired 3D volumes from the same patient (e.g., MRI T1WI/FLAIR and FDG/Tau/Aβ-PET, or CT and MRI/CBCT) and produces the target-modality volume in a single forward pass. All flow matching operates in a pre-trained VAE/VQGAN latent space to reduce the dimensionality of 3D volumes, and the spectral corrector is applied to the decoded volume's 3D FFT before inverse transform. Inference requires exactly one neural function evaluation (1-NFE) with r=0, t=1, eliminating iterative denoising.

## Method

SC-Flow reformulates medical image translation as a stochastic Brownian bridge in latent space, where the intermediate state z_t = z_0(1-t) + z_1·t + √(t(1-t))·ε injects Gaussian perturbations that expand distributional support and regularize the mean velocity field u_θ(z_t, r, t) learned via the MeanFlow identity. The key architectural addition is the Spectral Consistency Corrector (SCC): a lightweight encoder produces a conditioning vector from both source and generated volumes, an MLP predicts per-bin radial gain coefficients g_b, and these are applied as squared amplitude multipliers on the 3D FFT to align the generated power spectral density with the target domain's mean PSD. Training jointly minimizes the mean-flow velocity loss, a voxel-domain loss (L1/perceptual/SSIM), and a weighted log-MSE spectral loss over radially binned PSD energies, all end-to-end including the latent decoder. At inference, a single evaluation z_0 = z_1 − u_θ(z_1, 0, 1) followed by SCC correction and VAE decoding yields the translated volume.

## Results

On the synthRAD2025 CT→MRI task, SC-Flow achieves MAE 3.77, RMSE 8.53, PSNR 21.84 dB, and SSIM 78.40%, outperforming LBM (MAE 4.08) and MeanFlow (MAE 4.29). On CT→CBCT, SC-Flow reaches MAE 2.92, RMSE 5.53, PSNR 26.10 dB, and SSIM 87.69%. In downstream Alzheimer's diagnosis, SC-Flow improves F1 by +11.45 over the strongest baseline (Pix2Pix, 59.77→71.77) on NACC-FDG and AUC by +24.04 over MeanFlow (45.43→70.19) on A4-Tau, while also achieving the best FID (0.16/0.32) and MMD (0.1384/0.1176) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SC-Flow consistently outperforms all baselines on the primary translation metrics. Against the closest flow-matching competitor LBM [6], SC-Flow reduces MAE from 4.08→3.77 (CT→MRI) and 3.01→2.92 (CT→CBCT), with a notable SSIM gain of +3.81 on CT→MRI (78.40 vs 74.59), though LBM retains a slight SSIM edge on CT→CBCT (88.37 vs 87.69). Compared to MeanFlow [17], SC-Flow reduces MAE by over 20% and improves PSNR by more than 2.5 dB, confirming the benefit of the Brownian bridge and spectral correction. CycleGAN [71] is substantially outperformed across all metrics (e.g., MAE 7.02 vs 3.77 on CT→MRI). In downstream diagnostic evaluation, SC-Flow achieves F1 71.77 vs Pix2Pix's 59.77 and CycleGAN's 60.32 on NACC-FDG, and AUC 70.19 vs MeanFlow's 45.43 on A4-Tau, demonstrating that spectral consistency translates into clinically meaningful diagnostic utility. Medical-specific methods Causal-PETs [38] and PASTA [37] are surpassed on both reconstruction fidelity and downstream classification, with SC-Flow achieving FID 0.16 vs Causal-PETs' 0.69 on NACC-FDG.

## Relation to Prior Work

SC-Flow sits at the intersection of three threads: MeanFlow's average-velocity formulation for 1-NFE generation, Brownian bridge diffusion (BBDM) for endpoint-conditioned image-to-image translation, and standard Flow Matching ODE-based velocity learning. The key conceptual departure is replacing MeanFlow's deterministic linear interpolation with a stochastic Brownian bridge in latent space, which regularizes the velocity field under the narrow-support overlap typical of paired 3D medical volumes. The Spectral Consistency Corrector is a novel addition that has no direct predecessor in the flow-matching literature; it imports Parseval's-theorem reasoning from signal processing to impose an explicit frequency-domain gain constraint on the generated output, complementing the voxel-space supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving clinically competitive 3D medical image translation with a single neural function evaluation addresses a real deployment bottleneck: multi-step diffusion and even multi-NFE flow methods are too slow for real-time clinical workflows on large volumetric data. The spectral consistency corrector offers a general, lightweight mechanism that could be grafted onto other latent-space flow or diffusion translators to mitigate the well-known high-frequency attenuation problem. Open questions remain about whether the VAE latent space is expressive enough for subtle pathological structures, whether spectral priors can be folded directly into the flow dynamics rather than applied post-hoc, and whether the approach extends to spatiotemporal (4D) disease-progression modeling.
