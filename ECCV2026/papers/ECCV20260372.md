## Problem

Generating complete 3D objects from sparse, partially occluded observations is difficult because existing 3D generative models assume high-quality, well-posed inputs and struggle with unobserved geometry, while 2D amodal completion methods produce locally plausible but cross-view-inconsistent results that fail to yield coherent 3D structure. The core gap is the lack of a framework that simultaneously leverages strong 2D generative priors for plausible occluded content and explicit 3D geometric reasoning for multi-view consistency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 1–4 unposed RGB views of a scene or object along with automatically extracted visibility masks (via SAM), occlusion masks, and a text prompt (via Florence-2). Before the 3D generation stage, each view undergoes 2D amodal completion (OAAC) to fill occluded regions, and partial stereo point clouds are recovered online via a feed-forward MVS model (π³). The output is a complete, occlusion-free 3D object represented as a structured latent that is decoded into a textured mesh with both geometry and appearance, trained on objects with 20–60% occluded surface area.

## Method

GENA3D is a two-stage conditional flow-matching generator. Stage 1 (Sparse Structure Transformer) fuses multi-view DINOv2 features through a proposed View-Wise Cross Attention that applies cross-attention in parallel to each view and combines results via visibility-ratio-weighted averaging, preventing view dominance and geometric drift. A Stereo-Conditioned Cross Attention then encodes the partial MVS point cloud through a Sparse Structure VAE and injects the resulting geometric tokens into attention logits via a geometry-guided gating MLP (log(g+ε) added to QK^T/√D), actively modulating attention rather than passively concatenating geometry. Stage 2 passes the decoded sparse structure to a pretrained Structure Latent Transformer (from Amodal3R) conditioned on visibility and occlusion masks to produce the final structured latent, which is decoded by TRELLIS's Flow Transformer into a complete 3D object. Training uses the standard CFM objective with classifier-free guidance (drop rate 0.1) on 3D-FUTURE and ABO meshes with simulated face-expansion occlusions, requiring only ~16 hours on 8 RTX 6000 GPUs.

## Results

On the GSO dataset, GENA3D achieves the best FID across all sparse-view settings: 33.91 (1 view), 32.12 (2 views), and 30.73 (4 views), outperforming the next-best SAM3D (34.68) and Amodal3R (39.05/35.52/35.15). The Perceptual Coherence Score (PCS) shows the largest margin, with GENA3D reaching 61.9–67.0% versus 46.2–47.1% for Amodal3R and 21.1–28.6% for TRELLIS. On geometric coverage (COV), GENA3D consistently leads (39.19–39.48%) over all baselines. For visible-region faithfulness (Table 2), GENA3D achieves the best SSIM (0.832–0.838) and PSNR (16.52–16.99) while maintaining competitive LPIPS (0.208–0.231).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GENA3D outperforms TRELLIS by a wide margin on every metric and view setting (e.g., FID 30.73 vs. 45.41 at 4 views; PCS 67.0% vs. 23.2%), confirming that simply lifting 2D amodal completions to 3D without explicit geometric conditioning yields poor structural coherence. FreeSplatter performs worst overall, with FID degrading to 94.10 at 4 views and PCS dropping to 8.4%, indicating that pose-free Gaussian splatting struggles severely under occlusion. Against the closest competitor Amodal3R, GENA3D wins on FID (30.73 vs. 35.15 at 4 views), COV (39.48% vs. 38.83%), and PCS (67.0% vs. 46.2%), while matching or slightly trailing on KID (0.43 vs. 0.43) and MMD (5.48 vs. 5.51). SAM3D, evaluated only in the single-view setting, is the strongest single-view baseline (FID 34.68, PCS 46.5%), but GENA3D still leads on FID (33.91), COV (39.19%), and PCS (61.9%), with a small MMD gap (5.68 vs. 5.50). SceneGen is compared only qualitatively in in-the-scene results (Fig. 10), where GENA3D produces more geometrically consistent object-level reconstructions under severe occlusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

GENA3D sits at the intersection of 2D amodal completion (OAAC [1]) and 3D generative modeling (TRELLIS [68], Amodal3R [67]), repurposing the latter's pretrained Structure Latent Transformer and Flow Transformer as a decoding backbone while introducing two novel conditioning modules—View-Wise Cross Attention for balanced multi-view fusion and Stereo-Conditioned Cross Attention for logit-level geometric gating. Unlike prior approaches that either lift 2D completions directly into 3D (causing cross-view inconsistencies) or operate purely in 3D (lacking generative expressiveness), GENA3D treats amodal 3D generation as a conditional flow-matching problem where 2D appearance priors and partial MVS geometry jointly regulate the generative process. The use of MVS point clouds as active attention regulators rather than passive conditioning tokens is the key architectural departure from earlier geometry-conditioned 3D generation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Amodal 3D object generation is a prerequisite for practical robotic manipulation, AR/VR content creation, and embodied AI, yet prior methods struggle under the sparse, unposed, and severely occluded conditions of real-world capture. GENA3D demonstrates that a principled bridge between 2D generative priors and 3D geometric coherence can close much of the gap between synthetic-benchmark performance and in-the-wild robustness, with the added practical benefit of tolerating MVS reconstruction errors through self-correction. Key open questions remain: whether the approach scales to multi-object scenes with complex inter-object occlusions, how well it generalizes to object categories absent from 3D-FUTURE/ABO training, and whether the reliance on a separate 2D completion front-end (OAAC) can be replaced by an end-to-end differentiable pipeline.
