## Problem

Coordinate-based MLPs for implicit neural representations suffer from spectral bias, tending to fit low-frequency structure before fine-scale detail. Existing remedies—deeper/wider latent transformations, multi-resolution encodings, or equilibrium formulations—improve fidelity by increasing independently parameterized depth, auxiliary parameters, or training complexity. The paper asks whether the latent transformation can be made more effective at resolving fine-scale structure without adding independently parameterized layers, and provides a spectral account of why sinusoidal recurrence specifically enables this.

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D spatial coordinates (x, y) or 3D coordinates as input, lifted through a learnable sinusoidal positional encoding with a tunable frequency scale ω_in (not fixed random Fourier features). For the exact-reconstruction variant, the supervision target is a B-bit Gray-coded bipolar code in {−1,+1}^B rather than raw pixel intensities, and the network output is bounded by G(z)=z/√(1+z²) to match the bipolar range. In 3D settings the same recurrent decoder produces radiance/density (NeRF) or signed distance values (SDF), while in super-resolution it replaces the coordinate MLP in a pretrained LIIF pipeline under a matched ~50K-parameter budget.

## Method

The architecture replaces independently parameterized hidden layers with a single shared sinusoidal block—sin(ω·W_rec·h)—unrolled for R recurrent steps under a fixed parameter budget, preceded by a sinusoidal input projection and followed by a linear output projection. The key theoretical insight is that each sinusoidal layer, via the Jacobi-Anger expansion, maps a finite set of encoder tones to new spectral lines at all integer combinations (sums, differences, higher-order harmonics) of those tones, so repeated application progressively enriches the effective spectral support without adding parameters. Recurrent bias terms are omitted because they shift the phase-dependent derivative gate at every step, causing unstable high-frequency dynamics once R≥2. For exact quantized reconstruction, the paper introduces binarized supervision: targets are Gray-coded bipolar codes optimized with a cosine-similarity alignment loss, which reduces worst-case local code-space sensitivity by a factor of n compared to natural binary coding and enables zero bit-error recovery.

## Results

On 2D image fitting, the proposed recurrent sinusoidal INR (609K params, 100 iterations) achieves 58.16 dB PSNR / 0.9997 SSIM on Set5, 53.20 / 0.9989 on Kodak24, 46.28 / 0.9973 on DIV2K-100, and 61.39 / 0.9998 on FFHQ-600, surpassing all 791K-parameter, 1000-iteration baselines. The method reaches exact quantized reconstruction (PSNR = ∞, zero bit error) at 322±117, 447±327, 537±432, and 440±350 iterations on the four datasets respectively. In NeRF novel view synthesis, replacing the standard decoder with the recurrent sinusoidal decoder improves PSNR by 0.24–0.94 dB and reduces LPIPS by 0.08–0.14 across four LLFF scenes. For pretrained LIIF super-resolution at ~50K decoder parameters, the recurrent decoder consistently improves PSNR by 0.03–0.07 dB and SSIM by 0.001–0.002 over the standard MLP across Set5, Set14, and B100 at ×2–×4 scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest learnable-frequency spectral baselines, Ours (609K, 100 iters) outperforms iSIREN (791K, 1000 iters) by +6.36 dB on Set5 (58.16 vs 51.80), +0.53 dB on Kodak24 (53.20 vs 52.67), and +10.49 dB on FFHQ (61.39 vs 50.90), while using fewer parameters and 10× fewer iterations. Ours also surpasses GaborNet by +7.85 dB on Set5 and +3.82 dB on FFHQ, and FourierNet by +12.49 dB on Set5. Against feed-forward sinusoidal baselines, the gap is even larger: Ours exceeds SIREN by +19.62 dB on Set5 and FINER by +16.38 dB on Set5, all under a smaller parameter budget. In the non-sinusoidal recurrent ablation (Table 4), adding recurrence to Gaussian or PEMLP architectures degrades or fails to improve PSNR, confirming that the spectral enrichment mechanism is specific to sinusoidal activations. For NeRF (Table 8), the recurrent decoder improves over standard NeRF by +0.83 dB PSNR and −0.12 LPIPS on Flower, and by +0.94 dB / −0.14 LPIPS on Orchids. In pretrained LIIF super-resolution (Table 7b), the recurrent decoder yields consistent but modest gains (+0.03 to +0.07 dB PSNR) over the standard MLP at matched ~50K decoder parameters, with the largest perceptual improvement (LPIPS −0.008) at ×2 on Set5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SIREN's sinusoidal activation design by replacing independently parameterized hidden layers with a single shared sinusoidal block applied recurrently, drawing on the broader recurrence-as-depth literature (Elman, Schwarzschild et al.) and the equilibrium-network line (Bai et al., Huang et al.). The key conceptual departure from prior equilibrium-style INRs (iSIREN) is the use of finite explicit unrolling rather than a fixed-point formulation, motivated by a harmonic line-spectrum analysis via the Jacobi-Anger expansion that shows each sinusoidal layer generates integer-combination spectral lines. The binarized Gray-code supervision for exact discrete reconstruction is borrowed from recent bit-plane INR work [12] but is here combined with the recurrent architecture to enable lossless recovery under a fixed parameter budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The harmonic line-spectrum analysis provides a principled spectral account of why sinusoidal recurrence enriches effective bandwidth without adding parameters, offering a design rationale that goes beyond empirical tuning. Practically, achieving exact quantized reconstruction (∞ PSNR) with fewer parameters and ~100× fewer optimization steps than feed-forward baselines, while transferring to NeRF, SDF, and super-resolution, makes this a broadly applicable decoder component. The paper acknowledges that gains are not consistent under severe capacity constraints, leaving open the question of how the spectral enrichment mechanism interacts with very small model sizes. A natural next step is understanding whether the integer-combination spectral closure can be controlled or regularized to avoid the unbounded spectral growth observed at random initialization.
