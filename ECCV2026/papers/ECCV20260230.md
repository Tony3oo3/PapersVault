## Problem

Dynamic scene geometry estimation requires modeling both spatial structure and temporal motion, but existing 4D methods embed temporal cues directly into spatial features within a unified latent space. This serial fusion causes mismatched representations due to the inherent heterogeneity between spatial (structural consistency across views) and temporal (motion continuity across time) features, leading to unstable geometry estimates in dynamic scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260230/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB video sequences with arbitrary numbers of views V and time steps T (monocular-static, monocular-dynamic, or multi-view-static configurations) without requiring the modality type to be predetermined. Each frame is encoded by DINOv2 into image tokens augmented with view and time identifier tokens, organized into an adaptive visual grid. Outputs include camera intrinsics/extrinsics (9-dim vector), per-pixel depth maps, binary dynamic masks, 3D point maps (3×H×W), and 2D/3D trajectories for N randomly initialized query points, all predicted jointly by task-specific heads.

## Method

4D-VGGT adopts a divide-and-conquer strategy that factorizes spatiotemporal representation into two parallel masked-attention modules: a Cross-View Global Fusion (CVGF) module that performs intra-frame and inter-frame attention across all views within the same time step to capture spatial structure, and a Cross-Time Local Fusion (CTLF) module that applies sliding-window attention (window size S=5) combined with a GRU within each view to capture temporal motion continuity. An Adaptive Visual Grid organizes arbitrary view-time inputs into a shared token grid with explicit (v,t) indices, enabling the model to handle any camera configuration without architectural changes. Five task-specific prediction heads (spatial head for camera pose, DPT-based dense heads for depth/mask/point map, and a temporal head for tracking) consume the factorized features in a task-appropriate manner—spatial-only for pose, temporal-only for tracking, and both for dense tasks. Training proceeds in two stages: per-task optimization with frozen non-target heads, followed by multi-task joint fine-tuning of all heads with a weighted multi-task loss, using a random sampling strategy over the visual grid to simulate diverse input configurations.

## Results

4D-VGGT achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five geometry tasks. On camera pose estimation, it attains ATE of 0.065 on Sintel, 0.011 on TUM, and 0.017 on Bonn, outperforming the best 4D baseline π3 (0.074/0.014/0.020) by 12–36%. On depth estimation, it reaches AbsRel of 0.197 (Sintel), 0.038 (Bonn), and 0.035 (KITTI) with δ<1.25 scores of 72.9%, 98.2%, and 98.2% respectively. For point tracking on PointOdyssey, it achieves L-12 APD of 36.12 and L-24 of 34.98, surpassing the best foundation model St4RTrack (35.72/34.45).

## Baselines & Comparisons

4D-VGGT outperforms all 27 baselines across every task and dataset. On camera pose, it beats the strongest 4D competitor π3 by 12% on Sintel ATE (0.065 vs 0.074) and 36% on TUM ATE (0.011 vs 0.014), while also surpassing the best 3D model VGGT by 60% on Sintel ATE. On depth estimation, it edges out π3 (AbsRel 0.197 vs 0.210 on Sintel; 0.035 vs 0.037 on KITTI) and DA-3-Giant (0.038 vs 0.037 on Bonn, where DA-3-Giant is marginally better). For point map estimation on 7-Scenes, it achieves the best Accuracy (0.082) and Normal Consistency (0.896), outperforming VGGT4D (0.106/0.826) and π3 (0.095/0.776). On dynamic mask (DAVIS-16), it reaches JM 71.0, surpassing the best foundation model Easi3R (57.4) by 13.6 points and matching the specialized ABR method (70.9). For point tracking, it leads all foundation models on PointOdyssey (L-12: 36.12 vs St4RTrack's 35.72) and ADT (L-12: 35.27 vs St4RTrack's 34.89), while also outperforming specialized trackers like CoTracker3 (24.79) by over 11 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends VGGT's spatial attention architecture and TAPIR/CoTracker3's tracking formulation into a unified 4D framework, but departs fundamentally from prior 4D geometry methods (MonST3R, StreamVGGT, π3, POMATO) that embed temporal cues directly into spatial features within a single latent space. Instead, 4D-VGGT introduces a divide-and-conquer strategy with separate cross-view global fusion (spatial) and cross-time local fusion (temporal) modules guided by distinct attention masks, motivated by the heterogeneous nature of spatial structural consistency versus temporal motion continuity. The adaptive visual grid, supported by an empirical observation that DINOv2 features from different camera settings share similar distributions (KL divergence ~0.02), further generalizes the input to arbitrary view-time configurations—a capability absent in prior 4D methods that typically assume fixed camera setups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The divide-and-conquer spatiotemporal representation addresses a concrete failure mode in existing 4D geometry models—mismatched representations when spatial and temporal features are forced into a unified space—yielding consistent SOTA across five geometry tasks. The multi-setting, multi-task framework is practically valuable for real-world deployment where camera configurations vary. However, the fixed sliding window in the cross-time local fusion limits capture of fast-moving dynamics, and the authors acknowledge the need for event cameras or higher temporal resolution signals. Open questions include whether the parallel fusion strategy scales to longer sequences or more extreme motion, and whether the task-specific feature routing (spatial-only for pose, temporal-only for tracking) generalizes to new geometry tasks not seen during training.
