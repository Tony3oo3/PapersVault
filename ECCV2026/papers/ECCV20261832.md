## Problem

Underwater computer vision tasks are bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and accurately annotated training data. Existing synthesis methods either offer scene diversity without physically accurate medium control, or provide controllable degradation on fixed scenes without increasing semantic variety. No prior diffusion-based approach achieves independent, pixel-accurate control over water optical parameters while preserving scene structure and diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261832/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a natural-language text prompt and a set of physically meaningful water parameters Φ = {B^∞, β^D, β^B} (background light, direct attenuation, and backscatter coefficients) sampled from empirical distributions. It outputs an RGB underwater image whose degradation is governed by Φ, and critically, the same scene latent can be re-decoded under different Φ values to produce perfectly aligned clean/degraded image pairs at scale. The water parameters are continuous physical quantities rather than discrete style labels, enabling fine-grained interpolation between water types.

## Method

WaterGen decouples underwater image synthesis into two independently trained stages within an SDXL latent diffusion framework. Stage 1 fine-tunes the U-Net via LoRA (rank 32) on degradation-free underwater images (restored with SLURPP and captioned with BLIP-2) so the diffusion backbone learns diverse scene semantics without medium artifacts corrupting the latent space. Stage 2 trains a medium-conditioned decoder that, given the clean latent and Φ, uses a depth estimation module to compute pixel-wise transmission and backscattering maps, which are injected as explicit spatial conditions into the VAE decoder to modulate reconstruction. The decoder is trained with a bidirectional UIFM consistency loss (enforcing both forward J→I and inverse I→J physical validity) and a stochastic medium noise injection strategy that perturbs input latents to prevent the decoder from learning an identity mapping. This architecture ensures that medium control is a radiometric, spatially-aware decoding operation rather than a semantic conditioning signal, preserving scene geometry while enabling precise, independent water-condition manipulation.

## Results

On the SynTIDE caption set (5,451 prompts), WaterGen achieves UIQM 3.02±0.13, MUSIQ 69.26±0.88, and CLIP Score 0.2614±0.007 for degradation-free scene generation, outperforming both Atlantis (UIQM 2.83, MUSIQ 67.54, CLIP 0.2457) and TIDE (UIQM 2.37, MUSIQ 66.43, CLIP 0.2305). For medium control accuracy, WaterGen achieves RMSE 0.06, MAE 4.42°, and CIEDE2000 ΔE₀₀ 5.44, substantially lower than ControlNet+SDXL (RMSE 0.45, ΔE₀₀ 43.70) and T2I-Adapter+SDXL (RMSE 0.30, ΔE₀₀ 30.10). Downstream, adding WaterGen synthetic data improves restoration (e.g., UIQM 3.141 vs 3.067 for WaterNet) and segmentation (e.g., mIoU 75.6 vs 70.2 for SegFormer on UIIS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WaterGen outperforms both underwater generation baselines on all three metrics in Table 1: it beats Atlantis by +0.19 UIQM, +1.72 MUSIQ, and +0.016 CLIP Score, and beats TIDE by +0.65 UIQM, +2.83 MUSIQ, and +0.031 CLIP Score, while also offering explicit medium-parameter control that neither baseline supports. For medium injection accuracy (Table 4), WaterGen's decoder-based approach reduces RMSE from 0.45 (ControlNet+SDXL) and 0.30 (T2I-Adapter+SDXL) to 0.06, and cuts CIEDE2000 color error from 43.70 and 30.10 to 5.44, confirming that adapter-style conditioning in the diffusion U-Net cannot faithfully enforce pixel-level attenuation and backscattering. In downstream restoration (Table 2), WaterGen synthetic data improves all four restoration baselines (WaterNet, PhaseFormer, DeepWaveNet, Histoformer) on both UIQM and MUSIQ. For segmentation (Table 3), adding WaterGen synthetic data yields +5.4 mIoU for SegFormer on UIIS and +2.3 mIoU for ViT-Adapter on USIS10K over real-only training, matching or slightly exceeding the gains from the SynTIDE synthetic baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

WaterGen builds on the SDXL latent diffusion backbone and the Jaffe-McGlamery / Akkaynak-Treibitz underwater image formation model, but departs sharply from prior underwater generation methods in its architectural placement of physics. Existing diffusion-based approaches (Atlantis, TIDE) entangle scene semantics and medium degradation in a single denoising process, while physics-based methods (RUIG, RSUIGM) and style-transfer GANs (WaterGAN, UStyle) cannot generate novel scene content. The key conceptual departure is relocating medium simulation from the diffusion U-Net to the VAE decoder, treating attenuation and backscattering as a radiometric reconstruction problem rather than a generative one, and enforcing it with a bidirectional physics-consistency loss that has no direct analogue in prior controllable-diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled 'one scene, many waters' design directly addresses the data-scarcity bottleneck in underwater computer vision by producing perfectly paired clean/degraded images with physically accurate degradation at scale, which the paper validates with consistent (if modest) gains in restoration and segmentation. A key open question is how well the approach generalizes beyond the six water types and scene categories tested, and whether the reliance on SLURPP-restored images for Stage 1 training introduces residual artifacts that cap scene realism. The framework also raises the question of whether the decoder-based medium injection strategy transfers to other physically grounded generation tasks (e.g., atmospheric scattering, fog) where a similar scene/medium decoupling could apply.
