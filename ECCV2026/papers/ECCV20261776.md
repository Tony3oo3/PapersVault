## Problem

Concept erasure has become a mature research area for diffusion-based image generation, but autoregressive image generation models—now central to unified multimodal architectures—remain largely unprotected. A direct translation of diffusion-style negative guidance to the autoregressive setting fails in practice because unaligned visual prefixes cause distribution divergence that degrades model utility before effective erasure is achieved, and isolated token-level updates are too slow to suppress concepts that unfold over full generation sequences.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen autoregressive text-to-image model (LIQUID-7B at 512×512, EMU3-GEN at 720×720, or JANUS-PRO at 384×384) and a target concept prompt as input, and produces a LoRA-adapted model (rank 32, α=16, 5% dropout) that suppresses the target concept while preserving general generation quality. The teacher model generates harmful visual-token rollouts under the target prompt, and the student is trained via KL divergence over the full visual-token vocabulary at each position along the rollout. Evaluation uses dedicated classifiers (NudeNet, Q16, VLM ensemble) to measure Concept Detection Rate, with FID and CLIP-Score on MJHQ-30K tracking utility preservation.

## Method

OBLIVIATE introduces three design choices that make teacher-guided concept erasure work in the autoregressive setting. First, it aligns visual prefixes by reusing the teacher's harmful rollout as a shared prefix for both the conditional and pseudo-unconditional branches, yielding a stable target signal z_tgt = z_teacher(x̂_<k, ∅) − η(z_teacher(x̂_<k, c) − z_teacher(x̂_<k, ∅)) that isolates concept-bearing tokens without disrupting broader semantics. Second, it performs full-trajectory training, optimizing over all N token positions in a single rollout via causal masking rather than updating on isolated token windows, which is both more efficient and better suited to suppressing concepts that unfold across the generation chain. Third, it replaces hard cross-entropy token supervision with a KL divergence over the full predicted logit distributions, which naturally spreads probability mass and avoids over-penalizing non-concept-bearing tokens—critical because most generated visual tokens are generic and aggressive updates on them degrade utility. The student minimizes (1/N)Σ_k KL(p_tgt^(k) ‖ p_θ^(k)) over the visual vocabulary, effectively reallocating probability away from harmful continuations toward safer alternatives.

## Results

On the adversarial Ring-A-Bell (RAB) benchmark, OBLIVIATE reduces nudity detection (CDR) from 91.58 to 3.15 on LIQUID, from 82.10 to 11.57 on EMU3-GEN, and from 55.79 to 1.05 on JANUS-PRO, while preserving FID within ~1 point of the original model. In the unbranding setting, Coca-Cola CDR drops to near zero (5.22, 4.14, and 0.18) across the three models. For gory content, OBLIVIATE achieves the largest CDR reduction on all models (e.g., 87.65→39.06 on LIQUID), though absolute suppression is weaker than for explicit content.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OBLIVIATE outperforms all inference-time steering baselines (Negative Prompting, SLD*) by a wide margin on the adversarial RAB benchmark across all three models: 3.15 vs. 35.79 (NP) and 58.95 (SLD*) on LIQUID, and 1.05 vs. 18.95 (NP) and 22.10 (SLD*) on JANUS-PRO. Against the fine-tuning baseline EAR, OBLIVIATE achieves a substantially lower RAB CDR on JANUS-PRO (1.05 vs. 11.58) while preserving FID at 12.31 versus EAR's 31.63, indicating EAR's erasure comes at a severe utility cost. On MMA-Diff, EAR marginally edges OBLIVIATE on JANUS-PRO (0.80 vs. 1.00 CDR), but this is the only baseline win. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) preserves utility well but leaves high residual CDR (45.26 on RAB for LIQUID), confirming that standard fine-tuning is insufficient for robust concept erasure. In the unbranding setting, OBLIVIATE dominates all baselines, reducing Coca-Cola CDR to 0.18 on JANUS-PRO versus 32.19 (SFT) and 85.61 (EAR).

## Relation to Prior Work

OBLIVIATE adapts the negative-guidance teacher-student framework from diffusion-based concept erasure (ESD, Gandikota et al. 2023) to autoregressive image generation, where the analogous signal must be defined over next-token logit distributions rather than denoising steps. It draws on EraseFlow's (Kusumba et al. 2025) insight that trajectory-level updates improve erasure quality, and departs from the closest AR-specific prior work, EAR (Fan et al. 2025), by replacing disjoint token-window updates with full-rollout supervision and by introducing an aligned pseudo-unconditional branch that shares the harmful trajectory as visual prefix. The KL-based distribution matching objective is a deliberate departure from the cross-entropy token supervision used in both EAR and the naive autoregressive analog of ESD, motivated by the multimodal nature of visual token prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As unified multimodal architectures (LIQUID, EMU3, JANUS-PRO) gain traction, the safety gap for autoregressive image generation becomes increasingly urgent, and this paper provides the first broadly applicable erasure method across multiple such models with strong empirical results (e.g., RAB CDR 91.58→3.15 on LIQUID, near-zero brand detection). Key open questions remain: robustness under adaptive white-box attacks is untested, multi-concept erasure degrades substantially beyond two concepts (CDR rises to 47.56 for five classes), and the practical relevance of the entire line depends on whether autoregressive generation overtakes diffusion as the dominant paradigm. The finding that simple brand-name prompts outperform detailed visual descriptions for unbranding also suggests the method's sensitivity to prompt engineering warrants further study.
