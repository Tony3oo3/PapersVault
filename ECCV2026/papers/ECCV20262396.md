## Problem

Cross-view geo-localization with sequential video queries overlooks the complementary modality of natural-language route descriptions, which are often the only input available in human-robot interaction scenarios. Additionally, existing methods treat CLIP-style embeddings as spatially agnostic, failing to exploit trajectory geometry (headings, origin-destination bearing) that is readily available from mobile IMUs but historically ignored in cross-view matching.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262396/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts either a ground-level video clip (N frames sampled along a trajectory), a long-form route description (~129 tokens on average, exceeding CLIP's 77-token window and requiring positional-embedding interpolation), or both simultaneously, plus lightweight trajectory geometry (per-waypoint heading angles and a single OD bearing angle relative to True North, encoded via Fourier features). The output is a retrieved geo-tagged satellite patch at zoom level 20 from a gallery of ~38K candidates, from which the agent's location is directly read off. No metric-accurate coordinates or road-network/POI metadata are required.

## Method

TrajLoc employs three CLIP ViT-L/14 encoders (video, text, satellite) trained with a two-stage curriculum: Stage 1 aligns video and satellite embeddings via symmetric InfoNCE; Stage 2 freezes the video encoder and aligns the text encoder to the satellite encoder while applying a cosine-similarity regularization loss to prevent satellite-encoder drift. The key spatial component, TrajMod, is a FiLM-style plug-and-play module that takes Fourier-encoded trajectory geometry (waypoint headings concatenated with OD bearing) as input to a modality-specific MLP, producing per-channel scale (γ) and shift (β) parameters that modulate the frozen video or text query embeddings into spatially grounded representations. Video frames are aggregated by simple mean pooling rather than temporal transformers, keeping the architecture lightweight. TrajMod is trained with the same contrastive objective plus an additional text-video contrastive term that acts as a regularizer, discouraging modality-specific noise and encouraging both modulated embeddings to preserve the shared geometry-grounded factors.

## Results

On cross-view video geo-localization (SeqGeo-VL), TrajLoc achieves R@1=12.09%, R@5=35.77%, R@10=47.68%, and R@1%=80.21%, surpassing the strongest video baseline SeqGeo† (ViT-L/14) by 4.95 points in R@1 and 14.04 points in R@1%. On the novel cross-view text geo-localization task, TrajLoc reaches R@1=2.52% and R@1%=45.48%, a 2.6× improvement in R@1 over the best prior method CrossText2Loc (ViT-L/14@336, R@1=0.98%) and a 1.7× improvement in R@1% over EVA2-CLIP (ViT-L/14@336, R@1%=26.99%). Ablations confirm that TrajMod contributes +19.33 points in R@1% for text geo-localization and that co-training video and text modalities amplifies gains when trajectory conditioning is present.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrajLoc outperforms all nine baselines on both tasks. In video geo-localization, it beats the closest competitor SeqGeo† (ViT-L/14) by 4.95 R@1 points (12.09 vs. 8.14) and 14.04 R@1% points (80.21 vs. 66.17), while GARet and FlexGeo trail by roughly 8–9 R@1 points. The MLLM-based Qwen3-VL-Embedding (LoRA-tuned) reaches only 5.44% R@1, confirming that general-purpose VLM embeddings lack the spatial grounding needed for cross-view matching. In text geo-localization, TrajLoc (R@1=2.52%) surpasses CrossText2Loc (0.98%), CLIP ViT-L/14 (0.80%), EVA2-CLIP (0.89%), SigLIP (0.85%), Perception Encoder (0.66%), and Qwen3-VL-Embedding (0.93%) by 1.5–1.9 R@1 points, with the largest gap in R@1% (45.48 vs. 26.99 for EVA2-CLIP). Notably, scaling CLIP-style backbones from ViT-B to ViT-SO400M or increasing resolution yields only marginal gains (<0.1 R@1), indicating the bottleneck is spatial reasoning rather than model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends cross-view geo-localization along two axes simultaneously: from single-image text queries (CrossText2Loc) to sequential route descriptions, and from video-only sequential queries (SeqGeo, GaMa, CVLNet) to a unified video-plus-text formulation. It builds directly on CrossText2Loc's expanded positional embedding technique for long text and on SeqGeo's sequential video framework, but departs from both by introducing trajectory geometry (waypoint headings and OD bearing) as an explicit conditioning signal via a FiLM-style modulation module (TrajMod). The two-stage curriculum for co-training video and text encoders against a shared satellite encoder is a practical departure from prior single-modality training, motivated by the observation that text-to-satellite alignment is substantially harder than video-to-satellite alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: in GPS-degraded urban environments, users may only provide abstract route descriptions (e.g., directing a delivery robot), and the paper shows that trajectory geometry—readily available from phone IMUs and compasses—can dramatically improve cross-view matching when injected as explicit conditioning rather than left to the model to infer. The finding that a lightweight FiLM module on trajectory geometry yields +19.33% R@1% versus only +1.34% from text-prompting the same geometry into an MLLM is a clear signal that spatial reasoning in cross-view retrieval benefits from structured geometric signals rather than natural-language descriptions of geometry. Open questions include generalization beyond the single SeqGeo-VL dataset, robustness to noisy or missing trajectory data, and whether the approach scales to city-wide galleries where the current R@1 of ~12% (video) and ~2.5% (text) remains far from deployment-ready.
