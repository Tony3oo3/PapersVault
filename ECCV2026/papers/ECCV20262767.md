## Problem

Current MLLM-based 3D affordance segmentation compresses all reasoning into a single <SEG> token, discarding low-level geometric details such as edges, contours, and precise spatial coordinates. This semantic over-compression causes predicted masks to bleed into spatially adjacent but functionally distinct regions—a phenomenon the authors term geometric ambiguity—which fundamentally limits boundary precision in real-world manipulation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262767/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (N×3 coordinates) and a natural language instruction as input. The point cloud is encoded by a frozen Uni3D encoder into multi-scale dense features, while the instruction is processed by a ShapeLLM-7B MLLM that auto-regressively generates reasoning text culminating in a <SEG> token. The output is a per-point binary probability mask (N values in [0,1]) produced by a dot-product between the refined geometry-aware token and high-resolution point features, followed by a sigmoid activation.

## Method

Geo-DPO resolves geometric ambiguity through two complementary mechanisms. Structurally, the Hierarchical Geometry Adapter (HGA) performs cascaded cross-attention: the raw <SEG> token first queries shallow encoder features (F_low) to capture fine-grained contours, then the updated token queries intermediate features (F_mid) to incorporate broader part-level geometry, yielding a geometry-aware token h_geo that replaces the purely semantic query. For training, Contrastive Affordance Preference Optimization (CAPO) formulates segmentation as a preference ranking problem using a hybrid reward combining Soft-IoU (position) and cosine similarity between mask-weighted pooled encoder features (shape), with in-batch negative sampling where other instances' ground truths serve as geometrically distinct negatives. The total loss combines autoregressive text generation, standard BCE+Dice segmentation, and the DPO-style contrastive preference objective (λ=0.2), trained end-to-end with LoRA (rank 8) on the ShapeLLM-7B backbone.

## Results

On the SeqAfford benchmark, Geo-DPO achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three evaluation settings. In the Seen setting, it reaches 20.9% mIoU, 88.1% AUC, 0.610 SIM, and 0.091 MAE; in the Unseen setting, 14.8% mIoU, 85.6% AUC, 0.528 SIM, and 0.126 MAE; and in the Sequential setting, 15.1% mIoU, 86.5% AUC, 0.601 SIM, and 0.115 MAE. The most notable gain is over the previous SOTA SeqAfford, with +1.4% mIoU (Seen), +1.0% mIoU (Unseen), and +0.5% mIoU (Sequential), alongside consistent improvements in AUC and SIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Geo-DPO outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all three settings (Seen, Unseen, Sequential). Against the closest competitor SeqAfford [56] (previous SOTA), Geo-DPO gains +1.4% mIoU on Seen (20.9 vs. 19.5), +1.0% on Unseen (14.8 vs. 13.8), and +0.5% on Sequential (15.1 vs. 14.6), with AUC improvements of +1.2, +3.2, and +2.3 points respectively. Against LASO [19], the second-strongest baseline, the margins are larger: +4.6% mIoU (Seen), +2.4% (Unseen), and +0.8% (Sequential). The older 2D/3D grounding methods—ReferTrans [16], ReLA [20], 3D-SPS [25], and IAGNet [52]—trail by 5–11 mIoU points, reflecting their lack of 3D MLLM-based sequential reasoning. Notably, in the Sequential setting where geometric errors accumulate over multiple steps, Geo-DPO's advantage over SeqAfford narrows slightly (+0.5% mIoU) but the AUC gap widens to +2.3 points, suggesting the preference-based boundary supervision is most effective at suppressing mask overflow in multi-step tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SeqAfford pipeline (CVPR 2025), which introduced sequential 3D affordance reasoning via a 3D MLLM that emits a compressed <SEG> token to drive mask prediction. The key departure is diagnosing 'geometric ambiguity'—boundary overflow caused by losing low-level spatial detail in that single token—and addressing it from two complementary angles: a structural adapter (HGA) that re-injects multi-scale encoder features into the token via cascaded cross-attention, and a training objective (CAPO) that adapts the DPO preference-optimization paradigm (originally from NLP) to penalize geometric overflow in 3D segmentation. The architectural backbone is ShapeLLM-7B, and the 3D encoder is Uni3D, so the contribution is a targeted refinement of an existing MLLM-based affordance framework rather than a new pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Precise boundary localization is critical for downstream robotic manipulation, and the paper shows that preference-based geometric supervision (CAPO) can be a practical regularizer for dense 3D segmentation, a direction largely unexplored before. However, the framework inherits the inference latency of a 7B auto-regressive MLLM, limiting real-time deployment, and is validated only on rigid objects in a single benchmark (SeqAfford). Open questions include extending the preference mechanism to deformable or articulated objects, distilling the MLLM's reasoning into a lightweight real-time model, and generalizing the contrastive boundary reward to other 3D part-segmentation tasks.
