## Problem

Existing egocentric world simulators either lack explicit 3D grounding, causing structural drift under viewpoint changes, or treat the scene as static and fail to persistently update world states across multi-stage interactions. Additionally, they are trained on small-scale aligned multiview or synthetic data, severely limiting scalability and generalization to in-the-wild dexterous interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260293/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an egocentric scene image (hands removed via inpainting and reconstructed into a static point cloud), a per-frame camera trajectory (rotation, translation, intrinsics from DepthAnything3), and a 3D hand keypoint sequence projected onto the 2D observation plane. It produces a 61-frame, 832×480, 16 FPS egocentric interaction video plus an updated 3D scene point cloud that registers physical displacements of manipulated objects. The conditioning is structured as four spatially aligned latent channels: the rendered point-cloud background, the hand keypoint video, a binary mask marking unobserved regions, and the noisy target latent.

## Method

EgoSim decouples static 3D geometry from dynamic hand interactions: the static scene is rendered as a point-cloud video along the true camera trajectory to provide an unambiguous spatial anchor, while hand actions are encoded as cross-embodiment 3D keypoints (not dense meshes) projected to 2D. A Diffusion Transformer (DiT) initialized from Wan-2.1-Fun-14B-InP inpainting weights denoises in the latent space of a pretrained video VAE, with the four conditioning channels concatenated along the feature dimension (52 input channels), trained for 4,000 steps with a standard MSE noise-prediction loss. For long-horizon consistency, a training-free Interaction-aware State Updating module reconstructs per-frame point clouds from generated observations, identifies and tracks interactive objects via a VLM and SAM3, composites their latest geometry into the static background, and incrementally fuses consecutive states using Sim3 Umeyama alignment followed by TSDF fusion. A fully automated data pipeline extracts aligned quadruplets (static point cloud, camera trajectory, hand keypoints, video) from 400K monocular egocentric clips using DepthAnything3, HaMeR, and SAM3, enabling web-scale training without paired multiview captures.

## Results

On the EgoDex tabletop benchmark, EgoSim achieves PSNR 25.056, SSIM 0.896, and LPIPS 0.170, outperforming the strongest baseline InterDyn (PSNR 22.250, SSIM 0.830, LPIPS 0.255). The spatial consistency gains are particularly dramatic: Depth-ERR drops to 8.888 (vs. 44.345 for InterDyn, a 5× reduction) and Cam-ERR to 0.0013 (vs. 0.0226, a ~17× reduction). On the more challenging in-the-wild EgoVid dataset, EgoSim maintains the lead with PSNR 16.684 and Depth-ERR 19.260, roughly halving the spatial error of the next-best method (InterDyn at 38.180). Under continuous generation (121 frames with intermediate 3D state updates), EgoSim retains PSNR 19.165 and Depth-ERR 10.943, showing only marginal degradation from the single-clip setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoSim outperforms all four baselines on every metric across both EgoDex and EgoVid. The closest competitor is InterDyn, which uses hand-mask conditioning via ControlNet; EgoSim leads it by 2.8 dB in PSNR on EgoDex (25.056 vs. 22.250) and by 2.1 dB on EgoVid (16.684 vs. 14.612), while reducing Depth-ERR by roughly 5× (8.888 vs. 44.345) and Cam-ERR by ~17× (0.0013 vs. 0.0226). Mask2IV, which conditions on both hand and object segmentation masks, trails EgoSim by 4.4 dB in PSNR on EgoDex and 4.4 dB on EgoVid, with spatial errors roughly 2× higher. CosHand (per-frame image generation) and Wan-2.1-14B-InP (mask-guided inpainting) are the weakest baselines, with CosHand showing the worst Depth-ERR (56.524 on EgoDex) and Wan-2.1-14B-InP the worst SSIM (0.447 on EgoDex, 0.430 on EgoVid), reflecting their inability to maintain temporal coherence or 3D spatial grounding. The consistent pattern across all comparisons is that EgoSim's explicit 3D point-cloud rendering along the true camera trajectory provides an unambiguous spatial anchor that no 2D-mask-based baseline can match.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoSim builds directly on DWM's (Dexterous World Models) decoupling of static 3D scene geometry from action-induced dynamics, and on VIPE's per-frame point cloud fusion for separating moving objects from static backgrounds. The key departure is the introduction of a persistent, updatable 3D world state: while DWM reconstructs the scene once and never revises it, and PlayerOne/Hand2World encode scene context implicitly without explicit 3D representations, EgoSim closes the simulation loop by extracting post-interaction object geometry from generated frames and fusing it back into the point cloud via TSDF. The paper also departs from prior egocentric simulators' reliance on synthetic environments or expensive ego-exocentric paired captures by proposing a fully automated pipeline that extracts aligned scene–action–video quadruplets from web-scale monocular egocentric videos.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The updatable 3D state addresses a fundamental gap in world simulators: without it, manipulated objects revert to pre-interaction configurations in subsequent frames, breaking physical plausibility over long horizons. The scalable data pipeline and the EgoCap low-cost capture device are practically significant, as they remove the primary bottleneck (aligned multiview or synthetic data) that has limited prior egocentric simulators to small, domain-specific training sets. The cross-embodiment transfer—achieving robotic arm manipulation with only 200 fine-tuning steps after human-hand pretraining—suggests a path toward general-purpose embodied world models. Open questions include robustness in heavily occluded or highly dynamic scenes where monocular depth/pose estimation degrades, and whether physics-based contact constraints could further improve interaction fidelity beyond purely visual state tracking.
