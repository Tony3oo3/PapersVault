## Problem

Existing SSM-based image restoration methods rely on predefined, content-agnostic scanning trajectories (raster, window, Hilbert) that inevitably cross semantic boundaries, causing the recursive state to aggregate non-discriminative features from semantically conflicting regions. This is particularly detrimental for adverse weather restoration, where degradations are spatially non-uniform and concentrated in specific regions. The paper asks whether shifting from pixel-serial to semantic-guided scanning can prevent this information leakage and improve restoration quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262009/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single degraded RGB image (H×W×3) and produces a restored RGB image via a residual formulation I_rec = I_in + F_SSR(I_in), operating on 128×128 patches during training. A single unified model handles multiple weather degradations (rain streaks, haze, snow, raindrops) without task-specific conditioning. The superpixel partitioning is computed on-the-fly from intermediate features at each encoder/decoder level, so the scanning trajectory adapts to the content at every scale.

## Method

SSR is a 3-level U-shaped encoder-decoder whose core building block (S3M Block) replaces the standard SSM scan with a Superpixel-guided Selective Scan Mechanism: a superpixel generator partitions intermediate features into R=16 perceptually coherent regions, a permutation operator reorders flattened tokens so same-region tokens are contiguous, and the selective scan operator propagates state only within these semantic groups before an inverse permutation restores spatial layout. A Region-level Gating Mechanism (RGM) computes per-region mean and variance statistics, passes them through a two-layer MLP to produce channel-wise sigmoid gating factors, and modulates each token's contribution to the hidden state to suppress degradation outliers within each superpixel. Training uses a combined L1 pixel loss and a Pearson correlation loss on flattened sequences, with AdamW and cosine annealing over 300K iterations. The full model uses only 7.05M parameters and 54.27G FLOPs, substantially smaller than competing Transformer and SSM baselines.

## Results

SSR achieves the best average PSNR of 34.15 dB and SSIM of 0.9442 across four synthetic benchmarks (Snow100K-S, Snow100K-L, Outdoor, RainDrop). On the most challenging Outdoor dataset (joint rain+haze), SSR reaches 33.22 dB PSNR, outperforming the second-best method CPLPromptIR by 1.06 dB. The model uses only 7.05M parameters and 54.27G FLOPs, making it the most parameter-efficient method in the comparison while also requiring less than half the FLOPs of Restormer (141G) and EVSSM (122G). On real-world datasets, SSR achieves the best no-reference scores (Q-Align, NIQE, IL-NIQE) across Snow100K-Real, RainDrop-real, NTURain-real, and RainDS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSR outperforms all 18 compared baselines on the Outdoor benchmark (33.22 dB vs. 32.16 dB for CPLPromptIR, 32.08 dB for Histoformer, 32.06 dB for EVSSM), a margin of 1.06 dB over the strongest competitor. On Snow100K-L and RainDrop, SSR also achieves the best PSNR (32.70 and 32.82 dB respectively), surpassing CPLPromptIR by 0.43 and 0.09 dB. On Snow100K-S, SSR leads in PSNR (37.87 vs. 37.82 for CPLPromptIR) but trails slightly in SSIM (0.9656 vs. 0.9667), making it the only benchmark where it does not dominate both metrics. Against SSM-based competitors (MambaIRv2, EVSSM), SSR gains 0.5–1.0 dB PSNR across all four datasets, validating the superpixel-guided scanning advantage over raster or Hilbert trajectories. In terms of efficiency, SSR uses 7.05M parameters—80.4% fewer than CPLPromptIR (36M) and 57% fewer than Histoformer (16.61M)—while delivering superior restoration quality, and its 54.27G FLOPs is less than half that of Restormer (141G) or EVSSM (122G).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Mamba/SSM architecture for image restoration (MambaIR, VMambaIR) and repurposes superpixel clustering (from lightweight super-resolution work [55]) as a scanning-order controller. It departs from the prevailing content-agnostic scanning paradigm in SSM-based restoration—where raster, window, or Hilbert trajectories are fixed regardless of image content—by making the scan order semantically adaptive. The Region-level Gating Mechanism (RGM) extends the selective state update with per-superpixel channel modulation, a design not present in prior SSM restoration blocks. In the broader all-in-one weather restoration lineage (TransWeather → Histoformer → AWRCP → CPLPromptIR), SSR is the first to replace the Transformer backbone with a superpixel-guided SSM while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.05M-parameter footprint achieving SOTA (34.15 dB avg PSNR) at 54.27G FLOPs is practically significant for deployment-constrained settings, offering an 80% parameter reduction over the prior best (CPLPromptIR, 36M) with a 1.06 dB gain on the hardest benchmark. However, the paper itself acknowledges that practical inference speed (0.895 s) still lags behind optimized Transformers (0.716 s for Histoformer) due to immature SSM CUDA kernels and the irregular memory access patterns of superpixel permutation. An open robustness question remains: under extreme degradations (dense snow, torrential rain), the superpixel clustering that drives the entire scanning strategy may fragment or mis-segment, degrading the semantic guidance into noise-fitting paths. Future work on degradation-robust clustering and hardware-efficient SSM kernels will determine whether this semantic-guided scanning paradigm scales beyond the current benchmark regime.
