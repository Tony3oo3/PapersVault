## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) require a predefined concept set—either expert-annotated or LLM-generated from class names—which introduces label bias, limits dataset-specific discovery, and is costly to scale. Additionally, standard CBMs suffer from information leakage, where unmodeled visual features bypass the concept bottleneck and compromise the faithfulness of explanations.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input and produces a class label plus per-class open-vocabulary concept sets as output. Each image is converted into K diverse captions (K=5, or K=3 for ImageNet-1K) by a frozen LMM, which are then deterministically censored to remove class names and taxonomy terms before feeding the text classifier. The discovered concepts are literal text spans extracted from the generated captions, not abstract latent variables, making them directly inspectable and dataset-specific.

## Method

CaBM replaces the structured concept vector layer with a free-form natural language bottleneck in three stages: (1) a frozen Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct LMM generates K diverse, attribute-centric captions per image using a structured prompt with varying sampling temperatures, followed by deterministic taxonomy censoring that strips class names and group terms; (2) a RoBERTa-base text classifier is trained independently on these censored captions (never observing pixels), predicting labels by averaging logits across K captions at inference; (3) post-hoc concept discovery proposes candidate phrases via gradient×embedding attribution on correctly classified samples, rescores them with span-masking (erasure) to measure logit drops, and consolidates them into compact per-class concept vocabularies via HDBSCAN semantic clustering. The key architectural insight is that the discrete text interface and independent training of the two stages eliminate information leakage by construction, while the free-form language space enables truly open-vocabulary, data-induced concept discovery without any externally proposed concept set.

## Results

CaBM achieves top-1 test accuracy of 96.42% (CIFAR-10), 81.14% (CIFAR-100), 76.92% (CUB-200), 93.68% (Food-101), 86.57% (Flowers-102), and 75.12% (ImageNet-1K) while operating strictly in the text domain at inference—no image features are accessed by the classifier. On concept quality (CUB-200), CaBM attains 0.92 LLM-judged Semantics and 0.52 Purity, surpassing VLG-CBM (0.86 Semantics) and HybridCBM (0.46 Semantics). Under NEC-controlled downstream evaluation, CaBM-discovered concepts yield consistent gains over LLM-generated concepts (e.g., 76.25 vs. 75.79 ANEC-5 on CUB). Human intervention via concept injection improves accuracy by +12.2 to +13.8 points on the hardest 20 misclassified classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaBM outperforms all five CNN-based CBM baselines (PCBM, LF-CBM, LaBo, VLG-CBM, PS-CBM) on five of six datasets, with the largest margins on CIFAR-100 (+9 to +29 points) and CUB-200 (+7 to +18 points); the sole exception is Flowers-102, where all CNN-based baselines (94.4–97.9%) exceed CaBM's 86.57%. Against the stronger ViT-based HybridCBM, CaBM trails by 1.5–8.3 points across all six benchmarks, and it also falls short of CBM-Suite on CIFAR-100 (81.14 vs. 92.50), CUB (76.92 vs. 86.73), and ImageNet-1K (75.12 vs. 81.56). Compared to XBM, which retains image features at inference, CaBM loses on CUB (76.92 vs. 80.99) but wins on ImageNet-1K (75.12 vs. 67.83). On concept quality, CaBM matches VLG-CBM in Purity (0.52) while substantially improving Semantics (0.92 vs. 0.86) and Separation (0.16 vs. 0.07), and it surpasses HybridCBM in both Purity (0.52 vs. 0.40) and Semantics (0.92 vs. 0.46) despite lower Separation. Under the NEC-controlled downstream protocol, CaBM-discovered concepts consistently outperform VLG-CBM's LLM-generated concepts (e.g., 88.87 vs. 88.55 ANEC-5 on CIFAR-10; 76.25 vs. 75.79 on CUB), confirming that the data-driven extraction yields a more discriminative vocabulary.

## Relation to Prior Work

CaBM extends the Concept Bottleneck Model paradigm (Koh et al., 2020) by replacing the structured concept vector layer with free-form natural language captions generated by a frozen LMM, then training an independent text classifier on those captions. It departs from open-vocabulary CBMs (LaBo, VLG-CBM, HybridCBM, OpenCBM) that still require an externally proposed or LLM-generated concept set, and from Explanation Bottleneck Models (XBM) that retain image features at inference, by enforcing a strictly text-only recognition channel that is leakage-free by construction. The post-hoc concept extraction pipeline (gradient×embedding attribution → span-masking erasure → HDBSCAN clustering) draws on interpretability tools from NLP but applies them to recover dataset-specific, open-vocabulary concepts without any predefined dictionary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CaBM addresses two persistent practical pain points in CBMs—the cost and bias of specifying concept sets, and information leakage through soft concept layers—by making the bottleneck a discrete text interface that the classifier cannot shortcut. The key limitation is that CaBM is interpretable-by-design and cannot be retrofitted onto existing black-box vision encoders (unlike PCBM), restricting its use to greenfield deployments. An open question is how well the approach generalizes to tasks where visual evidence is extremely fine-grained or spatially structured (e.g., medical imaging, remote sensing), where free-form captions may lose critical discriminative detail. The competitive accuracy gap to ViT-backed CBMs (e.g., ~7 points on CUB) also suggests that the LMM's captioning fidelity remains a ceiling on performance.
