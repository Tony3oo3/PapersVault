## Problem

Multimodal LLMs excel at visual understanding but cannot generate images, while state-of-the-art generation systems operate in VAE latent spaces disconnected from semantic representation spaces. The core contradiction is that semantic encoders discard pixel-level details during representation learning, making direct generation in semantic space produce blurry, distorted outputs, yet fine-tuning the encoder for reconstruction triggers catastrophic forgetting of its discriminative features. Additionally, existing unified models rely on external visual encoders as alignment teachers, creating manifold mismatches between understanding and generation spaces.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model accepts a text prompt and produces a 256×256 RGB image via a diffusion transformer conditioned on learnable query embeddings inserted at reserved positions in the MLLM token sequence. For editing, it takes a reference image plus a text instruction and outputs a modified image. For understanding, it processes an image and text query to produce a text response. The tokenizer operates on 256×256 inputs with a 32× compression ratio, mapping to a compact latent space shared between the semantic and pixel streams.

## Method

SPAR introduces an asymmetric dual-stream unified tokenizer where a lightweight semantic stream (3 residual blocks) anchors the frozen encoder's discriminative features with minimal transformation, while a heavier pixel stream (6-layer Transformer encoder) bridges the dimensional gap to recover high-frequency spatial details discarded by the encoder's lossy compression; both streams fuse in a compact latent space before decoding. Training follows a progressive three-stage strategy: dual-stream initialization with pixel alignment loss, joint decoder training, and encoder fine-tuning with semantic self-distillation (frozen-encoder feature matching plus GAN) to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Dynamic Token Routing (DTR) enables each token to adaptively compute softmax-weighted fusion over K uniformly sampled MLLM layers, using the deepest-layer features as routing queries to satisfy differentiated hierarchical demands. The self-aligned generation paradigm leverages the dual-stream tokenizer itself as an internal alignment teacher for the DiT via cosine-similarity matching on intermediate DiT features, eliminating dependence on external representation networks.

## Results

SPAR-3B achieves a GenEval overall score of 0.91 and WISE overall of 0.64, ranking first among all compared unified and generation-only models. On ImageNet 50k reconstruction (256×256), SPAR attains rFID 0.27, PSNR 26.65, and SSIM 0.856, outperforming all unified tokenizers and remaining competitive with the best generative-only tokenizer (VA-VAE, rFID 0.28). On visual understanding, SPAR-3B matches or slightly exceeds the understanding-only InternVL3-2B baseline (e.g., MME-P 80.7 vs. 80.6, MMB 48.7 vs. 48.2), confirming no degradation from adding generation. On ImgEdit-Bench, SPAR-3B scores 4.01 overall, the best among open-source methods and within 0.19 of GPT-4o (4.20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPAR-3B (3B params) outperforms all unified models on GenEval (0.91 vs. BAGEL 0.82, OpenUni-L 0.85, Tar 0.84) and WISE (0.64 vs. BAGEL 0.52, OpenUni-L 0.52), and also surpasses the 12B FLUX.1-dev generation-only model (GenEval 0.82, WISE 0.50). Even the smaller SPAR-1B (1B) achieves GenEval 0.89 and WISE 0.57, exceeding all unified baselines including 7B models. On reconstruction, SPAR's rFID of 0.27 beats the best unified tokenizer DualToken (0.54) by 2× and matches the best generative-only VA-VAE (0.28) while achieving higher SSIM (0.856 vs. 0.790). On understanding, SPAR-3B slightly exceeds the understanding-only InternVL3-2B (MMB 48.7 vs. 48.2, SEED 73.3 vs. 72.7), showing the dual-stream design preserves and even enhances discriminative features. On image editing, SPAR-3B (4.01) leads all open-source methods by a wide margin over OmniGen2 (3.44) and UniWorld-V1 (3.26), approaching the proprietary GPT-4o (4.20). The ablation confirms both DTR and self-alignment contribute: removing DTR drops GenEval from 0.89 to 0.86, and removing the alignment loss drops WISE from 0.57 to 0.53.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of unified multimodal models (Janus-Pro, BAGEL, BLIP3-o, Show-O) and semantic-space generation (RAE, VA-VAE), which have shown that diffusion can operate in discriminative feature spaces but suffer from poor pixel reconstruction. SPAR departs from prior unified tokenizers (TokenFlow, DualViTok, DualToken) that force a single representation stream to serve both understanding and generation, instead explicitly decoupling these objectives through an asymmetric dual-stream design. It also breaks from the representation-guided generation paradigm (which aligns diffusion with external encoders like DINOv2) by proposing a self-alignment paradigm where the unified tokenizer itself serves as the internal alignment teacher, eliminating the dependency on disjointed external feature networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental tension in unified multimodal models: semantic encoders optimized for discriminative tasks inherently discard pixel-level details needed for high-fidelity generation, and naively fine-tuning them triggers catastrophic forgetting. The asymmetric dual-stream design and self-alignment paradigm offer a clean, well-motivated solution that practitioners building unified models can adopt. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions (experiments are at 256×256), whether the self-alignment paradigm generalizes to video or 3D generation, and whether the GAN discriminator in Stage III is strictly necessary or whether the dual-stream design alone suffices at larger scale.
