## Problem

Existing statistical shape analysis methods for tree-like 3D objects either model only the skeletal branching topology while ignoring 3D branch geometry, or approximate branch surfaces with local cylinders of fixed radius, losing detailed surface information. No prior framework simultaneously captures the full 3D surface geometry of individual branches and the structural variability in how branches interconnect, bifurcate, and change in number across a population of tree-shaped objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261470/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D tree surface meshes (50 to 15,000 branches) as input, where each branch is parameterized as a smooth surface over a unit cylinder Ω = [0,1]×S¹ and the full tree is encoded as a recursive hierarchy with bifurcation points on medial axes, normalized for translation and optional scale. Outputs include branch-wise and point-wise correspondences between trees, geodesic deformation paths, the Karcher mean shape, principal modes of variation via PCA, and novel tree shapes sampled from a fitted multivariate Gaussian. The tangent-based representation used for geodesics and statistics admits analytical inversion via cumulative trapezoidal integration, avoiding the expensive numerical inversion required by the original SRNF map.

## Method

The core contribution is generalizing the Square Root Normal Field (SRNF) representation from genus-0 surfaces to hierarchically branched 3D tree shapes, creating a novel Riemannian tree-shape space (TreeSRNF) with a recursive elastic metric that jointly measures branch bending/stretching (L² distance in SRNF space), bifurcation-point displacement (topological variation), and subtree geometric differences, weighted by tunable parameters (λ_m, λ_p, λ_s). Registration is solved iteratively: a global SO(3) rotation is first optimized, then at each hierarchical level the Hungarian algorithm matches side branches via a pairwise cost matrix combining bifurcation distance and SRNF alignment, while local and global reparameterizations are optimized per branch. For geodesics and statistical summarization, the authors introduce an equivalent tangent-vector representation (two orthogonal unit tangents scaled by √‖F_u‖ and √‖F_v‖) that is provably equivalent to SRNF under the cylindrical parameterization assumption but supports analytical inversion, enabling efficient linear interpolation for geodesics and standard linear PCA for modes of variation. A multivariate Gaussian with diagonal covariance (eigenvalues from PCA) is fitted to the registered tangent-space shapes to generate novel plausible trees by sampling within one standard deviation.

## Results

On 16 pairs of 3D tree shapes, the proposed method reduces mean geodesic distance error from 152.6 (unregistered) to 75.3, compared to 140 for Pan et al. [27]. Cycle consistency error at ε=0.1 is 0.1% (ours) versus 20.5% (Pan et al.), and at ε=0.01 it is 3% versus 33.5%. Registration of trees with 50–15,000 branches takes 43 s to 20 min on average, versus 2.5 min to 6 h for Pan et al. Geodesic computation averages 1.5 s and single-tree generation takes 0.01 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Pan et al. [27] (multi-scale space-time registration, 3DV 2021), the proposed method wins decisively on all four evaluation metrics: geodesic distance error drops to 75.3 vs. 140 (mean), cycle consistency error is 0.1% vs. 20.5% at ε=0.1, cumulative energy (description length) is higher, and computation time is 43 s–20 min vs. 2.5 min–6 h. Against Wang et al. [31] (extended SRVF with radial thickness, IEEE TPAMI 2023), the paper demonstrates superior representation fidelity via lower pointwise surface error (Fig. 3) and more faithful geodesic paths that preserve branch geometry (Fig. 6), though direct registration metrics cannot be compared because Wang et al. only computes skeleton-level correspondences rather than full surface correspondences. The paper notes that Wang et al.'s radial approximation causes visible geometry loss and excessive shrinkage along geodesics for trees with significant structural differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Square Root Normal Field (SRNF) representation from Jermyn et al. (ECCV 2012), originally designed for genus-0 surfaces, to tree-shaped 3D objects with variable branching structure. It builds on the recursive tree-shape metric of Duncan et al. (2018) for neuronal trees and departs from Wang et al. (TPAMI 2023), which approximated branch geometry via skeletal curves augmented with local thickness, by instead encoding the full 3D surface of each branch. A key methodological departure is the introduction of a tangent-vector-based shape space that is provably equivalent to SRNF under the cylindrical parameterization assumption, enabling analytical inversion for geodesic visualization without the expensive numerical inversion of the SRNF map.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly model full 3D surface geometry and branching-structure variability in tree-shaped objects, enabling registration, geodesic computation, PCA-based statistical summarization, and generative sampling for botanical trees and plants. The practical impact is significant for plant phenotyping, neuroscience (neuronal trees), and vascular network analysis, where prior methods lost detailed surface information through skeletal or radial approximations. Limitations include reliance on the cylindrical (orthogonal) parameterization assumption for the tangent-SRNF equivalence, validation primarily on botanical trees and tomato plants, and the need for null-branch padding when trees have different branch counts. The authors note future work on 4D spatiotemporal extension and broader tree-like structures.
