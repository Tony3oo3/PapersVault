## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting training remains a critical bottleneck (tens of minutes to hours) despite its real-time rendering capability, and existing acceleration methods treat GPU-level optimization and algorithmic design in isolation, leaving deep systemic bottlenecks unresolved: atomic data conflicts in gradient reduction, poor spatial locality from disordered Gaussian storage, and ambiguous densification metrics that produce suboptimal geometric growth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260717/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

LiteGS consumes a set of multi-view RGB images and an initial SFM point cloud, optimizing per-Gaussian parameters (3D position, covariance, RGB color, opacity) via supervised photometric loss. The output is a 3D Gaussian scene representation rendered in real-time for novel views. The framework supports two training regimes: a default 30K-iteration schedule for high-quality reconstruction and an aggressive 10K-iteration variant (LiteGS*) that trades parameter count for sub-minute convergence, with parameter scale highly configurable across both.

## Method

LiteGS is built on a system-algorithm codesign with three tightly coupled modules. At the GPU layer, a warp-based rasterization paradigm assigns one warp per tile with multiple pixels per thread, accumulating gradients in local registers before a single global write, and employs forward differencing (reducing per-pixel state from 7 to 3 variables) plus FP16 mixed-precision blending to maintain high occupancy. At the data layer, a Cluster-Cull-Compact pipeline uses 64-bit Morton codes for periodic spatial sorting, groups primitives into 128-element clusters for early AABB frustum culling, and compacts visible clusters into contiguous memory for coalesced access. At the algorithm layer, a variance-guided densification metric uses the spatial variance of per-pixel opacity gradients (computed at zero overhead by the warp rasterizer) to distinguish genuine geometric deficiency (high variance) from mere unconvergence (low variance), paired with gentle opacity decay instead of hard resets; this robust signal safely unlocks a 'space-for-time' aggressive splitting strategy that accelerates convergence around saddle points, compressing training to sub-minute timescales.

## Results

In the high-quality regime, LiteGS achieves 28.14 PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360, 24.61 PSNR on Tanks & Temples, and 30.36 PSNR on Deep Blending, surpassing the quality SOTA 3DGS-MCMC (28.08 / 24.29 / 29.67) while training 10×–18× faster (e.g., 128 s vs. 2319 s on Deep Blending) with roughly one-third the parameters. In the fast-reconstruction regime, LiteGS* reaches 27.60 PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360 in just 64 s (sub-minute), and on an RTX 4090 compresses further to 29–45 s across the three datasets. The warp-based rasterizer alone delivers a 7×–13× speedup over the original 3DGS atomic rasterizer and 2× over Tensor-Core-based methods at 5.7 M primitives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the quality SOTA 3DGS-MCMC, LiteGS wins on every metric: +0.06 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360, +0.32 dB on Tanks & Temples, and +0.69 dB on Deep Blending, while training 16×, 10×, and 18× faster respectively, using only ~1/3 of the Gaussian parameters. In the fast-reconstruction regime, LiteGS outperforms FastGS on PSNR (27.62 vs. 27.56 on Mip-NeRF 360) and is 1.7× faster (127 s vs. 215 s); the aggressive LiteGS* variant is 3.4× faster than FastGS (64 s vs. 215 s) while matching its PSNR. Compared to Mini-Splatting2*, LiteGS* is 3.3× faster (64 s vs. 214 s) with slightly higher PSNR (27.60 vs. 27.33). Against DashGaussian in the high-quality setting, LiteGS gains +0.48 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360 and is 2.1× faster (194 s vs. 412 s). The warp-based rasterizer also outperforms the Tensor-Core-based TC-GS by 2× in forward-pass latency at 5.7 M primitives, confirming that the system-level redesign surpasses hardware-specific tricks.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiteGS builds directly on the 3DGS formulation (Kerbl et al. 2023) and adapts the Cluster-Cull-Compact pipeline from Nanite virtualized geometry (Karis et al. 2021) to the dynamic training loop, while repurposing classical forward differencing (Heckbert 1989) for register compression in the Gaussian exponent evaluation. Its key departure from prior acceleration work (DISTWAR, Taming 3DGS, TC-GS) is the system-algorithm codesign philosophy: rather than patching a single bottleneck (atomic updates, per-splat backprop, or tensor-core mapping), LiteGS co-optimizes the GPU execution model, memory layout, and densification metric as a coupled system where each module reinforces the others. The variance-guided densification criterion is novel in using the spatial distribution of opacity gradients rather than their magnitude, a signal that becomes computable at zero overhead only because of the warp-based rasterizer's pixel-level statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-minute training milestone (64s on RTX 3090, ~30s on RTX 4090) and the 10×–18× speedup over the quality SOTA (3DGS-MCMC) while using roughly one-third the parameters make 3DGS practical for latency-sensitive applications like autonomous driving and VR where re-training or rapid scene updates are needed. The 'space-for-time' aggressive densification strategy is a useful conceptual contribution: it shows that when system throughput is decoupled from parameter count, one can trade parameter efficiency for convergence speed without quality loss. Open questions include whether the warp-based rasterization and cluster pipeline scale to very large scenes (tens of millions of Gaussians), whether the variance-guided metric generalizes to multi-resolution or unbounded scenes, and whether the aggressive densification strategy remains stable under more challenging occlusion patterns.
