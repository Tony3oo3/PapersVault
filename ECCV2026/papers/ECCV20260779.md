## Problem

Existing remote sensing image change captioning (RSICC) methods rely on conventional deep learning architectures whose limited model capacity constrains performance, while large vision-language models have not been post-trained for RSICC due to the scarcity of task-specific instruction and preference data. No framework exists that adapts a large VLM through supervised fine-tuning and preference alignment specifically for describing bi-temporal changes in remote sensing imagery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260779/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a pair of co-registered bi-temporal RGB remote sensing images (pre-change and post-change) as input and generates a free-form natural language caption describing the land-surface transitions between them. During data generation, binary change masks serve as geometric priors to condition the captioning model, but at inference time only the two images are required. The generated captions are fine-grained (average ~77 words) and structured around object, direction, change verb, and quantity elements.

## Method

RSICCLLM is a three-stage post-training framework built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. First, a data generation paradigm uses Qwen-VL-Max conditioned on binary change masks to synthesize ~35k instruction samples (RSICI) from LEVIR-CD and SYSU-CD. Second, Difference-aware Supervised Fine-tuning injects change representations into the vision encoder by computing a pixel-difference map T and a geometric-texture feature ΔS derived from eight CDConv-inspired kernels processed through Hough Transform voting, then fusing T and ΔS via cross-attention (T as query, ΔS as key/value) and adding the result to the original image embeddings before SFT. Third, Dual-Negative Preference Optimization (DNPO) constructs preference pairs using two complementary negative-sample strategies—info-filtered selection (choosing model-generated captions whose keyword-filtered BLEU/ROUGE similarity to ground truth falls within a threshold band) and replacement-based construction (swapping keywords within the ground truth from a contrastive pool grouped by part-of-speech)—and trains with a combined DPO loss plus a reverse-KL stabilization term to prevent policy collapse.

## Results

On the in-domain RSICI test set, RSICCLLM (7B) achieves BLEU-1 of 81.28, BLEU-4 of 62.78, ROUGE-1 of 53.54, ROUGE-L of 38.30, and Sentence-BERT Similarity (SBS) of 82.72, outperforming all 11 baselines across all 8 metrics. On the out-of-domain test set (S2Looking + CDD), it still leads with BLEU-1 of 69.51 and SBS of 73.86. In terms of efficiency, RSICCLLM processes 0.631 samples/s with only 7B parameters, roughly 6× faster than Qwen3-VL-235B (0.104 samples/s) while scoring ~11 SBS points higher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSICCLLM (7B) decisively outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric in both in-domain and out-of-domain evaluations. Against the strongest general-purpose VLMs (Qwen-VL-Max, Qwen3-VL-235B, InternVL-3.5-241B), it achieves roughly 3× higher BLEU-1 (81.28 vs. 26–28) and 10–12 higher SBS (82.72 vs. 71–73), despite being 30–35× smaller in parameter count. Against domain-specific change-captioning models (TEOChat-7B, CCExpert-7B), the gap is even more dramatic: BLEU-1 jumps from ~8.6 to 81.28 (~9×), and SBS from ~41 to 82.72. The base Qwen2.5-VL-7B without any RSICC-specific training scores only 7.40 BLEU-1 and 42.06 SBS, confirming that the post-training pipeline (Difference-aware SFT + DNPO) is responsible for the entire performance gain. In terms of throughput, RSICCLLM processes 0.631 samples/s versus 0.104 for Qwen3-VL-235B and 0.096 for InternVL-3.5-241B, making it ~6× faster while delivering superior accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of RSICC work (Hoxha et al., Liu et al.) that relies on conventional CNN/Transformer architectures with limited model capacity, and instead repurposes the post-training paradigm (SFT + preference optimization) that has proven successful in general-purpose VLMs. The Difference-aware SFT stage creatively repurposes Central Difference Convolution (originally from face anti-spoofing) and Hough Transform (from shape detection) to inject explicit change representations into a frozen VLM's vision encoder, while DNPO extends standard DPO with two complementary negative-sample construction strategies tailored to the open-ended nature of change captions. The data generation paradigm of using a powerful MLLM (Qwen-VL-Max) with binary masks as geometric priors to synthesize instruction data from existing CD datasets is a novel pipeline that bridges the data scarcity gap in RSICC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 7B domain-adapted model can outperform 235B+ general-purpose VLMs by ~11 SBS points while achieving 6× higher throughput, this work provides a practical, deployable solution for RSICC that was previously infeasible with conventional architectures. The release of RSICI (40k instruction samples) and RSICP (20k preference pairs) — the first preference dataset for RSICC — establishes critical infrastructure for future research in this area. Open questions include whether the Difference-aware SFT components (CDConv, Hough Transform) generalize to other bi-temporal tasks beyond building/land-cover changes, whether DNPO's negative-sample strategies transfer to other open-ended generation tasks, and how the approach scales to higher-resolution or multi-temporal (beyond bi-temporal) settings.
