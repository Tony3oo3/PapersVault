## Problem

Foundation models for monocular depth estimation achieve strong zero-shot generalization across diverse scenes but require 300–900M parameters and hundreds of GFLOPs, making them infeasible on embedded and mobile hardware. Lightweight depth networks (1–8M parameters) run in real time on edge devices but have been trained exclusively in single-domain, self-supervised settings and degrade severely under domain shift. ZipDepth addresses this gap by showing that a compact CNN can inherit cross-domain generalization from a foundation-model teacher while remaining deployable on power-constrained hardware.

## Input / Output

The network accepts a single RGB image (any aspect ratio; at inference the shortest side is resized to 384 px) and produces a full-resolution affine-invariant inverse-depth map at the original spatial dimensions (effective output stride 1). Because the output is scale-and-shift invariant, predictions must be aligned to ground truth via least-squares fitting before metric evaluation. The architecture is designed so that all operators map natively to TensorRT, CoreML, and ONNX Runtime without custom kernels or graph surgery.

## Method

ZipDepth is a 6.1M-parameter encoder-decoder CNN whose encoder uses RepVGG-style reparameterizable blocks (three parallel branches fused into a single 3×3 conv at inference) across four stages at strides 4–32, augmented with strip-pooling attention, squeeze-and-excitation, a global-context block, and SPPF for multi-scale context. The decoder performs coarse-to-fine FPN fusion and a dedicated half-resolution skip path, then upsamples to full resolution via a hardware-adaptive convex upsampling module: a GPU/TensorRT path using per-pixel softmax-weighted 3×3 neighbour blending (adapted from RAFT), and an NPU/mobile path using a learned gate that blends nearest-neighbour and bilinear interpolation. Training uses large-scale knowledge distillation from Depth Anything v2-Large as a teacher over 14.1M images spanning 17 heterogeneous domains, with a scale-and-shift-invariant L1 loss plus multi-scale gradient loss, requiring only two RTX 3090 GPUs for three days.

## Results

ZipDepth (6.1M params, 3.0 GMACs) achieves the best accuracy-efficiency trade-off among lightweight models across five zero-shot benchmarks: AR of 8.4 on NYUv2, 12.3 on KITTI, 10.0 on ETH3D, 8.8 on ScanNet, and 22.6 on DIODE, with δ₁ of 93.3, 86.4, 92.2, 92.1, and 73.9 respectively. On a Jetson Orin NX at 15 W, it runs at 34.4 FPS consuming ~397 mJ/frame — 17× less energy than DA-V2-Small and 137× less than DA-V2-Large. The gap to its teacher (DA-V2-Large) ranges from ~1.6 AR (DIODE) to ~4.6 AR (KITTI), while using 55× fewer parameters and over 200× less compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among lightweight models, ZipDepth achieves the best AR on NYUv2 (8.4 vs. Lite-Mono's 8.7) and ScanNet (8.8 vs. 9.6), and the highest δ₁ on DIODE (73.9 vs. 72.9). Lite-Mono, the strongest lightweight competitor, outperforms ZipDepth on KITTI (AR 10.9 vs. 12.3) and ETH3D (AR 8.7 vs. 10.0), but at 4.5× higher GMACs (13.5 vs. 3.0) and 4.7× lower throughput (7.3 vs. 34.4 FPS). Against the foundation-model baselines, ZipDepth trails DA-V2-Large by 1.6–4.6 AR depending on the benchmark, yet uses 55× fewer parameters and 200× less compute; it also consumes 17× less energy per frame than DA-V2-Small (397 vs. 6740 mJ) and 137× less than DA-V2-Large (397 vs. 54457 mJ). MiDaS-Small, GuideDepth, FastDepth, and PyDNet all show substantially worse zero-shot accuracy (AR 10–26 on KITTI) despite similar or lower parameter counts, confirming that single-domain self-supervised training is the primary bottleneck. The DPT and Omnidata-v2 baselines, while more accurate than lightweight models, still run at only 1.7 FPS on the Jetson and consume 8+ J/frame, making them impractical for energy-constrained deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZipDepth sits at the intersection of two previously incompatible lines of work: foundation-model depth estimators (Depth Anything v2, Marigold, DPT) that achieve strong zero-shot generalization but require 300–900M parameters and high-end GPUs, and lightweight self-supervised networks (PyDNet, FastDepth, GuideDepth, Lite-Mono) that run in real time on embedded hardware but degrade severely under domain shift. The paper departs from the lightweight lineage by replacing single-domain self-supervised training with large-scale knowledge distillation from a foundation teacher over 14.1M multi-domain images, and from the foundation-model lineage by replacing ViT/diffusion backbones with a reparameterizable CNN whose operators map natively to TensorRT, CoreML, and NNAPI. The hardware-adaptive convex upsampling (adapting RAFT's convex combination to depth) and the split-stem half-resolution skip are architectural contributions specific to this deployment-oriented design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZipDepth demonstrates that the zero-shot accuracy of foundation models can be largely captured in a 6.1M-parameter network trainable on two consumer GPUs in three days, enabling real-time dense depth on smartphones, drones, and embedded SLAM systems where foundation models are simply infeasible (0.3 FPS at 15 W for DA-V2-Large vs. 34–77 FPS for ZipDepth). The data-scale ablation showing continued gains up to 14.1M images suggests the compact architecture is not yet saturated, leaving room for further improvement. Key open questions include closing the remaining ~2–5 AR gap to the teacher, extending to metric depth or point-map prediction, and addressing temporal flickering on video sequences, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation of the single-image formulation.
