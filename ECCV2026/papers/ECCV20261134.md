## Problem

Pixel-aligned Gaussian splatting methods face a fundamental quality-efficiency dilemma: scaling input resolution to match the target output causes quadratic backbone computation growth, while rendering from low-resolution inputs produces insufficient Gaussian density, leading to blur and halo artifacts. No existing feed-forward pixel-aligned method can simultaneously achieve high-resolution output fidelity and stable, low-latency inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261134/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes exactly two sparse-view RGB images at a low resolution (e.g., 256×256) along with their camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and produces high-resolution novel-view renderings at 2× or 4× the input resolution (512×512 or 1024×1024). The key design constraint is that the backbone network always operates at the lower input resolution, while the output primitive density is decoupled from the backbone's computational grid via a subdivision factor K = (target/input)², so the same model generalizes across absolute scales as long as the ratio is preserved.

## Method

SubSplat introduces a Sub-pixel Gaussian Reparameterizer (SPGR) that subdivides each grid-anchored primary Gaussian into K fine-grained sub-pixel primitives using two dedicated prediction heads: a geometry head predicting sub-pixel offsets, depth residuals, log-scale residuals, and axis-angle rotations, and an appearance head predicting opacity distribution logits and bounded color modulation gains. Before subdivision, a three-stage deformable attention module aggregates multi-view geometry and appearance features, with the geometry descriptor conditioning the appearance path to ensure structural consistency. A footprint-aware opacity redistribution mechanism weights primitives inversely by their projected screen-space area, concentrating opacity on smaller primitives to sharpen edges while conserving total opacity (Σα_k = α_p). The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a weighted L2 + LPIPS loss against ground-truth high-resolution images, requiring no per-scene optimization.

## Results

On RealEstate10K, SubSplat achieves 25.52 PSNR / 0.850 SSIM / 0.167 LPIPS at ×2 (512×512) and 22.65 PSNR / 0.781 SSIM / 0.268 LPIPS at ×4 (1024×1024), all from 256×256 inputs, outperforming every baseline by 2.3–6.1 dB PSNR. On ACID, it reaches 26.03 PSNR at ×2 and 23.35 PSNR at ×4. In the cost-performance comparison (Table 2), SubSplat runs at 42 ms latency and 1.57 GB peak memory, versus 123–1960 ms and 4.27–9.56 GB for full-resolution baselines, while matching or exceeding their quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SubSplat outperforms all five pixel-aligned/depth-guided baselines on every metric at both ×2 and ×4 scales across both datasets. Against the strongest baseline HiSplat, SubSplat gains +2.26 dB PSNR at ×2 and +0.60 dB at ×4 on RealEstate10K, with the gap widening to +2.44 dB at ×2 on ACID. Against MVSplat (the backbone), the improvement is +6.06 dB PSNR at ×2 on RealEstate10K, confirming that the SPGR subdivision is the primary source of quality gain. PixelSplat, which assigns three Gaussians per pixel, degrades less than other baselines at higher scales but still trails SubSplat by 2.08 dB at ×2. In the efficiency comparison (Table 2), SubSplat at 42 ms / 1.57 GB is 3× faster than MVSplat (131 ms) and 47× faster than HiSplat (1960 ms) while matching or exceeding their PSNR. Against image-space upsamplers (Table 3), SubSplat beats MVSplat+HiT-SR by +1.64 dB PSNR and is 5× faster (42 ms vs. 215 ms), demonstrating that 3D-level densification is superior to 2D post-processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

SubSplat builds directly on the MVSplat pixel-aligned backbone and the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering framework, but departs from all prior pixel-aligned methods (PixelSplat, MVSplat, HiSplat, TranSplat, DepthSplat) by decoupling output primitive density from backbone input resolution. Where prior work either scales input resolution (incurring quadratic backbone cost) or accepts quality degradation at higher output scales, SubSplat introduces a Sub-pixel Gaussian Reparameterizer that subdivides each grid-anchored Gaussian into K fine-grained primitives, borrowing the deformable-attention mechanism from Deformable DETR for multi-view feature aggregation and the footprint-aware anti-aliasing formulations from Analytic-Splatting and Anti-Aliased 2DGS for opacity redistribution. The conceptual lineage is thus: 3DGS → pixel-aligned feed-forward splatting → sub-pixel subdivision as a resolution-scaling mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SubSplat resolves a fundamental quality-efficiency trade-off that has constrained pixel-aligned Gaussian splatting to fixed input-output resolution coupling, enabling real-time (24 FPS) high-resolution novel-view synthesis from low-resolution inputs with 3×+ latency reduction over full-resolution baselines. The practical impact is significant for AR/VR and mobile deployment where display resolution far exceeds what backbone networks can process in real time. Open questions include whether the fixed K-subdivision strategy (K = (target/input)²) can be replaced with content-aware density control, whether the approach generalizes beyond ×4 scale factors, and how the method behaves under extreme viewpoint changes where the primary-view anchoring assumption may degrade.
