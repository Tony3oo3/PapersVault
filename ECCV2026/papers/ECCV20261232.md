## Problem

Federated Class Incremental Learning (FCIL) suffers from Spatial-Temporal Catastrophic Forgetting (ST-CF), where client heterogeneity and sequential task forgetting reinforce each other. Existing methods address spatial and temporal interference with separate mechanisms during local optimization, but leave the directional interactions among adaptation vectors during server-side aggregation unregulated, allowing redundant or destructive updates to accumulate and distort the global model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261232/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Clients perform standard local training on non-IID partitions of RGB images (ViT-B/16 backbone) or text (T5-Small) and upload full model parameters exactly as in conventional FedAvg. The server produces two outputs: (1) a spatially aggregated global model for continued training, and (2) a compact inference module per task consisting of a unified direction vector τ_uni, a binary task-specific mask m_k, and a scalar rescaling factor s_k, which together reconstruct the task model as θ_pre + m_k ⊙ (s_k · τ_uni) without requiring the full parameter set on the client.

## Method

SUM reinterprets FCIL as a unified multi-task learning problem where both client updates (spatial axis) and task updates (temporal axis) are adaptation vectors in a shared parameter space, then performs geometric surgery via orthogonal projection at aggregation time. Spatial SUM projects each client's adaptation vector against all other clients' vectors within a round (with Z-score trimming to suppress outlier coordinates), removing directionally coupled components while preserving residual client-specific directions. Temporal SUM re-anchors each task's accumulated update to a common pretrained base and applies an online sequential projection against previously stored refined task vectors, ensuring the common-base condition required by the theoretical descent bound. After temporal surgery, refined task vectors are converted into compact inference modules through magnitude-based sparsification, a coordinate-wise sign-consensus (Elect-Sign) step, and per-task binary activation masks with scalar rescaling. All operations are purely server-side, introducing zero additional client computation, communication, or memory beyond standard federated training.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (β=0.05), SUM achieves 96.32% Final Averaged Accuracy (FAA), surpassing the centralized Joint baseline (92.75%) and the strongest FCIL baseline LoRM (82.76%) by 3.57 and 13.56 points respectively. Across all six vision datasets and three distribution levels, SUM attains the highest FAA in every setting, with the largest gains on out-of-domain EuroSAT (67.57% vs. LoRM's 48.81% at β=0.2) and fine-grained CUB-200 (79.43% vs. 60.06%). In the language domain, SUM reaches 94.11% FAA on CLINC-150 (β=0.2), outperforming LoRM (77.67%) by 16.44 points and exceeding Joint (95.53%) under the most heterogeneous split.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUM outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and distribution level. The closest competitor, LoRM [41] (closed-form spatio-temporal merging), trails SUM by 13.6 points on CIFAR-100 (β=0.05), 20.4 points on ImageNet-R, and 16.4 points on CLINC-150 (language). FL-oriented baselines (CCVR [30], FedProto [48]) and CL-oriented baselines (EWC [18], LwF [26], L2P [51], CODA-P [47]) all suffer severe degradation under non-IID splits, with EWC dropping below 10% on ImageNet-A. Model-merging baselines (FisherAvg [33], RegMean [16]) perform poorly because they aggregate without resolving directional conflicts. FCIL-specific methods that modify client training (TARGET [61], PILoRA [8], FOT [3]) remain 10–30 points below SUM, confirming that server-side geometric surgery at aggregation time is more effective than client-side constraints. Notably, SUM even surpasses the centralized Joint training baseline on CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R, and EuroSAT, attributed to flatter minima and larger class margins achieved by removing destructive directional components.

## Relation to Prior Work

SUM reinterprets Federated Class Incremental Learning through the lens of multi-task learning, where both client updates (spatial axis) and task updates (temporal axis) are treated as adaptation vectors in a shared parameter space—a representation inherited from Task Arithmetic [15] and model merging literature [13, 53]. It departs from prior FCIL methods (GLFC, TARGET, FOT, STAMP, LoRM) that address spatial and temporal interference separately through client-side modifications such as replay, distillation, or gradient alignment during local optimization. The key conceptual departure is performing geometric surgery (orthogonal projection) purely at server-side aggregation time, borrowing the directional-conflict-resolution philosophy of MTL gradient surgery (PCGrad, CAGrad) but applying it post-training to communicated parameters rather than during optimization. For inference, SUM extends EMR-Merging [13] with a sparsification–sign-consensus–task-mask pipeline to produce compact deployable modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The purely server-side design means SUM can be dropped into any existing federated training pipeline without modifying client computation, communication, or memory—a significant practical advantage for deployment. The finding that SUM surpasses centralized Joint training on several datasets challenges the assumption that joint optimization is an oracle, suggesting that explicit geometric regulation of directional conflicts at aggregation time yields flatter minima and better class separation. Open questions include the O(TD) server memory scaling for long task sequences (mitigated but not eliminated by compression), whether the common-base anchoring assumption degrades over very many tasks, and whether the approach extends naturally to non-class-incremental or task-incremental settings. The method's simplicity (a single projection step) also invites investigation into whether richer geometric operations (e.g., non-orthogonal decompositions) could further improve performance.
