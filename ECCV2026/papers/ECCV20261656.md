## Problem

Aerial 6DoF localization for UAVs typically depends on precise GNSS signals or radiometrically rich 3D reconstructions that are memory-intensive, computationally demanding, and difficult to scale in urban environments. Existing lightweight alternatives based on contour or wireframe alignment (e.g., LoD-Loc) lack robustness in close-range urban canyon scenarios with repetitive facades and occlusions, and no benchmark combines centimeter-accurate poses with standardized CityGML LoD semantic models and challenging low-altitude imagery.

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution UAV RGB image (5280×3956 px, ~1.6 cm GSD), an initial 6DoF pose prior (x, y, z, φ, θ, ψ), and a standardized CityGML semantic 3D city model (LoD1–LoD3) as input, and outputs a refined 6DoF camera pose. Roll and pitch are held fixed from gravity/IMU measurements, so the search operates only over the 4D subspace (x, y, z, ψ). The semantic 3D model is rendered on-the-fly via nvdiffrast GPU rasterization with frustum culling, producing per-class semantic masks and depth maps at candidate poses for comparison against foundation-model predictions.

## Method

SemCityLoc performs coarse-to-fine semantic-geometric surface alignment. In the coarse stage, a 4D cost volume is built by uniformly sampling poses around the prior and computing per-class IoU between DINOv3+DPT-predicted semantic masks and masks rendered from the LoD city model; the maximum-IoU pose is selected. In the refinement stage, a particle filter iteratively perturbs the coarse pose and scores each hypothesis by jointly combining a high-resolution semantic IoU term and a monocular depth term (MoGe-2 depth aligned to rendered depth via per-match scale-shift least-squares, inversely weighted by depth). Only a lightweight DPT segmentation head is trained on the frozen DINOv3 backbone per dataset (~88% mIoU), while MoGe-2 operates zero-shot, keeping the system modular and training-efficient. The key insight is that aligning semantically partitioned surfaces (roof, wall) rather than sparse edges introduces region-level structural constraints that substantially increase pose observability in repetitive and occluded urban geometry.

## Results

On the introduced SemCityLockeD benchmark (challenging low-altitude urban canyons), SemCityLoc achieves 69.15% recall at the strict 2 m–2° threshold, nearly doubling LoD-Loc's 35.11%, and reaches 89.36% at 5 m–5°. Mean positional error drops from 9.89 m (LoD-Loc) to 2.62 m, and mean rotation error from 1.78° to 0.42°. On Swiss-EPFL out-of-Place, SemCityLoc more than doubles recall at 2 m–2° (35.36% vs. 17.41%) and reduces mean translation error from 12.54 m to 3.09 m. On UAVD4L-LoD, SemCityLoc matches or slightly exceeds LoD-Loc across in-Traj and out-of-Traj settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemCityLoc decisively outperforms all three baselines on the primary SemCityLockeD benchmark. Against LoD-Loc [50] (the strongest prior method), SemCityLoc nearly doubles recall at 2 m–2° (69.15% vs. 35.11%) and cuts mean translation error by 74% (2.62 m vs. 9.89 m), with the gap widening in oblique and facade-occluded views. MC-Loc [35] (pretrained-feature pose refinement) achieves only 5.32% recall at 2 m–2° and 10.71 m mean translation error, indicating that generic feature matching is insufficient for close-range urban canyon geometry. CAD-Loc [23] (feature matching against imperfect 3D models) fails entirely on SemCityLockeD, yielding 0% recall at all thresholds, likely due to the high geometric abstraction of LoD models. On the cross-dataset evaluations, SemCityLoc matches or slightly exceeds LoD-Loc on UAVD4L-LoD (e.g., 99.19% vs. 96.57% at 5 m–5° in-Traj) and substantially outperforms it on Swiss-EPFL out-of-Place (35.36% vs. 17.41% at 2 m–2°), confirming that the semantic-geometric alignment advantage is most pronounced in geometrically ambiguous, close-range scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

SemCityLoc departs from the dominant paradigm of aerial localization that relies on dense radiometric 3D reconstructions or 2D orthophoto matching (OrthoLoC) by reframing the problem as structured semantic-geometric surface registration against standardized CityGML LoD models. It also moves beyond the contour/wireframe alignment of LoD-Loc v1/v2, which the authors identify as insufficient in close-range urban canyons with repetitive facades. The method repurposes foundation-model perception (DINOv3 segmentation, MoGe-2 monocular depth) as fixed perceptual priors and combines them with a 4D cost-volume search and particle-filter refinement, drawing on optimization strategies from visual localization (MC-Loc, CAD-Loc) but replacing feature-matching with region-level IoU and depth alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that standardized, government-published 3D city models (already available at scale—over 216 million worldwide) can substitute for expensive, privacy-sensitive dense reconstructions while achieving sub-second runtime suitable for on-board UAV deployment. The SemCityLockeD benchmark fills a clear gap by being the first to combine centimeter-accurate RTK poses, standardized LoD1–LoD3 models, and challenging low-altitude urban canyon imagery in a single dataset. Open questions include how the method generalizes to regions with incomplete or lower-accuracy city models, whether the dependence on a pose prior within ~50 m limits applicability in GNSS-denied scenarios, and whether the LoD2-approaches-LoD3 finding (semantic partitioning compensating for geometric detail) holds across diverse architectural styles.
