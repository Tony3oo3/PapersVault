## Problem

Existing graph-based embodied navigation systems treat unexplored frontiers as semantically unknown, forcing inefficient geometric search, while VLM-based semantic predictions—once embedded in memory—propagate errors through chains of dependent hypotheses that confidence attenuation alone cannot resolve. The paper addresses the absence of a framework that simultaneously leverages semantic predictions for goal-directed exploration and systematically retracts erroneous subgraphs when new evidence contradicts them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262053/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent consumes an RGB image, depth map, and 6-DoF pose, plus a navigation goal specified as a category label, free-form language description, or reference image; an occupancy map derived from accumulated depth provides geometric frontiers. The VLM (GPT-4o) is queried per-frontier to produce a categorical semantic distribution, and the system outputs a navigation action sequence, a persistent hypothesis graph (observed + hypothesis nodes linked by a dependency DAG), and, on QA benchmarks, a free-text answer scored by GPT-4 (LLM-Match). The graph is non-monotonic: it can shrink when cascade correction prunes refuted subgraphs.

## Method

HGR maintains a hypothesis graph that separates verified observed nodes from probabilistic hypothesis nodes, connected by traversable edges and a directed acyclic dependency graph recording derivation relationships. A semantic hypothesis module projects VLM-estimated categorical distributions onto each frontier and ranks exploration targets via a score combining goal-category probability, geodesic travel cost (λ_d=0.15), and Shannon-entropy uncertainty bonus (λ_h=0.10). Upon visitation, a prediction residual test fuses category mismatch, CLIP embedding divergence, and object-level Jaccard dissimilarity (weights 0.4/0.3/0.3); if the residual exceeds θ_refute=0.5, cascade correction performs a breadth-first traversal along the dependency DAG and deletes the refuted node together with all downstream dependents and their incident edges. This structural retraction—rather than soft confidence decay—allows the graph to contract, preventing error accumulation over long episodes and reducing revisits to erroneous regions by 4.5×.

## Results

On GOAT-Bench (primary benchmark, 278-subtask subset), HGR achieves 72.41% Success Rate and 56.22% SPL, outperforming the strongest baseline 3D-Mem (69.1% SR / 48.9% SPL) by +3.31% SR and +7.32% SPL. On the full 2,780-subtask validation set, HGR reaches 64.14% SR and 50.1% SPL versus 3D-Mem's 62.9% / 44.7%. On A-EQA, HGR scores 55.9 LLM-Match (45.0 LLM-Match SPL), and on EM-EQA it achieves 58.3 LLM-Match with only 3.1 average memory snapshots.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HGR outperforms all five baselines across every benchmark. Against 3D-Mem [31], the strongest graph-memory baseline, HGR gains +3.31% SR and +7.32% SPL on GOAT-Bench, with the SPL gap driven primarily by cascade correction eliminating revisits to erroneously predicted regions (4.5× reduction in revisit rate). Against ConceptGraph [8], HGR improves by +4.61% SR and +8.12% SPL on the GOAT-Bench subset, showing that dependency-aware hypothesis tracking surpasses open-vocabulary scene graphs that lack error retraction. The two semantic-frontier baselines ESC [35] (69.8% SR / 50.6% SPL) and SCOPE [30] (70.4% SR / 51.7% SPL) confirm that semantic scoring alone yields modest gains over 3D-Mem, while HGR's additional cascade correction contributes a further +2.0–2.6% SR and +4.5–5.6% SPL. On A-EQA, HGR (55.9 LLM-Match) surpasses Explore-EQA [25] (46.9) by +9.0 points and ConceptGraph-with-frontier (47.2) by +8.7, indicating that error-free scene memory translates directly into better answer quality. On EM-EQA, where exploration is fixed and only memory quality matters, HGR (58.3) still leads Multi-Frame (48.1) by +10.2 LLM-Match with a comparable snapshot count (3.1 vs. 3.0), isolating the benefit of dependency-aware memory over frame-based representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

HGR extends the graph-based embodied memory paradigm established by 3D-Mem and ConceptGraph, which maintain scene graphs of observed objects and spatial relationships, and the VLM-guided frontier exploration line (ESC, SCOPE, ReVoLT) that scores unexplored boundaries by semantic relevance. The key conceptual departure is treating VLM frontier predictions not as one-time scoring signals but as revisable hypothesis nodes embedded in a dependency DAG, enabling a non-monotonic memory that can contract by pruning entire erroneous subgraphs via cascade correction—whereas prior methods either ignore prediction errors or apply soft confidence attenuation that leaves the erroneous structure in place.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cascade correction mechanism offers a principled alternative to confidence decay for handling VLM prediction errors in long-horizon embodied tasks, and the diagnostic finding that 67% of prediction failures stem from specular and transparent surfaces provides a concrete, actionable target for improving VLM robustness in indoor settings. The super-additive interaction between semantic hypothesis and cascade correction (disabling both costs −9.0% SR vs. −4.7% and −3.8% individually) suggests that directed exploration and error retraction are complementary rather than redundant. Open questions include whether the dependency DAG generalizes to dynamic environments with moving objects, how residual calibration should adapt across scene types, and whether the approach scales to multi-agent or outdoor settings where prediction error rates may differ substantially.
