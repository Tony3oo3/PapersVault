## Problem

Conventional Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) encode images as continuous functions but lack explicit geometric structure, making local editing and integration with physics engines difficult. The closest prior work, MiRaGe, uses discrete Gaussian Splatting for editability but produces visible artifacts under significant geometric deformation and cannot provide a truly continuous image representation. No existing method unifies high-fidelity continuous INR reconstruction with interpretable, physically manipulable geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D RGB or RGBA image (e.g., 768×512 Kodak, up to 1020×1020 DIV2K) and optionally a monocular depth map for 3D lifting. During training, a multi-resolution hashgrid encoder provides fine-grained spatial features to the Gaussians, but at inference this encoder is discarded and only the learned Gaussian parameters (500k primitives with position, covariance, and embedding) remain. Outputs include the reconstructed image, the editable Gaussian set, geometry-consistent deformations, a 3D volumetric scene with novel-view rendering, and arbitrary-resolution upscaled images (demonstrated up to ×12) without retraining.

## Method

GaINeR represents a 2D image as a set of N learnable 2D Gaussians, each parameterized by a mean μ, covariance Σ, and a learnable embedding vector e. For any query coordinate, the model performs a radius-limited KNN search over Gaussian centers, aggregates the neighbors' embeddings via Gaussian-weighted averaging, and passes the result through an MLP decoder to predict RGB. The key architectural insight is that the Gaussians serve as spatially structured feature providers rather than renderers: the MLP decoder is conditioned only on the aggregated embedding, not on absolute coordinates, which grants spatial equivariance so that moving Gaussian means produces coherent, artifact-free deformations. Training jointly optimizes Gaussian parameters, the hashgrid encoder, and the MLP under a Smooth L1 loss, with alpha-channel masking for RGBA inputs. For 3D lifting, the decoder is extended to predict a density σ, KNN switches to Mahalanobis distance, and the rendering follows a NeRF-style volumetric integral, enabling single-image novel-view synthesis and direct 3D scene editing.

## Results

On the Kodak dataset, GaINeR achieves 77.09 dB PSNR and 0.9999 MS-SSIM, surpassing the strongest baseline MiRaGe (59.52 dB PSNR) by 17.6 dB. On DIV2K, GaINeR reaches 62.20 dB PSNR and 0.9999 MS-SSIM, outperforming MiRaGe (54.54 dB) by 7.7 dB. For ×8 super-resolution on DIV2K, GaINeR achieves 23.22 dB PSNR versus 17.33 dB for 3D Gaussian Splatting and 16.61 dB for MiRaGe. In physics-based deformation, GaINeR attains the highest MUSIQ (62.62) and CLIPIQA (0.530) scores, though it trails MiRaGe slightly on FID (376.34 vs. 372.41).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaINeR decisively outperforms all seven baselines on reconstruction PSNR across both Kodak and DIV2K. The largest margin is against MiRaGe (the closest competitor), with +17.6 dB on Kodak and +7.7 dB on DIV2K; against non-Gaussian INR baselines (SIREN, WIRE, I-NGP, NeuRBF) the gap exceeds 28 dB on Kodak. On MS-SSIM, GaINeR ties MiRaGe at 0.9999 on Kodak and edges it by 0.0001 on DIV2K, while all other baselines fall below 0.9991. In the controlled Gaussian-primitive comparison (Table 2, fixed 500k Gaussians, 30k iterations), GaINeR again leads MiRaGe (77.09 vs. 62.49 PSNR on Kodak) and GaussianImage (77.09 vs. 30.46). For physics-based editing, GaINeR wins on perceptual quality (MUSIQ 62.62 vs. 61.03; CLIPIQA 0.530 vs. 0.528) but is marginally worse on FID (376.34 vs. 372.41) compared to MiRaGe. In 2D-to-3D novel-view synthesis, GaINeR substantially outperforms the Explicit Point Cloud reprojection baseline (e.g., 20.58 vs. 8.57 PSNR on Hotdog with ground-truth depth), confirming the benefit of the continuous INR decoder over discrete geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaINeR sits at the intersection of two lines of work: implicit neural representations for 2D images (SIREN, WIRE, FINER, I-NGP) and Gaussian-splatting-based editable representations (3DGS, GaussianImage, MiRaGe). It departs from pure INRs by introducing explicit geometric structure (learnable Gaussian embeddings) that enables local editing and physics integration, and it departs from MiRaGe by using Gaussians not as renderers but as feature providers that condition a continuous MLP decoder. The multi-resolution hash encoding from I-NGP is repurposed as a training-time feature source for the Gaussian embeddings, while the KNN aggregation mechanism borrows from spatial interpolation in radiance field literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single representation can simultaneously achieve near-lossless reconstruction, geometry-consistent 2D editing, physics-based simulation (via MPM/Taichi Elements), and 2D-to-3D lifting, which prior methods handled in isolation. The key open limitation is that 3D lifting quality is bounded by off-the-shelf monocular depth estimation (Depth-Pro), and the approach has not been validated on video or large-scale 3D scene reconstruction. Whether the Gaussian-conditioned INR paradigm scales to high-resolution video or multi-view 3D settings remains an open question, as does the computational cost of the KNN search at inference for very large Gaussian sets.
