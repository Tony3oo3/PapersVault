## Problem

Monocular depth foundation models suffer from inherent scale ambiguity, producing misaligned metric predictions even when relative geometry is accurate. Multi-view foundation models resolve this through cross-view geometric constraints, but their performance collapses during single-view inference because the global cross-attention degenerates into standard intra-frame self-attention, discarding the learned scale-aware priors. The open challenge is how to transfer the robust scale perception of multi-view models into a single-view predictor without requiring additional views at inference time.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image (resized up to 576K pixels with aspect ratio preserved) and produces a metric depth map, focal length residuals (δx, δy), and a global metric scale factor. The final depth is explicitly decoupled into a scale-invariant structural component (normalized baseline prediction plus a dense shift offset Δd from a DPT head) and a global scale factor ŝ from an MLP head, combined as D_adj = ŝ·(D_base + Δd). During training, the model consumes multi-view image triplets (one reference + up to two source views) with ground-truth depth, intrinsics, and 6-DoF poses, enforcing a minimum 20% visual overlap between paired views.

## Method

EpiDistill transfers multi-view geometric knowledge to a single-view model through two coupled mechanisms. First, Depth-Guided Epipolar Attention (DGEA) constrains cross-view attention during multi-view training to tokens sampled along the epipolar line, with a Gaussian spatial bias centered at the ground-truth correspondence point to explicitly supervise attention localization. Second, Rectified Stereo Tokens (RST)—a learnable spatial token grid—substitute the missing source views during single-view inference; they interact with reference features via cross-attention to form reference-guided tokens, then undergo rectified epipolar attention constrained strictly to horizontal scanlines, simulating the simplified geometry of a rectified stereo setup (R=I, t=[b,0,0]). Knowledge is distilled from multi-view epipolar-aggregated features into single-view tokens via a cosine-similarity-plus-magnitude loss, while the frozen ViT backbone (UniDepthV2 or DepthPro) provides the base depth prediction that is refined by the lightweight DPT and MLP heads.

## Results

On the outdoor/mixed evaluation (Table 1), UniDepthV2+EpiDistill achieves an overall rank of 3.1, the best among image-only models, reducing A.Rel from 0.716 to 0.396 on DIODE (44.7% improvement) and from 0.176 to 0.137 on ETH3D (22.2% improvement) over the UniDepthV2 baseline. DepthPro+EpiDistill similarly improves from rank 8.8 to 6.4, with A.Rel dropping from 0.386 to 0.357 on DIODE. On indoor datasets (Table 2), UniDepthV2+EpiDistill matches the GT-intrinsic-dependent DepthAnything3 at an average rank of 2.4, with consistent small gains (e.g., 4.5% A.Rel reduction on Booster). On the DepthPerturb Dolly-Zoom benchmark, EpiDistill reduces A.Rel from 0.241 to 0.167 (UniDepthV2) and 0.248 to 0.178 (DepthPro) while maximizing focal-length prediction linearity (|PCorr| 0.965 and 0.911 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniDepthV2+EpiDistill outperforms its own baseline (UniDepthV2) on all four outdoor/mixed datasets, with the most dramatic gain on DIODE (A.Rel 0.716→0.396, −44.7%) and ETH3D (0.176→0.137, −22.2%). It also beats DepthPro+EpiDistill on every dataset, confirming the model-agnostic benefit is larger for the stronger backbone. Against other image-only methods, UniDepthV2+EpiDistill surpasses UniK3D on DIODE (0.396 vs 0.457) and ETH3D (0.137 vs 0.139), and MoGe-2 on DIODE (0.396 vs 0.407), though it trails UniDepthV1 on DIODE (0.396 vs 0.263) where UniDepthV1's aggressive scale regression happens to align. Notably, UniDepthV2+EpiDistill beats the GT-intrinsic-dependent DepthAnything3 on DIODE (0.396 vs 0.489) and DDAD (0.132 vs 0.137) without requiring camera parameters at inference, and achieves a lower overall rank (3.1) than Metric3Dv2† (4.5) despite the latter's access to ground-truth intrinsics. On indoor datasets, gains are smaller but consistent: UniDepthV2+EpiDistill matches DepthAnything3† at rank 2.4 and reduces Booster A.Rel by 4.5% over the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: multi-view foundation models (DepthAnything3, VGGT, MapAnything) that achieve globally consistent metric scale through cross-view attention, and monocular depth foundation models (UniDepthV2, DepthPro) that excel at relative depth but suffer from inherent scale ambiguity. The key departure is identifying the 'architectural collapse' problem—when multi-view models are forced into single-view inference, their cross-view attention degenerates into standard self-attention, discarding the very geometric priors that gave them scale robustness. EpiDistill addresses this by distilling depth-guided epipolar attention patterns into a single-view model, using learnable Rectified Stereo Tokens as a geometric anchor that preserves the cross-attention structure without requiring actual multi-view inputs at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic nature of EpiDistill (validated on both UniDepthV2 and DepthPro) makes it a practical plug-in for any ViT-based monocular depth model, and the near-parity with the multi-view upper bound (0.101 vs 0.100 A.Rel) demonstrates that multi-view geometric knowledge can be effectively compressed into single-view inference. The Dolly Zoom evaluation on DepthPerturb provides a compelling test of whether the model genuinely disentangles depth from intrinsics rather than memorizing scale. Limitations include the requirement for multi-view training data with ground-truth poses and intrinsics, modest gains on indoor datasets where scale variation is less extreme, and the fixed 37×37 rectified stereo token grid that may not adapt to all scene geometries. An open question is whether the rectified stereo formulation can be generalized to handle arbitrary (non-rectified) camera configurations at inference.
