## Problem

Astronomical CCD imaging remains noise-limited under practical observing conditions, and standard calibration pipelines remove structured artifacts but leave stochastic noise largely unresolved. Learning-based denoising is constrained by the scarcity of paired clean/noisy training data in scientific imaging, and existing noise synthesis methods are designed for consumer CMOS sensors whose noise statistics differ fundamentally from those of scientific CCDs (e.g., PRNU, per-amplifier readout effects, cosmic-ray hits, and persistent hot pixels).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260181/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-band, grayscale raw CCD exposure (e.g., G′, R′, or I′ band from a 2 m ground-based telescope) and produces a denoised version at the same spatial resolution. Training pairs are constructed by stacking 10–20 unregistered BANZAI-processed exposures into a high-SNR base image, then synthesizing a realistic noisy counterpart via the physics-based noise model; the U-Net is trained to map the synthesized noisy raw to the stacked base. Evaluation on real observations uses mocked PSF injection to compute FLUX-SNR, since true noise-free ground truth is unavailable.

## Method

The core contribution is a physics-based CCD noise synthesis pipeline that decomposes the image formation model into signal-dependent terms (Poisson photon shot noise with known system gain, per-pixel dark current with Gaussian residual modeling, and PRNU decomposed into a fixed-pattern component extracted from skyflats via high-pass filtering plus randomly sampled dust-donut artifacts) and signal-independent terms (fixed-pattern bias template plus row-wise and pixel-wise read noise modeled as Gaussian mixture distributions, and a salt-and-pepper impulse model for hot pixels and cosmic rays). A standard U-Net is trained with L1 loss on the synthesized noisy-to-clean pairs, requiring only ~100 calibration frames to parameterize the noise model. The approach is validated in-domain on MuSCAT-3 and zero-shot on the twin MuSCAT-4 instrument, demonstrating consistent gains in photometric fidelity (PSNR) and scientific accuracy (FLUX-SNR) over both traditional (BM3D, Drizzle) and learning-based (N2N, ELD, NMOH, PNNP) baselines.

## Results

On the MuSCAT-3 in-domain evaluation, the proposed method achieves a mean FLUX-SNR of 39.91 dB (G′: 41.21, I′: 38.50, R′: 40.03) and mean PSNR of 57.06 dB on synthetic pairs, outperforming all 9 baselines. On real observations, it attains the best background noise levels among learning-based methods (NMAD 2.43, STD 3.06) while preserving stellar PSF structure. In zero-shot transfer to the MuSCAT-4 instrument, the method maintains its lead with FLUX-SNR 39.87 dB and PSNR 58.85 dB, confirming cross-instrument generalization of the physics-based noise synthesis pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the highest FLUX-SNR (39.91 dB) across all bands, surpassing the closest learning-based competitor PNNP (37.60 dB) by 2.3 dB and NMOH (36.87 dB) by 3.0 dB, confirming that CCD-specific noise modeling (PRNU, per-amplifier read noise, dark current) is critical for photometric fidelity. On PSNR for synthetic pairs, the method leads with 57.06 dB versus PNNP's 56.92 dB and NMOH's 56.39 dB. Traditional denoisers BM3D and A-BM3D achieve lower NMAD/STD (1.17/2.21 for A-BM3D) but sacrifice FLUX-SNR (37.64 and 39.35 dB respectively) due to oversmoothing of stellar PSFs. Drizzle, the multi-image stacking baseline, performs worst on background noise (NMAD 6.15) and is not applicable to single-frame denoising. Self-supervised methods designed for consumer CMOS (N2N, ELD, SFRN, PMN) all fall 3–13 dB below the proposed method in FLUX-SNR, as they neglect CCD-specific artifacts like PRNU and fixed-pattern read noise. The zero-shot MuSCAT-4 results (Table 4) mirror these trends, with the method maintaining a 2.8 dB FLUX-SNR advantage over PNNP and 3.4 dB over NMOH, validating cross-instrument transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the physics-based noise synthesis paradigm established for consumer CMOS low-light denoising (particularly the GMM-based read-noise parameterization from NMOH [35] and the Poisson-plus-Gaussian signal-dependent/independent decomposition from [16, 64]) to the fundamentally different CCD architecture used in astronomical imaging. The key conceptual departure is modeling serial charge transfer to shared output amplifiers, which introduces segment-specific bias offsets, row-level striping, and PRNU from optical etaloning and dust donuts—none of which exist in per-pixel-amplifier CMOS sensors. The denoising architecture itself is a standard U-Net; the contribution lies entirely in the domain-specific noise formation model and the paired-data synthesis pipeline that makes supervised training feasible without true noise-free references.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Astronomical CCD observations cannot be denoised by simply extending exposure time (unlike consumer photography) due to atmospheric turbulence, source variability, and Earth's rotation, making per-exposure denoising scientifically essential. The paper's cross-instrument zero-shot generalization (MuSCAT-3 → MuSCAT-4) suggests the physics-based parameterization captures instrument-invariant noise structure, which is encouraging for broader adoption. However, validation is limited to two twin telescopes with the same filter set, and the absence of true noise-free ground truth (relying on stacked proxies) remains a fundamental evaluation limitation. Broader validation across diverse CCD sensors, wavelengths, and observing conditions is needed before the approach can be considered generalizable to the full range of astronomical instruments.
