## Problem

Existing instruction-based image editing benchmarks evaluate spatial layout, logic puzzles, and attribute binding but critically lack scenarios involving real-world physical dynamics such as gravity, fluid flow, and material deformation. This gap means current models' inability to reason about how physical laws govern state transitions remains unmeasured, and their reliance on static statistical priors produces physically implausible edits that go undetected by prior evaluation protocols.

## Input / Output

Each benchmark instance is a four-state trajectory (input, intermediate 1, intermediate 2, output) sampled from a single high-resolution real-world video, annotated with a global instruction, three step instructions, physical explanations, and scene invariants. Five run types probe different granularities: step-wise editing on consecutive pairs (TypeA–C), joint multi-step from input (TypeD), and global input-to-output editing (TypeE). A separate set of 35 Anti-Physics instances provides a single source image with a counterfactual prompt that explicitly contradicts physical law, testing whether models can suppress memorized priors. The PhyWorld method takes a source image and text instruction, generates 121-frame video sequences via a 5B image-to-video diffusion model, and outputs the final frame of the best-scoring sequence as the edited image.

## Method

PhyWorld is a training-free framework that repurposes the pretrained Wan2.2 TI2V-5B video generation model as an implicit physics reasoning engine for image editing. The editing prompt is first expanded into a detailed Temporal Editing Caption by Qwen-3.5 Max, which reasons about the physical procedure and describes the transition process. During generation, five Gaussian noise samples are denoised over 30 timesteps conditioned on the input image, with a latent reduction strategy pruning the 31-token VAE sequence down to 21 tokens at timestep 10 and 11 tokens at timestep 20 to improve efficiency while preserving fidelity. An adapted single-step Test-Time Scaling procedure (from EvoSearch) performs evolutionary selection at the end of generation, and a Video Reward Model scores all candidates to select the optimal sequence. The final frame of the winning video is extracted as the edited image, leveraging the video model's learned temporal causality as a substitute for explicit physics simulation.

## Results

On PhyEditBench normal data, ChronoEdit-14B achieves the highest overall score of 8.51, followed closely by Seedream4.0 (8.47) and GPT-Image-1.5 (8.23). PhyWorld (5B params) scores 6.43 overall on normal data, ranking competitively among open-source models despite being the most compact architecture. On the anti-physics subset, PhyWorld achieves the best open-source score of 6.39, surpassing all other open-source baselines and approaching closed-source leaders (Gemini-2.5: 7.07, GPT-Image-1.5: 7.04), while ChronoEdit-14B drops sharply to 4.99. Physical Plausibility scores range from 2.51 (Frame2Frame, anti-physics) to 8.42 (ChronoEdit-14B, normal), highlighting the difficulty of physics-grounded editing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On normal data, PhyWorld (6.43) trails the top video-based method ChronoEdit-14B (8.51) by 2.08 points and closed-source leaders Seedream4.0 (8.47) and GPT-Image-1.5 (8.23), but outperforms all other open-source static editors including UniWorld-V2 (7.08), Step1X-Edit (6.79), and BAGEL-Think (6.43, tied). On the anti-physics subset, PhyWorld (6.39) is the strongest open-source model, beating BAGEL-Think (6.16), Qwen-Image-Edit (6.16), and FLUX.1-Kontext-dev (6.07), while ChronoEdit-14B collapses to 4.99 and Frame2Frame to 3.81. PhyWorld's advantage on anti-physics is particularly notable given its 5B parameter size versus 14B (ChronoEdit) and 19B (Step1X-Edit). Among video-based methods, PhyWorld outperforms Frame2Frame (5.38 normal / 3.81 anti-physics) by 1.05 and 2.58 points respectively, demonstrating the benefit of test-time scaling and latent reduction. The paper notes that BAGEL-Think surpasses BAGEL on this benchmark, suggesting the physics tasks impose substantial reasoning demands that differentiate stronger reasoning-capable models.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhyWorld is a direct descendant of the video-as-reasoning paradigm for image editing, building on Frame2Frame's temporal editing captions and ChronoEdit's latent reduction strategy, while adapting EvoSearch's evolutionary test-time scaling from text-to-video to the image-to-video setting. On the benchmark side, the paper positions PhyEditBench as filling a specific gap: existing reasoning-centric editing benchmarks (KRIS-Bench, RISEBench, UniREditBench, WiseEdit) evaluate spatial logic and attribute binding but omit real-world physical dynamics, while physics video datasets (CLEVRER, Physion, NewtonGen) are synthetic and not structured for instruction-guided editing. The key departure from prior video-based editors is the integration of a Video Reward Model with a single-step evolutionary search at generation's end, trading EvoSearch's full iterative optimization for a more efficient I2V-compatible variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a concrete and measurable weakness: even top closed-source editors (GPT-Image-1.5, Seedream4.0) and the strongest open-source models struggle with multi-stage physical transitions, and performance drops sharply on anti-physics counterfactuals, suggesting current models rely on memorized statistical priors rather than genuine physical deduction. The finding that a compact 5B video-generation backbone (PhyWorld) rivals or exceeds much larger static editors on physics tasks offers a practical, training-free recipe for physics-aware editing. Open questions include whether the 273-instance scale is sufficient for robust conclusions, whether VLM-based (GPT-4o) scoring adequately captures subtle physical violations (human-VLM correlation is moderate at 0.56–0.65), and whether the video-as-reasoning paradigm can be extended beyond the specific physical categories covered here.
