## Problem

Current VLMs perform adequately at egocentric spatial reasoning (from the camera's viewpoint) but fail to generalize to allocentric perspectives when asked to adopt another entity's spatial frame of reference. No existing benchmark systematically evaluates this cross-viewpoint spatial localization gap, and prior spatial benchmarks remain limited to single-perspective or shallow spatial understanding tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260030/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark sample pairs a single RGB image (from ScanNet 3D scene reconstructions or MS-COCO) with a multiple-choice text question asking for a spatial relationship (e.g., 'If you stand at the bookshelf facing the window, where is the sofa?'). The model outputs one of 18 discrete directional labels (front, back, left, right, front-left, front-right, etc.), and evaluation is measured by accuracy. The five task types vary in whether the reference frame is the camera or a person in the scene, and whether the query target is an object or a person's gaze direction.

## Method

The paper introduces an automated 3D spatial annotation pipeline that combines ScanNet voxel data with Maximum Coverage Sampling for diverse frame selection and MS-COCO keypoints with Orient-Anything-Large for head/body orientation estimation, converting raw 3D coordinates into 18 discrete directional categories via angle-based mapping. ViewSpatial-Bench (5,712 samples, 1,338 scenes) spans five task types across camera and human perspectives, with a rule-based distractor generation scheme that excludes compound directions containing the correct answer's constituent parts. For the Multi-View Spatial Model (MVSM), the authors generate ~43K training samples using the same pipeline on ScanNet and MS-COCO training sets, fine-tuning VLM backbones (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B) on mixed batches of camera- and human-perspective samples to encourage unified 3D spatial representations. This targeted supervision yields a 46.24% absolute accuracy gain over the backbone, with consistent improvements across all five task types and multiple model architectures, confirming that the spatial reasoning deficit is remediable through perspective-aware training data.

## Results

On ViewSpatial-Bench, the best zero-shot proprietary model (Doubao-Seed-2.0) achieves 53.52% overall accuracy, while the best open-source model (Qwen3.5-Plus, 397B) reaches 52.28%, both barely exceeding the random baseline of 26.33% by a modest margin. After multi-view spatial fine-tuning, the MVSM (Qwen2.5-VL 3B backbone) achieves 82.09% overall accuracy, a +46.24% absolute improvement over its 35.85% backbone score. Camera-perspective tasks average 41.6% across models versus 37.3% for person-perspective tasks, confirming a persistent egocentric-allocentric gap. On transfer benchmarks, MVSM improves Route Planning on VSI-Bench by +9.54% and indoor spatial reasoning on VSI-App by +23.00% over its backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The MVSM (Qwen2.5-VL 3B + SFT) achieves 82.09% overall accuracy on ViewSpatial-Bench, surpassing the strongest proprietary baseline Doubao-Seed-2.0 (53.52%) by 28.57 points and the strongest open-source model Qwen3.5-Plus 397B (52.28%) by 29.81 points. Even the weakest evaluated model (LLaVA-OneVision 7B at 27.49%) is outperformed by the MVSM by over 54 points, indicating that targeted spatial fine-tuning of a small 3B model dramatically closes the gap with 100×+ larger models. On the transfer benchmark VSI-Bench, MVSM improves Route Planning from 21.90% to 31.44% (+9.54%) over its backbone, while on VSI-App it gains +23.00% on indoor and +9.00% on outdoor spatial reasoning. Notably, GPT-4o (34.98%) and Gemini-2.0-Flash (32.56%) perform only marginally above the random baseline (26.33%), confirming that even frontier proprietary models lack robust multi-perspective spatial reasoning. The consistent ~46% improvement across three different backbones (Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B, InternVL2.5 2B) validates that the gains are architecture-agnostic rather than model-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of spatial reasoning benchmarks for VLMs (EmbSpatial-Bench, What'sUP, VSI-Bench, 3DSRBench, SPHERE) by introducing a multi-perspective dimension: prior benchmarks predominantly evaluate egocentric (camera-frame) spatial understanding, while ViewSpatial-Bench explicitly requires models to adopt an allocentric (human-character) viewpoint. Methodologically, it departs from manual annotation approaches used by several prior benchmarks by proposing an automated 3D orientation pipeline leveraging ScanNet voxel data and MS-COCO keypoints with Orient-Anything, enabling scalable generation of directional labels. The fine-tuning component (MVSM) follows the established paradigm of supervised fine-tuning on task-specific spatial data, consistent with prior work like Multi-SpatialMLLM and targeted EmbSpatial-Bench fine-tuning, but applies it specifically to multi-perspective spatial localization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first systematic, large-scale evaluation of allocentric spatial reasoning in VLMs, a capability critical for embodied AI, robotics, and human-robot interaction where perspective-taking is essential. The finding that even frontier models barely exceed random chance on these tasks, yet a 3B model reaches 82% after targeted fine-tuning, suggests that spatial reasoning capacity is latent but unactivated by current pretraining—pointing to a data gap rather than an architectural limitation. Open questions include whether the multiple-choice format fully captures open-ended spatial reasoning, whether the indoor-outdoor performance gap (23% vs 9% improvement on VSI-App) will close with more diverse training data, and whether the automated pipeline generalizes to dynamic or partially observable 3D environments.
