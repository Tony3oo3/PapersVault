## Problem

Existing unified anomaly detection methods compress encoder features into low-dimensional latent spaces to enable reconstruction, but this compression inevitably discards semantic and spatial detail, degrading reconstruction quality and limiting the decoder's capacity to model complex normal patterns across diverse categories. The paper argues that dimensionality compression is not an inherent requirement for anomaly detection and that the editability it provides is not essential for reconstructing anomalies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262036/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 512² and center-cropped to 448², producing a 28×28 patch token sequence processed by a frozen DINOv3-ViT-L/16 encoder (d_t=1024, 24 layers). At inference, per-position cosine distances between frozen teacher features and decoder reconstructions are computed and upsampled to input resolution to yield a pixel-level anomaly map, while the aggregated score provides an image-level anomaly decision. Training is fully unsupervised, using only normal samples from each category under a single unified model.

## Method

HLRAD replaces the conventional latent compression bottleneck with a dimension-expanding pipeline: a frozen DiT encoder (28 blocks, d_s=1152, from RAE) re-encodes patch tokens into a higher-dimensional semantic space, and a Selective Gated Fusion module uses encoder attention weights to inject these semantic features only at spatially salient positions via channel-wise sigmoid gating, preserving fine-grained texture elsewhere. A dimension-expanding MLP with triple dropout then projects the fused features from d_t=1024 to d_d=2048, simultaneously blocking the identity-mapping shortcut and providing the decoder a wider working space. The high-dimensional decoder (8 Transformer blocks, 32 heads, DDT-style decoupled design) reconstructs multi-layer teacher features under a reverse-distillation cosine similarity loss with stop-gradient, and random head-dropping during training further prevents co-adaptation. This architecture avoids all latent compression, preserving full feature information while gaining greater modeling capacity for multi-class normal pattern reconstruction.

## Results

On MVTec-AD, HLRAD achieves image-level AUROC/AP/F1max of 99.7/99.9/99.3 and pixel-level AUROC/AP/F1max of 98.6/71.3/70.7, setting new state-of-the-art on all six metrics. On VisA, it reaches 99.0/99.0/96.6 (image) and 99.1/55.5/57.5 (pixel), with the pixel-level AP margin over the runner-up (Dinomaly, 53.2) being the largest gap in the table. On Real-IAD, HLRAD is second on image-level metrics (90.1/87.5/81.1 vs. INP-Former's 90.5/88.1/81.5) but ties or leads on pixel-level (99.0/47.7/50.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HLRAD achieves the best or tied-best result on 17 of 18 metrics across the three datasets, with the sole exception being Real-IAD image-level metrics where INP-Former [14] leads by a narrow margin (90.5 vs. 90.1 AUROC, 88.1 vs. 87.5 AP, 81.5 vs. 81.1 F1max). Against the closest competitor Dinomaly [7], HLRAD gains +2.0 on MVTec-AD pixel-level AP (71.3 vs. 69.3), +2.3 on VisA pixel-level AP (55.5 vs. 53.2), and +4.9 on Real-IAD pixel-level AP (47.7 vs. 42.8), indicating a consistent advantage in fine-grained anomaly localization. Compared to INP-Former [14], HLRAD wins on all MVTec-AD and VisA metrics (e.g., +1.0 P-F1 on MVTec-AD, +4.3 P-AP on VisA) and edges out on Real-IAD pixel-level AP/F1 (47.7/50.5 vs. 47.5/50.3). Older reconstruction-based baselines such as DiAD [9], MambaAD [8], and ViTAD [28] are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 10 points on VisA pixel-level AP and over 40 points on Real-IAD pixel-level AP. Embedding-based methods (SimpleNet [11], DeSTSeg [29]) and earlier unified frameworks (RD4AD [3], UniAD [27]) trail by even larger margins, confirming that the dimension-expansion strategy yields meaningful gains over both latent-compression and fixed-dimension reconstruction paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

HLRAD departs from the dominant latent-compression paradigm in unified anomaly detection (DiAD, HVQ-Trans, PMAD) by inverting the dimensionality flow: rather than compressing encoder features into a low-dimensional bottleneck, it projects them into a higher-dimensional space via a frozen DiT semantic encoder (from RAE) and a dimension-expanding MLP. The decoder design draws on DDT's wide-and-shallow architecture, while the training objective follows Dinomaly's reverse-distillation paradigm with cosine-similarity loss. The Selective Gated Fusion mechanism is novel, using encoder attention to spatially gate semantic feature injection, which has no direct analogue in prior unified anomaly detection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely held assumption that latent compression is a prerequisite for reconstruction-based anomaly detection, offering a principled alternative that preserves full feature information. However, the practical gains over the strongest baselines (Dinomaly, INP-Former) are modest—typically 0.1–1.0 points—and on Real-IAD (30 categories) image-level AUROC actually trails INP-Former by 0.4 points, suggesting the approach may not scale cleanly to very large category diversity. The tokenizer reconstruction-loss study (66–92% reduction vs. VAE) is the most compelling evidence for the expansion hypothesis, but whether this translates into meaningful gains on more complex or open-world anomaly detection settings remains an open question.
