## Problem

While 3D Gaussian Splatting dominates novel-view synthesis, triangles remain the standard primitive for rendering hardware, game engines, and downstream tasks requiring explicit geometry such as simulation, collision, and editing. No prior dense SLAM system uses triangles as its primary map representation for both tracking and mapping; existing methods rely on disconnected primitives (Gaussians, surfels, voxels) and generate meshes only as a secondary, decoupled step that does not inform the SLAM algorithm.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a stream of RGB-D frames (RGB image plus aligned depth map per frame) and incrementally maintains a triangle soup map where each vertex stores a 3D position, RGB colour, and scalar opacity, and each face is an ordered triplet of vertex indices. Outputs include the per-frame camera pose (SE(3)), the optimised triangle map, rendered depth and surface-normal maps, and—on demand—a connected mesh produced by restricted Delaunay triangulation of the vertex set, enabling immediate use in physics engines or interactive editing without a separate offline extraction step.

## Method

The map is a set of N vertices (position, colour, opacity) and M faces (index triplets), rendered via a differentiable rasteriser that projects each triangle into image space and accumulates colour using a 2D signed-distance-field window function with barycentric interpolation of vertex attributes. Tracking minimises a joint photometric (L1 + D-SSIM) and depth loss over the camera pose, with analytic SE(3) Jacobians evaluated directly in the CUDA backward pass to avoid ~80 automatic-differentiation iterations. Mapping jointly optimises keyframe poses and vertex parameters using photometric, depth, sensor-normal, and an equilateral-angle regularisation loss, while a co-visibility-based keyframing strategy selects which frames to replay. Densification splits over-covered triangles into four children via midpoint-vertex subdivision, and pruning removes low-opacity or oversized faces; face opacity is defined as the mean (rather than minimum) of vertex opacities so all three vertices receive gradients. The triangle soup can be converted to a watertight connected mesh on-the-fly through restricted Delaunay triangulation, supporting online deformation and collision checking.

## Results

On the Replica dataset, Triangle Splatting SLAM achieves the best Chamfer Distance of 0.95 cm (TSDF fusion) and 1.14 cm (pruned Delaunay), outperforming MonoGS-2D*+TSDF (1.36 cm) and MonoGS+TSDF (4.03 cm). For camera tracking, the method achieves 1.57 cm average ATE on TUM RGB-D and 0.34 cm on Replica, matching MonoGS (1.47 cm / 0.58 cm) and MonoGS-2D* (1.54 cm / 0.24 cm). Depth L1 error is 0.68 cm on Replica, beating MonoGS-2D* (0.74 cm) and MonoGS (4.52 cm). Pruned Delaunay mesh generation takes 15.66 s on average versus 33.44 s for TSDF fusion, offering a strong accuracy–speed trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MonoGS (3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM), the proposed method achieves a 4.3× lower Chamfer Distance (0.95 vs 4.03 cm) and 6.6× lower depth L1 error (0.68 vs 4.52 cm) on Replica, while matching tracking accuracy (1.57 vs 1.47 cm ATE on TUM). Against MonoGS-2D* (2D Gaussian Splatting re-implementation), the method wins on Chamfer Distance (0.95 vs 1.36 cm for TSDF; 1.14 vs 1.36 cm for pruned Delaunay) and depth L1 (0.68 vs 0.74 cm), but trails slightly on Replica ATE (0.34 vs 0.24 cm). Against MonoGS-2D (4DTAM), the method achieves comparable tracking (0.34 vs 0.36 cm ATE) while delivering substantially better geometry. Against older dense SLAM baselines (iMAP, NICE-SLAM, Vox-Fusion, Point-SLAM), the method matches or exceeds tracking accuracy on TUM (1.57 cm vs 2.17–10.34 cm) and provides explicit mesh output that those methods lack. The pruned Delaunay variant (1.14 cm Chamfer, 15.66 s) offers a practical middle ground, strictly outperforming MonoGS-2D*+TSDF in both accuracy and speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the offline differentiable triangle rendering pipeline of Mesh Splatting [7] and Triangle Splatting [8] into an online SLAM setting, using a triangle soup as the sole map representation rather than deferring to a secondary implicit structure. It departs from the dominant 3D/2D Gaussian Splatting SLAM line (MonoGS, GS-SLAM) by replacing volumetric primitives with explicit surface geometry, and from traditional dense SLAM (TSDF, surfels) by avoiding fixed-resolution grids and disconnected point clouds. Densification and splitting strategies are borrowed from Mini-Splatting [3] and Loop subdivision [16], while normal supervision follows 4DTAM [20]. The key conceptual departure is treating the triangle soup as a first-class SLAM primitive whose Delaunay triangulation yields a connected mesh on-the-fly, rather than generating a mesh as a post-hoc extraction step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified triangle representation unlocks online mesh-based capabilities—deformation, collision checking, and interactive editing—that are fundamentally unavailable with Gaussian or surfel SLAM, making it directly applicable to AR, robotics, and digital twin workflows. However, the system currently runs at 0.5–2.3 FPS, far from the 30 FPS needed for practical deployment, and the Delaunay mesh in unobserved regions can produce irregular surfaces. Open questions include enforcing manifoldness and preventing self-intersections during optimisation, integrating triangle-soup compression (e.g., DiffSoup [35]) to reduce primitive counts, and extending to monocular input. The moderate tracking accuracy gap versus MonoGS-2D* (0.34 vs 0.24 cm ATE on Replica) suggests the representation trades some pose precision for geometric fidelity and mesh utility.
