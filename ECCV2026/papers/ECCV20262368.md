## Problem

Pretrained flow models produce oversmoothed, diffuse samples because their velocity predictions are conditional means that average over multiple plausible outcomes. Existing guidance methods like CFG require an extra unconditional forward pass per step, and Autoguidance requires auxiliary checkpoints that are rarely released for large open models. The paper asks whether the ODE trajectory itself can supply a smoother velocity reference at zero additional evaluation cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262368/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MG is a drop-in modification to any Euler-based flow sampler: it consumes the same velocity v_θ(Z_t, t) already computed at each step and adds only a single EMA buffer m_t with the same shape as the latent state (e.g., 32×32×4 for ImageNet-256 latents, ~128×128×16 for FLUX.1-dev at 1024²). No extra network forward pass, auxiliary model, or unconditional branch is needed. The method is applied over a configurable time interval (default [0,1]) and is compatible with any conditioning signal (class label, text embedding) and any base sampler, including those already using CFG.

## Method

MG exploits the Rectified Flow marginal-preservation property: earlier timesteps lie on smoother Gaussian-blurred marginals, so past velocity estimates are intrinsically smoother references. It maintains a velocity momentum m_t as an exponential moving average of past velocities (m_{t_{i+1}} = (1−β)v_{t_i} + βm_{t_i}), then extrapolates the current velocity away from this reference in the Euler update: Z_{t_{i+1}} = Z_{t_i} + Δt[v_{t_i} + α(v_{t_i} − m_{t_i})]. The two hyperparameters α (extrapolation strength) and β (EMA decay) control the guidance magnitude and memory length, respectively. Because the EMA aggregates velocities from higher-noise, smoother states, the difference v_t − m_t isolates newly emerging detail, producing a sharpening effect analogous to CFG's conditional-minus-unconditional extrapolation but at zero additional FLOPs. The method is plug-and-play: it can be applied alone (replacing CFG) or stacked on top of CFG, and requires no retraining, auxiliary checkpoints, or architecture changes.

## Results

On ImageNet-256, Momentum Guidance (MG) achieves an FID of 1.553 at 16 NFEs (with CFG-int ω=1.4), improving over the CFG-int baseline (FID 2.352) by 33.9%. Without any CFG, MG reduces FID by 36.54% on average across sampling budgets; with CFG at scale 1.2, the average FID reduction is 25.42%. On SD3 (28 steps), MG improves HPSv2.1 from 22.87 to 27.37 at CFG=1 and from 29.98 to 30.43 at CFG=4; on FLUX.1-dev (50 steps), HPSv2.1 improves from 24.40 to 24.80 at CFG=1. On HunyuanVideo, VBench total score rises from 0.816 to 0.819 with a fixed α=0.6, β=0.2 setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MG consistently outperforms all compared guidance baselines on ImageNet-256 FID. Against CFG-int [39], adding MG reduces FID from 2.352 to 1.553 at NFE=16 (ω=1.4) and from 1.642 to 1.408 at NFE=32, while also improving recall (0.612→0.636 and 0.626→0.633). Against ADG [30], MG achieves substantially lower FID (1.553 vs. 2.324 at NFE=16, ω=1.4) with higher recall (0.636 vs. 0.570), indicating ADG trades diversity for precision more aggressively. Against CFG++ [12], MG dominates on FID (1.553 vs. 2.620 at NFE=16) while CFG++ achieves slightly higher precision at the cost of much lower recall. Against RAE† [81] (which uses an additional vision foundation model), MG on top of RAE still improves FID from 1.535 to 1.376 at NFE=50/2, showing MG is complementary to representation-based approaches. On text-to-image models (SD3, FLUX.1-dev), MG improves HPSv2.1 over vanilla CFG at every tested CFG scale, with the largest gains at low CFG (e.g., +4.5 HPSv2.1 at CFG=1 on SD3), confirming that MG adds value even when CFG is already active.

## Relation to Prior Work

Momentum Guidance sits within the family of inference-time guidance methods for flow and diffusion models, which all share the principle of extrapolating a primary velocity away from a smoother reference. CFG obtains that reference from an unconditional branch, Autoguidance from a weaker auxiliary checkpoint, and attention-based variants (SAG, PAG, SEG) from degraded self-attention outputs. MG departs from all of these by sourcing the smoother reference from the ODE trajectory itself: an exponential moving average of past velocity estimates, which are intrinsically smoother because earlier timesteps correspond to higher-noise marginals. This borrows the EMA mechanism from optimization (Adam, momentum SGD) and repurposes it as a zero-cost guidance signal at inference time, requiring no extra network evaluations or auxiliary models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-overhead property makes MG immediately deployable on large open models (FLUX.1-dev, SD3) where auxiliary checkpoints are unavailable and extra forward passes are costly, and the precision-recall frontier expansion (rather than a mere shift) suggests it captures a genuinely different axis of quality improvement than CFG scale tuning. However, the gains on large text-to-image models are modest (≈0.1–0.5 HPSv2.1 points), and the video results are marginal (0.816→0.819 VBench), leaving open whether the trajectory-EMA signal remains informative at the very high resolutions and long temporal horizons where flow models are most deployed. A key open question is whether the EMA reference can be made adaptive (e.g., time-varying β) to better match the non-uniform smoothness transition along the trajectory, and whether the approach extends naturally to stochastic samplers or non-rectified flow formulations.
