## Problem

Reinforcement learning for text-to-image generation critically depends on reward design, yet existing approaches either combine heterogeneous metrics (CLIP, OCR, realism) with fragile fixed weights or collapse diverse objectives into a single opaque scalar from preference models. Both paradigms lack interpretability, composability, and user control over which visual aspects are prioritized, making it difficult to diagnose failures or tailor optimization to specific generation goals.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image (512×512 for Phi3-3.8B via LlamaGen VQ decoder, 1024×1024 for Qwen2.5-0.5B via CosmosTokenizer, or native resolutions for diffusion backbones like Qwen-Image and FLUX). A key intermediate artifact is a dynamically generated rubric of M=10 prompt-specific binary criteria (e.g., object count, attribute accuracy, OCR fidelity, spatial relations), which is produced per-prompt by GPT-o4-mini and used to score each generated image before aggregation into a scalar reward for policy optimization.

## Method

RubricRL replaces fixed multi-reward ensembles or learned scalar reward models with a two-stage rubric-based reward: (1) a Rubric Generation Model (GPT-o4-mini) interprets each prompt and produces a decomposable checklist of 10 fine-grained visual criteria, with random permutation and multi-query aggregation to reduce bias and redundancy; (2) the same VLM scores each generated image against every criterion as a binary pass/fail, and the reward is the normalized mean of these scores. This reward plugs into standard policy optimizers—GRPO for autoregressive backbones and DiffusionNFT for diffusion backbones—making the framework architecture-agnostic. A dynamic rollout sampling strategy further improves training by oversampling N′ rollouts per prompt and selecting a hybrid subset (top-K high-reward plus random N−K) to balance exploitation and diversity, with advantage normalization computed only over the retained subset to prevent low-quality rollouts from distorting gradients.

## Results

On GenEval with the Phi3-3.8B AR backbone, RubricRL achieves an overall score of 0.8468, surpassing the strongest baseline (CLIPScore at 0.8086) by ~4% and the SFT baseline (0.7624) by 8.4%. With the smaller Qwen2.5-0.5B backbone, RubricRL reaches 0.6014 overall, a 14.8% improvement over SFT (0.5239). On diffusion backbones, RubricRL improves Qwen-Image (4B) from 0.8406 to 0.9091 (+6.85%) and FLUX.1-dev (12B) from 0.8121 to 0.8969 (+8.48%) on GenEval, consistently outperforming all six reward baselines across both model families and both benchmarks (GenEval and DPG-Bench).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RubricRL outperforms all six baselines across every backbone and benchmark. Against the single-reward baselines, it beats CLIPScore by 3.8% (Phi3) and 3.4% (Qwen2.5) on GenEval overall, HPSv2 by 4.3% and 4.5%, Unified Reward by 4.7% and 2.9%, and LLaVA-Reward-Phi by 8.3% and 7.8%. Against the composite multi-reward baselines, RubricRL surpasses AR-GRPO by 4.7% (Phi3) and 4.2% (Qwen2.5), and X-Omni by 3.9% and 4.5% respectively. On diffusion backbones, the margins widen: RubricRL leads X-Omni by 5.6% on Qwen-Image and 6.4% on FLUX, and leads CLIPScore by 5.7% and 4.5% respectively. The largest relative gains appear on the Counting and Color Attribute subcategories, where the rubric's explicit per-criterion scoring provides targeted supervision that scalar or fixed-weight rewards cannot match.

## Relation to Prior Work

RubricRL builds on the RL post-training paradigm for text-to-image generation established by GRPO (for autoregressive models) and DiffusionNFT (for diffusion models), but its core contribution is a new reward design that departs from both prior families: multi-reward ensembles (X-Omni, AR-GRPO) that combine CLIP, OCR, and realism scores with manually tuned weights, and unified scalar reward models (OneReward, LLaVA-Reward) that collapse diverse objectives into a single learned score. Instead, it uses a multimodal LLM (GPT-o4-mini) to dynamically generate a prompt-specific checklist of fine-grained visual criteria and score each independently, replacing the entire ensemble with a single reasoning-capable grader. The dynamic rollout sampling strategy (hybrid top-K + random) also extends prior work like DAPO's prompt-level filtering to a finer-grained, within-prompt selection mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path toward interpretable and user-controllable RL alignment for text-to-image generation: developers can inspect, extend, or reweight individual rubric criteria without retraining reward models, and the approach is architecture-agnostic across AR and diffusion backbones. Key limitations include reliance on a proprietary VLM API (GPT-o4-mini) for both rubric generation and scoring, which raises cost and reproducibility concerns, and observed grader failures on ambiguous counting scenarios that degrade performance for smaller base models. Open questions include whether the rubric pipeline can be distilled into a local model, how it scales to video or 3D generation, and whether the prompt-adaptive weighting can be made more principled than the current top-10 selection heuristic.
