## Problem

Autoregressive image generators trained via maximum-likelihood estimation lack direct optimization for sample quality and diversity, and existing RL-based alignment methods for AR models rely exclusively on instance-level rewards, trading off distributional coverage for per-sample quality. Unlike diffusion-model RL approaches that suffer from severe diversity collapse, no prior work explicitly optimizes for distribution-level fidelity during AR policy fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261070/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an ImageNet class label as conditioning and autoregressively generates a sequence of discrete tokens over a 16384-entry codebook (16× downsampling), which is decoded into a 256×256 RGB image. A notable practical property is that the tuned policy produces competitive samples without Classifier-Free Guidance, halving the 2× test-time inference overhead that standard AR generation incurs.

## Method

The paper casts class-conditional token synthesis as a Markov Decision Process and fine-tunes the AR policy via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), eliminating the need for a value network. The key novelty is a distribution-level Leave-One-Out FID (LOO-FID) reward: an exponential moving average of Inception feature moments tracks the generator's running distribution, and each sample's reward is the change in diagonal FID when that sample is excluded, directly incentivizing coverage and preventing mode collapse. This is combined with composite instance-level rewards (CLIPScore for semantic alignment and HPSv2 for perceptual preference) and an adaptive entropy bonus that dynamically adjusts its coefficient based on the gap between current and target token entropy, stabilizing multi-objective exploration throughout training.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, the proposed method consistently improves FID across all LlamaGen backbones: LlamaGen-B (7.06→6.31), LlamaGen-L (4.64→3.83), and LlamaGen-XL (3.96→3.82), while also boosting Inception Score (e.g., 196.78→215.90 for L) and CLIPScore. Without Classifier-Free Guidance, gains are even more dramatic—LlamaGen-L drops from FID 10.24 to 5.12 and IS rises from 82.38 to 143.32—halving test-time inference cost. Against MLE fine-tuning, 600 iterations of the proposed RL method (FID 3.83, IS 215.90) substantially outperforms 5000 MLE iterations (FID 4.54, IS 202.43).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the pretrained LlamaGen baselines, the method improves FID by 0.81 (L) and 0.14 (XL) while preserving or slightly increasing Recall, confirming gains come from policy refinement rather than overfitting. Versus AR-GRPO (the closest concurrent RL baseline), the proposed method achieves substantially lower FID (3.83 vs. 6.37 with CLIP+HPS; 6.43 vs. 9.34 with MANIQA added) and higher Recall (0.63 vs. 0.56), though AR-GRPO edges ahead on IS (223.95 vs. 215.90) and Precision (0.82 vs. 0.79) in the two-reward setting, reflecting a quality-coverage trade-off. Against VA-π, the method wins decisively in the no-CFG setting (FID 4.55 vs. 7.65, IS 148.16 vs. 102.96) and also achieves better FID and Recall with CFG (3.82 vs. 6.62; 0.67 vs. 0.58), though VA-π attains higher IS with CFG (212.60 vs. 195.58). On the VQGAN backbone, the method improves FID from 21.90 to 15.47 (no top-k) and 16.42 to 13.04 (top-k=100), with corresponding IS gains, demonstrating architecture generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines: autoregressive image generation (LlamaGen, VQGAN, Parti) and RL-based post-training (GRPO/PPO for LLMs, DDPO/DRaFT for diffusion models). It departs from concurrent AR-RL work (AR-GRPO, VA-π, STAGE) by introducing a distribution-level LOO-FID reward alongside standard instance-level signals (CLIPScore, HPSv2), rather than relying solely on per-sample quality scores. The EMA-based leave-one-out mechanism for attributing FID change to individual samples is a new technical contribution not present in prior AR or diffusion RL formulations, and the adaptive entropy bonus addresses a stability problem specific to multi-objective token-level policy optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication of eliminating Classifier-Free Guidance (halving AR inference cost) while matching or exceeding CFG-guided quality is significant for deployment of AR image generators. The stark MLE comparison (5000 iterations of continued training barely moves FID, while 600 RL iterations substantially improves it) suggests that reward-based policy optimization is a far more sample-efficient post-training paradigm than additional likelihood training, a finding that could reshape how AR generators are fine-tuned. Open questions include whether the LOO-FID reward generalizes to text-to-image (vs. class-conditional) settings, whether it scales to video or 3D AR models, and whether the reliance on Inception features as the distributional proxy limits applicability to domains where Inception is not a meaningful feature space.
