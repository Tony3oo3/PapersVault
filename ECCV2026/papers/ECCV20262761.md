## Problem

Aerial-ground person re-identification faces two coupled challenges that existing methods address incompletely: (1) in aerial imagery, pedestrians occupy a tiny fraction of the frame, and low-weight background activations persist through transformer residual connections, progressively contaminating identity embeddings; (2) the extreme geometric discrepancy between top-down aerial and horizontal ground views forces a shared backbone to approximate heterogeneous transformations, producing a compromised mapping suboptimal for both views. Prior approaches either perform hard token pruning (disrupting spatial topology) or keep backbone parameters static (leaving operator-level geometric conflicts unresolved).

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 256×128 pixels from either aerial (top-down UAV) or ground-level (CCTV) cameras, processed by a ViT-B/16 backbone pretrained on ImageNet. It produces a single view-invariant identity embedding per image, obtained by aggregating a decoupled identity token with spatially-refined local patch tokens through a two-way attention decoder. The embedding is used for cross-view retrieval, evaluated via Rank-1 accuracy and mAP under multiple query-gallery view combinations (A→G, G→A, A→C, C→A, A→W, W→A).

## Method

VIGA combines two complementary modules atop a ViT encoder with VDT-style identity-view disentanglement (subtracting a learned view token from the cls token to obtain a pure identity feature). First, Identity-Guided Sparsification (IGS) computes cosine similarity between the identity feature and each local patch, then applies a soft attenuation mask that scales the top-ρ (87.5%) most relevant patches to 1.0 and the remainder to ε=10⁻³, suppressing background noise while preserving the full 2D token grid and spatial continuity. Second, View-Conditioned Low-Rank Adaptation (VC-LoRA) injects view-conditioned residual branches into the decoder's FFN layers: lightweight hyper-networks generate low-rank factors A∈R^{d_in×r} and B∈R^{r×d_out} (r=16) from the view token, so the effective FFN weight becomes W(v)=W₀+α·AB, enabling per-view geometric specialization without duplicating backbone parameters. Training is end-to-end with a composite loss combining identity (cross-entropy + triplet), view classification (cross-entropy on the view token), and an orthogonality penalty that forces the identity and view representations to be angularly independent.

## Results

On LAGPeR, VIGA achieves 43.47% Rank-1 and 31.52% mAP on A→G, 36.05% Rank-1 and 34.44% mAP on G→A, and 25.11% Rank-1 and 20.18% mAP on the most challenging G→A+G setting, outperforming the strongest baseline SeCap by +1.68, +0.79, and +0.72 Rank-1 points respectively. On AG-ReID.v2, VIGA reaches 88.88% Rank-1 (A→C) and 88.96% Rank-1 (C→A), surpassing AG-ReIDv2 by +0.11 and +1.10 Rank-1 points on the core aerial-CCTV tasks. The method also improves mAP by up to +1.65 points over SeCap on AG-ReID.v2 (C→A: 81.51 vs 79.99), indicating a more consistent feature space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIGA outperforms all six baselines on LAGPeR across every evaluation protocol. Against the strongest prior method SeCap (CVPR 2025), VIGA gains +1.68 Rank-1 on A→G (43.47 vs 41.79) and +0.72 Rank-1 on G→A+G (25.11 vs 24.39), with consistent mAP improvements of +1.15 and +0.94 respectively. VDT (CVPR 2024) is surpassed by +3.32 Rank-1 on A→G and +5.61 Rank-1 on G→A+G, confirming that view-conditioned parameter adaptation resolves geometric conflicts that static decoupling alone cannot. On AG-ReID.v2, VIGA wins on the core aerial-CCTV tasks (A→C: 88.88 vs 88.77 for AG-ReIDv2; C→A: 88.96 vs 87.86) and on W→A (87.95 vs 88.61 for AG-ReIDv2, 87.95 vs 87.56 for SeCap), but trails AG-ReIDv2 on the wearable splits (A→W: 90.36 vs 93.62; W→A: 87.95 vs 88.61), likely because AG-ReIDv2 leverages additional attribute annotations that VIGA does not use. The plain ViT backbone is improved by +4.80 Rank-1 on A→G and +6.23 Rank-1 on G→A+G, demonstrating the cumulative benefit of both IGS and VC-LoRA over an unmodified transformer.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIGA builds directly on the VDT identity-view disentanglement strategy (subtraction-based separation of cls and view tokens) and repurposes the LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm for view-conditioned FFN modulation in a Re-ID decoder. It departs from prior token-pruning approaches (DTST, dynamic token selection) by replacing hard removal with identity-guided soft attenuation, and from static-backbone AG-ReID methods (VDT, SeCap) by introducing view-adaptive weight updates at the operator level rather than relying solely on feature-level decoupling or input-level prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As UAV surveillance becomes more prevalent, AG-ReID is a practically important cross-platform retrieval problem where geometric distortion and background clutter are severe; VIGA's joint treatment of activation-level noise and parameter-level geometric conflict offers a principled decomposition that practitioners can adopt. However, the improvements over the strongest baseline (SeCap) are modest (≈1–2% Rank-1), the method is validated only on ViT-B/16 across two datasets, and performance on wearable-device splits (A↔W) actually lags SeCap, leaving open whether the view-conditioned low-rank mechanism generalizes to more than two view categories or to larger backbones. The low absolute accuracy on the most challenging G→A+G protocol (~25% Rank-1) also signals that AG-ReID remains far from solved.
