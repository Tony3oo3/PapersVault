## Problem

Existing universal photometric stereo (UniPS) methods rely on fully end-to-end learning that implicitly infers illumination while predicting normals, causing illumination and geometry to remain entangled in learned representations. This lack of explicit physical constraints makes such models sensitive to distribution shifts and prone to geometric ambiguities, particularly in fine-scale detail recovery. No prior approach leverages the well-calibrated physics of CPS as an intermediate representation to guide UniPS under unconstrained lighting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a variable-length sequence of 6–24 RGB images (randomly sampled during training) captured under arbitrary, unknown illumination, with no requirement for calibrated light directions or controlled acquisition. It outputs a pixel-wise surface normal map N ∈ R^{3×H×W}. Internally, the lighting adapter produces N_out = 64 'Bridge Images' each paired with a fixed target illumination direction sampled uniformly over the spherical cap z > 0.6, but these intermediates receive no direct supervision. The method is evaluated at resolutions ranging from 612×512 (DiLiGenT) to 2048×1536 (LUCES).

## Method

Bridge-UniPS is a two-stage framework: a trainable lighting adapter followed by a frozen pretrained CPS network (Norm-PSN). The adapter uses a ConvNeXt backbone to extract features, then a Dynamic Neural Query module performs illumination-conditioned cross-attention where 64 predefined target light directions (mapped via MLP into channel space) serve as queries over the input image features, with a Global Confidence matrix and score-guided top-K selection filtering unreliable observations. The decoder reconstructs Bridge Images using attention-guided skip connections that dynamically weight multi-scale ConvNeXt features according to per-pixel cross-attention weights. Crucially, the adapter receives no direct supervision on Bridge Images or illumination directions; it is optimized solely through the final normal-estimation loss (cosine + L1) with gradients back-propagated through the frozen CPS network, which acts as a physics-aligned constraint that implicitly guides the adapter to produce CPS-compatible, illumination-consistent image-illumination pairs.

## Results

On the DiLiGenT benchmark (constrained illumination), Bridge-UniPS achieves a mean angular error (MAE) of 4.3° across 10 objects, outperforming the best universal method LC25 (4.7°) and the best calibrated method LL22a (6.5°), while using only 16 input images versus 96 for all baselines. On the LUCES benchmark (near-field unconstrained illumination), the method achieves 7.11° average MAE, surpassing the previous best universal method LC25 (9.43°) by 2.32° and the next-best HQ24 (11.21°) by 4.10°. The method also demonstrates robustness to reduced input: with only 4 images (K=4), it still achieves 6.0° on DiLiGenT, competitive with full-input calibrated methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bridge-UniPS outperforms all 11 compared baselines on both main benchmarks. On DiLiGenT, it beats the strongest universal competitor LC25 [28] by 0.4° (4.3° vs 4.7°) and the best calibrated method LL22a [29] by 2.2° (4.3° vs 6.5°), while using only 16 of 96 input images. On LUCES (near-field unconstrained lighting), the margin widens substantially: Bridge-UniPS achieves 7.11° average MAE versus LC25's 9.43° (−2.32°), HQ24's 11.21° (−4.10°), and IK23's 13.50° (−6.39°). The method wins on 13 of 14 LUCES objects and 9 of 10 DiLiGenT objects. Notably, on highly specular objects (Goblet, Bell, Cup), the advantage over universal baselines is largest (e.g., 6.2° vs 5.2° on DiLiGenT Goblet; 4.78° vs 8.78° on LUCES Bell), confirming the bridging strategy's benefit for complex reflectance. The only DiLiGenT object where a baseline wins is Ball (IK23: 1.5° vs Ours: 2.0°), a simple Lambertian sphere where the overhead of the adapter provides no advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of calibrated photometric stereo (CPS) and universal photometric stereo (UniPS), building directly on Norm-PSN (Ju et al., IJCV 2022) as a frozen downstream estimator. Unlike prior UniPS methods (IK22, IK23, LC25, HQ24) that adopt fully end-to-end learning with global illumination contexts or latent tokens, Bridge-UniPS introduces a bridging paradigm: a trainable lighting adapter transforms unconstrained observations into CPS-compatible image-illumination pairs, establishing an explicit physics-aligned intermediate representation. The Dynamic Neural Query module, which uses predefined target light directions as cross-attention queries to selectively retrieve pixel-level evidence, departs from the global aggregation strategies used in prior UniPS encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that a frozen, physics-grounded CPS model can serve as an implicit constraint for training a lighting adapter without any direct supervision on intermediate bridge images or illumination annotations, suggesting that pretrained CPS networks encode useful physical priors transferable to unconstrained settings. The catastrophic degradation from shuffled bridge-image/illumination pairings (44.5° vs 4.3° MAE) confirms that image-illumination consistency, not merely image quality, is what enables CPS-compatible inference. The authors acknowledge that scenes dominated by ambient illumination with minimal active lighting variation remain challenging, and future work should improve the adapter's sensitivity to subtle illumination cues. More broadly, the bridging paradigm—using a frozen physics-informed model as a training signal for an adapter—could inspire similar approaches in other inverse problems where calibrated solvers exist but unconstrained inputs are the practical reality.
