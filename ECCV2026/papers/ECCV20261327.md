## Problem

Language-driven localization of fine-grained geometric entities (faces, edges) on 3D CAD meshes is substantially harder than 2D referring segmentation because 3D geometry is highly viewpoint-sensitive: self-occlusion, foreshortening, and thin structures can render a target entity invisible or ambiguous from arbitrary projections. Existing 3D open-vocabulary segmentation methods operate on unstructured point clouds or voxels and do not output exact topological mesh entities, while global CLIP-based view selection fails on homogeneous, textureless industrial parts where fine-grained geometric awareness is required.

## Input / Output

The system takes an unstructured 3D triangle mesh (no parametric B-Rep data required) and a natural language query describing a geometric entity (e.g., 'the inner chamfered edge'). The mesh is rendered into 60 calibrated candidate views at 30° azimuth increments and elevations from −60° to 60°. The output is a binary labeling over discrete mesh faces or edges, produced by lifting 2D segmentation masks back to mesh topology via geometry-aware ray casting, yielding topologically consistent entity predictions.

## Method

MV-GEL combines two independently trained modules: GELviews, a prompt-conditioned view ranking network, and LISA-CAD, a domain-adapted reasoning segmentation backbone. GELviews encodes each rendered view with a LoRA-adapted CLIP vision encoder and the query with a LoRA-adapted CLIP text encoder, injects learnable view positional embeddings, applies FiLM or Cross-Attention modality fusion, and passes the fused features through a multi-layer Transformer encoder operating along the view dimension to capture cross-view geometric dependencies; a lightweight scoring head produces final ranking scores trained with a pairwise margin loss. LISA-CAD fine-tunes LLaVA-7B (with SAM mask decoder) on 54k CAD query–mask pairs, updating only LoRA adapters and the mask decoder. The selected top-K views are segmented by LISA-CAD, and the resulting 2D binary masks are projected onto the mesh via ray casting to produce the final face or edge labels.

## Results

On the 1,535-query test set (655 faces, 880 edges across 218 CAD meshes), MV-GEL with FiLM view selection at top-1 achieves a face F1 of 0.598 and edge F1 of 0.358, substantially outperforming all point-cloud baselines. The best external baseline, Find3D, reaches only 0.260 face F1 and 0.032 edge F1, yielding roughly 2.3× and 11× F1 improvements respectively. At top-3 views, MV-GEL (FiLM) attains 0.540 face F1 and 0.317 edge F1 while maintaining high recall (0.810 face, 0.708 edge), demonstrating that the ranking module identifies the most informative views early. The FiLM selector captures 95% of the ground-truth-view oracle face F1 (0.598 vs. 0.632) and over 90% of the oracle edge F1 (0.358 vs. 0.390).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MV-GEL decisively outperforms all three point-cloud baselines on both face and edge localization. Against Find3D (the strongest baseline), MV-GEL (FiLM@top1) improves face F1 from 0.260 to 0.598 (+130%) and edge F1 from 0.032 to 0.358 (+1019%), with the gap driven primarily by precision: Find3D achieves 0.955 face recall but only 0.172 precision due to severe over-segmentation, whereas MV-GEL balances recall (0.706) with much higher precision (0.588). PatchAlign3D and PartSLIP perform even worse, with edge F1 scores of 0.031 and 0.022 respectively, and face F1 of 0.242 and 0.114. The adapted Top-PCD variants (restricting to top-density predictions) slightly improve precision for Find3D and PatchAlign3D but still fall far short of MV-GEL, confirming that the gap is architectural rather than a thresholding artifact. The paper attributes the baselines' weakness to their reliance on unstructured point clouds without explicit boundary topology or deep language reasoning, causing them to 'bleed' across face boundaries and fail to resolve thin 1D edges.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LISA referring-segmentation framework (which operates on natural images) to the CAD mesh domain by fine-tuning it as LISA-CAD, and introduces GELviews, a prompt-conditioned view-ranking module that departs from prior CLIP-similarity-based view selection (Cap3D, ViewRefer) by incorporating geometric visibility reasoning via a transformer view-interaction head. It also draws on the multi-view 3D recognition lineage (MVCNN, view-graph architectures) and the point-cloud open-vocabulary segmentation line (PartSLIP, Find3D, PatchAlign3D), but explicitly positions itself as the first to target exact topological mesh entities (faces, edges) rather than point or voxel-level predictions. The geometry-aware ray-casting lifting step bridges 2D VLM segmentation to discrete mesh topology, a connection not explored in prior 3D referring segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that language-driven 3D localization on structured geometry is fundamentally a view-prioritization problem, not merely a segmentation problem, and that a lightweight learned ranker can recover 90–95% of the oracle view-selection ceiling. This reframing has practical implications for CAD automation, robotic manipulation, and scientific simulation where precise entity identification is required. Limitations include reliance on a single ABC-derived dataset, restriction to one entity per query, and evaluation on relatively simple mechanical parts; open questions include scalability to complex assemblies, multi-entity and compositional queries, and whether the view-ranking insight transfers to other structured 3D representations (B-Reps, SDFs).
