## Problem

Recent vision-language multi-modal ReID methods treat text descriptions as clean, deterministic anchors, ignoring inherent noise such as modality-mismatched phrases and semantically ambiguous expressions that corrupt representation learning. Additionally, prevailing approaches lack explicit mechanisms to reconcile fine-grained structural discrepancies between heterogeneous imaging modalities, even after high-level semantic alignment has been achieved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260462/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB, NIR, and TIR images (resized to 256×128 for person ReID or 128×256 for vehicle ReID) along with modality-agnostic text captions generated by a caption pipeline. Text is condensed via GeM pooling into a small set of tokens (2 is optimal) before fusion. The output is a per-modality identity embedding obtained by residual addition of the original global token with a CASF-fused representation, used for cross-modal retrieval ranking via cosine similarity.

## Method

The framework integrates three synergistic modules built on a shared CLIP backbone. The Semantic Cross-Modal Modulator (SCM) fuses visual and text global tokens via Gated Cross-Attention, projects the result to a Gaussian distribution, and samples a task-aware π-noise perturbation that is added to each modality's global token; these enhanced tokens then drive affine modulation of other modalities' local patch features, enabling semantics-guided cross-modal compensation. The Structure-Aware Prompt Adapter (SPA) initializes modality-specific learnable prompts from frozen DINOv3 global tokens (used only at initialization, then detached), prepends them to visual sequences, and processes all modalities through a shared MHSA+FFN adapter to enforce cross-modal structural consistency. The Context-Aware Sparse Fusion (CASF) distills a compact structural context signal from the adapter output and uses it to gate a sparsely-activated Mixture-of-Experts layer over global tokens, producing a fused representation that shields identity features from noisy local details. Training combines label-smoothing cross-entropy and triplet loss on both modality-specific and fused features, plus an MoE load-balancing auxiliary loss.

## Results

On RGBNT201 (person ReID), the proposed method achieves 80.6% mAP and 83.9% Rank-1, surpassing the strongest CLIP-based baseline IDEA† (80.2% mAP, 82.1% R-1) and the best ViT-based method TOP-ReID* (72.3% mAP, 76.6% R-1) by 8.3% mAP. On the vehicle ReID benchmarks, it reaches 89.9% mAP / 98.2% R-1 on RGBNT100 and 65.0% mAP / 77.2% R-1 on MSVR310, improving over IDEA† by 18.0% mAP and 14.8% R-1 on the more challenging MSVR310. The model uses 38.68 GFLOPs and 96.23M trainable parameters, fewer than IDEA† (43.73 GFLOPs) while achieving higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 26 baselines spanning CNN, ViT, and CLIP-based architectures across three benchmarks. On RGBNT201, the method outperforms all CLIP-based competitors: IDEA† by 0.4% mAP / 1.8% R-1, MambaPro† by 1.7% mAP, and DeMo† by 1.6% mAP / 1.6% R-1, while surpassing the best ViT-based method TOP-ReID* by a large margin of 8.3% mAP / 7.3% R-1. On RGBNT100, it leads IDEA† by 2.7% mAP and DeMo† by 3.7% mAP, and exceeds TOP-ReID* by 8.7% mAP. The most dramatic gains appear on MSVR310, where the method achieves 65.0% mAP versus 47.0% for IDEA† (+18.0%) and 49.2% for DeMo† (+15.8%), attributed to the CASF module's noise-resilient expert routing under extreme imaging conditions. In modality-missing experiments (Table 9), the method also outperforms DeMo on average (53.2% vs. 50.7% mAP on RGBNT201), with the largest gap under the M(RT) setting (37.8% vs. 26.3% mAP). The method uses fewer trainable parameters (96.23M) than DeMo† (98.79M) and substantially fewer than TOP-ReID* (324.53M) while achieving the best accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based multi-modal ReID line (IDEA, STMI, MambaPro, DeMo) by reframing the role of textual descriptions: rather than treating text as a clean semantic anchor to be fused directly, it draws on the Positive-Incentive Noise (π-noise) literature (Li, TNNLS 2022; Zhang et al., TPAMI 2025) to model text-induced uncertainty as a structured, task-aware perturbation that actively reinforces identity-discriminative features. It also departs from prior structural-alignment strategies (token permutation in TOP-ReID, modality decoupling in DeMo) by injecting DINOv3-derived geometric priors through learnable prompts and routing fusion through a context-conditioned sparse MoE, combining three previously separate ideas—noise-as-regularizer, prompt-based structural prior, and expert routing—into a single pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The π-noise reframing offers a principled way to handle the well-known problem of noisy or mismatched text annotations in vision-language ReID, and the robustness experiments (only 3.6% mAP drop under 20% text corruption vs. 18.6% for the baseline) suggest the approach could be practically valuable in deployment settings where captions are imperfect. The 18-point mAP gain on MSVR310 over the closest CLIP-based competitor highlights that structural-context-guided sparse fusion is particularly effective under extreme imaging conditions. Open questions include whether the DINOv3 initialization dependency generalizes to modality combinations beyond RGB-NIR-TIR, how sensitive the method is to the quality of the caption-generation pipeline, and whether the π-noise mechanism transfers to other vision-language tasks where textual noise is prevalent.
