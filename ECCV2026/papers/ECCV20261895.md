## Problem

Despite rapid progress in Latent Action Models (LAMs) that tokenize visual changes into action representations, there is no rigorous, standardized framework to evaluate the quality of these representations independently of downstream policy performance. Existing evaluations rely on manipulation task success rates or qualitative cluster visualizations, failing to decouple representation quality from policy architecture and lacking coverage across embodiments, tasks, and granularities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261895/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes two input formats: (1) curated image pairs separated by a fixed frame interval (s=5) for atomic and regression tasks, and (2) 9-frame video clips (under 5 seconds, resized to 224×224) for composite classification tasks, where a Motion-Guided Sampler selects frames to capture sufficient dynamic variation. Outputs are continuous latent action embeddings (not discretized codebook indices) that are probed by a 4-layer attentive classifier for action labels or by an MLP Action Expert to regress absolute or delta end-effector trajectories of 7-DoF, 12-DoF, or 16-DoF action chunks.

## Method

LARYBench introduces a unified evaluation framework with two complementary tasks: hierarchical semantic action classification (28 atomic kinematic primitives + 145 composite behaviors) and low-level control regression (absolute and delta end-effector poses). An automated VLM-based data engine re-segments and re-annotates a corpus of 1.2M videos, 620K image pairs, and 595K trajectories across 151 action categories and 11 robotic embodiments. The paper benchmarks 11 models across four paradigms—Embodied LAMs, General Semantic Encoders, Generative Pixel Encoders, and a newly proposed class of General LAMs that graft the LAPA VQ-VAE training paradigm onto frozen pre-trained vision backbones (DINOv2/v3, SigLIP2, MAGVIT2). Key findings show that off-the-shelf general vision encoders (e.g., V-JEPA 2.1, DINOv3) consistently outperform specialized embodied LAMs on both semantic and control tasks, and that latent-based feature spaces align more naturally with physical action space than pixel-reconstruction spaces.

## Results

On the LARYBench classification task (151 action categories, 1.2M videos), V-JEPA 2.1 achieves the best average accuracy of 76.29%, followed by V-JEPA 2 (75.39%) and DINOv3 (72.63%), all trained without any explicit action supervision. The best General LAM (LAPA-DINOv3) reaches 62.73% average, while the best Embodied LAM (villa-X) manages only 23.85%. On the regression task, DINOv3 attains the lowest absolute normalized MSE (0.18 avg.) and V-JEPA 2.1 the lowest delta MSE (0.50 avg.), with the best General LAM (LAPA-DINOv3) at 0.60/0.57 respectively, while Embodied LAMs average 0.82–0.96.

## Baselines & Comparisons

General semantic encoders (V-JEPA 2.1, DINOv3, SigLIP 2) dominate classification, with V-JEPA 2.1 leading at 76.29% average accuracy—roughly 3× the best Embodied LAM (villa-X at 23.85%) and 13.6 points above the best General LAM (LAPA-DINOv3 at 62.73%). Pixel encoders (Wan2.2, FLUX.2-dev) perform moderately on composite human actions but collapse on atomic robot tasks (Wan2.2: 14.91%), confirming that pixel-level reconstruction does not capture fine-grained kinematic primitives. Among General LAMs, the backbone choice is decisive: LAPA-DINOv3 (62.73%) outperforms LAPA-MAGVIT2 (44.62%) and LAPA-SigLIP2 (42.09%), showing that contrastive self-supervised priors yield superior action representations over generative or vision-language contrastive ones. On regression, DINOv3 achieves the best absolute MSE (0.18 avg.) and V-JEPA 2.1 the best delta MSE (0.50 avg.), while the best General LAM (LAPA-DINOv3) trails by 0.42 in absolute but narrows to 0.07 in delta, consistent with LAMs naturally encoding transition-like quantities. Embodied LAMs (LAPA, UniVLA, villa-X) are uniformly the weakest across both tasks, with absolute MSE of 0.87–0.96 and classification accuracy below 24%, indicating that domain-specific training on scarce robotic data actively constrains representation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LAPA's VQ-VAE framework for learning latent actions from video, but departs from it by substituting the pixel-level encoder with frozen pre-trained general vision backbones (DINOv2/3, SigLIP2, MAGVIT2), creating a new 'General LAM' model family. It also positions itself against the broader VLA and world-model literature (GR00T, V-JEPA 2, Cosmos) where latent actions serve as unification interfaces, and against prior evaluation approaches (downstream task performance, cluster visualization) that fail to decouple representation quality from policy performance. The key conceptual departure is treating general visual self-supervised pretraining as a sufficient source of action-relevant features, rather than requiring explicit action supervision or domain-specific robotic data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that off-the-shelf general vision encoders outperform purpose-built embodied LAMs by large margins (e.g., 76% vs. 19–24% classification accuracy) challenges the prevailing assumption that domain-specific training on scarce robotic data is necessary for effective action representations, suggesting a paradigm shift toward aligning control policies with general visual feature spaces. The insight that latent-based representations align better with physical action space than pixel-based ones further reframes how VLA architectures should be designed. Open questions include how to close the remaining gap on robot-specific actions in General LAMs, how to decode continuous (non-quantized) signals for real-time control, and whether these findings hold at the scale of full end-to-end policy learning rather than probing.
