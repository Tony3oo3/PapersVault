## Problem

Existing perspective-to-panorama generation methods require explicit geometric alignment between the perspective input and the equirectangular projection target, which in turn demands known camera metadata (FoV, yaw, pitch, roll). This dependency makes them brittle on in-the-wild data where such calibration is absent or noisy, and causes complete failure when external camera estimators produce errors. The paper asks whether a general-purpose architecture can learn the perspective-to-ERP mapping purely from data without any geometric prior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260534/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a perspective image or video with arbitrary FoV (30°–120°), pitch (±60°), and roll (±15°), plus a text caption, and produces a gravity-aligned 360° panoramic image at 1024×2048 or a panoramic video at 512×1024 with 81 frames, both in equirectangular projection. No camera intrinsics or extrinsics are provided at inference time; the model implicitly infers them. The output is always in a canonical upright orientation regardless of the input camera pose, and the generated panoramic videos are 3D-consistent enough to support downstream 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction.

## Method

360Anything fine-tunes a pre-trained diffusion transformer (FLUX.1-dev for images, Wan2.1-14B for videos) under a flow-matching objective, replacing the standard channel-concatenation conditioning with a simple sequence concatenation: the perspective input latents and the noisy panorama latents are appended along the token sequence, and the DiT learns the geometric correspondence via global self-attention without any explicit projection. A canonical coordinate constraint forces the model to generate gravity-aligned panoramas, requiring it to internally estimate the input camera pose to 'place' the perspective view on the 360° canvas. The paper identifies zero-padding in the VAE encoder as the root cause of ERP seam artifacts and introduces Circular Latent Encoding—circularly padding the panorama before VAE encoding and discarding the padded latents afterward—to produce a seamless latent target. Training data is canonicalized via COLMAP for per-frame pose alignment and GeoCalib for gravity estimation, and camera augmentation (random FoV, pitch, roll sampling) is applied to the conditioning crops to ensure robustness to arbitrary in-the-wild inputs.

## Results

On perspective-to-360° image generation, 360Anything achieves FID 8.0 and FAED 9.8 on Laval Indoor, and FID 22.4 and FAED 3.8 on SUN360, outperforming the prior SOTA CubeDiff (FID 9.5/25.5, FAED 18.4/7.6) across nearly all metrics. On perspective-to-360° video generation, 360Anything achieves PSNR 25.75 and FVD 483.4 on real camera trajectories, substantially surpassing the best baseline ViewPoint (PSNR 23.25, FVD 844.3). For zero-shot camera calibration, 360Anything attains a mean FoV error of 4.93° across three datasets and roll/pitch errors of 0.87°/2.56° on MegaDepth, competitive with dedicated supervised methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For image generation, 360Anything decisively outperforms OmniDreamer, PanoDiffusion, and Diffusion360 on all reported metrics (FID, KID, FAED, CLIP-score). Against the closest competitor CubeDiff, 360Anything wins on FID, KID, FAED, and CLIP-score on both datasets, with FAED reduced by nearly 50% (9.8 vs 18.4 on Laval Indoor; 3.8 vs 7.6 on SUN360), though it trails slightly on CLIP-FID for Laval Indoor (4.6 vs 3.2). For video generation, 360Anything surpasses all three baselines (Imagine360, Argus, ViewPoint) on every metric; the gap is largest on FVD (483.4 vs 844.3 for ViewPoint) and LPIPS (0.0468 vs 0.1364), indicating far better input preservation and spherical distortion fidelity. On zero-shot FoV estimation, 360Anything outperforms all supervised baselines (Perspective, WildCam, LeReS, UniDepth) but lags behind DUSt3R (4.06° vs 4.93° mean) and MoGe (2.91°) by 1–2 degrees. For camera orientation estimation, 360Anything beats MSCC, ParamNet, and UVp on both roll and pitch, trailing only GeoCalib by approximately 0.5°.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in perspective-to-panorama generation, where prior methods (PanoDiffusion, CubeDiff, Argus, Imagine360) explicitly project the conditioning image into ERP space using known camera intrinsics and extrinsics to create pixel-aligned conditioning. Instead, 360Anything repurposes the sequence-concatenation mechanism from recent image-editing DiTs (FLUX Kontext, Qwen-Image) to treat the perspective input and panorama target as a single token sequence, letting global self-attention learn the geometric mapping purely from data. The architecture is built on flow-matching DiTs (fine-tuning FLUX.1-dev for images and Wan2.1-14B for videos) operating in VAE latent space, and the Circular Latent Encoding contribution directly addresses a boundary artifact in the convolutional VAE encoder that prior works had only mitigated with inference-time tricks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the camera-calibration dependency, 360Anything makes perspective-to-panorama generation applicable to truly in-the-wild media where FoV and pose metadata are absent or noisy, which is the practical bottleneck for AR/VR and 3D world generation pipelines. The Circular Latent Encoding fix is a simple, zero-overhead architectural change that any VAE-based panorama generator can adopt, and the competitive zero-shot camera estimation results suggest the model develops genuine geometric understanding as a byproduct. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions and longer videos without the current 81-frame/512×1024 constraints, whether the implicit geometric understanding generalizes to extreme viewpoints or non-rigid scenes, and whether the canonicalization pipeline (COLMAP + GeoCalib) introduces its own errors on highly dynamic footage.
