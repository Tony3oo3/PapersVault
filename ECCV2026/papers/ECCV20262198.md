## Problem

Prompt-conditioned segmenters like SAM2 require explicit spatial prompts (points, boxes, masks) to guide mask decoding, yet fully automatic deployment provides no such prompts, creating a structural interface mismatch. Existing adaptations address this by injecting task-specific modules into intermediate features, which bypasses the native prompt-conditioned decoding pathway and leaves it weakly activated. This mismatch is especially acute in camouflaged object detection, where the absence of disambiguating prompts causes severe background leakage and unstable masks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 512×512 and produces a binary segmentation mask at the original resolution. No external prompts (points, boxes, or masks) are provided at inference; instead, the model internally synthesizes dense prompt logits at 128×128 resolution and continuous sparse token embeddings, which are routed through SAM2's frozen prompt encoder. The final output is a coarse mask from the task-specific decoder, optionally corrected by a lightweight pixel refinement head that adds a residual via a zero-initialized 1×1 convolution.

## Method

IP-SAM restores prompt-conditioned decoding without external prompts by synthesizing complementary intrinsic foreground/background prompts through a dual-branch Self-Prompt Generator (SPG), a 2-layer TwoWayTransformer decoder that extracts dense mask-like logits (P⁺, P⁻) and sparse token embeddings from SAM2's FPN features via cross-attention. These continuous logits are fed directly into SAM2's frozen prompt encoder to produce native prompt-space embeddings (Z⁺, Z⁻), preserving the pre-trained geometric manifold. Prompt-Space Gating (PSG) then uses the negative embedding Z⁻ to form a localized suppressive gate that asymmetrically attenuates background-correlated activations in Z⁺ before decoding, preventing deceptive camouflage textures from reaching the mask decoder. The purified condition Z, concatenated sparse tokens, and propagated output tokens drive a task-specific mask decoder (initialized from SAM2 weights) to produce the final mask. Only 21.26M parameters are trainable—comprising SPG, PSG, the mask decoder, and LoRA (r=32) on the SAM2 image encoder's attention projections—while the prompt encoder remains strictly frozen.

## Results

Under a deterministic no-external-prompt protocol, IP-SAM (SAM2-L, LoRA r=32) achieves state-of-the-art COD performance with only 21.26M trainable parameters: MAE 0.017 / S_m 0.906 on COD10K, MAE 0.032 / S_m 0.912 on CAMO, MAE 0.018 / S_m 0.920 on CHAMELEON, and MAE 0.026 / S_m 0.911 on NC4K. On medical polyp segmentation (trained on Kvasir-SEG), IP-SAM achieves mDice 0.913 / MAE 0.025 in-domain and mDice 0.840 / S_m 0.897 on CVC-ClinicDB, outperforming SAM-based adaptations. The model runs at 29 FPS on a single RTX 3090, with a stable COD10K MAE of 0.017 ± 0.001 across three seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IP-SAM outperforms the strongest specialist COD model (ZoomNeXt, 28.46M params) on all four benchmarks, reducing COD10K MAE from 0.018 to 0.017 and CAMO MAE from 0.041 to 0.032, while also surpassing it on structure-sensitive metrics (S_m 0.906 vs 0.898 on COD10K). Against the best feature-adapted SAM2 baseline (SAM2-L r80, 18.96M trainable params), IP-SAM improves COD10K MAE from 0.020 to 0.017 and CAMO MAE from 0.037 to 0.032, with larger gains on S_m (0.906 vs 0.890) and E_φ (0.947 vs 0.933). IP-SAM also beats the SAM2 Oracle (ground-truth single-point prompt) on COD10K (MAE 0.017 vs 0.030), demonstrating that dense intrinsic prompts with PSG provide richer conditioning than a single centroid under severe camouflage. Zero-shot SAM2-AMG collapses under camouflage (COD10K MAE 0.262, CHAMELEON MAE 0.500), and YOLOv8+SAM2 auto-prompting suffers 35.4% full-image fallbacks on COD10K (MAE 0.318), confirming the fragility of discrete coordinate-based auto-prompting. On medical polyp segmentation, IP-SAM surpasses MDSAM (mDice 0.913 vs 0.906 on Kvasir-SEG; 0.840 vs 0.796 on CVC-ClinicDB) and SAM2-Adapter (0.840 vs 0.801 mDice on CVC-ClinicDB), while matching or exceeding specialist models like LACFormer on overlap and structure metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant feature-space adaptation paradigm for SAM-based downstream tasks (adapters, lateral fusion, task-specific decoders) by arguing that bypassing SAM2's native prompt interface weakens the architecture's core design assumption. It builds directly on SAM2's frozen prompt encoder and mask decoder, introducing a Self-Prompt Generator (SPG) that synthesizes dense mask-like intrinsic prompts and a Prompt-Space Gating (PSG) module that uses background-derived embeddings as asymmetric suppressive constraints. While related to automatic prompting work (AO-Prompter, RSPrompter) that generates discrete geometric cues, IP-SAM instead produces continuous dense spatial priors and routes them through the frozen prompt manifold, combined with LoRA on the image encoder for lightweight domain adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a genuine structural mismatch in deploying prompt-conditioned foundation models in fully automatic settings and offers a clean, well-ablated solution that restores the prompt-conditioned decoding pathway without external prompts. The finding that prompt-space conditioning outperforms feature-space injection—even with a minimal CNN-based SPG—challenges the prevailing assumption that intermediate feature modulation is the primary adaptation lever. Limitations include dependence on foundation-model capacity (smaller SAM2 variants degrade), potential oversuppression in extremely ambiguous cases, and current scope limited to foreground-background segmentation; extending to multi-instance, semantic, or open-vocabulary parsing remains open.
