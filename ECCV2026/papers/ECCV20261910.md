## Problem

GRPO-based video post-training suffers from two structural failure modes: (i) fixed weighted aggregation of multi-aspect rewards induces shortcut-driven optimization where the model exploits one component at the expense of others (Goodhart's Law), and (ii) reward scores compress within prompt groups over training, eroding the within-group ranking separation that GRPO advantages depend on. Existing approaches either use static reward weights or rigid multi-stage schedules, neither of which adapts to the evolving informativeness of individual reward components during optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a generated video via SDE sampling from a flow-matching diffusion backbone (Wan2.1-T2V at 480×832×81 frames/16 fps for 1.3B, 240×416×53 frames/16 fps for 14B; HunyuanVideo at 240×416×53 frames/15 fps). During training, each prompt yields a group of G=16 candidate videos that are scored by K reward components instantiated with VLM evaluators (VideoAlign or Qwen2.5-VL 7B/72B), and the resulting per-component reward vectors drive the adaptive weighting and policy update.

## Method

TaRoS replaces fixed reward aggregation with a per-group, per-component adaptive weighting scheme. For each sampled group, it computes a utility score for every reward component combining (a) a performance-assessment term—sigmoid of the group mean reward minus a calibrated threshold—and (b) a within-group separation term based on the Hoyer sparsity index, which measures how well the component's scores discriminate among candidates. These utilities are converted to mixture weights via softmax, yielding a soft, continuous reweighting that automatically downweights saturated or uninformative components without hard stage boundaries. The weighted aggregated rewards are then standardized to form group-relative advantages and plugged into a standard GRPO clipped objective with importance-ratio clipping. The framework is evaluator-agnostic and backbone-agnostic, requiring only that reward components produce scalar scores per video, and it introduces no additional trainable parameters beyond the generator itself.

## Results

On Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B (VBench), TaRoS with Qwen2.5-VL-72B achieves a total score of 80.56, outperforming the untrained Wan baseline (79.58) and DanceGRPO with the same reward model (79.72). The quality score improves from 83.15 (Wan) to 83.66 (TaRoS+Qwen72B), and the semantic score rises from 65.31 to 68.15. On Wan2.1-T2V-14B, TaRoS with Qwen2.5-VL-72B reaches a total score of 82.09 versus 81.46 for the untrained baseline. On HunyuanVideo, TaRoS improves image quality from 64.61 to 68.83 and spatial relationship from 67.55 to 69.84.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DanceGRPO (the primary GRPO baseline), TaRoS consistently wins on total VBench score: 80.56 vs. 79.72 (Qwen72B reward) and 79.84 vs. 79.46 (VideoAlign reward) on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B. Notably, DanceGRPO degrades several metrics relative to the untrained Wan baseline (e.g., spatial relationship drops from 68.08 to 62.89 with VideoAlign), while TaRoS improves or maintains them, confirming that fixed reward aggregation induces shortcut exploitation. Against the untrained Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B, TaRoS with Qwen2.5-VL-72B gains +0.98 on total score, +0.51 on quality, and +2.84 on semantic score. On the larger Wan2.1-T2V-14B, TaRoS (Qwen72B) improves total score from 81.46 to 82.09 and spatial relationship from 74.97 to 79.06. On HunyuanVideo, TaRoS outperforms the baseline across all nine reported VBench dimensions, with the largest gains in image quality (+4.22) and multiple objects (+1.91), demonstrating cross-architecture generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DanceGRPO, which first applied GRPO-style group-relative preference optimization to video generation, and on the broader Flow-GRPO line of work that extends GRPO to flow-matching/diffusion models. It departs from prior multi-reward approaches (VRG, InstructVideo) that combine heterogeneous reward models via fixed weights or gradient surgery, by reframing the core problem through Goodhart's Law: the reward signal itself degrades as an optimization target under sustained GRPO training. The key conceptual departure is treating reward component informativeness (via within-group sparsity) as a first-class signal for adaptive reweighting, rather than relying on handcrafted stage scheduling or complex gradient manipulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Goodhart's Law diagnosis of GRPO reward failure provides a unifying lens for two previously treated separately as engineering issues (shortcut optimization and reward saturation), and the TaRoS framework offers a lightweight, plug-and-play mechanism that requires no additional value networks or gradient surgery. However, the method's reliance on a specific sparsity metric (Hoyer index) and per-component thresholds calibrated from training history raises questions about generalizability to very different reward landscapes or non-VLM evaluators. Open questions include whether the adaptive weighting interacts well with longer training horizons, whether the approach transfers to image generation or other modalities, and whether the modest but consistent VBench gains translate to perceptual quality at scale.
