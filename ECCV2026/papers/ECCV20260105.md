## Problem

Vision-Language Models generate hundreds to thousands of visual tokens per image, imposing heavy inference latency and memory costs. Existing parameter-free compression methods (token pruning or similarity-based merging) suffer pronounced performance degradation at high compression ratios because they discard informative content or distort the representation distribution, while parameterized alternatives introduce additional computational overhead. The paper asks whether frequency-domain redundancy in visual representations can be exploited for parameter-free compression that preserves semantic fidelity.

## Input / Output

The Fourier Compressor operates on the grid of visual features produced by a vision encoder (e.g., 24×24×1024 for LLaVA-v1.5's 576 tokens, or variable-resolution grids for Qwen-VL) and outputs a smaller spatial grid of compressed tokens (e.g., 16×16, 12×12, 8×8, or 6×6), which are then flattened, projected, and fed to the LLM alongside text. The compression ratio is controlled solely by the retained frequency band size C_h × C_w, requiring no learned parameters. For video, the same per-frame compression is applied zero-shot, reducing tokens from ~3193 to ~1328 per video.

## Method

The key insight is that visual representations in VLMs exhibit a non-uniform frequency distribution: low-frequency DCT coefficients capture global semantic structure and are robust to perturbations, while high-frequency coefficients encode fine-grained details and are highly sensitive. The method reshapes the encoder output into a 2D spatial grid, applies a 2D DCT along both spatial dimensions, retains only the top-left C_h × C_w low-frequency coefficients, and reconstructs a smaller spatial grid via 2D iDCT. The entire pipeline is implemented via FFT-based DCT/iDCT with O(N² log N) complexity and zero additional parameters. Because frequency truncation shifts the feature distribution, the model undergoes continued fine-tuning (projector + LLM for LLaVA; vision encoder + projector + LLM for Qwen) to adapt to the compressed representations.

## Results

Fourier-LLaVA retains 96% of the original LLaVA-v1.5-7B average accuracy (62.1 vs. 64.6) while compressing visual tokens from 576 to just 36 (93.75% reduction), reducing inference FLOPs by 83.8% and improving time-to-first-token by 31.2%. At 144 tokens (75% compression), Fourier-LLaVA matches the uncompressed base model's average score of 64.6 exactly. On Qwen2.5-VL-3B, the compressed model surpasses the vanilla baseline by 2.1% in average score (72.6 vs. 71.1) with a 57.3% token reduction, and on MVBench video tasks, Fourier-LLaVA retains 96.5% of base performance using only 12.5% of video tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fourier-LLaVA outperforms the parameterized MQT-LLaVA by 2.2% in average score at 144 tokens (64.6 vs. 62.8) and by 1.4% at 64 tokens (63.3 vs. 61.9), while also surpassing it on individual benchmarks like VQA v2 (77.7 vs. 76.4) and POPE (85.0 vs. 83.9). Against the parameter-free ATP-LLaVA, Fourier-LLaVA leads by 1.3% on VQA v2 and 2.4% on GQA at 144 tokens, and by 3.0% on VQA v2 at 88 tokens. Compared to LLaVA-PruMerge, Fourier-LLaVA achieves a 2.5% higher average at 36 tokens (62.1 vs. ~59.5 estimated from available columns) and leads on POPE by 7.7 points (84.0 vs. 76.3). On the Qwen-VL series, Fourier-Qwen2.5-VL-3B surpasses the vanilla baseline by 2.1% average and 7.9% on RealWorldQA, indicating that frequency-domain compression can even improve reasoning by suppressing noisy high-frequency components. On MVBench video tasks, Fourier-LLaVA (288 tokens) retains 96.5% of LLaVA-v1.5 (2304 tokens) performance while using only 12.5% of the visual tokens, outperforming Video-ChatGPT (32.7) and Video-LLaMA (34.1) with far fewer tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms in VLM visual token compression—heuristic token selection (FastV, ATP-LLaVA, SparseVLM) and similarity-based token merging (VisionZip, LLaVA-PruMerge)—by instead operating in the frequency domain. It repurposes the Discrete Cosine Transform, long established in traditional image compression (JPEG), to identify and remove redundant high-frequency components from visual token representations, drawing a direct analogy to how JPEG discards high-frequency DCT coefficients. The approach also connects to frequency-domain methods in NLP (FNet, Fourier-Transformer) and to DocPedia's use of DCT for document understanding, but is the first to apply 2D-DCT truncation as a parameter-free compression module on the output of a VLM vision encoder. Unlike parameterized alternatives (MQT-LLaVA, QueCC) that introduce learnable queries and additional transformer blocks, Fourier Compressor adds zero parameters and relies solely on FFT-accelerated DCT/iDCT operations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for VLM deployment: an 83.8% FLOPs reduction and 31.2% inference speedup with >96% accuracy retention, achieved without any architectural modification or additional parameters, makes this immediately applicable to resource-constrained settings. The zero-shot extension to video understanding (58.4% token reduction with only 3.1% average drop) suggests the frequency-domain redundancy structure is modality-agnostic, opening a path toward unified image-video compression. Open questions include whether the method can operate entirely without fine-tuning (current results require continued training to adapt to the altered feature distribution), whether other transforms (e.g., wavelets, learned bases) might further improve the compression-accuracy trade-off, and whether temporal frequency analysis could yield additional gains for video beyond per-frame spatial compression.
