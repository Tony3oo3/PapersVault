## Problem

Existing 3D generators operate in a one-shot fashion from text or image prompts, offering limited controllability for real-world workflows that require conditioning on 3D signals such as coarse sculpts, noisy photogrammetry scans, or block-out shapes. Prior work addresses specific regeneration sub-tasks (editing, enhancement, reconstruction) with task-specific architectures, but no unified framework handles the full spectrum of degraded or incomplete 3D inputs under a single model and training formulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262380/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a low-information 3D shape (coarse mesh, sparse point cloud from VGGT, or a mesh with a region masked out) encoded into C=512 VecSet tokens, plus an optional 2D guidance image encoded by DINOv2. The output is a complete, high-quality 3D surface decoded from K up to 2048 VecSet latents via SDF evaluation. The key asymmetry is that the conditioning shape uses far fewer tokens than the target (C ≪ K), exploiting VecSet's variable token count to reduce self-attention cost for simpler geometries.

## Method

MeshReGen conditions a pre-trained VecSet-based Diffusion Transformer on the input 3D shape by concatenating its encoded tokens (passed through a zero-initialized MLP plus positional embeddings, à la ControlNet) with the noisy target tokens, so both are processed by the same self-attention mechanism rather than a separate cross-attention branch. Only the target tokens are denoised while the conditioning tokens remain fixed throughout the diffusion process. Training data is constructed self-supervisedly from a ~1M-object base dataset: enhancement pairs are created by SDEdit-style noise-and-denoise on compositional grids, reconstruction pairs by running VGGT on rendered views to produce sparse point clouds, and editing pairs by removing points within a 3D mask and replacing them with learnable mask tokens. This avoids any task-specific annotations or paired datasets, and the same architecture and base weights serve all three applications with only the augmentation pipeline changing.

## Results

On the GSO reconstruction benchmark, VGGT+MeshReGen with 4 input views achieves CD=0.0081, F-score=0.4913, IoU=0.7574, PSNR=24.28, SSIM=0.9408, and LPIPS=0.0873, outperforming all multi-view baselines (AssetGen, MeshLRM, TRELLIS-multi) on every geometric metric. With only 2 views, MeshReGen already matches or exceeds 4-view baselines on IoU (0.7053 vs. 0.6703 for AssetGen) and LPIPS (0.0985 vs. 0.1024). For compositional 3D enhancement, MeshReGen improves ULIP-3D shape-view alignment from 0.2280 to 0.2294 and reduces MV-ImageReward from 0.1716 to 0.1003 over the DetailGen3D baseline on 623 decomposed objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against multi-view baselines on GSO, VGGT+MeshReGen (4 views) dominates: it beats AssetGen (4 views) by 27% in CD (0.0081 vs. 0.0111), 5.4% in F-score (0.4913 vs. 0.4663), and 13% in IoU (0.7574 vs. 0.6703), and similarly outperforms MeshLRM (4 views) on all geometric metrics. Notably, with only 2 views, MeshReGen already surpasses 4-view AssetGen on IoU (0.7053 vs. 0.6703) and LPIPS (0.0985 vs. 0.1024), demonstrating that the regeneration prior compensates for fewer input views. At 1 view, TRELLIS slightly edges out MeshReGen on CD (0.0122 vs. 0.0135) and F-score (0.4216 vs. 0.3658), but MeshReGen recovers the lead from 2 views onward. For 3D enhancement, MeshReGen improves over DetailGen3D on both ULIP-3D alignment and MV-ImageReward perceptual quality. In 3D shape editing (Edit3D-Bench), MeshReGen qualitatively outperforms VoxHammer in preserving source identity while capturing fine-grained structural changes, without requiring expensive inversion or post-optimization. For blockout-conditioned generation, MeshReGen produces finer geometric details than CLAY while preserving the blockout's proportions, despite not being explicitly trained on blockout data.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshReGen builds directly on the VecSet latent representation (3DShape2VecSet) and its Diffusion Transformer (DiT) conditioning pipeline, extending the standard image-conditioned generation setup to also accept a 3D shape condition via token concatenation in the same latent space. Conceptually, it departs from prior controllable 3D generators (CLAY, Hunyuan3D-Omni) that inject 3D controls through separate cross-attention modules, and from task-specific enhancement/editing methods (DetailGen3D, VoxHammer) that each require dedicated architectures. The paper's key architectural departure is showing that VecSet-level concatenation of conditioning and noisy tokens, with a zero-initialized MLP and positional embeddings, outperforms these cross-attention alternatives, while the self-supervised data-augmentation pipeline (SDEdit-style degradation, VGGT-based sparse reconstruction, masked point removal) eliminates the need for expensive paired 3D annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying 3D enhancement, reconstruction, and editing under a single regeneration formulation trained on automatically constructed triplets, MeshReGen offers a practical path toward general-purpose 3D asset pipelines that production teams (e.g., game studios, AR/VR) can deploy without task-specific model swaps. The negative result that existing generation frameworks (TRELLIS, CLAY) fail to generalize to degraded or incomplete inputs without fine-tuning is an important caution for the field. Limitations include reliance on a proprietary ~1M-object dataset, the VecSet representation (which the authors themselves note may be suboptimal compared to SLAT on active voxels), and the absence of dynamics or rigged-asset support. Open questions include whether the self-supervised degradation strategy transfers to truly out-of-distribution corruption types and whether the framework scales to scene-level or 4D generation.
