## Problem

Text-guided image editing suffers from an edit-preservation trade-off where stronger edits degrade identity and background fidelity, a phenomenon widely regarded as an inherent limitation of generative editing. This paper identifies trajectory instability during flow integration as a previously overlooked causal factor: unstable step-to-step update directions disrupt source semantics without meaningfully improving edit alignment, reframing the trade-off as a solvable dynamical problem rather than a fundamental constraint.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262382/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image and a target text prompt, and produces a single edited image at the same resolution (1024×1024 in FlowEdit-Data). It operates as a training-free plug-in on top of existing inversion-free flow editing pipelines (FlowEdit-style), requiring no retraining of the underlying generative backbone (SD3-medium or FLUX.1-dev). The only additional hyperparameters are the number of candidate directions K and the holding length H, with defaults K=3 and H=4.

## Method

V-HOLD stabilizes the editing trajectory by replacing per-step velocity recomputation with periodic candidate selection and directional holding. At anchor steps spaced H apart, it samples K stochastic perturbations to generate K candidate editing directions (differences between target- and source-conditioned velocity fields), then selects the candidate with the highest cosine similarity to a running reference direction. The selected direction is held unchanged for the next H integration steps, after which a new candidate is chosen and the reference is updated. This mechanism suppresses the accumulation of stochastic estimation noise in the update direction, reducing both directional instability and magnitude volatility. As a side benefit, reusing the held direction eliminates redundant velocity-field evaluations, cutting NFE by up to 25% and FLOPs by up to 74% compared to FlowEdit.

## Results

On PIE-Bench and FlowEdit-Data across FLUX.1-dev and SD3-medium backbones, V-HOLD achieves the lowest DINO Dist_src (semantic drift) in all four settings, e.g., 0.7862 vs. FlowEdit's 0.8004 on FLUX.1-dev/PIE-Bench and 0.7547 vs. 0.8831 on SD3-medium/FlowEdit-Data, while maintaining competitive CLIP_tgt alignment (0.2623 vs. 0.2583 and 0.2975 vs. 0.2985 respectively). V-HOLD also reduces trajectory instability by 74–86% and update-magnitude volatility by 29–58% relative to FlowEdit, and cuts NFE from 24–33 to 21–27, yielding the fastest wall-clock editing time (6.94 s on FLUX.1-dev, 2.18 s on SD3-medium).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest inversion-free baseline FlowEdit, V-HOLD consistently improves DINO Dist_src (preservation) in all four backbone–dataset settings (e.g., 0.7862 vs. 0.8004 on FLUX/PIE-Bench; 0.7547 vs. 0.8831 on SD3/FlowEdit-Data) while matching or slightly exceeding CLIP_tgt alignment; however, FlowEdit retains a small LPIPS advantage on FLUX.1-dev (0.2712 vs. 0.2874). Compared to FlowAlign, V-HOLD wins on DINO in both settings (0.8461 vs. 0.8640 on PIE-Bench; 0.7547 vs. 0.9389 on FlowEdit-Data) and on CLIP for SD3-medium, though FlowAlign edges ahead on CLIP for FLUX.1-dev (0.2482 vs. 0.2623 is actually V-HOLD winning). Against inversion-based methods (DDIB, SDEdit, iRFDS, RFEdit, RF-Inversion, FireFlow), V-HOLD achieves the best or near-best DINO in every setting, substantially outperforming them on semantic drift (e.g., 0.8461 vs. 1.1331 for DDIB on SD3/PIE-Bench), while CLIP_tgt scores are broadly comparable. In MLLM-based pairwise preference (Table 5), V-HOLD is preferred over FlowEdit on preservation (82–86% win-rate) and overall quality (66–79%) by both Qwen3-VL-8B and InternVL3.5-8B, though FlowEdit retains a slight edge on edit alignment in some settings (e.g., 61.6% vs. 38.4% on FlowEdit-Data with Qwen3-VL). V-HOLD also reduces NFE by 13–18% and FLOPs by up to 74% relative to FlowEdit, making it the most efficient method in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

V-HOLD builds directly on FlowEdit's inversion-free editing framework, where the editing direction is computed as the difference between target- and source-conditioned velocity fields and re-estimated at every integration step. Prior work on flow-based editing (RFEdit, FireFlow, DPM-Solver variants) has focused on improving numerical solver accuracy or inversion fidelity, while the paper's conceptual departure is to treat the *stability of the editing trajectory itself*—rather than solver precision—as the key lever for preservation. The method is positioned as a training-free plug-in that operates on top of existing inversion-free pipelines, requiring no retraining of the underlying generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes the widely accepted edit-preservation trade-off as partly attributable to a previously overlooked factor (trajectory instability), offering a new diagnostic lens for generative editing systems. Practically, V-HOLD reduces velocity-field evaluations (NFE) by 12–20% while improving preservation, making it an attractive drop-in for existing editing pipelines. However, the quality gains in DINO Dist are modest (e.g., ~0.01–0.14 absolute), and the authors acknowledge that different editing types (local attribute changes vs. large semantic transformations) may require different stabilization levels, leaving adaptive, edit-type-aware trajectory control as an open direction.
