## Problem

Contrastive vision-language pretraining (CLIP, SigLIP) optimizes discriminative image-text alignment, which is misaligned with the autoregressive next-token prediction objective used by downstream MLLMs. Existing generative alternatives (CapPa, AIMv2, OpenVision2) address this by cascading a vision encoder with a separate text decoder, but this indirect optimization and redundant architecture limit efficiency and scalability for training a dedicated vision encoder. The paper asks whether a single transformer trained directly with a language modeling objective can produce vision features that are both strong and naturally compatible with LLMs.

## Input / Output

During pretraining, each RGB image is split into non-overlapping patches (196 patches at 224×224, or 16–1024 patches at native aspect ratios in stage 2) and concatenated with its caption's subword tokens (Qwen3 tokenizer) into a single sequence [v₀,…,v_M, t₀,…,t_L]. When deployed as a vision encoder in an MLLM, the model takes only the RGB image, discards the tokenizer and LM head, and outputs visual feature tokens from the last LayerNorm layer, which are projected into the LLM's input space via a 2-layer MLP; the prefix-LM attention reduces to standard full attention in this mode.

## Method

GenLIP uses a single unified Transformer with two key modifications: (i) multimodal rotary position encoding (MRoPE) replaces absolute position embeddings to handle the interleaved visual-textual sequence, and (ii) prefix-LM attention gives image tokens bidirectional attention while text tokens attend causally, so the model learns P(T|I) via a standard autoregressive LM loss applied only to text tokens. A per-head gated attention mechanism (sigmoid gate on attention outputs) is introduced to prevent the attention-sink phenomenon where visual information collapses onto a few tokens, which otherwise degrades spatial diversity and causes training instability. Pretraining proceeds in two stages: 8B samples at fixed 224×224 resolution on Recap-DataComp-1B, followed by 39M samples at native aspect ratios (16–1024 tokens) on long-caption datasets for fine-grained detail. The entire pipeline requires no contrastive batch construction, no additional text encoder or decoder, and no auxiliary objectives—just one transformer and one language modeling loss.

## Results

Under frozen visual representation evaluation with LLaVA-NeXT-Qwen2.5-1.5B, GenLIP achieves ALL AVG scores of 61.5 (L/16), 62.6 (So/16), and 65.2 (g/16), outperforming SigLIP2 (pretrained on 5× more data) by 2.8, 2.0, and 3.7 points respectively. The advantage is most pronounced on Doc&OCR tasks, where GenLIP leads SigLIP2 by 4.3, 3.3, and 5.9 points at the three scales. Scaling the LLM to Qwen2.5-7B widens the gap further: GenLIP g/16 reaches 73.6 ALL AVG vs. SigLIP2 g/16's 68.9 (+4.7). GenLIP achieves these results using only 8.0B pretraining samples, compared to 12.0B–40.0B for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenLIP consistently outperforms all five primary baselines (CLIP, AIMv2, OVision2, SigLIP, SigLIP2) on ALL AVG across all three model scales, despite using only 8.0B pretraining samples versus 12.0B–40.0B for the baselines. Against the strongest baseline SigLIP2, GenLIP leads by 2.0–3.7 points on ALL AVG (Qwen2.5-1.5B) and by 2.4–4.7 points (Qwen2.5-7B), with the largest margins on Doc&OCR tasks (up to +5.9 at g/16). Against contrastive CLIP, GenLIP g/16 improves ALL AVG by 12.1 points (65.2 vs. 53.1). Against encoder-decoder generative methods AIMv2 and OVision2, GenLIP L/16 already surpasses them by 5.8 and 2.8 points respectively, and the gap widens at larger scales. In the standard LLaVA-NeXT setting (Table 4), GenLIP So/16 achieves 70.3 ALL AVG at 729 patches, outperforming RICE-ViT (68.6) and SigLIP2 (67.5) while using 5× less pretraining data. In the controlled same-data ablation (Table 6, 2.0B samples), GenLIP So/16 leads SigLIP by 2.8 and OVision2 by 1.3 on ALL AVG, confirming superior data efficiency of the generative single-transformer design.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenLIP departs from both dominant VLP paradigms: contrastive dual-encoder methods (CLIP, SigLIP, SigLIP2) that align modalities in a shared embedding space, and encoder-decoder generative methods (CapPa, AIMv2, OpenVision2) that cascade a text decoder on top of the vision encoder. Its core idea is to collapse the architecture to a single transformer trained with a standard autoregressive language modeling objective, letting the ViT predict text tokens directly from visual tokens without any contrastive loss or auxiliary text module. The two key technical modifications—prefix-LM attention (bidirectional for image tokens, causal for text tokens) and gated attention to mitigate attention sinks—are borrowed from existing LLM and ViT literature rather than being novel architectural contributions. The paper also distinguishes itself from single-transformer native MLLM work (SAIL, NEO) by targeting modular MLLM vision encoder pretraining from scratch rather than building an end-to-end multimodal model on top of a pretrained LLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that GenLIP offers a simpler, more data-efficient recipe for training vision encoders for MLLMs: a single transformer with one objective, no contrastive batch construction, and no text decoder, yet matching or exceeding SigLIP2 (pretrained on 5× more data) across diverse benchmarks. The native-aspect-ratio second-stage adaptation is particularly valuable for detail-sensitive tasks like OCR and chart understanding, which are critical for real-world MLLM deployment. Key limitations include validation only on academic-scale MLLMs (LLaVA-NeXT with Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B), pretraining capped at 1B unique image-text pairs, and dependence on high-quality synthetic captions. Open questions include whether the data-efficiency advantage persists at 10B+ sample scales, whether the approach generalizes to frontier MLLMs, and whether the gated attention mechanism transfers to other mixed-modality settings.
