## Problem

Existing inverse-rendering and reflection-aware Gaussian-splatting methods assume dense near-360° coverage and rely on volumetric splats that lack explicit surfaces, producing inconsistent normals and unreliable reflection directions. This makes them unable to recover coherent environment illumination from the partial, single-side reflective observations that are typical in practice (e.g., one side of a glass façade or polished object). PanoLess addresses this by formulating partial-view environment reconstruction as a well-posed problem with explicit handling of unobserved directions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260470/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of RGB images captured from a single hemisphere around a reflective surface, along with camera poses (from COLMAP) and a binary object mask. It produces a distant-illumination environment map stored as a 6×3×L×L trainable RGB cubemap (direct bilinear lookup, no intermediate MLP), a per-texel visibility map on the same cubemap grid indicating which directions are supported by observed reflections, surface normals, and novel-view renders. The far-field assumption means the environment is a function on S² independent of distance, valid when the reflector is small relative to its surroundings.

## Method

PanoLess represents the reflective surface with surface-aligned 2D Gaussian surfels (each carrying two tangent vectors that yield an explicit, geometrically consistent normal) and rasterizes them into screen-space albedo and geometry buffers. A deferred shading pass computes per-pixel reflection directions from the rasterized normals and view rays, then queries a learnable neural cubemap via direct bilinear lookup with sigmoid activation—no roughness-dependent attenuation or intermediate MLP—so the photometric loss provides an unattenuated gradient path to every cubemap texel. A visibility map is accumulated online during training by scattering opacity-weighted increments at each reflection direction, explicitly flagging which environment regions lack observational support. Training jointly optimizes all Gaussian parameters and the cubemap with a combined L1 + D-SSIM photometric loss, a depth-normal consistency regularizer, and an early-stage binary cross-entropy silhouette loss (first 3000 iterations) that anchors the geometry when multi-view consistency is unavailable from a single hemisphere.

## Results

On the Shiny Partial benchmark, PanoLess achieves an average environment-map PSNR of 13.69 dB, a gain of nearly 5 dB over the next-best baseline (MaterialRefGS at 8.89 dB), with SSIM of 0.471 and L1 of 0.195. For surface-normal estimation, PanoLess attains a mean angular error of 4.95° and places 89.36% of pixels within 5° of ground truth, compared to 11.64° MAE and 17.31% within 5° for the strongest baseline. The improvement is most pronounced on the planar Mirror scene (+4.8 dB PSNR, 97% of normals within 5°), where volumetric baselines fail to maintain globally consistent normal orientation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PanoLess outperforms all four baselines on every scene and every reported metric (PSNR, SSIM, L1, LPIPS, and normal angular error). Against the strongest baseline MaterialRefGS (NeurIPS 2025), PanoLess gains +4.80 dB average PSNR (13.69 vs 8.89) and reduces normal MAE from 11.64° to 4.95°, with 89.36% of pixels within 5° versus 17.31%. The gap is largest on the planar Mirror scene, where PanoLess achieves 13.31 dB PSNR and 97% of normals within 5°, while all baselines score below 8.6 dB and 0% within 5°. A controlled fairness experiment (retraining MaterialRefGS with PanoLess's silhouette loss) confirms the advantage stems from the surface-aligned 2D Gaussian representation rather than the auxiliary supervision: even with matched silhouette loss, MaterialRefGS still trails by 1.6–10.2 dB PSNR and 0% vs 97% within 5° on Mirror. Volumetric baselines (3DGS-DR, GShader) perform worst overall, with GShader producing near-random normals (38° MAE) due to its shortest-axis heuristic.

## Relation to Prior Work

PanoLess builds on 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) for surface-aligned geometry with explicit normals, departing from the volumetric 3DGS lineage (3DGS-DR, GaussianShader, Ref-Gaussian, MaterialRefGS) that produces ambiguous normals via shortest-axis heuristics. For environment lighting, it replaces the spherical-harmonic or MLP-decoded mip-mapped cubemaps used in prior reflection-aware splatting with a direct, single-level neural cubemap lookup, arguing that roughness-dependent attenuation actively starves the cubemap of gradient under sparse supervision. The paper also introduces a visibility map tracking per-direction observation coverage, a signal absent from all prior reflection-aware Gaussian-splatting or inverse-rendering methods, and formulates the problem under a single-hemisphere capture constraint that prior benchmarks (Ref-NeRF, Shiny Blender) do not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work opens a practical pathway for environment-aware perception in robotics and AR/VR where a full panoramic sweep is infeasible, and the visibility map gives downstream systems a principled confidence signal for gating relighting, inpainting, or scene insertion. Key limitations constrain broader impact: the method requires near-mirror reflectivity (matte surfaces yield zero cubemap gradient), assumes distant illumination (far-field), and is highly sensitive to camera-pose accuracy (PSNR drops ~6 dB at 0.5° rotation jitter). Open questions include whether the direct-cubemap pathway can be made robust to mixed diffuse-specular materials, whether the visibility map can drive learned inpainting of unobserved directions, and whether the approach extends to dynamic or multi-reflector scenes.
