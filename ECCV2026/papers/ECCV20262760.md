## Problem

Existing low-bit PEFT methods such as QLoRA and QA-LoRA cannot fine-tune ternary (1.58-bit) transformer backbones while keeping the merged model strictly in {−1, 0, 1}: QLoRA requires dequantizing to full precision before merging, and QA-LoRA only updates quantization parameters, neither yielding a deployable ternary model. This paper asks how to perform parameter-efficient fine-tuning that operates natively in the ternary domain and produces a merged model that remains ternary without any dequantization or requantization step.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained ternary backbone (e.g., SpinQuant-quantized Llama-3.2 1B/3B, BitNet b1.58 2B4T, Falcon-E, or a ternary ViT-B/16 from TernaryCLIP) and a supervised training set (Alpaca, GSM8K, or ImageNet-100). During fine-tuning, only the small Kronecker-factor proxies are updated while the ternary base weights stay frozen; at inference the output is a single merged ternary weight matrix of identical shape and precision to the original, producing text generations or class labels with zero additional FLOPs or memory overhead compared to the unadapted ternary model.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces LoRA's additive low-rank update with an element-wise multiplicative (Hadamard) update: a ternary mask Δ_tern ∈ {−1,0,1}^{d_out×d_in} is applied to the frozen ternary weights W_tern so that each entry is kept, zeroed, or sign-flipped, guaranteeing closure over {−1,0,1}. To keep the mask compact yet expressive, Δ_tern is factorized as the Kronecker product A⊗B of two smaller ternary matrices whose dimensions satisfy p·r = d_out and q·s = d_in, giving rank(A⊗B) = rank(A)·rank(B) while using only p·q + r·s trainable parameters (2d for square layers of size d, versus d² for full fine-tuning). Training optimizes real-valued proxies of A and B with a straight-through estimator that projects them to {−1,0,1} in the forward pass using per-matrix absolute-mean thresholds. After fine-tuning, the adaptation is merged by a single element-wise multiplication W_merged = W_tern ⊙ (A⊗B), discarding the proxies and yielding a deployable model that is bit-for-bit ternary with no inference-time adapter overhead.

## Results

On ternarized Llama-3.2-1B, the proposed 1.58-bit adapter achieves an average accuracy of 35.3 across nine benchmarks and a WikiText-2 PPL of 44.6, improving over the ternary SpinQuant backbone (33.2 avg, 86.5 PPL) and surpassing the 2-bit SpinQuant baseline (34.7 avg, 43.3 PPL) on seven of nine tasks. On Llama-3.2-3B, the method reaches 38.3 average accuracy and 22.3 PPL, outperforming the 2-bit SpinQuant baseline (32.6 avg, 41.7 PPL) on every benchmark. For the ternary ViT-B/16 on ImageNet-100, the merged 1.58-bit model achieves 83.0% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming requantized QLoRA (78.9%) and narrowing the gap to full ternary fine-tuning (85.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SpinQuant (2-bit), the proposed 1.58-bit adapter outperforms on 7/9 benchmarks for Llama-3.2-1B (e.g., ARC-e 39.7 vs 37.6, PiQA 58.2 vs 57.4) and on all 9 benchmarks for Llama-3.2-3B (e.g., ARC-c 23.8 vs 19.3, MMLU 24.8 vs 22.9), while also halving the PPL (22.3 vs 41.7 on 3B). Against SpinQuant (1.58-bit ternary backbone), the method improves average accuracy from 33.2→35.3 (1B) and 31.9→38.3 (3B), and reduces PPL from 86.5→44.6 (1B) and 45.6→22.3 (3B). Against GPTQ (2-bit), the adapter wins on most tasks for both model sizes, with particularly large gaps on ARC-e (39.7 vs 31.6 on 1B) and BoolQ (61.2 vs 43.1 on 1B). Against QLoRA requantized to 1.58-bit (Table 6), the method achieves 38.3 avg vs 37.5 and 22.3 PPL vs 22.9 on Llama-3.2-3B, confirming that native ternary merging avoids the accuracy loss from dequantize-requantize. On the ternary ViT-B/16 (Table 8), the merged 1.58-bit model (83.0% Top-1) outperforms requantized QLoRA (78.9%) by 4.1 points while remaining within 2.7 points of full ternary fine-tuning (85.7%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA family of parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods by replacing the standard additive low-rank update with a multiplicative, Kronecker-structured ternary mask applied via Hadamard product. It departs from QLoRA and QA-LoRA, which both operate in continuous floating-point space and require dequantization or requantization to merge, by constraining the entire adaptation to the discrete {−1, 0, 1} domain. The Kronecker decomposition draws on prior work (LoKr, KAdaptation, KronA) for compact high-rank updates, but is the first to apply it to discrete ternary sign-flip/zero operations rather than continuous weight shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work enables the first practical pathway to fine-tune 1.58-bit ternary LLMs and ViTs while preserving the memory and compute advantages of ternary quantization at inference, which is critical for edge and low-end hardware deployment. A key open limitation is the zero-locking property: pruned weights can never be reactivated, so the method can only redistribute signs among existing non-zero connections, which may cap recoverable accuracy for heavily pruned layers. Whether the Kronecker factorization generalizes to other discrete weight spaces (binary, 4-level) or to poorly factorable layer shapes remains an open question for future work.
