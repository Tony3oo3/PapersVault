## Problem

Existing vision encoders (CLIP, DINOv2, SigLIP2) systematically entangle object identity with background context, causing them to score a visually similar but distinct object on the same background higher than the true identity on a different background. This background conflation inflates the CLIP-I and DINO scores that the majority of personalized generation and image editing literature relies on for automated identity preservation evaluation, making those metrics unreliable.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (standardized to 512×512) and produces a 1152-dimensional ℓ₂-normalized embedding vector from which cosine similarity scores are computed. Training consumes structured tuples of an anchor image, up to P positive views of the same identity on different backgrounds, and K NearID distractors (distinct objects inpainted into the anchor's exact background). Evaluation uses a matched-context margin protocol: for each identity, cross-background positive similarity must exceed similarity to a same-background NearID distractor, aggregated into Sample Success Rate (SSR) and Pairwise Accuracy (PA).

## Method

NearID freezes a SigLIP2-so400m-patch14-384 backbone and trains only a lightweight Multi-head Attention Pooling (MAP) projection head (~15M of ~428M parameters, ~3.6%) to reshape the embedding geometry for identity. The training objective is a two-tier contrastive loss: a discrimination term (softmax cross-entropy over the global positive pool with NearID distractors explicitly added to the denominator) and a ranking regularizer (softplus penalty ensuring each NearID distractor logit exceeds the log-sum-exp of batch negatives), enforcing the strict hierarchy same identity > NearID distractor > random batch negative. The ranking term prevents collapse of the local semantic neighborhood that aggressive margin-based losses cause, while the discrimination term provides targeted identity-confusing competition absent from standard batch negatives. Training uses role-aware stochastic foreground masking (different black-out probabilities for anchors, positives, and distractors) and is jointly trained on the NearID dataset (19K identities, 316K+ distractors from four generative models) interleaved with Mind-the-Glitch part-level edits for continuous severity calibration.

## Results

Under the matched-context NearID evaluation protocol, the proposed method achieves 99.17% Sample Success Rate (SSR) and 99.71% Pairwise Accuracy (PA), compared to 30.74% SSR for the frozen SigLIP2 backbone and 10.31% for CLIP. On the part-level Mind the Glitch (MTG) benchmark, all standard encoders (CLIP, DINOv2, SigLIP2) score 0.0% SSR, while NearID reaches 35.0% SSR with a metric-to-oracle Pearson correlation of 0.465 (vs. 0.180 for frozen SigLIP2). On DreamBench++, the method improves metric-to-human alignment (M-H) from 0.516 to 0.545, with gains generalizing to animals (+0.105) and humans (+0.065) despite training exclusively on rigid objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NearID dominates all five baselines across every reported metric. Against the frozen SigLIP2 backbone (the method's own encoder before adaptation), it improves NearID SSR from 30.74% to 99.17% and MTG SSR from 0.0% to 35.0%, while also raising the metric-to-human correlation on DreamBench++ from 0.516 to 0.545. Compared to CLIP (SSR 10.31%) and DINOv2 (SSR 20.43%), the gains are even more dramatic, with both scoring 0.0% on MTG part-level discrimination. The dedicated VSM metric, trained directly on MTG, reaches only 7.0% MTG SSR and 0.190 M-H, far below NearID's 35.0% and 0.545 respectively. The largest VLM baseline, Qwen3-VL 30B, achieves 49.73% NearID SSR and 17.0% MTG SSR—still roughly half of NearID's performance on both benchmarks—confirming that even large vision-language models remain vulnerable to matched-context identity confusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SynCD multi-view synthesis pipeline (Kumari et al., ICCV 2025) by adding near-identity distractors as a structured negative signal absent from prior contrastive paradigms, and builds on the two-tier contrastive learning literature (InfoNCE, SupContrast, Circle Loss) by introducing an explicit three-tier similarity hierarchy enforced through a discrimination term plus a softplus ranking regularizer. It departs from domain-specific identity encoders (ArcFace, AdaFace) by targeting open-domain rigid objects, and from region-focused approaches like AlphaCLIP by requiring no masks at inference. The parameter-efficient adaptation strategy—freezing SigLIP2 and training only a MAP projection head (~3.6% of parameters)—follows the Perceiver/ViT-scaling lineage of lightweight pooling heads on frozen backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical vulnerability in the evaluation infrastructure of the personalization and image-editing community: the CLIP-I and DINO scores that most prior work relies on for automated identity preservation are shown to be fundamentally unreliable under matched-context conditions, with all standard encoders scoring 0% SSR on part-level edits. The released NearID dataset (19K identities, 316K+ distractors) and evaluation protocol provide a diagnostic tool for any vision encoder, and the lightweight training recipe is immediately adoptable. Limitations include scope restricted to rigid objects (no faces, no style), distractor realism bounded by inpainting model fidelity, and the absence of live subjects from training data, leaving open whether the approach transfers to identity-critical domains like face recognition or industrial inspection.
