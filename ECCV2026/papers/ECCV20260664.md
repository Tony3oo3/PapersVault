## Problem

Existing continual learning paradigms (CIL, DIL, OCL) assume either class-only or domain-only shifts with discrete task boundaries, failing to capture the continuous, concurrent, and unpredictable evolution of both classes and domains that characterizes real-world deployment. This paper introduces Online Versatile Incremental Learning (Online VIL), a scenario where class and domain distributions shift simultaneously and smoothly without explicit boundaries, and proposes TopFlow to address the resulting challenges of distinguishing class-discriminative from domain-specific knowledge under single-pass, memory-constrained streaming.

## Input / Output

The method consumes small streaming image batches (batch size 64) in a single pass with no access to prior inputs, processing them through a frozen pre-trained ViT backbone where only prompt parameters are updated. At each time step, the model observes only a locally limited window of the joint domain-class space, with non-stationary class-domain compositions that shift continuously. The output is a class label prediction, evaluated via anytime inference (A_AUC) and final accuracy (A_Last) metrics that account for the absence of clear task boundaries.

## Method

TopFlow combines two complementary mechanisms built on a frozen ViT with prompt-based adaptation. Domain-agnostic Flow Matching (DFM) exploits the observation that early ViT layers encode domain-specific patterns while deeper layers capture class semantics; it reformulates the Geodesic Flow Kernel into a contrastive loss that extracts a common subspace U via SVD of stacked intermediate and final-layer features, then pulls prompt-perturbed intermediate representations toward final-layer semantic anchors while pushing them away from domain-biased intermediate features, all normalized by the shared geometry U. Global Topology Preservation (GTP) maintains the global feature-space structure without storing past examples by maintaining k global prototypes (updated via EMA with Hungarian-algorithm matching to batch prototypes from FINCH clustering) and a learnable MLP that maps prototype pairs to relationship vectors; the GTP loss aligns batch-level pairwise relationships with their global counterparts via cosine distance, preventing feature-space contraction around currently observed classes.

## Results

In the non-replay Online VIL setting, TopFlow achieves 64.51 AAUC and 66.20 ALast on CORe50, 87.12 AAUC and 80.64 ALast on CLEAR100, and 48.52 AAUC and 32.18 ALast on iDigits, consistently outperforming all 17 baselines. The most notable gain is on CORe50 ALast, where TopFlow (66.20) surpasses the previous best MVP (52.84) by 13.4 points. With a replay buffer of 2000, TopFlow reaches 92.24 ALast on CORe50 and 92.97 ALast on CLEAR100, approaching the upper-bound joint fine-tuning accuracy of 91.66 and 94.36 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopFlow outperforms all 17 baselines on every dataset and metric in the non-replay Online VIL setting. The largest margin is on CORe50 ALast, where TopFlow (66.20) exceeds the strongest baseline MVP (52.84) by 13.4 points, and on CORe50 AAUC by 6.2 points (64.51 vs. 58.30). On CLEAR100, TopFlow leads MVP by 7.4 AAUC points (87.12 vs. 79.73) and 3.5 ALast points (80.64 vs. 77.11). In the replay-buffer setting (Table 2), TopFlow again dominates, reaching 92.24 ALast on CORe50 and 92.97 ALast on CLEAR100 with a 2000-sample buffer, while a surprising finding is that simple methods like ER (83.51 AAUC on CLEAR100) outperform more complex online methods such as DYSON (57.05) and OnPro (88.83), suggesting that existing online-incremental methods struggle under the heterogeneous, boundary-free shifts of Online VIL. The VIL-specific baseline ICON, designed for offline versatile incremental learning, achieves only 59.38 AAUC on CLEAR100 in the non-replay setting, well below TopFlow's 87.12, confirming that offline VIL methods do not transfer to the online streaming scenario.

## Relation to Prior Work

TopFlow builds directly on MVP (Moon et al., ICCV 2023) for its ViT-plus-prompt architecture and blurry-boundary task construction, and reformulates the Geodesic Flow Kernel (Gong et al., CVPR 2012) into an online, layer-wise contrastive objective (DFM) that operates without full source/target domain access. Conceptually, it departs from VIL (ICON, Park et al. 2024), which assumes discrete task increments with implicit structural cues, by removing all task boundaries and allowing continuous, simultaneous class and domain evolution. The GTP component extends prior topology-preservation methods (elastic Hebbian graphs, self-organizing maps) from offline settings requiring full past-data access to an online regime using EMA-updated prototypes and a learnable relationship mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Online VIL provides a more faithful stress test for continual learning systems than existing CIL/DIL/VIL benchmarks, and the finding that naive experience replay outperforms sophisticated online methods (DYSON, OnPro) in replay-buffer settings suggests the field may be over-engineering solutions for scenarios that don't reflect real deployment. The layer-wise ViT analysis (early layers encode domain, late layers encode class) is a reusable empirical insight for any prompt-based CL pipeline. Open questions include scalability to larger datasets (ImageNet-scale), generalization beyond ViT backbones, and whether the stochastic task construction adequately captures the multi-factor interactions of truly uncontrolled environments.
