## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP are optimized for image-level contrastive alignment, causing final-layer patch tokens to encode excessive global context that undermines the spatial localization needed for pixel-level segmentation. Existing training-free OVSS methods (SCLIP, ClearCLIP) recalibrate attention patterns but do not explicitly remove the global bias embedded in the feature representations themselves, leaving residual artifacts that degrade fine-grained segmentation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262415/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images resized so the shorter side is 448 px (560 px for Cityscapes) and a set of text prompts encoding class labels. Images are processed with a 448×448 sliding window at stride 224 (224×224 at stride 112 for Cityscapes), and no post-processing is applied to the output. The output is a pixel-level segmentation mask produced by assigning each patch to the class with the highest cosine similarity between its refined visual token and the corresponding text embedding.

## Method

CURE is a training-free, three-stage post-hoc refinement applied to penultimate-layer ViT patch tokens. First, the Reject-and-Reconstruct (RR) module discards tokens whose embedding norm exceeds a threshold of 40 (identified as globally biased outliers) and replaces each with the mean of its spatial neighbors within Chebyshev distance 2. Second, Global Contextual Debiasing (GCD) estimates a shared bias direction by averaging the remaining non-outlier tokens and subtracts α·bias from every token (α = 0.25 for ViT-B/16, 0.4 for ViT-L/14), removing the implicit global component that RR alone cannot address. Third, Spatial Correlation Refinement (SCR) builds an N×N affinity matrix from mid-level (3rd or 6th Transformer block) feature and value embeddings and uses it to re-weight the debiased final-layer tokens, restoring spatial coherence without altering the pre-trained embedding space. The key insight is modeling each patch token as a mixture of localized semantics and a shared global artifact, then decomposing and removing both the explicit (outlier) and implicit (common-direction) forms of that artifact.

## Results

CURE achieves an average mIoU of 40.1% with ViT-B/16 and 39.6% with ViT-L/14 across five benchmarks (VOC20, PC59, ADE20K, Cityscapes, COCO-Stuff). As a plug-and-play module, CURE yields average mIoU gains of +2.8% (ViT-B/16) and +7.7% (ViT-L/14) when integrated into six existing training-free baselines. Most notably, CURE narrows the ViT-L/14 vs. ViT-B/16 gap: SCLIP drops from 35.8% to 24.4% on the larger backbone, but CURE lifts it to 40.1%, surpassing its ViT-B/16 counterpart. The method adds negligible overhead (35.0 GFLOPs vs. 34.9 for ClearCLIP; 32.3 IPS vs. 35.3 IPS on RTX 3090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ViT-B/16, CURE (40.1% avg mIoU) matches ResCLIP (40.1%) and trails SCCLIP (42.4%), SFP (40.9%), and LPOSS (40.3%) by 1.2–2.3 points, while clearly surpassing ClearCLIP (37.9%, +2.2%) and SCLIP (35.8%, +4.3%). On ViT-L/14, CURE (39.6%) outperforms ClearCLIP (35.3%, +4.3%), ResCLIP (38.5%, +1.1%), and SCLIP (24.4%, +15.2%), but remains below SCCLIP (43.7%, −4.1%). The most striking comparison is SCLIP on ViT-L/14: without CURE it collapses to 24.4% (vs. 35.8% on ViT-B/16), but with CURE it recovers to 40.1%, demonstrating that the larger backbone's stronger global bias is the primary bottleneck. As a plug-and-play module, CURE consistently improves every baseline it is applied to (e.g., MaskCLIP ViT-L/14: 14.1% → 29.9%; NACLIP ViT-L/14: 35.5% → 39.4%), with the largest gains on the ViT-L/14 backbone where global bias is most severe. CURE also improves background-class segmentation (Table 3: ClearCLIP ViT-B/16 avg 39.1% → 42.5%) and long-tail categories (Table 5: ClearCLIP avg 22.4% → 24.3%), confirming that debiasing benefits underrepresented and spatially diffuse regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

CURE builds directly on ClearCLIP's decomposition of CLIP's final transformer block (removing residual connections and FFN) but departs from the broader line of attention-calibration methods (SCLIP, ResCLIP, SCCLIP) that modify attention patterns to reduce global context. Rather than adjusting attention, CURE reframes the problem as a post-hoc decomposition of patch features into local semantics and a shared global bias, then removes the bias through three complementary stages: norm-thresholded outlier rejection with neighbor reconstruction (RR), global-mean subtraction (GCD), and mid-level spatial correlation refinement (SCR). This positions CURE as a plug-and-play refinement layer that can be stacked on top of any existing training-free OVSS method without altering the pretrained encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a concrete failure mode of VLMs for dense prediction—global contextual bias that scales with model capacity—and shows that a lightweight, training-free post-processing pipeline can substantially recover localized semantics. The finding that ViT-L/14 underperforms ViT-B/16 in OVSS (due to stronger global bias) and that CURE reverses this gap (e.g., +15.7% mIoU on SCLIP/ViT-L/14) challenges the assumption that larger backbones are automatically better for segmentation. Limitations remain for highly dominant foregrounds and very small structures, and the orthogonality assumption underlying GCD's decomposition may not hold universally across all VLM architectures, leaving open whether the approach generalizes beyond the CLIP family.
