## Problem

Existing MLLM-based forgery detection methods treat the model as an immutable black box, focusing on external prompt design or appended decoders while ignoring internal optimization instability. The authors diagnose a specific failure mode—Representation Drift—where fine-tuning on tampered images corrupts the model's real-world knowledge, causing real and tampered representations to collapse into an overlapping space and bottlenecking real-image recognition. No prior work intervenes in the MLLM's internal data flow to regularize this drift.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 1024×1024 and produces three outputs: a three-way class label (real, fully synthetic, or tampered), a pixel-level segmentation mask localizing the manipulated region, and a natural-language explanation. Internally, the pipeline operates on patch-level features (N patches of dimension D_v from the CLIP ViT encoder) and generates a patch-resolution error map (H_p × W_p) that is bilinearly upsampled to match the SAM localization encoder's spatial resolution before being fused with the semantic <SEG> prompt.

## Method

AAP opens the MLLM black box by repurposing the frozen CLIP visual encoder's penultimate-layer output as a 'Truth Anchor'—a stable, corruption-free perceptual reference encoding real-world priors. An Anchoring Representation Loss (ARL), defined as mean cosine distance between a lightweight MLP-projected intermediate LLM layer's patch features and the Truth Anchor, is applied asymmetrically: it is included in the training loss only for real images, pulling their representations into a low-variance manifold, while for tampered images the same cosine distance is computed but detached from backpropagation to form a spatial Error Map. This Error Map is upsampled, projected into a high-dimensional discrepancy embedding, and fused via element-wise addition with the semantic <SEG> token prompt before being fed to the SAM mask decoder, providing dense spatial guidance for localization. Training uses LoRA (α=16) on LLaVA-v1.5-7B or LISA-7B-v1 with a composite loss (λ_cls·L_cls + λ_seg·L_seg + λ_arl·L_arl, where λ_arl=3.0), and the visual encoder remains frozen throughout, so all gains come from targeted regularization rather than parameter expansion.

## Results

On SID-Set, AAP-lisa achieves 97.1% overall detection accuracy and 97.0% F1, with a standout 97.8% accuracy on the Real category (vs. 89.1% for SIDA and 83.1% for LLaVA-7B). On OpenSDID cross-generator detection, AAP-lisa attains the best average accuracy of 85.3% (vs. 82.2% for SIDA-7B and 82.0% for OpenSDI), maintaining strong performance even on the most challenging Flux.1 generator (76.7% Acc). For pixel-level localization on SID-Set, AAP-lisa reaches 56.8% IoU and 93.9% AUC, surpassing the previous SOTA SIDA-7B (43.8% IoU, 87.3% AUC) by 13.0 and 6.6 percentage points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AAP-lisa decisively outperforms all 25 baselines on SID-Set detection, with the largest margin on the Real category: 97.8% Acc vs. 89.1% (SIDA) and 83.1% (LLaVA-7B), a gain of 8.7 and 14.7 points respectively. On OpenSDID cross-generator detection, AAP-lisa leads SIDA-7B by 3.1 points in average accuracy (85.3% vs. 82.2%) and OpenSDI by 3.3 points, with the advantage widening on unseen generators like Flux.1 (76.7% vs. 73.3% for SIDA-7B). For localization on SID-Set, AAP-lisa's 56.8% IoU surpasses SAFIRE (45.8%) by 11 points and SIDA-7B (43.8%) by 13 points, while also leading in AUC (93.9% vs. 87.3%). On OpenSDID localization, AAP-lisa edges out OpenSDI (43.7% vs. 42.7% avg IoU) and LISA-7B-v1 (43.7% vs. 39.2%), with the gap most pronounced on Flux.1 (31.6% vs. 16.2% IoU). Traditional CNN-based methods (MVSS-Net, PSCC-Net, ObjFormer) and frequency-based detectors (FreqNet, NPR) are consistently outperformed by 10–30+ points across all metrics, confirming the advantage of the MLLM-based open-box approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing black-box paradigm for MLLM-based forgery detection (exemplified by SIDA, FakeShield, ForgeryGPT), where the MLLM is treated as an immutable reasoning engine and all innovation is confined to external components like prompts, adapters, or appended decoders. Building on LLaVA's architecture and LISA's <SEG> token mechanism, the authors instead intervene in the MLLM's internal data flow by repurposing the frozen CLIP visual encoder's penultimate layer as a 'Truth Anchor' and applying an asymmetric alignment loss to intermediate LLM representations. The conceptual lineage is thus: standard MLLM fine-tuning → diagnosis of internal optimization instability (representation drift) → targeted internal regularization rather than external augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnosis of representation drift—where aggressive fine-tuning on tampered images corrupts the model's real-image representations—provides a useful explanatory framework for practitioners who observe degraded performance on authentic content when fine-tuning MLLMs for forensics. The 'open-box' paradigm of reshaping internal data flow rather than appending modules offers a generalizable design principle that could extend to other MLLM fine-tuning tasks beyond forgery detection. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video forgery, to MLLMs without a separate frozen visual encoder (e.g., natively multimodal models), and whether the asymmetric design principle holds when the forgery type is unknown at training time. The method's reliance on a pre-trained encoder with strong real-world priors also raises questions about performance in domains where such priors are weak or absent.
