## Problem

Existing diffusion-based end-to-end driving methods restrict generative modeling to the planning module alone, conditioning on fixed outputs from separate discriminative perception networks. This decoupled design propagates perception errors into the planner, prevents joint refinement between tasks, and ignores temporal dynamics. Additionally, sparse query-based diffusion planners suffer from severe training-inference distribution shift, where unsupervised queries drift from the training anchor distribution during iterative denoising.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261046/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB camera images (8 views at 640×352 for NAVSIM, 6 views for Bench2Drive) processed by a ResNet-34 backbone, along with a memory bank of historical task queries from previous frames. Noisy anchors (1024 total: 900 agent, 100 map, 24 planning) are sampled from a Gaussian centered on prior anchors and projected into a shared latent query space. At inference, only 2 DDIM denoising steps (step size 10) are used, and the top-1 scoring trajectory among 24 planning anchors is selected as the final ego trajectory, alongside per-instance agent predictions and vectorized map elements.

## Method

UniTeD introduces a Unified Diffusion Decoder that jointly denoises agent, map, and planning queries within a single generative process, enabling bidirectional cross-task information exchange through all-to-all self-attention at every layer. Each decoder block combines AdaLN-based conditional modulation (encoding the current noise timestep), temporal cross-attention with a memory queue, unified self-attention across all task tokens, and multi-scale deformable attention to ground queries in 3D camera features. A Temporal Transition Module (TTM) resolves the noise-level mismatch between historical and current frames by generating per-frame scale and shift parameters from embedded historical conditions (noise level, relative noise shift, frame interval), re-projecting past queries onto the current noise manifold. An Anchor Refresh Strategy (ARS) mitigates training-inference drift by selectively applying DDIM to high-confidence queries while resampling low-confidence ones from the original anchor noise distribution at each inference step.

## Results

On NAVSIM v1, UniTeD achieves a state-of-the-art PDMS of 90.2, surpassing the best generative-separate method DiffRefiner (89.4) by +0.8 and the best discriminative-unified method HiP-AD (88.6) by +1.6. On NAVSIM v2, UniTeD attains an EPDMS of 90.1, outperforming DiffRefiner (86.2) by a substantial +3.9. On Bench2Drive, UniTeD achieves a Driving Score of 87.3 using only the standard 200K training set, exceeding HiP-AD (86.8) and DiffRefiner (87.1, trained on 500K TF++ data). On nuScenes, UniTeD leads in map mAP (0.596), NDS (0.537), AMOTA (0.419), and minADE (0.58).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniTeD outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every benchmark. On NAVSIM v1, it beats the strongest generative-separate method DiffRefiner by +0.8 PDMS and the strongest discriminative-unified method HiP-AD by +1.6 PDMS, while using only camera input (no LiDAR) yet surpassing all C+L methods including GaussianFusion (+1.4) and DiffusionDrive (+2.1). On NAVSIM v2, the margin widens dramatically: UniTeD leads DiffRefiner by +3.9 EPDMS and DriveSuprim by +7.0. On Bench2Drive, UniTeD achieves 87.3 DS with only 200K training samples, edging out HiP-AD (+0.5) and DiffRefiner (+0.2) which were trained on the larger 500K TF++ dataset, and substantially outperforming DriveTransformer (+23.8). On nuScenes perception tasks, UniTeD leads HiP-AD by +2.5% in map mAP and +1.3% in AMOTA, and reduces motion prediction minADE from 0.61 to 0.58. The only near-tie is Bench2Drive DS where UniTeD (87.3) barely exceeds DiffRefiner (87.1), but UniTeD uses half the training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniTeD extends the truncated diffusion policy introduced by DiffusionDrive from planning-only to a joint perception-and-planning formulation, where agent, map, and planning anchors are simultaneously denoised in a shared generative space. Conceptually, it merges the unified-decoder paradigm of DriveTransformer and HiP-AD (which jointly process all task queries but with discriminative regression) with the generative modeling of DiffusionDrive and DiffRefiner (which apply diffusion only to planning while treating perception as fixed). The Temporal Transition Module and Anchor Refresh Strategy are novel mechanisms that address noise-level mismatch and training-inference distribution shift, respectively, problems that prior streaming or sparse diffusion driving frameworks had not explicitly resolved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that perception and planning can be jointly refined through iterative denoising, UniTeD offers a principled alternative to the dominant discriminative E2E pipeline and suggests that generative modeling can strengthen perception as well as planning. The TTM and ARS components are practical, modular techniques that could be adopted by other diffusion-based driving or multi-task generative systems. Open questions include whether the unified diffusion approach scales to longer horizons or more diverse sensor suites, whether the modest performance gains over strong discriminative baselines (e.g., +1.6 PDMS over HiP-AD) persist under stricter closed-loop evaluation, and whether the computational overhead of joint multi-task denoising is acceptable for real-time deployment.
