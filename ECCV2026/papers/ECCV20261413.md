## Problem

In learned image compression, each bitrate operating point is trained independently under the same rate-distortion objective, ignoring the natural information hierarchy across rate levels. While knowledge distillation has been applied to LIC, prior work follows the classical paradigm where teacher superiority stems from architectural capacity; in LIC, a higher-rate model of the same architecture is simply stronger because it is allowed more bits, yet no systematic study of latent-space distillation across bitrate operating points exists. Direct latent matching between high-rate teachers and low-rate students is problematic because the student cannot reproduce distortion-oriented teacher features without violating its tighter rate budget.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (randomly cropped to 256×256 patches during training) and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed image through the standard neural transform coding pipeline (analysis transform → quantization → entropy coding → synthesis transform). A key deployment constraint is that the decoder and entropy model remain fixed; only the encoder is optimized, making the approach compatible with JPEG AI-style frozen-decoder pipelines. The teacher is a single frozen high-rate encoder (λ=2) whose pre-quantization latent representations supervise multiple lower-rate student encoders of identical architecture.

## Method

The paper introduces a self-teacher distillation framework where a high-rate instance of the same codec (trained at λ=2) is frozen and used to supervise several lower-rate student encoders sharing the same architecture, with the student loss augmented by a latent-space distillation term: L = R + λD + β·L_KD. The core technical contribution is Variance-Normalized Latent Distillation (VNLD), which divides the per-channel squared residual between student and teacher pre-quantization latents by the teacher's channel-wise variance, effectively treating teacher variance as an inverse-confidence weight. High-variance channels encoding fine residual details are down-weighted so the low-rate student is not forced to reproduce components infeasible under its rate budget, while stable structural channels receive stronger supervision. VNLD introduces no learnable parameters, requires only one additional frozen-teacher forward pass during training, and adds zero inference-time cost. The framework is validated across HPCM, BMS, and SegPIC architectures on Kodak, Tecnick, JPEG AI, and CLIC Pro benchmarks, with consistent BD-rate gains of 1–4% over RD-only training.

## Results

On HPCM, VNLD achieves BD-rate reductions of −1.05% (PSNR), −0.94% (MS-SSIM), and −1.52% (LPIPS) on Kodak, −0.65%, −1.44%, and −1.80% on Tecnick, and −1.65%, −1.24%, and −1.48% on JPEG AI, all relative to the RD-only baseline. On the BMS architecture, VNLD yields −1.7% BD-rate in PSNR on Kodak. Under frozen-decoder JPEG AI constraints with SegPIC, VNLD reaches −4.25% BD-rate in LPIPS on the JPEG AI test set, the largest single gain reported.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three distillation objectives—latent MSE alignment, contrastive representation distillation (CRD), and the proposed VNLD—against an RD-only (no distillation) baseline across HPCM, BMS, and SegPIC architectures. VNLD consistently outperforms both MSE and CRD on nearly all dataset–metric combinations; for example, on HPCM/Kodak, VNLD achieves −1.52% LPIPS BD-rate versus −0.82% (MSE) and −1.05% (CRD). MSE is competitive on PSNR (−1.01% vs. −1.05% for VNLD on Kodak) but lags substantially on perceptual metrics. CRD is the weakest variant on PSNR (−0.32% on Kodak) though it occasionally matches VNLD on LPIPS for JPEG AI (−1.32% vs. −1.48%). Under the frozen-decoder SegPIC setting, CRD actually degrades performance on Kodak (+0.41% PSNR) and CLIC Pro (+4.90% PSNR), while VNLD maintains consistent gains (−0.47% PSNR on Kodak, −1.60% on CLIC Pro), highlighting that variance-aware weighting is critical when the decoder cannot adapt.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes knowledge distillation for learned image compression by identifying bitrate asymmetry (rather than model capacity) as the source of teacher superiority, departing from the classical KD paradigm where a larger or architecturally richer network supervises a smaller one. It builds on the variational compression framework of Ballé et al. and the self-distillation idea of Zhang et al., but applies them to a setting where the 'teacher' is simply a higher-λ instance of the same codec. Prior KD-in-LIC work (Chen et al. 2025, Fu et al. 2024) transfers knowledge across architectures to reduce complexity; here, the contribution is cross-rate supervision within a single architecture, motivated by the observation that latent richness scales with allocated bits rather than parameter count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is directly relevant to JPEG AI standardization, where decoders must remain fixed and only encoders can be updated, making encoder-side training improvements the primary lever for performance. The 1–2% BD-rate gains are modest in absolute terms but meaningful in competitive compression benchmarks where progress is incremental, and the frozen-decoder SegPIC results (up to −4.25% on JPEG AI test set) and domain-specialization results (up to −7.45% on Selfie) suggest practical deployment value. Open questions include whether the variance-normalization principle generalizes to video compression or multi-rate streaming, and whether the gains compound when combined with other encoder-side optimizations. The paper does not explore whether VNLD's benefits persist at very low bitrates (<0.1 bpp) where the rate constraint is most severe.
