## Problem

Existing video super-resolution models operate as deterministic black boxes at inference time, offering users no mechanism to correct artifacts, steer texture choices, or inject targeted visual intentions. Because super-resolution is inherently ill-posed, the same LR input admits multiple plausible HR reconstructions, yet current methods force users to accept whatever the model produces. This paper addresses the gap by formulating VSR as an interactive, human-in-the-loop process where sparse, editable keyframes serve as controllable anchors for the entire sequence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded LR video sequence (T×H×W×3) and a small set of sparse HR keyframe references (generated by an external ISR model such as Nano-Banana-Pro or PiSA-SR, or optionally guided by dual text prompts specifying task and content). The LR video is encoded via a 3D causal VAE into 16-channel latents with a temporal downsampling rate of 4, while keyframes are sparsely encoded only at their corresponding latent indices (zero elsewhere). The output is a temporally consistent HR video at the target resolution, produced via a single denoising step (t=399) followed by VAE decoding, with a tunable guidance scale s controlling how strongly the keyframe priors dominate over blind restoration.

## Method

SparkVSR builds on CogVideoX1.5-5B I2V pretrained weights and introduces a dual-encoding mechanism: the LR video is encoded into 16-channel latents via a 3D VAE, and sparse HR keyframes are encoded into a matching 16-channel reference latent (zero at non-keyframe positions), then concatenated along the channel dimension to form a 32-channel conditional input for a Diffusion Transformer. Training follows a two-stage keyframe-conditioned latent-pixel pipeline: Stage 1 trains the DiT entirely in latent space with MSE loss and a reference-dropout strategy (p_drop=0.1) to preserve blind-SR capability, while Stage 2 refines in pixel space using a joint video-image scheme with MSE, DISTS perceptual, and frame-consistency losses. At inference, a Reference-Free Guidance mechanism (adapted from classifier-free guidance) computes the final prediction as a weighted combination of conditional and unconditional DiT outputs, letting users dial the keyframe adherence scale s from 0 (pure blind SR) to >1 (amplified keyframe texture propagation). The one-step denoising at t=399, inspired by DOVE, balances retaining global LR structure while allowing the DiT to hallucinate high-frequency details conditioned on the fused latents.

## Results

On the real-world MovieLQ dataset, SparkVSR with PiSA-SR references (Ours‡) achieves the best perceptual scores across all metrics: MUSIQ 68.88, CLIP-IQA 0.6361, FasterVQA 0.8028, and DOVER 0.6212. On UDM10, the no-reference variant (Ours*) attains the highest fidelity (PSNR 26.62, SSIM 0.7756), while the Nano-Banana-Pro-referenced variant (Ours†) leads on perceptual quality (CLIP-IQA 0.5501, DOVER 0.6902). Across all five benchmarks, SparkVSR surpasses the strongest baselines by up to 24.6% on CLIP-IQA, 21.8% on DOVER, and 5.6% on MUSIQ, and establishes a superior Pareto front in the perception–distortion tradeoff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DOVE, SparkVSR wins decisively on perceptual and video-quality metrics: on UDM10, Ours† scores CLIP-IQA 0.5501 vs. DOVE's 0.5011 and DOVER 0.6902 vs. 0.5664, while DOVE retains a slight PSNR edge (26.52 vs. 23.70). Against SeedVR2-7B, SparkVSR dominates on every perceptual metric across all five datasets (e.g., CLIP-IQA 0.6252 vs. 0.2882 on UDM10; DOVER 0.6212 vs. 0.3130 on MovieLQ), though SeedVR2-7B occasionally matches or exceeds Ours* on PSNR. Against FlashVSR-Full, SparkVSR leads on CLIP-IQA and DOVER in all settings (e.g., 0.6361 vs. 0.5754 CLIP-IQA and 0.6212 vs. 0.5544 DOVER on MovieLQ), while FlashVSR-Full is competitive on MUSIQ for synthetic datasets. Against STAR, SparkVSR outperforms on virtually every metric and dataset, with the largest gaps on CLIP-IQA (e.g., 0.6361 vs. 0.3731 on MovieLQ) and DOVER (0.6212 vs. 0.5696). The no-reference variant Ours* consistently achieves the best PSNR and SSIM, confirming that the keyframe-conditioned design does not sacrifice structural fidelity when references are absent.

## Relation to Prior Work

SparkVSR builds directly on the CogVideoX1.5-5B I2V foundation model for its Diffusion Transformer backbone and 3D causal VAE, and adopts DOVE's one-step denoising strategy (t=399) and two-stage latent-pixel training paradigm. Its key conceptual departure is reframing VSR from a deterministic black-box mapping to a keyframe-conditioned interactive process, bridging the quality gap between state-of-the-art ISR models (Nano-Banana-Pro, PiSA-SR) and VSR by decomposing the problem: ISR handles per-frame spatial priors on sparse anchors, while a dedicated VSR model handles temporal propagation grounded in LR motion. This distinguishes it from prior keyframe-conditioned video generation methods (e.g., Align Your Latents, Generative Video Propagation), which lack the structural fidelity constraints essential for restoration, and from recent diffusion-based VSR (DOVE, SeedVR2, FlashVSR, STAR) that remain non-interactive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical pain point in video restoration workflows: users currently cannot correct artifacts or inject preferences into VSR outputs, limiting real-world adoption. The reference-free guidance mechanism provides a continuous, interpretable knob for the perception-distortion tradeoff, and the zero-shot generalization to old-film restoration and style transfer suggests the keyframe-propagation framework is a broadly useful primitive beyond VSR. Open questions include scalability to very long sequences (the 5B model and 3D VAE impose memory constraints), whether the approach degrades under fast or non-rigid motion where keyframe propagation becomes less reliable, and whether the reliance on a separate ISR model for keyframe generation introduces a compounding error pipeline that could be unified end-to-end.
