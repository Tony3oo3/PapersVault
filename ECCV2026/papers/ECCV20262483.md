## Problem

Standard 3D Gaussian Splatting fails in aerial scenes captured by sparse, anisotropic UAV flights due to two coupled issues: textureless regions (rooftops, roads) produce incomplete SfM point clouds that destabilize Gaussian initialization, and limited oblique viewpoints induce view-aligned degeneracies where Gaussians overfit training views rather than recover true geometry. Existing sparse-view 3DGS methods rely on depth priors, generic regularization, or feed-forward predictors trained on ground-level data, all of which suffer severe domain gaps when transferred to bird's-eye aerial perspectives.

## Input / Output

The method takes 3-7 sparse aerial RGB images with known camera poses (from onboard GNSS/IMU, not estimated by COLMAP) plus the sparse SfM triangulated points extracted under those poses. It produces a hybrid 3DGS scene combining explicit Gaussians (for fine detail) and virtual Gaussians anchored to parallel planes, rendered in real time via differentiable splatting. A dense colored point cloud is also generated as an intermediate artifact during the initialization stage, and the entire pipeline runs on a single 4090 GPU in roughly 9 minutes of training plus ~12 s per view for depth adaptation.

## Method

StratoSplat has two components that exploit the gravity-aligned layered structure of aerial scenes. First, a Layered Memory Guided Initialization sequentially adapts a pretrained depth diffusion model (Marigold) at test time, using a Layered Depth Image as compact multi-view memory: each view's depth prediction is constrained by a sparse loss at SfM points and a visibility loss against the LDI-rendered guidance, progressively densifying the memory and yielding a complete point cloud P_dense. Second, a Neural Multi-Plane Gaussian representation augments explicit Gaussians (initialized from P_dense, freely densified/pruned) with virtual Gaussians whose 3D positions are fixed at ray-plane intersections with K globally shared parallel planes (normal extracted via SVD of P_dense). Virtual Gaussian attributes (color, opacity, scale, rotation) come from a compact neural field Phi, enforcing spatial smoothness and view-independent color; a depth-dependent opacity bias b(t_k) prevents alpha-compositing collapse of the multi-layer stack. This asymmetric design lets explicit Gaussians capture high-frequency local detail while virtual Gaussians impose global geometric constraints that suppress view-aligned degeneracy.

## Results

On LEVIR-NVS (3 training views, 16 scenes), StratoSplat achieves a mean PSNR of 24.48 dB, SSIM of 0.83, and LPIPS of 0.15, surpassing the strongest 3DGS baseline FSGS (20.93 dB) by 3.55 dB and the aerial-specific NeRF method MPNeRF (15.54 dB) by 8.94 dB. On the larger-scale 3DAS 7-view benchmark (9 scenes across city, countryside, and port), StratoSplat maintains state-of-the-art performance, e.g., reaching 27.48 dB PSNR on Country Scene 0 versus 24.35 dB for vanilla 3DGS. The method also outperforms all feed-forward baselines (NoPoSplat ft, MVSplat ft, DepthSplat ft, TranSplat ft) by 4–9 dB in PSNR, confirming that scene-specific geometric priors outperform domain-shifted learned predictors under extreme sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StratoSplat wins on all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) against every baseline on both LEVIR-NVS and 3DAS. Against the strongest 3DGS baseline FSGS (20.93 dB), StratoSplat gains 3.55 dB in PSNR and 0.12 in SSIM, while reducing LPIPS from 0.27 to 0.15. The aerial-specific NeRF method MPNeRF (15.54 dB) is the closest competitor on SSIM (0.80 vs. 0.83) and LPIPS (0.19 vs. 0.15), but StratoSplat leads by 8.94 dB in PSNR, highlighting the advantage of explicit Gaussian representation over implicit volumetric fields under sparse views. Among depth-based 3DGS methods, DNGaussian (17.48 dB) underperforms even vanilla 3DGS (20.20 dB) due to sensitivity to monocular depth errors, while FSGS's soft depth supervision makes it the most robust depth-based baseline. Feed-forward methods (NoPoSplat ft at 19.75 dB, TranSplat ft at 16.79 dB) suffer severe domain gap from ground-level training data; even with per-scene fine-tuning and optimization (ft+opt), TranSplat reaches only 17.31 dB on novel views despite 49.76 dB on training views, confirming the view-aligned overfitting that StratoSplat's planar anchoring explicitly prevents.

## Relation to Prior Work

StratoSplat extends 3D Gaussian Splatting with two aerial-specific components: a layered memory (LDI) that guides sequential test-time depth adaptation for initialization, and virtual Gaussians anchored to learned parallel planes for geometric regularization. It departs from generic sparse-view 3DGS methods (FSGS, DNGaussian, DropoutGS) that rely on domain-shifted monocular depth priors or view-agnostic regularization, and from NeRF-based aerial methods (MPNeRF, UPNeRF) that encode multi-plane structure but inherit NeRF's optimization cost. The conceptual novelty lies in repurposing LDI—not as a rendering representation but as an incremental geometric memory—and in coupling explicit Gaussians with plane-constrained virtual Gaussians via a shared neural field, a hybrid not seen in prior 3DGS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that exploiting the inherent stratified geometry of aerial scenes (buildings, terrain, roads layering along gravity) provides a stronger and more practical prior than generic regularization or domain-shifted learned models, requiring no large-scale aerial training data. This is directly relevant to real-world UAV deployments where dense capture is infeasible and poses come from onboard GNSS/IMU. Limitations include the assumption of accurate poses (joint pose refinement is left to future work), reliance on the stratification assumption that may not hold for non-aerial or highly irregular scenes, and sensitivity to pose perturbations at moderate noise levels. Open questions include whether the plane-anchoring strategy generalizes to scenes with non-horizontal stratification and how the method scales to city-wide reconstructions.
