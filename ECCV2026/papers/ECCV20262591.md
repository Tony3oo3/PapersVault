## Problem

Most existing layout generation methods formulate the task as a single-shot prediction, placing all elements simultaneously without spatial feedback, which fails to capture the iterative, feedback-driven nature of human design and produces visually inconsistent or unbalanced compositions. The paper asks whether layout generation can be recast as a progressive, trajectory-level decision process optimized with rendered aesthetic feedback to yield stronger aesthetic-aware generators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262591/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each placement step, the model receives a multimodal prompt concatenating verbalized element descriptions (type, content, and bounding-box tokens for already-placed elements) along with the partially rendered canvas image, and autoregressively decodes discretized bounding-box coordinate tokens (pos_x, pos_y, pos_w, pos_h) for the next group of k=√N elements. Positions are quantized into 224 discrete coordinate tokens, and the full layout is rendered via Skia to produce the final RGB layout image. The backbone is InternVL-3 8B, operating on canvas dimensions W_c × H_c with elements typed as text, image, shape, or background.

## Method

i-Design formulates layout generation as a sequential decision process where elements are placed in z-ordered groups of size k=√N, each conditioned on the previously rendered partial canvas. Training proceeds in two stages: Progressive Imitation Learning (PIL) uses cross-entropy to predict all remaining groups given a ground-truth partial canvas, and Progressive Aesthetic Policy Optimization (PAPO) performs online policy optimization by sampling m=4 rollouts per prompt, rendering them, and scoring via pairwise comparisons from the DesignSense VLM judge. Pairwise preferences form a directed graph whose consensus scores are computed via damped power iteration (γ=0.85), with the ground-truth layout included as an anchor to stabilize the reward distribution. The resulting trajectory-level reward drives a groupwise policy gradient (adapting GSPO) that assigns a shared advantage to all future group predictions from the same canvas state, enabling stable long-horizon credit assignment across the progressive design trajectory.

## Results

On the Crello benchmark, i-Design-8B achieves a Mean IoU of 48.27%, surpassing the prior SoTA AesthetiQ-8B (42.83%) by 5.44 points, and attains the highest aesthetic win rates of 27.64% (GPT-4o) and 19.03% (O3). On WebUI, i-Design-8B reaches 53.71% mIoU and 31.29% GPT-4o win rate, outperforming AesthetiQ-8B (48.29% mIoU, 24.48% win rate). Model scaling from 1B to 8B yields consistent gains, with mIoU rising from 23.83% to 48.27% on Crello and GPT-4o win rate from 2.85% to 27.64%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

i-Design-8B outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric across both Crello and WebUI. Against the closest competitor AesthetiQ-8B [35], i-Design gains +5.44 mIoU (48.27 vs. 42.83) on Crello and +11.9 / +10.99 percentage points in GPT-4o / O3 win rates, respectively. The iterative method LaDeCo [31] is the second-strongest baseline (35.25 mIoU, 14.42% GPT-4o), but i-Design-8B exceeds it by 13.02 mIoU and 13.22 win-rate points. Diffusion-based LACE [8] and LLM-based LayoutNUWA [39] trail substantially, with mIoU of 23.18 and 25.74 respectively, and win rates below 5%. Older detection-style baselines LayoutDETR [46] and FlexDM [20] achieve the lowest scores (15.04 and 12.71 mIoU), confirming that single-shot geometric prediction without aesthetic feedback is insufficient. On WebUI, i-Design-8B (53.71 mIoU, 31.29% win rate) similarly dominates AesthetiQ-8B (48.29, 24.48%) and LaDeCo (41.16, 19.49%), with Desigen [42] and LayoutDETR [46] far behind at ~15–16 mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AesthetiQ line of work (same authors, CVPR 2025) by replacing its offline aesthetic-aware preference alignment (AAPA) with an online reinforcement learning stage (PAPO) that adapts Grouped Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) to layout generation. The key conceptual departure is reframing layout generation from a single-shot coordinate prediction problem into a progressive, trajectory-level decision process where elements are placed in groups conditioned on rendered intermediate canvases. The token-based verbalization scheme and InternVL-3 backbone are inherited from AesthetiQ, while the DesignSense VLM judge and Skia rendering pipeline ground the reward signal in actual visual composition rather than token-level losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive formulation with online aesthetic feedback offers a more faithful model of human design iteration and could generalize to other sequential creative tasks (e.g., multi-page documents, 3D scene composition). A notable limitation is the dependence on a dedicated VLM judge (DesignSense) for reward computation, which constrains the approach to domains where such judges exist or can be trained. Open questions include whether the consensus-based reward aggregation scales to larger candidate pools, whether the progressive inference overhead is acceptable in production settings, and whether the gains persist on more diverse or unconstrained design tasks beyond the Crello/WebUI benchmarks.
