## Problem

Current VLMs fail to reliably detect physically implausible content in T2V-generated videos, and existing evaluation approaches—either pre-defined prompt-based checks or end-to-end plausibility scorers—lack generalizability and interpretability. Preliminary experiments reveal that VLMs possess latent physical knowledge but are strongly biased by assuming input videos are real-world, causing them to overlook violations that humans easily perceive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260314/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video clip (typically under 10 seconds with clear physical interactions) paired with its text caption, and autoregressively generates a token sequence beginning with a binary plausibility judgment ([YES] or [NO]) followed by a free-form natural-language explanation of the violated physical principle. Beyond the discrete label, the model exposes token-level logits that enable a continuous Plausibility Score for fine-grained benchmarking of T2V systems, where logits for 'plausible' predictions are added and those for 'implausible' are subtracted into a cumulative score.

## Method

PhyDetEx is built by LoRA fine-tuning (rank 8, 3 epochs, lr 1e-4) of Qwen2.5-VL-7B on the PID training split of 2,588 contrastive video pairs, where each implausible video is generated by an LLM-rewritten caption that alters only the physical event while preserving all other scene context. This paired design forces the model to condition its decision solely on the physical content rather than exploiting generation-artifact shortcuts. Training uses a unified autoregressive language-modeling loss over the full output sequence (judgment token + explanation), so the same objective simultaneously teaches detection and interpretable reasoning. The key insight is that VLMs already encode implicit physics; a small number of contrastive examples with matched context is sufficient to unlock this capability without requiring large-scale implausible data.

## Results

On the Impossible Videos benchmark, PhyDetEx (7B) achieves an F1 Score of 87.8 and a Reasoning Score of 4.20, substantially outperforming the best baseline (Qwen3VL 8B at F1 81.1, Reasoning 3.20). On the PID test split, PhyDetEx reaches F1 83.5 and Reasoning Score 4.24, again leading all baselines (best F1: InternVL2.5 26B at 79.2; best Reasoning: Qwen3VL 30B A3 at 3.28). PhyDetEx achieves the highest Acc. Impl. on both datasets (86.4% and 75.6%), though it does not reach the highest Acc. Plaus., reflecting the strong plausibility bias of most VLMs. When used to benchmark T2V models, Sora 2.0 attains the highest plausibility rate (86.0%) and score (32.62), while open-source models range from 52.0% (CogVideoX1.5) to 66.0% (WanX2.1 1.3B / WanX2.2 14B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhyDetEx (7B) decisively outperforms all five baseline families on the F1 Score and Reasoning Score across both evaluation datasets. On Impossible Videos, it surpasses the strongest baseline Qwen3VL 8B (F1 81.1, Reasoning 3.20) by 6.7 F1 points and 1.0 reasoning points, and beats Qwen2.5VL 32B (F1 78.7) by 9.1 points. On the PID test split, it leads InternVL2.5 26B (F1 79.2) by 4.3 points and Qwen3VL 30B A3 (F1 77.7) by 5.8 points in F1, while its Reasoning Score of 4.24 exceeds the best baseline (Qwen3VL 30B A3 at 3.28) by nearly a full point. The most striking gap is in Acc. Impl.: PhyDetEx reaches 86.4% on Impossible Videos versus 65.2% for the best baseline (InternVL2.5 26B), and 75.6% on PID versus 61.6% (InternVL2.5 4B). Notably, PhyDetEx does not achieve the highest Acc. Plaus. (e.g., MiniCPM-V 4.5 scores 98.9% on Impossible Videos), but this reflects a degenerate bias toward always predicting 'plausible' rather than genuine detection ability, which the F1 Score correctly penalises.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two existing lines of physical plausibility evaluation in T2V: rule-based prompt checking (VideoPhy, T2VWorldBench) and end-to-end plausibility-score detectors (VideoPhy-2, VideoScore). It also builds on the Impossible Videos benchmark, which first evaluated VLMs on detecting implausible content, but addresses its two key limitations—reliance on explicitly implausible prompts and the real-vs-generated shortcut. The methodological lineage is VLM fine-tuning via LoRA (as in standard VLM adaptation), repurposed here with a contrastive paired-video construction strategy that isolates physical plausibility as the sole variable between paired samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhyDetEx provides a lightweight, interpretable evaluation tool that goes beyond binary plausibility scores by generating textual explanations of violated physical principles, making it practical for diagnosing specific T2V model failures. The benchmarking reveals a substantial gap between closed-source (Sora 2.0 at 86% plausibility rate) and open-source models (52–66%), highlighting that physical law adherence remains an unsolved challenge for the open community. Key limitations include reliance on purely visual modality (no audio), hallucinations on complex or non-visual physical events, and a test set of only 500 videos, leaving open whether the findings generalize to longer, more physically complex scenes. The authors suggest multi-modal integration and DPO-based improvement of T2V models as natural next steps.
