## Problem

World models for visual reinforcement learning degrade severely under task-irrelevant visual distractions because reconstruction objectives force them to model all pixels, including noise. Pre-trained vision models (PVMs) offer robust, semantically structured representations, but their perception-oriented features are misaligned with the dynamics and reward signals that control tasks require, and naively injecting them into a world model disrupts the temporal coherence of latent state evolution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 64×64 RGB image observations from visually distracting environments (Distracted DMC with dynamic video backgrounds, Textured Maze2D with complex surface textures) and outputs continuous control actions. The same image is processed by both the world model's convolutional encoder and a PVM (DINOv2 or CLIP) to produce structured visual features; the PVM's patch-level tokens serve as a queryable knowledge base for the recurrent state, while its global feature provides spatial grounding for the encoder.

## Method

VPA-WM integrates PVMs into a RePo-based RSSM world model through two mechanisms. First, Control-Oriented Alignment (COA) fine-tunes the PVM via LoRA on attention query/value projections using two auxiliary losses—Instant Action Prediction (reconstructing the action from consecutive PVM features) and Short-Horizon Return Estimation (predicting a short n-step discounted return)—decoupled from world model gradients to avoid catastrophic forgetting. Second, Spatio-Temporal Collaborative Integration (STCI) operates at two levels: Spatially Grounded Guidance aligns the encoder's instantaneous output with PVM global features via an MSE loss to anchor spatial attention on task-relevant structures, while Experience Query Integration lets the RSSM recurrent state cross-attend to PVM patch tokens to adaptively retrieve fine-grained visual evidence without breaking autoregressive dynamics. An adaptive weight computed from the EMA of COA losses modulates the SGG term, and the overall objective combines reward prediction, time-varying KL regularization, and the SGG loss—deliberately omitting pixel reconstruction to prevent modeling of irrelevant visual noise.

## Results

On Distracted DMC, DA-WM (DINOv2-based) achieves 572±84 on Walker Run, 804±43 on Cheetah Run, and 812±61 on Humanoid Stand, substantially outperforming MInCo (396, 622, 742) and Dreamer (141, 104, 28). On Standard DMC, DA-WM matches or exceeds Dreamer on most tasks (e.g., Walker Run: 557 vs 471; Cheetah Swingup: 862 vs 797) while showing almost no degradation when transferred to the distracted setting. On Textured Maze2D, both DA-WM and CA-WM achieve near-perfect success on Umaze (104–108) and lead on the harder Large maze (e.g., Wood Large: 297–328 vs MInCo 239, RePo 16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Dreamer, VPA-WM achieves dramatic gains on Distracted DMC: DA-WM scores 572 on Walker Run vs Dreamer's 141 (4×), 804 on Cheetah Run vs 104 (7.7×), and 812 on Humanoid Stand vs 28 (29×), while remaining comparable on Standard DMC (e.g., Walker Stand 940 vs 960). Against RePo, VPA-WM wins on the harder distracted tasks (Walker Run: 572 vs 259; Cheetah Run: 804 vs 551) and on Standard DMC Walker Run (557 vs 287) and Cheetah Run (789 vs 598), though RePo is competitive on Walker Stand under distraction (898 vs 947). Against MInCo (the strongest baseline), DA-WM leads on Walker Run (572 vs 396), Cheetah Run (804 vs 622), and Cheetah Swingup (850 vs 800) under distraction, while CA-WM leads on Humanoid Stand (866 vs 742); MInCo retains a slight edge on Walker Stand under distraction (962 vs 947). On Textured Maze2D, both VPA-WM variants outperform MInCo on the Large maze across all three textures (e.g., Wood Large: 297–328 vs 239) and match or exceed it on Umaze, while RePo collapses on Large mazes (7–16). Overall, VPA-WM wins or ties on the majority of tasks across all three benchmarks, with the largest margins on the most visually challenging conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

VPA-WM builds directly on the Dreamer/RePo RSSM world model architecture and the MInCo time-varying KL schedule, but departs from both the robust-MBRL line (Iso-Dream, Denoised MDP, RePo, MInCo) that learns task-agnostic features from scratch, and the PVM-for-RL line (R3M, DINO-WM, SAM-G) that injects frozen or rigidly-segmented visual features. The key conceptual departure is treating PVMs not as a replacement encoder or a static feature source, but as a dynamically queryable, control-aligned auxiliary signal integrated through two complementary mechanisms (spatial grounding and temporal cross-attention), with the PVM itself fine-tuned via LoRA under control-oriented objectives before integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-identified gap—objective misalignment between PVM semantics and RL control—by showing that lightweight, decoupled fine-tuning of PVMs (IAP + SHRE) followed by adaptive spatio-temporal integration can substantially improve robustness under visual distractions without sacrificing standard-environment performance. Limitations include evaluation only at 64×64 resolution, two PVM backbones, and two environment families, leaving open whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or 3D/embodied settings. The authors note future work on integrating VLMs for more stable dynamic modeling, and the adaptive weighting mechanism (Eq. 13) is a practical design choice that may transfer to other PVM-augmented RL pipelines.
