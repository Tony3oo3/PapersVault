## Problem

During robotic grasping and in-hand manipulation, the hand severely occludes the object, making single-view RGB 3D reconstruction fundamentally underconstrained. Existing occlusion-aware 3D generative methods rely solely on visual cues and produce reconstructions that may intersect the hand, miss true contact regions, or drift in metric scale. Physical interaction signals—proprioception and multi-contact touch—provide direct geometric constraints exactly where vision is missing, yet they have rarely been integrated as explicit constraints in dense 3D reconstruction under severe occlusion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single egocentric RGB image with instance segmentation masks for the visible object and hand/occluder, joint angles defining the robot kinematic chain, and fingertip tactile readings. The object is represented as a signed distance field on a 64³ camera-aligned grid sharing the same coordinate frame as the posed hand (obtained via forward kinematics), so the object pose is implicitly recovered. Touch is encoded as a two-channel 3D tensor (binary contact occupancy plus distance-to-nearest-contact) in the same grid. The final output is a metric-scale SDF that can be passed to a SLat refinement stage for a textured mesh at real-world scale.

## Method

A Structure-VAE learns a compact latent space for pose-aware SDF grids, and a conditional flow-matching transformer (Flow Transformer) is trained in this latent space—pretrained on vision-only object renders to learn a strong shape prior, then finetuned on simulated grasp scenes. During finetuning, the model conditions on visible RGB evidence with occlusion masks (via DINOv2 features), a hand latent obtained by encoding the rasterized hand SDF with the same Structure-VAE encoder, and tactile features fused with the hand latent through a lightweight 3D CNN and 1×1×1 projection. Two physics-based losses are added: a non-interpenetration loss that penalizes object interior mass inside the hand volume (using a smooth saturation function to avoid unstable gradients), and a contact-consistency loss that pulls the reconstructed surface toward observed contact voxels. At inference, the same physics energy is used as a decoder-guided control term in the Euler sampling loop, steering generation toward physically feasible shapes without retraining.

## Results

On the YCB simulation benchmark (22,680 test images across 36 objects), the proposed method achieves CD 0.033, NC 0.844, Voxel IoU 0.586, and EMD 0.184 overall, outperforming SAM3D (CD 0.039, NC 0.803, Voxel IoU 0.504, EMD 0.202) on all metrics except F@0.02 (0.189 vs. 0.228). The advantage grows with occlusion severity: at the most occluded bin B5, Voxel IoU reaches 0.532 vs. 0.337 for SAM3D and 0.262 for Amodal3R. For pose estimation, the method achieves 3D IoU 0.53 and ADD-S 0.07, substantially exceeding SAM3D (3D IoU 0.406, ADD-S 0.095). In the real-world humanoid robot setting, the method attains Voxel IoU 0.648 and NC 0.903, again surpassing SAM3D (0.510, 0.888) on geometry metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Amodal3R [73] (vision-only, same TRELLIS-style architecture), the proposed method dominates across all five reconstruction metrics: CD drops from 0.188 to 0.033, Voxel IoU rises from 0.339 to 0.586, and EMD falls from 0.314 to 0.184, confirming that proprioception and touch are essential under severe hand occlusion. Against SAM3D [65] (a stronger, larger vision-only model), the proposed method wins on CD (0.033 vs. 0.039), NC (0.844 vs. 0.803), Voxel IoU (0.586 vs. 0.504), and EMD (0.184 vs. 0.202), but loses on F@0.02 (0.189 vs. 0.228), which the authors attribute to SAM3D's advantage in the least-occluded regime where vision alone suffices. The gap widens dramatically with occlusion: at bin B5, Voxel IoU is 0.532 (Ours) vs. 0.337 (SAM3D) vs. 0.262 (Amodal3R), and 3D IoU for pose estimation is 0.356 vs. 0.150. In the real-world humanoid robot test, the method maintains its Voxel IoU lead over SAM3D (0.648 vs. 0.510) while Amodal3R degrades severely (0.352), though the F@0.02 gap with SAM3D persists (0.186 vs. 0.225), likely due to calibration and encoder noise in the physical setup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TRELLIS architecture [75] (Structure-VAE + flow-matching + SLat refinement) by repurposing its latent-space generative framework for occluded, physically grounded reconstruction. It adopts the occlusion-aware mask conditioning strategy from Amodal3R [73] but departs from the vision-only paradigm by injecting proprioceptive hand geometry and multi-contact tactile signals as 3D-space conditioning in a shared camera-aligned grid. This contrasts with prior tactile-in-robotics work (e.g., TouchSDF, ShapeMap 3D) that uses touch for local shape inference or pose estimation, and with physics-based 3D generation (DSO, Atlas3D) that enforces physical plausibility without hand-occlusion context. The key conceptual move is treating contact and non-interpenetration as differentiable geometric constraints on the SDF, bridging the gap between interaction sensing and dense generative reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to produce metric-scale, physically consistent amodal reconstructions by fusing proprioception and multi-contact touch into a generative pipeline, enabling direct integration into downstream robotic control (grasping, insertion, pose estimation) without post-hoc correction. The demonstrated cross-embodiment transfer (unseen end-effector on a real humanoid) suggests the shared 3D-frame formulation generalizes beyond the training hardware. Key limitations include the fixed 64³ grid resolution (limiting thin-structure fidelity), sensitivity to kinematic calibration errors, and the camera-aligned frame that complicates multi-view fusion. Open questions include whether a single-stage pipeline can replace the two-stage Structure-VAE + SLat design, whether touch can additionally infer physical properties (friction, compliance), and whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse interaction datasets.
