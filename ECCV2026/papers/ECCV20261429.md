## Problem

Existing point-based 3D representations cannot simultaneously support non-rigid geometry deformation and fine-grained texture editing. Gaussian Splatting methods with global UV maps lose valid texture correspondence after deformation, while per-primitive texture maps cannot represent deformations finer than individual Gaussians. Attention-based point renderers like PAPR handle geometry deformation naturally but lack any mechanism for local texture editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261429/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images to reconstruct a point cloud with learned features, then accepts a user-specified non-rigid deformation (displacing point positions) and an edited 2D texture atlas (modified in UV space) as editing inputs. The UV atlas is parameterized as n=4 or 8 charts, each at resolution 256√(2/n)×256√(2/n), and texture edits are applied by comparing original and edited atlas pixels to generate a binary mask that gates bilinear texture sampling during rendering.

## Method

PointGT extends PAPR's cross-attention point renderer with three components. First, two geometry regularizers (on-ray projection loss and on-surface neighborhood loss) are added during pre-training to produce well-conditioned ray-surface intersection points suitable for UV supervision. Second, a multi-chart UV atlas and 2D texture map are learned via a Nuvo-style optimization augmented with a novel normal-free distortion loss that penalizes anisotropic scaling and area deviation using Jacobian singular values of the 3D→2D mapping. Third, a deformation-aware canonical correspondence mechanism maps deformed-space intersections back to canonical space by orthogonally projecting onto the ray and fusing per-neighbor displacements (canonical minus deformed positions) weighted by deformed-space attention, enabling stable UV lookup that preserves texture attachment under arbitrary non-rigid edits.

## Results

On the DTU dataset with 5,000 primitives, PointGT achieves PSNR 33.25, SSIM 0.970, and LPIPS 0.024, outperforming all baselines including NeST-Splatting (PSNR 32.68) and GSTex (PSNR 28.92). On the Blender synthetic dataset at the same 5k budget, PointGT reaches PSNR 31.01, SSIM 0.976, and LPIPS 0.039, again leading all competitors. Under authors' recommended primitive counts (30k for PointGT vs. 73k–240k for baselines), PointGT still achieves the best PSNR (33.48 on DTU, 33.57 on Blender) and lowest LPIPS (0.023, 0.021) with roughly 3–8× fewer primitives. For simultaneous geometry-and-texture editing, VBench evaluation shows PointGT outperforms GSTex on imaging quality (+12.48%) and subject consistency (+2.16%) while matching on background consistency and motion smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointGT outperforms all five baselines on novel view synthesis in both the authors'-recommended and fixed-5k-primitive settings. Against the closest competitor NeST-Splatting [29], PointGT achieves higher PSNR (33.48 vs. 33.65 on DTU at recommended counts; 33.25 vs. 32.68 at 5k) and substantially lower LPIPS (0.023 vs. 0.042 on DTU), while using fewer primitives (30k vs. 80k). Against GSTex [15], the primary editing baseline, PointGT wins on PSNR (33.48 vs. 32.87 on DTU) and LPIPS (0.023 vs. 0.038), and in the editing-specific VBench evaluation gains +12.48% on imaging quality and +2.16% on subject consistency. Compared to the base PAPR [30] renderer, PointGT improves PSNR by over 4 dB on DTU (33.48 vs. 29.34) and 1.5 dB on Blender (33.57 vs. 32.07) at the same 30k point budget, demonstrating that the geometry regularizers and UV mapping add significant rendering fidelity. Texture-GS [26] and Textured Gaussians [2] trail PointGT by 3+ dB PSNR on DTU, largely because they require 90k–240k primitives to match quality that PointGT achieves with 30k.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointGT extends the attention-based point renderer PAPR by adding a learned multi-chart UV mapping (adapted from Nuvo) and two novel geometry regularizers (on-ray and on-surface) that make the attention-interpolated surface points suitable for UV atlas learning. It deliberately departs from the dominant 3D Gaussian Splatting editing line (Texture-GS, GSTex, Textured Gaussians, NeST-Splatting), arguing that Gaussian primitives—being rigid volumes—fundamentally cannot support simultaneous non-rigid deformation and fine-grained texture editing, whereas attention-based interpolation preserves surface continuity under deformation. The deformation-aware canonical correspondence mechanism (displacement fusion) is a new contribution not present in any prior UV-mapping or deformation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PointGT is the first method to enable persistent texture edits under non-rigid geometry deformation for point-based neural representations, addressing a gap that all prior Gaussian-splatting-based editing methods leave open. The deformation-aware correspondence and geometry regularizers are practical techniques that could transfer to other attention-based renderers. A key limitation is the reliance on optimization-based UV parameterization (Nuvo), which struggles with complex topology; the authors suggest learning-based UV mapping as a path forward. Open questions include scalability to multi-object scenes, real-time editing, and whether the correspondence mechanism generalizes to topology-changing deformations.
