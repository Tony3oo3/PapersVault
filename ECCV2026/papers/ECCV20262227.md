## Problem

End-to-end transformer trackers like MOTR suffer from a detection-association conflict: when detect and track queries are jointly decoded, track queries interfere with detection, degrading mAP by up to 6.3 points. Existing remedies either inject priors from external detectors (breaking self-containment) or require complex query-management logic. The paper asks whether the model can generate its own spatial detection priors internally, without any external detector.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262227/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video frames in clips of 5 frames, resized so the shorter side is 800 px (training) or 1333 px (inference) with the longer side capped at 1536 px. It produces tracked bounding boxes with persistent identities via a dual-pass decoder: a detection-only pass yields self-proposal queries (4D anchor + confidence-conditioned content embedding), which are concatenated with propagated track queries and refined in a shared tracking decoder. The backbone encoder runs once per frame; only the lightweight decoder is invoked twice.

## Method

SelfMOTR decouples proposal discovery from association by running a detection-only forward pass (detect queries only, no track queries) to generate confident 4D anchor proposals, which are then fed as proposal query tokens into the same shared tracking decoder alongside QIM-propagated track queries. Each proposal token carries the predicted box as its positional encoding and a learned shared embedding plus sine-cosine positional encoding of the confidence score as its content; a fixed set of 10 learned anchors is appended for robustness to missed detections. Training jointly optimizes the standard MOTR tracking loss and a proposal loss (focal + L1 + GIoU with Hungarian matching) at weight λ_prop = 0.5, with both passes sharing all decoder weights. A Track Attention Mass diagnostic, inspired by attention-sink analyses in LLMs, reveals that standard MOTR's detect queries develop unbalanced self-attention (overly track-dominant or overly localized), and that SelfMOTR's structural priors prevent this polarization, concentrating attention into a balanced regime near 0.4 mass with high entropy (~0.85). Ablations confirm that the detection-association conflict is nearly eliminated (AP drop of only 0.3 vs. 6.3 for MOTR) and that shared decoder weights outperform a dual-decoder variant by 4.8 AssA points.

## Results

On DanceTrack, SelfMOTR achieves 69.2 HOTA with 80.9 DetA, 59.3 AssA, 72.5 IDF1, and 89.9 MOTA, matching CO-MOT (69.4 HOTA) while attaining the highest association score among all end-to-end trackers. On the highly dynamic Bird Flock Tracking (BFT) dataset, SelfMOTR sets a new state-of-the-art with 71.1 HOTA, 82.7 IDF1, and 77.6 MOTA, surpassing all prior methods. On AnimalTrack, SelfMOTR achieves 45.5 HOTA and 53.7 IDF1, establishing the best end-to-end result and outperforming TrackFormer by +14.5 HOTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DanceTrack, SelfMOTR (69.2 HOTA) is within 0.2 of CO-MOT (69.4) and MOTRv3 (68.3), while achieving the highest AssA (59.3) and IDF1 (72.5) among all end-to-end methods, indicating superior identity preservation from natively aligned proposals. It trails MOTRv2 (69.9 HOTA, 83.0 DetA) by 0.7 HOTA, which is expected since MOTRv2 relies on an external YOLOX detector (79.7 AP vs. SelfMOTR's 71.2 AP) and uses 141M parameters versus SelfMOTR's 42M. On BFT, SelfMOTR achieves a clear SOTA margin of +1.5 HOTA over SambaMOTR (69.6) and +4.3 over OC-SORT (66.8), the strongest non end-to-end baseline. On AnimalTrack, SelfMOTR (45.5 HOTA) edges out TransTrack (45.4) and substantially outperforms TrackFormer (31.0, +14.5 HOTA), though it remains below the non end-to-end QDTrack (47.0). Overall, SelfMOTR matches or exceeds all detector-free end-to-end trackers on HOTA and association metrics while using fewer parameters than external-detector approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SelfMOTR extends the MOTR family of end-to-end transformer trackers by addressing the well-documented detection-association conflict that plagues joint decoding. While MOTRv2/v3 resolve this conflict by injecting external detection priors (YOLOX) and CO-MOT introduces shadow queries with coopetition label assignment, SelfMOTR departs from both by generating spatial proposals from the model's own detection-only forward pass, eliminating the need for a separate detector or complex query-management logic. The Track Attention Mass diagnostic, inspired by attention-sink analyses in large language models, provides a new mechanistic lens for understanding why generic detect queries fail under joint decoding, grounding the architectural choice in an interpretable observation rather than a heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that internal prior generation is a viable default design principle for unified tracking architectures, offering a detector-free path to competitive performance (SOTA on BFT, near-parity with CO-MOT on DanceTrack) while preserving the self-contained character of end-to-end tracking. The Track Attention Mass diagnostic is a reusable tool for probing query interactions in any joint-decoding transformer, potentially applicable beyond MOT. Limitations include a modest detection AP gap versus external-detector approaches (71.2 vs. 79.7) and the overhead of a second decoder pass, though the authors show a shallow proposal decoder suffices. Open questions include whether the self-proposal principle transfers to other unified architectures (segmentation, action recognition) and whether more adaptive, confidence-aware proposal scheduling could close the remaining detection gap.
