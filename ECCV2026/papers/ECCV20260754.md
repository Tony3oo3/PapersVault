## Problem

High-quality spatio-temporal scene graph datasets for video remain extremely limited in scale and density due to the prohibitive cost of dense frame-level manual annotation. Existing datasets annotate only a handful of frames per video, miss objects that appear or disappear mid-video, and suffer from severe long-tail bias and sparse relational labels, causing models to overfit to dataset-specific distributions and fail to generalize to open-vocabulary scenes.

## Input / Output

TRASER takes a raw video together with pre-computed panoptic object trajectories (per-frame binary segmentation masks with consistent instance IDs) as input; it does not jointly discover and track objects from scratch. The model outputs a structured JSON scene graph containing per-trajectory object labels, multi-class attributes, time-localized spatio-temporal relations across seven categories (spatial, functional, stateful, motion, social, attentional, event-level), and subject-relation-object triplets with temporal intervals. Evaluation uses an LLM-based semantic aligner (GPT-4o-mini constrained as a pairwise lexical matcher) to support open-vocabulary scoring beyond closed-set Recall@k.

## Method

TRASER augments Qwen2.5-VL-3B with a trajectory-aligned token arrangement that assigns each ViT token to an object via a coverage score computed by 2D average-pooling the object's binary mask over the token's exact pixel footprint, then temporally sorts tokens per object and concatenates them with [TRJ] boundary tokens. A dual Perceiver-Resampler module then compresses these variable-length token streams: an object-trajectory resampler uses 32 learnable latent queries to aggregate global semantics over each object's full temporal span, while a temporal-window resampler independently resamples tokens within disjoint time windows (Δt) to preserve fine-grained local motion and attribute/relation changes, with timestamp embeddings added for explicit temporal positioning. The two resamplers are complementary—the object-level one drives object recognition gains while the temporal-window one drives relation prediction gains—and their outputs are concatenated per object before autoregressive decoding by the language model. Training is supervised on SVG2 (636K synthetic videos) combined with academic benchmarks (LV-VIS, OVIS, VIPSeg, VidOR, VidVRD), freezing the ViT backbone and jointly fine-tuning the projector, resamplers, and LLM for one epoch on 8×H100 GPUs.

## Results

On the four VSG benchmarks (PVSG, VidOR, VIPSeg, SVG2_test), TRASER achieves 16.1/22.9/16.7 triplet recall, 16.9/25.0/18.7 relation recall, 86.5/72.7/91.4/79.0 object accuracy, and 27.1 attribute recall (SVG2_test), all under the lenient semantic criterion with temporal IoU 0.5. This represents gains of ~15–20 points in relation detection, 20–40 points in object prediction, and ~13–18 points in attribute prediction over the strongest open-source baselines. TRASER also surpasses GPT-5 by 13.5 points in object prediction (79.0 vs 65.5 on SVG2_test) and 3 points in attribute prediction (27.1 vs 24.1), while GPT-5 retains a small edge on PVSG triplet (16.6 vs 16.1) and relation (18.3 vs 16.9). In the downstream VideoQA task, feeding TRASER's scene graphs to GPT-4.1 yields +0.4 (AGQA 2.0) and +4.6 (Perception-Test) absolute accuracy over video-only input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest open-source VLMs (GLM-4.1-9B-Thinking, Qwen3-VL-4B-Thinking, MiniCPM-V 4.5), TRASER leads by 15–20 points on triplet and relation recall, 20–40 points on object accuracy, and 13–18 points on attribute recall across all benchmarks. Against proprietary models, TRASER outperforms GPT-4.1 and Gemini-2.5-Pro on every metric, and surpasses GPT-5 on object prediction (e.g., 79.0 vs 65.5 on SVG2_test, 86.5 vs 68.1 on VIPSeg) and attribute prediction (27.1 vs 24.1), though GPT-5 retains a narrow advantage on PVSG triplet (16.6 vs 16.1) and PVSG relation (18.3 vs 16.9). On VidOR, TRASER actually beats GPT-5 on both triplet (22.9 vs 19.7) and relation (25.0 vs 21.7). Fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B baselines (first-bbox and full-bbox-trajectory variants) confirm that standard fine-tuning alone is insufficient: even the best fine-tuned variant (Bbox Traj.) reaches only 46.1 object accuracy on SVG2_test versus TRASER's 79.0. In the VideoQA downstream task, TRASER's scene graphs improve GPT-4.1 accuracy by +0.4 on AGQA 2.0 and +4.6 on Perception-Test over video-only, and outperform Qwen2.5-VL's scene graphs by +1.5 and +2.8 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image scene graph lineage (Visual Genome → Synthetic Visual Genome) to the video domain, but departs from prior video scene graph work (PVSG, VidOR, VidVRD, Action Genome) in two fundamental ways: it replaces bounding-box annotations with dense panoptic segmentation trajectories, and it scales from hundreds to hundreds of thousands of videos via a fully automated pipeline (SAM2 + DAM + GPT-5). Architecturally, TRASER builds on Qwen2.5-VL-3B as a base VLM and repurposes the Perceiver-Resampler mechanism from Flamingo into a dual-module design (object-trajectory + temporal-window resamplers) that has no direct precedent in prior video scene graph generation, which typically relied on graph neural networks or transformer decoders over bbox features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SVG2's order-of-magnitude scale increase (636K videos, 6.6M objects, 52M attributes, 6.7M relations) addresses the core bottleneck in video scene graph research: prior datasets were too small and closed-vocabulary to train models that generalize to open-world scenes. The open-vocabulary evaluation framework (LLM-based semantic aligner with Cohen's κ validation) is a practical contribution that the field has lacked. Key open questions remain: whether the automated pipeline's error rates (85.4% relation accuracy) compound at scale, whether TRASER can be made fully end-to-end (currently requires pre-computed trajectories), and whether the scene-graph-as-intermediate-representation benefit (+1.5–4.6 on VQA) holds for more complex compositional reasoning beyond the tested benchmarks.
