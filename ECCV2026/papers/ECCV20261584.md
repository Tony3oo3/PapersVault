## Problem

Normalizing Flows provide exact density estimation for anomaly detection but suffer severe catastrophic forgetting under sequential task updates, since even minor parameter interference distorts the entire density manifold. Existing continual learning techniques (replay, regularization, LoRA-based PEFT) were designed for discriminative models and cannot be directly applied to NFs without violating their strict invertibility and exact Jacobian requirements. No prior method exploits the internal structural properties of coupling-based flows to achieve safe, parameter-level isolation for continual density estimation.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB product images, extracts multi-scale patch features from a frozen backbone (WideResNet-50-2 for MVTec-AD, ViT-B/16 for VisA), and feeds the integrated feature grid through a single Normalizing Flow. At inference, a prototype-based Mahalanobis routing step on the final-scale features selects the correct task adapter, after which the flow produces per-patch NLL scores; the image-level score is the mean of the top-3 patch scores, and the patch-level map is the full spatial NLL grid. The system operates in a task-agnostic, zero-replay setting where class identity is unavailable at test time.

## Method

DeCoFlow exploits the structural property that affine coupling layers remain invertible with exact log-determinant computation regardless of internal subnet architecture (Proposition 1), enabling each scale/shift subnet to be decomposed into a frozen universal base plus a task-specific LoRA adapter (rank 16) without breaking flow validity. To compensate for frozen-base rigidity, three modules are introduced: Task-Specific Alignment (LayerNorm + bounded affine transform to recalibrate input statistics), Auxiliary Coupling Layers (small task-specific MLP-based coupling layers appended after the main DCL stack to resolve residual cross-dimensional correlations), and Tail-Aware Loss (reweighting the top-2% highest-NLL patches at 85% weight to redirect limited adapter capacity toward low-density tail regions critical for anomaly discrimination). Training proceeds by jointly learning the base and Task-0 adapter, then permanently freezing the base; subsequent tasks optimize only their LoRA, TSA, and ACL parameters. The scale subnet receives spatial context via a 3×3 depthwise convolution while the shift subnet operates without context, reflecting their distinct roles in localized volume change versus global distribution translation.

## Results

On MVTec-AD (15-class CIL), DeCoFlow achieves 98.40% I-AUC and 58.20% P-AP with 0.00% forgetting measure (FM), surpassing the joint-training upper bound of PatchCore (97.80% I-AUC) and CADIC (97.80% I-AUC). On VisA (12-class CIL), it reaches 93.00% I-AUC with 0.00% FM, outperforming ReplayCAD (90.30%) and CADIC (89.10%), though pixel-level localization (37.00% P-AP) trails CADIC (43.80%) on fine-defect classes. The method uses only 2.27M parameters per task (2.5% overhead) and achieves 100% routing accuracy on both benchmarks, with inference at 11.1 FPS and 989 MB peak memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeCoFlow outperforms all continual-learning baselines on image-level detection (I-AUC) across both datasets: on MVTec-AD it leads CADIC (97.20%) by +1.20%p and ReplayCAD (94.80%) by +3.60%p; on VisA it leads CADIC (89.10%) by +3.90%p and ReplayCAD (90.30%) by +2.70%p. On MVTec-AD pixel-level localization (P-AP), DeCoFlow (58.20%) is within 0.20%p of CADIC (58.40%) and 1.20%p of the joint PatchCore upper bound (59.40%), while on VisA it trails CADIC (43.80%) by 6.80%p, a gap concentrated on fine-defect classes (chewinggum, pcb3) attributed to ViT-B/16's coarse 14×14 token grid rather than the density model. The defining advantage is forgetting: DeCoFlow achieves 0.00% FM on all four metric-dataset combinations, whereas the next-best methods (UCAD, CADIC) still exhibit 1.0–1.5% FM, and fine-tuning baselines suffer 21–42% FM. Among the fine-tuning baselines, FT_SimpleNet (70.80% I-AUC on MVTec) and FT_CFA (62.30%) are the strongest but remain far below DeCoFlow, confirming that direct parameter updates to NFs cause severe density-manifold distortion. The joint-training references (PatchCore, CADIC) serve as upper bounds that DeCoFlow now exceeds on I-AUC while using only 2.27M parameters per task versus full-model retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeCoFlow builds on the affine coupling layer structure from RealNVP (Dinh et al., 2017) and the low-rank adapter mechanism from LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), but its core contribution is a structural insight: the invertibility and Jacobian validity of coupling layers are invariant to internal subnet architecture, enabling parameter isolation that was previously unavailable for density-estimation models. This departs from prior continual learning methods (EWC, replay, progressive networks) that target discriminative decision boundaries, and from prior continual anomaly detection work (CADIC, UCAD, ReplayCAD) that relies on regularization, coresets, or prompts. The paper positions itself as the first to exploit NF-specific architectural invariance for forgetting-free continual learning, validated by showing the same frozen+LoRA strategy fails on autoencoders, VAEs, and teacher-student architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structural zero-forgetting guarantee (FM=0.00% under correct routing) offers a principled alternative to the stability-plasticity trade-off that plagues regularization- and replay-based continual learning, with only 2.5% parameter overhead per task. However, the guarantee is conditional on accurate Mahalanobis routing, which becomes the limiting factor for highly similar classes or very long streams; a mixed 4×4 CIL test on VisA shows I-AUC dropping to 77.97%, and the VisA P-AP gap (37.00% vs. CADIC's 43.80%) on fine-defect classes highlights that feature granularity, not density modeling, is the current bottleneck. Open questions include whether the coupling-independence principle extends to other generative architectures, how routing robustness scales with task similarity, and whether the frozen-base assumption holds under domain shift rather than class shift.
