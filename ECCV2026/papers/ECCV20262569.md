## Problem

Standard prompt-guided inversion of text-to-3D generative models fails when the source prompt is out-of-distribution relative to the shape, causing generation trajectories to collapse into latent 'sink traps' where the model becomes insensitive to textual variations. Unlike 2D diffusion models where any approximate prompt suffices for inversion, 3D models exhibit a fundamental mismatch between their geometric expressivity and text-reasoning capacity, making high-fidelity text-driven 3D editing without auxiliary 2D priors an open problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh (voxelized into a 64³ structure and encoded via the TRELLIS 3D-VAE into a latent z₀) and a target edit text prompt, with no source prompt required. It produces a noise latent z₁ in the flow model's latent space and, after resampling with the edit prompt, an edited 3D mesh. Inversion operates exclusively on the first-stage structure model G_S (dictating global geometry), while the second-stage texture model G_L is kept fixed and seeded to isolate geometric variation.

## Method

The core insight is that inverting with an empty prompt (rather than an approximate text prompt) stabilizes the sampling trajectory by eliminating the out-of-distribution guidance term in classifier-free guidance, which otherwise acts as a high-variance gradient that drives the latent into sparse density regions. Concretely, the method performs Euler inversion of the rectified flow ODE using a null conditioning signal, optionally combined with Null-Text Inversion (NTI) optimization of the unconditional embeddings along the forward trajectory (Adam, lr 1e-4, 10 inner steps per sampling step, CFG scale 5, 50 timesteps). For editing, the inverted noise latent is resampled with the target edit prompt, optionally re-applying NTI to preserve structural integrity. This decouples the model's geometric representation power from its linguistic sensitivity, enabling open-vocabulary 3D retargeting (e.g., changing a dancing demon to a dancing princess) using only the native 3D generative model without any 2D inpainting, masking, or image-based priors.

## Results

On the DT4D dataset (200 non-rigid humanoid/animal characters), the proposed unconditional inversion + text-editing pipeline achieves SigLIP 0.1469, CLIP 0.2829, and VLM score 3.98/5, outperforming all four baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, TRELLIS's native second-stage editing, scores only 0.0797 SigLIP and 2.65 VLM, roughly half the proposed method's alignment. Computationally, the pipeline requires only ~9 s per edit (three sampling passes), compared to over an hour for Vox-E's per-scene optimization and 150+ rendered views for VoxHammer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against TRELLIS's native second-stage editing (the strongest baseline), it nearly doubles SigLIP (0.1469 vs. 0.0797) and VLM score (3.98 vs. 2.65), while also leading on CLIP (0.2829 vs. 0.2489). VoxHammer, which relies on 2D inpainting with Flux, fails on 67% of DT4D samples due to latent value explosion and, when it does succeed, scores the lowest VLM (1.33) and SigLIP (0.0240). The two image-prior baselines, Vox-E and Instant3DiT, both require manual mask creation and achieve VLM scores of only 1.84 and 1.99 respectively, well below the proposed method's 3.98. Notably, the proposed method is the only one that performs meaningful geometric retargeting (e.g., changing a dancing demon to a dancing princess) while preserving pose, whereas TRELLIS's native editing produces only texture changes and VoxHammer frequently generates implausible geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Null-Text Inversion (NTI) from the 2D image-editing literature to native 3D flow-matching models (specifically TRELLIS), but makes a critical departure: where 2D NTI assumes 'any reasonable and editable prompt' suffices for inversion, the authors show this assumption collapses for 3D models, where approximate prompts actively degrade reconstruction. The work also positions itself against existing 3D editing pipelines (VoxHammer, Nano3D, 3D-Latte) that rely on auxiliary 2D inpainting or multiview-consistency models, arguing that the native 3D generative prior alone is sufficient when inversion is performed unconditionally. Conceptually, the paper bridges the rectified-flow inversion literature (Euler inversion, CFG) with the 3D VAE/structured-latent representation space, identifying a previously uncharacterized failure mode ('sink traps') in the joint text-geometry embedding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of sink traps and the expressivity mismatch between geometric capacity and text-reasoning in 3D generative models reframes how practitioners should think about 3D editing pipelines: the bottleneck is not the model's ability to represent shapes but its sensitivity to out-of-distribution text. This opens the door to inversion and editing of arbitrary user-provided meshes without requiring a 'ground truth' prompt, which is practically important given that 3D data repositories rarely include paired text. Key limitations remain: the method is bounded by the generative model's distribution (extreme OOD poses still fail), the quantitative editing evaluation is on a modest sample size (30 views), and the approach is specific to flow-based 3D models. Open questions include automatic detection of OOD/sink states and extension to downstream tasks like 3D morphing.
