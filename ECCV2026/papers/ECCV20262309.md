## Problem

Feed-forward geometry transformers (e.g., VGGT, DUSt3R) produce per-view depth and point maps that must be assembled into 3D geometry via post-hoc fusion (TSDF, Poisson), discarding the joint multi-view prior encoded in the transformer's intermediate features. This predict-then-fuse design causes two failure modes: incomplete reconstructions under sparse views (unobserved regions remain geometrically underspecified) and accumulating per-view inconsistencies that degrade fidelity at scale with many views.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unstructured set of 2–24 RGB images (rescaled to 448 px on the longer side) with no camera calibration or pose input, operating in a normalized coordinate space with a fixed bounding box relative to the first view. It outputs a dense signed distance field on a 64³ grid over the scene volume, from which surfaces are extracted via marching cubes, along with camera parameters. Inference completes in under three seconds, and the SDF values are well-defined (not merely projective) throughout the volume, including unobserved regions.

## Method

Fus3D replaces VGGT's per-view 2D decoder heads with a learned volumetric extraction transformer that operates directly on the backbone's intermediate multi-view features. A set of learned canonical embeddings (16³ voxels, 2048-dim) conditioned on 3D voxel positions serve as initial queries; multi-view features from four intermediate backbone stages are progressively injected via interleaved 2D-to-3D cross-attention and 3D self-attention blocks (8 blocks total), lifting 2D tokens into a structured volumetric latent without explicit camera projection. A simple convolutional upsampling decoder then maps the 16³ latent to a 64³ SDF grid. Training uses a validity-aware supervision scheme with binary masks that gracefully handle non-watertight meshes and unobserved regions by degrading the SDF loss to an unsigned variant and suppressing the Eikonal regularizer where the ground-truth field is unreliable, enabling scalable training on large real-world datasets without expensive mesh preprocessing.

## Results

On OBJAVERSE (8 views, 170 test scenes), Fus3D achieves F_ε=0.83, F_0.5ε=0.51, Chamfer distance=0.021, and SDF MAE=0.004, outperforming VGGT_ft+TSDF (F_0.5ε=0.43, SDF MAE=0.023) and VGGT_ft+Poisson (F_0.5ε=0.38, CD=0.044) on all metrics except EMD. On DTU in a fully feed-forward (pose-free) setting, Fus3D improves Chamfer distance by 29% over both VolRecon and UFORecon in both favorable (CD 2.432 vs. 3.678/3.415) and unfavorable (CD 3.525 vs. 8.878/4.942) view configurations. The method also generalizes from 8 training views to 24 inference views without degradation, while predict-then-fuse baselines accumulate errors at higher view counts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against dedicated SDF methods on DTU (feed-forward, no GT poses), Fus3D outperforms VolRecon by 34% in Chamfer distance on favorable views (2.432 vs. 3.678) and by 60% on unfavorable views (3.525 vs. 8.878), and beats UFORecon by 29% in both settings (2.432 vs. 3.415 favorable; 3.525 vs. 4.942 unfavorable). Against the predict-then-fuse paradigm on OBJAVERSE, Fus3D surpasses VGGT_ft+TSDF on F_0.5ε (0.51 vs. 0.43), Chamfer distance (0.021 vs. 0.022), and SDF MAE (0.004 vs. 0.023), but trails slightly on EMD (0.019 vs. 0.014), indicating marginally better volumetric distribution matching for the TSDF baseline. Against VGGT_ft+Poisson, Fus3D dominates on every reported metric, with a 2.1× improvement in Chamfer distance (0.021 vs. 0.044) and a 35% gain in F_0.5ε (0.51 vs. 0.38). Notably, Fus3D remains competitive even when baselines are supplied with COLMAP-estimated poses, suggesting the joint multi-view latent prior compensates for the absence of explicit camera conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Fus3D builds directly on VGGT's feed-forward geometry transformer as a frozen backbone and on the structured 3D latent decoder from Xiang et al. for the final SDF head, but departs fundamentally from the predict-then-fuse paradigm that dominates the field (VGGT, DUSt3R, Spann3R, CUT3R). Instead of routing transformer features through per-view 2D decoder heads and assembling geometry via TSDF or Poisson fusion, it introduces a learned volumetric extraction module that lifts 2D multi-view features into a 3D latent grid via interleaved cross- and self-attention, drawing conceptual inspiration from spatial-memory approaches but generalizing them to full 3D positional queries. The validity-aware SDF supervision scheme (Eikonal masking, unsigned fallback) is a practical contribution distinct from prior SDF training recipes that assume watertight meshes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the joint multi-view prior assembled inside a geometry transformer's intermediate features is sufficient to regress a complete, well-defined SDF without per-view prediction or post-hoc fusion, addressing two persistent failure modes (sparse-view holes and error accumulation at scale) that have limited practical deployment of feed-forward geometry transformers. Limitations include the relatively low 64³ output resolution, over-smoothed surfaces, restricted camera setups in training data, and the requirement of a known bounding box at inference. Open questions include whether tighter integration with the backbone (beyond read-only feature access), higher-resolution sparse decoders, and iterative multi-scale extraction can close the gap to per-scene optimization methods while preserving the feed-forward speed advantage.
