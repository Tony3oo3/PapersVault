## Problem

Existing zero-shot video moment retrieval methods rely on query-to-video similarity (either query-frame or query-caption) to generate and score candidate spans, but these signals are corrupted by a modality gap between text and visual features and a language-style gap between human-written queries and MLLM-generated captions. These gaps produce flat, noisy, or inconsistent similarity trajectories that lead to unreliable span proposals and unstable retrieval results.

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video sampled at 0.5–1 fps (dataset-dependent) and a natural language query as input, and outputs a single temporal span (start and end frame indices) localizing the relevant moment. Frame features and per-frame caption features are extracted using LLaMA-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct, and the entire pipeline is training-free, requiring no dataset-specific annotations or fine-tuning. The method uses a single MLLM backbone for both captioning and the final re-ranking stage, reducing the model footprint to 11B parameters compared to 22B in prior multi-model approaches.

## Method

Self-SiMS replaces query-dependent similarity with query-independent intra-video self-similarity for span generation: it builds a unified temporal self-similarity matrix (TSM) by equally weighting frame-feature and caption-feature similarity maps, then computes boundary scores via a contrastive kernel that rewards within-side similarity and penalizes cross-side similarity around each frame. Candidate spans are generated using instance-wise dynamic thresholds adapted to intra-video context variability, eliminating manual threshold tuning. For span scoring, the method combines a Query-Matching Span Score (mean of top-k query-caption similarities within the span, robust to language-style noise) with a Self-Matching Span Score (mean TSM similarity between the key frame and all other frames in the span, capturing internal consistency). Finally, the top-k candidate spans are re-ranked by an MLLM-based Yes/No verification that directly evaluates representative frames against the query, leveraging cross-modal reasoning to further reduce both modality and language-style gaps.

## Results

On QVHighlights (test), Self-SiMS achieves R1@0.5 of 59.7, R1@0.7 of 42.2, mAP@0.5 of 59.2, and mAP@avg of 38.3, outperforming the strongest zero-shot baseline Moment-GPT by 1.4, 4.5, 4.1, and 3.3 points respectively. On Charades-STA, Self-SiMS attains the highest mIoU (41.9) among all zero-shot methods, surpassing Moment-GPT (36.5) and TFVTG (38.4). On TVR, Self-SiMS reaches R1@0.5 of 13.5 and mIoU of 20.2, exceeding the best TFVTG configuration (9.6 and 18.6) by a wide margin while requiring no dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest zero-shot competitor Moment-GPT [38], Self-SiMS wins on QVHighlights across all four metrics (e.g., R1@0.7: 42.2 vs 37.7) and on Charades-STA mIoU (41.9 vs 36.5), but loses on ActivityNet-Captions R1@0.5 (28.2 vs 31.1) and R1@0.7 (13.8 vs 14.9), suggesting Moment-GPT's query-caption similarity still captures some fine-grained temporal cues that self-similarity misses. Against TFVTG [41], Self-SiMS dominates on QVHighlights (59.7 vs 21.6 R1@0.5) and TVR (13.5 vs 9.6 R1@0.5), but TFVTG edges ahead on Charades-STA R1@0.3 (64.8 vs 62.7) due to its tendency to produce longer spans that overlap more loosely with ground truth. Notably, Self-SiMS as a zero-shot method surpasses fully supervised baselines Moment-DETR (52.9) and UMT (56.4) on QVHighlights R1@0.5, and outperforms all weakly supervised methods (CPL, CPI, CNM) by a large margin. On TVR, Self-SiMS achieves the best results across all metrics without dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning, whereas TFVTG requires per-dataset stride and max-stride-factor settings to reach its best configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the zero-shot video moment retrieval (ZMR) line of work, departing from prior methods (TFVTG, Moment-GPT) that generate and score candidate spans via query-frame or query-caption similarity. Its core conceptual move is to replace these query-dependent signals with query-independent intra-video temporal self-similarity for span generation, borrowing the contrastive-kernel boundary detection idea from the event boundary detection literature (UBOCO). For span scoring, it retains query-caption similarity but restricts to top-k frames and augments with a self-similarity-based consistency score, then applies MLLM-based re-ranking. The paper thus repurposes a well-studied temporal cue (self-similarity) in a training-free ZMR context where it had not previously been exploited for candidate generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly identifying and quantifying the modality and language-style gaps via the Inner-to-Outer Ratio (IOR), the paper provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding why existing ZMR methods produce unstable spans, and demonstrates that a query-agnostic signal can outperform query-dependent ones for boundary detection. The training-free, single-MLLM design (11B parameters) is practically attractive compared to multi-model pipelines like Moment-GPT (22B total). Open questions include whether the self-similarity approach degrades on very long or highly heterogeneous videos, whether the method can operate without MLLM-generated captions (purely visual self-similarity), and whether the top-k scoring heuristic generalizes to domains with very short ground-truth spans.
