## Problem

Existing motion style transfer methods treat style as a discrete category or perform one-shot transfer toward a single target, leaving fine-grained continuous control of style intensity unexplored. In production animation workflows, directors iteratively request 'more' or 'less' style, but no prior method provides a reliable monotonic slider that responds predictably to a scalar intensity parameter, especially when intermediate-intensity ground-truth motions are unavailable in training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262590/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a neutral content motion and a stylized endpoint motion (both as 3D joint-position sequences in the MDM feature space) plus a user-specified scalar intensity α ≥ 0, and produces a single stylized motion sequence whose duration matches the content motion. At α=0 the output approximates the content motion, at α=1 it approximates the style endpoint, and α>1 extrapolates beyond the trained range. The content motion is consumed as a frozen TMR embedding projected into a conditioning token, while the style reference defines a direction in the same embedding space that is scaled by α to form the style conditioning token.

## Method

The core mechanism computes a style direction d_s in a frozen TMR embedding space from endpoint pairs (instance, prototype, or hybrid mode), then constructs a continuous style condition z_α = z_c + α·d_s that is projected into a conditioning token for a pretrained MDM diffusion denoiser. Training uses only endpoint supervision (α=0 targets the content motion, α>0 targets the style motion) combined with two latent-space regularizers: a linearity loss L_lin that enforces g(m̂_α) ≈ α·g(m_s) along the style axis, and a monotonicity loss L_mono that penalizes decreases in projected style strength across sampled α pairs. The diffusion model is fine-tuned via lightweight adaptor layers only (frozen MDM backbone, frozen TMR encoder), with an endpoint-aware α sampling scheme that oversupervises the boundaries while still exposing the model to intermediate and extrapolated intensities. This design decouples motion realism (handled by the diffusion loss) from controllability geometry (handled by the latent regularizers), enabling smooth monotonic scaling without requiring any intermediate-intensity ground-truth motions.

## Results

On the main comparison (averaged over PerMo, Bandai-Namco, and Xia), the proposed method achieves the best control-oriented metrics: SRA 0.295, MonoViol 0.05, LinErr 0.280, and ExtraErr 1.109, outperforming both baselines on all four. MCM-LDM retains an advantage on motion realism (FMD 0.381 vs. 0.457) and content preservation (CRA 0.808 vs. 0.606). In the user study, the proposed method achieves a distinguishability score of 4.92/7 versus 2.36/7 for MCM-LDM, and a monotonic-pass rate of 0.50 versus 0.00, confirming that the slider produces perceptually ordered style intensities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: DeepMotionEditing (Aberman et al., TOG 2020) and MCM-LDM (Song et al., CVPR 2024). Against DeepMotionEditing, the proposed method wins decisively on every metric: FMD 0.457 vs. 0.588, CRA 0.606 vs. 0.290, SRA 0.295 vs. 0.060, MonoViol 0.05 vs. 0.25, LinErr 0.280 vs. 0.326, and ExtraErr 1.109 vs. 1.196. Against MCM-LDM, the proposed method wins on all four control-specific metrics (SRA 0.295 vs. 0.265, MonoViol 0.05 vs. 0.11, LinErr 0.280 vs. 0.358, ExtraErr 1.109 vs. 1.174) but concedes on realism and content preservation (FMD 0.457 vs. 0.381, CRA 0.606 vs. 0.808). The per-dataset breakdown (Table 3) shows the controllability advantage is most pronounced on PerMo (MonoViol 0.03 vs. 0.22) and Bandai-Namco (LinErr 0.259 vs. 0.368), while on Xia the MonoViol gap narrows (0.07 vs. 0.05). The user study further confirms the practical gap: MCM-LDM's stylization scores are nearly flat across intensities (3.56/3.59/3.46), whereas the proposed method shows clear monotonic increase (3.92/4.61/5.18), and participants could distinguish intensity levels far more reliably (4.92 vs. 2.36 on a 7-point scale).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the pretrained MDM diffusion backbone and TMR motion-language embeddings, repurposing them as a frozen style-direction space rather than a text-conditioning signal. It departs from prior motion style transfer methods (MCM-LDM, StyleMotif, DeepMotionEditing) that treat style as a categorical label or a single target instance, by reframing the problem as continuous scalar-intensity control along a learned latent direction. The endpoint-supervised training with linearity and monotonicity regularization is a new loss design not present in prior MST pipelines, and the over-reaction extrapolation benchmark extends the evaluation protocol beyond in-range transfer quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in animation workflows where directors iteratively request 'more' or 'less' style, and shows that a simple scalar slider with endpoint supervision can produce monotonic, extrapolatable style variation without intermediate ground-truth. The over-reaction benchmark is a useful addition for separating interpolation quality from true out-of-range behavior. However, the user study is underpowered (11 participants, 6 cases, no significant p-values), large-α generation still risks artifacts without biomechanical constraints, and the method is limited to reference-based endpoint pairs rather than text-guided or unseen-style control, leaving open whether the slider generalizes beyond the trained style geometry.
