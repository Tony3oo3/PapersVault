## Problem

Existing few-shot text prompting methods for Vision-Language Models optimize a single aggregate metric (e.g., validation accuracy) that is dominated by easily recognizable samples, causing prompts to saturate prematurely and yield only marginal gains as more labeled shots become available. Additionally, these methods fail to preserve shot-driven improvements when the resulting prompts are transferred to larger, heterogeneous VLMs, limiting their practical utility across model families.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of labeled RGB images (1–16 shots per class) from a K-way classification task and produces a class label per image via cosine similarity between the image embedding and ensembled text-prompt embeddings. The optimized output is a collection of natural-language prompt banks (one per class, each containing multiple prompts), which can be pre-embedded and reused across any CLIP-style VLM with a single forward pass at inference time, requiring no gradient updates or model-specific parameters.

## Method

TPB treats each class-wise prompt bank collection as a weak classifier and sequentially aggregates M such classifiers into a strong ensemble using the SAMME.R variant of AdaBoost. The weak learner, Greedy Prompt Composition (GPC), operates in two stages: it first selects the best shared template from 80 CLIP templates to initialize all class banks, then greedily inserts prompts (allowing duplicates to approximate continuous weighting in a discrete space) from a large pool of hand-crafted and LLM-generated descriptions, minimizing the weighted classification error at each step. At each boosting round, misclassified training examples receive increased weights, forcing subsequent GPC iterations to target hard samples. Image augmentation (random crops and flips, factor a=4) is applied to the few-shot set before each GPC call to prevent the ensemble from memorizing the limited training examples and to sustain informative errors across rounds.

## Results

On eleven classification benchmarks using OpenAI CLIP RN50, TPB achieves 63.8% average top-1 accuracy at 1-shot and 70.3% at 16-shot, outperforming the strongest text-based baseline ProAPO by 1.1 pp and 5.9 pp respectively. The shot-driven gain from 1 to 16 shots is 6.5 pp for TPB, compared to only 1.7 pp for ProAPO and 0.3 pp for LLMbo, demonstrating superior shot scalability. In cross-model transfer to ViT-L and ViT-H targets, TPB reaches 82.07% and 84.24% average accuracy, exceeding ProAPO by 2.06 pp and 1.85 pp while preserving shot-driven gains that other text-based methods lose after transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPB outperforms all text-based baselines in both 1-shot and 16-shot settings: at 1-shot it leads ProAPO (62.7%) by 1.1 pp and LLMbo (61.1%) by 2.7 pp; at 16-shot it leads ProAPO (64.4%) by 5.9 pp and LLMbo (61.4%) by 8.9 pp. Against the continuous prompt method CoOp, TPB wins at 1-shot (63.8% vs. 59.3%) but loses at 16-shot (70.3% vs. 73.2%), though CoOp's learned vectors are model-specific and cannot be transferred. PEZ, the gradient-based hard-prompt method, is substantially weaker (42.4% at 1-shot, 61.1% at 16-shot) and transfers catastrophically, dropping below zero-shot CLIP on the target model. In cross-model transfer (Table 2), TPB beats ProAPO by 2.06 pp on ViT-L targets (82.07% vs. 80.01%) and 1.85 pp on ViT-H targets (84.24% vs. 82.39%), while also exceeding CoOp+EFT (74.05%/72.73%) and PromptSRC+EFT (75.20%/74.16%) by 7–12 pp, with the key advantage that TPB requires only a single forward pass at inference versus multiple passes for EFT-based transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

TPB repurposes the classic AdaBoost/SAMME.R ensemble framework for the VLM text-prompting setting, where each class-wise prompt bank collection serves as a weak classifier. Prior text-prompting methods (DCLIP, CuPL, LLMbo, ProAPO) construct a single prompt set optimized on an aggregate validation metric, which the paper argues saturates prematurely on easy examples. TPB departs from this by sequentially reweighting misclassified samples and greedily composing new prompts (via GPC) that specifically resolve those errors, while retaining the discrete, human-readable nature of text prompts that enables cross-model transfer—unlike soft-prompting methods (CoOp, PromptSRC) whose learned vectors are locked to a specific model's representation space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that TPB preserves shot-driven accuracy gains when prompts are re-embedded into larger, heterogeneous VLMs (ViT-L, ViT-H), a property that existing text-based baselines like ProAPO lose after transfer. The failure analysis of PEZ—showing that gradient-optimized 'text' prompts degenerate into incoherent token sequences that transfer below zero-shot—offers a useful diagnostic: transferability depends on prompts remaining close to fluent natural language, not merely on being discrete tokens. Open questions include whether the fixed prompt pool constrains performance on very fine-grained or ambiguous classes, whether the construction cost scales acceptably to thousands of classes, and whether the boosting mechanism extends to non-classification VLM tasks such as detection or segmentation.
