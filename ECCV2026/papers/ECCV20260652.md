## Problem

Video world models (e.g., Cosmos, Gen3R) forecast future scenes by generating RGB frames in VAE latent space, but this photometric objective is poorly coupled to 3D structure, producing geometrically inconsistent predictions with broken layouts and mislocalized objects. The paper argues that geometry-foundation-model (GFM) features, which already encode multi-view geometry and metric structure, are a more suitable predictive state space, but two obstacles arise: standard velocity-prediction flow matching collapses in the high-dimensional (d=1024) VGGT feature space, and autoregressive rollout accumulates exposure bias that compounds over long horizons.

## Input / Output

The method takes k observed RGB frames as history (e.g., k=2 on KITTI), encodes them through a frozen VGGT encoder (layers 0–4) into deterministic geometry tokens of shape N×1024 (N = 5 special tokens + N_p spatial patch tokens), and autoregressively predicts future chunks of m=4 frames with stride 1. Outputs are decoded via the frozen VGGT decoder (layers 5–47) and original 3D heads into future depth maps, 3D point clouds, and camera trajectories. A notable property is that the predicted future chunks are concatenated with the observed present and jointly re-decoded, so the model can also refine present-time geometry beyond what VGGT alone achieves from the sparse context.

## Method

VGGT-World freezes the entire 1.3B VGGT backbone and trains only a 0.43B temporal flow transformer that predicts future VGGT tokens chunk-by-chunk via continuous-time flow matching. Two key design choices address the high-dimensional prediction challenge: (1) a z-prediction parameterization that directly predicts the clean target latent rather than the velocity field, yielding substantially higher signal-to-noise ratio in the 1024-dim space; and (2) a dual-stream-to-single-stream architecture (8 cross-attention blocks for temporal context absorption, then 8 self-attention blocks for spatial refinement) that separates causal reasoning from local denoising. To mitigate exposure bias during autoregressive rollout, a two-stage latent flow-forcing curriculum is used: Stage 1 trains with ground-truth history (teacher forcing), and Stage 2 conditions the model on partially denoised states from its own ODE rollout, with a mixing coefficient λ linearly annealed from 0 to 1 to progressively bridge the train-test gap without destabilizing learning.

## Results

On KITTI depth forecasting, VGGT-World achieves a mean AbsRel of 0.065 (short-term) and 0.098 (mid-term), with δ₁ of 94.0% and 89.4%, respectively—reducing AbsRel by 21% and 15% over the strongest prior baseline (DINO-Foresight: 0.082/0.115). On Cityscapes, it reaches AbsRel 0.078/0.126 and δ₁ 93.8%/87.3%, improving AbsRel by up to 32% over DINO-Foresight. On TartanAir point-cloud forecasting (text-free, pose-free, 1-view), VGGT-World achieves Accuracy 1.16, Completeness 4.53, and Chamfer Distance 2.85, substantially outperforming Gen3R on Accuracy (3.03) while remaining competitive on CD (2.78). The model runs 3.6–5× faster than Cosmos-12B and Gen3R with only 0.43B trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGGT-World outperforms all seven baselines on KITTI and Cityscapes depth forecasting. Against the strongest latent-space baseline DINO-Foresight [23], it reduces AbsRel by 21% (0.065 vs 0.082) on KITTI short-term and by 32% (0.078 vs 0.114) on Cityscapes short-term, while improving δ₁ by 2.4 and 5.2 percentage points respectively. Pixel-space video generators Cosmos-4B/12B [1] underperform even the trivial Copy-Last VGGT baseline, confirming that scaling video generation without 3D priors prioritizes photometric realism over geometric consistency. Geometry-aware Aether [43] improves over Cosmos but still trails VGGT-World by 32% in AbsRel on KITTI. On TartanAir point-cloud forecasting, VGGT-World (text-free, pose-free) achieves Accuracy 1.16 vs Gen3R's 3.03 [19], a 62% reduction, though Gen3R retains a slight edge on Completeness (2.54 vs 4.53) and CD (2.78 vs 2.85) in the 1-view setting; when Gen3R is given explicit pose conditioning, its performance degrades further (CD 5.75), while VGGT-World remains stable. VISTA-ft [15] on Cityscapes (AbsRel 0.124/0.153) is also clearly surpassed by VGGT-World (0.078/0.126).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant video-generation world-model paradigm (Cosmos, Gen3R, Aether) by arguing that the key bottleneck is the choice of state representation rather than the forecasting mechanism. It builds directly on frozen VGGT as a geometry foundation model, adopting its layer-4 latent tokens (d=1024) as the world state, and on rectified flow matching as the generative operator, with the dual-stream/single-stream transformer architecture borrowed from FLUX.1 Kontext. Conceptually, it extends the latent world-model line (DINO-Foresight, DINO-World) by replacing semantic DINO features with geometry-centric GFM features, and departs from standard flow matching by substituting v-prediction with z-prediction to handle the high-dimensional, weakly-compressed GFM latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that frozen GFM features are a viable and substantially more efficient predictive state for 3D geometry forecasting, requiring only 0.43B trainable parameters and no ground-truth depth supervision, which lowers the barrier for academic labs to build geometry world models. The finding that a 12B video-generation model (Cosmos) underperforms a trivial copy-last baseline on depth accuracy challenges the assumption that scaling photometric generation improves geometric consistency. Open questions include whether action-conditioned rollouts can be integrated, how the approach scales to longer horizons beyond the observed sweet spot (~frame 7), and whether the z-prediction + flow-forcing recipe generalizes to other GFM backbones (π³, MapAnything) or to interactive/embodied settings.
