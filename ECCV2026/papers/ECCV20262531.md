## Problem

Existing object-level 3D scene reconstruction benchmarks suffer from limited registration accuracy and significant inter-object penetrations, making them unreliable for evaluating physically-plausible reconstruction. Additionally, current single-object methods like SAM 3D process each object independently, lacking explicit reasoning about spatial inter-dependencies, which leads to inaccurate global layouts and imprecise relative poses in cluttered, contact-rich scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262531/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a single RGB image and per-object pixelwise segmentation masks (extracted by SAM 3) as input. SAM 3D first produces voxel-based shape predictions and 7-DOF poses (unit quaternion rotation, 3D translation, isotropic scale) for each detected object; MOD then outputs only residual pose corrections (Δrotation, Δtranslation, Δscale) that are added to SAM 3D's predictions, leaving the predicted geometry unchanged. The final output is a scene-level 3D arrangement of all objects with refined, physically-consistent poses.

## Method

Multi-Object Decoder (MOD) is a lightweight plug-in (~81M parameters) appended to SAM 3D that jointly refines poses of all N objects in a scene. It processes aggregated shape and pose tokens through K=3 transformer blocks, each containing: (i) per-object self-attention on pose tokens, (ii) multi-object self-attention over a flattened sequence of all objects' pose tokens for cross-object spatial reasoning, and (iii) multi-object cross-attention where pose tokens attend to shape tokens of all objects to ground pose predictions in geometry. The decoder outputs residual pose updates added to SAM 3D's predictions. Training is supervised on MessyKitchens-synthetic (10,800 rendered images from 1,800 contact-rich scenes) with a weighted loss combining Chamfer distance for shape, a quaternion alignment term (1−⟨q,q̂⟩²) for rotation, and L2 losses for translation and scale, with ground truth obtained via Sim(3) ICP alignment between predicted and GT scenes.

## Results

On MessyKitchens (in-distribution), MOD achieves 0.445 object-level IoU and 0.472 scene-level IoU, improving over SAM 3D (0.409 / 0.431) and substantially over MIDI (0.186 / 0.238) and PartCrafter (0.071 / 0.133). On the out-of-distribution HouseCat6D benchmark, MOD reaches 0.404 object IoU and 0.458 scene IoU, a +7.9% and +8.4% gain over SAM 3D respectively. MOD is trained exclusively on MessyKitchens-synthetic (10.8k rendered images), and all OOD evaluations on GraspNet-1B, HouseCat6D, and GraspClutter6D are zero-shot. For contact accuracy (PhySIC metrics), MOD achieves 70.0% precision and 66.1% F1, outperforming SAM 3D (66.0% / 55.8%) and MIDI (38.9% / 43.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOD consistently outperforms all three baselines across all four benchmarks and both object-level and scene-level metrics. Against SAM 3D (the closest competitor and the base model MOD extends), gains are most pronounced on HouseCat6D (+7.9% object IoU, +8.4% scene IoU) and MessyKitchens (+3.6% object IoU, +4.1% scene IoU), while on GraspNet-1B and GraspClutter6D the improvements are smaller but still positive (+0.8–2.1% IoU). Against MIDI, MOD achieves roughly 2× object IoU on MessyKitchens (0.445 vs 0.186) and 2.4× on HouseCat6D (0.404 vs 0.092), with similar margins on scene-level metrics. PartCrafter, the only method not requiring segmentation masks, lags far behind on all datasets (e.g., 0.071 vs 0.445 object IoU on MessyKitchens). Notably, SAM 3D alone already surpasses MIDI and PartCrafter by large margins (e.g., +19.3% scene IoU over MIDI on MessyKitchens), confirming that the single-object foundation is strong and MOD's contribution is specifically in scene-level pose refinement via multi-object attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM 3D single-object reconstruction framework by adding a Multi-Object Decoder (MOD) that introduces multi-object self-attention and cross-attention over pose and shape tokens, enabling joint scene-level reasoning that SAM 3D's independent per-object processing lacks. Conceptually, it departs from generative multi-instance approaches (MIDI, PartCrafter) that rely on diffusion transformers trained on synthetic scenes, instead adopting a lightweight refinement strategy on top of a strong single-object foundation model. On the data side, it positions itself against existing benchmarks (GraspNet-1B, HouseCat6D, GraspClutter6D, T-LESS) by introducing a normals-aware registration pipeline with a portable structured-light scanner, achieving substantially lower registration error and inter-object penetration than prior datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MessyKitchens provides the first benchmark where contact accuracy and physical plausibility can be rigorously evaluated at the object level, filling a gap that limits the reliability of prior datasets for robotics and animation applications. The MOD plug-in demonstrates that even a small (~81M parameter) attention module trained purely on synthetic data can yield consistent zero-shot improvements across diverse real-world benchmarks, suggesting a practical path to scene-aware reconstruction without retraining the base model. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to deformable objects, scenes with more than 8 objects, or categories far from kitchenware, and whether the modest per-object gains compound meaningfully in downstream physics-based simulation or manipulation tasks.
