## Problem

Existing radiance field primitives (Gaussians, surfels, triangles) have fixed geometric expressiveness determined at design time, so quality can only be improved by proliferating more primitives. This makes scalability inherently tied to primitive count, with no mechanism to adjust the fidelity of individual primitives at runtime without retraining or post-hoc compression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262342/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated multi-view RGB images with known camera poses (standard for image-based 3D reconstruction) and produces rendered novel-view images via tile-based rasterization. Unusually, the same trained model can be rendered at multiple quality levels at runtime simply by truncating the number of active Fourier coefficients (K=1 through K=6), yielding a continuous rate-distortion curve without any retraining. Each primitive carries 70 parameters (6 Fourier amplitudes + 6 phases + center, rotation, opacity, circumradius, sharpness, and degree-3 spherical harmonics).

## Method

Fourier Splatting replaces the fixed disc/ellipsoid boundary of planar surfels with a closed curve parameterized as the modulus of a complex Fourier polynomial r_i(θ) = R_i|Σ c_{i,k} e^{ikθ}|, where each frequency k adds k-fold angular variation and the k=0 term recovers a standard disc. A squared-ℓ₁ amplitude normalization guarantees the boundary never exceeds the circumradius R_i. For differentiable rendering, a power window function provides compact support, and a straight-through estimator (STE) with a smooth sigmoid-softplus surrogate extends gradients beyond the hard boundary so Fourier coefficients can learn complex shapes rather than relying solely on circumradius inflation. Optimization uses the MCMC framework of 3DGS-MCMC with a custom densification strategy called HYDRA, which identifies high-gradient primitives and either performs a scale-preserving geometric split or invokes a pre-trained MLP to decompose multi-lobed boundaries into simpler angular segments, preserving the target distribution P(g). The Horner recurrence evaluates the Fourier boundary in K−1 complex multiply-adds, keeping rendering at 135 FPS for 960K primitives.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, Fourier Splatting achieves 27.65 dB PSNR, 0.824 SSIM, and 0.193 LPIPS on average, ranking first among all planar-primitive methods across every metric. On Tanks and Temples, it achieves 24.15 dB PSNR, 0.868 SSIM, and 0.137 LPIPS, delivering the best LPIPS among all tested baselines (including volumetric methods). At a matched 960K primitive budget on Tanks and Temples, Fourier Splatting (K=6) reaches 23.79 dB PSNR and 0.853 SSIM versus 22.94 dB and 0.827 for 2DGS, at 135 FPS and 2.08 GB VRAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among planar-primitive methods, Fourier Splatting outperforms Triangle Splatting by 0.67 dB PSNR on the Mip-NeRF 360 average (27.65 vs 26.98) and 2DGS by 0.81 dB (27.65 vs 26.84), while also achieving the best SSIM and LPIPS in that category. Against volumetric methods, it surpasses 3DGS on all three Mip-NeRF 360 metrics and matches or exceeds DBS on average LPIPS (0.193 vs 0.234) despite using a purely planar primitive. On Tanks and Temples, Fourier Splatting achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.137) among all baselines, beating even DBS (0.140) and Triangle Splatting (0.143), though it trails DBS (24.85) and BBSplat (25.12) in PSNR. Compared to the MCMC-based 3DGS-MCMC, Fourier Splatting is slightly behind on Mip-NeRF 360 PSNR (27.65 vs 27.84) but competitive on SSIM and LPIPS. In the scalability analysis against Octree-GS, Fourier Splatting degrades more gracefully when reducing detail, as quality loss comes from truncating Fourier coefficients rather than removing entire primitives, avoiding the focal-object vanishing artifacts seen in count-based LoD methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

Fourier Splatting inherits the oriented surfel parameterization and ray-plane intersection machinery from 2DGS, the tile-based alpha compositing from 3DGS, the power-window opacity profile from Triangle Splatting, and the MCMC densification/relocation framework from 3DGS-MCMC. Its central departure is replacing the fixed Gaussian or hard-edged disc boundary with a Fourier-encoded closed curve, making each primitive's shape a continuous, truncatable parameter rather than a fixed architectural choice. This positions the work at the intersection of two previously orthogonal lines of research—primitive expressiveness (GES, DBS, 3DCS, Triangle Splatting) and level-of-detail scalability (Octree-GS, LODGE, LOD-GS)—by embedding the LoD axis inside the primitive itself rather than in the primitive population.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that per-primitive expressiveness can be traded against bitrate at runtime without retraining, offering a principled alternative to the dominant prune-and-replace LoD paradigm that can cause focal objects to vanish entirely. However, the quality degradation is steep (PSNR drops from 22.4 to 12.7 going from K=6 to K=1 in the V3C compression experiment), and the method prioritizes peak fidelity over compression efficiency, leaving open whether scale regularization or learned coefficient quantization can close the gap. The 41-minute training time and 2.08 GB VRAM at a matched 960K budget (vs. 12 min / 1.4 GB for 2DGS) also raise practical adoption questions for large-scale or real-time training pipelines.
