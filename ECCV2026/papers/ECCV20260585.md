## Problem

Existing streaming MLLMs interleave perception and generation, which causes two critical failures in multi-turn video interaction: Memory Erosion, where early visual evidence is forgotten as the stream grows, and a Serialization Bottleneck, where autoregressive decoding halts further video ingestion. This paper addresses both by maintaining a persistent segment-level memory and decoupling input ingestion from text generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260585/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a continuously arriving video stream segmented into chunks (up to 64 frames per segment, segments up to 60 s) and text questions that arrive at arbitrary segment boundaries under strict streaming causality (only past segments and dialogue history are visible). For each visual segment it emits a compact memory note; for each question it produces a chain-of-thought rationale followed by a final answer, all conditioned on the accumulated memory bank and prior dialogue turns.

## Method

Think While Watching writes one segment-level memory note per arriving visual segment into a persistent memory bank, and answers each query by letting attention implicitly retrieve relevant notes from the bank. A custom segment-level streaming causal mask enforces strict causality across the interleaved received/generated unit sequence, while a decoupled streaming MRoPE positional encoding assigns independent position indices to input and output streams so that new segments can be ingested even while the model is mid-decode. Training follows a three-stage curriculum (single-round CoT, multi-round CoT, long-range with distractors and uncertainty handling) on a synthesized streaming CoT dataset. At inference, a dual KV cache pipeline overlaps watching and thinking, and an adaptive attention backend (Flash Attention for standard causal steps, memory-efficient attention for custom-masked steps) preserves segment-level causality while keeping decoding fast.

## Results

On StreamingBench with Qwen3-VL-4B, TWW single-turn S3 achieves 60.04% overall accuracy (+1.52% over the Thinking baseline of 58.52%), and on OVO-Bench it reaches 55.02% (+4.32% over 50.70%). With the 8B backbone, gains are larger: 62.04% on StreamingBench (+3.83%) and 57.07% on OVO-Bench (+3.25%). In the multi-turn protocol, TWW maintains competitive accuracy (57.40% on StreamingBench, 51.80% on OVO-Bench for 4B) while reducing average output tokens by 56.10% and 45.80% respectively. Naive streaming without aligned training collapses: Thinking online drops to 18.13% on StreamingBench and 16.21% on OVO-Bench versus 58.52% and 50.70% offline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against open-source streaming baselines, TWW (4B, single-turn S3) substantially outperforms all: 60.04% vs. StreamAgent-7B (57.02%), Dispider-7B (53.12%), VideoLLM-online-8B (32.48%), and Flash-VStream-7B (24.04%) on StreamingBench overall accuracy. On OVO-Bench, TWW 8B single-turn S3 (57.07%) surpasses StreamForest-7B (55.57%), StreamAgent-7B (49.40%), Dispider-7B (41.78%), and Flash-VStream-7B (33.61%). Against closed-source offline models, TWW remains below Gemini 1.5 Pro (70.26% StreamingBench, 63.00% OVO-Bench) and GPT-4o (64.10%, 59.54%), but the gap narrows considerably with the 8B backbone (62.04% and 57.07%). The most striking comparison is against naive streaming: Thinking online collapses to 18.13% on StreamingBench (−40.39% vs. offline), while TWW multi-turn S3 recovers to 57.40% while simultaneously cutting output tokens by 56%. The multi-turn protocol also reveals that TWW achieves near-offline accuracy (57.40% vs. 58.52% offline) with only 302.56 average tokens versus 689.22 for the Thinking baseline, demonstrating that segment-level memory enables efficient multi-turn reasoning without sacrificing quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant interleaved perception-generation paradigm in streaming video MLLMs (VideoLLM-online, StreamChat, LiveCC) by decoupling continuous visual ingestion from autoregressive text decoding via independent positional encodings and a dual KV cache. It builds on MRoPE from Qwen2-VL but extends it with a split input/output position scheme, and on KV-cache retrieval/compression work (StreamKV, V-REX, LiveVLM) by replacing token-level cache management with explicit segment-level memory notes written by the model itself. The three-stage streaming CoT training strategy and the segment-level causal mask are novel contributions that enforce strict streaming causality in a way prior interleaved systems did not formalize.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that naive multi-turn streaming collapses accuracy by over 40% relative to offline inference (across 2B/4B/8B scales) quantifies a gap that the field has underappreciated, and the proposed segment-level memory mechanism recovers most of that loss while cutting output tokens by 56% in multi-turn settings—a practically significant efficiency gain for deployment. The observation that streaming-aligned training transfers to offline long-video benchmarks (Video-MME +4.5%, LV-Bench +4.2%) suggests that causal streaming supervision is a useful regularizer beyond its immediate streaming use. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hours-long streams without memory-bounded eviction, how memory note quality degrades under adversarial or highly dynamic content, and whether the decoupled positional encoding generalizes to architectures beyond the Qwen3-VL family.
