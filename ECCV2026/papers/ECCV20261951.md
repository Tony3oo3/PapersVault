## Problem

Drone images captured from varying altitudes and oblique viewpoints exhibit complex nonlinear spatial transformations that distort structural dependencies when embedded in flat Euclidean spaces, causing cross-modal misalignment in image-text retrieval. Existing non-Euclidean approaches (e.g., hyperbolic embeddings) treat the manifold as a global embedding geometry but do not explicitly model second-order structural correlations grounded in SPD statistics, leaving a gap in geometry-aware structural alignment for aerial scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261951/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a drone RGB image (3×H×W) and a natural language caption as input, processing them through a RestV2-Base vision encoder and BERT-base-uncased text encoder respectively, both projected to a shared 512-dimensional space. The structural branch additionally consumes intermediate feature maps from the 4th stage of the visual encoder to compute covariance descriptors. Output is bidirectional retrieval: given an image, the top-K most relevant captions are returned, and given a text query, the top-K most relevant images are returned, evaluated via Recall@K and mean Recall.

## Method

SARA introduces two complementary modules operating on the SPD manifold. The MSFE module computes a second-order covariance descriptor from intermediate visual features, then applies a hierarchical sequence of manifold-aware layers (BiMap for SPD-to-SPD projection, ReEig for eigenvalue rectification, ReCov for off-diagonal correlation enhancement, and LogEig for tangent-space mapping) to produce a structure-aware visual descriptor; an analogous pipeline generates a textual structural representation. The LESA module then performs geometry-consistent alignment in the Log-Euclidean tangent space via Marginal Distribution Alignment (matching Fréchet means across modalities) and Conditional Distribution Alignment (pairwise structural matching for semantically corresponding samples), combined with bidirectional contrastive semantic losses and a learnable fusion module that concatenates semantic and vectorized structural features for a second-stage contrastive alignment. A theoretical result proves that minimizing the combined alignment loss upper-bounds the expected matching error under an L-Lipschitz similarity assumption, ensuring convergence.

## Results

On the ERA DIT benchmark, SARA achieves a mean recall (mR) of 48.36, surpassing the strongest Euclidean baseline HCCM (39.93) by +8.43 and the best manifold-based method HyCoCLIP (22.31) by +26.05. On UDV DIT, SARA reaches mR 22.16, improving over HCCM (19.33) by +2.83 and HyCoCLIP (10.42) by +11.74. On remote sensing datasets, SARA attains mR 29.18 on RSICD and 40.68 on RSITMD, outperforming the next-best methods SIPN (28.58) and UniAdapter (39.23) respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SARA outperforms all 16 baselines on both drone benchmarks. Against the strongest Euclidean method HCCM (ACM MM 2025), SARA gains +8.43 mR on ERA (48.36 vs. 39.93) and +2.83 mR on UDV (22.16 vs. 19.33), with particularly large I2T R@1 improvements (+4.05 on ERA, +0.12 on UDV). Among CNN-RNN baselines, VCSR (TGRS 2024) is the closest competitor on UDV (mR 20.67), yet SARA still leads by +1.49 mR. The two non-Euclidean baselines—MERU (ICML 2023, hyperbolic) and HyCoCLIP (ICLR 2025, curvature-regularized)—substantially underperform SARA (ERA mR 20.18/22.31 vs. 48.36; UDV mR 9.12/10.42 vs. 22.16), indicating that simply adopting a curved embedding space without explicit SPD structural modeling is insufficient for drone retrieval. On remote sensing datasets (Table 4), SARA edges out SIPN on RSICD (mR 29.18 vs. 28.58) and UniAdapter on RSITMD (40.68 vs. 39.23), confirming generality beyond drone-specific scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

SARA extends the SPD manifold learning lineage (SPDNet, Bures-Wasserstein normalization) from unimodal recognition tasks into the cross-modal retrieval setting, repurposing hierarchical SPD transformations (BiMap, ReEig, ReCov, LogEig) as a structural feature extractor for both image and text. It departs from recent non-Euclidean cross-modal methods (MERU, HyCoCLIP) that embed representations in hyperbolic space for semantic-level alignment, instead grounding alignment in second-order covariance statistics on the SPD manifold and performing distribution matching in the tangent space. The framework also builds on standard contrastive retrieval architectures (CLIP-style bidirectional InfoNCE) but augments them with Riemannian geometric constraints, positioning itself as a structural complement to purely semantic embedding approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that for viewpoint-heavy aerial imagery, modeling second-order spatial correlations as SPD matrices and aligning them under a Log-Euclidean metric yields substantial gains over both Euclidean and hyperbolic baselines, suggesting that the choice of manifold geometry and what is encoded on it (structure vs. semantics) matters as much as the embedding space itself. The ablation finding that textual SPD features alone degrade performance is a useful caution: not all modalities benefit equally from second-order geometric modeling. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-aerial domains with less pronounced spatial structure, whether learnable Riemannian metrics could further adapt to scene-specific curvature, and whether the eigendecomposition overhead scales to very high-resolution imagery.
