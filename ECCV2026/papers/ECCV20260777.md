## Problem

Existing two-stage RMOT methods that incorporate MLLMs treat them as caption generators, requiring autoregressive text decoding followed by an external text-matching module to produce referring decisions. This paradigm introduces significant inference latency, adds architectural complexity, and underutilizes the MLLM's native vision-language alignment by forcing it through an indirect generative bottleneck rather than leveraging its discriminative capacity directly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260777/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-candidate image crops (resized to 320×320) along with a natural-language referring expression and normalized bounding-box coordinates embedded in the prompt. It outputs a continuous matching probability per candidate via softmax over the Yes/No token logits, which drives a two-stage routing: high-confidence candidates are accepted or rejected immediately, while ambiguous ones (probability between 0.2 and 0.8) are refined in a video mode that jointly evaluates K=4 past frames from a memory bank. The final tracked trajectories are produced by an off-the-shelf tracker filtered by these binary decisions.

## Method

YesTrack reformulates referring as a binary image-text matching problem: instead of generating captions, it extracts the raw logits for the 'Yes' and 'No' tokens from a single forward pass of Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct and applies a two-token softmax to obtain a continuous confidence score, trained with standard BCE loss. A two-stage verification pipeline routes candidates efficiently—Stage 1 (frame mode) scores each crop against the expression, and Stage 2 (video mode) refines uncertain cases using temporal context from a memory bank. Two lightweight temporal consistency constraints are added at inference: Temporal Confidence Prior (TCP) biases the current-frame score upward when the identity was confidently matched across all previous K frames, and Temporal Reference Propagation (TRP) skips MLLM invocation on non-key frames by inheriting the most recent key-frame score, reducing redundant verification by a factor of Δ. The same discriminative paradigm extends to generic MOT (YesTrack-MOT), where the MLLM performs pairwise image-image identity verification between track crops and detection crops, replacing embedding-based ReID, combined with distance gating and Hungarian assignment.

## Results

On Refer-KITTI, YesTrack with TempRMOT* achieves the best overall HOTA of 54.00 and AssA of 66.57, surpassing the strongest end-to-end method DKGTrack (HOTA 52.08) by ~2 points. With the self-contained YesTrack-MOT tracker, it attains the best DetA of 46.84 and DetRe of 62.72. On the more challenging Refer-KITTI-V2, YesTrack-MOT leads with HOTA 43.75 and DetA 37.04, outperforming DKGTrack (HOTA 35.26) by over 8 points. For generic MOT on KITTI, YesTrack-MOT achieves HOTA 45.10, outperforming OC-SORT (41.34) and ByteTrack (40.83) under identical RF-DETR detections.

## Baselines & Comparisons

YesTrack outperforms all end-to-end baselines on Refer-KITTI: it beats DKGTrack (HOTA 52.08) by 1.92 points and TempRMOT (51.15) by 2.85 points, while also achieving the best AssA (66.57 vs. 66.04 for DKGTrack). Among two-stage methods, YesTrack surpasses ReferGPT(O) (HOTA 46.36) by 7.64 points and iKUN (44.56) by 9.44 points, demonstrating that the discriminative MLLM verifier is substantially more effective than caption-generation-based referring. On Refer-KITTI-V2, the gap widens: YesTrack-MOT (HOTA 43.75) exceeds DKGTrack (35.26) by 8.49 points and ReferGPT (30.12) by 13.63 points, indicating stronger generalization to diverse and ambiguous expressions. For generic MOT on KITTI, YesTrack-MOT (HOTA 45.10) outperforms OC-SORT (41.34), ByteTrack (40.83), TrackTrack (40.24), and BoT-SORT (38.92) under the same RF-DETR detections, confirming that MLLM-based pairwise verification is a viable replacement for embedding-based ReID. The only metric where YesTrack trails is AssA on Refer-KITTI with YesTrack-MOT (60.03 vs. 66.57 with TempRMOT*), reflecting the weaker tracking backbone rather than the referring module.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage RMOT paradigm (iKUN, ReferGPT) that employs MLLMs as autoregressive caption generators followed by external text-matching modules, instead reformulating referring as a binary Yes/No discriminative task that directly reads decision-token logits. Conceptually, it draws on the growing practice in LLM/agent literature of using large models as lightweight binary verifiers (e.g., Reflexion, Think-on-Graph) and transplants this idea into the video tracking domain. The tracking backbone is deliberately kept off-the-shelf, but the paper also instantiates a minimal MOT tracker (YesTrack-MOT) that replaces embedding-based ReID with the same MLLM-based pairwise image-image verification, extending the discriminative paradigm beyond referring into data association.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that MLLM-based tracking no longer requires autoregressive decoding, reducing per-candidate inference to a single forward pass and making real-time deployment more feasible even with a 2B-parameter model. The temporal consistency constraints (TCP, TRP) are architecture-agnostic and could be adopted by other two-stage RMOT pipelines. However, the two-stage design inherits tracking errors, the heuristic TCP is not learned, and TRP can propagate a single bad key-frame decision across multiple frames; the paper itself flags a more tightly coupled, learnable temporal module as future work. Evaluation is confined to KITTI-derived benchmarks, leaving open whether the discriminative paradigm generalizes to more diverse driving or indoor video settings.
