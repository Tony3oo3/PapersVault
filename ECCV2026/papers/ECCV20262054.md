## Problem

In Continual Generalized Category Discovery, unlabeled sessions contain ambiguous samples near evolving decision boundaries that existing pipelines force into hard pseudo-labels (known or novel), injecting persistent label noise and biasing learning toward familiar categories. In a continual, non-rehearsal setting, these early mis-assignments compound across sessions because past data cannot be revisited, causing underutilization of the most informative hard samples and progressive degradation of both old-class retention and new-class discovery.

## Input / Output

The model is first trained offline on labeled images from 50% of categories (80% of each class's training samples) using a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 backbone. It then receives sequential unlabeled sessions (each a batch of RGB images at the dataset's native resolution) containing a mixture of previously seen and newly emerging classes, without storing any past data. Outputs are per-class accuracy scores (New, Old, All) evaluated after each session, along with aggregate forgetting (M_f) and discovery (M_d) metrics across the full session sequence.

## Method

The framework introduces two cooperating modules on top of a teacher-student C-GCD baseline. Virtual Category Learning (VCL) identifies confusing samples via a Potential Category set built from top-2 prediction gap and teacher-student label disagreement, then assigns them to a temporary virtual category whose weight vector is generated by a self-attention transformer layer; the cross-entropy loss excludes PC-set logits so ambiguous samples contribute gradients without enforcing premature semantic commitment. Expanded Neighborhood Contrastive Learning (ENCL) extends standard neighborhood contrastive loss by incorporating neighbors-of-neighbors (m-nearest neighbors of each immediate neighbor) with an adaptive margin, enlarging the positive pool to capture indirect semantic relations that stabilize feature separation across sessions. The total objective is L_VC + λ₁·L_EN + λ₂·L_baseline, optimized end-to-end with weak/strong augmentation views fed through the ViT backbone.

## Results

On the final online session (Session-5), the proposed method achieves 61.28% All-accuracy on CIFAR-100, 53.25% on Tiny-ImageNet, and 76.72% on ImageNet-100, outperforming the strongest baseline Happy by 1.29%, 1.26%, and 1.74% respectively. The discovery metric M_d reaches 56.61 on C100 and 40.56 on Tiny, surpassing the next-best method (Happy) by 10.2–15.2%. Forgetting M_f is 27.94 on C100 (best among all methods) and 9.17 on Tiny (nearly matching Happy's 8.66), indicating strong retention of previously learned classes while incorporating new ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines on All-accuracy at Session-5 across all three datasets. The closest competitor is Happy [15] (the underlying C-GCD baseline), which the method surpasses by 1.29% on C100 (61.28 vs. 59.99), 1.26% on Tiny (53.25 vs. 51.99), and 1.74% on IN100 (76.72 vs. 74.98). Against GM [30], the gap widens to 7.17% on C100 and 6.17% on IN100, while MetaGCD [27] trails by 5.50% on C100. On the discovery metric M_d, the method leads Happy by 5.25 points on C100 (56.61 vs. 51.36) and 5.33 points on Tiny (40.56 vs. 35.23), confirming stronger novel-class identification. On forgetting M_f, the method achieves the best score on C100 (27.94 vs. Happy's 29.40) but is marginally worse on Tiny (9.17 vs. 8.66), suggesting a slight trade-off in old-class retention on that dataset. SimGCD [26] and VanillaGCD [23] show severe degradation on New-class accuracy (often below 5%), highlighting the fragility of hard pseudo-labeling under continual drift, whereas the proposed virtual-category mechanism maintains New accuracy above 30% in all sessions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Virtual Category Learning (VCL), originally proposed for semi-supervised dense prediction with limited labels [3], to the continual generalized category discovery setting, repurposing temporary virtual categories as a buffer for ambiguous unlabeled samples rather than a mechanism for handling label scarcity. It also extends the Expanded Neighborhood Contrastive Learning (ENCL) component from the static GCD method AMEND [2] into a continual regime, adding neighbors-of-neighbors and an adaptive margin to handle evolving representation spaces. The framework is instantiated on top of existing C-GCD baselines (Happy [15], MetaGCD [27]), positioning itself as a plug-in module that addresses the specific failure mode of premature pseudo-label commitment under continual drift, rather than proposing a new training paradigm from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The virtual-category mechanism offers a principled, low-overhead way to handle the pervasive ambiguity in unlabeled continual streams without the error compounding that hard pseudo-labels introduce, which is a practical concern for long-lived open-world systems. The 10-stage experiment and the 10–15% gain in discovery metric (M_d) suggest the approach sustains performance where prior methods degrade, though the overall accuracy improvements (1.3–1.7%) are moderate. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ViT backbones and the three benchmark datasets, how it behaves under more extreme class imbalance or very long session sequences, and whether the virtual-category buffer can be made fully self-regulating without the hand-tuned ambiguity threshold.
