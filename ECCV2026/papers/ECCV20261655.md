## Problem

Urban wind comfort assessment requires time-resolved CFD simulations that are computationally expensive (minutes to hours) and non-differentiable, making extensive design exploration and gradient-based layout optimization impractical. Existing neural surrogates either predict only temporally averaged fields—insufficient for comfort criteria defined as exceedance probabilities over threshold speeds—or struggle to maintain accuracy over long rollouts when trained from scratch on limited domain-specific data.

## Input / Output

The model takes a binary building footprint mask (256×256), a scalar inlet speed (0.1–20 m/s), and a domain size L (900–1400 m) as input. The footprint and inlet condition are encoded as a single conditioning frame (t=0) prepended to the sequence, with inlet speed in the red channel, zeros in green, and the fluid mask in blue. The output is a 112-frame time-resolved velocity field sequence at 256×256 resolution, where each frame encodes horizontal (u) and vertical (v) velocity components in the red and green channels respectively, rescaled to [−1,1] by a dataset-wide maximum speed; the blue channel carries the fluid mask. For inverse optimization, the same model additionally outputs updated continuous building coordinates.

## Method

WinDiNet fine-tunes LTX-Video, a 2B-parameter latent video diffusion transformer, on 13,000 procedurally generated 2D incompressible Euler simulations. Three key adaptations distinguish it from the pretrained backbone: (i) full fine-tuning of the DiT (outperforming LoRA by 5.9% VRMSE), (ii) replacing text-prompt conditioning with scalar embeddings via Fourier features and a small MLP projected into the transformer's cross-attention space, and (iii) VAE decoder fine-tuning augmented with physics-informed losses (divergence penalty, no-penetration constraint, and distance-weighted MSE near building walls) applied in a short pre-training stage. The model generates all 112 frames jointly in a single rectified-flow denoising pass requiring only 2 steps (~0.32 s on one H200 GPU), and because the entire pipeline is differentiable, a soft rasterizer maps continuous building coordinates to occupancy masks so that gradients from a composite wind-comfort loss (penalizing speeds above 15 m/s, above 5 m/s, and below 1 m/s) can backpropagate through the frozen surrogate to optimize building positions.

## Results

The best WinDiNet variant (Dec. FT Physics, scalar conditioning, full fine-tuning) achieves VRMSE 0.520, MAE 1.01 m/s, MRE 3.33%, and W1 0.83 on the 2,000-simulation test set, outperforming the strongest neural-operator baseline (RNO: VRMSE 0.563, MAE 1.19) by 7.6% in VRMSE and 15% in MAE. The only metric where a baseline retains a marginal lead is spectral divergence (OFormer 1.52 vs. WinDiNet 1.54). Inference of a full 112-frame 256×256 velocity sequence takes ~0.32 s on a single H200 GPU with 2 denoising steps, roughly three orders of magnitude faster than the incompressible Euler CFD solver used for data generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WinDiNet (Dec. FT Physics) beats all six neural-operator baselines on VRMSE, MAE, MRE, and W1. Against the strongest baseline RNO [28] (VRMSE 0.563), WinDiNet improves VRMSE by 7.6% (0.520 vs. 0.563) and MAE by 15% (1.01 vs. 1.19 m/s). The one-shot predictors AFNO [11] and FNO [25] (VRMSE ≈ 0.61) are outperformed by roughly 15% in VRMSE, while the autoregressive frame-to-frame models U-Net [39] and Poseidon [13] trail by a wide margin (VRMSE 1.467 and 0.781 respectively). OFormer [24] (VRMSE 0.585) is the closest competitor and retains a marginal 1.3% lead on spectral divergence (1.52 vs. 1.54), but WinDiNet surpasses it on every other metric. Notably, WinDiNet achieves these gains while operating through a lossy VAE bottleneck that none of the baselines require, and the paper shows that removing pretraining degrades VRMSE from 0.52 to 0.57 (DiT only) or 0.92 (DiT + VAE from scratch), confirming the pretrained video prior contributes substantially to the accuracy advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes LTX-Video, a 2B-parameter latent video diffusion transformer pretrained on natural video, as a differentiable surrogate for 2D incompressible urban wind simulation. It departs from the neural PDE solver line (FNO, AFNO, OFormer, RNO, Poseidon) by leveraging the temporal coherence and physical priors already encoded in a video generation backbone rather than training a purpose-built operator from scratch. The VAE adaptation strategy (frozen encoder + decoder fine-tuning with physics-informed losses) and the scalar-conditioning mechanism (Fourier-feature embeddings replacing text cross-attention) are novel adaptations specific to the physics-simulation use case, building on the broader trend of conditioning video diffusion on physical parameters (Force Prompting, DiffPhy, PhysCtrl) but targeting quantitative PDE agreement rather than perceptual plausibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3-orders-of-magnitude speedup over CFD combined with end-to-end differentiability enables a design workflow—gradient-based inverse optimization of building layouts for pedestrian wind comfort—that is impractical with conventional solvers. The finding that a general-purpose video model outperforms specialized neural PDE solvers (by 7.6% VRMSE over RNO) despite operating through a lossy VAE bottleneck suggests that pretrained physical priors in video models are more transferable to quantitative simulation than previously demonstrated. Key limitations include the 2D formulation, reliance on synthetic incompressible Euler data, and a single PDE family; the authors note that 3D extension is bottlenecked by data generation cost, and that autoregressive video models may introduce error accumulation problematic for inverse optimization.
