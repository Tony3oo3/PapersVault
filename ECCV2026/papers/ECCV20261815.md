## Problem

Implicit Neural Representations suffer from a capacity-convergence gap: high-capacity activations (SIREN, WIRE, FINER) offer rich spectral control but are acutely sensitive to initialization, while stable choices underfit high-frequency content. Because activation selection and initialization are tightly coupled per layer, manual tuning is unreliable and prior automated methods (e.g., MIRE's greedy layer-wise search) cannot guarantee global optimality. No principled framework existed to jointly optimize all layer-wise architectural decisions as a single global problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target signal in any of five modalities (2D image coordinates→RGB, 1D audio time→amplitude, 3D voxel coordinates→occupancy, spatiotemporal PDE coordinates→field value, or NeRF 3D position + view direction→radiance) and produces an optimized per-layer INR configuration: a categorical activation choice from a task-specific set, activation-specific continuous parameters (e.g., ω₀, s₀), a binary SIREN-style initialization flag, a per-layer learning rate, and a global positional-encoding toggle with scale. The configuration space is a 4-layer mixed discrete-continuous vector; each evaluation requires full end-to-end training (10k–30k iterations), making the objective an expensive black-box function.

## Method

OptiINR recasts INR configuration as a global optimization over a structured mixed-variable space and navigates it with Bayesian optimization. The surrogate is a GP with a product kernel: Matérn (smoothness parameter ν) over continuous variables and a Squared-Exponential ARD kernel over one-hot-encoded categorical activation choices, ensuring valid posterior concentration. Acquisition uses Monte Carlo Empirical Expected Improvement (EEI) with S posterior samples drawn via Matheron's rule, which amortizes the O(n³) matrix inversion across all S samples (reducing cost from O(Sn³) to O(n³ + Sn²)) and enables exact, parallelizable posterior evaluation. The search begins with 30 space-filling Latin Hypercube samples followed by 100 BO iterations. A key empirical finding is that the configuration space design itself is the dominant source of gains—grid and random search over OptiINR's space already outperform prior automated methods (FreSh, SPDER) by 3–6 dB—while BO provides an additional ~0.1–0.2 dB refinement over those baselines under the same budget.

## Results

OptiINR achieves the best performance across all five task modalities. On image representation, it reaches 41.38 dB PSNR on Kodak and 46.24 dB on DIV2K, surpassing the strongest baseline FINER by 1.14 dB and 0.68 dB respectively. On audio reconstruction, gains are dramatic: 60.84 dB on Bach (vs. 54.94 dB for FR), 49.60 dB on Counting (vs. 39.35 dB for FINER), and 68.39 dB on Two Speakers (vs. 56.36 dB for FR). On 3D occupancy at 512³ resolution, OptiINR attains 0.9936 IoU on Dragon and 0.9884 on Thai Statue, and on NeRF view synthesis it reaches 25.63 dB PSNR, outperforming ReLU+PE (25.05 dB) and WIRE (24.63 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OptiINR outperforms all eight baselines on every task where they are directly comparable. On image representation, it beats FINER (the strongest single-activation baseline) by 1.14 dB on Kodak and 0.68 dB on DIV2K, while margins over SIREN, Gauss, WIRE, FR, and IGA range from 2.9 to 5.5 dB. On audio, the gains are largest: OptiINR surpasses FR by 5.90 dB on Bach and 12.03 dB on Two Speakers, and outperforms FINER by 10.25 dB on Counting. On 3D occupancy, improvements over the best baseline (Gauss) are small in absolute IoU (0.0002 on Dragon, 0.0013 on Thai Statue) but correspond to ~27,000 additional correctly classified voxels at 512³ resolution. On NeRF, OptiINR exceeds ReLU+PE by 0.58 dB and WIRE by 1.00 dB, a meaningful margin given that typical NeRF architectural changes yield only 0.3–0.5 dB. In the optimization-strategy ablation, even grid search and random search over OptiINR's configuration space (41.02 and 41.23 dB) substantially outperform prior automated methods FreSh (37.41 dB) and SPDER (35.03 dB), indicating the search-space design itself is a major source of the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

OptiINR builds directly on the Bayesian optimization literature (Snoek et al. 2012; Daulton et al. 2022) for navigating expensive black-box objectives, adapting GP surrogates with product kernels and Matheron's-rule posterior sampling to the mixed discrete-continuous space of INR configurations. It departs most sharply from MIRE (Jayasundara et al. 2025), the closest prior automated method, which selects activations greedily and layer-by-layer; OptiINR instead formulates the problem as a single global joint optimization over all layers simultaneously, arguing (with a supplementary theorem) that greedy layer-wise choices cannot guarantee global optimality. The activation families searched (SIREN, WIRE, FINER, Gaussian) come from the INR spectral-bias literature, but the paper's contribution is the structured search space and the optimization procedure, not new activations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework reveals that optimal activation and initialization choices are strongly task-dependent—smooth images favor Gaussian/FINER++ early layers with SIREN deep layers, while texture-rich images prefer WIRE throughout—challenging the prevailing one-size-fits-all convention in INR design. A notable finding is that the search-space design itself (rather than the BO algorithm) accounts for most of the gain over prior automated methods like FreSh and SPDER, suggesting that carefully structured configuration spaces may be more impactful than sophisticated acquisition functions. Limitations include the fixed 4-layer/256-unit architecture, the high evaluation cost (~130 full trainings per task), and the absence of scalability analysis to deeper or wider networks; open questions include whether discovered patterns generalize across signals of the same modality and how OptiINR interacts with meta-learning initialization approaches.
