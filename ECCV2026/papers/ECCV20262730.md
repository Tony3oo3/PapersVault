## Problem

Existing multimodal explainability (MxAI) methods claim to capture cross-modal interactions, but no evaluation protocol provides the ground truth needed to distinguish true cross-modal reasoning (e.g., spatial composition) from shallow shortcuts (e.g., Bag-of-Words attribute matching). Current benchmarks are limited to unimodal settings or lack identifiability of the model's generative process, making it impossible to verify whether an explainer faithfully reveals the model's actual decision pathway or merely hallucinates a plausible rationale.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes S×S grid images populated with geometric objects (color, shape, position) paired with multi-hop spatial reasoning questions parameterized by a 4-tuple (Depth, QType, Form, Density), along with full model weights and gradients for the explainability methods under test. It produces pixel-level visual attribution heatmaps, token-level textual attribution maps, and a global cross-modal synergy scalar, all quantitatively scored against mathematically guaranteed ground-truth bounding-box masks using Relevance Mass Accuracy (RMA), IoU, and a novel additive fallacy check that tests whether synergy scores scale monotonically with compositional depth.

## Method

GRIDVQA-X constructs a closed-world diagnostic testbed by generating two parallel datasets: D_pure, which uses do-calculus to prove that only anchor and target objects causally determine the answer and algorithmically injects adversarial distractors into confuser regions (C_k) to destroy all four enumerated shortcut families, and D_spur, which restricts the attribute sampling pool so that counting target-attribute objects alone yields the correct answer (P=1.0), embedding a Bag-of-Words trap. Paired MDETR models (M_pure and M_spur) are trained via a two-phase explanation-guided protocol (visual grounding with L1+GIoU loss, then QA with dynamically weighted cross-entropy) on identical architectures, producing verifiably distinct reasoning behaviors confirmed by cross-evaluation (M_spur drops to 8–14% on multi-hop queries in D_pure). The evaluation pipeline then applies local methods (DIME, MultiSHAP, MultiViz) and global methods (EMAP, InterSHAP, PID) to both models, requiring a faithful explainer to produce divergent attributions: near-zero relevance on distractors for M_pure but high relevance for M_spur, and monotonically increasing synergy scores with depth only for M_pure.

## Results

All evaluated MxAI methods fail to distinguish between M_pure (genuine spatial-relational reasoning) and M_spur (Bag-of-Words shortcut). MultiViz exhibits 'absolute model blindness,' yielding statistically identical RMA (≈44%) for both models, while MultiSHAP paradoxically scores *higher* RMA on the shortcut model (64.5%) than on the faithful model (56.0%). At the global level, EMAP hallucinates ~60% cross-modal synergy on M_spur even when that model catastrophically fails on D_pure, and its synergy score for M_pure *decreases* with depth (78.6→64.1), violating the expected monotonic increase. InterSHAP similarly fails the Additive Fallacy check, with interaction scores plummeting from 0.921 (Depth 1) to 0.629 (Depth 3) on Mixed queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a diagnostic benchmark, so the 'baselines' are the MxAI methods under test, and the key finding is that *all* fail the zero-ambiguity diagnostic. DIME suffers from 'accidental faithfulness': its diffuse, unconstrained heatmaps happen to overlap the true spatial target when evaluating M_spur, generating an illusion of correct reasoning (RMA ≈28 for both models, IoU ≈20). MultiSHAP is the most precise object detector (IoU >50) but structurally *prefers* the shortcut model, scoring 64.5 RMA on M_spur versus 56.0 on M_pure, because Shapley marginals align better with independent 1-to-1 feature detectors than with entangled spatial intersections. MultiViz shows total spatial grounding collapse (IoU <7% across all settings) and is completely blind to the model's reasoning pathway. EMAP hallucinates deep cross-modal synergy (~60%) on M_spur simply because masking either modality degrades the shallow shortcut, and its synergy estimate for M_pure *decreases* with compositional depth (78.6→64.1), proving misalignment with the causal graph. InterSHAP echoes this failure with interaction scores dropping from 0.921 to 0.629 across depths on Mixed queries, confirming that even game-theoretic interaction estimators cannot quantify scaling compositional complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of causal reasoning (Pearl's do-calculus), synthetic compositional data generation (in the CLEVR/GQA tradition), and multimodal explainability evaluation. While prior benchmarks like CLEVR-XAI and OpenXAI evaluate unimodal explanation faithfulness, and LATEC provides a systematic evaluation protocol, none offer the identifiability of a model's generative process in a cross-modal setting. GridVQA-X repurposes the MDETR architecture as a controlled test subject and introduces a paired-model design (M_pure vs. M_spur) that has no direct precedent in the explainability literature, effectively creating a zero-ambiguity diagnostic environment where the 'correct' explanation is mathematically provable rather than human-annotated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that every evaluated MxAI method (DIME, MultiSHAP, MultiViz, EMAP, InterSHAP) fails to distinguish genuine spatial-relational reasoning from Bag-of-Words shortcuts—exposes a fundamental blindspot in the current explainability toolkit, meaning practitioners cannot trust these methods to reveal how multimodal models actually decide. The framework is explicitly positioned as a necessary but not sufficient condition: if methods fail in a perfectly controlled, zero-ambiguity setting, their faithfulness on noisy real-world data is even less reliable. Key limitations include the restriction to 2D grid-based spatial reasoning (no temporal, physical, or mathematical reasoning), a single architecture (MDETR), and the open question of whether any explainer can pass this test or whether the failure is structural to current methodological families.
