## Problem

Monocular depth estimation foundation models such as Depth Anything suffer critical performance degradation under adverse weather (fog, rain, snow, nighttime) because their learned representations entangle weather-specific appearance with scene geometry, preventing the model from distinguishing weather-induced style changes from genuine geometric structure. Existing remedies either restore the image first (risking geometric distortion) or require expensive real-weather depth annotations, leaving a gap for a lightweight, generalizable adaptation that preserves clean-scene performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262462/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (any resolution) potentially degraded by fog, rain, snow, or low-light conditions and produces a dense relative depth map at the same spatial resolution. Internally, a Style Filter first extracts a compact 64-dimensional weather embedding from the input, which then conditions the depth decoder. The depth output is affine-invariant (up to per-image scale and shift), consistent with the Depth Anything training objective, and sky regions are masked during training to avoid ill-posed supervision.

## Method

DA-W introduces a Style Filter trained with a contrastive loss over 7 real and 4 synthetic weather datasets, where real-synthetic pairs sharing the same weather type are treated as positives to learn a domain-aligned, weather-discriminative 64-D embedding invariant to scene content. This embedding is injected into a frozen Depth Anything v2 (DINOv2 encoder + DPT decoder) backbone via AdaLN-Zero modulation at five decoder stages (four multi-scale feature maps and the final fused feature), where lightweight MLPs map the embedding to per-channel shift, scale, and gate parameters that are zero-initialized so the initial output matches the base model. Training uses a two-stage protocol: Stage I trains the Style Filter, and Stage II fine-tunes only the DPT decoder head and modulation layers using a combined loss of teacher distillation (frozen ViT-L pseudo-labels), clean-synthetic pair alignment, and augmentation consistency for real-weather images, all measured with an affine-invariant disparity loss. This decoder-only, parameter-efficient design prevents catastrophic forgetting and preserves clean-scene generalization while adding weather-specific increments.

## Results

On real adverse-weather benchmarks (Table 1), DA-W achieves the best AbsRel on NuScenes-night (0.194), DS-rain (0.123), and DS-fog (0.101), and the best δ₁ on NuScenes-night (0.737), DS-rain (0.842), and DS-fog (0.896). On synthetic KITTI-C corruptions (Table 2), DA-W leads on Dark (0.126 AbsRel), Snow (0.107), and Motion (0.125). On clean benchmarks (Table 3), DA-W preserves the backbone's performance with small gains on KITTI (0.080 vs 0.083 AbsRel) and Sintel (0.232 vs 0.240), confirming no catastrophic forgetting. The paper reports an average 3.7% AbsRel improvement over baselines across its curated weather benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DA-W outperforms DepthAnything v2 and DepthAnything-AC on NuScenes-night (0.194 vs 0.200/0.198 AbsRel), DS-rain (0.123 vs 0.125), and DS-fog (0.101 vs 0.103), confirming that explicit weather embeddings with AdaLN-Zero conditioning improve robustness over the base backbone. Against the two-stage restoration pipeline MWFormer+DAv2, DA-W wins on DS-rain (0.123 vs 0.127 AbsRel) and DS-fog (0.101 vs 0.104) without requiring a separate preprocessing stage. The main weakness is RobotCar-night, where DepthAnything v3 (0.169 AbsRel) and DepthPro (0.237) substantially outperform DA-W (0.239), likely due to camera-response and exposure dynamics underrepresented in training. On DS-cloud, DA-AC edges out DA-W slightly (0.149 vs 0.151 AbsRel), suggesting dense cloud remains challenging. On clean benchmarks, DA-W matches or marginally exceeds DA v2 (e.g., KITTI 0.080 vs 0.083, Sintel 0.232 vs 0.240 AbsRel), validating that decoder-only adaptation with zero-initialized heads prevents catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Depth Anything v2 as a frozen backbone and on MWFormer's contrastive Style Filter training procedure, extending the latter to include cross-domain real-synthetic positive pairs in the contrastive loss. It departs from the dominant two-stage restoration-then-depth paradigm (e.g., MWFormer+DAv2, DarkIR+DAv2) and from physics-based degradation modeling (DepthAnything-AC, ACDepth) by explicitly disentangling weather style from scene geometry through a dedicated Style Filter, then injecting the resulting embedding via zero-initialized AdaLN-Zero adapters at the decoder only. The t-SNE diagnostic showing that standard ViT [CLS] tokens conflate weather and geometry provides the conceptual motivation for this disentanglement, positioning the work as a targeted fix for a specific representational failure mode in depth foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, parameter-efficient recipe for making depth foundation models weather-robust without catastrophic forgetting, which is directly relevant to autonomous driving and robotics deployments where adverse conditions are routine. The introduced mix-weather benchmark (11 co-occurring corruption combinations) fills a gap in evaluation, since prior benchmarks test single-factor degradations. Key limitations include evaluation only on the ViT-S variant, persistent weakness on RobotCar-night (suggesting camera-response and exposure dynamics beyond weather appearance remain unaddressed), and mixed results on dense fog. Open questions include whether the same conditioning interface scales to larger Depth Anything variants or other depth backbones, and whether the Style Filter's 64-d embedding is sufficient for finer-grained or novel weather types.
