## Problem

Prior In-Context Segmentation (ICS) methods optimize average segmentation accuracy but largely ignore system robustness: whether the model produces stable predictions for the same query under different reference choices. Because ICS relies on only a handful of in-context examples, predictions are highly sensitive to reference quality and viewpoint, and existing work either selects the best reference from a candidate pool (unavailable in real deployment) or handles only corrupted references without addressing inherently low-quality ones.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image paired with its binary mask (no category label or textual description is provided) and a query image, and produces a semantic segmentation mask for the query. In the multi-shot setting, multiple reference-mask pairs are processed independently for concept reasoning and then fused. All models (Qwen3-VL-4B MLLM and SAM3) are fully frozen, so no in-domain training data or parameter updates are required.

## Method

CG-ICS recasts ICS as a Promptable Concept Segmentation problem by deriving a textual concept and visual exemplars in parallel, then feeding both to a frozen SAM3 backbone. The Concept Reasoning module formulates concept selection as a score-guided tree search: a frozen MLLM (Qwen3-VL-4B) generates N candidate noun-phrase concepts per node (expansion), while SAM3 scores each candidate via a multiplicative Reference Fidelity (IoU of SAM3's semantic mask on the reference vs. the ground-truth mask) and Query Matchability (SAM3's presence score on the query) product; nodes below a pruning threshold are discarded and survivors are refined over up to K rounds. In parallel, the Visual Exemplar route stitches the reference and query horizontally, prompts SAM3 with the reference bounding box to segment matched instances, and extracts query-side bounding boxes as spatial exemplars. The final mask is obtained by a single SAM3 inference on the query image using the best-scoring textual concept and the extracted visual exemplar boxes jointly, combining category-level semantics with query-side spatial grounding to reduce variance across reference choices.

## Results

Under the standard ICS protocol, CG-ICS (training-free) achieves 89.3 mIoU on Pascal-5i (1-shot), 72.3 on COCO-20i (1-shot), 55.4 on LVIS-92i (1-shot), and 90.2 on FSS-1000 (1-shot), setting new state-of-the-art among all training-free methods. In the robustness-to-reference-selection protocol, CG-ICS attains the lowest coefficient of variation (CV) across all three benchmarks (7.8%, 12.9%, 30.1%), indicating the most stable predictions relative to its mean performance. Under corrupted-reference evaluation, CG-ICS suffers only a −3.1% mean mIoU drop, outperforming the strongest training-based baseline UNICL-SAM (−3.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among training-free baselines, CG-ICS dominates across all four benchmarks: it outperforms GF-SAM by +17.2 mIoU on Pascal-5i 1-shot (89.3 vs 72.1), +13.6 on COCO-20i 1-shot (72.3 vs 58.7), and +20.2 on LVIS-92i 1-shot (55.4 vs 35.2), and beats Matcher by similar margins. Against training-based methods, CG-ICS achieves SOTA on Pascal-5i 1-shot (89.3 vs DiffwS 88.3), LVIS-92i 1-shot (55.4 vs SANSA 50.3), and FSS-1000 1-shot (90.2 vs SANSA 90.0), while trailing UNICL-SAM on COCO-20i (72.3 vs 77.8 in 1-shot) and SANSA on COCO-20i 5-shot (72.6 vs 78.6) and FSS-1000 5-shot (89.3 vs 91.0). In the robustness-to-reference-selection protocol, CG-ICS achieves the lowest CV on every benchmark (7.8% on Pascal-5i, 12.9% on COCO-20i, 30.1% on LVIS-92i), substantially outperforming GF-SAM (27.3%/28.1%/44.3%) and even training-based SANSA (11.1%/17.6%/35.8%). Under corrupted references, CG-ICS records the smallest mean mIoU drop (−3.1%), beating UNICL-SAM (−3.9%), GF-SAM (−4.1%), and Matcher (−9.9%), while maintaining a high clean mIoU of 74.6.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper recasts in-context segmentation (ICS) as a Promptable Concept Segmentation (PCS) problem by leveraging SAM3's text-concept interface, departing from the dominant paradigm of prior ICS methods (GF-SAM, Matcher, SegGPT, SINE) that rely on low-level visual correspondences between reference and query. The conceptual lineage draws on two threads: the MLLM-based open-vocabulary understanding literature for concept generation, and SAM3's PCS capability for concept-driven segmentation. The tree-search mechanism for concept selection is a novel orchestration that uses SAM3 itself as a scoring oracle, while the visual exemplar extraction via stitched reference-query images repurposes SegGPT's concatenation trick for SAM3's exemplar prompting. Unlike prior robustness work (UNICL-SAM, Tang et al.) that addresses corrupted references or selects from a candidate pool, CG-ICS targets stability under arbitrary user-provided references without any search over alternatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing robustness to reference selection as a first-class evaluation objective with concrete metrics (Std, CV), this paper provides the ICS community with a more realistic deployment criterion beyond average mIoU. The finding that concept-based guidance reduces prediction variance by 3–4× while simultaneously improving accuracy suggests a general principle: higher-level semantic representations are inherently more invariant to input perturbation than pixel-level matching, which could inform other few-shot visual tasks. Limitations include dependence on two large frozen models (Qwen3-VL-4B and SAM3) for inference, the added latency of tree-search refinement, and evaluation restricted to image segmentation benchmarks. Open questions include whether the concept-guided paradigm extends to video ICS, whether lighter MLLMs suffice for concept generation, and whether the robustness gains persist under domain shift (e.g., medical or satellite imagery).
