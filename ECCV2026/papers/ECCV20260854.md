## Problem

Single-image 3D human reconstruction remains ill-posed due to occlusions and pose diversity. Feed-forward regressors produce over-smoothed textures and cannot hallucinate unseen geometry, while existing diffusion-based approaches either synthesize multi-view images (introducing view inconsistency and high cost) or operate in expensive 3D volumetric spaces (e.g., SyncHuman requires 51 s per image). The paper asks whether a purely 2D generative formulation can achieve both 3D consistency and high-fidelity texture recovery at near-real-time speed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (rendered at 896×640 in training) together with SMPL-X shape (β) and pose (θ) parameters, which define the canonical UV parameterization. The image is encoded by a frozen VAE (32×32×32 latent) and a frozen DINOv3 appearance encoder (768-channel features). The output is a canonical 3D Gaussian Splatting avatar whose primitives are parameterized as offsets from SMPL-X vertices in UV space, immediately animatable via Linear Blend Skinning. Full inference from image to 3DGS takes 0.71 s on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

DiGS-Avatar reformulates single-image 3D human reconstruction as diffusion-based completion of a partial UV latent in a canonical, surface-aligned 2D domain, making the result 3D-consistent by construction since every UV pixel maps to a unique surface point. A multi-view teacher network fuses per-view VAE latents via a UV-Alignment Transformer (with Plücker ray embeddings) to produce geometry-aligned pseudo-ground-truth UV latents, which supervise a single-view student diffusion model (Sana) that completes the partial latent obtained by inverse texture mapping. A Geometry-Aligned Semantic Aggregation (GASA) module then progressively injects multi-level DINOv3 appearance features into the 32×32 VAE latent via cross-attention at 32→64→128 resolutions, recovering fine texture without disrupting spatial integrity. The refined UV feature map is decoded into 3DGS attributes (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color) as explicit offsets from SMPL-X vertices, and the student is trained with a joint diffusion denoising loss and photometric rendering loss. Training follows a staged pipeline: teacher (60K iters) → student diffusion (20K iters) → joint fine-tuning on high-fidelity data (45K + 15K iters), totaling ~60 GPU-hours.

## Results

On the three in-distribution benchmarks, DiGS-Avatar achieves PSNR of 25.32 / 21.45 / 22.22 (HuGe100K / THuman 2.1 / 2K2K), outperforming the strongest feed-forward baseline IDOL by 2.57 / 2.63 / 4.05 dB respectively, and the best multi-view or 3D-native diffusion baselines by 3–7 dB. On the zero-shot SIZER benchmark, it reaches 23.54 PSNR and 0.932 SSIM, again leading all baselines. Inference takes only 0.71 s per image (vs. 2.25 s for IDOL, 5.13 s for LHM, and 51 s for SyncHuman), with a total training cost of ~60 GPU-hours on a single A100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiGS-Avatar outperforms all six baselines on every in-distribution metric. Against the strongest feed-forward regressor IDOL, it gains +2.57 dB PSNR on HuGe100K (25.32 vs 22.75) and +4.05 dB on 2K2K (22.22 vs 18.17), while also being 3× faster at inference (0.71 s vs 2.25 s). Versus the transformer-based LHM, DiGS-Avatar leads by 3.56 dB on HuGe100K and 1.83 dB on THuman 2.1, and is 7× faster (0.71 s vs 5.13 s). Multi-view diffusion methods (Human3Diffusion, SIFU) trail by 4–7 dB in PSNR and also suffer from visible geometric inconsistencies in qualitative comparisons. Native 3D generative models (TRELLIS, SyncHuman) are the closest in some LPIPS scores but fall 3–5 dB behind in PSNR and require 7–70× more inference time. On the zero-shot SIZER benchmark, DiGS-Avatar achieves the best PSNR (23.54) and SSIM (0.932), narrowly edging LHM (23.45 / 0.929) and clearly surpassing SyncHuman (19.56) and TRELLIS (19.94), confirming that the UV-latent completion prior generalizes beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiGS-Avatar builds directly on IDOL's UV-to-3DGS decoder and SMPL-X-aligned Gaussian representation, but departs fundamentally from both the multi-view diffusion paradigm (Human3Diffusion, AdaHuman) and the native 3D volumetric diffusion paradigm (TRELLIS, SyncHuman) by reformulating the generative step as a 2D UV-latent inpainting problem. The teacher-student distillation framework—where a multi-view teacher produces geometry-aligned pseudo-ground-truth UV latents to supervise a single-view diffusion student—is a novel training strategy not seen in prior single-image human reconstruction work. The GASA module, which progressively injects DINOv3 semantic features into the compressed VAE latent via coarse-to-fine cross-attention, represents a new mechanism for recovering high-frequency texture without breaking 3D consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.71-second inference time (70× faster than SyncHuman, 3× faster than IDOL) while achieving superior PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS across all benchmarks makes animatable 3D avatar generation practically deployable in real-time applications like VR, gaming, and telepresence. The key open limitation is the hard dependency on SMPL-X topology: extremely loose or non-rigid clothing that deviates from the body template can exceed the Gaussian offset capacity, capping geometric expressiveness. Future work on relaxing the SMPL-X constraint and exploring higher-resolution UV latents would address this bottleneck and potentially extend the approach to more diverse garment categories.
