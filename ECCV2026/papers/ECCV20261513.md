## Problem

Existing one-shot feed-forward 3D animatable head avatar methods, trained predominantly on frontal face videos, collapse when rendering views significantly deviate from the input camera pose, leaving side and back head regions ambiguous and unrealistic. This paper addresses the novel setting of one-shot feed-forward 360° full-head animatable avatar reconstruction in a single forward pass, where no prior method could produce consistent novel views from all angles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261513/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB face image as source, a driving image for expression/pose animation, and a target camera pose for rendering. It produces UV Gaussian attribute maps (K×K×14 encoding color, rotation, scale, opacity, and position offset) that define 3D Gaussian primitives rigidly bound to the FLAME mesh surface, enabling 360° view synthesis and real-time animation at 246 FPS without per-identity optimization. The UV-space binding means each Gaussian's 3D position is determined by its texel location on the parametric mesh, with a learned position offset for shape refinement.

## Method

The framework binds 3D Gaussian primitives to the FLAME mesh surface via UV parameterization, then extracts two complementary UV feature maps from the input: a coarse but complete global map sampled from a pretrained 3D GAN tri-plane (PanoHead) via feed-forward inversion, and a fine-grained but incomplete local map obtained by projecting 2D CNN image features onto the UV space with visibility masking. A symmetric UV-space inpainting module fuses these via a transformer cross-attention where global UV patch tokens query both the corresponding and the horizontally-flipped (symmetric) local windows, exploiting face and UV-space symmetry to recover occluded regions. A local detail enhancement stage further refines the inpainted features using an occlusion mask and symmetric convolution. A novel 3D total variation loss—applying TV to renderings where Gaussian positions replace colors—encourages full surface coverage and eliminates hole artifacts that UV-space TV losses introduce around eyes and mouth.

## Results

On the HDTF dataset, the method achieves 26.61 PSNR and 0.8642 SSIM for self-reenactment, surpassing the strongest baseline GAGAvatar (23.72 PSNR, 0.8177 SSIM) by a substantial margin, and also leads on keypoint distance (AKD 2.287) and pose distance (APD 0.5286). On VFHQ, it reaches 23.24 PSNR and 0.7995 SSIM for self-reenactment, again outperforming all baselines in reconstruction quality. The method achieves 246 FPS animation speed on an RTX 3090, exceeding LAM (231.74 FPS) and GAGAvatar (58.11 FPS), and is the first to produce one-shot feed-forward 360° animatable avatars with consistent side and back views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all seven baselines on reconstruction quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) for self-reenactment on both HDTF and VFHQ, with the largest gap over GAGAvatar (+2.89 PSNR on HDTF) and LAM (+3.06 PSNR on HDTF). It also leads on pose accuracy (APD) and keypoint distance (AKD) for self-reenactment, confirming more faithful motion transfer. However, it trails GAGAvatar on identity preservation (CSIM 0.8622 vs 0.8894 on HDTF self-reenactment) and expression accuracy (AED 0.3318 vs 0.3034), which the authors attribute to FLAME tracker limitations. For cross-identity reenactment, the method wins on CSIM over VFHQ (0.6757 vs 0.6681 for GAGAvatar) but lags on AED and APD, indicating slightly less precise motion mimicry when identity differs. Against the 2D baseline StyleHEAT, the gap is overwhelming across all metrics, underscoring the advantage of explicit 3D modeling. The 360° rendering capability and 246 FPS speed have no direct equivalent in any compared baseline, which are limited to near-frontal views or slower rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feed-forward 3D Gaussian head avatar line (GAGAvatar, LAM, GGHead) by addressing their fundamental limitation: near-frontal-only rendering due to training on monocular datasets. It introduces a UV-space inpainting strategy that fuses fine-grained local image features with coarse global full-head priors from a pretrained 3D GAN (PanoHead) and its feed-forward inversion, leveraging face/UV symmetry via a transformer-based cross-attention mechanism inspired by HumanSplat. The 3D total variation loss departs from the UV-space TV regularization in GGHead, recognizing that UV-adjacent texels (e.g., eyeballs vs. eyelids) can be spatially distant in 3D, making 3D-space TV more appropriate for full-head coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to produce one-shot, feed-forward, 360°-viewable animatable head avatars at real-time speed (246 FPS), opening the door to immersive VR/AR applications where users can orbit a reconstructed face. The UV-space inpainting paradigm—combining local detail with global generative priors—is a generalizable recipe that could transfer to other partial-observation 3D reconstruction problems. Limitations include dependence on the FLAME mesh (constraining head shape diversity), reliance on a fixed 3D GAN prior (limiting identity diversity and hair modeling), and cross-identity motion accuracy bounded by FLAME tracking quality. Open questions include whether the GAN prior can be replaced by diffusion-based full-head generators and whether the approach extends to full-body avatars.
