## Problem

Existing text-to-trajectory models face two coupled failures in multi-entity scenes: (1) conventional text encoders mix entire sentences without structured isolation, causing severe cross-entity feature leakage and action misattribution (the 'who does what' problem), and (2) directly regressing high-dimensional 3D coordinates from semantic embeddings ignores spatial topology, leading to exponential geometric error accumulation and frequent inter-entity collisions (the 'where to go' problem). No prior method explicitly disentangles static identity from dynamic motion in the latent space or introduces an intermediate geometric planning stage before 3D coordinate generation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multi-entity textual instruction (structured as stepwise, motion-centric prompts parsed by an LLM into per-entity verb-centric action tuples) and a single reference video frame as input. It outputs N continuous 3D trajectories X^n ∈ R^{L×3}, one per entity, spanning L frames at 8 FPS (average clip length 3.3 s). The reference frame is consumed twice: once through an open-vocabulary segmentation pipeline (Grounding DINO + SAM) to produce per-entity boolean masks, and once through a monocular depth model (Depth Anything) plus a visual backbone to construct the BEV prior. Single-entity baselines are evaluated under an Independent Aggregation Protocol where trajectories are generated separately before joint 3D projection.

## Method

ProgTraj-Director decomposes trajectory generation into two stages. First, the StructVLA module performs static-dynamic heterogeneous disentanglement: a static branch extracts entity identity features via masked visual encoding, while a dynamic branch maps LLM-parsed action tuples through a Spatial Motion Prior Network into directional motion features; these are bound via an outer-product tensor fusion followed by an orthogonal regularization (Eq. 4) that penalizes cosine similarity between distinct entities' intent embeddings, forcing their latent subspaces to be mutually perpendicular. Second, a Hierarchical Spatial Planning stage constructs a Geometry-Aware BEV by unprojecting depth-aware visual features into a 3D point cloud and voxel-pooling onto the top-down X-Z plane, then feeds this BEV grid as an explicit spatial prior into a 28-layer Diffusion Transformer that performs progressive 3D trajectory denoising under decoupled dual conditioning (semantic intent for direction, BEV for geometric layout). The total training objective is simply L_diff + λ·L_ortho, with no heuristic proxy losses.

## Results

On the multi-entity Stepwise-ME dataset, ProgTraj-Director achieves F1 = 0.875, CLIP = 53.11, Coverage = 0.913, FID = 43.65, and a Collision Rate of 7.3%, outperforming the strongest baseline Lang2Motion (F1 = 0.847, CR = 16.4%) on every metric. The most striking gain is in collision reduction: 7.3% vs. 16.4% (Lang2Motion) and 38.5% (GenDoP), validating the Geometry-Aware BEV prior. On the single-entity DataDop benchmark, the method marginally surpasses GenDoP (F1 0.416 vs. 0.414; FID 69.10 vs. 72.05) while substantially outperforming CCD, E.T., and Director3D. The Entity-Action Binding accuracy of 0.96 is unique to this method, as all baselines lack dedicated latent slots for multi-entity attribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary multi-entity Stepwise-ME benchmark, ProgTraj-Director wins against all four baselines on every reported metric. The largest margin is in Collision Rate: 7.3% versus 16.4% for Lang2Motion (the closest competitor), 15.8% for LANGTRAJ, 24.2% for NaviDIFF, and 38.5% for GenDoP, confirming that the BEV spatial prior is the key differentiator for physical plausibility. In semantic alignment (F1), the gap over Lang2Motion is modest (0.875 vs. 0.847), but the CLIP score gap (53.11 vs. 48.86) and Coverage gap (0.913 vs. 0.820) indicate broader distributional quality. On the single-entity DataDop set, ProgTraj-Director edges out GenDoP by small margins (F1 0.416 vs. 0.414; FID 69.10 vs. 72.05) while CCD, E.T., and Director3D trail significantly, suggesting the method generalizes well beyond multi-entity settings. The Entity-Action Binding accuracy (0.96) has no direct counterpart in any baseline, as those methods are identity-agnostic and cannot perform in-situ multi-entity attribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of vision-language trajectory generation and multi-entity spatial planning, departing from prior camera-trajectory work (CCD, E.T., Director3D) and single-entity tracking datasets (RealEstate10k, DL3DV-10K) that conflate cinematic camera styles with object interactions. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it repurposes standard components—CLIP-style visual encoders, Grounding DINO + SAM segmentation, Depth Anything, and a DiT diffusion backbone—into a two-stage pipeline that explicitly separates entity-action binding (via orthogonal tensor fusion) from spatial planning (via BEV-guided progressive denoising). The key conceptual departure is treating the 'semantic-physical gap' as a hierarchical problem solvable by a 2D geometric blueprint before 3D coordinate regression, rather than as a direct mapping challenge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Stepwise-ME dataset (31K clips, 8M frames, stepwise multi-entity annotations) fills a genuine gap in benchmarks that previously offered only static, sentence-level descriptions or single-entity tracking, making it a practical resource for the growing embodied world model community. The BEV-guided collision avoidance mechanism (reducing collision rate from ~16–38% to 7.3%) provides a concrete, interpretable spatial prior that downstream video generators can consume, positioning trajectory generation as a structural bridge toward controllable long-horizon video synthesis. However, the authors acknowledge clear degradation under crowded scenes, severe occlusions, and long compositional instructions, and the video-generation application remains preliminary—long-horizon planning and robust perception under partial observability remain open.
