## Problem

Existing visible-to-thermal image translation methods treat the task as a style-transfer problem, neglecting critical physical factors such as ambient environmental conditions (temperature, humidity, air pressure, wind speed) and vehicle motion states (static vs. moving), resulting in generated thermal images that are physically implausible. Compounding this, the scarcity of synchronized, calibrated visible-thermal aerial image pairs annotated with environmental and vehicle metadata has limited the ability to train context-aware generative models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260782/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 640×512 visible-light aerial image, a text prompt encoding interpolated environmental conditions (temperature, humidity, air pressure, wind speed) derived from GPS coordinates and an open-source climate platform, and per-vehicle state labels (static/moving/unknown) determined from temporal YOLOv11 detection sequences. It produces a 640×512 thermal infrared image. The framework is explicitly designed as an offline high-fidelity data generation engine (~10 s per image on a single RTX 4090) rather than a real-time onboard system, prioritizing physical consistency over latency.

## Method

AV2T-Gen builds on the Instruct-Pix2Pix diffusion pipeline, where a frozen VAE encodes visible and thermal images into latent vectors, and a trainable UNet with QKV attention performs iterative denoising in latent space. The key novelty is a multimodal conditioning mechanism: Ram-Grounding extracts vehicle bounding boxes and labels, which are fused with vehicle-state annotations; SAM generates precise object masks encoded as mask embeddings, while CLIP encodes both the complete labels (label embeddings) and the environmental condition text (prompt embeddings). Mask and label embeddings are concatenated and passed through a trainable MLP to form integrated contextual embeddings, which together with prompt embeddings constitute the final conditional input to the UNet. Training uses a composite loss combining L1 latent-space loss, Sobel-based edge loss, and VGG perceptual loss, with only the MLP and UNet parameters updated while all foundation models remain frozen. The authors also introduce a paired aerial visible-thermal dataset (~29,672 samples across four scenes) annotated with continuous environmental metadata and vehicle states.

## Results

On the combined all-scenes evaluation, AV2T-Gen achieves PSNR 24.08, SSIM 0.750, FID 67.45, and LPIPS 0.29, outperforming the strongest baseline ThermalGen (PSNR 23.94, FID 83.16) by a clear margin on FID (−15.71) and LPIPS (−0.08). On the downstream vehicle detection task, AV2T-Gen-generated thermal images yield mAP 15.6% and mAP₅₀ 35.9%, surpassing ThermalGen (11.7% / 29.4%) and sRGB-TIR (7.33% / 16.3%). Cross-dataset zero-shot evaluation on LLVIP confirms generalizability, with AV2T-Gen reaching PSNR 13.82 and FID 175.50 versus ThermalGen's 12.58 and 196.20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AV2T-Gen outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all four individual scenes and the combined set. The closest competitor is ThermalGen [36], a flow-based generative model, which trails AV2T-Gen by 0.14 dB in PSNR and 15.7 in FID on the all-scenes setting; the gap widens substantially on Scene 4 (FID 52.50 vs. 72.73). PID [19] and DiffV2IR [24] are diffusion-based methods that treat translation more as style transfer and achieve PSNR around 22.3 and FID above 106, roughly 1.7 dB and 40+ FID points worse than AV2T-Gen. sRGB-TIR [15], a GAN-based edge-guided method, performs comparably to PID and DiffV2IR on PSNR/SSIM but degrades sharply on Scene 3 (PSNR 18.58), indicating poor cross-scene robustness. On the downstream vehicle detection task (Table 9), AV2T-Gen's generated thermal images enable a YOLOv11 detector to reach mAP 15.6% and mAP₅₀ 35.9%, a 33% relative improvement over ThermalGen (11.7% / 29.4%) and a 21× improvement over PID (0.3% / 0.7%), confirming that the physical-consistency gains translate into practical detection benefits.

## Relation to Prior Work

AV2T-Gen builds directly on the Instruct-Pix2Pix diffusion framework, repurposing its VAE-UNet architecture for aerial visible-to-thermal translation. It departs from prior GAN-based (Pix2Pix, CycleGAN, ThermalGAN, EGGAN) and diffusion-based (PID, DiffV2IR, ThermalGen) methods by explicitly conditioning generation on environmental parameters (temperature, humidity, wind speed, air pressure) and vehicle motion state, rather than treating the task as unconstrained style transfer. The use of foundation models (SAM, CLIP, Grounded DINO) for mask and label extraction follows the F-ViTA paradigm but adds fine-grained physical priors through an MLP-fused conditioning vector that prior methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine data-scarcity bottleneck in aerial RGB-T research by contributing a 29,672-pair dataset with synchronized environmental and vehicle-state metadata, which is immediately useful for training and benchmarking context-aware translation models. However, the 10-second inference time and 15.5B parameter count confine the method to offline data generation rather than real-time deployment, and the evaluation is limited to four aerial scenes plus LLVIP. Open questions include whether the physical conditioning generalizes to ground-level or maritime scenarios, whether real-time sensor streams can replace the interpolated climate data, and whether the vehicle-state signal can be derived from temporal tracking history rather than manual annotation.
