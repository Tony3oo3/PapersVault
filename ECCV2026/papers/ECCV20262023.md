## Problem

Vision Transformers suffer from quadratic computational and memory complexity in self-attention, which becomes prohibitive at high resolutions (e.g., 1280×1280). While linear-complexity alternatives like SSMs (Vim, VMamba) and gated linear attention (ViT³) exist, they either rely on unidirectional scanning that conflicts with 2D spatial structure or perform only a single gradient step, limiting their expressiveness for dense prediction tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262023/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image of arbitrary resolution, splits it into 16×16 patches forming a 1D token sequence of length T = HW/256, and projects each patch to a D-dimensional embedding with learned position encodings. For classification, a mean-pool over the output sequence feeds a linear head; for detection and segmentation, the frozen backbone features are passed to ViT-Adapter + Mask R-CNN (at 1333×800) or UperNet (at 512×512), respectively. The hidden state W ∈ R^{nh×d×d} is updated via mini-batch gradient descent (batch size 16) along the sequence, making the number of adaptation steps T/b a key hyperparameter.

## Method

Vision-TTT reformulates visual sequence modeling as test-time training: the token sequence is projected into key (K), query (Q), and value (V) views, and the hidden state W is updated by gradient descent on an L2 reconstruction loss ‖W·x_t^K − x_t^V‖², with the output computed as z_t = W·x_t^Q. Two 2D-specific designs extend vanilla unidirectional TTT to vision: a dual-dataset strategy that runs self-supervised learning simultaneously in forward and backward directions, and a depthwise Conv2d preprocessing step that augments the dataset with local 2D context. The mini-batch gradient descent (b=16) is implemented with Triton kernels exploiting Tensor Core 16×16 matrix multiplication, achieving linear-time throughput. Theoretically, the subtractive discrepancy (V_t − W_{t-1}K_t) in the update rule creates a string of relative position anchors, giving Vision-TTT an inherently position-aware, globally radial effective receptive field that distinguishes it from both attention (input-selective) and SSM (multiplicative-decay) encodings.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, Vittt-T/S/B achieve 77.7%, 81.8%, and 82.7% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming the closest TTT-based competitor ViT³ (76.5%, 81.6%, 82.6%) and all other non-hierarchical baselines. On COCO2017 detection, Vittt-B reaches 48.6 AP^b and 43.0 AP^m, surpassing ViT³-B (47.4, 42.1) and Vim-B (46.6, 41.6). On ADE20K segmentation, Vittt-B achieves 50.3 mIoU, the best among all compared methods. At 1280×1280 resolution, Vittt-T reduces FLOPs by 79.4%, runs 4.72× faster, and uses 88.9% less memory than DeiT-T.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vittt consistently outperforms all non-hierarchical baselines across all three tasks. On ImageNet classification, Vittt-T/S gain +1.6%/+1.5% over Vim-T/S and +1.2%/+0.2% over ViT³-T/S, while Vittt-B edges out ViT³-B by +0.1% and DeiT-B by +0.9%. The advantage is most pronounced on dense prediction: on COCO detection, Vittt-S/B beat Vim-S/B by +1.4%/+2.0% AP^b, and on ADE20K segmentation, Vittt-B surpasses ViT³-B by +0.9% mIoU. Against DeiT (quadratic-complexity Transformer), Vittt-T achieves +5.5% Top-1 accuracy at 224×224 while using comparable FLOPs, and at 1280×1280 resolution Vittt-T is 4.72× faster with 88.9% less memory. Compared to Vim (SSM-based), Vittt matches or slightly exceeds FLOPs at S/B sizes but runs faster at all resolutions due to Tensor Core–native kernel design. ViT³, the closest TTT-based competitor, performs comparably on classification but lags by 0.5–1.2% on downstream tasks, which the authors attribute to its single-step gradient descent versus Vittt's multi-step (T/b) adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vision-TTT extends the Test-Time Training (TTT) paradigm from Sun et al. [47], originally formulated for unidirectional NLP sequence modeling, into the visual domain by treating 2D patch token sequences as self-supervised learning datasets. It departs from the closest competitor ViT³ [23], which performs a single gradient descent step (equivalent to gated linear attention), by replacing it with sequence-aware multi-step gradient descent (T/b steps), enabling continuous adaptation along the visual sequence. To bridge the gap between 1D TTT and 2D spatial data, the paper introduces a dual-dataset strategy (forward + backward) and Conv2d-based dataset preprocessing, positioning itself as a new Pareto front between expressiveness and linear-time efficiency against both quadratic-complexity Transformers (DeiT, ViT) and other linear models (Vim, VMamba, Vision-RWKV).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 79.4% FLOPs reduction and 4.72× speedup at 1280×1280 resolution make Vision-TTT a practically viable alternative to ViTs for high-resolution and long-sequence visual tasks where quadratic attention becomes prohibitive. The consistent outperformance over all linear-complexity baselines (Vim, VRWKV, ViT³) across classification, detection, and segmentation at every model scale suggests the multi-step gradient descent formulation is genuinely more expressive than single-step or SSM-based alternatives. Open questions include whether the Triton kernel implementation generalizes across hardware backends, whether the dual-dataset strategy transfers to other spatial modalities (video, 3D), and whether the approach can be further scaled beyond the 102M parameter base model tested here.
