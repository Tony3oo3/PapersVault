## Problem

Existing articulated object reconstruction methods require explicitly observable motion from multiple articulation states, yet in practice objects are most often encountered and captured in their closed (rest) configuration. This rest-state setting is inherently ill-posed: geometry, part semantics, and kinematic structure must all be inferred from a single static observation with no motion cues, and individual pretrained models produce noisy, mutually inconsistent predictions that cannot be naively combined.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts any rest-state mesh source—multi-view RGB renderings, a single RGB image (via image-to-3D generation), or existing 3D scans—and automatically determines the number of parts K without prior annotation. It outputs per-part surface meshes with globally consistent boundaries, per-joint parameters (axis direction, axis position, and type: revolute or prismatic), and completed volumetric meshes suitable for URDF export and physics simulation. The mesh is canonically aligned and re-rendered from N' viewpoints selected to maximize visibility of articulated components before processing.

## Method

Rest2Art operates in three stages grounded on an explicit mesh as the intermediate representation. First, an iterative co-refinement loop cross-validates a VLM's part hierarchy tree against SAM3 segmentation masks, using their disagreements (excess or missing detections) as correction signals, then lifts validated 2D masks onto the mesh via confidence-weighted per-face evidence accumulation and refines boundaries through multi-source shortest-path label propagation on the mesh adjacency graph. Second, since no motion is observed, a video diffusion model (Wan2.2 with VBVR LoRA) synthesizes articulation hypotheses conditioned on mask overlays; 2D trajectories are extracted with CoTracker3, unprojected using mesh-rendered depth, and fitted to rigid-body joint models (Rodrigues rotation for revolute, linear translation for prismatic) by minimizing reprojection error, with the mesh geometry resolving axis ambiguity by penalizing inter-part penetration and blending the fitted axis with the principal direction of the part boundary. Third, segmented shell patches are solidified into closed volumetric meshes via boundary-aware normal offsetting, with parametric interior geometry generated for hollow components like drawers.

## Results

On ACD-HSSD, Rest2Art achieves 74.49% joint type accuracy (highest among all rest-state methods), 11.35° axis angular error (tied with Articulate AnyMesh), and 0.85 dm axis positional error (best among rest-state methods). On ACD-ABO, it reaches 73.38% type accuracy and 4.78° angular error, substantially outperforming Articulate AnyMesh (38.94% type, 25.65° angular) while remaining competitive with two-state methods. On the real-world MultiScan dataset, Rest2Art achieves 17.23° angular error and 0.26 dm positional error, the best among all compared methods, with 65.78% type accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest rest-state reconstruction baseline, Articulate AnyMesh [31], Rest2Art wins on type accuracy (74.49% vs 67.12% on HSSD; 73.38% vs 38.94% on ABO) and positional error (0.85 vs 1.31 dm on HSSD), ties on angular error (11.35° on HSSD), and substantially outperforms on ABO angular error (4.78° vs 25.65°). Against two-state reconstruction methods (ArtGS, REArtGS, REArtGS++), Rest2Art achieves far lower angular error (11.35° vs 41–58° on HSSD) and higher type accuracy on HSSD (74.49% vs 33–59%), though REArtGS++ edges ahead on ABO type accuracy (81.28% vs 73.38%) at the cost of 45.92° angular error. Against generation-based methods (URDFormer, Singapo), Rest2Art achieves much higher type accuracy (74.49% vs 16–59% on HSSD) and competitive angular error, while Singapo's near-zero angular error (1.97°) reflects its canonical-space output requiring post-hoc scale alignment rather than true metric accuracy. On real-world MultiScan data, Rest2Art outperforms all baselines on angular error (17.23° vs 30–63°) and positional error (0.26 dm vs 3.45–29.60 dm), and matches REArtGS on type accuracy (65.78% vs 64.40%), though Articulate AnyMesh achieves higher scene coverage (61.70% vs 48.94%). The co-refinement ablation (Table 4) shows the iterative VLM–segmentation loop improves hierarchy tree accuracy by 10.2–20.3 points over either model alone, confirming that mutual correction is the key driver of the segmentation gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant multi-state articulated object reconstruction paradigm (Ditto, PARIS, ArtGS, REArtGS) by formalizing the rest-state-only setting as an ill-posed problem and addressing it without any observed motion. Rather than chaining pretrained models sequentially as Articulate AnyMesh does, it introduces an iterative co-refinement loop between a VLM and a segmentation model whose disagreements drive mutual correction, with all evidence grounded in an explicit mesh. The use of a video diffusion model as a hypothesis generator (rather than a source of ground-truth motion) constrained by mesh geometry is a repurposing of generative video models that prior articulated-object work has not explored. The mesh-based intermediate representation also distinguishes it from neural-field and 3DGS-based methods, enabling spatially consistent part boundaries and occlusion-aware fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Rest-state is the most common real-world configuration for articulated objects (furniture in product photos, scene scans), making this formulation practically important for building interactive digital twins at scale. The input-agnostic design (multi-view, single image, scanned mesh) broadens applicability, and the mesh-grounded verification strategy offers a general recipe for reconciling noisy foundation-model outputs. Key limitations include restriction to prismatic and revolute joints, dependence on video diffusion quality for motion hypotheses, and the co-refinement loop being capped at two rounds. Open questions include extending to more complex kinematic chains (ball joints, multi-DOF) and reducing reliance on the fidelity of generated articulation videos.
