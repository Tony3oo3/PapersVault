## Problem

Existing CLIP-based anomaly detection methods perform only coarse binary normal-vs-abnormal classification, failing to differentiate fine-grained defect types (e.g., scratch, crack, hole) that are essential for industrial root-cause analysis. Purely learnable soft prompts risk drifting from CLIP's natural-language manifold and weakening zero-shot transfer, while hand-crafted defect prompts (as in MultiADS) are time-consuming and difficult to scale as new defect categories emerge. The paper addresses this by asking how to compose prompts that retain explicit defect-type semantics while learning transferable anomaly context.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (resized to 518×518) and a set of defect-type descriptor strings (e.g., 'scratch', 'bent', 'contamination') as input. It produces three outputs: an image-level anomaly score, a binary pixel-level anomaly mask, and a (K+1)-channel multi-type segmentation mask where each channel corresponds to one defect type plus normal. At inference, prompts for previously unseen defect types are instantiated simply by swapping the textual anchor word, requiring no retraining or external descriptor generation.

## Method

DAPO learns hybrid defect-aware prompts of the form [W₁]…[Wₗ]⟨D⟩[anomaly][object], where the l=5 learnable context tokens are shared across all defect types (encoding a type-agnostic abnormal context) and ⟨D⟩ is a fixed defect-name anchor injecting type-specific semantics. E=10 prompt instances per defect type capture diverse aspects of the anomaly space. Training keeps the CLIP ViT-L/14@336 backbone fully frozen and updates only the prompt tokens and M=4 lightweight linear adapters (~0.15% of parameters) via a combined global cross-entropy loss (image-level) and local focal+dice loss over multi-layer patch embeddings. A progressive prompt injection strategy stabilizes optimization, and the shared-token design enables zero-shot instantiation of novel defect types at test time by replacing only the textual anchor.

## Results

On binary anomaly detection under distribution shift (trained on MVTec-AD, tested on VisA, MPDD, Real-IAD), DAPO achieves the best image-level AUROC across all targets: 84.9 (VisA), 81.2 (MPDD), and 84.3 (Real-IAD), yielding a 3.6% average improvement in AUROC and AP over the two strongest defect-aware baselines (AnomalyCLIP and MultiADS). For zero-shot multi-type anomaly segmentation, DAPO outperforms MultiADS by +7.2% F1 on MPDD and +6.0% on MAD-Real, with a 5.2% average gain in AUROC and F1 on unseen defect types. On the internal ASIC dataset, DAPO reaches 91.8 AUROC / 79.1 AP, substantially outperforming AnomalyCLIP (68.2 / 35.3) and MultiADS (91.1 / 61.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAPO consistently outperforms all six baselines on image-level AUROC and AP across VisA, MPDD, and Real-IAD. Against the closest competitor MultiADS, DAPO gains +1.3 AUROC on VisA (84.9 vs 83.6), +2.9 on MPDD (81.2 vs 78.3), and +5.6 on Real-IAD (84.3 vs 78.7); against AnomalyCLIP the margins are +2.8, +4.2, and +5.9 respectively. On the internal ASIC dataset the gap widens dramatically: DAPO (91.8 AUROC / 79.1 AP) nearly doubles AnomalyCLIP's AP (35.3) and exceeds MultiADS by 17.4 AP points, suggesting defect-aware prompting is critical under real production conditions. At the pixel level, DAPO's AUPRO (80–84) trails MultiADS and AnomalyCLIP (87–90) because its conservative heatmaps highlight discriminative evidence rather than expanding to full annotated regions. For multi-type segmentation (Table 1), DAPO wins on AP and F1 for MPDD (+7.2 F1) and MAD-Real (+6.0 F1) but slightly underperforms MultiADS on AUROC for VisA and MAD-Sim, where 'missing-component' defects lack local visual signal for patch-based alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAPO sits at the intersection of CLIP-based zero-shot anomaly detection (AnomalyCLIP, WinCLIP, AdaCLIP) and multi-type defect segmentation (MultiADS). It departs from purely learnable prompt approaches (AnomalyCLIP, CoOp/CoCoOp) by anchoring prompts with fixed defect-name tokens, and from hand-crafted prompt baselines (MultiADS) by introducing a small set of shared learnable context tokens that are reused across defect types. The hybrid composition—shared learnable tokens plus a swappable textual anchor—enables zero-shot instantiation of previously unseen defect descriptors without retraining, a capability absent in both the soft-prompt and hand-crafted-prompt lines of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in industrial inspection: distinguishing not just whether a defect exists but which type it is, enabling root-cause tracing and targeted corrective action. The shared-token design offers a scalable path to new defect categories (swap the anchor, keep the learned context), reducing the manual prompt engineering burden of MultiADS. Key limitations remain: absence-based defects (missing components) are poorly handled because patch-level ViT features lack object-level priors, and the conservative prediction strategy trades AUPRO for precision. Open questions include extending to other VLM backbones, incorporating object-level semantics for absence detection, and few-shot adaptation for rare defect types.
