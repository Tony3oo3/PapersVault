## Problem

Existing multi-teacher distillation frameworks force a student to perform direct, simultaneous feature alignment with multiple heterogeneous Vision Foundation Models (e.g., CLIP, MAE, DINOv3), which have disparate architectures and divergent pre-training objectives. This one-to-many matching induces representational dissonance and gradient interference, where signals from one teacher wash out the nuances of another, and existing methods resort to billion-scale proxy datasets to brute-force consensus, restricting applicability to specific architectures like ViTs.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (224×224 for classification, up to 4096×4096 for dense prediction) and produces a unified student model whose features can be adapted to classification, segmentation, depth estimation, and VQA. A key design detail is that all visual features—whether from CNN feature maps (H×W spatial tokens) or ViT patch sequences—are recast into a unified token representation, and the learnable tokens are channel-concatenated (not sequence-concatenated) with projected student features to avoid attention pollution from semantically conflicting teacher tokens. Spatial resolution mismatches between hybrid CNN-ViT teacher pairs are handled via bilinear interpolation to a common 4D tensor shape.

## Method

ACTok introduces learnable tokens (P_m) as flexible neural proxies that mediate knowledge transfer between a student and M heterogeneous VFM teachers, decoupling the student backbone from direct multi-teacher alignment. The core mechanism is an alternating interaction process: a cross-space module (depth-wise + grouped point-wise convolutions for CNNs, or enlarged-head attention for ViTs) aggregates consensus knowledge across all M projected feature spaces, followed by a parameter-shared intra-space module that consolidates space-specific knowledge within each teacher's domain; this alternation repeats N times. After the interaction, the learnable token is split from the student feature and aligned globally (Frobenius norm to each teacher's global token), while the student feature is aligned at the patch level using teacher-specific objectives (cosine similarity for CLIP/DINO, MSE otherwise). The framework is architecture-agnostic, requiring no modifications to the student backbone, and achieves competitive performance using only ImageNet-1K (1.2M images) rather than billion-scale proxy datasets.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification with a ViT-B/16 student distilled from CLIP, MAE, and DINOv3 ViT-B teachers using only 1.2M images, ACTok achieves 85.6% top-1 accuracy, surpassing Proteus (84.9%) and RADIOv2.5 (84.8%) while approaching DINOv3 (86.2%). On fine-grained classification, ACTok reaches 94.4% on Caltech101 (beating all individual teachers) and 91.1% on Aircraft (outperforming RADIOv2.5 by 7.6%). For dense prediction, the ViT-B student gains 24.3 mIoU over the MAE teacher on ADE20K segmentation (46.5 vs. 22.2) and improves depth estimation RMSE on NYUv2 from 0.347 to 0.300.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two primary distillation baselines, ACTok (ViT-B) outperforms Proteus on ImageNet (85.6 vs. 84.9), Caltech101 (94.4 vs. 91.9), and depth estimation (0.300 vs. 0.304 RMSE), while RADIOv2.5 is surpassed by large margins on fine-grained tasks (e.g., +7.6% on Aircraft, +3.2% on CUB) but retains a slight edge on ADE20K segmentation (48.9 vs. 46.5 mIoU). Compared to individual VFM teachers, ACTok exceeds CLIP on all fine-grained benchmarks and MAE on dense prediction (24.3 mIoU gain on ADE20K), yet DINOv3 remains stronger on ImageNet (86.2 vs. 85.6), Aircraft (92.6 vs. 91.1), and segmentation (50.9 vs. 46.5 mIoU). For CNN students (Table 2), ACTok-distilled ResNet-50 improves over the standard ImageNet-trained baseline on segmentation (20.5 vs. 12.4 mIoU) and depth (0.408 vs. 0.439 RMSE), while the ConvNeXt-B student with hybrid ViT teachers outperforms the Spark baseline on ImageNet (84.1 vs. 84.8 is actually Spark winning) — however, the all-ViT teacher ensemble yields the best overall CNN results. On the VQA task, ACTok surpasses E-RADIO-L on POPE and is comparable on VQAv2. Notably, ACTok achieves these results using only 1.2M ImageNet images, whereas RADIOv2.5 requires ~1.4B images, making it orders of magnitude more data-efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the direct feature-alignment paradigm established by AM-RADIO and RADIO-v2.5, which enforce simultaneous student-to-teacher matching across multiple VFMs and rely on billion-scale proxy datasets to resolve conflicts through brute-force consensus. Instead, ACTok reframes multi-teacher distillation as a token-mediated aggregation process, borrowing the learnable-token concept from multi-view aggregation (VGGT) and grounding representations, but repurposing it to mediate between heterogeneous teacher representational spaces via an alternating cross-space/intra-space interaction mechanism. The approach also extends traditional ensemble distillation (Hinton et al., You et al.) from conventional networks to the VFM setting while remaining architecture-agnostic across CNN and ViT students, unlike prior VFM distillation methods that are largely restricted to ViT-only pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical impact is data efficiency: ACTok achieves competitive multi-teacher distillation using only ImageNet-1K (1.2M images) rather than billion-scale datasets, making VFM knowledge consolidation accessible without prohibitive compute. The counter-intuitive finding that homogeneous ViT teacher ensembles outperform heterogeneous CNN-ViT mixes for CNN students challenges the natural assumption that architectural matching aids distillation, suggesting weaker inductive biases and finer spatial granularity in ViTs provide more transferable supervision. Key open questions include whether the approach scales gracefully to more than three teachers, how to mitigate the dataset bias between ImageNet-1K and web-scale pre-training corpora (e.g., LVD-1689M), and whether the token-mediated paradigm can be extended to video or 3D foundation models.
