## Problem

FR models trained on diffusion-based synthetic face data still underperform those trained on real images, due to two coupled limitations: (1) the controllable style space is constrained by capacity-limited conditioning signals, making balanced long-tail coverage difficult, and (2) identity is anchored to a single prototype embedding per subject, suppressing the intra-identity variation that naturally exists in real FR embeddings. No prior IPFS pipeline explicitly factorizes embedding-irrelevant style factors into recombinable tokens or models the embedding-inherent variation in a principled way.

## Input / Output

The model takes a pretrained FR identity embedding (e.g., from ArcFace/IR-50), a structured set of ~15 discrete text attribute tokens (covering pose, expression, age, illumination, background, hair, accessories), and a second reference identity embedding sampled from the same prototype pool. At inference, the dual-conditioned diffusion sampler produces a single synthetic face image per forward pass; iterating over virtual identity prototypes and reweighted attribute combinations yields datasets of 0.5M–6M images (50 per identity by default). The text attributes are extracted from real training images via pretrained detectors and a VLM (Qwen3-VL), then reweighted with an exponent α∈[0,1] to upsample long-tail values.

## Method

TextFace is a latent diffusion model with two dedicated cross-attention pathways in the U-Net: one for identity tokens (projected from the FR embedding) and one for text tokens (encoded by CLIP), injected in series across all stages. For embedding-irrelevant style diversity, the paper builds a discrete compositional attribute space regularized by attribute shuffling and dropout during training, and reweighted sampling during inference to expand long-tail coverage. For embedding-inherent variation, the key contribution is identity blending in score space: at each denoising step, the model computes dual-CFG guided noise predictions under two valid prototype embeddings and takes a convex combination (Eq. 14), which is derived as the score of a geometric mixture distribution (Eq. 11) that stays close to the anchor identity while injecting controlled perturbation. This preserves the text-controlled component of the denoiser output while perturbing only the identity-dependent residual, avoiding the extrapolation instability of embedding interpolation. A dual-condition CFG formulation separately weights identity and text guidance, keeping each condition fixed when estimating the other's contribution.

## Results

At the standard 0.5M synthetic-image scale (10K identities × 50 images), TextFace achieves the highest average verification accuracy of 93.91% across LFW, CFP-FP, CPLFW, AgeDB, and CALFW, surpassing the next-best MorphFace (93.32%) and UIFace (93.27%). Scaling to 6.0M images (120K × 50), TextFace reaches 95.50% average, with peak scores of 99.72% on LFW, 92.42% on CPLFW, and 95.52% on AgeDB, substantially narrowing the gap to real-data training on MS1MV2 (96.56% avg.) and Glint360K (97.34% avg.). The method also outperforms the real CASIA-WebFace training set (94.83% avg.) at the 5.0M synthetic scale (95.34% avg.).

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the 0.5M scale, TextFace (93.91% avg.) outperforms all prior IPFS methods, including MorphFace (93.32%), UIFace (93.27%), and VIGFace (92.56%), with the largest margins over GAN-based SynFace (74.75%) and 3D-based DigiFace (83.45%). The one benchmark where TextFace trails is CFP-FP at 0.5M (93.80% vs. VIGFace's 95.09%), which the authors attribute to VIGFace's use of precise 5-point landmarks for pose control versus TextFace's ~15 discrete textual attributes. At 1.0M and 1.2M scales, TextFace leads on LFW, CPLFW, and CALFW while remaining competitive on CFP-FP and AgeDB. At 5.0M and 6.0M scales, TextFace dominates all five benchmarks over the available baselines (HSFace at 5.0M: 95.34% vs. 92.97%; VIGFace at 6.0M: 95.50% vs. 94.91%), and notably surpasses the real CASIA-WebFace training set (94.83%) at the 5.0M synthetic scale. The gap to the strongest real-data baselines (MS1MV2 at 96.56%, Glint360K at 97.34%) narrows to roughly 1–2 points at 6.0M, a substantial improvement over prior synthetic pipelines that typically lag by 3–5 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

TextFace sits within the diffusion-based identity-preserving face synthesis (IPFS) lineage that began with IDiff-Face and DCFace and has been extended by Arc2Face, CemiFace, ID³, VIGFace, UIFace, and MorphFace. It departs from this line in two conceptual ways: (1) it replaces capacity-limited conditioning signals (landmarks, 3DMM maps, prototype+landmark pairs) with a compositional, language-based attribute space for style diversity, and (2) it introduces score-space identity blending—derived from the logarithmic opinion pool formulation of Heskes (1997)—as a principled alternative to anchoring generation to a single prototype embedding. The dual-condition CFG weighting scheme is adapted from MorphFace, and the entire architecture rests on the LDM/Stable Diffusion backbone with classifier-free guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TextFace narrows the synthetic-to-real performance gap for face recognition (95.50% avg. at 6M images vs. 97.34% for Glint360K real data), making privacy-friendly FR training more practical. The score-space blending insight—that blending denoiser predictions at two valid anchors is more stable than interpolating embeddings in the conditioning space—offers a general recipe for injecting controlled variation in any conditional diffusion setting. However, the gap to real-data training remains non-trivial, CFP-FP performance still lags behind VIGFace's landmark-conditioned approach, and the method's reliance on a VLM for attribute extraction and a large prototype pool raises practical deployment questions. Open questions include whether the compositional attribute framework generalizes to other biometric or identity-anchored generation tasks, and whether the blending strategy can be extended to multi-reference or distribution-level identity modeling.
