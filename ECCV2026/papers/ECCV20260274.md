## Problem

Existing instruction-based image editing models falter under Instruction-Visual Complexity (IV-Complexity), where cluttered scenes with multiple similar objects meet instructions requiring fine-grained referring, implicit semantics, or world-knowledge reasoning. Unified VLM+diffusion approaches treat the VLM as a coarse semantic encoder, failing to exploit its fine-grained grounding and reasoning to precisely localize and execute edits in complex scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260274/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a natural-language editing instruction as input. The VLM planner first produces structured region-aligned guidance — a set of bounding-box/hint pairs in JSON format (including a global hint for the full image and per-region hints for local edits, with optional negative hints to protect unchanged areas). The diffusion decoder then consumes this guidance to produce a single edited image, supporting multiple regional edits in one forward pass without iterative inpainting.

## Method

RePlan is a plan-then-execute framework coupling a VLM planner (Qwen2.5-VL 7B) with an MMDiT diffusion decoder (e.g., Flux Kontext). The planner performs chain-of-thought reasoning to decompose the instruction into region-hint pairs, and the decoder executes them via a training-free attention region injection: a binary mask with five rules (intra-group interaction, hint isolation, image-latent full interaction, region constraint, background constraint) restricts which tokens attend to which, preventing cross-region instruction contamination while preserving global coherence. The planner is further refined with two-stage GRPO reinforcement learning on only ~1K instruction-only samples — Stage 1 rewards format validity and reasoning length, Stage 2 adds image-level rewards (target, effect, consistency) from a 72B VLM judge. A rule-group attention acceleration using FlexAttention reduces the mask overhead from O(n²) to O(n·⌈k/W⌉), enabling near-FlashAttention speed even with multiple regions.

## Results

On the IV-Edit benchmark (800 instruction-image pairs), the best RePlan configuration (Flux.2-Klein-9B + RePlan) achieves an Overall score of 3.45 and Weighted score of 2.95 (out of 5), outperforming all open-source baselines. It attains the highest Quality (4.05) and Target (3.81) among open-source models, and the best Consistency (2.61) among open-source models. In a blinded human preference study (100 samples, 12 annotators), RePlan is preferred over the Flux.1-Kontext-dev baseline in 70% of non-tie cases (35% preferred vs. 15% for baseline, 50% ties).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RePlan (Flux.2-Klein-9B backbone) outperforms all open-source baselines on Overall: Bagel-Think (3.05), Uniworld-V1 (2.45), and InstructPix2Pix (2.06), with the largest gap over InstructPix2Pix (+1.39 Overall). Against the closed-source GPT-4o, RePlan wins on Overall (3.45 vs. 3.30) and Consistency (2.61 vs. 1.77), though GPT-4o retains an edge on Target (4.02 vs. 3.81). The strongest closed-source model, Gemini-Flash-Image, still leads on Overall (3.71 vs. 3.45) and Consistency (2.89 vs. 2.61), but RePlan surpasses it on Quality (4.05 vs. 3.89). Compared to its own backbone without RePlan (Flux.1-Kontext-dev), the human preference study shows a 70% win rate in non-tie comparisons, confirming the planner's contribution. RePlan also demonstrates backbone-agnostic gains: applying it to Qwen-Image-Edit lifts Overall from the base model's level to 3.05 with the best Target score (3.72) among all RePlan variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

RePlan builds directly on the MMDiT/Flux.1-Kontext architecture for diffusion-based editing and on Qwen2.5-VL as the planning backbone, but departs from prior VLM-guided editing methods (Qwen-Image, Bagel, UniWorld) that treat VLMs as coarse semantic encoders. Instead, it refines the VLM–diffusion interface to the region level: the VLM outputs explicit bounding boxes with per-region editing hints, and a training-free attention mask mechanism enforces region-specific token visibility inside the MMDiT. The GRPO reinforcement learning stage, adapted from DeepSeekMath-style group-relative optimization, is applied here for the first time to structured visual-editing planning with only ~1K instruction-only samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IV-Edit benchmark (~800 pairs) is the first to systematically stress-test fine-grained grounding, multi-region disambiguation, and knowledge-intensive reasoning in editing, filling a gap left by prior benchmarks that favor salient single-object scenes. The plug-and-play attention injection is practically useful for any MMDiT-based editor needing multi-region control without iterative inpainting. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond MMDiT architectures, how it scales to scenes with dozens of interacting regions, and whether the VLM's bounding-box accuracy (a known weakness even for strong models like Gemini-2.5-Pro) remains the bottleneck as visual complexity grows further.
