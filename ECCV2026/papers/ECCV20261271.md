## Problem

Multimodal medical image registration (e.g., MR-CT) is hindered by large appearance gaps that prevent registration encoders from learning modality-invariant anatomical correspondences. While vision foundation models (VFMs) like DINOv3 produce rich cross-modal representations, directly applying them to 3D medical volumes faces prohibitive computational cost, a 2D-to-3D domain shift, and a mismatch between general-purpose features and the localized goal of deformable registration. The paper asks how to transfer VFM cross-modal knowledge into a lightweight registration encoder so that the VFM is needed only during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired 3D MR and CT volumes (192×160×192 on Learn2Reg; 160×192×192 at 1 mm isotropic on SR-Reg) and outputs a dense 3D deformation field that warps the moving image to the fixed image. A key design detail is that the foundation-encoder branch reformats each 3D volume into a sequence of 2D three-channel inputs via the Data ReAssembly module, while the registration backbone still processes the full 3D volumes, so the predicted deformation field preserves volumetric consistency. At inference the VFM is discarded entirely, leaving only the lightweight registration network (≈1 M parameters for ModeT-based, ≈0.66 M for PiViT-based).

## Method

The framework augments a dual-stream registration backbone (ModeT or PiViT) with a frozen DINOv3 (ViT-B/16) foundation encoder whose features guide the registration encoder via a cosine-similarity alignment loss. Two novel modules bridge the domain gap: (1) Data ReAssembly extracts three adjacent axial slices (S_{n-1}, S_n, S_{n+1}) from the 3D volume, applies a learnable 1×1 convolution to synthesize two context channels, and concatenates them with the central slice to form a non-redundant 3-channel 2D input compatible with the VFM; (2) Hierarchical Representation Fusion extracts features from four intermediate ViT layers (3, 6, 9, 12), resamples each to the 3D spatial grid, and combines them with softmax-normalized learnable attention weights before a final convolution projects them to match the registration encoder's channel dimension. Training minimizes a total loss comprising the representation alignment loss, a mutual-information similarity loss (optionally augmented with Dice loss on segmentation labels), and a gradient-based smoothness regularizer on the deformation field.

## Results

On the SR-Reg brain MR-CT dataset, the proposed framework (Ours_M on ModeT backbone) achieves a DSC of 79.04% and HD95 of 1.97 mm, improving over the ModeT baseline by 6.74% in DSC and 0.61 mm in HD95, while maintaining only 1.03M parameters. On the more challenging Learn2Reg abdomen MR-CT dataset, Ours_M reaches 76.16% DSC and 7.12 mm HD95, outperforming all 10 competing methods. Cross-dataset transfer experiments (Learn2Reg → SR-Reg) confirm generalizability, with Ours_M and Ours_P consistently exceeding their respective backbones by ~2% DSC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed framework consistently outperforms all 8 baselines on both datasets. On SR-Reg, Ours_M (79.04% DSC) surpasses the strongest baseline UTSRMorph (75.20%) by 3.84% DSC and the backbone ModeT (72.30%) by 6.74%, while using the same 1.03M parameters as ModeT and far fewer than UTSRMorph (98.82M) or TransMorph (46.76M). On Learn2Reg, Ours_M (76.16% DSC, 7.12 mm HD95) beats NiftyReg (72.61%), LapIRN (70.91%), VoxelMorph (67.81%), TransMorph (70.97%), TransMatch (70.45%), UTSRMorph (75.22%), and PiViT (67.15%). The framework also demonstrates backbone-agnostic gains: when integrated into PiViT (Ours_P), it improves DSC from 67.15% to 71.26% on Learn2Reg and from 66.83% to 68.36% in cross-dataset transfer, confirming that the representation alignment strategy is transferable across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of VFM-assisted medical image registration (DINOReg, LLM-Morph, SAM-based methods) by shifting the VFM's role from inference-time feature extraction or auxiliary input to a training-only representation teacher. It builds on dual-stream registration backbones (ModeT, PiViT) and augments them with a foundation-guided alignment loss, departing from prior work that either requires the full VFM at inference or embeds large model layers directly into the network. The two proposed components—Data ReAssembly (bridging 3D volumes to 2D VFM inputs without redundant slice repetition) and Hierarchical Representation Fusion (aggregating multi-level ViT features rather than relying solely on the final layer)—address specific domain-shift and information-loss issues that prior VFM-for-registration methods overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's key practical advantage is that the VFM is discarded at inference, yielding a lightweight registration network suitable for clinical deployment—addressing a major limitation of DINOReg and LLM-Morph. The demonstrated backbone-agnosticism (consistent gains on both ModeT and PiViT) and cross-dataset generalization (frozen encoder transfer from abdomen to brain) suggest the approach could be broadly adopted as a plug-in for existing registration pipelines. Open questions remain: the method's performance is bounded by the cross-domain generalization of 2D natural-image VFMs to 3D medical volumes, and the reliance on a single foundation model (DINOv3) raises questions about robustness when the teacher provides biased or unreliable representations for certain anatomies. The trade-off between representational richness (large natural-image pretraining) and domain specificity (medical pretraining) is acknowledged but not resolved.
