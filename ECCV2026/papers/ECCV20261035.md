## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based style transfer methods treat geometry as a constraint to preserve rather than a medium for style, resulting in superficial color-only stylization that cannot capture the structural character of a target style (e.g., blocky patterns, particle-like forms). Simultaneously optimizing color and geometry in 3DGS is unstable because minor geometric miscalculations drastically degrade visual quality, so prior work adopts conservative designs that leave geometry fixed or only lightly perturbed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-reconstructed 3DGS scene (from multi-view content images across LLFF, Tanks & Temples, and MipNeRF-360) and a single 2D style image. The style image's depth is estimated via Depth Anything V2, and its edge map is extracted from the depth (not the color) to avoid noise from complex textures. The output is a fully stylized 3DGS scene where only the DC (zeroth-order) spherical harmonic color component and the geometry parameters (3D position, covariance, opacity) are modified, while higher-order SH coefficients remain fixed, enabling real-time rendering from arbitrary viewpoints.

## Method

The core contribution is a decoupled optimization scheme that alternates between color-only updates (10 inner steps) and geometry-only updates (90 inner steps) over 30 outer cycles, preventing the interference and over-stylization that arises from joint optimization. Both phases are driven by the proposed Geometry-Aware Contrastive Feature Matching (GCFM) loss, which extracts VGG-16 features from three modalities (RGB, depth, edge) of both the rendered and style images, concatenates them channel-wise, and for each spatial location in the rendered feature map identifies the nearest (positive) and farthest (negative) counterparts in the style feature map via cosine distance. The contrastive objective pulls each rendered feature toward its positive style counterpart while pushing it away from its negative counterpart, enforcing discriminative structural alignment. A geometry regularization term (active only during the geometry phase) penalizes deviations in opacity, scale, rotation, and rendered depth from the initial color-matched state to prevent Gaussian degeneration.

## Results

Across 72 scene–style combinations (8 scenes × 9 styles), the proposed method achieves the best SIFID of 1.1736, outperforming the next-best G-Style (1.2955) and SGSST (1.3936), and substantially beating StyleGaussian (3.3405) and CLIPGaussian (3.3685). Excluding StylizedGS (whose consistency advantage stems from overly smooth, desaturated renderings), the method ranks first in both short-range and long-range LPIPS and RMSE, indicating the best balance between style fidelity and multi-view consistency. Stylization completes in ~14 minutes per scene, roughly 2× faster than G-Style (29 min) and ~29× faster than StyleGaussian (410 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against five 3DGS-based style transfer baselines. Against StyleGaussian [21] and CLIPGaussian [8], the proposed method achieves a dramatic SIFID improvement (1.1736 vs. 3.3405 and 3.3685 respectively) while also winning on all four consistency metrics, reflecting both superior style fidelity and better multi-view coherence. Against G-Style [18], the closest competitor, the method wins on SIFID (1.1736 vs. 1.2955) and edges out on every consistency metric (e.g., short-range RMSE 0.034 vs. 0.035, long-range LPIPS 0.114 vs. 0.118). Against SGSST [4], the method leads on SIFID (1.1736 vs. 1.3936) and all consistency scores. The only baseline that outperforms the proposed method on consistency metrics is StylizedGS [36], but the authors attribute this to its overly smooth and desaturated renderings that artificially inflate consistency scores while sacrificing style fidelity (SIFID 2.2439 vs. 1.1736). In a user study with 37 participants, the proposed method was rated highest on style similarity, visual appeal, and content recognizability across all four tested scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS-based style transfer line (StyleGaussian, G-Style, StylizedGS, SGSST, CLIPGaussian) by explicitly treating geometry as a stylistic medium rather than a fixed constraint. It builds on feature-matching-based style transfer (ARF, Geometry Transfer) but departs from the positive-pair-only matching by introducing a contrastive objective with both positive and negative pairs, and from single-modal (RGB) matching by integrating depth and edge cues via GCFM. The decoupled optimization scheme—alternating color and geometry updates within outer cycles—is the key architectural departure, addressing the instability that prior joint-optimization approaches (e.g., CLIPGaussian) encounter when geometry and color gradients interfere.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap in 3DGS stylization: prior methods produce 'superficial' results that conform color to existing geometry without reshaping structure, limiting expressive potential. The decoupled optimization provides a practical, well-motivated recipe for stable geometry-aware stylization that could be adopted by other 3DGS editing tasks. Limitations include per-scene optimization (no real-time inference), reliance on a single style image (no text or multi-style control), and evaluation on relatively simple bounded scenes; open questions include scaling to large dynamic scenes, user-controllable geometry deformation strength, and whether the decoupled scheme generalizes to other 3DGS parameter-editing problems beyond style transfer.
