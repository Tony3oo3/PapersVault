## Problem

Standard 3D Gaussian Splatting and other 3D reconstruction methods fail on thermal infrared imagery due to a signal-to-noise inversion: thermal diffusion smooths true geometric boundaries into low-frequency blur, while uncooled microbolometer artifacts (fixed pattern noise, non-uniformity response) introduce structured high-frequency textures that mislead optimization into fitting hardware noise as spurious geometry. Compounding this, off-the-shelf thermal cameras produce inherently low-resolution observations, making high-fidelity super-resolution novel view synthesis from LR TIR inputs an unsolved problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view low-resolution TIR image sequences (4× bicubic-downsampled from native 720×480 or 480×640 resolutions) and produces both a learned 3D Gaussian Splatting scene representation and high-resolution (4×) novel TIR views rendered natively from the continuous Gaussian primitives. Supervision is applied in a calibrated observation domain: the HR rendering is mapped through a differentiable imaging chain (degradation modeling + tone adaptation) before being compared to the LR observations, so gradients flow back to the 3D primitives through this aligned space rather than directly from raw sensor pixels.

## Method

SupIR-GS introduces three coupled modules that operate between the 3DGS radiance rendering and the LR observation. First, PhysIR-Cal is a differentiable forward-calibration layer parameterized by learnable 16×16 gain/bias grids (for NUR/FPN), a radial vignetting model, and an optional view-dependent logit-domain tone mapping (for AGC), which decouples scene radiance from device-dependent sensor responses. Second, IRTone-Adapter is a per-pixel MLP that predicts tightly bounded local affine residuals (±0.08 gain, ±0.04 bias) to surface latent thermal gradients in texture-scarce regions without masking geometric errors. Third, a Frequency-Aware Curriculum Learning strategy uses a pretrained SwinIR-S teacher to generate confidence-masked high-frequency edge priors, activated via a cosine ramp-up schedule that shifts optimization from low-frequency photometric/SSIM losses to high-frequency detail refinement. The ablation reveals a strict dependency: PhysIR-Cal must establish a denoised geometric scaffold before the high-frequency prior and tone adapter become effective, yielding a 'denoise → enhance → refine' training sequence.

## Results

On the TI-NSD dataset (4× super-resolution from LR TIR inputs), SupIR-GS achieves 34.81 dB PSNR / 0.9455 SSIM / 0.2597 LPIPS (Indoor), 29.94 dB / 0.9066 / 0.2336 (Outdoor), and 34.24 dB / 0.9428 / 0.1453 (UAV), consistently outperforming all baselines. On ThermoScenes, it reaches 31.32 dB PSNR and 0.3368 °C MAE (Indoor) and 30.40 dB / 0.7957 °C MAE (Outdoor), with the lowest MAE_ROI among 3D-reconstruction baselines. In terms of efficiency, SupIR-GS trains in 16.42 min with 2792 MB peak memory and renders at 381 FPS, the highest throughput among compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SupIR-GS outperforms all six baseline groups across every subset of both datasets. Against the strongest 3D-SR baseline SRGS, it gains +2.72 dB PSNR (Indoor), +2.56 dB (Outdoor), and +2.46 dB (UAV) on TI-NSD, with LPIPS improvements of 0.017–0.056. Compared to direct 4× rendering of standard 3DGS variants, the margins are much larger: +6.58 dB over 3DGS(4×) and +12.60 dB over Scaffold-GS(4×) in the UAV subset, where thermal diffusion and FPN artifacts most severely degrade geometry. The cascaded DiffIISR+3DGS pipeline, while competitive on PSNR in Indoor scenes (30.45 vs. 34.81 dB), lags by 3–5 dB in Outdoor and UAV settings due to lack of multi-view consistency. Thermal3DGS(4×), the closest thermal-specific baseline, is surpassed by 4.66 dB (Indoor) and 10.84 dB (UAV) PSNR. On ThermoScenes, SupIR-GS achieves the lowest MAE_ROI (1.79 °C Indoor, 0.73 °C Outdoor) among 3D-reconstruction methods, confirming superior temperature fidelity for heated targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to the thermal infrared super-resolution novel-view synthesis setting, building on the 3DGS rendering pipeline [14] and its anti-aliasing/structure variants (Mip-Splatting, Scaffold-GS) as well as thermal-specific adaptations like Thermal3DGS [3] and ThermalGaussian [19]. The key conceptual departure is from prior thermal NVS methods that assume well-calibrated inputs and apply standard photometric supervision: here, a differentiable forward-imaging calibration layer (PhysIR-Cal) explicitly models NUR/FPN/vignetting degradations between the clean radiance rendering and the observed LR image, inverting the typical 'fit to raw pixels' paradigm. The frequency-aware curriculum learning draws on the spectral-bias literature and the SwinIR teacher, while the IRTone-Adapter is a lightweight analogue of local tone-mapping modules in HDR/low-light restoration, repurposed to surface latent thermal gradients in texture-scarce regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly decoupling sensor-induced high-frequency artifacts from scene radiance during 3DGS optimization, the paper addresses a fundamental mismatch (signal-to-noise inversion) that has limited the transfer of visible-spectrum 3D reconstruction to practical thermal imaging, where off-the-shelf sensors are inherently low-resolution. The ablation revealing that uncalibrated contrast enhancement actively degrades geometry (IRTone-Adapter alone drops PSNR) provides a useful cautionary principle for any degraded-imaging 3D reconstruction. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond 4× SR, to cooled sensors with different noise profiles, or to real-time streaming applications; the reliance on a cross-domain (RGB-pretrained) SwinIR teacher and the ~60% training overhead relative to vanilla 3DGS also limit immediate deployment. The smoothness-bias observation—that naive interpolation achieves competitive PSNR/MAE by erasing geometry—highlights a metric blind spot that the field should address in thermal evaluation protocols.
