## Problem

Standard text-only Chain-of-Thought in MLLMs forces continuous visual signals into discrete tokens early in the pipeline, causing 'early semantic collapse' that discards fine-grained visual evidence. While latent reasoning paradigms internalize visual states to avoid this, optimizing the resulting hybrid discrete-continuous action space with reinforcement learning remains unsolved: uniform clipping and Gaussian action assumptions fundamentally mismatch the hyperspherical geometry of layer-normalized LLM representations and the vastly different variance of discrete versus continuous importance ratios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261933/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a standard RGB image and a text question, producing a text answer. Internally, generation alternates between discrete vocabulary tokens and continuous 'canvas' steps bounded by special <|canvas_start|> and <|canvas_end|> control tokens; during canvas mode, the previous layer's hidden state is recurrently fed back as the next input embedding, bypassing the token vocabulary entirely. By default 16 latent canvas tokens are used at inference, and the SFT-stage canvas compressor (SigLIP2 encoder + cross-attention module) is discarded, incurring zero architectural overhead.

## Method

HyLaR extends a standard MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) into a hybrid discrete-continuous action space where the model dynamically switches between text generation and continuous latent recursion via control tokens. Training is two-stage: (1) cold-start SFT jointly optimizes cross-entropy for text and an MSE canvas loss that aligns hidden states with compressed ground-truth canvas embeddings (16 query tokens from a SigLIP2 + cross-attention compressor), with gradients flowing end-to-end into both the LLM backbone and compressor; (2) DePO (Decoupled Policy Optimization) refines the hybrid trajectory via RL with two key mechanisms: decoupled trust-region clipping that applies tighter bounds to latent positions (ε=0.05) than text positions (ε=0.2/0.28) to counteract the higher ratio volatility of continuous vMF actions, and a von Mises-Fisher distribution for the latent policy that exploits the native hyperspherical geometry of LLM hidden states, yielding an exact closed-form KL regularizer as a scaled cosine distance rather than high-variance sample-based estimation.

## Results

On high-resolution perception benchmarks, HyLaR-7B achieves 83.77% on V*, 75.00% on HRBench-4K, and 70.50% on HRBench-8K, improving over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone by +7.33, +7.00, and +6.75 points respectively. On general multimodal VQA, HyLaR-7B scores 73.67% (MMVP), 62.00% (MMStar), 70.32% (SeedBench2Plus), 57.14% (BLINK), and 63.68% (HallusionBench), with the largest gain of +7.11 points on HallusionBench over the backbone. Inference efficiency is preserved: HyLaR runs at 0.96× latency and 0.98× memory of the Qwen2.5-VL-7B long-CoT baseline, versus 2.28× and 3.64× for the multi-turn DeepEyes agent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, HyLaR-7B gains +7.33 on V*, +7.00 on HRBench-4K, and +6.75 on HRBench-8K, confirming that the hybrid latent reasoning mechanism adds substantial value over text-only CoT. Among visual-latent baselines, HyLaR-7B outperforms SkiLa (78.53→83.77 on V*, 72.12→75.00 on HRBench-4K) and Monet (80.1→83.77 on V*, 67.37→75.00 on HRBench-4K) by 3.7–7.6 points, establishing state-of-the-art among latent reasoning approaches. Against Think-with-Images agents, HyLaR-7B is competitive but slightly trails DeepEyes (85.6 on V*, 75.1 on HRBench-4K) and Thyme (77.0 on HRBench-4K, 72.0 on HRBench-8K), though it does so without external tool calls, achieving 0.96× latency versus 2.28× for DeepEyes. On general VQA (Table 2), HyLaR-7B leads all latent reasoning baselines on MMVP (73.67 vs. Laser 72.00, Monet 68.00) and HallusionBench (63.68 vs. Laser 67.72, LVR 65.19), while remaining competitive with agent models like ZoomEye (71.08 on HallusionBench). The 3B-scale experiment (Table 4) confirms the framework generalizes: HyLaR-3B beats both Qwen2.5-VL-3B and LVR-3B across all reported benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

HyLaR extends the latent-reasoning line of work (LVR, SkiLa, Monet, Laser) that internalizes visual thinking into continuous hidden states, but departs from these prior methods by adding a principled RL stage rather than relying solely on SFT or ad-hoc RL. The core algorithmic contribution, DePO, modifies PPO/GRPO-style policy optimization by recognizing two mismatches specific to hybrid discrete-continuous trajectories: (1) the importance-ratio variance differs sharply between token and latent positions, motivating decoupled trust-region clipping, and (2) layer-normalized LLM representations reside on a hypersphere, so the continuous policy is modeled with a von Mises–Fisher distribution rather than a Gaussian, yielding a closed-form cosine-distance KL. This geometric insight draws on prior representation-learning results (Wang & Isola 2020; Davidson et al. 2018) but applies them concretely to policy optimization in MLLMs for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is twofold: HyLaR achieves state-of-the-art latent-reasoning performance on high-resolution perception benchmarks while matching or beating tool-augmented agents (DeepEyes, Thyme) at 0.96× the inference latency and memory, removing the external-tool bottleneck. The DePO recipe—vMF spherical policy + decoupled clipping + closed-form KL—is a drop-in modification to standard PPO/GRPO pipelines that could generalize to other hybrid action spaces beyond vision. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 7B, how it behaves in open-ended agentic settings with longer horizons, and whether the SFT canvas-compression target is truly necessary or whether the latent space could be shaped entirely by RL rewards.
