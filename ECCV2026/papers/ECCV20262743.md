## Problem

Mineral prospectivity mapping is a positive-only problem: only sparse known deposits are labeled, while unlabeled regions are not reliable negatives, and the same geophysical signature can correspond to multiple plausible subsurface states. Existing pipelines either introduce biased pseudo-negatives or output a single deterministic score map, failing to capture the inherent one-to-many mapping from geo-images to occurrence locations. This paper reframes prospectivity as learning a conditional spatial density over occurrence points from positives alone, enabling probabilistic, multi-modal predictions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-channel geo-image tensor d ∈ R^{C×H×W} (e.g., magnetics, gravity, geochemistry, topography channels) as input and produces a stochastic set of N 2D point coordinates in normalized [0,1]² space representing mineral occurrence locations. Points are bilinearly sampled from the UNet feature map during both training and inference, and the number of generated points N is a free Monte Carlo budget (not an estimate of true deposit count). Multiple independent draws from the same input yield different point sets, exposing epistemic uncertainty through the spread of the conditional density.

## Method

GeoCFM learns a conditional velocity field v_θ(p, t | d) that transports a 2D Gaussian base distribution to the conditional occurrence density via an ODE, trained with the standard conditional flow matching regression objective (linear interpolation between noise and data points). The architecture conditions the point-wise velocity on the geo-image through a UNet that produces a dense feature map; for each point, local features are extracted via bilinear sampling at the point location, concatenated with the point coordinate and a sinusoidal time embedding, and passed through a shared ResNet MLP head to predict a 2D velocity. This point-conditioned design bridges dense raster inputs and sparse point supervision without requiring any negative samples. Inference integrates the learned ODE with K=50 Euler steps from a Gaussian initial point cloud to produce a generated occurrence set, with K providing a tunable quality-cost tradeoff.

## Results

On the USGS Earth MRI benchmark with a spatially disjoint tile split, GeoCFM achieves a Chamfer distance of 12.30 px, Sinkhorn OT of 0.085, F@5 of 0.151, KDE NLL of 10.500, and top-5% hit rate of 0.250, outperforming the strongest baseline (UNet-Seg) by a factor of ~3.8× on Chamfer distance and ~2.4× on Sinkhorn OT. On the synthetic magnetics-geochemistry benchmark, GeoCFM reaches CD 9.37 px and F@5 0.634, versus 29.46 px and 0.615 for UNet-Seg, while also leading on all remaining metrics. The method is stable under reduced positive availability (CD stays within 9.30–9.75 px on synthetic and 11.76–12.65 px on Earth MRI at 50% positives) and remains competitive with only 5 Euler steps (CD 12.00 on Earth MRI vs. 12.30 at K=50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoCFM wins decisively against every baseline on all five metrics in both benchmarks. Against GBDT (Friedman 2001), the strongest tree-based score-map model, GeoCFM reduces Chamfer distance from 52.98 to 12.30 px on Earth MRI (a 4.3× improvement) and from 32.13 to 9.37 px on the synthetic benchmark (3.4×), while also improving F@5 from 0.069 to 0.151 and top-5% hit rate from 0.102 to 0.250. Against OCSVM (Chen et al. 2019), the one-class positive-only baseline, GeoCFM achieves a 4.7× lower Chamfer distance on Earth MRI (12.30 vs. 58.38 px) and a 2.9× lower Chamfer on synthetic (9.37 vs. 45.56 px), with F@5 more than doubling (0.151 vs. 0.053 on Earth MRI). The gap is largest against non-conditional samplers (Uniform, Global KDE), confirming that conditioning on geo-images is essential. Even the strongest pseudo-negative score-map model, UNet-Seg, is outperformed by GeoCFM on every metric, indicating that the advantage stems from the generative, point-sampling formulation rather than simply better feature extraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoCFM repurposes conditional flow matching (Tong et al. 2023; Lipman et al. 2023) by inverting the standard conditioning direction: rather than generating images from labels, it generates sparse 2D point sets conditioned on dense geo-images. The point-conditioned UNet design draws on PointRend-style local feature sampling (Kirillov et al. 2020) and flow-based point-set generation (PointFlow, Yang et al. 2019), but applies them to a fundamentally different output space (sub-pixel occurrence coordinates rather than 3D point clouds). Conceptually, the paper departs from the decades-long mineral prospectivity tradition of deterministic per-pixel score maps (Bonham-Carter; Carranza; McMillan et al.) and from PU-learning work that still produces calibrated posteriors over pixels, instead treating occurrences as samples from a conditional spatial density that implicitly marginalizes over unobserved geology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing from deterministic score maps to conditional density sampling directly addresses the pseudo-negative bias that has plagued data-driven prospectivity mapping, and the multi-modal sampling output makes epistemic uncertainty explicit for target ranking. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D geological volumes, how to incorporate physics-informed or structural-contact constraints into the flow, and whether the occurrence count N can be estimated adaptively rather than fixed a priori. The evaluation is limited to a single real dataset (USGS Earth MRI) and a synthetic benchmark whose generator is designed to be favorable to the method's assumptions, so generalizability across mineral systems and geophysical modalities remains to be established.
