## Problem

Video diffusion transformers face a compounding double bottleneck: quadratic spatiotemporal attention cost per step multiplied by tens of iterative denoising steps, making both training and inference prohibitively expensive. Existing solutions address these in isolation—efficient architectures (linear/sparse attention) break under aggressive token dropping due to reliance on dense spatial grids, while few-step methods (distillation, consistency models) require expensive pre-trained teachers or auxiliary networks. No prior work provides a unified, teacher-free pipeline that simultaneously reduces per-step compute and sampling steps for video-scale training from scratch.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt (for text-to-video) or a class label (for Kinetics-700) as conditioning and produces a 480p video clip in latent space (decoded via VAE). A key operational detail is that generation completes in only 4 function evaluations (NFEs) versus 50 for standard iterative baselines, and the architecture applies 75% random token dropping to the middle transformer stack during training to reduce sequence length from N to N/4, with a short full-token fine-tuning phase at the end to close the train-inference gap.

## Method

EFLOW combines three innovations on top of the SoFlow solution-flow matching objective (which learns a bi-time function mapping a noised state at time t to any earlier time s, enabling teacher-free few-step training). First, Gated Local-Global Attention (GLGA) replaces softmax self-attention with a hybrid of denominator-free global linear attention (with RoPE) and local sliding-window softmax attention, fused via per-token sigmoid gating; because window attention operates over available tokens rather than a fixed grid, the block remains stable under 75% random token dropping. Second, Path-Drop Guided (PDG) training replaces the expensive unconditional CFG forward pass with a 'weak path' that skips the middle transformer blocks, cutting guidance overhead without a separate unconditional model. Third, a Mean-Velocity Additivity (MVA) regularizer enforces the exact semigroup property of the flow over long time jumps (s < ℓ < t), correcting integration errors that accumulate at 4-step inference and yielding a 38.5-point FVD improvement over PDG-SFM alone.

## Results

On Kinetics-700 (400K iterations), EFLOW's full pipeline (GLGA + PDG-SFM + MVA + 75% token dropping) achieves 146.7 FVD at only 4 NFEs, compared to 188.9 FVD for the softmax-attention flow-matching baseline at 40 NFEs. In terms of efficiency, EFLOW (1.5B) achieves 3.09 s diffusion inference latency at 480p, which is 45.3× faster than Wan 2.1-1.3B and 23.3× faster than Sana (2.0B). Training throughput reaches 0.105 it/s, a 2.5× speedup over standard solution-flow and 1.76× over flow matching on 480p video.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Sana-Video [7], GLGA achieves 189.7 FVD vs. Sana's 217.7 at matched compute (no token dropping), and Sana catastrophically degrades to 456.1 FVD under 75% token dropping while GLGA remains stable at 189.7. Sparse Linear Attention (SLA) [10] diverges to NaN across all model scales even at a reduced 80% sparsity, whereas GLGA remains stable and achieves lower FVD at every compute budget. For inference latency at 480p, EFLOW (3.09 s) is 1.4–1.8× faster than few-step distillation methods VSA [11] and rCM [23], 23.3× faster than Sana (2.0B) [6], and 45.3× faster than Wan 2.1-1.3B [1] (up to 234.6× vs. Wan 2.1-14B). In training throughput, EFLOW achieves 2.5× higher iterations/sec than standard solution-flow and 1.76× over flow matching, while S2DiT [9] and DMD [27] are positioned in Table 1 as requiring either auxiliary models or teacher distillation that EFLOW avoids entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

EFLOW sits at the intersection of two research threads that prior work has largely pursued in isolation: efficient VideoDiT architectures (linear attention in Sana/Sana-Video, sparse attention in SLA/VSA) and teacher-free few-step generation (SoFlow, MeanFlow). It builds directly on SoFlow's bi-time solution-flow objective for few-step training from scratch, adopts SPRINT's encoder–middle–decoder token-dropping paradigm, and uses the Wan transformer as its structural backbone. The key conceptual departure is replacing the convolutional local-mixing used by Sana-Video and S2DiT with a sliding-window attention branch (GLGA), which remains well-defined under aggressive random token removal, and substituting the expensive unconditional CFG pass with a cheap 'weak-path' forward that skips middle transformer blocks (PDG).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making guided solution-flow training practical at video scale without any pre-trained teacher or auxiliary discriminator, EFLOW lowers the barrier to training competitive few-step video generators from scratch, which is significant for groups without access to large pre-trained checkpoints. The 45.3× inference latency reduction and 2.5× training throughput gain are practically impactful for deployment and research iteration. Open questions include whether the 4-step quality gap versus 50-step baselines narrows with further scaling, whether the proprietary large-scale text-to-video dataset limits reproducibility, and whether the PDG weak-path approximation degrades controllability at higher guidance weights or more complex conditioning.
