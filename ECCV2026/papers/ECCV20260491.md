## Problem

Video polyp segmentation is bottlenecked by the prohibitive cost of dense per-frame pixel annotations, and existing weakly-supervised methods that propagate labels via optical flow are fragile in endoscopic environments due to specular reflections, fluid interference, and non-rigid peristalsis. A critical yet overlooked failure mode is dynamic occlusion: when a polyp disappears behind a mucosal fold, conventional cycle-consistency frameworks blindly enforce temporal alignment, hallucinating false-positive masks on healthy tissue and irreversibly corrupting the model's feature memory so the target cannot be re-identified upon reappearance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes colonoscopy video sequences resized to 352×352, with only the first frame of each 5-frame clip carrying a dense binary mask; the remaining four frames are unlabeled. At inference the heavy SAM teacher is entirely discarded and only the 12.7M-parameter PVTv2-B0 + FPN student runs frame-by-frame at 46.7 FPS on a single RTX 4090. Optionally, one point prompt per frame can be supplied at test time to further boost accuracy (the 'prompt-assisted' variant), while the 'prompt-free' variant relies solely on the student's learned spatiotemporal priors.

## Method

FSD-Net distills a frozen SAM-ViT-B teacher into a lightweight PVTv2-B0 + FPN student through three synergistic modules. (1) Semantic Flow Distillation (SFD) replaces optical flow with SAM's forward tracking to generate pseudo-labels, applying a pixel-level uncertainty weight Q_t = s_t^iou · |2p_t − 1| to suppress noisy teacher predictions before computing a weighted BCE loss. (2) Occlusion-Aware Cycle Consistency (OACC) runs the student's current prediction backward through SAM to form a cycle-consistency MSE, but gates it with a visibility score v_t derived from the SAM foreground region; when v_t falls below a threshold τ = 0.80 the adaptive weight w_t = max(0, v_t − τ) zeroes out the gradient, preventing hallucination during occlusion. (3) Prototype Re-Identification (PRM) maintains a momentum-updated (α = 0.90) feature prototype of the polyp and computes per-pixel cosine similarity against it; when the global maximum similarity exceeds 0.72, the module signals target reappearance and reactivates the suspended tracking flow. The total loss is L_total = 1.0·L_distill + 0.5·L_cycle + 0.2·L_smooth, trained end-to-end for 50 epochs with AdamW (lr = 1e-4, cosine annealing).

## Results

On the challenging SUN-SEG-Hard test set, FSD-Net with one point prompt per frame achieves mDice 87.04%, mIoU 79.20%, and mHD 20.64 mm, surpassing the best fully supervised baseline (META-UNet: 80.16% mDice) by over 6.8 points and the best semi-supervised foundation method (VP-SAM: 84.11% mDice) by 2.93 points. On CVC-612, the method reaches 93.54% mDice and 17.86 mm mHD, and on CVC-300 (static generalization) it attains 89.93% mDice and 12.38 mm mHD, both leading all compared methods. Even in the prompt-free setting (no test-time SAM or point prompt), the student alone achieves 85.28% mDice on SUN-SEG-Hard, still exceeding all fully supervised baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSD-Net (1 pt/frame) outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset split and metric. Against the strongest fully supervised method (META-UNet, 80.16% mDice on SUN-SEG-Hard), FSD-Net gains +6.88% mDice and −5.63 mm mHD while using only first-frame annotations instead of dense per-frame masks. Against the best semi-supervised foundation method VP-SAM (84.11% mDice, 22.52 mm mHD on SUN-SEG-Hard), FSD-Net improves by +2.93% mDice, +4.16% mIoU, and −1.88 mm mHD. On CVC-612, FSD-Net leads VP-SAM by +2.42% mDice (93.54 vs 91.12) and −2.38 mm mHD; on CVC-300 it leads by +2.76% mDice (89.93 vs 87.17). Even the prompt-free variant (no test-time SAM or point prompt) surpasses all fully supervised baselines on SUN-SEG-Hard (85.28 vs 80.52 mDice), demonstrating that the distilled student retains strong standalone capability. The gap over raw SAM (55.53% mDice) and MedSAM (68.23% mDice) highlights the critical role of the spatiotemporal distillation and occlusion-aware mechanisms over naive prompt-based inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends weakly-supervised video polyp segmentation by replacing the conventional optical-flow-based label propagation with semantic tracking from a frozen SAM teacher, positioning itself against both prior VPS methods (PNS+, DCNet) that treat frames independently and prior SAM adaptations (MedSAM, SAMed, VP-SAM) that focus on static or training-free inference. The key conceptual departure is the shift from low-level pixel motion cues to high-level semantic flow for supervision, combined with an occlusion-aware gating mechanism on cycle consistency that prior video segmentation frameworks (e.g., Wu & Wang's cross-video cycle consistency) lack. The prototype re-identification module draws on memory-augmented tracking ideas but applies them specifically to the polyp reappearance problem in endoscopy, where prior cycle-consistency methods blindly enforce temporal alignment and hallucinate false positives during occlusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine clinical deployment barrier: dense per-frame pixel annotations are prohibitively expensive, and the resulting 46.7 FPS student-only inference makes real-time endoscopic assistance feasible. The finding that naive cycle consistency actively degrades performance in endoscopy (0.86% mDice drop) while occlusion-aware gating recovers 5.89% is a useful diagnostic for the field, suggesting that temporal consistency losses need visibility-aware modulation in occlusion-prone domains. Open questions remain around highly camouflaged flat polyps where SAM's visibility estimation may fail, very long occlusion durations that erode prototype memory, and whether the approach generalizes beyond colonoscopy to other endoluminal or laparoscopic video tasks. The reliance on a single frozen SAM-ViT-B teacher also raises questions about robustness to domain shift in unseen clinical settings.
