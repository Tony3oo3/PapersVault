## Problem

Multimodal large language models produce lengthy chains of thought that are unnecessary for simple visual reasoning queries, creating substantial inference overhead. Existing draft-to-target routing methods designed for LLMs fail in multimodal settings: token-probability signals are coarse and uncorrelated with accuracy, special-token SFT is data-sensitive and restricts the search space, and both paradigms defer routing until after full generation while ignoring whether the target model can actually solve the routed instance.

## Input / Output

The system takes an image-question pair (I, Q) paired with a predefined formatting prompt P as input. Before any reasoning begins, the draft model generates only a short prefix containing a numeric draft confidence score d (and a target-model score t under JRL), from which a routing decision is extracted. The routed model then produces the full chain-of-thought response and final answer, meaning the expensive reasoning is only invoked by the model best suited to the specific query.

## Method

The paper proposes a Proactive Routing Paradigm (PRP) built on two GRPO-based training objectives: Draft Rating Learning (DRL), which trains the draft model to emit a self-assessed solvability score before reasoning using a reward that penalizes deviation from group-estimated accuracy, and Joint Rating Learning (JRL), which additionally predicts the target model's per-instance accuracy using pre-sampled target outputs as supervision. To prevent the draft scores from collapsing during RL training, a Dynamic Score Substitution mechanism replaces near-identical scores with sampled values closer to ground truth, while Task-specific Optimization applies separate advantages to rating tokens versus reasoning tokens to preserve base performance. At inference, a three-stage pipeline (pre-generate short rating prefix → compute a composite score s combining d and t with a tunable gap penalty τ → threshold-based routing) decides whether the draft or target model handles the query. An Internal Probability Ranking refinement uses the softmax logits over digit tokens at the rating position to break ties within the same score band, yielding finer-grained prioritization.

## Results

On MathVista, JRL-trained M_{d_joint} (7B draft) at 60% draft routing achieves 74.2% accuracy with 26.9s average latency, yielding a 2.41× speedup over the 38B target model (Skywork-R1V-38B, 74.1%, 64.8s) while slightly surpassing its accuracy. On MathVerse, JRL at 50% draft routing reaches 58.5% accuracy with 1.83× speedup (55.4s vs. 101.4s for the target). DRL achieves lossless performance on MathVista (74.3% at 50% draft, 1.49× speedup) but degrades more on MathVerse (57.2% at 40% draft). In terms of routing quality (AUC), JRL leads on ChartQA (83.61%) and MathVista (73.36%), while DRL and JRL both outperform both prior paradigms on Spearman correlation across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Token-probability-based routing (Paradigm 1, Mahaut et al.), JRL achieves substantially higher accuracy at the same 60% draft ratio on MathVista (74.2% vs. 71.6%, +2.6 pp) and at 50% on MathVerse (58.5% vs. 56.4%, +2.1 pp), while also delivering a better speedup (2.41× vs. 2.36× on MathVista). Against Special-token-based routing (Paradigm 2, Chuang et al.), JRL again wins on both benchmarks: 74.2% vs. 72.2% on MathVista and 58.5% vs. 56.9% on MathVerse, with a notably larger speedup advantage on MathVista (2.41× vs. 2.19×). Compared to random routing, JRL gains +2.6 pp on MathVista and +2.1 pp on MathVerse, confirming that the learned ratings carry meaningful signal. Most strikingly, JRL at 60% draft routing on MathVista (74.2%) marginally exceeds the full 38B target model (74.1%) while running 2.41× faster, demonstrating that capability-aware routing can surpass the stronger model by selectively allocating the most suitable hard samples to it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO training framework from DeepSeekMath [39] by adding draft-rating and joint-rating reward signals, repurposing group-relative advantages to teach a model to predict its own and a target model's solvability before generating any reasoning. It departs from two prior LLM routing paradigms—token-probability averaging [30] and SFT-based confidence tokens (Self-REF [10])—by (i) moving the routing decision from post-hoc (after full generation) to proactive (before the think phase), and (ii) replacing supervised fine-tuning with RL to avoid the data-distribution sensitivity and search-space collapse that SFT introduces. The joint rating component, which explicitly models the target model's per-instance capability, has no direct analogue in prior routing work, which typically assesses only the draft model's confidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone serving MLLM reasoning systems: routing 60% of queries to a 7B draft while matching or slightly exceeding a 38B target's accuracy (2.41× speedup on MathVista) is a concrete efficiency win that could reduce inference costs substantially in production. The finding that token-probability routing loses its monotonic accuracy correlation in multimodal long-CoT settings is a useful negative result that cautions against porting LLM routing heuristics to MLLMs. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond math/chart QA to open-ended visual reasoning, whether the 7B/38B capacity gap is essential or whether smaller gaps also benefit, and how the method scales when the target model is itself uncertain on many instances (the DRL-below-random case on MathVista hints at this fragility).
