## Problem

Prior attention-based distillation for MLLMs supervises Prompt-to-Vision attention with a uniform objective across all tokens, but the paper shows that (1) Response-to-Vision attention alignment strongly correlates with downstream performance while Prompt-to-Vision alignment does not, and (2) cross-modal attention patterns vary dramatically across individual response tokens (diffuse for function words, sharp for visual entities), making a single uniform distillation target suboptimal.

## Input / Output

The student MLLM takes an RGB image at 384px resolution (encoded by SigLIP-SO400M/14 into N visual tokens) and a text prompt, producing an autoregressive text response. During training, the distillation signal is extracted from intermediate decoder layers (relative depth 0.3–0.6): for each response token position, the model's attention distribution over the N visual tokens is computed, layer-averaged, and renormalized to form a proper probability vector that serves as the per-token supervision target.

## Method

TRAG reformulates the cross-modal distillation loss in two ways. First, it shifts the attention supervision from prompt tokens to response tokens, so the student's Response-to-Vision attention is anchored to the teacher's. Second, it decomposes the objective into per-token terms and adaptively blends forward KL (mean-seeking, promoting coverage) and reverse KL (mode-seeking, promoting concentration) using a weight λ_i derived from the teacher's attention entropy at that token: high-entropy (diffuse) distributions receive more forward KL, while low-entropy (sharp) distributions receive more reverse KL. Layer-level redundancy is exploited by averaging attention across intermediate layers rather than selecting specific layers. The full training objective combines this token-level cross-modal loss with standard language modeling and uni-modal distillation losses in a three-stage DPT-SFT-DFT schedule.

## Results

On the ≤0.5B regime, TRAG with Qwen2.5-0.5B achieves Avg₆ = 65.3 and Avg₈ = 60.2 on general VQA, surpassing the PT-SFT baseline (62.4/57.1) by +2.9/+3.1 points and LLaVA-KD (64.1/58.7) by +1.2/+1.5 points. On compositional reasoning, TRAG with Qwen1.5-1.8B reaches an average of 79.8, exceeding CompoDistill (76.8) by +3.0 points and LLaVA-KD (74.3) by +5.5 points. In attention fidelity evaluation, TRAG achieves a Response-to-Vision cosine similarity of ~0.72 at intermediate layers, substantially outperforming SFT (0.18), LLaVA-KD (0.32), and CompoDistill (0.41).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRAG consistently outperforms all four baselines across both VQA and compositional reasoning benchmarks. Against LLaVA-KD (Cai et al., ICCV 2025), TRAG gains +1.2 Avg₆ and +1.5 Avg₈ on Qwen2.5-0.5B, and +0.8/+0.8 on Qwen1.5-1.8B, with the largest CR advantage of +5.5 points (79.8 vs 74.3) on Qwen1.5-1.8B. Against CompoDistill (Kim et al., ICLR 2026), which uses uniform prompt-conditioned attention supervision, TRAG improves Avg₈ from 61.9 to 62.9 on Qwen1.5-1.8B and from 58.3 to 58.5 on Qwen1.5-0.5B, while achieving a much larger CR gain of +3.0 points (79.8 vs 76.8). Align-KD (Feng et al., CVPR 2025) and LLaVADI (Yoon et al., 2024) are reported only on different backbones (MobileLLaMA) and are not directly comparable under the same LLM, though TRAG on MobileLLaMA-1.4B improves over the PT-SFT baseline by +3.0 VQA Avg and +7.3 CR Avg. The ablation in Table 3 confirms that response-token supervision (VQA 60.2, CR 75.2) dominates prompt-token (58.6/69.2) and combined (58.4/71.2) supervision, validating the core design choice over prior prompt-focused methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the attention-based distillation line initiated by CompoDistill (Kim et al., ICLR 2026) and Align-KD (Feng et al., CVPR 2025), which supervise Prompt-to-Vision attention with uniform, layer-level objectives, and on LLaVA-KD (Cai et al., ICCV 2025) for the three-stage DPT-SFT-DFT training pipeline and uni-modal distillation components. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) an empirical re-examination showing that Response-to-Vision attention, not Prompt-to-Vision attention, is the signal that tracks downstream performance, and (2) replacing the uniform cosine-distance objective with a token-level, entropy-adaptive weighted combination of forward and reverse KL divergences. The paper thus reframes the cross-modal distillation problem from 'which layers to match' to 'which tokens to match and with what distributional geometry.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that prompt-conditioned attention alignment is a poor proxy for model capability while response-conditioned attention is strongly predictive challenges a default design assumption in prior MLLM distillation work and could redirect future attention-distillation research toward the generative phase. The entropy-adaptive KL weighting is a lightweight, architecture-agnostic mechanism that practitioners can drop into existing distillation pipelines. However, the evidence for the prompt-vs-response finding rests on correlation analysis over a single evaluation dataset (VIGC-InstData) and a limited set of student models, and the absolute performance gains over strong baselines (LLaVA-KD, CompoDistill) are modest (1–3 points), leaving open whether the effect persists at larger scales, with different vision encoders, or in multi-image/video settings.
