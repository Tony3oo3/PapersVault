## Problem

Continuous diffusion models operate in unbounded Euclidean spaces and rarely exploit the low-dimensional data manifold geometry that underpins their theoretical guarantees, while discrete token-based generation imposes sequential decoding orders that conflict with the parallel refinement paradigm of vision diffusion. The paper addresses this by asking how to explicitly constrain a continuous diffusion process to a compact geometric manifold without suffering the representation collapse that occurs when continuous latents are projected onto low-dimensional hyperspheres.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261770/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label as conditioning input and generates 256×256 RGB images. During training, images are first tokenized into sequences of 256 discrete tokens (vocabulary size V) using a pre-trained IBQ or GigaTok tokenizer; these tokens are projected onto an L-dimensional hypersphere surface where the diffusion process operates. At inference, the model iteratively denoises from Gaussian noise over 250 steps, applying classifier-free guidance with a configurable scale, and the final discrete token sequence is decoded back to pixels by the tokenizer's decoder.

## Method

MIND maps discrete image tokens onto a continuous low-dimensional hypersphere surface and performs forward/reverse diffusion constrained to this manifold, leveraging the fact that discrete tokens encode information categorically (via angular separation) and are thus immune to the magnitude-destruction that causes continuous latents to collapse under strict ℓ₂-normalization. For differentiable end-to-end training, a soft top-k aggregation mechanism computes softmax weights over the top-k predicted logits, takes a convex combination of the corresponding hypersphere embeddings, and re-normalizes to the surface, bridging the discrete logit space and the continuous diffusion manifold. A dual-branch input module combats spectral bias on low-dimensional inputs: one branch applies Random Fourier Features (sin/cos of a learnable projection) through an MLP, while a second branch uses deterministic sinusoidal encoding through an MLP whose final layer is zero-initialized for stable gradients. The DiT backbone is conditioned via adaptive LayerNorm on timestep and class label, and inference uses a multi-stage transition sampling scheme that shifts from soft top-k aggregation (early timesteps) to entropy-adaptive Top-k/Nucleus hybrid filtering (middle) to greedy decoding (late), progressively committing to high-confidence tokens.

## Results

Under a constrained 80-epoch budget without guidance, MIND-B (~130M params) achieves an FID of 22.73 on ImageNet 256×256, nearly halving the 43.47 FID of the vanilla DiT-B/2 baseline. With classifier-free guidance (cfg=2.0), MIND-B reaches FID 7.97, outperforming DiT-B/2 (10.95) and SiT-B/2 (9.07). At full training scale, MIND-B-G (~130M params) achieves FID 2.06, surpassing LlamaGen-3B (3.1B params, FID 2.18), while MIND-XL-G (~715M params) further reduces FID to 1.95, beating SiT-XL/2 (675M, FID 2.06) and DiT-XL/2 (675M, FID 2.27).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIND decisively outperforms its direct DiT backbone: at 80 epochs without guidance, MIND-B (FID 22.73) nearly halves DiT-B/2 (43.47), and at full training MIND-XL-G (1.95) beats DiT-XL/2 (2.27) by 0.32 FID. Against the flow-based SiT, MIND-B achieves FID 7.97 vs 9.07 at cfg=2.0 (80 epochs), and MIND-XL-G (1.95) edges out SiT-XL/2 (2.06) at full scale. In the discrete-model category, MIND-B-G (~130M) surpasses LlamaGen-3B (3.1B, FID 2.18), VAR-d24 (1.0B, FID 2.09), and eMIGM-B (208M, FID 2.32), though it is marginally behind IBQ-XXL (2.1B, FID 2.05) by 0.01. MIND-XL-G (1.95) beats all discrete baselines including IBQ-XXL and VAR-d24. Against continuous diffusion baselines, MIND-XL-G outperforms SimDiff (2B, FID 2.77) and DiT-XL/2 (675M, FID 2.27), but trails methods that leverage large-scale pretrained representation encoders (REPA-E: 1.12, RAE: 1.13), which the authors note use a fundamentally different inductive bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIND builds directly on the DiT transformer backbone but departs fundamentally from standard continuous diffusion by constraining the forward/reverse process to a low-dimensional hypersphere manifold populated by discrete VQ token embeddings. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate paradigms: discrete tokenization (IBQ, GigaTok) used in autoregressive and masked image generation, and continuous score-based diffusion. The paper explicitly distinguishes itself from Riemannian diffusion approaches (RDLM, RJF) that operate in high-dimensional continuous feature spaces, arguing instead that discrete tokens have an innate immunity to magnitude-destructive bottleneck collapse when projected onto compact manifolds. The soft top-k aggregation mechanism and multi-stage transition sampling are novel contributions that enable end-to-end differentiable training and structured inference across this hybrid discrete-continuous space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling data manifold geometry via discrete tokens on a hypersphere yields substantial FID improvements over vanilla DiT (nearly halving at 80 epochs) and achieves competitive absolute performance with far fewer parameters (130M vs. 3.1B for LlamaGen-3B), suggesting that geometric inductive bias can substitute for raw model scale. Open questions include whether the approach benefits from larger vocabulary sizes, whether it composes with recent trajectory formulations (mean flow, consistency models) for few-step generation, and whether the information loss inherent in vector quantization can be mitigated with residual terms. A key limitation is reliance on pre-trained tokenizer quality, and the absolute SOTA on ImageNet 256×256 is still held by representation-alignment methods (REPA-E, RAE) that use different inductive biases.
