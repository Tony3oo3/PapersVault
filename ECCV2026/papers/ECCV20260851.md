## Problem

Image-conditioned video diffusion models (I2V/TI2V) suffer from dynamic-degree degradation after supervised fine-tuning: motion strength and long-term temporal coherence collapse even as visual sharpness improves. Existing RL-based post-training methods for diffusion models are computationally prohibitive (requiring full reverse-trajectory storage), tightly coupled to specific samplers, and vulnerable to reward hacking over long video horizons, while motion is a low-level, high-dimensional signal that resists supervision via semantic labels or human preference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image (and optionally a text prompt for the TI2V setting) and produces a temporally coherent video clip. The reward models consume pixel-level intermediate features rather than raw frames: the instantaneous reward uses optical-flow transport residuals computed via Sobel operators and SEA-RAFT velocity fields, while the long-term reward uses point trajectories, visibility masks, and confidence scores from CoTracker3. Fine-tuning operates entirely in the forward diffusion process, making the framework agnostic to the choice of inference sampler (ODE, SDE, or otherwise).

## Method

SHIFT introduces two pixel-motion reward models—Instantaneous Motion Reward (IMR) based on the optical-flow transport residual δ ≈ ∂I/∂t + u·∇I, and Long-term Motion Reward (LMR) based on tracked point trajectory dynamics—each implemented as a ViT binary discriminator trained to separate real from generated video features. The fine-tuning objective is derived from a Forward KL formulation that anchors the model to the stationary data distribution rather than a drifting reference policy, yielding a hybrid loss that combines advantage-weighted regression on self-generated rollouts (online exploration) with standard SFT on real data (offline anchor). Group-relative advantages (à la GRPO) with temperature scaling and clipping provide stable reward signals, while adversarial co-training of the reward model on the latest generated samples prevents distributional drift and reward hacking. A noise-level alignment mechanism shares the same sampled noise level between the SFT and AWR terms to prevent the AWR objective from exploiting high-noise scales where appearance is unprotected. The entire procedure requires no reverse-trajectory storage, no specific diffusion sampler, and no human annotations.

## Results

On VBench-I2V with the SVD model, SHIFT achieves the best VBench Motion score (86.70) and Overall score (84.69), while maintaining appearance quality on par with the base model (83.69 vs. 83.68). The Motion Score reaches 4.40, comparable to FlowGRPO (4.44), at only 2.46× the SFT training cost versus FlowGRPO's 19.35×. On the 5B Wan2.2-TI2V model, SHIFT improves Overall from 86.27 to 86.40 and preserves Motion Score (2.93 vs. 2.96 base), whereas SFT collapses Motion Score to 1.94.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Track4Gen, SHIFT dramatically outperforms on motion metrics (VBench Motion 86.70 vs. 76.44; Motion Score 4.40 vs. 2.84) and Overall (84.69 vs. 82.15), while Track4Gen's lower FVD (316.91 vs. 396.45) is attributed to FVD's known insensitivity to temporal quality. Against DenseDPO, SHIFT wins on Motion (86.70 vs. 86.03), Overall (84.69 vs. 84.50), and FVD (396.45 vs. 399.73), with only a negligible appearance difference (83.69 vs. 83.73). Against FlowGRPO, SHIFT matches or exceeds on Motion (86.70 vs. 86.67), Overall (84.69 vs. 84.63), and Appearance (83.69 vs. 83.61), while FlowGRPO edges ahead on Motion Score (4.44 vs. 4.40); however, SHIFT is roughly 7.9× cheaper in training time (2.46× vs. 19.35× SFT). Overall, SHIFT is the only method that simultaneously improves motion fidelity, overall quality, and motion magnitude without degrading appearance, at a fraction of FlowGRPO's computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHIFT positions itself at the intersection of reward-weighted regression for diffusion models (Lee et al. 2023, Nair et al. 2021) and group-relative advantage computation (GRPO), but departs from both by deriving its objective from a Forward KL formulation anchored to the data distribution rather than a Reverse KL constraint on a reference policy. It also breaks from MDP-based RL for diffusion (DDPO, FlowGRPO) by operating entirely in the forward diffusion process, making it sampler-agnostic and avoiding the memory cost of storing denoising trajectories. For motion supervision specifically, SHIFT contrasts with semantic-level approaches (Track4Gen's point-tracking losses, DenseDPO's human preference labels) by using low-level pixel-motion rewards derived from optical-flow residuals and point trajectories, and it introduces adversarial reward-model co-training as a mitigation for reward hacking that prior video RL methods address only partially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-documented and practically important failure mode—dynamic-degree collapse in fine-tuned I2V/TI2V models—offering a solution that is ~8× cheaper than the strongest RL baseline (FlowGRPO) while matching or slightly exceeding its motion metrics. The adversarial reward-model co-training and noise-level alignment mechanisms provide a reusable recipe for stabilizing reward-driven fine-tuning in high-dimensional generative settings, potentially applicable beyond video. Open questions include whether the pixel-motion rewards generalize to longer video horizons or more complex physical interactions, whether the approach transfers to text-to-video without image conditioning, and whether the modest quality gains over FlowGRPO would widen with larger models or more diverse training data.
