## Problem

Modern 3D generative models produce meshes or implicit fields that lack the parametric surfaces and exact topology required by CAD workflows, while existing B-Rep reconstruction pipelines treat face segmentation as deterministic prediction on discrete point samples. This deterministic framing is brittle because CAD-style face decompositions are inherently ambiguous—multiple valid segmentations correspond to the same geometry—and discretized predictions destabilize downstream topology recovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261204/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a surface point cloud with normals (16,384 points) or a single RGB image as input. It produces a segmented triangle mesh where each triangle carries a face-instance label, which is then converted to a processed B-Rep via Point2CAD surface fitting and intersection/trimming. A key design choice is decoupling conditioning from querying: the model encodes input into compact latent sets and can decode labels at arbitrary query locations, enabling evaluation on arbitrary point sets or dense mesh sampling (100k Poisson-disk points) without retraining.

## Method

BRepFacetGen couples a frozen pretrained TripoSG geometry VAE (producing geometry latent set Z_g) with a learned label VAE that encodes dense one-hot face-instance labels into a probabilistic latent space Z_l conditioned on Z_g, explicitly modeling permutation ambiguity via random label shuffling during training. For generative settings where only Z_g is available, a flow-matching generator p_θ(Z_l|Z_g) is trained to sample compatible label latents; its velocity network exploits the shared FPS anchor structure of both latent sets, using latent-wise channel concatenation and self-attention rather than cross-attention for conditioning. Decoding jointly evaluates the geometry SDF on a 3D grid (marching cubes to mesh) and queries the label decoder at each triangle center to produce a fully segmented mesh. The core insight is formulating CAD structure inference as a latent variable problem on continuous surfaces, yielding multiple plausible dense segmentations that stabilize downstream surface fitting and topology recovery compared to single deterministic predictions on sparse points.

## Results

On the ParSeNet test set (~700 shapes), BRepFacetGen achieves 0.8046 mIoU and 0.7470 F1 for point-cloud instance segmentation, outperforming the strongest baseline SED-Net (0.8010 mIoU) and HP-Net (0.5896 F1). For full point-to-B-Rep reconstruction, the method attains the lowest curve CD (0.085) and surface CD (0.057), the highest topology scores (FE 0.315, EV 0.304), and the best detection F1 across vertices (0.558), curves (0.579), and surfaces (0.770), substantially exceeding the strongest segmentation-to-fitting baseline HP-Net+Point2CAD (F1: 0.232/0.240/0.328). In the image-to-B-Rep setting, the method also outperforms CAD-Dreamer on both geometric fidelity and segmentation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRepFacetGen outperforms all four segmentation-driven baselines (ParSeNet, HP-Net, SED-Net, PrimitiveNet) across every metric in both the segmentation and B-Rep reconstruction evaluations; the largest gap is in detection F1, where the method reaches 0.770 surface F1 versus 0.328 for the strongest segmentation baseline (HP-Net+Point2CAD). Against the direct B-Rep methods, ComplexGen achieves a slightly lower vertex CD (0.205 vs. 0.233) but is substantially worse on curve CD (0.173 vs. 0.085), surface CD (0.106 vs. 0.057), topology (FE 0.186 vs. 0.315), and detection F1 across all primitive types. Split-and-Fit attains a marginally lower surface CD (0.051 vs. 0.057) but trails on vertex CD (0.416 vs. 0.233), curve CD (0.154 vs. 0.085), topology (FE 0.167 vs. 0.315), and detection F1 for vertices and curves. In the image-to-B-Rep setting, CAD-Dreamer is outperformed on both geometric fidelity (lower CD, higher IoU) and segmentation quality (higher mIoU and F1), and even applying HP-Net to TripoSG-reconstructed geometry still underperforms the full BRepFacetGen pipeline, confirming that the gains come from the geometry-conditioned label-latent formulation rather than the geometry backbone alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of 3D foundation models (which produce meshes or implicit fields) and segmentation-driven B-Rep reconstruction (ParSeNet, HP-Net, SED-Net, Point2CAD). It builds directly on TripoSG's frozen geometry VAE and 3DShape2VecSet's latent-set representation for the geometry backbone, and on Point2CAD for the downstream segment-fit-assemble pipeline. The key conceptual departure is from prior segmentation-driven methods that treat face decomposition as a deterministic prediction on discrete point samples: here, segmentation is reframed as a geometry-conditioned latent variable problem (a label VAE plus a flow-matching generator) over continuous surfaces, explicitly modeling permutation ambiguity and structural non-uniqueness rather than resolving them via Hungarian matching or clustering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a practical bridge between the rapidly advancing 3D generative model ecosystem and CAD-native B-Rep representations, enabling multi-modal (point, image) CAD-ready synthesis without requiring construction-sequence data. The generative formulation's ability to sample multiple plausible segmentations (with ARI ~0.93 across 10 draws) opens a path toward uncertainty-aware CAD reconstruction. Limitations include dependence on the frozen TripoSG backbone for geometry quality, multi-step flow-matching inference cost, and reliance on Point2CAD rather than kernel-grade surface fitting; extending to text/sketch conditioning and CAD-aware fine-tuning are natural next steps.
