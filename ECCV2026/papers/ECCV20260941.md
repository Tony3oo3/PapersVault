## Problem

Multimodal LLMs for video understanding process frames as isolated 2D snapshots, lacking the explicit 3D geometric grounding that relates observations across viewpoints. Camera pose—the position and orientation defining how each view embeds in a shared coordinate frame—is absent from these models, causing consistent failures on spatial reasoning tasks. This paper argues that pose is the lightest 3D signal capable of closing this gap by enforcing global consistency through rigid-body constraints and disentangling camera motion from scene dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260941/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of N RGB video frames (typically 32–128 frames, each encoded by SigLIP2-SO400m into K visual tokens) alongside a natural-language question. It produces two outputs simultaneously: a free-form text answer via next-token prediction, and per-frame camera pose parameters (translation t∈ℝ³, rotation quaternion q∈ℝ⁴, and horizontal/vertical field of view f∈ℝ²) regressed from the LLM's final-layer hidden states. The pose is expressed relative to the first frame's coordinate system, with the first frame distinguished by a dedicated learnable token.

## Method

Cambrian-P introduces two learnable camera pose tokens (c_first for frame 1, c_rest for all others) appended after each frame's visual tokens, followed by a linear projector and a VGGT-style pose head (four self-attention layers plus a linear layer) that regresses pose from the LLM's hidden states. Training combines standard next-token prediction with a scale-normalized weighted L1 pose loss, but the key contribution is an interleaved training strategy that mixes VQA samples (uniform frame sampling, no augmentation) with dedicated pose-only samples (dynamic random sampling, heavy augmentation like color jitter and blur, pose loss only) to resolve the conflicting sampling and augmentation requirements of the two tasks. A random jitter perturbation is additionally applied to VQA frame indices to prevent memorization of fixed frame-pose correspondences. Analysis shows the VQA gains arise from stronger pose-supervised representations learned during training rather than from inference-time pose conditioning, and that improvements are disproportionately larger for spatially distant objects, indicating the model develops more global spatial reasoning.

## Results

On VSI-Bench, Cambrian-P achieves 73.7% average accuracy, a 4.5% gain over its no-pose counterpart Cambrian-S† (69.2%) and over 5% gains over other spatial-specialist models (Cambrian-S 7B: 67.5%, SenseNova-SI 8B: 68.7%, GeoThinker 7B: 68.5%). On VSTI-Bench, Cambrian-P reaches 68.9% average with a 20% improvement on the camera movement direction subtask (87.7% vs. 67.7% without pose). For streaming camera pose estimation, Cambrian-P achieves the best ATE on ScanNet (0.078) among all streaming methods, outperforming CUT3R (0.096) and Spann3R (0.096), while remaining competitive on TUM-dynamic (0.046 ATE) and Sintel (0.239 ATE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct no-pose counterpart Cambrian-S† (trained on the same VSI-590K data), Cambrian-P gains 4.5% on VSI-Bench average (73.7 vs. 69.2), with the largest per-subtask improvements on Route Plan (+13.9 pts) and Relative Direction (+7.5 pts), tasks requiring global spatial understanding. Compared to other spatial-specialist models sharing the same Qwen2.5-7B backbone (Cambrian-S 7B at 67.5%, SenseNova-SI 8B at 68.7%, GeoThinker 7B at 68.5%), Cambrian-P leads by 5–6.2%. On VSTI-Bench, Cambrian-P (68.9%) surpasses GeoThinker 8B (67.4%) and VLM-3R 7B (58.8%), with a particularly striking 20-point gain on the camera movement direction subtask (87.7% vs. 67.7% for its own no-pose variant). For streaming pose estimation on ScanNet, Cambrian-P achieves the lowest ATE (0.078) among all streaming methods, beating CUT3R (0.096), Spann3R (0.096), and StreamVGGT (0.127), though it trails offline models like π³ (0.031) and VGGT (0.035). On out-of-distribution general VQA benchmarks (MVBench, EgoSchema, Perception Test), Cambrian-P consistently improves over its no-pose baseline by 1.5–3.0 points, and adding pseudo-annotated pose from in-the-wild videos further boosts EgoSchema from 71.2% to 73.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cambrian-P builds directly on the Cambrian-S MLLM (SigLIP2 + Qwen2.5) and borrows the per-frame pose-token and self-attention pose-head design from VGGT, but departs from prior spatial-intelligence approaches that inject 3D features from off-the-shelf encoders (VLM-3R, VG-LLM) or rely on heavy dual-encoder architectures (G²VLM). Rather than treating camera pose as a 3D-vision-only signal, the paper repositions it as a lightweight supervisory loss within a standard MLLM training loop, reconciling the conflicting frame-sampling and augmentation requirements of VQA and pose estimation through an interleaved training strategy. This contrasts with reinforcement-learning-based spatial tuning (Spatial-SSRL, SPACER) and pseudo-labeling pipelines, offering a simpler, more scalable path to spatial grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single learnable pose token per frame plus a small regression head is sufficient to close a 4.5–6.5% gap on spatial reasoning benchmarks and, more broadly, to improve general video QA (MVBench, EgoSchema) when scaled with pseudo-annotated poses from in-the-wild video—suggesting pose is a fundamental inductive bias for video understanding, not merely a spatial-reasoning aid. The finding that pose supervision yields larger gains for distant objects than nearby ones provides mechanistic evidence that the model learns more global, cross-frame spatial reasoning. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to embodied/robotic settings, whether it scales to very long videos beyond 128 frames, and whether the pseudo-annotation pipeline (VIPE-based) can be replaced by self-supervised pose learning to remove the dependency on a separate pose estimator.
