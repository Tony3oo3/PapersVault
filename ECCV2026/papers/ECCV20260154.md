## Problem

Most existing amodal segmentation methods are trained and evaluated on in-domain benchmarks, exhibiting limited zero-shot generalization to unseen scenes and categories. While recent foundation-model-based approaches (e.g., SAMBA) improve zero-shot performance, they remain architecturally bound to a single backbone and do not fully exploit the complementary priors available from multiple vision foundation models. The paper asks whether a lightweight, trainable segmentation network can achieve state-of-the-art amodal completion by treating frozen foundation models purely as plug-and-play prior extractors.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single RGB image and the visible (modal) bounding box of the target object as the sole input; no category labels or additional prompts are required. Internally, the image is adaptively cropped around the target using depth-polarity-guided expansion of the bounding box, then resized to 256×256 before encoding. The output is a category-agnostic binary amodal mask at the original image resolution, delineating the complete object silhouette including occluded regions.

## Method

HIDA combines two frozen foundation models—SAM (for visible mask extraction) and Depth Anything V2 (for pseudo-depth estimation)—with a lightweight trainable ViT segmentation network (77.86M parameters), requiring no fine-tuning of the foundation models. A key design is adaptive image cropping: an occlusion score computed from pseudo-depth polarity relative to the visible mask boundary determines per-side expansion ratios, concentrating the receptive field on occlusion boundaries and hidden regions while suppressing irrelevant background. Within the ViT encoder, an occlusion-aware token-routing soft MoE module constructs a routing signal from depth gradients, mask boundaries, and downsampled mask features, then applies per-token softmax gating over K lightweight convolutional experts to adaptively handle varied occlusion patterns. A load-balancing regularizer prevents expert collapse, and the overall loss combines BCE + Dice segmentation loss with the load-balancing term. Training is performed on the synthetic pix2gestalt dataset (849K images) for 15 epochs on a single RTX 4090, with all foundation-model priors precomputed and cached offline.

## Results

Under fully supervised training, HIDA achieves mean IoU of 85.56% on COCOA-cls, 93.33% on D2SA, and 91.50% on KINS, setting new state-of-the-art on all three benchmarks. In the zero-shot setting (trained only on pix2gestalt with random initialization), HIDA† reaches 83.16% on COCOA-cls and 91.11% on D2SA, outperforming the strongest zero-shot baseline SAMBA† (81.82% and 90.98% respectively), though it trails SAMBA† on KINS (86.71% vs. 88.47%). The fully supervised gains are most pronounced on scene-biased datasets (D2SA: +4.23 over C2F-Seg; KINS: +9.28 over C2F-Seg), highlighting practical value for scenario-targeted applications.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest zero-shot competitor SAMBA†, HIDA† wins on COCOA-cls by +1.34 IoU (83.16 vs. 81.82) and on D2SA by +0.13 IoU (91.11 vs. 90.98), but loses on KINS by −1.76 IoU (86.71 vs. 88.47). In the fully supervised setting, HIDA outperforms the best supervised baseline C2F-Seg by +5.28 on COCOA-cls, +4.23 on D2SA, and +9.28 on KINS, and surpasses SDAmodal by +5.55 on COCOA-cls. Among zero-shot methods, HIDA† also beats Pix2Gestalt† by +4.08 on COCOA-cls and +9.29 on D2SA, and SDXL-Inpainting† by +9.51 and +10.58 respectively. The gap over the general-purpose SAM baseline is substantial: +10.06 on COCOA-cls and +6.46 on D2SA in the zero-shot setting, confirming that the depth-aware cropping and occlusion-aware MoE contribute meaningfully beyond SAM's visible-mask capability alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

HIDA extends the zero-shot amodal segmentation line initiated by SAMBA [29], which fine-tuned SAM with added structures, by instead treating two vision foundation models (SAM and Depth Anything V2) as strictly frozen, plug-and-play prior extractors and training only a lightweight ViT segmentation network. The adaptive cropping strategy that fuses pseudo-depth polarity with visible-mask geometry to allocate per-side expansion is a departure from prior methods that simply enlarge bounding boxes uniformly. The occlusion-aware token-routing soft MoE module adapts the soft MoE paradigm [34] to the specific challenge of bridging the representation gap between visible and occluded regions, a problem not explicitly addressed in prior amodal segmentation architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design—freezing large foundation models and training only ~78M parameters—offers a practical recipe for zero-shot amodal segmentation that avoids the compute cost of fine-tuning foundation models, making the approach accessible on a single GPU. The identified limitation of depth aliasing under severe contact occlusions (where PDM assigns ambiguous or inverted depth ordering at boundary pixels) highlights an open question about whether explicit boundary-ownership reasoning or more reliable depth cues are needed for robust completion. Inference cost (0.8 s per instance on an RTX 4090) remains a barrier for real-time deployment, motivating future work on lighter depth and segmentation backbones.
