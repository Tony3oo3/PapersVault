## Problem

Existing mixture-model-based occupancy prediction methods (e.g., GaussianFormer, SplatSSC) train with voxel-wise cross-entropy loss, whose gradients vanish when primitives are far from ground-truth occupied voxels. This prevents the network from relocating primitives out of empty regions, causing spurious primitives to persist and wasting both representational capacity and computation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261608/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and camera intrinsics as input and produces a 60×60×36 voxel grid with 11 semantic classes (0.08 m voxel size) along with the refined primitive parameters. A fixed set of superquadrics (or Gaussians) is randomly initialized in 3D space and then relocated and deformed by the network; the occupancy grid is extracted via a novel density-threshold voxelization (threshold 0.5) rather than per-voxel classification. Notably, the FLM training loss operates directly on occupied voxel coordinates without requiring full-grid voxelization, reducing computation since only ~3% of voxels are occupied in Occ-ScanNet.

## Method

FLM-Occ reformulates occupancy prediction as maximum-likelihood estimation over occupied voxels, replacing voxel classification with a feed-forward likelihood-maximization objective (FLM loss) that has two terms: a volume-sum term that compresses total primitive volume and a density-sum term that pulls primitives toward occupied locations. A key reparameterization defines mixture weights as normalized primitive volumes, implicitly enforcing simplex constraints and aligning each weight with spatial extent. The gradient of the FLM loss with respect to primitive positions does not vanish even when a primitive is far from any occupied voxel, enabling global relocation of randomly initialized primitives. Architecturally, FLM-Occ uses a single Depth Anything v2 encoder (fine-tuned ViT) to extract features, which guide four refinement blocks (MLP with deformable aggregation and RoPE self-attention) to update superquadric parameters in an unconstrained manner, followed by probabilistic voxelization for geometry and semantics.

## Results

On Occ-ScanNet, FLM-Occ with 1024 superquadrics achieves 71.7% IoU and 64.0% mIoU, surpassing the prior SoTA SplatSSC (1200 Gaussians, 62.8% IoU / 51.8% mIoU) by +8.9% IoU and +12.2% mIoU while using 3.7× fewer primitives and running 3.7× faster. Remarkably, even with only 32 superquadrics (2.7% of SplatSSC's count), FLM-Occ reaches 65.3% IoU and 55.9% mIoU, still exceeding SplatSSC. On Occ-ScanNet-mini2, the 1024-SQ configuration achieves 72.9% IoU and 65.7% mIoU, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLM-Occ decisively outperforms all six baselines on both datasets. Against the strongest prior method SplatSSC (1200 Gaussians, 62.8% IoU / 51.8% mIoU on Occ-ScanNet), FLM-Occ with 1024 superquadrics gains +8.9% IoU and +12.2% mIoU, while the 32-SQ variant (65.3% / 55.9%) still surpasses SplatSSC despite using only 2.7% of its primitives. Against EmbodiedOcc (16,200 Gaussians, 53.6% / 45.2%) and EmbodiedOcc++ (16,200 Gaussians, 54.9% / 46.2%), FLM-Occ with 32 SQs leads by +11.7% and +10.4% IoU respectively, demonstrating that the MLE-based training objective enables far more efficient primitive utilization. The voxel-based baselines (MonoScene, ISO) and query-based DiScene trail by 20+ points in mIoU, confirming the advantage of mixture-model representations. On Occ-ScanNet-mini2, the same pattern holds: FLM-Occ (1024 SQ) achieves 72.9% / 65.7%, beating SplatSSC by +11.4% IoU and +16.8% mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the line of mixture-model-based occupancy prediction (GaussianFormer, GaussianFormer-2, SplatSSC, EmbodiedOcc, QuadricFormer), which refine a fixed set of geometric primitives to model 3D scenes. The key departure is the training objective: rather than the standard voxel-wise cross-entropy classification used by all prior methods, FLM-Occ reformulates the problem as maximum likelihood estimation over occupied voxels, which the authors show via gradient analysis enables global primitive relocation that classification gradients cannot achieve. The reparameterization of mixture weights as normalized primitive volumes is a novel technical device that resolves the simplex constraint and enables end-to-end training of a standard mixture model, bridging the gap between classical MLE and feed-forward neural network training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 37× reduction in primitives (32 superquadrics vs. 1200 Gaussians) while simultaneously improving mIoU by 4+ points and running 3.7× faster is a substantial efficiency gain that makes mixture-model-based occupancy prediction practical for real-time embodied AI applications. The gradient analysis identifying why voxel classification inherently fails to relocate distant primitives provides a clear mechanistic explanation that could inform training objectives in other mixture-model-based 3D reconstruction tasks. Open questions include whether the fixed-primitive-count limitation can be addressed with learnable pruning/splitting, whether the FLM formulation scales to large-scale outdoor scenes where occupied voxel counts are much higher, and whether the global density threshold can be replaced by learned adaptive boundaries.
