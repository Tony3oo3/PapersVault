## Problem

Prior bounds on the local Lipschitz constant of dot-product self-attention depend only on input and weight norms, completely disregarding the attention map distribution — a core non-linear mechanism that fundamentally shapes the Jacobian's spectral properties. This omission prevents understanding how attention concentration (e.g., attention sinks or uniform distributions) affects model sensitivity, gradient dynamics, and training stability.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images fed into Vision Transformer backbones (ViT-B for training, ViT-L for analysis). CIFAR-10/100 images (32×32) use patch size 4 yielding 7×7=49 tokens, while Imagenette (224×224) uses patch size 16 yielding 14×14=196 tokens. The JaSMin regularizer computes per-head, per-layer attention map statistics g₁(P_{i,:}^h) during the forward pass with O(1) memory overhead, and the model outputs standard class labels. The theoretical analysis targets the Jacobian of individual self-attention blocks, with the full model Jacobian norm computed via power iteration for evaluation.

## Method

The paper derives refined interlacing bounds for all singular values of the softmax Jacobian M(x) = diag(x) − xxᵀ, proving that g_k(x) = x_(k)(1 − x_(k) + x_(k+1)) upper-bounds the k-th singular value, tightening both the classical eigenvalue interlacing theorem and the Gershgorin-based bound from Nair (2026). Leveraging these bounds, Theorem 3 establishes a new upper bound on the self-attention Jacobian spectral norm that explicitly depends on max_i ‖M(P_{i,:}^h)‖₂, capturing how attention distribution concentration modulates local Lipschitzness. The paper further shows that both uniform and categorical attention distributions drive this term toward zero, causing gradient vanishing for Q and K projections. The practical contribution is JaSMin, a lightweight regularizer that penalizes log(g₁(P_{i,:}^h)) (or the ratio g₁/g_k for k>1) summed across all heads and layers, directly controlling the self-attention Jacobian norm with O(BLHN²) FLOPs overhead and O(1) memory, improving adversarial robustness on CIFAR-100 and Imagenette without significant clean-accuracy loss.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ViT-B, JaSMin_{k=10, λ=1e-3} achieves 91.08% standard accuracy (matching the 91.05% baseline) while substantially improving adversarial robustness: 48.90% under FGSM-2 (vs. 46.64% baseline), 30.29% under PGD-2 (vs. 27.47%), 15.47% under PGD-4 (vs. 13.11%), and 2.45% under AutoAttack-2 (vs. 2.10%). On Imagenette, JaSMin_{k=30, λ=1e-2} boosts PGD-4 accuracy from 15.13% to 20.67% and AutoAttack-2 from 6.86% to 10.62%, at a modest 0.93% drop in standard accuracy (97.07% vs. 98.00%). The regularizer also reduces the mean spectral norm of the full model Jacobian, as confirmed by power-method measurements on the test set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole experimental baseline is Specformer [18], which penalizes the largest singular values of the Q, K, V projection matrices. On CIFAR-100, JaSMin_{k=10} outperforms both Specformer configurations across every adversarial metric: it gains +2.26 pp over Specformer(1e-2,1e-2,1e-2) on FGSM-2 (48.90 vs. 44.90), +2.21 pp on PGD-2 (30.29 vs. 28.08), and +2.86 pp on PGD-4 (15.47 vs. 12.61), while also matching or exceeding the baseline's standard accuracy. On Imagenette, JaSMin_{k=30} improves PGD-4 by +4.69 pp over Specformer (20.67 vs. 15.98) and AutoAttack-2 by +3.31 pp (10.62 vs. 7.31), though it trades ~1 pp of standard accuracy. The key advantage of JaSMin over Specformer is that it directly targets the attention-map-dependent term in the Lipschitz bound rather than the weight-matrix singular values, yielding a tighter and more informative regularization signal at O(1) extra memory versus Specformer's O(LD) storage for singular vectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work bounding the local Lipschitz constant of dot-product self-attention, building directly on Kim et al.'s closed-form Jacobian decomposition [22] and Nair's Gershgorin-based 1/2-Lipschitz bound for softmax [33]. The key departure is that all prior bounds (Dasoulas et al. [6], Castin et al. [3], Specformer [18]) depend only on input and weight norms, completely ignoring the attention map distribution; here the authors introduce refined interlacing bounds on all softmax Jacobian singular values (Theorem 2) that make the self-attention Lipschitz constant explicitly a function of attention probability distributions. The resulting JaSMin regularizer targets this distribution-dependent term, contrasting with Specformer's approach of penalizing singular values of Q/K/V weight matrices directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical explanation linking attention sinks, rank collapse, and gradient vanishing to the softmax Jacobian spectrum, showing that both categorical and uniform attention distributions suppress Q/K gradients through a unified mechanism. This offers a practical, O(1)-memory diagnostic and regularizer for controlling per-head Lipschitz constants during training. However, empirical validation is limited to ViT-B on CIFAR-100 and Imagenette with modest robustness gains (e.g., ~5–6 points on PGD-4), and the bound is linear in sequence length N (worse asymptotically than Castin et al.'s O(√N) bound), leaving open whether the approach scales to long-sequence LLMs or large-scale vision models where attention sinks are most problematic.
