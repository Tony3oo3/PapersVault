## Problem

Existing training-free 'cache-then-forecast' acceleration methods for Diffusion Transformers suffer severe quality degradation at high acceleration ratios because they perform discrete polynomial extrapolation on intermediate representations that are misaligned with the continuous diffusion ODE trajectory and numerically unstable. This manifests as spatial error cascading across network depth, exponential noise amplification from finite-difference derivative estimation, and Runge's phenomenon at interval edges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261018/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

ResilPhase is a training-free, plug-and-play inference accelerator applied on top of existing DiT models (FLUX.1-dev, HunyuanVideo, DiT-XL/2, SDXL). It takes the same inputs as the base model—text prompts for text-to-image/video or class labels for class-conditional generation—and produces the same outputs (images at the model's native resolution, or video clips). The only change is the inference schedule: full forward passes run every N steps while intermediate steps are filled by the extrapolator, with no architectural modification or retraining required.

## Method

ResilPhase reformulates accelerated DiT inference as stable macro-trajectory extrapolation in ODE space through three synergistic components. First, it shifts the prediction target from layer-wise micro-features to the Global Drift (GD), the end-to-end state displacement between the model's final output and initial input, which eliminates spatial error cascading (reducing error from O((1+L_f)^L) to O(1)) and removes the need to cache block-level features. Second, it replaces derivative-based solvers (Taylor, Hermite) with a derivative-free Barycentric Lagrange extrapolator of order m, whose precomputed barycentric weights reduce per-step cost to O(m) and completely bypass the chaotic higher-order temporal derivatives that destabilize finite-difference approaches. Third, it introduces a plug-and-play Phase Mapping that non-linearly projects uniform discrete timesteps into a bounded phase domain—via Chebyshev nodes for class-conditional tasks or a data-driven tanh-based Balanced Mapping for text-conditioned tasks—strictly minimizing the node-dependent term in the Lagrange error bound and suppressing Runge's phenomenon at extrapolation edges.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev at an aggressive ~5× speedup (11 steps), ResilPhase achieves an ImageReward of 1.0258 and CLIP Score of 32.847, surpassing the full 50-step baseline (1.0804) by only ~5% while delivering a 52% lower LPIPS (0.3834 vs. 0.8012 for TaylorSeer). On HunyuanVideo at ~5× speedup, ResilPhase attains a VBench score of 79.78 with the lowest LPIPS (0.3341) among all methods. On DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet) at 4.41× speedup, ResilPhase achieves an FID of 2.832, outperforming the 50-step DDIM baseline (2.367) by only 0.47 FID while most caching baselines collapse above FID 15.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResilPhase outperforms all 10 baselines on the primary FLUX.1-dev text-to-image task at aggressive speedups. The most dramatic gap is against TaylorSeer, which suffers a 64% ImageReward drop (0.6241 vs. 1.0258) and 2× higher LPIPS (0.8012 vs. 0.3834) at comparable ~4.7× speedup, confirming the derivative-based instability the paper targets. FreqCa is the closest competitor on ImageReward (1.0130) but trails significantly on LPIPS (0.6877 vs. 0.3834) and PSNR (28.120 vs. 29.536), indicating worse perceptual fidelity. SpeCa and PFDiff remain competitive at moderate speedups (~3.5×) but degrade faster at higher ratios. On HunyuanVideo, ResilPhase edges out TeaCache (VBench 79.78 vs. 79.77) while achieving 10% lower LPIPS (0.3341 vs. 0.3760). On DiT-XL/2 ImageNet at 4.41×, ResilPhase (FID 2.832) dramatically outperforms SpeCa (6.866), ClusCa (15.206), and FORA (16.694), with Δ-DiT collapsing to FID 34.110 at a similar speedup tier.

## Relation to Prior Work

ResilPhase departs from the dominant 'cache-then-forecast' paradigm (TaylorSeer, HiCache, SpeCa, FreqCa) on three axes: it replaces layer-wise micro-feature prediction with a single ODE-aligned Global Drift target, swaps derivative-based solvers (Taylor, Hermite) for a derivative-free Barycentric Lagrange extrapolator, and introduces Phase Mapping (Chebyshev/Balanced) to regularize the extrapolation domain. Conceptually, it repurposes classical numerical analysis tools—barycentric weights, Chebyshev node distributions, and Runge's phenomenon analysis—that had not previously been applied to diffusion acceleration. The paper positions itself as diagnosing a shared root cause (discrete extrapolation on misaligned, numerically unstable representations) underlying the quality collapse of all existing forecasting methods at high acceleration ratios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play Phase Mapping mechanism is particularly impactful: it can be dropped into existing derivative-based accelerators (demonstrated with TaylorSeer and HiCache) to reduce their extrapolation error bounds without architectural changes, making it broadly adoptable. The rigorous error-bound analysis (Lipschitz cascading, node-dependent polynomial bounds) provides a theoretical foundation that prior heuristic caching methods lacked. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-DiT architectures (e.g., U-Net-based diffusion), whether even higher acceleration ratios (>10×) remain stable, and whether the data-driven Balanced Mapping hyperparameter (α) can be learned automatically rather than fixed. The ~5× speedup with competitive fidelity is practically significant for real-time deployment of FLUX-class and video generation models.
