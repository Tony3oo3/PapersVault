## Problem

Optical-SAR fusion object detection is hindered by two critical gaps: the absence of a large-scale, standardized paired dataset suitable for end-to-end multi-source fusion (existing efforts like OGSOD target cross-modal distillation rather than joint detection), and the lack of a unified algorithmic framework that explicitly addresses the severe cross-domain distribution shift between optical and SAR modalities. Without both a benchmark and a tailored method, fair comparison and progress in this area remain stalled.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260023/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes co-registered 512×512 optical (Sentinel-2 RGB, 10 m or 60 m resolution) and SAR (Sentinel-1 SLC, VH or VV polarization) image pairs that are aligned only at the geographic-coordinate level rather than pixel-precisely, reflecting realistic acquisition conditions. It outputs oriented bounding boxes (angle normalized to [0, π/2)) with class labels across six categories—bridges, harbors, oil tanks, playgrounds, airports, and wind turbines—evaluated under the standard COCO AP protocol (AP50, AP75, mAP).

## Method

E2E-OSDet is an end-to-end optical-SAR fusion detector built on a YOLOv11 backbone with a YOLOv8 oriented head, introducing three complementary modules to bridge the optical–SAR domain gap. The Filter Augment Module (FAM) projects the low-dimensional SAR input into a richer feature space using classical operators (HOG, Canny, Haar, Grad, WST), narrowing the distributional mismatch before fusion. The Cross-modal Mamba Interaction Module (CMIM) leverages Mamba's selective state-space model to perform efficient sequence-level cross-modal interaction, alleviating feature-domain bias without the complexity of prior Mamba-based fusion designs. The Area-Attention Fusion Module (AFM) applies spatially localized attention to amplify salient target regions in the fused feature map, improving discrimination in dense or cluttered scenes. All three modules are trained jointly from scratch (SGD, lr 0.01, 300 epochs, batch 64) and are shown via ablation to be individually beneficial and mutually compatible, yielding a 61.4 mAP that surpasses the best prior fusion method (CFT, 59.9) by 1.5 points.

## Results

On the M4-SAR dataset, the proposed E2E-OSDet achieves the best overall performance among all fusion methods with mAP 61.4%, AP50 85.7%, and AP75 70.3%, using only 24.7M parameters and 20.9 ms inference time. The best single-source optical baseline (YOLO-Master) reaches mAP 56.3%, while the best prior fusion method (CFT) achieves mAP 59.9%, meaning E2E-OSDet improves over the strongest single-source input by 5.1% and over the best prior fusion method by 1.5%. All multi-source fusion methods consistently outperform their corresponding single-modal baselines, confirming the complementary value of optical-SAR fusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E2E-OSDet (mAP 61.4%) outperforms all 8 fusion baselines, with the largest margins over CMADet (+5.7%), COMO (+3.5%), CSSA (+3.4%), and MHFNet (+3.4%). Among the strongest prior fusion methods, CFT (59.9%), MMIDet (59.8%), and CLANet (59.6%) form a tight cluster, and E2E-OSDet surpasses each by 1.5–1.8 mAP points. Notably, E2E-OSDet achieves this with substantially fewer parameters (24.7M vs. 48–54M for CFT, CLANet, MMIDet) and faster inference (20.9 ms vs. 29–47 ms), indicating a favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-off. Compared to the best single-source optical detector (YOLO-Master, mAP 56.3%), E2E-OSDet gains 5.1 mAP, and compared to naive optical+SAR concatenation (YOLO-Master O+S, mAP 48.5%), the gain is 12.9 mAP, underscoring that effective cross-modal fusion—not mere channel stacking—is critical. The ablation (Table 3) further shows that each of the three proposed modules (FAM, CMIM, AFM) contributes independently, with AFM providing the largest single-module gain (+2.0 mAP over baseline CSSA).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-source object detection line (previously explored for RGB-infrared and RGB-depth pairs) to the optical-SAR domain, adapting fusion architectures like CSSA, CFT, and CLANet via the MSRODet toolkit. It builds directly on CSSA (Cao et al., CVPR 2023) as the architectural baseline and incorporates Mamba's state-space modeling (Gu & Dao, COLM 2024) into a simplified cross-modal interaction module. The work departs from prior optical-SAR efforts (OGSOD-1.0/2.0), which were designed for cross-modal knowledge distillation with SAR-only test sets, by introducing the first end-to-end fusion detection framework (E2E-OSDet) with a large-scale paired dataset covering multi-resolution, multi-polarization, and multi-scene conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

M4-SAR (112K image pairs, ~1M instances) fills a critical gap as the first large-scale, standardized benchmark for optical-SAR fusion detection, enabling fair comparison across methods that previously lacked a common evaluation protocol. The 5.7% mAP gain from fusion over single-source baselines validates the complementarity hypothesis, but the geographic-only (non-pixel-level) alignment and limited six-category coastal scope constrain applicability to tasks requiring precise spatial correspondence or broader semantic diversity. Open questions include whether the E2E-OSDet modules (particularly the Mamba-based CMIM) generalize to dynamic targets, whether lightweight variants can deploy on edge hardware, and whether temporal modeling can mitigate the non-simultaneous acquisition gap.
