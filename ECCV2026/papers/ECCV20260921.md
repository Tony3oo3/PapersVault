## Problem

End-to-end autonomous driving systems suffer from catastrophic forgetting when sequentially acquiring new driving skills, and they frequently capture spurious correlations induced by unobservable confounders, leading to decision failures under covariate shift. Existing E2E-AD methods improve modeling capacity or interpretability but leave the combination of lifelong learning and causal deconfounding unexplored, and no closed-loop lifelong learning benchmark exists for CARLA-based driving.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB image sequences and LiDAR point clouds, processed independently by RegNetY backbones before cross-modal fusion into an 8×8×256 BEV feature map. The output is a non-autoregressive trajectory of 10 waypoints (2D coordinates) plus a scalar target speed, generated in parallel via a dual-branch head that scores K_t dynamic trajectory anchors and predicts per-anchor geometric offsets, with the final trajectory selected by Top-K routing over temperature-scaled probabilities.

## Method

DeLL builds on a Transfuser++ backbone and introduces two DPMM-governed knowledge spaces—a Feature Knowledge Space clustering fused scene representations and a Trajectory Knowledge Space clustering ground-truth expert trajectories—maintained online via memoVB variational inference so that cluster counts grow without pre-specification. The knowledge anchors serve as discrete mediator variables in a cascaded front-door adjustment module (FFEM for scene features, TFEM for trajectory features), implemented as cross-attention with gated fusion to intercept spurious backdoor paths from unobserved confounders. An evolutionary trajectory decoder maps the dynamically expanding trajectory anchor pool into planning tokens, performing parallel cross-attention against scene context and a dual-branch prediction (coarse anchor selection + fine offset regression) to replace autoregressive waypoint generation. During lifelong training, the backbone is frozen after the first task while the DPMM spaces, causal modules, and decoder continue to update in an alternating schedule with the network.

## Results

On the Bench2Drive full-data benchmark, DeLL achieves the highest driving score (DS) of 86.86% and the best average multi-ability success rate of 68.90%, surpassing the previous best (HiP-AD at 86.77% DS, SimLingo at 67.03% multi-ability). In the lifelong learning setting, DeLL reduces the forgetting ratio from 44.50% to 33.97% and raises backward transfer from 52.83% to 79.63% compared to the TF++ baseline, while maintaining a 68.97% driving score on the final data-scarce task versus 60.89% for the baseline. Inference requires ~1.69 GB GPU memory and ~26.29 ms per forward pass.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the full-data setting (Table 3), DeLL achieves the highest DS (86.86%) by a narrow margin over HiP-AD (86.77%) and SimLingo (85.07%), and leads in multi-ability mean (68.90% vs. 67.03% for SimLingo), though it trails HiP-AD slightly on overall SR (68.63% vs. 69.09%). DeLL shows the strongest GiveWay performance (60.00%) among all methods, indicating better handling of data-scarce scenarios. In the lifelong learning setting (Table 1), DeLL outperforms the TF++ baseline on every sequential task, with the largest gap on the final GiveWay task (DS 68.97% vs. 60.89%; SR 42.73% vs. 30.00%). Against adapted lifelong-learning baselines (Table 2), DeLL matches or beats Baseline+PackNet on all metrics (FR 33.97 vs. 34.11, BT 79.63 vs. 74.50, DS 74.69 vs. 74.46) at equal training cost, and dominates Baseline+ER on every metric while using 35% less cached data. The evolutionary trajectory decoder and both causal enhancement modules (FFEM, TFEM) each contribute measurable gains in the ablation, with the full model achieving the best FR (33.97%), BT (79.63%), and multi-ability SR (52.08%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Dirichlet Process Mixture Model (DPMM) from its traditional role as a clustering tool into a dual-purpose mechanism: it simultaneously constructs dynamic knowledge spaces to mitigate catastrophic forgetting and supplies the mediator variables required for Pearl's front-door causal adjustment. The backbone (Transfuser++) and the evolutionary trajectory decoder draw on the transformer-based E2E-AD lineage (Transfuser, DriveTransformer, HiP-AD), while the lifelong learning component adapts ideas from continual learning (PackNet, Experience Replay) without relying on their rehearsal or pruning machinery. Conceptually, the paper carves new ground by treating the non-parametric cluster count as a natural solution to the 'unknown number of future tasks' problem in driving, and by using learned knowledge anchors as causal mediators rather than as mere prototypes for retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeLL is the first framework to formalize and evaluate lifelong learning in closed-loop E2E-AD, and the proposed Bench2Drive-based protocol (with vertical/horizontal/comprehensive metrics) gives the community a reusable benchmark for this previously unaddressed setting. The notable backward-transfer gain (52.83% → 79.63%) and the fact that DPMM-based dynamic expansion matches or beats both rehearsal and architectural methods at equal compute cost suggest a promising direction for open-world deployment. However, the causal deconfounding claims rest on a somewhat loose mapping of cluster centers to front-door mediators, all experiments are confined to the CARLA simulator with a single benchmark, and the alternating DPMM–network optimization introduces non-trivial training overhead—leaving the simulation-to-reality gap and the practical scalability of the non-parametric component as significant open questions.
