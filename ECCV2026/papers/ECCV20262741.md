## Problem

Existing image editing benchmarks lack high-quality facial expression samples with paired ground-truth references, making objective evaluation of fine-grained expression editing impossible. Moreover, widely-used metrics exhibit systemic biases: fidelity-weighted scoring rewards lazy editing (outputting the source unchanged), while alignment-weighted scoring rewards overfit editing (exaggerated, identity-destroying distortions). No prior benchmark or evaluation protocol simultaneously addresses both the data gap and the metric bias specific to facial expression manipulation.

## Input / Output

Each benchmark triplet consists of a source facial image (curated from SFEW 2.0 and DFEW movie clips with diverse in-the-wild backgrounds), a text editing instruction (either a simple one-liner like 'change the expression from neutral to happy' or a dense instruction describing specific muscle movements, eye curvature, and mouth opening), and a human-verified ground-truth target image. The evaluation protocol consumes the source image, the generated target image, the instruction text, and the GT reference to produce a scalar FED-Score decomposed into Fidelity (ArcFace cosine similarity, background RMSE, MLLM perceptual quality), Alignment (MLLM semantic consistency, MLLM GT-based expression alignment), and Relative Expression Gain (LPIPS ratio with Gaussian penalty). A 20k+ in-the-wild training set of source–target pairs is also released for fine-tuning.

## Method

The benchmark is constructed via a five-stage pipeline: (1) source screening from movie-clip datasets, (2) candidate target generation using a state-of-the-art editing model (Nano Banana) with templated instructions across seven basic emotions, (3) coarse-grained expression verification using a 5-model MLLM ensemble that regroups seven expressions into three polarities (positive/neutral/negative) to boost recognition accuracy from ~65% to ~85%, (4) fidelity ranking via ArcFace identity similarity and background RMSE to retain only the top-2 candidates, and (5) human-in-the-loop verification with majority voting. The FED-Score evaluation protocol multiplies three orthogonal dimensions—Fidelity (mean of ID, BG, PQ), Alignment (mean of SC, GTA), and REG (exp(−(LPIPS_ratio−1)²/2σ²) with σ=0.5)—so that failure in any single dimension suppresses the overall score. The REG metric normalizes the model's actual facial-region perceptual change by the GT's expected change, naturally penalizing both lazy editing (ratio≪1) and overfit editing (ratio≫1). The multiplicative aggregation, combined with MLLM-based perceptual scoring (Gemini-2.5-Pro) for PQ, SC, and GTA, achieves 0.77 agreement with human 2AFC judgments, substantially outperforming all single-metric baselines.

## Results

On FED-Bench (747 triplets, 7 basic emotions), Qwen-Image-Edit-Plus achieves the highest FED-Score of 0.469 under Dense instructions and 0.492 under Simple instructions, leading in GTA (5.7/5.8) while maintaining competitive ID (0.58/0.63) and REG (1.18/1.13). SeedDream 4.0 (0.379/0.413) and FLUX.2 Pro (0.377/0.400) follow, with the top-3 ranking stable across instruction granularities. The paper's fine-tuned FLUX-Kontext-FED reaches 0.332 (Dense) and 0.325 (Simple), substantially outperforming the base FLUX-Kontext-Dev (0.243/0.120), validating the 20k+ training set. InstructPix2Pix scores near zero (0.001/0.004) due to catastrophic quality degradation (PQ≈0) and extreme overfit editing (REG=2.56).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen-Image-Edit-Plus dominates all 18 benchmarked models on FED-Score under both instruction types, with its advantage driven primarily by GTA (5.7 vs. 4.3 for SeedDream 4.0 and 4.0 for FLUX.2 Pro), indicating superior expression alignment. SeedDream 4.0 and FLUX.2 Pro trail by ~0.09 in FED-Score but compensate with stronger SC (9.1/9.0 vs. 8.8) and slightly better ID. FLUX-Kontext-Dev achieves the second-highest ID (0.72) yet suffers from lazy editing (REG=0.67), dropping to 10th place; the paper's fine-tuned FLUX-Kontext-FED corrects this (REG=0.95, ID=0.68) and jumps to 5th. DreamOmni2 exhibits the most extreme lazy editing (highest ID=0.81 but REG=0.45), while InstructPix2Pix shows the most extreme overfit editing (REG=2.56, PQ≈0), both receiving near-zero FED-Scores. OmniGen2 and Bagel are penalized heavily for background destruction (BG=63.7 and 47.2 respectively), and Step1X/Step1X v1p2 show overfit tendencies (REG=1.72/1.31) with weak GTA (1.3/3.0). The key finding is that no model simultaneously achieves high fidelity and accurate expression manipulation—fine-grained instruction following (SC, GTA) is the primary bottleneck across all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a dedicated evaluation space for facial expression editing that prior general-purpose editing benchmarks (Imgedit, EditBench, I2ebench, MagicBrush, HQ-Edit) leave unaddressed, as they lack high-quality facial samples, expression-specific instructions, and—critically—ground-truth reference images. Methodologically, it repurposes existing components (ArcFace for identity, LPIPS for perceptual distance, Grounded SAM 2 for segmentation, MLLMs for perceptual scoring) into a novel decoupled protocol (FED-Score) inspired by VIEScore's MLLM-judging framework, but introduces the Relative Expression Gain metric as a new mechanism to simultaneously penalize lazy and overfit editing. The data construction pipeline is itself a contribution: a cascaded MLLM-based coarse-grained expression recognition strategy (regrouping 7 emotions into 3 polarities) combined with multi-model voting and human verification, which no prior facial editing dataset has employed at this scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FED-Bench is the first benchmark to provide pixel-level ground-truth references for facial expression editing, enabling objective quantitative evaluation that was previously impossible; the 20k+ in-the-wild training set and the demonstrated fine-tuning gains on FLUX-Kontext-Dev make it immediately actionable for practitioners. A key open limitation is that all GT images are pseudo-ground-truth (synthesized by a single generator), so each triplet reflects one plausible target rather than a unique answer; collecting truly paired in-the-wild expression data remains future work. The benchmarking of 18 models reveals that fine-grained instruction following (SC, GTA) is the primary bottleneck across the field, and that no current model simultaneously achieves high fidelity and accurate expression manipulation—findings that should redirect research effort toward instruction-conditioned expression control rather than further fidelity improvements alone.
