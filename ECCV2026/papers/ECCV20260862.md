## Problem

Existing image watermarks survive only in the initial frames of image-to-video (I2V) generated videos due to inherent semantic drift, enabling attackers to perform 'IP laundering via temporal cropping'—simply discarding early extractable frames to obtain watermark-free derivative works. Simply increasing forward embedding strength cannot overcome the saturation point of non-linear generative divergence, making long-term robustness across the full temporal horizon an unsolved challenge.

## Input / Output

For encoding, the method takes a 256×256 RGB image and a 32-bit binary watermark message, producing a visually indistinguishable watermarked image (PSNR > 36 dB enforced for fair comparison); arbitrary input resolutions are supported via a residual rescaling and aggregation strategy. For extraction, the method accepts either a single image (direct decoder pass) or a video clip, where each frame is first temporally inverted toward the reference frame and then decoded, with final watermark recovered via bit-majority voting across all L frames. The decoder is a ConvNeXt backbone followed by an MLP that outputs a soft vector binarized into the 32-bit message.

## Method

LoT-Pass introduces a bidirectional defense against temporal drift: (1) a Temporal Evolution Simulation module during training that applies SD-VAE compression-reconstruction and Gaussian random warping to the watermarked image, proactively expanding the decoder's robustness boundary to match I2V-induced distortions; and (2) a Temporal Inversion Module at inference that uses RAFT-based optical flow to warp each drifted frame back into the reference frame's coordinate system, pulling unextractable late-stage frames into the decoder's extractable manifold. The encoder is a MUNIT-based feature fusion network with a JND module for imperceptible embedding, trained jointly with a ResNet discriminator under adversarial loss and LPIPS constraints. Training follows a two-stage schedule—first converging without the noise layer, then activating it with adaptive per-noise reweighting based on validation error—and includes a 50% probability of routing noised images through the temporal inversion module so the decoder learns aligned-signal features. This combination of forward robustness expansion and backward frame recovery is the core architectural distinction from prior one-directional watermarking approaches.

## Results

On classic image distortions (Table 3), LoT-Pass achieves near-perfect bit accuracy across all 12 test conditions, with 99.98% on identity, 99.94% on Gaussian blur, and 97.53% on random bending — a >22 percentage-point improvement over the second-best method (WAM at 74.78%). On I2V generation, LoT-Pass achieves SOTA frame accuracy (FACC) across all four open-source models (CogVideoX, Wan, Hunyuan, SVD) and two commercial models (Kling, Hailuo), with RDD_Kling=120 and RDD_Hailuo=47. Under a 20% frame-drop attack on CogVideoX, LoT-Pass retains VACC=0.910 and RDD=13, while most baselines drop to near-zero robustness. The model is lightweight (51.1M params, 0.27 GB peak memory) with PSNR 38.58 dB and LPIPS 0.010 on watermarked images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoT-Pass outperforms all seven baselines on the core I2V robustness metrics (FACC, VACC, RDD) across all four open-source and two commercial video generation models, with the gap widening at later frames where baselines degrade sharply. Among baselines, WAM and TrustMark show the strongest I2V survival (their large-scale speckled watermark patterns retain higher VAE latent differences), yet both still fall well short of LoT-Pass's temporal persistence. On classic distortions, LoT-Pass is competitive with WAM and Robust-Wide on most conditions but achieves a decisive 22+ point advantage on random bending (97.53% vs. 74.78% WAM, 60.88% Robust-Wide), validating its temporal evolution simulation. SSLWM, CIN, MuST, and VINE are clearly outperformed, with several dropping below 60% ACC on cropping or bending. Under the frame-drop attack (Table 4), LoT-Pass maintains VACC ≥ 0.874 on CogVideoX and Wan even after discarding 20% of initial frames, whereas baselines lose extractability almost immediately. On SVD (non-prompt-guided), all methods including LoT-Pass show severely degraded RDD (≤1), reflecting the uncontrolled degradation inherent to that model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image watermarking literature (SSLWM, CIN, MuST, WAM, TrustMark, VINE, Robust-Wide) by identifying a fundamentally different adversarial setting: temporal semantic drift introduced by I2V generation models, which causes watermarks to degrade progressively across frames. It departs from prior work's unidirectional robustness paradigm (making the signal survive distortions) by introducing a bidirectional strategy—expanding the robustness boundary via VAE-reconstruction and random-warping simulation during training, and actively rewinding drifted frames via RAFT-based optical-flow inversion at inference. The encoder leverages MUNIT-style feature fusion with JND masking, while the decoder uses ConvNeXt, both standard components repurposed for the temporal-robustness objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As I2V models (SVD, CogVideoX, Wan, Hunyuan, Kling, Hailuo) become the default pipeline for turning still images into video, the temporal-cropping attack identified here is a trivial and practical bypass of all existing image watermarks, making long-term robustness a genuine regulatory and copyright need. The paper's new metrics (FACC, VACC, RDD) provide the field with a standardized way to measure temporal watermark persistence, which was previously undefined. Open questions remain: how the approach scales to very long videos or to text-to-video settings where no reference frame exists, whether optical-flow inversion degrades under large semantic changes (e.g., scene transitions), and whether the 256×256 training resolution limits practical deployment at higher resolutions.
