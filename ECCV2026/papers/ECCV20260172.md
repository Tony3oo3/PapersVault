## Problem

Existing training-free visual token pruning methods for MLLMs rely on attention weights as a proxy for importance, but self-attention in deep networks acts as a low-pass filter that suppresses high-frequency components (textures, edges) while amplifying redundant low-frequency background tokens trapped in attention sinks. This causes attention-based pruning to discard precisely the fine-grained details that matter for perception tasks, and global unstructured pruning further disrupts spatial continuity, degrading performance on detail-sensitive benchmarks.

## Input / Output

SWIFT operates on high-resolution images (evaluated at 840×840, producing 900 visual tokens) paired with a text prompt, and is inserted as a plug-and-play module between the 2nd and 3rd LLM layers during the prefilling stage. It outputs a compressed visual token sequence (reduced by 50%, 66%, or 75%) that replaces the original sequence for all subsequent LLM layers, yielding a shorter KV cache and faster decoding while producing the same text response format as the uncompressed model.

## Method

SWIFT introduces a 'frequency-spatial' dual-stream selection mechanism. The Frequency-Aware Indicator (FAI) decomposes the attention matrix into a uniform low-frequency basis (1/n · 11ᵀ) and a high-frequency residual, then scores each visual token by the column mean of the residual to identify tokens carrying edges and textures that deviate from the global average. The Spatial-Window Integration (SWI) module remaps the 1D token sequence back to a 2D grid and enforces independent Top-K retention within each local window, preventing spatial blind spots and positional bias that arise from global sorting. A hybrid score fuses the frequency rank and the standard attention rank with a balancing coefficient λ≈0.5, and the final pruned set is selected by per-window Top-K on this combined score, requiring no retraining and adding less than 2% overhead to TTFT.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B at 50% compression (288 tokens retained), SWIFT achieves near-lossless performance: 64.5 MMB, 1607 MME, 85.2 POPE, and 105.4 Nocaps, closely matching the vanilla model (64.1, 1610, 86.4, 105.6). Under extreme 75% compression (144 tokens), SWIFT retains 62.6 MMB and 1607 MME with only a 5.1% average drop across 8 benchmarks, while delivering a 2.66× inference speedup and 69.07% KV cache reduction. On the edge-oriented Qwen2.5VL-3B at 75% compression, SWIFT scores 74.0 MMB and 53.4 MMStar, surpassing FastV at a less aggressive 66% compression (74.3 MMB, 50.6 MMStar), demonstrating superior token efficiency for small models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWIFT consistently outperforms both FastV and SparseVLM across all three compression ratios on LLaVA-1.5-7B. Against FastV, the gap widens sharply with compression: at 90% pruning, SWIFT leads by +14.4 on Nocaps (89.1 vs. 74.7), +9.9 on Flickr30k (65.0 vs. 55.1), and +9.0 on POPE (75.3 vs. 66.3), confirming FAI's role in preserving high-frequency details that attention-based pruning discards. Against SparseVLM, the advantage is even more pronounced at extreme compression, with SWIFT scoring 89.1 vs. 50.9 on Nocaps (+38.2) and 65.0 vs. 36.6 on Flickr30k (+28.4) at 90% pruning, indicating that text-guided attention scoring is particularly vulnerable to the low-pass filtering problem. On the edge-targeted Qwen2.5VL-3B, SWIFT at 75% compression (225 tokens) matches or exceeds FastV at 66% compression (306 tokens) on MMStar (53.4 vs. 50.6) and OK-VQA (38.0 vs. 38.2), while leading by +2.6 on MMB and +3.4 on Nocaps, demonstrating that SWIFT extracts more semantic value per retained token in capacity-constrained models.

## Relation to Prior Work

SWIFT departs from the dominant training-free token pruning paradigm (FastV, SparseVLM) that equates attention weight with token importance, reframing the problem through a signal-processing lens: self-attention in deep layers acts as a low-pass filter that suppresses high-frequency detail. Its Frequency-Aware Indicator draws on attention matrix factorization (Lee et al., 2025) to isolate high-frequency residuals, while the Spatial-Window Integration module addresses positional bias and spatial collapse identified in recent analyses of global pruning (Wen et al., 2025). Conceptually, SWIFT positions itself as a 'frequency-spatial' dual-stream alternative to purely attention-score-based selection, building on the plug-and-play inference-stage compression framework established by FastV but replacing its single scoring criterion with a hybrid frequency-plus-attention formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying MLLMs on edge devices, SWIFT offers a concrete, zero-retraining recipe that delivers meaningful speedup (2.10×–2.66×) and KV-cache reduction (up to 69%) with modest accuracy loss, making it a practical building block for on-device multimodal inference. The frequency-domain reinterpretation of self-attention as a low-pass filter provides a useful diagnostic lens that could inform future pruning, merging, or distillation strategies beyond token selection. Open questions include whether the 50/50 attention-frequency balance generalizes to video MLLMs or much larger models, whether the approach transfers to architectures without explicit 2D spatial token grids, and whether the spectral preservation benefit persists under iterative multi-turn dialogue where KV cache accumulates.
