## Problem

Existing 3D semantic scene graph generation methods suffer from two coupled failures: unstable 3D object geometry caused by noisy depth estimates during 2D-to-3D lifting (leading to fragmented or incorrectly merged instances), and relational sparsity because relations are inferred independently per frame without accumulating spatiotemporal evidence. Treating scene graph construction as a sequence of independent frame-level predictions prevents the system from building coherent, globally consistent scene structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260800/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB-D image sequence along with camera intrinsics and per-frame 6-DoF poses, processing frames incrementally at ~108 ms/frame. Each object is represented not as a point or bounding box but as a full 3D Gaussian distribution (3D mean vector and 3x3 covariance) estimated from mask-guided depth samples, and the output graph's directed edges carry predicate labels drawn from a dataset-specific relation vocabulary. The global graph is built online via incremental merging, so the output grows and refines as new frames arrive rather than being produced in a single batch pass.

## Method

DeWorldSG reformulates 2D-to-3D lifting as direct estimation of instance-level 3D Gaussians from depth: SAM-guided binary masks isolate per-object pixels, a Dual-Domain Depth Refinement step first applies spatial median filtering (removing flying pixels) then 1D depth-axis clustering (isolating the dominant depth layer), and the surviving pixels are back-projected to compute the Gaussian mean and covariance. Cross-frame object identity is established by jointly thresholding Hellinger distance (geometric) and an inner-product class distance (semantic), after which Gaussians are fused via observation-frequency-weighted averaging that also accounts for mean separation in the covariance. For relations, a frozen V-JEPA 2 video backbone encodes 16-frame union-crop clips of candidate object pairs (selected by a geometric proximity cost), and a lightweight MLP probe predicts predicate distributions that are temporally accumulated per class-pair; these world-model priors are injected only when the entropy of the visually aggregated relation distribution exceeds a threshold, via uncertainty-aware additive fusion that supplements ambiguous edges without overriding strong visual evidence.

## Results

On the 3DSSG benchmark, DeWorldSG achieves 50.2% Relationship Recall, 75.0% Object Recall, and 57.3% Predicate Recall, surpassing the prior SoTA (FROSS) by +77.4%, +20.2%, and +23.2% respectively. Under class-balanced mRecall, it reaches 70.6% Object and 36.0% Predicate, also leading all baselines. On ReplicaSSG, DeWorldSG gains +71.3% in Relationship Recall, +35.6% in Object Recall, and +62.9% in Predicate Recall over FROSS, and retains 89.2% of its GT-pose Object Recall when using ORB-SLAM3 estimated poses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeWorldSG outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric across both 3DSSG and ReplicaSSG. Against the closest prior SoTA, FROSS [13], it more than doubles Relationship Recall (50.2% vs. 27.9%) and nearly doubles Predicate Recall (57.3% vs. 33.0%) on 3DSSG, while also improving Object Recall by 12.6 absolute points. Point-cloud-based methods (3DSSG [42], SGFN [45], MonoSSG [44]) that rely on explicit 3D geometry are surpassed on all metrics despite DeWorldSG using only depth-guided lifting without scene-level point-cloud reconstruction. Among lifting-based baselines, VGFM [10] and Kim [21] show particularly weak relational reasoning (19.6% and 9.1% Rel. Recall), which DeWorldSG's spatiotemporal aggregation and world-model priors address directly. On ReplicaSSG, the gap over FROSS widens further in relative terms (+71.3% Rel., +62.9% Pred.), and DeWorldSG maintains 89.2% of its GT-pose Object Recall under ORB-SLAM3 pose noise, whereas FROSS drops only marginally, confirming robustness in realistic online settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeWorldSG extends the 2D-to-3D lifting paradigm introduced by FROSS [13], which first represented lifted objects as 3D Gaussians for incremental scene graph merging, by replacing FROSS's representative-depth lifting with mask-guided instance-level depth estimation and a Dual-Domain Depth Refinement pipeline that explicitly models geometric uncertainty. It also departs from the frame-wise relation inference common to all prior lifting methods (FROSS, VGFM, SGFN) by aggregating spatiotemporal predicate evidence across a 16-frame buffer and injecting priors from a frozen V-JEPA 2 video world model, borrowing the geometric proximity cost from PRISM-0 [8] for candidate pair selection. The paper positions itself against both geometry-first point-cloud methods (3DSSG, SGFN, MonoSSG) and prior lifting pipelines, arguing that probabilistic depth-aware object modeling plus temporal relation accumulation can match or exceed explicit 3D reconstruction without its computational overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The large absolute gains (27.9% → 50.2% Relation Recall on 3DSSG) and the demonstrated robustness to pose noise (89.2% of GT-pose performance with ORB-SLAM3) make this a practically relevant pipeline for online robotic manipulation and AR anchoring at ~108 ms/frame. However, the ablation reveals that the headline world-model prior contributes only ~2.4 points of Relation Recall over the DINOv2 image-level baseline, while the depth-refinement and instance-masking stages account for the bulk of the improvement—suggesting the core contribution is really the depth-aware Gaussian estimation rather than the world-model integration. Open questions include whether the V-JEPA 2 prior generalizes beyond indoor scenes, whether the 2D detector's cross-frame label inconsistency (acknowledged as a limitation) can be resolved without full 3D tracking, and whether the approach scales to outdoor or highly dynamic environments.
