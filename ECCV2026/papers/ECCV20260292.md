## Problem

Traditional in-camera colorimetric mapping relies on 1D correlated color temperature (CCT) interpolation between pre-calibrated transforms along the Planckian locus, an assumption that breaks down for modern LED illuminants whose chromaticity coordinates deviate substantially from this locus. This mismatch causes angular reproduction errors exceeding just-noticeable color differences in LED-lit scenes, exposing a fundamental limitation of characterizing illumination with a single scalar.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260292/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes only the 2D CIE xy chromaticity coordinates of the estimated white point (two scalar values) as input to the network, not the full image. It outputs a single global 3×3 color correction matrix (or a 3×6 root-polynomial mapping in the extended variant) that is applied uniformly to all pixels of the white-balanced raw image to produce CIE XYZ colors. Because the CCM is computed once per image in a single forward pass, the total inference cost is 0.01 MMACs at 1080×720, essentially identical to traditional CCT interpolation.

## Method

CCM-MLP is a lightweight ReLU MLP with one hidden layer of 32 neurons and a linear output of 8 neurons (the 9th CCM entry is fixed to 1 to remove scale ambiguity), trained with a cosine loss that minimizes angular error between corrected and ground-truth XYZ patches. The key insight is replacing 1D CCT interpolation with a learned mapping in 2D chromaticity space, trained on a lightbox dataset of 790 illuminant SPDs generated by a Telelumen multispectral LED system covering both Planckian and off-locus sources. Gaussian noise (σ=0.05) is added to input chromaticity coordinates during training to improve generalization to out-of-domain illuminants. The model is only 1.54 KB, making it a drop-in replacement for the CCM stage in the existing DNG pipeline while handling non-Planckian illuminants that CCT-based methods cannot represent.

## Results

On the lightbox dataset, the proposed 2D CCM-MLP(RP) achieves mean angular errors of 2.36° (Sony), 2.60° (Canon), 3.27° (Pixel), and 3.28° (Samsung), reducing angular reproduction error by 22% on average in LED-lit scenes compared to the standard 2-CCM DNG interpolation (3.31°/3.86°/4.56°/4.51°). The model is extremely lightweight at 2.70 KB with only 0.01 M MACs, comparable to the 0.07 KB / 0.01 M MACs of traditional 2-CCM interpolation, versus 69 KB / 13.48 M MACs for per-pixel methods like EXPINV. On the LSMI multi-illuminant dataset, 2D CCM-MLP(RP) achieves 2.14° (Galaxy) and 1.69° (Sony) angular error, outperforming 2-CCM (3.88°/8.14°) and 1D CCM-MLP (2.52°/1.88°). On the NUS dataset, where illuminants are predominantly Planckian, 1D and 2D CCM-MLP perform comparably (~1.28° vs 1.29° on Nikon), confirming the advantage is specific to non-Planckian LED sources.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard 2-CCM DNG interpolation [1] and 3-CCM [25], the 2D CCM-MLP(RP) reduces mean angular error by roughly 1° across all four cameras (e.g., Pixel: 3.27° vs 4.56°/4.53°), with the gap widening at the 90th percentile (7.25° vs 9.98°/9.95° on Pixel). Compared to the nearest-neighbor baseline [42], CCM-MLP 2D wins on mean error for every camera (e.g., Samsung: 3.60° vs 4.15° for 2D NN), and the advantage is larger than for 1D NN (5.48°). Against per-pixel direct-prediction methods CCCNN [36] and EXPINV [31], CCM-MLP 2D achieves lower mean angular error (Pixel: 3.60° vs 4.00°/3.84°) and is ~45× smaller (1.54 KB vs 69 KB) with ~1300× fewer MACs (0.01 M vs 13.48 M); however, the 2D variants of CCCNN and EXPINV can surpass CCM-MLP at higher percentiles (90th) under the most challenging illuminations, since per-pixel networks model color-dependent corrections. On the NUS dataset, where illuminants are mostly Planckian, 1D and 2D CCM-MLP perform nearly identically (Nikon: 1.29° vs 1.28°), confirming that the 2D chromaticity advantage is concentrated in LED-dominated, off-locus regions. All methods benefit from the 2D input representation, but CCM-MLP extracts the most from it while remaining a single global 3×3 (or 3×6) transform rather than a per-pixel network.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage white-balance-plus-CCM framework established by Karaimer and Brown (CVPR 2018) and formalized in the Adobe DNG specification, which relies on 1D CCT-based interpolation between pre-calibrated 3×3 matrices along the Planckian locus. The key conceptual departure is replacing the 1D CCT parameterization with 2D CIE xy chromaticity coordinates as the input to CCM prediction, motivated by the observation that modern LED illuminants deviate substantially from the Planckian locus where CCT is well-defined. The paper also positions itself against per-pixel direct-prediction methods (CCCNN, EXPINV) that learn illuminant-conditioned colorimetric mappings but require expensive per-pixel evaluation and camera spectral sensitivities, arguing instead for a single global transform predicted in one forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for industry: a 1.5 KB MLP with 0.01 MMACs that drops into the existing DNG pipeline and reduces angular error by 22% on average under LED lighting addresses a real gap as LED illumination becomes dominant. However, the method inherits the dependency on imperfect white-balance estimators, suffers from increased metamerism under narrow-band LED spectra, and shows diminishing returns near the Planckian locus (as confirmed on the NUS dataset). Open questions include whether broader LED coverage in calibration (or synthetic augmentation) can close the out-of-distribution gap, and whether the approach integrates cleanly with next-generation spatially-varying WB estimators for multi-illuminant scenes.
