## Problem

Existing data pruning methods rely on compressed sample-level embeddings to measure importance, but this creates an Embedding-Concept Misalignment: geometric proximity in embedding space does not reliably reflect semantic redundancy at the concept level, causing rare yet important concepts to be systematically discarded. Additionally, pruning decisions made in opaque embedding space are uninterpretable, preventing practitioners from auditing which semantic concepts are preserved or lost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of image-text pairs where each sample consists of an RGB image and an accompanying text caption (either the original annotation or a VLM-generated caption). Concept extraction is performed purely on the text via spaCy dependency parsing, so the image itself is not directly processed for graph construction. The output is a retained subset of samples (e.g., 7.5% or 20% of the original) together with a dataset-level concept graph whose node weights encode concept frequencies, providing a human-inspectable map of the pruned dataset's semantic landscape.

## Method

MCL represents each image-text pair as a sample-level concept graph whose nodes are atomic semantic units (Entities, Events, Attributes) extracted via dependency parsing, with edges encoding co-occurrence and dependency relations. These graphs are aggregated into a dataset-level concept graph where each node's weight counts its occurrence frequency across all samples. Concept importance is defined as a product of an inverse-fraction rarity term and a degree-based structural participation term, normalized independently within each semantic category to prevent scale imbalance. Pruning is then formulated as a budget-constrained concept coverage maximization (an NP-hard set function) and solved via a greedy marginal-gain strategy: at each step, the sample whose uncovered concepts yield the largest weighted sum is selected, ensuring that repeatedly encountered concepts contribute diminishing returns while rare, relationally rich concepts are prioritized.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-mix-665k with only 7.5% (50k) of training data retained, MCL achieves 99.0% relative performance to the full-data baseline, outperforming DataTailor (98.6%), TIVE (97.2%), and InsTag (97.0%). At the 20% (133k) budget, MCL reaches 100.5% relative performance, surpassing ICONS (99.9%) and COINCIDE (99.3%). On COCO 2017 object detection with 70% of training data, MCL achieves AP50:95 of 40.12, exceeding the full-data baseline (40.06) and outperforming DivBS (39.74) and PFB (39.69). The entire MCL pruning pipeline completes in 1.7 hours on 4×RTX 3090 GPUs, compared to days for TIVE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCL consistently outperforms all 15 compared baselines on the primary LLaVA-1.5 benchmark. At the aggressive 7.5% budget, MCL (99.0% Rel.) beats the strongest competitor DataTailor (98.6%) by 0.4 points and TIVE (97.2%) by 1.8 points; embedding-based methods like EL2N (90.1%) and IFD (83.6%) lag substantially. At the 20% budget, MCL (100.5%) surpasses both COINCIDE (99.3%) and ICONS (99.9%), the two strongest 133k-budget competitors. On COCO 2017 object detection at 70% data, MCL (AP 40.12) exceeds the full-data baseline (40.06) and beats diversity-based methods DivBS (39.74) and PFB (39.69) by 0.4–0.5 AP points, while loss-based methods EL2N (34.35) and InfoBatch (34.95) degrade by over 5 AP points. Notably, MCL also demonstrates superior cross-benchmark stability: while InsTag excels on MME-C (345.0) but collapses on GQA (52.5), and LESS is strong on SEED-I (61.2) but weak on MME-C (281.8), MCL maintains near-optimal scores across all eight evaluation benchmarks simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCL departs from the dominant paradigm of embedding-based data pruning (EL2N, DivBS, PFB, DataTailor, COINCIDE), which treats each sample as an indivisible vector and measures redundancy or diversity in a learned geometric space. Rather than building on a specific prior method, it repurposes classical submodular coverage maximization by instantiating the ground set with atomic semantic concepts (entities, events, attributes) extracted via dependency parsing, and the universe with individual samples. The conceptual shift—from optimizing geometric spread in a coupled embedding to optimizing explicit concept-level coverage—positions MCL as a structural alternative to the embedding-centric lineage, while the greedy selection mechanism itself is a standard tool from combinatorial optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical contribution is interpretability: practitioners can inspect which concepts are over- or under-represented in a pruned dataset, something opaque embedding methods cannot offer, and the entire pipeline runs in ~1.7 hours versus days for gradient- or clustering-based alternatives. However, the method's effectiveness is tightly coupled to caption quality and the completeness of dependency-parsing-based concept extraction; for datasets lacking reliable text annotations or for tasks where fine-grained visual semantics (spatial layout, texture) dominate, the concept graph may be insufficient. Open questions include whether the framework extends to caption-free image-only corpora, how it behaves at truly web-scale (tens of millions of samples), and whether the greedy approximation gap becomes problematic at very aggressive pruning ratios.
