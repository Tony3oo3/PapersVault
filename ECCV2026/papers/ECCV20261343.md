## Problem

Federated Class-Incremental Learning (FCIL) faces three intertwined challenges: catastrophic forgetting from incrementally overloading a shared global model, gradient inconsistency from Non-IID client data, and temporally misaligned class arrivals across clients. Existing FCIL methods treat the global model as a monolithic entity, lacking fine-grained mechanisms to modulate stability and plasticity per parameter or per sample, and fail to reconcile adaptability to new classes with retention of previously acquired knowledge under heterogeneous, asynchronous task settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, variable for DomainNet) and outputs a class label. During training, each sample is routed to a dynamically determined subset of experts (out of K=4) whose logits are combined with Shapley-weighted coefficients; during inference, a distilled lightweight gating network selects Top-2 experts and aggregates their predictions with normalized softmax weights, eliminating the need for costly marginal-contribution computation at deployment.

## Method

FedFMX decomposes the model into a shared backbone and K expert-specific subspaces (each with a dedicated body and classification head), then routes each sample to an adaptive expert subset via three coupled modules. The Fisher-Routed Expert Scoring (FRES) module maintains a diagonal Fisher information estimate per expert (EMA of squared gradients) and computes a stability cost (Fisher-weighted gradient norm) and plasticity gain (gradient magnitude normalized by parameter rigidity) to score expert suitability before activation. The Adaptive Expert Selection (AES) module formulates subset selection as a cooperative game, computing exact Shapley values over the candidate expert set and iteratively expanding the active set until marginal coalition gain falls below a threshold. A Routing-aware Regularization (RAR) scheme adds an entropy-based load-balancing loss and a KL-distillation objective that trains a lightweight gating network to mimic the Shapley-derived teacher distribution, enabling efficient forward-only inference. The paper proves an O(1/T) convergence rate under a bounded activated-subspace gradient residual assumption.

## Results

Under the fine-grained QLI setting (α_Q=4), FedFMX achieves 64.68% on CIFAR-10, 50.32% on CIFAR-100, 19.14% on Tiny-ImageNet, and 44.29% on DomainNet, consistently outperforming all 13 baselines. The advantage is most pronounced on Tiny-ImageNet, where FedFMX surpasses the next-best method (FedCBDR, 15.32%) by nearly 4 percentage points. Under the DLI setting (α_D=0.5), FedFMX similarly leads with 64.68% (CIFAR-10), 50.32% (CIFAR-100), 19.14% (Tiny-ImageNet), and 44.29% (DomainNet). The method also demonstrates robustness to increasing label imbalance, maintaining stable accuracy as α_Q grows from 2 to 6, while most baselines degrade.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedFMX outperforms all 13 baselines across the four datasets under both QLI and DLI settings. Against the strongest FCIL baselines, FedFMX leads FedCBDR by +3.23% on CIFAR-10, +3.35% on CIFAR-100, and +3.82% on Tiny-ImageNet (α_Q=4), though the two are nearly tied on DomainNet (44.29 vs 44.37). Similarly, FedFMX surpasses pFedMxF by +3.30% (CIFAR-10), +3.44% (CIFAR-100), and +3.87% (Tiny-ImageNet). The gap widens substantially on Tiny-ImageNet, where the fine-grained 20-task setting amplifies forgetting; FedFMX's Fisher-guided routing and adaptive expert selection preserve prior knowledge more effectively than replay-based (Re-FED, 12.06%) or distillation-based (FL-CLIP, 11.85%) approaches. General FL methods (FedMut, FedCross, FedLSA, FedWMSAM) and traditional CIL adaptations (FedEWC, FedLwF) lag far behind, confirming that neither generic federated aggregation nor simple regularization suffices for FCIL. The MoE-based pFedMoE, while competitive on CIFAR-10 (47.96%), suffers significant degradation on harder datasets (11.02% on Tiny-ImageNet), highlighting the limitation of static expert assignment without stability-aware routing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the diagonal Fisher information matrix from EWC-style continual learning (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) — traditionally used as a post-update parameter regularizer — into a pre-update routing criterion that scores expert suitability before activation. It sits at the intersection of two active lines of work: MoE-based federated learning (pFedMoE, FedMoE) and federated class-incremental learning (TARGET, LANDER, Re-FED, pFedMxF), neither of which had previously combined Fisher-guided routing with cooperative-game-based expert selection. The Shapley-value formulation for adaptive expert subset selection departs from the static Top-K gating common in MoE literature, introducing a game-theoretic perspective on expert collaboration that is novel in the FL context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FCIL is a practically important but underexplored setting where clients receive new classes asynchronously under Non-IID conditions, and the Fisher-routed MoE framework offers a principled mechanism to balance stability and plasticity at the sample level rather than the parameter level. The O(1/T) convergence guarantee under bounded subspace residual provides theoretical grounding that prior FCIL methods largely lack. Open questions include scalability beyond K≤8 experts (where exact Shapley computation becomes infeasible), applicability to non-vision modalities, and whether the distillation-based gating generalizes when the expert pool grows substantially. The modest computational overhead (~10% FLOPs increase over pFedMoE) makes the approach deployable, though the 20% client sampling and 100-client setup may not reflect all real-world federated deployments.
