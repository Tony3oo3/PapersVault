## Problem

Existing egocentric hand gesture datasets describe lighting only qualitatively (e.g., 'indoor', 'bright') and rarely document spatial illuminance variation, making it impossible to systematically evaluate how recognition accuracy degrades across specific illuminance ranges. This gap hinders the development of illumination-robust gesture recognition for intelligent reality (AR/VR/MR) systems that must operate reliably across diverse real-world lighting conditions and mobility scenarios.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 21-joint 3D hand skeletons extracted by MediaPipe Hands (confidence threshold 0.5–0.7) from 4K/30 fps egocentric GoPro HERO13 video. The static partition uses a single representative frame per sequence as a 42×3 tensor (dual-hand, zero-padded for single-hand gestures), while the dynamic partition feeds overlapping T=16-frame windows (stride 8) extended to 6 channels by appending a mid-frame displacement vector to encode dominant motion direction. Output is a 19-class softmax gesture label.

## Method

The baseline is a spatiotemporal graph convolutional network (ST-GCN) with 9 spatial graph convolution layers (channels 64→256), temporal kernels of size 9, and 254,601 parameters, operating on the 21-joint hand skeleton graph. An enhanced variant (E-ST-GCN) adds a tri-attention mechanism comprising joint reliability gating (confidence-weighted joint selection), spatial attention over joint relationships, and temporal attention over motion patterns. Training uses AdamW for 120 epochs (batch 32) with weighted cross-entropy loss to address class imbalance across 19 gesture classes. The dataset's core contribution is a bi-conditional lighting protocol (full: 320±123 lux, low: 207±134 lux) with calibrated illuminance measured at ~80 spatial points across a 4.95× dynamic range (29–523 lux), combined with a 3×2 factorial mobility-lighting design (sitting/standing/walking × full/low), enabling reproducible illumination-robustness benchmarking that no prior egocentric gesture dataset provides.

## Results

On the dynamic (temporal) partition, E-ST-GCN with tri-attention achieves 89.24% validation accuracy (F1 = 0.879), while the no-attention variant reaches 88.57% (F1 = 0.878). On the static (single-frame) partition, the no-attention ST-GCN slightly outperforms the attention variant at 81.45% vs. 80.38% accuracy, isolating a 7.3-percentage-point gain from temporal information alone. Inference speed ranges from 1,661 FPS (static, no attention) to 614 FPS (dynamic, with attention) on an RTX 3090, confirming real-time feasibility for AR/VR deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external gesture-recognition methods; instead it establishes internal baselines to characterise the dataset. The primary comparison is static vs. dynamic input: adding temporal sequences (T=16-frame windows with displacement channels) lifts accuracy by 7.3 pp (81.45% → 88.57% no-attention; 80.38% → 89.24% with attention), confirming that motion cues are essential for distinguishing trajectory-dependent gestures such as clockwise vs. counter-clockwise circles. The tri-attention mechanism (joint reliability + spatial + temporal) provides a marginal 0.67 pp gain on the dynamic partition but actually hurts static performance by 1.07 pp, since temporal attention is uninformative on single frames. In the dataset comparison (Table 4), BiCE-HG is the only egocentric gesture dataset offering calibrated lux measurements (80 spatial points), three mobility scenarios (sit/stand/walk), and a bi-conditional lighting protocol, whereas EgoGesture, FPHA, HoloGesture, H2O, and HaGRID all describe lighting only qualitatively and lack spatial illuminance documentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in egocentric gesture datasets by introducing calibrated spatial illuminance documentation (80 lux measurement points) alongside a 3×2 mobility-lighting factorial design, a dimension entirely absent from prior egocentric gesture benchmarks (EgoGesture, FPHA, H2O, HaGRID, HoloGesture) that describe lighting only qualitatively. The recognition baselines (ST-GCN with optional tri-attention) follow the standard skeleton-based graph convolutional pipeline established by Shen et al. and Memmi et al., with MediaPipe Hands as the pose estimator, so the methodological contribution is deliberately minimal to keep focus on the dataset. The paper positions itself as filling a reproducibility gap: no prior egocentric dataset enabled controlled, quantified illumination-robustness evaluation across a defined lux range.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BiCE-HG provides the first reproducible substrate for studying how egocentric gesture recognition degrades across a 4.95× illuminance range (29–523 lux) with spatial gradients, which is directly relevant to AR/VR deployment where users move between bright and dim zones. However, the evaluation is limited: no participant-independent split is performed (the authors acknowledge this), the participant pool is small (n=23), and the gesture vocabulary (19 classes) is modest compared to EgoGesture (83) or FPHA (45). Open questions include whether lighting-robust domain adaptation or meta-learning over mobility state can close the remaining gap on motion-sensitive gestures (circular drawing at 18–50%), and whether the spatial illuminance metadata enables meaningful per-zone accuracy stratification beyond the aggregate numbers reported here.
