## Problem

Synthesizing robust 3D adversarial camouflage textures is hindered by two optimization hurdles: sparse rendering visibility causes spatially inconsistent gradients that produce high-frequency artifacts, and cross-view gradient conflicts lead to destructive interference where updates suppressing detection in one view increase visibility in another. Existing methods rely on fixed-grid or pixel-wise parameterizations that constrain the optimization space and lack mechanisms to reconcile conflicting multi-view gradient signals.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D vehicle mesh with fixed UV mapping (Audi E-Tron in CARLA), a target object detector (YOLOv3 as white-box surrogate), and a distribution of camera viewpoints (6 elevation angles × 360° azimuth sweep, 5–20 m distance). It outputs a 2048×2048 RGB adversarial texture map, parameterized internally by two 64×64 learnable grids (Content Grid for color, Flow Grid for geometric offsets) that are upsampled and resampled via differentiable bilinear interpolation. The texture is rendered through PyTorch3D with binary visibility masks restricting gradient flow to observable surfaces.

## Method

The Deformable Texture Field (DTF) replaces discrete pixel optimization with two low-resolution learnable grids: a Content Grid encoding color patterns and a Flow Grid encoding geometric deformation offsets. The high-resolution texture is synthesized by bilinearly upsampling the flow field to a dense deformation map, then performing inverse bilinear sampling from the content grid at deformed coordinates, which enforces an intrinsic smoothness prior while allowing adaptive warping of representational capacity toward salient regions. The Gradient-Aligned Meta-Optimization (GAMO) strategy structures training as a dual-phase loop: an inner loop of k=16 SGD steps explores the local loss landscape for a sampled viewpoint (with optimizer state reset each episode), and an outer loop updates global parameters via the trajectory displacement (φ_k − Θ). This multi-step trajectory implicitly introduces a second-order regularization term that maximizes the inner product of gradients across conflicting viewpoints, steering optimization toward a shared manifold where view-specific gradient directions are mutually supportive rather than contradictory.

## Results

On the transferability benchmark (Table 1), the proposed method achieves the lowest AP@0.5 on 4 out of 5 advanced detectors: 45.44% on RTMDet, 27.84% on ConvNeXt, 13.36% on DINO, and 35.56% on Grounding DINO, compared to normal detection rates of 94.49%, 95.36%, 85.95%, and 91.24% respectively. In physical evaluation (Table 2), the method reduces YOLOv3 AP@0.5 to 34.53% at 0° elevation and 18.53% at 45°, and Faster R-CNN AP@0.5 to 70.20% at 0° and 23.39% at 45°. The method also achieves lower per-view FLOPs (140.84) and latency (135.17 ms) than GRAC (8505.60 GFLOPs, 430.10 ms) and RAUCA (973.21 GFLOPs, 265.67 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on the majority of evaluation settings. Against the strongest prior method GRAC [11], it achieves improvements of 7.46% on RTMDet (45.44 vs. 52.90), 4.82% on ConvNeXt (27.84 vs. 32.66), 9.67% on DINO (13.36 vs. 23.03), and 11.83% on Grounding DINO (35.56 vs. 47.39), though it is slightly weaker on GLIP (43.72 vs. 41.42), which the authors attribute to GLIP's language-grounded matching being less sensitive to texture perturbations. In physical evaluation, the method beats GRAC by 7.19% on YOLOv3 at 0° (34.53 vs. 41.72) and by 13.15% on Faster R-CNN at 20° (41.15 vs. 54.30). Earlier baselines DAS [28], FCA [27], DTA [24], and ACTIVE [25] are substantially outperformed across all detectors, with DAS showing the weakest attack (e.g., 92.61% AP on RTMDet vs. 45.44% for the proposed method). RAUCA [33] is the second-strongest baseline but is still surpassed by 5.59–15.21% depending on the detector.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the physical adversarial camouflage line (FCA, DAS, DTA, ACTIVE, RAUCA, GRAC) by replacing pixel-wise or fixed-grid UV-map parameterization with a continuous Deformable Texture Field that decouples appearance (Content Grid) from geometry (Flow Grid). Its second departure is the reinterpretation of first-order meta-learning (MAML) as a gradient-alignment mechanism for multi-view optimization, contrasting with GRAC's explicit gradient reweighting and with standard multi-task solvers like PCGrad or MGDA. Conceptually, the paper frames the core difficulty as a structural mismatch between static 2D texture parameters and dynamic 3D-to-2D observations, positioning both DTF and GAMO as complementary solutions to spatial discontinuity and cross-view destructive interference respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Physical adversarial camouflage on vehicles is a concrete safety concern for autonomous driving, and this work demonstrates that current detectors (including vision-language models like Grounding DINO) remain vulnerable to printed 3D textures under diverse real-world conditions. The finding that a meta-learning-inspired optimizer outperforms purpose-built multi-task gradient surgery methods (PCGrad, MGDA) while also being more efficient than prior camouflage pipelines suggests the inner-outer trajectory structure may be a broadly useful tool for any multi-view or multi-task 3D optimization. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the single Audi E-Tron mesh, how it fares against emerging defense mechanisms (adversarial training, input transformations), and whether the 64×64 grid resolution is sufficient for more geometrically complex objects.
