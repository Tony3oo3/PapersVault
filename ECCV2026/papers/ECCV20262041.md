## Problem

3D CT volumes contain massive feature redundancy and lesion sparsity, making it difficult to extract information-rich representations without incurring prohibitive computational costs. Existing token compression strategies—spatial pooling, mask-guided cropping, and pure attention-weight selection—either discard critical local detail or are vulnerable to low-semantic 'sink tokens' that dominate attention and obscure fine-grained findings essential for radiology reporting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262041/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a 3D CT volume (resampled to 480×480×240 voxels for CT-RATE or 256×256×64 for CTRG-Chest-548K) along with optional clinical metadata (chief complaint, medical history, symptoms). The CT-ViT encoder initially produces 4,096 raw patch tokens, which are compressed to 160 tokens (1 global + 15 local per structure across 10 anatomical regions) before reaching the report-generation LLM. The system outputs a full radiology report; during training, ground-truth abnormalities are prefixed to the reference report to boost clinical awareness, and the intermediate abnormality-proposal LLM produces a ~45-token shortlist of candidate findings per volume.

## Method

The framework imitates the radiologist's coarse-to-fine visual search in two stages. First, a ViT-based image-text alignment architecture extracts one global token and a pool of K=200 local patch tokens per anatomical structure, regularized by a novel mask-guided sparse negative-entropy loss that penalizes attention to out-of-structure patches (using SAT-generated segmentation masks only at training time). An abnormality-proposal LLM (D_pro, Llama-3.2-3B with LoRA) then takes only the global tokens plus clinical metadata to propose a shortlist of candidate abnormalities, which prompts an Abnormality-Prompted Local Token Filter (AP-LTF) to select K′=15 most informative patch tokens per structure via a combined score fusing cosine similarity to the abnormality query, a learned linear score, and the original attention weight—thereby preventing sink tokens from dominating. A second report-generation LLM (D_ful) composes the full report from the global tokens, filtered local tokens, and metadata. Finally, GRPO-based collaborative refinement optimizes D_pro's proposals against a composite reward (CE-F1 + BLEU-4) while D_ful and AP-LTF are frozen, closing the training-inference distribution gap between ground-truth-parsed and predicted abnormalities.

## Results

On CT-RATE, the proposed dual-LLM framework achieves a clinical efficacy F1 of 0.415, substantially outperforming the previous SOTA (Liu et al. [26]) at 0.315 (+32%), with precision 0.467 and recall 0.429. On CTRG-Chest-548K, it reaches CE F1 of 0.399, BLEU-4 of 0.335, and ROUGE-L of 0.504, again surpassing all baselines. On radiology-specific metrics, the method attains a RaTEScore of 0.679 (vs. 0.569 for Liu et al.) and a GREEN score of 0.433 (vs. 0.376 for Liu et al. and 0.429 for μ² Tokenizer), confirming superiority across both clinical and linguistic evaluation dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best CE F1 on both datasets, improving over the closest CTRG-specific baseline Liu et al. [26] by 32% on CT-RATE (0.415 vs. 0.315) and 2% on CTRG-Chest-548K (0.399 vs. 0.393). Among X-ray-oriented baselines (R2Gen, R2GenCMN, M2KT, R2GenGPT, PromptMRG), CE F1 scores range from 0.066 to 0.299 on CT-RATE, far below the proposed 0.415, indicating that 2D report-generation strategies do not transfer well to volumetric CT. Among dedicated CTRG methods, 3D-CT-GPT [8], CT2Rep [18], and M3D [2] all score below 0.17 CE F1 on CT-RATE due to their coarse spatial-pooling representations, while Reg2RG [12] reaches 0.253 by leveraging region-specific cropping but still trails the proposed method by 0.162 F1. On NLG metrics, the proposed method is competitive but not uniformly best: Liu et al. [26] retains a slight edge in BLEU-1 on CT-RATE (0.540 vs. 0.535) and CTRG-Chest-548K (0.545 vs. 0.532), while the proposed method leads in BLEU-4, METEOR, and ROUGE-L on both datasets. On the radiology-specific GREEN metric, the proposed method (0.433) narrowly surpasses μ² Tokenizer [24] (0.429) and clearly outperforms Liu et al. [26] (0.376), while its RaTEScore of 0.679 exceeds Liu et al.'s 0.569 by 19%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the structure-wise image-text alignment framework of Liu et al. (IPMI 2025), which introduced per-structure global and local token extraction for CTRG, by addressing two key limitations: (1) the vulnerability of attention-weight-based token selection to 'sink tokens' (low-semantic tokens that attract disproportionate attention), and (2) the lack of a mechanism to prioritize clinically relevant findings. The authors depart from prior single-LLM CTRG pipelines (3D-CT-GPT, M3D, CT2Rep) by introducing a dual-LLM architecture that mirrors the radiologist's coarse-to-fine visual search: a lightweight abnormality-proposal LLM first generates a candidate shortlist from a single global token per structure, which then conditions an Abnormality-Prompted Local Token Filter (AP-LTF) to select informative patch tokens. The mask-guided sparse negative-entropy loss draws on prompted segmentation (SAT) to provide direct spatial supervision during training, complementing the indirect text-based alignment of prior work, while GRPO-based collaborative refinement adapts a reinforcement-learning technique from the LLM alignment literature to close the training-inference distribution gap between the two LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 32% absolute F1 improvement over the previous SOTA (0.315→0.415) on CT-RATE is clinically meaningful, as it directly reduces missed findings in automated radiology reporting. The coarse-to-fine paradigm grounded in cognitive science of visual search offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc token compression, and the AP-LTF mechanism provides a general recipe for combining multiple evidence sources (semantic similarity, learned scores, attention weights) to avoid the well-documented sink-token pathology. However, the framework remains at structure-level granularity and does not yet capture lesion-level details (e.g., individual pulmonary nodules), which the authors acknowledge as a key limitation. Open questions include whether the dual-LLM + GRPO pipeline generalizes beyond chest CT to other 3D modalities (MRI, ultrasound), whether the 3B LLM capacity is sufficient for more complex multi-organ reporting, and whether the SAT-based segmentation masks, used only at training time, can be replaced by lighter alternatives to reduce data preparation overhead.
