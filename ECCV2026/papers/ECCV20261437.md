## Problem

Existing safety research on multimodal models studies jailbreak attacks on understanding (LLMs, LVLMs) and generation (T2I) systems in isolation, assuming they are separate unidirectional pipelines. Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) break this assumption by tightly coupling generation and understanding within a shared backbone, enabling bidirectional signal propagation that prior work has not systematically examined. This paper asks whether this cross-functionality reciprocity itself constitutes a structural source of vulnerability unique to UMMs.

## Input / Output

For the G2U direction, the input is a single harmful text query (e.g., from AdvBench, HarmBench), which is decomposed into a deictic action string and a visual-attribute object description; the object description is rendered into an image by the UMM's generation head, and the pair (action text, generated image) is fed back to the understanding head to elicit a harmful text response. For the U2G direction, the input is a text prompt with harmful visual intent (e.g., from I2P, T2IRiskyPrompt), which the understanding head rewrites into a visually amplified description that is then passed to the generation head to produce an unsafe RGB image. Notably, both pipelines rely solely on the internal capabilities of a single UMM without any external model, and evaluation uses LLaMA-Guard-3-8B for text safety and a combination of Q16, NudeNet, and Qwen2.5-VL-3B for image safety.

## Method

RICE (Reciprocal Interaction-based Cross-functionality Exploitation) is a prompt-engineering attack framework with two complementary pathways that exploit the shared representation space of a UMM. In G2U, the understanding head first decomposes a harmful query into a deictic action component (preserving behavioral intent while replacing the sensitive noun with a context-dependent reference) and a visual-attribute object component (describing only appearance, not procedure); the object description is then rendered into an image by the generation head, and the resulting (action text, image) pair is fed back to the understanding head, creating a closed-loop text→image→text interaction that bypasses lexical safety filters. In U2G, the understanding head performs visual-fidelity expansion—amplifying object attributes, spatial structure, and physical realism while deliberately avoiding procedural reasoning or step-by-step planning that would trigger the model's internal safety deliberation—and the resulting visually explicit prompt is passed to the generation head. The ablation study confirms that the attack's effectiveness depends on the cross-functionality reconstruction step (generating an image from the decomposed object and feeding it back), not merely on semantic decomposition or naive image injection, establishing reciprocity as the structural vulnerability.

## Results

On the G2U attack, RICE achieves 89.62% ASR on AdvBench and 82.50% on HarmBench with Bagel, and 92.69% on AdvBench and 86.00% on HarmBench with Janus-Pro-7B, substantially outperforming the strongest multimodal baseline FigStep-Pro (65.19%/74.25% on Bagel; 76.92%/79.75% on Janus). On the U2G attack, RICE reaches 42.32% NudeNet ASR on I2P with Bagel and 25.35% with Janus, exceeding the best T2I jailbreak baselines (Sneaky: 38.23%/21.16%; DACA: 35.02%/18.80%). Across all five G2U benchmarks and both models, RICE consistently attains the highest ASR, with gains of 15–25 percentage points over the next-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RICE outperforms all seven baselines across every G2U benchmark and both UMMs. Against text-only jailbreak methods (PAIR, AutoDAN, GCG), RICE gains 20–50 percentage points in ASR; for example, on AdvBench with Bagel, RICE reaches 89.62% versus GCG's 61.54% and PAIR's 64.04%. Against the strongest multimodal baseline FigStep-Pro, RICE still leads by 14.43–24.34 points (e.g., 89.62% vs. 65.19% on Bagel/AdvBench; 92.69% vs. 76.92% on Janus/AdvBench). On the U2G side, RICE surpasses SneakyPrompt, DACA, and SGT on most detector–benchmark combinations; on I2P with Bagel, RICE achieves 42.32% NudeNet ASR versus Sneaky's 38.23% and DACA's 35.02%, and on the Explicit subset with Janus it reaches 35.09% Q16 versus SGT's 30.06%. The Self-CoT baseline (which uses the model's own reasoning to refine prompts) actually *reduces* ASR relative to Vanilla on Janus (e.g., 13.53% vs. 18.37% Q16 on I2P), confirming that procedural reasoning activates safety deliberation, whereas RICE's visual-only expansion avoids this suppression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the well-established jailbreak attack literature for LLMs (PAIR, AutoDAN, GCG), LVLMs (FigStep), and T2I models (SneakyPrompt, DACA, SGT) into a new setting: exploiting the internal generation-understanding reciprocity within a single Unified Multimodal Model. The closest prior work, STAR-Attack, uses an external model (GPT-4o) to orchestrate a one-directional generation-assisted jailbreak, whereas RICE operates entirely within the UMM's own capabilities and examines both G→U and U→G pathways. Conceptually, the paper departs from the modular assumption underlying all prior multimodal safety work—that understanding and generation can be analyzed independently—by treating the shared backbone's bidirectional information flow as the attack surface itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple semantic decomposition plus a single generation step within the same model yields 12–15 percentage-point ASR gains over decomposition alone (ablation in Table 3) provides concrete evidence that UMM safety alignment does not account for internal cross-functionality coupling, a structural gap that modular safety filters cannot address. However, the evaluation is limited to two UMMs (Bagel, Janus-Pro-7B) and the U2G direction covers only the sexual-content domain, leaving open whether the reciprocity vulnerability generalizes to other UMM architectures, other harm categories, or to models with stronger internal safety deliberation. A key open question is whether defenses can be designed that monitor or constrain intermediate signals flowing between the generation and understanding pathways without degrading legitimate cross-functionality performance.
