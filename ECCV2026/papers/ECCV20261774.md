## Problem

Existing relightable full-body avatar methods rely on explicit 3D intrinsic decomposition with analytic reflectance models, requiring accurate geometry registration and careful optimization that limits both light transport quality and facial expressiveness. To the authors' knowledge, no prior method demonstrates strong facial animation alongside relighting, limiting applicability in telepresence, gaming, and virtual production. The paper asks whether relighting can be entirely decoupled from avatar modeling and treated as a learned image-space post-process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261774/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, D-Rex takes skeleton poses, FLAME expression parameters, an HDR environment map (projected to the target view and tone-mapped via Reinhard), and a virtual camera viewpoint. The EVA avatar renders flat-lit (albedo-like) frames at 540×1024, which are then fed to the diffusion relighting model operating at 371×704 in 57-frame chunks. Output is a photorealistic, relit full-body avatar video; long-form sequences beyond 57 frames are stitched via linear blending with a 32-frame overlap to suppress inter-chunk illumination variance.

## Method

D-Rex decouples relighting from avatar modeling into two independently trained components: (1) an Expressive Virtual Avatar (EVA) trained on flat-lit light-stage frames to produce albedo-like renderings for arbitrary pose, expression, and viewpoint, and (2) a video diffusion relighting model (Cosmos DiffusionRenderer) fine-tuned via LoRA on paired flat-lit-to-relit frame pairs. Only the forward renderer of DiffusionRenderer is retained; the flat-lit avatar rendering is treated as an albedo approximation while metalness, roughness, and normal G-buffers are zeroed. The LoRA fine-tuning uses a standard diffusion denoising loss on single-image frame pairs, yet the pre-trained video prior preserves view and temporal consistency across 57-frame chunks without any explicit temporal regularization. This design means the relighting stage is a plug-and-play post-process applicable to any white-light avatar system, requiring no modification to the underlying avatar to support relighting.

## Results

On the novel view/motion/light setting (the harder evaluation), D-Rex achieves PSNR 26.30, LPIPS 0.077, and SSIM 0.936, outperforming the closest baseline (MeshAvatar + DiffusionRenderer) by 0.57 dB in PSNR and 0.003 in LPIPS. Under the easier novel view/motion setting, D-Rex reaches PSNR 26.62, LPIPS 0.077, SSIM 0.937. The PBR-based MeshAvatar baseline lags by over 4 dB in PSNR, and the zero-shot IC-Light baseline is dramatically inferior at PSNR ~10.4. Results are averaged across 4 subjects with ~10% of lighting conditions and motion frames held out.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-Rex outperforms all three constructed baselines on every metric in both evaluation settings. The closest competitor is MA/DR (MeshAvatar as a flat-lit driving avatar + DiffusionRenderer for relighting), which achieves PSNR 25.73 vs. D-Rex's 26.30 under novel view/motion/light — a gap of ~0.57 dB that the authors attribute to EVA's superior expressive rendering (facial detail, dynamic clothing) compared to MeshAvatar's rigid geometry. The PBR-based MeshAvatar (MA/PBR) trails by over 4 dB in PSNR and produces flat appearance with geometry artifacts, failing to model complex illumination effects like Fresnel reflectance and specular highlights. The zero-shot IC-Light baseline (EVA/IC) is dramatically inferior (PSNR ~10.4 vs. 26.3), producing overexposed, view-inconsistent results. Notably, the authors observe that the diffusion relighting model in MA/DR begins to act as an enhancement model, introducing finer facial and hand detail, but still cannot match D-Rex's overall quality due to the weaker driving avatar.

## Relation to Prior Work

D-Rex sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: expressive full-body avatar rendering (EVA, 3D Gaussian Splatting-based) and image-space diffusion relighting (DiffusionRenderer/Cosmos). It departs fundamentally from the dominant PBR-based relightable avatar paradigm (MeshAvatar, RANA, IntrinsicAvatar) by eliminating explicit 3D intrinsic decomposition and analytic BRDFs entirely, instead treating relighting as a learned image-to-image translation. The key architectural choice is LoRA fine-tuning of a pre-trained video diffusion relighting model on person-specific flat-lit/relit frame pairs, which inherits temporal and spatial consistency from the prior without requiring video-level paired supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By fully decoupling relighting from avatar modeling, D-Rex makes its relighting stage directly applicable to any existing white-light avatar system, lowering the barrier to adding relightability to expressive avatars. However, the method requires expensive light stage capture (40 cameras, 331 LEDs), runs at ~0.5 FPS precluding real-time use in telepresence or VR/AR, and exhibits small inter-inference drift that must be masked via linear blending. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to arbitrary humans via large-scale multi-illumination training, whether single-step flow-matching can close the real-time gap, and whether light effects absent from training data (e.g., far occlusion shadows) can be handled.
