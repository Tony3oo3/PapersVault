## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods adopt view-centric representations that predict pixel-aligned Gaussian maps for each input view, causing the same subject to be redundantly encoded across views. This inter-view redundancy inflates computation and transmission costs as view count or resolution grows, limiting scalability for real-time applications such as immersive live streaming and holographic communication where both fidelity and compactness are critical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260825/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes calibrated multi-view RGB images (typically 8 views at 512×512, with zero-shot evaluation up to 1024×1024) along with per-pixel object masks and camera intrinsics/extrinsics. It produces a single compact 3D Gaussian Splatting representation of approximately 71k Gaussians (roughly 33% of view-centric baselines) in one feed-forward pass, with the scene normalized to a 2×2×2 cube and each of M point tokens decoded into K=16 Gaussians with position offsets, scales, rotations, SH coefficients, and opacities.

## Method

PointSplat performs human-centric prediction directly in 3D space rather than per-view. It first extracts appearance tokens by patchifying images with Plücker ray embeddings and applying mask-guided top-n sampling, then estimates a coarse visual hull via space carving and voxelizes it. A DDA-based ray-casting step from anchor views selects surface points within the hull, removing redundant interior geometry and establishing explicit 2D-3D correspondences that enrich point embeddings with ray features. A Point-Image Transformer with alternating global, point-wise, and image-wise attention fuses the appearance and geometry token streams, and a final decoding head predicts per-point Gaussian parameters. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with only L1 and LPIPS photometric losses, requiring no depth or geometric supervision.

## Results

On DNA-Rendering (8 views, 512×512), PointSplat achieves 27.18 PSNR, 0.891 SSIM, and 0.071 LPIPS using only 71k Gaussians with 0.40 s inference time. On ActorsHQ under the same protocol, it reaches 27.62 PSNR, 0.887 SSIM, and 0.084 LPIPS. These are the best scores across all feed-forward baselines while using roughly 33% of the Gaussians required by view-centric methods (e.g., 71k vs. 204k for GS-LRM and 212k for DepthSplat). On synthetic benchmarks (THuman2.0, 8 views), PointSplat further attains 34.30 PSNR and 0.015 LPIPS, outperforming GPS-Gaussian (31.08 PSNR) by over 3 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointSplat outperforms all feed-forward baselines on every metric across both real-world datasets. On DNA-Rendering, it leads GS-LRM by 8.9 dB PSNR (27.18 vs. 18.25) while using only 35% of its Gaussians (71k vs. 204k), and beats DepthSplat by 3.2 dB PSNR with 3× fewer Gaussians (71k vs. 212k). Against GPS-Gaussian, which scales linearly with target views (51k×N Gaussians, 0.07×N s), PointSplat achieves 4.8 dB higher PSNR at a fixed 71k Gaussians and 0.40 s regardless of view count. On ActorsHQ, PointSplat matches GPS-Gaussian on SSIM (0.887) and LPIPS (0.084) but surpasses it by 3.8 dB PSNR (27.62 vs. 23.81). LVSM, while competitive on ActorsHQ PSNR (24.00), is 2.5× slower (1.0×N s) and produces over-smoothed, mosaic-like renderings. On synthetic THuman2.0 (8 views), PointSplat leads GPS-Gaussian by 3.2 dB PSNR (34.30 vs. 31.08) and DepthSplat by 8.0 dB (34.30 vs. 26.32), confirming consistent gains in controlled settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointSplat departs from the dominant view-centric paradigm in feed-forward 3D reconstruction (GS-LRM, DepthSplat, GPS-Gaussian, MVSplat), where pixel-aligned Gaussian maps are predicted per view and then unprojected into 3D, by instead predicting Gaussian primitives directly in 3D space anchored to a point set. The geometry proxy is obtained via classical space carving (Kutulakos & Seitz, 1999), and the fusion architecture borrows the alternating attention mechanism from VGGT (Wang et al., 2025) to integrate point-wise and image-wise tokens. The core 3DGS representation follows Kerbl et al. (2023), but the key architectural departure is the Point-Image Transformer that operates on a 3D point set rather than per-view pixel grids, eliminating the inter-view redundancy inherent in view-centric designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For immersive live streaming and holographic communication, the combination of feed-forward speed, compact representation (~33% fewer Gaussians), and robustness to varying view counts and resolutions makes this approach practically relevant for bandwidth-constrained deployment. The paper demonstrates that 3D-centric prediction is not merely an efficiency trick but also improves rendering quality, challenging the implicit assumption that per-view prediction is necessary for fidelity. Open questions include extending the method to unbounded scenes (memory constraints), constructing temporally consistent 4D representations, and whether the approach generalizes beyond human-centric subjects to arbitrary objects or scenes without mask supervision.
