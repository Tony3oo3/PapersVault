## Problem

Video diffusion models lack explicit geometric supervision during training, producing artifacts such as object deformation, spatial drift, and depth violations. Existing geometric reward signals either rely on handcrafted epipolar constraints or compute inconsistency in pixel space, where intensity noise corrupts the error metric. Additionally, no prior work has explored reward-guided test-time scaling for causal (streaming) video models, which expose a richer sequential search space than bidirectional generators.

## Input / Output

The reward model consumes a generated video clip (a sequence of N frames) and a text prompt, and outputs a scalar geometric consistency score computed as the mean pointwise reprojection error across valid point-frame pairs. For alignment, the system takes a text prompt and produces a selected or optimized video clip: in post-hoc mode it fine-tunes a bidirectional DiT on preference pairs, while in test-time scaling mode it searches over seed and temporal axes to return the highest-rewarded clip from a causal autoregressive generator without any parameter update.

## Method

VIGOR computes a geometry-based reward by leveraging VGGT to feed-forward estimate camera intrinsics, extrinsics, and dense depth maps, then back-projecting sampled 2D points to 3D world coordinates and reprojecting them into other frames to measure pointwise L2 reprojection error—decoupling geometric inconsistency from pixel intensity. A geometry-aware sampling (GAS) strategy exploits the shallow global-attention layers of VGGT to identify geometrically salient patches (top-τ% by attention score), filtering out low-texture and non-semantic regions like sky or ground. The reward is applied through two complementary pathways: (a) post-hoc alignment via SFT and Flow-DPO on a bidirectional model using the curated GB3DV-25k preference dataset (25,600 geometry-ranked video pairs), and (b) test-time scaling on a causal autoregressive model where three search strategies—Search on Start, Search on Path, and Beam Search—use the reward as a path verifier over the seed×temporal search space, achieving O(KSN) complexity without retraining.

## Results

In the Best-of-N test-time scaling setting (Table 1), the proposed Reproj-Pts reward achieves the best 3D reconstruction quality (PSNR 22.66, SSIM 0.7665, LPIPS 0.2330) and the lowest epipolar inconsistency (EPI 3.442) among all compared rewards, while also attaining the highest overall VBench score of 84.52%. In the post-hoc alignment setting (Table 2), SFT+DPO with the Reproj-Pts reward on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B further improves geometric consistency to SSIM 0.7977, LPIPS 0.1789, and EPI 2.127, while boosting subject consistency to 97.05% and imaging quality to 76.63% on VBench. For streaming (causal) video generation, all three proposed search strategies (SoS, SoP, Beam Search) exhibit consistent scaling with budget, with Beam Search achieving the strongest 3D reconstruction and SoP the best overall VBench trend.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Epipolar baseline [26] (Sampson-distance-based reward), the proposed Reproj-Pts reward wins on PSNR (22.66 vs 22.45), SSIM (0.7665 vs 0.7557), LPIPS (0.2330 vs 0.2432), and VBench total (84.52% vs 84.50%) in the TTS setting; in post-hoc alignment it further leads on SSIM (0.7977 vs 0.7973), LPIPS (0.1789 vs 0.1818), EPI (2.127 vs 2.187), and subject/background consistency. Against Reproj-Pix [11] (pixelwise warping error via VGGT), the pointwise formulation yields substantially larger gains: +1.59 PSNR, +0.040 SSIM, −0.071 LPIPS, and −1.11 EPI, confirming that decoupling geometric error from pixel intensity is critical. Both baselines substantially outperform the unmodified CausVid [50] baseline (PSNR 19.68, VBench 83.33%), validating that any geometry-aware reward improves selection. In the streaming TTS experiments, Causal-Forcing [53] serves as the unmodified baseline; all three search strategies (SoS, SoP, Beam Search) improve over it on every geometric and perceptual metric, with Beam Search achieving the largest 3D reconstruction gains at higher budgets. The Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B [41] baseline (PSNR 22.45, EPI 2.832, VBench SC 95.98%) is improved by SFT+DPO(Reproj-Pts) to PSNR 23.54, EPI 2.127, and SC 97.05%, demonstrating that the reward transfers effectively to a different model family.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VGGT as a pretrained geometric foundation model to extract camera parameters and depth maps, and on Flow-DPO for preference alignment of rectified flow video generators. It departs from prior geometric reward approaches (Epipolar-DPO, VideoGPA) by replacing pixel-space or epipolar-distance metrics with a pointwise cross-frame reprojection error computed in 3D, coupled with a geometry-aware sampling strategy that leverages VGGT's shallow attention layers to focus on semantically meaningful regions. A key conceptual extension is applying reward-guided test-time scaling to causal autoregressive (streaming) video models, which the authors note has not been previously explored, enabling structured search over the temporal generation axis rather than simple Best-of-N selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIGOR offers a practical, training-free pathway (TTS) to improve geometric consistency in open-source streaming video generators without retraining, which is valuable given the compute constraints of the community. The pointwise reprojection formulation and geometry-aware sampling provide a more physically grounded and robust reward than pixel-intensity-based alternatives, and the GB3DV-25k preference dataset enables reproducible post-hoc alignment experiments. Open questions include whether the reward generalizes to longer-horizon or interactive generation, how the TTS search strategies scale beyond 16-frame clips, and whether the DD collapse under DPO can be addressed more elegantly than with ad-hoc auxiliary losses.
