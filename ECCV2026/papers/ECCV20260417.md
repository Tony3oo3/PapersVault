## Problem

VLM-based zero-shot anomaly detection methods learn prompts in unconstrained text space or condition them on coarse global image features, providing little fine-grained regularization across domains. This leads to semantic drift under domain shifts and prevents the discovery of reusable normal/anomalous visual primitives, limiting cross-category generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260417/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 518×518 and processes it through a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14@336 backbone, extracting multi-layer patch embeddings from selected intermediate layers. It produces two outputs: a scalar image-level anomaly score (combining global and pooled patch evidence) and a pixel-level anomaly map of shape H×W with values in [0,1], obtained by fusing multi-layer patch logit margins via a temperature-controlled smooth maximum and upsampling. At test time, no target-category images are required; the model generalizes zero-shot to unseen industrial and medical categories.

## Method

VarProtoAD grounds prompt learning on fine-grained visual prototypes by fitting a variational von Mises-Fisher (vMF) mixture to ℓ2-normalized multi-layer patch tokens, yielding layer-wise normal (96) and abnormal (24) prototype banks on the unit hypersphere that align naturally with cosine-based VLM matching. Learnable context tokens are then conditioned on these prototypes via a confidence-gated cross-attention mechanism: queries from context tokens attend to projected prototype keys, and a token-wise gate derived from the maximum attention probability suppresses noisy updates when attention is diffuse. The abnormal prompt set combines explicit prompts conditioned on abnormal prototypes with implicit deviation prompts derived by transforming normal prototypes, broadening coverage of open-ended defect semantics. Multi-layer patch logit margins are fused with a temperature-controlled smooth maximum to produce both pixel-level anomaly maps and image-level scores, and the full model is trained on auxiliary data with image-level cross-entropy, pixel-level BCE+Dice, and a prototype loss (vMF reconstruction plus separation and orthogonality regularizers).

## Results

On 15 industrial and medical benchmarks, VarProtoAD achieves the best overall image-level detection performance with 95.6% AUROC and 96.3% AP, outperforming the strongest baseline Bayes-PFL (94.7% AUROC / 93.3% AP) by +0.9% AUROC and +3.0% AP. For pixel-level localization, it reaches 94.8% pixel AUROC and 87.2% PRO, improving over Bayes-PFL by +2.0% AUROC and +2.6% PRO. Gains are particularly pronounced in the medical domain, where pixel-level AUROC rises from 87.7% (Bayes-PFL) to 92.2% and PRO from 76.2% to 81.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VarProtoAD outperforms all five baselines on both image-level and pixel-level metrics across the 15-dataset benchmark. Against the closest competitor Bayes-PFL, it gains +0.9% AUROC and +3.0% AP on image-level detection, and +2.0% AUROC and +2.6% PRO on pixel-level localization; the gap widens substantially in the medical domain (e.g., ColonDB pixel AUROC 88.3% vs. 82.1%, Endo 94.6% vs. 89.2%). Compared to AnomalyCLIP (the direct prompt-learning predecessor), VarProtoAD improves overall image-level AUROC from 93.1% to 95.6% and pixel-level PRO from 79.0% to 87.2%. AdaCLIP, which conditions prompts on global image features, trails by 2.3% AUROC (image-level) and 5.9% AUROC (pixel-level), confirming that fine-grained prototype conditioning is more effective than coarse global-feature conditioning. WinCLIP and APRIL-GAN, which rely on handcrafted or ensembled prompts without learned context tokens, are outperformed by 10+ points in AUROC on most datasets, underscoring the benefit of learned, prototype-anchored prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

VarProtoAD extends the prompt-learning paradigm for zero-shot anomaly detection established by AnomalyCLIP (object-agnostic context tokens optimized in text space) and AdaCLIP (instance-conditioned prompts driven by global image features). The key conceptual departure is replacing coarse global-feature conditioning with fine-grained, patch-level visual prototypes learned via a variational von Mises-Fisher mixture on the ℓ₂-normalized hypersphere, which aligns geometrically with the cosine-similarity matching used by CLIP-style VLMs. This positions the work at the intersection of hyperspherical clustering, variational inference, and conditional prompt learning (in the CoCoOp lineage), but applies it specifically to anchor prompt updates in reusable local visual primitives rather than in unconstrained text space or single global descriptors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical limitation in VLM-based ZSAD—semantic drift of prompts under cross-domain shifts—by grounding prompt learning in transferable, layer-wise visual anchors, yielding consistent gains across 15 industrial and medical benchmarks with particularly notable improvements in medical localization (e.g., ColonDB pixel AUROC 82.1→88.3%). Open questions include whether the vMF prototype mechanism generalizes beyond CLIP to other VLM backbones, whether the number of prototypes and layer selection can be adapted automatically per domain, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D anomaly detection where patch geometry differs. The reliance on an auxiliary labeled dataset (MVTec-AD or ClinicDB) for prototype learning also limits applicability in truly label-free cold-start scenarios.
