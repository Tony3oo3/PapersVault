## Problem

Existing unsupervised adversarial fine-tuning methods for CLIP vision encoders optimize representations only at a single fixed perturbation endpoint, providing minimal constraint within the broader local input region. This endpoint-only paradigm yields weak robustness generalization across perturbation magnitudes and causes severe semantic drift in downstream LVLM outputs (e.g., hallucinated or incorrect captions) when attack strength deviates from the training budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261919/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224, matching CLIP ViT-L/14) and requires no image-text pairs or labels. It produces a fine-tuned ViT-L/14 vision encoder whose output is a d-dimensional ℓ₂-normalized embedding in the shared CLIP space, designed to remain stable under ℓ∞-bounded perturbations up to 8/255. The adversarial direction δ(x) is generated once per training step via 10-step PGD and reused to construct all intermediate path samples without additional optimization.

## Method

PACO regularizes visual embeddings along a continuous local path parameterized as x_λ = x + λδ(x), where δ(x) is the PGD-optimal perturbation direction. Stage I (anchor establishment) minimizes a cosine-similarity loss between the trainable encoder's adversarial-endpoint embedding and the frozen pre-trained encoder's clean embedding, preventing catastrophic semantic drift. Stage II (progressive path regularization) introduces a path-consistent loss that aligns intermediate embeddings f_θ(x_λ) to a stop-gradient linear interpolation target z_λ between the clean and anchored adversarial features, enforcing near-linear feature evolution. A time-dependent schedule progressively increases λ from 0 to λ_max=2.0, exposing the encoder to increasing perturbation intensities. A theoretical bound (Theorem 1) shows the two-stage design jointly controls the finite-scale directional Lipschitz constant, with the first term governed by endpoint alignment and the second by path deviation from the interpolation target.

## Results

On LLaVA-7B under untargeted APGD-Ensemble attacks, PACO achieves the best average CIDEr of 87.5 (clean), 50.3 (ε=2/255), 35.3 (ε=4/255), and 16.0 (ε=8/255) for image captioning, and 49.9%, 30.1%, 20.9%, and 11.7% accuracy for VQA, outperforming the strongest baseline Sim-CLIP by +6.3 CIDEr at ε=2/255 and +1.7 points on VQA. On zero-shot classification across 13 datasets, PACO attains 61.1% clean accuracy and 45.8% at ε=2/255, exceeding Sim-CLIP by +2.4 and +4.1 points respectively. Under targeted attacks at ε=8/255, PACO maintains 0% attack success rate with 80.1 average CIDEr, while all baselines show non-zero ASR (1.6–4.8%) and lower caption quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PACO consistently outperforms all three robust baselines (TeCoA, FARE, Sim-CLIP) across every evaluation setting. On image captioning, PACO leads Sim-CLIP (the strongest baseline) by +3.5 CIDEr on clean images and by +6.3, +4.8, and +1.5 CIDEr at ε=2/255, 4/255, and 8/255 respectively; the gap over FARE and TeCoA is even larger (e.g., +10.0 CIDEr over TeCoA at ε=2/255). On VQA, PACO achieves 30.1% at ε=2/255 versus 28.4% for Sim-CLIP and 26.4% for FARE. On zero-shot classification, PACO's 61.1% clean accuracy exceeds Sim-CLIP (58.7%) by 2.4 points and TeCoA (51.8%) by 9.3 points, while its robust accuracy at ε=4/255 (31.7%) surpasses Sim-CLIP (28.9%) by 2.8 points. Under targeted attacks at ε=8/255, PACO is the only method achieving 0% ASR while also producing the highest CIDEr (80.1 vs. 70.8 for Sim-CLIP), indicating superior defense against both attack success and quality degradation. Vanilla CLIP, while achieving the highest clean scores (99.2 CIDEr, 67.7% zero-shot), collapses to near-zero under any perturbation, confirming that robustness and clean performance are in tension without PACO's path regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

PACO extends the unsupervised adversarial fine-tuning (U-AFT) paradigm exemplified by FARE and Sim-CLIP, which align adversarial embeddings to a frozen clean reference via a single endpoint loss. The key departure is the introduction of path-consistent regularization: rather than constraining only the clean-to-adversarial endpoint, PACO parameterizes a continuous local path along the PGD direction and enforces near-linear feature evolution at intermediate points, drawing on local-complexity theory from the ReLU-network literature. The two-stage schedule (anchor first, then progressive path regularization with a dynamic λ schedule) is a practical training strategy not present in prior U-AFT methods, which optimize a single fixed-perturbation objective throughout training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PACO offers a label-free, model-agnostic recipe for hardening CLIP vision encoders inside LVLMs, addressing a practical gap where prior U-AFT methods trade clean accuracy for robustness and fail to generalize across perturbation budgets. The path-regularization mechanism is lightweight (one extra forward/backward pass per step) and integrates seamlessly into existing LLaVA pipelines. Limitations include evaluation restricted to CLIP ViT-L/14 trained on ImageNet only, and the POPE hallucination results still show a notable gap to vanilla CLIP, suggesting that robustness-oriented fine-tuning inherently suppresses fine-grained visual cues. Open questions include whether the path-regularization idea transfers to other encoder architectures (e.g., SigLIP, DINOv2), to text-modality attacks, and whether the two-stage schedule can be replaced by a single continuous objective.
