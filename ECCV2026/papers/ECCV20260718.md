## Problem

Existing text-to-CAD generation methods either quantize continuous geometric parameters into discrete tokens (command-sequence approaches) or incur high token costs (code-based approaches), both of which introduce or obscure small dimensional errors that violate industrial tolerance requirements. Compounding this, standard evaluation metrics like Chamfer Distance and IoU measure shape-level similarity and are insensitive to the precise numerical deviations that matter in precision-critical engineering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260718/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt specifying a CAD model with explicit dimensions (e.g., 'a cylinder with a 5mm radius') and produces a parametric B-rep model at true metric scale, along with a structured design plan where all geometric parameters are written symbolically in angle brackets, typed as length (L) or angle (A), and bound to explicit units (meters, degrees, millimeters). Generation proceeds step-wise: at each step the model emits one plan followed by one command sequence (sketch-extrude pair, chamfer, or fillet), with parameters retrieved from a dictionary rather than predicted as tokens.

## Method

Pointer-CAD v2 introduces a Plan-Then-Construct framework that decouples parameter reasoning from geometric construction. In the plan stage, the LLM generates a structured, dimension-aware design plan with all parameters in symbolic, non-quantized form. In the construction stage, a pointer mechanism retrieves the correct parameter from a dictionary via cosine similarity between a predicted embedding and encoded parameter embeddings, eliminating discretization error entirely. Parameters are encoded through sign-preserving logarithmic normalization, Fourier frequency features, a gated MLP projection, and RoPE positional encoding. The system is built on Qwen2.5 (0.5B/1.5B) and trained supervised on the newly constructed OmniCAD-Plan (202K) and OmniCAD-Plan+ (209K) datasets, which add plan-level annotations to Recap-OmniCAD. The paper also introduces three hierarchical geometry accuracy metrics (Vertex, Edge, Face) that enforce strict parametric consistency within a tolerance scaled to the bounding box, replacing shape-level metrics for evaluating dimensional fidelity.

## Results

On OmniCAD-Plan, Pointer-CAD v2 (1.5B) achieves 91.25% RMR@3, 92.76% Vertex Accuracy, 90.33% Edge Accuracy, and 89.18% Face Accuracy, outperforming CADmium-1.5B (81.15/79.36/78.25/74.19) and Pointer-CAD-1.5B (72.10/64.67/62.35/61.69) across all three geometric levels. On the more complex OmniCAD-Plan+ dataset, the 1.5B model maintains 90.97% RMR@3 and 92.60% Vertex Accuracy while baselines degrade substantially (CADmium drops to 43.41% RMR@3). Against general-purpose LLMs (Qwen3-235B, Claude Opus 4.5, GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Pro), Pointer-CAD v2-1.5B improves over the best LLM (Gemini 3 Pro) by 21.30% on OmniCAD-Plan and 21.54% on OmniCAD-Plan+ averaged over the three-level metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the command-sequence baseline Pointer-CAD, Pointer-CAD v2-1.5B improves RMR@3 from 72.10% to 91.25% (+19.15%) and Face Accuracy from 61.69% to 89.18% (+27.49%) on OmniCAD-Plan, with the largest gap on Edge Arc accuracy (62.40% → 94.23%). Against the code-based baseline CADmium, the improvement is also substantial: RMR@3 rises from 81.15% to 91.25% and Face Accuracy from 74.19% to 89.18%, with CADmium's Edge Arc accuracy near zero (5.61%) versus 68.53% for Pointer-CAD v2. On the harder OmniCAD-Plan+ dataset (with chamfer/fillet operations), CADmium degrades severely (RMR@3 drops to 43.41%) while Pointer-CAD v2 retains 90.97%, highlighting robustness to increased geometric complexity. Against four general-purpose LLMs (Qwen3-235B-A22B, Claude Opus 4.5, GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Pro) prompted to generate CadQuery code, Pointer-CAD v2-1.5B outperforms all of them by 16–35 points on RMR@3 and 20–30 points on Face Accuracy, with Gemini 3 Pro being the strongest LLM baseline (74.65% RMR@3 vs. 90.89% for Ours-1.5B on OmniCAD-Plan). Under conventional shape-level metrics (F1, Chamfer Distance), gains are more modest (e.g., Arc F1 improves from 51.00 to 63.59; Mean CD from 3.69 to 3.15), confirming that the proposed parametric accuracy metrics are more sensitive to the dimensional precision improvements that shape-level metrics miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Pointer-CAD (v1), which introduced a pointer mechanism for explicit geometric entity referencing in command-sequence CAD generation, by decoupling parameter reasoning from geometric construction through a Plan-Then-Construct paradigm. It departs from the quantized numerical token space used across command-sequence methods (DeepCAD, CAD-MLLM) and addresses the token inefficiency of code-based approaches (CADmium, CADQuery) by using a pointer-based retrieval mechanism over a continuous parameter dictionary. The planning stage draws on the broader planning-based CAD generation line (FreeCAD, CAD-Assistant, Seek-CAD) but uniquely establishes an explicit, unit-grounded linkage between plan outputs and metric-scale parameters that prior planning methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in industrial CAD generation where small dimensional deviations (negligible at the shape level) violate production tolerances, and demonstrates that widely used shape-level metrics (CD, IoU) are fundamentally insensitive to such errors. The proposed hierarchical Vertex/Edge/Face accuracy metrics and the OmniCAD-Plan dataset with structured plan annotations provide the community with tools to evaluate parametric fidelity beyond visual similarity. Limitations include occasional failures on very fine features (small holes, thin cylinders), the added computational cost of the plan stage, and evaluation restricted to sketch-extrude, chamfer, and fillet operations. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-body assemblies, more complex boolean operations, and whether the plan stage can be made more efficient without sacrificing precision.
