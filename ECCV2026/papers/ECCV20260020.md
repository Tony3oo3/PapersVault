## Problem

Autoregressive video diffusion models lack robust long-term memory: they fail to preserve object identity when objects reappear after prolonged occlusion (object permanence) and cannot effectively retrieve information from distant history (memory capacity). The paper identifies the root cause as a training-inference misalignment—standard linear training data rarely presents objects exiting and reappearing within a short window, so models learn myopic transition probabilities rather than long-range retrieval—compounded by the computational trade-off between sequence length and receptive field that limits how much history can be attended to.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and a compressed representation of historical video frames as conditioning, operating at 480×832 resolution and 15 FPS. The history is not fed as raw frames but is spatiotemporally downsampled under a fixed token budget, with the compression ratio dynamically switching based on the diffusion timestep (temporally dense/spatially coarse at high noise, spatially fine/temporally sparse at low noise). The output is a minutes-long autoregressive video (demonstrated up to 60+ seconds), generated frame-by-frame with the compressed history providing a comprehensive receptive field over the entire prior duration.

## Method

Ring Forcing introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, a ring-structured training strategy concatenates each video with its reversed counterpart so that the ground-truth target clip is embedded as a distant subsequence in the conditioning history, turning long-range retrieval into a necessity for minimizing training loss; a random head crop prevents boundary leakage and a Bernoulli context-drop balances strict history adherence with open-ended generative diversity. Second, a timestep-composed compression operator dynamically selects between a temporally dense (global structure) and a spatially detailed (local texture) view of history depending on the noise level, with a cyclic temporal shift ensuring full coverage over long histories under a fixed sequence-length budget. Third, a sparse RoPE mechanism maps compressed history tokens back to their original continuous spatiotemporal coordinates rather than discrete latent indices, preserving the pretrained model's relative-position priors under variable compression. The entire framework is trained with a rectified-flow velocity-matching objective and fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 128) on Wan2.1/Wan2.2 backbones, keeping inference cost virtually identical to the base model.

## Results

On the A-D-R Benchmark, Ring Forcing achieves 0.723 Texture / 0.839 Geometry / 0.823 Semantic consistency at a 1-second disappearance gap, compared to 0.181/0.297/0.707 (LongLive), 0.236/0.368/0.712 (LongCat), and 0.252/0.287/0.692 (FramePack). At a 5-second gap, Ring Forcing maintains 0.515/0.761/0.831 while all baselines drop below 0.30 on Texture and Geometry. For gaps of 15 s, 30 s, and 60 s, only Ring Forcing is evaluated because the 'appear' segment falls outside the maximum context window of all baselines. On the General 60-second benchmark, Ring Forcing scores 5.822 Aesthetic, 2.261 Dynamics, and 4.22 Overall Quality (human eval), outperforming all three baselines on every metric except Motion Smoothness (0.986 vs. 0.991).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ring Forcing decisively outperforms all three baselines on every consistency metric (Texture, Geometry, Semantic) at every gap duration where baselines are evaluated. The advantage is most dramatic at the 1-second gap, where Ring Forcing's Texture score of 0.723 exceeds LongLive (0.181), LongCat (0.236), and FramePack (0.252) by 2.9×–4.0×, indicating that baselines suffer catastrophic identity loss even after a brief occlusion. At 5 seconds, Ring Forcing still leads by a wide margin (0.515 vs. ≤0.290 Texture), while for gaps of 15 s, 30 s, and 60 s the baselines are entirely excluded because the appear segment falls outside their maximum context window. On the General 60-second benchmark, Ring Forcing wins on Aesthetic (5.822 vs. 5.622–5.808), Dynamics (2.261 vs. 1.056–2.196), Naturalness (3.922 vs. 3.031–3.875), Instruction Following (25.471 vs. 24.805–25.341), and human-rated Overall Quality (4.22 vs. 2.25–4.19), while trailing only marginally on Motion Smoothness (0.986 vs. 0.991). Notably, LongLive's Dynamics score (1.056) is roughly half that of Ring Forcing, reflecting its tendency to degenerate into static or repetitive outputs due to over-reliance on an initial-frame attention sink.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ring Forcing builds directly on the Wan autoregressive video diffusion backbone (Wan2.1/2.2) and positions itself against two lines of long-video work: retrieval-based context management (Context-as-Memory, Deep Forcing, WorldMem) and compression-based history packing (FramePack, WorldPack, TinyHistory). It departs from these by introducing an explicit training objective—ring-structured sequences that embed the ground-truth target into distant history—rather than relying solely on architectural or inference-time mechanisms to surface relevant context. The timestep-composed compression and sparse RoPE are novel engineering contributions that complement prior compression strategies by exploiting the diffusion model's noise-level-dependent behavior and preserving pretrained spatiotemporal priors under variable downsampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and previously under-quantified failure mode in long video generation (object identity loss after occlusion) and provides both a diagnostic explanation (myopic attention from linear training data) and a practical remedy. The A-D-R benchmark is a useful, reproducible evaluation protocol that the field can adopt to measure memory retrieval independently of general video quality. Open questions remain: performance degrades noticeably at 60-second gaps (texture score drops from 0.74 to 0.40), the method is validated only on single-shot videos, and it is tied to the Wan architecture—whether the ring-structured training and timestep composition transfer to other backbones or multi-shot narrative settings is untested.
