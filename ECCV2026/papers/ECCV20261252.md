## Problem

Existing weakly supervised referring expression comprehension (WREC) methods operate exclusively on flat, unary anchor representations, where any relational or contextual cues are absorbed back into individual anchor embeddings. This structurally flat visual space cannot explicitly represent the compositional and relational nature of referring expressions (e.g., 'the chair with a bag on it'), causing inter-object dependencies to become entangled with appearance cues and limiting grounding accuracy on complex descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261252/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a natural language referring expression (max 15 tokens, GloVe-initialized) as input. Visual features are extracted from a frozen YOLOv3 backbone (top K=17 anchors by objectness score) and enriched with DINOv2 and Depth Anything v2 features via a dynamic routing mechanism. The output is a single bounding box decoded by the detection head, selected by combining unary object compatibility and pairwise relational confidence scores (weighted with λ=0.6).

## Method

SVCR explicitly constructs a structured visual representation space with two complementary components: (i) Unary Object Representations (UOR, K×D) that project enriched anchors onto N learnable semantic basis vectors and fuse the result via gated integration to enhance instance-level discriminability, and (ii) Pairwise Relational Representations (PRR, K×K×D) that model each anchor pair using concatenated semantic features and an 8-dimensional geometric descriptor (relative position, scale, IoU, distance, orientation), modulated by a learnable gating weight. A compositional alignment mechanism then matches UOR against subject noun-chunk embeddings and PRR against full-sentence embeddings, each optimized with InfoNCE contrastive loss under weak supervision. A hierarchical consistency loss encourages relational confidence to concentrate on top unary-ranked anchors, and a diversity regularization (Frobenius-norm orthogonality constraint) stabilizes the learned semantic bases. At inference, the final grounding score is a weighted sum of symmetric pairwise similarity and unary similarity per anchor.

## Results

On the standard YOLOv3 backbone, SVCR achieves 71.06% / 72.60% / 65.55% IoU@0.5 on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, 54.17% / 60.22% / 44.63% on RefCOCO+, and 56.99% on RefCOCOg val, setting new state-of-the-art among weakly supervised methods. With a stronger YOLOv5 backbone (SVCR*), performance rises to 74.51% / 76.93% / 70.46% on RefCOCO and 60.40% on RefCOCOg val, narrowing the gap to fully supervised methods (e.g., TCRT at 91.07% on RefCOCO val) to under 17 points. The metric used throughout is IoU@0.5 (predicted box IoU > 0.5 with ground truth).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVCR outperforms the strongest prior WREC method DViN [3] by +3.39% on RefCOCO val, +6.61% on RefCOCO testB, +2.70% on RefCOCO+ testA, and +1.95% on RefCOCOg val, with the largest gap on the harder testB split. Against WeakMCN [4], a multi-task WREC/WRES method, SVCR gains +2.72% and +2.93% on RefCOCO testA/testB, +2.27% and +2.89% on RefCOCO+ val/testA, and +2.37% on RefCOCOg val. Earlier WREC baselines (RefCLIP [10], APL [17], DTWREG [25], IGN [42], ARN [14]) are surpassed by 10–39 points across splits, confirming that the structured representation paradigm yields substantial gains over flat anchor matching. Even with the stronger YOLOv5 backbone, SVCR* still trails the best fully supervised method TCRT [2] by roughly 16–17 points on RefCOCO val, but narrows the gap to under 12 points on RefCOCO+ testA (64.15% vs. 90.76%) and under 27 points on RefCOCOg val (60.40% vs. 86.81%), demonstrating that structured compositional alignment recovers a meaningful fraction of the supervision gap without any box-level annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the anchor-based WREC pipeline established by RefCLIP [10] and the foundation-model feature enrichment strategies from DViN [3] and WeakMCN [4], but departs fundamentally from their shared assumption that all visual semantics—relational, contextual, and attribute-based—can be collapsed into flat unary anchor embeddings. Prior relation-aware methods (e.g., [15]) use message passing to enrich anchors but ultimately treat the result as a single unary vector for contrastive matching; SVCR instead preserves pairwise relational structure as an independent representation unit and introduces a two-granularity compositional alignment (unary↔subject-chunk, pairwise↔full-sentence) that has no direct precedent in the WREC literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly separating unary and pairwise visual representations, the paper offers a principled solution to the long-standing difficulty of grounding relational expressions under weak supervision, and the modular design (UOR, PRR, compositional alignment) can be grafted onto other WREC methods as shown with WeakMCN. Limitations remain: the framework still depends on a frozen YOLOv3/v5 detector for anchor proposals, struggles with negation and heavy visual ambiguity, and has not been extended to pixel-level tasks like segmentation. Open questions include whether the structured representation generalizes to transformer-based backbones, whether the pairwise component scales efficiently to denser anchor sets, and how to incorporate explicit negation or exclusion reasoning.
