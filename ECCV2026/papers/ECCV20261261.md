## Problem

Egocentric video understanding tasks such as video QA, online narration, and activity anticipation have been studied in isolation with dedicated benchmarks, but no unified framework exists to evaluate whether vision-language models can reason about past, present, and future events under the streaming constraint of partial observability. Moreover, prior benchmarks do not assess whether model confidence tracks the inherent answerability of a query, leaving 'confidently wrong' behaviors undetected.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261261/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a sequential stream of egocentric video clips (Ego4D sessions of ~230–300 s) under a strict online prefix constraint: at query time t, only clips x₁…xₜ are accessible, with no look-ahead. Queries are multiple-choice (4 options: ground truth, two temporally-adjacent hard negatives, one semantically-absurd negative, all shuffled) posed at arbitrary timestamps, and the model must output a letter answer, a confidence score extracted from the next-token probability mass over the option set, and an interaction-visibility state label. The benchmark spans 1,997 videos (165 h) and ~4,800 QA pairs across 56 scenarios in six task settings.

## Method

EgoSAT introduces a unified streaming formulation that maps retrospective, present, and prospective queries into a single partial-observability framework, and quantifies answerability through two parameter-free metrics: Surprise (cross-modal CLIP visual/semantic shift between a context window and the imminent future, fused via empirical distribution transform and probit) and Branchiness (entropy × Rao's quadratic entropy of the conditional successor-interaction distribution). Confidence is measured by probing the model's next-token distribution over the forced MCQ letter set, enabling predictability-aware calibration diagnostics. For streaming evaluation, the authors propose ROI-TimeChat-Online, a training-free variant that prioritizes KV-cache tokens within hand- and gaze-centered interaction regions during dynamic token dropping, and apply lightweight LoRA SFT to align output format. The benchmark is built on Ego4D with quality filtering via rejection tags, and MCQ validity is verified through blind-model and distractor analyses confirming that options alone do not leak the answer.

## Results

On EgoSAT, humans achieve 70.63% accuracy on short-horizon anticipation and 76.25% on short-horizon retrieval, substantially outperforming all evaluated models. Among proprietary models, Gemini 2.5 Pro leads on present narration (69.63% recall, 89.02% precision) but trails Qwen2.5-VL-72B on short-horizon anticipation (29.92% vs. 43.06%) and short-horizon retrieval (39.80% vs. 51.87%). Online streaming models (e.g., TimeChat-Online-7B) consistently underperform offline counterparts of similar scale, with state-switch accuracy near 0% across all online models. After lightweight LoRA SFT, TimeChat-Online-7B jumps to 61.63% on short-horizon anticipation and 42.79% on multi-step anticipation, yet still falls well short of the human baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Proprietary models (Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4) dominate present narration recall/precision but are outperformed by Qwen2.5-VL-72B on short-horizon anticipation (29.92% vs. 43.06%) and short-horizon retrieval (39.80% vs. 51.87%), suggesting that open-weight models with strong temporal grounding can surpass proprietary systems on egocentric temporal tasks. All online streaming models (TimeChat-Online, Flash-VStream, LLaVA-OV1.5) trail their offline counterparts by 5–20 points on anticipation and retrieval, with state-switch accuracy collapsing to near 0%, confirming that irreversible KV-cache compression severely degrades streaming performance. The ROI-augmented variant (ROI-TimeChat-Online) improves present narration by ~3% over vanilla TimeChat-Online but degrades prospective and retrospective tasks, indicating that hand/gaze-centric token selection omits background context needed for temporal reasoning. After LoRA SFT, TimeChat-Online-7B achieves 61.63% on short-horizon anticipation—nearly doubling its zero-shot score—yet remains 9 points below the human baseline, and its confidence remains poorly calibrated on unpredictable queries (conf_wrong ≈ 45–53% vs. conf_correct ≈ 53–59%), revealing persistent miscalibration even after task alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously studied-in-silo tasks—video question answering, online video narration, and activity anticipation—into a single streaming formulation where frames arrive sequentially and models must reason about past, present, and future under partial observability. Methodologically, it builds on TimeChat-Online's dynamic KV-cache dropping for streaming inference, extending it with a training-free ROI-aware token selection module that prioritizes hand- and gaze-centric regions. For confidence evaluation, it adapts the MCQ next-token probability scoring framework of Tsvilodub et al. to the streaming setting, and introduces a principled answerability quantification (surprise + branchiness) that has no direct precedent in prior streaming benchmarks such as OVO-Bench, StreamingBench, or IPIBench, which focus on accuracy under simplified streaming conditions without modeling inherent unpredictability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoSAT provides the first systematic testbed for evaluating whether VLMs can serve as real-time egocentric assistants that reason across temporal horizons, and its answerability-aware diagnostics expose a critical gap: models are frequently 'confidently wrong,' with self-reported confidence failing to track whether a query is even answerable given the observed prefix. This finding has direct safety implications for deployed streaming assistants that must decide when to act versus abstain. Open questions include how to design streaming architectures with adaptive memory that avoids the irreversible information loss observed in KV-cache-based online models, and whether lightweight SFT can close the remaining gap to human-level temporal reasoning without sacrificing generalization across scenarios.
