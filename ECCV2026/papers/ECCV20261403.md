## Problem

Predictive Coding (PC) resolves the update-locking problem of backpropagation through local weight updates, but routing error signals between layers still requires an autograd Jacobian-transpose (J^T) product—the last non-local operation in the framework. Prior weight-feedback methods (e.g., Feedback Alignment) replaced J^T with fixed random matrices and failed on deep CNNs, leading to the assumption that exact error transport without a global backward pass is inherently impossible. This paper shows that for the standard Act(Norm(L(x))) layer class, the exact J^T factors into three locally available terms, making the autograd dependency avoidable.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and produces a class label via cross-entropy loss. Unlike single-pass backpropagation, each training step involves a forward pass that caches pre-activations and per-channel normalization gains, followed by T=20 iterative Gauss-Seidel sweeps over the error variables before a local weight update. The output error is initialized as the difference between the one-hot target and the softmax of the top-layer output, then clipped to a fixed norm before the inner loop begins.

## Method

The core contribution is Observation 1: for any layer f(x) = Act(Norm(L(x))) with frozen normalization statistics, the exact Jacobian transpose factors as J^T v = L^T(s ⊙ σ'(pre-act) ⊙ v), where L^T is the symmetric synapse (transposed convolution or weight matrix), σ' is the pointwise activation derivative, and s = γ/σ_run is the per-channel normalization gain. Substituting this identity into the PC error-transport step yields WF-Act-PC, which computes all inter-layer error routing from locally cached quantities with no autograd VJP in the inner loop. The algorithm adopts the error-based reparametrization and top-to-bottom Gauss-Seidel sweep from ePC, optimizes errors with RMSProp over T iterations, and applies a soft spectral-norm clipping (rank-1 correction via power iteration) on the slow learning timescale for stability. On CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet, WF-Act-PC is the only PC method whose accuracy improves monotonically with depth, surpassing iPC by up to 22.3 pp and reaching BP-level accuracy on deep architectures.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (50 epochs, 5 seeds), WF-Act-PC achieves 88.25% (VGG-5), 92.15% (VGG-7), 93.57% (VGG-9), and 92.76% (ResNet-18) top-1 accuracy, improving monotonically with depth—the only PC method to do so. On CIFAR-100, it scales from 65.64% (VGG-5) to 71.07% (ResNet-18), while iPC collapses from 56.07% to 29.45%. On Tiny-ImageNet (200 classes), WF-Act-PC reaches 61.1% (ResNet-18) vs. 46.0% (VGG-5), exceeding per-architecture-tuned BP by +1.9 pp at the deeper depth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against iPC (the strongest classical PC baseline), WF-Act-PC wins on every architecture, with the margin growing from +2.7 pp (VGG-5/CIFAR-10) to +22.3 pp (ResNet-18/CIFAR-10) and from +9.6 pp (VGG-5/CIFAR-100) to +41.6 pp (ResNet-18/CIFAR-100). Against DPC-CN and ePC, WF-Act-PC is the only PC method whose accuracy increases with depth; both competitors degrade on deeper VGGs (e.g., ePC drops from 88.27% to 86.81% on CIFAR-10 VGG-5→9, while WF-Act-PC rises from 88.25% to 93.57%). Against BP (tuned) under a controlled same-recipe comparison, WF-Act-PC exceeds BP by +0.8/+1.1/+1.2 pp on CIFAR-10 VGG-7/9/ResNet-18 and by +1.6/+0.9 pp on CIFAR-100 VGG-5/ResNet-18, but trails tuned BP by 0.24 pp on CIFAR-100 VGG-7 and 0.84 pp on CIFAR-100 VGG-9. The benchmark's own BP references (BP-CE, BP-SE), which use a stronger per-architecture recipe, exceed WF-Act-PC on ResNet-18 for both datasets (92.83/93.21% vs. 92.76% on CIFAR-10). On Tiny-ImageNet, WF-Act-PC beats per-architecture-tuned BP at both depths (+1.0 pp VGG-5, +1.9 pp ResNet-18).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads that each held half the solution: Feedback Alignment (FA) showed that W^T approximates J^T for simple σ(Wx) layers but lacked the normalization structure of modern networks, while Predictive Coding (PC) routinely froze BatchNorm statistics during inner-loop inference without examining the Jacobian consequence. Building on the error-based formulation and Gauss-Seidel sweep of ePC [7] and the local weight-update framework of iPC [22], the paper's core contribution is Observation 1: for Act(Norm(L(x))) layers with frozen normalization, the exact J^T factors into three locally available terms (L^T, σ′, and the normalization gain s), a result that is elementary chain rule but was overlooked because neither community connected frozen statistics to Jacobian structure. The method departs from prior PC by replacing the autograd VJP in error transport with this closed-form local formula, and from FA by showing that the 'approximation' was actually a missing correction rather than a fundamental limitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is significant for the bio-plausible learning community because it removes the last non-local operation (autograd J^T) from predictive coding error transport, and empirically demonstrates for the first time that a PC method can show positive depth scaling—accuracy improving monotonically from VGG-5 to ResNet-18—where all prior PC variants degrade. However, the method retains the weight-symmetry assumption shared by all PC, requires a non-synapse-local spectral norm control (albeit on a slow timescale), and incurs ~3× wall-clock overhead from the iterative inner loop. Open questions include whether the approach extends to attention-based architectures (via frozen LayerNorm), scales to ImageNet-1K and deeper ResNets, and whether learned approximate feedback matrices can replace the symmetric-synapse assumption to close the remaining gap to fully-tuned backpropagation on the hardest configurations.
