## Problem

Open-source Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) remain unreliable on multi-image tasks, consistently scoring well below commercial models on benchmarks like MMIU. The paper identifies the underlying mechanism—attention fragmentation—where recurring background attention sinks, amplified by causal masking's one-way access, divert attention from task-relevant cues and produce order sensitivity and recency bias. Prior post-hoc fixes (proportional redistribution, uniform bidirectional interpolation) fail to address the root cause because they preserve the fragmented distribution or open all links indiscriminately.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a single interleaved causal sequence of visual patch tokens (from a ViT grid) and text instruction tokens, producing a standard text response. AR is a training-free, inference-time edit applied to the pre-softmax attention score matrix at each decoder layer: it modifies only the visual-visual submatrix while preserving text autoregression. The routing prior is extracted from the first decoder layer's text-to-vision attention weights, and the sparse set of unmasked cross-image links is determined by mean-thresholding global relevance scores over a valid (non-sink) visual token pool.

## Method

Attention Remasking (AR) is a lightweight, training-free edit to the attention score matrix with three coupled operations. First, it identifies visual sink tokens via a dimension-based massive-activation criterion and masks their columns to −∞, reclaiming the trapped attention budget. Second, it leverages the LMM's own first-layer text-to-vision attention—uncorrupted by deep-layer fragmentation—to compute a global relevance score per visual token, then mean-thresholds over the non-sink pool to isolate a sparse set of task-relevant tokens; for each query, it selectively relaxes the causal mask to open forward links only to relevant tokens in subsequent images. Third, it explicitly routes the freed sink attention mass to these newly unmasked links via a normalized routing distribution π, rather than letting a plain softmax diffuse it proportionally across all remaining keys. The use of layer-1 grounding is a structural requirement: ablations show that extracting the routing mask from deeper layers degrades performance below baseline because deep-layer attention is already corrupted by sinks. Implementation leverages PyTorch SDPA to route the custom sparse mask to Memory-Efficient Attention backends, avoiding O(N²) materialization in deeper layers.

## Results

Attention Remasking (AR) improves average accuracy across all five multi-image benchmarks (MMIU, MuirBench, MIRB, LIBench, MIBench) for every open-source LMM tested. On Qwen3-VL-8B, AR lifts accuracy from 55.6% to 59.4% on MMIU (+3.8 pp), from 51.3% to 54.9% on MuirBench (+3.6 pp), and from 63.5% to 67.6% on MIBench (+4.1 pp). Gains are even larger on weaker backbones: LLaVA-OneVision-7B improves by +4.3 pp on MMIU and +4.8 pp on MIBench. AR also reduces order sensitivity (flattening the position-accuracy slope) and lowers late-layer attention entropy, while adding only ~2.9% latency overhead on an A100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AR outperforms both baselines (VAR and SoFA) on every benchmark across all four open-source models, with no losses observed. Compared to VAR (post-softmax proportional redistribution), AR gains 3–5 pp on most benchmarks (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B: 59.4 vs. 55.9 on MMIU; 67.6 vs. 63.5 on MIBench), because VAR preserves the fragmented, position-skewed attention pattern and cannot create forward cross-image links. Compared to SoFA (causal-to-bidirectional interpolation), AR gains 2–4 pp (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B: 59.4 vs. 56.7 on MMIU; 69.2 vs. 66.2 on MIRB), since SoFA opens all forward links indiscriminately without routing attention to task-relevant tokens. AR is also shown to be orthogonal to Delimiter Token Scaling (DTS, Lee et al. 2026): combining both methods on Qwen3-VL yields compounding accuracy gains beyond either alone, confirming that sink-attention routing and boundary-reinforcement address independent failure modes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the attention-sink literature (Gu et al. for the dimension-based identification criterion; Kang et al. for visual sink redistribution in single-image LMMs) and extends it to the multi-image setting, where causal masking introduces a positional asymmetry absent in prior analyses. It departs from VAR (post-softmax proportional redistribution) and SoFA (uniform causal-to-bidirectional interpolation) by operating pre-softmax, masking sink columns, and selectively opening only task-relevant forward links guided by the model's own first-layer text-to-vision grounding. The theoretical contribution—bounding the interaction between high-entropy dispersion and skewed sink shares via Pinsker's inequality—provides a mechanistic link to recency bias that prior empirical position-bias studies lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AR offers a training-free, low-overhead inference edit that narrows the multi-image accuracy gap between open-source LMMs and leading commercial models by roughly 4–5 points across four architectures and five benchmarks, making it immediately deployable. The mechanistic diagnosis (sinks + causal skew → fragmented attention → recency bias) gives practitioners a concrete target for architectural fixes, and the finding that even Gated-Attention-equipped Qwen3-VL still exhibits visual sinks suggests the problem is structural to ViT + softmax rather than a training artifact. Open questions include whether the first-layer grounding assumption generalizes to video or very long multi-image sequences, whether architectural changes (e.g., register tokens, non-softmax attention) will eventually obviate the need for post-hoc remasking, and whether the method transfers to settings where images are heterogeneous in resolution or modality.
