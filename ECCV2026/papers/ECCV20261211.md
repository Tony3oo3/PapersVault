## Problem

Existing Retinex-based low-light image enhancement methods assume ideal Lambertian reflection (ignoring specular reflection) and construct physical constraints in image space, which limits generalization across datasets and requires complex loss functions to explicitly supervise intermediate variables during training. The paper asks whether reformulating the imaging physics to retain specular reflection and imposing constraints in an implicit feature space can yield a more robust and interpretable enhancement network without additional parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261211/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a single low-light RGB image (3×H×W) and produces an enhanced RGB image of the same spatial resolution. Internally, the input is converted to a separable color space (YCbCr by default, with HVI and LAB also tested) to decouple brightness from chrominance before enhancement. Training uses 320×320 random crops with a batch size of 8, and inference is evaluated on 512×512 images; the full model has 2.62M parameters and 8.68 GFLOPs, making it a lightweight design.

## Method

TPCNet reformulates the Kubelka-Munk image formation model by retaining the specular reflection coefficient ρ_f (rather than assuming it is zero as in classical Retinex), yielding a triple physical constraint (TPC) linking detection E, illumination e, and material reflectivity R through E = α·e·R + (1−α)·e/2 with α = 1−2ρ_f. The network instantiates this TPC in an implicit feature space (C×H×W) via three modules: a Light Feature Estimator (LFE) that predicts ê and α̂, a Reflectivity Feature Estimator (RFE) that computes R̂ from the constraint R̂ = (Ê − L̄/2) ⊙ L̂′, and a Dual-Stream Cross-Guided Transformer (DCGT) with a four-scale U-structure that jointly refines R̂ and ê. The core attention mechanism, CG-MSA, uses a pair downsampler to halve spatial resolution before self-attention, reducing computation to 25% of conventional multi-head self-attention while maintaining cross-guidance between reflectivity and illumination streams. A Color-Association Mechanism (CAM) then recombines enhanced brightness with multi-scale color features to suppress color bias, and the final output is obtained by applying Eq. (3) in feature space followed by a residual color-space mapping back to RGB.

## Results

On LOL-v2-Real, TPCNet achieves 24.978 dB PSNR, 0.882 SSIM, and 0.102 LPIPS, outperforming the closest competitor HVI-CIDNet (24.111 dB) by 0.867 dB. On LOL-v2-Synthetic, TPCNet reaches 26.032 dB PSNR and 0.041 LPIPS, and on the real-world VILNC-Indoor dataset it attains 24.634 dB PSNR, surpassing all Retinex-based methods by over 1 dB. The model achieves these results with only 2.62M parameters and 8.76 GFLOPs (35.1 ms runtime on 512×512), making it among the most efficient SOTA methods. On no-reference datasets (MEF, NPE, LIME, DICM, VV), TPCNet ranks first overall, achieving NIQE 3.625, MUSIQ 58.070, and PI 2.982.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPCNet outperforms all 14 baselines on every metric across the three reference datasets (LOL-v2-Real, LOL-v2-Synthetic, VILNC-Indoor). Against the closest lightweight competitor HVI-CIDNet [51], TPCNet gains +0.867 dB PSNR on LOL-v2-Real, +0.327 dB on LOL-v2-Synthetic, and +0.978 dB on VILNC-Indoor, while using comparable FLOPs (8.76G vs 7.57G). Compared to the Retinex-based RetinexFormer [3], TPCNet improves PSNR by 2.184 dB (LOL-v2-Real) and 0.899 dB (VILNC-Indoor) while using only 56% of its FLOPs (8.76G vs 15.57G). On no-reference datasets, TPCNet achieves the best overall rank, beating QuadPrior [43] on NIQE (3.625 vs 4.666) and PI (2.982 vs 3.455) with only 0.8% of its FLOPs, though QuadPrior leads on MUSIQ (62.241 vs 58.070). TPCNet also surpasses the heavier SMG [50] (17.8M params, 42.93G FLOPs) and RQ-LLIE [25] (13.48M params, 216.78G FLOPs) on all reference-dataset metrics while being 6–8× more efficient in computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Retinex-based LLIE lineage (RetinexNet, RetinexFormer, CIDNet) by grounding the image formation model in the more general Kubelka-Munk theory rather than the simplified Lambertian assumption. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) retaining the specular reflection coefficient ρ_f as a learnable variable, which splits the illumination into two complementary components and yields a 'triple' constraint (detection–illumination–reflectivity) instead of the traditional binary I = L⊙R; and (2) constructing these physical constraints in an implicit feature space (R^{C×H×W}) rather than in the image space, thereby avoiding the need for complex loss functions to explicitly supervise intermediate variables. The design also draws on the physical quadruple priors of QuadPrior [43] for illumination-invariant feature extraction, and the CG-MSA module is positioned as a lightweight alternative to the cross-guided attention mechanisms in RetinexFormer and CIDNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically relevant finding is that imposing physical constraints in feature space rather than image space substantially improves cross-dataset generalization (e.g., ~3 dB PSNR gain over RetinexFormer when transferring from LOL-v2-Real to LCDP), suggesting a broader principle for interpretable network design beyond LLIE. The CG-MSA, requiring only 25% of standard MSA FLOPs, is a plug-and-play efficiency module that could benefit other transformer-based enhancement or restoration tasks. Limitations include the Taylor-series approximation (mean error ~7.6%) and the ignored wavelength dependence, which may matter for extreme spectral conditions; the paper does not explore whether the TPC framework extends to video, multi-spectral, or 3D imaging. Open questions include whether the feature-space constraint principle generalizes to other physically-motivated tasks (denoising, dehazing) and whether the specular-reflection modeling captures real-world non-Lambertian surfaces adequately.
