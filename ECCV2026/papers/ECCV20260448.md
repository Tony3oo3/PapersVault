## Problem

Existing diffusion model acceleration methods optimize post-training quantization and timestep reduction independently, and rely on proxy costs (FLOPs, BitOps) that diverge from real hardware latency. Because removing denoising steps reshapes the error landscape traversed by a quantized model, and because DiT activation statistics vary non-monotonically across timesteps, these two axes are tightly coupled and cannot be optimized in isolation under a unified hardware budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260448/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DiffPro takes a pretrained diffusion denoiser (DiT-XL/2 for class-conditioned 256×256 ImageNet, or SDXL U-Net for text-conditioned 1024×1024 COCO), a small calibration set (512 examples), and explicit latency/memory budgets as planning inputs. It outputs a fixed deployable plan specifying per-layer bit widths (W4/W8/FP16), group sizes, a pruned timestep schedule (e.g., 500 of 1000 steps), and per-sample/per-timestep activation scales. At inference time the model consumes Gaussian noise plus a class label or text prompt and produces a single decoded image, with all quantization and scheduling decisions already baked in.

## Method

DiffPro is a five-stage, training-free pipeline that jointly optimizes per-layer precision and the denoising schedule under measured INT8 kernel timing. Stage 1 computes a composite sensitivity score per layer by fusing PCA dimensionality (95% variance components), diagonal Hessian curvature, temporal drift across timestep phases, and Jacobian-amplified quantization noise, with weights learned via OLS regression (R²=0.76). Stage 2 converts this ranking into a bit plan via ROI knapsack seeding followed by GPTQ-based evolutionary search screened by teacher-student drift. Stage 3 introduces Dynamic Activation Quantization (DAQ), which computes symmetric INT8 scales per sample, per timestep, and per channel group using 99.9th-percentile clipping, adding only 3.6% overhead. Stage 4 prunes low-drift timesteps while protecting the last 20% of steps (the refinement tail), and Stage 5 performs a greedy joint search that alternates between step removal and bit-width reduction, choosing whichever move yields the highest latency savings per estimated FID loss until hardware budgets are met.

## Results

On DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet 256×256), DiffPro achieves FID 5.89 (ΔFID 3.62 over full precision) with 7.9× model compression (2575→327 MB), 50% timestep reduction, and 2.7× end-to-end speedup (6.88→2.55 s on A30). On SDXL U-Net (COCO 2014 1024×1024), DiffPro reaches FID 26.50 (ΔFID 2.0) with 5.6× compression (12877→2317 MB) and 45% energy reduction. The W8A8 variant attains FID 5.12 at 2.68 s, nearly matching TQ-DiT's FID 4.91 at 25% lower latency. On FLUX.1-dev and PixArt-α, DiffPro achieves FID 20.5 and 73.54 respectively, outperforming SVDQuant and ViDiT-Q on both FID and image reward.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DiT-XL/2, DiffPro (FID 5.89) outperforms Q-DiT (6.40) by 0.51 FID and PTQ4DiT (7.75) by 1.86 FID at lower latency (2.55 s vs. 3.21 s and 3.85 s). TQ-DiT achieves a lower FID (4.91) but requires 2× more timesteps and incurs 41% higher latency (3.60 s vs. 2.55 s); DiffPro's W8A8 variant (FID 5.12) closes this gap to 0.21 FID at 25% lower latency. On SDXL, DiffPro (FID 26.50) beats GPTQ W8/8 (28.82) by 2.32 FID, GPTQ W4/8 (29.47) by 2.97 FID, and TMPQ-DM (27.91) by 1.41 FID, while also achieving the smallest model size (2317 MB vs. 2854–6439 MB). On FLUX.1-dev, DiffPro (FID 20.5, IR 0.943) surpasses SVDQuant (FID 20.7, IR 0.935) and ViDiT-Q (FID 21.2, IR 0.921). On PixArt-α, DiffPro (FID 73.54, IR 0.901) outperforms all compressed baselines, with ViDiT-Q W4/8 degrading most severely (IR 0.573) and SVDQuant incurring the largest FID increase (79.39 vs. 73.34 full precision).

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffPro sits at the intersection of two well-studied but previously decoupled lines of work: post-training quantization for diffusion models (PTQ4DM, Q-Diffusion, PTQ4DiT, Q-DiT) and timestep/schedule reduction (DDIM, AutoDiffusion, OSS). While TMPQ-DM already framed joint timestep-precision selection, it relied on proxy costs (FLOPs/BitOps) and lacked dynamic activation handling; DiffPro departs by grounding all decisions in measured INT8 kernel latency and introducing per-sample, per-timestep, per-group activation scaling (DAQ) calibrated against the quantized student. The composite sensitivity metric (fusing PCA dimensionality, diagonal Hessian curvature, temporal drift, and Jacobian-amplified noise) extends single-signal bit-allocation heuristics used in GPTQ-based methods, and the drift-guided timestep selector explicitly accounts for how quantization noise reshapes step importance—something prior pruning methods ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: 7.9× model compression and 2.7× speedup on DiT-XL/2 with only a 3.6 FID increase, plus 45% energy reduction, make high-fidelity diffusion inference feasible on edge and mid-range GPUs without retraining. The ablation showing that drift-guided pruning outperforms uniform thinning by 2.5 FID at matched precision validates that quantization and scheduling are genuinely coupled, a finding that should inform future joint-optimization frameworks. Open questions remain: the FID gap to full precision (5.89 vs. 2.27) is still substantial, the method has not been validated on video diffusion or very large models (e.g., FLUX at full resolution), and the one-time ~53-minute planning cost, while amortizable, may be prohibitive for rapidly iterating teams. Whether the composite sensitivity weights transfer cleanly to fundamentally different architectures (e.g., MMDiT-style dual-stream transformers) is untested.
