## Problem

Pretrained deep denoisers used as implicit priors in Plug-and-Play and Regularization-by-Denoising reconstruction algorithms exhibit a consistent peak-and-collapse failure mode: reconstruction quality improves over early iterations, reaches a peak, then degrades abruptly, making these pipelines unreliable in practice. Existing remedies either require retraining the denoiser under convergence constraints or restrict it to special operator classes, precluding the use of powerful off-the-shelf pretrained models. This paper seeks a drop-in stabilization mechanism that works with any black-box IR operator without retraining or architectural modification.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a wrapper around any existing PnP or RED pipeline: it receives the same inputs as the vanilla algorithm (a noisy measurement b, a known forward operator A, and a noise level σ_n) and outputs a reconstructed image after a fixed number of iterations. The black-box reconstruction operator T is accessed solely through input-output evaluations—no internal gradients or architecture knowledge are required. The only additional component is a lightweight contractive anchor denoiser S (a single 3×3 convolutional layer with 64 channels and trainable spline nonlinearities), whose fixed point p is precomputed once and used as the reference for the stability index throughout the iteration.

## Method

The core mechanism is adaptive sequential averaging: at each iteration, a stability index η_p(x_k, T) = ‖T(x_k)−p‖/‖x_k−p‖ is computed to measure local expansiveness of the black-box operator around the anchor's fixed point p. When η exceeds 1, a mixing weight θ_k is computed in closed form as the smallest root of η_p(x_k, T_θ)² = 1, and the update becomes x_{k+1} = (1−θ_k)T(x_k) + θ_k S(x_k); otherwise θ_k = 0 and the vanilla update is used. The contractive anchor S = D_σ ∘ prox_{ρf} is built from a one-layer convolutional denoiser D(x) = (1−γτ)x − γW^T ψ(Wx) whose nonexpansiveness is guaranteed by a reparameterization of W (ensuring ‖W‖ ≤ 1) and whose spline nonlinearities ψ_j have trainable slopes constrained to [0,1], making D a κ-contraction for κ ∈ [0.9, 0.99]. A key theorem proves that for any target ξ > κ, the stability index of the averaged operator T_θ is bounded by ξ whenever θ ≥ θ̃(x, ξ), and a corollary shows that enforcing ξ = 1 eventually guarantees bounded iterates, thereby preventing the divergent collapse observed in vanilla PnP.

## Results

On CBSD10 deblurring (σn=0.02), the proposed stabilization keeps final PSNR within 0.01–0.1 dB of peak PSNR across all PnP frameworks, whereas Vanilla-PnP and Equivariant-PnP frequently diverge (marked X) or lose 3–10 dB after the peak. For Gaussian deblurring with PnP-HQS+GS-DRUNet, the method achieves 30.56 dB peak PSNR, matching DPIR (30.69 dB) while remaining stable over 10,000 iterations. For 2× super-resolution with PnP-HQS+GS-DRUNet, the method attains 27.99 dB, slightly exceeding DPIR (27.93 dB) and DEAL (27.87 dB). The computational overhead is approximately 20% over vanilla PnP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vanilla-PnP achieves comparable or marginally higher peak PSNR in most settings but suffers severe post-peak collapse: final PSNR drops by 3–10 dB or the iterates diverge entirely (X) in 8 out of 14 tested framework/denoiser combinations. Equivariant-PnP [33] (Terris et al., CVPR 2024) shows the same instability pattern, diverging in 10 of 14 settings, confirming that random group transformations do not reliably prevent collapse. Against the tuned reconstruction pipelines, the proposed method matches or slightly exceeds DPIR [40] (e.g., 30.56 vs. 30.69 dB on Gaussian deblurring; 27.99 vs. 27.93 dB on 2× SR) and GSPnP [14] (30.56 vs. 30.58 dB), while DEAL [25] (Pourya et al., ICML 2025) is competitive at 30.45 dB on Gaussian deblurring. The critical distinction is that the proposed method achieves these numbers with a drop-in wrapper requiring no retraining, no architectural modification, and no per-task parameter tuning, whereas DPIR, GSPnP, and DEAL each require task-specific training or careful hyperparameter selection. The anchor S alone (without the black-box operator) yields 27.55–28.71 dB, confirming that the stabilization mechanism preserves the quality of the original denoiser rather than replacing it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical sequential averaging framework from bilevel optimization (Sabach & Shtern, 2017) and repurposes it as a stabilization wrapper for denoiser-driven reconstruction operators. It departs from the two dominant lines of convergent PnP work—(i) co-training denoisers with the proximal solver (RED, GSPnP, DEAL) and (ii) restricting denoisers to nonexpansive or proximally compatible operator classes (MMO, SPC-DRUNet)—by treating the pretrained denoiser as an unmodified black box and controlling its local expansiveness externally via an adaptive blend with a lightweight contractive anchor. The anchor itself draws on Lipschitz-constrained convolutional reparameterizations (Araujo et al., 2023) and trainable spline nonlinearities from the Goujon et al. line of work on convex/weakly-convex regularizers, but is designed specifically as a single-step contractive denoiser rather than an iterative regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The peak-and-collapse failure mode makes standard PnP and RED pipelines unreliable in practice, and this paper provides a solver-agnostic, denoiser-agnostic, drop-in fix that requires no retraining of the main network and no additional hyperparameter tuning beyond the anchor's own training. The finding that a modestly trained anchor (≈300 epochs) suffices for reliable stabilization, and that the method recovers near-DPIR performance (e.g., 30.70 vs. 30.69 dB on motion deblurring) without the careful pipeline design of DPIR, is practically significant for the large community using DeepInverse-style workflows. Open questions include whether the stabilization principle can be folded into end-to-end training to yield inherently stable IR systems, whether the 20% computational overhead is acceptable in latency-sensitive settings, and whether the stability index η captures all relevant failure modes of modern diffusion-based or transformer denoisers beyond the tested architectures.
