## Problem

Existing VAD benchmarks suffer from scene bias (over-representation of urban roadways), severe long-tail anomaly distributions, and overly simplistic normal behaviors, limiting reliable evaluation of modern detection systems. Meanwhile, the emerging Video Anomaly Understanding (VAU) task requires structured temporal narratives and multi-step causal reasoning, but constructing such benchmarks is prohibitively expensive due to dense manual annotation demands. The paper addresses both gaps by proposing a fully synthetic, generation-based benchmark with balanced anomaly coverage and built-in semantic annotations.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes static RGB images (sourced from COCO 2017, MIT Indoor Scenes, and web-sourced infrastructure photos) as starting frames, combined with LLM-generated multi-segment text storylines that specify camera angles, actions, and temporal progression. It produces 41-second long-form videos (or 16-second short clips) at 16 FPS, yielding 4,962 VAD videos spanning 1.68M frames across 31 anomaly types and 6 scene categories, plus 1,385 VAU videos with event-level and video-level text annotations. The VAD output includes frame-level binary labels, while the VAU output provides structured multi-granularity descriptions generated automatically from the storylines without manual annotation.

## Method

The pipeline operates in four stages: (1) a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-32B) classifies each source image into one of six scene categories and assigns a contextually appropriate anomaly type from a manually curated per-scene set; (2) an LLM generates a multi-step storyline decomposed into 7–8 descriptive segments (long) or 2–3 (short), each specifying camera motion, actions, and temporal progression; (3) the Wan diffusion model synthesizes video clips sequentially, where the last frame of clip l serves as the first frame of clip l+1, ensuring temporal continuity across the full 41-second narrative without the ghosting, prompt-forgetting, or motion artifacts of one-shot or looping approaches; (4) a hybrid VideoScore + manual filtering stage removes the bottom 5% by quality score and verifies physical plausibility. For VAU annotations, an LLM aggregates event-level storyline segments into coherent video-level summaries, and a VLM refiner cross-references these against actual visual content to correct misalignments, eliminating the need for manual text labeling.

## Results

On the Pistachio VAD test set, Fed-WSVAD achieves the highest Overall AP of 71.9% (AUC 83.2%), while PEL4VAD attains the highest Overall AUC of 83.7% (AP 71.0%). Performance drops sharply for likelihood-based MULDE (63.4% AUC, 34.9% AP) and the training-free VADTree (72.5% AUC but only 25.2% AP), revealing that neither density-estimation nor zero-shot VLM approaches handle Pistachio's enriched normal diversity. For the VAU task, InternVL3-14B achieves the best average F1-Score of 29.39%, with a consistent gap between event-level (~32%) and video-level (~26%) understanding across all MLLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the nine VAD baselines, Fed-WSVAD (71.9% AP) and PEL4VAD (83.7% AUC) lead overall, both leveraging CLIP-based vision-language features; Fed-WSVAD's federated aggregation of diverse local anomaly distributions gives it the edge in AP, while PEL4VAD's prompt-enhanced temporal context excels in AUC. The likelihood-based MULDE collapses to 34.9% AP because its density-estimation boundary cannot accommodate Pistachio's rich normal behaviors (handshaking, vehicle boarding, etc.). The training-free VADTree suffers a catastrophic AP drop to 25.2% (despite 72.5% AUC), as its zero-shot VLM reasoner generates severe false positives on complex-but-normal scenes. In cross-dataset generalization (Table 3), Pistachio-trained CLIP-TSA outperforms UCF-Crime-trained CLIP-TSA on XD-Violence (86.2% vs. 81.9% AUC) and MSAD (77.1% vs. –), confirming transferability, though it lags on low-resolution UCF-Crime (61.6% vs. 82.6% AUC). For VAU, InternVL3-14B (29.4% avg F1) edges out Qwen2.5-VL-7B (28.1%) and LLaVA-Next-Video-7B (26.4%), but all models show a 5–7 point gap between event-level and video-level F1, indicating that long-horizon narrative comprehension remains unsolved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of constructing VAD benchmarks by curating Internet-collected or surveillance-sourced videos (UCF-Crime, ShanghaiTech, Street Scene, NWPU Campus) and instead repurposes modern video generation models (Wan) and VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3) as a controlled synthesis pipeline for benchmark construction. Conceptually, it extends the idea that generative models can produce training data (as in OVVAD's pseudo-anomaly synthesis) to the much more ambitious goal of building an entire evaluation benchmark with precise scene, anomaly-type, and temporal-narrative control. For the VAU task, it builds on the emerging line of work (HAWK, Holmes-VAU) but addresses the annotation bottleneck by using generation storylines as ground-truth semantic labels, eliminating manual labeling entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pistachio addresses three persistent weaknesses in VAD evaluation—scene bias, long-tail anomaly distribution, and insufficient temporal complexity—that have limited the reliability of published results for over a decade. The first fully-annotated, zero-manual-label VAU benchmark with multi-anomaly co-occurrence opens a previously intractable evaluation space for causal and narrative reasoning. However, the acknowledged domain gap (e.g., 61.58% AUC on UCF-Crime vs. 82.56% from native training) and residual character-identity drift in generated videos mean synthetic data complements rather than replaces real-world benchmarks, leaving open the question of how to close the synthetic-to-real fidelity gap for low-resolution, fixed-angle surveillance scenarios.
