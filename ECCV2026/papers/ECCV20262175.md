## Problem

Global CLS-token embeddings from frozen ViT backbones collapse under large domain shift, mapping unrelated classes to nearly identical vectors and making prototype-based few-shot classification fail. Part-level patch descriptors are more resilient across domains, but the few existing patch-matching methods (DeepEMD, H-OT, TF-OCM) formulate classification as optimal transport or bipartite assignment, requiring iterative solvers whose cost grows quadratically or worse in the number of support tokens, making them impractical at higher shot counts. No training-free, closed-form patch-based classifier existed that could scale to many shots without learned parameters.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a query image and a set of support images with class labels (5-way 5-shot episodes, 224×224 RGB), processed by a frozen DINO ViT-S/16 backbone to yield ℓ₂-normalized patch tokens (excluding the CLS token) and a global CLS embedding per image. It outputs a predicted class label via argmax over C class scores, along with per-patch sigmoid gate values r_p* ∈ [0,1] that indicate the variational posterior probability each patch is discriminative foreground for the predicted class. A single globally fixed configuration (π=0.7, ε=0.01, α=0.6) is reused across all twelve datasets with no per-domain retuning.

## Method

VPG formulates patch-based few-shot classification as probabilistic evidence accumulation under a discriminative variational model: each query patch carries a Bernoulli latent variable (foreground vs. clutter) with prior π, and minimizing the per-patch assignment free energy yields, in closed form, a sigmoid gate r_p* = σ(logit(π) − s_ε) and a bounded softplus evidence score e = softplus(logit(π) − s_ε) capped at −log(1−π). The matching cost s_ε is a soft-min (log-sum-exp) over cosine distances between the query patch and a class support-token dictionary, controlled by temperature ε. The final class score fuses the aggregated local patch evidence D(X,c) = Σ_p a_p·e_p with a global CLS cosine similarity term via a product-of-experts posterior, parameterized by a single mixing weight α. Because all operations reduce to one matrix multiply per class followed by elementwise sigmoid and softplus, inference is O(CPNd) with no iterative Sinkhorn or LP solver, no learned parameters, and no gradient updates, making it 37× faster than H-OT and 7210× faster than DeepEMD at 5-shot.

## Results

On the CDFSL benchmark (5-way 5-shot, frozen DINO ViT-S/16), training-free VPG achieves 67.55% average accuracy, surpassing the best training-free baseline REAP (66.75%) and exceeding the previous training-free state of the art TF-OCM by +5.35 points. With lightweight episodic fine-tuning (5 gradient steps on the last ViT block), VPG+FT reaches 71.06% average, outperforming the best fine-tuned baseline REAP+FT (68.38%) by 2.68 points. On Meta-Dataset unseen domains, VPG+FT averages 81.8%, exceeding DIPA (78.5%). In-domain, VPG+FT achieves 99.25% on miniImageNet 5-way 5-shot and 99.46% on CUB 5-way 5-shot, both surpassing H-OT with the same DINO backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Training-free VPG outperforms the strongest training-free baseline REAP (66.75%) by +0.80 points on CDFSL average, and beats the previous training-free SOTA TF-OCM by a substantial +5.35 points. Notably, VPG without any gradient updates surpasses all fine-tuned baselines on EuroSAT (94.02% vs. REAP+FT's 91.92%, +2.10) and CropDiseases (96.89% vs. 96.74%), indicating that patch-level gating captures discriminative structure that global CLS fine-tuning cannot recover. With episodic fine-tuning, VPG+FT (71.06%) exceeds REAP+FT (68.38%) by 2.68 points, with the largest gains on EuroSAT (+5.47) and ISIC2018 (+3.90). On Meta-Dataset unseen domains, VPG+FT (81.8%) surpasses DIPA (78.5%) by 3.3 points, with the largest margin on CIFAR-10 (+11.3). On in-domain miniImageNet 5-shot, VPG (TF) beats H-OT with the same DINO backbone by +2.15 points (96.06% vs. 93.91%), while VPG+FT extends this to +5.34 (99.25% vs. 95.94%); however, training-free VPG slightly trails H-OT† on CUB 5-shot (95.57% vs. 95.94%), a gap closed by fine-tuning (99.46% vs. 95.94%). Computationally, VPG classifies an episode in 12 ms, making it 37× faster than H-OT (Sinkhorn) and 7,210× faster than DeepEMD (LP solver).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the transport-based patch-matching lineage (DeepEMD, H-OT, TF-OCM) that formulates few-shot classification as optimal transport or bipartite assignment requiring iterative solvers. Instead, it repurposes the variational inference framework—specifically free-energy minimization over Bernoulli latent variables—to derive a closed-form sigmoid gate and bounded softplus evidence score, eliminating the need for Sinkhorn iterations or LP solvers. The global-local fusion via product-of-experts and the soft-min cost are standard energy-based tools, but their combination with the discriminative variational assignment is the paper's conceptual contribution, positioning VPG as a training-free alternative to both CLS-token-based cross-domain methods (REAP, FLoR, CD-CLS) and iterative part-matching solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bounded-evidence guarantee (each patch contributes at most −log(1−π) ≈ 1.20) provides a formal robustness certificate absent from prior patch-matching methods, making part-level reasoning practical for training-free inference without per-domain tuning. The practical impact is significant: a single fixed configuration (π=0.7, ε=0.01, α=0.6) across twelve datasets and four benchmark suites, with 37× speedup over Sinkhorn and 7210× over LP solvers, lowers the barrier to deploying few-shot classifiers in resource-constrained or rapidly-shifting domains. Key limitations remain: the positive-only evidence score cannot model negative evidence against a class, the structured-background extension (β) is left as future work, and the method is tied to ViT patch-token representations, leaving open whether the variational gating principle transfers to other architectures or tokenization schemes.
