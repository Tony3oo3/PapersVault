## Problem

High-accuracy image parsing (matting and segmentation) demands fine-grained boundary and transparency estimation that goes well beyond conventional dense prediction. Existing approaches either lack semantic awareness (salient-object matting), produce coarse masks (MLLM-based reasoning segmentation), or require repetitive human interaction (interactive matting), leaving a gap between semantic understanding and pixel-level fidelity that no fully automatic system has closed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260545/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image, a natural-language referring instruction, and an initial bounding box, and iteratively produces either a binary segmentation mask or a soft alpha matte with continuous transparency values in [0, 1]. The MLLM does not predict pixels directly; instead it emits a 32×32 textual grid map where each patch is one of four semantic tokens (<human>, <bg>, <unknown>, <other>), compressed via Row-wise Run-Length Encoding so that a full grid typically yields far fewer than the 1024-token worst case. Only the <target> and <unknown> regions are extracted from the grid and converted into an attention-mask prompt for the Action Model, which then performs the actual pixel-level refinement over 3 refinement loops by default.

## Method

Kiroshi couples an MLLM policy (Ovis2.5-2B) with a grid-aware Action Model (DINOv2 encoder + Mask2Former-style decoder) in a plan-and-act MDP. The key architectural mechanism is a grid map used as a structured attention mask inside the transformer decoder: patches marked by the MLLM receive attention score 0 while all others receive −∞, constraining the decoder to refine only the semantically relevant regions. Training proceeds in two stages: (I) supervised fine-tuning where the MLLM learns to parse masks into grid descriptors and the Action Model is trained with ℓ₁ + Laplacian loss, and (II) DPO post-training using preference pairs mined from the Action Model's own rollouts—residual maps guide grid sampling, and transitions that improve MSE are labeled positive while those that degrade it are labeled negative, yielding within-context preference pairs under identical states. At inference, an MLLM-as-Judge strategy drives multi-round refinement, with the grid map progressively shifting toward boundary and uncertain regions, making the refinement process both semantically interpretable and quality-aware.

## Results

On the HiFiRefMS benchmark, Kiroshi achieves the best performance across all segmentation and matting metrics. For segmentation, it reaches maxFβ=0.875, Fβ=0.820, MAE=0.033, Sα=0.866, and HCE=1218, surpassing the strongest salient-based baseline (DiffDIS, maxFβ=0.829) by 4.6 points in maxFβ. For matting, it attains MSE=0.0263, SAD=26.34, Grad=23.71, and Conn=13.48, outperforming the best interactive method (ZIM, SAD=36.41) by 10.07 in SAD and the best MLLM-based method (SegAgent, SAD=87.19) by 60.85 in SAD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Kiroshi outperforms all 16 baselines across every metric on both the segmentation and matting subsets of HiFiRefMS. Against the strongest salient-based method (DiffDIS), Kiroshi improves maxFβ by 4.6 points (0.875 vs. 0.829) and reduces SAD by 46.03 (26.34 vs. 72.37), showing that contrast-driven methods cannot match semantic-aware refinement. Against the best interactive method (ZIM), Kiroshi gains 10.1 points in maxFβ (0.875 vs. 0.774) and reduces SAD by 10.07 (26.34 vs. 36.41), demonstrating that fully automated agentic refinement surpasses even GT-box-guided interactive matting. Against MLLM-based baselines, the gap is largest: Kiroshi reduces SAD from 87.19 (SegAgent) to 26.34 and from 96.72 (Text4Seg) to 26.34, a 3.3× improvement, confirming that pixel-level fidelity is the critical bottleneck for existing MLLM segmentation approaches. The cross-dataset analysis (Table 5) further shows that Kiroshi's iterative refinement yields consistent gains (×1→×3), whereas plugging external matting modules onto Text4Seg or SegAgent masks shows diminishing or even negative returns with additional iterations, validating the necessity of the agentic design.

## Relation to Prior Work

Kiroshi sits at the intersection of three research threads: interactive matting (ZIM, HQ-SAM, SAM), MLLM-based reasoning segmentation (LISA, Text4Seg, SegAgent), and preference-optimized alignment (DPO/RLHF). It departs from interactive methods by replacing unstable pixel-level point prompts with structured grid-level attention masks, and from MLLM segmentation methods by decoupling semantic reasoning (MLLM) from pixel-level refinement (a Mask2Former-style Action Model) into an iterative MDP loop. The trajectory-mining strategy for generating DPO preference pairs directly from the Action Model's own iterative rollouts is a novel data curation approach that avoids the need for external reward models or human annotation of pixel-level quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that fully automatic, semantically-aware high-fidelity matting is achievable without human intervention, closing a gap that interactive methods have left open. The HiFiRefMS benchmark provides the first real-world, multi-instance, text-referenced evaluation for high-fidelity matting and segmentation, addressing the synthetic-data limitation of RefMatte. Open questions include whether the 3-round agentic loop generalizes to extremely complex scenes with many overlapping instances, the computational cost of multi-round inference relative to single-shot methods, and whether the grid-prompting + trajectory-DPO paradigm transfers to other dense prediction tasks beyond matting.
