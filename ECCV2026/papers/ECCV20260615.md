## Problem

Humanoid whole-body manipulation in complex 3D spatial environments faces two coupled challenges: (1) fixed-perspective perception fails when objects are occluded or arranged in intricate spatial configurations, and (2) data-driven policies trained on limited real-robot demonstrations generalize poorly to out-of-distribution spatial layouts and novel objects. Existing approaches either rely on costly task-specific teleoperation data with poor cross-task transfer, or use VLMs for planning but lack active spatial perception and whole-body coordination mechanisms.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a natural-language instruction, RGB-D frames from a 2-DoF active Realsense D435i camera mounted on the humanoid's neck, and proprioceptive joint states. It produces a structured action set A = {a_cam, a_dex, a_upper, a_lower} covering active camera viewpoint changes, dexterous hand joint poses, upper-body arm joint poses, and lower-body locomotion commands (v_x, ω_y, h_t), which are ultimately converted to joint torques via PD controllers. The camera action space is a 2-DoF neck motion, while the base movement space is 4-DoF (x, y, z translations plus z-axis rotation), giving the VLM a discrete set of perceptual and locomotive primitives to invoke.

## Method

The framework is a hierarchical multi-agent system with two tightly coupled components operating in a perception-planning-control closed loop. The Active Spatial Brain uses a VLM to dynamically control the camera viewpoint and base position for active spatial perception, maintains a movement-conditioned memory bank (archiving only images since the last base move plus a textual execution log) to preserve spatial consistency, and performs adaptive task planning with closed-loop replanning that can re-invoke failed sub-tasks. The Generalizable Action Cerebellum decomposes whole-body control into lower-body locomotion (an A*-based navigation agent on a depth-derived grid map, an optimization-based end-effector reachable-space solver that minimizes base displacement subject to a shoulder-reachability constraint, and an RL policy from HOMIE for joint-level tracking) and upper-body manipulation (a grasp agent that synthesizes hand-object interaction images via a multimodal generative model, reconstructs 3D geometry with Hyper3D, estimates human hand pose with EasyHOI, and retargets to the dexterous hand via fingertip alignment; plus a post-grasp trajectory agent that selects parameterized action primitives—push, pull, place, rotate—from VLM-predicted 2D keypoints lifted to 3D and converted to joint commands via IK). No task-specific real-robot data is required; generalization comes from the VLM's spatial reasoning and the foundation models' learned manipulation priors.

## Results

On the five spatial manipulation tasks, the proposed framework achieves 70–85% success rate in the easy (in-domain) setting and 55–70% in the hard (out-of-domain) setting, consistently outperforming all baselines. The most striking result is on Hard Task 4 (Obstacle Avoidance), where the method reaches 60% while both data-driven baselines (TrajBooster and Ψ₀) drop to 0%. The performance gap between easy and hard settings is far smaller for the proposed method (e.g., Task 3: 80→55) than for data-driven baselines (TrajBooster: 55→10; Ψ₀: 70→25), demonstrating superior generalization without any task-specific robot data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method wins on 9 out of 10 task-difficulty combinations against CaP (the VLM-driven baseline), tying only on Easy Task 4 (both 80%). Against the data-driven baselines, the advantage is more pronounced: TrajBooster is outperformed by 10–50 percentage points across all settings, with the largest gap on Hard Task 3 (55% vs. 10%) and Hard Task 4 (60% vs. 0%). Ψ₀ is similarly outperformed on all 10 combinations, with particularly large margins on Hard Task 3 (55% vs. 25%) and Hard Task 4 (60% vs. 0%). The critical finding is that data-driven methods suffer catastrophic degradation from easy to hard (e.g., TrajBooster Task 4 drops from 60% to 0%), whereas the proposed framework maintains a much smaller drop (80% to 60%), confirming that its multi-agent VLM-based design generalizes far better to out-of-distribution spatial configurations without requiring task-specific robot data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant data-driven paradigm for humanoid whole-body manipulation (HOMIE, TrajBooster, Ψ₀) by eliminating the need for task-specific robot demonstrations entirely, instead leveraging VLMs for spatial perception and planning. It extends VLM-driven manipulation approaches (CaP, Inner Monologue, ProgPrompt) by introducing active camera control, a memory bank for spatial consistency, and a decoupled Brain/Cerebellum architecture that handles whole-body coordination. For lower-body locomotion it reuses HOMIE's RL policy as a low-level executor, while for upper-body manipulation it repurposes foundation generative models (Hyper3D, EasyHOI) for grasp synthesis rather than training task-specific policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a data-free, VLM-driven multi-agent system can match or exceed data-driven baselines on spatially complex humanoid manipulation, directly addressing the data bottleneck that has constrained the field. The spatial intelligence benchmark reveals a concrete gap: current VLMs handle coarse relational reasoning well but fail at fine-grained metric perception from 2D observations, suggesting that hybrid architectures (VLM planning + geometric solvers) will remain necessary. Limitations include evaluation on a single robot platform (Unitree G1), small trial counts (10–30), and baselines fine-tuned on only ~20 trajectories, leaving open whether the performance gap persists with more thoroughly trained data-driven methods or in truly dynamic, multi-object scenes.
