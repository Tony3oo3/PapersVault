## Problem

Directly adapting unified multimodal models (UMMs) to In-Image Machine Translation (IIMT) suffers from two fundamental failures: an understanding-generation conflict, where the semantically valid translation predicted by the understanding branch diverges from the exact target string used as generation supervision, and spatial position misalignment, where diffusion-based rendering places translated text in incorrect regions. Existing open-source UMMs adapted to IIMT produce near-zero BLEU scores, far below both specialist cascaded pipelines and proprietary systems like GPT-Image-1.

## Input / Output

The model receives a source RGB image containing scene text (256×256 during training; 512×512 on Translatotron-V, 48×512 on IIMT30k) together with a natural-language instruction specifying the source and target languages. It jointly outputs an autoregressively decoded translated text sequence and a diffusion-synthesized translated image in which the source text is visually replaced by the target-language rendering while non-text content is preserved. A third auxiliary output—a pixel-level text-region mask—is predicted to provide spatial grounding for the generation branch.

## Method

UniTranslator couples a Qwen2.5-VL understanding backbone with an MMDiT diffusion generator through two dedicated modules. The Understand-Generation Alignment Module (UGAM) uses the understanding representation as query and the shared multimodal representation as key/value in a cross-attention layer followed by an MLP, producing a soft condition embedding that tells the diffusion branch how the predicted translation aligns with the visual space, thereby resolving the semantic-to-pixel supervision clash. The Spatial Mask Decoder (SMD) applies cross-attention between the understanding representation (query) and visual features (key/value), then decodes via deconvolution to a pixel-level text-region mask supervised by BCE + Dice loss, injecting explicit spatial cues into generation. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 freezes all pretrained backbones and trains only UGAM and SMD to establish a stable cross-modal interface; Stage 2 jointly fine-tunes LoRA adapters (rank 64) in Qwen2.5-VL, UGAM, SMD, and MMDiT attention projections with a cosine schedule, reducing the train-test gap between teacher-forced and autoregressive decoding. The total loss is a weighted sum of autoregressive text prediction, diffusion denoising, and mask prediction objectives (λ_und=0.1, λ_gen=1, λ_mask=0.1).

## Results

On Translatotron-V (De→En), UniTranslator achieves BLEU 25.03, Structure-BLEU 24.86, and SSIM 0.8184, outperforming GPT-Image-1 (BLEU 20.75, SSIM 0.7369) and the specialist Translatotron-V (BLEU 15.39, SSIM 0.7832). On the more realistic IIMT30k benchmark, it obtains the highest BLEU (16.3/14.7 valid/test) and COMET (59.9/59.8%) with the lowest FID (18.6/8.9). On the multilingual PRIM benchmark, UniTranslator achieves the best average BLEU/COMET (12.8/50.7) and lowest FID (22.9) among all end-to-end models, surpassing VisTrans (11.3/47.0, FID 28.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniTranslator substantially outperforms all open-source unified multimodal models (Qwen-Image, GLM-Image, Janus, Bagel, Uniworld, LongCat-Image) by a wide margin, achieving BLEU ~25 versus ~0.2 on De→En, indicating that existing UMMs essentially fail at IIMT without task-specific adaptation. Against the strongest proprietary baseline GPT-Image-1, UniTranslator wins on De→En (BLEU 25.03 vs 20.75; Structure-BLEU 24.86 vs 16.01; SSIM 0.8184 vs 0.7369) and Fr→En (BLEU 27.77 vs 27.38; SSIM 0.8060 vs 0.7081), but loses on En→De (BLEU 13.41 vs 19.61), suggesting GPT-Image-1 retains an advantage in that direction. Compared to the specialist end-to-end Translatotron-V, UniTranslator improves BLEU by ~10 points on De→En (25.03 vs 15.39) while also achieving higher SSIM (0.8184 vs 0.7832). On IIMT30k, UniTranslator beats the strongest specialist DebackX on both translation quality (BLEU 16.3 vs 14.9; COMET 59.9 vs 51.2) and visual fidelity (FID 18.6 vs 20.4). On PRIM, UniTranslator surpasses the end-to-end VisTrans on average BLEU/COMET (12.8/50.7 vs 11.3/47.0) and FID (22.9 vs 28.8), while cascade systems (PARSeq-mTransformer-Render, PEIT-Render) achieve comparable translation scores but with much worse FID (101–104 vs 22.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established threads: in-image machine translation (IIMT), previously addressed by cascaded OCR-MT-editing pipelines or specialized end-to-end models (Translatotron-V, PEIT, VisTrans), and unified multimodal models (UMMs) that jointly perform visual understanding and image generation. Rather than building a new IIMT pipeline from scratch, the paper repurposes a pre-trained UMM (Qwen2.5-VL for understanding, MMDiT for generation) and introduces two targeted modules—UGAM for cross-branch semantic alignment and SMD for pixel-level spatial supervision—to resolve the understanding-generation conflict and spatial misalignment that generic UMMs exhibit on IIMT. It departs from prior IIMT work by treating translation comprehension and visual text rendering as a single jointly-optimized problem rather than a loosely coupled pipeline, and departs from prior UMM work by adding explicit alignment and spatial-grounding mechanisms not present in architectures like Bagel, UniWorld, or Janus-Pro.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper validates that the unified multimodal paradigm can match or exceed specialist IIMT systems on translation quality and visual fidelity, suggesting a viable path toward general-purpose image-level text translation without task-specific pipelines. The bidirectional reinforcement finding—that understanding loss improves generation metrics and generation loss improves understanding metrics—hints at a deeper synergy in joint optimization that could inform broader multi-task multimodal training. However, limitations remain: the model still struggles with highly stylized typography (graffiti, neon cursive) and extremely complex backgrounds, and the evaluation is concentrated on synthetic or semi-synthetic benchmarks (Translatotron-V, IIMT30k, PRIM) with limited coverage of truly wild real-world scenes. Open questions include whether the two-stage training protocol generalizes to other UMM backbones, whether the approach scales to lower-resource language pairs, and whether the large gap between proprietary UMMs (GPT-Image-1) and open-source ones on IIMT will narrow as foundation models improve.
