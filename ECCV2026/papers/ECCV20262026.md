## Problem

Integrating visuotactile sensing into online reinforcement learning for contact-rich manipulation requires tactile simulators that are simultaneously physically faithful, numerically stable under large deformations, and fast enough for large-scale parallel training—yet existing methods either sacrifice fidelity (penalty-based) or stability (MPM), and few support multi-GPU acceleration. Additionally, zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for tactile-based RL policies remains largely unresolved due to both structured (dynamics, material, contact) and stochastic (noise, unmodeled effects) domain gaps.

## Input / Output

The RL policy consumes a compact observation consisting of the 7-DoF end-effector pose, a 7×9×2 tactile marker displacement field from a single GelSight Mini finger, and the previous action—deliberately excluding object pose and visual data to emphasize tactile reliance. The simulator outputs low-dimensional (9×7) marker displacement fields rather than high-resolution (320×240) RGB tactile images, chosen for superior sensitivity to distinct contact modes and better regularity for RL. Actions are incremental Cartesian updates executed through a Cartesian impedance controller with critical damping.

## Method

Tac2Real builds on the PNCG-IPC solver, extending it with an explicit frictional contact potential D(x) and implementing it in Taichi for GPU-parallel kernels that require only gradient evaluations, diagonal Hessian entries, and dot products—enabling a CCD-free line search and tens-of-iterations convergence. A Ray-based multi-node, multi-GPU architecture distributes tactile simulation across 64 GPUs (4 nodes × 16 RTX 4090s), achieving 4,465 FPS at 4,096 parallel environments, and integrates as a lightweight plugin into Isaac Lab or MuJoCo. For sim-to-real transfer, TacAlign applies four sequential stages: (i) bidirectional alternating minimization of impedance controller gains to minimize trajectory discrepancy, (ii) CMA-ES optimization of IPC material parameters (E, ν, ρ, μ) against recorded marker displacement fields, (iii) task-specific fine-tuning of Isaac Lab friction and contact stiffness, and (iv) domain randomization of parameters, geometry, and sensing channels during PPO training.

## Results

On the 8 mm peg insertion task, Tac2Real achieves a 91.7% zero-shot sim-to-real transfer success rate (55/60 trials) with full TacAlign calibration, dramatically outperforming TacSL (15.0%) and Tacchi (8.3%). In simulation, Tac2Real and TacSL achieve comparable success rates (77.6% vs. 78.9% for 8 mm peg insertion; 70.2% vs. 70.8% for nut threading), both far exceeding Tacchi (17.3% and 15.2%) and the no-tactile baseline (16.8% and 31.3%). Generalization to larger peg diameters (12 mm and 16 mm) yields 93.3% real-world success rates. In parallel simulation benchmarks on 16 RTX 4090 GPUs, Tac2Real reaches 4,465 FPS (pseudo-structured mesh) and 1,665 FPS (unstructured mesh) at 4,096 environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tac2Real decisively outperforms both baselines in real-world zero-shot deployment: on the 8 mm peg insertion task, Tac2Real achieves 91.7% success versus 15.0% for TacSL and 8.3% for Tacchi — a 6× and 11× improvement, respectively. In simulation, Tac2Real and TacSL perform nearly identically (77.6% vs. 78.9% on 8 mm peg insertion; 70.2% vs. 70.8% on nut threading), indicating that the simulation fidelity of both methods is comparable; the critical differentiator is the TacAlign calibration pipeline, which TacSL lacks. Tacchi, based on MPM, underperforms in both simulation and reality due to numerical instability (particle splashing under large deformations) and poor friction modeling, yielding only 17.3% and 8.3% success rates. The no-tactile baseline (16.8% sim, 6.7% real) confirms that tactile feedback is essential for these contact-rich tasks. Ablation within TacAlign shows that task-based calibration (level 3) is the most critical component: removing it drops real-world success from 91.7% to 76.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

Tac2Real builds on the Incremental Potential Contact (IPC) family of elastodynamic solvers, specifically adopting the newer PNCG-IPC variant [28] that replaces Newton's method with a GPU-parallelizable nonlinear conjugate gradient scheme, and extends it with a frictional contact term absent from the original formulation. It positions itself against the three-stage evolution of visuotactile simulation (MPM-based Tacchi/Tacchi 2.0, IPC-based TacIPC/Taccel, and penalty-based TacSL) by combining high-fidelity IPC physics with multi-node multi-GPU parallelization via Taichi and Ray, and by outputting low-dimensional marker displacement fields directly rather than rendering RGB images. The TacAlign calibration pipeline departs from prior ad-hoc domain randomization by introducing a structured, four-stage procedure (controller alignment, baseline IPC parameter identification via CMA-ES, task-specific friction/stiffness tuning, and randomization) that addresses both deterministic and stochastic sources of sim-to-real gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for tactile-based RL has been largely unresolved, and Tac2Real's 91.7% real-world success rate on blind peg insertion with a systematic, reproducible calibration pipeline represents a concrete step toward closing this gap. The finding that TacSL matches Tac2Real in simulation (78.9% vs 77.6%) yet collapses to 15% in the real world underscores that simulation fidelity alone is insufficient—structured calibration of controller dynamics and contact parameters is the critical differentiator. Limitations include validation on a single sensor type (GelSight Mini), a narrow task family (peg insertion, nut threading), and a modest 58.3% real-world success rate for nut threading, leaving open questions about generalization to dexterous hands, deformable objects, and other sensor modalities. The multi-GPU PNCG-IPC architecture and plugin-based integration with Isaac Lab/MuJoCo are likely to become a practical foundation for scaling tactile RL beyond the current single-task demonstrations.
