## Problem

6D object pose estimation is bottlenecked by the cost and difficulty of collecting large-scale real images with accurate pose annotations, while synthetic renders—though pose-labeled—suffer from a reality gap in texture, lighting, and background that limits generalization. The paper asks whether geometry renders can be translated into visually realistic images while strictly preserving the pose-defining silhouette and structure, enabling low-cost construction of pose-labeled pseudo-real training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262695/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a geometry render (from point clouds or CAD meshes) and a binary object mask, both cropped to an object-centric 256×256 square and encoded into the VAE latent space. It produces a pseudo-real RGB image at the same resolution that preserves the input pose and geometric structure while allowing free variation in texture, illumination, and background. At inference, a strength parameter (default 0.6) and a style seed control the degree of appearance deviation, enabling multiple diverse outputs per input geometry.

## Method

GenerationPose is a latent DiT-B/2 diffusion model conditioned on three complementary signals. A lightweight ControlNetGeom branch encodes the geometry render through the same VAE, aligns features to the DiT token grid, and injects geometry priors via two pathways: cross-attention cond_tokens in every block and zero-initialized residual hints in the first K blocks (a strong-early/weak-late design that locks pose structure before texture refinement). A mask-aware self-attention mechanism applies a large negative bias to block foreground-to-background attention, preventing background contamination of object-region tokens. Training is supervised on pose-aligned triplets from uCO3D with a dual-mask strategy (dilated mask for attention, eroded mask for loss), combining the standard diffusion objective with periodic foreground-weighted L1 and perceptual losses. The model is trained on point-cloud renders and evaluated zero-shot on CAD/mesh BOP renders, demonstrating cross-render-style generalization without fine-tuning.

## Results

On downstream 6D pose estimation, adding GenerationPose pseudo-real data to GDR-Net improves ADI.10d from 91.00 to 92.71 on LineMOD, 52.90 to 56.20 on LM-O, and 60.10 to 78.73 on YCB-V. For WDR, the same data boosts ADI.10d from 83.01 to 85.55 (LM), 37.90 to 44.76 (LM-O), and 27.50 to 41.40 (YCB-V). In the data-setting ablation (Table 5), GenerationPose alone achieves 79.87 ADI.10d on LineMOD—far above PBR renders (40.96) and close to real data (83.01)—while Real+Ours (85.55) outperforms both Real alone (83.01) and Real+PBR (83.54). Geometry preservation is strong: NCD stays below 1.5% and BF1@5 exceeds 0.94 across all four BOP test datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two downstream pose estimators—GDR-Net and WDR—with and without GenerationPose-generated training data across three BOP datasets. Adding the generated data consistently improves both estimators on all datasets, with the largest gains on the more challenging unseen-object settings: YCB-V sees +18.63 ADI.10d for GDR-Net (60.10→78.73) and +13.90 for WDR (27.50→41.40), while LM-O gains +3.30 and +6.86 respectively. On the seen-object LineMOD dataset, improvements are smaller but still positive (+1.71 for GDR-Net, +2.54 for WDR), suggesting the generated data is most valuable when real annotations are scarce. In the data-setting comparison (Table 5), GenerationPose alone (79.87 ADI.10d) dramatically outperforms PBR renders (40.96) and approaches real-data performance (83.01), while combining Real+Ours (85.55) surpasses Real+PBR (83.54), confirming that the pseudo-real images provide more effective complementary supervision than standard PBR renders.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenerationPose sits at the intersection of latent diffusion (LDM), Diffusion Transformer backbones (DiT), and conditional control (ControlNet), repurposing each for a task-specific appearance-transfer pipeline. It departs from prior GAN-based synthetic-to-real methods (pix2pix, CycleGAN, SimGAN) by replacing adversarial training with diffusion denoising, and from standard ControlNet by transplanting its residual-hint and cross-attention mechanism onto a DiT backbone rather than a U-Net. The mask-aware self-attention with FG→BG blocking is a novel spatial constraint not present in prior conditional diffusion designs, and the cross-domain protocol (training on uCO3D point-cloud renders, evaluating zero-shot on BOP CAD/mesh renders) goes beyond the typical same-domain evaluation used in prior sim-to-real appearance transfer work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in 6D pose estimation—expensive, hard-to-scale real data collection—by showing that geometry-conditioned diffusion can produce pose-labeled pseudo-real images that meaningfully improve downstream estimators (WDR, GDR-Net) even under a challenging cross-render-domain shift. The strength-controlled DDIM sampling offers a useful knob for balancing geometric fidelity against appearance diversity, which is directly relevant for data augmentation pipelines. Key limitations remain: the method does not model external occluders or occluder-object interactions, and performance may degrade under severe occlusion or inaccurate masks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to category-level (rather than instance-level) pose estimation and whether occlusion-aware conditioning can be integrated without sacrificing the pose-preservation guarantee.
