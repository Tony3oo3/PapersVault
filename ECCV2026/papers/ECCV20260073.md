## Problem

Existing stochastic human motion prediction (SHMP) models hard-code skeleton kinematics as a network structural prior, requiring a separately trained instance for each kinematics format and making cross-dataset training and zero-shot inference on unseen or partial skeletons impossible. The paper formalizes this as a fundamental bottleneck and introduces two novel zero-shot tasks: predicting motion for full-body kinematics unseen at training time, and handling occluded or missing limbs without explicit training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260073/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a past motion sequence of T_P frames represented as bone direction vectors (the relative 3D direction from each joint to its parent, rather than absolute joint positions) along with the kinematics adjacency matrix A as an explicit input. It outputs multiple plausible future trajectories of T_F frames in the same bone-direction representation. Because the adjacency matrix is an input rather than a fixed architectural constraint, the model natively accepts any number of joints and any graph topology, including partial skeletons with missing limbs, without retraining or retargeting.

## Method

EquiFusion is a latent diffusion model whose autoencoder and denoiser are built entirely from permutation-equivariant operations, ensuring the learned parameters are independent of the number of joints (formalized as Lemma 1 and Theorem 2). The core building blocks are a graph convolution (Eq. 3) that aggregates features using the degree-normalized adjacency matrix A, and a graph attention layer (Eq. 4) that computes Q, K, V through the same equivariant convolution, both treating joints as the token dimension rather than the time dimension. The kinematics connectivity A is passed as an explicit input at every layer, so the same weights handle any skeleton topology. A deterministic transformer autoencoder maps motions to a latent space where a diffusion denoiser generates future trajectories conditioned on the past embedding. The bone-direction motion parametrization (Eq. 5) guarantees physically consistent limb lengths by construction, eliminating the need for the network to learn this prior and enabling trivial handling of missing leaf joints.

## Results

On the zero-shot kinematics benchmark (trained on AMASS, tested on H36M), EquiFusion(A) achieves 7.86 cm ADE, 10.47 cm FDE, and 5.86° MAE, outperforming the best retargeted baseline SkelDiff (10.81 cm ADE, 14.99 cm FDE, 14.95° MAE) by up to 27% in precision. The multi-dataset variant EquiFusion(A+N) further improves to 7.71 cm ADE and 10.21 cm FDE. In terms of realism, EquiFusion achieves 7.06 FID and 0.69% stretching, compared to SkelDiff's 7.62 FID and 5.25% stretching, while delivering perfect body realism (0% stretching, 0% jitter) by design through its bone-direction parametrization. The model is 75% more compact than SkelDiff while halving training and inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquiFusion outperforms all seven baselines on every metric in the zero-shot kinematics setting (Table 1). Against the strongest diffusion-based competitor SkelDiff [19], EquiFusion(A) reduces ADE by 27% (7.86 vs. 10.81 cm), FDE by 30% (10.47 vs. 14.99 cm), and MAE by 61% (5.86° vs. 14.95°), while also achieving lower FID (7.06 vs. 7.62) and dramatically lower stretching (0.69% vs. 5.25%). The VAE-based DivSamp [21] achieves the highest diversity (APD 47.78) but at the cost of the worst realism (FID 47.78, str 5.63%), confirming the paper's observation that diversity and realism are often in tension. Among the earlier baselines, BeLFusion [7] and GSPS [64] are the closest in precision (ADE ~9.2–9.3 cm) but still trail EquiFusion by ~15–17% in ADE and ~28–30% in FDE. Critically, all baselines require a retargeting step [35] to operate on H36M's 17-joint skeleton, introducing an estimated upper-bound error of ~22 mm that EquiFusion avoids entirely by accepting the adjacency matrix as input. On the out-of-distribution MoYoga benchmark (Table 2), EquiFusion(A+N) further narrows the gap, achieving 0.492 m ADE and 7.10 CMD versus SkelDiff's 0.567 m ADE and 15.71 CMD.

## Relation to Prior Work

EquiFusion builds on the latent diffusion framework established by Rombach et al. and adapted to SHMP by BeLFusion and SkelDiff, but fundamentally departs from all prior SHMP methods by replacing hard-coded kinematic structure with an explicit adjacency matrix input and a permutation-equivariant architecture. While previous approaches (SkelDiff, BeLFusion, CoMusion, Motron) embed joint-specific weights or treat joints as feature channels in temporal transformers, EquiFusion draws on graph convolution and graph attention theory (Kipf & Welling; Veličković et al.) to ensure weights are shared across joints and invariant to their ordering. The paper also introduces a bone-direction motion representation as a middle ground between absolute 3D positions and rotation-based parametrizations, a choice not previously explored in SHMP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making kinematics an explicit input rather than a structural prior, EquiFusion breaks the single-dataset training barrier and enables a single model to handle any current or future skeleton configuration, including partial/occluded limbs, without retraining or lossy retargeting. This positions the work as a necessary step toward foundation models for human motion, where cross-dataset and cross-kinematics generalization is essential. Key limitations include the bone-direction representation's inability to handle non-leaf joint occlusions (e.g., missing elbow with present hand), and open questions about how heterogeneous motion distributions across datasets affect cross-dataset training quality. The finding that the equivariant architecture alone (not just additional data) drives most of the performance gain suggests the inductive bias is the critical ingredient, not data volume.
