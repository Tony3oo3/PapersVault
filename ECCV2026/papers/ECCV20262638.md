## Problem

Event-based small object detection is difficult because small or low-contrast targets produce sparse, fragmented, and intermittent event responses that are easily overwhelmed by background noise. Existing frame-based and SNN-based approaches model temporal continuity only implicitly through local aggregation or spiking dynamics, lacking an explicit propagation rule to preserve weak trajectories as coherent structures under strong clutter.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262638/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse asynchronous event streams voxelized at 1 ms temporal resolution within 8-second windows, represented as a sparse tensor with voxel indices (b, x, y, t) and normalized attributes (x̃, ỹ, t̃, polarity). It produces per-voxel binary segmentation masks for event-level small object detection, evaluated on the EV-UAV dataset where targets average only 6.8×5.4 pixels under complex UAV backgrounds.

## Method

PACT formulates event evolution as advection under a local velocity field, enforcing that features originating from the same moving entity remain approximately conserved along transport trajectories while noise triggers lose alignment. The encoder-decoder architecture comprises three coupled modules: T-FE extracts motion-aware features via multi-branch dilated sparse convolutions and predicts a bounded 2D displacement with a gating weight that fuses transported and observed features based on transport agreement; ATC estimates the velocity field and computes an advection residual as a consistency score; AFR propagates features along the velocity field in the decoder using K perturbed velocity hypotheses with consistency-aware softmax aggregation to connect fragmented responses into continuous trajectories. Training uses a joint objective combining binary cross-entropy segmentation loss with a Smooth-L1 velocity regularizer supervised by pseudo velocity targets constructed from sparse temporal matching of foreground voxels across neighboring time slices.

## Results

On the EV-UAV benchmark (147 sequences, 2.3M event-level annotations, average target size 6.8×5.4 px), PACT achieves 75.90% Temporal IoU, 80.05% ACC, 91.84% P_d, and 0.76×10⁻⁴ F_a, outperforming the strongest baseline EV-SpSegNet (55.18% IoU, 65.02% ACC) by 20.72% in IoU and 15.03% in accuracy. PACT uses only 2.9M parameters and 58 ms inference per 8 s window, compared to 34.0M parameters for EV-SpSegNet. The ablation confirms that the full T-FE + ATC + A-FR pipeline is necessary: removing any single component drops IoU by 4–21 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PACT outperforms all 14 baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest point-based baseline EV-SpSegNet (ICCV 2025), PACT gains +20.72 pp in Temporal IoU (75.90 vs 55.18), +15.03 pp in ACC (80.05 vs 65.02), +14.31 pp in P_d (91.84 vs 77.53), and halves F_a (0.76 vs 1.63 ×10⁻⁴), while using 11.7× fewer parameters (2.9M vs 34.0M). Among event-native detectors, the gap is even larger: RVT (CVPR 2023) reaches only 43.21% IoU and Spike-YOLO (ECCV 2024) 43.94%, both roughly 32 pp below PACT. Frame-based detectors (SSD, Faster R-CNN, DETR, YOLOv10) perform worst (25–33% IoU) because 50 ms event aggregation destroys the temporal structure needed for tiny targets. The ablation further shows that ATC alone on unstructured features actually increases F_a to 4.76×10⁻⁴, confirming that advection consistency requires the motion-aware substrate provided by T-FE to be beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

PACT departs from both dominant paradigms in event-based detection—frame aggregation pipelines and spiking neural networks—by introducing an explicit physics-based propagation rule rather than relying on implicit temporal continuity. Conceptually, it repurposes the advection equation from fluid dynamics and the contrast-maximization tradition in event vision (Benosman, Gallego, Zhu et al.) as a differentiable transport operator embedded within a sparse encoder-decoder architecture. The framework does not build on a single prior method but synthesizes ideas from advection physics, sparse convolution, and trajectory-consistency gating into a unified mechanism where feature evolution is constrained by local velocity fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The advection-consistency formulation offers an interpretable, physics-grounded criterion for separating weak but motion-coherent event responses from background noise, which is a persistent challenge in low-SNR event streams. The ablation revealing that advection constraints applied to unstructured features actively degrade performance (ATC alone drops IoU from 59.90 to 55.11) highlights that the physics prior is only effective when paired with motion-aware feature extraction—a coupled design insight with broader implications for physics-informed deep learning. A key open limitation is the single first-order velocity field, which fails in multi-target scenes with large inter-target velocity variation; extending the transport model to time-varying or multi-component motion fields is identified as necessary future work.
