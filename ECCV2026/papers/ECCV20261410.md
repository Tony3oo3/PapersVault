## Problem

Existing diffusion-based methods for inverse problems either require computationally expensive gradient backpropagation through the score network, rely on strong approximations of the posterior, or are restricted to linear measurement models. While restart-based sampling was recently shown to improve unconditional diffusion by combining ODE low-discretization-error with SDE error-contraction, no prior work extended this idea to posterior inference with a simple, general restart strategy applicable to both linear and non-linear problems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261410/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded measurement y ∈ R^m, a differentiable measurement operator h: R^n → R^m (which can be any linear or non-linear function, e.g., downsampling, masking, blur, Fourier magnitude, or a neural-network blur), and a pre-trained unconditional diffusion score network s_θ(x, σ). It outputs a single reconstructed image x_0 at 256×256 resolution. No retraining or task-specific model modification is required; the only task-specific hyperparameters are the restart noise level σ_restart, the number of gradient updates per ODE step, the learning rate, and the regularization weight λ.

## Method

RePS alternates between two stages: (1) solving a measurement-conditioned ODE for N_ode steps from the current noise level down to σ_0, and (2) restarting by adding Gaussian noise to return the sample to a higher noise level σ_r. Each ODE step uses Tweedie's formula to express the update in terms of E[X_0|x_t], then computes a MAP estimate x̂_{0|y} by minimizing a data-fidelity term ‖y − h(x)‖² plus a prior term ‖x − E[X_0|x_t]‖² via gradient descent in x_0 space—avoiding backpropagation through the score network entirely. The restart noise level σ_r is annealed from a task-specific σ_restart down to σ_min using a polynomial schedule, concentrating restarts at lower noise levels. Unlike prior restart-based methods (Li & Wang, DiffPIR) that take only a single ODE step per restart, RePS performs multiple conditioned ODE steps per restart, directly guiding the trajectory toward the posterior mode before each restart, which yields faster convergence and better reconstruction quality across both linear and non-linear inverse problems.

## Results

On FFHQ, RePS achieves the best overall PSNR across all five linear tasks: 29.95 (4× super-resolution), 24.32 (box inpainting), 32.49 (random inpainting), 29.92 (Gaussian deblur), and 32.27 (motion deblur), with corresponding LPIPS values of 0.155, 0.122, 0.090, 0.146, and 0.115. On ImageNet, RePS leads on four of five linear tasks (e.g., 26.12 PSNR / 0.259 LPIPS for 4× SR; 28.95 / 0.169 for motion deblur). For non-linear tasks, RePS achieves 30.41±4.45 PSNR on phase retrieval (FFHQ), 29.02±1.76 on non-linear deblur, and 27.96±3.54 on HDR reconstruction, outperforming DAPS, DPS, and DCDP on most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RePS outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of tasks. Against the closest competitor, Li & Wang [17], RePS wins on 4× SR, box inpainting (LPIPS), Gaussian deblur, and motion deblur on both datasets, but loses on random inpainting (32.49 vs. 34.11 PSNR on FFHQ; 28.97 vs. 29.67 on ImageNet). DAPS [36], identified as the strongest baseline, is beaten by RePS on all linear tasks on FFHQ and on four of five on ImageNet; the sole exception is phase retrieval on ImageNet, where DAPS achieves 22.32±6.51 PSNR versus RePS's 20.12±7.18. DPS [1] and DDRM [14] are consistently outperformed by large margins (e.g., 4+ dB PSNR on 4× SR ImageNet). Among non-linear baselines, RED-diff [19] outperforms RePS on non-linear deblur (30.86 vs. 29.02 PSNR on FFHQ), while RePS leads on phase retrieval and HDR. DCDP [18] and DiffPIR [37] are uniformly outperformed across all tasks where they are reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

RePS extends the restart-sampling idea from Xu et al. [35] (unconditional diffusion) to posterior inference, and unifies several recent plug-and-play diffusion methods—Li & Wang [17], DiffPIR [37], DAPS [36], and DCDP [18]—under a single two-dimensional design space (conditioning within vs. after ODE integration; number of ODE steps per restart). The key departure from prior work is that RePS performs multiple measurement-conditioned ODE steps between restarts, directly guiding the trajectory toward the posterior mode rather than first producing an unconstrained sample and then correcting it, and it does so without backpropagating through the score network (unlike DPS, DDNM, or DMPlug). This makes it applicable to arbitrary differentiable measurement operators, linear or non-linear, while retaining the low discretization error of ODE solvers and the error-contraction benefit of stochastic restarts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework gives practitioners a clear taxonomy of existing diffusion-based inverse-problem solvers and identifies a previously underexplored design axis (interleaved conditioning within the ODE), which RePS shows yields faster convergence and better reconstruction quality. The method's avoidance of score-network backpropagation makes it substantially cheaper than gradient-through-network approaches while matching or exceeding their quality, which is practically important for large-scale or iterative applications. Limitations include per-task tuning of the restart noise level σ_restart, residual gaps versus DAPS on certain non-linear tasks (e.g., phase retrieval on ImageNet), and the reliance on a MAP approximation rather than the true posterior mean. Open questions include whether the restart schedule can be made fully adaptive, whether the framework extends to 3D volumes or other modalities, and whether the conditioning-within-ODE design space can be further exploited with learned or data-driven restart policies.
