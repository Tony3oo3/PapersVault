## Problem

Egocentric and exocentric human motion capture from head-mounted devices have been studied in isolation: egocentric methods cannot faithfully reconstruct invisible body parts (e.g., lower body while sitting), while exocentric methods struggle with global motion ambiguity, body occlusions, and fast camera motion. No prior work exploits the natural multi-observer structure that arises when multiple subjects each wear an HMD, turning every participant into both a motion subject and a mobile camera of others.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the method consumes the observer's RGB image, both subjects' head positions and 6D orientations (from VIO SLAM), and optionally the wearer's left/right wrist poses (from wristbands or VR controllers), supporting both 1-point (head-only) and 3-point (head + wrists) tracking configurations. The output is a sequence of SMPL pose parameters: 21 joint rotations and root orientation in 6D representation, plus root 3D position recovered analytically via forward kinematics from the known head position rather than being directly predicted. The model does not predict SMPL shape parameters but accepts subject-specific or mean-identity shapes as input.

## Method

EgoExoMoCap first runs a lightweight EgoNet (MLP-Mixer temporal network) on egocentric signals to produce coarse SMPL poses, which are projected onto the observer's image to generate region proposals for ViTPose 2D keypoint detection. Detected 2D keypoints are unprojected into 3D rays using the observer's camera intrinsics and extrinsics, scaled by the inter-person head distance to resolve scale ambiguity, and transformed into the wearer's head-local frame to yield a canonical, rotation-invariant ray representation. A DINOv3 CLS token from the same crop feeds a small MLP that produces per-keypoint confidence scores, softly gating unreliable exocentric rays when the wearer is occluded or out of view. Per-frame Ego and Exo tokens are fused by a Spatial Transformer (retaining only the ego token output to enforce an egocentric inductive bias), followed by a 96-frame Temporal Transformer for smoothness, with two MLP heads decoding root orientation and joint rotations. Training is two-stage: EgoNet is first trained on egocentric signals alone, then the full fusion pipeline is trained end-to-end with EgoNet and DINOv3 frozen, using L1 losses on root orientation, joint rotations, and 3D joint positions.

## Results

On Nymeria (3-point tracking), EgoExoMoCap achieves 5.72 cm MPJPE, 2.72 cm upper-body error, and 10.05 cm lower-body error, outperforming all egocentric and exocentric baselines. Under 1-point tracking (head only), it reaches 8.28 cm MPJPE and 11.44 cm lower-body error. On EgoHumans, the method achieves 7.62 cm MPJPE (3-point) and 11.54 cm (1-point), with multi-observer fusion further reducing 3-point MPJPE to 7.11 cm and lower-body error to 8.87 cm. The only metric where a baseline wins is Jitter, where RPM achieves 1.29 (3-point) vs. EgoExoMoCap's 2.16, attributed to RPM's PCAF smoothness module.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoExoMoCap outperforms all four egocentric baselines (AvatarPoser, EgoPoser, EgoAllo, RPM) on MPJPE, upper-body, and lower-body position errors under both 3-point and 1-point tracking; for example, on Nymeria 3-point it reduces MPJPE from 7.74 cm (EgoPoser) to 5.72 cm and lower-body error from 13.96 cm to 10.05 cm. Against the exocentric baseline PromptHMR, the gap is even larger: 5.72 vs. 13.48 cm MPJPE (3-point), with the finetuned variant (10.65 cm) still well behind. The naive ego-exo fusion baseline (PromptHMR+EgoPoser) achieves 6.47 cm MPJPE but suffers higher MPJVE (17.49 vs. 12.77) and Jitter (3.08 vs. 2.16), confirming that the paper's learned gating and ray-based canonicalization yield both more accurate and smoother results. The sole exception is Jitter, where RPM's PCAF module produces the smoothest output (1.29 vs. 2.16), at the cost of substantially worse position accuracy (12.19 cm MPJPE). On EgoHumans, the same pattern holds, and multi-observer fusion further improves lower-body error from 13.64 cm (single observer) to 8.87 cm, demonstrating the scalability advantage of the distributed setup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously isolated paradigms—egocentric body tracking from HMDs (AvatarPoser, EgoPoser, EgoAllo, RPM) and exocentric monocular human motion estimation (PromptHMR, WHAM)—into a single distributed framework where each participant is simultaneously a motion subject and a mobile observer. The ray-based 3D representation is adapted from Ray3D [103], but the authors add camera-extrinsic decoupling, inter-person depth scaling, and ego-space canonicalization to handle the fast, frequent head motions of a handheld observer. The learned visibility gating via DINOv3 CLS tokens is a novel application of foundation-model features for per-keypoint confidence, replacing the brittle ViTPose confidence scores that fail under occlusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing motion capture to 'two people wearing smart glasses,' EgoExoMoCap removes the need for multi-camera rigs or mocap suits, making in-the-wild full-body motion data collection dramatically more accessible for embodied AI, VR/AR, and robotics. Key limitations remain: the system assumes factory-calibrated and time-synchronized HMDs, does not predict body shape, degrades under heavy mutual occlusion, and is not yet real-time. Open questions include whether stereo HMD cameras, 3D scene reconstruction, and physical-plausibility constraints (foot-ground contact, self-penetration) can close the remaining gap to studio-quality capture, and whether the approach extends to larger groups or longer unbounded sequences.
