## Problem

Unpaired image dehazing methods cast restoration as unsupervised domain translation but suffer from severe semantic hallucinations because the discriminator overfits to high-level semantics rather than low-level clarity, causing the generator to fabricate false content. Steering the discriminator toward low-level clarity in an unpaired setting is largely unexplored, and defining a clear objective for 'clarity' without paired supervision is non-trivial.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260270/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single hazy RGB image (256×256) as input and produces a dehazed RGB image of the same resolution. Training uses unpaired data (3577 clean and 2903 hazy images from RESIDE-Unpaired) with no pixel-level correspondence. At inference, only the dehazing generator G_{X→Y} is deployed, and the same framework generalizes to low-light image enhancement by swapping the degradation space.

## Method

DCL builds on a CycleGAN-Turbo backbone and introduces two components. First, Degradation-Shared Clarity Feature Learning (DCFL) formulates discriminator training as a multi-task problem: an Adversarial AutoAugment policy network dynamically mines diverse, under-fitted degradation variants (noise, rain, blur, etc.) by maximizing the discriminator's loss, and Pareto Optimization via MGDA extracts the shared descent direction across these degradation tasks, preventing overfitting to any single degradation type. Second, Enhance-to-Resist (E2R) applies a random high-frequency degradation to the generator's output and requires a pre-trained VAE texture encoder to still recover the original texture features, providing a self-supervised signal that forces the generator to embed robust, input-supported high-frequency details. The key insight is that explicit low-level degradations create strong surface-statistical shortcuts that suppress the discriminator's reliance on semantics, while the shared clarity direction across diverse degradations provides a well-defined, degradation-agnostic target for adversarial learning.

## Results

On the RTTS dataset, DCL achieves the best HazDesNet score (0.278, lower is better) among all methods, outperforming the next-best DehazeFormer (0.319) by 12.8%. On Fattal's dataset, DCL achieves the best scores across all four metrics: HazDesNet 0.098, CLIPIQA 0.587, MUSIQ 68.83, and MANIQA 0.438, surpassing the strongest baselines (D4+ and CycleGAN-Turbo) by substantial margins. On FoggyDriving (Person_Public), DCL leads with HazDesNet 0.193, CLIPIQA 0.408, MUSIQ 59.42, and MANIQA 0.322, again outperforming all competitors on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCL achieves the best HazDesNet (haze removal) score on all three test datasets, with the largest margin on Fattal (0.098 vs. D4+'s 0.115) and FoggyDriving Person_Public (0.193 vs. DehazeFormer/D4+'s 0.257). On Fattal, DCL dominates all four metrics, beating the strongest baseline D4+ by 14.5% on CLIPIQA (0.587 vs. 0.546) and 15.3% on MANIQA (0.438 vs. 0.380). On RTTS, DCL wins on HazDesNet but trails DehazeSB (0.377) and Diff-Dehazer (0.356) on CLIPIQA, and D4+ (0.289) on MANIQA, suggesting that perceptual quality metrics still favor methods with stronger generative priors in some settings. Among unpaired methods, DCL consistently outperforms CycleGAN-Turbo [39] (its backbone) on HazDesNet across all datasets (e.g., 0.278 vs. 0.372 on RTTS, 0.098 vs. 0.163 on Fattal), confirming that the degradation-guided discriminator and E2R module add substantial value over the base translation framework. Physical-prior methods (DehazeFormer, KANet, D4+) and hybrid diffusion-based methods (Diff-Dehazer, DehazeSB) all underperform DCL on haze removal extent, validating the paper's claim that rigid atmospheric-scattering assumptions limit real-world generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CycleGAN-Turbo unpaired translation backbone [39] but departs from it by reframing the discriminator's role: rather than letting it learn arbitrary domain statistics (which leads to semantic hallucinations), DCL explicitly injects low-level degradations to create surface-statistical shortcuts that the discriminator exploits instead of high-level semantics. The degradation mining mechanism repurposes Adversarial AutoAugment [56] as a policy for dynamically selecting hard degradation variants, while the shared-clarity extraction borrows the MGDA Pareto optimization framework [43] from multi-task learning and applies it to discriminator gradient updates—a use case not previously explored in image restoration. Conceptually, the work sits at the intersection of shortcut-learning theory (surface statistics vs. semantics) and multi-objective optimization, positioning itself against both physical-prior dehazing methods (D4, DehazeSB) and pure generative translation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and visually obvious failure mode of unpaired dehazing—semantic hallucination—by providing a principled mechanism (degradation-injected multi-task discriminator training) rather than ad-hoc dataset curation. The E2R self-supervised texture enhancement paradigm, validated on both dehazing and low-light enhancement, suggests the relative-clarity hypothesis may generalize to other restoration tasks. However, the theoretical guarantees rely on assumptions (local feature decomposition, zero-mean degradation features) that are hard to verify in practice, and the method has only been tested on haze and low-light; whether the degradation-agnostic clarity direction transfers to rain, blur, or motion artifacts remains open. The moderate quantitative gains over strong baselines (e.g., ~0.04 HazDesNet improvement on RTTS) suggest the approach is effective but not a dramatic breakthrough.
