## Problem

Precise 3D spatial orchestration in text-to-video generation remains difficult because existing control modalities present a trade-off: natural language is too imprecise for exact trajectories, while dense per-frame structural signals (depth maps, edge maps) are impractical to author for dynamic scenes with deformable objects. The paper tackles this by introducing a sparse, 3D-aware 'blocking' paradigm that decouples high-level choreography (layout, motion paths, timing) from fine-grained object deformations, letting the generative model infer the latter.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a sparse set of oriented 3D bounding boxes (center, scale, rotation per object per keyframe), and camera parameters (static or dynamic) as input; for editing mode, an input video is additionally provided. The 3D boxes are rendered into 2D DNOCS control frames via the specified camera, and the control video can be a per-frame mixture of DNOCS-only frames, input video segments, black (empty) frames, or spatially composited blends with gray masks for removal. Output is a photorealistic video (or edited video) consistent with the specified spatial layout, trajectories, occlusion ordering, and text description.

## Method

The key insight is to render oriented 3D boxes into image-space conditioning signals rather than tokenizing 3D parameters, since video DiTs operate in 2D and lack explicit 3D geometric reasoning. The proposed DNOCS encoding colors each box surface using a spherical color wheel derived from local normalized coordinates (encoding orientation) and modulates brightness by inverse depth with exponential falloff (encoding global depth ordering and occlusion), producing a single 3-channel signal that makes perspective, depth ordering, and occlusion directly observable. The control signal is injected via a ControlNet-style VACE pathway into a frozen Wan 2.2 DiT backbone, with only the VACE modules LoRA-fine-tuned (rank 64) for 10k steps on 4×H100 GPUs. Training data (~10K video pairs) is generated by an automated pipeline that extracts per-frame oriented 3D boxes from in-the-wild videos using GroundingDINO, SAM segmentation, monocular depth estimation, 3D box fitting, and Kalman filtering for temporal consistency.

## Results

On nuScenes, LooseControlVideo achieves a Trajectory Error of 5.79 px and Occlusion Accuracy of 92.69%, substantially outperforming the 2D-flow VACE baseline (7.86 px, 73.91%) and the 2D-box VACE-ft baseline (16.66 px, 42.45%). On HO-3D and BEHAVE, LCV further reduces TrajErr to 3.9 and 5.8 px respectively, with OcclAcc of 94.1% and 90.2%, while achieving the lowest Rigid Motion Consistency error (0.122 and 0.207) and Global Motion Field Agreement (0.045 and 0.062). Against the 3D-aware 3DTrajMaster baseline, LCV improves TrajErr by roughly 10–12× and OcclAcc by ~25 percentage points on both interaction datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VACE with 2D optical flow (both zero-shot and fine-tuned), LCV wins decisively on TrajErr (5.79 vs. 7.86/6.78 on nuScenes; 3.9 vs. 6.3/5.4 on HO-3D), OcclAcc (92.69 vs. 73.91/79.32), RMC (0.318 vs. 0.566/0.528), and GMFA (0.066 vs. 0.135/0.093), confirming that image-space flow lacks the 3D orientation and depth-ordering cues needed for multi-object choreography. Against VACE-ft with 2D bounding boxes, LCV trades a small Contain score (87.93 vs. 96.33 on nuScenes) for large gains in every motion and occlusion metric (TrajErr 5.79 vs. 16.66; OcclAcc 92.69 vs. 42.45; RMC 0.318 vs. 0.735), showing that axis-aligned 2D boxes cannot encode 6-DOF rotation or depth-ordered occlusion. Against 3DTrajMaster, the closest 3D-aware competitor, LCV achieves ~10–12× lower TrajErr (3.9 vs. 47.2 on HO-3D; 5.8 vs. 52.6 on BEHAVE) and ~25 pp higher OcclAcc (94.1 vs. 67.5; 90.2 vs. 64.8), demonstrating that full oriented-box rendering with DNOCS provides far stronger geometric grounding than trajectory-only 3D conditioning. In a 2-AFC user study (16 participants), LCV is preferred over Depth-only, Optical-Flow, and 2D-Box baselines in 78.1%, 87.5%, and 92.2% of pairwise comparisons respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ControlNet/VACE lineage of residual-injection conditioning for video diffusion by replacing dense 2D structural signals (depth maps, optical flow, 2D boxes) with rendered oriented 3D bounding boxes encoded via a novel DNOCS representation that fuses NOCS-style local orientation with global depth modulation. It departs from prior 3D-aware video control efforts (e.g., 3DTrajMaster, CameraCtrl) that either restrict to trajectory-only signals or limit entity count, by showing that a simple image-space rendering of 6-DOF boxes provides sufficient geometric prior for multi-object scenes with occlusion and interaction. The key conceptual departure is rejecting token-based 3D parameter injection (which the authors show fails to converge) in favor of amortizing the 3D-to-2D projection into the control signal itself, aligning the conditioning domain with the DiT's native 2D image space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'blocking' metaphor offers a practical, low-authoring-cost interface for directorial video control that decouples global choreography from local deformation, potentially lowering the barrier for non-expert users to author complex multi-object scenes. The automated training-data pipeline (GroundingDINO + SAM + monocular depth + 3D Kalman filtering) makes the approach reproducible without manual 3D annotation. Key open questions remain: identity-to-box assignment in multi-character scenes is unresolved, temporal rhythm still requires manual authoring, and whether the DNOCS encoding generalizes beyond the Wan 2.2 backbone to other DiT architectures is untested. The method's reliance on a ControlNet-style residual pathway also raises questions about how much of the improvement comes from the representation versus the fine-tuning procedure.
