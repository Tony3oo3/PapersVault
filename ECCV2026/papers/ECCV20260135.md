## Problem

Reconstructing physically plausible 3D human-scene interactions from a single image faces a fundamental speed-accuracy trade-off: optimization-based methods (e.g., PhySIC) enforce explicit contact but require ~20 s per instance, while feed-forward methods are fast yet perform no interaction reasoning, producing floating and interpenetration artifacts. What is missing is a learned HSI prior that resolves these geometric inconsistencies in a single feed-forward pass without costly test-time optimization.

## Input / Output

GRAFT takes a single RGB image and produces refined SMPL-X parameters (pose θ∈R¹⁶⁵, translation t∈R³, shape β∈R¹⁰) plus a 3D scene pointmap in a shared metric frame. The method operates iteratively over T=3 steps, predicting corrective deltas (Δθ, Δt, Δβ) and a uniform scale factor rather than absolute poses, and re-probes the scene point cloud after each update. It supports two modes: end-to-end (fusing MapAnything image features via cross-attention) and geometry-only (using only 3D point-cloud probes), the latter enabling plug-and-play refinement of any existing feed-forward method without retraining.

## Method

GRAFT amortizes geometry-based optimization into a recurrent transformer by predicting Interaction Gradients—learned corrective parameter updates that drive an implausible human-scene state toward a physically grounded configuration. The interaction state is encoded as 24 compact HSI tokens (21 body joints, 2 hands, 1 full-body), each grounded via Geometric Probes that retrieve nearest-neighbor scene points and compute relative offsets and surface normals, capturing ~500 geometric parameters versus ~4K–10K in prior HSI-prior representations. A 5-layer transformer alternates self-attention among HSI tokens (modeling inter-limb support-contact dependencies) with constrained cross-attention to multi-scale MapAnything features sampled at each token's 3D anchor, yielding a sparse connectivity pattern that tightly couples geometry with local visual evidence. Crucially, the loop is recurrent: after each decoded update, geometric probes are recomputed from the refined mesh, giving the network direct physical feedback that progressively resolves penetrations and establishes contact. Training uses per-step supervision over the full rollout trajectory with pose, vertex, and mean-normalized vertex losses, curriculum rollout, and visual-anchor dropout, without any explicit contact or interpenetration losses—these emerge implicitly from the geometric probe inputs and GT-pose regression.

## Results

On PROX-test, GRAFT achieves a contact F1 of 0.594, improving by 121.6% over Human3R (0.268) and 151.3% over UniSH (0.236), while matching or exceeding the optimization-based PhySIC (F1 0.479) at ~100× lower runtime (0.2 s vs. 20 s). On RICH-100, GRAFT reaches F1 0.578 (vs. PhySIC 0.606, Human3R 0.368) with the best PA-MPJPE among feed-forward methods (46.32 mm). In geometry-only plug-in mode, GRAFT boosts Human3R's contact F1 from 0.268→0.387 on PROX-test and 0.368→0.479 on RICH-100 without retraining. A three-way user study (n=53, 912 responses) shows GRAFT preferred in 64.8% of trials.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against feed-forward baselines, GRAFT dominates on all interaction metrics: on PROX-test it improves contact F1 by 121.6% over Human3R (0.594 vs. 0.268) and 151.3% over UniSH (0.594 vs. 0.236), while also achieving the best PA-MPJPE (49.73 mm) among all feed-forward methods. Against the optimization-based PhySIC, GRAFT wins on PROX-test (F1 0.594 vs. 0.479; Precision 0.556 vs. 0.550) but trails slightly on RICH-100 (F1 0.578 vs. 0.606), with the gap attributed to RICH's mixed indoor/outdoor domain differing from GRAFT's training distribution. Compared to PROX and HolisticMesh, GRAFT is substantially faster (0.2 s vs. 73 s and 5 min respectively) while achieving far superior contact F1 (0.594 vs. 0.219 and 0.383 on PROX-test). In geometry-only plug-in mode, GRAFT applied atop Human3R improves F1 by 44% on PROX-test (0.268→0.387) and 30% on RICH-100 (0.368→0.479) without any retraining, confirming its utility as a transferable HSI prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRAFT synthesizes three research threads: (1) optimization-based HSI methods (PROX, PhySIC) that explicitly reason about contact but are slow; (2) feed-forward human-scene reconstructors (Human3R, UniSH) that are fast but skip interaction reasoning entirely; and (3) iterative refinement architectures from optical flow and human pose (RAFT, ReFit, WiLoR, LVD) that amortize multi-step correction into recurrent networks. The paper's conceptual departure is to treat the human-scene relationship as a corrective problem—given a misaligned state, predict the interaction gradient—rather than a generative one, and to use geometric probes (nearest-neighbor scene points with normals) as the recurrent feedback signal. This makes it the first work to apply iterative refinement specifically to 3D human-scene interaction, and the first to demonstrate that a compact 24-token geometric representation suffices as a transferable HSI prior without visual features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRAFT breaks the long-standing speed-accuracy tradeoff in HSI reconstruction, matching optimization-based interaction quality at ~100× lower runtime, which makes physically plausible human-scene reconstruction viable for real-time applications (embodied AI, AR, sports analytics). The geometry-only plug-and-play mode—improving Human3R's contact F1 by 44% without retraining or image access—is a practically important capability that could be adopted by any existing feed-forward pipeline. Key limitations include dependence on upstream scene/human estimation quality, a rigid-scene assumption in the nearest-point probes (deformable surfaces unmodeled), and training on pseudo-labeled data that bounds absolute accuracy. Open questions include whether richer training data (beyond 75k InHabitants images) closes the remaining gap to optimization methods, and whether the geometric-probe formulation extends to deformable or dynamic scenes.
