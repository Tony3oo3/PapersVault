## Problem

Autoregressive in-context imitation learning methods discretize continuous robot actions into tokens and predict them sequentially, causing compounding prediction errors that are especially severe under the distribution shift between seen and unseen task configurations. While flow-matching policies can model continuous actions in parallel and mitigate this error accumulation, incorporating in-context learning into a flow-matching framework for robot manipulation remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The model consumes current multi-view RGB images (224×224, encoded by SigLIP), the current proprioceptive state, a text instruction, and a multimodal in-context demonstration consisting of 8 subsampled image frames, 128 proprioceptive states, and 128 actions from a single expert trajectory. The output is a continuous action chunk of horizon H (e.g., joint-space or end-effector commands), generated by integrating a learned velocity field over 10 forward Euler steps from a Gaussian prior. Unlike autoregressive baselines that emit one discrete token at a time, the entire action chunk is produced in parallel, and the in-context demonstration is compressed into a fixed-size set of latent tokens before conditioning the action expert.

## Method

ContextFlow adopts a mixture-of-experts architecture with a context expert (300M parameters, Gemma-style transformer) and an action expert (initialized from π₀, fine-tuned with LoRA rank 32). Three perceiver-style multimodal context compressors—separate for images, states, and actions—use alternating cross-attention and self-attention layers over 32 learnable query tokens to distill long, redundant demonstration sequences into compact fixed-size latent representations, reducing the quadratic attention cost of encoding hundreds of timesteps. The context expert encodes the current observation, text tokens, and compressed demonstration latents, while the action expert refines noisy action tokens through K=10 flow-matching steps, attending to context tokens via a block-wise causal attention mask that permits caching context representations across steps. Training minimizes a conditional flow-matching loss with a Beta(1.5, 1) time sampling distribution, and the model is trained for 20K iterations with a cosine LR schedule, fully fine-tuning the vision encoder and context expert while applying LoRA to the action expert.

## Results

On LIBERO unseen task configurations, ContextFlow achieves 73.5% average success rate (64.0% on LIBERO-Spatial, 83.0% on LIBERO-Object), outperforming the autoregressive ICRT baseline by 35 percentage points and matching the task-specific fine-tuned π₀ (FT) at 72.5% without any fine-tuning. On real-world ALOHA tasks, ContextFlow achieves 40% success on a new pen-uncapping bimanual configuration and 40–50% on single-arm pick-and-place, while ICRT succeeds in only 2/10 trials on the easiest single-arm task and 0/10 on all bimanual tasks. The context compressor ablation shows a 9.5-point gain over ContextFlow-Plain, and removing proprioceptive states/actions from the in-context demonstration causes a 44.5-point drop on unseen configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ICRT [11], the primary autoregressive in-context imitation baseline, ContextFlow wins decisively: 73.5% vs. 38.5% average success on LIBERO unseen configurations (+35 points), and on real-world ALOHA tasks ContextFlow achieves 40–60% success across single-arm and bimanual configurations while ICRT scores 0/10 on all bimanual tasks and only 2/10 on the easiest single-arm task. Against π₀ [4] (text-prompted, no in-context demos), ContextFlow improves by 29 points (73.5% vs. 44.5%), and critically matches the fine-tuned variant π₀ FT (73.5% vs. 72.5%) without requiring any task-specific fine-tuning, avoiding catastrophic forgetting. Against OpenVLA-OFT [17], ContextFlow outperforms by 33.5 points (73.5% vs. 40.0%), with the gap driven largely by LIBERO-Object where OpenVLA-OFT scores only 17.0% versus ContextFlow's 83.0%. The paper also introduces ContextAR as a controlled autoregressive baseline sharing the same vision backbone and π₀ initialization; ContextFlow-Plain (flow matching, no compressor) beats ContextAR by 10.5 points, isolating the benefit of the flow-matching paradigm itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

ContextFlow extends the π0 vision-language-action flow model by adding in-context demonstration conditioning, while departing from the dominant autoregressive paradigm (ICRT, LipVQ-VAE) that discretizes continuous actions into tokens for next-token prediction. The key architectural departure is replacing sequential token decoding with conditional flow matching over continuous action chunks, motivated by the compounding-error problem under distribution shift. Perceiver-style context compressors are adapted from vision-language pretraining (BLIP-2, Perceiver) to distill long multimodal demonstrations into fixed-size latent tokens, a repurposing not previously applied to in-context robot imitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that ContextFlow matches a task-specific fine-tuned VLA (π0 FT) on unseen configurations without any fine-tuning, enabling faster and more flexible adaptation without catastrophic forgetting. However, generalization remains limited to unseen objects and spatial arrangements within shared manipulation primitives, not fundamentally different task types; scaling training data diversity (e.g., adding LIBERO-90) only partially addresses this. Open questions include whether the flow-matching advantage persists at larger model scales, whether the perceiver compressors generalize to substantially longer or more heterogeneous demonstrations, and whether the approach transfers to tasks requiring fundamentally different motor primitives.
