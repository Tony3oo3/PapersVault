## Problem

World models for complex, human-like embodiments operate in high-dimensional joint-action spaces (48 dimensions for 15 joints), causing search-based planners like CEM to scale poorly and produce suboptimal solutions. This paper asks whether a low-level world model can be made tractable for planning by lifting it to a lower-dimensional, visually grounded action space without modifying the world model itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261813/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a context of K+1 egocentric RGB frames, a corresponding sequence of 48-dim body poses (pelvis position + Euler angles for 15 joints), and an 8-dim high-level action vector encoding 2D image-space waypoints for four leaf joints (pelvis, head, left hand, right hand). It outputs a sequence of T low-level joint actions (T×48), which are fed autoregressively into the frozen PEVA world model to produce T future observation frames. Waypoints can be partially masked (sparse input), and at inference they are either specified manually or discovered by CEM search over the 8-dim space.

## Method

The core contribution is a Lifted World Model (LWM) that composes a lightweight goal-conditioned diffusion policy with a frozen low-level world model (PEVA). The policy, built on a diffusion policy backbone augmented with a DINOv3 image encoder, pose context, 3D positional embeddings, and waypoint masking during training, maps a single 8-dim waypoint action to a sequence of 48-dim joint actions. Planning then runs CEM in the 8-dim waypoint space rather than the 48-dim joint space, reducing both dimensionality and effective sequence length. Ground-truth waypoints for training are computed by forward-kinematics projection of the goal pose onto the current observation frame. The key insight is that 2D image-space waypoints are low-dimensional, visually interpretable, and well-suited to search, while the policy captures short-term motion patterns (locomotion, reaching, grasping) that make the lifted model generalize to unseen environments.

## Results

On 128 hybrid navigation + interaction tasks from Nymeria, Lifted CEM (2D waypoints) achieves a leaf-joint MJE of 0.411 m and all-joint MJE of 0.374 m, reducing the initial distance (0.704 m) by 33 cm. In contrast, PEVA CEM searching directly in 48-dimensional joint space reduces the initial distance by only 8.8 cm (all-joint MJE 0.616 m), a 3.8× smaller improvement. Lifted CEM also outperforms policy-only baselines (Diffusion Policy: 0.585 m all-joint MJE; HDP: 0.512 m) and generalizes to unseen environments with only a 0.029 m increase in all-joint MJE (0.333 → 0.362 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lifted CEM (2D) decisively outperforms PEVA CEM [3], which searches directly in the 48-dimensional joint-action space: 0.411 m vs. 0.637 m leaf MJE (a 35% reduction in absolute error), and the improvement over initial distance is 3.8× larger (33 cm vs. 8.8 cm). Against the policy-only baselines, Lifted CEM also wins: Diffusion Policy [7] achieves 0.605 m leaf MJE and HDP [24] achieves 0.540 m, both substantially worse than Lifted CEM's 0.411 m, even though HDP is explicitly trained on the goal-observation-conditioned planning task. The 2D waypoint formulation also outperforms its 3D-depth-augmented variant (0.411 vs. 0.453 m leaf MJE), confirming that the lower-dimensional search space is more effective for CEM. In the unseen-environment setting (Table 5), Lifted CEM with a held-out policy still achieves 0.362 m all-joint MJE, beating PEVA CEM (0.553 m) by a wide margin, demonstrating that the lightweight policy generalizes without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper composes a lightweight diffusion-based policy (building on Diffusion Policy and NoMaD's goal-conditioned architecture) with the frozen PEVA world model to create a 'Lifted World Model' that accepts low-dimensional waypoint actions instead of 48-dimensional joint displacements. The lifting concept is inspired by hierarchical motor control in neuroscience (visuospatial goals in parietal cortex translated to joint commands by motor cortex), and the waypoint action space departs from prior goal-conditioned approaches (NoMaD, Diffusion Policy) that use high-dimensional goal images. For planning, the paper applies CEM in the reduced 8-dimensional waypoint space rather than the 48-dimensional joint space, extending the standard world-model planning pipeline (PlaNet, TD-MPC2, Dreamer) to a setting where naive search is intractable due to action dimensionality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The lifting framework offers a practical, modular recipe for making high-dimensional world models plannable without retraining them, which is important as world models scale to richer embodiments. The finding that goal observations carry almost no useful information for egocentric agents (only 1.3 cm MJE reduction vs. 8.8 cm for waypoints) challenges the common practice of using goal images as conditioning signals in first-person settings. Limitations include a fixed 8-step policy horizon, dependence on a single embodiment (XSens 15-joint), and evaluation on one dataset (Nymeria); open questions include whether multi-level lifting, alternative high-level action spaces, or learned (rather than hand-designed) abstractions can further improve planning for longer horizons and more complex tasks.
