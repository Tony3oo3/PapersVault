## Problem

Existing dance generation methods produce movements that resemble improvisation rather than professional choreography, because they overlook the structural principles choreographers actually use: organizing motion into independent phrases, aligning movements with musical beats and energy, and applying intentional stylistic variation. HiChor addresses this by grounding generation in these choreographic primitives, producing full-length choreography with phrase-level diversity, music-choreography alignment, and semantic text control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261195/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pop music track as primary input, which is segmented into N phrases of 4 beats each using an All-in-One Analyzer; musical features (onset strength, RMS energy, and 4800-dim Jukebox deep features) are extracted per phrase via Librosa. An optional semantic text prompt (e.g., 'Climax of world tour') is decomposed by Mistral-7B into discrete feature-intensity targets. The output is a full-length 3D motion sequence in SMPL pose parameters (72-dim per frame), structured as concatenated phrase-level choreography with smooth inter-phrase transitions, typically exceeding 200 seconds for a complete song.

## Method

HiChor generates choreography through three hierarchical stages, each implemented as a transformer-based diffusion model: (1) beat-aware generation from pure noise per phrase, (2) energy-aware enhancement, and (3) global plausibility enhancement on the concatenated sequence. Stages 2 and 3 use the ChoreoEdit pipeline (inspired by SDEdit), which noises a reference choreography to a controllable timestep t and then denoises under a new target condition, trading off preservation of prior structure against injection of new musical features. Training incorporates feature-aware Pearson correlation losses that enforce empirically observed music-choreography relationships (beat↔acceleration, energy↔velocity/kinetic energy) alongside standard reconstruction and physical plausibility terms. For semantic text stylization, an LLM decomposes user text into six feature targets at discrete intensity levels, which are mapped through a pre-computed music-choreography correlation matrix to modulate the ChoreoEdit timesteps, controlling how strongly beat-driven versus energy-driven characteristics are emphasized.

## Results

On the EvalPop dataset, HiChor achieves the best scores across all seven metrics: FID_k of 45.32, FID_g of 18.20, Phrase Diversity of 6.44, Beat Alignment of 0.366, Energy Alignment (Phrase) of 0.642, Energy Alignment (Global) of 0.558, and Text Stylization Score of 0.85. These values approach the Ground Truth choreography (Phrase Div. 8.63, Beat 0.542, Energy Phrase 0.692, Energy Global 0.711) while substantially outperforming all generative baselines. The FID scores indicate that the added structural constraints do not degrade motion naturalness relative to state-of-the-art diffusion-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiChor outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest dance-generation baseline MEGADance, HiChor improves FID_k by 14.94 (45.32 vs. 60.26), Phrase Diversity by 0.15 (6.44 vs. 6.29), Beat Alignment by 0.089 (0.366 vs. 0.277), and Energy Alignment (Phrase) by 0.039 (0.642 vs. 0.603). Against LODGE, the closest long-sequence diffusion baseline, HiChor gains 19.8 in FID_k (45.32 vs. 65.12) and 0.079 in Beat Alignment (0.366 vs. 0.287), while also surpassing it on both energy alignment metrics. For text stylization, HiChor scores 0.85, exceeding TM2D (0.60) by 0.25 and SmooDi (0.55) by 0.30, confirming that the LLM-based semantic decomposition and timestep modulation produce feature changes more consistent with professional choreographer references. Notably, HiChor's FID_g (18.20) is comparable to EDGE (18.08) and LODGE (18.22), showing that the hierarchical structure does not sacrifice geometric naturalness.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiChor extends the SDEdit editing paradigm to the choreography domain via the ChoreoEdit pipeline, applying it hierarchically across beat, energy, and plausibility stages rather than as a single edit step. It departs from prior music-conditioned dance generation (FACT, LODGE, BeatIt, EDGE) by explicitly grounding generation in choreographic theory—phrase-level independence, beat/energy alignment, and inter-phrase continuity—rather than treating the output as a flat motion sequence. The text stylization module connects to the motion stylization line (SMooDi, TM2D) but replaces direct style transfer with LLM-decomposed feature targets that modulate diffusion timesteps, and the feature-aware Pearson correlation loss draws on Laban Movement Analysis to formalize music–motion coupling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes dance generation as a structured choreography problem and provides the first full-length pop-music evaluation benchmark (EvalPop) with metrics tied to choreographic principles, addressing a gap where prior datasets only cover short or genre-specific clips. However, the evaluation set is small (10 songs, 4 dancers), training relies solely on AIST++, and the authors acknowledge the output does not yet match professional performance quality. Open questions include whether the hierarchical SDEdit approach generalizes beyond pop music, whether scaling EvalPop into a training set closes the quality gap, and whether the LLM-based stylization transfers to more nuanced or multi-attribute style descriptions.
