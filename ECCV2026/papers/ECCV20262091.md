## Problem

Synchronized exo-ego video pairs introduce abrupt spatio-temporal and geometric discontinuities at the viewpoint transition, violating the smooth-motion assumptions underlying standard video diffusion models. Prior Exo2Ego methods treat the task as a direct condition-output mapping, which struggles to produce temporally coherent outputs across the large viewpoint and pose gap. This paper identifies the synchronization-induced jump as the central obstacle rather than a conditioning-quality issue.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262091/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single exocentric video (9 frames at 256×256, sampled with a skip-frame step of 4 to satisfy WFLF's 4n+1 constraint) and per-frame camera poses for both the (typically static) exocentric camera and the egocentric camera, encoded as per-pixel Plücker embeddings. It produces a 9-frame egocentric video plus a 9-frame interpolated transition segment, both generated in a single joint denoising pass. No text prompts, 3D priors, multi-view exo inputs, or hand-object interaction labels are required.

## Method

Syn2Seq-Forcing reframes Exo2Ego as sequential signal modeling by concatenating exocentric frames, a pseudo-interpolated transition segment, and egocentric frames into one continuous sequence S = [X, I, G], with a correspondingly interpolated pose stream P = (P_x, P_i, P_g). The transition frames I are pre-generated by a frozen WFLF interpolator (WAN2.2 Lightning 8-step LoRA in FLF2V mode) conditioned on the last exo frame and first ego frame, while poses are interpolated via Slerp (rotation) and linear interpolation (translation). The sequence is modeled with a Diffusion Forcing Transformer using per-frame independent noise levels and Plücker pose embeddings, trained in two stages: pretraining on 356k direct exo-ego pairs, then category-specific finetuning on 40k clips where each step randomly selects either the Exo→Interp or Interp→Ego sub-task. At inference, the model conditions on clean exo frames and denoises a 2T-length noise suffix to jointly produce both the transition and ego segments. A key empirical finding is that video-only interpolation (with ego poses and zero-filled transition poses) already yields large gains over direct generation, confirming that spatio-temporal discontinuity—not pose geometry—is the dominant bottleneck.

## Results

On the Ego-Exo4D benchmark, Syn2Seq-Forcing achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all three evaluated categories (Health, Bike, Cooking). The headline gains are most pronounced in SSIM: 0.5728 on Health, 0.4721 on Bike, and 0.4531 on Cooking, surpassing the strongest baseline (Exo2EgoSyn) by 0.09, 0.08, and 0.05 respectively. In PSNR, the method reaches 16.71 (Health), 15.63 (Bike), and 14.39 (Cooking), outperforming Exo2EgoSyn by 1.09, 0.50, and 0.49 dB. LPIPS scores are also consistently the lowest (best), e.g., 0.4828 on Health vs. 0.4991 for Exo2EgoSyn.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Syn2Seq-Forcing outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three categories. The closest competitor is Exo2EgoSyn [31] (same group), which uses a single predicted egocentric frame and per-frame camera control on a pretrained video generator; Syn2Seq-Forcing beats it by ~1.1 dB PSNR and ~0.09 SSIM on Health, and by ~0.5 dB PSNR and ~0.08 SSIM on Bike. The general-purpose camera-controlled video generators — TrajectoryCrafter [51], Wan Fun Control [1], and Wan VACE [19] — trail significantly, with PSNR gaps of 1.5–3.1 dB and SSIM gaps of 0.09–0.15, confirming that standard video diffusion models struggle with the abrupt exo-to-ego viewpoint jump. Notably, the paper excludes the interpolated transition frames from metric computation to ensure a fair comparison with methods that cannot produce such transitions, meaning the reported ego-only numbers already reflect the benefit of the sequential formulation. Two additional prior Exo2Ego methods (Exo2Ego-V [26] requiring 4 exo views, and EgoExo-Gen [48] with no public code) are omitted from quantitative comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Diffusion Forcing Transformer (DFoT) framework for flexible-history video diffusion and the WAN2.2/WFLF latent diffusion model as a frozen interpolator, but departs fundamentally from the condition-output paradigm used by prior Exo2Ego methods (Exo2EgoSyn, PMYS, Exo2Ego-V, EgoExo-Gen). Rather than conditioning a generator on the exo video and poses to predict the ego video in one shot, it reframes the problem as sequential signal modeling: the exo, interpolated, and ego segments form a single continuous sequence where past frames condition future frames. This also sidesteps the reliance on explicit 3D priors or multi-view exocentric inputs that characterize most prior approaches, using interpolation as a lightweight proxy for depth reasoning instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that video-frame interpolation alone (without pose interpolation) captures most of the performance gain reframes the core difficulty of Exo2Ego generation as spatio-temporal discontinuity rather than geometric mismatch, which could redirect future work toward transition-aware modeling. The unified sequential formulation naturally extends to Ego2Exo and potentially longer multi-view sequences, offering a general scaffold for cross-view synthesis. However, the evaluation is limited to 9 frames across three Ego-Exo4D categories with a single exocentric view, and the frozen WFLF interpolator introduces a quality ceiling that joint end-to-end training might lift. Open questions include whether the interpolation-as-depth-proxy strategy generalizes to highly dynamic scenes or longer horizons where accumulated errors could compound.
