## Problem

Most state-of-the-art 3D generative models rely on diffusion or flow-matching formulations that refine scenes holistically through iterative denoising, making incremental construction, structured completion, and open-ended outpainting less natural. Autoregressive transformers have proven highly effective for structured sequence modeling in language and vision but remain comparatively underexplored for 3D scene synthesis, where the lack of a canonical ordering and the need to jointly capture geometry, appearance, and spatial coherence pose unique challenges.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model starts from a single BOS token; for completion or outpainting, it consumes the serialized token sequence of a partial scene as a prefix prompt. Internally, 3D Gaussian primitives (position, opacity, size, rotation, color) are projected into a sparse voxel grid at 20 cm resolution, compressed by a sparse 3D CNN, and discretized via lookup-free quantization into a codebook of 4,096 entries. The model operates on spatial chunks with a 16,384-token context window, and generation proceeds by alternating position-token and feature-token predictions until an EOS token is emitted, with temperature 0.9 and nucleus sampling (p=0.9).

## Method

GaussianGPT compresses 3D Gaussian scenes into discrete latent grids using a sparse 3D convolutional autoencoder with lookup-free quantization (LFQ), then serializes the grid via a fixed xyz column-wise traversal into interleaved position and feature tokens with separate vocabularies. A GPT-2-based causal transformer (built on the nanochat backbone) models the joint distribution of occupancy and features through next-token prediction, with 3D rotary positional encoding that encodes actual voxel coordinates in the attention mechanism rather than 1D sequence indices, plus a 4th rotary dimension to disentangle position from feature tokens. Training uses standard cross-entropy with teacher forcing, masking invalid vocabulary entries at each step. The autoregressive formulation natively supports scene completion (partial tokens as prefix), large-scale outpainting (sliding-window context with backtracking resampling of empty columns), and controllable sampling via temperature, all within a single model without architectural changes.

## Results

On unconditional chair generation (PhotoShape), GaussianGPT achieves the best FID (5.68), KID (1.835), and Coverage (67.40) among all compared methods, while remaining competitive on MMD (4.278 vs. L3DG's 4.241). On scene chunk generation (3D-FRONT), GaussianGPT outperforms L3DG on appearance (FID 94.85 vs. 100.98), layout quality (FID 84.14 vs. 93.84), and completion consistency (CLIP-Sim 0.851 vs. 0.843 at 50% context), though L3DG retains an edge in geometric diversity (COV 0.692 vs. 0.548) for unconditional generation. For scene completion, the gap narrows and reverses in GaussianGPT's favor as more context is provided, with MMD dropping to 0.057 at 50% context versus L3DG's 0.084.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against L3DG (the closest prior method using the same Gaussian representation), GaussianGPT achieves substantially better FID (5.68 vs. 8.49) and KID (1.835 vs. 3.147) on shape generation, indicating cleaner and more faithful renderings, while L3DG retains a marginal advantage in MMD (4.241 vs. 4.278), reflecting slightly better geometric diversity. On scene-level tasks, the trade-off is more nuanced: L3DG produces more geometrically diverse unconditional samples (COV 0.692 vs. 0.548), but GaussianGPT dominates on appearance, layout, and completion metrics, with the advantage growing as more context is provided (e.g., MMD 0.057 vs. 0.084 at 50% completion). Against diffusion-based methods (DiffRF, EG3D) and GAN-based methods (π-GAN), GaussianGPT achieves clear and consistent wins across all four metrics on shape synthesis, with FID improvements of 2.4–47 points. The paper positions its autoregressive formulation as complementary rather than strictly superior: it trades some geometric diversity for cleaner structure, stronger appearance/layout quality, and unique capabilities (completion, outpainting, temperature-controlled sampling) that diffusion baselines do not natively support.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussianGPT builds directly on the L3DG autoencoder architecture (sparse 3D CNN encoder-decoder for structured Gaussian grids) and replaces its standard vector quantization with lookup-free quantization (LFQ) for improved codebook utilization. Its key conceptual departure is from the dominant diffusion/flow-matching paradigm in 3D generation, instead framing scene synthesis as next-token prediction over a serialized latent grid using a GPT-2-style causal transformer. The 3D rotary positional encoding and separate position/feature vocabularies are novel adaptations that inject spatial inductive bias into the autoregressive formulation, distinguishing it from prior autoregressive 3D work that focused on meshes or octrees rather than full Gaussian scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that autoregressive transformers are a viable and complementary alternative to diffusion for structured 3D scene generation, unlocking natural support for scene completion, outpainting, and temperature-controlled sampling within a single model. The trade-off observed—stronger appearance and layout quality at the cost of geometric diversity compared to L3DG—suggests the two paradigms capture different aspects of the scene distribution and may be combined in future work. Open questions include whether the approach scales to real-world (noisy, incomplete) data, how to mitigate quality degradation in large-scale outpainting, and whether the fixed xyz chunking can be replaced with more flexible spatial orderings for arbitrarily shaped scenes.
