## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods for vision-language models implicitly assume that adapting every test sample is beneficial, yet per-sample analysis under TPT reveals that over 90% of adaptations are either negligible (prediction unchanged) or harmful (correct prediction flipped to incorrect). This raises the question of whether ineffective adaptations can be identified and skipped at the per-sample level to reduce computational overhead while preserving or improving accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260579/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and generates N=64 augmented views via AugMix, for which CLIP's visual encoder and text encoder (using the template 'a photo of a [class]') produce zero-shot probability distributions over K classes. The output is a binary adapt/skip decision determined by comparing a CAS score against a threshold γ, followed by either the adapted class label (from the TTA framework) or the zero-shot class label. The threshold γ directly controls the skip ratio, allowing practitioners to tune the efficiency-accuracy trade-off at deployment time.

## Method

The paper introduces Cross-Augmentation Similarity (CAS), a training-free scoring function that measures prediction agreement across test-time augmented views to decide whether adaptation is needed. Given the high-confidence set S of augmented views (selected by a percentile entropy threshold ρ), CAS computes a weighted sum of cosine similarities between each augmented view's zero-shot probability vector and the original view's probability vector, gated by an indicator that the predicted labels match. Views with higher distributional similarity to the original prediction receive larger normalized weights, so the final score reflects both label consistency and distributional alignment. A higher CAS score indicates the prediction is already stable across views, so the sample is skipped and the zero-shot prediction is used directly; a lower score triggers the full TTA adaptation pipeline. CAS is plug-and-play, requiring no additional training or architectural changes, and is validated across four TTA frameworks (TPT, R-TPT, STS, ZERO) and multiple augmentation strategies.

## Results

CAS achieves an average AUC of 89.42% under TPT and 92.62% under ZERO across ImageNet and its four variants, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline MCM (57.16% and 61.06% AUC, respectively). In terms of AEP, CAS attains 62.44% (TPT) to 63.90% (STS) average across the five ImageNet benchmarks, matching or slightly exceeding full-adaptation accuracy while skipping ~85% of samples. On ImageNet specifically, CAS with ZERO reaches 94.58% AUC and 69.36% AEP, a 30.36-point AUC improvement over MCM. On fine-grained datasets (10 benchmarks), CAS achieves an average AUC of 87.97% (TPT) and 91.25% (ZERO), again far exceeding MCM (67.90% and 67.02%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAS is compared against two OOD-detection-based selection strategies: Energy [Liu et al., NeurIPS 2020], which uses a logit-based energy score, and MCM [Ming et al., NeurIPS 2022], which measures confidence via visual–textual feature alignment. CAS decisively outperforms both on AUC across all four TTA frameworks and all five ImageNet benchmarks: for example, under TPT on ImageNet, CAS reaches 91.10% AUC versus 57.27% (MCM) and 50.77% (Energy), a gap of roughly 34 and 40 points respectively. On AEP the margins are smaller but consistent—CAS improves average AEP by 0.8–1.5 points over MCM and 1.1–1.6 points over Energy across the ImageNet family. The advantage is most pronounced under STS (92.32% vs. 57.88% AUC) and ZERO (92.62% vs. 56.89% AUC), where the training-free or lightweight adaptation leaves more room for selective skipping to help. On fine-grained datasets the pattern holds: CAS achieves 87.97–91.42% average AUC versus 67.02–67.90% for MCM and 60.46–61.69% for Energy, confirming that the cross-augmentation similarity signal is far more informative than global confidence scores for identifying which samples truly benefit from adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the TPT framework for test-time prompt tuning of CLIP, repurposing its augmented views and confidence-selection mechanism as the substrate for a new scoring function. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant TTA efficiency literature (TTL, STS) that accelerates the adaptation process itself, instead asking whether adaptation should be performed at all per sample. It also draws on OOD detection scoring functions (Energy, MCM) as baselines and distinguishes itself from selective classification, which rejects predictions after inference rather than before adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that over 90% of TTA adaptations are negligible or harmful challenges the implicit assumption in the field that adaptation is universally beneficial, and the 3× speedup while maintaining or improving accuracy makes this practically relevant for deployment. The paper explicitly positions CAS as a simple baseline and invites the community to develop stronger scoring functions, leaving open questions about extending selective adaptation beyond episodic TTA, beyond image classification, and to larger model backbones. A key limitation is that all experiments use only ViT-B/16, so generalization to larger or differently-architected VLMs remains unverified.
