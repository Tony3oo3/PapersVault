## Problem

Prototype networks (e.g., ProtoPNet) suffer from prototype collapse, where multiple learned prototypes degenerate into nearly identical vectors attending to the same discriminative region, undermining compositional interpretability. The authors show this is not merely an architectural flaw but a geometric inevitability driven by Neural Collapse: terminal cross-entropy optimization suppresses intra-class variance and forces unconstrained prototypes toward a rank-one limit. Prior mitigations rely on soft orthogonality penalties that are systematically overpowered by the dominant classification gradient.

## Input / Output

AMP takes RGB images resized to 448×448 (cropped via bounding boxes) and processes them through a standard CNN backbone (VGG16, ResNet34/50, or DenseNet161) to produce a spatial feature tensor F ∈ R^{D×H×W}. The model outputs a class label via a weighted sum of max projection energies, and for explanation it produces per-direction spatial activation heatmaps (energy maps M̃) plus a nearest-neighbor training patch retrieved from cached features to ground each active basis direction into a discrete, visually verifiable exemplar.

## Method

AMP parameterizes each class's K prototypes as an orthonormal basis matrix U_c constrained to the Stiefel manifold St(D,K), making rank-one collapse structurally infeasible. A learnable non-negative diagonal capacity matrix Σ_c is injected to enable dynamic rank calibration, updated via proximal soft-thresholding that enforces exact L1 sparsity and prunes redundant dimensions. Two spatial gauge-fixing regularizers—spatial entropy minimization and pairwise heatmap overlap suppression—break the rotational invariance of the subspace projector to encourage localized, non-overlapping part evidence. Training uses a decoupled optimization scheme: Euclidean SGD for the backbone, Riemannian gradient descent with QR retractions for the Stiefel bases, and proximal gradient steps for the capacity weights, all under a composite loss combining cross-entropy, spatial regularization, and sparsity penalties.

## Results

On CUB-200-2011 with ResNet50, AMP achieves 88.4% top-1 accuracy, the highest among all intrinsically interpretable models, surpassing the previous best MGProto (86.6%) by 1.8 points while remaining within 0.8 points of the strongest black-box classifier PMG (89.2%). On Stanford Cars with ResNet50, AMP reaches 92.0%, exceeding MGProto (90.5%) by 1.5 points. AMP also sets state-of-the-art interpretability scores, achieving 76.80 Consistency, 49.20 Stability, 28.10 OIRR, and 3.45 DAUC on CUB-200-2011, and 50.20 Consistency, 76.40 Stability, 33.50 OIRR, and 5.65 DAUC on Stanford Cars, all best among both interpretable and black-box baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among intrinsically interpretable baselines, AMP consistently outperforms all competitors across every backbone. The largest gaps are against the original ProtoPNet (84.0% on CUB-R50, 88.7% on Cars-R50), where AMP gains 4.4 and 3.3 points respectively, and against ProtoKNN (84.9%, 89.9%), where AMP gains 3.5 and 2.1 points. Against the strongest interpretable baseline MGProto (86.6% CUB, 90.5% Cars), AMP still leads by 1.8 and 1.5 points. Compared to black-box fine-grained classifiers, AMP trails PMG by 0.8 points on CUB and 1.6 points on Cars, and RA-CNN by 0.3 and 0.9 points respectively, but surpasses NTS-Net by 0.5 and 0.6 points. On interpretability metrics (Table 2), AMP dominates all baselines: it leads MGProto by 5.4 points in Consistency and 3.4 in Stability on CUB, and by 5.2 and 2.2 on Cars, while also achieving the lowest OIRR and DAUC values, indicating the most causally faithful explanations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ProtoPNet lineage of case-based interpretable classifiers by reframing the well-documented prototype collapse problem through the lens of Neural Collapse theory, arguing that rank-one degeneracy is a geometric inevitability of terminal cross-entropy dynamics rather than a fixable architectural flaw. Prior diversity-enforcing methods (TesNet, MGProto, CBC, ProtoArgNet) rely on soft orthogonality or spatial-separation penalties that the paper shows are systematically overpowered by classification gradients; AMP departs by replacing these with a hard Stiefel-manifold constraint that makes collapse infeasible by construction, drawing on the Riemannian optimization literature (Edelman, Absil) typically applied to weight matrices rather than learned prototypes. The dynamic rank calibration via proximal soft-thresholding and the spatial gauge-fixing regularizers are novel additions that address the rotational ambiguity inherent in subspace-based scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit theoretical bridge from Neural Collapse to prototype collapse provides a principled diagnosis that should inform future interpretable-architecture design beyond fine-grained classification, and the Stiefel formulation offers a clean, general recipe for enforcing representational diversity where soft penalties fail. However, the evaluation is limited to two fine-grained datasets with ~200 classes and moderate image counts; scalability to ImageNet-scale or open-vocabulary settings, and the added implementation complexity of Riemannian SGD with QR retractions, remain open practical questions. Whether the hard orthogonality constraint generalizes to other interpretable paradigms (e.g., concept bottleneck models, attention-based explainers) or to non-Euclidean feature spaces is unexplored.
