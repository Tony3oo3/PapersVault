## Problem

Standard Diffusion-DPO widens the preference margin between winner and loser outputs, but this does not guarantee improved absolute generation quality: both branches' reconstruction errors can increase simultaneously, degrading even the preferred output. The paper identifies this as a fundamental gap between relative alignment (margin expansion) and absolute error control (preserving the preferred sample), with no existing safeguard preventing the winner's loss from rising during DPO training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261892/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a text prompt paired with a winner and loser image, computing per-branch denoising residuals at a sampled diffusion timestep t. At inference, it takes a text prompt and produces a generated image at the model's native resolution (e.g., 512×512 for SD 1.5, 1024×1024 for FLUX.1-dev) using standard 50-step sampling with guidance scale 7.5. The method is architecture-agnostic, operating on both UNet-based (SD 1.5, SDXL) and DiT-based (Ovis-U1, FLUX.1-dev) backbones, and extends to image editing tasks on unified models.

## Method

Diffusion-SDPO introduces a per-step safe scaling factor λ_safe that adaptively downweights the loser branch's gradient based on its geometric alignment with the winner's gradient in output space. A first-order Taylor analysis of the linearized preference objective L^pref = L^w − λ·L^l yields the closed-form bound λ ≤ ||g^w||²/(g^wᵀg^l), where g^w and g^l are the output-space gradients of the winner and loser losses; a safety slack μ∈[0,1] contracts this to λ_safe = (1−μ)||g^w||²/(g^wᵀg^l), guaranteeing the winner's loss is non-increasing at each step. The scaling is applied by multiplying only the backpropagated loser gradient (via a detach-and-rescale trick in the DPO loss), adding negligible computational overhead since it reuses signals already available from the standard backward pass. The method is a plug-in compatible with any DPO-style diffusion alignment framework (Diffusion-DPO, DSPO, DMPO) and requires no architectural changes or additional model parameters.

## Results

On SD 1.5 (HPS V2 prompts), DMPO+SDPO achieves the best overall scores: PickScore 0.2182, HPS 0.2848, Aesthetic 5.8574, CLIP 0.3612, and ImageReward 0.7061, outperforming the SD 1.5 baseline (HPS 0.2697, IR −0.0469) by a wide margin. On the 12B FLUX.1-dev model, naive Diffusion-DPO catastrophically degrades all metrics (HPS drops from 0.2889 to 0.2467, GenEval from 0.66 to 0.23), whereas Diff.-DPO+SDPO improves every metric over the base model (HPS 0.2991, GenEval 0.71, DPG-Bench 86.36). On the unified Ovis-U1 DiT, SDPO lifts HPS V2 from 0.2986 to 0.3082 and image-editing scores (ImgEdit 4.00→4.11, GEdit-EN 6.42→6.60) while also improving structured alignment (GenEval 0.89→0.91, DPG-Bench 83.72→84.84).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diffusion-DPO [38], SDPO yields the most dramatic relative gains: on SD 1.5, HPS improves from 0.2743 to 0.2827 and ImageReward from 0.1705 to 0.6211; on FLUX.1-dev, naive DPO collapses (HPS 0.2467, GenEval 0.23) while DPO+SDPO surpasses the base model on all seven metrics (HPS 0.2991, GenEval 0.71, DPG 86.36). Against DMPO [21], SDPO produces the largest absolute improvement among all base methods (HPS +0.0082, IR +0.389 on SD 1.5), suggesting DMPO's divergence-minimization objective is particularly susceptible to the winner-degradation pathology. Against DSPO [48], gains are smaller but consistent (HPS +0.0010, IR +0.0095 on SD 1.5), which is expected since DSPO already regularizes branch imbalance. Against MaPO [13] and DPOP [26], SDPO-augmented Diffusion-DPO outperforms both on every reported metric (e.g., HPS 0.2827 vs. 0.2760 for MaPO and 0.2780 for DPOP; IR 0.6211 vs. 0.3308 and 0.3735). Against Diffusion-KTO [22], SDPO wins on HPS (0.2827 vs. 0.2766) and IR (0.6211 vs. 0.5593) but trails slightly on PickScore (0.2174 vs. 0.2164 is actually a win; CLIP 0.3600 vs. 0.3420). Overall, SDPO is a strict improvement over every DPO-style baseline it is applied to, with the magnitude of gain inversely correlated with how well the base method already protects the winner branch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., CVPR 2024) and its variants DSPO and DMPO, which adapted Bradley-Terry preference optimization to the multi-step denoising setting. It departs from these methods by identifying a specific failure mode—winner-branch degradation caused by gradient misalignment between winner and loser—and proposing a first-order, geometry-aware safe scaling factor as a plug-in modifier rather than a new objective. The approach is conceptually related to gradient-projection and trust-region ideas in LLM alignment (DPOP, Cal-DPO) but is instantiated in output space to avoid parameter-space Jacobian computation, making it lightweight and architecture-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is a minimal, model-agnostic safeguard that prevents a known and practically damaging failure mode (catastrophic quality collapse under DPO finetuning) across UNet and DiT backbones up to 12B parameters, with negligible computational overhead. Its main limitation is the first-order approximation, which can weaken under strong loss curvature, and the need to tune the slack parameter μ per base method. Open questions include whether second-order or trust-region formulations can remove the μ sensitivity, and whether the output-space gradient proxy remains reliable under highly non-convex regimes or very long training schedules.
