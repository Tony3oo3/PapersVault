## Problem

Existing multimodal LLM post-training paradigms face a fundamental tension: static SFT/RL data cannot adapt to the model's evolving weaknesses, while self-evolution methods that synthesize new training data introduce hallucinated reasoning paths and cumulative cognitive drift. This paper asks how to achieve faithful, sustained reasoning improvement at the capability frontier without relying on either fixed distributions or unverified synthetic supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261034/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) receives an RGB image paired with a natural-language question and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trajectory followed by a final answer. During training, K=4 stochastic rollouts are generated per sample to assess success counts; in the scaffold-augmented SFT phase, teacher-provided hints (1–3 sentences) are temporarily prepended to the output prefix rather than the input, and the RL stripping phase removes them so the model must solve the problem from the original image-question pair alone. Evaluation uses greedy decoding for top-1 accuracy across eight benchmarks.

## Method

Anchor Evolution (AnE) is an iterative post-training framework with three coupled stages. First, Failing-Frontier Discovery performs K stochastic rollouts on a seed SFT dataset, partitions samples by success count (c=0 → failing frontier, 1≤c<K → volatile, c=K → redundant), and uses a 72B teacher to extract failure-relevant search keywords and actionable hints from the failed trajectories. Second, Truth Anchor Expansion embeds the keywords via Qwen3-Embedding-8B and retrieves top-N verified samples from a 24M-sample ground-truth database (FineVision) through cosine-similarity ranking, ensuring new training data is factually grounded rather than synthetically generated. Third, the Scaffold-Stripping Mechanism constructs SFT tuples by relocating the teacher hint from the input to the output prefix of successful guided rollouts, then applies RL (GSPO on veRL) on the same problems without hints to strip the scaffold and internalize the reasoning path as intrinsic capability. These three stages repeat in an iterative evolution cycle, with each round re-probing the updated model's failing frontier, yielding a 10.3% average improvement over the base model across eight benchmarks.

## Results

AnE-3rd achieves a top-1 accuracy of 61.9% average across eight multimodal reasoning benchmarks, improving the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct base model (51.6%) by 10.3 percentage points. The largest absolute gains appear on EMMA (24.6% → 34.6%, +10.0) and MathVision (25.1% → 43.9%, +18.8), while MathVista reaches 81.2% and MMSTAR reaches 69.9%. Across three iterative evolution rounds, AnE shows consistent monotonic improvement (59.9 → 60.5 → 61.9 avg), with the most challenging benchmarks (MathVision, EMMA) continuing to gain in later rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnE-3rd (61.9% avg) outperforms the strongest static-data baseline, OpenMMReasoner-7B (60.4%), by 1.5 points, winning on MathVista (+1.7), MathVision (+0.3), LogicVista (+1.3), MMMU (+0.3), and EMMA (+10.1), though trailing slightly on MathVerse (−1.5) and SciQA (−0.2). Against the self-evolution baseline C2-Evo, AnE achieves substantially higher average accuracy (61.9% vs. 68.7% on MathVista only, as C2-Evo reports limited benchmarks). Compared to reflective methods SRPO and ADHint, AnE surpasses both on average, with the Scaffold-Stripping mechanism contributing the key advantage. AnE-3rd also beats SPARK-VL-7B (56.9%) by 5.0 points and Revisual-R1 (56.7%) by 5.2 points on average, though Revisual-R1 retains an edge on MathVision (48.8 vs. 43.9) and LogicVista (52.3 vs. 51.3). The iterative nature of AnE is validated by consistent per-round gains (+2.7, +0.6, +1.4 avg), whereas Static Evolution plateaus and Self-Evolution degrades in Round 3 due to hallucinated synthetic data (hallucination rates of 6.5/9.3/7.8% vs. 0.2/0.8/0.7% for AnE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of iterative SFT-RL post-training and retrieval-augmented data curation for multimodal reasoning. It departs from static-data pipelines (OpenVLThinker, OpenMMReasoner) by making training data adaptive to the model's evolving failure frontier, and from synthetic self-evolution methods (C2-Evo, MMEvol) by grounding data expansion in verified external databases rather than teacher-generated samples. The Scaffold-Stripping Mechanism—SFT with teacher hints followed by RL to remove them—repurposes the curriculum-learning intuition of scaffolding in a way not previously formalized for multimodal post-training, positioning the work as a synthesis of retrieval-augmented generation, diagnostic error analysis, and staged RL into a unified iterative paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in iterative post-training: synthetic data expansion introduces hallucinated reasoning paths that compound across rounds, while static data cannot track the model's shifting capability frontier. The demonstrated hallucination-rate gap (0.2–0.8% for AnE vs. 6.5–9.3% for self-evolution) and the finding that random data expansion actively degrades performance (−3.1% avg) provide concrete evidence that data provenance matters as much as data volume. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the Qwen2.5-VL-7B family, how sensitive it is to the quality and coverage of the external database (FineVision, 24M samples), and whether the offline retrieval pipeline can be made online or amortized for deployment at scale. The scaffold-stripping mechanism is a clean, potentially reusable technique for any setting where a stronger model can provide hints that a weaker model must eventually internalize.
