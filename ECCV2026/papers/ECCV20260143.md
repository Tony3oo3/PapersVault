## Problem

Existing image immunization methods against diffusion-based editing either require computationally intensive per-image optimization at inference time or rely on additional pretrained neural networks that demand GPU acceleration, limiting real-world scalability. The paper addresses the unexplored question of whether a single, image-agnostic universal adversarial perturbation (UAP) can provide effective immunization without any test-time adaptation or specialized hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260143/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method takes an arbitrary RGB source image and a pre-computed UAP (trained once with ε=10/255, smaller than the ε=16/255 used by image-specific baselines) and produces an immunized image via a simple element-wise addition (x_new + δ), requiring zero GPU memory and near-zero latency. The UAP is trained either on 10,000 image-prompt pairs from LAION-2B-en (data-dependent) or on synthetic jigsaw-puzzle inputs (data-free), and a single trained UAP generalizes to all unseen images without re-optimization.

## Method

The core contribution is a targeted UAP trained with two complementary losses that operate directly on cross-attention outputs across all U-Net layers: (1) a target semantic injection loss that minimizes the L2 distance between the cross-attention responses of the UAP-perturbed source image (conditioned on a target prompt like 'Ronaldo') and those of a genuine target image under the same prompt, and (2) a source semantic suppression loss that maximizes the L2 distance between cross-attention outputs of the original and perturbed images under the original prompt, thereby erasing source semantics. Training is a one-time optimization over the dataset (20 epochs) using the Semantic Attack optimization strategy extended to a universal setting. At inference, immunization reduces to adding the frozen UAP to any new image, eliminating per-image optimization and inference-time neural networks entirely. The approach also supports a data-free variant that trains solely on synthetic inputs using only the injection loss, and demonstrates strong black-box transferability across Stable Diffusion V1.4/V2.0 and InstructPix2Pix.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion V1.5 (white-box), the proposed universal method achieves PSNR↓ 14.19, SSIM↓ 0.332, VIFp↓ 0.082, and LPIPS↑ 0.606, outperforming all image-specific baselines (EA: 14.75/0.386/0.088/0.584; DA: 14.71/0.382/0.106/0.552; SA: 16.28/0.431/0.138/0.490) and the optimization-free FastProtect (16.44/0.472/0.162/0.451) on most immunization metrics. Critically, this is achieved with near-zero inference cost (~0 s, 0 GB GPU) versus 315–2707 s and 6.5–30.8 GB for image-specific methods. The data-free variant (OursDF) also remains competitive, ranking second-best across most metrics while using no real training data and a smaller perturbation budget (ε=10/255 vs. 16/255).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two PhotoGuard variants (EA, DA), the proposed method achieves lower PSNR (14.19 vs. 14.75/14.71), lower SSIM (0.332 vs. 0.386/0.382), and lower VIFp (0.082 vs. 0.088/0.106), indicating stronger immunization, while reducing inference time from 315–2707 s to ~0 s. Compared to Semantic Attack (SA), Ours wins on PSNR (14.19 vs. 16.28), SSIM (0.332 vs. 0.431), VIFp (0.082 vs. 0.138), and LPIPS (0.606 vs. 0.490), though SA has a slight edge on FSIM (0.637 vs. 0.642) and imperceptibility (PSNR↑ 30.82 vs. 29.41). Against the optimization-free FastProtect (FP), Ours dominates on all immunization metrics (e.g., PSNR 14.19 vs. 16.44, SSIM 0.332 vs. 0.472) while matching its near-zero latency. The data-free variant (OursDF) also surpasses FP on PSNR, SSIM, and VIFp despite using no real data. In black-box transferability (SD V1.4, V2.0, InstructPix2Pix), Ours consistently outperforms all baselines on LPIPS and Feat.Sim(C), confirming that the semantic injection mechanism generalizes across architectures without model-specific tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of research: targeted universal adversarial perturbations (UAPs) in classification, where a single input-agnostic perturbation drives diverse inputs toward a specific target label, and image-specific immunization methods for diffusion-based editing (e.g., Semantic Attack, PhotoGuard) that require costly per-image optimization. The key conceptual departure is transplanting the targeted-UAP objective into the diffusion editing defense setting and formulating it through cross-attention manipulation rather than pixel-level or encoder-level disruption. The two proposed losses—target semantic injection (aligning cross-attention with a target image) and source semantic suppression (maximizing deviation from the original)—are novel formulations that operate directly on U-Net intermediate features, extending the optimization strategy of Semantic Attack from per-image to a single universal perturbation trained across a dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a single pre-computed UAP applied via simple addition eliminates per-image GPU optimization, reducing inference cost to near zero while matching or exceeding image-specific methods under a tighter perturbation budget (10/255 vs. 16/255). The data-free variant, which requires no real training images, further lowers the barrier to deployment in privacy-sensitive or resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to newer architectures (e.g., FLUX, rectified-flow transformers), how it fares against adaptive purification that explicitly reconstructs original semantics, and whether the one-time UAP training cost (20 epochs on 10K pairs) remains acceptable as target diversity grows. The paper also acknowledges that reverse-engineering of the protection remains a concern, though diverse UAPs per prompt partially mitigate this.
