## Problem

Monocular 3D object detection typically follows a 2D-detect-then-lift-to-3D recipe, where depth and absolute scale are underconstrained from a single image, making small range errors dominate 3D overlap and learned scale priors brittle under camera, motion, or scene domain shifts. This paper asks whether detection can be performed directly in a metric 3D space reconstructed from RGB, bypassing the error-amplifying 2D-to-3D lifting pathway entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260645/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a causal sliding window of T RGB frames (T=5 at inference) as multi-view input, optionally augmented with camera intrinsics and extrinsics; when metadata is missing, the backbone estimates poses and scale internally. It outputs a fixed-size set of M class-agnostic, metric-scale 3D oriented bounding boxes (center, dimensions, 6D rotation, confidence) for the current frame only, along with a per-window scale factor ρ that converts all up-to-scale geometric regressions into physical units. The architecture is designed for online operation, producing detections at 8.3 FPS with 6.4 GB peak GPU memory on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

Map-Det3D repurposes MapAnything, a feed-forward metric 3D reconstruction model, as the geometric encoder of a DETR-style detection transformer: a 16-layer multi-view transformer fuses T input views into multi-scale feature maps and predicts a disentangled scale factor ρ, which are then fed to a deformable decoder that iteratively refines object queries. The key architectural contribution is an up-to-scale 3D bounding box head that regresses all geometric attributes (center, log-depth, log-dimensions, 6D rotation) in an unscaled coordinate system and converts them to metric units via ρ, thereby decoupling scale estimation from per-object depth regression. During training, the multi-view transformer and scale head are unfrozen at a reduced learning rate to adapt the reconstruction prior toward object-centric features, while Hungarian matching on auxiliary 2D boxes drives bipartite assignment and a disentangled corner loss (L1 for center/depth/dimensions, Chamfer distance for rotation) provides deep supervision across all decoder layers.

## Results

On the in-domain CA-1M validation set, Map-Det3D achieves 16.9 AP₂₅ and 3.5 AP₅₀, outperforming all monocular baselines (CuTR: 13.5/2.4, Cube R-CNN: 4.6/1.0) and the offline multi-view ImVoxelNet (10.1/2.3) while operating online with a sliding window. On the zero-shot ScanNet200 benchmark, Map-Det3D reaches 15.2 AP₁₅ and 9.7 AP₂₅, surpassing all open-vocabulary monocular methods (best prior: 11.7 AP₁₅). For per-scene evaluation on ScanNetV2 without ground-truth depth, Map-Det3D achieves 27.6 AP₁₅, 22.7 AP₂₅, and 6.2 AP₅₀, setting a new SOTA among RGB-only online methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Map-Det3D outperforms all image-based baselines: it beats the strongest monocular method CuTR by 3.4 AP₂₅ (16.9 vs 13.5) and 1.1 AP₅₀ (3.5 vs 2.4) on CA-1M, and surpasses Cube R-CNN by a wide margin (16.9 vs 4.6 AP₂₅). It also exceeds the offline multi-view ImVoxelNet by 6.8 AP₂₅ (16.9 vs 10.1) while operating causally with a sliding window. On zero-shot ScanNet200, Map-Det3D (15.2 AP₁₅) outperforms all open-vocabulary monocular methods including OVMono3D (11.7), DetAny3D (11.7), 3D-MOOD (11.2), and LabelAny3D (11.2), and dramatically outperforms CuTR (4.3 AP₁₅) despite both being trained on CA-1M, confirming the gain comes from architecture rather than data. For per-scene ScanNetV2 evaluation, Map-Det3D (27.6 AP₁₅) surpasses BoxFusion's RGB-only variant (18.5), OnlineAnySeg (24.2), EmbodiedSAM (8.2), and SpatialLM (9.0), though it trails full BoxFusion (29.2) which uses ground-truth depth. Against point-cloud methods (FCAF3D: 29.3 AP₂₅, TR3D: 22.0 AP₂₅), Map-Det3D remains behind as expected given the absence of direct metric depth, but narrows the gap to TR3D to 5.1 AP₂₅.

## Relation to Prior Work

Map-Det3D repurposes the feed-forward 3D reconstruction model MapAnything as the geometric encoder of a DETR-style detection transformer, departing from the dominant 2D-to-3D lifting paradigm in monocular 3DOD where objects are first detected in the image plane and 3D attributes are regressed via learned priors. The detection head follows Cube R-CNN's 6D rotation parameterization and disentangled corner loss, while the multi-view temporal aggregation and training strategy draw on VGGT. The key architectural departure is the up-to-scale 3D bounding box head, which regresses all geometric attributes in an unscaled coordinate system and recovers metric outputs through the FF3R-predicted scale factor, thereby decoupling scale estimation from per-object depth regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a reconstruction-oriented model can be effectively repurposed as a detection backbone, suggesting a broader principle: strong geometric inductive biases from FF3R pretraining transfer to object-centric tasks without requiring task-specific depth supervision. The zero-shot gap over CuTR (15.2 vs. 4.3 AP15 on ScanNet200, same training data) isolates the architectural contribution from data effects. However, the method is currently limited to indoor scenes (trained only on CA-1M) and class-agnostic detection, leaving open whether the FF3R-as-encoder recipe generalizes to outdoor, large-scale, or open-vocabulary settings. The modest absolute performance relative to point-cloud methods (e.g., 16.9 vs. 29.3 AP25 on CA-1M) also underscores that RGB-only metric 3D detection remains substantially harder than sensor-based approaches.
