## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for diffusion models, particularly destruction-based approaches like STEREO and SUMA, achieve strong erasure but cause excessive collateral damage to non-target generation quality through uncontrolled parameter updates or rigid static mappings. This is especially acute for closely related same-domain peers (e.g., preserving 'Will Smith' when erasing 'Barack Obama'), which remain unsolved by prior work.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained text-to-image diffusion model (SD1.4 or FLUX), a target concept to erase (which may span multiple tokens), and a user-specified generic anchor concept (e.g., 'Person'). It outputs modified cross-attention K and V projection weights, with all other model parameters held fixed. The loss is computed independently per token and the EOS token, then averaged, and applied uniformly across all cross-attention layers with a single set of hyperparameters.

## Method

PPAA introduces a bounded push-pull mechanism in the cross-attention key-value space. The push loss penalizes cosine similarity between the updated and original target representations exceeding a margin m_push, enforcing minimum semantic divergence. The pull loss anchors the updated target toward the generic anchor using a relative ratio—sim(Wp_e, W0p_g) / sim(W0p_e, W0p_g)—rather than an absolute threshold, making m_pull a percentage-based constraint that adapts to each layer's initial target-anchor similarity and can be applied uniformly across layers. Two preservation losses (cosine alignment of the generic anchor embedding and L2 consistency of denoising predictions at the terminal timestep under the null prompt) further limit semantic drift. Only the cross-attention K and V projection matrices are optimized, yielding a parameter-efficient fine-tuning procedure with a single controllable knob (m_push) for the erasure-preservation trade-off.

## Results

On the CCE adversarial robustness protocol (nudity erasure), PPAA achieves an attack success rate (ASR) of 0.20 while maintaining the best preservation score (CMMD = 1.43), compared to DUO (ASR 0.01, CMMD 6.44) and STEREO (ASR 0.04, CMMD 139.47). Under UnlearnDiff, PPAA attains a Pre-ASR of 0.08 and ASR of 0.83 with an average attack time of 11.46 minutes, competitive with SUMA (ASR 0.83) and substantially better than ESD/UCE/MACE (ASR 0.92). Across the four concept categories (Celebrity, Artistic Style, IP Entity, Nudity), PPAA achieves Pareto-optimal erasure-preservation trade-offs on three categories and matches DUO on Nudity, while varying only a single hyperparameter (m_push).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPAA achieves Pareto-optimal erasure-preservation trade-offs over ESD, UCE, MACE, and AC on Celebrity, Artistic Style, and IP Entity categories, where those baselines either erase ineffectively or excessively distort preserved concepts. Against DUO, PPAA trades a small robustness gap (CCE ASR 0.20 vs. 0.01) for a 4.5× improvement in preservation (CMMD 1.43 vs. 6.44), and against STEREO the preservation advantage is dramatic (CMMD 1.43 vs. 139.47) at the cost of higher ASR (0.20 vs. 0.04). On UnlearnDiff, PPAA matches SUMA exactly (ASR 0.83) while requiring a shorter average attack time (11.46 vs. 11.80 min) and a lower Pre-ASR (0.08 vs. 0.16). On FLUX, PPAA outperforms both ESD (which causes significant semantic shift in non-target concepts like Donald Duck) and UCE (which fails to erase Mickey Mouse), confirming generalizability to modern architectures. Overall, PPAA positions itself as the best preservation-oriented method among weight-editing approaches, with robustness competitive to SUMA and orthogonal to STEREO's Stage-1 adversarial discovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-attention-based concept erasure lineage (ESD → AC → MACE/UCE → SUMA) by replacing unbounded or rigid absolute-distance mapping objectives with a bounded cosine-similarity push-pull mechanism. The key conceptual departure is the use of a relative similarity ratio (updated similarity divided by initial target-anchor similarity) rather than an absolute margin, which yields an adaptive, layer-aware threshold that can be applied uniformly across all cross-attention layers. The denoising preservation loss is adapted from DUO's preference-optimization framework, and the overall formulation is designed to slot in as a Stage-2 erasure objective within adversarial two-stage pipelines like STEREO and SUMA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete gap in prior erasure methods: the degradation of closely related same-domain peer concepts (e.g., 'Will Smith' when erasing 'Barack Obama'), which broader-domain preservation metrics like COCO-based FID/CMMD mask. The single-knob m_push parameter and the relative-ratio formulation make the method practical and interpretable for practitioners who need to tune erasure strength without per-layer or per-concept hyperparameter searches. A key open question is whether combining PPAA with STEREO's adversarial discovery stage (Stage 1) can simultaneously achieve strong robustness and the preservation gains demonstrated here, since the current method does not explicitly optimize against prompt-based recovery attacks.
