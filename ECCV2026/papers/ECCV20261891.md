## Problem

Fine-tuning MLLMs on specialized tasks causes catastrophic forgetting of general pre-trained capabilities, and existing model merging methods either rely on heuristic scoring functions without theoretical justification or optimize only for downstream performance while neglecting knowledge preservation. The paper addresses the gap of lacking a principled framework that jointly optimizes both task specialization and general knowledge retention in a single merging step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261891/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two weight vectors (pre-trained w^pt and fine-tuned w^ft), a small calibration set sampled from pre-training tasks (8 samples per task) for estimating the pre-training Hessian diagonal, and the fine-tuning dataset for estimating the fine-tuning Hessian diagonal. It produces either a dense per-parameter mixing ratio vector (soft mixing, CGM) or a binary selection mask of sparsity K (hard mixing, CGM†) applied to the fine-tuned weights, yielding a single merged model. The Hessian estimates are stored as O(d) diagonal vectors, making the approach memory-efficient relative to full-matrix second-order methods.

## Method

CGM formulates a joint objective that minimizes the sum of second-order Taylor approximations of both the fine-tuning and pre-training losses around their respective optima, weighted by a balancing coefficient α. Under a diagonal Hessian approximation, the problem decouples into independent per-parameter subproblems, yielding a closed-form mixing ratio λ_i* = αh_i^pt / (h_i^ft + αh_i^pt) that assigns each parameter a blend proportional to its relative curvature in the two loss landscapes. The hard-mixing variant CGM† reframes merging as a sparse L0-constrained selection: it computes a curvature-aware score c_i = ½(h_i^ft − αh_i^pt)Δ_i² for each parameter and reverts only the K% parameters with the smallest scores (i.e., those critical for general knowledge but unimportant for the new task) back to their pre-trained values. Hessian diagonals are estimated via the empirical Fisher Information Matrix or, for stronger performance, via Hutchinson trace estimation with Hessian-vector products. The key distinction from prior work is that both the soft and hard mixing rules are derived analytically from a single joint optimization objective rather than hand-crafted heuristics.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B fine-tuned on OKVQA, CGM† achieves the best overall balance with an Hscore of 62.8 and Avg of 65.0, while retaining 65.7 Pre-Avg (vs. 66.1 for the pre-trained model) and scoring 60.2 on the target task. CGM (soft mixing) achieves Hscore 61.9 and Avg 63.7 with 64.2 Pre-Avg. On Qwen-2.5VL-3B fine-tuned on LaTeX-OCR, CGM† reaches Hscore 72.7 and Avg 71.1, outperforming the best baseline (Magnitude, Hscore 68.5) by over 4 points while maintaining 70.6 Pre-Avg. Across all four experimental settings, CGM and CGM† consistently achieve the highest Hscore and Avg, demonstrating superior trade-offs between task specialization and general knowledge retention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGM and CGM† outperform all four model-merging baselines (Tailor, DARE, Grafting, Wanda) and the Magnitude heuristic on both Hscore and Avg across all four experimental settings. On LLaVA-OKVQA, CGM† (Hscore 62.8) surpasses the strongest baseline Grafting (59.5) by 3.3 points and Tailor (59.6) by 3.2 points, while also achieving the highest Pre-Avg (65.7) among all merged models. On Qwen-2.5VL-LaTeX-OCR, the gap widens: CGM† (Hscore 72.7) beats Magnitude (68.5) by 4.2 points and Grafting (66.1) by 6.6 points. DARE and Wanda perform closest to the fine-tuned model, indicating they fail to recover pre-trained knowledge effectively. The ablation (Table 5) further confirms that the full curvature-aware score c_i = ½(h_i^ft − αh_i^pt)Δ_i² is necessary: using only magnitude (Δ_i²) drops Hscore to 54.0, and using only fine-tuning curvature (h_i^ft Δ_i²) drops it to 53.1, both far below the full CGM† score of 62.8.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-merging line of work for MLLM adaptation (Spider, Model Tailor, DARE, Grafting) by replacing heuristic or single-objective scoring with a joint, curvature-aware optimization derived from second-order Taylor approximations of both the pre-training and fine-tuning loss landscapes. It is closely related to Fisher Merging [28], which yields a similar closed-form mixing ratio under a Laplace approximation of the posterior; CGM instead minimizes a weighted joint loss and is not restricted to the Fisher Information Matrix for curvature estimation. The sparse variant CGM† draws on the parameter-selection paradigm of SparseGPT and Wanda but introduces a curvature-aware ranking score that explicitly balances both task curvatures rather than relying on magnitude or single-task importance alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a clean, closed-form recipe (element-wise operations, O(d) cost) that practitioners can apply post-fine-tuning without retraining, making it attractive for MLLM deployment pipelines. The finding that retaining only ~10% of identified pre-trained parameters suffices to prevent catastrophic forgetting is practically useful and suggests the relevant knowledge is concentrated in a small, structured subset of parameters. Open questions include whether the diagonal Hessian approximation remains adequate for very large or deeper architectures, how the method extends to multi-step continual learning (beyond single-task adaptation), and whether the structured column-wise selection patterns generalize across layers and modalities.
