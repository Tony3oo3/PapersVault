## Problem

Existing multi-module embodied navigation frameworks face a fundamental trade-off: pipeline architectures propagate errors downstream, redundant decision architectures are bottlenecked by the slowest large-model branch, and global-guided architectures produce fixed plans that cannot adapt to new observations. This paper addresses how to integrate the strong semantic reasoning of multimodal large language models into embodied navigation without sacrificing real-time control responsiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262068/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives 360×640 RGB images, corresponding depth maps, and camera poses at each step, along with a task goal specified as text or an image. It outputs discrete navigation actions (MOVE FORWARD 0.25 m, TURN LEFT/RIGHT 30°) executed at 0.5 s per step on a Stretch robot embodiment (1.41 m height, 17 cm base radius). Beyond the action sequence, the system internally produces calibrated success probabilities for each object and frontier candidate, a navigation mode label (exploration, verification, or navigation), and a next waypoint coordinate selected via expected-cost optimization over a frontier graph.

## Method

AMCoNav couples a real-time four-layer transformer decoder (RMDM) that scores object and frontier queries conditioned on room/object guidance and the task goal, with four zero-shot MLLM sub-modules (RORM, RNRM, RTPM, TDM) that run in independent sub-threads and are invoked on demand rather than every step. A latency-tolerant Multimodal Shared Context (MSC) mediates communication: it maintains a global object/frontier instance set updated via IoU-based Hungarian matching, a 2D room-type field with Gaussian-decay confidence merging to absorb delayed MLLM predictions, and curated snapshot subsets for frontier and key-object coverage. The Bayesian Probabilistic Decision Fusion Module (BPDFM) calibrates raw RMDM and TDM scores into success probabilities via nonlinear fitting, fuses them with a two-source Bayesian update, and switches among three modes—exploration (solved by state-compressed dynamic programming over a frontier graph under a step budget), verification (sampling geometrically separated viewpoints around a disputed target), and navigation (committing to the highest-probability candidate). Training uses a multi-task objective (decision, object class, room type, relevant-object, relevant-room) with CE and BCE losses over 20 epochs on HM3D-OVON, ScanRefer, and Multi3DRefer, with frontier labels collected via the optimal policy to match BPDFM's cost-aware selection.

## Results

On HM3D-OVON, AMCoNav achieves 60.0% SR on both Val Seen and Val Seen Synonyms, and 53.3% SR on Val Unseen, representing an average 8.7% improvement over the prior state-of-the-art (MTU3D). On SG3D, AMCoNav reaches 24.5% sequence-level SR and 8.3% task-level SR. On GOAT-Bench, it attains 55.2% SR (Val Seen) and 48.8% SR (Val Seen Synonyms), matching MTU3D on Val Unseen at 47.2%. The SPL trade-off is notable: AMCoNav scores lower SPL than MTU3D on HM3D-OVON Val Seen (15.6 vs. 23.6) and on all GOAT-Bench splits, reflecting the extra steps introduced by the verification mode.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMCoNav achieves the highest SR on all three HM3D-OVON splits, outperforming the closest modular baseline MTU3D by +5.0 (Val Seen), +15.0 (Val Seen Synonyms), and +12.5 (Val Unseen) points, and surpassing MSGNav by +5.0 points on Val Unseen. The SPL trade-off is most visible on HM3D-OVON Val Seen, where MTU3D's 23.6 SPL exceeds AMCoNav's 15.6, but AMCoNav recovers the SPL advantage on Val Seen Synonyms (20.9 vs. 14.7) and Val Unseen (14.8 vs. 12.1). On SG3D, AMCoNav edges out MTU3D on both s-SR (24.5 vs. 23.8) and t-SR (8.3 vs. 8.0) but trails on SPL (11.3 vs. 16.5), indicating the verification mechanism adds steps without proportionally improving sequential task completion. On GOAT-Bench, AMCoNav leads MTU3D on Val Seen SR (55.2 vs. 52.2) and Val Seen Synonyms SR (48.8 vs. 48.4), ties on Val Unseen SR (47.2), but consistently reports lower SPL (e.g., 25.5 vs. 30.5 on Val Seen). Against end-to-end baselines such as Uni-NaVid and DAgRL, AMCoNav's SR advantage is substantial (60.0 vs. 41.3–43.9 on HM3D-OVON), confirming that the asynchronous multi-module design with Bayesian fusion yields meaningful gains over both monolithic and simpler modular approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

AMCoNav builds directly on MTU3D's online query representation and modular navigation architecture, adopting the same 3D scene representation, training data, and overall pipeline structure. Its key departure is the asynchronous collaboration paradigm: rather than the synchronous pipeline (cascading errors), redundant parallel decision-making (latency bottlenecked by the slowest branch), or static global-guided planning (irrevisable plans), AMCoNav introduces a latency-tolerant Multimodal Shared Context that decouples large-model inference from the real-time control loop, combined with a Bayesian probabilistic fusion module for calibrated score merging. The Bayesian fusion with state-compressed dynamic programming for exploration cost minimization is a novel algorithmic contribution not present in prior modular navigation systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that asynchronous multi-module collaboration can yield meaningful navigation quality gains (up to +15 SR points on synonym splits) while preserving real-time execution, addressing a core tension in embodied AI between reasoning depth and control latency. The generalization across five different LLMs (GPT-5 through Qwen-VL-Pro) with SR ≥ 56% makes the framework practically deployable. However, the consistent SPL drop (e.g., 23.6→15.6 on HM3D-OVON Val Seen) reveals a real efficiency cost from the verification mode, and the authors acknowledge that the real-time branch remains a bottleneck under ambiguous observations. Open questions include whether confidence-based dynamic gating can bypass low-confidence real-time predictions without degrading the fusion, and whether the Bayesian calibration transfers robustly to unseen environments.
