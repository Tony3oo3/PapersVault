## Problem

Existing 3D thermal modeling methods are confined to static environmental snapshots and cannot simulate thermal distributions under unseen conditions or capture temporal temperature evolution driven by ecological factors. They typically regress radiance directly without decoupling the underlying thermodynamic properties (emissivity, heat capacity, absorptivity), which limits both interpretability and generalization to new scenes or time periods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260785/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a visible-light 3D Gaussian model reconstructed from drone RGB imagery (1920×1080), per-Gaussian spatial features extracted via Feature_GS, Fourier-encoded normalized time, and five meteorological scalars (wind speed, ambient temperature, relative humidity, solar irradiance, air pressure) interpolated from global climate data. It produces 4D spatiotemporal temperature maps (640×512, ~15 cm/pixel GSD) renderable from arbitrary viewpoints, along with per-Gaussian continuous physical attributes (emissivity, convective heat transfer coefficient, volumetric heat capacity, absorptivity) that are constrained to remain within physically plausible ranges.

## Method

ThermoGS couples 4D Gaussian Splatting with the surface energy-balance PDE (V·c·∂T/∂t = Q_absorb − Q_rad − Q_conv) by embedding the Gaussian ellipsoid geometry (semi-major axes a, b, c) directly into the thermodynamic terms, so that surface area, radiation area, solar-projected area, and volume are computed at real-world scale rather than as abstract parameters. A deepened multi-layer residual MLP takes Fourier-encoded time, geometric positional encoding, spatial features, and environmental scalars as input, with parallel heads predicting temperature and the four physical properties. Training enforces physics through a composite loss combining image reconstruction (L1 + SSIM), edge preservation, temporal smoothness, a finite-difference PINN loss aligning numerical ∂T/∂t with the network's derivative, an ODE integration consistency loss, and a physical-consistency loss that regularizes predicted attributes toward empirically tabulated reference values per land-cover class. This decoupling of intrinsic material properties from transient temperature states allows the model to generalize across unseen scenes, times, and environmental conditions while maintaining sub-1 °C prediction accuracy.

## Results

On the Thermo Dataset (S1–S4 average, full-day evaluation), ThermoGS achieves a temperature MAE of 0.932 °C, PSNR of 31.83 dB, and SSIM of 0.960, confirming the paper's claim of sub-1-degree prediction error. On the NTR Dataset (nighttime-only sequences), ThermoGS further reduces MAE to 0.896 °C (average across four scenes) with PSNR 34.94 dB and SSIM 0.978. The method also predicts per-Gaussian physical quantities (emissivity, convective coefficient, volumetric heat capacity, absorptivity) with MAE of 0.018, 0.534, 5.86, and 0.019 respectively, far tighter than NTR-Gaussian's 0.470, 32.80, and 160.80.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ThermoGS outperforms all four baselines on every metric in both evaluation protocols. Against 4DGS [31], which performs pure pixel interpolation without physical constraints, ThermoGS improves MAE by 0.54 °C (1.475 → 0.932) and PSNR by 3.01 dB on the Thermo Dataset. Against ThermalGCS [18], a static thermal reconstruction method, the gain is 0.46 °C in MAE, confirming that modeling temporal dynamics and environmental inputs is essential. Against NTR-Gaussian [33], the closest competitor (also physics-informed but limited to nighttime), ThermoGS reduces MAE by 0.20 °C on the full-day Thermo Dataset and by 0.27 °C on the nighttime NTR Dataset, demonstrating that explicitly modeling solar absorption (absorptivity α) is critical for daytime accuracy while the thermodynamic PDE coupling still provides an edge even at night. Against Thermal3D-GS [2] (evaluated only on the NTR Dataset), ThermoGS achieves 0.54 °C lower MAE (1.432 → 0.896) and 3.15 dB higher PSNR, showing that 4D temporal modeling plus per-Gaussian physical property inference surpasses static 3D thermal Gaussian approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

ThermoGS extends the 4D Gaussian Splatting line of work (which itself generalizes 3DGS to dynamic scenes) by coupling the explicit Gaussian ellipsoid geometry directly with thermodynamic PDEs via Physics-Informed Neural Networks. It departs from prior thermal reconstruction methods (ThermoNeRF, Thermal-NeRF, NTR-Gaussian, Thermal3D-GS, ThermalGCS) that either reproduced static thermal textures or handled only nighttime dynamics, by decoupling intrinsic material properties (emissivity, convective heat transfer coefficient, volumetric heat capacity, absorptivity) from transient temperature states and modeling full-day solar absorption. The key conceptual shift is from data-driven radiance regression to physics-constrained simulation where the Gaussian ellipsoid's semi-axes and orientation feed directly into surface area, projected area, and volume terms in the energy balance equation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to achieve physically-consistent, full-day 3D thermal simulation under arbitrary environmental conditions, moving the field beyond reconstruction toward genuine simulation. The Thermo Dataset (40K synchronized TIR frames across 4 urban scenes with meteorological metadata) provides a much-needed benchmark for dynamic thermal tasks. However, the clear-sky assumption, limited scene diversity (4 scenes), 24-hour training time, and reliance on a single drone platform (DJI Dock Matrice 4TD) constrain immediate practical adoption. Open questions include extension to cloudy/overcast conditions, indoor thermal dynamics, scalability to city-scale scenes, and whether the per-Gaussian physical property inference generalizes to materials not represented in the training distribution.
