## Problem

Self-supervised ViTs are known to encode dense geometric information, but standard linear probing treats the feature space as a black box, leaving it ambiguous whether poor probe performance reflects absent geometry, non-linear entanglement within individual patches, or spatial dispersion across tokens. The paper asks how distinct pre-training objectives (self-distillation, masked reconstruction, hybrid) shape the topological encoding format of geometric primitives within the latent representation space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261557/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes frozen ViT-Large patch features (N×D per layer, with multi-scale concatenation yielding 4D) from DINOv2, MAE, and iBOT backbones, along with converged linear probe weights (C×D or C×4D) trained on NYU Depth V2 depth (256 bins), surface normals, and 40-class segmentation. It produces SVD-derived task-aligned bases, projected feature tensors evaluated under a frozen head, spectral-weighted per-layer energy distributions, and readability gap measurements (local entanglement vs. spatial fragmentation) across the three-tier probe hierarchy. No additional training is required for the subspace intervention stage; all analysis is post-hoc on already-converged weights.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, a three-tier probing hierarchy (Linear → token-wise MLP → DPT decoder) quantifies the readability gap by decoupling local non-linear folding from global spatial fragmentation. Second, SVD is applied directly to the converged linear probe weights W = UΣVᵀ to extract the top-k right singular vectors V_k as a task-aligned orthonormal basis; backbone features are then orthogonally projected onto this aligned subspace S_k, a random subspace R_k of equal dimensionality, and the orthogonal residual S_k^⊥, all evaluated through the same frozen linear head so that performance variation is attributable solely to the selected subspace. A spectral-weighted energy metric (Eq. 5) partitions each singular vector into layer-specific blocks to reveal cross-layer routing of geometric signal. Single-layer subspace interventions at ranks k=8 through full capacity trace the layer-wise evolution of geometric compactness, while subspace similarity across three random probe initializations confirms the extracted bases reflect intrinsic structure rather than optimization artifacts.

## Results

On NYU Depth V2 (SA-δ₁), DINOv2-L achieves 0.9157 with a linear probe, rising to 0.9483 with a DPT decoder, while MAE-L starts at only 0.6033 linear and reaches 0.7022 with DPT. The spatial fragmentation gap (DPT minus MLP) is +0.0158 for DINOv2 versus +0.0632 for MAE, confirming that masked reconstruction disperses geometric signals across spatial tokens. In terms of compressibility, MAE recovers over 98% of its linear potential at rank k=32, whereas DINOv2 requires k≥64 to converge, reflecting its richer linearly-aligned geometric detail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three self-supervised ViT paradigms—DINOv2 (self-distillation), MAE (masked image modeling), and iBOT (hybrid)—on monocular depth estimation over NYU Depth V2. DINOv2 dominates in linear accessibility, achieving 0.9157 SA-δ₁ with a simple linear probe, a 0.3124 margin over MAE (0.6033) and a 0.0959 margin over iBOT (0.8198). MAE suffers the largest spatial fragmentation gap (+0.0632), indicating its geometric primitives are scattered across patches and require global receptive fields; DINOv2's gap is only +0.0158. iBOT occupies an intermediate position, with modest gains from linear to DPT (+0.0326 total), suggesting its hybrid objective balances linear readability with local detail. In cross-layer energy routing, DINOv2 concentrates 72.5% of geometric energy in intermediate layers (l12, l18), while MAE and iBOT distribute energy near the uniform 0.25 baseline across all four depths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear probing tradition (Alain & Bengio, Belinkov) by moving from black-box output evaluation to internal subspace diagnostics, repurposing SVD on converged probe weights as a post-hoc interpretability instrument rather than a compression tool. It builds directly on the DPT decoder architecture as the top-tier probe in its three-tier readability hierarchy and connects to the mechanistic interpretability line (attention head dissection, dictionary learning) by isolating task-aligned geometric directions. The analysis is grounded in the SSL topology literature, which has established that contrastive/self-distillation objectives constrain representations onto low-dimensional manifolds while MAE preserves high-dimensional variance for pixel reconstruction, but this paper is the first to quantify how these topological differences manifest specifically for geometric primitives via controlled subspace intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled diagnostic for distinguishing whether a probe's failure reflects absent information, local non-linear entanglement, or global spatial fragmentation—resolving a long-standing ambiguity in representation evaluation. The finding that explicitly decodable geometry is highly compressible into low-rank subspaces (k=8 for DINOv2 early layers) has direct practical implications for lightweight decoder design and feature routing in dense prediction pipelines. The layer-wise task affinity (geometry peaking at intermediate layers, semantics at terminal layers) suggests that relying solely on terminal features for multi-task dense prediction is sub-optimal, motivating depth-aware feature selection. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to ViT-Large on NYUv2 for the primary analysis, the inability of linear intervention to capture non-linear entanglements, and the absence of cross-domain or larger-scale (ViT-G) validation.
