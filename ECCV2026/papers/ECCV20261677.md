## Problem

Existing feed-forward 4D head avatar methods either require a fixed number of input views or handle only single-reference settings, and they entangle identity appearance with expression and pose variations, causing artifacts and identity drift under novel conditions. Additionally, dense Gaussian or UV representations needed for high fidelity incur prohibitive memory and computation costs, limiting practical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261677/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 1 to 32+ RGB portrait images (512×512) with per-frame camera pose and FLAME expression parameters extracted by the GAGAvatar tracker, supporting incremental reconstruction where the avatar is progressively refined as more images are added. It outputs a canonical 3D Gaussian head avatar of M+5023 primitives (where M depends on UV resolution, e.g., ~20K at 128×128) that can be animated to arbitrary novel views and expressions; the fast variant (without motion refinement) renders at 468 FPS, while the full model achieves 31 FPS with higher fidelity.

## Method

FFAvatar uses an alternating attention Transformer (inspired by VGGT) that interleaves intra-image and inter-image self-attention over DINOv3 features augmented with camera and expression embeddings, producing a global identity-consistent appearance representation that is invariant to viewpoint and expression. A sparse-to-dense cross-modal alignment module first cross-attends FLAME vertex positions (5023 points) against the global appearance tokens, then densifies features in the UV domain via barycentric interpolation over the rasterized mesh, and finally applies a second cross-attention stage to enforce global consistency across the fused sparse+dense features. Animation is driven by FLAME LBS with corrective blendshapes, and a plug-and-play Motion-Aware Refinement Module (MARM) predicts compact residual Gaussian attribute offsets conditioned on pose, expression, and camera parameters to capture identity-specific nonlinear dynamics beyond the parametric template. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on VFHQ (~3M frames) with a frozen DINOv3 backbone, requiring only a single forward pass at inference with no per-subject optimization.

## Results

On the VFHQ novel-expression benchmark, FFAvatar achieves 23.35 PSNR, 0.864 SSIM, and 0.081 LPIPS in the few-shot (8-image) setting, outperforming all feed-forward baselines on every quality metric. In the one-shot setting it reaches 21.82 PSNR and 0.843 SSIM, competitive with LAM (22.65 PSNR) while matching the best LPIPS (0.108 vs. 0.109). On NeRSemble (unseen multi-view data, zero-shot generalization), the few-shot variant attains 24.08 PSNR and 0.075 LPIPS for novel expressions, surpassing GPAvatar (22.58 PSNR) and FastAvatar (22.49 PSNR). The fast variant (without motion refinement) renders at 468 FPS, the highest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the one-shot setting, FFAvatar is competitive but not uniformly best: LAM [23] leads on PSNR (22.65 vs. 21.82), AED (0.102 vs. 0.109), APD (0.134 vs. 0.149), and rendering speed (219 vs. 31 FPS), while FFAvatar edges out LAM on SSIM (0.843 vs. 0.829) and LPIPS (0.108 vs. 0.109). In the few-shot (8-image) setting, FFAvatar dominates every quality metric—PSNR 23.35, SSIM 0.864, LPIPS 0.081, CSIM 0.861, AED 0.079, APD 0.114—beating GPAvatar [9] by 0.44 dB PSNR and 0.073 LPIPS, and FastAvatar [62] by 5.23 dB PSNR. On NeRSemble (zero-shot multi-view generalization), FFAvatar achieves the best LPIPS and SSIM in both novel-view and novel-expression few-shot settings (e.g., 0.075 LPIPS / 0.881 SSIM for novel expressions vs. 0.135 / 0.845 for FastAvatar). The fast variant (no motion refinement) reaches 468 FPS, far exceeding FastAvatar (97 FPS) and GPAvatar (5 FPS), at only a 0.15 dB PSNR cost. For train-from-scratch methods (FlashAvatar, GHA, GaussianAvatars), FFAvatar's few-shot results surpass them by 4–10 dB PSNR without any per-subject optimization. Qualitative comparisons on Ava-256 show FFAvatar preserving sharper facial details and more stable identity than FastGHA [28] and Avat3r [35], though these two are excluded from quantitative tables due to unavailable code.

## Relation to Prior Work

FFAvatar builds on the feed-forward 3D reconstruction lineage (VGGT-inspired alternating attention, DINOv3 frozen backbones) and the 3D Gaussian Splatting head-avatar line (GAGAvatar, FlashAvatar, GaussianAvatars), while anchoring geometry to the FLAME parametric model. It departs from recent multi-canonical feed-forward methods (Avat3r, FastGHA) that construct separate view-dependent canonical subspaces per input image, instead using a single unified canonical Gaussian field whose size is independent of the number of reference views. The sparse-to-dense hierarchical representation and the plug-and-play motion refinement module are novel architectural contributions not present in prior feed-forward avatar pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The incremental, variable-input design makes FFAvatar practically useful for real-world pipelines where reference images arrive progressively or are AI-generated (e.g., via diffusion models), a setting where prior feed-forward methods either fail or require retraining. The sparse-to-dense paradigm offers a reusable recipe for balancing 3DGS representation capacity against training cost that could transfer to other avatar or body-reconstruction tasks. Open questions include whether the FLAME prior limits expressiveness for extreme expressions or non-standard faces, how the method scales to full-body avatars, and whether the motion refinement module generalizes to audio-driven or text-driven animation without additional supervision.
