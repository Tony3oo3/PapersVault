## Problem

Attention sinks and register tokens in vision transformers have been linked to global, low-frequency image structure, but existing work has studied them primarily through interpretability analyses and linear probes. It remains open whether these structures can be operationalized as plug-and-play generative priors without retraining large pretrained models, requiring a method to identify where register structure appears in a frozen backbone, isolate the relevant feature directions, and consolidate the signal into tokens a generative decoder can consume.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed through a frozen ViT-L/14 backbone such as DINOv2 or OpenCLIP) and a text prompt (e.g., a class name) as input. Register features are extracted from the input image, projected into the VQ-LL-32 codebook space via nearest-neighbor or Procrustes alignment, and inserted as a single global prior token at position z_0 in the HCT-style 1D token sequence. The output is a generated image decoded by a fully frozen HCT decoder; optionally, only the injected token z_0 is optimized for a few steps under a CLIP-based objective while all other tokens remain fixed.

## Method

RegToken is a training-free, three-stage procedure that converts register-associated structure in a frozen ViT into compact global prior tokens. First, Normalized Feature Norms (NFN) are computed across layers and submodules to identify the κ layers (typically 3–5) where feature amplification associated with sink behavior is strongest. Second, within those layers, outlier patch tokens are detected via robust norm thresholds, and TokenRank-guided channel importance (combining WriteMass with the stationary distribution of the attention Markov chain) selects the top-k register neurons defining the register subspace. Third, a projection-and-conservation update transfers register-subspace energy from outlier patches to dedicated register tokens while approximately preserving mean projection magnitude. The resulting register tokens are mapped to the nearest codebook entry and injected into a frozen HCT/TiTok 1D token generation pipeline, where they act as global control signals that improve FID, IS, CLIPScore, and SigLIP without modifying any pretrained weights.

## Results

On ImageNet with a frozen DINOv2-L/14 backbone and HCT decoder (VQ-LL-32 codebook, 1000-seed CLIP top-1% association, token optimization), RegToken achieves FID-5k of 20.1, IS of 289, CLIPScore of 0.45, and SigLIP of 3.9, outperforming all baselines including the trained-register prior (FID-5k 20.4) and the no-prior HCT baseline (FID-5k 21.2). The same ranking holds on OpenCLIP-L/14 (FID-5k 20.3, IS 287, CLIP 0.42, SigLIP 3.8). RegToken also accelerates test-time optimization, reducing Steps@τ from 74 to 52 while maintaining lower variance across seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegToken (training-free) outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric for both DINOv2 and OpenCLIP backbones. Against the no-prior HCT baseline [1], RegToken reduces FID-5k from 21.2 to 20.1 and raises SigLIP from 3.5 to 3.9. Against the vanilla test-time register method [14], RegToken improves FID-5k from 21.5 to 20.1 and CLIPScore from 0.40 to 0.45, indicating that NFN-guided layer selection and TokenRank-weighted fusion yield a more informative global prior than heuristic layer/channel selection. Most notably, RegToken surpasses the trained-register prior [5] (which requires explicit register tokens during training) on all four metrics (FID-5k 20.1 vs. 20.4, IS 289 vs. 285, CLIP 0.45 vs. 0.41, SigLIP 3.9 vs. 3.8), demonstrating that a training-free extraction of register structure can match or exceed a trained alternative. The gains are consistent across backbone families, though absolute improvements are slightly smaller on OpenCLIP than DINOv2, suggesting stronger backbone–codebook alignment benefits the method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the test-time register mechanism of Jiang et al. [14], which used heuristic layer windows and manually chosen channel rankings, by replacing both with principled diagnostics: NFN-based layer localization from PLoP [12] and TokenRank-guided channel importance from the Markov-chain attention analysis of Erel et al. [7]. Conceptually, it extends the interpretability line of work on registers (Darcet et al. [5], Jiang et al. [14]) from 'what do registers encode?' to 'can that encoding be directly consumed by a frozen generative decoder?', bridging the gap between diagnostic analyses and plug-and-play generation. The generation pipeline itself builds on the compact 1D token framework of HCT [1] and TiTok [34], into which the extracted register tokens are injected as a single global prior without modifying any pretrained weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that structures long dismissed as attention artifacts can be harvested as lightweight, training-free global priors for tokenized generation, offering a zero-cost quality boost (FID 20.5→20.1, SigLIP 3.6→3.9) and faster test-time optimization (Steps@τ 74→52) on frozen pipelines. However, the gains are modest, and the strongest results rely on a same-image prior that functions as an upper-bound diagnostic rather than a text-only zero-shot protocol; cross-image priors degrade noticeably, limiting immediate deployment in text-to-image settings. Open questions include whether the low-frequency interpretation generalizes beyond ImageNet-class images, whether the approach transfers to autoregressive or diffusion-based token decoders, and whether the NFN+TokenRank localization remains stable at larger model scales (e.g., ViT-g or beyond).
