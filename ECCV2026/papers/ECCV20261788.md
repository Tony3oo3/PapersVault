## Problem

Existing automatic evaluation metrics for sign language production (SLP) rely on naive backtranslation (e.g., BLEU-4 on sign-to-text output) or single-reference motion comparison, both of which correlate poorly with judgements from native deaf raters who assess a far broader set of linguistic and visual properties. No prior metric simultaneously captures grammatical correctness, phonological accuracy, motion fluency, and generation fidelity in a way that aligns with structured human rater protocols developed with deaf experts.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a source sentence in text form and a generated sign output in any of several representations (RGB video, pose/skeleton sequence, SMPLX mesh animation, or hybrid Gaussian-splatting rendering), which are first normalised into a shared structured sample via multi-modal extraction (SMPLX body, MANO hands, FLAME face, RTMPose whole-body, depth). Output is a fixed set of four per-dimension scores on a 0–4 scale, an overall understandability score (arithmetic mean), a full per-tool memory trace with scores/confidences/evidence, and a grounded natural-language assessment generated by an LLM that explains but never modifies the numeric scores.

## Method

BackTranslation2.0 (BT2) is a deterministic two-phase agentic pipeline rather than an open-ended tool-calling agent: every sample follows the same fixed sequence of 10 base tools (Phase 1) and 4 LLM-based cross-reference comparison tools (Phase 2). Phase 1 extracts segment- and sequence-level evidence using specialised classifiers—transformer handshape/location models, an SL-GCN indexing classifier with FastDTW trajectory matching for directionality, a conditional flow-matching bottleneck for motion fluency, and deterministic kinematic/visual-metric scores—writing structured records into a shared memory trace. Phase 2 does not average independent predictions; instead, LLM modules cross-reference expected-side evidence (pseudo-gloss order, dictionary phonological specifications, non-manual expectations) against detected-side evidence from Phase 1, producing grounded comparison labels. Final dimension scores are computed by fixed weighted formulas over validated tool outputs with renormalisation for missing subcomponents, ensuring reproducibility and interpretability without any LLM step altering numeric scores.

## Results

On the Known Corruptions subset, BT2 Overall achieves perfect direction-normalised Pearson r = 1.00 and Spearman ρ = 1.00 with human understandability ratings, matching or exceeding all reference-based alternatives. On the Synthetics subset, BT2 Overall reaches r = 0.42 / ρ = 0.60 (understandability) and r = 0.52 / ρ = 0.66 (quality), outperforming all reference-free baselines. Per-component, BT2 Phonology attains ρ = 0.90 on Human SRT and BT2 Fidelity reaches ρ = 0.94 on Synthetics quality, while BT2 Overall on Human SRT is negative (r = −0.43 / ρ = −0.30) due to severe score compression (74% of ratings ≥ 4, ICC ≈ 0). Ablation confirms the Phase 2 LLM cross-referencing contributes an 85% gain in synthetic-understandability ρ over deterministic-only scoring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BT2 dominates all reference-free baselines (BLEU-4, BLEURT, ChrF, ROUGE) across every subset; these text-surrogate metrics show near-zero or negative correlation with human judgements, confirming the paper's central critique of naive backtranslation. Against reference-based methods, BT2 wins decisively on Known Corruptions (ρ = 1.00 vs. SVAE's 1.00 and SiBLEU's −0.50) and on Synthetics quality (ρ = 0.66 vs. SignCLIP p2p's 0.70 and SVAE's 0.31, where BT2 Fidelity alone reaches 0.94). However, on Human SRT, reference-based methods substantially outperform BT2 Overall: SiBLEU achieves ρ = 0.90, SVAE ρ = 0.80, and SignCLIP p2p ρ = 0.80, versus BT2's −0.30, a gap the authors attribute to score compression (74% ceiling ratings, ICC ≈ 0) rather than a methodological deficiency. Notably, BT2's per-component Phonology score (ρ = 0.90) matches the best reference-based result on Human SRT, indicating the metric captures articulatory proficiency when genuine variation exists. The ablation further shows that removing the LLM cross-referencing stage drops synthetic-understandability ρ by 85% (0.60 → 0.09), demonstrating that the gain over deterministic baselines comes from structured linguistic reasoning rather than engineering alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant backtranslation paradigm (BT1) for sign language production evaluation, which relies on sign-to-text translation and text metrics like BLEU-4, by instead decomposing quality into four linguistically grounded dimensions assessed through specialised tools. It repurposes existing perception models (SMPLX, MANO, FLAME, WiLoR, RTMPose) and sequence-alignment techniques (DTW, SL-GCN) not as end-to-end evaluators but as evidence-generating components within a deterministic agentic pipeline, with an LLM serving as a cross-referential reasoning layer rather than the primary scorer. The framework is explicitly aligned with the SLP Tier taxonomy's five core attributes, positioning itself as the quantitative metric that taxonomy calls for but does not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a long-standing gap in SLP evaluation: no prior automatic metric captures the multi-dimensional linguistic and visual properties that deaf raters actually assess. However, the negative correlation on natural Human SRT (ρ = −0.30) reveals that score compression and low inter-rater agreement in natural signing remain unsolved, and the framework's BSL-specific resources (pseudo-gloss generator, BSL dictionary, directional reference set) limit immediate generalisation to other sign languages. Open questions include whether the agentic cross-referencing layer generalises beyond BSL, whether the 20-rater protocol scales, and whether the deterministic pipeline's complexity is justified over simpler multi-metric ensembles for production deployment.
