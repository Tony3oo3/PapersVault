## Problem

Existing movie dubbing methods either rely on external forced aligners to predict integer-scaled phoneme durations (damaging naturalness) or use unified cross-attention for implicit alignment (as in AlignDiT), which suffers from reference-audio interference that corrupts target-text alignment, especially in in-the-wild scenarios with arbitrary reference speakers. The paper addresses how to achieve precise lip-synchronization without external aligners while preventing cross-modal interference during the denoising process.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference audio clip (extracted as a mel-spectrogram with a binary temporal mask obscuring the target region), a silent video (lip regions cropped and resized to 96×96, encoded by a pre-trained AV-HuBERT into 1,024-d embeddings, then upsampled to match mel-frame resolution), and a textual script (encoded by a 4-block ConvNeXtV2 stack at 512-d hidden dimension, expanded to mel-level via padding and cross-attention). The output is a 100-dimensional mel-spectrogram vector field, integrated via an Euler ODE solver with 32 function evaluations to produce the final speech waveform.

## Method

CoSyncDiT is a 22-layer Diffusion Transformer under the OT-CFM paradigm that partitions the denoising trajectory into three cognitively ordered phases: (1) Acoustic Style Adapting uses multi-head self-attention with Time-AdaLN to establish speaker timbre and text relationships from the masked reference audio without any visual signal; (2) Fine-grained Visual Calibrating injects lip-motion features through a zero-initialized learnable gate as a subtle rhythm-aware residual, preserving the already-acquired style; (3) Time-aware Context Aligning places multi-head cross-attention at the bottom of the stack with time-modulated AdaLN to retrieve linguistic context from fully mature visual-acoustic representations. A Joint Semantic and Alignment Regularization (JSAR) mechanism further constrains the process: frame-level InfoNCE contrastive loss (using AV-HuBERT audio features as ground truth) enforces temporal consistency on the cross-attention outputs, while a CTC loss on the final hidden states preserves pronunciation correctness. An acoustic-semantic classifier-free guidance scheme with decoupled scales (λ_a, λ_s) enables independent control over timbre fidelity and content accuracy at inference time.

## Results

On CelebV-Dub (Setting 1), CoSyncDiT achieves 65.21% SPKSIM, 4.29% WER, and 84.61% EMOSIM, outperforming the strongest baseline AlignDiT by 5.50% in speaker similarity and 5.19% in WER. Under the more challenging Setting 2, it attains 53.44% SPKSIM, 6.39% WER, 80.29% EMOSIM, 0.381 Sync-KL, and 3.47 DNSMOS. In zero-shot evaluation on the out-of-domain CinePile-Dub (Setting 1), the method reaches 60.04% SPKSIM and 5.59% WER, while under the most constrained zero-shot Setting 2 it still secures 47.24% SPKSIM and 7.53% WER, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSyncDiT outperforms all eight baselines on the majority of metrics across both CelebV-Dub and CinePile-Dub. Against the strongest competitor AlignDiT, it gains +5.50% SPKSIM (65.21% vs. 59.71%) and reduces WER from 9.48% to 4.29% on Setting 1, while on Setting 2 it improves SPKSIM by 3.95% (53.44% vs. 49.49%), WER by 6.79% (6.39% vs. 13.18%), and Sync-KL by 0.032 (0.381 vs. 0.413), tying on DNSMOS (3.47). InstructDub achieves a marginally lower WER (5.64% vs. 6.39%) on Setting 2 but collapses in speaker similarity (22.85% vs. 53.44%) and emotion similarity (74.03% vs. 80.29%). ProDubber matches CoSyncDiT on WER (6.41% vs. 6.39%) but trails substantially in SPKSIM (23.13% vs. 53.44%), EMOSIM (71.38% vs. 80.29%), and Sync-KL (0.432 vs. 0.381). On the zero-shot CinePile-Dub benchmark, AlignDiT's WER degrades to 20.98% (Setting 1) and 23.92% (Setting 2), whereas CoSyncDiT maintains 5.59% and 7.53% respectively, confirming superior cross-domain robustness. The remaining baselines (HPMDubbing, StyleDubber, EmoDubber, FlowDubber, HD-Dubber) are consistently outperformed by large margins on all reported metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on AlignDiT's implicit-alignment paradigm for synchronized speech generation via cross-attention in a diffusion transformer, and on the OT-CFM flow-matching formulation popularized by F5-TTS for TTS. It departs from AlignDiT's homogeneous cross-attention injection across all DiT layers by partitioning the denoising trajectory into three cognitively-motivated phases (acoustic style self-attention, gated visual residual injection, and bottom-placed cross-attention for text alignment), and from earlier explicit-alignment dubbing methods (StyleDubber, ProDubber, InstructDubber) by eliminating any dependency on external forced aligners. The JSAR mechanism further distinguishes the work by combining frame-level InfoNCE contrastive learning on intermediate context outputs with CTC semantic regularization on final hidden states, a pairing not seen in prior dubbing or flow-matching TTS literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored failure mode in implicit-alignment dubbing: reference-audio interference that corrupts target-text alignment in in-the-wild scenarios, and provides both a diagnostic (CTC-based alignment visualization) and a structural fix (sequential phase decomposition with zero-initialized gating). The zero-shot generalization from vlog/drama training to live-action movie evaluation (CinePile-Dub) is a meaningful step toward deployment-ready dubbing. Open questions include whether the three-phase cognitive decomposition generalizes beyond dubbing to other multimodal generation tasks, whether the AV-HuBERT-based contrastive target in JSAR can be replaced by learned representations to reduce dependency on a fixed pretrained model, and how the method scales to longer sequences or multi-speaker dialogues.
