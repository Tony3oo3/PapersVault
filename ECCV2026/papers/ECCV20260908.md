## Problem

Existing inference-time scaling methods for text-to-image generation focus on when and how often to verify candidates during intermediate denoising steps, treating per-trajectory generation cost as fixed. Moreover, standard NFE-based comparisons ignore verifier overhead, distorting efficiency rankings; under wall-clock evaluation, simple Best-of-N already matches or outperforms guided search techniques, suggesting compute is better spent on broader exploration than repeated intermediate verification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260908/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a wall-clock time budget T (in seconds) and produces a single generated image that maximizes a verifier score V(y, x). The budget constraint is end-to-end wall-clock time covering all generation, verification, refinement, and CPU-GPU data transfer costs, rather than the NFE count used in prior work. The draft configuration φ* is precomputed once per model via discrete optimization on a 120-prompt calibration set and reused across all prompts at inference time without per-prompt tuning.

## Method

Flash-BoN generates a large pool of M inexpensive draft candidates using a precomputed configuration φ* that jointly combines three complementary acceleration knobs—timestep truncation, layer skipping, and activation proxies (second-order Taylor extrapolation from cached features)—optimized once per model via dual annealing to maximize speedup subject to a minimum LPIPS fidelity constraint. A multi-stage verification procedure then selects the best draft: pointwise VLM scoring prunes the pool to the top ρ₁ fraction, sparse adjacent pairwise Elo comparisons refine the ranking among survivors, and a dense pairwise tournament among the final ρ₂ subset identifies the winner, using roughly 1.5M verifier calls versus 0.5M² for exhaustive pairwise ranking. Finally, the selected draft's cached latent state is refined to full quality by resuming the remaining denoising steps with no acceleration, exploiting the Markovian property of diffusion sampling. The approach is model-agnostic (applied to Wan 2.1 1.3B/14B and FLUX.1-dev) and functions as a drop-in module that can be layered onto orthogonal techniques like Reflection-Tuning or BFS.

## Results

Flash-BoN achieves the highest normalized AUC across all nine model-benchmark combinations in Table 1, leading by 1–8 points over the next-best baseline (BoN). On GenAI-Bench, Flash-BoN scores 0.66/0.68/0.68 on Wan 1.3B/14B/FLUX versus 0.65/0.60/0.60 for BoN, with the gap widening at larger scales. On GenEval and UniGenBench, Flash-BoN similarly leads (e.g., 0.49 vs 0.42 on GenEval/Wan 14B; 0.61 vs 0.54 on UniGenBench/FLUX). When layered onto Reflection-Tuning, the Flash draft strategy boosts AUC/Time from 0.46 to 0.62, and in RL post-training Flash-Flow-GRPO reaches 0.699 VQAScore at step 60, matching the baseline's final 0.692 with roughly 10× fewer gradient steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flash-BoN outperforms all four baseline families—ZOS (Ma et al.), BFS/DFS (Zhang et al.), plain BoN, and Reflection-Tuning (Zhuo et al.)—across every benchmark and model scale. The margin over BoN, the strongest single baseline, is modest on Wan 1.3B (e.g., 0.66 vs 0.65 on GenAI-Bench) but grows to 8 points on Wan 14B (0.68 vs 0.60) and FLUX (0.68 vs 0.60), reflecting the greater benefit of cheap drafts when per-image generation cost is high. Guided search methods (BFS, DFS, ZOS) scale poorly: their AUC curves flatten on larger models because frequent intermediate verifier calls consume the wall-clock budget, leaving fewer candidates to explore. When the Flash draft strategy is applied to Reflection-Tuning, AUC/Time jumps from 0.46 to 0.62 (a 35% relative gain), while the same strategy applied to BFS yields a smaller improvement (0.49 → 0.55) because BFS's local search cannot exploit the broader candidate pool. In the RL post-training setting, Flash-Flow-GRPO (draft-guided rollout selection) reaches the baseline Flow-GRPO's final VQAScore of 0.692 by step 60, achieving roughly 10× faster convergence with identical hyperparameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes diffusion-model acceleration techniques (timestep truncation, layer skipping, activation proxies from TaylorSeers [18] and related work) that were previously designed to speed up single generations, redirecting them toward a different objective: expanding the candidate pool under a fixed wall-clock budget. It departs from the dominant inference-time scaling literature (Ma et al. [21], Singhal et al. [26], Zhang et al. [43]) by challenging the assumption that intermediate verification scheduling is the primary axis of improvement, and by arguing that NFE-based comparisons systematically undercount verifier overhead. The multi-stage Elo-based verification and the modular combination with Reflection-Tuning [45] and Flow-GRPO [19] position Flash-BoN as a drop-in layer atop existing scaling pipelines rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The wall-clock reframing is a practical correction: practitioners deploying T2I models under latency constraints should expect simple broad exploration to outperform guided search, and the draft-and-refine paradigm offers a concrete, per-model-calibrated recipe to exploit this. The 10× faster RL convergence via draft-guided rollout selection suggests the draft pool is a broadly useful primitive beyond inference-time scaling. Open questions include extending the approach to text-to-video (where temporal consistency complicates draft refinement), training verifiers specifically calibrated for draft-quality images, and whether the per-model discrete optimization of φ* generalizes across architectures or requires re-tuning.
