## Problem

Deterministic relightable avatar methods produce over-smooth renderings because mapping skeletal motion to fine-grained appearance details (wrinkles, shading) is inherently a one-to-many problem, while existing generative relighting methods lack physical consistency and explicit 3D control needed for free-viewpoint rendering. No prior approach combines physics-grounded 3D controllability with the stochastic detail synthesis required for photorealistic full-body relighting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261389/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-frame skeletal motion from DDC-tracked meshes, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and a target HDR environment map (256×512×3, transformed to camera space) to produce temporally coherent, photorealistic video renderings of the full-body character at arbitrary viewpoints. Training uses the RHC dataset with 40 synchronized cameras at 60 Hz and 1,015 HDR maps from Laval Indoor; test sequences are 128 consecutive relit frames under a fixed environment. The generative stage supports long-form video via error recycling, going beyond the limited generation horizon of standard video diffusion models.

## Method

GRA reformulates the relighting task as p(z|θ,κ,E) = p(z|z′,E), where z′ is a deterministic coarse rendering, decomposing the problem into three stages: (1) a person-specific DDC mesh is textured with UV-space albedo and roughness maps optimized via differentiable microfacet (Cook-Torrance) rendering under the target environment map; (2) RelightNet, a UNet operating in UV space, refines the coarse texture by learning illumination- and pose-dependent effects beyond the BRDF approximation, conditioned on the environment map via cross-attention; (3) a finetuned WAN2.1-VACE video diffusion model (Latent Flow Matching) synthesizes fine-scale details as a video-to-video task over the coarse renderings. The key architectural novelty is an Atemporal Cross-Attention block that injects per-frame environment map latents into the DiT backbone without temporal cross-attention, encoding per-frame lighting correspondence as an inductive bias. VACE's concept decoupling separates reactive frames (to be refined) from inactive anchor frames for temporal stability, and an Error Recycling strategy re-injects reconstruction residuals during training to prevent drift in long-horizon generation.

## Results

On the RHC dataset (4 subjects, 40 cameras, 1,015 HDR environment maps), GRA achieves the best perceptual quality across all subjects: LPIPS of 6.42/5.64/7.40 and FVD of 49/48/52 on Subjects 1–3, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline MeshAvatar (LPIPS 9.09/8.03/10.16; FVD 77/74/64). GRA also attains the highest PSNR on Subjects 1 and 2 (30.49 and 31.15 dB) and the highest SSIM on Subjects 1 and 2 (87.52 and 87.50). The authors note that PSNR/SSIM favor deterministic, over-smooth outputs, and that LPIPS and FVD better reflect the perceptual gains of the generative refinement stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRA outperforms all four baselines on LPIPS and FVD across every subject, with the largest margins over IntrinsicAvatar (IA), which suffers from SMPL tracking inaccuracies and deterministic pose conditioning (e.g., LPIPS 22.52 vs. 7.40 on S3; FVD 621 vs. 52). MeshAvatar (MA) is the strongest deterministic baseline, achieving competitive PSNR/SSIM and the second-best FVD (77/74/64), but its hybrid explicit-implicit representation and restrictive BRDF assumptions limit fine-scale material response, yielding LPIPS roughly 2–3 points worse than GRA. Relighting4D (R4D) with DDC tracking achieves the best PSNR and SSIM on Subject 3 (29.32 / 87.67) but produces over-smooth renderings, reflected in its much higher LPIPS (10.62 vs. 7.40) and FVD (186 vs. 52). DNF-Avatar (DNFA) improves perceptual sharpness over its teacher IA via a more expressive Gaussian representation but inherits SMPL tracking limitations and remains less robust than MA. Overall, GRA's generative refinement stage is the key differentiator, closing the perceptual gap that deterministic relightable avatar methods cannot address.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRA positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work: deterministic relightable 3D avatar methods (R4D, IntrinsicAvatar, MeshAvatar, DNF-Avatar) that provide explicit 3D control but produce over-smooth appearance due to their one-to-one pose-to-texture mapping, and generative relighting methods (image/video diffusion) that achieve photorealism but lack physical consistency and viewpoint controllability. The paper's key architectural departure is a divide-and-conquer reformulation that decomposes the joint distribution p(z|θ,κ,E) into a deterministic physics-based coarse rendering z′ (microfacet BRDF + RelightNet) followed by a conditional video diffusion model (WAN2.1-VACE) that resolves the inherently stochastic fine-scale details. This builds directly on DDC for the parametric body, RHC for the UV-space UNet and dataset, and Stable Video Infinity's Error Recycling for long-form temporal coherence, but the specific hybrid pipeline and Atemporal Cross-Attention lighting conditioning are novel combinations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that coupling an explicit physics-based rendering stage with a video generative model yields both 3D controllability and photorealistic fine detail for full-body humans — a combination neither line of work achieves alone. The physics grounding enables out-of-distribution generalization to OLAT lighting and near-field point sources without retraining, suggesting that explicit reflectance models serve as effective inductive biases even when the final output is generative. Key limitations include the person-specific training requirement, the absence of high-resolution face and hand modeling, and the approximate treatment of near-field effects. Open questions include whether the hybrid formulation scales to multi-subject or real-time settings, and whether the generative refinement stage can be made more efficient for practical deployment.
