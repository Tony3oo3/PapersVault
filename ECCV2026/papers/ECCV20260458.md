## Problem

Existing video generation benchmarks evaluate temporal consistency only while the target object remains continuously in view, and the few that force objects out of view do so in static scenes where nothing changes during occlusion. No prior benchmark tests whether a generative model can maintain and update the evolving physical state of an object after it leaves the field of view and correctly recover it upon reappearance, under simultaneous camera motion and scene dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260458/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance provides a reference image (first frame at 1920×1080), a text prompt describing the scene and physical process, and optionally an explicit camera trajectory (per-frame camera-to-world poses). The model produces a short generated video of T frames, which is compared against a ground-truth video of the same scene. Clips are annotated with two keyframe indices (d_start and r_start) that partition the video into Visible, Disappeared, and Reappear phases, and GT frames are linearly resampled to match the generated video's frame count before per-frame metric computation.

## Method

MEMOBENCH is built around a three-phase disappear-and-reappear paradigm: a target object undergoes a physical process, the camera pans away while the process continues, and the camera returns to reveal the updated state. The 360-clip dataset is curated via two parallel pipelines—196 synthetic clips rendered in Unreal Engine 5 with per-frame RGB, metric depth, intrinsics, and poses, and 164 real-world clips captured with a panning camera whose extrinsics are estimated via MapAnything. The evaluation suite combines automated metrics (visual quality via LAION aesthetic + CLIP-IQA+, motion smoothness via RAFT optical flow, object identity via DINOv2 patch tokens, 3D consistency via Depth Anything V2 depth maps, Object Reappearance Score via SAM-3 text-prompted detection, pixel-level PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS, and camera controllability via MapAnything pose estimation) with an LLM-judged VQA pipeline where Gemini-3.1-Pro generates 24 polarity-balanced Yes/No questions per clip across four diagnostic dimensions (Instruction Following, Object & Background Consistency, Continuity of Memory, Physics Adherence), refined through ground-truth filtering, failure-clip filtering, and human cross-validation (92.9% agreement, κ=0.85).

## Results

Across 10 world generation models evaluated on 360 MEMOBENCH clips, no model achieves an Object Reappearance Score (ORS) above 0.582, indicating that reliable object memory across occlusion remains unsolved. HunyuanWorldPlay leads among camera-conditioned models with ORS 0.582 and Camera Controllability 69.9, while Stable Virtual Camera achieves the best pixel-level fidelity (PSNR 15.36, SSIM 0.523, LPIPS 0.455) but lower perceptual quality (Visual Quality 43.3). LTX-Video tops several consistency metrics (Motion Smoothness 84.4, Object Consistency 81.6, 3D Consistency 94.1) but this is largely an artifact of camera inactivity rather than genuine trajectory execution. On VQA dimensions, LingBot-World leads Instruction Following (64.2) while HunyuanWorldPlay leads Physics Adherence (63.6), and the highest Continuity of Memory score (57.0, LTX-Video) is again inflated by static viewpoints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the five CI2V models, HunyuanWorldPlay dominates on memory-specific metrics (ORS 0.582, CamCtrl 69.9) and VQA Physics Adherence (63.6), while LingBot-World leads on Instruction Following (64.2) and CI2V pixel fidelity (PSNR 14.41). The two 3D-based models split the trade-off: Stable Virtual Camera achieves the best pixel-level fidelity (PSNR 15.36, SSIM 0.523) and strong camera control (65.2), whereas Matrix-Game 2.0 produces the sharpest frames (Visual Quality 61.2) but drifts from the GT trajectory (CamCtrl 17.3) and scores lowest on VQA Object & Background (12.7) and Physics Adherence (21.8). Among I2V models, LTX-Video inflates consistency metrics (MotSmooth 84.4, ObjConsist 81.6, 3DConsist 94.1) through near-static camera behavior, while CogVideoX and Open-SoRA show weaker performance across most dimensions. No single model simultaneously achieves strong camera controllability, high ORS, and competitive visual quality, confirming that object permanence under the disappear-and-reappear paradigm remains an open challenge across all three model categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of video generation evaluation and cognitive science's object permanence concept. While prior benchmarks (VBench, WorldScore, World-in-World, VideoPhy-2) evaluate visual quality, physical adherence, or scene consistency under continuous visibility, MEMOBENCH is the first to explicitly test whether a generative model maintains and updates an object's state after it leaves the field of view and reappears in a changed state. It repurposes existing evaluation tools (SAM-3 for detection, DINOv2 for identity, RAFT for flow, Depth Anything V2 for geometry, LLM-based VQA) into a unified diagnostic suite organized around the Visible-Disappear-Reappear paradigm, combining camera-controllable generation with dynamic physical processes that game engines cannot model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a fundamental gap: no current model reliably maintains object memory across occlusion (max ORS ≈ 0.58), and camera conditioning does not produce object permanence as a byproduct of trajectory control. The finding that camera inactivity systematically inflates consistency metrics (LTX-Video's near-static frames score well on smoothness, depth consistency, and identity) is a methodological warning applicable to all video generation evaluation. Open questions include whether persistent state representations, memory-aware training objectives, or longer context windows can close the gap, and how to design evaluation protocols that are robust to the static-viewpoint gaming strategy.
