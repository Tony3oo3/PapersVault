## Problem

Large-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction from practical high-altitude nadir strip flights suffers from three coupled failures: vertical building facades become geometrically collapsed 'blind spots' due to the absence of oblique views, textureless regions (roads, vegetation) yield sparse SfM anchors that cause floating artifacts, and uniform spatial partitioning produces severe GPU load imbalance across heterogeneous scene blocks. Existing divide-and-conquer 3DGS frameworks assume dense multi-angle coverage and do not address these observation-constrained, load-imbalanced conditions simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only nadir (top-down) aerial images at high resolution (4032×3024 in the self-collected dataset), a sparse SfM point cloud with camera poses, and a text prompt for the diffusion module. No oblique, street-level, or additional 3D training data is required. The output is a full 3D Gaussian Splatting scene parameterized by anisotropic Gaussians, from which novel-view renderings and extractable point clouds (for geometry completeness evaluation against COLMAP ground truth) can be produced. The diffusion module is conditioned on the nearest available real training view to maintain semantic consistency with the observed scene.

## Method

DPGS integrates three mechanisms into a unified 3DGS pipeline. First, Dense Co-visibility Geometric Initialization uses RoMa to predict dense correspondence fields between co-visible image pairs, triangulates them into a dense 3D point cloud, and applies an adaptive reliability filter that fuses network-predicted certainty with multi-view reprojection error to prune outliers before initializing Gaussians. Second, Diffusion Prior-Guided Geometry Completion constructs a stratified spiral descent trajectory of virtual cameras to expose facade blind spots, renders degraded views, and refines them with a fine-tuned SD-Turbo model augmented by a View-Mixing mechanism in the UNet that propagates texture cues from the nearest real roof view to the target facade; the refined images serve as pseudo-labels for a periodic Side-View Loss (L1 + SSIM) that drives Gaussians to grow into unobserved regions. Third, Observation Density Balanced Partitioning (ODBP) formulates scene segmentation as a METIS graph partitioning problem where each voxel's weight is the cumulative visibility track length of its contained points rather than raw point count, dynamically balancing rendering load across sub-blocks; boundary consistency is maintained via frozen 'Ghost Gaussian' auxiliary anchors in an expanded context volume.

## Results

On the four standard benchmarks (Rubble, Building, Sci-Art, Residence from Mill 19 and UrbanScene3D), DPGS achieves the best PSNR on 3 of 4 scenes (25.36 on Rubble, 21.74 on Residence, 21.17 on Building) and the best LPIPS on all four. On the ultra-large-scale MatrixCity dataset, DPGS reaches 29.80 PSNR / 0.895 SSIM / 0.115 LPIPS, outperforming Momentum-GS (29.11 / 0.881 / 0.180). On the self-collected high-altitude nadir dataset, DPGS attains 21.36 PSNR and 0.431 LPIPS, and in 3D geometry evaluation achieves the best Completeness (49.41) and lowest Accuracy error (24.69) among all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPGS outperforms DOGS on Rubble (25.36 vs 25.12 PSNR) and Residence (21.74 vs 20.53 PSNR), but trails DOGS on Building (21.17 vs 21.38) and Sci-Art (22.46 vs 23.02). Against CityGaussian and VastGaussian, DPGS wins on all four standard benchmarks in PSNR and LPIPS, with the largest margin on Residence (21.74 vs 21.39 / 21.11 PSNR). BlockGaussian is the closest competitor on Building (21.00 vs 21.17) and Sci-Art (21.81 vs 22.46), but DPGS still leads. On the ultra-large-scale MatrixCity dataset, DPGS surpasses Momentum-GS by 0.69 dB in PSNR (29.80 vs 29.11) and reduces LPIPS from 0.180 to 0.115; MixGS is not evaluated on MatrixCity. On the self-collected nadir dataset, DPGS leads Momentum-GS (21.36 vs 21.05 PSNR) and MixGS (21.36 vs 20.13 PSNR), and in 3D geometry completeness DPGS achieves 49.41 versus 32.85 (BlockGaussian), 32.31 (Momentum-GS), and 25.53 (MixGS), confirming its advantage in recovering unobserved facade geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPGS sits at the intersection of three research threads: large-scale 3DGS reconstruction (VastGaussian, DOGS, CityGaussian, BlockGaussian), diffusion-based 3D completion (Difix3D, Skyfall-GS, DreamFusion), and dense matching foundation models (RoMa, ASpanFormer, UFM). It departs from prior large-scale 3DGS work by explicitly targeting the nadir-only observation regime of real-world high-altitude strip flights rather than the dense multi-angle coverage assumed by benchmarks like UrbanScene3D and MatrixCity. The key conceptual departure is treating generative hallucination as a first-class geometric supervision signal for unobserved facades, rather than a post-hoc inpainting step, and coupling this with a rendering-load-aware graph partitioning (ODBP) that replaces the point-density heuristics used by BlockGaussian and VastGaussian.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap between benchmark assumptions (dense orbiting trajectories) and real-world aerial mapping (sparse nadir strip flights), making its findings directly relevant to photogrammetry and digital-twin pipelines. The philosophical framing—that structural completeness for downstream machine perception (autonomous navigation, VR) can justify semantically consistent hallucination over strict photometric fidelity in observation-denied regions—opens a productive debate about what 'correct' reconstruction means. Open questions include whether the diffusion-guided completion generalizes to scenes with highly irregular architecture, the computational overhead of the spiral side-view sampling at city scale, and whether the ODBP load-balancing strategy remains effective when scene complexity is dominated by a single dominant structure rather than distributed heterogeneity.
