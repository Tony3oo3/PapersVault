## Problem

Existing dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting methods model the entire hand-object interaction scene with a single unified deformation field and rely primarily on 2D supervision, leading to geometric ambiguities during close interactions such as collapsed clearances, blurred contact boundaries, and non-physical merging of hand and object surfaces. These methods also struggle with the drastic, highly localized motions and frequent mutual occlusions inherent to HOI scenes, and prior approaches either require object shape/pose priors or exclude the interacting hand from reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261750/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB egocentric video as the sole visual input, supplemented by coarse MANO hand parameters from an off-the-shelf 4D hand tracker (Ye et al.) that contribute less than 3% of total runtime. No object shape, category, or pose prior is required; object Gaussians are randomly initialized within an expanded AABB of the MANO vertices, while background Gaussians come from SfM sparse point clouds. The output is a fully disentangled dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (separate hand, object, and background Gaussian sets) supporting novel view synthesis at arbitrary timestamps, trained in approximately 1 hour 20 minutes on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

The core representation introduces two per-Gaussian learnable parameters—weight w (modulating motion smoothness and occlusion strength) and radius o (controlling local edge sharpness via an RBF kernel)—forming an interaction-aware hand-object Gaussian that adopts a piecewise linear hypothesis to reduce boundary blur. Three separate implicit deformation fields (hand, object, background) replace the single-field design; critically, the object field F_O concatenates key-frame hand positions with object positions as input, enabling interaction-aware deformation that captures contact-induced motion. Explicit 3D regularizations complement 2D photometric losses: a Chamfer-distance hand loss aligns hand Gaussians to MANO vertices, a hand-guided rotation loss (using SVD-based rotation averaging modulated by the interaction weight w) suppresses non-physical object flipping, and a bidirectional Chamfer interaction loss enforces physical proximity without interpenetration. A five-phase progressive optimization (initialization → warm-up → HOI refinement with LBS-inspired neighbor blending → background pretraining → collaborative reconstruction) stabilizes convergence under the complex dynamics of HOI scenes.

## Results

On HOI4D, the method achieves 30.32 PSNR / 0.93 SSIM / 0.29 LPIPS on translation scenes and 24.16 PSNR / 0.86 SSIM / 0.37 LPIPS on translation-and-rotation scenes, outperforming the best baseline (Deform3DGS at 26.33 PSNR) by ~4 dB in translation. On HO3D (translation-and-rotation), it reaches 25.19 PSNR / 0.89 SSIM / 0.15 LPIPS, surpassing the next-best method (SC-GS at 20.37 PSNR) by nearly 5 dB. Full-frame evaluation on HOI4D pushes PSNR to 33.03 (translation) and 24.02 (rotation), confirming consistent gains across evaluation protocols.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. On HOI4D, it beats Deform3DGS (the strongest general dynamic-GS baseline) by 3.99 dB PSNR on translation and 0.59 dB on rotation, while SC-GS collapses to 17.32 PSNR under rotation due to its sparse control-point design failing on complex HOI motion. On HO3D, the gap widens dramatically: Deform3DGS drops to 9.68 PSNR (sensitive to the dataset's pose noise), 4DGS reaches 19.44, and SC-GS 20.37, whereas the proposed method holds at 25.19 PSNR. BIGS, a specialized HOI method, reports only 3.85 PSNR in full-scene evaluation (it reconstructs foreground only); on the fair foreground-only comparison (†), the proposed method still leads with 28.16 vs. 24.51 PSNR. HOLD, designed for geometry rather than view synthesis, achieves 18.03 PSNR and trails all 3D-GS-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 4D Gaussian Splatting line (4DGS, Deform3DGS, SC-GS) by decomposing the dynamic scene into separate hand, object, and background implicit fields rather than a single unified deformation field, and by introducing two interaction-aware parameters (weight w and radius o) that modulate occlusion and edge sharpness. It builds directly on Deform3DGS for background modeling and noise stabilization, and on LBS-inspired blending for local rigid refinement, but departs from prior HOI methods (EgoGaussian, BIGS, HOLD) by requiring no object shape or pose priors and reconstructing the full scene including the hand. The progressive five-stage optimization and explicit 3D Chamfer-based regularizations (hand, object rotation, interaction proximity) represent a new training recipe not present in the single-field 4D-GS baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: prior-free, monocular, egocentric reconstruction of the full hand-object-background scene with both geometry and appearance, a setting where existing methods either require object templates, exclude the hand, or only recover geometry. The interaction-aware parameters and hand-conditioned object field offer a reusable design pattern for any dynamic scene with tight inter-object coupling. However, the approach remains limited to rigid objects and struggles with extremely rapid motion or complex trajectories, and the multi-stage progressive pipeline adds implementation complexity that may hinder adoption. Open questions include whether the piecewise-linear interaction hypothesis generalizes to articulated or deformable objects, and whether the separate-field decomposition can be unified into a single differentiable pipeline.
