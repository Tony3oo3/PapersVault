## Problem

State-of-the-art humanoid locomotion systems either rely on brittle pose-estimation-retargeting pipelines that accumulate errors and introduce high latency, or bypass visual perception entirely via MoCap or sparse text commands. No existing framework genuinely interprets rich visual content from video before imitating, leaving a critical gap between visual understanding and physically grounded control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260923/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 5-second video clips at 30 FPS in either egocentric or third-person perspective, processed by Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct with task-specific prompts (e.g., 'describe the motion of the first-person individual' for egocentric). Combined with real-time proprioceptive states, the pipeline outputs 23-DoF joint position commands directly executable on a Unitree G1 humanoid, eliminating all intermediate pose estimation and retargeting steps. The full video-to-action latency is reduced to 1.84 s versus 9.22 s for pose-driven baselines.

## Method

RoboMirror employs a three-stage 'understand-reconstruct-control' pipeline. First, Qwen3-VL extracts a semantic video latent l_VLM from raw video. Second, a flow-matching diffusion model (DiT with AdaLN conditioning) reconstructs a kinematically grounded motion latent l_motion from l_VLM, trained against VAE-encoded ground-truth motion latents; the key insight is that generative reconstruction outperforms direct cross-modal alignment (InfoNCE) at bridging the visual-semantic to motion-dynamics gap. Third, a MoE-based teacher policy (5 experts, PPO with privileged simulator states) is distilled into a compact 4-layer MLP diffusion student via a DAgger-like procedure, where the student denoises actions conditioned on the reconstructed motion latent and observation history. At inference, DDIM sampling over the student diffusion model yields smooth, deployable actions in real time without any pose estimation or retargeting.

## Results

On the Motion-X dataset, RoboMirror achieves the best motion-video retrieval accuracy (MV-R@3 = 72.3%) and lowest FID (19.53) among all methods, outperforming ViMo (MV-R@3 = 67.8%, FID = 21.03) and Vid2Mot (MV-R@3 = 68.8%, FID = 31.27). On Nymeria, it reaches MT-R@3 of 64.60 and MM-T of 16.97, substantially ahead of Vid2Mot (49.34 and 25.67). In motion tracking, RoboMirror achieves a 99% success rate on Nymeria (IsaacGym) versus 92% for the baseline, and reduces end-to-end pipeline latency from 9.22 s to 1.84 s (an 80% reduction) while maintaining comparable tracking error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is ViMo [31], a video-to-motion generation method. On Motion-X, RoboMirror outperforms ViMo on every reported metric: MT-R@3 (77.66 vs 76.92), MV-R@3 (72.3 vs 67.8), FID (19.53 vs 21.03), MM-T (8.17 vs 8.48), MM-V (6.24 vs 7.42), and DTW (0.23 vs 0.31), with the largest gap in video-motion retrieval accuracy (+4.5 points). The simpler Vid2Mot baseline (LoRA-finetuned VLM with frozen VAE decoder) is substantially outperformed across all datasets, e.g., FID drops from 31.27 to 19.53 on Motion-X and MT-R@3 rises from 49.34 to 64.60 on Nymeria. In the motion tracking evaluation (Table 2), RoboMirror's internal baseline (LoRA-finetuned Qwen3-VL directly guiding the policy) is beaten by 3.7% absolute success rate on Nymeria (0.99 vs 0.92 in IsaacGym) and 4% on Motion-X (0.95 vs 0.91), with consistently lower joint and keypoint position errors. The pose-driven ablation (Table 5) further confirms that the latent-driven approach matches or slightly exceeds pose-driven tracking (0.95 vs 0.94 success) while cutting latency by 80% (1.84 s vs 9.22 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboMirror extends the latent-driven, retargeting-free generation paradigm introduced by RoboGhost [19] from language conditioning to video conditioning, replacing sparse text prompts with dense visual inputs processed by a VLM (Qwen3-VL). It departs from the dominant 'pose estimate → retarget → track' pipeline (ASAP, OmniH2O, KungfuBot, HDMI) by arguing that kinematic mimicry is architecturally incapable of semantic understanding, and instead uses a flow-matching diffusion model to reconstruct motion latents from VLM-derived video semantics. The MoE-based teacher policy draws on KungfuBot2's expert-mixture tracking, while the student policy follows a DAgger-like distillation into a compact diffusion model, echoing BeyondMimic's distillation-into-diffusion strategy but conditioned on video-derived latents rather than explicit reference motions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating pose estimation and retargeting from the inference loop, RoboMirror reduces video-to-action latency by 80% (9.22 s → 1.84 s) and enables a capability—egocentric-video-driven locomotion without any explicit pose supervision—that traditional pipelines simply cannot provide. The 'reconstruction over alignment' principle (Table 4) offers a general design insight for cross-modal generation: generative reconstruction of a target-space latent from a source-space latent outperforms contrastive alignment, a finding that could transfer beyond humanoid control. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dynamic or multi-person motions, how robust the VLM latent is to domain shift (e.g., novel scenes, occlusions), and whether the 4-layer MLP diffusion policy can handle the full expressiveness of whole-body manipulation tasks.
