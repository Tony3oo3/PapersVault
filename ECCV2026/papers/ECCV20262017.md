## Problem

Prototype-based similarity learning for few-shot object detection suffers from two structural bottlenecks: (i) inter-class similarity margin collapse, where style-related components in visual prototypes produce spurious similarity across unrelated classes, causing systematic misclassification; and (ii) insufficient geometric cues for localization, since cosine similarity encodes only class-level directional affinity rather than spatial information like boundaries, shape, and scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262017/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image, k-shot support images with bounding boxes for novel classes, and class name text strings. A frozen DINOv2 ViT-L backbone extracts visual features while a frozen CLIP ViT-L text encoder provides class text embeddings; a lightweight mapping function bridges the two spaces. At inference, no fine-tuning is performed—prototypes and semantic masks are constructed solely from the k-shot support set, and the top-5 categories are selected by similarity before detection. Output is a set of bounding boxes with per-class confidence scores, produced via a spatial integral layer that converts predicted mask logits to boxes.

## Method

The framework combines two components. Text-Anchored Semantic Mask (TSMa) maps CLIP text features into the DINOv2 space via a two-layer tanh warping, computes per-channel alignment through element-wise products of normalized visual and text features, clusters channels with k-means (k=2) to identify semantically-aligned channels, and ranks them via TF-IDF scoring to suppress class-agnostic channels; a learnable rank-based soft mask is then applied to the class prototype to produce a Semantic Prototype that enlarges inter-class similarity margins. Stage-Aligned Hierarchical Autoregressive Regression (SHARe) reformulates localization as an S-stage autoregressive process that injects ViT features in reverse depth order—deep layers (high-level semantics) guide early coarse localization while shallow layers (edges, textures) refine spatial details in later stages—using RoIAlign-extracted region cues gated by previous-stage mask logits. Training uses focal loss for classification, L1 and GIoU losses for box regression, and auxiliary BCE/Dice losses for mask supervision, with the backbone kept frozen throughout.

## Results

On the COCO 2014 few-shot benchmark, the proposed method achieves 47.4 nAP, 65.6 nAP50, and 51.5 nAP75 in the 30-shot setting, outperforming the previous SOTA (PiDiViT) by +10.1 nAP, +7.1 nAP50, and +9.8 nAP75. In the 10-shot setting, gains are even larger: +10.1 nAP, +7.9 nAP50, and +10.6 nAP75 over PiDiViT. On COCO 2017 one-shot, the method achieves 43.7 average nAP50 across four splits (+10.9 over PiDiViT), and on Pascal VOC it reaches 68.1 average nAP50 (+6.9 over PiDiViT) across 1/2/3/5/10-shot settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all 18 baselines across every reported metric and dataset. Against the closest prototype-based competitors, PiDiViT [42] and DE-ViT [39], gains are substantial: +10.1 nAP and +9.8 nAP75 over PiDiViT in the 30-shot COCO setting, and +13.4 nAP over DE-ViT. The method also surpasses CD-ViTO [12] by +11.5 nAP (30-shot). Compared to fine-tuning-based methods (FSCE, RetentiveRCNN, HeteroGraph, NIFF, DiGeo), the advantage is even more pronounced, with nAP gaps exceeding 25–35 points, highlighting the benefit of the training-free paradigm. Among meta-learning approaches, LVC [20] and CrossTransformer [16] are the strongest, yet the proposed method still leads by +20.6 nAP over LVC and +26.0 nAP over CrossTransformer in the 30-shot setting. On Pascal VOC, the method achieves the best average nAP50 (68.1) across all splits and shot settings, surpassing the previous best (NIFF at 59.4 for 1-shot Split-1, DE-ViT at 60.2 average) by 6.9 points on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototype-based similarity learning paradigm for few-shot object detection established by DE-ViT and PiDiViT, which freeze a ViT backbone and match query features against class prototypes via cosine similarity. It departs from this line by diagnosing two structural failure modes—inter-class similarity margin collapse and insufficient geometric cues in similarity scores—and addressing them with a text-anchored channel masking mechanism (borrowing the CLIP-to-DINOv2 mapping from Talk2DINO) and a multi-stage autoregressive regression that injects hierarchical ViT features in reverse depth order. The contribution is thus a targeted repair of the prototype-matching pipeline rather than a new detection paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The +10.1 nAP gain over the previous SOTA on COCO 30-shot is unusually large for FSOD, and the ablations convincingly attribute it to two complementary mechanisms (classification separability vs. localization precision). However, the approach adds non-trivial complexity—CLIP text features, a learned mapping function, k-means channel clustering, TF-IDF scoring, and multi-stage regression—to what was originally a simple training-free inference pipeline, raising questions about robustness when text descriptions are noisy or unavailable. Open questions include whether the text-anchored masking generalizes to open-vocabulary or cross-domain settings where class names may be ambiguous, and whether the reverse-order layer injection strategy transfers to other backbone architectures beyond DINOv2 ViT-L.
