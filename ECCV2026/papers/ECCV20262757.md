## Problem

Forecasting-based feature caching methods for Diffusion Transformers (e.g., TaylorSeer, HiCache) accumulate prediction errors over long skip intervals, causing the accelerated generation trajectory to progressively drift from the reference full-compute trajectory. A natural remedy—training an online regression model to correct these deviations—is blocked by a fundamental label-acquisition problem: once earlier steps have been skipped, even a full forward pass at the current timestep yields a feature computed on an already-drifted state, not the unbiased reference feature needed as supervision.

## Input / Output

GP-Refiner is a plug-and-play module that operates on intermediate layer feature vectors of a DiT model during the denoising loop. At each timestep it consumes the baseline cache predictor's output (a high-dimensional feature vector) and produces a corrected feature vector; when the posterior variance exceeds a threshold, it instead triggers a full network forward pass. The end-to-end system takes a text prompt and outputs a generated RGB image, evaluated on 200 DrawBench prompts at 50-step (or 8-step for Qwen-Image-Lightning) diffusion schedules.

## Method

The key statistical observation is that residuals between full-compute-step features obtained under caching and the corresponding reference features follow an approximately zero-mean Gaussian distribution (verified via histograms and Q-Q plots with R²≈0.97–0.99). This allows treating each full-compute feature as a Gaussian-noisy observation of the true reference feature, thereby resolving the label-unavailability dilemma. GP-Refiner then applies Gaussian Process Regression with an angular RBF kernel over a FIFO window of h=10 most recent (baseline-prediction, full-compute-feature) pairs to produce a corrected mean estimate at each skipped step, incurring negligible overhead. Crucially, the GPR posterior variance provides a real-time confidence measure: when σ²_t exceeds a preset threshold l, the system triggers a full forward computation to recalibrate the state, establishing an adaptive speed–quality trade-off without any offline training.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev (50-step reference), GP-Refiner integrated with TaylorSeer achieves PSNR 29.550, SSIM 0.6972, and LPIPS 0.3497 at 5.78× FLOPs reduction (643.13T vs. 3719.50T), while ClusCa+ours reaches the best PSNR of 29.823 and lowest LPIPS of 0.3334 among all acceleration schemes. On Qwen-Image, TaylorSeer+ours improves PSNR from 28.20 to 29.48 (+1.28 dB) and reduces LPIPS from 0.65 to 0.29 relative to the N=7 baseline, achieving 6.19× FLOPs speedup. The method maintains approximately 3.1–3.4× latency speedup across all configurations while consistently outperforming every baseline caching method on all three quality metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GP-Refiner consistently outperforms all six compared caching baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS at comparable or better FLOPs budgets. Against TaylorSeer (N=6), the +ours variant improves PSNR by +0.61 dB (28.943→29.550) and reduces LPIPS from 0.4020 to 0.3497 while also reducing FLOPs from 744.80T to 643.13T. Against HiCache (N=6), the improvement is PSNR +0.59 dB (29.104→29.694) with LPIPS dropping from 0.3742 to 0.3881 (slightly higher LPIPS but substantially better PSNR/SSIM). Against ClusCa (N=6), the gain is PSNR +1.01 dB (28.818→29.823) and LPIPS reduction from 0.4093 to 0.3334. Against reuse-based methods FORA (N=7), DuCa (N=8), and TeaCache (l=0.6), the proposed method achieves 0.5–1.5 dB higher PSNR and 0.05–0.20 lower LPIPS, confirming that the GP-based correction is effective regardless of the underlying caching paradigm (reuse vs. forecasting).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of forecasting-based feature caching (TaylorSeer, HiCache, FoCa) and the emerging 'forecast-and-correct' paradigm (SpeCa, DiCache), but departs from both by introducing a non-parametric, training-free correction layer rather than verification/probing signals. The key conceptual departure is the statistical observation that caching residuals locally follow a zero-mean Gaussian distribution, which reframes the correction problem as a noisy-observation regression task solvable by Gaussian Process Regression without requiring ground-truth labels. Unlike prior corrective methods that trigger recomputation based on heuristic thresholds or lightweight probes, GP-Refiner exploits GPR's native posterior variance to provide a principled, uncertainty-driven adaptive computation schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of GP-Refiner makes it immediately applicable to any existing forecasting-based caching pipeline without retraining, which is practically valuable given the rapid proliferation of DiT architectures. The Gaussian residual observation, while locally validated, raises open questions about whether it holds under extreme acceleration ratios, for video diffusion, or for architectures with very different feature dynamics. A limitation is that the method was evaluated only on text-to-image generation with a fixed FIFO window (h=10), and the interaction between the GP correction and the underlying caching schedule's hyperparameters (skip interval N, Taylor order) has not been exhaustively explored. The uncertainty-adaptive scheduling also introduces a tunable threshold whose optimal value may vary across models and prompts.
