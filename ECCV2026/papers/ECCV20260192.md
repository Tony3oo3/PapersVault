## Problem

Existing feature caching methods for diffusion model acceleration apply a uniform prediction strategy across all timesteps, models, and feature dimensions, implicitly assuming homogeneous feature dynamics. The paper reveals that hidden features exhibit markedly different continuity properties both across denoising stages and across individual feature dimensions within the same stage, causing cumulative prediction errors and significant quality degradation under high acceleration ratios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260192/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LinCa operates on the cumulative residual features at the final layer of a pre-trained diffusion model during iterative denoising. For text-to-image and text-to-video tasks, the original input is a text prompt; for image editing, it is a reference image plus a text prompt. The method produces the same outputs as the base model (generated images at standard resolutions, generated videos, or edited images) but with 5–7× fewer forward passes. Training requires only 100–200 pre-generated feature sequences per model and timestep segment, with no access to the diffusion model weights needed during training.

## Method

LinCa decomposes each cached feature vector through a lightweight learnable invertible network (stacked blocks of orthogonal 1×1 convolutions and additive coupling layers with small MLPs) into M=3 sub-components that cluster dimensions by continuity. Each sub-component is then predicted with a differentiated order: the 0th-order (unstable) component is directly reused from the nearest cache, while higher-order components are extrapolated via closed-form Hermite interpolation matched to their continuity. The strict invertibility of the decomposition network guarantees lossless reconstruction back to the original feature space, forming an end-to-end Decompose-Predict-Reconstruct pipeline. The denoising process is partitioned into S segments, and an isomorphic but independently parameterized predictor is trained per segment using a combined end-to-end feature-space loss and per-component prediction loss, requiring only ~1 hour on a single 12 GB GPU without loading the base model.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev, LinCa achieves 5.51× FLOPs speedup at N=8 with an ImageReward of 1.0162 (+2.3% over the 50-step original), outperforming all baselines at comparable or higher acceleration. On Qwen-Image, LinCa reaches 6.95× speedup at N=10 with ImageReward 1.0524 and PSNR 29.10, while competing methods like TaylorSeer drop to 0.7318 and FORA to 0.4812. On HunyuanVideo, LinCa achieves 5.50× speedup with a VBench score of 80.16 (vs. 80.66 baseline), and on Qwen-Image-Edit it reaches 7.08× speedup with GEdit-EN OS of 7.40. Across all settings, LinCa adds less than 0.2% parameters and trains in under 1 hour on a single 12 GB GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LinCa outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated model and task. The closest competitor is HyCa, which achieves 1.0043 ImageReward at 5.00× FLOPs speedup on FLUX.1-dev, while LinCa reaches 1.0162 at 5.51× — a 1.2% quality gain at 10% higher speed. TaylorSeer and FoCa, the leading polynomial-prediction methods, degrade noticeably at high N (TaylorSeer: 0.9793 at 4.16×; FoCa: 0.9741 at 4.99×), whereas LinCa maintains or exceeds original quality. FORA and TeaCache suffer severe quality collapse at high acceleration (FORA: 0.8276, TeaCache: 0.8421 on FLUX.1-dev; FORA: 0.4812 on Qwen-Image at N=6). On Qwen-Image at N=10, LinCa (1.0524) leads TaylorSeer (0.7318) by 44% in ImageReward and HyCa (1.0432) by a narrow margin while achieving 6.95× vs. 6.25× speedup. On HunyuanVideo, LinCa (VBench 80.16 at 5.50×) edges out Clusca (79.99 at 4.99×) and SpeCa (79.98 at 5.23×). On Qwen-Image-Edit at N=10, LinCa (OS 7.40) substantially outperforms DuCa (6.41) and TaylorSeer (6.31), confirming robustness in the editing domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

LinCa extends the feature caching paradigm for diffusion model acceleration (FORA, ToCa, DuCa, TaylorSeer, FoCa, SpeCa, Clusca, HyCa) by introducing a learnable decomposition step that prior methods lack. While existing caching approaches apply a single uniform prediction strategy (reuse, Taylor expansion, or Hermite interpolation) across all feature dimensions and timesteps, LinCa uses a lightweight invertible network—drawing on the reversible residual network architecture of Gomez et al. (2017)—to separate cached features into sub-components with distinct continuity properties before applying differentiated-order polynomial prediction. The key conceptual departure is treating the feature vector as heterogeneous rather than monolithic, and the key architectural departure is enforcing strict invertibility to guarantee lossless reconstruction back to the original feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LinCa offers a practically attractive acceleration recipe: 5–7× speedup with near-lossless quality, <0.2% parameter overhead, and training in under an hour on a 12 GB GPU without loading the base model, making it easy to deploy alongside existing distilled or quantized checkpoints. The empirical observation that feature dimensions within a single model and timestep segment exhibit markedly different temporal continuity (some stable, some with abrupt mutations) provides a concrete motivation for adaptive caching strategies and could inform future work on dimension-aware or frequency-aware inference. Open questions include whether the fixed M=3 decomposition is optimal across architectures, whether the invertible network can be further compressed or replaced by other lossless mappings, and whether the per-segment training protocol scales gracefully to very long video generation or multi-modal settings.
