## Problem

High-fidelity 3D Gaussian Splatting models routinely contain millions of primitives, incurring large storage and rendering costs, yet existing compaction methods depend on GPU-intensive retraining with calibrated multi-view images. This assumption fails in practical deployment where Splat assets are produced via generation, editing, or mesh/point-cloud conversion pipelines that provide no training imagery, and where end users render on resource-constrained devices. A training-free, CPU-friendly, representation-preserving simplification framework that operates post hoc on arbitrary pretrained 3DGS models remained largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

NanoGS consumes a standard pretrained 3DGS asset (positions, covariances, opacities, and spherical-harmonic appearance coefficients) with no requirement for camera poses, training images, or scene-specific optimization. It outputs a compacted Gaussian Splat model using the identical 3DGS parameterization, so the result drops directly into existing splatting renderers without any format conversion. The method runs on CPU (under 90 s for the largest outdoor scenes) and composes with orthogonal bit-level codecs such as Self-Organized Gaussians for further storage reduction.

## Method

NanoGS decouples simplification into three interchangeable stages: a sparse k-nearest-neighbor merge graph that restricts candidate pairs to spatially local neighbors (reducing evaluation from O(N²) to O(kN)), an I-divergence merge cost that scores each candidate by the KL divergence between the two-Gaussian mixture and its single-Gaussian approximation plus a squared-ℓ₂ appearance discrepancy, and a Mass-Preserved Moment Matching (MPMM) operator that fuses a selected pair by computing mass-weighted (not opacity-weighted) spatial moments and composing opacities via the Porter-Duff over rule. The pipeline proceeds iteratively: each pass selects a set of low-cost disjoint edges, merges all pairs in parallel via MPMM, and periodically refreshes the KNN graph to capture newly adjacent candidates. Mass-weighting biases merges toward larger primitives, empirically reducing holes and improving stability under extreme compression ratios down to 0.1% of the original primitive count.

## Results

NanoGS achieves the best PSNR at every compaction budget (ρ ∈ {0.1, 0.01, 0.001}) across all four benchmarks (NeRF-Synthetic, Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks & Temples, Deep Blending). Averaged over datasets, it improves over the best baseline by +2.40 dB at ρ=0.1, +4.84 dB at ρ=0.01, and +5.46 dB at ρ=0.001, with the largest single-scene gain reaching +6.52 dB on NeRF-Synthetic. The advantage widens substantially under aggressive compression: at ρ=0.001, NanoGS retains 19.04 dB PSNR on NeRF-Synthetic versus 12.64 dB for the best baseline (LightGS), and 17.20 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 versus 11.97 dB. Runtime is under 90 s on CPU for the largest scenes (Mip-NeRF 360 outdoor) to reach ρ=0.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NanoGS outperforms all three baselines (LightGS, PUP3DGS, GHAP) on PSNR at every compaction ratio and on every dataset, with no losses or ties. At the moderate ρ=0.1 budget, NanoGS leads by +4.56 dB over LightGS on NeRF-Synthetic (25.81 vs. 21.25) and by +2.59 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 (21.97 vs. 19.38). The gap widens dramatically at ρ=0.001: on Mip-NeRF 360, NanoGS scores 17.20 dB while LightGS drops to 11.97 dB and GHAP to 8.52 dB; on Tanks & Temples, NanoGS reaches 13.54 dB versus 9.36 dB (LightGS) and 5.33 dB (GHAP). LightGS is consistently the strongest baseline, particularly at ρ=0.1, while PUP3DGS and GHAP degrade more sharply under aggressive compression, with PUP3DGS suffering the steepest drop on real-world scenes (e.g., 15.59 → 8.81 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 from ρ=0.1 to ρ=0.001). All baselines rely on calibrated training images or post-compression fine-tuning, whereas NanoGS operates training-free on the pretrained model alone, making it applicable in settings where source imagery is unavailable.

## Relation to Prior Work

NanoGS carves out a distinct niche in 3DGS compaction by operating entirely post-hoc on pretrained models without calibrated images or GPU optimization, departing from both training-based compaction (Compact3DGS, Taming 3DGS) and image-guided pruning (LightGS, PUP3DGS). Its closest conceptual relative is GHAP, which also performs Gaussian mixture reduction, but NanoGS replaces GHAP's global optimal-transport objective with local pairwise merging over a sparse KNN graph and a Monte Carlo I-divergence cost, eliminating the need for a subsequent appearance-refinement optimization. At the operator level, the mass-preserving moment matching resembles the merge in H3DGS, but NanoGS elevates candidate selection and merge cost to first-class, swappable stages rather than absorbing them into a training loop, drawing on classical Bregman-divergence and Gaussian mixture reduction literature for its principled cost formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, CPU-only, representation-preserving pipeline addresses a concrete deployment gap: Splat assets produced by generative, editing, or conversion pipelines often lack the calibrated imagery that existing compaction methods require, and end-user devices cannot run re-optimization loops. The modular three-stage framework (topology, cost, operator) is a clean design pattern that makes each component independently upgradable—e.g., swapping in a learned perceptual cost or extending to many-to-one merging—without redesigning the pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic 4DGS representations, whether the pairwise-only merging becomes a bottleneck at extreme ratios, and whether the large margins over baselines (evaluated without their fine-tuning stages) persist under a more symmetric comparison protocol.
