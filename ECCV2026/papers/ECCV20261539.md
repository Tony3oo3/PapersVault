## Problem

The most capable web agents today are proprietary, hosted services with undisclosed training data and recipes, limiting reproducibility and scientific understanding of what drives performance. This opacity is especially concerning for autonomous agents operating on the open web, where trustworthy deployment depends on transparency and auditable behavior. The paper addresses this by providing a fully open, reproducible foundation for multimodal web agents, including training data, model weights, and evaluation tooling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261539/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives a single webpage screenshot (no HTML, accessibility tree, or specialized APIs), the task instruction, the last 10 prior actions, and the current page URL and title. It outputs a JSON object containing a natural-language thought followed by a browser action from a 13-action space (e.g., mouse_click, keyboard_type, scroll, goto). Spatial coordinates in actions are normalized to [0,100] with two decimal places during training and denormalized to viewport pixels at execution time, making the policy resolution-agnostic.

## Method

MolmoWeb is built on the Molmo2 architecture (Qwen3 language model + SigLIP2 vision encoder) and trained end-to-end via supervised fine-tuning on MolmoWebMix, a mixture of 278K+ task trajectories, atomic skill trajectories, and 10.5M GUI perception examples (referring-expression grounding and screenshot QA). Synthetic trajectories are generated by two complementary pipelines: a single AxTree-based LLM agent and a multi-agent Planner–Operator–Verifier harness that decomposes tasks into subgoals and verifies completion via screenshots, achieving higher success rates (78.5 vs. 74.4 on WebVoyager). The key design choice is a purely visual observation space—no DOM or AxTree—motivated by interpretability, robustness to page-structure changes, and lower token cost. The paper further demonstrates that test-time scaling via parallel rollouts with best-of-N selection (using a VLM judge) yields large gains, reaching 94.7% pass@4 on WebVoyager, and that nucleus sampling (p=0.8, T=0.7) substantially outperforms greedy decoding by avoiding repetitive failure loops.

## Results

MolmoWeb-8B achieves 78.2% on WebVoyager, 35.3% on Online-Mind2Web, 42.3% on DeepShop, and 49.5% on WebTailBench (100 max steps), establishing state-of-the-art among open-weight web agents. The smaller MolmoWeb-4B reaches 75.2% on WebVoyager and 35.6% on DeepShop, already surpassing all prior open-weight models on those benchmarks. With test-time scaling via best-of-4 parallel rollouts, MolmoWeb-8B reaches 94.7% pass@4 on WebVoyager and 60.5% pass@4 on Online-Mind2Web, compared to 78.2% and 35.3% at pass@1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-weight models, MolmoWeb-8B outperforms Fara-7B on all four benchmarks (78.2 vs. 73.5 WebVoyager, 35.3 vs. 34.1 Online-M2W, 42.3 vs. 26.2 DeepShop, 49.5 vs. 38.4 WebTailBench), with the largest margin on DeepShop (+16.1 pts). It also clearly beats UI-TARS-1.5-7B (78.2 vs. 66.4 WebVoyager) and GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking (78.2 vs. 66.8 WebVoyager), and substantially exceeds Holo1-7B (78.2 vs. 55.4 WebVoyager). Against set-of-marks (SoM) agents built on closed frontier models, MolmoWeb-8B surpasses the GPT-4o SoM agent on every benchmark (78.2 vs. 65.1 WebVoyager, 42.3 vs. 16.0 DeepShop) and nearly matches o3 on WebVoyager (78.2 vs. 79.3), though it trails o3 and GPT-5 SoM agents on Online-M2W and DeepShop by 6–20 points. The Yutori Navigator API reports 64.7% on Online-M2W, well above MolmoWeb-8B's 35.3%, though no other benchmark scores are available for direct comparison. Overall, MolmoWeb-8B is the strongest fully open (weights + data + code) web agent, closing much of the gap to proprietary SoM agents while operating on screenshots alone without AxTree or SoM annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

MolmoWeb builds on the authors' own Molmo2 VLM backbone (Qwen3 + SigLIP2) and departs from the dominant paradigm of web agents that rely on DOM or accessibility-tree representations by operating purely on screenshots. It positions itself against both open-weight web agents (Fara-7B, Holo1-7B, UI-TARS) and closed API agents (GPT-4o, o3, Gemini computer-use), explicitly rejecting the opacity of proprietary training data and recipes. The key methodological departure is the MolmoWebMix dataset, which combines a multi-agent (Planner–Operator–Verifier) synthetic trajectory pipeline, human crowdworker demonstrations, atomic skill trajectories, and large-scale GUI perception data (grounding + screenshot QA) into a single SFT mixture—without distilling from any proprietary vision-based agent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The full release of training data, model checkpoints, code, and a unified evaluation harness directly addresses long-standing reproducibility concerns in web-agent research, giving the community a transparent baseline to build upon. The finding that a well-trained 8B vision-only model outperforms SoM agents on much larger closed frontier models (GPT-4o) suggests that targeted data curation can compensate for raw parameter scale and richer input modalities, potentially reshaping how the field allocates compute. Open questions remain: the 5–10+ point gap to the non-visual AxTree teacher, the limited gains from human data, and whether self-distillation from best-of-N rollouts or RL can close the single-rollout performance ceiling. Latency, OCR on small text, and error-recovery from stuck states are acknowledged practical limitations that limit near-term deployment.
