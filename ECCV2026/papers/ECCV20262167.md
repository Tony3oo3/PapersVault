## Problem

Step distillation for diffusion models has largely saturated at 4–8 steps, and further acceleration via lower-resolution generation at early timesteps introduces severe quality degradation due to cross-resolution distribution gaps. These gaps arise because modern diffusion models are trained with a multi-stage curriculum (low-res pretraining followed by high-res fine-tuning), so the low-resolution distribution is fundamentally mismatched with the high-resolution target. The paper addresses how to bridge this distributional inconsistency while enabling few-step, multi-resolution cascaded inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a generated image or video at a target high resolution (1024px for images, 720p for video) through a multi-resolution cascaded schedule. Inference begins at a lower resolution (512px or 480p) for the first few denoising steps, then upsamples and continues at the target resolution for the remaining steps (e.g., 2+2 NFE for images, 3+3 for video). The upsampling uses bilinear interpolation, and the total NFE is split across resolution stages governed by a logSNR threshold (default −2.5).

## Method

RMD partitions the denoising trajectory into resolution-specific intervals using logSNR curves, with a resolution-dependent logSNR shift (Eq. 2) to align timesteps across scales. Within each interval, the generator's low-resolution output is upsampled to the teacher's resolution via a differentiable transformation, and distribution matching is performed by minimizing a reverse KL divergence between the upsampled generator distribution and the teacher's high-resolution distribution at matched logSNR timesteps. A fake score model approximates the generator's intractable score, yielding a practical gradient (Eq. 8). A key design choice is the noise re-injection strategy during upsampling: instead of pure Gaussian or pure predicted noise, a resolution-aware mixture (α=0.2 predicted + stochastic) balances trajectory inheritance with stochastic flexibility to bridge large resolution gaps. Training uses a warm-up phase on the low-logSNR (semantic) interval before full end-to-end optimization, and the resolution-aware weighting λ_{r_i} balances contributions across stages.

## Results

On SDXL, RMD achieves a 33.4× speedup (2+2 NFE) while matching or exceeding the base model's quality: HPS 31.99 vs. 29.14, Aesthetic 6.73 vs. 6.48, and CLIP 35.13 vs. 35.64. On PixArt-α, RMD delivers a 21.0× speedup with HPS 32.23, Aes 6.89, and CLIP 33.89, outperforming the base model on all three metrics. For video generation on Wan2.1-T2V-14B, RMD achieves a 25.6× speedup (3+3 NFE) with VBench total score 82.51 and T2V-CompBench 54.00, surpassing both DMD2 and TDM while retaining 98.5% of the base model's VBench score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RMD consistently outperforms all six baselines on quality metrics while delivering substantially higher speedups. Against the closest competitor TDM, RMD wins on HPS (31.99 vs. 31.93 on SDXL; 32.23 vs. 32.05 on PixArt-α) and Aesthetic score (6.73 vs. 6.70; 6.89 vs. 6.82) while achieving 1.67× and 1.68× greater speedup respectively. DMD2 is outperformed on all three metrics across every backbone (e.g., HPS 31.99 vs. 31.30 on SDXL, 32.23 vs. 31.36 on PixArt-α). The adversarial distillation baselines (SDXL-Turbo, SDXL-Lighting, YOSO, SD3.5-Turbo) show the largest quality gaps: SDXL-Turbo lags by 2.53 HPS and 1.27 CLIP, while YOSO on PixArt-α drops 1.63 HPS and 2.06 CLIP below RMD. On video generation (Wan2.1-T2V-14B), RMD surpasses both DMD2 and TDM on VBench total (82.51 vs. 80.30/80.48) and T2V-CompBench (54.00 vs. 52.81/52.27) while achieving 25.6× speedup versus their 16.7×. The only metric where RMD trails the base model is CLIP score on SDXL (35.13 vs. 35.64) and SD3.5 (32.51 vs. 34.76), a small gap attributable to the reduced number of high-resolution refinement steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distribution matching distillation (DMD) framework [48] and its trajectory-level variant TDM [22] by introducing a cross-resolution dimension: instead of compressing a fixed-resolution denoising trajectory into fewer steps, RMD partitions the trajectory into resolution-specific segments aligned via logSNR curves (borrowing the resolution-dependent scaling from SD3 [6]). Prior multi-resolution cascaded methods (Bottleneck Sampling [41], Relay Diffusion [40], PixelFlow [4]) either required training from scratch or were training-free heuristics that did not explicitly address the distribution gap induced by multi-stage curriculum training. RMD's key departure is formulating this gap as a learnable distribution-matching objective within the DMD/TDM score-distillation framework, with a predicted-noise re-injection mechanism to stabilize cross-resolution upsampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By combining step reduction with resolution reduction, RMD breaks through the diminishing returns of pure step distillation (which saturates around 4–8 steps) and achieves 33.4× speedup on SDXL and 25.6× on Wan2.1-14B while matching or exceeding the quality of 4-step baselines. The method is architecture-agnostic (validated on UNet and DiT backbones, image and video), making it a practical drop-in for deploying large diffusion models under latency constraints. Open questions include how the approach scales beyond 3 resolution stages (the 4-stage 128→256→512→1024 configuration shows notable quality degradation), whether the logSNR-based alignment generalizes to non-rectified-flow schedulers, and whether the method can be combined with other efficiency techniques (e.g., token pruning, caching) for further gains.
