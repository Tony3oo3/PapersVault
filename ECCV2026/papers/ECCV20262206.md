## Problem

Existing video frame interpolation methods designed for perspective images degrade sharply on spherical (equirectangular) video due to severe geometric distortions near the poles, non-uniform pixel density, and large pixel displacements. The only prior dedicated omnidirectional interpolation method (360VFI) still suffers significant quality loss as flow magnitude increases, leaving a clear gap in handling large-motion and high-distortion regions of 360° video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262206/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two consecutive equirectangular spherical frames and an arbitrary temporal position t ∈ (0,1), producing an intermediate spherical frame at that position along with bidirectional optical flow estimates. Each input frame is additionally rotated 90° around the x-axis to generate an orthogonal view, so the network effectively processes four images (two primitive, two orthogonal). The final output is synthesized from N=5 candidate intermediate frames per branch, fused by a lightweight convolutional head, rather than a single warped reconstruction.

## Method

SVI360 is a dual-branch coarse-to-fine architecture built on AMT's all-pairs correlation volumes and recurrent update blocks, where a primitive branch and a 90°-rotated orthogonal branch are processed in parallel across three resolution stages. The key mechanism is a Spherical Refiner that rotates orthogonal-branch flow and intermediate features back to the primitive domain and uses them, together with Dual-Cost Collaborative Lookup (DCCL) cross-view correlation cues, to predict residual corrections that specifically correct polar distortions. At the final stage, each branch outputs N=5 candidate flow/mask/residual sets; the orthogonal candidates are inverse-rotated and jointly fused with the primitive candidates via a Spherical Multi-field Refinement head. Training uses a Spherical Weighted Charbonnier loss with a cos(θ) latitude-dependent weight that down-weights heavily distorted polar pixels, combined with a census loss for illumination-robust structure preservation.

## Results

On FlowScape (middle-frame interpolation), SVI360 achieves 38.68 dB PSNR and 37.73 dB WS-PSNR, surpassing the second-best method AMT-G by +0.63 dB and +0.33 dB respectively, while also attaining the best SEPE (15.22 vs. 25.91 for AMT-G) and AE (19.74 vs. 20.90). On Flow360 (arbitrary-timestep interpolation), SVI360 leads all methods with 34.02 dB PSNR, a +0.25 dB margin over AMT-G. On the real-world ODV360 benchmark, SVI360 achieves 29.25 dB PSNR and 0.9332 SSIM, again outperforming AMT-G. On the 360VFI benchmark, SVI360 attains the best WS-PSNR/WS-SSIM across Middle (29.98/0.9409), Hard (28.95/0.9264), and Extreme (25.92/0.8880) difficulty splits, with the largest gap over AMT-G appearing at the Extreme level (+0.37 dB WS-PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVI360 is compared against six perspective VFI methods (SuperSloMo, FILM, RIFE, VFIFormer, IFRNet-L, AMT-G) retrained on spherical benchmarks, plus five additional 360°-specific baselines on the 360VFI dataset (360VFI, DQBC, EMA-VFI, EBME, UPR-Net). On FlowScape, SVI360 outperforms AMT-G (the strongest competitor) by +0.63 dB PSNR and +0.33 dB WS-PSNR, and dramatically improves SEPE (15.22 vs. 25.91), though it trails AMT-G by 0.10 px on EPE (12.36 vs. 12.26). On Flow360 and ODV360, SVI360 achieves the best score on every reported interpolation metric, with margins of +0.25 dB PSNR (Flow360) and +0.18 dB PSNR (ODV360) over AMT-G. On 360VFI, SVI360 wins on Middle, Hard, and Extreme splits (e.g., +0.37 dB WS-PSNR at Extreme), while AMT-G retains a negligible 0.03 dB PSNR edge on the Easy split. Earlier perspective methods (SuperSloMo, FILM, RIFE, VFIFormer) show 1–6 dB PSNR degradation relative to SVI360, confirming that planar-projection assumptions break down on equirectangular data. The 360°-specific baselines on 360VFI (360VFI, DQBC, EMA-VFI, EBME, UPR-Net) all trail SVI360 by 1–4 dB WS-PSNR, with the gap widening as motion magnitude increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVI360 is a dual-branch adaptation of the AMT coarse-to-fine interpolation architecture (all-pairs correlation volumes, multi-field transforms) to spherical geometry, incorporating the orthogonal-view hypothesis and Dual-Cost Collaborative Lookup (DCCL) strategy from PriOr-Flow. It departs from PriOr-Flow by extending the orthogonal branch beyond flow refinement to also contribute directly to frame synthesis via multi-field fusion, and from 360VFI (the sole prior omnidirectional VFI method) by replacing learned distortion offsets with a geometrically grounded cross-view equivariance mechanism. The spherical weighted Charbonnier loss is a targeted modification of the standard reconstruction term to counteract polar-region dominance in the loss landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SVI360 is the first method to consistently outperform all prior approaches across every difficulty tier (Easy through Extreme) on the 360VFI benchmark, closing a notable gap where previous methods degraded sharply under large flow magnitudes. The dual-branch equivariance principle, validated here for interpolation after being established for optical flow, suggests a reusable design pattern for other spherical reconstruction tasks (segmentation, depth). However, the 365 ms/frame latency (~3 fps) renders the method unsuitable for real-time VR, limiting its near-term impact to offline post-production and compression pipelines; reducing computational cost to meet real-time constraints remains the key open problem.
