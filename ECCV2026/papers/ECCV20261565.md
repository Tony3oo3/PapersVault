## Problem

Automated block decomposition of 3D CAD models into sweepable sub-blocks is a critical preprocessing step for hexahedral mesh generation, yet existing RL-based methods either handle only 2D models or depend on large-scale offline datasets and pre-training, making them impractical for small-batch industrial settings where task-matched training data is unavailable. No pure online RL framework has been demonstrated for 3D CAD block decomposition that learns entirely through interaction with a single target model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 3D B-rep solid model as input, processed through the OpenCascade geometric kernel to extract a geometric Attributed Adjacency Graph (gAAG) with face-level and edge-level attributes. The output is a sequence of planar cutting operations that recursively decompose the model into sweepable sub-blocks, where the final decomposition is directly taken from the highest-reward episode during online learning—no separate inference pass is required. Termination of a sub-block in the decomposition tree occurs when it is sweepable or its volume falls below 1% of the original model volume.

## Method

The decomposition is formulated as a tree-structured MDP solved with Soft Actor-Critic, where each cutting action branches one block into multiple children rather than producing a linear trajectory. Two core architectural designs enable lightweight pure online learning: (1) a decoupled frozen feature encoder that uses a pre-trained AAGNet GCN on the gAAG to produce global state features without participating in RL gradient updates, drastically reducing trainable parameters; and (2) a continuous-to-discrete action projection mechanism where the Actor outputs a 6D ideal cutting-plane feature (point + normal) that is matched via cosine similarity against a pre-computed candidate surface matrix, guaranteeing geometric validity while retaining SAC's continuous-space optimization stability. The reward function combines a fixed per-step penalty, a dense sweepable-volume-and-quality reward (weighted aspect ratio and compactness), and a sparse terminal reward proportional to final sweepable volume ratio. The agent learns purely online through a closed-loop explore-act-reward-update cycle on the target model, terminating when a sliding-window reward fluctuation criterion is met or a maximum episode limit is reached.

## Results

On the cross-paradigm comparison (Table 3), the proposed pure online RL framework achieves 95.79% SVR and 96.47% QHMR with 12.17 average cutting steps, closely matching the Hybrid-FT baseline (96.10% SVR, 96.82% QHMR) while requiring zero pre-training. The total delivery time is 36.05 min per target model, compared to 138.09 min for the offline RL baseline and 173.65 min for Hybrid-FT, yielding a 4.8× speedup over the hybrid paradigm. On the self-built dataset ablation (Table 2), the full method achieves 100% SVR and 97.43% QHMR with 14.04 steps in 34.12 min total time, outperforming the DCA ablation (86.39% SVR, 69.27% QHMR) and matching JT-RL quality with 13% less RL time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two cross-paradigm baselines: the state-of-the-art offline RL method by Zhang et al. [35] and a hybrid fine-tuning variant that initializes from offline pre-trained weights. Against the offline RL baseline, the proposed method wins decisively on all quality metrics (SVR 95.79% vs 90.01%, QHMR 96.47% vs 93.82%) and requires roughly half the cutting steps (12.17 vs 22.37), while also reducing total delivery time from 138.09 min to 36.05 min by eliminating the 137.6-min pre-training phase. Against Hybrid-FT, the proposed method is essentially tied on decomposition quality (0.31% SVR gap, 0.35% QHMR gap) and identical on per-target runtime (36.05 min), but achieves a 4.8× reduction in total delivery time (36.05 vs 173.65 min) by requiring no offline dataset or pre-training. The key trade-off is that Hybrid-FT achieves marginally higher quality (96.10% vs 95.79% SVR), but at the cost of a 137.6-min one-time pre-training investment that is only amortizable across large homogeneous batches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based CAD block decomposition line of work from DiPrete et al. (2D, 2023) and Zhang et al. (offline 3D, 2025) by formulating the first pure online RL framework for 3D models. It inherits the tree-structured MDP formulation and branching transition model from Zhang et al., the planar cutting surface extraction strategy from Wu & Gao (2014), and the SAC algorithm as the policy optimization backbone. The key architectural departures are the decoupled frozen AAGNet feature encoder (avoiding end-to-end joint training) and the continuous-to-discrete action projection mechanism that bridges SAC's continuous output space with discrete geometric operations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck in industrial hexahedral meshing: the absence of large-scale labeled datasets for block decomposition makes offline RL paradigms infeasible for small-batch, topology-divergent production settings. The finding that pure online learning on a single model matches hybrid fine-tuning within 0.3% quality gap suggests that task-specific pre-training may be unnecessary for this class of geometric decision problems. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-planar cuts, high-genus topologies, and whether the per-model online optimization cost (~36 min) is acceptable at scale; the current planar-cut-only action space and single-model learning scope limit immediate industrial adoption.
