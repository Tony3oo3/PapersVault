## Problem

Existing VLM-based 3D indoor layout generation methods model inter-asset relations implicitly or enforce only local pairwise constraints, producing scenes that are locally plausible but globally semantically inconsistent. Additionally, their reliance on gradient-based optimization initialized from VLM predictions makes them highly sensitive to the non-convex, discontinuous feasible layout space, frequently converging to physically infeasible configurations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260983/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form natural language scene description and a set of unlabeled 3D assets (retrieved via CLIP-based matching from 3D-FUTURE, Objaverse, and HSSD-200) as input, and produces a complete 3D scene layout specifying the pose (x, y, z, θ) for each asset within a bounded room environment. Scenes can contain up to 112 objects across 11 room types, with assets organized hierarchically into floor-level and furniture-level groups. The VLM backbone (GPT-4o or GPT-4.1) is queried iteratively for semantic constraint generation and conflict resolution rather than for direct pose prediction.

## Method

The pipeline decouples semantic coherence from physical feasibility into two dedicated modules. Global Semantic Verification (GSV) formalizes VLM-predicted pairwise relations into a directed scene graph, then applies rule-based verification predicates (cycle/distance/wall/occupancy consistency, relational completeness, and semantic compatibility) to detect and prune contradictory configurations through iterative VLM-guided edge refinement until a globally consistent constraint graph is obtained. Global Physical Feasibility Search (GPFS) then optimizes asset poses using a hybrid evolutionary-gradient strategy: a population-based evolutionary search with swap-based crossover and center-guided Gaussian mutation (incorporating intra-group attraction and pairwise repulsion) performs global exploration from random initialization, followed by gradient descent on the top-μ elites for local refinement. This design eliminates dependence on VLM-proposed initialization and enables robust optimization in the highly non-convex feasible space.

## Results

On the 11-room-type benchmark (33 scenes, up to 112 objects per scene), the proposed method achieves average CF = 97.0, IB = 97.0, Pos. = 85.2, Rot. = 82.3, and PSA = 78.5, substantially outperforming all baselines on every metric. The GPT-4.1 variant marginally improves over GPT-4o on Pos. (85.2 vs. 83.3 on Bedroom) and Rot. (82.3 vs. 80.0 on Bedroom). On the ablation subset of 5 scene types, the full pipeline reaches CF = 100.0 and IB = 100.0, confirming near-perfect physical feasibility when the hybrid evolutionary-gradient optimizer converges.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dominates all four baselines on every metric across all 11 room types. Compared to LayoutGPT, the largest gap is on IB (97.0 vs. 24.2) and PSA (78.5 vs. 16.6), reflecting LayoutGPT's reliance on local pairwise constraints that fail to enforce global physical feasibility. Against Holodeck, the advantage is most pronounced on CF (97.0 vs. 6.7) and Pos. (85.2 vs. 24.2), where Holodeck's static scene-graph assumption breaks down under dense or entangled layouts. Versus I-Design and LayoutVLM (which report nearly identical average scores of CF ≈ 76.8, IB ≈ 34.3, PSA ≈ 18.0), the proposed method improves CF by ~20 points, IB by ~63 points, and PSA by ~60 points, demonstrating that the combination of explicit graph-level semantic verification (GSV) and hybrid evolutionary-gradient search (GPFS) is critical for both semantic coherence and physical plausibility in open-vocabulary settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-based 3D indoor layout generation paradigm established by LayoutGPT, Holodeck, I-Design, and LayoutVLM, which rely on implicit relational modeling or local pairwise regularization during gradient-based optimization. It departs from this lineage by introducing two explicit mechanisms: (1) a rule-based scene graph verification step (GSV) that enforces global semantic consistency before optimization, repurposing scene graph representations from retrieval and comparison tasks into a constraint-validation role; and (2) a hybrid evolutionary-gradient optimizer (GPFS) that replaces the standard VLM-initialized gradient descent with population-based global exploration followed by local refinement. The paper does not build on a single prior architecture but rather recombines scene graph formalism, evolutionary search, and differentiable optimization into a unified pipeline that decouples semantic coherence from physical feasibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of semantic verification from physical optimization addresses a structural weakness in current VLM-based layout methods, where local pairwise constraints cannot guarantee scene-level consistency. The hybrid evolutionary-gradient strategy offers a practical recipe for tackling highly non-convex, discontinuous feasible spaces that arise from collision, support, and stability constraints—relevant beyond indoor layout to any constrained 3D arrangement problem. However, the pipeline's dependence on GPT APIs introduces run-to-run stochasticity in asset selection and constraint generation, and the evaluation relies on GPT-4o as a visual-language judge over only 33 scenes, limiting confidence in the reported margins. Open questions include whether the graph verification step can be learned end-to-end rather than rule-based, and whether the evolutionary component scales to scenes with hundreds of objects.
