## Problem

Generative monocular depth estimation models achieve superior detail fidelity over discriminative regressors, but their O(N²) attention complexity and multi-step denoising make high-resolution inference computationally prohibitive. Naively replacing standard attention with linear kernels and reducing to one-step sampling introduces severe structural misalignment, pixel-level detail loss, and sampling noise, leaving a gap between generative quality and discriminative speed.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB conditioning image and a noisy depth map (concatenated along the channel dimension) as input, and directly outputs a clean relative depth map in pixel-space without any VAE or latent autoencoder. Depth is normalized to log-scale using per-image 2nd/98th percentile clipping and rescaled to unit variance. Trained at 1024×768, the architecture scales to 1080P and 1440P at test time thanks to its linear-time attention, with a single forward pass producing the final depth map.

## Method

Lapis replaces all multi-head self-attention layers in a DiT-Large backbone (520M params) with kernel-based linear attention (φ(x)=elu(x)+1), reducing complexity to O(N). A Patch-level Consistency Module enforces global coherence by injecting frozen DINOv2 semantic features via extended AdaLN-Zero conditioning and adds a residual 3×3 depth-wise convolution (ConvFFN) to suppress inter-patch grid artifacts. A Pixel-level Refinement Module recovers sharp boundaries by skip-connecting raw input pixels (with learnable positional encoding) through PixelShuffle upsampling and AdaLN-Zero-modulated ConvFFN decoder blocks. The model performs one-step x-prediction in pixel-space under a flow-matching linear schedule, grounded in the manifold assumption that natural depth resides on a low-dimensional manifold, and is trained with a joint velocity MSE loss plus multi-scale L1 gradient matching loss.

## Results

On native-resolution zero-shot evaluation across five benchmarks (NYUv2, KITTI, ETH3D, ScanNet, DIODE), Lapis achieves an average AbsRel of 4.1% and δ₁ of 98.2%, surpassing the strongest generative baseline Pixel-Perfect Depth (4.2% / 98.0%) and the best discriminative model MoGe-2 (4.8% / 97.1%). At 1080P test-time resolution, Lapis maintains a 5.1% average AbsRel, a ~10% relative improvement over Pixel-Perfect Depth (5.7%), while at 1440P it reaches 5.2% versus 6.5% for Pixel-Perfect Depth. In terms of efficiency, Lapis reduces inference latency by 7.6× at 1080P (119 ms vs. 905 ms) and 10.9× at 1440P (225 ms vs. 2457 ms) compared to Pixel-Perfect Depth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lapis outperforms the strongest prior generative model, Pixel-Perfect Depth [69], on all five native-resolution benchmarks in AbsRel (e.g., 3.8 vs. 3.7 on NYUv2, 5.1 vs. 5.4 on KITTI, 4.8 vs. 4.9 on DIODE) and matches or exceeds its δ₁ (98.2% vs. 98.0%). Against the best discriminative baselines, Lapis surpasses MoGe-2 [64] by 0.7% in average AbsRel (4.1 vs. 4.8) and 1.1% in δ₁ (98.2 vs. 97.1), and outperforms Depth Anything 3 [40] by 1.2% in AbsRel. At high resolutions (1080P/1440P), Lapis leads Pixel-Perfect Depth by 0.6–1.3% AbsRel and all other baselines by 1–3%, while being 7.6×–10.9× faster in latency. Compared to multi-step generative models like Marigold v1.1 [31] and Lotus-2 [24], Lapis achieves substantially better accuracy (e.g., 4.1% vs. 7.7% and 5.6% average AbsRel) with far lower latency (119 ms vs. 758 ms and 6953 ms at 1080P). The only marginal trade-off is that Lapis's 3.8% AbsRel on NYUv2 is slightly behind Pixel-Perfect Depth's 3.7%, though it wins on every other dataset and in overall average.

## Relation to Prior Work

Lapis sits at the intersection of three research threads: pixel-space diffusion Transformers for depth estimation (Pixel-Perfect Depth), linear attention for efficient sequence modeling (Katharopoulos et al.), and flow-matching-based one-step generation. It departs from the dominant latent-space generative paradigm (Marigold, Lotus-2) by operating directly in pixel-space to avoid VAE information loss, and from standard-attention DiTs by replacing all multi-head self-attention with kernel-based linear attention to achieve O(N) scaling. The key architectural departure is the coarse-to-fine hierarchy—Patch-level Consistency Module (semantic prior via DINOv2 + local convolutional bias) and Pixel-level Refinement Module (skip-connection pixel correspondence)—which the authors identify as necessary to close the dual fidelity gap (structural misalignment and detail loss) that naively linearizing a DiT introduces for dense regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: Lapis delivers SOTA zero-shot accuracy while reducing inference latency by 7.6×–10.9× at 1080P/1440P compared to the closest generative competitor, making high-resolution depth estimation feasible in near-real-time settings. The finding that a frozen DINOv2 semantic prior is the single most critical component (removing it causes AbsRel to jump from 4.8 to 19.8) suggests that global geometric coherence in linear-attention models is better delegated to external encoders than learned end-to-end. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other dense prediction tasks (normal estimation, optical flow), whether the DINOv2 dependency can be removed or replaced with lighter encoders, and whether the one-step x-prediction strategy in pixel-space can be further stabilized for even higher resolutions (4K+) without additional architectural changes.
