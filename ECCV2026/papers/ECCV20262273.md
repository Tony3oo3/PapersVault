## Problem

Directly transferring inference-time visual token pruning into on-policy RL for VLA models causes training instability or collapse: pruning during rollouts but not during updates (or under a different budget) breaks the likelihood-ratio alignment in GRPO, producing large KL divergence and persistent performance degradation. The paper identifies this exploration-exploitation pruning mismatch as the core obstacle to integrating pruning into the RL loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262273/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy receives a single RGB image (encoded into N visual patch tokens) and a fixed text instruction per episode. Before the language backbone, a pruning module reduces the visual tokens to a budget k (sampled from a spectrum distribution over keep ratios), and the pruned sequence is concatenated with instruction and special tokens. The transformer backbone outputs chunked action tokens, which are de-tokenized and unnormalized into continuous robot control actions; the pruning configuration (k, ε_t) is stored per transition and replayed during actor updates.

## Method

PARL-VLA enforces exploration-exploitation pruning consistency by recording each rollout's token budget k and selector perturbation ε_t, then replaying that exact configuration when recomputing action log-probabilities during GRPO updates, keeping likelihood ratios aligned with the rollout distribution. The pruning module scores visual tokens via text-conditioned cosine similarity (using a gated-pooled instruction query) and selects the top-k tokens with a stochastic hard Top-k rule optimized through a straight-through estimator, enabling end-to-end co-adaptation of the pruner and policy. Training follows a loose-to-aggressive curriculum over keep ratios (ρ from [0.75,1.0] down to [0.25,0.5]), exposing the policy to varying information density so a single checkpoint remains reliable across evaluation budgets. The framework is instantiated on OpenVLA-OFT with GRPO, discarding 55% of visual tokens on average while matching in-distribution success and improving out-of-distribution robustness by up to 6.6 points.

## Results

On LIBERO-Plus, PARL-VLA (High budget, 70% keep ratio) improves the overall mean robustness success from 70.2% (RLinf) to 74.0%, with the largest gain on the Goal suite (66.7% → 73.5%) and lighting perturbations (62.7% → 68.7%). On in-distribution LIBERO, PARL-VLA matches RLinf at 94.3–94.9% average success across token budgets. On RoboTwin2.0 real-robot evaluation (405 trials), PARL-VLA achieves 46.9% success (95% CI: 42.1–51.8%), outperforming RLinf at 37.0%. Pruning removes 55% of visual tokens on average, yielding ~1.3× faster rollout policy forward and ~1.7× faster policy updates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PARL-VLA consistently outperforms all three baselines across every evaluation setting. Against RLinf (full-token RL baseline), PARL-VLA improves LIBERO-Plus mean robustness by 3.8 points (74.0% vs 70.2%) while matching in-distribution LIBERO success (94.3–94.9% vs 94.4%), and achieves a 9.9-point gain on RoboTwin2.0 real-robot trials (46.9% vs 37.0%). Against FastV (evaluation-only pruning), PARL-VLA gains 7.0 points on LIBERO-Plus mean (74.0% vs 67.0%), 1.9–2.5 points on LIBERO in-distribution, and 25.7 points on real-robot success (46.9% vs 21.2%), demonstrating that pruning integrated into the RL loop substantially outperforms post-hoc inference-time pruning. Against VLA-Pruner (temporal-aware evaluation-only pruning), PARL-VLA gains 7.5 points on LIBERO-Plus mean (74.0% vs 66.5%), 2.5–3.1 points on LIBERO, and 20.2 points on real-robot success (46.9% vs 26.7%). The key finding is that evaluation-only pruning baselines actually degrade robustness relative to the full-token RLinf baseline, whereas pruning-aware on-policy training improves it, confirming that the exploration-exploitation pruning consistency mechanism is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: on-policy RL post-training for VLA models (RLinf, SimpleVLA-RL) and inference-time visual token pruning (FastV, SparseVLM, VLA-Pruner). Prior pruning methods are applied as a post-hoc inference acceleration or during supervised fine-tuning, treating the pruned observation as a fixed preprocessing step. PARL-VLA departs by embedding pruning inside the RL loop itself, identifying that the rollout-update pruning mismatch breaks the likelihood-ratio alignment that GRPO requires, and resolving it through a replay mechanism that records and reuses per-sample pruning configurations. The spectrum-training curriculum (loose-to-aggressive keep ratios) further extends fixed-budget pruning into a multi-budget training strategy that has no direct analogue in prior inference-time pruning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for RL-based VLA training, which is bottlenecked by repeated policy forward passes: removing 55% of visual tokens yields ~1.3× faster rollouts and ~1.7× faster updates without sacrificing in-distribution success, making large-scale VLA-RL more tractable. The robustness gains (up to 6.6 points on LIBERO-Plus, 46.9% vs 37.0% on real-robot RoboTwin2.0) suggest that forced information scarcity during training acts as a regularizer against visual distribution shift. Open questions include whether the pruning-consistency principle generalizes beyond GRPO to other on-policy algorithms (PPO, SAC), whether the spectrum curriculum can be replaced by a learned budget distribution, and whether the approach extends to multi-view or video-based VLA policies where temporal token redundancy is more complex.
