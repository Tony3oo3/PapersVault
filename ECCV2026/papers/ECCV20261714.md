## Problem

Incorporating higher-order geometric structures (coplanarity, parallelism, wireframe connectivity) into bundle adjustment breaks the block-diagonal landmark Hessian that enables efficient Schur complement elimination, and formulating such constraints as direct 3D penalties introduces scale-dependent weighting that degrades conditioning and biases the optimizer toward shrinking the reconstruction. No prior framework integrates diverse geometric primitives and their inter-feature relations while preserving the asymptotic complexity and numerical stability of classical point-only BA.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261714/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (resized to ≤800 px on the longer side) with detected 2D keypoints and line segments, along with per-image group associations (lines→vanishing points, points/lines→plane segments) and wireframe point-line junctions derived from spatial incidences. Cross-image group matchings are established by voting over matched features or IoU checks of warped segmentation masks, and 3D constraints are instantiated only when supported by more than 3 images. The output is a jointly optimized set of camera poses, 3D points, 3D line segments (in Plücker coordinates), and group parameters (plane normals/offsets, vanishing point directions), all refined under a unified pixel-space cost.

## Method

The paper introduces a taxonomy separating features (points, lines with direct 2D measurements) from groups (planes, VPs, other primitives encoding higher-order relations), and places group parameters alongside cameras in the non-eliminated block of the normal equations so that each residual involves at most one feature, preserving the block-diagonal structure over the feature Hessian. Group constraints are formulated as 2D reprojection differences: a feature is projected onto the group surface and the pixel displacement between the original and constrained reprojections serves as the residual, which implicitly weights each constraint by the feature's Fisher information (inverse covariance) without requiring ad hoc 3D scaling. Cross-feature wireframe constraints are reformulated so that one feature's 2D observation acts as a fixed measurement while the other is the optimization variable, decoupling the two features in the Hessian while still enforcing their geometric relation. All residuals live in pixel space, enabling a consistent convergence criterion and avoiding the scale-gauge ambiguity of mixed 2D/3D formulations. The resulting system is solved with Ceres using analytical Jacobians, and the Schur complement reduction yields a camera-plus-group system whose sparsity pattern is identical to classical BA, giving comparable per-iteration runtime even with thousands of lines and dozens of groups.

## Results

On camera pose accuracy (relative pose AUC), Holistic SfM achieves 92.5°/93.4°/94.2° (AUC@3°/5°/10°) on Hypersim and 87.4°/89.4°/90.6° on ScanNet++, consistently outperforming both COLMAP and Point-Line SfM across all four evaluated datasets. On geometry, group constraints improve point inlier ratio at 1 mm from 9.3% to 11.5% and line length recall at 1 mm from 72.1 m to 93.2 m on Hypersim. Runtime remains comparable to classical point-only BA: on 1DSfM scenes, Holistic SFM runs at ~1.2–1.5× the point baseline (e.g., 59.2 min vs. 46.4 min on Tower of London), while producing reconstructions with 2.8k lines and 117 group primitives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: COLMAP (point-only SfM) and Point-Line SfM (adding 3D lines with Plücker parameterization). Holistic SfM wins on all four datasets for camera pose AUC. The largest gains are on ScanNet++ (indoor, rich geometric structure), where Holistic SfM improves AUC@3° by 3.4 points over COLMAP (87.4 vs. 84.0) and by 2.7 points over Point-Line SfM (87.4 vs. 84.7). On Hypersim, the improvement over COLMAP is 3.2 points at AUC@3° (92.5 vs. 89.3), while the margin over Point-Line SfM is smaller (92.5 vs. 92.2), suggesting that lines alone capture much of the benefit and groups add the remaining gain. On ETH3D and 7Scenes, Holistic SfM also leads all baselines by 1–3 points across thresholds. In terms of runtime, Holistic SFM is ~1.3× slower than the point baseline on 1DSfM but still faster than or comparable to COLMAP's total pipeline time, and substantially faster than the 2–4× overhead reported by prior line-based SfM systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classical bundle adjustment (Triggs et al.) and the authors' own prior work on 3D line mapping (Liu et al., CVPR 2023) and point-line SfM (Liu et al., ECCV 2024) by introducing a unified treatment of higher-order geometric groups (planes, vanishing points, primitives) and cross-feature wireframe constraints. The key departure is from prior approaches that either enforce structural relations via 3D regularization terms (point-to-plane, point-to-line distances) or direct pairwise feature constraints, both of which break the block-diagonal Hessian structure or introduce scale-dependent weighting. By reinterpreting groups as camera-like variables in the non-eliminated Schur block and expressing all structural constraints as 2D reprojection differences, the paper preserves the sparsity pattern that underpins efficient large-scale BA solvers like Ceres and COLMAP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, tuning-free way to incorporate rich geometric priors (coplanarity, parallelism, wireframe junctions) into SfM/SLAM pipelines without the ad hoc 3D weight calibration that plagues existing methods, as confirmed by the ablation showing 3D constraints degrade with excessive weighting. The Fisher information interpretation gives a clean statistical grounding for how well-observed features anchor group surfaces. A key limitation is the reliance on reasonably accurate upstream associations (feature-group and point-line matching), which the authors acknowledge as a practical bottleneck; integrating confidence-aware association selection or learned structural priors (e.g., via VLMs like SAM3, hinted at in Fig. 1) is a natural next step. The framework's generality—supporting arbitrary projection operators as groups—opens the door to incorporating new primitive types as they become available from modern perception models.
