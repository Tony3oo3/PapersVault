## Problem

State-of-the-art monocular metric geometry estimation models suffer from a persistent 'scale-collapse' phenomenon where distant landmarks and vast landscapes are metrically underestimated (e.g., predicting the Arc de Triomphe as 18.8 m wide instead of 44.8 m). The paper identifies this as a training data bottleneck: existing metric-scale datasets are restricted to narrow hardware-constrained scenarios (street-level LiDAR, short-range indoor scans) or synthetic data lacking real-world semantic complexity, leaving models without sufficient scale-diverse, in-the-wild supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260603/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model (WildMoGe) takes a single unconstrained RGB image as input and outputs a metric depth map, a metric 3D point map, and predicted camera intrinsics. The MetricScenes dataset provides supervision in the form of dense metric depth maps constructed from three sources: Internet photo collections (AerialMegaDepth, MegaScenes) scaled via geo-tagged metadata, and stereo video (Stereo4D) scaled via known camera baselines. Depth maps are initially sparse (lacking foreground transients or distant backgrounds) and are completed via the proposed two-stage Poisson method before serving as training labels.

## Method

The paper introduces MetricScenes, a 99,711-image dataset curated from AerialMegaDepth, MegaScenes, and Stereo4D, where absolute metric scale is recovered by registering geo-tagged street-view images to SfM reconstructions (via MASt3R matching, Doppelganger disambiguation, and RANSAC similarity alignment to ECEF coordinates) or by aligning predicted stereo baselines to documented physical camera baselines. A key technical contribution is a two-stage edge-aware Poisson completion algorithm: Stage 1 solves a log-domain Poisson equation using only sparse background MVS anchors to establish correct background scale without foreground distortion; Stage 2 then uses the completed background and MoGe-2's foreground prediction as joint anchors in an edge-weighted Poisson solve (Eq. 3) that preserves sharp depth discontinuities and prevents scale drift. The final model, WildMoGe, is obtained by fine-tuning MoGe-2 ViT-Large-Normal on MetricScenes for ~3 epochs with a low learning rate (1e-6 backbone, 1e-5 other), which significantly mitigates scale-collapse on in-the-wild scenes while maintaining competitive performance on standard benchmarks.

## Results

On the MetricScenes test set, WildMoGe dramatically outperforms MoGe-2 on metric geometry: metric point Relp drops from 11.7 to 7.59, and metric depth δ₁ nearly doubles from 37.7% to 73.8%. On standard benchmarks (10 datasets), WildMoGe matches or slightly exceeds MoGe-2 on relative geometry (Point Scale-inv. Relp 10.1 vs. 10.8) while showing a small regression on metric depth (Relp 15.6 vs. 15.7, δ₁ 73.4% vs. 76.8%), attributed to domain shift. Across all baselines, WildMoGe achieves the best relative geometry scores on standard benchmarks and the best metric geometry scores on the in-the-wild MetricScenes test set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MoGe-2 (the primary baseline and backbone), WildMoGe wins decisively on the MetricScenes in-the-wild test set: metric depth δ₁ jumps from 37.7% to 73.8% and metric point Relp drops from 11.7 to 7.59, while relative geometry also improves (Point Scale-inv. Relp 5.24 vs. 6.76). On standard benchmarks, WildMoGe is essentially on par with MoGe-2, with a slight edge in relative geometry (Point Relp 10.1 vs. 10.8) but a small regression in metric depth δ₁ (73.4% vs. 76.8%), which the authors attribute to domain shift. Against UniDepth V2, WildMoGe outperforms on both relative geometry (Point Relp 10.1 vs. 11.6) and metric geometry (Point Relp 8.39 vs. 10.1; Depth Relp 15.6 vs. 21.3) on standard benchmarks. Depth Pro and MASt3R are substantially weaker on metric geometry (Depth Relp 27.6 and 49.7 respectively vs. WildMoGe's 15.6). DA V3 and Metric3D V2 show intermediate relative geometry but lack full metric point-map predictions, limiting direct comparison. Overall, WildMoGe is the only method that simultaneously achieves top-tier relative geometry and strong metric accuracy in unconstrained in-the-wild settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a data-driven response to the scale-collapse problem that architectural innovations (Metric3D's virtual camera space, UniDepth's self-prompting module, MoGe-2's DINOv2 global-token scale prediction) have failed to resolve. It builds directly on MoGe-2 as the base model, extending its single-stage logarithmic Poisson completion with a two-stage edge-aware variant that decouples foreground and background metric anchoring. The dataset construction pipeline repurposes existing SfM/MVS infrastructure (MASt3R-SfM, COLMAP, π³) and geo-referenced metadata to recover absolute scale from sources that were previously only usable for scale-invariant training, effectively converting the semantic diversity of Internet photo collections and VR180 stereo video into metric supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central claim—that scale-collapse in in-the-wild metric depth estimation is fundamentally a training-data bottleneck rather than an architectural limitation—redirects the field's attention from model design to data curation, and the 99K-image MetricScenes dataset provides a concrete, reusable resource to that end. However, the quantitative evidence is somewhat circular: the primary test set uses pseudo-labels derived from the same pipeline that generated the training data, and the model shows a slight regression on standard benchmarks (e.g., metric depth δ₁ drops from 76.8 to 73.4), suggesting the gains may partly reflect in-distribution familiarity rather than true generalization. Open questions include whether the two-stage Poisson completion strategy transfers to other base models, whether the dataset can be expanded to cover additional scale ranges (e.g., very large landscapes, microscopic scenes), and whether joint training with the original MoGe-2 data can recover the small benchmark regression without sacrificing the in-the-wild gains.
