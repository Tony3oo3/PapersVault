## Problem

Existing multimodal latent reasoning methods rely on external supervision (auxiliary images, annotated regions) and ignore the intrinsic visual attention dynamics that drive reasoning. The paper identifies a 'Perception Gap' in distillation: student models trained with standard SFT can closely match a teacher's textual output while attending to fundamentally divergent visual regions, effectively relying on language priors rather than grounded perception. This gap is particularly severe for fine-grained visual grounding tasks where knowing *where* to look matters as much as *what* to say.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text instruction as input and autoregressively generates K=4 continuous latent visual tokens (serving as 'Visual Information Containers') before producing the final textual response. The latent tokens are not discrete tokens but continuous hidden states that compress the teacher's visual semantics and gaze trajectory. White-box access to the teacher (Qwen2.5-VL-32B) is required to extract attention maps and last-layer hidden states for supervision; the student (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) operates on the same image-text interface at inference time with no additional inputs.

## Method

LaViT distills a 32B teacher's internal visual cognition into a 3B student via two alignment objectives: a semantic reconstruction loss (cosine similarity between projected student latent states and the teacher's contextualized visual embeddings) and a trajectory alignment loss (KL divergence between student and teacher attention distributions over visual patches, applied only to the final latent token to enforce a compression bottleneck). A key architectural innovation is Curriculum Sensory Gating, which modulates the direct image-to-text attention path via a time-dependent bias ln(γ(t)) that starts near −∞ (blocking direct visual access) and smoothly ramps to 0 over a warm-up period, physically compelling the model to route visual information through the latent tokens before gradually restoring full perception. Teacher attention maps are sparsified to Top-N=8 patches to filter low-confidence noise, and the training data (LaViT-SFT-15K) is filtered through a three-stage pipeline (correctness, difficulty, visual-alignment) to ensure high-quality supervision. The total loss combines next-token prediction with a fixed-weight distillation term (λ=0.3), avoiding complex multi-stage scheduling.

## Results

LaViT-3B achieves headline gains of +16.94% on BLINK Relative Depth (78.23%) and +15.67% on Relative Reflectance (45.52%) over the Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline, while also improving MMVP by +5.0% (67.33%) and MMStar by +3.87% (54.07%). On the BLINK IQ-Test, LaViT reaches 32.0%, surpassing GPT-4o (30.0%) and the 32B teacher (30.0%). The compact 3B model outperforms the larger Qwen2.5-VL-7B on five of seven evaluated benchmarks, demonstrating that latent visual-thought alignment is more parameter-efficient than simple scale-up for perception-intensive reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the same-size Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline, LaViT wins on all seven benchmarks, with the largest margins on Relative Depth (+16.94) and Relative Reflectance (+15.67). Compared to the larger Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LaViT-3B outperforms on five of seven tasks (MMVP 67.33 vs 66.7, V* 82.61 vs 77.39, Rel. Depth 78.23 vs 71.77, IQ-Test 32.0 vs 26.0, Rel. Ref. 45.52 vs 38.8) but trails on Spatial (81.82 vs 87.4) and MMStar (54.07 vs 58.9). Against the SOTA latent-reasoning model LVR-7B, LaViT wins on Relative Depth (78.23 vs 76.61) and Relative Reflectance (45.52 vs 42.5) but loses on MMVP (67.33 vs 72.0), V* (82.61 vs 84.4), Spatial (81.82 vs 89.5), and MMStar (54.07 vs 59.4). LaViT also surpasses GPT-4o on Relative Depth (78.23 vs 64.52), IQ-Test (32.0 vs 30.0), and Relative Reflectance (45.52 vs 38.8), while GPT-4o retains the edge on MMStar (63.9 vs 54.07). Among 3B reasoning baselines, LaViT dominates DMLR and PAPO across nearly all metrics, and edges out Naive SFT on perception and reasoning tasks though Naive SFT slightly leads on MMStar (55.53 vs 54.07).

## Relation to Prior Work

LaViT sits at the intersection of two active threads: latent visual reasoning in MLLMs (which compresses intermediate reasoning into continuous tokens supervised by static signals like auxiliary images or bounding boxes) and knowledge distillation for vision-language models (which typically aligns logit distributions or hidden-state features). The paper departs from both by arguing that neither static visual supervision nor output-level alignment captures the *dynamic* attention trajectory that underlies grounded reasoning. Its core methodological novelty is the combination of white-box attention-trajectory distillation (KL divergence on gaze maps plus cosine alignment on contextualized visual semantics) with a Curriculum Sensory Gating mechanism that progressively opens the direct visual path, a design not present in prior latent-reasoning or distillation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Perception Gap diagnosis—students reproduce teacher text while attending to different visual regions—reframes a practical failure mode in MLLM distillation that was previously invisible, and the finding that Top-N sparsification makes the student *more* stable than the teacher (CV 0.102 vs. 0.392) suggests distillation can act as a denoising filter rather than mere imitation. Limitations include the strict white-box requirement (only open-weight teachers with accessible attention maps), evaluation confined to a single model family (Qwen2.5-VL) and a handful of benchmarks, and a modest 15K training set. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to black-box or proprietary teachers, whether the curriculum schedule generalizes beyond 1000 training steps, and whether the gains persist on broader, more diverse reasoning suites.
