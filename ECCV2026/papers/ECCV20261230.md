## Problem

Sparse decision-based black-box attacks have received limited attention in semantic segmentation, where the search space scales with dense, multi-class outputs. The most representative existing method, RFPAR, suffers from severe query inefficiency in this setting because its image-centric pixel accumulation concentrates queries on a few easy-to-exploit regions, failing to cover heterogeneous vulnerabilities across multiple semantic classes. Additionally, standard decision rewards generate misleading positive feedback for reversion and fluctuation events, biasing the agent toward suboptimal regions.

## Input / Output

SegPAR takes a single RGB image and produces a sparse adversarial perturbation where a small fraction of pixels (targeting ~5% within each predicted class region) are set to binary values {0, 255}. The attack operates under a strict budget of 1,000 forward queries using only hard-label decisions (no confidence scores or gradients), with class-wise masks fixed from the initial model prediction and never updated. Evaluation is performed on 100 validation images from Cityscapes (19 classes), ADE20K (150 classes), and VOC2012 (20 classes) against CNN and Transformer segmentation models.

## Method

SegPAR reformulates RFPAR into a class-centric exploration paradigm: instead of conditioning the RL agent on the full image, it constructs per-class cropped states from bounding boxes of predicted class masks, and the agent samples perturbation locations via a masked softmax distribution that assigns zero probability outside the target class region, preventing signal contamination from non-target pixels. The agent outputs a 4-channel score map (1 spatial + 3 RGB channels) and independently samples Bernoulli decisions for each RGB channel at n sampled locations. A key innovation is the discrepancy reward R_d, which evaluates attack progress relative to the initial prediction rather than step-to-step transitions, yielding r=+1 only for newly misclassified pixels, r=−1 for reverted pixels, and r=0 for maintained or fluctuating pixels—thereby eliminating the misleading positive feedback that the standard reward assigns to reversion and fluctuation events. The policy is trained via REINFORCE with label-only queries over up to 100 steps, storing the best perturbation and discrepancy mask in memory before reinitializing for the next step.

## Results

SegPAR_Rd achieves the lowest Robust MIoU (R.MIoU) across all four victim models and three datasets under a 1,000-query budget. On Cityscapes, it drops PSPNet's MIoU from 0.793 to 0.057 and DeepLabV3's from 0.798 to 0.101, with sparsity as low as 3.33–3.53%. On VOC2012, it reaches R.MIoU of 0.238 (DeepLabV3) and 0.240 (PSPNet) at only 2.24–2.29% sparsity with ~680 queries—substantially fewer than the ~990+ queries needed by baselines. Against Transformer-based models (SegFormer, SETR), SegPAR still achieves R.MIoU of 0.341 and 0.481 on Cityscapes at ~3.9% sparsity, far below the 10.51% sparsity required by PointWise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegPAR_Rd decisively outperforms all three decision-based black-box baselines (PointWise, SparseEvo, RFPAR_Rs) on every dataset and model, achieving 30–70% lower R.MIoU at 1–3% lower sparsity. Against the closest black-box competitor RFPAR_Rs, SegPAR reduces Cityscapes R.MIoU from 0.176 to 0.101 (DeepLabV3) and from 0.153 to 0.057 (PSPNet), while also using fewer perturbed pixels. In the pixel-accumulation ablation (Table 3), applying the discrepancy reward to Pixle and Sparse-RS also improves their MIoU reduction on ADE20K and VOC2012, confirming the reward's generality. Against white-box baselines, SegPAR_Rd outperforms PGD0 and approaches sPGD under the same 1,000-step budget (Fig. 6), though sPGD retains a slight edge at the lowest sparsity levels. On adversarially trained models (DDCAT, SAT), SegPAR maintains its lead over all black-box baselines, with R.MIoU drops of 0.384–0.428 on Cityscapes and 0.297–0.332 on VOC2012.

## Relation to Prior Work

SegPAR directly extends RFPAR (Song et al., NeurIPS 2024), a reinforcement-learning-based sparse pixel attack originally designed for image classification and object detection, by reformulating its image-centric exploration into a class-centric paradigm suited to dense multi-class prediction. The paper also adapts decision-based sparse attacks from the classification literature (PointWise, SparseEvo) and score-based accumulation methods (Sparse-RS, Pixle) as baselines for the segmentation setting, establishing what the authors frame as the first systematic benchmark for decision-based black-box sparse attacks on semantic segmentation. The discrepancy reward is a targeted fix for a specific failure mode of the standard transition-based reward in cumulative attack frameworks, drawing on the four-way pixel-state decomposition to identify where the standard reward provides misleading positive signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practically relevant threat model—sparse, decision-only perturbations that mimic real-world failure sources like sensor defects or localized physical tampering in autonomous driving perception pipelines—and demonstrates that even under strict query and sparsity budgets, segmentation models remain highly vulnerable. The observation that adversarial training (DDCAT, SAT) provides substantially less protection at lower resolutions (VOC2012) than at higher resolutions (Cityscapes) raises open questions about the resolution-dependence of robustness in dense prediction. A key limitation is that the discrepancy reward's effectiveness depends on the class-centric search-space reduction; applied to image-centric methods on high-resolution images, it can stagnate due to sparse informative reward events, suggesting the two components are tightly coupled rather than independently transferable.
