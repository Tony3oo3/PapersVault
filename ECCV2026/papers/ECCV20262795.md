## Problem

Linear probing on frozen vision foundation model features achieves high accuracy with far fewer labeled samples than classical multiclass sample complexity theory (Ω(dk log k/ε²)) would permit, yet no formal generalization certificate explains this discrepancy. The fundamental difficulty is that classical PAC-Bayes and Rademacher bounds treat all d feature dimensions equally, yielding vacuous certificates (gap O(√(d/n))) for the high-dimensional, low-sample regime where linear probing actually operates in practice.

## Input / Output

The method consumes ℓ₂-normalized frozen features (d-dimensional vectors, d ranging from 384 to 1536) extracted by a pretrained ViT encoder from RGB images, along with the empirical feature covariance Σ̂ computed from unlabeled data. It produces a PAC-Bayes generalization bound — a numerical upper certificate on the population 0-1 risk — parameterized by the participation ratio d_eff and the empirical classification margin γ̄, rather than a single class prediction. The bound is computed per-class with a union-bound penalty over k classes, and hyperparameters (ε, σ²) are jointly optimized via grid search to tighten the certificate.

## Method

The core device is a data-dependent Gaussian prior π = N(0, (σ²/d_eff)Σ̂) aligned to the feature covariance spectrum, which assigns broad prior variance to principal directions (large λ_j) and tight variance to noise directions (small λ_j). This causes the KL divergence between prior and posterior to decompose across the eigenbasis of Σ̂, with only the d_eff 'active' dimensions contributing meaningfully to the complexity term. The resulting PAC-Bayes bound (Theorem 3) scales as O(√(d_eff·Φ/n)) where Φ captures the classifier's alignment with the top eigenspace, rather than the classical O(√(d/n)). A water-filled posterior construction is essential for non-vacuity: an isotropic posterior yields a Θ(d) lower-order correction that makes the bound vacuous, while the eigenbasis-shaped posterior keeps this term controlled. The framework also yields a distribution-shift theorem showing d_eff increases linearly with subspace misalignment, and a label-efficient representation quality score S = γ̄²/d_eff that ranks models with zero or minimal labels.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (δ=0.05), the spectral PAC-Bayes bound is non-vacuous for all 10 discriminatively pretrained models, with DINOv2-B achieving the tightest certificate: bound 0.114 vs. training-holdout error 0.081 (ratio 1.40×). The representation quality score S = γ̄²/d_eff achieves Spearman ρ=0.91 with empirical linear-probing accuracy across 12 models, outperforming all prior transferability metrics. The label-free d_eff alone reaches ρ=0.85, and distribution-shift predictions (Δ_shift vs. bound) are perfectly co-monotonic (ρ=+1.00) across 7 target domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed score S = γ̄²/d_eff (Spearman ρ=0.91) outperforms all five prior transferability metrics on ImageNet-1K linear-probing accuracy ranking: PACTran-N (ρ=0.82), NLEEP (ρ=0.80), LogME (ρ=0.78), GBC (ρ=0.77), and H-score (ρ=0.76). Notably, d_eff alone—computed with zero labels—achieves ρ=0.85, surpassing H-score, LogME, and GBC while matching NLEEP, and requiring no labeled data at all. The gap over PACTran-N (the closest prior method, ρ=0.82) is 0.09 in Spearman correlation, and S requires only a small labeled set (≈50 examples) for the margin term. On the bound-tightness front, the spectral prior yields a 1.40× bound/error ratio for DINOv2-B, whereas replacing it with an isotropic prior produces a vacuous bound of 2.34 (Table 5 ablation), confirming that the spectral structure—not mere dimensionality reduction—is the source of the improvement over classical PAC-Bayes certificates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper converts the long-standing intuition that effective dimension (participation ratio) governs sample efficiency in structured feature spaces into the first formal PAC-Bayes certificate for linear probing on frozen vision foundation-model features. It builds directly on the PAC-Bayes framework (Catoni, McAllester) and data-dependent priors (Dziugaite & Roy), but departs from prior intrinsic-dimensionality analyses (Aghajanyan et al., Li et al.) that motivated the idea without producing non-vacuous certificates, and from PACTran (Ding et al.) which provided transferability metrics without generalization guarantees. The spectral data-dependent prior aligned to the feature covariance is the key technical departure from isotropic priors used in prior PAC-Bayes work for deep networks, and the paper explicitly positions itself against Lotfi et al.'s non-vacuous LLM bounds by showing the linear-probing setting admits tighter, spectrally-structured certificates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.40× bound-to-error ratio for DINOv2-B on ImageNet is remarkably tight for a PAC-Bayes certificate and provides the first formal explanation for why 768-dimensional linear classifiers generalize from far fewer samples than Ω(d) theory demands. The derived representation quality score S = γ²/d_eff offers a practical, label-efficient tool for model selection (ρ=0.91 vs. 0.82 for PACTran), and d_eff alone (zero labels, ρ=0.85) enables rapid encoder comparison. Key open questions include closing the training-vs-validation gap on ImageNet (the bound does not serve as an absolute certificate on the standard test split), extending the framework to nonlinear heads and PEFT adapters, and incorporating per-class margin structure into the distribution-shift analysis to predict accuracy degradation rather than merely certifying when the bound loosens.
