## Problem

Existing clinical AI systems in oncology are static predictors that estimate a single outcome from observed patient states, unable to model how disease evolves under specific treatments over longitudinal follow-ups. This limits their utility for treatment planning, which is fundamentally a sequential, counterfactual decision-making problem requiring simulation of how alternative interventions reshape future physiological states. While world models have succeeded in robotics and control, their adaptation to medicine remains limited by irregular clinical time intervals, heterogeneous multimodal patient context, and the need for interpretable, survival-aware decision support.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a 3D pre-treatment MRI volume (H×W×D×C) encoded by a frozen MRI-CORE foundation model with LoRA adapters, a serialized clinical profile (demographics, molecular biomarkers, prior therapies) encoded via MedGemma, a continuous follow-up time gap encoded with sinusoidal embeddings, and a free-text therapy description encoded into a dense vector. Outputs include a predicted post-treatment latent disease state, a continuous survival risk score, a calibrated one-year survival probability, and—through iterative MLLM-based search (K=3 rounds)—a recommended therapy policy. The framework also supports autoregressive multi-step rollout over arbitrary treatment schedules without requiring paired imaging at intermediate timepoints.

## Method

CLARITY models disease evolution as latent state transitions rather than pixel-space image synthesis. A Transformer-based Post-Treatment Latent Predictor takes the concatenated input [z_pre, h_clin, γ(Δt), h_drug] and predicts z_post, trained with an ℓ1 latent consistency loss plus a soft-label contrastive objective that uses cosine similarities of treatment text embeddings as soft targets to structure the latent space. A Survival Predictor with bidirectional cross-attention between pre- and post-treatment latents produces both a risk score (Cox partial likelihood) and a calibrated survival probability (Brier loss). The key decision-making innovation is Inverse Survival Evaluation: an MLLM (GPT-5) proposes candidate therapy sets, the Actor scores each via latent rollout, and the resulting survival risk signals are fed back as structured prompts to iteratively refine proposals under an entropy-regularized long-horizon objective, balancing exploitation of low-risk trajectories with exploration to avoid premature convergence.

## Results

On the primary MU-Glioma-Post benchmark, CLARITY achieves 57.1% F1 and 39.9% Jaccard for treatment planning, a 9.2-point absolute F1 improvement over the strongest medical baseline (Huatuo-Vision, 46.4%). Zero-shot generalization to UCSF-ALPTDG yields 50.2% F1, and independent training on ISPY-2 breast cancer reaches 53.9% F1, both outperforming all baselines. For survival prediction, CLARITY attains a C-index of 0.794, surpassing the best survival baseline (SurvTRACE, 0.713) by 8.1 points and producing a log-rank p-value of 0.0017 in Kaplan-Meier stratification versus 0.0763 for the diffusion-based MeWM baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLARITY outperforms all seven treatment-planning baselines across all three datasets. The strongest competitor, Huatuo-Vision (a medical-domain MLLM), is beaten by 9.2 F1 points on MU-Glioma-Post (57.1 vs. 46.4), 6.1 points on UCSF-ALPTDG (50.2 vs. 44.1), and 5.2 points on ISPY-2 (53.9 vs. 48.7). General-purpose LLMs (GPT-5, Claude-4.5-Sonnet, Qwen3-VL) perform substantially worse, with F1 scores ranging from 35.8 to 50.0, confirming that direct prompting without a simulation-to-decision loop is insufficient for treatment planning. The re-implemented diffusion-based MeWM* baseline trails CLARITY by 13.5 F1 points on MU-Glioma-Post, validating the paper's design choice of latent-state dynamics over pixel-space reconstruction. On survival prediction (Table 2), CLARITY's C-index of 0.794 exceeds SurvTRACE (0.713), MeWM* (0.701), RSF (0.672), and DeepSurv (0.664), with the largest gap (8.1 points) over the strongest survival-specific baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLARITY sits at the intersection of world models (DreamerV3, Genie, VJEPA) and medical survival analysis, but departs from both in key ways. From the world-model lineage, it replaces pixel-space generative reconstruction (as in MeWM and other diffusion-based medical predictors) with direct latent-state transitions, arguing that clinical decision-making requires robust feature-level dynamics rather than visually plausible image synthesis. From the survival-analysis lineage (DeepSurv, RSF, SurvTRACE), it moves beyond static risk estimation to sequential, treatment-conditioned trajectory simulation, framing treatment planning as an entropy-regularized planning problem over imagined futures. The repurposing of an MLLM (GPT-5) as a therapy policy agent within a closed-loop simulation-to-decision framework, rather than as a standalone predictor, is the paper's most distinctive architectural choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that latent-space disease dynamics with survival-aware feedback can outperform both diffusion-based medical generators and general-purpose LLMs on treatment planning, while being 9–15× more computationally efficient—making iterative multi-step planning practically feasible. The zero-shot transfer from one brain-tumor cohort to another (UCSF-ALPTDG) without fine-tuning is encouraging for generalization, though the small cohort sizes (203–985 patients) limit confidence. Open questions include whether the MLLM policy agent is truly necessary versus a simpler learned policy, how the framework scales to multi-organ or multi-modality settings, and whether the 40-case expert evaluation is sufficient for clinical trust. The broader implication is that world-model-style simulation could become a standard component of clinical decision support, but prospective clinical validation remains essential.
