## Problem

Most camera-controlled video generation methods rely exclusively on supervised fine-tuning, leaving online reinforcement learning post-training largely unexplored. Two barriers persist: designing verifiable, geometry-aware rewards for high-dimensional video outputs is nontrivial, and the continuous nature of camera trajectories makes instance-level rewards sparse, leading to inefficient credit assignment during optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260735/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an initial RGB frame (for image-to-video), a text prompt, and a user-specified 3D camera trajectory encoded as per-frame Plücker line coordinates (6×h×w per frame, derived from camera intrinsics and extrinsics). Videos are 2–10 seconds long with the longer side resized to 512 pixels, and the ST-VAE downsamples by 4× temporally and 8× spatially. The output is a temporally coherent video that follows the prescribed camera path while preserving scene layout and subject dynamics.

## Method

CAMVERSE post-trains a ~2B-parameter flow-matching video diffusion transformer via GRPO, where the key innovation is a verifiable geometry reward that converts 3D camera alignment into dense segment-level feedback. For each generated rollout, a feed-forward 3D model (π³) estimates per-frame camera extrinsics; the generated trajectory is aligned to the reference via an Umeyama similarity transform to resolve scale ambiguity. Both trajectories are partitioned into non-overlapping segments, and within each segment the relative end-to-start transform is computed, yielding per-segment translation and rotation errors that are converted to alignment scores. Group-relative advantages (z-score normalized across G rollouts) drive a clipped PPO-style objective with KL regularization against the SFT reference policy, while SDE sampling in the first three denoising steps injects exploration noise. LoRA (r=64) is applied to all linear layers, and a 315k-video dataset with diverse large-amplitude camera motions and dynamic subjects provides the training signal.

## Results

On the mixed test set (1k clips from RealEstate10K and the curated dataset), CAMVERSE (SFT + RL) achieves a translation error of 0.0293 and rotation error of 0.5140 in the T2V setting, reducing both by 25.8% and 21.0% respectively over its own SFT baseline (CAMVERSE*). In the I2V setting, the full model reaches 0.0286 translation error and 0.4685 rotation error, with geometric consistency of 0.9226. Visual quality (HPSv3) peaks at 5.91 in T2V; in I2V it drops slightly from 5.02 (SFT) to 4.87 (SFT+RL), consistent with the reward not directly optimizing perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAMVERSE dominates both baselines by large margins across all camera-control metrics. Against CameraCtrl [18], the full model reduces T2V translation error from 0.0887 to 0.0293 (−67%) and rotation error from 1.4586 to 0.5140 (−65%); in I2V the gaps are even wider (0.1696→0.0286 translation, 3.2809→0.4685 rotation). Against AC3D-5B [3], CAMVERSE cuts T2V translation error by 31.5% (0.0428→0.0293) and rotation error by 43.7% (0.9120→0.5140), while also improving geometric consistency from 0.8820 to 0.9173 and visual quality from 4.69 to 5.91. The SFT-only variant (CAMVERSE*) already surpasses both baselines, confirming the benefit of the curated 315k-sample dataset and training recipe; the additional RL post-training then provides a further 15–26% reduction in trajectory errors. The only metric where the full model does not improve is I2V visual quality (5.02→4.87), which the authors attribute to the geometry reward not directly optimizing perceptual fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based online RL post-training paradigm (Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO) from image/text generation to camera-controlled video diffusion, introducing a verifiable geometry reward that converts 3D camera trajectory alignment into dense segment-level feedback. It departs from prior camera-control methods (CameraCtrl, AC3D, CamCo) that rely exclusively on supervised fine-tuning by adding an RL stage that actively reinforces correct camera motion through a 3D reconstruction model (π³) as a reward oracle. The camera conditioning via Plücker embeddings and the flow-matching diffusion backbone follow established conventions, while the segment-wise relative pose comparison (rather than absolute trajectory matching) is the key methodological departure motivated by local smoothness requirements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that online RL post-training can meaningfully improve camera-control accuracy in video diffusion beyond what SFT achieves, opening a path toward incorporating strong 3D geometric priors into generative video models. The 315k-video dataset with diverse large-amplitude camera motions addresses a real gap in available training data. However, the VQ drop in I2V after RL, the persistent dataset bias toward straightforward translation (visible in failure cases), and the reliance on a separate 3D reconstruction model for reward computation all remain open challenges. The authors note future integration into world models and embodied AI data engines as promising directions.
