## Problem

Representation Alignment (REPA) accelerates latent-space diffusion transformer training by aligning intermediate features with a pretrained semantic encoder, yet naively applying it to pixel-space diffusion (JiT) degrades FID as training progresses at 256×256 resolution. The paper traces this failure to an information asymmetry: pixel space carries O(H×W) degrees of freedom while the semantic encoder produces a compact bottleneck representation, causing the model to overfit to the narrow feature space and collapse diversity among visually distinct but semantically similar images (termed 'feature hacking').
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261571/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label and Gaussian noise as input and generates 256×256 RGB images directly in pixel space without any VAE or tokenizer, using x-prediction under a flow-matching objective. During training, an intermediate JiT feature map is additionally fed through the Masked Transformer Adapter alignment branch against frozen DINOv2 features, but this branch is discarded at inference so sampling incurs no extra cost. Evaluation uses 50K samples with a 50-step Heun ODE solver and classifier-free guidance over interval [0.1, 1].

## Method

PixelREPA replaces the point-wise MLP projection in standard REPA with a Masked Transformer Adapter (MTA) that transforms the alignment target rather than forcing pixel features to conform to a compressed semantic space. The MTA consists of a 2-layer shallow Transformer adapter with self-attention (enabling contextual aggregation across tokens) and a random partial masking strategy (ratio r=0.2) applied to the adapter input, which breaks per-token correspondence to prevent shortcut regression and acts as an information bottleneck narrowing the pixel-to-semantic gap. The alignment loss is cosine similarity between frozen DINOv2 patch features and the adapter output, added to the JiT flow-matching loss with weight λ=0.1. Because the MTA operates only on a detached alignment branch during training, the main denoising pathway retains full-resolution tokens and the model incurs zero additional inference cost.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, PixelREPA-B/16 reduces FID from 3.66 to 3.17 (13.4% improvement) and increases IS from 275.1 to 284.6 over vanilla JiT-B/16, while achieving >2× faster convergence. PixelREPA-H/16 reaches FID 1.81 and IS 317.2, surpassing both JiT-H/16 (FID 1.86) and the nearly 2× larger JiT-G/16 (FID 1.82, IS 292.6). The improvement is consistent across scales: L/16 gains 10.6% relative FID reduction and H/16 gains 2.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixelREPA consistently outperforms its direct JiT baselines at every scale: B/16 improves FID from 3.66→3.17, L/16 from 2.36→2.11, and H/16 from 1.86→1.81, while also surpassing the 2B-parameter JiT-G/16 (FID 1.82) with only 953M parameters. Among pixel-space baselines, PixelREPA-H/16 (FID 1.81) beats PixelFlow (1.98), PixNerd (2.15), VDM++ (2.12), SiD (2.44), RIN (3.95), and ADM-G (7.72), but is outperformed by SiD2 UViT/1 (1.38) and SiD2 UViT/2 (1.73), which use a U-ViT backbone rather than a plain ViT. Against latent-space methods, PixelREPA-H/16 beats DiT-XL/2 (2.27) and SiT-XL/2 (2.06) but trails REPA+SiT-XL/2 (1.42), LightningDiT (1.35), DDT (1.26), and RAE (1.13), which benefit from the lower-dimensional latent denoising space. The key takeaway is that PixelREPA is the strongest plain-ViT pixel-space method at 953M parameters, closing the gap to latent-space SOTA while retaining the architectural simplicity and lack of tokenizer dependency that motivate pixel-space diffusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: REPA (Yu et al., 2024), which accelerates latent Diffusion Transformer training by aligning intermediate features with a frozen DINOv2 encoder via an MLP projection, and JiT (Li & He, 2025), which removes the latent tokenizer entirely and performs x-prediction flow matching directly in pixel space with a pure ViT. The key departure is identifying that REPA's alignment mechanism, effective in the compressed latent space, becomes harmful in the high-dimensional pixel space due to an information asymmetry between the denoising space and the semantic target. PixelREPA replaces the point-wise MLP with a Masked Transformer Adapter (a 2-block self-attention adapter plus random token masking), drawing on the masking principle from MAE (He et al., 2022) and the information bottleneck perspective to make the alignment target compatible with pixel-space representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'feature hacking' as a structured failure mode—where alignment to a compressed semantic target collapses diversity specifically on pixel-diverse but semantically clustered subsets—provides a principled diagnostic for when representation alignment helps versus hurts diffusion training. The practical impact is significant for the growing pixel-space diffusion community: PixelREPA achieves >2× faster convergence and better final FID without modifying the main denoising pathway, and the H/16 variant outperforms the nearly 2× larger G/16 model. Open questions include whether the information-asymmetry analysis and MTA fix generalize to other pixel-space architectures (SiD2, PixelFlow), whether the optimal masking ratio scales with resolution, and whether similar failure modes appear in other alignment-based acceleration techniques (e.g., attention alignment, multi-feature fusion for video).
