## Problem

DiT-based audio-driven portrait animation models suffer from high inference latency (e.g., ~10 minutes for a 15-second clip on 8×A800), and existing training-free diffusion caching methods are designed for text-conditioned generation where conditions are low-frequency and global. In portrait animation, the driving audio is a high-frequency, local signal while the reference image is a static visual prior, creating asymmetric dynamics that timestep-level caching disrupts and module-level caching cannot handle due to O(L) memory blowup.

## Input / Output

SyncCache is a training-free inference-time acceleration applied to existing DiT-based portrait animation models (HunyuanVideo-Avatar, Wan-S2V). It consumes a reference portrait image and a driving audio clip, and additionally requires a human mask (either natively available from the model or extracted via a lightweight 46M-parameter detector in ~0.07 s). The output is a talking-face video generated through the standard denoising pipeline, with the caching strategy controlling which DiT blocks are recomputed versus reused at each timestep.

## Method

SyncCache decouples computation along two axes of asymmetry. First, Spatially-Asymmetric Probing modulates the first-block-output L1 error with a soft human mask (weight ω=2) so that accumulated error in dynamic human regions triggers timely full recomputation, preventing premature cache reuse that would blur lip and muscle motion. Second, Modality-Decoupled Caching exploits the observation that inter-block residuals between lightweight audio blocks are temporally stable: during reuse steps, the heavy self-attention and MLP layers are bypassed by reusing cached residuals, while the audio blocks (contributing <1% of latency) are always recomputed to maintain frame-accurate lip synchronization. Third, a Memory-Adaptive Optimal Selection strategy introduces a continuous cache ratio σ and formulates the choice of which residual boundaries to cache as an offline dynamic programming problem that minimizes inter-block temporal instability, reducing memory from O(L_a) toward O(1) with a single calibration forward pass and zero online overhead.

## Results

On HunyuanVideo-Avatar (50-step baseline, 524 s latency), SyncCache-fast achieves a 4.12× speedup (127 s) while maintaining the best LPIPS (0.1172), SSIM (0.8493), and Sync-C (6.902) among all accelerated methods, compared to the original FID of 25.27 and Sync-C of 6.963. SyncCache-slow (3.34×, 157 s) is near-lossless, reaching LPIPS 0.1010, SSIM 0.8618, FID 25.65, and Sync-C 6.944. On Wan-S2V (40-step baseline, 113 s), SyncCache delivers a 3.75× speedup (30.15 s) with the best LPIPS (0.1775), PSNR (19.80), and Sync-C (6.791) across all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyncCache outperforms all eight baselines on both HunyuanVideo-Avatar and Wan-S2V across every reported metric. Timestep-level caching methods (TeaCache, MagCache, DiCache, Δ-DiT, CGCache) saturate at 1.4–3.2× speedup and consistently degrade Sync-C (lip-sync) by 0.1–0.3 points relative to the original, because their global step-skipping interrupts continuous high-frequency audio injection. The module-level TaylorSeer-series (TaylorSeer, SpeCa, ClusCa) fails entirely with CUDA OOM on 8× A800 80 GB due to O(L) memory scaling, making them inapplicable at the tested resolution and duration. SyncCache-slow (3.34×) achieves near-lossless fidelity (Sync-C 6.944 vs. 6.963 original; LPIPS 0.1010 vs. 0.1321 for Δ-DiT), while SyncCache-fast (4.12×) still dominates all baselines in both perceptual quality and audio alignment. On Wan-S2V, SyncCache (3.75×) improves LPIPS by 0.006–0.011 and Sync-C by 0.075–0.113 over the strongest timestep-level baselines (DiCache, CGCache), confirming the advantage of modality-decoupled caching under a compressed 40-step sampler.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free diffusion caching line (TeaCache, MagCache, DiCache, TaylorSeer) from text-conditioned video generation to audio-driven portrait animation, where the modality dynamics are fundamentally asymmetric. It builds directly on the first-block-output probing mechanism from TeaCache [21] and CGCache [13] but departs from the uniform-dynamics assumption by introducing spatial asymmetry (human-mask-weighted probing) and modality asymmetry (decoupling lightweight audio blocks from the heavy visual backbone). The memory-adaptive cache selection via offline dynamic programming is a new formulation not present in prior caching work, which typically caches at fixed granularities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4×+ speedup with near-lossless lip-sync quality addresses a critical practical bottleneck for creative workflows (10 min → ~2 min on 8×A800), and the O(1) memory footprint resolves the OOM failures that plague module-level caching methods like TaylorSeer at scale. The finding that optimal cache boundaries are a stable property of the model architecture (robust even to silent calibration samples) suggests the technique could transfer to other asymmetric multimodal DiT tasks beyond portrait animation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to tasks without a clear lightweight/heavy modality split, and whether the human-mask dependency limits applicability to non-portrait domains.
