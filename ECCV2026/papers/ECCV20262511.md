## Problem

Existing single-image-to-3D diffusion models suffer from two critical limitations: geometric artifacts (non-watertight surfaces, topological errors, floating geometry) inherited from noisy web-crawled training data, and an inability to align outputs with subjective, human-centric quality criteria such as surface smoothness or feature sharpness. Preference optimization techniques that have proven effective for LLM alignment (e.g., DPO) remain largely unexplored in the 3D generation domain, where defining and optimizing for 'quality' is more complex and multifaceted.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, encoded via CLIP into conditioning embeddings, and produces a watertight 3D mesh as output. Generation operates in a compressed latent set space (an unordered point representation learned by a Shape VAE), so the diffusion model never directly predicts mesh vertices or faces. The framework also supports controllable surface style (smooth vs. sharp) by conditioning on style labels learned during DPO training, enabling user-driven stylistic steering beyond the base image prompt.

## Method

The framework builds on Hunyuan3D-2's rectified flow diffusion model over a latent set representation, fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 64 on the coarse geometry transformer's linear layers) for parameter efficiency. The key novelty is a dual preference-optimization strategy: Direct Reward Optimization (DRO), which re-weights the standard denoising loss by a binary oracle signal (checking watertightness, minimum face count, and curvature variance) to amplify gradients for high-quality samples and suppress them for poor ones, and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), which uses pairwise smooth-vs-sharp preference pairs generated by perturbing the Gaussian curvature weight in a NeurCADRecon SDF reconstruction. A data curation pipeline first reconstructs ~100k watertight meshes from raw Objaverse/ShapeNet/ABO assets using the neural SDF method with a double-trough Gaussian curvature loss, ensuring geometric plausibility before preference fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Toys4k benchmark, the DRO-tuned model achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.0036 and F-Score of 77.97, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline Trellis (CD 0.0083, F-Score 73.12) by 56.6% in CD and 4.85 points in F-Score. Rendering quality metrics also improve, with PSNR-N of 37.05 and LPIPS-N of 0.021 on normal maps. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that DRO fine-tuning yields the best geometry (CD 0.0036, F-Score 77.97) over SFT alone (CD 0.0045, F-Score 76.28) and SFT+DPO (CD 0.0048, F-Score 76.77).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor Trellis (CD 0.0083, F-Score 73.12), the DRO-tuned model reduces Chamfer Distance by 56.6% (to 0.0036) and gains 4.85 F-Score points. Over CLAY (CD 0.0124, F-Score 72.59), the improvement is even larger: 71% lower CD and 5.38 F-Score points, and CLAY also serves as the user-study comparator where Ours-DRO wins 60% of pairwise votes. The base model Hunyuan3D-2 (CD 0.0089, F-Score 72.79) is surpassed by 59.6% in CD and 5.18 F-Score points, confirming the benefit of preference fine-tuning on top of the pre-trained backbone. The older reconstruction-based methods InstantMesh (CD 0.0224) and LN3Diff (CD 0.0299) are far behind, with CD values 6–8× higher, highlighting the gap between multi-view reconstruction pipelines and direct latent diffusion with preference alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Hunyuan3D-2 rectified flow architecture as its generative backbone and adapts Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from the LLM alignment literature to the 3D latent diffusion setting. Its key conceptual departure is the introduction of Direct Reward Optimization (DRO), which replaces DPO's pairwise Bradley-Terry formulation with a re-weighted denoising loss driven by a binary rule-based oracle (watertightness, face count, curvature variance), eliminating the need for paired preference data. The data curation pipeline repurposes NeurCADRecon's Gaussian curvature loss as a controllable geometric prior, and LoRA is borrowed from LLM fine-tuning to keep the preference-tuning stage parameter-efficient. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first to bring explicit preference alignment to 3D generation, noting that while DPO has been applied to image diffusion, the 3D domain had lacked such mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in DRO's simplicity: a rule-based oracle requiring no human annotation or paired data can meaningfully improve geometric fidelity, lowering the barrier to preference-aligned 3D generation. However, the 'preference' captured here is narrow—essentially geometric validity (watertightness, smoothness) rather than true aesthetic or semantic alignment—so the gap between metric-driven quality and human-perceived beauty remains largely unaddressed. The authors themselves flag learning aesthetic reward models from human feedback and extending to scene-level generation as future directions. An open question is whether the DRO > DPO ablation result generalizes beyond this specific oracle design and dataset scale, or whether it reflects the particular simplicity of the binary criteria used.
