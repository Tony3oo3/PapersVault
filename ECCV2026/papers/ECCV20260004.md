## Problem

Existing event-based motion estimators (ETAP, MATE) depend on hours of synthetic event data generated through expensive rendering and interpolation pipelines, which introduces periodic artifacts and a significant sim-to-real gap that limits generalization to complex real-world motion. The paper asks how to achieve competitive event motion estimation from only ~25 minutes of unannotated real-world recordings, without any ground-truth motion labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260004/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The motion estimator takes an asynchronous event stream and a set of query points (pixel coordinates with timestamps) as input; events are first organized into B multi-scale stacks with exponentially increasing temporal windows, then compressed to a 3-channel representation by a U-Net concentration network before entering the tracker backbone. RGB frames are used exclusively for pseudo-label generation during training and are not required at inference. The model jointly outputs sparse point trajectories with per-point visibility and confidence, plus dense optical flow, in a single forward pass. For frame interpolation, boundary RGB frames and the inter-frame event stream additionally produce interpolated frames at arbitrary timestamps via a conditioned diffusion transformer.

## Method

TETO is a teacher-student distillation framework where a pretrained RGB tracker (AllTracker) generates pseudo trajectory and flow labels on curated real event-RGB paired sequences (~25 min from EHPT-XC and ERF-X170FPS, selected for beamsplitter alignment, motion diversity, and high frame rate). A key design choice is motion-aware query sampling: the teacher's optical flow is decomposed via RANSAC into a global affine ego-motion component and a residual object-motion mask, and the majority of query points are oversampled from dynamic regions to prevent overfitting to dominant camera motion. The student reuses the AllTracker architecture unchanged, with only the concentration network added to convert multi-scale event stacks into a 3-channel input compatible with the pretrained ConvNeXt encoder; training uses a visibility-weighted trajectory L1 loss plus a dense flow consistency loss. For video frame interpolation, the motion estimates condition a pretrained video diffusion transformer at two levels: bidirectional optical flow warps boundary-frame latents for coarse spatial alignment, while point trajectories supervise self-attention maps via a Huber loss to maintain fine-grained geometric correspondence throughout denoising, complemented by an event motion mask derived from raw event accumulations that directs generation capacity toward dynamic regions.

## Results

On EVIMO2, TETO achieves state-of-the-art point tracking with AJ=67.9, δ_avg=81.4, and OA=92.2, surpassing ETAP (66.1/78.9/89.5) which trains on ~5 hours of synthetic data, using only ~25 minutes of real recordings. On DSEC optical flow, TETO in-domain achieves EPE=1.39 and AE=4.31, outperforming the best prior unsupervised method EZPAI (EPE=1.78, AE=6.44) by 21% and 33% respectively. For video frame interpolation on BS-ERGB, TETO-VFI attains the best FID (7.65 at ×3) and LPIPS (0.0689 at ×3) across all interpolation rates, and on HQ-EVFI achieves FID=17.39 and LPIPS=0.0401 at ×7, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On EVIMO2 point tracking, TETO outperforms all three baselines on every metric: it beats ETAP (the strongest event-based tracker) by +1.8 AJ, +2.5 δ_avg, and +2.7 OA, and surpasses the RGB teacher AllTracker by +10.0 AJ and +9.0 δ_avg, despite operating on events rather than RGB. On DSEC optical flow, TETO in-domain (EPE=1.39, AE=4.31) dominates all eight compared methods including EZPAI (1.78/6.44), VSA-SM (2.22/8.86), and MotionPriorCM (3.20/8.53); even zero-shot, TETO's AE of 6.08 already undercuts the best in-domain-trained unsupervised method EZPAI (6.44). For VFI on BS-ERGB, TETO-VFI achieves the lowest FID and LPIPS at all three rates (×1, ×3, ×6), beating TimeLens-XL by 4.46 FID at ×3 and CBMNet-Large by 3.58 FID at ×3, while RE-VDM trails by 9.01 FID at ×3. On HQ-EVFI (zero-shot cross-domain), TETO-VFI leads with FID=17.39 and LPIPS=0.0401 at ×7, outperforming CBMNet-Large (18.92/0.0717) and TimeLens-XL (26.50/0.0601). The ablation in Table 8 further confirms that replacing TETO's motion estimator with E2FAI degrades FID from 10.11 to 13.34, validating that motion estimation quality directly governs synthesis fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

TETO adapts the AllTracker architecture [24] to the event domain via a concentration network (borrowed from [41]) that compresses multi-scale event stacks into a 3-channel representation, then fine-tunes the entire pipeline with pseudo-labels from a pretrained RGB teacher. Conceptually, it extends the data-quality-over-scale trend initiated by CoTracker3 [30] and AnthroTAP [32] in the RGB tracking literature to the event-camera setting, deliberately departing from ETAP [20] and MATE [22], which train on hours of synthetic event sequences (EventKubric, EvPointOdyssey) and suffer from a sim-to-real gap. For the VFI component, TETO conditions a pretrained video diffusion transformer (Wan [54]) on explicit motion estimates, contrasting with RE-VDM [7] and EventDiff [60] that rely on implicit motion cues encoded in event representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical takeaway is that ~25 minutes of carefully curated real event-RGB data, combined with motion-aware query sampling and RGB-teacher distillation, can match or exceed methods trained on 5+ hours of synthetic sequences, offering a far cheaper and more generalizable training recipe for event motion estimation. This challenges the prevailing assumption in the event community that large-scale synthetic pipelines are necessary, though the approach still requires paired event-RGB recordings and a strong pretrained RGB teacher, limiting its applicability to unpaired or single-modality settings. Open questions include whether the distillation framework can operate without any RGB supervision (e.g., via self-supervised event pretraining), whether the curation criteria generalize to new sensor configurations, and whether the explicit-motion-prior conditioning strategy transfers to other generative tasks beyond frame interpolation.
