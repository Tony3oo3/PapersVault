## Problem

When CLIP-based VLMs are adapted to fine-grained domains via LoRA, the resulting domain experts produce logits that are not globally calibrated across domains. Merging these experts into a unified multi-expert-domain (MED) classifier causes cross-domain logit interference: domains with higher-magnitude logits dominate the joint softmax even for out-of-domain inputs, degrading accuracy. Existing MoE-based merging approaches (MoLE, Phatgoose) rely on unsupervised gating with load-balancing losses and do not address the logit miscalibration problem.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image and a set of class labels (text prompts) drawn from the union of multiple domain-specific label sets, with no domain label provided. It outputs a class probability distribution over all candidate classes via a single softmax, enabling zero-shot classification across arbitrary domain compositions. The domain-specific LoRA adapters are pre-trained independently (16-shot per class) and kept frozen; only the lightweight gating network and per-domain temperature scalars are learned during MED training.

## Method

MED-DSLC is a mixture-of-experts architecture that combines two mechanisms to restore global logit comparability. First, domain-supervised routing: since the domain of each training example is trivially derivable from its class label, the gating network is trained with an explicit domain classification loss (cross-entropy on the gating weights) rather than relying on unsupervised load-balancing objectives, yielding more accurate expert selection. Second, domain-wise logit scaling: a learnable temperature parameter τ_d per domain rescales that domain's logits before the joint softmax, compensating for domain-specific magnitude shifts introduced by independent LoRA training while preserving within-domain ranking. At each adapted layer, the LoRA residual is a gated weighted sum of domain-specific LoRA residuals; the VLM backbone and all LoRA parameters remain frozen, and only the gating network (a linear layer + softmax) and D scalar temperatures are optimized. The total training loss combines a cross-entropy classification loss over the full union label set with the domain routing loss, weighted by λ.

## Results

On the cross-domain evaluation (union of 9 fine-grained datasets, 783 classes), MED-DSLC achieves 85.51% mean accuracy on the Base split and 71.80% on the All split, slightly surpassing the oracle Expert-LoRA upper bound (85.48% and 71.60% respectively). This represents a +15.39-point improvement over LoRA-Mean (70.12%) on Base and +6.83 points on All. On the in-domain evaluation, MED-DSLC reaches 85.89% (Base) and 72.33% (All), closely matching the oracle. The method is highly data-efficient, retaining 71.71% mean accuracy on the All split even with only 1-shot training for the gating network.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MED-DSLC outperforms all four compared baselines across both Base and All splits in the cross-domain protocol. Against MoLE (Wu et al., ICLR 2024), the closest MoE-based competitor, MED-DSLC gains +13.7 points on Base (85.51% vs. 71.80%) and +6.0 points on All (71.80% vs. 65.85%), with the gap driven primarily by domain supervision and logit scaling. Against PHATGOOSE (Muqeeth et al.), MED-DSLC leads by +12.5 points on Base and +12.0 points on All, with PHATGOOSE actually falling below LoRA-Mean on the All split (59.77% vs. 64.97%), indicating its sigmoid gating fails under cross-domain competition. Against KnOTS-DARE-TIES (Stoica et al.), the best SVD-based merging method, MED-DSLC gains +7.3 points on Base and +3.1 points on All. Against the unadapted CLIP backbone, MED-DSLC improves by +19.3 points on Base and +9.3 points on All, confirming that the merged expert system substantially recovers fine-grained domain knowledge while preserving zero-shot generalization. Notably, MED-DSLC slightly exceeds the Expert-LoRA oracle on both splits, suggesting that the mixture-of-experts combination adds robustness beyond any single domain expert.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MoE-based LoRA merging line initiated by MoLE (Wu et al., ICLR 2024) and Phatgoose (Muqeeth et al., 2024), which combine independently trained domain adapters via learned gating. The key departure is twofold: (1) it replaces unsupervised load-balanced routing with domain-supervised gating, exploiting the fact that domain labels are trivially derivable from class labels in the MED setting, and (2) it introduces per-domain temperature scaling to correct cross-domain logit miscalibration—a problem prior MoE-LoRA works address only implicitly through balancing losses. The underlying VLM backbone (CLIP/SigLIP) and LoRA adaptation follow standard practice from Hu et al. (ICLR 2022) and Zhou et al. (IJCV 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MED problem is practically critical as the ecosystem of specialized LoRA adapters grows to thousands, making unified deployment essential. The paper's identification of logit miscalibration (not just routing) as a primary barrier to multi-domain zero-shot inference reframes how practitioners should think about adapter merging. The solution is remarkably lightweight—only D scalar temperature parameters plus a small gating network—and achieves near-oracle performance with as few as one training example per class for routing. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hundreds of domains, whether it extends to generative or retrieval tasks where logit calibration is less well-defined, and whether the supervised routing assumption (domain labels available at training) holds in truly open-world settings.
