## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models systematically reproduce demographic biases from training data (e.g., generating female nurses and male CEOs), and existing debiasing methods are limited by requiring retraining, batch-level coupling, or prompt-specific tuning. The paper addresses the gap of a training-free, per-sample intervention that works across architectures without modifying model weights or requiring auxiliary classifiers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261428/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method consumes a standard text prompt and a frozen pre-trained diffusion model (SD-1.5, SD-2.1, SDXL, or SANA), along with offline-precomputed steering vectors and gating thresholds for each target attribute. It produces a single generated image per prompt with the demographic attribute distribution shifted toward uniformity for neutral prompts, while leaving attribute-specific prompts (e.g., 'a male nurse') untouched. The intervention is applied at every cross-attention layer and denoising step, modifying CA outputs in-place before the standard denoising pipeline continues.

## Method

EquiSteer steers cross-attention (CA) activations at inference time using precomputed steering vectors derived from contrastive prompt pairs (attribute-present vs. attribute-absent). Three components address the failure modes of naive steering: (1) a prompt-aware gate computes the maximal dot-product between early-step CA activations and each attribute's steering vector at a calibrated layer, skipping intervention when the signal exceeds a threshold (indicating an attribute-specific prompt); (2) for neutral prompts, the CA output is orthogonalized against the subspace spanned by all attribute steering vectors via a precomputed projection matrix, removing pre-existing bias signals; (3) the target attribute is then injected at an adaptive magnitude set to match the typical dot-product strength observed on attribute-specific prompts, ensuring the injected signal is neither too weak nor ambiguous. The entire pipeline requires no weight updates, no external classifiers, and operates independently per sample, scaling to multi-concept debiasing by applying the full gate–orthogonalize–inject sequence sequentially for each concept.

## Results

On SD-1.5 gender debiasing (attribute-neutral prompts), EquiSteer achieves an average parity gap Δ of 0.051, compared to 0.167 for TEI (the strongest training-free baseline), 0.167 for FairDiff, 0.244 for SelfDisc, 0.264 for FTDiff, and 0.403 for vanilla SD — a 87% reduction over vanilla and 47% over TEI. On SDXL and SANA, EquiSteer reduces the average Δ to 0.075 and 0.097 respectively, versus 0.381 and 0.473 for vanilla. On image quality, EquiSteer maintains CLIP Score of 26.63 (vs. 26.42 vanilla, 26.56 TEI) and CMMD of 0.519 (vs. 0.532 vanilla) on SD-1.5, confirming minimal quality degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquiSteer decisively outperforms all six baselines on the primary gender-debiasing metric (average parity gap Δ). Against the strongest training-free baseline TEI [16], EquiSteer achieves Δ=0.051 vs. 0.167 on SD-1.5 (3.3× improvement) and 0.075 vs. 0.242 on SDXL (3.2× improvement). Among finetuning-based methods, FairDiff [6] and SelfDisc [17] are the closest competitors (Δ=0.167 and 0.244 on SD-1.5), but both require retraining and model weight access; UCE [9] and FTDiff [30] perform substantially worse (Δ=0.400 and 0.264). On SDXL, FairImagen [7] is also compared (results in supplementary) but EquiSteer still leads. On image quality (CLIP Score, CMMD), EquiSteer matches or slightly exceeds vanilla and TEI, while finetuning baselines like UCE degrade CLIP Score from 26.42 to 18.10, confirming that EquiSteer achieves superior fairness without the quality trade-offs of retraining-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

EquiSteer directly repurposes the cross-attention steering mechanism from CASteer [8], which was designed for concept suppression, and redirects it toward fairness by injecting attribute-specific steering vectors instead of erasing them. It departs from the three main prior debiasing families—finetuning-based (FairDiff, FTDiff, Fair Mapping), batch-level distributional guidance (Balancing Act), and text-embedding intervention (TEI, FairImagen)—by operating per-sample at the cross-attention activation level without retraining, external classifiers, or prompt modification. The gating and orthogonalization components are novel additions motivated by the observation that early-step CA activations already encode whether a prompt specifies an attribute, a mechanistic insight drawn from recent attribution analyses [1].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EquiSteer offers a practical, drop-in debiasing layer that requires no weight access, no additional data, and no batch coupling, making it applicable to proprietary or frozen diffusion models across architectures. The ability to debias four demographic concepts simultaneously in a single pass, while preserving attribute-specific prompt behavior, addresses a key practical gap that batch-level and embedding-level methods leave open. Limitations include weaker performance on compositional/multi-subject prompts (the image-level gate cannot independently control two subjects) and reliance on CLIP/BLIP classifiers for evaluation, which the authors acknowledge can be conservative. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-demographic biases (e.g., disability, socio-economic status) and whether per-subject gating could resolve the multi-entity limitation.
