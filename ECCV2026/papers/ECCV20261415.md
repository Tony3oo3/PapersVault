## Problem

Large-Chunk Test-Time Training (LaCT) enables efficient long-context 3D/4D reconstruction via chunk-wise fast-weight updates, but its fully plastic inference-time dynamics cause catastrophic forgetting and overfitting, forcing it to operate with a single chunk spanning the entire input sequence. This prevents LaCT from handling arbitrarily long sequences in a single pass and limits its applicability to dynamic 4D scenes where illumination, pose, and motion continuously evolve during inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261415/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes V posed RGB images at arbitrary view-time combinations, each augmented with Plücker ray maps (ray direction and moment) and a normalized timestamp map to form 10-channel per-view feature maps, which are patchified into tokens. It produces novel view-time rendered images via two interchangeable decoders: a lightweight linear head that directly predicts RGB patches (LVSM-style), or pixel-aligned 4D Gaussian Splatting primitives (V×H×W Gaussians, 20 parameters each) rasterized into target views (LRM-style). The architecture supports arbitrary input lengths and timestamps, rendering any novel view-time combination in a single forward pass.

## Method

The core contribution is Large-Chunk Elastic Test-Time Training (LaCET), which augments each LaCT chunk update with an Elastic Weight Consolidation-inspired consolidation step: after the per-chunk fast-weight update (using a SwiGLU-MLP network), a Fisher-weighted penalty softly pulls important parameters back toward anchor weights, where importance is tracked online via an EMA of squared or SI-style update statistics. Anchor weights evolve via a streaming-EMA policy, forming a low-pass filter over the fast-weight trajectory that balances stability and plasticity across chunks. This transforms the base transformer into a self-refining yet elastic 4D learner that adapts to new scene conditions while retaining prior knowledge, enabling robust multi-chunk adaptation over long sequences. The full Fast Spatial Memory (FSM) model is pretrained on a curated mixture of static 3D and dynamic 4D datasets using photometric supervision (L2 + LPIPS), with a long-context curriculum that gradually increases resolution, temporal span, and input view count.

## Results

On the Stereo4D 4D NVS benchmark (256×256), FSM-LVSM achieves 32.16 PSNR, 0.043 LPIPS, and 0.931 SSIM, substantially outperforming MoVieS (27.19 PSNR) and 4DGT (24.62 PSNR). On the NVIDIA benchmark, FSM-LVSM reaches 23.90 PSNR and 0.105 LPIPS, surpassing the strongest optimization-based baseline MoSca (21.45 PSNR) and the feed-forward MoVieS (19.16 PSNR). For 3D NVS on DL3DV (256×256), FSM-LVSM attains 26.69 PSNR and 0.091 LPIPS, competitive with tttLVSM (26.90 PSNR at 540×960) while using a much lower resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSM-LVSM dominates all rendering-based and optimization-based baselines on both 4D benchmarks: on Stereo4D it gains +4.97 PSNR over MoVieS and +7.54 PSNR over 4DGT, while on NVIDIA it surpasses MoSca (the strongest optimization-based method) by +2.45 PSNR and MoVieS by +4.74 PSNR. FSM-LRM is also competitive, outperforming MoVieS on NVIDIA (+1.01 PSNR) and 4DGT by +6.04 PSNR, though it trails MoVieS slightly on Stereo4D (27.29 vs. 27.19 PSNR at different resolutions). On the 3D DL3DV benchmark, FSM-LVSM (26.69 PSNR at 256×256) is within 0.21 PSNR of tttLVSM (26.90 at 540×960) while achieving a markedly lower LPIPS (0.091 vs. 0.185), and outperforms tttLRM (25.07) and GS-LRM (23.02). The paper notes that metrics are resolution-dependent, so the 256×256 comparison is the fairest cross-method setting; at higher resolutions the gap to tttLVSM narrows further.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Large-Chunk Test-Time Training (LaCT) [69], which enables chunk-wise fast-weight adaptation for long-context 3D reconstruction, by adding an Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) [21] regularizer to stabilize multi-chunk inference-time updates. The elastic consolidation operator is borrowed from continual learning, where it prevents catastrophic forgetting across tasks, and is repurposed here to prevent fast-weight drift across temporal chunks in 4D scene reconstruction. The resulting LaCET block is instantiated within two rendering decoders (LVSM-style direct RGB prediction and LRM-style 4D Gaussian Splatting), connecting the TTT line of work with the broader LRM/LVSM family of feedforward reconstruction models [12,15,31,45,66,69].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FSM is positioned as the first large-scale 4D reconstruction model that handles arbitrarily long input sequences in a single forward pass, directly addressing the activation-memory bottleneck that limits current LRM/LVSM approaches to bounded context lengths. The elastic mechanism provides a principled, lightweight solution to the plasticity-stability tradeoff in test-time training, and the ablations clearly show it mitigates both overfitting and the camera-interpolation shortcut that plagues fully plastic LaCT. Key open questions remain: whether photometric supervision alone can guarantee geometrically faithful 4D reconstruction, how to handle unposed dynamic inputs, and whether the elastic prior generalizes beyond the specific SwiGLU fast-weight architecture used here.
