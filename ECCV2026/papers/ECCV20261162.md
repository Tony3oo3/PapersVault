## Problem

Existing unified segmentation models (e.g., Spider, EVP, FOCUS) rely on static visual prompts or generic semantic prompts that fail to capture the large frequency-domain discrepancies across diverse concealed scenarios (RGB camouflage, CT lung infection, ultrasound polyp, infrared target). This paper identifies that different concealed tasks exhibit markedly distinct and interpretable spectral characteristics, yet no prior unified method explicitly exploits frequency-domain modulation to bridge these cross-scenario gaps within a single parameter-shared model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261162/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts heterogeneous image modalities—RGB, CT, ultrasound, and infrared—at standard resolutions and produces a single binary segmentation mask per input. A single model with 100% parameter sharing is trained once on all six scenarios jointly (batch size 18, three samples per task per iteration), and the pre-trained encoder remains frozen throughout; only ~5M parameters (≈5% of the base model) in the FreqDirect modules and MLP decoder are fine-tuned. Frequency operations (2D FFT) are applied channel-wise at intermediate encoder stages with reduced spatial resolution to keep computational overhead modest.

## Method

FreqDirect embeds a Mixture of Frequency Experts (MoFE) into intermediate transformer layers: features are mapped to a lower-dimensional space, transformed to the frequency domain via FFT, partitioned into N radial frequency bands, and each band is processed by a dedicated expert that applies separate learnable affine transformations on amplitude and phase before recombining into a complex representation. A dynamic router computes input-dependent weights (via spatial attention + MLP + softmax) to adaptively aggregate expert outputs, enabling task-specific frequency selection. To counteract the loss of local spatial structure inherent in global Fourier operations, a Spatial Commonality Anchor (SCA) branch aggregates multi-scale spatial features to preserve boundary continuity and shape consistency. The two paths are integrated and the result is transformed back via iFFT; the entire module is inserted into all four transformer stages and trained with BCE + IoU loss under joint multi-task sampling, requiring only a single training run to generalize across all concealed scenarios.

## Results

FreqDirect (Swin-L) achieves the best unified-model performance across all six concealed scenarios: Sα=0.8811 / Fβw=0.8166 on COD10K, IoU=0.8624 / Dice=0.8202 on PolypSeg, IoU=0.8646 on COVID-19 (CT), IoU=0.8526 / Dice=0.8226 on BUSI, IoU=0.9051 / MSE=0.0485 on GDD, and Fα=17.68 / Pd=94.38 on IRSTD-1K. These results surpass the strongest unified baselines (Spider, EVPv2, FOCUS) on nearly every metric and match or exceed task-specific specialist models (e.g., FSEL 0.8730 Sα on COD10K, ASPS 0.7994 IoU on PolypSeg, GhostingNet 0.8930 IoU on GDD). On zero-shot unseen scenarios (CDS2K, PlantCAMO), FreqDirect also leads all unified competitors, e.g., Sα=0.5607 vs. FOCUS 0.5672 on CDS2K and Sα=0.6881 vs. EVPv2 0.6841 on PlantCAMO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest unified baseline Spider (Swin-B), FreqDirect (Swin-L) gains +3.5 pts Sα and +9.4 pts Fβw on COD10K, +5.8 pts IoU on COVID-19, and reduces Fα from 27.18 to 17.68 on IRSTD-1K. Compared to EVPv2 (MiT-B4), FreqDirect (MiT-B4) improves Sα by +1.7 pts on COD10K, IoU by +2.7 pts on COVID-19, and Fα by 4.69 on IRSTD-1K, while using a comparable parameter budget. FOCUS (DINOv2-L), which relies on a 500M-parameter foundation model, is outperformed by FreqDirect (Swin-L) on COD10K (+3.0 Sα), COVID-19 (+16.6 IoU), and IRSTD-1K (Fα 17.68 vs. 80.09), despite FOCUS having ~5× more parameters. Against task-specific specialists, FreqDirect (Swin-L) surpasses FSEL (0.8811 vs. 0.8730 Sα on COD10K), ASPS (0.8624 vs. 0.7994 IoU on PolypSeg), and GhostingNet (0.9051 vs. 0.8930 IoU on GDD), while MSHNet retains a slight edge on IRSTD-1K Fα (15.03 vs. 17.68). On zero-shot unseen scenarios, FreqDirect consistently leads all unified baselines, e.g., Sα 0.5607 vs. FOCUS 0.5672 on CDS2K and Sα 0.6881 vs. EVPv2 0.6841 on PlantCAMO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Mixture of Experts paradigm from the spatial domain into the frequency domain, building on the MoE architectures of Riquelme et al. [42] and Zhu et al. [60], but applies expert routing to FFT-partitioned spectral bands rather than spatial feature maps. It departs from prior unified segmentation models (EVP, Spider, FOCUS) that rely on static visual prompts or generic semantic prompts, instead proposing dynamic, input-aware frequency modulation as the adaptation mechanism. The frequency-domain perspective for concealed object detection builds on prior work (Zhong et al. [58], Lin et al. [30], Cong et al. [8]) that showed spectral features aid camouflage detection, but extends this from single-task, static frequency fusion to a multi-task, dynamically routed expert framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreqDirect offers a practical, parameter-efficient (~5M trainable params) recipe for unifying eight heterogeneous concealed segmentation tasks in a single model, which is valuable for practitioners who need one deployable model across medical, camouflage, and infrared domains. The ablation confirming that frequency-domain MoE outperforms spatial-domain MoE with identical routing provides evidence that spectral structure is genuinely informative for cross-domain generalization in low-contrast settings, not merely an architectural convenience. Open questions remain around ultra-weak frequency patterns (infrared small targets, near-invisible boundaries) where the method still fails, and whether finer-grained frequency decomposition or learned (rather than fixed radial) band partitioning could close this gap. The finding that joint training consistently outperforms separate training even on zero-shot tasks suggests that shared frequency representations encode transferable structure, but the degree to which this scales to truly novel modalities (e.g., MRI, hyperspectral) is untested.
