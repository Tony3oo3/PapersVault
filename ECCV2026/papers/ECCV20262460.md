## Problem

Transformer-based image restoration models rely on self-attention with quadratic complexity, making them impractical for high-resolution images. Existing mitigations—window-based or sparse attention—sacrifice global context modeling, while vanilla linear attention, though linear-complexity, produces inherently low-rank attention maps that severely limit feature diversity and restoration quality. The paper asks whether one can retain full global perception at linear complexity without the representational bottleneck of standard linear attention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262460/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For image restoration, LAformer accepts a single degraded RGB image at arbitrary resolution (evaluated up to 1280×720 on GoPro) and outputs a restored high-quality image of the same size, operating across seven degradation types (motion blur, defocus blur, haze, snow, raindrops, low-light, and all-in-one). For visual generation, the model takes a class label as conditioning and produces a 256×256 RGB image via either a diffusion or flow-matching objective, matching the parameter and FLOP budgets of DiT/SiT baselines at four scales (33M–682M).

## Method

The key insight is that the linear attention map ψ(Q)ψ(K)ᵀ has rank bounded by min(N, C), which for high-resolution IR (N ≫ C) yields highly correlated weights and impoverished output features. Rank Enhanced Linear Attention (RELA) addresses this by adding a lightweight 5×5 depthwise convolution term to the linear attention output, effectively composing a sparse local-attention matrix with the global linear-attention matrix to restore full-rank expressiveness at negligible extra cost. LAformer stacks Dual Attention Blocks in a U-Net, where each block combines RELA (spatial global), a Channel Attention Block (channel global via global average pooling), and a Convolutional Gated FFN (local detail via depthwise conv + GLU), all with linear complexity and no softmax or window shifting. The same architecture is adapted for class-conditional generation by replacing the U-Net with a DiT/SiT-style transformer trained under diffusion or flow-matching objectives, where LAformer outperforms both baselines at every scale.

## Results

On motion deblurring (GoPro), LAformer-B achieves 33.40 dB PSNR / 0.965 SSIM, surpassing the previous best HL-Diff (33.33/0.964) and Restormer (32.92/0.961). On real-world deblurring (RealBlur-R), LAformer-B reaches 41.07 dB PSNR, a 0.87 dB gain over GRL. For diffusion-based image generation on ImageNet 256×256, LAformer (682M) achieves FID 11.31, substantially outperforming DiT-XL/2 (19.47) and DiG-XL/2 (18.53) at comparable parameter counts. In flow-based generation, LAformer (682M) reaches FID 10.98 vs. SiT-XL/2 (17.19) and FlowDCN-XL/2 (11.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAformer consistently outperforms all compared methods across 7 IR tasks. On motion deblurring, LAformer-B beats HL-Diff (NeurIPS'23) by 0.07 dB on GoPro and GRL (CVPR'23) by 0.87 dB on RealBlur-R. On dehazing, LAformer-T surpasses FocalNet (ICCV'23) by 0.35 dB on SOTS-Indoor (41.17 vs 40.82) and 0.80 dB on SOTS-Outdoor (38.51 vs 37.71). On desnowing, LAformer-T edges out FocalNet by 0.24 dB on CSD (37.42 vs 37.18). For raindrop removal, LAformer-S exceeds MOERL (ICCV'25) by 0.17 dB (33.38 vs 33.21) and AST (CVPR'24) by 1.06 dB. In all-in-one restoration, LAformer-B outperforms DiffUIR (CVPR'24) on all five sub-tasks, with the largest gain in enhancement (+1.69 dB PSNR). For visual generation, LAformer achieves dramatically lower FID than DiT-XL/2 (11.31 vs 19.47, a 8.16 FID improvement) and SiT-XL/2 (10.98 vs 17.19, a 6.21 FID improvement) at similar parameter scales, while also matching or beating the more recent DiG and FlowDCN variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the linear attention mechanism of Katharopoulos et al. (ICML 2020), identifying its inherent low-rank limitation in the attention map (bounded by min(N, C)) as the root cause of performance degradation in image restoration. It departs from the dominant window-based (SwinIR, Uformer) and sparse-attention (ART, AST) paradigms that sacrifice global context for efficiency, instead preserving full global receptive field at linear complexity. The architectural design follows the U-Net backbone established by Restormer and Uformer, while the generation experiments position LAformer as a direct competitor to DiT and SiT. Notably, this is the first work to demonstrate linear attention's viability in high-resolution IR, whereas prior linear-attention vision works (EfficientViT, Flatten Transformer, Hydra Attention) targeted classification or detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling global-context modeling at linear complexity for high-resolution IR, removing the softmax and window-shifting bottlenecks that constrain current Transformer-based restorers. The rank analysis provides a clean theoretical diagnosis for why vanilla linear attention underperforms, and the fix (a single 5×5 depthwise convolution) is remarkably lightweight, making it easy to adopt in existing architectures. The extension to diffusion- and flow-based generation (outperforming DiT-XL/2 by ~8 FID at comparable scale) suggests linear attention could become a standard backbone for generative models. Open questions include whether the approach scales to text-to-image generation, video restoration, and whether the rank limitation is fully resolved or merely mitigated at larger channel dimensions.
