## Problem

Existing talking avatar methods generate only simple facial or body motions without the ability to perceive their environment and perform text-driven interactions with specific objects in a given scene. This gap stems from two core difficulties: scene-action grounding (locating and interacting with designated objects within a reference image) and the control-quality dilemma, where sparse textual conditioning forces a trade-off between instruction-following and visual fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260960/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image defining the scene, a structured text prompt describing the desired interaction, and speech audio for lip synchronization; optionally, explicit skeleton sequences or object trajectories can be provided for driven mode. The primary output is a photorealistic talking avatar video (short side 704px) performing grounded object interactions within the reference scene. An intermediate motion representation—rendered skeletal keypoints and object bounding box trajectories in RGB format (short side 256px)—is co-generated in parallel and injected at the feature level into the video stream.

## Method

InteractAvatar is a dual-stream Diffusion Transformer that decouples perception/planning from video synthesis into two isomorphic modules: a Perception and Interaction Module (PIM) that jointly learns scene detection and text-aligned motion generation via a task-embedding mechanism and an environment perception training curriculum (alternating conditional continuation and perception-as-generation), and an Audio-Interaction Aware Generation Module (AIM) that synthesizes the final video with Wav2Vec-based audio cross-attention and a spatial face mask for stable lip-sync learning. The key architectural insight is a Motion-to-Video (M2V) aligner that injects layer-wise feature residuals from PIM into corresponding AIM layers via zero-initialized linear projections, enabling parallel co-generation rather than a cascaded pipeline and thereby mitigating the control-quality dilemma. Both modules are trained under a unified Flow Matching objective initialized from wan2.2-5B, following a three-stage curriculum (PIM pre-training → AIM audio pre-training → joint end-to-end fine-tuning) with a 4:1 data ratio balancing joint generation against external driving to support flexible multimodal control.

## Results

On the GroundInter benchmark (600 test cases), the full InteractAvatar model (TA2MV mode) achieves the best scores across all interaction metrics: VLM-QA 29.07, Hand Quality 0.973, Object Quality 0.147, Dynamic Degree 0.783, and Pixel-Level Interaction 0.850. Compared to the strongest audio-driven baseline Wan-S2V, the method improves HQ by ~180% (0.931 vs 0.336) and OQ by ~111% (0.133 vs 0.063) while maintaining comparable subject consistency (DINO_subject 0.671 vs 0.670) and even surpassing it on lip-sync confidence (6.04 vs 5.43). The text-driven variant (T2MV) achieves the highest VLM-QA (29.05) and PI (0.852), demonstrating that self-generated motion from PIM yields the most semantically aligned interactions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InteractAvatar outperforms all seven baselines across every interaction metric on GroundInter. Against audio-driven methods (Wan-S2V, HY-Video-Avatar, Fantasy-Talking, OmniAvatar), the TA2V variant achieves substantially higher HQ (0.931 vs. 0.336–0.745) and OQ (0.133 vs. 0.063–0.118), with a ~4x gap in Dynamic Degree over Wan-S2V (0.765 vs. 0.095), while matching or exceeding their lip-sync confidence (6.04 vs. 5.43–5.98). Against the pose-driven UniAnimate-DiT (which uses PIM-generated motions as input), the TAM2V variant wins on all metrics, most notably OQ (0.141 vs. 0.082) and PI (0.832 vs. 0.669), attributed to pose-driven methods ignoring object morphology. Against text-driven methods, the T2MV variant surpasses VACE by 2.31 VLM-QA points (29.05 vs. 26.74) and 0.147 PI (0.852 vs. 0.705), and dominates HuMo by 4.93 VLM-QA points (29.05 vs. 24.12) and 0.361 PI (0.852 vs. 0.491), as HuMo generates new scenes rather than preserving the reference environment. On the real-scene test set (Table 5), the method also leads all baselines, confirming generalizability beyond synthetic images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the wan2.2-5B video DiT as its backbone and positions itself at the intersection of three existing lines of work: audio-driven talking avatar synthesis (HY-Video-Avatar, Wan-S2V, Fantasy-Talking), human-object interaction video generation (MIMO, AnimateAnyone2, ManiVideo), and subject-consistent video generation (Phantom, HuMo). The key architectural departure is the dual-stream design that decouples scene perception and motion planning (PIM) from video rendering (AIM) into parallel co-generating modules connected by a layer-wise residual M2V aligner, rather than the sequential cascaded pipelines or single-stream concatenation approaches used in prior HOI and avatar work. The environment perception training curriculum, which interleaves detection-like grounding with motion continuation, is a novel training strategy not seen in prior avatar or HOI generation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper formalizes Grounded Human-Object Interaction (GHOI) for talking avatars and provides the first dedicated benchmark (GroundInter, 600 cases) for evaluating it, filling a gap where prior HOI and avatar benchmarks did not jointly assess scene grounding, interaction plausibility, and lip-sync. The dual-stream parallel co-generation framework offers a practical recipe for combining structural control (motion) with high-fidelity rendering without the control-quality trade-off that plagues single-stream approaches. However, the method is limited to single-person scenes, and the large metric gaps over baselines partly reflect task mismatch (comparing against methods not designed for grounded HOI), so the true competitive landscape for this specific task remains to be established. Open questions include multi-person interaction, longer-horizon generation, and whether the PIM's detection-based grounding generalizes to novel object categories unseen in training.
