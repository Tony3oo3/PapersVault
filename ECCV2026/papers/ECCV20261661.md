## Problem

Current VLA models for autonomous driving rely on imitation learning, which suffers from distribution shift and causal confusion in closed-loop driving. While online reinforcement learning could address these issues through trial-and-error, applying it to VLA models is hindered by the inefficiency of exploration in continuous trajectory action spaces. No prior work has successfully demonstrated effective online RL training for VLA-based autonomous driving in an interactive simulator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261661/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view camera images (encoded by EVA-02-L into scene token embeddings) and text navigation instructions. The Decision Expert outputs a pair of discrete meta-actions (one from 7 speed options, one from 6 path options), which the Action Expert then maps through a VAE-GRU decoder into a continuous trajectory: 6 speed waypoints spanning 3 seconds at 2 Hz and 20 path waypoints spanning 20 meters at 1-meter intervals. During RL rollouts, pre-computed scene token embeddings serve as compact state representations to enable large-batch PPO training without repeated vision-encoder passes.

## Method

MindDrive decouples a single LLM (Qwen2-0.5B or Qwen2.5-3B) into a Decision Expert and an Action Expert via distinct LoRA adapters over a shared base, with a common EVA-02-L vision encoder. The Decision Expert performs scenario reasoning and selects discrete meta-actions, while the Action Expert autoregressively encodes scene+instruction into a hidden state, extracts logits at special tokens, and passes them through a VAE-GRU decoder to produce continuous speed and path trajectories. Training proceeds in two stages: first, imitation learning on planning QA pairs and reasoning data establishes a one-to-one language-to-trajectory mapping (using BC, cross-entropy, VAE-KL, and detection losses); second, online RL in parallel CARLA simulators refines the Decision Expert via PPO with sparse binary rewards (+1 destination reached, −1 penalty event, 0 otherwise), GAE-based advantage estimation, and KL-divergence regularization against the IL policy to prevent catastrophic forgetting. The key insight is that by restricting RL exploration to a finite set of linguistic meta-actions rather than the continuous trajectory space, trajectory-level environment rewards can be efficiently back-propagated into the LLM's reasoning space, enabling sample-efficient trial-and-error learning in just two online training epochs.

## Results

On the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, MindDrive-L (Qwen2-5.3B, Online RL) achieves a Driving Score of 80.59 and Success Rate of 58.26%, setting a new state-of-the-art. The lightweight MindDrive (Qwen2-0.5B, Online RL) reaches 78.04 DS and 55.09% SR, outperforming the same-size IL baseline ORION (72.89 DS, 45.83% SR) by 5.15 DS and 9.26% SR. Online RL training yields consistent gains over the IL-only versions: +2.19 DS / +5.79% SR for the 0.5B model and +4.81 DS / +7.55% SR for the 5.3B model. MindDrive-L also achieves the highest mean Multi-Ability score (60.81%) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MindDrive (0.5B, Online RL) outperforms the same-size IL baseline ORION by 5.15 DS and 9.26% SR, and surpasses the larger ORION (Vicuna-7B) by 0.30 DS and 0.47% SR, demonstrating that the online RL framework compensates for the smaller model size. Against the strongest E2E IL method PGS, MindDrive-L leads by 2.51 DS and 9.62% SR. Among RL-based methods, MindDrive beats the online RL E2E method Raw2Drive by 6.68 DS and 4.85% SR, and the offline RL VLA method RecogDrive by 6.68 DS and 9.64% SR. MindDrive-L also edges out the SOTA offline RL VLA method AutoVLA by 1.75 DS and 0.53% SR. In the Multi-Ability sub-metrics, MindDrive shows particularly large gains in Overtaking (+55.56% over RecogDrive) and Give Way (+30% over RecogDrive), while remaining slightly behind ORION in Emergency Brake and Traffic Sign, suggesting the online RL primarily strengthens proactive decision-making rather than reactive safety responses.

## Relation to Prior Work

MindDrive extends the VLA architecture introduced by ORION (same group, ICCV 2025) by replacing its pure imitation-learning training with a two-stage pipeline: IL to establish a language-to-trajectory mapping, followed by online closed-loop RL in the CARLA simulator. Architecturally, it departs from prior VLA driving models (ORION, DriveMoE, AutoVLA) by splitting a single LLM into a Decision Expert and an Action Expert via distinct LoRA adapters, enabling the RL signal to operate over discrete linguistic meta-actions rather than continuous trajectories. This positions MindDrive between two existing paradigms—offline RL in action space (RecogDrive, AutoVLA) and online RL in language space (AlphaDrive, VLM-RL)—by bridging them through a dynamic language-action mapping that makes online exploration tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that online RL can effectively train a VLA model for autonomous driving in an interactive simulator, opening a path toward closed-loop policy improvement that IL alone cannot provide. The practical finding that only two RL epochs are needed before catastrophic forgetting sets in, and that a sparse binary reward suffices without complex reward engineering, makes the approach accessible to the community. Key limitations remain: evaluation is confined to CARLA/Bench2Drive with no real-world validation, the inability to synchronize parallel simulators precludes GRPO-style parallel policy evaluation, and the discrete meta-action space (7 speed × 6 path) may not capture the full richness of driving decisions. Whether the language-space RL trick generalizes to real-world deployment or more complex multi-agent scenarios is an open question.
