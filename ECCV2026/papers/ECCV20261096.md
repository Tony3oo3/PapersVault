## Problem

Existing 3D large multimodal models either produce text-only conversational responses without explicit point-level grounding, or generate 3D masks/boxes from text but lack interactive dialogue and metric-aware outputs. No prior system unifies verifiable 3D grounding (point-level masks) with metric measurements in real-world units within a single conversational interface, particularly at the part level and for multi-object queries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261096/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a colored point cloud (L points, each with [x,y,z,r,g,b]), a natural-language query, and an optional aligned RGB image. The output is a free-form textual response that may embed metric values in physical units (meters, centimeters) and, when the LLM emits a <SEG> trigger token tied to a phrase wrapped in <p>...</p> tags, a binary point-level mask (m ∈ {0,1}^N) segmenting the referred object or part. Multi-object queries can trigger multiple masks in a single response, and multi-turn dialogue reuses previously grounded entities across turns.

## Method

Ground3D-LMM couples a sparse 3D U-Net point encoder (initialized from SSTNet, with superpoint downsampling to M superpoints) to Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct via a linear projector that maps point features into the LLM's joint latent space as M <|point|> tokens. When the LLM generates a <SEG> trigger, a lightweight segmentation head extracts the corresponding latent state, transforms it into a learnable query, and refines it through cross-attention with dense point features, self-attention, and an FFN before a mask decoder produces the binary mask. Training is end-to-end with a joint loss (BCE + Dice for segmentation, autoregressive cross-entropy for text), LoRA on the LLM backbone, and stratified learning rates (1e-6 encoder, 1e-5 LLM, 1e-4 task modules). The companion Ground3D dataset (~2.5M QA pairs over ~2.5K ScanNet/ScanNet++ scenes) is built via a five-stage pipeline: Qwen3-VL-30B proposes objects/parts, SAM2 generates 2D masks, depth back-projection lifts them to 3D, geometric attributes and spatial relations are computed, and Qwen3-VL-8B synthesizes grounded QA at three levels (single-object, pairwise relational, multi-turn dialogue).

## Results

On the proposed Ground3D-ScanNet object-level benchmark, Ground3D-LMM (3D+2D) achieves 42.22 mIoU overall, outperforming the strongest 3D baseline UniSeg3D (22.97) by nearly 20 points and the image-only baseline (32.52) by ~10 points. On Ground3D-ScanNet++, the same model reaches 30.42 mIoU versus 10.84 for UniSeg3D. Cross-dataset evaluation confirms generalization: on Reason3D, Ground3D-LMM (3D+2D) attains 41.29 mIoU (vs. 39.82 for MLLM-For3D+VideoLISA), and on ScanRefer it reaches 41.30 mIoU, surpassing the previous best (UniSeg3D, 29.10) by over 12 points using only a 4B backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ground3D-LMM decisively outperforms all baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against UniSeg3D [35] (the strongest open-vocabulary 3D segmentation baseline), it gains +14.4 mIoU on Ground3D-ScanNet object (37.40 vs. 22.97, 3D-only) and +13.7 mIoU on ScanRefer (38.72 vs. 29.10). Against Reason3D [16], the margin is even larger: 37.40 vs. 9.31 mIoU on Ground3D-ScanNet, and 36.35 vs. 31.20 on the Reason3D benchmark itself. The image-only baseline (Qwen-VL + Grounded SAM back-projection) is the closest competitor on Ground3D-ScanNet (32.52 mIoU), yet Ground3D-LMM still leads by ~5 points with 3D-only input and by ~10 points with 3D+2D. On Reason3D, Ground3D-LMM with a 4B backbone and 3D-only input (36.35 mIoU) surpasses MLLM-For3D [15] equipped with a 7B model plus image input (31.94 mIoU), and with 3D+2D it extends the lead to 41.29 vs. 39.82 (MLLM-For3D+VideoLISA). On ScanRefer, the instance-level grounding gap is widest: Ground3D-LMM (3D+2D) reaches 41.30 mIoU versus 30.45 for the next-best method (MLLM-For3D+VideoLISA), a +10.85 point advantage, while even the 3D-only variant (38.72) exceeds all prior 3D methods by at least 9.6 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying three previously separate research threads—3D referring expression grounding (ScanRefer, Kestrel, SegPoint), open-vocabulary 3D segmentation (UniSeg3D, Open3DIS, PartSLIP), and metric spatial reasoning (SD-VLM, N3D-VLM)—into a single conversational interface. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it repurposes standard components (sparse U-Net point encoder, a 4B VLM backbone, cross-attention segmentation head) and introduces a lightweight <SEG> trigger mechanism that lets the LLM decide when to invoke point-level grounding mid-conversation. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the joint requirement: every grounded response must simultaneously produce a verifiable point mask and a metric value in real-world units, a combination no prior system was designed to handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3D Grounded Measurement task and the ~2.5M-pair Ground3D dataset provide the field with a standardized benchmark for evaluating the intersection of grounding and measurement, which was previously fragmented across separate benchmarks. Practically, this is relevant to robotics clearance checking, AR/VR surface measurement, and assistive agents that need to both identify and quantify scene elements. Limitations include reliance on VLM-generated annotations (Qwen3-VL) that may introduce systematic biases, a relatively lenient δ=1.25 success threshold, and evaluation confined to indoor ScanNet/ScanNet++ scenes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or dynamic scenes, whether the 4B backbone is sufficient for more complex multi-object reasoning, and whether the VLM-based annotation pipeline scales without degrading metric accuracy.
