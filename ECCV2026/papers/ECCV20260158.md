## Problem

Long-tailed video action recognition remains significantly underperforming compared to balanced settings, with accuracy dropping from 92% to 64% on UCF-101 under imbalance. While generative data augmentation has shown promise for image long-tailed learning, video action recognition requires capturing temporal dynamics and motion, making it a largely underexplored direction. Existing long-tailed video methods operate at the feature or logit level, unable to inject new appearance and motion diversity into the training set.

## Input / Output

The recognition model (VideoMAE ViT-B) takes a video clip resized to 224×224 and outputs a class label over C action classes. The generation pipeline produces 4-second clips at 16 FPS and 480×832 resolution using WAN 2.1-14B, conditioned on text prompts crafted by Gemini 2.5 Pro. The augmented training set combines real clips with generated clips, all sharing the same class labels, and the final model is evaluated on balanced test sets reporting class-average accuracy across head, tail, and few-shot partitions.

## Method

Gen2Balance fills underrepresented classes with synthetic videos via a structured prompting pipeline: a multimodal LLM generates an Action Profile (definition, positive/negative constraints) grounded in a few in-context exemplar videos, then produces diverse prompts varying along nine axes (environment, camera, demographics, props, etc.) to condition WAN 2.1-14B. Training uses a two-stage strategy: Stage 1 fine-tunes on the combined real+synthetic set with Balanced Softmax loss whose margins are computed from real-data class frequencies (not augmented frequencies), creating a super-margin effect for tail classes; Stage 2 rehearses on real data only at a reduced learning rate to rectify the synthetic-to-real domain shift. This separation ensures the model exploits abundant generated data for feature learning while keeping decision boundaries calibrated to true class priors.

## Results

On K100-LT (imbalance ratio 198), Gen2Balance achieves 72.6% average class-accuracy, surpassing the strongest non-generative baseline (Logit Adj. at 65.6%) by +7.0% and the best generative baseline (Li et al. at 54.4%) by +18.2%. On UCF-LT (imbalance ratio 24), it reaches 88.9% average class-accuracy, a +5.1% improvement over the best baseline (cRT at 83.8%). Few-shot class accuracy reaches 62.2% on K100-LT and 86.7% on UCF-LT, and on the 22 RareAct compositional actions, Gen2Balance achieves 59.7% versus 11.3% for CE and 27.8% for Logit Adj.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gen2Balance outperforms all eight baselines on both K100-LT and UCF-LT. Against the strongest algorithmic baselines, it gains +7.0% over Logit Adj. (65.6→72.6) and cRT (65.6→72.6) on K100-LT, and +5.1% over cRT (83.8→88.9) on UCF-LT, with the largest relative gains concentrated in few-shot classes (62.2% vs. 55.5% for Logit Adj. on K100-LT). The two generative baselines that share the same synthetic data underperform Gen2Balance substantially: Sariyildiz et al. (52.8%) and Li et al. (54.4%) on K100-LT, indicating that the training strategy (two-stage with real-frequency Balanced Softmax) is critical, not just the generated data. On the RareAct compositional actions, Gen2Balance (59.7%) more than doubles Logit Adj. (27.8%) and quintuples CE (11.3%), confirming effectiveness for genuinely rare verb-noun compositions. Notably, Gen2Balance retains competitive head-class accuracy (88.4% on K100-LT, 93.4% on UCF-LT) while improving tail and few-shot performance, avoiding the head-tail trade-off seen in post-hoc logit correction methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the long-tailed video recognition line (VideoLT, LMR, MOVE, MEDC) by moving from feature-space or logit-level interventions to pixel-space synthesis via a pre-trained text-to-video model (WAN 2.1). It builds on the image-domain generative augmentation paradigm (SYNAuG, Fill-Up, LTGC) and the LLM-driven prompting strategy of LTGC, but adapts both to the temporal and semantic-ambiguity challenges unique to video. The two-stage training with real-frequency-based Balanced Softmax departs from prior generative-augmentation work (Li et al., SYNAuG) that either uses standard cross-entropy or augmented-frequency re-weighting, and from decoupling methods (cRT, BBN) that separate representation and classifier training without synthetic data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Gen2Balance provides the first systematic demonstration that current text-to-video models, when carefully prompted, produce clips of sufficient quality to close most of the long-tail gap in video action recognition, and the released 140K-video dataset makes this reproducible. The practical finding that partial balancing (B=330) captures 79% of the performance gains at only 27% of the generation cost is important for adoption, as full balancing requires ~9K GPU-hours. Open questions include whether the approach generalises to open-vocabulary or egocentric settings, how sensitive results are to the underlying video generator's quality (the paper uses WAN 2.1 but does not test Veo 3 or Sora 2), and whether the domain-shift correction in Stage 2 remains necessary as generative fidelity improves.
