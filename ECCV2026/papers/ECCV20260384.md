## Problem

Personalized segmentation and personalized retrieval both require instance-level matching across independent images, yet they have evolved as separate tasks with distinct solutions. The only prior unified approach (PDM) relies on Stable Diffusion features, making it over 75× slower than necessary, and SAM 2's video-tracking pretraining introduces a spatio-temporal continuity bias that prevents direct cross-image instance discrimination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260384/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a reference image with a flexible prompt (binary mask, bounding box, or single point) and either a single target image (for segmentation) or a gallery of candidates (for retrieval). Multiple reference images can be provided simultaneously for few-shot retrieval, where each is stored in the memory bank and attended jointly. Internally, features operate at 1/16 spatial resolution (H/16 × W/16 tokens), and the retrieval score is a single scalar in [0,1] per candidate image, while the segmentation output is a full-resolution binary mask.

## Method

FoundYou repurposes SAM 2's memory attention mechanism—originally designed to propagate identity across temporally adjacent video frames—to match a specific instance across independent images. To remove the inherited temporal continuity bias, AdaptFormer blocks (bottleneck dimension 0.5×) are inserted in the last two Image Encoder stages, trained with a binary cross-entropy retrieval loss using DINOv2-mined hard negatives. A lightweight retrieval decoder (two rounds of bidirectional cross-attention with a learnable token) aggregates dense memory-conditioned features into a global similarity score, while the frozen SAM 2 mask decoder produces segmentation masks from the same features. A self-distillation loss (KL divergence between frozen SAM 2 and adapted model mask logits) preserves spatial structure needed for accurate segmentation. The entire SAM 2 backbone remains frozen; only 5.9 M parameters (adapters + retrieval head) are trained, yielding a 52 M-parameter model at 90 FPS.

## Results

FoundYou achieves 96.4 mIoU on PerSeg and 62.6 mIoU on PerMIS for personalized segmentation, and 92.1 mAP on PerMIR and 32.5 mAP on ILIAS for personalized retrieval, all with a single 52 M-parameter model running at 90 FPS. Compared to the only prior unified method PDM, FoundYou improves by +18.4 mIoU on PerMIS and +17.8 mAP on ILIAS while being 75× faster and 20× smaller. It also surpasses the strongest task-specific specialists: +14.8 mIoU over PerSAM-F on PerMIS and +6.1 mAP over AMES on ILIAS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoundYou outperforms all 15 baselines across every benchmark it is evaluated on. Against the only prior unified method PDM [49], it gains +18.4 mIoU on PerMIS, +6.9 mIoU on PerSeg, +26.8 mAP on PerMIR, and +17.8 mAP on ILIAS, while running 75× faster (90.2 vs. 1.2 FPS) with 23× fewer parameters (52M vs. 1200M). Among segmentation specialists, FoundYou beats PerSAM-F [74] by +14.8 mIoU on PerMIS and +1.1 mIoU on PerSeg, and GF-SAM [73] by +17.0 mIoU on PerMIS. Among retrieval specialists, it surpasses AMES [53] by +6.1 mAP on ILIAS and +53.9 mAP on PerMIR, and RoMa v2 [16] by +5.9 mAP on ILIAS. On category-level retrieval (Table 3), FoundYou also leads or matches the best baseline (AMES) across all nine domain-specific benchmarks, e.g., +1.1 on Cars196, +1.1 on iNaturalist, and +0.7 on ROxford, confirming that instance-level training transfers to fine-grained category retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes SAM 2's memory attention mechanism—originally designed to propagate object identity across temporally adjacent video frames—for cross-image instance matching, a setting with no positional or contextual continuity. It departs from PDM (the only prior unified solution), which relies on Stable Diffusion features and SAM prompting, and from task-specific methods like PerSAM (DINOv2-based matching + SAM) and AMES (DINOv2-based retrieval), by showing that SAM 2's tracking pretraining already encodes instance-level identity that can be unlocked with lightweight AdaptFormer adapters (5.9M params) and a compact retrieval head. The self-distillation loss and offline hard-negative mining via frozen DINOv2 are practical design choices that distinguish the training recipe from prior adaptation work on SAM 2 (e.g., SANSA, FS-SAM2), which target category-level generalization rather than instance-level discrimination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single 52M-parameter model at 90 FPS handles both personalized segmentation and retrieval, making instance-level personalization feasible in resource-constrained settings (robotics, personal media, cultural heritage) where PDM's 1200M diffusion-based pipeline is infeasible. The introduction of few-shot personalized retrieval (multiple references at inference) addresses a realistic user workflow that prior methods cannot handle without ad-hoc score averaging. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video-based personalization (tracking the same object across a user's video collection), how performance scales to galleries beyond 100M images without the SigLIP pre-filter, and whether the temporal-continuity bias can be further reduced for highly occluded or viewpoint-extreme cases.
