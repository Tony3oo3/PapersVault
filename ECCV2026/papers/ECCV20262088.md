## Problem

Existing multi-camera view recommendation architectures process historical shots and candidate views as a single concatenated sequence through a unified Transformer encoder, entangling two distinct reasoning tasks: modeling temporal dynamics of the past and evaluating a set of future choices. This architectural bottleneck limits the model's ability to independently interrogate history for each candidate, capping performance at 37.16% Precision@0.5 on the TVMCE benchmark.

## Input / Output

At each editing boundary, the model receives P=16 past frames (the recently shown shots) and C=6 synchronized candidate camera views. Visual features are extracted by a frozen SwinV2-Tiny backbone and projected to D=768, then augmented with learnable camera identity embeddings and sinusoidal temporal offset encodings (clamped at 500 frames). The output is a per-candidate sigmoid probability score; the correct shot is identified as the one exceeding a decision threshold (τ=0.5 for the standard benchmark, τ=0.3 for the optimized macro-averaged protocol).

## Method

The proposed Dual-Transformer architecture explicitly decouples temporal memory construction from candidate evaluation into two separate Transformer branches. A Temporal Encoder (1-layer Transformer) processes the 16 past tokens—formed by summing visual features, sinusoidal offset encodings, and the current camera identity embedding—to produce a historical memory matrix M that serves as both Key and Value. The 6 candidate tokens (visual features plus their own camera identity embeddings) are first refined by a symmetric Candidate Transformer Encoder, then act as Queries in a Multi-Head Cross-Attention module (H=8 heads) against the memory, allowing each candidate to independently retrieve the most relevant historical context. A final 4-head self-attention layer lets candidates interact post-retrieval before a two-layer MLP head (192 hidden units, GELU, LayerNorm) produces recommendation scores. Training uses Focal Loss (α=0.25, γ=2.0) to handle the extreme class imbalance of one correct shot among five distractors, with the SwinV2 backbone kept frozen and only the Transformer branches and projection layers optimized via AdamW (lr=1e-5) for 30 epochs.

## Results

On the TVMCE benchmark, the proposed Dual-Transformer with SwinV2-Tiny backbone and Focal Loss achieves a new state-of-the-art Precision@0.5 of 69.65%, substantially surpassing the previous best of 37.16% (Lee et al., 2025). Even with the same SwinV1 backbone used by prior work, the architecture alone yields 56.60% Precision@0.5, a ~20-point gain over the previous SOTA. The model also achieves a Recall@1 of 76.31% and, after threshold tuning (τ=0.3 macro), a test F1-score of 76.52%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines on TVMCE. Against the original TC-Transformer [13] (22.50%), the proposed method achieves a 47-point improvement in Precision@0.5. Against the previous state-of-the-art Lee et al. [9] (37.16%), the gain is approximately 32 points with the SwinV2 backbone (69.65%) and about 20 points even with the same SwinV1 backbone (56.60%). Against the multimodal concert-editing framework of Gonzálbez-Biosca et al. [6] (17.65%), the improvement is over 50 points. Notably, the authors re-implement Lee et al.'s single-transformer architecture under both SwinV1 (47.97%) and SwinV2 (54.06%) backbones, isolating the architectural contribution: the Dual-Transformer design alone adds roughly 15.6 points over the re-implemented SOTA with the same SwinV2 backbone, confirming that the decoupled cross-attention structure is the primary driver of the performance gain rather than the backbone choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the unified-encoder paradigm established by the TC-Transformer (Rao et al., 2022) and refined by Lee et al. (2025), both of which process historical shots and candidate views as a single concatenated sequence through one transformer. The key architectural departure is an explicit decoupling: a dedicated temporal encoder builds a memory of past frames, and candidate views then query this memory via cross-attention, separating the 'understand the past' and 'evaluate the choices' sub-tasks. The paper also inherits the camera-identity embedding and sinusoidal temporal offset encoding from Lee et al. [9], and adopts Swin Transformer V2 as the visual backbone, extending the backbone ablation beyond the SwinV1 used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~20-point Precision@0.5 gain over prior SOTA and the demonstration that 20% of a single video's editing data suffices for editor-style personalization make this practically relevant for production pipelines where labeling is expensive. However, the evaluation is confined to the TVMCE dataset (14 hours of edited video), the prior SOTA comparison relies on a re-implementation (no public code from the original authors), and the model remains purely visual with no audio or motion cues. Open questions include whether the decoupled architecture scales to more than six candidate cameras, whether the cross-attention mechanism generalizes to longer temporal contexts, and whether multimodal integration (audio dynamics, actor movement) can further close the gap to human editing quality.
