## Problem

Existing stylized motion generation methods encode each style motion into a single unstructured embedding, which fails to capture intra-style diversity—the fact that a single style (e.g., 'aeroplane') should manifest through multiple distinct motion patterns rather than one rigid form. Moreover, these unstructured features entangle content and style information from the reference motion, causing generated outputs to deviate from the intended content text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260604/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D style motion sequence (joint positions over L_s timesteps) and a content text prompt as input, and produces a stylized 3D human motion sequence of length L_c. The style motion is encoded through a frozen VAE into a 5-vector latent representation, while the text is encoded via a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14. At inference, the user can select specific global and local prototypes to control the degree and character of stylistic expression, enabling diverse outputs from the same style category.

## Method

ClusterStyle replaces the single style embedding with a set of K non-learnable prototypes per style category, updated via EMA over assigned feature centroids. Prototype assignment is formulated as an unsupervised optimal transport problem solved with the Sinkhorn algorithm, and two contrastive losses (inter-style and intra-style) shape the prototype space to be both discriminative across categories and diverse within each category. Hierarchical clustering is applied at both the global level (entire sequence) and local level (temporal segments of window size w), yielding a concatenated style representation. A Stylistic Modulation Adapter (SMA) with separate cross-attention branches for content and style injects the style features into the pretrained MLD latent diffusion model, and a prototype-based guidance term during denoising steers generation toward a chosen sub-style pattern.

## Results

On the stylized motion generation task (Table 1), ClusterStyle achieves FID 1.137, R-Precision (Top-3) 0.708, MM-Dist 3.610, and SRA 78.101, outperforming the strongest baseline BiFlow by 27% in FID, 15.5% in R-Precision, and 1.1% in SRA. On motion style transfer (Table 2), ClusterStyle achieves FID 0.768, FSR 0.078, and SRA 73.849, improving over StyleMotif by 44% in FID and 5.03% in SRA while also attaining the lowest foot-skating ratio. The method trades a slight loss in FSR (0.113 vs. StyleMotif's 0.097) and Diversity (8.719 vs. BiFlow's 9.303) for substantially better content alignment and style fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two older autoencoder-based baselines, ClusterStyle is decisively superior: Aberman [1] (FID 3.309, SRA 54.4) and Motion Puzzle [33] (FID 6.127, SRA 63.8) are outperformed by large margins on every metric. Among recent diffusion-based methods, SMooDi [86] (FID 1.609, R-Precision 0.571, SRA 72.4) is surpassed by ClusterStyle on FID (−29%), R-Precision (+24%), and SRA (+7.9%), though SMooDi achieves slightly higher Diversity (9.235 vs. 8.719). BiFlow [40] is the closest competitor on FSR (0.118 vs. 0.113) and Diversity (9.303 vs. 8.719), but ClusterStyle leads on FID (1.137 vs. 1.527), MM-Dist (3.610 vs. 4.292), R-Precision (0.708 vs. 0.613), and SRA (78.101 vs. 77.042). StyleMotif [27] achieves the best FSR (0.097) and competitive SRA (77.650), yet ClusterStyle still wins on FID (1.137 vs. 1.551), MM-Dist (3.610 vs. 4.354), R-Precision (0.708 vs. 0.586), and SRA (78.101 vs. 77.650). In the motion style transfer task (Table 2), ClusterStyle dominates all three reported metrics (FID 0.768, FSR 0.078, SRA 73.849) over every baseline, with the largest gap over StyleMotif (FID −44%, SRA +5.03%).

## Relation to Prior Work

ClusterStyle builds on the motion latent diffusion model (MLD) as its generative backbone and positions itself against the SMooDi/BiFlow/StyleMotif line of work, which all encode style motions into a single unstructured embedding via ControlNet-like architectures. The key conceptual departure is borrowing the prototype-based clustering paradigm from vision (e.g., ClustSeg, Rethinking Semantic Segmentation) to represent each style category with multiple non-learnable centroids rather than one learned vector, and extending this to a hierarchical (global + local temporal) formulation. The Stylistic Modulation Adapter replaces the ControlNet-style fusion with a dual cross-attention mechanism that separately handles content and style, drawing on the cross-attention conditioning pattern from EnergyMogen.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly modeling intra-style diversity through multiple prototypes, ClusterStyle addresses a gap that prior stylized motion methods overlook: a single style (e.g., 'aeroplane') should yield varied expressions rather than a rigid, uniform output. The prototype-based formulation also improves content-text alignment because prototypes capture style-relevant patterns more cleanly than unstructured embeddings that conflate content and style. Open questions include whether the fixed number of prototypes (K_g=3, K_l=30) generalizes to open-vocabulary or unseen styles, whether the Sinkhorn-based assignment scales to very large style datasets, and whether the hierarchical local clustering adds meaningful value beyond global prototypes for longer or more complex motions.
