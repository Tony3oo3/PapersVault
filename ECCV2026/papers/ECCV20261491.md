## Problem

Physics-informed 4D generation methods either impose hand-crafted constitutive laws or learn per-scene dynamics from scratch, preventing the learning of a shared cross-scene dynamics structure. This leaves a gap toward a true world model that can infer intrinsic dynamics in unseen scenes without per-scene optimization. The paper asks whether a unified intrinsic dynamics representation can be learned directly from multi-scene visual observations to enable zero-shot generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261491/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB videos (10 views × 400 frames for synthetic scenes, 3 views × 19 frames for real scenes) and computes optical flow between consecutive frames, which is embedded via a pretrained Tora encoder into spatiotemporal features before projection to a 128-dim dynamics latent. The output is a pair of continuous constitutive functions (elastic stress and plastic corrected deformation gradient) parameterized as a linear combination of D shared basis functions weighted by scene-specific coefficients; these functions are then evaluated inside an MPM simulator to produce 4D dynamics rollouts at 32³ or 70³ grid resolution.

## Method

NeuIDO formulates world dynamics modeling as a neural operator learning problem using a DeepONet-style branch-trunk architecture: the branch network (a dynamics encoder built on optical flow + Tora encoder + projector) maps a video to a compact latent, which two MLP heads convert into elastic and plastic coefficient vectors; the trunk network parameterizes shared basis functions over the deformation gradient for stress and corrected deformation-gradient responses. A two-stage training strategy addresses the sparsity of video supervision: Stage I pretrains the trunk on a large collection of known constitutive laws (elastic, plasticine, sand) with per-trajectory learnable latents to build a structured constitutive representation space, and Stage II freezes the trunk and trains only the video encoder to align visual observations with this space via differentiable MPM simulation and video reconstruction loss. Zero-shot inference is purely feed-forward (no per-scene optimization), while few-shot adaptation inserts LoRA modules (r=16) into the trunk and jointly refines the scene latent for 100 iterations, enabling rapid specialization to out-of-distribution dynamics.

## Results

On the synthetic NeuMA dataset, NeuIDO with few-shot adaptation achieves an average chamfer distance of 1.216, outperforming all baselines including NeuMA (1.329) and VisionLaw (1.620). In the zero-shot setting (no per-scene optimization), NeuIDO achieves an average CD of 1.643, comparable to VisionLaw (1.620) and slightly behind NeuMA (1.329). On real-world Spring-Gaus scenes, NeuIDO (with few-shot adaptation) achieves the highest PSNR across all three scenes, surpassing Spring-Gaus, NeuMA, and VisionLaw. The few-shot adaptation requires only 100 iterations, compared to ~1000 iterations needed by NeuMA even when initialized from an NCLaw-pretrained model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuIDO with few-shot adaptation (CD 1.216) outperforms all five baselines on the synthetic dataset, beating the closest competitor NeuMA (1.329) by ~8% in average chamfer distance. In the zero-shot setting, NeuIDO (1.643) is competitive with VisionLaw (1.620) but trails NeuMA (1.329), with the gap concentrated on the JellyDuck scene where distribution shift from pretraining is largest. PAC-NeRF (114.80) and NCLaw (12.860) are orders of magnitude worse, reflecting their reliance on predefined constitutive models or particle trajectory supervision respectively. On real-world Spring-Gaus scenes, NeuIDO achieves the highest PSNR across all three scenes, outperforming Spring-Gaus (limited spring-mass expressivity), NeuMA (dependent on NCLaw pretraining), and VisionLaw (prone to local minima in complex scenes). Notably, NeuIDO's few-shot adaptation converges in 100 iterations versus ~1000 for NeuMA, highlighting the efficiency gain from the pretrained unified dynamics representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuIDO directly adopts the branch-trunk neural operator architecture from DeepONet [33] to parameterize the mapping from video observations to constitutive functions, and extends the per-scene differentiable-simulation paradigm of NeuMA [6] by replacing scene-specific optimization with a shared, pretrained operator space. The key conceptual departure is from prior physics-informed 4D methods (PAC-NeRF, NeuMA, VisionLaw) that independently infer dynamics for each scene: NeuIDO instead learns a unified cross-scene dynamics representation via a two-stage strategy (pretraining on known constitutive laws, then aligning video to that space), reframing physics-informed 4D generation as a world-modeling problem grounded in the Universal Approximation Theorem for neural operators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single pretrained operator can infer plausible intrinsic dynamics zero-shot from unseen videos and adapt in ~100 LoRA iterations (vs. ~1000 for NeuMA), NeuIDO offers a practical pathway toward physics-informed world models for embodied agents that must reason about novel environments without per-scene optimization. However, the evaluation is limited to small-scale scenes (6 synthetic, 3 real, 10 generalization) with only elastic and plastic MPM constitutive laws, leaving open whether the operator formulation scales to more complex material behaviors (viscoelasticity, fracture, fluid-structure interaction), multi-object contact, or higher-fidelity simulators. The reliance on a fixed MPM simulator and 3DGS initialization also constrains generality, and the gap between zero-shot and few-shot performance on distribution-shifted scenes (e.g., JellyDuck) suggests the pretrained representation space may not yet cover the full diversity of real-world dynamics.
