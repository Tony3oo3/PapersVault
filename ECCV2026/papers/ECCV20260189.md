## Problem

General-purpose VLMs struggle with marine video understanding because informative events are sparse, unpredictable, and unevenly distributed across long, visually redundant underwater footage, and no existing dataset or method specifically targets event-centric reasoning in this domain. Current marine benchmarks focus on static image-level tasks or global scene description, overlooking the fine-grained temporal and causal dynamics (e.g., predator-prey interactions, rare species appearances) that carry the most ecological significance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260189/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a marine video clip (segmented into scenes by TransNet, with up to 50k tokens per sequence) and a text question spanning five reasoning dimensions (semantic, contextual, spatial, temporal, causal). During inference, the VLM produces a multi-turn trajectory of interleaved reasoning steps and tool invocations (SAM3 bounding boxes, temporal grounding intervals) that progressively condense the visual context before emitting a final answer in task-specific formats (yes/no, MCQ, open-end, or exact-match). The tool outputs replace prior visual tokens in the dialogue history, effectively pruning redundant frames at each turn.

## Method

EVT-R1 decomposes event-centric marine video understanding into a sequential decision-making process where a VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B) dynamically decides at each turn whether to invoke a visual tool (spatial grounding via SAM3, temporal localization) or produce a final answer. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: one epoch of SFT on MarineEVT to close the domain knowledge gap, followed by GRPO-based reinforcement learning with a dual-reward design that separately scores tool-invocation validity/accuracy (R_tool) and answer correctness (R_ans) at each turn, enabling turn-level credit assignment rather than only final-output rewards. The GRPO objective uses group-relative advantages with clipped ratios and KL regularization against a reference policy, optimized via LoRA (lr=5e-6) on a single H800 GPU. This dual-reward mechanism outperforms standard GRPO (which rewards only the final answer) by +0.69–0.94 average accuracy and produces more stable reward curves with lower variance in later training stages.

## Results

On the MarineEVT benchmark (2,000 QA pairs), EVT-R1 achieves an average accuracy of 48.89 across five reasoning dimensions (Semantic, Contextual, Spatial, Temporal, Causal), outperforming the best open-source model Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (43.67) by +5.22 and the best closed-source model GPT-5-Mini (37.71 without tools, 40.35 with tools) by +11.18 and +8.54 respectively. The largest gains appear in Spatial Reasoning (30.60 vs. 22.40 for Qwen3-VL) and Temporal Reasoning (20.75 vs. 14.00), confirming that visual tool invocation most improves the dimensions where general-purpose VLMs struggle most. Compared to SFT-only fine-tuning (45.27) and GRPO post-training only (35.58), EVT-R1's SFT+RL pipeline with dual rewards yields +3.62 and +13.31 average gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVT-R1 (48.89 avg) decisively outperforms all five open-source video VLMs without tools, with the closest competitor Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct at 43.67 (gap: +5.22), and the weakest Video-LLaVA-7B at 21.92 (gap: +26.97). Against closed-source models, EVT-R1 beats GPT-5-Mini without tools (37.71) by +11.18, GPT-5-Mini with tools (40.35) by +8.54, Gemini-3.0-Flash (37.59) by +11.30, and Grok-4-1-FR (30.57) by +18.32. In the token-compression comparison (Table 4), EVT-R1 surpasses the best VisionZip-compressed model (InternVL2-8B at 30.23) by +18.66, indicating that compressing tokens is fundamentally insufficient for localizing sparse marine events. Against temporal-centric baselines (Table 5), EVT-R1 outperforms the best key-frame selection method MaxInfo (30.37) by +18.52 and the best temporal localization framework VideoITG (39.57) by +9.32. Among the paper's own training variants, SFT+RL (EVT-R1, 48.89) beats SFT-only (45.27) by +3.62 and GRPO-only (35.58) by +13.31, while the dual-reward EVT-R1 further edges out standard GRPO with SFT warm-up (48.20) by +0.69.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning paradigm from DeepSeek-R1 to the multimodal video domain, adapting it with a dual-reward structure (tool-use + answer accuracy) that departs from prior work assigning rewards only to final outputs. Conceptually, it builds on VTool-R1's idea of VLMs learning to invoke visual tools via RL, but shifts the focus from image editing to temporal/spatial localization in video, and grounds the entire pipeline in a newly curated marine domain. It also positions itself against token compression methods (VisionZip, PVC) and keyframe selection approaches (MaxInfo, AKS), arguing that explicit tool-based localization outperforms passive compression for sparse-event video understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MarineEVT provides the first event-centric marine video benchmark (20K QA pairs, 20 dimensions), filling a clear gap where prior marine datasets were image-level or limited to static video attributes. The EVT-R1 framework demonstrates that multi-turn visual tool invocation with RL can substantially close the gap between open-source and commercial VLMs on domain-specific video tasks, suggesting a generalizable recipe for sparse-event domains beyond marine (e.g., surgical, scientific). However, the evaluation is confined to a single base model (Qwen3-VL-8B) and a self-constructed dataset, leaving open whether the dual-reward design and tool-integrated reasoning transfer to other domains or scale to larger models. The reliance on GPT-5 for data generation and annotation verification also raises questions about annotation quality and potential bias propagation.
