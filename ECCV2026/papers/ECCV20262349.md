## Problem

Self-supervised vision transformers exhibit emergent object segmentation properties, but whether their object grouping aligns with human perceptual behavior in naturalistic scenes remains untested. Prior work on model-object perception has focused on low-level Gestalt cues or simple stimuli, leaving open whether the alignment extends to complex natural scenes and what representation-level property drives it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262349/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The behavioral task presents naturalistic COCO2017 scenes with a central fixation dot and a peripheral dot at ~3° (close) or ~6° (far) visual angle, placed via object annotations to guarantee valid same/different conditions with matched distances. For modeling, patch features are extracted from the two dot locations in the last layer, concatenated (central first), and fed to a 2-layer MLP; the same pipeline predicts binary same/different labels (trained on ~30k synthetic trials) or continuous reaction times (trained on 1,020 behavioral trials via 10-fold nested cross-validation with 10 seeds per fold). Object-centricity is quantified as the AUC of an ROC curve built from affinity maps (cosine similarity of the central patch to all other patches) against ground-truth object boundaries.

## Method

The paper introduces a large-scale two-dot behavioral benchmark (72 participants, 255 images × 4 conditions = 1,020 trials) and evaluates nine vision models (DINO/DINOv2/DINOv3/MAE ViT-B, supervised ViT-B and ConvNext-B, and DINOv3-distilled ConvNext-B) on grouping accuracy and noise-normalized RT prediction. A novel object-centric metric is proposed: for each trial, the affinity map from the central dot is thresholded and scored against the ground-truth object mask, yielding a per-model ROC AUC that captures how well patch-level similarity reflects object identity without object-level supervision. Across models, this AUC strongly predicts behavioral alignment (Spearman r = 0.950, p = 0.0001), with DINO-trained transformers leading. The key mechanistic intervention is Gram alignment: supervised models are fine-tuned on ImageNet classification while distilling the full (h×w)×(h×w) pairwise cosine-similarity Gram matrix from DINOv3 ViT B, which consistently improves grouping accuracy (8–18 pp), object-centric AUC, and RT prediction, with Transformer architectures showing larger gains than convolutional ones.

## Results

On the two-dot object grouping benchmark (1,020 trials over 255 COCO2017 images), DINOv3 ViT B achieves the highest grouping accuracy at 91.9%, followed by DINOv2 ViT B (89.0%) and DINOv3 ConvNext B (86.7%). Supervised Transformer models (IN21k ViT B: 72.2%, IN1K ViT B: 70.6%) and supervised convolutional models (IN21k ConvNext B: 67.4%, IN1K ConvNext B: 60.0%) perform substantially worse despite sharing the same architecture. In behavioral alignment (noise-normalized Spearman correlation with human reaction times), the same ordering holds, with DINOv3 ViT B reaching the highest value. Object-centric AUC (ROC of patch affinity maps) correlates strongly with behavioral alignment across all 9 models (Spearman r = 0.950, p = 0.0001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DINOv3 ViT B (91.9%) outperforms DINOv2 ViT B (89.0%) by 2.9 points and DINO ViT B (76.5%) by 15.4 points, showing steady improvement across DINO generations. The critical comparison is training objective vs. architecture: DINOv3 ViT B (91.9%) beats IN21k ViT B (72.2%) by 19.7 points despite identical ViT-Base architecture, and DINOv3 ConvNext B (86.7%) beats IN21k ConvNext B (67.4%) by 19.3 points despite identical ConvNext-Base architecture. MAE ViT B (80.7%) falls between the DINO self-distillation models and supervised models, indicating reconstruction-based self-supervision captures some but not all object-centric structure. Among supervised models, large-dataset pretraining (ImageNet-21K) provides a modest 1.6–16.4 point advantage over ImageNet-1K-only training, but cannot close the gap to self-supervised models. The Gram alignment experiment further shows that distilling DINOv3's Gram matrix structure into supervised models yields 8–18 point gains in grouping accuracy, with Transformer models benefiting more than convolutional ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical two-dot psychophysics paradigm (Korjoukov et al., 2012) from a handful of hand-selected stimuli to a large-scale behavioral benchmark on naturalistic COCO scenes, and connects it to the emergent object-centric properties recently documented in self-supervised vision transformers (DINO, DINOv2, DINOv3). It builds on prior evidence that self-attention in ViTs performs perceptual grouping and that DINO-family models encode semantic segmentation in their attention maps, but departs from contour-integration studies (Lonnqvist et al., 2025) by testing grouping in ecologically valid settings where object boundaries are ambiguous. The Gram alignment experiment extends the DINOv3 design principle of Gram anchoring into a distillation recipe for supervised models, isolating pairwise feature correlation structure as the active ingredient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first large-scale behavioral benchmark directly linking vision model representations to human object-grouping reaction times, and shows that a simple object-centricity metric (ROC AUC on patch affinity maps) predicts behavioral alignment across architectures and training objectives with r=0.95. The Gram alignment result offers a practical, low-cost technique to improve supervised models' object-centricity without retraining from scratch, with implications for dense prediction tasks. A notable gap remains between the best model (DINOv3 ViT B) and the human noise ceiling, and the paper acknowledges that perfect object-centricity is not the goal—future work should explore the optimal balance between object-level organization and fine-grained feature sensitivity, and whether closing the behavioral gap requires new architectural or training innovations beyond the DINO family.
