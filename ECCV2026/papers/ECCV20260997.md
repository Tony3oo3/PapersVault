## Problem

Appearance-based gaze estimation models achieve strong benchmark accuracy but are too large, slow, or GPU-dependent for real-time browser deployment, while existing browser-based trackers (e.g., WebGazer) rely on simple regression and degrade significantly under natural head motion. No prior system simultaneously delivers lightweight real-time inference, head-pose-aware 2D gaze prediction, and few-shot per-user personalization entirely on-device within a web browser.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260997/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a single RGB webcam frame, from which MediaPipe extracts a 468-point 3D facial landmark mesh and a relative pose matrix; the eye region is warped to a fixed 128×512×3 patch via a homography derived from those landmarks. Gaze output is a 2D point-of-gaze coordinate normalized to [−0.5, 0.5]² with origin at screen center (device-agnostic), plus an open/closed eye-state flag from eye aspect ratio and a metric head pose [R|t] in centimeters. Calibration requires as few as 9 screen-coordinate samples per user, and continuous clickstream interactions can incrementally expand the support set during a session.

## Method

WebEyeTrack combines two novel components: (1) a metric head-pose pipeline that reprojects MediaPipe's UVZ landmarks into 3D XYZ space using camera intrinsics, recovers absolute face scale from a fixed iris-diameter constant (α = 1.2 cm) via pixel-ratio scaling, and iteratively refines depth through a radial Procrustes alignment with similar-triangle XY updates (converging in ≤10 iterations); and (2) BlazeGaze, a 670 KB CNN built from BlazeBlocks that encodes the eye patch into a 512-D embedding and predicts 2D PoG via a 3-layer MLP conditioned on the metric head pose. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 jointly optimizes an encoder–decoder–gaze-MLP with a multi-objective loss (pixel reconstruction, frequency-weighted L2 gaze error, and a contrastive embedding-consistency loss that aligns latent distances with normalized gaze distances); Stage 2 freezes the encoder, discards the decoder, and applies first-order MAML to the gaze MLP so that a new user's model adapts from k ≤ 9 calibration samples in a few gradient steps. The entire pipeline—landmark detection, head-pose computation, gaze inference, and MAML adaptation—runs in-browser via TensorFlow.js, keeping all frames and embeddings on-device.

## Results

BlazeGaze achieves a Point-of-Gaze (PoG) error of 2.32 cm on GazeCapture, 4.56 cm on MPIIFaceGaze, and 7.53 cm on EyeDiap, while requiring only 0.15 GFLOPs, 0.16 M parameters, and 0.88 ms inference latency (1137 FPS) on an Intel i7-11700F CPU. In the cross-dataset Eye of the Typer evaluation, WEBEYETRACK's PoG error increases only 20% (7.24 → 8.72 cm) over a 20-minute session, compared to a 49% increase (7.79 → 11.62 cm) for WebGazer (Mann-Whitney U test, p < 0.05). The model is 670 KB in size and runs fully on-device in the browser via TensorFlow.js.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GazeCapture, BlazeGaze (2.32 cm) is essentially tied with AFF-Net (2.30 cm) and clearly outperforms SAGE (2.72 cm), TAT (2.66 cm), iTracker (2.81 cm), and EFE (2.48 cm). On MPIIFaceGaze, BlazeGaze (4.56 cm) trails the most accurate methods—EFE (3.89 cm) and AFF-Net (4.21 cm)—but is competitive with Gaze360 (4.66 cm) and substantially ahead of older baselines like RT-Gene (5.36 cm) and DilatedNet (5.07 cm). On EyeDiap, BlazeGaze (7.53 cm) is outperformed by Gaze360 (6.37 cm) and CA-Net (6.30 cm), though it beats AFF-Net (9.25 cm) and iTracker (10.13 cm). The decisive advantage is efficiency: BlazeGaze uses 0.15 GFLOPs and 0.16 M parameters versus 26.14 GFLOPs / 1.94 M for AFF-Net and 202 GFLOPs / 3.92 M for DilatedNet, yielding 1137 FPS versus 9.1 and 0.8 FPS respectively—a 125× speedup over AFF-Net. In the real-world Eye of the Typer video evaluation, WEBEYETRACK's temporal drift (20% error increase) is significantly lower than WebGazer's (49%), with a statistically significant difference (p < 0.05), demonstrating that metric head pose conditioning and MAML personalization provide robustness that click-only recalibration cannot match.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of appearance-based gaze estimation (MPIIGaze, GazeCapture, EyeDiap lineage) and browser-based eye tracking (WebGazer, iTracker lineage), explicitly positioning itself against both. Architecturally, BlazeGaze adapts BlazeFace's BlazeBlocks for gaze-specific encoding, while the metric head pose component repurposes Gower's Procrustes analysis into a novel radial variant combined with iris-based face scaling. The few-shot personalization layer applies MAML in the spirit of FAZE and SAGE, but targets a 670 KB model deployable via TensorFlow.js rather than a GPU-dependent pipeline, and the continuous clickstream calibration extends WebGazer's interaction protocol into the meta-learning loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By delivering competitive 2D PoG accuracy (2.32 cm on GazeCapture) at 0.16 M parameters and 2.4 ms on-device latency, WebEyeTrack makes gaze estimation viable in privacy-preserving browser contexts without specialized hardware, a gap that has persisted since WebGazer's 2016 release. The temporal-stability finding (20% drift over 20 minutes vs. WebGazer's 49%) addresses a practical failure mode that prior browser tools have not resolved. Open questions include the sensitivity of the fixed iris-diameter assumption across diverse populations and camera geometries, the generalizability of MAML adaptation beyond the k ≤ 9 regime, and whether the approach extends to 3D gaze or multi-display settings.
