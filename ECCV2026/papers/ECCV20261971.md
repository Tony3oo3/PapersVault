## Problem

Existing vision-language agents for image restoration operate in a training-free manner, relying on heuristic task scheduling and exhaustive tool traversal, which yields sub-optimal restoration paths and prohibitive inference cost. The core bottleneck is the absence of a learned policy capable of degradation-aware task ordering and direct tool selection, since a VLM cannot efficiently handle these decisions without explicit training on restoration feedback.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261971/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a degraded RGB image suffering from unknown composite degradations (e.g., noise, haze, blur, low light, compression) and iteratively produces a restored high-quality image through a sequence of tool invocations. At each step the VLM observes the full image state history and outputs a structured action specifying both the restoration task (denoising, dehazing, deblurring, super-resolution, etc.) and the specific specialist model to invoke, rather than enumerating all candidates. The process terminates when the agent determines no further restoration is beneficial.

## Method

TIR-Agent builds on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct and is trained via a two-stage pipeline: supervised fine-tuning on 39,827 demonstration trajectories followed by reinforcement learning with 4,000 image pairs. Two key designs enable effective RL: (i) Exploration-Driven Perturbation (EDP) injects controlled randomness into SFT data by shuffling task execution order (probability α^t=0.3) and interpolating tool selection toward a uniform distribution (α^m=0.4), preventing the policy from collapsing to narrow deterministic patterns; (ii) Multi-Dimensional Adaptive Reward (MAR) computes per-metric advantages in a decoupled manner and dynamically re-weights them via softmax over clipped EMA-relative deviations, so that under-optimized metrics receive higher gradient signal and reward hacking across heterogeneous metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, MANIQA, CLIPIQA, MUSIQ) is mitigated. A globally shared MC-Pool of 32 RTX 3090 GPUs provides asynchronous, lock-based tool invocation to support up to 128 concurrent rollouts during RL training.

## Results

On MiO100 Group A, TIR-Agent achieves PSNR 22.07, SSIM 0.6935, LPIPS 0.2874, MANIQA 0.3907, CLIPIQA 0.5454, and MUSIQ 63.69, outperforming all 6 all-in-one models and 3 training-free agents on most metrics. On Group B, it reaches PSNR 22.80 and SSIM 0.7340, the best among all compared methods. Inference time is 61.58 s (Group A), yielding a ≥2.5× speedup over AgenticIR (184.57 s) and ≥5× over 4KAgent (333.14 s) by eliminating exhaustive tool traversal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIR-Agent outperforms all six all-in-one models (AirNet, PromptIR, MiOIR, DA-CLIP, InstructIR, AutoDIR) on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and MANIQA across all three MiO100 groups, with the largest PSNR gap over AirNet reaching +2.94 dB (Group A). Among training-free agents, TIR-Agent beats AgenticIR by +1.03 dB PSNR and 0.0117 SSIM (Group A), and 4KAgent by +0.59 dB PSNR, while also achieving 2.5× and 5× lower inference time respectively. TIR-Agent slightly trails 4KAgent on CLIPIQA (0.5454 vs 0.5544) and MAIR on CLIPIQA (0.5454 vs 0.4751 in Group A), indicating a minor trade-off on this single NR metric. Against proprietary models (GPT-5.2, Claude Opus 4.5, Gemini 3.0 Pro), TIR-Agent achieves superior LPIPS, MANIQA, CLIPIQA, and MUSIQ scores but is marginally behind Gemini 3.0 Pro on PSNR (22.07 vs 22.23) and SSIM (0.6935 vs 0.7085) in Group A. On out-of-domain degradation combinations (D=2 and D≥3), TIR-Agent achieves the best score on 11 out of 12 metric-dataset pairs, with the advantage widening as the number of composite degradations increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant training-free agent paradigm for image restoration (AgenticIR, MAIR, 4KAgent), which relies on LLM-based heuristic planning and exhaustive tool traversal at inference time. It repurposes the broader agentic RL paradigm—where LLM agents learn tool-use policies through trial-and-error interaction—by applying it for the first time to the image restoration domain, where well-defined FR/NR quality metrics provide natural reward signals. The two key algorithmic contributions (EDP for exploration diversity and MAR for multi-metric reward balancing) are novel mechanisms tailored to the specific challenges of IR trajectory learning, while the MC-Pool infrastructure addresses the practical bottleneck of GPU-intensive tool invocation during RL sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TIR-Agent demonstrates that a domain-specific 8B VLM trained with RL can outperform frontier proprietary models (GPT-5.2, Claude Opus 4.5, Gemini 3.0 Pro) on no-reference image quality metrics while achieving 2.5× faster inference, suggesting that learned tool-calling policies are a viable path to efficient, high-quality restoration systems. The analysis of proprietary model biases (over-invoking denoising, under-invoking super-resolution, preferring suboptimal tools) provides useful diagnostic insight into why general-purpose VLMs underperform on specialized visual tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger tool libraries, whether the FR-dependent training can be made label-free, and whether the EDP/MAR mechanisms transfer to other multi-tool agentic domains beyond IR.
