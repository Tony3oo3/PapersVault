## Problem

Existing event camera simulators infer timestamps by differencing discretely rendered frames, forcing many threshold crossings to share a small set of rendering-schedule timestamps — a failure mode the authors term timestamp batching. This degrades severely under fast motion and occlusion, where finite-difference slope estimates mix distinct visibility regimes and collapse events onto a few discrete times. The paper asks whether an explicit 3D scene representation with analytic time derivatives can eliminate this bottleneck entirely.

## Input / Output

TIDES consumes a pre-fitted dynamic 4D Gaussian splatting scene (learned from RGB video) together with 6-DoF camera trajectories (ground-truth or estimated via COLMAP/Easi3R) and queries the renderer at arbitrary continuous timestamps rather than fixed frame intervals. It outputs an asynchronous event stream of (timestamp, pixel location, polarity) tuples, optionally augmented with sensor-realistic jitter, throughput-limited drops, and burst-induced timestamp spreading from a tile-level readout arbiter. All baseline simulators share the same 4DGS rendering source, isolating differences in timestamping, thresholding, and readout modeling.

## Method

TIDES performs a forward-mode time-derivative Gaussian splatting pass: it computes exact per-Gaussian time derivatives via a Jacobian-vector product through the deformation network, then analytically propagates them through the EWA screen-space projection, opacity clamping, and front-to-back compositing chain to yield visibility-consistent (L, L̇, α, α̇) in a single render. Per-pixel threshold crossings are then solved in continuous time using a linear (or optional quadratic) local model of log-luminance, supporting multiple crossings per step without temporal upsampling. A risk score built from transmittance rate |α̇|, contributor count c, and coverage proxy b drives adaptive sub-stepping, shrinking Δt only where occlusion dynamics invalidate the local linear model. An optional tile-scanning arbiter conditions throughput, drop probability, and timestamp jitter on the same renderer-derived burst and visibility cues to reproduce finite-bandwidth sensor artifacts.

## Results

TIDES achieves the best event-stream fidelity across all four paired RGB-event benchmarks (EDS, HS-ERGB, BS-ERGB, DSEC) under both a fixed 10× query budget and its adaptive stepping mode. On IG-NLL (lower is better), TIDES(adaptive) scores 0.00373 (EDS), 0.00234 (HS-ERGB), 0.00425 (BS-ERGB), and 0.06844 (DSEC), outperforming the next-best baseline by 8–72% depending on dataset. On the motion-scaled Chamfer distance, TIDES(adaptive) achieves 0.0426 (EDS), 0.0138 (HS-ERGB), 0.0323 (BS-ERGB), and 0.1206 (DSEC), again leading all competitors. In downstream transfer (train on simulated, test on real), TIDES events yield the best PSNR for E2VID reconstruction (18.16 dB) and VFI (34.31 dB), the lowest depth RMSE (10.82), and the highest segmentation mIoU (17.69).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIDES outperforms all four baselines on every fidelity metric across all four datasets. Against ESIM (the standard frame-differencing baseline), TIDES(adaptive) reduces IG-NLL by 21% on EDS (0.00373 vs 0.00475) and by 29% on DSEC (0.0684 vs 0.0968), with Chamfer improvements of 9–20%. V2E, which adds photoreceptor and noise modelling, is the closest competitor on EDS IG-NLL (0.00406 vs 0.00373) but still trails TIDES by 8%; on HS-ERGB the gap widens dramatically (0.00582 vs 0.00234, a 60% reduction) because V2E's frame-driven timestamps collapse under dynamic occlusion. DVS-Voltmeter, which models stochastic event timing, is competitive on Chamfer for HS-ERGB (0.01487 vs 0.01377) and BS-ERGB (0.03390 vs 0.03226) but loses on IG-NLL by 25–35% across all datasets, indicating its stochastic model captures spatial support but not fine-grained timing. ICNS/IEBCS, which adds readout arbitration, performs worst on HS-ERGB IG-NLL (0.00834) and BS-ERGB Chamfer (0.04503), suggesting its arbitration layer amplifies upstream timestamp-batching errors. In downstream transfer, TIDES events consistently yield the best PSNR, LPIPS, depth RMSE, and mIoU, confirming that the fidelity gains translate to practical task performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIDES builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for real-time differentiable rendering and 4DGS for dynamic scenes, repurposing the learned 4DGS scene as a forward simulation substrate rather than the inverse reconstruction target that dominates event-based NeRF/splatting literature. It departs fundamentally from all prior event simulators (ESIM, V2E, DVS-Voltmeter, ICNS/IEBCS, PECS), which infer timestamps by differencing discretely rendered frames, by instead computing instantaneous per-pixel log-luminance derivatives via forward-mode automatic differentiation (Jacobian-vector products) through the rendering pipeline itself. The paper is the first to frame event simulation as a forward problem from a learned 4DGS scene, and introduces a tile-level readout arbiter conditioned on renderer-native burst and visibility cues, extending the sensor-modeling line of work (v2e, ICNS) with scene-derived degradation signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TIDES addresses timestamp batching, the dominant failure mode in frame-based event simulation, by making threshold-crossing prediction a well-posed continuous-time root-finding problem rather than an interpolation over discrete samples. The approach is modular and agnostic to the underlying 4DGS reconstruction method, so improvements in dynamic scene reconstruction will directly translate to better event fidelity. A key limitation is that simulation quality is bounded by the 4DGS scene fit, and the paper does not address how well the method scales to very large or highly cluttered dynamic scenes. Open questions include whether the adaptive stepping and readout arbiter generalize to non-Gaussian-splatting renderers, and whether the continuous-time formulation can be extended to multi-spectral or polarization event sensors.
