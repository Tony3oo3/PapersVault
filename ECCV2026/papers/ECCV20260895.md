## Problem

Lightweight real-time detectors suffer from a semantic bottleneck: the architectural constraints needed for high inference speed limit their ability to capture high-level semantics, capping accuracy and hindering on-device deployment. While Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) encode rich, robust semantics, transferring them to resource-constrained detectors is challenging due to large architectural and learning-objective disparities, and prior distillation methods rely on task-specific teachers rather than general-purpose foundation models.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a standard RGB image and outputs object bounding boxes with class labels using the unmodified RT-DETR architecture, incurring zero additional latency or parameters. During training only, the same image is additionally processed by a frozen VFM (DINOv3-ViT-Base) to produce patch-level semantic features that serve as alignment targets; this VFM is entirely absent at deployment, so the inference pipeline and model size remain identical to the base detector.

## Method

The paper introduces a training-only distillation framework with two components. The Deep Semantic Injector (DSI) aligns the AIFI output F5—the deepest feature in RT-DETR and the sole feature subjected to self-attention—with VFM patch representations via a linear feature projector and a patch-wise cosine similarity loss, chosen over MSE for superior performance. The Gradient-guided Adaptive Modulation (GAM) mechanism dynamically adjusts the DSI loss weight λ each epoch by computing the L1 gradient norm ratio of the target module relative to total gradients and steering it toward a target interval [ρ−δ, ρ+δ], preventing the semantic loss from either under-supervising early or over-dominating late in training. The key design insight is injecting semantics exclusively at F5 rather than at backbone features, since F5 acts as the principal semantic source for cross-scale fusion and its gradient naturally backpropagates to update both AIFI and the backbone synergistically. The framework is architecture-agnostic and VFM-agnostic, requiring no modification to the detector at inference.

## Results

RT-DETRv4 achieves state-of-the-art COCO AP across all four model scales: 49.8 (S), 53.7 (M), 55.4 (L), and 57.0 (X), at inference speeds of 273/169/124/78 FPS on T4 GPU. The L variant reaches 55.4 AP with only 31M parameters and 91 GFLOPs, surpassing DEIM-L (54.7 AP) and YOLOv13-L (53.4 AP) at comparable or lower compute. The X variant attains 57.0 AP, exceeding DEIM-X (56.5 AP) and YOLOv12-X (55.2 AP) without any additional inference overhead, as the VFM teacher is used only during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RT-DETRv4 outperforms all compared baselines at every scale. Against the closest DETR-based competitor DEIM, it gains +0.8 AP (S: 49.8 vs 49.0), +1.0 AP (M: 53.7 vs 52.7), +0.7 AP (L: 55.4 vs 54.7), and +0.5 AP (X: 57.0 vs 56.5) at identical parameter and FLOP budgets. Against D-FINE, the margins are larger: +1.3/+1.4/+1.4/+1.2 AP across S/M/L/X. Among YOLO variants, RT-DETRv4-L (55.4 AP) surpasses YOLOv12-L (53.7 AP) and YOLOv13-L (53.4 AP) by 1.7–2.0 AP, while RT-DETRv4-X (57.0 AP) exceeds YOLOv12-X (55.2 AP) by 1.8 AP. Against earlier RT-DETR iterations (RT-DETRv2/v3), the gains are 1.3–2.4 AP, confirming that the DSI+GAM distillation framework provides consistent improvements regardless of the base DETR architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RT-DETR/DEIM lineage of real-time DETR detectors by introducing a training-only distillation framework that injects semantics from a frozen Vision Foundation Model (DINOv3) into the AIFI module's output. It departs from prior knowledge distillation for detection (FGD, DETRDistill, CrossKD), which rely on task-specific teacher detectors, by instead using a general-purpose VFM as an external semantic prior. The Information Bottleneck principle motivates targeting the deepest transformer feature (F5) specifically, since it is the only feature receiving self-attention and thus the principal carrier of global semantics in the hybrid encoder. The Gradient-guided Adaptive Modulation strategy addresses the optimization instability that arises from the architectural and objective gap between ViT-based VFMs and CNN-hybrid detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-inference-overhead property makes this framework immediately deployable in industrial real-time pipelines, and its generality means any future VFM advance can be absorbed without architectural changes. However, evaluation is limited to COCO val, leaving open whether the gains transfer to more challenging or domain-specific benchmarks (e.g., autonomous driving, medical imaging). The paper also does not explore whether the approach extends to segmentation or other dense prediction tasks, nor does it analyze the sensitivity to VFM scale (only ViT-Base is tested). A key open question is whether the semantic bottleneck diagnosis generalizes to non-DETR architectures where the deepest feature is not uniquely identifiable.
