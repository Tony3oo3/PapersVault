## Problem

Existing prototypical part-based interpretable classifiers (ProtoPNet, PIP-Net, LucidPPN, etc.) rely on multi-stage training pipelines, multiple auxiliary regularization losses to prevent prototype collapse, and sometimes external part-discovery models. This structural complexity stems from the 'moving target' problem: learnable prototypes in a shifting latent space make it difficult to simultaneously learn discriminative features and align prototypes, requiring careful hyperparameter balancing across competing objectives.

## Input / Output

OPAL takes standard RGB images resized to 224×224 (with TrivialAugment) and produces two outputs: a class label and m=5 localized evidence patches per predicted class. Each evidence patch is a single spatial maximizer from the feature map, linearly rescaled back to input resolution, providing a direct, traceable link between the model's computation and the specific image region driving the prediction. No part-level annotations, segmentation masks, or bounding-box supervision are required—only image-level class labels.

## Method

OPAL replaces unconstrained prototype learning with a fixed geometric structure: each class c is assigned a dedicated orthonormal subspace S_c in R^K (K=C·m), with m canonical unit-norm prototype directions and a single class anchor a_c defined as the normalized block-uniform vector. A 1×1 convolution projects backbone features to K channels, then channel-wise softmax enforces spatial competition (channels sum to 1 at every location), and global max pooling selects a single spatial maximizer per channel, tying each prototype coordinate to one localized image region. Classification is reformulated as distance-based alignment to the fixed anchors using Euclidean distance on L2-normalized embeddings (equivalent to cosine similarity), trained end-to-end with standard cross-entropy in a single stage. The combination of orthonormal anchoring and competitive aggregation is shown to be sufficient to induce semantic sparsity and prevent prototype collapse without any auxiliary separation, clustering, or diversity losses.

## Results

On five fine-grained benchmarks (CUB, CARS, PETS, DOGS, FLOWER), OPAL with ConvNeXt-Tiny achieves top-1 accuracy of 85.6%, 90.3%, 92.8%, 86.9%, and 96.1% respectively, surpassing the non-interpretable CNN baseline on all five datasets with an average gain of +1.56 percentage points. OPAL attains the best interpretable-model accuracy on 4 of 5 datasets, ranking only behind TesNet (92.6%) and LucidPPN (91.6%) on CARS. Across 45 backbone-scale experiments (ResNet-50/101/152, EfficientNetV2-S/M/L, ConvNeXt-T/S/B), OPAL improves or matches the corresponding CNN in 39 cases with a mean gain of +2.94 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPAL outperforms all eight interpretable baselines on at least 4 of 5 datasets. Against the strongest competitors, OPAL beats TesNet on CUB (+1.0), PETS (+4.1), DOGS (+6.2), and FLOWER (+12.7) but trails it on CARS (90.3 vs. 92.6). Versus LucidPPN, OPAL wins on CUB (+4.1), PETS (+0.7), DOGS (+7.4), and FLOWER (+1.1) but loses on CARS (90.3 vs. 91.6). Against PIP-Net, OPAL leads on every dataset, with the largest margins on DOGS (+6.1) and CARS (+2.1). The gap over earlier methods (ProtoPNet, ProtoTree, ProtoPShare, ProtoPool) is substantial, with OPAL exceeding ProtoPNet by 6.4 pp on average and ProtoPool by 5.5 pp on average across the five benchmarks. Notably, OPAL is the only method that consistently surpasses the non-interpretable ConvNeXt-Tiny backbone on all five datasets, whereas most baselines underperform the plain CNN on at least one benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPAL sits within the prototypical part-based network lineage initiated by ProtoPNet but fundamentally departs from its core assumption that prototypes must be learned as free parameters in an unconstrained latent space. Rather than discovering prototype locations through multi-stage optimization with auxiliary separation, clustering, and diversity losses, OPAL fixes the output geometry a priori by assigning each class to a predefined orthonormal subspace, drawing on insights from proxy-based metric learning and neural collapse theory. The spatial competition mechanism (channel-wise softmax + global max pooling) also departs from the soft attention maps and concentration losses used by prior interpretable methods, replacing them with a hard, winner-takes-all aggregation that requires no additional supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that OPAL reduces interpretable fine-grained classification to a single-stage, standard cross-entropy training loop, removing the multi-stage pipelines and hyperparameter-sensitive auxiliary losses that have made prior prototype-based methods cumbersome to adopt. The ablation evidence that orthonormal anchoring alone (without CWS or GMP) already recovers most of the gain, and that the full combination consistently beats the non-interpretable baseline, suggests the field may have over-engineered the regularization needed to prevent prototype collapse. Key open questions include extending the framework to vision transformers (where spatial locality is not inductive), adapting the fixed per-class prototype count to variable class complexity, and exploring controlled cross-class prototype sharing for recurring attributes.
