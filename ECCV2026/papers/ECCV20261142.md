## Problem

Autoregressive video diffusion models suffer from unbounded error accumulation (exposure bias) that degrades visual quality and camera controllability over long trajectories, while existing mitigations like Diffusion Forcing and Self Forcing only address local drift without explicitly bounding error propagation. The paper tackles the gap of generating high-fidelity, camera-controlled videos spanning 30+ seconds without the compounding drift that causes purely autoregressive methods to lose trajectory adherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261142/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single initial RGB frame, a sequence of 6-DoF camera poses (reconstructed via π3 every 0.5 s with slerp/lerp interpolation), and a text caption as conditioning signals, and produces a dense video sequence up to 32 seconds at 10 fps (480×832 resolution based on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B). Internally, the pipeline first generates 21 sparse keyframes treated as independent images (no temporal VAE compression) at a fixed stride of 8 frames, then fills in dense segments of variable length using a sliding-window autoregressive rollout with 1-frame overlap between segments.

## Method

DCARL decomposes long-video synthesis into two specialized DiT-based flow-matching generators: a Keyframe Generator that jointly denoises all sparse keyframes in a single pass (bypassing sequential error propagation) with spatial-structural preservation via image-level encoding, and an Interpolation Generator that synthesizes dense segments autoregressively, conditioned on look-back/look-ahead keyframes and one clean preceding frame for local coherence. Two key mechanisms address interpolation failure modes: Motion-Inductive Noisy Conditioning perturbs keyframe latents with calibrated noise (α_c=0.7, σ_c=0.3) during both training and inference to prevent the model from copying keyframe pixels instead of learning motion dynamics, and Seamless Boundary Consistency replaces the first p latents of each segment with the preceding segment's clean latents at every denoising step to eliminate junction fractures. A perturbation-analysis proposition formalizes why the divide-and-conquer scheme bounds cumulative error (convex combination of keyframe errors plus a local constant) versus the linear/exponential growth of purely autoregressive rollout.

## Results

On the ODV-YT dataset (32-second videos), DCARL achieves an overall FID of 19.2 and FVD of 203.7, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline SEVA (FID 22.2, FVD 548.0) and all autoregressive methods. For camera trajectory adherence, DCARL reports ATE 0.237 and ARE 7.669°, the best among all methods. On the zero-shot nuScenes evaluation (16-second videos), DCARL maintains FID 19.6, FVD 225.4, ATE 0.045, and ARE 5.274°, again leading all baselines without any fine-tuning on the target domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCARL outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric (FID, FVD, ATE, ARE) in both the in-distribution ODV-YT and zero-shot nuScenes evaluations. Against the closest competitor SEVA (a keyframe-interpolation method), DCARL reduces FVD from 548.0 to 203.7 (−63%) and ATE from 0.294 to 0.237 (−19%) on ODV-YT, while on nuScenes the FVD gap widens to 487.9 vs. 225.4 and ATE drops from 0.117 to 0.045. The purely autoregressive baselines (DiffF, SelfF, DeepF) show severe degradation over time: their per-segment FVD roughly triples or quadruples from the first 8 s to the last 8 s, whereas DCARL's per-segment FVD increases only modestly (191.4 → 313.8). Vista, a driving world model, exhibits the worst long-horizon drift (FVD 3774.5 in the final segment) and the highest ATE (0.641), confirming that iterative autoregressive rollout without structural anchoring is the primary failure mode. The zero-shot nuScenes results further demonstrate that DCARL's divide-and-conquer design generalizes to unseen driving scenes without fine-tuning, maintaining ATE 0.045 versus 0.092–0.195 for all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCARL sits at the intersection of two established lines: autoregressive video diffusion (Diffusion Forcing, Self Forcing, Deep Forcing, Vista) and keyframe-interpolation generation (NUWA-XL, SEVA). It departs from purely autoregressive methods by introducing a dedicated keyframe generator that jointly synthesizes sparse structural anchors across the full timeline, thereby bypassing sequential error propagation. It also departs from prior keyframe-interpolation work (which typically uses a single coupled generator for both global and local synthesis) by explicitly decoupling the two stages into separate DiT-based flow-matching models, each trained without temporal compression for the keyframe stage. The Motion-Inductive Noisy Conditioning and Seamless Boundary Consistency modules address specific failure modes (keyframe duplication, segment-boundary fractures) that prior interpolation-based methods do not explicitly handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Stable, camera-controllable video generation over 32-second horizons is a prerequisite for practical world models in autonomous driving and terrain reconstruction, and DCARL demonstrates that divide-and-conquer keyframe anchoring can bound the drift that has plagued autoregressive rollouts. The strong zero-shot transfer to nuScenes (ATE 0.045 vs. 0.09–0.20 for baselines) suggests the structural-anchoring principle generalizes beyond the training distribution. Open questions include whether the dual-generator design can be unified into a single latent-space model, whether richer control priors (semantic maps, LiDAR, 3D layouts) can be integrated without retraining, and whether the 32-second horizon can be extended further without degrading the keyframe-to-interpolation fidelity ratio.
