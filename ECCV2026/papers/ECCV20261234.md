## Problem

Medical LVLMs suffer from modality bias where predictions are driven by spurious background correlations (scanner signatures, co-occurring pathologies) rather than lesion evidence, producing hallucinated or clinically irrelevant outputs. Existing DPO-based alignment methods inherit these dataset biases because they lack an explicit mechanism to disentangle harmful direct background effects from the valuable indirect lesion-background dependencies that are essential for accurate diagnosis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261234/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes composite image pairs constructed by stitching a background-only image (lesion region zeroed out via Med-SAM segmentation) with either the original image (factual condition) or a Gaussian-noise-perturbed version (counterfactual condition), paired with a clinical query that instructs the model both images belong to the same patient. At inference, the model receives a standard single medical image and clinical query, producing either a VQA answer or a free-text radiology report. The backbone is LLaVA-Med-1.5-mistral-7B with LoRA (rank 128, scaling 256) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

## Method

CoMedPO formulates a structural causal model where the medical image decomposes into background B and lesion L, both feeding into an ensemble embedding E that determines the reward R. The key insight is decomposing the total causal effect of B on R into a harmful Natural Direct Effect (B→R shortcut) and a valuable Total Indirect Effect (B→E→R capturing clinically meaningful spatial/anatomical context). The counterfactual DPO loss (Eq. 12) computes a causal reward contrast by subtracting the log-likelihood ratio under the counterfactual composite input from that under the factual composite input, for both preferred and dispreferred responses, thereby canceling the direct background bias symmetrically. The total objective combines this counterfactual loss with standard DPO on original single-image preference pairs (weighted by λ=1.0), preserving standard alignment while injecting causal debiasing. A convergence theorem guarantees that optimizing the TIE reward drives the policy toward a causal-optimal solution even when the SFT reference model carries background bias.

## Results

On the SFT-enhanced setting, CoMedPO achieves 74.40% closed-ended accuracy on SLAKE, 74.90% on VQA-RAD, 34.13 ROUGE-L on IU-XRay, and 14.58 ROUGE-L on MIMIC-CXR, setting new state-of-the-art across all four benchmarks. The largest gains appear on closed-ended VQA tasks (e.g., +6.58 points over MMedPO on SLAKE-Closed) and report generation (e.g., +4.00 ROUGE-L over MMedPO on IU-XRay). CoMedPO also demonstrates superior cross-dataset generalization, with only a 2.39-point drop on VQA-RAD Closed when trained on SLAKE, compared to 5.58 for MMedPO and 3.98 for DPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMedPO outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric in both the standard and SFT-enhanced settings. Against the closest competitor MMedPO (SFT), CoMedPO gains +6.58 on SLAKE-Closed (74.40 vs. 67.82), +7.17 on VQA-RAD-Closed (74.90 vs. 67.73), +4.00 ROUGE-L on IU-XRay (34.13 vs. 30.13), and +1.36 ROUGE-L on MIMIC-CXR (14.58 vs. 13.22). Against SPPO (SFT), the strongest VQA baseline, CoMedPO leads by +7.41 on SLAKE-Closed and +1.59 on VQA-RAD-Closed, while also surpassing it on all report-generation metrics. Against DPO (SFT), gains are even larger: +10.17 on SLAKE-Closed and +9.96 on VQA-RAD-Closed. Notably, CoMedPO also reduces background shortcut sensitivity (53.78% vs. 65.34% on VQA-RAD-Closed under background-only input), confirming that its gains come from genuine lesion-based reasoning rather than exploiting spurious correlations.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMedPO extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) by replacing the standard reward with a causally decomposed one, and builds directly on MMedPO (Zhu et al., 2025) as its medical preference-optimization base. The key departure from MMedPO is the introduction of a structural causal model that explicitly separates the harmful direct effect of background on reward (NDE) from the clinically valuable indirect effect of lesion-background interaction (TIE), then derives a counterfactual DPO loss from this decomposition. This connects the medical alignment literature to Pearl's causal mediation framework, a bridge that prior medical preference-optimization work (which relied on statistical weighting or noise perturbation) had not made.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal decomposition offers a principled, model-agnostic mechanism for reducing hallucination in medical LVLMs without discarding useful anatomical context, which is critical as these models move toward clinical deployment. The empirical validation—CoMedPO performing *worse* on background-only inputs while improving on full inputs and cross-dataset transfer—provides direct evidence that the shortcut is genuinely removed rather than merely masked. Open questions include whether the TIE reward can be computed without explicit lesion segmentation (currently relying on Med-SAM), whether the Gaussian-noise counterfactual is a faithful intervention, and whether the framework extends to 3D volumetric imaging or multi-lesion scenarios where the causal graph becomes more complex.
