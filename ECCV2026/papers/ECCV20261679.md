## Problem

Existing multimodal human-sensing fusion methods assume a fixed sensor configuration and are prone to modality collapse, where optimisation disproportionately relies on the strongest modality and suppresses weaker sensors. No prior framework supports a single trained model that performs 3D pose estimation or activity recognition across all 31 non-empty subsets of five sensing modalities without retraining, leaving a gap in robustness under realistic missing-modality conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261679/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts any non-empty subset of five synchronised modalities—WiFi CSI (3×114×10 tensor at 10 Hz), depth image (1×224×224), LiDAR point cloud (1024×3), mmWave point cloud, and RGB image (3×224×224)—with missing modalities simply omitted from the token sequence rather than represented by placeholder tokens. For HPE it outputs 17 3D joint positions (J×3 coordinates); for HAR it outputs a class logit vector over C action classes. A single set of task-specific parameters handles all 31 subset configurations at inference without any retraining or architectural modification.

## Method

LEO-Fuse freezes modality-specific unimodal encoders (ResNet-18, PointTransformer, DGCNN, 1D CNN), maps their latents into a shared 512-D space via per-modality adapters, and fuses the available modality tokens with a mask-aware transformer encoder that mean-pools over a variable-length token set. The core novelty is a task-specific Local Expert Orchestration (LEO) head that performs dense local modality routing: for HPE it computes per-joint softmax gates over the available modality tokens (reflecting that, e.g., mmWave captures torso geometry well but fails on extremities), while for HAR it routes at the class-logit level. Training combines a structured K-based subset sampling distribution, soft-target gate supervision derived from per-joint per-modality prediction errors, unimodal deep supervision on each modality branch, and an EMA-based mode-aware loss balancer that gently up-weights high-loss subsets. This design yields an implicit cross-modal regularisation effect, improving LiDAR singleton HPE by 35.6% and mmWave by 11.5% over dedicated unimodal baselines.

## Results

On MM-Fi, LEO-Fuse achieves 75.5 mm average MPJPE and 45.7 mm PA-MPJPE across all 31 non-empty modality subsets, outperforming X-Fi (103.7 mm / 58.2 mm) by 27.2% and 21.3% respectively. For HAR with HAR-trained encoders, the 31-subset average top-1 accuracy reaches 95.7% (98.4% on the 15 subsets comparable to X-Fi's 62.5%). On XRF55, LEO-Fuse attains up to 95.5% HAR accuracy across 7 RF subsets, winning 6 of 7. The best single HPE configuration is D+L+R+I at 57.2 mm MPJPE, slightly beating the full five-modality set (58.8 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only directly comparable baseline is X-Fi [3], the sole other method evaluating all 31 MM-Fi subsets under the same protocol. LEO-Fuse outperforms X-Fi on 30 of 31 subsets in MPJPE (75.5 vs. 103.7 mm average) and 29 of 31 in PA-MPJPE, with the two losses occurring on the W+R pair (156.0 vs. 117.2 mm) and WiFi-only PA-MPJPE (108.4 vs. 105.3 mm). The largest single-subset gains are on LiDAR-including configurations (+53.4% on singleton L, +44.0% on W+L), confirming that frozen encoders with per-joint gating exploit spatial structure more effectively than X-Fi's iterative cross-attention. For HAR, LEO-Fuse (HAR-trained encoders) achieves 98.4% on the 15 subsets comparable to X-Fi's 62.5%, a 35.9-point margin. The remaining baselines (mmDiff 65.3 mm, mmSkeleton 71.0 mm, WiLHPE 151.8 mm, VST-Pose 169.7 mm, Balanced MM 51.2 mm, STPM 95.69%, ActionFi 99.08%, RFusion ~90.1%) use fixed modality sets and different protocols (Protocol 1, 5-fold CV, or unspecified splits), so they are not directly comparable to LEO-Fuse's 31-subset average; they serve as contextual reference points for unimodal or fixed-pair performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEO-Fuse carves new ground at the intersection of modality-invariant sensing (X-Fi [3]) and mixture-of-experts routing (MoE3D [20], PoseMoE [21]), repurposing the MoE gating idea as per-joint modality reliability modelling rather than expert selection. It departs from X-Fi's iterative cross-attention and Binomial dropout by freezing unimodal encoders, omitting absent modalities at the fusion input (rather than using placeholder tokens [26]), and introducing a mask-aware transformer with joint-wise local routing. The training strategy—weighted subset sampling, soft-target gate supervision from per-modality errors, and EMA-based mode-aware loss balancing—addresses modality collapse [1, 16] without the manual per-modality probability tuning that X-Fi requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a single trained model handles all 31 sensor subsets without retraining, which is directly relevant to real deployments where sensors fail intermittently. The cross-modal regularisation effect—where subset-invariant training *improves* weak-modality singletons (LiDAR +35.6%, mmWave +11.5%) over dedicated unimodal baselines—suggests a useful inductive bias for multi-sensor systems. Key limitations include the inability to handle zero-shot or continual learning settings, the W+R pair degradation when no geometric anchor is present, and the requirement for fully supervised training per dataset. Open questions include whether the local routing generalises to more than five modalities or to multi-person settings, and whether the frozen-encoder assumption holds when encoders must be jointly fine-tuned for new domains.
