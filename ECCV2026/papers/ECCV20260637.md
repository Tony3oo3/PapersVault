## Problem

Continual Test-Time Adaptation (CTTA) must adapt a model online to a sequence of evolving target domains without labels, but purely source-free methods suffer from compounding self-training errors and catastrophic forgetting under long-term distribution shift. Retaining the full source dataset is often infeasible due to privacy or licensing constraints, yet discarding all source information leaves the model without a persistent reference to ground its representations. The paper asks whether a compact, privacy-conscious summary of the source distribution—specifically one produced by Dataset Distillation—can serve as a stable anchor that plugs into existing CTTA algorithms to prevent drift.

## Input / Output

At test time, the method consumes a stream of unlabeled RGB target images (e.g., 5,000 per corruption type on ImageNet-C, or 7.5 million images on CCC) alongside a pre-computed set of source-distilled anchors (N_a synthetic images, each paired with a soft label and a latent feature vector, with IPC as low as 1). Each incoming target sample is individually matched to its nearest anchor via cosine similarity in the feature extractor's latent space, so the anchor set is consumed in a context-aware, per-sample manner rather than as a uniform replay buffer. The output is a class prediction per target sample plus updated model parameters that balance target adaptation against source preservation.

## Method

DO-ALL operates in two phases. Before deployment, Dataset Distillation compresses the source dataset into a tiny set of synthetic anchors, each storing a synthetic image, its soft label under the source model, and its latent feature. During CTTA, each target sample is matched to its most semantically aligned anchor, and three complementary objectives regularize the update: (1) a direct replay loss enforcing KL consistency between the model's predictions on anchors and their stored soft labels, (2) a MixUp-style manifold-smoothing loss that interpolates each target with its matched anchor, and (3) a layer-wise MMD loss aligning evolving target features with anchor features in the latent space. A harm-adaptive blending mechanism then selectively reverts parameter groups whose gradient-based harm scores exceed a threshold back toward the source initialization, preventing accumulated drift over long streams. The framework is plug-and-play: it adds these anchor-guided losses on top of any existing CTTA objective (EATA, RMT, ROID, etc.) without modifying the base architecture or adaptation strategy, and the distilled anchors can be generated with any DD method or even a different backbone than the CTTA model.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (severity 5, online CTTA), DO-ALL consistently reduces average classification error across all tested baselines: ROID+DO-ALL achieves 53.6% error (vs. 54.5% for ROID alone), ASR+DO-ALL reaches 53.7% (vs. 54.3%), EATA+DO-ALL drops to 56.6% (vs. 58.0%), and RMT+DO-ALL improves to 57.4% (vs. 59.8%). On the long-horizon CCC benchmark, ROID+DO-ALL improves average accuracy from 33.6% to 34.7%, with the largest gain in the hardest setting (13.2% → 15.5%). The best overall result is ROID+DO-ALL at 53.6% average error on ImageNet-C, outperforming the strongest standalone baseline (ASR at 54.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DO-ALL is a plug-and-play module that improves every CTTA baseline it is paired with. On ImageNet-C (severity 5), ROID+DO-ALL (53.6%) beats the best standalone baselines ASR (54.3%) and ROID (54.5%), while EATA+DO-ALL (56.6%) and RMT+DO-ALL (57.4%) each improve their respective baselines by 1.4 and 2.4 percentage points. On the CCC benchmark, ROID+DO-ALL (34.7% avg accuracy) surpasses ROID (33.6%), TCA (32.9%), RDumb (32.6%), and EATA (30.8%), with the most pronounced gain in the Hard setting (15.5% vs. 13.2%). The method does not replace any baseline's adaptation objective; it adds anchor-guided replay, MMD alignment, and harm-adaptive blending on top, so the gains are complementary rather than competitive. Across diverse DD backends (WMDD, DELT, SRe2L) and cross-architecture settings (RN18→RN50, ViT→CNN), DO-ALL maintains consistent improvements, confirming that the benefit is not tied to a specific distillation algorithm or backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Dataset Distillation (DD), a technique originally developed for compressing training sets, as a compact source-reference mechanism within Continual Test-Time Adaptation (CTTA). Prior CTTA methods that retain source information (e.g., RMT's feature prototypes, CoTTA's stochastic parameter restoration, or source-statistics-based approaches) store either raw features or aggregate statistics; DO-ALL instead stores a small set of synthetic images with soft labels and latent features, each optimized to preserve class-discriminative structure. The framework is explicitly plug-and-play: it does not alter the adaptation objective or architecture of existing CTTA algorithms (EATA, RMT, ROID) but adds anchor-guided replay, MixUp-style manifold smoothing, MMD alignment, and harm-adaptive blending as complementary regularization terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DO-ALL addresses a practical deployment constraint—source data cannot be retained due to privacy or licensing—while providing a persistent anti-forgetting anchor that purely source-free CTTA methods lack. The observed positive correlation between distilled-anchor quality (measured by standalone validation accuracy) and CTTA improvement suggests that future advances in DD will automatically translate into stronger CTTA stabilization, creating a productive feedback loop between the two subfields. Open questions remain around abrupt or adversarial distribution shifts, optimal anchor selection under highly non-stationary streams, and whether the modest 1–2% error reductions observed here scale to more extreme or multi-modal settings. The cross-architecture transferability (e.g., RN18-distilled anchors applied to RN50 CTTA) is practically valuable but leaves open how well this holds for fundamentally different representation spaces at larger scale.
