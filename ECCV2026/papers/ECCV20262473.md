## Problem

Higher-order ODE solvers are widely assumed to improve sample quality at a fixed number of function evaluations (NFE) in diffusion and flow-based generative models, yet practitioners consistently observe that second- and fourth-order methods can underperform first-order Euler in the 10–20 NFE regime. This degradation lacks a principled explanation grounded in numerical analysis: prior convergence theories treat learning error and discretization error additively, and existing backward error analyses of diffusion ODEs assume access to an exact vector field, so the multiplicative coupling between step size and derivatives of the learning error goes unmodeled.

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on pretrained generative models (EDM diffusion and Rectified Flow) without retraining. It consumes a noise sample, the learned velocity field f̂, and a total NFE budget N; under the equal-NFE convention, Euler takes N steps of size T/N while Heun takes N/2 steps of 2T/N and RK4 takes N/4 steps of 4T/N. The adaptive solver estimates a local roughness score from already-computed velocities via finite differences, incurring zero additional function evaluations, and outputs a single generated RGB image (32×32 CIFAR-10, 64×64 CelebA-HQ, or 256×256 ImageNet) whose quality is measured by FID-10k.

## Method

The core mechanism is a backward error analysis applied to the decomposed learned field f̂ = f + ε, where f is a smooth target and ε is structured learning error that grows off the data manifold. Substituting this decomposition into the modified equations of Euler, Heun, and RK4 exposes interaction terms of the form h^k ∇^k ε that are absent from classical BEA and can dominate the standard truncation error B·h^{p+1} at practical step sizes, causing a nominally order-p solver to behave as lower order (pre-asymptotic order collapse). From these terms the paper derives an explicit crossover step size h* depending on the ratio of first- to higher-order derivative norms of ε, and a roughness score R = h/h* computable at zero extra NFE from stored trajectory velocities. An adaptive sampler (Algorithm 1) uses R to switch between Euler and higher-order steps on the fly, preserving strict equal-NFE accounting. The theory is validated on toy manifolds and pretrained EDM/Rectified Flow checkpoints on CIFAR-10, CelebA-HQ, and ImageNet, confirming the predicted Euler → Heun → RK4 hierarchy and the crossover thresholds.

## Results

On Rectified Flow (CIFAR-10), the adaptive solver achieves FID 8.01 at NFE 32, surpassing the best static method (Euler at NFE 64, FID 8.16) and outperforming all fixed-order solvers at the same budget (Euler 9.54, Heun 9.70, RK4 12.92). On EDM (ImageNet), the full three-way hierarchy is confirmed: Euler dominates at NFE 4–8, Heun leads at NFE 16 (FID 7.27), and RK4 surpasses Heun at NFE 64 (FID 5.76 vs. 5.77), with the adaptive solver matching the best static baseline at NFE 32 and 64 (FID 5.79, 5.76). At low NFE (4–8), higher-order solvers degrade severely (e.g., RK4 FID 378.49 vs. Euler 55.97 on RectFlow CIFAR-10 at NFE 4), confirming the predicted pre-asymptotic order collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed roughness-adaptive solver against three fixed-order baselines—Euler (1st order), Heun (2nd order), and RK4 (4th order)—across four model/dataset settings. On Rectified Flow (CIFAR-10), the adaptive solver wins at every NFE budget from 8 to 64, achieving FID 7.31 at NFE 64 versus 8.16 (Euler), 8.19 (Heun), and 10.09 (RK4). On Rectified Flow (CelebA-HQ), it wins at NFE 8, 16, and 32 (e.g., FID 35.25 vs. 42.55 for Euler at NFE 16) but is slightly behind Heun at NFE 64 (12.68 vs. 12.26). On EDM (CIFAR-10), the adaptive solver ties the best static method at NFE 4, 32, and 64 but underperforms at NFE 8 and 16, where the roughness estimator appears to over-select lower-order steps. On EDM (ImageNet), it ties Euler at NFE 4–8, loses to Heun at NFE 16 (11.41 vs. 7.27), and matches or beats the best static solver at NFE 32 and 64. Overall, the adaptive solver matches or improves upon the best static baseline at the majority of NFE budgets, with the largest gains in the low-to-moderate NFE regime (8–32) where fixed higher-order solvers suffer most from pre-asymptotic order collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classical backward error analysis (BEA) from Hairer et al. and its recent application to diffusion ODE samplers by Gao et al. by decomposing the learned vector field as f̂ = f + ε, revealing interaction terms h^k ∇^k ε that are absent when the field is assumed exact. It departs from prior convergence analyses (Chen et al., Lee et al., De Bortoli) that treat learning and discretization errors additively, instead identifying multiplicative coupling that produces a new phenomenon—pre-asymptotic order collapse. The adaptive solver (Algorithm 1) is orthogonal to prior schedule-optimization work (Align Your Steps, LD3) and embedded-error estimators (Kong et al.), operating on solver-order selection rather than step placement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled theoretical explanation for a widely observed but unexplained empirical phenomenon: higher-order ODE solvers systematically underperform Euler at practical NFE budgets (10–20) in diffusion and flow models. The derived crossover step size h* and zero-extra-NFE roughness diagnostic offer a concrete, model-agnostic criterion for solver selection that practitioners can adopt without retraining. Key limitations include the ODE-only scope (SDE samplers with injected noise may counteract off-manifold drift), the reliance on a manifold assumption for analytical tractability, and the fact that the theory bounds trajectory distance rather than FID directly. Open questions include extending the BEA framework to stochastic modified equations and combining order-adaptation with non-uniform time-step schedules.
