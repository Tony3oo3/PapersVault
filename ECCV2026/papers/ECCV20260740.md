## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based hand avatar methods deform Gaussian primitives through pose-driven transformations in Euclidean space, lacking an explicit structural association with the underlying skeleton. This makes it difficult to preserve fine-grained details such as fingernails and clear finger separations under complex articulations where fingers interact closely and self-occlude. The paper addresses this gap by asking how to give each Gaussian an interpretable, skeleton-relative structural identity that remains consistent across poses.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes multi-view RGB video sequences (5 FPS) from InterHand2.6M along with per-frame MANO pose parameters θ and shape parameters β; the hand is initialized from the 778-vertex MANO mesh with random color and opacity. At inference, given a novel pose (θ, β) and camera parameters, the model renders photorealistic hand images in real time on a single A100 GPU. For novel-view evaluation, models are trained on 10 views and tested on 15 held-out views; for novel-pose evaluation, the last 50 poses are held out.

## Method

The core contribution is the Structural Coordinate Space (SCS), which reparameterizes each Gaussian by its geometric relationship to a hybrid bone basis. The basis combines static bones (MANO topology augmented with cross-finger pseudo-bones, deformed via LBS in posed space) with virtual bones (learned in canonical space via attention over static bones with interpolation factors and pose-dependent offsets). Each Gaussian's structural coordinate P is computed as the concatenation of per-bone products of radial distance (exponential decay for static bones, raw distance for virtual bones) and angular cosine similarity. Intra-pose consistency is enforced by feeding P together with per-Gaussian geometry and appearance embeddings into MLP decoders that produce non-rigid attribute offsets (position, covariance, color). Inter-pose consistency is enforced by matching Gaussians across frames through their SCS coordinates and penalizing attribute divergence, weighted by a pose-similarity factor, which regularizes deformation to be coherent for structurally corresponding Gaussians.

## Results

On the InterHand2.6M dataset, HandSCS achieves state-of-the-art novel pose synthesis with average gains of +1.24 dB PSNR, +0.008 SSIM, and a 33.4% relative LPIPS reduction over the strongest baseline (GauHuman) across five subjects. For sparse-view novel view synthesis (10 training views, 15 test views), HandSCS improves PSNR by +3.78 dB and SSIM by +0.017 over GauHuman, while reducing LPIPS by 37% relatively. The method maintains real-time rendering speed on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HandSCS outperforms all four baselines on every metric in both novel pose and novel view synthesis. Against the strongest 3DGS-based baseline GauHuman [8], HandSCS gains +1.24 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS by 33.4% on novel pose, and +3.78 dB PSNR with 37% LPIPS reduction on novel view. Against 3DGS-Avatar [36], the gains are comparable in magnitude, confirming that the structural coordinate space provides a meaningful advantage over pose-conditioned MLP offsets alone. Against the NeRF-based HandAvatar [2], HandSCS leads by a wider margin (e.g., +1.55 dB PSNR on test/Capture0), reflecting both the structural modeling benefit and the rendering efficiency of Gaussians. The only exception is LiveHand [30], which achieves slightly higher SSIM on two subjects in the sparse-view novel view task due to NeRF's smooth interpolation across viewpoints; however, HandSCS still leads on PSNR and LPIPS in that setting and dominates on all novel pose metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS-based avatar line (3DGS-Avatar, GauHuman, Human Gaussian Splatting) by introducing a skeleton-relative Structural Coordinate Space (SCS) that reparameterizes each Gaussian primitive through its geometric relationship to the hand skeleton, rather than treating Gaussians as purely spatial entities deformed by LBS or pose-conditioned MLPs. It builds on the MANO kinematic topology for the static bone basis and on per-Gaussian embeddings (Bae et al., ECCV 2024) for local appearance, but departs from prior work by adding pose-adaptive virtual bones and a distance-rotation descriptor that gives every Gaussian an explicit, interpretable structural identity. The inter-pose consistency loss, which matches Gaussians across frames via their SCS coordinates, is a new mechanism not present in prior 3DGS avatar methods that rely solely on shared canonical-space parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SCS formulation offers a generalizable principle—assigning each rendering primitive an explicit structural coordinate relative to an articulated skeleton—that could transfer to full-body or multi-joint avatars beyond hands, where self-occlusion and inter-part contact are equally challenging. However, the method is tightly coupled to MANO's 15-joint topology and has been validated only on the InterHand2.6M dataset with a single hand per subject, leaving open whether the virtual-bone mechanism scales to denser skeletons or to subjects with atypical hand shapes. The reliance on per-frame structural-coordinate matching for the inter-pose loss also raises questions about computational cost at longer sequences or with more Gaussians.
