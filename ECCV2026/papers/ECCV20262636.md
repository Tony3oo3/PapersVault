## Problem

Explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning improves text-to-motion generation quality but introduces significant inference latency from sequentially generating reasoning tokens. Prior latent reasoning work targets language-only tasks with fixed-length reasoning and verifiable answers, leaving open how to compress variable-length (1–9 step) spatio-temporal motion reasoning into efficient continuous representations without sacrificing quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262636/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural-language motion description as input and produces a sequence of 10–50 discrete motion tokens (from a 512-entry VQ-VAE codebook with temporal downsampling factor 4) that decode to a 22-joint, 263-dimensional 3D motion sequence. At inference, the model processes a fixed-length latent region (K_max × c = 16 latent tokens) via multi-pass forward before autoregressively decoding motion tokens, generating no explicit reasoning text. The entire pipeline is cast as conditional language modeling on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct with an extended vocabulary of 512 motion tokens plus boundary and latent markers.

## Method

LaCT-Motion uses a three-stage practice-to-mastery recipe: (i) Practice trains explicit CoT motion generation via SFT; (ii) Internalization progressively replaces the first k reasoning steps with k×c continuous latent tokens through a three-phase curriculum (explicit → partial → fully latent) with stochastic stage sampling, using a multi-pass forward mechanism that iteratively overwrites each latent token's embedding with the preceding hidden state to form a differentiable recurrence; (iii) Perfection refines the fully latent policy with GRPO using format, motion-similarity, and semantic-similarity rewards. The key architectural insight is that latent tokens serve as placeholder slots whose embeddings are replaced by contextualized hidden states during forward passes, enabling the model to perform multi-step spatio-temporal reasoning entirely in continuous space. At inference, the multi-pass KV cache is directly reused for autoregressive motion decoding, eliminating the sequential generation cost of explicit CoT while retaining structured intermediate computation.

## Results

On HumanML3D, LaCT-Motion achieves the best R-Precision across all top-k settings (Top-1: 0.577, Top-2: 0.783, Top-3: 0.873) and the lowest MM-Dist (2.506) among all compared methods, indicating the strongest text-motion alignment. Its FID of 0.167 is competitive but not the lowest (TBYM: 0.040, MoMask: 0.045), reflecting a trade-off between alignment quality and distributional realism. All results are obtained under the fully latent regime with no explicit reasoning tokens at inference, completing full test-set evaluation in ~7 minutes versus ~1 hour for explicit CoT methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaCT-Motion outperforms all 16 baselines on R-Precision (all top-k) and MM-Dist. Against MotionGPT-3 [55], the strongest prior method on R-Precision, it improves Top-1 by +0.035 (6.5% relative) and reduces MM-Dist by 0.262 (9.5% relative). Against Motion-R1 [24], which uses explicit CoT + GRPO, LaCT-Motion gains +0.062 on Top-1 (12.0% relative), demonstrating that latent internalization surpasses explicit reasoning. Against UniMo [38] (explicit CoT + SFT), the gain is +0.038 on Top-1 (7.1% relative). Against the concurrent latent-reasoning method TBYM [28], LaCT-Motion improves Top-1 by +0.040 and MM-Dist by 0.389, though TBYM achieves a substantially lower FID (0.040 vs. 0.167), reflecting its motion-native latent space versus LaCT-Motion's LLM-internalized linguistic CoT. On FID, LaCT-Motion trails MoMask (0.045) and TBYM (0.040) but remains competitive with T2M-GPT (0.116) and MotionGPT-3 (0.065) in the same order of magnitude.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent-reasoning paradigm from language models (CoDi's self-distillation of explicit CoT into continuous space) to the multimodal domain of text-to-motion generation, where reasoning steps are variable-length (1–9 atomic steps) and encode spatio-temporal semantics absent from prior latent-reasoning work. It builds directly on Motion-R1's explicit-CoT-plus-GRPO recipe for motion generation, but replaces the explicit reasoning chain with a curriculum-compressed latent representation and a multi-pass hidden-state injection mechanism. The GRPO reinforcement stage is adapted from DeepSeek-V3's value-function-free policy optimization, extended here to operate without access to discrete reasoning tokens by using verifiable motion-level rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that continuous latent tokens surpass explicit CoT in text-motion alignment (R-Precision Top-1: 0.577 vs. 0.515) challenges the working assumption that explicit step-by-step reasoning is necessary for high-quality generation, suggesting that the bottleneck in prior latent-reasoning work was the compression mechanism rather than the latent representation itself. The three-stage practice-to-mastery recipe (SFT → curriculum latent compression → GRPO) offers a generalizable training pattern for other multimodal generation tasks where explicit reasoning incurs prohibitive inference cost. Key limitations include evaluation on a single benchmark (HumanML3D) at a single model scale (3B), and the comparison to Motion-R1 relies on cited numbers since that code is unreleased; open questions include whether the latent-surpasses-explicit gap persists at larger scales, on more diverse motion corpora, and for compositional or long-horizon generation.
