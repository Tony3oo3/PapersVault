## Problem

Existing one-step distillation methods for diffusion-based super-resolution fail when applied to DiT architectures, producing severe grid-like periodic artifacts and suffering from a fundamental trajectory mismatch between the pretrained noise-to-data path and the new LR-to-HR restoration path. This mismatch is exacerbated under severe real-world degradation, where the domain gap between the two trajectories widens considerably, and the single large Euler step amplifies high-frequency spectral leakage unique to DiT's patch-based tokenization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262425/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image (degraded via the RealESRGAN pipeline) and produces a high-resolution output in exactly one forward pass (NFE=1). The LR image is consumed through two parallel encoders: a VAE encoder producing spatial latents and a Qwen3-VL-4B vision-language model producing semantic token embeddings that replace conventional text conditioning. Training and evaluation use 1024×1024 crops at ×4 upscaling, with base generators being Z-Image-Turbo (6B parameters) and FLUX.2-klein-4B (4B parameters), both fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 256).

## Method

StrSR is a one-step adversarial distillation framework with two key regularizers. First, an asymmetric discriminative distillation uses a pretrained CLIP-ConvNeXt-XXLarge as a PatchGAN discriminator (rather than a DiT discriminator, which collapses without progressive distillation), combined with relativistic average GAN loss and approximated R1 regularization to stably bridge the multi-step-to-one-step trajectory gap. Second, a Frequency Distribution Loss (FDL) minimizes the sliced Wasserstein distance between predicted and target features in both amplitude and phase components after DFT, directly suppressing the high-frequency spectral leakage that causes DiT-specific grid artifacts. The generator is a DiT conditioned on dual inputs (VAE spatial latents + VLM semantic tokens), trained in two stages: 40k steps with L1+LPIPS+RaGAN, followed by 20k steps adding FDL, all in bf16 on 4 A800 GPUs.

## Results

On DIV2K-val (×4), StrSR-FLUX achieves the best LPIPS (0.2992) and DISTS (0.1288) among all one-step and multi-step methods, while StrSR-Z-Image leads in QualiCLIP (0.7316) and MUSIQ (64.6547) among one-step baselines. On the real-world RealSR benchmark, StrSR-FLUX attains the best LPIPS (0.2599), DISTS (0.1665), NIQE (3.8090), and MANIQA (0.6333) among one-step methods. On RealLQ250, StrSR-FLUX achieves the best NIQE (3.4693), MANIQA (0.6138), and QAlign (4.2851), while StrSR-Z-Image leads in MUSIQ (70.1176). Both variants maintain competitive inference speed (~1 step) despite using 4B–6B parameter backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StrSR-FLUX achieves the best LPIPS (0.2992) and DISTS (0.1288) on DIV2K-val, surpassing all one-step baselines (next best: TSD-SR at 0.3453/0.1526) and even outperforming multi-step methods like SeeSR (0.3473/0.1580). On RealSR, StrSR-FLUX leads one-step methods in LPIPS (0.2599 vs. TSD-SR's 0.2874), DISTS (0.1665 vs. 0.1844), NIQE (3.8090 vs. 3.8310), and MANIQA (0.6333 vs. 0.6139), while StrSR-Z-Image achieves the best QualiCLIP (0.7417) and MUSIQ (68.5793) among one-step. On RealLQ250, StrSR-FLUX attains the best NIQE (3.4693), MANIQA (0.6138), and QAlign (4.2851), with StrSR-Z-Image leading in MUSIQ (70.1176 vs. TSD-SR's 71.0490 being the only metric where a baseline edges ahead). The closest DiT-based competitor, TSD-SR, is consistently outperformed on perceptual metrics (LPIPS, DISTS) by 0.03–0.05, confirming that the proposed FDL and asymmetric discriminator specifically address DiT's grid-artifact weakness. StrSR trades some PSNR/SSIM fidelity (e.g., 21.70 vs. CTMSR's 23.93 on DIV2K-val) for substantially better perceptual and no-reference quality, consistent with the perception-distortion tradeoff in generative SR.

## Relation to Prior Work

StrSR sits at the intersection of three research threads: rectified-flow-based generative modeling (Liu et al., ICLR 2023), adversarial diffusion distillation for one-step inference (Sauer et al., ECCV 2024), and frequency-domain regularization (Ni et al., CVPR 2024). It departs from prior one-step SR distillation work (OSEDiff, TSD-SR, HYPIR, FluxSR) by identifying a DiT-specific failure mode—high-frequency spectral leakage in patch tokens that produces grid-like artifacts far more severe than in UNet backbones—and addressing it with a repurposed sliced-Wasserstein frequency distribution loss. The asymmetric discriminator choice (frozen CLIP-ConvNeXt rather than a DiT) is a deliberate architectural departure from the symmetric generator-discriminator paradigm used in ADD and SeedVR2, motivated by the observation that DiT patchification destroys the local texture sensitivity needed to detect periodic artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As DiT architectures (FLUX, SD3, Z-Image) become the default backbone for high-quality generative models, making one-step distillation work reliably with them is a practically critical gap; StrSR provides a working recipe with clear diagnostics for why naive distillation fails. The spectral-leakage explanation for DiT grid artifacts is a useful conceptual contribution that could inform future architecture design or training schedules. However, the 4B–6B parameter count limits on-device deployment, PSNR/SSIM still trail multi-step methods, and the pipeline does not handle text-rich regions—leaving open whether the spectral constraint can be internalized into the DiT training objective itself rather than applied as a post-hoc loss.
