## Problem

Dense scaling of CLIP-style vision encoders increases both compute cost and inference latency, making it impractical for high-resolution images and long videos. While Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) has enabled efficient scaling in LLMs, the MoE design space for vision encoders remains underexplored, and prior MoE vision encoders have failed to match SOTA dense models on core benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262606/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes RGB images at resolutions from 224×224 (B/32, B/16) up to 448×448 (H/14) and videos as 8 uniformly sampled frames, producing a single visual embedding per input for contrastive alignment with text. Each MoE layer contains 32 fine-grained experts (each 1/4 the hidden width of the original dense MLP) plus shared experts, with top-k routing activating only 1/8 or 1/4 of the specialized experts per token, keeping activated parameters at 0.1B–1.1B while total parameters reach up to 3.5B.

## Method

MoE-ViE replaces dense MLP blocks in the vision tower with fine-grained MoE layers where each expert has reduced hidden width, enabling more experts under a comparable compute budget; shared experts are always active to preserve global context while routed experts capture token-specific transformations, and sigmoid gating with renormalization replaces softmax to avoid expert competition. For load balancing, the paper proposes a magnitude-aware variant of loss-free balancing that replaces the sign() update with a z-score correction, scaling the bias adjustment by the degree of imbalance for more stable routing. A custom Triton kernel implements Grouped GEMM (aggregating all expert MatMuls into one GPU task) and kernel fusion (combining MatMuls with activations) to achieve >2.5× inference speedup over naive implementations. For video finetuning, frame-level distillation from the image-pretrained teacher combined with freezing of MoE experts and text-tower MLPs prevents catastrophic forgetting of image representations while allowing video-specific adaptation.

## Results

On zero-shot image classification, MoE-ViE-H/14 (3.5B total, 1.1B activated) achieves 88.3% average accuracy across six benchmarks, with 93.2% on the challenging ImageNet-A, outperforming PEcoreG/14 (1.9B activated) by +0.6% on ImageNet-A while using only 58% of the activated parameters. At the L scale, MoE-ViE-L/16 (0.3B activated) reaches 86.2% average classification, marginally exceeding PEcoreL/14 (86.1%) and SigLIP2-L/16 (85.0%). In VLM alignment with Llama 3.1 8B, MoE-ViE-H/14 achieves 75.2% average image score and 58.9% average video score, surpassing PEcoreG/14 (69.2% / 48.6%) and InternViT2.5/14 (68.1% / 48.7%) despite having 1.7× fewer activated parameters. The optimized Triton kernel reduces inference latency to 82.6 ms (bsz=16), approximately 76% of PEcoreG/14's 101.2 ms, while the vanilla MoE implementation would take 318.8 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest dense baseline PEcore (Bolya et al., NeurIPS 2025), MoE-ViE wins at the B and L scales with identical activated parameters (76.8% vs 74.6% avg. classification at B; 86.2% vs 86.1% at L), and at the H scale MoE-ViE-H (1.1B activated) matches or exceeds PEcoreG (1.9B activated) on ImageNet-A (93.2% vs 92.6%) and video classification (76.5% vs 76.2%) while trailing by only 0.3% on overall image classification. In VLM alignment (Table 5), MoE-ViE-H decisively outperforms PEcoreG by +6.0 points on average image tasks and +10.3 on video tasks, and also surpasses InternViT2.5/14 (5.5B activated) and AIMv2 3B/14 (2.7B activated) on both image and video benchmarks. Against prior MoE vision encoders—LIMoE (Mustafa et al., 2022), CLIP-UP (Wang et al., 2025), and CLIP-MoE (Zhang et al., 2025)—MoE-ViE achieves substantially higher zero-shot accuracy; for example, MoE-ViE-L/16 reaches 86.2% average classification versus CLIP-UP-L/14's unreported average but 81.2% on ImageNet Val alone, and MoE-ViE-B/16 scores 76.8% versus LIMoE-B/16's 73.7% on ImageNet Val. On fine-grained benchmarks (Table 4), MoE-ViE-H/14 outperforms PEcoreG/14 on average fine-grained classification (83.9% vs 83.8%) and OCR (80.4% vs 79.1%), and on the VLM alignment captioning task MoE-ViE-H (127.5) trails SigLIP2-g-opt (129.1) by 1.6 points, the only clear loss among same-scale comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-style contrastive vision encoder (Radford et al., 2021) by replacing dense MLP blocks with a fine-grained Mixture-of-Experts design, building on the Perception Encoder training pipeline (Bolya et al., 2025) for unified image-video pretraining. It departs from prior MoE vision encoders (LIMoE, CLIP-MoE, CLIP-UP), which used full-capacity experts as drop-in MLP replacements, by adopting reduced-width experts with shared experts and sigmoid gating, drawing on insights from DeepSeekMoE in the LLM literature. The load-balancing mechanism modifies the loss-free approach of Wang et al. (2024) by replacing the sign() update with a z-score-scaled correction, and the video finetuning strategy introduces frame-level distillation combined with expert freezing to prevent catastrophic forgetting of image representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic demonstration that MoE vision encoders can match or exceed SOTA dense encoders (PEcoreG) at substantially lower compute, closing a gap that prior MoE-CLIP works left open. The practical Triton kernel (Grouped GEMM + kernel fusion) is essential for making MoE latency competitive, and the video finetuning recipe (distillation + freezing) addresses a real deployment concern for unified image-video encoders. Open questions include whether the fine-grained topology advantage persists at even larger scales, whether the approach transfers to other encoder architectures (e.g., convolutional or hybrid), and whether the expert specialization observed here can be leveraged for more targeted downstream adaptation.
