## Problem

Existing multimodal reward models are predominantly vision-centric, compress nuanced preferences into opaque scalar scores, and depend on expensive and inconsistent human annotation—creating a bottleneck for scalable, interpretable alignment across text, image, video, and audio. No prior work unifies rubric-grounded, criterion-level reward signals with automated preference synthesis across all four modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261687/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a multimodal context x (text, image, video, or audio) and a candidate response pair (y_A, y_B), the model generates a strict-JSON structured record containing: overall scores ŝ_A, ŝ_B ∈ [0,10], a categorical verdict ℓ ∈ {A, B, E} (E = tie), five criterion-level comparative justifications (fluency/coherence, relevance, accuracy/completeness, reasoning quality, safety/ethical alignment), and a redundant verdict copy for robust parsing. The same schema is used both as the SFT training target and as the basis for GRPO reward computation, tying the rubric directly to optimization rather than serving only as inference-time prompting.

## Method

Omni-RRM is built on a Qwen2.5-Omni backbone and trained in two stages. First, an automated preference synthesis pipeline constructs the Omni-Preference dataset (41k samples) via capability-gap sampling (strong vs. weak generator) followed by dual-teacher reconciliation (GPT-4o-mini + Gemini-2.0-Flash) with a strict consensus filter that retains only pairs where both teachers agree on a non-tie verdict consistent with their scores and rubric justifications. Stage 1 (SFT with LoRA) teaches the model to produce schema-compliant, rubric-grounded structured outputs via standard NLL loss. Stage 2 (GRPO) refines discrimination on low-margin pairs using a bounded composite reward R = w_fmt·R_fmt + w_pref·R_pref + w_rub·R_rub, where R_fmt enforces JSON validity, R_pref rewards correct preference and score-verdict consistency, and R_rub encourages substantive criterion-level justifications as structural regularization. The five rubric criteria are shared across modalities but instantiated with modality-specific evidence (e.g., visual grounding for images, temporal ordering for video, acoustic event recognition for audio), enabling cross-modal reward calibration within a single unified model.

## Results

Omni-RRM-7B (SFT+GRPO) achieves 70.4% five-benchmark Overall preference accuracy, a +17.0% relative gain over its Qwen2.5-Omni-7B backbone (60.2%). It sets state-of-the-art among open-source reward models on video (80.2% on ShareGPT-Video) and audio (66.8% on Audio-HH-RLHF, 65.0% on TA2T), and attains the highest MM-RewardBench score (72.9%) among all evaluated models. Its overall accuracy closely matches Gemini-2.5-Pro (70.6%) despite being an order of magnitude smaller. In Best-of-N inference, Omni-RRM-7B improves over self-consistency by +1.9 on MMMU, +1.7 on Video-MME, and +2.6 on AVQA at N=10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against proprietary MLLMs, Omni-RRM-7B (70.4% overall) narrowly trails Gemini-2.5-Pro (70.6%) but substantially outperforms GPT-4o-mini (58.3%) and Gemini-2.0-Flash (66.2%); on video it surpasses all proprietary models (80.2% vs. 78.8% for Gemini-2.5-Pro), and on audio it leads all proprietary baselines (66.8% vs. 66.5% for Gemini-2.5-Pro on Audio-HH). Among open-source reward models, Omni-RRM-7B dominates on video (80.2% vs. 77.8% for UnifiedReward-think-7B and 58.7% for R1-Reward-7B) and is the only model evaluated across all five benchmarks, where it achieves the highest overall score (70.4% vs. 60.9% for Omni-RewardModel-BT). On MM-RewardBench, it edges out R1-Reward-7B (72.9% vs. 72.3%) and UnifiedReward-think-7B (72.9% vs. 71.4%), while on VL-Reward it trails Gemini-2.5-Pro (67.1% vs. 79.6%) and UnifiedReward-think-7B (67.1% vs. 66.6%, a near-tie). The +17.0% relative gain over the Qwen2.5-Omni-7B backbone confirms that rubric-grounded SFT+GRPO post-training, rather than raw model scale, drives reward-modeling performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning recipe from DeepSeek R1/DeepSeekMath to the reward-modeling domain, applying group-sampling and relative-advantage optimization to sharpen preference discrimination on low-margin pairs. It departs from prior omni-modal reward models (Omni-Reward, LLaVA-Critic, UnifiedReward-Think) by replacing opaque scalar scores or free-form critiques with a fixed five-criterion rubric that enforces structured, auditable justifications before the final verdict. The automatic preference synthesis pipeline (capability-gap sampling + dual-teacher reconciliation) builds on RLAIF-style ideas but introduces rubric-grounded consensus filtering to ensure label fidelity without human annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical bottlenecks in multimodal alignment: the cost and inconsistency of human preference annotation, and the opacity of scalar reward signals that hinder debugging and controllable improvement. The cross-modal transfer finding—that audio reward performance benefits substantially from joint vision/video training under a shared rubric—suggests a strategic path for extending reward modeling to under-resourced modalities. Open questions include whether the fixed five-criterion rubric generalizes to new modalities or task domains, whether the dual-teacher consensus filter scales to more heterogeneous model pairs, and whether the rubric-grounded structure remains beneficial as base models grow more capable.
