## Problem

While MLLMs are increasingly applied to pixel-level segmentation, it remains unclear whether the full MLLM pipeline actually improves segmentation over its underlying vision encoder, and what mechanisms drive any degradation or recovery across the stack. Prior analyses have shown MLLMs can underperform their vision backbones on vision-centric tasks, but no work has performed causal interventions to identify where segmentation competence arises or is lost within the adapter-style MLLM architecture.

## Input / Output

The framework takes 336×336 RGB images (divided into 576 patches of size 14) and text prompts, producing per-patch semantic class labels that are reshaped into 2D segmentation maps for pixel-level evaluation. The primary diagnostic output is a layerwise mIoU profile tracking segmentation quality from the vision encoder output, through the adapter, and across every intermediate LLM layer, revealing where spatial information is lost and recovered. Attention knockout and bidirectional attention variants produce modified layerwise profiles that isolate the contribution of specific attention pathways.

## Method

The paper introduces a three-part diagnostic framework for adapter-style MLLMs (LLaVA-1.5 with CLIP, DINOv2, or SigLIP encoders paired with Vicuna-7B). First, layerwise linear probing trains independent linear probes on frozen embeddings at every pipeline stage, revealing a consistent representation drop-off at the adapter (trading spatial fidelity for cross-modal alignment) followed by progressive recovery across LLM layers. Second, attention knockout selectively blocks attention to correctly or incorrectly classified tokens across all LLM layers, demonstrating that correctly classified tokens act as semantic anchors whose attention signals pull misclassified neighbors toward the correct label, confirming cross-token attention as the operative refinement mechanism. Third, an image-only bidirectional attention mask (granting full attention among image tokens while preserving causal masking for text) eliminates context starvation at early raster-order positions, with text-aligned encoders (CLIP, SigLIP) benefiting more than vision-only encoders (DINOv2). The framework is validated across multiple architectures (OneVision, DeepSeek-VL) and nine VQA benchmarks to confirm that bidirectional image attention preserves language capabilities.

## Results

Layerwise linear probing on ADE20K reveals a consistent segmentation mIoU drop-off at the adapter for all three encoders (CLIP, DINOv2, SigLIP), followed by progressive recovery across LLM layers; CLIP's LLM-layer representations ultimately exceed the vision encoder baseline, while DINOv2 (vision-only) recovers least. Bidirectional attention among image tokens yields a +14.35 pp pixel accuracy gain (23.2% relative) at patch 0 under causal attention, with peak mIoU improvements of +0.42 (CLIP), +0.32 (DINOv2), and a sustained monotonic rise from 35.39 to 41.26 (SigLIP) across LLM layers. On VQA benchmarks, bidirectional attention preserves language understanding for CLIP and SigLIP (e.g., GQA 62.6→62.7, MME Perception 1483→1538 for CLIP) while DINOv2 shows minor regressions (GQA 62.1→60.5, VizWiz 51.4→45.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic analysis paper rather than a method comparison, so the primary 'baselines' are internal ablations. Causal attention serves as the default baseline; bidirectional image-token attention is the intervention. For segmentation, bidirectional attention wins on all three encoders (CLIP +0.42 mIoU, DINOv2 +0.32, SigLIP +5.87 peak), with the largest gains concentrated at early patch positions where causal masking starves tokens of visual context. For language understanding, CLIP and SigLIP essentially tie or slightly improve under bidirectional attention (e.g., CLIP MME-P 1483→1538, SigLIP GQA 61.1→62.1), while DINOv2 loses on most VQA benchmarks (GQA −1.6, MMB −2.4, VizWiz −5.6), consistent with its weaker text alignment. The attention knockout experiments further show that blocking the correct class (removing semantic anchors) degrades segmentation below the unmodified baseline in mid-to-late layers, while blocking the incorrect class accelerates self-correction, confirming cross-token attention as the operative refinement mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the attention knockout technique from Geva et al. [15] (originally for tracing factual recall in autoregressive LMs) and Neo et al. [29] (applied to MLLM visual information extraction at the output position) to probe a fundamentally different question: whether cross-token attention among image patches drives spatial self-refinement across LLM layers. It extends the linear probing paradigm used in prior diagnostic analyses of MLLM representations (Zhang et al. [45], Fu et al. [13]) from classification and correspondence tasks to semantic segmentation, and from the vision encoder in isolation to the full encoder–adapter–LLM stack. The bidirectional-attention ablation reaches the same architectural conclusion as STAMP [26] from a mechanistic rather than empirical standpoint, and the training protocol follows the standard LLaVA-1.5 two-stage procedure [24].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic account of where and how segmentation competence is lost and recovered within an MLLM, identifying cross-token attention to correctly classified 'semantic anchor' tokens as the operative refinement mechanism. This has direct design implications: it suggests that segmentation-capable MLLMs should preserve or restore bidirectional access among image tokens (as STAMP does empirically) and that text-aligned encoders (CLIP, SigLIP) are better positioned to benefit from LLM-layer refinement than vision-only ones (DINOv2). Key limitations include reliance on LLaVA-type adapter architectures, linear probes that may underestimate capacity achievable with richer decoders, and global (all-layer) knockout that cannot isolate individual layers' contributions. Open questions include whether the semantic-anchor mechanism persists under task-specific fine-tuning, whether it extends to non-adapter architectures (e.g., unified token sequences), and whether the findings generalize to video or 3D segmentation.
