## Problem

VLA policies become brittle under deployment distribution shift when only limited target-domain demonstrations are available. Subgoal-image planning mitigates this by providing an explicit visual target, but the target image specifies what future scene to reach without encoding how the robot should transition toward it. The paper asks whether the internal denoising-time hidden states already produced inside a diffusion visual planner can be directly reused as action-conditioning transition cues, without training an additional flow, video generator, or feature extractor.

## Input / Output

The system consumes multi-view RGB images (one overhead plus wrist-mounted cameras at 30 Hz), a natural-language task instruction, and a low-dimensional robot joint-state vector (16-dim on LIBERO, 7-DOF per arm on OpenArm). It produces a continuous action chunk of H=50 steps (each 7-dim, zero-padded to a fixed 32-dim interface) generated by 10-step Euler ODE integration in latent space, alongside a subgoal image at a fixed 10-frame horizon. The action chunk is executed in a receding-horizon loop with replanning every m≤H steps.

## Method

TDSR-VLA decouples a Stable Diffusion 3 (MM-DiT) Vision Planner from a Gemma-based flow-matching Action Expert. The Vision Planner, fine-tuned with LoRA, generates a subgoal image and exposes two denoising-time conditioning hidden sequences: an early-step Sequence Representation (SR) anchored to the current observation and a final-step Updated Sequence Representation (USR) reflecting instruction- and subgoal-aligned updates. The Action Expert injects these via a phase-specific layer-wise prefix key-value rule: lower layers (0–11) use SR as prefix keys and SigLIP tokens of current views as prefix values to stabilize current-state grounding, while upper layers (12–17) use USR as keys and SigLIP tokens of the generated subgoal as values to guide subgoal-aligned trajectory generation. Training proceeds in two stages—LoRA fine-tuning of the planner followed by Action Expert training on precomputed, cached planner outputs with the planner frozen—preventing planning-control coupling and enabling stable learning from only 100 demonstrations per task.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark (without robot-demonstration pretraining), TDSR-VLA achieves 82.0% success on Goal and 56.0% on Long, outperforming OpenVLA (79.2% / 53.7%) on both suites and surpassing π0 (48.0%) on Long. On real-world bimanual OpenArm tasks, TDSR-VLA attains the highest mean score of 0.83 (95% CI [0.767, 0.879]), with the largest margin on object-cooperative tasks (0.88 vs. 0.70 for GR00T N1.5). The per-chunk end-to-end latency is 3453.4 ms, dominated by Vision Planner inference (3014.8 ms, ~87% of total).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OpenVLA (with robot-demonstration pretraining), TDSR-VLA wins on both LIBERO suites—Goal 82.0 vs. 79.2 and Long 56.0 vs. 53.7—despite using no pretraining data. Against π0 (no pretraining), TDSR-VLA trades Goal performance (82.0 vs. 89.0) for a substantial Long-horizon gain (56.0 vs. 48.0), and on real-world OpenArm tasks it clearly outperforms π0 (0.83 vs. 0.71 overall; 0.88 vs. 0.63 on object-cooperative tasks). Against GR00T N1.5, the strongest real-world baseline, TDSR-VLA achieves a +0.083 paired gain (0.83 vs. 0.74, 95% CI [0.004, 0.163]), with the gap widening to +0.18 on object-cooperative bimanual tasks (0.88 vs. 0.70). Against SmolVLA, TDSR-VLA leads by a wide margin (0.83 vs. 0.58) on real-world tasks. The consistent pattern is that TDSR-VLA's advantage grows with task coordination demands and horizon length, while its main weakness is per-chunk latency (~3.5 s) relative to direct VLM-to-action baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

TDSR-VLA extends the subgoal-image planning line of work (e.g., [5, 25, 41]) by repurposing the internal denoising-time hidden states of a Stable Diffusion 3 / MM-DiT Vision Planner as action-conditioning signals, rather than generating separate motion artifacts (flow tokens, video features, or synthetic demonstrations) as prior methods do. The Action Expert builds on the conditional flow-matching paradigm [21, 34] for continuous action generation and adopts layer-wise prefix key-value conditioning [20] as the injection mechanism, creating a hierarchical structure where lower layers ground actions in current observations (via SR) and upper layers align with the planned transition (via USR). This positions the work at the intersection of diffusion-based visual planning and flow-matching action generation, with the key departure being that no additional generative model is trained to produce transition cues—they are extracted for free from the planner's existing denoising process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a diffusion planner's intermediate denoising states carry action-relevant transition information beyond the final subgoal image, offering a data-efficient path to long-horizon bimanual manipulation without robot-demonstration pretraining. However, the sequential planning-then-acting architecture introduces a significant ~3.4 s per-chunk latency (87% from Vision Planner inference), which limits real-time applicability and is acknowledged as the primary systems bottleneck. Open questions include whether asynchronous subgoal generation can close this gap, whether the SR/USR decomposition generalizes to other diffusion backbones or single-arm settings, and whether the approach scales beyond the six-task, 100-demo-per-task evaluation protocol used here.
