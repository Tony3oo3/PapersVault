## Problem

Current SSM-based salient object detection methods rely on increasingly complex spatial scanning strategies to force a single architecture to capture the full frequency spectrum, overlooking the inherent spectral biases of different neural paradigms. The paper demonstrates through cross-dataset spectral analysis that CNNs and SSMs exhibit complementary frequency preferences, meaning either alone creates representational blind spots, yet no prior work has systematically exploited this complementarity for general SOD.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261829/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

LFNet accepts five input configurations through a unified architecture: single RGB, RGB+depth, RGB+thermal, video frame sequences, and RGB+depth+thermal, all resized to 512×512. A Modality Fusion Block within the ConvNeXt stream adapts to the input type—applying a 1×1 projection for single-modal, a single LFM for dual-modal, and cascaded LFMs for tri-modal—without requiring cross-attention. The output is a per-pixel saliency segmentation mask produced at four hierarchical scales with multi-scale supervision.

## Method

LFNet employs parallel VMamba-Small and ConvNeXt-Pico backbones to extract complementary spectral representations, then fuses them via a Liquid Fusion Module (LFM) inspired by Closed-form Continuous-time (CfC) dynamics. The LFM treats VMamba features as an evolving memory state and ConvNeXt features as an exogenous stimulus, computing a dynamic spatial gate G_i from concatenated features to perform content-aware interpolation: f_i = Conv((1−G_i)·f̃^v + G_i·f̃^c), where high G_i prioritizes grid-based spatial cues and low G_i retains sequence-based context. A Saliency-Guided Upsampling (SGU) operator then restores resolution using a dual-branch spectral-spatial co-design—applying FFT with learnable complex weights in the spectral branch and stacked 3×3 convolutions in the spatial branch—to suppress aliasing while preserving sharp boundaries. The full model is trained end-to-end with multi-scale BCE + IoU loss over 50 epochs using AdamW with cosine annealing.

## Results

LFNet (43.23M params) achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five general SOD tasks. On RGB SOD, it reaches 93.6% S_m on DUTS, 90.4% on DUT-O, 94.6% on HKU-IS, 89.6% on PASCAL-S, and 95.4% on ECSSD, outperforming the strongest Mamba-based competitor Samba (49.59M) by 0.4–1.6% S_m while using ~13% fewer parameters. On RGB-D SOD, LFNet (44.65M) attains 95.0% S_m on NJUD and 96.3% on DUTLF-D, surpassing VST-S++ (143.15M) by up to 2.2% S_m with only 31% of its parameters. On the tri-modal VDT-2048 benchmark, LFNet sets a new SOTA of 94.2% S_m and 91.9% F_m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LFNet consistently outperforms all 35 baselines across the five general SOD tasks. Against the strongest Mamba-based competitor Samba [18], LFNet wins on every dataset in every task: +0.4–1.6% S_m on RGB SOD, +0.1–0.7% on RGB-D, +0.1–0.7% on RGB-T (with a tie on VT5000 S_m at 92.8%), +0.6–1.8% on VSOD, and +0.4% on VDT-2048, while using 13–24% fewer parameters. Against the best Transformer-based methods (VSCode-S, VST-S++, ICON-S), LFNet achieves gains of 1.0–3.5% S_m on RGB SOD and up to 2.2% on RGB-D (NJUD), with a parameter count of only 31–58% of those methods. Against CNN-based baselines (CSF-R2, EDN, MENet, JL-DCF, SP-Net), LFNet leads by 3–10% S_m, confirming the advantage of the heterogeneous SSM+CNN fusion over purely convolutional architectures. The only near-tie is on ECSSD E_m (97.7% vs. Samba's 97.8%) and VT5000 S_m (92.8% vs. Samba's 92.8%), where the difference is within 0.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Samba line of work (CVPR 2025), which first adapted SSMs to general SOD, by arguing that single-paradigm architectures inherently suffer from spectral biases. Rather than engineering increasingly complex scanning strategies within a pure SSM (as prior Mamba-based SOD methods do), the authors introduce a heterogeneous dual-backbone design pairing VMamba with ConvNeXt, motivated by a cross-dataset spectral analysis showing complementary frequency preferences. The fusion mechanism itself is a repurposing of Liquid Neural Network / Closed-form Continuous-time dynamics—originally developed for sequential and robotic control—recast as a spatial state-stimulus gating operation for dense feature aggregation. The Saliency-Guided Upsampling module further departs from standard decoder upsampling by incorporating a learnable FFT-based spectral branch alongside spatial convolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spectral analysis providing empirical evidence that CNNs and SSMs occupy complementary frequency bands is a useful diagnostic that could inform hybrid architecture design beyond SOD. The liquid fusion paradigm offers a scalable, attention-free mechanism for multi-modal integration that avoids the quadratic cost of cross-attention, which is practically relevant as SOD tasks expand to tri-modal and video settings. However, the framework still requires training two full backbones in parallel, and the spectral complementarity finding is validated only on VMamba-Small and ConvNeXt-Pico; whether it generalizes to other SSM variants (e.g., Mamba-2, Hymba) or larger scales remains open. The authors note future work on extending the paradigm to broader dense prediction tasks and lightweight edge deployment.
