## Problem

DiT-based video diffusion models use a unified 3D full-attention mechanism that entangles spatial and temporal tokens, making it difficult to independently control motion, subject appearance, and background without per-video fine-tuning or auxiliary adapters. Existing training-free video manipulation methods were designed for UNet architectures with explicitly decoupled spatial and temporal layers, leaving a gap for disentangled, training-free control within DiT-based video diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261670/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference video (encoded by a 3D VAE into latent space, providing motion dynamics and background context) and a single subject image (providing target appearance) as input. SAM2 is used to extract a subject mask from the subject image and a background mask from the reference video for region-specific guidance. The output is a personalized video in which the subject's appearance is replaced by the subject image while the reference video's motion trajectories and background are preserved; the initial generation latent is constructed by replicating a single random noise F times along the frame axis to ensure temporal consistency.

## Method

SA-V2V exploits the observation that the 3D unified attention matrix in DiTs decomposes functionally: diagonal (intra-frame) blocks encode spatial appearance while off-diagonal (inter-frame) blocks encode motion dynamics. For motion control, stochastic temporal injection sparsely transfers inter-frame attention features from the reference video using a random Bernoulli mask, preventing spatial misalignment when the subject differs in position or scale from the reference object; this is applied only during the first 30% of denoising steps. For appearance control, Target-aware Feature Guidance (TFG) performs masked linear interpolation of attention features—S-TFG transfers subject appearance using the subject mask, while B-TFG preserves background using the background mask—and the two are applied alternately across timesteps during the first 25% of denoising to avoid feature interference. The entire pipeline is training-free and inversion-free, operating directly on the attention features of a pretrained CogVideoX-5B model without any parameter updates or additional adapters.

## Results

On the 200-pair evaluation set (DAVIS, Pexels, VBench), SA-V2V achieves the best background preservation (0.8768 masked CLIP-I), lowest FVD (66.55), and highest average VideoScore (2.7646) among all methods. It also leads on four of five VideoScore sub-metrics (VQ 2.65, TC 2.55, DD 3.15, FC 2.50). The only metric where SA-V2V is not best is subject adherence (0.8140), where MotionClone edges ahead at 0.8243, though MotionClone's background score drops to 0.7604 and FVD rises to 85.87.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SA-V2V outperforms all four baselines on background preservation (0.8768 vs. 0.8503 CtrlX, 0.7604 MotionClone, 0.7984 DualReal, 0.8240 JointTuner) and FVD (66.55 vs. 67.85–85.87), confirming superior motion fidelity and scene consistency. Against MotionClone, which achieves the highest subject score (0.8243), SA-V2V trades a small 0.01 subject gap for a 0.12 background improvement and a 19-point FVD reduction, indicating MotionClone's image adapter overfits to the subject at the expense of the rest of the scene. Compared to the training-based DualReal and JointTuner, SA-V2V wins on FVD by 17 and 8 points respectively while requiring zero per-video optimization (vs. 23–41 min training). The user study (29 participants) further confirms SA-V2V's dominance, with 76.1% preference for background, 69.0% for motion, and 72.1% for overall video quality—far exceeding the next-best method (JointTuner at 10.1% background, 8.0% motion). The sole metric where SA-V2V trails is TVA (2.8731 vs. 2.9968 JointTuner), suggesting slightly less temporal-aesthetic smoothness, though this is offset by gains in all other quality dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

SA-V2V builds on the attention-modulation paradigm from training-free consistent generation (RefDrop, Tewel et al.), extending their linear-interpolation-of-attention scheme into a masked, target-aware form (TFG) for video. It departs from training-based DiT customization methods (JointTuner, DualReal, DreamRelation) that require per-video LoRA optimization, and from UNet-based training-free approaches (CtrlX, MotionClone) that exploit explicitly decoupled spatial/temporal layers. The key conceptual departure is the structural observation that DiT's 3D unified attention implicitly factorizes into spatial (diagonal) and temporal (off-diagonal) blocks, enabling disentangled control without architectural modification or additional adapters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The attention-decomposition insight is broadly applicable to any DiT-based video model and provides a principled interface for future training-free manipulation of motion and appearance independently. The zero-training-time requirement (vs. 20–40 min per video for baselines) makes the approach practically attractive for interactive or large-scale personalization. However, the method is limited to single-subject videos, and the stochastic Bernoulli masking for temporal injection is a heuristic that may not generalize to complex multi-object motion or large spatial misalignments. Extending TFG to multi-subject settings and replacing stochastic injection with learned or deterministic alignment remain open challenges.
