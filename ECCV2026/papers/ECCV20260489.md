## Problem

Standard DPO for diffusion models relies on the Bradley-Terry model, which collapses multi-dimensional human preferences (aesthetic quality, semantic alignment, safety, etc.) into a single scalar reward. This aggregation creates a fundamental reward conflict: when a globally preferred sample is worse on a specific dimension, the model is forced to unlearn desirable features of that dimension, actively degrading it. No prior approach resolves this conflict within the DPO loss itself—existing work either filters out conflicting pairs, merges separate models, or uses alternative objectives.

## Input / Output

At training, the method takes a text prompt and a 5-dimensional preference outcome vector γ ∈ {−1,0,1}^D (encoding win/lose/tie per axis: human label, PickScore, Aesthetic, HPSv2, CLIP) for each image pair. At inference, the model takes a text prompt and optionally a target preference condition, using Classifier-Free Guidance between 'win' and 'lose' conditions to amplify specific reward dimensions without external reward models. The output is a generated RGB image (512×512 for SD1.5, 1024×1024 for SDXL), and the preference vector is encoded as natural-language tokens (e.g., 'win tie') processed through a text encoder before injection into the U-Net.

## Method

MCDPO introduces a disentangled Bradley-Terry objective that conditions the diffusion policy on a per-dimension preference outcome vector γ, so the conditional policy p_θ(x|c,γ) learns a distinct implicit reward for each γ configuration. The training loss (Eq. 17) is symmetric, simultaneously optimizing both γ^wl and γ^lw conditions, which forces the model to modulate its implicit reward independently per dimension. A context-aware reward conditioning module encodes γ as text tokens, refines the embedding via self-attention with the prompt and cross-attention with the image latent, and injects it into the U-Net through parallel cross-attention blocks (zero-initialized to preserve pretrained weights). Dimensional reward dropout randomly zeros individual γ entries during training to prevent easier dimensions from dominating gradients. A brief MCSFT pre-training stage (frozen U-Net, training only the conditioning modules) initializes condition-following before the MCDPO alignment phase, and at inference, CFG between win and lose conditions enables dynamic, multi-axis reward steering without additional training.

## Results

On SD1.5 (Pick-a-Pic v2 test set), MCDPO achieves an average win rate of 81.47%, substantially outperforming all baselines: Diffusion-DPO (66.74%), Diffusion-KTO (71.48%), and DSPO (73.24%). The most dramatic gains appear in Aesthetic score (91.88% vs. 65.08% for DPO) and HPSv2 (93.44% vs. 70.28%), while MCDPO uses only 18% of the training data (~180K pairs) compared to the full 1M pairs used by baselines. On SDXL with just 3% of data (~30K pairs), MCDPO again leads with 70.72% average, beating Diffusion-DPO (63.99%), MAPO (62.02%), and DSPO (60.82%). The sole metric where MCDPO trails is CLIP (57.64% vs. 57.80% for DPO), which the authors attribute to a natural multi-objective trade-off that can be recovered at test-time via CFG-based reward steering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCDPO decisively outperforms Diffusion-DPO [23] across all metrics except CLIP (57.64% vs. 57.80%), with the largest margins in Aesthetic (+26.8 pts) and HPSv2 (+23.2 pts) on the PickV2 test set, despite using only 18% of the training data. Against Diffusion-KTO [10], MCDPO leads on every metric including CLIP (57.64% vs. 56.64%), with an average gap of ~10 points. Versus DSPO [32], MCDPO wins on PickScore (+9.8), Aesthetic (+16.0), HPSv2 (+11.5), ImageReward (+5.6), and MPS (+8.0), but trails on CLIP (57.64% vs. 59.16%). On SDXL, MCDPO also dominates MAPO [6] by a wide margin (70.72% vs. 62.02% average), where MAPO shows particularly weak PickScore (50.65%) and MPS (52.26%). The consistent CLIP deficit is explained as a multi-objective trade-off; the authors demonstrate that test-time CFG steering targeting CLIP recovers it to 59.0% without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., CVPR 2024) and the original DPO formulation (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023) by identifying a structural limitation in the Bradley-Terry model when applied to multi-dimensional preferences: the scalar aggregation of heterogeneous reward axes creates sign conflicts that force the model to unlearn desirable features. Prior work addresses multi-preference alignment through parameter merging, gradient regularization, SFT-based in-context reward modeling, or filtering to conflict-free pairs, but all avoid rather than resolve the conflict within the DPO loss itself. MCDPO departs from these by introducing a disentangled BT objective that conditions the policy on a per-dimension preference outcome vector, transforming conflicting pairs from obstacles into supervision signals within a single network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a fundamental ambiguity in how DPO-based alignment handles multi-dimensional human preferences, and the resulting conditional framework enables dynamic, multi-axis control at inference via CFG without external reward models—a practical capability absent from prior DPO methods. The strong sample efficiency (18% and 3% data outperforming full-data baselines) and the finding that conflict-only training surpasses random-sampling DPO suggest that the reward conflict, long treated as noise to be filtered, is actually an underutilized learning signal. Key limitations include reliance on proxy reward models (CLIP, HPSv2, PickScore, Aesthetic Predictor) to construct the preference outcome vector, which upper-bounds alignment quality and introduces preprocessing overhead; whether the approach generalizes to open-ended or continuously-valued preference axes beyond discrete win/lose/tie remains open.
