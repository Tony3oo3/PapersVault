## Problem

Existing HOI video generation methods rely on dense frame-wise control signals (pose sequences, mesh templates, coordinate maps) that are expensive to annotate, suppress the generative model's inherent motion priors, and propagate input jitter into physically implausible outputs. No prior work achieves precise sparse temporal control—specifying interaction states at only a few designated timestamps while autonomously synthesizing natural transitions between them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262547/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image for identity, a 16 kHz audio sequence, and K sparse hand-object interaction keyframes (typically 4) at user-specified timestamps, all at 512×512 resolution. Keyframes are pre-masked to hand-object regions with morphological refinement and HSV jittering to decouple pose from appearance. The output is a 10-second, 25 FPS audio-driven HOI video where the specified interaction poses appear at their designated timestamps and the model hallucinates physically plausible intermediate dynamics.

## Method

Built on the Wan2.1-DiT flow-matching backbone, the framework introduces three core mechanisms. First, Time-Controlled Rotary Positional Embedding (TiRoPE) assigns each interaction keyframe the temporal positional index of its target timestamp in the RoPE encoding, so self-attention naturally anchors the keyframe's pose and texture to the correct moment while preserving spatial indices. Second, a Motion Prior Injection Module feeds the keyframes into a frozen Qwen2.5-VL to extract high-level hand-object pose-change semantics, compresses them via a Q-Former into 64 motion guidance tokens, and injects these through dedicated cross-attention layers in every DiT block to govern intermediate-frame dynamics. Third, a decoupled two-stage training strategy separates concerns: Stage 1 (LoRA on DiT) learns appearance transfer and TiRoPE-anchored pose adherence, while Stage 2 (Q-Former + cross-attention only) learns motion transitions, preventing feature entanglement between the MLLM's abstract reasoning and the DiT's visual synthesis.

## Results

On the SparseHOI-5K test set, SparseCtrl-HOI achieves the best FID (89.1) and FVD (629.6), substantially outperforming the nearest baseline OmniAvatar-BL (FID 129.3, FVD 1110.9) and all other methods. It also attains the lowest MS-RAFT warping error (0.45) and the highest HOI-VLM interaction score (2.92), indicating superior temporal smoothness and physical plausibility. On the AnchorCrafter test set, SparseCtrl-HOI matches AnchorCrafter on FID (91.4 vs. 91.9) and FVD (725.8 vs. 726.4) while achieving a markedly lower MS-RAFT (0.27 vs. 0.43) and higher HOI-VLM (3.00 vs. 2.90), all using only 4 sparse keyframes instead of dense frame-wise controls. The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is Sync-C on SparseHOI-5K, where OmniAvatar scores 7.04 vs. 6.98.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseCtrl-HOI outperforms all four baselines on FID, FVD, MS-RAFT, and HOI-VLM across both test sets. Against AnchorCrafter (the dense-control method), it achieves comparable FID/FVD on the AnchorCrafter test set (91.4 vs. 91.9) while halving the MS-RAFT warping error (0.27 vs. 0.43) and improving HOI-VLM from 2.90 to 3.00, all with only 4 keyframes instead of per-frame pose/mesh/depth inputs; on the harder SparseHOI-5K set the gap widens dramatically (FID 89.1 vs. 258.4, HOI-VLM 2.92 vs. 1.18). Against OmniAvatar (the audio-driven baseline), SparseCtrl-HOI wins on FID (89.1 vs. 131.2), FVD (629.6 vs. 1207.0), MS-RAFT (0.45 vs. 0.47), and HOI-VLM (2.92 vs. 2.76), while OmniAvatar retains a marginal Sync-C advantage (7.04 vs. 6.98) due to its simpler first-frame conditioning. VACE and Phantom trail significantly on all fidelity metrics (FID 212.9/254.2 vs. 89.1) and produce physically implausible artifacts such as floating or oversized objects, with HOI-VLM scores of 2.86 and 1.56 respectively. The OmniAvatar-BL ablation (same inputs, standard RoPE, no TiRoPE or motion priors) confirms that the proposed components contribute meaningfully: removing them degrades FID from 89.1 to 129.3 and MS-RAFT from 0.45 to 0.49.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the flow-matching DiT paradigm from OmniAvatar and the Wan2.1 backbone, repurposing them for a new control regime: sparse temporal keyframe conditioning rather than dense frame-wise guidance. It departs from the dominant HOI video generation literature (AnchorCrafter, ByteLoom, HOMA, VHOI), which either requires per-frame pose/mesh/coordinate annotations or converts sparse spatial cues into dense temporal constraints. The Q-Former compression of MLLM motion priors borrows the BLIP-2 architectural pattern, but applies it to a novel role—encoding high-level hand-object pose-change semantics as cross-attention guidance for a diffusion transformer. The TiRoPE mechanism is a targeted modification of standard RoPE that assigns keyframes their target timestamp indices, a simple but previously unexplored trick for temporal anchoring in video DiTs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the annotation burden from frame-wise sequences to just four keyframes, this work makes HOI video generation far more practical for real-world e-commerce pipelines where dense pose capture is infeasible. The SparseHOI-5K dataset, with its multi-modal annotations (hand/object masks, object-free inpainted frames, synchronized audio), fills a gap that no prior public dataset addressed for audio-driven live-commerce HOI. Open questions include whether the MLLM-derived motion priors generalize beyond the e-commerce product-introduction domain to more complex multi-object or full-body interactions, and whether the decoupled training strategy scales to longer videos or more keyframes without the current 4-keyframe constraint.
