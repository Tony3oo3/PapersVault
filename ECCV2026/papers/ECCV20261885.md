## Problem

Existing dynamic scene reconstruction methods built on Gaussian Splatting or NeRF optimize for photometric consistency but remain agnostic to instance-level structure, causing identity drift, foreground-background entanglement, and unstable semantic association in highly dynamic scenarios. Prior work that adds segmentation or language queries follows a 'reconstruction-then-understanding' pipeline, treating geometry and semantics as loosely coupled components rather than mutually reinforcing processes, and typically fails under fast or complex motions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-view video (32 cameras at 4K/30 fps for ST-NeRF; 20 cameras at 1080p for MPEG GSC) along with text prompts (e.g., 'basketball player') that drive SAM3 instance extraction and MLLM caption generation. It produces a unified 4D Gaussian representation where each foreground primitive carries an 8-dimensional learnable semantic feature, from which the system renders novel views, outputs per-pixel instance segmentation masks across all frames, and computes per-frame relevance scores for open-vocabulary text queries. The semantic supervision is derived from temporally consistent SAM3 masks and 6-dimensional sentence embeddings compressed from MLLM-generated captions via a lightweight autoencoder.

## Method

Director enforces instance-level consistency directly at the Gaussian primitive level by embedding learnable semantic features into each dynamic Gaussian and jointly optimizing them with motion and appearance. The scene is explicitly decomposed into a static background layer (optimized once over the full sequence) and a dynamic foreground layer where two MLP decoders predict instance identity (cross-entropy against SAM3 masks) and language-aligned embeddings (L1 against MLLM-derived sentence vectors), while a KL-divergence term over k-nearest 3D neighbors enforces spatial semantic coherence. For temporal tracking, SEA-RAFT optical flow warps Gaussians from the previous frame via multi-view least-squares triangulation, followed by an as-rigid-as-possible refinement stage that optimizes only positions and rotations. During per-frame training, geometry-aware SDF losses computed from instance silhouettes penalize Gaussians that project outside their assigned mask, and temporal regularization terms prevent abrupt attribute changes, all optimized end-to-end for 8,000 iterations per frame.

## Results

On the basketball dataset, Director achieves the best rendering quality (PSNR 38.30, SSIM 0.965, LPIPS 0.047) and the best 4D segmentation accuracy (mIoU 0.831, Recall 0.876, F1 0.888), substantially outperforming the closest reconstruction-then-understanding baseline TaoGS w/ Sem (mIoU 0.578, F1 0.676). On the broader rendering benchmark (Table 1), Director leads all methods on both ST-NeRF (PSNR 38.91) and MPEG GSC (PSNR 38.24), surpassing Spacetime Gaussian by ~1 dB PSNR on ST-NeRF. The method also enables open-vocabulary instance querying via cosine similarity in the learned semantic embedding space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Director outperforms all six baselines on both rendering and segmentation metrics. Against the pure rendering methods (4DGS, Spacetime Gaussian, TaoGS), Director achieves 1.0–4.3 dB higher PSNR on ST-NeRF and 0.4–2.1 dB on MPEG GSC, with the largest gap over 4DGS (38.91 vs. 34.72 PSNR on ST-NeRF). Among 4D segmentation methods, Director's mIoU of 0.831 exceeds SA4D (0.524) and SADG (0.500) by over 30 points, and the reconstruction-then-understanding TaoGS w/ Sem (0.578) by 25 points, demonstrating that jointly optimizing geometry and semantics at the primitive level is critical. 4-LEGS produces the worst rendering (PSNR 31.66) and does not support instance-level queries, limiting its comparison to rendering only. Qualitatively, Director avoids the fast-motion blur of 4DGS, the over-smoothing of TaoGS, and the inter-instance entanglement that plagues decoupled two-stage pipelines like TaoGS w/ Sem.

## Relation to Prior Work

Director extends the per-Gaussian motion tracking paradigm of dynamic 3DGS (building directly on 3DGS [28] and the two-layer decomposition from DualGS [23]) by embedding learnable semantic feature vectors into each Gaussian primitive and supervising them with SAM3-derived instance masks and MLLM-derived sentence embeddings. It departs from the prevailing 'reconstruction-then-understanding' pipeline used by prior 4D segmentation methods (SA4D, SADG) and language-field approaches (LERF, LangSplat, 4DLangSplat), which treat geometric modeling and semantics as loosely coupled stages. The spatial coherence of semantic features is enforced via a KL-divergence regularization borrowed from Gaussian Grouping [79], while temporal stability is achieved through optical-flow-guided warping (SEA-RAFT) and an ARAP refinement stage inspired by HiFi4G [24].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By jointly optimizing reconstruction, tracking, and semantic understanding at the primitive level, Director demonstrates that instance-consistent constraints can simultaneously improve rendering fidelity and segmentation accuracy in highly dynamic scenes—a coupling that sequential pipelines cannot achieve. The open-vocabulary querying capability (identity + segment retrieval) makes the 4D representation directly usable for downstream tasks without post-hoc annotation. Key limitations include slow per-frame training (~10 min/frame), sensitivity to many hand-tuned loss weights, and a compact 6-dimensional semantic embedding that constrains representational richness. Open questions include whether the joint-optimization advantage persists in more diverse scene categories, whether the semantic bottleneck can be alleviated without prohibitive compute, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the controlled multi-view capture settings evaluated here.
