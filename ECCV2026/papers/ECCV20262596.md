## Problem

Existing large-scale dataset distillation methods rely on a decoupled SQUEEZE-RECOVER-RELABEL pipeline that implicitly compresses information twice (data→model parameters→synthetic images), causing severe information loss and distribution shift. This shift not only degrades cross-architecture generalization and inflates computational cost during the RECOVER stage, but also renders the pre-trained relabeler unreliable, producing sub-optimal labels that undermine the RELABEL strategy's effectiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262596/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an original labeled image dataset (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) and a frozen pre-trained model as the observer. It first selects N=4 representative images per class via RDED's patch-based selection, then initializes each distilled image by concatenating and resizing those N images into a single canvas. The output is a compact synthetic image set (IPC = 1, 10, 50, or 100) paired with multi-view soft labels generated by passing RandomCrop-augmented views of each distilled image through the pre-trained model.

## Method

CIM abandons the dual-compression SQUEEZE-RECOVER paradigm and instead directly minimizes an information gap between original and distilled samples. It formalizes the 'effective information' of a sample as the feature distribution induced by an observer group, defines the pairwise information gap as KL divergence between these distributions, and proves an upper bound (Theorem 1) that reduces the intractable KL to an expected squared L2 distance in feature space. Concretely, for each distilled image, RandomCrop generates N augmented views, and the loss (Eq. 9) aligns intermediate-layer features of the N original images with the corresponding views under multiple geometric transformations, balancing semantic and textural fidelity. An extended RELABEL stage then produces soft labels across all transformation views rather than a single one-shot prediction, and the student model is trained by matching its outputs to these multi-view soft labels.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with IPC=10 and ResNet-18, CIM achieves 48.7% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming the previous SOTA methods NRR-DD (46.1%) and DELT (45.8%) by 2.6% and 2.9% respectively. On Tiny-ImageNet at IPC=10, CIM reaches 53.3%, surpassing GIFT (49.2%) and RDED (48.4%). On CIFAR-10 at IPC=10 with ResNet-18, CIM achieves 66.2%, a substantial margin over RDED (47.3%) and SRe²L (39.4%). The full ImageNet-1K distillation at IPC=10 completes in 80 minutes on a single RTX-4090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIM outperforms all 15 baselines on the majority of dataset–IPC–architecture combinations. On ImageNet-1K IPC=10 (ResNet-18), CIM leads NRR-DD by 2.6% and DELT by 2.9%, with a larger gap over GIFT (43.2%), RDED (41.1%), WMDD (38.2%), G-VBSM (35.7%), Teddy (34.1%), and SRe²L (31.1%). At IPC=50, CIM (60.4%) edges out NRR-DD (60.1%) and DELT (59.2%); at IPC=100, CIM ties DELT at 62.4% while surpassing WMDD (60.7%) and CDA (58.0%). On small-scale datasets the margins are even wider: on CIFAR-10 IPC=10 (ResNet-18), CIM (66.2%) exceeds RDED (47.3%) by 18.9 points, and on CIFAR-100 IPC=10 it beats RDED (53.4%) by 8.3 points. In cross-architecture evaluation (Table 4, IPC=10), CIM's distilled data generalizes best to unseen backbones—e.g., 54.4% on DenseNet-121 vs. 47.4% for RDED and 43.2% for SRe²L—confirming that the information-gap minimization preserves both semantic and textural fidelity. The only baseline that matches CIM at ImageNet-1K IPC=100 is DELT (62.4% vs. 62.4%), and RDED is the sole method that beats CIM on Tiny-ImageNet IPC=50 (61.2% vs. 58.6%), likely due to its optimization-free patch-selection strategy at higher budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

CIM departs from the dominant information-extraction paradigm (SRe²L, G-VBSM, RDED, NRR-DD, DELT) that relies on a SQUEEZE→RECOVER→RELABEL pipeline, arguing that the implicit dual-compression (data→model→images) causes severe information loss and distribution shift. It builds on RDED's optimization-free subset selection for initializing distilled images and extends SRe²L's RELABEL stage with multi-transformation soft labels inspired by Shen & Xing's knowledge distillation framework. Conceptually, CIM replaces the inversion-based recovery with a direct, metric-driven optimization that minimizes a KL-divergence-bounded feature gap between original and synthetic samples, positioning itself as a unification of feature-matching and information-theoretic objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical demonstration (Proposition 1) that RELABEL degrades under distribution shift is a useful diagnostic for the field, explaining why prior extraction-based methods struggle with cross-architecture generalization and low-IPC settings. The practical efficiency gain—distilling ImageNet-1K at IPC=10 in 80 minutes on a single RTX-4090—makes large-scale dataset distillation accessible to individual researchers and small labs. Open questions include whether the information-gap formulation extends to ImageNet-21K or non-image modalities, whether the RDED selection mechanism is truly optimal (the paper shows it is selection-agnostic but RDED still wins), and whether the middle-layer alignment heuristic generalizes to architectures without clear depth hierarchies (e.g., ViTs).
