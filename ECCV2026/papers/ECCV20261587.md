## Problem

Streaming 3D reconstruction maintains a persistent latent state updated online from incoming frames, but the state update rule is the critical failure point: uniform overwrites (CUT3R) cause catastrophic forgetting, while heuristic attention-based gates (TTT3R) lack an explicit uncertainty representation, and both degrade beyond the training horizon. No prior method provides a principled, uncertainty-aware mechanism to balance memory retention against adaptation to new evidence.

## Input / Output

FILT3R operates as a drop-in replacement for the state-update step in a CUT3R-style recurrent backbone, consuming a stream of RGB frames and producing per-frame depth maps, camera pose trajectories, and 3D point cloud reconstructions. The filtering layer adds only one N-dimensional variance vector, one per-stream EMA scalar, and one N×D candidate buffer on top of the existing N×D persistent state, keeping the memory footprint identical to CUT3R (no attention-map caching). All hyperparameters are tuned once on a held-out development set and shared across all benchmarks.

## Method

FILT3R recasts the recurrent token update as an adaptive Kalman-style filter in latent token space, where the persistent state is a belief and the decoder's candidate is a noisy measurement. A per-token variance p_t is propagated via the standard prediction step (p_t⁻ = p_{t-1} + q_t) and updated with the Joseph form, yielding a gain k_t = p_t⁻/(p_t⁻ + r) that governs the interpolation s_t = s_{t-1} + k_t ⊙ (s̃_t − s_{t-1}). Process noise q_t is estimated online from EMA-normalized temporal drift of consecutive candidate tokens through a sigmoid gate, while measurement noise r is a fixed scalar anchor. In stable regimes the variance contracts and gains decay at O(1/t), extending the effective memory horizon; during genuine scene change, drift spikes inflate q_t and the gain rises to rapidly integrate new evidence. This formulation subsumes uniform overwrite (r→0) and fixed-gain EMA as special cases, and the ablations confirm that both adaptive process noise and recursive variance propagation are necessary for long-horizon stability.

## Results

On 7-Scenes 3D reconstruction at length 1000 (well beyond training horizon), FILT3R achieves mean Accuracy 0.054, Completeness 0.028, and Normal Consistency 0.535, compared to CUT3R (0.233/0.101/0.511) and TTT3R (0.145/0.051/0.525). On long-horizon camera pose (TUM-800), origin-aligned ATE drops from 0.493 (CUT3R) and 0.214 (TTT3R) to 0.107, while standard ATE improves from 0.173/0.097 to 0.057. On metric-scale depth (Bonn-500), FILT3R achieves Abs Rel 0.089 and δ<1.25 of 94.4%, versus 0.101/90.6% (CUT3R) and 0.100/92.1% (TTT3R). FILT3R maintains the same constant-memory footprint as CUT3R (3503 MB) while TTT3R nearly doubles memory (6294 MB) due to cached attention maps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FILT3R consistently outperforms CUT3R (uniform overwrite) across all three tasks and all sequence lengths, with the gap widening dramatically at longer horizons: on 7-Scenes at length 1000, Accuracy improves from 0.233 to 0.054 (4.3× reduction), and on TUM-800 pose, ATE_orig drops from 0.493 to 0.107 (4.6× reduction). Against TTT3R (heuristic attention-based gating), FILT3R also wins on every metric, with the most pronounced gains at length 1000 on 7-Scenes (Acc 0.145→0.054) and at 800 frames on TUM pose (ATE_orig 0.214→0.107), confirming that uncertainty propagation provides a principled advantage over instantaneous heuristic gates. Against Point3R (explicit spatial pointer memory), FILT3R achieves substantially better Accuracy on 7-Scenes at all lengths (e.g., 0.020 vs 0.045 at length 300) and avoids the OOM failure that Point3R suffers beyond ~600 frames, while remaining competitive on NRGBD at shorter horizons. The ablation further shows that a tuned fixed-β EMA (β=0.05) can marginally beat FILT3R on TUM-800 ATE (0.049 vs 0.057) but at the cost of sharply degrading rotation error (RPE-r 0.658 vs 0.362) and depth quality, highlighting that FILT3R's adaptive gain is necessary to simultaneously preserve local motion accuracy and long-horizon stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

FILT3R builds directly on CUT3R's recurrent streaming architecture (persistent latent tokens updated per frame) and the classical adaptive Kalman filtering framework of Mehra (1970), recasting the state-update problem as stochastic estimation in token space. It departs from prior streaming update rules—uniform overwrite (CUT3R) and heuristic attention-based gating (TTT3R)—by introducing per-token variance propagation and an adaptive process-noise estimate from EMA-normalized temporal drift, while deliberately fixing measurement noise to a scalar to avoid correlated-noise instability. The paper shows that both overwrite and fixed-gate policies are special cases of its Kalman-style gain, positioning FILT3R as a unifying, interpretable layer rather than a new backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, constant-memory, plug-and-play nature of FILT3R makes it immediately deployable on top of any frozen streaming 3D model, and the consistent long-horizon gains across depth, pose, and reconstruction (with the gap widening beyond the training horizon) address a core practical failure mode in online scene understanding. A notable negative result is that making measurement noise adaptive from attention statistics *worsens* long-horizon stability due to correlated co-excitation with process noise, suggesting that reliable per-token measurement-quality signals require external cues beyond internal attention. Open questions include extending the formulation to attention/KV-cache memories without an explicit recurrent state, and whether the linear-Gaussian assumption holds under more aggressive scene dynamics.
