## Problem

Existing neural SLAM methods built on geometric foundation models (e.g., MASt3R-SLAM, VGGT-SLAM) rely on aligning local submaps and suffer from global geometric inconsistency, while streaming variants (StreamVGGT, Stream3R) process new frames without revisiting past estimates, causing drift and mismatch. No prior work has integrated the full SLAM pipeline—incremental frontend tracking plus global backend refinement—into a single neural architecture, leaving a gap between the efficiency of streaming and the consistency of global optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262398/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a sequential stream of monocular RGB images, which are pre-encoded into patch tokens augmented with register tokens before entering the transformer backbone. Outputs include per-frame local pointmaps (not in a fixed world coordinate), confidence maps, and camera poses, all decoded from shared map tokens that serve as an implicit neural scene representation. Keyframe selection is triggered when relative translation to the last keyframe exceeds a threshold (τ=0.1), and the backend runs periodically every T=10 keyframes.

## Method

SLAM-Former is a single 36-layer transformer (initialized from Pi3 weights) that simultaneously acts as a causal frontend for incremental tracking/mapping and a full-attention backend for global refinement, distinguished only by attention masks and KV cache state. The frontend processes new keyframes using accumulated KV caches from prior frames, while the backend refines all map tokens in one forward pass with full attention, then writes its updated KV cache back to the frontend to reduce drift. Training jointly optimizes three modes in each iteration—causal (frontend), mixed (frontend conditioned on backend-refined cache), and full-attention (backend)—with a combined depth, pointmap, and relative-pose loss. A KV pruning mechanism based on Max-Min Diversity Problem retains a diverse subset of key/value pairs, reducing frontend complexity from O(n) to O(γn) and backend from O(n²) to O(γn²) while preserving reconstruction quality down to 12.5% retention.

## Results

On TUM RGB-D (uncalibrated), SLAM-Former achieves an average ATE RMSE of 0.039 m, outperforming the best uncalibrated baselines ViSTA-SLAM (0.052 m) and VGGT-SLAM (0.053 m) by roughly 25–30%. On 7-Scenes reconstruction, SLAM-Former achieves the best accuracy (0.017 m), completeness (0.037 m), and Chamfer distance (0.027 m), with accuracy ~47% lower than the next-best method. On Replica (uncalibrated tracking), SLAM-Former reaches 0.030 m average ATE, a ~50% reduction over SLAM3R (0.066 m) and the best uncalibrated baseline EC3R-SLAM (0.041 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLAM-Former outperforms all uncalibrated baselines across every tracking benchmark: on TUM RGB-D it beats ViSTA-SLAM (0.052 m) and VGGT-SLAM (0.053 m) by ~25–30% in average ATE; on 7-Scenes it leads ViSTA-SLAM (0.055 m) by 24%; and on Replica it surpasses EC3R-SLAM (0.041 m) by 27%. In the calibrated setting, SLAM-Former (0.039 m on TUM) is competitive with but slightly behind MASt3R-SLAM (0.030 m) and DROID-SLAM (0.038 m), which benefit from known intrinsics. For dense reconstruction on 7-Scenes, SLAM-Former achieves 0.017 m accuracy versus 0.025 m for EC3R-SLAM and 0.052 m for VGGT-SLAM, a 47% improvement over the next-best method. On Replica reconstruction, SLAM-Former (2.09/1.56 Acc./Comp.) edges out EC3R-SLAM (2.07/1.63) on accuracy and clearly leads on completeness. The ablation (Table 6) confirms the backend is critical: frontend-only TUM ATE degrades from 0.039 m to 0.134 m, a 3.4× increase, validating the frontend–backend cooperation design.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLAM-Former unifies the SLAM pipeline into a single transformer, departing from both traditional multi-model SLAM systems (ORB-SLAM, DROID-SLAM) and recent geometric-foundation-model SLAM (MASt3R-SLAM, VGGT-SLAM) that rely on pairwise or submap-wise alignment and suffer from global inconsistency. It also moves beyond streaming 3D reconstruction methods (StreamVGGT, CUT3R, Stream3R) that perform only causal incremental updates without revisiting past estimates. The architecture is initialized from Pi3's permutation-equivariant weights and borrows the Max-Min Diversity token pruning strategy from DivPrune, but the key departure is the three-mode joint training (causal frontend, mixed frontend-backend, full-attention backend) that enables a single set of weights to serve both incremental tracking and global refinement, with KV cache sharing as the cooperation mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully neural SLAM system with a complete frontend-backend pipeline in one transformer, and the conceptual equivalence between full-attention backend and loop-closure detection offers a clean neural replacement for a long-standing SLAM component. The KV pruning result (12.5% retention preserving accuracy) suggests sub-linear information growth in map tokens, opening a path toward near-linear complexity for long-sequence SLAM. Limitations include still trailing DROID-SLAM on clean synthetic data (Replica), heavy training cost (32 A100s, 11 hours), and evaluation limited to indoor scenes; outdoor, large-scale, and real-time deployment remain open questions.
