## Problem

Cross-embodiment dexterous grasping requires generating stable grasps for robot hands with vastly different kinematic structures (varying joint counts, link topologies, and coupling constraints) without per-hand architectural components or exhaustive grasp annotations. Existing interaction-oriented methods rely on fixed graph topologies or point-to-point distance matrices that cannot scale to morphologically diverse hands, while object-oriented methods depend on slow inverse-kinematics optimization that fails on complex geometries and partial observations.

## Input / Output

The method takes an object point cloud (512 uniformly sampled points with surface normals, normalized to an object-centric frame) and a robot hand URDF file parsed into L_e rigid links, each with a mesh from which 512 points with normals are uniformly sampled regardless of link size. It outputs per-link SE(3) poses (3D translation + axis-angle rotation) in the object-centric frame, which are then converted to executable joint angles via Pyroki inverse kinematics under URDF joint-limit constraints. The variable-length link token sequence means the model natively handles hands from 2-finger grippers to 21-link anthropomorphic hands without architectural changes.

## Method

SOMO reformulates dexterous grasping as a 3D assembly problem, predicting SE(3) poses for rigid links rather than hand-specific joint angles, so a single 8-layer transformer denoiser with AdaLN-Zero conditioning processes variable-length token sequences across all embodiments. A frozen TripoSG 3D VAE encoder maps both object point clouds and individual link meshes into a shared geometric latent space, enabling the cross-attention pathway to reason about shape complementarity between links and object surfaces. The key training innovation is a classifier-free paradigm where the unconditional branch is trained not on label-dropped data but on a morphology prior: physically valid hand configurations generated by forward-kinematic self-exploration (random joint sampling within URDF limits with a coordinated open/close blend), requiring no object-grasp annotations. At inference, classifier-free guidance composes the morphology prior (what valid configurations look like) with object conditioning (where to place links) via two forward passes per denoising step, enabling zero-shot transfer to hands that participated only in the morphology-prior path during training.

## Results

On 10 unseen objects across 7 robot hands, SOMO achieves 78.93% average grasp success rate (0.02 s/grasp), outperforming T(R,O) (63.72%, 0.03 s) and D(R,O) (64.45%, 0.39 s) by over 14 pp. With extended training data, SOMO improves to 81.02% while baselines remain flat (65.87% and 63.91%). In the zero-shot annotation-free setting, SOMO reaches 93.30% on Allegro and 80.40% on Barrett using only the morphology prior, versus 43.20% and 60.80% for D(R,O)'s CIP pretraining. SOMO degrades by only 13.4 pp when scaling from 3 to 7 hands, compared to 30.2 pp for T(R,O).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOMO decisively outperforms both interaction-oriented baselines in the full 7-hand setting: 78.93% vs. 63.72% (T(R,O)) and 64.45% (D(R,O)) average success, a gap of ~15 pp, while also being 2–20× faster (0.02 s vs. 0.03 s and 0.39 s per grasp). The advantage is most pronounced on morphologically challenging hands—LeapHand (65.30% vs. 59.00% and 42.00%) and EZGripper (97.20% vs. 39.30% and 78.80%)—where fixed-topology or point-to-point representations struggle. Against CEDex (compared only on 3 hands), results are mixed: SOMO leads on Allegro (94.80% vs. 88.1%) and Shadow (93.50% vs. 85.0%) but trails on Barrett (88.80% vs. 93.1%). In the annotation-free zero-shot setting, SOMO's morphology prior yields 36+ pp average improvement over D(R,O)'s CIP pretraining (e.g., 93.30% vs. 43.20% on Allegro, 32.40% vs. 2.80% on LeapHand), demonstrating that the classifier-free training paradigm is the key differentiator for unseen embodiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOMO reinterprets classifier-free guidance [8] by replacing the standard label-dropping unconditional path with a morphology prior learned from forward-kinematic self-exploration, so the unconditional branch encodes what valid hand configurations look like rather than merely 'no condition.' It draws on the 3D assembly literature [10,14,30] to reformulate grasping as per-link SE(3) pose prediction, sidestepping the fixed graph topologies and hand-crafted BPS features of T(R,O) [6] and the quadratic point-to-point distance matrices of D(R,O) [33]. The frozen TripoSG VAE [15] provides a shared geometric embedding for both link meshes and object surfaces, unifying the two domains in a way that prior cross-embodiment methods (which entangle morphology-specific geometry with interaction patterns) do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The annotation-free transfer capability—where a novel hand needs only its URDF and link meshes, with no teleoperation or simulation grasp data—directly addresses the linear cost bottleneck of curating per-embodiment datasets, making cross-embodiment deployment practically scalable. The 13.4 pp degradation from 3 to 7 hands (vs. >30 pp for baselines) suggests that morphological diversity can be a scaling advantage rather than an interference source, a property not observed in prior interaction-oriented methods. Open questions include whether the approach extends to cluttered scenes with inter-object contacts, whether tighter coupling between SE(3) generation and kinematic feasibility can reduce the residual IK failures on underactuated hands, and whether the real-world 80% success rate over 10 objects generalizes to broader sim-to-real gaps.
