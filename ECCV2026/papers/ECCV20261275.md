## Problem

Existing multi-modal OOD detection methods for action recognition exploit multi-modality only during training via regularization, then apply off-the-shelf unimodal OOD detectors at inference, discarding the complementary information across modalities. This paper identifies that the relationship between uni-modal and multi-modal predictions is a strong indicator of normality that is altered for OOD samples, and proposes the first post-hoc detector that explicitly leverages this multi-modal structure at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method operates on already-trained multi-modal action recognition networks (SlowFast encoders for RGB video and optical flow, optionally audio) and requires only a small validation set of ID samples to fit per-class linear mappings between uni-modal and multi-modal logits. It produces a single scalar OOD score per sample by combining a modality-aware prediction score, a feature-space Mahalanobis score, and the multi-modal logits in a normalized virtual-logit formulation. The approach is training-free and compatible with any existing multi-modal training procedure (A2D, DPU, FM).

## Method

The key insight is that for in-distribution samples, the multi-modal head's predictions can be closely approximated by a class-conditional linear mapping from the uni-modal heads' logits (Pearson correlation ≈ 0.998), but this relationship breaks down for OOD samples. The modality-aware score s(x) is the L2 distance between the softmax of the linearly-predicted multi-modal distribution and the actual multi-modal softmax. A complementary feature-space score r(x) captures off-manifold deviations via Mahalanobis distance in the low-variance principal component directions (linear invariants) of the concatenated multi-modal features, computed with Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage. Both scores are scaled to match the statistics of the highest multi-modal logit and integrated as virtual OOD logits alongside the C class logits in a normalized softmax (Eq. 9), so that confident class predictions suppress the OOD probability while abnormal multi-modal representations amplify it.

## Results

On the Kinetics-600 Far-OOD benchmark (the most challenging setting with the largest ID variety), the proposed modality-aware detector achieves an average FPR of 39.7 and AUC of 89.1, outperforming the next-best method (DPU_p) by 6.2 FPR points (a 13.5% relative improvement) while using the same trained network. On HMDB51 Far-OOD, the method reaches an average FPR of 10.1 and AUC of 97.0, and on Near-OOD detection it achieves 43.1 average FPR and 84.5 AUC. Across all 14 experiments on the Multi-OOD benchmark (5 datasets, up to 3 modalities), the method consistently outperforms all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three families of multi-modal OOD training methods, each evaluated with their best-performing off-the-shelf detector variant (probability-based, feature-based, or hybrid). On Kinetics-600 Far-OOD, the proposed method (39.7 avg FPR) outperforms A2D_p (50.2), A2D_f (52.1), A2D_h (54.5), FM (54.8), DPU_p (45.9), DPU_f (48.3), and DPU_h (48.3) — a margin of 6.2 FPR over the closest competitor (DPU_p) and up to 15.1 FPR over the weakest baseline. On HMDB51 Far-OOD, the method achieves 10.1 avg FPR vs. DPU_h's 11.1 and A2D_h's 13.2, with a 10.0 FPR advantage over FM (20.0). On Near-OOD, the improvement is smaller (43.1 vs. A2D_p's 44.4 avg FPR), and the method does not win on every individual dataset (e.g., Kinetics Near-OOD FPR of 63.0 vs. DPU_f's 66.6 is a win, but the margin is modest). Notably, the proposed method uses the same DPU-trained network weights as the DPU baselines, isolating the benefit purely to the inference-time scoring function. The method also generalizes to triple-modal settings (audio + video + flow) and to early/mid-fusion architectures, where it maintains a 5.2–5.8 FPR advantage over DPU variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two distinct lines of work: the data-invariants framework for feature-space OOD scoring (Doorenbos et al., ECCV 2022), which provides the Mahalanobis-based off-manifold detection in the multi-modal feature space, and the virtual-logit integration strategy from ViM (Wang et al., CVPR 2022), which motivates the final score normalization. It departs from the dominant paradigm in multi-modal OOD detection (A2D, DPU, FM), where all prior methods exploit modality relationships only during training via regularization while applying off-the-shelf uni-modal detectors at inference. The key conceptual departure is identifying that the linear relationship between uni-modal and multi-modal predictions—not the disagreement between uni-modal predictions, which motivated A2D—serves as a stronger and more direct OOD signal at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first post-hoc OOD detector that explicitly leverages multi-modality at inference, filling a gap where all prior multi-modal OOD methods discarded modality-specific information during scoring. The finding that the uni-to-multi-modal prediction relationship outperforms modality disagreement challenges the core assumption underlying A2D and its successors, suggesting the field's training-time regularization may be optimizing a weaker signal than necessary. A practical limitation is the reliance on architectures with exposed uni-modal classification heads, though the authors show this can be added post hoc. Open questions include whether the linear-mapping assumption holds for more than three modalities or for fundamentally heterogeneous modalities (e.g., text + video), and whether the approach extends to other multi-modal domains beyond action recognition.
