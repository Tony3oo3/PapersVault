## Problem

Existing memory-augmented video generation methods reconstruct a single global 3D scene from historical views to condition new generation, but pose and depth estimation errors cause the same surfaces to be placed at slightly different 3D locations across views. When fused, these cross-view misalignments accumulate into noisy geometry that produces ghosting, drift, and hallucinated content in rendered anchor videos, degrading long-horizon spatial consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261851/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a bank of per-frame local point clouds (estimated via TTT3R from historical or previously generated frames) along with a target camera trajectory and optional text prompt, and produces an 81-frame video segment at 480×832 resolution. The target trajectory is partitioned into chunks of D=8 frames, and up to K=4 local memories are retrieved per chunk, rendered as anchor clips, and fed as conditioning latents. Newly generated frames are converted back into local point-cloud memories and appended to the bank, enabling iterative extension beyond the 81-frame backbone limit.

## Method

AnchorWeave replaces a single fused global point cloud with a collection of per-frame local point-cloud memories, each stored with its camera pose in a shared world coordinate system, thereby avoiding cross-view misalignment accumulation. A coverage-driven retrieval strategy greedily selects up to K local memories per chunk that maximize additional visible-area coverage along the target trajectory, and each selected memory is rendered into an anchor video under the target poses. A multi-anchor weaving controller (ControlNet-style DiT blocks injected into the first third of backbone layers) jointly processes all K anchor latents via a shared cross-anchor attention module that enables information exchange across anchors, then fuses them through a pose-conditioned weighted sum where an MLP encodes retrieved-to-target relative camera poses into per-anchor importance weights. The fused control feature plus a target-camera-pose embedding are injected into the video diffusion backbone (CogVideoX-5B or Wan2.2-5B) during the first 90% of denoising steps, and the entire pipeline operates in an update-retrieve-generate loop for long-horizon generation.

## Results

On the partial-revisit evaluation over RealEstate10K and DL3DV (500 videos), AnchorWeave with the Wan2.2-5B backbone achieves the best overall quality score of 80.98 (VBench), with PSNR 21.04 and SSIM 0.6739, outperforming the strongest baseline SEVA (79.66 total, PSNR 21.13, SSIM 0.6711) on all perceptual quality dimensions. The CogVideoX-5B variant also leads on total quality (80.30) and SSIM (0.6727) over all baselines. Ablations confirm that local point-cloud memory improves PSNR by ~4.6 dB over global point-cloud conditioning (20.96 vs. 16.31), and increasing retrieval from K=1 to K=4 raises PSNR from 19.01 to 20.96.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnchorWeave (Wan2.2-5B) outperforms all seven baselines on total VBench quality (80.98 vs. 79.66 for SEVA, the closest competitor) and on every perceptual sub-metric (subject consistency, background consistency, motion smoothness, temporal flickering, aesthetic and imaging quality). On reconstruction fidelity, SEVA edges out AnchorWeave by a narrow margin on PSNR (21.13 vs. 21.04), while AnchorWeave leads on SSIM (0.6739 vs. 0.6711). Against single-anchor baselines (Gen3C, ViewCrafter, TrajCrafter, EPiC), AnchorWeave's K=1 variant already surpasses all of them on total quality and SSIM, and the full K=4 model widens the gap substantially (e.g., PSNR 21.04 vs. 18.23 for Gen3C). Memory-based baselines SPMem and Context-as-Memory trail AnchorWeave by 3–4 dB in PSNR and ~0.09 in SSIM, with SPMem suffering from severe hallucinations due to noisy global point-cloud rendering. The paper notes that AnchorWeave's advantage over SEVA is most pronounced in temporal quality (motion smoothness 96.45 vs. 94.31; temporal flickering 94.15 vs. 91.00), attributed to preserving the pretrained video backbone's motion prior rather than replacing it with multi-view conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnchorWeave builds directly on SPMem [92], which introduced the iterative update-retrieve-generate loop for long-horizon video generation conditioned on a globally fused 3D point cloud. The key conceptual departure is rejecting global 3D reconstruction as the memory substrate: instead of fusing per-frame geometry into a single scene (which accumulates cross-view misalignment), AnchorWeave retains independent per-frame local point clouds and learns to reconcile their residual inconsistencies through a multi-anchor weaving controller with shared attention and pose-guided fusion. This positions the paper within the broader camera-controllable video generation lineage (Gen3C, ViewCrafter, TrajCrafter, EPiC) and memory-augmented generation (Context-as-Memory, SEVA, Spatia, MagicWorld), but distinguishes itself by treating multi-view geometric reconciliation as a learnable generation-time problem rather than a reconstruction-time one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that local geometric memories substantially outperform globally fused point clouds (≈4.6 dB PSNR gap) challenges the prevailing assumption in 3D-informed video generation that a unified scene representation is necessary for spatial consistency, suggesting that learned reconciliation at generation time can be more robust than geometric fusion at reconstruction time. This opens a path toward scalable long-horizon world models where memory grows incrementally without the compounding error of global reconstruction. Open questions include how the approach handles dynamic content (moving objects, lighting changes), whether the coverage-driven retrieval scales efficiently to very long histories (thousands of frames), and whether the multi-anchor overhead can be reduced without sacrificing the consistency gains.
