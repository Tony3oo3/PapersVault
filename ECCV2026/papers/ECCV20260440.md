## Problem

Video temporal grounding models trained under a closed-world assumption exhibit systematic temporal hallucinations in open-world deployment, confidently producing spurious intervals for events that do not exist in the video. Existing mitigations rely on offline-constructed negative samples or static relevance classifiers, which learn fixed fabrication patterns and fail under distribution shifts or unseen hallucination triggers. The core gap is the absence of a mechanism to dynamically calibrate the model's decision boundary at inference time, distinguishing genuine evidence-supported grounding from null-attribution states.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an untrimmed video (encoded as video-modality tokens) and a natural language query, processed through a frozen Qwen2.5-VL backbone with a LoRA adapter. The model autoregressively generates a structured text completion containing a rationale followed by an <answer> field; a deterministic parser g(·) extracts either a (start, end) temporal interval or an evidence-absence signal ('not found'). The absence decision is restricted to the <answer> tag to prevent misclassifying negative phrasing in the reasoning chain, and video features are cached across streaming steps to support online updates without re-encoding.

## Method

HRVTG is a test-time adaptation framework that reformulates VTG as an online GRPO policy optimization problem on a frozen grounding backbone. For each incoming positive sample, three self-supervised counterfactual probes are constructed on-the-fly: textual alteration (action reversal, temporal misalignment, attribute substitution), visual pruning (excising the pseudo-GT evidence interval), and hybrid fabrication (generating a query from a pseudo hard-negative clip while removing it from the video). The GRPO update is guided by three verifiable rewards: a consistency anchor reward (IoU ≥ δ against the frozen reference on positive samples), a hallucination repulsion reward (rejection keyword on counterfactual samples), and a lightweight format reward. An asymmetric KL elastic constraint applies a stronger regularization weight ρ > 1 on positive samples to preserve grounding while allowing greater policy deviation on counterfactual samples to facilitate selective abstention, enabling the model to continuously reshape its decision boundary throughout inference without any human annotation.

## Results

On Charades-RF, HRVTG (3B) improves mIoU_Cond from 41.48 to 43.29 and F1_Balanced from 45.12 to 68.30 over the Only-positive baseline, while the 7B model lifts F1_Balanced from 78.27 to 81.94. On ActivityNet-RF, the 3B model achieves a dramatic F1_Balanced gain from 60.51 to 83.91 with mIoU_Cond rising from 26.61 to 28.03. On the more challenging TVGBench-RF (covering all three hallucination types), the 3B model's F1_Balanced surges from 9.78 to 58.83, demonstrating strong robustness against complex multimodal misattributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares HRVTG against two baselines: the Base Model (Qwen2.5-VL without VTG fine-tuning) and the Only-positive Model (fine-tuned exclusively on genuine positive samples, representing the traditional always-answer paradigm). HRVTG consistently outperforms both on all metrics across all three benchmarks. Against the Only-positive Model, the most striking gain is in F1_Balanced: +23.18 points on Charades-RF (3B), +23.40 on ActivityNet-RF (3B), and +49.05 on TVGBench-RF (3B), where the Only-positive model nearly collapses (F1_Bal = 9.78) due to its inability to reject fabricated queries. Grounding metrics (mIoU_Cond, R@0.3) are also slightly improved or preserved, confirming that the consistency anchor reward prevents catastrophic forgetting. The Base Model, lacking any VTG training, is substantially weaker on grounding but sometimes shows higher F1_Rfs due to its general tendency to refuse, making it a less informative comparison. The Only-positive Model is the most relevant baseline, and HRVTG's advantage over it is the core contribution: achieving selective prediction without sacrificing localization quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of MLLM-based video temporal grounding (VTimeLLM, Time-R1, TimeZero) and test-time adaptation (Tent, CoTTA, TTRL), but departs sharply from prior selective-prediction VTG work (RaTSG, CausalVTG, RA-RFT) that learns a static rejection boundary via offline negative samples. The key conceptual shift is moving calibration from training time to inference time: rather than baking a fixed refusal policy into weights, HRVTG uses self-supervised counterfactual probes and GRPO to reshape the decision boundary on the fly over a frozen backbone. The optimization machinery is borrowed directly from DeepSeek-R1's GRPO, while the pseudo-GT intervals that anchor all three probe types come from Time-R1's strong grounder, making the framework a test-time policy layer atop an existing generative VTG model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The always-answer bias in VTG is a genuine deployment blocker for high-stakes applications (surveillance, medical video, robotics), and this paper offers a practical, annotation-free remedy that can be bolted onto any frozen MLLM grounder without retraining. However, the experimental validation is limited: the paper compares only against a base model and an only-positive fine-tuned model, omitting direct comparisons to the most relevant prior selective-prediction methods (RaTSG, CausalVTG, RA-RFT) that it critiques in related work. Open questions include whether the counterfactual probe pipeline scales to long-form or multi-event videos, how sensitive the approach is to the quality of the frozen reference grounder, and whether the streaming TTA assumption (i.i.d. test stream) holds in real deployment where query distributions shift non-stationarily.
