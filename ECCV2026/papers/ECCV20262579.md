## Problem

Existing multi-view vision pipelines that combine point and line features rely on separate, heavy networks for each feature type and CPU-bound LSD post-processing with quadratic complexity, making real-time or embedded deployment impractical. No prior joint point-line extractor achieves state-of-the-art quality on both feature types while remaining lightweight enough for efficient inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262579/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (trained at 800×800, evaluated up to 1600 px on the long side) and produces, in one forward pass, a set of 2D keypoints with subpixel-accurate coordinates, a 128-dimensional descriptor per keypoint, and a set of line segments defined by their endpoints. Line segments are not directly regressed by the network; instead, the network predicts a dense distance field that is converted to segments via a GPU-accelerated LSD post-processing stage, with the gradient orientation computed on-the-fly rather than predicted by the network.

## Method

UPAL builds on the ALIKED-n16 encoder (four convolutional blocks with deformable convolutions in the last two) and adds three lightweight decoder branches: a Score Map Head with Differentiable Keypoint Detection for keypoints, a Sparse Deformable Descriptor Head for descriptors, and a three-convolution branch that predicts only a line distance field (omitting the angle field that DeepLSD regresses, which the authors show is detrimental). Training is a two-stage knowledge-distillation procedure: the distance-field branch is first trained against DeepLSD ground truth, then the encoder is frozen while the keypoint and descriptor branches are distilled from SuperPoint+DaD heatmaps and ALIKED descriptors respectively. The LSD post-processing is accelerated by moving gradient and angle computation to the GPU, downsampling the seed-point grid with stride 2, retaining only the top 20% of pixels by distance-field value, and parallelizing the angle computation, reducing LSD latency from ~233 ms to ~11 ms with no performance loss. The resulting model has only 0.78 M parameters, achieving a 4× GPU speedup and 10× smaller memory footprint over the ALIKED + DeepLSD pipeline while matching or exceeding its accuracy on homography estimation, relative pose, line repeatability, and visual localization.

## Results

UPAL achieves a 4× GPU speedup (70 ms vs. 286 ms) and a 12× smaller parameter count (0.78 M vs. 9.2 M) over the ALIKED + DeepLSD pipeline while matching or exceeding its performance. On the 7Scenes Stairs visual localization benchmark, UPAL with points + lines attains 54.6% pose accuracy at 5 cm/5°, outperforming the best prior point-line method (Wireframe, 53.8%) and all point-only baselines. On HPatches line detection, UPAL achieves the highest repeatability (61.9% at 5 px) among all compared detectors, and on ScanNet indoor relative pose estimation it leads all lightweight methods (AUC@5° = 15.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UPAL outperforms all lightweight point baselines (SIFT, SuperPoint, DISK, XFeat, ALIKED) on indoor ScanNet pose estimation and HPatches homography, while remaining competitive on outdoor MegaDepth (AUC@5°: 58.2 vs. ALIKED's 59.8). Against heavyweight methods (DeDoDe v2, DaD+DeDoDe v2, Ripe, RDD), UPAL matches or exceeds accuracy at a fraction of the latency (e.g., 80 ms vs. 485 ms for DaD+DeDoDe v2 on MegaDepth). In line detection (Table 4), UPAL achieves the highest repeatability across all thresholds on both HPatches and RDNIM, surpassing DeepLSD (its teacher) on RDNIM (13.3% vs. 10.1% at 1 px) and ScaleLSD, while being 3.4× faster than DeepLSD (141 ms vs. 473 ms). Among joint point-line extractors, UPAL beats Wireframe and PLNet on every evaluated task (homography, pose estimation, line detection, visual localization) while using 7–10× fewer parameters. The only baseline that edges out UPAL is DISK on ETH3D 3D-reconstruction completeness (4.41% vs. 4.45% at 5 cm), where DISK's reinforcement-learning-trained keypoints are particularly well-suited to the matching protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

UPAL unifies the ALIKED point-feature architecture (encoder, DKD keypoint detection, SDDH deformable descriptors) with a lightweight line-detection branch adapted from DeepLSD, which itself builds on the classical LSD algorithm. Rather than training from scratch, the paper uses a student-teacher distillation framework where SuperPoint and DaD supervise keypoints, ALIKED supervises descriptors, and DeepLSD supervises the line distance field. This departs from earlier joint point-line extractors (PLNet, Wireframe) that attempted end-to-end joint prediction but underperformed their individual SOTA counterparts; UPAL instead shows that a compact network can match or exceed those teachers by selectively distilling the best component from each.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× speedup and 10× memory reduction over the ALIKED + DeepLSD pipeline make hybrid point-line pipelines viable on embedded and low-compute platforms, broadening access to geometric perception beyond high-end GPU servers. The finding that only three additional convolutional layers on a point extractor suffice for SOTA line detection, and that removing DeepLSD's angle-field prediction actually improves accuracy, challenges the assumption that more learned components are needed for line geometry. Open questions include whether a jointly learned point-line matcher (beyond the current endpoint-matching + Sinkhorn assignment) could further close the gap to heavyweight GNN-based matchers, and whether the distillation approach generalizes to other feature types (e.g., junctions, curves).
