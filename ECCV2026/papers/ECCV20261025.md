## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models fail on rare attribute-object pairings (e.g., 'a thorny dolphin') because the high-frequency prior of the base object suppresses the rare attribute during denoising, causing attribute omission or semantic drift. Existing training-free fixes like R2F substitute rare tokens with frequent proxies but do not monitor or correct the object-attribute balance step-by-step, leaving the trajectory free to drift toward one dominant component.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt decomposed into one object term and m attribute terms, along with one reference image per concept (object and each attribute) that serves a dual role: as a CLIP similarity target for per-step monitoring and as an IP-Adapter image-prompt condition. It produces a single RGB image at the backbone's native resolution (SD 3.5, 24 denoising steps) in which the rare attribute is coherently expressed on the base object. No additional training data or fine-tuning is required; all reference images are prepared offline per prompt.

## Method

RADIANCE reframes inference as a closed-loop feedback controller with three training-free modules operating on a pretrained flow-based diffusion backbone. The Compositional Similarity Monitor (CSM) decodes each intermediate latent via the VAE and computes CLIP cosine similarities against the object and attribute reference images, yielding per-step signals that reveal which component is dominating or lagging. The Bidirectional Scale Controller (BSC) translates these signals into a reactive restoring force by applying both positive and negative IP-Adapter scales: positive scales reinforce a lagging concept while negative scales actively suppress a dominant one, a capability the paper identifies as critical for correction rather than purely additive guidance. The Feedback Guidance Scheduler (FGS) decides when to intervene by comparing similarity trends between consecutive steps and a T_early threshold that separates early concept formation from late structural refinement, setting scales to (1,−1), (−1,1), or (0,0) accordingly. For multi-object prompts, two additional mechanisms—Delayed Adapter Activation (disabling IP-Adapter for the first T_delay steps) and Layer-wise Alternating Guidance (cycling reference images across transformer layers)—prevent premature fusion of distinct objects while preserving individual identities.

## Results

On RareBench (the primary benchmark for rare concept generation), RADIANCE with SD 3.5 achieves an overall GPT-4o compositional score of 84.7, outperforming the SOTA baseline R2F (81.7) by 3.0 points and the raw SD 3.5 backbone (75.9) by 8.8 points. The largest gains appear in Property (97.5 vs. 90.0 for R2F) and Shape (89.4 vs. 82.5), while Texture scores are comparable (89.4 vs. 89.4), suggesting backbone saturation in material binding. On T2I-CompBench, RADIANCE reaches 75.9 overall, surpassing R2F (72.4), FLUX (74.9), and SD 3.5 (74.2), with the most notable margin in multi-object Shape (61.8 vs. 51.6 for R2F).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RADIANCE (SD 3.5) achieves the best overall score of 84.7 on RareBench, beating the closest SOTA baseline R2F (SD 3.5, 81.7) by 3.0 points, with the largest margins in Property (+7.5) and Shape (+6.9); R2F retains a slight edge only in Relation (64.4 vs. 63.1). Against the raw SD 3.5 backbone (75.9), RADIANCE improves by 8.8 points overall, and against FLUX (67.3) the gap widens to 17.4 points. On T2I-CompBench, RADIANCE (75.9) outperforms R2F (72.4) by 3.5 points, with the most pronounced advantage in multi-object Shape (61.8 vs. 51.6) and multi-object Texture (71.3 vs. 68.3), while R2F edges ahead slightly in single-object Color (89.1 vs. 88.6). Attention-based methods (InitNO, 63.0) and LLM-grounded planners (RPG, 36.4; LMD, 30.7) trail substantially, confirming that static or layout-based guidance cannot match the adaptive feedback loop. In user studies, RADIANCE is preferred over R2F in 69.2% of pairwise comparisons overall (84.6% on basic prompts), and over SD 3.5 in 74.3% of comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

RADIANCE builds on IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention mechanism for image-prompt conditioning and CLIP-based similarity scoring, but reframes compositional T2I inference as a closed-loop feedback control problem rather than a one-shot guidance problem. It departs from prompt-level strategies like R2F (which substitutes rare tokens with frequent proxies at selected timesteps) and from static attention-based methods (Attend-and-Excite, SynGen) by introducing real-time monitoring of object-attribute balance and bidirectional (positive and negative) scale correction. The multi-object extension via Delayed Adapter Activation and Layer-wise Alternating Guidance is a novel architectural scheduling strategy not present in prior compositional or rare-concept generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, backbone-agnostic design (validated on both SD 1.5 and SD 3.5) makes RADIANCE a practical plug-in for existing T2I pipelines, and the feedback-control framing offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc prompt engineering for compositional generation. The key open limitation is spatial reasoning: the paper acknowledges that high-order spatial arrangements (left-of, behind) remain governed by the backbone's geometric priors, leaving a gap for densely populated scenes. Future work on integrating the semantic controller with spatially-aware guidance or cross-object interaction modeling is a natural next step, and the negative-scale IP-Adapter finding opens the door to more general 'suppressive' conditioning in diffusion models.
