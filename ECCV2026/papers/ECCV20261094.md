## Problem

Existing zero-shot skeleton-based action recognition methods face a fundamental topological mismatch between skeleton and text manifolds: contrastive approaches enforce only soft alignment without explicit intra-class compactness, while stochastic generative models (VAEs, diffusion) produce dispersed features that drift from true class centers. Additionally, both paradigms suffer from domain shift, as prototypes inferred purely from seen-class semantics fail to align with the actual distribution of unseen test samples during static inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261094/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes skeleton sequences (J joints × 3 coordinates × F frames × P subjects) encoded by a pretrained Shift-GCN or ST-GCN, paired with GPT-4-generated text descriptions at two granularities (holistic summary and phase-wise decomposition into initiation/execution/completion), encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder. At inference, the model synthesizes pseudo-skeleton features per candidate class via ODE integration and matches the real skeleton feature against these pseudo-features using maximum cosine similarity, then fuses this generative score with a rectification score computed from a running memory bank of high-confidence test predictions to produce the final class label.

## Method

ProtoFM reframes zero-shot skeleton action recognition from heterogeneous skeleton-text alignment to homogeneous skeleton–pseudo-skeleton matching by learning a Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) Transformer that transports Gaussian noise to the skeleton feature distribution along a deterministic ODE trajectory conditioned on concatenated global and local CLIP text features. A Prototype-Guided Center (PGC) loss anchors one-step ODE projections of intermediate flow states to learnable class prototypes (with a stop-gradient warm-up to stabilize early training), enforcing intra-class compactness, while a Triplet Flow Matching loss enforces inter-class separability by penalizing alignment error for mismatched text-skeleton pairs. At inference, a training-free Prototype Memory Bank Guided Rectification (PMGR) strategy generates per-class support sets via ODE sampling, maintains a priority-based memory bank of high-confidence test features, and fuses the generative matching score with a rectification score from empirically computed visual prototypes to mitigate domain shift without additional training.

## Results

On NTU RGB+D 60 (XSub), ProtoFM achieves 87.3% ZSL accuracy on the 55/5 split and 73.2% on the 48/12 split, with GZSL harmonic means of 76.1% and 61.5% respectively. On NTU RGB+D 120 (XSub), it reaches 82.9% ZSL accuracy (110/10 split) and 69.5% (96/24 split), with GZSL H of 64.2% and 56.0%. Under the random-split protocol (Table 2), ProtoFM attains 94.1% ZSL and 78.2% GZSL on NTU-60, 72.1%/62.1% on NTU-120, and 71.5%/55.6% on PKU-MMD I, outperforming all baselines across every dataset and setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProtoFM achieves the best ZSL accuracy across all four standard splits in Table 1, outperforming the nearest competitors by clear margins: on NTU-60 55/5 it leads Neuron [3] and FS-VAE [35] (both 86.9%) by 0.4 points, while on the more challenging 48/12 split it surpasses Neuron (62.7%) by 10.5 points and FS-VAE (57.2%) by 16.0 points. On NTU-120 110/10, ProtoFM (82.9%) exceeds DVTA [20] (74.9%) by 8.0 points and TDSM [9] (74.2%) by 8.7 points; on 96/24 it beats TDSM (65.1%) by 4.4 points. In the GZSL setting, ProtoFM's harmonic means also lead: 76.1% on NTU-60 55/5 (vs. FS-VAE 75.7%), 61.5% on 48/12 (vs. Neuron 59.1%), 64.2% on NTU-120 110/10 (vs. FS-VAE 63.3%), and 56.0% on 96/24 (vs. FS-VAE 54.7%). Under the random-split protocol (Table 2), ProtoFM extends its lead to 94.1% ZSL on NTU-60 (vs. TDSM 88.9%) and 72.1% on NTU-120 (vs. TDSM 69.5%), while on PKU-MMD I it matches FS-VAE's 71.2% ZSL with a slight edge (71.5%) and achieves the best GZSL (55.6% vs. 54.9% for SCoPLe). No baseline is competitive with ProtoFM on any of the reported splits or datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtoFM departs from the dominant ZSSAR paradigm of heterogeneous cross-modal alignment (skeleton ↔ text) by reframing the problem as homogeneous same-domain matching: a conditional flow matching Transformer synthesizes pseudo-skeleton features from text via deterministic ODE integration, so the final classification compares real skeleton features against generated pseudo-skeleton features in a shared space. Conceptually, it inherits the generative-alignment lineage of VAE-based methods (SynSE, SA-DVAE, FS-VAE) and the diffusion-based TDSM, but replaces stochastic sampling with a deterministic transport path, and adds a prototype-guided geometric regularizer absent in prior generative ZSSAR work. The test-time memory-bank rectification strategy extends the test-time adaptation thread (PGFA, DynaPURLS, Skeleton-Cache) into the generative setting, using accumulated high-confidence predictions to calibrate class prototypes without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing from cross-modal alignment to same-domain generative matching offers a cleaner inductive bias for ZSSAR: by eliminating the skeleton-text modality gap at inference, decision boundaries become tighter and more interpretable, and the deterministic ODE path avoids the sampling variance that plagues VAE/diffusion baselines. The large ZSL gap on harder splits (e.g., +10.5 over Neuron on NTU-60 48/12) suggests the generative approach scales better as the number of unseen classes grows, though the paper does not ablate this scaling behavior explicitly. Open questions include whether the GPT-4-generated descriptions and frozen CLIP encoder are bottlenecks for truly open-world action vocabularies, whether the flow-matching advantage over diffusion is consistent across architectures, and how the method behaves when the pretrained skeleton encoder has not seen the target domain at all.
