## Problem

Existing video quality metrics fail to diagnose 3D spatial geometric inconsistencies in generated videos: fidelity-centric metrics like FVD are insensitive to geometric warping, while consistency-focused metrics like MEt3R and VBench are confounded by valid foreground motion. No established metric explicitly isolates and quantifies the stability of the static 3D background amidst complex dynamic content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260419/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of RGB video frames as input and leverages four off-the-shelf estimators (VGGT for global camera poses and intrinsics, Video Depth Anything for per-frame dense depth, DELTA for dense 2D point tracking, and SegAnyMo for moving-object segmentation) without any additional training. It outputs a single scalar SGC score (lower is better) per video, decomposed into component scores: local inter-segment rotational/translational variance, global pose rotational/translational variance, and cross-frame depth consistency error, all computed per consecutive frame pair and aggregated over the sequence.

## Method

SGC is grounded in the physical principle that all static background points must be consistent with a single shared camera transformation. The pipeline first isolates the static background via SegAnyMo, then partitions it into depth-consistent sub-regions using k-means clustering on 1D depth values. For each sub-region, a local relative camera pose is estimated via PnP using 2D feature tracks (DELTA) and their back-projected 3D points (from depth and intrinsics). Geometric inconsistency is quantified by measuring the variance among these local poses and their deviation from the global VGGT pose, supplemented by a depth-warping error term. The final SGC score is a PCA-weighted sum of Z-score-normalized component metrics, with weights learned once on a calibration dataset to avoid subjective tuning.

## Results

On the unified benchmark of 1,296 videos, real-world anchors establish the lower bound for SGC: OpenVid (0.0530), nuScenes (0.0613), and RT-1 (0.0639). Among 10 generative models, Cosmos (V2V) achieves the best SGC score of 0.0722, while Latte (0.3226), Modelscope (0.3129), and Seine (0.2837) exhibit the most severe geometric inconsistencies. SGC rankings diverge sharply from fidelity-centric metrics: Latte and Modelscope score well on FVD (~800) and VBench-BC (>0.91) yet rank poorly on SGC, revealing that high per-frame texture quality masks underlying 3D structural collapse. Controlled perturbation experiments confirm SGC responds monotonically to geometric degradation (e.g., Spatial Depth Warp inflates nuScenes SGC from 0.0613 to 0.2252) while remaining robust to camera motion (R² ≤ 0.27 with ECD on real data).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGC produces fundamentally different rankings than all five baseline categories. Against MEt3R and MEt3R(+MOS) (2D feature-consistency metrics), SGC identifies Cosmos as the best generative model (SGC 0.0722 vs. MEt3R 0.0741), but the gap widens for weaker models: Latte scores 0.3226 on SGC yet only 0.1707 on MEt3R, revealing that 2D texture persistence masks 3D structural collapse. Against VBench (BC, DD, weighted composite), the divergence is stark: Latte and Modelscope achieve VBench-BC > 0.91 and FVD ~800, yet SGC scores of 0.32 and 0.31 place them near the worst, while VideoCrafter (SGC 0.097) ranks far above them on VBench-weighted (0.71 vs. 0.44/0.33). Against FVD, SGC is nearly orthogonal—FVD rewards per-frame appearance and cannot distinguish geometrically coherent from incoherent videos with similar textures. Against FVMD and TRAJAN (motion/physics proxies), SGC complements rather than conflicts: FVMD measures object kinematics while SGC isolates background rigidity, and TRAJAN's trajectory-based scoring captures different failure modes. The key takeaway is that SGC exposes critical 3D geometric failures (warping, perspective breakdown, object impermanence) that all five baseline categories systematically miss, making it a necessary complementary diagnostic rather than a replacement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of video quality assessment and 3D geometric reasoning, departing from both fidelity-centric metrics (FVD, CLIPScore) that ignore geometric structure and consistency-focused metrics (MEt3R, VBench-BC, FVMD) that are confounded by foreground dynamics. Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes off-the-shelf components—VGGT for global camera poses, Video Depth Anything for temporally consistent depth, SegAnyMo for motion segmentation, DELTA for dense tracking, and classical PnP for local pose estimation—into a unified diagnostic pipeline whose core novelty is the physical principle that all static background points must obey a single camera transformation, quantified via variance among depth-partitioned local pose estimates. The PCA-based objective weighting of component metrics and the explicit static-background isolation represent methodological departures from prior consistency benchmarks that either use manual weighting or evaluate the full frame.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SGC fills a concrete evaluation gap: it reveals that several SOTA generative models (Latte, Modelscope, Seine) exhibit severe 3D geometric failures invisible to FVD and VBench-BC, providing a diagnostic signal that can guide training toward truly spatially coherent outputs. The finding that Cosmos achieves near-real-world SGC scores yet high MEt3R(+MOS) error suggests 3D structural preservation and 2D texture persistence are genuinely distinct axes, reframing how the community should interpret consistency metrics. Open questions include whether SGC can be differentiable enough to serve as a training signal, how it behaves on very long or highly dynamic sequences where depth estimation degrades, and whether the PCA weighting generalizes across domains without recalibration. The reliance on foundational estimators (VGGT, Video Depth Anything) trained primarily on static scenes remains a potential vulnerability for extreme dynamic content.
