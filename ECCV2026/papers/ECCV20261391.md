## Problem

Existing stereo matching methods produce noisy, ambiguous cost volumes in early feature matching, forcing reliance on heavy 3D convolutions, large pre-trained backbones, or monocular depth priors to recover accurate disparities. This post-processing overhead is the primary bottleneck for efficiency and real-time deployment, and it limits cross-domain generalization. The paper asks whether stabilizing the cost volume at the source can eliminate these expensive corrective mechanisms while preserving accuracy.

## Input / Output

LiteMatch takes a rectified stereo image pair (left and right RGB views) and produces a full-resolution disparity map. Cost volume computation operates at one-fourth resolution, with a learned convex upsampling mask recovering full-resolution output in a single forward pass (base model) or a small number of GRU-based refinement steps (IR variant). The model is trained exclusively on Scene Flow (35,454 pairs) and evaluated zero-shot on KITTI 2012/2015, Middlebury-F, ETH3D, and DrivingStereo, with inference measured at 384×1248 resolution.

## Method

LiteMatch employs a dual-encoder architecture: a Cross-View Correspondence Encoder (CVCE) using multi-head cross-attention with a learnable scale to model dense left-right correspondence, and a High-Frequency Encoder (HFE) that processes FFT high-pass-filtered inputs to preserve edges and texture; the two streams are fused via a learnable sigmoid gate initialized to zero. The key training innovation is the CVC-Loss, a voxel-wise binary cross-entropy applied to softmax-normalized cost distributions that simultaneously maximizes the correct disparity bin probability and explicitly suppresses every incorrect bin, yielding 35% lower entropy and faster convergence than standard categorical cross-entropy. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 jointly optimizes the encoders and cost volume with CVC-Loss plus smooth L1, while Stage 2 freezes all Stage-1 parameters and trains a transformer-based refinement module (disparity-context encoder + correspondence encoder + hierarchical decoder) with regression loss. The base model performs single-pass non-iterative refinement (3.36M params, 22 FPS), and an optional GRU-based iterative refinement head (9.58M params) achieves SOTA accuracy with fewer iterations than competing iterative methods, all without 3D convolutions, monocular priors, or large pre-trained backbones.

## Results

LiteMatch (9.58M params) achieves the best zero-shot EPE on Middlebury-F (4.11) and ETH3D (0.21), and the best Bad3.0 on KITTI-2015 (4.09%) among all methods without monocular priors, while using 39× fewer parameters than MonSter (388.8M). On Scene Flow (in-domain), LiteMatch attains 0.39 EPE and 1.92% D1-all, outperforming MonSter (0.37 EPE, 2.02% D1) and Mocha-Stereo (0.41 EPE, 2.35% D1). On DrivingStereo (zero-shot), LiteMatch achieves the best average D1-all of 2.00%, beating MonSter (2.69%) and DEFOM-Stereo (2.05%). The base model (3.36M params) runs at 22.2 FPS, roughly 2–8× faster than all compared iterative methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiteMatch (9.58M) outperforms all stereo-only baselines without monocular priors: it beats IGEV-Stereo (12.6M) by 0.9–1.0 px on KITTI Bad3.0 and by 0.12 px on ETH3D EPE, and surpasses Mocha-Stereo (97.1M) by 1.97 px on Middlebury-F EPE while using 10× fewer parameters. Against monocular-prior methods, LiteMatch matches or exceeds MonSter (388.8M) on Middlebury-F (EPE 4.11 vs 4.19, Bad2.0 15.1% vs 15.2%) and ETH3D EPE (0.21 vs 0.24), though MonSter retains a slight edge on KITTI-2015 Bad3.0 (4.00 vs 4.09) and ETH3D Bad1.0 (2.00 vs 2.05). On DrivingStereo zero-shot, LiteMatch achieves 2.00% average D1-all, beating MonSter (2.69%) by 0.69 points and DEFOM-Stereo (2.05%) by 0.05 points, with particularly large gains in Foggy conditions (1.51% vs 2.94% for MonSter). On the ETH3D fine-tuned leaderboard, LiteMatch achieves the best Bad1.0 (0.35% Noc, 0.52% All), surpassing MonSter (0.44%/0.70%) and DEFOM-Stereo (0.70%/0.78%). The base model (3.36M) already outperforms IGEV-Stereo and Selective-IGEV on KITTI-2015 EPE (1.21 vs 1.35/1.34) while running at 22.2 FPS versus 16.1/14.5 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiteMatch departs from the dominant paradigm of correcting noisy cost volumes through heavy 3D convolutions (PSMNet, GA-Net) or many iterative refinement steps (RAFT-Stereo, IGEV-Stereo, Selective-IGEV), arguing instead that instability should be addressed at the source via distribution-level supervision (CVC-Loss) and complementary feature extraction (cross-attention + FFT-based high-frequency filtering). It also explicitly rejects the recent trend toward monocular depth priors and large pretrained backbones (MonSter, FoundationStereo, DEFOM-Stereo), positioning itself as a pure-stereo, prior-free alternative. The iterative refinement head borrows the GRU-based update mechanism from RAFT-Stereo, and the frequency-aware component draws on Selective-IGEV's motivation, but the core contribution is the reframing of cost volume stabilization as a training-time loss design problem rather than an architectural one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for resource-constrained deployment: a 9.58M-parameter model matching or exceeding 388M-parameter monocular-prior methods on ETH3D and DrivingStereo while running at 22 FPS with ~1.4 GB memory opens the door to real-time stereo on edge devices. The CVC-Loss is a simple, potentially transferable idea (voxel-wise BCE on softmax-normalized costs) that could benefit other cost-volume-based methods beyond stereo. Open questions include whether the two-stage training protocol is strictly necessary, how the FFT-based high-pass filter generalizes to very low-texture or synthetic scenes, and whether the approach extends to multi-view or video stereo without the current single-pair assumption.
