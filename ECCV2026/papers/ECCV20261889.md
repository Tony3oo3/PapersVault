## Problem

Existing VLM prompt-learning methods optimize a single entangled feature space where class-specific discriminative cues and class-agnostic transferable knowledge are tightly intertwined. This entanglement causes class-specific adaptation to erode zero-shot transfer, produces inconsistent cross-modal representations across layers, and drives representation tokens to collapse onto class centroids, destroying the structure needed for novel-class generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261889/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a hand-crafted class text prompt ('a photo of a [class]') as input. The image is processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone with 5 learnable representation tokens injected starting at layer 6, while the text prompt is encoded by the frozen text encoder. The output is a class posterior probability: for base classes, a weighted combination (α=0.7) of the class-token feature (preserving pre-trained semantics) and the representation-token feature (capturing task-specific adaptation); for novel classes, only the class-token feature is used to retain generalization.

## Method

DiscoVL explicitly factorizes the adapted representation space into a shared class-agnostic subspace and a task-specific subspace through two complementary modules. The Disentangled Cross-modal Representation Aligner (DCRA) employs a multi-branch low-rank residual architecture with E attention-gated subspaces per layer, enabling dynamic subspace selection, and injects bidirectional cross-modal feedback (text→image in lower layers, image→text in upper layers) controlled by learned fusion gates to progressively align modalities while decoupling shared from task-specific information. The Orthogonal Adversarial Representation Learning (OARL) module combines an adversarial triplet loss that aligns each image feature with its same-class representation feature while separating cross-class features, with an orthogonal regularization term (Frobenius-norm penalty on the projection residual) that enforces feature independence and prevents representation tokens from collapsing onto class centroids. A cosine consistency loss further anchors the updated class features to frozen CLIP embeddings, and the total objective balances classification, generalization, and orthogonal adversarial triplet terms with hyperparameters α, β, γ.

## Results

On the base-to-novel generalization task (average over 11 datasets), DiscoVL achieves 85.98% Base, 77.61% Novel, and 81.58% Harmonic Mean, improving over the previous best method MMRL (85.68/77.16/81.20) by +0.30/+0.45/+0.38 points respectively. On cross-dataset evaluation, DiscoVL attains the highest average accuracy of 67.48%, surpassing MMRL (67.25%) and CoPrompt/SkipT. (67.00%). In the 16-shot few-shot setting, DiscoVL reaches 84.58% average accuracy, exceeding PromptSRC by 1.71% and SkipT. by 0.67%. The method also reduces trainable parameters by 55.7% relative to MMRL (2.21M vs. 4.99M) while improving HM by 0.26%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiscoVL outperforms all 14 baselines on average HM across the 11-dataset base-to-novel benchmark, with the closest competitor being MMRL (81.20 vs. 81.58 HM). On individual datasets, DiscoVL achieves the best HM on 7 out of 11 benchmarks; the largest single-dataset gain is +1.11% HM on EuroSAT over MMRL. On cross-dataset evaluation (Table 2), DiscoVL leads with 67.48% average, edging out MMRL (+0.23%), CoPrompt (+0.48%), and SkipT. (+0.48%), though it trails SkipT. by 0.03% on EuroSAT specifically. On domain generalization (Table 3), DiscoVL achieves 60.83% average, beating MMRL (+0.24%) and PromptSRC (+0.18%) but falling slightly short of SkipT. (61.20%); it does secure the best score on ImageNet-A (51.30%) and the largest relative gain on ImageNet-R (+0.47% over MMRL). In few-shot learning, DiscoVL dominates at extreme low-shot settings (1-shot: 73.48%, 2-shot: 76.63%) and maintains the lead at 16-shot (84.58%), surpassing PromptSRC by 1.71% and SkipT. by 0.67%.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiscoVL extends the MMRL/MMRL++ line of work, which injects learnable representation tokens into upper transformer layers of frozen CLIP encoders and factorizes projection weights into a shared kernel plus a low-rank residual. The key architectural departure is replacing the single-branch residual with a multi-branch gated aligner (DCRA) that dynamically selects among E subspaces per layer, and adding bidirectional cross-modal feedback (text→image in lower layers, image→text in upper layers) absent from prior representation-learning methods. On the loss side, the paper adapts AAPL's adversarial triplet mechanism by appending an orthogonal Frobenius-norm regularization (OARL) to prevent representation tokens from collapsing onto class centroids, a failure mode the authors argue is under-addressed by existing prompt-learning regularizers (CoCoOp, PromptSRC, KgCoOp).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The disentanglement framing—explicitly separating a class-agnostic shared subspace from a task-specific subspace—offers a principled explanation for why prompt-learning methods degrade on novel classes, and the orthogonal adversarial constraint provides a concrete mechanism to preserve pre-trained semantics during adaptation. However, the empirical gains over the strongest baseline (MMRL) are modest (≈0.3–0.5% HM on average), and all experiments use ViT-B/16 with a fixed 16-shot protocol, leaving open whether the disentanglement benefit scales to larger backbones or more extreme low-data regimes. The authors note future directions including dynamic prompt schedulers and streaming/sequential settings, but the current evaluation does not test whether the shared/task-specific split generalizes beyond single-image classification to detection or segmentation.
