## Problem

Large-vocabulary instance segmentation is bottlenecked by long-tailed category distributions, and existing synthetic data paradigms each fail in complementary ways: text-to-image methods generate diverse scenes but produce noisy pseudo-labels that are especially unreliable for rare classes, while copy-paste image-to-image methods yield accurate labels but introduce contextual artifacts that actually degrade rare-class performance. No prior approach simultaneously achieves scene diversity, label reliability, and contextual realism for underrepresented categories.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes real LVIS training images (100K images, 1,203 categories), a sampled category set per prompt (5–10 categories drawn from the full LVIS vocabulary), and natural-language instructions generated by GPT-4o or a VLM. It produces a hybrid synthetic dataset of 200K T2I images (from 40K prompts via Flux.1-dev) and 80K I2I-edited images (via Flux Kontext), each paired with instance-level annotations (masks, bounding boxes, class labels). The T2I images carry weakly-labeled supervision refined online, while the I2I images carry precise ground-truth annotations for the inserted rare-class instances.

## Method

The framework couples two complementary synthesis branches under a semi-supervised EMA teacher-student training loop. The T2I branch generates diverse scenes and applies text-consistent filtering (retaining only instances whose predicted class appears in the generating prompt) to reduce label noise; an EMA-updated teacher then progressively refines pseudo-labels during training, adapting to the T2I domain and improving rare-class coverage. The I2I branch, VRAIN, is a two-stage 'place-and-verify' pipeline: a VLM selects a semantically fitting rare category and generates a placement instruction for an instruction-based editor (Flux Kontext), and a verification stage localizes the edit via SSIM difference, detects the new object with an open-vocabulary detector, confirms it with a spatially-grounded VLM filter, extracts masks with SAM, and resolves occlusions against existing annotations. The student model is jointly trained on real data, I2I data with ground-truth labels, and T2I data with hybrid supervision (offline pseudo-labels merged with dynamically thresholded online teacher predictions via IoU matching), so that the teacher's rare-class expertise from I2I data bootstraps more accurate T2I pseudo-labels.

## Results

On the LVIS validation set, the proposed hybrid T2I-I2I framework achieves AP^box of 38.1 and AP^mask of 34.0 with ResNet-50, and AP^box of 50.7 and AP^mask of 45.2 with Swin-L, surpassing all baselines. For rare categories, the method reaches AP^box_r of 33.9 (ResNet-50) and 49.1 (Swin-L), representing gains of up to +9.9 and +7.7 points over the real-only baseline respectively. The method also demonstrates consistent scaling: the gap between ResNet-50 and Swin-L widens for rare-class AP (33.9 → 49.1), confirming that the synthetic data benefits larger backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three data-synthesis baselines: MosaicFusion (T2I), DiverGen (I2I copy-paste), and X-Paste (I2I copy-paste), all evaluated under identical CenterNet2 training protocols on LVIS. The proposed method wins on every metric for both backbones. Against the strongest I2I baseline X-Paste, the hybrid method gains +1.4 AP^box and +4.3 AP^box_r (ResNet-50), and +0.6 AP^box and +0.9 AP^box_r (Swin-L). Against the T2I baseline MosaicFusion, the gains are substantially larger: +4.0 AP^box and +9.5 AP^box_r (ResNet-50), confirming that T2I alone is insufficient for rare classes. Notably, DiverGen's rare-class-targeted variant actually degrades rare-class AP from 41.4 to 34.5 (Swin-L), demonstrating that naive copy-paste harms contextual realism, whereas VRAIN's instruction-based editing with VLM verification avoids this failure mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate data-synthesis paradigms for instance segmentation—text-to-image generation (MosaicFusion, DatasetDM) and image-to-image copy-paste augmentation (X-Paste, DiverGen)—into a single hybrid pipeline, arguing that each addresses a complementary weakness of the other. It adapts the EMA teacher-student framework of Tarvainen & Valpola [34] from standard semi-supervised learning to progressive pseudo-label refinement on domain-shifted T2I data, and repurposes instruction-based diffusion editing (Flux Kontext, InstructPix2Pix lineage) as a context-aware placement mechanism in place of naive paste. The VRAIN 'place-and-verify' module is the paper's core architectural novelty, combining a VLM-driven instruction proposer, an instruction-based editor, and a multi-stage verification chain (SSIM diff → open-vocab detection → VLM semantic filter → SAM masking) that has no direct precedent in prior I2I augmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone tackling large-vocabulary instance segmentation: the hybrid pipeline is a drop-in data augmentation strategy that requires no re-annotation and scales with backbone capacity, demonstrated on LVIS with consistent gains across ResNet-50 and Swin-L. The counterintuitive finding that rare-class-targeted copy-paste (DiverGen) *degrades* rare-class AP by ~7 points challenges the assumption that more targeted synthetic data always helps, pointing to contextual realism as a first-order concern. Key limitations remain: reliance on off-the-shelf generative models that imperfectly follow instructions, and the verification pipeline's dependence on a large VLM (InternVL3-14B) for each edit, which constrains throughput. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other long-tailed benchmarks (e.g., COCO with fine-grained splits) and whether the T2I branch's 200K-image generation cost can be reduced without sacrificing rare-class coverage.
