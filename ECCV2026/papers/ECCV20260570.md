## Problem

No large-scale dataset or benchmark exists for recognizing and localizing semantic co-speech gestures in unconstrained, in-the-wild settings. Existing gesture datasets are either laboratory-recorded with limited speaker diversity, lack word-level semantic annotations, or do not provide frame-accurate temporal boundaries, making it impossible to train models that distinguish the sparse, meaning-bearing gestures from the far more common non-semantic beat gestures in natural discourse.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB video clips as a 4-second (100-frame) query interval embedded within a longer 10-second (250-frame) temporal context window, processed through a frozen SHuBERT backbone to yield per-frame hand shape and position features. For the classification task, the output is a single binary semantic-gesture probability; for the recognition-and-localization task, the model jointly produces a word-class label (from a 155-word vocabulary) and per-frame binary gesture-presence probabilities that delineate the precise temporal boundary of the gesture within the clip.

## Method

Both models build on a frozen SHuBERT sign-language backbone whose multi-layer hidden states are fused via a learned softmax-weighted layer aggregation into a single per-frame feature sequence. The semantic classifier applies a Transformer encoder with Rotary Position Embeddings over the full 10-second context, then uses a cross-attention decoder where the 4-second query attends to the entire context memory, enabling the model to contrast the sparse semantic gesture against the speaker's baseline beat-gesture kinematics. The word recognition and localization model uses a Transformer encoder followed by a per-frame MLP+sigmoid head for dense temporal localization, and a localization-weighted average of encoder features fed to a classification head for word prediction. Training proceeds in two stages: weakly-supervised pre-training on 67k pseudo-labeled clips (with approximate boundaries from a high-confidence classifier) followed by fine-tuning on 15k manually verified clips with precise frame-level annotations, using a combined categorical cross-entropy and frame-level BCE loss.

## Results

On the GRW test set, the proposed model achieves 75.83% accuracy and 79.91% precision for semantic gesture classification, with 93.20% accuracy on high-confidence (≥0.9) predictions—substantially outperforming the strongest VLM baseline Gemini (67.30% accuracy, 68.21% high-conf). For word-level gesture recognition, the model reaches 18.35% top-1, 37.30% top-5, and 51.90% top-10 accuracy, and 0.6726 mIoU for temporal localization, surpassing all baselines by wide margins. On the external AVS-Spot benchmark, the model achieves 77.3% gesture spotting accuracy versus JEGAL's 63.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed model outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric. Among frozen-feature baselines, SemMom_Dinov3 is the strongest (10.05% Acc@1, 29.65% Acc@10), yet the proposed model exceeds it by 8.3 and 22.25 points respectively. Among fine-tuned gesture-specific models, JEGAL is the closest competitor (10.43% Acc@1, 0.5368 mIoU), but the proposed model leads by 7.92 points in Acc@1 and 0.136 in mIoU; on the external AVS-Spot benchmark, the gap widens to 77.3% vs. 63.6% spotting accuracy. Among VLMs, Gemini-3.5 is the strongest (13.95% Acc@1, 30.15% Acc@5, 0.4658 mIoU) but still trails the proposed model by 4.4 points in Acc@1 and 0.207 in mIoU, with Gemini being particularly weak on fine-grained temporal localization. Intern-VL and Qwen3-VL perform poorly, with Qwen3-VL achieving only 0.2071 mIoU—barely above random—indicating that current open-source VLMs struggle with precise gesture boundary prediction. For semantic gesture classification (Table 5), the proposed model's 93.20% high-confidence accuracy far exceeds Gemini's 68.21%, validating its utility for large-scale pseudo-label mining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of co-speech gesture research and in-the-wild video understanding. Prior co-speech gesture datasets (Trinity, Talking With Hands, BEAT) are lab-recorded with limited speaker diversity, while in-the-wild datasets (Speech2Gesture, PATS) lack semantic gesture annotations entirely. The paper repurposes SHuBERT, a sign-language pretrained backbone, as a frozen visual feature extractor for co-speech gesture tasks — a cross-domain transfer not previously explored in this setting. The cross-attention mechanism between a short query clip and a long motion context for semantic gesture classification is a novel architectural choice, and the pseudo-labeling pipeline for scaling word recognition and localization without prohibitive annotation cost is a practical contribution building on standard weak-supervision ideas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRW is the first large-scale benchmark with verified semantic word labels and frame-accurate temporal boundaries for in-the-wild co-speech gestures, filling a critical gap that has limited both gesture recognition and semantically-grounded gesture synthesis. The finding that 97.5% of gestures begin before the spoken word and 85.7% end after it has direct implications for any gesture-speech alignment or synthesis pipeline. A key limitation is that the data comes from public-facing discourse (lectures, talk shows, interviews), so gesture patterns in casual or private conversation may differ. The still-modest word recognition accuracy (18.35% top-1 over 100 classes) and the significant gap to VLMs on fine-grained localization suggest the task remains far from solved, leaving room for future multimodal approaches that better integrate linguistic context.
