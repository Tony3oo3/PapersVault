## Problem

Existing adapters for vision-language models, such as MMA, enhance adaptation flexibility through multimodal prompt interactions but do not explicitly regulate cross-modal interaction density or alignment capacity. As a result, token-level interactions remain dense and alignment can extend to minor or unstable coupling directions, limiting generalization to novel classes under few-shot supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261333/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed by a frozen ViT-B/16 CLIP backbone into 16×16 patch embeddings) and text class descriptions that are first augmented from simple templates (e.g., 'a photo of a [tench]') into richer sentences via the DeepSeek LLM. The output is a class label predicted through CLIP's standard contrastive softmax over cosine similarities between the projected image and text embeddings, evaluated under 16-shot base-to-novel, cross-dataset, domain generalization, and 1–16-shot few-shot settings.

## Method

SDSA inserts a shared two-stage squeezing adapter into both the image and text encoders of a frozen CLIP model. In the shallow squeezing stage, a Bernoulli token-level mask (mask_ratio = 0.10) randomly drops a fraction of token activations along the sequence dimension, imposing structured sparsity before a fully connected projection compresses features into a bottleneck of dimension m = 128. In the deep squeezing stage, a shared low-rank transformation (rank r = 16, with ReLU nonlinearity) is applied identically to both modalities, consolidating alignment into a compact set of dominant shared directions; this is followed by a cross-attention module where image tokens attend to text tokens and vice versa, enabling selective cross-modal refinement. The adapter output is added back to the CLIP encoder via residual connections with small scaling factors (α = 0.05 for the low-rank branch, β = 0.01 for the cross-attention branch), keeping the total trainable parameters at 4.79 M while the pre-trained CLIP weights remain frozen.

## Results

On the base-to-novel generalization task (16-shot training, zero-shot novel evaluation), SDSA achieves an average novel-class accuracy of 78.34% and harmonic mean (HM) of 80.96% across 11 datasets, the best among all compared methods. Its average base-class accuracy is 83.77%, slightly below 2SFS (85.55%) and PromptSRC (84.26%) but competitive with CoPrompt (84.00%). In cross-dataset evaluation (trained on ImageNet, tested on 10 target datasets), SDSA attains the highest average accuracy of 67.32%, outperforming MMA (66.61%) and MaPLe (66.30%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDSA achieves the highest average novel-class accuracy (78.34%) and HM (80.96%) across all 11 datasets, surpassing the closest competitors CoPrompt (77.23% novel, 80.48 HM) and 2SFS (75.48% novel, 80.20 HM) by 1.1 and 2.9 points on novel accuracy respectively. On base-class accuracy, SDSA (83.77%) trails 2SFS (85.55%) and PromptSRC (84.26%) by 1.8 and 0.5 points, indicating a slight trade-off toward generalization. In cross-dataset evaluation, SDSA leads with 67.32% average, beating MMA (66.61%) and MaPLe (66.30%), with particularly large gains on FGVC Aircraft (30.33% vs. 25.33% for MMA) and UCF-101 (70.43% vs. 68.32%). For domain generalization, SDSA achieves the best scores on ImageNet-A (51.33%) and ImageNet-R (77.57%) while remaining within 0.2 points of the best on ImageNet-V2 and ImageNet-Sketch. In few-shot settings (1–16 shots), SDSA is competitive with PLOT and MMA, often matching or exceeding them on Aircraft, SUN397, and UCF-101, though PLOT retains a slight edge on EuroSAT at 4 and 8 shots.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDSA extends the shared multi-modal adapter paradigm introduced by MMA [35] by inserting a two-stage squeezing mechanism between the bottleneck projection and the residual connection. The shallow squeezing stage (Bernoulli token masking) is a novel regularizer not present in prior adapter or prompt-learning methods, while the deep squeezing stage combines a shared low-rank transformation (analogous to LoRA [38] but applied across modalities in a shared subspace) with a cross-attention module reminiscent of MaPLe [17]. The paper explicitly positions itself against prompt-based methods (CoOp, CoCoOp, PromptSRC, CoPrompt) and single-modality adapters (CLIP-Adapter, Tip-Adapter), arguing that none of them regulate cross-modal interaction density or constrain alignment capacity to a compact shared subspace.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete architectural gap—uncontrolled dense token interactions and unbounded alignment directions in existing VLM adapters—and shows that explicitly regulating both yields better base-to-novel generalization across 11 datasets. However, the method introduces 4.79M trainable parameters (roughly 7× MMA's 0.67M), and the authors acknowledge that performance remains sensitive in extremely few-shot (1-shot) settings, where the gap over strong baselines narrows or reverses. Open questions include whether the two-stage squeezing design transfers to non-classification VLM tasks (detection, segmentation), whether the masking ratio and rank can be learned rather than hand-tuned, and whether the approach scales to larger backbones (ViT-L/14, ViT-H/14) where the parameter overhead becomes more significant.
