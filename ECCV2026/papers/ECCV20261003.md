## Problem

Existing Virtual Try-On methods are designed for single-garment replacement and rely on cloth-agnostic representations that mask out the original outfit, irreversibly discarding the inner-layer context needed for real-world dressing. Moreover, standard VTON benchmarks contain only single-item swap pairs, so models have never observed the compositional logic of layering garments. This paper formalizes Layering Virtual Try-On (LVTON) — compositing a new garment over an existing outfit while preserving inner layers — and shows that current paradigms fundamentally fail at this task.

## Input / Output

The method takes four inputs: a fully-clothed person image (no mask required), a reference garment image, a target pose image, and a free-form text instruction specifying the interaction type (e.g., 'add a gray sweater,' 'remove a dark blue blouse,' 'swap X for Y'). It outputs a photorealistic image where the specified garment edit is applied while non-target inner layers (collars, sleeves, hems) remain visually intact. The pose input is critical because real-world layering inevitably introduces spatial misalignment between the source and target body configurations, unlike the fixed-pose assumption in standard VTON.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage disentangled training paradigm applied via LoRA to Qwen-Image-Edit (an MMDiT diffusion backbone with VAE tokenizer and Qwen2.5-VL multimodal encoder). Stage 1 learns general VTON priors from an automatically generated synthetic dataset: frames from fashion videos are segmented (SAM 2), inpainted with a novel outfit (Flux.1 Kontext), and then paired across temporally distant frames (i≠j) to enforce pose-variant, mask-free supervision that teaches robust garment deformation and identity preservation. Stage 2 fine-tunes on a small real-world LVTON dataset (5,768 pairs from 60 videos) to learn specific layering logic, augmented by temporal reversal that converts 'add' sequences into 'remove' sequences, effectively doubling the training signal. The key insight is that general VTON priors and layering-specific knowledge are distinct competencies; disentangling them allows the model to acquire robust deformation priors from abundant synthetic data and then efficiently specialize on scarce layering examples, achieving SOTA on both the new LVTON benchmark and traditional VTON benchmarks.

## Results

On the novel LVTON benchmark (532 test pairs), the proposed method achieves SSIM 0.843, LPIPS 0.127, FID 48.96, and KID 1.463×10⁻³, outperforming all baselines including OmniTry (SSIM 0.810, FID 54.59) and Nano Banana (SSIM 0.798, FID 49.18). On traditional VTON benchmarks, the full two-stage model sets new SOTA: on VITON-HD it reaches SSIM 0.928, LPIPS 0.055, FID 3.235, KID 0.200×10⁻³, and on DressCode it reaches SSIM 0.948, LPIPS 0.042, FID 2.598, KID 0.315×10⁻³, surpassing the previous best (FitDit: SSIM 0.899/0.926, FID 4.731/2.638). A 30-participant user study confirms superiority on layering-specific criteria (Occlusion 4.75, Inner-Visibility 4.54, Overall Layering 4.57 on a 5-point scale) versus the next-best method (Nano Banana: 3.49/3.39/2.83).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the LVTON benchmark, the proposed method wins across all four metrics against every baseline. The closest competitor is OmniTry (SSIM 0.810 vs. 0.843; FID 54.59 vs. 48.96), while Nano Banana is second-closest on FID (49.18 vs. 48.96) but trails significantly on SSIM and KID. Any2AnyTryon and HunyuanImage-3.0-Instruct are substantially worse, with FIDs of 89.57 and 88.07 respectively, indicating severe layering failures. On VITON-HD, Ours (SSIM 0.928, FID 3.235) surpasses the previous SOTA FitDit (SSIM 0.899, FID 4.731) by a wide margin, though FitDit retains a marginal KID advantage (0.190 vs. 0.200). On DressCode, Ours (SSIM 0.948, FID 2.598, KID 0.315) dominates FitDit (SSIM 0.926, FID 2.638, KID 0.499) on all metrics. Among mask-free baselines, CATVTON (SSIM 0.870, FID 5.425 on VITON-HD) and MuGa-VTON (SSIM 0.898, FID 7.240) are the strongest but remain well below the proposed method, confirming that mask-free alone is insufficient without the two-stage disentangled training paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant mask-based VTON paradigm (VITON-HD, HR-VITON, StableVITON, etc.) that requires cloth-agnostic person representations, arguing this fundamentally precludes modeling layered garments. It builds on the pre-trained Qwen-Image-Edit diffusion model (a multimodal MMDiT with Qwen2.5-VL conditioning) and applies LoRA fine-tuning, positioning itself within the emerging mask-free VTON line (OmniTry, Any2AnyTryon, CATVTON) but addressing their critical data bottleneck: none were trained on sequential layering examples. The key conceptual departure is a two-stage disentangled training paradigm—first learning general VTON priors from synthetic pose-variant video pairs, then fine-tuning on a small real-world layering dataset—rather than end-to-end training on a single dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LVTON addresses a practically important gap: real-world dressing involves sequential layering (adding a jacket over a shirt), not just single-garment replacement, and no prior benchmark or method was designed for this. The two-stage disentangled paradigm and the video-based synthetic data pipeline offer a reusable recipe for tackling data-scarce editing tasks more broadly. However, the stage 2 dataset is small (5,768 pairs from 60 videos), the temporal reversal augmentation imperfectly simulates garment removal physics, and stage 1 quality is bounded by upstream segmentation and inpainting models—scaling the layering data distribution remains an open challenge for handling atypical or complex multi-layer interactions.
