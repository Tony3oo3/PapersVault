## Problem

Accurately reconstructing full multi-object 3D scenes from sparse RGB-D observations remains a core challenge for scalable real-to-sim robotics. Existing generative methods treat shape generation, occlusion completion, and 6-DoF pose estimation as separate stages, compounding errors under clutter, symmetry, and noisy depth, while also lacking support for part-level reconstruction and multi-view conditioning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261397/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts one or two RGB-D views, each providing an RGB image, a depth map (from FoundationStereo to simulate realistic sensor noise), camera intrinsics, and binary segmentation masks for target objects or parts. During training, inputs are dynamically cropped to the object region with 20%–100% padding, and depth is converted into a normalized point map filtered by the mask. The output is a complete 3D textured mesh (via FlexiCubes geometry + Gaussian Splatting appearance baked onto the mesh), a Sim(3) pose (rotation, translation, isotropic scale) in the normalized camera frame, and—when applicable—part-level shape and pose, all produced jointly in a single forward pass without post-hoc registration.

## Method

RECGEN is a two-stage rectified-flow framework. Stage 1 jointly generates a sparse voxel structure (encoded as a 16×16×16×8 latent via a 3D convolutional VAE) and a Sim(3) pose using a transformer with AdaLN and cross-attention over DINOv2 image features, point-map features, and mask features; pose parameters are concatenated as an extra token so shape and pose are denoised together. Stage 2 uses a sparse transformer to generate local latents conditioned on the predicted structure, pose, and multimodal features, decoded into a mesh (FlexiCubes) and a Gaussian Splatting representation for texture. Key design choices include point-map conditioning for direct depth exploitation, dynamic cropping with mask tokens to preserve scene context under occlusion, pose-conditioned texturing to resolve symmetry ambiguity, and multi-view support via view-ID embeddings with stochastic single-view dropout. The model is trained with Conditional Flow Matching on a 198K-asset synthetic dataset of occluded compositional scenes (3.2M rendered views), initialized from TRELLIS pretrained weights (~1.2B parameters), and augmented with realistic stereo noise and z-score pose normalization for robustness.

## Results

RECGEN achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four object datasets (HB, ReOcS, LM-O) and the part dataset (ArtVIP). On object-centric benchmarks, RECGEN (1-view) reaches 92.7% ADD-SB@0.1 and 71.1% ADD-SB@0.05 on average, compared to 84.2% / 38.6% for SAM3D—nearly doubling accuracy at the stricter 5% threshold. On the ArtVIP part-level benchmark, RECGEN reduces CD_norm by half versus SAM3D (0.026 vs. 0.056) and improves ADD-SB@0.05 by +38.2 pp (84.0% vs. 45.8%). Overall, RECGEN outperforms SAM3D by 30.1% in geometric shape quality, 9.1% in texture reconstruction, and 33.9% in pose estimation, while using ~80% fewer training meshes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECGEN outperforms all three baselines—SAM3D, Any6D (both InstantMesh and TRELLIS variants), and SceneComplete—on every metric across all five evaluation datasets. Against the closest competitor SAM3D, RECGEN (1-view) improves ADD-SB@0.05 from 38.6% to 71.1% on average across object datasets (a 33.9% relative gain in pose estimation) and reduces CD_norm from 0.039 to 0.033 (30.1% shape quality improvement), while being trained on ~80% fewer meshes. The gap is most dramatic on ReOcS (heavily occluded, symmetric objects), where RECGEN reaches 89.5% ADD-SB@0.05 versus SAM3D's 43.6%, and on the ArtVIP part benchmark, where RECGEN halves CD_norm (0.026 vs. 0.056) and more than doubles ADD-SB@0.05 (84.0% vs. 45.8%). Any6D and SceneComplete trail substantially: SceneComplete achieves 0.0% DRE@0.05 on all object datasets (failing to estimate scale under occlusion), and Any6D's modular pipeline (generate-then-register) compounds errors, yielding CD_norm values 2–7× worse than RECGEN. In downstream robotic manipulation replay, RECGEN's reconstructions yield a 59% success rate versus SAM3D's 38%, confirming that the geometric and pose advantages translate to practical utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

RECGEN builds directly on the TRELLIS structured 3D latent framework and rectified flow matching, adopting its 1.2B-parameter transformer backbone and sparse voxel representation. It departs from modular pipelines (Any6D, SceneComplete) that decouple shape generation from pose registration, and from SAM3D's monocular-only, object-only formulation, by unifying joint shape-pose estimation, multi-view conditioning, part-level decomposition, and pose-conditioned texturing into a single generative framework. A key conceptual departure is the introduction of a large-scale synthetic occluded-object dataset and the encoding of segmentation masks as positional signals rather than hard pixel masks, enabling the model to leverage surrounding scene context for occlusion reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RECGEN addresses a practical bottleneck in real-to-sim robotics: constructing high-fidelity digital twins from sparse RGB-D observations without manual scanning. The demonstrated 59% vs. 38% replay success rate in teleoperated manipulation and generalization to in-the-wild DROID scenes suggest immediate utility for scalable policy evaluation and imitation learning. Open questions include whether the synthetic-to-real gap persists under more diverse sensor noise, whether the 2-view limit can be extended to arbitrary multi-view configurations, and whether the appearance quality (still slightly behind SAM3D after ICP) can be closed with larger training data or multi-resolution training. The 80%-less-data result also raises the question of how much of the gain comes from the occluded synthetic dataset design versus the architectural choices.
