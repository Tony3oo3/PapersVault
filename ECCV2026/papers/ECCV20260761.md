## Problem

3D Gaussian splatting couples geometry and appearance in each primitive, forcing millions of splats to represent high-frequency textures even on simple planar surfaces. Existing textured-splatting methods either store per-primitive image textures (memory scales quadratically with resolution) or query a neural field at every ray-primitive intersection (too slow for real-time). The paper addresses how to decouple geometry from appearance while preserving real-time rendering speeds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260761/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of calibrated RGB images with COLMAP-estimated camera poses and produces a compact 3D scene representation consisting of ~400K surfels plus a shared multiresolution hash-grid neural field (160 MB total). Rendering is a two-pass rasterization: the first pass alpha-composites non-textured surfel colors and collects the top-K=2 highest-weight intersections per pixel via a k-buffer; the second pass queries the neural field only at those K world-space positions and blends the resulting textured radiance into the initial render, yielding a final RGB image at 50 FPS.

## Method

Nexels represent geometry as surfels with a generalized Gaussian kernel parameterized by a per-axis gamma that interpolates between a standard Gaussian (γ=1) and a hard-edged quad (γ→∞), enabling near-opaque flat surfaces with sharp boundaries. Appearance is split into a per-primitive spherical-harmonic color (as in 2DGS) and a shared world-space neural field (Instant-NGP: 16-level hash grid + 2-layer MLP) that outputs view-dependent radiance. The key efficiency mechanism is a fixed-budget texturing pass: only the K=2 highest-blending-weight primitives per pixel are sent to the neural field, bounding per-pixel computation and enabling real-time rendering. Anti-aliasing is handled by down-weighting fine hash-grid levels with a Zip-NeRF-style factor based on focal length and intersection depth. Training uses 30K iterations of differentiable rendering with a composite loss (L1 + D-SSIM image loss, texture loss, alpha regularization, opacity L1, and grid weight regularization) plus adaptive density control that splits and prunes surfels to maintain a target primitive count P.

## Results

With only 400K primitives, Nexels achieves PSNR 27.35 / LPIPS 0.201 on Mip-NeRF360, PSNR 23.55 / LPIPS 0.155 on Tanks & Temples, and PSNR 27.04 / LPIPS 0.222 on the custom high-texture dataset, all at 160 MB memory and 50 FPS rendering. This matches or exceeds 3DGS (2.7M primitives, 640 MB) in perceptual quality (LPIPS) while using 9.7× fewer primitives and 5.5× less memory on outdoor scenes, and 17.5× fewer primitives and 10.2× less memory on the custom scenes. On the custom dataset, Nexels achieves the best PSNR (27.04) and LPIPS (0.222) among all compared methods, outperforming DBS (26.72 / 0.273) and 3DGS (26.65 / 0.224).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS, Nexels achieves comparable or better LPIPS (0.201 vs 0.214 on Mip-NeRF360; 0.222 vs 0.224 on Custom) with 9.7× fewer primitives and 5.5× less memory on outdoor scenes, and 17.5× fewer primitives and 10.2× less memory on the custom high-texture scenes. Against DBS, which uses 7.75× more primitives (3.1M vs 0.4M) on Mip-NeRF360, Nexels trades a small PSNR gap (27.35 vs 28.10) for substantially lower memory (160 MB vs 340 MB) and matches or beats DBS on LPIPS for the custom dataset (0.222 vs 0.273). Among textured-splatting baselines, Nexels outperforms NeST-Splatting (LPIPS 0.201 vs 0.212 on Mip-NeRF360; 0.222 vs 0.246 on Custom) while rendering at 50 FPS versus 23 FPS, and beats BBSplat (0.201 vs 0.231) and GS-Texturing (0.201 vs 0.241) on LPIPS with equal or fewer primitives. On Tanks & Temples, Nexels achieves the best LPIPS (0.155) among all primitive-based methods, narrowly trailing DBS (0.144) and BBSplat (0.147). The main trade-off is that DBS and ZipNeRF achieve higher PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 (28.10 and 28.53 vs 27.35), but the paper argues PSNR under-penalizes blurry reconstructions of fine details, and LPIPS better reflects perceptual quality for textured scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) for its surfel-based geometry representation and differentiable rasterization, and on Instant-NGP for its multiresolution hash-grid neural field architecture. It departs from prior textured Gaussian splatting methods (BBSplat, NeST-Splatting, GS-Texturing) by introducing a top-K per-pixel strategy inspired by depth-buffered mesh rasterization: the neural field is queried only for the K most relevant primitives per pixel rather than at every ray-primitive intersection, which is the key mechanism enabling real-time rendering. The generalized Gaussian kernel with a per-axis gamma parameter also distinguishes it from prior non-Gaussian kernels (beta, polynomial) by producing quad-like shapes with hard straight edges rather than elliptical ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 31× primitive reduction on indoor scenes and 9.7× on outdoor scenes while maintaining perceptual parity with 3DGS challenges the assumption that millions of primitives are necessary for high-frequency appearance, suggesting that the geometry-appearance coupling in standard splatting is the primary bottleneck. The top-K neural field querying strategy is a general technique applicable beyond texturing, and the sparse surfel representation is naturally compatible with ray-tracing and level-of-detail structures for city-scale scenes. Key limitations include dependence on tensor-core GPUs for real-time performance, noise artifacts in unseen regions, and inability to model motion blur or depth-of-field, leaving open questions about robustness under non-ideal imaging conditions and scalability to very large scenes.
