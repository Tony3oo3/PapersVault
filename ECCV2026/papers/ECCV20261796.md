## Problem

Traditional visual SLAM pipelines require streaming entire image frames from sensor to processor, transferring vast amounts of redundant pixel data that is subsequently discarded during feature extraction. This creates severe bandwidth, latency, and power bottlenecks that limit robustness under rapid motion. The paper asks whether a collaborative architecture—splitting computation between in-sensor pixel processors and a PC back-end with only sparse bidirectional data exchange—can eliminate these bottlenecks while maintaining full SLAM functionality.

## Input / Output

The PPA front-end internally captures 256×256 image frames and processes them entirely in-pixel, outputting only ~200 bytes per frame (2 bytes per tracked feature for location, 2 bytes per feature for a 2-bit BRIEF descriptor piece, plus 1 byte of pose data received from the back-end). The 256-bit BRIEF descriptors are assembled incrementally over 128 consecutive frames (~64 ms at 2000 FPS) on the PC side. The back-end consumes these sparse feature streams to produce camera pose estimates and a sparse point-feature map, and feeds pose estimates back to the sensor to enable rotation-invariant tracking and descriptor generation.

## Method

The system splits VSLAM computation across a SCAMP-7 Pixel-Processor-Array front-end (256×256 SIMD pixel-processors) and a modified ORB-SLAM3 back-end. The front-end performs point-feature detection and tracking at up to 2000 Hz using a modified Descriptor-In-Pixel (DIP) algorithm enhanced with two contributions: (1) Maximum Local Contrast (MLC) per-pixel exposure control that captures each region at its optimal contrast level, and (2) pose-aided rotational invariance, where the back-end streams estimated camera orientation into the pixel-processors so that 8-bit in-pixel descriptors and 256-bit BRIEF sampling patterns can be bit-shifted to compensate for inter-frame rotation. The back-end replaces ORB-SLAM3's expensive descriptor-matching-based keypoint association with trivial positional tracking (features move only 1–2 pixels between frames at 2000 Hz), using descriptor matching only as a fallback for occlusion-reappearance cases. This bidirectional sparse communication—features and descriptor fragments flowing sensor-to-PC, pose estimates flowing PC-to-sensor—reduces per-frame data transfer by >1000×, per-frame energy by ~20×, and back-end tracking time by ~90% compared to a camera-based pipeline.

## Results

The PPA-based VSLAM system achieves a >1000× reduction in per-frame data transfer (~200 bytes vs. >500,000 bytes for RealSense) and a ~20× reduction in energy consumption per frame ((1.9±0.1)×10⁻⁵% vs. (4.53±0.14)×10⁻⁴%). Under aggressive rapid-motion trajectories, the PPA system loses tracking only 2.2 times on average (1.44% of frames) compared to 80.2 times (9.06%) for the RealSense-based ORB-SLAM3. In terms of pose accuracy, the PPA approach achieves a translation APE RMSE of 0.046 m and rotation APE RMSE of 5.49°, substantially outperforming the RealSense baseline (0.113 m and 8.00° respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard ORB-SLAM3 pipeline using an Intel RealSense D455 depth camera at 60 FPS, which streams full images to the PC for feature detection and matching. The PPA-based approach decisively outperforms this baseline on every reported metric. In tracking robustness under aggressive motion, the PPA system loses tracking 2.2 times on average versus 80.2 for RealSense (a ~36× improvement), with only 1.44% of frames affected versus 9.06%. In pose accuracy (Table 5), the PPA system achieves roughly 2.5× lower translation APE RMSE (0.046 m vs. 0.113 m) and 1.5× lower rotation APE RMSE (5.49° vs. 8.00°). On the efficiency side, the PPA front-end reduces back-end tracking computation by ~90% (1,554 μs vs. 14,834 μs per frame) and cuts per-frame data transfer by over 1000×, while consuming ~20× less energy per processed frame. The paper notes that real-time ORB-SLAM3 was 'barely functional' under the tested rapid-motion trajectories, whereas the PPA system operated in real time at 2000 Hz.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of PPA-based visual odometry work (Bose et al. 2017, Murai & Lisondra's BIT-VO/BIT-VIO) by moving from unidirectional sensor-to-PC data flow to a genuinely bidirectional collaborative architecture, where the PC back-end transmits pose estimates back into the pixel-processor array to enable rotation-invariant feature tracking and descriptor generation. It builds directly on the Descriptor-In-Pixel (DIP) algorithm for in-pixel feature detection and tracking, and integrates it with ORB-SLAM3's mapping and relocalisation back-end, replacing the traditional image-streaming front-end with sparse feature data at up to 2000 Hz. The 'visual computation continuum' framing positions this within a broader argument that computation should be distributed along the sensing-to-processing path rather than concentrated at the back-end, drawing an analogy to biological vision where the retina performs low-level processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bidirectional communication concept—where the back-end's pose estimate is fed back to the sensor to improve its own feature tracking—is a genuinely new architectural pattern for visual pipelines that could inspire other sensor-compute co-designs beyond PPAs. However, the demonstration is constrained to a single academic prototype (SCAMP-7, 256×256, 180 nm process), monocular operation, and relatively short trajectories, leaving open whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, stereo, or long-duration mapping. The 1000× bandwidth and 20× per-frame energy reductions are architecturally inevitable given sparse-vs-dense data transfer, so their practical significance depends on PPA hardware maturing to competitive process nodes and resolutions. A key open question is whether the in-pixel feature quality (limited by 8-bit descriptors and 23-bit memory per pixel-processor) is sufficient for the mapping and relocalisation tasks that ORB-SLAM3's back-end still handles.
