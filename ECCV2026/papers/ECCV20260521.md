## Problem

Implicit neural representation (INR) methods for arbitrary-scale MRI super-resolution suffer from spectral bias, prioritizing low-frequency components and producing blurry reconstructions that lack high-frequency anatomical detail. Additionally, existing multi-contrast INR frameworks rely on simple global concatenation of contrast features, failing to exploit the frequency-domain nature of MRI (acquired in k-space) or to densely fuse multi-contrast features with positional coordinates within the implicit decoder.

## Input / Output

The network simultaneously accepts a target low-resolution MRI and a reference MRI of arbitrary resolution and contrast (e.g., PDW as reference, FS-PDW or T2W as target), both treated as complex-valued two-channel inputs. The reference image is unconstrained in size and sequence type, enabling flexible multi-contrast guidance. LR inputs are generated by k-space downsampling (retaining only the central 1/k² frequency content), and the model is trained with random scale factors uniformly sampled from [1, 4] at stride 0.1, producing HR output at any queried resolution via continuous coordinate mapping.

## Method

MArFE employs a dual-branch architecture with a shared RDN encoder that extracts features from both target and reference images, aligning them via nearest-neighbor upsampling to a uniform size before feeding them into an implicit decoder. The decoder incorporates a Fourier Reparameterization Module (FRM) that decomposes features into fixed Fourier orthogonal bases after sinusoidal activation, explicitly modeling spectral content to counteract INR spectral bias. A Non-Local Galerkin-type Linear Attention (NLGLA) module reformulates the Fourier Neural Operator as a kernelized linear attention mechanism, reducing computational complexity from O(N²) to O(N) while preserving global frequency-domain interactions and coupling spatial coordinates with deep features via linear projection. Training is supervised by a hybrid loss combining standard L1, a Global k-space Loss with magnitude- and distance-aware weighting in the frequency domain, and a spatial High-Frequency Loss computed via Gaussian low-pass subtraction, jointly enforcing fidelity across both domains.

## Results

On the fastMRI dataset, MArFE achieves an average PSNR of 17.879 dB and SSIM of 0.964 across scale factors ×1.5 to ×8, outperforming the closest competitor DualArb (17.607 dB / 0.963). On the IXI dataset, MArFE reaches 21.492 dB / 0.779 average, surpassing DualArb by 0.79 dB in PSNR. At the standard ×4 scale, MArFE attains 31.080 dB (fastMRI) and 38.041 dB (IXI), leading all baselines by 0.5–1.2 dB over the next-best method. The method also demonstrates strong out-of-distribution robustness at ×6 and ×8, where it maintains the largest margin over competing ASSR methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MArFE consistently outperforms all eight baselines on both datasets across every tested scale factor. The closest competitor is DualArb (MICCAI 2023), the only other multi-contrast ASSR method; MArFE leads it by 0.27 dB average PSNR on fastMRI and 0.79 dB on IXI, with the gap widening at higher scales (e.g., ×8: 26.871 vs 26.250 on fastMRI, 30.625 vs 28.681 on IXI). Fixed-scale multi-contrast methods McMRSR and WavTrans are evaluated by extrapolating their ×2 and ×4 trained weights to other scales; they trail MArFE by 0.4–0.7 dB on fastMRI and 1.0–2.0 dB on IXI at ×4. Among single-contrast ASSR baselines (Meta-SR, LIIF, HIIF, Diinn, SRNO), MArFE gains 0.4–1.2 dB at ×4 on IXI, with the largest margin over HIIF (38.041 vs 27.650 dB), which degrades sharply at higher scales. Notably, MArFE's advantage is most pronounced in the out-of-distribution regime (×6, ×8), where it maintains the largest PSNR gap over all competitors, underscoring its robustness to unseen scale factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

MArFE sits at the intersection of three research threads: INR-based arbitrary-scale super-resolution (LIIF, HIIF, SRNO, Dual-Arb), Fourier Neural Operators for continuous-domain mapping, and multi-contrast MRI SR (McMRSR, WavTrans). It departs from prior INR-based ASSR methods by explicitly modeling MRI's complex-valued k-space nature through a Fourier Reparameterization Module and a Non-Local Galerkin-type Linear Attention (NLGLA) that reformulates the FNO integral operator as a linear-complexity kernelized attention. The dual-branch architecture for target and reference images extends Dual-Arb's multi-contrast formulation, while the dual-domain loss (global k-space + spatial high-frequency) addresses the spectral bias that plagues vanilla INR decoders in a way tailored to MRI's concentrated frequency spectrum.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-domain framing is well-motivated for MRI (acquired in k-space) and the arbitrary-scale capability has direct clinical utility where fixed-scale models would require separate training per resolution. The downstream segmentation validation via ANTs adds practical credibility beyond PSNR/SSIM. However, the evaluation is limited to 2D slices on two datasets, the complex-valued treatment (real/imaginary as separate channels) is a simplification of true complex arithmetic, and generalization to 3D volumetric data, multi-modal sequences (fMRI, DTI), and real-world acquisition artifacts (motion, B1 inhomogeneity) remains open. The approach's reliance on a shared RDN encoder also constrains scalability to larger anatomical regions.
