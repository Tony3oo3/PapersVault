## Problem

Segment-based paint-bucket colourisation pipelines assign colours to enclosed regions via direct reference-to-target matching, but this per-region top-1 correspondence is brittle when regions are ambiguous fragments (e.g., thin occluded pieces) where multiple reference regions look similarly plausible. Existing methods lack a mechanism to resolve such ambiguity by leveraging spatial context from similar reference views or temporal continuity from adjacent frames in the target video.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target video of T binarised line-sketch frames (each flood-filled into closed regions) and a reference set of R coloured frames with segmented regions carrying discrete palette labels. It outputs a per-region colour label from the fixed palette for every region in every target frame, yielding fully colourised animation frames. The framework is backbone-agnostic: it operates on any feature extractor that produces dense per-pixel features, which are average-pooled within region masks to form descriptors, and requires no task-specific training.

## Method

PECA is a training-free, plug-and-play inference framework with three sequential stages. First, Active Reference Expansion generates geometrically augmented views of the given reference shots and selects a budgeted subset (B views from mB candidates) via a greedy facility-location objective that maximises target-region coverage while limiting distractor exposure. Second, Probability Aggregation converts top-k correspondence similarities into temperature-scaled softmax weights and soft-votes them into per-region colour probabilities over the finite palette simplex, replacing the brittle top-1 copy. Third, Cyclic-gated Temporal Fusion refines these probabilities across adjacent frames using a product update, but only along bidirectionally cycle-consistent nearest-neighbour links, preventing error propagation from unreliable temporal matches. The three stages are complementary: ARE strengthens spatial support, PA unifies noisy evidence in colour space, and CT exploits temporal continuity, with fixed hyperparameters (top-k=64, τ=0.05, B=31, m=4) across all experiments.

## Results

On the PBC-3D one-shot design-sheet colourisation benchmark, PECA applied to the trained DACoN 1.1 backbone achieves 72.04% segment accuracy (Acc) and 77.08% Acc-Thresh, improving over the prior SOTA DACoN 1.1 by +4.03 and +4.21 points respectively. On frozen foundation backbones without any colourisation training, PECA yields substantially larger gains: DINOv2 ViT-L/14 improves from 57.49% to 61.38% Acc (+3.89), while SAM2.1-Large jumps from 34.54% to 46.65% Acc (+12.11). On the newly introduced Anita-Pirate long-shot benchmark (206 frames, ~140 segments/frame), PECA on DACoN 1.1 reaches 41.24% Acc versus 38.16% for the base, and on DINOv2 ViT-L/14 improves from 28.55% to 31.01% Acc in the in-between setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior SOTA trained segment-matching method DACoN 1.1 [23], PECA consistently wins on all five metrics: +4.03 Acc, +4.21 Acc-Thresh, +0.93 Pix-Acc, +3.01 Pix-F-Acc, and +0.31 Pix-B-MIoU on PBC-3D one-shot design-sheet colourisation. Against the earlier segment-based BasicPBC-Ref [6], PECA-on-DACoN 1.1 leads by a wide margin (72.04% vs 52.55% Acc). All pixel-generative baselines (ColorFlow [52], MangaNinja [18], AniDoc [22], Cobra [53], MagicColor [48]) are substantially outperformed on segment-level accuracy, with the best pixel-generative method (MagicColor at 21.48% Acc) trailing PECA-on-DACoN 1.1 by over 50 points. On frozen foundation backbones, PECA delivers the largest relative gains: SAM2.1-Large improves by +12.11 Acc and DINOv3 ConvNeXT-L by +10.98 Acc, demonstrating that test-time context reasoning can unlock strong colourisation performance even without task-specific training. The gains persist under multi-reference protocols (5-shot and max-shot in Table 2), where PECA-on-DACoN 1.1 reaches 79.03% Acc with max-shot references, confirming that additional references do not dilute PECA's contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

PECA builds directly on the segment-matching formulation established by DACoN [23] and BasicPBC [7], where region colourisation reduces to finding correspondences between target and reference regions via pooled feature descriptors. Rather than improving the backbone or introducing stronger matching architectures, PECA departs from this lineage by treating inference as a test-time reasoning problem: it augments the reference bank with target-conditioned views (submodular facility-location selection), converts noisy top-k matches into palette-space probability distributions via soft voting, and refines across time using cycle-consistent temporal links. This positions PECA as a model-agnostic, training-free wrapper that is orthogonal to and composable with any existing segment-matching backbone, from task-trained models like DACoN to frozen foundation models like DINOv2 or SAM2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that PECA's plug-and-play design means production pipelines can adopt it without retraining, and the ~12-point gains on frozen backbones suggest that test-time context reasoning can partially substitute for task-specific supervision in palette-constrained colourisation. The newly introduced Anita-Pirate benchmark (206 frames, ~140 segments/frame, 10–20× longer than prior test sets) addresses a real gap in evaluating long-shot colourisation where reference coverage is sparse. Key limitations remain inherent to the palette-constrained formulation: missing colours or regions in references cannot be recovered, and line leakage still causes failures. The authors identify interactive reference growth and open-world palette acquisition as natural next steps toward production toolkits.
