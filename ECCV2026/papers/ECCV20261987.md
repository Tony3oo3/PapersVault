## Problem

Joint 3D human-object interaction reconstruction from a single image is hindered by a fundamental structural mismatch: the human body follows a fixed SMPL template while objects exhibit diverse, irregular shapes and topologies. Existing vertex-level methods impose uniform positional constraints that bias optimization toward the human template, producing consistently poor object reconstruction and misaligned contacts. The paper addresses this by asking whether a single, shared 3D feature space can encode both entities without requiring object-specific templates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 448×448 RGB image (background removed) plus 2D human and object segmentation masks, from which crop-adjusted camera intrinsics and an analytical camera translation are derived. It outputs a 3D human mesh and a 3D object mesh extracted via marching cubes from 512³ voxel occupancy grids, all in a sphere-normalized camera coordinate frame (XYZ ∈ [−0.5 m, 0.5 m]). A final pose-fitting stage additionally produces SMPL-H parameters (pose θ, shape β, global orientation ψ, translation t_h) for downstream use.

## Method

The framework has three stages. First, HOIT-VAE encodes paired human-object point clouds (40 960 points each, with normals and Fourier positional encoding) into a compact 32×32×3 triplane latent (16 channels) via a cross-attention + self-attention transformer, then decodes it to a 256×256×3 feature map and queries 512³ voxel grids with MLPs to produce a semi-continuous SDF-based occupancy field, preserving thin structures that binary occupancy loses. Second, HOIT-T predicts the triplane latent from a single image using frozen DINO-ViT-B/14 tokens (1024 × 768-d) as cross-attention context, with a camera embedding (from crop intrinsics and analytical translation) injected via adaptive LayerNorm into 12 transformer blocks; it is supervised by MSE, cosine-similarity, and KL losses against the HOIT-VAE posterior. Third, a pose-fitting stage initializes SMPL-H parameters via Hand4Whole and refines them by minimizing Chamfer distance to the triplane-decoded human surface plus a DPoser-X pose prior, recovering hand details lost in triplane compression.

## Results

On BEHAVE, the method (without pose-fitting) achieves the best combined F-score@0.01m of 0.5723 and object F-score of 0.5391, outperforming all baselines on these metrics. On InterCap, it leads across all three F-score metrics: human 0.6323, object 0.6192, and combined 0.6432. With pose-fitting, the method attains the best contact recall (0.684 on BEHAVE, 0.560 on InterCap) and contact precision (0.726 on InterCap), while the HOIT-VAE alone achieves near-perfect reconstruction (F-score > 0.93) on BEHAVE, InterCap, and HODome.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HOI-TG [48], the strongest template-based baseline, the paper's method (w.o. Fit.) surpasses it by +0.2113 on object F-score and +0.0369 on combined F-score on BEHAVE, while HOI-TG retains a +0.0353 edge on human F-score (0.5913 vs. 0.5560). On InterCap, the gap widens dramatically: the method outperforms HOI-TG by +0.1858 on human, +0.4074 on object, and +0.2479 on combined F-score. Against CONTHO [38], the advantage on object metrics is even larger (+0.2214 on BEHAVE, +0.3887 on InterCap), confirming that contact-map-based supervision over-constrains local geometry at the expense of global object shape. Compared to HDM [55] (the closest template-free competitor using diffusion), the method improves combined F-score by +0.1119 on BEHAVE and +0.1088 on InterCap, while also matching or exceeding HDM on object F-score. CHORE [56] is consistently the weakest baseline across all metrics and datasets. With pose-fitting, the method achieves the best contact recall on both datasets (0.684 on BEHAVE vs. 0.554 for HOI-TG; 0.560 on InterCap vs. 0.473 for HOI-TG), indicating more accurate interaction geometry despite a slight drop in surface-level F-scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant vertex-level paradigm in HOI reconstruction (HOI-TG, CONTHO, CHORE, STACKFLOW), which imposes explicit contact constraints or relative offsets between human and object vertices, by instead encoding both meshes into a shared triplane latent space. It builds directly on HDM's [55] template-free formulation and ProciGen dataset, as well as Direct3D's [53] latent triplane encoding strategy, but repurposes the triplane as a *unified* representation for two structurally different entities rather than a single object. The key conceptual departure is that the triplane's orthogonal feature planes provide a common geometric coordinate system that avoids the template-bias problem inherent in vertex-level methods, where the fixed SMPL topology dominates optimization over diverse object shapes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified triplane formulation offers a principled way to handle the structural asymmetry between parametric human bodies and template-free objects, a gap that has caused persistent object reconstruction errors in prior HOI methods. The practical pipeline (VAE prior + image-to-triplane transformer + pose-fitting) is modular and could be extended to multi-human or multi-object settings, which the authors identify as future work. However, limitations remain: generalization to unseen object categories is limited, heavily occluded scenes are problematic, and the triplane compression inherently loses fine hand details that the pose-fitting stage only partially recovers. Whether the triplane representation scales to more complex interaction scenarios (multiple objects, dynamic sequences) remains an open question.
