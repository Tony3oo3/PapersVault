## Problem

In adverse weather, LiDAR and 4D RADAR returns become highly sparse, motivating camera fusion to compensate. However, weather-induced occlusions (snow, rain, fog) corrupt only certain spatial regions of the camera image, and existing camera-fusion methods learn confidence maps solely through the detection loss without direct supervision, causing unreliable gating that can degrade performance. The paper addresses this gap by explicitly supervising per-pixel reliability estimation to identify and suppress weather-corrupted image regions while preserving discriminative cues from visible regions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB camera image (cropped 80 px from the top and rescaled to 320×640), a LiDAR point cloud, and a 4D RADAR point cloud. The LiDAR-RADAR backbone is pretrained and frozen; only the camera branch (Swin-Tiny + GeneralizedLSSFPN neck), BEV fusion encoder, and detection head are trained. At inference, the network outputs 3D object bounding boxes (Sedan class, IoU=0.5) and a per-pixel reliability map of shape [1, H_feat, W_feat] that gates camera features before Lift-Splat-Shoot view transformation; no voxel-to-image projection or similarity computation is needed at test time.

## Method

RAF introduces a weakly-supervised reliability estimation pipeline built on top of frozen LiDAR-RADAR backbones (3D-LRF or L4DR). During training, MLP projectors map LiDAR-RADAR voxel features and camera features into a shared latent space, and cosine similarity is computed at projected pixel locations; image-level cleanliness labels (Clean/Noisy) supervise these projectors via a BCE similarity loss (L_sim). Calibration-Aware Local Matching (CALM) then searches a w×w window around each projected pixel and selects the maximum-similarity match, providing tolerance to calibration errors without additional learnable parameters. The resulting similarity scores serve as sparse pseudo reliability labels at projected locations, which train a lightweight CNN reliability estimator (3×3 conv + BN + ReLU + 1×1 conv + sigmoid) via a second BCE loss (L_rel). At inference, the predicted reliability map gates camera features through a Hadamard product F_gated = F_cam ⊙ (α + (1−α)R), where α=0.2 controls retention strength, before the gated features enter the BEV fusion encoder and detection head.

## Results

On K-Radar (Sedan class, IoU=0.5), L4DR (RAF) achieves 82.0 AP_BEV and 57.4 AP_3D overall, improving over the L4DR LiDAR-RADAR baseline (77.5 / 53.5) by +4.5 AP_BEV and +3.9 AP_3D. 3D-LRF (RAF) reaches 71.0 AP_BEV and 43.2 AP_3D, a gain of +6.5 AP_BEV and +7.4 AP_3D over its LiDAR-RADAR baseline (64.5 / 35.8). The gains are most pronounced under adverse weather (e.g., Heavysnow: L4DR RAF 60.4 vs. baseline 53.8 AP_BEV), while under Clean conditions RAF preserves or slightly exceeds baseline performance, confirming that reliability gating suppresses noise without sacrificing clean-image accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAF consistently outperforms all five baselines on K-Radar. Against the two LiDAR-RADAR baselines it builds upon, L4DR (RAF) gains +4.5 AP_BEV / +3.9 AP_3D over L4DR (Baseline), and 3D-LRF (RAF) gains +6.5 AP_BEV / +7.4 AP_3D over 3D-LRF (Baseline). Against camera-RADAR methods, L4DR (RAF) exceeds CRN (C+L+R) by +15.6 AP_BEV and RobuRCDet (C+L+R) by +17.1 AP_BEV, while also surpassing their AP_3D scores. Against the three-modality SAMFusion, L4DR (RAF) leads by +25.3 AP_BEV and +20.6 AP_3D. Notably, the Frozen variant (camera added without reliability supervision) sometimes degrades below the LiDAR-RADAR baseline under adverse weather (e.g., 3D-LRF Frozen drops to 30.8 AP_BEV on Noisy vs. 38.8 for Baseline), whereas RAF maintains or exceeds baseline performance in all visibility splits, confirming that explicit reliability supervision is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAF extends the LiDAR-RADAR fusion line of work (3D-LRF, L4DR) by adding a camera branch with explicit per-pixel reliability supervision, departing from prior camera-fusion methods like RobuRCDet and CRN that learn confidence maps solely through the detection loss without direct supervision. The modular frozen-backbone design positions RAF as a plug-in module rather than a new end-to-end architecture, and the Calibration-Aware Local Matching (CALM) mechanism addresses a practical calibration-error problem that prior cross-modal alignment methods assume away. Conceptually, the paper bridges the gap between scalar modality-weighting approaches and full reliability-map estimation by introducing weakly-supervised pseudo-label generation from cross-modal feature similarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that naive camera fusion can actively degrade LiDAR-RADAR detection under adverse weather, and that unsupervised confidence maps make things worse—a useful negative result for practitioners designing multi-sensor pipelines. The modular design (frozen backbone, only ~3.4M additional parameters) makes RAF easy to adopt on top of existing LiDAR-RADAR detectors, which has practical value for autonomous driving stacks. However, evaluation is limited to the Sedan class on K-Radar and VoD, and reliability estimation currently covers only the camera modality; extending it to LiDAR and RADAR degradation (as the authors note) remains an open question. Whether the pseudo-label strategy generalizes to other weather types or sensor configurations beyond the tested datasets is also unexplored.
