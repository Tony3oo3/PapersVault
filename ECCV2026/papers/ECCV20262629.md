## Problem

Shared-microlens (Q-cell) sensors introduce four directional sub-aperture phase shifts within each 2×2 cluster, and under defocus these shifts create periodic polarity alternation directly in the RAW domain. Conventional demosaicing methods assume intra-cluster photometric consistency and propagate this structured bias into grid-aligned checkerboard and color-bleeding artifacts, yet no existing approach models this phase-induced directional imbalance during training without requiring measured PSFs or sensor-specific optical calibration.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full-resolution Q-cell RAW mosaic (Hexadeca 4×4 or Quad 2×2 clustered CFA) as delivered by the sensor/ISP pipeline, where phase cues are entangled with CFA sampling, lens shading, PRNU, and focus-dependent variations—no separate sub-aperture streams or ground-truth PSFs are provided. It produces a full-resolution RGB image via a compact ~0.9M-parameter U-Net operating on 128×128 training crops and 512×512 inference tiles with 64-pixel overlap, targeting symmetric defocus blur while suppressing structured 2×2 periodic artifacts.

## Method

SPA is a calibration-free synthetic RAW generation framework that injects physically inspired sub-aperture phase shifts into training data. It constructs a defocus-dependent base kernel via a thin-lens Butterworth formulation, then derives four directional sub-aperture kernels (TL, TR, BL, BR) by multiplying the base kernel with orthogonal linear attenuation fields and combining them for diagonals; front/back focus polarity is encoded by the sign of the circle-of-confusion radius. A patch-wise CRA-inspired tilt parameter (δ ~ Uniform(−0.15, 0.15)) introduces controlled anti-correlated scaling between orthogonal attenuation fields to model local chief-ray-angle asymmetry without absolute sensor coordinates. The four blurred views are interleaved into a clustered CFA mosaic, corrupted with Poisson-Gaussian noise, and paired with a symmetric-blur target; a compact U-Net is trained with L1 loss at 50% SPA probability to balance phase-aware and neutral cases. The key insight is that exposing the network to phase-induced RAW structures during training—rather than increasing capacity or estimating explicit phase maps—enables it to distinguish natural high-frequency content from structured sub-aperture periodicity and suppress the resulting artifacts.

## Results

On synthetic Q-cell RAW (averaged over Kodak, McM, Set5, Set14, Urban100), PhaseUNet (0.90M params) achieves 36.60 dB PSNR / 0.0434 LPIPS on Hexadeca (4×4) and 37.15 dB PSNR / 0.0401 LPIPS on Quad (2×2), outperforming the strongest baseline NAFNet (35.31 / 0.0719 and 35.68 / 0.0673) by +1.29 and +1.47 dB PSNR respectively. On real Q-cell RAW, PhaseUNet attains the highest CLIPIQA (0.467 on Quad, 0.390 on Hexadeca) and the lowest SLI/NER, reducing Quad NER from ~2.9 (DPN) to 7×10⁻⁵, indicating near-complete elimination of 2×2 Nyquist-frequency spectral peaks. The defocus-severity ablation shows SPA reduces LPIPS by 48%, 85%, and 95% under slight, moderate, and severe defocus, with PSNR gains up to +13.87 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhaseUNet outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric for both Hexadeca and Quad CFAs. Against NAFNet (17.11M params, the strongest baseline), PhaseUNet gains +1.29 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.0719 to 0.0434 on Hexadeca, and +1.47 dB / 0.0673→0.0401 on Quad, while using only 5% of the parameters. KLAP (17.80M) trails PhaseUNet by 2.96 dB PSNR on Hexadeca and 3.03 dB on Quad, with LPIPS roughly 2× higher. PyNet-Q×Q (1.07M, comparable size) is the weakest baseline at 28.35 dB PSNR on Hexadeca, reflecting its lack of phase-aware training. On real Q-cell RAW (Table 3), PhaseUNet achieves the best CLIPIQA on Quad (0.467 vs. 0.409 for DPN) and the lowest SLI/NER across both sensor types, with NER dropping from 2.93 (DPN) to 7×10⁻⁵ on Quad—confirming that the gains are not an artifact of synthetic evaluation but generalize to uncalibrated real captures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines: clustered-CFA demosaicing (DPN, KLAP, PyNet-Q×Q) and dual-pixel sub-aperture PSF modeling (Abuolaim et al.). It departs from both in a specific way: prior demosaicing methods assume photometric consistency within same-color clusters, while prior dual-pixel work targets depth or image-domain deblurring rather than RAW-domain demosaicing artifacts. SPA repurposes the thin-lens/Butterworth PSF formulation from dual-pixel literature as a synthetic data augmentation kernel, injecting four-directional sub-aperture imbalance into clustered RAW mosaics before CFA masking—something neither line of work has done for demosaicing training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As mobile CIS increasingly adopt 2×2 shared-microlens Q-cell designs for dense PDAF, the resulting grid-aligned demosaicing artifacts represent a practical failure mode that current ISP pipelines do not address. The calibration-free, physics-inspired augmentation is attractive for deployment because it avoids per-sensor PSF measurement, but validation is limited to two prototype sensors without ground truth, and the synthetic-to-real transfer relies on CLIPIQA and the proposed SLI/NER metrics rather than paired references. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to spatially varying, field-dependent phase shifts across the full sensor, and whether the 50% SPA mixing ratio and kernel family remain optimal under broader PDAF geometries (e.g., 1×2 two-photodiode layouts).
