## Problem

Existing holistic co-speech gesture generation methods align speech semantics with individual body parts in isolation, causing cross-articulator inconsistency (e.g., semantically informative hand motion paired with neutral head/face behaviour). Moreover, dominant generative paradigms such as diffusion and standard flow matching optimise only for distribution matching via distance-based objectives without exposure to semantically incongruent negatives, leading models to produce generic beat-like gestures rather than sparse, semantically grounded iconic or metaphoric gestures.

## Input / Output

The model takes a speech audio waveform (encoded by HubERT) and a text transcript (encoded by BERT) as conditioning inputs, both temporally aligned to the motion latent length. It produces a full-body SMPL-X gesture sequence decomposed into four body-part streams (hand, upper body, lower body, face), each quantised through region-specific RVQ codebooks before decoding. Temporal context spans 64 frames on BEAT2 and 196 frames on SHOW, with generation performed in a composite latent space that is projected back to the VQ codebook manifold at inference.

## Method

The approach uses a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 learns body-part-specific temporal RVQ-VAEs that compress SMPL-X motion into discrete latent token sequences. Stage 2 introduces two key components: (a) a Semantics-Aware Composite Module (SACM) that concatenates all four region latents into a single holistic motion representation and aligns it with fused text-audio embeddings via sequence-level cosine loss and symmetric CLIP-style InfoNCE contrastive loss, enforcing cross-modal consistency at the whole-body level; and (b) a Contrastive Flow Matching (CFM) objective that, in addition to the standard flow-matching velocity regression toward the ground-truth trajectory, introduces a repulsion term against a negative velocity computed from a mismatched (incongruent) audio-text condition paired with the same noise seed. The combined training loss balances the CFM objective with the SACM semantic alignment loss, and at inference the learned conditional ODE is integrated in a few steps to produce the composite latent, which is then decomposed, quantised, and decoded into full-body SMPL-X motion.

## Results

On BEAT2 (25 speakers), HolisticSemGes achieves an FGD of 2.247, nearly halving the best baseline (SemTalk at 4.264), while also attaining the highest Beat Consistency (0.780 vs. 0.727) and Diversity (120 vs. 116). On SHOW (4 speakers), the method records FGD 18.92, BC 0.831, and Diversity 112, outperforming all five baselines on every metric. A user study with 30 participants further confirms significant perceptual gains in naturalness and speech-content alignment over SemTalk and EMAGE (p < 0.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HolisticSemGes outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both datasets. The largest margin is on BEAT2 FGD, where the method achieves 2.247 versus SemTalk's 4.264 (a ~47% reduction) and GestureLSM's 4.268, indicating substantially closer alignment with the ground-truth motion distribution. On Beat Consistency, the method leads SemTalk (0.780 vs. 0.727 on BEAT2; 0.831 vs. 0.829 on SHOW), showing tighter speech–gesture temporal synchronisation. RAGGesture, while competitive on diversity (114 on BEAT2), suffers the lowest BC (0.475), reflecting its reliance on external retrieval that disrupts rhythmic alignment. EMAGE and the original SHOW pipeline trail on FGD by 3+ points on BEAT2, consistent with their lack of explicit semantic grounding. On the smaller SHOW dataset, margins narrow (e.g., FGD 18.92 vs. SemTalk 20.17), but the method still wins all three metrics, suggesting consistent generalisation across speaker counts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the holistic co-speech gesture generation line (EMAGE, TalkSHOW, GestureLSM) that uses per-body-part RVQ-VAEs over SMPL-X parameters, and on flow-matching-based motion synthesis (GestureLSM, MotionFlow). It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) replacing part-wise semantic alignment with a composite full-body latent representation aligned via CLIP-style contrastive objectives (SACM), and (2) augmenting standard flow matching with a contrastive term that explicitly repels the velocity field from trajectories induced by mismatched audio-text conditions. Unlike retrieval-based semantic grounding methods (RAG-Gesture, Semantic Gesticulator) or two-stage rhythm-plus-semantics pipelines (SemTalk), the approach learns semantic grounding end-to-end within the generative objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized gap: distribution-matching objectives (MSE, likelihood) in gesture generation produce rhythmically plausible but semantically generic motions, failing to generate sparse iconic or metaphoric gestures. The contrastive flow-matching formulation offers a general recipe—applicable beyond gestures—for making flow-based generators semantically selective by training against negative conditions. Open questions include whether the in-batch random permutation strategy for constructing negatives scales to longer sequences or more diverse semantic categories, whether the composite (concatenated) representation is optimal versus learned cross-attention fusion, and how the approach generalizes to unseen speakers or cross-lingual settings beyond the two datasets tested.
