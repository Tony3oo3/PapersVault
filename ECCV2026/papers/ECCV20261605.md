## Problem

Existing prompt-learning methods for domain generalized semantic segmentation encode each class as a single holistic textual prompt and apply it uniformly across all pixels, offering no mechanism to adapt when only a subset of object regions is visible due to viewpoint changes, occlusion, or environmental variation. This produces diffuse attention that fails to distinguish object parts from background and cannot match limited visible content under partial occlusion, leaving the spatial grounding of prompts uncontrolled.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261605/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 512×512) and a set of pre-generated region-level text prompts (K = C_cls × R, where R=3 regional prompts per class) as input, and produces a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask over 19 driving-scene classes. The text prompts are generated offline by an LLM using a structured template and encoded once by the frozen VLM text encoder; at inference, no target-domain data or additional text is required. Multi-scale features from both the VLM and DINOv2 encoders are concatenated and decoded by a Mask2Former head to yield the final mask.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-branch architecture combining a frozen VLM (CLIP-L or EVA02-L) with a frozen DINOv2-L encoder, where Spatial Hierarchical Prompts (SHP) decompose each class into R=3 region-level geometric anchors (each specifying Region Cue, Part, Geometry, and Boundary Relation) that provide complementary coverage under arbitrary viewpoints. The Hierarchical Prompt Injector (HPI) performs cross-attention between these prompt embeddings and patch tokens at deep encoder layers to produce prompt-conditioned residual updates, then gates each residual with two complementary confidence maps: Semantic Alignment Confidence (SAC) determines whether a prompt is relevant at a given patch, and Spatial Coherence Confidence (SCC) enforces 2D spatial continuity via multi-scale convolutions. Two auxiliary losses—Spatial Localization Loss (aligning prompt attention maps with ground-truth masks) and Semantic Consistency Loss (suppressing activations for absent classes)—directly supervise prompt-to-region correspondences that VLMs trained with image-level contrastive objectives lack natively. Only the HPI modules, linear fusion adapters, and Mask2Former decoder are trained; all foundation model weights remain frozen.

## Results

On the synthetic-to-real G→{Cityscapes, BDD100K, Mapillary} benchmark, HPI with CLIP-L achieves 70.62% average mIoU (74.05 / 63.14 / 74.67), and with EVA02-L achieves 69.39% average. On the real-to-real C→{BDD100K, Mapillary} setting, HPI with EVA02-L reaches 72.74% average mIoU (66.23 / 79.25). On the S→{C, B, M} setting, HPI (CLIP-L) attains 52.72% average mIoU, improving over the strongest multi-model baseline MFuser by 2.38 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HPI (CLIP-L) achieves 70.62% average mIoU on G→{C,B,M}, outperforming the strongest multi-model baseline MFuser (67.82%) by 2.80 mIoU and the best single-foundation method SoMA (68.27%) by 2.35 mIoU. The gain is most pronounced on Mapillary (+3.00 over SoMA) and Cityscapes (+2.23 over SoMA), while HPI trails FisherTune by 0.16 mIoU on BDD100K. On the real-to-real C→{B,M} setting, HPI (EVA02-L) reaches 72.74% average, surpassing SoMA (71.74%) by 1.00 and MFuser (71.87%) by 0.87, with the largest margin on Mapillary (+1.32 over SoMA). On the S→{C,B,M} setting, HPI (CLIP-L) improves over MFuser (CLIP-L) by 2.38 mIoU on average, with a 3.60 mIoU gain on BDD100K. Against earlier CNN-based baselines (SAN-SAW, WildNet, SHADE, TLDR, FAMix), HPI leads by 20+ mIoU on average, reflecting the advantage of foundation-model-based approaches over conventional backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-learning-for-DGSS line (CoOp, MAPLE, TQDM, DPMFormer) by replacing holistic class-level textual prompts with region-level geometric anchors (SHP) and by introducing spatially adaptive, confidence-gated injection (HPI) into frozen VLM and DINOv2 encoders. It also builds on the multi-foundation-model fusion paradigm established by CLOUDS and MFuser, but departs from those works by making the prompt signal spatially selective rather than uniformly applied, and by adding explicit auxiliary supervision (Spatial Localization and Semantic Consistency losses) to ground prompts onto object regions. The cross-attention-based residual formulation and the dual SAC/SCC gating mechanism are new architectural contributions not present in prior prompt-injection DGSS methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The region-level prompt decomposition addresses a concrete failure mode—occlusion and viewpoint change—that prior holistic prompts cannot handle, and the adaptive injection mechanism prevents negative transfer in background or occluded regions, making the approach more robust in cluttered driving scenes. The 2–3 mIoU gains over strong multi-model baselines (MFuser, SoMA) across multiple source-target pairs and backbones (CLIP-L, EVA02-L) suggest the technique is broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the LLM-generated regional prompts transfer to unseen categories or domains without regeneration, whether the dual-encoder (VLM + DINOv2) requirement limits practicality, and whether the approach extends beyond driving-scene segmentation to other dense prediction tasks.
