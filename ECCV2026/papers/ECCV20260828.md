## Problem

Generating coherent 4D scenes from a single static image requires inferring both 3D geometry and future motion simultaneously, yet existing paradigms decouple these: generate-then-reconstruct methods produce geometrically inconsistent multi-view videos, while reconstruct-then-generate methods are constrained to externally imposed motions on a fixed static structure. This decoupling causes spatiotemporal inconsistencies, structural collapse, and an inability to model self-initiated, large-scale scene dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a text prompt as input. It first estimates monocular depth via UniDepthv2 to initialize a dense point cloud (N=H×W points), then predicts per-point 3D displacement trajectories over 49 frames at 512×368 resolution. The final output is a dense 4D point cloud sequence P ∈ R^{T×N×3} rendered into novel-view dynamic videos along arbitrary user-defined camera trajectories via the 4D-ViSM module, which rasterizes per-frame 3D Gaussians and inpaints projection holes with a finetuned video diffusion model.

## Method

MoGe4D formulates 4D synthesis as geometry-conditioned dense trajectory prediction: a Diffusion Transformer (4D-STraG, built on Wan2.1-14B) predicts relative point displacements ΔP_t = P_t − P_0 in a unified flow-matching denoising process, with depth latents concatenated alongside image and noise latents to inject structural priors. Two key mechanisms ensure quality: (1) a depth-guided motion normalization that scales each point's displacement by its viewing-frustum width/height at the initial depth, making the target distribution scale-invariant across depth ranges; and (2) a Motion Perception Module (MPM) that extracts patch-level motion features via OmniMAE and applies token-wise MAdaNorm modulation inside each DiT block to guide where dynamic color (i.e., 3D displacement) should appear. A lightweight Trajectory Encoder/Decoder converts 3D displacements into RGB motion maps compatible with the pretrained VAE. For rendering, 4D-ViSM finetunes Wan2.1 with LoRA to inpaint holes in Gaussian-Splatting-rasterized frames, producing temporally consistent novel-view videos.

## Results

On VBench (Table 1), MoGe4D achieves the best Dynamic Degree (1.0000 in Group I, 0.9500 in Group II, 0.9000 in Group III), Aesthetic Quality (0.5613/0.4820/0.4820), and Imaging Quality (0.6230/0.5828/0.5939) across all three comparison groups. In the VLM-based 4D consistency evaluation (Table 2), MoGe4D attains average scores of 3.80, 3.83, and 3.76 (out of 5) in Groups I–III, substantially outperforming the strongest baselines (4Real: 3.35, DimensionX: 2.80, Gen3C: 2.22). For geometric accuracy, MoGe4D reduces mean trajectory error to 0.058 (Group II) and 0.042 (Group III), roughly 4–6× lower than the next-best method, while maintaining competitive reprojection errors (0.614/0.639). Inference on a single A100 GPU takes 6 minutes for 49 frames at 512×368, making it 5× faster than Free4D at equivalent resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 4Real (optimization-based, Group I), MoGe4D wins decisively on Dynamic Degree (1.00 vs 0.77), Aesthetic Quality (0.56 vs 0.49), and Imaging Quality (0.62 vs 0.51), but trails on Subject Consistency (0.88 vs 0.93) and Background Consistency (0.94 vs 0.97), reflecting 4Real's per-scene optimization advantage in static fidelity. In Group II against GenXD and DimensionX (single-image 3D reconstruction), MoGe4D leads on Subject Consistency (0.82 vs 0.80/0.76), Background Consistency (0.90 vs 0.88/0.85), Aesthetic Quality (0.48 vs 0.41/0.46), and Imaging Quality (0.58 vs 0.52/0.55), while GenXD and DimensionX match or slightly exceed it on Dynamic Degree (1.00 vs 0.95). In Group III against Free4D and Gen3C under complex multi-directional camera trajectories, MoGe4D dominates on Aesthetic Quality (0.48 vs 0.36/0.38) and Imaging Quality (0.59 vs 0.36/0.48) by wide margins, and leads on Subject and Background Consistency. In the VLM-based 4D consistency evaluation, MoGe4D's average scores (3.76–3.83) exceed the best baseline by 0.45 (vs 4Real) to 2.53 (vs Free4D), with the largest gaps in Motion-Geometry Coupling and Temporal Texture Stability. On trajectory error, MoGe4D (0.042–0.058) is 4–6× more accurate than Gen3C (0.197) and Free4D (0.252), confirming superior pixel-level 3D tracking coherence. In runtime, MoGe4D (6 min, 49 frames) is 5× faster than Free4D (30 min, 16 frames) and 8× faster than Gen3C (50 min, 121 frames) at comparable or higher resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoGe4D revisits the reconstruct-then-generate paradigm for single-image 4D synthesis (Animate124, 4D-fy, Gen3C) but fundamentally tightens the interface between static geometry and motion generation by conditioning a unified diffusion process on an initial geometric prior and predicting dense 4D point trajectories directly, rather than treating geometry and dynamics as disconnected stages. Architecturally, it builds on Wan2.1's VAE and DiT backbone, adapting them with a trajectory encoder/decoder, depth-guided motion normalization, and a Motion Perception Module (MAdaNorm) to inject motion-aware priors. The paper also departs from the generate-then-reconstruct line (4Real, DimensionX, Free4D) by avoiding the video-geometry mismatch that plagues those pipelines, and introduces TrajScene-60K to address the data scarcity that has constrained prior scene-level 4D generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tight geometry-motion coupling in a single diffusion process offers a principled alternative to cascaded pipelines, and the 5× speedup over Free4D at equivalent resolution (6 min vs. 30 min on A100) makes single-image 4D synthesis more practically deployable. TrajScene-60K (60K samples, 3M+ frames, ~12B 3D point annotations) fills a critical data gap for scene-level 4D training. However, the method remains bounded by monocular depth quality, struggling with extreme lighting and unstructured crowds, and the reliance on DELTA for pseudo-GT trajectories introduces a ceiling on geometric accuracy. Open questions include whether more robust visual foundation models can extend the approach to challenging photometric conditions, and whether the dense trajectory formulation can be made lighter for real-time or resource-constrained deployment.
