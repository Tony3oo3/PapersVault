## Problem

Existing diffusion-based dataset distillation methods either generate synthetic images (suffering from distribution shift between the diffusion prior's training data and the target dataset) or require fine-tuning the diffusion model for each new dataset. Additionally, these methods necessitate repeated distillation when changing class combinations or compression ratios (IPC), making them impractical for large-scale, multi-setting applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262165/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes original dataset images (resized so the smallest edge is 256 px), per-class label text prompts (e.g., 'An image of [class]'), and a frozen pre-trained Stable Diffusion model. It produces a compact synthetic dataset of 224×224 patches cropped from the original images; when IPC ≤ 10, multiple patches are concatenated into a single image following the RDED protocol, while for higher IPC each patch stands as an individual sample. The entire pipeline is one-step: the same distilled dataset serves all IPC settings and class subsets without re-running distillation.

## Method

The core idea is to use a pre-trained diffusion model as a patch-level localizer rather than an image generator, thereby sidestepping distributional bias. In Stage I, the method computes a differential noise map M(z|c) = E_{ε,t}[ε_θ(N_t(z,ε,t),t,c) − ε_θ(N_t(z,ε,t),t,φ)] via classifier-free guidance over timesteps sampled in [0.1, 0.7]; this map is upsampled to pixel resolution and average-pooled over 224×224 windows to yield a representativeness score R(p|c) per patch, from which the top-scoring patches per class are selected. In Stage II, DIFT intermediate-layer features (computed at t=160) are extracted for all selected patches and clustered via K-means (32 centers); clusters are ranked by their median R(p|c) and patches within each cluster by distance to the centroid, with the final dataset drawn from the top 10 clusters. This two-stage selection-and-aggregation pipeline enforces both class-relevant representativeness and intra-class diversity while requiring no model training or fine-tuning.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-18, the proposed method achieves 46.1% (IPC=10), 61.0% (IPC=50), and 63.0% (IPC=100) top-1 accuracy, consistently outperforming all baselines. On ImageWoof at IPC=10, the method reaches 44.5%, surpassing the next-best IGD (39.8%) by 4.7 points. On low-resolution datasets, the method achieves 66.2% on CIFAR-10 (IPC=50, ConvNet) and 58.5% on TinyImageNet (IPC=50, ResNet-18), matching or exceeding prior optimization-based methods like TESLA and RDED.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and IPC setting. IGD [4] and D³HR [34] are the strongest competitors on ImageNet-1K, with the proposed method exceeding them by 0.6–1.5 points (e.g., 61.0 vs. 60.3 at IPC=50). RDED [28] is competitive on ImageNette (61.4 vs. 61.7 at IPC=10) but falls behind by 1–3 points on ImageWoof and ImageNet-1K. SRe²L [31] is consistently the weakest baseline, trailing by 10–25 points. On low-resolution datasets (Table 2), the method surpasses optimization-based baselines DSA [32], IDM [33], and TESLA [14] on CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet (e.g., 63.8 vs. 62.3 for D⁴M on CIFAR-100 IPC=50 with ResNet-18), while remaining competitive with RDED on CIFAR-10. D⁴M [27] and MGD³ [3] (Stable Diffusion–based generative methods) are beaten by 1–4 points on high-resolution benchmarks, confirming that the selection-based paradigm avoids the distribution-shift issues inherent to generative approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of diffusion-based dataset distillation (Minimax, IGD, D⁴M, MGD³) that uses pre-trained diffusion models to *generate* synthetic images, instead repurposing the diffusion prior for *localization*—identifying the most class-relevant patches within real images via classifier-free-guidance differential noise maps. It builds directly on Li et al.'s (ICCV 2023) formulation of diffusion models as zero-shot classifiers and on Tang et al.'s (NeurIPS 2023) DIFT features for cross-image correspondence, combining both into a two-stage selection-and-clustering pipeline. The decoupled distillation framework established by SRe²L and RDED (which avoids pixel-level optimization) provides the practical scaffolding that makes large-scale distillation feasible, and this paper inherits that evaluation protocol while replacing their classifier-based patch scoring with a diffusion-based one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-step, training-free distillation process is a significant practical advance: it eliminates the combinatorial explosion of repeated distillation runs across class subsets and IPC settings that plagues all prior methods, and the 4× speedup from using only 300 source images makes the pipeline tractable on consumer GPUs. However, the method remains tied to the quality of the pre-trained Stable Diffusion prior, and its performance gains over strong baselines (e.g., IGD, D³HR) are consistent but modest (1–3 points on ImageNet-1K), leaving open whether the selection-only paradigm can match or exceed generative approaches when the diffusion prior is well-aligned with the target domain. A natural open question is whether combining selection with targeted generation (as the authors briefly note) could close the remaining gap, and whether the approach transfers to non-ImageNet domains where the SD prior may be less informative.
