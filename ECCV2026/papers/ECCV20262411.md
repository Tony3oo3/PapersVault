## Problem

Style transfer for structured visualizations (charts, diagrams, tables) demands capabilities fundamentally distinct from natural-image stylization: visual elements encode data through geometry, dense text must be preserved exactly, and style and content share the same visual vocabulary, causing leakage. Even frontier models like GPT-Image-1.5 and Nano-Banana-Pro produce inconsistent transfer, structural distortion, and content leakage, and the natural fix—training on forward-synthesized restyling data—inherits these same failures because the synthesis itself is unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262411/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes two RGB images simultaneously: a style reference chart (I_s) and a content chart (I_c), processed through a multi-image conditioning mechanism in the MMDiT architecture. The output is a single styled chart that must preserve the content's data-encoding geometry (bar heights, arc angles, point positions) and all textual labels exactly (verified by OCR F1), while adopting the style reference's color palette, typography, mark geometries, and decorative elements. Unlike natural-image style transfer, the fidelity constraint is zero-tolerance on geometry and text, making even small distortions semantically corrupting.

## Method

The core contribution is ChartForge, a reverse-generation data pipeline that inverts the natural synthesis direction: instead of restyling a content chart toward a style reference (where models fail), it first generates a stylized target from a style exemplar with independently sampled content, then restyles that target under a different visual family to produce the content counterpart, guaranteeing structural alignment and style-content disentanglement by construction. A style-space resampling stage recycles restyled variants as new style references to expand coverage beyond flat-design infographics, and a four-dimensional LLM+OCR filtering stage (visual quality, content consistency, stylistic consistency, OCR F1) retains only 100,744 high-quality triplets from ~308K candidates. ReChart is then obtained by progressively fine-tuning Qwen-Image-Edit in two stages: first on general-domain style transfer data to establish multi-image alignment, then on CHARTSTYLE-100K for chart-specific specialization, yielding the best overall performance while reducing content leakage to 0.05.

## Results

On CHARTSTYLE-BENCH (300 content–style pairs), ReChart achieves the best Overall score of 3.03 (harmonic mean of Content and Style, penalized for leakage), the highest Content Consistency of 3.96/5, the best Semantic Consistency of 0.89, and the lowest Content Leakage rate of 0.05 (tied with GPT-Image-1 and GPT-Image-1.5). Its OCR F1 of 0.76 is competitive with the strongest baselines (Nano-Banana 0.77, GPT-Image-1.5 0.73). ReChart's Style Similarity (2.90) trails Nano-Banana-Pro (3.58) and Nano-Banana-2 (3.75), and its CLIP Stylistic Fidelity (0.49) is below several open-source editors, indicating a trade-off between structural fidelity and aggressive style adoption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest commercial baselines, ReChart outperforms GPT-Image-1.5 on Content (3.96 vs 3.30), Overall (3.03 vs 2.82), Semantic (0.89 vs 0.80), and OCR (0.76 vs 0.73), while GPT-Image-1.5 edges ahead on Style (3.04 vs 2.90). Versus Nano-Banana-Pro, ReChart leads on Content (3.96 vs 3.79), Overall (3.03 vs 2.84), Semantic (0.89 vs 0.77), and Leakage (0.05 vs 0.32), but Nano-Banana-Pro scores higher on Style (3.58 vs 2.90) and Fidelity (0.54 vs 0.49). Compared to its base model Qwen-Image-Edit, ReChart improves Content by +1.21, Overall by +1.45, and reduces Leakage from 0.51 to 0.05, at the cost of a slight Fidelity drop (0.62→0.49). Traditional style-transfer methods (CSGO, StyleStudio) and open-source editors (FLUX.2-dev, Edit-R1, OmniGen2) are far behind on all metrics, with Overall scores below 1.4 and severe content leakage or rendering artifacts. The paper also reports a user study where ReChart achieves a 71.9% non-loss rate against GPT-Image-1.5 and 61.1% against Nano-Banana-Pro across 920 pairwise comparisons per baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the style transfer literature (CSGO, StyleStudio, InstantStyle, IP-Adapter) from natural images to structured visualizations, where data-encoding geometry and dense text impose zero-tolerance fidelity constraints absent in prior formulations. It builds directly on Qwen-Image-Edit (a flow-matching MMDiT editor) as the base model, applying a two-stage progressive fine-tuning curriculum. The key methodological departure is ChartForge, a reverse-generation data pipeline that synthesizes the stylized target first and then derives a structure-aligned content image from it, inverting the natural forward-synthesis direction that prior chart editing work (ChartGalaxy, ChartMimic, ChartLLaMA) implicitly assumes. This avoids the style-content leakage that forward pipelines inherit from the very models they rely on for generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CHARTSTYLE-100K and CHARTSTYLE-BENCH are the first large-scale resources dedicated to structured visualization style transfer, filling a gap where no prior dataset or benchmark existed for this specific task. The finding that all current frontier editors (GPT-Image-1.5, Nano-Banana-Pro, FLUX.2-dev) exhibit significant content leakage or structural distortion on charts suggests the task is genuinely harder than general image editing, and the reverse-generation data construction principle may generalize to other structured-visual editing domains (diagrams, tables, flowcharts). A persistent open challenge identified across all models is multi-scale text rendering: OCR F1 drops sharply below 14px for every method tested, indicating that robust small-text preservation remains unsolved. The modest overall performance gap between ReChart and the best commercial baselines (3.03 vs. 2.84) suggests further gains will require architectural advances beyond data curation alone.
