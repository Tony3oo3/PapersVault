## Problem

Existing monocular dynamic 3D reconstruction methods fail to produce consistent 360° geometry because their reliance on 2D-native tracking priors causes initialized 3D points to overfit to visible surfaces, leaving occluded regions geometrically ambiguous and unrecoverable during optimization. Additionally, no existing benchmark provides sufficient train-test view disparity (up to 135°) to properly evaluate true 360° reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261720/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a casual monocular video sequence, per-frame depth maps (from LiDAR or pretrained estimators like Video Depth Anything), and camera intrinsics/extrinsics. It outputs a 4D Gaussian representation consisting of static anisotropic Gaussians in a canonical frame, each deformed over time by SE(3) transformations derived from a hierarchical motion tree, enabling differentiable rasterization-based rendering from arbitrary viewpoints including extreme extrapolations up to 135° from training cameras.

## Method

The core contribution is AnchorTAP3D, a 3D-native tracker that resolves the depth ambiguity of occluded regions by conditioning a transformer-based 3D tracker (TAPIP3D backbone) on high-confidence 2D track points filtered by a confidence threshold and lifted to 3D via inverse projection. Within a sliding temporal window (L=16 frames, 8-frame overlap), these anchor points provide geometry-consistent spatial-temporal constraints that suppress the error drift inherent in naive 3D tracking. The resulting 3D trajectories initialize a HiMoR-style hierarchical motion representation where trajectories are clustered by velocity via k-means, rigid motions are estimated per cluster via Procrustes alignment, and a tree of nodes encodes shared motion bases. Optimization then applies ARAP rigidity regularization (pairwise distance and relative transformation consistency across non-adjacent frames) alongside RGB (D-SSIM + LPIPS), mask, depth consistency, and 2D tracking losses to refine the 4D Gaussian representation.

## Results

On the iPhone dataset (mean over 5 scenes), 4DGS360 achieves CLIP-I 0.9015, CLIP-T 0.9754, and LPIPS 0.3877, outperforming the strongest baseline HiMoR (0.8907, 0.9750, 0.4639) by a notable margin on LPIPS (−0.076). On the newly introduced iPhone360 benchmark (extreme 70°–135° novel views), 4DGS360 attains the best CLIP-I on 5 of 6 scenes (e.g., 0.9021 on Block2, 0.9147 on Jacket) and the best LPIPS on 4 of 6 scenes. At pixel level, the method improves PSNR from 16.24 to 16.34 on iPhone and from 10.99 to 11.34 on iPhone360 over HiMoR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

4DGS360 outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric across both the iPhone and iPhone360 datasets. Against the closest competitor HiMoR [25], the gains are most pronounced on LPIPS (0.3877 vs 0.4639 on iPhone mean; 0.2569 vs 0.3122 on iPhone360 Block2), indicating substantially better perceptual fidelity, while CLIP-I and CLIP-T improvements are more modest (+0.011 and +0.0004 on iPhone mean). Against SoM [49], the advantage is larger on CLIP-I (+0.041) and LPIPS (−0.095) on iPhone mean, reflecting SoM's weaker handling of occluded geometry. On the iPhone360 extreme-view benchmark, MoSca [23] trails 4DGS360 by 0.05–0.21 in CLIP-I depending on scene, confirming that 2D-track-based initialization degrades sharply under large view extrapolation. NeRF-based baselines (T-NeRF [11], HyperNeRF [35]) and earlier 3DGS methods (Deformable 3DGS [62], Marbles [44]) are consistently outperformed by 0.03–0.11 in CLIP-I, with the largest gaps on LPIPS (up to 0.33 lower for Deformable 3DGS). The ablation further shows that replacing AnchorTAP3D with naive 2D unprojection or unanchored 3D tracking (TAPIP3D) degrades occluded-region reconstruction, validating that the anchor-guided initialization is the key differentiator over all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on HiMoR's hierarchical motion representation (tree-structured nodes with shared motion bases) and TAPIP3D's transformer-based 3D tracking architecture, but departs from the dominant initialization paradigm in monocular dynamic 4D reconstruction. Prior methods (MoSca, SoM, HiMoR) initialize 3D Gaussians by unprojecting 2D track points with per-frame depth maps, which the authors identify as the root cause of occluded-region geometry failure. AnchorTAP3D inverts this logic: it uses high-confidence 2D tracks as spatial-temporal anchors to condition a 3D tracker, thereby resolving depth ambiguity for occluded points without additional training. The paper also extends the iPhone dataset into iPhone360, increasing train-test angular disparity from <45° to 70°–135° to enable genuine 360° evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The iPhone360 benchmark fills a concrete gap: no prior monocular dynamic dataset allowed evaluation at viewpoints more than ~45° from training, making '360° reconstruction' claims unverifiable. The finding that ARAP rigidity regularization was silently failing on corrupted initial geometry (rather than the optimization being insufficient) reframes where effort should be invested in this task line. Open questions remain: the method still cannot synthesize backgrounds invisible in the input video (requiring diffusion priors), pixel-level metrics (PSNR/SSIM) remain low under extreme extrapolation, and overall performance is bounded by the quality of pretrained 2D/3D trackers. The demonstrated compatibility with DIFIX3D+ suggests a promising path toward full 360° including unseen backgrounds.
