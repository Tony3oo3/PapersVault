## Problem

Existing LiDAR generation methods produce only isolated single frames or non-interactive 4D sequences, while LiDAR prediction methods require multiple historical frames and can only deterministically forecast along fixed trajectories. Neither paradigm supports the long-horizon, frame-by-frame, interactive generation needed for closed-loop autonomous driving simulation where only an initial frame is available and the ego vehicle's trajectory evolves with each decision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261742/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

LaGen takes a single-frame LiDAR point cloud as the sole historical input, augmented per-frame by 3D bounding boxes with semantic labels (produced by the simulator from the previous action, not future information) and ego-vehicle states (speed, acceleration, steering angle, and ego-to-global transformation). The 3D point cloud is converted to a 2-channel range image (depth + intensity) via spherical projection before entering the latent diffusion pipeline. Output is a sequence of LiDAR point clouds generated autoregressively, one frame at a time, forming a temporally consistent 4D scene.

## Method

LaGen builds a Latent Diffusion Model (VAE + UNet with Transformer layers) over range images, guided by a multimodal conditional encoder that injects the previous frame's latent (modulated by FiLM with the relative sensor transformation), per-category bounding-box projection masks (via cross-attention using current and previous frame features as key/value), and ego-state embeddings (via timestep embedding + cross-attention). Two key modules enhance spatiotemporal consistency: a Scene Decoupling Estimation (SDE) module that splits the previous frame into foreground (points within bounding boxes) and background, estimates their current-frame positions via nearest-center matching across adjacent frames, and feeds these object-level estimates into the diffusion process; and a Noise Modulation (NM) module that injects Gaussian noise of randomly sampled intensity into the previous frame's latent features, reducing over-reliance on potentially erroneous autoregressive inputs and mitigating error accumulation over long horizons.

## Results

On nuScenes, LaGen achieves an MMD of 0.11×10⁻⁴ and JSD of 2.77×10⁻², outperforming the best conditional baseline (LiDARCrafter: MMD 1.52, JSD 5.43) by over 13× on MMD and ~2× on JSD. On KITTI-360, LaGen reduces MMD to 1.41×10⁻⁴ (vs. 13.36 for LiDARGen) and JSD to 5.75×10⁻² (vs. 9.31 for LiDM). For long-horizon prediction at 9.5 s, LaGen (with bounding boxes) achieves a Chamfer Distance of 2.34 m² and L1 error of 0.32 m, compared to 55.47 m² and 9.95 m for 4D-Occ (2-frame input), demonstrating a >20× reduction in error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaGen decisively outperforms all single-frame generation baselines (LiDARGen, LiDM, RangeLDM) on both MMD and JSD, with MMD reduced by 17×, 30×, and 17× respectively. Among conditional 4D scene generation methods, LaGen beats UniScene (MMD 2.40→0.11), OpenDWM (2.21→0.11), DriveLiDAR4D (1.84→0.11), and LiDARCrafter (1.52→0.11) on generation fidelity, and also leads on temporal consistency (CTC at 4 frames: 2.22 vs. 3.90–5.05 for competitors; CTC at 10 frames: 3.84, the only method reporting this horizon). On the long-horizon prediction task, LaGen with a single input frame surpasses 4D-Occ (2-frame and 6-frame) and ViDAR (2-frame and 6-frame) across all horizons up to 9.5 s; for example, at 5.5 s the Chamfer Distance is 1.69 m² (LaGen w/ BBox) versus 7.73–12.30 m² for 4D-Occ and 3.25–3.79 m² for ViDAR, and the Absrel error is 3.40% versus 29–40% for 4D-Occ and 30–36% for ViDAR. The box-free variant of LaGen still outperforms all prediction baselines, confirming that the model captures underlying scene dynamics even without explicit object conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaGen builds on the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) architecture and the range-image representation for LiDAR data established by Caccia et al. and RangeLDM, but departs from prior LiDAR generation work (LiDARGen, LiDM, UniScene, OpenDWM, LiDARCrafter) by making generation autoregressive and frame-by-frame rather than one-shot or non-interactive. It also diverges from LiDAR prediction methods (4D-Occ, ViDAR) that require multiple historical frames, instead operating from a single initial frame with bounding-box and ego-state conditioning. The noise modulation strategy draws conceptually from Diffusion Forcing and StreetScapes in the video generation literature, adapting the idea of injecting noise into conditioning to mitigate train-inference mismatch in autoregressive settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LaGen addresses a practical gap in autonomous driving: the inability to run closed-loop LiDAR simulation where the ego vehicle's trajectory changes at each step, since existing prediction methods are locked to fixed multi-frame inputs and predetermined trajectories. The interactive, frame-by-frame generation enables mid-sequence editing (moving or removing objects) that naturally supports what-if scenario evaluation. Limitations include restriction to single-return spinning LiDAR, potential failure of the nearest-distance object matching in crowded scenes, and the fact that bounding-box conditioning, while effective, constrains the model to simulator-provided ground-truth boxes rather than learned perception outputs. Open questions include extension to multi-return and solid-state LiDAR, and whether the framework can operate without explicit bounding-box supervision in fully end-to-end settings.
