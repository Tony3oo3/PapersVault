## Problem

Applying SAM2-style streaming video segmentation to 3D medical volumes by treating axial depth as time is unstable: anatomical structures branch, merge, or vanish across slices, and the standard FIFO memory queue accumulates early errors into later slices (memory drift). Static spatial prompts on a single slice provide no guidance for disconnected or newly appearing components, and existing promptable medical adaptations still rely heavily on local appearance consistency without an explicit model of cross-slice anatomical evolution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an anisotropic 3D CT or MRI volume (standardized to 1.0 mm³ isotropic spacing, intensity-clipped to the 0.5–99.5 percentile), a single interactive prompt (bounding box or click) placed on one representative center slice, and a short LLM-generated anatomical description (e.g., 'pancreas transitions from a bulbous head to a tapering tail') encoded by BioMedCLIP. It outputs a dense volumetric binary mask of shape T×1×H×W, propagated bidirectionally from the prompted slice through the entire volume, with the SAM2-Hiera-Large backbone kept frozen throughout inference.

## Method

DiffuPrompt augments frozen SAM2 with two trainable components: (1) an LLM-conditioned Neural ODE that, starting from a VAE-encoded latent of the prompted slice (image ∥ mask), integrates a semantic-conditioned UNet vector field along the axial axis to produce a continuous latent trajectory, which is projected through a VAE decoder and SAM2 memory encoder into a persistent prior memory bank of key-value pairs; and (2) a dual-stream spatial gate that, at each slice, retrieves features from both the prior memory and SAM2's observation-driven working memory via separate cross-attention passes, then fuses them with a per-pixel sigmoid gate (MLP over concatenated query and both retrieved features) so that image evidence dominates where it is reliable and the trajectory prior fills in ambiguous or topologically changing regions. Training uses supervised volumetric masks with a combined Dice + BCE segmentation loss, a latent consistency term, and an adjacent-slice smoothness regularizer on the ODE states, while only LoRA adapters (r=8) on the image encoder, the ODE vector field, and the gate MLP are updated.

## Results

Under a single center-slice bounding-box prompt with a frozen SAM2 backbone, DiffuPrompt achieves 81.2% DSC (HD95 4.92 mm, NSD 79.6%) on MSD (10-task macro average), 82.4% DSC (HD95 5.64 mm, NSD 80.4%) on BraTS 2023, and 82.7% DSC (HD95 4.46 mm, NSD 80.5%) on BTCV (13 organs). These are the best promptable results in the comparison, exceeding the strongest promptable baselines (AoP-SAM, CA-SAM2) by 1.7–4.2 DSC points across datasets. The method narrows the gap to fully supervised closed-set models (nnU-Net) to within ~3 DSC points while operating in a single-prompt, backbone-frozen setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffuPrompt outperforms all 11 promptable baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest promptable competitors, it gains +1.7 DSC over AoP-SAM on MSD (81.2 vs 79.5), +1.9 DSC over AoP-SAM on BraTS (82.4 vs 80.5), and +4.2 DSC over CA-SAM2 on BTCV (82.7 vs 78.5), with corresponding HD95 reductions of 0.83 mm, 0.57 mm, and 3.44 mm respectively. The advantage over natural video tracking models (Vanilla SAM2, SAM2Long, Efficient-SAM2) is substantial—15–24 DSC points—confirming the memory-drift failure mode the paper targets. Against fully supervised closed-set references, DiffuPrompt trails nnU-Net by 3.0–3.5 DSC and SwinUNETR by 1.1–1.4 DSC, but operates under a fundamentally different protocol (single prompt, frozen backbone, no per-organ training labels). Notably, DiffuPrompt also shows the lowest DSC standard deviation among promptable methods on BraTS (±3.6 vs ±4.8 for AoP-SAM), indicating more stable propagation across heterogeneous tumor cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM2 video-object-segmentation paradigm to 3D medical volumes by treating axial depth as a temporal axis, building directly on SAM2's streaming memory architecture and prior medical adaptations (MoE-SAM, GA-SAM, VP-SAM, CA-SAM2). Its key departure from existing memory-augmented VOS methods (RMem, SAM2Long, MA-SAM2) is the introduction of a persistent, LLM-conditioned Neural ODE trajectory prior that exists independently of observed frame history, fused with the standard FIFO working memory through a learned spatial gate. The use of Neural ODEs for continuous anatomical evolution and LLM-generated organ descriptors as conditioning signals repurposes ideas from generative medical imaging and language-conditioned diffusion into a segmentation memory context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and clinically relevant failure mode—memory drift in SAM2-style axial propagation when structures branch, disappear, or lose contrast—offering a practical backbone-frozen, single-prompt solution that narrows the gap to fully supervised models (e.g., 82.7% vs. 86.2% DSC on BTCV) without requiring task-specific training data. A key open question the authors flag is the risk of trajectory priors conflicting with genuine pathology or unusual anatomy, pointing toward the need for explicit uncertainty calibration and image-versus-prior disagreement detection. The method's reliance on a frozen SAM2-Hiera-Large backbone and lightweight LoRA adapters makes it relatively accessible, but the ~4-point DSC gap to nnU-Net and the single-prompt protocol limit its applicability to dense, multi-organ, or fully automated clinical pipelines.
