## Problem

Existing multi-agent discussion attacks assume adversarial messages are freely exchanged, yet practical deployments increasingly deploy anomaly detectors to monitor and block such messages. This paper identifies the resulting discussion-monitored scenario as largely unexplored and asks whether monitoring alone is sufficient to secure multi-agent discussions, or whether effective stealthy attacks remain possible under continuous detection constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261803/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multi-agent discussion instance defined by a directed graph topology over N agents, T discussion rounds, and multimodal inter-agent messages (text plus images such as sketches). From these observable interactions it recovers Friedkin-Johnsen parameters (intrinsic opinions, stubbornness matrix, influence matrix) via calibrated LLM-based evaluation and constrained least-squares. The output is a discrete adversarial configuration: a set of agents to hijack (bounded to (N−1)/3) and, for each hijacked agent, a set of target neighbors to adversarially influence (bounded to (M−1)/3 per agent), computed in an average of 0.54 seconds.

## Method

The core contribution is an adversarial-aware opinion-dynamics formulation that extends the Friedkin-Johnsen model with two modifications: adversarial agents are modeled as very stubborn broadcasters with fixed expressed opinion at the adversarial stance, and their selective influence on targets is captured as a small additive increment p to the corresponding influence-matrix entries with proportional rescaling of remaining weights. This yields a closed-form expression (Eq. 4) for the expected final discussion outcome under any adversarial configuration. To solve the resulting combinatorial optimization over agent and target selections, the paper reformulates it as a Stackelberg leader-follower problem: the leader sub-problem (selecting adversarial agents) is solved by enumeration, while the follower sub-problem (assigning targets) is shown via a Taylor expansion under the stealthiness constraint to reduce to at most (2N²−N−1)/9 closed-form sub-problems (Theorem 1). The derived configuration is then plugged into existing attack frameworks (MCA, MAD-Spear) by replacing their original agent-selection heuristics, achieving over 40% attack success while maintaining detection rates below 9%.

## Results

Under the discussion-monitored scenario, the proposed formulation-driven attack strategy achieves ΔAcc of 43.5% and ΔAgr_adv of 49.5% (SelfCheckGPT) when integrated with MAD-Spear on MMLU, and 45.3% / 51.1% on MMMU, while maintaining detection rates below 7%. For MCA, the same strategy yields ΔAcc of 28.7% (MMLU) and 32.6% (MMMU). These results are 4–10× higher than the original agent-selection strategies of the respective baselines (e.g., 6.9% for MAD-Spear original on MMLU) and 2–3× higher than the best heuristic variant, all while keeping detection rates comparable to or lower than the baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed formulation-driven agent-selection strategy against the original strategies of two baseline attacks—MCA [3] and MAD-Spear [6]—as well as six heuristic variants (based on out-degree, persona similarity, persuasiveness, stubbornness, and intrinsic opinion). On MMLU with SelfCheckGPT, the proposed strategy with MAD-Spear achieves ΔAcc of 43.5%, outperforming MAD-Spear's original strategy (6.9%) by over 6× and the best heuristic variant (22.1%) by nearly 2×. Similarly, with MCA, the proposed strategy reaches 28.7% ΔAcc versus 2.6% for MCA's original strategy and 14.2% for the best variant. Critically, these gains come without increasing detection rates: the proposed strategy maintains detection rates of 4.3–6.6% (MMLU) and 3.4–5.4% (MMMU), comparable to or slightly lower than the baselines, confirming that the attack remains stealthy under continuous anomaly monitoring.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Friedkin-Johnsen (FJ) opinion-dynamics model from sociology [10] into an adversarial-aware formulation that captures two behaviors absent from the standard model: adversarial agents as fixed-stance broadcasters and selective influence toward designated targets. For solving the resulting combinatorial optimization, it adapts the Stackelberg leader-follower decomposition from opinion-control literature [20], which renders an otherwise intractable search (exponential in N) solvable in under a second. Conceptually, it departs from prior multi-agent attack work (MCA [3], MAD-Spear [6]) by introducing the discussion-monitored scenario, where anomaly detectors (SelfCheckGPT, RAGAs, G-Safeguard) continuously block adversarial messages — a constraint those prior methods do not account for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding — that a principled, formulation-driven attack still achieves 40%+ success under continuous anomaly monitoring — challenges the assumption that deploying standard detectors is sufficient to secure multi-agent discussions. This has direct implications for practitioners deploying multi-agent debate systems in high-stakes domains (medical diagnosis, legal prediction) who may currently rely on message-level monitoring as their primary defense. Open questions include: what complementary defenses (e.g., agent-level authentication, consensus mechanisms) would actually neutralize such attacks, whether the FJ-based formulation generalizes to non-GPT-4o base models or larger agent populations, and whether the low detection rates (4–8%) observed here reflect a fundamental limitation of current anomaly detectors or an artifact of the specific stealth-oriented prompt engineering used.
