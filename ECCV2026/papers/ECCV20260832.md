## Problem

Quadruped vision-language-action systems struggle to bridge high-level semantic reasoning with low-level continuous locomotion control. Existing approaches either map language directly to actions (blurring the reasoning-execution boundary and yielding unstable long-horizon grounding) or use latent intermediate representations that sacrifice transparent, inspectable reasoning structure. This paper addresses the gap by explicitly coupling structured chain-of-thought planning with executable continuous quadruped commands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260832/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes synchronized RGB images, depth maps, and LiDAR point clouds alongside a natural-language instruction at each timestep. Multimodal observations are tokenized by modality-specific encoders (DepthAnything V2, Point Transformer v3) and projected into the LLaMA3-8B hidden space via learnable linear adapters. The output is a single structured string containing a reasoning trace in <think> tags followed by an executable command in <answer> tags, which is deterministically parsed into planar velocity (Vx, Vy), yaw rate (ω), and a discrete high-level behavior token (e.g., posture or skill switching).

## Method

MobileVLA-R1 follows a reasoning-then-execution design: a NaVILA-initialized LLaVA-style backbone first generates a multi-granularity chain-of-thought plan conditioned on fused RGB-depth-point tokens and the instruction, then emits a machine-parseable command that is decoded into continuous locomotion velocities and discrete behaviors. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) supervised fine-tuning on MobileVLA-CoT, a 134K-sample multi-granularity dataset (episode-, step-, and navigation-level CoT traces synthesized from R2R, RxR, and QUARD via Gemini 2.5-Flash) to establish the structured reasoning format and action executability; (2) offline GRPO that samples N=8 candidate outputs per input, scores them with a weighted combination of movement reward (cosine similarity in a normalized velocity-yaw space), discrete action reward (binary match), and format reward (template adherence), and updates the policy using group-relative advantages with KL regularization to a frozen SFT reference. LoRA (r=16) is applied to projection layers and attention blocks while all modality encoders remain frozen, keeping the pretrained visual representations intact.

## Results

On VLN-CE Val-Unseen, MobileVLA-R1 achieves SR of 68.3% and SPL of 65.2 on R2R-CE (NE=4.05), and SR of 71.5% and SPL of 66.8 on RxR-CE (NE=3.92, nDTW=76.1), outperforming the strongest prior method CorrectNav (SR 65.1%, SPL 62.3%) by 3.2 and 2.9 points respectively. On the QUARD quadruped benchmark, MobileVLA-R1 attains an average success rate of 0.73 across six tasks, surpassing MoRE (0.60) and QUART (0.44). In real-world Unitree Go2 deployment, MobileVLA-R1 reaches 93–100% SR on Workspace and Corridor tasks with NE as low as 0.96 m, compared to 80–89% SR for NaVILA and 0–67% for GPT-4o.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On VLN-CE, MobileVLA-R1 outperforms all 22 compared navigation baselines on both R2R-CE and RxR-CE. The closest competitor, CorrectNav [63], achieves SR 65.1% and SPL 62.3% on R2R-CE, while MobileVLA-R1 improves to 68.3% and 65.2% (+3.2 SR, +2.9 SPL); on RxR-CE the gap widens to +2.2 SR and +3.5 SPL. Against the direct architectural predecessor NaVILA [12], MobileVLA-R1 gains +14.3 SR and +16.2 SPL on R2R-CE, and +22.2 SR and +22.8 SPL on RxR-CE. RL-based VLN-R1 [41] and video-based Uni-NaVid [64] trail by 38.1 and 21.3 SR points respectively on R2R-CE. On QUARD, MobileVLA-R1 (avg 0.73) beats MoRE [67] (0.60) by 13 points and QUART [14] (0.44) by 29 points, with the largest margins on hard tasks (Crawl: 0.58 vs 0.49; Unload: 0.44 vs 0.33). In real-world deployment, MobileVLA-R1 surpasses NaVILA [12] by 10–13 SR points on Complex instructions and GPT-4o [29] by up to 96 SR points on Corridor-Complex, confirming that structured CoT reasoning plus GRPO yields more reliable long-horizon execution than either a VLA backbone alone or a general-purpose LLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

MobileVLA-R1 builds directly on NaVILA as its VLA backbone and adopts the LLaVA-style encoder-projector-LLM architecture, extending it with depth and point-cloud encoders for richer 3D perception. Conceptually, it departs from prior quadruped VLA work (QUART, MoRE) that generates policies directly from observations by inserting an explicit multi-granularity Chain-of-Thought reasoning layer between language understanding and continuous motor commands. The training paradigm combines supervised CoT alignment with offline GRPO (from the DeepSeekMath line of work), applying group-relative policy optimization—previously used for LLM mathematical reasoning—to structured embodied action generation, a combination not previously explored in the quadruped navigation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit structured reasoning (CoT) plus offline RL refinement can meaningfully close the gap between high-level instruction semantics and low-level continuous locomotion on physical quadruped platforms, validated by real-world Unitree Go2 deployment. The MobileVLA-CoT dataset (134K multi-granularity samples) provides a reusable resource for training reasoning-aligned embodied agents. Key limitations include the dependence on remote H20 inference for the 8B backbone (205–245 ms latency), which constrains fully autonomous deployment in bandwidth-limited or highly dynamic settings, and the evaluation is limited to a single robot platform and a small number of real-world episodes (160). Open questions include whether the CoT+GRPO paradigm transfers to other legged morphologies or manipulation-heavy tasks, and whether the reasoning overhead can be reduced for fully onboard execution.
