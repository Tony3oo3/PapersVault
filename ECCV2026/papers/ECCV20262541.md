## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) adapters for Vision Transformers refine each token independently through identical bottleneck transformations, implicitly assuming adaptation should occur in token space. This token-wise formulation overlooks the structured relationships among tokens that naturally arise in visual scenes (objects, parts, semantic components), potentially causing redundant updates and spatially inconsistent feature refinement. The paper identifies the choice of adaptation space as a critical yet underexplored design dimension in PEFT.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images processed by a frozen ViT backbone (ViT-B/16, ViT-L/16, or Swin-Base) into patch token embeddings of shape N×D, and produces class labels for downstream classification. The adapter module operates on the patch tokens (excluding the [CLS] token) at each transformer block, outputting a residual update added to the original embeddings. Evaluation spans 19 VTAB-1k tasks (800 training samples each) and five few-shot FGVC benchmarks under 1–16 shot settings, with the backbone fully frozen and only the adapter modules plus classification head trained.

## Method

HyperAdapter shifts adaptation from token space to hyperedge space by constructing a soft hypergraph over ViT patch tokens. K learnable prototype vectors serve as latent hyperedges; each token is softly assigned to hyperedges via temperature-scaled cosine routing, producing a differentiable N×K incidence matrix. Token features are aggregated into K compact hyperedge representations, refined through a lightweight bottleneck adapter (down-project to rank r=8, nonlinearity, up-project), and the resulting updates are diffused back to tokens via the incidence matrix with a learnable scaling factor. The design is a drop-in parallel replacement for standard adapters, and the paper proves that token-wise adapters are recovered as the special case K=N with M=I, while also establishing permutation equivariance and a low-rank update structure of rank at most min(K, r).

## Results

On VTAB-1k with a ViT-B/16 backbone, HyperAdapter achieves 77.6% average accuracy across 19 tasks using only 0.44M trainable parameters, outperforming the strongest PEFT baselines (Res-Tuning 76.3%, RepAdapter 76.1%, SSF 75.7%) and full fine-tuning (68.9% with 85.8M params). Gains are most pronounced on Structured tasks (+1.9% over the token-wise adapter baseline), reflecting the benefit of hyperedge-level grouping for spatially structured reasoning. On ViT-L/16, HyperAdapter reaches 77.7% average (1.18M params), and on Swin-Base it achieves 77.6% (0.60M params), confirming architecture-agnostic effectiveness. In few-shot FGVC settings (1–16 shots), HyperAdapter consistently leads, with the largest margins in the ultra-low-shot (1–4) regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperAdapter (77.6%) outperforms all 12 PEFT baselines on VTAB-1k average accuracy. The closest competitors are Res-Tuning (76.3%, +1.3 gap), RepAdapter (76.1%, +1.5 gap), and SSF (75.7%, +1.9 gap); the largest margin is over BitFit (65.2%, +12.4). On Structured tasks specifically, HyperAdapter's advantage is most visible: it surpasses LoRA (69.2% structured) by a wide margin while using fewer parameters (0.44M vs. 0.29M for LoRA, but LoRA's structured score is actually higher at 69.2 vs. 63.8 — the overall win comes from Natural and Specialized consistency). Against the strongest adapter-based methods (AdaptFormer 74.7%, ARC 75.8%), HyperAdapter gains 2.8–2.9 points with a comparable parameter budget (0.44M vs. 0.13–0.16M). On the larger ViT-L/16 backbone, HyperAdapter (77.7%) beats RepAdapter (76.8%) and ARC (75.1%) by 0.9 and 2.6 points respectively, and on Swin-Base it edges out RepAdapter (77.0%) by 0.6 points while using 0.60M vs. 0.39M parameters. The ablation in Table 4 confirms that the gain over a token-wise adapter with matched capacity (+1.3%) comes from the hyperedge routing mechanism itself, not from additional parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bottleneck adapter architecture of Houlsby et al. (2019) by reformulating the adaptation operation from token space to a learned hyperedge space, where tokens are softly grouped via prototype-based routing before a shared low-rank transformation is applied. It departs from the implicit token-wise assumption shared by all major PEFT families (adapters, LoRA, prompt tuning, scaling & shifting) by arguing that the *adaptation space* itself is an underexplored design dimension. The hypergraph machinery is borrowed from vision GNN and hypergraph literature (Vision HGNN, hypergraph ViTs), but is repurposed here as a lightweight, modular routing layer inside a frozen-backbone adapter rather than as a backbone modification, and the authors prove that standard token-wise adapters are a strict special case (K=N, M=I).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'adaptation space' framing gives the PEFT community a new axis along which to design adapters, and the drop-in, backbone-agnostic design (works on ViT-B, ViT-L, Swin-Base) makes it easy to adopt. Gains are most pronounced on Structured VTAB tasks (+1.9 pp over the token-wise baseline), suggesting that explicit group-level modeling helps when spatial/relational reasoning matters. Open questions include whether the static prototype-based hypergraph can be made dynamic per input, whether the approach transfers to generative or multimodal ViTs, and whether the modest parameter overhead (0.44 M vs. 0.29 M for the token-wise baseline) is justified at larger scale. The paper does not explore multi-task or continual-learning settings where the structural inductive bias might be most valuable.
