## Problem

LVLMs inherit CLIP-pretrained vision encoders yet underperform CLIP-based methods at zero-shot and few-shot image classification, revealing a mismatch between their rich internal representations and weak final output predictions. This gap is particularly surprising because LVLMs jointly process vision and text tokens through a shared LLM decoder, which should enable stronger multimodal reasoning than CLIP's independent encoders. The paper investigates whether LVLMs' internal attention heads, combined with prompt conditioning, can unlock classification performance that the model's own output fails to deliver.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262352/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB query images, a small labeled support set (K images per class), and a text prompt that can be conditioned at three levels (task, domain, class). Rather than using the LVLM's final output token, it extracts L2-normalized attention vectors from the last token position across all heads in every LLM decoder layer. Output is a set of class probabilities produced by averaging softmax predictions over a sparse ensemble of top-ranked heads, with three variants: HEC-V (vision-heads, few-shot), HEC-T (text-heads, zero-shot), and HEC-VT (combined, vision-text few-shot). The text-head set is selected once per model on ImageNet and reused across all downstream tasks.

## Method

HEC operates in two stages: prompt conditioning and head selection. First, a text prompt (e.g., 'What breed is that dog?') is concatenated with vision tokens before the LLM decoder, steering the summary token and attention heads toward domain- and class-discriminative representations. Second, for each of the M = L×H attention heads, the method computes an attention vector for the last token and ranks heads using a GDA-derived soft-accuracy score on the support set (for vision-heads) or a zero-shot dot-product score against class text embeddings (for text-heads). The top-k heads are then ensembled by averaging their per-class softmax probabilities, yielding a training-free classifier. The GDA formulation assumes a shared covariance across classes, which stabilizes estimation under few-shot conditions and captures anisotropic feature geometry. Three classifier variants combine vision-heads (HEC-V), text-heads (HEC-T), or both (HEC-VT) with a weighted sum, requiring no fine-tuning and only a single hyperparameter α for the combined variant.

## Results

On the vision-text-few-shot setting (N-way 4-shot, 12 datasets), HEC-VT achieves 83.0% average accuracy, outperforming all LVLM-based baselines by over 3% and surpassing the best CLIP-based method (GDA at 81.5%). In the vision-few-shot setting (no class names), HEC-V reaches 82.4% average, beating DINOv3 Probing (76.7%) and its own vision backbone DFN Probing (75.9%) without any hyperparameter tuning. For zero-shot classification (10-way), HEC-T improves Qwen2-VL by +10.1% (76.1% → 86.2%), exceeding its DFN backbone (84.7%) by 1.5%, and improves LLaVA-OV by +2.4% (79.7% → 82.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEC-VT (83.0% avg) outperforms the strongest CLIP-based baseline GDA (81.5%) by 1.5% and ProKeR (81.0%) by 2.0%, making it the only LVLM method to surpass the best CLIP-based approach on average. Against the closest LVLM competitor SAVs (80.0%), HEC-VT gains 3.0% and HEC-V gains 2.4%, with the ablation attributing most of the gain to the SAR+GDA head ranking rather than domain conditioning alone. In the vision-few-shot setting (Table 1), HEC-V (82.4%) beats DINOv3 Probing (76.7%) by 5.7% and its own DFN backbone (75.9%) by 6.5%, while also outperforming SAVs (80.0%) by 2.4% on 9/12 datasets. For zero-shot (Table 2), HEC-T on Qwen2-VL (86.2%) exceeds its DFN backbone (84.7%) by 1.5%, but HEC-T on LLaVA-OV (82.1%) still trails SigLIP (85.0%) by 2.9%, indicating CLIP backbones retain an edge on saturated benchmarks. The paper notes HEC-VT consistently wins on less object-centric datasets (DTD, SUN, UCF) while CLIP-based methods still lead on 5/12 datasets overall, likely due to Qwen2-VL's post-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAVs line of work on selecting sparse attention heads from LVLMs for few-shot classification, replacing nearest-centroid head selection with a GDA-based ranking (Soft-Accuracy Ranking) and probability ensembling. It also builds on the Gaussian Discriminant Analysis framework from prior CLIP-based adaptation work, repurposing it to rank and combine individual attention heads rather than operating on pooled embeddings. A key conceptual departure from both CLIP-based methods and prior LVLM adaptation is the use of multi-level prompt conditioning (task, domain, class) to steer the LLM decoder's internal representations at inference time, enabling domain-specific feature extraction that is architecturally impossible with independent vision-text encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LVLMs' poor classification performance is not an inherent architectural limitation but a consequence of how their outputs are extracted, offering a training-free path to close the gap with CLIP-based methods. The finding that prompt conditioning improves class separability in internal representations opens a practical avenue for domain adaptation without fine-tuning, which is particularly relevant for fine-grained and under-represented domains. However, limitations remain: the method requires access to intermediate attention vectors (incompatible with API-only models), class conditioning is restricted to small class sets, and CLIP backbones still outperform on many saturated benchmarks. Open questions include whether the head-selection mechanism generalizes to generation tasks, whether more complex prompts or in-context examples can further close the remaining gap, and whether the approach transfers to other LVLM architectures beyond Qwen2-VL and LLaVA-OV.
