## Problem

Current customized image-editing pipelines deploy one LoRA per visual effect plus a separate acceleration LoRA, and linearly compose them at inference. As the effect library grows, this modular design incurs prohibitive storage, slow and error-prone routing, and—most critically—severe feature interference (concept bleeding, semantic drift, degraded style fidelity) from multi-LoRA aggregation. The paper asks whether dozens of heterogeneous effects and few-step sampling can be consolidated into a single LoRA without these conflicts.

## Input / Output

At inference the model receives a 1024×1024 source image and a text prompt that begins with an effect-specific orthogonal trigger token followed by a VLM-generated descriptive caption (e.g., "[v^i] oil-painting style portrait"). It produces a single edited image in 8 NFE steps (vs. 80 for the unaccelerated base), with the trigger word selecting which of the 50 embedded effects to apply. The asymmetric conditioning means the student never sees the teacher's original training prompt, only the trigger-plus-caption pair.

## Method

CollectionLoRA distills 50 effect-specific teacher LoRAs plus a Lightning acceleration LoRA into one student LoRA on Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 via a multi-teacher on-policy distillation objective. Three mechanisms address the resulting optimization challenges: (1) Probabilistic Dual-Stream Routing (PDSR) stochastically alternates between a general-domain stream (20K unannotated images, standard DMD) and an effect stream (≈20 paired images per effect with a dynamically mounted teacher LoRA) to prevent catastrophic forgetting; (2) Automated Asymmetric Conditioning (AAC) uses a VLM to auto-caption each effect and assigns orthogonal trigger tokens, so teacher and student consume different prompts, enforcing latent-space concept isolation; (3) the Decoupled Hybrid Objective (DHO) combines a Trajectory-Anchored Flow Matching loss (regressing the student's velocity field toward the teacher-generated target to stabilize cold-start gradients) with a Target-Simulation distribution-matching loss under dual-timestep bounds (t_gen < 750, t_critic > 500) that restores high-frequency detail lost by pure regression. A step-dependent schedule linearly trades TA-FM weight down and TS weight up over 5,000 training steps, and the fake-score critic is updated at a 5:1 ratio to the generator.

## Results

On EffectBench (5,000 instructions per model), CollectionLoRA consolidates 50 visual effects into a single LoRA with 8 NFE, achieving CLIP 0.727, DreamSim 0.425, EditReward 1.052, VSA 4.380, and a Bad Case Rate of 0.087. These results surpass the single-effect teacher (Base, 80 NFE) on style alignment, instruction-following, and failure rate while using 10× fewer function evaluations. The method also outperforms the naive multi-task baseline (FM+Lightning) by a wide margin on every metric except DINO subject consistency (0.600 vs. 0.611), which the authors argue is unreliable under complex visual transformations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the single-effect teacher (Qwen-Image-Edit-2509, 80 NFE), CollectionLoRA (8 NFE) wins on CLIP (0.727 vs. 0.726), DreamSim (0.425 vs. 0.434), EditReward (1.052 vs. 1.007), VSA (4.380 vs. 4.075), and BCR (0.087 vs. 0.141), while trailing slightly on DINO (0.600 vs. 0.611) — a gap the authors attribute to DINO's insensitivity to semantic failures under extreme stylization. Against the accelerated single-effect baseline (Base+Lightning, 8 NFE), CollectionLoRA improves CLIP by +0.010, DreamSim by −0.016, EditReward by +0.066, VSA by +0.479, and reduces BCR from 0.168 to 0.087, demonstrating that multi-effect consolidation does not sacrifice per-effect quality. Against the naive multi-task flow-matching baseline (FM+Lightning, 8 NFE), CollectionLoRA dominates across all metrics: CLIP +0.024, DreamSim −0.043, EditReward +0.123, VSA +0.230, and BCR reduced from 0.217 to 0.087, confirming that the PDSR/AAC/DHO framework effectively prevents the concept bleeding and oversmoothing inherent in naive multi-concept fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD/DMD2) from its original single-teacher, homogeneous setting to a multi-teacher, heterogeneous multi-concept regime, where the student must simultaneously match dozens of effect-specific teacher distributions. It also unifies DMD under the broader on-policy distillation (OPD) taxonomy, drawing on the LLM distillation literature's insight that correcting the student's own explored states with teacher signals is more stable than offline imitation. The key departure from prior few-step distillation work (Consistency Models, DMD, Decoupled-DMD) is the introduction of a hybrid objective (TA-FM + Target Simulation) specifically designed to prevent gradient vanishing when the student-teacher distribution gap is large during cold-start, a problem that single-teacher DMD never encounters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying LoRA-based customization at scale, this paper offers a concrete path to eliminate the linear storage growth, routing latency, and inter-module interference that plague multi-LoRA pipelines, consolidating 50+ effects into a single 2.2 GB module with 8-step inference. The incremental extension result (adding effects 51–54 without retraining) suggests a practical path toward open-ended effect libraries. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the Qwen-Image-Edit base model, how it behaves at even larger scales (500+ effects), and whether the per-effect teacher LoRAs can themselves be generated automatically to remove the multi-stage training dependency.
