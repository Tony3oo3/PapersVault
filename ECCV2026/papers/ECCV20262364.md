## Problem

Multimodal Diffusion Large Language Models (MDLLMs) use parallel masked decoding to generate text concurrently, but the decoder ranks candidate tokens purely by textual likelihood without verifying localized visual support. The paper formalizes this as an objective mismatch: the language-only proxy score incentivizes language shortcuts, causing hallucinated tokens to be committed prematurely and propagate as incorrect context across subsequent parallel decoding steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262364/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on an RGB image (tokenized into a sequence of image tokens) paired with a text prompt, producing a generated text response through T parallel diffusion steps. At each step, a fixed budget of k_t masked positions is unmasked simultaneously; VISAGE intercepts this commitment schedule by re-ranking the candidate tokens before they are finalized. The framework is entirely training-free and requires no architectural modification—it only reads the last-layer cross-attention weights already computed during the forward pass.

## Method

VISAGE estimates a proxy discrepancy for each candidate token by computing the spatial Shannon entropy of its last-layer cross-attention distribution over image tokens: grounded tokens exhibit concentrated (low-entropy) attention, while language-shortcut tokens produce spatially uniform (high-entropy) distributions. To prevent a single spurious head from bypassing the penalty, entropies across M heads are aggregated via a discrete β-quantile operator (default β=0.25), enforcing a localization consensus that requires a specific fraction of heads to independently show concentrated visual support. The resulting robust grounding entropy H_i is converted into a penalty multiplier g_i = 1/(1+H_i), and tokens are re-ranked by the linear score u_i = c_i · g_i^α (α=0.5), so that visually ungrounded proposals are downweighted before commitment. The paper provides a stability guarantee showing that if the entropy estimator approximates the true proxy discrepancy within ε_t, the objective loss is bounded by 2k_t·ε_t, and an entropy-stride mechanism allows computing the grounding signal every few steps to reduce latency to near-baseline cost.

## Results

On the primary MMaDA backbone, VISAGE achieves 29.44% accuracy on MMMU-val (+8.59% relative over the 27.11% baseline), 36.83% on HallusionBench (+7.75% relative over 34.18%), and 76.17 F1 on POPE (+0.26% relative over 75.97%), while incurring only a −0.81% relative drop on MME (1372.05 vs. 1383.29). The method also generalizes without architecture-specific tuning: on LLaDA-V it lifts MMMU-val from 46.67 to 47.11 and MME from 1883.57 to 1900.64, and on Lumina-DiMOO it improves MMMU-val from 29.33 to 30.13 and MME from 1125.23 to 1142.16. Inference overhead is minimal: with an entropy stride of 8, latency is 8.23 s/image, essentially identical to the 8.22 s/image baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MMaDA base (standard confidence-based unmasking), VISAGE wins on three of four benchmarks: MMMU-val (29.44 vs. 27.11), HallusionBench (36.83 vs. 34.18), and POPE (76.17 vs. 75.97), while conceding a small 0.81% relative drop on MME (1372.05 vs. 1383.29), which the authors attribute to MME's inclusion of cognition tasks (commonsense reasoning, translation) where excessive visual penalization is counterproductive. Against the adapted Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD) baseline, VISAGE outperforms on all four benchmarks, with the largest margin on HallusionBench (36.83 vs. 34.80, +2.03 absolute), indicating that contrastive logit comparison penalizes generic statistical priors but fails to enforce the concentrated spatial verification that entropy-based re-ranking provides. Cross-architecture comparisons confirm the method is not tied to MMaDA: on LLaDA-V, VISAGE gains +0.44 on MMMU-val and +17.07 on MME over the base; on Lumina-DiMOO, it gains +0.80 on MMMU-val and +16.93 on MME, with no architecture-specific hyperparameter tuning. The POPE gain is modest (+0.20 absolute) partly because the dataset inherits MS COCO label noise, and the authors note that VISAGE's exhaustive spatial verification sometimes correctly identifies objects absent from ground-truth annotations, capping the measurable improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of multimodal diffusion LLMs (MDLLMs) and hallucination mitigation by reframing the problem: rather than treating hallucination as a capacity or alignment deficiency (the dominant framing in training-time alignment work like DPO/RAFT or external-verification pipelines like Woodpecker), it identifies a structural flaw in the parallel unmasking algorithm itself — a misspecified proxy objective that ranks tokens by language-only confidence. It departs from autoregressive inference-time interventions (VCD, head suppression, information-bottleneck constraints) that are incompatible with parallel decoding, and from other training-free diffusion-LM decoding methods (CORE, DyStruct) that operate purely on text. The specific mechanism — spatial Shannon entropy of cross-attention with β-quantile head consensus as a grounding penalty — repurposes standard attention statistics in a novel decoding-time re-ranking role.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, near-zero-overhead nature (entropy stride 8 achieves 1.00× latency) makes VISAGE immediately deployable on any MDLLM that exposes token confidence and visual cross-attention, and the cross-architecture validation on LLaDA-V and Lumina-DiMOO supports a decoding-level rather than architecture-specific fix. However, the practical gains are moderate (MME actually drops 0.81%, POPE gains are negligible due to label noise), and the framework does not address temporal grounding for video-based MDLLMs, which the authors acknowledge as a key limitation. The conceptual reframing — hallucination as a quantifiable, position-wise optimization error rather than a global generative failure — is a useful lens that could inform future decoding objectives, but the entropy-based estimator is a heuristic proxy whose relationship to the true multimodal objective is only loosely bounded.
