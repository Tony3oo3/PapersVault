## Problem

Existing DPO-based medical report generation methods construct preference pairs by directly pairing ground-truth reports with model predictions, which entangles critical clinical findings with clinically irrelevant linguistic variations (connective phrases, word order, syntax) and dilutes the alignment signal. Additionally, these methods perform text-centric optimization conditioned on a single static image, lacking explicit vision-language alignment, and ignore 'latent risks'—correct but highly uncertain entity predictions that undermine clinical reliability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single medical image (chest X-ray or endoscopy frame) as input and produces a free-text diagnostic report as output. During post-training, the framework operates on preference quadruplets (x₁, x₂, y₁, y₂) where two images are paired with two factually-aligned reports, and the ECD module parses reports into structured entity sets with assertion statuses (Present/Absent) extracted via RaTENER. A retrieval stage augments each training instance with a contrastive image from the training database that is clinically congruent with the model's prediction but visually distinct from the query.

## Method

DPO-Clin introduces three components: (1) an Entity-level Clinical Diagnostic (ECD) module that uses RaTENER to extract entities, performs threshold-constrained linear assignment for cross-report entity matching, categorizes discrepancies into four error types (correct matches, extraneous, missing, false assertions), and guides GPT-4o to generate a linguistically-aligned preferred report that preserves the prediction's phrasing while correcting only clinical facts; (2) M²DPO, a retrieval-augmented multi-modal DPO variant that enforces textual preference inversion triggered by visual context switches—using a two-stage retrieval (ECD-driven textual filtering followed by MedKLIP visual similarity) to find a contrastive image, so the model must ground textual discrepancies in specific pathological visual regions; (3) a counterfactual data construction pipeline that identifies correct-but-uncertain entities via maximum token-level entropy, replaces them with second-most-probable candidates verified against a RadGraph knowledge graph, and constructs additional M²DPO quadruplets to suppress latent risks. The total training objective jointly optimizes both explicit error mitigation and latent risk suppression with dynamic binary masking to exclude invalid instances.

## Results

On MIMIC-CXR with the RADAR baseline, DPO-Clin achieves 0.490 macro-F1 and 0.654 micro-F1 (CheXbert clinical efficacy), 0.372 RadGraph F1, and 0.567 RaTEScore, outperforming the strongest competitor MMedPO (0.478/0.639/0.356/0.546) across all clinical metrics. On the cross-domain IU X-Ray test set, DPO-Clin (RADAR) reaches 0.362 macro-F1 and 0.580 micro-F1, again leading all baselines. On the in-house endoscopy dataset, DPO-Clin attains a 0.852 binary F1, surpassing MMedPO (0.834) by 1.8% and the R2GenGPT baseline (0.798) by 5.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPO-Clin consistently outperforms all four DPO-based competitors (SIMA, MMedPO, RRG-DPO, and standard DPO) on both CXR datasets and the endoscopy dataset across nearly all clinical and RRG-exclusive metrics. Against the strongest competitor MMedPO, DPO-Clin gains +1.2 points in macro-F1 (0.490 vs. 0.478) and +1.5 in micro-F1 (0.654 vs. 0.639) on MIMIC-CXR (RADAR), and +1.7/+1.2 on IU X-Ray. RRG-DPO is the closest competitor on RaTEScore for MIMIC-CXR (0.550 vs. 0.567 for DPO-Clin with RADAR), but DPO-Clin still leads by 1.7 points. SIMA, a general-domain self-improvement method, shows the weakest clinical gains, trailing DPO-Clin by 2.6 macro-F1 points on MIMIC-CXR (RADAR). On the endoscopy dataset, DPO-Clin's 0.852 F1 surpasses MMedPO (0.834) by 1.8% and RRG-DPO (0.813) by 3.9%, confirming modality-agnostic superiority. In the DPO-variant ablation (Table 4), M²DPO outperforms both standard DPO and SimPO on every metric, validating the multi-modal preference inversion mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO-based post-training paradigm for medical report generation (established by RRG-DPO, MMedPO, and SIMA) by rethinking how preference pairs are constructed. It departs from the standard practice of pairing raw GT reports with model predictions by introducing an entity-level clinical diagnostic (ECD) module that isolates clinically relevant discrepancies from linguistic noise, and by proposing M²DPO, a multi-modal DPO variant that enforces preference inversion under visual context switches. The counterfactual data construction for latent risk mitigation draws on knowledge-graph verification (RadGraph) and token-level entropy, extending the preference optimization objective beyond explicit factual errors to uncertain-but-correct predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent reliability problem in MRG—factual errors that undermine clinical trustworthiness—by showing that preference optimization can be made clinically targeted rather than uniformly token-level. The ablation revealing that substituting linguistically-aligned reports with raw GT reports improves n-gram metrics while degrading clinical metrics is a useful diagnostic for the field, highlighting the tension between surface similarity and diagnostic accuracy. Open questions include the dependence on GPT-4o for preference report generation (cost, reproducibility), whether the retrieval-based M²DPO generalizes to modalities without large training databases, and whether the ECD module's reliance on RaTENER limits applicability to domains with well-annotated entity schemas.
