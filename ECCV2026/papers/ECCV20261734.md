## Problem

MRI is inherently noisy and noise-free reference images are unobtainable, forcing existing denoisers to rely on surrogate objectives (blind-spot masking, synthetic degradation) that introduce domain shifts, violate voxel-independence assumptions, and result in incomplete or biased noise removal. The paper identifies that training on physical, repeated acquisitions constitutes a near-ideal Noise2Noise setting that has been previously neglected, and that diffusion-based re-noising can extend this to single-noisy datasets without the information loss inherent in degradation-based training.

## Input / Output

The denoiser takes a single noisy structural brain MRI volume (T1w, T2w, or FLAIR at 0.7–1 mm resolution) and optionally co-acquired contrast images (e.g., T2w for T1w denoising) as additional input channels after registration. It is implemented as a 2.5D U-Net processing 5-slice slabs across axial, coronal, and sagittal planes, with predictions averaged across planes at inference. The output is a single denoised volume approximating the clean signal, with voxels outside a FastSurfer brain mask down-weighted during training.

## Method

YADO trains a 213M-parameter 2D U-Net (five ResNet stages with self-attention) via physical Noise2Noise (pN2N): two independently acquired, co-registered images of the same subject serve as input and target, yielding the same optimal estimator as hypothetical clean-reference training without any synthetic degradation. For datasets lacking physical repeats, Renoise2Noise (ReN2N) first trains a conditional diffusion model (v-prediction, √cos noise schedule) on repeated acquisitions to generate synthetic noise-decorrelated pairs that mimic real acquisition noise, then trains the denoiser on these pairs. Cross-modality guidance concatenates registered co-acquired contrasts as additional input channels, tightening the posterior estimate of the clean signal. The framework yields four variants (u/g × pN2N/ReN2N), all trained with L2 loss under a brain mask using AdamW, and evaluated against independently acquired reference scans to avoid rewarding noise replication.

## Results

Across 14 test conditions (T1w, T2w, FLAIR; 0.7–1 mm; multiple vendors), YADO consistently outperforms all 17 baselines in rank-based analysis. On the primary 1 mm T1w benchmark (OASIS-3 trained), gYADO(ReN2N) achieves 94.59% SSIM on CHDI, 94.59% on HCP-YA, and 94.59% on RS, with PVS CNR of 2.85, while uYADO(ReN2N) reaches 94.37% SSIM on RS and 2.74 PVS CNR. ReN2N improves over pN2N in 20 of 22 experiments, demonstrating better generalization to unseen datasets. Notably, 7 of 17 baseline methods rank worse than the Identity (no-op) function, and the classical BM4D filter outperforms most learning-based approaches, ranking second only to S-N2Void.

## Baselines & Comparisons

YADO (all four variants) outperforms every one of the 17 baselines in the overall rank-based aggregation across 14 test conditions. Among learning-based single-noisy methods, Ner2N [53] and S-N2Void [8] are the closest competitors, achieving 93.42% and 92.62% SSIM on RS (1 mm) respectively, versus 94.37–94.59% for YADO(ReN2N); however, both suffer from partial identity decay and domain-shift artifacts that YADO avoids. The classical BM4D filter [48] surprisingly ranks second overall (behind only S-N2Void among non-YADO methods), outperforming expensive GAN-based (RED-WGAN) and diffusion-based (DDM²) approaches, highlighting that many learning-based methods fail to beat simple non-local filtering on real-world MRI. DDM² [83] with diffusion sampling collapses catastrophically (51% SSIM on OASIS), while its regression-sampling variant [63] recovers to 87–91% but still trails YADO by 3–7 SSIM points. N2Score [31] shows a critical failure mode with PVS CNR of only 1.06 (vs. 2.74–2.85 for YADO), indicating it fails to preserve perivascular space contrast. For multi-contrast methods, N2Contrast [78] and YODA [63] both underperform YADO's guided variants, confirming that explicit denoiser conditioning on co-acquired contrasts is more effective than indirect cross-modal noise independence. Zero-shot methods (DIP [75], Pixel2Pixel [47]) are competitive on SSIM but lag YADO by 1–4 points and incur >1 hour inference time versus 16 seconds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions MRI denoising by reviving the Noise2Noise (N2N) training paradigm [44] with physically repeated acquisitions (pN2N), an approach the authors argue was previously neglected in favor of surrogate objectives like blind-spot masking (N2Void) or synthetic degradation (Ner2N). The key conceptual departure is that these surrogate objectives introduce domain shifts and violate the spatial noise-independence assumptions that hold for natural images but not for MRI. To extend pN2N to single-noisy datasets, the paper introduces Renoise2Noise (ReN2N), which uses a diffusion model [24] trained on repeated acquisitions to synthesize decorrelated noisy pairs, and adds cross-contrast conditioning as an explicit denoiser guidance mechanism. The denoiser architecture itself is a standard 2.5D U-Net; the contribution lies entirely in the training formulation and evaluation methodology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 7 of 17 state-of-the-art learning-based denoisers underperform the identity function on real-world MRI, and that a 2012 classical filter (BM4D) outperforms most expensive deep-learning methods, challenging the field's assumption that learning-based approaches inherently dominate. The evaluation methodology critique—showing that synthetic-noise references can make identity denoising appear superior to perfect denoising—has broad implications for how the community benchmarks denoisers. Open questions include whether ReN2N's substantial one-time compute cost (80 GPU-days for re-noising) is justified at scale, how YADO handles low-quality or anisotropic conditioning contrasts, and whether the framework extends to dynamic or non-brain imaging. The practical impact is significant: YADO matches the quality of brute-force 7-acquisition averaging from a single routine scan, directly benefiting clinical and research neuroimaging pipelines.
