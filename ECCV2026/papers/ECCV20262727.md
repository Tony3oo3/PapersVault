## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based SLAM systems assume that high-fidelity rendering is the primary determinant of tracking accuracy, but the inherent inaccuracies in high-dimensional Gaussian parameters (position, scale, color) produce spurious rendering artifacts that create sharp local minima in the photometric alignment cost function, causing pose drift under fast motion or aggressive viewpoint changes. No prior work addresses this by making the rasterization process itself robust to parameter noise rather than merely minimizing rendering error.

## Input / Output

The system consumes synchronous RGB-D image streams with known camera intrinsics and operates on a dual-track paradigm: non-keyframes use the canonical Gaussian map directly for rapid pose estimation, while keyframes undergo the CB-KNN regularization pass. Outputs include the camera pose trajectory, a persistent 3D Gaussian scene map (whose canonical parameters are never modified by the smoothing), and per-frame rendered color, depth, and silhouette (accumulated opacity) images. The silhouette map serves as a coverage mask (threshold 0.99) to restrict the tracking loss to well-constrained pixels.

## Method

CB-KNN (Corrective Blurry KNN) is a transient, plug-and-play correction applied at the CUDA rasterization level exclusively during keyframe processing. For each pixel, it identifies the K-nearest contributing Gaussians and performs two dual-dimensional corrections: (1) shifting each Gaussian's 2D projected position toward the local neighborhood centroid by a factor α∈[0.1,0.3] to bridge structural gaps, and (2) replacing each Gaussian's color with a contribution-weighted average of its neighbors to suppress outlier-induced color noise. The neighborhood size K adapts dynamically via K = K₀·max(0.5, 1+β·γ/ρ), scaling up during aggressive motion or in sparse regions and shrinking in dense static areas. These corrections are frame-specific and never written back to the canonical map, so the smoothed rendering acts as an on-the-fly regularizer that flattens the non-convex pose-optimization landscape (analogous to Graduated Non-Convexity) without degrading long-term scene fidelity. The approach is demonstrated to be framework-agnostic by integrating into MonoGS with a 31% ATE reduction on Replica.

## Results

On the Replica dataset, the proposed CB-KNN method achieves an average ATE RMSE of 0.28 cm, outperforming SplaTAM (0.36 cm) and Hier-SLAM (0.32 cm). On the more challenging real-world TUM-RGBD dataset, the method reduces ATE RMSE from 5.63 cm (SplaTAM) to 3.67 cm, and on ScanNet from 11.88 cm to 8.46 cm. For rendering quality on Replica, the method achieves 34.31 dB average PSNR and 0.97 SSIM, slightly surpassing SplaTAM (34.11 dB, 0.97 SSIM) while maintaining competitive LPIPS (0.09 vs. 0.10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 3DGS-based SLAM baselines on every dataset where comparisons are available. Against the primary baseline SplaTAM [22], CB-KNN reduces ATE RMSE by 22% on Replica (0.28 vs. 0.36 cm), 35% on TUM-RGBD (3.67 vs. 5.63 cm), and 29% on ScanNet (8.46 vs. 11.88 cm). On Replica, it also beats the strongest non-3DGS baseline Hier-SLAM [24] (0.28 vs. 0.32 cm) and Point-SLAM [35] (0.28 vs. 0.52 cm). On TUM-RGBD, the improvement over Point-SLAM [35] is 59% (3.67 vs. 8.92 cm), and over Vox-Fusion [49] it is 68% (3.67 vs. 11.31 cm). On ScanNet, the method surpasses Co-SLAM [41] (8.46 vs. 8.78 cm) and Hier-SLAM [24] (8.46 vs. 11.36 cm). For rendering on Replica, the method slightly edges out SplaTAM in PSNR (34.31 vs. 34.11 dB) and LPIPS (0.09 vs. 0.10), while Point-SLAM [35] reports higher PSNR (35.17 dB) due to its reliance on ground-truth point cloud priors not available in real-time settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) for scene representation and SplaTAM (Keetha et al., 2024) as the base SLAM pipeline, but departs from the prevailing assumption in 3DGS-SLAM that maximizing rendering fidelity is the primary driver of tracking accuracy. Instead, it reframes the problem through the lens of rasterization robustness, drawing an analogy to Graduated Non-Convexity to argue that a controlled smoothing of the rendering cost landscape is more beneficial than a sharp, artifact-laden one. The CB-KNN mechanism is positioned as a practical, plug-and-play alternative to a full rasterizer redesign, borrowing the kernel-smoothing concept from classical statistics but applying it transiently and adaptively within the differentiable 3DGS rendering loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that intentionally introducing a controlled blur during rendering stabilizes pose optimization without degrading the stored map—challenges a widely held assumption in the 3DGS-SLAM community and offers a simple, drop-in regularization that practitioners can adopt without re-architecting their pipelines. The generalizability test on MonoGS (31% ATE reduction) strengthens the case that CB-KNN is a broadly applicable module rather than a system-specific trick. Open questions remain about whether the keyframe-only application strategy is sufficient for highly dynamic scenes, how the adaptive K mechanism behaves in large-scale outdoor environments with very sparse Gaussian distributions, and whether the transient correction interacts adversely with aggressive map densification over long sequences.
