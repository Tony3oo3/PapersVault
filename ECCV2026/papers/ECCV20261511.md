## Problem

Existing multi-modal (LiDAR+Camera) human motion capture methods rely on explicit sensor calibration that propagates errors under changing viewpoints, constraining deployment to fixed, highly-overlapped layouts. Additionally, prior approaches degrade significantly under severe point cloud noise or partial sensor failures, which are common in real-world environments. The paper asks whether calibration-free multi-modal alignment is possible under arbitrary sensor configurations and how to achieve robust global trajectory estimation in noisy, cluttered scenes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequentially sampled LiDAR point clouds (downsampled to 256×3 via farthest point sampling) and bounding-box-normalized 2D keypoints (17×2) from an arbitrary number of uncalibrated sensors, with no assumption on relative poses between them. It outputs full SMPL parameters: shape (β∈R¹⁰), body pose (θ∈R²³ˣ³), global orientation (τ∈R³), and global translation (Γ∈R³). Local pose and shape are estimated in a sensor-independent human-centric coordinate frame, while the global trajectory is defined relative to each LiDAR's coordinate system, with the NTT module predicting per-LiDAR translations in a batched manner.

## Method

Sen-Cap comprises two coupled modules. The Unified Across-Sensor Motion Estimator (UAME) first aligns each LiDAR's 3D joint representations (encoded by a PointNet) into a shared human-centric coordinate system via GRU-predicted rotation vectors, eliminating explicit calibration; it then fuses aligned 3D and 2D temporal features through a bottleneck attention mechanism where a single learnable token acts as the cross-attention query over concatenated multi-modal features, forcing reliability-aware modality reweighting through a compact information bottleneck. The Noise-Resilient Trajectory Tracker (NTT) replaces the standard single-offset prediction with an iterative refinement loop (N=3 steps) that incrementally shifts the point cloud centroid toward the true root joint, with a hinge-style penalty loss discouraging large per-step displacements. A Sensor Dropout training strategy randomly masks feature tokens from both modalities during training, enabling the unified model to generalize to fewer or more sensors at test time without retraining.

## Results

On FreeMotion (training view, LiDAR+Camera), Sen-Cap achieves 47.46/57.84 mm joint/vertex error (PS), 75.16/81.65 mm (PST), 9.94° angular error, 3.01 m/s² acceleration error, and 3.83 mm SUCD. On Human-M3 (training view), it reaches 42.59/52.09 mm (PS), 73.50/77.53 mm (PST), and 5.71° angular error. In cross-domain evaluation on the noisy outdoor LiDARHuman26M and fast-motion RELI11D datasets (trained only on AMASS + FreeMotion), Sen-Cap achieves 191.59/216.73 mm (PST) and 81.11/85.48 mm (PST) respectively, outperforming all baselines on most metrics without fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sen-Cap outperforms the closest hybrid baseline FreeCap [50] by a wide margin on both primary datasets: on FreeMotion, joint/vertex error (PS) drops from 53.31/65.50 to 47.46/57.84 mm and (PST) from 95.97/102.91 to 75.16/81.65 mm; on Human-M3, (PS) improves from 55.45/68.52 to 42.59/52.09 mm and (PST) from 96.47/102.67 to 73.50/77.53 mm. Against LiDAR-only baselines, Sen-Cap (L+C) beats LiveHPS++ [34] on FreeMotion J/V(PS) (47.46/57.84 vs. 54.15/57.74) and J/V(PST) (75.16/81.65 vs. 88.91/97.53), and on Human-M3 J/V(PS) (42.59/52.09 vs. 55.67/68.73) and angular error (5.71° vs. 10.26°). All camera-only baselines (WHAM, GVHMR, GENMO, PromptHMR) are substantially worse on joint/vertex error, with the best camera method GENMO [21] still 14.9 mm higher on FreeMotion J/V(PS) (62.37 vs. 47.46). In cross-domain evaluation (Table 2), Sen-Cap leads on RELI11D across all metrics (e.g., 61.23/73.28 mm PS vs. 78.45/95.41 for LiveHPS++) and on LiDARHuman26M for J/V(PST) (191.59/216.73 vs. 196.30/221.71 for LiveHPS++) and acceleration error (3.03 vs. 4.67 m/s²), though it is slightly behind on LiDARHuman26M J/V(PS) (161.32 vs. 141.03 for LiveHPS++). The LiDAR-only variant (Ours†) matches LiveHPS++ closely, confirming that the multi-modal fusion provides the primary gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sen-Cap builds directly on the LiveHPS/LiveHPS++ line of LiDAR-based motion capture (adopting FPS sampling and the overall pipeline structure) and on FreeCap's optimization-based cross-sensor matching for multi-person scenarios, while borrowing WHAM's 2D keypoint normalization. Its key conceptual departure is replacing explicit or on-the-fly calibration with a Human-Centric Space Alignment that projects all sensor observations into a shared, sensor-agnostic coordinate frame, combined with a bottleneck attention fusion that adaptively re-weights modalities. For global trajectory estimation, it replaces the single-offset prediction of prior LiDAR pipelines with an iterative refinement tracker (NTT), motivated by the observation that centroid-based normalization under noise forces the network to predict abnormally large offsets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating calibration and supporting arbitrary sensor counts without retraining, Sen-Cap addresses a major practical barrier to deploying multi-sensor motion capture in real-world settings (sports, robotics, large-scale venues) where fixed, pre-calibrated layouts are infeasible. The noise-resilience results—over 2× error reduction under severe clutter—are well-evidenced and directly relevant to outdoor and cluttered indoor environments. Open questions include scalability to very large sensor arrays, dense multi-person scenes beyond the current matching mechanism, and whether the human-centric alignment generalizes to non-human or articulated-object tracking. The real-time operation claim, while practical, is supported primarily by architectural design rather than a detailed latency analysis in the main text.
