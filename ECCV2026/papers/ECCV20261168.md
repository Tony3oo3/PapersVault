## Problem

Existing on-device diffusion models (e.g., SnapGen, Mobile Diffusion) are limited to text-to-image generation and lack support for text-guided image editing, forcing users to deploy two separate models that increase memory consumption and system complexity on resource-constrained mobile devices. No prior work has demonstrated a single compact network that unifies both generation and editing for on-device use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261168/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a text prompt (prepended with a [Generate] token) and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image; for editing, it takes a source image plus an editing instruction (prepended with an [Edit] token) and produces a 1024×1024 edited image. Internally, the source image is encoded by a 2.5M-parameter TinyVAE into a 4-channel latent at 8× downsampling, then spatially concatenated with the target latent along the width dimension to form a two-panel input (target|source for editing, target|blank for generation). After DMD2 distillation, inference requires only 4 denoising steps, completing in under 3 seconds on iPhone 17 Pro.

## Method

DreamLite builds a 0.39B-parameter U-Net (pruned from SDXL with expanded separable convolutions, multi-query attention, and removed high-resolution self-attention) conditioned by Qwen3-VL-2B text embeddings. The key architectural insight is an in-context conditioning mechanism that unifies generation and editing at the input level: the target and condition latents are concatenated horizontally, with a blank latent representing 'no visual condition' for generation, eliminating the need for separate branches or channel-concatenation that degrades generative priors. Training follows a task-progressive curriculum (T2I pretraining → editing pretraining with foreground-emphasis loss → unified joint training) to prevent task interference in the compact backbone, followed by SFT on 0.5M curated samples and ReFL-based reinforcement learning using HPSv3 (generation) and EditReward (editing) as task-specific reward models. Finally, DMD2 distillation compresses the sampling trajectory to 4 steps without CFG, enabling real-time mobile inference.

## Results

DreamLite (0.39B) achieves a GenEval score of 0.72 for text-to-image generation and an ImgEdit score of 4.11 for text-guided image editing, outperforming all prior on-device models (SnapGen 0.70, SnapGen++ 0.66, SANA-0.6B 0.64 on GenEval; EditMGT 2.89, VIBE 3.85 on ImgEdit). On the DPG benchmark, DreamLite scores 85.8 overall, surpassing SnapGen++ (85.2) and SANA-0.6B (83.6). After 4-step DMD2 distillation, the model generates or edits a 1024×1024 image in under 3 seconds on iPhone 17 Pro with peak runtime memory of 2.5 GB (generation) and 3.5 GB (editing).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, DreamLite (0.72) outperforms all on-device baselines: SnapGen (0.70), SnapGen++ (0.66), SANA-0.6B (0.64), and Nitro-E-GRPO (0.70), while remaining competitive with much larger unified models like OmniGen2 (0.80, 4B) and BAGEL (0.82, 7B), though trailing LongCat-Image (0.87, 6B) and Z-Image (0.84, 6B). On ImgEdit, DreamLite (4.11) surpasses the lightweight editing models VIBE (3.85, 1.6B) and EditMGT (2.89, 0.96B) as well as DeepGen1.0 (4.03, 2B), but falls short of the top server-side models LongCat-Image (4.49) and Z-Image (4.30). On DPG, DreamLite (85.8) edges out SnapGen++ (85.2) and SANA-0.6B (83.6) but is slightly below LongCat-Image (86.8) and BAGEL (85.1). On GEdit-EN, DreamLite (6.88) beats EditMGT (6.33) but trails VIBE (7.28) and LongCat-Image (7.55). Overall, DreamLite is the strongest sub-0.5B model across all four benchmarks, closing the gap with 4–12× larger unified models while being deployable on mobile hardware.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamLite extends the compact U-Net lineage from SnapGen (pruned SDXL backbone with separable convolutions and MQA) by adding an in-context spatial concatenation mechanism borrowed conceptually from FLUX.1 Kontext's latent-space conditioning, but adapted to a sub-0.5B U-Net where channel-concatenation (InstructPix2Pix-style) would degrade generative priors. The training recipe composes known components—flow matching, DMD2 step distillation, and ReFL-based RL with task-specific reward models (HPSv3, EditReward)—into a task-progressive curriculum (T2I → Edit → Unified Joint) that is specifically designed to stabilize a single compact network across both tasks. The key departure from prior on-device work (SnapGen, SnapGen++, Mobile Diffusion) is the unification of generation and editing in one model, and the departure from prior unified models (FLUX Kontext, BAGEL, OmniGen2) is the aggressive parameter reduction to enable mobile deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DreamLite demonstrates that a single 0.39B model can handle both T2I generation and instruction-based editing on a smartphone in under 3 seconds at 1024×1024, eliminating the need to ship two separate models and reducing memory pressure on constrained devices. The task-progressive training ablation reveals that an intermediate editing stage is necessary for compact unified models to internalize in-context referencing before joint optimization—a practical recipe likely transferable to other small-capacity multi-task diffusion settings. Limitations include a measurable quality drop after 4-step DMD distillation (GenEval 0.72→0.70, ImgEdit 4.11→3.8) and continued gaps versus 4–12B unified models on complex compositional and spatial-reasoning tasks. Open questions include whether the in-context U-Net formulation generalizes to more complex editing operations (multi-image, video) and whether the task-progressive curriculum extends beyond the binary generate/edit split.
