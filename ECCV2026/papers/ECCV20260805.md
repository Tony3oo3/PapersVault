## Problem

Existing AIGI detectors rely on generator-specific artifacts that become obsolete as new architectures emerge and as images undergo multi-round cross-platform propagation (chain degradations), causing sharp performance drops in real-world conditions. No prior method simultaneously addresses both the open-ended evolution of generative models and the cumulative quality degradation from social media sharing, leaving a critical gap in reliable detection under realistic deployment scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260805/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, resized so the shorter side is 512 pixels, then center-cropped to 224×224 at inference (random 224×224 crops during training). It produces a binary real/fake label with a sigmoid confidence score. Internally, the image is projected into the Stable Diffusion VAE latent space for near-real sample generation, and encoded by a DINOv3 ViT-H+/16 backbone (fine-tuned via LoRA) for feature-space envelope estimation.

## Method

REM shifts detection from learning generator artifacts to modeling the stable boundary of the real image distribution via three modules. (1) Manifold Boundary Reconstruction (MBR) encodes real images with the SD VAE, injects controlled Gaussian noise into a random subset of latent dimensions, and decodes to produce diverse near-real samples that form a local envelope around the real manifold. (2) The Envelope Estimator (EE) learns a smooth decision boundary separating real from near-real samples using binary cross-entropy plus a tangency regularization that penalizes off-manifold feature displacement via PCA-based tangent-space projection. (3) Cross-Domain Consistency (CDC) pairs a frozen DINOv3 anchor with a fine-tuned DINOv3 learner, enforcing both anchor consistency (learner stays near anchor space) and residual consistency (learner–anchor residuals remain invariant under simulated chain degradations), thereby stabilizing the envelope across quality domains. Training runs for a single epoch on 4 A100 GPUs (~30 min) with Adam at lr=1e-4 and batch size 256.

## Results

REM achieves a 94.9% average balanced accuracy (B.Acc) across 12 benchmarks (6 ideal + 5 in-the-wild + RealChain), representing a 6.9% improvement over the strongest prior method DDA (88.0%). On ideal benchmarks, REM reaches 97.6% avg B.Acc (vs. DDA 91.1%, Aligned 73.4%, DRCT 82.2%), and on in-the-wild benchmarks it scores 94.5% (vs. DDA 88.6%). On the severely degraded RealChain-CD setting, REM maintains 84.2% B.Acc while the next-best method DDA drops to 68.0%, a gap of 16.2 points that underscores the robustness of real-distribution envelope modeling under chain degradations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REM outperforms all nine baselines across every benchmark group. Against the strongest prior method DDA [10] (NeurIPS 2025), REM gains +6.5% on ideal benchmarks (97.6 vs. 91.1), +5.9% on in-the-wild benchmarks (94.5 vs. 88.6), and +16.2% on RealChain-CD (84.2 vs. 68.0). The alignment-based method Aligned [38] achieves 72.2% overall but collapses to 58.0% under chain degradations, while DRCT [8] (ICML 2024) reaches only 69.8% overall and 55.4% on RealChain-CD. Artifact-driven detectors (NPR, SAFE, C2P-CLIP, AIDE, FatFormer, UnivFD) all fall below 61% overall and degrade to near-chance (49–56%) on RealChain-CD, confirming their fragility under real-world propagation. The largest single-benchmark gap appears on EvalGEN, where REM scores 99.2% versus DDA's 97.2% and all artifact-driven methods below 46%, highlighting that even the best prior methods struggle with the newest generation of diffusion and autoregressive generators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant artifact-driven paradigm (NPR, SAFE, AIDE, C2P-CLIP) that learns generator-specific forgery cues, arguing instead that the real image distribution—constrained by physical imaging principles—is a more stable and generalizable anchor. It also builds conceptually on the dataset-alignment line (DRCT, Aligned, DDA), explicitly observing that those methods' success stems from implicitly modeling the real distribution boundary through reconstruction perturbations rather than from strict pixel-level correspondence. The MBR module repurposes VAE latent-space perturbation (from Stable Diffusion's VAE) as a controlled manifold-exploration tool, while the Envelope Estimator introduces tangency regularization from differential geometry to enforce smooth boundary geometry, and the CDC module leverages DINOv3's self-supervised invariance as a semantic anchor under degradations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The real-centric framing offers a principled answer to the fundamental fragility of artifact-driven detectors under generator evolution and multi-round propagation, and the 18.4% gap on the severely degraded RealChain benchmark underscores how large the practical failure mode is for existing methods. The RealChain benchmark itself is a valuable contribution, as prior evaluations largely ignored cross-platform compression chains and user post-processing. Open questions remain: performance on RealChain CD still plateaus around 84%, the approach requires ~100k real images for optimal envelope coverage, and the binary real/fake formulation does not yet address attribution to specific generators or extension to video, which the authors flag as future work.
