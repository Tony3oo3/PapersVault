## Problem

Existing deep functional map methods for non-rigid 3D shape matching rely primarily on geometric prior constraints (orthogonality, bijectivity) while neglecting self-supervised prior constraints, resulting in suboptimal performance under non-isometric deformations and topological noise. Moreover, these methods incur substantial computational overhead from dense least-squares solvers or test-time fine-tuning, limiting their practical efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261179/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D triangle meshes (X, Y) with arbitrary vertex counts and produces dense pointwise correspondences as a binary |V_Y|×|V_X| matrix. Features are extracted via DiffusionNet (discretization-agnostic), and the coarse soft map is computed as a softmax of the feature similarity matrix with a temperature parameter. At inference, near-isometric scenarios recover correspondences from the hybrid spectral basis space (concatenated Laplacian and elastic eigenfunctions), while non-isometric scenarios use direct nearest-neighbor search in the learned feature space.

## Method

The architecture is a dual-branch network with symmetric intrinsic (orthonormal Laplacian basis) and extrinsic (non-orthogonal elastic basis) functional map learning streams, both supervised by a coarse-to-fine strategy that aligns a differentiable softmax soft map with a non-differentiable refined map. Three contrastive energy terms—spatial (soft map vs. refined pointwise map), spectral (coarse vs. refined functional map), and spatial-spectral (coarse functional map vs. refined pointwise map projected to spectral domain)—serve as self-supervised losses alongside geometric priors. The refinement module extends multi-scale Laplacian commutativity to non-orthogonal bases using Parseval-normalized filters, enabling closed-form refinement without matrix inversion. Crucially, the method eliminates the computationally expensive k²×k² least-squares solver entirely, relying solely on pointwise map constraints for training. The paper further proves that these contrastive energies promote feature discrimination and that prior self-supervised losses in the literature are special cases of the proposed formulation.

## Results

The method is evaluated using mean geodesic error (×100, lower is better) across six benchmark datasets. On near-isometric FAUST, it achieves 1.6 (second-best after HybridFMaps at 1.4), and on SCAPE it reaches 2.0. On the challenging SHREC'19 cross-dataset setting, it attains 4.0 (SCAPE→S19), substantially outperforming HybridFMaps (13.0) and DeepFAFM (7.9). On non-isometric SMAL and DT4D-H, it achieves 2.9 and 3.5 respectively, beating all baselines including the closest competitor HybridFMaps (3.5 and 3.9). On TOPKIDS (topological noise), it scores 4.9, again the best among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest unsupervised competitor HybridFMaps [6], the proposed method wins decisively on SCAPE→SHREC'19 (4.0 vs. 13.0), SMAL (2.9 vs. 3.5), DT4D-H (3.5 vs. 3.9), and TOPKIDS (4.9 vs. 5.0), while conceding only FAUST F (1.6 vs. 1.4). Compared to DiffZO [50], it dominates on SCAPE→S19 (4.0 vs. 6.9), SMAL (2.9 vs. 4.3), and DT4D-H (3.5 vs. 4.1), with a marginal loss on FAUST→S19 (4.4 vs. 4.2). Against DeepFAFM [48], it outperforms on SCAPE S19 (4.0 vs. 7.9), SMAL (2.9 vs. 3.9), DT4D-H (3.5 vs. 4.2), and TOPKIDS (4.9 vs. 6.3). On non-isometric benchmarks, the method surpasses even the fully supervised GeomFMaps [20] (SMAL: 2.9 vs. 4.1) and all axiomatic approaches by wide margins (e.g., MWP [33] scores 20.9 on SMAL vs. 2.9). The largest absolute gains appear on SHREC'19 cross-dataset transfer, where the method reduces error by 60–70% relative to prior state-of-the-art, confirming superior generalization to unseen topologies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep functional maps paradigm (FMNet, GeomFMaps) by introducing a coarse-to-fine contrastive strategy that aligns network-predicted coarse maps with axiometrically refined maps, drawing the refinement machinery from DeepFAFM's multi-scale Laplacian commutativity and ZoomOut's spectral-to-spatial conversion. It departs from the dominant dual-branch architectures (HybridFMaps, ULRSSM) by eliminating the dense least-squares solver entirely, replacing spectral functional map optimization with pointwise-map-only supervision. The paper further unifies prior self-supervised losses (from Cao et al. 2024 and Magnet & Ovsjanikov 2024) as special cases of its proposed contrastive energy, positioning its framework as a generalization of existing self-supervised constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating the k²×k² least-squares solver addresses a persistent computational bottleneck in functional map learning, making the approach more accessible for large-scale or real-time applications. The theoretical proof that contrastive energies promote feature discrimination provides a principled justification for the self-supervised design and suggests these energies could be dropped into other functional map pipelines (demonstrated with ULRSSM). Open questions include whether the coarse-to-fine strategy extends naturally to partial matching, multi-shape matching, or settings where the refinement step itself is unreliable (e.g., highly non-isometric deformations where spectral refinement may introduce bias).
