## Problem

Set-based 3D VAEs (e.g., VecSet) are compact and enable efficient diffusion-based generation, but their reconstruction fidelity lags behind voxel-based methods due to two fundamental limitations: global cross-attention decoding imposes excessive smoothness that suppresses local geometric detail, and the latent set is far sparser than sparse voxel grids, restricting spatial resolution. This paper closes the fidelity gap while preserving the compactness advantage of set-based representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260743/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a surface point cloud (3D positions + normals) and a set of voxel anchor positions sampled from active voxel centers (obtained from an off-the-shelf generative model such as HY3D2.1), producing a compact anchored VecSet of M latent tokens (4K–40K). The decoder queries a dense 3D grid to predict a truncated SDF (truncation distance τ=1/128), which is then extracted into a watertight mesh via iso-surface algorithms (e.g., marching cubes). Unlike voxel methods that require 50K–210K active voxels, MSVS-VAE operates with roughly 10× fewer tokens while matching or exceeding their reconstruction quality.

## Method

MSVS-VAE introduces three key components. First, Hierarchical Point-Shuffle Upsampling progressively densifies the anchored VecSet by splitting each latent into r sub-latents with halved channel dimension and predicted geometric offsets, then refining via self-aggregation, increasing spatial density over L stages while keeping total feature budget bounded. Second, AVS-Conv replaces global cross-attention with a geometry-aware local operator that retrieves K nearest neighbors per query and aggregates features using dynamic weights predicted from relative positional offsets (inspired by PointConv), reducing decoding complexity from O(MN) to a favorable local scale. Third, Multi-Scale Query Decoding independently queries features from all upsampling scales via separate AVS-Conv operators, concatenates them, and fuses with a lightweight head so that coarse latents supply stable global context while fine latents capture localized geometry. Training uses a progressive chunk-based strategy (global pre-training followed by localized crop fine-tuning) with TSDF L1+L2 reconstruction loss and KL regularization.

## Results

On Objaverse, MSVS-VAE with 20K tokens achieves MD=1.695 and F1@0.001=98.930, outperforming the strongest voxel-based baseline SparseFlex-1024 (MD=2.165, ~210K tokens) and SparC3D (MD=2.463, ~50K tokens) while using only ~10% of SparseFlex's token budget. At 40K tokens, MSVS-VAE reaches MD=1.395 and F1@0.001=99.621 on Objaverse, surpassing Lattice-40K (MD=2.838, F1@0.001=96.787) by a wide margin. On Dora-Bench Level-4, MSVS-VAE-40K achieves S-MD=3.15 and S-F=97.9%, beating SparseFlex (S-MD=3.08, S-F=96.2%) and SparC3D (S-MD=3.22, S-F=94.4%). Decoding latency scales far more favorably: at 40K tokens, MSVS-VAE queries in 213 ms versus 3068 ms for Lattice (14.4× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest set-based baseline Lattice, MSVS-VAE wins decisively at every token budget: at 40K tokens, MSVS-VAE achieves MD=1.395 vs. Lattice's 2.838 on Objaverse (a 2× improvement) and F1@0.001 of 99.621 vs. 96.787. At the same 4K budget as Dora and HY3D2.1, MSVS-VAE reduces Objaverse MD from 17.352 (Dora) and 11.485 (HY3D2.1) to 5.644. Against voxel-based methods, MSVS-VAE at 20K tokens (MD=1.695 on Objaverse) outperforms SparC3D (~50K tokens, MD=2.463) and Direct3D-S2 (~74K tokens, MD=3.301), and even beats SparseFlex-1024 (~210K tokens, MD=2.165) while using only ~10% of its tokens. On Dora-Bench Level-4 sharp-edge metrics, MSVS-VAE-40K (S-MD=3.15, S-F=97.9%) surpasses SparseFlex (S-F=96.2%) and SparC3D (S-F=94.4%), though SparseFlex retains a slight S-MD edge (3.08 vs. 3.15). In decoding efficiency, MSVS-VAE's AVS-Conv yields 5.5×/9.4×/14.4× speedups over Lattice at 10K/20K/40K tokens, with latency growing sub-linearly (144→213 ms) versus Lattice's near-quadratic growth (792→3068 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

MSVS-VAE extends the anchored VecSet (VoxSet) representation introduced by Lattice [17] and the original VecSet [49], which encode 3D shapes as compact latent sets decoded via global cross-attention. The key architectural departure is replacing that global cross-attention with AVS-Conv, a local KNN-weighted aggregation operator inspired by PointConv [43], and introducing a hierarchical point-shuffle upsampling mechanism (drawing on PU-GCN [32] and sub-pixel convolution [38]) to progressively densify the latent set. This positions the work as a targeted fix for the two identified limitations of prior VecSet VAEs—excessive global smoothness from full-set attention and insufficient spatial resolution from sparse latents—without abandoning the compactness advantage of set-based representations over sparse voxel grids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 20K-token set-based VAE matches or exceeds voxel-based methods using 50K–210K tokens while decoding ~10× faster, MSVS-VAE substantially narrows the long-standing fidelity gap between the two representation paradigms, which is critical because the VAE is the primary bottleneck in latent 3D diffusion pipelines. The AVS-Conv operator and hierarchical upsampling are clean, modular components likely to be adopted in future set-based 3D generative systems. Open questions remain: sharp-feature extraction under continuous implicit fields is still challenging, decoding efficiency still trails voxel methods, and extension to texture/appearance modeling is unaddressed, limiting immediate applicability to full asset generation.
