## Problem

When LiDAR encounters reflective surfaces, specular reflections produce virtual points that mimic real structures and degrade downstream 3D perception. Existing removal methods rely on multi-stage pipelines that first estimate reflective surfaces and then perform symmetry detection, making them sensitive to surface noise and prone to error accumulation across stages. No prior approach performs virtual point removal as a unified, end-to-end process without explicit surface reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw 3D multi-return LiDAR point cloud (containing both real and reflection-induced virtual points) and projects it into a 256×512 equirectangular range map with L=32 layers at 5-meter intervals starting from 5 m. The network outputs a per-layer 2D binary label map in the ERP domain, which is unprojected back to 3D via the inverse layer-centric mapping to yield a cleaned point cloud with virtual points removed. Training supervision is a per-pixel BCE loss computed only over valid (non-empty) ERP pixels.

## Method

The core representation is a Layer-Wise Nearest Point Map that, for each layer reference depth d_i, assigns the nearest 3D point along each ray to that layer rather than binning points by fixed distance intervals; this keeps real and reflected returns on the same layer when d_i falls between their ranges, preserving geometric continuity for symmetry learning. On top of this map, a Ray-Path-Aware Attention module computes surface normals from the first layer, derives reflected ray directions via the standard reflection law, samples the reflected trajectory within the observed multi-return interval, and projects it back to the ERP to form physically constrained attention paths along which only K nearest neighbors are attended. These attention paths are integrated into a Symmetry Attention Block (with LeFF for local enhancement and GPSA for cross-layer interaction) stacked in a nine-block U-Net encoder-decoder. The model is trained with a synthetic-to-real strategy: pre-trained on 3,000 physically ray-traced synthetic scenes generated from Semantic3D, then fine-tuned on small real-world datasets (UNIST LS3DPC, 3DRN) for 20 epochs.

## Results

On the UNIST LS3DPC dataset, the proposed method with cross fine-tuning achieves the best F1 score of 0.7895 (Precision 0.8194, Recall 0.7618), outperforming the strongest baseline Fang et al. (F1 0.7580) by 3.2 points. On the 3DRN dataset, the cross fine-tuned model reaches F1 0.8062 (Precision 0.8239, Recall 0.7894), surpassing Fang et al. (F1 0.7840) by 2.2 points. Even without any real-data fine-tuning (synthetic-only training), the method matches or exceeds all baselines on 3DRN (F1 0.7874 vs. 0.7840). Inference time drops from 2+ hours (conventional pipelines) to 8.2 seconds, a speedup of over two orders of magnitude.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three baselines on both datasets in F1 score. On UNIST LS3DPC, it beats Fang et al. [3] (the strongest baseline) by 3.2 F1 points (0.7895 vs. 0.7580) and Yun and Sim [25] by 7.4 points (0.7895 vs. 0.7151). On 3DRN, it surpasses Fang et al. by 2.2 F1 points (0.8062 vs. 0.7840) and Yun and Sim by 19.2 points (0.8062 vs. 0.6143). Notably, even the synthetic-only variant (no real-data fine-tuning) already matches or exceeds Fang et al. on 3DRN (F1 0.7874 vs. 0.7840), demonstrating strong synthetic-to-real transfer. The proposed method also achieves the highest Precision in every setting, which is critical for avoiding erroneous removal of valid geometry. In terms of runtime, the end-to-end ERP-based inference (8.2 s) is roughly 150× faster than the multi-stage 3D pipelines (2+ hours), while simultaneously delivering better accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established multi-stage pipeline paradigm for virtual point removal (Yun & Sim 2019, Lee et al. 2023, Fang et al. 2025), which sequentially estimates reflective surfaces and then performs 3D symmetry verification. Instead, it reformulates the problem as end-to-end ray-aligned reasoning in the 2D equirectangular projection domain, drawing on range-map representations for LiDAR (Uhrig et al., Lu et al.) and transformer-style attention (UFormer, ViT) but introducing two purpose-built components: a layer-centric multi-return map and a physics-constrained attention mechanism. The synthetic-to-real training strategy borrows from domain adaptation literature but is applied here to overcome the scarcity of annotated reflection data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-orders-of-magnitude speedup (8.2 s vs. 2+ hours) makes virtual point removal practical for near-real-time or large-scale mapping pipelines where prior methods were prohibitively slow. The ablation showing that naive interval-based projection collapses F1 from 0.79 to 0.20 underscores that representation design is as critical as the network architecture for multi-return LiDAR tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to airborne or mobile LiDAR with different ray geometries, how it handles cascaded or multi-bounce reflections, and whether the synthetic-to-real gap narrows further with more diverse reflection materials in training.
