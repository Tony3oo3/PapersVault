## Problem

Existing category-level 3D object understanding focuses on pose estimation and fails to capture fine-grained semantics needed for reasoning about object parts, functions, and interactions. No benchmark or method exists for predicting semantically consistent 3D correspondences directly in camera space from single RGB-D images, with prior work limited to 2D camera-space matching or 3D object-space methods requiring pre-reconstructed meshes. This paper identifies single-view category-level 3D correspondence in camera space as the missing link and introduces both a benchmark (HouseCorr3D) and a method (Morpheus) to address it.

## Input / Output

Given a query RGB-D image, a target RGB-D image (both at 448×448 resolution) of objects from the same category, and a query 3D point in the query camera space, the method predicts the corresponding 3D point in the target camera space. The correspondence is mediated through barycentric coordinates on a shared template mesh: the query point is projected onto the deformed query mesh, encoded as barycentric coordinates relative to a face, and transferred to the identically-topologized target mesh. The model also outputs a 6D pose (from a pretrained diffusion network) and the full deformed mesh in camera space, enabling amodal correspondence for occluded regions that 2D methods cannot handle.

## Method

Morpheus represents all instances of a category as identity-preserving affine deformations of a shared canonical template, so that semantic correspondences emerge implicitly from persistent vertex identity without any explicit correspondence supervision. The canonical shape is stored as a signed distance field converted to a mesh via Differentiable Marching Tetrahedra; instance-specific deformations are produced by a coordinate-conditioned MLP that takes template vertex positions and a latent code (extracted by a DINOv2 ViT-S backbone with a ResNet head) and outputs per-vertex scale and translation offsets. A pretrained 6D pose diffusion network places the deformed mesh in camera space, decoupling pose from shape to avoid the entanglement failures observed in MagicPony. Training proceeds in two stages: first refining the category template with Chamfer distance, amodal 2D mask, distance-transform, and Eikonal losses, then learning instance deformations with added L2 deformation and edge-smoothness regularizers. The key insight is that constraining every instance to deform the same template while matching observed data forces semantically aligned correspondences to arise as a byproduct, eliminating the need for correspondence labels.

## Results

On HouseCorr3D, Morpheus achieves 31.3 PCK@0.1 in 2D, 43.7 in 3D modal, 40.8 in 3D amodal, and 41.5 in 3D (modal+amodal), setting a new state of the art across all settings. The closest 3D baseline, GenPose++ (GP++), reaches 37.0 in 3D modal and 34.3 in 3D (M+A), a gap of 6.7 and 7.2 pp respectively. On the real-world ROPE subset (5 classes, 24 instances), Morpheus transfers to 44.7 (2D) and 34.8 (3D) PCK@0.1, outperforming GP++ (37.0 / 25.1) by 7.7 and 9.7 pp. Ablation shows instance-specific deformations add 3.5 pp over the rigid-template variant (Morpheus w/o Def.), with gains up to 14.7 pp on high-variation categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Morpheus outperforms all five baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the strongest 3D baseline GenPose++ (GP++), Morpheus gains 6.7 pp in 3D modal (43.7 vs. 37.0) and 7.2 pp in 3D modal+amodal (41.5 vs. 34.3), confirming that a learned deformable template surpasses rigid pose-based point transfer. The closest morphable-prior baseline, Common3D, is beaten by over 27 pp in 3D modal (43.7 vs. 16.0) and 29 pp in 3D amodal (40.8 vs. 11.2), attributed to Common3D's weak RGB-depth integration and inconsistencies between its implicit and explicit deformations. Among 2D feature-matching methods, NOCS (26.4) and DINOv2 (16.4) in 3D modal are far behind, and none can address the amodal setting since occluded points lack depth. MagicPony+GP++ performs worst among 3D methods (7.5 in 3D modal), illustrating that entangling canonical and camera space during training degrades 3D correspondence reliability. On the real ROPE subset, Morpheus leads GP++ by 9.7 pp in 3D (34.8 vs. 25.1), validating synthetic-to-real transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

Morpheus extends the morphable-model lineage (3DMM, SMPL) from faces and bodies to diverse everyday objects, adapting the affine-deformation framework of MagicPony [56] to act on mesh vertices rather than the SDF directly, and grounding the pipeline in a pretrained 6D pose diffusion model from Omni6DPose [68]. Conceptually, it departs from prior 3D correspondence methods (KeypointNet, DenseCorr3D) that require pre-reconstructed meshes as input, and from 2D feature-matching approaches (DINOv2, NOCS) that cannot reason about occluded geometry or enforce 3D consistency. The key positioning is that correspondences are established directly in camera space via a shared deformable template, rather than in normalized object-centric spaces (NOCS) or on pre-reconstructed meshes, eliminating the brittle multi-stage pipeline of reconstruction → pose → canonicalization → matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HouseCorr3D fills a genuine gap: no prior benchmark enables evaluation of single-view category-level 3D correspondence in camera space with amodal labels and explicit symmetry handling, making it a likely reference for future work in this direction. The demonstration that semantically meaningful correspondences emerge implicitly from a shared morphable prior—without any correspondence supervision—suggests a broader principle for 3D understanding: structural identity in a template can substitute for explicit semantic labels. Key open questions include handling topological variation (the 19pp drop on different-topology pairs exposes a fundamental limitation of fixed-connectivity templates), reducing pose sensitivity (up to 14pp recovery with GT pose), and extending beyond synthetic household objects to open-world categories with greater shape diversity.
