## Problem

Lipschitz-by-design networks (LipNets) provide efficient, deterministic robustness certificates for classification but have never been adapted to dense prediction tasks like semantic segmentation. Existing certification approaches for segmentation—randomized smoothing (SEGCERTIFY, LOCALIZEDLP) and formal verification—either require thousands of Monte Carlo samples per image (making them unusable in real-time) or fail to scale to high-resolution, multi-million-parameter models. This paper bridges that gap by introducing the first real-time compatible certifiably robust semantic segmentation framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (1024×1024 for Cityscapes, 128×128 for Oxford-IIIT Pet, endoscopic frames for Kvasir-SEG) and produces a per-pixel class prediction map alongside deterministic robustness certificates computed in the same forward pass. Certificates are expressed as worst-case lower bounds on pixel accuracy (CRPA), false negative rate (FNR), or prediction stability under ℓ₂ adversarial perturbations of user-specified radius ε. Because the certificate is derived from a single knapsack solve over pixel-wise margins, it is valid simultaneously for all ε values without re-running inference.

## Method

The core architecture is a DeepLabV3 variant whose convolutional and linear layers are re-parameterized to be L-Lipschitz in ℓ₂ norm using the ORTHOGONIUM and DEEL-LIP libraries, so that the global Lipschitz constant L is known analytically as the product of per-layer constants. The key theoretical contribution is a generalized certification framework that converts the worst-case performance question (Q1) and the generalized robustness radius question (Q2) into a unidimensional knapsack problem over pixel-wise robustness margins: each pixel ω has a cost c_ω = (L·R̲^ω)² representing the squared output-space budget needed to flip its prediction, and the knapsack capacity is (Lε)². For Q1, pixels are sorted by ascending cost and greedily selected until capacity is exhausted, yielding the maximum number of flippable pixels and hence the CRPA lower bound; for Q2, the certificate is simply the square root of the sum of the n_γ smallest squared margins. This formulation runs in O(|S| log |S|) time, is trivially parallelizable on GPU, and requires only one forward pass—achieving ~600× speedup over SEGCERTIFY while delivering comparable certificate tightness.

## Results

On Cityscapes (1024×1024), the proposed LipNet achieves a Certifiably Robust Pixel Accuracy (CRPA) of 81.80% at ε=0.1 and 77.34% at ε=0.17, with a single forward pass taking ≈0.1 s. Clean pixel accuracy is 92.07% (vs. 94.41% for the unconstrained DeepLabV3). On the Kvasir-SEG polyp detection task, the forward pass requires only ≈0.05 s while providing non-vacuous worst-case FNR bounds (e.g., max FNR ≤ 0.612 at ε=0.1). The method is approximately 600× faster than SEGCERTIFY at inference on an NVIDIA A100 GPU while delivering comparable certificates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is SEGCERTIFY (Fischer et al., ICML 2021), the state-of-the-art randomized smoothing method for certified segmentation. On Cityscapes at ε=0.1, the LipNet achieves 81.80% CRPA versus 83.13% for SEGCERTIFY (σ=0.2), a gap of only ~1.3 percentage points, but the LipNet requires a single forward pass (≈0.1 s) while SEGCERTIFY needs 80 inferences over ~62 s per image (~600× slower). At ε=0.17, the gap widens to ~7.5 points (77.34% vs. 84.84%), reflecting the inherent conservatism of deterministic Lipschitz over-approximation at larger radii. In a model-agnostic ablation on the same LipNet (Oxford-IIIT Pet, 128×128), SEGCERTIFY requires approximately 10³ Monte Carlo samples to match the LipNet's single-pass certificate, corresponding to a ~2000× runtime penalty. The paper also demonstrates that SEGCERTIFY faces a strict trilemma: certifying larger radii with high accuracy demands massive MC budgets, while keeping inference fast forces σ low and collapses certificates to zero. Overall, the LipNet trades a small certificate tightness gap (especially at larger ε) for orders-of-magnitude speedup, making it the only certifiably robust segmentation method compatible with real-time deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Lipschitz-by-design networks (Tsuzoku et al., Anil et al.), which were previously restricted to classification and regression, to the semantic segmentation setting by constraining a DeepLabV3 architecture via ORTHOGONIUM and DEEL-LIP reparametrizations. It departs from the dominant randomized-smoothing line of work for segmentation (SEGCERTIFY, LOCALIZEDLP) by replacing Monte-Carlo probabilistic certificates with a single-pass deterministic bound, and introduces a knapsack-based reduction that converts the worst-case pixel-misclassification problem into a greedily solvable combinatorial optimization. The generalized Q1/Q2 certification framework unifies pixel accuracy, FNR, stability, and IoU under a single Lipschitz-output-ball formulation, going beyond the pixel-wise classification certificates that prior segmentation methods provided.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first certifiably robust semantic segmentation method compatible with real-time inference (<0.1 s on 1024×1024 images), making deterministic robustness guarantees practical for safety-critical deployments such as autonomous driving and medical polyp detection. The ~600× speedup over SEGCERTIFY and the explicit accuracy–robustness operating-point control via temperature offer a compelling alternative to the efficiency/accuracy/robustness trilemma inherent in randomized smoothing. Key limitations include restriction to ℓ₂ norm, a ~2.3 pp clean-accuracy drop from Lipschitz constraints, and a widening deterministic gap at larger ε that remains unaddressed; future work on hybrid feasibility constraints and input-distribution assumptions could tighten bounds while preserving real-time compatibility.
