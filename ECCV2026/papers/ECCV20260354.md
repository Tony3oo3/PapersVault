## Problem

Existing non-prehensile manipulation methods for grasping (e.g., CORN, DyWA) require a predefined target object pose, but in practice objects admit multiple graspable configurations and the desired pose is unknown in advance. A pose-reaching objective optimizes geometric similarity to one configuration, which can force the policy away from nearby graspable basins and fails to capture whether the object is actually ready for a stable grasp. The paper argues that grasp preparation should be formulated as progressively improving a graspability measure rather than tracking a specific pose.

## Input / Output

The policy receives a partial point cloud (rendered via nvdiffrast in simulation; fused from three RealSense D435IF depth cameras in the real world) along with robot proprioceptive state, and a GRU aggregates temporal context to handle occlusion and contact dynamics. It outputs three quantities per step: a non-prehensile manipulation action, a scalar predicted graspability distance (used both as a dense learning signal during training and as a scale-invariant stopping criterion at execution), and a terminal grasp pose that is only consumed at the manipulation-to-grasp transition. No target pose or reference configuration is provided as input at test time.

## Method

GOMP replaces the target-pose objective with a graspability field: a soft-min geometric distance from the current object state to a set of graspable reference configurations (synthesized offline via SE(3)-DiffusionFields), regularized by a temporal anchor term to prevent oscillation between competing grasp modes. The teacher policy is trained with reinforcement learning using potential-based reward shaping on this distance, providing dense credit assignment without requiring grasp attempts during training. A student policy with a GRU recurrent module is then distilled from the teacher to operate on partial observations, and the same predicted graspability distance drives both the manipulation reward and a scale-invariant transition threshold (normalized by object bounding-box diagonal) that triggers grasp execution. This yields a single closed-loop policy that manipulates, decides when to stop, and executes the grasp without external planners or manually specified stopping conditions.

## Results

In simulation, GOMP (teacher) achieves 84.0% success on seen objects and 77.9% on unseen objects, with average completion times of 4.6 s and 4.8 s respectively. The student policy (operating on partial observations) reaches 75.5% and 68.1% success at 6.1 s and 6.2 s. On the real Franka robot, the full manipulation-to-grasp pipeline achieves 62% end-to-end success (31/50 trials) across 10 diverse objects, with 97% grasp success (31/32) conditioned on successful manipulation, compared to only 8% success from direct grasping without prior manipulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOMP substantially outperforms all target-pose baselines. The retrained CORN/DyWA teacher (36.1% seen, 28.8% unseen) is the strongest direct baseline, yet GOMP's student policy exceeds it by 39.4 and 39.3 percentage points on seen and unseen objects respectively. Even the Nearest-reference Oracle—which at every step selects the closest graspable reference as its target, representing the fairest upper bound for pose-reaching—reaches only 52.0% seen and 50.2% unseen, still well below GOMP's student (75.5%, 68.1%). The pretrained CORN/DyWA checkpoints perform poorly (6.1% unseen without stability check, 1.9% with) because their original training targets stable resting poses, whereas GOMP's references are graspable but not always dynamically stable, exposing a fundamental mismatch between pose-reaching and grasp-preparation objectives. GOMP also completes tasks faster (4.6–6.2 s) than all baselines (5.3–12.9 s), indicating that optimizing graspability rather than tracking a fixed pose yields more efficient manipulation trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

GOMP builds directly on the CORN/DyWA line of goal-conditioned non-prehensile manipulation, reusing its object-centric encoder, teacher-student distillation framework, and nvdiffrast-based partial observation pipeline. The key departure is replacing the single-target pose-reaching objective with a set-based graspability field: instead of conditioning the policy on a predefined configuration, the policy optimizes a scalar distance to a graspable set constructed offline from a grasp synthesis model (SE(3)-DiffusionFields). This positions the work between goal-conditioned manipulation (CORN, DyWA) and affordance-guided grasping, arguing that the manipulation objective should be a property of the object state rather than alignment with a specific grasp or pose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified graspability signal that simultaneously drives manipulation, provides dense reward shaping, and determines the manipulation-to-grasp transition eliminates the need for external planners or hand-tuned stopping criteria, yielding a genuinely closed-loop single-policy pipeline. Limitations include reliance on an offline grasp synthesis model to define the graspable set (not learned end-to-end), evaluation restricted to a single Franka arm with a parallel jaw on tabletop objects, and consistent failure on deformable/slippery objects (Snack). Open questions include whether the graspability field generalizes across gripper morphologies, whether the reference set can be learned online, and whether the approach extends to multi-object or cluttered scenes.
