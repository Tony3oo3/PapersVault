## Problem

Point-supervised oriented object detection for aerial images must recover full box geometry (size and angle) from only a single point per object, yet existing methods rely on complex multi-stage pipelines (pseudo-labeling, multi-view augmentation, SAM integration) that multiply computational cost and training complexity. The field lacks a simple, compact baseline that achieves competitive performance through lightweight, easily transferable components without external dependencies or duplicated computations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262178/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, each sample provides an RGB image (1024×1024 patches for DOTA/CODrone, 800×800 for DroneVehicle) and per-object annotations consisting solely of a 2D point coordinate and a one-hot class label—no box, angle, or size information. The detector outputs pixel-wise oriented bounding boxes (x, y, w, h, θ) with class scores on the P3 FPN layer, where positive boxes are those whose estimation pixel falls within the neighborhood of a point annotation. All geometric supervision is derived from the network's own predictions and geometric priors rather than ground-truth boxes.

## Method

PlacePriorBase uses a standard FCOS-style architecture (ResNet-50 backbone, FPN, PSC angle coder) trained in a single stage with three plug-and-play loss terms grounded in the 'placement prior'—the observation that aerial objects are placed in a natural context rather than arbitrarily. The angle prior loss regularizes neighborhood angle alignment using a distance-weighted mean of vec(θ)=[cos 4θ; sin 4θ] vectors, capturing parallel/perpendicular alignment. The size prior loss models same-class object sizes as a linear function of position via per-image ridge regression (C+2 parameters), providing approximate size targets without any size annotation. The Voronoi Watershed RBox loss is training-free: it partitions the image by point annotations, extracts a prior angle from the default watershed basin's enclosing RBox, re-runs watershed with directional priorities informed by that angle, and supervises the full rotated box via Gaussian Wasserstein Distance. These losses are discarded at inference and can be dropped into other point-supervised detectors (demonstrated on PointOBB-v3, Point2RBox-v2, and Point2RBox-v3) with consistent gains.

## Results

On four aerial datasets, PlacePriorBase (the simple baseline) achieves mAP@0.5 of 40.75 on DroneVehicle, 21.70 on CODrone, 42.05 on DOTA-v1.0, and 32.38 on DOTA-v1.5, reaching on average 82.45% of the end-to-end Point2RBox-v2 performance. When the proposed loss terms are integrated into SOTA baselines, PlacePrior+P2R2 achieves 56.37 / 24.11 / 52.63 / 43.06 and PlacePrior+P2R3 achieves 64.15 / 30.43 / 61.09 / 51.61 on the same four datasets, consistently outperforming the respective base methods. Training is significantly more efficient: PlacePriorBase requires only 14.19 ms per batch versus 85.36 ms for Point2RBox-v2, with 1443.7 MB GPU memory versus 2706.7 MB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PlacePriorBase (the standalone simple baseline) outperforms PointOBB-v2 on all four datasets, with the largest gains on CODrone (+3.59 mAP) and DOTA-v1.5 (+1.79 mAP), while achieving comparable or slightly better results on DroneVehicle (+0.51) and DOTA-v1.0 (+0.37). When the proposed loss terms are plugged into Point2RBox-v2, PlacePrior+P2R2 improves over the base method by +1.89 on DroneVehicle, +1.59 on CODrone, +1.31 on DOTA-v1.0, and +1.41 on DOTA-v1.5. Similarly, PlacePrior+P2R3 surpasses Point2RBox-v3 by +1.72 on DroneVehicle, +1.69 on CODrone, +1.20 on DOTA-v1.0, and +1.29 on DOTA-v1.5. Against the strongest RBox-supervised upper bound (RTMDet), PlacePrior+P2R3 still trails by 5–10 mAP points, confirming the inherent gap between point-supervised and fully-supervised detection. The paper also shows that PlacePriorBase trains in 14.19 ms/batch versus 85.36 ms for Point2RBox-v2, a ~6× speedup, while using roughly half the GPU memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Point2RBox-v2 architecture (ResNet-50 + FPN + PSC angle coder) but deliberately strips away its multi-view augmentation, consistency losses, and dense Voronoi watershed computation in favor of three lightweight geometric loss terms grounded in a 'placement prior' for aerial scenes. Conceptually, it departs from the PointOBB and Point2RBox lineages—which rely on pseudo-labeling stages, multi-view transformations, or SAM-based pseudo-label refinement—by showing that contextual regularizers (neighborhood angle alignment, per-class size proportionality, and training-free watershed-derived RBox targets) can substitute for these heavier mechanisms. The Voronoi Watershed RBox loss is inspired by Point2RBox-v2's watershed segmentation but re-frames it as a training-free target generator using enclosing-box angle priors rather than on-line network angle estimates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a compact, single-stage baseline that reduces training time by roughly 6× (14.19 ms vs. 85.36 ms per batch) while remaining competitive with or complementary to SOTA point-supervised detectors, lowering the barrier for future research in this area. The plug-and-play nature of the three loss terms—demonstrated by consistent gains when grafted onto PointOBB-v3, Point2RBox-v2, and Point2RBox-v3—suggests they could serve as a standard regularization toolkit for the subfield. However, the placement prior is explicitly acknowledged as not universally valid (misaligned objects, variable same-class sizes, texture-misled watershed regions), and its applicability is confined to ground-plane aerial imagery; whether these geometric priors transfer to non-aerial or 3D-oriented detection remains open.
