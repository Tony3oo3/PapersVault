## Problem

Monocular visual odometry cannot recover absolute scene scale, and existing plenoptic VO methods (e.g., SPO) project points between only two consecutive frames without performing a full bundle adjustment, making them sensitive to drift and less robust. The paper addresses the need for a drift-resilient, metric-scale VO pipeline that tightly integrates the plenoptic camera model into both front-end tracking and back-end optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260115/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of raw focused plenoptic images (e.g., Raytrix R5 or R32), each of which is preprocessed into a totally focused image, a virtual depth map, and a virtual depth uncertainty map. It outputs a camera pose trajectory, a sparse 3D point cloud in metric coordinates, and an absolute scale estimate. The pipeline operates in a sliding window over keyframes, with older keyframes marginalized via prior constraints, and requires no external depth sensor or IMU.

## Method

PRISM-VO formulates a sparse photometric plenoptic bundle adjustment that jointly optimizes camera poses, inverse depths, and affine brightness parameters within a sliding window, using the focused plenoptic projection model (Galilean mode) rather than a pinhole model. The energy function combines photometric residuals on totally focused images with variance-weighted inverse virtual depth residuals, where each depth residual is weighted by the inverse of its measurement variance so that reliable near-range depth dominates. An adaptive cross-modal weight η is updated online by matching the average Gauss-Newton curvature of the two residual types, automatically increasing depth influence in close-range scenes and photometric influence in distant scenes. The nonlinear system is solved with Levenberg-Marquardt iterations using a Schur complement that exploits the block-sparse Hessian structure, and keyframes leaving the window are marginalized. Multi-scale pyramids for the depth and uncertainty maps are built via variance-weighted averaging, and point selection uses an adaptive inverse-depth cutoff combined with gradient-based scoring to favor reliable nearby points while maintaining image coverage.

## Results

On the LiFMCR dataset (7 scenes, Vicon ground truth), PRISM-VO achieves the best translational RMSE on 6 out of 7 sequences, with values ranging from 9.32 mm (scene 05) to 110.87 mm (scene 07), and the best rotational RMSE on 4 sequences (all under 3°). On the Synchronized Stereo and Plenoptic VO Dataset (11 loop-closure sequences), PRISM-VO outperforms the plenoptic baseline SPO on alignment error (4/6/10 sequences under 1%/2%/4% vs. 2/6/9) and rotation error (6/8/10 under 1°/2°/4° vs. 3/7/8), while both methods recover absolute scale within 10% on 8–10 sequences. Against monocular methods, PRISM-VO is the only approach that reliably recovers metric scale, and it exhibits consistently lower scale drift than DSO while matching DPVO on overall alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the plenoptic-specific baseline SPO, PRISM-VO wins on alignment error (4/6/10 vs. 2/6/9 sequences under 1%/2%/4%) and rotation error (6/8/10 vs. 3/7/8 under 1°/2°/4°), while scale drift is comparable (7/7/10 vs. 7/8/9 under 1.05/1.1/1.3); PRISM-VO also succeeds on one sequence where SPO fails entirely. Against DSO, PRISM-VO shows consistently lower scale drift and similar alignment error, but DSO occasionally achieves lower rotational RMSE (e.g., 1.97° vs. 2.86° on LiFMCR scene 03). ORB-SLAM3 fails on all 11 sequences of the drift dataset (likely due to limited field of view) and produces the worst translational RMSE on 5 of 7 LiFMCR scenes (up to 773 mm). Against DPVO, PRISM-VO matches or slightly trails on alignment error but is the only method that recovers absolute metric scale; on LiFMCR, DPVO achieves the best translational RMSE only on scene 07 (5.87 mm vs. 110.87 mm) while PRISM-VO dominates on the remaining six scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM-VO adapts the sliding-window photometric bundle adjustment architecture of DSO to the focused plenoptic camera model, replacing the pinhole projection with the plenoptic projection and adding an inverse virtual depth residual term. It departs from the prior plenoptic VO method SPO, which only projects points between two consecutive frames without multi-frame optimization, by performing a full sparse bundle adjustment across a keyframe window with the plenoptic geometry explicitly modeled in both front-end tracking and back-end optimization. The depth priors and camera model are inherited from the authors' own LiFCal calibration and Zeller et al.'s probabilistic depth estimation pipeline, making this a tightly integrated system rather than a plug-in depth source.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRISM-VO demonstrates that a single focused plenoptic camera can recover metric-scale 3D reconstruction and competitive trajectory accuracy without stereo, RGB-D, or IMU sensors, which is valuable for compact robotic and AR systems where sensor fusion is impractical. The adaptive cross-modal weighting (η) that automatically balances photometric and depth residuals based on scene geometry is a practical contribution that avoids manual tuning. Limitations include reduced spatial resolution, narrower field of view compared to monocular cameras, and degraded depth accuracy at long range; the paper does not address real-time performance or integration with loop closure for long-term SLAM. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to unfocused plenoptic cameras or to dynamic scenes with moving objects.
