## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-image diffusion models such as FLUX and SD3 are confined to sub-2MP resolutions because attention complexity grows quadratically with token count and high-quality 4K–8K training data are scarce and expensive. Existing workforces a trade-off: either retrain on costly synthetic high-resolution datasets or use training-free upscaling that sacrifices efficiency and global coherence. No prior method achieves both ultra-high-resolution fidelity and near-linear computational scaling while reusing pretrained weights without native high-resolution training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260378/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a low-resolution guidance latent (generated at 256×256 by the same base model) as joint inputs to the denoising process, producing ultra-high-resolution RGB images up to 8K×8K or larger. The low-resolution guidance is not a user-provided image but an internally generated anchor that encodes global layout; the framework supports recursive generation where a high-res output can serve as the low-res guidance for an even larger output. Training is performed exclusively on 1K×1K images (10K samples generated by FLUX.1-dev), yet inference scales to arbitrary resolutions without additional data.

## Method

UltraImageGen partitions high-resolution latents into fixed 256×256 local windows and reorders tokens in window-first order so that each dense attention block maps to a constant spatial window regardless of image size, reducing attention from O(N²) to O(N·l²) with l=16 latent tokens per side. A low-resolution guidance latent (256×256) is injected with RoPE positional indices scaled by ρ=4 to project onto the high-res spatial grid, providing global semantic anchors that each local window attends to alongside its own and neighboring tokens. Only the Q, K, V projections processing the guidance tokens are adapted via LoRA (rank 16), while the original projections for high-res windows remain frozen, preserving pretrained local generative capability. An attention mask enforces that high-res tokens attend locally plus to text and guidance, guidance tokens attend globally among themselves, and text tokens attend to all. At inference, the low-res guidance is upsampled and noise-mixed to warm-start the high-res latent, bypassing early denoising steps and yielding a 10.77× total inference speedup at 8K over FlashAttention-2 with only 7.3 GB additional VRAM.

## Results

At 4K×4K resolution, UltraImageGen achieves FID 67.03, FID_patch 61.78, IS 17.21, CLIP-IQA 0.4505, and CLIP Score 0.3231, outperforming all training-free baselines and matching or exceeding training-based methods that require native 4K data. With the stronger FLUX.2 backbone, results improve to FID 61.38, IS 18.02, and CLIP-IQA 0.4594. On the GenEval benchmark, overall scores remain stable at 0.67 from 1K through 4K, confirming resolution-agnostic quality. At 8K resolution, the method achieves a 10.77× total inference speedup and 130.4× reduction in attention FLOPs compared to FlashAttention-2, with only 7.3 GB additional VRAM versus 15.6 GB for dense attention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UltraImageGen (FLUX.1) achieves the best FID (67.03), FID_patch (61.78), IS (17.21), and CLIP-IQA (0.4505) among all 13 baselines, beating the strongest training-based method URAE (FID 67.39, IS 17.11) and the best training-free method HiFlow (FID 69.18, IS 17.13) on these core quality metrics. It slightly trails UltraPixel on CLIP Score (0.3231 vs. 0.3251) and HiFlow/CLEAR+SDEdit on IS_patch (13.31 vs. 13.43/13.78), indicating marginally weaker local patch diversity. The efficient-attention baseline CLEAR+SDEdit lags substantially on FID (88.23 vs. 67.03) and FID_patch (87.19 vs. 61.78), confirming that local-window attention with global guidance preserves coherence better than sliding-tile linearization. Training-free methods HiDiffusion and FreCas perform poorly (FID >170), while DemoFusion and DiffuseHigh remain mid-tier (FID ~75–82). With the FLUX.2 backbone, UltraImageGen extends its lead to FID 61.38 and IS 18.02, surpassing all baselines by a wider margin and achieving sub-30-second 4K generation via the 4-step distilled model.

## Relation to Prior Work

UltraImageGen builds directly on the FLUX MMDiT backbone, modifying its self-attention blocks with a hierarchical local-window mechanism and a low-resolution global guidance pathway. It departs from training-based high-resolution methods (SANA, Diffusion-4K, UltraPixel) that require native 4K+ data, and from training-free cascades (HiFlow, I-MAX, DemoFusion) that inherit significant runtime overhead, by instead using a single-pass fixed-window attention with LoRA-adapted LR anchors trained only on 1K data. The window-first token permutation is a practical departure from prior local-attention designs (CLEAR, STA, SwinFormer) that require growing window sizes or multi-stage repair to maintain long-range coherence at higher resolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that ultra-high-resolution (8K+) synthesis is achievable on a single 96GB GPU with >10× speedup and ~10GB memory savings over dense attention, removing the need for expensive native high-resolution training data—a significant practical barrier for the field. The recursive generation capability (using output as LR guidance for even higher resolutions) opens a path to arbitrarily large-scale synthesis, though its stability beyond 8K is not yet validated. Open questions include whether the fixed 256×256 window size remains optimal for all content types (e.g., fine text, repetitive textures), whether the approach extends cleanly to video generation, and whether the LoRA adaptation quality degrades at extreme aspect ratios or resolutions far beyond the 1K training range.
