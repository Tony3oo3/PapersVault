## Problem

No existing framework unifies understanding and generation across the full Motion–Text–RGB tri-modality within a single architecture. Prior methods handle only restricted subsets (MotionGPT covers Motion-Text; UniPose covers static Pose-Image) and rely on discrete VQ-VAE tokenization that introduces quantization errors, disrupts temporal continuity, and complicates cross-modal alignment with the continuous RGB feature space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260269/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts, 269-dimensional continuous motion sequences, and RGB images (single reference for Vision-to-Motion; motion + text for editing) in any combination, and produces 3D motion sequences, natural-language captions, or edited RGB images. At inference the vision-fused encoder is entirely discarded, so motion encoding requires only the motion sequence itself. The backbone is a 1.5B Show-o2 LLM, and generation uses Euler ODE sampling through modality-specific flow heads in the respective latent spaces.

## Method

UniMotion treats motion as a first-class continuous modality symmetric to RGB. A Cross-Modal Aligned Motion VAE (CMA-VAE) encodes motion into a continuous latent via a motion-only encoder, while a vision-fused encoder (frozen HRNet + motion-guided grid sampling) provides a richer posterior during training; Dual-Posterior KL Alignment (DPA) distills the vision-fused posterior into the motion-only encoder using reverse KL, injecting visual-semantic priors without requiring images at inference. Within the shared Show-o2 backbone, a dual-path embedder (semantic MLP+Transformer branch and a detail-preserving MLP+positional-encoding branch) maps both motion and RGB latents into the LLM hidden space, while hybrid attention (bidirectional within motion spans, causal globally) and modality-routed LoRA (rank-32, ~2% extra params) let motion and text/RGB adapt independently. Latent Reconstruction Alignment (LRA) pre-trains the motion pathway via a self-supervised Motion-to-Motion task: the model reconstructs its own CMA-VAE latent from Gaussian noise conditioned on the LLM's fused embedding, co-calibrating the embedder, backbone, and flow head before any sparse text supervision is introduced.

## Results

UniMotion achieves state-of-the-art results across all seven tri-modal tasks it supports. On HumanML3D, it attains T2M R@3 of 0.841 (vs. 0.802 for MG-MotionLLM and 0.778 for MotionGPT), M2T BertScore of 41.2 (vs. 36.7 and 32.4), Motion Prediction ADE of 3.172 cm (vs. 4.745 for MotionGPT), and Motion Editing R@3 of 84.94% (vs. 73.23% for MG-MotionLLM). On Human3.6M, it reaches V2M MPJPE of 75.0 mm (vs. 81.8 for UniPose) and V2T BLEU-4 of 21.9 (vs. 17.3 for UniPose, 12.1 for Show-o2). For Motion-guided Image Editing, UniMotion achieves 0.67 motion accuracy (vs. 0.59 for OpenPose+ControlNet and 0.50 for UniPose+Show-o2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniMotion outperforms all motion-text baselines on their respective tasks: it beats MotionGPT [14] by 6.3 points on T2M R@3 (0.841 vs. 0.778) and by 8.8 points on M2T BertScore (41.2 vs. 32.4), and surpasses MG-MotionLLM [28] by 11.71 points on Motion Editing R@3 (84.94 vs. 73.23). Against the strongest generation-only baselines on HumanML3D, UniMotion leads MoMask [9] (0.807) and DiverseMotion [22] (0.802) on T2M R@3, though MoMask achieves a lower FID (0.045 vs. 0.194), reflecting a trade-off between semantic alignment and single-distribution fidelity. On Vision-to-Motion, UniMotion (75.0 MPJPE) surpasses UniPose [18] (81.8) and ChatPose [6] (146.7) among MLLM methods, though specialist models like SMPLer [34] (50.8) and TokenHMR [5] (52.4) remain stronger. For Motion-guided Image Editing, UniMotion (0.67 Mot.Acc) outperforms both the UniPose+Show-o2 pipeline (0.50) and OpenPose+ControlNet (0.59), demonstrating the advantage of end-to-end latent-space reasoning over staged pipelines. The motion representation ablation (Table 6) confirms CMA-VAE's superiority over VQ-VAE (MotionGPT) and MLD-VAE (MLD) on both reconstruction (APE 3.53 vs. 17.15 and 9.28) and downstream transfer across all tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniMotion extends Show-o2's unified Text-RGB MLLM by inserting a continuous motion pathway architecturally symmetric to RGB, departing fundamentally from the discrete VQ-VAE tokenization paradigm used by MotionGPT and MG-MotionLLM for motion, and from the static-pose-only formulations of UniPose and HMVLM. The flow-matching generation mechanism is inherited from prior work, but the key conceptual departure is treating motion as a first-class continuous modality rather than a sequence of codebook indices, which eliminates quantization errors at the architecture level. Two novel alignment strategies—Dual-Posterior KL Alignment for injecting visual-semantic priors into the motion encoder, and Latent Reconstruction Alignment for self-supervised pathway calibration—address the heterogeneous supervision problem that prior unified frameworks have not confronted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first framework to support all seven any-to-any tasks across Motion, Text, and RGB in a single 1.5B model, UniMotion establishes a reference architecture for motion-aware MLLMs and demonstrates that continuous representations with proper alignment can outperform both discrete-token and specialist approaches. The LRA self-supervised warm-up technique is a practical, transferable strategy for bootstrapping any newly introduced modality pathway in a unified LLM, and the DPA posterior-distillation approach offers a general recipe for injecting cross-modal supervision without requiring the auxiliary modality at inference. Key limitations include single-person scope, a remaining gap to specialist methods on Vision-to-Motion, and reliance on paired image data for DPA; open questions include whether the per-span hybrid attention extends naturally to multi-person interaction and whether the continuous-motion paradigm scales to longer sequences or more complex kinematic constraints.
