## Problem

VLA policies fine-tuned with RL and frozen VLM reward evaluators (e.g., CLIP image-text similarity) can produce observations that score highly on the evaluator without physically completing the task—a failure mode the paper formalizes as *stage hallucination*. This arises from two gaps: (1) a reward signal gap where standard positive/negative text contrast leaves wide margins for visual shortcuts (e.g., hovering above an object scores as 'grasped'), and (2) memory fragility where compressing long-horizon task history blurs stage-specific details and amplifies credit mis-assignment. No prior work systematically diagnoses or mitigates this failure mode in long-horizon VLA reinforcement fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes multi-view RGB images (third-person and wrist cameras) processed through SigLIP and DINOv2 ViT towers, a text task instruction, and a 6D relative gripper-to-object pose (3D translation + 3D axis-angle rotation) extracted from end-effector and object transforms. It outputs continuous action chunks via OpenVLA-OFT's parallel action decoding with L1 regression. A separate frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder scores image-text triplets (positive, negative, and hard-negative stage predicates) to produce dense reward signals, while a lightweight MLP world model (2×256 units) operates on the 6D pose space for intrinsic exploration rewards.

## Method

EvoVLA augments a fixed OpenVLA-OFT backbone with three synergistic modules trained jointly via PPO (dual critics, GAE, ρ=0.6 intrinsic weight). Stage-Aligned Reward (SAR) introduces counterfactual hard negatives—textual descriptions of near-miss states generated by a VLM prompting workflow—into CLIP contrastive scoring, so the max{s⁻, sʰ} term penalizes visually similar but physically incomplete observations; temporal smoothing via a running average stabilizes the resulting stage reward. Pose-Based Object Exploration (POE) replaces pixel-level curiosity with a 6D relative-pose representation learned by paired forward/inverse MLP world models, producing a stop-gradient prediction-error bonus that drives exploration toward geometrically novel gripper-object configurations immune to lighting and camera-motion distractors. Long-Horizon Memory maintains a compact store of backbone latents, retrieves Top-K items via attention with sinusoidal temporal encoding, fuses them with the current latent through a learned sigmoid gate, and uses that gate to modulate the intrinsic progress reward, suppressing spurious signals during unstable manipulation. The key design principle is that no single module suffices: SAR corrects the reward landscape, POE grounds exploration geometrically, and Memory stabilizes credit assignment across extended episodes, and removing any one forfeits ≥2.4 success points.

## Results

On the Discoverse-L benchmark (3 multi-stage tasks, 18–74 stages), EvoVLA achieves 69.2% average success rate, improving +10.2 points over the strongest baseline OpenVLA-OFT (59.0%). It also reaches 50% success in ~6×10⁵ environment steps versus ~9×10⁵ for OpenVLA-OFT (1.5× sample efficiency) and reduces the Hallucination Rate from 38.5% to 14.8%. On real-robot deployment across four tasks (three Sim2Real + one on-robot Insert), EvoVLA attains 54.6% average success (+11.0 over OpenVLA-OFT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoVLA outperforms all five baselines on every task and the overall average. Against the weakest baseline Octo (29.2%), EvoVLA gains +40.0 points; against OpenVLA (37.4%), +31.8; against π₀ (45.9%), +23.3; and against π₀-FAST (52.4%), +16.8. The most informative comparison is against OpenVLA-OFT (59.0%), the backbone EvoVLA is built upon: EvoVLA adds +10.2 points on average with the largest per-task gain on the longest-horizon Bridge task (+11.2), confirming that the improvements come from the SAR/POE/Memory modules rather than a stronger backbone. On real-robot evaluation, EvoVLA similarly beats OpenVLA-OFT by +9.2 to +13.4 points across four tasks, with the largest margin on the unseen Insert task (55.2% vs. 41.8%), demonstrating that the hallucination-suppression mechanism transfers beyond simulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvoVLA builds directly on the OpenVLA-OFT backbone and the common practice of using frozen CLIP image-text similarity as a VLM-based reward evaluator for RL fine-tuning of VLA policies (as in RoboCLIP and related VLM-reward work). It departs from prior curiosity-driven exploration (ICM, RND, Plan2Explore) by replacing pixel-level novelty with pose-grounded intrinsic rewards, and from memory-augmented VLA systems (MemoryVLA, JARVIS-1) by tying selective context retrieval explicitly to reward-level gating rather than purely to action conditioning. The key conceptual departure is the formalization of 'stage hallucination'—the exploitation of coarse appearance-based rewards by near-miss observations—and the use of counterfactual hard negatives in CLIP scoring to close this gap, which prior VLM-reward methods did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stage hallucination formalization and the Hallucination Rate metric give the community a concrete diagnostic for a failure mode that likely plagues any VLA policy trained with VLM-based rewards on multi-stage tasks, making it easier to detect and report reward exploitation. The Discoverse-L benchmark with ground-truth stage annotations (18–74 stages) is a useful resource for studying long-horizon credit assignment. Limitations include evaluation on only three tasks and a single robot platform (AIRBOT-Play), a 7B backbone, and a parallel-jaw gripper; whether the pose-grounded curiosity and hard-negative reward generalize to dexterous hands, deformable objects, or substantially longer horizons (>100 stages) remains open. The reliance on Gemini 2.5 Pro (or similar VLMs) for stage-dictionary generation also raises questions about robustness when the prompting workflow is applied to genuinely novel task families.
