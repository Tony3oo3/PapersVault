## Problem

Flow matching trains with a fixed Euclidean (MSE) criterion that is unaware of the data manifold, causing finite-capacity models to generalize incorrectly and produce out-of-distribution samples. While adversarial flow models (AFMs) address this in discrete time, it remains unclear how to incorporate adversarial training into continuous-time flow modeling, where the timestep interval can be made arbitrarily small.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261831/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian noise z~N(0,I) as input, optionally conditioned on a class label (ImageNet) or text prompt (text-to-image), and produces a generated image by integrating the learned velocity field ODE from t=1 to t=0. The discriminator operates in the derivative (velocity) space rather than the position space, consuming the Jacobian-Vector Product of the discriminator network along the predicted velocity direction. In practice, CAFMs are applied as a 10-epoch post-training procedure on existing flow-matching checkpoints (SiT-XL/2 at 256px, JiT-H/16 at 256px, ZImage at 512px), requiring no architectural changes to the generator.

## Method

CAFMs replace the fixed MSE criterion of flow matching with a learned discriminator that operates in the derivative space via Jacobian-Vector Products (JVP). The discriminator D(x_t, t) learns a scalar potential, and discrimination is performed by evaluating the directional derivative D_jvp(x_t, t, v_t, T) along the velocity direction, explicitly reflecting the physical property of velocity as a derivative of position. The generator G(x_t, t) retains the same form as standard flow matching (predicting the marginal velocity field), while the adversarial objective uses a bounded contrastive function f(a,b)=(a-1)²+(b+1)² and a centering penalty to prevent logit drift. A key practical finding is that continuous-time formulation eliminates the gradient-vanishing problem of discrete AFMs, removing the need for gradient penalties. For post-training, the optimal transport regularization weight is set to zero (λ_ot=0) to fully eliminate the Euclidean norm bias inherited from the pre-trained FM model, and the discriminator is updated N=16 times per generator step to drive it toward optimality.

## Results

On ImageNet 256px class-conditional generation, CAFM post-training (10 epochs) reduces the guidance-free FID of latent-space SiT-XL/2 from 8.26 to 3.63 and pixel-space JiT-H/16 from 7.17 to 3.57. In the guided setting, the best FID improves from 2.06 to 1.53 (SiT, CFG 1.3) and from 1.86 to 1.80 (JiT, CFG 1.8). On text-to-image generation (ZImage, 512px), CAFM post-training raises the GenEval overall score from 0.81 to 0.85 and the DPG-Bench overall score from 83.7 to 85.2, with the largest gains in guidance-free generation (GenEval 0.60→0.85, DPG 72.25→77.21).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the SD-VAE latent-space setting (Table 3), CAFM achieves the best guidance-free FID (3.63) among all compared 675M-parameter DiT-based models, outperforming SiT+REPA (5.90), DDT-XL (6.27), SiT+Disperse (7.43), and the original SiT (8.26) by 2.27–5.99 FID points. In the guided setting, CAFM (1.53) beats SiT (2.06), Disperse (1.97), and DiT (2.27), but is surpassed by REPA (1.42) and DDT-XL (1.26), which use additional representation-alignment or decoupled-architecture tricks. In pixel space (Table 4), CAFM post-trained JiT-H/16 achieves the best guidance-free FID (3.57) among models under 2B parameters, beating JiT (7.17) and ADM (10.94), though SiD (2B, 2.77) still leads. In guided pixel-space generation, CAFM (1.80) is competitive with JiT-G/16 (2B, 1.82) but trails SiD2 (653M, 1.38). For text-to-image, CAFM consistently outperforms the FM baseline on both GenEval and DPG-Bench across all sub-tasks, with the largest relative gains in guidance-free settings (e.g., GenEval counting 0.42→0.81, DPG overall 72.25→77.21).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Adversarial Flow Models (AFMs, Lin et al. 2026) from discrete-time to continuous-time flow modeling, while retaining the same generator architecture as standard flow matching (Lipman et al. 2023). The key conceptual departure is replacing the fixed Euclidean MSE criterion with a learned discriminator that operates in the velocity (derivative) space via Jacobian-Vector Products, rather than in position space as in AFMs. This positions CAFMs alongside other manifold-aware alternatives—Riemannian flow matching, perceptual losses, and representation autoencoders—but differs by jointly training the criterion network with the generator, avoiding the generator-hacking problem of fixed perceptual metrics. The paper frames its contribution primarily as a post-training recipe for existing flow-matching models rather than a new training paradigm from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight—that the loss criterion, not the architecture or the target flow, determines how a finite-capacity model generalizes—provides a clean and broadly applicable lever for improving sample fidelity without changing the underlying probability flow. The practical recipe (10 epochs of adversarial post-training on a pre-trained FM model) is simple to adopt and yields large FID reductions, making it likely to be integrated into existing flow-model pipelines. Open questions include whether the gains hold in low-density regions of the data distribution, whether the approach extends to video or other modalities, and how the adversarial objective relates to different f-divergences minimized by GANs. The paper acknowledges that guidance remains a complementary (orthogonal) technique and that no formal guarantee of true distribution matching is provided.
