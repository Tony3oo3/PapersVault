## Problem

Cross-scene hyperspectral image classification suffers from context shift: boundary mixing and class co-occurrence patterns change across scenes, breaking local decision rules learned on the source domain. Existing domain generalization methods align either features or predictions in a one-sided manner, producing partial adaptation where representations and decisions mismatch under context shift. The paper identifies that local neighborhood context is the missing conditioning variable needed to enforce stable, context-faithful generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes P×P×B hyperspectral patches (13×13×48 for Houston, 9×9×102 for Pavia, 9×9×270 for WHU-Hi) and outputs a single per-pixel class label for the center pixel. During training, neighborhood ground-truth labels within the patch are used to compute a co-occurrence histogram q* and a homogeneity score h, which serve as context variables for supervision and alignment; at test time, no context is computed and inference follows the standard center-pixel argmax protocol with no target-domain data or post-processing.

## Method

CAJA couples two context-conditioned mechanisms under a shared context C=(q*, h). Context-Aware Supervision (CAS) replaces softmax-CE with per-class sigmoid-BCE, applies homogeneity-adaptive label smoothing (s = α(1−h)+β) to temper over-confidence at boundaries, and enforces center-context consistency via a symmetric Bernoulli KL between the view-averaged prediction and the neighborhood histogram q*, weighted by w_ctx = γ(1−h) to focus on mixed regions. Context-Conditioned Joint Alignment (CCJA) discretizes h into bins and quantizes q* into codes to form context buckets, then within each bucket aligns both second-order feature statistics (CORAL) and bucket-mean prediction distributions (symmetric Bernoulli KL) between the real source and an FSSG-generated pseudo-source view, with risk weighting ω(b) that up-weights low-homogeneity buckets. A lightweight spectral-spatial encoder (involution + 1×1 conv spectral branch, 3×3 conv spatial branch, fused by a learnable softmax gate) provides features for alignment, and the entire framework operates under the single-source DG setting with no target data and no change to the pixel-wise inference protocol.

## Results

On three cross-scene HSI benchmarks, CAJA achieves the highest overall accuracy (OA) and Cohen's Kappa (KC) among all compared methods. Specifically, CAJA reaches 85.05% OA / 73.94 KC on Houston18, 89.75% OA / 87.57 KC on Pavia Center, and 87.77% OA / 80.84 KC on WHU-Hi HanChuan, with mean OA/KC/mIoU of 87.52% / 80.78 / 72.84 across datasets. Per-class accuracy points cluster closer to the overall bar for CAJA, indicating gains extend beyond majority classes, and error bars are notably smaller, reflecting lower variance across 10 runs. Boundary pixels (≈9.31% of Houston samples) show a 20.28% relative error reduction for CAJA versus interior pixels, confirming the context-aware design targets the most fragile regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAJA is compared against five strong SSDG baselines: SDENet [40], S²AMSNet [2], FDGNet [29], FSENet [38], and FSSG [32]. CAJA consistently outperforms all five on both OA and KC across Houston18, Pavia Center, and WHU-Hi HanChuan, with the largest margins over FSENet and FSSG, which rely on one-sided (feature-only or prediction-only) alignment. The attribution ablation (Table 1) shows that FSSG augmentation alone adds only +1.13% OA over plain BCE, whereas CAJA combined with FSSG yields +5.07% over FSSG alone, confirming the gain comes from context-aware supervision and joint alignment rather than augmentation. Qualitative comparisons (Fig. 4) show CAJA produces fewer boundary artifacts, sharper edges, and more coherent region predictions than FSSG and FSENet, particularly on thin structures (Houston18), large paved areas (Pavia Center), and small roof patches (HanChuan). The concurrent improvement in KC alongside OA (rather than OA-only gains) indicates CAJA avoids majority-class-biased adaptation that some baselines exhibit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-source domain generalization (SSDG) line for cross-scene HSI classification, building directly on FSSG for pseudo-source generation and on CORAL/Bernoulli-KL alignment primitives. It departs from prior one-sided DG methods (feature-only via MMD/CORAL or prediction-only via divergence) by formulating a context-conditioned joint alignment objective that simultaneously constrains representation statistics and output distributions within shared neighborhood-context buckets. It also diverges from patch-based HSI classifiers that assume single-class patches or apply post-hoc smoothing, instead converting neighborhood label histograms and homogeneity into continuous training-time supervisory signals while preserving the standard pixel-wise single-label inference protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete failure mode—partial-alignment pathology at class boundaries—identified through quantitative analysis (boundary pixels show 20.28% higher cross-scene error), and provides a deployment-friendly fix requiring no target data and no inference-time overhead. The attribution ablation showing that context-aware supervision (CAS) contributes more than source-side augmentation (FSSG) is a useful practical signal for HSI practitioners. Open questions include whether the context-bucket discretization generalizes to scenes with very different class compositions, whether the approach extends to multi-source or semi-supervised settings (acknowledged as future work), and whether the lightweight involution-based encoder is the right backbone or whether the method transfers to transformer-based HSI classifiers.
