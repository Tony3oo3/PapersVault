## Problem

Existing conditional image generation with diffusion models falls into two camps: task-specific architectures that improve performance but generalize poorly, and training-free loss guidance that compresses rich high-dimensional conditions into a single scalar objective. This scalarization creates an information bottleneck that loses spatial and fine-grained detail, while stepwise gradient updates accumulate errors along the reverse diffusion trajectory, degrading final sample quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262694/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For style transfer, the method takes a text prompt and a style reference image and produces a 512×512 stylized image; for super-resolution and deblurring, it takes a degraded observation (4× downsampled + Gaussian noise, or Gaussian-blurred with kernel 61/σ=3.0 + noise) and produces a 256×256 restored image. All tasks are unified under a posterior-sampling formulation where the condition is treated as a high-dimensional data signal rather than a scalar loss, and the method operates entirely in latent space using a pretrained U-Net (SD v1.4 or LDM) without any fine-tuning.

## Method

DICT is a training-free inference framework with two complementary mechanisms. First, Data Injection encodes the condition into latent space, adds stochastic noise at multiple levels, and blends the noise-perturbed condition latent with the current denoising state via weighted summation during early reverse-diffusion steps; the noise prediction on the injected condition acts as a natural filter that adaptively distills task-salient signals while suppressing irrelevant content. Second, Contrastive Trajectory Refinement imposes a margin-based contrastive objective between adjacent denoising states, enforcing that each step's predicted clean latent is closer to the condition than the previous step's, thereby acting as a discrete Lyapunov stabilizer that prevents monotonic error accumulation. The framework is backbone-agnostic (validated on both U-Net and DiT/PixArt) and requires no task-specific architectural modifications, using only lightweight task-dependent preprocessing of the condition and a small set of weighting hyperparameters.

## Results

On text-driven style transfer (40K images, 512×512), DICT achieves the lowest Style Loss (0.6313) and CLIP Loss (4.2334) among all 11 baselines, with a competitive Text Score of 0.2952 (second only to TFG's 0.3092). On 4× super-resolution (1K FFHQ images, 256×256), DICT attains the highest PSNR (28.86 dB) and lowest LPIPS (0.1573), with SSIM of 0.8233 (slightly below FPS-SMC's 0.8283). On deblurring (Gaussian kernel σ=3.0), DICT achieves the best SSIM (0.7736) and LPIPS (0.2236), with PSNR of 27.58 dB marginally below DCDP (27.91 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On style transfer, DICT decisively outperforms all task-specific methods (StyleShot, StyleStudio, StyleCrafter, DEADiff, InstantStyle, StyleAlign, CSGO, StyleDrop) on both Style Loss and CLIP Loss, with the largest gap over DEADiff (Style Loss 0.63 vs. 1.77) and StyleStudio (CLIP Loss 4.23 vs. 7.79). Among loss-guided baselines, DICT substantially beats FreeDoM (Style Loss 0.63 vs. 2.75; CLIP Loss 4.23 vs. 11.11) and TFG (Style Loss 0.63 vs. 2.11), though TFG edges out DICT on Text Score (0.3092 vs. 0.2952). On super-resolution, DICT leads all baselines in PSNR by a clear margin (28.86 vs. 27.63 for FPS-SMC, the runner-up) and in LPIPS (0.1573 vs. 0.1717 for DOC), while FPS-SMC retains a narrow SSIM advantage (0.8283 vs. 0.8233). On deblurring, DICT wins on SSIM (0.7736 vs. 0.7665 for FPS-SMC) and LPIPS (0.2236 vs. 0.2325 for DCDP), but trails DCDP on PSNR by 0.33 dB (27.58 vs. 27.91). Across all three tasks, DICT consistently outperforms the loss-guided baselines (TFG, FreeDoM) by large margins, and matches or exceeds the strongest task-specific methods on perceptual quality metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DICT builds directly on the latent diffusion model framework (Rombach et al.) and PLMS multi-step sampling (Liu et al.), positioning itself as a training-free inference method that departs from two dominant paradigms: task-specific architectures (StyleShot, PSLD, SITCOM) and scalar loss-gradient guidance (TFG, FreeDoM). Its key conceptual departure is replacing the scalar loss bottleneck with direct noise-perturbed condition injection into early denoising steps, drawing inspiration from SDEdit's latent initialization but extending it to multi-step, adaptive, guidance-conditioned injection. The Contrastive Trajectory Refinement mechanism is novel, framing step-to-step improvement as a discrete Lyapunov constraint rather than a per-step loss minimization, which has no direct precedent in the diffusion sampling literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, unified training-free recipe that works across three structurally different conditional generation tasks (style transfer, SR, deblurring) without task-specific architectures, which is valuable for practitioners who want a single inference pipeline. However, the method still relies on a task-dependent preprocessing operator M(·) and per-task hyperparameter schedules, limiting its claim to full task-agnosticism. Open questions include whether the contrastive trajectory objective generalizes to video diffusion or multi-condition settings, and whether the noise-injection strategy remains effective when the condition is highly degraded or semantically distant from the target. The method's dependence on careful tuning of α_data, γ_data, and η schedules across tasks suggests the unified framework is more of a design template than a truly plug-and-play solution.
