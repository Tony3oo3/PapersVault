## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models are bottlenecked by computationally heavy vision encoders (e.g., SigLIP-400M) that account for up to 50% of end-to-end inference time on mobile devices, yet prior efficiency efforts focus almost exclusively on token compression or smaller LLMs while treating the encoder as a fixed, monolithic component. No prior work designs a vision encoder for LVLMs with explicit on-device latency optimization, and existing efficient ViTs are tuned for ImageNet classification rather than the dense semantic reasoning required by multimodal generation.

## Input / Output

UltraViT consumes a high-resolution RGB image (benchmarked at 512×512 and 1024×1024 px) and, within the full UltraVLM pipeline, a text query. The encoder outputs a compact sequence of visual tokens (F ∈ R^{N×C}) that are projected via a lightweight MLP and fed to a 1.5B Qwen2 LLM for autoregressive text generation. The pyramidal topology natively reduces the token count by 16× relative to a flat SigLIP encoder at the same input resolution, and the entire encoder is deployed as an INT8-quantized model on a Qualcomm NPU (Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra), achieving ~149 inferences/sec at 512×512.

## Method

UltraViT is a four-stage pyramidal encoder that strategically assigns a different spatial mixer to each stage—depthwise/pointwise convolutions at high resolution, an interleaved conv+sparse-attention mix, channel-partitioned single-head attention, and full single-head attention at the lowest resolution—selected via systematic on-device latency measurements rather than FLOPs estimates. The sparse mixer is further adapted for NPU execution by fusing the sampling operator into strided convolutions, replacing transposed convolutions with nearest-neighbor upsampling, and compressing value projections by 4×. Pre-training uses a novel two-stage strategy: first, dense feature distillation from a high-resolution teacher (aligned via pixel-shuffle reshaping and a dual patch-level + global cosine-similarity loss), then generative pre-training against a frozen, vision-aligned Qwen2 LLM whose capacity is dynamically switched between 0.5B and 1.5B to balance alignment quality and training cost. This co-design of latency-informed heterogeneous architecture and generative pre-training yields state-of-the-art LVLM accuracy while running ~1.7× faster than FastVLM on-device.

## Results

UltraVLM (UltraViT + LLaVA-OV 1.5B LLM) achieves 60.5 GQA, 82.9 SQA, 68.9 TextVQA, 71.8 DocVQA, 72.2 ChartQA, and 49.8 MMSTAR, outperforming the re-trained FastVLM on 8 of 11 benchmarks with the largest gains on TextVQA (+6.0), ChartQA (+6.2), and DocVQA (+5.1). The UltraViT encoder runs at 148.9 inferences/sec on a Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra (INT8, Qualcomm NPU), which is 19.1× faster than SigLIP-400M (7.6 inf/s) and 1.7× faster than FastViT-HD (91.4 inf/s). The pyramidal design natively produces 16× fewer visual tokens than LLaVA-OV without any external token-pruning module, while pre-training requires only 235M unique images versus 2B for FastVLM's contrastive stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary encoder-centric baseline FastVLM (re-trained under the identical 7.1M-sample LLaVA-OV recipe), UltraVLM wins on 8 of 11 benchmarks, with the largest margins on TextVQA (+6.0), ChartQA (+6.2), DocVQA (+5.1), and MMSTAR (+5.7); it trails only on MMMU (38.1 vs 41.1, −3.0) and by negligible amounts on POPE (−0.2) and RealWorldQA (−0.6). Compared to LLaVA-OV with SigLIP-400M, UltraVLM improves GQA by +1.9, SQA by +7.3, and ChartQA by +8.0 while running 19.1× faster on-device and emitting 16× fewer tokens. Against token-restricted QwenVL-2.5 and QwenVL-3 (256 tokens), UltraVLM surpasses them on GQA, SQA, TextVQA, DocVQA, InfoVQA, MME, and MMSTAR, despite using a smaller LLM backbone and no proprietary training data. Versus post-hoc token-pruning methods (VisionZip, PyramidDrop, VisPruner, HiRED), UltraVLM outperforms all of them on every benchmark while being the only approach that also accelerates the vision encoder itself (19.1× speedup vs 1.0× for all pruning methods). In pre-training efficiency, UltraViT reaches state-of-the-art results after seeing only 235M unique images (4.1B total samples), roughly 3× fewer samples and 10× fewer unique images than FastVLM's 2B-image contrastive stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

UltraViT builds on the MetaFormer block topology and borrows specific spatial mixer primitives from EdgeViTs (sparse attention) and SH-ViT (channel-partitioned attention, downsampling blocks), but departs fundamentally from their homogeneous design philosophy by systematically assigning different mixers to different pyramidal stages based on real on-device latency measurements rather than theoretical FLOPs. In the LVLM efficiency landscape, it positions itself against the dominant strategies of token compression and smaller LLMs by targeting the vision encoder itself, with FastVLM as the closest prior effort that also designs a custom encoder for LVLMs. The two-stage pre-training (dense distillation followed by generative supervision from a frozen, capacity-mixed LLM) explicitly rejects the standard contrastive (CLIP/SigLIP) paradigm, arguing it is misaligned with the generative token prediction that LVLMs require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes a practical blueprint for edge-deployable LVLMs by showing that the vision encoder—long treated as a frozen, monolithic bottleneck—can be redesigned from the ground up to cut inference latency by ~19× over standard 400M-parameter encoders while simultaneously reducing token count by 16× through its pyramidal structure. The sample-efficiency of the pre-training pipeline (3× fewer total samples than FastVLM's contrastive regime) lowers the barrier to training competitive vision encoders. Open questions include whether the heterogeneous mixer selection generalizes to video inputs or substantially different NPU hardware, and whether the generative pre-training stage can be further compressed or replaced as LLM backbones continue to shrink for edge use.
