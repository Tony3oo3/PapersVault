## Problem

SAM2-based visual object trackers select the highest-confidence decoder mask for memory updates without verifying short-horizon temporal consistency, causing drift under occlusion, rapid motion, and distractors. Existing extensions (motion filtering, distractor checks) still rely on confidence-driven selection and lack a systematic, trajectory-level mechanism to validate which candidate mask should become future evidence in the autoregressive memory stream.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video frames and a single initial mask prompt on the first frame; all subsequent frames rely solely on memory-guided decoding. It produces a per-frame segmentation mask (and derived bounding box) that is written to the host tracker's memory bank under the baseline's default FIFO update schedule. SENTRY operates entirely outside the backbone and memory module, so it integrates as a drop-in module on any SAM2-based tracker without architectural modification, adding roughly 0.4–0.6 GB VRAM and ~25% latency overhead at the L scale.

## Method

SENTRY is a training-free, plug-and-play refine-before-write module that replaces confidence-driven mask selection with consistency-validated memory admission. It first aggregates diverse per-frame candidates from decoder hypotheses (filtered by relative IoU and occlusion score), Automatic Mask Generation proposals (filtered by cosine similarity to a first-frame template), Soft-NMS deduplication, and a constant-acceleration Kalman motion prior. Each candidate is then backtracked over a fixed τ=10-frame window using SAM2's promptable segmentation to form a candidate tracklet pool, while a neighbor tracklet pool of previously rejected masks provides negative distractor context. Cycle-consistent bipartite matching (Hungarian algorithm) is applied over IoU-based trajectory similarity between candidate tracklets and the target/neighbor tracklets, so candidates whose backward trajectories align with distractor motion are implicitly suppressed. The mask with the highest target-trajectory similarity is admitted to memory, with a Kalman-only fallback when all candidates fall below a minimum reliability threshold.

## Results

SENTRY-D4S (SENTRY applied to DAM4SAM-L) achieves new zero-shot SOTA on six of nine benchmarks: LaSOT (S=76.3, NP=84.7, P=82.4), LaSOT_ext (S=61.8, NP=76.6, P=73.8), GOT-10k (AO=82.1, SR₀.₅=92.6, SR₀.₇₅=78.2), VOT20 (Q=0.732, Acc=0.804, Rob=0.970), VOT22 (Q=0.759, Acc=0.805, Rob=0.979), and DiDi (Q=0.705, Acc=0.737, Rob=0.956). The largest single-benchmark gain is SENTRY-SR on TNL2K, improving SAMURAI by +17.8% in Success score (from 50.6 to 68.4), attributed to SENTRY's temporal consistency check replacing SAMURAI's linear Kalman motion model. Across all five host trackers and four model scales (T/S/B/L), SENTRY yields consistent gains averaging 0.7% (SAM2), 4.5% (SAMURAI), and 1.5% (DAM4SAM) in Success score, while adding only ~25% runtime overhead (32.8 FPS vs. 44.0 FPS for SAM2-L on A100) and 0.4–0.6 GB VRAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SENTRY-D4S outperforms its base tracker DAM4SAM-L on all nine benchmarks, setting new zero-shot SOTA on LaSOT (S: 76.3 vs. 75.1), LaSOT_ext (S: 61.8 vs. 60.9), GOT-10k (AO: 82.1 vs. 81.1), VOT20 (Q: 0.732 vs. 0.723), VOT22 (Q: 0.759 vs. 0.750), and DiDi (Q: 0.705 vs. 0.694). SENTRY-SR delivers the most dramatic improvement over SAMURAI on TNL2K (+17.8% S, from 50.6 to 68.4), where SAMURAI's linear Kalman motion model fails under non-linear trajectories; on VOT22, SENTRY-SR gains +2.1% Q and +3.1% Acc over SAMURAI. SENTRY-S2 provides more modest but consistent gains over SAM2 (e.g., +1.7% S on LaSOT, +0.9% Q on VOT20). On VOTS24, SENTRY-D4S (Q=0.715, Acc=0.794) trails the supervised S3-Track (Q=0.722, Acc=0.784) by 0.7% in Q but matches or exceeds it in Acc, while outperforming all other zero-shot methods. Against supervised trackers on LaSOT, SENTRY-D4S (S=76.3) surpasses SPMTTrack-B (74.9) and DUTrack344 (74.1), and on GOT-10k its AO of 82.1 exceeds all listed supervised baselines (top: 77.8 for DUTrack344), demonstrating that the training-free refinement closes or inverts the zero-shot-to-supervised gap on several benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SENTRY builds directly on SAM2's streaming memory architecture and the bipartite-matching-with-neighbor-tracklets idea from NeighborTrack [14], but repositions both at a different interface: rather than improving current-frame prediction or adding motion/distractor priors (as SAMURAI and DAM4SAM do), it intervenes at the memory-write decision, validating each candidate mask for short-horizon temporal consistency before it becomes future tracking state. The paper explicitly contrasts this 'refine-before-write' admission rule with prior SAM2 extensions that treat temporal consistency as a scoring or trajectory-selection problem, and with memory-based VOS trackers (XMem, RMem, FEELVOS) that update memory via frame-level confidence or fixed schedules without explicit backward re-tracking verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first unified all-scale re-evaluation of five open-source SAM2-based trackers across nine benchmarks fills a significant reproducibility gap, and the finding that confidence-only mask selection is the dominant drift source (rather than backbone capacity or motion modeling) redirects attention to the memory-write interface as a high-leverage intervention point. The training-free, plug-and-play design means the technique can be adopted without retraining, but the ~25% FPS overhead and persistent failure after extreme occlusion (~100 frames) limit its applicability in latency-critical or very long-occlusion settings. Open questions include whether the fixed τ=10 window and global thresholds can be made adaptive per scene, and whether the neighbor-tracklet mechanism generalizes to multi-object or open-vocabulary tracking beyond the single-object SAM2 pipeline.
