## Problem

Existing video diffusion models cannot provide fine-grained, physically accurate control over dynamic hair rendering. Two fundamental barriers exist: (1) the large, task-dependent set of physics parameters (mass, stiffness, damping, wind, gravity, human motion) has highly entangled visual effects, making it infeasible for a single diffusion model to learn their individual contributions, and (2) training such a model would require ground-truth physics annotations that are nearly impossible to infer from real videos.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference portrait image, a set of user-specified physics parameters (intrinsic hair properties like mass/stiffness/damping and external forces like wind direction/strength and human motion), and a camera trajectory as input. The 3D hair strand model is first estimated from the reference image via DiffLocks, then simulated in Blender's particle system. The output is an 81×832×480 RGB video where hair dynamics follow the specified physics and the camera follows the given trajectory. Control signals are 3-channel per-frame images combining strand maps (in hair regions) and sparse 2D DWPose keypoints (in non-hair regions).

## Method

CONTROLHAIR decouples physics reasoning from video generation through a three-stage cascade. Stage 1 uses a physics simulator (Blender particle system) to convert arbitrary physics parameters and a 3D hair model into per-frame hair geometry, acting as a universal physics encoder that unifies diverse parameter sets into a common geometric representation. Stage 2 projects this 3D geometry onto 2D along the camera trajectory and extracts strand maps (via HairStep U-Net) and human poses (via DWPose), merging them into a 3-channel control image per frame. Stage 3 feeds these control signals (encoded by a 3D convolutional network) and the reference image (encoded by Wan 2.1's VAE plus a 2D conv encoder for its geometric features) into a Wan 2.1 DiT backbone fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 128), initialized from UniAnimate-DiT weights. The key design insight is that the diffusion model only needs to learn photorealistic inpainting from geometry, while all physics reasoning is offloaded to the simulator, enabling free swapping of simulators and physics models without retraining the diffusion component.

## Results

On the control-signal-to-video reconstruction task (10 diverse videos), CONTROLHAIR achieves PSNR 17.15, SSIM 0.668, and LPIPS 0.370, outperforming Wan2.1 (PSNR 11.65, SSIM 0.518, LPIPS 0.499) and UniAnimate-DiT (PSNR 15.78, SSIM 0.637, LPIPS 0.390). This corresponds to improvements of up to 47% in PSNR, 29% in SSIM, and 26% in LPIPS over the weakest baseline. Qualitatively, only CONTROLHAIR successfully reproduces the specified wind direction and multi-step head rotation sequence, while both baselines fail to produce the correct hair dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONTROLHAIR is compared against two baselines: Wan2.1 (text-prompt-conditioned video diffusion) and UniAnimate-DiT (pose-conditioned video diffusion). CONTROLHAIR wins on all three metrics against both baselines. Against Wan2.1, the gains are substantial (PSNR +5.5 dB, SSIM +0.15, LPIPS −0.13), reflecting that text prompts cannot encode per-frame physics. Against UniAnimate-DiT, the improvements are more modest (PSNR +1.37, SSIM +0.031, LPIPS −0.020), since UniAnimate-DiT already receives per-frame human poses but lacks hair-specific control signals (strand maps). Qualitatively, under strong wind both baselines fail to move the hair at all, while under a multi-step head rotation Wan2.1 only turns right (never left) and UniAnimate-DiT controls head pose but not hair dynamics; only CONTROLHAIR follows the full specified sequence for both head and hair.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the controllable video diffusion line (ControlNet, UniAnimate-DiT, Wan 2.1) by inserting a 3D physics simulator as a front-end that converts diverse physics parameters into a unified per-frame geometry representation, which is then projected to 2D strand maps and poses as control signals. It departs from prior physics-informed generation work (e.g., PhysAnimator, which is limited to 2D mesh deformation) by operating on full 3D strand-level hair simulation and by making the diffusion model agnostic to the specific physics model through the geometry bottleneck. The strand-map control signal design draws on HairStep, and the overall architecture follows UniAnimate-DiT's encoder-DiT-decoder structure, fine-tuned via LoRA on a curated 10K video dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CONTROLHAIR is the first framework to enable user-specified, fine-grained physics control (stiffness, wind, damping, human motion) over hair dynamics in generated video, filling a gap that text- and pose-conditioned diffusion models cannot address. The decoupled simulator→geometry→diffusion pipeline is generalizable to other deformable objects and to swapping simulators without retraining, which is a practical architectural insight. Key limitations include the ~20-minute offline runtime, under-constrained single-image 3D hair reconstruction that can propagate errors, and dependence on simulator fidelity; open questions include real-time inference, multi-view 3D hair priors, and whether neural simulators or vision-language models can automate parameter selection.
