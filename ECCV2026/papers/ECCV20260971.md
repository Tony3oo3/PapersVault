## Problem

Multimodal LLMs suffer from 'visual laziness': they encode correct visual features internally but default to strong language priors during decoding, producing hallucinations that contradict the image. Existing preference-optimization methods (DPO, DA-DPO) optimize outcome-level text rewards, which inadvertently reinforce linguistic shortcuts because a correct answer reached via language priors is still rewarded. No prior alignment method explicitly constrains the model to derive its response from visual evidence rather than textual correlation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a text instruction, producing a free-form text response. During training, each sample is processed through a dual-path forward pass: a 'seeing' path with normal cross-modal attention and a 'counterfactual blind' path where text-query rows are masked from visual-token key/value columns in every transformer layer, yielding a functionally blind state without modifying input tensors. The training signal is the log-likelihood gap between these two states for the preferred response, and the model additionally develops emergent zero-shot spatial grounding (bounding-box localization) without any explicit coordinate supervision.

## Method

VIGIL is an offline post-training framework that augments standard DPO with a Counterfactual Visual Decoupling (CVD) loss derived from a Lagrangian relaxation of a mutual-information constraint. The CVD loss applies the Bradley-Terry preference model to the same preferred response under seeing vs. blind attention masks, penalizing 'blind confidence'—cases where the model remains highly certain even after visual evidence is removed. A dynamic gating factor α = 1 − tanh(|VIG gap|) adaptively scales the CVD term, reducing regularization pressure on samples that already exhibit strong visual grounding. The final objective L_VIGIL = L_DPO(y_w, y_hard) + λ·α·L_CVD combines semantic alignment (with hallucination-like hard negatives) and the geometric visual-anchoring constraint in a single unified loss, requiring no additional reference models or online sampling.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, VIGIL achieves 86.9 F1 on POPE-Adv (+4.1 over SFT), 46.5 on AMBER-Gen (+4.0), 49.5 on MathVista (+1.5), and 72.5 on MMBench (+1.3), yielding an average score of 67.3 (+2.7). At the 72B scale, gains amplify: VIGIL reaches 89.8 on POPE-Adv (+5.3), 52.2 on AMBER-Gen (+5.4), 56.6 on MathVista (+2.5), and 78.5 on MMBench (+1.3), for an average of 72.1 (+4.0). VIGIL also improves zero-shot RefCOCOg localization by 4.3 points over SFT without any bounding-box supervision, and matches DA-DPO's full-data performance using only 25% of the preference data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIGIL outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric at both 7B and 72B scales. Against standard DPO, VIGIL gains +4.1 (7B) and +5.3 (72B) points on POPE-Adv and +4.0/+5.4 on AMBER-Gen, confirming that outcome-level preference optimization alone cannot enforce visual grounding. Compared to SimPO (reference-free), VIGIL leads by +3.8/+5.0 on POPE-Adv, showing that optimization stability alone is insufficient without modality-specific constraints. Against the strongest baseline DA-DPO (difficulty-aware reweighting), VIGIL still wins by +2.7/+2.4 on POPE-Adv and +1.7/+2.4 on AMBER-Gen, indicating that physical attention masking provides a stronger supervision signal than numerical loss reweighting. On the 72B model, VIGIL also surpasses HA-DPO by +3.6 on POPE-Adv and +2.8 on MathVista, where HA-DPO actually degrades reasoning (−0.3) while VIGIL improves it (+2.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

VIGIL extends the DPO preference-optimization lineage (Rafailov et al., 2023) into the multimodal setting by adding a counterfactual visual decoupling constraint derived from an information-theoretic objective (Visual Information Gain as PMI). It departs from DA-DPO (Qiu et al., 2025), which addresses hallucination through implicit difficulty reweighting, by instead imposing an explicit physical intervention—zeroing text-to-visual attention weights—to create a counterfactual blind state. The hard-negative mining component is borrowed from DA-DPO, while the overall framing contrasts with inference-time methods like Visual Contrastive Decoding and with outcome-based RL (GRPO), which the authors argue cannot naturally encode seeing-vs-blind comparisons without prohibitive compute.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized but under-addressed failure mode (visual laziness) with a clean, implementable mechanism (attention masking) that requires no architectural changes, additional reference models, or inference-time overhead. The emergent spatial grounding on RefCOCOg without any bounding-box supervision is a notable secondary finding, suggesting that causal visual anchoring and spatial localization are coupled in the latent space. Key limitations include the simplicity of the zero-out masking (which may not capture object-level or semantic cross-modal conflicts in cluttered scenes) and the open question of whether the observed scaling advantage for larger models stems from knowledge capacity or latent geometry. The 25%-data efficiency claim is practically significant but would benefit from validation on additional preference datasets.
