## Problem

Flow Matching models require tens of sequential neural network evaluations to integrate their ODE trajectories, creating a major latency bottleneck for real-time generation. Existing parallel samplers (ParaDiGMS, ParaSolver) target diffusion models and impose a 2–3× NFE overhead, necessitating multi-GPU setups that are impractical for standard hardware. No prior method achieves parallel sampling for FM that actually reduces total computation below the sequential baseline.

## Input / Output

ParaFlow is a training-free, plug-and-play wrapper around any existing FM model (evaluated on SD3 and Flux). It takes the same inputs as the base model—an initial noise vector drawn from N(0,I) and a text prompt—and produces a generated RGB image at resolutions from 512×512 to 2048×2048. The user controls a parallel window size (default p=8) and a convergence tolerance δ that trade off speed against fidelity; with r=1 model evaluations per iteration, the total NFE can be lower than the sequential Euler solver's N.

## Method

ParaFlow recasts the autoregressive Euler update as a system of Triangular Nonlinear Equations (TNEs) over the full trajectory, then solves it via step-level parallel fixed-point iteration. The key innovation is a velocity approximation scheme: instead of evaluating the neural network at every timestep within the parallel window, it uses Newton's first-order divided difference on the two most recently computed velocities to extrapolate future velocities, reducing per-iteration model calls to just r (as low as 1). A sliding-window implementation with a tolerance-based stopping criterion advances the window by the number of contiguously converged states, keeping memory and compute bounded. Theoretically, the method converges to the exact autoregressive trajectory in at most N iterations, and the cumulative approximation error is bounded by O(K/N³), which is negligible for typical step counts (N ∈ {25, 50, 100}).

## Results

On Flux (1024×1024, N=100), ParaFlow with velocity approximation (r=1, single GPU) achieves a 4.3× wall-clock speedup (20.45s vs. 87.94s sequential Euler) while maintaining FID of 62.28 vs. 61.47 and CLIP of 31.92 vs. 32.31. On Stable Diffusion 3 (1024×1024, N=100), the same configuration yields a 3.5× speedup (5.20s vs. 18.22s) with FID 61.96 vs. 61.54. Critically, ParaFlow (r=1) requires only 22.93 NFEs on Flux versus 187.95 for the non-approximated 8-GPU parallel baseline, a nearly 8× reduction in total function evaluations while being faster in wall-clock time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParaFlow is compared against two baselines: (1) the standard sequential Euler solver on a single GPU, and (2) a non-approximated parallel variant (r=N, 8 GPUs) that represents the prior parallel sampling paradigm (ParaDiGMS/ParaSolver-style). ParaFlow (r=1, 1 GPU) consistently outperforms the 8-GPU non-approximated baseline in wall-clock time across all step counts on both Flux and SD3: at N=100 on Flux, 20.45s vs. 22.16s; at N=100 on SD3, 5.20s vs. 5.55s. The NFE advantage is dramatic: ParaFlow requires only 22.93 NFEs versus 187.95 for the 8-GPU baseline on Flux (N=100), and 28.34 vs. 211.78 on SD3 (N=100). In terms of quality, ParaFlow (r=1) shows a small but consistent FID increase of ~0.8–1.0 over sequential Euler (e.g., 62.28 vs. 61.47 on Flux at N=100), while the non-approximated 8-GPU baseline actually matches or slightly improves FID (61.45 vs. 61.47). The CLIP score drops slightly for ParaFlow (31.92 vs. 32.31 on Flux at N=100), suggesting a minor semantic alignment cost from the velocity approximation, though this is visually imperceptible per the paper's qualitative analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

ParaFlow extends the parallel sampling paradigm established for diffusion models (ParaDiGMS, ParaTAA, ParaSolver) to the Flow Matching setting, which has distinct vector-field dynamics and straight-line probability paths that prior diffusion-specific schedules do not capture. The key conceptual departure is the velocity approximation via Newton's first-order divided difference, which replaces expensive redundant model evaluations with lightweight polynomial extrapolation, fundamentally changing the computational profile of parallel ODE solving. Unlike ParaSolver's hierarchical initialization or ParaDiGMS's Picard iterations, ParaFlow's approximation exploits the temporal smoothness inherent in FM trajectories, enabling single-GPU execution with fewer total NFEs than the sequential baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ParaFlow offers a training-free, plug-and-play accelerator that makes high-performance parallel sampling practical on commodity single-GPU hardware, removing the multi-GPU and high-bandwidth interconnect requirements that have limited prior parallel samplers to research clusters. The theoretical guarantee of cubic error decay (O(K/N³)) and convergence to the exact autoregressive trajectory provides strong confidence that the speedup comes from hardware-aware parallelism rather than approximation artifacts. Open questions include whether the approach extends to higher-order solvers (Heun, DPM-Solver), video generation with longer trajectories, and whether the velocity smoothness assumption holds for all FM training objectives (e.g., non-rectified flows). The diminishing speedup at low step counts (1.4–1.6× at N=25) suggests the technique is most impactful in the high-fidelity, high-step regime where sequential cost is greatest.
