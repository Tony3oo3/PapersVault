## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image editing methods (DragonDiff, DiffEditor, GeoDiffuser) are designed for single-object manipulation and degrade significantly when multiple objects must be relocated simultaneously, especially in complex rearrangements like object shuffling. No training-free framework can handle synchronized multi-object navigation while maintaining visual coherence across inpainting, relocation, and background preservation subtasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261603/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, bounding boxes for N target objects (converted to binary masks via SAM), and a desired 2D target coordinate for each object, along with a text prompt describing the scene. It produces a single edited image where all objects are simultaneously relocated, vacated regions are inpainted, and the background is preserved. Experiments use SD v1.5 at 768×768 with 50-step DDIM denoising; object scale is assumed unchanged, and the framework is plug-and-play (also demonstrated on Flux).

## Method

SIMON is a training-free framework built on DDIM inversion and KV replacement that introduces three modules. (1) Content-aware Attention modifies self-attention with region-specific multiplicative scaling (γ) and additive biasing (δ) in three contexts: object relocation (query in target mask, key in original mask), inpainting (query in inpainting mask, key in reference mask), and background preservation (both in background mask), preventing attention diffusion across visually similar objects. (2) Multi-object Energy Guidance defines a composite energy E = E_ipt + E_relo + E_bg, where each term uses a similarity function g(a,b) between generated and cached features within designated masks, combined with spatially-aware weighting to enforce subtask-specific consistency in a single forward pass. (3) Latent Initialization overwrites the inpainting-region latents at z_T with fresh Gaussian noise and pins background latents to their DDIM-inversion values for the first k denoising steps, eliminating residual artifacts from the original image. All three modules are training-free and can be integrated into any pre-trained diffusion backbone without fine-tuning.

## Results

On the 3D-FUTURE multi-object navigation benchmark (1,000 images, 2–5 objects each), SIMON achieves the best background consistency (MSE 0.209×10⁻³, LPIPS 0.969×10⁻¹), navigation consistency (MSE 1.649×10⁻³, LPIPS 4.531×10⁻¹), and identity fidelity (DINOv2 0.869) among all methods. Its FID of 22.826 is competitive with the training-based MagicFixup (21.964) while being substantially better than all training-free baselines (~30). SIMON also achieves the best inpainting reconstruction fidelity (MSE 1.964×10⁻³, LPIPS 0.670) while maintaining strong residual suppression, and runs in only 30.3% of DiffEditor's inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIMON outperforms all three training-free baselines (DragonDiff, DiffEditor, GeoDiffuser) on every metric in Table 1. Against the strongest training-free method, DiffEditor, SIMON improves background MSE by 45.3% (0.209 vs 0.384), background LPIPS by 47.8% (0.969 vs 1.856), and FID by 24.4% (22.826 vs 30.193), while using only 30.3% of its runtime. Against the training-based MagicFixup (pre-trained on 5M images), SIMON wins on background consistency (MSE 0.209 vs 0.340; LPIPS 0.969 vs 1.422), navigation consistency (MSE 1.649 vs 1.337—actually MagicFixup is slightly better here), and DINOv2 identity fidelity (0.869 vs 0.772), while MagicFixup retains a marginal FID advantage (21.964 vs 22.826). Against the Insert+LaMA two-stage pipeline (trained on 159K pairs), SIMON dominates on background consistency, navigation consistency, and FID, with DINOv2 essentially tied (0.869 vs 0.867). GeoDiffuser is the weakest baseline overall, with the highest errors on both background and navigation consistency, suggesting its geometry-based attention constraints do not generalize well to multi-object rearrangement.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIMON builds directly on the energy-based conditioning framework of Dhariwal & Nichol (NeurIPS 2021) for steering diffusion sampling, the DDIM inversion procedure for mapping images into latent space, and the KV replacement strategy from DragDiffusion/DiffEditor for caching attention features. It departs from prior single-object drag methods (DragonDiff, DiffEditor, GeoDiffuser) by formulating a composite energy function with three subtask-specific terms and a content-aware attention mechanism that operates simultaneously across all target objects, rather than sequentially. The training-free design contrasts with MagicFixup, which achieves multi-instance editing but requires pre-training on 5 million images, and with SceneDiffusion, which handles complex layouts but only under single-object settings with substantial memory overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that simultaneous multi-object manipulation can be achieved training-free with a single forward pass, substantially reducing inference time compared to sequential editing pipelines while matching or exceeding the quality of methods trained on millions of images. The curated 3D-FUTURE benchmark (1,000 images, 3,405 objects with annotated target positions) provides a reusable evaluation resource for the community. Key open questions remain around illumination consistency and occlusion handling, which the authors acknowledge as limitations; they suggest physically-motivated lighting constraints in the energy term as a promising direction. The generalization to transformer-based backbones (Flux) is shown qualitatively but not yet validated with the same quantitative rigor as the UNet-based experiments.
