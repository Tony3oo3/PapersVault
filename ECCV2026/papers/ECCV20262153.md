## Problem

Vision-language models struggle with ultra-long interleaved image-text sequences due to the quadratic scaling of self-attention, and existing remedies—aggressive visual token pruning, architectural replacement with less expressive backbones, or data-centric approaches—each sacrifice either fidelity or the Transformer's precise reasoning capability while largely ignoring the textual modality. Compounding this, current long-context multimodal benchmarks rely on artificial retrieval tasks or padded filler documents, failing to evaluate genuine narrative-level comprehension.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a system prompt, a long interleaved context of alternating RGB images and text segments, and a text question; only the context is compressed while the prompt and question pass through unchanged. Images and text are each partitioned into fixed 100-token chunks, and each chunk is distilled into 4 layer-specific tokens per decoder layer via modality-specific Q-Former compressors. The compressed features are not appended as extra tokens but are injected via element-wise addition into dedicated placeholder positions within the decoder's hidden states at every layer, so the attention mechanism operates on a drastically shortened sequence while still accessing fine-grained global context.

## Method

VLZip introduces hierarchical context distillation with multi-layer injection within a pure Transformer (Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone). A shared visual Q-Former with L×M_v learnable queries compresses each 100-token image chunk into M_v tokens tailored for each of the L decoder layers in a single forward pass; a parallel textual Q-Former (preceded by a lightweight Qwen2.5-0.5B encoder and projection) performs the same for text chunks. At every decoder layer, the corresponding layer-specific compressed features are added element-wise to the hidden states of placeholder tokens before self-attention, providing continuous fine-grained global context without lengthening the sequence. Training follows a four-stage curriculum: (1) visual compressor pre-training on single-image instruction data, (2) textual compressor pre-training via a reconstruction objective on long-document corpora, (3) joint interleaved fine-tuning with both compressors and the LLM decoder unfrozen, and (4) long-context consolidation on ultra-long sequences. This unified compression of both modalities enables training up to 120K tokens (6× the baseline) and inference beyond 280K tokens on a single A100-80GB GPU, with a memory-scaling path to 2M tokens.

## Results

On the proposed LongVLBench, VLZip (3B) achieves an average score of 61.9, a 31.4% relative improvement over the next-best model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 47.1), with a 100% inference success rate across all length bins. At the extreme >128K token range, VLZip scores 7.60 versus only 0.16 for the uncompressed Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline. On MMLongBench ICL-128k, VLZip reaches 58.8, substantially outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-7B (44.0) and all other baselines. Inference is 23.6× faster than the base model at extreme lengths, and peak memory scales to 2M tokens under 50 GB on a single A100-80 GB GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLZip (3B) outperforms all eight baselines on LongVLBench average, with the largest margins over Mantis (61.9 vs. 6.30) and VisionZip (61.9 vs. 24.7), both of which suffer severe OOM or zero-score failures beyond 32K tokens. Against the strongest baselines, VLZip beats Qwen2.5-VL-7B (47.1) by 14.8 points and LongLLaVA-9B (45.0) by 16.9 points, despite being 2–3× smaller. At the critical >128K bin, VLZip scores 7.60 while the uncompressed Qwen2.5-VL-3B drops to 0.16 and Long-VITA-14B to 0.00, confirming that unified compression is essential for ultra-long narrative reasoning. On MMLongBench ICL-128k, VLZip (58.8) surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-7B (44.0) by 14.8 points and all other models by >40 points, validating the multi-layer injection design. On short-context benchmarks, VLZip retains >90% of baseline performance on most tasks but shows moderate drops on perception-heavy GQA (53.8 vs. 60.0) and AI2D (71.6 vs. 78.6), while actually exceeding the baseline on knowledge-heavy OKVQA (51.1 vs. 42.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

VLZip unifies two previously separate lines of work: soft-prompt compression from NLP (GIST tokens, In-Context Autoencoder) and visual token reduction in VLMs (GlimpsePrune, VisionZip, DeepStack). It departs from visual pruning methods by avoiding irreversible token discarding, and from architectural-overhaul approaches (State Space Models in LongLLaVA, LongMamba) by remaining within a pure Transformer. The Q-Former mechanism borrowed from BLIP-2 is repurposed as a hierarchical compressor that generates layer-specific soft prefixes for both modalities simultaneously, while the multi-layer injection strategy extends DeepStack's single-modality stacking to a unified visual-textual setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VLZip provides a practical path to ultra-long multimodal reasoning on standard hardware (120K training, 2M inference under 50GB), a capability that was previously infeasible without architectural compromises. The introduction of LongVLBench addresses a genuine evaluation gap by replacing artificial NIAH tasks and padded documents with chronologically coherent narrative sequences. Open questions include whether asymmetric chunk/token configurations for image vs. text (left as future work) could further improve performance, and whether the moderate short-context perception trade-off (GQA: 60.0→53.8, AI2D: 78.6→71.6) can be mitigated without sacrificing long-context gains.
