## Problem

Large 3D foundation models like MASt3R (688M parameters) achieve state-of-the-art stereo reconstruction but are computationally prohibitive for deployment on resource-constrained platforms such as lunar landers and rovers. The paper investigates how far such models can be compressed via knowledge distillation for a targeted operational domain, and which architectural and training choices most affect the accuracy-efficiency trade-off.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261277/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes uncalibrated RGB stereo image pairs of lunar terrain (varying baselines, altitudes, and illumination) and produces, per pixel, a 3D point in the first camera's coordinate frame, a confidence score, and a 24-dimensional local feature descriptor; camera pose and surface slopes are derived from these predictions. Notably, students are trained entirely from teacher pseudo-ground truth without requiring any camera pose annotations, making the pipeline applicable to domains where ground-truth labels are scarce or unavailable.

## Method

A fine-tuned MASt3R teacher (ViT-Large encoder, 12-block cross-attention decoder) generates dense pseudo-ground truth to supervise a family of lightweight students that retain the same dual-decoder prediction pipeline but swap in lighter encoders (MobileNetV3, DINOv2 ViT-Small, ViT-Tiny) and reduced decoders (512-dim/6-block or 256-dim/4-block). The key technical contribution is a structured SVD-based initialization that projects the teacher's decoder weights into the student's smaller latent space via truncated singular value decomposition, combined with a uniform stride layer mapping (e.g., selecting every other teacher block) to preserve hierarchical structure. Training combines output-level geometry loss (confidence-aware 3D regression + InfoNCE descriptor matching) with a margin-filtered cosine feature-alignment loss that projects student encoder features into the teacher's representation space via a learnable linear layer, focusing optimisation on the most misaligned spatial positions. All students share a single teacher forward pass per training iteration, reducing effective distillation cost.

## Results

On the StereoLunar test set (1200 stereo pairs), the best student S2 (ViT-Small encoder, 154.9M params, 4.4× smaller than the 688.6M teacher) achieves a Chamfer distance of 0.53% AbsRel (vs. 0.46% for the teacher), a median VCRE of 3.56% of image diagonal (vs. 1.15%), and a Slope MAE of 7.59° (vs. 7.22°). The most aggressively compressed student S3 (94.9M, 7.3× smaller) retains a Chamfer of 0.68% and Prec@10% of 92.7%, nearly matching S2's 92.1%. All students achieve approximately 2× inference speedup over the teacher, with S3 running in 3.47 s vs. 8.36 s at N=128 pairs on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the lunar-adapted MASt3R teacher (688.6M params) from [8], which serves as the upper-bound reference. S2 (154.9M) closes most of the gap, achieving only 15% higher Chamfer distance (0.53% vs. 0.46%) and 3.1× higher median VCRE (3.56% vs. 1.15% of image diagonal) while being 4.4× smaller and 2.1× faster. S3 (94.9M, 7.3× smaller) degrades further on Chamfer (0.68%) but surprisingly matches or slightly exceeds S2 on Prec@10% (92.7% vs. 92.1%), indicating that pose estimation is more robust to decoder compression than dense geometry. The CNN-based S1 (MobileNet) clearly underperforms all ViT-based students on every metric, with Chamfer more than double that of S2 (1.02% vs. 0.53%) and Prec@5% collapsing to 36.8% versus 82.8%. Additionally, the distillation pipeline (teacher pseudo-GT + SVD init + feature alignment) outperforms a fully supervised baseline trained directly on ground-truth annotations (Exp. E: 0.65% Chamfer), confirming that the teacher's dense predictions provide a richer supervisory signal than sparse labels alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends knowledge distillation from the NLP and 2D vision settings to dense geometric 3D foundation models, specifically compressing a lunar-adapted MASt3R teacher (itself following the CroCo cross-view completion paradigm) into lightweight students. It departs from prior 3D-model distillation efforts (DUNE, [26], [9]) that either fuse encoders without a full reconstruction pipeline or modify attention for streaming, by jointly compressing both encoder and dual cross-view decoder and introducing a structured SVD-based weight initialization to bridge the dimensional gap. The feature-level alignment loss draws directly on FitNets-style hint distillation, while the SVD truncation is inspired by low-rank compression in Lillama but extended to handle mismatched input/output dimensions across teacher and student.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that a 7.3× smaller student (S3) retains near-teacher reconstruction quality on lunar stereo pairs provides a concrete recipe for deploying 3D geometry models on resource-constrained platforms (rovers, landers, embedded robotics), where the 688M-parameter teacher is simply infeasible. The methodological template—domain-adapted teacher, pseudo-GT supervision, feature alignment, structured initialization—is broadly applicable to other deployment-constrained geometric tasks. Key limitations include the single-domain evaluation (lunar only), the confounded CNN-vs-ViT comparison (different pretraining), and the reliance on a single teacher architecture; whether the decoder-redundancy finding and SVD initialization transfer to other 3D foundation models (DUSt3R, VGGT) or other domains remains open.
