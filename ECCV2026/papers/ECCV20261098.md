## Problem

Existing music-driven dance video generation methods struggle to simultaneously produce expressive, rhythm-aligned motion and high visual fidelity. This stems from two gaps: the absence of large-scale, high-quality dance video datasets with effective collection pipelines, and the lack of principled framework-level solutions for injecting music conditioning into video generation foundation models that were trained without audio supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261098/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

OmniDance accepts a reference image (first frame), an optional text prompt, and an optional music audio track, and generates a 5-second dance video at 704×1280 resolution (77 frames at 16 FPS) via 40-step flow-matching sampling. It unifies three conditioning modes—TI2V, MI2V, and MTI2V—within a single model, allowing seamless modality switching at inference without retraining. For longer sequences, a sliding-window scheme reuses the last frame of each clip as the starting frame of the next, enabling iterative chaining.

## Method

Built on WAN2.2-TI2V-5B (a 30-block DiT with a spatio-temporal VAE at 4×16×16 compression), OmniDance introduces a Music-Text Progressive Specialization architecture that adds an audio cross-attention module to every DiT block, with the audio residual scaled by (ℓ/L)^γ (γ=1.5) so shallow layers prioritize text for global semantic layout while deeper layers progressively amplify music for rhythm-aware motion refinement. Training follows a three-stage easy-to-hard curriculum: Stage I adapts the backbone to dance-specific text (music frozen), Stage II introduces the music branch under text guidance (TI2V + MTI2V) with zero-initialized residual projections, and Stage III trains on all three tasks to enable genuine music-only generation. At inference, a Modality-Specialized CFG assigns time-dependent guidance scales that shift emphasis from text (λ_t: 5→1) to music (λ_m: 4→2) along the sampling trajectory, aligning each modality's influence with the frequency band it governs. The approach is trained on the newly introduced CIPE-Dance dataset (~300k clips, 400+ hours), constructed via a six-stage Progressive Expert pipeline using Qwen3-VL models and annotated with five-dimensional choreography-informed captions.

## Results

On the CIPE-Dance test set (100 clips), OmniDance achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three generation tasks. For TI2V, it attains the best imaging quality (IQ 67.74), motion smoothness (MS 99.24), temporal flickering (TF 98.46), and kinetic FID (FIDk 13.55 vs. 19.15 for WAN2.2-TI2V-5B). For MI2V, it leads in subject consistency (SC 92.63), geometric FID (FIDg 1.64), and beat alignment score (BAS 0.293 vs. 0.283 for WAN-S2V). Under the combined MTI2V setting, OmniDance reaches the highest overall consistency (OC 13.08), subject consistency (SC 94.34), and background consistency (BC 94.54), approaching ground-truth beat alignment (BAS 0.287 vs. 0.288 GT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the TI2V setting, OmniDance outperforms all three foundation-model baselines (CogVideoX1.5-5B, HunyuanVideo, WAN2.2-TI2V-5B) on nearly every metric, with the most notable gains in kinetic FID (13.55 vs. 19.15 for WAN2.2, a 29% reduction) and motion smoothness (99.24 vs. 98.08). In the MI2V setting, OmniDance surpasses Hallo2 (adapted from talking-head to full-body), WAN-S2V, and Echomimic-V3 on subject consistency (92.63 vs. 89.92–90.77), geometric FID (1.64 vs. 1.81–2.83), and beat alignment (BAS 0.293 vs. 0.244–0.283), though WAN-S2V retains a slight edge in aesthetic quality (AQ 55.81 vs. 54.96). The MTI2V task has no direct external baselines; OmniDance is the only method evaluated, and it achieves near ground-truth beat alignment (BAS 0.287 vs. 0.288) while substantially improving subject and background consistency over the GT reference (SC 94.34 vs. 91.51; BC 94.54 vs. 93.62). Overall, OmniDance wins or ties on the majority of metrics across all three tasks, with the largest margins in motion fidelity (FID) and music-motion synchronization (BAS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the WAN2.2-TI2V-5B video generation foundation model, augmenting its DiT architecture with a music cross-attention branch rather than training a dance-specific generator from scratch. It departs from prior music-driven dance generation paradigms (2D keypoint prediction, 3D SMPL parameter estimation, and small-scale end-to-end diffusion) by leveraging a large pretrained TI2V backbone and injecting music as a complementary high-frequency conditioning signal through a depth-aware progressive specialization scheme. The CIPE-Dance dataset (300k clips, 400+ hours) addresses a critical gap relative to existing 3D dance datasets like FineDance (~8 hours), enabling the scale of supervision that prior methods lacked. The curriculum training and modality-specialized CFG strategies are positioned as principled solutions to the problem of integrating audio into models trained without audio supervision, distinguishing this work from ad-hoc fusion approaches in DabFusion, MusicInfuser, and MuseDance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CIPE-Dance dataset is likely to become a standard benchmark for dance video generation, given its scale (300k clips, 30+ genres, diverse environments) and the progressive expert pipeline that makes it reproducible. The unified TI2V/MI2V/MTI2V framework offers a practical recipe for adding music conditioning to existing video foundation models without retraining from scratch, a pattern that could generalize to other audio-visual generation tasks. Key limitations include the 5-second clip length, single-dancer constraint, and lack of real-time inference (~4.5–7.2 min per clip), which the authors acknowledge as important open problems. The ablation revealing that skipping the anchored music-integration stage (Stage II) actually improves beat alignment at the cost of visual quality highlights a non-trivial trade-off that future work on multi-modal video generation will need to navigate.
