## Problem

Current image-to-video models condition on a single first frame and must hallucinate the appearance of unseen body or garment regions (e.g., the back of a shirt), producing visually inconsistent results that are unacceptable in e-commerce and other fidelity-critical applications. No existing method can fuse multiple reference views of the same subject or garment to generate a video whose appearance remains consistent across all viewpoints shown during the animation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262131/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a conditioning first-frame image, a text prompt, and up to nine clean reference images (two canonical frontal/back views, three k-means-clustered multi-view frames, and four garment-segmented crops) as input, and produces a temporally coherent animated person video at 720p. Unlike the noisy video tokens that lie along the flow-matching trajectory, the reference images are clean latents encoded by a VAE and treated as a distinct modality. During training the person region in the conditioning frame is randomly masked to force reliance on references, but at inference the full unmasked frame is used.

## Method

MVI2V extends a pretrained I2V diffusion backbone (Wan2.1 single-stream or a 15B in-house dual-stream model) by adding a structurally identical forward stream dedicated to reference image tokens, upgrading the architecture to dual- or triple-stream. Video and reference tokens interact exclusively through a shared self-attention layer (concatenated before the attention computation), while each modality retains its own cross-attention and FFN parameters, preventing cross-modality interference. The new stream's weights are initialized via LoRA adapters (≈0.3 B extra parameters, ~2.1% of the 14B base) rather than full duplication, keeping training cost low. An auxiliary inpainting subtask randomly masks the subject's bounding box in the conditioning frame during training, compelling the model to reconstruct appearance from reference images alone and avoiding the local optimum where the first frame's strong prior causes references to be ignored. A targeted data pipeline filters for single-person clips with large angular rotation, selects diverse reference frames via canonical-view matching plus k-means clustering, and augments the set with garment crops segmented by Gemini and Sa2va.

## Results

On the proposed 300-sample multi-view benchmark, MVI2V-Wan2.1 achieves the highest garment consistency scores across all four metrics: GPT_person 0.862, GPT_garment 0.585, DINO_person 0.728, and DINO_garment 0.656, while also maintaining or improving video quality (Subject Consistency 0.927, Background Consistency 0.938, Motion Smoothness 0.988). On the dual-stream in-house backbone, MVI2V-In-House further improves GPT_person to 0.868 and DINO_person to 0.776 over the In-House baseline (0.692 and 0.700). On the original VBench I2V benchmark, MVI2V-Wan2.1 preserves single-image-to-video capability with Subject Consistency 0.953 (vs. 0.938 for Wan2.1) and Motion Smoothness 0.987 (vs. 0.980), confirming no degradation of foundational I2V performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVI2V-Wan2.1 outperforms the Wan2.1 baseline on all four garment consistency metrics (e.g., GPT_garment 0.585 vs. 0.409, a 43% relative gain) while also improving video quality (Subject Consistency 0.927 vs. 0.916). Against Token-Concat, MVI2V achieves substantially higher garment consistency (GPT_person 0.862 vs. 0.797; DINO_garment 0.656 vs. 0.620) and avoids the video quality degradation that Token-Concat introduces (Subject Consistency 0.927 vs. 0.902). Compared to Concat-ID, MVI2V further improves GPT_garment (0.585 vs. 0.542) and DINO_garment (0.656 vs. 0.613) while matching or exceeding video quality scores. On the dual-stream in-house backbone, MVI2V-In-House lifts GPT_person from 0.692 to 0.868 and DINO_person from 0.700 to 0.776, confirming the architecture's generalizability across single-stream and dual-stream DiT backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MMDiT-based video diffusion architecture (as in CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo, and Wan2.1) by introducing a dedicated third forward stream for multi-view reference images, fused via self-attention with the video token stream. It departs from single-frame I2V methods that must hallucinate unseen garment views, and from video virtual try-on that conditions on a source video plus a garment image, by formulating a task where multiple static reference views of the same subject jointly condition the generation. The use of LoRA to parameterize the new stream and the inpainting subtask to prevent shortcut learning are practical training strategies that build on standard parameter-efficient fine-tuning and data augmentation ideas, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a concrete and commercially important gap: e-commerce video generation requires strict multi-view appearance fidelity that single-frame I2V models cannot guarantee. The method's generality across single-stream and dual-stream backbones, combined with the modest parameter overhead (~2%), makes it a practical drop-in extension for existing video generation pipelines. Open questions include how the approach scales to more than a handful of reference views, whether it generalizes to non-human subjects (e.g., products, vehicles), and how to handle the case where only one or two reference views are available in practice. The small benchmark (300 samples) and reliance on GPT-based scoring for garment consistency also leave room for more rigorous evaluation.
