## Problem

Lane-level HD map generation from aerial imagery remains challenging because existing methods either produce rasterized outputs requiring brittle post-processing to recover topology, or fail to handle the large variation in lane density across scenes and the local-to-global consistency needed for city-scale deployment. A generative approach that directly produces vectorized lane graphs with explicit directed connectivity, while remaining robust to uncertain lane counts and tile boundaries, is missing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260347/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 px aerial image tile (covering 76.8 m × 76.8 m) and outputs a directed lane graph consisting of variable-length lane centerlines (each a 20-point 2D polyline) and four binary adjacency matrices encoding successor, predecessor, left, and right relations. For city-scale mapping, overlapping tiles are processed in a fixed raster order, with boundary features from already-predicted neighboring tiles injected as additional conditioning, and a 32 px rejection margin suppresses boundary artifacts.

## Method

MapDreamer uses a two-stage pipeline: a transformer-based VAE compresses variable-cardinality lane graphs (geometry + topology) into a fixed-dimensional latent space, and a DiT-style latent diffusion model denoises Gaussian samples toward these latents conditioned on DINOv3 aerial features injected via cross-attention at every step. To handle unknown lane counts at inference, a transformer-based lane cardinality module estimates the number of lanes, and a fixed set of five ghost lane latents (encoded from out-of-bounds polylines) serve as capacity buffers that the existence head can activate or suppress. For global map generation, a boundary attention branch embeds intersection points, tangent directions, and side indicators from already-predicted neighboring tiles as additional cross-attention tokens, avoiding the error accumulation of scene inpainting. The VAE decoder acts as a structural prior that regularizes sampled latents toward geometrically and topologically valid lane configurations.

## Results

On the local tile evaluation (strict threshold ω₁=1.2 m), MapDreamer achieves GEO₁ F₁ of 0.640 and TOPO₁ F₁ of 0.562, substantially outperforming BGFormer (0.458 and 0.345 respectively). On the global 5000×5000 px evaluation, MapDreamer reaches GEO₁ F₁ of 0.584 and TOPO₁ F₁ of 0.401, surpassing both BGFormer (0.352 / 0.189) and LaneGNN (0.545 / 0.327). Under the relaxed threshold (ω₂=2 m), the same trend holds with GEO₂ F₁ of 0.768 (local) and 0.720 (global), and TOPO₂ F₁ of 0.702 (local) and 0.557 (global). Rasterized IoU₁ improves from 0.354 (BGFormer local) to 0.482 (MapDreamer local) and from 0.280 (BGFormer global) to 0.454 (MapDreamer global).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BGFormer [3], MapDreamer wins decisively on every metric in both local and global settings: local GEO₁ F₁ improves by 18.2% (0.640 vs 0.458) and TOPO₁ F₁ by 21.7% (0.562 vs 0.345); globally the gaps widen to +23.2% GEO₁ F₁ and +21.2% TOPO₁ F₁. The paper attributes BGFormer's weakness to abrupt failures when confidence thresholding removes nodes or edges due to incorrect cardinality estimates, whereas MapDreamer degrades more gracefully. Against LaneGNN [4] (global evaluation only), MapDreamer achieves higher GEO₁ F₁ (0.584 vs 0.545, +3.9%) and TOPO₁ F₁ (0.401 vs 0.327, +7.4%), driven primarily by substantially higher recall (0.598 vs 0.451 for GEO₁) while LaneGNN retains a precision advantage (0.701 vs 0.575). Notably, LaneGNN was initialized with 178 ground-truth poses per tile, a privileged setup that MapDreamer does not require. The relaxed-threshold results confirm the gains are structural rather than merely sub-metric offset corrections.

## Relation to Prior Work

MapDreamer directly adapts the two-stage VAE + latent diffusion pipeline from Scenario Dreamer [20], repurposing it from unconditional simulation scenario generation to fully conditional lane graph reconstruction from aerial imagery. It departs from the dominant deterministic-prediction paradigm in lane-level map generation (BGFormer's Bézier graph regression, LaneGNN's GNN-scored candidate edges) by framing the problem as conditional sampling in a learned latent space, where the VAE decoder imposes geometric and topological plausibility as an implicit structural prior. The aerial conditioning via frozen DINOv3 features with cross-attention at every denoising step distinguishes it from prior diffusion-for-maps work (MapDiffusion, LaneDiffusion) that operates on BEV features or raw polyline queries without a structured graph latent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Lane-level HD map generation remains a bottleneck for scalable autonomous driving deployment, and this work demonstrates that generative diffusion in a structured latent space can produce topologically consistent maps with substantially better junction-level connectivity than deterministic baselines. The ghost lane latents mechanism offers a practical, generalizable solution to the variable-cardinality problem that plagues set-based vector map prediction, and the boundary-attention sliding-window strategy provides a concrete path to city-scale deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Argoverse 2's geographic and sensor coverage, how it integrates with online vehicle-sensor updates, and whether flow matching can further reduce the 100-step DDPM sampling cost while preserving the iterative refinement benefits.
