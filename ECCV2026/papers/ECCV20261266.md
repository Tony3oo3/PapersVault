## Problem

Standard SFT-trained VLMs for autonomous driving suffer from reasoning hallucinations and conservative biases in complex dynamic scenes, while existing remedies—tool-augmented pipelines and explicit Chain-of-Thought generation—introduce fragile external API dependencies and prohibitive inference latency (thousands of milliseconds) that preclude real-time vehicular deployment. The paper tackles the fundamental trade-off between deep System-2 logical reasoning and low-latency System-1 execution in driving VLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261266/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single RGB driving scene image (from nuScenes) paired with a text VQA instruction, and produces a structured response containing an intermediate reasoning trajectory and a final multiple-choice driving action. The Stage-2 Teacher outputs both in XML-style tags (think/answer), while the Stage-3 Student bypasses explicit reasoning entirely, emitting only the answer token sequence (~29 tokens average) to achieve 416 ms inference on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

CritiqueDriveVLM is a three-stage training pipeline built on Qwen3-VL-8B. Stage 1 performs warm-up SFT to enforce output format and trains a frozen multi-dimensional verifier that scores responses on perception, logic, and safety (each 0/0.5/1.0) while generating targeted text critiques. Stage 2 applies Critique-Driven Multi-Turn RL via GRPO: the verifier provides fine-grained scalar rewards and natural-language critiques across up to K=2 turns, and a step-decay multi-turn penalty forces the policy to internalize reasoning rather than over-relying on external feedback. Stage 3 introduces Latent Thought Distillation, which aligns the Student's hidden representation at the <answer> token with the Teacher's fully converged hidden state at the final  token via a cosine-similarity objective, combined with standard cross-entropy loss—compressing System-2 reasoning into the Student's latent space without any explicit CoT generation.

## Results

On the DriveLMM-o1 benchmark, the Stage-2 Teacher achieves a state-of-the-art MCQ of 76.54% and Overall Reasoning of 80.48%, surpassing the base Qwen3-VL-8B (55.54% MCQ) by over 21 points without any external tool calls. The Stage-3 Student, distilled via Latent Thought Distillation, retains 68.59% MCQ while reducing average generation to 28.83 tokens and inference latency to 416 ms—an 88% reduction from the Teacher's 3482 ms. The Teacher also leads on Traffic Rule Adherence (86.21%) and Relevance (85.46%), while OmniDrive-R1 marginally edges ahead on Risk Assessment (82.31% vs. 81.63%) and Scene Awareness (83.75% vs. 82.27%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Stage-2 Teacher outperforms all driving-specific baselines on the headline MCQ metric: 76.54% vs. OmniDrive-R1 (73.62%), AgentThink (71.35%), and DriveLMM-o1 (62.36%), and surpasses the general-purpose GPT-4o (57.84%) by nearly 19 points. On Overall Reasoning, the Teacher (80.48%) narrowly beats OmniDrive-R1 (80.35%) and AgentThink (79.68%). The Teacher leads decisively on Traffic Rule Adherence (86.21% vs. 85.42% for OmniDrive-R1) and Relevance (85.46% vs. 84.99% for AgentThink), but concedes small margins on Risk Assessment (81.63% vs. 82.31%) and Scene Awareness (82.27% vs. 83.75%) to OmniDrive-R1, and on Missing Details (79.17% vs. 79.56%) to AgentThink. The distilled Stage-3 Student achieves 68.59% MCQ with only 28.83 tokens and 416 ms latency, substantially outperforming the CoT-free Qwen3-VL-8B SFT baseline (61.73% MCQ, 343 ms) while matching the latency of non-reasoning models. Critically, the Teacher reaches its SOTA accuracy entirely through internalized verifier-guided RL, avoiding the fragile external API dependencies and 5416 ms latency of AgentThink.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three active research threads: VLM-based autonomous driving (DriveLMM-o1, AgentThink, OmniDrive-R1), RL with verifiable rewards (DeepSeekMath/GRPO, AlphaDrive), and knowledge distillation of reasoning capabilities (MiniLLM, CODI, 'Distilling System 2 into System 1'). It departs from tool-augmented CoT frameworks by replacing brittle external API calls with an internal multi-dimensional verifier that provides scalar and textual feedback during GRPO training. The key conceptual departure is in the distillation stage: rather than token-level imitation of CoT or final answers, it aligns the student's hidden state at the <answer> token with the teacher's hidden state at the final  token, compressing multi-turn reasoning into a single latent representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 88% latency reduction (3482 ms → 416 ms) while retaining 68.59% MCQ (vs. 76.54% for the teacher) addresses a critical deployment bottleneck for real-time autonomous driving, making VLM-based reasoning practically viable on vehicle hardware. However, validation is confined to a single benchmark (DriveLMM-o1), a single base model (Qwen3-VL-8B), and a single underlying dataset (nuScenes), leaving open whether the latent alignment strategy generalizes across architectures, modalities (e.g., multi-camera video), or driving scenarios. The authors themselves note the need to extend to temporal logic in video streams, and the 7-point MCQ gap between teacher and student suggests that some reasoning depth is irreversibly lost in the compression.
