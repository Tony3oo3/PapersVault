## Problem

Hybrid event-frame sensors integrate an Active Pixel Sensor and an Event Vision Sensor on a single chip, but the resulting circuit complexity introduces non-trivial, layout-dependent noise patterns that remain unmodeled and uncalibrated. Existing event simulators rely on hand-tuned hyperparameters and contrast-based rules that do not capture hybrid-sensor-specific effects such as cross-coupled fixed-pattern noise and illumination-dependent EVS triggering, leaving a significant simulation-to-real domain gap for downstream vision tasks.

## Input / Output

H-ESIM takes a 3200 fps sRGB video (1296×1024) as temporal input, along with per-sensor calibrated noise parameters (six APS coefficients β_a per Quad-Bayer position, six EVS coefficients β_e, fixed-pattern maps N_row, N_BLC, N_DP, and hardware threshold Θ). It produces RAW APS frames at the sensor's native resolution (e.g., 3246×2448 for the Quad-Bayer layout) and a temporally aligned EVS event stream of discrete ±1 triggers. The APS path includes a full NumPy-based ISP (BLC, demosaic, color correction, gamma) for RAW→sRGB verification, while the EVS path outputs per-pixel ON/OFF events with trigger rates matching the calibrated Q-function probabilities.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified statistical noise model that jointly describes APS and EVS pixels sharing the same photoreceptor. For APS, the output is modeled as ideal signal plus Gaussian shot noise, exposure-dependent dark-current noise, and fixed-pattern/quantization terms, with aggregate variance fit as a second-order polynomial in (I_c, Δt) per Quad-Bayer position. For EVS, the log-domain voltage difference is decomposed into a signal term S and a Gaussian noise term N_e whose variance depends on illumination and dark current; the Q-function then links the discrete ±1 event output to continuous noise statistics, enabling an inverse-Q calibration that recovers σ_n² from observed event probabilities. Calibration proceeds in two stages: APS parameters are estimated from multi-exposure dark and illuminated frames via least-squares variance fitting, while EVS parameters are optimized by gradient descent on the nonlinear inverse-Q relationship, with an affine mapping bridging APS digital intensity to the EVS logarithmic voltage domain. H-ESIM then applies these calibrated parameters in a controllable sRGB→RAW inverse pipeline (inverse gamma, inverse color matrix, white balance, Bayer resampling, fixed-term injection, stochastic noise sampling) for APS, and a log-difference comparator with calibrated threshold and noise for EVS, all implemented in NumPy/PyTorch for reproducibility.

## Results

On the Eiger sensor, HR-INR fine-tuned with H-ESIM achieves the best VFI performance with PSNR 35.24 dB, SSIM 0.8862, and LPIPS 0.0787, outperforming the same model trained with ESIM (PSNR 32.41, LPIPS 0.1979) by 2.83 dB in PSNR and 60% reduction in LPIPS. On GEN2, HR-INR with H-ESIM reaches PSNR 35.52 dB and LPIPS 0.0419. For deblurring on Eiger, H-ESIM fine-tuning yields the largest gains for eSL (CLIP-IQA 0.307→0.435, MUSIQ 18.74→22.97) and EFNet (CLIP-IQA 0.221→0.425, MUSIQ 16.83→21.52), while MAER shows only marginal improvement (CLIP-IQA 0.330→0.337).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four VFI methods (TimeLens, CBMNet, TimeLens-XL, HR-INR) and three deblurring methods (eSL, MAER, EFNet), each evaluated with and without H-ESIM fine-tuning. H-ESIM consistently outperforms generic simulators (ESIM, v2e) across all methods and both sensors; the largest gains appear on Eiger, where stronger fixed-pattern noise from the embedded EVS pixels makes the domain gap more pronounced (e.g., HR-INR LPIPS drops from 0.1979 to 0.0787). TimeLens-XL with H-ESIM surpasses TimeLens-XL with v2e by 1.57 dB PSNR on Eiger, confirming that calibrated noise statistics matter beyond simple contrast-based event generation. For deblurring, eSL and EFNet benefit substantially from H-ESIM (CLIP-IQA gains of +0.128 and +0.204 respectively), while MAER shows only a +0.007 CLIP-IQA improvement, suggesting its architecture is less sensitive to noise-domain mismatch. Overall, H-ESIM fine-tuning improves every method tested, with the magnitude of improvement correlating with the severity of hybrid-sensor-specific noise artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends standard CMOS camera noise models (Foi et al., Johnson) and event-pixel circuit analyses (Graca & Delbrück) into a single unified statistical framework that jointly describes APS and EVS noise within hybrid sensors. It departs from prior event simulators (ESIM, v2e, DVS-Voltmeter), which rely on hand-tuned contrast thresholds or Brownian-motion assumptions, by introducing a Q-function-based calibration that links observed event probabilities to physical illumination and dark-current parameters. The inverse colorimetric pipeline and NumPy ISP library build on prior RGB-event ISP work (Lu et al., 2024) but are repurposed here to inject calibrated, position-dependent noise rather than merely convert between color spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hybrid event-frame sensors are moving from lab prototypes to commercial platforms (MIPI challenges, mobile/drones), yet the absence of a calibrated noise model has limited simulation-to-real transfer for downstream tasks. By demonstrating that H-ESIM fine-tuning consistently improves VFI and deblurring over generic simulators, the paper establishes a reproducible path for training on synthetic hybrid-sensor data. Key open questions include extending the Gaussian noise approximation to extreme low-light or high-temperature regimes, modeling bandwidth-limited readout, and generalizing the per-position Quad-Bayer calibration to arbitrary pixel layouts.
