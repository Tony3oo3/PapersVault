## Problem

Existing unsupervised anomaly detection methods fall into two camps: matching-based approaches (e.g., PatchCore) that store dense raw feature banks and suffer from redundancy and high computational cost, and reconstruction-based approaches (e.g., INP-Former, Dinomaly) that use transformer attention to implicitly generate prototypes, introducing architectural complexity and poor interpretability. No prior method achieves both the explicitness of feature matching and the efficiency of on-the-fly reconstruction in a simple, modular pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB query image (resized to 512×512, center-cropped to 448×448, then bilinearly resized back to 512×512) and a pre-built prototype bank of size 8192 constructed via K-Means clustering of normal training features from two complementary encoders (DINOv3-small primary, ResNet18 auxiliary). It produces an image-level anomaly score in [0,1] and a pixel-wise anomaly heatmap at the spatial resolution of the input, with the prototype bank dynamically adapted per query image during inference.

## Method

FDP generates adaptive prototypes through four explicit steps: (1) K-Means clustering of normal deep features builds a compact raw prototype bank; (2) a Dynamic Prototype Adapter retrieves top-K category-relevant prototypes via class-token cosine similarity, then refines them via multi-head attention with learnable query tokens attending to the test image's patch tokens, producing a compact dynamic bank; (3) Complementary Prototype Reconstruction computes per-token reconstruction weights from inverse cosine distances (softmax-normalized) and forms the reconstructed prototype as a weighted sum of dynamic bank members, with the same weights shared across primary (DINOv3) and auxiliary (ResNet18) banks that are fused via zero-convolution; (4) a Hybrid Residual combines the multiplicative cosine residual with a similarity-gated subtractive term to suppress object-structure clutter before feeding a lightweight segmentation head. Training proceeds in two phases: first, DPA and CPR are trained with a cosine-similarity loss on normal samples only; second, the segmentation head is trained on synthetic anomalies (generated via DTD) using an L1 + focal loss, with the backbone and prototype bank frozen.

## Results

In the multi-class setting, FDP achieves an average score of 89.7% across 12 metrics on MVTec-AD, MVTec-3D, and Real-IAD, outperforming the next-best method WeakREST (86.7%) by 3.0 points. On MVTec-AD, FDP reaches 99.7% I-AUROC, 79.1% P-AP, and 96.6% P-PRO; on MVTec-3D it attains 91.9% I-AUROC and 70.0% P-AP. In terms of efficiency, FDP runs at 192 FPS (512×512) or 358 FPS (392×392) with only 38.7M parameters, roughly 5× faster than INP-Former (38 FPS) and over 10× faster than PatchCore (13 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FDP achieves the highest average score (89.7%) across all 12 metrics, beating the closest competitor WeakREST (86.7%) by 3.0 points and INP-Former (86.5%) by 3.2 points. The most consistent advantage is in P-AP (pixel-level average precision): FDP leads by 2.0 points over WeakREST on MVTec-AD (79.1 vs 77.1), by 14.1 points over INP-Former on MVTec-3D (70.0 vs 55.9), and by 5.0 points over WeakREST on Real-IAD (60.6 vs 55.6). On MVTec-AD I-AUROC, FDP ties INP-Former at 99.7%, but on MVTec-3D INP-Former edges ahead slightly (92.6 vs 91.9 I-AUROC, 97.0 vs 96.8 P-PRO, 99.2 vs 98.9 P-AUROC). Against older matching-based methods like PatchCore and PaDiM (cross-class setting), FDP gains 22.4 and 25.9 P-AP points respectively on MVTec-AD, and substantially outperforms reconstruction-based methods like Dinomaly (85.4 avg) and DeSTSeg (68.0 avg). In the single-class setting (Table 2), FDP also leads all competitors, achieving 91.0% average vs INP-Former's 87.4%, with the largest gap on P-AP (81.5 vs 70.2 on MVTec-AD). In the cross-class setting (Table 3), FDP's 89.3% average far exceeds HETMM (82.4%) and PatchCore (79.7%), while Dinomaly collapses to 49.8% average, confirming that explicit dynamic prototype adaptation generalizes better than implicit attention-based reconstruction under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

FDP positions itself explicitly between the two dominant UAD paradigms: matching-based methods (PatchCore, WeakREST, PRN) that store dense raw feature banks and perform nearest-neighbor search, and reconstruction-based methods (Dinomaly, INP-Former, DeSTSeg) that use transformer attention to implicitly generate prototypes. Rather than adopting either extreme, FDP repurposes K-Means clustering to build a compact prototype bank (borrowing from the matching side) and introduces a lightweight Dynamic Bank Adapter with multi-head attention to condition the bank on the test image (borrowing from the reconstruction side), but performs the final prototype assignment as an explicit weighted nearest-neighbor average rather than an implicit attention mapping. The dual-backbone design (DINOv3 + ResNet18 fused via zero-convolution) and the hybrid residual formulation are novel combinations not present in either prior lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving SOTA multi-class UAD accuracy (mAD 93.5 on MVTec-AD) at 192–477 FPS, roughly 5–10× faster than competing transformer-based methods, making real-time industrial deployment more feasible. The explicit, four-step pipeline (cluster → adapt → weighted-NN reconstruct → fuse) offers interpretability that attention-based implicit reconstruction lacks, which matters for safety-critical inspection settings. A key open question the authors acknowledge is that the dynamic extraction mechanism can contaminate the prototype bank when the test image is dominated by large defects, motivating future defect-aware adaptation modules. The very low CKA score (≈0.38) between DINOv3 and ResNet18 features suggests the complementary-backbone strategy could generalize beyond UAD to other tasks where self-supervised artifacts are problematic.
