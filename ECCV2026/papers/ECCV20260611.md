## Problem

DiT-based video generation is latency-intensive due to iterative full-frame denoising, and existing cloud-edge acceleration methods rely on static inter-step decoupling that ignores inter-frame temporal redundancy, causes temporal artifacts from the capability gap between large and small models, and cannot adapt to fluctuating bandwidth and compute resources. This paper asks whether shifting the collaboration axis from denoising steps to individual frames—guided by a training-free information-density signal—can simultaneously improve temporal coherence and end-to-end efficiency under dynamic cloud-edge conditions.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text prompt and produces a complete video (e.g., 5 s / 81 frames at 720P for Wan2.1, 6 s / 49 frames at 480P for CogVideoX). Unusually, the output is assembled from two heterogeneous sources: the cloud large model denoises only a sparse set of high-entropy keyframes (transmitted as ~1 MB of latent data), while a 160M-parameter edge model synthesizes the remaining intermediate frames via motion-aware interpolation, so the final video is a stitched sequence of cloud-generated anchors and edge-generated fills.

## Method

EcoVideo introduces an entropy-orchestrated inter-frame decoupling paradigm with three components. First, during a 10% warm-up stage, token-level self-attention entropy is aggregated per frame via mean pooling and stabilized with an exponential moving average to yield a training-free frame-wise information-density score. Second, high-entropy keyframes are denoised on the cloud large model while non-keyframe latents are injected as frozen context tokens (stop-gradient keys/values) to preserve global temporal layout; the edge then completes intermediate frames using EcoVFI, a modified EDEN interpolator that greedily allocates refinement steps to the most challenging intervals based on a composite difficulty score combining optical-flow magnitude, entropy gap, DINO feature distance, and pixel appearance difference. Third, a lightweight scheduler maps real-time bandwidth and cloud/edge compute to an optimal keyframe budget K and interpolation depth D by solving a small discrete utility-maximization problem, enabling closed-loop adaptation to system dynamics.

## Results

On VBench, EcoVideo achieves overall scores of 0.846 (Wan2.1-14B), 0.830 (Wan2.2-A14B), and 0.775 (CogVideoX-5B), outperforming the best step-decoupling baseline by an average of 0.084 across all three model families. In terms of efficiency, EcoVideo delivers 1.84×, 1.59×, and 2.03× end-to-end speedup over cloud-only baselines, while HybridSD and EC-Diff achieve at most 1.27×, 1.02×, and 0.98× respectively. Communication volume drops from 17.23 MB (Wan) / 5.67 MB (CogVideoX) for step-splitting baselines to 1.10 MB / 0.32 MB, and edge latency falls from 648–779 s to 96 s (Wan) and from 71–108 s to 22 s (CogVideoX).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EcoVideo is compared against two inter-step decoupling cloud-edge baselines: HybridSD (early steps on cloud large model, late steps on edge small model) and EC-Diff (adds noise-gradient approximation and adaptive switching-point search). On VBench Overall, EcoVideo wins decisively across all three model families: 0.846 vs. 0.677/0.683 (Wan2.1), 0.830 vs. 0.784/0.792 (Wan2.2), and 0.775 vs. 0.725/0.721 (CogVideoX), with the largest gap on aesthetic quality (e.g., 0.649 vs. 0.435/0.442 on Wan2.1) and overall consistency (0.254 vs. 0.152/0.156). On efficiency, both baselines are at or below cloud-only throughput in most settings (HybridSD: 0.79–0.89×; EC-Diff: 0.98–1.27×), whereas EcoVideo achieves 1.59–2.03× speedup. The communication volume advantage is particularly stark: EcoVideo transmits 1.10 MB (Wan) or 0.32 MB (CogVideoX) versus 17.23 MB or 5.67 MB for both baselines, and edge latency drops from 649–779 s to 96 s (Wan) and from 71–108 s to 22 s (CogVideoX). Under dynamic resource stress (cloud contention, edge contention, bandwidth reduction), EcoVideo maintains 1.48–2.91× speedup while HybridSD and EC-Diff degrade to 0.69–1.68×, confirming the benefit of adaptive frame-level orchestration over fixed step splitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the inter-step decoupling paradigm established by HybridSD and EC-Diff, which split denoising steps between a cloud large model and an edge small model, and instead proposes inter-frame decoupling that partitions work along the temporal axis. It builds on EDEN's video frame interpolation by modifying its interval scoring into EcoVFI, and draws on attention-entropy analysis from autoregressive modeling literature as a training-free proxy for frame-wise information density. The use of DINO features and RAFT optical flow for difficulty scoring in the interpolation module repurposes standard vision tools in a novel cloud-edge orchestration context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment bottleneck for DiT video generation services, showing that a 160M interpolation model on the edge can replace a 1.3B–2B diffusion model while actually improving temporal coherence over step-splitting baselines. The system-aware scheduler that adapts keyframe budget and interpolation depth to real-time bandwidth and compute is a step toward production-ready cloud-edge video generation. Open questions remain around extreme motion or scene-change scenarios where interpolation may fail, and the paper acknowledges that joint optimization with cloud-side acceleration (parallelism, caching, sparse attention) is left for future work.
