## Problem

Dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting methods struggle to model physically plausible motion: existing approaches either depend on external priors (optical flow, 2D tracks) that provide indirect, screen-space motion cues prone to error propagation, or enforce strict KNN-based rigidity that ignores Gaussian scale/opacity and fails under non-rigid deformation. The paper asks how to enforce locally coherent motion directly from image supervision without external guidance while still permitting natural non-rigid dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261459/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a model-agnostic regularization module that plugs into existing dynamic 3DGS frameworks (RTD, Ex4DGS, MoDecGS, Grid4D) without altering their architectures. It consumes the same monocular video frames and camera poses as the base model, and the only additional input is the per-pixel alpha-blending weights already computed during rasterization. The output remains a time-varying 3D Gaussian Splatting representation rendered at the base model's resolution (800×800 for D-NeRF, 536×960 for HyperNeRF, 480×270 for NeRF-DS), with no extra rendering cost—only roughly 2× training time overhead from covariance and SVD operations.

## Method

The core idea is a ray-based grouping strategy that re-purposes the 3DGS rasterization pipeline: for each pixel, Gaussians whose α-blending contribution weight w_i exceeds a threshold τ are clustered into a group, naturally encoding occlusion, scale, and opacity without explicit 3D distance metrics. Two regularization terms are then applied within each group. First, Motion Coherence Regularization (MCR) penalizes directional inconsistency via a cosine-similarity loss between each Gaussian's displacement and the group mean displacement, but deliberately ignores magnitude differences—this 'relaxed' constraint enforces coherent direction while allowing non-rigid speed variation. Second, Spectral Regularization (SR) computes the 3×3 covariance of Gaussian positions within each group at consecutive time steps (using Welford's algorithm for single-pass online computation) and penalizes the Huber loss between their sorted eigenvalue spectra, preserving local shape statistics while remaining invariant to rigid rotation and permitting localized deformation. The final objective augments the standard L1 + DSSIM photometric loss with weighted MCR and SR terms, and the entire pipeline introduces no architectural changes to the underlying dynamic 3DGS model.

## Results

On D-NeRF, the method improves PSNR by an average of 1.19 dB over the four baseline models, with the largest gain of +2.35 dB for MoDec-GS (29.33 → 31.68) and Grid4D+Ours achieving the best overall PSNR of 42.20. On HyperNeRF, Grid4D+Ours attains the highest MS-SSIM of 0.856, and on the more challenging NeRF-DS dataset, MoDec-GS+Ours delivers the largest PSNR gain (+0.83 dB, reaching 23.76) along with the best LPIPS_V of 0.184. Training time increases by roughly 2×, but no additional rendering cost is introduced.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed regularization is integrated into four dynamic 3DGS baselines (RTD, Ex4DGS, MoDec-GS, Grid4D) and consistently improves all of them across three datasets. On D-NeRF, MoDec-GS+Ours shows the largest PSNR gain (+2.35 dB) and Grid4D+Ours achieves the best absolute PSNR (42.20), surpassing D3DGS (40.43) and GaGS (39.07). On HyperNeRF, Grid4D+Ours attains the highest MS-SSIM (0.856) and PSNR (25.56), outperforming RTD (25.17) and MoDec-GS (24.92). On the more challenging NeRF-DS with specular objects, MoDec-GS+Ours delivers the strongest improvement (+0.83 dB PSNR, reaching 23.76) and the best LPIPS_V (0.184), beating the original NeRF-DS method (23.60, 0.182). Against non-Gaussian baselines such as NeRF, D-NeRF, and TiNeuVox, all Gaussian-splatting variants with the proposed regularization substantially outperform them on every reported metric. The ablation further confirms that ray-based grouping outperforms KNN grouping, and that combining MCR with SR (the full configuration) is superior to either term alone or to ARAP-style rigidity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting rasterization framework and its dynamic extensions (deformation fields, spline trajectories, hash-grid encodings), but departs from two dominant strategies for enforcing motion coherence: external priors (optical flow, 2D tracks, monocular depth) and KNN-based grouping with strict ARAP rigidity. The key conceptual departure is repurposing the alpha-blending visibility mechanism already computed during rasterization as a grouping function, replacing Euclidean-distance KNN with ray-based clustering that naturally respects Gaussian scale, opacity, and occlusion. The relaxed rigidity formulation—cosine-similarity motion coherence plus eigenvalue-spectrum preservation—sits between unconstrained motion and full ARAP, targeting the gap where prior methods either over-constrain non-rigid deformations or under-constrain incoherent trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating dependence on external prior models, this work addresses a practical fragility in monocular dynamic 3DGS: when optical flow or depth estimators fail (textureless regions, specular surfaces, topological changes), reconstruction quality degrades sharply. The model-agnostic design (validated across four architectures spanning deformation-field and spline-based formulations) makes the regularization a drop-in component for the broader dynamic 3DGS community. Limitations include roughly 2× training overhead from covariance computation and SVD, and the method still operates at the level of local geometric consistency rather than encoding explicit physical dynamics (e.g., gravity, contact, articulation). Open questions include whether finer-grained physical constraints or learned grouping can further improve performance under highly complex or restricted motion conditions.
