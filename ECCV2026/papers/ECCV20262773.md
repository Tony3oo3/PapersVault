## Problem

Multimodal keypoint matching requires descriptors to handle fundamentally different sensing physics (e.g., MRI vs. ultrasound, RGB vs. event camera), but existing solutions either retrain descriptors for each modality pair or rely on large pre-trained models with high runtime. No prior method enables an already-trained monomodal descriptor to operate across modalities without modifying its training pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262773/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes ℓ2-normalized descriptor vectors (dimension D) extracted at independently detected keypoint locations in each modality, along with source and target modality labels. Descriptors are pooled and whitened via PCA before training, and a dual-anchor sampling strategy ensures balanced spatial coverage by detecting half the training keypoints in each modality. The output is a crossed descriptor in the target modality's feature space, matched via standard nearest-neighbor search; an optional test-time adaptation (TTA) step refines matches using RANSAC inliers as pseudo-labels for a closed-form ridge regression correction.

## Method

CrossFeat introduces a geometry-appearance disentanglement: a shared MLP encoder splits each descriptor into a deterministic geometry code (d_g = D) and a variational appearance code (d_a = D/2), where the appearance head outputs mean and log-variance sampled via reparameterization. A FiLM-conditioned residual MLP crosser, modulated by learned source and target modality embeddings, transforms only the appearance code while the geometry code bypasses it entirely, architecturally guaranteeing geometric preservation. A FiLM-conditioned decoder recombines the two codes and ℓ2-normalizes the output back onto the unit sphere. Training jointly optimizes six losses—reconstruction, geometry alignment (L2 between co-located geometry codes), crossing (cosine distance to target descriptor), adversarial modality classification with gradient reversal on the geometry code, modality-conditioned KL regularization with free bits, and InfoNCE contrastive discrimination—using fixed weights selected once via offline search. The entire crossing network contains only ~0.5–0.6M parameters and trains in 1–3 hours on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On the Medical task, Cross(SIFT)+NN+TTA achieves SR@1 of 79.2% and AUC@1 of 0.96, substantially outperforming the best sparse baseline SP+MINIMA-LG (SR@1=37.5%, AUC@1=0.26) and the best dense baseline MINIMA-RoMa (SR@1=66.2%, AUC@1=0.46). On the Driving task, CrossFeat reaches SR@1 of 85.3% and AUC@1 of 0.94, while all sparse baselines remain below 10% SR@1 and dense methods peak at 32.3% (MINIMA-LoFTR). On the particularly challenging Satellite task, CrossFeat is the only method producing non-zero success rates (SR@1=34.0%, AUC@1=0.87), whereas all baselines—including MatchAnything and MINIMA-RoMa—fail entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrossFeat (applied to SIFT) dominates all sparse baselines across every task. On Medical, it achieves SR@1=79.2% vs. SP+MINIMA-LG (37.5%), SIFT+NN (11.2%), and SP+LG (23.8%); on Driving, SR@1=85.3% vs. SIFT+NN (9.4%) and SP+MINIMA-LG (0.4%); on Satellite, SR@1=34.0% while all sparse baselines score 0.0%. Against dense methods, Cross(SIFT)+NN+TTA outperforms MINIMA-RoMa on Medical (SR@1 79.2 vs. 66.2; AUC@1 0.96 vs. 0.46) and Driving (SR@1 85.3 vs. 3.3; AUC@1 0.94 vs. 0.15), and is the only method with non-zero results on Satellite (SR@1=34.0% vs. 0.0% for MatchAnything and MINIMA-RoMa, 1.0% for MINIMA-LoFTR). The closest competitor is MINIMA-RoMa on Medical at larger thresholds (SR@5=100.0 vs. 97.4), but CrossFeat still leads on AUC@1 by a wide margin (0.96 vs. 0.46). Notably, CrossFeat achieves these gains with ~100× fewer parameters and ~5–10× faster inference than dense matchers, operating on a lightweight ~0.5–0.6M-parameter crossing network rather than large correlation-based architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

CrossFeat carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines of work: test-time descriptor adaptation (RootSIFT, Steerers, Affine Steerers) and cross-modal matching (MINIMA, MatchAnything, XoFTR). Prior test-time methods target geometric invariance within a single modality, while prior cross-modal methods either retrain descriptors per modality pair or rely on large pretrained models. CrossFeat repurposes the test-time adaptation paradigm for the appearance gap between modalities, introducing an architecturally explicit geometry-appearance disentanglement (VAE-style appearance head + deterministic geometry code) so that a lightweight FiLM-conditioned MLP modifies only appearance while preserving the geometric structure of any existing descriptor. The training objective combines gradient-reversal modality invariance, InfoNCE contrastive discrimination, and a modality-conditioned variational prior, drawing on domain-adversarial and VAE literature but applying them to the descriptor-space crossing problem for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: any off-the-shelf descriptor (SIFT, SuperPoint, DISK, ALIKED) can be made cross-modal with a ~0.5M-parameter network trained in 1–3 hours, achieving competitive or superior results to dense matchers (MINIMA-RoMa, MatchAnything) at ~100× fewer parameters and 5–10× faster inference. This offers a simple, modular alternative to the current trend of ever-larger cross-modal pretrained models. A key limitation is that the modality pair must be known at inference time, and the TTA step can reduce precision when initial inliers are sparse (as on the satellite task). Open questions include whether the modality gap can be estimated automatically from descriptors or image statistics, and whether the disentanglement generalizes to continuous modality transitions rather than discrete pairs.
