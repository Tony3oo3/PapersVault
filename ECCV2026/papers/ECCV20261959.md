## Problem

Existing interactive 3D world generation methods reconstruct static geometry and appearance but leave scene-scale environmental dynamics—clouds, water, smoke—entirely unmodeled. Modeling such dynamics interactively is difficult because motion must remain globally coherent as the scene progressively expands, while still supporting low-latency user feedback within seconds rather than minutes of optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261959/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single RGB image plus user-provided camera poses, text prompts for semantic expansion, and sparse motion seed points with directional hints (segmented via SAM) as input. It produces a progressively expanding 4D Gaussian Splatting scene with a learned global motion field, rendered as temporally coherent looping 4D video sequences. Each interactive expansion step completes in ~12 seconds (9 s scene generation + 3 s motion alignment and field update) on a single RTX 5090, enabling real-time user feedback during world construction.

## Method

LivingWorld represents environmental dynamics as a continuous Eulerian motion field F_θ: R³→R³ parameterized by a multiresolution hash encoding and lightweight MLP, decoupling motion from individual Gaussian primitives. For each newly generated view, a pretrained Eulerian flow predictor estimates a 2D velocity field, which is lifted to sparse 3D scene-flow samples via depth unprojection. A geometry-aware alignment module then reprojects these samples into previously generated views and solves a closed-form rotation (Kabsch/SVD) and scale to resolve directional and magnitude ambiguities, consolidating all views into a globally consistent motion representation. During rendering, Gaussians are advected bidirectionally (forward and backward) along the learned field with a linear opacity scheduler, producing temporally stable looping dynamics without any video-based refinement or per-Gaussian optimization.

## Results

On a 60-scene benchmark (clouds, water, smoke/fog, fire), LivingWorld achieves the highest PhysReal score (0.655) and Aesthetic Quality (0.639), with near-best Motion Smoothness (0.995) and Temporal Flickering (0.989) among all methods. Its runtime of 12 seconds per expansion step is 12×–300× faster than all baselines (140 s for Veo 3.1 up to 3,580 s for PerpetualWonder). In a 2AFC human preference study, LivingWorld is preferred over every baseline on all four VBench criteria, with win rates of 82–92% against PerpetualWonder and 66–78% against the video-generation models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three video-generation baselines, LivingWorld wins on Aesthetic Quality, Motion Smoothness, Temporal Flickering, PhysReal, and runtime, but trails Veo 3.1 on frame-level Imaging Quality (0.673 vs. 0.694) and CogVideoX by a narrow margin (0.673 vs. 0.677). Versus the 4D-scene baselines, LivingWorld outperforms 4DGS-Cinemagraphy on Imaging, Aesthetic, Flicker, PhysReal, and runtime (12 s vs. 1,980 s), while 4DGS-Cinemagraphy edges it out by 0.001 on Motion Smoothness (0.996 vs. 0.995). Against PerpetualWonder, LivingWorld dominates on every metric, with the largest gaps in PhysReal (0.655 vs. 0.554) and runtime (12 s vs. 3,580 s). In the alignment-module comparison (Table 3), LivingWorld's geometry-aware alignment achieves the best global consistency (cosine 0.91, magnitude ratio 0.84) and lowest flow-magnitude variance (FMV 0.29) in 12.1 s, whereas the optimization-based 3D-MOM from 4DGS-Cinemagraphy requires 600 s and yields substantially weaker global metrics (cosine 0.66, FMV 1.66). In the 2AFC study, LivingWorld is preferred over all five baselines on every criterion, with the strongest margins against PerpetualWonder (82–92% win rate) and the smallest against Veo 3.1 (58–75%), reflecting Veo's superior per-frame visual fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: interactive 3D scene expansion (WonderWorld), Eulerian motion field estimation for image animation (Holynski et al., 3D Cinemagraphy), and 4D scene generation with dynamic Gaussians (4DGS-Cinemagraphy, PerpetualWonder). Its key conceptual departure is rejecting the dominant video-driven optimization paradigm for 4D motion recovery: rather than synthesizing multi-view videos and then fitting a dynamic representation to them, LivingWorld directly constructs a continuous 3D Eulerian motion field via a multiresolution hash encoding and aligns it across expanding views using a closed-form Kabsch rotation/scale solution. The scene generation backbone is WonderWorld's render-refine-repeat pipeline, but the motion component is entirely new, replacing per-Gaussian Lagrangian deformation with a global spatially-indexed velocity field that decouples motion cost from scene complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12-second per-expansion-step runtime (vs. 140–3580 s for baselines) makes interactive 4D world generation with environmental dynamics practically feasible for the first time, opening the door to real-time creative tools and simulation environments where users can iteratively shape both geometry and motion. The hash-based motion field is a clean, scalable representation that could be adopted beyond this specific pipeline for any task requiring spatially-indexed continuous motion queries. However, the framework is scoped to fluid-like environmental dynamics (clouds, water, smoke, fire); integrating articulated object motion, human motion, or interacting multi-body dynamics remains open, as does handling non-rigid scene deformation during expansion. The evaluation, while thorough on its 60-scene benchmark, compares against heterogeneous baselines (video generators vs. 4D scene methods), making direct quality comparisons somewhat indirect.
