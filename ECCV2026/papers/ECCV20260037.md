## Problem

Disability is common yet almost entirely absent from vision-side controlled evaluations of VLM behavior, and prior counterfactual audits focus on gender, race, or coarse physical attributes while rarely isolating limb deficiencies as the visual factor of interest. The paper asks whether a VLM changes its judgment or wording simply because a visible limb-deficiency cue (e.g., a prosthesis or residual limb) is present, even when the prompt is unrelated to physical ability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260037/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation unit consists of a single RGB image (never both depictions jointly) paired with one text prompt from a fixed bank of 48 prompts spanning six socially consequential topics (competence, leadership, teamwork, professionalism, communication, hiring) plus background-content control prompts. The model produces three output forms: Yes/No/Uncertain decisions, 1-5 ordinal ratings, and open-ended free-form text. All prompts are screened to exclude any wording that directly asks about physical ability, and the paired shift is defined as Δ = (counterfactual − original), so negative values indicate higher ratings or more affirmative judgments for the limb-deficiency depiction.

## Method

The benchmark constructs 462 matched counterfactual image pairs (924 images, 22,176 queries) by starting from licensed real-world photos of individuals with limb deficiencies (LDPose, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), annotating limb-deficiency cues with segmentation masks, and using Qwen-ImageEdit to remove only the localized limb cue while preserving identity, background, and activity; edits are restricted to the masked region and verified by human quality control plus a blinded spot-the-edit test (<10% detection). Both depictions in each pair are then queried separately with identical prompts, and outputs are compared within the pair to attribute any drift to the single controlled visual substitution. Evaluation covers six model families (Qwen3-VL, DeepSeek-VL2, Gemma-3, Ministral-3, GPT-5, Gemini-2.5) using structured metrics (mean rating shift with 95% CI, two-sided sign test, TVD for Yes/No/Uncertain drift) and open-ended metrics (Jaccard/BERTScore text similarity, NLI entailment/neutral/contradiction rates, length shift, and positive/negative regard shifts), with control-prompt exact-match consistency reported as a noise floor.

## Results

Across 18 model variants, 17 of 18 show a statistically significant negative mean rating shift (higher 1–5 ratings when the limb-deficiency cue is visible), with Gemini-2.5 showing the largest shift (Δ = −0.404, p = 1.64×10⁻⁹) and GPT-5 a smaller but significant shift (Δ = −0.089, p = 3.52×10⁻³). Decision drift (TVD) ranges from 1.84 pp (GPT-5) to 13.53 pp (DeepSeek-VL2), with the shift concentrated in Yes→Uncertain rather than Yes→No. For open-ended responses, most models show negative Δ Positive (more positive-regard language for cue-visible depictions), e.g., DeepSeek-VL2 (−3.66 pp) and Gemini-2.5 (−1.18 pp), while GPT-5 shows near-zero Δ Positive but a consistent increase in negative-regard language (Δ Neg = −0.53 pp). Only one variant (Ministral-3 3B-Reasoning, Δ = +0.071) reverses the rating direction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper, so the 'baselines' are the 18 VLM variants evaluated under an identical paired protocol. Proprietary models show smaller but still significant shifts: GPT-5 has the lowest TVD (1.84 pp) and smallest rating shift (−0.089), while Gemini-2.5 shows a moderate TVD (3.86 pp) but the largest rating shift (−0.404). Among open-weight models, DeepSeek-VL2 exhibits the most extreme decision drift (TVD = 13.53 pp, ΔYes = −11.90 pp), followed by Ministral-3 8B-Instruct (TVD = 10.20 pp) and Gemma-3 27B-it (TVD = 11.52 pp, though its very low control consistency of 0.235 inflates structured noise). Within the Qwen3-VL family, Instruct variants are consistently more sensitive than Thinking variants in both rating and regard shifts, suggesting alignment style modulates cue sensitivity. For open-ended responses, GPT-5 is an outlier: its BERTScore F1 is markedly lower (37.0 vs. ~90 for most open-weight models), indicating larger paired answer drift, yet its NLI profile shows higher entailment and lower contradiction, suggesting reframing rather than semantic reversal. The same-cue edit control confirms the main rating shift is not an artifact of the editing pipeline (Δ Rating = −0.151 vs. −0.152), and the single-person subset (n=392) actually shows a stronger shift (−0.169) than the full set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the counterfactual/matched-image evaluation paradigm established by PAIRS, SocialCounterfactuals, and Howard et al. to a previously unexamined axis: limb-deficiency visibility. Prior counterfactual VLM audits isolate gender, race, or coarse physical attributes, while disability-focused evaluations (VizWiz-style benchmarks, AccessEval, Panda et al.) typically use uncontrolled image comparisons or treat disability as a broad label. INCLUSIVECFIMAGEBIAS departs from both lines by constructing tightly controlled pairs where only a localized limb cue (prosthesis or residual limb) changes, enabling direct attribution of output drift to that single visual substitution. The editing pipeline leverages Qwen-ImageEdit with masked-region constraints, building on recent work on counterfactual image generation reliability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Disability affects roughly 16% of the global population, yet limb-deficiency visibility has been almost entirely absent from vision-side controlled VLM evaluations; this benchmark fills that gap with a concrete, scalable regression test for disability-facing deployment. The finding that cues push ratings upward (over-accommodation) rather than downward challenges the common 'disability penalty' intuition and suggests alignment heuristics may introduce a different, subtler form of criterion drift. Key limitations include male-skewed demographics, English-only prompts, restriction to limb deficiencies, and noise from diffusion-based editing. Open questions include whether paired-consistency training or guardrails can eliminate depiction-conditioned instability, and whether the pattern generalizes to other disability types and non-English settings.
