## Problem

In flow matching, the marginal velocity field is an average over all sample-wise velocities passing through a given intermediate state. When these velocities conflict (pointing toward different target modes), the averaged direction can steer trajectories into low-density regions, producing blurry or degraded outputs. Prior solutions require costly retraining with modified architectures or objectives, preventing direct use of strong off-the-shelf flow models.

## Input / Output

FDS operates as a plug-and-play inference-time module that wraps around any pre-trained flow model and standard ODE solver without modifying either. It consumes the same inputs as the base model (Gaussian noise, class labels, text prompts, or degraded images) and produces the same outputs (generated or restored images at the target resolution, e.g., 256×256 for ImageNet/CIFAR-10). The only additional computation is evaluating the divergence of the velocity field via Hutchinson's trace estimator at each refinement step, with a default configuration of N=M=1 that adds roughly 1.5× wall-clock overhead over the base solver.

## Method

The core theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes that the irreducible CFM residual—the mean-squared discrepancy between the marginal velocity and individual sample-wise velocities—admits a closed-form expression in terms of the divergence of the marginal velocity field, up to known schedule-dependent coefficients. This makes local velocity ambiguity computable at inference time using only the pre-trained model, via Hutchinson's trace estimator. FDS exploits this signal through zero-order spatial refinement: at each timestep t > T_trunc (default 0.5), it generates M random Gaussian perturbations of the current state, evaluates the divergence surrogate at each candidate, and selects the one with minimum divergence as the refined state before the next solver step. Unlike higher-order solvers that reduce temporal integration error, FDS performs a spatial intervention at fixed time, relocating the trajectory away from high-ambiguity regions. The method is solver-agnostic (Euler, Heun, UniPC, RK4) and guidance-agnostic (CFG, CFG-Zero*, TFG), requiring no external signals or reward models.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with JiT-L/16 and the Heun solver (99 NFEs), FDS reduces FID from 2.713 to 2.496, outperforming the compute-matched baseline (Heun with 153 NFEs, FID 2.886). On CIFAR-10 with EDM (conditional) and Heun, FID drops from 1.904 to 1.786, again beating the compute-matched 153-NFE baseline (FID 1.910). FDS consistently improves FID across all four model/solver configurations in Table 1, with the largest absolute gain (0.35 FID) on ImageNet JiT-B/16 with Euler (4.151 → 3.799). In text-to-image generation, FDS boosts ImageReward from 82.36 to 89.33 on SD3-Medium and from 92.95 to 94.63 on FLUX.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against compute-matched baselines (same wall-clock time via more NFEs), FDS wins on FID in every configuration: e.g., Euler+FDS (50 NFEs, FID 2.319) beats Euler† (77 NFEs, FID 2.515) on CIFAR-10 EDM Cond., and Heun+FDS (99 NFEs, FID 2.496) beats Heun† (153 NFEs, FID 2.886) on ImageNet JiT-L/16. Against training-based crossing-resolution methods, EDM+FDS (FID 2.44) substantially outperforms both HRF (FID 4.96) and VRFM (FID 5.27) on CIFAR-10 in a parameter-matched setup, suggesting that modeling the full multi-modal velocity distribution during training introduces a capacity bottleneck that FDS avoids entirely. On text-to-image, FDS improves ImageReward by +6.97 on SD3-Medium (82.36→89.33) and +1.68 on FLUX.1 (92.95→94.63), with consistent gains in CLIP score. For inverse problems, TFG+FDS reduces FID by 0.85 on deblurring (64.02→63.17) and 2.40 on 4× super-resolution (65.54→63.14), while also lowering LPIPS. FDS also stacks additively with advanced inference methods: it improves UniPC FID from 6.21 to 5.59, RK4 FID from 3.77 to 3.45, and CFG-Zero* FID from 4.10 to 3.78, confirming its plug-and-play compatibility without conflicting with existing enhancements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023), where the marginal velocity field is the conditional mean of sample-wise velocities. It departs from two prior lines of work: (1) training-based methods like HRF and VRFM that redesign the objective to model the full multi-modal velocity distribution at crossings, and (2) inference-time solvers (UniPC, DPM-Solver, higher-order ODE methods) that reduce temporal discretization error. FDS instead introduces a spatial refinement of the intermediate state at a fixed timestep, using the divergence of the learned velocity field as a data-free proxy for the unobservable local discrepancy—a theoretical link (Theorem 1) not previously established in the flow matching literature. The zero-order perturbation mechanism is inspired by recent inference-time scaling work (Ma et al., 2025), but applied here to a fundamentally different objective: minimizing local velocity ambiguity rather than maximizing a reward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FDS provides a clean, theoretically grounded, and practically lightweight (N=M=1 suffices) way to improve flow-based generation without retraining, making it immediately applicable to large off-the-shelf models like SD3-Medium and FLUX.1. The key open question is whether the divergence-based signal remains informative at very high NFEs or in video generation where trajectory crossings may be more complex. A limitation is that the method still incurs extra forward passes (roughly 1.5× wall-clock), and the divergence surrogate, while empirically reliable, is an approximation of the true discrepancy. Broader implications include whether similar spatial-refinement principles can be extended to other generative paradigms (score-based diffusion, normalizing flows) where analogous multi-modality issues arise.
