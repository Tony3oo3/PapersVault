## Problem

Existing test-time scaling methods for robotic manipulation only scale the action space while ignoring the intermediate reasoning that guides action prediction, causing a planning-execution misalignment. Additionally, because embodied tasks are inherently sequential and long-horizon, these methods fail to exploit historical trajectory context or iterative feedback, treating each timestep as an independent decision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261721/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a natural-language task instruction, the current RGB observation (front and/or wrist camera), and a sliding-window history buffer of the most recent K reasoning-action pairs and observations. It outputs a selected robot action (discretized into 256 bins per continuous dimension, following RT-2) paired with its intermediate reasoning trace, whose format varies by the underlying VLA model (textual subtasks for Embodied-R1, bounding boxes and motion primitives for E-CoT, spatial trajectory keypoints for MolmoAct, or subtask plans for π0.5). The framework is model-agnostic and plug-and-play, wrapping around any VLA that produces intermediate reasoning.

## Method

E-TTS performs reasoning-action joint sampling, drawing M reasoning candidates and N actions per reasoning to form structured joint pairs, then scores each pair via a dual-verifier mechanism: a zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-7B reasoning verifier assesses semantic coherence and groundedness, while a LLaVA-7B action verifier (trained on 90k paired good/bad rollouts with a graded Bradley-Terry objective) evaluates physical feasibility. Both verifiers receive the history buffer as context to model temporal dependencies. An adaptive ε-greedy selection with a confidence threshold η picks the best pair or triggers a feedback-guided refinement loop, in which the reasoning verifier generates corrective textual feedback that is concatenated to the instruction to condition the next sampling round. The joint score is the product of normalized reasoning and action scores, ensuring only pairs with both coherent reasoning and feasible actions are selected, and all modules are independently configurable.

## Results

E-TTS achieves a maximum absolute improvement of 33.14% in simulation (E-CoT on SimplerEnv WidowX: 6.67% → 39.81% average success rate) and 26.62% in real-world settings (MolmoAct-Finetuned: 22.13% → 48.75%), with an average gain of 13.52% across all evaluated configurations. On the LIBERO benchmark, MolmoAct+Ours reaches 90.78% average success rate (vs. 85.57% baseline), and on SimplerEnv Google Robot it achieves 80.00% (Visual) and 77.77% (Variant) success. The framework requires no additional expert data or retraining, and the optimal scaling configuration yields a 150% relative success-rate improvement with only a 46.6% latency increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the E-CoT baseline (6.67% avg), E-TTS achieves a 33.14-point absolute gain to 39.81% on SimplerEnv WidowX. Compared to the naive test-time scaling baseline (23.61%), E-TTS gains 16.2 points, demonstrating that history-aware verification, joint scoring, and feedback refinement are critical beyond simple multi-sample selection. Against RoboMonkey (26.38%), E-TTS achieves a 50.91% relative improvement (13.43 points absolute) while incurring only a 7.8% increase in execution time. On SimplerEnv Google Robot, MolmoAct+Ours outperforms the MolmoAct baseline (71.05% → 80.00% Visual; 65.68% → 77.77% Variant) and the naive TTS variant (74.36% → 80.00%). On LIBERO, MolmoAct+Ours reaches 90.78% average, surpassing both the MolmoAct baseline (85.57%) and the naive TTS variant (81.75%, which actually degrades performance on the Goal suite from 87.60% to 70.00%). On VLAbench, π0.5+Ours improves average success rates across all five reported tracks (e.g., Track 1: 0.39 → 0.42; Track 6: 0.25 → 0.26), with the largest gains on tasks requiring multi-step spatial reasoning such as select_poker (0.46 → 0.63).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior embodied test-time scaling methods (RoboMonkey, RoVer, TACO, Hume) that scale only the action space, arguing instead that reasoning and action must be jointly sampled and scored as coupled units. It also introduces two embodied-specific mechanisms absent from open-loop TTS: a sliding-window history buffer for temporal context and a closed-loop feedback-guided refinement loop that re-conditions the policy on its own failures. Conceptually, it repurposes the parallel-sampling + verifier-selection paradigm from LLM test-time scaling (e.g., Large Language Monkeys, Self-Refine) and adapts it to sequential, history-dependent robotic manipulation, while the action verifier's Bradley-Terry preference training draws on the DPO/RLHF lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of E-TTS makes it immediately applicable to a wide range of existing VLA models, which is practically valuable given the high cost of collecting new expert demonstrations. The consistent gains across four architectures, six environments, and three embodiments strengthen the generality claim, and the observed emergent self-correction in real-world rollouts hints at broader robustness benefits. Open questions remain around the latency ceiling for latency-sensitive tasks, the optimal allocation of compute between reasoning and action scaling under tighter budgets, and whether the history buffer and feedback loop scale gracefully to much longer horizons or multi-robot settings.
