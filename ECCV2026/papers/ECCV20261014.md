## Problem

Cryo-ET segmentation networks routinely produce voxel-accurate but topologically broken masks—fragmented membranes, merged organelles, collapsed cavities—because existing topology-aware losses only encourage correct topology through gradient pressure rather than structurally enforcing it. No matter how well-tuned, a soft penalty cannot guarantee that violations are eliminated, and violations that survive training persist at test time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a cryo-ET tomogram as 128³ crops, decomposed into three orthogonal 2D slice stacks (xy, xz, yz) that are each encoded by a shared SAM ViT-B backbone. It outputs a per-class segmentation mask together with a repair certificate recording the number of projection iterations, the exact set of edited voxel positions, and the spatial edit sparsity (fraction of voxels changed). The topology projection operates on a downsampled grid (s=2) for dimensions d∈{0,2}, and at inference a learned prior head supplies the correction target without requiring ground-truth topology.

## Method

TopoFuse's central contribution is a Differentiable Topology Projection layer that reframes topology enforcement as a forward-pass operator rather than a training loss. At each iteration, it computes the persistence diagram of the current downsampled prediction, finds the optimal bottleneck matching to a target diagram, identifies the critical voxels of unmatched features, and applies sparse logit edits only at those locations (typically 3.1% of voxels) until the bottleneck distance falls below a threshold or a maximum of 5 steps is reached. The tri-planar SAM encoder handles cryo-ET anisotropy by processing each axis-aligned slice stack independently, and a FiLM-conditioned fusion layer adaptively weights the three views based on a topology descriptor of the current prediction. At inference, a topology prior head predicts Betti counts and persistence budgets from fused features, constructing a pseudo-diagram target that replaces the oracle, making the system fully self-contained. During training, projection steps are unrolled with gradients flowing through PH via the critical-cell rule, and an auxiliary loss on pre-projection logits maintains a direct gradient path to the encoder.

## Results

Across three cryo-ET benchmarks (EMD-0506, EMPIAR-10499, EMPIAR-10045), TopoFuse with the learned prior achieves a mean Dice of 84.9 and mean Betti Number Error (BE₀) of 1.24, compared to 80.3 Dice and 2.68 BE₀ for the strongest soft-loss baseline (BettiMatching). This corresponds to a 54% reduction in BE₀ (p < 0.001, paired Wilcoxon) and a 4.6 pp Dice gain, while editing only 3.1% of voxels (median spatial sparsity). With oracle topology targets, TopoFuse reaches 85.6 Dice and 0.86 BE₀, and the 0.35 BE₀ gap between learned and oracle priors is attributed entirely to prior-head prediction error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoFuse (learned prior) outperforms all six baselines on both Dice and BE₀ across all three benchmarks. Against the strongest topology-loss baseline, BettiMatching [20], TopoFuse gains +4.6 pp Dice (84.9 vs. 80.3) and reduces BE₀ by 54% (1.24 vs. 2.68), with the gap widening on the most anisotropic dataset (EMPIAR-10045: +5.0 pp Dice, BE₀ 1.58 vs. 3.34). The non-differentiable post-hoc method TopoPost achieves the lowest BE₀ among baselines (2.55) but still trails TopoFuse by 51% on BE₀ and 6.4 pp on Dice. Standard architectures without topology awareness (3D U-Net, nnU-Net, CryoSAM) show the largest BE₀ values (3.54–4.39), confirming that voxel-level objectives alone leave topological violations unaddressed. The plain tri-planar backbone (no topology) sits at 75.3 Dice / 3.41 BE₀, and adding topology losses incrementally (clDice → HuTopo → BettiMatching) yields diminishing returns, whereas the projection operator produces a step-change improvement. On the synthetic SYN benchmark at SNR=0.10, TopoFuse achieves 84% exact topology recovery (BE₀=0) versus 61% for BettiMatching and 52% for the plain backbone, with the advantage widening at lower SNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of topology-aware segmentation, where persistent homology enters as a soft loss term (clDice, Hu et al., BettiMatching) that encourages topologically correct outputs through gradient pressure. Building on the differentiability result of Bruel-Gabrielsson et al. [4] (gradients of PH are non-zero only at critical voxels), the authors repurpose this mechanism not for loss computation but for identifying exactly which voxels to edit, reframing topology as a forward-pass projection operator that structurally enforces a target diagram. The tri-planar SAM encoder and FiLM fusion are borrowed from the broader SAM-adaptation and conditional-modulation literature but are specifically motivated by cryo-ET's missing-wedge anisotropy, and the projection design draws on implicit-differentiation and post-hoc-correction methods (Clough et al.) while adding sparsity guarantees and a repair certificate absent from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from topology-as-loss to topology-as-forward-pass-operator is a clean architectural principle that could generalize to any domain where persistent-homology targets are specifiable (vascular, neuronal, cardiac imaging), and the repair certificate provides a practical audit trail that gradient-regularised methods fundamentally cannot offer. Key open questions include: enforcement of d=1 tunnels (currently excluded due to O(n^1.5) cost), uncertainty-aware prior estimation to close the 0.35 BE0 gap between learned and oracle targets, and cross-tile consistency for whole-tomogram processing. The 4% non-convergence rate and the over-correction failure mode (wrong Betti count from the prior head) are honest limitations that bound the method's reliability in the most anisotropic, thin-membrane cases.
