## Problem

Existing LoRA-based and prompt-based continual learning methods suffer from two compounding limitations: task-specific adapters cause unbounded parameter growth as the task sequence lengthens, and replay buffers or stored feature representations introduce substantial memory overhead and privacy risks. No prior method simultaneously achieves rehearsal-free learning, constant inference cost, end-to-end optimization, and bounded memory across arbitrarily long task sequences.

## Input / Output

COLA takes standard RGB images (224×224) from class-incremental benchmarks (ImageNet-R, ImageNet-A, CUB200) and outputs a class label from the cumulative set of all classes encountered so far. The frozen ViT-B/16 backbone processes each image, and a classification head spanning all previously seen classes produces the final prediction. No task identifier or prompt retrieval is needed at inference time; the single consolidated LoRA adapter handles all tasks uniformly.

## Method

COLA inserts a single shared low-rank adapter (rank 10) on the query and value projections of every ViT attention layer, reused across all tasks without per-task parameter expansion. The key mechanism is a soft-orthogonal projection matrix P = I − α·Σ_global/‖Σ_global‖_F, constructed from a global covariance that accumulates the dominant eigenstructure of all prior task features via an Oja-inspired streaming update (per-batch task covariance updated with decay, consolidated into the global covariance every K batches). Projected features are combined with the original low-rank features through a residual scaling factor γ, balancing stability (orthogonal guidance away from prior subspaces) and plasticity (task-specific expressiveness). At task transitions, the task covariance is folded into the global covariance and reset, while the LoRA parameters continue training on the new task—no SVD, no stored exemplars, and no expanding adapter bank are required.

## Results

On ImageNet-R (25 tasks), COLA achieves 76.01% Acc and 82.11% AAA, outperforming the strongest baselines CoSO (77.24% AAA) by 4.87% and SD-LoRA (74.71% AAA) by 7.40%. On ImageNet-A (10 tasks), COLA reaches 59.20% Acc and 67.46% AAA, surpassing SD-LoRA (57.76% Acc) and CoSO (56.43% Acc). On CUB200 (10 tasks), COLA attains 74.51% Acc and 84.38% AAA, the highest among all compared methods. COLA also achieves the lowest forward-pass cost (32.22 GFLOPs) with 0.37M learnable parameters and zero stored features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COLA outperforms all 10 baselines on every dataset and metric reported. The closest competitors are CoSO and SD-LoRA: on ImageNet-R (25 tasks), COLA's 82.11% AAA exceeds CoSO's 77.24% by 4.87 points and SD-LoRA's 74.71% by 7.40 points, a gap that widens with task length (CoSO drops from 83.12% to 77.24% AAA between 10 and 25 tasks, while COLA stays within 1.3 points). On ImageNet-A, COLA leads SD-LoRA by 1.44% Acc and CoSO by 2.77% Acc; on CUB200, the margins are 1.61% over SD-LoRA and 2.28% over CoSO. Prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, HiDe-Prompt) trail COLA by 3–10% Acc across all benchmarks, and RanPAC, while competitive on AAA for shorter sequences, degrades sharply at 25 tasks (74.11% vs. COLA's 82.11%). COLA also achieves the lowest GFLOPs (32.22 vs. 35.12 for InfLoRA/SD-LoRA/CoSO) with no stored features, whereas HiDe-Prompt stores 0.15M features and InfLoRA stores 0.10M.

## Relation to Prior Work

COLA builds directly on LoRA's low-rank adaptation mechanism (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) and repurposes Oja's rule (1982) as an online covariance update for continual learning. It departs from the dominant paradigm in CL-PEFT methods: prompt-based approaches (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) that maintain expanding prompt pools, and LoRA-based methods (InfLoRA, SD-LoRA, PLAN) that accumulate task-specific adapters, both of which suffer unbounded parameter or memory growth. Unlike subspace-orthogonality methods (OGD, GPM, CoSO) that enforce hard projection constraints via per-task SVD, COLA replaces strict orthogonality with a soft, covariance-guided projection that operates on a single fixed-size adapter and a single global covariance summary updated online, eliminating both per-task parameter storage and exemplar replay.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COLA is the first method to simultaneously satisfy all four desirable properties (rehearsal-free, inference-efficient, end-to-end, memory-efficient) identified in the paper's Table 1, which is practically important for deployment in resource-constrained or privacy-sensitive settings where storing exemplars or growing parameters is infeasible. The 4.8% AAA improvement over CoSO on 25-task ImageNet-R demonstrates that the soft-projection approach scales better than hard SVD-based orthogonality over long sequences. However, evaluation is limited to 200-class benchmarks and ViT/DeiT backbones; the authors themselves note that extension to vision-language models (CLIP) and non-uniform task distributions (Gaussian task patterns) remains open, and the modest GFLOPs advantage (32.22 vs. 35.12) suggests the computational gains are secondary to the architectural simplicity.
