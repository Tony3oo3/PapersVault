## Problem

Existing controllable remote sensing image generation methods suffer from two fundamental bottlenecks: semantic conflict, where dominant global concepts in text prompts overshadow fine-grained local details due to feature entanglement in Euclidean embedding space, and physical inconsistency, where purely data-driven diffusion models lack explicit frequency-domain constraints, leading to structural collapse and radiometric distortion. These issues are particularly acute for RS imagery due to its overhead perspective, multi-level scene complexity, and strict electromagnetic scattering properties.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt combined with one or more spatial conditions (HED edge maps, MLSD line segments, depth maps, sketches, segmentation masks, road maps) and optionally a cross-modal image (optical or SAR) at 512×512 resolution, producing either an optical or SAR remote sensing image. Multi-condition combinations (e.g., Sketch+Depth, HED+Segmentation) are supported simultaneously, and cross-modal generation allows producing SAR from optical input or vice versa. Training proceeds in two stages: 2 epochs of text-to-image pre-training on ~1.2M samples followed by 5 epochs of fine-tuning on 66K controllable multi-modal pairs, all at 512×512 with a global batch size of 32.

## Method

SGP² is a DiT-based diffusion framework that introduces three synergistic modules aligned to the coarse-to-fine denoising progression. The Grassmann Miner decomposes text embeddings via SVD into orthogonal global (Ug) and detail (Ud) subspaces on the Grassmann manifold, then evolves them along timestep-parameterized geodesic trajectories with complementary sin/cos scheduling so that semantic focus shifts from global layout to fine-grained details as denoising progresses. The Physical Prior Expert employs a MixRouter that dynamically allocates alignment weights between multi-level DINOv3 features and DiT layers based on the current timestep—prioritizing deep semantic layers early and shallow structural layers late—replacing rigid single-level REPA-style alignment. A frequency-aware Spectral Loss applies FFT to jointly constrain amplitude (log-smoothed L1) and phase (normalized real/imaginary error) in the frequency domain, with a temporal weight that shifts emphasis from global radiometric consistency to fine-grained structural fidelity. The total training objective combines the standard DiT denoising loss with the alignment and spectral losses, trained with CAME-8bit in bf16 precision.

## Results

On the RSICD testset for text-to-optical image generation, SGP² achieves IS=21.23, FID=44.17, CLIP Score=24.58, and Overall Accuracy=86.27, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On the DFC25 valset for text-to-SAR generation, SGP² reaches SSIM=0.569 and PSNR=23.14, surpassing the fine-tuned JoDI baseline by +10.9% SSIM and +3.16% PSNR. For single-condition controllable generation (e.g., HED edges), SGP² achieves SSIM=0.59 for optical and 0.55 for SAR, consistently leading over CRS-Diff and JoDI(FT) across all six condition types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGP² outperforms all RS-specific baselines on the RSICD testset: it improves FID by 6.55 over CRS-Diff (44.17 vs. 50.72), by 8.98 over Text2Earth (44.17 vs. 53.15), and by 13.07 over AeroGen(FT) (44.17 vs. 58.12), while also leading on IS, CLIP Score, and OA. Against the strongest general-purpose baseline JoDI(FT), SGP² gains +17.7% IS, −22.8% FID, +14.5% CLIP Score, and +27.6% OA, demonstrating that the Grassmann Miner and Physical Prior Expert provide substantial benefits over a fine-tuned DiT backbone alone. The only metric where SGP² does not lead is CLIP Score vs. InstancedDiff (24.58 vs. 24.70), but InstancedDiff's FID is far worse (138.60 vs. 44.17) because its outputs lack a proper overhead RS perspective. For SAR generation on DFC25, SGP² beats JoDI(FT) on SSIM (0.569 vs. 0.513), PSNR (23.14 vs. 22.43), ENL (5.31 vs. 5.12), and RaRes (1.76 vs. 1.80), confirming cross-modal transfer from optical pre-trained VFMs. In single- and multi-condition controllable generation (Tables 4–5), SGP² consistently leads over CRS-Diff, JoDI(FT), CtrlNet, and U-CtrlNet across all condition types (HED, MLSD, depth, sketch, segmentation, road) for both optical and SAR modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the REPA line of work (Yu et al., 2025) from single-level high-semantic feature alignment to a multi-level, time-aware alignment paradigm using a dynamic MixRouter that shifts emphasis from deep VFM layers (global semantics) to shallow layers (fine structure) across denoising steps. For text conditioning, it departs from the standard Euclidean treatment of text embeddings (as in ToRA, DATE) by projecting them onto the Grassmann Manifold via SVD and evolving them along geodesic trajectories, a geometric approach previously explored in deep network design (Huang et al., 2018) but not applied to diffusion text conditioning. The frequency-domain Spectral Loss (joint amplitude and phase constraints via FFT) is a novel addition to the REPA-style alignment objective, and the multi-modal (optical + SAR) controllable generation setting extends prior single-modality controllable RS generation (CRS-Diff, AeroGen) to cross-sensor simulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MMEarth-1.5M dataset with strictly co-registered optical-SAR pairs fills a genuine gap for cross-modal RS generation research, and the coarse-to-fine alignment principle (matching semantic focus and physical prior intensity to denoising stage) offers a reusable design pattern for other conditional generation tasks. However, the evaluation is limited to 512×512 resolution and two modalities, and the Grassmann Miner adds non-trivial computational overhead (SVD per forward pass) whose scaling behavior at higher resolutions or longer sequences remains untested. Open questions include whether the manifold-based text conditioning generalizes beyond RS to other structured domains, whether the spectral loss transfers to video or 3D generation, and whether the multi-modal framework can extend to hyperspectral or LiDAR modalities.
