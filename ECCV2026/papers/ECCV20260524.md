## Problem

Urban-scale VPR datasets exhibit a severe long-tailed geographic distribution (roughly 300:1 ratio between head and tail classes) that biases models toward frequently photographed locations while systematically failing in sparsely covered, visually diverse areas. Existing VPR methods overlook this imbalance, and their retrieval metrics (e.g., L2 distance) treat all feature distributions uniformly, further underweighting tail-class features at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260524/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query RGB image and a geo-tagged reference database partitioned into C square grid cells of side length M meters. A DINOv2 backbone extracts 768-dimensional descriptors from each image. In the mixed pipeline, a classifier first filters the full database (e.g., 41.2M images on SF-XL) down to ~450 candidate images from predicted geographic classes, after which a retrieval metric re-ranks those candidates to return the top-K matched images and their geographic coordinates.

## Method

DAPR is a model-agnostic plug-in framework with two modules. The Low-visit Bias (LB) loss rebalances training by coupling three mechanisms: inverse-frequency class weighting (w_c ∝ p̂_c^{-β}) to amplify tail-class gradients, prior-based logit adjustment (ν_c = -κ·log(p̂_c/(1-p̂_c))) to correct classifier bias, and Large Margin Cosine similarity to enforce angular separation; it is instantiated as L_lb^cls for classification and L_lb^rt (built on Multi-Similarity) for retrieval. The second module, a Multi-scale Distance Search Mechanism, replaces L2 retrieval with Characteristic Function Distance (CFD): features are mapped to the frequency domain via empirical characteristic functions evaluated at K stratified multi-scale frequency points, then decomposed into amplitude (distributional compactness) and phase (directional similarity) components. An adaptive reliability ratio r^(j) = Ā_q/Ā_j between query and candidate-class mean amplitudes modulates the amplitude-vs-phase weighting, enabling fair comparison between dense head-class clusters and dispersed tail-class features without requiring class labels at inference.

## Results

On the large-scale SF-XL benchmark, DAPR-M (mixed pipeline with DINOv2 backbone) achieves 89.7% R@1 on test v1 and 94.3% R@1 on test v2, surpassing the previous best mixed-pipeline baseline (D&C+CosPlace, 71.4%/87.6%) by +18.3% and +6.7% respectively, while running at 74 ms per query (over 60× faster than full-database retrieval). As a plug-in, the LB loss improves SALAD from 87.6% to 88.0% R@1 (v1) and BoQ from 83.7% to 88.8% R@1 (v1) on SF-XL, and yields consistent gains on MSLS (+2.5% R@1 for BoQ*) and Pitts30k (+0.4% R@1 for SALAD*). Tail-class R@1 improves by +1.47% (SF-XL) and up to +3.37% (Pitts30k, BoQ*), confirming the method targets the sparsely sampled locations where existing models fail most.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAPR-M with DINOv2 (89.7%/94.3% R@1) decisively outperforms all prior mixed-pipeline baselines: D&C+CosPlace (71.4%/87.6%) by +18.3%/+6.7%, and D&C classification-only (61.0%/79.1%) by +28.7%/+15.2%. Among retrieval-only methods, DAPR-M surpasses SALAD (87.6%/93.5%) by +2.1%/+0.8% and BoQ (83.7%/92.8%) by +6.0%/+1.5% on R@1, while also being 65× and 288× faster at inference. The LB loss as a plug-in improves CosPlace by +1.7% R@1 (v1) and D&C by +4.1% R@1 (v1), with larger gains at R@5 (+7.0% for D&C), indicating the benefit is most pronounced for tail classes. In the loss-function ablation (Table 6), LB Loss (89.7%/94.3%) beats Logit Adjustment (88.0%/93.3%) by +1.7%/+1.0% and Focal Loss (86.4%/94.0%) by +3.3%/+0.3% on R@1, confirming that coupling reweighting with logit adjustment outperforms addressing either failure mode alone. On small-scale benchmarks, BoQ* gains +2.5% R@1 on MSLS and +0.3% on Pitts30k over its original, while SALAD* gains +0.7% and +0.4% respectively, validating cross-dataset generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Divide&Classify (D&C) mixed-pipeline architecture by identifying a previously overlooked long-tailed geographic distribution problem in urban-scale VPR datasets and addressing it through a unified loss that combines inverse-frequency reweighting, prior-based logit adjustment (from Menon et al.), and Large Margin Cosine similarity (from CosFace) into a single plug-and-play module. The retrieval stage further departs from standard L2/cosine re-ranking by adopting Characteristic Function Distance from empirical statistics, decomposing feature similarity into amplitude (distributional compactness) and phase (directional) components with adaptive weighting. As a model-agnostic framework, it is positioned as orthogonal to backbone choice and aggregation strategy, demonstrated by consistent gains when grafted onto SALAD, BoQ, CosPlace, and D&C across multiple benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that tail-class geographic regions are simultaneously the most visually diverse and the least supervised exposes a compounding failure mode that undermines real-world VPR deployment, particularly in safety-critical applications like autonomous patrol in low-traffic zones. The plug-and-play nature of the LB loss and the label-free CFD metric make the framework immediately adoptable by existing VPR pipelines without architectural changes. An open question the authors flag is whether the long-tail phenomenon should be characterized at the feature level (intra-class coherence) rather than the sample-count level, which could reveal additional difficulty structure beyond frequency. The modest +1.7% gain from CFD over the loss alone also suggests that training-time rebalancing captures most of the available improvement, leaving the retrieval-stage distributional correction as a secondary but complementary contribution.
