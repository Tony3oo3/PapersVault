## Problem

Existing TAS dataset condensation methods rely on cVAE-based iterative latent optimization, which is computationally expensive (thousands of optimization steps per segment), produces over-smoothed reconstructions due to the unimodal Gaussian prior, and imposes rigid block-wise temporal structure by reusing a single latent code across consecutive frames. This paper asks whether condensation can be reformulated as a deterministic latent mapping problem that preserves fine-grained temporal dynamics without per-instance optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260976/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted I3D frame features (D-dimensional vectors per frame) along with per-frame conditioning variables: the action class label and the frame's normalized relative position within its segment. It produces a sparse set of K latent anchor codes per segment (K ≪ segment length ℓ) stored in the DDIM noise manifold, which are then interpolated in latent space and decoded via the DDIM reverse flow to reconstruct full frame-level features for downstream TAS training. Compression is applied exclusively along the temporal axis, with a target average of K̄=8 anchors per segment yielding compression ratios as low as 2.4% on Breakfast.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces cVAE iterative inversion with DDIM's deterministic forward flow: each anchor frame is mapped to its latent noise representation in a single forward pass (no optimization), and reconstruction proceeds via the deterministic reverse flow. Action segments are modeled as continuous trajectories in this latent space, with intermediate frames recovered through linear interpolation between anchor latents rather than nearest-neighbor inflation. An adaptive allocation strategy starts every segment at K=2 anchors and iteratively assigns one additional anchor to the segment with the highest mean reconstruction error until a global budget B is exhausted, concentrating representational capacity where temporal dynamics are most complex. The diffusion model itself is a lightweight ε-predictor conditioned on action label and normalized sequence position, trained with the standard denoising objective. This design eliminates the O(NS) optimization cost of GNI (S=10,000 steps) in favor of O(NK̄T) with small T (10–50), while latent-space interpolation preserves fine-grained temporal structure that feature-space interpolation or repeated-code decoding cannot capture.

## Results

On Breakfast with ASFormer, the adaptive variant (Ours) achieves 68.0% frame-wise accuracy and 71.3% Edit score, surpassing GNI (62.8% Acc) by 5.2 points while using only 24% of the original temporal data (ρ=0.24). On 50Salads with ASFormer, the fixed-allocation variant (Ours†) reaches 75.1% accuracy, within 2% of the full-data upper bound (77.1%) at a 1.4% storage ratio. On GTEA with ASFormer, Ours attains 73.4% Acc and 80.5% Edit, outperforming GNI (71.6% Acc, 76.4% Edit) and approaching the original-data performance of 74.3% Acc. Across all three datasets and both MSTCN and ASFormer backbones, both variants consistently outperform every baseline including GNI at both K̄=8 and K̄=64.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is GNI [5], the only prior TAS condensation method, which uses iterative cVAE inversion with 10K optimization steps. The proposed method decisively outperforms GNI across all three datasets and both backbones: on Breakfast+ASFormer, Ours achieves 68.0% Acc vs. GNI's 62.8% (+5.2 points) and 71.3% Edit vs. 66.3% (+5.0 points); on 50Salads+ASFormer, Ours† reaches 75.1% Acc vs. GNI's 70.5% (+4.6 points). Notably, increasing GNI's anchor budget from K̄=8 to K̄=64 (matching the overall compression ratio) yields no improvement and even degrades Edit score on GTEA (71.9%→66.9%), revealing an inherent expressiveness bottleneck in the cVAE latent space. In contrast, the proposed method at K̄=8 already surpasses GNI at K̄=64 on every metric. The adaptive variant (Ours) shows particular strength on segmental metrics (Edit, F1) for long, complex sequences like Breakfast, while the fixed variant (Ours†) edges ahead on raw accuracy for shorter datasets like 50Salads. Both variants remain within 2–3% of the full-data upper bound, confirming that the condensation preserves nearly all task-relevant information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly departs from the GNI framework (Ding et al., CVPR 2025), the sole prior work on TAS condensation, by replacing its cVAE-based iterative latent optimization with deterministic DDIM forward/reverse flow mappings. The conceptual shift is from treating condensation as a per-segment inversion problem (solving for optimal latent codes via 10K optimization steps) to a trajectory-anchoring problem where sparse latent points are mapped in a single forward pass and interpolated in latent space. The broader dataset condensation literature (gradient matching, trajectory matching, distribution matching for image classification) informs the problem framing, but the paper's specific contribution is the application of DDIM's near-bijective data-noise correspondence to structured temporal video features, combined with an adaptive budget allocation mechanism not present in prior condensation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 2.4% condensation ratio on Breakfast can match full-data training performance is practically significant for storage-constrained video analytics pipelines, and the elimination of per-segment optimization (replacing 10K gradient steps with a single DDIM forward pass) makes condensation orders of magnitude cheaper. The GNI plateau—where increasing latent codes from 8 to 64 degrades Edit score—provides useful evidence that cVAE's unimodal Gaussian prior imposes a hard information bottleneck for temporal dynamics. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond I3D features to newer backbones (e.g., VideoMAE, SlowFast), whether the deterministic DDIM assumption degrades on highly stochastic or short-duration actions, and whether the adaptive allocation strategy transfers to other video condensation settings beyond TAS.
