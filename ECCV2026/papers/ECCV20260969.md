## Problem

Existing vision-language model benchmarks for plant science focus almost exclusively on macroscopic, field-level imagery, leaving microscopic plant image understanding largely unexplored. No standardized benchmark exists to systematically evaluate VLMs on the fine-grained cellular and subcellular structures, domain-specific terminology, and diverse imaging modalities (light, fluorescence, electron) that are central to plant pathology, botany, and cell biology.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260969/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a single microscopic plant image (converted to JPEG at original resolution, sourced from light, fluorescence, or electron microscopy) with a text question in closed VQA format. For the eight identification tasks, the model selects from four candidate options; for counting, it outputs a single integer; for localization, it outputs absolute pixel coordinates (x1, y1, x2, y2) in a fixed format, with format violations treated as failures. All 9,718 VQA pairs are derived from expert-verified metadata, with distractors sampled from semantically valid values within the same field to ensure biological plausibility.

## Method

PlantMicro is constructed by aggregating 5,410 microscopy images from peer-reviewed publications and open repositories spanning mycology, nematology, botany, and cell biology, then standardizing all metadata under a unified curation protocol with dual-biologist verification. Ten benchmark tasks are defined over this metadata: four image-level tasks (modality, domain, resin, stain), four biological-level tasks (host, pathogen, organelle, organ), plus target counting and object localization. Evaluation is conducted in zero-shot and 1-shot in-context settings across closed-source (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5) and open-source (CLIP, LLaVA, QwenVLM) VLMs, with additional ablations on chain-of-thought prompting and retrieval-augmented generation. The key empirical finding is that VLMs achieve near-ceiling performance on global visual cues (modality >95%) but drop to near-random on fine-grained biological recognition (~30%), with error analysis attributing most failures to domain knowledge deficiency rather than perceptual errors.

## Results

On the PlantMicro benchmark (5,410 microscopy images, 9,718 VQA pairs), Gemini 2.5-Pro achieves the highest overall macro accuracy of 62.93% and micro accuracy of 65.07% across eight identification tasks, while GPT-5-mini reaches 57.26% macro / 61.17% micro. Performance is highly task-dependent: modality identification is near-saturated (GPT-5-mini 99.73%, Gemini 2.5-Pro 99.56%), whereas host identification (best: GPT-5 at 34.93%) and pathogen classification (best: GPT-5-mini at 43.91%) remain only modestly above the ~25% random-guessing baseline. Among open-source models, QwenVLM-7B leads with 51.98% macro / 48.58% micro, still trailing the best closed-source model by over 10 percentage points. On counting, GPT-5 achieves the lowest MAE (2.07) and RMSE (3.02); on localization, QwenVLM-7B attains the best mAP@0.5 (65.05) and IoU (68.64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source VLMs, Gemini 2.5-Pro leads overall (62.93% macro / 65.07% micro), edging out GPT-5-mini (57.26% / 61.17%) and GPT-5 (56.79% / 60.56%); notably, GPT-5-mini surpasses GPT-5 on pathogen classification (43.91% vs. 42.71%) and organ prediction (71.60% vs. 64.19%), suggesting smaller models can be more robust in fine-grained microscopic contexts. Gemini 2.5-Flash is the strongest on host identification (45.37%) and resin identification (85.19%), while GPT-5 leads on host identification (34.93%) and stain recognition (58.46%). Among open-source models, QwenVLM-7B is the clear leader (51.98% macro / 48.58% micro), outperforming LLaVA-Next-13B (41.20% / 39.63%) and LLaVA-13B (37.24% / 41.88%); CLIP variants perform worst, with CLIP-ViT/32 falling below the random baseline on resin (9.64% vs. 25.21%) and organ prediction (16.05% vs. 23.90%). On counting, GPT-5 achieves the best MAE (2.07) and RMSE (3.02), while QwenVLM-7B is the best open-source counter (MAE 3.38, RMSE 9.24). On localization, QwenVLM-7B surprisingly outperforms all closed-source models, reaching mAP@0.5 of 65.05 and IoU of 68.64 versus GPT-5's 56.66 and 50.53, indicating stronger spatial grounding in the open-source model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two existing benchmarking traditions: field-level plant VLM benchmarks (CDDM, AgroBench, Agri-CM3) that evaluate macroscopic imagery, and biomedical microscopy benchmarks (MicroBench, BiomedCLIP) that target human/animal tissue. PlantMicro carves new ground by being the first to systematically evaluate VLMs on microscopic *plant* imagery, combining domain-specific biological tasks (resin identification, stain recognition, host/pathogen classification from cellular structures) with standard VQA, counting, and localization formats. It does not propose a new model or training method; rather, it repurposes the VQA evaluation paradigm to a previously unexplored visual domain, drawing on expert-curated metadata from public microscopy datasets to construct its question-answer pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even frontier models like GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5-Pro achieve only ~35% accuracy on fine-grained pathogen classification—barely above random—exposing a substantial domain-knowledge gap that general-purpose VLM pretraining has not addressed. The RAG experiments showing ~20-point improvements for open-source models suggest that retrieval-based knowledge augmentation is a practical near-term path, while the error analysis (knowledge deficiency dominating over perception errors) points toward domain-specific pretraining or fine-tuning as the longer-term solution. Open questions include whether the benchmark's relatively small scale (5,410 images, 9,718 VQA pairs) limits statistical power, whether performance generalizes to unseen hosts or modalities, and whether the multiple-choice format masks deeper reasoning failures that open-ended evaluation would reveal.
