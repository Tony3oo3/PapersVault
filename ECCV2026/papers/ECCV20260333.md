## Problem

Existing retrieval-augmented text-to-motion (RAG-T2M) methods suffer from two structural limitations: retrieval alignment is performed at the global level, ignoring the hierarchical body-part structure of human motion, and the fusion of retrieved evidence into the generator relies on simple concatenation or vanilla cross-attention without considering compatibility between the retrieved information and the motion latent topology. The paper shows that enforcing structural consistency across alignment granularity, latent representation (1D vs. 2D), and fusion mechanism is critical for high-quality motion synthesis.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text prompt as input and produces a 3D human motion sequence as output. Motion is discretized via a pretrained 2D-RVQ-VAE (6 quantization layers, 512 codes each) into a 2D joint-time token grid (J×T), preserving spatial-temporal structure rather than flattening to a 1D sequence. At inference, relevant motion-text pairs are retrieved from an external database using the HBM-aligned embedding space, and the retrieved embeddings (R_m, R_t) are injected into the masked generative backbone to condition generation.

## Method

ReMoMask combines three structure-aware components. First, Hierarchical Bidirectional Momentum (HBM) contrastive learning jointly aligns text with global motion and K=6 part-level motion representations (arms, legs, backbone, root) using bidirectional InfoNCE losses under a momentum-encoder paradigm with a large negative queue. Second, Topology Structured Masking (TSM) adaptively modulates per-part masking probabilities based on text-part semantic relevance, preserving semantically critical body parts as anchors while aggressively masking less relevant regions to force part-level grounding. Third, Semantic Spatial-Temporal Attention (SSTA) injects retrieved knowledge via an asymmetric Q-K-V design over the 2D token grid: the Query is derived solely from motion latents to preserve topology, the Key integrates a compressed semantic token (concatenation of prompt, retrieved text, and retrieved motion) to modulate attention weights, and the Value combines current latents with retrieved motion R_m to enrich content while excluding textual semantics to prevent domain mismatch.

## Results

On HumanML3D, ReMoMask achieves the best FID of 0.026 and R-Precision Top-1 of 0.566, outperforming the strongest non-RAG baseline LaMP (FID 0.032, Top-1 0.557) and the best RAG baseline ReMoDiffuse (FID 0.103, Top-1 0.510). On SnapMoGen, ReMoMask reaches FID 13.509 and Top-1 0.811, surpassing MoMask++ (FID 15.060, Top-1 0.802) and all RAG baselines. For text-to-motion retrieval, HBM achieves R@1 of 18.49 on HumanML3D (68% improvement over ReMoGPT's 11.00) and 12.43 on SnapMoGen (39% over MotionPatches' 8.92).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReMoMask outperforms all 14 baselines on FID across all three datasets, with the largest margins against RAG-T2M methods: on SnapMoGen, it reduces FID from 68.46 (ReMoDiffuse) and 44.12 (ReMoGPT) to 13.51, and on HumanML3D from 0.103 (ReMoDiffuse) and 0.205 (ReMoGPT) to 0.026. Among non-RAG baselines, ReMoMask beats LaMP (the strongest T2M method) by 0.006 FID and 0.009 Top-1 on HumanML3D, and by 0.010 FID on KIT-ML. On retrieval, HBM surpasses MotionPatches (the prior best) by 7.69 R@1 points on HumanML3D (18.49 vs. 10.80) and by 3.51 on KIT-ML (16.75 vs. 14.02), while TEMOS and TMR are far behind at R@1 of 2.12 and 5.68 respectively on HumanML3D. The only metric where ReMoMask does not lead is Diversity on KIT-ML (10.922 vs. LaMP's 10.929) and MModality on KIT-ML (1.978 vs. LaMP's 1.973), which the authors attribute to RAG methods prioritizing semantic alignment over diversity. Generalization experiments confirm HBM improves ReMoDiffuse FID from 0.103 to 0.091 and ReMoGPT from 0.205 to 0.165 when swapped in as the retrieval encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReMoMask builds directly on MoMask's masked generative backbone with 2D spatial-temporal tokens and the RAG-T2M paradigm established by ReMoDiffuse and ReMoGPT. Its key conceptual departure is identifying two structural design axes—alignment granularity (global vs. part-level) and fusion compatibility (1D vs. 2D latent topology interacting with the fusion mechanism)—that prior RAG-T2M methods treated as independent or ignored entirely. The empirical study in Section 3, showing that 2D latents with cross-attention consistently outperform 1D latents with concatenation, provides the evidence base for the SSTA module and the broader structural-consistency argument.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that retrieval alignment and fusion design must be structurally consistent with motion topology—offers a useful design principle for the RAG-T2M community beyond this specific architecture. The finding that preserving 2D spatial-temporal structure in motion latents materially improves semantic injection is a practical takeaway for anyone building on masked or tokenized motion models. However, the framework's performance remains dependent on retrieval database quality and coverage, and the paper does not explore whether the structural-consistency argument extends to non-masked backbones (e.g., diffusion) or to longer-horizon motions where part-level semantics may shift over time.
