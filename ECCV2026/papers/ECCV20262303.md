## Problem

VLMs demonstrate strong sequential planning in the text domain but perform near-random on visual planning tasks, especially when an erroneous step must be detected and corrected. Existing planning benchmarks are overwhelmingly text-only, and visual reasoning benchmarks lack temporal action sequences with error-prone instructions, leaving a critical gap in evaluating whether VLMs can genuinely reason over evolving visual scenes rather than shortcutting to the final state.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262303/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an initial scene image, a goal scene image, a partially executed action sequence containing exactly one intentional error, and five MCQ options for the remaining steps. For step completion, the model selects one of five options; for error detection, it identifies the erroneous action from the initial context or selects 'none of the above.' A key design constraint is a cheating safeguard: one distractor option also reaches the goal state but omits the required error-correction step, forcing the model to reason about the full action sequence rather than matching the final image.

## Method

SGI (Scene Graph Incremental Update) is a training-free method that decomposes long-horizon visual reasoning into iterative text-space operations. First, the VLM generates vanilla scene graphs for the initial and goal images, encoding objects as nodes, attributes as properties, and spatial relationships as edges. Second, starting from the initial scene graph, the VLM sequentially simulates each action in the initial context to produce an updated context scene graph, then independently simulates each MCQ option's action sequence from that context graph to produce option-specific scene graphs. Third, the VLM scores the similarity between each option's resulting scene graph and the goal scene graph (0–100), selecting the highest-scoring option. This incremental formulation (CoT ⊆ SG ⊆ SGI) avoids the need to internally interpolate visual intermediate states, instead operating entirely in the textual scene-graph space where VLMs reason more reliably, yielding ~4.4% average improvement over vanilla scene graphs on CoSPlan.

## Results

On CoSPlan Step Completion, SGI yields an average gain of ~4.4% over vanilla Scene Graph (SG), with improvements up to 10.3% (Intern-VLM 2 on Blocks-World-E: 18.9%→29.2%), 4.8% (Intern-VLM 3 on Maze-E: 50.1%→54.8%), and 10.0% (GPT-4o on Maze-E: 46.1%→56.1%). For Error Detection, SGI gains average 4.1%, 1.7%, and 9.2% for Intern-VLM 2, Intern-VLM 3, and GPT-4o respectively. Most open-source VLMs (Llama3-8B, Qwen2-VL, Janus-Pro) perform at or below the 20% random baseline on Step Completion, while Gemini-3-pro achieves the highest scores (71.3% SG / 71.5% SGI on Blocks-World-E). On the external PlanBench Blocksworld Task 8, Qwen2-VL-8B with SGI scores 14.7 vs. 13.8–14.1 for Vanilla/CoT/SG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGI is compared against two prompting baselines: Chain-of-Thought (CoT, Wei et al. 2022) and Scene Graph (SG, Chen et al. 2023). The paper establishes a strict hierarchy CoT ⊆ SG ⊆ SGI: SG consistently outperforms CoT across all four CoSPlan tasks and all evaluated models (e.g., GPT-4o on Maze-E: CoT 45.6% vs. SG 46.1%; Intern-VLM 3 on Maze-E: CoT 48.8% vs. SG 50.1%), and SGI in turn outperforms SG by an average of 4.4% on Step Completion and 4.1% on Error Detection. The largest SGI gains appear on GPT-4o (up to +13.2% on Robo-VQA-E Error Detection) and Intern-VLM 2 on Blocks-World-E (+10.3% Step Completion), suggesting SGI is most beneficial when the base model already has moderate visual grounding. On the external PlanBench Blocksworld Task 8, SGI (14.7) marginally beats CoT (14.1) and SG (13.9) for Qwen2-VL-8B. SGI also reduces cheating rates (e.g., Intern-VLM-2 drops from 43% to 39% shortcut selections), indicating the iterative per-option simulation forces genuine reasoning rather than final-state matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scene graph prompting paradigm (Chen et al., 2023) by introducing incremental, step-by-step refinement of scene graphs (SGI) rather than one-shot graph generation, creating a hierarchy CoT ⊆ SG ⊆ SGI. It also builds on text-domain sequential planning benchmarks (PlanBench, ALFWorld) and visual spatial reasoning evaluations (SpatialEval, SURDS), but departs from them by introducing error-prone corrective planning in the visual domain—a setting where prior benchmarks either assume perfect instructions or evaluate only static scenes without temporal state evolution. The CoSPlan benchmark is positioned as the first to combine vision, language, temporal multi-step actions, and intentional error injection in a unified evaluation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical and previously underappreciated weakness: even frontier models like GPT-4o and GPT-5.1 drop from near-perfect accuracy in error-free visual planning to roughly 50% with a single injected error, revealing that their 'reasoning' may rely on pattern-matching to the goal state rather than genuine step-by-step simulation. SGI provides a practical, training-free mitigation (~4.4% average gain) but the single-error constraint and MCQ format limit ecological validity; extending to multi-error cascades, video-based planning, and interactive agent settings are natural next steps. The finding that text-only reformulations dramatically outperform visual reasoning on the same tasks also raises open questions about whether VLMs truly 'see' intermediate states or merely pattern-match on textual descriptions.
