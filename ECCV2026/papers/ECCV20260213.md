## Problem

Standard VLM pre-training relies on text-guided visual QA that emphasizes high-level semantic reasoning but fails to encode fine-grained geometric and spatial structure critical for embodied manipulation. Existing spatial-aware VLA approaches inject 3D or depth priors late in the pipeline or as separate modules, isolating physical grounding from the open-vocabulary semantic representations learned during pre-training. The paper asks how to embed spatial and physical knowledge directly into the foundational pre-training phase so that it simultaneously strengthens both abstract reasoning and actionable robot control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260213/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image and a text instruction, producing a text response for embodied reasoning tasks. During training, the visual tokens from the backbone's final layer are additionally fed through a lightweight 2-layer MLP connector into a DiT-based depth generator that synthesizes a depth map via flow matching, providing structural supervision. For the GEM-VLA extension, key-value tokens extracted from the backbone's attention blocks over the multimodal observation history condition a separate DiT action expert that outputs continuous action chunks; bounding boxes and point annotations in the training data are normalized to [0, 1000] for resolution invariance.

## Method

GEM augments a Qwen3-VL backbone with a Sana-based DiT depth generative head conditioned on the backbone's projected visual tokens, trained with a flow-matching objective that learns the vector field transforming noise into the ground-truth depth map. A progressive three-stage training recipe prevents modality interference: Stage 1 freezes the backbone and DiT to align the connector, Stage 2 freezes the backbone to warm up the generative head, and Stage 3 unfreezes all components for joint optimization with total loss L_CE + 0.1·L_flow. The companion GEM-4M dataset (~4M QA pairs) covers embodied grounding, physical/spatial reasoning, and spatiotemporal planning, with pseudo depth maps generated by DepthAnythingv3 where ground truth is unavailable. For robot deployment, a DiT action expert is appended and co-trained with the depth head using a flow-matching action loss conditioned on the backbone's key-value tokens, enabling end-to-end transfer of learned geometric priors to continuous control.

## Results

On spatial understanding benchmarks, GEM-8B achieves 70.6 on VSI-Bench (vs. 57.9 for Qwen3-VL-8B base), 86.6 on CV-Bench, 35.3 on MMSI-Bench, and 79.4 on EmbSpatial, setting new state-of-the-art among open-source models. GEM-2B similarly improves VSI-Bench from 50.4 to 62.8. On the LIBERO VLA benchmark, GEM-VLA achieves a 96.1% average success rate across four task suites, surpassing π0 (94.2%) and DepthVLA (94.9%). In real-world robot manipulation, GEM-VLA reaches a 43% average success rate, substantially exceeding the previous state-of-the-art of 28.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEM-8B outperforms the proprietary Gemini-3-Pro by a wide margin on VSI-Bench (70.6 vs. 53.0) and CV-Bench (86.6 vs. 82.5), but trails it on MMSI-Bench (35.3 vs. 45.9) and EmbSpatial (79.4 vs. 81.0). Among open-source spatial specialists, GEM-8B surpasses SenseNova-SI-8B (67.9), CambrianS-7B (67.5), and VLM-3R (60.7) on VSI-Bench, while also leading on CV-Bench over all open-source baselines. On object grounding benchmarks (Table 2), GEM-8B exceeds Gemini-3-Pro by ~10% on average across RefSpatial, Where2Place, and RoboSpatial. For VLA tasks, GEM-VLA achieves 96.1% on LIBERO, beating π0 (94.2%), DepthVLA (94.9%), and DreamVLA (92.6%), with the largest gap on the Long suite (89.3 vs. 85.2 for π0). In real-world evaluation, GEM-VLA (43% avg.) substantially outperforms both π0-FAST and π0.5 (28.7% previous SOTA), particularly on deformable object manipulation and long-horizon table bussing tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEM departs from the dominant paradigm of scaling VQA-style pre-training for embodied VLMs by introducing depth map generation as an intrinsic auxiliary objective during the pre-training phase, rather than treating spatial/physical knowledge as a late-stage module or separate input modality. Architecturally, it builds on the DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and flow-matching (Lipman et al., 2023) frameworks for the generative head, conditioned on the visual tokens of a standard autoregressive VLM backbone (Qwen3-VL). This positions GEM between two existing lines of work: (1) semantic-focused embodied VLMs that lack fine-grained geometric cues, and (2) spatially-enhanced VLAs that inject 3D/2.5D inputs or implicit spatial priors only at the action-prediction stage. The progressive three-stage training recipe is a practical contribution to stabilize the joint optimization of the autoregressive and diffusion components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a relatively simple auxiliary generative task (depth synthesis) can meaningfully close the gap between high-level semantic reasoning and low-level physical grounding in embodied models, with practical impact on both benchmark performance and real-world robot manipulation. The released GEM-4M dataset and the GEM-VLA deployment provide concrete resources for the embodied AI community. Open questions include whether depth is the optimal generative target compared to other 3D representations (e.g., point clouds, occupancy grids), whether the approach generalizes to multi-view or video-based embodied settings, and whether the benefits persist at larger model scales or with more diverse robot embodiments. The modest margin over the strongest VLA baselines on LIBERO (~1.2%) suggests further gains may require richer physical supervision beyond single-view depth.
