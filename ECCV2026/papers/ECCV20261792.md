## Problem

Existing virtual try-on (VTON) and virtual try-off (VTOFF) datasets contain only static person-garment pairs without support for instruction-level control, while general-purpose editing datasets lack consistent garment-person correspondences needed for fashion-specific tasks. No prior benchmark unifies VTON, VTOFF, and text-guided garment editing (e.g., changing color, pattern, material, or structural elements like sleeve length) within a single framework, leaving controllable, instruction-driven fashion manipulation underexplored.

## Input / Output

For instruction-driven VTON, the model receives a person image, a garment image (same garment in paired setting, a different garment in unpaired setting), and a natural-language edit instruction, producing a photorealistic person image wearing the edited garment at 768×1024 resolution. For instruction-driven VTOFF, the input is a person image plus a text instruction specifying both garment identity and modification, and the output is an edited in-shop catalog-style garment image. Evaluation employs an inverse-editing protocol: the model reconstructs the original real image from its synthetic edited counterpart using an inverse instruction, ensuring reference-based metrics are computed against real ground truth rather than synthetic targets.

## Method

Dress-EM unifies a frozen MLLM (InternVL-3.5-8B), a lightweight trainable connector, and a DiT backbone (initialized from Stable Diffusion 3 medium) into a single architecture. The MLLM jointly encodes the visual reference and text instruction in one forward pass, producing fused token embeddings; the connector then splits these into a 2048-dim global guidance vector (via masked mean pooling) and a 77×4096 refined token sequence (via a small Transformer stack), providing both high-level semantic intent and fine-grained spatial conditioning to the DiT. The DiT denoises in VAE latent space conditioned on both the multimodal embeddings and VAE latents of the visual reference (concatenated masked-person + garment latents for VTON; person latent for VTOFF), enabling localized edits while preserving global coherence. Training keeps the VAE and MLLM frozen, optimizing only the connector and DiT end-to-end with a standard diffusion loss, using mixed supervision over both original and edited images to maintain alignment with real textures while learning instruction-driven modifications.

## Results

On the paired instruction-driven VTON benchmark, Dress-EM achieves the best FID (3.79), KID (1.13), LPIPS (0.0628), DISTS (0.0733), and DINO-I (0.9383), outperforming all baselines. In the more challenging unpaired VTON setting, Dress-EM attains the lowest FID (5.82) and KID (1.44), while matching CatVTON on DINO-I (0.6476). For instruction-driven VTOFF, Dress-EM achieves FID 5.06, KID 0.73, and DINO-I 0.8071, substantially outperforming the next-best method CatVTON (FID 6.78, KID 1.45, DINO-I 0.7918) and general-purpose editors like FLUX.2 Klein (FID 17.45, KID 7.48).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dress-EM consistently outperforms all four baselines across the three benchmark settings. Against the task-specific VTON/VTOFF models, CatVTON is the closest competitor (FID 4.57 vs. 3.79 in paired VTON; FID 6.78 vs. 5.06 in VTOFF), but Dress-EM still leads by a clear margin on FID, KID, and DINO-I. Any2AnyTryon trails further behind, with notably weaker DINO-I in the unpaired VTON setting (0.4996 vs. 0.6476), suggesting poor semantic edit fidelity when the garment is unseen. The general-purpose editors FLUX.2 Klein and Qwen-Image-Edit are substantially weaker on VTOFF (FID 17.45 and 24.09 respectively vs. 5.06 for Dress-EM), confirming that domain-specific multimodal conditioning is critical for garment-level reconstruction. The only metric where Dress-EM does not strictly lead is unpaired VTON DINO-I, where FLUX.2 Klein edges ahead by 0.007 (0.6549 vs. 0.6476), though Dress-EM still dominates on FID and KID in that setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying three previously fragmented research threads—VTON/VTOFF datasets (Dress Code, VITON-HD), general-purpose instruction-based editing datasets (UltraEdit, HQ-Edit, AnyEdit), and narrow fashion-editing approaches (EditGarment, T-FIT)—into a single quadruplet-structured benchmark with natural-language editing instructions. Architecturally, Dress-EM follows the emerging MLLM-plus-DiT paradigm (analogous to recent unified editing frameworks) but repurposes it for the specific cross-domain challenge of garment-person transformations, where input and target images belong to fundamentally different visual domains. The automated curation pipeline itself composes existing tools (Qwen3-VL for attribute extraction, FLUX.2 Klein for garment editing, FitDiT for try-on synthesis, GPT-5 for verification) in a novel multi-stage orchestration that no prior fashion dataset has attempted at this scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dress-ED fills a critical gap: no prior dataset simultaneously supports instruction-driven VTON, VTOFF, and garment editing with verified garment-person correspondences, making it likely to become the standard benchmark for this emerging subfield. A key limitation is that all edited images are synthetically generated (via FLUX.2 Klein and FitDiT), so the dataset's ceiling is bounded by the quality of these generators, and the inverse-editing evaluation protocol, while clever, adds an extra transformation step that may not reflect real user workflows. Open questions include whether the 768×1024 resolution and three garment categories generalize to full-body outfits, accessories, or multi-garment scenarios, and whether the template-based instruction generation captures the full diversity of real user editing intents.
