## Problem

Descriptor-free visual localization eliminates high-dimensional descriptor storage and preserves scene privacy, yet its accuracy lags far behind descriptor-based pipelines. The paper identifies this gap as stemming from insufficient geometric discriminability at three levels: underutilized local directional and metric cues, absence of global context to resolve ambiguities in repetitive structures, and overfitting to a single keypoint detector's distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261594/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D keypoints from any detector (SIFT, SuperPoint, DISK, R2D2, DeDoDe-v2) on a query image and a 3D point cloud from top-k retrieved database images, along with per-keypoint RGB colors. Both 2D and 3D points are back-projected to bearing vectors on the normalized image plane to create a shared geometric representation, making the pipeline detector-agnostic at test time. The output is a set of 2D-3D correspondences (via Sinkhorn optimal transport followed by learned outlier rejection) from which a 6-DoF camera pose is recovered by PnP-RANSAC.

## Method

GeoMix builds on the A2-GNN backbone and introduces three complementary mechanisms. Locally, a unified geometric embedding concatenating relative displacement with its unit direction is injected into both the annular convolution and max-pooling branches, enriching neighborhood aggregation with fine-grained directional and metric structure. Globally, N_g=4 learnable context nodes per point set aggregate scene-wide information via cross-attention, exchange context among themselves via self-attention, and broadcast back to local features, enabling disambiguation beyond the local KNN receptive field. At the training level, Mix-Training jointly optimizes over three keypoint detectors (SIFT, SuperPoint, DISK) on the 2D query side while keeping the SIFT-based 3D map fixed, exploiting the descriptor-free property that makes keypoints from any detector interchangeable without descriptor-space alignment. The full pipeline uses bearing/RGB feature encoders, L alternating self-attention and cross-attention GNN layers, a differentiable Sinkhorn optimal transport layer for soft correspondence estimation, and a learned outlier rejection classifier, trained end-to-end with a matching loss plus rejection loss on MegaDepth.

## Results

On MegaDepth (k=10 retrieved images), GeoMix achieves reprojection AUC@1/5/10px of 21.51/65.61/73.76%, reducing the 75th-percentile rotation error from 4.60° (A2-GNN) to 0.52° (89% reduction) and translation error from 0.48 m to 0.05 m (90% reduction). On Cambridge Landmarks, GeoMix reaches 18 cm / 0.66° median error, compared to 41 cm / 1.49° for A2-GNN and 173 cm / 5.87° for GoMatch. On 7Scenes, it achieves 11 cm / 3.04° average, versus 15 cm / 4.04° for A2-GNN. On Aachen Day-Night, GeoMix outperforms all descriptor-free baselines across all four detectors, with the largest gains under nighttime conditions (e.g., 27.8/42.5/61.4% at 0.25m/0.5m/5m thresholds with SIFT vs. 11.7/20.6/38.5% for A2-GNN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoMix substantially outperforms all three prior descriptor-free methods on MegaDepth: over GoMatch it improves AUC@5/10px by +29.94/+28.77% and cuts 75th-percentile rotation error from 16.65° to 0.52°; over DGC-GNN it gains +13.91/+13.75% AUC and reduces rotation error from 5.41° to 0.52°; over the closest competitor A2-GNN it improves AUC@5/10px by +11.20/+11.52% and reduces rotation error from 4.60° to 0.52° (89%) and translation from 0.48 m to 0.05 m (90%). On Cambridge Landmarks, GeoMix (18 cm / 0.66°) narrows the gap to the descriptor-based SuperPoint+SuperGlue reference (10 cm / 0.23°) while using only 69 MB of map storage versus 3215 MB, and it outperforms the E2E scene-coordinate regressors DSAC* (14 cm / 0.35°) and HSCNet (13 cm / 0.30°) in storage efficiency without per-scene retraining. On 7Scenes, GeoMix (11 cm / 3.04°) beats A2-GNN (15 cm / 4.04°) and DGC-GNN (17 cm / 4.81°) but remains behind the descriptor-based SP+SG (3 cm / 1.09°) and E2E methods (DSAC*: 3 cm / 1.36°; HSCNet: 3 cm / 0.90°). On Aachen Day-Night, GeoMix consistently leads all descriptor-free baselines across SIFT, SuperPoint, R2D2, and DISK, with the largest margins at night (e.g., 27.8% vs. 11.7% at 0.25 m/2° with SIFT), though it still trails the SP+SG descriptor-based reference (86.7% vs. 27.8% at the same threshold). The Mix-Training strategy also enables zero-shot transfer to unseen detectors (R2D2, DeDoDe-v2), where it reduces median rotation error from 1.94° to 0.34° (R2D2) and from 0.39° to 0.17° (DeDoDe-v2) compared to single-detector training.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoMix builds directly on the A2-GNN descriptor-free GNN backbone, inheriting its bearing-vector representation, annular convolution, optimal transport matching, and outlier rejection modules. It departs from prior descriptor-free methods (GoMatch, DGC-GNN, A2-GNN) at three levels: enriching local geometry with directional and distance-aware edge embeddings, introducing learnable global context nodes for scene-wide cross-attention, and—most distinctively—proposing Mix-Training, a multi-detector training strategy that exploits the detector-agnostic property inherent to geometry-only matching. This last component has no direct analogue in descriptor-based pipelines, where each detector is tightly coupled to its own descriptor space, making cross-detector training impractical without explicit alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoMix substantially narrows the long-standing accuracy gap between descriptor-free and descriptor-based localization (89% rotation error reduction, up to 90% translation error reduction over prior descriptor-free SOTA) while retaining the practical advantages of compact 69 MB maps, zero descriptor storage, and inherent privacy. The Mix-Training insight—that the descriptor-free formulation's detector-agnosticism enables multi-detector training as a regularizer—is a clean conceptual contribution that generalizes zero-shot to unseen detectors. However, a meaningful gap to descriptor-based methods (e.g., SuperPoint+SuperGlue) persists, particularly under high outlier ratios where purely geometric cues lack sufficient discriminability, and the 3D maps remain tied to a fixed SIFT-based reconstruction, introducing residual bias. Future work on diversifying the 3D reconstruction side or incorporating lightweight appearance cues could further close this gap.
