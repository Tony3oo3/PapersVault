## Problem

Vision-language models degrade on long videos because irrelevant frames confuse their language components, yet naively adding more frames is impractical. Existing query-to-frame retrieval methods suffer from three coupled failures: biased relevance (original questions, especially multiple-choice distractors, mislead scoring), limited diversity (relevance-only ranking yields near-duplicate evidence), and temporal collapse (selections concentrate in a narrow temporal region while key evidence may be temporally dispersed).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262720/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an arbitrary-format text question (open-ended, multiple-choice, or captioning) and a video uniformly sampled into candidate frames (e.g., 2048 frames from a 34-minute video; for streaming, only the 60-second window [t−60, t] is accessible). It outputs a strict budget of K frames (8, 16, or 32) that are fed to a downstream video VLM to produce a text answer. The QSRanker additionally emits per-frame structured relevance scores (OBJECT, ACTION, LOCATION, each in {1,…,5}) and query-level importance weights, making the retrieval interpretable.

## Method

QSVideo decouples question understanding from visual grounding via two stages. QSRanker first uses a Qwen3-4B LLM as a Question Analyzer to transcribe the original question into a retrieval-friendly query and assign discrete importance weights (1–5) to OBJECT, ACTION, and LOCATION; a fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct Semantic Ranker then scores each query–frame pair along those three dimensions, trained via LoRA (rank 32) with cross-entropy on 5-token score heads using Qwen-VL-30B-generated annotations on 35K video–query pairs. QSRetrieval greedily selects frames by maximizing λ·relevance + (1−λ)·minimum L2 visual distance to already-selected frames, constrained within K disjoint temporal windows to enforce coverage. For long videos it iteratively picks a global relevance anchor and expands locally within a temporal radius r; for streaming videos it traverses windows from most-recent to oldest, using the previous selection as the diversity reference, thereby encoding recency bias by construction.

## Results

On LVBench (long video, avg. 67 min), QSVideo with an 8B backbone achieves 49.1% overall accuracy with 32 frames, surpassing GPT-4o (48.9%, 60 frames) and GLM-4V-Plus (48.7%, ≤300 frames), and outperforming the backbone MiniCPM-o 2.6 by +7.1 pp. With 16 frames, QSVideo reaches 45.8%, exceeding InternVL2.5-78B (43.6%) and SEAL (45.9% with 34B). On StreamingBench (60 s context), QSVideo achieves 79.36% with 16 frames (SOTA) and 77.68% with 8 frames, improving over the backbone by +14.24 pp and +11.32 pp respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LVBench, QSVideo (8B, 32 frames) achieves 49.1% overall, narrowly beating GPT-4o (48.9%, 60 frames) and GLM-4V-Plus (48.7%, ≤300 frames) while using far fewer frames and a much smaller model. At 16 frames, QSVideo (45.8%) surpasses SEAL (45.9%, 34B) and InternVL2.5-78B (43.6%, 72B), and improves over its own backbone MiniCPM-o 2.6 (38.9%) by +6.9 pp. On StreamingBench, QSVideo (8B, 16 frames) reaches 79.36%, outperforming StreamForest (77.26%, 1 fps), InfiniPot-V (76.4%, 0.5 fps), and Claude 3.5 (74.04%, 20 frames); at 8 frames it scores 77.68% vs. LLaVA-OV (72.12%) and InternVL2 (68.52%). The plug-and-play experiments (Table 3) confirm consistent gains across MiniCPM-V 2.6 (+4.60 pp at 8 frames), InternVL2 (+2.76 pp), and LLaVA-OV (+2.36 pp), demonstrating model-agnostic applicability. Notably, QSVideo is 18.7–19.2× faster than SEAL and 2.0× faster than AdaReTaKe in end-to-end latency (Table 4), achieving 22.5–23.1 fps throughput.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of query-aware frame retrieval and video VLMs, departing from prior cross-modal rankers (SeViLA, FRAG, Frame Selector) that rely on single binary or holistic relevance scores by introducing structured OBJECT/ACTION/LOCATION scoring with query-adaptive weighting. It also moves beyond methods like SEAL that use separate detectors for scenes, objects, and actions by unifying semantic understanding within a single VLM ranker, and extends retrieval-only approaches (BOLT, FFS, TimeSearchR) by explicitly co-optimizing relevance, visual diversity, and temporal coverage in a single selection objective. The question-analyzer component (an LLM that transcribes arbitrary questions into retrieval-friendly queries) is a novel preprocessing step not present in prior frame-selection work, motivated by the failure of using raw multiple-choice questions as retrieval queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design—where QSVideo can be attached to any existing video VLM without retraining the backbone—makes the approach immediately deployable and practically important for resource-constrained settings where 8B models and 16–32 frame budgets are the norm. A key open question is whether the structured three-dimension relevance decomposition generalizes to domains where the OBJECT/ACTION/LOCATION taxonomy is insufficient (e.g., abstract reasoning, multi-hop temporal causality), and whether the uniform candidate-frame sampling assumption holds for extremely long or highly dynamic videos. The paper also leaves open how the method interacts with emerging streaming architectures that maintain persistent memory, since QSVideo's recency-first traversal is a simpler alternative to learned memory policies.
