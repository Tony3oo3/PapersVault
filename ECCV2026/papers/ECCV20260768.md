## Problem

Continual learning for anomaly detection with multimodal large language models remains unexplored, despite real-world AD systems requiring incremental adaptation to new categories, domains, and modalities. Existing parameter-efficient continual learning methods face a fundamental tension: shared parameter spaces enable knowledge transfer but cause semantic entanglement across heterogeneous tasks, while fully isolated parameters prevent forgetting but block cross-task complementarity. Anomaly detection exacerbates this challenge through triple heterogeneity across modalities (2D/3D), domains (industrial/medical), and defect scale variability.

## Input / Output

The model accepts 2D RGB images, 3D point clouds, and free-form text instructions as input, processed through LLaVA-OV-7B's visual encoder which extracts patch-level features (B×M×C tensor) used both for visual anchor computation and LLM conditioning. At inference, the model produces structured text responses containing anomaly labels, spatial descriptions of defect regions, and multi-step reasoning, supporting multi-round human-machine interaction. The visual anchor routing mechanism computes cosine similarity between an input instance's mean patch feature and stored per-task anchors to dynamically select the appropriate PrivLoRA expert at test time.

## Method

CL-Anomaly employs a dual-path MoE-LoRA architecture on a frozen LLaVA-OV-7B backbone. The task-private path assigns each task a dedicated PrivLoRA expert updated exclusively during its training phase, with inference-time routing via visual anchor similarity, physically isolating parameter spaces to eliminate semantic entanglement. The task-shared path uses a standard MoE-LoRA with a softmax gating function over n=4 shared experts, enabling cross-task knowledge transfer in a unified representation space. The key innovation is Layer-Adaptive Knowledge Transfer (LAKT): during training, it accumulates per-layer gating probability vectors to compute task relevance scores, identifies top-K key experts per layer at zero extra cost, and performs dynamic momentum merging with asymmetric coefficients (β=0.75 for current-task-key experts, 1−β for previous-task-key experts, 0.5 for shared) to selectively preserve layer-wise critical knowledge while fading irrelevant parameters. At inference, outputs from both paths are combined with a fixed λ=0.5 weighting: h = W₀x + λf_p(x) + (1−λ)f_s(x).

## Results

On the Anomaly-Instruct-125K benchmark spanning 8 datasets (WebAD-1/2/3, MVTec-AD, BMAD, MVTec-3D, Real3D-AD, Anomaly-ShapeNet), CL-Anomaly achieves an average accuracy of 86.34% (Avg), 85.61% (Last), and a backward transfer of only −0.96 (BWT), approaching the multi-task upper bound of 88.38%. In the final-stage comprehensive evaluation, CL-Anomaly attains 85.61% accuracy, 89.61% precision, 78.00% recall, and 82.51% F1-score, substantially outperforming all continual learning baselines. The method incurs only 1.4% parameter overhead relative to the 8020.92M pretrained backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CL-Anomaly outperforms all four continual learning baselines across Avg, Last, and BWT metrics. Compared to the strongest baseline CL-MoE (CVPR 2025), CL-Anomaly improves Avg by 5.71 points (86.34 vs. 80.63), Last by 7.53 points (85.61 vs. 78.08), and reduces forgetting from −5.90 to −0.96 BWT. Against MoELoRA (NeurIPS 2024), the gains are 5.30 (Avg), 7.77 (Last), and 5.82 (BWT) points; against LoRA, 3.76 (Avg), 7.63 (Last), and 6.93 (BWT). HiDe-LLaVA, which uses parameter isolation, achieves the best BMAD Avg (89.18) but suffers severe forgetting on class-incremental WebAD tasks (BWT −10.65 on WebAD-3), whereas CL-Anomaly's dual-path design balances both regimes. In zero-shot anomaly detection (Table 5), CL-Anomaly (75.41 VisA / 95.86 DTD) is slightly below IAD-R1-7B (76.09 / 91.18) on VisA but surpasses it on DTD, and trails AnomalyOV-7B (78.53 / 94.33) on VisA while leading on DTD, confirming competitive generalization without explicit zero-shot design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three active research threads: parameter-efficient continual learning for MLLMs (MoELoRA, CL-MoE, HiDe-LLaVA), anomaly detection with vision-language models (AnomalyOV, IAD-R1), and traditional continual anomaly detection without language models. Its core architectural departure is the dual-path design that simultaneously isolates task-specific parameters (PrivLoRA, inspired by HiDe-LLaVA) and shares a unified expert space (MoE-LoRA, inspired by MoELoRA/CL-MoE), rather than committing to one strategy. The Layer-Adaptive Knowledge Transfer mechanism, motivated by the empirical observation that expert activation patterns differ systematically across layers, extends the momentum-merging idea from CL-MoE with a layer-wise selectivity criterion that prior methods treat uniformly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing anomaly detection as a continual MLLM problem, the paper opens a practically important direction for industrial quality control and medical imaging, where new product categories, imaging domains, and modalities (2D→3D) emerge continuously and historical data privacy precludes full retraining. The near-zero BWT (−0.96 vs. −5.90 for the prior SOTA) and the demonstration of positive backward knowledge transfer (task accuracy improving after subsequent tasks) suggest the framework could reduce the operational cost of maintaining detection pipelines. Open questions include whether the visual-anchor routing generalizes to highly abstract or synthetic anomaly types, how the approach scales beyond ~8 sequential tasks, and whether the layer-adaptive strategy transfers to non-LoRA PEFT methods or to larger MLLM backbones beyond LLaVA-OV-7B.
