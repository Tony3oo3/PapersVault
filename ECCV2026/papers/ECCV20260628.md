## Problem

Universal multi-reference image editing models have advanced rapidly and show strong generalization in visual editing, yet their strengths and limitations for virtual try-on remain underexplored due to the absence of a dedicated benchmark covering realistic and diverse VTON settings. Existing VTON benchmarks focus on the most canonical shop-to-model scenario and rely on coarse distributional metrics (FID, KID) that overlook critical factors such as identity preservation, garment fidelity, and overall image quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260628/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 24,220 test image pairs across five tasks of increasing complexity (Shop2Model, Shop2MultiModel, Shop2MultiView, Model2Model, MultiShop2Model), each pairing a person/model image with one or more garment/cloth images. Universal editing models consume these as multi-reference inputs with a single unified text instruction (zero-shot, no task-specific fine-tuning), while specialized VTON models additionally require auxiliary conditions (OpenPose, human parsing, DensePose, agnostic masks, cloth masks) provided per sample. VTEdit-QA outputs three scores (model consistency, cloth consistency, image quality) on a 0–5 scale, aggregated via the minimum rule into a single overall score.

## Method

VTEdit-Bench organizes 13,521 human sources and 10,926 clothing sources into five tasks with progressively increasing reference complexity, from a single frontal model to multi-person, multi-view, model-to-model transfer, and multi-item composition. For fine-grained evaluation, VTEdit-QA leverages GPT-4o as a reference-aware VLM evaluator: it receives the source model image plus the generated output to score model consistency, the source cloth image plus the output to score cloth consistency, and the output alone to score image quality, with detailed reasoning for each score. The framework is validated against human preferences collected via pairwise comparisons (3 annotators, 15k+ annotations) aggregated with the Bradley-Terry model, achieving an average SRCC of 0.72 with human rankings versus 0.75 inter-annotator agreement. The paper benchmarks eight universal multi-reference editors (Flux.2, Qwen-Image-Edit-2511, Flux.2-klein, DreamO, OmniGen2, UNO, DreamOmni2, Uniworld) against seven specialized VTON baselines (IDM-VTON, CatVTON, FastFit, StableVITON, ITA-MDT, OOTD-Diffusion, Any2AnyTryon) using both FID/KID and VTEdit-QA.

## Results

On VTEdit-Bench (24,220 test pairs across 5 VTON tasks), Flux.2 achieves the best average FID/KID rank (2.2) among all 15 evaluated models, followed by Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 (3.0) and FastFit (4.8). On the canonical Shop2Model task, specialized models IDM-VTON (FID 7.40) and OOTD-Diffusion (FID 7.49) lead, but universal editors Flux.2 (FID 10.88) and Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 (FID 9.53) remain competitive. Under the proposed VTEdit-QA fine-grained evaluation, Flux.2-klein attains the highest overall scores across all three shop-based tasks (3.96/3.99/3.56), surpassing the best specialized baseline IDM-VTON (3.60/2.73/1.65), while on the hardest MultiShop2Model task FastFit still leads (overall 2.90 vs. Flux.2-klein's 2.68).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the canonical Shop2Model task, specialized VTON models IDM-VTON (FID 7.40) and OOTD-Diffusion (FID 7.49) outperform all universal editors, but the gap narrows to ~3 FID points for Flux.2 (10.88) and Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 (9.53). As task complexity increases (Shop2MultiView, Shop2MultiModel), universal models degrade far more gracefully: Flux.2 stays best on both (FID 36.51 and 65.81), while ITA-MDT collapses from FID 12.23 to 143.61 and StableVITON from 16.86 to 111.44. Under VTEdit-QA fine-grained scoring, Flux.2-klein surpasses all specialized baselines on shop-based tasks (overall 3.96/3.99/3.56 vs. IDM's 3.60/2.73/1.65), indicating better joint preservation of identity, garment, and image quality. On the extended Model2Model task, CatVTON leads (VTEdit-QA overall 2.25) with Flux.2 close behind (2.06), while Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 lags (1.17) due to identity drift. On MultiShop2Model, FastFit remains the strongest (FID 11.11, VTEdit-QA 2.90), and even the best universal editor Flux.2-klein (2.68) trails, exposing multi-item compositional control as a persistent weakness for universal editors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established threads: specialized VTON models (IDM-VTON, CatVTON, FastFit, etc.) and universal multi-reference image editing models (Flux.2, Qwen-Image-Edit-2511, etc.). Rather than proposing a new generation method, it repurposes both families of existing models under a unified evaluation protocol, positioning universal editors as a potential paradigm shift away from task-specific VTON pipelines. The evaluation methodology draws on the Bradley-Terry model for human preference aggregation and adapts VLM-based scoring (GPT-4o) for reference-aware quality assessment, extending the coarse FID/KID paradigm that dominates prior VTON benchmarks (OpenVTON-Bench, VT-Bench, StreetVTON).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that top universal editors generalize more stably to harder VTON scenarios (multi-view, multi-person) while specialized models degrade sharply, suggesting a practical path toward unified VTON systems. However, multi-item composition (MultiShop2Model) and strict identity preservation remain clear bottlenecks for universal editors, and the VTEdit-QA finding that FID/KID can mask semantic failures (e.g., IDM scores well distributionally but poorly on reference consistency) argues for constraint-satisfaction-style evaluation in future VTON work. Open questions include whether VTON-specific fine-tuning of universal editors can close the multi-item gap, and whether spatially grounded multi-reference binding can improve compositional control.
