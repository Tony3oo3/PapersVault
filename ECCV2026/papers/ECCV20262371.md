## Problem

Existing pruning methods for diffusion and flow models treat them as discriminative networks, relying on weight magnitudes or gradient-based metrics that ignore how pruning distorts the generative output distribution. One-shot pruning further causes irreversible mode collapse and distribution degradation. The paper asks: how can we adaptively compress transformer-based diffusion/flow models while preserving distributional expressiveness, diversity, and condition-fidelity in a task-aware manner?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262371/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained DiT-XL/2 or SiT-XL/2 transformer backbones (675M parameters) performing class-to-image generation at 256×256 resolution. A class label conditions the generation, and the pruned model outputs RGB images sampled via DDPM (for DiT) or ODE/SDE solvers (for SiT) at 50 steps. Pruning is applied at the transformer block level, reducing the model to 435M (35%) or 334M (50%) parameters while maintaining the same input/output interface.

## Method

EntPruner introduces Conditional Entropy Deviation (CED), a generative-specific importance metric that measures the absolute entropy change of the network output after removing each block, capturing both drift toward randomness (positive CED) and mode collapse (negative CED). Blocks are ranked by CED and pruned progressively over k=4 training stages rather than all at once. At each stage, the optimal subnetwork is selected via a zero-shot NAS formulation that jointly minimizes the NTK condition number (capturing convergence stability) and the ZiCo score (capturing gradient-based trainability), combined through a rank-voting mechanism with a parameter-count regularization term. This eliminates the need for validation-based pruning decisions and automatically adapts the pruning schedule to training dynamics.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with 30% block-level pruning of SiT-XL/2, EntPruner achieves FID 2.69 (vs. 2.15 for the full model), IS 225.05, and a 1.33× inference speedup, outperforming all three pruning baselines. On downstream fine-grained datasets (CUB, Flowers, ArtBench) at 35% pruning with an ODE sampler, EntPruner achieves FID 5.24, 12.32, and 16.16 respectively, surpassing full fine-tuning on Flowers (FID 12.32 vs. 12.47). At 50% pruning, the method delivers a 2.22× speedup with an average FID degradation of only 1.76 over the full model across the three downstream datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LD-Pruner, EntPruner achieves a dramatic FID improvement on ImageNet (2.69 vs. 6.81, a 60.5% relative reduction) and consistent gains on all three downstream datasets at both 35% and 50% pruning ratios (e.g., FID 5.24 vs. 5.70 on CUB at 35%). Against BK-SDM, EntPruner reduces FID from 3.48 to 2.69 on ImageNet while matching its 1.33× speedup, indicating that heuristic block-pair pruning is less effective than entropy-guided selection. Against Diff-Pruning, the gap is narrower (FID 2.69 vs. 2.79 on ImageNet), but EntPruner still leads on IS (225.05 vs. 223.79) and Recall (0.59 vs. 0.58), and the advantage widens substantially on the out-of-distribution ArtBench dataset (FID 16.16 vs. 18.74 at 50% pruning with SDE). Overall, EntPruner wins or ties on every reported metric across all three baselines, with the largest margins over LD-Pruner and BK-SDM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the progressive channel pruning paradigm (Guo et al., 2020) to block-level structured pruning of diffusion and flow Transformers, but reframes the scheduling problem as a zero-shot NAS task using OTov2-style subnetwork selection. It departs from prior diffusion pruning methods (BK-SDM, Diff-Pruning, LD-Pruner) that rely on weight magnitudes or task-agnostic operator importance by introducing Conditional Entropy Deviation (CED), a generative-specific metric that measures how removing a block shifts the output distribution's entropy. The zero-shot selection stage borrows the NTK condition number and ZiCo gradient statistics from the NAS literature, combining them via a voting mechanism to determine pruning timing and extent without validation overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.22× inference speedup at 50% pruning with only ~1.8 FID degradation offers a practical path to deploying large diffusion/flow Transformers on resource-constrained hardware, and the CED metric provides a principled, distribution-aware alternative to the discriminative heuristics that dominate current pruning practice. However, the evaluation is limited to 256×256 class-conditional generation on two architectures (DiT, SiT) and four datasets, leaving open whether the approach transfers to text-to-image, video, or higher-resolution settings. The observation that a 30%-pruned DiT outperforms full fine-tuning on Flowers (FID 11.99 vs. 21.05) hints at a regularization effect of pruning that deserves further investigation, but the result is reported on a single dataset without ablation isolating the mechanism.
