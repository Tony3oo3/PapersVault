## Problem

Pose-free feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods exhibit counterintuitive degradation when scaling from sparse to dense input views: global attention mass diffuses over an ever-larger token pool, weakening cross-view correspondence, and uniform voxel fusion averages many Gaussians into over-smoothed representations. No prior method restores positive view scaling in the dense-view regime.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260856/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of uncalibrated RGB images (no poses or intrinsics provided) resized to a shorter side of 518 px, and in a single forward pass predicts camera intrinsics and extrinsics, per-pixel 3D Gaussian primitives (centers, covariances, colors, opacities), and depth maps. The predicted Gaussians are voxelized and rendered via differentiable rasterization to produce novel-view images. Training uses 4–24 views per scene; evaluation spans 3 to 72 input views, with the dense-view setting (32–72 views) being the primary target.

## Method

AVSplat builds on a VGGT-style 24-layer transformer backbone and introduces two components. First, Assist View Preconditioning (AVP) inserts a lightweight per-view conditioning step before global aggregation: each view selects K=4 assist views via a submodular coverage objective that balances token-level support, view reliability, and inter-assist diversity, then performs a single cross-attention interaction with those cached assist tokens, combined through a gated residual that ramps from zero to one over the first 10K training steps. This focuses the subsequent global attention on geometrically plausible correspondences rather than diffuse search. Second, Occupancy-guided Voxel Fusion (OVF) replaces uniform averaging within each voxel with a temperature-aware softmax over candidate Gaussians, where the temperature decreases with voxel occupancy (sharper attribution in crowded voxels) and is further modulated by a geometric-consistency factor derived from the variance of 3D point positions; a top-mass truncation at 0.8 cumulative weight suppresses outlier contributions. Both modules are differentiable and trained jointly with the backbone on DL3DV for 40K steps.

## Results

On the dense-view DL3DV benchmark (64 input views), AVSplat achieves 21.67 dB PSNR, 0.717 SSIM, and 0.265 LPIPS, outperforming AnySplat (21.14 / 0.684 / 0.282) by 0.53 dB in PSNR. On Mip-NeRF 360 at 64 views, AVSplat reaches 20.11 dB PSNR versus AnySplat's 19.23 dB (0.88 dB gap), and on VR-NeRF it scores 21.70 dB versus 21.26 dB. Critically, AVSplat's PSNR increases monotonically from 21.54 to 21.67 dB across 32→64 views on DL3DV, while AnySplat saturates and slightly degrades; on the overlap-heavy ACID stress test, AVSplat gains 0.79 dB over AnySplat at 72 views (23.24 vs. 22.45 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AnySplat (the primary dense-view baseline), AVSplat wins on PSNR at all three dense view counts across all three datasets, with the largest gap on Mip-NeRF 360 at 64 views (+0.88 dB) and on DL3DV at 32 views (+0.49 dB). On VR-NeRF at 48 views, AnySplat edges out AVSplat on SSIM (0.745 vs. 0.734) and LPIPS (0.338 vs. 0.317), but AVSplat leads on PSNR (21.62 vs. 21.60) and pulls ahead at 64 views. The most telling comparison is the scaling trend: AVSplat's PSNR rises steadily from 32 to 64 views on DL3DV (21.54→21.67), whereas AnySplat plateaus and slightly drops (21.05→21.14→21.14), and on ACID AnySplat degrades from 23.12 to 22.45 dB between 48 and 72 views while AVSplat improves (23.15→23.24). NoPoSplat and FLARE are only evaluated in the sparse-view setting (3/6/16 views) because they run out of memory at 32+ views; in that regime AVSplat is competitive with AnySplat and clearly ahead of both NoPoSplat and FLARE on PSNR (e.g., 17.33 vs. 16.36 vs. 13.52 on Mip-NeRF 360 at 3 views), though AnySplat retains a small PSNR edge at 16 views on Mip-NeRF 360 (17.81 vs. 18.13 for AVSplat, where AVSplat actually wins).

## Relation to Prior Work

AVSplat extends the feed-forward 3DGS pipeline established by AnySplat, which itself builds on the VGGT geometry transformer for pose-free multi-view encoding. The key departure is a targeted diagnosis of why dense-view inputs degrade prior methods: global attention mass diffuses across an ever-larger token pool, and uniform voxel averaging acts as a low-pass filter under high occupancy. Rather than changing the backbone or the Gaussian representation, AVSplat inserts two lightweight modules—Assist View Preconditioning before global aggregation and Occupancy-guided Voxel Fusion after Gaussian prediction—leaving the rest of the AnySplat/VGGT architecture intact. This positions the work as a surgical fix to a known scaling failure mode within the existing pose-free feed-forward 3DGS lineage (NoPoSplat, FLARE, AnySplat) rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Restoring positive view scaling means practitioners can simply add more uncalibrated views to improve reconstruction without architectural changes or per-scene optimization, which is practically valuable for real-world capture where dense imagery is readily available. The attention-diffusion diagnosis and the submodular assist-view selection offer a reusable recipe that could transfer to other long-sequence transformer encoders beyond 3DGS. Limitations include modest absolute gains (~0.5 dB) at fixed view counts, evaluation capped at 72 views, and reliance on a single VGGT-derived backbone; whether the approach generalizes to other geometry encoders or to even denser inputs (hundreds of views) remains open. The ACID overlap stress test, where the gap widens to 0.79 dB at 72 views, suggests the method's value grows precisely in the redundant-view regime where prior pipelines fail most.
