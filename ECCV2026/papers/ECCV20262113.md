## Problem

Recent dataset-free object removal methods manipulate self-attention within the masked region to prevent the diffusion model from referencing it, but they fail in two ways: they treat the masked region as the sole foreground, misinterpreting non-target objects outside the mask as background and regenerating them, and they apply uniform attention constraints that blur textures and misalign structures across diverse background subtypes. Both failures stem from the absence of explicit background-aware reasoning about what lies behind the target and what constitutes clean background context.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a binary target mask as input, requiring no paired before/after data or additional training. Internally, an MLLM (InternVL3-78B) and Tag2Mask models (Grounding DINO + SAM2) partition the latent spatial domain (H/8 × W/8) into three binary masks: target foreground, non-target foreground, and clean background. The output is a full-resolution image where the masked region is reconstructed with structurally faithful background while all unmasked regions remain nearly unchanged (BG Pres. ≈ 0.90).

## Method

EraseLoRA is a two-stage, dataset-free plug-in for any pretrained diffusion backbone. Stage 1 (BFE) uses an MLLM to identify semantic tags in the image, classify them into target foreground, non-target foregrounds, and background subtypes, then localizes non-target foregrounds via Tag2Mask to produce clean background cues. Stage 2 (BRSA) performs test-time optimization by inserting LoRA adapters (rank 32, 500 iterations) into the frozen diffusion backbone, jointly optimizing a Background Reconstruction Loss (L2 on the clean-background mask to anchor fidelity) and a Background Puzzle Loss (1 − Dice between the dominant background-subtype attention map and the valid-region indicator, enforcing that each background subtype integrates coherently as a puzzle piece). This replaces the hard attention-blocking of prior methods with soft, loss-driven attention regulation, and the framework is model-agnostic across diffusion backbones (SD3.5-M, SDXL, Flux) and MLLM scales (7B–78B).

## Results

On OpenImages V7, EraseLoRA achieves a Background Similarity (BG Sim.) of 0.743 and Foreground Similarity (FG Sim.) of 0.151, improving BG Sim. by +0.14 (≈23% relative) over the SD3.5-M baseline (0.605) and nearly halving FG Sim. (0.286→0.151). On RORD, the gains are even larger: BG Sim. rises to 0.779 (+0.20, ≈34% relative) and FG Sim. drops to 0.138 (from 0.319). EraseLoRA also maintains Background Preservation (BG Pres.) around 0.90–0.92, roughly 0.18 higher than all dataset-driven methods, indicating minimal perturbation of unmasked regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EraseLoRA outperforms all dataset-free baselines on both benchmarks: versus AttentiveEraser, BG Sim. improves from 0.559 to 0.743 (+33%) and FG Sim. drops from 0.276 to 0.151 (−45%) on OpenImages; versus DesignEdit, BG Sim. improves from 0.600 to 0.743 (+24%) and FG Sim. drops from 0.255 to 0.151 (−41%). Against dataset-driven methods, EraseLoRA achieves the highest BG Sim. (0.743 vs. 0.709 for SmartEraser on OpenImages; 0.779 vs. 0.768 for SmartEraser on RORD) and the lowest FG Sim. (0.151 vs. 0.148 for SmartEraser on RORD, 0.138 vs. 0.148 on RORD), while maintaining BG Pres. ≈0.90–0.92, roughly 0.18 above the best dataset-driven method (0.742 for SDXL-Inpainting). The only metric where EraseLoRA does not lead is BG Pres. on OpenImages, where the raw SD3.5-M baseline (0.934) slightly exceeds EraseLoRA (0.924), though both are far above all dataset-driven methods. Overall, EraseLoRA is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best background fidelity and foreground suppression without requiring any paired training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

EraseLoRA departs from the dominant dataset-free object-removal paradigm (AttentiveEraser, DesignEdit) that manipulates self-attention within the masked region to redirect the diffusion model toward unmasked context. Instead of attention surgery, it repurposes MLLMs in a novel role—as background-aware reasoners that infer occluded background subtypes and identify non-target foregrounds causing unintended regeneration—then injects these cues via test-time LoRA optimization with reconstruction and puzzle losses. The method also draws on the broader MLLM-for-editing literature (GenArtist, FireEdit) but inverts their use: rather than generating new foreground content from visible context, it reasons about what lies *behind* the removed target. As a model-agnostic plug-in, it sits alongside dataset-driven approaches (PowerPaint, SmartEraser, EntityErasure) but requires no paired training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of non-target foreground misinterpretation as a fundamental failure mode in dataset-free removal provides a clear diagnostic that prior methods lacked, and the three-label annotations plus BG Sim./FG Sim. metrics give the community a more nuanced evaluation protocol for unpaired settings. The practical limitation of ~3 minutes per image (mitigable to ~141 iterations with early stopping) and MLLM hallucinations in heavily occluded scenes remain open challenges. The authors point toward video object removal as a natural extension where shared temporal context could amortize per-frame optimization cost, and the plug-and-play design across backbones (SD3.5-M, SDXL, Flux) and MLLMs (7B–78B) suggests broad adoptability once latency is addressed.
