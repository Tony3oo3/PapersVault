## Problem

Self-supervised novel view synthesis (NVS) has not scaled despite abundant video data, because existing methods rely on brittle multi-network pipelines whose capacity allocation is hard to predict, and they require curated static-scene videos that constitute a tiny fraction of available visual data. Dynamic content in unconstrained video corrupts camera pose representations, causing training instabilities that prevent direct use of the dominant form of visual data at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260298/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

RayDer takes a set of unposed, uncalibrated RGB images (typically 8 views extracted from a video clip at ~0.5 s spacing, 256² resolution) and produces both per-frame camera poses (SE(3) + intrinsics, encoded as Plücker rays) and a synthesized novel view at a queried target pose. The model operates in two modes within the same backbone: a camera-estimation mode (images → poses) and an NVS mode (images + poses + target pose → target view image). A per-view dynamic state token is predicted during training but is zeroed at inference, so the deployed system requires no additional inputs beyond the input views and target pose.

## Method

RayDer consolidates RayZer's three separate ViT subnetworks (camera estimator, scene reconstructor, rendering decoder) into a single shared transformer backbone conditioned on token role via adaptive layer norms, reducing scaling decisions to one network. A minimal per-view dynamic state embedding is predicted alongside camera poses to absorb time-varying scene content as a nuisance factor; during training the target state is randomly zeroed (state dropout) so the model learns to synthesize both with and without state conditioning, eliminating the inference-time dependency. Attention is factorized so input tokens attend only among themselves while target tokens attend to both, enabling KV caching and parallel target prediction (~7× compute reduction). Random-order autoregressive training over views forces geometric rather than temporal pose encoding, and shallow local high-resolution layers with neighborhood attention add high-frequency capacity at minimal cost. The entire system is trained end-to-end with a photometric reconstruction loss on unposed video, requiring no pose labels or pretrained geometric priors, and exhibits clean power-law scaling in data, model size, and compute.

## Results

On the open-set NVS benchmark (Table 4), RayDer-L (743M, self-supervised, no pose GT at inference) achieves 17.11/21.38 PSNR on LLFF, 16.01/17.92 on DTU, 21.10/19.09 on CO3D, 20.07/17.23 on WildRGBD, 16.25/18.74 on Mip-NeRF 360, and 16.84/14.55 on Tanks & Temples. In the closed-set DL3DV-10K dense-view setting (Table 5), RayDer-B reaches 24.51 PSNR / 0.142 LPIPS / 0.758 SSIM, surpassing RayZer (24.36/0.209/0.757) and LVSM (23.69/0.242/0.723). When trained on the same dynamic SpatialVid data, self-supervised RayDer-B outperforms pose-supervised LVSM by +2.9 dB PSNR (28.35 vs 25.44) while requiring ~60× less compute than generating the pseudo-GT poses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RayDer outperforms the closest self-supervised baseline E-RayZer (246M) by 3–6 dB PSNR across nearly all open-set benchmarks (e.g., 17.11 vs 10.44 on LLFF-R, 21.10 vs 12.94 on CO3D-R), attributable to training on 10× more video data and the unified architecture. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitor SEVA (1.3B), RayDer wins on 5 of 6 datasets (LLFF +3.1, DTU +1.5, CO3D +2.7, WRGBD +0.3, T&T +1.5) but trails slightly on Mip-NeRF 360 (16.25 vs 16.70). RayDer also surpasses the larger Kaleido (3.1B) on LLFF (+1.8), WRGBD (+2.0), and T&T (+3.1). In the closed-set DL3DV comparison, RayDer-B edges out RayZer by +0.15 dB PSNR and substantially improves LPIPS (0.142 vs 0.209). Most strikingly, self-supervised RayDer-B trained on dynamic SpatialVid beats pose-supervised LVSM on the same data by +2.9 dB PSNR (28.35 vs 25.44), while the pseudo-GT pose generation via MegaSaM costs ~60× more compute than training RayDer-B itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

RayDer builds directly on the RayZer [28] / LVSM [29] lineage of self-supervised feed-forward NVS, which trains separate ViT subnetworks for camera estimation, scene reconstruction, and rendering on unposed static-scene video. The key architectural departure is consolidating all three components into a single transformer backbone, drawing inspiration from the VGGT [70] / VGG-SfM [71] unification philosophy in large 3D vision models. A minimal per-view dynamic state variable is introduced as a nuisance factor to absorb time-varying content during training, enabling stable learning on unconstrained video while preserving the static-scene NVS objective. The paper also positions itself against the broader multi-network self-supervised NVS family (Pensieve, Less3Depend, E-RayZer) and against methods that bootstrap from pretrained geometric backbones (DUST3R, MASt3R).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the two main obstacles to scaling—brittle multi-network optimization and corruption of pose representations by dynamic content—RayDer turns self-supervised NVS into a well-posed single-model scaling problem with clean power-law behavior (R² > 0.99) across three orders of magnitude in data and four model sizes. The finding that aggregating all major static-scene NVS datasets still underperforms training on general video reframes the field's data bottleneck: quantity and trainability matter more than domain alignment. Open questions include extending to full 4D NVS while retaining generic-video training, replacing the regression decoder with a generative or uncertainty-aware one for unobserved regions, and whether the scaling laws hold at substantially larger scales. The practical significance is further underscored by the result that self-supervised pose learning outperforms MegaSaM pseudo-ground-truth supervision by ~3 dB on dynamic data, at a fraction of the compute cost.
