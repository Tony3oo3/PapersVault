## Problem

Visual markers (e.g., Set-of-Mark) empirically improve GUI grounding in VLMs, but the underlying mechanism—how explicit spatial cues influence internal representations—remains unexplained. This limits the ability to replicate marker benefits without modifying the input image, and prevents more precise control over the model's spatial reasoning.

## Input / Output

DeCoPatch operates on standard VLM inference: a GUI screenshot (RGB image) paired with a natural-language instruction is fed to the model, which outputs a normalized point coordinate in [0,1]² indicating the target UI element's location. Crucially, the method requires no modification to the input image or model weights; it intervenes purely on internal activations during decoding, so the screenshot is consumed exactly as in base inference while the output coordinate is steered by the intervention.

## Method

DeCoPatch is a two-stage, training-free causal intervention applied at inference time. In the prefill stage, it computes a Vision-Text Ratio (VTR) score for each FFN neuron in the last 6 decoder layers—defined as the log-ratio of mean-squared activation energy over vision tokens versus text tokens—and caches a sparse top-k mask (k=64) selecting the most vision-relevant neurons. In the decoding stage, at every generated token, the cached mask scales the selected neuron activations by a fixed factor α=1.3 before the FFN down-projection, effectively injecting a low-rank additive perturbation into the residual stream. The key mechanistic insight, validated via SVD of marker-induced hidden-state deltas, is that the causal effect of visual markers is concentrated in a low-rank latent subspace within late decoder layers (≈layers 25–35), so targeting only k neurons per layer suffices to replicate the grounding benefit with <1% runtime overhead.

## Results

Across four benchmarks (OSWorld-G, ScreenSpot-Pro, AndroidControl-Curated, ScreenSpot-v2) and eight VLMs, DeCoPatch consistently outperforms both the unmodified base model and the Set-of-Mark (SoM) physical-marker baseline. On ScreenSpot-v2, InternVL2.5-7B achieves 84.91% average accuracy with DeCoPatch, surpassing base (79.32%) and SoM (80.58%) by 5.59 and 4.33 percentage points respectively. On OSWorld-G, Qwen2.5-VL-7B improves from 30.32% (base) to 32.09% (DeCoPatch), and Jedi-7B-1080p reaches 54.26% overall. Runtime overhead is negligible, with latency changes within ±1% for Qwen3-VL-8B and within −11% for UI-Venus-Ground-7B across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is Set-of-Mark (SoM) [Yang et al., 2023], which overlays numbered visual markers on screenshots as explicit spatial cues. DeCoPatch outperforms SoM on the overall metric for 7 out of 8 models on OSWorld-G (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 32.09% vs. 30.67%; InternVL2.5-7B: 32.09% vs. 30.14%) and on ScreenSpot-v2 for all models (e.g., InternVL2.5-7B: 84.91% vs. 80.58%; Qwen2.5-VL-3B: 83.57% vs. 81.76%). On ScreenSpot-Pro, DeCoPatch also beats SoM for most models (Qwen3-VL-8B: 48.96% vs. 47.07%; UI-Venus-Ground-7B: 51.38% vs. 50.82%), though the margin is smaller for already-strong grounding models. The random-neuron control (rand) consistently underperforms or matches the base model, confirming that gains are not from arbitrary activation perturbation but from the targeted VTR-based neuron selection. The key advantage of DeCoPatch over SoM is that it achieves equal or better grounding without modifying the input image, avoiding potential occlusion of fine-grained UI details that physical markers can introduce.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeCoPatch sits at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability and GUI grounding. It extends the neuron-localization paradigm established by knowledge neurons (Dai et al., 2022) and skill neurons (Wang et al., 2022) by introducing a vision-text ratio (VTR) scoring criterion tailored to multimodal models, and by applying the resulting intervention at decode time rather than as a one-time parameter edit. On the GUI grounding side, it departs from the dominant SoM-style approach of overlaying physical markers on screenshots, instead reproducing their effect through targeted activation scaling of a sparse set of FFN neurons. The paper is explicitly positioned as the first to leverage causal intervention techniques to enhance VLM grounding performance, distinguishing itself from prior GUI grounding methods (SeeClick, UGround, GUI-Actor, etc.) that improve grounding through training data, architecture changes, or test-time sampling without inspecting internal representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mechanistic finding that visual-marker gains are mediated by a low-rank subspace in late decoder FFN layers provides a concrete, testable explanation for a widely used but previously unexplained empirical trick, and opens the door to task-specific interventions beyond GUI grounding (e.g., document understanding, chart reading). Practically, DeCoPatch offers a near-zero-overhead alternative to physical markers that avoids the risk of markers obscuring fine-grained UI details, which is relevant for high-resolution professional software. Open questions include whether the VTR-based neuron selection generalizes to other vision-language tasks, whether the low-rank structure persists across model scales and architectures beyond the 3B–12B range tested, and whether more sophisticated interventions (e.g., direction-specific patching rather than uniform scaling) could yield larger gains.
