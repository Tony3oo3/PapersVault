## Problem

Existing Transformer-based SR methods rely on grouped attention (window-based or category-based) that imposes rigid, symmetric boundaries on token interactions. This overlooks the inherent asymmetry in token similarity—where one token may need to pull information from distant neighbors without reciprocal need—limiting flexible, token-adaptive feature aggregation and capping reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260219/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image and reconstructs a high-resolution version at ×2, ×3, or ×4 scale. Training proceeds in two stages: 500K iterations on 50×50 patches (batch 60) followed by fine-tuning on 75×75 patches (batch 30), using the Muon optimizer with a MultistepLR schedule. Two model variants are provided—full IET (19.7M params, 240 channels, 8 blocks × 4 layers) and IET-light (783K params, 54 channels, 8 blocks × 3 layers)—with all FLOPs measured at a 1280×640 output resolution.

## Method

The core contribution is Individualized Exploratory Attention (IEA), which replaces fixed grouped attention with a per-token index matrix I ∈ R^{N×k} that records each token's own asymmetric set of attention candidates, reducing complexity from O(N²) to O(kN). Candidates are initialized via a dilated local scope (dense attention in a small region plus uniform sparse sampling from a larger area with dilation factor d), then refined layer-by-layer through two mechanisms: a propagation step that explores two-hop neighbors (selecting top-k₁ one-hop neighbors, then collecting their top-k₂ neighbors and unioning with the original set) and a sparsification step that prunes low-attention-score tokens via TopK. Propagation is applied only in the last layer of the first four blocks with decreasing k₁/k₂ values, while a Similarity-Fused FFN (SF-FFN) in the final two blocks performs cross-channel fusion by concatenating each token with its single most similar neighbor. The full IET network uses LePE positional encoding after the first block and a progressive attention mechanism from PFT for finer similarity modeling.

## Results

On the classical SR benchmark (Table 7), IET achieves state-of-the-art PSNR across all five test sets at ×2, ×3, and ×4 scales with 19.7–19.9M parameters and 1.26–5.02T FLOPs. At the most challenging ×4 scale, IET reaches 33.22 dB on Set5, 29.35 dB on Set14, 28.06 dB on BSD100, 28.43 dB on Urban100, and 32.81 dB on Manga109, outperforming the closest competitor PFT by 0.07–0.23 dB. On the lightweight setting (Table 8), IET-light (783–801K params, 69.4–277.4G FLOPs) surpasses PFT-light by up to 0.36 dB on ×2 Urban100 and 0.21 dB on ×4 Urban100 while using comparable or lower FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IET consistently outperforms all 19 compared baselines across every dataset and scale. Against the closest competitor PFT [27] (which shares similar FLOPs of 1.26T at ×4), IET gains +0.23 dB on Urban100, +0.07 dB on Set5, and +0.18 dB on Manga109 at ×4. The advantage over category-based ATD [43] is larger: +0.26 dB on ×4 Urban100 and +0.37 dB on ×2 Urban100, confirming that token-adaptive individualized candidates outperform fixed semantic grouping. Window-based methods (SwinIR, CAT-A, HAT) trail by 0.4–1.0 dB on Urban100, highlighting the limitation of rigid spatial windows. In the lightweight setting, IET-light beats PFT-light by +0.36 dB on ×2 Urban100 and +0.21 dB on ×4 Urban100, and exceeds SwinIR-light by +0.22 dB on ×2 Urban100 while using only 9.1% of SwinIR's FLOPs. No baseline is beaten by IET on any reported metric; the method achieves the best PSNR and SSIM on all five test sets at all three scales for both the full and lightweight variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant paradigm of grouped attention in Transformer-based SR, where tokens are restricted to fixed spatial windows (SwinIR, HAT, CAT) or semantic clusters (ATD, CATANet). It builds directly on PFT's CUDA-based sparse matrix multiplication infrastructure and progressive attention mechanism, and follows the overall block architecture of SwinIR and ATD. The key conceptual departure is replacing rigid group boundaries with a token-adaptive, asymmetric candidate selection process: each token independently propagates its attention scope through two-hop similarity exploration while pruning low-relevance connections, rather than being confined to a predefined window or category.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the symmetric, group-constrained attention assumption in most efficient Transformers is suboptimal for SR, and that a content-aware, one-way neighbor selection process can capture more relevant long-range dependencies at comparable FLOPs. The ablation isolating propagation (semantic two-hop expansion) as the primary driver over sparsification (pruning) provides useful guidance for designing efficient attention. However, the approach is validated only on standard SR benchmarks at moderate resolutions, and the CUDA sparse-matrix implementation may limit practical deployment; whether the individualized exploratory paradigm generalizes to other restoration tasks or to larger-scale image/video settings remains an open question.
