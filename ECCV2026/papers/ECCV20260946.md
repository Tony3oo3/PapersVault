## Problem

Established SSL methods like FixMatch fail to finetune VLMs for semi-supervised few-shot learning, underperforming even FSL baselines that ignore unlabeled data entirely. The paper identifies the root cause as VLMs producing 'flat' softmax probability distributions that yield zero utilization of unlabeled data and weak supervision signals. Additionally, existing SSFSL methods largely neglect open-source VLMs and open-world data that have proven effective in the related FSL literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260946/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images in three forms: a small labeled set L (K-shot per class, K∈{4,8,16}), a larger unlabeled set U, and retrieved open data R (500 noisy-labeled images per dataset from LAION-400M). Class names are encoded as text embeddings to initialize the classifier. The VLM (OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 or DINOv2 ViT-B/14) encodes each image into a d-dimensional visual embedding, which is projected through a learned classifier to produce a C-way class label. Evaluation follows a validation-free protocol with hyperparameters fixed across all five fine-grained benchmarks.

## Method

SWIFT (Stage-Wise Finetuning with Temperatures) addresses the flat-softmax problem with two temperatures: a confidence temperature T_conf (set to 0.01) sharpens pseudo-label probabilities so that the default 0.8 confidence threshold in FixMatch actually selects unlabeled examples, and a learnable loss temperature T_loss (initialized to 0.07) strengthens the cross-entropy supervision during finetuning. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) classifier initialization on few-shot labeled data with T_loss, (2) semi-supervised finetuning of the visual encoder and classifier using FixMatch with both temperatures plus a CE loss on retrieved open data R, and (3) a final few-shot finetuning pass on L alone to mitigate the class imbalance, noisy labels, and domain shift in R. This stage-wise design with temperatures is plug-and-play, improving both FixMatch and DebiasPL by 14–20% accuracy, and generalizes from VLMs to self-supervised models like DINOv2.

## Results

On five fine-grained benchmarks (semi-Aves, FGVC-Aircraft, Stanford Cars, EuroSAT, DTD), SWIFT achieves mean accuracy of 71.5%, 76.3%, and 79.7% under 4-, 8-, and 16-shot settings, respectively, outperforming the SOTA FSL method SWAT (which also uses retrieval augmentation) by 4.1–5.7 points. SWIFT even rivals fully supervised learning (74.8–76.0%), which assumes ground-truth labels for all unlabeled data. The temperature-based fix alone improves FixMatch by 14–20 accuracy points (e.g., from 39.3% to 57.7% at 4-shot) and DebiasPL by 16–21 points, enabling successful VLM finetuning for the first time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWIFT outperforms the SOTA FSL method SWAT (which also exploits retrieval augmentation) by 4.1, 5.3, and 5.7 points at 4-, 8-, and 16-shot, respectively, demonstrating that unlabeled data can be effectively leveraged when temperatures are applied. Against the SOTA SSL method FineSSL (prompt learning with a frozen VLM), SWIFT gains 3.9–10.8 points, confirming that finetuning a VLM substantially outperforms prompting it. The temperature fix alone transforms FixMatch from a failing baseline (39.3% at 4-shot, underperforming even zero-shot) into a competitive method (57.7%), a 18.4-point improvement, and similarly boosts DebiasPL by 20.7 points. SWIFT surpasses all adapter-learning FSL methods (CLAP, CMLP, Linear Probing) by 10–15 points and all prompt-learning methods (CoOp, PLOT) by 20+ points. Notably, SWIFT (79.7% at 16-shot) approaches but does not fully match fully supervised learning with retrieval augmentation (77.2%), closing the gap to within 2.5 points while using only pseudo-labels for unlabeled data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: semi-supervised few-shot learning (SSFSL) and VLM-based few-shot learning (FSL). It builds directly on FixMatch's pseudo-labeling framework and the stage-wise retrieval-augmented finetuning paradigm from Liu et al. (CVPR 2025), but departs from prior SSFSL by replacing ImageNet-pretrained backbones or frozen-VLM prompt learning with full VLM finetuning augmented by open-world data retrieval. The key conceptual departure is the diagnosis that VLMs' contrastive pretraining produces flat softmax distributions, which silently breaks the confidence-threshold mechanism in standard SSL methods—a failure mode not previously identified in the SSFSL or SSL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that established SSL methods catastrophically underperform FSL baselines when applied to VLMs, and that a simple temperature adjustment resolves the issue, provides a practical and broadly applicable fix for anyone attempting semi-supervised adaptation of vision-language models. SWIFT's ability to rival fully supervised learning (which uses ground-truth labels for unlabeled data) suggests that the semi-supervised gap can be largely closed with the right training recipe. Open questions include whether the temperature-based approach generalizes to other SSL paradigms beyond FixMatch-style methods, whether the validation-free protocol holds under more extreme class imbalances, and whether the reliance on LAION-400M retrieval introduces data leakage risks on datasets closer to the pretraining distribution.
