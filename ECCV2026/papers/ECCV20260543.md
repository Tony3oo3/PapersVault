## Problem

Existing keypoint detection models are domain-specific experts (e.g., human pose, animal pose) that fail on objects with different anatomies, and prior general keypoint detection datasets (MP-100, UniKPT) are too small (~20k–418k instances) and noisy to train a robust general model. The paper asks how to build a single model that detects keypoints across 1,587 object categories spanning humans, animals, insects, furniture, vehicles, clothes, and medical scans, given only visual examples, text descriptions, or both as prompts.

## Input / Output

The model receives a 384×384 query image plus either a support image with N annotated keypoints (visual prompt, enabling K-shot detection), N keypoint text descriptions (text prompt, enabling zero-shot detection), or both. The number of output heatmaps adapts to the number of prompts supplied, so the same model handles objects with 8 to 68 keypoints. At test time, visual- and text-induced heatmaps are fused via validity-indicator-weighted averaging; at training time, missing keypoints form negative samples for learning.

## Method

GKDT uses a fully-tuned DINOv3 ViT as visual backbone and a frozen DINOv3 text encoder (with a lightweight text adaptation transformer) to extract image and text features. A kernel generation (KG) transformer takes textual and visual keypoint prototypes as tokens, applies self-attention to let modalities exchange information and cross-attention to aggregate query-image context, then expands them into per-keypoint convolution kernels (1×1 or 3×3). These kernels are applied non-parametrically to the upsampled query features via L2-normalized convolution, producing heatmaps without any learned detection head weights. Training employs two key strategies: mix-modal prompted training (each episode randomly samples from {visual, text, both} to match real-world prompt diversity) and dynamic importance sampling that adaptively removes head-class samples from the pool while retaining tail-class samples, gradually balancing the long-tail distribution across 10 super-categories.

## Results

On 22 single-object test sets spanning animals, insects, humans, furniture, vehicles, clothes, and medical scans, GKDT achieves over 90% PCK@0.1 on 17 out of 22 datasets under multimodal ('both') prompting. Headline numbers include 98.48% on CUB (unseen birds), 96.86% on NABird (unseen birds), 93.59% on COCO, and 99.62% on Hand X-ray (unseen medical images) under text prompting. In multi-object settings, GKDT-H with Grounding DINO achieves 77.2% AP on COCO and 71.3% AP on Human-Art, approaching the expert ViTPose-H (79.1% / 67.7%) while remaining a general model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GKDT decisively outperforms all four general keypoint detection baselines across all 22 test sets and all three prompt modes. Against training-free DINOv3, GKDT gains 20–90+ points (e.g., 93.59% vs. 3.13% on COCO text), confirming that the KG transformer and prompt conditioning are essential. Against CapeFormer (visual-only), GKDT leads by 15–25 points on visual prompts (e.g., 88.64% vs. 66.78% on COCO). Against OpenKD, GKDT improves by 11–23 points on visual prompts (88.64% vs. 65.26% on COCO) and by 11–13 points on text prompts (93.59% vs. 82.40% on COCO). Against X-Pose (text-only comparison), GKDT leads by 7.5 points on COCO (93.59% vs. 86.05%) and by 17.4 points on Human-Art (90.48% vs. 73.06%), while X-Pose slightly edges GKDT on Vinegar Fly text (98.13% vs. 98.91% is actually GKDT winning; X-Pose wins on Desert Locust 98.59% vs. 99.49% — GKDT wins). In multi-object COCO pose estimation, GKDT-H with Grounding DINO reaches 77.2% AP, within 1.9 points of the expert ViTPose-H (79.1%) while being a general model that also handles animals, insects, and medical scans.

## Relation to Prior Work

GKDT extends the prompted keypoint detection line (OpenKD, X-Pose, CapeFormer) by replacing their domain-specific or small-scale training with a unified 1.3M-instance dataset (MegaKPT) and a DINOv3 self-supervised backbone, making it the first DINOv3-based model for general keypoint detection. The core detection mechanism generalizes ViTPose++'s 1×1 convolution head: rather than learning fixed kernel weights, a Kernel Generation (KG) transformer synthesizes per-keypoint convolution kernels from visual and/or textual prompts, enabling non-parametric detection of arbitrary keypoint counts. Unlike X-Pose's end-to-end box-and-keypoint regression, GKDT focuses on single-object keypoint understanding and composes with external detectors (e.g., Grounding DINO) for multi-object scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MegaKPT (1.3M instances, 1587 categories, 740 keypoint types across 29 datasets) is a substantial resource that corrects annotation noise in prior unified datasets and adds expert medical keypoint texts for the first time, enabling zero-shot medical landmark detection. The consistent >90% PCK@0.1 on 17 of 22 test sets under multimodal prompting demonstrates that a single model can practically serve diverse real-world applications from insect pose to orthodontic scans. Open questions remain: multi-object performance is still bottlenecked by the upstream detector (78.2% AP with GT boxes vs. 77.2% with G-DINO on COCO), and the near-perfect medical scores may overstate generalizability to unstructured clinical settings. Whether the KG transformer's kernel-generation paradigm transfers to 3D or video keypoint detection is unexplored.
