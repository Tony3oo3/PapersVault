## Problem

Existing inference-time hallucination mitigation in LVLMs relies on visual attention as a diagnostic proxy, but the paper identifies an 'anchoring trap' where hallucinations persist even when attention is correctly localized, because parameterized linguistic priors override visual grounding. The fundamental mechanism behind this failure—how hidden states geometrically decouple from visual evidence during generation—remains unexplored, leaving no principled signal for detecting or correcting the divergence.

## Input / Output

ORBIT is a plug-and-play inference-time module that wraps around standard LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B/32B) without altering their input/output interface: an RGB image and text prompt go in, a text response comes out. Internally, the visual subspace is precomputed once during the prefill phase via randomized SVD on visual-token activations (cached for zero decoding latency), while the linguistic subspace is recomputed each decoding step from a sliding window of K=5 most recent non-visual token hidden states. Intervention is triggered only on layers where the GSTI exceeds a 3σ statistical threshold, so the computational overhead is incurred sparsely rather than at every token-layer pair.

## Method

The paper formulates the Bi-Subspace Hypothesis: faithful generation keeps hidden states anchored in a static visual subspace (principal components of visual features, layer-specific), while hallucination manifests as an abrupt departure into a dynamic linguistic subspace (principal components of recent token context). The Geo-Semantic Turbulence Index (GSTI) quantifies this departure as a sigmoid-gated product of Visual Deviation (fraction of activation energy orthogonal to the visual subspace) and Semantic Volatility (cosine distance between consecutive layer-wise velocity vectors), with an online 3σ anomaly trigger. Upon triggering, ORBIT executes a three-step geometric rectification: (1) residual extraction by projecting the hidden state onto the orthogonal complement of the linguistic subspace to isolate suppressed visual signal, (2) visual re-anchoring via dot-product attention against cached visual features with nucleus sampling (top-p=0.9) to construct a denoised correction vector confined to the visual subspace, and (3) iso-norm injection that blends the corrected visual centroid back into the hidden state while preserving its Euclidean norm, with adaptive strength modulated by turbulence severity and attention entropy. The entire pipeline is architecture-agnostic, requires no retraining, and adds only ~1.8× latency over the baseline.

## Results

On LLaVA-v1.5-7B, ORBIT achieves POPE F1 of 87.5 and reduces CHAIR_S from 51.2 (baseline) to 38.1, while on Qwen2.5-VL-7B it reaches POPE F1 of 90.3 and cuts CHAIR_S by 30.5% relative (26.5→18.4). ORBIT also improves general VQA (MME 1525.8 on LLaVA, 1988.2 on Qwen) and fine-grained perception (CLEVR 46.4 / 75.5) over all baselines. Inference overhead is modest at ×1.8 latency (7.73s vs. 4.21s baseline), comparable to SID (×1.5) and VAR (×1.7), and far below OPERA (×6.9) or HALC (×6.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORBIT outperforms all nine baselines on every hallucination metric for both LLaVA-v1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B. The largest gains appear on CHAIR_S: ORBIT reduces it to 38.1 on LLaVA (vs. best baseline AGLA at 43.2) and to 18.4 on Qwen (vs. best baseline VAR at 25.5), a 30.5% relative improvement over the Qwen baseline. On POPE F1, ORBIT leads the strongest competitor VAR by +1.4 (LLaVA) and +1.2 (Qwen). Notably, several baselines degrade the stronger Qwen model—AGLA drops POPE F1 from 88.4 to 87.7 and DoLa from 88.4 to 86.8—whereas ORBIT consistently improves it. On efficiency (Table 6), ORBIT's ×1.8 latency overhead is competitive with SID (×1.5) and VAR (×1.7) while delivering substantially better CHAIR_S (38.1 vs. 44.0 and 44.2), and is far more efficient than OPERA (×6.9) and HALC (×6.3). ORBIT also avoids the 'refusal trap,' maintaining generation length (100.3 tokens) close to the baseline (102.0), unlike DoLa (97.4) and OPERA (95.5) which shorten outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant inference-time hallucination-mitigation paradigm that treats visual attention as the primary diagnostic signal (e.g., VAR, OPERA), arguing instead that attention is an unreliable proxy due to an 'anchoring trap' where parameterized linguistic priors override correct visual grounding. It also moves beyond logit-level contrastive decoding (VCD, DoLa) by operating directly on hidden-state geometry, modeling cross-layer token evolution as sequences constrained by a static visual subspace and a dynamic linguistic subspace constructed via SVD. While related to latent-space steering methods, ORBIT is distinguished by its adaptive, anomaly-triggered intervention (GSTI) and its closed-loop protocol of residual extraction, visual re-anchoring, and iso-norm injection rather than a fixed projection direction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bi-subspace geometric framing offers a mechanistic explanation for *why* hallucinations occur (abrupt decoupling from the visual subspace) rather than merely *where* to intervene, which could inform future training-time or architectural designs. The plug-and-play nature and the fact that ORBIT improves general VQA alongside hallucination metrics (rather than merely suppressing output) make it practically attractive for deployment. Open questions include whether the SVD-based subspace construction scales efficiently to very long visual sequences (e.g., video or high-resolution multi-image inputs), whether the geometric diagnosis generalizes to non-English or code-generation settings, and whether the anchoring trap can be addressed at the training level to reduce reliance on inference-time correction.
