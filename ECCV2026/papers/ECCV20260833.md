## Problem

Tightly-coupled unified models for text-to-image generation suffer from understanding-generation trade-offs and require massive interleaved training data, while existing decoupled multi-model pipelines rely on rigid prompt engineering or expensive Best-of-N sampling, limiting both flexibility and performance. The paper asks whether a single multimodal model can serve as an autonomous reasoning agent that iteratively drives external image generators to produce high-quality outputs without modifying the generators themselves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260833/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text prompt (user query) and produces a final generated image, but the internal process yields a full multimodal reasoning trajectory: a sequence of reasoning traces, refined prompts, generated images (via an 8-step distilled FLUX.1-dev tool), judgment traces, and reflection traces across up to n_max=3 interaction rounds. The base multimodal model is Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and the image generation tool is deployed as an independent server; inference requires only 2 GPUs (one for policy, one for generation).

## Method

GenAgent designates a single multimodal model as the central reasoning brain that plans, invokes image generators as callable tools, judges outputs, and iteratively reflects until criteria are met or a round limit is reached. Training proceeds in two stages: first, hint-guided SFT distills multi-turn tool-invocation and reflection trajectories from a stronger teacher (Qwen3-VL-235B / Gemini 2.5 Pro) to cold-start agentic behaviors; second, end-to-end agentic RL via GRPO optimizes the full long-horizon pipeline using a hybrid reward combining pointwise outcome rewards (MLLM-verified final image quality) and pairwise process rewards (consistent quality improvement across consecutive image pairs), with a round-aware trajectory resampling strategy that uniformly samples across interaction-round subsets to prevent capability collapse. This decoupled design enables cross-tool generalization (trained on FLUX.1-dev, transfers to Qwen-Image and Sana1.5) and test-time scaling across interaction rounds without retraining the agent.

## Results

On GenEval++, GenAgent with FLUX.1-dev achieves 0.561 overall, a +23.6% improvement over the base FLUX.1-dev (0.325), and with Qwen-Image reaches 0.725, approaching GPT-4o (0.739). On WISE, GenAgent scores 0.69 (FLUX) and 0.72 (Qwen-Image) versus 0.55 for base FLUX.1-dev (+14.0%) and 0.80 for GPT-4o. On Imagine, GenAgent achieves 6.825 (FLUX) and 7.794 (Qwen-Image) versus 6.072 for base FLUX.1-dev and 8.560 for GPT-4o, establishing a clear lead over the second-best open-source method T2I-R1 (6.780).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenAgent with FLUX.1-dev (0.561) substantially outperforms the base FLUX.1-dev (0.325) by +23.6% on GenEval++ and +14.0% on WISE (0.69 vs. 0.55). Against unified architectures, GenAgent w/ Qwen-Image (0.725) nearly matches GPT-4o (0.739) on GenEval++ and surpasses all open-source unified models (Janus Pro 7B: 0.246, T2I-R1: 0.311, Bagel: 0.371), while on WISE it reaches 0.72, exceeding Bagel w/ Self-CoT (0.70) and approaching GPT-4o (0.80). Among decoupled methods, GenAgent decisively beats T2I-Copilot (0.496), PromptEnhancer (0.382), and ReflectionFLOW (0.361) on GenEval++, and also leads on WISE (0.69 vs. 0.67 for T2I-Copilot) and Imagine (6.825 vs. 6.740). Notably, even the single-rewrite variant without tool invocation (Ours w/o tool: 0.482) outperforms PromptEnhancer and ReflectionFLOW, indicating that the agentic iterative loop provides the largest marginal gains. When swapped to the stronger Qwen-Image tool, GenAgent achieves 0.725 on GenEval++ and 7.794 on Imagine, demonstrating cross-tool generalization and weak-to-strong scalability without any retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenAgent departs from both dominant paradigms in text-to-image generation: tightly-coupled unified architectures (Janus-Pro, Bagel, T2I-R1) that require massive interleaved training data and suffer understanding-generation trade-offs, and rigid multi-agent pipelines (T2I-Copilot, ReflectionFlow) that rely on brittle prompt engineering or expensive Best-of-N sampling. Instead, it repurposes the 'thinking with images' agentic reasoning concept (pioneered by DeepEyes and OpenAI O3 for visual understanding) to the generation side, treating off-the-shelf diffusion models as callable tools orchestrated by a single multimodal LLM. The training methodology extends GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) with a hybrid pointwise/pairwise reward mechanism and round-aware trajectory resampling specifically designed for long-horizon, multi-turn agentic rollouts where trajectory length correlates with distinct cognitive skills.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's key practical implication is that generation quality can be improved by simply swapping the underlying image generator (FLUX → Qwen-Image) without retraining the agentic brain, offering a modular upgrade path absent in unified architectures. The demonstrated cross-tool generalization and test-time scaling through interaction rounds suggest a scalable recipe for future generative systems. However, two open questions remain: the reward models (MLLM-based judges) are vulnerable to reward hacking compared to rule-based alternatives, and the framework currently only supports full-regeneration tools rather than local editing, limiting efficiency for minor corrections. Whether this agentic-tool paradigm extends to video generation or more complex multi-modal outputs is unexplored.
