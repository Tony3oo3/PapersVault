## Problem

Existing text-to-3D motion generation models rely on dense 3D coordinate-level supervision, which is over-determined and encourages fitting to dataset-specific realizations rather than learning generalizable motion structure. This leads to strong reconstruction metrics but poor out-of-distribution generalization, limited multi-modality, and reduced diversity for the same text prompt. The paper asks whether relaxing supervision from exact 3D point-matching to structural consistency can improve both generalization and diversity.

## Input / Output

At inference, LaxMotion takes a text prompt and outputs a complete 3D human motion sequence factorized into a global root trajectory and 3D relative limb vectors. During training, the generator receives a relaxed observation signal consisting of the 3D global trajectory and a single-view 2D projection of relative limb vectors (derived by projecting available 3D mocap data), rather than the full 3D pose. This 2D observation is used only as a training-time supervisory cue and is not required at test time, meaning the model must infer the full 3D structure from text alone at inference.

## Method

LaxMotion rethinks supervision granularity through three synergistic components. First, a representation reformulation decouples motion into global trajectory and relative limb vectors, then replaces the 3D relative pose with its 2D projection to create a set-valued (under-determined) training target. Second, a relaxed observability paradigm feeds only this partial 2D+trajectory observation to the generator during training, preventing overfitting to exact coordinate patterns. Third, a Relaxation Regularization module enforces structural coherence without 3D ground truth via four terms: a view-consistent projection loss anchoring generated 3D to observed 2D cues, a cross-view plausibility loss that projects the generated 3D under random rotations and checks reconstruction fidelity against a frozen VQ-VAE 2D motion prior, an orientation loss coupling foot direction to body orientation, and a feature consistency loss aligning latent encodings of projected and original observations. The framework is instantiated on both token-based (MoMask) and diffusion-based (MDM) architectures, with the prior term (commitment loss) active only in the VQ-VAE setting.

## Results

On HumanML3D, LaxMotion(MoMask-Based) achieves an FID of 0.054 and MModality of 2.046 without any direct 3D pose supervision, matching the FID of fully 3D-supervised MoMask (0.045) while substantially improving multimodality (2.046 vs. 1.241). When fused with 3D-supervised features and fine-tuned, LaxMotion(3D Finetune) reaches an FID of 0.034 and the highest QM Score of 13.715, surpassing all 3D-supervised baselines. On KIT-ML, LaxMotion(3D Finetune) similarly achieves the top QM Score (6.913) with FID 0.135, confirming the advantage of relaxed supervision across datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3D-supervised baselines on HumanML3D, LaxMotion(MoMask-Based) achieves an FID of 0.054, slightly above MoMask (0.045) and Fg-T2M++ (0.089), but with markedly higher MModality (2.046 vs. 1.241 and 2.625 respectively), yielding a QM Score of 8.805 that edges out Fg-T2M++ (8.799). The 3D-finetuned variant pushes FID to 0.034 and QM to 13.715, surpassing all 3D-supervised methods including ReMoDiffuse (FID 0.103, QM 5.593) and T2M-GPT (FID 0.116, QM 5.449). Against 2D-supervised methods (Table 3), LaxMotion(MoMask-Based) dominates under the global setting: FID 0.054 vs. Motion-2-to-3's 0.321 and MAS's 11.893, with R-Precision Top1 of 0.780 vs. 0.697 and 0.418. Under the relative (no-trajectory) setting, LaxMotion still outperforms Motion-2-to-3 (FID 0.137 vs. 0.779) and matches 3D-supervised MoMask (FID 0.137 vs. 0.154) while achieving higher MModality (1.311 vs. 0.996). The MDM-based variant shows higher FID (0.475) but retains strong MModality (2.844), indicating that the relaxed supervision benefit is architecture-agnostic. Overall, the paper demonstrates that removing 3D coordinate-level supervision does not sacrifice quality and consistently improves diversity and the joint QM metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaxMotion builds directly on two established text-to-motion architectures—MoMask (token-based VQ-VAE) and MDM (diffusion-based)—but fundamentally rethinks their training signal. Where all prior 3D-supervised methods (T2M, MDM, MLD, MotionDiffuse, ReMoDiffuse, T2M-GPT, MotionLCM, MoMask, Fg-T2M++) regress toward exact 3D joint coordinates, and where prior weakly-supervised approaches (MAS, Motion-2-to-3, MVLift) still retain coordinate-level objectives or require 3D fine-tuning, LaxMotion eliminates 3D pose-level losses entirely, replacing them with 2D kinematic cues, a 3D trajectory signal, and a suite of structural consistency regularizations (cross-view plausibility via a frozen 2D VQ-VAE prior, orientation coupling, and feature-level alignment). The motion factorization into global trajectory and relative limb vectors provides the geometric bridge that makes this 2D-to-3D inference well-posed without dense 3D labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central claim—that 3D coordinate supervision is not necessary for high-quality, diverse text-to-motion generation—challenges a foundational assumption in the field and opens a path toward training on monocular in-the-wild video at scale, bypassing the cost and coverage limitations of mocap. However, the strongest quantitative results (FID 0.034, QM 13.7) come from a '3D Finetune' variant that fuses relaxed-supervision features back into a 3D-supervised model, suggesting the pure relaxed paradigm is not yet a complete replacement. The in-the-wild generalization claim (Fig. 5) remains qualitative, and the QM score, while useful, is a metric introduced by the authors, so independent validation of the diversity-quality tradeoff is still needed. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences, more complex multi-person scenes, or truly unconstrained video without any 3D trajectory signal.
