## Problem

Prior 3D scene generation methods either produce visible grid-like artifacts (SynCity's tile-based approach), suffer from geometric holes and limited resolution (3DTown, NuiScene), or require large-scale domain-specific 3D scene datasets that do not exist at scale. The paper addresses how to generate globally coherent 3D scenes of arbitrary size and complexity from text prompts without grid constraints, without brittle heuristics, and without relying on unavailable scene-scale training data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a theme prompt (e.g., 'a bustling medieval town square'), a style prompt (e.g., 'a bright sunny day'), and optionally a set of local prompts each tied to a specific spatial window for fine-grained layout control. It produces a full 3D scene as 3D Gaussian Splats renderable from arbitrary viewpoints. Internally, the pipeline first generates a dimetric 2D template (1344×672 px by default, scalable to 3136×1568 px) using 896-px-wide overlapping windows, then converts it to 3D with a latent resolution chosen as integer multiples of the core patch size, giving independent control over scene extent and detail level.

## Method

SynCity 3000 is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 generates a 2D dimetric scene template by running a latent diffusion model (Flux ControlNet in inpainting mode) over partially overlapping sliding windows, averaging noise estimates across all windows at every denoising step (MultiDiffusion-style) to ensure global coherence. Stage 2 fine-tunes TRELLIS's two diffusion generators (sparse voxel structure and structured latents) to operate in a convolutional manner: the latent grid is divided into overlapping windows, each conditioned on a corresponding masked image crop, with additional 3D context tokens (surrounding voxels) and extended positional encodings to provide geometric context beyond the core. To prevent catastrophic forgetting, training alternates between the convolutional objective (core + context) and the original TRELLIS objective (core only) with probability p=0.5, applying loss only to the core. The fine-tuning is enabled by a synthetic dataset engine that procedurally places Objaverse-XL objects on randomized terrains in Blender, rendering 320k dimetric views with DINOv2 features projected onto voxel grids.

## Results

On template faithfulness (Table 1), SynCity 3000 achieves LPIPS 0.3993, SSIM 0.5247, and PSNR 14.41 dB, outperforming TRELLIS (0.4094 / 0.4966 / 13.59), TripoSG (0.4182 / 0.4953 / 12.28), and Hunyuan3D-2.1 (0.4689 / 0.4490 / 11.54). On geometric reconstruction quality (Table 3), the method maintains consistent performance across scene scales: at the larger 2240×1120 px template, it achieves F-score 0.7536 and PSNR 12.61 dB versus TRELLIS's 0.6967 and 11.64 dB, while Chamfer distance improves from 0.0392 to 0.0302. In user studies (N=27), the method wins 100% of layout-control comparisons against SynCity and 71.6–100% of faithfulness comparisons against image-to-3D baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TRELLIS [62], the primary image-to-3D baseline, SynCity 3000 wins on all three template-faithfulness metrics (LPIPS 0.3993 vs 0.4094, SSIM 0.5247 vs 0.4966, PSNR 14.41 vs 13.59) and on geometric quality at both scales (F-score 0.7029 vs 0.6685 at 1344×672; 0.7536 vs 0.6967 at 2240×1120), though Chamfer distance is marginally worse at the smaller scale (0.0166 vs 0.0137) but clearly better at the larger scale (0.0302 vs 0.0392). Against TripoSG [35] and Hunyuan3D-2.1 [24], the method dominates on all faithfulness metrics, with Hunyuan3D-2.1 showing the largest gap (LPIPS 0.4689 vs 0.3993), and users prefer SynCity 3000 100% of the time in forced-choice faithfulness comparisons. Against SynCity [15], the direct predecessor, users unanimously (100%) prefer SynCity 3000's layout control, and 63% prefer its overall generated scenes, with qualitative comparisons (Fig. 6) showing the elimination of the grid-like tile artifacts. Against NuiScene [31] and 3DTown [80], the method wins 74.1% and 59.3% of user preference votes respectively for overall scene quality, with the 3DTown comparison being the closest, likely because 3DTown also leverages TRELLIS with a depth-based geometric prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SynCity (same group) by replacing its fixed grid-of-tiles generation with a convolutional sliding-window approach inspired by MultiDiffusion, enabling globally coherent scenes of arbitrary size. The 3D stage repurposes TRELLIS, an object-centric structured-latent diffusion model, by fine-tuning it for convolutional inference with context tokens and masked conditioning—adapting an object-scale architecture to scene-scale without redesigning it from scratch. The synthetic data engine (random Objaverse-XL objects on procedural terrain) addresses the data scarcity that constrains other scene-generation methods like LT3SD, X³, and NuiScene, which all require large domain-specific 3D scene datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The convolutional adaptation of an off-the-shelf object-centric 3D generator to scene-scale inference is a general recipe that could transfer to other image-to-3D models, and the synthetic data engine offers a practical workaround for the persistent lack of large-scale 3D scene training data. However, the pipeline remains tied to dimetric (top-down) projection, limiting its applicability to scenes best viewed from above, and the synthetic training data (random objects on flat terrain) is far from the complexity of real urban or natural environments. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger scenes without quality degradation, whether more realistic synthetic data (e.g., with physics-based placement) would improve generalization, and whether the convolutional fine-tuning strategy transfers to other 3D generation backbones.
