## Problem

Generating physically valid origami folding sequences from sparse text descriptions remains an open challenge: generative foundation models produce semantically plausible but physically infeasible fold sequences, while optimization-based computational origami methods enforce geometric validity but require precise 3D mesh inputs. The core difficulty is that origami operates under strict topological and kinematic constraints (flat-foldability, self-intersection avoidance) where a single invalid crease in early steps can render all subsequent folds mathematically infeasible, demanding long-horizon constructive reasoning that jointly satisfies physical laws and semantic intent.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text prompt (semantic goal) and a crease pattern (CP) graph with vertices in [0,1]², edges carrying mountain/valley/unknown labels, and a dynamic state vector tracking signed dihedral angles, progress ratios, and crease types. The CP is canonicalized via lexicographic vertex reindexing and dihedral symmetry augmentation to ensure permutation invariance. Output is a structured folding program: an autoregressive sequence of tokens from a unified vocabulary combining discrete operation tokens, canonicalized graph-index tokens, and quantized continuous geometric parameters (fold angles, progress ratios), specifying which edges to fold, in what direction, and to what extent at each step.

## Method

Learn2Fold is a neuro-symbolic framework that decouples semantic proposal from physical verification through three coupled components. A lightweight decoder-only transformer (fine-tuned with LoRA via MLE on ~10⁴ expert folding steps) proposes structured action tokens conditioned on the canonicalized CP graph and text goal. A graph-structured world model, trained on ~76K synthetic transitions from a deterministic Level-0 simulator with counterfactual perturbations, learns residual state updates and per-edge constraint-violation likelihoods, serving as a differentiable surrogate simulator for short-horizon lookahead. At inference, a model-predictive-control loop samples K=8 candidate actions via nucleus sampling, filters them through a hard symbolic constraint kernel (Level-0 simulator) that discards invalid folds and provides diagnostic failure reasons, then ranks surviving candidates by fusing proposal log-likelihood, goal-progress cost, and the world model's soft violation scores; if no valid candidate passes a confidence threshold, the system constructs a negative constraint from the predicted violation mask and re-samples.

## Results

On the OrigamiCode benchmark (25 categories, 3,840 prompts), Learn2Fold achieves a step-level F1 of 0.739, substantially outperforming the strongest LLM baseline (GPT-5.1, F1 = 0.266) by +47.3 points. At the trajectory level, Learn2Fold reaches a Category Success Rate of 0.891 and Edge-IoU of 0.582, compared to 0.675 and 0.132 for the best baselines (GPT-5.1 and GPT-5.2 respectively). The method also attains the highest Precision (0.766) and Recall (0.711), indicating both accurate and complete folding action prediction across all difficulty tiers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Learn2Fold dominates all four baselines across every reported metric. Against the LLM baselines (Gemini, GPT-5.1, GPT-5.2), the gap is most pronounced in Precision (0.766 vs. 0.124–0.287) and Edge-IoU (0.582 vs. 0.094–0.132), reflecting that unconstrained LLMs propose semantically plausible but geometrically misaligned actions. GPT-5.1 achieves the highest Cat-SR among LLM baselines (0.675) but still trails Learn2Fold by 21.6 points, while GPT-5.2 collapses to 0.160 Cat-SR, suggesting severe long-horizon failure. Against BrickGPT (the only finetuned, physics-aware baseline), Learn2Fold improves F1 by +49.3 points (0.739 vs. 0.246) and Edge-IoU by +53.2 points (0.582 vs. 0.051); BrickGPT's reactive rollback yields higher Precision than raw LLMs (0.397) but its trial-and-error strategy produces incomplete sequences, limiting Recall to 0.225. Overall, the results indicate that neither unconstrained semantic generation nor reactive rollback alone suffices for long-horizon origami, and that the neuro-symbolic combination of LM proposal, world-model lookahead, and hard constraint verification is necessary for robust performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three largely disjoint threads: (1) optimization-based computational origami (TreeMaker, Origamizer) that guarantees physical validity but requires precise mesh inputs; (2) generative LLM/VLM approaches that handle semantic intent but produce physically infeasible sequences; and (3) world-model planning from model-based RL, repurposed here as a differentiable surrogate simulator over crease-pattern graphs. Rather than building on a single prior method, Learn2Fold synthesizes LLM-based program induction, graph-structured world models, and MPC into a unified neuro-symbolic pipeline, with the key architectural departure being the explicit decoupling of semantic proposal from physical verification. The closest prior work, BrickGPT, also combines autoregressive proposals with a physics verifier, but Learn2Fold replaces reactive rollback with proactive world-model lookahead and causal failure attribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that physically valid long-horizon action generation from sparse text is achievable through neuro-symbolic coupling, offering a template applicable to other constraint-heavy constructive tasks beyond origami. The OrigamiCode dataset (5,760 sequences, 75,000 trajectories) and the Level-0 simulator provide reusable infrastructure for the community. However, OOD trajectory success remains modest (27.7%), the evaluation is limited to 25 categories on a single GPU, and the baselines (prompted GPT-5.x, finetuned BrickGPT) are relatively weak, leaving open whether the large reported gaps persist against stronger domain-adapted competitors. A key open question is whether the learned world model generalizes to crease patterns with fundamentally different topologies or whether it merely interpolates within the training distribution.
