## Problem

State-of-the-art learned image compression requires independently trained models for each rate-distortion operating point, inflating storage and deployment costs. Existing variable-rate approaches that condition a shared dense backbone via affine modulation or latent scaling suffer from severe gradient conflict: low-rate smoothing gradients destructively interfere with high-rate detail preservation, causing unified models to consistently underperform their single-rate counterparts.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a discrete rate parameter λ (one of six Lagrange multipliers) and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed image. Because the routing network and CAT modulations depend solely on the rate embedding λ_e and not on the image content, expert assignments and sub-band scaling parameters can be precomputed and cached in a lookup table, introducing zero routing overhead at inference. Training uses 256×256 random crops from OpenImages; evaluation spans Kodak (768×512), Tecnick (1200×1200), and CLIC Professional sets (up to 2K resolution).

## Method

MixCompress replaces dense bottleneck layers in the analysis transform, synthesis transform, and hyper-prior network with sparsely gated Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) or Mixture-of-Depths (MoD) blocks. Each block contains a shared expert plus N=4 routed experts, with top-K=2 selection driven by a router conditioned only on the rate embedding λ_e; this structural isolation confines gradient conflict to the shared expert while decoupling low-rate and high-rate updates into disjoint expert subspaces. The MoD variant further scales representational capacity by stacking progressively deeper Bi-RWKV blocks in higher-index experts, so high-rate samples access more compute. Complementing the routing, Conditional Auxiliary Transforms (CAT) apply a 2D Haar wavelet decomposition whose sub-band energy scaling is modulated by a FiLM head conditioned on λ_e, enabling rate-adaptive frequency compaction without altering the entropy model. Additive Gaussian noise with linear annealing (σ₀=1.0, E_anneal=20) stabilizes expert utilization during training, and the entire framework is trained for the same duration as a single single-rate model while jointly optimizing all six rate points.

## Results

On the four standard benchmarks (Kodak, CLIC-V, CLIC-T, Tecnick), MixCompress-LALIC-MoD achieves BD-rate(PSNR) savings of −20.12%, −25.73%, −28.71%, and −28.88% relative to VTM-23.1, respectively. The homogeneous MoE variant achieves −18.81%, −24.16%, −26.80%, and −27.05%. Both variants are trained as a single unified model for the same duration as one single-rate checkpoint, yet surpass the individually optimized LALIC baseline (−18.81%, −23.96%, −26.43%, −26.03%) on all four datasets. Inference latency increases only marginally (0.257 s encode / 0.190 s decode for MoD vs. 0.234 s / 0.184 s for LALIC), with 120 M active parameters out of 156 M total.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest single-rate baseline LALIC [13], MixCompress-LALIC-MoD wins on all four datasets by 1.3–2.9 BD-rate points (e.g., −28.88% vs. −26.03% on Tecnick), while the MoE variant matches LALIC exactly on Kodak (−18.81%) and leads by 0.2–1.0 points elsewhere. Against the other single-rate SOTA methods FAT [22] and TCM [24], MoD leads by 3.0–8.5 points across all benchmarks. Against the two re-implemented dense-modulation VBR baselines, the gap is stark: LALIC-QRAF [29] loses 10.8–14.5 points to MoD, and LALIC-CondConv [9] loses 5.0–7.3 points, confirming that dense parameter modulation suffers severe gradient interference. The paper also notes that QRAF's failure is compounded by an architectural incompatibility with Bi-RWKV's LayerNorm, which squashes the latent-scaling signal before it can influence downstream representations. Overall, MixCompress establishes that sparse structural specialization (MoE/MoD) plus rate-aware auxiliary transforms (CAT) closes and then exceeds the single-rate performance ceiling while requiring only one trained checkpoint instead of six.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends variable-rate learned image compression (VBR-LIC) by replacing the dense parameter modulation strategies (conditional convolutions, latent gain scaling) used in prior VBR methods like QRAF and CondConv with sparsely gated Mixture-of-Experts and Mixture-of-Depths modules. It builds on the LALIC and LIC-TCM single-rate backbones and the AuxT wavelet shortcut framework, extending the latter to rate-conditioned CAT transforms. The conceptual departure is framing the VBR performance gap as a gradient conflict problem in multi-objective optimization, drawing on MoE literature from NLP and vision to provide structural (rather than parametric) specialization across rate points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single MixCompress checkpoint replaces six independently trained single-rate models while matching or exceeding their performance, reducing storage from ~397M to ~156M parameters. The gradient conflict analysis (cosine similarity distributions, channel utilization heatmaps) provides a clear mechanistic diagnosis of why dense modulation fails, which could inform other multi-task or multi-objective learning settings. Open questions include whether the MoD depth-scaling advantage generalizes to video compression or continuous (rather than discrete) rate control, and whether the approach scales to larger backbones where expert count and routing overhead become more significant.
