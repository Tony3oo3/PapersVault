## Problem

Existing streaming VideoLLMs focus on efficient visual token management and memory retrieval but lack explicit analytical reasoning, while directly applying Chain-of-Thought after the user query incurs unacceptable response latency in real-time scenarios. The paper addresses how to achieve the accuracy gains of test-time scaling (CoT reasoning) without adding post-query latency, by shifting reasoning to the pre-query streaming phase.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260094/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a streaming video at 2 fps, accumulating frames into discrete clips whenever the visual token count reaches a preset capacity L, and receives a text query at an arbitrary point during the stream. During consumption, it emits intermediate streaming thoughts (short textual summaries) that are appended to a long-term FIFO textual memory; upon query arrival, it produces a final text response grounded in both the accumulated thoughts and the current short-term visual buffer (a sliding window of the most recent L visual tokens). The entire process operates under a fixed context window, with overlong sequences handled via temporal segmentation.

## Method

VST formulates streaming video understanding as a multi-turn conversation where the LLM (Qwen2.5-VL base) autoregressively generates a streaming thought z^k after each video clip c^k, conditioned on the accumulated textual memory m^{k-1}, before any query arrives. A streaming attention mask restricts visual-token attention to a sliding window of the latest L tokens while keeping all non-visual tokens fully visible, enforcing temporal causality during training. The two-stage post-training pipeline first applies VST-SFT (next-token prediction on synthesized streaming-thought sequences with the causal mask) to bootstrap the thinking-while-watching protocol, then VST-RL (GRPO with verifiable rewards on the final answer, where the group-relative advantage is assigned to all tokens in the trajectory) to refine reasoning quality end-to-end. Training data is synthesized via a knowledge-graph pipeline: entities and relations are extracted from video clips into a temporal graph, multi-hop evidence chains are sampled via DFS, and Gemini generates streaming CoT rationales aligned to those chains, yielding 100K high-quality samples.

## Results

On the primary online benchmark StreamingBench, VST-7B achieves 79.5% overall accuracy, surpassing the previous open-source SOTA Streamforest (77.3%) and proprietary GPT-4o (73.3%) by +2.2% and +6.2% respectively. On OVO-Bench, VST-7B reaches 59.3% overall, outperforming Streamo (57.9%) and Streamforest (55.6%). On the offline reasoning benchmark VideoHolmes, VST-7B scores 41.9%, exceeding Video-R1 (36.5%) by +5.4% while maintaining a QA latency of 0.56 s versus Video-R1's 8.80 s (15.7× faster).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On StreamingBench, VST-7B (79.5%) clearly outperforms all open-source online baselines—Streamforest (77.3%), TimeChatOnline (75.4%), and Dispider (67.6%)—and also surpasses proprietary GPT-4o (73.3%) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (75.7%) by 6.2% and 3.8% respectively. On OVO-Bench, VST-7B (59.3%) beats the strongest open-source online models Streamo (57.9%) and Streamforest (55.6%), but remains slightly below GPT-4o (59.5%) and notably below Gemini 1.5 Pro (63.0%). On the offline reasoning benchmark VideoHolmes, VST-7B (41.9%) exceeds Video-R1 (36.5%) by +5.4% while being 15.7× faster in QA latency (0.56 s vs. 8.80 s), though it is marginally below GPT-4o (42.0%). On LongVideoBench, VST-7B (58.0%) ties LongVILA-R1 (58.0%) and trails GPT-4o (66.7%) by 8.7%. On VideoMME (w/o subtitles, Overall), VST-7B (64.9%) is competitive with LongVILA-R1 (65.1%) and REVISOR (65.7%) but below the untrained Qwen2.5-VL-7B (73.7%), suggesting the streaming training slightly trades off offline generalization. Overall, VST-7B establishes a new open-source SOTA on both primary online benchmarks while remaining competitive on offline tasks, with the key differentiator being its ability to perform explicit CoT reasoning without incurring post-query latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: streaming video understanding (VideoLLM-online, Dispider, TimeChat-Online, Streamforest), which optimizes context-window efficiency for real-time perception but treats the LLM as a passive decoder, and test-time scaling via CoT/RL for VideoLLMs (Video-R1, LongVILA-R1, REVISOR), which improves reasoning but only post-query in offline settings. VST departs from both by introducing a 'thinking-while-watching' mechanism that interleaves autoregressive CoT generation with incoming video clips, amortizing reasoning latency over playback. The training recipe builds on Qwen2.5-VL and adapts the R1-style GRPO reinforcement learning paradigm to a multi-turn streaming conversation with a novel streaming attention mask that enforces temporal causality during SFT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VST addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying VideoLLMs for embodied AI and interactive assistants: the inability to perform explicit multi-step reasoning without violating real-time latency constraints. The key open question is whether the additional LLM token consumption for streaming thoughts (averaging 7 seconds per 16–32 second clip) remains acceptable at scale, and whether latent or sub-symbolic reasoning could reduce this overhead. The paper also notes that its text-guided memory management is orthogonal to existing visual memory mechanisms, leaving their combination as an unexplored direction. The consistent gains across 3B–32B scales suggest the paradigm is broadly applicable, but the reliance on a knowledge-graph-based synthesis pipeline for training data raises questions about generalization to domains where entity-relation structure is less clear.
