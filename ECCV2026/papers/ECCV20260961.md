## Problem

Mainstream latent diffusion models allocate latent tokens linearly with data scale (resolution, frame rate), implicitly assuming that larger inputs always contain more information. In practice, the information content of a sample is governed by its semantic and structural complexity, not its pixel count, so a fixed scale-to-token mapping is either wasteful for simple content or insufficient for complex content. This paper asks whether information complexity and data scale can be treated as orthogonal, independently controllable dimensions in a latent diffusion framework.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt or class label together with user-specified generation parameters: the number of hierarchical token levels (1 to n, controlling detail/quality) and the target output resolution and frame rate (chosen at decode time). The diffusion model produces a fixed-shape hierarchical latent tensor of shape (t/p_t) × (h/p_hw) × (w/p_hw) × n, where the spatial-temporal grid is determined by a fixed patch count k and k_t rather than by absolute resolution. The DCS-Tok decoder then maps this scale-independent latent to pixels at any resolution or frame rate, enabling the same latent to yield, e.g., a 256 px image or a 512 px image, or a 12 fps or 48 fps video, without re-running the diffusion model.

## Method

DCS-LDM comprises a scale-independent hierarchical tokenizer (DCS-Tok) and an extended diffusion transformer (DCS-DiT). DCS-Tok uses a 1D patchifier that fixes the number of spatial-temporal patches and scales patch size with input resolution and frame rate, so that the same semantic region maps to the same latent position regardless of scale; asymmetric training (randomly pairing different-scale variants of the same sample) makes the decoder resolution-agnostic. Each patch is encoded into n ordered latent tokens via learnable query tokens, with level causality enforced through Pixel-to-Latent Attention (PLA), Latent-to-Pixel Attention (LPA), and Level Causal Attention (LCA), ensuring that level-i tokens depend only on levels 0 through i. DCS-DiT extends the DiT architecture with AdaLN and optional cross-attention blocks and introduces a level-asynchronous timestep schedule: each level i is assigned a staggered SNR window [τ_i^s, τ_i^e] controlled by a scaling factor ω, so structural (early) levels consistently achieve higher SNR than detail (later) levels at any global timestep. A cache-based acceleration mechanism stores key/value tensors from already-generated levels, making sequential coarse-to-fine generation as computationally efficient as generating all levels in parallel, and allowing early termination once the user is satisfied with the current detail level.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 label-to-image, DCS-LDM (1 level) + REPA achieves gFID 2.44 (w/ CFG) and IS 277.3, competitive with LightningDiT (gFID 1.35, IS 295.3) and REPA (gFID 1.42, IS 305.7). On UCF101 24fps 256×256 label-to-video, DCS-LDM (1 level) achieves gFVD 77.10 (w/ CFG), close to VFRTok (71.34) and OmniTokenizer (88.89). The key efficiency advantage is at higher resolutions: at 512px, DCS-LDM with 1 token level completes generation in 1943 ms versus 6743 ms for DiT, a 3.5× latency reduction, while maintaining comparable quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet label-to-image, DCS-LDM (1 level) + REPA achieves gFID 2.44 w/ CFG, slightly behind LightningDiT (1.35) and REPA (1.42) but ahead of DiT (2.77), SiT (2.06), and DetailFlow (2.62); with full REPA integration it reaches gFID 1.39 and IS 311.6, marginally surpassing REPA and LightningDiT. Among autoregressive baselines, DCS-LDM outperforms LlamaGen (gFID 2.18) and MaskGiT (gFID 6.18 w/o CFG) but trails FlexTok (gFID 1.86 w/ CFG). On UCF101 label-to-video, DCS-LDM (gFVD 77.10 w/ CFG) is competitive with VFRTok (71.34) and OmniTokenizer (88.89), though slightly behind VFRTok on reconstruction (PSNR 29.88 vs. 31.68). The critical differentiator is efficiency: at 512px, DCS-LDM with 1 token level generates in 1943 ms versus 6743 ms for DiT (3.5× faster), and even 2 levels (3671 ms) remain nearly 2× faster, while DiT's latency scales linearly with resolution. The paper positions DCS-LDM as matching SOTA quality at the first token level while offering a unique flexible computation-quality tradeoff that no baseline provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCS-LDM builds on the DiT architecture [29] and LightningDiT's modern transformer components (SwiGLU, RMSNorm) [44], while adopting the 1D patchifier-depatchifier style and asymmetric training from VFRTok [50]. It departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm in latent diffusion where token count scales linearly with resolution or frame rate, instead proposing a hierarchical, scale-independent latent space where token *levels* encode complexity and the decoder handles arbitrary output scales. This positions it against FlexTok/SEMANTICIST (arbitrary-length latents for fixed complexity), VFRTok (fixed complexity, variable frame rate), and DetailFlow (sub-linear scale-complexity from an entropy view), by treating complexity and scale as fully orthogonal dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of complexity from scale offers a practical efficiency lever: generating a 512px image with one token level takes ~1943 ms versus 6743 ms for DiT, a 3.5× latency reduction, while the coarse-to-fine paradigm lets users preview structure cheaply before committing to full-detail generation. However, the system-level comparisons (Tables 2–3) use only the first token level, so the quantitative advantage of the full hierarchical design over SOTA at matched quality remains underdemonstrated. Open questions include how to optimally allocate token levels during generation (the content-adaptive allocation in §4.7 is preliminary), whether the 1D depatchifier's memory overhead scales acceptably at higher resolutions, and whether the level-asynchronous timestep strategy transfers to video generation at scale.
