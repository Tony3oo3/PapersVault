## Problem

Existing MLLM-based IQA methods rely on internally represented, semantically biased visual features that are insensitive to low-level perceptual degradations such as noise, blur, and compression artifacts. Text-only reasoning lacks verifiable visual anchors, while region-based approaches introduce unstructured pixel patches that still depend on the model's semantic interpretation rather than explicit perceptual evidence. This paper addresses the gap by grounding quality reasoning in structured, tool-generated visual evidence that directly isolates specific degradation attributes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261243/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a textual prompt (e.g., 'Assess the quality of this image') and produces a multi-step reasoning chain that interleaves tool invocations, the resulting visual evidence images (noise residual maps, gradient magnitude distributions, Fourier magnitude spectra, etc.), textual analysis of each evidence item, and a final quality score normalized to [1,5]. During inference the context grows dynamically as the model autonomously selects and invokes a subset of 15 available perceptual tools (averaging ~2.34 calls), so the effective input to the LLM expands beyond the original image tokens to include progressively acquired evidence images that are downsampled and tokenized for the language model's context window.

## Method

IQA-T1 augments a frozen MLLM backbone (Qwen3-VL-4B) with a library of 15 deterministic perceptual tools spanning seven quality-relevant attributes (structure, sharpness, noise, artifacts, luminance, color, naturalness), each mapping an input image to a structured visual representation that highlights degradation patterns invisible to standard MLLM encodings. Training proceeds in two stages: first, supervised fine-tuning on the newly constructed Q-Tool dataset (11k multimodal reasoning chains generated via GPT-4o under human-curated perceptual templates and verified across tool appropriateness, evidence grounding, and logical consistency) instills structured reasoning, correct tool-invocation syntax, and evidence-to-judgment association; second, GRPO reinforcement learning optimizes the adaptive tool-invocation policy using a four-component reward (format compliance, exponential scoring accuracy, a piecewise tool-usage reward encouraging 1–4 invocations, and a repetition penalty) that balances prediction accuracy against evidence efficiency and redundancy. The key distinction from prior region-based or text-only reasoning is that the model does not merely crop or describe the image but actively queries external analysis functions whose outputs serve as verifiable, attribute-specific visual evidence within the reasoning chain.

## Results

IQA-T1 achieves the best average PLCC/SRCC of 0.795/0.784 across seven IQA benchmarks (KonIQ, SPAQ, LIVE-Wild, KADID, PIPAL, CSIQ, AGIQA), surpassing all prior methods. It sets new state-of-the-art results on CSIQ (0.850/0.840), PIPAL (0.480/0.490), KonIQ (0.942/0.925), and SPAQ (0.905/0.908). The dynamic tool invocation strategy uses only 2.34 tools on average (vs. 15 for all-tools), with inference time of 5.09 s and peak memory of 8.67 GB on a single RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against handcrafted metrics (NIQE, BRISQUE) and non-MLLM deep learning models (NIMA, HyperIQA, DBCNN, MUSIQ, ManIQA), IQA-T1 achieves substantially higher PLCC/SRCC across all seven benchmarks, with the largest gaps on PIPAL (0.480 vs. 0.457 for ManIQA) and CSIQ (0.850 vs. 0.771 for MUSIQ). Among MLLM-based methods without reasoning (CLIP-IQA+, C2Score, Q-Align, DeQA), IQA-T1 matches or exceeds score accuracy while additionally producing interpretable, evidence-grounded reasoning chains; notably, DeQA leads on KonIQ (0.953 vs. 0.942) but IQA-T1 surpasses it on CSIQ (0.850 vs. 0.787) and PIPAL (0.480 vs. 0.472). Among MLLM-based reasoning methods, IQA-T1 achieves the best average PLCC/SRCC (0.795/0.784) versus Zoom-IQA (0.787/0.768), VisualQuality-R1 (0.771/0.748), and Q-Insight (0.765/0.740). It wins decisively on CSIQ (+0.053 PLCC over Zoom-IQA) and PIPAL (+0.012 PLCC over Zoom-IQA), but trails slightly on KADID (0.686 vs. 0.703 for VisualQuality-R1) and AGIQA (0.754 SRCC vs. 0.760 for VisualQuality-R1), suggesting the tool-based evidence paradigm is most beneficial for synthetic and algorithm-induced distortions where low-level cues dominate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant MLLM-based IQA paradigm (text-only reasoning à la Q-Insight, VisualQuality-R1, and region-based visual reasoning à la Zoom-IQA, Q-Probe) by replacing unstructured pixel patches or purely semantic inference with structured, tool-generated visual evidence (noise residual maps, Fourier spectra, gradient coherence maps). Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of the 'Thinking with Images' tool-driven exploration line and traditional signal-processing IQA, repurposing classical perceptual analysis operators as callable tools within an MLLM reasoning loop. The training recipe combines SFT on a purpose-built dataset with GRPO-based RL (borrowed from the DeepSeek-R1 lineage) to learn an adaptive tool-invocation policy, a combination not previously applied to IQA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation of MLLMs—their insensitivity to low-level degradations due to semantic bias—and provides a practical, interpretable remedy that produces evidence-grounded rationales rather than opaque scores. The Q-Tool dataset (11k verified reasoning chains) is the first resource enabling this paradigm, lowering the barrier for future work. Open questions include whether the fixed library of 15 tools scales to novel or compound degradations, whether the approach transfers to other low-level vision tasks (e.g., video quality, 3D quality), and whether the modest inference overhead (≈5 s, 8.7 GB peak) remains acceptable at deployment scale. The framework's generality across backbones (Qwen3-VL-2B/4B, InternVL3.5-4B) suggests the tool-evidence mechanism is architecture-agnostic, but the absolute performance gap between backbones hints that the approach may still benefit from stronger base models.
