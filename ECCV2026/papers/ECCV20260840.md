## Problem

DETR-based detectors underperform RCNN- and YOLO-series methods on cervical lesion detection due to three compounding challenges: large intra-class scale variation (single cells to large clusters), subtle inter-class morphological differences among lesion subtypes, and high inter-annotator variability that introduces noisy bounding boxes. Existing DETR adaptations for this task lack mechanisms to stabilize query matching under these conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260840/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB cervical cytology images of varying resolutions (e.g., 2048×2048 px for HMCHH, 1376×1020 px for CRIC) and produces bounding boxes with lesion class labels (11 abnormal cell types on CDetector, a single 'abnormal' class on CRIC/HMCHH). During training, Cellpose is run zero-shot on each image to generate multiscale cell priors at three diameter settings (15, 120, 240 px), but at inference the auxiliary branch is entirely discarded and only the main DINO branch outputs predictions without NMS.

## Method

CerDETR augments the DINO detector with a training-only Prior Corrector branch that shares the transformer decoder and prediction head weights with the main branch, so its auxiliary loss provides additional gradients for joint optimization. The branch comprises three components: (1) Auto MPG, which exploits Cellpose's flow-field divergence for boundary delineation and magnitude for cell-body probability to generate multiscale priors covering nuclei, cytoplasm, and clusters without additional training; (2) an O2M ICG matching strategy that assigns priors to GT boxes using IoU thresholds plus a containment rule (assigning ambiguous priors to the smallest encompassing GT), increasing positive sample density and tolerating annotation noise; and (3) a Prior Query Enhance module that sums sinusoidal position encoding with learnable label and scale embeddings into each prior query, injecting semantic and size context to improve intra-class scale modeling and inter-class discrimination. At inference, the entire auxiliary branch is removed, leaving a standard DETR pipeline with no additional overhead.

## Results

On the representative multi-class CDetector dataset, CerDETR achieves 39.1% AP, 68.1% AP50, 39.8% AP75, and 66.2% AR, surpassing the best RCNN baseline (DCC-MSI, 36.1% AP) by +3.0 and the best DETR baseline (DEIM, 35.4% AP) by +3.7. On CRIC and HMCHH, CerDETR reaches 41.4% and 41.7% AP respectively, outperforming all RCNN, YOLO, and DETR baselines by 4–6 AP points. On the out-of-domain BCCD blood cell dataset, CerDETR achieves 65.8% AP and 76.6% AR, exceeding the strongest baseline (DEIM, 63.2% AP) by +2.6 AP, confirming strong cross-domain generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CerDETR achieves the best AP on all three cervical lesion datasets, outperforming every RCNN, YOLO, and DETR baseline. On CDetector, it surpasses the strongest RCNN method DCC-MSI (36.1% AP) by +3.0, the best YOLO variant YOLOv12-l (35.2%) by +3.9, and the best prior DETR method DEIM (35.4%) by +3.7. On CRIC, the margin is largest: +6.2 AP over DPD-Net (35.2%) and +4.6 over DINO (36.8%), the strongest DETR baseline on that dataset. On HMCHH, CerDETR leads YOLOv12-l (37.5%) by +4.2 AP and DEIM (37.7%) by +4.0 AP. For cross-domain generalization on BCCD, CerDETR (65.8% AP, 76.6% AR) exceeds DEIM (63.2%) by +2.6 AP and DINO (61.3%) by +4.5 AP, while matching or exceeding the inference speed of comparable DETR models (20.1 FPS, same as DINO). Notably, task-specific DETR methods (Fei et al., GAA-DETR) underperform even generic DETR baselines on CDetector, highlighting the difficulty of the task and the effectiveness of CerDETR's cell-prior design.

## Relation to Prior Work

CerDETR builds on DINO as its main detection backbone and repurposes Cellpose (a generalist cell segmentation model) as a training-free prior generator, creating a bridge between segmentation and detection that prior cervical lesion DETR work (Fei et al., GAA-DETR) did not exploit. Conceptually, it departs from the standard DETR paradigm of learned queries by introducing a training-only auxiliary Prior Corrector branch that injects spatially grounded, multiscale cell priors into the transformer decoder, with a novel ICG matching strategy that combines IoU and containment cues to handle annotation noise. The Prior Query Enhance module extends the query embedding space with learnable label and scale embeddings, a design choice not present in prior DETR variants applied to cellular detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cervical cancer remains the fourth most common cancer worldwide, and reliable automated screening is critical; CerDETR's consistent SOTA across three cervical datasets plus generalization to blood cell detection suggests the cell-prior-guided DETR paradigm could transfer to other cellular analysis tasks. Key limitations include the small scale of existing cervical datasets (a few thousand images), persistent annotation subjectivity that the ICG strategy mitigates but does not eliminate, and the fact that Auto MPG is currently training-free due to incomplete single-cell annotations. Open questions include whether a learnable prior generator (enabled by better cell-level annotations) could further improve performance, and whether the approach scales to whole-slide-image analysis or larger multi-center datasets.
