## Problem

3DGS-based SLAM systems suffer from unbounded Gaussian primitive growth during online mapping, imposing prohibitive GPU memory and rendering latency that scale nearly linearly with trajectory length. Existing compaction strategies rely on heuristic importance-score pruning or offline post-processing, which lack global fidelity guarantees, ignore intrinsic statistical overlap between primitives, and disrupt gradient continuity required for stable online tracking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260928/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MASC operates as a plug-and-play module consuming the current 3D Gaussian primitive map (each primitive parameterized by a 3D spatial mean, 3×3 covariance, opacity scalar, and spherical-harmonics coefficient vector) and producing a compacted set of 'Super-Gaussians' with approximately 60% fewer primitives. The compaction is triggered dynamically during the mapping loop based on iteration count, and the output map is immediately usable for both rendering and pose optimization without re-initialization. The method is modality-agnostic, accepting either monocular RGB or RGB-D input streams through its host SLAM backbone.

## Method

MASC reformulates online map reduction as a continuous signal reconstruction task on a Riemannian manifold. It first constructs a topology-aware graph whose edge weights are derived from Symmetrized Kullback-Leibler divergence between Gaussian PDFs (capturing statistical overlap rather than Euclidean proximity), then performs an incremental Nyström spectral embedding with O(nm) complexity to project the map into a low-dimensional eigenspace. Redundant primitives are identified via K-Means clustering in this spectral space, and each cluster is collapsed into a single Super-Gaussian whose covariance is computed as the exact Bures-Wasserstein barycenter (solved via closed-form fixed-point iteration), guaranteeing conservation of first- and second-order radiance field moments. The final retention ratio is not hard-coded but adaptively governed by an objective that jointly minimizes spectral reconstruction error and a volumetric dilation penalty on the aggregated covariances.

## Results

On the Replica dataset (RGB-D), MASC+DROID-Splat achieves an ATE of 0.29 cm with PSNR 39.16 dB and SSIM 1.000 while reducing peak GPU memory from 14.0 GB to 5.0 GB (2.8× reduction) and boosting rendering throughput from 145 to 350 FPS (2.4× speedup) at ~60% primitive reduction. On TUM RGB-D, MASC+Photo-SLAM improves tracking accuracy from 1.32 cm to 0.98 cm ATE at 40% retention, while MASC+DROID-Splat maintains SSIM ≥ 0.99 across all sequences. The method achieves near-lossless fidelity (PSNR drop < 0.5 dB) compared to uncompressed baselines while eliminating ~60% of redundant Gaussians.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DROID-Splat (the strongest 3DGS-SLAM baseline), MASC matches tracking accuracy exactly (ATE 0.29 cm) while cutting GPU memory by 64% (14→5 GB) and more than doubling rendering speed (145→350 FPS), at the cost of a negligible 0.5 dB PSNR drop. Against Photo-SLAM, MASC+Photo-SLAM improves ATE from 0.60 to 0.30 cm (50% reduction) and boosts tracking FPS from 42 to 65, demonstrating that spectral aggregation acts as a geometric regularizer for odometry. Compared to Gaussian-SLAM, which reports the highest PSNR (42.08 dB) but no tracking or resource metrics, MASC+DROID-Splat achieves competitive rendering quality (39.16 dB) with full SLAM functionality and 2.8× lower memory. Against Splat-SLAM, MASC+DROID-Splat achieves better ATE (0.29 vs 0.35 cm) and comparable LPIPS (0.037 vs 0.060). On TUM RGB-D, MASC+DROID-Splat matches or exceeds DROID-Splat on SSIM (0.99 across all sequences) while MASC+Photo-SLAM achieves the best ATE on fr1/desk (1.50 cm vs 1.54 cm baseline), confirming that compaction can actively improve tracking in dynamic scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

MASC builds directly on the DROID-Splat and Photo-SLAM 3DGS-SLAM backbones, inserting its compaction module as a plug-and-play component within their mapping loops. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant heuristic-pruning paradigm (importance-score-based removal, Euclidean spatial grouping) and from offline structural aggregation methods (Gaussian mixture reduction, optimal-transport-based herding) by reframing map reduction as a continuous spectral signal reconstruction problem on a Riemannian manifold. The theoretical toolkit is drawn from graph signal processing (Nyström spectral embedding, Dirichlet energy analysis) and optimal transport (Bures-Wasserstein barycenters), neither of which had previously been applied to online 3DGS-SLAM map maintenance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 2.8× GPU memory reduction and 2.4× rendering speedup while maintaining or even improving tracking accuracy makes long-term 3DGS-SLAM deployment on resource-constrained platforms substantially more feasible. The counterintuitive finding that aggressive compaction can act as a geometric regularizer that improves ATE (e.g., 1.32→0.98 cm on TUM) challenges the assumption that removing primitives always degrades tracking. Open questions include whether the spectral approach scales to very long trajectories (hours of continuous mapping), whether it generalizes to dynamic scenes with moving objects, and whether the Nyström landmark selection strategy remains stable under non-stationary map growth patterns.
