## Problem

Vision-based gait models have historically failed to follow empirical scaling trends: performance saturates or degrades when backbone size exceeds ~10M parameters, and no single framework unifies the traditionally separate problem spaces of gait recognition and gait-based healthcare analysis. This paper asks why gait models cannot scale and whether a single self-supervised foundation model can serve both identification and clinical tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260734/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes binary silhouette sequences at 64x44 resolution, sampled as non-overlapping clips of 16 frames each, and can also accept human parsing maps as an alternative or complementary modality. During pretraining, each sequence yields two augmented views (teacher and student clips); during downstream use, the frozen teacher encoder produces a spatiotemporally pooled gait representation that feeds a task-specific head for rank-1 identity retrieval, scoliosis/depression binary classification, or multi-attribute gait estimation.

## Method

FoundationGait builds on a BYOL-style teacher-student contrastive framework (InfoNCE with EMA teacher updates) but introduces a part-aware encoding strategy in the student branch: each clip is horizontally split into p parts, each part is encoded independently through the full backbone, and the resulting part feature maps are reassembled into a global representation. The key insight is that applying part pooling late on an already-computed feature map is too late for large models, as deep networks cause part features to entangle and become homogeneous, so part-awareness must be enforced throughout the encoding process to preserve fine-grained local body-part diversity. A hyperparameter set P={1,2,4,8} is used during training, with each sub-batch assigned a different p value, and a shift-window operation along the height dimension adds local variation. The model (DeepGaitV2 backbone scaled to 0.13B parameters) is pretrained on WebGait-2M, a curated collection of 2.35M sequences from 12 public datasets spanning recognition and healthcare domains, and demonstrates that this part-aware design enables consistent scaling gains from 0.006B to 0.13B where prior methods fail.

## Results

In self-supervised evaluation (no fine-tuning), FoundationGait-0.13B pretrained on WebGait-2M achieves 48.0% rank-1 on Gait3D and 64.5% rank-1 on OU-MVLP, setting new milestones for robust cross-condition gait recognition. After fine-tuning, the 0.13B model reaches 87.2% mean accuracy on CCPG (matching the RGB-based BigGait), 79.3% rank-1 / 74.0% mAP on Gait3D, and 53.25% rank-1 on CCGR-MINI. On healthcare tasks, fine-tuned FoundationGait-0.03B attains 97.3% accuracy on Scoliosis1K and 75.1% accuracy on D-Gait, while linear probing alone yields 85.7% accuracy on RA-GAR gait attributes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary self-supervised baseline GaitSSB [10], FoundationGait-0.13B (WebGait-2M) achieves substantial gains: +20.6% rank-1 on Gait3D (48.0% vs 27.4%), +20.1% on OU-MVLP (64.5% vs 44.4%), and +11.3% on CCPG-CL (27.6% vs 16.3%). Notably, scaling GaitSSB from 0.006B to 0.13B actually degrades performance (e.g., −5.5% on OU-MVLP), whereas FoundationGait scales monotonically from 0.03B to 0.13B. In fine-tuning, FoundationGait-0.13B surpasses the supervised DeepGaitV2 [9] on all three recognition benchmarks: +3.9% mean on CCPG (87.2% vs 83.3%), +4.9% rank-1 on Gait3D (79.3% vs 74.4%), and +4.9% rank-1 on CCGR-MINI (79.3% vs 74.4%). Using only sparse silhouettes, FoundationGait-0.13B matches the RGB-based BigGait [85] on CCPG (87.2% vs 87.2% mean) and approaches the SoTA RGB model BiggerGait [84] (93.0% mean). On healthcare, fine-tuned FoundationGait-0.03B outperforms ScoNet-MT [97] on Scoliosis1K (97.3% vs 95.2% accuracy) and CLIP-GAR [67] on RA-GAR linear probe (85.7% vs 84.3% accuracy, +2.2% F1), demonstrating strong cross-task transfer without task-specific architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

FoundationGait extends the BYOL-style self-supervised pretraining framework of GaitSSB [10] and the DeepGaitV2 CNN backbone [9], but departs critically from both by introducing a part-aware encoding strategy that splits input silhouettes into horizontal strips *before* backbone processing rather than applying part-pooling to already-computed feature maps. This addresses a diagnosed failure mode absent from prior work: as gait models grow beyond ~10M parameters, part features entangle and become homogeneous, causing performance to saturate or degrade. The paper also positions itself against the LVM-transfer direction (BigGait, BiggerGait) by demonstrating that a from-scratch, silhouette-only model with the right architectural inductive bias can match or exceed RGB-based LVM approaches on recognition benchmarks, while additionally covering healthcare tasks that LVM-transfer methods ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that vision-based gait models can follow empirical scaling trends (up to 20×), and the WebGait-2M corpus (2.35M sequences from 12 datasets) provides a much more diverse pretraining resource than the previously dominant GaitLU-1M. The unification of recognition and healthcare tasks under one pretrained model opens a path toward practical, multi-purpose gait analysis systems. Key open questions remain: whether the part-aware strategy transfers to ViT-based backbones (crucial for bridging with LLMs), whether scaling beyond 0.13B toward 1B continues to help, and how to extend the framework to RGB inputs where the field is increasingly moving. The 0.13B model's tendency to over-predict positives on class-imbalanced healthcare tasks also suggests that size and calibration interact in ways not yet understood.
