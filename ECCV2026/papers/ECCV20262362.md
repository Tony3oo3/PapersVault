## Problem

Rectified Flow has become the dominant paradigm for efficient image generation, yet its memorization dynamics remain largely unexplored. Existing membership inference attacks (MIAs) developed for diffusion models do not extend to flow matching, and standard generalization-gap metrics fail to capture the time-dependent, sample-specific memorization inherent to RF's continuous ODE trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262362/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack takes a candidate image x (32×32 or 64×64 RGB, or 512×512 in VAE latent space for MSCOCO), a trained RF velocity network v_θ, and a continuous timestep t ∈ [0,1]. It produces a scalar membership score T_mc_cal(x, t) computed over N=5 Monte Carlo noise draws; lower scores indicate higher likelihood of training-set membership. The score is evaluated across the full t-range to expose the temporal memorization profile, and a single threshold on the score yields a binary member/non-member label.

## Method

The paper progressively constructs three test statistics from the FM/CFM training objectives: T_naive (single-noise reconstruction error), T_mc (Monte Carlo-averaged velocity prediction error), and T_mc_cal (T_mc divided by JPEG-compressed byte length to remove spatial-complexity bias). The key theoretical insight is that at t=0.5 the covariance Σ_{x_t,v}=(2t−1)I vanishes, making the noisy input statistically orthogonal to the target velocity and forcing the network to exploit non-linear capacity to memorize sample-specific features—this is formalized via the Linearity Gap between v_θ and the closed-form LMMSE estimator v_linear. As a mitigation, the paper replaces uniform timestep sampling with a Symmetric Exponential (U-shaped) distribution p(t;α) that concentrates training density near the boundaries t→0 and t→1, thereby reducing exposure to the vulnerable intermediate regime while preserving FID.

## Results

The complexity-calibrated test statistic T_mc_cal achieves AUC of 84.89% (CIFAR-10), 79.43% (SVHN), and 92.96% (TinyImageNet), with TPR@1%FPR of 27.88%, 16.46%, and 50.03% respectively. Compared to the uncalibrated T_mc baseline, calibration boosts AUC by 8.5–16.5 points and TPR@1%FPR by 14.9–44.7 points across the three datasets. The naive single-sample statistic T_naive achieves the weakest performance (AUC 68–72%, TPR@1%FPR 1.9–4.1%), confirming that both Monte Carlo averaging and complexity calibration are necessary for a strong MIA on Rectified Flow.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's internal baselines are the two earlier test statistics: T_naive (single-sample FM-based) and T_mc (Monte Carlo CFM-based). T_mc consistently outperforms T_naive in AUC across all three datasets (+2.9 to +4.1 points), though it slightly underperforms on SVHN TPR@1%FPR (1.54% vs. 1.89%), likely due to the small number of MC samples. The complexity-calibrated T_mc_cal is the clear winner, surpassing T_mc by 8.5–16.5 AUC points and 14.9–44.7 TPR@1%FPR points, with the largest gains on TinyImageNet (the most visually diverse dataset). The paper also notes that T_mc underperforms T_naive at N_mc=1 but surpasses it from N_mc≥2 onward, and prescribes N_mc=5 as the practical operating point. No external MIA baselines from the diffusion-model literature are directly benchmarked in a head-to-head table; the comparison is entirely within the proposed statistic family.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the membership inference attack (MIA) literature—previously developed for diffusion models (Duan et al., Fu et al., Kong et al.)—to the Rectified Flow framework by deriving test statistics directly from the Flow Matching and Conditional Flow Matching objectives. It draws on the LMMSE/Wiener filter analysis used in diffusion model generalization studies (Kadkhodaie et al., Li et al.) to formalize a 'linearity gap' that quantifies non-linear memorization, and repurposes the Symmetric Exponential timestep distribution from Lee et al. (NeurIPS 2024) with a novel justification rooted in memorization dynamics rather than loss landscape shape. The complexity calibration step builds on the input-complexity bias identified by Nalisnick et al. and Serrà et al. for likelihood-based generative models, adapting it to the RF reconstruction-error setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As Rectified Flow underpins major production systems (Stable Diffusion 3, FLUX, Ideogram), having a principled MIA and a lightweight training-time mitigation (U-shaped timestep sampling) that preserves FID is practically valuable for privacy auditing of these models. The midpoint-peak memorization result provides a concrete, testable prediction about where in the ODE trajectory to focus privacy defenses. Open questions include whether the orthogonality-based peak generalizes to non-rectified flow variants and multi-step (2-Rectified Flow) settings, whether the JPEG-based complexity calibration holds at higher resolutions or in latent spaces, and whether the U-shaped sampling interacts adversely with other regularization techniques at scale.
