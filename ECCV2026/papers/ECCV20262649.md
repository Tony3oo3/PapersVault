## Problem

Federated remote sensing semantic segmentation suffers from structured client heterogeneity: sensor diversity induces spectral (radiometric) shifts while geographic variability induces spatial (texture/morphology) shifts. Existing prototype-based federated methods treat this as a single unstructured distributional mismatch, enforcing one shared prototype per class that conflates both shift types and produces over-averaged, domain-inconsistent class representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262649/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds RS images (1024×1024 for LoveDA; 6000×6000 at 5 cm GSD for Potsdam; ~2494×2064 resampled to 5 cm for Vaihingen) in RGB or IRRG format paired with pixel-wise label maps over 5–7 land-cover classes. Raw images and dense feature maps never leave the client; only per-class sufficient statistics (sum and count of ℓ₂-normalized pixel embeddings) and compact modulator parameters are uploaded to the server. The output is a pixel-wise segmentation mask produced by a shared encoder-decoder backbone (ResNet-50 or SegFormer-B0) whose predictions are guided locally by domain-adaptive prototypes.

## Method

FedDap maintains server-side invariant class prototypes as shared semantic anchors, and each client learns a lightweight modulator that transforms these anchors into Domain-Adaptive Prototypes (DAPs) via two FiLM-based branches: a spectral branch that extracts channel-wise mean/std from shallow encoder features to correct sensor-induced radiometric shifts, and a spatial branch that concatenates multi-scale SPP with global statistics from deep features to accommodate region-dependent texture and morphology. A class-wise shift-aware gating network (sigmoid MLP over both shift descriptors) dynamically fuses the two branch outputs into the final DAPs. Training combines a pixel-to-prototype contrastive objective (cosine-similarity cross-entropy) against both the DAPs and the invariant anchors, plus a cosine-drift regularizer that penalizes prototype deformation. Federated optimization uses FedAvg for the backbone, count-weighted aggregation for global prototypes, and periodic cluster-wise synchronization of modulators based on uploaded shift signatures, ensuring DAPs remain local and privacy-preserving.

## Results

On the LoveDA Urban/Rural spatial-shift benchmark, FedDap achieves 50.68 mIoU, outperforming the strongest baseline FedSeg (50.04) by +0.64 points. On the Potsdam(IRRG) vs. Vaihingen(RGB) compound spectral+spatial shift, FedDap reaches 70.42 mIoU, surpassing FedSeg by +1.60 and FedAvg by +3.25 points, with the largest per-class gains on Bldg (+3.48) and Vegt (+4.11). Under the isolated spectral-shift setting (Potsdam RGB vs. IRRG), FedDap attains 75.80 mIoU vs. FedSeg's 74.70. On the cross-dataset LoveDA-RGB + Potsdam-IRRG setting with SegFormer-B0, FedDap achieves 39.25 global mIoU, exceeding FedSeg by 1.21 points and outperforming local-only training on both domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedDap consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluated scenario. Against the strongest prototype-based baseline FedSeg [18], FedDap gains +0.64 mIoU on LoveDA Urban/Rural, +2.37 mIoU on Potsdam(IRRG) vs. Vaihingen(RGB), and +1.10 mIoU on the isolated spectral-shift setting. Against FPL [6], the second-strongest method on the spatial-shift benchmark, FedDap leads by +0.35 mIoU (73.43 vs. 73.08) on Potsdam(IRRG) vs. Vaihingen(IRRG) and by +1.90 mIoU on the compound shift. Generic FL baselines FedAvg [17], MOON [10], FedProx [12], FedDyn [1], and FedOpt [20] all trail FedDap by 2–5 mIoU points on the compound spectral+spatial shift, with FedDyn and FedOpt being the weakest (65.78 and 65.77 mIoU respectively vs. FedDap's 70.42). FedProto [25], the closest prototype-sharing baseline, is outperformed by +0.71 mIoU on LoveDA and +0.71 mIoU on the compound shift, confirming that a single shared prototype per class is insufficient for structured RS heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedDap extends the prototype-based federated learning paradigm (FedProto, FPL) by replacing the single global prototype per class with Domain-Adaptive Prototypes generated through FiLM-style conditional modulation. The key conceptual departure is from prior work that treats client heterogeneity as an unstructured distributional mismatch: here, RS-specific domain shifts are explicitly decomposed into spectral (sensor/radiometric) and spatial (regional/structural) components, each handled by a dedicated modulation branch. The modulation mechanism itself draws on FiLM and AdaIN, but is applied to prototypes rather than feature maps, and is fused via a learned shift-aware gate rather than a fixed or cascaded composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured decomposition of RS domain shifts into spectral and spatial factors provides a principled, interpretable framework that goes beyond generic non-IID assumptions, and the privacy-preserving design (DAPs instantiated locally, never transmitted) makes it practical for real federated RS deployments. However, the evaluation is limited to two domain groups of five clients each, and the performance gains over strong baselines are modest (0.35–1.60 mIoU), suggesting the structured-shift hypothesis, while well-motivated, may not yet capture the full complexity of real-world RS heterogeneity. Open questions include scalability to more than two latent domains, handling highly imbalanced client participation, and reducing dependence on dense pixel-level labels.
