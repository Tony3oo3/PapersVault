## Problem

Existing diffusion-based approaches treat controllable generation and dense prediction as separate tasks, and prior unified models either duplicate the entire backbone (JointNet) or fine-tune it directly (Marigold-style), incurring heavy computation or catastrophic forgetting of generative priors. Crucially, these methods overlook the complementary mechanism between perception and generation: perceptual constraints can enforce spatial alignment in generation, while generative priors can enrich perceptual detail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262487/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt plus a spatially varying condition (depth map, surface normal, sketch, human pose, canny edges, or an RGB image for perception mode) and simultaneously outputs an RGB image, a depth map, and a surface normal map. During training, the condition is randomly sampled per sample: generation samples receive a random spatial condition or NONE (for joint generation), while perception samples use the original RGB image as condition. Perception data is resized to 576×768, and generation data requires a minimum edge length of 768 pixels.

## Method

UNIGP introduces a DUGP (Disentangled Unified Generation and Perception) branch that copies only the image branch of MMDiT (not the full backbone) to model non-RGB distributions, following Occam's razor. The DUGP branch contains a zero-initialized Condition Patcher, Stacked Control Layers that inject spatial conditions into the frozen backbone via a zero-initialized linear addition, and Stacked Perception Layers that read backbone features while bypassing the additive path—this gradient isolation ensures perception losses never alter the pre-trained generative weights. Training uses a simple binary loss weighting (λ_g, λ_p ∈ {0,1}) assigned per-sample based on data source: generation samples from MultiGen-20M (1M) activate the generation loss, while perception samples from Hypersim and Virtual KITTI 2 (59K) activate the depth/normal loss, enabling joint optimization on a strictly heterogeneous dataset without task-specific architectures.

## Results

On zero-shot depth estimation, UNIGP (trained on only 59K images) achieves AbsRel of 5.2 on NYUv2, 8.3 on KITTI, 6.0 on ETH3D, and 5.5 on ScanNet, matching or surpassing DepthAnything V2 (62.6M images) on ETH3D and coming within ~1 AbsRel on indoor benchmarks. On surface normal estimation, UNIGP achieves mean angular error of 16.4° (NYUv2), 14.9° (ScanNet), 17.3° (iBims-1), and 35.0° (Sintel), matching the SOTA regression model DSINE. On controllable generation, UNIGP achieves the best FID across all five spatial conditions (17.95 depth, 18.60 normal, 15.91 canny, 17.02 sketch, 15.40 pose) and the lowest condition RMSE (6.89 depth, 7.12 normal), outperforming both single-control and multi-control baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On controllable generation (Table 2), UNIGP outperforms all seven baselines—ControlNet, SD3-CNet, UniControlNet, CNetPlus, JointNet, and UniCon—on FID and CLIP score across all five spatial conditions (depth, normal, canny, sketch, pose), with the largest margins on pose (FID 15.40 vs. 18.95 for CNetPlus) and depth RMSE (6.89 vs. 12.50 for SD3-CNet). On depth estimation (Table 3), UNIGP trained on only 59K images achieves an average rank of 2.38, narrowly behind DepthAnything (2.25, trained on 62.6M images) and ahead of all other generative methods including Marigold (3.56), GeoWizard (4.81), and the unified models JointNet (10.00), OneDiffusion (8.88), and JoDi (9.13); it decisively outperforms regression baselines MiDaS, Omnidata, and DPT. On surface normal estimation (Table 4), UNIGP (avg. rank 1.58) slightly edges out the SOTA regression model DSINE (1.67) and clearly surpasses all generative baselines including StableNormal (3.63), GeoWizard (5.29), and JoDi (4.83). The unified-model baselines JointNet and UniCon are substantially outperformed on both generation and perception, confirming that prior unified approaches fail to capture the complementary benefits between the two task families.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNIGP builds directly on the MMDiT (SD3) architecture, extending it with a DUGP branch that copies only the image branch to model non-RGB distributions, following an Occam's razor principle. It departs from two dominant paradigms: JointNet-style full-backbone duplication (which doubles parameters) and Marigold-style full fine-tuning (which erases generative priors). In the unified-model lineage (JointNet, UniCon, OneDiffusion, JoDi), UNIGP is the first to explicitly quarantine perception gradients from the backbone via a bypassed additive path, and to demonstrate measurable cross-task complementarity rather than treating generation and perception as merely conditional variants of the same task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single 3B-parameter model can rival task-specific experts in both controllable generation and dense prediction while preserving the backbone's generative priors, with perception trained on only 59K samples versus 62.6M for regression baselines. The complementary-benefit finding—that perception training sharpens generation's spatial alignment and generation training enriches perception's high-frequency detail—suggests a general principle for multi-task diffusion training that could extend to other modalities (segmentation, albedo, shading, as hinted in the supplement). Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than two non-RGB modalities without further parameter growth, whether the 2× inference cost (5.07s → 9.54s) is acceptable for real-time applications, and whether the binary loss routing generalizes to more than two task families.
