## Problem

Video-based embodied world models for robotic manipulation suffer from a fundamental entanglement: accurate dynamics modeling requires low-level temporal reasoning at low resolution, while high-fidelity visual synthesis demands expansive generation at high resolution. Existing monolithic video generators must solve both simultaneously, resulting in either slow inference (too costly for iterative planning) or coarse predictions that lose contact-rich details critical for manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261563/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an initial RGB observation (downsampled to 256×384 for the preview stage, full 480×720 for refinement) and a language instruction as conditioning. It produces a 49-frame video at 720p resolution in the 3D VAE latent space, which can be decoded to pixels; optionally, a vision-only Diffusion Policy action expert converts the predicted frames into an executable action sequence for closed-loop manipulation. The preview stage outputs low-resolution latents that can be independently decoded for fast dynamics inspection without full refinement.

## Method

DVG-WM decomposes world modeling into a two-stage pipeline: a preview stage (CogVideoX-5B with LoRA rank-128 at 256×384, 50 denoising steps) learns low-resolution dynamics capturing physical interaction patterns, and a refinement stage (CogVideoX-2B at 480×720, only 4 NFEs) restores high-frequency details. The key architectural innovation is a flow-matching-based cascading mechanism that learns a vector field to directly map the linearly upsampled low-resolution latents to high-resolution latents along a straight-line probability path, avoiding the redundant generation-from-noise of conventional diffusion cascading. A latent degradation mechanism (perturbing the upscaled LR latents rather than pixel-level downsampling) is used during refinement training to break the tight source-target correlation and encourage regeneration of contact-rich structures like gripper-object interactions. The system is trained in two phases: first the world model alone via the flow-matching objective, then jointly with the Diffusion Policy action expert via imitation learning, allowing gradients from the action loss to align visual predictions with downstream planning.

## Results

On LIBERO, DVG-WM achieves the best video quality across most metrics: PSNR 20.019, SSIM 0.783, LPIPS 0.120, FVD 152.36, and object-level accuracy of 89%, outperforming all baselines. Inference time is 88.7 s, yielding up to 3.97× speedup over LVP-14B (354.2 s) and 2.67× over CogVideoX-5B (236.8 s). On real-world tasks across two robot platforms, DVG-WM equipped with a Diffusion Policy action expert achieves a 62.2% average success rate versus 34.0% for CogVideoX, a 28.2-point improvement, with the largest gains on contact-rich tasks like adapter insertion (52.4% vs 18.2%) and make a toast (38.2% vs 14.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVG-WM outperforms CogVideoX-5B on every LIBERO metric (PSNR +0.73, SSIM +0.022, LPIPS −0.018, FVD −18.88, Object +13 pp) and is 2.67× faster at inference. Against Wan2.1-14B, DVG-WM leads on all quality metrics and is 3.52× faster. Compared to LVP-14B, DVG-WM improves PSNR by 0.44, FVD by 35.33, and Object accuracy by 9 pp while running 3.97× faster. The only metric where DVG-WM trails a baseline is SSIM vs. LongScape (0.783 vs. 0.788), a marginal 0.005 gap, while DVG-WM still wins on PSNR, LPIPS, FVD, and object accuracy. On real-world manipulation, DVG-WM's 62.2% average success rate substantially exceeds CogVideoX's 34.0%, with the gap widening on long-horizon, contact-rich tasks (e.g., assemble gears: 51.4% vs. 22.6%), confirming that the disentangled dynamics-preview plus refinement design translates into more reliable action planning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CogVideoX video diffusion backbone and the flow matching framework, repurposing them into a two-stage disentangled pipeline for embodied world modeling. It departs from prior goal-conditioned video world models (KeyWorld, LongScape, GE-Act, Act2Goal, LVP) that treat dynamics learning and high-fidelity visual synthesis as a single monolithic generation task, instead explicitly separating them into a low-resolution dynamics preview and a high-resolution refinement stage connected by flow matching. The latent degradation mechanism also distinguishes it from standard video super-resolution approaches that rely on pixel-level degradation, and the Diffusion Policy action expert follows the established vision-only policy learning paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.97× inference speedup with maintained or improved video quality addresses a practical bottleneck for real-world iterative planning, where world models must be queried repeatedly. The 28.2% average improvement in real-world manipulation success rates across two robot platforms and nine tasks provides strong evidence that disentangled dynamics learning translates to better action planning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer horizons beyond 49 frames, more complex multi-object scenes, or settings where robot state is available, and whether the specific resolution split (256×384 preview, 480×720 refinement) is optimal or task-dependent.
