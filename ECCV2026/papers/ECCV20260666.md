## Problem

VLM-based robotic planners treat each decision step as an isolated observation-to-action mapping, reinferring scene geometry from raw pixels while remaining unaware of how prior actions have reshaped the environment. This exposes two coupled deficits—ungrounded spatial perception (no persistent 3D anchoring across steps) and untracked causal history (no record linking actions to induced state transitions)—causing perceptual errors to compound and occluded objects to be catastrophically forgotten in long-horizon manipulation.

## Input / Output

At each step the system consumes an RGB-D frame, a goal specification (either a reference image of the target configuration or a natural-language instruction), and the accumulated memory from all prior steps. The VLM first identifies task-relevant objects, SAM3 produces pixel-accurate masks, and depth maps yield per-object point clouds; these are distilled into compact Spatio-Temporal Fusion Tokens (visual patch tokens + 3D centroid + per-axis Gaussian shape parameters + timestamp). The output is a two-stage action: the VLM emits a semantic action directive conditioned on the causal graph, which is then deterministically resolved against stored STF-Token geometry to produce a concrete 6-DoF end-effector pose.

## Method

RoboStream is a training-free framework built on two synergistic mechanisms. Spatio-Temporal Fusion Tokens (STF-Tokens) compress each object's filtered VLM visual encoder patches (selected by mask IoU threshold) together with its point-cloud centroid and a Gaussian shape vector (mean, std, min, max per axis) into a single identity-persistent primitive, shifting reasoning from pixel-level reconstruction to structured object-instance references. A Causal Spatio-Temporal Graph (CSTG) organizes these tokens into a spatial scene graph—nodes carry sliding-window buffers of length K, edges encode pairwise Euclidean distance and directional offset—augmented with a causal memory log that timestamps every action-triggered state transition (displacement, occlusion, collision, subtask completion). At planning time, the VLM receives a context-augmented prompt assembled from the CSTG, an identity-annotated observation, and the goal; it performs chain-of-thought precondition verification before emitting a semantic action, which is then instantiated into a 6-DoF pose by deterministic geometry resolution against the graph. The entire pipeline requires no fine-tuning or environment-specific adaptation and is instantiated with Qwen3-VL backbones at 8B, 32B, and 235B scales.

## Results

On the 8 long-horizon RLBench tasks, RoboStream-235B achieves a 90.5% average success rate, substantially outperforming SoFar (28.0%) and VoxPoser (26.5%) without any fine-tuning. On real-world Franka block-building (Hard), RoboStream-235B reaches 44.4% success versus 11.1% for both SoFar and VoxPoser, and on the occlusion-based hide-and-restore task it achieves 88.9% where both baselines score 0%. On zero-shot SIMPLER (WidowX+Bridge), RoboStream-8B attains 74.8% average success, exceeding zero-shot SoFar (58.3%) and Bridge-finetuned SpatialVLA (42.7%). On 6-DoF SpatialBench, RoboStream-32B scores 48.9% overall, surpassing SoFar (43.9%) and GPT-4o (36.2%), and on Open6DOR V2 it reaches 52.2% integrated 6-DoF success versus SoFar's 48.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboStream consistently outperforms SoFar across all five benchmarks: on RLBench long-horizon tasks (90.5% vs 28.0% for RoboStream-235B), real-world block building (44.4% vs 11.1%), SIMPLER WidowX (74.8% vs 58.3% for the 8B variant), 6-DoF SpatialBench (48.9% vs 43.9%), and Open6DOR V2 (52.2% vs 48.7%). Against VoxPoser, the gap is even wider on long-horizon tasks (90.5% vs 26.5%) and real-world manipulation (44.4% vs 11.1%), where VoxPoser's per-step 3D value-map reconstruction fails to track cumulative state changes. On SIMPLER, RoboStream-8B (zero-shot) surpasses OXE-finetuned RT-2-X by a substantial margin (95.7% vs 78.7% on Pick Coke Can under VM), demonstrating that geometric grounding transfers without domain-specific training. On SpatialBench, RoboStream-32B exceeds GPT-4o (48.9% vs 36.2%) and SoFar-32B (48.9% vs 45.3%), with the largest gains on absolute position tasks where pixel-level inference is most error-prone. The paper also reports that RoboStream outperforms memory-augmented approaches SAM2Act and MemoryVLA on four RLBench long-horizon cases across all model scales, though specific numbers for these comparisons are not tabulated.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboStream carves new ground by identifying two coupled representation deficits—ungrounded spatial perception and untracked causal history—that are shared across the three dominant lines of VLM-based manipulation (geometry-aware constraint generation like SoFar/VoxPoser, closed-loop reasoning like Inner Monologue, and hierarchical planning like HiRobot). Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, it repurposes scene-graph structures from 3D scene understanding and VLM visual token mechanisms into a unified, training-free pipeline: STF-Tokens convert per-object appearance and 3D geometry into identity-persistent primitives, while a Causal Spatio-Temporal Graph (CSTG) maintains a sliding-window log of action-triggered state transitions. The framework explicitly departs from fine-tuning-based spatial VLMs (SpatialVLM, RoboRefer) and memory-augmented VLA approaches (SAM2Act, MemoryVLA) by requiring no retraining and by structuring memory as a causal graph rather than a flat retrieval buffer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ablation reveals that causal memory tracking (CSTG) is the dominant factor for long-horizon success (90.5% → 14.5% when removed), while geometric grounding (STF-Tokens) contributes a more modest 11-point marginal gain—challenging the field's implicit assumption that spatial precision is the primary bottleneck. The training-free design and large margins over SoFar/VoxPoser (90.5% vs 28% on RLBench; 44.4% vs 11.1% on real-world block building) suggest that structured causal state tracking is a broadly applicable missing component for any VLM planner operating over multi-step horizons. Open questions include whether the decoupled planning-execution architecture can be replaced by an end-to-end VLA that internalizes CSTG reasoning, whether the framework extends to contact-rich and dexterous manipulation, and whether the sliding-window memory suffices for truly open-ended horizons beyond the tested 8-step tasks.
