## Problem

Medical VLMs suffer from a fundamental cognitive misalignment: their reliance on discrete tokenization causes quantization loss for continuous pathological features, progressive attenuation of visual evidence over long reasoning chains, and a tendency to encode generic linguistic priors rather than case-adaptive anatomical context. Existing remedies—retrieval-augmented generation and soft-prompt injection—provide static, causally unverified knowledge that remains a brittle external dependency, while general latent-computation methods lack the structured anatomical priors and causal calibration that medical diagnosis demands.

## Input / Output

The method takes a medical image X ∈ R^{H×W×3} (processed at native dynamic resolution per Qwen3-VL's default) and a clinical query q, and produces a diagnostic analysis and conclusion sequence y. Internally, a set of N=16 compact diagnostic implicit memory vectors M ∈ R^{16×4096} is synthesized and injected into the VLM hidden stream between the question encoding and answer generation, so the model attends to latent priors via standard self-attention without architectural modifications. In the final IMT variant, the external anatomical encoder is entirely removed at inference and memory is generated autonomously by a lightweight 2-layer MLP module, yielding inference latency and memory footprint nearly identical to the base VLM.

## Method

MedSynapse-V evolves diagnostic implicit memory through a three-stage progressive training paradigm. Stage I (Meta Query for Prior Memorization) uses N learnable meta-query probes in a 2-layer cross-attention sampler to condense spatial features from a frozen MedSAM3 anatomical encoder into compact memory vectors, followed by a next-token-prediction warmup to align the encoder's feature space with the VLM's hidden space. Stage II (Causal Counterfactual Refinement) freezes the sampler and performs GRPO-based policy optimization with a composite reward combining diagnostic accuracy and a novel causal counterfactual term: region-level feature masks from MedSAM3 zero out diagnostically relevant areas to produce an interventional memory M′, and the log-likelihood ratio between the original and interventional conditions quantifies each memory element's causal contribution, pruning spurious components. Stage III (Intrinsic Memory Transition) distills the refined memory into an autonomous pathway via a privileged-autonomous dual-branch scheme: a teacher branch conditioned on encoder-derived memory and a student branch using an MLP-generated autonomous memory are aligned through full-vocabulary generalized Jensen-Shannon divergence (JSD_β) at every generation position, with gradients flowing only to the student module so that the anatomical encoder can be completely discarded at inference.

## Results

MedSynapse-V achieves the highest average score of 61.4% across seven medical multimodal benchmarks (VQA-RAD, SLAKE, PathVQA, PMC-VQA, MMMU Health & Medicine, MedXpertQA-MM, GMAI-MMBench) with the anatomical encoder, and retains 59.6% in the encoder-free IMT variant. The IMT variant surpasses the strongest RL-enhanced baseline MMedExpert-R1 (55.7%) by +3.9 pp without any auxiliary module at inference, with the largest margins on visual-grounding benchmarks (VQA-RAD +9.0, SLAKE +7.0, PathVQA +6.7). The 4B variant (54.7% with encoder) still outperforms the 8B MediX-R1 (49.9%), and inference latency for the IMT variant (2.6 s/sample) is comparable to zero-shot Qwen3-VL-8B (2.8 s) versus 5.8 s for full-scale CoT methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedSynapse-V (IMT, encoder-free) outperforms all 15 baselines on the average score, leading the strongest RL baseline MMedExpert-R1 (55.7%) by +3.9 pp and the best medical VLM HuatuoGPT-V (53.5%) by +6.1 pp. The margins are largest on visual-grounding benchmarks where discrete CoT tokens attenuate early visual evidence: VQA-RAD +9.0, SLAKE +7.0, and PathVQA +6.7 over MMedExpert-R1. Among latent-space methods, even the most sophisticated adapted baseline IVT-LR (50.5%) trails MedSynapse-V (IMT) by 9.1 pp, confirming that latent compression without structured anatomical priors and causal calibration encodes statistical shortcuts rather than diagnostic logic. The 4B variant (54.7% with encoder) surpasses the 8B MediX-R1 (49.9%) by 4.8 pp, demonstrating parameter efficiency. On the hardest expert-level benchmark MedXpertQA-MM, the gap narrows (26.8 vs. 27.5 for MMedExpert-R1 in IMT mode), suggesting complex multi-step reasoning still benefits more from encoder-derived priors than closed-ended recognition. MediX-R1 leads on PMC-VQA (56.2 vs. 58.5 for MedSynapse-V IMT) due to multilingual pretraining breadth, but this advantage does not extend to radiology-specific tasks where MedSynapse-V dominates.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedSynapse-V sits at the intersection of latent-space reasoning (Coconut, CODI, SoftCoT) and RL-enhanced medical VLMs (Med-R1, MMedExpert-R1 via GRPO), but departs from both in critical ways. Unlike general latent methods that compress reasoning into continuous vectors without domain structure, it injects structured anatomical priors from a frozen segmentation encoder (MedSAM3) and calibrates them via a novel causal counterfactual reward that quantifies each memory element's diagnostic contribution through region-level feature masking. Unlike existing medical RL approaches that align the output token distribution, it operates alignment signals directly on latent memory representations and then distills them into autonomous internal generation via a privileged-autonomous dual-branch JSD objective, eliminating all auxiliary modules at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: 16 compact latent vectors replace 150–400 verbose CoT tokens while matching or exceeding their accuracy, cutting inference latency by roughly 55% relative to full CoT baselines. The finding that latent compression without clinical grounding (Coconut, MCOUT, IVT-LR) barely exceeds zero-shot performance underscores that domain priors and causal calibration are prerequisites, not optional add-ons, for latent reasoning in medicine. Open questions include whether the framework scales to differential diagnosis spaces with hundreds of competing hypotheses, whether the 1.4 pp gap between encoder-retained and encoder-free inference on complex reasoning benchmarks (MedXpert, GMAI) can be closed, and whether the three-stage training pipeline generalizes beyond the Qwen3-VL backbone family.
