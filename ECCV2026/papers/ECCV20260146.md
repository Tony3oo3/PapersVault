## Problem

Video world models generate photorealistic futures but fail to preserve inter-frame correspondences—pixels depicting the same 3D surface point do not follow physically consistent trajectories—making them unreliable for extracting executable robot actions. Standard pixel- or latent-space reconstruction losses cannot enforce this consistency because the mapping from scene geometry to pixels is many-to-one, and existing remedies that add explicit geometric outputs (depth, normals) require large-scale annotations and modify the generative model's output space.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single initial RGB frame and a natural-language instruction as input and produces a rollout of N future video frames. At inference, only the video DiT is used (the geometry branch is discarded), so generation incurs zero additional cost over a standard video diffusion model. A separate Adaptive Inverse Dynamic System (AIDS) then converts the generated rollout into an executable 6-DoF end-effector trajectory by chaining off-the-shelf vision models (SAM 2, CoTracker3, FoundationPose, GraspGen, Qwen3.5-VL) with a confidence-gated tracker and a geometry-kinematics pose fallback, requiring no task-specific training.

## Method

GEM-4D introduces a dual flow-matching training framework in which a Video DiT learns standard latent video generation while a parallel Geometry DiT predicts the velocity of a frozen geometry foundation model's (PAGE-4D) dense representation, conditioned solely on the video backbone's intermediate features m_t. Because the Geometry DiT has no direct access to pixels, camera parameters, or depth maps, minimizing its flow-matching loss forces m_t to encode the depth, relative camera pose, and scene flow that jointly determine inter-frame correspondences. The coupling is asymmetric—the geometry branch reads from video features but never writes back—and is discarded entirely at inference, yielding a single-stream generator. The joint objective L = L_FM^vid + α·L_FM^geo decomposes gradients into an appearance-transport term and a geometry-induced term that propagates correspondence structure into the backbone. An ablation confirms that distilling full 4D geometry features outperforms both explicit depth supervision and using VGGT (a static-scene model) as the geometry teacher.

## Results

On the real-world Droid dataset, GEM-4D achieves the best FVD (31.82 vs. 33.17–35.56 for baselines), highest SSIM (82.05), and lowest depth AbsRel (20.13), with a 30% reduction in Chamfer distance (0.2001 vs. 0.2670 for CogVideoX). On the synthetic RLBench dataset, GEM-4D further dominates with FVD 27.94 (vs. 34.06–49.20), PSNR 23.36, and Chamfer 0.0702 (a 4.1× improvement over CogVideoX's 0.2913). For downstream manipulation, GEM-4D improves real-world task success from 58–65% (TesserAct) to 75–87% on Droid, and achieves 63–82% success on RLBench simulation tasks versus 0–49% for TesserAct.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEM-4D outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric in both real and synthetic domains. Against CogVideoX (the strongest general video generator), GEM-4D reduces FVD by 10.4% (real) and 30.5% (synthetic) and cuts Chamfer distance by 25% (real) and 76% (synthetic), indicating substantially better geometric consistency. Against TesserAct (the closest 4D world-model baseline), GEM-4D improves depth δ₁ by 11.4 points on real data (78.19 vs. 66.80) and by 4.9 points on synthetic data (74.13 vs. 69.26), while also achieving lower FVD and higher PSNR. Against Geometry-Forcing (a feature-alignment method), GEM-4D gains 4.4 points on SSIM and 8.5 points on δ₁ (real), confirming that distilling full 4D geometry features is more effective than aligning to a single geometric representation. On downstream manipulation, GEM-4D's advantage is most dramatic: it reaches 75–87% success on real Droid tasks (vs. 58–65% for TesserAct) and 63–82% on RLBench (vs. 0–49% for TesserAct), with CogVideoX's generated videos being too geometrically inconsistent to produce reliable trajectories at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEM-4D sits at the intersection of video world models for embodied control (UniPi, TesserAct) and feed-forward 4D geometry estimation (PAGE-4D, VGGT, DUSt3R). It departs from prior geometry-aware video models (TesserAct, 3DFlowAction, RoboTransfer) that modify the output space to explicitly predict depth, normals, or flow fields; instead, GEM-4D keeps a single-stream RGB output and injects geometric structure purely through feature-level distillation during training, sharing REPA's philosophy of foundation-model supervision but applying it to a frozen 4D geometry teacher rather than a 2D vision encoder. The dual flow-matching formulation (video velocity + geometry velocity conditioned on shared intermediate features) is the paper's core architectural contribution, building directly on the flow-matching generative framework and the Video DiT backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-additional-inference-cost design (geometry branch discarded at test time) makes geometry-consistent video generation practically deployable for robot planning, and the 20-point real-world manipulation gain (61%→81%) demonstrates that correspondence consistency is a meaningful bottleneck for action extraction. However, the inverse dynamics pipeline relies on a chain of off-the-shelf tools (CoTracker3, FoundationPose, GraspGen, Qwen3.5-VL) whose individual failure modes compound, and the manipulation baselines are limited to CogVideoX and TesserAct. Open questions include whether the distillation signal transfers to longer-horizon or multi-object scenes, whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific PAGE-4D teacher (the VGGT ablation hints at sensitivity to the geometry prior), and whether the human-study evaluation protocol on Droid scales to broader deployment.
