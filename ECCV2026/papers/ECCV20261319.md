## Problem

Existing 3D body fitting methods to clothed humans face a fundamental trade-off: dense correspondence approaches (e.g., NICP) capture fine details but produce overweight, biomechanically implausible bodies from loose garments, while sparse-marker methods (e.g., ETCH) handle clothing well but lack expressiveness for hands and face and fail entirely on partial inputs where markers are missing. No prior method simultaneously achieves local expressiveness for fine-grained parts and global robustness to diverse clothing, pose variation, and incomplete scans.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw 3D point cloud of a clothed human (potentially noisy, partial, or single-view) and produces SMPL-X parameters (shape β∈ℝ¹⁰, pose θ∈ℝ⁵⁵ˣ³, expression ψ∈ℝ¹⁰, translation t∈ℝ³) yielding a topologically consistent 10,475-vertex mesh, along with separately fitted MANO hand parameters. The pipeline is designed to handle partial inputs where up to 50% of the surface may be occluded, and the implicit dense correspondence representation allows querying anchor points at any location in the feature space even when corresponding input points are absent.

## Method

ETCH-X follows a two-stage 'undress first, then dense fit' paradigm. Stage 1 (Masked Undress) uses an Equivariant Point Network to produce SO(3)-equivariant features, then a self-attention direction head and a Point Transformer magnitude head regress SE(3)-equivariant tightness vectors that displace clothed surface points to the underlying body; a binary tightness mask (also via Point Transformer) enforces zero displacement on exposed skin regions. Stage 2 (Dense Fit) encodes the undressed inner points into an implicit feature volume via IF-Nets, then a LoVD neural field with 16 region-specific MLP heads predicts ordered offsets to downsampled SMPL-X template vertices, refined by test-time iterative optimization. A hand refinement step re-samples densely around the initial hand position, applies a trained classifier to remove non-hand points (addressing self-contact), and fits MANO via a dedicated hand LVD network. The critical design choice is full decoupling of the two stages, enabling independent supervised training on composable data sources: CLOTH3D for garment diversity in undressing, AMASS for body pose diversity, and InterHand2.6M for hand gesture diversity in dense fitting.

## Results

On CAPE, ETCH-X achieves V2V-All of 1.484 cm and MPJPE-All of 1.764 cm, outperforming all baselines by 5.3%–73.2% (V2V) and 11.9%–68.6% (MPJPE). On 4D-Dress, the gains are larger: V2V-All of 1.897 cm and MPJPE-All of 2.317 cm, a 21.2%–74.7% V2V improvement over competitors. On the out-of-distribution BEDLAM2.0 test set (all methods trained on 4D-Dress), ETCH-X achieves MPJPE-All of 4.033 cm vs. ETCH's 15.031 cm (80.8% reduction) and V2V-All of 3.429 cm vs. 12.209 cm (71.9% reduction), demonstrating strong generalization to unseen clothing and body shapes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETCH-X outperforms all five baselines across every metric on both CAPE and 4D-Dress. Against the tightness-agnostic methods NICP and ArtEq, ETCH-X reduces V2V-All by 14.3% and 32.5% on CAPE, and by 53.5% and 38.4% on 4D-Dress, confirming that explicit tightness modeling is essential for loose garments. Among tightness-aware competitors, IPNet is the weakest (V2V-All 5.529 on CAPE, 7.495 on 4D-Dress), while PTF is closer but still trails ETCH-X by 36.5% (CAPE V2V) and 42.4% (4D-Dress V2V). The most direct comparison is with ETCH, the predecessor: ETCH-X improves V2V-Hands by 35.8% on CAPE (2.215 vs. 3.449) and 39.3% on 4D-Dress (3.101 vs. 5.108), and MPJPE-All by 11.9% on CAPE and 33.0% on 4D-Dress. On the OOD BEDLAM2.0 benchmark, ETCH's entangled architecture fails catastrophically (V2V 12.209, MPJPE 15.031), while ETCH-X's disentangled design generalizes far better (V2V 3.429, MPJPE 4.033), a gap the authors attribute to ETCH's inability to decouple garment diversity from pose diversity during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

ETCH-X directly extends ETCH (Li et al., ICCV 2025) by retaining its SE(3)-equivariant tightness vector regressor for clothing-aware undressing, while replacing ETCH's explicit sparse surface markers with the implicit dense correspondence mechanism from NICP (Marin et al., ECCV 2024). The key architectural departure is the decoupling of 'undress' and 'dense fit' into independently trainable modules, which neither ETCH (entangled sparse fitting) nor NICP (no undressing) achieves. The model is also upgraded from SMPL to SMPL-X, adding articulated hands and facial expressions, and a re-sampling-based hand refinement step is introduced to address the sparsity of hand points in full-body fitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled 'undress first, then dense fit' paradigm enables scalable training on composable data sources—simulated garments (CLOTH3D) for tightness regression and large-scale pose libraries (AMASS, InterHand2.6M) for dense fitting—without requiring scarce paired clothed-scan data. This addresses a practical bottleneck in the field: collecting ground-truth SMPL-X annotations for clothed humans is expensive, while simulated garments and mocap poses are abundant. Limitations include ~10-second inference time (not real-time), reliance on simulated garments whose diversity may not cover all real-world clothing, and the single-view partial scan assumption. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-person scenes, hybrid LiDAR captures, and whether further garment simulation diversity can close the remaining gap on loose clothing.
