## Problem

In exemplar-free class-incremental learning, existing methods transport old-class Gaussian statistics into the evolving feature space via a decoupled post-hoc adapter trained after backbone optimization. This separation allows unconstrained backbone updates to degrade the legacy manifold topology, and Mahalanobis evaluation amplifies transport errors along low-variance directions, eroding decision margins between old and new classes. The paper asks whether feature transport can be enforced as a geometry-aware constraint during primary training rather than as a separate retroactive calibration step.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-100 and CUB-200) and outputs a class label via a Gaussian Bayes/Mahalanobis rule over all previously seen classes. Internally, a ResNet-18 backbone produces 512-D features that are projected into a 64-D prototype space where class-conditional Gaussians are maintained. After each task, old-class statistics are pushed forward through the learned adapter in a training-free sampling stage, and the final classifier is a Mahalanobis distance over the assembled task-t statistics.

## Method

The Geometry-Anchored Transport Framework couples feature transport with backbone optimization through two mechanisms. First, an Analytic Geometric Anchor (AGA) computes a closed-form affine prior via ridge-regularized Generalized Least Squares aligned with the Mahalanobis metric; the solution takes a Sylvester-equation form whose spectral denominator (γ_j + ρλ_i) provides anisotropic shrinkage that dampens transport error along low-variance, decision-critical directions. Second, a Topology-Aware Evolution loss integrates a forward residual MLP (calibrating localized non-linear corrections against the AGA prior) and a backward distiller (anchoring the legacy manifold by requiring the new representation to reconstruct old features) directly into the backbone's training objective. The AGA prior is refreshed periodically via EMA smoothing to avoid high-variance fluctuations, and the final adapter A_t(z) = P̄_t z + b̄_t + g(z; θ_t) is used for a training-free pushforward of old-class Gaussians. A margin-stability theorem (Theorem 2) formally bounds how transported moment perturbations affect Mahalanobis decision boundaries, connecting the analytic anchor's tail-error suppression to classification correctness.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (T=10), the proposed framework achieves 52.0% A_last and 64.4% A_inc, outperforming the strongest baseline DPCR (50.2%/62.8%) by +1.8/+1.6 pp and AdaGauss (46.8%/60.9%) by +5.2/+3.5 pp. On TinyImageNet (T=10), it reaches 37.2% A_last and 50.6% A_inc, exceeding DPCR by +2.9/+3.7 pp. On ImageNet-100 (T=10), it attains the highest A_last of 53.2% (vs. AdaGauss 51.1%), while on CUB-200 (T=10) it is competitive with AdaGauss (52.4% vs. 52.9% A_last) but trails EFC at T=20 (43.7% vs. 46.1% A_last).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The framework achieves the best A_last and A_inc across all four CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet settings, beating the closest competitor DPCR by +1.8 to +3.2 pp in A_last and +1.6 to +3.7 pp in A_inc. On ImageNet-100 (T=10), it leads in A_last (53.2% vs. AdaGauss 51.1%, DPCR 49.9%) but LDC reports a higher A_inc (69.4† vs. 67.4), though LDC's T=10 numbers are taken from the original paper. On CUB-200 with a pretrained backbone, the method is slightly behind AdaGauss at T=10 (52.4% vs. 52.9% A_last) and clearly behind EFC at T=20 (43.7% vs. 46.1% A_last), where milder representation drift limits the benefit of geometry-anchored transport. DPCR could not be reliably reproduced on CUB-200 under the pretrained protocol. Overall, the gains are most pronounced on from-scratch benchmarks with longer task sequences (T=20), where accumulated representation drift is the dominant failure mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on AdaGauss (Rypeść et al., NeurIPS 2024), preserving its class-conditional Gaussian bookkeeping and anti-collapse loss while replacing the decoupled post-hoc adapter with a training-time geometric constraint. It departs from the broader EFCIL transport lineage (SDC, ADC, LDC, EFC, DPCR) by reframing feature transport from a separate post-task calibration step to an endogenous optimization constraint, combining a closed-form Mahalanobis-aligned analytic prior (solved via a Sylvester equation) with a topology-aware backward distiller that restricts backbone evolution. The closest competitor, DPCR (ICML 2025), also estimates semantic shift but does so after backbone training; this paper's central distinction is the timing of the geometric constraint—enforced during primary optimization rather than applied retroactively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual shift from post-hoc transport to training-time geometry anchoring offers a principled alternative to the dominant 'train-then-adapt' paradigm in EFCIL, and Theorem 2 provides a concrete margin-stability bound linking transport perturbation magnitudes to classification correctness. The ablation finding that optional post-hoc refinement is generally unnecessary (and occasionally counterproductive) challenges the assumption that additional adapter fine-tuning always helps, suggesting that well-coupled co-evolution reaches a stable configuration on its own. Limitations include reliance on a single class-conditional Gaussian per class and an affine global prior that may struggle under severe nonlinear or non-stationary representation shifts; the CUB-200 results (where EFC outperforms at T=20) indicate the gains are most pronounced when representation drift is substantial. Open questions include generalization to mixture-of-Gaussians or implicit density surrogates and whether alternative anchor formulations (low-rank, kernelized, orthogonality-constrained) can capture more complex global deformations.
