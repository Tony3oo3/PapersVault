## Problem

Existing underwater object tracking methods fall into two categories with distinct failures: frame-level trackers produce inconsistent feature styles and weak temporal correlations by processing each frame independently, while video-level trackers still suffer from persistent feature degradation and accumulated tracking drift under severe underwater color distortion and low visibility. The paper asks whether feature-level enhancement—applied after the visual encoder—can simultaneously correct underwater degradations and enforce temporal consistency for robust tracking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260802/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an underwater RGB video sequence where the first frame is annotated with a bounding box [x_c, y_c, w, h]; template frames are resized to 128×128 and search regions to 256×256. At each time step t≥2, the input additionally includes three reference frames sampled from a dynamic memory pool of historical search results. The output is a predicted bounding box (coordinates via regression) and a confidence score (via classification), produced by a lightweight dual-branch head operating on ViT token features of shape D×N where N=HW/P².

## Method

FeatTracker extends ODTrack's autoregressive ViT-Base framework with two feature-level consistency modules. The Short-Range Temporal Feature Consistency (SRTFC) module applies a diffusion-like enhancement—using a 4.8M-parameter U-Net to predict condition-dependent noise residuals that progressively refine features—then fuses the enhanced feature with a FIFO memory pool of K historical features weighted by cosine-similarity softmax, followed by style-adaptive normalization and 1D temporal smoothing. The Long-Range Temporal Feature Consistency (LRTFC) module applies a 2D Haar wavelet decomposition to each autoregressively propagated temporal token, separating it into low-frequency structural and high-frequency detail components, then uses learned sigmoid gating to selectively recombine these components with the current token before projecting back to the original feature dimension. Training uses a hybrid strategy over LaSOT, WebUOT-1M, HUT290, and UVOT400 with focal + L1 + GIoU loss; at inference the multi-step diffusion is collapsed to a single forward pass, yielding 32.35 FPS on a single GPU.

## Results

FeatTracker achieves state-of-the-art AUC on all four underwater object tracking benchmarks: 67.62% on WebUOT-1M, 61.50% on UW-COT220, 61.25% on VMAT, and 81.40% on UTB180, while also leading in Precision on the latter three (58.35%, 78.07%, and 86.52% respectively). The model runs at 32.35 FPS with 98.7M parameters, maintaining real-time capability. On the VMAT benchmark, the full framework yields a +6.59 AUC gain over the ODTrack baseline (61.25% vs. 54.66%), with the largest single-module contribution coming from the combination of DFE and SRTFC (+1.61 AUC over DFE alone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FeatTracker outperforms all 14 baselines on AUC across all four benchmarks. The closest competitor is UOSTrack* (hybrid-trained) on WebUOT-1M, where FeatTracker leads by only 0.12 AUC (67.62 vs. 67.50), and DAM4SAM on UTB180, where the margin is 0.19 AUC (81.40 vs. 81.21). Against the direct ODTrack baseline, FeatTracker gains +3.80 AUC on WebUOT-1M, +1.12 on UW-COT220, +6.59 on VMAT, and +11.44 on UTB180, with the largest relative improvement on VMAT where ODTrack's autoregressive token propagation degrades most under underwater conditions. Among underwater-specific trackers, FeatTracker surpasses PUTrack* by +2.98 AUC on WebUOT-1M and +10.08 on VMAT, and OKTrack* by +15.59 on WebUOT-1M. On Precision, FeatTracker leads all methods on UW-COT220 (58.35%), VMAT (78.07%), and UTB180 (86.52%), with the most substantial margin over MCITrack on VMAT (+3.15 P). The only metric where FeatTracker is not the fastest is FPS (32.35), trailing HIPTrack (92.14) and UOSTrack* (70.08), though it remains competitive with ODTrack (34.42) and ARTrack (37.31).

## Relation to Prior Work

FeatTracker builds directly on ODTrack's online dense temporal token learning framework, extending it from a video-level to a feature-level tracking paradigm. It departs from frame-level trackers (which apply enhancement or domain adaptation per-frame, causing inconsistent feature styles) and video-level trackers (which model inter-frame dependencies via autoregressive mechanisms but still suffer from accumulated feature drift) by intervening at the feature space after the ViT encoder. The SRTFC module borrows the iterative denoising concept from diffusion models but repurposes it for feature-space perturbation rather than pixel-space generation, while the LRTFC module draws on multiresolution wavelet analysis (Haar DWT) to decompose temporal tokens into structural and detail components during autoregressive propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The feature-level tracking paradigm offers a principled middle ground between input-level enhancement and sequence-level modeling, and the consistent SOTA across four underwater benchmarks (WebUOT-1M, UW-COT220, VMAT, UTB180) validates that post-encoder feature refinement can address degradations that neither preprocessing nor autoregressive frame matching fully resolves. However, the 32.35 FPS throughput is notably lower than high-speed baselines (e.g., HIPTrack at 92 FPS), and the 700-step diffusion schedule, while optimal in ablation, introduces a hyperparameter sensitivity that may not transfer across domains. Open questions include whether the feature-level consistency framework generalizes to other degraded sensing modalities (e.g., sonar, thermal) and whether the specific diffusion-plus-wavelet combination is essential or whether simpler temporal smoothing could capture most of the gain.
