## Problem

Existing VLLM-based AIGI detectors primarily fine-tune the language side while underemphasizing low-level visual forensic cues, and they depend on manually crafted prompts or human-annotated rationales, limiting scalability and robustness to prompt variation. No prior framework reuses the same binary authenticity label as a shared supervision signal across visual adaptation, instruction selection, and language-side generation in a unified manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262050/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and an automatically optimized instruction prompt as input. During visual adaptation, the image is stochastically cropped to a local region (1/8 to 1/4 of original dimensions) and its patches are randomly permuted before encoding. The output is a binary authenticity label (Authentic or Generated) accompanied by a structured four-step textual explanation; when the initial prediction is incorrect, the text is regenerated under an explicit label directive so that the language module is always trained on label-consistent outputs.

## Method

UC-VLM builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and applies three coordinated stages under a single binary supervision signal. First, visual adaptation uses stochastic local cropping plus random patch shuffling to suppress global semantics and force the vision encoder (LoRA rank 16) to attend to local forensic artifacts, trained with binary cross-entropy. Second, instruction optimization uses GPT-4o to generate and iteratively refine a pool of N=5 candidate prompts over M=10 rounds with top-k=2 selection, automatically discovering the most effective instruction formulation without manual prompt design. Third, label-conditioned regeneration collects a training set where misclassified samples are re-prompted with an explicit label directive, then fine-tunes the language module (LoRA rank 8) on this set while freezing the vision encoder. The key design choice is that the same binary authenticity label drives all three stages—visual discrimination, prompt selection, and text generation—rather than requiring separate rationale annotations or handcrafted prompts.

## Results

On GenImage, UC-VLM achieves 96.1% average accuracy across eight generator subsets, exceeding the strongest prior method DEUA (91.5%) by 4.6 percentage points. On the high-resolution Chameleon benchmark, UC-VLM reaches 69.6% accuracy under ProGAN training and 77.9% under SDV1.4 training, surpassing the best baseline AIDE by 11.2% and 15.3% respectively. UC-VLM also delivers the best or near-best per-generator accuracy on GenImage, with particularly strong results on BigGAN (98.8%), VQDM (98.1%), and SDV1.4 (99.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UC-VLM outperforms all 15 GenImage baselines and all 10 Chameleon baselines on average accuracy. On GenImage, the closest competitor is DEUA (91.5%), followed by DRCT (89.5%) and VIB-Net (88.9%); UC-VLM's 96.1% represents a 4.6-point margin over DEUA, with particularly large gaps on BigGAN (+14.6 pts over DEUA) and VQDM (+5.0 pts). On Chameleon, the gap is even more pronounced: UC-VLM's 77.9% (SDV1.4) exceeds AIDE (62.6%) by 15.3 points, and its 69.6% (ProGAN) exceeds AIDE (58.4%) by 11.2 points, while all other baselines cluster between 53.8% and 62.6%. Conventional CNN/Transformer baselines (ResNet-50, DeiT-S, Swin-T, CNNSpot, Spec, F3Net, GramNet) remain far behind, with average GenImage accuracies between 64.2% and 74.8%, confirming that purely visual architectures struggle with cross-generator generalization. Among recent VLM-based and frequency-aware detectors (FatFormer, NPR, AIDE, FIND), UC-VLM consistently leads, suggesting that the unified binary-supervised multi-stage training (visual adaptation + instruction optimization + label-conditioned regeneration) provides a meaningful advantage over single-objective fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

UC-VLM builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B as its vision-language backbone and positions itself against two prior lines: (1) purely visual AIGI detectors (CNNSpot, DIRE, NPR, DEUA) that treat binary labels solely as classification targets and offer no textual output, and (2) recent VLLM-based detectors (FakeVLM, FakeReasoning, AIGI-Holmes) that produce explanations but depend on human-annotated rationales and handcrafted prompts. The key conceptual departure is repurposing the binary authenticity label as a shared supervision signal across three coordinated stages—visual adaptation via patch shuffling, LLM-driven instruction optimization, and label-conditioned language regeneration—rather than introducing additional annotation sources. The visual adaptation mechanism (stochastic cropping + patch permutation) draws on forensic-cue literature (upsampling artifacts, frequency irregularities) but embeds it within a VLM's patch-based encoding rather than a dedicated CNN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine scalability bottleneck: prior VLLM-based AIGI detectors require human-written rationales that do not transfer across new generators or datasets, while UC-VLM's label-conditioned regeneration produces training text from binary labels alone. The 11–15% accuracy gains on the high-resolution Chameleon benchmark (test-only, commercial generators) over specialized detectors like AIDE suggest that VLLM-based approaches can now compete with or exceed purpose-built forensic classifiers on the hardest settings. However, the authors explicitly disclaim faithfulness of generated explanations (hallucination risk), the method is validated on a single backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and two benchmarks, and failure cases reveal persistent confusion with artistic/stylized content. Open questions include whether the instruction-optimization loop generalizes to video or multi-modal AIGI detection, whether the sequential (rather than joint) training schedule is essential or an artifact of the specific backbone, and whether the binary-supervised text outputs can ever be trusted as verified forensic evidence rather than auxiliary signals.
