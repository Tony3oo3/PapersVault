## Problem

Existing token reduction methods for diffusion models inherit input-token-similarity matching from ViT paradigms, but diffusion models require a recovery stage that makes the true objective minimizing recovery error between restored and dense outputs. This creates a fundamental misalignment: input similarity does not reflect output similarity, so matched tokens' outputs poorly represent the reduced tokens' original outputs, leading to unnecessary quality degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262543/cropped_page0_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

DiTo is a training-free inference-time acceleration applied to DiT-based text-to-image models (Flux with 35 steps, SD3 with 50 steps), taking a text prompt and producing a 1024×1024 RGB image. The method operates by reducing the token sequence length during self-attention at a configurable reduction ratio (0.25 or 0.50), then recovering the full token count via output copying, so the final output has identical spatial dimensions to the unaccelerated model. Evaluation uses ImageNet-1k class names as prompts with 3,000 generated images across three seeds.

## Method

DiTo shifts token matching from input-based to output-based similarity by exploiting temporal consistency in diffusion: prior-step output token similarity serves as a reliable proxy for current-step output similarity. The pipeline decouples matching from reduction/recovery into an interleaved schedule of Matching steps (full computation, establish correspondences) and Reduction steps (reduced computation, reuse correspondences), with PMR-guided Interval Scheduling determining the optimal matching frequency offline by maximizing reuse while keeping Pair Match Ratio above a threshold. To prevent blocking artifacts from repeated reuse concentrating errors in localized regions, Frequency-aware Token Matching subtracts a selection-history penalty from candidate similarity scores, balancing the reduction targets across spatial tokens.

## Results

On Flux (35 steps, 1024×1024), DiTo at a 0.25 reduction ratio achieves PSNR 25.21 dB, SSIM 0.8552, LPIPS 0.2385, and FID 31.95 with a 9.0% latency reduction (24.25 s vs. 26.64 s baseline). At the more aggressive 0.50 ratio, DiTo maintains PSNR 22.04 dB and FID 33.47 while cutting latency by 18.6% (21.69 s). On SD3 (50 steps), DiTo at 0.25 ratio reaches PSNR 26.83 dB and FID 27.83 with 7.4% speedup, and at 0.50 ratio achieves PSNR 23.16 dB and FID 28.89 with 16.4% speedup. Across both models, DiTo delivers 1.6–3.9 dB higher PSNR than all baselines at comparable or better latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiTo outperforms all four token-reduction baselines (ToMeSD, ToFu, SiTo, ToMA) on every quality metric (FID, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) at both 0.25 and 0.50 reduction ratios on both Flux and SD3. The PSNR advantage is most pronounced against ToMeSD and ToFu, which degrade severely at 0.50 ratio (FID 90.00 and 83.79 on Flux, respectively), while DiTo maintains FID 33.47. Against the strongest baseline ToMA, DiTo still gains 2.24–3.62 dB PSNR at 0.25 ratio and 1.55–2.31 dB at 0.50 ratio, with comparable or slightly better latency (21.69 s vs. 21.62 s on Flux at 0.50). On CLIP score, DiTo matches or slightly exceeds the vanilla model (23.85–24.01 vs. 23.71 on Flux), whereas baselines drop below the baseline. Against the caching-based method ToCa (compared in the appendix), DiTo achieves comparable latency with substantially lower memory overhead, since it stores only compact index metadata rather than full feature tensors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the ViT-style token reduction paradigm (ToMe, ToFu, SiTo, ToMA) that has been directly ported to diffusion models, arguing that input-token similarity is fundamentally misaligned with the recovery-error objective that generative tasks require. It repurposes the well-known temporal consistency of activations across diffusion timesteps—previously exploited for feature caching and step distillation—as a proxy for output-token similarity, enabling a matching stage that is decoupled from and cheaper than the reduction stage. The PMR-guided interval scheduling and frequency-aware penalty are new mechanisms specific to this output-centric formulation, with no direct analogue in prior ViT or DM token reduction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean conceptual reframing—matching should reflect output similarity, not input similarity—provides a principled lens for evaluating and designing future token reduction methods for any generative transformer, not just DiTs. The 1.6–3.9 dB PSNR gap over strong baselines at comparable latency is practically significant for high-resolution text-to-image pipelines where even small fidelity losses compound. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video DiTs (where temporal consistency is already the primary axis), how it behaves under very aggressive reduction ratios (>0.5), and whether the offline PMR calibration transfers across model scales or fine-tuned checkpoints without re-measurement.
