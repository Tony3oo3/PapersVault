## Problem

Existing driving VQA benchmarks (DriveLM, OmniDrive, NuScenes-QA) evaluate models on present-state perception or immediate reactive planning, leaving unexplored the ability to anticipate and reason about complex, long-horizon, multi-agent future events under counterfactual conditions. Additionally, while World Models generate visually realistic future videos, there is no principled way to assess whether those predictions are semantically coherent and actually useful for downstream foresight reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261137/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives 7 front-camera frames sampled at 2 fps from a 3-second historical window, ego-vehicle trajectories encoded as (x, y, θ) waypoints at 2 Hz, and a multiple-choice text query. For the WM-oriented evaluation, predicted future video and trajectories from a World Model (Epona or DrivingWorld) are appended as additional input. Outputs are single-select or multi-select text answers; continuous-valued tasks (e.g., relative distance) are discretized into labeled bins to maintain a unified accuracy metric. The dataset spans 15-second nuScenes clips segmented into a 3 s observation window and a 12 s future horizon, with QA pairs conditioned on variable-length prefixes to probe different temporal horizons.

## Method

FSU-QA is constructed via an automatic annotation pipeline over nuScenes that leverages 3D object trajectories, HD-map lane geometry, and kinematic states to generate 21K+ QA pairs across nine tasks organized into three cognitive levels: low-level spatio-temporal dynamic reasoning (speed/turn/lane change, relative distance/position), mid-level VRU-centric risk assessment (pedestrian intent, ego-VRU position, risk area), and high-level counterfactual prediction (rule-grounded what-if collision reasoning). Counterfactual ground truth is derived through closed-world geometric intersection checks rather than simulation, validated by human adjudication (89% agreement, κ=78%). The benchmark introduces a proxy WM-evaluation protocol: VLMs serve as discriminative probes, and the semantic utility of WM-generated futures is quantified by downstream QA accuracy gains over a zero-shot baseline, with shuffled cross-scene controls confirming that gains reflect genuine semantic content rather than incidental token augmentation. Fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B on the 18K training split (Qwen3-VL-8B-FI) demonstrates that targeted foresight supervision substantially closes the reasoning gap, outperforming all zero-shot baselines including GPT-5.

## Results

On FSU-Bench, GPT-5 achieves the highest zero-shot overall accuracy (48.66%), followed by Claude-Sonnet-4.5 (46.38%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (45.86%). All 13 evaluated VLMs struggle substantially on high-level Counterfactual Prediction (CFP), with the best score仅为 24.36% (Llama 4 Maverick), while low-level tasks like Lane Change reach 91–97%. Fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B on FSU-QA yields Qwen3-VL-8B-FI with 59.59% overall accuracy, surpassing every zero-shot baseline by over 10 points and reaching 50.20% on CFP—more than doubling the best zero-shot CFP score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among zero-shot baselines, GPT-5 (48.66%) leads overall, driven by strong mid-level scores on Ego-VRU Relative Position (46.91%) and Relative Position (32.32%), while Claude-Sonnet-4.5 (46.38%) edges ahead on Risk Area (49.33%). Qwen2.5-VL-72B (45.86%) dominates low-level geometric tasks (Lane Change 97.50%, Turn Change 90.17%) but drops sharply on mid-level multi-agent reasoning. Llama 4 Maverick (45.47%) achieves the best zero-shot CFP score (24.36%), yet still falls far below the finetuned model. Gemini variants (41.43–44.04%) are the weakest group, particularly on Risk Area (25–55%) and CFP (12–18%). The FSU-QA finetuned Qwen3-VL-8B-FI (59.59%) outperforms every zero-shot model by ≥10.9 points overall and more than doubles the best CFP score (50.20% vs. 24.36%), confirming the dataset's targeted supervision effect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing driving VQA from present-state perception and reactive planning (as in NuScenes-QA, DriveLM, OmniDrive) toward long-horizon, counterfactual, multi-agent semantic reasoning, which the authors term Foresight Intelligence. It repurposes the nuScenes driving corpus and the VQA task format but departs from prior work by requiring language-grounded causal judgments over 12-second futures rather than geometric path generation or immediate control decisions. The proxy World-Model evaluation protocol—using VLMs as discriminative judges of WM-generated semantic content—is a methodological departure from standard pixel-level generation metrics (FVD, FID) and from prior WM evaluation that focuses on visual realism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FSU-Bench provides the first systematic, multi-level benchmark for foresight reasoning in driving VLMs, and the finding that even GPT-5 scores only ~21% on counterfactual prediction highlights a genuine and persistent capability gap that scale alone does not close. The proxy WM-evaluation protocol offers a practical, task-grounded alternative to pixel-level metrics for assessing whether generated futures are semantically useful, though it is acknowledged to be probe-model dependent. Key open questions include cross-domain generalization beyond nuScenes, whether the 89% human-annotator agreement on counterfactual labels generalizes to open-world settings, and whether the fine-tuning gains (Qwen3-VL-8B-FI reaching 59.59 vs. 48.66 for GPT-5) transfer to unseen driving distributions.
