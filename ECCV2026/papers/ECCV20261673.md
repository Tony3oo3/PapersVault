## Problem

Modern distribution-based one-step distillation methods (VSD, adversarial distillation) assume Teacher and Student share the same latent space, i.e., identical VAE and latent resolution. This Shared-Space constraint blocks knowledge transfer from high-capacity 1024×1024 teachers (SD 3.5, Flux, Kolors) into compact 512×512 students (SD 1.5, SD 2.1) whose latent tensors are inherently incompatible in both spatial shape and VAE parameterization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a 512×512 RGB image in a single network function evaluation (NFE=1) using an unchanged SD 1.5 or SD 2.1 backbone. At inference time, the trained Bridge can optionally map the 512×512 student latent into the teacher latent grid and decode with the teacher VAE to yield a 1024×1024 output without retraining the student denoiser. Training data is entirely synthetic (~2M images) generated by the teacher model from JourneyDB/LAION prompts, avoiding dependence on large real-image datasets.

## Method

The paper introduces Bridge (B_φ), a ~5M-parameter latent interface that maps student latents z_S into the teacher latent space ẑ_T without modifying the student backbone. Bridge factorizes into two stages: a frozen spatial prior (the first n decoding blocks of the Student VAE decoder, with n=1) that expands the latent grid from h×w to H×W, and a learnable SwinIR projection head g_φ that aligns channels and semantics into the teacher representation. Bridge is trained with two complementary objectives: an L1 latent reconstruction loss against paired teacher latents, and an Attention Fidelity loss that minimizes reverse KL divergence between attention maps induced by ẑ_T and z_T in a frozen teacher denoiser (at t=1, τ=3.0). Once Bridge is trained and frozen, standard DMD2-style one-step distillation (VSD + adversarial loss) is applied to the student, with the Bridge converting student outputs into teacher-compatible space so that all distillation objectives operate in a single shared representation. The approach is mechanism-agnostic: student predictions are reparameterized to x̂_0 before teacher supervision, eliminating discretization mismatches between diffusion and flow-matching formulations.

## Results

Cross-Space Distillation via Bridge substantially improves compact one-step students across all five evaluation metrics. For the SD 1.5 student (0.86B params, 1 NFE), distilling from SD 3.5 Medium raises HPSv3 from 5.37 to 9.42 and ImageReward from −0.29 to 0.62; merging all five teacher-distilled checkpoints further pushes HPSv3 to 10.53 and HPSv2 to 29.07. For the SD 2.1 student, the merged checkpoint reaches HPSv3=9.75 and HPSv2=30.00, approaching the multi-step teacher scores (e.g., SD 3.5 Medium: HPSv3=10.86, HPSv2=30.02) while retaining one-step inference. Against the pruning baseline (OBS-Diff at 30% sparsity, 1.5B params), Bridge-enabled distillation at 0.86B params achieves HPSv3=9.42 vs. 2.76 and ImageReward=0.62 vs. −0.36.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DMD2-initialized SD 1.5 student (HPSv3=5.37, IR=−0.29), Bridge distillation from any single teacher yields 3.3–4.1 point HPSv3 gains and flips ImageReward positive; the merged checkpoint reaches HPSv3=10.53, exceeding even the SDXL teacher's 9.25. Similarly, the SiD-initialized SD 2.1 student (HPSv3=6.42, IR=0.10) improves to HPSv3=9.75 and IR=0.74 after merging, surpassing the SDXL teacher on HPSv2 (30.00 vs. 28.36). Compared to the OBS-Diff pruning baseline (30% sparsity, 1.5B params: HPSv3=2.76, IR=−0.36), Bridge-enabled distillation at a smaller 0.86B budget achieves HPSv3=9.42 and IR=0.62, a 6.7-point HPSv3 advantage with fewer parameters. The five multi-step teachers (SDXL, Kolors, PixArt-Σ, FLUX.2-klein-4B, SD 3.5 Medium) serve as upper-bound references at 20–50 NFE; the merged one-step student closes most of the gap on HPSv2 and ImageReward while remaining 3–5× lighter and 20–50× faster in inference steps. Notably, the merged SD 1.5 checkpoint (HPSv3=10.53) exceeds the individual SDXL and PixArt-Σ teachers on HPSv3, demonstrating that cross-teacher knowledge combination via parameter averaging can surpass any single source.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends distribution-based one-step distillation (VSD, DMD2, adversarial distillation) by removing the Shared-Space assumption that Teacher and Student must inhabit the same latent resolution and VAE. Rather than designing new compact architectures or pruning large teachers (which hits quality cliffs), it reuses existing compact backbones (SD 1.5, SD 2.1) and introduces a lightweight Bridge module—combining a frozen Student VAE decoder prefix for spatial upsampling with a SwinIR-based projector for channel/semantic alignment—that maps Student latents into the Teacher space so standard distillation losses apply unchanged. The attention-fidelity objective draws on MiniLLM's reverse-KL distillation and X2i's attention-matching idea, repurposed here for latent-space alignment rather than cross-modal transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: SD 1.5's massive ecosystem (LoRAs, ControlNets, community tooling) can now inherit the generative quality of SD 3.5 or Flux without retraining the backbone, and the ~5M-parameter Bridge adds negligible inference overhead. The model-merging result—averaging five teacher-distilled checkpoints to exceed any single teacher on HPSv3—suggests a path toward unifying capabilities from heterogeneous model families into one deployable student. Open questions include whether the Bridge generalizes to video or 3D generation, whether it can be made even lighter (e.g., a single conv layer), and whether the approach extends to cross-modal distillation (e.g., image-to-video teachers into image students). The 20-hour 8×H100 training cost, while modest, still limits accessibility compared to inference-time-only methods.
