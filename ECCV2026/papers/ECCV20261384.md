## Problem

Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods for driving scenes either generate a dense set of Gaussians for every pixel in every frame (causing multi-view inconsistency, floating artifacts, and high storage cost) or aggregate in voxel grids (limited by resolution and 3D convolution overhead). Neither paradigm provides a principled mechanism to decide when new Gaussians are actually needed versus when existing ones suffice, nor to distinguish stable geometric backbones from transient error-compensation primitives.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N uncalibrated RGB images at 518x294 resolution (4 frames spaced 5 apart during training) with no camera pose or intrinsics provided. It outputs a compact set of anisotropic 3D Gaussians (about 49,556 per view vs. 152,292 for baselines) each carrying position, opacity, covariance, view-dependent color, a generation confidence, and a time-decayed reuse confidence, alongside predicted camera poses, dense depth maps with depth confidence, and binary dynamic/static masks. The first frame initializes the full scene; subsequent frames only emit supplementary Gaussians where reprojection error indicates under-reconstruction.

## Method

EAGS builds on the VGGT backbone (DINOv2 patch tokens plus Alternating Attention) and introduces an error encoder that renders previously generated Gaussians into the current view, computes L1 plus Sobel-gradient photometric residuals, and feeds them through 3x3 convolutions to produce a pixel-wise generation confidence C_g that gates on-demand Gaussian expansion. A dual-confidence mechanism decouples instantaneous necessity (C_g) from long-term persistence (C_r^t), where C_r^t is derived by coupling C_g with depth confidence and applying an exponential temporal decay whose rate sigma is inversely proportional to initial reuse confidence. Static Gaussians are pruned via differentiable voxelization with a soft-gating mask (steep sigmoid on C_r^t), while dynamic Gaussians are voxelized only within a single frame to avoid cumulative drift. A Gaussian distillation loss forces the pruned subset's rendering to match the full-set rendering, concentrating scene information into a minimal backbone and preventing the catastrophic collapse that occurs when confidence-coupled opacity is pruned without this constraint.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset test split (202 scenes, 4 input frames), EAGS achieves the best rendering quality with PSNR 28.02, SSIM 0.849, and D-RMSE 3.44, while using only 49,556 per-view Gaussians—roughly one-third of the 152,292 used by most feed-forward baselines—and an inference time of 0.16 s. Under zero-shot evaluation on nuScenes and Argoverse2 (trained only on Waymo), EAGS attains PSNR 26.14 / SSIM 0.813 and PSNR 27.41 / SSIM 0.825 respectively, outperforming the strongest pose-free baseline DGGT by +0.83 and +1.07 dB in PSNR. The method is also the only pose-free approach in the main table that simultaneously leads on all three rendering metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAGS outperforms the closest pose-free competitor DGGT [4] by +0.61 dB PSNR and +0.003 SSIM while using only 49,556 Gaussians versus DGGT's 152,292 (≈3× fewer) and running 2.4× faster (0.16 s vs 0.39 s). Against the VGGT++ backbone [34], EAGS gains +5.52 dB PSNR and +0.100 SSIM, showing that the dual-confidence and error-aware modules contribute substantially beyond the shared transformer. Among pixel-wise feed-forward methods, EAGS surpasses STORM [42] by +1.64 dB PSNR and −2.04 D-RMSE, and DepthSplat [41] by +4.76 dB PSNR. Voxel-based baselines EVolSplat [21] and UniSplat [27] trail by +4.68 and +0.20 dB PSNR respectively, with EAGS also using fewer Gaussians than both. In zero-shot transfer to nuScenes and Argoverse2, EAGS leads DGGT by +0.83 and +1.07 dB PSNR, though DGGT retains a marginal LPIPS advantage on nuScenes (0.152 vs 0.159). All per-scene optimization methods (3DGS, DeformableGS, EmerNeRF, PVG) are outperformed on PSNR and SSIM while requiring 14–29 minutes of optimization per scene versus EAGS's single 0.16 s forward pass.

## Relation to Prior Work

EAGS builds directly on the VGGT backbone (DINOv2 + Alternating Attention) for multi-view feature extraction and on the 3DGS anisotropic Gaussian representation for rendering, but departs fundamentally from both pixel-aligned feed-forward methods (DGGT, DrivingForward, MVSplat) and voxel-based approaches (VolSplat, EVolSplat). Where prior feed-forward methods adopt a 'generate-everything' strategy that produces one Gaussian per pixel per frame, EAGS introduces an error-driven, on-demand generation mechanism coupled with a dual-confidence (generation + reuse) framework that explicitly separates instantaneous appearance fitting from long-term geometric persistence. The differentiable voxelization and Gaussian distillation loss further distinguish it from prior pruning strategies that rely on hard thresholds or simple confidence cutoffs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3× reduction in per-view Gaussians (49,556 vs. ~152,292) while achieving SOTA rendering quality addresses a critical bottleneck for real-time autonomous driving perception, where storage and downstream processing costs scale with primitive count. The zero-shot transfer to nuScenes and Argoverse2 without fine-tuning suggests the dual-confidence mechanism captures transferable geometric priors rather than dataset-specific patterns. However, dynamic object handling remains limited to single-frame voxelization to avoid cumulative drift, and the catastrophic collapse observed when removing the distillation loss (PSNR 28.02 → 13.04) reveals a fragile coupling between confidence and opacity that may limit straightforward extension to longer sequences or more complex dynamic scenes.
