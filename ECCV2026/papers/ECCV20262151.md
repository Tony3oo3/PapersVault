## Problem

Standard 3D Gaussian Splatting assumes static scenes with consistent illumination, but in-the-wild photo collections exhibit drastic appearance variations (lighting, weather) and pervasive transient objects that violate multi-view consistency. Existing appearance models rely on discrete per-image embeddings that cannot capture continuous environmental changes or spatially-varying local illumination, while transient suppression methods suffer from convergence mismatch with rapid 3DGS optimization or lack end-to-end adaptability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262151/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unconstrained multi-view RGB images (e.g., Photo Tourism landmarks, NeRF-OSR outdoor scenes) along with a 64-dimensional appearance latent code that conditions the 3D appearance field. It produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation renderable at 58 FPS, and additionally supports appearance manipulation: interpolating between two latent codes for smooth transitions, or sampling random vectors from the learned N(0,I) prior for unconditional novel appearance synthesis without any target reference image.

## Method

WilLaGS integrates three synergistic components. First, a β-VAE (β=2) maps input images into a structured 64-dim continuous latent space, learning a generative manifold of global appearance that avoids the overfitting of independent per-image embeddings. Second, a hypernetwork conditioned on the latent code z generates three orthogonal Tri-Plane feature planes (64×64×32 each), forming a 3D neural appearance field that each Gaussian queries at its spatial position to obtain dynamic, spatially-varying illumination features; these are fused with a learnable intrinsic feature and view direction through a lightweight color decoder. Third, a self-supervised Teacher-Student mechanism uses EMA-updated teacher weights to render a temporally smoothed pseudo-ground-truth, then computes VGG-16 perceptual feature differences against the actual ground truth to produce a binary static mask that down-weights transient regions in the L1+D-SSIM reconstruction loss, eliminating the need for external segmentation models or auxiliary uncertainty networks.

## Results

On the Photo Tourism dataset, WilLaGS achieves 25.84 dB PSNR on Sacre Coeur (+2.28 dB over the strongest baseline AsymGS at 23.56 dB), 24.54 dB on Trevi Fountain, and 29.94 dB on Brandenburg Gate, setting new state-of-the-art across all three scenes. On NeRF-OSR, WilLaGS leads on PSNR for europa (24.38), lwp (23.36), and st (23.43), while trailing GS-W by 0.17 dB on stjohann (25.55 vs 25.72) but still winning on SSIM (0.906 vs 0.894) and LPIPS (0.161 vs 0.276). Training requires only 0.9 GPU hours per scene with 58 FPS rendering, substantially faster than WildGaussians (7.8 hrs) and AsymGS (5.6 hrs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WilLaGS outperforms all 11 baselines on PSNR across all three Photo Tourism scenes, with the largest margin over AsymGS (+2.28 dB on Sacre Coeur, +0.63 dB on Trevi Fountain, +1.45 dB on Brandenburg Gate). On NeRF-OSR, WilLaGS leads on PSNR for europa (+1.07 dB over GS-W), lwp (+1.11 dB), and st (+0.30 dB), but narrowly trails GS-W on stjohann PSNR (25.55 vs 25.72) while still winning on SSIM and LPIPS for that scene. Among NeRF-based methods (NeRF-W, Ha-NeRF, CR-NeRF, K-Planes), WilLaGS achieves 3–8 dB higher PSNR, reflecting the advantage of explicit 3DGS representation. In terms of efficiency, WilLaGS trains in 0.9 GPU hours—roughly 8.7× faster than WildGaussians (7.8 hrs) and 6.2× faster than AsymGS (5.6 hrs)—while rendering at 58 FPS, competitive with other 3DGS-based methods. The closest overall competitor is AsymGS on PT and GS-W on NeRF-OSR, both of which use hierarchical illumination decomposition but lack the generative latent-space modeling and self-supervised masking that WilLaGS introduces.

## Relation to Prior Work

WilLaGS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its explicit scene representation and on the β-VAE framework for structured latent learning, but departs from the dominant paradigm of discrete per-image appearance embeddings (NeRF-W, Ha-NeRF, GS-W, WildGaussians) by learning a continuous, generative appearance manifold. It also unifies two problems that prior work treats as decoupled—appearance variation modeling and transient object suppression—into a single training loop, replacing both auxiliary uncertainty networks (which suffer convergence mismatch with 3DGS) and external pre-trained segmenters (which lack end-to-end adaptability) with a self-supervised Teacher-Student EMA perceptual mask. The Tri-Plane hypernetwork conditioned on the global latent code to produce spatially-varying local illumination is a novel architectural combination not present in prior 3DGS-in-the-wild methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generative latent space enables capabilities that discrete embeddings fundamentally cannot: unconditional appearance synthesis by sampling from N(0,I), smooth interpolation between lighting conditions, and strict multi-view consistency under a single latent code—opening a path toward controllable, physics-plausible relighting of reconstructed scenes. The self-supervised masking mechanism avoids the well-known training instability of jointly optimized auxiliary networks while remaining dataset-agnostic, which is practically important for deployment on arbitrary photo collections. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets (PT and NeRF-OSR), the method lacks explicit physical interpretability of the latent dimensions, and dense occlusions remain an open challenge; scaling to large-scale urban scenes and adding semantic control over the latent manifold are natural next steps.
