## Problem

Finetuning pretrained semantic encoders (e.g., DINOv2, MAE) for image reconstruction recovers fine-grained visual details but causes a sharp collapse in effective dimensionality of the resulting feature space. Under this geometry, standard velocity prediction in flow matching must fit orthogonal noise directions outside the low-dimensional signal manifold, making optimization inefficient and degrading generation quality. Prior work either compressed latents to lower dimensions (discarding information) or relied on frozen encoders (limiting reconstruction fidelity), leaving the potential of high-dimensional reconstruction-tuned representations for generation largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input, processed by a frozen Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct VLM with 64 learnable query tokens to extract conditioning embeddings for a 1B-parameter DiT. Generation operates in the high-dimensional feature space of a reconstruction-tuned representation autoencoder (1024-dim for finetuned DINOv2-L, 768-dim for MAE-B), producing 256×256 or 512×512 RGB images via a 100-step Euler sampler. The diffusion model is trained from scratch on a 34M subset of the BLIP3-o pretraining dataset (web-scale image-text pairs).

## Method

The paper identifies an effective-dimensionality collapse in reconstruction-tuned encoders via SVD analysis of patch embeddings, showing that finetuning for reconstruction forces features onto a much lower-dimensional subspace (e.g., R99 drops from 965 to 726 for DINOv2-L). The key theoretical insight is that under this geometry, the standard velocity target v = ε − x₀ decomposes into a manifold-aligned component Q(ε_l − c₀) plus an orthogonal term (1/t)(I − QQᵀ)z_t^h that is uninformative about the clean signal yet dominates in high dimensions, wasting model capacity. To bypass this, the paper replaces v-prediction with x₀-prediction: the DiT directly outputs the clean representation x_θ, and the loss is computed as ‖(x₀ − x_θ)/t‖², which mathematically eliminates the orthogonal noise regression. The full pipeline pairs a reconstruction-finetuned encoder (with semantic preservation loss) as the tokenizer, a frozen VLM for text conditioning, and a randomly initialized DiT trained with the x₀-prediction objective, achieving consistent gains over v-prediction baselines on GenEval, DPG-Bench, and COCO-30k FID.

## Results

On the finetuned DINOv2-L tokenizer (1024-dim), x0-prediction achieves GenEval 39.48, DPG-Bench 71.83, and COCO-30k FID 16.80, substantially outperforming the same tokenizer with standard v-prediction (GenEval 30.96, DPG 67.47, FID 29.97). For MAE-RAE (768-dim), x0-prediction improves GenEval from 36.17 to 40.89, DPG-Bench from 67.54 to 73.90, and FID from 22.20 to 17.24. At larger scale (90M dataset + SFT), the method reaches GenEval 79.69 and DPG-Bench 81.15 at 512 resolution, surpassing Scale-RAE (GenEval 77.43, DPG 78.47 at 224).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the frozen DINOv2-L baseline from RAE [65] (v-prediction, GenEval 37.63, DPG 70.21, FID 18.34), the paper's finetuned DINOv2-L with x0-prediction wins on all three metrics (GenEval 39.48, DPG 71.83, FID 16.80), confirming that reconstruction finetuning plus x0-prediction surpasses the frozen semantic encoder. Critically, the same finetuned tokenizer with standard v-prediction collapses to GenEval 30.96 and FID 29.97, demonstrating that the optimization difficulty is specific to v-prediction in the low-effective-dimensionality regime. Against Scale-RAE [53] at larger scale, the paper's 1B DiT with x0-prediction (GenEval 79.69, DPG 81.15 at 512) outperforms Scale-RAE's 2.4B DiT (GenEval 77.43, DPG 78.47 at 224) despite using a smaller model and higher resolution. The low-dimensional bottleneck variant (32-dim, v-pred) achieves slightly higher GenEval (40.99) and DPG (73.04) but worse FID (17.64 vs. 16.80), showing that x0-prediction in full 1024-dim space preserves fine-grained detail that compression sacrifices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of representation autoencoders (RAE, Scale-RAE, AlignTok) and diffusion parameterization choices (v-prediction vs. x0-prediction). It extends AlignTok's reconstruction finetuning of semantic encoders to high-dimensional latent spaces and adopts JiT's x0-prediction formulation, but the key departure is the analysis: the paper identifies that reconstruction finetuning induces an effective-dimensionality collapse that specifically breaks v-prediction in high-dimensional spaces, a failure mode not previously connected to the geometry of the latent. Unlike prior RAE work that either keeps encoders frozen (RAE) or compresses latents to low dimensions (AlignTok), this paper argues for operating directly in the full high-dimensional reconstruction-tuned space with a modified prediction target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is simple: if you finetune a semantic encoder for reconstruction and then train a diffusion model on its features, switch to x0-prediction to avoid a significant optimization bottleneck. The effective-dimensionality diagnostic (R_τ via SVD) gives practitioners a cheap way to predict when this regime applies. Open questions include whether the benefit persists at higher resolutions beyond 512, whether it extends to video generation, and whether other parameterizations (e.g., score-based) face the same orthogonal-noise issue. The paper is limited to 256×256 and 512×512 experiments with a 1B DiT, so broader architectural and resolution generalization remains to be established.
