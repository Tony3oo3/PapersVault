## Problem

Existing co-speech motion generation methods produce rhythmic, beat-synchronized gestures but cannot perform meaningful, context-specific actions while speaking. In real co-speech scenarios, gestures and actions spatially and temporally overlap, creating interference that undermines text-to-action correspondence. Additionally, no large-scale dataset exists that simultaneously contains both co-speech gestures and semantically rich actions, as prior co-speech datasets (TED talks, news broadcasts) lack actions while text-to-motion datasets exclude speech.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt describing the desired action, speech audio (represented as MFCC features), and an optional scalar gesture intensity signal in [0,1] as inputs, and produces 3D SMPL-X body and hand motion sequences at 22 fps (after 8× downsampling from 176-frame input). The text is embedded via CLIP and injected through cross-attention, while the intensity signal is modulated into the decoder via AdaLN-Zero. Two independent Pyramid-VQ tokenizers handle body and hands separately, each producing a 10-level hierarchy of discrete codes that are decoded autoregressively in a coarse-to-fine temporal order.

## Method

The core contribution is Pyramid-VQ, a residual-VQ autoencoder that explicitly binds each quantization layer to a distinct temporal resolution scale (T₁≤T₂≤…≤T_L=T), so that shallow layers act as low-pass filters capturing global action semantics while deeper layers encode high-frequency gesture details. Codebook sizes follow a large-to-small schedule (512→32) to match the representational needs of each scale. A CLIP-based semantic regularization with layer-wise decaying strength (λ_d=(1−d/L)²) further enforces that shallow layers align with textual semantics. At generation time, a 12-layer decoder-only Transformer with cross-attention to CLIP text and MFCC audio conditions predicts motion tokens autoregressively in a flattened coarse-to-fine order, with a shared hidden state ensuring body-hand synchronization. Gesture intensity is computed as the reconstruction error difference between shallow-only and full-stack decoding, then injected via AdaLN-Zero to enable explicit user control over how much co-speech gesture overlays the base action.

## Results

On the CoHuMo benchmark, CtrlCoMo achieves the best performance across all four evaluation metrics: FGD of 22.27, MAJE of 8.932, MM-Dist of 1.257, and R-Precision Top-1/Top-2/Top-3 of 0.373/0.520/0.608. The closest competitor, TalkSHOW, reaches a comparable FGD (22.98) but lags substantially on text-motion alignment (R-Precision Top-1 of 0.245 vs. 0.373). Probtalk is the strongest baseline on R-Precision (0.310 Top-1) yet still trails CtrlCoMo by 6.3 points. Ablation studies confirm that replacing Pyramid-VQ with a plain residual VQ degrades FGD from 22.27 to 109.63 and R-Precision Top-1 from 0.373 to 0.308.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CtrlCoMo outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against TalkSHOW [75], the closest FGD competitor (22.98 vs. 22.27), CtrlCoMo gains a 12.8-point advantage in R-Precision Top-1 (0.373 vs. 0.245), indicating far superior text-to-action alignment. Probtalk [40] is the strongest baseline on semantic alignment (R-Precision Top-1 = 0.310) but still trails CtrlCoMo by 6.3 points and has a notably worse FGD (29.85 vs. 22.27). Syntalker [7] and SemGes [36] are substantially weaker: Syntalker's FGD is 80.57 (3.6× worse) and SemGes's is 183.74 (8.3× worse), while both achieve R-Precision Top-1 below 0.19. The gap is most pronounced on R-Precision, where CtrlCoMo's text-conditioned generation with gesture-action disentanglement yields consistent 7–20 point improvements over all baselines, confirming that explicit action control via text is the primary differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the residual VQ-VAE framework (Lee et al., CVPR 2022) by binding each quantization layer to a specific temporal resolution scale, drawing inspiration from VAR's multi-resolution image generation, to create a coarse-to-fine hierarchy that separates global actions from local gestures. It also bridges two previously separate research threads: co-speech gesture generation (TalkSHOW, EMAGE, Probtalk), which conditions on audio but lacks semantic action control, and text-to-motion generation (T2M-GPT, MotionCLIP), which captures actions but excludes co-speech scenarios. The CLIP-based layer-wise semantic regularization departs from the global text-motion alignment in MotionCLIP by imposing a depth-decaying constraint that specifically guides shallow layers toward text semantics. The CoHuMo dataset addresses a clear gap identified in the literature: existing co-speech datasets (BEAT, TED, SHOW) contain only gestures, while action datasets (HumanML3D, KIT-ML) exclude speech, leaving no benchmark for the combined setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 370-hour CoHuMo dataset is the most practically significant contribution, as it is the first large-scale benchmark combining co-speech gestures with semantically rich actions across 20+ scene types and 5+ languages, filling a gap that has limited progress in this area. The Pyramid-VQ disentanglement mechanism offers a reusable architectural pattern for separating multi-scale motion components, and the explicit gesture-intensity control via AdaLN provides a practical interface for avatar applications. Limitations include the focus on upper-body motion, reliance on pseudo-3D annotations from video (rather than mocap), and evaluation on only the authors' own dataset, which limits generalizability claims. Open questions include whether the temporal-resolution-based disentanglement generalizes to full-body or multi-person scenarios, and whether the CLIP-based regularization is the optimal mechanism for enforcing layer-wise semantic separation.
