## Problem

Existing video editing frameworks operate on single continuous shots and, when naively extended to multi-shot sequences, suffer from severe identity drift and cumulative error propagation across discontinuous temporal boundaries. This is compounded by the absence of large-scale multi-shot training data and the lack of architectural mechanisms for cross-shot semantic awareness, making coherent editing across diverse viewpoints, scales, and poses an unsolved problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262480/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-shot input video (discontinuous temporal segments), per-shot editing masks, a hierarchical text prompt (one global caption plus per-shot descriptions), and a user-edited first frame as the visual anchor. At 480×832 resolution, the edited first frame's modifications are propagated across all subsequent shots in a single inference pass, producing a temporally coherent edited multi-shot video. Unlike chunked inference approaches that partition long sequences into independent segments, MSEditor processes the full multi-shot sequence jointly to avoid cross-chunk identity drift.

## Method

MSEditor builds on the Wan2.1-14B diffusion transformer backbone and introduces three key components. A Supervisory Adapter (zero-initialized, ControlNet-style) injects multi-view RGB and segmentation-mask features into the backbone during training, teaching the model cross-shot identity-consistent representations from repurposed multi-view video datasets (3,400 sequences × 10 viewpoints) annotated via double-reprojection. A Cross-Shot Packing Strategy dynamically groups semantically related shots (selected by DINOv2 embedding similarity above a threshold) into a joint self-attention window, where an anchor shot retains full temporal resolution and other shots contribute distilled reference frames, enabling cross-shot feature interaction without prohibitive memory cost. A Sparse Cross Attention module (adapted from HoloCine) routes each shot's visual queries to only its corresponding global and per-shot text embeddings, preventing semantic confusion from dense attention over long structured prompts. Training uses a first-frame editing paradigm with MSE loss across all shot-mask pairs, and inference requires a single forward pass with 50 flow-matching steps.

## Results

On the curated 600-video multi-shot editing benchmark, MSEditor achieves the best scores across all seven metrics: Aesthetic Score 6.05, inter-shot subject consistency 0.9370, inter-shot background consistency 0.9447, intra-shot subject consistency 0.9501, intra-shot background consistency 0.9462, global semantic consistency 0.1912, and shot-level semantic consistency 0.1886. In the fair-comparison setting where VACE and VideoPainter are retrained on the same multi-view dataset, MSEditor still leads on every metric, with the largest gaps in inter-shot subject consistency (+0.0069 over VACE) and global semantic consistency (+0.0184 over VACE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSEditor outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest single-shot baselines, VACE (Aesthetic 5.91, inter-shot subject 0.9237) and Ditto (Aesthetic 5.73, inter-shot subject 0.9287), MSEditor gains +0.14 and +0.08 in Aesthetic Score respectively, and +0.0133 and +0.0083 in inter-shot subject consistency. The gap widens substantially on semantic consistency: MSEditor's global score of 0.1912 exceeds VACE (0.1649) by 15.9% and Ditto (0.1514) by 26.3%. In the fair-comparison table (Table 2), where VACE and VideoPainter are retrained on the same multi-view dataset, MSEditor still leads VACE by +0.0069 in inter-shot subject consistency and +0.0184 in global semantic consistency, and leads VideoPainter by +0.0134 in inter-shot subject consistency and +0.0225 in global semantic consistency. TokenFlow and InsV2V, which were not retrained, show the largest overall gaps, with InsV2V trailing by 0.16 in Aesthetic Score and 0.0779 in shot-level semantic consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSEditor extends the single-shot video editing lineage (TokenFlow, InsV2V, VACE, Ditto) into the multi-shot regime by introducing a Supervisory Adapter (ControlNet-style) that injects cross-shot supervision into a Wan2.1 DiT backbone. It repurposes multi-view video datasets (SynCamMaster, Neural 3D Video Synthesis) as a proxy for the absent multi-shot training data, and borrows the Sparse Cross Attention mechanism from HoloCine for hierarchical textual conditioning. The key architectural departure is the Cross-Shot Packing strategy, which replaces the standard batch-dimension concatenation of shots with a DINOv2-guided dynamic grouping that enables cross-shot self-attention within a single inference pass, avoiding the chunked-inference paradigm used by prior long-video methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to address consistent editing across discontinuous shots, filling a practical gap for professional filmmaking and advertising workflows where content is inherently multi-shot. The finding that retraining strong single-shot baselines (VACE, VideoPainter) on the same multi-shot data still yields a significant performance gap validates that the architectural contributions (Cross-Shot Packing, Supervisory Adapter) are the primary drivers rather than data alone. Open questions include scalability to very long multi-shot sequences beyond the 81-frame context, the fidelity of multi-view data as a true proxy for narratively diverse multi-shot content, and whether the first-frame editing paradigm generalizes to more complex multi-region or temporal editing intents.
