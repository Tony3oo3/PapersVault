## Problem

Directly applying SAM2 to 360-degree panoramic video object segmentation produces implausible results due to equirectangular projection distortion, semantic discontinuity at the 0°/360° seam, and sparse object evidence in SAM2's short-term memory that causes identity drift under occlusion. Existing 360VOS methods (PSCFormer, 360VOTS) only partially address these geometric and temporal challenges, leaving a gap in robust, promptable panoramic video segmentation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes equirectangular-projected 360° video frames (H×W×3) with an initial prompt (mask, point, or box) on the first frame, and produces a temporally-consistent binary segmentation mask per frame. The model operates on a memory bank of 6 short-term frames plus L=2 long-term object pointers, with clips capped at 100 frames during training. The output mask is selected from three candidate masks by predicted IoU score, and the occlusion score gates whether the object is considered visible in the current frame.

## Method

PanoSAM2 adapts SAM2's Hiera-T/S backbone with three lightweight modules. The Pano-Aware Decoder applies left-right wrap padding (PC Block) with 3×3 and 5×5 convolutions to build seam-consistent receptive fields, then fuses the previous-frame mask through a frozen memory-encoder downsampler for iterative distortion refinement near high-latitude regions. The Long-Short Memory Module stores only compact object pointers for long-term frames (selected via occlusion-score-weighted sampling), computes a long-short similarity attention matrix to reweight short-term memory, and uses FiLM conditioning to synthesize pseudo long-term memory that is concatenated with short-term memory for attention-based feature conditioning. The Distortion-Guided Mask Loss projects the ground-truth mask into a BFoV to estimate foreground proportion, then assigns pixel-wise weights that emphasize high-distortion regions and boundary-adjacent hard negatives, reprojected back to spherical space for weighted BCE supervision. Training uses AdamW with StepLR for 80 epochs, introducing LSMM after epoch 20, with GT-mask memory correction every 8 frames.

## Results

On the 360VOTS test set, PanoSAM2 (Hiera-S) achieves a J&F score of 65.8, surpassing the SAM2 (Hiera-S) baseline by +5.6 points and the best 360-specific method PSCFormer-L by +3.3. On PanoVOS validation, PanoSAM2 (Hiera-S) reaches J&F of 78.1 (+6.7 over SAM2 Hiera-S), and on PanoVOS test it achieves 73.4 (+4.1). The method also demonstrates strong zero-shot transfer: pretrained only on 360VOTS, it outperforms SAM2 by +0.7 J&F on PanoVOS validation without any PanoVOS-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PanoSAM2 outperforms all 20 compared baselines on both 360VOTS and PanoVOS. Against the direct SAM2 baselines, PanoSAM2 (Hiera-S) gains +5.6 J&F on 360VOTS (65.8 vs. 60.2) and +6.7 on PanoVOS validation (78.1 vs. 71.4), with the F-score improving by +8.0 on 360VOTS, indicating substantially sharper boundaries. Against the 360-specific PSCFormer (the only other panoramic-aware method), PanoSAM2 leads by +3.3 J&F on 360VOTS (65.8 vs. 62.5) and +0.2 on PanoVOS validation (78.1 vs. 77.9), while also achieving higher F-scores. Against SAM2Long, which adds a training-free memory tree, PanoSAM2 still leads by +4.7 J&F on 360VOTS (65.8 vs. 61.1), showing that the LSMM's learned long-term pointer is more effective than heuristic tree-based memory selection. On the zero-shot generalization test (Table 3), PanoSAM2 pretrained solely on 360VOTS beats SAM2 by +0.7 J&F on PanoVOS validation and +1.6 on PanoVOS test, and outperforms all other zero-shot methods (PerSAM, SAM-PT, SAM-I2V) by 20+ points, confirming that the geometry-aware design transfers without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

PanoSAM2 builds directly on SAM2's promptable video segmentation architecture, adapting its mask decoder and memory bank for equirectangular panoramic video. It departs from prior 360VOS approaches (PSCFormer's left-right padding, 360VOTS's BFoV windowing) by embedding geometry awareness into SAM2's internal decoder and memory rather than relying on external search regions or output-level corrections. Among SAM2-based VOS extensions (SAM2Long, SAM-I2V, CamSAM2), PanoSAM2 is the first to jointly address spherical seam continuity, projection distortion, and long-term memory sparsity specific to 360-degree capture, using a Pano-Aware Decoder, a Distortion-Guided Mask Loss, and a Long-Short Memory Module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that lightweight, architecture-specific adaptations (not full retraining) can substantially close the gap between perspective and panoramic VOS while preserving SAM2's user-friendly prompting interface, making 360VOS more accessible for VR/AR, autonomous driving, and embodied AI applications. Limitations include single-object-only tracking, failure under fast latitude shifts with severe distortion, and identity swaps among visually similar distant objects. Open questions include extending to multi-object 360VOS, richer prompt modalities, and whether the distortion-aware loss and memory design transfer to other projection types (cubemap, fisheye) or to 360 image segmentation tasks.
