## Problem

Long-form video-language models face prohibitive quadratic attention scaling and KV-cache overhead as frame counts grow, yet existing token pruning methods rely on local, frame-level heuristics that misallocate budgets under observational noise, accumulate decision bias over long horizons, and fail to track non-stationary information density during scene transitions. The paper identifies these three control limitations—observational stochasticity, accumulated decision bias, and non-stationarity of information density—as the core gaps that deterministic pruning strategies cannot resolve.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames encoded into patch-level token embeddings (N tokens per frame) and a text query, operating entirely at inference time without any training or fine-tuning. It produces a dynamically budgeted, pruned visual token sequence per frame (retaining as few as 10% of original tokens) that is fed into the LLM backbone for video understanding. The budget B_t is computed causally per frame using a running maximum of the demand signal, so no future frames are needed, and the two-stage selection first applies vision-only filtering pre-LLM and then refines with task-aware attention at an intermediate LLM layer M.

## Method

S³-prune models token demand as a latent state tracked by a Kalman filter. It fuses two multi-scale signals—Spatial Uncertainty (SU), the normalized L2 shift of each patch embedding between adjacent frames, and Segment Transition (ST), the cosine distance between consecutive segment CLS centroids—into a single observation z_t after online z-score normalization. The Kalman filter (Eqs. 6–11) recursively estimates the latent demand d̂_t and its variance P_t, with the Kalman gain K_t adaptively suppressing transient spikes under high noise while tracking consistent signals. The token budget is then set as B_t = ⌈ρ · φ_t/Φ_t · N⌉ where φ_t = d̂_t(1+√P_t) adds an uncertainty margin, and Φ_t is the running maximum for causal normalization. Finally, a hierarchical two-stage selection picks the top-B_t tokens by SU pre-LLM, then retains the top ⌊α·B_t⌋ by maximum query-to-token attention at an intermediate LLM layer, combining vision-level redundancy removal with task-aware semantic distillation.

## Results

On LLaVA-OV-7B at 25% token retention, S³-Prune preserves 100.6% of vanilla average performance across seven benchmarks (MVBench 58.50, EgoSchema 61.11, LongVideoBench 43.12, VideoMME 59.01/62.34, MLVU 64.71, NextQA 78.91), outperforming the closest competitor HoliTom (100.1%). Under aggressive 10% retention, S³-Prune retains 98.3% average performance on LLaVA-OV-7B and 96.9% on LLaVA-VID-7B, while baselines degrade more sharply (e.g., VisionZip drops to 94.1% and VidCom² to 92.3%). On Qwen-3-VL-8B at 10% retention, S³-Prune achieves 93.3% average accuracy with 77.2% LLM time reduction and 4.1% peak memory savings versus vanilla.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S³-Prune outperforms all four baselines across both LLaVA backbones at both 25% and 10% retention ratios. Against HoliTom (the closest competitor), S³-Prune gains +0.5% average at 25% retention on LLaVA-OV-7B (100.6% vs. 100.1%) and +0.3% at 10% (98.3% vs. 98.0%), with the largest per-benchmark margin on VideoMME w/ subtitle (+1.4 at 25%, +1.7 at 10%). Against FastVid, the gap widens under aggressive compression: S³-Prune leads by +0.8% average at 10% on LLaVA-OV-7B (98.3% vs. 97.5%) and +0.3% on LLaVA-VID-7B (96.9% vs. 96.6%). Against VidCom², S³-Prune achieves a substantial +3.4% average advantage at 10% on LLaVA-VID-7B (96.9% vs. 92.3%), primarily driven by NextQA (79.07 vs. 71.85) and VideoMME. Against DyCoke, S³-Prune leads by +1.0% at 25% and +2.0% at 10% on LLaVA-OV-7B, with DyCoke showing the largest degradation on VideoMME w/ subtitle (56.81 at 25%, 56.03 at 10%) due to its lack of temporal stabilization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of token pruning for Video-VLMs and control-theoretic signal processing. While prior one-stage methods (FastVid, VidCom2, Entropy-Select) and two-stage methods (HoliTom, DyCoke, PruneVid) rely on static or local heuristics for token selection, S³-Prune reframes budget allocation as a latent-state estimation problem, applying a Kalman filter to smooth noisy spatio-temporal demand signals over long video horizons. The joint SU+ST demand signal draws on ideas from frame-uniqueness (VidCom2) and segment-transition detection (FastVid's DySeg), but the stability accumulation module and the uncertainty-aware budget expansion (Eq. 12) are novel contributions not present in any prior pruning framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, architecture-agnostic design makes S³-Prune a practical drop-in for any Video-VLM pipeline, and the consistent ~0.5–1% gains over strong baselines at 10% token retention across four different backbones (LLaVA-OV, LLaVA-VID, Qwen-2.5-VL, Qwen-3-VL) suggest the Kalman-based stabilization is a broadly applicable principle rather than a model-specific trick. Open questions include whether the heuristic Q/R tuning generalizes to unseen video domains (e.g., medical or industrial footage with different noise characteristics), whether the approach extends naturally to streaming/real-time inference where the full frame sequence is unavailable, and whether the two-stage LLM refinement can be replaced by lighter-weight query-relevance signals to further reduce latency. The widening performance gap at 512–768 frames hints that the method's advantage grows with sequence length, but this regime remains underexplored in the literature.
