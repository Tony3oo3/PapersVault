## Problem

Predicting deformable object dynamics is a fundamental challenge for robotic manipulation, yet systematic comparison of 2D video world models versus 3D particle world models has been impossible due to the absence of diverse, large-scale, multi-view real-world datasets with high-fidelity 3D annotations and synchronized tactile data. Existing benchmarks are limited in object diversity, rely on synthetic environments, or lack the contact-induced deformation signals needed to evaluate world models under realistic occlusions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261783/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The capture system records 41 calibrated surround-view RGB streams at 720×1280 and 30 FPS alongside bimanual tactile gripper signals (software-synchronized at 30 Hz) and robot proprioception (6D wrist pose + gripper openness). The annotation pipeline converts these into up to 1,600 dense 3D particle trajectories per frame, per-frame 3DGS geometry, and binary contact labels. Downstream world models consume the particle trajectories and action sequences to predict future 3D states (evaluated via Chamfer distance and rendered image quality) or future video frames (evaluated via PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS), while the MPC planner outputs robot action sequences to reach a goal configuration.

## Method

The core pipeline decouples per-frame geometry recovery from temporal tracking: 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructs high-fidelity object geometry per frame (using segmentation masks to isolate the object), while CoTracker3 tracks up to 1,600 grid-sampled points in 2D across all 41 views over 15-frame clips with stride 5. These 2D tracks are lifted to 3D via depth back-projection from the 3DGS, fused across views with RANSAC, and then refined through a physics-informed optimization combining a bidirectional Chamfer shape loss, ARAP local rigidity, Laplacian velocity smoothness, and a tactile no-slip regularizer at activated taxels (λ_local=20, λ_lap=0.1, λ_tactile=1.0). This yields temporally consistent particle trajectories even under heavy gripper occlusion, reducing Chamfer tracking error by 5× over vision-only tracking. The resulting annotations benchmark four world models—PGND, ParticleFormer, PhysTwin (3D particle), and post-trained Cosmos 2B (2D video)—across per-episode, multi-episode, and multi-object generalization settings, revealing that 3D models win in low-data regimes while 2D video models leverage scale for zero-shot object generalization.

## Results

In the per-episode setting (Table 3), the physics-based PhysTwin achieves the lowest Chamfer distance (CD 0.014) and track error (0.021) for reconstruction, while ParticleFormer leads in future prediction PSNR (26.29 dB). In the multi-episode setting (Table 4), Cosmos (2B video model) achieves the best reconstruction PSNR (27.75 dB) but ParticleFormer outperforms it in future prediction (CD 0.051 vs. Cosmos's unavailable 3D metrics; PSNR 25.20 vs. 24.95). In the zero-shot multi-object setting (Table 5), Cosmos achieves the highest PSNR (25.04 dB) and lowest LPIPS (0.037), while ParticleFormer achieves the best 3D metrics (CD 0.038, Track Error 0.048). The tracking pipeline with tactile integration reduces Chamfer distance error to 2.71×10⁻⁵ m², five times lower than visual-only tracking (1.41×10⁻⁴ m²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four world models across three generalization levels. In the per-episode (low-data) regime, PhysTwin [30] dominates both reconstruction and prediction with the lowest CD (0.014) and track error (0.021/0.025), confirming that explicit physics priors are critical when data is scarce. In the multi-episode setting, Cosmos [51] (2B video diffusion model) achieves the best reconstruction PSNR (27.75 dB) by preserving visual textures directly in 2D space, but ParticleFormer [27] clearly wins future prediction (CD 0.051 vs. PGND's 0.130; PSNR 25.20 vs. Cosmos's 24.95), showing that 3D structural priors generalize better to unseen interaction sequences. PGND [86] consistently underperforms both ParticleFormer and Cosmos in multi-episode and multi-object settings, with its prediction CD degrading to 0.130 (multi-pisode) and 0.429 (multi-object). In the zero-shot multi-object setting, Cosmos achieves the best image quality (PSNR 25.04, LPIPS 0.037) thanks to large-scale pre-training, but suffers from action misalignment over long horizons; ParticleFormer retains the best 3D geometric accuracy (CD 0.038, Track Error 0.048), while PhysTwin is excluded as it cannot generalize to novel object geometries without per-object optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deformable object dataset lineage (ClothSim2Real, Robo360, PokeFlex, DOT, PGND) by being the first to combine 41 surround-view calibrated cameras with bimanual tactile sensing at scale (198 objects, 23.3M frames), and by providing markerless 3D particle annotations via a 3DGS + CoTracker3 pipeline. It also positions itself at the intersection of two world-modeling paradigms—action-conditioned 2D video generation (Cosmos, video diffusion) and 3D particle dynamics (ParticleFormer, PGND, PhysTwin)—providing the first unified real-world benchmark to systematically compare them. The annotation pipeline builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting for per-frame geometry and CoTracker3 for 2D correspondence, but departs from prior GS-based tracking (DeformGS, PhysGaussian) by decoupling geometry recovery from temporal tracking and incorporating tactile signals as a physics-informed regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Deform360 fills a critical gap: prior deformable datasets were either small, synthetic, marker-based, or lacked tactile and multi-view coverage, making systematic evaluation of world models for deformable dynamics nearly impossible. The key empirical finding—that 3D particle models with structural priors dominate in low-data regimes while 2D video models leverage pre-training scale for zero-shot object generalization—provides a clear decision framework for practitioners choosing between paradigms. Open questions remain: whether 3D world models can be pre-trained at scale (PointWorld is mentioned but not yet available), how to design effective reward functions on generated video for model-based planning, and whether richer tactile sensing (beyond normal-axis pressure) can resolve slip and highly plastic material limitations.
