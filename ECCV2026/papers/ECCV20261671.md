## Problem

Precisely manipulating objects in a single photograph (translation, rotation, scaling) while obeying 3D physical constraints remains unsolved for diffusion-based editors. Existing 2D methods violate perspective, hallucinate ghosting at the original object position, and forcing structural proxies into the latent space disrupts variance homogeneity, causing self-attention leakage and background blur. The core difficulty is asymmetric: the relocated object must rigidly follow prescribed 3D geometry, yet the uncovered background needs generative freedom to synthesize plausible content.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB source image at 720×480, a user-specified 3D transformation (rotation, translation, scale) applied to a target object delineated by a spatial mask, and a text prompt for semantic context. It produces a single photorealistic composite image where the object has been rigidly transformed and the background is seamlessly inpainted. The entire pipeline is training-free, orchestrating off-the-shelf models (VGGT for depth, SV3D for multi-view completion, Wan2.2-VACE for denoising) on a single A800 GPU with ~18 minutes total runtime.

## Method

GeoEdit follows a Lift-Manipulate-Render-Denoise pipeline: it decoupled-reconstructs the scene and target object in 3D (monocular depth + multi-view diffusion for occluded surfaces), registers them via correspondence-aware similarity transformation, applies the user's rigid transformation, and renders a geometry-aligned proxy with a structural depth map. The core contribution is Dual-Branch Denoising, which repurposes a video diffusion backbone (Wan2.2-VACE) whose temporal self-attention preserves object identity without fine-tuning. Within a narrow denoising window (t=47 to t=40 out of 50 steps), the foreground latent is replaced with a forward-noised proxy at exactly matching noise variance (variance-homogeneous injection), making the injected signal statistically indistinguishable from the native denoising path and suppressing self-attention leakage. Outside this window, the background denoises freely under the generative prior to fill disoccluded holes and harmonize boundaries. A warm-start initialization at t=47 anchors the global color layout from the proxy, avoiding the semantic ambiguity of pure-noise initialization.

## Results

On GeoEditBench (50-pair stratified subset), GeoEdit achieves the highest PSNR (23.499), DINO similarity (0.961), and lowest LPIPS (0.114) and DreamSim (0.027) among all compared methods. On geometry-aware metrics, GeoEdit attains 94.9% PoseMap IoU and 57.9% Object IoU, substantially outperforming the next-best baselines (80.0% and 51.3% respectively). In human preference evaluation (25 raters, Krippendorff's α = 0.74), GeoEdit scores 4.810/5, nearly double the runner-up (Flux-Kontext at 2.714), and also leads the VLM-based AI judge (4.125/5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoEdit outperforms all five baselines on 8 of 9 quantitative metrics, losing only on CLIP similarity to NanoBanana (0.952 vs. 0.976), which reflects NanoBanana's stronger semantic alignment but weaker geometric precision. The most decisive margin is on PoseMap IoU: GeoEdit reaches 94.9% versus 80.0% for Image Sculpting (the next best) and 60.0% for 3DiT, confirming that the 3D lifting pipeline enforces rigid transformations far more accurately than 2D or SDS-based alternatives. On Object IoU, GeoEdit (57.9%) leads Flux-Kontext (51.3%) by 6.6 points and 3DiT (33.5%) by 24.4 points. In human preference, GeoEdit (4.810/5) nearly doubles Flux-Kontext (2.714) and Image Sculpting (2.619), while 3DiT scores lowest (1.191), consistent with its reliance on synthetic training data limiting real-world generalization. NanoBanana, despite the CLIP edge, trails GeoEdit on every geometry-aware and perceptual metric (PoseMap IoU 75.0% vs. 94.9%, DreamSim 0.031 vs. 0.027), indicating its edits are semantically plausible but geometrically imprecise.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoEdit sits at the intersection of single-image 3D reconstruction (VGGT, SV3D) and diffusion-based editing, but its core methodological contribution is the Dual-Branch Denoising with variance-homogeneous injection, which extends the region-dependent scheduling idea from Time-to-Move to the image-editing setting and builds on the masked blending of Blended Diffusion while explicitly preserving the DiT's homogeneous noise assumption. Unlike prior 3D-aware editors (Image Sculpting, Object-3DIT, BlenderFusion, 3DiTScene) that either require external 3D software, synthetic training data, or SDS-based optimization, GeoEdit is fully training-free and supports full rigid transformations (rotation, scaling, translation) via a Lift-Manipulate-Render-Denoise pipeline. The repurposing of a video diffusion backbone's temporal prior for single-image identity preservation is a notable architectural choice that departs from the standard image-diffusion editing paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap—precise 3D object manipulation in photographs without external 3D tools—and the variance-homogeneous injection provides a clean, generalizable principle for injecting structural constraints into DiTs without disrupting their attention mechanism. However, the pipeline's computational cost (~18 minutes, 44 GB VRAM on a single A800) and its dependence on multiple off-the-shelf models (VGGT, SV3D, Wan2.2-VACE) limit immediate adoption, and error propagation from monocular depth estimation remains a fundamental vulnerability. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-rigid deformations, whether the 81-frame video context can be shortened without structural degradation, and whether the variance-matching principle transfers to other DiT-based editing tasks beyond object manipulation.
