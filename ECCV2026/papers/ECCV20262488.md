## Problem

Sparse point clouds provide insufficient appearance evidence, causing geometric bias that confuses objects with similar shapes but distinct appearances (e.g., coplanar doors/windows with walls, reflective appliances adjacent to surfaces). Existing boundary-aware methods rely solely on geometric cues, while 2D-3D fusion approaches suffer from projection misalignment and occlusion-induced loss, leaving no unified 3D representation that natively encodes both geometry and appearance without cross-modal artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262488/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard point clouds (coordinates, RGB color, normals; 9D per point) as the sole inference input, but requires an offline preparation stage using pre-computed 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions (~1.5M Gaussians per scene from SceneSplat-7K) to extract opacity and anisotropic scale attributes. During preparation, each point is augmented from 9D to 13D by appending aggregated Gaussian scale (3D) and opacity (scalar). At inference, the model is Gaussian-free, consuming only the original point geometry and producing per-point semantic labels plus a binary boundary prediction map.

## Method

G2P operates in two stages. In the preparation stage, a covariance-aware Gaussian-to-Point alignment uses Mahalanobis distance (respecting each Gaussian's anisotropic ellipsoid shape) to match each point to k=20 nearest Gaussians, then aggregates opacity and scale via inverse-Mahalanobis weighting; a Sonata-based appearance encoder is self-supervised on the augmented representation (replacing normals with opacity to inject view-consistent visibility cues), and scale-magnitude thresholds combined with semantic label discontinuities generate boundary pseudo-labels. In the training stage, a PT v3 backbone with BFANet's boundary-semantic block is jointly supervised by semantic loss (cross-entropy + Lovász-softmax), boundary loss (BCE + Dice), and a cosine-similarity distillation loss that transfers the pre-trained appearance encoder's features into the backbone via a mapping MLP. This design preserves original point geometry for accurate spatial reasoning while distilling Gaussian-derived appearance and boundary cues, enabling Gaussian-free inference without 2D features or language supervision.

## Results

On ScanNet v2, G2P achieves 78.4 mIoU, surpassing the PT v3 baseline (77.5) by +0.9 and the boundary-aware BFANet (78.0) by +0.4, while remaining competitive with 2D-3D fusion methods that rely on external pre-training (ODIN: 77.8, PonderV2: 77.0). On ScanNet200, G2P reaches 36.6 mIoU and 83.8 OA, improving over PT v3 by +1.4 mIoU. The method also generalizes to ScanNet++ (48.7 mIoU) and Matterport3D (55.9 mIoU), and on instance segmentation (ScanNet200) attains 23.2 mAP with PointGroup, edging out PT v3 (23.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

G2P outperforms the strongest geometric baseline PT v3 by +0.9 mIoU on ScanNet v2 and +1.4 mIoU on ScanNet200, and beats the boundary-aware BFANet by +0.4 mIoU on ScanNet v2. Against 2D-3D fusion methods (ODIN 77.8, PonderV2 77.0, UniPre3D 77.6), G2P is competitive or superior without requiring any external pre-training or 2D image inputs. The only method that edges out G2P on ScanNet v2 is PT v3+PPT (78.6 vs. 78.4), but that variant relies on large-scale external pre-training. On geometrically challenging classes (doors, windows, refrigerators, shower curtains), G2P shows its largest gains: +6.0 IoU on refrigerator and +7.2 IoU on shower curtain over PT v3, with a 69.2 average IoU on the challenging subset versus 66.8 for PT v3. On the ScanNet200 hidden test set, G2P (35.7 mIoU) is within 0.3 of BFANet (36.0) and achieves the best Tail-class IoU (20.2). For instance segmentation, G2P improves mAP25 by +1.7 over PT v3 (41.8 vs. 40.1) while matching mAP50 and mAP.

## Relation to Prior Work

G2P sits at the intersection of three research threads: boundary-aware point cloud segmentation (JSENet, CBL, BFANet), 2D-3D cross-modal fusion (VMVF, ODIN, PonderV2), and 3D Gaussian Splatting for scene understanding. It departs from 2D-3D fusion by avoiding explicit image-to-point projection, instead extracting 3D-native appearance cues (opacity, anisotropic scale) from pre-optimized Gaussians and transferring them to points via Mahalanobis-distance-based correspondence. It builds on Sonata's self-supervised encoder architecture (repurposed as a teacher that encodes opacity in place of normals) and BFANet's boundary-semantic block, integrating both into a PT v3 backbone. The key conceptual departure from prior GS-based segmentation work (which operates in Gaussian space for rendering) is the explicit Gaussian-to-Point alignment that preserves original point geometry while borrowing GS-derived visibility and boundary cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

G2P offers a practical path to mitigate geometric bias in point cloud segmentation without requiring 2D image features, language models, or cross-modal projection at inference—only a point cloud and pre-computed GS attributes. The class-level gains on geometrically ambiguous objects (refrigerator +6.0 IoU, shower curtain +7.2 IoU over PT v3) validate the core hypothesis that opacity and scale encode appearance-relevant information that pure geometry misses. However, the approach depends on an offline GS preparation stage and the availability of high-quality GS reconstructions (SceneSplat-7K covers indoor scenes only), limiting immediate applicability to outdoor or real-time settings. Open questions include whether the GS-to-point alignment can be made online or learned end-to-end, and whether the opacity/scale cues generalize to scenes where GS optimization quality is lower.
