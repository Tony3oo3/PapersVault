## Problem

Single-image dehazing methods rely on the Atmospheric Scattering Model, whose single-scattering and homogeneous-media assumptions are routinely violated by real-world haze, producing residual fog and color drift. Additionally, large-scale paired hazy/clear data are impractical to collect, and existing synthetic pipelines cannot reproduce the spatially varying, multiple-scattering complexity of natural haze. This paper addresses both gaps simultaneously by replacing the ASM with the Radiative Transfer Equation and building a scalable VLM-driven data generation pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single hazy RGB image (resized to 512×512) and produces a dehazed RGB image of the same resolution. Internally, the image is encoded into a 4×64×64 latent tensor via the frozen Stable Diffusion 2.1 VAE, where all flow-matching and RTE regularization operate; the final latent is decoded back to pixel space. At inference, the ODE is solved with explicit Euler integration (recommended >10 steps) to balance speed against trajectory accuracy. The data pipeline additionally consumes text prompts (e.g., 'inject a dense, irregular billow of heavy smoke…') fed to Qwen2.5-VL or Gemini to synthesize hazy counterparts from clean references.

## Method

RTE-FM-Dehazer formulates dehazing as a flow-matching ODE in VAE latent space, where a U-Net predicts a spatially varying velocity field that transports the hazy latent toward the clean latent. The key innovation is an RTE-derived regularizer: the diffusion-absorption operator (−D∇²z + μ_a z), discretized with a 5-point Laplacian stencil, is computed at each training step and the velocity field is penalized via an L² term to align with this physical direction, yielding a combined loss of transport fit plus λ·RTE fit. This embeds the continuous operator (−D∇² + μ_a I) into the learning problem, so the ODE trajectory follows a physically regularized path rather than a bare straight line. For training data, a two-stage VLM pipeline generates hazy images from clean references and aligns them via dense keypoint matching and sub-pixel homography warping, producing the 50k-pair P-HAZE dataset. The entire system is trained end-to-end for 200 epochs on a single RTX 4090 with Adam (lr 1e-4, halved every 50 epochs, batch 64, λ=0.5).

## Results

RTE-FM-Dehazer, trained solely on the 50k P-HAZE synthetic dataset, achieves the highest PSNR on four of six benchmarks (I-HAZE 18.81 dB, NH-HAZE 19.53 dB, SMOKE 18.67 dB, P-HAZE 20.29 dB) and the lowest LPIPS on five of six (best: I-HAZE 0.145, NH-HAZE 0.233, SMOKE 0.222). On the most challenging real-world benchmarks, it outperforms the strongest competitor LHTD by +3.9 dB PSNR and −37% LPIPS on NH-HAZE, and by +3.0 dB PSNR and −37% LPIPS on SMOKE. On the synthetic RESIDE-6K set, DEANet and DehazeFormer retain higher PSNR (~31.5 dB vs. 21.8 dB) and lower LPIPS (0.012 vs. 0.068), confirming the domain-shift advantage of the proposed method on real imagery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest real-world competitor LHTD (CVPR 2025), RTE-FM-Dehazer wins by +3.9 dB PSNR and 0.138 LPIPS on NH-HAZE and by +3.1 dB PSNR and 0.114 LPIPS on SMOKE, but LHTD retains a narrow +0.28 dB PSNR edge on D-HAZE. On I-HAZE, the proposed method leads all baselines by a wide margin (18.81 dB PSNR vs. 16.96 for DEANet, the next best). On the synthetic RESIDE-6K benchmark, DEANet (31.78 dB) and DehazeFormer (31.42 dB) substantially outperform RTE-FM-Dehazer (21.77 dB), reflecting the domain gap between depth-map-synthesized haze and the VLM-generated P-HAZE training distribution. Among VQGAN-prior methods, RIDCP and IPC-Dehaze achieve competitive SSIM on I-HAZE (0.817, 0.809) but trail RTE-FM-Dehazer on PSNR and LPIPS across all real-world benchmarks. Classical and early deep baselines (DCP, AODNet, C2PNNet) are uniformly outperformed by 5–10 dB PSNR on real datasets, confirming that the RTE-regularized flow matching formulation provides a substantially stronger inductive bias for non-uniform, multi-scattering haze than hand-crafted priors or unregularized transport.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) as the generative backbone but departs from prior dehazing work by replacing the ubiquitous Atmospheric Scattering Model with the Radiative Transfer Equation as the physical prior, arguing that ASM's single-scattering and homogeneity assumptions break down in real-world haze. It also diverges from the dominant diffusion-based and VQGAN-based generative dehazing approaches (DehazeDiff, RIDCP, IPC-Dehaze) by using a deterministic ODE trajectory regularized at each step by an RTE-derived diffusion-absorption term, avoiding stochastic sampling artifacts. The VLM-driven data pipeline (Qwen2.5-VL / Gemini + homography alignment) represents a new direction in dehazing data synthesis, moving beyond ASM-based or depth-map-based pair generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The P-HAZE dataset (50k VLM-generated, geometry-aligned pairs) and the VLM-based synthesis pipeline offer a reusable, scalable solution to the chronic scarcity of real hazy/clear training pairs, potentially benefiting the broader low-level vision community. The finding that a physics-inspired RTE regularizer outperforms both vanilla flow matching and the traditional dark-channel prior as a constraint on the velocity field suggests that soft, differentiable physical priors are more compatible with modern generative frameworks than hard heuristic losses. Open questions include whether operating in VAE latent space (as acknowledged by the authors) limits fidelity in ultra-dense haze, whether the approach scales to video or 3D radiance fields, and whether the 10+ Euler steps required for quality can be reduced via distillation or learned step schedules.
