## Problem

Face super-resolution lacks a publicly available, racially diverse paired dataset with fixed train/test splits, making prior results irreproducible and evaluations biased toward overrepresented demographic groups. Additionally, existing Mamba-based restoration methods rely on fixed global scanning paths that treat all facial regions uniformly, failing to allocate more modeling capacity to identity-critical areas like eyes and mouth while simultaneously preserving global facial structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261917/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB facial image (512×512 HR source downsampled by ×4 or ×8) degraded via bicubic interpolation, Gaussian blur followed by downsampling (7×7 kernel, σ=1.6), or downsampling plus additive Gaussian noise (σ=30), and produces a corresponding high-resolution 512×512 facial image. The introduced SFHQFSR benchmark provides 70K paired LR/HR images across four degradation settings (BI×4, BI×8, BD×4, DN×4) with fixed 60K/10K train/test splits, enabling direct reproducibility of all reported results.

## Method

GLASNet is a hybrid state-space network whose core building block (HSSMB) alternates between a Visual State-Space Module (VSSM) for global long-range semantic propagation and a Region-Adaptive State-Space Module (RASSM) that dynamically adjusts its scanning path via DCNv3-predicted spatial offsets, concentrating capacity on structure-rich regions (eyes, nose, mouth) while reducing it on homogeneous skin areas. After each state-space pass, a Multi-domain Refiner sequentially applies channel attention (identity semantics), spatial attention (geometric alignment), and frequency attention (high-frequency texture recovery) to harmonize cues across domains. The full architecture stacks 6 groups of 6 HSSMBs (180 channels, λ=1) with learnable residual scaling factors, trained end-to-end with Adam (lr=2e-4) for 800K iterations at batch size 1. The companion SFHQFSR dataset is generated by conditioning ControlNet on face parsing maps from FFHQ/CelebA-HQ, then filtering by Laplacian sharpness (>160) and classifying via a FairFace-trained model to ensure racial, gender, and age diversity.

## Results

On the standard bicubic ×4 FSR benchmark, GLASNet achieves 34.70 dB PSNR / 0.9158 SSIM / 0.1999 LPIPS on SFHQFSR, 32.51 / 0.8725 / 0.1993 on CelebA, and 29.57 / 0.8878 / 0.1614 on Helen, outperforming the best prior FSR method (SFMNet) by up to 0.28 dB PSNR and 0.0026 SSIM. At the more challenging ×8 scale, GLASNet reaches 30.86 dB PSNR on SFHQFSR and 24.58 dB on Helen, maintaining a 0.22 dB lead over SFMNet. In the SOTA comparison under the hardest DN×4 setting (Table 7), GLASNet attains the best PSNR (24.50), SSIM (0.7292), LPIPS (0.4004), FID (54.11), and IDS (0.79) while also being the fastest at 0.1426 s/image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLASNet outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and degradation setting. Among FSR methods, SFMNet [36] is the closest competitor, trailing GLASNet by 0.28 dB PSNR (SFHQ ×4), 0.22 dB (SFHQ ×8), and 0.35 dB (Helen BD×4); SISN [31] and SPARNet [4] lag further behind by roughly 1 dB PSNR. General SISR methods (RCAN, EDSR, SwinIR, PFT) consistently underperform FSR-specific methods, with PFT [30] being the strongest SISR baseline but still 1.0 dB below GLASNet on SFHQ ×4. HiFaceGAN [44] achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.1418 on SFHQ ×4) but sacrifices fidelity severely (32.06 dB PSNR), illustrating a perception–fidelity trade-off. In the SOTA comparison (Table 7, Helen DN×4), GLASNet beats VmambaIR [34] by 0.23 dB PSNR and 0.16 FID, surpasses the diffusion-based DiT4SR [10] by 1.02 dB PSNR while being ~380× faster (0.14 s vs 54.3 s), and outperforms FaceMe [26] by 0.48 dB PSNR with a 484× speed advantage. KEEP [11], a video-based method, is the weakest baseline at 23.45 dB PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLASNet extends the Mamba/VSSM line of state-space models for image restoration by replacing the fixed global scan path with a hybrid that alternates between global (VSSM) and region-adaptive (RASSM) scanning, where the latter uses DCNv3-style deformable offsets to redirect attention toward identity-critical facial regions. The dataset contribution departs from the common practice of synthesizing FSR pairs from FFHQ/CelebA with unreleased splits, instead generating 70K paired images via ControlNet conditioned on face parsing maps to ensure racial diversity and reproducibility. The Multi-domain Refiner (channel + spatial + frequency attention) is a straightforward but effective addition that addresses the paper's stated limitation of prior methods treating these domains separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SFHQFSR dataset fills a genuine gap in the FSR community by providing a public, fixed-split, racially diverse paired benchmark, which should improve reproducibility and enable subgroup-aware evaluation that prior non-public synthetic datasets could not support. The region-adaptive scan is a reasonable architectural idea, but the performance gains over the strongest baseline (SFMNet) are modest (~0.2–0.3 dB), and all experiments use synthetic degradations (bicubic, blur, Gaussian noise) rather than real-world uncontrolled degradation, leaving open how well the approach transfers to genuinely wild FSR. A key limitation is that the dataset is entirely synthetic (ControlNet-generated), so its fidelity to real sensor degradation and its long-term utility as a benchmark remain to be validated by the community.
