## Problem

In incomplete multimodal learning, test-time modality missingness causes task representations to deviate systematically from their full-modality counterparts, yet prior methods either impute missing inputs (suffering hallucination) or learn robustness without explicitly modeling this representational shift. The paper asks whether the *difference* between full and partial representations can be leveraged as a privileged signal to guide expert specialization in a mixture-of-experts framework, rather than relying solely on the degraded incomplete embedding for routing decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260542/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multimodal inputs that vary by domain (RGB+Depth+IR for face anti-spoofing, RGB+DoLP+AoLP+NIR for material segmentation, audio+visual for emotion recognition, image+text for food classification). During training, each sample is processed twice—once with all modalities present and once with a stochastically masked subset—so the model jointly sees both the complete and incomplete fused representations. At inference, only the available modalities are fed through the feature router and experts, producing a class label or pixel-wise segmentation mask without requiring access to missing inputs.

## Method

MARS introduces a dual-router MoE architecture where a privileged residual router conditions expert selection on z_res = z_full − z_partial, explicitly encoding how missingness reshapes the task representation, while a deployable feature router learns to replicate this routing from incomplete inputs alone via KL-divergence distillation. Experts operate on the incomplete feature z_partial, so routing (conditioned on the residual) and computation (on the partial embedding) are decoupled. A discrepancy-aware noise regularization adaptively increases the Gaussian routing noise variance for experts where the two routers disagree most, encouraging stochastic exploration that makes experts robust to imperfect imitation at test time. A discrepancy-guided modality sampling strategy further prioritizes training on modality combinations exhibiting the largest router disagreement, balancing learning across incomplete configurations.

## Results

On CASIA-SURF (face anti-spoofing), MARS achieves an average ACER of 2.43%, reducing the best baseline DMRNet (3.58%) by 1.15 points, with the largest gain in the IR-only setting (3.96% vs. 8.98%). On MCubeS (material segmentation), MARS reaches 0.4773 mIoU versus DMRNet's 0.4683. On CREMA-D (emotion recognition), MARS attains 65.52% average accuracy vs. DMRNet's 61.35%, and on UPMC Food-101 (food classification) it scores 91.59% vs. SimMLM's 84.81%. MARS consistently achieves the best result across all modality combinations on every dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARS outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and modality combination. On CASIA-SURF, it reduces average ACER from 3.58% (DMRNet, the strongest baseline) to 2.43%, with the most dramatic improvement in the IR-only setting where error drops from 8.98% to 3.96%—more than halving the error. Among MoE-based competitors, MARS beats Flex-MoE (3.69%), MoMKE (3.66%), and SimMLM (4.67%) by 1.26–2.24 ACER points on average. On MCubeS segmentation, MARS achieves 0.4773 mIoU, surpassing DMRNet (0.4683) and Flex-MoE (0.4540) while using fewer FLOPs (716G vs. 1297G for MoMKE). On CREMA-D, MARS gains 4.17 points over DMRNet (65.52% vs. 61.35%), and on UPMC Food-101 it leads SimMLM by 6.78 points (91.59% vs. 84.81%), though it slightly trails SimMLM in the full-modality food classification case (92.72% vs. 94.99%). Representation-learning baselines (HeMIS, LCR, RFNet, mmFormer, ShaSpec) and distillation-based MMANet are all substantially outperformed, confirming that explicit residual-guided expert specialization provides a clear advantage over modality-invariant or uncertainty-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

MARS builds directly on the sparsely-gated Mixture-of-Experts framework (Shazeer et al., ICLR 2017), extending its noisy top-K routing mechanism with a discrepancy-aware noise regularization that goes beyond the original load-balancing purpose. Within the incomplete multimodal learning (IML) literature, it departs from imputation-based methods (which hallucinate missing data) and representation-robustness methods (LCR, RFNet, MMANet, DMRNet) that operate solely on the degraded partial representation. It also moves beyond prior expert-based IML approaches (MoMKE, SimMLM, Flex-MoE) that impose structural constraints tying experts to modalities or modality combinations, instead letting a residual signal—computed by contrasting full- and partial-modality features—drive expert specialization without predefined roles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The residual-guided routing offers a principled way to make multimodal systems robust to test-time modality loss without requiring generative imputation or discarding modality-specific cues, and the dual-router design with distillation makes the approach deployable without full-modality access at inference. The discrepancy-aware noise regularization is a practical mechanism for bridging the inevitable train-test routing gap, and the oracle experiment (ACER 0.94) validates that the experts themselves are well-specialized. Open questions include whether the privileged training requirement (full modalities always available during training) limits applicability to settings where training data is also incomplete, and whether the residual signal generalizes to more than three modalities or to streaming/online scenarios where full-modality references are unavailable.
