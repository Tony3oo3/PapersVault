## Problem

Existing DETR-based detectors for small object detection implicitly entangle spatial aggregation with frequency aliasing and truncation during multi-scale fusion and downsampling, causing high-frequency details critical for localizing tiny objects to be progressively attenuated. Dual-branch CNN-ViT backbones (e.g., DEIMv2) fuse heterogeneous representations via simple concatenation without explicit frequency-aware modeling, overlooking the complementary spectral properties of CNNs (high-frequency preserving) and ViTs (low-pass filtering), which limits fine-grained representation for SOD.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB aerial images resized to 800×800 as input and outputs bounding boxes with class labels in a standard end-to-end DETR set-prediction format (no NMS). Evaluation follows the COCO protocol with up to 300 detections per image, reporting AP at IoU thresholds 0.50–0.95 and scale-specific AP_S/AP_M/AP_L. The target scenarios are UAV and satellite imagery where objects are extremely small—on AITODv2 the average object size is only 12.7 pixels with 86% of instances under 16 pixels.

## Method

FSDC-DETR builds on DEIMv2's dual-branch backbone (DINOv3 ViT + HGNetv2 CNN) and introduces three progressively integrated frequency-spatial modules. First, DBFSAF applies Frequency Dynamic Convolution (FDConv) to the concatenated dual-branch features for adaptive frequency diversification, then performs Partial Frequency-Spatial Refinement (PFSR) using a Multi-Kernel Spatial Refine Unit (MKSRU) with dilated depth-wise convolutions and a Frequency Processing Unit (FPU) with channel-attention gating to jointly enhance spatial structure and frequency-sensitive responses. Second, SFS-FF within the hybrid encoder splits channels into spatial (processed via FFT→3×3 conv→IFFT), frequency (processed via Spatial Refine Module), and residual paths, enabling structure-aware cross-scale aggregation that preserves fine-grained details. Third, FSD-Down replaces conventional convolutional downsampling with a learnable 2D discrete wavelet transform parameterized by grouped convolution, adaptively calibrating sub-band responses (LL, LH, HL, HH) via a learnable scaling factor ρ to retain informative high-frequency components during resolution transitions while maintaining spatial consistency.

## Results

On VisDrone-DET2019, FSDC-DETR (40.3M params) achieves 31.1 AP, 52.3 AP50, and 21.0 AP_S, surpassing the strongest DETR-based baseline RT-DETRv4-L (26.4 AP) by 4.7 AP and the base DEIMv2-L (24.7 AP) by 6.4 AP. On AITODv2, it reaches 32.3 AP and 31.3 AP_S, outperforming DEIMv2-L by 6.6 AP and 6.9 AP_S respectively. The small-object gains are particularly pronounced: AP_S improves from 14.2 to 21.0 (+48% relative) on VisDrone-DET2019 and from 24.4 to 31.3 on AITODv2, while also delivering strong medium and large object scores (41.8 AP_M, 58.1 AP_L on VisDrone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSDC-DETR outperforms all compared baselines on both benchmarks across every reported metric. Against the closest DETR-based competitors at similar scale, it surpasses RT-DETRv4-L (31.0M) by 4.7 AP on VisDrone and 5.5 AP on AITODv2, and DEIMv2-L (32.0M) by 6.4 AP and 6.6 AP respectively. The gap widens substantially on small objects: AP_S improves by 6.8 over DEIMv2-L and 4.9 over RT-DETRv4-L on VisDrone, and by 6.9 over DEIMv2-L on AITODv2. Compared to the SOD-specific methods UAV-DETR-R50 (42.0M) and CSFPR-DETR (14.1M), FSDC-DETR leads by 5.5 and 7.8 AP on VisDrone, and by 14.8 and 15.9 AP on AITODv2, indicating that explicit frequency-spatial collaborative modeling provides a substantially larger advantage than prior frequency-aware or UAV-specific designs. Even the largest YOLO variants (56–64M params) trail FSDC-DETR by 8–10 AP on VisDrone and 14–15 AP on AITODv2, underscoring the effectiveness of the DETR-based end-to-end formulation combined with the proposed frequency-preserving modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

FSDC-DETR is a direct architectural extension of DEIMv2 (Huang et al., 2025), which introduced a dual-branch HGNetv2 + DINOv3 backbone for real-time DETR detection. The key departure is that DEIMv2 fuses the two branches via simple channel concatenation and a 1×1 convolution, ignoring their well-documented frequency-domain disparity (ViTs act as low-pass filters; CNNs preserve high-frequency content). FSDC-DETR inserts three purpose-built modules—DBFSAF (drawing on FDConv and SFS-Conv), SFS-FF (using FFT/IFFT for frequency-spatial interaction in the hybrid encoder), and FSD-Down (building on HWD wavelet downsampling)—to explicitly model and preserve high-frequency cues throughout the pipeline. This positions the work at the intersection of frequency-domain learning for dense prediction and DETR-based detection, rather than as a new detection paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~6.4 AP gain over DEIMv2-L (and ~48% relative improvement on AP_S) is substantial for SOD and suggests that the frequency-domain mismatch in dual-branch fusion is a previously under-addressed bottleneck. However, the base model (DEIMv2) is itself very recent (arXiv 2025) and still evolving, so the absolute gains may shift as the underlying architecture matures. Open questions include whether the FFT/DWT operations introduce prohibitive latency for real-time deployment, whether the frequency-spatial collaborative design generalizes beyond aerial and industrial SOD to other small-object domains, and whether the gains are robust across backbone choices beyond the specific HGNetv2+DINOv3 pairing.
