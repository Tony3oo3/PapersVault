## Problem

Existing audio-visual segmentation methods struggle with multi-source entanglement (overlapping sounds prevent isolation of individual emitters) and audio-visual misalignment (small, distant, or off-screen sources lack reliable visual anchors). Prior approaches either use unidirectional audio-to-visual alignment that prevents visual cues from refining audio representations, or rely on KNN-based semantic approximation from a feature bank that loses weaker-source semantics when multiple sources coexist.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261012/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a raw audio waveform (resampled to 16 kHz, center-cropped/padded to fixed duration) and a corresponding video clip, encoded by HTSAT and MiT-B5 respectively. A fixed prototype memory bank of 355 (AVS-Semantic) or 105 (VPOSS) class-specific anchors, pre-built via K-means++ clustering of single-source HTSAT embeddings, is loaded at inference. The output is a pixel-level segmentation mask highlighting all audible regions; during training, an augmented waveform branch (WavAugment: reverberation, pitch shift, compression, volume jitter) feeds only the contrastive module and does not affect the main segmentation path.

## Method

DDAVS comprises three modules: (1) an Audio Query Module that uses a Q-Former to extract n=5 learnable queries from encoded audio, then refines them via cross-attention against a fixed prototype memory bank, anchoring each query to a structured class-aware semantic space; (2) a training-only Contrastive Optimization Module that encodes a WavAugment-perturbed waveform, extracts parallel queries, and applies an InfoNCE-style loss to enlarge inter-query margins under acoustic variation; (3) a delayed bidirectional Audio-Visual Alignment Module that applies dual cross-attention exclusively in transformer blocks 3 and 4 (out of 4), where audio queries first attend to visual tokens for sound-relevant filtering, then the updated audio representations inject discriminative acoustic cues back into the visual stream, completing a symmetric alignment cycle. The delayed placement filters low-level pixel noise while the bidirectional design enables mutual refinement, and the prototype bank provides stable semantic grounding even for unseen or off-screen sources.

## Results

On AVSBench, DDAVS achieves state-of-the-art J&F of 92.4 on AVS-Objects-S4 (single-source), 76.0 on AVS-Objects-MS3 (multi-source), and 52.9 on AVS-Semantic, surpassing the previous best by +1.3, +0.4, and +2.0 J&F respectively. On the VPO benchmark, DDAVS reaches 75.03 J&F on VPO-SS, 76.11 on VPO-MS, and 72.84 on VPO-MSMI, with the largest gains in multi-source (+1.81 over DDESeg) and multi-source multi-instance (+3.54 over RAVS) settings. The model uses 150.29M parameters and 85.72G FLOPs, adding only +2.16G FLOPs over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDAVS outperforms all 24 compared baselines across every AVSBench and VPO subset. On the single-source AVS-Objects-S4, it leads DDESeg (91.1) by +1.3 J&F and ICF (90.1) by +2.3 J&F. The advantage is most pronounced in multi-source and semantic settings: on AVS-Objects-MS3 it beats CCFormer (75.6) by +0.4 J&F, and on AVS-Semantic it surpasses AAVS (50.9) by +2.0 J&F and VCT (50.4) by +2.5 J&F. On VPO, DDAVS gains +1.81 J&F over DDESeg on VPO-MS and +3.54 J&F over RAVS on VPO-MSMI, confirming that the disentangled audio semantics and delayed bidirectional alignment are particularly effective when multiple sound sources coexist or when spatial ambiguity is high. Notably, DDAVS also wins on the relatively easy VPO-SS setting (75.03 vs. RAVS 74.97), indicating consistent rather than scenario-specific improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDAVS extends the query-based audio disentanglement paradigm (Q-Former-style learnable queries) by anchoring extracted audio semantics to a structured prototype memory bank built via K-means++ clustering of single-source embeddings, departing from DDESeg's KNN-based feature bank which the authors argue loses weaker-source semantics under multi-source coexistence. It also generalizes AVESFormer's delayed alignment from unidirectional to bidirectional cross-attention, operating exclusively in the final two Transformer blocks to filter low-level noise while enabling symmetric audio-visual refinement. The contrastive optimization module repurposes InfoNCE-style contrastive learning not for cross-modal alignment (as in CAVP or DiffusionAVS) but for enhancing intra-audio-query discriminability under waveform-level augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DDAVS establishes a clear SOTA on both AVSBench and VPO with particularly strong gains in multi-source and multi-instance settings (+3.8 J&F on AVS-MS3, +4.45 on VPO-MSMI), validating that structured semantic grounding and delayed bidirectional alignment are effective remedies for the dominance bias toward louder or larger sound sources. The out-of-bank robustness experiment (graceful degradation even with all prototypes removed) and the t-SNE evidence of mixture interpolation rather than collapse provide mechanistic insight into why the prototype bank helps. However, validation remains confined to curated benchmark categories with well-defined misalignment ranges; open questions include whether the prototype bank approach generalizes to open-vocabulary or streaming audio-visual settings, and whether the fixed K-means++ bank can be replaced by a learned, adaptive memory for truly novel sound classes.
