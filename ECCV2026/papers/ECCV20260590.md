## Problem

Agentic multimodal LLMs achieve strong reasoning by iteratively invoking visual tools (zoom, crop, OCR), but the resulting strict data dependency across tool-calling steps (agentic depth D) causes linear latency growth and prevents batch-level parallelization. Existing acceleration methods—token-level speculative decoding, visual token pruning, KV-cache compression—operate within the agentic loop and cannot eliminate the sequential tool invocations that dominate end-to-end latency. The paper asks whether the entire tool-use loop can be bypassed for queries that do not genuinely require multi-step perception.

## Input / Output

The system accepts a text query paired with a single RGB image and produces a text answer. Internally, the small speculative model (Qwen3-VL-2B) generates the answer token-by-token while retaining the full vocabulary logit distribution at each position, which feeds the separability gate; the large agentic model (DeepEyes or Thyme) may invoke up to 5 perception tool steps (zoom-in, crop, OCR) before emitting its final answer. All reported latencies include tool execution time, and greedy decoding (temperature 0) is used throughout.

## Method

SpecEyes lifts speculative acceleration from the token level to the agentic level via a four-phase funnel: (1) the large agentic model performs a single-token binary judgment of tool necessity, (2) a stateless small MLLM speculatively answers all tool-free queries in parallel, (3) a cognitive gate based on answer separability S_sep—defined as the margin between the top logit and the mean of the top-K logits, normalized by their standard deviation—decides acceptance or fallback using min-token aggregation as a worst-case guard, and (4) rejected queries fall back to the full sequential agentic loop. The separability metric is scale-invariant and label-free, avoiding softmax miscalibration. A heterogeneous parallel serving architecture exploits the small model's stateless concurrency to mask the large model's stateful serial execution, yielding a throughput speedup of approximately 1/(1−βα), where β is the tool-free screening ratio and α is the gate acceptance rate.

## Results

SpecEyes (min) with the DeepEyes backbone achieves a 1.73× average wall-clock speedup while improving average accuracy from 81.39% to 84.26% across V* Bench, HR-Bench, and POPE. The largest gains appear on POPE (2.13–2.19× speedup with accuracy improvements of up to +6.7 pp on the Adversarial split) and V* Relative Position (82.89% → 89.47% at 1.90×). With the Thyme backbone, SpecEyes (min) yields a 1.42× average speedup and raises accuracy from 82.29% to 83.99%. HR-Bench 8K is the hardest regime, where speedup drops to ~1.0× because high-resolution queries suppress both the screening ratio β and the gate acceptance rate α.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the full agentic baselines (DeepEyes w/ tools, Thyme w/ tools), SpecEyes (min) matches or exceeds accuracy on every split while delivering 1.4–2.2× speedup; the most notable accuracy gains are on POPE Adversarial (+6.7 pp with DeepEyes, +4.6 pp with Thyme) and V* Relative Position (+6.6 pp with DeepEyes), suggesting that bypassing unnecessary tool trajectories reduces hallucination errors. Compared to the tools-disabled ablation (DeepEyes/Thyme w/o tools), SpecEyes sacrifices some raw speedup (1.73× vs 3.53×) but avoids the catastrophic accuracy collapse on POPE (85.13% vs 46.90% on Adversarial with DeepEyes), confirming that the cognitive gate correctly routes tool-required queries back to the agentic loop. SpecReason [42], the closest prior speculative-reasoning method, consistently *decelerates* inference (0.37–0.61× with DeepEyes, 0.43–0.53× with Thyme) due to extra draft/verification turns and token overhead, while also degrading POPE accuracy to as low as 49.10%; SpecEyes outperforms it on both speed and accuracy across all seven splits. The Qwen3-VL-2B draft-only upper bound (4.13× avg speedup, 78.93% avg accuracy) shows the theoretical ceiling; SpecEyes captures most of the latency saving while preserving full agentic-level accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpecEyes lifts the speculative decoding paradigm from the token level (Leviathan et al., ICML 2023) to the agentic level, where the unit of speculation is an entire tool-use trajectory rather than a sequence of tokens. It departs from prior efficient-reasoning work (SpecReason, RelayLLM) that accelerates individual steps within a fixed agentic loop, and from efficient-perception methods (token pruning, KV-cache compression) that reduce per-step compute, by instead questioning whether the loop is necessary at all for a given query. The cognitive gating mechanism via answer separability among top-K logits is a novel calibration-free confidence signal, distinct from the softmax-based geometric-mean confidence used in prior speculative decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As agentic MLLMs (OpenAI o3, Gemini Agentic Vision) become the dominant paradigm for visual reasoning, the sequential tool-calling bottleneck becomes a critical deployment barrier; SpecEyes offers a clean, training-free acceleration that preserves accuracy while converting per-query latency savings into multiplicative throughput gains. The finding that bypassing unnecessary tool trajectories can improve hallucination robustness (POPE Adversarial: 78.43% → 85.13%) suggests that forced tool use may introduce spurious visual evidence, a counterintuitive insight worth further study. The main limitation is that speculation is restricted to agentic depth D=0 (fully tool-free), so benchmarks where tools are genuinely required (HR-Bench 8K) see near-zero speedup; multi-depth speculation (D=1,2,…) is the natural next step but remains open.
