## Problem

Zero-shot subject-driven video generation (SDV-Gen) currently demands either per-subject tuning (~200 A100 GPU-hours per identity) or large-scale subject-video pair datasets with 10K–200K A100 GPU-hours of fine-tuning, making the field inaccessible. The paper asks whether a single model can be adapted to generate videos of unseen subjects at test time using only subject-image pairs and a tiny set of arbitrary videos, without any subject-video supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261928/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the model receives one reference image of the target subject and a free-form text prompt, and directly outputs a short video (13 latent frames for CogVideoX) showing that subject in a new scene with varied motion and context—no per-subject tuning or additional reference frames are needed. The reference image is VAE-encoded and prepended as conditioning tokens alongside T5-encoded text, while the video is generated by the pretrained diffusion transformer backbone in a single forward pass.

## Method

The key idea is to decompose SDV-Gen into two complementary objectives—identity injection from subject-image pairs and motion-awareness preservation from a small pool of arbitrary videos—and optimize them via stochastic task switching (80% image steps, 20% video steps) rather than joint or sequential training. LoRA adapters are applied to attention and normalization layers that interact with reference-image tokens, while the rest of the DiT backbone stays frozen; a special <CLS> token anchors identity in the text stream. To prevent the degenerate solution of copying the reference frame across time, the video objective uses random reference-frame sampling and image-token dropout. Gradient analysis over training checkpoints shows the two task gradients rapidly converge to near-orthogonal directions in the trainable parameter subspace, explaining why simple stochastic switching remains stable without explicit gradient surgery. The recipe is demonstrated on CogVideoX-5B, Wan 2.1-1.3B, and Wan 2.2-5B, each fine-tuned for 288 A100-hours on 200K subject-image pairs and 4,000 Pexels videos—roughly 1% of the compute used by prior zero-shot baselines.

## Results

On VBench (Table 2), the CogVideoX-5B variant achieves the best DINO-I subject fidelity (61.23) and second-best Dynamic Degree (76.25) among all zero-shot SDV-Gen methods, while the Wan 2.2-5B variant attains the highest Dynamic Degree (82.50) and best CLIP-I (76.97). On OpenS2V-Eval v1.1 (Table 3), the Wan 2.2-5B variant scores a Total of 56.84 with only 288 A100-hours of training, closely matching Phantom-14B (57.02, ~30K hrs) and outperforming commercial models Kling 1.6 (56.67), Pika 2.1 (53.12), and Vidu 2.0 (52.90). The Wan 2.1-1.3B variant surpasses VACE-1.3B in Total score (51.45 vs 51.13) despite using ~240× less training compute (288 hrs vs ~70K hrs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest zero-shot SDV-Gen baselines (Phantom-1.3B and VACE-1.3B, both on Wan 2.1-1.3B), the CogVideoX-5B variant wins decisively on Dynamic Degree (76.25 vs 67.08 and 43.75), CLIP-I (76.19 vs 73.00 and 73.54), and DINO-I (61.23 vs 53.55 and 54.47), while Phantom and VACE retain a small edge on Motion Smoothness (~0.5 pts) and CLIP-T (~0.05–0.1 pts). Compared to MAGREF-480P, Ours (CogVideoX-5B) leads on Dynamic Degree (+11.2), CLIP-I (+5.0), and DINO-I (+12.7), with MAGREF slightly ahead on Motion Smoothness (98.86 vs 98.23). Against the SDI-Gen→I2V pipelines (OminiControl, BLIP, IP-Adapter), the method achieves substantially higher Dynamic Degree (76.25 vs 47.9–51.9) at the cost of slightly lower CLIP-I than BLIP (76.19 vs 79.27), indicating a better fidelity–motion trade-off. On OpenS2V-Eval, the Wan 2.2-5B variant (Total 56.84, 288 hrs) matches Phantom-14B (57.02, ~30K hrs) and beats Kling 1.6 (56.67) and Pika 2.1 (53.12), though it trails VACE-14B (61.75, ~210K hrs) primarily on FaceSim (48.78 vs 64.65) and Amplification (14.38 vs 19.69). Qualitatively, per-subject tuning methods (CustomCrafter, Still-Moving) show identity drift and detail loss under motion, whereas the zero-shot method maintains sharper subject textures and more consistent identity across frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on OminiControl's subject-image conditioning formulation (MM-DiT with reference-image tokens) for the identity injection objective, but departs fundamentally from prior zero-shot SDV-Gen methods (VACE, Phantom) that assume both identity and motion must be jointly supervised by subject-video pairs. The key conceptual shift is decomposing SDV-Gen into two separable subtasks—identity from subject-image pairs and motion preservation from a small pool of arbitrary videos—connected by stochastic task switching rather than a single joint loss. The gradient orthogonality analysis positions the work within multi-task learning, showing that the simpler stochastic switching scheme achieves what Gradient Surgery would enforce via explicit projection, but at roughly half the per-step gradient cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1% compute reduction (288 A100-hours vs. 10K–210K for prior zero-shot baselines) dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for subject-driven video generation, making the task accessible to smaller research groups and practitioners. The gradient orthogonality finding provides a useful mechanistic explanation for why the decomposition works, though the theoretical analysis is explicitly local and second-order, not a formal guarantee for the non-convex DiT landscape. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-subject or long-horizon video generation, whether 4,000 arbitrary videos suffice for highly diverse motion distributions, and whether the object-centric training data (Subject-200K) limits face-centric applications despite reasonable generalization observed.
