## Problem

Joint audio-visual diffusion models (e.g., LTX-2, Veo 3) achieve high-fidelity synchronized generation but rely on bidirectional full-sequence attention, incurring ~197 s latency for a 5-second clip and precluding real-time streaming. Naively porting causal distillation techniques from video-only models to dual-stream architectures triggers severe training instability—Softmax collapse and gradient explosions—due to the extreme 25:3 temporal frequency asymmetry between audio and video latents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261994/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a single text prompt, OmniForcing produces temporally synchronized streaming video (480p, 3 latent FPS) and audio (25 latent FPS) in 1-second macroblocks. The first chunk (Global Prefix + Block 1) is emitted in ~0.7 s (TTFC), after which subsequent blocks stream at ~25 FPS on a single H200 GPU. Video decoding uses a sliding-window VAE (W=2 past blocks, 288 ms/block) while audio uses lossless cumulative decoding (≤14 ms/block), both running concurrently.

## Method

OmniForcing distills the 19B bidirectional LTX-2 dual-stream DiT into a block-causal autoregressive generator via a three-stage pipeline: (I) bidirectional DMD for few-step denoising, (II) causal ODE regression to adapt weights to an Asymmetric Block-Causal Mask (1-second macroblocks of 3 video latents × 384 spatial tokens vs. 25 audio tokens, with a zero-truncation Global Prefix), and (III) joint Self-Forcing DMD where the model autoregressively unrolls its own KV-cache to correct cross-modal exposure bias. To stabilize Stage II against Softmax collapse from extreme audio token sparsity, the method introduces S=16 learnable Audio Sink Tokens anchored in the Global Prefix with an Identity RoPE constraint (cos θ=1, sin θ=0), acting as a position-agnostic global memory buffer that expands the attention denominator and quenches gradient explosions. At inference, a Modality-Independent Rolling KV-Cache decouples the 14B video and 5B audio streams into independent FFN sub-layers with only brief cross-modal attention synchronization, reducing per-step context to O(L) and enabling asymmetric parallel execution on a single GPU.

## Results

On JavisBench, OmniForcing achieves FVD 137.2 and FAD 5.7, ranking second overall behind only the bidirectional LTX-2 teacher (FVD 125.4, FAD 4.6) and surpassing the strongest non-teacher baseline JavisDiT++ (FVD 141.5, FAD 5.5). It attains the best CLIP text-consistency score (0.322) across all methods, including the teacher (0.318), and a DeSync of 0.392 closely tracking the teacher's 0.384 while dramatically outperforming JavisDiT++ (0.832). Critically, OmniForcing generates a 5-second 480p audio-visual clip in ~5.7 s wall-clock time with a TTFC of ~0.7 s and sustained ~25 FPS on a single H200 GPU—a ~35× speedup over the 197 s offline teacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two T2A+A2V cascaded baselines (TempoTokens, TPoS), OmniForcing dominates on FVD (137.2 vs. 539.8 and 839.7) and all cross-modal consistency metrics, confirming that decoupled pipelines cannot match joint modeling. Against the four T2V+V2A cascaded baselines (ReWaS, See&Hear, FoleyCrafter, MMAudio), OmniForcing wins on FAD (5.7 vs. 6.1–9.4), AV-IB (0.269 vs. 0.110–0.198), and DeSync (0.392 vs. 0.849–1.099), with the largest gap on temporal synchronization. Among joint T2AV models, OmniForcing outperforms MM-Diffusion, JavisDiT, and UniVerse-1 on every reported metric; against the strongest joint baseline JavisDiT++, it wins on FVD (137.2 vs. 141.5), AV-IB (0.269 vs. 0.198), and DeSync (0.392 vs. 0.832) but trails slightly on FAD (5.7 vs. 5.5), TA-IB (0.162 vs. 0.164), and CLAP (0.401 vs. 0.424). Relative to the LTX-2 teacher, OmniForcing sacrifices modest quality (FVD +11.8, FAD +1.1, DeSync +0.008) but achieves a ~35× runtime reduction (5.7 s vs. 197 s) and uniquely enables real-time streaming at ~25 FPS with 0.7 s TTFC; it also edges out the teacher on CLIP text-consistency (0.322 vs. 0.318).

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniForcing extends the causal distillation paradigm established by CausVid and Self-Forcing from unimodal video to dual-stream joint audio-visual generation, using LTX-2 (a 14B+5B bidirectional DiT) as the teacher. The critical departure is that naively porting video-only causal masking to the asymmetric 3 FPS / 25 FPS dual-stream setting triggers Softmax collapse and gradient explosion in the sparse audio stream, necessitating new architectural stabilizers (Audio Sink Tokens with Identity RoPE) and a physical-time-based Asymmetric Block-Causal Alignment that have no analogue in prior video-only work. The three-stage pipeline (Bidirectional DMD → Causal ODE Regression → Joint Self-Forcing DMD) composes existing distillation objectives in a novel multi-modal sequence, and the Modality-Independent Rolling KV-Cache exploits the intra-layer decoupling between the two streams for parallel inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a heavy bidirectional joint audio-visual foundation model can be distilled into a real-time streaming engine (~25 FPS, 0.7 s TTFC) on a single GPU, a 35× speedup that opens the door to interactive and latency-sensitive multi-modal applications (live content, AR/VR, real-time dubbing). The architectural insights—particularly that extreme cross-modal token density mismatch requires position-agnostic sink tokens rather than standard normalization tricks—are likely transferable to other asymmetric multi-modal streaming settings. Limitations include modest quality gaps versus the teacher in cross-modal consistency and synchrony (attributed to the restricted causal receptive field), dependence on a specific teacher architecture, and evaluation on a single benchmark (JavisBench) with limited long-sequence testing. Open questions include whether the asymmetric block-causal design generalizes to more than two modalities or to higher resolutions where the compute asymmetry between streams becomes even more extreme.
