## Problem

Online handwriting generation produces executable stroke trajectories but lacks fine-grained visual texture, while offline methods synthesize realistic images but discard temporal stroke order. Unifying the two paradigms is blocked by the absence of an explicit, differentiable physical model linking stroke kinematics to pixel-level brush appearance and by the lack of paired trajectory–image training data.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a set of style reference images and a target text string (up to 9 characters) as input. It produces three outputs: an online stroke sequence of 2D pen positions with one-hot pen states (pen-down/pen-up/end-of-stroke, truncated to 250 points), a rendered handwriting image at 64 px height, and an optional diffusion-refined offline image. A key detail is that pen pressure is not measured but derived as a velocity-based proxy (inverse relationship between speed and pressure), and the six brush parameters (base width, spread sensitivity, ink density, boundary falloff, and pressure bounds) are predicted from the style images rather than specified by the user.

## Method

The core contribution is a compact six-parameter physical brush model and a fully differentiable renderer R that maps a stroke trajectory and brush parameters to a pixel-level image via a velocity-to-pressure proxy, pressure-dependent stroke width, a distance-based footprint kernel with tunable sharpness, exponential ink opacity, and max-composition blending. A transformer-based stroke generator G (extended from the single-character SDT architecture to word-level via character-sequence cross-attention) predicts the trajectory autoregressively with a 20-component Gaussian mixture per step, while a DINOv3-based brush parameter observer O with intra- and inter-image attention predicts the six rendering parameters from style references. Because no paired trajectory–image dataset exists, the authors synthesize one by rendering 155,840 word-level trajectories from IAM-OnDB and CASIA-OLHWDB with randomly sampled brush parameters and real background textures. Finally, an off-the-shelf handwriting diffusion model is used zero-shot as an image enhancer: the rendered image is VAE-encoded, noised at a tuned intermediate timestep, and denoised conditioned on the text and style images to recover realistic texture without retraining the diffusion backbone.

## Results

On the online handwriting task (Table 2), the proposed stroke generator achieves a DTW of 0.2936 (multi-letter) and 0.3462 (single-letter), substantially outperforming SDT (0.8155 and 0.6056 respectively). On offline handwriting generation (Table 4), One-DM+Ours achieves FID 25.89 and BFID 8.15 on IAM Words, and FID 14.45 and BFID 10.47 on CVL Words, improving over the One-DM backbone alone (FID 29.00/19.45, BFID 16.35/15.18). The differentiable renderer alone (Table 3) achieves FID 11.81 and BFID 11.03 on the synthetic paired dataset, far surpassing all offline baselines (best: One-DM at FID 52.26). A user study confirms perceptual gains, with One-DM+Ours obtaining an average rank of 2.840 versus 3.406 for One-DM alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDT [10] on the online trajectory task, the proposed stroke generator reduces DTW by more than 60% in the multi-letter setting (0.2936 vs. 0.8155) and by ~43% in the single-letter setting (0.3462 vs. 0.6056), demonstrating that extending the architecture from single-character to word-level generation yields substantial gains. Among offline baselines, One-DM [9] is the strongest standalone model (FID 29.00 on IAM, 19.45 on CVL), but attaching the proposed renderer as a structural prior (One-DM+Ours) improves FID to 25.89/14.45 and BFID to 8.15/10.47, while also reducing HWD and LPIPS. DiffPen [22] performs poorly on realism metrics (BFID 151.96 on IAM) and the renderer-guided variant (DiffPen+Ours) improves FID from 77.70 to 76.86 on IAM but still lags One-DM+Ours by a wide margin. Emuru [25] is the weakest baseline across nearly all metrics (FID 89.89 on IAM, CER 3.0583), confirming that autoregressive approaches struggle with multi-character word generation. The paper also shows that standard offline models fail on the synthetic paired dataset (Table 3), where the renderer alone achieves FID 11.81 versus 52.26 for the best offline baseline (One-DM), highlighting that pixel-level models cannot exploit trajectory structure without an explicit physical rendering bridge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously independent lines of work—online trajectory-based handwriting generation (SDT, Deepwriting, CoSE) and offline image-based generation (One-DM, DiffPen, Emuru)—by introducing a differentiable six-parameter brush model inspired by classical computer graphics brush simulators (Hairy Brushes, Chu & Tai). It extends SDT's single-character transformer to word-level stroke prediction and repurposes pretrained diffusion models as zero-shot appearance enhancers, while the brush parameter observer leverages DINOv3 features to extract physically interpretable style parameters. The key conceptual departure is treating the stroke-to-image mapping as a differentiable physical process rather than an end-to-end learned function, which enables gradient flow across the motion and appearance domains and synthetic paired-data construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled bridge between executable trajectories and realistic images, directly enabling robotic calligraphy without additional post-processing—a practical capability absent from purely image-based or purely trajectory-based methods. The synthetic paired-data pipeline (rendering online datasets into offline images) alleviates the scarcity of aligned trajectory-image pairs, broadening the applicability of existing trajectory-only corpora. Open questions include extending from word-level to coherent long-form sentence generation, calibrating surrogate brush parameters to true physical units for cross-tool transfer, and whether the max-composition rendering strategy generalizes to overlapping multi-stroke scenes with varying ink wetness.
