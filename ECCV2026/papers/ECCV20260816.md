## Problem

Native fMRI data from diverse sources exhibit substantial heterogeneity in spatial resolution (2–4 mm) and temporal sampling (720–3000 ms), yet mainstream deep learning pipelines enforce uniformity by warping all subjects into a standard template space—a process that takes hours per subject, introduces interpolation and partial-volume artifacts, and erases subject-specific anatomical variability. This makes scalable, parameter-sharing foundation modeling on voxel-level fMRI impractical, as existing native-space approaches rely on subject-specific projection networks that cannot generalize across heterogeneous cohorts.

## Input / Output

The model ingests native 4D fMRI volumes (3D spatial × time) directly in each subject's original scanner space, with no resampling or template normalization; the NIfTI affine header supplies the subject-specific voxel-to-world transformation needed for physical-unit tokenization and positional encoding. After discarding ~80% background patches, each subject yields roughly 4,000 spatiotemporal tokens. Downstream, a learnable class token is prepended and processed by a lightweight transformer head to produce diagnosis logits (binary or multi-class), while the Mamba backbone simultaneously provides voxel-level latent feature representations for transfer or probing.

## Method

FlexiBrain defines patch sizes in real-world physical units (e.g., 12 mm spatial, 6 s temporal) rather than fixed voxel counts, and continuously resizes shared base convolution kernels to the target voxel dimensions via a Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse projection (inherited from FlexiViT), enabling seamless parameter sharing across resolutions without retraining. A continuous physical 3D positional encoding maps each patch's center to world coordinates using the NIfTI affine matrix and encodes them with log-spaced sinusoidal embeddings, preserving anatomical awareness across irregular grids after background removal. The backbone replaces quadratic self-attention with a linear-complexity Mamba (Selective SSM) to handle ~4,000-token sequences efficiently, and adopts a JEPA latent-predictive objective—masking visible tokens, predicting their EMA-targeted latent representations with a shallow Mamba predictor—rather than noisy voxel-wise reconstruction. A lightweight Mixture-of-Experts module appended to the context encoder introduces structural asymmetry that prevents the trivial constant-mapping collapse inherent to JEPA, while the overall training is self-supervised on mixed-resolution data (ABIDE, ADNI, ADHD-200) followed by fine-tuning on downstream diagnosis tasks.

## Results

FlexiBrain achieves the best or second-best accuracy on 4 out of 5 downstream diagnosis tasks, with the largest gain of 12.49 percentage points on ADNI(MCI) (79.16% vs. 66.67% for NeuroSTORM). On the external PPMI dataset (leave-one-dataset-out), FlexiBrain reaches 74.31% ACC / 70.54% F1, outperforming the next-best baseline (NeuroSTORM, 68.25% ACC) by over 6 points. The only task where FlexiBrain does not lead is ADNI(AD), where NeuroSTORM achieves 84.26% ACC versus FlexiBrain's 82.34%. Across all tasks, FlexiBrain operates directly on native-space fMRI without template normalization, reducing per-subject preprocessing from ~5 hours to ~3 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexiBrain outperforms all seven baselines on ADHD-200 (69.76% ACC vs. SwiFT's 62.50%, the next best), ABIDE (65.45% vs. BrainMass's 63.33%), ADNI-MCI (79.16% vs. NeuroSTORM's 66.67%, a 12.5-point margin), and PPMI (74.31% vs. NeuroSTORM's 68.25%). The sole exception is ADNI-AD, where NeuroSTORM leads with 84.26% ACC versus FlexiBrain's 82.34% (a ~2-point gap), though FlexiBrain's F1 (79.41%) remains competitive with NeuroSTORM's 83.91%. Among the large-scale pretraining baselines (BrainMass, Brain-JEPA, BrainHarmonix-F, NeuroSTORM), FlexiBrain consistently surpasses them on 4 of 5 tasks, with the most dramatic advantage on ADNI-MCI where the gap exceeds 12 points. Notably, FlexiBrain achieves these results without template normalization or external data augmentation, whereas most baselines require full nonlinear registration to MNI space.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexiBrain extends the continuous kernel-resizing mechanism from FlexiViT (Beyer et al., CVPR 2023) to the 4D fMRI domain, redefining patch sizes in physical units (mm, s) rather than fixed voxel counts to achieve resolution-agnostic parameter sharing. It departs from the dominant template-space paradigm (MNI normalization) that underpins most voxel-level fMRI models (SwiFT, Brain-JEPA, NeuroSTORM) by operating directly in native space, and from prior native-space approaches (MindEye2) that relied on subject-specific projection networks by enabling a single shared tokenizer across heterogeneous resolutions. The backbone combines two established ideas—Mamba's linear-complexity state-space modeling and JEPA's latent predictive objective—with a lightweight MoE module to prevent representation collapse, a combination not previously applied to fMRI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the hours-long nonlinear template normalization step and replacing it with a 3-minute minimal preprocessing, FlexiBrain removes a major practical and scientific bottleneck for scaling fMRI foundation models to tens of thousands of subjects. The 12-percentage-point gains across five diagnosis tasks, validated on an external dataset (PPMI) and supported by ablations isolating each component, suggest that preserving subject-specific anatomy in native space is not merely a convenience but a source of genuinely richer signal. Open questions remain: scanner-center domain shift is not resolved by resolution-adaptive tokenization alone, the framework still assumes motion-corrected (not raw) data, and whether the physical-unit tokenization strategy transfers to other 4D medical imaging modalities (e.g., PET, ultrasound) is untested.
