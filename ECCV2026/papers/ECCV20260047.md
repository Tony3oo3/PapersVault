## Problem

Single-image reflection removal is fundamentally ill-posed, and existing multi-view approaches either require specialized hardware (polarization, NIR, flash pairs) or complex multi-frame sequences with fragile explicit optical flow that fails under severe reflections. No practical method exploits the ubiquitous dual-camera systems on modern smartphones, which can capture image pairs seamlessly with a single shutter press, to provide the geometric cues needed for robust layer separation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260047/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of mixture images (M₁, M₂) from two viewpoints and their corresponding camera poses (3D translation + unit quaternion rotation per view). Images are encoded into latent space via a frozen VAE, and the relative pose is computed as a 7-dimensional vector (unit-normalized translation direction + relative quaternion). The U-Net receives a 16-channel input formed by channel-wise concatenation of noisy transmission latents and clean mixture condition latents from both views, and simultaneously denoises both views to produce clean transmission layers T₁ and T₂. Training operates at 768×768 resolution.

## Method

Built on Stable Diffusion v2.1 with a frozen VAE and modified U-Net, the framework introduces two key mechanisms. First, a frame-aware spatial attention applied at the 16×16 and 32×32 upsampling layers enforces a directed attention pattern: noisy transmission queries attend exclusively to clean mixture key/value pairs from both views, preventing cross-view noise leakage while reusing pretrained projection matrices without architectural changes. Second, the relative camera pose is encoded through a lightweight 2-layer MLP and fused with the diffusion timestep embedding as a soft conditional signal (scaled by γ=0.15), providing geometric guidance that disentangles depth-induced parallax without brittle 3D constraints. Training uses a two-stage curriculum: Stage 1 establishes core decomposition on moderate reflections with stochastic view-replacement and pose-dropout augmentations; Stage 2 fine-tunes on strong reflections (α_r ~ N(0.8, 0.2)) with additional degradations like motion blur and chromatic aberration, optimizing the standard DDPM loss with conditional dropout masks.

## Results

On the real-world DUALREF-V dataset, the method achieves 24.17 PSNR, 0.7591 SSIM, and 0.1638 LPIPS, surpassing the best single-image baseline DAI (21.57 PSNR) by 2.6 dB (~12% relative improvement). On the semi-synthetic DUALREF-R test set, it reaches 28.19 PSNR and 0.0983 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline WSeat (27.11 PSNR). On the Niklaus dual-view dataset, the method attains 25.09 PSNR and 0.0865 LPIPS, exceeding the best baseline WSeat (23.72 PSNR) by 1.4 dB. The method also achieves the best no-reference quality scores (CLIPIQA 0.4705, MUSIQ 64.81) on DUALREF-V.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all eight baselines across all three evaluation datasets on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and MUSIQ. Among single-image methods, DAI (21.57 PSNR on DUALREF-V) and WSeat (27.11 PSNR on DUALREF-R) are the strongest, yet the dual-view approach exceeds them by 2.6 dB and 1.1 dB respectively, confirming that cross-view parallax provides substantial disambiguation beyond what any single-image prior can achieve. The flow-based dual-view method Niklaus* (18.36 PSNR on DUALREF-V) and the multi-frame TF15 (18.65 PSNR) are notably weaker, as explicit optical flow degrades under severe reflection interference. On the Niklaus dataset, RDNet achieves the best CLIPIQA (0.5989) among baselines, but the proposed method still edges it out (0.5937 vs. 0.5989 is actually a loss on CLIPIQA for RDNet; the method wins on all other metrics including PSNR 25.09 vs. 23.27). Against the burst-photography method of Chugunov et al. [9], the proposed approach achieves comparable or superior visual quality while running 60× faster (3 s vs. 3 min per scene), since it avoids per-scene optimization. Fine-tuned single-image variants (ZF24*, DSIT*) close the gap somewhat but remain 3–4 dB behind on real-world data, indicating that the dual-view signal is not easily absorbed by single-view architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Stable Diffusion v2.1 latent diffusion architecture, repurposing its pre-trained U-Net and VAE for a conditional restoration task rather than generation. It departs from single-image reflection removal (SIRR) methods that are fundamentally ill-posed, and from multi-view approaches that rely on explicit optical flow (RAFT) or 3D reconstruction—both of which are fragile under severe reflection interference. The key architectural departure is the frame-aware spatial attention mechanism, which enforces a directed attention pattern (noisy transmission queries attend only to clean mixture conditions) to prevent cross-view noise leakage while preserving pre-trained generative priors, and the use of camera pose as a soft conditional embedding rather than a hard geometric constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DUALREF dataset is a significant contribution, providing the first comprehensive dual-view reflection removal benchmark with trajectory-aligned real-world ground truth—previously unavailable and a major bottleneck for the field. The finding that dual-view captures most of the multi-view quality gain (with diminishing returns beyond two views) validates a practical design principle for mobile photography where dual cameras are ubiquitous. However, the approach fundamentally relies on effective parallax; in near-zero-displacement scenarios it degrades to single-image performance, and color drift persists in challenging cases. Open questions include whether the directed attention pattern generalizes to other multi-view restoration tasks and whether the pose encoding can be replaced by learned implicit geometry.
