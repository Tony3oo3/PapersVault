## Problem

Token-level consistency constraint methods for multi-object T2I diffusion (e.g., TokenCompose) rely on binary segmentation maps from SAM to supervise per-noun cross-attention maps, but suffer from two compounding issues: (1) segmentation map bias, where unreliable SAM masks cause the model to overfit spurious regions, and (2) semantic overlap conflict, where independently optimizing each noun's attention map produces contradictory gradients when objects spatially overlap. The paper asks how to make object-wise consistency constraint robust to noisy supervision and to inter-object semantic competition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260951/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, ELDiff takes only a free-form text prompt and produces a generated image with no additional masks, layout inputs, or inference-time manipulations. During training, it additionally consumes pre-computed binary segmentation maps (generated once offline by Grounded-SAM on 4,526 COCO/Visual Spatial Reasoning image-caption pairs) as soft supervision signals for the evidential losses. The method is a fine-tuning strategy that can be plugged into any existing T2I diffusion backbone (SD v1.4, v2.1, SDXL, v3.5, Qwen-Image) without architectural changes.

## Method

ELDiff adds two novel losses to the standard denoising objective (L_LDM). The pixel evidence loss reinterprets each noun token's cross-attention map as evidence in a Dirichlet distribution via a small MLP evidence network: high uncertainty (loose Dirichlet) in mislabeled regions reduces the cross-entropy penalty, preventing overfitting to biased segmentation maps, while a KL-divergence term suppresses evidence for incorrect classes. The token conflict loss treats per-noun cross-attention maps as separate evidence sources and applies the Dempster-Shafer combination rule to compute a conflict coefficient in overlapping mask regions; an intra-object consistency term pulls each noun's attention mass into its target region, and an inter-object conflict term (gated by a sigmoid threshold) penalizes co-activation in shared pixels. The total objective L_LDM + L_PixEvi + L_TokCon is optimized end-to-end for ~24k steps, and at test time the model requires no extra computation beyond standard diffusion sampling.

## Results

On SD v1.4, ELDiff achieves the best Object Accuracy (OA) of 0.5563, surpassing the closest competitor TokenCompose (0.5215) by 0.0348, and improves MG4 and MG5 scores to 0.3305 and 0.0931 respectively (vs. 0.2881 and 0.0328 for TokenCompose). For photorealism, ELDiff attains the lowest FID(C) of 19.25 and highest CLIP(C) of 0.3276, outperforming all baselines. Crucially, ELDiff requires no additional inference-time manipulation, maintaining the same 7.54 s latency as the unmodified SD v1.4 baseline. The gains generalize across backbones: on SD v2.1, OA improves to 0.7116 (vs. 0.6010 for TokenCompose), and on Qwen-Image, the GenEval overall score rises from 0.87 to 0.90.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELDiff outperforms all 16 compared baselines across every evaluated metric and backbone. The closest competitor is TokenCompose [61], the direct predecessor that ELDiff extends: on SD v1.4, ELDiff improves OA by +0.0348, MG4 by +0.0424, MG5 by +0.0603, FID(C) by −1.87, and CLIP(C) by +0.0164, while matching its inference latency. Among training-free methods on T2I-CompBench (SDXL), ELDiff beats the second-best (SynGen [52]) by +0.0504 on Color, +0.0414 on Shape, and +0.0316 on Texture, and surpasses R2F [46] and InitNO [17] on all attribute-binding and spatial-relationship scores while maintaining the lowest latency (8.07 s vs. 10.14–19.42 s). On the stronger SD v2.1 backbone, ELDiff's OA of 0.7116 exceeds IterComp [67] (0.6341) and TokenCompose (0.6010) by +0.0775 and +0.1106 respectively. On SD v3.5, ELDiff achieves OA 0.8644 vs. TokenCompose's 0.8233 and IterComp's 0.8208. On Qwen-Image, ELDiff raises GenEval overall from 0.87 (base) / 0.88 (TokenCompose) to 0.90, and improves GenEval 2 Soft-TIFA_AM by +1.55 and Soft-TIFA_GM by +3.37 over the base model. Notably, ELDiff matches or beats the unmodified SD v1.4 baseline on latency (7.54 s) while all inference-time methods (Attend-Excite, Layout, Self-Cross, InitNO) incur 1.5×–3.4× higher latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token-level consistency constraint paradigm introduced by TokenCompose, which uses Grounding DINO and SAM segmentation maps to supervise per-noun cross-attention maps during diffusion fine-tuning. It departs from that line by identifying two failure modes—segmentation map bias and semantic overlap conflict—and addressing them through Evidential Deep Learning (Dirichlet-based pixel uncertainty) and Dempster-Shafer Theory (evidence combination for conflict quantification), both of which were previously applied to classification and segmentation but not to T2I diffusion training. The method is positioned as a drop-in replacement for the standard cross-entropy supervision in TokenCompose-style pipelines, requiring no architectural changes or inference-time modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for reliable segmentation maps at test time and adding no inference overhead, ELDiff offers a practical path to improving compositional generation in production diffusion pipelines. The consistent gains across five backbones (SD v1.4 through Qwen-Image) suggest the evidential regularization is a general-purpose fix rather than a backbone-specific hack. However, the authors acknowledge a persistent weakness in attribute binding (color, shape, texture), and the paper leaves open whether the DST-based conflict mechanism can be extended to finer-grained attribute-level constraints or to video/3D generation settings where spatial overlap is more complex.
