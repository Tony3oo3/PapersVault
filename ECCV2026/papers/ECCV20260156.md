## Problem

CLIP-style visual encoders are pretrained almost exclusively on descriptive image-text alignment, leaving their capacity for visually grounded commonsense and compositional reasoning unexplored. It remains unclear whether such reasoning can be injected into CLIP's representation space through pretraining alone, without architectural modifications, while preserving the original descriptive alignment that makes these encoders effective for retrieval.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260156/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images at resolutions of 224, 336, or 384 pixels (depending on backbone scale) paired with text prompts that are either descriptive captions or visually grounded reasoning statements. During training, Stage 1 jointly supervises on both descriptive and reasoning captions with a time-varying mixture weight, while Stage 2 additionally attaches lightweight MLP classification heads (discarded at inference) that predict multi-label reasoning categories from image features and single-label categories from text features. At inference, the output is a standard CLIP-style visual embedding and text embedding in a shared space, directly usable as a drop-in encoder in MLLMs like LLaVA-NeXT.

## Method

ReasonCLIP-58M is a two-stage continual pretraining framework that progressively injects visually grounded reasoning into CLIP/SigLIP backbones without modifying their architecture. Stage 1 (Reasoning-Aware Alignment) trains on ReasonLite-42M using a dual-supervision objective that blends descriptive and reasoning caption alignment losses with a linearly decaying weight λ(t), plus an L2 regularization term anchoring parameters to their initial values to prevent representational drift. Stage 2 (Explicit Reasoning Supervision) trains on ReasonPro-16M, dropping descriptive captions and instead aligning images with category-specific reasoning captions across five predefined reasoning types (Spatial/Geometric, Attribute/State, Creature/Action, Temporal/Phase, Intuitive Physics), augmented by a category-discrimination branch imposing multi-label BCE on image features and single-label CE on text features to enforce separability of reasoning patterns. The 58.9M training pairs are constructed from CC12M by first regenerating high-quality descriptive captions with Qwen2.5-VL-72B, then extracting visual evidence and generating grounded reasoning statements with Qwen2.5-VL-72B (Stage 1) and Qwen3-VL-32B (Stage 2), with multi-label category annotation filtering to retain only images supporting at least three reasoning types.

## Results

On zero-shot retrieval, ReasonCLIP-L/14-336 (Stage 1) achieves 65.1 R@1 on COCO-5K I→T (vs. 50.0 for base CLIP) and 67.2 R@1 on RCLIP-V3-5K I→T (vs. 55.9), while ReasonSigLIP-So/14 (Stage 2) reaches 73.7 R@1 on COCO and 72.4 R@1 on RCLIP-V3. On the proposed RCLIP-Bench, ReasonCLIP-L/14-336 improves V1 (Visual Grounding) from 20.3 to 27.2 and V3 (Structured Reasoning) from 20.3 to 25.4 over base CLIP. For compositional reasoning, ReasonCLIP yields average gains of +5.6 to +7.7 points across WhatsUp, VALSE, CREPE, SugarCREPE, and SugarCrepe++ without any task-specific fine-tuning. As a drop-in visual encoder in LLaVA-NeXT, ReasonCLIP improves OKVQA from 34.0 to 35.8 and MMVP from 58.7 to 62.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReasonCLIP consistently outperforms its base CLIP backbone across all retrieval benchmarks: at L/14-336 scale, Stage 1 improves COCO I→T R@1 from 50.0 to 65.1 (+15.1) and RCLIP-V3 from 55.9 to 67.2 (+11.3). Against data-centric baselines, ReasonCLIP-L/14-336 (Stage 1) surpasses MetaCLIP (51.8) and DataComp (53.4) on COCO by 13+ points despite using only 58M reasoning samples vs. their 0.4B–1.4B pretraining corpora. On compositional reasoning (Table 5), ReasonCLIP-B/32 achieves 57.7 average without fine-tuning, beating READ-CLIP (64.1 with 100K MS-COCO fine-tuning) on SugarCrepe++ TOT (61.5 vs. 66.2 is a loss, but ReasonCLIP+READ reaches 67.0), and outperforming NegCLIP (59.6) and CE-CLIP (58.4) on average. ReasonSigLIP-So/14 (Stage 2) reaches 72.4 R@1 on RCLIP-V3, surpassing SigLIP-So/14 (70.5) and matching SigLIP2-G/16 (49.5→73.0 at a different scale). As an MLLM backbone, ReasonCLIP in LLaVA-NeXT improves OKVQA (+1.8), GQA (+0.6), and MMVP (+3.6) over the original CLIP-based LLaVA-NeXT, with no additional inference cost. The one notable loss is that Stage 2 slightly degrades COCO retrieval relative to Stage 1 for CLIP backbones (e.g., 61.3 vs. 65.1 at L/14-336), suggesting explicit category supervision can partially disrupt descriptive alignment in smaller-capacity models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) and SigLIP (Tschannen et al., 2025) lineage by introducing reasoning-oriented continual pretraining as a third axis of improvement, distinct from the two dominant directions of descriptive data scaling (DataComp, MetaCLIP, SigLIP) and architectural refinement (EVA-CLIP, PE-Core, Long-CLIP). Unlike compositional reasoning methods such as READ-CLIP, TripletCLIP, and NegCLIP that operate via fine-tuning or auxiliary losses on small datasets, ReasonCLIP-58M injects reasoning supervision at the pretraining level through a two-stage progressive strategy (open-form reasoning → category-structured reasoning) while preserving the original contrastive alignment objective. The framework is explicitly non-intrusive: no architectural modifications are made, and the resulting encoder serves as a drop-in replacement in downstream MLLMs like LLaVA-NeXT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper validates that CLIP-style representation spaces remain highly extensible for reasoning without architectural changes, and that structured reasoning supervision at pretraining scale (58M samples) yields consistent gains across retrieval, compositional reasoning, and MLLM benchmarks. The introduction of RCLIP-Bench with its three-tier diagnostic hierarchy (visual grounding → evidence awareness → structured reasoning) addresses a real gap in evaluation, since existing benchmarks like WinoGAViL conflate perceptual accuracy with reasoning ability. A notable open question emerges from the ablations: Stage 2's explicit category-level supervision can partially disrupt the visual-semantic balance established in Stage 1, suggesting that the optimal degree of structured reasoning supervision depends on model capacity and backbone architecture — a tension that remains unresolved at scale. The 58M training corpus is also modest compared to industrial CLIP pretraining (0.4B–10B pairs), leaving open whether the paradigm scales proportionally.
