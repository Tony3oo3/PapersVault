## Problem

Existing face personalization adapters inject identity and edit conditions through Euclidean token mixing at the conditioning interface, which treats coupled facial attributes as independent offsets. This leads to identity drift under expression or pose edits, texture leakage, and order-dependent results under compositional multi-attribute edits, because the Euclidean algebra at the interface is mismatched with the curved, coupled geometry of valid face states. The paper asks whether conditioning geometry can be rectified at training time without modifying the diffusion sampler at inference.

## Input / Output

FlowFace takes N reference face images (default N=4, up to 8), a text prompt, and optional attribute descriptors (pooled from the same SDXL text encoder) as input, along with a binary face-region mask that localizes identity injection. Identity is extracted via a frozen InsightFace antelopev2 encoder into 512-D embeddings, which are mapped to manifold coordinates of dimension d_m=512 and resampled into T=16 identity tokens. The output is a single identity-preserving face image generated by the standard SDXL DDIM sampler with classifier-free guidance scale 7.5, with no modification to the denoising rule.

## Method

FlowFace freezes the SDXL VAE, text encoders, and UNet backbone, training only lightweight conditioning modules. A fiber-structured dual-stream manifold encoder separates geometry-sensitive variation (base stream) from identity-sensitive cues (fiber stream), with the fiber refined by self-attention and a transport signal from the base, producing manifold positions U. For multi-attribute edits, a SIREN-parameterized vector field network maps attribute descriptors to smooth update directions, and a truncated BCH expansion (order 2) composes two edits as X + Y + ½[X,Y], introducing an explicit Lie-bracket interaction term that reduces order-induced discrepancies. A region-gated cross-attention branch injects resampled identity tokens into the UNet, masked to facial areas. Training adds two geometry regularizers: a geodesic-consistency loss that learns a local SPD metric (via Cholesky factorization) to align a discrete geodesic-length surrogate with perceptual identity distance, and a directional field-smoothness loss penalizing covariant acceleration of the edit field under the learned metric.

## Results

On the identity-cluster benchmark (5,000 test identities from FFHQ/CelebA-HQ), FlowFace achieves the best SimTarget (64.8), CLIP-T (77.2), Paste Score (6.5), and FID (76.8) among all compared methods. In expression control, FlowFace reaches 52.37% Expr.Acc with AU-MSE of 0.024 and 3D Exp-MSE of 0.512, substantially outperforming the strongest diffusion baseline HiFi-Portrait (36.42%, 0.036, 0.978). The method also reduces order-sensitivity under compositional edits, cutting the OrderGap-ID from 0.146 (additive fusion) to 0.052.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowFace outperforms all six diffusion-based baselines on SimTarget, CLIP-T, Paste Score, and FID simultaneously. The closest competitor is HiFi-Portrait, which edges out FlowFace on SimRef (71.8 vs. 71.3) but trails by 4.4 points on SimTarget, 4.7 on CLIP-T, 4.9 on Paste, and 2.4 on FID. Against InstantID, FlowFace gains +6.5 SimTarget and +5.4 CLIP-T while reducing Paste by 3.9 points. IP-Adapter and PhotoMaker show the largest gaps, with FlowFace improving SimTarget by +9.0 and +10.6 respectively. FastComposer, the weakest baseline overall, is surpassed by FlowFace by +16.2 SimTarget and −15.7 FID. On expression control (Table 2), FlowFace dominates all baselines including the non-diffusion GANimation, achieving 52.37% Expr.Acc versus 36.42% (HiFi-Portrait) and 28.14% (GANimation), with roughly half the AU-MSE and 3D Exp-MSE of the best diffusion competitor.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowFace repurposes ideas from Riemannian geometry (learned SPD metrics, geodesic-length surrogates), Lie theory (truncated BCH expansions, Lie brackets), and geometric deep learning (fiber-bundle base-fiber decompositions) into the face-personalization setting, where prior adapters (IP-Adapter, PhotoMaker, InstantID, HiFi-Portrait) inject identity through essentially Euclidean token mixing. Rather than following the line of Riemannian score-based diffusion that modifies the sampler itself, FlowFace performs all geometric rectification at training time on lightweight conditioning modules while leaving the SDXL DDIM sampler untouched. The Lie-algebraic composition operator is a direct import from differential geometry that has no clear analogue in existing diffusion personalization pipelines, which rely on commutative additive or attention-based fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously under-addressed failure mode—order-induced inconsistency under compositional attribute edits—and shows that a first-order Lie-bracket interaction term can roughly halve the permutation gap, which is a practical concern for any multi-attribute editing workflow. The training-time-only geometric rectification strategy is attractive because it avoids the numerical fragility and compute overhead of manifold-aware sampling at SDXL scale. Limitations include reduced robustness under extreme pose changes or severe occlusion, and the evaluation is confined to a single backbone (SDXL) and a single benchmark (FFHQ/CelebA-HQ clusters), leaving open whether the geometric framing transfers to other domains or architectures. A key open question is whether the learned SPD metric captures genuine manifold structure of the face distribution or merely acts as a learned re-weighting that happens to correlate with identity distance.
