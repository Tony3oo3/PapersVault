## Problem

MMDiT-based models (FLUX, SD3.5, Qwen Image) generate images solely through text-visual attention interactions, yet it remains unclear how different internal blocks contribute to rendering specific semantic attributes like color, shape, and spatial relationships. Existing analyses focus on isolated components (attention maps, RoPE, skip connections) without a systematic investigation of how blocks collectively interact with textual conditions, leaving no principled way to selectively strengthen prompt adherence at inference time.

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes a text prompt and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image using the EDM sampler over 28 denoising steps, with the only modification being scaled textual hidden states at a small set of pre-identified blocks. For editing, it additionally consumes a source image whose self-attention features are injected into the target generation to preserve visual content while the enhanced text drives the edit. For acceleration, the same text prompt yields an image after skipping less-critical blocks, reducing inference time from ~37s to ~25s on a 4090 GPU with minimal quality loss.

## Method

TexTailor is a training-free, inference-time framework that first identifies which MMDiT blocks are most responsive to textual enhancement via a systematic probing pipeline (removing, disabling, and ×2-scaling text hidden states per block, evaluated with DINOv2, CLIP Score, and VQA). Based on the finding that a common high-signal region exists across attributes, it selects a small fixed set of blocks per model (e.g., {3,9,15,21} for SD3.5, {2,7,12,17,22} for FLUX) and scales their textual hidden states by a factor λ (default 1.5) during denoising. Two tailoring granularities are supported: full-sentence enhancement (scaling all text tokens uniformly) and token-level enhancement (scaling only tokens corresponding to target attributes like color or count, located via the tokenized prompt). The same block-selection principle extends to editing (enhancing target text embeddings in critical blocks for precise attribute changes) and acceleration (skipping blocks identified as less critical for semantics), all without any parameter updates.

## Results

On SD3.5-Large, TexTailor (full sentence) improves T2I-CompBench overall from 56.92% to 63.00% and GenEval from 66.42% to 71.63%, with the largest gains on Shape (+11.5%), Texture (+9.6%), and Color (+7.7%). On FLUX.1-Dev, GenEval overall rises from 65.38% to 68.26%, and on Qwen Image from 85.51% to 87.77%. Image quality metrics (HPSv2, Aesthetic Score) remain essentially unchanged, confirming that the text-alignment gains do not come at the cost of synthesis quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TACA (the only T2I-generation baseline), TexTailor wins decisively on compositional attributes: on SD3.5, full-sentence tailoring lifts Color from 0.7434 (TACA) to 0.8052, Shape from 0.5784 to 0.6744, and Texture from 0.7444 to 0.8428; on FLUX the gaps are similar (e.g., Shape 0.5482 vs. 0.5126 at r=64). TACA's gains are modest and sometimes negative on individual attributes, whereas TexTailor is consistently positive. For image editing, TexTailor outperforms Stable Flow on CLIP_txt (36.20 vs. 35.26) and human preference (59.0% vs. 41.0%) while keeping CLIP_img nearly identical (0.9637 vs. 0.9642), indicating more faithful instruction following without degrading visual fidelity. For inference acceleration, TexTailor's block-skipping is faster than TeaCache (24.5 s vs. 26.6 s on a 4090) with only a marginal HPSv2 drop (28.86 vs. 28.90), and the two methods are complementary—combining them yields further speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of interpretability work on diffusion models (P2P, MasaCtrl, FreeU for UNets; Stable Flow, FreeFlux, E-MMDiT for MMDiTs) by providing the first systematic, multi-operation probing framework (removing, disabling, enhancing) applied uniformly across multiple MMDiT architectures (SD3.5, FLUX, Qwen Image) and semantic attributes. Unlike prior works that isolate a single mechanism (RoPE shifting, attention decomposition, timestep-aware re-weighting à la TACA), TexTailor treats block-wise text-visual interaction as a unified object of study and derives actionable block selections from the analysis. The resulting inference-time method—scaling text hidden states in a handful of identified blocks—is conceptually closer to Attend-and-Excite's cross-attention amplification but operates at the block-selection level rather than the attention-map level, and requires no learned parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic block-wise analysis offers the community a reusable diagnostic tool for understanding MMDiT internals, and the finding that a few blocks in a 'high-signal' region can be enhanced to improve alignment (or skipped to accelerate inference) provides a practical, training-free lever for practitioners deploying these models. However, the method's reliance on a fixed, model-specific block set limits its adaptability to novel prompt structures, and the authors acknowledge it cannot resolve highly complex compositional prompts due to pretraining constraints. Open questions include whether dynamic, prompt-aware block selection (beyond the current static set) could yield further gains, and whether the observed block roles transfer to video MMDiT variants or to models trained with different objectives.
