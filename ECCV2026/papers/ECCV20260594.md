## Problem

Rolling shutter (RS) relative pose estimation has remained impractical because the state-of-the-art solver requires a minimum of 20 point correspondences, making RANSAC-based robust estimation prohibitively expensive (over a million iterations at 50% inliers). The paper closes this gap by introducing affine correspondences into RS two-view geometry, reducing the minimal sample to 7 and making RS-aware robust estimation feasible in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260594/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two RS images with 7 affine correspondences, where each correspondence pairs a point match with a local 2×2 affine transformation between image patches (available from affine-covariant detectors like AffNet or dense matchers like RoMa at negligible extra cost). It outputs the 5-DoF relative pose (rotation R and unit translation t) plus 12 RS motion parameters (per-camera angular velocity ω_k and translational velocity v_k, k=1,2), with the full 17-DoF solution refined by joint Levenberg-Marquardt on the inlier set. The algebraic solver itself runs in ~1.2 ms per hypothesis, and the full SupeRANSAC pipeline completes in 3.7–7.5 s on TUM-RS sequences.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution is a set of RS-corrected affine constraints: when differentiating the row-dependent epipolar constraint, the perturbation of a point's image position shifts its readout row, introducing ∂Ẽ/∂τ_k correction terms absent in the global-shutter formulation. Each AC thus yields 3 equations (1 epipolar + 2 affine), giving 21 equations for the 17 unknowns from 7 ACs. The solver linearizes in the 12 RS parameters (justified by their physical smallness, ‖ω‖ < 0.15 rad), then eliminates them via null-space projection of the 21×12 Jacobian, leaving 9 polynomial consistency conditions on the 5 pose unknowns. Using Cayley parameterization for R and a linear-in-t factorization, the problem reduces to 10 degree-4 polynomials in 3 variables with algebraic degree 20, solved via action matrices on a 40×56 Macaulay matrix. The resulting candidates are integrated into SupeRANSAC with PROSAC sampling, MAGSAC scoring, and LM refinement using a combined epipolar + affine cost that also alleviates the v–t coupling that makes translational velocity poorly conditioned from point correspondences alone.

## Results

On the TUM-RS benchmark (stride 10, AffNet features), the proposed RS-7AC solver achieves AUC@5° = 0.502, improving over GS-5PC (0.428) by 17% relative and over RS-44PC (0.267) by 88%. With RoMa dense features, AUC@5° reaches 0.897, surpassing all baselines including RS-44PC (0.870) and GS-5PC (0.861). The translational velocity error drops to 0.056 m (AffNet) versus 11.26–13.11 m for the point-based RS solvers, a >200× improvement attributable to affine constraints resolving the v–t coupling. On the global-shutter EuRoC MAV dataset, the solver matches GS-5PC (AUC@5° = 0.512 vs. 0.521 at stride 10) while correctly recovering near-zero RS parameters (ε_ω ≈ 0.016 rad, ε_v ≈ 0.035 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the RS-specific baselines (RS-20PC and RS-44PC of Dai et al.), the proposed method wins decisively on pose AUC: with AffNet features at stride 10, AUC@5° improves from 0.101/0.267 to 0.502, a gap the paper attributes to RANSAC's exponential sensitivity to sample size (at 30% outliers, the probability of drawing an all-inlier sample drops from ~0.08 for k=7 to ~10⁻⁷ for k=44). On translational velocity, the advantage is even more dramatic: RS-7AC achieves ε_v ≈ 0.05 m versus 11–13 m for the point-based solvers, because affine constraints provide row-dependent gradient information that disentangles v from t. Against the GS baselines (GS-5PC, GS-2AC), the proposed method also wins on pose AUC (0.502 vs. 0.428 for GS-5PC with AffNet; 0.897 vs. 0.861 for GS-5PC with RoMa), confirming that RS-aware modeling yields a systematic accuracy gain beyond what a smaller GS sample can achieve. The one area where the proposed method is slightly weaker is angular velocity estimation in the synthetic oracle setting (~2× higher ω error than RS-44PC), though under full RANSAC with outliers this gap closes and the proposed method still wins. On the EuRoC MAV global-shutter dataset, the proposed solver ties GS-5PC within 1% AUC while additionally recovering near-zero RS parameters, whereas the Dai et al. solvers degrade substantially (AUC@5° = 0.43–0.50) due to their large minimal samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: rolling-shutter (RS) two-view geometry (Meingast et al.'s first-order model, Dai et al.'s 20-PC minimal solver) and affine-correspondence (AC) constraints for camera geometry (Barath & Hajder's 2-AC essential matrix solver, Bentolila & Francos' conic epipolar constraints). The key conceptual departure is deriving RS-corrected affine constraints that account for the coupling between point perturbations and the row-dependent essential matrix — a coupling with no analogue in the global-shutter formulation. The solver itself draws on standard algebraic-geometry machinery (Cayley parameterization, null-space projection, action matrices) but applies them to a novel 17-DoF system where the 12 RS unknowns are eliminated via a fixed null-space basis before solving a degree-20 polynomial system in the 5 pose unknowns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the RANSAC sample from 20 to 7 correspondences, the paper makes RS-aware relative pose estimation computationally practical for the first time (iterations drop from ~10⁶ to ~600 at 50% inliers), which is significant given that RS sensors equip virtually all consumer devices. The finding that translational velocity is fundamentally unresolvable from point correspondences alone due to the v-t coupling — and that ACs provide the missing spatial-gradient information — has implications beyond this specific solver for any RS estimation pipeline. Limitations include the linearization assumption (valid for |ω| < 0.15 rad, covering most consumer scenarios but not extreme motion), the fixed null-space basis approximation, and the 1.2 ms solver cost which, while acceptable at k=7, would be prohibitive at larger sample sizes. Open questions include extension to higher-order RS models, multi-view RS SfM, and whether the v-t coupling insight generalizes to other RS-aware estimation problems.
