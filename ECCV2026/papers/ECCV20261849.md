## Problem

Text-to-SVG generation has advanced rapidly with multimodal large models, but reliable evaluation remains underexplored. Existing protocols either treat SVG as source code or render it to a raster and apply generic image metrics (MSE, LPIPS, FID, CLIPScore), which fail to capture SVG-specific perceptual qualities such as geometric integrity, spatial layout rationality, and structural correctness. No unified, human-aligned benchmark exists that evaluates the rendered visual outcome while accounting for vector-specific structured attributes.

## Input / Output

The scorer jointly consumes three modalities per instance: the natural-language text prompt, the rasterized rendering of the SVG (the primary perceptual target), and the raw SVG source code (providing complementary structural cues like path closure and coordinate anomalies). Output is a fixed structured format containing a 1–5 Likert score for each of four dimensions (Aesthetic, Semantic, Spatial, Structural) paired with a short evidence-grounded rationale citing specific visual or code-level observations. The benchmark itself has two tiers: Part I uses binary Yes/No diagnostic questions (500 instances) and Part II uses the four-dimension 1–5 scoring (500 instances), with gold labels defined over the rendered outcome.

## Method

SVGEval introduces a four-aspect rubric with a hierarchical attribution principle (explicit geometric/spatial defects are penalized under Structural/Spatial before residual impressions are attributed to Aesthetic) to reduce cross-dimension ambiguity. The benchmark is built from ~10k candidates mixing model-generated SVGs (diverse failure patterns) and real SVGs (quality anchors), with topic-aware stratified sampling and multi-round human annotation plus expert adjudication. For the explainable scorer, the authors use Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B as a teacher to perform score-conditioned rationalization—given a human gold score, the teacher identifies concrete visual/code evidence supporting that score under dimension-specific constraints—producing image-rationale-score triples via reasoning distillation. The student (Qwen3-VL 30B A3B) is then supervised-fine-tuned on these triples, minimizing generation loss over both rationale text and score tokens. Ablations confirm that removing the rendered image causes the sharpest degradation (MAE 0.96 vs. 0.58), removing SVG code mildly hurts Spatial/Structural, and removing rationale supervision turns the scorer into a black-box regressor with substantially worse calibration.

## Results

On SVGEval's scoring task (Part II), SVGEval-Scorer achieves a MAE of 0.5772 and Adjacent Accuracy of 71.95%, outperforming the best general-purpose MLLM (Gemini-3 Pro, MAE 0.7068, Adj. Acc. 66.57%) by 18.4% in MAE and 5.4 points in Adj. Acc. On the binary diagnosis task (Part I), general MLLMs range from 69.2% (GPT-4o-mini) to 89.4% (Gemini-3 Flash) accuracy. Per-dimension analysis reveals that Spatial is the hardest dimension for all models (MAE 0.84–1.61 for general MLLMs), while SVGEval-Scorer reduces Spatial MAE to 0.5167 and Structural MAE to 0.5840, closing the gap most substantially on these SVG-specific aspects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVGEval-Scorer outperforms all 12 general-purpose MLLMs on the scoring task, with the largest margin over Gemini-3 Pro (MAE 0.5772 vs. 0.7068, a 18.4% relative reduction) and the weakest model Grok-4.1-Fast (MAE 0.5772 vs. 1.3614, a 57.6% reduction). The advantage is most pronounced on the Spatial dimension, where SVGEval-Scorer achieves MAE 0.5167 versus 0.8400 for Gemini-3 Pro and 1.6080 for InternVL3.5, indicating that dedicated vision-grounded training is critical for layout/topology reasoning. On the binary diagnosis task, no single model dominates: Gemini-3 Flash leads at 89.4% accuracy, while GPT-4o-mini trails at 69.2%, and performance on Part I does not strongly correlate with Part II scoring quality. Ablations confirm that removing the rendered image (noimg) causes the largest degradation (MAE jumps to 0.9557), removing reasoning supervision (nothink) degrades to 0.8330, and removing SVG code (nosvg) has the mildest effect (0.6220), establishing that visual grounding is the most essential input modality while SVG code provides complementary structural cues particularly for Spatial and Structural dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of SVG generation evaluation and multimodal quality assessment. It departs from prior SVG benchmarks (VGBench, SVGenius, VectorGym) that focus on task completion or code-level correctness, and from raster-centric metrics (FID, LPIPS, CLIPScore) that ignore vector-specific structure. Conceptually, it repurposes the rubric-guided LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm (G-Eval, Prometheus) and the multi-aspect quality assessment philosophy of Q-Bench/Q-Bench+, but specializes both to SVG's structured geometry and spatial composition. The reasoning-distillation approach for generating explainable rationales from gold scores is a practical extension of knowledge distillation applied to evaluation rather than generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SVGEval provides the first unified, vision-grounded benchmark that exposes a consistent and practically important weakness in current MLLMs: they handle semantic and aesthetic judgments well but fail on spatial layout and geometric structure assessment, precisely the dimensions that determine SVG usability. The trained scorer offers a practical, interpretable evaluation tool for the growing SVG generation ecosystem. Open questions include whether the spatial/structural gap will close as MLLMs improve, how to handle inherent subjectivity in Likert-scale annotations (the authors suggest modeling expected perceptual variance), and whether more explicit image-SVG cross-referencing strategies can further improve robustness against rasterization artifacts.
