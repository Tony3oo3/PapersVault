## Problem

Existing masked self-supervised methods for skeleton action recognition either reconstruct raw joint coordinates (sensitive to noise and temporal discretization) or predict learned latent embeddings (semantic but lacking explicit spatiotemporal relational geometry). Neither approach encodes the coordinated multi-joint motion structure that underlies action semantics, nor does it provide principled invariance to frame-rate variation and irregular sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261130/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D skeleton sequence of shape T×J×3 (joint coordinates over time) and lifts it into continuous paths via piecewise-linear interpolation followed by a lead-lag transformation (d'=6). These continuous paths are then decomposed into multi-scale log-signature tokens across three spatial levels (individual joints, kinematic joint pairs, and multi-joint anatomical chains) and two temporal views (global over the full sequence, local over strided windows), yielding 152 tokens per sequence. The pretrained EMA teacher encoder produces a learned representation vector per sequence, which is used for linear probing or fine-tuning to output an action class label.

## Method

Path-JEPA replaces the prediction target in a JEPA framework with path-signature features computed from rough path theory. Each skeleton sequence is lifted to continuous lead-lag paths, and truncated log-signatures (level 2, 22 coefficients) are extracted over joint, edge, and chain paths at multiple temporal scales, with each token concatenating a global and a local signature view. A novel signature-augmented masking strategy propagates joint-space masks to all dependent signature tokens (edge tokens with a masked endpoint, chain tokens traversing a masked joint), preventing information leakage through relational paths and enforcing anatomically coherent prediction via kinematic subtree expansion. The student transformer encoder processes only visible tokens with structure-aware attention biases (temporal distance, scale, window IoU, kinematic graph distance), while a cross-attention predictor reconstructs masked token embeddings to match EMA-teacher targets under a cosine JEPA loss.

## Results

On linear evaluation (frozen encoder + linear probe), Path-JEPA achieves 87.6% on NTU60 X-Sub, 92.9% on NTU60 X-View, 81.5% on NTU120 X-Sub, 81.8% on NTU120 X-Set, 93.4% on PKU-MMD Phase I, and 56.7% on PKU-MMD Phase II. Under fine-tuning, it reaches 94.4% (NTU60 X-Sub) and 98.2% (NTU60 X-View), surpassing all prior self-supervised methods and rivaling supervised approaches like CTR-GCN (92.6% / 96.9%). Path-JEPA also demonstrates superior frame-rate robustness, varying by only 1.0% across 15–60 fps on NTU60 X-Sub, compared to 2.9–5.0% drops for S-JEPA and SkeletonMAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Path-JEPA outperforms all 14 baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest JEPA-style competitor S-JEPA [1], it gains +2.3% on NTU60 X-Sub (87.6 vs 85.3), +3.1% on NTU60 X-View, +1.9% on both NTU120 protocols, and +3.2% on PKU-MMD Phase II. Against the strongest feature-prediction baseline GFP [48], improvements are +1.7% (NTU60 X-Sub), +0.9% (NTU60 X-View), +2.4% (NTU120 X-Sub), and +0.5% (PKU Ph-II). Coordinate-reconstruction methods trail substantially: MAMP [37] is 2.7–3.8% behind on NTU60 and 2.7–2.9% behind on NTU120, while SkeletonMAE [52] lags by 9–15% across all protocols. Among contrastive methods, ActCLR [28] is the strongest but still 3.3–7.2% below Path-JEPA on NTU60/NTU120. Under fine-tuning (Table 2), Path-JEPA (94.4% / 98.2% on NTU60) surpasses all self-supervised baselines including MaskCLR [22] (93.5% / 97.5%) and MAMP (93.1% / 97.5%), and exceeds supervised ST-GCN [53] (81.5% / 88.3%) and 2s-AGCN [44] (88.5% / 95.1%) by wide margins, approaching CTR-GCN [9] (92.6% / 96.9%) without explicit graph design.

## Relation to Prior Work

Path-JEPA sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: the JEPA-style masked prediction framework for skeletons established by S-JEPA and GFP, and the path-signature methodology from rough path theory that Yang et al. and others applied to supervised landmark action recognition. The key departure is repurposing path signatures—from fixed supervised input features to self-supervised prediction targets—so that the model must infer geometrically structured motion descriptors (displacement, Lévy area, higher-order couplings) rather than raw coordinates or black-box latent embeddings. The Rough Transformer's pattern of processing signature-patched tokens with self-attention informs the encoder design, but Path-JEPA is the first to combine multi-scale kinematic tokenization (joint/edge/chain) with signature-augmented masking in a JEPA training loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically relevant finding is the near-invariance to frame-rate changes (1.0% variation across 15–60 fps vs. 2.9–5% for baselines), which follows directly from the reparameterization invariance of path signatures and could simplify deployment in settings with irregular or variable sampling. The semi-supervised gains at 1% labels (+4.7% over S-JEPA) suggest the geometric interpretability of signature coefficients aids generalization under extreme data scarcity. Open questions include whether the signature degree and window sizes can be learned jointly with the model rather than set heuristically, whether the approach extends to multi-modal (RGB+skeleton) or other structured time-series domains, and whether the computational overhead of multi-scale tokenization remains acceptable at longer sequence lengths.
