## Problem

On-policy reinforcement learning for diffusion models is fundamentally self-limiting: by restricting exploration to the model's own narrow capability distribution, it suffers from reward saturation (overfitting to locally optimal samples) and mode collapse (catastrophic quality degradation). No prior method provides a stable, external quality reference that grounds the policy update in a globally consistent understanding of what 'good' looks like, leaving the model's optimization direction myopic and locally accurate but globally inconsistent.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated image (512×512) or video (480p) as output. During training, a dual-model architecture is maintained: a trainable DiT network for policy updates and an EMA snapshot network for stable sample generation. Off-policy anchor samples are pre-generated offline from a stronger model (Qwen-Image for images, Wan2.1-14B for videos) and retrieved per prompt, while on-policy samples are generated online from the snapshot model. For video, a dynamic sampling strategy generates low-fidelity snapshots—single-frame for spatial rewards and low-resolution/high-frame-count for temporal rewards—to reduce computational cost.

## Method

DiffusionCompass formulates a unified training objective L = λ₁L_ON + λ₂L_OFF + λ₃L_KL that synergistically combines on-policy exploration (via DiffusionNFT's contrastive vector-field alignment) with three off-policy anchoring mechanisms. First, a distribution-aware off-policy loss weights positive and negative anchors by their proximity to the current policy (w = e^{−αd}), guiding the network toward high-quality samples while associating low-quality ones with null prompts via classifier-free guidance. Second, a perceptual anchored reward multiplies the scalar reward by the maximum DINOv2 feature similarity between the on-policy sample and off-policy positive references, mitigating reward hacking. Third, reward renormalization computes the optimal probability p over the combined on-policy and off-policy reward distribution, providing a stable quality baseline. This dual-regime design ensures off-policy anchors prevent collapse and overfitting while on-policy exploration avoids mere imitation of a static dataset.

## Results

On the GenEval compositional image generation benchmark, DiffusionCompass achieves an overall score of 0.97 in just 1K iterations, outperforming FlowGRPO (0.95, 5K iterations) and DiffusionNFT (0.95, 1K iterations). On the OCR text-rendering task, it reaches 0.975 accuracy in 64 GPU hours versus 0.915 for FlowGRPO (600 GPU hours) and 0.970 for DiffusionNFT (64 GPU hours). On the comprehensive 16-dimension VBench video benchmark, the 14B model attains a total score of 87.16 in 160 iterations, surpassing CausVid (83.88), HunyuanVideo (83.43), and Wan2.1-14B (83.69), while the 1.3B model reaches 85.00 in 320 iterations. Diversity metrics confirm robustness: Group SSIM drops to 0.262 (vs. 0.324 for DiffusionNFT) and Pixel Vendi Score rises to 1.87 (vs. 1.46), indicating significantly better sample diversity without sacrificing reward performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffusionCompass outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every benchmark. Against the primary on-policy RL baselines, it beats DiffusionNFT by +0.02 on GenEval (0.97 vs. 0.95), +0.005 on OCR (0.975 vs. 0.970), +0.1 on PickScore (23.8 vs. 23.7), and +1.48 on VBench total (85.00 vs. 83.52 for 1.3B), while also achieving substantially better diversity (Group SSIM 0.262 vs. 0.324). Against FlowGRPO, the gains are larger: +0.02 on GenEval with 5× fewer iterations (1K vs. 5K), +0.06 on OCR with ~10× fewer GPU hours (64 vs. 600), and +0.4 on PickScore. On the video benchmark, the 14B model (87.16) surpasses CausVid (83.88) by +3.28, HunyuanVideo (83.43) by +3.73, Wan2.1-14B (83.69) by +3.47, CogVideoX-5B (81.91) by +5.25, and Open-Sora-2.0 (81.71) by +5.45. For image generation, it exceeds GPT-4o (0.84), Janus-Pro (0.80), Qwen-Image (0.91), SD3.5-L (0.71), and FLUX.1-Dev (0.66) on GenEval. Notably, the method also wins on out-of-domain generalization metrics (Image Quality 5.00 vs. 4.45, CLIP Score 0.306 vs. 0.280) and additional reward dimensions (ImageReward 1.70 vs. 1.64, VideoOCR 0.433 vs. 0.412), confirming that the off-policy anchoring prevents the reward overfitting that degrades baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffusionCompass builds directly on DiffusionNFT as its on-policy optimizer, extending the flow-matching RL paradigm by adding a static off-policy quality-anchoring objective. Conceptually, it translates the off-policy guidance insight from the LLM RL literature (where GRPO/DAPO variants have shown that purely on-policy learning traps models in their own distribution) to the diffusion model setting, where prior work (FlowGRPO, DiffusionNFT, DPO variants) has relied exclusively on on-policy rollouts. The specific mechanisms—distribution-aware filtering via stop-gradient distance, perceptual anchoring through DINOv2 similarity, and reward renormalization over combined on/off-policy sets—are novel combinations, but each draws on established ideas (classifier-free guidance for negative samples, EMA snapshot models, GRPO-style reward normalization).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized practical failure mode of on-policy diffusion RL (reward overfitting and mode collapse) with a clean, modular solution that is model-agnostic and compatible with different on-policy backbones (validated on both DiffusionNFT and FlowGRPO). The dynamic sampling strategy for video generation is a practically important efficiency contribution, halving GPU hours while improving spatial-relationship scores. Open questions include: how sensitive performance is to the quality and diversity of the off-policy corpus, whether the approach scales to much larger models or longer video horizons, and whether the 'essential' framing of off-policy anchoring holds when the on-policy model is already very strong. The modest absolute gains over DiffusionNFT (e.g., 0.975 vs 0.970 OCR) suggest the benefit is most pronounced in multi-reward or diversity-critical settings rather than single-reward saturation.
