## Problem

Existing camera-based SpO₂ estimation methods rely on predefined frequency-domain filtering to separate pulsatile (AC) and baseline (DC) components, which propagates in-band noise, assumes temporal stationarity, and degrades under motion or illumination variation. Additionally, most approaches use discrete recurrent models for temporal modeling despite physiological signals evolving in continuous time, leading to instability under varying frame rates. FUSE addresses both gaps by replacing spectral decomposition with physics-grounded differential constraints and adopting continuous-time neural dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB facial video clips of 120 frames, preprocessed via OpenFace 68-landmark alignment and cropped to 32×32 spatial resolution, and produces a frame-wise SpO₂ waveform (one scalar per frame). The AC and DC feature tensors are learned end-to-end without any bandpass or lowpass filtered pseudo-labels; instead, their temporal and spatial structure is enforced through differential equation residuals computed on the learned feature maps during training only, introducing no inference-time overhead.

## Method

FUSE models SpO₂ estimation as a coupled wave-transport dynamical system at three hierarchical levels. At the temporal level, AC features are regularized as band-limited harmonic oscillators via a second-order ODE constraint (∂²x/∂t² + ω²x ≈ 0) with frequencies sampled from a physiological cardiac band, while DC features are constrained to low-frequency dynamics. At the spatial level, the AC feature field is interpreted as a transported scalar density ρ with a latent velocity field v, regularized by an advection PDE residual (∂ρ/∂t + v·∇ρ ≈ 0), a near-incompressibility divergence penalty, and an anti-collapse energy term to prevent trivial constant solutions. At the global level, fused AC/DC embeddings from parallel PhysNet 3D-CNN backbones drive a continuous-time latent dynamical system (LTC, CTRNN, or Neural-ODE) whose output is a linear readout of the SpO₂ waveform. Training minimizes a combined objective of MSE + Pearson correlation on the SpO₂ signal plus weighted harmonic ODE, advection, divergence, and energy regularization terms, all computed in the time domain without any frequency-domain operations.

## Results

FUSE (CTRNN variant) achieves the best MAE across all three benchmarks: 0.15% on PURE, 0.24% on BH-rPPG, and 0.56% on VIPL-HR, with Pearson correlation up to 0.94 on BH-rPPG. The LTC variant matches this on PURE (MAE 0.15, r=0.92) while the Neural-ODE variant is slightly behind (MAE 0.17 on PURE). Compared to the closest prior method ACuRE, FUSE reduces MAE by approximately 46% on PURE (0.15 vs. 0.28), 38% on BH-rPPG (0.24 vs. 0.39), and 22% on VIPL-HR (0.56 vs. 0.72).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUSE outperforms all nine compared baselines on every dataset where overlap exists. The closest competitor, ACuRE [2], is surpassed by a wide margin: FUSE (CTRNN) reduces PURE MAE from 0.28 to 0.15 (−46%), BH-rPPG MAE from 0.39 to 0.24 (−38%), and VIPL-HR MAE from 0.72 to 0.56 (−22%), while also achieving higher Pearson correlation on BH-rPPG (0.94 vs. 0.92) and VIPL-HR (0.91 vs. 0.87). PHASE [3] and Akamatsu et al. [4] are the next-strongest baselines but remain substantially behind, with PHASE at MAE 0.53 on PURE and 0.97 on BH-rPPG, and Akamatsu at 0.61 and 1.90 respectively. The remaining methods (Sun et al., Hu et al., ROSE-Net, SHINE, ITSCAN, Cheng et al.) report MAE values between 0.54 and 1.95, all well above FUSE's 0.15–0.56 range. Notably, FUSE also exhibits lower variance (e.g., ±0.05 on PURE vs. ±0.17 for ACuRE), indicating more consistent performance across cross-validation folds.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUSE is a direct successor to ACuRE (same first author), replacing its frequency-domain AC/DC pseudo-label generation with physics-informed differential constraints: a band-limited harmonic ODE for temporal oscillation and an advection-based PDE for spatial transport of the pulsatile density. The continuous-time temporal backbone (LTC, CTRNN, Neural-ODE) and the PhysNet 3D-CNN encoder are inherited from prior rPPG work, while the key conceptual departure is framing AC/DC separation as a coupled wave-transport dynamical system rather than a spectral decomposition problem. The paper positions itself within the broader camera-based SpO2 estimation literature (PHASE, CCSpO2Net, ROSE-Net, SHINE) as a physics-guided alternative to the implicit frequency filtering that most prior methods rely on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating bandpass filtering, FUSE addresses a persistent vulnerability in rPPG-based SpO2 estimation—noise propagation within the passband and degradation under non-stationary conditions—offering a more principled inductive bias grounded in fluid-dynamics analogies. The consistent cross-dataset gains across three frame rates (15–30 fps) suggest the continuous-time formulation genuinely improves sampling invariance, which is practically important for deployment across heterogeneous camera systems. Open questions include whether the transport PDE captures true hemodynamic propagation or merely acts as a spatial smoothness regularizer, whether the approach extends to other vital signs (HR, respiration) as the authors suggest, and whether the training-time derivative computation cost limits scalability to longer sequences or higher resolutions.
