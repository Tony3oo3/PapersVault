## Problem

Existing continual learning methods for vision-language models assume each new task introduces entirely new domain knowledge, but real-world task sequences routinely share visual concepts, language patterns, and even training samples across stages. When tasks overlap, MoE-based methods that assign one expert per task with fixed routing split similar inputs across different experts and degrade sharply, yet no prior method detects or exploits this shared structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260104/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method processes sequential tasks, each consisting of (image, text query, target response) triples, and adapts a frozen LLaVA-1.5-7B backbone via LoRA adapters. At each task, frozen CLIP vision and text encoders extract embeddings that are concatenated into a joint representation used for GMM fitting, overlap detection, and router training. The system produces task-appropriate outputs—class labels for classification, text answers for VQA, and captions for generation—while maintaining performance on all previously seen tasks without storing raw data from past tasks.

## Method

SOCiaL fits a lightweight Gaussian mixture model (GMM) to each task's embedding distribution, which serves three roles: measuring directional semantic overlap via asymmetric density-based coverage scores, generating synthetic replay embeddings for the router, and providing a closed-form mergeable representation of task context. When mutual overlap exceeds a threshold, the corresponding LoRA adapters are merged in full weight space by averaging the actual weight deltas (B_i A_i) rather than mixing low-rank factors, eliminating the quadratic cross-term artifacts of standard LoRA merging; the merged delta is compressed back to LoRA format via rank-r truncated SVD and briefly fine-tuned with L2 regularization toward the merged initialization. The router is formulated as a contextual bandit with binary reward, trained via policy gradient with a learned baseline and entropy regularization on real current-task embeddings plus GMM-generated synthetic replay from all prior tasks, allowing it to adapt elastically as experts are added or merged.

## Results

On the disjoint-task CoIN benchmark (8 tasks), SOCiaL achieves 70.17% last accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline HiDe-LLaVA (62.82%) by 7.35 points and even exceeding the multi-task oracle (67.40%). On UCIT (6 tasks), SOCiaL reaches 72.23%, outperforming HiDe-LLaVA (64.19%) by 8.04 points. On the novel UCIT-O benchmark with controlled semantic overlap, SOCiaL achieves 64.50%, 70.18%, and 69.30% under Protocols I–III respectively, beating the strongest baseline by 3.07, 4.74, and 9.77 points, with the gap widening as overlap severity increases. Merging also reduces deployed adapters from 10 to as few as 3 on the most overlapping protocols.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOCiaL outperforms all six baselines across all three benchmarks. Against the strongest MoE baseline HiDe-LLaVA [6], SOCiaL gains +7.35 points on CoIN (70.17 vs. 62.82), +8.04 on UCIT (72.23 vs. 64.19), and +3.07 to +9.77 on UCIT-O, with the gap widening as semantic overlap increases. The regularization-based baselines LwF [13], EWC [12], and L2P [30] trail SOCiaL by 15–30+ points on CoIN and UCIT, and on UCIT-O Protocol III they collapse to 59.53% (EWC) and 56.78% (O-LoRA) versus SOCiaL's 69.30%. The LoRA-based O-LoRA [29] is the strongest non-MoE baseline but still lags SOCiaL by 11–13 points on UCIT-O. The MoELoRA [4] baseline degrades most severely under overlap (43.26% on Protocol III), confirming that fixed-routing MoE architectures are particularly vulnerable when tasks share content. Notably, SOCiaL also surpasses the multi-task oracle on UCIT-O Protocol III (69.30 vs. 62.81), because multi-task training exposes identical samples under conflicting task labels, whereas SOCiaL trains separate experts and merges only when GMM-confirmed overlap justifies consolidation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MoE-based continual learning for VLMs (MoELoRA, HiDe-LLaVA) by challenging their core assumption that sequential tasks are disjoint. It repurposes per-task GMMs as a unified mechanism for overlap detection, synthetic replay, and routing guidance—roles previously handled by separate components or ignored entirely. The interference-free LoRA merging in full weight space departs from the standard low-rank factor averaging used by Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, and DARE, eliminating cross-term artifacts that grow quadratically with the number of merged experts. The elastic contextual-bandit router generalizes fixed-classifier routing to handle a dynamically changing expert pool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UCIT-O benchmark is the first to systematically study continual VLM learning under controlled semantic overlap, filling a gap between the artificial disjoint-task setting and real-world deployment where tasks inevitably share visual concepts and language patterns. The threefold reduction in deployed adapters has direct practical value for serving cost and memory. Open questions include whether the GMM assumption (diagonal covariance, fixed component count) generalizes to highly non-Gaussian or very high-dimensional task distributions, and whether the approach scales beyond 10 tasks or to larger model families without the per-task fitting cost becoming prohibitive.
