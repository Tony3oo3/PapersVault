## Problem

Current reasoning segmentation models entangle visual perception and cognitive deduction into opaque end-to-end black boxes, severely limiting interpretability, zero-shot generalization, and scalability. Existing benchmarks remain confined to isolated single-instance or homogeneous-category queries, lacking a systematic taxonomy for complex real-world manipulation-oriented reasoning (e.g., cross-category tool inference, part-level grounding, attribute-based differentiation). This creates a dual bottleneck: models cannot explain their reasoning, and the field lacks both diverse evaluation scenarios and a scalable path to generate training data without costly human annotation.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a single RGB image and an implicit reasoning query (e.g., 'Divide the dessert into several pieces') that may require multi-step semantic deduction rather than direct noun-phrase matching. It outputs a final segmentation mask formed by the union of selected instance masks, along with the explicit mask indices chosen by the LLM and a step-by-step logic chain explaining the deduction. The dense semantic descriptions generated per mask (via DAM) serve as an intermediate, human-readable representation that can be cached and reused for unlimited subsequent queries on the same image without redundant visual re-computation.

## Method

GEAR-Seg explicitly decouples reasoning segmentation into three transparent, plug-and-play stages: (1) SAM 2 in Everything Mode produces class-agnostic instance masks via Mask NMS, (2) the Describe Anything Model (DAM) generates context-aware, attribute-rich text descriptions for every mask—constituting the core pixel-to-text translation that replaces sparse category prompts with dense physical and spatial details, and (3) a standalone LLM (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-32B, or Gemini) evaluates semantic entailment between the query and the description set to output valid mask indices. This modular architecture enables zero-cost cognitive upgrades (swapping the LLM without retraining) and two operational modes: autonomous reasoning segmentation for implicit queries and prompt-constrained referring segmentation for explicit attribute grounding. The same pipeline doubles as a scalable data engine, automatically generating GEAR-131K (38k+ images, 656k QA-mask pairs with explicit logic chains) across five reasoning categories, which then supervises lightweight end-to-end students (LISA-13B, YOLOv8s) through their native task-specific losses in a pure data-distillation paradigm.

## Results

On ReasonSeg, GEAR-Seg achieves a zero-shot gIoU of 57.5 and ncIoU of 54.0, outperforming the fully fine-tuned LISA-13B (56.2 gIoU, 52.8 ncIoU) while trailing on standard cIoU (47.5 vs. 62.9) due to SAM 2's tendency to over-segment large targets. On LLM-Seg40k, GEAR-Seg reaches 52.2 gIoU, a 6.7-point margin over the fine-tuned LLM-Seg-7B (45.5 gIoU). For zero-shot fruit instance segmentation, GEAR-Seg leads the second-best method (SDM) by 13.2, 10.0, and 4.8 percentage points in mAP₅₀:₉₅ on StrawDI_Db1, Mega_Blueberry, and Mega_Peach respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRES (zero-shot referring), GEAR-Seg dominates by a wide margin, achieving 57.5 vs. 22.4 gIoU on ReasonSeg and 52.2 vs. 14.2 on LLM-Seg40k. Versus LISA, GEAR-Seg in zero-shot mode surpasses the fine-tuned LISA-13B on gIoU (57.5 vs. 56.2) and ncIoU (54.0 vs. 52.8) on ReasonSeg, though it trails substantially on raw cIoU (47.5 vs. 62.9) owing to SAM 2's over-segmentation of large objects in high-resolution images. On LLM-Seg40k, GEAR-Seg (zero-shot) outperforms the fine-tuned LLM-Seg-7B by 6.7 gIoU points (52.2 vs. 45.5) but is slightly behind on cIoU (52.9 vs. 54.2). For fruit instance segmentation, GEAR-Seg outperforms all three baselines—Grounded SAM, YOLO-World, and SDM—across every dataset and metric, with the largest gaps on StrawDI_Db1 (0.506 vs. 0.374 mAP₅₀:₉₅ over SDM) and Mega_Blueberry (0.420 vs. 0.320). In the distillation setting (Table 8), GEAR-Seg-generated pseudo-labels for YOLOv8s recover up to 93.2% of the human-annotated upper bound on Mega_Blueberry (0.714 vs. 0.766 mAP₅₀:₉₅), substantially exceeding Grounded SAM (0.662) and YOLO-World (0.623) as label sources.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant end-to-end paradigm in reasoning segmentation (LISA, LLM-Seg, PixelLM), which injects visual tokens directly into LLMs, by explicitly decoupling perception (SAM 2), semantic description (DAM), and logical deduction (a plug-and-play LLM) into three transparent modules. The pixel-to-text translation strategy draws on the agentic AI literature's decomposition of complex tasks into tool-orchestrated sub-tasks, but applies it specifically to segmentation by converting every mask into a dense, attribute-rich textual description before reasoning. On the data side, it extends prior automated generation pipelines (LLM-Seg's GPT prompts, PixelLM's LLaMA descriptions) by leveraging unconstrained SAM 2 proposals and DAM's fine-grained captions rather than relying on predefined vocabularies or existing annotations, and it introduces a five-category reasoning taxonomy (Commonsense, Functional, Manipulation-related, Part-based, Attribute-based) absent from prior benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GEAR-131K benchmark (39k images, 656k QA-mask pairs across five reasoning categories) fills a significant gap: it is the only existing dataset simultaneously supporting multi-target grounding, part-level reasoning, attribute-based differentiation, and manipulation-related scenarios at scale, making it a practical resource for embodied AI and robotic manipulation research. The data-centric distillation results—showing that pseudo-labels from the automated pipeline can match or exceed human-annotated baselines when scaled—offer a compelling path to reducing annotation costs for downstream deployment. Key open questions remain: the cascading error analysis reveals that DAM hallucination accounts for ~79% of failures in long-tail dense segmentation, highlighting that LVLM reliability in fine-grained physical attribute perception is still a critical bottleneck; additionally, the ~34-second per-image inference latency, while amortizable for offline generation, remains impractical for real-time robotic control without the distillation step.
