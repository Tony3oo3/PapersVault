## Problem

Prior security analyses of face recognition systems (FRSs) have focused on medium-security thresholds (FMR = 10⁻³), leaving high-security regimes (FMR = 10⁻⁶, confidence score ≥ 99) largely unexplored. Existing score-based impersonation attacks either require unrealistic query budgets (4,000–300,000) or their success rate drops to near zero under stringent thresholds, creating a critical gap in understanding FRS vulnerability in the exact settings recommended for law-enforcement and financial applications.

## Input / Output

The adversary submits 100 non-adaptive facial images (an Orthogonal Face Set) to the target FRS API and receives only scalar confidence scores (0–100 for AWS Rekognition), which must be numerically converted to cosine similarities since the internal scoring formula is undisclosed. The output is a single 128×128 reconstructed facial image that, when submitted to the target FRS, yields a confidence score exceeding the high-security threshold (e.g., 99 for AWS). No adaptive or iterative querying is performed; all queries are fixed in advance, making the attack resistant to rate-limit detection.

## Method

The attack estimates the enrolled face's template in a surrogate model's embedding space by solving a linear system Ay = s − ε, where A is the OFS template matrix and s is the score vector, then inverts the estimated template to a face image. Three error sources that cause failure under high thresholds are addressed: (E1) metric distortion between surrogate and target spaces is corrected via a correction matrix S⁻¹ (where S is the pairwise similarity matrix of OFS elements), reinterpreting the estimate as a projection in a Σ-weighted metric; (E2) the subspace projection gap is minimized by replacing the baseline OFS with the top-100 PCA principal components of MS1MV3 embeddings, which is optimal for average projection error; (E3) inversion error is reduced by SPNet, a new inverse model that merges NbNet's architecture with StyleGAN-style AdaIN mapping, DSCasConv skip connections, and micro-level modifications (GELU activations, pixel-wise normalization, no final tanh). Trained on MS1MV3 with combined pixel and multi-FRS identity losses, SPNet achieves 0.9782 inversion precision, enabling 92.8% ASR on AWS Rekognition at threshold 99 with only 100 queries.

## Results

Against AWS Rekognition at the high-security confidence threshold of 99 (recommended for law enforcement), the proposed SPNet+CP attack achieves 92.80% ASR on LFW, 95.24% on CFP-FP, and 96.87% on AgeDB, using only 100 black-box score queries per identity. On open-source FRSs at FMR=10⁻⁶, the attack achieves 32.63%–89.27% ASR in the black-box setting and 76.83% in the white-box setting. The baseline method (Kim et al. [36]) drops to 0.60% ASR at threshold 99 on LFW, whereas the proposed method maintains >92% ASR under the same conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Kim et al. [36] (IEEE S&P 2024), a non-adaptive score-based attack using 100 queries. At the high-security threshold of 99, the reproduced baseline achieves only 0.60% ASR on LFW, while the proposed SPNet+CP reaches 92.80%—a gap of over 92 percentage points. Even at the moderate threshold of 90, the baseline drops to 12.54% versus 99.83% for the proposed method. Three iterative score-based attacks (Park et al. [49], Razzhigaev et al. [52], Razzhigaev et al. [53]) were also adapted to the 100-query regime; all achieve only ~1% ASR even at the looser FMR=10⁻⁴ threshold, confirming that gradient-free iterative methods are infeasible under practical rate limits. The paper's ablation further shows that each proposed technique (correction matrix, PCA-based OFS, SPNet) independently contributes to closing the gap, with the correction matrix and PCA-based OFS alone lifting ASR from 0.60% to 56.10% at threshold 99, and SPNet providing the final jump to 92.80%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-adaptive score-based face reconstruction framework of Kim et al. [36] (IEEE S&P 2024), which demonstrated that 100 orthogonal face set (OFS) queries suffice to estimate a target template in a surrogate embedding space. The key departure is a systematic decomposition of the attack pipeline into three error sources (metric distortion E1, subspace projection gap E2, and inversion error E3) and targeted fixes for each: a correction matrix (building on [35]) to tame metric distortion, PCA-based OFS to minimize projection gap, and a new inverse model (SPNet) that merges AdaIN style-mapping [47] with DSCasConv skip connections [58] atop the NbNet architecture [41]. Unlike prior score-based attacks requiring 4,000–300,000 adaptive queries, this work maintains the 100-query non-adaptive budget while extending feasibility from moderate (FMR=10⁻³) to high-security (FMR=10⁻⁶) thresholds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is stark: the confidence threshold of 99 recommended by AWS for law-enforcement use-cases can be breached with only 100 black-box queries, undermining the assumption that high-security system configurations alone ensure robustness. The paper identifies partial mitigations (stronger metric distortion via [35], uniform template distributions via [14]) but shows they reduce rather than eliminate the vulnerability, leaving open the question of whether any FRS architecture can be inherently resistant to geometric score-based attacks. Further open questions include the feasibility of physical-world attacks (print/occlusion + presentation attack detection), systematic characterization of surrogate-target misalignment across architectures, and whether adaptive query detection can distinguish the non-adaptive OFS pattern from legitimate traffic.
