## Problem

Existing LiDAR self-supervised learning methods define their objectives exclusively on measured (visible) LiDAR returns, leaving occluded and no-return regions unconstrained. This visible-surface bias is sufficient for per-point tasks like segmentation but creates a systematic transfer gap to 3D object detection, where pseudo-instance centroids are biased toward visible surfaces rather than true geometric centers, and object extent must be inferred from incomplete measurements.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse LiDAR point clouds (e.g., 32-beam scans from nuScenes) and generates two independently augmented views, one of which is randomly masked at 60% ratio with 1m mask size to induce pseudo-occlusion. It produces pretrained sparse per-voxel representations from a PTv3 or CenterPoint CNN encoder, which serve as a frozen or fine-tunable backbone for downstream CenterPoint 3D detection, semantic segmentation, and panoptic segmentation. The predictor additionally outputs features for hallucinated (unobserved) neighborhood voxels, extending the representation beyond the measured point set.

## Method

GhostPoint extends self-distillation with an instance-centric neighborhood sampling procedure: pseudo-instances discovered by the teacher encoder are dilated with a 3D kernel (ks=5) to form a neighborhood voxel set spanning visible, masked, and no-return regions, and a fixed-size subset is sampled for supervision. A lightweight predictor (two PTv3-style transformer blocks) infers representations for non-visible neighborhood voxels, with tokens initialized via distance-weighted KNN interpolation (k=3) from the nearest visible occupied voxels. Supervision is asymmetric: occupied voxels (including masked ones) receive targets from the teacher encoder, while truly unobserved voxels receive hallucinated targets from the teacher predictor (EMA), unifying pseudo-occlusion reversal with genuine occlusion modeling. The full loss combines encoder-level distillation on visible voxels with predictor-level distillation on non-visible voxels, using complementary Softmap (KL divergence) and center-offset objectives, trained with a two-stage warmup that stabilizes the encoder before activating the predictor branches.

## Results

Under decoder probing on nuScenes, GhostPoint achieves 59.5 mAP and 64.2 NDS, surpassing the strongest prior SSL method (PointINS) by 2.8 mAP and 1.7 NDS. Under full fine-tuning, GhostPoint reaches 67.5 mAP and 71.2 NDS on nuScenes, exceeding the fully supervised PTv3 baseline (63.8 mAP / 68.4 NDS) by 3.7 mAP. On Waymo, GhostPoint leads all SSL baselines by 2.5 mAP under probing (60.0 vs. 57.5) and by 0.6 mAP under fine-tuning (70.1 vs. 69.5). Label-efficiency experiments show GhostPoint matching full-label supervision with only 10% of annotations (63.5 mAP vs. 57.9 mAP for supervised PTv3 at 10%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GhostPoint outperforms all five compared SSL baselines (PSA, SONATA, NOMAE, DOS, PointINS) on both nuScenes and Waymo under both probing and fine-tuning protocols. The largest margin is under probing on nuScenes, where GhostPoint leads PointINS by 2.8 mAP (59.5 vs. 56.7) and DOS by 4.1 mAP (59.5 vs. 55.4). Under fine-tuning, GhostPoint surpasses the supervised PTv3 baseline by 3.7 mAP on nuScenes (67.5 vs. 63.8) and by 2.2 mAP on Waymo (70.1 vs. 67.9), indicating that the hallucination-based pretraining yields representations better aligned with 3D detection than fully supervised training. On Waymo, per-category gains are most pronounced for Pedestrian (+2.7 mAP over PointINS under probing) and Cyclist (+2.8 mAP), categories most affected by occlusion. Cross-dataset transfer (Waymo→nuScenes probing) also favors GhostPoint (43.4 mAP vs. 41.6 for PointINS), and segmentation transfer is preserved (74.2 mIoU vs. 74.4 for PointINS), confirming the gains are not at the expense of per-point quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

GhostPoint extends the self-distillation lineage for LiDAR point clouds (DOS, PointINS) by adding a predictor-level supervision branch that operates on unobserved voxels generated via instance voxel dilation. While prior SSL methods (Sonata, DOS, NOMAE, PointINS) define all learning signals exclusively on measured LiDAR returns, GhostPoint introduces a neighborhood predictor that hallucinates latent features in occluded and no-return regions adjacent to discovered pseudo-instances. The conceptual inspiration draws from point cloud completion, but is repurposed into a fully self-supervised setting where the teacher predictor's own EMA outputs serve as hallucination targets, eliminating the need for ground-truth geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a systematic transfer gap: SSL representations that approach supervised quality on segmentation underperform substantially on 3D detection, and attributes this to a representation mismatch between surface-aligned pretraining objectives and box-level detection labels. This framing is useful for the broader SSL-for-3D-perception community and suggests that future methods should explicitly account for unobserved structure. Limitations include a residual gap to fully supervised training under probing, a 30% pretraining overhead, and no evaluation on other occlusion-affected modalities (e.g., radar), leaving open whether the hallucination mechanism generalizes beyond LiDAR.
