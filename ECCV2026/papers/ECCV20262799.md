## Problem

Existing neural cloth simulators such as NeuralClothSim rely on a 2D curvilinear parameterisation of the garment surface, which restricts them to single-panel cloths and makes multi-panel garments with seams and holes intractable. Additionally, these methods are tied to a single input representation (high-quality triangle meshes), preventing direct use of point clouds, 3D Gaussians, or implicit fields from off-the-shelf reconstruction pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262799/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the surface normal field and Weingarten map (curvature tensor) at each sample point, extracted differently per modality: from UDF gradients for implicit inputs, area-weighted vertex normals and cotangent Laplacians for meshes, PCA for point clouds, and Gaussian splat orientations for 3D Gaussians. Boundary conditions are specified as a scalar distance function in 3D (zero on pinned regions, one elsewhere), and external forces (e.g., gravity) are applied as body loads. The output is a continuous neural deformation field (5-layer MLP with SIREN activations) that can be queried at arbitrary spatial resolution post-training, from which a deformed mesh is extracted by displacing reference vertices.

## Method

NeuralGarSim reformulates the nonlinear Kirchhoff-Love shell model entirely in 3D Euclidean space using Tangential Differential Calculus (TDC), eliminating the need for any 2D parametric domain. The deformation is a coordinate-based MLP with SIREN activations that maps 3D reference points to displacements; membrane and bending strains are computed from tangential directional gradients of the displacement and director-difference fields, then projected onto the tangent plane via the TDC projection operator. Training minimises the total potential energy (internal elastic energy plus external work) via Monte Carlo quadrature over uniformly sampled surface points, with the internal energy evaluated using either an isotropic linear model or an orthotropic StVK model integrated through thickness via Simpson quadrature. This parameterisation-free formulation naturally handles arbitrary topologies (multiple panels, seams, holes) and supports all four input modalities under a unified physical model, while training 2–4× faster than NeuralClothSim by avoiding Christoffel-symbol differentiation on a 2D domain.

## Results

On the Belytschko shell benchmarks, NeuralGarSim matches analytical solutions closely: 0.487 (Square Plate, exact), 0.2987 (Scordelis-Lo Roof vs. 0.3024 analytical), and 1.820e-5 (Pinched Cylinder vs. 1.825e-5 analytical). Training is 2–4× faster than NeuralClothSim, with an average of 31 min 35 s versus 1 h 6 min 10 s across six test cases. In discretisation-independence experiments, NeuralGarSim achieves wrinkle-region Chamfer distances of 0.011–0.013, roughly 3× lower than DiffCloth (0.034–0.035), confirming resolution-agnostic wrinkle patterns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeuralClothSim [37], NeuralGarSim matches or slightly improves physical accuracy on all three Belytschko benchmarks (e.g., 0.2987 vs. 0.2965 on Scordelis-Lo) while training 2–4× faster (average 31 min vs. 1 h 6 min), attributed to eliminating 2D parametric-domain differentiation. Against DiffCloth [50] and DiffARCSim [52] in discretisation-independence tests, NeuralGarSim produces wrinkle-region Chamfer distances of 0.011–0.013 versus 0.034–0.035 for DiffCloth, a roughly 3× improvement, while both mesh-based baselines show visible wrinkle shifts across mesh resolutions. Against Guo et al. [26] and Bastek et al. [4] on the Belytschko benchmarks, NeuralGarSim is the only method reporting all three test cases and matches analytical values to within 1.2% (Scordelis-Lo) and 0.3% (Pinched Cylinder). MeshGraphNets [68] is reported only in the appendix as a learned dynamics surrogate rather than a quasistatic energy minimiser, so no direct numerical comparison is made in the main text. The multi-modal evaluation (Table 4) has no direct baseline comparison since no other method in the set supports UDF, point-cloud, or 3D Gaussian inputs natively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends NeuralClothSim [37], which first coupled neural deformation fields with Kirchhoff-Love shell energy minimisation but was constrained to a 2D curvilinear parametric domain supporting only single-panel cloths. The key conceptual departure is the adoption of Tangential Differential Calculus (TDC) [17,72]—previously applied only to classical FEM shell discretisations—to formulate the full nonlinear Green-Lagrange strain tensors directly in ℝ³, eliminating all dependence on parametric coordinates, Christoffel symbols, and metric tensors. The method also builds on differentiable unsigned distance fields (DUDF) [20] for implicit surface representation and on 3D Gaussian splatting [41] as a novel input modality, making it the first neural cloth simulator to accept arbitrary 3D representations without UV unwrapping or topology-specific preprocessing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the 2D parameterisation bottleneck, NeuralGarSim unlocks multi-panel garments with seams and holes for neural simulation—a setting that was fundamentally inaccessible to NeuralClothSim and that mesh-based differentiable simulators handle only through explicit remeshing. The multi-modal input support (UDF, mesh, point cloud, 3D Gaussians) makes the method directly compatible with off-the-shelf reconstruction pipelines like GaussianGarments, bridging a practical gap between 3D reconstruction and physics-based simulation. However, the method remains quasistatic: it lacks inertia, damping, and collision handling, so it cannot yet produce dynamic cloth animation. Extending the TDC-neural-field framework to full dynamics, robust self-collision, and inverse problems (e.g., material parameter recovery) are the natural next steps that would determine whether this formulation becomes the standard for neural garment simulation.
