## Problem

World-model-based driving planners suffer from two bottlenecks: limited zero-shot generalization across datasets and sensor domains, and inconsistency between imagined visual futures and generated trajectories due to loosely coupled or cascaded video-action pipelines. The paper asks whether large-scale pretrained video generation models, which already encode rich spatiotemporal and physical priors, can serve as a foundation for generalizable driving video-action models that jointly produce coherent future scenes and aligned driving trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260117/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 4 history frames at 832×480 resolution (2 FPS) from a single front camera, a frozen text-encoded language instruction (high-level command), and the current ego velocity (vx, vy). It outputs a chunk of K=8 sequential 3-D actions (x, y position and yaw per step, covering 4 s) together with N=8 future video frames as latent representations decoded by the VAE. At inference only 2 flow-matching sampling steps are used, and the rolling-horizon setup re-encodes the updated history buffer at each step to chain short continuations into long-horizon rollouts.

## Method

DRIVEVA fine-tunes the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video generation backbone (3D causal VAE + DiT) so that a single DiT decoder jointly denoises future video latents and action tokens within one shared latent generative process, rather than treating video prediction and trajectory generation as separate branches. The condition block (history latents from the causal VAE plus MLP-embedded ego state) is kept noise-free, while the generative target block (future video latents concatenated with action tokens) is denoised via a conditional flow-matching objective with linear interpolation. Text instructions are injected through cross-attention to keep the spatiotemporal token stream compact. A progressive video-continuation module recursively rolls out future clips to preserve long-horizon structural consistency. The key empirical finding is that video-level supervision is the primary driver of planning gains (PDMS jumps from 71.4 to 90.9 when video loss is added), and full fine-tuning of the 5B backbone outperforms LoRA or from-scratch training, confirming that adapting the pretrained video prior under joint video-action supervision is essential.

## Results

On NAVSIM v1, DRIVEVA achieves a PDM score of 90.9, surpassing the strongest world-model baseline PWM (88.1) by 2.8 points and the best traditional end-to-end method DiffusionDrive (88.1) by 2.8 points. In zero-shot cross-dataset transfer to nuScenes (trained only on NAVSIM), DRIVEVA reduces average L2 error from 3.99 m to 0.84 m (−78.9%) and average collision rate from 0.36% to 0.06% (−83.3%) relative to PWM. On Bench2Drive (CARLA v2) in a real-to-simulation zero-shot setting, DRIVEVA lowers average L2 error from 2.80 m to 1.33 m (−52.5%) and collision rate from 3.76% to 1.79% (−52.4%) compared to PWM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRIVEVA outperforms all 20 compared baselines on NAVSIM PDMS. Among traditional end-to-end methods, it leads DiffusionDrive (88.1) by 2.8 points and ReCogDrive-IL (86.5) by 4.4 points, while using only a single front camera (no LiDAR, no multi-view). Among world-model methods, it surpasses the closest competitor WoTE (88.3, which uses multi-modal inputs including LiDAR) by 2.6 points, and PWM (88.1) by 2.8 points. The zero-shot cross-domain results are the paper's strongest differentiator: on nuScenes, DRIVEVA (0.84 m avg L2, 0.06% collision) beats PWM (3.99 m, 0.36%) by 78.9% and 83.3% respectively, and also outperforms DriveVLA-W0 (1.43 m, 0.77%). On Bench2Drive (CARLA), DRIVEVA (1.33 m, 1.79%) reduces L2 error by 52.5% and collision rate by 52.4% over PWM (2.80 m, 3.76%), and by 55.7% and 29.0% over DriveVLA-W0 (3.00 m, 2.52%). Notably, DRIVEVA even surpasses methods fine-tuned on nuScenes (e.g., UniAD 1.03 m, GenAD 0.91 m) without any target-domain training, demonstrating that the unified video-action formulation learns genuinely transferable planning priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRIVEVA builds on the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video generation backbone and the DiT architecture, repurposing a large-scale video diffusion model as a driving world model. It departs from prior world-model-based planners (LAW, Epona, PWM, DriveVLA-W0) that treat future video prediction as an auxiliary signal or loosely coupled branch, instead unifying video latents and action tokens in a single shared flow-matching generative process. The paper also positions itself against VLA models pretrained on static image-text pairs, arguing that video-level spatiotemporal priors are necessary for robust planning generalization that VLM-based approaches cannot provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that video supervision is the primary driver of planning gains (+19.5 PDMS over action-only training) challenges the prevailing assumption in the field that video prediction is merely an auxiliary or interpretability aid, suggesting a rethinking of how world models should be trained for driving. The strong zero-shot transfer (78.9% lower L2, 83.3% lower collision on nuScenes; 52.5%/52.4% on CARLA) without target-domain fine-tuning is practically significant for deployment across diverse sensor configurations and geographies. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-camera inputs, higher frame rates, and real-time closed-loop deployment, and whether the gains persist when the video backbone is replaced or when the driving domain shifts more substantially (e.g., different vehicle dynamics).
