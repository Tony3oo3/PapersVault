## Problem

In reference-based remote sensing super-resolution, existing diffusion-based methods face a fundamental trade-off: unconstrained interaction between low-resolution (LR) and reference (Ref) conditions in a unified attention mechanism causes inter-source competition, leading to either over-reliance on reference textures (producing artifacts in changed regions) or under-utilization of reference information (insufficient detail recovery). Prior approaches that mitigate this by introducing explicit land cover change priors (e.g., Ref-Diff) degrade in practical settings where precise change detection masks are unavailable, leaving the architectural design for balancing LR structural fidelity and Ref texture exploitation as an open problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution remote sensing image (bicubically downsampled at ×8, ×10, or ×16) and a spatially aligned high-resolution reference image from the same geographic location but a different acquisition time. The LR image is bicubically upsampled to the target resolution (512×512 for SECOND/FUSU/CNAM-CD, 480×480 for Real-RefRSSRD) before being encoded alongside the reference image as conditioning modalities. The output is a single high-resolution super-resolved image at the target resolution, generated via a 40-step Euler ODE solver in latent space.

## Method

DS-DiT modifies the MM-DiT architecture by replacing the text branch with LR and Ref conditioning modalities, but critically decouples their interaction: the noisy image tokens produce a single shared set of Q, K, V projections that are dispatched to two parallel joint attention paths (one with LR tokens, one with Ref tokens), and the resulting updated noisy tokens are summed, preventing direct LR–Ref competition within a single softmax. A Patch-Level Weighting (PLW) module then adaptively fuses LR and Ref features per-patch via a learned weight map (softmax over a 2-dim MLP output) injected through a zero-initialized linear layer, compensating for local detail loss in global attention. At inference, an autoguidance strategy scales the Ref-branch contribution by a coefficient λ (λ=1 for strong, λ=0 for weak reference) and extrapolates the velocity field between the two predictions, amplifying reference texture utilization without additional training. The model is trained with a conditional rectified flow (flow matching) objective, initialized from SD3 pretrained MM-DiT denoising branch weights, and trained for 80k steps on 4 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On SECOND ×8, DS-DiT achieves LPIPS 0.2174, FID 15.87, and DISTS 0.1090, outperforming the next-best M³-DiT (0.2417, 22.66, 0.1356) by 10%, 30%, and 19% respectively. At the more challenging ×16 scale, DS-DiT maintains its lead with LPIPS 0.2905, FID 21.58, and DISTS 0.1334, while GAN-based baselines degrade severely (e.g., TTSR FID jumps to 134.68). On the real-world cross-sensor Real-RefRSSRD dataset (×10), DS-DiT achieves the best scores across all five metrics (LPIPS 0.3261, FID 31.56, DISTS 0.1809, CLIPIQA 0.6456, MUSIQ 57.09). On no-reference metrics, DS-DiT slightly trails Ref-Diff on MUSIQ (49.04 vs. 51.36 on SECOND ×8), which the authors attribute to Ref-Diff generating richer but less faithful textures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DS-DiT decisively outperforms both GAN-based baselines (TTSR and DATSR) across all reference-based metrics on every dataset and scale; at ×16 on SECOND, TTSR's FID degrades to 134.68 while DS-DiT achieves 21.58. Among diffusion-based methods, DS-DiT surpasses CoSeR* and CreatiLayout* on all reference-based metrics (LPIPS, FID, DISTS) by margins of 5–25%, confirming the benefit of the decoupled siamese attention over both U-Net cross-attention and joint M³-DiT attention. The closest competitor is Ref-Diff, which uses explicit land-cover change priors; DS-DiT beats it on all reference-based metrics (e.g., FID 15.87 vs. 28.48 on SECOND ×8) but trails slightly on no-reference MUSIQ (49.04 vs. 51.36), which the authors explain as Ref-Diff producing visually richer but less faithful textures. The M³-DiT ablation (unconstrained three-modality attention) is consistently outperformed by DS-DiT, validating that decoupling LR–Ref interaction mitigates inter-source competition. On the real-world cross-sensor Real-RefRSSRD benchmark, DS-DiT achieves the best score on all five metrics, demonstrating robustness beyond synthetic degradation settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MM-DiT architecture from Stable Diffusion 3, repurposing the text conditioning branch into two parallel image-conditioning branches (LR and Ref) for reference-based super-resolution. It departs from the straightforward M³-DiT design—where all three modalities interact freely in a single joint attention—by introducing a decoupled siamese structure inspired by CreatiLayout, where the noisy image tokens share a single Q/K/V set dispatched to two independent attention paths. The training paradigm follows rectified flow (Liu et al.), and the inference-time autoguidance strategy adapts classifier-free guidance ideas (Ho & Salimans; Karras et al.) to the siamese setting without requiring unconditional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core practical limitation in remote sensing RefSR: the difficulty of selectively exploiting reference textures in unchanged regions while suppressing erroneous transfers in changed regions, all without requiring external change-detection masks. The training-free autoguidance and the PLW module offer practical, plug-in improvements that could be adopted in other multi-condition diffusion settings. A key open question the authors identify is how to extend the per-patch weighting to spatially misaligned reference images (e.g., different viewpoints or regions), which limits generalizability beyond the spatially-aligned RS setting where the method was designed.
