## Problem

Current Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) rely on in-modality non-RGB data during training to handle modalities like thermal or depth, creating a generalization gap when encountering truly unseen visual modalities. The paper asks whether LMMs can generalize across versatile visual modalities without any in-modality training data, by exploiting the fact that all modalities are distinct samplings of the same physical world sharing invariant semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261718/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a non-RGB image (thermal, depth, X-ray, optical flow, or a fabricated modality) paired with a text question; for modality-aware understanding tasks, a modality context string describing the physical meaning of the sensor (e.g., color-to-temperature mapping) is inserted between the image and the question. The model outputs a single option letter for multiple-choice VQA or a free-form image description. The benchmark (VVM-Bench) spans 6 modalities and 11 VQA sub-tasks split into perception (no modality context) and understanding (with modality context) subsets.

## Method

VVM-Tuning is a two-stage instruction-tuning framework built on a fabricated modality synthesis pipeline. First, RGB images are converted to grayscale, their histograms are randomized via cubic spline interpolation and random flipping, then painted with one of 22 pseudo-color maps and augmented with noise/blurring to produce diverse non-RGB-like images while preserving scene semantics. The training data is split into modality-unaware perception tasks (semantic description/QA and photographic description/QA) that disentangle invariant scene semantics from modality-varying visual appearances, and modality-aware understanding tasks (modality description/QA) that use synthesized textual modality contexts to teach the model to link physical attributes (e.g., temperature, depth) to specific visual cues. All tasks are trained jointly via standard SFT cross-entropy loss over ~50k samples for one epoch, with 12k RGB instruction samples retained to preserve general capabilities. The key insight is that by learning to separate what is invariant (semantics) from what varies (appearance) across fabricated modalities, the model acquires a modality-agnostic perception base that, combined with in-context modality knowledge at inference, enables zero-shot adaptation to unseen modalities.

## Results

On VVM-Bench's perception subset (no modality context), VVM-Tuning yields an average 8.2% accuracy improvement across 5 base models and 6 visual modalities (3 real: Thermal, Depth, X-ray; 3 fabricated: Optical Flow, Synthesized 1, Synthesized 2). The largest gain is LLaVA-1.5 7B at +13.6% (60.4%→74.1%), while the smallest is Qwen-3-VL 4B at +5.3% (77.6%→82.8%). On the understanding subset (with modality context), the average improvement is 3.8%, with LLaVA-1.5 gaining +5.6% and Qwen-3-VL 4B gaining +2.0%. All models achieve at least 6% improvement on real modalities, confirming cross-modal generalization from synthetic training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares VVM-Tuning against three baseline LMM families: LLaVA-1.5 (7B), Qwen-2.5-VL (3B, 7B), and Qwen-3-VL (4B, 8B), each evaluated before and after the proposed fine-tuning. VVM-Tuning consistently outperforms every baseline on both perception and understanding subsets across all six modalities. The gains are inversely correlated with base model strength: LLaVA-1.5 7B benefits most (+13.6% perception, +5.6% understanding), while the strongest baseline Qwen-3-VL 4B shows the smallest gain (+5.3% perception, +2.0% understanding), suggesting diminishing returns as the base model already encodes more robust visual priors. On real modalities (Thermal, Depth, X-ray, Optical Flow), all models improve by at least 6% on perception, validating that synthetic training transfers to physically grounded sensing. On fabricated modalities (Synthesized 1, Synthesized 2), improvements are larger (7.5–14.2% average Δ), indicating the model learns appearance-agnostic perception rather than memorizing specific modality patterns. The understanding subset shows a notable modality-context effect: removing context drops accuracy by up to 32.75% (Depth), confirming that modality knowledge is not injected during training but is instead supplied at inference via prompt.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual instruction tuning paradigm (LLaVA) by decomposing visual-language alignment into modality-unaware perception (semantic + photographic) and modality-aware understanding (modality context), then training on fabricated non-RGB images rather than real in-modality data. It departs from prior non-RGB LMM work (Infrared-LLaVA, SpatialBot, EarthGPT) that fine-tunes on domain-specific paired data, and from cross-modal binding approaches (ImageBind, PandaGPT) that require RGB+non-RGB pairs, by targeting zero-shot generalization to entirely unseen modalities. The synthetic image pipeline (histogram warping via cubic spline + random color maps + augmentations) and the VVM-Bench benchmark (extending VS-TDX with fabricated modalities and a perception/understanding split) are the concrete methodological contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of whether LMMs can generalize to unseen visual modalities without any in-modality training data, providing both a training recipe and a dedicated benchmark (VVM-Bench) that the community can adopt. The consistent 8.2%/3.8% improvements across five model families validate the decomposition, but the synthetic gap in understanding tasks (fabricated modalities improve less than real ones) reveals that physical meaning is harder to fabricate than visual appearance, leaving open whether truly novel sensing modalities (e.g., LiDAR, hyperspectral) can be handled. A key open question is whether scaling the synthetic diversity or incorporating physics-informed generation can close this gap.
