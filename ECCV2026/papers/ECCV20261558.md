## Problem

Extracting routable road networks from satellite imagery requires accurate topology recovery beyond pixel-level segmentation, but two structural challenges remain poorly addressed: overpasses create visually overlapping but topologically disconnected crossings that induce false shortcuts, and occlusions from trees or buildings break visual continuity, demanding long-range structural reasoning. Prior holistic methods restrict connectivity reasoning to local neighborhoods around each vertex, missing global context, while fully dense pairwise approaches are computationally expensive and structurally uninformed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB satellite image at 1 m spatial resolution (2048×2048 for City-scale/Global-scale, 400×400 for SpaceNet) and produces a vectorized road network graph as a set of sparse vertices with binary connectedness labels on candidate edges, plus an overpass/underpass mask identifying multi-level crossing endpoints. During inference, a sliding-window strategy handles arbitrarily large regions, and overlapping predictions on the same edge are averaged to yield a final confidence score.

## Method

LineGraph2Road uses a pre-trained SAM ViT-B encoder with a lightweight decoder producing three probability maps (keypoint, road, overpass/underpass), from which a Coupled NMS algorithm extracts sparse vertices while preserving topology-critical endpoints near crossings. These vertices form a global but sparse Euclidean graph by connecting all pairs within a distance threshold d_nei, balancing long-range context with local structure. The key architectural insight is transforming this graph into its line graph, where each node represents a candidate edge, and applying a 3-layer Graph Transformer to perform binary node classification for connectedness prediction. This reformulation lets the model reason directly over edge-edge structural relationships (e.g., whether two candidate edges share a vertex and should both be present) rather than aggregating endpoint embeddings, which obscures structural differences. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a combined BCE loss on segmentation masks and edge connectedness labels, using random patch cropping and 90° rotation augmentation.

## Results

On City-scale, LineGraph2Road with the overpass head achieves a TOPO-F1 of 83.77 and APLS of 70.40, setting a new state-of-the-art across all four metrics. On SpaceNet, the non-overpass variant reaches TOPO-F1 of 84.24 and APLS of 73.94, the best balance of precision (92.82) and recall (77.11) among all methods. On Global-scale (in-domain), the overpass variant attains APLS of 68.70, a substantial improvement over the previous best of 62.19 (SAM-Road++), while also leading in recall (50.85) and F1 (64.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LineGraph2Road outperforms all eight baselines on TOPO-F1 across both City-scale and SpaceNet. On City-scale, the overpass variant leads SAM-Road++ (80.01) by 3.76 F1 points and RNGDet++ (78.44) by 5.33 points, while also improving APLS from 68.34 to 70.40. On SpaceNet, the non-overpass variant achieves the highest F1 (84.24) and APLS (73.94), surpassing SAM-Road++ (81.57 / 73.44) and SAM-Road (80.52 / 71.64); its precision (92.82) is marginally below SAM-Road++ (93.68) but its recall (77.11) exceeds all competitors. On Global-scale, the overpass variant improves APLS by 6.51 points over SAM-Road++ (68.70 vs. 62.19) and F1 by 1.72 points (64.05 vs. 62.33), though precision is slightly lower (89.65 vs. 88.95 for SAM-Road++). The paper attributes the non-overpass variant's edge on SpaceNet to the scarcity of overpass structures in that dataset (387 crossings over 408 km² vs. 4,180 over 720 km² in City-scale), where the dedicated overpass head adds little benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM-Road / SAM-Road++ lineage, which pairs a Segment Anything encoder with a transformer-based connectivity decoder, but departs from their local-only neighborhood reasoning by constructing a global but sparse Euclidean graph over all detected keypoints. The key architectural departure is the line-graph transformation: rather than predicting edge presence by aggregating endpoint embeddings (as in standard GNN link prediction or graph autoencoders), each candidate edge becomes a node in the line graph and connectivity is predicted via node classification with a Graph Transformer. The explicit overpass/underpass segmentation head and Coupled NMS strategy are novel additions not present in prior SAM-based road extractors, addressing a failure mode (non-planar crossings) that local reasoning alone cannot resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Accurate road topology recovery is critical for navigation, disaster response, and urban planning, and overpass misconnections remain a persistent source of routing errors in deployed systems. The line-graph formulation offers a clean, low-overhead architectural principle that could transfer to other image-to-graph extraction tasks (e.g., power grids, river networks) where edge-level structural reasoning is needed. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large regions without the sliding-window averaging heuristic, how it performs in regions with dense multi-level highway interchanges beyond what City-scale captures, and whether the relatively low overpass detection F1 (~50%) limits practical deployment in complex urban environments.
