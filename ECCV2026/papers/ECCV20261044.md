## Problem

Prior maps used to augment online vectorized HD map construction are frequently biased due to outdated information, road reconstruction, or sensor misalignment, yet existing methods either treat priors as fully reliable or handle only limited noise patterns. The critical gap is the absence of explicit, spatially-varying reliability modeling that can identify which regions of a prior map are trustworthy versus erroneous and propagate that knowledge to guide both query initialization and feature fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261044/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes six surrounding RGB camera images (360° FOV, 2 Hz) and a biased prior map provided in both rasterized (H×W×3 channel) and vectorized (N instances of ordered point sequences with semantic types) formats. It outputs a vectorized HD map consisting of lane dividers, road boundaries, and pedestrian crossings as ordered point sets within a [-15,15] m × [-30,30] m BEV range, evaluated via Chamfer-distance-based AP at 0.5/1.0/1.5 m thresholds. During training, the rasterized prior is randomly mixed at the patch level between the biased and ground-truth versions (Bernoulli sampling), while supervision labels are always computed from the GT–biased pair to decouple supervision from input corruption.

## Method

PriorMaskMap comprises three tightly coupled modules. First, the Prior-Aware Confidence Estimator (PACE) concatenates BEV features with the rasterized prior and feeds them through a U-Net head to predict a per-grid three-class confidence map (reliable, erroneous, empty). Second, the Dual-Prior Query Generator (DPQG) encodes the vectorized prior via hierarchical intra/inter-instance attention and the rasterized prior via positional+label embeddings, then applies a binary mask derived from PACE's confidence to zero out unreliable points before merging both token streams and updating learnable queries via cross-attention. Third, the Confidence-Guided Prior Fusion (CGPF) performs multi-band spatial diffusion of the confidence field (tighter kernels for high-reliability bands, wider kernels for low-reliability bands) to produce a soft mask that modulates the injection of multi-view perspective features into the BEV representation, followed by a shallow CNN that computes a late-stage fusion weight by comparing the enhanced BEV features against the rasterized prior, yielding a refined confidence mask that reweights the prior before final BEV context generation for the transformer decoder.

## Results

On nuScenes with biased priors (mixed topology-altering corruptions), PriorMaskMap achieves 81.4 mAP at 24 epochs, surpassing the strongest retrained baseline HRMapNet† (79.7 mAP) by 1.7 points and all no-prior baselines (best: DAMap at 72.6 mAP). At 110 epochs, PriorMaskMap reaches 85.0 mAP versus HRMapNet†'s 83.4 mAP, maintaining a 1.6-point advantage. Per-class, the method achieves 85.1 AP on lane dividers and 80.9 AP on road boundaries, with pedestrian-crossing AP at 78.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the biased-prior regime (the paper's primary evaluation), PriorMaskMap (81.4 mAP) outperforms the strongest retrained baseline HRMapNet† (79.7 mAP) by 1.7 points, and substantially exceeds SQD-MapNet† (55.5) and PrevPredMap† (64.3), which degrade severely when their priors are corrupted. Against no-prior baselines, PriorMaskMap surpasses the best (DAMap, 72.6 mAP) by 8.8 points, demonstrating that even a biased prior, when selectively exploited, provides net benefit. Among prior-based methods evaluated in their original (clean-prior) configurations, PriorMaskMap's biased-prior score (81.4) exceeds MapTracker (76.1), UniPrevPred (74.0), and PriorFusion (70.4), indicating that explicit confidence estimation recovers more value from imperfect priors than methods designed for clean ones. On the spatially non-overlapping Geo-split, PriorMaskMap maintains a 3.4 mAP advantage over HRMapNet† (64.2 vs. 60.8), and on the StreamMapNet split the gap widens to 4.9 mAP (64.6 vs. 59.7), confirming robust generalization. Per-bias-type analysis shows the largest gains over HRMapNet† on lane widening (+3.7 mAP) and narrowing (+2.7 mAP), while lane addition and reduction yield smaller but consistent improvements (+0.6 and +0.5 mAP respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

PriorMaskMap builds directly on HRMapNet's rasterized prior embedding and training protocol, and on PriorDrive's hierarchical (intra/inter-instance) attention for encoding vectorized map points. It departs from the broader prior-enhanced mapping line (P-MapNet, Uni-PrevPredMap, MapEX) by rejecting the assumption that prior maps are fully reliable or only subject to limited noise patterns. Instead, it introduces an explicit per-grid confidence estimation stage (PACE) that classifies prior regions as reliable, erroneous, or blank, and uses this signal to gate both query initialization (DPQG) and BEV feature fusion (CGPF)—a spatially-varying reliability mechanism that prior methods have not modeled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical and increasingly relevant gap: as autonomous driving systems rely more on pre-existing HD/SD maps, those maps inevitably become stale or inconsistent with the current road environment, and naively trusting them degrades performance. By showing that even a prior with 60% of lanes topologically corrupted can still yield 81.4 mAP (vs. 72.6 for the best no-prior method), the paper demonstrates that selective prior exploitation is both feasible and beneficial. Open questions include whether the confidence estimation generalizes to real-world map changes beyond the synthetic Syn-D-Maps corruptions, whether temporal fusion of sequential predictions (mentioned as future work) can further close the gap to clean-prior performance, and whether the approach extends to other map element types or sensor modalities.
