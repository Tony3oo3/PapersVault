## Problem

Existing multimodal trackers perform cross-modal fusion only at specific layers using quadratic-complexity attention or weak parameter-light adapters, while ignoring the complementary information across different feature layers within each modality. This limits the propagation of cross-modal cues and causes information loss when relying solely on the final layer, degrading tracking robustness under occlusion, illumination changes, and fast motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260616/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker receives a pair of co-registered images (RGB plus one auxiliary modality: thermal infrared, depth, or event) which are converted into token sequences and fed through a shared 12-layer Transformer backbone. A frozen fully convolutional network head predicts a single target bounding box per frame, with the template updated across frames during tracking. The system runs at approximately 43.6 FPS on an NVIDIA RTX 4090, trained with 60,000 template-search pairs per epoch.

## Method

PSMTrack introduces three progressive modules built on selective state space models. First, DBME is deployed after every Transformer block: it computes the absolute difference between RGB and X features, models it with a Mamba block gated by tanh, and injects the result back via bidirectional (forward + reversed) SSM scanning in a residual manner, progressively enhancing each layer's representation. Second, CLI uses a modality-shared router to dynamically select top-k (k=3) layers, then applies SCSA denoising and a modified NC-SSD (with adaptive 1×1 convolution and SCConv replacing depthwise convolution) to perform cross-layer interaction, compensating for information lost in the final layer. Third, HSMF concatenates the two modality features channel-wise and processes them through a local spiral Mamba (k×k patches scanned in spiral order, both inside-out and outside-in) followed by a global spiral Mamba over the entire H×W feature map, achieving near-linear-complexity cross-modal fusion that preserves local boundary details while enforcing global identity consistency.

## Results

On RGB-T tracking, PSMTrack achieves 76.5% PR and 61.2% SR on LasHeR, and 91.2% MPR and 67.3% MSR on RGBT234, setting new state-of-the-art on both benchmarks. On RGB-D tracking, it reaches 64.2% F-score on DepthTrack and 77.8% EAO on VOT-RGBD2022. On RGB-Event tracking (VisEvent), PSMTrack attains 62.7% AUC and 79.9% precision. The tracker runs at approximately 43.6 FPS on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSMTrack outperforms the closest competitor STTrack [12] on all RGB-T metrics (76.5 vs 76.0 PR on LasHeR; 91.2 vs 89.8 MPR on RGBT234) and on all RGB-D and RGB-E metrics, with margins of 0.5–1.4 points. The gap widens substantially against adapter-based methods: BAT [1] trails by 6.3 PR on LasHeR, while SDSTrack [11] and OneTracker [10] lag by 10+ PR points. On VOT-RGBD2022, PSMTrack achieves 77.8% EAO and 82.9% Accuracy, surpassing STTrack (77.6/82.5) and SDSTrack (72.8/81.2), though STTrack edges it slightly on Robustness (93.7 vs 93.5). On VisEvent, PSMTrack leads STTrack by 0.8 AUC and 1.3 precision, and outperforms the strongest non-Mamba baseline (OneTracker) by 1.9 AUC. Overall, PSMTrack achieves SOTA on all five benchmarks, with the largest gains over non-SSM methods (e.g., +25 PR over OSTrack on LasHeR) and consistent but modest improvements over the prior SOTA STTrack.

## Relation to Prior Work

PSMTrack sits at the intersection of two active research threads: multimodal visual object tracking (RGB-T, RGB-D, RGB-E) and state-space-model-based sequence modeling. It directly extends VSSD's non-causal state-space duality (NC-SSD) into a cross-layer interaction module with adaptive convolution and spatial-channel attention, and adapts Vision Mamba's bidirectional SSM scanning into a difference-guided per-layer enhancement (DBME). Compared to prior multimodal trackers (STTrack, SDSTrack, OneTracker, ViPT) that perform cross-modal fusion only at selected layers or via lightweight adapters, PSMTrack's key conceptual departure is the *progressive* design: differential cues are modeled and injected at every Transformer layer, and a spiral-scanning Mamba handles final fusion at both local and global spatial scales, rather than relying on a single fusion point or quadratic self-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in multimodal tracking—insufficient exploitation of inter-layer and inter-modal complementarity—by showing that layer-by-layer differential enhancement plus cross-layer routing yields consistent (if modest) gains across five benchmarks. The spiral-scanning formulation for 2D spatial Mamba is a reusable idea that could transfer to other dense-prediction tasks beyond tracking. However, the margins over the strongest baseline (STTrack) are small (≈0.5–1 percentage point), and the architecture stacks several SSM variants (bidirectional SSM, NC-SSD, spiral Mamba) whose individual contributions are hard to disentangle from the Transformer backbone. Open questions include whether the spiral scanning order is genuinely superior to other 2D raster or Hilbert-curve orderings, and whether the progressive per-layer Mamba overhead is justified at higher resolutions or longer sequences.
