## Problem

Computing exponential and logarithmic maps on raw geometric data (point clouds, triangle soups, noisy scans) remains challenging because classical discrete differential geometry methods require explicit mesh connectivity, while implicit-surface approaches rely on iterative projection or geodesic tracing that is expensive and numerically unstable near high curvature or thin structures. The paper asks whether these fundamental Riemannian operators can be learned directly from unstructured, noisy observations without any connectivity assumption.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261819/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of coarse boundary points (sampled from SDFs, occupancy fields, point clouds, triangle soups, or Functa) with no mesh connectivity required, normalized to [-1,1]. For a given source point p on the surface, it produces either a 3D surface point q (Exp map, given a tangent vector v) or a 3D tangent vector v (Log map, given a surface point q). All operations use relative 3D coordinates in the ambient space rather than local 2D tangent charts, avoiding the rotational ambiguity of tangent-plane bases. After a single backbone forward pass (~0.5 s for 15k vertices), each query is answered in O(1) time with roughly 5 matrix multiplications and 3 trilinear/bilinear interpolations.

## Method

The core idea is to treat Exp and Log as a spatially coherent field governed by local geometry, and to learn a continuous Geometric Embedding Field (GEF) that encodes this local structure. An octree-based CNN (O-CNN) with a 3D U-Net backbone encodes the input point cloud into a volumetric feature field; for any source point p, trilinear interpolation retrieves a local embedding z_p. This embedding then dynamically synthesizes a compact triplane representation via a FiLM-style hypernetwork mechanism, which is queried with the relative coordinates between p and the target point and decoded by a tiny MLP (ExpNet or LogNet). Training uses a multi-objective loss combining L1 reconstruction against POTPOURRI3D ground truth, Jacobian-based geometric regularizers enforcing local isometry and conformality, and a cycle-consistency term linking Exp and Log; a data-filtering step removes ~29.6% of cut-locus samples to avoid ambiguous supervision. The entire pipeline is translation-invariant and requires no explicit connectivity, enabling robust evaluation on noisy, non-uniform, and non-manifold inputs.

## Results

On the primary ShapeNet test set (1,000 shapes, 16.6k vertices), the method achieves a Mean Distance Distortion of 0.110 and Mean Angular Error of 11.1° for the Logarithmic Map, outperforming the Heat Method (0.250 / 16.7°) and the simple Projection baseline (0.115 / 14.6°). For the Exponential Map, it reaches an average Euclidean error of 0.026, beating both the Tracing baseline (0.036) and Projection (0.049). Inference time is 0.012 s per Log query and 0.019 s per Exp query, roughly 15× faster than Heat/Tracing while remaining an order of magnitude slower than the trivial Projection baseline. On noisy and non-uniform inputs the method maintains its lead in angular error (17.8° vs. 19.4° for Proj. on noisy data) and Exp error (0.038 vs. 0.055 for Proj.).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: the Vector Heat Method [43] (mesh-based, requires connectivity), a simple tangent-plane Projection, and a heuristic geodesic Tracing method for the Exp map. On the Logarithmic Map, the proposed method consistently achieves the lowest Mean Angular Error across all four test conditions (e.g., 11.1° vs. 14.6° for Proj. and 16.7° for Heat on ShapeNet), while matching or slightly exceeding Projection on Mean Distance Distortion (0.110 vs. 0.115 on ShapeNet; 0.169 vs. 0.166 on RawShapeNet, a marginal loss). For the Exponential Map, the method clearly outperforms both Tracing (0.026 vs. 0.036 on ShapeNet) and Projection (0.026 vs. 0.049), with the gap widening on noisy inputs (0.038 vs. 0.055). In terms of speed, the method is ~15× faster than Heat/Tracing (0.012–0.019 s vs. 0.10–0.42 s) but ~10× slower than the trivial Projection baseline (0.001–0.003 s). On the generalization tests (DFAUST, Kinect-v2), the method again leads in angular error (16.51° vs. 19.04° on DFAUST; 8.37° vs. 9.29° on Kinect-v2) and Exp error (0.043 vs. 0.071 on DFAUST), confirming robustness to unseen shapes and sensor noise without fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of neural shape representations (octree CNNs, triplanes) and classical Riemannian geometry operators. While GeGnn and Huberman et al. precompute fields for geodesic distances, and the Vector Heat Method / Affine Heat Method compute Exp/Log on well-formed meshes, no prior work learns these vector-valued maps directly from raw, connectivity-free point clouds. The method repurposes FiLM-style hyper-modulation and triplane representations from 3D generation literature, applying them to instantiate local geometric mapping rules conditioned on a global embedding field, thereby decoupling intrinsic geometry from explicit mesh topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for mesh connectivity, this framework makes intrinsic geometric operations (geodesic interpolation, texture parameterization, deformation transfer) accessible to noisy scans, triangle soups, and implicit surfaces where classical algorithms break down. The O(1) per-query inference after a single backbone pass is practically significant for interactive applications. Key limitations remain: accuracy is bounded by the quality of POTPOURRI3D supervision, octree/triplane resolution may smooth fine-scale curvature, and large-scale parameterization still requires a separate stitching step rather than an end-to-end solution. Future work on geometry-informed unsupervised learning and integrated global consistency could address these gaps.
