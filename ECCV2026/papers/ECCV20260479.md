## Problem

Existing cinematic background replacement systems cannot simultaneously generate backgrounds that move consistently with foreground camera motion, preserve foreground identity and fine textures, and achieve scene-consistent relighting. Most methods assume pixel-perfect mattes, produce static or misaligned backgrounds under professional camera movements (push, pan, tracking), and introduce visible compositing artifacts at boundaries. No open-source or commercial solution addresses all four requirements—motion consistency, illumination consistency, boundary robustness, and foreground consistency—within a single framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a foreground video (extracted onto green screen via SAM3 segmentation), 1–3 background reference images sampled from designated temporal locations (begin/middle/end), and an optional structured text prompt as input. It produces a 1080P (1920×1056), 24 FPS, 97-frame (~4 s) complete video where the background exhibits camera-aware motion aligned with the foreground's cinematographic dynamics, and the foreground is relit to match the target scene's illumination. During training, the foreground conditioning is deliberately illumination-perturbed (HSV jitter, contrast, color temperature, gamma) so the model learns to recover appropriate lighting from background cues rather than copying input illumination.

## Method

PAI-Studio reformulates background replacement as an in-context conditional generation task on a WAN 2.2-5B Diffusion Transformer backbone. All heterogeneous conditions—text tokens, foreground video latents, background reference latents, and the noisy output latents—are encoded by a shared video VAE and concatenated into a single token sequence processed by full bidirectional self-attention, enabling joint reasoning over foreground dynamics, reference appearance, and the evolving denoising state without separate control branches. A key mechanism is temporal positional encoding cloning: each background reference token is assigned the positional encoding of its designated output frame, and foreground tokens are aligned to their corresponding denoising frame encodings, providing explicit temporal correspondence for multi-frame background control and motion-aligned synthesis. Training uses LoRA (rank 80) with AdamW at 5×10⁻⁵ on 8 H200 GPUs for ~6,000 steps on the 30K-scale CineStudio dataset, which is reverse-engineered from real inference challenges: clips are filtered for significant camera/background motion via optical flow, foregrounds are segmented with SAM3 (retaining realistic mask imperfections), background references are inpainted with Gemini-3 Pro Image, and structured JSON annotations from Gemini-3 provide camera-motion and semantic guidance. The global generative formulation (rather than local inpainting) naturally handles imperfect mask edges by reconstructing plausible transitions from spatiotemporal context.

## Results

On the Cine-Restore subset (multi-frame setting), PAI-Studio achieves MSE 0.00645, SSIM 0.789, LPIPS 0.197, and Motion Consistency 0.894, substantially outperforming the best commercial baseline Kling O1 (MSE 0.0190, SSIM 0.682, LPIPS 0.341, Motion Cons. 0.721). On the Cine-NBG subset (no ground truth), the method scores 8.16/10 on Motion Consistency, 9.58/10 on Foreground Preservation, and 9.22/10 on Illumination Harmony, leading all baselines by a clear margin. Multi-frame background control via positional encoding cloning further improves over single-frame conditioning across all metrics (e.g., LPIPS drops from 0.303 to 0.197).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAI-Studio outperforms all five baselines across every metric on both Cine-Restore and Cine-NBG. Against the open-source VACE baseline, the gap is largest on Foreground Preservation (9.58 vs. 2.44 on Cine-NBG) and Motion Consistency (0.894 vs. 0.792 on Cine-Restore multi-frame), indicating VACE struggles with identity preservation and camera-aware motion. Among commercial APIs, Kling O1 is the strongest competitor, achieving 0.0190 MSE and 9.04 Edge Quality in multi-frame mode, yet PAI-Studio still leads by ~3× on MSE (0.00645) and by 0.58 points on Edge Quality. Runway Aleph is the weakest baseline, scoring only 0.323 on Motion Consistency and 4.38 on FG-BG Fusion, suggesting it produces largely static backgrounds. Switchx achieves the highest Foreground Preservation among baselines (8.88) but lags significantly on Motion Consistency (7.12) and FG-BG Fusion (6.16) on Cine-NBG, revealing a trade-off between identity retention and scene integration. The user study confirms these quantitative findings: PAI-Studio receives 29.7–35.6% of best-result votes versus ~21–24% for Kling O1, with statistically significant margins (p < 0.01).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) video generation paradigm (WAN 2.2-5B backbone) and the in-context conditional generation formulation from VACE, but departs from the cascaded-pipeline tradition of matting → inpainting → relighting by unifying all conditions into a single bidirectional-attention token stream. The key architectural departure is temporal positional encoding cloning, which anchors multi-frame background references to specific output timestamps—a mechanism absent from prior video editing or inpainting methods that typically treat backgrounds as static or weakly dynamic. Conceptually, the paper reframes cinematic background replacement from a local inpainting problem to a global video synthesis problem, leveraging the DiT's sequence-based formulation to jointly reason over foreground dynamics, reference appearance, and denoising state without separate control branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CineStudio dataset (30K clips) and Cine-Benchmark (Cine-Restore + Cine-NBG) provide the first systematic evaluation infrastructure for camera-aware background replacement, filling a gap where prior work lacked standardized metrics for motion consistency and illumination harmony. The practical impact for virtual production is significant: the method handles imperfect segmentation masks and achieves scene-consistent relighting without dedicated matting or relighting modules, reducing pipeline complexity. However, open questions remain—evaluation relies heavily on Gemini-based perceptual scoring (creating a potential circularity with Gemini-3 used for data annotation), the method is limited to ~4-second clips at 1080P, and it is unclear whether the approach scales to multi-subject scenes or real-time inference. The reliance on a single strong video understanding model (Gemini-3) for both structured annotation and evaluation also raises questions about generalizability beyond that model's biases.
