## Problem

Existing RGB-T object detectors rely on heavy dual backbones and perform dense pixel-to-pixel cross-modality fusion across the entire image, incurring hundreds of GFLOPs that limit practical deployment. The paper observes that most image regions are smooth backgrounds (sky, ground) easily handled by lightweight single-modality models, making full-image fusion wasteful. The gap addressed is how to retain the accuracy benefits of cross-modality fusion while restricting it to only the sparse foreground regions that actually need it.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a spatially aligned RGB–thermal infrared image pair (e.g., 1024×768 on M3FD, 640×512 on FLIR, 1280×1024 on LLVIP) and outputs class-labeled bounding boxes (6 classes on M3FD, 3 on FLIR, 1 on LLVIP). The input is consumed in two distinct passes: the full-resolution pair first feeds two independent lightweight RPNs, and only the resulting sparse RoI feature pairs (fixed 5×5 spatial size after RoI Align) are passed to the expensive fusion module. This two-pass consumption means computational cost scales sub-linearly with image area, since the FER module's cost depends solely on the number of proposals rather than image resolution.

## Method

SFEDet instantiates a sparse fusion mechanism in two stages: (1) two YOLOv8-Small RPNs (one per modality) rapidly scan the full image to produce high-recall RoIs, whose union is pruned by NMS into sparse, non-overlapping proposals; (2) a Fusion-driven Examination and Refinement (FER) module processes only these sparse RoI features via multi-step cross-modality fusion (differential-enhanced group-wise fusion with softmax-normalized per-group weights, followed by auxiliary backbone-feature enhancement) and two-step box refinement. Rolling Convolution (a group convolution with a channel-rolled counterpart merged by a pointwise conv) replaces all standard convolutions in FER to reduce parameters while increasing depth. A denoising training strategy injects noisy variants of GT boxes into the proposal list so the FER module learns to correct large localization errors. Training is two-stage: RPNs are pre-trained independently, then the full detector is trained end-to-end with a 0.005× learning-rate decay on RPN parameters.

## Results

On M3FD (1024×768), SFEDet achieves mAP 61.0 and AP50 89.8 with only 24.5M parameters and 112.6G FLOPs, compared to ICAFusion (120M, 370G, AP50 88.2) and Fu-Mamba (288M, 1133G, AP50 88.0). On FLIR (640×512), SFEDet reaches mAP 43.0 and AP50 81.7 at 69.0G FLOPs, while the strongest baselines (Fu-Mamba, COFNet) achieve mAP 45.9 and 44.6 respectively but at 472G and 197G FLOPs. On LLVIP (1280×1024), SFEDet attains mAP 65.9 and AP50 96.8 at 163G FLOPs, matching COFNet's mAP (65.9) at roughly one-quarter the computational cost (163G vs 628G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFEDet uses only 24.5M parameters (CSP-S backbone) versus 72–288M for all compared baselines, and 69.0G FLOPs on FLIR versus 133–472G. On FLIR, SFEDet trails the top mAP by ~3 points (43.0 vs Fu-Mamba's 45.9) but at roughly 1/7 the FLOPs; its AP50 (81.7) is within 3.2 points of Fu-Mamba (84.9). On LLVIP, SFEDet matches COFNet's mAP exactly (65.9) while using only 163G vs 628G FLOPs (≈3.8× cheaper), and its AP50 (96.8) is within 1.2 of EI2Det's best (98.0). On M3FD, SFEDet achieves the highest AP50 (89.8) among all methods, surpassing ICAFusion (88.2) and Fu-Mamba (88.0) while using ~30% of their FLOPs. The lighter SFEDet-Lite variant further reduces FLOPs to 53.6G on FLIR with only 0.1 AP50 loss, demonstrating tunable efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

SFEDet repurposes the classic two-stage detection paradigm (RPN + RoI refinement) from single-modality detectors like Faster R-CNN and YOLOv8, but redirects the expensive cross-modality fusion to operate only on sparse RoI features rather than the full image. It departs from the dominant dense-fusion line of work (ICAFusion, EI2Det, COFNet, Fu-Mamba) that applies pixel-to-pixel or attention-based fusion uniformly across the entire feature map, arguing that most image regions are smooth backgrounds requiring no fusion. The Rolling Convolution and denoising training strategy are novel architectural and training components, while the group-wise differential fusion (DEGF) draws on channel-wise weighting ideas from prior RGB-T fusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sparse fusion mechanism offers a principled path to sub-linear computational scaling with image resolution, which is practically critical for deploying RGB-T detectors on high-resolution or edge platforms where current methods incur hundreds of GFLOPs. The demonstrated robustness of the FER module to poorly localized proposals (maintaining AP50 even when proposal IoU drops to 0.6) suggests the framework could tolerate cheaper or noisier proposal generators in future designs. Open questions include whether the sparse-fusion philosophy generalizes to other multi-modal detection settings (e.g., RGB-LiDAR, RGB-event), how performance degrades in extremely dense scenes where the sparsity assumption weakens, and whether the two-backbone requirement can be further reduced through shared or single-backbone variants.
