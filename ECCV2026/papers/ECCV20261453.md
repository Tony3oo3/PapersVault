## Problem

Sparse-view 4D reconstruction from in-the-wild cameras (separated by ~90°) relies on monocular depth priors that are inconsistent across both views and time. Existing methods like MonoFusion handle spatial and temporal alignment in separate stages, requiring foreground segmentation masks and point tracking models, and fail to leverage temporal cues for cross-view alignment or vice versa. No prior work jointly resolves cross-view and cross-time depth inconsistencies in a single unified optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261453/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N synchronized sparse cameras (typically 3–4, separated by ~90°) capturing T frames of a dynamic scene, along with per-frame monocular depth maps from Depth Anything V2 and sparse 3D points from MASt3R. Camera poses are assumed known or estimated. The output is a set of spatio-temporally aligned depth maps (one per view per frame) that serve as both geometric initialization and dense supervision signal for a downstream 4D Gaussian splatting model, which then renders novel views and novel time steps.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified spatio-temporal depth alignment framework that represents all per-view, per-frame depth corrections as a set of spatio-temporal neural fields. Each view maintains a shared 2D spatial encoding and a 1D depth encoding across all T frames, while a per-view deformation MLP predicts depth offsets along ray directions; temporal variation is injected via low-rank residual weight conditioning (VM decomposition with rank R=8) on the MLP's hidden-layer weights, so static regions automatically produce consistent outputs while dynamic regions activate frame-specific residuals—no segmentation or tracking required. The alignment is optimized jointly across all views and time steps using a novel multi-view depth-order loss (penalizing reprojected points that violate cross-view depth ordering), a scale-and-shift-invariant L1 loss, and standard SfM-fitting, structure-preservation, and cross-view matching losses inherited from MAtCha. The resulting aligned depths initialize and supervise deformable Gaussian splatting backends (MonoFusion, deformable 2DGS), yielding 4–6 dB PSNR gains and 14–40% AbsRel reductions over baselines.

## Results

On Ego-Exo4D, applying UniFusion's aligned depths to deformable 2DGS reduces AbsRel from 0.376 to 0.222 (−40%) and improves PSNR from 34.15 to 34.66 dB; for MonoFusion, AbsRel drops from 0.277 to 0.238 (−14%) with PSNR rising to 31.63 dB. On the more challenging EgoHuman novel-view synthesis benchmark, UniFusion yields +6.26 dB PSNR over MonoFusion (20.41 vs. 14.15) and +3.88 dB over deformable 2DGS (22.76 vs. 18.88), with LPIPS dropping by over 1.1 in both cases. The spatio-temporal alignment also runs ~10× faster than naive per-frame MAtCha alignment (1 hour vs. ~half a day for 100 frames from 4 views).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniFusion is not a standalone reconstruction method but a depth-alignment module that improves existing backends. Against MonoFusion [70] (the primary prior-work baseline), UniFusion's aligned depths boost PSNR by +1.22 dB on Ego-Exo4D and a dramatic +6.26 dB on EgoHuman novel-view synthesis, while reducing AbsRel by 14% and LPIPS by over 1.1 on EgoHuman. Against deformable 2DGS (a simple baseline without dedicated depth supervision), UniFusion cuts AbsRel by 40% (0.376→0.222) on Ego-Exo4D and gains +3.88 dB PSNR on EgoHuman, confirming that proper depth initialization/supervision matters more than architectural complexity. Against MV-SOM-SD [65] and Dyn3D-GS [43], Def-2DGS+Ours achieves substantially higher PSNR (34.66 vs. 28.37 and 24.28) and lower AbsRel (0.222 vs. 0.398 and 0.612) on Ego-Exo4D. On EgoHuman, SplatFields [45] achieves 17.74 dB PSNR, well below Def-2DGS+Ours (22.76 dB) and MonoFusion+Ours (20.41 dB), indicating that unified spatio-temporal depth alignment provides a stronger geometric prior than SplatFields' sparse-view approach for multi-human dynamic scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniFusion extends MAtCha's per-view chart alignment (designed for static multi-view depth consistency) into the spatio-temporal domain by adding residual weight conditioning borrowed from ResFields, enabling a single neural field to jointly correct depth across all views and time steps. It departs from MonoFusion, which treats spatial and temporal alignment as separate stages requiring foreground segmentation masks and point-tracking models, by unifying both into one optimization that handles static and dynamic regions uniformly. The downstream 4D reconstruction leverages standard deformable Gaussian splatting backends (MonoFusion, deformable 2DGS) rather than proposing a new rendering architecture, positioning the contribution squarely in the depth-alignment stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the quality of spatio-temporal depth alignment is a more critical bottleneck for sparse-view 4D reconstruction than the complexity of the downstream rendering pipeline—a simple deformable 2DGS with well-aligned depths outperforms MonoFusion's multi-stage pipeline by 4–6 dB PSNR on novel views. Eliminating dependencies on segmentation masks and tracking models simplifies deployment and removes a source of error accumulation, while the 10× speedup over per-frame alignment makes the approach more practical. Open questions include whether the alignment quality ceiling imposed by monocular depth foundation models can be broken, whether the method scales to longer sequences or more cameras, and how it integrates with emerging feed-forward multi-view geometry models for real-time applications.
