## Problem

Existing zero-shot 3D understanding methods reformulate the task as video keyframe understanding with MLLMs, but face two intrinsic bottlenecks: (1) scene-scanning videos provide only finite, fixed viewpoints that often lack query-relevant critical perspectives, and (2) MLLMs implicitly infer spatial layout from keyframes, struggling to maintain unified spatial cognition under significant viewpoint variation. This paper addresses both by proposing a collaborative multi-agent framework that actively plans viewpoints and explicitly documents the 3D scene into a structured cognitive map.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260907/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a textual query, a 3D scene (point cloud/mesh from ScanNet processed by Mask3D for bounding boxes and category labels), and real camera images from the scene video. When planned viewpoints cannot be matched to existing real images (pose distance threshold τ_D = 0.8), rendered images at 512×512 are synthesized to supplement missing perspectives. Outputs vary by task: a 3D bounding box for visual grounding, a text answer for QA, or a spatial location with orientation for situation estimation. The internal state is a holistic cognitive map consisting of an object information table (bounding boxes, labels, progressively filled attributes) and a bird's-eye-view image annotated with consistent instance identifiers.

## Method

The framework decouples 3D understanding into two specialized agents operating in a closed loop: a Planning Agent (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) that filters candidate objects via a retrieval-augmented matching function over the cognitive map, plans K=8 viewpoints on the BEV plane, and maps each to either a real image or a rendered image based on pose distance; and a Perception Agent (GPT-4o) that annotates candidate objects with unique instance identifiers across views, extracts fine-grained appearance and physical attributes, and provides feedback to eliminate mismatched candidates or request additional viewpoints. The key architectural insight is the holistic cognitive map—a shared structured state combining an object information table and a BEV with instance identifiers—that enables cross-view identity consistency and serves as the communication medium between agents. Rather than relying on static object-centric keyframe selection, the Planning Agent actively seeks viewpoints that capture spatial relationships between multiple objects or resolve attribute ambiguities, while the Perception Agent prioritizes real images for fine-grained semantics and rendered images for spatial layout. The iterative process (up to 6 rounds) terminates when the Perception Agent confirms attribute conformity, spatial consistency, and observation sufficiency.

## Results

On 3D visual grounding, the method achieves 58.1% Acc@0.25 and 50.9% Acc@0.5 on ScanRefer (full zero-shot), and 61.2% Acc@0.25 on Nr3D, surpassing the previous zero-shot SOTA (CSVG) by 8.5% and 11.1% on ScanRefer. On 3D question answering, it reaches 54.8% EM (57.1% EM-R) on SQA3D and 91.1 CIDEr / 28.7 EM on ScanQA, exceeding most fully supervised methods. On 3D-assisted dialog, it achieves 47.4 BLEU-1, outperforming the fully supervised 3D-LLM by 8.4 BLEU-1. On situation estimation (SQA3D), it attains 20.7% Acc@0.5m and 48.9% Acc@1.0m, the best among all methods including supervised ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest zero-shot 3D grounding baselines, the method outperforms CSVG by 8.5% Acc@0.25 and 11.1% Acc@0.5 on ScanRefer and by 2.0% Acc@0.25 on Nr3D, and surpasses SPAZER by 0.8% Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer and 1.8% on Nr3D. On 3D question answering, it beats SpatialPrompting by 2.1% EM on SQA3D and 3.4 CIDEr on ScanQA, while also exceeding fully supervised methods LEO (50.0% EM), Chat-Scene (54.6% EM), and Scene-LLM (54.2% EM) on SQA3D. On 3D-assisted dialog, it surpasses the fully supervised 3D-LLM by 8.4 BLEU-1 and the zero-shot Agent3D-Zero by 14.6 BLEU-1. On situation estimation, it outperforms the best supervised baseline 3DSA-LLM by 3.3% Acc@0.5m and 12.0% Acc@1.0m. The method also reduces inference cost from 7.5k tokens / 42.3s (SeqVLM) to 4.3k tokens / 30.2s while achieving 12.4% higher Acc@0.25 on Nr3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant zero-shot 3D understanding paradigm of static object-centric keyframe selection (SeqVLM, SPAZER, SpatialPrompting), which is fundamentally limited by the finite and fixed viewpoints in scene-scanning videos. It repurposes the multi-agent collaboration concept—previously explored in embodied navigation and video understanding—into 3D scene understanding by decoupling viewpoint planning from perceptual verification into two specialized agents. The key architectural novelty is the 'holistic cognitive map' (an object information table plus a BEV annotated with instance identifiers) that serves as an explicit, structured, and progressively updated shared state between agents, contrasting with prior methods that rely on implicit spatial inference from keyframes or coarse scene-graph representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving zero-shot performance that surpasses most fully supervised methods across six diverse 3D benchmarks, this work demonstrates that explicit scene documentation combined with active viewpoint planning can close the gap that supervised training typically provides, reducing dependence on large-scale 3D-text paired annotations. A key limitation is the reliance on a pretrained 3D detector (Mask3D) for initial bounding boxes, making overall performance contingent on detection quality; the authors note that integrating online scene reconstruction could mitigate this. Open questions include whether the multi-agent overhead scales efficiently to much larger environments (e.g., city-scale), whether the cognitive map abstraction generalizes to dynamic or partially observed scenes, and whether the framework's gains persist as MLLM backbones improve independently.
