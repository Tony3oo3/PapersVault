## Problem

Existing language-guided prompt-based continual learning methods face a resource-efficiency gap: state-of-the-art approaches like LEAPGen and ConvPrompt require LLM-generated class descriptors or LLM decoders that are impractical for real-world deployment, while simpler methods like LGCL underperform due to high cosine similarity (0.73–0.96) between class text embeddings. No existing method effectively and cohesively blends vision and language modalities within the prompt generation mechanism using only lightweight, readily available resources.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed through a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone into patch embeddings, class features, or class tokens) and a pre-computed text embedding encoded from the plain class name string (no LLM-generated descriptors). At inference, the model first predicts a task ID via soft matching against task-level and class-level learnable keys, then generates a 3-element prompt and produces a single predicted class label from an MLP head. The entire pipeline operates rehearsal-free with no stored exemplars, and the text modality requires only a standard text encoder applied to class names.

## Method

D-VLAM introduces a 3-element prompt (P_K1, P_K2, P_V) generated by task-wise Conv1D generators (kernel size 3, ~4 parameters each per head) distributed across all MSA heads of the ViT, where P_K1 and P_K2 are produced from the vision embedding and P_V from the text embedding. The key mechanism is Differential Prefix Tuning (DPT), which replaces standard 2-element prefix tuning with a differential attention formulation: (Softmax(Q[P_K1⊕K]ᵀ/√D) − λᵗ·Softmax(Q[P_K2⊕K]ᵀ/√D))·[V⊕P_V], where the task-wise learnable coefficient λᵗ controls how much the noisy vision-derived attention is subtracted, effectively mixing the two modalities at the attention level. Three VL mixing strategies (Complementary-VL, Complementary-LV, Aggregate-VL) are demonstrated, all achieving comparable performance. Training jointly optimizes generators, λᵗ, task-level keys Kᵗ, class-level keys L_cᵗ, and an MLP classifier via a weighted sum of intra-task, inter-task, key-similarity, and class-similarity losses, with a dissimilarity constraint ensuring P_K1 and P_K2 remain distinct.

## Results

On ImageNet-R with 10 tasks, D-VLAM achieves 86.54% final average accuracy (FAA), 87.97% cumulative average accuracy (CAA), and 0.35% final forgetting measure (FFM), outperforming the closest competitor LEAPGen (83.18% FAA, 85.06% CAA, 2.21% FFM) by 3.4%, 2.9%, and 1.86% respectively. On CIFAR-100 with 10 tasks, D-VLAM reaches 98.48% FAA and 98.63% CAA with only 0.11% FFM. The advantage grows with task count: on CIFAR-100 20-task, D-VLAM achieves 98.93% FAA versus LEAPGen's 87.81%, a 11.1% gap. D-VLAM also requires only 0.16M trainable parameters, roughly 40× fewer than LEAPGen (6.35M) and 8× fewer than ConvPrompt (1.28M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-VLAM outperforms all 17 baselines across every dataset and task split. Against the closest prompt-based competitor LEAPGen [30], D-VLAM gains 1.5–3.5% FAA and 1.3–4% CAA on ImageNet-R, and up to 11.1% FAA on CIFAR-100 20-task (98.93 vs. 87.81), while reducing FFM from 2.21% to 0.35% (10-task ImageNet-R). Among other prompt-based methods, D-VLAM leads ConvPrompt [37] by 9.1% FAA and CPrompt [12] by 10.2% FAA on ImageNet-R 10-task, with FFM reductions of 4.0 and 5.8 percentage points respectively. The language-guided baseline LGCL [18] trails D-VLAM by 17.9% FAA on ImageNet-R 10-task, confirming the paper's argument that text-embedding-as-loss-anchor is suboptimal due to high inter-class embedding similarity. LoRA-based methods (LAE, InfLoRA, CL-LoRA, C-LoRA) and CLIP-based methods (MG-CLIP, CLAP4CLIP, Cont-CLIP) all fall 5–35% behind D-VLAM in FAA on CIFAR-100 10-task. The generative approach GMM [6] achieves 87.59% FAA on CIFAR-100 10-task, still 0.9% below D-VLAM, while requiring an LLM decoder. MOS [42] performs poorly in rehearsal-free settings (27.07% FAA on ImageNet-R 10-task), making it an outlier that the authors exclude from margin calculations.

## Relation to Prior Work

D-VLAM extends the prompt-based rehearsal-free continual learning line by repurposing the differential attention mechanism from the Differential Transformer [57] to tune prompt generators rather than ViT weights, and by combining it with the task-wise Conv1D generator architecture inherited from LEAPGen [30] and ConvPrompt [37]. The key departure from prior language-guided methods (LGCL, ConvPrompt, LEAPGen) is the rejection of LLM-generated class descriptors in favor of simple class names, and the introduction of a 3-element differential prefix (P_K1, P_K2, P_V) that cohesively mixes vision and language modalities within the attention computation rather than treating them as separate loss anchors or generator inputs. The method also builds on standard prefix tuning [24] by adding a task-wise learnable coefficient λ^t that controls the differential subtraction between two softmax attention maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40× parameter reduction (0.16M vs. 6.35M for LEAPGen) while achieving higher accuracy and lower forgetting is practically significant for deployment in resource-constrained settings where LLM access is unavailable or unaffordable. However, the ablation reveals that the differential prefix tuning mechanism itself—the paper's central architectural novelty—contributes only ~1% accuracy and 0.5% FFM improvement over the non-differential version, suggesting the bulk of gains come from the task-wise structure and class-level keys rather than the differential mixing. Open questions include whether the soft task-ID prediction mechanism generalizes to domain-incremental or task-free settings, and whether the approach extends beyond image classification to more complex vision tasks where the class-name-as-text-modality assumption may be insufficient.
