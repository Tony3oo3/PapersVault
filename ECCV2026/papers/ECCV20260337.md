## Problem

Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) has become the de facto paradigm for few-step video generation, but its purely local, per-timestep supervision imposes no structural constraint on how denoising updates compose across steps, causing per-step errors to accumulate and degrade quality as step count increases (the 'compositionality deficit'). In autoregressive real-time generation, this issue is compounded by KV-cache quality variability: model-generated caches drift in quality across rollouts, creating a train-inference mismatch that fixed-step training cannot address.

## Input / Output

For non-autoregressive distillation, the method takes a text prompt (T2V, 832×480) or a reference image plus text prompt (I2V, Wan 2.1 14B) and produces a short video in 2–4 NFEs. For autoregressive real-time generation, it takes a text prompt and streams video chunks sequentially, where each chunk is conditioned on the model-generated KV cache of all prior chunks; the KV cache itself is a quality-varying, model-produced signal rather than a fixed external input. The method is plug-and-play, requiring no architectural or inference-pipeline changes to the underlying backbone.

## Method

Salt combines two key mechanisms. First, SC-DMD augments standard DMD with a shortcut self-consistency (SC) loss that penalizes the semigroup defect of the student's Euler transport operator: for a sampled triple (t_s, t_m, t_e), it enforces that the direct one-step update from t_s to t_e agrees with the composed two-step update t_s→t_m→t_e, explicitly regularizing multi-step composition while DMD preserves mode-seeking sharpness. Second, for autoregressive distillation, Salt introduces cache-aware mixed-step training that alternates K∈{2,4,8} denoising steps during rollout, exposing the model to diverse KV-cache quality regimes; the SC loss is activated only at K=8 (the longest composition chain), while a TRD-style cache-conditioned relational feature alignment loss steers low-step (K=2,4) outputs toward higher-step reference outputs in spatial token-relation space. The full training objective is L_DMD + λ_SC·L_SC·1[K=8] + λ_align·L_align·1[K∈{2,4}], with the critic updated via standard DMD alternation.

## Results

On non-autoregressive T2V (Wan 2.1 1.3B, 4 NFE), SC-DMD achieves a VBench Total Score of 83.36 and Quality Score of 84.76, outperforming DMD (82.78 / 84.39) and rCM (82.73 / 83.65). On autoregressive real-time generation at 4 NFE, Salt consistently improves all three backbones: LongLive Total rises from 84.40 to 84.93 (Semantic 81.53→83.00), Self Forcing Total from 84.20 to 84.47, and Causal Forcing Total from 84.62 to 85.08. At the extreme 2-NFE budget, Salt-Causal Forcing reaches 84.80 Total and 85.63 Quality, surpassing the original 4-step Causal Forcing baseline (84.62 / 85.41). On 30-second VBench-Long rollouts, gains transfer: LongLive Semantic improves from 63.88 to 64.74 and Causal Forcing Semantic from 60.25 to 62.77.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DMD (the primary distribution-matching baseline), SC-DMD wins on Total, Quality, and Semantic at both 4 NFE (83.36 vs 82.78) and 2 NFE (82.85 vs 82.41) in the non-autoregressive setting. Against rCM, SC-DMD improves Total by 0.63 and Quality by 1.11 at 4 NFE, though rCM retains a Semantic advantage (79.04 vs 77.77), reflecting rCM's trajectory-based detail preservation. In the autoregressive setting, Salt outperforms all three baselines on Total and Quality: LongLive gains +0.53 Total and +1.47 Semantic (81.53→83.00), Self Forcing gains +0.27 Total and +0.53 Quality, and Causal Forcing gains +0.46 Total and +0.55 Quality. Notably, the 2-step Salt-Causal Forcing model (84.80 Total) surpasses the original 4-step Causal Forcing baseline (84.62 Total), demonstrating that the compositional regularization enables quality gains even when halving the inference budget. On the I2V benchmark (Table 1), Salt achieves the best I2V Score (93.90) and Imaging Quality (72.16) at 4 NFE, beating LightX2V on temporal coherence metrics (Background Consistency 95.97 vs 95.87, Temporal Flickering 97.41 vs 96.30) while LightX2V retains a slight edge on Dynamic Degree (60.33 vs 52.85).

## Relation to Prior Work

Salt builds directly on Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) as its primary distribution-alignment mechanism, while borrowing the semigroup/composition property from shortcut models and continuous-time flow map distillation (AYF) as a *regularizer* rather than the sole training signal. This is a deliberate departure: prior work either uses trajectory-level consistency (which causes mode-averaging in video) or distribution matching alone (which lacks compositional structure), whereas SC-DMD combines both. For the autoregressive setting, the paper extends the Self Forcing / Causal Forcing / LongLive training paradigm by treating the KV cache as a quality-parameterized condition and introducing mixed-step rollout with cache-conditioned feature alignment, a training strategy not present in those baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a 'compositionality deficit' in DMD — where more denoising steps paradoxically degrade quality — is a useful structural diagnosis that likely applies to any multi-step distillation pipeline, not just video. The practical impact is enabling stable 2–4 NFE real-time video generation across multiple AR backbones without architecture changes, which is directly relevant to deployment. However, the absolute gains are modest (≈0.5–1 VBench point), Dynamic Degree sometimes decreases, and the method adds non-trivial training complexity (mixed-step scheduling, reference alignment branches). Open questions include whether the semigroup regularizer helps in single-step or image settings, whether the cache-aware strategy generalizes to other KV-cache mechanisms (e.g., sliding-window, quantized caches), and whether the approach transfers to audio or 3D generation.
