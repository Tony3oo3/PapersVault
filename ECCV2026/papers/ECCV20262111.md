## Problem

Existing VLM-SAM pipelines for Referring Remote Sensing Image Segmentation suffer from two fundamental bottlenecks: (1) Architectural Weak-Coupling, where strictly unidirectional information flow wastes SAM's pixel-level structural priors and causes localization drift, and (2) Object-Centric Semantic Bias, where pre-trained VLMs rely on dominant object semantics as a shortcut while remaining insensitive to spatial descriptions critical for dense remote sensing scenes. Current adaptations largely coast on standalone foundation model capabilities without achieving genuine multi-modal synergy tailored to the extreme homogeneity and multi-scale complexity of RS imagery.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a remote sensing image (resized to 512×512 for SAM 2 and 1024×1024 for SigLIP 2, with no data augmentation) paired with a natural language referring expression, and outputs a binary segmentation mask at the original image resolution. Training employs LoRA (r=16) on both SigLIP 2 and SAM 2 encoders, updating only 7.2% of the total 1.455B parameters, with the LGCD module and SAM decoder as additional trainable components. Spatial counterfactual text perturbations are pre-generated offline using Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.

## Method

CROSS introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, Linguistic-Guided Cascaded Distillation (LGCD) harvests visual features from three intermediate SigLIP 2 layers (blocks 9, 18, 27), refines each via text-conditioned cross-attention, then distills SAM 2's geometric structure by aligning pairwise cosine-similarity Gram matrices across spatial locations, weighted by a text-guided soft mask that routes only linguistically relevant topology while preserving background consistency (α=0.1). Second, Perspective-Spatial Contrastive Learning (PSCL) constructs an asymmetric InfoNCE objective with two hard-negative sources: perceptive negatives mined as top-K background patches with high text similarity (violating spatial constraints), and spatial counterfactual negatives generated by inverting positional descriptors or swapping subject-object relations in the referring expression. The total training loss combines cross-entropy, Dice, distillation (λ₁=0.5), and PSCL (λ₂=0.2) terms, trained with AdamW at lr=1e-4 over 300 epochs on 8 GPUs using DeepSpeed ZeRO-2.

## Results

On RefSegRS, CROSS achieves state-of-the-art cIoU of 83.25% and gIoU of 79.51%, outperforming the strongest competitor RSRefSeg-2 (81.24% cIoU, 77.39% gIoU) by +2.01% and +2.12% respectively. The advantage grows substantially under stricter overlap thresholds: +3.69% at Pr@0.8 and +6.92% at Pr@0.9 over RSRefSeg-2. On the larger RRSIS-D benchmark, CROSS leads in cIoU (79.89% vs. 79.45%) and Pr@0.9 (32.82% vs. 31.05%), though it trails RSRefSeg-2 slightly on Pr@0.6 (−1.22%) and gIoU (−0.25%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CROSS outperforms all 21 compared baselines on every metric of RefSegRS (Table 1), with the most dramatic gains at high-precision thresholds: +6.92% over RSRefSeg-2 at Pr@0.9 and +3.69% at Pr@0.8, indicating substantially tighter boundary adherence. On RRSIS-D (Table 2), CROSS leads in cIoU (+0.44%) and Pr@0.9 (+1.77%) over RSRefSeg-2, but concedes Pr@0.6 (−1.22%), Pr@0.7 (−0.61%), and gIoU (−0.25%), which the authors attribute to extreme scale diversity of dense small objects where the model prioritizes global logical grounding over heuristic boundary alignment. Against the next-strongest foundation-model-based methods (RS2-SAM2, SegEarth-R1), CROSS maintains consistent leads of 1–4% in cIoU and 5–12% in Pr@0.9 across both datasets. Older non-foundation-model baselines (LAVT, CrossVLT, RMSIN, FIANet) trail by 4–7% in cIoU and by more than 30 points at Pr@0.9, underscoring the paradigm shift enabled by VLM+SAM architectures. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that both LGCD (+2.33% cIoU over baseline) and PSCL (+0.10% additional) contribute, with the combined visual+spatial negatives in PSCL outperforming either negative type alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-SAM pipeline established by RSRefSeg 2 (Chen et al., TGRS 2026), which coupled SigLIP with SAM via LoRA for RRSIS, but fundamentally restructures the information flow between the two components. Where prior work treats SAM as a passive mask decoder consuming VLM prompts in a strictly unidirectional manner, CROSS reverses part of this flow by distilling SAM's geometric affinity matrices back into SigLIP's intermediate layers (LGCD), and adds a dual-constraint contrastive objective (PSCL) that explicitly penalizes spatial-linguistic inconsistencies. The contrastive component draws on InfoNCE-style hard-negative mining, while the distillation mechanism repurposes Gram-matrix alignment from representation learning literature as a soft structural regularizer conditioned on cross-attention weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The perturbation analysis revealing that current VLM-SAM pipelines are largely insensitive to spatial descriptor changes (e.g., swapping 'left' for 'right') while still producing high similarity scores is a useful diagnostic that highlights a practical failure mode in deployment. The cascaded distillation idea—injecting a segmentation model's structural priors into a vision-language encoder rather than the reverse—offers a generalizable architectural pattern beyond RRSIS. However, the gIoU deficit on RRSIS-D relative to cIoU gains suggests boundary alignment for extremely small, dense objects remains unresolved, and the reliance on an external LLM (Qwen2.5-7B) for counterfactual generation introduces a preprocessing dependency that limits end-to-end efficiency. Whether the bidirectional VLM-SAM coupling generalizes to other dense prediction tasks (e.g., natural-image referring segmentation, video grounding) remains an open question.
