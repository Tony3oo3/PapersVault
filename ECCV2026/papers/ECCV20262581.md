## Problem

Whether modern vision-language-action (VLA) policies internally represent task progress in their activations is an open interpretability question critical for safe robot deployment. Prior monitoring formulations rely on coarse binary violation labels or hand-crafted sensor thresholds, but in realistic rollout sets violation incidence is near-universal, leaving a degenerate target with little discriminative variance. No prior work has systematically tested whether VLA residual-stream activations encode progress in sparse, linearly decodable, and intervention-compatible directions.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes layer-20 residual-stream activations (d=4096) at action-token positions from 750 cached OpenVLA rollouts on LIBERO, encoded through a pre-trained SAE (d_sae=16384, k=32) into sparse latent features, alongside suite-normalized geometric progress scores derived from end-effector displacement telemetry. Outputs include a binary high-vs.-low progress label (top/bottom quartiles, middle 50% discarded), a ranked list of progress-associated SAE features scored by a composite effect-size × significance metric, linear-probe AUROC/F1/PR-AUC metrics, and per-feature intervention deltas (action-norm shifts, XYZ sign inversions) from direct feature-setting experiments.

## Method

SafeSAE-VLA is a five-stage analysis pipeline: (1) cache residual activations at action tokens across layers 16/20/24, (2) relabel episodes into high- vs. low-progress quartiles using suite-normalized geometric progress, (3) run per-feature Mann-Whitney U tests with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction (α=0.05) over 16,384 SAE dimensions, ranking features by |rank-biserial r| × (−log₁₀ p_adj), (4) evaluate lightweight logistic-regression probes on full and top-k feature subsets, and (5) perform direct feature-setting interventions on OpenVLA. The key design choice is replacing a degenerate binary safety label with a variance-preserving quartile contrast, and the central finding is that 1,117 of 1,881 active SAE features pass FDR < 0.05 while the top-20 alone retain 0.894 AUROC (vs. 0.918 full), showing progress is concentrated in a compact set of named, writable directions. Dense raw-activation probes (0.975 AUROC) match or exceed the SAE readout, so the contribution is positioned as an interpretability and intervention interface rather than a stronger classifier, with direct on-OpenVLA feature-setting confirming task-local behavioral shifts beyond matched-random controls.

## Results

On 750 LIBERO episodes with OpenVLA, a logistic probe over layer-20 SAE features (d_sae=16,384) achieves 0.918 AUROC for separating high-progress from low-progress episodes, with 1,117 of 1,881 active features passing FDR < 0.05. The top-20 ranked features alone retain 0.894 AUROC, confirming that most separability mass is concentrated in a compact subset of directions. Per-suite AUROC ranges from 0.985 (goal) and 0.947 (object) down to 0.700 (long) and 0.667 (spatial, n=14). Under a pooled all-episode protocol with bootstrap CIs, dense raw-activation LR reaches 0.975 and PCA-20 reaches 0.966, both exceeding the full SAE (0.947), so the sparse basis is positioned as an interpretability and intervention interface rather than a peak-accuracy classifier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The SAE feature probe does not outperform dense baselines on raw AUROC: raw-activation logistic regression (0.975) and PCA-20 (0.966) both exceed the full SAE (0.947) and top-20 SAE (0.926) under the pooled protocol. However, the SAE clearly dominates motion-only controls (action magnitude 0.572, end-effector velocity 0.711) and random 20-feature SAE subsets (0.783), confirming that the signal is not reducible to gross movement statistics or arbitrary feature selection. The paper explicitly scopes its contribution as interpretability rather than predictive dominance: the top-20 SAE directions are named, inspectable, and writable, enabling direct feature-setting interventions that shift an independent raw-activation readout by +0.763 (95% CI [0.65, 0.87]) versus +0.078 for matched-random features (permutation p=0.005). A pre-registered specificity batch (n=64) shows task-local behavioral change (violation incidence 1.00→0.50, Δ=−0.50, Wilcoxon p=1.5×10⁻⁸) without altering task success, distinguishing the effect from closed-loop repair. Cross-model diffusion-policy interventions on Octo-small-1.5 in SimplerEnv provide secondary qualitative support, with top feature F1383 showing full XYZ sign inversion between zero-ablation and amplification modes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the SAE-based mechanistic interpretability toolkit developed for language models (Bricken et al., Cunningham et al., Templeton et al.) into the embodied-AI setting, applying sparse dictionary learning to OpenVLA's residual stream to recover progress-related structure. It departs from prior robot safety monitoring, which relies on black-box classifiers over observations or latent states, by instead exposing a sparse, named, and intervention-compatible feature basis. The VLA model family (OpenVLA, RT-2, π0, Octo) is treated as a visual decision backbone whose internal progress representations had, to the authors' knowledge, not been systematically analyzed before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic demonstration that VLA policy progress is linearly decodable from a compact set of SAE feature directions, providing an interpretable and intervention-ready interface for monitoring robot manipulation without policy retraining. Key limitations temper the impact: the progress signal is geometric (end-effector displacement) rather than semantic, the top-20 features score at chance on actual task success, and intervention evidence is task-local rather than closed-loop causal repair. Open questions include whether feature-setting interventions can shift task success in closed-loop settings, whether the sparse progress code generalizes across VLA architectures and real-robot rollouts, and how to incorporate explicit goal-distance or completion metadata to move from geometric to semantic progress.
