## Problem

Compact latent diffusion models lose fine-grained structural details (facial features, small objects) because the encoder compresses high-resolution inputs into a spatially downsampled latent space, discarding high-frequency information. Simply increasing latent resolution is computationally prohibitive since cost scales quadratically with spatial size, and existing multi-scale or test-time refinement approaches introduce significant overhead or require architectural changes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (evaluated at 768×1024) and a text prompt specifying target lighting or weather conditions, producing a relit or translated image. For paired tasks (human relighting, driving scene relighting) it uses pix2pix-Turbo; for unpaired weather/time-of-day translation it uses CycleGAN-Turbo. Saliency regions are task-specific: face and eye bounding boxes (via InsightFace) for human relighting, and object bounding boxes (via YOLO-World or ground-truth annotations) for driving scenes.

## Method

The core contribution is a saliency-guided warp-unwarp framework that locally enlarges semantically important regions before encoding into the latent space, then applies an inverse warp after diffusion-based translation to restore original geometry. Saliency is computed via kernel density estimation over part-level bounding boxes (e.g., eyes within faces), with a fixed bandwidth of 128 controlling warp intensity. The inverse warp is approximated using a piecewise tiling of locally invertible bilinear mappings (following LZU), introducing only ~3 ms latency per warp/unwarp with no learned parameters. The framework is model-agnostic and requires no architectural modification—only LoRA fine-tuning of the base I2I model. Additionally, the paper introduces a FLUX-based synthetic paired data pipeline combining outpainting, depth-conditioned generation, Background Prompt Substitution, and ChatGPT-based quality filtering to produce 10K training pairs in roughly one day on 8 GPUs.

## Results

On human relighting (VITON-HD), the full method achieves a user-study average of 4.33/5, substantially outperforming IC-Light (3.47) and DreamLight (3.48), with the largest gains in person identity (4.36 vs. 3.43/3.52) and clothing identity (4.43 vs. 3.46/3.50). On driving scene weather/time-of-day translation (BDD100K Day→Night), adding warping with GT bounding boxes reduces FID from 19.2 to 17.5 and Clean-FID from 31.3 to 17.2 over the CycleGAN-Turbo baseline. On ROADWork driving relighting, the method scores 4.54/5 average (Golden Sunlight) versus 3.70 (IC-Light) and 3.94 (DreamLight). The warp-unwarp module adds only 0.006 s inference latency (3 ms warp + 3 ms unwarp) on 768×1024 images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IC-Light [52], the proposed method wins decisively on all four user-study criteria for human relighting (4.33 vs. 3.47 average on VITON-HD; 4.23 vs. 3.28 on StreetTryOn) and on driving scene relighting (4.54 vs. 3.70 on ROADWork Golden Sunlight), with the largest gap in identity preservation and lighting faithfulness. Against DreamLight [29], the margin is similarly large: 4.33 vs. 3.48 on VITON-HD and 4.54 vs. 3.94 on ROADWork, with DreamLight particularly weak on StreetTryOn (3.12 avg). Against the pix2pix-Turbo / CycleGAN-Turbo baselines [32] (used as the underlying I2I model), the warping module itself yields consistent gains: on BDD100K Day→Night, FID drops from 19.2 to 17.5 and Clean-FID from 31.3 to 17.2; on cross-dataset Cityscapes→ACDC Foggy, FID drops from 308.8 to 169.2 and DINO-Struct from 9.18 to 2.53. The method also matches or slightly exceeds the no-warp variant of its own pipeline (e.g., 4.33 vs. 3.56 on VITON-HD), confirming that the warping step is the primary source of improvement rather than the synthetic data pipeline alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes saliency-guided image warping—previously developed for discriminative tasks like detection and segmentation (Learning to Zoom, Fovea, Two-Plane Prior, InstanceWarp)—for the first time in generative image-to-image translation. The key departure is performing the inverse warp in image space rather than feature space, making the approach model-agnostic and compatible with any latent diffusion backbone without architectural changes or encoder retraining. On the data side, it contrasts sharply with prior relighting methods (IC-Light, DreamLight) that rely on complex, closed-source synthetic pipelines requiring millions of training images, instead using a simple FLUX-based outpainting pipeline with only 10K pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The warp-unwarp framework offers a nearly free (3 ms overhead, zero learnable parameters) plug-and-play solution to a well-known limitation of compact LDMs—loss of fine-grained structural detail due to spatial downsampling—without the quadratic cost of increasing latent resolution. The synthetic data pipeline is independently valuable, demonstrating that high-quality paired relighting data can be generated at a fraction of the scale and cost of prior approaches. Open questions include whether the technique transfers to multi-step diffusion models (only one-step baselines were tested), how it behaves under fast video motion where frame-by-frame application may flicker, and whether the approximate inverse warp introduces compounding artifacts in iterative or multi-stage pipelines.
