## Problem

Text-guided image editing cannot specify the precise spatial scale, direction, and magnitude needed for fine-grained structural portrait retouching (e.g., jawline refinement, eye enlargement, body proportion adjustment), and existing exemplar-based editing methods lack the sensitivity to perceive and transfer such extremely subtle, localized modifications. Compounding this, constructing cross-identity training pairs with strictly aligned retouching operations is severely hindered by variations in shot scale, pose, and occlusion, creating a critical data bottleneck.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a visual exemplar pair (source portrait and its retouched counterpart) that implicitly encodes the retouching operations, plus a separate query portrait image, and produces a retouched version of the query with the same structural modifications applied. Unlike text-guided approaches, no textual instruction is provided; the operations are inferred purely from the pixel-level differences in the exemplar pair. Training images are cropped to approximately 4 MP using facial bounding boxes and body masks, and the model operates at 1024×1024 resolution for the Face variant.

## Method

MirrorPPR comprises a Retouching Operation Extractor (frozen MAE encoder + trainable R-Former with learnable query tokens that attend to patch-level features to produce a compact edit representation H_edit) and a pre-trained Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 dual-stream DiT backbone into which H_edit is injected via a trainable connector and LoRA modules on the attention blocks. The extractor is first pre-trained with an auxiliary reconstruction proxy task (MLP + ViT decoder with MSE + LPIPS loss), then jointly fine-tuned end-to-end with the DiT under a flow-matching objective. A key data contribution is the Self-Augmentation paradigm: the query pair is synthesized by applying a shared random spatial augmentation (scaling, cropping, rotation, flipping) to both exemplar images, which preserves exact operation alignment while breaking pixel-level coordinate correspondence to prevent shortcut learning. Training follows a progressive curriculum on the 47M-pair MirrorPPR47M dataset, moving from a simulated subset with pronounced landmark-guided deformations to a professional subset of authentic commercial retouching operations.

## Results

On the challenging ProPortrait-500 benchmark (27 professional operations, cross-identity), MirrorPPR-Pro achieves PSNR 32.65, SSIM 0.927, LPIPS 0.200, and Face Similarity 0.960, substantially outperforming all baselines. On the simpler SimFace-100 benchmark (8 simulated operations), MirrorPPR-Face reaches PSNR 32.25, SSIM 0.909, LPIPS 0.186, and Face Similarity 0.937. The method also receives 79.0% overall preference in a user study, confirming that its advantage is perceptually meaningful.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Multi-reference editing models (Qwen-Image-Edit-2511, FLUX.2-dev, Nano Banana 2, Seedream 4.5) and exemplar-based methods (Qwen-ICEdit-LoRA, RelationAdapter, EditTransfer) all fail catastrophically on structural portrait retouching, with PSNR scores ranging from 9.36 to 18.32 and Face Similarity as low as 0.220, indicating they misinterpret the task as image blending or face swapping. Text-guided models perform better in pixel-level reconstruction (e.g., Nano Banana 2 reaches PSNR 27.45 and LPIPS 0.183) but severely compromise identity, with Face Similarity dropping to 0.667 for Nano Banana 2 and 0.501 for Qwen-Image-Edit-2511. MirrorPPR-Pro outperforms the strongest text-guided baseline (Nano Banana 2) by +5.2 PSNR, +0.023 SSIM, and a decisive +0.293 Face Similarity, while also beating the best exemplar-based method (EditTransfer) by +14.3 PSNR and +0.503 Face Similarity. The gap is most dramatic on the Face Similarity metric, where MirrorPPR-Pro (0.960) nearly doubles the best text-guided score (0.667), confirming that exemplar-based transfer preserves biometric identity far more faithfully than language-driven editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of exemplar-based image editing (EditTransfer, VIRAL, RelationAdapter) and portrait retouching (PPR10K, BeautyGAN), but departs from both in a critical way: existing exemplar-based methods target tasks with pronounced visual transformations, while structural portrait retouching involves extremely subtle, localized geometric modifications that those methods lack sensitivity to capture. Architecturally, it builds on the Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 dual-stream DiT backbone and borrows the MAE-based feature extraction idea from Moto, but introduces a dedicated Retouching Operation Extractor (R-Former) and a data self-augmentation paradigm specifically designed to handle the cross-identity operation misalignment problem that plagues exemplar-based training for fine-grained edits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 47M-pair MirrorPPR47M dataset and the self-augmentation paradigm together address a genuine data bottleneck for structural portrait retouching, making this task practically trainable for the first time at scale. The latent-space vector additivity finding opens a path toward compositional, user-controllable retouching without retraining. However, the task remains narrow (portrait-specific structural edits), the professional subset relies on a single commercial API, and generalization to non-portrait structural editing or to operations beyond the 27 curated types remains unexplored. Whether the additive latent structure holds for more complex, interacting operations (e.g., simultaneous jawline narrowing and eye enlargement with anatomical constraints) is an open question.
