## Problem

Existing camera-controlled video generation methods condition on a single input modality—explicit pose trajectories, reference videos, or text descriptions—forcing users to commit to one interface and preventing flexible, heterogeneous camera-motion specification. No prior framework provides a shared representation that aligns equivalent camera trajectories across all three modalities, so cross-modal transfer, composition, and interpolation of camera motion remain unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260262/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a source image (for I2V) or source video (for V2V), a camera-motion reference in any of three modalities (reference video, per-frame camera extrinsics, or natural-language motion description), and a global appearance text prompt. The source is compressed by a 3D VAE (4× temporal, 8× spatial downsampling), while the motion reference is encoded by a modality-specific encoder into a fixed-length sequence of motion tokens. The output is a generated video whose camera trajectory matches the reference, produced via rectified-flow denoising in the VAE latent space; I2V is handled by zero-filling all source latent frames except the first.

## Method

TriMotion builds on the Wan latent video diffusion transformer and introduces two key components. First, a Unified Motion Embedding Space maps video (via VGGT camera tokens), text (via frozen T5 tokens cross-attended by learnable motion queries), and pose (via an MLP on flattened 3×4 extrinsics) into a shared token sequence, trained with InfoNCE global alignment, frame-wise temporal cosine matching, and a shared pose-regression loss to preserve geometric fidelity. Second, a Latent Motion Consistency objective uses a frozen Motion Embedding Predictor (3D convolutions + temporal transformer) to estimate the motion embedding from the reconstructed clean latent during diffusion training, applying a dual-granularity cosine loss that enforces trajectory fidelity entirely in latent space without pixel-space decoding. The motion embedding is injected into each transformer block via a block-specific projection MLP with residual addition, and only the 3D spatial-temporal attention layers and projection MLPs are updated during fine-tuning, preserving the pretrained generative prior while supporting joint I2V/V2V training.

## Results

On the V2V task, TriMotion (Pose) achieves the best FVD (221.59), the lowest rotation error (E_rot = 0.9488), and the best source preservation (FVD-V = 172.25, CLIP-V = 0.9071), outperforming all baselines. On I2V, TriMotion (Pose) attains the best motion accuracy (E_rot = 1.0113, E_trans = 3.8441, CamMC = 4.3087) while remaining competitive on visual quality (FVD = 317.85, FID = 39.11). The text-conditioned variant also leads V2V on FVD (237.87) and CLIP-V (0.9041), demonstrating that the shared motion space transfers effectively across modalities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In V2V, TriMotion (Pose) achieves the best FVD (221.59 vs. 241.38 for CamCloneMaster and 253.65 for ReCamMaster) and the best source preservation (FVD-V 172.25 vs. 195.89 for CamCloneMaster), while also leading on rotation error (0.9488 vs. 1.3184 for ReCamMaster). TrajectoryCrafter attains the lowest E_rot (0.6954) and CamMC (3.5711) but suffers substantially worse FVD (299.96) and FVD-V (267.18), indicating that its explicit point-cloud warping introduces visual artifacts. In I2V, CamCloneMaster edges out TriMotion (Pose) on FVD (312.13 vs. 317.85), but TriMotion dominates on all three motion-accuracy metrics (E_rot 1.0113 vs. 1.5235, E_trans 3.8441 vs. 4.0527, CamMC 4.3087 vs. 4.9585). MotionClone is the weakest baseline overall, with FVD of 601.77 in I2V and the highest motion errors across all metrics. The text-conditioned TriMotion variant is particularly notable in V2V, achieving the second-best FVD (237.87) and the best CLIP-V (0.9041) among non-pose variants, confirming that the unified motion space preserves geometric fidelity even from purely linguistic input.

## Relation to Prior Work

TriMotion builds on the Wan latent video diffusion transformer as its generative backbone and adopts VGGT's alternating-attention camera-token module for video motion encoding, but its core departure is reframing camera-controlled video generation as a cross-modal alignment problem rather than a single-modality conditioning task. Prior methods (MotionCtrl, CamI2V, ReCamMaster, CamCloneMaster) each commit to one input type—explicit poses, reference videos, or geometry-aware attention—whereas TriMotion learns a shared motion embedding space via InfoNCE contrastive alignment, temporal token matching, and pose regression so that video, pose, and text inputs describing the same trajectory become interchangeable. The latent motion consistency loss, which regularizes the diffusion backbone in latent space via a frozen motion embedding predictor, is a novel architectural addition not present in prior camera-control pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modality-agnostic formulation has direct practical value: users can specify camera motion in whatever form is most natural (a text prompt, a reference clip, or a precise trajectory) without retraining or switching models, and the shared space enables zero-shot applications like sequential motion composition and cross-modal interpolation. The Motion Triplet Dataset, with its LLM-generated geometry-grounded captions paired to Unreal Engine 5 multi-camera videos, provides a reusable resource for future multi-modal camera-control research. Key open questions include whether the framework generalizes to extreme or discontinuous trajectories (the paper notes boundary outpainting artifacts) and whether the approach extends beyond camera motion to other cinematic attributes such as color grading or lens effects.
