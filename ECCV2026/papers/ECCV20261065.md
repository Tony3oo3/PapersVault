## Problem

Existing video object insertion methods suffer from two critical limitations: they lack robust 4D scene understanding to propagate a user-specified object placement across complex camera motions and dynamic occlusions, and mask-conditioned generative models rigidly confine edits within the object boundary, failing to synthesize physical optical interactions (cast shadows, reflections) that naturally extend into the surrounding scene. Additionally, no open-source training data exists that pairs source videos with optically integrated target videos for supervised learning of these effects.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video, a single 2D reference object image, and a user-specified 3D placement (rotation, translation, scale) anchored in one reference frame. The reference image is lifted into a local 3D point cloud via TRELLIS before being placed in the reconstructed scene. The pipeline produces a temporally coherent binary mask sequence (refined with SAM 2) as an intermediate artifact, and a final photorealistic video where the object is geometrically and photometrically embedded, including synthesized shadows and reflections in the surrounding scene.

## Method

InsertAnywhere is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (Geometrically Grounded Mask Propagation) reconstructs a 4D scene via Uni4D, lets the user anchor the object's 3D pose in one frame, then propagates it temporally using KNN aggregation of SEA-RAFT scene flow to track local surface dynamics, followed by camera-aligned reprojection and SAM 2 refinement to yield a coherent mask sequence. Stage 2 (Optics-Aware Video Synthesis) fine-tunes the VACE mask-conditioned video diffusion model on the newly constructed ROSE++ dataset using LoRA adapters, with a first-frame anchoring technique that conditions the video model on a high-quality image-level insertion. The key novelty is Optics-Aware Representation Alignment: during training, an extended mask (object region plus its optical footprint, extracted via pixel-wise source-target difference) is tokenized through the same diffusion backbone, and an L2 alignment loss with stop-gradient on the extended-mask branch forces the fine-mask features to match the extended-mask features, teaching the model to synthesize shadows and reflections beyond the object boundary at inference time when only the tight mask is provided.

## Results

On VOIBench, InsertAnywhere achieves the highest scores on 5 of 6 metrics: CLIP-I (0.8132), DINO-I (0.5669), Background Consistency (0.9503), Subject Consistency (0.9534), and Imaging Quality (0.7473). The only metric where it does not lead is Motion Smoothness (0.9925 vs. Kling's 0.9940), a gap the authors note falls within VBench's per-clip noise. The largest margins over the next-best method appear in CLIP-I (+0.063 over Señorita) and Imaging Quality (+0.040 over Kling), confirming gains in both identity preservation and overall visual fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InsertAnywhere outperforms all six baselines on 5 of 6 metrics, with the sole exception being Motion Smoothness where Kling edges ahead by 0.0015 (0.9940 vs. 0.9925). Against the strongest commercial tool, Kling, InsertAnywhere leads by +0.178 in CLIP-I, +0.064 in DINO-I, +0.017 in Background Consistency, +0.004 in Subject Consistency, and +0.040 in Imaging Quality. Among open-source methods, the gap is even wider: over Señorita (the best open-source baseline on CLIP-I at 0.7499), InsertAnywhere gains +0.063 in CLIP-I and +0.117 in DINO-I. The VACE-with-our-mask ablation (0.7368 CLIP-I, 0.6046 Imaging Quality) confirms that the optics-aware synthesis stage, not merely the 4D mask, drives the quality improvement. Pika-Pro and AnyV2V trail substantially on all metrics, with Pika-Pro scoring below 0.50 on CLIP-I and AnyV2V showing the weakest DINO-I (0.2217), indicating poor subject identity preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two prior lines: mask-conditioned video diffusion models (VACE) that preserve backgrounds but are rigidly confined to the mask boundary, and mask-free VOI models (AnyV2V, ReVideo, Señorita) that can hallucinate optical effects but degrade unedited regions. It departs from both by coupling a 4D scene reconstruction pipeline (building on Uni4D, TRELLIS, SEA-RAFT, and SAM 2) for geometrically grounded mask propagation with a novel Optics-Aware Representation Alignment loss that trains the diffusion backbone to synthesize shadows and reflections beyond the primary mask. The ROSE++ dataset, derived from the ROSE object-removal benchmark, fills a data gap that had blocked supervised learning of insertion-induced optical effects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Production-grade VOI for advertising and film post-production has been blocked by the inability to simultaneously control object placement and render physically plausible lighting interactions; this paper addresses both gaps with a practical, end-to-end pipeline. The open-sourcing of ROSE++ and the introduction of VOIBench give the community reusable assets for training and evaluating optics-aware insertion. Key limitations remain: the 4D reconstruction pipeline is computationally heavy, complex physics-aware interactions (e.g., an object deforming a soft surface) are explicitly out of scope, and the method's reliance on a single anchor frame may struggle with very long or highly dynamic sequences. Whether the alignment-loss trick generalizes to other mask-conditioned generative tasks beyond VOI is an open question.
