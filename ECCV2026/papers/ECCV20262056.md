## Problem

Existing spatial intelligence benchmarks for VLMs focus on macroscopic, human-scale objects, leaving unexplored whether these models can perceive and reason about invisible microscopic entities such as atoms and molecules. Scientific discovery in structural biology and drug design requires a specialized form of spatial reasoning—reconstructing 3D structures from 2D projections and inferring physical interactions like hydrogen bonds—that is fundamentally different from everyday spatial tasks. This paper asks whether current VLMs possess this 'Microscopic Spatial Intelligence' (MiSI) and, if not, what is needed to elicit it.

## Input / Output

Inputs are orthographic projection images of protein-ligand complexes rendered at 1160×803 resolution in ChimeraX, paired with text instruction prompts. Unit tasks provide three views (top, front, left) of the initial complex plus one post-operation front view, while interaction tasks supply all six orthographic projections to mitigate atom-name overlap. Outputs follow two formats: cloze completions specifying axis and discretized magnitude (1.0 Å bins for translation/zooming, 15° bins for rotation), or multiple-choice selections among four candidate images; interaction tasks additionally require structured atom-pair tuples (e.g., 'ARG 221 NH2 A, O22') separated by semicolons.

## Method

MiSI-Bench decomposes microscopic spatial intelligence into four elementary operations—translation, rotation, zooming, and interaction—and constructs nine tasks (four unit, five composite) from ~4,000 PDBbind complexes, yielding 163,514 QA pairs over 587,975 images. Composite tasks chain multiple operations (e.g., sequential rotation-rotation, ligand docking requiring both translation and rotation to recover native binding) and generate decoys via controlled perturbations of magnitude, sign, or axis. The model contribution is supervised fine-tuning of Qwen2.5VL-7B on the 150,597 training QA pairs, which the authors show unlocks spatial reasoning that surpasses all larger closed-source VLMs (o3, Claude Sonnet 4.5, GPT-5-mini) and even human evaluators on rotation tasks (~90% vs. ~26% human). Ablations confirm the model relies on visual content rather than language shortcuts (performance collapses to near-random with random images) and generalizes across paraphrased instructions, while its persistent weakness on hydrogen-bond identification highlights the need for explicit domain-knowledge integration beyond spatial pattern learning.

## Results

On MiSI-Bench(tiny), the fine-tuned Qwen2.5VL-7B-SFT achieves an average score of 62.96, substantially outperforming all closed-source VLMs (best: Claude Sonnet 4.5 at 34.37) and approaching but not reaching human-level performance (81.18). The SFT model surpasses humans on Rotation (99.71 vs. 70.18), Translation-Rotation (88.44 vs. 32.00), and Rotation-Rotation (89.59 vs. 26.00), but lags significantly on scientifically-grounded tasks such as Pocket-Ligand Interaction (10.72 vs. 82.78) and Residue-Ligand Interaction Positive (63.46 vs. 100.00). Among closed-source models, performance is near-random on composite spatial tasks (Trans-Rot and Rot-Rot scores cluster around 20–34%), while distance-based unit tasks (Translation, Zooming) yield somewhat higher but still poor scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The SFT 7B model (Qwen2.5VL-7B-SFT, avg 62.96) outperforms every closed-source baseline by a wide margin: it exceeds Claude Sonnet 4.5 (34.37) by ~29 points, O3 (33.65) by ~29 points, and the weakest model Gemini-2.5-flash-lite (16.00) by ~47 points. On spatial transformation tasks (Translation, Rotation, Trans-Rot, Rot-Rot), the SFT model achieves near-perfect scores (99.84, 99.71, 88.44, 89.59) while all closed-source models remain below 58 on Translation and below 44 on Rotation, and cluster near random (20–40) on the composite Trans-Rot and Rot-Rot tasks. However, on domain-knowledge-intensive tasks the SFT model still trails humans substantially: Pocket-Ligand Interaction (10.72 vs. 82.78) and Residue-Ligand Interaction Positive (63.46 vs. 100.00), where even the best closed-source models score below 34. Among closed-source models, Claude Sonnet 4.5 ranks highest overall, with O3 and Claude Opus 4 close behind; the large open-source Qwen3-VL-235B (23.34) underperforms most closed-source models, suggesting that raw parameter scale without domain-specific fine-tuning is insufficient for microscopic spatial reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining Microscopic Spatial Intelligence (MiSI) as a distinct capability separate from macroscopic spatial reasoning benchmarks (VIS-Bench, MuriBench, LEGO-Puzzles) and from coordinate-based 3D molecular modeling (GNNs, equivariant transformers). Rather than building on a specific prior method, it repurposes VLMs—previously applied to general scene understanding or scientific data analysis—by feeding them orthographic projections of protein-ligand complexes, mirroring how human structural biologists visualize molecules in tools like ChimeraX. The benchmark is constructed from the PDBbind dataset, a standard in structure-based drug discovery, but the task formulation (spatial transformation + relational identification on 2D projections of 3D molecules) is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark gap: all SOTA VLMs (o3, Claude Sonnet 4.5, GPT-5-mini) score 27–34% average versus 81% for humans, and the fine-tuned 7B model, while excelling at spatial transformations, still scores only ~11% on scientifically-grounded tasks like pocket-ligand hydrogen bond identification. This underscores that generic spatial reasoning and domain-specific scientific knowledge are separable bottlenecks, and that progress toward scientific AGI requires explicit integration of structural biology priors during pre-training. Open questions include whether the SFT gains generalize beyond the PDBbind distribution, how to inject domain knowledge without sacrificing spatial reasoning, and whether the orthographic-projection formulation is the optimal visual interface for VLMs.
