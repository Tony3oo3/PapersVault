## Problem

VLMs exhibit strong visual reasoning but fail to translate text instructions into precise 6D object poses in 3D scenes, particularly when inferring in a one-shot, feed-forward manner from a single image. This spatial gap is exacerbated by depth ambiguity, occlusions, and complex geometric constraints that a single observation cannot resolve. The paper asks whether iterative closed-loop visual feedback, combined with targeted inference-time techniques, can enable pre-trained VLMs to achieve accurate 6D pose rearrangement without any fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either an RGB-D image with camera intrinsics (lifted to a point cloud via off-the-shelf segmentation) or a compositional 3D mesh scene, along with a natural-language instruction specifying the desired goal state. The output is the target object's 6D pose (rotation matrix in SO(3) and translation vector in world coordinates), produced as a sequence of incremental updates rather than a single prediction. Each update consists of a 3D translation in normalized axis units and a single-axis rotation (axis + angle), which are composed iteratively until the evaluator confirms the scene satisfies the instruction.

## Method

A single pre-trained VLM operates in two alternating roles within a closed loop: an evaluator that judges whether the current rendered scene is faithful to the text instruction and selects the most informative supporting view, and a proposer that predicts an incremental 6D pose update for the target object. Three inference-time techniques make the loop effective: (i) multi-view reasoning with supporting view selection renders K equally spaced cameras around the scene to resolve depth ambiguity and occlusion; (ii) object-centered coordinate system visualization overlays explicit 3D axes on the target object to provide unambiguous directional and scale cues; and (iii) single-axis rotation prediction decomposes the SO(3) search into one axis-aligned rotation per step, which current VLMs can handle reliably. A context memory of prior evaluations, pose updates, and rationales is accumulated across iterations to prevent oscillation and support stable convergence. The entire framework is training-free and requires no new modules, working across closed-source (GPT-5.2, Gemini-2.5-Flash Lite) and open-source (InternVL3-14B) VLMs.

## Results

On the Open6DOR V2 benchmark (4,370 tasks), our method with GPT-5.2 achieves 94.4% position success, 56.6% rotation success, and 46.4% overall 6-DoF success, substantially outperforming SoFar (93.7%, 31.3%, 26.3%) especially on rotation-sensitive tasks. In downstream robot manipulation (Open6DOR V2, 528 tasks), our training-free approach reaches 14.1% 6-DoF success versus 12.9% for SoFar, 4.1% for OpenVLA, and 0.6% for SpatialVLA. On SIMPLER, our method matches or exceeds SoFar on 3 of 4 tasks while remaining entirely training-free, achieving 66.7% success on Put Eggplant in Yellow Basket versus 8.3% for SoFar.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SoFar [31], the strongest prior method, our approach wins decisively on rotation (56.6% vs. 31.3% overall) and 6-DoF (46.4% vs. 26.3%) while matching on position (94.4% vs. 93.7%), confirming that the gap is primarily in orientation reasoning. Open6DOR-GPT [8] is far weaker, achieving only 61.0% position success with no rotation capability, and is outperformed even by our weakest VLM (InternVL3-14B at 81.0% position). In robot manipulation, VLA baselines OpenVLA [18] (4.1%) and SpatialVLA [33] (0.6%) fail almost entirely without task-specific fine-tuning, while our training-free method achieves 14.1% 6-DoF success, surpassing SoFar (12.9%). On SIMPLER, our method ties SoFar on Put Spoon on Towel (58.3%) and Put Carrot on Plate (45.8% vs. 70.8%, where SoFar wins), but wins on Stack Green Block (37.5% vs. 33.3%) and Put Eggplant in Yellow Basket (66.7% vs. 8.3%), demonstrating that the closed-loop VLM agent can match or exceed a method trained on 300K orientation examples without any training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-as-agent paradigm (established in GUI, web, and game settings) to the specific domain of 3D object pose rearrangement, departing from prior language-conditioned rearrangement methods (Open6DOR-GPT, SoFar, Dream2Real) that rely on structured textual scene graphs, simulation-assisted candidate scoring, or one-shot prediction. Rather than introducing new modules or fine-tuning, it repurposes pre-trained VLMs in alternating evaluator/proposer roles within a closed-loop rendering pipeline, supported by three inference-time 3D grounding techniques (multi-view reasoning, coordinate visualization, single-axis rotation). The approach is conceptually closer to VIGA's iterative generate-render-verify loop in graphics than to the scene-graph-based search of SoFar, but applied to continuous 6D pose space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that training-free, closed-loop VLM agents can match or exceed task-specific VLA models on manipulation benchmarks, suggesting a path to generalizable robot control without per-task fine-tuning. However, the inference latency of repeated VLM queries and rendering makes real-time deployment challenging, and absolute 6-DoF success rates (~40%) remain moderate. Open questions include whether the closed-loop approach can be accelerated (e.g., via distillation or early termination heuristics), whether it generalizes beyond tabletop scenes to more complex multi-object or deformable-object settings, and whether the inference-time techniques transfer to other 3D reasoning tasks beyond pose prediction.
