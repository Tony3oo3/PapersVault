## Problem

Existing attention-based token pruning methods for MLLMs rank visual tokens by absolute text-visual attention scores, implicitly assuming these scores reflect instruction-conditioned semantic relevance. The paper shows this assumption fails because attention is dominated by a model-induced prior: even without any textual instruction, MLLMs consistently attend to certain task-agnostic regions, suppressing instruction-relevant tokens and causing them to be discarded under aggressive token budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260500/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (encoded into a sequence of visual tokens, e.g., 576 for LLaVA-1.5 or 2048 for Video-LLaVA) and a text instruction, and produces a text response while physically reducing the visual token sequence to a user-specified budget K. A null token (the separator \n) is inserted between the visual and instruction tokens; at a designated early pruning layer (L=2 by default), both the prior and posterior attention distributions are extracted from the same attention matrix, after which unselected visual tokens are permanently removed from hidden states and KV cache so that all subsequent layers operate on the shortened sequence.

## Method

PriorTR is a training-free, single-pass token reduction method that explicitly separates instruction-conditioned attention from the model-induced prior. It exploits the causal attention structure: a null token placed between visual and instruction tokens cannot attend to the instruction under the causal mask, so its attention over visual tokens yields an instruction-agnostic saliency prior Q, while instruction tokens' attention over the same visual sequence yields the task-conditioned posterior P, both extracted from one forward pass. Each visual token is then scored by its V-usable information S = P · log(P/Q), a masked additive surrogate of the KL divergence that jointly weights relevance magnitude (P) and relative lift over the prior (log(P/Q)), naturally suppressing prior-dominated tokens where P ≈ Q. The top-K tokens are physically retained by gathering their hidden states and KV-cache entries, so deeper layers compute over a truly shortened sequence, achieving real latency, memory, and FLOPs reductions without any additional training or model modification.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B across 12 multimodal benchmarks, PriorTR achieves 99.5% average normalized accuracy at 192 retained tokens (66.7% reduction), 98.2% at 128 tokens (77.8% reduction), and 94.5% at 64 tokens (88.9% reduction), consistently outperforming all five training-free baselines. The advantage is most pronounced under aggressive compression: at K=64, PriorTR leads the next-best method (VisPruner) by 2.8 points and the attention-only baseline FastV by 23.8 points. On Qwen3-VL-8B at 11.1% token ratio, PriorTR retains 87.8% of baseline accuracy versus 75.5% for FastV. In video QA (Video-LLaVA, 2048→194 tokens), PriorTR+Merging matches the uncompressed baseline (38.7% GPT accuracy) and exceeds it under Gemini evaluation (41.6% vs. 40.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PriorTR outperforms all five baselines at every token budget on LLaVA-1.5-7B. Against the closest competitor VisPruner (ICCV'25), PriorTR leads by 1.0, 1.6, and 2.8 points (Avg.%) at K=192, 128, and 64 respectively, with the gap widening as the budget tightens. SparseVLM (ICML'25) is competitive at moderate budgets (97.4% at K=192) but degrades sharply to 81.4% at K=64, a 13.1-point gap versus PriorTR. PDrop (CVPR'25) maintains reasonable accuracy at K=192 (96.0%) but collapses to 74.4% at K=64, trailing PriorTR by 20.1 points. FastV (ECCV'24), the original attention-ranking baseline, is consistently the weakest method, scoring only 70.7% at K=64—23.8 points below PriorTR—confirming that raw attention magnitude is a poor proxy for instruction-conditioned relevance under aggressive compression. PruMerge (ICCV'25) underperforms even FastV at K=192 (89.1% vs. 89.8%) and does not close the gap at tighter budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention-based visual token pruning paradigm established by FastV and its successors (PDrop, SparseVLM, VisPruner, PruMerge), which rank tokens by raw text-to-visual attention at an early layer and retain the top-K. The key departure is the identification of a model-induced prior: even without any instruction, MLLMs allocate high attention to task-agnostic regions, so absolute attention magnitude conflates intrinsic saliency with instruction-conditioned relevance. PriorTR corrects this by estimating the prior via a null token (the post-image separator) and scoring tokens with a log-likelihood-ratio (P·log(P/Q)) derived from the usable-information framework of Xu et al. (ICLR 2020), all within a single forward pass exploiting causal attention structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The prior-dominated masking diagnosis is a useful conceptual correction for the entire attention-based pruning literature: any method that ranks by raw attention magnitude inherits this bias, and the effect worsens under aggressive budgets where a few wrong selections are irreversible. The single-pass null-token implementation is elegant and broadly applicable, making the correction nearly free in practice. Open questions include whether the prior structure is stable across model scales and architectures (only LLaVA-1.5, Qwen3-VL, and Video-LLaVA are tested), whether alternative null-token choices or learned priors could further improve correction, and how the method interacts with emerging dynamic-resolution or video-native MLLMs where the visual token distribution differs substantially.
