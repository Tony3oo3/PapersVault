## Problem

Recovering an object's texture, reflectance, and illumination from a single image is fundamentally ambiguous because surface reflectance acts as a frequency filter that irreversibly attenuates high-frequency illumination details. Prior generative methods either handle only textureless objects (DRM) or omit illumination entirely, leaving no approach that stochastically samples all three radiometric constituents simultaneously. The paper shows this ambiguity can be resolved by exploiting the physical constraint that multiple objects in a scene share the same illumination while differing in material.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261203/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a multi-object scene together with known 3D shapes (surface normal maps) for each object, and stochastically produces per-object spatially-varying diffuse texture maps, per-object uniform reflectance parameters (metallic, roughness, specular strength under the Disney BRDF), and a single shared illumination environment map represented as a mirror reflectance map on a Gaussian sphere. The reflectance and illumination estimation operates in angular space (reflectance maps indexed by surface normal) rather than image space, and the method generates 10 stochastic samples per input, reporting the top-3 mean for evaluation.

## Method

MultiGP uses a cascaded three-stage architecture: (1) a latent diffusion model extracts per-object texture conditioned on appearance and shape latents; (2) a multi-object diffusion model operates on texture-free reflectance maps, where the forward process is a deterministic rendering equation with additive Gaussian noise rather than a standard Markov chain, and two novel mechanisms—Coordinated Scheduling (linearly interpolating each object's reflectance to mirror state over K shared steps so all objects converge to one illumination simultaneously) and Axial Attention (cross-object attention at matched surface-normal directions to fuse complementary spectral information)—guide convergence to a consistent shared environment map; (3) a ControlNet refines textures by conditioning on the residual between a Mitsuba 3 path-traced render and the observed image, enforcing physical consistency without collapsing generative diversity. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on path-traced multi-object scenes with per-object reflectance-map triplets.

## Results

On the synthetic multi-object test set (36 object triplets), MultiGP achieves the best illumination logRMSE of 1.28 (vs. 1.48 for DRM and 1.64 for DPI), PSNR of 13.54, SSIM of 0.42, and reflectance logRMSE of 1.81. For texture estimation, MultiGP attains RMSE 0.081, PSNR 29.99, SSIM 0.97, and LPIPS 0.031, outperforming all baselines including DPI (RMSE 0.15) and RGB↔X (RMSE 0.11). Runtime is 25.3 s per 1024×1024 image on one A100, roughly 50× faster than DPI (1167.7 s) and DiffusionLight (1280.1 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiGP outperforms all six baselines on every metric where they overlap. Against DRM [10] (the closest single-object competitor), MultiGP improves illumination logRMSE from 1.48 to 1.28 and SSIM from 0.34 to 0.42, while also reducing reflectance logRMSE from 1.85 to 1.81. Against DPI [28], the gains are larger: illumination logRMSE drops from 1.64 to 1.28 and reflectance logRMSE from 2.22 to 1.81; on texture, MultiGP's RMSE of 0.081 beats DPI's 0.15 by nearly 2×. DiffusionLight [32] is surpassed on illumination (logRMSE 1.28 vs. 1.60, PSNR 13.54 vs. 11.37) but does not estimate reflectance. On texture, MultiGP (RMSE 0.081, LPIPS 0.031) clearly beats RGB↔X [45] (RMSE 0.11, LPIPS 0.053), GP Motion [14] (RMSE 0.13, LPIPS 0.071), and DiffusionRenderer [24] (RMSE 0.11, LPIPS 0.051). The single-object ablation (MultiGP single) confirms that the multi-object consensus is essential: removing cross-object information degrades illumination logRMSE from 1.28 to 1.44 and reflectance logRMSE from 1.81 to 2.05.

## Relation to Prior Work

MultiGP extends the Diffusion Reflectance Map (DRM) framework from Enyo & Nishino (CVPR 2024), which performs stochastic inverse rendering of reflectance and illumination for a single textureless object, to a multi-object setting that additionally recovers spatially-varying texture. It also adapts architectural components (U-ViT3D, temporal/axial attention) from Han et al.'s generative perception of shape and material from video (NeurIPS 2025). Conceptually, the paper departs from prior generative perception work—which sampled shape and material but not illumination—by jointly sampling all three radiometric constituents and exploiting the physical constraint that co-located objects share one illumination, turning an ill-posed single-object inversion into a better-constrained multi-object problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-object consensus principle provides a physically grounded path toward resolving the fundamental ambiguity of single-image inverse rendering without requiring multi-view captures or known lighting, which is directly relevant to embodied agents needing material and illumination estimates for grasping, relighting, and scene prediction. The proposed ambiguity-aware evaluation metric (SH-PCA log-likelihood and Mahalanobis distance) offers a more faithful way to score stochastic inverse rendering methods than point-estimate metrics, and could be adopted broadly. Key limitations include the requirement for known object geometry, the assumption of distant (environment) illumination, and the no-cross-talk approximation; relaxing these toward joint shape estimation and near-field lighting is identified as important future work.
