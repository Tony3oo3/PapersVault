## Problem

Adapting pretrained VLMs like CLIP to biomedical imaging is challenging because full fine-tuning is computationally prohibitive, medical data are scarce, and fine-grained inter-class differences (e.g., cataract vs. glaucoma) cause severe semantic confusion. Existing Visual Reprogramming (VR) methods for VLMs rely on a single shared visual prompt focused on positive class attributes, overlooking confusing negatives and producing miscalibrated predictions in fine-grained medical scenarios. No prior VR framework has been designed specifically for the biomedical domain.

## Input / Output

The method takes a biomedical or natural RGB image and applies two learnable perturbations (δ⁺ and δ⁻) restricted to a fixed 16-pixel border mask, leaving the central image content unchanged. On the text side, it consumes LLM-generated positive attribute descriptions and confusion-aware negative attribute descriptions per class. The output is a class probability distribution over all candidate classes, computed from gated fusion of top-k attribute similarity scores from both experts, yielding a single predicted class label.

## Method

BioMedVR introduces a Confusion-aware Mixture-of-Prompt Experts (MoPE) that decouples visual reprogramming into two role-specialized experts: a positive expert (δ⁺) that aligns the reprogrammed image with descriptive/discriminative attributes for main-class recognition, and a negative expert (δ⁻) that suppresses visually similar but semantically incorrect categories using LLM-generated confusion-aware attributes. Their top-k similarity logits are adaptively fused via a learnable 2D gating vector (softmax), and the fused logits feed a standard cross-entropy classification loss. A Confusion-Suppression Loss additionally penalizes cases where the maximum negative-expert score across confusing classes exceeds the positive-expert score by less than a margin m, explicitly sharpening decision boundaries. Only δ⁺, δ⁻, and the gating vector are trained (≈0.30M parameters) while the CLIP encoders remain frozen, making the approach architecture-agnostic and privacy-preserving.

## Results

On 16-shot biomedical classification across 11 datasets, BioMedVR achieves 76.4% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest VR baseline AttrVR (73.0%) by 3.4 points and the best prompt-learning method BiomedCoOp (71.2%) by 5.2 points. In the zero-shot setting with the BiomedCLIP backbone, BioMedVR reaches 47.6% average accuracy versus 40.9% for BiomedCLIP alone. On seven natural-image benchmarks, BioMedVR attains the best or near-best few-shot accuracy in all cases (e.g., 80.1% on UCF101, 93.9% on EuroSAT and OxfordPets), while using only 0.30M trainable parameters compared to ~150M for full CLIP fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BioMedVR (76.4% avg) outperforms all three VR baselines: AttrVR (73.0%, +3.4 pts), AR (72.6%, +3.8 pts), and VP (68.3%, +8.1 pts) on 16-shot biomedical classification. Against prompt-learning methods, BioMedVR surpasses BiomedCoOp (71.2%, +5.2 pts), CoCoOp (66.6%, +9.8 pts), and CoOp (63.9%, +12.5 pts), though BiomedCoOp retains a slight edge on CT-Kidney (80.9% vs 74.0%) due to its domain-specialized BiomedCLIP backbone. In zero-shot, BioMedVR with BiomedCLIP (47.6%) improves over BiomedCLIP alone (40.9%) by 6.7 points, and over standard CLIP (28.1%) by 19.5 points. On natural-image benchmarks, BioMedVR matches or exceeds AttrVR on all seven datasets (e.g., UCF101: 80.1% vs 78.6%; DTD: 66.0% vs 65.9%) and leads AR by up to 6.2 points on DTD. The method uses only 0.30M trainable parameters (2× AttrVR's 0.15M) while delivering the highest accuracy, compared to ~150M for full CLIP fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

BioMedVR extends the Visual Reprogramming (VR) lineage—originating from VP [2] and refined by AR [8] and AttrVR [7]—from natural-image benchmarks to biomedical imaging for the first time. Its core departure from AttrVR is the introduction of LLM-generated confusion-aware negative attributes alongside positive descriptive attributes, coupled with a dual-expert (positive/negative) Mixture-of-Prompt Experts architecture and a margin-based Confusion-Suppression Loss. The paper also positions VR as an input-space alternative to prompt-learning methods (CoOp, CoCoOp, BiomedCoOp), emphasizing architecture-agnosticism and privacy preservation for medical deployments where access to model internals is restricted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By bringing VR into the biomedical domain with explicit confusion modeling, BioMedVR offers a lightweight (0.30M trainable parameters vs. ~150M for full CLIP fine-tuning), interpretable, and privacy-preserving route to few-shot medical image diagnosis that does not require modifying pretrained encoder weights. The differential-diagnosis-inspired framing—separating a true class from its most confusable alternatives—aligns with clinical reasoning and could inform explainable AI in radiology and pathology. Key limitations include the offline, LLM-dependent generation of confusion-aware attributes (quality varies with the language model), the absence of expert-curated candidate lists, and the lack of clinically grounded evaluation protocols, leaving open whether the approach translates to real-world diagnostic workflows.
