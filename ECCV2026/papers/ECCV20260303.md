## Problem

Traditional absolute pose solvers with unknown focal length require at least 3.5 point correspondences, and while gravity-aware solvers reduce this count, none exploit the local geometric information (scale, orientation) embedded in feature descriptors like SIFT or ORB. This paper addresses the unexplored problem of jointly estimating absolute pose and focal length using affine or orientation-covariant features combined with an IMU-derived gravity vector, enabling solvers that operate on a single or two correspondences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260303/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The solvers consume 2D-3D correspondences lifted from a pre-built SfM reconstruction, a gravity direction from an IMU, and per-feature affine descriptors (scale and orientation from SIFT/DoG or SuperPoint+S3Esti) or orientation-only descriptors (ORB). The reference camera focal length is assumed known; the query focal length is the unknown to be estimated alongside the 6-DoF pose. UP1PfAC requires a single affine correspondence, while UP2PfORI requires two orientation-covariant correspondences, both far fewer than the 3.5 points needed by conventional PnPf solvers.

## Method

The key insight is deriving polynomial constraints that relate the local affine transformation matrix A to the query camera pose with an unknown focal length f, under a gravity constraint that reduces rotation to a single yaw angle parameterized via the tangent half-angle substitution r = tan(θ/2). For UP1PfAC, four affine-pose equations (Eqs. 7–10) and two point-projection equations (Eqs. 12–13) form a 5×4 linear system in translation; exploiting the block-triangular structure of the coefficient matrix yields three subdeterminant equations that reduce to a quartic in r, with f and t recovered by back-substitution. For UP2PfORI, two orientation-covariant constraints (Eq. 11) plus four point equations form a 4×4 system whose determinant, combined with one orientation constraint, again produces a quartic in r. Both solvers yield four candidate solutions, from which the previously discarded equation selects the best hypothesis, limiting RANSAC to a single hypothesis per sample.

## Results

On the Cambridge Landmarks dataset (SuperPoint+LightGlue), UP1PfAC achieves the best semi-calibrated performance across all five scenes, e.g., 57.7 cm position error and 0.13° rotation error on GreatCourt versus 61.5 cm / 0.14° for P4Pf, while also running faster (22.6 ms vs. 26.9 ms). On the more challenging Aachen Day-Night benchmark, UP1PfAC reaches 48.5% recall at the strict 0.25 m/2° threshold (Day, SuperPoint) compared to 46.1% for P4Pf and 45.6% for P3.5Pf, and 97.9% at the relaxed 5 m/10° threshold. In terms of raw solver speed, UP1PfAC runs in 2586 ns per hypothesis, roughly 7× faster than P3.5Pf (19118 ns) and slightly faster than P4Pf (3179 ns), while UP2PfORI runs in 2149 ns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the point-based semi-calibrated solvers (P4Pf, P3.5Pf, UP2.5Pf), UP1PfAC consistently wins on the strictest localization threshold (0.25 m/2°) on Aachen by 2–3 percentage points (48.5% vs. 46.1/45.6/46.5%) and on position error on Cambridge by ~4 cm (57.7 cm vs. 61.3–61.6 cm on GreatCourt). However, P4Pf retains a slight edge at the intermediate 0.5 m/5° threshold on Aachen (74.0% vs. 70.8%), likely because its 4-point sampling provides more geometric redundancy at moderate accuracy levels. UP1PfAC also achieves the lowest focal length error (0.009 normalized) among all semi-calibrated methods on both datasets. In terms of speed, UP1PfAC (2586 ns) is ~7× faster than P3.5Pf (19118 ns) and ~1.2× faster than P4Pf (3179 ns), while UP2PfORI (2149 ns) is the fastest proposed solver. Compared to the calibrated solvers (P1AC, UP2P, UP1SIFT), the proposed methods trade a small amount of translation accuracy for the ability to estimate focal length, but improve rotation error in most scenes (e.g., 0.13° vs. 0.15–0.16° on Cambridge GreatCourt).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the affine-correspondence pose estimation line initiated by Barath and Kukelova (who derived constraints linking local affine features to camera geometry) and Ventura et al.'s P1AC/UP1SIFT solvers, by introducing an unknown focal length parameter and a gravity prior from IMU data. The PnPf problem (absolute pose with unknown focal length) was previously addressed exclusively with point-based solvers (P4Pf, P3.5Pf) requiring 3.5 correspondences, while gravity-aware solvers (UP2P, UP2.5Pf) assumed known focal length. The paper's core contribution is the first derivation of polynomial constraints that simultaneously exploit affine/orientation-covariant feature geometry, IMU-derived gravity, and unknown focal length, yielding minimal solvers with 1 or 2 correspondences instead of 3.5 points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the minimal sample size from 3.5 points to 1 affine or 2 orientation-covariant features, the proposed solvers significantly accelerate RANSAC-based robust estimation, which is critical for real-time localization on constrained devices (XR headsets, drones, embedded robotics). The paper demonstrates that within modern frameworks like GraphCut-RANSAC, the speed advantage translates to faster convergence with comparable or better accuracy on both Cambridge Landmarks and Aachen Day-Night. Open questions include how well these algebraic solvers compete with increasingly capable learned localization pipelines, and whether the compounded real-world error sources (normal estimation noise, descriptor mismatches, IMU drift) will erode the geometric priors as scenes become more complex.
