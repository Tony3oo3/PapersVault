## Problem

Aligning video generative models to human preferences via RL and distilling them for few-step inference are typically treated as disconnected sequential stages, creating a dilemma: RL before distillation incurs prohibitive multi-step sampling costs and complex ODE-to-SDE conversions, while RL after distillation destabilizes the compressed model and causes visual collapse. No existing framework unifies both objectives in a single, stable optimization pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262545/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and generates 5-second videos at 16 FPS and 832×480 resolution using only 4 denoising steps (vs. 100 for the base model). Preference guidance is supplied either as pre-annotated positive video samples (DM-PairLoss, using the ConsistID dataset) or as reward-model scores over a group of K=8 generated outputs (DM-GroupLoss, using VidProM prompts). The fake denoising model is updated every step while the generator is updated every 5 steps, with both sharing the same latent-space samples.

## Method

The key insight is that both DMD distillation and preference alignment can be expressed as score-space distribution-matching gradients, making them natively compatible within a shared computational pathway. DM-Align derives a preference-guided gradient by approximating the intractable target score (from the Bradley-Terry optimal policy) using the fake model's denoising prediction on a preferred sample, via Tweedie's equivalence. Two concrete losses are provided: DM-PairLoss substitutes a pre-annotated positive sample as the preferred anchor (DPO-inspired), and DM-GroupLoss dynamically constructs the anchor from the top-k of a reward-scored group, weighting each sample by its advantage (GRPO-inspired). Both gradients are linearly combined with the DMD gradient (λ_DMD = λ_align = 0.5) and applied to the generator in a single training loop, entirely bypassing MDP formulation, trajectory probability estimation, and multi-step sampling.

## Results

On the ConsistID dataset, DM-Align (Pair) achieves the highest VBench average score of 82.78 at only 4 NFE, outperforming the raw model (78.20 at 100 NFE), standalone DMD2 (79.68), and the two-stage Flow-DPO+DMD2 pipeline (79.89). On VidProM, DM-Align (Group) reaches an average score of 84.40 at 4 NFE, surpassing DanceGRPO (82.76 at 100 NFE) and DanceGRPO+DMD2 (80.54). In human evaluation (GSB protocol), DM-Align (Pair) yields +48% and +32% net preference over the raw model and DMD2 respectively, while DM-Align (Group) achieves +30% and +12%. Training efficiency is dramatically improved: DM-Align requires only 51 NFE and 15 s per step versus 1536 NFE and 423 s for DanceGRPO (~30× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standalone DMD2 (distillation only), DM-Align (Pair) improves the average VBench score from 79.68 to 82.78 and Aesthetic Quality from 57.64 to 60.89, while DM-Align (Group) lifts the average from 78.88 to 84.40 and Dynamic Degree from 51.64 to 80.19. Against the two-stage sequential pipelines (Flow-DPO+DMD2 and DanceGRPO+DMD2), DM-Align wins decisively: the sequential approaches suffer severe post-distillation collapse (Dynamic Degree drops from 67.13→53.43 for Flow-DPO and 75.78→60.85 for DanceGRPO), whereas DM-Align maintains or exceeds the pre-distillation RL gains (66.25 and 80.19 respectively). Against standalone RL at 100 NFE (Flow-DPO, DanceGRPO), DM-Align matches or exceeds their alignment gains while operating at 4 NFE, delivering a ~30× training speedup (15 s vs 423 s per step). On the Textual Alignment reward metric, DM-Align (Group) scores 1.65 versus 1.40 for DanceGRPO+DMD2 and 1.37 for DanceGRPO alone. In human GSB evaluation, DM-Align (Pair) achieves +48% net preference over the raw model, the largest margin among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD/DMD2) for few-step video generation and RL-based preference alignment methods (DPO, GRPO, Flow-DPO, DanceGRPO) for aligning generative models with human feedback. The key conceptual departure is identifying a 'gradient compatibility' between distillation and alignment: both can be expressed as score-space distribution-matching gradients, enabling a unified single-stage optimization rather than the sequential pipelines (RL-then-distill or distill-then-RL) used by prior work. Unlike concurrent joint-optimization efforts (FlashDMD, DMDR, Diff-Instruct++) that add off-the-shelf RL losses to a DMD objective, this paper formulates preference alignment natively within the DM framework via sample-guided score differences, entirely bypassing the MDP/SDE adaptations that traditional diffusion RL requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework eliminates the fundamental dilemma in video generation pipelines—RL-before-distillation is prohibitively expensive (multi-step sampling, complex MDP conversions) while RL-after-distillation causes catastrophic model collapse—by unifying both objectives in a single training loop with ~30× training efficiency over DanceGRPO. The finding that directly adding DanceGRPO loss to DMD performs nearly identically to DMD alone (Figure 6) is a useful negative result showing that naive loss combination fails without the score-space reformulation. Limitations include restriction to DMD-based distillation (excluding GAN-based alternatives), lack of strict convergence guarantees for the weighted joint loss, and the observation that excessive alignment weight (1:3 ratio) destabilizes generation. Open questions include integration with advanced DMD variants (phased DMD, trajectory distribution matching) and GAN-based distillation, and whether the score-space unification extends beyond video to other generative modalities.
