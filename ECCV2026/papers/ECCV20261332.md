## Problem

Existing single-image-to-3D scene generation methods produce individual assets but neglect the structural layout (floors, walls, built-in structures), causing floating objects, misplacement, and interpenetration. Moreover, a single perspective image provides only a limited field of view, making coherent global layout recovery fundamentally ill-posed. This paper addresses both limitations by reformulating the input as a 360° equirectangular projection (ERP) image that captures the full spatial context of an indoor environment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 360° ERP image of an indoor room as input, which is decomposed into six cubemap face images (120° FoV each) for conditioning and a depth map for geometric initialization. The output is a complete textured 3D indoor scene mesh in canonical [-0.5, 0.5]³ space, comprising both the structural layout and individual assets with PBR materials, along with 3D oriented bounding boxes (center, extents, yaw) for each detected asset. The scene is generated at 512³ voxel resolution for geometry and material, with coarse structure at 64³.

## Method

InSpace is a three-stage flow-matching pipeline built on TRELLIS.2's sparse O-Voxel representation. Stage 1 lifts an estimated depth map to a 3D point cloud via equirectangular back-projection, yielding a Partial Scene Geometry (PSG) and calibrated camera center as spatial priors. Stage 2 generates a coarse 16³ voxel latent via a DiT with view-selective cross-attention, where each voxel attends only to the cubemap faces visible from its 3D position (computed via cosine similarity against face normals with a soft sigmoid mask), followed by a 3D U-Net that detects asset OBBs; at inference, Layout-Guided Structure Inversion initializes denoising from a noised PSG encoding. Stage 3 produces detailed geometry and PBR material through a global-local hybrid attention: global self-attention across all scene components ensures spatial coherence, while asset-selective cross-attention directs each asset to attend exclusively to its OBB-projected 2D region in the cubemap features, recovering fine-grained appearance. The entire framework is trained with rectified flow matching and finetuned from pretrained TRELLIS.2 weights using a frozen DINOv3 ViT-L/16 image encoder.

## Results

On the ERP-FRONT test set, InSpace with view-selective cross-attention and Layout-Guided Structure Inversion (t₀=0.7) achieves a coarse-structure Voxel IoU of 57.09 and F1 of 68.78 (Stage 2), and for full scene generation (Stage 3) reaches a scene-level Chamfer Distance of 0.63×10³, F1@0.02 of 82.10, PSNR of 19.17 dB (top-down) / 11.22 dB (interior), and LPIPS of 0.220 / 0.650. At the asset level, CD drops to 1.72×10³ with F1@0.02 of 79.52. The view-selective cross-attention alone lifts Stage-2 IoU from 44.36 (standard cross-attention) to 57.54, roughly halving CD from 15.92 to 7.68 (×10³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three single-image 3D scene generation methods—SceneGen [46], MIDI [19], and SAM3D [6]—but defers all quantitative and qualitative comparisons to the supplementary material (Sec. 4), so no direct numerical head-to-head results appear in the main text. The key qualitative distinction is that all three baselines operate on a single perspective image and generate individual assets without modeling structural layout (floors, walls), leading to floating objects, interpenetration, and spatial misalignment, whereas InSpace generates a complete scene with structural layout and well-grounded assets from a single ERP image. The paper notes that PanoContext-Former [12], the closest prior ERP-based method, produces inaccurate geometry, lacks texture support, and has no public code, making it infeasible to compare. InSpace also generalizes to the realistic ReplicaPano dataset, producing spatially coherent 3D indoor environments beyond the synthetic ERP-FRONT training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

InSpace builds directly on TRELLIS.2's sparse voxel (O-Voxel) framework and its DiT-based flow-matching pipeline, repurposing the three-stage generation (sparse structure → geometry → material) for full indoor scenes rather than isolated assets. It departs from prior single-image scene generation methods (SceneGen, MIDI, SAM3D) by replacing the limited-FOV perspective input with a 360° ERP image, and from part-aware generation (Omnipart) by decomposing scenes into structural layout plus individual assets rather than semantic parts. The closest prior work, PanoContext-Former, also targets ERP-to-indoor-scene generation but produces only coarse geometry without texture and has no public code. The key architectural departure is the view-selective cross-attention mechanism, which spatially grounds each voxel latent to only the cubemap faces visible from its 3D position, addressing a conditioning mismatch inherent to panoramic inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By generating both structural layout (floors, walls) and textured assets in a single pipeline, InSpace eliminates the floating/interpenetration artifacts that plague asset-only methods and removes the need for post-hoc physics-based optimization, making it directly applicable to robotics simulation, digital twins, and embodied agent training. The ERP-FRONT dataset (29K paired ERP-image–mesh pairs) fills a critical data gap that previously prevented training on this task. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-room or outdoor-adjacent scenes, how well it generalizes beyond the synthetic 3D-FRONT aesthetic to photorealistic captures (only lightly tested on ReplicaPano), and whether the 3D U-Net OBB detector can handle highly cluttered or non-rigid layouts.
