## Problem

Existing adversarial training for semantic segmentation relies on prediction-driven attacks that push features across decision boundaries among in-distribution classes while remaining close to the ID feature manifold, never exposing models to genuinely atypical, low-density representations. The paper asks whether norm-bounded perturbations can induce OOD-like feature deviations and, if so, how to exploit this qualitatively distinct failure mode to strengthen both attacks and defenses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260878/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image, a segmentation model (PSPNet or DeepLabv3 with ResNet-50 backbone), and pre-computed ID semantic prototypes maintained via exponential moving average (momentum 0.99) over clean mini-batches. It produces adversarial samples constrained to an l∞-norm ball (ε=8/255, step size α=3/255) whose latent features exhibit OOD-like properties (lower energy scores, near-uniform softmax). These adversarial samples feed into adversarial training, yielding a robust segmentation model that outputs standard pixel-wise class masks at inference with no additional cost.

## Method

SegOOD introduces two complementary objectives in latent feature space, optimized jointly with a standard prediction-driven loss via PGD-style iterative updates. The Feature Wasserstein Separation (FWS) objective maximizes the Wasserstein distance between clean ID semantic prototypes and adversarial feature clusters; to reduce the O(N³) transport cost to O(C³), clean features are aggregated into per-class prototypes while adversarial features are clustered via F-SLIC, a feature-aware superpixel method that combines normalized negative cosine similarity in feature space with spatial proximity. The Weighted K-Nearest-Neighbor Separation (WKS) objective enforces local deviation by penalizing each adversarial superpixel prototype whose cosine similarity to its K nearest ID prototypes exceeds a margin γ=0.3, with weights proportional to superpixel area to prevent dilution by numerous small clusters (formalized in Theorem 2). For adversarial training, the paper evaluates two strategies: Ours-Co (unified objective) and Ours-Sep (alternating prediction-only and OOD-only objectives across half the training samples each), the latter providing complementary supervision at both the prediction and feature levels.

## Results

On Pascal VOC with PSPNet (3-iteration AT), Ours-Sep achieves 30.15 mIoU under the SegOOD attack, substantially outperforming the best prior method SegPGD3-AT (19.82) by over 10 points, while maintaining competitive clean accuracy (74.39 vs. 75.38 for SegPGD3-AT). On Cityscapes, the gap widens further: Ours-Sep reaches 32.02 mIoU under SegOOD versus 14.72–19.03 for all baselines. With 7 attack iterations, Ours-Sep sustains 31.10 mIoU under SegOOD on Pascal VOC, compared to 14.83–17.39 for the strongest baselines, confirming the advantage is not an artifact of iteration count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ours-Sep (separate AT with SegOOD + SegPGD) dominates all six baselines on the SegOOD attack column by a wide margin: 30.15 mIoU on Pascal VOC (PSPNet) versus 19.82 (SegPGD3-AT), 16.73 (SEA3-AT), 15.20 (DDC-AT), 14.87 (CosPGD3-AT), and 13.98 (RPPGD3-AT). The advantage is even more pronounced on Cityscapes, where Ours-Sep reaches 32.02 versus 14.72–19.03 for the same baselines. On standard ID-style attacks (SegPGD, CosPGD, RPPGD), Ours-Sep also achieves the highest or near-highest mIoU, indicating that the OOD-focused training does not sacrifice robustness to conventional threats. DDC-AT, the earliest AT method, remains the weakest across nearly all attack columns. The unified Ours-Co variant is consistently weaker than Ours-Sep, confirming that separating prediction-driven and OOD-driven adversarial samples during training yields complementary supervision. Against black-box transfer attacks (Table 5), Ours-Sep again leads by 8–13 mIoU points over the best baseline, particularly under transfer-based SegOOD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial training literature for semantic segmentation (DDC-AT, SegPGD, CosPGD, RPPGD) by identifying a fundamental limitation of prediction-driven attacks: under mild regularity assumptions, they are confined to tangent directions of the ID feature manifold and cannot produce OOD-like representations. It builds on the PGD iterative framework and the SLIC superpixel algorithm, but repurposes both for a qualitatively different objective—driving features away from the ID manifold via Wasserstein separation and weighted KNN constraints in latent space. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) formalizes why prior attacks remain on-manifold, providing a principled motivation for the new OOD-oriented objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a new axis of adversarial vulnerability in segmentation: OOD-like feature deviations that existing ID-style AT cannot address, and demonstrates that incorporating such perturbations into training yields substantially better robustness (e.g., ~30 mIoU vs. ~15–20 under SegOOD attack) without inference overhead. Open questions include whether the strong manifold-regularity assumptions (Assumptions 1–4) hold for modern transformer-based segmenters, whether the OOD-like attack paradigm transfers to other dense prediction tasks (detection, depth), and whether the two-dataset/two-architecture evaluation (PSPNet, DeepLabv3 on Pascal VOC and Cityscapes) generalizes to larger backbones and more diverse scenes. The F-SLIC clustering and prototype-based compression are practical but may not scale trivially to very high-resolution inputs.
