## Problem

Diffusion models excel at from-scratch generation but struggle with versatile content generation tasks (inpainting, outpainting, motion completion, 3D texturing) that require producing content conditioned on partially observed inputs. Training-based approaches are costly and generalize poorly across tasks, while existing training-free synchronization methods (e.g., SyncSDE) only guide the pre-generated region and leave the unobserved region unconstrained, producing globally inconsistent results when the missing area is large.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-generated content tensor (image latent, motion sequence, or rendered mesh view), a binary mask delineating the unobserved region, and a text conditioning embedding. For image inpainting it operates in the latent space of pretrained VAEs (512×512 or 1024×1024); for outpainting it autoregressively generates 512×512 patches with a 384-pixel stride to produce 2048×512 wide images; for 3D texturing it iterates over 10 overlapping viewpoints using depth-conditioned ControlNet before baking into a single texture map. The output is a fully completed sample in the same modality as the input, with the pre-generated region preserved exactly.

## Method

ALM modifies the reverse diffusion sampling process by introducing, at each timestep, an explicit update ΔY_t applied only to the unobserved region. This update is derived as a score-based gradient step on a composite objective combining a conditional likelihood term (aligning the unobserved region with the pre-generated context) and a joint log-density term (keeping the blended sample in high-density regions of the data distribution), with both scores estimated via the frozen pretrained noise-prediction network. The key theoretical contribution is an acceleration strategy that collapses N iterative refinement steps into a single update by exploiting the L-Lipschitz continuity of the noise network, yielding an O(1) per-timestep cost with a decaying hyperparameter schedule to maintain the small-update assumption. The final sampling step then uses a modified DDIM reverse process that blends the optimized unobserved region with the pre-generated content, requiring no retraining of the underlying diffusion model.

## Results

On image inpainting (Table 1), ALM achieves LPIPS 0.283 / MSE 0.143 / M-SSIM 0.351 on AFHQ and LPIPS 0.251 / MSE 0.126 / M-SSIM 0.417 on CelebA-HQ, outperforming all training-based (SDI, BrushNet, PowerPaint) and training-free (SyncSDE, HD-Painter) baselines on every metric. The M-SSIM gap is particularly large: ALM scores 0.351 vs. 0.302 (SyncSDE) on AFHQ and 0.417 vs. 0.368 (SDI) on CelebA-HQ, confirming the benefit of explicit unobserved-region optimization. On wide image generation (Table 4), ALM achieves FID 83.41 and KID 42.98, beating SyncSDE (FID 85.82, KID 51.84) and StochSync (FID 113.21, KID 92.10). On human motion completion (Table 5), ALM reaches FID 0.311 (first-half) and 0.236 (last-half), substantially outperforming the training-based CondMDI (FID 0.620 and 0.362) and training-free Reconstruction Guidance (FID 11.342 and 7.322).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALM outperforms every baseline across all four tasks. In image inpainting (Table 1), ALM beats the strongest training-based method SDI by 0.017 LPIPS and 0.049 M-SSIM on CelebA-HQ, and the strongest training-free method SyncSDE by 0.021 LPIPS and 0.049 M-SSIM on AFHQ; HD-Painter and BrushNet trail further behind. Against large-scale models (Table 2), ALM (SDXL backbone) surpasses FLUX-Inpainting by 0.018 LPIPS and 0.120 M-SSIM on AFHQ, and Qwen-Image-Inpainting by 0.017 M-SSIM on CelebA-HQ, all without any task-specific training. In wide image generation (Table 4), ALM reduces FID from 85.82 (SyncSDE) to 83.41 and KID from 51.84 to 42.98, while also achieving the highest Aesthetic Score (6.133) and Q-Align (4.581); StochSync is the weakest at FID 113.21. For human motion completion (Table 5), ALM achieves FID 0.311/0.447/0.236 across three scenarios, beating training-based CondMDI (0.620/0.594/0.362) by roughly 2× and training-free Reconstruction Guidance (11.342/12.806/7.322) by over 30×. In 3D mesh texturing (Table 6), ALM attains the best FID (155.46) and KID (74.41), outperforming SyncSDE (165.04/82.75), StochSync (163.24/82.83), and task-specific methods like TEXTure (184.66/94.69) and TexPainter (191.17/112.48).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SyncSDE (CVPR 2025), which provides a probabilistic score-factorization framework for diffusion synchronization but only guides the pre-generated region, leaving the unobserved region to emerge implicitly. ALM departs from this by formulating synchronization as explicit score-based likelihood maximization over the unobserved variable, introducing a composite objective (conditional likelihood + joint log-density) that directly updates the unobserved region at each diffusion step. It also positions itself against the broader synchronization family (SyncTweedies, StochSync) and training-based inpainting/completion methods (BrushNet, PowerPaint, CondMDI), arguing that a single training-free sampling modification can replace task-specific fine-tuning across modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single training-free sampling modification transforms diverse pretrained diffusion backbones (SD, SDXL, FLUX, motion U-Nets, ControlNet) into versatile content generators without retraining, and the one-step acceleration yields ~185× speedup over the iterative formulation. The key open question is how the method behaves when the unobserved region constitutes the vast majority of the output (e.g., very large outpainting or sparse keyframe motion), where the Lipschitz-based one-step approximation may degrade. Broader implications include whether the likelihood-maximization principle can be extended to other structured generation settings (e.g., multi-image consistency, 4D content) beyond the pairwise pre-generated/unobserved split assumed here.
