## Problem

Hyperspectral imaging models remain tightly coupled to specific sensing hardware: minor changes in spectral band discretization, spatial resolution, or sensor type can invalidate trained networks, despite all sensors observing the same underlying material reflectance physics. Existing hyperspectral foundation models (e.g., SpectralGPT, HyperSIGMA, HyperFree) are predominantly trained on homogeneous satellite imagery and implicitly assume fixed spectral configurations and spatial scales, causing significant performance degradation when deployed across heterogeneous ground-level, medical, or driving scenarios. The paper identifies three structural sources of cross-sensor inconsistency—spectral coordinate misalignment, spatial-scale heterogeneity, and domain data imbalance—that jointly break the physical commonality shared across sensors.

## Input / Output

The model consumes hyperspectral image cubes with highly variable spectral dimensions (15–826 bands), wavelength coordinate vectors, and physical spatial scales spanning seven orders of magnitude (10⁻⁷ to 10¹ m/pixel). Rather than treating band count as a fixed tensor dimension, the input is reparameterized through a shared continuous wavelength space, so the same architecture handles any sensor configuration without architectural modification. The encoder outputs a domain-agnostic token sequence (N×D) that is adapted via lightweight task-specific heads for classification, segmentation, detection, change detection, anomaly detection, and saliency detection.

## Method

OneHSI is a ViT-Huge masked autoencoder with three physically motivated modules. SCORE reparameterizes each spectral channel as a sample of a shared continuous function over wavelength by applying a learnable mapping Φ_map to normalized wavelength coordinates, producing a coordinate-conditioned projection matrix that aligns heterogeneous band discretizations into a common D-dimensional spectral basis, followed by FiLM modulation to absorb residual sensor-specific statistics. SEAT decouples relational modeling from scale-specific adaptation: self-attention layers are shared across all domains to preserve long-range spectral-spatial dependencies, while feed-forward layers are replaced by a mixture of scale-aware experts gated by Gaussian affinity over log₁₀(physical scale), with top-K routing and an expert-balancing loss to prevent collapse. CORE enforces balanced per-domain mini-batch sampling and a domain-adversarial objective with gradient reversal to suppress sensor-discriminative cues. Training jointly optimizes a reconstruction loss (75% MAE masking in the physically aligned feature space), an expert-balancing loss, and a domain-adversarial loss, yielding a single encoder that generalizes across remote sensing, proximal, medical, and driving domains.

## Results

Across 16 benchmarks spanning six tasks (classification, segmentation, detection, change detection, anomaly detection, saliency) and four domains (remote, proximal, medical, driving), OneHSI achieves state-of-the-art performance on the majority of settings. Headline numbers include 95.53% OA on NaSC-TG2 (vs. 94.51% for S²Mamba), 81.02% Precision / 36.21% mAP on M2SODAI maritime detection (vs. 76.58%/33.42% for HyperFree), and 76.85% KA on HistologyHSI-GB (vs. 72.01% for MambaHSI, a +4.84% gain under extreme micrometer-scale resolution). In the controlled re-training comparison (Table 3), OneHSI uses only 363G FLOPs versus 510G (HyperFree) and 841G (HyperSIGMA) while achieving the highest OA on all four tested domains under both full fine-tuning and frozen-encoder protocols.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneHSI outperforms all hyperspectral foundation model baselines (HyperSIGMA, HyperFree) on nearly every benchmark, with the largest margins in cross-sensor and cross-scale settings: +4.45% Precision on M2SODAI detection over HyperFree, +4.84% KA on HistologyHSI-GB over MambaHSI, and +2.94% OA on HSDermos melanoma classification over PWS-CS. Against task-specific SOTA methods (SpectralFormer, MambaHSI, S²Mamba, SS-Mamba), OneHSI wins on 14 of 16 benchmarks; the sole exception is NPU object detection mAP, where SS-Mamba edges OneHSI by 0.65% (78.33 vs. 77.68), though OneHSI leads on Precision (96.64 vs. 96.43) and AP@0.5 (96.39 vs. 95.84). In the controlled re-training experiment (Table 3), OneHSI surpasses both HyperSIGMA and HyperFree on all four domains under both full fine-tuning and frozen-encoder protocols while using 29–57% fewer FLOPs (363G vs. 510G/841G). Few-shot segmentation (Table 4) shows OneHSI leading in 1-shot OA on all four domains (e.g., 85.06% vs. 81.43% on HeiPorSPECTRAL), with the gap widening at 10 shots. Robustness tests confirm OneHSI degrades only 0.36% OA under 5 nm wavelength jitter and 2.59% average OA under sensor noise, roughly half the degradation of HyperSIGMA (5.03%) and HyperFree (5.60%).

## Relation to Prior Work

OneHSI builds on the MAE self-supervised pre-training paradigm (He et al., CVPR 2022) and the sparse mixture-of-experts routing mechanism (Riquelme et al., NeurIPS 2021), but departs from prior hyperspectral foundation models (SpectralGPT, HyperSIGMA, HyperFree, SpectralEarth) that are predominantly trained on homogeneous satellite imagery and implicitly assume fixed spectral bands and spatial scales. The key conceptual departure is treating spectral channels as samples of a shared continuous wavelength function (SCORE) rather than arbitrary tensor dimensions, and decoupling scale-invariant relational attention from scale-conditioned feed-forward transformation (SEAT). This positions OneHSI as a physics-motivated unification of three previously unaddressed structural heterogeneities—spectral misalignment, spatial-scale inconsistency, and domain imbalance—within a single architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent practical bottleneck: hyperspectral models trained on one sensor configuration often fail on another, undermining reproducibility and cross-platform deployment. By demonstrating consistent gains across 16 benchmarks spanning four domains and six tasks with lower FLOPs than competing foundation models, OneHSI provides evidence that physics-aligned inductive biases can substitute for brute-force scale. However, the framework assumes reliable wavelength metadata and does not explicitly model atmospheric or medium effects, limiting its applicability to uncalibrated or heavily degraded acquisitions. Open questions include whether the continuous-wavelength reparameterization generalizes to sensors with highly non-uniform spectral response functions, and whether the approach scales to truly large-scale (billions of pixels) pre-training regimes.
