## Problem

Existing panorama-based 2D lifting methods for 3D scene generation emphasize appearance synthesis but neglect intrinsic perception of geometry, textures, and PBR materials, typically relying on off-the-shelf depth estimators that produce scenes incompatible with modern graphics pipelines. Additionally, no publicly available panorama dataset covers both indoor and outdoor scenes with dense, pixel-aligned geometry and material annotations, limiting the training of unified panorama perception models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261947/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

OmniX accepts perspective RGB images, text prompts, RGB panoramas, or masked panoramas as conditioning inputs, all spatially aligned at 512×1024 equirectangular resolution and sharing a common 2D positional encoding. The framework produces equirectangular panoramas in any combination of modalities (RGB, distance, surface normal, albedo, roughness, metallic) via a generative chain (image → panorama → panorama with intrinsic properties), and ultimately a PBR-ready 3D mesh with spherical UV-mapped material textures. Multiple conditioning inputs and multiple target outputs can be specified simultaneously through a MIMO flow matching formulation.

## Method

OmniX repurposes the pre-trained Flux.1-dev 2D flow matching model via two key architectural innovations. First, Circular Synchronization enforces circular translation equivariance in all spatial operators—replacing standard padding with circular padding for convolutions and applying token padding plus attention masking for RoPE-based attentions—achieving seamless equirectangular generation as a training-free structural constraint without modifying pretrained weights. Second, the Separate-Adapter design assigns modality-specific LoRA modules to each input type, enabling flexible cross-modal adaptation while preserving the pretrained generative prior and allowing new modalities to be added with minimal weight disruption. Training uses a standard flow matching velocity-prediction loss on the PanoX synthetic dataset (10,000+ instances, 60,000 panoramas rendered in Unreal Engine 5), with 12 adapter variants covering generation, perception, and masked completion tasks.

## Results

On panorama generation (Laval Indoor), OmniX achieves the best FID (7.4), CLIP-FID (2.5), FAED (5.2), and CLIP Score (28.47), outperforming CubeDiff (FID 9.5, FAED 18.4). On SUN360, OmniX attains the best KID (0.66), CLIP-FID (6.5), and CLIP Score (27.54). For panoramic intrinsic estimation on PanoX-OutDomain, OmniX achieves albedo PSNR of 17.76 (vs. 10.91 for the best baseline DiffusionRenderer), roughness PSNR of 16.21 (vs. 10.45), and metallic PSNR of 18.87 (vs. 14.45). On geometry estimation, OmniX achieves the best normal angular error (mean 27.14°, 30° threshold 0.663) and second-best depth accuracy (AbsRel 0.158) behind only MoGe (AbsRel 0.106).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On panorama generation, OmniX outperforms the strongest baseline CubeDiff on Laval Indoor across FID (7.4 vs. 9.5), CLIP-FID (2.5 vs. 3.2), FAED (5.2 vs. 18.4), and CLIP Score (28.47 vs. 27.02), though CubeDiff retains a slight edge on KID (0.32 vs. 0.41). On SUN360, OmniX wins on KID (0.66 vs. 1.33), CLIP-FID (6.5 vs. 8.1), and CLIP Score (27.54 vs. 25.00) but trails CubeDiff on FID (39.4 vs. 25.5) and FAED (8.7 vs. 7.6). For intrinsic decomposition, OmniX dramatically surpasses all five baselines (RGB↔X, MGNet, IDArb, IID, DiffusionRenderer) on albedo, roughness, and metallic PSNR/LPIPS on PanoX-OutDomain, with the largest gap over DiffusionRenderer on albedo (17.76 vs. 10.91 PSNR). On geometry estimation, OmniX achieves the best normal accuracy (mean 27.14°, 30°-threshold 0.663) over all six baselines including DiffusionRenderer (mean 97.19°) and MGNet (mean 79.96°), while MoGe retains the best depth AbsRel (0.106 vs. OmniX's 0.158) despite using 100× more training data (9.0M vs. 0.087M depth annotations). The panoramic-specific baselines DepthAnywhere and DepthAnyCamera are substantially outperformed by OmniX on both depth and normal metrics, confirming the advantage of the unified flow-matching formulation over dedicated depth estimators.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniX extends the 2D lifting paradigm for 3D scene generation by repurposing the pre-trained Flux.1-dev flow matching model with lightweight LoRA adapters, departing from prior panorama generation methods (CubeDiff, Diffusion360, PanoDiffusion) that focus solely on appearance and rely on off-the-shelf depth estimators. The key conceptual departure is treating panorama perception of intrinsic properties (albedo, roughness, metallic, normals, distance) as a generative task within the same unified framework, rather than as a separate regression problem. The circular synchronization technique offers a structural alternative to prior latent-level blending strategies (circular blending, latent rotation) by enforcing circular translation equivariance directly in spatial operators, and the modality-specific adapter design (Separate-Adapter) generalizes the single-LoRA adaptation pattern to handle multiple cross-modal inputs simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniX bridges a critical gap between 2D generative models and production graphics pipelines by producing PBR-ready 3D scenes (with albedo, roughness, metallic, normals) from a single image, enabling relighting, simulation, and free exploration in standard engines like Blender. The PanoX dataset addresses a real scarcity in the field as the first panorama benchmark covering both indoor and outdoor scenes with dense geometry and material annotations. However, the authors themselves note that 2D image priors may have limited transferability to PBR material estimation, distance accuracy remains insufficient for smooth surfaces, and metallic prediction generalizes poorly—suggesting that the fundamental mismatch between neural rendering and physically-based rendering is not yet resolved. Open questions include whether dedicated 3D-aware priors or larger-scale PBR panorama data could close these gaps.
