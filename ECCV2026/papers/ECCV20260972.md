## Problem

Existing cross-modal jailbreak attacks on MLLMs optimize visual and textual perturbations independently via cross-entropy minimization, causing the two modalities to drive model representations toward separate, misaligned subregions of the vulnerability space rather than reinforcing each other. Additionally, most cross-modal methods require expensive per-query optimization and lack universal transferability across diverse harmful queries. The paper asks whether the apparent misalignment between visual and textual jailbreak directions is actually a projection of a single shared vulnerability axis that can be explicitly extracted and used to coordinate both modalities.

## Input / Output

The method takes a benign 224×224 natural photograph (deliberately unrelated to the harmful content) and a set of N diverse harmful text queries as training input, and produces a single universal perturbation pair: a continuous pixel-level visual perturbation δ_v bounded by ‖δ_v‖_∞ ≤ 32/255 and a discrete 20-token textual suffix δ_t. At inference, the same pair is appended to any new harmful query and combined with the same benign image in a single forward pass, requiring no per-query optimization. The textual suffix is optimized over the model's vocabulary via gradient-ranked candidate selection, while the visual perturbation is a dense additive noise pattern on the image.

## Method

Co-Steer operates in two stages. First, it extracts a universal jailbreak steering vector v_jailbreak by collecting last-token representation shifts at a peak cross-modal fusion layer (e.g., layer 15 for InternVL2-8B) from successful visual and textual jailbreaks across 100 diverse queries, then applying SVD to the 2N×d shift matrix to identify the principal vulnerability direction. Second, it jointly optimizes the universal perturbation pair via alternating block coordinate descent—momentum PGD for the continuous visual perturbation and GCG-style greedy coordinate search for the discrete textual suffix—under a combined loss that adds a steering alignment term L_steer to the standard cross-entropy loss. L_steer penalizes the cosine deviation of each modality's induced representation shift from v_jailbreak, forcing both perturbations to push the model along the same vulnerability axis rather than drifting toward modality-specific local optima. Training uses batches of diverse harmful queries to enforce universality, and the resulting pair transfers zero-shot to unseen queries (3–6 pp degradation) and to black-box commercial models (49–53% ASR on GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, and Qwen2.5-VL-72B).

## Results

Co-Steer achieves state-of-the-art attack success rate (ASR) across all nine benchmark–model combinations: 84.65–88.94% on MM-SafetyBench, 78.00–91.53% on HarmBench, and 89.39–93.29% on AdvBench, evaluated on InternVL2-8B, Qwen2-VL-7B, and MiniGPT-4-13B. On black-box transfer to commercial models (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro), Co-Steer reaches 49.03–52.71% ASR, surpassing the strongest baseline BAP by 7.62–8.95 pp. The universal perturbation pair requires only a single forward pass at test time and generalizes to 1,580 unseen queries with only 3–6 pp degradation from training performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Co-Steer outperforms all five baselines across every benchmark–model combination. Against UMK [36], the closest cross-modal baseline that optimizes visual and textual perturbations independently with cross-entropy only, Co-Steer gains 2.21–7.85 pp on MM-SafetyBench, up to 23.50 pp on HarmBench (InternVL2), and 3.08–6.46 pp on AdvBench, confirming that explicit steering alignment adds substantial value over independent optimization. Against BAP [40], the strongest single-modality-aware method, Co-Steer leads by 27.91 pp on HarmBench (InternVL2) and 7.62–8.95 pp on black-box commercial transfer. Text-only GCG [44] and PAIR [6] are consistently outperformed by 20–40 pp, highlighting the advantage of cross-modal coordination. Visual-only VAJM [29] performs poorly on InternVL2 (20.54% on MM-SafetyBench) and shows minimal cross-model transfer (10.55–14.69%), confirming that image perturbations alone lack transferability. The gains are largest on models with stronger safety alignment (InternVL2, Qwen2-VL), where independent optimization struggles to overcome safety barriers without coordinated multi-modal pressure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-modal jailbreak attack line (JPS, CAMO, UMK, BAP) by introducing an explicit representation-space coordination mechanism absent from prior methods. It builds directly on GCG's greedy coordinate descent for the textual suffix and momentum PGD for the visual perturbation, but departs from the standard cross-entropy-only optimization by adding a steering alignment loss derived from the representation-engineering literature (Zou et al. 2023; Ball et al. 2024; Pan et al. 2025). The key conceptual departure is the empirical claim that visual and textual jailbreak shifts are misaligned projections of a single SVD-extracted vulnerability direction, reframing cross-modal coordination as directional alignment rather than independent optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that MLLM jailbreak vulnerabilities concentrate in a shared low-rank subspace across modalities and queries provides a mechanistic explanation for why cross-modal attacks work and suggests that future safety training should explicitly regularize representation geometry in intermediate fusion layers. The universal perturbation pair's transfer to closed-source commercial models (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Qwen2.5-VL-72B at ~49–53% ASR) indicates the vulnerability is structural rather than model-specific, raising practical safety concerns. Open questions include whether the steering vector generalizes to non-ViT architectures, whether the ~50% variance concentration holds for newer safety-aligned models, and whether the white-box source-model requirement for vector extraction can be relaxed to a fully black-box setting.
