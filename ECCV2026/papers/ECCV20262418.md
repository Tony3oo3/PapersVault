## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for autonomous driving VQA are fundamentally optimized for discrete token generation, making them ill-suited for regressing continuous spatial coordinates. When forced to output bounding-box coordinates, these models suffer severe hallucinations and localization failures, conflating logical reasoning with precise spatial grounding. This paper asks whether decoupling localization from reasoning—by having the VLM predict only discrete object IDs—can eliminate coordinate hallucination without any architectural modification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes six surround-camera images (standardized to 224×224, with a 336×336 variant also tested) that have been pre-annotated with detected bounding boxes drawn directly on the image, each labeled with a unique discrete ID at its center and the camera perspective in the upper-left corner, alongside a textual driving query. The VLM outputs a natural-language response containing only discrete object references in the format ⟨id, camera_view⟩ (e.g., ⟨c3, CAM_FRONT⟩), never raw coordinates. A deterministic offline lookup table then maps each predicted ID back to its 2D center coordinates (x, y), producing the final grounded spatial output.

## Method

The Object-Centric Prompting Strategy (OCPS) operates in three stages: (1) an off-the-shelf detector extracts candidate bounding boxes, which are filtered via IoU matching against ground truth during training (threshold τ) or by highest confidence during inference; (2) the filtered boxes are drawn onto the raw images with unique IDs, and the VLM is fine-tuned (via LoRA or LLaMA-Adapter v2) with standard cross-entropy loss to predict only these discrete IDs rather than regressing coordinates; (3) at inference, a cached dictionary D(id, view) → (x, y) deterministically converts predicted IDs back to precise spatial coordinates. The key insight is that this transforms an error-prone continuous regression problem into a reliable discrete classification task, exploiting the VLM's natural strength in token-level prediction while requiring zero structural changes to the underlying architecture. The approach is validated as model-agnostic across DriveLM-agent (LLaMA-7B), Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and InternVL-3.5-4B, yielding consistent 8–12% Accuracy and 10–20% Match improvements over each baseline.

## Results

On the DriveLM-nuScenes dataset, the Object-Centric Prompting Strategy (OCPS) consistently improves all three tested VLMs. For DriveLM-agent, Accuracy rises from 0.6024 to 0.6850 (+8.26%) and Match from 32.29 to 42.68 (+10.39%). For the stronger Qwen2.5-VL, gains are even larger: Accuracy jumps to 0.8194 (+11.85%) and Match to 54.56 (+20.02%). InternVL-3.5 achieves the highest absolute scores with Accuracy 0.8263, Match 57.76, and Final 0.6724, outperforming all other configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares OCPS against three VLM baselines: DriveLM-agent (LLaMA-7B), Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and InternVL-3.5-4B. OCPS wins decisively on every metric for all three baselines. DriveLM-agent gains +8.26% in Accuracy and +10.39% in Match, with Final improving from 0.5621 to 0.6023. Qwen2.5-VL shows the largest relative improvement, with Accuracy surging by 11.85% (0.7009→0.8194) and Match by 20.02% (34.54→54.56), lifting Final from 0.5814 to 0.6520. InternVL-3.5 achieves the strongest absolute performance with OCPS, reaching 0.8263 Accuracy and 57.76 Match, and its CIDEr score nearly triples from 0.1598 to 0.4568. The only minor trade-off is a negligible Chat score drop (e.g., 72.71→72.63 for DriveLM-agent), which the authors attribute to the stricter object-centric output format. Overall, OCPS is a consistent, model-agnostic plug-and-play improvement that never degrades any baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of visual prompting for MLLMs and autonomous driving VQA. It builds on the visual prompting paradigm (Set-of-Mark, VIP-LLaVA) that overlays markers on images to guide attention, but departs from general-purpose markers by introducing an object-centric representation: detected bounding boxes annotated with discrete IDs that the VLM must predict rather than continuous coordinates. It also extends the driving VQA line (DriveLM, DriveVLM, nuScenes-QA) by explicitly addressing the spatial grounding bottleneck through a decoupling strategy—transforming coordinate regression into discrete ID matching with an offline lookup table—rather than modifying model architecture as prior grounding approaches (Shikra, LLaVA-Grounding, Ferret-v2) do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, model-agnostic nature of the approach (no structural modifications, just data representation changes) makes it immediately applicable to any existing driving VLM pipeline, and the consistent gains across three architecturally distinct models (LLaMA-based, Qwen, InternVL) strengthen the case for broad adoption. However, validation is limited to a single dataset (DriveLM-nuScenes), and the Ground Truth ablation reveals that detection quality remains a significant bottleneck (e.g., Match 42.68 vs. GT 53.05), suggesting that improvements in upstream object detection would yield further gains. Open questions include generalizability to other driving benchmarks (Waymo, Argoverse), whether the approach transfers to 3D grounding or multi-frame temporal reasoning, and how it interacts with increasingly strong native spatial capabilities in newer VLMs.
