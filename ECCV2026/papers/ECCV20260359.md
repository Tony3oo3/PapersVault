## Problem

Hybrid NeRF–3D Gaussian Splatting representations (e.g., EKS) combine neural field fidelity with explicit geometry for editing, but their reliance on stochastic volumetric sampling and per-sample 3D KNN lookups creates a severe computational bottleneck that reduces frame rates to non-interactive levels. This prevents real-time rendering, seamless physics-engine integration, and topology-agnostic scene manipulation in unbounded environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260359/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Training consumes multi-view RGB images with calibrated camera poses to optimize a set of Neural Anchors (3D Gaussians with latent feature vectors initialized from a multi-resolution hash grid). At inference, the system takes camera rays as input and produces a rendered RGB image via single-pass volumetric compositing, while simultaneously maintaining an editable scene representation where each anchor is a self-contained entity carrying explicit geometry (position, covariance, opacity) and an implicit appearance code, enabling rigid or non-rigid manipulation without retraining.

## Method

IRIS replaces stochastic ray marching with an analytical Ray Intersection Selector (RIS) that solves ray–oriented-ellipsoid intersection equations (offloaded to GPU RT cores via OptiX with icosahedron proxies) to identify only structurally meaningful sample points, eliminating empty-space processing. A Ray-Coherent Aggregation (RCA) module then performs a vectorized 1D sliding-window KNN along the sorted ray, weighting neighbors by Mahalanobis distance with a spatial validity mask to prevent blending of disjoint objects, thereby bypassing costly 3D spatial searches. A shallow MLP decodes the aggregated latent vector and view direction into view-dependent color and a truncated-exponential density, which is spatially modulated by the explicit Gaussian opacity before point-based volumetric integration. Visibility-aware pruning removes unvisited anchors over training, and Infinitesimal Surface Transformation adjusts the query view direction into the local deformed surface frame to preserve specular consistency under non-rigid editing.

## Results

On the Mip-NeRF 360 indoor split, IRIS achieves 30.85 average PSNR, outperforming all editable baselines (Radiance Meshes 29.29, EKS 28.48) and explicit static methods (3DGS 30.41, Triangle Splatting 30.80), trailing only the purely implicit Mip-NeRF 360 (31.72). On the NeRF Synthetic dataset, IRIS reaches 32.65 PSNR (vs. EKS 33.12) while rendering at 6.04 FPS—approximately 30× faster than EKS (0.183 FPS)—and trains in 51 minutes versus EKS's 26 hours. On Deep Blending, IRIS attains state-of-the-art fidelity among editable methods (29.33/30.32 PSNR on drjohnson/playroom), and on the NeuralEditor deformation benchmark it matches EKS closely (25.70 vs. 25.92 PSNR) while suppressing ghosting artifacts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary editable baseline EKS [30], IRIS wins decisively on indoor Mip-NeRF 360 (30.85 vs. 28.48 PSNR, +2.37 dB) and Deep Blending (29.33/30.32 vs. 29.22/29.67), while EKS retains a narrow edge on NeRF Synthetic PSNR (33.12 vs. 32.65) and the deformation benchmark (25.92 vs. 25.70). IRIS substantially outperforms EKS in efficiency: 6.04 FPS vs. 0.183 FPS (~33×) and 51 min vs. 26 h training. Against Radiance Meshes [15], IRIS wins on indoor Mip-NeRF 360 (30.85 vs. 29.29) and Deep Blending but trails on outdoor scenes (24.11 vs. 24.73). Compared to static methods, IRIS matches or exceeds 3DGS and Triangle Splatting on indoor scenes (30.85 vs. 30.41/30.80) while remaining editable, and is competitive on outdoor scenes (24.11 vs. 24.64/24.27). On the deformation benchmark, IRIS (25.70 PSNR, 0.932 SSIM) is within 0.22 dB of EKS and clearly ahead of NeuralEditor (25.12) and GaMeS (24.76), while the ablation in Table 5 shows IRIS's RCA aggregation outperforms EKS's KNN by 0.58 PSNR at 16× higher FPS (0.30 vs. 0.019).

## Relation to Prior Work

IRIS builds directly on the hybrid Gaussian-encoded neural field paradigm introduced by EKS [30], which uses 3D Gaussians as spatial anchors for implicit neural features but relies on stochastic volumetric sampling and 3D KNN lookups for feature aggregation. The key departure is replacing both of these bottlenecks: the Ray Intersection Selector (RIS) substitutes probabilistic ray marching with analytical ray-ellipsoid intersection (inspired by 3DGRT [17] and offloaded to OptiX RT cores), while Ray-Coherent Aggregation (RCA) replaces costly 3D KNN with a 1D sliding-window interpolation along the sorted ray. This positions IRIS as a computational re-architecture of the EKS-style pipeline rather than a new representation, retaining the Neural Anchor concept and hash-grid feature initialization while fundamentally changing how samples are selected and features are combined.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30× rendering speedup over EKS (0.183 → 6.038 FPS) with only a marginal PSNR drop (33.12 → 32.65 on NeRF Synthetic) makes interactive, physics-driven editing of neural scenes practically feasible for the first time in this class of methods. The explicit anchor structure also enables topology-agnostic editing and seamless physics engine integration without grid warping, addressing a key limitation of purely implicit approaches. However, the authors acknowledge that finite anchor coverage degrades far-field geometry in unbounded outdoor scenes, and the 1D RCA sliding window remains sensitive to its size parameter W, introducing floater artifacts at sub-optimal settings. Open questions include whether the analytical intersection strategy scales to scenes with millions of primitives and whether the 1D aggregation can be made more robust without reverting to 3D searches.
