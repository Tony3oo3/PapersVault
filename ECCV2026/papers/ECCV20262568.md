## Problem

Existing visual navigation systems implicitly depend on temporally ordered video streams or auxiliary sensors (depth, LiDAR, odometry) to maintain spatial consistency. When these priors are removed—as when deploying robots on crowd-sourced or pre-recorded unordered image collections—current methods suffer from severe perceptual aliasing, noisy topological connections, and catastrophic mapping failures. This paper asks whether reliable closed-loop RGB-only navigation is achievable from purely unordered images without any temporal, odometric, or multimodal priors.

## Input / Output

The system consumes an unordered bag of RGB images (no temporal ordering or odometry), a single goal image, and a live RGB observation stream during execution. It produces a weighted directed topological graph (edge weights = verified local feature inlier counts), a probability distribution over all graph nodes (the belief state), a sequence of visual subgoal images, and low-level velocity commands generated by a pluggable image-based local planner (ViNT or NoMaD). Notably, the graph is built entirely offline from the unordered collection, while the belief and subgoal sequence are updated online at each observation step.

## Method

ULVN integrates three co-designed modules to control error accumulation. For mapping (RAVEL), it retrieves candidate pairs via VPR-trained MegaLoc descriptors indexed in FAISS, verifies them with LightGlue geometric matching using a one-shot data-driven threshold calibrated from k-means clustering of inlier counts, then extracts a Maximum Spanning Forest skeleton and reinserts strong loop closures via a dynamic weight threshold to balance structural reliability with topological redundancy. For localization (BPL), it maintains a belief over all graph nodes using a cumulative reachability matrix (paths up to K=3 hops) for topological prediction, fused with observation likelihoods through an entropy-adaptive mechanism: the normalized Shannon entropy of the prior belief directly sets the fusion weights, so the filter automatically shifts reliance from visual observation to topological prediction when perception degrades. For planning (BASS), it extracts the widest path (maximizing minimum edge weight) via Dijkstra's algorithm and executes in closed loop, triggering dynamic replanning whenever the MAP node deviates from the planned path by more than a topological distance threshold.

## Results

On the end-to-end navigation task in GRScenes, ULVN+NoMaD achieves a 71.9% Success Rate with 0.42 average collisions and 0.7978 SPL, substantially outperforming Uni-Navid (32.0% SR) and UniGoal (61.6% SR). For topological mapping, RAVEL attains an F1-score of 0.7365 and 99.70% accuracy, surpassing PlaceNav top-2 (F1 0.6043) and ViNT top-2 (F1 0.5815). For global localization, BPL reaches 95.49% accuracy on all nodes and 93.99% on the Difficult Path subset, compared to MegaLoc (91.09%/89.03%), ViNT (86.58%/70.50%), and JIST (84.94%/82.25%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ULVN outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated task. On topological mapping, RAVEL (F1 0.7365) decisively beats VGGT (F1 0.1629), PlaceNav top-2 (F1 0.6043), and ViNT top-2 (F1 0.5815), with the gap widening under visual noise (RAVEL drops only 6.56% in F1 vs. 15.70–18.43% for ViNT variants). On localization, BPL (95.49% overall, 93.99% on Difficult Path) surpasses MegaLoc (91.09%/89.03%), ViNT (86.58%/70.50%), and JIST (84.94%/82.25%), with the advantage most pronounced on complex routes with intersections and loops where 1D temporal-distance methods like ViNT collapse. On end-to-end navigation, ULVN+NoMaD (71.9% SR, 0.42 collisions) nearly doubles Uni-Navid's success rate (32.0%) and improves over UniGoal (61.6%) by 10.3 percentage points while halving collisions. The ablation replacing BPL with temporal-distance localization (ULVN+ViNT w. d_temp) drops SR from 68.1% to 59.6%, confirming that graph-based belief propagation is strictly necessary for reliable global localization even when the local planner was trained with temporal distance supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical topological mapping and visual place recognition lineage (FAB-MAP, SeqSLAM) and the recent 1D sequential localization filters (PlaceNav, Xu et al.) to operate on arbitrary 2D graphs without temporal ordering or odometric priors. Where prior RGB-only navigation systems (ViNT, NoMaD, GNM) implicitly assume temporally ordered video streams that yield a one-dimensional landmark chain, ULVN deliberately removes this structure, forcing the system to infer connectivity from appearance alone via calibrated geometric verification and Maximum Spanning Forest pruning. The belief propagation localization module generalizes the 1D sequential filters of PlaceNav and Xu et al. to handle branching and loops, while the entropy-adaptive fusion mechanism is a novel contribution to the Bayesian filtering literature for visual navigation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unordered, odometry-free, RGB-only setting is practically critical for deploying navigation on robots using crowd-sourced or pre-recorded image collections (e.g., real estate photos), where temporal structure and auxiliary sensors are unavailable. A notable open question highlighted by the results is the persistent gap between ~95% localization accuracy and ~71% navigation success rate, revealing that the local visual planner (ViNT/NoMaD) remains the dominant bottleneck rather than the topological framework itself. Future work could explore whether tighter integration of the belief state into the local planner, or training planners directly on unordered image pairs, could close this gap. The released dataset and evaluation pipeline also provide a much-needed benchmark for this underexplored setting.
