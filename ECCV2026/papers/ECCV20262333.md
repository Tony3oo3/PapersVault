## Problem

Existing joint image-feature diffusion models couple low-level VAE latents with high-level semantic features, but the semantic representation space is constructed independently of the generative objective (e.g., via fixed PCA) and remains frozen during training. This raises the question of whether the representation space guiding diffusion should itself adapt to the generative task rather than being externally imposed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262333/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes Gaussian noise as input and jointly denoises both VAE latents (or raw pixels at 256×256) and a low-dimensional semantic representation (8 or 16 channels) extracted from a frozen visual encoder such as DINOv2. The output is a synthesized image, with the representation tokens serving as an auxiliary co-denoised modality that provides semantic structure. In the pixel-space variant, a DeCo encoder-decoder architecture operates on downsampled images with a lightweight pixel decoder reconstructing full-resolution outputs.

## Method

CoReDi replaces the fixed PCA projection of prior joint diffusion models with a learnable linear projection g_φ(z₀) = z₀W_φ that is optimized end-to-end alongside the diffusion transformer, allowing the semantic representation space to coevolve with the generative objective. Stable coevolution requires three ingredients: (1) a stop-gradient on the clean representation target in the flow-matching loss to prevent trivial minimization, (2) batch normalization after the projection to stabilize feature scale and preserve the noise schedule, and (3) explicit regularization (feature variance, orthogonality, or covariance) to prevent per-channel feature collapse. Image and representation tokens are fused via a merged-tokens strategy (channel-wise summation of separate embeddings) before processing by a DiT/SiT backbone, with modality-specific decoding heads predicting velocities for each. The framework extends naturally to pixel-space diffusion by integrating the coevolving representation into DeCo's encoder-decoder architecture, requiring only minimal architectural modifications.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with classifier-free guidance (SiT-XL/2 backbone), CoReDi achieves FID 1.58, sFID 4.33, and IS 297.2 after only 400 epochs, outperforming REPA (FID 1.80 at 800 epochs) and ReDi (FID 1.72 at 800 epochs) while halving the training budget. Without CFG, CoReDi-B/2 reaches FID 16.4 at 400K iterations versus ReDi-B/2 (21.4) and SiT-B/2 (33.0); at XL/2 scale, CoReDi achieves FID 3.4 at 2M iterations, matching ReDi-XL/2 (3.3 at 4M) with half the compute and substantially beating REPA-XL/2 (5.9) and SiT-XL/2 (8.3). In pixel space, CoReDi-L/16 matches DeCo-L/16's FID of 31.3 at 200K iterations already at 100K (FID 31.5), demonstrating a 2× convergence speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoReDi consistently outperforms all baselines on FID. Against SiT-XL/2 (the standard flow-matching baseline), CoReDi improves FID from 2.06 to 1.58 with CFG and from 8.3 to 3.4 without CFG at XL scale, while also requiring far fewer training iterations. Against REPA, CoReDi achieves FID 1.58 vs 1.80 (with CFG) and 3.4 vs 5.9 (without CFG at XL), showing that joint coevolving representations provide stronger guidance than feature alignment alone. Against ReDi (the closest prior joint image-feature method using fixed PCA projections), CoReDi wins on FID at B/2 scale (16.4 vs 21.4 without CFG) and at XL with CFG (1.58 vs 1.72), while at XL without CFG the two are essentially tied (3.4 vs 3.3) but CoReDi reaches this in half the iterations (2M vs 4M). In pixel space, CoReDi-L/16 matches DeCo-L/16's 200K-iteration FID (31.3) at just 100K iterations (31.5), confirming a 2× convergence acceleration. CoReDi also generalizes across visual encoders, improving over ReDi's fixed PCA projection for DINOv2 (30.9→24.7 FID), MoCo v3 (38.2→33.5), SigLIPv2 (36.2→29.1), and MAE (40.3→37.1) at 200K steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the joint image-feature diffusion paradigm introduced by ReDi [21], which couples VAE latents with fixed PCA-projected semantic features from a frozen encoder, by replacing the static projection with a learnable linear layer that coevolves with the diffusion model. It also builds on DeCo [28] for a pixel-space variant, adapting the frequency-decoupled encoder-decoder architecture to jointly model raw pixels and coevolving representations. Conceptually, the stabilization recipe draws heavily on self-supervised learning techniques—stop-gradient (à la BYOL/SimSiam), batch normalization as implicit collapse prevention (à la SimCLR), and variance/covariance/orthogonality regularizers (à la VICReg/Barlow Twins)—repurposed to prevent degenerate solutions when both the representation input and its denoising target become trainable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is a concrete, minimal recipe (learnable projection + stop-gradient + BN + one of three regularizers) that makes adaptive semantic representations viable in joint diffusion, with the finding that all three stabilization components are individually necessary. The extension to pixel-space diffusion via DeCo suggests the approach is not tied to VAE latents, potentially opening the door to higher-fidelity generation without reconstruction bottlenecks. However, all experiments are limited to unconditional ImageNet-256 generation, and it remains open whether coevolving representations transfer to conditional (text-to-image) settings, scale to higher resolutions, or generalize beyond the specific DiT/SiT backbones tested. The spatial-structure analysis (LDS, CDS, RMSC) provides a plausible mechanistic explanation but is correlational rather than causal.
