## Problem

Existing reasoning visual task (RVT) methods suffer from three coupled limitations: token-based visual encoding destroys fine-grained spatial-temporal geometry, supervised fine-tuning produces outputs without verifiable reasoning chains and poor out-of-domain generalization, and each task type (segmentation, grounding, summarization, VQA) demands a separate architecture and training pipeline. No prior framework unifies diverse RVTs across image and video modalities while preserving spatial-temporal continuity and learning reasoning without task-specific tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes an image or video sequence (T frames, where T=1 for images) paired with an implicit text query (e.g., 'segment the object used to hold hot beverages') and produces a task-appropriate output: instance names with segmentation mask file paths, bounding box coordinates, natural language scene summaries, or textual VQA answers. The LLM first constructs a structured JSON digital twin representation (video-level metadata, frame-level spatial descriptions, instance-level masks/depth/semantics) from vision foundation models, then reasons over this representation rather than raw pixels. The full output is a structured rollout sequence with explicit think tokens, a DAG construction plan, the DT representation, iterative code-execution cycles, a task-type tag, and a final answer block.

## Method

DT-R1 trains a 7B LLM (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) via GRPO with LoRA to generate a structured rollout: (1) chain-of-thought analysis of the query, (2) a text-encoded DAG specifying which vision foundation models (SAM2, DepthAnything2, OWLv2, DINOv2, Qwen2.5-VL) to apply and their execution dependencies, (3) iterative reasoning over the resulting DT representation using executable Python code for spatial/temporal computations, and (4) task-type identification followed by the final answer. The novel rule-based reward combines format validation (correct token ordering, acyclic DAG with valid model dependencies) and accuracy validation (code executability, correct task identification, IoU>0.5 for segmentation/grounding or LLM-as-judge for text outputs), with externally generated DT representations and execution results excluded from the policy gradient to prevent reward hacking. This pure RL approach—requiring no annotated reasoning chains or task-specific decoders—enables a single model to handle all four RVT types across both image and video, achieving 1.54 s/query inference (10× faster than the prior zero-shot JiT baseline) while outperforming task-specific SFT models by 4–17% across six benchmarks.

## Results

On ReVOS video reasoning segmentation, DT-R1 achieves an overall J of 0.875 and F of 0.863, surpassing the previous best method JiT (J=0.655, F=0.688) by 33.6% and 25.1% respectively. On JiTBench, DT-R1 attains J scores of 0.837, 0.808, and 0.785 across difficulty levels 1–3, exceeding JiT by 4.5–4.2–3.8%. For image reasoning segmentation on ReasonSeg, DT-R1 reaches gIoU of 0.746 (short queries) and 0.812 (long queries), a 12.8–12.9% improvement over JiT, and generalizes to the out-of-domain LLM-Seg40K with gIoU 0.641 vs. JiT's 0.485 (+15.6%). On RVTBench grounding, DT-R1 achieves cIoU of 0.670/0.647/0.625/0.602 across levels 1–4, and on VQA it scores 0.642 BLEU-4 at level 4 versus VideoR1-7B's 0.462.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DT-R1 consistently outperforms all baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against LISA (7B/13B) [14], the end-to-end reasoning segmentation baseline, DT-R1 improves overall J on ReVOS from 0.391–0.398 to 0.875 (+122–123%), and on ReasonSeg from gIoU 0.483–0.554 to 0.746 (+34–54%). Compared to the zero-shot digital twin method JiT [31], which is the closest prior work, DT-R1 gains 33.6% J on ReVOS, 4.2–4.5% J on JiTBench, 12.8–12.9% gIoU on ReasonSeg, and 15.6% gIoU on out-of-domain LLM-Seg40K, demonstrating that RL training over DT representations substantially exceeds zero-shot DT reasoning. Against the RL-based segmentation method Seg-R1 [43], DT-R1 achieves gIoU 0.746 vs. 0.628 on ReasonSeg (+11.8%), confirming that the unified DT-representation framework generalizes better than task-specific RL. On video VQA, DT-R1 surpasses the RL-based VideoR1-7B [6] by 18.0 BLEU-4 points at difficulty level 4 (0.642 vs. 0.462), and on grounding it exceeds the task-specific GroundMore [5] by 15.8 cIoU points at level 1 (0.670 vs. 0.542), validating that a single unified RL model can match or beat dedicated architectures across all four RVT types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the just-in-time digital twin (JiT) framework [31], which demonstrated that structured DT representations preserve spatial-temporal continuity for reasoning segmentation in a zero-shot manner, by introducing RL training (GRPO) so that LLMs learn to autonomously plan and construct task-specific DT representations rather than relying on fixed pipelines. It also builds on the RVT formulation [28] from the same group, which unified reasoning segmentation, grounding, summarization, and VQA under a common implicit-query paradigm, and on the RL-for-LLM reasoning line (DeepSeek-R1, Seg-Zero, Seg-R1) that showed pure RL can elicit reasoning without supervised chain-of-thought data. The key departure from prior RVT methods (LISA, VISA, ScanReason) is the replacement of token-based visual encoding and SFT with a structured rollout that interleaves DT construction, iterative CoT reasoning, and executable Python code, all optimized end-to-end via a rule-based reward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DT-R1 demonstrates that a single RL-trained LLM can handle four distinct visual reasoning task types across image and video modalities without task-specific architectural modifications, outperforming dedicated models on all six evaluated benchmarks. The 10× inference speedup over JiT (1.54s vs 15.24s per query) despite invoking multiple vision foundation models is practically significant for deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger LLMs and more complex spatial-temporal relationships, whether distilled or cached foundation models can reduce the multi-model inference overhead, and whether the DT representation can be extended to incorporate audio or other sensory modalities. The reliance on rule-based rewards and IoU thresholds for segmentation/grounding also raises questions about reward design for more open-ended visual tasks.
