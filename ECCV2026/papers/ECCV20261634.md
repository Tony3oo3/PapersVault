## Problem

Existing multi-reference image generation (MRIG) benchmarks only cover simple pairwise combinations of a few reference types, failing to evaluate complex scenarios with numerous diverse references. Furthermore, open-source models exhibit severe performance degradation as the number of mixed-type reference images increases, creating a large gap with state-of-the-art closed-source models that remains unaddressed by prior training methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261634/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 2–7 reference images spanning up to four simultaneously active types (subject, background, style, lighting, pose) together with a text prompt describing the desired composition, and outputs a single generated image that integrates all referenced visual elements. Training and evaluation use both synthetic data (generated via a T2I + segmentation + editing pipeline) and real-world images from LAION-5B, with the base models being Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 (20B) and FLUX.2 [klein] (9B).

## Method

DyRef is a two-stage training framework. Stage I performs supervised fine-tuning with LoRA under a Flow Matching objective on ~14,000 synthetic multi-reference samples to establish basic MRIG capability. Stage II applies reinforcement learning via Flow-GRPO with two key modifications: (1) Difficulty-aware Advantage Reweighting (DAR) computes per-group weights inversely proportional to mean reward scores, dynamically up-weighting underperforming sample groups that contain more mixed-type references so the policy focuses on harder compositions; (2) Discriminative Reward Scaling (DRS) applies a transformation function to CLIP/SigLIPv2 cosine-similarity rewards to enlarge intra-group score differences, counteracting the overly concentrated reward distributions that weaken gradient signals. A CSD style-alignment reward is additionally incorporated to target the particularly weak style-transfer dimension.

## Results

On the proposed OmniRef-Bench, Qwen-2511 + DyRef achieves an average objective score of 0.71 and MLLM score of 8.38, surpassing all open-source baselines by at least 12% (objective) and 29% (MLLM) and exceeding closed-source Nano Banana Pro (0.70 objective) while remaining comparable in MLLM evaluation (8.38 vs. 8.50). On the single-image editing benchmark ImgEdit, Qwen-2511 + DyRef reaches 4.32 overall, outperforming both closed-source Seedream4.0 (4.18) and GPT Image 1 (4.20). On DreamBench++, the method achieves the highest CP×PF score of 0.62, a 17% improvement over the base model (0.53). The method also generalizes to FLUX.2 [klein] (9B), lifting its OmniRef-Bench MLLM score from 6.49 to 8.03.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest open-source baselines, DyRef delivers large gains: Qwen-2511 + DyRef (MLLM Avg 8.38) outperforms OmniGen2 (3.79), DreamOmni2 (5.18), BAGEL (4.64), and the base Qwen-2511 (4.97) by 120%, 61%, 80%, and 69% respectively. In objective metrics, the method (0.71 Avg) surpasses Nano Banana Pro (0.70) and Seedream4.5 (0.67), while in MLLM evaluation it trails Nano Banana Pro by only 0.12 points (8.38 vs. 8.50) but leads Seedream4.5 by 0.25 (8.38 vs. 8.13). On ImgEdit, Qwen-2511 + DyRef (4.32) beats both closed-source Seedream4.0 (4.18) and GPT Image 1 (4.20), as well as all open-source models. On DreamBench++, the method achieves the best CP×PF of 0.62 versus 0.53 for base Qwen-2511 and 0.56 for FLUX.2 [klein]. The style metric shows the largest relative improvement (e.g., MLLM Style jumps from 1.61 to 8.67 for Qwen-2511), confirming that the CSD reward and DAR reweighting specifically target the weakest dimension of prior models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Flow-GRPO, a recent RL framework for training flow-matching diffusion models, by introducing two targeted modifications: Difficulty-aware Advantage Reweighting (DAR), which draws conceptual inspiration from focal loss in dense object detection to upweight harder samples, and Discriminative Reward Scaling (DRS), which addresses the well-known concentration of CLIP/SigLIPv2 similarity scores. The base model is Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 (a dual-stream MMDiT architecture), and the overall two-stage SFT-then-RL pipeline follows the standard paradigm for adapting diffusion models to specialized tasks. The paper also positions itself against existing MRIG benchmarks (MultiBanana, DreamOmni2, XVerse) by arguing they underrepresent complex multi-type reference combinations, motivating the new OmniRef-Bench.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central empirical finding—that open-source MRIG models degrade sharply as the number and diversity of reference images increases, while closed-source models remain stable—provides a concrete diagnostic for the open-source/closed-source gap in personalized generation. The practical impact is significant: a relatively lightweight RL stage (no new data, only reweighting and reward scaling) closes most of this gap on both complex MRIG and standard single-image editing benchmarks across two architectures of different scale. Open questions include whether the MLLM-based evaluation (Gemini 3 Flash) introduces systematic biases that the DRS mechanism only partially corrects, whether the approach transfers to even higher reference counts or novel reference types beyond the five studied, and whether the reward-model concentration issue is specific to CLIP-family encoders or more general.
