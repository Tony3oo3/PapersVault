## Problem

Radiology and pathology foundation models produce feature embeddings on topologically disjoint manifolds due to extreme macro-to-micro scale mismatch. Conventional static fusion (cross-attention, linear projection) forces these heterogeneous manifolds to overlap, mixing unaligned semantic neighborhoods and causing topological degeneracy and semantic entanglement. The paper asks whether multimodal integration can be reformulated as a smooth, structure-preserving transport rather than a violent static projection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes feature tokens extracted by frozen vision-language foundation models (CONCH for pathology WSIs, BiomedCLIP for radiology MRI/CT) projected into a shared D=128 space, along with a pre-constructed knowledge base of clinical concept embeddings elicited by an LLM from WHO/AJCC guidelines. It produces a patient-level representation used for both classification (tumor grade/stage) and survival analysis (Cox risk score), accompanied by per-prototype epistemic uncertainty estimates that modulate the final aggregation.

## Method

SeekFlow operates in three stages. First, a Knowledge-Routed Evidence Manifold constructs K learnable Gaussian prototypes (shared and modality-specific) anchored by LLM-elicited clinical concepts; a router synergizes data-driven MLP logits with VLFM-derived cosine-similarity priors to softly assign tokens to prototypes via sigmoid gating. Second, Knowledge-Guided Flow Matching trains a vector field via an EM-like loop: the E-step solves a per-prototype Sinkhorn OT problem with a density-aligned cost to compute barycentric targets, and the M-step regresses the CFM velocity with AdaLN conditioning on timestep, modality, and prototype identity; at inference, tokens are transported to prototypes via Euler ODE integration under a Top-K Gumbel-Sigmoid sparsity mask. Third, an Uncertainty-Aware Evidential Aggregator applies Dempster-Shafer logic to compute per-prototype belief masses and global uncertainty, then fuses via reliability-weighted combination and uncertainty-biased multi-head self-attention to yield the final patient representation.

## Results

On survival analysis (C-index), SeekFlow achieves 0.793 on TCGA-LGG, 0.563 on TCGA-GBM, and 0.740 on Gastric Cancer, outperforming the best baselines by 4.1, 1.3, and 1.4 points respectively. On classification tasks, SeekFlow reaches AUC 0.901 / ACC 0.739 on TCGA Glioma Grading, AUC 0.820 / ACC 0.595 on Chondrosarcoma Staging, and AUC 0.931 / ACC 0.832 on Chondrosarcoma Grading, setting new state-of-the-art across all six classification benchmarks. The most notable margin is on Chondrosarcoma Staging, where SeekFlow surpasses the second-best method (CTF) by 4.4 AUC points and 3.3 ACC points, confirming its ability to integrate critical macroscopic radiological semantics that pathology-only methods miss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeekFlow outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and task. Among unimodal pathology methods, TransMIL is the strongest on TCGA-LGG survival (C-index 0.730) but still trails SeekFlow by 6.3 points; CLAM leads on Gastric Cancer (0.726) yet is surpassed by 1.4 points. Among multimodal fusion baselines, M4Survive is the closest competitor on TCGA-LGG (0.752 vs. 0.793, a 4.1-point gap), while PIBD is nearest on TCGA-GBM (0.550 vs. 0.563). On classification, CTF is the strongest baseline on Chondrosarcoma Staging (AUC 0.776) but SeekFlow extends this to 0.820; MCAT leads on Chondrosarcoma Grading (AUC 0.924) yet SeekFlow reaches 0.931 with a substantially larger ACC margin (0.832 vs. 0.782). Notably, several multimodal methods (MCAT, SurvPath, M4Survive) degrade to near-random performance on TCGA-GBM (C-index ≈ 0.49–0.52), whereas SeekFlow maintains 0.563, demonstrating superior robustness to extreme tumor heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeekFlow builds on conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) and entropic optimal transport (Cuturi, 2013) as its transport backbone, but departs fundamentally from prior multimodal fusion work (MCAT, SurvPath, MOTCat, PIBD) that relies on static operations—cross-attention, linear projections, or discrete OT plans—to align heterogeneous feature spaces. The key conceptual departure is reframing fusion as a continuous, topology-preserving vector-field transport toward knowledge-anchored clinical prototypes, rather than a one-shot static mapping. The uncertainty-aware aggregation layer additionally draws on Dempster-Shafer Theory, which is relatively underutilized in medical imaging fusion compared to attention-based or averaging schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in precision oncology: radiology and pathology foundation models produce features on topologically disjoint manifolds, and naive fusion degrades performance. The knowledge-grounded flow matching paradigm offers a principled, interpretable alternative to black-box fusion, and the DST-based uncertainty quantification is directly relevant for clinical trust. However, validation is limited to three datasets with two modalities each, the flow matching training (EM-like loop with Sinkhorn OT per prototype) introduces non-trivial computational overhead, and the reliance on LLM-elicited clinical concepts raises questions about generalizability to tasks without well-established WHO/AJCC guidelines. Open questions include whether the continuous transport advantage persists at larger scale or with more than two modalities, and whether the knowledge routing can be automated without expert-curated guideline prompts.
