## Problem

Existing training-free diffusion-based low-light enhancement methods (e.g., AGLLDiff) steer sampling via weighted combinations of heterogeneous pixel-space losses (exposure, structure, color), whose competing gradients cause unstable exposure control and hue distortion. These approaches also entangle luminance and chroma in RGB space and lack an explicit global model of a smooth, monotonic exposure trajectory, making weight tuning fragile and color fidelity poor on challenging scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262495/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-light RGB image, resizes it to 256×256, and feeds it into a frozen DDPM UNet pretrained on ImageNet with a 1000-step noise schedule. Luminance is always computed in linear RGB (inverse-gamma converted from sRGB before the Y = 0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B computation), and the final output is gamma-encoded back to sRGB. The guidance correction is applied only to the last 10 reverse diffusion steps; earlier steps follow the vanilla DDPM update unchanged, and the output is bicubically rescaled to the original input resolution.

## Method

LUCE replaces multi-objective pixel-space guidance with a single scalar energy defined in a low-dimensional monotonic curve domain. A unified luminance target fuses the current clean-estimate luminance with an input-derived exposure prior (global gamma correction plus per-image affine rescaling to a fixed target mean) via a time-dependent convex combination β_t that shifts from exposure-prior-dominated to backbone-fidelity-dominated as t→0. This target is enforced by fitting a piecewise-linear LUT (B=32 knots) under a monotonicity constraint, regularized with ℓ₁ smoothness and ℓ₂ identity-shape penalties; the resulting curve-domain energy is differentiated with respect to x_t (treating the curve parameters as fixed) and injected as a single gradient correction into the DDPM update. To realize the curve-edited luminance in RGB without hue shift, a per-pixel scalar gain s_t(p) = Ỹ_t(p)/max(Y_t(p), ε) scales all three channels uniformly, preserving the RGB direction and hence hue before gamut clipping. The entire pipeline is training-free: the backbone, noise schedule, and base sampler remain frozen, and only the last K=10 steps receive the luminance-guided correction.

## Results

On paired benchmarks, LUCE achieves 22.81/0.85/0.15 (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS) on LOL-v1, 20.21/0.84/0.19 on LOL-v2 (Real+Synthetic), and 18.80/0.56/0.32 on LSRW, ranking best or second-best among all unsupervised methods on every metric. On unpaired benchmarks (LIME, NPE, MEF, DICM, VV), LUCE attains the best BRISQUE (18.03) and second-best noise level (0.76) within the unsupervised group. Inference time is 10.53 s per 256×256 image on a single A100, roughly half that of AGLLDiff (20.43 s) with comparable parameter and MAC counts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest training-free diffusion baseline AGLLDiff, LUCE improves PSNR on all three paired datasets (22.81 vs 21.81 on LOL-v1, 20.21 vs 19.59 on LOL-v2, 18.80 vs 16.92 on LSRW) while matching or exceeding SSIM and LPIPS, and runs in roughly half the inference time (10.53 s vs 20.43 s). Compared with the strongest unsupervised non-diffusion baselines, LUCE outperforms QuadPrior on SSIM and LPIPS across all three paired sets (e.g., 0.85 vs 0.80 SSIM on LOL-v1) and beats NeRCo on SSIM/LPIPS on LSRW (0.56 vs 0.51, 0.32 vs 0.34) while trailing by only 0.02 dB in PSNR. On unpaired benchmarks, LUCE achieves the best BRISQUE (18.03) among all unsupervised methods, surpassing LightenDiffusion (18.53) and AGLLDiff (19.55), and the second-best noise level (0.76) behind only NeRCo (0.65). Against curve-based methods like Zero-DCE and Zero-DCE++, LUCE gains 7–8 dB in PSNR and substantially higher SSIM, reflecting the advantage of diffusion-prior quality over lightweight tone-curve estimation. Supervised methods (DiffLL, RetinexFormer) still lead in absolute PSNR on paired data, but LUCE closes the gap to within 1–5 dB while requiring no paired training data or task-specific architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

LUCE builds on the DDPM sampling framework and the gradient-injection mechanism popularized by classifier-free guidance, but departs sharply from the dominant training-free diffusion paradigm (AGLLDiff, PGDiff) that aggregates multiple heterogeneous pixel-space losses with heuristic weights. Conceptually, it inherits the interpretable tone-curve idea from Zero-DCE and its variants but transplants it into a frozen diffusion sampler as a single scalar energy in a low-dimensional monotonic LUT space, eliminating the need for multi-loss balancing. The hue-preserving per-pixel scalar-gain transform is a lightweight chromatic decoupling that has no direct analogue in prior diffusion-guidance work, which typically edits RGB channels jointly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By collapsing multi-objective pixel-space guidance into one curve-domain luminance energy, LUCE offers a cleaner, more stable, and more interpretable formulation for training-free diffusion-based enhancement that could generalize to other low-level tasks (denoising, tone mapping) where competing gradient objectives cause instability. However, the framework still relies on hand-crafted exposure priors (fixed gamma, fixed target mean) and a fixed guidance schedule, limiting adaptability to spatially heterogeneous or extremely dark scenes. Practical adoption is also constrained by the ~10 s per-image inference cost of a 1000-step DDPM at 256×256, which remains orders of magnitude slower than lightweight CNN baselines. Open questions include whether the curve-domain formulation transfers to latent diffusion models, higher resolutions, RAW pipelines, and video, and whether the exposure prior can be learned or made scene-adaptive without reintroducing training.
