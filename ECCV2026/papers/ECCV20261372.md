## Problem

Multimodal LLMs can generate full dermatology diagnostic narratives from a single image, but no standardized benchmark or automated evaluator exists to assess whether those narratives are clinically accurate, safe, and well-reasoned. Existing dermatology benchmarks target image-level tasks (classification, segmentation) with categorical labels, leaving free-text diagnostic reasoning unevaluated, while expert grading is too costly to scale for iterative model development.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261372/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DermBench takes a dermatology image, prompts a candidate model to produce a diagnostic narrative under a fixed instruction, and an LLM judge (DeepSeek-R1) scores it against a clinician-certified reference on six dimensions (Accuracy, Safety, Medical Groundedness, Clinical Coverage, Reasoning Coherence, Description Precision), each on a 0–5 integer scale. DermEval operates reference-free: given only the image and the generated narrative, it emits a structured multi-section critique (one titled paragraph per dimension with evidence and an actionable suggestion) plus the six numeric scores. The benchmark spans 4,000 images across 23 disease categories sourced from DermNet, and the evaluator is a 7B LLaVA model fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 64).

## Method

The pipeline has three components. First, a dual-stream dataset construction: a high-quality stream uses Gemini-2.5 Pro to caption observable findings, then an LLM simulates hierarchical clinical reasoning conditioned on the ground-truth label, and board-certified dermatologists revise narratives until all six dimensions score 5; a regular stream repeats the same captioning but omits the label, yielding narratives of varying quality that dermatologists score 0–5. Second, DermBench uses DeepSeek-R1 as an LLM judge that compares the candidate narrative to the certified reference and assigns six scores, with an explicit candidate tag to mitigate position bias (verified by an order-swap test showing ≤0.11 mean absolute difference). Third, DermEval is trained in two stages on LLaVA v1.5-7B: Stage 1 uses token-level cross-entropy to learn a canonical, machine-parsable evaluation format; Stage 2 applies Score-Oriented REINFORCE with an exponential moving average baseline (β=0.9), where an external LLM parses the six scores from the generated text, the reward is negative MSE against physician scores, and policy gradients are restricted to generated tokens only, with a combined loss weighting of 0.5·L_RL + 1.0·L_TEXT.

## Results

DermEval achieves a mean absolute error of 0.178 (out of 5) against expert annotations across six clinical dimensions, substantially outperforming DermBench's 0.363, confirming that the reference-free evaluator closely tracks physician judgment. On the main benchmark (Table 4), Gemini-2.5 Flash and GPT-4o-mini lead on Accuracy (≈3.14–3.20) and Description Precision (≈4.30–4.48), while GPT-o4-mini tops Safety (≈4.25–4.29). LLaVA-Med-7B is consistently the weakest model across all six dimensions (Accuracy ≈1.17–1.21, Safety ≈2.16–2.20). The two evaluators agree within 0.05 on every model–dimension pair, and an order-swap stress test shows the LLM judge is minimally sensitive to presentation order (mean absolute difference 0.04–0.11).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates nine multimodal models rather than proposing a new generation model, so the 'comparison' is a cross-model benchmark. Gemini-2.5 Flash and GPT-4o-mini are the strongest overall, leading on Accuracy (3.14 vs. 3.13 on DermBench) and Description Precision (4.34 vs. 4.48), with GPT-4o-mini edging ahead on Medical Groundedness (3.52 vs. 3.44). GPT-o4-mini is the clear Safety leader (4.29 DermBench / 4.25 DermEval), surpassing Gemini-2.5 Flash (4.09/4.13) by roughly 0.2 points. Medical-domain models HuatuoGPT-Vision and LLaVA-Med-7B underperform general-purpose models: HuatuoGPT-Vision sits mid-pack (Accuracy ≈2.44–2.48) while LLaVA-Med-7B is last on every dimension (Accuracy ≈1.17–1.21, Safety ≈2.16–2.20), a gap of roughly 1.9–2.0 points below the top models. Reasoning-focused models (Vision-R1, LLaVA-o1, InternVL3) cluster in the middle tier, with LLaVA-o1 slightly ahead on Reasoning Coherence (3.24/3.20) but trailing on Accuracy (2.62–2.67). The two evaluators (DermBench vs. DermEval) produce near-identical rankings with per-cell differences under 0.05, validating DermEval as a scalable reference-free alternative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of medical VLMs (LLaVA-Med, HuatuoGPT-Vision, PanDerm, DermINO) and LLM-as-judge evaluation (MT-Bench, LLaVA-Critic, Prometheus), but departs from both in a key way: prior dermatology benchmarks evaluate categorical labels or image-level tasks, while general-purpose evaluators lack domain-specific grounding. The authors repurpose the chain-of-thought reasoning paradigm (MedCoT, ReasonMed) not for training diagnostic models but for structuring the evaluation rubric itself, and they combine a reference-anchored LLM judge (DermBench, using DeepSeek-R1) with a reference-free evaluator trained via score-oriented REINFORCE with EMA baseline (DermEval, a LoRA-finetuned LLaVA-7B) to cover both controlled benchmarking and scalable per-case assessment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first evaluation infrastructure purpose-built for dermatology diagnostic narratives rather than image classification, and the six-dimension rubric (Accuracy, Safety, Medical Groundedness, Clinical Coverage, Reasoning Coherence, Description Precision) explicitly targets clinical trustworthiness in a way that generic text-similarity or preference metrics cannot. The reference-free DermEval enables routine, scalable monitoring of model outputs without requiring gold references at inference, which is critical for ongoing deployment. Limitations include reliance on a single source dataset (DermNet), a 7B evaluator whose own diagnostic ceiling may constrain its scoring of very strong models, and the absence of prospective clinical validation; open questions include generalization to other specialties, handling of multi-image or longitudinal inputs, and integration with human-in-the-loop oversight in real clinical workflows.
