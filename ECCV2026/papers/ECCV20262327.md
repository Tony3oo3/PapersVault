## Problem

Translating clinical observations into statistically validated conclusions from medical imaging requires coordinating expertise across radiology, programming, and biostatistics, a fragmented process that bottlenecks discovery. Prior AI-scientist and co-scientist systems operate on tabular or text data and do not ground autonomous hypothesis testing in medical images, nor do they provide power-aware epistemic classification that distinguishes true nulls from underpowered analyses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262327/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a natural-language hypothesis and a medical imaging dataset (cardiac or brain MRI with associated patient metadata), interacting with data exclusively through a constrained API layer that exposes cohort discovery, segmentation masks, and geometry-aware measurement utilities. It produces two complementary outputs: a mechanically derived four-label evidence classification (SUPPORTED, REFUTED, UNDERPOWERED, INVALID) computed by the Evidence Classification Operator from observed statistics and a pre-specified SESOI, and a three-way agent verdict (YES/NO/INCONCLUSIVE) from structured discussion. All intermediate and final outputs are structured, versioned artifacts (JSON plans, segmentation requests, executable Python scripts, statistical summaries) rather than free-text, enabling independent re-verification of every step.

## Method

VERITAS decomposes the hypothesis-testing workflow into four sequential phases handled by three role-specialized agents (Principal Investigator, Medical Imaging Specialist, Statistician) plus a Critic: (1) collaborative analysis planning with a programmatic feasibility validator, (2A) promptable neural segmentation via SAT to ground all evidence in image-derived masks, (2B) sandboxed iterative code generation and execution for statistical testing (up to 8 revision rounds per round), and (3) structured interpretation producing a final verdict. The key architectural insight is the Evidence Classification Operator, a deterministic function that maps observed statistics (p-value, effect size, sample size) and a smallest effect size of interest to one of four epistemic labels without any agent judgment, directly operationalizing the Lakens et al. four-outcome decision framework. A constrained dataset API ensures agents never access raw files, standardizing provenance and enabling automated off-contract access checks. The multi-agent decomposition with role specialization enables locally-deployed 8–30B open-weight models to match or exceed frontier-model single-agent baselines, facilitating secure on-site clinical deployment.

## Results

VERITAS achieves 81.4% majority-vote verdict accuracy and 76.3% majority-vote evidence-label accuracy with frontier models (GPT-5.2), and 71.2% / 67.8% with locally-deployed open-weight models (8–30B), outperforming all single-model baselines in both classes. Completion rates are 87.5% (frontier) and 78.1% (local). VERITAS also produces the highest rate of independently verifiable statistical outputs (86.6% frontier, 77.3% local) among methods operating on raw data, and is the only method to achieve non-zero L5 (multivariate) evidence accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper ablates five single-model baselines (SMa–SMe) that progressively add code execution, API access, iterative self-correction, and structured phase guidance, isolating each capability dimension. VERITAS outperforms the strongest local baseline (SMd, 66.1% verdict) by +5.1 pp and the strongest frontier baseline (SMc, 76.3% verdict) by +5.1 pp in majority-vote verdict accuracy. The multi-agent contribution over a single-model pipeline (SMe) is +10.2 pp for local models (71.2% vs. 61.0%) and +20.4 pp for frontier models (81.4% vs. 61.0%), suggesting multi-agent discussion scales with base-model capability. On evidence-label accuracy, VERITAS leads by +15.3 pp (local) and +11.9 pp (frontier) over the best single-model baselines. Critically, VERITAS is the only method achieving non-zero L5 evidence accuracy (16–30%), while all baselines score 0% on L5 despite up to 92% verdict accuracy there, indicating baselines produce correct YES/NO guesses without valid statistical backing. VERITAS also achieves the highest verifiability rate (86.6% frontier) among raw-data methods, meaning even its failures remain diagnosable through artifact inspection.

## Relation to Prior Work

VERITAS sits at the intersection of three prior threads: (1) the four-outcome equivalence-testing framework of Lakens et al. [26], which the paper operationalizes as a mechanical Evidence Classification Operator rather than manual analytical guidance; (2) multi-agent scientific-reasoning systems (MetaGPT, Robin, AI Scientist, Popper), from which it inherits the structured-artifact design to curb cascading hallucinations but departs by grounding all evidence in executable code and image-derived measurements rather than tabular or text data; and (3) promptable medical segmentation models (SAT, MedSAM), which serve as the vision backbone. The key positioning claim is that no prior system unifies vision-grounded measurement, executable statistical validation, and power-aware epistemic classification in a single auditable pipeline—imaging agents stop at perception, medical agents target per-patient diagnosis, and statistical-evidence frameworks remain manual.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The epistemic evidence-label framework (SUPPORTED/REFUTED/UNDERPOWERED/INVALID) is a contribution beyond medical imaging: any benchmark involving statistical testing can adopt it to avoid conflating underpowered nulls with true negatives, a common evaluation pitfall. The demonstration that 8–30B locally-deployed models achieve 71.2% verdict accuracy—within 10pp of frontier models—suggests structured multi-agent decomposition can substitute for raw model scale, enabling secure on-site clinical deployment. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to other modalities (CT, histopathology), longitudinal or causal analyses, and how segmentation uncertainty should propagate into the evidence label rather than being treated as a point estimate.
