## Problem

Existing one-step Real-SR methods directly substitute degraded LR latents for Gaussian noise at initialization, inducing a substantial distribution shift that causes trajectory deviation and prior collapse under extreme acceleration. This results in over-smoothed outputs or unnatural artifacts because the pre-trained generative prior is not properly preserved during task-specific fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260327/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded low-resolution image, encodes it to latent space via a fixed VAE encoder, and reconstructs the corresponding high-resolution image in a single forward pass (one function evaluation) at ×4 scaling. Training operates on 512×512 latent patches (128×128 LR inputs), while 1024×1024 inference uses a tiled strategy. The output is a decoded HR latent that is explicitly constrained to remain within the natural image manifold via the allomorphic generative flow.

## Method

Allo{SR}² treats image generation and restoration as allomorphic trajectories converging to the same HR manifold, and rectifies the one-step SR flow through three mechanisms. First, SNR-Guided Trajectory Initialization identifies an optimal intermediate timestep t* where the LR latent's degradation severity statistically matches the pre-trained flow's noise schedule, bypassing the highly noisy region. Second, Flow-Anchored Trajectory Consistency (FATC) supervises the instantaneous velocity field along rescaled intermediate states between z_H and z_L, enforcing a low-curvature linear path for accurate one-step Euler integration. Third, Allomorphic Trajectory Matching (ATM) performs self-adversarial distillation by jointly modeling a global generative flow and a local SR flow within a shared-weight unified velocity field, minimizing KL divergence between their score functions to transfer the distortion-free generative prior to the SR trajectory. The entire framework is built on FLUX.1-dev with LoRA (rank 64) on both the VAE encoder and DiT, trained for 10K iterations with AdamW.

## Results

Allo{SR}² (1-step) achieves the best no-reference perceptual scores across all four benchmarks: NIQE of 4.18 / 5.08 / 5.82 / 3.59, MUSIQ of 72.00 / 70.11 / 66.95 / 72.51, and MANIQA of 0.6432 / 0.6725 / 0.6264 / 0.6303 on DIV2K-Val, RealSR, DRealSR, and RealLQ250 respectively. It also attains the lowest FID on the two real-world paired datasets (112.88 on RealSR, 132.48 on DRealSR), confirming effective distributional alignment with the natural image manifold. On pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM) it trails CTMSR and TSD-SR, which the authors attribute to the well-known perception–distortion trade-off in Real-SR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the six one-step baselines, Allo{SR}² consistently outperforms all competitors on NIQE, MUSIQ, and MANIQA across every dataset, with the largest margins over CTMSR (e.g., MANIQA 0.6725 vs. 0.5269 on RealSR) and ResShift/SinSR. It also achieves the best FID on both real-world paired benchmarks (112.88 on RealSR, 132.48 on DRealSR), beating the closest one-step rival TSD-SR (114.45 / 134.98) and all multi-step methods. However, TSD-SR retains an edge on pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM) and on LPIPS and CLIPIQA across all datasets, reflecting its stronger pixel-level reconstruction at the cost of perceptual naturalness. Against the multi-step baselines (StableSR 200-step, SeeSR 50-step, ResShift 15-step), Allo{SR}² matches or exceeds their perceptual quality while reducing function evaluations by 15×–200×, though it trails on PSNR/SSIM where ResShift and CTMSR lead. Overall, the method trades a small amount of pixel fidelity for substantially better distributional realism, positioning it as the strongest one-step option when perceptual quality and natural-image manifold alignment are prioritized.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Flow Matching paradigm (Lipman et al.) for its velocity-field formulation and on the self-adversarial dual-flow architecture of TwinFlow (Cheng et al.) for its parameter-shared generative/SR trajectory design. It departs from prior one-step Real-SR methods (SinSR, OSEDiff, TSD-SR, CTMSR) that naively replace Gaussian noise with LR latents, by reframing the problem through an 'allomorphic' lens: generation and restoration are treated as structurally analogous trajectories converging to the same HR manifold, with the SR flow as a condition-constrained subset of the generative flow. The SNR-guided initialization borrows the metric from OMGSR, while the ATM loss adapts the Score Distillation (DMD) gradient to a self-adversarial setting where the teacher and student share weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

One-step Real-SR is practically critical for deployment, and this paper delivers a competitive method that reduces function evaluations by 15–200× over multi-step baselines while achieving SOTA on most no-reference perceptual metrics. The allomorphic framing offers a principled mechanism to prevent prior collapse during fine-tuning, which is a recurring failure mode in the field. Open questions include whether the SNR-based initialization generalizes to arbitrary degradation types beyond the Real-ESRGAN pipeline, whether the approach extends to video SR or other restoration tasks, and whether the reliance on a specific large foundation model (FLUX.1-dev) limits broader applicability. The method still trades off PSNR/SSIM for perceptual quality, consistent with the known perception-distortion tradeoff, so it does not resolve the fundamental tension between fidelity and realism.
