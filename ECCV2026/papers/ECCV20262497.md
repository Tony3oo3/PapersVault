## Problem

Pixel-wise I2I regression is simple and stable but over-smooths ill-posed, multi-modal targets, while generative alternatives (diffusion, GANs) require additional networks, task-specific tuning, and many-step inference. The paper asks whether the progressive refinement benefits of continuous-time transport (rectified flow) can be obtained while preserving the simplicity, stability, and inductive biases of existing supervised I2I regression backbones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262497/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded RGB image (or video frame) as input and produces a restored image (or frame) at the same spatial resolution. During training, the network receives a channel-wise concatenation of the input image x and a noise-corrupted target state y_t = (1−t)y + tε, expanding input channels (e.g., 3→6 for RGB); at inference, the state is initialized as pure Gaussian noise and refined over N=3 explicit Euler steps. For super-resolution, the interpolation state is constructed in the LR domain to match the model input shape, while the prediction target and loss are defined in the HR domain.

## Method

I2I-RFR reformulates a standard I2I regressor as a rectified-flow-consistent ODE refiner by (i) augmenting the backbone input with a noise-corrupted target state via channel-wise concatenation, (ii) training with a t-reweighted pixel loss ‖(y − f([x;y_t]))/t‖, and (iii) solving the induced ODE at inference with a few explicit Euler steps. The key insight is that this t-reweighted regression objective is mathematically equivalent to rectified-flow velocity regression, with the velocity field induced analytically as v_θ(x,y_t,t) = (y_t − f([x;y_t]))/t, so the network remains a standard clean-target regressor while the overall model becomes an RF-consistent ODE refiner. The paper shows that direct x₀-prediction (predicting the clean image) is critical for I2I backbones—replacing it with explicit v-prediction causes severe degradation—because restoration architectures are designed to suppress degradations and output clean images, not to regress noise-containing velocity fields. No explicit time embeddings are needed since the corruption level is directly visible in the concatenated y_t input, and Beta(2,1) sampling of t counterbalances the t⁻¹ loss weighting for stable optimization.

## Results

On image super-resolution (Table 1), SwinIR+I2I-RFR achieves 32.84 PSNR / 0.9028 SSIM / 0.1665 LPIPS on Set5, marginally improving PSNR (+0.04) and LPIPS (−0.0022) over the original SwinIR while maintaining comparable distortion. The most dramatic gain comes from retraining the Palette diffusion backbone with I2I-RFR, which jumps from 22.28 to 32.09 PSNR on Set5 (+9.81 dB) and from 0.3035 to 0.1914 LPIPS on Urban100 (−0.1121). On low-light blur enhancement (LOLBlur), LEDNet+I2I-RFR reaches 27.42 PSNR / 0.874 SSIM / 0.138 LPIPS, outperforming both the original LEDNet (26.04/0.845/0.159) and LEDNet+Adv. (24.55/0.811/0.153). On video restoration (DAVIS), RRTN+I2I-RFR improves PSNR by +3.21 dB and LPIPS by −0.068 over the original RRTN, while inference requires only 3 Euler steps without distillation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

I2I-RFR consistently improves LPIPS (perceptual quality) over the original regression baselines across all SR backbones (NLSN, SwinIR, U-Net), at the cost of small PSNR/SSIM drops that reflect the known perception–distortion trade-off in ill-posed super-resolution. The Palette diffusion backbone, when retrained with I2I-RFR's direct-target objective and 3-step Euler inference, surpasses all matched regression baselines on every SR benchmark (e.g., 32.09 vs. 32.84 PSNR on Set5 for SwinIR+I2I-RFR, but with far fewer steps and no noise schedule). On deblurring (RealBlur-J), I2I-RFR improves all three metrics for NAFNet (+0.12 PSNR, −0.0108 LPIPS), Restormer (+0.31 PSNR, −0.0114 LPIPS), and FFTFormer (+0.39 PSNR, −0.0204 LPIPS). On LOLBlur, LEDNet+I2I-RFR (27.42 PSNR, 0.138 LPIPS) outperforms both the original LEDNet and the adversarial variant LEDNet+Adv. (24.55 PSNR, 0.153 LPIPS) on all metrics, while DarkIR+I2I-RFR-CL achieves the best overall result (27.72 PSNR, 0.103 LPIPS). For underwater enhancement, I2I-RFR yields large gains for the Tang et al. diffusion backbone (+10.93 PSNR on LSUI) and consistent improvements on UIEB for SFGNet, though SFGNet on LSUI shows a slight PSNR drop (−1.45) likely due to its tightly coupled frequency-domain design. In the reference comparison (Table 6), SwinIR+I2I-RFR (12M params, 3 steps, 0.15 s) achieves substantially higher PSNR (29.22) than prior-based methods like ResShift (26.97), DiffBIR (25.17), and SeeSR (25.49), though ResShift attains the best LPIPS (0.1458 vs. 0.2420), confirming that I2I-RFR occupies a fidelity-favorable operating point rather than dominating every perceptual metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Rectified Flow and Flow Matching frameworks (Liu et al., ICLR 2023; Lipman et al., ICLR 2023), which learn a time-dependent velocity field for continuous-time transport. However, it departs from prior RF work in a key way: existing RF models target unconditional or weakly-conditioned generation (e.g., text-to-image) with purpose-built time-conditioned backbones, whereas I2I-RFR repurposes strongly-conditioned, pixel-aligned I2I regression architectures (SwinIR, Restormer, LEDNet, etc.) as RF-consistent ODE refiners by merely expanding input channels and reweighting the pixel loss. The method also deliberately adopts x0-prediction (clean-target prediction) rather than the standard v-prediction used in most diffusion and flow models, a choice the paper shows is critical for I2I backbones. It positions itself between simple regression (stable but over-smoothed) and full generative pipelines (GANs, diffusion, latent models) that require additional components and complex training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

I2I-RFR offers a low-friction upgrade path for the large ecosystem of task-specialized I2I restoration backbones: practitioners can adopt it by expanding input channels and swapping the loss, with 3-step Euler inference and no distillation, GAN, or pretrained prior required. The parameterization ablation (v-prediction catastrophically degrading performance across multiple backbones) provides a useful cautionary result for anyone adapting flow-based objectives to restoration. Limitations include a perception-distortion trade-off in highly pixel-aligned settings like super-resolution, and occasional degradation for backbones with tightly coupled frequency/gradient priors (e.g., SFGNet). Open questions include whether the reformulation extends to non-photographic domains, how it interacts with composite loss designs at scale, and whether more solver steps or adaptive step schedules further improve the quality-efficiency frontier.
