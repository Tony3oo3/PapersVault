## Problem

Existing open-set and zero-shot text recognition methods are confined to the CJK script family (at most Latin), and no proven script-agnostic framework handles multiple script families—Indic, Latin, CJK, and Yi—within a single model. Commercial VLMs, despite large token sets, completely fail on minor scripts like Yi (0% word accuracy), revealing a fundamental gap in glyph-level shape discrimination that open-set recognition aims to fill.

## Input / Output

The framework takes scene text word images (RGB) and glyph template images (rendered from fonts, serving as side information for prototype generation) as input. It produces a word-level transcription sequence and, per character position, either a recognized label or an [unk] rejection flag for out-of-set characters. During inference, the prototyping module is frozen and prototypes are pre-cached, so only the RoI Feature Extractor and Open-Set Classifier are active, reducing the runtime footprint. Domain-specific batch normalization layers are inserted per data source (real words, synthetic words, synthetic characters, glyph templates) to handle distributional shifts across scripts and rendering styles.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified, script-agnostic multi-task co-training framework where heterogeneous tasks (character recognition on synthetic glyphs and word recognition on real/synthetic scene text) share a partially shared convolutional backbone with domain-specific batch normalization and domain-specific RoI samplers. For word-level alignment, the paper proposes a CAM-CTC structure that combines a Convolutional Attention Module (for spatial character localization) with CTC loss (for sequence-level alignment), which proves more robust across diverse scripts than either position-wise attention or CTC-only baselines. The Open-Set Classifier computes cosine similarity between extracted character features and dynamically generated prototypes (from glyph templates via a prototyping module), with a single learnable threshold appended as the [unk] score to enable rejection of unregistered characters without retraining. A heterogeneous synthetic data generator produces both character-level samples (rendered glyph masks with augmentation) and word-level samples (shuffled graphemes re-rendered with random fonts and backgrounds), and co-training with both levels simultaneously yields the largest gains on unseen scripts, outperforming either alone or simply increasing batch size.

## Results

On the Generalized Zero-Shot Learning benchmark, the unified framework (Ours-Uni) achieves 46.30% ACR on Japanese, 22.84% on Korean, 14.22% on synthetic Yi, 12.88% on Bengali (val), and 9.72% on Gujarati (val), demonstrating the first simultaneous zero-shot recognition across CJK, Indic, and Yi script families. The CJK-only variant (Ours-CJK) reaches 16.10% ACR on Yi, while GPT-5.2 achieves 0% ACR on the same 50-sample Yi subset. On the Open-Set Text Recognition task, Ours-Uni attains 75.38% ACR and 85.70% F-measure for rejection, approaching the SOTA WnA-XL (77.35% ACR, 87.21% FM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest CJK-focused baseline WnA [21], Ours-Uni trails by 1.72 points on Japanese (46.30 vs. 48.02) and 1.16 points on Korean (22.84 vs. 24.00), but WnA has no reported results on Yi, Bengali, or Gujarati. Compared to CFOR [23], Ours-Uni gains 1.83 points on Japanese (46.30 vs. 44.47) and 0.70 on Korean (22.84 vs. 22.14), while also being the only method evaluated on all three script families simultaneously. On Indic scripts, Ours-Uni substantially outperforms the Indic-only ablation (Ours-IND): 12.88 vs. 8.04 on Bengali-val and 9.72 vs. 7.52 on Gujarati-val, showing that cross-family co-training transfers useful features. On the Open-Set Text Recognition task (Table 4), Ours-Uni achieves 85.70% FM, slightly below CFOR (87.91%) and WnA-XL (87.21%), with a marginally lower recall (78.34 vs. 86.36/80.68) but comparable precision (~94.6%), indicating a slightly higher hallucination rate. The most striking comparison is against GPT-5.2 on the 50-sample Yi subset: Ours-Uni achieves 18.00% ACR and 48.67% CER, while GPT-5.2 yields 0% ACR and 148.44% CER, exposing a fundamental capability gap in commercial VLMs for rare scripts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the open-set text recognition (OSTR) line of work initiated by Liu et al. [24] and advanced by CFOR [23] and WnA [21], all of which are confined to the CJK script family. It departs from prior methods in two key ways: (1) it replaces the position-wise decoder with a CAM-CTC structure borrowed from OpenCCD [25] and Rosetta [3], and (2) it introduces heterogeneous multi-task co-training with both synthetic character and word-level data across Indic, Latin, and CJK script families simultaneously. The implementation is built directly on the WnA codebase [21] with the MoE routing removed, positioning this as a simplification and generalization of that prior architecture rather than a fundamentally new design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to demonstrate zero-shot recognition across multiple script families (Indic, CJK, Yi) in a single model, addressing a real equity gap for minority scripts where even GPT-5.2 achieves 0% word accuracy on Yi. However, absolute performance remains far from practical (16% ACR on Yi, ~10% on Gujarati), and the paper itself acknowledges the framework is not yet feasible for real-world zero-shot deployment. The most important open question is the mechanism behind severe inter-script conflicts within the Indic family (near-total collapse for Bengali/Gujarati when trained jointly), which the authors flag as their next research direction. Coverage of handwritten modality and additional script families (Arabic, etc.) also remains unaddressed.
