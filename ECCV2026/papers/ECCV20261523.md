## Problem

Long-form video QA under fixed visual token budgets suffers from a fundamental tension: uniform sampling misses critical evidence in long videos, while similarity-based frame selection fails when the question's key objects are not visually explicit. Existing MLLM-based selectors are too computationally expensive to perform global exploration across entire videos, creating an inherent trade-off between coverage and efficiency that degrades accuracy especially in medium and long video regimes.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a long-form video (decoded at 1 fps, up to 3600 frames) and a text question (multiple-choice with M options or open-ended) as input. It outputs a curated set of K frames (default K=32, up to 512 for high-capacity models like Qwen3-VL) that are fed to a frozen MLLM, which then produces the final answer. The method is plug-and-play: it requires no fine-tuning of the underlying MLLM and operates as a front-end frame selection module, with the MLLM's own prediction entropy serving as the routing signal for whether additional selection is needed.

## Method

ReQuest comprises three components: (1) a lightweight BLIP-based selector with a 4-layer transformer and learnable score token, trained to regress pseudo-labels computed as the difference between an MLLM's answer probability on a video segment and its baseline probability on a zero-tensor visual input, thereby isolating visual contribution from text priors; (2) a Re-thinking Router that computes prediction entropy over answer options and applies a length-aware threshold (stricter for short videos, more permissive for long ones) to decide whether to trigger the selector or accept the uniform-sampling answer directly; (3) an adaptive greedy NMS sampler that modulates its temporal suppression interval using the same entropy signal—shrinking the interval under high uncertainty to capture tightly clustered evidence and recovering uniform-like spacing under low uncertainty. The key design insight is using the MLLM's own response uncertainty as a self-guided signal, enabling selective computation only where the model is genuinely uncertain, while the selector generalizes across different MLLM backbones without retraining.

## Results

On Video-MME (without subtitles), applying REQUEST to LLaVA-Video (7B, 32 frames) improves overall accuracy from 62.6% to 65.6%, with the largest gains on medium (59.3→64.1) and long (52.2→55.8) subsets. On MLVU, accuracy rises from 64.7% to 71.7%, and on LongVideoBench from 58.0% to 60.1%. The plug-and-play gains transfer to other backbones: LLaVA-OneVision improves from 58.7% to 60.9% on Video-MME, and Qwen3-VL-8B (512 frames) improves from 70.0% to 71.1% on Video-MME and from 62.7% to 66.3% on LongVideoBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REQUEST is a plug-and-play module that improves three base MLLMs without fine-tuning: LLaVA-Video gains +3.0 on Video-MME overall and +7.0 on MLVU; LLaVA-OneVision gains +2.2 on Video-MME and +5.7 on MLVU; Qwen3-VL-8B gains +1.1 on Video-MME and +3.6 on LongVideoBench. Against dedicated long-video methods, LLaVA-Video+REQUEST (65.6% Video-MME overall) outperforms Frame-Voyager (57.5%), LongVU (60.9%), VideoTree (60.6%), and LLoVi (54.7%), though NVILA (64.0%) with 1024 frames remains competitive on overall accuracy while using far more input frames. On the long-video subset specifically, REQUEST (55.8%) surpasses LongVU (59.5% at 1fps but with much higher decode cost) and NVILA (54.8%), while LongVU retains a slight edge due to its 1fps dense observation. The method also generalizes to the high-capacity Qwen3-VL-32B, improving Video-MME from 73.9% to 75.7%, confirming that question-adaptive frame allocation benefits even models with large temporal budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two main lines of frame selection for long-video QA: (1) similarity-based methods (BOLT, MDP3, Q-Frame, AKS) that rely on CLIP/SigLIP cosine similarity and struggle when key objects are absent from the question, and (2) MLLM-based selectors (VideoAgent, VideoTree, Frame-Voyager) that offer deeper semantic understanding but require expensive multi-stage sparse-to-dense pipelines. ReQuest departs from both by distilling MLLM response signals into a lightweight BLIP-based selector (avoiding the multi-stage cost) and by introducing an uncertainty-gated re-thinking router that triggers selection only when needed, combined with an adaptive NMS strategy borrowed from object detection but modulated by prediction entropy. The pseudo-labeling approach—subtracting a text-only baseline from segment-level MLLM probabilities to isolate visual contribution—is a novel supervision signal not seen in prior frame-selection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (no MLLM fine-tuning) and demonstrated transferability across three different backbones (LLaVA-Video, LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen3-VL at 8B and 32B) make this a practically deployable technique for long-video understanding pipelines. The finding that even high-temporal-capacity models like Qwen3-VL benefit from selective frame allocation over denser uniform sampling (512 frames peak; 1024/2048 degrade) challenges the assumption that more frames always help under fixed token budgets. Open questions include: the routing threshold τ₀ must be manually calibrated per benchmark, the method is evaluated only on multiple-choice and open-ended QA (not grounding or generation), and it remains unclear how the approach scales to videos beyond ~30 minutes or to streaming/online settings where the full video is not available upfront.
