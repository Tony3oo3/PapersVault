## Problem

Decades of lunar orbital missions have produced multi-modal remote sensing data (optical, hyperspectral, thermal, radar, gravity, compositional) that remains fragmented across incompatible archives, and no benchmark or foundation model exists for evaluating or leveraging machine learning on lunar data. Earth observation foundation models fail to transfer to the Moon due to the absence of atmosphere, vegetation, and the fundamentally different sensor suite, leaving a gap in AI-assisted lunar science and resource prospecting.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 256×256 pixel patches at 128 pixels-per-degree (~237 m/pixel) comprising 28 z-score-normalized channels organized into seven physically distinct modality groups (surface, thermal, spectral, gravity, radar, Hapke, composition), with spatial coverage ranging from 16% (Mini-RF radar) to 100% (LOLA, GRAIL). Missing data is zeroed after normalization, and the full 46,080×23,040 global map is stored as memory-mapped arrays enabling unlimited random crop sampling during pretraining. Downstream, the 768-dimensional pooled encoder representation feeds classification, regression, or segmentation heads, while segmentation tasks use the 16×16 spatial token grid from the surface group directly.

## Method

MG-MAE extends the masked autoencoder to multi-modal lunar data through three key mechanisms. First, per-group multi-channel convolutional tokenizers (7 Conv2d layers instead of 28) jointly tokenize channels within each physical modality, capturing intra-group correlations such as contiguous M3 spectral bands. Second, a shared ViT-Base encoder processes all group tokens with cross-modal self-attention, while key-dimension attention masking (−∞ on keys only, not queries) gracefully excludes placeholder tokens from groups with no valid data in a given patch. Third, two lunar-specific training strategies address the 16–100% coverage heterogeneity: coverage-adaptive masking sets each group's mask ratio proportionally to its global coverage (m_g = 0.75 + 0.15·(c_g − c̄)), and complementary masking (with probability 0.5) retains 100% of tokens in 1–2 anchor groups while masking others at 90%, forcing cross-modal reconstruction through a shared decoder with cross-attention. The loss combines proportionally weighted per-group MSE (self-balancing harder groups like radar upward), an InfoNCE contrastive term aligning mean-pooled group features, and a spectral continuity regularization (L2 on second-order finite differences across the 8 M3 bands) enforcing physically plausible mineral reflectance reconstructions.

## Results

MG-MAE achieves the best performance across all six downstream tasks. The headline gains are +16.2% geology classification accuracy (56.3% finetune vs. 40.1% scratch) and +14.7 mIoU on crater segmentation (0.768 vs. 0.621). On composition regression, linear probing reaches R²=0.924, surpassing even finetuning (0.908), while cross-modal thermal prediction achieves R²=0.987. Mare segmentation, the simplest task, still benefits from pretraining with +3.5 mIoU (0.936 vs. 0.901).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MG-MAE outperforms all eight baselines on every task where they overlap. Against ImageNet-pretrained ViT-B [7], the gap is largest on geology (56.3% vs. 33.2%, +23.1 points), confirming that natural-image features transfer poorly to planetary data. Vanilla MAE [11] pretrained on the same lunar data but without modality grouping or missing-modality masking trails MG-MAE by 11.5 points on geology and 10.0 mIoU on craters, isolating the value of the grouped architecture. Three Earth-observation foundation models (SatMAE [6], Prithvi-EO-2.0 [32], TerraMind [12]) provide only marginal gains over scratch (+1.2 to +1.9% geology) when adapted via a learned linear projection, far below MG-MAE's +16.2%. On crater segmentation, MG-MAE finetune (0.768 mIoU) beats the task-specific U-Net on DEM [30] by +5.6 mIoU and the CNN on optical imagery [13] by +8.5 mIoU. For composition regression, MG-MAE linear (R²=0.924) exceeds the classical Lucey band-ratio algorithm [16] by +24.3 R² points and a random forest on hand-crafted spectral indices by +14.0 points, demonstrating that self-supervised multi-modal pretraining surpasses domain-expert-designed single-task methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

MG-MAE extends the masked autoencoder framework (MAE) and its multi-modal adaptations (MultiMAE, SatMAE) to the lunar domain, where the core challenge is highly non-uniform instrument coverage (16–100%) rather than the near-complete coverage assumed by Earth observation models. The per-group convolutional tokenizers and shared ViT encoder follow the SatMAE/MultiMAE design, but three mechanisms are specific to the lunar setting: coverage-adaptive masking that scales per-group mask ratios to instrument coverage, spectral continuity regularization exploiting the contiguous M³ hyperspectral bands, and key-dimension attention masking for entirely absent modality groups. The paper also positions itself as the lunar analogue of GEO-Bench and PANGAEA for Earth, and Mars-Bench for Mars, filling a gap where no prior multimodal pretraining or evaluation protocol existed for lunar remote sensing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first common protocol for evaluating foundation models on lunar data, which is critical as the Artemis program and commercial missions generate increasingly complex multi-modal datasets. The near-zero transfer from Earth observation foundation models (best EO model gains only +1.9% over scratch) is a clear warning that domain-specific pretraining is essential for planetary science, and the ablations show that proper handling of missing modalities is the single most important architectural choice. Open questions include whether the approach scales to ViT-Large, how it generalizes to polar regions with severely limited coverage, and whether the framework transfers to other bodies (Mars, Mercury) with comparable multi-modal archives. The release of the 28-channel dataset and evaluation code lowers the barrier for the small community working on lunar ML.
