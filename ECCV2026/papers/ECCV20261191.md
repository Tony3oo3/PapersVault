## Problem

Existing benchmarks for embodied agents prioritize high-level spatial reasoning and strategic planning, leaving the fine-grained action intelligence required for physical interaction underexplored. Prior action benchmarks like FAVOR-Bench target third-person video description at motion level but fail to capture the granular execution sequences, causal-intentional reasoning, and first-person actionable knowledge that an embodied agent needs to physically replicate and generalize actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261191/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes video frames sampled at model-specific frame rates (typically 4 fps, with some models at 1 fps or 8 fps) paired with a text question. Lower-tier tasks (Physical Interaction, Temporal-Causal Relation) produce multiple-choice answers scored by accuracy, while higher-tier tasks (Intentional Understanding, Evaluative Judgment) produce open-ended text responses scored on Correctness and Detailedness via GPT-assisted evaluation. Counterfactual questions across all tiers impose a gating mechanism: the response is scored zero unless the model first explicitly rejects or corrects the question's false premise.

## Method

CFG-Bench organizes fine-grained action intelligence into a four-tiered cognitive framework grounded in Norman's Action Cycle: Physical Interaction (how an action is executed), Temporal-Causal Relation (how actions connect in time and causality), Intentional Understanding (why an action is performed), and Evaluative Judgment (how well an action was done). The dataset comprises 1,368 videos from five sources (EgoTaskQA, Charades-Ego, EgoExo4d, Something-Something-V2, FineAction) and 19,562 QA pairs across 11 tasks, generated through a human-AI collaborative pipeline with GPT-4o drafting, multi-stage filtering (blind/single-frame removal, Qwen3-Max ambiguity detection), and expert verification. The hybrid QA design uses closed-ended MCQs for objective lower tiers and open-ended questions for higher-order reasoning tiers, with counterfactual variants in every tier to combat hallucination. For transfer evaluation, the authors SFT Qwen2.5-VL-7B and InternVL3-8B on CFG-Bench data and demonstrate significant gains on EMBODIEDBENCH (EB-ALFRED, EB-Manipulation) and LIBERO, confirming that fine-grained action knowledge is foundational for both planning and low-level control.

## Results

On CFG-Bench, the best proprietary model Gemini-3-Pro achieves 65.60% average accuracy on close-ended tasks (Avg_c) and 5.67/10 on open-ended tasks (Avg_o), still far below the human baseline of 95.85% and 9.05/10. Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct leads with 57.68% Avg_c and 5.09 Avg_o, outperforming the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (51.28%/4.61). In transfer evaluation, SFT on CFG-Bench data boosts Qwen2.5-VL-7B's EB-ALFRED score from 4.7 to 9.7 and EB-Manipulation from 9.6 to 15.3, while SFT on FAVOR-Bench data actually degrades performance (4.3 and 8.2 respectively). On LIBERO, CFG-Bench SFT improves the Long-horizon split from 94.4 to 95.2 and Goal from 98.6 to 99.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-3-Pro leads all 11 tasks with 65.60% Avg_c and 5.67 Avg_o, outperforming Gemini-2.5-Pro (59.52%/5.40) by ~6 points on close-ended and ~0.3 on open-ended, and GPT-5 (55.44%/4.13) by ~10 points on close-ended. Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct (57.68%/5.09) surpasses the 10× larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (51.28%/4.61) on both metrics, demonstrating that architectural iteration beats raw scaling. The embodied-specialized RoboBrain2.0-7B (44.53%/4.72) outperforms its Qwen2.5-VL-7B base (40.71%/4.38) on open-ended tasks, confirming that embodied fine-tuning helps, though it trails general-purpose models on close-ended physical interaction. Cosmos-Reason1-7B (52.05%/4.80) shows strong temporal-causal reasoning (CRS: 5.19) but weak physical interaction (FAU: 55.97). In the critical transfer evaluation, SFT on CFG-Bench data nearly doubles Qwen2.5-VL-7B's EB-ALFRED score (4.7→9.7) and boosts EB-Manipulation (9.6→15.3), while SFT on FAVOR-Bench data degrades both (4.3 and 8.2), proving that fine-grained cognitive action knowledge—not just motion-level description—transfers to embodied planning and control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two benchmark lineages: embodied agent evaluation (VSI-Bench, ECBench, EMBODIEDEVAL, EMBODIEDBENCH) and action understanding (FAVOR-Bench, Motion-Bench, EgoTaskQA). It departs from the former by arguing that spatial reasoning and high-level planning benchmarks abstract away the fine-grained physical execution details that are the actual bottleneck for embodied interaction, and from the latter by reframing 'fine-grained' from third-person descriptive fidelity to a four-tiered cognitive structure (Physical Interaction, Temporal-Causal Relation, Intentional Understanding, Evaluative Judgment) grounded in Norman's Action Cycle. The counterfactual question mechanism with a gating requirement for false-premise rejection is a methodological departure from standard QA benchmark design, explicitly targeting the acquiescence failure mode in MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that SFT on CFG-Bench data roughly doubles high-level planning scores (EB-ALFRED: 4.7→9.7) and boosts low-level manipulation (EB-Manipulation: 9.6→15.3) on EMBODIEDBENCH, while FAVOR-Bench data shows no such transfer—suggesting that cognitive/intentional/evaluative knowledge, not just motion detail, is the foundational capability for embodied agents. The MCQ-vs-open-ended performance gap (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro scoring 81.9% on FI as MCQ but only 5.18/10 open-ended) reveals that current models exploit textual plausibility rather than visual grounding, a diagnostic that should reshape how the field evaluates video understanding. Open questions include whether the four-tier framework generalizes beyond daily-life and hand-object manipulation to more complex multi-agent or tool-use scenarios, and whether the GPT-assisted open-ended scoring (Krippendorff's α ≈ 0.74) is sufficient for fine-grained quality assessment at scale.
