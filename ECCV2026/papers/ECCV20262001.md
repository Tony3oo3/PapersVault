## Problem

Existing zero-shot object counting methods rely on either automated detection to select a few image patches as exemplars or on generic text embeddings, both of which fail to capture the full intra-class appearance diversity present in a specific image. Patch-based approaches accumulate detection errors and miss instances with atypical appearances, while text embeddings remain agnostic to the concrete visual variations of target objects. This paper asks whether a single, holistic multi-modal representation can be synthesized directly from the image and class name to serve as a more complete and discriminative counting exemplar.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 384×384) and a free-form textual class name as input. It produces a spatial density map of the same resolution, from which the final integer count and per-object locations are extracted via Local-Maxima Detection (LMD) with a 3×3 filter and a 0.05 threshold, rather than the conventional summation of the density map. The LMD step makes the output robust to background noise and additionally yields object coordinates.

## Method

HER-Count synthesizes Hyper-Exemplar Representations (HERs) by feeding concatenated image tokens and a text sequence [class_name, </s>] into a frozen MLLM (Qwen3-VL-2B), then extracting the </s>-position embeddings from the last n=4 transformer layers and projecting them through an MLP to form a set of n HER vectors. A Hierarchical Fusion Strategy injects each HER into a corresponding intermediate layer of the HRNet-W32 vision encoder via element-wise (Hadamard) multiplication, so that shallow HERs guide texture-level features and deep HERs guide semantic-level features in a progressive manner. A Global Discrimination Enhancement (GDE) loss, applied to the deepest HER, simultaneously pulls it toward positive anchors (same-image and same-class HERs) and away from negative anchors at both the image-sample and semantic-class levels, using a log-ratio similarity formulation that automatically up-weights hard samples. The full framework is trained end-to-end with MSE on the density map plus the GDE term (λ=0.1), and the MLLM's rich prior knowledge provides generalization to unseen classes at test time.

## Results

On FSC147, HER-Count achieves MAE 13.56 and RMSE 55.52 on the validation set, outperforming the previous best method T2ICount (MAE 13.78, RMSE 58.78) on both metrics. On the FSC147 test set, HER-Count attains RMSE 95.31, the best among all compared methods, though T2ICount reports a lower MAE of 11.76. On the cross-dataset CARPK benchmark, HER-Count achieves MAE 5.19 / RMSE 6.77 (C→C) and MAE 5.56 / RMSE 7.14 (F→C), substantially outperforming all zero-shot and few-shot baselines. The method also runs at 14.1 FPS with 2.1B parameters, and a CLIP-based variant (0.44B params) reaches 27.1 FPS with competitive accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On FSC147 validation, HER-Count achieves the best MAE (13.56) and RMSE (55.52), beating the closest competitor T2ICount by 0.22 MAE and 3.26 RMSE, and DAVE_prm by 1.92 MAE and 2.95 RMSE. On the FSC147 test set, HER-Count attains the lowest RMSE (95.31) but T2ICount reports a lower MAE (11.76 vs. 14.23), indicating HER-Count has fewer large errors while T2ICount has smaller average deviations on this split. On CARPK, HER-Count dominates all baselines by a wide margin: in the cross-domain F→C setting it achieves MAE 5.56 / RMSE 7.14, outperforming the best few-shot method BMNet+ (MAE 10.44 / RMSE 13.77) and the best zero-shot method VA-Count (MAE 10.63 / RMSE 13.20) by roughly 2× in RMSE. In the C→C setting, HER-Count (MAE 5.19 / RMSE 6.77) also surpasses the 3-shot methods GMN (7.48/9.90) and CounTR (5.75/7.45). The efficiency comparison shows HER-Count (CLIP variant) matches T2ICount's accuracy with 5× fewer parameters and 19× faster inference (27.1 vs. 1.4 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms in zero-shot object counting: (1) detect-and-select methods (ZSC, VA-Count, DAVE) that rely on automated detectors to extract a small number of image patches as exemplars, and (2) text-embedding methods (CLIP-Count, T2ICount) that match generic class-level text vectors against image regions. Rather than either approach, HER-Count repurposes a multi-modal large language model (Qwen3-VL-2B) to synthesize holistic 'Hyper-Exemplar Representations' from the full image and class name jointly, eliminating the detection stage and its error accumulation. The hierarchical fusion strategy and the global discrimination enhancement loss are novel architectural and training contributions layered on top of this MLLM-based exemplar synthesis, building on standard density-map estimation and HRNet-style vision encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HER-Count is the first end-to-end MLLM-based framework for zero-shot counting, and its cross-dataset generalization on CARPK (MAE 5.56 vs. 10.63 for the next-best method) is a notably large margin that suggests the holistic exemplar representation captures transferable structure. The finding that a 437M-parameter CLIP variant approaches the performance of 2B-parameter baselines implies the framework design, not raw model scale, drives the gains—useful for practitioners with limited compute. Open questions include whether the FSC147 test-set inconsistency (HER-Count MAE 14.23 vs. T2ICount 11.76) reflects a genuine generalization weakness or dataset-specific noise, and whether the MLLM's prior knowledge provides true zero-shot transfer to truly novel categories or merely acts as a stronger multi-modal encoder.
