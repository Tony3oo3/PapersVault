## Problem

All-in-one image restoration frameworks struggle with feature interference in shared backbones (prompt-based methods) and insufficient expert specialization (MoE-based methods). Existing routing mechanisms rely on unconstrained linear embeddings that introduce class-distance bias, destabilizing similarity-based expert selection, and they neglect spatially non-uniform degradations and the granularity mismatch between patch-based training and full-resolution inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261146/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image and produces a restored image of the same resolution. Internally, the input is consumed by two CLIP encoders (content and degradation) to extract routing signals, and the GLGF module additionally partitions the image into a fixed 4×4 grid of crops for degradation severity estimation. The 4-level encoder-decoder backbone (with [4,6,6,8] Transformer blocks) processes the image at multiple scales, with each of the 8 backbone layers independently selecting one of 3 experts, yielding up to 3⁸ = 6561 unique inference paths per input.

## Method

SLER-IR replaces each backbone layer with three parameter-independent experts and performs layer-wise dynamic expert selection via cosine-similarity gating on a unit hypersphere. Degradation representations are L2-normalized (spherical uniform embedding) and optimized with a triplet-constrained hyperspherical contrastive loss that sharpens angular decision boundaries and eliminates the class-distance bias of linear spaces. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 uses probabilistic (softmax) routing with joint L1 + contrastive loss to learn stable degradation-aware representations, while Stage 2 freezes the router and switches to deterministic top-1 selection with L1-only loss to refine expert specialization. A Global-Local Granularity Fusion (GLGF) module constructs a content semantic patch map (via FiLM-calibrated CLIP patch tokens) and a degradation severity patch map (from 4×4 crop-wise CLS tokens), fuses them through cross-attention into a restoration prior, and injects this prior into the backbone via guided cross-attention to bridge the train-test granularity gap.

## Results

On the five-task all-in-one benchmark (dehazing, deraining, denoising, deblurring, low-light), SLER-IR achieves the best average PSNR/SSIM of 31.73/0.928, surpassing the previous best (MoCE-IR) by 1.15 dB and 0.009. On the three-task setting, it reaches 33.14/0.922, outperforming MoCE-IR by 0.41 dB. The largest per-task gains are +2.59 dB on SOTS (dehazing), +1.22 dB on GoPro (deblurring), and +0.96 dB on LOL (low-light enhancement) over the strongest prior method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLER-IR outperforms all eight compared baselines on the five-task average. Against the closest competitor MoCE-IR (CVPR'25), it gains +1.15 dB PSNR and +0.009 SSIM overall, with the largest per-task margins on dehazing (+2.95 dB over MoCE-IR on SOTS) and deblurring (+1.22 dB on GoPro). VLU-Net (CVPR'25) is second-closest at 30.11/0.905, trailing SLER-IR by 1.62 dB. Among prompt-based methods, InstructIR (ECCV'24) and PromptIR (NeurIPS'23) achieve 29.55/0.907 and 29.15/0.904 respectively, both roughly 2 dB below SLER-IR. General-purpose restorers (Restormer, NAFNet, MPRNet) and weather-specific models (TransWeather, AirNet) lag further behind, with AirNet scoring only 25.49/0.846 on the five-task average. Notably, SLER-IR wins on every individual task in both the three-task and five-task settings, indicating consistent rather than task-specific superiority.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLER-IR builds directly on the PromptIR architecture (4-level encoder-decoder with [4,6,6,8] Transformer blocks) but departs from both dominant paradigms in all-in-one restoration: feature-modulation methods (AirNet, PromptIR, InstructIR) that adapt a shared backbone via prompts, and local MoE methods (MoCE-IR, WM-MoE) that route within isolated modules. The key conceptual departure is replacing linear degradation embeddings with hyperspherical (unit-sphere) representations and performing layer-wise expert routing across all eight backbone layers, yielding 3^8 = 6561 compositional inference paths. A two-stage training protocol (probabilistic → deterministic routing) and a Global-Local Granularity Fusion module for spatially non-uniform degradations further distinguish it from prior expert-based approaches that typically use single-level or task-ID gating.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hyperspherical embedding addresses a concrete geometric pathology (class-distance bias in linear spaces) that can destabilize similarity-based routing, and the ablation showing a ~3 dB jump from the contrastive loss alone suggests this representation geometry is a critical, previously underappreciated factor in expert routing quality. The layer-wise compositional routing paradigm offers a principled way to handle composite degradations without combinatorial parameter explosion. Open questions include whether the CLIP-based degradation extractor generalizes to truly unseen or novel degradation types, how the approach scales beyond 5 tasks or more than 3 experts per layer, and whether the spherical geometry advantage persists at larger model scales or with alternative backbone architectures.
