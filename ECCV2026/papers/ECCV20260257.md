## Problem

Most leading stereo matching methods rely on cost volumes, which incur memory overhead that scales linearly with disparity range and are typically processed at low resolution (e.g., 1/4 scale), limiting accuracy on detailed regions. While WAFT demonstrated that cost volumes can be replaced by high-resolution feature-space warping for optical flow, it remained unclear whether this substitution would work for stereo matching, where displacements span hundreds of pixels and are restricted to the horizontal epipolar line.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260257/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a rectified stereo image pair (I_L, I_R) at arbitrary resolution and produces a dense disparity field d ∈ R^{H×W} mapping each left-view pixel to its horizontal offset in the right view. Warping and iterative refinement operate at half resolution (H/2 × W/2), with the final disparity upsampled to full input resolution via convex upsampling. Disparity is discretized into B=40 bins over a maximum range of D_max=800 pixels for the initial classification step.

## Method

WAFT-Stereo replaces cost-volume indexing with backward warping of right-view features at each iteration, eliminating the memory overhead of 3D cost volumes and enabling high-resolution processing. The architecture comprises three stages: (1) a pretrained DAv2 backbone fine-tuned with LoRA adapters plus a DPT head for feature extraction; (2) a classification module (ViT-S + DPT) that predicts a soft distribution over 40 disparity bins, supervised by soft-cross-entropy loss, providing a coarse initial estimate via soft-argmax; and (3) a recurrent updater (ViT-S + DPT with ResNet high-resolution blocks) that backward-warps right-view features using the current disparity and regresses residual updates with a Mixture-of-Laplace loss over T−1 iterations. The key insight is that the classification formulation itself—not cost-volume-specific designs—enables fast convergence on large displacements, since pure regression from zero struggles with hundreds-of-pixel offsets typical in high-resolution stereo. This design achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on ETH3D, KITTI, and Middlebury while being 1.8–6.7× faster than leading cost-volume-based methods.

## Results

WAFT-Stereo (DAv2-L, 5 iterations) achieves the best zero-shot BP-0.5-noc of 0.89 on ETH3D, reducing error by 61% over the strongest prior zero-shot baseline (FoundationStereo, 2.31) and by 81% on BP-1-noc. On fine-tuned benchmarks, it achieves the best BP-2-noc of 1.18 on KITTI-2012 (13% reduction over MonSter++), best D1-noc of 1.21 on KITTI-2015 (6% reduction), and best RMSE-noc of 5.61 on Middlebury (6% reduction). The model runs at 106 ms per 540p pair on an L40 GPU, a 6.7× speedup over FoundationStereo (708 ms) and 1.8× over S2M2-XL (195 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WAFT-Stereo outperforms all 14 compared baselines on at least one primary metric across the three benchmarks. Against the strongest fine-tuned competitors, it beats MonSter++ [7] by 6% on ETH3D BP-0.5 (0.89 vs 1.28), 13% on KITTI-2012 BP-2 (1.18 vs 1.30), and 6% on Middlebury RMSE (5.61 vs 6.03), while being 2.0× faster. It surpasses S2M2-XL [30] on ETH3D BP-0.5 (0.89 vs 0.93) and KITTI D1 (1.21 vs 1.29), though S2M2 retains a lead on Middlebury BP-2-noc (1.15 vs 2.53), a gap the authors attribute to a single challenging scene (Classroom2E). In the zero-shot setting, WAFT-Stereo reduces ETH3D BP-0.5 by 61% over FoundationStereo [57] (0.89 vs 2.31) and KITTI-2015 D1 by 9% over S2M2-XL (2.79 vs 3.12). BridgeDepth [11] is the only method faster than WAFT-Stereo (51 ms vs 106 ms), but WAFT-Stereo achieves substantially better accuracy (e.g., 0.89 vs 1.59 on ETH3D BP-0.5-noc). Older baselines such as RAFT-Stereo [23], CREStereo [22], and CroCo-Stereo [56] are outperformed by large margins across all benchmarks (e.g., 7.04 vs 0.89 on ETH3D BP-0.5-noc for RAFT-Stereo).

## Relation to Prior Work

WAFT-Stereo directly extends the warping-based iterative refinement framework from WAFT (optical flow) to stereo matching, replacing the cost-volume indexing that dominates the RAFT-Stereo lineage (RAFT-Stereo, MonSter++, FoundationStereo, S2M2). The key conceptual departure is demonstrating that the classification-before-regression strategy—previously implemented via cost-volume softmax or optimal transport in BridgeDepth and S2M2—can be realized with a standard ViT operating on warped features, without any cost-volume-specific architecture. Architecturally, it also simplifies WAFT's U-Net adaptation layer by using LoRA fine-tuning of a frozen DepthAnythingV2 backbone, and replaces high-resolution skip connections with ResNet blocks in the recurrent updater.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that cost volumes are dispensable for SOTA stereo matching challenges a design assumption that has underpinned the field for years, and the 1.8–6.7× speedup at equal or better accuracy makes high-resolution, real-time stereo matching more practical for autonomous driving and AR. The strong zero-shot generalization (81% ETH3D error reduction trained purely on synthetic data) suggests the warping formulation may be more robust to sim-to-real gaps than cost-volume designs. Open questions include whether the BP-2 gap on Middlebury (driven by a single illumination-challenging scene) reflects a fundamental limitation of warping for large photometric discrepancies, and whether the classification step's benefit generalizes to other correspondence tasks beyond 1-D disparity.
