## Problem

Pixel-level semantic left-right prediction of in-the-wild images remains unsolved: existing methods either detect extrinsic symmetry axes (failing under occlusion, partiality, and pose variation), rely on annotated keypoint supervision for semantic correspondence, or operate only on 3D shapes. No prior work performs dense, unsupervised, pixel-wise left-right understanding directly on single-view images across diverse object categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261442/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (in-the-wild or rendered) and a binary object mask (e.g., from SAM) as input. A frozen vision foundation model (Stable Diffusion + DINOv3) extracts per-pixel feature maps, which are fed into a lightweight predictor. The output is a dense per-pixel left-right prediction map with values in [-1, 1], where the sign encodes semantic left vs. right and the magnitude reflects confidence. During training, the same pipeline also consumes rendered views of 3D meshes, producing per-vertex left-right values via pixel-to-vertex aggregation.

## Method

Pix2LR introduces a hybrid unsupervised training strategy that jointly uses a medium-scale 3D shape dataset (BECOS, containing human- and quadruped-animal-like meshes) and in-the-wild image datasets (SPAIR-71K, PF-PASCAL) spanning many more categories. The key architectural insight is swapping the order of left-right prediction and surface aggregation relative to the 3D-only method χ: a lightweight pixel-wise predictor P_Φ first maps image feature maps to dense left-right values, and only then are pixel predictions aggregated to vertices for rendered images using known camera correspondences. This design keeps both the 3D-shape and image data paths unobstructed, enabling consistent joint training. Training is fully unsupervised, combining three 3D geometric losses (dissimilarity, total variation, fifty-fifty) computed on per-vertex values from rendered shapes with an image-based symmetric-consistency loss that penalises agreement between predictions on original and horizontally-flipped in-the-wild images.

## Results

On SPAIR-71K (in-the-wild images with large pose variation), Pix2LR achieves 86.8% average keypoint accuracy (AVG acc) and 86.8% overall accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline χ_BeCoS (77.3%/77.3%) by ~9.5 points and GeoAware (63.2%/63.2%) by ~23.6 points. On PF-PASCAL, the method reaches 93.9% AVG acc and 100% overall acc, surpassing χ_BeCoS (67.2%/79.0%) by a large margin. On rendered BECOS images with dense pixel-wise ground truth, Pix2LR scores 87.4% AVG acc, exceeding χ_BeCoS (71.4%) by 16 points and GeoAware (73.7%) by 13.7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pix2LR consistently outperforms all three baselines across all three evaluation settings. Against χ_BeCoS [30] (the closest competitor, a vertex-wise 3D left-right predictor adapted to pixels), Pix2LR gains ~9.5 points on SPAIR-71K, ~26.7 points on PF-PASCAL, and ~16 points on rendered BECOS images. Against GeoAware [35], which uses vision foundation model features for left-right pose estimation, Pix2LR leads by ~23 points on SPAIR-71K and ~44 points on PF-PASCAL, though GeoAware slightly edges out Pix2LR on rendered BECOS images (73.7% vs 87.4% — actually Pix2LR still wins here). The raw feature baselines SD+DINO [36] and SD+DINOv3 perform near chance level (~50–57%) on in-the-wild images, confirming that simply using foundation model features without a dedicated left-right predictor is insufficient. Notably, Pix2LR generalises to unseen object categories (cars, aeroplanes, trains) that are absent from both the 3D shape training set (BECOS: humans and quadrupeds only) and the image training sets, demonstrating that the hybrid 3D+image training transfers left-right semantics across categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the vertex-wise semantic left-right prediction framework χ [30] from 3D shapes to 2D images by swapping the order of left-right prediction and surface aggregation, enabling a single predictor to operate consistently on both rendered and in-the-wild images. It builds on the observation (from [3,6,35]) that vision foundation models (Stable Diffusion, DINOv3) encode rich left-right semantics, and on Diff3F [4] for decorating 3D shapes with image-based features. The key departure from prior 2D symmetry work [26,27,33,34] is the shift from geometric axis detection to dense pixel-wise semantic prediction, and from the semantic correspondence community [8,29,35] is the move from sparse keypoint-level to full pixel-level output without requiring annotated keypoint pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unsupervised framework for dense pixel-wise semantic left-right prediction on in-the-wild images, filling a gap between 3D vertex-wise methods and 2D symmetry-axis detectors. The finding that a medium-scale 3D dataset of only human and quadruped animal shapes provides sufficient geometric prior to generalize to entirely unseen categories (cars, trains, aeroplanes) suggests that left-right semantics are a shared structural property rather than a category-specific one. Open questions include handling objects with multiple or rotational symmetries, improving predictions for thin structures (bicycle handlebars), and whether larger or more diverse 3D shape datasets would further improve robustness to unusual poses and out-of-distribution views.
