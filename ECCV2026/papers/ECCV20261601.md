## Problem

Teacher-student domain adaptive object detection methods rely exclusively on proposal-level pseudo-boxes for target-domain supervision, which suffer from substantial classification and localization noise under domain shift and from fixed confidence thresholds that discard challenging instances. The paper identifies that image-level predictions—obtained by aggregating proposal scores—achieve significantly higher accuracy than individual pseudo-boxes, yet have been entirely overlooked as a supervision source in DAOD despite their extensive use in weakly supervised detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261601/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB target-domain images through a standard Faster R-CNN pipeline (VGG-16 or ResNet-101 backbone) and produces two complementary outputs: fine-grained bounding boxes with class labels for detection, and a C-dimensional vector of per-category presence probabilities at the image level. The image-level predictions are derived entirely from existing RPN and detection-head outputs via a parameter-free aggregation mechanism, requiring no additional network modules or annotation cost beyond what the teacher already computes.

## Method

MGPL introduces a dual-competition aggregation mechanism that converts RPN proposals into reliable image-level predictions: each proposal first undergoes top-k intra-proposal class competition to sharpen semantic probabilities, then its objectness score is routed to a single category via argmax, and proposals compete within each category by objectness before quality-weighted aggregation yields the final image-level vector. A coarse-to-fine collaborative filtering step then computes per-proposal scores as the element-wise product of proposal class probabilities and image-level predictions, and selects an adaptive threshold by maximizing between-class variance (an Otsu-style criterion) over the score histogram, eliminating hand-tuned confidence thresholds. The student is trained with a combined objective: standard source-domain detection loss plus a binary cross-entropy image-level loss (weight λ_img) and a proposal-level detection loss on filtered pseudo-boxes (weight λ_prop), enabling the two granularities to mutually reinforce—coarse-grained supervision stabilizes category learning while fine-grained supervision refines localization.

## Results

On Pascal VOC → Clipart1k, MGPL achieves 51.4% mAP@50, surpassing the previous best (CAT, 49.1%) by 2.3% and the fully-supervised Oracle (45.0%) by 6.4%. On Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes, MGPL reaches 52.7% mAP, outperforming DSD-DA (52.3%) and CMT (51.9%). On KITTI → Cityscapes, MGPL achieves 64.3% AP on the 'car' category, matching CMT (64.3%) and approaching the Oracle (64.4%), while substantially outperforming all other baselines (next best: SIGMA++ at 49.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGPL achieves the best mAP on all three benchmarks. On Pascal VOC → Clipart1k, it outperforms the strongest prior method CAT (49.1%) by 2.3% and CMT (47.0%) by 4.4%, with particularly large gains on classes like 'tv' (62.4% vs. 51.8% for HTCN) and 'plant' (62.9% vs. 57.5% for CAT). On Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes, MGPL (52.7%) edges out DSD-DA (52.3%) and CMT (51.9%), and surpasses the AT baseline (49.8%) by 2.9%. On KITTI → Cityscapes, MGPL (64.3%) ties CMT (64.3%) and nearly matches the Oracle (64.4%), while the next-best non-Oracle method (SIGMA++, 49.5%) trails by 14.8%. The ablation confirms that the multi-granularity combination (image-level + instance-level) is the primary driver: adding image-level supervision alone yields only 46.1% vs. 45.7% for AT, but combining both granularities jumps to 50.6%, and the collaborative filtering adds a further 0.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Adaptive Teacher (AT) framework for teacher-student domain adaptive object detection, adopting its EMA teacher updates, weak-strong augmentation, and adversarial feature alignment. It departs from the prevailing paradigm in which teacher-student DAOD methods rely exclusively on proposal-level pseudo-boxes as supervision, drawing instead on the weakly supervised object detection literature where image-level labels provide global semantic guidance. The key conceptual departure is treating image-level predictions—derived by aggregating RPN proposals via a dual-competition mechanism—as a first-class supervision signal alongside instance-level pseudo-boxes, a combination previously unexplored in DAOD despite its extensive use in WSOD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that aggregated image-level predictions are more reliable than individual proposal-level pseudo-boxes under domain shift challenges the implicit assumption that finer-grained supervision is always preferable, and the multi-granularity framework offers a practical recipe for combining both. The 6.4% mAP improvement over the fully-supervised Oracle on Pascal VOC → Clipart1k is notable, though the approach is architecturally tied to two-stage Faster R-CNN detectors, and extending the dual-granularity supervision to one-stage or transformer-based detectors (e.g., DETR variants) remains an open and non-trivial problem. The coarse-to-fine filtering mechanism, which requires no manually tuned thresholds, could be adopted more broadly in pseudo-labeling pipelines beyond DAOD.
