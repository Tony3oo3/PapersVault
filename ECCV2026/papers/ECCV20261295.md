## Problem

Existing 3D scene generation methods—whether retrieval-based, text-driven, or single-image reconstruction—produce scenes that lack instance-level interactivity and physical plausibility, making them unsuitable for direct import into physics simulators for robotic manipulation data synthesis. Generated instances often exhibit fused or broken topology, and layouts suffer from interpenetration, floating objects, and boundary violations, preventing reliable motion planning and large-scale trajectory generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261295/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a free-form text description (expanded by an LLM into a detailed prompt and rendered into a reference image via Seedream) or a single RGB photograph of a tabletop. The output is a set of canonicalized 3D instance meshes, each parameterized by a 3D model, anisotropic scale (R³), translation (R³), and a single vertical-axis rotation (R), assembled in Isaac Sim with convex-decomposition collision geometry, gravity, and friction. The resulting environment supports automated motion planning (via Curobo) and multimodal data collection (multi-view RGB, joint states, actions), as well as in-scene editing (add, remove, replace, rearrange) to derive scene variants without re-running the full pipeline.

## Method

TabletopGen is a training-free, three-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (Generative Instance Extraction) segments instances via MLLM + GroundedSAM-v2, completes occluded masks with a multimodal generative model, reconstructs 3D meshes via Hunyuan3D-3.0, and corrects each mesh to a Z-up canonical orientation. Stage 2 (Pose and Scale Alignment) is the core novelty: it decouples layout recovery into a Differentiable Rotation Optimizer (DRO) that minimizes a tri-modal loss (soft IoU silhouette + one-sided Chamfer edge + DINOv2 perceptual) over a differentiable renderer to recover per-instance yaw, and a Top-View Spatial Alignment (TSA) module that synthesizes a top-view image, extracts 2D bounding boxes, selects a scaling anchor via a Ratio-Matched Area-Weighted Score (RMA-Score), and infers globally consistent translations and metric scales constrained by MLLM-inferred stacking relations. Stage 3 assembles all instances in Isaac Sim with physical properties and runs a motion planner to synthesize collision-free manipulation trajectories, validated by zero-shot real-to-sim-to-real transfer on an AgileX PiPER arm using a π0.5 policy fine-tuned solely on 2,000 synthetic trajectories per task with domain randomization.

## Results

On the 78-sample image-driven test set, TabletopGen achieves the best scores across all metrics: LPIPS 0.4483, DINOv2 0.8383, CLIP 0.9077, and a GPT-4o average rating of 6.19/7 (vs. 4.33 for the second-best MIDI). The most striking gap is in physical plausibility: TabletopGen's object-level collision rate (Col_O) is 0.42% and scene-level (Col_S) is 7.69%, compared to 8–17% Col_O and 68–99% Col_S for all baselines. In the text-to-scene setting (30 pairs), TabletopGen scores 6.27/7 average GPT-4o rating versus 4.12 (MesaTask) and 4.08 (Holodeck-table), while maintaining near-zero collisions (0.20% Col_O). A 128-participant user study confirms these gains, with TabletopGen selected as the overall preference in 83.13% of cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TabletopGen outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the three image-driven methods (ACDC, Gen3DSR, MIDI), the advantage is most dramatic in physical plausibility: MIDI, the strongest image-driven baseline, still suffers 17.39% object-level collisions and 98.72% scene-level collisions, whereas TabletopGen reduces these to 0.42% and 7.69% respectively, a >40× reduction in Col_O. On perceptual quality, TabletopGen's DINOv2 score (0.8383) exceeds MIDI's (0.7070) by ~19%, and its GPT-4o average (6.19) is 43% higher than MIDI's (4.33). In the text-to-scene setting, TabletopGen (6.27 avg) substantially beats MesaTask (4.12) and Holodeck-table (4.08) on all four GPT-4o sub-scores; Holodeck-table achieves zero collisions only by enforcing hard 2D bounding-box constraints that produce sparse, unrealistic layouts, while MesaTask still has 63.33% scene-level collisions. The user study (128 participants) further confirms the gap: TabletopGen's mean rating of 5.56/7 is 56% higher than MIDI's 3.57, and it is the overall preference in 83.13% of pairwise comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

TabletopGen carves out a specific niche within 3D scene generation by targeting simulation-ready tabletop environments for robotic manipulation, a setting where prior methods fall short: retrieval-based approaches (ACDC, Gen3DSR) are constrained by fixed asset libraries, LLM-driven text-to-scene methods (Holodeck, MesaTask) are designed for room-scale layouts and ignore tabletop-specific physical constraints, and single-image reconstruction methods (MIDI) produce fused or broken instance meshes without physical priors. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, the paper repurposes a pipeline of existing components—GroundedSAM for segmentation, Hunyuan3D for image-to-3D, DINOv2 for perceptual matching, and Isaac Sim for physics—into a training-free, decoupled framework whose core novelty lies in the DRO+TSA pose-and-scale alignment strategy that injects tabletop-specific physical priors (Z-support, XY-boundary, stacking relations) absent from prior scene generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete bottleneck in embodied AI: the cost and limited diversity of real-world manipulation data collection, and demonstrates that a fully synthetic, training-free pipeline can produce scenes whose trajectories support zero-shot real-to-sim-to-real policy transfer on a physical arm without any real-world fine-tuning. This is a strong practical signal that generative scene construction can serve as a scalable data engine for VLA training. However, the scope is limited to single-tabletop scenes (no multi-level shelves, cabinets, or multi-room environments), the zero-shot transfer is validated on only three pick-and-place/stacking tasks with one arm, and the method's reliance on a chain of external models (MLLM, text-to-image, image-to-3D, differentiable renderer) introduces compounding error sources that may limit robustness on highly cluttered or atypical scenes.
