## Problem

Diffractive Snapshot Spectral Imaging (DSSI) encodes spectral information non-uniformly across space, concentrating effective coding cues in texture-rich regions. Existing reconstruction networks compensate by relying heavily on global attention modules to propagate spectral features into texture-less areas, which makes them short-sighted in locally coded regions and computationally redundant. The paper asks whether a progressive local-to-global decomposition can match or exceed global-only performance at substantially lower cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260842/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D RGB coded image (896×896 central crop) produced by a diffractive optical element that simultaneously encodes 42 spectral bands (480–900 nm, 10 nm spacing) into one measurement, with 14-bit Poisson and Gaussian (σ=5×10⁻³) noise. Because the diffractive encoding is direction-dependent, the test protocol applies two fixed metamers plus flip and transpose augmentations, expanding the effective test set by 16×. The output is a full hyperspectral cube of shape H×W×42.

## Method

LGSRNet is a two-stage progressive U-shaped network. The local subnetwork (depth 2) uses depthwise convolutions and a Spatially-Guided Channel Attention (SGCA) module—where spatial attention first highlights texture-rich regions before channel attention is computed—plus a low-rank bottleneck to recover fine spectral details in effectively coded areas. The global subnetwork (depth 1–2) employs a global attention module (LVSSM or SMSA) to model long-range dependencies and propagate local cues into weakly coded smooth regions. An Edge-Aware Gated Fusion (EAGF) module adaptively injects local features into global decoders by constructing an edge-aware gating map via depthwise convolutions on layer-normalized features, balancing high-frequency detail preservation against low-frequency smoothness. Training uses an Effective Coding-Based Sample Selection (ECSS) strategy that discards patches where the coded signal is dominated by noise, combined with a two-stage loss: full-image L1+SSIM on the final output and a masked L1+SSIM (ECL) on the intermediate local output restricted to effectively coded regions.

## Results

On the ICVL dataset, LGSRNet achieves 42.76 dB PSNR, 0.0284 SAM, and 0.9800 SSIM, outperforming the strongest baseline LLDN (42.40 dB) by 0.36 dB while using 24% fewer FLOPs (102.34 vs. 135.36 GFLOPs). On VNIRSR, LGSRNet reaches 37.77 dB PSNR and 0.0394 SAM, exceeding LLDN by 0.62 dB. The lightweight variants LGSRNet-S (63.67 GFLOPs) and LGSRNet-T (47.16 GFLOPs) retain competitive accuracy (42.55 and 42.35 dB on ICVL) at substantially lower computational cost, with inference latencies of 98 ms and 69 ms respectively on an RTX 4090, compared to 172 ms for LLDN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGSRNet outperforms all 12 baselines on both ICVL and VNIRSR across PSNR, SAM, and SSIM. Against the closest DSSI-specific competitor LLDN [35], LGSRNet gains +0.36 dB PSNR on ICVL and +0.62 dB on VNIRSR while reducing FLOPs by 24% (102.34 vs. 135.36 GFLOPs) and cutting inference latency from 172 ms to 135 ms. Compared to the prior two-stage DSSI method Xu's [25], LGSRNet improves by +0.22 dB (ICVL) and +0.86 dB (VNIRSR) at roughly half the FLOPs (102.34 vs. 205.30). Against spectral-SR baselines MST++ [4] and CESST [26], LGSRNet leads by +0.47 dB and +1.99 dB on ICVL respectively, confirming that the local-to-global decomposition better exploits DSSI's spatially uneven coding. The natural-image-restoration baselines Restormer [29] and MPRNet [30] trail by +0.58 dB and +0.57 dB on ICVL, with MPRNet incurring 2.7× more FLOPs. The tiny variant LGSRNet-T (47.16 GFLOPs) still surpasses DPU [32] and CSST [16] by +1.21 dB and +1.02 dB on ICVL, demonstrating that the architecture scales effectively to very low budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-stage progressive restoration paradigm of MPRNet [30] and the two-stage DSSI framework of Xu et al. [25] by reformulating spectral reconstruction as a local-to-global process that follows the spatially uneven coding order inherent to diffractive optical elements. It departs from prior DSSI reconstruction networks (MST++, LLDN) that rely on monolithic global attention modules, arguing that such designs are short-sighted in texture-rich regions and computationally redundant. The key architectural departure is the explicit separation into a local subnetwork (using depthwise convolutions and spatially-guided channel attention) that is directly supervised on effectively coded regions, followed by a global subnetwork (using LVSSM or SMSA) that propagates local cues via an edge-aware gated fusion mechanism, rather than the parallel or alternating local-global integration used by CESST, SPECAT, and S²Trans.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DSSI offers a more compact and light-efficient alternative to CASSI, and this work makes its reconstruction practical by achieving SOTA quality at roughly half the FLOPs and 20–60% lower inference latency of prior methods, which matters for embedded and real-time spectral imaging. The local-to-global decomposition is principled rather than generic: it is directly motivated by the observation that diffractive coding is spatially nonuniform, and the ablations confirm that this ordering (local before global) outperforms parallel or alternating arrangements. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to other PSF-encoded tasks (depth, polarization) as the authors speculate, and whether the gains hold on larger or more diverse datasets beyond the two 42-band benchmarks used here.
