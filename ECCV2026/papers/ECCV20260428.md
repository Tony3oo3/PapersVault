## Problem

Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models achieve competitive text-to-image quality with fewer decoding steps than raster-scan autoregression, but late-scale refinement dominates inference time due to quadratic token growth and costly attention. Existing accelerations either filter tokens heuristically (causing severe artifacts) or rely on extra decision models requiring per-model fine-tuning, leaving no training-free, FlashAttention-compatible mechanism for precise token pruning in VAR.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260428/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ARVAR is a plug-and-play accelerator applied to existing VAR backbones (HART, Infinity) that take a text prompt as conditioning and produce a 1024×1024 RGB image. The text prompt is injected at the earliest coarse-scale step and propagated through the multi-scale token hierarchy; the final high-resolution token grid is decoded to the output image. The method operates entirely at inference time with no architectural modification, requiring only a few lines of code to integrate into any VAR model.

## Method

ARVAR exploits two empirically discovered properties of VAR: (1) attention patterns at corresponding layers remain consistent across generation steps, and (2) conditional and unconditional branches progressively converge at later scales. Attention Retrospect (AR) leverages cross-step attention consistency by computing token saliency from self-attention maps at a low-resolution step (where full attention is cheap), upsampled to the current resolution, then pruning low-salience tokens and reconstructing the full activation map via scatter with cached activations from an earlier step—bypassing the need to materialize attention matrices at high resolution and preserving FlashAttention compatibility. Branch Halving (BH) drops the unconditional branch in the final step(s), substituting its output with the conditional branch's output, halving FLOPs and peak memory. The two components are orthogonal and combine to deliver 1.56× and 3.01× speedups on HART and Infinity respectively with negligible quality degradation.

## Results

ARVAR achieves 1.56× speedup (0.60s vs 0.94s) on HART and 3.01× speedup (0.88s vs 2.63s) on Infinity-2B, both with FlashAttention enabled. On MJHQ30K, ARVAR on Infinity improves SSIM from 0.6870 to 0.7203, reduces LPIPS from 0.1526 to 0.1256, and lowers FID from 30.79 to 29.65 compared to FastVAR, while also surpassing the unaccelerated Infinity baseline on FID (29.65 vs 31.06). On GenEval, ARVAR on Infinity reaches 0.69 overall accuracy (vs 0.68 for Infinity and 0.68 for FastVAR+Infinity), and ARVAR on HART reaches 0.51 (vs 0.49 for HART and 0.50 for FastVAR+HART).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FastVAR (the primary acceleration baseline), ARVAR wins on every metric for both backbones: on HART it delivers higher speedup (1.56× vs 1.47×), lower latency (0.60s vs 0.64s), and substantially better fidelity (SSIM 0.7455 vs 0.6970, LPIPS 0.1186 vs 0.1597, PSNR 22.47 vs 20.96 on GenEval; FID 27.67 vs 28.19 on MJHQ30K); on Infinity the gap widens (3.01× vs 2.80×, FID 29.65 vs 30.79, SSIM 0.7203 vs 0.6870). Against ToMe and SparseVAR on HART (Table 4), ARVAR achieves the highest speedup (1.56× vs 1.40× and 1.50×) and best fidelity across SSIM, LPIPS, PSNR, and FID. On the cross-paradigm GenEval comparison (Table 3), ARVAR on Infinity (0.69 overall, 0.88s) outperforms diffusion models SDXL (0.55, 4.4s), PixArt-Sigma (0.54, 2.6s), and SD3-medium (0.62, 4.5s) on both quality and latency, and dominates autoregressive baselines LlamaGen (0.32, 40.2s) and Show-o (0.65, 48.7s) by a wide margin in speed while matching or exceeding their GenEval scores. On HPSv2.1 and ImageReward (Table 6), ARVAR on Infinity scores 30.05 and 0.9118 respectively, beating FastVAR (29.92, 0.9046) and ToMe (29.74, 0.8875) while maintaining the highest speedup (3.01×). The HART and Infinity baselines serve as the unaccelerated backbones into which ARVAR is plugged; ARVAR consistently improves FID over the unaccelerated baseline on Infinity (29.65 vs 31.06) while matching or slightly exceeding GenEval accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARVAR builds directly on the VAR next-scale prediction paradigm (Tian et al., 2024) and its scaled instantiations HART and Infinity, targeting the well-identified bottleneck of quadratic attention cost at high-resolution scales. It departs from prior VAR accelerators—FastVAR's frequency-based token filtering, SkipVAR's learned step-skipping, and ToMe/SparseVAR's merging/pruning—by introducing the first attention-driven token pruning mechanism for VAR, grounded in an empirical finding that attention patterns at corresponding layers remain consistent across generation steps. The Branch Halving component is a new observation specific to VAR's classifier-free-guidance structure, noting that conditional and unconditional branches converge at late scales, enabling safe 50% FLOPs reduction without the per-model fine-tuning that SkipVAR requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.01× speedup on Infinity with negligible quality degradation and full FlashAttention compatibility makes ARVAR a practical drop-in accelerator for high-resolution VAR deployment, addressing a real latency bottleneck (0.94s → 0.60s on HART, 2.63s → 0.88s on Infinity). The empirical characterization of cross-step attention consistency as a VAR-specific property (absent in DiT) opens a path for further attention-aware compression techniques in hierarchical generative models. Limitations include documented Branch Halving failure modes (fine attribute binding, rare-attribute drift, asymmetry collapse) and the method's dependence on VAR's multi-scale structure, leaving open whether attention-driven pruning generalizes to single-scale autoregressive or diffusion settings.
