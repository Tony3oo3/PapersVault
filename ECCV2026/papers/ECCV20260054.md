## Problem

Monocular metric depth estimation lags far behind relative depth because recovering physical scale from a single image is intrinsically ill-posed. Prior relative-to-metric approaches (e.g., MoGe-2) rely on a single global scale factor, which cannot account for spatially varying scale drift or residual ray-direction bias in point-map geometry. Additionally, focal-length distribution mismatch between training and test data causes sharp performance drops on camera-mismatched benchmarks, yet this data-side bottleneck has not been systematically addressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a metric 3D point map (per-pixel 3D coordinates in real-world units) from which metric depth and surface normals are derived. Internally, Stage 1 outputs an affine-invariant point map and a validity mask, while Stage 2 applies a pixel-wise scale field and a ray-direction correction field to convert the relative point map into metric geometry. The point-map representation (rather than a scalar depth map) is central to the formulation, as it enables explicit decomposition into range and ray-direction components for calibration.

## Method

FoundationGeo is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 trains a DINOv3-initialized ViT-Large encoder with a lightweight upsampling CNN decoder on a curated 10.2M-image multi-domain corpus, using multi-scale feature fusion (element-wise summation of encoder feature maps) and complementary losses (global alignment, multi-scale patch losses, surface-normal consistency, edge loss, mask loss) to produce a high-fidelity affine-invariant point map. Stage 2 introduces two lightweight spatial calibration fields: a pixel-wise scale field Ŝ ∈ ℝ^{H×W} that performs spatially varying metric rescaling (p̃ = Ŝ ⊙ p̂′), and a ray-direction correction field Δ that parameterizes bounded angular perturbations along an orthonormal tangent basis to fix directional bias while preserving range. Training uses a coupled metric L1 loss plus decoupled field-specific objectives (log-domain scale-field loss with closed-form least-squares targets, Huber angular consistency loss, and tanh-magnitude regularization) to prevent the fields from redundantly absorbing geometric prediction. A Blender-based data engine renders 23,700 additional images across diverse focal lengths to repair under-covered camera-intrinsic regimes, improving zero-shot robustness under intrinsic shift.

## Results

On zero-shot monocular metric depth estimation across seven benchmarks, FoundationGeo achieves an average AbsRel of 14.8% and δ₁ of 80.8%, yielding the best average rank (2.32) among all methods that do not require ground-truth camera intrinsics. This represents a 5.7% relative improvement in AbsRel and a 5.2% improvement in δ₁ over the closest prior method MoGe-2 (15.7% / 76.8%). The Stage-1 relative base model (FoundationGeo-Base) also achieves the best overall relative-depth performance with 4.87% AbsRel and 96.0% δ₁, and the 6k-token variant attains the best boundary F1 average rank (1.67) across iBims-1, HAMMER, and Sintel.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoundationGeo outperforms all intrinsics-free baselines on average metric depth: it beats MoGe-2 (15.7→14.8 AbsRel, 76.8→80.8 δ₁), UniDepth V2 (21.3→14.8 AbsRel), DepthPro (27.6→14.8), and DA V1/V2 (38.9→14.8) by wide margins. The key differentiator is cross-domain stability: UniDepth V1 collapses on ETH3D (56.9 AbsRel) and MoGe-2 degrades sharply on KITTI (18.1 AbsRel), whereas FoundationGeo remains competitive on every benchmark, achieving the best single-dataset result on HAMMER (22.4 AbsRel, 69.6 δ₁). Metric3D V2 reports a lower average AbsRel (18.3) but is excluded from ranking because it requires ground-truth camera intrinsics at inference. On relative depth, FoundationGeo-Base (4.87 AbsRel, 96.0 δ₁) edges out MoGe-2 (4.92 / 95.7) and UniDepth V2 (5.34 / 95.5), confirming that the upgraded DINOv3-initialized backbone with multi-scale feature fusion yields a stronger affine-invariant prior. On boundary sharpness, the 6k-token FoundationGeo variant (avg rank 1.67) surpasses DepthPro (2.00) despite DepthPro operating at a higher native resolution (1536×1536), with the largest gap on iBims-1 (15.7 vs 14.3 F1).

## Relation to Prior Work

FoundationGeo extends the relative-to-metric paradigm established by MoGe-2, which converts high-quality affine-invariant predictions into metric geometry via a single global scale factor. The key departure is replacing that global scale with two lightweight pixel-wise spatial fields—a scale field for spatially varying metric alignment and a ray-direction correction field for angular bias—motivated by empirical evidence that scale misalignment is inherently local and that directional error is orthogonal to scale. The relative backbone itself is upgraded from MoGe's CNN decoder design by initializing with DINOv3 and training on a larger, more diverse 10.2M-sample corpus with multi-scale feature fusion, while the data-side contribution (focal-length coverage analysis and Blender-based augmentation) addresses a bottleneck that prior relative-to-metric methods have not explicitly diagnosed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of focal-length distribution mismatch as a primary bottleneck for zero-shot metric generalization provides a concrete, actionable axis for data design that goes beyond indiscriminate dataset mixing, and the controlled Single-Focal vs. Diverse-Focal ablation validates this as a practical lever. The pixel-wise calibration framework is a natural and well-motivated upgrade over global scaling, and the two-stage recipe (strong relative prior → lightweight metric fields) offers a reusable template for future metric geometry work. Remaining open questions include whether the residual gap on heavily camera-mismatched benchmarks (e.g., DIODE, DDAD) can be closed without explicit intrinsic conditioning, and whether the framework extends to temporal or multi-view settings where cross-frame consistency could further reduce monocular ambiguity.
