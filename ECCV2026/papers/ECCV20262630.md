## Problem

Existing training-free 3D editing methods (e.g., FlowEdit, AnchorFlow) apply a uniform global scaling factor to the source–target velocity difference when steering VecSet-based generative models, causing semantic artifacts such as collapsed preserved regions, incomplete edits, and mesh tearing. The root cause is that these methods ignore the strong per-token spatial locality of VecSet latents: although tokens interact globally via self-attention, each token's geometric influence is concentrated on a specific local region, so a token-shared control signal perturbs editable and preserved areas simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source 3D object encoded as a VecSet latent (4096 tokens of dimension d in the Hunyuan3D 2.1 backbone), a source condition (text prompt or reference image), and a target text prompt describing the desired edit. It produces an edited 3D mesh decoded directly from the modified latent, plus a baked texture map generated through a multi-view diffusion pipeline (MV-Adapter) with inpainting for missing texels. Notably, the editing is mask-free: no explicit spatial segmentation or region annotation is required, as localization emerges entirely from the per-token directional discrepancy signal.

## Method

TanGO formulates each ODE step of the editing trajectory as an instantaneous one-step optimal control problem in the tangent space of the generative dynamics. It perturbs the source velocity with a per-token control input u_i(t) that maximizes first-order progress toward the target velocity direction Δv_i(t) while penalizing control energy quadratically, subject to a span constraint that restricts updates to the minimal-intervention direction; this yields the closed-form gain g_i*(t) = d_i(t)/ρ. The token-wise demand d_i(t) = 1 − cos θ_i(t) is derived from a von Mises-Fisher negative log-likelihood over normalized source and target velocities, making it scale-invariant, bounded in [0, 2], and informative of whether a token already follows the target-conditioned flow. A mean-gain normalization λ_eff(t) = λ·η/(ḡ(t)+ε) stabilizes the overall guidance energy across timesteps without altering the relative per-token steering pattern. A separate texture-baking stage renders multi-view images via MV-Adapter, constructs UV maps, inpaints missing texels, and bakes the result onto the edited mesh.

## Results

On the TanGOEdit benchmark (100 samples), TanGO achieves DINO-I 0.6510, CLIP-I 0.7679, and CLIP-T 0.2301, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, AnchorFlow, scores 0.6426 / 0.7492 / 0.2124, while FlowEdit trails further at 0.6127 / 0.7205 / 0.2081. In a user study, participants preferred TanGO over AnchorFlow at 79% (prompt alignment), 84% (visual quality), and 87% (shape preservation). Inference time is 28.73 s per edit, comparable to FlowEdit (28.13 s) and AnchorFlow (28.76 s), and far faster than MVEdit (632.45 s) and EditP23 (59.32 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TanGO outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest competitor AnchorFlow [36], TanGO gains +0.0084 DINO-I, +0.0187 CLIP-I, and +0.0177 CLIP-T, with the largest relative improvement in CLIP-T (≈8.3%), indicating notably better semantic alignment with the target prompt. FlowEdit [19] is the second-closest baseline but lags TanGO by a wider margin (−0.0383 DINO-I, −0.0474 CLIP-I, −0.0220 CLIP-T). MVEdit [7] and EditP23 [2] are substantially weaker, scoring below 0.60 on DINO-I and below 0.50 on CLIP-I, reflecting their multi-view inpainting-based pipelines that struggle with 3D coherence. On a separate 150-sample mask-aware 3D evaluation (Edit3DBench + TanGOEdit), TanGO additionally surpasses VoxHammer [21] and Nano3D [32] on Uni3D_text, CD_preserve, and NC_preserve metrics. Inference speed is essentially identical to FlowEdit and AnchorFlow (~28.7 s), confirming that the per-token optimal-control overhead is negligible.

## Relation to Prior Work

TanGO builds on the flow-matching 3D generation paradigm (Hunyuan3D 2.1, TripoSG) and the training-free editing framework established by FlowEdit and AnchorFlow, which steer latent trajectories via source-target velocity differences. Its key departure is replacing the globally shared scaling factor with a per-token optimal control law in tangent space, motivated by an empirical observation that VecSet tokens—despite global self-attention—exhibit strong spatial locality in their geometric influence. The vMF-inspired directional discrepancy for computing per-token demand is a novel mechanism not present in prior 3D editing work, and the TanGOEdit benchmark extends existing evaluation protocols with rigid and non-rigid editing tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that VecSet tokens are more spatially local than previously assumed opens a path toward mask-free, token-level control in set-based 3D representations, potentially generalizing beyond Hunyuan3D to other VecSet backbones. However, the quantitative gains over AnchorFlow are modest (e.g., CLIP-T 0.2301 vs. 0.2124), and the authors acknowledge that high-frequency geometric detail preservation is ultimately bounded by the underlying 3D VAE's reconstruction capacity. Open questions include whether the per-token steering principle transfers to non-VecSet representations (e.g., SLAT in TRELLIS) and whether more expressive latent spaces or hybrid pixel-3D refinement could overcome the VAE bottleneck.
