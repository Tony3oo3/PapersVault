## Problem

Transferring articulated motion from monocular videos to rigged 3D characters is hindered by the reconstruct-then-retarget paradigm, which propagates intermediate 3D reconstruction errors and restricts applicability to categories with parametric templates (e.g., SMPL, SMAL). When source and target differ substantially in morphology, these cascaded errors are further amplified, and no general framework exists for cross-category retargeting without category-specific priors or large-scale training data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video and a rigged 3D target mesh (with fixed kinematic tree and skinning weights; unrigged meshes are auto-rigged via UniRig or Pinocchio) and produces a temporally coherent pose trajectory of per-joint axis-angle rotations and root translations applied to the target rig. Camera intrinsics are fixed and no per-frame extrinsics are estimated; global source-target alignment is handled by a per-frame SE(3) root transform. Optimization runs in approximately 5 minutes on a single RTX 4090 for a 10K-vertex mesh over 5K iterations.

## Method

MorphGS formulates retargeting as a target-driven analysis-by-synthesis problem: it directly optimizes target morphology and pose so that a differentiable 3D Gaussian renderer reproduces the source video, bypassing any intermediate source 3D reconstruction. The key mechanism is a rig-coupled morphology parameterization that factorizes time-invariant shape (learnable bone lengths, a global scale, and per-vertex rest-space offsets anchored to skeleton-dependent convex-combination points) from time-varying joint rotations, preventing shape-pose entanglement. The target mesh is represented as articulated 3D Gaussians initialized from vertices, posed via forward kinematics and LBS, and rendered with 3D Gaussian splatting. Pose is parameterized by a single time-conditioned MLP (sinusoidal embedding → per-joint rotations + root translation) for temporal coherence. The optimization objective combines photometric/mask rendering losses, dense 2D-3D keypoint supervision from SHIC-style semantic correspondence, diffusion-based novel-view synthesis for occluded regions, and temporal smoothness regularization, applied in a staged schedule (global alignment → bone lengths and rotations → full parameters including Gaussian attributes).

## Results

On the Mixamo-Humanoids benchmark, MorphGS achieves PMD 1.91×10⁻³ and FVMD 8.82×10⁻³, outperforming the best baseline (SPT+) by 35% in PMD and 30% in FVMD. On DT4D-Quadrupeds, MorphGS reaches PMD 1.33×10⁻³ and FVMD 8.81×10⁻³, roughly 2.5× lower PMD than the next-best method (NPR+ at 3.28). On the more challenging DT4D-Others split (non-standard topologies like birds and whales), MorphGS achieves PMD 1.95×10⁻³ and FVMD 12.83×10⁻³, again substantially ahead of Transfer4D (7.16, 17.52) and Pinocchio+ (7.59, 14.23). On real-world DAVIS videos, MorphGS attains average FVMD of 12.74 (human) and 12.49 (animal), beating the strongest baselines by 2–9 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MorphGS outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric across all three synthetic splits. Against SPT+ (Liao et al., ECCV 2022), MorphGS reduces PMD from 2.96 to 1.91 (−35%) and FVMD from 12.56 to 8.82 (−30%) on Mixamo; SPT+ is only evaluated on humanoids due to lack of pretrained weights for non-human shapes. Against NPR+ (Wang et al., CVPR 2020), MorphGS achieves roughly 2–3× lower PMD (e.g., 1.33 vs 3.28 on DT4D-Quadrupeds) and ~45% lower FVMD, with the gap widening on real-world animal videos (12.49 vs 19.00 average FVMD). Against Transfer4D (Maheshwari et al., CVPR 2023), which requires RGB-D input, MorphGS cuts PMD by 3–4× (1.91 vs 7.65 on Mixamo; 1.95 vs 7.16 on DT4D-Others) and FVMD by 30–45%. Against Pinocchio+ (Baran et al., ACM TOG 2007), MorphGS improves PMD by 2–4× and FVMD by 20–30%; notably, on DT4D-Others the Pinocchio+ baseline is given ground-truth source mesh sequences to avoid error propagation, yet MorphGS still wins by a wide margin, underscoring the advantage of the target-driven formulation over the reconstruct-then-retarget paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

MorphGS departs from the dominant reconstruct-then-retarget paradigm (SPT, NPR, Pinocchio, Transfer4D) by reframing motion retargeting as a target-driven analysis-by-synthesis problem, directly optimizing the target's morphology and pose through differentiable 3D Gaussian rendering rather than first recovering an intermediate 3D source representation. It builds on SHIC for dense 2D-3D semantic correspondences and on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the differentiable renderer, but the key conceptual contribution is the rig-coupled morphology parameterization (bone lengths, global scale, local offsets) that factorizes time-invariant shape from time-varying joint rotations, preventing the shape-pose entanglement that plagues unconstrained optimization. This positions the work at the intersection of inverse graphics, articulated 3D modeling, and Gaussian-based rendering, without requiring parametric templates (SMPL/SMAL) or category-specific training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the intermediate 3D source reconstruction, MorphGS avoids the error propagation and category restrictions that limit existing pipelines, enabling motion transfer to targets without parametric templates or pretrained pose manifolds (e.g., birds, whales, robots). The practical cost is modest (~5 min on a single RTX 4090), making it accessible for content creation. However, the framework assumes compatible kinematic topology between source and target, limiting transfer across fundamentally different joint hierarchies. Open questions include whether physics-based priors or explicit soft-tissue deformation modeling could resolve ambiguities under severe self-occlusion and extend the approach to non-rigid or topologically dissimilar characters.
