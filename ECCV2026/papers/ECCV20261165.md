## Problem

Existing text-to-vector sketch generation methods produce the entire sketch in one shot, offering no part-level control, interpretability, or localized editing. The one prior part-by-part method (SketchAgent) relies on a closed-source VLM in zero-shot mode, yielding simplistic icon-style outputs that cannot be adapted to higher fidelity or specific domains. The paper identifies the lack of scalable part-annotated training data and the absence of a training strategy that provides dense visual feedback across intermediate generation steps as the two key gaps preventing free-text guided, progressive vector sketch synthesis.

## Input / Output

At each generation turn, the agent receives the rasterized rendering of the current canvas, a global object caption, the text description of the next part to draw, descriptions and vector paths of all previously drawn parts, and the count of remaining parts. The output is a sequence of cubic Bezier curve paths encoded as minimal SVG commands (M and C with eight integer coordinates each), separated by newlines, with all shared SVG attributes (stroke width, opacity) fixed externally. Coordinates are rounded to the nearest ten during SFT but kept as original integers during RL to preserve precision.

## Method

The method trains a Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B backbone (via LoRA rank 64) in two stages. Stage 1 (SFT) uses cross-entropy loss on input/output pairs augmented with up to 20 random part permutations per sketch, teaching the model valid path format and single-turn sketching conditioned on an oracle partial canvas. Stage 2 introduces a novel multi-turn process-reward GRPO algorithm: instead of computing a single terminal reward on the final rendering, the agent receives dense per-step rewards at every intermediate canvas state, using DreamSim cosine similarity between the rendered partial sketch and the ground-truth partial rendering plus a path-count regularizer to prevent trajectory length inflation. Rewards are normalized within each step across the group (rather than globally), and advantages are set equal to the per-step normalized reward, enabling precise credit assignment to individual parts. The training data is ControlSketch-Part, a 35,000-sketch dataset enriched with an automated VLM-based annotation pipeline (Gemini 3.0 Pro) that performs multi-stage part decomposition, critique, refinement, and path-to-part assignment with diagnostic color-coded visualizations.

## Results

The full model (SFT + multi-turn process-reward GRPO) achieves the highest Long-CLIP cosine similarity among all tested methods, with the ablation on Qwen2.5-VL-3B confirming the training strategy: multi-turn process-reward GRPO scores 0.298 vs. 0.286 for multi-turn outcome-reward and 0.281 for single-turn RL. In double-blind user studies comprising 3,092 pairwise comparisons (426 per baseline for step quality from 142 participants; 560 per baseline for final output quality from 146 participants), the method was consistently preferred over every baseline in both evaluation settings. The SFT-only variant is outperformed by the full SFT+RL model, validating the contribution of the RL stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SketchAgent (Claude Sonnet 4.5, zero-shot sequential sketching), the proposed method wins on both Long-CLIP faithfulness and user preference for both final output quality and step-by-step generation quality; SketchAgent produces clean part structure but favors simple geometric primitives and occasionally generates misplaced or distorted components. Against Gemini 3.1 Pro (used as a one-shot whole-sketch generator), the method wins on Long-CLIP and user preference; Gemini 3.1 Pro shares a preference for simple geometries and symmetric layouts and occasionally fails to produce a complete object. Against the SDXL + SwiftSketch pipeline (text-to-image followed by image-to-sketch), the method wins decisively on all metrics; this pipeline trails all other baselines because errors from the text-to-image stage compound in the subsequent sketch conversion, and SwiftSketch degrades when the intermediate image lacks a clear foreground subject. Overall, the paper's method outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric (Long-CLIP cosine similarity and both user-study preference questions).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-vector sketch generation task from one-shot or holistic stroke generation (Sketch-RNN, BezierSketch, ChiroDiff, CLIPDraw, DiffSketcher) to a progressive, part-by-part formulation, building most directly on SketchAgent's sequential VLM-based sketching while replacing its closed-source zero-shot prompting with a trained open-weight VLM agent. The training methodology adapts GRPO from DeepSeekMath to a multi-turn setting with intermediate-state (process) rewards rather than terminal-only rewards, drawing on the dense credit assignment idea from rendering-aware RL for vector graphics. The data pipeline repurposes the ControlSketch dataset by adding a generic, VLM-driven multi-stage annotation process (propose → critique → refine) for part decomposition and path assignment, filling a gap the authors identify as the key bottleneck for part-aware sketching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ControlSketch-Part dataset and the automated annotation pipeline are immediately reusable resources for anyone working on structured vector graphics generation, and the multi-turn process-reward GRPO formulation is a clean, generalizable training recipe for any iterative visual generation task where intermediate states are observable. However, the agent is constrained to the 15 object categories in ControlSketch and struggles with out-of-domain parts or unseen categories, and editing is limited to part-level (not stroke-level) operations. Open questions include whether the process-reward advantage generalizes to more complex multi-object scenes, whether the annotation pipeline scales to truly open-vocabulary sketches, and whether the VLM's inherent difficulty with precise numerical coordinates can be mitigated without sacrificing the end-to-end generative formulation.
