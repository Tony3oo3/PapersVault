## Problem

Existing dynamic reconstruction methods (NeRF-based voxels, Gaussian-based ellipsoids) use primitives designed for static appearance rendering, which are center-driven, rely on predefined particle-primitive bindings, and cannot capture non-uniform deformation or sharp evolving boundaries. This mismatch between rendering and simulation limits physically meaningful dynamic reconstruction and generalization to new material or force conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261469/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes posed multi-view video (12 views at 448×448 for synthetic GSO scenes, 3 views at 1920×1080 for real-world captures) plus object masks (from SAM), using the first 16 frames as observations. It outputs a dynamic 4D convex field of N polytopes (each with K=6 points and time-invariant color/opacity/smoothness/sharpness parameters), recovered physical parameters (Young's modulus E, Poisson's ratio ν), and can render novel views or roll forward to predict 8 future frames. Training completes in ~6 minutes on a single RTX A6000.

## Method

PhysConvex represents each dynamic object as material-space deformable convex primitives whose boundaries (vertices and half-space support planes) are advected by physics rather than moving only the primitive center. A mesh-free reduced-order simulator uses an MLP-based neural skinning field to define M=10 physics-informed deformation eigenmodes directly over convex supports, with time-varying handle transformations as reduced DOFs integrated via implicit Newton-based time stepping. Training is two-stage: first, the undeformed convex field is reconstructed from the initial multi-view frame via differentiable convex splatting; second, physical parameters (E, ν) and skinning weights are jointly optimized end-to-end through differentiable simulation and rendering against single-view video. The same convex primitives simultaneously serve as rendering elements (via occupancy-based α-blending splatting), deformation carriers, and physical supports where mass, elastic energy, and contacts are evaluated, eliminating the need for separate simulation particles or background grids.

## Results

On the Google Scanned Objects benchmark (12 objects), PhysConvex achieves a mean PSNR of 30.00 dB, SSIM of 0.962, and FoVVDP of 8.247 for dynamic reconstruction, outperforming all baselines. For physical system identification, it attains the lowest mean MAE of 0.37 on log(E) and 0.04 on Poisson's ratio ν. Training completes in ~6 minutes per scene on a single RTX A6000 with 23,681 convex primitives, faster than Vid2Sim (8 min) and Spring-GS (32 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysConvex outperforms all four baselines on dynamic reconstruction (PSNR, SSIM, FoVVDP) and physical system identification (log(E), ν). Against the closest competitor Vid2Sim (w/o LGM), PhysConvex gains +1.43 dB PSNR (30.00 vs 28.57), +0.010 SSIM, and reduces log(E) MAE from 0.51 to 0.37 and ν MAE from 0.06 to 0.04, while training 2 minutes faster. Against PAC-NeRF, the improvement is substantial: +7.94 dB PSNR and log(E) MAE reduced from 2.50 to 0.37. Against GIC and Spring-Gaus, PhysConvex leads by ~9 dB PSNR and ~1.7–1.8 FoVVDP points. The only metric where PhysConvex trails Vid2Sim is FoVVDP on future-state prediction (7.683 vs 7.770), a marginal 0.087-point difference, while it still leads on PSNR (25.71 vs 25.07) and SSIM (0.948 vs 0.945) for that task.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysConvex extends the static 3D Convex Splatting (3DCS) representation to the dynamic, physics-driven regime, and integrates it with the model-order-reduction (MOR) simulation lineage (Simplicits, CROM, LICROM) that uses neural skinning eigenmodes as mesh-free deformation bases. It departs from the dominant physics-integrated NeRF/Gaussian frameworks (PAC-NeRF, Spring-GS, GIC, Vid2Sim) by rejecting the separation between rendering primitives and simulation particles: instead of center-driven Gaussians or voxels coupled to external MPM grids or spring-mass systems, each convex primitive's boundary is itself the material support where mass, stress, and contacts are evaluated. The boundary-driven vertex/surface advection replaces the center-driven kinematics that prior hybrid Eulerian-Lagrangian methods rely on, giving spatially non-uniform deformation without predefined particle-primitive bindings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of rendering, deformation, and physical simulation into a single convex primitive offers a cleaner design principle for physics-informed reconstruction and may reduce the representational mismatch that plagues current Gaussian/NeRF + MPM pipelines. The practical efficiency gain (≈6 min training, fewer primitives than Gaussian baselines) makes the approach more accessible for iterative or real-time applications. However, the method still requires posed multi-view video, object masks, and constrained physical settings (gravity, simple contacts), and the evaluation is limited to 12 synthetic GSO objects plus a small real-world set; open questions include scalability to multi-object scenes, complex contact interactions, and whether the convex hull assumption holds for highly concave or topologically changing deformations.
