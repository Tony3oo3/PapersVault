## Problem

Existing forensic detectors target narrow forgery families and output only binary labels or heatmaps without explanation, while vision-language models can produce natural-language justifications but rely on visible semantic cues and cannot exploit invisible forensic microstructures (sensor noise, compression artifacts, demosaicing residuals). No single system simultaneously detects a broad spectrum of forgery types, localizes manipulations, and grounds every claim in verifiable forensic evidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260385/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single RGB image as input, divides it into patches, and produces a Forensic Knowledge Graph (FKG) — a typed graph of subject-predicate-object triplets linking Region nodes to Source, Post-Processing, Compression, and Content entities. The FKG is then serialized with Set-of-Mark visual overlays (numbered region markers on the image) and fed to a VLM, which outputs an authenticity verdict, forgery type label, localization mask, and a forensic justification text that references specific graph evidence (e.g., camera model discrepancies, compression history).

## Method

The framework comprises three sequentially trained stages. First, a self-supervised Forensic Backbone Network learns general-purpose forensic embeddings from high-pass-filtered patches using a combined pairwise same-image alignment and contrastive loss, requiring no forgery or source labels. Second, a Forensic Region Proposal Network (FRPN) partitions the image into forensically homogeneous regions via a Hybrid Graph Attention Transformer that alternates global self-attention (capturing long-range forensic similarity) with local k-nearest-neighbor graph attention (suppressing attention dilution), trained with Hungarian matching plus a pairwise similarity loss. Third, Task Expert MLPs predict per-region source, post-processing, and compression attributes, refined by a Transformer Reasoning Module that models cross-task dependencies; these predictions instantiate the FKG ontology deterministically. A VLM then generates human-interpretable explanations from the FKG using Iterative Context Refinement (ICR), which iteratively selects in-context demonstrations targeting the VLM's specific fabrication and omission patterns to maximize report faithfulness without fine-tuning.

## Results

On FKG-50K, FKG achieves 0.92 accuracy and 0.94 AUC overall for forgery detection, outperforming the best VLM (GPT-5: 0.66/0.72) and the best forensic detector (TruFor: 0.70/0.81). Per-category AUC on FKG-50K ranges from 0.88 (AI-edits) to 0.99 (splicing). On out-of-distribution datasets, FKG remains competitive (DSO-1: 0.96 AUC, Synthbuster: 0.96 AUC) while being the only method that generalizes across all four forgery types simultaneously. For forgery type classification, FKG reaches 0.87 overall accuracy versus 0.31 for the best VLM (Qwen3 VL) without oracle access; for localization, FKG achieves 0.94 F1 versus 0.39 for the best VLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FKG decisively outperforms all seven VLM baselines (GPT-5, Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.0, Qwen3 VL, Gemma 3, Llama 4 Scout, Nemotron Nano) on FKG-50K overall detection (0.92 vs. 0.53–0.66 ACC), with the gap widening on splicing and traditional edits where VLMs score near chance (0.50–0.55). VLMs do match or slightly exceed FKG on fully AI-generated OOD datasets (e.g., GPT-5: 0.93 ACC on GenImage vs. FKG's 0.92; NPR: 0.98 AUC on Synthbuster vs. FKG's 0.96), but they collapse on localized manipulations. Among forensic detectors, each specialized system wins on its home domain—CAT-Net achieves 1.00 AUC on DSO-1 (splicing) and MVSS-Net reaches 0.97 AUC on CASIAv2—yet both drop to 0.33–0.49 AUC on AI-generated content, while AI-generation detectors (NPR, UFD, DE-FAKE) show the inverse pattern (0.97–0.98 AUC on Synthbuster but 0.49–0.52 on splicing). FKG is the only method that maintains ≥0.85 AUC across all four forgery categories on FKG-50K and ≥0.85 AUC on all four OOD datasets, confirming its general-purpose advantage. On forgery type classification (Table 2), FKG's 0.87 overall accuracy exceeds the best oracle-assisted VLM (Sonnet 4: 0.48) by 39 points, and on localization (Table 3), FKG's 0.94 F1 surpasses the best forensic system (TruFor: 0.46 overall) and best VLM (Qwen3 VL: 0.39 without oracle) by large margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—specialized forensic detectors (CAT-Net, TruFor, MVSS-Net for splicing; NPR, UFD, DE-FAKE for AI-generated content) and VLM-based explainers (ForgeryGPT, SIDA, FakeShield)—into a single framework that outputs structured knowledge graphs rather than binary labels or heatmaps. Conceptually, it extends the intrinsic fingerprint paradigm of Stamm & Liu and Bayar & Stamm by learning forensic embeddings via self-supervised contrastive objectives rather than task-specific supervision, and introduces a hybrid graph-attention transformer for region proposal that has no direct predecessor in the forensic literature. The Iterative Context Refinement strategy repurposes in-context learning and judge-LM evaluation from the prompt-optimization literature but redirects the optimization target from answer correctness to faithfulness against a structured evidence graph, a distinction the authors argue prior prompt-tuning methods do not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FKG-50K dataset (50K images with full provenance, manipulation lineage, and ground-truth knowledge graphs) fills a critical gap: no prior forensic dataset captures the structured multi-attribute provenance needed to evaluate systems that reason over source identity, compression history, and modification lineage simultaneously. The consistent finding across eight VLMs—that even oracle-assisted models achieve only 0.16–0.24 correctness on forensic justification—challenges the growing assumption that VLMs can serve as forensic explainers and suggests that invisible statistical traces remain inaccessible to current multimodal architectures. Open questions include whether the self-supervised backbone generalizes to novel generators not seen during training, how the framework performs under adversarial perturbations (cropping, rescaling, re-compression), and whether the knowledge-graph representation can be extended to video or multi-frame authentication.
