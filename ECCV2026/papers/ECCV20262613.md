## Problem

Reliable deployment of human motion forecasters requires uncertainty estimates that are simultaneously structured (preserving spatio-temporal and skeletal correlations), calibrated (with finite-sample coverage guarantees), and efficient (single-pass inference). Existing Bayesian and ensemble methods require repeated stochastic forward passes, while conformal calibration alone neither separates epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty nor preserves trajectory covariance structure, leaving a gap for a unified, single-pass framework that delivers all three properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262613/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a T-frame 3D pose prefix (e.g., T=50) and a joint graph (kinematic tree or kNN graph), operating in displacement space relative to the last observed pose x_T. It produces an H-frame future trajectory (e.g., H=25), a per-horizon epistemic scale κ_t(x), a full structured Gaussian covariance (Kronecker product of temporal and spatial factors), and axis-aligned 95% marginal prediction tubes (one interval per joint per coordinate per horizon step). The tubes are marginal rather than joint ellipsoids, and the covariance is parameterized as a separable matrix-normal with a graph-Laplacian precision for skeletal coupling.

## Method

SPARC places a conjugate matrix-normal Bayesian model on the final linear layer of a deterministic siMLPe/DCT backbone, yielding an analytic per-horizon epistemic scale κ_t(x) = 1 + φ_t(x)ᵀΛ_{n,t}^{-1}φ_t(x) computed via a single quadratic form (no MC sampling). Proposition 1 proves that integrating out the last-layer weights inflates the predictive covariance exactly as κ_t(x)·Σ_str,t(x), preserving the full correlation structure of the structured aleatoric head (MN-GraphJ: Cholesky-parameterized temporal covariance ⊗ graph-Laplacian spatial precision). A time-coupled shrinkage stabilizer blends horizon-wise κ_t with a pooled global scale, and an optional per-joint leverage factor further modulates conformal tube widths. Split conformal calibration on a held-out set converts standardized residuals into per-joint quantiles q_{t,j}, producing 95% marginal prediction tubes with finite-sample validity under exchangeability. The entire pipeline requires one forward pass at inference plus closed-form quadratic forms, with all Bayesian and conformal statistics fitted offline.

## Results

Across nine dataset/protocol blocks (Human3.6M, AMASS, LaFAN1, CMU-MoCap, 3DPW, CHICO, HA4M, AnDy), SPARC achieves the best mean rank on NLL (MR_NLL = 1.00) and on the combined MPJPE+NLL criterion (MR = 2.69), while retaining competitive point accuracy (MR_MPJPE = 4.39) and the most efficient calibrated tubes (MR_W95(CP) = 2.50). The only method that beats SPARC on raw MPJPE rank is the deterministic MeanFT fine-tune (MR_MPJPE = 2.22), but MeanFT provides no calibrated uncertainty. SPARC's advantage is most pronounced on density quality: it ranks first on NLL on every dataset/protocol block, with the next-best probabilistic method (Bridge-CQR) at MR_NLL = 3.00.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPARC dominates on density quality (NLL) and calibrated tube efficiency (W95(CP)) against all 16 baselines, with the closest competitors being Bridge-CQR (MR_NLL = 3.00, MR_W95(CP) = 9.11) and CoMusion (MR_W95(CP) = 3.78). On the combined MPJPE+NLL criterion, SPARC (2.69) beats Bridge-CQR (3.81) and CoMusion (6.25), the two strongest probabilistic baselines. The only baseline that outperforms SPARC on raw MPJPE rank is the deterministic MeanFT fine-tune (2.22 vs. 4.39), but MeanFT outputs no uncertainty estimates and thus cannot be compared on NLL or tube metrics. Among sampling/generative baselines, deep ensembles (MR_MPJPE+NLL = 6.89) and CoMusion (6.25) are the closest, yet both require multiple stochastic forward passes or diffusion sampling, whereas SPARC operates in a single deterministic pass. Diffusion-based methods (SkeletonDiffusion, BeLFusion, TransFusion, MotionMap, SLD-HMP) are consistently outperformed on all four metrics, with gaps of 3–13 rank positions on NLL. The ablation (Fig. 2) further shows that the hybrid MN-GraphJ structured covariance contributes the largest within-family gain (MR_MPJPE+NLL drops from 5.28 to 2.47), while time-coupled κ_t shrinkage trades a small NLL rank cost for the best conformal tube-width rank (2.56 vs. 4.78).

## Relation to Prior Work

SPARC sits at the intersection of three established lines: (1) the siMLPe/DCT-bridge deterministic MLP backbone for motion prediction [18], (2) conjugate Bayesian last-layer methods that yield closed-form predictive uncertainty at single-pass cost [12, 33, 41, 47, 51], and (3) split conformal prediction for distribution-free finite-sample calibration [1, 29, 50]. The key departure from prior neural-linear work is that the Bayesian last layer is paired with a structured (matrix-normal + graph-Laplacian) aleatoric covariance rather than the diagonal or isotropic likelihoods typical in regression settings, so the analytic epistemic scale κ_t(x) inflates a full spatio-temporal covariance while preserving its correlation structure (Prop. 1). This contrasts with MC dropout/ensembles [15, 26] that require repeated stochastic passes, and with conformal-only calibration [42] that does not separate epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty or encode skeletal/temporal dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For human-robot interaction and safety-critical deployment, SPARC provides a practical single-pass uncertainty layer that yields calibrated 95% marginal prediction tubes with finite-sample validity under exchangeability, plus an interpretable κ risk score that monotonically stratifies prediction error (highest-κ decile shows 1.79× average MPJPE). The main limitations are that conformal guarantees are marginal rather than conditional, the κ signal is representation-dependent and not a universal OOD detector, and exchangeability violations under distribution shift weaken the coverage guarantee. Open questions include whether conditional conformal guarantees can be achieved for trajectory outputs, whether richer (non-separable) coordinate dependencies improve NLL at acceptable compute cost, and whether the κ-based abstention strategy generalizes across fundamentally different capture modalities.
