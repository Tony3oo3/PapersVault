## Problem

Real-world low-light face images suffer compound degradations (low illumination, severe noise, blur) that existing cascaded pipelines handle poorly due to error accumulation, while generic joint restoration models lack explicit facial priors and fail to recover fine-grained identity features under complex real-world conditions. No prior method simultaneously enforces physically plausible photometry and high-frequency facial structure without requiring paired training data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded low-light face image at 256×256 resolution as input and produces a normal-light, high-fidelity face image at the same resolution. It is entirely training-free, operating within a pre-trained unconditional DDPM (ImageNet-trained, 256×256) with T=10 denoising timesteps, and additionally consumes two off-the-shelf priors at inference: a pre-trained Retinex decomposition network for photometric constraints and a blind face restoration network (OSDFace) for structural guidance. No paired training data or fine-tuning is required.

## Method

PASDiff reformulates joint low-light enhancement and face restoration as a multi-objective energy-guided diffusion sampling process. At each reverse-diffusion timestep, it estimates the clean image x̂₀ and applies two orthogonal guidance signals: (1) Physics-Aware Photometric Constraints combining a spatially-varying exposure loss (derived from inverse intensity weighting in HSI space) and a Retinex-based reflectance loss (using a pre-trained Retinex network as a chromatic anchor), and (2) a Style-Agnostic Structural Injection (SASI) that uses AdaIN to align the first- and second-order statistics of an off-the-shelf face restoration prior with the current diffusion state, thereby distilling high-frequency structural gradients while statistically filtering out the prior's intrinsic lighting and color biases. The aggregated gradient from all three losses (exposure, reflectance, structural) shifts the diffusion mean at each step, steering the trajectory toward a manifold that is simultaneously photometrically natural and structurally faithful to the input identity.

## Results

On the synthetic FFHQ benchmark, PASDiff achieves a PSNR of 22.59 dB, substantially outperforming the best joint baseline FDN (18.56 dB) and the best cascaded pipeline LightenDiffusion→DiffBIR (18.20 dB), while also attaining the lowest LPIPS (0.2559) and LMD (1.6213). On the real-world WildDark-Face benchmark, PASDiff scores 53.18 MUSIQ, 0.3658 MANIQA, and 0.7057 HyperIQA, leading all baselines on perceptual quality metrics. For face recognition accuracy using InsightFace, PASDiff reaches 71.43%, exceeding the second-best method (LightenDiffusion→DiffBIR at 63.96%) by over 8 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASDiff outperforms all nine baselines on nearly every metric. Against the strongest cascaded pipeline (LightenDiffusion→DiffBIR), PASDiff gains +4.39 dB PSNR, reduces LPIPS from 0.3063 to 0.2559, and improves face recognition accuracy from 63.96% to 71.43%; however, the cascaded method achieves a slightly lower FID (121.47 vs. 127.04) on WildDark-Face, suggesting marginally better distributional realism in that single metric. Against the best joint general-restoration method FDN, PASDiff leads by +4.03 dB PSNR, −0.187 LPIPS, and +36.94 MUSIQ, with FDN's Fourier-decoupling approach failing to recover high-frequency facial structure (LMD 2.3465 vs. 1.6213). The other joint methods (DarkIR, LEDNet, LIEDNet, VQCNIR, URWKV) are consistently 3–9 dB lower in PSNR and 2–3× worse on no-reference quality scores, confirming that discriminative architectures struggle with the compound low-light + blur + noise degradation. The reverse-order cascade (DiffBIR→LightenDiffusion) performs worst overall (PSNR 16.62, face recognition 49.68%), illustrating the error-accumulation problem the paper targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

PASDiff sits at the intersection of diffusion-based inverse problem solving (DPS, DDRM, FreeDoM) and Retinex-based low-light enhancement, but departs from both by formulating a multi-objective energy that simultaneously enforces photometric constraints (spatially-varying exposure via inverse intensity weighting from CUDI, and Retinex reflectance anchoring) and structural fidelity (via an AdaIN-based style-agnostic injection from an off-the-shelf face restorer). Unlike prior diffusion guidance methods that handle a single degradation type, and unlike cascaded LLIE→BFR pipelines that accumulate errors, PASDiff treats the joint task as a single constrained generative process in a training-free manner, requiring no paired data or fine-tuning of the diffusion backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free formulation is practically significant: it avoids the 'dual burden' of simultaneously learning photometric correction and structural hallucination from paired data, which the authors show leads to identity loss and color drift in end-to-end training. The WildDark-Face benchmark (700 real-world images) fills a gap in evaluation for compound low-light face degradations. Open questions remain around inference speed (iterative diffusion sampling vs. feed-forward networks) and the fundamental ill-posedness of chromaticity recovery in near-pitch-black regions where color information is irreversibly destroyed. The AdaIN-based decoupling of structural semantics from photometric style is a reusable technique that could transfer to other compound-degradation restoration settings.
