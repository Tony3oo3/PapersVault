## Problem

Reconstructing 3D scenes of objects behind glass enclosures (e.g., in museums) is severely degraded by high specular reflections (HSR) that violate the per-point appearance consistency assumption underlying 3D Gaussian Splatting. Existing approaches either remove reflections in 2D without multi-view geometric reasoning or treat them as dynamic distractors, both of which fail under strong or view-dependent reflections. The paper identifies two previously uncharacterized properties of HSR in 3DGS—geometry-inconsistency and concept-preservation—that motivate a joint geometric and semantic solution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (80 per scene in GlassFlash3D) with known camera poses, and internally derives depth from the evolving 3DGS scene to project pixels into 3D for cross-view tracking. The ControlNet sub-module takes a 4-channel conditional input (3 RGB channels of the rendered image + 1 geometry-consistency channel) at each diffusion step. The final output is a reflection-free 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, per-view geometry-consistency maps, and de-reflected rendered images, with convergence determined by the mean consistency exceeding a threshold τ.

## Method

GlassGS alternates two stages in a self-cycling refinement loop. Stage 1 (Geometry-Consistency Perception) projects each pixel into 3D via the current 3DGS depth, tracks its color across adjacent viewpoints, and computes a per-pixel consistency score (Eq. 1); this map down-weights reflection-contaminated regions in both the L1 and D-SSIM losses (Eq. 2) during 3DGS optimization. Stage 2 (Reflection-Free Concept Learning) first learns a reflection-free scene embedding via textual inversion on the contaminated patches, then feeds a 4-channel condition (rendered image + consistency map) into a ControlNet-augmented Stable Diffusion U-Net to synthesize de-reflected images. The ControlNet is pre-trained on 100K synthetic pairs from GlassFlash2D, where a 2D Gaussian blur approximates the 3D neighborhood sampling of the consistency computation (Eq. 5). Each iteration re-renders the updated 3DGS, recomputes consistency maps, and concatenates original and de-reflected observations for the next 3DGS optimization (Eq. 12), progressively suppressing residual reflections until consistency stabilizes.

## Results

On the GlassFlash3D dataset, GlassGS achieves the best SSIM (0.68), MAE (31.99), Mask PSNR (18.94), Mask SSIM (0.71), and Mask MAE (24.68) among all methods, while tying the best PSNR (15.90) with DSRNet+3DGS and SpotLessSplats. The most striking gain is in Mask MAE, where GlassGS reduces the error from 30.82 (SpotLessSplats) to 24.68, a ~20% improvement, indicating substantially better object-region reconstruction. On the NeuS-HSR real-world dataset (qualitative only, no ground truth), GlassGS achieves the best suppression of specular highlights compared to all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GlassGS outperforms all four baselines on every metric except PSNR, where it ties (15.90) with DSRNet+3DGS and SpotLessSplats. Against Vanilla 3DGS, GlassGS improves SSIM by 0.06, reduces MAE by 4.06, and cuts Mask MAE by 7.91, confirming that unmodified 3DGS is highly sensitive to specular reflections. The 2D-removal pipelines (DSRNet+3DGS and YTMT+3DGS) offer modest gains over vanilla 3DGS but remain well below GlassGS on object-centric metrics (Mask MAE: 32.67/33.21 vs. 24.68), demonstrating that single-image reflection removal without multi-view geometric reasoning is insufficient for 3D reconstruction. SpotLessSplats, which treats reflections as dynamic distractors, is the strongest baseline (Mask MAE 30.82) yet still trails GlassGS by 6.14 in Mask MAE and 1.19 in Mask PSNR, because reflections differ fundamentally from moving objects in their transparency and view-dependent texture alteration. On the NeuS-HSR real-world scenes (qualitative), GlassGS achieves the cleanest specular-spot suppression, while SpotLessSplats leaves residual reflections and introduces additional noise due to its lack of explicit cross-view consistency enforcement.

## Relation to Prior Work

GlassGS builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework [15] but departs from prior reflection-aware 3DGS methods (NeRFReN, NeuS-HSR, Flash-Splat, RefGaussian) that model reflections through physical decomposition or deferred shading in 3D space. Instead, it introduces a geometry-consistency map to flag reflection-contaminated pixels across views and couples this with a diffusion-based (ControlNet + Stable Diffusion) reflection-free image generator, iterating between 3D refinement and 2D de-reflection in a self-cycling loop. This positions the work at the intersection of 3DGS optimization, multi-view geometric reasoning, and generative diffusion priors, distinguishing it from both 2D-only reflection removal (DSRNet, YTMT) and dynamic-distractor suppression (SpotLessSplats) that lack explicit cross-view consistency enforcement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first dedicated dataset (GlassFlash3D) for 3DGS reconstruction of glass-enclosed objects, addressing a practically important gap in cultural heritage digitization and virtual museum applications. However, the method's reliance on synthetically generated reflection data (GlassFlash2D) creates a domain gap that limits generalization to out-of-distribution real-world reflections, as acknowledged in the NeuS-HSR qualitative results. Open questions include whether the geometry-consistency map can be made more precise to reduce false detections, whether the iterative loop converges reliably under extreme or dynamic lighting, and whether the diffusion-based de-reflection can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives for real-time deployment.
