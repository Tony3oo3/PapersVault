## Problem

Existing active test-time adaptation (ATTA) methods for vision-language models treat sparse human annotations as local corrections rather than structured updates to the shared cross-modal decision space. This is particularly limiting for VLMs whose visual and textual semantics drift jointly under distribution shift, leaving no principled mechanism to consolidate delayed, sparse feedback into stable evolution of class boundaries in both modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262237/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images arriving as streaming mini-batches (size 128) and, per cycle, queries a small set of samples (budget κ=16 each for central and boundary subsets) for binary yes/no verification from an oracle. During inference, 63 augmented views per test sample are generated and aggregated. The output is a per-image class label prediction plus an updated prototype state: per-class visual prototypes (normalized averages of a small cache of discriminative features, capacity H=3) and per-class textual prototypes (base CLIP text embeddings plus learned residual vectors).

## Method

PAA treats class-wise visual and textual prototypes as an evolving state and updates it through a three-step cycle. The Probe step scores each sample against current visual prototypes to select central samples (high cosine similarity, reinforcing core semantics) and boundary samples (large nearest-prototype distance, exposing shifting distributions), with per-class budgets adaptively split by combining a global stream distribution and the current mini-batch distribution. The Anchor step converts binary verification into a prototype-regularized update combining four losses: binary cross-entropy for confidence calibration, an Info-NCE cross-modal alignment loss between visual and textual prototypes, an intra-class compactness term pulling verified positives toward their visual prototype, and an inter-class separation term constraining textual residual vectors to move along discriminative directions. The Amend step maintains a memory bank of previously mispredicted samples and, in later cycles, detects temporal discrepancies (a wrong prediction flipping to a different label with lower entropy) and distills them back into the prototypes as a slow, delayed correction signal. Only LayerNorm parameters in the frozen visual encoder and per-class textual residual vectors are updated, keeping the adaptation lightweight.

## Results

On the Natural Distribution Shifts benchmark with ViT-B/16, PAA achieves 68.19% average top-1 accuracy (67.23% OOD average), surpassing the strongest non-active TTA baseline BoostAdapter (66.33%) by 1.86% and the best ATTA baseline EATTA (65.17%) by 3.02%. Gains are most pronounced under severe shifts: PAA reaches 66.59% on ImageNet-A (+1.76% over BoostAdapter) and 82.93% on ImageNet-R (+1.98% over BoostAdapter). On the Cross-Domain benchmark, PAA achieves 73.60% average accuracy with ViT-B/16, outperforming all 12 baselines by 2.11%–10.02%. Efficiency-wise, PAA completes ImageNet-A adaptation in 17 minutes on a single RTX 3090, comparable to TDA (11 min) and BoostAdapter (17 min) while delivering the highest accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAA outperforms all 12 baselines across both benchmarks. Against the strongest non-active TTA methods, PAA (68.19% avg, ViT-B/16) beats BoostAdapter (66.33%) by 1.86%, ZERO (66.10%) by 2.09%, BATCLIP (65.99%) by 2.20%, and DPE (65.93%) by 2.26%. Against the two ATTA baselines extended to the VLM setting, PAA surpasses EATTA (65.17%) by 3.02% and SimATTA (64.91%) by 3.28%, indicating that prototype-structured updates from sparse binary feedback are more effective than the local-correction strategies used by prior ATTA methods. The margin over zero-shot CLIP (59.11%) is 9.08%, and over prompt-tuning baselines TPT (62.44%) and DiffTPT (62.28%) is 5.75% and 5.91% respectively. Gains are largest under the most severe shifts (ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R), where PAA leads BoostAdapter by 2.06% and 1.98% respectively, confirming that the prototype-anchored decision space is particularly beneficial when the target distribution departs substantially from the pre-training domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAA sits at the intersection of two lines of work: VLM-specific test-time adaptation (TPT, TDA, DPE, BoostAdapter, BATCLIP) and active test-time adaptation (SimATTA, EATTA). Prior VLM TTA methods rely on pseudo-labels or heuristic strategies that lack directional guidance for where class boundaries should move, while prior ATTA methods (originally vision-only) treat queried annotations as local corrections rather than structured updates to a shared decision space. PAA departs from both by treating class-wise visual and textual prototypes as a single evolving state and consolidating sparse binary feedback into prototype-regularized updates (cross-modal alignment, intra-class compactness, inter-class separation) plus a memory-driven slow controller, rather than per-sample gradient corrections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for deploying VLMs under evolving distributions where only sparse, delayed human feedback (binary yes/no) is available, achieving near-full-label accuracy (68.19% vs. 68.84%) at a fraction of the annotation cost. The prototype-anchored design keeps the number of trainable parameters small (LayerNorm + per-class residuals), making it deployable on a single GPU with ~17 min adaptation time on ImageNet-A. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to open-vocabulary or multi-label settings, how it behaves under non-stationary sequential shifts beyond the tested ImageNet variants, and whether lighter adapter designs can further reduce the latency the authors acknowledge as a limitation.
