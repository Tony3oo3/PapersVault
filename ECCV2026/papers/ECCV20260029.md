## Problem

VLM-based visual grounding and detection serialize 2D bounding boxes into 1D coordinate token streams, forcing strictly sequential token-by-token decoding that creates a significant inference bottleneck and mismatches the inherently coupled geometry of box coordinates. Existing multi-token prediction (MTP) approaches are structure-agnostic, grouping tokens into arbitrary chunks that can span box boundaries and object categories, leading to spurious correlations and degraded localization accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image at native resolution (processed by a Moon-ViT encoder preserving fine-grained spatial detail) and a free-form natural language query as input. Output is a sequence of fixed-length atomic blocks (length 6), where each block encodes either a bounding box (four quantized coordinates normalized to [0,1000] plus structural tokens), a semantic label, a negative indicator, or an end signal. Three on-demand inference modes (Fast, Slow, Hybrid) allow users to trade off throughput against robustness depending on deployment constraints.

## Method

LocateAnything introduces Parallel Box Decoding (PBD), which treats each bounding box as an indivisible atomic unit predicted in a single parallel forward pass rather than as a sequence of independent coordinate tokens. Training employs a dual-formulation strategy: a single concatenated input sequence contains both a standard NTP stream (preserving causal reasoning) and a block-wise MTP stream (where tokens within each box-aligned block share bidirectional attention while blocks follow causal ordering), jointly optimized with a specialized attention mask that isolates the two streams. At inference, a Hybrid mode runs MTP by default but monitors top-1 token probability and top-5 coordinate spread to detect format irregularity or spatial ambiguity, falling back to localized NTP re-decoding only for the problematic block. The model is trained on LocateAnything-Data (138M queries, 785M boxes across 12M images spanning six task categories) using a two-stage supervised fine-tuning protocol that emphasizes dense multi-object scenes in the second stage.

## Results

On LVIS, LocateAnything-3B achieves 50.7 mean F1 (F1@0.5=62.3, F1@0.95=31.1), improving +3.8% over Rex-Omni-3B (46.9) at the same model size. On COCO, it reaches 54.7 mean F1, surpassing Rex-Omni (52.9) and all other VLMs. On dense detection, it scores 39.9 mean F1 on VisDrone (+4.1 over Rex-Omni) and 58.7 on Dense200. Throughput reaches 12.7 boxes/sec under Hybrid Mode, 2.5× faster than Rex-Omni (5.0 BPS) and ~11× faster than Qwen3-VL (1.1 BPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the most closely related method Rex-Omni-3B (same 3B scale), LocateAnything improves mean F1 by +3.8% on LVIS (50.7 vs 46.9) and +1.8% on COCO (54.7 vs 52.9), while running 2.5× faster (12.7 vs 5.0 BPS). The high-IoU gains are especially pronounced: F1@0.95 jumps from 20.7 to 31.1 on LVIS and from 15.9 to 19.3 on COCO, indicating substantially tighter box predictions. On dense detection (VisDrone), LocateAnything leads Rex-Omni by +4.1 mean F1 (39.9 vs 35.8), and on document layout (M6Doc) by +14.5 (70.1 vs 55.6). On GUI grounding (ScreenSpot-Pro), LocateAnything-3B achieves 60.3 average F1, surpassing the much larger GUI-Owl-32B (58.0) and Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B (53.7). Compared to general-purpose VLMs using textual coordinate decoding (Qwen3-VL-4B/8B), LocateAnything gains 7–9% mean F1 on COCO while being 10–12× faster in throughput. The only notable trade-off is a slight drop in F1@0.5 on LVIS (62.3 vs 64.3 for Rex-Omni), but the large F1@0.95 improvement more than compensates in the mean metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: VLM-based detection/grounding (Rex-Omni, Qwen-VL, Grounding DINO) that serialize bounding boxes into token sequences, and Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) methods from NLP (SDLM, Block Diffusion, Medusa) that predict multiple tokens in parallel. The key departure is structural: rather than applying MTP with arbitrary chunk boundaries (as in SDLM or Block Diffusion), LocateAnything aligns each MTP block with a complete geometric unit (a bounding box or point), treating the four coordinates as an indivisible atomic prediction. This box-aligned formulation, combined with a dual NTP+MTP training objective and a hybrid inference fallback, is the paper's core architectural contribution, distinguishing it from both pure autoregressive VLMs and structure-agnostic parallel decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 2.5× throughput improvement over the closest VLM baseline (Rex-Omni) while simultaneously improving high-IoU localization accuracy suggests that the token-by-token serialization of box coordinates was not merely a speed bottleneck but also an accuracy limiter. The 138M-sample LocateAnything-Data dataset provides a substantial resource for the community. However, the method is currently limited to supervised fine-tuning; the authors explicitly identify reinforcement learning for block-level decoding policy optimization as an important next step to reduce fallback frequency and improve worst-case performance in dense/long-tail scenarios. Whether the structural-alignment principle generalizes beyond 2D boxes (e.g., to 3D volumes, segmentation masks, or arbitrary geometric primitives) remains an open question.
