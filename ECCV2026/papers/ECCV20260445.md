## Problem

Existing inference-time scaling methods for video diffusion either impose priors on initial noise or evaluate rewards only on fully decoded final videos, leading to error accumulation, delayed and sparse reward signals, and prohibitive computational cost that prevents the use of stronger search algorithms. The critical gap is the inability to reliably evaluate intermediate latents during the denoising trajectory, which blocks early pruning of poor candidates and efficient multi-candidate search.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260445/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a single generated video (default: 33 frames at 832×480) using the Wan2.1-1.3B diffusion backbone with 50 denoising steps and CFG scale 5.0. Internally, the latent reward model consumes partially denoised latents at intermediate timesteps (10, 15, 20) along with the prompt and timestep index, scoring them without any video-space decoding. The search maintains N candidate trajectories (default N=6) and outputs the single highest-cumulative-reward latent decoded into the final video.

## Method

LatSearch introduces a latent reward model built on Qwen2-VL-3B that scores partially denoised latents at arbitrary timesteps on visual quality, motion quality, and text alignment via special query tokens, trained with a combination of regression loss (anchoring to similarity-weighted video-level rewards) and pairwise preference loss (preserving relative ordering). Video-level rewards are grounded to intermediate latents through cosine similarity between the intermediate latent and the final clean latent, providing a credit-assignment mechanism without explicit latent-level ground truth. On top of this model, LatSearch performs Reward-Guided Resampling and Pruning (RGRP): N candidate trajectories are generated by perturbing a base noise, periodically scored at scheduled timesteps, resampled via reward-normalized multinomial probabilities with uniqueness pruning to remove duplicate seeds, and finally pruned to the single candidate with the highest cumulative reward before decoding. This importance-sampling-inspired procedure balances exploration and exploitation while operating entirely in latent space, avoiding the repeated full-video decoding that dominates the cost of prior search-based methods.

## Results

On VBench-2.0, LatSearch (best configuration, N=6) achieves an average score of 53.84 (+1.94 over baseline), while the enhanced variant LatSearch' reaches 55.25 (+3.35) with only 2.13× the baseline inference time (164.41s vs. 77.21s). LatSearch' outperforms EvoSearch (55.01) by 0.24% in quality while being 4.77× faster (164.41s vs. 783.76s). The method also generalises across backbones, improving CogVideoX-2B by +3.01 and Wan2.1-14B by +1.03 on average VBench-2.0 score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two noise-optimisation baselines, LatSearch clearly wins: FreeInit degrades the average VBench-2.0 score by 2.08 points and FreqPrior by 1.53, while LatSearch improves it by +1.94 to +3.35. Against VideoReward (a search-based method using final-video rewards), LatSearch' achieves a 2.45-point higher average (55.25 vs. 52.80) at 1.72× less inference time (164.41s vs. 283.63s). Against EvoSearch, the strongest baseline, LatSearch' edges out the average score by 0.24 points (55.25 vs. 55.01) while reducing inference time from 783.76s to 164.41s — a 4.77× speedup. Notably, LatSearch' dominates on Physics (53.41 vs. 41.80 for EvoSearch) and Human Fidelity (84.01 vs. 86.73, a small loss), while EvoSearch leads on Creativity (59.25 vs. 57.53) and Controllability (26.18 vs. 23.96). Under equal compute budget (~142s), LatSearch outperforms FreqPrior by 2.44 points in average quality, confirming that latent-space reward guidance is more effective than frequency-domain noise priors at matching computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inference-time scaling paradigm for diffusion models (inspired by LLM test-time compute) to video generation, building on the Wan2.1 diffusion backbone. It departs from two existing lines of work: noise-optimisation methods (FreeInit, FreqPrior) that inject priors into initial noise without intermediate monitoring, and noise-search methods (VideoReward, EvoSearch) that evaluate only fully decoded final videos. The key conceptual departure is introducing a latent reward model—trained with a combination of regression and preference losses on a Qwen2-VL backbone—that scores partially denoised latents at arbitrary timesteps, enabling process-level supervision rather than endpoint-only evaluation. The search mechanism (RGRP) draws on sequential Monte Carlo and importance sampling ideas, but applies them in latent space to avoid the costly decode-and-rescore loop of prior search-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a ~5× reduction in inference time compared to EvoSearch while achieving comparable VBench-2.0 quality, making inference-time scaling for video generation computationally feasible at scale. The latent reward model is architecture-agnostic (validated on Wan2.1-1.3B/14B and CogVideoX-2B/5B), suggesting broad applicability. However, the quality gains over baseline remain modest (+1.9 to +3.4 points), and the cosine-similarity credit assignment is an acknowledged approximation lacking theoretical grounding. Open questions include whether learned temporal credit-assignment functions can replace the similarity heuristic, whether the approach extends to audio-video or editing tasks, and whether convergence guarantees can be established for the SMC-inspired resampling procedure.
