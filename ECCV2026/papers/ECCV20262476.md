## Problem

Existing self-supervised monocular depth estimation models are typically single-scale, degrade significantly in dynamic driving scenes where moving objects violate the static-camera assumption, and lightweight variants sacrifice structural fidelity at object boundaries. The paper asks whether a self-supervised framework can close the robustness gap with large supervised foundation models while remaining deployable on resource-constrained automotive edge devices without auxiliary supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (640×192 for KITTI, 416×128 for Cityscapes) from monocular video sequences and outputs a per-pixel depth map. Training is entirely self-supervised via photometric reprojection over adjacent video frames, requiring no semantic labels, optical flow, pseudo-depth maps, or other auxiliary cues. The model family spans five scales (Nano through X-Large) with FLOPs from 0.7G to 24.6G, enabling deployment from mobile SoCs (37.6 FPS on Snapdragon 8 Elite for Nano) to desktop GPUs.

## Method

FlexDepth uses a YOLOv11-based multi-scale encoder paired with a Scale-Driven Decoder (SDD) that adaptively selects its constituent components per model scale: smaller models (Nano/Small) use High-Efficiency Bottleneck post-convolutions with a conventional prediction head, while larger models (Medium/X-Large) use High-Performance Bottleneck (CSP-based) post-convolutions with an inverted prediction head that upsamples before the final convolution. All scales employ a dynamic upsampling operator that predicts spatial offsets via a convolutional layer and performs non-uniform grid sampling, replacing content-agnostic bilinear interpolation. Training follows a two-stage static-dynamic decoupled strategy: Stage 1 jointly optimizes the depth and pose networks via standard photometric reprojection loss; Stage 2 exploits epoch-wise disparity inconsistency (early vs. late training outputs) to construct an adaptive static-dynamic mask, then retrains the depth network with a masked normal-distribution loss and geometric depth smoothing that up-weights static regions, all without any additional architectural complexity or external priors.

## Results

On KITTI (Eigen split), Flex-X-Large achieves the best Abs Rel of 0.093 and δ<1.25 of 0.910 with only 24.6 GFLOPs, outperforming heavier methods like Self-Distillation (0.095, 64.5G) and MonoViT (0.099, 59.7G). On Cityscapes, Flex-X-Large reaches Abs Rel 0.086 and δ<1.25 0.926 at 19.6 GFLOPs, surpassing DSI-MonoViT (0.088, 47.7G) and ProDepth (0.095, 35.5G). The smallest variant, Flex-Nano, requires only 0.7 GFLOPs and runs at 37.6 FPS on Snapdragon 8 Elite while matching Lite-Mono-Tiny's accuracy (Abs Rel 0.110) at 4× lower compute. Zero-shot transfer to Make3D yields Abs Rel 0.279 for Flex-X-Large, beating GeoDepth (0.296) and Lite-Mono (0.305).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On KITTI, Flex-X-Large (Abs Rel 0.093, 24.6G) outperforms Self-Distillation (0.095, 64.5G) and MonoViT (0.099, 59.7G) with roughly 2.6–3× fewer FLOPs, and beats TinyDepth (0.096, 13.3G) on RMSE log (0.167 vs 0.171) despite similar FLOPs. Flex-Nano matches Lite-Mono-Tiny's Abs Rel (0.110) at only 0.7G vs 2.8G FLOPs, and Flex-Small (0.104, 2.8G) surpasses Lite-Mono-Small (0.110, 4.7G) on both error and compute. On Cityscapes, Flex-X-Large (0.086, 19.6G) beats DSI-MonoViT (0.088, 47.7G) and ProDepth (0.095, 35.5G), while Flex-Large (0.087, 9.2G) outperforms PuriLight (0.099, 5.7G) with only 1.6× the FLOPs. In the dynamic-region ablation (Table 5), Flex-X-Large achieves 0.091 Abs Rel on dynamic objects vs 0.097 for DSI-MonoViT and 0.115 for ProDepth, using no auxiliary cues (no pixel motion, no pseudo-depth) and 2.4–1.8× fewer FLOPs. On zero-shot Make3D, Flex-X-Large (Abs Rel 0.279) beats GeoDepth (0.296) and Lite-Mono (0.305). Under the matched LS-alignment protocol with improved GT, Flex-X-Large (Abs Rel 0.063) surpasses DA2 ViT-L zero-shot (0.092) at 640×192 resolution, though DA2 retains a slight edge on δ<1.25 (0.956 vs 0.952) at its native 1722×518 resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexDepth extends the self-supervised monocular depth estimation lineage established by Monodepth2 (photometric consistency, minimum reprojection loss, auto-masking) and the dynamic-scene handling framework of DSI (masked normal distribution loss, geometric depth smoothing), while borrowing the learned-offset upsampling mechanism from Liu et al. (ICCV 2023). Its key conceptual departure is a deliberate shift of design emphasis from encoder to decoder: the authors argue that the U-Net-style inductive bias in decoders is the true bottleneck for lightweight models, and introduce a Scale-Driven Decoder that swaps constituent modules (HEB vs. HPB post-convolution, dynamic vs. bilinear upsampling, conventional vs. inverted prediction head) based on model scale. The two-stage static-dynamic decoupled training generalizes DSI's fixed-threshold masking into an adaptive, epoch-discrepancy-driven mask, removing the need for dataset-wide statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge-deployed driving perception: a 0.7 GFLOPs model (Flex-Nano) achieving 37.6 FPS on Snapdragon 8 Elite while remaining competitive with 2.8–5 GFLOPs baselines gives automotive engineers a concrete, low-cost option. The systematic scale family (Nano through X-Large) with matched training protocols provides a useful reference for accuracy-efficiency trade-offs in self-supervised MDE. However, the method is explicitly scoped to driving scenarios, and the authors acknowledge limited applicability to indoor or close-range occluded settings. An open question is whether the two-stage decoupled training and adaptive masking generalize to non-driving dynamic scenes (e.g., indoor robotics) without re-tuning the structural-evolution threshold function.
