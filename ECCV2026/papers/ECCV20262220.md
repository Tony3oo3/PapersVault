## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models allow humans to correct individual concept predictions, but each intervention incurs manual annotation cost, making it critical to maximize accuracy gains from as few interventions as possible. Prior methods either treat concepts independently (ignoring inter-concept correlations) or, as in Concept Realignment (CR), train a separate neural network to adjust non-intervened concepts—introducing extra training uncertainty and discarding correlation information already encoded in the full CBM encoder.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262220/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and, over T iterative rounds, receives human-provided ground-truth values for one or a group of queried concepts selected by an intervention policy (e.g., UCP). At each round the input image is perturbed by a bounded update (‖x_T − x‖_∞ ≤ T·η, with measured L∞ ≈ 0.002 and SSIM 0.999 for CBM), and the final output is a refined k-dimensional concept vector (with intervened dimensions clamped to ground truth) passed through the classifier to yield a class label. The perturbation is tied to human feedback rather than adversarial objectives, keeping visual changes imperceptible in practice.

## Method

Causal Intervention (CI) is a training-free procedure that exploits the fact that trained CBM parameters encode usable concept-correlation structure, verified by showing strong Spearman alignment (0.849 signed) between ground-truth concept annotation correlations and the learned linear-layer weight correlations. When a human supplies the ground-truth value c_i for queried concept i, CI computes a first-order Taylor-expansion-based perturbation Δx = −η·sign(∇_x L(ĉ_i, c_i)) applied to the input image, propagating the correction through the shared x→c encoder pathway so that non-intervened concepts are adjusted consistently with learned inter-concept dependencies. Over multiple rounds, gradients from all previously intervened concepts are accumulated in a single sign-based update, and a final clamp replaces intervened concept predictions with their ground-truth values. Unlike CR, no additional network is trained; the entire realignment is achieved at inference time using only the existing CBM parameters, reducing implementation complexity and eliminating initialization/hyperparameter uncertainty.

## Results

On the overall Intervention-AUC metric, CI achieves the highest score in nearly all 9 dataset–model configurations, with a 5.07% average gain over the mean of all competing methods. In the critical low-intervention regime (first 10 steps), CI leads by an average of 8.59% over all baselines and outperforms its main competitor, Concept Realignment (CR), by 3.90% (AUC@10) and 2.88% (AUC@20). On the E-CBM architecture (MNIST-Add), CI reaches an AUC(10) of 605.1 versus 601.8 for the best baseline (UCP), while using only 1022 MB GPU memory. CI converges to near-optimal accuracy within ~20 intervention steps, whereas most baselines require exhausting all concept interventions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CI outperforms all six baselines across every dataset–model configuration in both the overall and early-intervention (AUC@10) settings. Against the strongest policy-only baselines (UCP, CCTP, ECTP, EUDTP, COOP, Random), CI gains 1.5–10+ points in overall AUC depending on the setting, with the largest margins on CUB-Individual (e.g., 10661.75 vs. 10434.94 for UCP on CEM, a 2.2% gain). The most direct comparison is with Concept Realignment (CR) [17], the only prior method that explicitly realigns non-intervened concepts: CI beats CR by 3.90% on AUC@10 and 2.88% on AUC@20 on average, while avoiding CR's extra realignment network and its associated training uncertainty. On the E-CBM architecture (MNIST-Add), CI achieves the best AUC(All) of 1957.8 and AUC(10) of 605.1, edging out UCP (1942.6 / 601.8) with lower GPU memory (1022 MB vs. 1296 MB). The one near-tie is CR on AwA2-Int-CEM (8424.78 vs. 8421.38), where CR's dedicated retraining of Int-CEM gives it a slight edge, though CI still wins on CUB for the same model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CBM intervention literature (Koh et al. 2020; Espinosa Zarlenga et al. 2022, 2023) by addressing the concept-realignment step that prior intervention policies (UCP, CCTP, ECTP, EUDTP, COOP) largely ignored. Its most direct predecessor is Concept Realignment (CR, Singhi et al. ECCV 2024), which trains a separate neural network to adjust non-intervened concepts after human correction. CI departs from CR by arguing that the trained CBM's own x→c encoder already encodes usable inter-concept correlations, so feedback can be propagated through a bounded gradient step on the input rather than through an additional trained module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For high-stakes deployments (medical imaging, autonomous driving) where each human annotation is costly, reducing the number of interventions needed to reach a target accuracy has direct practical value. The training-free nature of CI removes a source of variance (initialization, hyperparameter tuning of a second network) and simplifies deployment. Limitations include evaluation only on relatively small datasets (AwA2, CUB-200, MNIST-Add), an explicitly non-SCM 'causal' framing, and input perturbations that, while bounded, accumulate over many steps (SSIM drops to 0.934 for 112 individual CEM interventions). Open questions include whether the learned-correlation structure persists at ImageNet scale, whether the approach transfers to non-vision modalities, and whether the sign-based update is optimal compared to full gradient descent on x.
