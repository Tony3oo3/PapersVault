## Problem

Collaborative perception systems are jointly degraded by pose noise (from GNSS/IMU localization errors) and BEV feature noise (from sensor imperfections and interpolation), and these two noise sources interact to amplify each other during cross-agent fusion. Existing work has largely addressed pose noise in isolation, while feature noise and its coupling with pose noise remain underexplored. Moreover, prior BEV denoising methods rely on explicit dense intermediate supervision (e.g., ground-truth semantic layouts) that is impractical to obtain in real-world multi-agent deployments.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes LiDAR point clouds from the ego agent and neighboring CAVs, 6-DoF GNSS/IMU poses, and multi-scale BEV features extracted by a shared encoder (PointPillars, SECOND, or VoxelNet). During training, multi-level Gaussian pose perturbations (σ_t, σ_r sampled from {0.0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6} m/deg) and fixed high-magnitude feature noise (σ_f = 0.2) are injected; at inference, noise injection is disabled and the pipeline runs a single end-to-end forward pass. Outputs include calibrated relative poses from the pose-graph solver, denoised multi-scale BEV features, and final 3D bounding boxes with AP@0.5/0.7 as the primary metric.

## Method

NoiseGT is built on the principle that noise serves as a self-supervised teacher rather than a mere disturbance, implemented through two plug-and-play modules. The Pose Calibration Module (PCM) performs multi-level noise injection during training—uniformly sampling perturbation scales from S={0.0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6}—followed by graph-based alignment refinement via an agent-object pose graph (using CoAlign's solver), yielding distributionally robust calibration that generalizes to unseen noise magnitudes up to 1.2 m/1.2°. The Feature Denoising Module (FDM) injects fixed high-magnitude isotropic Gaussian noise (σ_f=0.2) into multi-scale BEV features and trains a lightweight U-Net denoiser with a self-supervised L2 consistency loss to reconstruct the unperturbed features, following a 'high-noise-over-low-noise' strategy that enforces a stationary, corruption-agnostic invariance objective without requiring clean intermediate supervision. The two modules are jointly optimized with the downstream detection loss (L_total = L_det + 1.0·L_denoise), and the fixed high-variance noise smooths the denoiser Jacobian to favor a Lipschitz-bounded mapping, reducing sensitivity to heterogeneous real-world corruptions such as structured BEV dropout.

## Results

At the most challenging noise setting (σ_t, σ_r) = (1.2 m, 1.2°), NoiseGT achieves AP@0.5/0.7 of 0.745/0.661 on V2X-Sim, 0.691/0.564 on DAIR-V2X, and 0.901/0.804 on OPV2V. These correspond to relative improvements of 12.03%/14.56% over CoAlign on V2X-Sim, 2.67%/0.89% on DAIR-V2X, and 6.12%/9.69% on OPV2V. Under clean conditions (0.0/0.0), NoiseGT matches or slightly exceeds the best baselines (e.g., 0.896 AP@0.5 on V2X-Sim vs. 0.885 for V2X-ViT), confirming that the noise-driven training does not sacrifice clean-weather performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoiseGT outperforms all seven baselines at the most severe noise level (1.2 m, 1.2°) on all three datasets for both AP@0.5 and AP@0.7. The strongest baseline, CoAlign [21], is surpassed by 12.03%/14.56% (relative) on V2X-Sim, 2.67%/0.89% on DAIR-V2X, and 6.12%/9.69% on OPV2V. The gap widens with increasing noise: at 0.0/0.0, NoiseGT leads CoAlign by only 1.4%/1.4% on V2X-Sim, but by 12.0%/14.6% at 1.2/1.2, demonstrating that the noise-driven training specifically targets robustness rather than clean-condition accuracy. On DAIR-V2X, gains are smaller (2.67%/0.89%) because denser urban scenes make pose-graph association harder, partially offsetting the denoising benefit. Against diffusion-based CoDiff [14] under a compute-matched single-step budget on DAIR-V2X, NoiseGT achieves higher AP (e.g., 0.747/0.593 vs. 0.716/0.504 at 0.0/0.0) while running at 60 ms vs. 127.7 ms latency and 341 MB vs. 769 MB GPU memory. The method also generalizes across backbones (SECOND, VoxelNet) and pose solvers (RoCo), consistently improving over the respective baselines by 3–5% relative under severe noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

NoiseGT builds directly on the intermediate-fusion collaborative perception pipeline (particularly CoAlign's agent-object pose-graph optimization) and the broader line of pose-correction methods (RoCo, GraphPS, VIPS). It departs from recent diffusion-based BEV denoising approaches (DiffBEV, BEVDiffuser) that require explicit dense intermediate supervision (semantic layouts, depth distributions) by reframing denoising as a self-supervised, in-network regularizer that uses injected noise as its own training signal. The 'high-noise-over-low-noise' strategy and multi-level pose perturbation training are novel repurposings of data-augmentation and denoising-autoencoder ideas, applied jointly to both pose and feature uncertainty in a single unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap that prior collaborative perception work has largely overlooked: the simultaneous and coupled degradation from pose noise and feature noise in real deployments. The 'noise as teacher' principle removes the dependency on clean intermediate supervision, making robust collaborative perception more scalable without expensive dense annotations. Limitations remain for extreme pose errors (≥6 m) and non-zero-mean feature bias, and the authors note that adaptive, confidence-aware regularization is needed for truly variable real-world conditions. The plug-and-play, solver-agnostic, and backbone-agnostic design (validated across PointPillars, SECOND, VoxelNet, and two pose solvers) positions it as a broadly applicable refinement layer for existing collaborative perception stacks.
