## Problem

Standard outcome-based RL (e.g., GRPO) applied to medical reasoning suffers from autoregressive credit assignment failure: because medical tasks lack deterministic verifiers, binary correctness rewards indiscriminately reinforce spurious correlations and dataset shortcuts, producing the 'Right Answer, Wrong Reason' trap. Additionally, extreme heterogeneity in medical query difficulty causes unbounded gradient variance—trivial samples yield vanishing gradients while hallucinatory rationales explode optimization instability—making naive self-improvement unreliable.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a multimodal clinical query (RGB medical image plus text prompt) or a text-only clinical question and produces a structured chain-of-thought response consisting of a latent rationale sequence r followed by a final diagnostic answer a. During training, N=8 rollouts are generated per prompt, and a verification step constructs a separate prompt that masks the original query and asks the model to predict the answer from the rationale alone, effectively testing whether the reasoning is a sufficient statistic for the conclusion.

## Method

CARE reformulates medical self-improvement as intrinsic experience curation governed by two theoretical conditions. First, Causal Sufficiency is enforced via Agreement-Based Self-Verification: a trajectory is admitted only if the model, conditioned solely on the generated rationale with the query masked, reproduces the same final answer—empirically instantating a do-calculus intervention that acts as a gradient debiasing operator (Theorem 2). Second, Proximal Learnability restricts training to trajectories whose length-normalized NLL falls within a dynamically computed quantile window [τ_low, τ_high], guaranteeing variance-bounded optimization within the model's zone of proximal development (Theorem 3). Curated experiences are then optimized via a dual-stream objective combining on-policy GRPO (with group-relative advantages derived from the unified admission score) and difficulty-weighted experience replay, where replay samples are importance-weighted by their NLL to prioritize frontier-of-capability trajectories.

## Results

On medical multimodal benchmarks, CARE-7B (built on Hulu-Med-7B) achieves 85.6 on OmniVQA, 68.2 on PMC-VQA, 79.3 on VQA-RAD, 88.1 on SLAKE, 67.1 on PathVQA, 31.2 on MedXQA, and 53.0 on MMMU-Med, outperforming its backbone by 1.3–2.2 points across all seven datasets. On medical text benchmarks, CARE-7B reaches 62.4 on MMLU-Pro-Med, 75.0 on MedQA, and 81.1 on MMLU-Med, consistently exceeding Hulu-Med-7B and HuatuoGPT-V. The ablation (Table 3) shows that removing Causal Sufficiency causes the largest single-component drop (−0.8 avg), while the full CARE pipeline yields +1.5 average over the SFT baseline and +1.1 over standard GRPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARE-7B outperforms its Hulu-Med-7B backbone on all seven multimodal benchmarks (e.g., +1.4 on OmniVQA, +2.2 on MedXQA) and all six text benchmarks (e.g., +1.5 on MedQA, +1.6 on MMLU-Med), confirming that the RL curation pipeline adds reasoning gains beyond the SFT architecture. Against the strongest open medical VLM Lingshu-7B, CARE-7B leads by 2.7 on OmniVQA, 11.9 on PMC-VQA, 11.4 on VQA-RAD, 5.0 on SLAKE, and 5.2 on PathVQA. Compared to proprietary models, CARE-7B surpasses GPT-4.1 on five of seven multimodal benchmarks (OmniVQA 85.6 vs 75.5, PMC-VQA 68.2 vs 55.2, VQA-RAD 79.3 vs 65.0, SLAKE 88.1 vs 72.2, PathVQA 67.1 vs 55.5) but trails on MedXQA (31.2 vs 45.2) and MMMU-Med (53.0 vs 75.2), where larger proprietary models retain an advantage. Standard GRPO (outcome-only) yields only +0.4 average over the SFT baseline, whereas full CARE achieves +1.5, with the Causal Sufficiency filter contributing the largest single-component improvement. The OOD robustness test (injecting spurious symptoms) shows CARE degrades by only 3.8% versus 15.3% for Standard GRPO, empirically validating that causal alignment reduces shortcut reliance.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARE builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) for its on-policy group-relative advantage computation, but departs fundamentally from standard outcome-based RL by reframing the training signal as an intrinsic experience curation problem governed by two theoretical conditions: causal sufficiency (instantiated via do-calculus-inspired agreement-based self-verification) and proximal learnability (dynamic entropy-bounded NLL windows). In the medical VLM lineage, it extends the RL-for-medical-reasoning line (Med-R1, MedVLM-R1, MedCCO) by addressing what the authors formalize as the 'Right Answer, Wrong Reason' trap — a credit-assignment failure specific to domains lacking deterministic verifiers. The dual-stream objective combining on-policy exploration with difficulty-weighted replay is a practical synthesis of ideas from experience replay in offline RL and the group-relative structure of GRPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 45% of correct-answer rationales from standard GRPO rely on spurious shortcuts (vs. 13.8% for CARE) is practically significant for clinical deployment, where interpretability and reliability matter as much as accuracy. The agreement-based self-verification mechanism offers a scalable, annotation-free way to enforce reasoning quality, which is valuable given the cost of expert medical annotation. However, open questions remain: the framework is validated only on 7B models, the self-verification step adds inference overhead per rollout, the theoretical proofs (particularly Theorem 1 and the variance bound) are somewhat informal, and it is unclear whether the causal-sufficiency filter generalizes to non-medical domains or to longer multi-step reasoning chains. The modest per-component ablation gains (~0.3–0.5% each) also raise questions about whether the full theoretical apparatus is necessary or whether simpler heuristics could capture most of the benefit.
