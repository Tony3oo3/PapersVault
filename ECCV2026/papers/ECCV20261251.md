## Problem

Mobile GUI agents send screenshots containing PII to untrusted third-party routers that can exploit their own MLLMs to extract sensitive user data. Existing adversarial perturbation methods for visual privacy fail the critical dual challenge of this scenario: they either protect PII at the cost of destroying the agent's task utility, or preserve functionality while leaving privacy exposed. No prior method explicitly decouples these two conflicting objectives for the mobile agent setting.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a raw mobile screenshot (RGB, 3×H×W, pixel values in [0,1]) and a derived contrastive attention map as input, and produces an L∞-bounded perturbation δ with ‖δ‖∞ ≤ ε (ε = 128/255) that is added to the original image and clipped to [0,1]. The resulting protected screenshot x_adv is forwarded to the untrusted router. The generator is a lightweight U-Net (~300 MB) designed for on-device inference with sub-0.3 s latency per image, and it requires no per-image optimization at test time.

## Method

DualTAP trains a U-Net-style perturbation generator using two coupled innovations. First, a contrastive attention module computes saliency maps via NLL gradients over privacy-oriented QA pairs (S_p) and normal-task QA pairs (S_n) using a frozen surrogate MLLM, then takes ReLU(S_p − S_n) to isolate regions uniquely sensitive to privacy cues; this map is injected into the generator's decoder at every resolution level via FiLM-style affine modulation (scale γ and shift β predicted by lightweight convolutions). Second, a dual-task adversarial objective simultaneously minimizes a task-preservation loss (NLL on normal-task answers) and maximizes a privacy-interference loss (NLL on privacy-extraction answers), weighted by α and β, so the generator learns to suppress PII confidence while maintaining normal-task accuracy. Training uses a white-box surrogate (InternVL3-5-2B) with gradients, relying on perturbation transferability to black-box target MLLMs (GPT-5, Gemini-2.0, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, Holo1.5, UI-TARS). The generator is optimized with Adam (lr 1e-4, batch 4, 20 epochs) on a single GPU, and at inference it produces the perturbation in a single forward pass without iterative optimization.

## Results

On the PrivScreen dataset, DualTAP reduces the average privacy leakage rate (LR) by 31.7 percentage points (a 2.6× relative improvement over the strongest baseline) while maintaining an 80.8% task success rate, a negligible drop from the 83.6% unprotected baseline. On GPT-5, LR drops from 97.14% to 23.33% and BertScore from 0.9260 to 0.1589 (a 5.8× reduction). On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LR falls from 97.14% to 32.86% while accuracy is preserved at 88% (vs. 89% unprotected). Inference latency is under 0.3 s per image, comparable to the fastest baseline (AnyAttack) and far faster than FOA-Attack (~120 s) or VIP (~600 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualTAP dominates all four baselines on every privacy metric (LR, BertScore, CS, BLEU) across all six target MLLMs. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, DualTAP achieves LR = 32.86% versus 95.24% (AnyAttack), 83.81% (FOA-Attack), 94.76% (VIP), and 93.57% (AVIH), while simultaneously maintaining the highest accuracy (88%) among all defenses. On GPT-5, the gap widens further: DualTAP's LR of 23.33% is roughly 3–4× lower than the best baseline (FOA-Attack at 80.95%), and its BertScore of 0.1589 is ~4× lower than any competitor. The key differentiator is task preservation: baselines that reduce leakage (e.g., FOA-Attack, AVIH) sacrifice 5–19 accuracy points, whereas DualTAP loses at most 1–3 points relative to the unprotected original. VIP, the region-specific method, is the closest competitor on accuracy but still leaks at 80–95% LR, indicating that targeted masking alone is insufficient against MLLM-based extraction. Inference speed also favors DualTAP: at <0.3 s per image it matches AnyAttack and is 400× faster than VIP (~600 s) and ~400× faster than FOA-Attack (~120 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial perturbation line of work for visual privacy (AnyAttack, FOA-Attack, VIP, AVIH) by reframing the problem as a dual-objective optimization: prior methods treat privacy protection as a single goal (disrupt MLLM recognition of sensitive content), whereas DualTAP explicitly decouples two conflicting objectives—preserving task utility for the agent's MLLM while suppressing PII extraction by a separate router MLLM. The contrastive attention module (saliency difference between privacy-QA and task-QA gradients) is a novel architectural component that repurposes gradient-based attribution for spatially targeted perturbation allocation, and the U-Net generator with affine modulation borrows from style-transfer-style conditioning to inject the attention signal at multiple resolutions. The PrivScreen dataset is new, filling a gap in mobile-screenshot-specific privacy benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As mobile GUI agents become mainstream, the honest-but-curious router threat is a realistic and under-addressed privacy risk with direct GDPR/CCPA implications; DualTAP provides the first practical, lightweight (<0.3 s, ~300 MB) on-device solution that nearly eliminates the privacy-utility trade-off. Key open questions include: whether the L∞-bounded perturbation remains effective against future, more robust MLLMs; how the method scales to multi-turn, long-horizon tasks where the router accumulates cross-screenshot context; and whether the surrogate-to-target transferability assumption holds as commercial models diverge further from open-source surrogates. The evaluation, while broad across six MLLMs, is limited to a single dataset of ~500 screenshots and a narrow threat model (single-screenshot, honest-but-curious).
