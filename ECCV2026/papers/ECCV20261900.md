## Problem

Existing functional map pipelines for non-rigid shape matching suffer from two coupled failures: geometric descriptors alone cannot disambiguate symmetric or cross-category regions, and pointwise correspondences recovered from truncated spectral bases exhibit local spatial inconsistencies even when the global spectral alignment appears plausible. Prior attempts to inject semantic cues from vision foundation models tend to override local geometric structure, and no prior work has addressed the spectral-truncation-induced local mismatch through a continuous transport mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D triangular meshes as input. Each mesh is rendered from multiple viewpoints into depth- and normal-guided images, which are processed by a frozen DINOv2 encoder to produce vertex-level semantic features via back-projection and multi-view averaging; geometric features are computed per-vertex by DiffusionNet. The output is a dense soft correspondence matrix (n_X × n_Y) obtained from fused feature similarity, along with bidirectional k×k functional map matrices in the Laplace-Beltrami eigenbasis.

## Method

SGMatch fuses geometric and semantic features through a Semantic-Guided Local Cross-Attention (SGLCA) module that combines channel-wise semantic gating (sigmoid MLP on projected semantic features modulating geometric channels) with cross-attention restricted to mesh neighborhoods, so that semantic context disambiguates parts without disrupting local structure. The fused features drive a standard regularized functional maps solver (bijectivity + orthogonality losses) and a temperature-scaled softmax point-wise map. To suppress local inconsistencies from spectral truncation, the paper adapts conditional flow matching as a trajectory-level transport regularizer: spectral heat diffusion stabilizes feature endpoints, a linear interpolation path defines the flow, and an MLP velocity field conditioned on time via sinusoidal embeddings and FiLM is trained with a Charbonnier loss under similarity-based importance sampling. This CFM term acts as an implicit spatial-coherence prior—locally inconsistent correspondences produce mutually incompatible displacement targets that a shared finite-capacity velocity network cannot fit—rather than as an explicit Laplacian smoothing penalty.

## Results

On the non-isometric SMAL benchmark, SGMatch achieves a mean geodesic error of 2.5 (×100), surpassing the previous best (HybridFMap, 3.3) by 24%. On DT4D-H, it attains 1.0 (intra-class) and 3.4 (inter-class), matching or slightly improving over the strongest baselines. Under topological noise on TOPKIDS, SGMatch yields a 42% reduction in mean geodesic error over the best competing method. On near-isometric SHREC'19 (cross-dataset generalization), it achieves the best score of 3.3, ahead of HybridFMap and DenoisFMap (both 3.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGMatch clearly outperforms all baselines on SMAL (2.5 vs. 3.3 for HybridFMap, the previous best), a 24% relative improvement that the paper attributes to semantic disambiguation under large non-isometric deformations. On DT4D-H inter-class, it edges out HybridFMap (3.4 vs. 3.5) and substantially beats ULRSSM (4.1) and DeepFAFM (3.9), indicating that semantic cues help maintain cross-category consistency where purely geometric descriptors fail. On DT4D-H intra-class, SGMatch (1.0) ties HybridFMap and is marginally behind ULRSSM and DeepFAFM (both 0.9), suggesting the semantic advantage is most pronounced in the harder inter-class regime. On near-isometric SHREC'19, SGMatch (3.3) beats HybridFMap and DenoisFMap (3.6) and DiffuMatch (3.9), with the gap widening relative to training-distribution datasets (FAUST: 1.4 vs. 1.5; SCAPE: 1.8 vs. 1.8), confirming that semantic priors contribute most under distribution shift. Under topological noise (TOPKIDS), SGMatch achieves a 42% geodesic-error reduction over the best axiomatic/unsupervised competitor, a setting where Laplace-Beltrami-based descriptors degrade most severely. Overall, the method is competitive or best across all six benchmarks, with the largest margins appearing precisely where geometric invariance breaks down.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGMatch sits squarely within the deep functional maps lineage, inheriting the spectral decomposition and point-wise recovery pipeline from Ovsjanikov et al. and the structured regularization (bijectivity, orthogonality) from Ren et al. Its two departures are: (1) lifting 2D foundation-model semantic features (DINOv2 via Diff3F-style multi-view distillation) into the functional map descriptor space through a novel local cross-attention module that treats semantics as structure-aware anchors rather than replacements for geometry, and (2) repurposing conditional flow matching—not as a generative model but as a trajectory-level transport regularizer that penalizes spatially incoherent correspondences arising from spectral truncation. This positions the work at the intersection of the semantic-feature-for-shape-matching line (Diff3F, EchoMatch, DV-Matcher) and the recent push toward smoother point-wise recovery (synchronous diffusion, locality-preserving refinement).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete, well-identified failure modes of functional maps—descriptor ambiguity in symmetric regions and local spatial inconsistency from truncated spectral bases—and shows that semantic guidance and trajectory-level transport regularization each target one of these. The 42% geodesic-error reduction on TOPKIDS under topological noise and the 24% gain on SMAL over prior SOTA are practically meaningful for applications like texture transfer and pose transfer where such degradations are common. Key open questions remain: the framework is restricted to full-shape correspondence (partial matching is explicitly out of scope), and its reliance on 2D foundation-model features means performance is bounded by the domain coverage of those external encoders, leaving medical or industrial domains where such priors are weak as an open challenge.
