## Problem

Existing dual-teacher adversarial robustness distillation methods (e.g., CIARD) fail to fully exploit the joint supervision of a natural teacher and a robust teacher. Specifically, their inner maximization relies on hard-label or single-teacher objectives, their repulsion regularization only covers adversarial outputs, and their robust-teacher fine-tuning ignores inter-teacher consistency, collectively producing conflicting optimization signals that degrade the student's accuracy-robustness trade-off.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260608/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student model (ResNet-18, MobileNet-V2, or PreActResNet-18) receives standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and outputs a class probability vector over 10, 100, or 200 classes respectively. During training, adversarial perturbations are generated online via 10-step PGD with an L∞ budget of 8/255 and step size 2/255; the student is simultaneously supervised on both clean and adversarial inputs, while the two frozen-then-fine-tuned teachers (e.g., ResNet-56 + WRN-34-10 for CIFAR-10) provide soft-label guidance.

## Method

GCMRD introduces three coupled modules over the dual-teacher distillation loop. (1) Multi-teacher Collaboration Inner Maximization (MCIM) replaces the hard-label or single-teacher KL attack objective with the sum of KL divergences between the student's adversarial logits and both teachers' clean outputs, so that generated adversarial examples reflect a consistent attack preference across the teacher ensemble. (2) Full-Repulsion Regularization (FRR) extends CIARD's push loss by adding a second repulsion term that also distances the student's clean-output logits from the natural teacher's erroneous adversarial predictions, turning the teacher's mistakes into negative supervision for both the natural and adversarial tasks simultaneously. (3) Consistency Fine-tuning (CFT) replaces CIARD's hard-label CE update of the robust teacher with a KL objective toward the natural teacher's clean-sample soft labels on both clean and adversarial inputs, creating a 'cyclic consistency' in which every optimization signal—clean distillation, robust distillation, repulsion, and teacher fine-tuning—is ultimately anchored to the natural teacher's clean-data oracle, thereby indirectly harmonizing the student's competing accuracy and robustness objectives.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, GCMRD achieves 89.26% clean accuracy and 55.01% robust accuracy under PGD_TRADES (ε=8/255), outperforming the previous best dual-teacher method CIARD (88.87% / 54.46%) and the single-teacher RSLAD (83.99% / 55.73%). On CIFAR-100, GCMRD reaches 65.81% clean accuracy and 30.41% PGD_TRADES robustness, surpassing CIARD (65.73% / 29.45%) and B-MTARD (65.08% / 29.94%). GCMRD also leads on FGSM, PGD_SAT, and CW∞ across both datasets, and achieves the best black-box transfer robustness (e.g., 67.24% PGD_TRADES transfer on CIFAR-10 vs. 66.28% for CIARD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCMRD consistently outperforms all six baselines across every attack and both datasets. Against the closest competitor CIARD (the prior SOTA dual-teacher method), GCMRD gains +0.39% clean accuracy and +0.55% PGD_TRADES robustness on CIFAR-10, and +0.08% clean / +0.96% PGD_TRADES on CIFAR-100. Compared to the balanced multi-teacher B-MTARD, GCMRD improves PGD_TRADES robustness by +0.61% (CIFAR-10) and +0.47% (CIFAR-100) while also matching or exceeding its clean accuracy. Single-teacher distillation methods (ARD, RSLAD) trail GCMRD by 4–5% in clean accuracy and 2–5% in adversarial robustness, confirming the advantage of the dual-teacher paradigm. Direct adversarial training (TRADES) remains the weakest baseline, with GCMRD surpassing it by 6.26% clean and 1.18% PGD_TRADES on CIFAR-10. Notably, GCMRD is the only method that simultaneously leads in both clean accuracy and adversarial robustness, demonstrating a superior accuracy–robustness trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

GCMRD extends the dual-teacher adversarial robustness distillation paradigm established by MTARD (Zhao et al., ECCV 2022 / TPAMI 2024) and refined by CIARD (Lu et al., ICCV 2025), which introduced push regularization and cyclic fine-tuning of the robust teacher. The key departure is a 'global consistency' principle: rather than treating the natural and robust teachers as independent experts, GCMRD couples them at three levels—using both teachers' soft outputs to guide adversarial example generation (extending RSLAD's single-teacher KL attack), extending CIARD's push loss to also repel the student's clean predictions from the natural teacher's erroneous adversarial outputs, and replacing CIARD's hard-label fine-tuning of the robust teacher with KL alignment to the natural teacher's clean predictions. Conceptually, the paper argues that all optimization signals should be anchored to the natural teacher's clean-data oracle, creating a cyclic consistency that indirectly harmonizes the student's accuracy and robustness objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The accuracy-robustness trade-off remains a central bottleneck for deploying adversarially robust models in resource-constrained settings, and GCMRD demonstrates that more principled exploitation of dual-teacher supervision can improve both sides of the trade-off simultaneously. However, the gains over the strong CIARD baseline are modest (roughly 0.3–0.5 percentage points in clean accuracy and 0.1–0.5 points in robust accuracy on CIFAR-10), and all experiments are confined to CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet with relatively small student networks. Open questions include whether the global-consistency fine-tuning strategy generalizes to larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet), to more than two teachers, or to transformer-based backbones, and whether the modest improvements persist under stronger attacks or larger perturbation budgets.
