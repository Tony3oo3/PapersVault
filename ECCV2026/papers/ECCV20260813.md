## Problem

Existing broadcast-based V2X collaborative perception methods align collaborative features using only 2D BEV warping, which assumes all agents share the same horizontal plane and ignores 3D spatial misalignment caused by differing sensor heights and pitch angles. This leads to substantially degraded 3D object detection performance. Additionally, standard ego-centric data augmentation (e.g., DPTP) shifts collaborators' point clouds out of their detection range, causing significant information loss during training.

## Input / Output

The ego-vehicle processes its own LiDAR point cloud through a shared PointPillars backbone (0.4 m × 0.4 m pillars) to produce BEV features, while collaborative agents broadcast their pre-extracted BEV features and poses in a single communication round. The model outputs 3D bounding boxes with class labels; during training it additionally predicts a fixed number of 3D points per proposal pillar as an auxiliary reconstruction target. Detection ranges are dataset-specific (e.g., DAIR-V2X: x ∈ [−100.8, 100.8] m, y ∈ [−40, 40] m, z ∈ [−3.5, 1.5] m).

## Method

CoGoal3D is a two-stage broadcast-based framework. Stage 1 (multiscale 3D-Aware Global Fusion) replaces 2D BEV warping with 3D position encoding—transforming each agent's pillar centers into the ego coordinate system via relative pose—and 3D-aware deformable cross attention that jointly performs spatial alignment and feature fusion at multiple scales, followed by an RPN to generate proposals. Stage 2 (Reconstruction-Guided Local Refinement) applies BEV RoI pooling, self-attention with proposal-relative 3D position encoding, and two parallel heads: a detection head for refined 3D boxes and an auxiliary head that reconstructs 3D points within each proposal, supervised by a Ground Truth Optimization step that aligns collaborator points to ego GT boxes to remove calibration and temporal artifacts. A Multi-Agent Collaborative Data Augmentation strategy uses local rotation around GT objects (instead of global rotation), global scaling, and per-agent flipping to maximize diversity while keeping all points within each agent's detection range. The model is trained end-to-end with RPN loss, refinement loss, and Chamfer Distance reconstruction loss.

## Results

On DAIR-V2X (val set), CoGoal3D achieves 3D AP@0.7 of 50.55% and BEV AP@0.7 of 72.16%, surpassing the previous best DI-V2X (39.24% / 65.39%) by 11.31% and 6.77% respectively. On V2V4Real and V2X-Real (test sets), the method outperforms DSRC by 10.34% and 11.97% in 3D AP@0.7, and by 6.65% and 12.34% in BEV AP@0.7. The full model runs at 16.8 FPS, while the stage-1-only variant reaches 24.8 FPS, both at real-time speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoGoal3D outperforms all nine baselines on both BEV and 3D AP metrics across all three datasets. Against the strongest prior method DI-V2X (handshake-based, AAAI 2024), CoGoal3D gains 11.31% in 3D AP@0.7 (50.55 vs. 39.24) and 6.77% in BEV AP@0.7 (72.16 vs. 65.39) on DAIR-V2X, despite using the more communication-efficient broadcast paradigm. Compared to the latest broadcast-based CoSDH (CVPR 2025), the improvement is 13.77% in 3D AP@0.7 (50.55 vs. 36.78) and 7.32% in BEV AP@0.7. On V2V4Real, CoGoal3D beats DSRC (AAAI 2025) by 10.34% in 3D AP@0.7 and 6.65% in BEV AP@0.7; on V2X-Real, the margins are 11.97% and 12.34% respectively. Notably, even the stage-1-only variant (3D-AGF without RGLR) already surpasses all baselines in 3D AP@0.7 on DAIR-V2X (42.66 vs. 39.24 for DI-V2X), confirming that the 3D-aware fusion module alone provides substantial gains. In terms of speed, CoGoal3D's full model (16.8 FPS) is slower than CoAlign (29.1 FPS) and DSRC (26.1 FPS) but faster than CoSDH (6.7 FPS) and ERMVP (12.9 FPS), while the stage-1 model matches CoAlign's speed at 24.8 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the broadcast-based V2X collaborative perception line (DiscoNet, CoBEVT, CoAlign, CoSDH) by replacing their 2D BEV warping with a multiscale 3D-aware deformable attention fusion that explicitly encodes height and attitude differences between agents. It borrows the two-stage RPN-plus-refinement structure from single-vehicle 3D detectors (Pillar-RCNN, PV-RCNN) and adapts it to the collaborative setting, adding an auxiliary 3D point reconstruction head supervised by a Ground Truth Optimization procedure. The MCDA data augmentation strategy departs from the ego-centric DPTP paradigm by interleaving local object-centric rotations with global scaling and per-agent flipping, a design choice specific to multi-agent geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap in V2X deployment: real-world sensor mounting height and pitch differences make 2D BEV warping fundamentally inadequate for 3D detection, and the ~10-point 3D AP@0.7 gains across three datasets quantify how costly that inadequacy is. The MCDA strategy's demonstrated generality (Table 6, consistent gains when plugged into DI-V2X, ERMVP, DSRC, CoSDH) makes it a drop-in improvement for the broader collaborative perception community. Open questions include scalability beyond two agents, applicability to camera-based or mixed-modality V2X, and whether the two-stage cost (16.8 FPS vs. 24.8 FPS for stage 1 alone) is acceptable for latency-critical deployment.
