## Problem

Transfer-based black-box adversarial attacks rely on ensembles of surrogate models to improve transferability, but prior work has used small, fixed ensembles without systematically studying whether scaling the number of surrogates yields further gains. This paper asks whether a predictable scaling law exists between ensemble cardinality and attack success rate, and identifies that naive logit-averaging ensembles fail to scale due to gradient conflict, while idealized ERM convergence theory cannot quantitatively predict behavior under strict ℓ∞ boundary constraints and correlated surrogates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural RGB image (224×224 from ImageNet-1K NIPS 2017), a target class label, and an ensemble of T surrogate models randomly sampled from a pool of 64 pretrained classifiers (or 12 CLIP models for MLLM attacks), under an ℓ∞ perturbation budget of ε=8/255 (classifiers) or ε=16/255 (MLLMs). It produces a single adversarial example per source-target pair, and the key output metric is Attack Success Rate on black-box targets, which for MLLMs is measured via an LLM-as-Judge protocol where GPT-4o semantically evaluates whether the model's generated description mentions the target concept without indicating image corruption. The central empirical finding is that ASR follows a log-linear relationship with log₂(T), with T ranging from 1 to 64 (classifiers) or 1 to 12 (CLIP surrogates).

## Method

The core algorithm is SSA-CWA, which combines Spectrum Simulation Attack (frequency-domain input transformation for finding generalizable adversarial patterns) with Common Weakness Attack (gradient alignment via minimizing second-order Taylor series terms across ensemble members to resolve gradient conflict). The key mechanistic insight is that naive logit-averaging ensembles suffer from gradient conflict—gradients from different surrogates point in random directions and cancel out as T grows—whereas CWA's alignment objective forces the optimizer to find a common vulnerability direction, unlocking the scaling behavior. The paper empirically discovers and rigorously verifies a robust log-linear scaling law (ASR = a + b·log₂(T)) across standard classifiers, SOTA defenses (DiffPure, HGD, adversarial training), and MLLMs, showing that scaling distills model-specific non-robust features into robust semantic features of the target class. For MLLM attacks, the formulation shifts from cross-entropy on logits to cosine embedding loss between the image encoder output and a fixed target text embedding, targeting the CLIP vision encoder that underlies modern multimodal models.

## Results

The paper discovers a robust log-linear scaling law: Attack Success Rate (ASR) scales linearly with log₂(T), where T is the ensemble cardinality, with R² values of 0.94–0.99 across 7 standard classifiers and 8 target labels (Table 1). Applied to SOTA MLLMs via CLIP surrogates (T=12, ε=16/255), the scaled attack achieves 90.0% average ASR on Qwen3-VL-235B and 85.0% on GPT-4o, while Claude-3.5-Sonnet resists at only 31.0% (Table 3). On defended models, scaling improves ASR by over 30% on DiffPure, over 20% on adversarially trained models, and over 50% on HGD. The log-linear trend persists across untargeted attacks, multiple optimizers, and cross-modal transfer to MLLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between logit-averaging ensembles (MI-FGSM, DIMI-FGSM, VMI-FGSM) and gradient-conflict-resolving ensembles (MI-CWA, VMI-CWA, MI-SVRE) in Fig. 6b. All three logit-averaging strategies fail to exhibit any log-linear scaling: their ASR stagnates at a low plateau regardless of ensemble size, even when augmented with input diversity (DI) or variance tuning (VMI). In contrast, all three conflict-resolving strategies (MI-CWA, VMI-CWA, MI-SVRE) demonstrate clear log-linear ASR growth with T, matching the behavior of the paper's main SSA-CWA method. This confirms that the scaling law is unlocked specifically by gradient alignment mechanisms, not by raw compute or input augmentation alone. The SSA-CWA combination (SSA + CWA) achieves the strongest absolute ASR among all tested configurations, validating the choice of combining frequency-domain augmentation with second-order gradient alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CWA (Chen et al., ICLR 2024) for gradient alignment and SSA (Long et al., ECCV 2022) for frequency-domain input augmentation, combining them into the SSA-CWA attack algorithm. It departs from prior ensemble attack work, which typically fixes the surrogate count at 4–8 models and treats ensembling as a static technique, by systematically scaling the ensemble cardinality from 1 to 64 and identifying gradient conflict as the critical barrier that prevents naive logit-averaging from benefiting from scale. The paper also extends the scaling-laws paradigm from LLM pretraining (Kaplan et al., 2020) to the adversarial-attack domain, where the 'training data' are surrogate models rather than text tokens, and further generalizes the attack target from standard classifiers to CLIP-based vision encoders underlying commercial MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery of a robust log-linear scaling law (ASR ∝ log T) reframes adversarial robustness evaluation: rather than designing ever-more-intricate attack algorithms on small ensembles, the field should treat ensemble scaling as a fundamental, measurable threat axis. The 85–90% ASR achieved on GPT-4o and Qwen3-VL-235B, contrasted with Claude-3.5-Sonnet's 31%, provides the first systematic robustness hierarchy across SOTA MLLMs under a unified protocol. Open questions include whether the log-linear regime persists beyond T=64 or saturates earlier for more diverse surrogate pools, whether defenses can be specifically designed to counter the semantic-feature distillation that scaling induces, and whether the law holds for non-CLIP vision encoders or video/multimodal inputs.
