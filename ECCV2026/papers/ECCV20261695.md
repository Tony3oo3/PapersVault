## Problem

Existing K-adaptive Slot Attention methods suffer from two coupled limitations: they lack an explicit criterion for slot-binding quality, causing slots to capture coarse, diffuse regions rather than fine-grained object structures, and they introduce a slot-count penalty into the reconstruction objective, creating a conflicting optimization signal between reducing active slots and maintaining fidelity. As a result, K-adaptive methods still lag significantly behind strong K-fixed baselines, especially on real-world scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, encodes it with a frozen DINO ViT-B/16 into N patch-level features, and produces a set of K_max slots (where K_max = max object count + 1 for background). During training, a quality-guided selection mask determines which slots participate in a gated decoder that reconstructs the DINO features; during inference, no selection is applied and all K_max slots compete via argmax on the slot-attention map, yielding a per-image variable number of object regions. For the property-prediction task, the same slots are fed into MLP or linear predictors to output object class labels and 2-D center coordinates.

## Method

QASA introduces an unsupervised Slot-Quality metric defined as the ratio of a slot's attention mass within its winning region (where it is the argmax slot) to its total mass, capturing how purely a slot binds to its own object. A Quality-Guided Slot Selection algorithm greedily adds slots in descending quality order until a coverage-rate threshold ρ is met, with a novelty constraint that skips slots whose mass largely overlaps already-covered tokens. The selected slots are fed into a gated decoder (either a Transformer with dual K/V and logit gating, or an MLP with hard logit masking) that suppresses unselected slots during reconstruction, thereby decoupling slot selection from the reconstruction objective and eliminating the slot-count-penalty conflict. At inference, all slots compete freely over tokens via the slot-attention map, producing a K-adaptive partition without any selection step.

## Results

On COCO, QASA (Transformer decoder, K-adaptive) achieves 36.7 mBOi and 35.0 mIoU, surpassing the strongest K-fixed baseline SPOT (35.0 mBOi, 33.0 mIoU) and outperforming K-adaptive baselines AdaSlot (27.4 mBOi) and MetaSlot (29.5 mBOi) by 9.3 and 7.2 points respectively. On PASCAL VOC, QASA reaches 49.7 mBOi and 47.9 mIoU, again exceeding SPOT (48.3 mBOi). For object property prediction on COCO, QASA attains 59.6% top-1 and 85.0% top-5 classification accuracy, improving over the best baseline AdaSlot (49.3% top-1) by 10.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against K-adaptive baselines, QASA wins decisively: it outperforms AdaSlot by 9.3 mBOi on COCO (36.7 vs. 27.4) and MetaSlot by 7.2 mBOi (36.7 vs. 29.5), with an average 8.4% mBOi gain across all four datasets. Against the strongest K-fixed baseline SPOT, QASA wins on both real-world datasets (COCO: 36.7 vs. 35.0 mBOi; VOC: 49.7 vs. 48.3 mBOi) but trails slightly on the synthetic MOVi-C (46.9 vs. 47.3) and MOVi-E (39.1 vs. 40.1), which the authors attribute to the narrower object-count distribution in synthetic scenes favoring a well-chosen fixed K. QASA also surpasses DINOSAUR on COCO (36.7 vs. 31.6 mBOi) and VOC (49.7 vs. 44.0 mBOi), and exceeds SLATE on every reported metric. For property prediction, QASA (Transformer) leads all baselines on top-1 (59.6% vs. AdaSlot's 49.3%) and top-5 (85.0% vs. 79.0%) classification, though AdaSlot retains a small edge on R²-X (72.7 vs. 67.9); the MLP variant of QASA closes that gap (74.0 R²-X). The comparison with SPOT+FS+RC is noted as not directly comparable since that method uses an additional foreground/background indicator learned with extra supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

QASA extends the Slot Attention lineage (Locatello et al. 2020) through the DINO-based feature-reconstruction paradigm of DINOSAUR and the Transformer decoder with patch-order permutation from SPOT. Its key departure is from prior K-adaptive methods (AdaSlot, MetaSlot, CoSA), which either couple slot-count penalties to the reconstruction objective (creating conflicting gradients) or select slots via prominence-based criteria (PCA, codebook quantization). QASA instead decouples selection from reconstruction entirely, introducing an unsupervised slot-quality metric derived from the competitive attention structure itself, and a gated decoder that suppresses unselected slots without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a K-adaptive method surpasses K-fixed baselines on real-world data (COCO, VOC) challenges the working assumption that a fixed slot budget provides an essential inductive bias for object discovery. This opens the door to practical deployment where object counts vary widely and per-dataset K sweeps are infeasible. Limitations include: on synthetic data with concentrated object-count distributions, K-fixed methods still hold an edge; the quality thresholds (ρ, τ, μ) require per-dataset setting (though robustly); and the method is validated only within the Slot Attention family with ViT-B/16 + DINO, leaving open whether the quality metric transfers to video, larger backbones, or non-attention set-structured bottlenecks.
