## Problem

Existing attention linearization methods (QT-ViT, Performer, EfficientViT) achieve linear complexity but require full retraining or fine-tuning to be applied, making them impractical for large-scale pretrained vision transformers. This paper addresses the gap of applying linear attention to pretrained models in a completely training-free manner, using only a small unlabeled calibration set and no end-to-end backpropagation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261822/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained vision transformer backbones (CLIP, SigLip, DETR, RT-DETR) and takes standard RGB images and text prompts as input, producing class labels, bounding boxes, or image-text retrieval similarity scores depending on the downstream task. A key operational detail is that LATTE is resolution-adaptive: at inference time it can dynamically switch between the linearized and original softmax attention based on input sequence length, deploying the linear variant for high-resolution inputs (large N) and retaining exact softmax for low-resolution inputs. Calibration requires only a small unlabeled subset (e.g., subsamples of COCO training data) to tune the expansion center and normalization scalars per layer.

## Method

LATTE replaces softmax attention in a selected subset of transformer blocks with a second-order Taylor expansion of the exponential kernel centered on a tunable, input-adaptive anchor point α (rather than the fixed zero-point used by QT-ViT). The expansion yields a symmetric feature map ψ_m(x) = [(x_i/d^{1/4} + m)²] where m = √(1−α), reducing the quadratic similarity to an inner product in O(d) dimensions while preserving O(Nd²) overall complexity. A second contribution, Joint Post-Squaring Normalization (JPN), scales squared queries and keys by the product of their ℓ₂-norms in the squared space, inversely modulating attention scores by the joint token-pair energy to mimic softmax's dynamic range compression. Layer selection is performed via a hybrid cosine-distance + MSE importance score computed between original and linearized block activations; layers with the lowest sensitivity are linearized while the rest retain exact softmax. The entire procedure is training-free: only the scalar α, normalization factors δ_q/δ_k, and optionally a lightweight diagonal scaling vector σ on the output projection are calibrated on a small unlabeled set, with no gradient flow through the pretrained weights.

## Results

At 67% layer linearization, LATTE retains 95–98% of original model performance across all tested architectures. On RT-DETR (COCO), LATTE achieves mAP@0.5:0.95 of 46.7 vs. 40.6 for QT-ViT (original: 49.0). On CLIP (MSCOCO Captions), LATTE preserves 77.1/59.7 Text/Image Recall vs. 42.0/31.2 for QT-ViT (original: 79.4/61.1). LATTE also achieves up to 15× reduction in attention GFLOPs at T=1000 and up to 5.8× latency speedup over FlashAttention at long sequence lengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QT-ViT (the primary baseline), LATTE consistently wins across all four architectures: on RT-DETR it gains +6.1 mAP@0.5:0.95 (46.7 vs. 40.6), on CLIP it gains +35.1 Text Recall (77.1 vs. 42.0), and on SigLip it gains +5.6 Text Recall (87.9 vs. 82.3). In the multi-method comparison on RT-DETR (Fig. 2), LATTE outperforms both EfficientViT and Performer once more than half the layers are linearized, where the other methods degrade sharply while LATTE maintains stable accuracy. Against FlashAttention, LATTE is not a direct accuracy competitor but a speed comparison: at long sequence lengths (T ≥ 500), LATTE achieves up to 5.8×, 3.2×, and 3.8× latency speedups over FlashAttention depending on feature dimension, confirming its advantage in the long-sequence regime where quadratic attention becomes prohibitive. On cross-dataset generalization (Table 3), LATTE also dominates QT-ViT: on SugarCrepe it retains 95.91/95.51 Text/Image Recall at 75% linearization versus QT-ViT's catastrophic 5.30/10.20, and on CIFAR-10 it achieves 90.56% Top-1 vs. QT-ViT's 86.88% at 54% linearization.

## Relation to Prior Work

LATTE directly extends QT-ViT's quadratic Taylor expansion of softmax attention by replacing the fixed expansion point (zero) with a tunable, input-adaptive anchor calibrated on a small dataset, and introduces Joint Post-Squaring Normalization to stabilize the kernel's dynamic range. It also borrows the denominator-padding strategy from EfficientViT to maintain proper normalization within the linearized framework. The key conceptual departure is the training-free, post-hoc paradigm: whereas QT-ViT, Performer, and EfficientViT were designed and validated in training-from-scratch or fine-tuning settings, LATTE treats linearization as a layer-wise calibration problem that preserves all pretrained weights, positioning itself as a plug-and-play deployment tool rather than an architectural redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free framing is practically significant: it enables immediate acceleration of existing pretrained ViTs (CLIP, DETR, RT-DETR, SigLip) without the compute cost of retraining, making linear attention accessible to resource-constrained and edge-deployment scenarios. The 15× FLOP reduction at T=1000 and the ability to linearize >50% of layers while retaining >95% performance are compelling for high-resolution and long-sequence inference. However, the layer-selection heuristic (MSE + cosine distance) does not generalize uniformly across architectures (notably DINOv2), and the calibration procedure, while lightweight, still requires task-specific data. Open questions include whether more holistic, cross-layer sensitivity metrics could improve layer selection, and whether the approach scales to very large or NLP-centric transformer backbones beyond the brief RoBERTa experiment.
