## Problem

Zero-Shot Quantization for Object Detection (ZSQ-OD) must quantize detectors without access to the original training data, but existing noise-optimization-based methods (e.g., TSOD) suffer severe performance collapse in low-bit regimes (W4A4, W3A3). While off-the-shelf generative models show promise for diversifying synthetic training sets in classification, adapting them to OD introduces three unresolved challenges: the need for information-dense multi-instance images, extreme class-wise distribution imbalance, and noisy pseudo-labels that mislead QAT.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261367/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained YOLOv5 or YOLOv11 detector (trained on MS-COCO 2017) and an off-the-shelf text-to-image diffusion model as inputs, with no access to original images or ground-truth labels. It produces a quantized detector at target bit-widths (W8A8 through W3A3) using a 2k-image synthetic training set selected from a 160k pool generated via text prompts. The pseudo-labels are assigned by the same pretrained detector, and the teacher's soft predictions (not hard labels) serve as QAT targets.

## Method

GoodQ is a three-stage QAT pipeline. Stage 1 (Information-Dense Prompting) generates a 160k image pool using a diffusion model with prompts templated as 'A {D} photo of {Q} {C1} and {Q} {C2}' to ensure multiple object categories coexist per image, then assigns pseudo-labels via the pretrained detector. Stage 2 (Intrinsic Distribution-Aware Selection) estimates the original dataset's class-wise BBox distribution data-free from the detection head's bias parameters (achieving r=0.87 Pearson correlation with ground truth), then greedily selects 2k images to match this skewed distribution while prioritizing information-dense frames. Stage 3 (Teacher-guided Adaptive Noise Reduction) bypasses noisy pseudo-labels entirely by using the full-precision teacher's soft predictions as QAT targets, combined with QFocal-based adaptive weighting that up-weights instances most susceptible to quantization-induced error, and is combined with the standard distillation loss L_distill.

## Results

On MS-COCO validation with a 2k calibration set, GoodQ achieves 35.0/53.6 mAP/mAP50 on YOLOv5-s at W8A8, matching TSOD (35.4/54.0) which uses ground-truth labels. At W4A4, GoodQ reaches 21.0/36.1 (YOLOv5-s) and 32.2/45.9 (YOLOv11-s), substantially outperforming the label-free TSOD† variant (15.0/27.9 and 6.58/9.61 respectively). At the extreme W3A3 setting, GoodQ achieves 10.2/19.7 (YOLOv5-s) and 14.2/22.1 (YOLOv11-s), while TSOD† collapses to 2.56/5.20 and 0.18/0.40, demonstrating a 4–78× mAP advantage in the most challenging low-bit regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GoodQ is compared against three baselines: LSQ/LSQ+ (real-data QAT), TSOD (noise-optimized ZSQ-OD with GT labels), and TSOD† (label-free variant of TSOD). At W8A8, GoodQ is competitive with TSOD (35.0 vs 35.4 mAP on YOLOv5-s) and approaches LSQ with real data (32.6), despite having no access to original images or labels. At W4A4, GoodQ decisively wins: it outperforms TSOD† by 6.0 mAP on YOLOv5-s (21.0 vs 15.0) and by 25.6 mAP on YOLOv11-s (32.2 vs 6.58), and even surpasses TSOD (which uses GT labels) on YOLOv11-s by 18.1 mAP. At W3A3, the gap widens dramatically—GoodQ achieves 14.2 mAP on YOLOv11-s while TSOD† drops to 0.18, a 79× difference. The key takeaway is that GoodQ's generative-model-based approach is the only zero-shot method that maintains usable detection performance below W4A4, where all optimization-based baselines collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot quantization (ZSQ) paradigm from image classification (GenQ, MixQ) to object detection, building directly on TSOD's task-specific QAT objective (L_detect + L_distill) while replacing its noise-optimization-based data synthesis with off-the-shelf diffusion-generated images. The key departure is identifying three OD-specific challenges absent in classification—information density, class-wise imbalance, and pseudo-label noise—and addressing each with dedicated components (IDP, IDAS, TANR). The QAT loss design further incorporates QFocal-based adaptive weighting from Li et al. (NeurIPS 2020) to handle the noisy supervision inherent in generative-model-produced training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GoodQ demonstrates that ZSQ-OD can be pushed to W3A3, a regime where prior optimization-based methods (TSOD†) essentially collapse, making extreme-bit deployment on edge devices more feasible without access to original training data. The finding that data diversity (measured via CLIP embedding dispersion) is the critical factor for low-bit robustness, rather than mere sample count or BBox density, provides a useful design principle for future synthetic-data quantization work. Limitations include evaluation restricted to the YOLO family on MS-COCO with a fixed 2k calibration set; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to two-stage detectors, other datasets with different class structures, and whether the bias-term-based distribution estimation holds for non-COCO taxonomies.
