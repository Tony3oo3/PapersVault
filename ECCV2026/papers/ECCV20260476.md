## Problem

Existing mmWave point cloud HAR datasets are small-scale and collected under homogeneous single-source radar settings, so current methods overfit to device-specific statistical patterns and fail under real-world distribution shifts from heterogeneous radar sources (different models, frequency bands). The paper addresses this by introducing both a large-scale heterogeneous benchmark (UniMM-HAR) and a modeling strategy that explicitly decouples source-specific interference from action semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260476/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes mmWave radar point cloud sequences standardized to shape [32, 64, 5] (T frames, P points, channels x/y/z/Doppler/intensity), where each point carries a per-point radial Doppler velocity. A frozen CLIP text encoder processes action-category prompts using the template 'a mmWave point cloud of a person [CLS]' to produce K×C_text embeddings. The output is a 33-class action label (21 daily + 12 rehabilitation activities) predicted by fusing backbone classifier logits with text-similarity scores.

## Method

DAP-Net centers on the Dual-space Doppler Reparameterization (D²R) module, which uses action-consistent spatio-temporal Doppler patterns as source-invariant motion anchors. In the geometric space, DGR first computes a per-frame soft motion threshold via Doppler-sorted Soft Quantile (DSQ) with a learnable quantile parameter, then performs Tri-branch Motion-Aware Point Densification (TMPD): fast (high-Doppler) points are r-fold duplicated to increase density in motion-salient regions, slow points are preserved, and raw points are randomly sampled to fill a fixed target cardinality. In the feature space, MFR aggregates fast-point embeddings into a motion summary vector and applies FiLM-style channel-wise affine recalibration (scaling γ and shifting β via MLPs) to the dense point cloud features, enhancing motion-sensitive channels while suppressing source-specific bias. A Text Alignment Module (TAM) projects the global backbone feature into the CLIP text embedding space and fuses the resulting similarity scores with classifier logits, providing a stable semantic anchor. The entire D²R module is parameter-efficient and plug-and-play, compatible with any point cloud backbone (validated on PointMLP, UST-SSM, and PST-Transformer).

## Results

On UniMM-HAR, DAP-Net achieves 80.72% accuracy under the Cross-Subject (C-Sub) protocol and 81.82% under the Cross-Set (C-Set) protocol, outperforming all 10 baselines on both settings. The largest margin is on C-Sub, where DAP-Net exceeds the best baseline (3DIInAction, 73.43%) by 7.29 points. On C-Set, DAP-Net surpasses the strongest competitor (PST-Transformer, 79.70%) by 2.12 points. Notably, DAP-Net is the only method that simultaneously achieves >80% on both protocols, whereas baselines that excel on one protocol (e.g., DGCNN: 71.70% C-Sub but 52.18% C-Set) degrade sharply on the other.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAP-Net wins against all 10 baselines on both C-Sub and C-Set protocols. On C-Sub, it beats the strongest baseline 3DIInAction (73.43%) by 7.29 points and UST-SSM (71.50%) by 9.22 points; on C-Set, it surpasses PST-Transformer (79.70%) by 2.12 points and PointMLP (78.13%) by 3.69 points. A striking pattern emerges: dense point cloud methods designed for LiDAR (PointNet, DGCNN, PointMLP) and temporal point cloud methods (PST-Transformer, 3DIInAction, UST-SSM) show severe inconsistency across protocols—e.g., DGCNN scores 71.70% on C-Set but only 52.18%, while 3DIInAction scores 73.43% on C-Sub but drops to 57.81% on C-Set—indicating they overfit to source-specific patterns. mmWave-specific baselines (RadHAR at 42.49%/48.47%, FastHAR at 53.72%/61.16%) perform worst overall, confirming that single-source mmWave methods fail under heterogeneous radar configurations. DAP-Net's consistent >80% on both protocols demonstrates that its Doppler-guided reparameterization and text alignment effectively decouple source-specific bias from action semantics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes dense point cloud backbones (PointMLP, PST-Transformer, UST-SSM) originally designed for LiDAR or depth-camera data to handle the sparsity and noise of mmWave radar point clouds, using Doppler velocity as a motion prior that prior mmWave HAR methods largely ignored (using it only for frame selection or preprocessing). Conceptually, the D2R module draws on FiLM-style conditional feature modulation and quantile-based adaptive thresholding, while TAM borrows CLIP-style cross-modal alignment, but the specific combination of Doppler-guided geometric densification plus text-space anchoring for heterogeneous radar sources is new. The UniMM-HAR dataset unifies three previously independent single-source benchmarks (RadHAR, mRI, MM-Fi) into a heterogeneous multi-source evaluation, departing from the homogeneous single-source assumption that underlies all prior mmWave point cloud HAR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The heterogeneous multi-source framing addresses a genuine deployment gap: real-world mmWave sensing involves mixed device models and frequency bands, yet all prior methods were validated under single-source assumptions, making cross-source generalization an open and practically critical question. The plug-and-play D2R module (9–15% gains across three backbones) offers a low-cost path to improve existing mmWave pipelines without architectural redesign. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond three radar sources, whether scaling TAM to LLM-based text encoders yields further gains, and whether the 33-action, 62-subject scale is sufficient to capture the full diversity of real multi-source deployments. A limitation is that all evaluation is confined to UniMM-HAR, so external validation on truly independent heterogeneous hardware remains to be done.
