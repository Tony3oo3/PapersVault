## Problem

Through-plane MRI super-resolution faces an efficiency-fidelity trade-off: feed-forward regressors are fast but oversmooth at large slice-thicknesses, while sampling-based generative methods improve fidelity at prohibitive inference cost. Existing approaches also assume discrete scale degradation, failing to generalize across the continuous slice-thickness variation encountered in clinical practice where slice-profile integration governs through-plane bandwidth loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260725/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DRIFT operates as a 2D slice-wise model on 128×128 patches, taking thick-slice MRI patches with continuous slice-thickness metadata (input T_i and target T_hr) as input. The model is applied independently to each slice orthogonal to the degraded axis, and predicted slices are composed back into a 3D isotropic volume at T_hr resolution. Slice-thickness is treated as a continuous variable (sampled from U(T_hr, 6.0] mm during training) rather than a discrete scale factor, and the inverse thickness τ = 1/T is used as the conditioning signal to reduce numerical scale disparity.

## Method

DRIFT is a two-stage thickness-conditioned rectified flow framework. Stage 1 (Anatomical Projection Network) deterministically maps LR patches to a coarse HR manifold via a CNN trained with Charbonnier + SSIM loss, shortening the residual transport distance for Stage 2. Stage 2 learns a straight-line rectified-flow velocity field from the APN output to the HR target, with slice-thickness and time injected via AdaGN conditioning; the output layer is zero-initialized so training begins as identity refinement. A Physics-Aware Difficulty (PAD) metric computed from slice-thickness metadata (PAD = 1 − T_hr/T_i) drives an Adaptive Integration Scheduler that allocates 0–15 ODE steps without any image-dependent difficulty network. A Consistent Endpoint Trajectory Alignment (CETA) loss regularizes one-step endpoint predictions between proximal thickness pairs (ΔT = 1 mm) to enforce thickness-consistent reconstructions, and an endpoint-biased U-shaped timestep distribution (α = 2.0) concentrates training on the most sensitive refinement regions.

## Results

On fixed-scale through-plane MRI SR, DRIFT achieves 37.64/0.952 PSNR/SSIM on HCP ×6 (4.2 mm) and 35.93/0.936 on HCP ×8 (5.6 mm), outperforming the strongest fixed-scale baseline SwinIR by up to 3.54 dB in PSNR. Under arbitrary-scale settings (continuous thickness), DRIFT surpasses the best INR-based baselines (LTE, SA-INR) by 3.29–6.29 dB in PSNR at 6.0 mm and 4.71–4.72 dB at 6.5 mm, while remaining robust at the out-of-distribution 6.5 mm thickness. In terms of efficiency, DRIFT achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.043) at ~20 s per volume on HCP ×8, improving over ArSSR (0.112) by ×3.3 while being ×14 faster, and uses only 8–13 NFEs versus 15 (ResShift) or 2000 (TPDM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRIFT wins decisively across all 10 baselines on every dataset and scale. Against fixed-scale regression baselines, DRIFT improves PSNR by 3.54 dB over SwinIR on HCP ×6, 2.59 dB on MIND ×5, and 1.11 dB on IDEAS ×5, with SSIM gains up to 0.054; AFCM and TPDM lag further behind (TPDM drops to 22.50 PSNR on MIND ×5). Against diffusion-based ResShift, DRIFT gains 4.75 dB on HCP ×6 and 5.59 dB on HCP ×8 while using fewer NFEs (8–13 vs. 15 fixed). Against arbitrary-scale INR baselines, DRIFT leads by 6.29 dB over LTE on HCP ×8.57 (6.0 mm) and 4.71–4.72 dB at the out-of-distribution 6.5 mm thickness, where INR methods (LIIF, LTE, ArSSR) show only marginal improvements. In zero-shot fastMRI evaluation, DRIFT outperforms the BME-X foundation model on no-reference metrics (sharpness 0.291 vs. 0.238; NIQE 5.30 vs. 7.22; BRISQUE 21.16 vs. 62.46). On efficiency, DRIFT achieves ×3.3 lower LPIPS than ArSSR at ×14 lower runtime, and ×6 better LPIPS than TPDM at ×450 lower runtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRIFT extends the two-stage posterior-mean-then-transport paradigm of PMRF [20] to through-plane MRI super-resolution, replacing the generic posterior-mean estimator with a thickness-conditioned Anatomical Projection Network (APN) that exploits the deterministic, slice-profile-governed degradation of MRI acquisition. The rectified-flow refinement stage builds directly on Flow Matching [17] and Rectified Flow [18], but departs from image-dependent adaptive schedulers (e.g., AdaDiffSR) by deriving the ODE step budget from a closed-form physics metric (PAD) computed from slice-thickness metadata alone. The training degradation model is also novel relative to prior through-plane SR work: instead of isotropic downsampling or simple averaging, DRIFT simulates thick-slice inputs via an SLR-derived slice-profile kernel, faithfully reproducing the stair-step morphology of clinical acquisitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DRIFT addresses a practical clinical bottleneck—thick-slice MRI volumes with stair-step artifacts—by showing that a single model can handle continuous slice-thickness variation while adaptively allocating inference cost, achieving the best perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.043) at ~20 s/volume versus TPDM's ~450× slower runtime with 6× worse LPIPS. The finding that a simple metadata-based difficulty metric outperforms image-dependent scoring networks is a clean conceptual result for physics-informed adaptive inference. A key limitation is that main experiments fix the target thickness to each dataset's native resolution; extending to variable target resolutions and cross-protocol generalization remains open. The zero-shot transfer to real clinical scans (fastMRI, in-house) without retraining suggests practical deployability, though broader multi-vendor validation would strengthen the claim.
