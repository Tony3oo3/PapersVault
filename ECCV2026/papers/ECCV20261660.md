## Problem

Progress in 3D facade semantic segmentation is limited by the absence of large-scale, geographically diverse benchmarks with standardized semantics and centimeter-level geometric accuracy. Existing datasets cover narrow regions, use inconsistent taxonomies, or lack the precision needed for fine-grained architectural parsing, making cross-country generalization evaluation unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261660/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes centimeter-accurate 3D point clouds (xyz) acquired via static TLS (Leica RTC360, BLK360) and mobile MLS (Velodyne HDL-64E/VLP-16) across UK, Germany, and Singapore, totaling 2.7B annotated points. The primary evaluation uses only xyz coordinates to isolate geometric difficulty, with an optional ablation adding projected RGB and laser intensity. Outputs are per-point semantic labels at three hierarchical granularities: LoFG3 (15 fine-grained architectural classes), LoFG2 (5 functional categories), and LoFG1 (single facade class), grounded in CityGML, IFC, and AAT standards.

## Method

UnderOneFacade is a benchmark and empirical study rather than a new architecture. It systematically evaluates six representative point cloud segmentation models—PointNet++, KPConv (point-based), DGCNN (graph-based), and PTv1, PTv3, OctFormer (transformer-based)—under a unified 100-epoch training protocol with unweighted cross-entropy and identical preprocessing. The evaluation protocol spans three axes: per-country and overall (sensor-heterogeneous) splits, hierarchical semantic granularity (LoFG2 vs. LoFG3), and zero-shot cross-continental transfer (Europe→Asia and Asia→Europe). Key findings include DGCNN achieving the best overall mIoU (33.4) despite being a 2018 architecture, transformer models underperforming on fine-grained facade elements, radiometric features helping small elements but sometimes degrading overall accuracy, and a clear asymmetry in cross-continental transfer driven by class distribution imbalance.

## Results

On the most challenging LoFG3 fine-grained benchmark, the best model (DGCNN) achieves only 33.4 mIoU overall, with per-country mIoU ranging from 28.3 (UK) to 33.4 (Germany). Overall accuracy (OA) peaks at 77.8% (DGCNN, UK) but drops to 41.5% (KPConv, Singapore), revealing severe cross-country degradation. Notably, the graph-based DGCNN outperforms all three transformer-based baselines (PTv1: 15.1, PTv3: 27.3, OctFormer: 27.3 mIoU overall), indicating that current transformer architectures have not yet effectively captured facade geometric structure. Per-class F1 scores confirm the long-tail challenge: dominant classes like wall (71.7 F1) and terrain (73.8 F1) are well-segmented, while rare elements like stairs (9.1 F1) and deco (9.2 F1) remain nearly unsolved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGCNN (graph-based, 2018) achieves the best overall mIoU of 33.4, outperforming the strongest transformer (PTv3/OctFormer at 27.3) by 6.1 points and PointNet++ (29.7) by 3.7 points. Among transformers, PTv1 is the weakest overall (15.1 mIoU) despite achieving the highest per-country mIoU on Germany (41.6), suggesting strong overfitting to a single region. OctFormer leads on Singapore (34.6 mIoU) while DGCNN leads on Germany (33.4 mIoU), showing no single architecture dominates all regions. KPConv performs poorly overall (15.0 mIoU) with near-zero F1 on most fine-grained classes, indicating its deformable kernel design is ill-suited to thin facade structures. The key takeaway is that the long-standing assumption that newer transformer architectures should outperform earlier point/graph methods does not hold for fine-grained facade segmentation, with the 2018 DGCNN remaining the strongest baseline across the full cross-continental benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct cross-continental extension of ZAHA (Wysocki et al., WACV 2025), inheriting its Level-of-Facade-Granularity (LoFG) hierarchical taxonomy and the German subset while adding UK and Singapore acquisitions to achieve a 4.5× scale increase. It departs from prior facade benchmarks (ArCH, TUM-FAÇADE, ZAHA) that were geographically narrow, single-sensor, or lacked architecturally grounded class definitions, by unifying multi-sensor (TLS + MLS) point clouds under a harmonized taxonomy aligned with CityGML, IFC, and AAT standards. The empirical evaluation reuses well-established point cloud architectures (PointNet++, KPConv, DGCNN, PTv1/v3, OctFormer) as baselines rather than proposing a new method, positioning the contribution squarely as a benchmark and empirical study.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

At 2.7B annotated points across three continents, UnderOneFacade is the largest facade segmentation benchmark to date and enables the first systematic cross-continental generalization evaluation in this domain. The finding that the best model (DGCNN) reaches only ~33 mIoU on fine-grained LoFG3, and that transformer architectures underperform a 2018 graph-based method, signals that fine-grained facade understanding remains far from solved and that current architectures may not capture the geometric structure of repetitive, thin architectural elements. Key open questions include whether the observed cross-continental asymmetry (Asia→Europe outperforming Europe→Asia) is driven by class balance or architectural style, how to disentangle sensor modality from geographic domain shift, and whether the hierarchical LoFG taxonomy can be extended to additional cities and sensing setups.
