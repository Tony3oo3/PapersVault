## Problem

Existing interactive 3D segmentation methods suffer from two persistent failure modes: coarse single-resolution voxel grids blur fine object boundaries under limited clicks, and confusing background structures trigger high-confidence false positives. Both issues are amplified under cross-dataset distribution shifts (e.g., dense RGB-D reconstructions vs. sparse LiDAR scans), where fixed refinement heuristics and purely click-driven decoding generalize poorly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262254/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a point cloud voxelized into a coarse grid (voxel size v) together with a sequence of user clicks (positive or negative) in metric coordinates, and iteratively produces a binary per-voxel segmentation mask by thresholding sigmoid logits. A key structural detail is that refinement operates on a finer sub-grid (v_f = v/η, η=2) restricted to a click-centered ROI with an adaptive radius, then max-aggregates fine logits back to the coarse grid via a residual fusion with weight α=0.7, so the final output remains at coarse resolution while benefiting from local high-resolution decoding.

## Method

NegROI couples two mechanisms within a shared sparse-voxel encoder and Easy3D two-way transformer decoder: (i) click-centric multi-resolution ROI refinement, which re-voxelizes only points within an adaptive-radius ROI around the current click at a finer resolution, decodes fine logits with the weight-shared encoder/decoder, and fuses them back to coarse logits via max-aggregation and a residual update; and (ii) scene-conditioned negative prompts, where K learnable queries cross-attend to scene tokens to form background prototypes that are appended to the interaction token sequence to explicitly suppress hard false positives. An uncertainty-driven selective refinement step filters ROI points by a coarse uncertainty score (u_j = 1 − 2|σ(s_j) − 0.5|) to concentrate fine decoding on ambiguous boundary regions. Training adds a boundary-aware hard-negative loss that supervises the averaged negative-prompt attention to focus on the top-k highest-confidence false-positive voxels adjacent to ground-truth boundaries, plus a diversity regularizer penalizing off-diagonal cosine similarity among negative prototypes to prevent prompt collapse.

## Results

On the unified train-on-ScanNet40 protocol, NegROI achieves IoU@1 of 72.1% on ScanNet40, 66.9% on S3DIS, and 47.5% on KITTI-360, outperforming the strongest baseline Easy3D by +3.9, +1.2, and +1.2 points respectively at the single-click budget. At IoU@10, NegROI reaches 83.6% (ScanNet40), 87.8% (S3DIS), and 87.7% (KITTI-360). Under the ScanNet20 single-click protocol, NegROI attains mAP of 59.6 (seen-only) and 43.0 (seen+unseen), surpassing Easy3D (56.1 / 39.2) and AGILE3D (53.5 / 24.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NegROI outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and click budget. Against the closest competitor Easy3D [25], NegROI gains +3.9 IoU@1 on ScanNet40 (72.1 vs 68.2), +1.2 on S3DIS (66.9 vs 65.7), and +1.2 on KITTI-360 (47.5 vs 46.3), with the gap widening at intermediate budgets (e.g., +3.9 IoU@3 on KITTI-360: 71.4 vs 66.7). Against AGILE3D [34], the advantage is substantially larger: +9.1 IoU@1 on ScanNet40, +8.4 on S3DIS, and +12.7 on KITTI-360, reflecting AGILE3D's weaker handling of sparse outdoor geometry. InterObject3D [12] and Point-SAM [37] trail by 28–45 IoU@1 points, indicating that attention-guided decoding without explicit background prompting or fine-grid refinement struggles under domain shift. Under the ScanNet20 single-click protocol (Table 2), NegROI also beats the non-interactive Mask3D [24] by +8.1 mAP (seen-only) and +37.7 mAP (seen+unseen), confirming that the interactive framework's negative prompting and ROI refinement benefit even the first-click prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

NegROI builds directly on Easy3D's two-way transformer decoder as its core interaction mechanism, extending it with two orthogonal innovations: scene-conditioned negative prompts (conceptually borrowing the prompt-query paradigm from SAM-style 2D segmentation and open-vocabulary 3D methods) and click-centric fine-grid ROI refinement (adapting the 'refine-where-needed' principle from 2D interactive segmentation, e.g., PointRend, to 3D voxel grids). The boundary-aware hard negative mining for prompt supervision draws on the 2D interactive segmentation literature's focus on hard regions, while the diversity regularizer addresses a prompt-collapse issue analogous to those in multi-query detection. Unlike prior interactive 3D methods (InterObject3D, AGILE3D, Point-SAM) that rely solely on click-driven decoding at a single resolution, NegROI explicitly couples local multi-resolution refinement with structured background suppression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practically important failure modes in interactive 3D segmentation—boundary blurring under sparse clicks and hard false positives from confusing background—that are amplified under cross-dataset distribution shifts (dense RGB-D vs. sparse LiDAR). The modular design (negative prompts + ROI refinement) is composable and could be grafted onto other interactive 3D frameworks, and the cross-dataset evaluation protocol (train on ScanNet40, test on S3DIS/KITTI-360) provides a useful robustness benchmark. Limitations include reliance on oracle click simulation, added computational cost from the ROI branch, and the fact that the method remains class-agnostic; the authors note that fully differentiable ROI selection and click-driven test-time adaptation remain open.
