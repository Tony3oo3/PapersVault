## Problem

3D medical vision-language pre-training is limited by three compounding challenges: severe data scarcity (only ~78k paired CT-report pairs versus hundreds of millions for natural-image CLIP), prohibitive computational costs from volumetric inputs, and a large domain shift between the long radiology reports used during training and the short prompts used at inference for zero-shot classification. As a result, natural-image VLE recipes do not transfer directly to 3D medical imaging, and no prior work has combined self-supervised and vision-language objectives or introduced a text-generation objective in this domain.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D CT volumes resampled to 2 mm isotropic and cropped to 160×160×160 for vision-language objectives (1 mm isotropic sub-crops for the MAE objective), paired with radiology reports that are LLM-restructured into eight clinical subsections with each sentence labelled as positive or negative finding. The Primus-M vision encoder produces dense 48×48×48 token embeddings and a global embedding via multi-head attention pooling; the CXR-BERT text encoder produces sentence-level embeddings projected into the shared text space. Downstream, these embeddings support zero-shot classification via short prompts (e.g., "{abnormality} present"), report generation through an LLaVA-style adapter feeding Qwen2.5-1B, linear classification probes, retrieval ranking, and fine-tuned semantic segmentation masks.

## Method

COLIPRI jointly optimises four objectives in a staged fashion: (1) standard CLIP contrastive alignment between global image and report embeddings, (2) a novel Opposite Sentence Loss (OSL) that constructs binary positive/negative sentence pairs (e.g., "lung mass present" vs. "No lung mass present") and trains a temperature-scaled softmax to explicitly learn presence-versus-absence discrimination, directly closing the long-report-to-short-prompt domain gap, (3) a parallel (masked) radiology report generation objective using a lightweight EVA-02 decoder with cross-attention to the vision encoder, where clinical subsection headers remain unmasked to guide generation, and (4) a vision-only masked autoencoder objective trained on unpaired CT volumes (NLST) to learn dense spatial features. The vision-language and vision-only objectives are trained in alternating batches to avoid masking interference. The final COLIPRI-CRM model combines all four losses (L = 0.5·L_CLIP + 0.5·L_OSL + λ_RRG·L_RRG + λ_MAE·L_MAE), pre-trained on CT-RATE (paired) and NLST (unpaired), and achieves state-of-the-art results across zero-shot classification, report generation, retrieval, classification probing, and semantic segmentation.

## Results

On classification probing, COLIPRI-CRM achieves 61.28 AUPRC / 86.12 AUROC on CT-RATE and 52.55 AUPRC / 76.57 AUROC on RAD-ChestCT, exceeding the strongest baseline Merlin by ~6.5 AUPRC and ~3.5 AUROC points on CT-RATE. For zero-shot classification, COLIPRI-CM reaches 80.52 AUROC on CT-RATE and COLIPRI-CRM reaches 73.23 AUROC on RAD-ChestCT, outperforming fVLM (77.8 / 68.0 AUROC) without requiring segmentation masks at inference. In report-to-image retrieval, COLIPRI-CRM achieves Recall@5 of 35.1% versus 2.9% for CT-CLIP. On segmentation, COLIPRI-CRM matches or exceeds the SOTA MAE pre-training baseline across LiTS, Lung, HVS, and KiTS23 after fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COLIPRI-CRM outperforms all 3D medical VLE baselines across both classification datasets: it beats Merlin (the strongest prior 3D VLE) by 6.5 AUPRC and 3.5 AUROC on CT-RATE, and by 7.3 AUPRC and 5.7 AUROC on RAD-ChestCT. Against CT-CLIP, the improvement is dramatic (~35 AUPRC points on CT-RATE), though the authors note CT-CLIP underperforms in their probing setup compared to its originally reported numbers. The 2D baselines MI2 and Curia are far weaker (AUPRC < 50), confirming that 3D-native pre-training is essential. In zero-shot classification, COLIPRI-CM surpasses fVLM by 2.7 AUROC on CT-RATE (80.52 vs 77.8) and COLIPRI-CRM surpasses fVLM by 5.2 AUROC on RAD-ChestCT (73.23 vs 68.0), while fVLM degrades 10 points between the two datasets. For segmentation, COLIPRI-CRM matches or slightly exceeds the pure MAE pre-training baseline (e.g., 81.11 vs 80.32 DSC on LiTS at 250k steps) but still trails the ResEnc-L CNN reference (88.17 on KiTS23), indicating transformer architectures remain behind CNNs for dense prediction. In retrieval, COLIPRI-CRM's Recall@5 of 35.1% is over 12× that of CT-CLIP (2.9%), with the MAE objective contributing the largest single gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

COLIPRI extends the CLIP contrastive pre-training paradigm to 3D medical imaging by combining it with two previously unexploited objectives in this domain: a radiology report generation (RRG) loss following the image-captioner formulation of Tschannen et al., and a vision-only masked autoencoder (MAE) objective for leveraging unpaired CT volumes. The key methodological departure from prior 3D medical VLEs (CT-CLIP, Merlin, fVLM) is the Opposite Sentence Loss (OSL), which explicitly trains the model to discriminate short presence/absence prompts—the format used at zero-shot inference—rather than only aligning with the long, multi-sentence reports seen during training. The paper is also the first in 3D medical imaging to jointly optimize self-supervised and vision-language objectives, bridging a gap the authors identify between the natural-image VLE literature and the 3D medical setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COLIPRI establishes a new SOTA baseline across five distinct downstream tasks (classification probing, zero-shot classification, report generation, retrieval, and segmentation) for 3D chest CT, and the OSL is a simple, general-purpose technique that could transfer to any domain where training captions are long but inference prompts are short. However, the authors note that clinical accuracy metrics remain well below practical thresholds, the RRG objective contributes minimally to report generation quality (its main benefit appears to be retrieval), and a substantial gap persists between linear-probe and zero-shot classification performance. Open questions include whether the OSL generalizes beyond medical imaging, whether scaling to larger and more diverse CT datasets would close the clinical gap, and whether transformer encoders can eventually match the dense-prediction performance of CNN backbones like ResEnc-L.
