## Problem

Existing controllable video generation methods for egocentric hand-object interactions rely on dense 2D point tracks or implicit pose representations that collapse 3D geometry into spatially ambiguous signals, causing severe motion inconsistencies and hallucinated artifacts under the frequent mutual occlusions inherent to first-person views. No prior approach provides an explicit, occlusion-aware 3D control mechanism that preserves structural integrity while supporting fine-grained articulation and cross-embodiment generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260408/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference RGB image and a sequence of sparse 3D hand joint trajectories (up to 42 joints per hand in camera coordinates, projected via known intrinsics) as control signals, and produces a temporally coherent egocentric video with the specified hand-object interaction. The 3D joints are unconstrained spatial coordinates rather than rigid skeletal parameters, enabling the same interface to drive both human and robotic hands. At inference, users can either supply full trajectory sequences or interactively drag individual joints in 3D space for fine-grained micro-manipulation.

## Method

Built on the WAN 2.1 flow-matching DiT backbone with LoRA (rank 64), the method injects control through two lightweight (~20k parameter) embedding streams. The first stream extracts occlusion-aware motion features: in the source frame, local VAE latent context is aggregated around each joint via Gaussian heatmaps while a pairwise occlusion penalty (spatial overlap × depth-ordering sigmoid) suppresses features from occluded joints; these purified features are then propagated to target frames using a differentiable Z-buffer that applies softmax over log-heatmap intensity plus a learnable depth-weighted term to resolve foreground/background conflicts. The second stream constructs 3D geometric embeddings by fusing sinusoidal positional encodings of projected 3D coordinates with learnable per-joint identity embeddings, splatting them onto the spatial grid via the same Gaussian heatmaps, and processing the result through a causal Conv3D head to enforce temporal consistency. Both streams are concatenated with the noisy latent and visual condition tensor along the channel dimension before entering the DiT blocks. Training uses 1M Ego4D clips annotated via an automated YOLO + WiLoR + MANO pipeline, with 5% stochastic joint masking to ensure robustness to tracker failures.

## Results

On Ego4D, the proposed method achieves the best scores across all metrics: FID 67.70, FVD 259.99, PSNR 15.86, SSIM 0.443, MPJPE 1.42 mm, and MPVPE 1.70 mm. On EgoDex, it similarly leads with FID 39.62, FVD 174.73, PSNR 24.74, SSIM 0.846, MPJPE 1.80 mm, and MPVPE 1.82 mm. The method achieves at least a 16% FVD reduction over MotionStream on Ego4D and a 68% MPJPE reduction over WAN-Move* on EgoDex, while also winning a 2AFC user study (30 participants, 30 sequences) on both perceived quality and motion accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all four baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the closest competitor WAN-Fun (pose-based, ControlNet-style), the gap is modest on EgoDex (FID 39.62 vs 39.92, MPJPE 1.80 vs 1.85) but substantial on Ego4D, where WAN-Fun's 2D skeleton projections suffer from topological ambiguity, yielding 14% worse FVD and 19% higher MPJPE. Against track-based methods WAN-Move and MotionStream, the advantage is dramatic in hand accuracy: MPJPE drops from 9.11–9.99 (Ego4D) and 5.21–5.76 (EgoDex) to 1.42 and 1.80 respectively, a 68% reduction over WAN-Move* on EgoDex, because 2D point tracks discard depth and structural integrity. Mask-based Mask2IV performs worst overall (FID >93, MPJPE >7.6 on both datasets), confirming that 2D masks cannot resolve depth ambiguity in egocentric hand interactions. Notably, retraining WAN-Move on the authors' egocentric data (WAN-Move*) improves its FVD slightly but has negligible effect on hand accuracy, suggesting the limitation is inherent to the 2D track representation rather than data coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the WAN flow-matching video generation backbone with a lightweight LoRA-adapted control module, positioning itself against three existing motion-control paradigms: 2D point-track methods (WAN-Move, MotionStream), 2D mask/skeleton methods (Mask2IV, WAN-Fun/ControlNet), and implicit latent pose codes. The key conceptual departure is replacing these 2D or implicit representations with explicit sparse 3D hand joint coordinates, motivated by the need to resolve mutual occlusion ambiguities inherent in egocentric hand-object interactions. The occlusion-aware conditioning (source-frame feature filtering plus 3D Z-buffer-style propagation weighting) and the direct 3D geometric embedding stream are novel architectural contributions that have no direct counterpart in prior track- or pose-based control literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides the first large-scale (1M-clip) egocentric video dataset with accurate 3D hand trajectories, filling a critical gap for training and benchmarking controllable egocentric generators. The cross-embodiment result—controlling diverse robotic hands with a single finetuned model using the same 3D joint interface—suggests a practical pathway toward unified human-to-robot video world models for dexterous manipulation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-hand or full-body interactions, whether the 20k-parameter control module remains effective at higher resolutions or longer temporal horizons, and whether the occlusion-aware weighting generalizes beyond the specific MANO parameterization used here.
