## Problem

The Muon optimizer, which accelerates training by orthogonalizing momentum matrices, achieves strong early-stage speedups on Diffusion Transformers but plateaus in late-stage convergence, failing to surpass AdamW in final generative quality. The paper identifies the root cause: standard DiT implementations concatenate functionally distinct weights (QKV, AdaLN, FFN) into unified tensors for efficiency, and applying Muon's Newton-Schulz orthogonalization to these fused matrices induces implicit subspace coupling that distorts update directions across unrelated parameter blocks.

## Input / Output

The model performs class-conditional image generation at 256×256 resolution in the latent space of a pretrained VAE (VA-VAE or SD-VAE). During training, the network receives a noisy latent sample x_t (linearly interpolated between data and Gaussian noise), a timestep t, and a class conditioning signal c, and regresses the target vector field (z − x) via a flow-matching objective. At inference, generation proceeds by integrating the learned probability-flow ODE backward from t=1 to t=0 with 30 NFEs and model guidance, yielding a 256×256 RGB image.

## Method

CMuon (Chunked Muon) modifies the Muon optimizer by partitioning fused 2D weight matrices into their original functional sub-components before orthogonalization: QKV projections are split into 3 chunks of [1152,1152], FFN gate+up into 2 chunks of [3072,1152], and AdaLN modulation into 6 chunks of [1152,1152] (for DiT-XL with d=1152). Each chunk is independently orthogonalized via Newton-Schulz iterations, eliminating the shared preconditioner that vanilla Muon imposes across heterogeneous blocks. The paper provides a formal analysis showing that stacked Muon replaces block-specific preconditioners (G_i^T G_i)^{-1/2} with a global (G^T G)^{-1/2}, mixing gradient covariance structures and distorting descent geometry when principal directions are misaligned. To preserve update magnitude, the Moonlight scaling factor is recomputed per-chunk so the total Frobenius norm of the stacked update remains unchanged, and an optional √N_chunk learning-rate rescaling further accelerates early-stage convergence without affecting final quality.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 256×256, CMuON achieves a FID of 1.18 on the 675M DiT-XL model at 200 epochs (30 NFE, VA-VAE), surpassing AdamW's 1.21 at 400 epochs — a >2× training speedup. On the 130M DiT-B model, CMuON reaches FID 3.03 at 200 epochs and 2.57 at 400 epochs, compared to AdamW's 3.49 and 2.78 respectively. Vanilla Muon matches or slightly beats AdamW in early training (e.g., FID 1.65 vs 1.66 at 80 epochs on DiT-XL) but plateaus, converging to nearly the same FID as AdamW by 200–400 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMuON consistently outperforms both baselines across all model scales, VAE choices, and training horizons. Against AdamW, CMuON achieves a 0.12 FID improvement at 200 epochs on DiT-XL (1.18 vs 1.30) and a 0.21 FID improvement at 400 epochs on DiT-B (2.57 vs 2.78), corresponding to more than a 2× reduction in training time to reach a target FID. Against vanilla Muon, CMuON closes the late-stage gap: while Muon plateaus at FID 1.29 at 200 epochs on DiT-XL (nearly matching AdamW's 1.30), CMuON continues to improve to 1.18. On DiT-B at 400 epochs, Muon ties AdamW exactly (2.78), whereas CMuON achieves 2.57. The advantage is consistent under both VA-VAE and SD-VAE, confirming the gains are not VAE-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Muon optimizer (Jordan et al., 2024), which replaces per-element adaptive scaling with matrix-level momentum orthogonalization via Newton-Schulz iterations, and positions itself within the broader LMO-based optimizer family (Scion, Gluon, PolarExpress). Its key departure is a diagnostic insight specific to Diffusion Transformer architectures: standard DiT implementations concatenate functionally distinct projections (AdaLN scale/shift, QKV, FFN gate+up) into single 2D tensors for efficiency, and applying Muon's joint orthogonalization to these fused matrices induces implicit cross-subspace coupling that distorts update directions. The proposed fix—Chunked Muon (CMuon)—simply partitions these tensors back into their original functional sub-components before orthogonalization, a modification that prior Muon-for-DiT work (HunyuanVideo, pixel MeanFlow) did not identify despite observing late-stage convergence plateaus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 2× training speedup over AdamW for DiTs is a substantial practical win, as DiT pre-training is computationally expensive and this reduction translates directly to lower cost for training foundation-scale generative models. The diagnosis of subspace interference from fused weight tensors is broadly applicable: any architecture that concatenates heterogeneous projections (common in efficient transformer implementations) may suffer similar degradation under Muon-style optimizers, making this insight relevant beyond DiTs. Open questions include whether the speedup persists at video-DiT scales (e.g., HunyuanVideo's 8.3B parameters), whether chunking interacts with other training accelerators like REPA, and whether the √N_chunk rescaling trick generalizes to non-power-of-two chunk counts. The paper's results are limited to ImageNet-256 class-conditional generation, so transfer to text-to-image or video settings remains unverified.
