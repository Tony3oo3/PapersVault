## Problem

Converting dense multi-camera studio captures into compact, photorealistic 3D head assets at scale remains unsolved: per-subject optimization methods (NeRF, 3DGS fitting) are too slow for thousands of identities, while existing feed-forward methods use pixel-aligned representations whose memory and compute grow linearly with input view count and resolution, preventing full exploitation of high-resolution multi-camera rigs. The paper targets a method that scales gracefully across identities, views, and resolution while producing compact, renderable head assets without test-time optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes N calibrated, time-synchronized RGB images (1000×750 or 1024×667) along with their camera extrinsics and intrinsics, where N can range from 1 (monocular) to 16 or more views. It outputs a fixed set of 65K UV-parameterized 3D Gaussians anchored to a neutral head template (256×256 UV map with 23 channels encoding position offset, scale, quaternion, opacity, and first-order SH colors), plus a separate set of background Gaussians on a sphere template, and a compact 2D latent representation. The UV formulation means the output Gaussian count is independent of input view count or resolution, unlike pixel-aligned methods.

## Method

HeadsUp uses an encoder-decoder architecture where a cross-attention transformer aggregates multi-view features (patch embeddings concatenated with 6D Plücker camera embeddings, processed by parallel CNN encoders for foreground and background) into a compact 2D latent, which a convolutional decoder then maps to the UV Gaussian parameter map. The key design choice is anchoring all foreground Gaussians to a fixed neutral head template in a canonical coordinate system (Frankfurt plane), which provides a shared geometric prior and decouples output capacity from input resolution. Training is two-stage: first at 2× downscale with L1, multi-scale LPIPS, adversarial, position, mask, and TV losses, then a high-resolution finetuning stage that adds region-specific perceptual losses around the eyes and mouth and computes discriminator/LPIPS losses on downsampled outputs for stability. An explicit background model on a sphere template eliminates reliance on imperfect foreground masks, allowing the foreground Gaussians to focus on high-frequency details like hair strands and jewelry.

## Results

On the Internal10K dataset, HeadsUp (10 views, 65K Gaussians) achieves PSNR 29.25, LPIPS 0.117, AKD 10.39, and CSIM 0.922, substantially outperforming Avat3r (6 views, 1.1M Gaussians) which scores PSNR 24.37, LPIPS 0.362, AKD 12.99, and CSIM 0.787. On Ava-256 with 16 input views, HeadsUp reaches PSNR 26.13, LPIPS 0.110, AKD 4.27, and CSIM 0.914, compared to Avat3r's PSNR 22.31, LPIPS 0.359, AKD 6.10, and CSIM 0.775 at 6 views. Notably, HeadsUp uses only 65K 3D Gaussians—over 10× fewer than Avat3r's 0.8M–1.1M—while achieving these gains, and Avat3r is GPU-memory-limited to 6 views whereas HeadsUp scales to 16+ views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is Avat3r [Kirschstein et al., ICCV 2025], the state-of-the-art feed-forward 3D head reconstruction method. HeadsUp wins decisively on every metric except SSIM on Internal10K (0.821 vs. 0.831, a negligible 0.01 gap). On Internal10K, HeadsUp improves PSNR by +4.88 dB (29.25 vs. 24.37), reduces LPIPS by 68% (0.117 vs. 0.362), lowers AKD by 20% (10.39 vs. 12.99), and raises identity similarity CSIM by 0.135 (0.922 vs. 0.787)—all while using 65K Gaussians versus Avat3r's 1.1M. On Ava-256, the gap widens with more views: at 16 views HeadsUp gains +3.82 dB PSNR, reduces LPIPS by 69%, and improves CSIM by 0.139 over Avat3r's 6-view result. A critical practical advantage is that Avat3r is GPU-memory-bottlenecked at 6 views due to its pixel-aligned Gaussian formulation, whereas HeadsUp's UV-parameterized design scales to 16+ views without additional memory pressure.

## Relation to Prior Work

HeadsUp sits at the intersection of feed-forward 3D head reconstruction (LRMs, Avat3r, FastGHA) and UV-parameterized Gaussian representations (LAM, PanoLAM, FastAvatar), but departs from both in a key architectural way: by encoding multi-view features into a compact 2D latent via cross-attention before decoding to UV-space Gaussians, it decouples the number of output Gaussians from input view count and resolution. This contrasts with pixel-aligned methods like Avat3r and FastGHA, where Gaussian count scales linearly with input views, creating memory bottlenecks for dense captures. The explicit background Gaussian model also represents a departure from prior work that relies on imperfect foreground matting, and the two-stage training with region-specific perceptual losses is a practical contribution not seen in comparable feed-forward head methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a compact 65K-Gaussian UV representation, trained on 10K+ subjects, can match or exceed the quality of methods using 10× more Gaussians while accepting arbitrarily many high-resolution input views — a practical efficiency win for production multi-camera pipelines. The learned latent space enabling text-driven identity generation and blendshape animation without per-subject optimization suggests a path toward unified reconstruction-and-generation pipelines for digital humans. Open questions include whether the neutral template constrains extreme expressions or non-standard head shapes, how the method generalizes beyond controlled studio captures to in-the-wild or sparse-view settings, and whether the internal 10K dataset's scale advantage can be replicated on public data.
