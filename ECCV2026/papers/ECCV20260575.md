## Problem

Existing 2D-to-3D pose lifting methods rely on explicit MPJPE supervision that enforces joint coordinate accuracy but ignores implicit motion semantic consistency between 2D and 3D sequences, and they treat all 17 joints as a flat set without exploiting the hierarchical structure of the human body (intra-part uniformity and inter-part coordination). This leads to generated 3D poses that are numerically close to ground truth yet physiologically implausible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260575/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 2D skeleton sequence of shape T×J×2 (where T is the number of frames, e.g., 81 or 243 for Human3.6M and 27 or 81 for MPI-INF-3DHP, and J=17 joints), with 2D keypoints sourced either from a pre-trained CPN detector or from ground-truth annotations. It produces a 3D skeleton sequence of shape T×J×3. For the discriminator, the 2D and 3D sequences are concatenated along the coordinate dimension into a T×J×5 tensor before DCT and feature extraction.

## Method

SemGAN employs a two-stage adversarial training strategy. Stage 1 uses the pre-trained MoDi motion generator to synthesize diverse 3D sequences and project them to 2D, yielding semantically consistent positive pairs that expand motion coverage beyond the limited ground-truth data. Stage 2 trains the SPH-Generator, which first extracts spatio-temporal features via stacked Transformers, then partitions the 17 joints into five Basic Functional Parts, applies a GCN within each part and pools to a semantic prototype, performs self-attention across the five prototypes for inter-part coordination, and finally uses cross-attention from part-level prototypes to joint-level learnable queries to regress 3D coordinates. The ECF-Discriminator provides implicit supervision through two parallel branches: a time-domain branch with alternating Spatial-MHSA and Temporal-MHSA layers, and a frequency-domain branch that applies DCT-II, splits coefficients into three non-uniform bands (10% low / 20% mid / 70% high), encodes each band independently, computes cross-band Shannon entropy differences to capture energy-flow smoothness, and fuses all features via an attention-weighted mechanism. The generator is jointly optimized with explicit MPJPE loss and the implicit adversarial loss from the ECF-Discriminator.

## Results

On Human3.6M with CPN-detected 2D poses (T=243), SemGAN achieves a state-of-the-art average MPJPE of 39.7 mm and P-MPJPE of 31.6 mm, outperforming STCFormer (40.5 mm) and KTPFormer (40.1 mm). With GT 2D poses (T=243), it reaches 18.8 mm MPJPE. On MPI-INF-3DHP (T=81), SemGAN attains 16.5 mm MPJPE, 98.7% PCK, and 86.2% AUC, setting a new best on MPJPE and AUC over KTPFormer (16.7 mm, 85.9% AUC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemGAN (T=243) achieves 39.7 mm MPJPE on Human3.6M (CPN), beating the closest competitors STCFormer (40.5 mm), KTPFormer (40.1 mm), and MixSTE (40.9 mm) by 0.4–0.8 mm. At the shorter T=81 setting, SemGAN (41.5 mm) still surpasses STCFormer (42.0 mm) and MHFormer (43.0 mm at T=351), demonstrating efficiency with fewer input frames. On MPI-INF-3DHP (T=81), SemGAN edges out KTPFormer by 0.2 mm in MPJPE (16.5 vs. 16.7) and 0.3% in AUC (86.2% vs. 85.9%), while at T=27 the two methods are nearly tied (19.3 vs. 19.2 mm). A parameter-matched flat Transformer baseline (36.7 M params, 145.2 G FLOPs) yields 40.4 mm MPJPE, confirming that SemGAN's 39.7 mm gain stems from its hierarchical semantic modeling and cross-frequency constraints rather than additional capacity. SemGAN also outperforms all GCN-based baselines (UGCN, GLA-GCN) by 4–5 mm, highlighting the advantage of the Transformer+GAN formulation over purely graph-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dominant 2D-to-3D lifting paradigm by introducing an adversarial training framework that complements explicit MPJPE supervision with implicit motion-semantic consistency constraints. It builds directly on MoDi as a generative module to synthesize diverse 3D motion sequences for Stage-1 positive pair construction, and draws on the Transformer-based spatio-temporal modeling lineage (PoseFormer, STCFormer, MixSTE, KTPFormer) for the generator backbone. The key conceptual departure is the hierarchical decomposition of the skeleton into five Basic Functional Parts with semantic-prototype-guided cross-attention, combined with an entropy-based cross-frequency discriminator that operates in the DCT domain—neither of which appears in prior 2D-to-3D lifting methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two underexplored aspects of 3D pose estimation—temporal motion-semantic consistency and multi-granularity body-part coordination—that prior methods largely ignore in favor of pure coordinate regression. The hierarchical BFP modeling and cross-frequency entropy discrimination offer reusable design patterns for kinematically-aware pose generation. However, the gains over strong baselines (KTPFormer, STCFormer) are modest (~0.4 mm on Human3.6M, ~0.2 mm on MPI-INF-3DHP), and the two-stage pipeline's dependence on MoDi adds training complexity. Open questions include whether the adversarial component generalizes to unseen action categories beyond the training distribution, whether the fixed five-part decomposition transfers to non-standard skeletons, and whether the frequency-band ratios (10%-20%-70%) remain optimal for different motion regimes.
