## Problem

CLIP-based class-incremental learning suffers from two complementary forms of catastrophic forgetting: encoder drift, where visual representations progressively shift as the model adapts to new tasks, and classifier overwriting, where gradient-based updates corrupt decision boundaries for previously learned classes. Existing methods typically address forgetting at only one level—prompt-based approaches freeze the encoder but limit task-specific learning, while adapter-based methods enable adaptation but still accumulate drift and rely on gradient-trained classifiers. This paper identifies that both sources must be addressed jointly for effective anti-forgetting.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images processed through a CLIP ViT-B/16 visual encoder with LoRA adapters (rank 8 on query/value projections) and produces a class label probability distribution. At inference, the final prediction fuses the analytic classifier's visual logits with a text-embedding-based semantic modulation coefficient computed from the frozen CLIP text encoder, using prompts of the form 'a photo of a {class}'. No visual exemplars are stored; only incrementally accumulated sufficient statistics (feature covariance and class-feature correlation matrices) and up to M=5 LoRA anchor checkpoints are retained across tasks.

## Method

The framework tackles forgetting at two levels. For the encoder, multi-anchor distillation stores multiple historical LoRA parameter states as anchors and enforces cosine-similarity consistency with all anchors simultaneously using exponentially decayed weights (γ=0.9), providing long-range constraints that bound cumulative feature drift beyond what single-step distillation can control; this is complemented by standard feature distillation for short-range smoothness and a drift-aware early stopping criterion that monitors negative-class text-embedding similarity. For the classifier, a text-guided analytic classifier derives weights in closed form from incrementally accumulated sufficient statistics (R = FᵀF and G = YᵀF), completely bypassing gradient-based optimization and making the solution mathematically equivalent to joint training on all data seen so far. Two regularization terms define the 'text-guided' aspect: a text prior (λ₂‖W−T‖²_F) anchors weights near frozen CLIP text embeddings for semantic grounding, and text subspace regularization (λ₃‖TWᵀ‖²_F) penalizes energy along the text embedding subspace, steering the classifier to learn discriminative visual directions orthogonal to text semantics. At inference, the analytic classifier's visual logits are element-wise modulated by exponential text-embedding similarity scores before normalization, recombining the orthogonal visual signal with drift-invariant semantic priors.

## Results

Under the 10-task setting, the method achieves 90.68% average accuracy and 83.93% last accuracy on CIFAR-100, 89.83%/84.87% on ImageNet-R, 89.76%/84.05% on ImageNet-100, and 86.28%/78.81% on CUB-200, setting new state-of-the-art on all four benchmarks. The advantage grows with sequence length: under 50 tasks on CIFAR-100, the method reaches 85.47% average accuracy versus 83.68% for the strongest baseline (SECA), a gap that widens from +2.7% (10 tasks) to +1.8% average but +3.1% last accuracy. On ImageNet-100, the last-accuracy margin over the best prior method reaches +4.2% (84.05% vs. 79.82% for SECA), highlighting the benefit of the closed-form analytic classifier under long sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 19 baselines on every benchmark under the 10-task protocol. Against the strongest CLIP-based baseline SECA [1], it gains +2.71% average and +2.17% last accuracy on CIFAR-100, +1.85% average and +4.23% last on ImageNet-100, +0.91% average and +1.29% last on ImageNet-R, and +0.59% average and +1.85% last on CUB-200. Among analytic learning methods, AnaCP [14] is the closest competitor (84.35% avg on CIFAR-100), yet the proposed method still leads by +6.33% average, demonstrating the benefit of combining multi-anchor distillation with text-guided regularization over pure analytic solutions. Prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) trail by 8–13% in average accuracy, with CODA-Prompt [18] particularly weak on ImageNet-100 (64.37% avg), confirming that freezing the encoder limits task-specific adaptation. Among CLIP-based adapter/fusion methods, RAPF [4] and CLAP [6] are the nearest competitors on CIFAR-100 (86.38% and 86.30% avg), but both still rely on gradient-trained classifiers and lose 4.3–4.4% average accuracy to the proposed closed-form approach. The gap widens under longer sequences (20 and 50 tasks), where the method's advantage over SECA grows to +1.49% average on CIFAR-100 (20 tasks) and +1.79% (50 tasks), consistent with the design goal of bounding cumulative drift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the analytic continual learning line initiated by ACIL (recursive ridge regression with incrementally accumulated sufficient statistics) by embedding CLIP's text embeddings into the closed-form classifier solution as both a semantic prior and a reference subspace for orthogonal regularization. On the encoder side, it departs from single-anchor distillation used in prior CLIP-based CIL methods (e.g., RAPF, CLAP) by introducing multi-anchor distillation with exponentially decayed weights, providing long-range constraints that bound cumulative drift over extended task sequences. The paper's key positioning is that prior CLIP-based continual learning methods address forgetting at only one level (encoder via prompts/adapters, or classifier via gradient-based training), whereas this work tackles both simultaneously with a unified exemplar-free framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form analytic classifier provides a structural guarantee against classifier-level forgetting—incorporating new classes is mathematically equivalent to joint training on all data seen so far—which is a stronger guarantee than what regularization or distillation can offer. The observation that the performance advantage widens with longer task sequences (10→20→50 tasks) suggests the framework is particularly relevant for long-horizon deployment scenarios where cumulative drift and overwriting compound. Open questions include whether the linear decision boundary assumption limits expressiveness on highly nonlinear tasks, whether compressed anchor representations can reduce the linear memory overhead of storing multiple encoder checkpoints, and whether the text subspace regularization generalizes to non-CLIP vision-language models.
