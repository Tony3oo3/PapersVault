## Problem

Existing multimodal face anti-spoofing methods generalize poorly to unseen domains because they directly align noisy multimodal features in which spoof-relevant cues are entangled with domain- and modality-specific variations, forcing both useful and nuisance patterns to match across domains. Additionally, fine-tuning on limited source domains produces overly smoothed decision boundaries in the joint multimodal space, leaving hard target samples near the boundary. The paper argues that a 'purify then guide' strategy—first suppressing group-dependent artifacts, then aligning to a stable pre-trained geometry—is needed to resolve this tension.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB, IR, and DEPTH face images (resized to 224×224, tokenized into 14×14 patch tokens with 512-dim embeddings) along with multiple text prompts describing real and spoof faces, processed by a frozen CLIP text encoder and a fine-tuned CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder. It outputs a binary real/spoof classification via a shallow classifier operating on adapted visual and text embeddings. Notably, the framework handles test-time missing modalities (missing DEPTH, IR, or both) without any modality-specific dropout or retraining, relying on the purified and softly aligned representations to compensate.

## Method

MMDA is a CLIP-based framework built on a 'purify then guide' principle with three key components. First, the Modality-Domain Joint Differential Attention (MD2A) module reformulates differential attention by feeding same-domain, different-sample features into the noise branch, so the differential response estimates a group-shared residual bias (Δ_g) that is subtracted from the main branch, purifying the fused multimodal representation while preserving spoof-discriminative cues. Second, the Representation Space Soft (RS2) alignment scheme softly pulls purified visual features toward class-specific text subspaces spanned by multiple prompts in CLIP space, using a smoothed binary cross-entropy on minimum cosine distance rather than collapsing features onto a single text embedding. Third, the U-shaped Dual Space Adaptation (U-DSA) module introduces deep parameter-efficient MoE-based adaptation layers with a feedback path that remaps deeper representations back to shallower, more domain-invariant layers, with RS2 losses applied at multiple depths to anchor the overall representation to the pre-trained CLIP geometry. The CLIP text encoder remains frozen throughout, and the image encoder is fine-tuned jointly with MD2A, U-DSA, and the classifier using AdamW at a learning rate of 5×10⁻⁶.

## Results

Under Protocol 1 (complete modalities, four Leave-One-Out domain splits), MMDA achieves an average HTER of 4.00% and average AUC of 98.94%, with every individual split reaching HTER ≤ 6.25% and AUC ≥ 98.18%. Compared to the strongest prior multimodal DG method DADM, MMDA reduces average HTER from 13.63% to 4.00% (−9.63 pp) and improves average AUC from 92.96% to 98.94% (+5.98 pp). Under Protocol 2 (missing modalities at test time), MMDA maintains 10.14% average HTER and 95.33% AUC, and under Protocol 3 (limited two-source training) it reaches 6.30% HTER / 98.35% AUC on the most challenging PS→CW split.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMDA outperforms all 14 baselines on every one of the four LOO splits in Protocol 1. The closest competitor is DADM [45] (avg HTER 13.63%, AUC 92.96%); MMDA reduces HTER by 9.63 pp and gains 5.98 pp AUC over it, with the largest per-split gap on CSW→P (19.03% → 4.34% HTER). Among unimodal DG methods, FLIP [25] is the strongest (16.11% HTER, 90.83% AUC), still trailing MMDA by 12.11 pp HTER. Among multimodal FAS methods that do not explicitly target DG, MMDG [23] (19.24% HTER) and CMFL [10] (31.01% HTER) are the best, both substantially outperformed. The raw CLIP backbone [34] without any FAS-specific adaptation achieves 24.63% HTER, confirming that the MD2A purification and RS2/U-DSA alignment steps contribute the majority of the gain. Under Protocol 2 (missing modalities), MMDA again leads all baselines, reducing average HTER from 21.66% (DADM) to 10.14%, and under Protocol 3 (limited two-source training) it cuts HTER from 20.40% to 6.30% on the hardest PS→CW split.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates Differential Attention (DA) [Ye et al., ICLR 2025] for the multimodal domain-generalization setting: instead of contrasting two views of the same sample, MD2A feeds a same-domain, different-sample feature into the noise branch to estimate and subtract group-shared domain/modality residuals. It also repurposes CLIP's frozen text encoder as a domain-agnostic semantic anchor, departing from prior CLIP-based FAS work that typically fine-tunes prompts or adapters without explicitly preserving the pre-trained visual-text geometry. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against both unimodal DG FAS methods (which ignore modality-specific biases) and prior multimodal DG methods like MMDG and DADM (which align noisy fused features directly), arguing that purification before alignment is the missing step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'purify then guide' decomposition offers a clean, reusable principle for any multimodal task where domain and modality artifacts entangle with task-relevant signals before alignment. The U-DSA finding that the deepest adapted layer never yields the best AUC—shallow/mid-depth remapped representations outperform—provides a practical design guideline for parameter-efficient adaptation on pre-trained backbones. Limitations include sensitivity of MoE layers in pre-trained spaces and evaluation on only four datasets; open questions include whether the same-domain pairing strategy in MD2A generalizes to truly unseen modality combinations or to settings where no same-domain sample exists at inference time.
