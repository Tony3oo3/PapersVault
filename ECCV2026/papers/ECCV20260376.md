## Problem

Applying Denoising Score Matching directly to raw joint coordinates for skeleton-based video anomaly detection causes structural collapse: isotropic Gaussian noise destroys kinematic constraints (bone lengths, symmetries), and this degradation compounds as the temporal window expands, wasting model capacity on physically impossible poses. Additionally, off-the-shelf pose estimators introduce coordinate jitter and tracking failures that warp the learned energy landscape, yet prior methods treat extracted poses as perfect ground truth.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 18-point skeleton pose sequences of shape 2×V×T (two image coordinates, V joints, T frames) extracted by AlphaPose, along with per-joint confidence scores in [0,1]. Each sequence is scored over a sliding window of T=12 frames (current plus 11 preceding). The output is a single scalar anomaly score per sequence, computed as the confidence-weighted maximum of standardized energy estimates across L=10 discrete noise scales, with temporal Gaussian 1D-smoothing applied to the aggregated scores before evaluation.

## Method

STEP projects pose sequences into a compact, whitened PCA space (top K=48 principal components) so that injected isotropic noise perturbs motion along learned basis trajectories, generating semantically meaningful action variations while preserving natural human proportions. Training uses a multi-scale Denoising Score Matching objective in this PC-space, with a sequence-level confidence weight c(x) downweighting the loss for low-confidence tracks to prevent tracking failures from distorting the energy landscape. The energy model is a σ-modulated Residual MLP (4 hidden blocks, dim 1024) where the noise scale σ is explicitly routed to every residual block as a conditioning signal, rather than only concatenated at the input. At test time, the model evaluates the sequence across all noise scales, standardizes each energy estimate using training-set statistics, selects the maximum, and multiplies by the sequence confidence to produce the final anomaly score.

## Results

On the UBnormal dataset, STEP achieves 90.1% ± 0.4 AUROC on the Full test set and 90.9% on the Human-Related split, setting a new state-of-the-art with a 12.2% absolute improvement over the previous best (SeeKer, 77.9%). On ShanghaiTech, STEP reaches 86.2% ± 0.1 AUROC (Full) and 87.7% (HR), matching or slightly exceeding STG-NF (85.9% / 87.4%). On the MSAD-HR split, STEP achieves 74.1% AUROC, outperforming SeeKer (61.1%) by 13% and STG-NF (55.7%) by 18%. The entire scoring pipeline runs in under 1 ms for 50 persons per frame on a GTX 1080.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STEP outperforms all 12 skeleton-based baselines on both primary benchmarks. The most direct methodological comparison is with MULDE [20], whose DSM framework applied to raw coordinates achieves only 80.6% AUROC on UBnormal (T=1); STEP's PC-space projection and confidence weighting lift this to 90.1%, a 9.5-point gain that the paper attributes to resolving structural collapse in high-dimensional coordinate space. Against the strongest probabilistic baselines, STG-NF [10] (71.8% UBnormal Full, 85.9% ShanghaiTech Full) and SeeKer [3] (77.9% / 85.5%), STEP leads by 18.3 and 12.2 points on UBnormal, and by 0.3 and 0.7 points on ShanghaiTech respectively. On the MSAD-HR split, STEP (74.1%) surpasses SeeKer (61.1%) by 13 points and STG-NF (55.7%) by 18 points. Older reconstruction/prediction-based methods (MoCoDAD, COSKAD, MPED-RNN, etc.) trail by 10–40 points, confirming that explicit density estimation in a structure-preserving space is substantially more effective than auto-encoding or future-frame prediction for skeleton-based VAD.

## Relation to Prior Work

STEP builds directly on MULDE's [20] formulation of anomaly detection as energy estimation via Denoising Score Matching, but departs fundamentally from modeling the data distribution in raw joint-coordinate space. The key conceptual move is projecting temporal pose sequences into a compact, whitened PCA space before applying DSM, motivated by the observation that isotropic Gaussian noise in raw coordinates destroys kinematic structure (bone lengths, symmetries) in a way that compounds with temporal window length. This positions STEP alongside other probabilistic density estimation approaches for skeleton-based VAD (STG-NF with Normalizing Flows, SeeKer with autoregressive keypoint factorization) but replaces their specialized architectures with a simple σ-modulated Residual MLP operating in PC-space, and introduces soft confidence weighting as an alternative to the hard pose-dropping heuristics used by prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12.2% AUROC gain on UBnormal is substantial for skeleton-based VAD and demonstrates that a simple PCA projection plus a lightweight MLP can outperform complex spatio-temporal graph architectures, suggesting that representation choice matters more than architectural sophistication for this task. The structural-collapse diagnosis (raw-coordinate DSM degrading ~10% as T grows from 2 to 32) is a broadly applicable insight for any score-matching application on structured, low-dimensional manifolds. However, the framework's per-subject independence limits it to single-person anomalies, and it inherits all errors from the upstream pose estimator; explicitly modeling multi-person relational dynamics purely from pose features remains an open direction. The sub-millisecond inference cost makes the approach practically deployable for real-time surveillance, which is a meaningful advantage over heavier diffusion- or flow-based competitors.
