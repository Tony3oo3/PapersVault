## Problem

Text-based chain-of-thought reasoning in multi-modal LLMs is slow (hundreds to thousands of autoregressive tokens), collapses rich reasoning distributions into a single surface realization, and propagates errors through long chains. Existing latent reasoning alternatives are either designed for text-only models or require costly auxiliary image annotations, leaving no principled way to perform efficient latent reasoning in multi-modal settings without additional visual supervision.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (encoded via a vision encoder into visual tokens) and a text query, and produces a final text answer. Instead of generating explicit reasoning text, the model outputs K=3 continuous latent thought vectors (last-layer hidden states of the language model) at designated reasoning positions, which are fed back as input embeddings for subsequent positions via standard self-attention. No special tokens are added to the vocabulary, and the external decoder and projection head used during training are entirely discarded at inference, so the only generation cost is the 3 latent vectors plus the answer tokens.

## Method

CoLT introduces a lightweight external decoder (Qwen3-0.6B, same family and vocabulary as the Qwen3-VL-8B backbone) that provides two complementary step-level supervision signals: forward decoding, where the decoder takes latent thought h_k and is trained to generate the textual reasoning of the next step r_{k+1}, and backward decoding, where the decoder processes preceding text r_{k-1} and its hidden states are aligned (via normalized L2 loss with stop-gradient) to the model's latent thought h_k. A third internal supervision signal uses a 2-layer MLP projection head to predict the next latent step from the current one, enforcing coherent step-to-step transitions via cosine similarity. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on OneThinker CoT annotations with the combined loss (task + forward + backward + internal), followed by GRPO reinforcement learning on 50K samples where the auxiliary losses are removed, allowing the model to explore latent strategies beyond the supervised signals. This three-way supervision regularizes the unconstrained continuous latent space without requiring any auxiliary visual annotations, achieving 10.1× end-to-end speedup and 22.6× text-decoding speedup over text CoT while outperforming both latent reasoning and latent visual reasoning baselines.

## Results

CoLT achieves 79.1% average accuracy across eight multi-modal benchmarks (SeedBench, MMBench, ChartQA, TextVQA, ScienceQA, MMStar, AI2D, MMT-Bench), outperforming the same Qwen3-VL-8B backbone with textual CoT (75.7%) by +3.4% and direct answering (69.5%) by +9.6%, while using only 3 latent thought vectors. The largest per-benchmark gains over textual CoT are on ChartQA (+9.6%) and TextVQA (+6.1%). Inference speed is reduced by 10.1× end-to-end and 22.6× in text decoding time compared to text CoT, with generation time dropping from ~7.2 s to 0.32 s per sample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLT outperforms all five latent reasoning baselines on the same Qwen3-VL-8B backbone: SIM-CoT (74.0% avg) by +5.1%, SoftCoT (72.2%) by +6.9%, CODI (70.8%) by +8.3%, ICoT (69.5%) by +9.6%, and CCoT (68.3%) by +10.8%, with the largest per-benchmark margin on TextVQA (+11.1% over SIM-CoT). Against latent visual reasoning methods that require auxiliary image annotations, CoLT beats LVR (73.6%) by +5.5% and Multimodal CoT (69.6%) by +9.5%, while eliminating the need for costly image-based supervision. Compared to the same backbone with explicit textual CoT (75.7%), CoLT gains +3.4% on average, with particularly strong improvements on ChartQA (+9.6%) and TextVQA (+6.1%), suggesting that latent representations better capture structured visual reasoning than verbose text. CoLT also surpasses open-source MLLMs LLaVA-OneVision-1.5 and InternVL3 on most benchmarks where they report results, and approaches proprietary models like GPT-5-high on MMBench (84.6 vs. 83.8) and AI2D (85.4 vs. 89.7). The only benchmark where CoLT trails a proprietary model by a notable margin is ChartQA (74.7 vs. GPT-5's 86.2), though it still leads all open-source and latent-reasoning methods on that task.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLT extends the latent reasoning line (CoCoNut, CODI, SoftCoT, SIM-CoT) from text-only LLMs to multi-modal models, while departing from latent visual reasoning approaches (LVR, MoNet, LaCoT) that require costly auxiliary image annotations. Its key conceptual departure is the three-way step-level supervision design: a lightweight external decoder operating in both forward (latent→text) and backward (text→latent) modes, combined with an internal step-to-step prediction head. This repurposes SIM-CoT's auxiliary-decoder idea but applies it bidirectionally and adds a structural regularizer, all within the language model's own hidden space without any visual encoder for intermediate representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.1× end-to-end speedup with equal or better accuracy across eight diverse benchmarks makes latent reasoning practically viable for deployment of multi-modal reasoning models, where text CoT's autoregressive token generation is the dominant inference bottleneck. The finding that K=3 latent steps is optimal (and that more steps degrade performance) provides a clear, actionable design principle. Open questions include whether adaptive step counts by problem difficulty can further improve efficiency, whether the approach transfers to video or multi-image settings, and whether the reliance on CoT annotations for training (OneThinker) can be reduced or eliminated. The robustness-to-noise advantage, while consistent, is moderate in magnitude and may not hold under more severe or structured corruptions.
