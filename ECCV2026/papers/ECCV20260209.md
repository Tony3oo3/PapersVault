## Problem

Existing training-free video editing methods follow a data-to-data paradigm (inversion-based or inversion-free) that requires many costly iterations and still produces distortions in editing-irrelevant regions. This paradigm is fundamentally incompatible with modern few-step generative models (consistency models, rectified flow, distribution-matching distillation) that are optimized for noise-to-data generation, so the speed and quality gains from these advances have not transferred to video editing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video, a target text prompt, and optionally a user-provided or model-edited first-frame image as a visual prompt. It produces an edited video of arbitrary length via autoregressive chunk-wise streaming generation, operating in the latent space of pre-trained streaming generators (Self Forcing or LongLive). With 5 steps, inference runs at under 0.32 seconds per frame on a single A100 GPU; standard comparisons use 15 steps. The visual prompt is consumed as an additional forward pass that provides a previous-chunk KV cache, requiring no architectural modification.

## Method

StreamEdit reformulates video editing as source-conditioned noise-to-data streaming generation, preserving few-step sampling while injecting source-video conditions. A dual-branch sampler denoises source and target in parallel under shared noise, with the source branch following a fixed-endpoint linear interpolation. A self-attention bridge transfers source priors into target generation through three mechanisms: time-dependent query blending for structure/motion preservation, masked key blending for editing consistency, and delayed source KV injection (starting at t=0.5) for background detail. Cross-attention grounding computes adaptive editing-region masks from foreground-background attention differences without extra models, and cross-attention boosting applies additive regional score enhancement as a controllable editing-strength valve. Source-oriented guidance corrects stochastic sampling errors in editing-irrelevant regions by projecting the source-branch velocity residual onto the target branch, analogous to classifier-free guidance.

## Results

On FiVE-Bench, StreamEdit (Self Forcing) achieves the best background preservation across all metrics: Structure Distance 10.27×10³, PSNR 28.47, LPIPS 49.84×10³, and SSIM 87.52×10², while also delivering the fastest inference at 0.60 s/frame. StreamEdit (LongLive) attains the highest text-alignment CLIPS score (27.32) and FiVE editing accuracy (55.04) among text-only methods; with optional visual prompting, FiVE Acc further rises to 61.19. All StreamEdit variants outperform the next-best baseline (UniEdit-Flow) by a wide margin on LPIPS (49.84 vs. 223.07) and SSIM (87.52 vs. 78.60), while running 1.9×–45× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StreamEdit dominates on background-preservation metrics: Ours (SF) achieves LPIPS 49.84×10³ versus 223.07 for UniEdit-Flow, 94.61 for Wan-Edit, and 185.21 for VideoGrain, indicating far fewer structural distortions in editing-irrelevant regions. On editing effectiveness, Ours (LL) reaches FiVE Acc 55.04 and CLIPS.edit 22.13, surpassing UniEdit-Flow (50.10 / 20.92) and Wan-Edit (46.97 / 21.23); with visual prompting, Ours (LL)§ pushes FiVE Acc to 61.19, a 11-point gain over the strongest text-only baseline. Inference speed is a decisive advantage: StreamEdit runs at 0.60–0.76 s/frame, roughly 1.9× faster than UniEdit-Flow (1.11 s), 4.4× faster than Pyramid-Edit (1.44 s), and up to 45× faster than VideoGrain (27.12 s) and DMT (25.98 s). The only metrics where baselines retain an edge are NIQE (TokenFlow 4.01, VideoGrain 4.08 vs. StreamEdit 4.38–4.61) and temporal consistency (VidToMe 90.06 vs. Ours (SF) 90.93 — actually StreamEdit wins here), suggesting a minor trade-off in perceived image quality that does not affect structural fidelity or editing accuracy. For long-video editing (470+ frames), a user study with 20 participants ranks Ours (LL, 5 steps) first in overall quality, background preservation, and editing fidelity over StreamV2V [42] and AdaFlow [91].

## Relation to Prior Work

StreamEdit departs from the dominant data-to-data editing paradigm (inversion-based methods like UniEdit-Flow and inversion-free methods like FlowEdit) by reframing video editing as source-conditioned noise-to-data generation. It builds on pre-trained streaming video generators (Self Forcing, LongLive) that already exploit KV caching and autoregressive chunk-wise generation, and on few-step sampling techniques from consistency models and distribution matching distillation. The key conceptual move is that these fast generation capabilities were previously inaccessible to editing pipelines because data-to-data methods are tightly coupled to their own iterative procedures; StreamEdit's dual-branch sampling with a self-attention bridge and cross-attention grounding/boosting decouples source conditioning from the sampling trajectory, allowing the distilled few-step sampler to operate natively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm shift from data-to-data to noise-to-data editing unlocks a dramatic efficiency gain (0.32 s/frame at 5 steps vs. 1–27 s/frame for baselines) while simultaneously improving edit quality and background preservation, and it extends naturally to length-unrestricted autoregressive editing. The training-free, model-agnostic design (validated on two distinct streaming generators) suggests the approach can be dropped into future fast video generators as they emerge. Open questions include whether the self-attention bridge generalizes to non-DiT architectures, how the method handles multi-object or spatially complex edits beyond single-region prompts, and whether real-time interactive editing is achievable with further distillation.
