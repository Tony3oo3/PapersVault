## Problem

Existing skill determination methods rely on global feature aggregation or static temporal modeling that treats all stages with fixed importance, failing to capture how human evaluators progressively form and refine their cognitive understanding as they observe a sequence of actions. In particular, the primacy effect—where early-stage performance anchors subsequent judgments—and the iterative integration of prior observations with domain knowledge remain unmodeled, limiting the ability to reflect human-like sequential evaluation.

## Input / Output

SPAR consumes pre-extracted video feature sequences (I3D features for EPIC-Skills and BEST, VST features for LOGO, RG, and Fis-V) consisting of 400 clips for pairwise ranking or 48 clips for score prediction, partitioned into fixed-length temporal windows (size 16 for ranking, 2 for scoring). For absolute score prediction, a reference exemplar video with a known ground-truth score is required to anchor the predicted relative shift into an absolute value. The framework outputs a pairwise ranking margin, stage-specific cognitive states with signed approval signals, cumulative cognitive trajectories, and per-window importance weights that collectively drive the final score.

## Method

SPAR comprises three coupled components. The Stage-aware Cognitive Memory (SaCM) maintains a cumulative cognitive state that is recursively updated at each temporal window: current segment features are filtered through a Cognitive Window Filtering module (temporal embeddings + DyT-stabilized projections), then adaptively modulated by scale/shift parameters derived from the prior cumulative state, and finally fused across K=4 latent dimensions via attention to produce a stage-specific cognitive representation with a probabilistic score. The Temporal Window Weighting (TWW) module computes intra-window pairwise similarity attention and inter-window contextual weights to identify decisive action stages, aggregating stage-specific predictions into the overall score. The Judgment Bias Mitigation (JBM) loss selectively penalizes stages where cumulative or stage-specific approval signals contradict the ground-truth ranking direction, using counterfactual vector flipping and mask-based gating to correct drift without over-constraining consistent evaluations. Training combines a dynamic margin ranking loss, a Mean-Variance Probability loss over the Gaussian score difference, and the JBM term under supervised learning.

## Results

On the EPIC-Skills pairwise ranking benchmark, SPAR achieves 89.14% average accuracy, surpassing the previous best method Rhythmner (88.45%) by 0.7%. On the BEST dataset, SPAR reaches 88.56% average accuracy, improving over the second-best ASAAS (85.34%) by 3.2%. For absolute score prediction on LOGO, SPAR attains a Spearman's ρ of 0.749 and R-ℓ2 of 3.743, outperforming QTD (ρ=0.698, R-ℓ2=4.948) and ASGTN (ρ=0.704, R-ℓ2=5.695), though trailing the most recent 2025 methods CaFlow (ρ=0.856) and PHI (ρ=0.835). On RG and Fis-V, SPAR achieves competitive average ρ of 0.816 and 0.802 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPAR achieves state-of-the-art on both pairwise ranking benchmarks: on EPIC-Skills it outperforms Rhythmner [27] (88.45%) and ASAAS [59] (88.32%) by 0.7% and 0.8% respectively, and on BEST it surpasses ASAAS [59] (85.34%) by a substantial 3.2% margin, with particularly large gains on Braid Hair (+6.4%) and Scrambled Eggs (+2.6%). On the LOGO score-prediction dataset, SPAR (ρ=0.749, R-ℓ2=3.743) clearly beats prior methods such as QTD [6] (ρ=0.698), ASGTN [25] (ρ=0.704), and T2CR [19] (ρ=0.681), but is outperformed by the most recent 2025 approaches CaFlow [15] (ρ=0.856, R-ℓ2=1.425), PHI [63] (ρ=0.835, R-ℓ2=2.752), and MLAVL [44] (ρ=0.804, R-ℓ2=2.269). On RG and Fis-V, SPAR (avg ρ 0.816 and 0.802) remains competitive but trails CaFlow (0.841/0.805) and MLAVL (0.849/0.823), suggesting that the cognitive-memory formulation excels in relative ranking but has not yet fully closed the gap with multimodal or instruction-guided methods on absolute scoring. Overall, SPAR's advantage is most pronounced in pairwise comparison settings where modeling progressive evaluator cognition and temporal attribution provides the greatest benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPAR builds on the reference-based inference mechanism from CoRe [54] to extend pairwise ranking to absolute score prediction, and adopts the dynamic margin and Mean-Variance Probability losses from HARP/CoffeeCraft [53] for its optimization objective. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant line of AQA work (RAAN, AAS, ASAAS, T2CR, ASGTN) that treats temporal segments via static attention, global aggregation, or segment-level importance scoring, by explicitly modeling a progressive, gated update of a cumulative 'cognitive state' that conditions each stage's interpretation on all prior observations. The Temporal Window Weighting and Judgment Bias Mitigation components are novel architectural and loss-level additions not present in prior skill-determination frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cognitive-state framing offers a more interpretable account of how evaluative judgments accumulate over long videos, and the JBM loss provides a principled mechanism for correcting stage-level inconsistencies that prior methods ignore. However, SPAR achieves SOTA only on pairwise ranking benchmarks (EPIC-Skills, BEST); on absolute score prediction (LOGO, RG, Fis-V) it trails recent methods like CaFlow, PHI, and MLAVL by several points in Spearman correlation, suggesting the cognitive modeling benefit is most pronounced in relative comparison settings. Open questions include whether the progressive cognitive state generalizes to truly long-form or multi-person videos beyond the 400-clip regime tested, and whether the 'cognitive' interpretation translates into actionable insights for human-in-the-loop evaluation systems.
