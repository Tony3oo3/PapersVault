## Problem

In active 3D reconstruction with 3D Gaussian Splatting, uncertainty-based next-best-view selection becomes unreliable in early training stages because uncertainty estimates are tightly coupled to incomplete, sparsely distributed geometry. Additionally, existing methods fail to convert uncertainty signals into targeted learning signals, leaving weakly contributing Gaussians in under-observed regions undersupervised due to vanishing gradients during rasterization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260375/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method starts from 4 uniformly distributed RGB views and incrementally adds one view every 100 training iterations until reaching 20 views, operating on a pre-defined set of candidate camera poses. At each selection step, it generates SA-Points by triangulating dense correspondences (predicted by MASt3R or DA-v3) between a training image and a rasterized extrapolated render from a perturbed pose, filtered by reprojection error. The outputs are the selected next-best-view pose, a 3DGS reconstruction, SA-Points as a 3D point cloud for coverage estimation, and per-Gaussian uncertainty estimates used for both view scoring and residual supervision.

## Method

SA-ResGS introduces a two-stage view selection pipeline: first, SA-Points from triangulation define observed voxels in a 3D grid, and candidate views are pre-filtered by computing normalized Hamming distance between hash-encoded binary occupancy vectors (following Instant-NGP spatial hashing) of observed voxels and each candidate's frustum voxels, retaining only the top 20% most geometrically complementary views; second, Fisher information-based uncertainty scoring is applied only within this filtered subset for final selection. For training, the paper proposes the first residual supervision strategy for 3DGS: at each iteration, two renders are produced from the same view—one with the full Gaussian set and one with a guided subset combining 90% random Gaussians plus the top-10% most uncertain (ranked by low opacity and large scale). Both renders are supervised independently with L1+SSIM loss against the ground-truth image, and the subset branch amplifies gradients for occluded, weakly contributing Gaussians by an effective factor of 1/(1−α_k) per masked high-opacity Gaussian ahead, emulating ResNet-style skip connections without modifying the rasterizer.

## Results

On NeRF-Synthetic, SA-ResGS (MASt3R) achieves the best PSNR of 26.58 dB and SSIM of 0.907, outperforming the strongest 3D-based baseline FisherRF (25.19 dB / 0.892) by 1.39 dB. On Mip-NeRF 360, SA-ResGS reaches 21.41 dB PSNR and 0.613 SSIM, surpassing FisherRF (20.64 dB / 0.595) and CrossScore (21.08 dB / 0.612). On the extended benchmark (Deep Blending + Tanks and Temples), SA-ResGS attains 20.40 dB PSNR and 0.732 SSIM, leading FisherRF by 0.75 dB. LPIPS performance is competitive but slightly behind CrossScore on Mip-NeRF 360 (0.451 vs. 0.448) and the extended set (0.361 vs. 0.356), which the authors attribute to smoother color reconstruction in uncertain regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SA-ResGS consistently outperforms FisherRF (the primary 3D-based baseline) on PSNR and SSIM across all three datasets, with margins of 1.39 dB on NeRF-Synthetic, 0.77 dB on Mip-NeRF 360, and 0.75 dB on the extended benchmark. Against the 2D-based baselines, SA-ResGS dominates on PSNR and SSIM (e.g., 26.58 vs. 25.24 for MUSIQ on NeRF-Synthetic), but CrossScore achieves marginally better LPIPS on Mip-NeRF 360 (0.448 vs. 0.451) and the extended set (0.356 vs. 0.361), suggesting CrossScore preserves slightly sharper perceptual detail in those settings. Rule-based methods (Random, ACP) are clearly inferior, with ACP lagging by over 3 dB PSNR on NeRF-Synthetic. The DA-v3 variant of SA-ResGS confirms the gains are not tied to a specific 3D lifting backbone, maintaining the same ranking pattern. On the extended benchmark with unbalanced, real-world camera distributions, SA-ResGS shows the largest absolute improvement over FisherRF, indicating the physically grounded prefiltering is most beneficial under challenging view configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SA-ResGS builds directly on FisherRF (ECCV 2024) as its NBV selection baseline and on the 3DGS rendering pipeline (Kerbl et al., 2023), extending both with two orthogonal contributions. It departs from prior post-hoc uncertainty methods (FisherRF, BayesRays, PRIMU) by decoupling view selection from 3DGS-internal training dynamics through a physically grounded SA-Points pre-filter, and from existing vanishing-gradient remedies (pixelSplat, PAPR) by introducing the first ResNet-inspired residual supervision mechanism specifically targeting under-contributing Gaussians in 3DGS. The 3D lifting component (MASt3R or DA-v3) is borrowed from the broader 3D reconstruction literature and used as a modular, swappable module rather than a core contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in active 3D reconstruction: uncertainty estimates in 3DGS are tightly coupled to training dynamics and become unreliable in early, sparse-view stages, leading to clustered or redundant view selections. The residual supervision mechanism is a reusable technique that could transfer to other 3D representation formats beyond 3DGS, and the 40% runtime reduction (while improving quality) makes the approach more deployable. Open questions include scalability to very large outdoor scenes, sensitivity to the quality of the 3D lifting module under large viewpoint changes, and whether the hash-encoded voxel representation remains sufficient for high-resolution or unbounded environments.
