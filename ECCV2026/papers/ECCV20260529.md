## Problem

Current world model evaluation relies on perceptual quality and semantic alignment metrics that cannot detect violations of fundamental physical laws such as momentum and energy conservation. This gap is compounded by the ill-posed problem of recovering metric-scale 3D kinematics from uncalibrated monocular video rollouts, and by the absence of targeted safety-critical collision data in existing benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes uncalibrated monocular video clips (no camera intrinsics, no metric scale, no ego-motion reference) along with a text description of the collision configuration and manually annotated actor identities in the first frame. It outputs per-actor 3D trajectories in a global coordinate frame and seven scalar physics metrics grouped into three families: spatio-temporal consistency (warp error, flow divergence), momentum and energy conservation (linear momentum residual, angular momentum residual, kinetic energy gain), and world-dynamics integrity (instance stability via Simpson index, appearance drift via CLIP angular deviation).

## Method

CrashTwin couples a 38K crash dataset (25K CARLA synthetic sequences with full state logs plus 12K curated real-world clips) with a calibration-free 3D reconstruction pipeline that incrementally combines CenterTrack 3D tracking, SAM2 instance segmentation for fragment relinking, Metric3D V2 for metric-scale depth correction, DROID-SLAM for ego-motion estimation, and Kalman filtering to produce stable global trajectories under heavy occlusion and rapid motion. Physics evaluation then operates on these trajectories within a short collision window: spatio-temporal consistency is measured via optical-flow warping error and flow-field divergence within instance masks; conservation laws are checked through normalized residuals of linear momentum, angular momentum about the contact point, and kinetic energy (penalizing only unphysical energy gain); and world-dynamics integrity is assessed via a Simpson index for tracking identity stability and mean frame-to-frame angular deviation of L2-normalized CLIP embeddings for appearance coherence. The framework is validated by benchmarking six state-of-the-art world models and by demonstrating that physics post-training on the synthetic split reduces violations, particularly in spatio-temporal and identity metrics, while momentum and energy conservation remain the hardest dimensions to correct.

## Results

Across all six benchmarked world models, momentum and energy conservation metrics remain severely violated: the best open-source model (Cosmos-Predict2-14B) still exhibits a linear momentum residual of 0.683, angular residual of 0.763, and energy gain of 0.605, while the best proprietary model (Hailuo 2.3) scores 0.766, 0.781, and 0.729 respectively. Spatio-temporal consistency is comparatively better, with Veo 3.1 achieving the lowest warp error (0.0097) and Cosmos-Predict2-14B the lowest flow divergence among open-source models (0.718). Instance stability ranges from 0.601 (Seedance) to 0.725 (Wan 2.2-5B), and appearance drift from 0.297 (Hailuo) to 0.359 (SkyReel), indicating moderate identity preservation but persistent physical breakdowns during collision events.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks six world models (three open-source: SkyReel-1.3B, Wan 2.1-14B/2.2-5B, Cosmos-Predict2-2B/14B; three proprietary: Veo 3.1, Hailuo 2.3, Seedance V1 Pro) under a unified physics-grounded protocol. No model achieves physically consistent collision dynamics: all exhibit momentum residuals (J_p) above 0.68, meaning at least 68% of pre-impact momentum is unaccounted for. Cosmos-Predict2-14B leads among open-source models on all three conservation metrics (J_p=0.683, J_H=0.763, J_E=0.605), outperforming SkyReel-1.3B by 0.27 in momentum residual and 0.34 in energy gain. Among proprietary models, Hailuo 2.3 achieves the best appearance drift (0.297) and competitive momentum scores, while Veo 3.1 has the lowest warp error (0.0097) but still shows a 0.746 energy gain. The key finding is that spatio-temporal smoothness and identity stability are substantially easier for current models than physically correct momentum/energy exchange, with a clear gap between the best model scores and the ground-truth reference (J_p=0.309, J_H=0.462, J_E=0.250). Post-training on CrashTwin data narrows this gap (e.g., J_p drops from 0.889 to 0.648) but does not close it, confirming that physics priors remain a critical bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant evaluation paradigm for video generation and world models, which relies on perceptual proxies (FVD, FID, VBench) or VLM-based commonsense judges, by directly measuring classical conservation laws (linear/angular momentum, kinetic energy) from uncalibrated video rollouts. It also distinguishes itself from crash-oriented generation models (DrivingGen, AVD2, Ctrl-Crash) that either condition on full trajectories or produce untestable ego-collisions, by evaluating unconstrained world models on their intrinsic physical reasoning. Technically, the calibration-free reconstruction pipeline repurposes existing vision foundation models (SAM2, DROID-SLAM, Metric3D V2, CenterTrack, SEA-RAFT) in a novel compositional pipeline specifically designed to recover metric-scale multi-agent kinematics under the occlusion and rapid motion of collision events.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CrashTwin provides the first large-scale (38K events), physics-grounded benchmark that quantitatively exposes how current SOTA world models—both open-source and proprietary—violate fundamental conservation laws during multi-agent collisions despite high visual quality, a failure mode invisible to standard perceptual metrics. The finding that physics post-training closes the gap in spatio-temporal consistency and identity stability far more than in momentum/energy exchange suggests that embedding rigid-body dynamics into generative models remains an open challenge requiring stronger physical priors. Limitations include the focus on vehicle-to-vehicle collisions (a narrow but safety-critical subset), the reliance on a calibration-free pipeline whose sub-meter reconstruction error still introduces uncertainty into conservation-law checks, and the fact that the benchmark evaluates only two-actor interactions, leaving multi-body and pedestrian-vehicle scenarios unaddressed.
