## Problem

Volume denoising under extremely low SNR remains challenging because classical structural priors (TV, NLM) and untrained neural representations blur sharp 3D edges and corners, while data-driven denoisers require large paired clean/noisy 3D training sets that are often unavailable. No existing training-free method explicitly models 3D junction geometry (multi-surface intersections, corners, bent boundaries) to preserve sharp volumetric structure while rejecting noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262792/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a noisy 3D volume (e.g., 256³ CT volumes, 1024×1024×300 cryo-ET volumes, or voxelized 256³ point-cloud grids) or raw noisy projection measurements (e.g., 20 views at 256² for sparse-view CT). It outputs a denoised or reconstructed 3D volume; for point clouds, the denoised voxel grid is converted back to 100k points. In the CT inverse-problem setting, 3D FoJ operates inside a proximal gradient loop on projections rather than on a pre-reconstructed volume, so the input is the Radon-domain measurement vector b and system matrix A.

## Method

3D FoJ parameterizes each overlapping 3D patch (default 10³, stride 2) as a junction of three planes intersecting at a freely positioned vertex, partitioning the patch into up to eight constant-intensity regions (typically M=3 active). A global objective combines local patch fidelity, inter-patch boundary-plane alignment via a smooth boundary-strength field, and regional appearance consistency via a global averaged color field; binary region indicators are replaced by differentiable arctan-based Heaviside functions to enable gradient-based optimization. Optimization proceeds in two stages: a discrete coordinate-descent initialization that greedily fits plane orientations and vertex positions per patch in parallel, followed by joint gradient-based refinement (Adam) of all junction parameters with closed-form updates for region intensities. The method is fully training-free and requires no neural network; it can be deployed as a direct volume denoiser or as a drop-in proximal regularizer within any volumetric inverse-problem solver, where a single 3D FoJ update per proximal step steers the solution toward the piecewise-planar junction manifold.

## Results

On low-dose CT reconstruction (256³ volumes from 20 sparse views with Poisson noise), 3D FoJ achieves the best average MS-SSIM across all three noise levels: 0.754 (P50), 0.788 (P100), and 0.838 (P1000), and the best 3D PSNR at P50 (15.88 dB) and P100 (16.14 dB). On point cloud denoising (28 shapes, Chamfer-L2), 3D FoJ reduces error by 1–3 orders of magnitude over all baselines, e.g., 0.15 ± 0.40 vs. 2.15 ± 6.47 (PointCVaR) at 10% outlier noise and 0.16 ± 0.41 vs. 62.33 ± 191.76 (PointCleanNet) at 40k spread noise. On cryo-ET (IMOD centriole), 3D FoJ achieves the best FSCe/o resolution at the 0.25 threshold (32.14 Å) without any training data, while remaining competitive with SC-Net at the 0.5 threshold (58.24 vs. 55.60 Å).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CT reconstruction, 3D FoJ outperforms all four baselines on MS-SSIM at every noise level, with the largest margin over NAF (0.754 vs. 0.423 at P50) and a clear lead over 3D-TV (0.754 vs. 0.703 at P50). On 3D PSNR, 3D FoJ leads at P50 (15.88 vs. 15.29 for R²-Gaussian) and P100 (16.14 vs. 15.31 for 3D-TV), but 3D-TV edges it out at P1000 (16.96 vs. 16.72), suggesting the structural prior is most beneficial under extreme noise. On point cloud denoising, 3D FoJ dominates all baselines by a wide margin: at 10% outlier noise it achieves Chamfer-L2 of 0.15 vs. 2.15 (PointCVaR) and 2.17 (PointCleanNet), and at 40k spread noise it reaches 0.16 vs. 62.33 (PointCleanNet) and 154.41 (PointCVaR). On cryo-ET, 3D FoJ wins the 0.25 FSC threshold (32.14 Å vs. 39.86 for SC-Net and 52.76 for NMSG) but trails NMSG at the 0.5 threshold (58.24 vs. 67.10 Å), indicating the piecewise-planar bias is most advantageous for resolving fine structural detail rather than coarse resolution. NLM is consistently the weakest baseline across all three tasks, confirming that simple averaging is insufficient for low-SNR 3D denoising.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly generalizes the 2D Field of Junctions (FoJ) model [35] from ICCV 2021 to a fully volumetric 3D setting, replacing intersecting lines and 2D wedges with intersecting planes and 3D wedge regions. Conceptually, it occupies a distinct niche in the denoising landscape: unlike classical priors (TV, NLM) that blur edges, untrained neural priors (Deep Image Prior, INRs) that lose boundary structure at low SNR, or data-driven denoisers (diffusion models, trained networks) that require large 3D training sets, 3D FoJ is an explicit geometric parameterization requiring no training data while preserving sharp corners and surfaces. The proximal-gradient formulation (Eq. 10–15) positions 3D FoJ as a drop-in structural regularizer for any volumetric inverse problem, extending its utility beyond direct denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, hallucination-free nature of 3D FoJ is practically important for medical and scientific imaging where 3D clean/noisy training pairs are scarce or unavailable, and where learned priors risk fabricating structure. The method's generality across CT, cryo-ET, and point clouds suggests a reusable structural prior for the broader class of low-SNR volumetric inverse problems. Key limitations include the local piecewise-planar inductive bias (curved structures require small patches or more regions), computational cost scaling with volume size, and the fact that CT evaluation is on synthetic data while cryo-ET quantitative results cover only one dataset. Open questions include whether the approach scales to clinical-resolution volumes, how it performs on highly curved biological structures, and whether the piecewise-planar assumption can be relaxed without losing the noise-robustness advantage.
