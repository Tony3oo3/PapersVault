## Problem

Current open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding methods built on 3D Gaussian Splatting suffer from two fundamental limitations: (1) spatial ambiguity from unstructured, overlapping Gaussians forces probabilistic feature registration that produces semantic bleeding and ray-like spillovers at object boundaries, and (2) multi-level semantic ambiguity is resolved by training separate feature fields per SAM mask hierarchy, imposing massive preprocessing overhead and diluting fine-grained details through mask-level pooling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262083/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (typically 130-300 views per scene) and a free-form text query. Images are processed through AM-RADIO at its native 512x512 resolution, then upscaled to full input resolution via a self-correcting sliding window with Gaussian-kernel blending and SCRA/SCGA denoising, yielding dense per-pixel language-aligned feature maps. The output is a 3D language-aligned feature field stored on explicit sparse voxels, from which 3D object retrieval (cosine-similarity thresholding in 3D), 2D localization (rendered relevancy maps), and point-cloud semantic segmentation (nearest-voxel feature transfer) are all derived without additional per-task training.

## Method

LESV replaces 3DGS with Sparse Voxel Rasterization (SVRaster), whose explicit, disjoint voxel grid enables deterministic, view-independent feature registration that bypasses the global depth-sorting and alpha-blending required by Gaussians, reducing peak memory to ~25 GB with O(1) spatial batching. The SVRaster geometry is regularized during training with patch-wise depth consistency (normalized L2 on local depth patches) and analytic normal consistency losses derived from monocular priors, suppressing the fragmented or hollow geometry that would otherwise corrupt volumetric fusion. A multi-level TSDF mesh extracted from the voxel grid provides a view-consistent reference depth; the absolute discrepancy between this mesh depth and per-view rendered depth yields a continuous confidence map that multiplicatively gates the Gaussian-kernel fusion weights, down-weighting geometrically inconsistent projections. For multi-level semantic ambiguity, the method exploits the emergent dense alignment of AM-RADIO's spatial patch tokens, projected through the SigLIP language head without explicit dense supervision, so that a single forward pass produces features simultaneously responsive to part-level, component-level, and category-level queries, eliminating the need for hierarchical SAM mask pooling. The sliding window upsampling strategy partitions high-resolution inputs into overlapping crops, aggregates per-crop AM-RADIO features with a 2D Gaussian kernel to avoid boundary discontinuities, and applies RADSeg's thresholded cosine-similarity attention (SCRA/SCGA) as a semantic filter to remove high-frequency noise while preserving sharp object boundaries.

## Results

On the ScanNet open-vocabulary point cloud understanding benchmark (19-class), LESV achieves 53.22% mIoU and 70.41% mAcc, a +21.56% absolute mIoU improvement over the leading 3DGS baseline Dr. Splat (31.66% mIoU). On LERF 3D object retrieval, LESV attains 56.11% average mIoU and 85.21% Acc@25, the best Acc@25 among all methods. On 2D object localization, LESV matches the average localization accuracy of the dedicated 2D method LangSplat-v2 (84.12% vs. 84.10%) while outperforming it on the complex ramen scene (63.40% vs. 51.80% mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LESV decisively outperforms Dr. Splat [11], the primary direct-registration 3DGS baseline, on all three benchmarks: +21.56% mIoU on ScanNet 19-class (53.22 vs. 31.66), +3.44% mIoU and +8.30% Acc@25 on LERF 3D retrieval (56.11/85.21 vs. 52.67/76.91). Against LaGa [4] (multi-level SAM masks), LESV trades a small mIoU deficit (56.11 vs. 58.82) for a superior Acc@25 (85.21 vs. 82.88) while reducing preprocessing from ~190 min to ~20 min. On 2D localization (Table 2), LESV loses mIoU to Occam's LGS [5] (56.84 vs. 61.28) and LangSplat-v2 [16] (56.84 vs. 59.88), but matches LangSplat-v2's localization accuracy (84.12% vs. 84.10%) without any 2D-specific training. LangSplat [22] and OpenGaussian [30] are far behind on 3D retrieval (10.97% and 38.36% mIoU respectively), confirming that distillation-based and contrastive-learning 3DGS pipelines are poorly suited to direct 3D querying. The ablation (Table 5) further shows that swapping Dr. Splat's SAM+CLIP features to AM-RADIO yields only marginal gains (+0.66% mIoU), whereas LESV with AM-RADIO jumps to 56.11% mIoU, confirming the gains come from the dense per-voxel registration paradigm rather than the encoder alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

LESV departs from the dominant 3D Gaussian Splatting paradigm (LangSplat, Dr. Splat, OpenGaussian, LaGa) by replacing unstructured, overlapping Gaussians with Sparse Voxel Rasterization (SVRaster) as the geometric backbone, enabling deterministic rather than probabilistic feature registration. For feature encoding, it shifts away from the standard SAM+CLIP mask-pooling pipeline toward AM-RADIO's dense, language-aligned patch tokens, exploiting the emergent property that agglomerative vision foundation models retain spatial-to-semantic alignment without explicit dense supervision. The geometric regularization draws on patch-level depth normalization from DNGaussian and analytic normal constraints from MonoSDF, while the self-correcting aggregation borrows SCRA/SCGA mechanisms from RADSeg.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gains are substantial: peak memory drops from >256 GB (Dr. Splat) to ~25 GB, and per-scene preprocessing is reduced by over 8×, making dense per-pixel feature registration feasible on standard hardware. The +21.56% mIoU improvement on ScanNet point cloud understanding over the leading 3DGS baseline is significant, though partly attributable to the structured-geometry advantage that was already anticipated. Key open questions remain: the effective feature resolution is capped at 256 (below SAM mask resolution), limiting performance on small-object scenes, and the method cannot render features into novel views in real time. Whether sparse coefficient fields or other compact representations can bridge the gap to real-time 3D language rendering is an active direction.
