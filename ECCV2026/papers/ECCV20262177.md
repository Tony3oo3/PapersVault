## Problem

Orthogonality-based LoRA methods for rehearsal-free class-incremental learning enforce strict disjoint direction allocation across tasks, treating all previously used low-rank directions as permanently and equally protected. Under tight rank budgets and long task sequences, this uniform hard protection progressively shrinks the effective update space, causing gradient-subspace mismatch and weakening plasticity. The paper addresses the gap of direction-wise heterogeneity in sensitivity: some directions encode strongly consolidated knowledge while others remain safely reusable, yet no prior method estimates this per-direction uncertainty to calibrate protection strength.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images through a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet-21K, fine-tuned on ImageNet-1K) with LoRA modules inserted into key and value projections of attention blocks. At inference, predictions are obtained via Monte Carlo sampling from the stored variational posteriors, yielding both a class label and a calibrated uncertainty estimate (reported as NLL and ECE). The rank budget r (default 10) is fixed across all tasks, and no exemplars or prompt pools are maintained, keeping inference overhead comparable to a single forward pass.

## Method

VD-LoRA replaces rigid geometric exclusion with a recursive variational posterior over each LoRA direction coefficient, where the posterior from the most recent activation of a direction serves as its prior in later tasks; the resulting posterior precision acts as a direction-wise consolidation strength that penalizes updates to highly confident directions more heavily via a closed-form Gaussian KL term. Before training each task, a lightweight gradient-probing stage computes a utility score for every candidate direction that jointly weights alignment with the current task gradient and residual posterior uncertainty, and the top-r directions are selected for adaptation. Unselected directions remain frozen but their uncertainty is used to construct scaled perturbations on layer inputs, enforcing prediction consistency via a stop-gradient KL loss to improve robustness to future reuse. At inference, posterior predictive averaging over the stored direction posteriors produces uncertainty-aware class predictions.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (N=10), VD-LoRA achieves 89.69% final accuracy and 92.72% average anytime accuracy (AAA), outperforming the strongest baseline LoRA-DRS (88.52/92.01) by over 1 point in Acc. On ImageNet-A, VD-LoRA reaches 60.27% Acc and 70.18% AAA, surpassing CL-LoRA (59.14/69.03) and LoRA-DRS (59.05/69.49). On VTAB, VD-LoRA attains 94.11% Acc and 94.45% AAA, leading CL-LoRA (93.66/94.13) and SD-LoRA (93.26/93.83). On ImageNet-R with 20 tasks, VD-LoRA maintains 77.53% Acc and 83.90% AAA, a 1.4-point Acc advantage over CL-LoRA (76.11/83.23), with the gap widening as task sequences grow longer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VD-LoRA outperforms all nine baselines across every reported dataset and metric. Against the strongest LoRA-based competitors, it leads CL-LoRA by 2.83 Acc points on CIFAR-100 (89.69 vs. 86.86) and by 1.13 Acc on ImageNet-A (60.27 vs. 59.14), while also achieving the best calibration (ECE 0.0120 vs. 0.0204). Over LoRA-DRS, the closest competitor on CIFAR-100, VD-LoRA gains 1.17 Acc and 0.71 AAA, and on VTAB it surpasses LoRA-DRS by 4.66 Acc (94.11 vs. 89.45). Prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) trail by 3–7 Acc points on CIFAR-100 and by 17–20 points on ImageNet-A, confirming the advantage of low-rank weight-space adaptation over prompt-space methods. Orthogonality-based baselines (InfLoRA, BiLoRA) show the largest degradation under long task sequences (e.g., InfLoRA drops to 70.09 Acc on ImageNet-R N=20 vs. VD-LoRA's 77.53), validating the paper's claim that uniform hard protection shrinks the effective update space. The advantage is most pronounced in the low-rank regime (r=1, r=5) and in long-horizon settings (N=20), where VD-LoRA's adaptive direction reuse preserves plasticity that rigid orthogonality cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

VD-LoRA sits at the intersection of two research threads: orthogonality-based LoRA for rehearsal-free CIL (InfLoRA, BiLoRA, PLAN), which enforces disjoint direction allocation to isolate task knowledge, and variational Bayesian continual learning (Nguyen et al.), which propagates recursive posteriors across tasks to balance stability and plasticity. The key departure is moving from a binary 'once-used, always-frozen' protection rule to a direction-wise, uncertainty-calibrated soft protection scheme: the recursive variational posterior over individual LoRA direction coefficients provides a per-direction consolidation strength that replaces uniform hard orthogonality. This allows the method to selectively reuse low-risk directions while still preserving strongly consolidated ones, something neither the deterministic LoRA-CIL line nor the full-weight Bayesian CL line addresses at the granularity of individual low-rank basis vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and increasingly relevant bottleneck: under tight rank budgets (r=1–10) and long task sequences (20+ tasks), uniform orthogonality progressively exhausts the available update space, causing gradient-subspace mismatch and plasticity collapse. By showing that direction-wise uncertainty tracking restores task-aligned capacity without sacrificing stability, VD-LoRA offers a principled recipe for LoRA-based CIL that scales to longer horizons. Open questions include whether the approach extends to online or task-free continual adaptation, whether adaptive rank allocation (deciding how many directions to activate per task) can further improve efficiency, and whether the variational posterior approximation remains accurate when directions are reused many times across very long sequences.
