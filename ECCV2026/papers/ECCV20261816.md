## Problem

Existing wearable motion capture methods are specialized to fixed sensor configurations (e.g., six-IMU suits or HMD-centric rigs) and cannot generalize to the diverse, unpredictable combinations of consumer devices people actually wear. No prior dataset jointly captures multiple commodity sensor types—smartphones, smartwatches, pressure insoles, and a VR headset—synchronized with high-quality multi-camera ground truth, making systematic cross-modal complementarity analysis impossible.

## Input / Output

The model accepts any arbitrary subset of five input channels: smartphone IMU (orientation, angular velocity, linear acceleration in device-local frame), smartwatch IMU (same signals), per-foot insole signals (16-zone plantar pressure + embedded IMU), VR headset 6-DoF pose in SE(3), and an optional discrete action label. All continuous signals are subsampled to 30 Hz and processed in 90-frame (3-second) sliding windows. The output is a full-body 3D skeletal trajectory as per-frame joint positions J_t ∈ R^{J×3}; at inference, the model produces K=10 samples whose average serves as the Bayes-optimal L2 estimate.

## Method

WHIP is a conditional flow-matching generative model built on a DiT backbone (8 transformer blocks, d=768, 12 heads) that learns a velocity field transporting Gaussian noise to the motion distribution conditioned on available sensors. The key architectural mechanism is a set of per-modality cross-attention modules: each sensor type has a dedicated module that is conditionally activated at inference—absent sensors are simply skipped—and the updates are aggregated with a 1/√|M| normalization to keep residual variance stable regardless of how many sensors are active. Global conditioning (flow time τ and action label) is injected via adaptive layer normalization, while time-aligned sensor signals are encoded as per-frame tokens through modality-specific MLPs. Training uses the conditional flow-matching objective with random per-sensor dropout (retention probability 0.5), exposing the model to all 2⁷ sensor subsets and making it robust to missing modalities at test time.

## Results

On the WHIP dataset, WHIP achieves 61.7 mm N-MPJPE using all IMU sensors (phones + watches), outperforming IMUPoser (75.5 mm) by 13.8 mm. With the HMD alone, WHIP reaches 104.6 mm N-MPJPE, beating EgoEgo (108.4 mm) and EgoAllo (125.9 mm). In the full-sensor setting (HMD + all IMUs), WHIP achieves 52.6 mm N-MPJPE, surpassing HMDPoser (74.6 mm) by 22 mm. On the cross-dataset Nymeria evaluation, adding wrist, pelvis, and contact cues progressively reduces N-MPJPE from 87.8 mm (VR-only) to 68.7 mm (full).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WHIP outperforms every compared baseline across all sensor configurations in which they overlap. Against IMUPoser [24], WHIP reduces N-MPJPE by 13.8 mm (61.7 vs 75.5) in the all-IMU setting and by 8.8 mm (82.0 vs 91.8) in the head+watches setting. For HMD-only reconstruction, WHIP (104.6 mm) beats EgoEgo [17] by 3.8 mm, EgoAllo [16] by 21.3 mm, and MocapEvery [16] by 36.6 mm. In the HMD + two watches configuration, WHIP (68.7 mm) surpasses AvatarPoser [14] by 9.5 mm, AGRoL [8] by 11.1 mm, and MocapEvery [16] by 17.4 mm. In the full-sensor setting, WHIP (52.6 mm) leads HMDPoser [7] by a substantial 22.0 mm margin. Notably, WHIP is trained for the more general task of handling arbitrary sensor subsets, yet still outperforms methods trained exclusively for their specific sensor configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

WHIP builds on the DiT transformer backbone and the conditional flow-matching generative framework, adapting both to the heterogeneous-sensor conditioning problem. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm in wearable motion capture—where methods like DIP, IMUPoser, AvatarPoser, and SolePoser each target a fixed, modality-specific sensor configuration—by formulating reconstruction as a problem over *arbitrary* subsets of consumer wearables (phones, watches, insoles, HMD). The per-modality cross-attention design with random modality dropout during training is the architectural mechanism that enables this generalization, and the systematic cross-modal complementarity analysis (marginal scores, pairwise interactions, Pareto frontier) has no direct precedent in prior wearable MoCap literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset is the first to jointly synchronize smartphones, smartwatches, pressure insoles, and a VR headset with professional multi-camera MoCap, filling a clear gap where prior benchmarks were siloed by modality. The sensor-complementarity findings—particularly that Watch+Phone outperforms any HMD-inclusive pairing at two devices, and that insoles exhibit the strongest pairwise synergy despite modest standalone contribution—offer practical guidance for hardware selection under budget constraints. Open questions include whether these complementarity rankings hold across activity types, whether the model can operate in real time on-device, and how conclusions shift when additional signals (e.g., hand tracking) are available.
