## Problem

Pre-trained image restoration models collapse on out-of-distribution real-world degradations, yet adapting them is difficult because target-domain paired ground truth is unavailable, traditional unpaired UDA requires intrusive architectural modifications, and zero-shot foundation models introduce prohibitive test-time latency and semantic hallucinations. The paper asks: can we distill the powerful generative prior of a large foundation model into a fast, unmodified restoration network using only unlabeled low-quality target observations?

## Input / Output

During adaptation, the method consumes only unlabeled, low-quality (LQ) degraded images from the target domain—no paired HQ counterparts, no target-domain clean images, and no manual annotations are required. At inference time, the adapted 1.3B-parameter LDM student takes a single degraded RGB image and produces a restored high-quality image in one forward pass with zero added latency relative to the original baseline. The offline adaptation pipeline (pseudo-target generation + distillation) takes roughly 3 hours on 4 TPUv5p chips followed by 4 hours on 32 TPUv5p chips, a one-time cost that is fully decoupled from deployment.

## Method

GMD reframes domain adaptation as geometric manifold alignment in three offline stages. Stage 0 runs the in-distribution LDM on OOD inputs to obtain off-manifold predictions that retain global structure but carry unmodeled artifacts. Stage 1 uses a frozen 12B FLUX.1.dev rectified flow model as a projection operator: a forward ODE lifts the off-manifold estimate into the isotropic noise prior (marginalizing artifacts), then a guided reverse ODE with classifier-free guidance (w=3.5) and attention injection (caching self-attention keys/values from the forward pass) projects the trajectory back onto the natural image manifold while rigidly locking spatial layout to prevent identity shift. A quality-gated NIMA filter (threshold ≥ 4.0) rejects off-manifold pseudo-targets before distillation. Stage 2 fine-tunes the student with a dual-objective loss that mixes 90% in-distribution paired data (structural anchor) with 10% filtered OOD pseudo-targets (manifold alignment), preventing catastrophic forgetting and error accumulation while internalizing the generative prior.

## Results

On REDS deblurring (GoPro→REDS), GMD achieves LPIPS 0.179, MUSIQ 63.67, FID 31.64, and CLIPIQA 0.404, outperforming all 8 baselines on every perceptual metric while remaining within 0.16 dB of the fully-supervised upper bound (PSNR 24.35 vs. 24.51). On RealBlur-J, GMD improves MUSIQ from 50.71 (baseline) to 61.33 and FID from 25.74 to 20.33. For real-world 2× SR (DIV2K→DPED-iPhone), GMD reduces NIQE from 5.47 to 4.30 and raises MANIQA from 0.543 to 0.627, surpassing StableSR and SeeSR. Critically, GMD matches the quality of online 12B FLUX projection (e.g., FID 31.64 vs. 32.10 for FLUX Kontext) while reducing inference latency from 7.67 s to 1.17 s by distilling the prior entirely offline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMD decisively wins on all perceptual quality metrics (LPIPS, NIMA, MUSIQ, FID, CLIPIQA) against every baseline on both REDS and RealBlur-J, with the largest gaps over generative-prior methods DA-CLIP (FID 42.90 vs. 31.64 on REDS) and AdaIR (FID 49.67 vs. 31.64). Against distortion-oriented CNN baselines (DeblurGANv2, Restormer, Uformer, MPRNet, Hi-Diff), GMD trades ~2–3 dB PSNR and ~0.1 SSIM for substantially better perceptual scores (e.g., MUSIQ 63.67 vs. 54.24 for Uformer on REDS), reflecting the paper's explicit perceptual-quality objective. On real-world 2× SR (DPED-iPhone), GMD surpasses StableSR (NIQE 4.30 vs. 4.48; MANIQA 0.627 vs. 0.607) and SeeSR (NIQE 4.30 vs. 5.11; MUSIQ 59.45 vs. 60.19, a near-tie) while also beating DA-CLIP by a wide margin. Compared to online zero-shot FLUX projections (SDEdit, FLUX Kontext, RF-Solver), GMD matches or slightly exceeds their perceptual quality (FID 31.64 vs. 32.10–40.09) while eliminating the 2.4–6.5 s additional inference latency and the 12B parameter overhead. GMD remains slightly below the fully-supervised upper bound on most metrics (e.g., MUSIQ 63.67 vs. 65.04 on REDS; FID 20.33 vs. 17.63 on RealBlur-J), confirming that the unsupervised adaptation recovers most but not all of the information available with paired ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

GMD sits at the intersection of unsupervised domain adaptation for image restoration and knowledge distillation from generative models. It departs from traditional UDA methods (CycleGAN-like frameworks, Deflow) that require architectural modifications or target-domain HQ images, and from zero-shot foundation-model approaches (SDEdit, RF-Solver) that incur prohibitive test-time latency and hallucination risk. The key conceptual move is repurposing a frozen 12B rectified-flow T2I model (FLUX) as an offline manifold projection operator, using ODE inversion with attention injection to generate pseudo-targets, then distilling into a 1.3B LDM student via source-anchored fine-tuning—effectively converting a test-time generative prior into a one-time training cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-inference-latency guarantee makes GMD practically deployable in settings where running a 12B model at test time is infeasible, and the architecture-agnostic design (validated on both diffusion and CNN students) broadens its applicability. However, the approach still requires a large foundation model for the offline pseudo-target generation phase (~3 hours on 4 TPUs), and the quality-gated filtering relies on a heuristic NIMA threshold whose optimal value may vary across domains. Open questions include whether the manifold-alignment framing generalizes to tasks beyond deblurring and SR (e.g., denoising, demosaicing), whether smaller teacher models can maintain the same fidelity, and how the method behaves under extreme domain shifts where the quality gate rejects most samples.
