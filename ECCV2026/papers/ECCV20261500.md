## Problem

Feed-forward reconstruction models (FFRMs) adapted to streaming via temporal causal attention face a fundamental dilemma: retaining all historical KV pairs causes rapid memory inflation, while enforcing fixed memory budgets leads to catastrophic forgetting of crucial context as the stream grows. No existing method can sustainably process thousands of frames on consumer-grade hardware without either exploding memory or degrading reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261500/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB video stream frame-by-frame in an online fashion, processing each new frame against a regulated KV-cache of historical context. It outputs per-frame camera poses, dense depth maps, and point maps in a single forward pass. All experiments run on a single 24GB NVIDIA RTX 3090, and the method is designed to handle sequences of up to 1,000+ frames with memory growing linearly at only ~1% of tokens per frame rather than the full token count.

## Method

RegVGGT is a training-free token regulation strategy built on the key observation that a token's initial attention saliency reliably predicts its long-term importance across the stream. It decouples the 24 transformer layers into two groups: query-agnostic layers (L_qa, layers 1–10 and 18–24) that permanently retain only the first frame's KV pairs as global anchors, and query-specific layers (L_qs, layers 11–17) that admit at most the top 1% most salient tokens per incoming frame. Saliency is estimated independently per attention head using a 2×2 spatially downsampled query subset, and exact attention scores are recovered on-the-fly via Log-Sum-Exp (LSE) statistics cached by FlashAttention, avoiding materialization of full attention maps. This yields a memory footprint that grows linearly at an extremely low rate while preserving geometric consistency, and the strategy is plug-and-play, transferable to other streaming baselines like Evict3R.

## Results

On camera pose estimation at 1000 frames, RegVGGT achieves ATE of 0.135 m and RPE-rot of 1.431° on TUM Dynamics, and ATE of 0.997 m and RPE-rot of 6.656° on ScanNet, outperforming the best baseline (XStreamVGGT) by 22% on TUM ATE and by 40% on ScanNet RPE-rot. On 3D reconstruction at 300 frames, RegVGGT achieves mean accuracy of 0.038 m on 7-Scenes (vs. 0.129 m for XStreamVGGT, a 70% reduction) and 0.068 m on NRGBD (vs. 0.102 m). Critically, while fixed-budget baselines degrade sharply as sequence length grows (e.g., Evict3R's 7-Scenes accuracy error surges >50% from 200 to 300 frames), RegVGGT remains stable with minimal degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegVGGT comprehensively outperforms all three fixed-budget streaming baselines across every metric and sequence length. XStreamVGGT [24] is the strongest baseline but still trails RegVGGT by 22–35% on ATE and by 35–40% on RPE-rot at 1000 frames; its advantage narrows at shorter sequences (800 frames) but the gap widens with length. InfiniteVGGT [35] is consistently mid-pack, losing to XStreamVGGT on translation metrics but winning on rotation for TUM at 800 frames; it degrades most severely at 1000 frames (RPE-rot 11.6° vs. RegVGGT's 6.7°). Evict3R [17] is the weakest baseline, showing the largest degradation with sequence length (ScanNet RPE-rot jumps from 14.8° at 800 frames to 15.3° at 1000 frames, while RegVGGT stays flat at ~6.7°). Against the unregulated StreamVGGT backbone [38] in challenging high-motion scenarios (Table 8), RegVGGT at γ=0.10 matches the backbone's ATE (0.154 vs. 0.159 m) while retaining only 10% of tokens per frame, confirming that the regulation introduces negligible quality loss even under stress.

## Relation to Prior Work

RegVGGT builds directly on StreamVGGT's temporal causal attention formulation for streaming 3D reconstruction, which itself extends VGGT's offline global-attention architecture to incremental frame-by-frame processing. It departs from concurrent memory-bounded streaming methods (Evict3R, InfiniteVGGT, XStreamVGGT) that enforce a fixed global token budget, instead proposing a per-frame regulated admission rate (1%) grounded in three empirical observations about token saliency temporal consistency, layer-specific attention structure, and head-specific attention patterns. The FlashAttention-compatible LSE-saliency mechanism is a novel practical contribution that enables exact attention-score-based token selection without materializing full attention matrices, complementing the broader FlashAttention ecosystem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to process thousands of frames on a single 24GB consumer GPU with only 1% per-frame token retention opens streaming 3D reconstruction to resource-constrained deployment settings (robotics, AR, autonomous driving) that were previously infeasible. The plug-and-play transferability demonstrated on Evict3R suggests the regulated-memory principle could generalize beyond VGGT-like architectures. Open questions include whether the 1% retention rate and the specific layer decoupling (75% of layers needing only frame-1 anchors) transfer to other transformer backbones or to scenes with highly non-stationary geometry, and whether the temporal consistency of saliency holds under domain shift or adversarial conditions.
