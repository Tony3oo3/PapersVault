## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting has become the dominant representation for novel view synthesis and spatial mapping, but it provides no principled signal about where its renderings are unreliable. Existing uncertainty estimation methods for 3DGS either require stochastic architectural modifications that degrade rendering fidelity and lack plug-and-play compatibility, or operate in parameter space via Hessian approximations that poorly capture view-dependent uncertainty. This paper addresses the need for a lightweight, post-hoc, pixel-wise uncertainty estimator that works with any 3DGS variant without altering the base representation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fully trained 3DGS model with all parameters frozen, the per-pixel reconstruction residuals (a fixed combination of L1 and DSSIM with λ=0.2) computed across all training views, and the corresponding camera poses. It outputs per-primitive uncertainty channels parameterized by spherical harmonics (degree 3 by default, adding 16 scalars per Gaussian), which are rasterized via the standard alpha-blending pipeline to produce a per-pixel uncertainty map at any arbitrary novel viewpoint at the same speed as RGB rendering.

## Method

The core insight is that reconstruction residuals on training views serve as a reliable proxy for expected photometric error in novel views, and this signal can be decomposed into per-primitive contributions via a linear least-squares formulation. Specifically, the volumetric blending weights (opacity × transmittance) for all pixels across all training views form a matrix A, and the per-primitive uncertainty values u are solved from min‖y − Au‖², where y contains the pixel-wise residuals; view-dependence is incorporated by absorbing SH basis functions into A, preserving linearity. Because the system is too large for direct solvers (millions of primitives × 16 SH coefficients), SGD is used, with gradients backpropagated only to the uncertainty channels while A remains fixed. A Bayesian-inspired L2 regularization term pushes the SH uncertainty functions toward a maximal isotropic uncertainty level b in viewing directions unsupported by training data, mitigating the sparse-view overfitting problem where low training residuals falsely signal zero predictive uncertainty.

## Results

On the primary UE benchmark (Table 1), the method achieves AUSE of 0.214 (DSSIM) and Pearson correlation of 0.547 on Mip-NeRF360, 0.233/0.571 on Tanks & Temples, and 0.356/0.244 on Deep Blending, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin (e.g., >3× higher Pearson and <half the AUSE vs. Var3DGS on Mip-NeRF360). Training overhead is minimal at 12.8–14.0% over vanilla 3DGS. In downstream tasks, uncertainty-guided AVS reaches PSNR 20.676 / SSIM 0.615 / LPIPS 0.344 on Mip-NeRF360, and uncertainty attenuation improves pose-agnostic SCD mIoU by up to +29.1% (Feature Diff.) and pose-agnostic AD AUPRO by up to +9.3% (SplatPose).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all three UE baselines on every dataset and metric. Against FisherRF [21], it reduces AUSE (DSSIM) from 0.606 to 0.214 on Mip-NeRF360 and flips Pearson from negative (−0.009) to strongly positive (0.547); against Manifold [37], AUSE drops from 0.559 to 0.214 while Pearson rises from −0.005 to 0.547; against Var3DGS [33], AUSE falls from 0.495 to 0.214 and Pearson jumps from 0.160 to 0.547, all at a fraction of the training overhead (12.8% vs. >100%). In active view selection, the method surpasses FisherRF (PSNR 20.676 vs. 20.266) and Manifold (20.676 vs. 19.732), and even the fairness-corrected Manifold† variant (20.676 vs. 20.088). For pose-agnostic SCD, uncertainty attenuation improves MV3DCD-ZS mIoU from 0.382 to 0.439 (+14.9%) and F1 from 0.526 to 0.593 (+12.7%), and boosts the already strong Online-SCD from 0.486 to 0.498 mIoU. In pose-agnostic AD, the method lifts SplatPose AUPRO from 0.700 to 0.765 (+9.3%) and SplatPosePlus AUPRO from 0.761 to 0.798 (+4.9%), with consistent AUROC gains of +0.7–1.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the scene representation, adding a per-primitive uncertainty channel that is rendered via the same α-blending pipeline used for color. It departs from the two dominant families of prior 3DGS uncertainty work: stochastic formulations (Stochastic-GS, Var3DGS, Manifold) that model distributions over Gaussian parameters at the cost of architectural modification and sampling overhead, and post-hoc parameter-space methods (FisherRF, POp-GS) that estimate epistemic uncertainty via Hessian/Fisher approximations. The key conceptual shift is formulating uncertainty estimation as a Bayesian-regularized linear least-squares problem over reconstruction residuals in rendered image space, rather than in parameter space, making it a lightweight, architecture-agnostic post-hoc step that preserves baseline fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a single, actionable per-pixel reliability signal that is directly consumable by downstream perception pipelines, this work moves 3DGS from a rendering engine toward a trustworthy spatial map for autonomous agents. The plug-and-play design (no architecture changes, ~13% training overhead) makes it broadly applicable across the rapidly expanding 3DGS ecosystem. Open questions remain: the spatial granularity of uncertainty is bottlenecked by Gaussian density in coarsely reconstructed regions, and the single scalar conflates aleatoric, epistemic, and optimization-related effects rather than disentangling them. Whether the residual-as-proxy assumption generalizes to dynamic scenes or extremely sparse captures beyond four views is untested.
