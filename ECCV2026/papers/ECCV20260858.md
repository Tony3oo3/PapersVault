## Problem

Existing learning-based image-to-point cloud registration methods follow an overlap-then-registration paradigm that first predicts a visible region and then restricts pose optimization to that region. This sequential decomposition is inherently fragile: inaccurate overlap prediction can discard geometrically consistent structures and irreversibly constrain pose estimation, a problem that is especially severe under sparse LiDAR observations and repetitive outdoor structures. The paper asks whether pose and geometry can instead be treated as jointly optimized, interdependent variables within a single end-to-end framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query RGB image (resized to 154×518 for KITTI, 154×308 for nuScenes, or 476×630 for indoor scenes, split into 14×14 patches) and a pre-built 3D point cloud (downsampled to 40,960 points, voxelized at 0.15 m outdoors or 0.025 m indoors). It outputs a 6-DoF camera pose (rotation as a normalized quaternion, translation as a 3-vector) in SE(3), a per-pixel depth map, and a per-pixel 3D point map in the world frame. The depth and point map losses are computed only on valid projected pixels determined by ground-truth LiDAR projection, and the pose loss supervises both intermediate and final predictions across four refinement stages.

## Method

PGCR reformulates registration as pose-geometry coupled reasoning: a learnable camera token representing the current pose hypothesis is iteratively updated through stacked transformer layers that alternate intra-modal self-attention and cross-modal attention between DINO-encoded image tokens and KPConv-encoded point features. Two key mechanisms make the coupling effective. First, Pose-guided Progressive Refinement (PPR) predicts an intermediate pose every two layers and uses it to filter the 3D point set to geometrically plausible regions via a decaying pixel/translation tolerance, so that cross-attention progressively focuses on visible, informative structures. Second, Geometry-aware Pose Refinement (GPR) projects the predicted depth map, point map, final image tokens, and camera token into a shared embedding space, fuses them through an MLP with average pooling, and regresses a refined pose, explicitly enforcing consistency between the estimated pose and the dense geometric predictions. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with a multi-task loss combining pose (intermediate + final), depth, and point map supervision.

## Results

On KITTI, PGCR achieves an RTE of 0.15 m, RRE of 0.44°, and a perfect 100% registration accuracy, surpassing the previous best (ICL) by 25.0% in translation and 64.5% in rotation error. On nuScenes, PGCR attains 0.43 m RTE and 0.88° RRE with 99.51% accuracy, reducing errors by 12.2% and 49.1% over the strongest baseline GraphI2P. On indoor benchmarks, PGCR achieves 0.035 m RTE / 2.22° RRE on RGB-D Scenes V2 and 0.049 m RTE / 2.62° RRE on 7-Scenes, outperforming all prior methods. PGCR also achieves the fastest inference time (0.17 s) among recent transformer-based approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGCR outperforms all 11 compared baselines across every metric on all four benchmarks. On KITTI, the closest competitor ICL [30] reports 0.20 m RTE and 1.24° RRE, while PGCR reduces these to 0.15 m and 0.44° (25% and 64.5% improvements respectively) and achieves 100% accuracy versus ICL's 97.49%. On nuScenes, GraphI2P [2] is the strongest baseline at 0.49 m / 1.73°, and PGCR further reduces errors to 0.43 m / 0.88° (12.2% and 49.1% gains). Among indoor methods, PGCR halves the RTE of CA-I2P [11] on RGB-D Scenes V2 (0.035 m vs. 0.061 m) and reduces RRE from 2.56° to 2.22°; on 7-Scenes it improves over B2-3Dnet [9] from 0.076 m / 3.23° to 0.049 m / 2.62°. Notably, PGCR also achieves the fastest inference time (0.17 s) compared to CoFiI2P (0.18 s), VP2P-Match (0.19 s), and ICL (0.24 s), while CorrI2P [36] is an order of magnitude slower at 8.96 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant overlap-then-registration paradigm (CorrI2P, CoFiI2P, VP2P-Match) by reformulating image-to-point cloud registration as a pose-geometry coupled reasoning problem, where camera pose and scene geometry are jointly optimized rather than sequentially decomposed. It repurposes well-established components—DINO for image encoding, KPConv for point cloud encoding, DPT for dense prediction, and iterative cross-modal attention (in the spirit of LoFTR and GeoTransformer)—but reorganizes them around a learnable camera token that drives progressive 2D-3D interaction. The key conceptual departure is that visibility and correspondence emerge implicitly through geometric consistency during joint optimization, eliminating the explicit overlap prediction stage that prior methods treat as a prerequisite.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the fragile overlap-prediction bottleneck, PGCR addresses a well-known failure mode in outdoor registration where sparse LiDAR and repetitive structures make overlap estimation unreliable, and it does so while achieving the fastest inference (0.17 s) among transformer-based competitors. The fact that a single unified formulation (no indoor-specific heuristics) outperforms dedicated indoor methods on RGB-D Scenes V2 and 7-Scenes suggests the pose-geometry coupling is a more general inductive bias than modality-specific design. Open questions include whether the dense geometry predictions (depth map, point map) can be directly exploited for downstream tasks like 3D reconstruction or SLAM, and whether the approach degrades gracefully under extreme sparsity or large domain shifts beyond the tested benchmarks.
