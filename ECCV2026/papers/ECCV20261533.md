## Problem

Progress in multimodal dental AI is hindered by the absence of large-scale datasets that jointly provide registered 3D intraoral scans, paired 2D intraoral photographs, and clinician-authored reports under multi-center, multi-vendor conditions. Existing resources are unimodal or only loosely aligned across modalities, preventing robust cross-modal reasoning for occlusal and orthodontic analysis. Furthermore, it remains unclear whether state-of-the-art general-purpose multimodal LLMs can produce clinically faithful orthodontic reports from such data.

## Input / Output

The IOP-only track consumes five standardized intraoral RGB photographs per patient (frontal, left/right buccal, upper/lower occlusal views at ~10 MP resolution, cropped and aligned to the anatomical region of interest), while the IOS-only track consumes mutually registered upper and lower arch point clouds (~114K vertices each, 65×40×35 mm bounding box) stored in a common RAS reference frame with occlusion preserved. The output is a template-conformant structured report consisting of discrete field:value pairs (e.g., molar/canine occlusion class, crowding severity, overbite, curve of Spee, midline deviation, transverse findings, missing teeth) plus short standardized narrative segments, designed to minimize hallucination surface area and enable automatic slot-level scoring.

## Method

IOS-Qwen augments Qwen3-VL-4B with a PointTransformer encoder that converts registered IOS point clouds into geometry tokens, which are projected via a lightweight MLP and concatenated with the vision-language prompt for token-by-token report generation. Three training strategies are explored: (♣) frozen LLM with jointly trained encoder and projector from scratch; (♦) PointTransformer pre-trained with per-field multi-class classification heads before the heads are replaced by the projector; and (♠) LoRA fine-tuning of Qwen while the base model and point encoder remain frozen. Two prompting regimes are evaluated: one-shot generation of all fields in a single response, and field-by-field multi-turn generation (one inference per field, ~9× computational cost but substantially improved coverage). The dataset itself was assembled via YGGDRASIL, a containerized Django-based web platform that enforces de-identification at ingestion, runs modality-aware inference pipelines (IOS segmentation, occlusal classification, CBCT segmentation) to pre-populate findings, and supports speech-to-text dictation and collaborative multi-center annotation with append-only audit logging.

## Results

On the IOP-only zero-shot track (300-patient test set), Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct leads among off-the-shelf MLLMs with BLEU-1 0.757, ROUGE-L F1 0.746, and Accuracy 0.580, yet all models show low clinical fidelity (RadFact F1 ≤ 0.368) and high variance. On the IOS-only track, the proposed IOS-Qwen♠ (LoRA fine-tuning of Qwen3-VL-4B with a PointTransformer encoder) achieves the best scores across all metrics: BLEU-1 0.781, Accuracy 0.632, Coverage 1.000, SBERT-sim 0.959, RadFact F1 0.372, and the highest clinical-preference Elo of 1048 (win rate 0.583). Inter-rater agreement (Accuracy 0.740, RadFact F1 0.650) and intra-rater agreement (Accuracy 0.860, RadFact F1 0.810) establish the human ceiling, showing that even the best model remains well below clinician-level consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the six off-the-shelf MLLMs evaluated zero-shot on intraoral photographs, Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct achieves the highest text-overlap metrics (BLEU-1 0.757, ROUGE-L F1 0.746) and the best task-specific Accuracy (0.580), but its clinical RadFact F1 (0.358) and Elo (934) are modest. MedGemma-4B-IT, despite lower text metrics, earns the highest clinical-preference Elo (1016) and win rate (0.554) among off-the-shelf models, suggesting clinicians value its report style even when lexical overlap is lower. GPT-5.2 and Gemini 3 Flash show high variance (±0.17–0.24 on BLEU-1) and mid-range Accuracy (0.447 and 0.411), while Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct is the weakest overall (BLEU-1 0.527, Coverage only 0.638). The proposed IOS-Qwen♠ outperforms every off-the-shelf model on Accuracy (+5.2 pp over Qwen3-VL-4B), SBERT-sim (+1.7 pp), RadFact F1 (+0.4 pp over Qwen2.5-VL-7B), and clinical Elo (+32 over MedGemma-4B), confirming that geometry-aware 3D conditioning yields clinically more faithful reports than 2D-only zero-shot inference. However, even IOS-Qwen♠ remains substantially below inter-rater agreement (Accuracy 0.740, RadFact F1 0.650), indicating significant room for improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the medical report-generation line (chest X-ray, brain MRI) into the dental domain by introducing the first dataset that jointly provides registered upper/lower intraoral scans, paired intraoral photographs, and clinician-authored orthodontic reports. Architecturally, IOS-Qwen is a straightforward fusion of a PointTransformer 3D encoder (following Point-BERT-style pre-training) with the Qwen3-VL vision-language backbone, connected via a lightweight projector and optionally LoRA-adapted. It departs from prior dental VLM work (DentVLM, Oral-GPT, ChatIOS), which targets 2D radiographs or single-modality 3D scans, by requiring cross-modal reasoning between high-resolution 3D geometry and 2D photometric appearance within a shared occlusal frame.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset and YGGDRASIL platform address a concrete bottleneck in dental AI: the absence of multi-center, multi-vendor, multimodal resources with surface-level registration, enabling the community to move beyond unimodal or loosely-aligned benchmarks. The systematic negative result—that GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Flash, MedGemma, and other SOTA MLLMs produce clinically unfaithful reports (e.g., MedGemma-27B predicting 'Class I' for every patient)—quantifies the gap and motivates geometry-aware adaptation. Open questions include whether the template-constrained formulation generalizes beyond occlusal assessment, whether the 1,000-patient scale suffices for robust cross-center generalization, and whether the modest IOS-Qwen gains (accuracy 0.632 vs. inter-rater 0.740) can be closed with richer 3D encoders or larger training sets.
