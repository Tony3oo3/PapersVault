## Problem

Transferring human hand motion demonstrations to dexterous robot hands is hindered by a kinematic gap: the demonstrated joint motion is often not jointly realizable by the robot under contact dynamics. Existing tracking approaches rely on fixed reward weights or per-sequence hand-crafted tuning to balance object tracking against motion fidelity, which breaks under limited interaction budgets and does not generalize across clips of varying difficulty. The open question is how to adapt the task-success vs. motion-style trade-off online without manual per-clip tuning.

## Input / Output

The policy observes the current simulator state (robot joint positions/velocities, object poses/velocities) together with the reference time index t, and consumes time-indexed targets: a retargeted joint trajectory q_t^ref, per-object translation and quaternion targets, and link-level binary contact events with object-local contact points. It outputs a residual joint displacement a_t ∈ R^D that is added to the reference to form the position target q_t^tar = q_t^ref + a_t, keeping exploration anchored near the demonstrated motion while permitting corrective deviations. One policy is trained per reference clip under a fixed 5000-update PPO budget, and the resulting joint commands can be streamed to a real bimanual xArm7+xHand platform at 250 Hz.

## Method

ConTrack formulates reference tracking as a constrained MDP where object pose tracking is a constraint (J_g ≥ α·J_g*) and style fidelity (joint and contact matching) is the objective to maximize. A scalar dual variable λ is updated online via λ ← λ + η(α − Ĵ_g/J_g*), and the sigmoid of λ sets a convex mixing weight w_task that blends task and style advantages in the PPO update, automatically shifting optimization pressure toward whichever objective is currently under-satisfied. To stabilize long-horizon learning, an adaptive mid-trajectory reset library stores simulator states from policy-reachable rollouts and samples reset frames proportional to exp(−u_k/τ), where u_k is a survival ratio that concentrates training on the current failure boundary. Contact priors from reference annotations provide an auxiliary style reward that penalizes both missed contact events and contact-point displacement, anchoring the style objective to demonstrated interaction patterns.

## Results

Under a fixed 5000 PPO-update budget across 13 clips from GRAB, ARCTIC, and DexterHand, ConTrack achieves a mean progress of 0.899 (±0.195) and contact F1 of 0.784 (±0.072), substantially outperforming all baselines on both metrics. Object pose tracking is competitive: mean translation error is 0.026 m and rotation error is 0.272 rad, with ManipTrans achieving slightly lower pose errors (0.012 m / 0.207 rad) but at the cost of much lower progress (0.743) and contact fidelity (F1 0.620). Per-tier, ConTrack reaches near-perfect progress on GRAB (0.996) and strong progress on ARCTIC (0.944), while DexterHand in-hand rotation remains the hardest tier (0.733 mean progress).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConTrack dominates on the two metrics that capture long-horizon tracking success: progress (0.899 vs. 0.743 for ManipTrans, 0.246 for DexMachina, 0.444 for SPIDER) and contact F1 (0.784 vs. 0.620, 0.708, 0.191). ManipTrans achieves the lowest object pose errors (0.012 m translation, 0.207 rad rotation) but sacrifices finger fidelity (0.277 rad error) and contact accuracy, reflecting its residual-RL design that prioritizes object tracking over hand motion. DexMachina preserves finger kinematics best (0.147 rad) and maintains reasonable contact F1 (0.708), yet its functional-retargeting objective yields only 0.246 mean progress under the same budget, indicating it cannot sustain tracking through contact-rich phases. SPIDER, a purely optimization-based retargeting method without policy learning, stays close in joint space (0.157 rad finger error) but fails catastrophically on object tracking (0.201 m translation error, 1.104 rad rotation error) and contact fidelity (F1 0.191) once dynamics dominate. Overall, ConTrack is the only method that simultaneously achieves high progress, high contact fidelity, and competitive object pose accuracy, validating the constrained formulation that allocates remaining control authority to style after satisfying the task constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConTrack sits at the intersection of reference-tracking RL for physics-based character control (DeepMimic, ASE) and dexterous hand-object manipulation from human demonstrations (ManipTrans, DexMachina, SPIDER). Its core methodological departure is importing the constrained-RL / Lagrangian-relaxation framework from CPO and constrained style learning to replace fixed task-style reward weights with an online dual-variable update, so the trade-off adapts per-clip and per-phase without manual tuning. The adaptive mid-trajectory reset library extends curriculum-style mid-clip training by refreshing stored states from policy-reachable rollouts rather than copying reference configurations, which is critical in contact-rich settings where reference states may be physically inconsistent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, interpretable mechanism (the target ratio α) for practitioners to navigate the task-success vs. motion-fidelity trade-off in dexterous tracking without per-sequence reward engineering, and the Pareto-frontier visualization makes this trade-off concrete. Limitations include reliance on high-quality contact annotations, a sim-to-real gap that is only partially bridged (joint-command streaming rather than closed-loop perception), and persistent difficulty on the hardest in-hand rotation clips under a fixed budget. Open questions include whether the constrained formulation generalizes to multi-task or zero-shot settings, and whether richer sim-to-real alignment (force sensing, visual feedback) can close the remaining execution gap on real hardware.
