## Problem

Standard softmax attention in transformers amplifies dominant positive scores while suppressing negative ones, producing noisy attention patterns that dilute relevant features and misallocate weight to adversarial or irrelevant tokens. Existing remedies like differential attention subtract two attention signals but project both branches onto a single value subspace, risking cancellation of useful overlapping signals. The paper asks whether explicitly modeling positive and negative token interactions in separate, quasi-orthogonal subspaces can improve discriminability without losing informative signal.

## Input / Output

For image classification, DnA consumes 224×224 RGB images (patchified into 16×16 tokens) and outputs class labels; for video, it takes 8 frames at 224×224 and produces action class labels; for video LLMs, it ingests video frames plus a text question and generates a text answer. DnA is a drop-in replacement for the self- or cross-attention module inside existing ViT, TimeSformer, and VisCoP architectures, so the overall input format, tokenization, and output head remain unchanged from the base model.

## Method

DnA replaces each attention head with two parallel branches: a positive branch that applies softmax to Q⁺Kᵀ/√d and projects onto a value subspace V⁺, and a negative branch that applies softmin (i.e., softmax on negated scores) to Q⁻Kᵀ/√d and projects onto a separate value subspace V⁻, with a learnable scalar α_h balancing the two. The key architectural distinction from differential attention is the use of two independent value matrices W_{V⁺} and W_{V⁻}, so the branches operate in quasi-orthogonal subspaces rather than a shared one, preventing cancellation of overlapping useful signals. This design is theoretically grounded in a classification-error bound showing that larger principal angles between class subspaces reduce error, and in a maximum-entropy argument justifying the simultaneous use of softmax and softmin when neither extreme is known a priori. The authors prove (Theorem 3) that Gaussian initialization of the value matrices yields quasi-orthogonal subspaces with high probability, eliminating the need for explicit orthogonality constraints during training. DnA is integrated into ViT-B for ImageNet-1K, TimeSformer for video action recognition, and the VisCoP cross-attention adapter in VideoLLaMA3 for egocentric video QA.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, DnA with a ViT-B backbone achieves 81.9% top-1 test accuracy, an absolute gain of +0.8% over the ViT-B baseline (81.1%) and +0.4% over both differential attention and cog attention (81.5%). On ImageNet-A (adversarial robustness), DnA achieves 27.7% accuracy, 24.2 RMSE, and 40.7 AURRA, outperforming all baselines on calibration and reliability metrics. For video understanding, DnA integrated into TimeSformer yields +1.3% (CS) and +4.0% (CV2) on Toyota Smarthome and +1.2% (CS) on NTU RGB+D 60, while in the VideoLLaMA3 + VisCoP pipeline it achieves +0.5% average on Ego-in-Exo PerceptionMCQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DnA consistently outperforms all six baselines across tasks. Against ViT-B (Dosovitskiy et al., ICLR 2021), DnA gains +0.8% top-1 on ImageNet-1K test and improves ImageNet-A RMSE from 26.6 to 24.2. Against Differential Attention (Ye et al., ICLR 2025), DnA achieves +0.4% on ImageNet-1K test and +0.2% AURRA on ImageNet-A; the paper attributes this to DnA's two separate value subspaces avoiding signal cancellation that occurs when both branches share a single V. Against Cog Attention (Lv et al., 2024), DnA also leads by +0.4% on ImageNet-1K test and +1.9 AURRA on ImageNet-A. For video, DnA integrated into TimeSformer (Bertasius et al., ICML 2021) beats the baseline by +1.3%/+4.0% on Toyota Smarthome and +1.2%/+0.6% on NTU60, while differential attention actually degrades Toyota Smarthome CS (66.6 vs. 67.5). In the video LLM setting, DnA within the VisCoP (Reilly et al., ECCV 2026) adapter outperforms both the raw VideoLLaMA3 (Zhang et al., 2025) baseline (72.8 avg.) and VisCoP without DnA (78.1 avg.), reaching 78.6% average on Ego-in-Exo PerceptionMCQ.

## Relation to Prior Work

DnA extends differential attention [62], which was designed for NLP and projects both positive and negative query-key interactions onto a single shared value subspace, by introducing two quasi-orthogonal value subspaces (V+ and V−) and replacing the second softmax with a softmin activation grounded in maximum-entropy theory. It also relates to cog attention [40], which introduces negative attention weights but without the subspace-separation principle. The theoretical motivation draws on subspace classification bounds (principal angles) [25] to argue that separating positive and negative token interactions into distinct subspaces reduces classification error, a perspective not exploited in prior attention variants for vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling negative token interactions in separate subspaces yields consistent gains across image classification, video transformers, and video LLMs, with the most notable improvement (4.0%) on cross-view video action recognition where adversarial context is prevalent. The quasi-orthogonality result (Theorem 3) and empirical validation (intruder dimensions, Frobenius inner product analysis) provide a principled justification for the design, suggesting the denoising principle could generalize to other attention-based architectures. However, the added parameters (up to 23% in video transformers) and the modest 0.8% ImageNet-1K gain limit immediate adoption; the authors acknowledge the need for more parameter-efficient implementations as future work.
