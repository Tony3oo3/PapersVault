## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models for video spatial reasoning are semantics-first and lack explicit mechanisms for maintaining spatial consistency across viewpoints. This leads to two practical failures: adjacent frames introduce redundant spatial evidence that inflates memory and computation without proportional accuracy gains, and mismatched or redundant evidence causes unstable, viewpoint-dependent answers. The paper asks whether geometric consistency can serve as both an evidence-organization principle and an explicit learning signal to address both issues simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of RGB video frames and a natural-language spatial query. Each frame is processed by a frozen SigLIP2 encoder for patch-level visual tokens and a frozen VGGT geometry encoder for spatial tokens plus explicit camera pose and per-frame depth maps. The output is a single text answer (multiple-choice label or numerical estimate). Internally, the Geometry-Consistent Memory is capped at M=64 entries with 512-dim features organized into 4 chunks, and hierarchical retrieval selects top-K=8 chunks and frames, keeping the final context compact regardless of video length.

## Method

ConsiSpace introduces a Geometry-Consistent Memory (GCM) with two complementary banks: an implicit bank storing packed visual-spatial evidence tokens and an explicit bank storing aligned camera poses and depth cues. The memory lifecycle is governed by three geometry-guided operations: (1) gated writing that suppresses redundant frames when inter-frame translation and rotation fall below thresholds, (2) geometry-consistent fusion that merges entries within a spatial neighborhood by semantic similarity, and (3) coarse-to-fine retrieval that combines semantic relevance with a direction-consistency term to filter geometrically inconsistent evidence. After supervised fine-tuning on VSI-590K and a newly constructed nuScenes-10K dataset, the model is further refined by Unified Consistency Self-Supervised Reinforcement Learning (UC-SSRL), which samples two views of the same question and optimizes a unified reward combining answer-distribution KL divergence, metric-distance agreement, and symmetric topological-relational KL, updating only LoRA parameters to enforce cross-view stability without additional annotations.

## Results

ConsiSpace with UC-SSRL achieves 76.6 average on VSI-Bench, 53.0 on OSI-Bench, and 57.5/58.1 (Sufficient-Coverage/Uniform-50) on MMSI-Video-Bench, improving the cross-benchmark average by 12.6 points over the strongest baselines. On VSI-Bench, it outperforms the prior best specialized model SpaceMind (69.6) by +7.0 points and the open-source backbone Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (57.4) by +19.2 points. On OSI-Bench, it surpasses VLM-3R (40.3) by +12.7 points, and on MMSI-Video-Bench it exceeds VLM-3R by +14.9 to +15.0 points across both input settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConsiSpace (UC-SSRL) wins across all three benchmarks against every baseline tested. On VSI-Bench, it leads SpaceMind (69.6) by +7.0, VLM-3R (60.9) by +15.7, and the open-source backbone Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (57.4) by +19.2 points. On OSI-Bench, the margin over VLM-3R (40.3) is +12.7 points and over Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (31.2) is +21.8 points, with particularly large gains on metric (NA) sub-tasks. On MMSI-Video-Bench, ConsiSpace exceeds VLM-3R by +14.9 (Sufficient-Coverage) and +15.0 (Uniform-50), and surpasses proprietary models like Gemini 2.5 Flash-Thinking (36.7/35.2) by over 20 points. The UC-SSRL stage adds a consistent +5.4 to +7.2 points over the SFT-only variant across benchmarks, confirming that consistency rewards refine viewpoint stability beyond what supervised fine-tuning alone provides. No baseline outperforms ConsiSpace on any benchmark or sub-task reported in the main tables.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConsiSpace sits at the intersection of spatial-aware MLLMs (VLM-3R, SpaceMind, GeoThinker, Spatial-MLLM) and memory-augmented video understanding, but departs from both by treating geometric consistency not as an input feature or training signal but as an explicit operational criterion governing the full memory lifecycle—when to write, how to fuse, and what to retrieve. The UC-SSRL objective extends the self-supervised RL line (Spatial-SSRL, Spacer) by deriving rewards from multi-view task consistency rather than intrinsic pretext tasks, and by operating post-SFT with only LoRA updates. The geometry foundation model (VGGT) is used as a frozen cue provider rather than a jointly trained component, distinguishing it from approaches that fuse geometry features end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a compact, geometry-gated memory can simultaneously improve accuracy and reduce peak GPU memory (35 GB vs. 78.5 GB at 200 frames) compared to dense-evidence baselines, which is practically important for long-video deployment. The robustness to 30% noisy geometry (Table 8) suggests the framework does not require perfect 3D estimates, lowering the barrier to real-world use. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the single Qwen3-VL-8B backbone, whether it handles dynamic scenes where static-scene consistency assumptions break down, and whether the VGGT dependency can be replaced by lighter geometry estimators without degrading the memory operations.
