## Problem

Existing incremental 3D object detection methods (SDCoT, SDCoT++, AIC3DOD) rely on pseudo-labeling to retain old-class knowledge but were designed for only 1–2 incremental stages. In long-incremental settings (≥3 stages), a self-reinforcing cycle emerges: distribution shift on novel classes degrades old-class performance, which corrupts pseudo-labels, which further accelerates forgetting. The paper formalizes this with a Wasserstein-distance-based forgetting upper bound and shows empirically that even the strongest baselines collapse to near-zero mAP by the 10th stage.

## Input / Output

At each incremental stage the detector receives indoor 3D point clouds reconstructed from RGB-D scans, but ground-truth annotations cover only the novel classes introduced at that stage; old-class objects present in the same scenes remain unlabeled. The method additionally consumes a fixed-budget memory bank (10% of the full training set) of previously annotated scenes, which provides supervised signals for old classes. Output is oriented 3D bounding boxes with class labels for all seen classes, evaluated at IoU 0.25; learning-dynamics monitoring uses per-class recall at IoU 0.5.

## Method

LDMR converts per-class recall trajectories (learning dynamics) measured at periodic sub-stage checkpoints into two training decisions. First, human-like intra-stage review splits each stage into I=5 sub-stages, computes a scene-level forgetting metric from recall drops on the memory bank, and boosts the sampling weight of heavily-forgotten scenes in the next sub-stage so the model re-learns what it just lost. Second, scene-aware cross-stage memory evolution scores every candidate scene by a learnability term (class under-learning weight × log object count × cumulative positive recall gain) and a diversity term (distance in a 2-D embedding of learning-dynamics patterns from already-stored scenes), then performs iterative add/evict updates to the memory bank. This rehearsal-based mechanism is complementary to the pseudo-labeling and distillation inherited from the SDCoT++ backbone, and the paper shows via ablation that both the adaptive review and the learned memory selection are necessary for the gains.

## Results

On SUN RGB-D (40 classes, TR3D backbone), LDMR achieves 29.12 / 25.10 / 19.38 mAP@0.25 at 3/5/10 incremental stages, outperforming the strongest pseudo-labeling baseline SDCoT++ (22.92 / 14.28 / 8.45) by 6.20 / 10.82 / 10.93 mAP respectively. On ScanNetV2 (35 classes, TR3D), LDMR reaches 39.00 / 28.38 / 17.82 mAP@0.25, exceeding SDCoT++ (35.15 / 20.61 / 7.06) by 3.85 / 7.77 / 10.76 mAP. The gains grow with the number of stages, with the 10-stage protocol showing the largest margin, confirming that LDMR's learning-dynamics-driven review and memory evolution are most effective under prolonged incremental sequences. Joint training (non-incremental upper bound) reaches 33.26 / 51.52 mAP@0.25 on SUN RGB-D / ScanNetV2 (TR3D).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDMR outperforms all four baselines across every protocol and backbone. Against SDCoT++ [41] (the strongest pseudo-labeling baseline), LDMR gains 6.20 mAP at 3 stages and 10.93 mAP at 10 stages on SUN RGB-D (TR3D), and 3.85 to 10.76 mAP on ScanNetV2 (TR3D), with the gap widening as stages increase. Against AIC3DOD [4], which uses a point-transformer backbone and room-layout constraints, LDMR leads by 6.69 mAP at 3 stages and 10.27 mAP at 10 stages on SUN RGB-D (TR3D). Against SDCoT [40], the original static-dynamic co-teaching method, LDMR improves by 7.02 mAP at 3 stages and 10.66 mAP at 10 stages on SUN RGB-D (TR3D). Against CPDet3D [43], adapted as a sparse-supervised baseline, LDMR achieves substantially higher performance (29.12 vs. 8.71 at 3 stages on SUN RGB-D TR3D). Notably, adding a random memory bank to SDCoT++ (SDCoT++ & Random Memory) already yields a large boost (e.g., 15.54 vs. 8.45 at 10 stages on SUN RGB-D TR3D), but LDMR's scene-aware memory evolution and intra-stage review push performance further to 19.38, confirming that the learning-dynamics-driven selection strategy matters beyond merely having a memory bank.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the incremental 3D object detection (I3DOD) line of work—SDCoT, SDCoT++, and AIC3DOD—which rely on pseudo-labeling and teacher-student distillation for short-incremental (T=2) settings, by formulating and addressing the long-incremental (T≥3) variant where these methods suffer severe degradation. It departs from prior I3DOD by introducing a memory-bank-based rehearsal mechanism (first applied to 3D detection) and by driving both intra-stage review and cross-stage memory evolution with per-class learning-dynamics signals, drawing an explicit analogy to the human 'learn-evaluate-review' process. The theoretical analysis (Wasserstein-based forgetting bound) connects the empirical degradation to gradient misalignment under distribution shift, providing a principled justification for the proposed review mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-reinforcing cycle diagnosis (distribution shift → old-class forgetting → accumulated pseudo-label error → further forgetting) gives the field a concrete mechanistic explanation for why pseudo-labeling-based I3DOD breaks down over long horizons, and the memory-bank approach offers a practical remedy that is orthogonal to and combinable with existing pseudo-labeling strategies. Limitations include evaluation only on indoor benchmarks (SUN RGB-D, ScanNetV2) with a fixed 10% memory budget, and the sub-stage review adds training overhead (I=5 checkpoints per stage). Open questions include whether the learning-dynamics-driven selection generalizes to outdoor/autonomous-driving 3D detection, open-vocabulary settings, or much larger class spaces, and whether the 2D embedding for diversity is sufficient for high-dimensional scene representations.
