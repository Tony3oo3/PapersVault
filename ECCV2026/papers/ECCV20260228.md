## Problem

Existing source-agnostic diffusion-based I2I translation methods (SDEdit, ILVR) modify image regions indiscriminately, failing to preserve domain-invariant structure, while mask-guided approaches (DiffEdit) rely on a single fixed threshold at one time step and often require source-domain knowledge. No prior method adaptively determines which regions to occlude at each noise level during the reverse diffusion process while remaining fully source-agnostic.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260228/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB source image from any domain and a pretrained unconditional diffusion model trained exclusively on the target domain; for PIE-Bench it additionally accepts a text editing prompt and operates in the VAE latent space of Sable Diffusion v1.5. The source image is noised at every time step using a shared noise vector ε_const to isolate prediction discrepancies. The output is a translated image in the target domain plus a sequence of progressively growing binary masks [M_T, …, M_1], each dilated and Gaussian-blurred (kernel 11, σ=5) before being applied during RePaint inpainting over 1000 DDIM steps.

## Method

PM-Edit computes a time-dependent mask at every DDIM step by taking the element-wise L1 distance D_t between the network's x_0-projection f_θ of the noised masked reference and the generated latent, then binarizing against a dynamic threshold δ_t = μ_{D_t} + λσ_{D_t}. The statistics μ and σ are precomputed once per target-domain model by measuring prediction discrepancies on in-domain samples at each time step, replacing the arbitrary fixed threshold used in DiffEdit. The mask is cumulative (M_t = M_{t+1} ∪ ΔM_t), so it grows as finer details emerge from noise, ensuring large structural edits happen early while local refinements occur late. Discrepancies are averaged over N=10 noise-seed batches to suppress outliers. Finally, a modified RePaint inpainting procedure applies the corresponding mask M_t at each backward step (with periodic 10-step reversals every 20 steps, repeated twice), producing a globally coherent translated image without any source-domain training.

## Results

On the Wild→Dog task (AFHQ), PM-Edit achieves the best FID (55.64), KID (17.2×10³), and LPIPS (0.445) among all compared methods, outperforming CycleDiffusion (FID 56.10, KID 19.5, LPIPS 0.465). On Cat→Dog, PM-Edit attains the best KID (18.3) and LPIPS (0.415), though CycleDiffusion edges ahead on FID (58.87 vs. 61.31). On Male→Female (Celeba-HQ), PM-Edit achieves the best KID (42.10), SSIM (0.586), and LPIPS (0.356), while CycleDiffusion retains a lower FID (45.04 vs. 50.99). On PIE-Bench, PM-Edit leads all baselines in structure distance (19.68×10³), LPIPS (83.51×10³), SSIM (0.819), and CLIP similarity (26.43 whole / 22.81 edited).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PM-Edit consistently outperforms the two source-agnostic diffusion baselines (ILVR and SDEdit) across all three translation tasks and all four metrics, with particularly large margins on KID (e.g., 17.2 vs. 36.6 on Wild→Dog) and LPIPS (0.445 vs. 0.530). Against the strongest source-domain-trained competitors EGSDE and CycleDiffusion, PM-Edit wins on KID and LPIPS in every task (e.g., KID 17.2 vs. 19.5 and LPIPS 0.445 vs. 0.465 on Wild→Dog; KID 42.10 vs. 45.11 on Male→Female) while remaining source-agnostic, though CycleDiffusion retains a lower FID on Cat→Dog (58.87 vs. 61.31) and Male→Female (45.04 vs. 50.99). On the Cat→Dog task, PM-Edit also surpasses all five optimal-transport-based baselines (NOT, DIOTM, ASBM, DSBM, EGEOT) on KID and LPIPS, with EGEOT being the closest FID competitor (53.29). On PIE-Bench, PM-Edit achieves the best structure distance (19.68×10³ vs. 22.13 for LatentEdit), best LPIPS (83.51×10³ vs. 84.45 for Wang et al.), best SSIM (0.819), and highest CLIP similarity (26.43 whole / 22.81 edited) among all five text-guided editing baselines. The paper notes that KID and LPIPS are considered more reliable than FID and SSIM respectively for small evaluation sets, which contextualizes the FID gaps with CycleDiffusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mask-guided diffusion paradigm introduced by DiffEdit, which computes a single binary mask at one fixed time step using a constant threshold, into a progressive framework where the mask is refined at every DDIM step with a time-dependent statistical threshold. It builds on RePaint's iterative inpainting procedure but replaces the static mask with a sequence of evolving masks, and leverages DDIM's deterministic sampling to compute prediction discrepancies between the noised reference and the generated sample. Unlike source-dependent methods (EGSDE, CycleDiffusion) that require training or energy functions on the source domain, PM-Edit is fully source-agnostic, needing only a pretrained target-domain denoiser and a precomputed set of per-time-step discrepancy statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The source-agnostic, training-free nature of the method makes it immediately applicable to any target domain for which a diffusion model exists, lowering the barrier to practical I2I translation in settings like medical imaging or industrial inspection where paired data is unavailable. The single hyperparameter λ providing a clean realism–faithfulness tradeoff is a practical advantage over methods requiring per-task tuning. A stated limitation is the assumption that source and target share the same modality, and the authors suggest that more sophisticated discrepancy metrics could enable cross-domain translation. Open questions include whether the precomputed threshold statistics transfer robustly across architectures and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation.
