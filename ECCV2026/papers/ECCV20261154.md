## Problem

State-of-the-art composed visual data retrieval methods rely on multimodal large language models to fuse a reference image/video with a textual modification, incurring prohibitive query-time latency that limits scalability. While recent work showed that simple spherical linear interpolation in embedding space can be effective, it uses a single dataset-level weight that ignores the fact that the optimal balance between visual and textual cues varies per query. The core difficulty is that the optimal interpolation weight is defined by discriminability against hard negatives rather than proximity to the positive target, making it intractable to compute directly at scale.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image (or the middle frame of a reference video) and a short textual modification as the composite query, and produces a single fused embedding in the BLIP-2 shared space via spherical linear interpolation with a predicted scalar weight α ∈ [0,1]. At inference, a compact memory bank of 1,024 EMA-updated target prototypes supplies conditioning tokens to the weight predictor, since true batch negatives are unavailable. The weight predictor itself is a 2-layer transformer encoder plus 3-layer MLP with only 1.63M parameters (0.14% of the full model), adding merely 0.03 ms per query.

## Method

SRAIN encodes the reference visual input and modification text independently through a frozen ViT-G/14 and Q-Former (BLIP-2), then fuses them via spherical linear interpolation with a sample-specific weight predicted by a lightweight module. The key training innovation is batch-wise rank-aware weight estimation: for each mini-batch, K=101 candidate weights are evaluated and the optimal α* is selected as the one that minimizes the rank of the positive target among all B targets in the batch, providing a near-optimal ground-truth label without exhaustive search. A conditioned weight prediction mechanism addresses the dependence of α* on hard negatives by feeding the predictor the positive target embedding and m−1 hardest negatives as auxiliary tokens during training, and at inference substitutes top-n prototypes retrieved from the memory bank across multiple candidate weights. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 fine-tunes the Q-Former with a hard-negative contrastive loss using the estimated α*, and Stage 2 freezes the encoders and trains the weight predictor to regress α* via MSE, preventing the supervision signal from drifting as encoder representations change.

## Results

On WebVid-CoVR, SRAIN achieves R@1 of 61.07, R@5 of 84.90, R@10 of 91.20, and R@50 of 98.40, setting a new state of the art for composed video retrieval without external models. On CIRR, SRAIN reaches R@1 of 50.95, R@5 of 81.21, and R@10 of 89.60, surpassing all prior methods. On CIRCO (zero-shot), SRAIN attains mAP@5 of 26.16 and mAP@50 of 30.81, outperforming all methods that do not rely on external knowledge and remaining competitive with MLLM-based approaches. On FashionIQ (fine-tuned), SRAIN achieves average R@10 of 49.26 and R@50 of 70.27, matching or exceeding the best prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On WebVid-CoVR, SRAIN outperforms Thawakar et al. [40] (which uses MiniGPT4-generated captions and cross-attention) by 0.95% in R@1 (61.07 vs. 60.12) and CoVR-2 [42] with cross-attention by 1.25% in R@1 (61.07 vs. 59.82), while using only linear interpolation and no external models. On CIRR, SRAIN surpasses CoVR-2 [42] by 0.08% R@1, 0.41% R@5, and 0.76% R@10, and beats CoVR [43] by 1.26% R@1. On CIRCO (zero-shot), SRAIN outperforms all non-external-knowledge baselines including CoVR-2 [42] (26.16 vs. 25.06 mAP@5), BASIC [26] (26.16 vs. 19.98), and LinCIR [11] (26.16 vs. 19.71), but trails MLLM-based methods like OSrCIR [38] (30.47 mAP@5) and CIReVL [15] (26.77 mAP@5) that leverage GPT-4o/GPT-4. On FashionIQ (fine-tuned), SRAIN edges out CoVR-2 [42] on average R@10 (49.26 vs. 48.86) but is marginally behind on R@50 (70.27 vs. 70.46), and outperforms CLIP4CIR [4] by 13.87% R@10 and CoVR [43] by 0.73% R@10. The key differentiator is that SRAIN achieves these results with a 1.63M-parameter weight predictor (0.14% of total model size) and no MLLM dependency, yielding ×24 higher throughput than MLLM-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRAIN extends the spherical linear interpolation (Slerp) framework of Jang et al. [14], which demonstrated that a single dataset-level interpolation weight between visual and textual embeddings can yield competitive zero-shot composed retrieval. The key departure is making the weight sample-specific and rank-aware, addressing the observation that the optimal balance between reference and modification varies per query. Conceptually, the paper also positions itself against the dominant MLLM-based fusion paradigm (CIReVL, OSrCIR, CoLLM) and cross-attention fusion (CoVR, CoVR-2, Thawakar et al.), arguing that a lightweight scalar prediction plus linear interpolation can match or exceed these heavier approaches without external model calls.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 24× throughput improvement over MLLM-based methods while matching or exceeding their retrieval accuracy makes composed visual retrieval practically deployable in latency-sensitive applications like media editing and recommendation. The large oracle gap (71.95 vs. 61.07 R@1 on WebVid-CoVR) indicates the current weight predictor underutilizes the potential of sample-wise weighting, leaving clear room for improvement in prediction accuracy, especially for extreme weight values. Open questions include whether the memory-bank conditioning generalizes to truly open-world retrieval databases, whether the two-stage training constraint can be relaxed, and whether the approach extends to multi-turn or iterative query refinement settings.
