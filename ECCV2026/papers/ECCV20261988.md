## Problem

Visual Autoregressive (VAR) modeling suffers from two inherent deficiencies in multi-scale representation learning: a single shared Transformer architecture must simultaneously capture global semantics at low scales and fine-grained details at high scales, inducing optimization conflicts; and the causal coarse-to-fine generation process causes semantic errors at early scales to propagate and amplify in later predictions, degrading final output quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261988/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label as conditioning and generates 256×256 (or 512×512) RGB images via next-scale prediction, producing tokens from small to large scales in 10 autoregressive steps with parallel decoding within each scale. The tokenizer decomposes each image into K multi-scale residual representations, and the model predicts quantized tokens at each scale conditioned on all preceding accumulated residual features.

## Method

MEPA introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, a Scale-aware Token-routed Mixture of Experts (STMoE) replaces the shared FFN with multiple expert FFNs, where the router computes token-to-expert affinity by combining learnable scale embeddings with token features, enabling adaptive expert selection that varies both across scales and within a scale based on token semantics and spatial position—avoiding the routing homogenization of pure scale-based routing while maintaining load balance via an auxiliary loss. Second, a Semantic Guidance (SG) loss aligns intermediate-layer VAR representations with pretrained DINOv3 features, but critically applies this alignment to cumulatively aggregated residual features at early and intermediate scales (scales 1–5, 1–6, 1–7) rather than the final complete representation, directly targeting the causal error-propagation problem by strengthening semantic correctness where it matters most for downstream predictions.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, MEPA-d16 (585M params, 10 steps) achieves FID 2.32 and IS 311.26 after 200 epochs, outperforming the VAR-d16 baseline (FID 3.55) by 1.23 FID and VAR-d20 (FID 2.95) by 0.63 FID. Under a reduced 100-epoch budget, MEPA-d16 reaches FID 2.65, still surpassing VAR-d16 trained for its full 200 epochs (FID 3.55) and VAR-d20 for 250 epochs (FID 2.95), demonstrating a ~2× training speedup. The smaller MEPA-d12 (252M params) achieves FID 2.86 at 200 epochs, beating the larger VAR-d16 (310M, FID 3.55) with fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEPA-d16 (200 ep, FID 2.32) outperforms all VAR-family baselines: VAR-d16 (FID 3.55, +1.23), VAR-d20 (FID 2.95, +0.63), FlexVAR-d20 (FID 2.41, +0.09), and SpectralAR-d20 (FID 2.49, +0.17), while also matching or exceeding the best GAN (StyleGAN-XL, FID 2.30) and diffusion (DiT-XL/2, FID 2.27) methods at 10× fewer generation steps. The training-efficiency advantage is most striking at 100 epochs: MEPA-d16 (FID 2.65) beats VAR-d16‡ (FID 4.10) by 1.45 FID and FlexVAR-d20‡ (FID 5.77) by 3.12 FID, and even surpasses the fully-trained VAR-d16 (200 ep, FID 3.55) and VAR-d20 (250 ep, FID 2.95). MEPA-d12 (252M params) achieves FID 2.86, beating the larger VAR-d16 (310M, FID 3.55) with ~19% fewer parameters. Against non-VAR AR methods, MEPA-d16's FID 2.32 is competitive with MAR-B (FID 2.31, 800 epochs) and PAR-XL (FID 2.61, 300 epochs) while requiring only 200 epochs and 10 generation steps. MEPA's IS of 311.26 is the highest among all compared methods, exceeding VAR-d20 (302.6) and SpectralAR-d20 (305.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VAR (Visual Autoregressive Modeling) paradigm by introducing a scale-aware token-routed Mixture of Experts (STMoE) to decouple representation learning across the multi-scale residual hierarchy, and adapts the representation alignment concept from REPA (which targets diffusion transformers) to the fundamentally different VAR residual feature space. The key conceptual departure is that VAR's residual, coarse-to-fine structure creates an inherent feature gap with self-supervised encoders' complete patch representations, requiring a novel residual aggregation scheme rather than naive alignment. The MoE design evolves from a scale-based router (SMoE) to a token-level router that incorporates scale embeddings, addressing routing homogenization and load imbalance within the same scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2× training speedup with simultaneous FID improvement addresses a practical bottleneck for AR image generation, where training cost has been a persistent concern relative to diffusion models. The finding that semantic guidance must target early/intermediate scales (not the final aggregated representation) to prevent error propagation is a useful design principle specific to causal multi-scale generation. However, the evaluation is limited to ImageNet class-conditional generation, the 512×512 results are preliminary (66 epochs), and the approach's transferability to other AR paradigms (next-token, next-patch) or to text-to-image settings remains unexplored.
