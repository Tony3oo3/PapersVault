## Problem

While dataset distillation methods have made rapid empirical progress, the factors that make one distilled surrogate more effective than another remain poorly understood. Prior work largely enforces distributional similarity to the original data, yet the most distributionally similar surrogate is not always the most effective for training. This paper asks what properties beyond global distributional proximity govern a surrogate's training effectiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260927/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real labeled image dataset (256×256 RGB), a discrete visual tokenizer (e.g., multi-scale VQ-VAE with 10 scales), and a pre-trained diffusion model (DiT or LDM). It produces a distilled synthetic dataset of IPC-specified images per class, along with a per-dataset structural score computed as a weighted linear combination of three token-level statistics (JSD, HHI, COV) averaged over all surrogate samples. The tokenizer maps each image to a fused probability vector over a finite codebook vocabulary, enabling direct statistical analysis of visual primitive composition.

## Method

The paper introduces a two-part framework. First, a training-free structural score assesses distilled dataset quality by computing three complementary token-level metrics per image: Jensen-Shannon Divergence to the class token centroid (contextual fit), Herfindahl-Hirschman Index over the fused multi-scale token distribution (compositional balance), and coverage of TF-IDF-identified class-discriminative tokens (categorical presence). A linear regression on these metrics predicts validation accuracy, revealing that balanced token composition (low HHI) is the dominant factor, while distributional divergence from the original data does not reliably correlate with performance. Second, the Token-Guided Dataset Distillation (TGDD) method leverages this score for generation: it clusters real images in the discrete token space (after PCA and L2-normalization), ranks candidates within each cluster by structural score to select anchors, averages anchor embeddings into mode latents, and steers diffusion denoising toward these modes. This discrete-space clustering and score-based anchor selection replaces the continuous-feature centroids used in prior diffusion-based DD methods, yielding more effective guidance across multiple tokenizers and diffusion backbones.

## Results

On ImageWoof (the primary fine-grained benchmark), TGDD achieves 60.3% top-1 accuracy with ResNetAP-10 at IPC=50, outperforming the prior SOTA MGD³ (56.5%) by 3.8 points and MiniMax (56.3%) by 4.0 points. At IPC=100, TGDD reaches 67.3% (ResNetAP-10) and 70.1% (ResNet-18), maintaining a consistent lead over MGD³ (66.5% and 68.8% respectively). On ImageNet-1k at IPC=50, TGDD achieves 60.3% top-1 accuracy, marginally surpassing MGD³ (60.2%) and MiniMax (58.6%). The paper reports up to a 5.4% improvement over MGD³ on ImageWoof (IPC=70, ResNet-18: 65.1% vs. 59.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGDD consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every tested configuration. Against the closest competitor MGD³ [3] (the prior SOTA diffusion-based DD method), TGDD gains 3.8 points at IPC=50 (ResNetAP-10, ImageWoof) and up to 5.4 points at IPC=70 (ResNet-18). MiniMax [11] is the second-strongest generative baseline, trailing TGDD by 4.0 points at IPC=50 on ImageWoof and 1.7 points on ImageNet-1k (IPC=50). Optimization-based methods (DM [47], SRe²L [44]) and selection-based methods (RDED [35], Herding [42]) are substantially weaker, with DM lagging TGDD by over 13 points at IPC=50 on ImageWoof. On ImageNet-100, TGDD surpasses IDC-1 [16] by 2.2 points (IPC=20, ResNetAP-10: 35.2% vs. 33.0%). In the cross-architecture study (Table 10), TGDD with discrete tokenizers (VQ-VAE, VQGAN, BEiTv2) outperforms continuous guidance from DINOv2 [25], CLIP [29], and VAE [17] by 2–4 points on ImageWoof, confirming that the discrete structural score provides a more effective anchor selection signal than continuous feature centroids.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MGD3 (Chan Santiago et al., ICML 2025), which introduced mode-guided diffusion for dataset distillation by clustering in continuous feature space and steering denoising toward cluster centroids. The key departure is a shift from continuous embeddings to discrete visual tokenizers (VQ-VAE, VQGAN, BEiTv2), where images are represented as finite-vocabulary token sequences, enabling direct statistical analysis of compositional structure. Rather than relying on distributional proximity metrics (gradient matching, feature regression, optimal transport) that dominate prior DD work, the paper introduces a training-free structural score combining JSD, HHI, and TF-IDF-based coverage to assess and guide surrogate quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that token composition balance (HHI) is the strongest predictor of distilled dataset quality, while distributional divergence (JSD) does not reliably correlate with performance, challenges the prevailing assumption in DD that closer distributional matching yields better surrogates. The structural score generalizes across domains (ImageNet → EuroSAT) without retraining, offering a lightweight diagnostic tool for practitioners. Open questions include whether the linear combination of metrics is optimal, whether the approach extends to non-visual or multimodal tokenization, and whether the discrete-token perspective can inform data selection beyond the distillation setting.
