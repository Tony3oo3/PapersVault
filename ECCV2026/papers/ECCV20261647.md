## Problem

Streaming video editing is blocked by two fundamental bottlenecks: (1) naively truncating bidirectional diffusion models to causal execution causes a severe attention distribution shift that produces flickering and structural forgetting, and (2) recomputing dense FFN and Attention modules over static, unedited background regions every frame incurs prohibitive per-frame latency that precludes real-time interactive deployment on edge devices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261647/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source video stream processed in sequential chunks of 3 latent frames (no future frames accessed) and a text editing prompt. The source latent and noisy latent are channel-wise concatenated rather than token-wise appended, preserving the original sequence length to avoid quadratic attention blowup. Output is a stream of edited frames at 12.66 FPS (79 ms per frame), with strict pixel-level preservation guaranteed in all regions outside the active editing mask.

## Method

LiveEdit employs a progressive three-stage distillation pipeline: Stage 1 trains a bidirectional DiT (Wan2.1-T2V 1.3B) with full attention under MSE loss to acquire editing capability; Stage 2 introduces chunk-wise causal attention via teacher forcing to transition the architecture to autoregressive execution without structural collapse; Stage 3 applies Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) with a frozen Real Score and trainable Fake Score to compress inference to 4 NFEs, initialized directly from Stage 2 weights to bypass expensive ODE initialization. At inference, an AR-oriented Mask Cache computes the L2 distance between the previous chunk's edited and source latents to produce a binary spatial mask, routing only active editing tokens through full Self-Attention while directly reusing cached intermediate features for static background tokens, dynamically pruning ~70% of redundant computation per frame.

## Results

On a 120-pair streaming video editing benchmark, LiveEdit (with Mask Cache) achieves the best Text Alignment (0.270), Background Consistency (0.956), and Motion Smoothness (0.992) among all baselines. The no-cache variant further attains the highest Dynamic Degree (0.282) and Imaging Quality (0.720). In terms of efficiency, the 4-step DMD model with AR-oriented Mask Cache achieves 12.66 FPS (79 ms per frame), a dramatic speedup over the 100-NFE bidirectional baselines, making it suitable for real-time interactive and AR applications.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiveEdit (W/ Cache) outperforms all six baselines on Text Alignment, with the largest margin over StreamDiffusion (0.270 vs. 0.239) and StreamV2V (0.270 vs. 0.244), confirming superior instruction adherence. Against the strongest offline editor InsV2V, LiveEdit leads on TA (0.270 vs. 0.259), BC (0.956 vs. 0.943), and DD (0.256 vs. 0.196) while matching on IQ (0.708). Among streaming baselines, LiveEdit dominates StreamDiffusionV2 on TA (0.270 vs. 0.252), AQ (0.581 vs. 0.539), and IQ (0.708 vs. 0.653), and beats StreamV2V on every metric except IQ (0.708 vs. 0.712). The only metric where a baseline edges out the cached model is Aesthetic Quality, where StreamDiffusion scores 0.590 vs. 0.581, though the no-cache variant recovers to 0.584. Overall, LiveEdit is the only method that simultaneously achieves top-tier editing fidelity and real-time throughput (12.66 FPS), whereas all baselines either sacrifice background consistency (StreamDiffusion, BC 0.886) or require offline batch processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: bidirectional video diffusion editing (Wan2.1-T2V, LucyEdit, InsV2V), streaming autoregressive video generation (StreamDiffusion, Self Forcing, Diffusion Forcing), and step-distillation for fast inference (DMD, progressive adversarial distillation). Its key conceptual departure is identifying that streaming *generation* mechanisms (serial feedback, generation-oriented KV caches) are fundamentally misaligned with streaming *editing*, which requires strict preservation of unedited regions rather than free-form motion synthesis. The three-stage distillation pipeline (bidirectional → causal via teacher forcing → 4-step DMD) is structurally similar to FlashVSR's progressive acceleration for super-resolution, but adapted here to handle the attention distribution shift and region-specific control that general editing demands.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiveEdit is among the first frameworks to make text-guided streaming video editing practically deployable at 12.66 FPS, opening the door to real-time AR and interactive editing applications that were previously infeasible. The empirical finding that Self-Attention features are temporally redundant (safe to cache) while FFN features are not provides a useful architectural insight for future efficient video models. However, the evaluation is limited to a 1.3B model, 20K training pairs, and a 120-pair benchmark, leaving open questions about scalability to larger models, multi-object or multi-edit scenarios, and robustness to rapidly changing scenes where the 70% token-pruning assumption may break down.
