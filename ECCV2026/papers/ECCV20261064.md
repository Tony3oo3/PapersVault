## Problem

Direct preference optimization (DPO) for multimodal LLMs overfits to superficial linguistic cues—high-frequency phrases and spurious correlation tokens—rather than causally grounded visual evidence, producing fluent but hallucinated outputs. Static preference supervision cannot handle distributional shifts across visual-textual contexts, and simply scaling augmented preference data (e.g., 5× LLM-based augmentation) fails to close the hallucination gap, indicating the issue lies in the optimization landscape rather than data coverage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261064/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, TARS takes a standard RGB image and a free-form text prompt and generates an autoregressive text response, identical to the base LLaVA-v1.5 pipeline. During training, the text prompt is dynamically perturbed at the token level: a CLIP-based cross-modal alignment score identifies visually agnostic tokens (those with minimal image-text grounding), which are then masked or synonym-replaced per sample. The model is trained on 4.8k preference pairs (preferred vs. dispreferred responses) from the RLHF-V dataset, with no expert or GPT-4V feedback required.

## Method

TARS reformulates DPO as a min-max game: the inner maximization applies worst-case perturbations to visual-agnostic tokens (selected adaptively via a confidence-margin rule on CLIP similarity scores) to induce distributional shifts, while the outer minimization enforces preference alignment under these shifts. A novel spectral alignment loss computed via FFT on hidden states regularizes the model in the frequency domain, exploiting the DFT shift property so that local token perturbations contribute only bounded additive terms to each frequency bin—preserving dominant low-frequency semantic structure while tolerating position-specific noise, unlike spatial losses (ℓ₂, cosine) that reintroduce spurious positional correlations. The final objective combines the standard DPO loss on perturbed inputs with the spectral preference loss weighted by λ, trained end-to-end on LLaVA-v1.5 (7B/13B) with only 4.8k preference samples, halving the hallucination rate (26.4%→13.2%) and outperforming 5× data augmentation on all benchmarks.

## Results

On LLaVA-v1.5-7B, TARS (Mask) reduces the AMBER hallucination rate from 35.4% (base) to 13.2%, halves the DPO baseline (26.4%), and drops the cognition inconsistency score from 4.2 to 0.4. On the 13B scale, TARS achieves a Hal-Rate of 12.5% and Cover of 59.8%, surpassing GPT-4o on Hal-Rate (12.5% vs. 17.6%) while approaching it on Cover (59.8% vs. 60.9%). On OBJHal, response-level CRs drops from 54.0% to 12.0% (7B) and 50.0% to 14.6% (13B), and POPE accuracy reaches 88.7% (+8.7 pp over base LLaVA-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TARS (Mask) on LLaVA-v1.5-7B substantially outperforms standard DPO on every AMBER metric: Hal-Rate 13.2% vs. 26.4%, Cover 59.6% vs. 56.6%, and Cog 0.4 vs. 2.5. Against CHiP-DPO, TARS wins on Hal-Rate (13.2% vs. 19.9%) and Cog (0.4 vs. 1.0), though CHiP-DPO achieves a lower OBJHal CRs (7.3 vs. 12.0). Compared to OPA-DPO, TARS trades a marginal Hal-Rate difference (13.2% vs. 12.5%) for a large Cover advantage (59.6% vs. 47.4%) and a lower Cog (0.4 vs. 0.9). At the 13B scale, TARS beats GPT-4o on AMBER Hal-Rate (12.5% vs. 17.6%) and Cog (0.6 vs. 0.8) while trailing slightly on Cover (59.8% vs. 60.9%), and it surpasses DeepSeek-VL2-27B on Hal-Rate (12.5% vs. 16.3%) despite being roughly 2× smaller. On POPE, TARS achieves 88.7% accuracy and 97.5% precision (7B), exceeding all DPO-based baselines and confirming that hallucination reduction does not come at the cost of factual grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO-based hallucination mitigation line (CHiP-DPO, OPA-DPO) by reformulating static preference optimization as a min-max game over visual-agnostic tokens, drawing on adversarial training ideas from vision-language pretraining. It departs from prior DPO variants in two specific ways: (1) the inner maximization uses CLIP-based cross-modal alignment scores to selectively perturb tokens with minimal visual grounding, and (2) a novel FFT-based spectral alignment loss replaces spatial regularization, motivated by the DFT shift property that makes frequency magnitudes robust to local token perturbations. The paper explicitly contrasts its optimization-landscape-reshaping approach with data augmentation, arguing that adversarial perturbation per sample is more effective than simply scaling the preference dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 4.8k adversarially perturbed samples outperform 28.8k LLM-augmented samples suggests that how you shape the optimization landscape matters more than raw data volume for preference alignment, a principle that could generalize beyond MLLM hallucination. However, the evidence is confined to LLaVA-v1.5 (7B/13B) and Muffin-13B, and the spectral loss's practical gain over spatial alternatives is modest (~1.9 pp in Hal-Rate), leaving open whether the FFT regularization transfers to other architectures or modalities. A key open question is whether the min-max formulation's benefits persist at larger scales or with more diverse preference data, and whether the CLIP-based token selection generalizes to models without a separate vision-language encoder.
