## Problem

Existing preference-based alignment methods for diffusion models, such as Diffusion DPO, derive their supervision targets from the forward noising process q(x_{t-1} | x_t, x_0) conditioned on terminal samples, which does not reflect the model's actual backward denoising transitions at inference time. This mismatch means the per-step supervision signal is only an approximation of what the model truly does during reverse denoising, limiting alignment quality. The paper asks whether preference supervision can be grounded directly in the backward denoising process rather than in forward-process surrogates.

## Input / Output

For the training-free variant (TF-CPP), the method consumes an original text prompt c and a semantically degraded variant c^- (constructed by randomly removing 40–70% of tokens or via LLaMA-based rewriting), along with images generated by a frozen reference model under each condition. For the data-driven variant (DD-CPP), it consumes preference-labeled image pairs (x_0^w, x_0^l | c) from datasets like Pick-a-Pic. The output is a fine-tuned diffusion model (U-Net or DiT-based) that produces images with improved text-image alignment or aesthetic quality; no new output format is introduced beyond standard image generation.

## Method

DDSPO formulates preference optimization in score space at each diffusion timestep by comparing denoising scores from a contrastive policy pair (winning and losing denoising policies) rather than from forward-process noise targets. The key loss (Eq. 4) pushes the student model's predicted noise ε_θ toward the winning policy's score ε_★^w while pushing it away from the losing policy's score ε_★^l, weighted by a reference-model baseline, all within a sigmoid-gated Bradley-Terry objective. Two instantiations of the contrastive pair are provided: DD-CPP fine-tunes separate winning and losing models on preferred/dispreferred samples, while TF-CPP uses a single pretrained reference model conditioned on the original prompt c (winning direction) and a degraded prompt c^- (losing direction), requiring no additional training or preference labels. This stepwise, policy-grounded supervision replaces the forward-process approximation used by Diffusion DPO, and the paper shows Diffusion DPO is a special case of DDSPO where the contrastive policies are defined as the forward posterior. The approach is architecture-agnostic, applying to both U-Net diffusion models and DiT-based flow-matching models.

## Results

On SD-1.4 with TF-CPP-constructed preference data, DDSPO achieves the best GenEval score (0.5045) and lowest FID (16.39) among all preference-optimization methods, outperforming D-DPO (0.4841 GenEval, 18.02 FID) and DSPO (0.4841 GenEval, 18.28 FID). Across diverse backbones, DDSPO consistently improves alignment: SDXL gains +0.082 GenEval (0.5229→0.6049) and +0.082 CompBench (0.4034→0.4857), while SD3-M gains +0.026 GenEval (0.7123→0.7384) and +0.059 CompBench (0.5155→0.5740). For aesthetic quality on SDXL with Pick-a-Pic, DDSPO reaches 29.09 HPSv2 and 22.71 PickScore, competitive with SPO (29.21/23.11) and MAPO (28.22/22.30).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDSPO outperforms D-DPO on all four metrics (GenEval +0.020, CompBench +0.010, FID −1.63, IS +1.46), confirming that stepwise contrastive-policy-pair supervision is more effective than forward-process-based supervision. Against DSPO, DDSPO wins on GenEval (+0.020), FID (−1.89), and IS (+2.23), while DSPO narrowly leads on CompBench (0.3793 vs 0.3823 — actually DDSPO also wins here by 0.003). The DSPO+CPP variant (DSPO with the same CPP targets) also improves over vanilla DSPO, showing the benefit of CPP supervision is objective-agnostic. Against SOTA alignment methods, DDSPO (without human annotations) beats CaPO on both GenEval (0.6049 vs 0.5900) and CompBench (0.5064 vs 0.4652) on SDXL, and on SD3-M surpasses CaPO by +0.028 GenEval and +0.050 CompBench; however, Itercomp slightly edges DDSPO on GenEval (0.6108 vs 0.6049) while DDSPO leads on CompBench (0.5064 vs 0.4644). For aesthetic quality, DDSPO trails SPO on HPSv2 (29.09 vs 29.21 on SDXL) but leads on PickScore (22.71 vs 23.11 — actually SPO leads here too at 23.11); DDSPO's TF-CPP variant (no human data) remains competitive at 28.78 HPSv2 / 22.70 PickScore.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDSPO extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) to diffusion models by redefining preference supervision from final samples to intermediate denoising transitions, showing that Diffusion DPO (Wallace et al., 2024) is a special case where the contrastive policy pair is replaced by the forward-process posterior. It also generalizes DSPO (Zhu et al., 2025) by substituting its forward-process-derived score targets with policy-grounded backward denoising scores from a contrastive pair. Unlike SPO (Liang et al., 2025), which requires a step-aware reward model and stochastic multi-candidate sampling, DDSPO derives stepwise contrastive targets directly from a policy pair, making it applicable to both diffusion and deterministic flow-matching architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stepwise contrastive policy pair formulation provides a principled way to align diffusion models without human-annotated preference data (via TF-CPP) or reward models, and the training-free variant's competitiveness with supervised methods is practically significant for practitioners lacking preference datasets. However, aesthetic quality gains remain modest relative to D-KTO and SPO on some metrics, and FID increases across all preference-optimization methods suggest a distribution-shift trade-off that is not fully resolved. Open questions include whether the contrastive policy pair idea extends to video or 3D generation, whether the efficient unpaired variant can be further tightened, and whether the forward-process mismatch identified here affects other diffusion fine-tuning paradigms beyond preference optimization.
