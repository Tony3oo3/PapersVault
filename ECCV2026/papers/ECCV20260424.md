## Problem

Most masked autoencoder frameworks for video and audio rely on random masking, which ignores the spatiotemporal coherence of video and the structured time-frequency patterns of audio spectrograms. This paper asks whether data-independent, structured masking—derived from filtered noise distributions—can better align with modality-specific inductive biases and improve representation learning without any additional computational overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260424/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips partitioned into spatiotemporal patches, audio spectrograms partitioned into time-frequency patches, or joint audio-visual pairs. Structured masks are precomputed as tensors (e.g., Green3D masks of shape N×64×64×64) and resized at training time to match the input volume (e.g., 14×14×8), undergoing standard augmentations like random flipping. The masked autoencoder then reconstructs the full input from visible tokens, and the trained encoder (with decoder discarded) provides learned representations for downstream tasks.

## Method

The core mechanism filters white noise through Gaussian kernels to produce color-noise masks with controlled spectral properties, then selects the noise type per modality. For video, Green3D masking convolves 3D white noise with two 3D Gaussian kernels of scales σ₁ < σ₂ (randomly sampled from [0.5, 2] per sequence) to yield mid-frequency spatiotemporal patterns that evolve smoothly across frames, unlike static tube masking. For audio, Regularized Blue Noise (R-BN) starts from blue noise (white minus low-pass filtered white) and applies an iterative optimization that, at each spatial position, selects the candidate mask minimizing a four-directional clustering score within a local window, enforcing uniform separation of visible spectrogram patches. For audio-visual pretraining, Green3D and R-BN are applied independently to each stream. All masks are fully data-independent, precomputed, and introduce zero additional training cost, making the approach a plug-and-play replacement for random masking in existing frameworks.

## Results

On Something-Something V2 (in-domain), Green3D masking improves VideoMAE from 69.6% to 70.8% top-1 accuracy (+1.2%) and SIGMA from 71.2% to 72.0% (+0.8%), while on Kinetics-400 it yields +0.5% and +0.6% respectively. For unsupervised video object segmentation on DAVIS, VideoMAE+Green3D achieves 38.2 mIoU in clustering, a +8.7% gain over the random-masking baseline and +7.2% over motion-guided MGMAE. On audio, R-BN masking improves AudioMAE by +0.7% on AudioSet-20K and +0.9% on AudioSet-2M, and MaskSpec by +1.1% and +0.5% respectively. In the audio-visual setting, combining Green3D and R-BN within CAV-MAE yields +0.6% to +0.8% across audio-only, video-only, and joint evaluation on VGGSound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against random-masking video baselines (VideoMAE, CMAE-V, OmniMAE, MME), Green3D consistently improves top-1 accuracy by 0.5–1.2% on SSv2 and K400, with the largest gains on the temporally demanding SSv2 benchmark. Against motion-guided data-adaptive methods (MGM, MGMAE), VideoMAE+Green3D matches or surpasses them on SSv2 (70.8 vs. 70.6 for MGM in-domain; 69.7 vs. 68.9 for MGMAE cross-domain) while being fully data-independent and incurring zero additional computational cost, whereas MGMAE is ~1.5× slower. On unsupervised video object segmentation (DAVIS clustering), VideoMAE+Green3D (38.2 mIoU) outperforms both MGM (36.5) and MGMAE (31.0), confirming stronger spatiotemporal object representations. For audio, R-BN improves over AudioMAE (36.1→36.8 on AS-20K) and MaskSpec (32.3→33.4 on AS-20K), outperforming all listed audio baselines including SS-AST and MAE-AST without requiring extra speech data or predefined time-frequency rules. In the audio-visual CAV-MAE setting, the combined Green3D+R-BN masking beats the random-masking CAV-MAE baseline by +0.6% to +0.8% across all three evaluation modes (audio-only, video-only, audio-visual) on VGGSound. On the SEVERE generalization benchmark, SIGMA+Green3D achieves a mean score of 67.3 versus 64.2 for SIGMA (+3.1%), surpassing all other methods including MME (65.0) and MGM (62.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends ColorMAE (Hinojosa et al., ECCV 2024), which introduced 2D color-noise-based masking for image transformers, to the spatiotemporal (video) and time-frequency (audio) domains. For video, it generalizes 2D green noise to a 3D formulation (Green3D) that produces masks evolving smoothly across frames, departing from both random tube masking (VideoMAE, SIGMA) and data-adaptive motion-guided masking (MGMAE, MGM) by remaining fully data-independent and precomputable. For audio, it introduces an optimization-based regularization (R-BN) on top of vanilla blue noise to enforce uniform patch separation in spectrograms, addressing a gap left by prior data-independent audio masking (SpecAugment, MaskSpec). The paper positions itself as a zero-overhead, plug-and-drop replacement for random masking across existing masked autoencoder frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a precomputed, modality-specific mask tensor can be dropped into any existing MAE-style pipeline with no architectural changes, no auxiliary networks, and no additional compute, yet consistently yields 0.5–1.2% gains across video, audio, and audio-visual benchmarks. The finding that a simple frequency-filtered noise pattern can match or exceed motion-guided methods (e.g., outperforming MGM in-domain on SSv2 and MGMAE cross-domain) without optical flow or motion vectors is a useful data point for practitioners seeking efficient pretraining. Open questions include whether the modality-to-color mapping (green→video, blue→audio) generalizes to other modalities (text, 3D point clouds, multi-sensor), whether the stochastic sampling of (σ₁, σ₂) is truly necessary or a hyperparameter that could be fixed, and whether the gains persist at larger model scales (ViT-L/H) or with higher masking ratios where the reconstruction task becomes extremely difficult.
