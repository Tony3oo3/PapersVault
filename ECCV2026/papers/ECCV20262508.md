## Problem

Existing training-free image editing methods based on diffusion inversion suffer from three interrelated limitations: ODE inversion introduces reconstruction errors requiring costly multi-evaluation corrections, SDE inversion (e.g., DDPM inversion) achieves exact reconstruction but exhibits poor editing plasticity and artifacts under large semantic changes, and both paradigms lack reliable mechanisms to preserve unedited regions without user-provided masks or additional inference branches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262508/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image (512×512) and a free-form text prompt describing the desired edit as the sole inputs—no masks, no blended-word tokens, and no tuning parameters are required. It outputs a single edited image that faithfully reconstructs the source in unedited regions while applying the prompt-specified modification. The pipeline runs on pretrained DiT-based backbones (PixArt-Σ, SD3, SD3.5) and completes in approximately 1.8 seconds per image on an RTX 5090, requiring only 38 NFEs.

## Method

MIEdit reformulates DDPM inversion as a multi-history-step SDE inversion using a predictor-corrector scheme (third-order predictor, fourth-order corrector) derived from SA-Solver, storing per-step noise terms during inversion and replaying them during generation for exact reconstruction in as few as 19 steps. To fix the plasticity collapse under large edits, the method replaces the standard linear β schedule with a power-interpolated schedule (p=1.9) at inference time, which lowers the latent noise level at each timestep, reducing the variance of the stored noise term and preventing trajectory divergence. For regional control, IASM leverages classifier-free guidance during inversion by setting the inversion negative prompt to the target prompt, then computes cosine dissimilarity between source- and target-conditioned multi-layer feature maps to produce semantic angle masks that guide the entire generation trajectory—eliminating the need for external masks, attention-based region detection, or separate inference branches.

## Results

On PIE-Bench, MIEdit (SD3) achieves the best background preservation across all metrics: PSNR 29.44, LPIPS 40.03, MSE 23.49, and SSIM 91.17, while also attaining the highest CLIP similarity of 22.73 (SD3.5) among all methods. On the new EditEval++ benchmark, MIEdit (SD3) reaches the top EditScore of 4.30 and CLIP similarity of 23.30, outperforming FlowEdit (3.84, 22.62) and FlowAlign (4.02, 22.91). MIEdit requires only 38 NFEs, making it the most efficient method tested (vs. 60–2816 for baselines), and runs in 1.8 s/image on an RTX 5090, faster than FlowEdit (2.6 s) and FlowAlign (2.4 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIEdit dominates on background preservation: its SD3 variant achieves PSNR 29.44, LPIPS 40.03, MSE 23.49, and SSIM 91.17, surpassing the closest competitor FlowEdit (26.56 / 55.30 / 34.23 / 89.45) by a wide margin, and outperforming all ODE-inversion methods (DDIM+P2P, DI+MasaCtrl, iRFDS, FTEdit) by 3–12 dB in PSNR. On editing fidelity (CLIP), MIEdit (SD3.5) scores 22.73, edging out FlowEdit (22.50) and DI+PnP (22.62), while older methods like LEDITS++ (20.92) and CycleDiffusion (21.68) trail by 1–2 points. On the EditEval++ benchmark, MIEdit (SD3) achieves the highest EditScore (4.30 vs. FlowAlign 4.02, FlowEdit 3.84, CycleDiffusion 3.66) and best CLIP (23.30), confirming gains on more diverse and challenging tasks. In terms of efficiency, MIEdit requires only 38 NFEs and 1.8 s/image, compared to FlowEdit (66 NFE, 2.6 s), FlowAlign (66 NFE, 2.4 s), and FlowCycle (6633 NFE, 270 s), making it the fastest method by a large margin. The only metric where MIEdit does not lead is structure distance on PIE-Bench, where CycleDiffusion (11.15) and MIEdit-PixArt-Σ (11.10) are nearly tied, though MIEdit-SD3 (12.07) is slightly behind FlowEdit (12.29) by a negligible 0.22 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIEdit extends the DDPM inversion line (Huberman-Spiegelglas et al., CVPR 2024) by reformulating it within a unified SDE inversion framework and upgrading from low-order to multi-history-step (higher-order) predictor-corrector sampling inherited from SA-Solver (Xue et al., NeurIPS 2023). It departs from prior SDE-inversion and inversion-free editing methods in two key ways: (1) a power-interpolated β schedule applied at inference time to suppress latent-scale variance explosion that plagues multi-history-step inversion under large edits, and (2) IASM, which shifts automatic mask generation from the sampling phase to the inversion phase by exploiting CFG feature-map cosine dissimilarity, eliminating the need for external masks or blended-word prompts. The method is evaluated on PixArt-Σ and Stable Diffusion 3/3.5, positioning it alongside recent flow-based editing approaches (FlowEdit, FlowAlign, FlowCycle) while retaining the exact-reconstruction guarantee of SDE inversion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in combining near-exact reconstruction, strong background preservation, and competitive edit fidelity at 38–48 NFE—substantially fewer than ODE-inversion baselines (150–2816 NFE)—making training-free editing more accessible for real-time or resource-constrained settings. IASM removes a persistent practical bottleneck (manual or attention-derived masks) and the EditEval++ benchmark (30 tasks, 1,160 triplets) fills a gap left by EditEval's 7-task, 150-sample scope. Open questions include whether the power-interpolated schedule and IASM transfer cleanly to video or 3D diffusion, whether the approach degrades under very short or ambiguous prompts, and whether the multi-history-step variance analysis generalizes beyond the linear SDE formulation used here.
