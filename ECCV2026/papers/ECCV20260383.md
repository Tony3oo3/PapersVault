## Problem

Indoor scene generation pipelines produce structured building shells with walls and openings but stop there, leaving facades, roofs, and stylistic details unspecified. Unconstrained 3D generative models cannot reliably complete these exteriors because they violate the fixed footprint and opening semantics required for interior-exterior alignment, and large-scale paired datasets linking structured layouts to textured exteriors do not exist.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260383/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a format-agnostic building scaffold (parsed from indoor scene graphs, CityGML, or IFC/CAD) that encodes wall segments with normals, floor/ceiling polygons, and door/window opening placements in both world and wall-local coordinates, together with a free-form text style prompt. It produces a single assembled exterior mesh with 4k PBR material maps (diffuse, normal, roughness), parametric roof geometry, and style-consistent door/window assets inserted into the predefined openings, with the scaffold's footprint topology and wall boundaries preserved exactly by construction.

## Method

ShellMaker decomposes exterior completion into three coordinated stages that enforce structural constraints by construction rather than by conditioning a generative model. The structure stage parses the scaffold, decomposes the footprint via straight-skeleton or greedy rectangle partitioning, and generates parametric roofs from five canonical types controlled by pitch, overhang, and ridge parameters. The style stage uses GPT-4.1 to refine the free-form prompt into part-aware text-to-3D prompts for each architectural component, synthesizes textured meshes via a two-stage pipeline (Nano Banana reference image → Trellis-2 PBR mesh), and retrieves compatible wall-roof texture pairs using a precomputed compatibility matrix combining material co-occurrence, color harmony, frequency alignment, and CLIP similarity. The assembly stage performs geometry-aware opening carving via silhouette extraction and boolean subtraction, semantics-aligned asset placement, and scale-consistent UV parameterization with auto-rescaling based on each texture's dominant spatial frequency, ensuring the final mesh exactly matches the scaffold's structural layout.

## Results

ShellMaker achieves a footprint IoU of 0.992 and opening center L2 error of 0.091, indicating near-perfect preservation of scaffold geometry, while matching the best unconstrained baseline (Trellis-2) on CLIP-Sim (0.315 vs. 0.318) and substantially outperforming all baselines on the view-independent UNI3D metric (0.424 vs. 0.401, 0.258, 0.265). In the 2AFC LLM evaluation, ShellMaker wins over 70% of comparisons against all three baselines, with structural plausibility win rates of 84.1% (vs. Trellis-2), 92.8% (vs. SpaceControl), and 91.2% (vs. SAT-Skylines). Ablations confirm that joint texture retrieval and LLM prompt refinement each contribute meaningfully to style alignment, while structural metrics remain stable across all variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Trellis-2 (unconstrained image-to-3D reconstruction), ShellMaker trades a marginal 0.003 CLIP-Sim deficit for a dramatic structural advantage: footprint IoU jumps from 0.492 to 0.992 and opening center error drops from 0.914 to 0.091, confirming that unconstrained generative models cannot enforce hard geometric constraints. Against SpaceControl and SAT-Skylines (which inject geometric shape priors into the Trellis-1 latent space), ShellMaker wins on every metric: it improves CLIP-Sim by 0.041–0.044, UNI3D by 0.159–0.166, footprint IoU by 0.180–0.231, and reduces center L2 error by 0.37–0.43. The 2AFC LLM evaluation corroborates these quantitative gaps, with ShellMaker achieving 80.1% and 87.4% overall win rates over SpaceControl and SAT-Skylines respectively, and 71.2% over Trellis-2 (where Trellis-2's stronger appearance modeling narrows the style-fidelity margin to 48.3%). The paper notes that ShellMaker's slightly lower Opening IoU relative to its ablation variants is expected, as stylized arched or decorative openings naturally deviate from the scaffold's rectangular semantic boxes while remaining structurally correct.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShellMaker carves new ground by reframing building exterior synthesis as a scaffold-completion problem under hard geometric constraints, rather than as unconstrained or softly-conditioned 3D generation. It departs from controllable 3D generation methods (SpaceControl, SAT-Skylines) that inject geometric priors into latent spaces for approximate alignment, instead treating the scaffold footprint and opening placements as immutable and never modifying them. The pipeline repurposes existing tools—Trellis-2 for part-level mesh reconstruction, Nano Banana for reference image synthesis, GPT-4.1 for prompt refinement, and CLIP for texture retrieval—into a coordinated procedural-generative-retrieval architecture that no prior building generation system has combined. It also extends the procedural building modeling tradition (Müller et al., Nishida et al.) by replacing hand-crafted grammars with language-driven style control while retaining parametric roof generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ShellMaker addresses a practical gap in the indoor scene generation pipeline: current systems produce structurally valid interiors but leave exteriors unspecified, making outputs unusable for digital twins, urban simulation, or architectural visualization. The format-agnostic scaffold abstraction (supporting indoor generators, CityGML, and CAD/IFC) makes the system broadly applicable, and the quantitative structural consistency metrics (footprint IoU, opening alignment) provide a reusable evaluation framework for constraint-satisfaction in 3D generation. Key limitations include single-floor facade treatment (no per-story articulation), retrieval-based textures that cap stylistic diversity, and occasional degenerate part geometry. Open questions include whether generative texture synthesis can replace retrieval for unconventional styles, and whether learned layout models could be integrated to jointly generate structure and exterior rather than requiring a pre-existing scaffold.
