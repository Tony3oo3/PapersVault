## Problem

Existing training-free controllable motion generation methods rely on differentiable constraint objectives, yet many real-world constraints (safety thresholds, contact events, simulation validity checks) provide only discontinuous, sparse, or black-box feedback. Moreover, when multiple heterogeneous constraints with differing signal strengths and spatial-temporal scopes are imposed simultaneously, naive signal aggregation causes constraint imbalance and scope interference that degrade both constraint satisfaction and motion coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260595/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a set of K heterogeneous motion constraints (e.g., keyframe head-height targets, barrier avoidance, limited-area walking, wall touching, object moving, and MuJoCo physics-simulation validity checks), and a pre-trained MDM backbone on HumanML3D. It outputs a full-body 3D motion sequence of N frames × J joints × D features that satisfies all constraints while preserving temporal coherence and minimizing foot skating. Constraints can be specified per-frame, per-joint, or over the full sequence, and may be evaluated via differentiable losses or arbitrary query-based evaluators.

## Method

MIC casts the reverse diffusion sampling process as a stochastic optimal control problem, where the step-wise guidance term is the control input. The optimal control law (derived via HJB equation and Feynman-Kac formula) admits two equivalent instantiations: for criterion-based constraints, an importance-sampling estimator with cross-entropy proposal adaptation that requires only forward constraint evaluations (no gradients); and for continuous objective-based constraints, a gradient-based form computed on the Tweedie denoised estimate. Both enter the diffusion dynamics through the same control interface. A bilevel coordination mechanism then reconciles multiple constraints: an integral feedback regulator adaptively accumulates constraint weights proportional to ongoing violations, and a weighted least-squares control allocator distributes the step-wise control budget across joints and frames according to each constraint's effective scope, yielding a closed-form solution that minimizes inter-constraint interference while preserving the motion prior.

## Results

On the open-set benchmark (Table 1), MIC achieves the best constraint satisfaction across all four tasks: C.Err of 0.009 (HSI-2), 0.004 (HSI-3), 0.008 (GEO-1), and 0.004 (HOI-1), with unsuccess rates of 9.4%, 25.0%, 9.4%, and 3.1% respectively. The simulation pass rate reaches 87.5% (HSI-2), 71.9% (HSI-3), 78.1% (GEO-1), and 93.8% (HOI-1), substantially exceeding all baselines. On the known-constraint HSI-1 task (Table 2), MIC achieves the lowest C.Err (0.009), lowest unsuccess rate (6.8%), highest pass rate (85.7%), and best FID (0.494) while maintaining competitive motion quality (Skating 0.074, Max Acc. 0.093).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIC outperforms all five baselines on every task and every reported metric. Against the unconstrained MDM [61], the improvement is dramatic: on HSI-2, the unsuccess rate drops from 100% to 9.4% and the simulation pass rate rises from 0% to 87.5%. Compared to ProgMoGen [41] (the strongest gradient-based baseline), MIC reduces C.Err by roughly 10× (e.g., 0.009 vs. 0.097 on HSI-2) and nearly doubles the pass rate (87.5% vs. 56.3%), while also achieving lower skating (0.172 vs. 0.180). DNO [31] is outperformed on all constraint metrics; for instance, on HOI-1 MIC achieves an unsuccess rate of 3.1% versus DNO's 21.9% and a pass rate of 93.8% versus 71.9%. ReAlign [67] is beaten on C.Err and pass rate across all four tasks (e.g., HSI-2: 0.009 vs. 0.067 C.Err; 87.5% vs. 46.9% Pass). PriorMDM [52], evaluated only on GEO-1 and HOI-1, fails to satisfy any constraint (100% unsuccess rate, 0% pass rate), whereas MIC achieves 9.4% and 3.1% unsuccess rates respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic optimal control formulation for diffusion models introduced by Huang et al. (ICML 2024) for non-differentiable rule-guided music generation to the domain of human motion synthesis, adapting the importance-sampling-based control law to handle criterion-based motion constraints without gradients. It departs from prior training-free controllable motion generation methods (ProgMoGen, DNO, ReAlign, PriorMDM), which all rely on differentiable surrogate objectives, by unifying both continuous objective-based and criterion-based constraints under a single control interface. The bilevel coordination mechanism (integral feedback regulator + weighted least-squares control allocator) is a novel practical addition not present in prior single-constraint or gradient-only guidance approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free framework to jointly enforce heterogeneous (differentiable and non-differentiable) motion constraints within a single inference-time mechanism, which is practically important for real-world applications where constraints like safety thresholds, contact events, and simulation-based validity checks are inherently non-smooth. The control-theoretic unification provides a principled foundation that could transfer to other diffusion-based generation tasks with mixed constraint types. Limitations include evaluation on a single backbone (MDM) and dataset (HumanML3D), the added computational cost of importance sampling with cross-entropy updates at each denoising step, and the open question of whether the coordination mechanism scales to many more constraints or longer sequences without degradation.
