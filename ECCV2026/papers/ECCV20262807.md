## Problem

Existing concept erasure techniques (CETs) for text-to-image diffusion models face a fundamental trade-off: stronger edits improve erasure robustness but over-suppress semantically adjacent benign concepts, while weaker edits preserve utility but leave the model vulnerable to re-emergence attacks. A key source of this trade-off is that most CETs define erase/retain concept banks using static, proxy-based retrieval (CLIP embeddings, LLM-curated lists) that do not model how prompts actually steer the denoising trajectory, and no unified metric exists to quantify the erasure-utility balance.

## Input / Output

PARSE takes a pretrained latent diffusion model (SD 1.4), a target concept specified as a text prompt (e.g., 'A photo of a garbage truck'), and a small set of target exemplar images for trigger discovery. It outputs an edited diffusion model whose cross-attention value matrices are modified by a closed-form linear projector P applied at every UNet layer (V ← PV), with no gradient-based weight optimization. A one-time offline vocabulary index of CFG steering embeddings (~4.9 h on 2×4 H100 GPUs, ~2.6 GB) is precomputed; given a new target, the full erasure pipeline including up to 5 adaptive expansion iterations completes in ~10.7 min on a single H100 (~4 GB).

## Method

PARSE is a training-free, three-stage framework. First, Knowledge Search performs two-stage retrieval over the frozen text-encoder vocabulary: Stage 1 ranks all tokens by cosine similarity of their classifier-free-guidance steering embeddings (ε_c − ε_u, spatially RMS-pooled and concatenated across UNet layers) to the target's steering embedding; Stage 2 filters false positives via CLIP classification on cached generated images, yielding a diffusion-grounded erase bank and its complement as the retain bank. Second, Preservation-aware Subspace Projection constructs a multi-directional projector P = I − EEᵀ where the erase basis E is first orthogonalized against the retain span (E ← orth((I − RRᵀ)E)), ensuring span(E) ⊥ span(R) so that retain directions pass through unchanged—unlike ADaVD's rank-1 erase-only projection. Third, Adaptive Subspace Expansion iteratively discovers re-emergence triggers via textual inversion on the edited model, computes each trigger's residual after projecting out both retain and existing erase spans, and appends only 'strong' triggers (‖r̃‖ > η) to the erase basis, re-orthogonalizing against retain at each step. The entire pipeline is zero-shot and closed-form, requiring no fine-tuning of model weights.

## Results

PARSE achieves state-of-the-art erasure effectiveness with target ASR of 2.09% (NSFW), 0% (Van Gogh style), and 0% (garbage truck object), while maintaining near-identical utility to the unedited SD 1.4 model (FID 14.24–14.42 vs. 14.23; CLIP 31.29–31.35 vs. 31.36). Under adversarial attacks, PARSE attains the lowest CCE ASR across all three categories (3.41%, 6.02%, 8.58%) and the lowest RAB ASR (2.70%) for NSFW, substantially outperforming the next-best method STEREO (5.43%, 16.89%, 9.76% CCE; 3.11% RAB). The method completes erasure in 10.7 minutes on a single H100 GPU, with a one-time vocabulary indexing cost of ~4.9 h on 2×4 H100s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PARSE outperforms all 12 baselines on CCE attack ASR across every concept category, with the largest margins over fine-tuning methods (ESD: 86.14→3.41 on NSFW; UCE: 71.23→3.41) and the direct value-space predecessor AdaVD (86.28→3.41 on NSFW, 74.80→6.02 on Style, 95.46→8.58 on Object). The closest competitor STEREO is beaten on both robustness and utility: PARSE achieves lower CCE ASR (3.41 vs. 5.43 NSFW, 6.02 vs. 16.89 Style, 8.58 vs. 9.76 Object) and substantially better FID (14.24 vs. 16.71, 14.30 vs. 16.25, 14.42 vs. 16.42) and CLIP (31.30 vs. 29.87, 31.35 vs. 30.65, 31.29 vs. 30.61), indicating STEREO over-suppresses adjacent concepts. AdvUnlearn is competitive on UD attack (5.42 vs. 5.41 NSFW) but is far weaker on CCE (67.68 vs. 3.41) and degrades utility more (FID 15.82 vs. 14.24, CLIP 28.23 vs. 31.30). Adversarially trained methods RACE and RECE show strong RAB resistance (11.42 and 8.66 vs. PARSE's 2.70) but sacrifice utility (RACE FID 20.38, CLIP 28.62) or remain vulnerable to CCE/UD. On utility, PARSE matches or exceeds the unedited SD 1.4 baseline (FID 14.24 vs. 14.23; CLIP 31.30 vs. 31.36), whereas most baselines show measurable degradation, confirming that preservation-aware projection avoids the over-suppression problem that plagues prior CETs.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARSE builds directly on ADaVD's training-free value-space projection for concept erasure (Wang et al., CVPR 2025), extending its rank-1 orthogonal-complement projector into a multi-directional, retain-orthogonalized subspace. It departs from the broader CET literature (ESD, CA, UCE, RACE, RECE, AdvUnlearn, STEREO) by replacing static or proxy-embedding concept banks with diffusion-grounded retrieval: classifier-free guidance steering embeddings rank vocabulary tokens by how similarly they steer denoising, rather than relying on CLIP similarity or human/LLM curation. The adaptive expansion via textual-inversion triggers connects to adversarial-robustness CETs (STEREO, RACE) but enforces a preservation constraint that prior trigger-discovery methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The erasure-utility trade-off is the central tension in concept erasure, and PARSE's preservation-aware projection plus BEUS metric give the field a principled way to measure and optimize this balance rather than reporting ASR and FID in isolation. The finding that diffusion-grounded retrieval outperforms proxy-space (CLIP) identification for defining erase/retain banks is a useful design insight for future CETs. Open questions include whether the linear value-space projection assumption holds for highly compositional or multi-word concepts, whether the approach scales to larger diffusion backbones (e.g., SDXL, Flux), and whether the vocabulary-granularity retrieval can be extended to sub-token or compositional representations.
