## Problem

Existing dance motion generation models are either music-driven black boxes with no semantic control or text-conditioned systems that ignore musical rhythm. When both text and music are naively fused via standard cross-attention, dense high-frequency acoustic signals overwhelm sparse semantic text prompts, causing 'modality collapse' where the network reverts to music-only generation and ignores the user's choreographic intent. No prior work provides a principled mechanism to let text dictate kinematic structure while music only modulates temporal beat alignment, nor a rigorous protocol to evaluate zero-shot editability under conflicting text-music conditions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a two-level text prompt (a high-level dance concept/technique label plus a low-level detailed body-movement description, both encoded via a pretrained CLIP text encoder) and a music clip (features extracted at 30 Hz via a pretrained Jukebox model) as conditioning signals. The output is a 5-second 3D dance motion sequence at 30 FPS in SMPL parameterization (24×6 joint rotations in 6D representation, 3D global root translation, and 10-D body shape), canonicalized to face forward with the floor on the positive xy-plane. For long-term generation, 2.5-second segments are stitched with overlapping dynamics CFG and latent blending.

## Method

STREAM is a DDPM-based diffusion transformer (UNet-like backbone with 9 BEAM layers, D=512) that strictly decouples text and music conditioning pathways to prevent modality collapse. Text semantics are injected globally via Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN), physically transforming the motion query onto the correct kinematic manifold, while music is routed exclusively through a Bimodal Energy-Based Attention Module (BEAM) whose alignment drift term depends on music only through its self-similarity matrix—conveying temporal structure (when to repeat or vary) without semantic content. The BEAM's Dual Energy-Based Cross-Attention minimizes a composite energy function combining a text-alignment lse term and a music-alignment drift term, and a Bayesian Update Module iteratively refines the text embedding via MAP estimation as the motion materializes, while the music condition remains strictly frozen to preserve true acoustic anchors. At inference, a Hierarchical Classifier-Free Guidance scheme applies text guidance (λ_t) before music guidance (λ_m) with λ_t > λ_m, guaranteeing the semantic manifold is established before rhythmic modulation is layered on top.

## Results

On Motorica++ (text+music conditioning), STREAM achieves the best motion quality with FID_k=7.32 and FID_g=6.87, substantially outperforming the next-best POPDG (FID_k=27.02, FID_g=8.56) and all text-only baselines (e.g., MDM FID_k=37.29). STREAM also attains the highest Editable Dance Score (EDS=0.6539) with perfect semantic preservation (S_text=1.0) and strong rhythmic adaptation (S_music=0.4870), demonstrating that it avoids the modality collapse seen in prior multimodal approaches. On AIST++ (music-only), STREAM is competitive, achieving the best diversity (Dist_k=8.74, Dist_g=7.63) while remaining within ~1.5 FID_k of the best FID (Bailando, 28.16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Motorica++, STREAM (A+T) decisively outperforms all baselines on motion quality: FID_k=7.32 vs. POPDG's 27.02 and TM2D*'s 126.46, and FID_g=6.87 vs. UniMuMo's 169.14, indicating far more realistic and diverse generated motions. On the proposed EDS metric, STREAM achieves 0.6539, narrowly beating POPDG (0.6501) and MDM (0.6467), while substantially surpassing the multimodal baselines TM2D* (0.5952) and UniMuMo (0.5162), which suffer from modality collapse. Notably, STREAM is the only method to achieve S_text=1.0 (perfect semantic preservation) while maintaining competitive S_music=0.4870, whereas text-only baselines like MDM (S_text=0.9684) and MLD-5 (S_text=1.0 but S_music=0.3727) sacrifice rhythmic alignment. On AIST++ (music-only), STREAM trades a small FID_k penalty (+1.4 over Bailando) for the best diversity scores (Dist_k=8.74, Dist_g=7.63), suggesting it generates more varied motions at the cost of slightly less fidelity. The ablation in Table 3 confirms that removing the music alignment energy or AdaLN degrades BAS by 0.01–0.02, validating the architectural decoupling design.

## Relation to Prior Work

STREAM extends the energy-based attention lineage established by Park et al. (NeurIPS 2023) and the Hopfield-network interpretation of self-attention (Ramsauer et al., 2021) to the multimodal dance generation setting, where prior methods (TM2D, UniMuMo, LDA) fuse text and music through standard cross-attention and suffer from modality collapse. The key architectural departure is a strict pathway decoupling: global text semantics are injected via AdaLN to define the kinematic manifold, while music enters only through a self-similarity-based alignment energy term that carries temporal structure but no semantic content. This design, combined with Hierarchical Classifier-Free Guidance (text guidance applied before music guidance), is a principled response to the observed failure mode where dense acoustic signals overwhelm sparse textual prompts in standard fused-attention architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in choreographic workflows: existing music-driven dance generators are black boxes that ignore user intent, while text-only motion models lack rhythmic grounding. The modality-decoupling principle (text dictates structure, music decorates timing) is a clean architectural recipe that could transfer to other multimodal generation tasks where one modality is semantically sparse and the other is temporally dense. However, the Motorica++ dataset is small (97 sequences, 4.61 hours), limiting generalizability claims, and the system is restricted to single-person dance. Open questions include whether the strict energy-based decoupling scales to multi-dancer choreography with inter-person interactions, and whether the EDS metric generalizes beyond the specific text-music mismatch protocol defined here.
