## Problem

Existing one-step diffusion super-resolution methods (OSEDiff, TSD-SR) rely on Variational Score Distillation where the discriminator is trained solely with a diffusion loss, producing weak and unstable gradients that fail to guide the generator toward fine-detail reconstruction. Additionally, these methods lack explicit semantic alignment between reconstructed and ground-truth images, leading to perceptual inconsistencies and hallucination artifacts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 128×128 low-quality RGB image (bicubically upsampled before inference) and a degradation-aware text prompt extracted via DAPE, and produces a 512×512 high-quality super-resolved image in a single forward pass. The generator operates entirely in the VAE latent space of SD2.1-base, with the LQ image encoded by a trainable VAE encoder, processed by a fine-tuned UNet, and decoded by a frozen VAE decoder. Inference takes approximately 0.19 seconds on a single NVIDIA 5880 GPU.

## Method

ASDSR redesigns Adversarial Score Distillation under a WGAN framework where the generator is optimized with a full adversarial loss (Eq. 8) that combines a frozen pre-trained score function ε_φ and a trainable LoRA regularizer ε_ψ, while the regularizer is updated with a complete WGAN discriminator loss that computes the penalty term on the ground-truth latent z_t rather than the generator's output ẑ_t, yielding more stable and structured supervision signals. The one-step generator (trainable VAE encoder + LoRA-fine-tuned UNet + frozen VAE decoder, all initialized from SD2.1-base with rank-4 LoRA) is trained with three losses: a reconstruction loss (MSE + LPIPS), a CLIP-based semantic consistency loss that minimizes cosine distance between CLIP image embeddings of the reconstruction and ground truth, and the ASD regularization loss. This GT-latent penalty formulation is the key distinction from prior ASD, which computed the penalty on the generator's noisy latent and suffered from unstable gradients during early training. The approach achieves one-step inference with comparable speed to SinSR and TSD-SR while delivering substantially better fidelity and perceptual quality across synthetic and real-world benchmarks.

## Results

On the DrealSR benchmark, ASDSR achieves the best full-reference scores among all methods: PSNR 28.77 dB, SSIM 0.7973, and LPIPS 0.2896, outperforming the closest multi-step baseline ResShift (28.69 / 0.7875 / 0.3525) and one-step baselines OSEDiff (27.92 / 0.7836 / 0.2968) and TSD-SR (26.20 / 0.7170 / 0.3116). On RealSR, ASDSR attains the best SSIM (0.7382) and MANIQA (0.6507), with PSNR 25.69 dB and LPIPS 0.2805, trailing only TSD-SR on LPIPS (0.2798). On DIV2K-Val, ASDSR achieves the best SSIM (0.6210) but is slightly behind ResShift on PSNR (24.12 vs. 24.71) and TSD-SR on LPIPS (0.2927 vs. 0.2736). Inference requires only 1 step at 0.19 s per 512×512 image, comparable to other one-step methods (SinSR 0.12 s, TSD-SR 0.12 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASDSR clearly outperforms the closest one-step baselines OSEDiff and TSD-SR on fidelity metrics: on DrealSR it gains +0.85 dB PSNR over OSEDiff (28.77 vs. 27.92) and +2.57 dB over TSD-SR (28.77 vs. 26.20), while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.2896 vs. 0.2968 and 0.3116). Against the multi-step ResShift (15 steps), ASDSR matches or exceeds PSNR on DrealSR (28.77 vs. 28.69) with 15× fewer inference steps, and achieves substantially better LPIPS (0.2896 vs. 0.3525). On no-reference perceptual metrics (CLIPIQA, MUSIQ), TSD-SR retains a lead on DrealSR (0.7309 vs. 0.6885 CLIPIQA; 66.13 vs. 64.83 MUSIQ) and RealSR (0.7199 vs. 0.6598 CLIPIQA), suggesting TSD-SR's target score distillation produces slightly more 'impressive' but less faithful outputs. Against multi-step SUPIR (50 steps) and SeeSR (50 steps), ASDSR achieves comparable or better fidelity at 1/25th to 1/50th the inference cost (0.19 s vs. 19.42 s and 6.36 s). On DIV2K-Val, the synthetic benchmark, ResShift still leads on PSNR (24.71 vs. 24.12) and TSD-SR on LPIPS (0.2736 vs. 0.2927), indicating the advantage of ASDSR's stabilized penalty is most pronounced under complex real-world degradations rather than ideal bicubic downsampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets Adversarial Score Distillation (ASD) [44] through a WGAN lens, departing from the VSD paradigm used by OSEDiff [46] and TSD-SR [6] where the discriminator is updated solely via diffusion loss. The key conceptual shift is moving the penalty term from the generator's noisy latent to the ground-truth latent, which the authors argue provides a more stable regularization signal during early training. Additionally, the method introduces CLIP-based semantic consistency loss as a lightweight perceptual regularizer, a component absent from prior one-step distillation approaches for Real-ISR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

One-step inference is essential for deploying diffusion-based super-resolution in real-world applications, and the training instability of VSD-based distillation has been a practical bottleneck limiting fine-detail reconstruction. The GT-latent penalty trick and WGAN formulation offer a concrete, well-ablated recipe for stabilizing one-step distillation that could transfer to other restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ×4 SR, whether the CLIP guidance introduces subtle hallucination risks at scale, and whether the LoRA-based fine-tuning of the VAE encoder is truly necessary or a workaround for limited training data.
