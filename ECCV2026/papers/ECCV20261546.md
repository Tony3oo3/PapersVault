## Problem

Visual navigation policies struggle in cluttered and dynamic environments because RGB observations lack explicit geometric cues, and existing data sources are limited: real-world collection is expensive and restricted to front-view-only configurations, while synthetic data suffers from sim-to-real gaps and rarely covers hazardous scenarios like narrow squeezing or dynamic obstacle avoidance. This paper addresses the gap by learning diverse, challenging navigation capabilities from privileged RL experts in simulation and transferring them to a visual-only VLA policy that generalizes to the real world.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261546/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sliding window of k=8 frames from four fisheye cameras (front, right, back, left) undistorted to perspective images, plus a point goal position encoded as a textual prompt. Visual tokens are organized hierarchically: 16 fine-grained tokens for the latest observation and 4 coarse-grained tokens per view per history frame, yielding 192 total visual tokens. The output is a continuous omnidirectional velocity command [v_x, v_y, v_yaw] (clipped to max 1.5 m/s, 1.0 m/s, π/4 rad/s) produced at approximately 7 Hz by a two-layer MLP action head on top of a 7B LLM.

## Method

MM-Nav trains three capability-specific RL experts (reaching, squeezing, avoiding) with privileged depth observations in tailored IsaacLab environments using PPO, then distills their knowledge into a multi-view VLA student built on SigLIP visual encoding and a Qwen2-7B LLM backbone. Training proceeds in two stages: initial behavior cloning on 500k successful expert trajectories plus 100k real-world VQA samples, followed by iterative DAgger-style online refinement where the deployed VLA collects teacher actions in simulation. A key innovation is capability-balanced data aggregation: the data ratio across the three capabilities is dynamically adjusted each iteration based on the weighted-travel-time performance gap between the VLA and each RL expert, preventing underdeveloped skills from being neglected. Co-training on VQA data exposes the shared VLM backbone to real-image statistics, closing the sim-to-real gap and enabling zero-shot transfer to unseen real-world scenes.

## Results

On the InternVLA-N1 System-1 point-goal navigation benchmark, MM-Nav (multi-view) achieves 86.3% SR / 81.1% SPL on Home and 89.9% SR / 85.5% SPL on Commercial, yielding an average SR of 88.1%—a +15.9 point improvement over the best prior method (SIDP at 72.2% avg SR). On the custom IsaacLab benchmark, MM-Nav achieves the highest SR across all four scenes (Reaching 80%, Squeezing 71%, Avoiding 68%, Mixed 47%) while maintaining the lowest collision rates (19–32%) compared to baselines that suffer 57–96% collision rates in challenging scenes. The model operates at ~7 Hz inference with 360° RGB-only observations, outperforming RGB-D and depth-based methods despite lacking explicit geometric input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM-Nav (multi-view, RGB-only) outperforms all seven baselines on the InternVLA-N1 S1 benchmark by large margins: +23.1 SR over SIDP (63.2→86.3 Home), +26.0 over NavDP (60.3→86.3), and +43.3 over iPlanner (43.0→86.3) on Home; similar gaps hold on Commercial. Notably, MM-Nav surpasses RGB-D and depth-based methods (SIDP, NavDP, InternVLA-N1) despite using only RGB, demonstrating that the VLA backbone's learned visual representations compensate for the absence of explicit depth. On the custom IsaacLab benchmark, the advantage is even more pronounced in challenging scenes: MM-Nav achieves 71% SR in Squeezing vs. 18% for NavDP and 4% for ViPlanner, and 68% in Avoiding vs. 27% for NavDP, reflecting the benefit of 360° surround-view perception and continuous velocity control over single-front-camera, discrete-action baselines. The single-view variant (79.9/86.7 SR) still beats all prior methods, confirming that the core VLA training paradigm—not just multi-view input—drives the gains, while the multi-view extension adds a further +4.8 SR on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

MM-Nav extends the video-based VLA navigation lineage (NaVid, Uni-NaVid) by replacing single-view input with a 360° four-camera surround-view and by replacing monolithic expert data with trajectories from three capability-specific RL experts (reaching, squeezing, avoiding). The iterative refinement loop is a direct extension of DAgger, augmented with a capability-balanced data aggregation scheme that dynamically reweights training data based on per-capability performance gaps. The co-training on real-world VQA data follows the strategy established by Uni-NaVid and TrackVLA for bridging the sim-to-real gap, but here it is combined with the multi-expert distillation paradigm, which prior VLA navigation works have not explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single VLA student trained on combined multi-capability expert data outperforms each of its specialized RL teachers suggests that capability integration yields emergent robustness beyond what any single expert provides—a useful principle for composing skills in embodied agents. The 7 Hz inference speed with full 360° perception from a 7B model makes the approach practically deployable on legged robots, and the >20% performance drop without VQA co-training provides strong evidence that real-world visual understanding is a critical component of sim-to-real transfer. Open questions include whether the multi-expert paradigm scales to more than three capabilities, whether cross-embodiment transfer is possible, and whether the approach generalizes beyond point-goal navigation to instruction-following or object-search tasks.
