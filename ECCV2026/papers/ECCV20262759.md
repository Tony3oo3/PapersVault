## Problem

Existing anisotropic meshing methods require a pre-computed mesh and metric field as input, while state-of-the-art surface reconstruction from point clouds produces isotropic or resolution-adaptive meshes that do not align triangle shapes with the curvature tensor's eigenstructure. Extending Nash-embedding-based high-dimensional anisotropic formulations to unstructured point clouds remains unexplored, as prior methods assume continuous manifolds or explicit mesh connectivity that point sets lack.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262759/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unstructured 3D point cloud (N points, normalized) with oriented normals estimated via WNNC as input, and produces a lightweight anisotropic triangular mesh whose elongated triangles align with local curvature principal directions, along with the intermediate 8D Euclidean point embedding and per-point curvature tensor estimates. For large-scale inputs (hundreds of thousands of points, e.g., ScanNet scenes), a patch-based meta-embedding inference strategy handles arbitrary point counts without retraining by processing overlapping FPS-based patches and aligning them via affine transformations.

## Method

A Point Transformer V3 backbone is trained in a supervised setting to predict a 5D coordinate extension (yielding an 8D embedding) from concatenated 3D positions and normals, using a neighborhood distance-based MAE loss (NDHDE) that preserves local Riemannian geometry without requiring explicit edge connectivity. Ground-truth training pairs are generated via barycentric-invariant weight transfer: points sampled on mesh triangles are mapped to their high-d counterparts using the same barycentric weights applied to pre-computed 8D vertex embeddings. At inference, PCA on 3D nearest neighbors estimates the 2D tangent subspace at each embedded point, and a particle-based optimization constrained to these tangent spaces produces uniformly sparse seeds in 8D. A Tangential Delaunay Complex is then constructed by clipping embedded disks with bisectors (using re-entrant clipping for d>3), extracting a manifold mesh, and projecting back to 3D via truncation to yield the final anisotropic approximation.

## Results

On 75 synthetic scanned models from Myles et al.'s dataset, HD-PEA achieves the best surface accuracy across all metrics: CD = 0.649×10⁻⁵, F1 = 0.989, NC = 0.979, HD = 0.798×10⁻², while also producing the highest anisotropic mesh quality G = 0.702 with only 7,028 vertices and 13,989 faces. On 80 models from Thingi10K, it achieves CD = 0.496×10⁻⁵, F1 = 0.996, NC = 0.987, HD = 0.678×10⁻² with 5,180 vertices. The method runs in ~14.6 seconds per model, compared to 31,538 s for LMR and 4,659 s for SIREN, while producing fewer mesh elements than all competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HD-PEA outperforms all nine baselines on every surface accuracy metric (CD, F1, NC, HD) and the anisotropic mesh quality G. The closest competitor is NDC (128³), which achieves CD = 0.749 vs. 0.649 for HD-PEA but uses 3× more vertices (21,011 vs. 7,028) and produces a lower G (0.588 vs. 0.702). The two-stage NDC+NASM pipeline, which applies anisotropic remeshing to an NDC-reconstructed mesh, achieves CD = 0.792 and G = 0.576, confirming that intermediate mesh artifacts degrade anisotropic quality. LMR, the most recent adaptive meshing method, achieves a competitive G = 0.629 but suffers from CD = 10.048 (15× worse) and requires 31,538 s per model versus 14.6 s for HD-PEA. Voxel-based methods (Poisson, POCO) and implicit-representation methods (SIREN, NKSR) are substantially worse on both accuracy and mesh quality, with POCO showing CD > 90 and SIREN producing over 696K vertices. HD-PEA uniquely combines the best accuracy, the best anisotropic quality, the fewest mesh elements, and a practical runtime in a single end-to-end pipeline from raw point clouds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the high-dimensional Euclidean embedding framework for anisotropic meshing (Zhong et al. 2018, NASM 2024) from mesh-based inputs to unstructured point clouds, replacing dot-product-based anisotropy losses (infeasible without explicit edge connectivity) with pairwise distance losses in a Point Transformer V3 backbone. It also generalizes the Tangential Delaunay Complex and Restricted Voronoi Cell reconstruction (Boltcheva & Lévy 2017) from 3D to 8D ambient space, and adapts particle-based anisotropic optimization (Zhong et al. 2013) to point-based manifolds via PCA-estimated tangent subspaces. The key conceptual departure is bridging the Nash Embedding Theorem to discrete point sets through barycentric-invariant isometric propagation, eliminating the need for an intermediate mesh that prior two-stage pipelines (e.g., NDC+NASM) require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first deep learning method to produce anisotropic surface meshes directly from raw point clouds without any pre-computed mesh or metric field, closing a gap between neural surface reconstruction (which yields isotropic meshes) and classical anisotropic remeshing (which requires mesh input). The PMEI inference strategy enables scalability to scene-level data (ScanNet) without retraining, and the curvature tensor estimation as a byproduct opens downstream applications in rendering and simulation. Open questions include: the training data generation still relies on ground-truth meshes (raising a circular-dependency concern for truly mesh-free settings), performance degrades on thin structures with incomplete sampling, and generalization beyond 2D manifolds or d=8 embeddings remains unexplored.
