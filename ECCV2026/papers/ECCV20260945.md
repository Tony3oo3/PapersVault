## Problem

SAM 3D Body (3DB) achieves state-of-the-art monocular full-body human mesh recovery but requires several seconds per image due to serial multi-stage dependencies (sequential multi-crop encoding, iterative MHR-to-SMPL fitting), precluding real-time deployment. Prior acceleration efforts redesign only isolated ViT components and require extensive retraining, failing to resolve the cross-stage compound latency of comprehensive pipelines like 3DB while risking degradation of its pre-trained generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260945/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces full-body 3D human mesh parameters in the MHR representation (18,439-vertex topology), optionally projected to the 76-dimensional SMPL kinematic manifold (3 global orientation + 63 body pose + 10 shape coefficients). Internally, a YOLO11-Pose detector and MoGe-2 FOV estimator derive body/hand bounding boxes and camera intrinsics, after which three 512×512 crops (body, left hand, right hand) are processed in a single batched backbone pass. The MHR-to-SMPL conversion projects vertices via precomputed barycentric coordinates before feeding a 4,500-dim flattened vertex vector into the feedforward MLP.

## Method

Fast SAM 3D Body is a training-free acceleration framework that holistically reformulates the 3DB inference pathway through three shifts. First, spatial dependency decoupling replaces the body-decoder-dependent hand localization with a lightweight 2D pose prior: wrist keypoints from YOLO11-Pose analytically yield hand bounding boxes, enabling all three crops to be encoded in one batched TensorRT/CUDA-Graph backbone forward pass instead of three sequential passes. Second, compute-aware decoder pruning gates intermediate kinematic predictions to a subset of layers (e.g., {0,1,2} of 5), removes the keypoint-prompted refinement pass, and compiles the entire graph statically for near-zero kernel-launch overhead. Third, a lightweight 3-layer MLP (~2.5M parameters, 4500→512→256→76) trained on iterative-fitting pairs replaces the hundreds-of-steps MHR-to-SMPL optimization with a single forward pass, achieving >10,000× speedup on this conversion while preserving millimeter-level joint accuracy. All 3DB weights remain frozen; only the small MLP and an AMASS-trained pose denoiser are learned.

## Results

Under the Oracle protocol (GT bounding boxes), Fast SAM 3D Body achieves PA-MPJPE of 30.4 mm on 3DPW and 37.3 mm on EMDB, matching or slightly exceeding the original 3DB (33.8 and 38.2 mm respectively) on the alignment-invariant metric, while incurring a modest +4–8 mm increase in MPJPE and PVE. Under the Automatic protocol (detected boxes), the method frequently matches or surpasses 3DB (e.g., EMDB MPJPE 65.3 vs. 67.9 mm; Harmony4D PVE 44.0 vs. 44.1 mm) and achieves a PCK@0.05 of 70.2 on LSPET, surpassing 3DB's 67.8. End-to-end throughput reaches up to 10.9× speedup in Automatic mode (0.6→6.5 FPS on Harmony4D) and 4.2–6.2× in Oracle per-crop mode. The neural MHR-to-SMPL conversion alone accelerates the iterative fitting by ~10,000× (0.23→2398 FPS on EMDB) with near-identical joint accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct predecessor 3DB [43], Fast SAM 3D Body trades a small amount of global alignment accuracy (MPJPE +4–8 mm, PVE +4–6 mm under Oracle) for up to 10.9× end-to-end speedup, while actually improving PA-MPJPE on 3DPW (30.4 vs. 33.8) and EMDB (37.3 vs. 38.2) and surpassing 3DB on LSPET PCK (70.2 vs. 67.8). Under the Automatic protocol the gap narrows further: the method matches 3DB on Harmony4D (PVE 44.0 vs. 44.1) and RICH (PA 33.8 vs. 33.9), and wins on EMDB MPJPE (65.3 vs. 67.9). Compared to prior image-based HMR methods, the advantage is substantial: NLF-L+fit* [34] achieves 33.6 PA on 3DPW vs. 30.4 for Ours; CameraHMR [30] reaches 35.1 PA vs. 30.4; and SMPLer-X-H [3] lags at 46.6 PA. Video-based methods (WHAM [35], TRAM [41], GenMo [21]) are competitive on 3DPW (34.6–35.9 PA) but do not report results on RICH, Harmony4D, COCO, or LSPET, and require multi-frame input. On hand reconstruction (FreiHAND), the lightweight hand decoder maintains within 0.3 mm of 3DB on PA-MPJPE/PA-MPVPE while achieving 4.6× faster inference (45.5 vs. 9.8 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM 3D Body (3DB) [43] and the MHR body model [8], preserving 3DB's pretrained weights while holistically reformulating its multi-stage inference pipeline. It departs from prior HMR acceleration work (token merging, efficient attention, layer pruning) that redesigns ViT architectures and requires retraining, instead applying system-level optimizations—spatial dependency decoupling, static graph compilation via TensorRT/CUDA Graphs, and decoder pruning—that keep the backbone frozen. The neural kinematic projection (a 2.5M-parameter MLP replacing iterative MHR-to-SMPL fitting) is a novel contribution to cross-topology mesh conversion, complementing but not subsuming the architectural efficiency literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving ~10× end-to-end speedup and a 10,000× conversion speedup while preserving reconstruction fidelity, this work makes state-of-the-art full-body HMR viable for latency-critical applications like real-time humanoid teleoperation and on-device deployment, demonstrated concretely on a Unitree G1 at 65 ms/frame. The training-free design preserves 3DB's in-the-wild generalization, avoiding the accuracy risks of retraining. Limitations include degradation under close multi-person contact, deep self-occlusion, and extreme limb extension; open questions include whether the feedforward MHR-to-SMPL mapping generalizes to unseen body shapes or pathological poses outside the training distribution, and whether the approach transfers to other multi-stage 3D pipelines beyond 3DB.
