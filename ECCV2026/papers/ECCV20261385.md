## Problem

Existing fine-grained and long-tailed recognition methods are typically evaluated in isolation under closed-set assumptions, yet real-world plant taxonomy deployment involves co-occurring challenges—spatiotemporal domain shift, hierarchical taxonomic structure, and previously unseen taxa—that jointly degrade performance. Standard margin-based classifiers like AM-Softmax suffer from prototype misalignment under long-tailed distributions, where dominant head-class gradients persistently repel tail-class prototypes away from their class-conditional means, degrading rare-taxa discrimination.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB plant images resized to 614×512×3 and produces a d-dimensional L2-normalized embedding vector via a ResNet-101 backbone, which is scored against class prototypes using cosine similarity to yield a taxonomic label at family, genus, or species rank. The same embedding geometry supports open-set rejection: low-norm embeddings (weak prototype alignment) signal potential novel taxa for expert review, with a threshold calibrated at 95% TPR.

## Method

TaxoNet introduces a dual-margin penalization loss that applies asymmetric margins at the logit level: a larger target margin m_y = m_0 + Δ_y strengthens the attractive gradient for tail-class embeddings, while a larger non-target margin m_j = Δ_j suppresses the repulsive gradient that head-class samples would otherwise impose on tail-class prototypes. The class-relative margin Δ_c is computed from the log-normalized class prior via a power function with a single learnable exponent γ, regularized to track the prior. A norm-guided sample selection strategy augments each batch with tail-class examples (via AugMix) and retains the lowest-norm embeddings, adaptively prioritizing hard and morphologically diverse instances without naive oversampling. The paper provides a theoretical proof that the dual-margin loss bounds the head-class gradient influence on any tail-class prototype by exp(m_yi − m_c), formally establishing the mechanism by which prototype misalignment is mitigated.

## Results

On the full TaxoNet configuration (dual-margin + norm-guided selection + regularization), macro-averaged recall reaches 72.90% on AA-Central, 67.67% on AA-West, 64.96% on AA-East, 83.21% on iNat-Plantae, and 90.40% on NAFlora-Mini, outperforming the strongest SOTA baseline (LDAM) by 5.1, 4.9, 2.8, 1.6, and 0.4 percentage points respectively. Rank-1 accuracy is competitive (91.92% on AA-Central vs. 92.31% for LDAM), with the small R@1 trade-off reflecting a deliberate rebalancing toward tail-class performance. In open-set evaluation at 95% TPR, TaxoNet achieves 91.28% TNR on AA-Central and 89.84% on AA-West, surpassing LDAM (88.59% and 87.09%). Against multimodal foundation models on iNat-Plantae species-level classification, TaxoNet attains 83.21% macro-recall versus 35.28% (GPT-4o CoT) and 41.46% (Gemini-2.5-FLASH CoT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TaxoNet consistently outperforms all long-tailed learning baselines (CBL, LDAM, LA) on macro-averaged recall across every dataset, with the largest gains on AA-Central (+5.1 pp over LDAM) and AA-West (+4.9 pp), while sacrificing at most 0.4 pp in R@1 on AA-Central. Compared to the margin-based AM-Softmax backbone, TaxoNet's dual-margin mechanism adds 8.1 pp recall on AA-Central (72.90 vs. 64.77), confirming that the class-relative margins are the primary driver of tail-class improvement. Against multimodal LLMs, TaxoNet achieves roughly 2× the species-level macro-recall of GPT-4o (83.21% vs. 35.28%) and Gemini-2.5-FLASH (83.21% vs. 41.46%), with the gap widening dramatically for tail taxa (76.67% vs. 16.67% and 26.67%). BioCLIP, the domain-specific vision-language foundation model, reaches 70.09% species-level recall but still trails TaxoNet by 13.1 pp, and is far behind at the genus level (53.42% vs. 95.96%). In open-set recognition, TaxoNet's base margin yields 91.28% TNR on AA-Central at 95% TPR, exceeding LDAM (88.59%) and CBL (86.34%), indicating better separation of known from unknown taxa in the embedding space.

## Relation to Prior Work

TaxoNet extends AM-Softmax (CosFace) from face recognition into the ecological domain by introducing a dual-margin penalization mechanism that applies class-dependent margins to both target and non-target logits, going beyond the single additive margin of AM-Softmax and the logit-level adjustments of LDAM, CBL, and Logit Adjustment. The norm-guided sample selection strategy is repurposed from MagFace's embedding-norm-based quality assessment, adapted here to prioritize tail-class and high-variability plant specimens. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior ecological taxonomy work that treats fine-grained discrimination, long-tailed imbalance, domain shift, and open-set recognition as separate problems, instead formulating a unified embedding objective that addresses their co-occurrence under a single training framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded (Propositions 1–2) explanation for why head-class dominance causes tail-class prototype misalignment and how the dual-margin loss bounds the repulsive gradient, offering a principled alternative to heuristic reweighting. Its practical impact is strongest for plant biodiversity monitoring platforms (iNaturalist, Pl@ntNet, Flora Incognita) where rare and endemic taxa are most conservation-critical. Limitations include the plant-only scope, reliance on a single ResNet-101 backbone, and the norm-guided selection's incompatibility with non-margin-based losses; open questions include whether the theoretical bounds tighten with deeper architectures, whether the approach transfers to other taxa groups (insects, birds), and how to integrate multi-view or multimodal reasoning for the visually ambiguous failure cases the authors identify.
