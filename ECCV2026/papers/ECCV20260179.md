## Problem

Large-scale diffusion models synthesize display-referred sRGB images with unknown, heterogeneous photo-finishing effects, but cannot directly produce physically meaningful linear raw data. Existing inverse-ISP methods assume a fixed, known rendering pipeline and fail when applied to diffusion-generated images that entangle diverse and uncontrolled ISP transformations. This domain gap prevents generative models from serving as a scalable source of scene-referred linear data for low-level vision tasks.

## Input / Output

RawGen accepts either an sRGB image (encoded by the frozen VAE encoder into a conditioning latent) or a text prompt (routed through the pretrained FLUX.1-Kontext text-to-latent pathway to obtain an intermediate sRGB latent). The output is a scene-referred CIE XYZ linear image, which is then deterministically mapped to a target camera's linear raw-RGB space using that camera's DNG calibration matrices and a chosen correlated color temperature; optional heteroscedastic noise injection and CFA remosaicing can produce single-channel mosaiced raw images.

## Method

RawGen fine-tunes a pretrained rectified-flow DiT (FLUX.1-Kontext) via LoRA adapters on attention projections, using a many-to-one training objective where N diverse sRGB variants of the same scene (generated by randomizing white balance, tone mapping, and contrast parameters) are all mapped to a single CIE XYZ anchor latent. The denoiser is trained with a v-prediction loss to predict the rectified-flow velocity from a noisy XYZ latent conditioned on a randomly sampled sRGB variant latent, learning to suppress photo-finishing variability and recover a shared linear representation. A second stage fine-tunes the VAE decoder with an L1 reconstruction loss to decode XYZ-domain latents into linear XYZ images. At inference, the model solves the reverse ODE to obtain an XYZ latent, decodes it, and applies a deterministic, non-trainable color-space transform using target-camera DNG metadata to produce device-specific raw output without retraining.

## Results

On the MIT-Adobe FiveK sRGB-to-XYZ reconstruction benchmark, RawGen achieves a mean PSNR of 23.66 dB and SSIM of 0.8486 across five expert photo-finishing styles, outperforming the strongest baseline CIE XYZ Net (21.69 dB / 0.8070) by ~2 dB and InvISP (8.92 dB / 0.2713) by ~15 dB. In the latent-compactness evaluation (100 color-graded variants per prompt), RawGen reduces mean L2 distance to centroid by 44% under PCA (160.0 vs. 286.8 for raw sRGB) and 61% under t-SNE (10.69 vs. 27.65), confirming superior suppression of photo-finishing variability. For downstream tasks trained on 3K synthetic raw samples, RawGen-generated data yields illuminant estimation angular error of 3.14° mean (vs. 4.21° for Graphics2RAW and 3.02° for real data) and denoising PSNR of 50.63 dB at ISO 1600, approaching or exceeding real-data-trained baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RawGen consistently outperforms all six baselines across every evaluation. Against CIE XYZ Net [2], RawGen gains ~3.6 dB PSNR and ~0.04 SSIM on the five-expert reconstruction task, and even when CIE XYZ Net is retrained under the same many-to-one protocol (PSNR 21.69), RawGen still leads by ~2 dB. InvISP [62] is the weakest baseline, trailing RawGen by over 7 dB in one-to-one settings and collapsing to 8.92 dB PSNR under many-to-one retraining, indicating severe sensitivity to unknown photo-finishing. Raw-Diffusion [48], the closest diffusion-based competitor, is outperformed by ~3–5 dB PSNR and shows the worst latent compactness (PCA distance 318.2 vs. RawGen's 160.0), suggesting it fails to suppress color-variability as effectively. For downstream task augmentation, RawGen-generated raw data surpasses Graphics2RAW [54] on all three tasks (illuminant estimation 3.14° vs. 4.21° mean angular error; denoising 50.63 vs. 49.37 dB at ISO 1600) and approaches real-data performance (3.02° / 49.80 dB), while EnlightenGAN [30] and UPI [9] trail by 1–4° in illuminant estimation and 1–2 dB in denoising PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

RawGen repurposes a pretrained rectified-flow DiT (FLUX.1-Kontext) with native image conditioning, fine-tuning it via LoRA to denoise CIE XYZ latents conditioned on sRGB inputs, and additionally fine-tuning the VAE decoder for the linear domain. It departs fundamentally from prior inverse-ISP methods (CIE XYZ Net, InvISP, Raw-Diffusion) that assume a fixed one-to-one ISP mapping, instead adopting a many-to-one training paradigm where multiple photo-finished sRGB variants of the same scene are anchored to a single linear reference. This formulation is motivated by the observation that diffusion models are trained on heterogeneous, unknown ISP pipelines, creating a domain gap that fixed-pipeline inverse-ISP methods cannot bridge. The text-to-raw generation pathway is new, leveraging the base model's text-to-latent route as an intermediate sRGB conditioning signal before the conditional denoiser produces the linear output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RawGen addresses a persistent bottleneck in low-level vision: the scarcity of large-scale, camera-specific raw datasets. By enabling scalable text-driven raw synthesis that matches or exceeds real-data performance on downstream tasks (illuminant estimation, neural ISP, denoising), it offers a practical path to augmenting training without physical capture. Key limitations remain: device-specific fidelity depends on DNG metadata and simplified noise models, and PSF/lens-shading effects are not modeled. Open questions include whether the camera-specific mapping itself can be learned end-to-end, whether the approach generalizes to cameras with very different spectral sensitivities or dynamic ranges, and whether the many-to-one invariance holds under more extreme or spatially varying photo-finishing operations.
