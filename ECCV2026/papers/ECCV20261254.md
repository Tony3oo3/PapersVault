## Problem

Distilled autoregressive video diffusion models enable real-time synthesis but suffer from severe cascading error accumulation over long sequences, causing temporal drift and visual degradation. Existing Test-Time Optimization (TTO) methods fail to mitigate this drift in extended generation because of unstable reward landscapes for long-range consistency and the extreme optimization sensitivity of distilled parameters, where even infinitesimal gradients trigger reward collapse. The paper asks whether long-horizon stability can be achieved purely through inference-time intervention without any retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a 30-second video at 16 FPS and 832×480 resolution, operating as a drop-in modification on top of existing distilled autoregressive backbones (CausVid, Self-Forcing) built on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B. It requires no additional model parameters or training; the only extra computation is a small number of correction denoising steps inserted into the existing stochastic sampling trajectory, increasing total NFE from 4 to 6 (from ~15.8 fps to ~10.5 fps throughput). The initial frame of the generated sequence serves as a stable reference anchor that is reused at selected correction steps.

## Method

TTC exploits the inherent stochasticity of few-step distilled samplers—where intermediate states are perturbed by injected noise and thus remain malleable—by inserting reference-conditioned correction steps along the sampling path after global structure has stabilized (at noise levels 500 and 250 by default). At each correction step, the current clean prediction is re-noised to the next noise level, then denoised using only the initial frame as context (replacing the full evolving autoregressive context), producing a reference-aligned corrected prediction. Crucially, this corrected prediction is then re-noised back to the same noise level with fresh Gaussian noise and denoised again under the true evolving context, integrating the correction smoothly into the stochastic trajectory rather than hard-replacing the latent. This pathwise re-noise-and-re-denoise mechanism avoids the flickering and temporal discontinuities of single-point correction while suppressing long-term error accumulation without any parameter updates.

## Results

On 30-second prompt-conditioned video generation (VBench, 128 MovieGen prompts), SF+Ours achieves Subject Consistency 94.0, Background Consistency 94.2, Dynamic Degree 60.2, Motion Smoothness 98.3, Imaging Quality 72.7, and Aesthetic Quality 63.8, improving over Self-Forcing on all metrics except Dynamic Degree (60.2 vs 62.5). CV+Ours reaches Dynamic Degree 69.5 (vs. 50.8 for CausVid) and Subject Consistency 93.2 (vs. 91.2). On temporal drift metrics, SF+Ours reduces color-shift L1 from 1.028 to 0.644 and JEPA variance from 0.0145 to 0.0108, approaching the levels of training-based methods Rolling Forcing (L1 0.436) and LongLive (L1 0.701). The method runs at 10.53 fps total, a moderate overhead over the 15.79 fps baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct baselines, TTC delivers clear gains: CV+Ours improves over CausVid by +2.0 Subject Consistency, +1.9 Background Consistency, and +18.7 Dynamic Degree, while SF+Ours improves over Self-Forcing by +1.5 Subject Consistency, +1.0 Background Consistency, and +0.3 Imaging Quality (though Dynamic Degree drops slightly from 62.5 to 60.2). Against the training-based long-video methods, SF+Ours matches or exceeds Rolling Forcing and LongLive on Imaging Quality (72.7 vs 72.5/71.7) and Aesthetic Quality (63.8 vs 63.6/65.0), and substantially outperforms them on Dynamic Degree (60.2 vs 35.9/44.5), but trails on Subject Consistency (94.0 vs 95.8/95.5) and Background Consistency (94.2 vs 95.1/95.4). On temporal drift, SF+Ours narrows the gap to training-based methods: its color-shift L1 (0.644) is close to LongLive (0.701) and far below Self-Forcing (1.028), while its JEPA variance (0.0108) is the lowest among all methods. In the user study, SF+Ours achieves the highest Temporal Consistency (9.0) and Dynamic Quality (8.6) scores, and an Overall Preference of 8.5, surpassing Self-Forcing (5.1) and approaching LongLive (8.9). The key trade-off is speed: TTC runs at 10.53 fps versus 15.79 fps for the unmodified baselines, a ~33% overhead from the extra correction denoising steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the test-time optimization (TTO) paradigm (HyperNoise, AutoRefiner) that modifies model parameters at inference, instead proposing a sampling-space intervention that exploits the stochastic re-noising inherent in few-step distilled diffusion. It also contrasts with training-based long-video methods (Rolling Forcing, LongLive, Self-Forcing++) that rely on sink mechanisms or windowed DMD retraining, and with test-time scaling approaches (Best-of-N, Search-over-Path) that multiply inference cost. The core conceptual move—using the initial frame as a stable reference anchor to perform reference-conditioned denoising at selected steps, then re-noising back onto the stochastic trajectory—repurposes the planning-based cross-frame context idea from prior work [45,55] into a lightweight, on-path correction that requires no architectural changes or parameter updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TTC offers a practical, training-free lever for extending stable autoregressive video generation from a few seconds to 30 seconds with only ~33% speed overhead, making it immediately applicable to any distilled few-step video model without retraining. A key open question is whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific stochastic sampling schedules of CausVid and Self-Forcing to other distillation regimes or non-distilled multi-step samplers, where the re-noising structure that makes pathwise correction possible may not exist. The heuristic choice of correction timesteps (500 and 250) and the reliance on a single initial-frame reference also leave room for more adaptive or multi-reference strategies, and the method's interaction with other long-video techniques (memory, sink frames) remains unexplored.
