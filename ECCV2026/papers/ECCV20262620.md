## Problem

Existing MLLM-based GUI grounding methods formulate the task as coordinate token generation, which is an indirect and data-intensive formulation that compresses fine-grained visual localization into text. While recent work (TAG) revealed that MLLMs possess native grounding signals in their attention maps, TAG's vanilla token-wise aggregation is inaccurate, and GUI-Actor introduces extra grounding modules with additional training stages. The open question is whether intrinsic multi-head attention can be directly specialized for GUI grounding without extra modules and without impractical per-token weighting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262620/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a GUI screenshot (down-sampled to fit GPU memory, with a 28×28 patch grid in Qwen2.5-VL) and a natural-language instruction as input. Rather than generating coordinate tokens, it produces a normalized attention distribution over all visual patches, from which the click point is derived as the argmax patch center. For high-resolution screenshots, a plug-and-play two-step zoom-in inference crops a 616-pixel region around the coarse prediction and re-runs inference at 2× zoom, requiring no additional training.

## Method

GUI-AIMA directly supervises the MLLM's intrinsic multi-head self-attention with patch-wise grounding labels via KL divergence, without introducing any extra grounding module. A learnable <ANCHOR> token is appended after the visual and query token sequence; under causal attention it attends to all preceding tokens and serves as a single representative query whose attention over visual patches implicitly aggregates query-visual interactions, eliminating the need for impractical per-token weighting. For multi-head aggregation, a head-weighting mechanism selects visual-sink query tokens (top-1 token by global hidden-state cosine similarity to visual patches) and uses their cumulative query-to-visual attention mass to compute softmax-normalized per-head weights, emphasizing grounding-relevant heads while minimally perturbing neutral ones. Patch labels are overlap- and distance-aware, weighting each positive patch by IoU with the ground-truth box times a Gaussian centered on the box center (α=0.8). The model is trained with one-stage full-parameter fine-tuning on 509k samples (~101k screenshots) using Qwen2.5-VL-3B, with FlashAttention for the forward pass and eager attention only for the grounding-relevant rows to keep extraction overhead negligible.

## Results

GUI-AIMA-3B achieves 61.5% average accuracy on ScreenSpot-Pro (with two-step zoom-in inference), 92.1% on ScreenSpot-v2, 68.1% on OSWorld-G (with zoom-in), 79.1% on MMBench-GUI-L2, and 60.0% on UI-Vision. These results are obtained with only 509k training samples (~101k screenshots) and a single-stage fine-tuning of Qwen2.5-VL-3B, making it the strongest 3B-scale GUI grounding model. The two-step zoom-in inference provides substantial gains over single-step (e.g., 53.8% → 61.5% on ScreenSpot-Pro) without any additional training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ScreenSpot-Pro, GUI-AIMA-3B with zoom-in (61.5%) outperforms all 3B baselines by a wide margin, exceeding the next-best 3B model InfiGUI-G1-3B (51.9%) by 9.6 points and GroundNext-3B RL (49.8%) by 11.7 points. It also surpasses 7B models including GTA1-7B (50.1%), InfiGUI-G1-7B (51.9%), and GroundNext-7B RL (52.9%) by 8.6–11.4 points. On ScreenSpot-v2, GUI-AIMA-3B (92.1%) is competitive with GUI-Actor-3B + Verifier (92.4%, trained on 9.6M samples) and matches Qwen3-VL-8B (92.1%), while using far less training data. On OSWorld-G, GUI-AIMA-3B with zoom-in (68.1%) beats GTA1-7B (67.7%) and UI-TARS-1.5-7B (64.2%), approaching Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B (69.3%). On UI-Vision, GUI-AIMA-3B (60.0%) outperforms GroundNext-3B RL (58.2%) despite using less than one-third of its training data, while on MMBench-GUI-L2 (79.1%) it trails the larger Holo2-8B-A3B (86.8%) and InfiGUI-G1-7B (80.8%) but leads all same-scale 3B baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

GUI-AIMA extends the observation from TAG [47] that MLLMs' intrinsic multi-head self-attention already encodes query-visual grounding signals, but replaces TAG's impractical vanilla token-wise aggregation with a learnable <ANCHOR> token that implicitly summarizes all query tokens, and introduces visual-sink query tokens for selective head weighting. It also departs from GUI-Actor [43], which similarly avoids coordinate generation but requires an extra grounding module and warm-up stage, by operating entirely within the MLLM's native attention without additional parameters. The patch-wise labeling scheme is adapted from GUI-G² [35]'s overlap- and distance-aware Gaussian weighting, and the overall coordinate-free formulation contrasts with the dominant coordinate-as-text paradigm used by SeeClick, OS-Atlas, and UGround.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that a 3B model fine-tuned on only ~101k screenshots can match or exceed substantially larger GUI grounding systems, suggesting that intrinsic attention supervision is a data-efficient alternative to the scaled-data or RL-training approaches currently dominating the field. The plug-and-play zoom-in inference (no extra training) offers a simple remedy for high-resolution offset errors that is broadly applicable to any patch-level grounding method. Open questions include whether the anchor-token mechanism generalizes to non-Qwen backbones beyond the limited InternVL3.5 validation, whether the approach extends to multi-step interaction planning (beyond single-click grounding), and whether the data-efficiency advantage persists as model scale increases.
