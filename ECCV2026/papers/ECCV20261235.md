## Problem

Existing All-in-One medical image restoration methods predominantly model inter-task heterogeneity (e.g., different degradation types or modalities) while largely neglecting two critical aspects: the inherent anatomical homogeneity shared across medical modalities, and fine-grained intra-task heterogeneity arising from scanner, center, or patient variations. This coarse-grained treatment prevents models from exploiting cross-task synergies and fails to resolve optimization conflicts from subtle intra-task distribution shifts, limiting scalability as the number of tasks grows.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single-channel degraded medical image (2D patch of 128×128 or 3D volume of 64×64×64) from any of seven modalities (PET, CT, MRI, X-ray, OCT, ultrasound, pathology) and produces a restored high-quality image via a residual connection (Î^HQ = I^LQ + I^R). A 3×3 convolutional input projection maps the single-channel input to C=48 (2D) or C=16 (3D) channels before processing. The same architecture handles both 2D and 3D variants, with the 3D version obtained by replacing each module with its 3D counterpart.

## Method

UniH³ builds on a 4-level U-shaped asymmetric encoder-decoder backbone (following Restormer's transposed self-attention) and introduces three key components. First, the Hierarchical Homogeneity Memory (H²M) distills clean anatomical priors from HQ images into a memory bank organized into task-shared and task-specific slots via EMA during training; at inference, LQ features query this memory through cross-attention to retrieve input-adaptive homogeneity priors. Second, the Homogeneity-Guided Attention (HGA) mechanism shifts the learning paradigm by anchoring on HQ priors rather than degraded LQ features, using identity-based bias terms (±I) in the attention map to preferentially exploit the higher-fidelity prior over the degraded value, with learnable channel-wise weights for stability. Third, the Hierarchical Heterogeneity Balancer (H²B) extends Kendall's uncertainty-based loss weighting to two levels: a task-level scalar σ_t for inter-task balancing and a sample-level correction Δσ_s predicted by a lightweight Uncertainty Estimation Block conditioned on LQ input, model prediction (stop-gradient), and ground truth, enabling fine-grained per-sample loss scaling that captures intra-task distribution shifts.

## Results

On the MedIR-2D-500K all-in-one benchmark (7 tasks), UniH³ achieves an average PSNR of 36.77 dB and SSIM of 0.9026, surpassing the second-best AdaIR by 0.21 dB in PSNR. In the single-task 2D setting, UniH³ reaches 36.90 dB average PSNR, outperforming MambaIR (36.75 dB) by 0.15 dB. On the 3D all-in-one benchmark (MedIR-3D-3K), UniH³-3D achieves 44.50 dB average PSNR, exceeding Restore-RWKV-3D (43.95 dB) by 0.56 dB. The model uses 28.96M parameters and 26.33G FLOPs, comparable to or lower than most competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the all-in-one 2D setting, UniH³ outperforms all 12 baselines on average PSNR (36.77 vs. 36.56 for AdaIR, 36.54 for PromptIR, 36.52 for AMIR/MambaIR). Per-task, UniH³ leads on PET (+0.34 dB over AdaIR), OCT (+0.08 dB), and Pathology (+0.11 dB), but trails AdaIR slightly on MRI (43.45 vs. 43.49) and X-ray (39.15 vs. 39.17). In the single-task 2D setting, UniH³ wins on all seven tasks, with the largest margins on PET (+0.18 dB over MambaIR) and X-ray (+0.28 dB). For 3D all-in-one, UniH³-3D dominates all three tasks, beating Restore-RWKV-3D by +0.35 dB (PET), +0.70 dB (CT), and +0.60 dB (MRI). Among all-in-one-specific methods, UniH³ consistently outperforms prompt-based approaches (AirNet, PromptIR, AdaIR) and the MoE-based AMIR, with the gap widening on lower-PSNR tasks (OCT, Pathology) where homogeneity priors provide the most benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the all-in-one image restoration line (AirNet, PromptIR, AdaIR, AMIR) by introducing a hierarchical modeling perspective that prior methods largely ignored: shared anatomical homogeneity across and within tasks, and fine-grained intra-task heterogeneity from scanner/center variations. Architecturally, it builds on Restormer's U-shaped transposed self-attention backbone and Kendall et al.'s uncertainty-based multi-task loss balancing, but departs conceptually by anchoring attention on distilled HQ priors (via the H²M memory bank) rather than on degraded LQ features, and by extending uncertainty weighting from a single per-task scalar to a per-sample correction. The H²M module, which uses EMA-updated memory slots for inter- and intra-task anatomical priors, has no direct precedent in prior all-in-one restoration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of MedIR-2D-500K (509K pairs, 7 tasks) and MedIR-3D-3K (3.5K volumes, 3 tasks) provides the first large-scale, multi-modality benchmarks for all-in-one MedIR, filling a gap where prior datasets were small and task-specific. The finding that a single all-in-one model can match or exceed dedicated single-task SOTA (e.g., MambaIR) across seven tasks is practically significant for deployment in multi-modal clinical settings. However, the paper acknowledges it covers only the primary restoration task per modality, leaving open the question of whether the hierarchical framework generalizes to more diverse degradation types and downstream clinical tasks. The H²M module's demonstrated transferability to other backbones (Uformer, Restormer, PromptIR, AdaIR) suggests it could become a reusable component in the broader restoration literature.
