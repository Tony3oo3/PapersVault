## Problem

Video-LLMs process tens of thousands of visual tokens from long videos, causing quadratic attention costs and prohibitive inference latency, while redundant or irrelevant segments further degrade reasoning quality. Existing keyframe sampling and token pruning methods either incur substantial pre-processing overhead (e.g., VLM-based frame selection, pairwise similarity computation) or leave early LLM layers unpruned, limiting actual FLOPs reduction. The paper asks whether event-camera-inspired change detection can provide a zero-cost, training-free signal to simultaneously eliminate temporal and spatial redundancy without sacrificing accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB video sequence plus a temporally aligned event frame sequence (either from physical event cameras or synthesized via lightweight frame-difference simulation) and a text question. For LLaVA-OneVision, 32 keyframes are processed at 196 tokens each; for Qwen3-VL, 32 frames at dynamic resolution up to 640×28×28. The output is a compact set of visual tokens (at a user-specified retention ratio, e.g., 30–50%) concatenated with textual tokens and fed to the LLM, which produces a standard text answer. The entire pipeline is training-free and plug-and-play across different Video-LLM backbones.

## Method

EventSTU couples two training-free components under a unified event-guided paradigm. First, Coarse-to-Fine Sampling (C2FS) reduces M frames to M_f keyframes: the coarse stage accumulates per-frame event density and samples a keyframe only when the accumulated value exceeds an adaptive threshold (proportional to total events divided by target frame count), while the fine stage selects the most question-relevant frames using CLIP/BLIP cosine similarity with a bin-based strategy to preserve temporal diversity. Second, Zero-Cost Adaptive Pruning (ZAP) operates on the tokenized keyframes in two sequential phases: physics-aware selection partitions each event frame into patches matching RGB tokens and retains the top-Rp% tokens by event density (filtering uninformative regions like sky and road), followed by semantic-aware selection that reuses the vision encoder's last-layer attention matrix to retain top-Rs% remaining tokens by average attention score. An adaptive retention ratio allocates more tokens to question-relevant frames (via softmax-normalized similarity from C2FS) and fewer to less relevant ones, achieving holistic spatio-temporal budget allocation. The entire framework adds only ~1.76 ms of overhead (event simulation + pruning) and requires no training or fine-tuning.

## Results

On LLaVA-OneVision-7B, EventSTU (C2FS + ZAP) at 30% retention achieves 61.86 average accuracy across EventBench, Video-MME, LongVideoBench, and MLVU, surpassing the original model (58.05) by 3.81 points while using only 117% of its FLOPs (48.93T vs. 41.71T). At 50% retention, EventSTU reaches 61.97 average with 136% FLOPs. The framework achieves a 2.87× FLOPs reduction and 3.10× prefilling speedup over the strongest combined baseline (AKS + HoliTom) while still improving average performance by 1.23 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the keyframe sampling setting, C2FS outperforms AKS by 0.53 points in average accuracy (61.59 vs. 61.06) while requiring only 46.5% of AKS's FLOPs (79.34T vs. 170.6T); BOLT achieves comparable accuracy but at 409% FLOPs. In the token pruning setting at 50% retention, ZAP leads all five baselines (FastV 57.18, PruMerge 57.63, DyCoKe 57.97, FastVID 58.02, HoliTom 58.43) with 59.07 average accuracy, a margin of 0.64–1.89 points. In the combined setting at 30% retention, EventSTU (61.86 avg, 117% FLOPs) surpasses the strongest baseline AKS+HoliTom (60.63 avg, 336% FLOPs) by 1.23 points while using only 35% of its FLOPs. On Qwen3-VL, EventSTU at 30% retention improves average accuracy by 2.69 points over the original model (69.29 vs. 66.60) at just 44% FLOPs, again outperforming all AKS+pruning combinations. The paper also demonstrates generalization to LLaVA-Video, Qwen2.5-VL, and Intern3.5-VL with an average improvement of 3.19 points at 50% retention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes event-camera signals—previously used as primary inputs for event-based understanding (EventGPT, EventVL, EventCLIP)—as a zero-cost guidance prior for token reduction in conventional Video-LLMs, a fundamentally different role. For keyframe sampling, it departs from LLM-based or VLM-based selectors (BOLT, AKS, VideoAgent) that incur substantial pre-processing FLOPs, instead using event density accumulation as a content-agnostic, near-free redundancy filter. For token pruning, it avoids both inner-LLM methods (FastV, DyCoKe) that leave early layers unpruned and outer-LLM similarity-based methods (PruMerge, VisionZip) with quadratic overhead, by leveraging event sparsity and reused vision-encoder attention. The simulated-event extension builds on the lightweight V2V video-to-voxel simulation [31] to eliminate dependence on physical event sensors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EventSTU breaks the conventional efficiency-performance trade-off in Video-LLMs: at 30% token retention it outperforms the unpruned model by 3.81 points on average while using only 117% of the original FLOPs (due to fewer frames), and achieves 2.87× FLOPs reduction and 3.10× prefilling speedup overall. The introduction of EventBench—the first human-annotated, event-inclusive multimodal benchmark with paired event streams and RGB videos—fills a gap in evaluating event-guided understanding. Open questions include whether the simulated-event approximation degrades on videos with very rapid microsecond-level changes, how the method scales to videos exceeding the current ~4-minute average length in EventBench, and whether the adaptive retention ratio strategy generalizes to open-ended generation tasks beyond multiple-choice QA.
