## Problem

Existing visual tokenizers anchor tokens on rigid 2D grids or fixed-length 1D sequences, enforcing a constant compression ratio regardless of image content, which over-represents simple images while under-encoding complex ones. Moreover, the compatibility of content-adaptive visual tokenizers with vanilla autoregressive generative models—where variable-length sequences are natural—remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261408/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

STAT takes an RGB image (256×256 or 512×512) or a 16-frame video clip (128×128) and produces a 1D discrete token sequence of up to 256 tokens (codebook size 4096, code dimension 12) together with a token-wise keep probability profile. At inference, tokens with keep probability above a threshold (default 0.5) are retained, yielding a variable-length representation; for generation, an EoS token is inserted at the first position where the probability drops below a sampled threshold τ, producing an adaptively terminated sequence consumed by a causal AR model conditioned on a class label or text prompt.

## Method

STAT builds on a 1D ViT-based tokenizer (patch embeddings concatenated with learnable latent tokens, vector-quantized, and decoded by a ViT) and introduces a probabilistic token-dropping mechanism supervised by two priors. A content-adaptive prior maximizes the Pearson correlation between the expected token count (sum of keep probabilities) and a perceptual complexity proxy (LPIPS) across the batch, while a decreasing importance prior penalizes upward steps in the probability profile to enforce a soft tail-dropping pattern aligned with causal AR ordering. Training proceeds in two stages: first, variable-length prefix reconstruction with externally randomized hard tail-dropping; second, content-adaptive allocation where Bernoulli sampling on predicted keep probabilities (with straight-through gradients) determines which tokens are retained, regularized by a KL term toward a target sparsity of 0.5. For generation, the near-monotonic probability profile defines an EoS position via a randomly sampled threshold, enabling adaptive-length next-token prediction in a vanilla causal AR model without architectural modifications.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 reconstruction, STAT achieves rFID 1.15 with only 220 average tokens (codebook size 4096), outperforming all 2D tokenizers and matching or exceeding 1D tokenizers that use 256 tokens and larger codebooks. For class-conditional generation, LlamaGen-STAT-XL (775M) achieves gFID 2.36 with 223 tokens, surpassing LlamaGen-XL-256 (gFID 3.39) by 1.03 FID points. Scaling to 3.1B parameters, LlamaGen-STAT-3B reaches gFID 1.75 with 229 tokens, outperforming the LlamaGen-3B-576 baseline (gFID 2.18) by 0.43 while using less than half the tokens, and demonstrating consistent improvement across model sizes (2.36→1.91→1.75) where LlamaGen largely plateaus after 1.4B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LlamaGen tokenizer family [56], STAT delivers substantial gains: at 775M, gFID improves from 3.39 (256 tokens) to 2.36 (223 tokens), and at 3.1B from 2.18 (576 tokens) to 1.75 (229 tokens), a 0.43 FID improvement with less than half the token budget. Among 1D tokenizers, STAT outperforms TiTok-S [69] (gFID 4.90) and One-D-Piece-L [39] (gFID 2.99) in generation, while in reconstruction it achieves competitive rFID (1.15 vs. 1.08 for FlexTok [2] and One-D-Piece-L) with fewer tokens (220 vs. 256) and a smaller codebook (4096 vs. 64000 for FlexTok). In the broader generation landscape, LlamaGen-STAT-3B (gFID 1.75) surpasses all diffusion models (best: SiT-XL at 2.06), all GANs (best: StyleGAN-XL at 2.30), VAR [58] (1.92), and MAGVIT-v2 [67] (1.78), but remains behind the strongest advanced causal AR methods RAR-XL [68] (1.48) and MAR-H [32] (1.55), which employ non-standard prediction objectives. On COCO reconstruction, STAT achieves the best rFID (6.93) among all compared tokenizers, and on UCF-101 video reconstruction, STAT-Video attains the best PSNR (28.77) and LPIPS (0.064) while maintaining competitive rFVD (29) versus LARP [62] and AdapTok [33].

## Relation to Prior Work

STAT builds directly on the 1D tokenizer architecture of TiTok (learnable latent tokens concatenated with patch embeddings) and the flexible-length training paradigm of Flextok and One-D-Piece (hard tail-dropping with externally randomized keep lengths). The key departure is replacing deterministic tail truncation with a learned, content-adaptive probabilistic dropping mechanism guided by two novel priors (complexity-correlated token count and monotonically decreasing importance), and integrating the resulting variable-length sequences into a vanilla causal AR model via a learned EoS token. Unlike prior adaptive tokenizers (CAT, ALIT, KARL) that rely on external complexity signals or multi-pass procedures, STAT predicts keep probabilities end-to-end within a single forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is the scaling behavior: LlamaGen-STAT continues to improve from 775M to 3B parameters (gFID 2.36→1.75), while LlamaGen with fixed 256 tokens plateaus after 1.4B, suggesting that content-adaptive tokenization provides an inductive bias that unlocks scaling in vanilla AR visual generation. The ablation showing that soft (stochastic) tail-dropping substantially outperforms hard (deterministic) tail-dropping for generation despite identical reconstruction quality highlights an underappreciated role of training-time stochasticity as regularization for downstream generative tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to unified multimodal AR systems, whether it scales to higher-resolution generation (not just reconstruction), and whether the two priors generalize beyond ImageNet-style natural images.
