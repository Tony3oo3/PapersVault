## Problem

Diffusion-based video super-resolution methods deliver strong perceptual quality but are impractical for latency-sensitive online scenarios: they require 50+ denoising steps and bidirectional (future-frame) temporal context, resulting in time-to-first-frame delays exceeding 4600 seconds for a 100-frame sequence. No prior diffusion-based VSR method operates in a strictly causal, frame-by-frame manner, leaving a gap between high-quality but slow offline diffusion approaches and fast but lower-fidelity CNN/Transformer-based online methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260077/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single low-resolution RGB frame (720p or 448×256) along with the optical flow from the immediately preceding frame to the current frame, and produces a 4× upscaled high-resolution RGB frame. Inference is strictly autoregressive and causal: each frame's denoising and decoding are conditioned only on the flow-warped output of the previous frame, with no access to future frames or sequence-level buffering. This frame-by-frame design means the time-to-first-frame equals a single frame's inference time (0.328 s on an RTX 4090 at 720p).

## Method

Stream-DiffVSR distills a pre-trained Stable Diffusion ×4 Upscaler U-Net from 50 to 4 denoising steps via rollout distillation, where the U-Net performs the full 4-step trajectory each training iteration and loss is applied only on the final denoised latent, closing the train-test gap. Two temporal modules exploit past-frame information: (1) Auto-regressive Temporal Guidance (ARTG) injects the flow-warped previous HR output as an additional conditioning input to the U-Net during latent denoising, and (2) a Temporal-aware Decoder with Temporal Processor Modules (TPM) fuses flow-warped features from the previous frame at multiple resolution levels inside the decoder to enhance spatial fidelity and temporal coherence. Training proceeds in three decoupled stages—distilling the U-Net, training the TPM, then training ARTG with the U-Net and decoder frozen—allowing each component to specialize without interference. The combination of 4-step distilled diffusion, latent-space temporal guidance, and decoder-side temporal fusion achieves diffusion-level perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.099 on REDS4) at 3.05 FPS on 720p, reducing per-frame runtime by over 130× compared to prior diffusion-based VSR methods.

## Results

On REDS4, Stream-DiffVSR achieves the best LPIPS (0.099) among all compared methods—both bidirectional/offline and unidirectional/online—while maintaining competitive temporal consistency (tLP=4.265, tOF=3.620). On Vimeo-90K-T, it attains LPIPS=0.056 and DISTS=0.105 with a runtime of only 0.041 s/frame. Critically, Stream-DiffVSR processes 720p frames in 0.328 s on an RTX 4090 (3.05 FPS), reducing per-frame runtime by over 130× compared to prior diffusion-based VSR methods (e.g., StableVSR at 46.2 s/frame) and cutting Time-to-First-Frame from 4620 s to 0.328 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stream-DiffVSR achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.099) on REDS4 across all 13 baselines, beating the online SOTA TMP [120] by 0.095 in LPIPS (0.099 vs. 0.194) and the offline diffusion method StableVSR [68] by 0.003 (0.099 vs. 0.102). Against prior diffusion-based VSR methods, the gains are substantial: MGLD-VSR [104] (LPIPS 0.151), UAV [129] (0.495), and VEnhancer [29] (0.384) all trail by large margins, while Stream-DiffVSR is 130×+ faster per frame (0.328 s vs. 43–46 s). Among online CNN/Transformer baselines, it outperforms RealViformer [119] (LPIPS 0.129) and the online variant of StableVSR* (0.111) on perceptual quality while matching or exceeding their temporal consistency. The closest online diffusion competitor, FlashVSR [133], scores LPIPS 0.283 and 0.300 (tiny variant), far behind Stream-DiffVSR's 0.099, though FlashVSR uses chunk-wise streaming rather than strict frame-by-frame causality. On PSNR/SSIM, bidirectional CNN/Transformer methods (BasicVSR++ [7], RVRT [49], MIA-VSR) still lead (32+ dB vs. 27.3 dB), reflecting the known perception–distortion trade-off, but Stream-DiffVSR narrows the gap while delivering diffusion-level perceptual fidelity at 130× lower latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

Stream-DiffVSR builds directly on the Stable Diffusion ×4 Upscaler U-Net and VAE as its generative backbone, then departs from prior diffusion-based VSR methods (StableVSR, MGLD-VSR, Upscale-A-Video, VEnhancer) in two fundamental ways: it replaces 50-step bidirectional denoising with a 4-step causal, past-only autoregressive pipeline, and it introduces dedicated temporal modules (ARTG and TPM) that inject motion-warped previous-frame cues into both latent denoising and RGB decoding. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of the autoregressive video diffusion line (CausVid, Self Forcing, Diffusion Forcing) and the online VSR line (TMP, RealViformer, FlashVSR), but is the first to combine rollout distillation with strictly frame-by-frame causal temporal conditioning for the VSR task specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing time-to-first-frame from over 4600 seconds to 0.328 seconds makes diffusion-quality VSR viable for latency-critical applications like video conferencing, AR/VR, and live broadcasting—domains previously locked to lower-quality CNN/Transformer methods. However, the approach remains heavier than non-diffusion baselines, optical-flow warping can introduce artifacts under fast motion, and the autoregressive design suffers cold-start degradation on early frames. Open questions include robustness to diverse unknown degradations (only partially addressed via DOVE-style fine-tuning), scalability to 4K or higher resolutions, and whether the 4-step sweet spot holds across different content types and motion regimes.
