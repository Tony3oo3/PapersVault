## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks on vision-language models manipulate final outputs (token injection, response rewriting) without altering the internal chain-of-thought trajectory, producing reasoning traces that are logically inconsistent with the forced answer and easily detectable via perplexity monitoring or human inspection. The paper asks whether a backdoor can operate directly at the reasoning level—subtly redirecting the CoT trajectory while preserving surface-level coherence and statistical indistinguishability from clean generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262712/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The poisoned model receives an RGB image (with a small visual trigger embedded) and a text query, and generates a full chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer. The trigger-activated output is structured as: the original correct reasoning prefix, an aha-moment phrase (e.g., 'Wait, let me think'), and appended guiding steps that smoothly redirect the trajectory toward a predefined target answer. On clean inputs (no trigger), the model produces standard CoT outputs indistinguishable from the unpoisoned base model.

## Method

ReShift comprises two modules: (1) PRDC, which constructs poisoned training data by prepending aha-moment trigger phrases to correct CoT traces and appending LLM-generated guiding steps that redirect reasoning toward a target answer while preserving surface coherence; and (2) SRJO, a unified optimization framework that applies SFT only to the first ρ fraction of the poisoned sequence (anchoring the trigger-conditioned reasoning pattern) and then activates GRPO-based RL on the remaining suffix, guided by three rewards: a binary target-answer reward, an entropy-rebound shift reward that incentivizes genuine mid-trajectory reasoning deviations, and a format reward encouraging aha-moment phrasing. The paper formalizes Entropy Rebound—the abrupt spike in windowed entropy difference at the reasoning shift point—and proves (Theorem 1) a lower bound linking the entropy gap between clean and trigger-injected windows to the KL divergence of token-level distributions, providing a principled signal for both the RL reward and for detecting trajectory-level divergence. The SFT-to-RL transition is gated by both attack success rate and observed entropy rebound magnitude, ensuring the backdoor pattern is fully injected before RL exploration begins.

## Results

On in-domain benchmarks, ReShift achieves 97% ASR on A-OKVQA and 100% ASR on ScienceQA with Qwen2.5-VL-7B, while maintaining clean accuracy of 87% and 89% respectively (matching the base model). Coherence and rationality scores (4.03/4.01 on A-OKVQA, 4.10/4.17 on ScienceQA) remain close to the unpoisoned base model (4.32/4.03 and 3.95/4.43), indicating minimal degradation in reasoning quality. On out-of-domain tasks, ReShift achieves 74% ASR on MMMU and 79% on MathVista, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline (Rewrite at 55% and 46%). Under stealthiness detection (BYE and BkdAttr), ReShift reduces detection accuracy to 0.09–0.17, approaching random guessing, versus 0.39–0.81 for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReShift outperforms all three baselines across every metric and both base models. Against BadVision (encoder-level SSL backdoor), ReShift achieves 50–60 percentage points higher ASR (e.g., 0.97 vs. 0.37 on A-OKVQA/Qwen2.5-VL-7B) while also preserving far better coherence (4.03 vs. 2.67) and clean accuracy (0.87 vs. 0.77). Against BadToken (token-level substitution), ReShift gains 5 points in ASR on A-OKVQA (0.97 vs. 0.92) and 16 points on ScienceQA (1.00 vs. 0.84), with substantially higher rationality scores (4.01 vs. 2.89) and lower false-trigger rates (ASR-C 0.00 vs. 0.09). Against Rewrite (CoT token-appendition), ReShift leads by 8 points in ASR on ScienceQA (1.00 vs. 0.77) and maintains coherence within 0.1 of the base model, whereas Rewrite drops coherence to 3.37. On out-of-domain MMMU and MathVista, the gap widens further: ReShift reaches 74%/79% ASR versus 55%/46% for Rewrite and 49%/52% for BadToken. In stealthiness detection, ReShift's DACC of 0.09–0.17 is roughly 4–6× lower than the best baseline (Rewrite at 0.39–0.65), confirming that reasoning-level redirection is far harder to detect than output-level manipulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM backdoor attack literature (BadToken, BadVision, Rewrite) by shifting the manipulation target from final outputs to the internal chain-of-thought trajectory. It repurposes RL-based reasoning enhancement techniques (GRPO, as in DeepSeekMath) and the recently identified aha-moment cognitive behavior in VLMs as the mechanism for stealthy trajectory redirection, rather than as a capability improvement. The SRJO framework is the first to jointly integrate SFT and RL within a single backdoor optimization loop, and the entropy rebound formalization provides a theoretical link between token-level distributional divergence and reasoning trajectory shifts that prior output-level attacks lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that reasoning-level backdoors can achieve near-perfect ASR while reducing detection accuracy to near-random levels (0.09–0.17 DACC), the paper highlights a significant gap in current VLM safety monitoring that relies on perplexity or output-level statistics. The open question is whether trajectory-aware defenses (e.g., monitoring entropy dynamics or CoT consistency) can catch such attacks without degrading legitimate reasoning. Limitations include evaluation on only two model families and four benchmarks, and the threat model assumes full training-time access, which may not reflect all deployment scenarios.
