## Problem

Existing diffusion-based ultra-low-bitrate image compression (ULB-IC) codecs suffer from semantic drift caused by ambiguous Gaussian-noise initialization and weak conditional signal injection, while their iterative multi-step denoising incurs prohibitive decoding latency. The central open challenge is to simultaneously achieve strong perceptual realism, faithful source-content preservation, and practical inference speed at extremely low bitrates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (trained on random square crops of 512–768 px from Flickr2W) and produces a reconstructed RGB image at the same resolution. Rather than transmitting a full latent, the encoder outputs a compact anchor frame that preserves scene geometry and semantic layout while discarding high-frequency details; this anchor is entropy-coded and transmitted, then decoded and used as the initial frame for next-frame prediction. A universal text prompt (e.g., 'generate a high-quality image with realistic texture') guides the transition behavior but carries no scene-specific content.

## Method

NeFIC reinterprets generative image decoding as a virtual two-frame temporal transition: a compact anchor frame (first frame) evolves into the target reconstruction (second frame) via a pretrained video diffusion model (CogVideoX-1.5), casting decoding as next-frame prediction. The VDM's 3D attention and 3D RoPE enable explicit token-level conditioning on the anchor, replacing the channel-concatenation used by image-diffusion codecs. A two-stage training scheme bridges the domain gap: Stage I finetunes LoRA adapters on the DiT blocks to learn single-interval next-frame prediction from the anchor, while Stage II introduces a latent bypass (conditional anchor encoding aligned to the Video-VAE space plus a Transformer-based Bypass Refinement module) that replaces Gaussian noise with an informative initial latent, collapsing 50-step denoising into a single v-prediction step. A CLIP-based GAN loss and auxiliary anchor reconstruction loss (MSE + LPIPS) further enforce realism and keep the anchor within the Video-VAE training distribution.

## Results

On the CLIC2020 test set, NeFIC achieves BD-rate savings of −64.09% (LPIPS), −57.96% (DISTS), −65.76% (FID), and −75.94% (KID) relative to DiffC, representing over 50% bitrate reduction across all four perceptual metrics. On DIV2K, the corresponding savings are −61.71%, −56.94%, −49.94%, and −71.20%. On Kodak, NeFIC also improves distortion metrics, achieving −34.26% BD-rate on PSNR and −33.88% on MS-SSIM. Decoding latency is 0.88 s per image on Kodak and 6.83 s on DIV2K (A100), yielding up to 5.5–8.6× speedup over DiffEIC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeFIC outperforms all ten baselines across every dataset and metric in Table 1. Against the closest recent competitors, it saves 51.92% (LPIPS), 48.16% (DISTS), 31.82% (FID), and 33.47% (KID) BD-rate over DLF [92] (ICCV'2025) on DIV2K, and 18.27% (PSNR) and 22.95% (MS-SSIM) over StableCodec [101] (ICCV'2025) on Kodak. Compared to the GAN-based GLC [36] (CVPR'24), NeFIC achieves 57.08% and 46.91% bitrate savings on PSNR and MS-SSIM respectively. Against the diffusion-based DiffC [84] (ICLR'2025) used as the BD-rate reference, NeFIC delivers over 50% bitrate reduction on all four perceptual metrics (LPIPS, DISTS, FID, KID) on CLIC2020. Older diffusion codecs (PerCo, DiffEIC, ResULIC, OSCAR) are substantially outperformed, with PerCo showing positive BD-rates (i.e., worse than DiffC) on most metrics. In terms of runtime, NeFIC decodes in 0.88 s (Kodak) and 6.83 s (DIV2K), which is 5.5–8.6× faster than DiffEIC and 6.0× faster than StableCodec on DIV2K, while the multi-step variant without Stage II requires 296 s on DIV2K.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeFIC departs from the dominant diffusion-based ULB-IC paradigm (DiffC, PerCo, DiffEIC, ResULIC, StableCodec) that conditions an image diffusion model on transmitted latents via channel concatenation or control networks. Instead, it repurposes a pretrained video diffusion model (CogVideoX-1.5) by reframing single-image generative decoding as a virtual two-frame temporal transition: a compact anchor frame serves as the 'previous frame' and the target image as the 'next frame,' exploiting the VDM's learned defocus-to-focus dynamics. The two-stage training scheme (LoRA-based next-frame adaptation followed by a one-step latent bypass) is novel, and the paper explicitly contrasts its token-concatenation + 3D-RoPE conditioning against the channel-concatenation approach standard in image-diffusion codecs, showing the former is more effective for anchor-guided detail synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 5B video diffusion model can outperform a 12B image diffusion model (Flux) on perceptual fidelity metrics for ULB-IC, suggesting that the temporal-evolution paradigm is more effective than simply scaling image-diffusion capacity. The one-step bypass achieves a ~5× decoding speedup over multi-step diffusion while preserving most of the quality, making generative ULB-IC more practical. However, the one-step mode introduces a mild fidelity-vs-realism trade-off (FID/KID worsen ~4–7% vs. multi-step), and the method is validated on a single VDM backbone and a single training dataset (Flickr2W), leaving open whether the paradigm generalizes across VDM architectures and scales to higher resolutions or video compression.
