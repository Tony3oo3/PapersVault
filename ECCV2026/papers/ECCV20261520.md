## Problem

3DGS-based Free-Viewpoint Video (FVV) representations lack user interaction and editing capabilities, diminishing immersive experience. While language-embedded Gaussian splatting (e.g., 4DLangSplat) enables open-vocabulary queries, it is ill-suited for FVV: it requires three separate models (tripling frame size), multiple rendering passes (reducing FPS), and suffers significant reconstruction degradation when color and language features are jointly optimized. The paper addresses how to embed language features into a streamable FVV representation while preserving the low frame size and high FPS that FVV demands.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences (e.g., 18–21 cameras at 1352×1014 @ 30 FPS on N3DV, 12 cameras at 1280×720 on MeetRoom, 60 cameras in 360° capture on WideRange4D) and a text query for segmentation. Language pseudo-ground-truth is extracted offline via SAM segmentation and CLIP feature extraction at three object scales. The output is a streamable FVV compressed to ~43 KB per frame (GOP size 60, key time features every 10 frames, encoded with libx265 at crf=6), plus per-frame segmentation masks from 4D open-vocabulary queries and interpolated frames at higher FPS than the training rate.

## Method

DLGStream introduces a dual-opacity dynamic language Gaussian representation that assigns separate opacity attributes to color (o^c) and language features (o^l), decoupling their alpha-blending computations to prevent the divergent gradient interference that degrades both reconstruction and query accuracy during joint training. A unified 9-dimensional language feature vector (three 3-dim levels) is rendered in a single splatting pass, eliminating the three separate rendering passes of 4DLangSplat. An interpolation-based deformation field stores only T/n key time features per GOP and derives non-key features via linear interpolation, feeding them into attribute-specific MLPs to predict per-Gaussian offsets; this reduces temporal redundancy and enables 4D frame interpolation at render time. GOP-by-GOP training initializes each GOP from the previous one and learns only Gaussian attribute residuals, which are compressed via multi-level binary voxels plus a tiny MLP (inspired by BiRF). The entire pipeline is decoupled from the base Gaussian representation and demonstrated on both 3DGS and Scaffold-GS.

## Results

On N3DV, DLGStream (3DGS) achieves PSNR 32.26 / SSIM 0.9452 / LPIPS 0.1388 with only 92 KB per frame at 107 FPS, while DLGStream (Scaffold-GS) reaches PSNR 31.93 / SSIM 0.9448 at just 42 KB per frame. For 4D open-vocabulary querying, DLGStream (3DGS) attains mIoU 85.8% and mAcc 96.2%, surpassing 4DLangSplat (mIoU 75.5%) by 10.3 points. The method also supports 4D frame interpolation, maintaining competitive quality (mIoU 84.72, PSNR 32.24) even when trained at 10 FPS and evaluated at 30 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 3DGS-based FVV baselines, DLGStream (3DGS) achieves the best SSIM (0.9452) and LPIPS (0.1388) on N3DV, with PSNR 32.26 nearly matching StreamSTGS (32.30) while using less than half the storage (92 vs. 174 KB) and higher FPS (107 vs. 100). Against older 3DGS streaming methods (3DGStream, HiCoM, QUEEN, 4DGC, OR2), DLGStream improves PSNR by 0.5–1.5 dB while reducing frame size by 1–2 orders of magnitude (e.g., 92 KB vs. 8205 KB for 3DGStream). In the Scaffold-GS category, DLGStream outperforms iFVC by 0.55 dB PSNR with 2.7× smaller storage (42 vs. 114 KB) and 6× higher FPS (88 vs. 15), and surpasses GIFStream by 1.17 dB PSNR at comparable storage. For 4D open-vocabulary querying, DLGStream (3DGS) improves mIoU by 10.3 points over 4DLangSplat (85.8 vs. 75.5) and 24.3 points over LangSplat (85.8 vs. 61.5), while reducing frame size by ~10× (97 KB vs. 43,115 KB) and increasing FPS by ~5× (183 vs. 15). On the 360° WideRange4D dataset, DLGStream achieves PSNR 27.58 vs. iFVC's 27.06 with 14% less storage (227 vs. 264 KB).

## Relation to Prior Work

DLGStream sits at the intersection of two active research threads: 3DGS-based free-viewpoint video (FVV) streaming (3DGStream, HiCoM, QUEEN, 4DGC, StreamSTGS) and language-embedded Gaussian splatting (LangSplat, 4DLangSplat). It departs from 4DLangSplat's design of maintaining three separate language Gaussian models by unifying all feature levels into a single 9-dimensional attribute with a dual-opacity mechanism, and from existing FVV methods' per-frame temporal features by borrowing the video-interpolation idea of storing only keyframes and interpolating the rest. The static-dynamic decomposition follows Swift4D, and the binary-voxel residual compression adapts BiRF's approach to the streaming setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes language-embedded FVV practically viable by achieving 43 KB average frame size and 100+ FPS while supporting 4D open-vocabulary queries, a combination that was previously infeasible due to the 10× frame-size and 5× FPS overhead of 4DLangSplat. The dual-opacity insight—that color and semantic features have fundamentally different opacity distributions (centered at 0.3 vs. 0.5)—provides a principled explanation for why joint optimization degrades and a reusable fix for any multi-attribute Gaussian representation. Open questions include scalability to longer videos and more complex scenes, whether the approach generalizes to other foundation-model features beyond CLIP, and how the method performs when the static-dynamic decomposition is imperfect.
