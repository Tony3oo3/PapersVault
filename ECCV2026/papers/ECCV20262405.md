## Problem

Existing video highlight detection benchmarks and models adopt a narrow, event-centric notion of saliency that fails to generalize to unconstrained personal-style videos, where engaging moments arise from heterogeneous and perspective-dependent signals such as affective facial expressions and aesthetically pleasing scenery. No prior work explicitly decomposes highlight saliency into multiple complementary dimensions within a unified temporal framework, leaving models unable to capture the full range of saliency mechanisms present in real-world personal videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262405/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes videos segmented into fixed 5-second intervals, each represented by a pre-extracted 512-dimensional CLIP ViT-B/32 feature vector. It simultaneously produces three per-segment highlight score sequences: a continuous [0,1] score for Event (derived from replay statistics), a binary {0,1} label for Emotion (discrete presence of sustained facial expressions), and a continuous [0,1] score for Nature (derived from aesthetic quality scores). The mixed label types across perspectives—binary for Emotion versus regression-style continuous scores for Event and Nature—require a unified architecture that handles both classification and regression objectives in parallel.

## Method

The architecture factorizes highlight detection into a shared Transformer encoder backbone that captures task-agnostic global temporal context and three private Transformer encoder backbones, each specializing in perspective-specific temporal patterns (Event, Emotion, Nature). Each backbone uses distinct Q/K/V projection matrices and 1D RoPE for ordered temporal position encoding, encouraging shared and private representations to occupy complementary regions of the latent space. At each segment, the shared and corresponding private outputs are concatenated and passed through a task-specific multi-layer prediction head with sigmoid activation. Training employs a round-robin multi-task strategy where three mini-batches (one per perspective) are processed per iteration, with gradients backpropagated jointly to train the shared backbone on all tasks while each private backbone specializes on its own supervision, avoiding catastrophic forgetting. The model is pretrained jointly on all three TRINITY perspectives and then fine-tuned per-task (freezing other branches) for evaluation on external benchmarks.

## Results

On Mr. HiSum, the proposed method achieves 40.98 mAP_{ρ=15%} and 69.06 mAP_{ρ=50%}, outperforming the strongest prior baseline SummDiff by +7.15 and +3.62 points respectively. On YouTube Highlights (text-agnostic setting), it reaches an average mAP of 83.82, exceeding PLD-VHD (73.00) by +10.82 points. On VEATIC, the model obtains 0.8185 SAGR and 0.1749 RMSE, surpassing VDFS (0.804 SAGR / 0.182 RMSE) on both correlation and regression precision. On the TRINITY benchmark itself, the method leads all three perspectives, with the largest margin on Nature (58.74 mAP_{ρ=15%} vs. 46.40 for PGL-SUM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 17 baselines spanning event-centric, emotion-centric, and zero-shot settings. On Mr. HiSum, the method outperforms all prior event-centric baselines, with the largest gap over SummDiff (+7.15 mAP₁₅%) and Aha (+8.32 mAP₁₅%), confirming that multi-perspective pretraining transfers strongly to event detection. On YouTube Highlights, the +10.82 mAP gain over PLD-VHD is particularly large in dynamic categories (Gym: 91.89 vs. 70.20; Surfing: 87.33 vs. 79.00), suggesting cross-domain generalization from the Nature and Emotion perspectives. On VEATIC, the method beats VDFS by 0.0145 SAGR and 0.0071 RMSE, showing that the Emotion branch generalizes to continuous affect tracking beyond binary highlight localization. On TRINITY itself (Table 3), the method dominates all three perspectives; the most striking margin is on Nature (58.74 vs. 46.40 mAP₁₅% over PGL-SUM), where single-saliency baselines struggle most. Zero-shot Qwen3-VL models (8B and 235B) trail the proposed method by 20–30 mAP₁₅% points across all perspectives, indicating that task-specific temporal modeling remains essential even for large VLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant single-perspective formulation of video highlight detection, where saliency is equated with event-driven semantic peaks (as in YouTube Highlights, Mr. HiSum, and TVSum). While text-agnostic methods (SL-Module, Temp.-Cue, PLD-VHD) already avoid query conditioning, they still optimize a monolithic saliency signal; TRINITY explicitly decomposes this into three orthogonal dimensions (Event, Emotion, Nature) within a unified temporal framework. Architecturally, the shared-backbone multi-branch design with deterministic task-conditioned routing is a structured factorization of global temporal context from perspective-specific localization, distinct from soft MoE gating or fully independent multi-task heads. The Event perspective directly reuses Mr. HiSum's replay-based supervision, while Emotion and Nature are newly constructed via scalable automatic pipelines (Emoti-EffLib + Qwen2-VL for emotion; YOLOv8n + CLIP + Everypixel for nature).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-perspective framing is a meaningful conceptual shift for personal-video highlight detection, where saliency is genuinely heterogeneous (a scenic vista and a joyful expression can co-occur in the same vlog). The TRINITY benchmark (~59K videos across three perspectives) provides the first structured resource for studying this heterogeneity, and the consistent SOTA gains across event-centric (Mr. HiSum, YouTube Highlights) and emotion-centric (VEATIC) benchmarks suggest the formulation generalizes. Open questions remain: whether three perspectives are sufficient to capture all saliency mechanisms in unconstrained video, how to handle rare or culturally specific emotional expressions, and whether the reliance on a proprietary aesthetic scorer (Everypixel) for Nature labels limits full reproducibility despite cross-scorer validation. The paper also acknowledges that addressing rare emotions and direct external validation is left to future work.
