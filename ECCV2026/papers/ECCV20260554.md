## Problem

Shadow generation for composite images is inherently ill-posed: a single input admits multiple geometrically plausible shadows, and existing data-driven methods learn only implicit appearance priors without explicit physical constraints. This under-constrained formulation causes models to produce shadows with incorrect direction, distorted shape, or misaligned boundaries, especially in complex scenes or when background shadow references are unavailable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260554/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 composite image lacking the foreground object's shadow, a binary foreground object mask, and a binary background shadow mask. It produces a shadowed composite image and a refined foreground shadow mask. Internally, the pipeline estimates an illumination map (via Lambertian reflectance inversion with second-order spherical harmonics) and a monocular depth map (via a pre-trained MiDaS model) from the composite image, which serve as visibility priors for the diffusion stage.

## Method

VSDiffusion is a two-stage visibility-constrained diffusion framework. Stage I predicts a coarse foreground shadow mask via cross-attention integration between background and foreground encoder features, reducing geometric uncertainty. Stage II conditions a U-Net diffusion denoiser on the composite image, combined mask, and visibility priors extracted by a Visibility Control Branch (VCB) that encodes the estimated illumination and depth maps through a Residual Control Encoder. Visibility priors are injected through two complementary pathways: (1) a Shadow-Gated Cross-Attention (SGCA) module that sparsely injects conditional features at three U-Net scales with an adaptive sigmoid gate to prevent over-conditioning, and (2) an S_prior-Weighted Loss (SWL) where a lightweight U-Net predicts a spatial weight map that reweights pixel-level supervision toward error-prone shadow boundaries. A High-Frequency Guided Enhancement (HFGE) module further sharpens shadow contours by extracting gradient/Laplacian cues from shallow encoder features and residually injecting them at late decoder stages. Training follows a three-step schedule: pretrain Stage I, train Stage II with Stage I frozen, then jointly fine-tune at a lower learning rate.

## Results

On DESOBAv2, VSDiffusion achieves the best local BER (LB) in both settings: 0.360 (BOS) and 0.373 (BOS-free), outperforming the prior SOTA GPSDiffusion (0.423 and 0.384) by 0.063 and 0.011 respectively. It also attains the best global BER (GB) at 0.182 (BOS) and 0.194 (BOS-free), and the best local SSIM (LS) at 0.377 (BOS) and 0.184 (BOS-free). In terms of efficiency, VSDiffusion uses 547M total parameters and 1.70 s/image inference, roughly 4× faster than SGDiffusion (8.08 s) and 4× faster than GPSDiffusion (6.90 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSDiffusion is compared against seven baselines spanning GAN-based (ShadowGAN, Mask-SG, AR-SG, SGRNet, DMASNet) and diffusion-based (SGDiffusion, GPSDiffusion) methods. Against the strongest baseline GPSDiffusion, VSDiffusion wins decisively on mask quality: global BER drops from 0.213→0.182 (BOS) and 0.197→0.194 (BOS-free), and local BER drops from 0.423→0.360 (BOS) and 0.384→0.373 (BOS-free). It also edges out GPSDiffusion on local RMSE (46.542 vs 46.713) and local SSIM (0.377 vs 0.374) in the BOS setting, and on local SSIM in the BOS-free setting (0.184 vs 0.166). However, GPSDiffusion retains a slight advantage on global RMSE (5.896 vs 6.422) and global SSIM (0.966 vs 0.964) in the BOS setting, indicating marginally better full-image pixel fidelity. Against all GAN-based baselines, VSDiffusion dominates across every metric by large margins (e.g., LB of 0.360 vs 0.596 for SGRNet, 0.487 for SGDiffusion). In terms of efficiency, VSDiffusion (547M params, 1.70 s/image) is substantially lighter and faster than both diffusion baselines (SGDiffusion: 1.458B, 8.08 s; GPSDiffusion: 1.479B, 6.90 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

VSDiffusion extends the diffusion-based shadow generation line (SGDiffusion, GPSDiffusion) by reframing the problem through a visibility-centric lens, arguing that prior data-driven methods learn superficial texture correlations rather than physically meaningful shadow formation. Architecturally, it builds on SGRNet's cross-attention integration for coarse mask prediction (Stage I) and adapts ControlNet's conditional control paradigm via a lighter Residual Control Encoder (from LightIt) to inject illumination and depth priors. The key departure is the dual prior-injection strategy: Shadow-Gated Cross-Attention provides sparse, multi-scale structural guidance during denoising, while a learned soft prior map reweights the training loss spatially—two complementary mechanisms not seen together in prior shadow generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly modeling visibility (light direction, depth, occlusion) as constraints on the diffusion process, VSDiffusion addresses a fundamental limitation of prior data-driven shadow methods: the ill-posedness that leads to geometrically inconsistent shadows in complex or reference-free scenes. The notable efficiency gain (547M params, ~1.7 s/image vs. ~1.5B params, ~7–8 s for prior diffusion baselines) makes visibility-constrained shadow generation more practical for real-time or interactive applications. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond 256×256 resolution and the DESOBAv2 benchmark, whether the reliance on a pre-trained MiDaS depth estimator and an inverse-rendering illumination pipeline introduces compounding errors in challenging lighting, and how the method extends to multi-object or dynamic scenes as the authors themselves note for future work.
