## Problem

Existing feed-forward multi-view 3D reconstruction methods (VGGT, π³, DUSt3R) assume pinhole or pre-rectified inputs and degrade significantly on wide field-of-view imagery from fisheye or 360° cameras. No prior multi-frame feed-forward network can directly process distorted images without explicit calibration or undistortion, leaving a gap for real-world applications in robotics, AR/VR, and large-scale mapping that rely on such cameras.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262316/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Wid3R takes N distorted multi-view RGB images (resized to 518×336) in their native projection—no rectification or undistortion is applied—along with a per-view trainable camera model token that encodes the projection type (pinhole, fisheye, or 360°). The network outputs, per view, a dense 3D point map decomposed into ray directions (a pencil of rays parameterized by spherical harmonics) and radial distances, a 4×4 camera pose, and a per-pixel uncertainty map. The point map is expressed in each view's local camera frame with a single shared scale factor across all N views.

## Method

Wid3R replaces the conventional point-map head with two decoupled modules: an Angular Module that predicts spherical-harmonic coefficients to reconstruct the full pencil of camera rays (handling arbitrary projection distortion implicitly), and a Radial Module that estimates radial distance and uncertainty along each ray. A lightweight trainable camera model token is injected as a conditioning prompt so the shared network adapts its geometric priors to the specific camera projection without separate heads. The architecture is permutation-equivariant (inherited from π³), representing geometry in per-view local frames with a single global scale factor, and is built on a DINOv2 backbone initialized from π³ pretrained weights. Training uses an asymmetric ray loss (following UniK3D) to compensate for limited wide-FOV supervision, a Huber-weighted pose loss, and a camera augmentation strategy that unprojects pinhole depth into 3D and reprojects onto randomly sampled distorted camera models to increase data diversity across projection types.

## Results

On zero-shot camera pose estimation, Wid3R achieves AUC@30 of 79.93 on Stanford2D3D (360°), 74.61 on Zip-NeRF (fisheye), and 68.20 on FIORD (fisheye), surpassing the strongest baseline π³ by +77.87, +33.67, and +19.85 respectively. For point map reconstruction on Stanford2D3D, Wid3R reduces mean accuracy error to 0.197 (vs. 0.380 for π³) and improves normal consistency to 0.729 (vs. 0.557). In monocular 360° depth estimation on Matterport3D, Wid3R achieves δ₁.₂₅ of 0.948, matching or exceeding dedicated single-view methods like Elite360D (0.899) and UniK3D (0.858). For large-scale localization on Matterport3D, Wid3R registers all 37/37, 20/20, and 31/31 frames in ~3 seconds, compared to ~3–30 minutes for SfM-based pipelines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Wid3R decisively outperforms both VGGT [76] and π³ [82] across all three pose estimation benchmarks, with the largest gap on Stanford2D3D (360°) where AUC@30 jumps from 2.06 (π³) to 79.93 (Wid3R), indicating that prior feed-forward methods essentially fail on spherical imagery. For point map reconstruction (Table 5), Wid3R improves mean accuracy on Stanford2D3D from 0.380 (π³) to 0.197 and on Matterport3D from 0.315 to 0.094, while also achieving the best normal consistency (0.729 vs. 0.557). In large-scale localization (Table 4), Wid3R matches or exceeds the SfM-based EDM+IM360 pipeline [33] in registration count (37/37, 20/20, 31/31) while reducing runtime from ~3 minutes to ~3 seconds, though it trails slightly in AUC@3° on Scene 1 (31.64 vs. 49.16). For monocular 360° depth (Table 6), Wid3R (δ₁.₂₅ = 0.948) surpasses all foundation-model-based methods (DAC: 0.773, UniK3D: 0.858, RPG360: 0.859) and approaches dedicated 360° methods trained directly on Matterport3D (Elite360D: 0.899, Depth Anywhere: 0.917), despite being trained in a multi-view setting with mixed camera types. The ablation (Table 7) confirms that the camera model token and multi-camera training data each contribute independently: removing the token degrades Acc. from 0.248 to 0.284, and training only on 360° data (without pinhole/fisheye) yields Acc. of 0.220 with higher variance.

## Relation to Prior Work

Wid3R extends the permutation-equivariant feed-forward reconstruction paradigm of π³ by replacing its point-map head with a decoupled angular (spherical harmonics) and radial module, drawing the ray-based formulation from UniK3D's single-view universal camera estimation. It departs from all prior multi-view feed-forward methods (DUSt3R, VGGT, π³) that assume pinhole or rectified inputs by operating directly in ray space with a trainable camera model token for distortion-aware conditioning. The camera model token mechanism is novel in the multi-view setting, whereas prior work (DAC, UniK3D) applied similar ideas only to single-view depth estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Wid3R is the first multi-frame feed-forward method to handle 360° imagery, opening a path toward calibration-free 3D reconstruction for robotics, AR/VR, and large-scale mapping where fisheye and spherical cameras are standard. The ablation showing that training on diverse camera types (pinhole + fisheye) substantially improves 360° performance despite 360° data comprising <1% of training samples suggests cross-camera geometric transfer is more effective than previously assumed. Key limitations include the absence of dynamic scene handling and the observation that the ray-based module underperforms the simpler π³-based variant on fisheye (FIORD), indicating the angular decomposition may not always be necessary for moderate distortion.
