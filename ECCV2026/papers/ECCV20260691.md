## Problem

Identifying which objects have been added, removed, or moved between two captures of the same scene is critical for robotics, construction monitoring, and disaster response, yet remains extremely challenging under large viewpoint shifts, lighting changes, and occlusions. Prior benchmarks either assume near-duplicate viewpoints, provide only semantic (not instance-level) annotations, or require task-specific training data, leaving a gap for rigorous evaluation of multiview, instance-level change detection across diverse camera trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260691/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two RGB video sequences (or a single image pair) captured before and after scene changes, with no requirement for calibrated cameras, depth sensors, or aligned viewpoints. It outputs per-view 2D segmentation masks for each changed object, a change-type label (Added, Removed, or Moved), and a 3D visualization of the changes. A key structural requirement is cross-view instance consistency: the same physical object detected in multiple frames must be reported as one changed object rather than multiple, which is evaluated via the obj/sc AP metric.

## Method

SceneDiff is a training-free pipeline that co-registers both sequences into a shared 3D space using the π³ feed-forward geometry model, then selects frame pairs with >50% bidirectional co-visibility to ensure comparable perspectives. For each paired view, it computes three region-level change scores: a geometric score from asymmetric depth reprojection (only positive depth differences indicate removed objects, avoiding ambiguity from occlusion), an appearance score from DINOv3 feature reprojection, and a region-matching score from mean-pooled feature comparison that does not depend on geometry. SAM segments each frame into object regions, and the three scores are combined via weighted sum and thresholded with maximum entropy. Detected regions are then associated across frames into consistent object instances using an iterative merging procedure (inspired by ConceptGraph) that combines feature cosine similarity and 3D point-cloud proximity, and change types are assigned by one-to-one greedy matching between pre- and post-sequence objects.

## Results

On the SceneDiff Benchmark (SD-V subset), SceneDiff achieves 39.1 px/im IoU, 43.8 obj/im AP, and 22.8 obj/sc AP, outperforming the strongest baseline (finetuned CYWS-2D at 24.5 obj/im AP) by a large margin. On the more challenging SD-K subset (egocentric kitchen scenes), SceneDiff reaches 20.8 px/im IoU, 20.9 obj/im AP, and 10.6 obj/sc AP. On the established two-view RC-3D benchmark, SceneDiff achieves 65.3 AP50 (Both views), surpassing the best prior method CYWS-3D* (50.0) by 30.6% relative improvement. On PASLCD, SceneDiff attains 47.3 mIoU, edging out MV3DCD (46.1) by 1.2 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneDiff outperforms all seven baselines across every metric on both SceneDiff subsets. Against the strongest pair-wise detector, finetuned CYWS-2D (24.5 obj/im AP on SD-V), SceneDiff leads by 19.3 points in obj/im AP and is the only method reporting obj/sc AP (22.8 vs. no comparable output from CYWS). The 3DGS-based 3DGS-CD [25] and MV3DCD [10] baselines struggle severely with large viewpoint changes, scoring near-zero obj/im AP (1.0 and no box output, respectively) on SD-V. The VLM baseline (Qwen2.5-VL + SAM3) correctly names only 113/369 changed objects in SD-V, revealing bottlenecks in both recognition and localization. On RC-3D, SceneDiff (65.3 AP50) beats CYWS-3D* (50.0) by 15.3 points and CYWS-2D (14.0) by 51.3 points. On PASLCD, SceneDiff (47.3 mIoU) narrowly surpasses MV3DCD (46.1) and substantially outperforms CYWS-2D (27.3) and CSCDNet (12.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing three pretrained foundation models (π³ for geometry, SAM for segmentation, DINOv3 for appearance features) into a training-free object-level change detection pipeline, departing from prior work that either requires task-specific training (CYWS, RC-3D), relies on explicit 3D reconstruction like 3DGS or NeRF (3DGS-CD, MV3DCD), or operates at the pixel/point level. The key conceptual departure is the 'align-then-diff' strategy: co-registering scenes into a shared 3D space first, then comparing at the object region level rather than the pixel level, which the authors argue greatly simplifies matching. The benchmark itself is novel in providing the first multiview, instance-level, cross-trajectory change detection dataset, filling a gap left by prior benchmarks that assume similar viewpoints or offer only semantic (not instance) annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SceneDiff Benchmark addresses a practically important but previously under-benchmarked setting (multiview, instance-level, different camera trajectories) with dense annotations and a scene-level metric (obj/sc AP) that captures whether the same physical object is consistently identified across views. The training-free design means the method automatically benefits from future improvements in foundation models, which is a compelling property for a rapidly evolving field. However, absolute performance remains modest (obj/sc AP of 22.8 on SD-V), and the paper acknowledges inherent ambiguities (e.g., swaps of identical objects), reliance on pose estimation quality, and inability to detect semantic state changes or fine-grained deformations—significant open questions for the field.
