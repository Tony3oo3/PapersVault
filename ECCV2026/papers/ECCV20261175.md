## Problem

Existing Gaussian-based novel view synthesis methods, such as 3DGS, adopt a single-view optimization paradigm where only one camera view supervises each gradient step. This leads to unstable gradient updates for 3D Gaussian kernels, as individual Gaussians may satisfy one view while conflicting with physical constraints visible from other viewpoints, ultimately degrading rendering quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261175/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, initialized from an SfM point cloud, and produces optimized 3D Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, spherical-harmonic color, opacity) for real-time alpha-blended rendering of novel views. A key operational detail is the cross-intrinsic guidance: a 4-layer image pyramid (downsampling factors 1, 2, 4, 8) is constructed by rescaling focal length and principal point, with more views (up to 8× the base count) fed at the coarsest layer to provide holistic multi-view constraints before fine-scale refinement.

## Method

MVGS is a plug-and-play optimizer that replaces single-view supervision with multi-view regulated training, where gradients from M views are summed (not averaged) per iteration, yielding a larger effective learning rate for jointly observed Gaussians and provably reducing gradient variance as M increases. A cross-intrinsic guidance scheme trains from a 4× downsampled pyramid to full resolution, using more views at coarser scales to establish global multi-view consistency before sculpting fine details. To handle minimal overlap among training views, a multi-view cross-ray densification strategy combines (i) multi-view augmented densification, which lowers the split threshold when normalized camera translations indicate large viewpoint discrepancy, and (ii) cross-ray densification, which locates high-loss 2D regions via sliding windows, casts rays from their vertices, computes intersection planes across views to form 3D cuboids, and densifies Gaussians specifically within those multi-view overlapped volumes.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, MVGS integrated into 3D-HGS achieves the best PSNR of 30.21 (vs. 29.66 for vanilla 3D-HGS), while Scaffold-GS(+Ours) reaches 29.82 PSNR and 3DGS(+Ours) reaches 29.61 PSNR. On the more challenging Tanks&Temples dataset, improvements are most pronounced: Scaffold-GS(+Ours) jumps from 23.96 to 25.54 PSNR (+1.58 dB) and 3D-HGS(+Ours) from 24.45 to 25.56 PSNR (+1.11 dB). On Deep Blending, Scaffold-GS(+Ours) achieves 30.37 PSNR with LPIPS of 0.153, substantially outperforming all other methods. Notably, MVGS achieves these gains with fewer 3D Gaussians (e.g., 0.8M vs. 1.1M for 3DGS) at the cost of longer training time (2.4h vs. 0.3h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVGS consistently outperforms all compared baselines across all three datasets. Against the strongest non-MVGS Gaussian method (3D-HGS), MVGS gains +0.55 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360 (30.21 vs. 29.66) and +1.11 dB on Tanks&Temples (25.56 vs. 24.45). Against 3DGS, the improvement is +0.92 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 and +1.30 dB on Tanks&Temples. Against Scaffold-GS, gains are +0.98 dB (Mip-NeRF 360) and +1.58 dB (Tanks&Temples). On Deep Blending, Scaffold-GS(+Ours) leads all methods with 30.37 PSNR and 0.153 LPIPS, surpassing the next best (2DGS at 29.93 PSNR) by 0.44 dB. Against NeRF-based methods (Mip-NeRF 360, Instant-NGP, Plenoxels), MVGS-equipped methods dominate by 1–6 dB PSNR, confirming the advantage of explicit Gaussian representations with multi-view regularization. The largest absolute gains appear on Tanks&Temples, the hardest dataset with texture-less regions and lighting changes, where multi-view constraints most effectively stabilize optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVGS is a plug-and-play optimization layer built directly on top of 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) and its variants (Scaffold-GS, 3D-HGS), departing from the single-view-per-iteration training paradigm that all existing Gaussian-based methods follow. Rather than proposing a new scene representation, it restructures the optimization loop by aggregating gradients from multiple views simultaneously, drawing on image-pyramid-style coarse-to-fine training and extending 3DGS's adaptive densification with multi-view-aware splitting. It contrasts with prior multi-view feature approaches (MVSplat, LatentSplat) that rely on pre-trained networks, instead imposing multi-view consistency directly through the loss and densification pipeline without external models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means any existing Gaussian-based pipeline can adopt MVGS without architectural changes, making it a practical drop-in improvement for the broader 3DGS ecosystem. However, the training cost increase is substantial (e.g., 0.3h → 2.4h for 3DGS on Mip-NeRF 360), which limits its applicability in time-constrained or resource-limited settings. An open question is how the optimal number of multi-view inputs scales with scene complexity and dataset size, since the paper shows performance degrades beyond a moderate view count. The method also assumes access to well-calibrated multi-view images with sufficient overlap, leaving its behavior on sparse or uncalibrated captures unexplored.
