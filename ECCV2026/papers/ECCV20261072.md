## Problem

Existing referring image segmentation models passively process static images from fixed viewpoints, making them unsuitable for Embodied AI agents that must actively explore continuous 360° environments to locate and segment targets. Applying 3D reconstruction is computationally prohibitive, while flattening panoramas introduces severe geometric distortion and wraparound artifacts. The paper introduces Active Panoramic Referring Segmentation (APRS) to bridge this gap, requiring an agent to iteratively adjust its viewing direction to find and segment a language-specified target.

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives a local perspective view (120°×90° FoV at 1024×768 pixels), a natural-language instruction, and the EgoSphere memory map (a 1024×512 equirectangular canvas progressively filled with back-projected observations plus spatial prompts). The agent outputs a discrete viewpoint adjustment (Δθ, Δφ in multiples of 30°) or a [STOP] signal; upon stopping, an active alignment phase re-centers the target and SAM-3 produces the final segmentation mask. Episodes are capped at 20 steps, and success requires both angular proximity to the target centroid and IoU ≥ 0.5.

## Method

PanoSeeker couples a Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct VLM (fine-tuned via LoRA, r=64) with EgoSphere, an explicit spatial-visual memory that incrementally back-projects each local view onto a fixed-resolution 360° equirectangular canvas via inverse gnomonic projection, augmented with a crosshair, 30° lat-long grid, and rendered exploration trajectory to convert search into a visual fill-in-the-blank task. Training proceeds in two stages: Supervised Fine-Tuning on expert-annotated trajectories (minimizing negative log-likelihood of expert actions), followed by GRPO reinforcement learning (G=8, ε=0.2, 6000 steps) with a per-step efficiency reward encouraging geodesic progress toward the target and a terminal reward combining a success bonus with IoU. Once the target is located, an Active Alignment module applies a final angular correction to center the target optically before SAM-3 generates the segmentation mask, mitigating boundary truncation and occlusion effects.

## Results

On the APRS benchmark (7,420 samples, 4,971 scenes), PanoSeeker achieves 75.4% Success Rate (SR), 4.8 Average Steps (AS), 0.57 SPL, and 55.8% mIoU, outperforming all baselines across every metric. Compared to the strongest VLM-agent baseline (GPT-5.2), PanoSeeker improves SR by 6.3 points, reduces AS from 6.2 to 4.8, and boosts SPL from 0.36 to 0.57 (~58% relative gain). Against the best static method (VisionReasoner, 66.2% SR, 47.7% mIoU), PanoSeeker gains 9.2 points in SR and 8.1 points in mIoU, while also enabling active exploration that static methods cannot perform.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PanoSeeker outperforms all 8 baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest VLM-agent baseline GPT-5.2 (69.1% SR, 6.2 AS, 0.36 SPL, 53.2% mIoU), PanoSeeker gains +6.3 SR, −1.4 AS, +0.21 SPL, and +2.6 mIoU, with the SPL improvement (~58% relative) being the most dramatic. The gap widens further against Gemini-3-Flash (64.9% SR, 0.31 SPL) and Qwen3-VL-30B (62.9% SR, 0.29 SPL), confirming that EgoSphere's geometric spatial memory provides a structural advantage over text-log memory regardless of the underlying VLM. Compared to the best static method VisionReasoner (66.2% SR, 47.7% mIoU), PanoSeeker achieves +9.2 SR and +8.1 mIoU, demonstrating that active exploration with spatial reasoning substantially outperforms single-pass panorama processing. Against heuristic scanning (best: VisionReasoner at 49.5% SR, 0.26 SPL), PanoSeeker more than doubles SPL and gains +25.9 SR, showing that learned active search is far more efficient than fixed-order traversal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of referring image segmentation, active perception, and agent memory. It departs from static RIS methods (VLT, CRIS, LISA, SAM4MLLM) by requiring the agent to actively adjust its viewpoint in a continuous 360° environment, and from prior embodied referring tasks (Refer360, REVERIE) by demanding genuine active exploration rather than passive multi-view selection or dense step-by-step instruction following. The EgoSphere memory repurposes the equirectangular panorama as a fixed-resolution spatial canvas—an idea borrowed from panoramic vision—rather than the textual logs or flat visual buffers used in prior agent memory systems (MemGPT, Memory-Bank, VisMem). The training pipeline combines standard SFT on expert trajectories with GRPO-based RL, extending the RL-for-perception trend (Seg-Zero, VisionReasoner) to the active navigation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The APRS benchmark (7,420 samples, 4,971 scenes, four spatial-reasoning difficulty levels) provides a scalable, cost-effective alternative to resource-intensive 3D reconstruction benchmarks like ScanRefer, making active-perception evaluation more accessible. The EgoSphere design—mapping sequential observations onto a constant-size panoramic canvas—offers a clean solution to the context-length bottleneck in long-horizon agent memory, a problem that scales poorly with interaction history in existing systems. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to true 3D environments (beyond simulated panoramic projections), how it performs with longer trajectories or more complex multi-object instructions, and whether the RL-optimized policy transfers across scene types without retraining.
