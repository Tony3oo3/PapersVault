## Problem

Existing 4D occupancy forecasting methods rely on discrete tokenization followed by autoregressive generation, which suffers from geometric distortion in static structures during long-horizon prediction, inconsistent temporal coherence due to causal step-by-step decoding, and a two-stage pipeline that constrains the predictor by the tokenizer's representational capacity. The paper addresses how to jointly ensure geometric consistency of static structures, plausible semantic evolution of dynamic scenes, and cross-frame coherence without these limitations.

## Input / Output

The method takes N_h=4 historical 3D occupancy grids (200×200×16 voxels at 0.4 m resolution, 18 semantic classes, covering [-40 m, -40 m, -1 m] to [40 m, 40 m, 5.4 m]) and corresponding ego-poses (translation + unit quaternion) as input, optionally supplemented by camera images processed through STCOcc to produce occupancy. It outputs N_f=6 future 4D semantic occupancy volumes at the same voxel resolution and simultaneously predicts future relative ego-poses; the decoder also reconstructs the historical segment as an auxiliary supervision signal, producing a unified (N_h+N_f)-frame occupancy volume.

## Method

GAST operates entirely in continuous BEV feature space, eliminating discrete tokenization and autoregressive decoding. The Progressive Explicit-Implicit Generation (PEIG) module first warps the current BEV feature into each future ego-coordinate via differentiable grid sampling with predicted poses (explicit rigid propagation), then applies channel-wise affine modulation conditioned on relative pose deltas through an MLP (implicit motion-aware adaptation), and finally refines local details via deformable cross-attention that grounds future BEV queries in the current observation. The Dual-Path Spatio-Temporal Modeling (DPSTM) module runs two parallel branches: a Global Spatial Context Aggregator transforms all historical and future features into a shared world coordinate and fuses them across three BEV resolutions (50×50, 100×100, 200×200) in a coarse-to-fine hierarchy, while a Temporal Dynamics Extractor propagates context through a 2-layer ConvGRU over the concatenated time sequence. The two branches are fused by channel concatenation and a convolution, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a combined weighted cross-entropy + Lovász-Softmax occupancy loss over both historical reconstruction and future forecasting, plus an L2/quaternion ego-pose regression loss.

## Results

On Occ3D-nuScenes with ground-truth 3D occupancy and ego trajectory, GAST achieves 47.40% mIoU and 56.24% IoU (averaged over 1–3 s), surpassing the previous SOTA I²-World by 7.67% mIoU and 6.44% IoU. With a predicted ego trajectory, GAST reaches 27.38% mIoU and 35.90% IoU, outperforming DFIT-OccWorld by 4.67% mIoU and 3.63% IoU. In long-term forecasting (8 s horizon), GAST attains 24.12% mIoU and 39.06% IoU, exceeding COME by 5.05% mIoU and 9.10% IoU. GAST also achieves a 2.84× speedup over I²-World (80.03 ms vs. 227.09 ms latency) while using fewer parameters (12.09 M vs. 22.67 M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAST outperforms all ten compared baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the closest SOTA, I²-World [16], GAST gains 7.67% mIoU and 6.44% IoU under the GT-trajectory setting (47.40% vs. 39.73% mIoU) and 13.74% mIoU in zero-shot Occ3D-Waymo evaluation (57.47% vs. 43.73%). In the predicted-trajectory setting, GAST leads DFIT-OccWorld [43] by 4.67% mIoU (27.38% vs. 22.71%) and Occ-LLM [34] by 6.39% mIoU. Among diffusion-based methods, GAST surpasses COME [23] by 3.15% mIoU (GT) and DOME [7] by 10.30% mIoU (GT), while also being 2.84× faster than I²-World and 54.6× faster than COME in inference latency. In long-term 8-second forecasting, GAST extends its lead, outperforming COME by 5.05% mIoU and DOME by 8.29% mIoU, with the gap widening at later timestamps (e.g., 15.96% vs. 11.96% mIoU at 8 s vs. COME). The camera-input setting (via STCOcc) shows GAST beating I²-World-STC by 1.19% mIoU and DOME-STC by 5.63% mIoU, confirming robustness across input modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAST departs from the dominant autoregressive tokenization paradigm (OccWorld, I²-World, OccLLaMA) that compresses 3D occupancy into discrete VQ-VAE tokens and generates them sequentially, instead operating entirely in continuous BEV feature space. It inherits the BEV compression strategy from OccWorld but replaces the two-stage tokenize-then-generate pipeline with a unified end-to-end architecture combining explicit pose-driven geometric warping, implicit motion-aware modulation, and deformable cross-attention refinement. The dual-path spatio-temporal module (global spatial aggregation in a shared world coordinate system plus ConvGRU-based temporal dynamics) is a novel architectural choice that contrasts with both autoregressive and diffusion-based (DOME, COME) approaches to cross-frame modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.84× speedup with fewer parameters (12M vs. 22M for I²-World) while achieving 7.67% mIoU improvement makes GAST practically attractive for real-time or near-real-time occupancy forecasting in autonomous driving simulation and planning. The explicit geometric prior (pose-driven warping) provides a clean, interpretable mechanism for preserving static structure that is absent in purely learned autoregressive or diffusion-based methods. Open questions include whether the BEV-based formulation scales to larger spatial extents or more complex multi-agent dynamics, and whether the reliance on accurate ego-pose prediction (or ground-truth poses) limits deployment in highly dynamic or sensor-degraded scenarios.
