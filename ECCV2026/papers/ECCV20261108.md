## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting optimization produces noisy Gaussian primitives because sparse SfM initialization causes SGD to get trapped in local minima. Existing remedies like 3DGS-MCMC inject random SGLD perturbations only on mean positions, leaving the covariance (spatial structure) untouched and suffering from slow convergence near saddle points. The paper identifies that both the mean positions and the spatial support of each primitive must be jointly denoised, not merely repositioned.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images and an SfM point cloud (intentionally denser and noisier on Mip-NeRF 360 to stress-test robustness) and outputs a compact set of 3D anisotropic Gaussian primitives parameterized by means, rotation quaternions, scaling factors, opacities, and spherical-harmonic coefficients. Training runs for 35K iterations on Mip-NeRF 360 and 30K on Tanks and Temples / Deep Blending, and the final representation uses fewer primitives than all compared baselines while achieving the highest NVS quality, demonstrating a quality-compactness trade-off advantage.

## Method

Denoising-GS reformulates 3DGS training as a spatial-aware denoising process over Gaussian primitives. First, a momentum-biased stochastic exploration replaces vanilla SGLD by maintaining an exponentially decayed history of past gradients, so the injected noise follows the optimization flow rather than acting as pure random perturbation. Second, the Spatial Gradient-based Denoising module transforms mean gradients into each primitive's local coordinate frame, detects when the dominant mean-gradient axis aligns with the dominant scaling-gradient axis, and adds a corrective displacement Δμ along that axis to prevent the mean from drifting into the region that the scaling is simultaneously expanding into. Third, an Uncertainty-based Denoising stage approximates per-primitive Fisher Information from the outer product of L2-loss gradients over all views, computes uncertainty as the sum of six singular values (three for mean, three for scaling), and prunes the top 10% most uncertain primitives. Finally, a Spatial Coherence Refinement step computes mean k-NN distances to flag under-represented regions and selectively splits the sparsest 0.05% of primitives (repeated five times) to restore structural completeness without inflating the primitive count.

## Results

Denoising-GS achieves state-of-the-art NVS quality across all three benchmarks: on Mip-NeRF 360, it reaches SSIM 0.897 / PSNR 29.925 / LPIPS 0.142; on Tanks and Temples, SSIM 0.868 / PSNR 24.561 / LPIPS 0.149; and on Deep Blending, SSIM 0.906 / PSNR 29.872 / LPIPS 0.239. Notably, it attains the best LPIPS on all three datasets (e.g., 0.142 on Mip-NeRF 360 vs. 0.149 for Mip-Splatting and 0.186 for 3DGS-MCMC), indicating superior perceptual quality. In terms of representation efficiency, Denoising-GS requires the fewest Gaussian primitives among top-performing methods (e.g., 5.32M on Bicycle vs. 5.90M for 3DGS-MCMC and 7.93M for Mip-Splatting), saving over 10% primitives compared to vanilla 3DGS and 3DGS-MCMC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Denoising-GS outperforms the two direct baselines (vanilla 3DGS and 3DGS-MCMC) on every metric across all three datasets, with the largest gains in LPIPS (e.g., 0.142 vs. 0.186 on Mip-NeRF 360 for 3DGS-MCMC) and PSNR on Tanks and Temples (24.561 vs. 24.290). Against the strongest recent GS-based methods, Denoising-GS leads on Mip-NeRF 360 over Mip-Splatting (PSNR 29.925 vs. 29.456; LPIPS 0.142 vs. 0.149) and Pixel-GS (PSNR 29.925 vs. 29.454), and on Tanks and Temples over all competitors (PSNR 24.561 vs. 23.880 for GS-LPM, the next best). On Deep Blending, Denoising-GS achieves the best SSIM (0.906) and LPIPS (0.239), though Taming 3DGS edges it out on PSNR (29.968 vs. 29.872), suggesting a slight trade-off in that specific scene. In terms of primitive efficiency (Table 2), Denoising-GS uses the fewest Gaussians among top-PSNR methods, saving ≥10% over vanilla 3DGS, 3DGS-MCMC, and GS-LPM, while Mip-Splatting and Pixel-GS require substantially more primitives for comparable or lower quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3DGS-MCMC, which introduced SGLD-based stochastic exploration to escape local minima during Gaussian primitive optimization, by replacing its purely random perturbations with momentum-biased stochastic exploration that preserves the spatial optimization trajectory. The key conceptual departure is reframing the entire 3DGS optimization loop as a 'denoising process' for Gaussian primitives, drawing an analogy to point cloud denoising but extending it to jointly refine both mean positions and covariance (spatial structure). The Spatial Gradient-based Denoising module addresses a specific failure mode—redundant updates when mean translation is counteracted by concurrent scaling along the same axis—that prior gradient-aware methods (Pixel-GS, AbsGS) did not explicitly handle through coordinated mean-scaling updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The denoising perspective provides a principled unifying framework for the densification, pruning, and positional refinement steps that were previously treated as independent heuristics in 3DGS pipelines. Achieving state-of-the-art NVS quality with the fewest primitives (saving 10%+ over 3DGS-MCMC) is practically significant for deployment where memory and rendering speed matter. However, the paper acknowledges a trade-off: pruning in textureless regions (sky, uniform walls) can slightly degrade rendering quality, and the approach has not yet been extended to flattened primitives or mesh extraction, which are important downstream applications.
