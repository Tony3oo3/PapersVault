## Problem

Reconstructing articulated 3D objects from a single image requires jointly inferring geometry, part structure, and motion parameters, but the entanglement between motion cues and object structure makes direct articulation regression unstable. Existing approaches address this through multi-view supervision, retrieval-based assembly from pre-built libraries, or auxiliary video generation and handcrafted motion priors, each sacrificing scalability, efficiency, or generalization. MonoArt tackles this by formulating monocular articulated reconstruction as a progressive structural reasoning process that eliminates reliance on external motion templates or multi-stage pipelines.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a canonical 3D mesh (via a frozen TRELLIS backbone at 64³ sparse voxel resolution) along with per-part articulation parameters: soft part masks over 100,000 sampled surface points, joint type (fixed/revolute/prismatic/continuous), unit joint axis, pivot point, symmetric motion limits, and a single-root kinematic tree. The part-aware reasoning operates on triplane feature maps at 128×128 resolution with 100 dual queries, and a confidence threshold at inference automatically determines the number of articulated components.

## Method

MonoArt is a four-stage progressive pipeline: (1) a frozen TRELLIS 3D generator produces canonical geometry and sparse voxel latents; (2) a Part-Aware Semantic Reasoner projects geometry-aligned point features onto triplanes, refines them with a self-attention transformer, and samples back to point embeddings supervised by triplet loss for discriminative part separation; (3) a Dual-Query Motion Decoder initializes position (spatial anchors) and content (semantic) queries from global object context, then iteratively refines both over 6 layers via self-attention, cross-attention to part features, and CLIP text-prototype fusion, with Hungarian-matched confidence scores to prune invalid parts; (4) a Kinematic Estimator regresses joint parameters from the refined queries (using centroid-based residual prediction for the pivot) and predicts the kinematic tree via a bilinear category-compatibility matrix with single-root, cycle-free constraints. Training follows a four-phase curriculum: warm up the reasoner with triplet loss, train query initialization with category supervision, jointly optimize the decoder and regressor, then train the tree predictor with all preceding modules frozen.

## Results

On the 7-category PartNet-Mobility split, MonoArt achieves an F-Score of 0.728, joint type accuracy of 88.26%, axis error of 0.209 rad, and pivot error of 0.085, substantially outperforming SINGAPO (F-Score 0.572, Type Acc. 77.12%, Pivot Err. 0.201) and URDFormer (F-Score 0.275, Type Acc. 35.22%). On the full 46-category setting, MonoArt attains the best CD (1.25×10⁻²), F-Score (0.670), PSNR (18.55), CLIP similarity (0.907), Type Accuracy (67.47%), and Pivot Error (0.108), though it trails PhysX-Anything on axis direction error (0.423 vs. 0.289 rad). Inference time is 20.5 s per instance, comparable to SINGAPO (19.6 s) and far faster than retrieval-based methods (ArtAny 229.9 s, PhysXAny 256.8 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SINGAPO (7-class setting), MonoArt improves F-Score by 0.156 (0.728 vs. 0.572), Type Accuracy by 11.1 points (88.26% vs. 77.12%), and reduces Pivot Error by 58% (0.085 vs. 0.201), while running at a comparable speed (20.5 s vs. 19.6 s). Against URDFormer, MonoArt dominates on every metric, with F-Score more than doubling (0.728 vs. 0.275) and Type Accuracy rising from 35.22% to 88.26%. In the 46-class setting, MonoArt outperforms PhysX-Anything on CD (1.25 vs. 1.88), F-Score (0.670 vs. 0.531), PSNR (18.55 vs. 17.07), Type Accuracy (67.47% vs. 63.35%), and Pivot Error (0.108 vs. 0.173), but concedes Axis Error (0.423 vs. 0.289 rad). Compared to Articulate-Anything, MonoArt achieves a 40% lower CD (1.25 vs. 2.07), 14.15 points higher Type Accuracy (67.47% vs. 43.32%), and runs 11× faster (20.5 s vs. 229.9 s). PhysXGen is the weakest baseline on both splits, with MonoArt surpassing it on all seven metrics in the 46-class setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

MonoArt builds on TRELLIS as a frozen canonical geometry generator and departs from the dominant paradigms in monocular articulated reconstruction: retrieval-based assembly (SINGAPO, ArtAny), VLM-guided reasoning (PhysX-Anything, ArtAny), and auxiliary video generation (DreamArt). Its key conceptual departure is framing articulation inference as a progressive structural reasoning pipeline—geometry → triplane part embeddings → dual-query motion decoding → kinematic regression—within a single end-to-end architecture, eliminating the need for external motion templates, handcrafted priors, or multi-stage pipelines. The dual-query formulation (disentangling spatial anchors from semantic content) draws on DETR-style query refinement, while the kinematic tree prediction via a learnable bilinear compatibility matrix is a novel structural contribution not seen in prior articulated reconstruction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MonoArt offers a practically important efficiency gain (20.5 s vs. 229–257 s for VLM-based baselines) alongside SOTA accuracy on PartNet-Mobility, making monocular articulated reconstruction viable for real-time or near-real-time applications like robotic manipulation and articulated scene generation. The framework's generalization to IsaacSim-based robot interaction and SAM 3D scene augmentation demonstrates downstream utility beyond benchmark metrics. Open questions remain around handling extreme scale imbalances (tiny parts on large objects) and generalization to novel topologies or uncommon articulation patterns not well-represented in PartNet-Mobility's 46 categories.
