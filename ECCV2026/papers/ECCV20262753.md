## Problem

Current long-video QA systems rely on uniform frame sampling, which inevitably misses sparse query-critical moments while introducing irrelevant context. While agentic search frameworks have been proposed to retrieve relevant clips, their RL post-training depends on outcome-only answer accuracy, providing no verifiable signal for whether the agent actually constructed query-specific visual evidence. This paper addresses the missing verification mechanism that would enable dense, stepwise rewards for video context construction analogous to unit tests in math and code.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long video (pre-segmented into 8-second clips and indexed with ViCLIP video embeddings plus subtitle-to-frame temporal mappings) and a natural language question. The agent produces a multi-turn search trajectory using XML-style tags (think, search, search_subtitle, summary, answer), where each search action returns at most 16 uniformly sampled frames from top-k retrieved clips, and the final output is a text answer. The per-turn 16-frame budget is a hard constraint to fit within the VLM's visual context window.

## Method

VSeek formulates long-video QA as a sequential decision process where a VLM (initialized from Qwen3-VL-4B-Thinking) alternates between reasoning tokens and natural-language search queries against a ViCLIP-based video index and subtitle index. The key contribution is VETL, a neuro-symbolic pipeline that compiles each question-answer pair into a temporal logic specification via an iterative LLM generate-verify loop, extracting atomic visual primitives (objects, actions, relations) and their temporal relationships. These primitives serve as visual 'unit tests': the RL reward R_VETL is the fraction of required primitives detected (via ViCLIP) in the agent's retrieved video context, providing dense stepwise feedback beyond binary answer correctness. Training uses GRPO with a composite reward R = 0.7·R_em + 0.3·R_VETL, where the token mask prevents the model from memorizing retrieved tool outputs, and the temporal operators in the specification are used only to disentangle semantics from ordering rather than to enforce chronological retrieval.

## Results

VSeek-EM (post-trained with GRPO + exact-match reward on Qwen3-VL-4B-Thinking) achieves Pass@1 scores of 60.9 on LongVideoBench, 72.0 on Video-MME, and 67.9 on MLVU, improving over the base model by +9.2, +13.9, and +9.9 percentage points respectively. On the out-of-domain benchmarks, VSeek-EM reaches 38.8 on LVBench and 32.3 on CGBench-mini. The VETL reward variant provides an additional ~1.2 pp gain on MLVU over the exact-match-only variant, and the paper reports up to 8% Pass@1 and 15% Pass@4 improvements over base models across benchmarks. In terms of efficiency, VSeek processes only ~31 frames per query on average versus 64 for static baselines, while VideoTree requires 169–241 frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSeek-EM substantially outperforms all agentic and static baselines on in-domain benchmarks: it beats VideoTree by +15.5 pp on LongVideoBench (60.9 vs 45.4), +15.7 pp on Video-MME (72.0 vs 56.3), and +21.8 pp on MLVU (67.9 vs 46.1). Against the strongest static baseline Qwen3-VL-4B-Thinking, VSeek-EM gains +9.2 to +13.9 pp on Pass@1 across the three ID benchmarks. On out-of-domain benchmarks (LVBench, CGBench-mini), gains are more modest (+3.2 pp and +0.7 pp on LVBench and CGBench-mini respectively), and the authors attribute this to a 'specialization bottleneck' where learned search strings overfit to the in-distribution corpus. VSeek-EM also surpasses the proprietary GPT-5.2 (64-frame) baseline on all three ID benchmarks (e.g., 72.0 vs 67.7 on Video-MME) while using fewer frames (~31 vs 64). The VETL reward variant shows a small but consistent edge over the exact-match-only variant on ID tasks (e.g., +1.2 pp on MLVU), confirming that verified evidence-seeking rewards provide a denser training signal than outcome-only supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

VSeek extends the text-based agentic search paradigm of Search-R1 (think/search/information/answer token protocol) to the video domain, replacing the search engine with a ViCLIP-based visual retriever and subtitle index. The VETL reward mechanism adapts temporal-logic specification generation from prior neuro-symbolic video work (NEUS-QA, Sharan et al.) but repurposes it from post-hoc verification into a dense RL training signal—a use no prior neuro-symbolic video method has attempted. The RL post-training follows GRPO as used in math/code reasoning, but the key departure is that the reward is not outcome-only answer accuracy; instead, it scores whether the agent's retrieved visual context satisfies a formal checklist of atomic primitives, bridging the verification gap that has limited RL for video understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that verifiable, stepwise rewards derived from formal specifications can meaningfully improve agentic video search, offering a template for bringing the 'unit test' philosophy from math/code RL into multimodal domains. However, the VETL reward's advantage over exact-match training is modest (≈1.2% on MLVU) and degrades on out-of-distribution benchmarks, suggesting the learned search strings overfit to in-distribution content. Open questions include whether parallel (fan-out) search, more expressive temporal logic families, and stronger multimodal retrievers can close the OOD gap and scale the approach to hour-long or multi-hour videos without the current sequential bottleneck.
