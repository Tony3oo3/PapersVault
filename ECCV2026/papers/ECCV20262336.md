## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks assess either local recognition or coarse global summarization, leaving a critical gap in whether MLLMs can maintain coherent, temporally grounded entity representations across scene transitions and temporal gaps. Many current tasks can be solved from a single frame or via language priors without genuine temporal reasoning, and heavy reliance on manual annotation limits scalability and diagnostic granularity.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes raw video clips (average 55.3 s, up to 659 s) sampled at 20 frames for open-source models or 128 frames for proprietary models, paired with text questions in binary, multiple-choice, or chronological-ordering formats. The automated pipeline first extracts temporally grounded entity representations—bounding-box trajectories, actions, outfits, and scene contexts per timestamp—then programmatically instantiates CRP-aligned reasoning templates to produce 1,006 QA pairs with real (intra-clip) and synthetic (cross-clip) distractors. Scoring is deterministic exact-match accuracy, eliminating LLM-based judging.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully automated entity-centric pipeline with three stages: (1) ensemble detection combining Detectron2 and OWLv2 via spatial IoU consensus (≥0.5) to reduce missed or duplicate detections; (2) entity tracking using OSNet ReID embeddings clustered into top-4 main characters, refined with face recognition and multi-MLLM majority voting for identity consistency; and (3) contextual recognition where Gemini-2.5-Pro annotates per-segment actions, outfits, and scenes on highlighted entity clips. On top of these representations, the Compositional Reasoning Progression (CRP) framework decomposes narrative reasoning into three escalating dimensions—entity existence (temporal continuity), entity changes (action/outfit/scene transitions), and entity ambiguity (disambiguation among visually similar entities)—with forward, backward, and agnostic temporal reasoning patterns to probe directional bias. QA pairs are generated programmatically from template slots filled with extracted attributes, then linguistically refined by GPT-4o and quality-reviewed by human annotators (70% unanimous validity, Fleiss' κ=0.767). The paper evaluates 20 MLLMs (13 open-source, 7 proprietary) and reveals a fundamental trade-off: general-purpose models show strong perceptual grounding but weak temporal continuity, while video-specialized models capture temporal context yet hallucinate entity attributes.

## Results

On NARRATIVETRACK, Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the highest average accuracy of 83.80%, followed by Gemini-3.1-Pro (83.40%) and GPT-4.1 (74.85%). Among open-source models, Qwen-2.5-VL-32B leads general-purpose MLLMs at 56.96%, while Video-LLaMA2-72B is the best video-specialized model at 49.70%. Performance drops sharply on ordering questions (e.g., Qwen-2.5-VL-32B scores only 19.51% on action-change ordering) and on entity ambiguity, revealing a fundamental trade-off between perceptual grounding and temporal reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NARRATIVETRACK is evaluated against 11 model families spanning open-source general-purpose (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3), open-source video-specialized (Video-LLaMA2, LLaVA-NeXT-Video, LLaVA-Video, Video-LLaVA, VILA, mPLUG-Owl3, VideoChat2), and proprietary MLLMs (Gemini 2.0/2.5/3.x, GPT-4o/4.1). The key finding is that general-purpose MLLMs outperform video-specialized ones: Qwen-2.5-VL-32B (56.96%) beats Video-LLaMA2-72B (49.70%) by 7.26 points, despite the latter being 2.25× larger and explicitly trained for video. All open-source models trail proprietary models by 15–35 points (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro at 83.80% vs. best open-source at 56.96%). Scaling within a family yields inconsistent gains: InternVL3-38B improves over 8B by +6.66%, but LLaVA-NeXT-Video-34B actually underperforms its 7B counterpart (39.36% vs. 42.94%). Proprietary models also show internal variation: Gemini-3.1-Pro (83.40%) slightly trails Gemini-2.5-Pro (83.80%), and GPT-4.1 (74.85%) outperforms GPT-4o (72.27%) by 2.58 points. The paper further shows that increasing frame density beyond 20 frames degrades open-source model performance, and that removing visual input drops GPT-4o accuracy by 30.52%, confirming the benchmark requires genuine multimodal grounding rather than language priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of video understanding benchmarks and cognitive narratology. While prior video QA benchmarks (Video-MME, NExT-QA, TVQA) test local recognition or global summarization, and VELOCITI isolates agent-action binding within short clips, none systematically evaluate whether models maintain coherent entity representations across scene transitions and temporal gaps. The automated pipeline repurposes standard components—multi-object detection (Detectron2, OWLv2), person re-identification (OSNet), and MLLM-based contextual annotation (Gemini-2.5-Pro)—into a novel entity-centric extraction pipeline, but the key conceptual contribution is the Compositional Reasoning Progression framework that decomposes narrative understanding into entity existence, changes, and ambiguity as a structured diagnostic ladder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a fundamental and previously unquantified trade-off: general-purpose MLLMs provide strong perceptual grounding but weak temporal continuity, while video-specialized MLLMs capture temporal dynamics but hallucinate entity contexts. The finding that neither scaling model size nor increasing frame density beyond ~20 frames improves entity tracking challenges the common assumption that more temporal coverage or larger models will close the narrative understanding gap. Open questions include whether bidirectional temporal modeling or entity-centric training objectives can resolve the forward-only directional bias, and whether the framework extends to non-human entities. Limitations include restriction to human subjects, a relatively small clip count (406), and reliance on a proprietary MLLM (Gemini-2.5-Pro) for contextual annotation, which introduces a potential circularity in evaluation.
