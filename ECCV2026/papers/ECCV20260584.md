## Problem

Current VLM evaluations conflate language priors with genuine spatial reasoning, as traditional QA formats allow models to exploit semantic shortcuts for ungrounded correct answers, while existing spatial probes lack 3D metric precision. This makes it impossible to determine whether a model's spatial reasoning is physically anchored in its own visual perception or merely pattern-matching over linguistic co-occurrence statistics. CRISP addresses this by diagnosing the alignment between a model's implicit structural perception and its explicit reasoning outputs.

## Input / Output

The model receives a single-view RGB image (from nuScenes outdoor or ScanNet++ indoor scenes) paired with either a spatial QA question or a list of numerical object IDs for scene graph construction; camera intrinsics are deliberately withheld to test whether models can recover metric scale from world-knowledge priors. For SGC, the model outputs an object-centric star-topology subgraph where nodes carry 3D dimensions and ego-centric distance, and edges combine semantic directional predicates (e.g., front/left) with metric Euclidean distances. The consistency score is computed by symbolically deriving QA answers from the generated graph via a deterministic rule-based solver and comparing them against the model's direct QA response, flagging [FAILED] when the graph lacks sufficient structure.

## Method

CRISP introduces a dual-task diagnostic paradigm coupling discriminative Spatial QA (seven competencies spanning distance, size, direction, counting, ranking, view transformation, and multi-hop logical deduction) with generative 3D Scene Graph Construction, where objects are referenced by unique numerical IDs to prevent semantic leakage and the SGC uses an object-centric star-topology to mitigate generation drift. The core novelty is the Self-Consistency Score: a two-step protocol that first parses the model's SGC into a structured graph and employs a deterministic solver to infer QA answers, then evaluates alignment between derived and direct QA responses, distinguishing true grounding from 'consistent hallucination' where a model is coherently wrong. An intervention-based diagnostic protocol further disentangles perceptual from reasoning deficits by contrasting performance under ground-truth 3D SG injection versus self-predicted graphs, revealing whether models possess robust latent reasoning engines bottlenecked by a perception-reasoning disconnect. The benchmark comprises 1,162 curated scenes (strict 1:1 indoor/outdoor ratio) and 9,839 procedurally generated QA pairs with visibility filtering (score ≥ 0.8) and diversity filtering to eliminate semantic redundancy.

## Results

On the CRISP benchmark (1,162 scenes, 9,839 questions), the best proprietary model Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves 58.93 QA, 64.58 SGC, and 57.28 Consistency, while the best open-source model Qwen3-VL-8B reaches 55.34 QA, 62.35 SGC, and 52.16 Consistency. Gemini-3-Flash attains the highest SGC score (68.79) driven by strong metric estimation (S_est = 65.25) and relation accuracy (S_rel = 72.33). Notably, specialized spatial models (Cambrian-S: 47.13 SGC; VG LLM: 47.23 SGC) underperform generalist baselines like Qwen2.5-VL-7B (42.70 SGC) on consistency (34.22 and 35.29 vs. 35.62), revealing fragmented cross-task alignment despite spatial post-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.5-Pro leads in QA (58.93) and Consistency (57.28), while Gemini-3-Flash dominates SGC (68.79) via superior metric estimation; GPT-5-Mini sits in between with balanced scores (54.15 QA, 66.70 SGC, 56.75 Con.). Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL-8B achieves the best QA (55.34) and Consistency (52.16), with Qwen3-VL-32B marginally ahead on SGC (62.45 vs. 62.35), indicating diminishing returns from scaling. The most striking finding is that both specialized spatial baselines—Cambrian-S (47.13 SGC, 34.22 Con.) and VG LLM (47.23 SGC, 35.29 Con.)—underperform even the weakest generalist model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 42.70 SGC, 35.62 Con.) on consistency, suggesting spatial post-training activates local structural cues without ensuring the reasoning engine leverages them. The blind-test ablation further shows that proprietary models gain 18–32 points on SGC with visual input (genuine perception), whereas Qwen2.5-VL gains only 3.24 points, exposing a semantic-geometric gap where vision aids object identification but not spatial localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRISP builds on the 3D Scene Graph formulation (Armeni et al., ICCV 2019) as a structured spatial representation and adopts QA scoring metrics from VSI-bench (Yang et al., CVPR 2025), but departs fundamentally from prior spatial reasoning benchmarks (SPACE, BSA, 3DSR Bench, OmniSpatial, etc.) that rely solely on discriminative QA. Where existing diagnostic works (Brown et al., Tong et al.) identify semantic shortcuts in VQA, and cognitive-map probes (SIGBench, THEORY OF SPACE) attempt macroscopic process evaluation, CRISP introduces a generative metric bottleneck—forcing models to externalize implicit 3D geometry into a structured graph—and a cross-task consistency metric that no prior benchmark provides. The intervention protocol (feeding GT or predicted SGs back as input) is also novel, borrowing the oracle-guidance idea from structured reasoning literature but applying it to diagnose the perception-reasoning alignment gap specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central finding—that proprietary models possess robust latent reasoning engines bottlenecked by a perception-reasoning disconnect rather than intrinsic logical deficiency—reframes the development roadmap for embodied VLMs, suggesting that structural alignment (not more reasoning depth) is the primary target. The counterintuitive result that specialized spatial models (Cambrian-S, VG LLM) underperform generalist baselines on QA while showing fragmented cross-task consistency raises questions about whether spatial post-training inadvertently encourages shortcuts. Key limitations include the restriction to static single-view scenes (excluding dynamic multi-view reasoning), the acknowledgment that 'Perception-Reasoning Disconnect' is an empirical behavioral syndrome rather than a proven causal mechanism, and the relatively modest dataset size (1,162 scenes). Open questions include whether the consistency protocol generalizes to video/spatiotemporal settings and whether the intervention findings hold under different architectural families.
