## Problem

Extending pre-trained multimodal understanding models with image generation triggers catastrophic forgetting because high-variance generative gradients overwhelm the converged optimization landscape of understanding tasks. Existing remedies like Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) mitigate this via physical parameter isolation but sever cross-modal synergy and introduce parameter overhead. The paper asks whether a unified sparse MoE architecture can resolve routing collapse while enabling generative training to reciprocally enhance understanding, with zero additional parameters.

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts for image generation and text+image pairs for multimodal understanding, producing either a generated RGB image (synthesized via flow matching on VAE latents) or a text response (next-token prediction). It is initialized from the 30B-parameter Hunyuan-A3B VLM and trained on a proprietary corpus mixing T2I, T2I-Long, language modeling, and multimodal understanding data at a 3:3:2:2 ratio. Generation fidelity is evaluated on COCO-30K (FID, CLIPScore, HPSv2) and T2I-CompBench, while understanding is measured on MMLU and OCRBench.

## Method

Symbiotic-MoE re-architects the FFN layer of a sparse MoE Transformer by partitioning 128 experts per layer into 96 understanding experts (Text/ViT tokens) and 32 generation experts (VAE tokens) via hard modality-based routing, while retaining shared experts that process all token types as a cross-modal semantic bridge. A Knowledge-Inherited Initialization strategy slices the original router weights to initialize the two decoupled sub-routers, preserving routing priors and achieving near-zero cold-start degradation. Training employs a Progressive Strategy: differential learning rates (1e-4 for generative components, 1e-6 for understanding/shared components) balance the maturity gap between modules, and a Warmup Gradient Shielding mechanism applies stop-gradient to generative backward passes through shared experts during early training, preventing noisy generative signals from corrupting pre-trained semantics before the generative module stabilizes.

## Results

On COCO-30K, Symbiotic-MoE achieves a FID of 13.65, substantially outperforming Standard MoE (26.27), MoT (19.87), and Bagel (18.42), while also leading on CLIPScore (0.31) and HPSv2 (0.23). On T2I-CompBench, it scores 0.49 overall (Color 0.55, Shape 0.35, Texture 0.56), the best among all methods. Most notably, Symbiotic-MoE boosts understanding beyond the no-generation baseline (Only_LM_MMU): MMLU rises from 0.405 to 0.507 (+20.1%) and OCRBench from 662 to 768 (+13.8%), demonstrating that generative training can reciprocally enhance perception and reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Symbiotic-MoE outperforms all baselines on every reported metric. Against Standard MoE (naive unified co-tuning), it reduces FID from 26.27 to 13.65 and recovers MMLU from 0.308 to 0.507, showing that routing collapse in the naive setup causes both generation failure and catastrophic forgetting. Against MoT (structural 96/32 expert partitioning without shared experts), Symbiotic-MoE improves FID by 31% (19.87→13.65) and T2I-CompBench by 14% (0.43→0.49), while also surpassing MoT's MMLU (0.392) and OCRBench (583) by large margins. Bagel, which uses the same MoT architecture but with independent QKVs, is similarly outperformed on all generation metrics (FID 18.42 vs 13.65) and understanding (MMLU 0.396 vs 0.507). Critically, Symbiotic-MoE exceeds the Only_LM_MMU baseline (trained without any T2I data) on both MMLU (0.507 vs 0.405) and OCRBench (768 vs 662), providing direct evidence that the generative objective acts as a constructive regularizer rather than a zero-sum competitor to understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant structural-isolation paradigm (Mixture-of-Transformers, adapter-based decoupling) that physically segregates generation and understanding parameters to avoid gradient conflict. Instead, it repurposes the native sparse MoE routing mechanism—previously used purely as a capacity-scaling tool—into a task-disentanglement device via Modality-Aware Expert Disentanglement, while retaining shared experts as a cross-modal semantic bridge. The progressive training strategy (differential learning rates, warmup gradient shielding) builds on continual-learning intuitions about protecting pre-trained knowledge but applies them specifically to the MoE routing dynamics that prior unified-multimodal work (Chameleon, Emu3, Transfusion, Show-o, Janus, OmniGen) had not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely held 'seesaw' assumption that generation and understanding are zero-sum competitors, providing mechanistic evidence (shared-expert probing, subtractive ablation, training dynamics) that pixel-level generative training acts as a fine-grained visual regularizer that reciprocally sharpens discriminative perception. The zero-parameter-overhead design within a native MoE backbone makes the approach directly applicable to the dominant architecture family for large LLMs, lowering the barrier to unified omni-modal pre-training. Open questions include whether the 96/32 expert split and the shared-bridge mechanism generalize to larger model scales, additional modalities (video, audio), and whether the observed understanding gains persist after extended training or domain-specific fine-tuning.
