## Problem

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models such as DUSt3R, VGGT, and Depth Anything 3 achieve state-of-the-art performance in a single forward pass, yet their internal mechanisms remain poorly understood. The central question is whether these models internally encode geometric principles like epipolar geometry analogous to classical multi-view pipelines, or whether their capabilities arise predominantly from learned data priors — a distinction that determines their robustness to out-of-distribution scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261715/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The studied models consume sets of RGB images (pairs or multi-view collections) and jointly predict camera intrinsics, extrinsics, dense depth maps, 3D point maps, and 3D point trajectories in one forward pass. For the interpretability analysis, the paper relies on a custom ShapeNet synthetic dataset rendered in Blender with complete geometric ground truth (fundamental matrices, dense correspondences, camera parameters) across controlled focal lengths (24–100 mm) and angular baselines (10°–120°), supplemented by real-world datasets (DTU, ETH3D, MipNeRF360) for out-of-distribution validation.

## Method

The paper performs a three-part mechanistic study: (1) layer-wise probing with two-layer MLPs trained to recover the fundamental matrix from intermediate representations using Sampson distance as the loss, localizing where epipolar geometry is encoded; (2) analysis of QK attention space to identify correspondence-matching patterns in specific heads and layers across views; and (3) attention knockout interventions at four locality levels (whole head, image map, block, patch) to establish causal links between correspondence heads and epipolar geometry encoding. The key finding is a geometric phase transition in middle layers (layers 12–14 for VGGT/DA3, early decoder blocks for DUSt3R) where fundamental matrix recovery coincides with the emergence of point correspondences in attention heads, confirmed causally by targeted interventions. A separate robustness analysis applies input-level perturbations (occlusions, symmetric/repetitive scenes, lighting and camera-configuration changes) to VGGT, comparing it against classical SIFT+8-point and SuperGlue+8-point pipelines to quantify the contribution of learned priors versus geometric reasoning.

## Results

The paper is an interpretability study rather than a method comparison, so headline numbers are qualitative-to-quantitative findings. Probing recovers the fundamental matrix from intermediate layers of all three models (DUSt3R, VGGT, DA3) across ShapeNet, ETH3D, and DTU, with a sharp emergence around layers 12–14 in VGGT/DA3 and early decoder blocks in DUSt3R. Attention knockout on high-correspondence heads in VGGT causes near-complete failure of epipolar geometry (Sampson error spikes), while intervening on random early/late layers has minimal impact; disabling just four heads in a single VGGT layer already produces substantial degradation. Under occlusion, VGGT retains ~50% of matching heads on occluded patches and shows only slight Sampson error increase, whereas SIFT+8-point and SuperGlue+8-point baselines degrade significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares VGGT against two classical/learned correspondence pipelines: SIFT + 8-point algorithm [Lowe, 2004; Hartley, 1997] and SuperGlue + 8-point algorithm [Sarlin, 2020]. Under occlusion (masking 3×3 patch neighborhoods on one view), VGGT shows only a slight increase in Sampson error, preserving epipolar geometry, while both SIFT+8-point and SuperGlue+8-point exhibit clear and substantially larger degradation in geometric accuracy. VGGT also retains approximately 50% of correspondence-matching attention heads on occluded patches, indicating it partially hallucinates missing correspondences rather than discarding them, a behavior neither classical baseline can replicate. In scenes with repetitive/symmetric objects, VGGT remains comparatively robust when subtle lighting cues exist, outperforming both baselines, but all methods fail under fully symmetric configurations where the problem is geometrically underconstrained. The attention-knockout causal analysis (not a baseline comparison per se) further shows that the geometric advantage is mechanistically grounded in specific correspondence-matching attention heads, not merely a data-prior artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interpretability toolkit from NLP (probing classifiers, attention knockout à la Geva et al.) to feed-forward 3D reconstruction transformers, applying it specifically to the question of whether epipolar geometry is internally encoded. Prior work on 3D-aware representations (El Banani et al., An et al., Stary et al.) provided observational evidence that correspondences or 3D structure exist in intermediate features, but did not establish causal responsibility. This paper departs from that observational tradition by performing targeted attention-knockout interventions to show that correspondence-matching heads are causally necessary for fundamental matrix recovery, and by adding a data-prior perspective (occlusions, symmetry ambiguities) that contrasts geometric reasoning with learned appearance priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal identification of specific attention heads as the mechanism linking correspondence matching to epipolar geometry gives practitioners a concrete handle for understanding failure modes and for potential architectural interventions. The finding that VGGT hallucinates ~50% of occluded correspondences rather than gracefully degrading is a practical limitation that matters for safety-critical deployment of feed-forward reconstruction. Open questions include whether the geometric phase transition observed in middle layers generalizes to truly out-of-distribution camera configurations, whether the causal mechanism transfers to newer or differently-architected models, and whether the hallucination behavior can be suppressed without sacrificing the robustness that learned priors provide.
