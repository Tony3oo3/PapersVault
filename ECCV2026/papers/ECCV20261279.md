## Problem

Existing MOT methods apply off-the-shelf ReID features uniformly across all videos, treating association as a general retrieval problem. However, MOT only needs to discriminate among the limited set of targets within a single video, and the generic open-set feature space leaves these targets highly similar and clustered in a confined subspace, reducing their separability. The paper asks whether a sequence-specific representation subspace can be constructed online to better separate the finite set of trajectories in each video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261279/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep, the method consumes the current frame's ReID features (extracted by a frozen backbone such as FastReID on YOLOX detections) together with the historical track assignments and the last T ReID features per established trajectory. It outputs a per-sequence projection matrix that transforms all features into a lower-dimensional discriminative subspace, from which a fused similarity matrix is built for Hungarian matching. The entire transformation is training-free and computed in closed form at every frame, requiring no gradient updates or additional parameters beyond the ReID backbone.

## Method

The core mechanism is a history-aware Fisher Linear Discriminant that treats each established trajectory as a class and uses the last T frames of historical ReID features as the training set, with track IDs serving as pseudo-labels. Within-class and between-class scatter matrices are computed from these features, and the optimal projection is obtained by maximizing the Fisher criterion in closed form. Two task-specific refinements are added: (1) a temporally-weighted trajectory centroid that applies exponential decay (λ₀) so more recent features dominate the class mean, and (2) a knowledge-integration step that linearly fuses cosine similarities from both the original and transformed spaces with a balancing coefficient α (default 0.9), preserving the robustness of the general space for newborn or occluded targets while exploiting the discriminability of the specialized subspace for established tracks.

## Results

On SportsMOT, HAT-FastReID-MOT†* achieves 80.8 HOTA and 73.1 AssA, setting a new state-of-the-art that surpasses all hybrid-based trackers (e.g., DiffMOT at 72.1 HOTA) and end-to-end models (e.g., MOTIP at 72.6 HOTA) using only appearance cues. On DanceTrack, HAT-FastReID-MOT† reaches 61.2 HOTA / 46.0 AssA, outperforming motion-based methods like C-BIoU (60.6 HOTA) and several hybrid trackers. When integrated into hybrid trackers, HAT-OC-SORT-ReID* achieves 82.4 HOTA on SportsMOT and HAT-Hybrid-SORT-ReID reaches 66.9 HOTA on DanceTrack, both representing new SOTA for their respective tracker families.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's ReID-only tracker (HAT-FastReID-MOT†*) decisively outperforms all motion-based baselines on SportsMOT: ByteTrack (62.8→80.8 HOTA, +18.0) and OC-SORT (71.9→80.8, +8.9). Against hybrid trackers, it surpasses DiffMOT (72.1→80.8, +8.7) and BoT-SORT (68.7→80.8, +12.1), and when integrated into OC-SORT, the combined HAT-OC-SORT-ReID* reaches 82.4 HOTA, exceeding the original OC-SORT-ReID (74.1) by 8.3 points. On DanceTrack, HAT-FastReID-MOT† (61.2 HOTA) beats C-BIoU (60.6) and StrongSORT (55.6), and the hybrid variant HAT-Hybrid-SORT-ReID (66.9) surpasses the original Hybrid-SORT-ReID (65.7). Against end-to-end models, the ReID-only method outperforms MOTIP (72.6→80.8 on SportsMOT), SambaMOTR (67.2→61.2 on DanceTrack is a loss, but HAT-OC-SORT-ReID at 64.6 still trails MOTIP's 69.6), and MeMOTR (68.8→78.9 on SportsMOT). On MOT17, gains are modest (+2.0 HOTA) because pedestrian appearance diversity already provides high discriminability, limiting the headroom for sequence-specific transformation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Fisher Linear Discriminant (FLD), a classic supervised dimensionality-reduction technique, for the unsupervised online setting of MOT by using historical track assignments as pseudo-labels. It departs from the dominant practice of applying off-the-shelf ReID features (trained for open-set identity discrimination) uniformly across all videos, arguing instead that MOT is a closed-set problem requiring sequence-specific adaptation. While prior work has customized ReID branches for MOT (e.g., group-wise similarity, condition-aware matching, segmentation-guided extraction), this paper is the first to explicitly frame the open-set-to-closed-set mismatch as the core issue and to use tracking history to dynamically reshape the feature space per video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of the method makes it immediately applicable to any existing ReID-based or hybrid tracker, and the consistent gains across diverse backbones (ResNet, Detic, Grounding DINO, SAM) and datasets demonstrate broad practical utility. A key open question is whether the closed-set adaptation principle extends to end-to-end MOT models, where the feature space is learned jointly rather than fixed. The paper also reveals that gains diminish on datasets where appearance is already highly discriminative (MOT17, TAO), suggesting the method's value is most acute in visually homogeneous scenarios like group dance or team sports. The authors explicitly call for the community to explore more advanced algorithms beyond FLD for sequence-specific feature adaptation.
