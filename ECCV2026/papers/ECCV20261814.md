## Problem

Existing culture-aware co-speech gesture generation methods rarely evaluate under speaker-disjoint splits, so apparent cultural effects may be confounded with speaker-specific gesturing style. Moreover, no prior work learns explicit, speaker-independent cultural representations that can condition a diffusion-based generator to produce culturally appropriate gestures for unseen speakers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261814/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes 5-second aligned windows of audio (64-dim mel-log, onset strength, 1024-dim wav2vec), LaBSE sentence embeddings, a 1-second seed motion (5 VQVAE tokens), and a learned culture embedding. Motion is represented as 9 upper-body 3D keypoints at 15 FPS in 6D continuous format, quantized by a pretrained VQVAE into 25 tokens of 512 dimensions. The output is a 4-second target motion sequence (20 VQVAE tokens) decoded back to 9-keypoint 3D poses, generated in under 14 ms for real-time use.

## Method

SICAGE operates in two stages. First, a feed-forward encoder processes audio and text features (via attention-pooled branches) and projects them into a culture embedding, trained with either Fishr regularization (minimizing gradient variance across 64 randomly sampled speaker domains per step, plus a supervised contrastive loss enforcing speaker invariance) or adversarial learning (a gradient-reversal-layer speaker classifier that strips speaker identity from the shared representation). Second, ALaDiT is a 10-layer hierarchical Transformer diffusion model that denoises VQVAE motion tokens using self-attention on the concatenated seed and noisy target, cross-attention to a temporally aligned low-level audio context (mel-log + onset + wav2vec), and AdaIN conditioning with a high-level context (LaBSE sentence embedding concatenated with the culture embedding). Additional cosine-alignment losses pull the generated motion embedding toward both the audio and text/culture contexts, and a culture-classification head on the output enforces cultural consistency. The key design choice is that culture is learned exclusively from audio and text (never from motion), ensuring the embedding is available at inference and decoupled from the generation target.

## Results

On TED4C-L with speaker-disjoint splits, ALaDiT/FI achieves the lowest Fréchet Gesture Distance (FGD) of 1.03±0.15, substantially outperforming the best DSG+ variant (2.52±0.21) and MDM/FI (7.59±0.59) in motion realism. It also attains the highest Beat Alignment Score (22.63%) and Semantic Relevance Gesture Recall (68.09%) among all ALaDiT variants, while MDM/FI leads on Cultural Expressivity F1 (47.09% vs. 44.61%). The user study (N=20) confirms FI is significantly preferred over ADV (6.06 vs. 5.65, p=0.033) and over NC on Cultural Match (6.16 vs. 5.81, p=0.038).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALaDiT/FI decisively outperforms both baselines on FGD (motion realism): 1.03 vs. 2.52 for the best DSG+ variant (FI+Align) and 7.59 for MDM/FI, indicating substantially closer alignment with real motion distributions. Against MDM, ALaDiT/FI also wins on SRGR (68.09% vs. 51.86%) and Diversity (110.27 vs. 109.37), though MDM/FI achieves a higher CE F1 (47.09% vs. 44.61%), suggesting MDM's simpler architecture may overfit cultural patterns in the motion classifier. Against DSG+, ALaDiT/FI wins on FGD by a wide margin (1.03 vs. 2.52) and on SRGR (68.09% vs. 68.46% for DSG+/FI, a near-tie), while DSG+/FI+Align edges out ALaDiT/FI on BAS (22.67% vs. 22.63%) and Diversity (111.13 vs. 110.27). The DSG+/FI+Align ablation shows that adding explicit multimodal alignment to DSG+ recovers much of the FGD gap (4.89→2.52) but still cannot match ALaDiT, confirming that the architecture's native alignment mechanism is critical. Overall, ALaDiT/FI provides the best trade-off across all five metrics, with the largest margin on the most important quality metric (FGD).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends culture-aware co-speech gesture generation (prior work by Gjaci et al. [19], MCGD [54]) by reframing cultural representation learning as a domain generalization problem where each speaker is a domain. It builds directly on the Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) [50] for the diffusion backbone and repurposes two established domain-generalization techniques—Fishr gradient-variance regularization [46] and gradient-reversal adversarial training [16]—to produce speaker-invariant cultural embeddings. The key conceptual departure from prior culture-aware gesture work is the explicit speaker-disjoint evaluation protocol, which the authors argue is necessary to disentangle genuine group-level cultural patterns from memorized individual gesturing styles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TED4C-L dataset (106 hours, 764 speakers, four cultures with speaker-disjoint splits) fills a critical gap: no prior public dataset of comparable scale supports rigorous cultural generalization evaluation for gesture generation. The finding that motion alone carries a detectable culture signal (45% vs. 25% random) validates the task's feasibility while also revealing substantial intra-cultural variability. Open questions include whether country-level labels are too coarse, whether the 9-keypoint upper-body representation captures enough cultural signal (hands/fingers are excluded), and whether the learned embeddings truly reflect culture or are confounded with language-specific prosody and semantics. The modular SICAGE framework also lowers the barrier for future work to swap in finer-grained cultural annotations or richer motion representations.
