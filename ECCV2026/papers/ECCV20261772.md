## Problem

Continual vision-language learning methods lack a principled, instance-level mechanism that explicitly quantifies how a candidate update would interfere with previously learned competencies and uses that signal to drive both expert selection and capacity expansion. Existing approaches rely on heuristic or distribution-level routing gates and pre-scheduled rank growth, leaving cross-task conflict unmeasured at the sample level and causing overlapping update subspaces that overwrite old skills.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261772/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images, text queries, or image-question pairs through a frozen backbone (CLIP ViT-B/16 for discriminative tasks; LLaVA 1.5-7B for generative VQA) and produces task-appropriate outputs: class labels, free-form text answers, cross-modal embedding vectors, or binary image-text consistency scores. At training time, routing decisions require per-sample probe gradients computed via a backbone-only forward pass; at inference, a lightweight distilled router predicts sparse Top-2 expert gates from frozen-backbone features alone, eliminating gradient computation at deployment.

## Method

IAR executes a per-sample measure-select-grow loop over a bank of LoRA experts. In the measure step, a backbone-only probe gradient is whitened by a diagonal Fisher/NTK metric, and its squared projection energy onto each expert's EMA-stabilized update subspace yields a metric-consistent interference score. The select step routes each sample to the K_r=2 least-interfering experts via a Top-K soft gate augmented with a utilization-aware capacity penalty to prevent expert collapse. The grow step computes the residual of the whitened probe outside the union of all expert subspaces; when the residual ratio exceeds a threshold, LoRA rank is increased by one along the leading residual principal direction (extracted via top-1 SVD of the unwhitened residual), injecting minimal new capacity only where existing experts are insufficient. A cross-coherence packing regularizer simultaneously maximizes principal angles between expert subspaces to maintain geometric separability, and a KL-distilled router replaces the gradient-based oracle at inference.

## Results

On MTIL (11 domains, 16-shot), IAR achieves 73.0 Avg / 70.7 Last / 78.1 Transfer, improving over the strongest baseline LADA by +1.1 Avg and +1.1 Last. On the task-agnostic X-TAIL protocol, IAR reaches 69.8 Avg / 68.0 Last / 81.2 Transfer (+0.9 Last over LADA). For generative VQA (VQA v2), IAR attains 52.4 AP with only 2.3 forgetting, surpassing CL-MoE (51.3 AP, 2.9 F) and SMoLoRA (50.9 AP, 2.5 F). On continual retrieval (CVLR, 5 domains), IAR achieves 33.7 R@1 and 32.6 Last R@1, outperforming C-CLIP (32.5 / 31.1) by +1.2 and +1.5 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IAR outperforms all 14 classification baselines on every metric across MTIL, X-TAIL, and ConStruct-VL. The closest competitor is LADA [32], which IAR surpasses by +1.1 Avg / +1.1 Last on MTIL, +0.8 Avg / +0.9 Last on X-TAIL, and +0.7 macro accuracy on ConStruct-VL. Among MoE-based methods, DDAS [48] and DIKI [39] trail IAR by 2.0 and 1.5 Avg points on MTIL respectively, confirming that interference-aware routing provides a meaningful advantage over distribution-aware or heuristic expert selection. On VQA, IAR beats CL-MoE [13] (the strongest AP baseline) by +1.1 AP on VQA v2 and +0.7 on NExT-QA, while also achieving the lowest forgetting (2.3 vs. 2.9 for CL-MoE, 2.5 for SMoLoRA [43]). On continual retrieval, IAR exceeds C-CLIP [27] by +1.2 R@1 and +1.5 Last R@1, and surpasses DKR [5] by +1.8 R@1, demonstrating consistent gains across discriminative and generative regimes under matched parameter budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads—continual vision-language learning (momentum/geometry preservation in CLIP-style models, MoE-adapter routing in VQA), parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA, and interference-free subspace geometry (InfLoRA, DMNSP)—into a unified instance-level framework. Where prior MoE-adapter methods (DDAS, DIKI, CL-MoE) rely on heuristic or distribution-level gates and pre-scheduled capacity, IAR introduces a Fisher/NTK-whitened projection energy as a per-sample interference signal that simultaneously drives expert selection, subspace packing via principal-angle regularization, and residual-guided LoRA rank growth. The conceptual departure is treating cross-task conflict not as a global regularizer but as a measurable, instance-level quantity that closes a measure-select-grow loop at each training step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IAR provides a principled, task-agnostic mechanism for continual VLM adaptation that avoids large replay buffers and task identifiers, which is practically important for deployed systems facing non-stationary data streams. The empirical validation that interference energy correlates with downstream forgetting and that rank growth is sparse yet concentrated in mid-high layers gives the framework interpretability beyond a black-box router. Open questions include whether the diagonal Fisher approximation suffices for larger backbones (ViT-L/14 sensitivity is only briefly mentioned), how the method interacts with replay or data-selection strategies, and whether the distilled inference router retains fidelity under severe distribution shift. The modest but consistent gains across four heterogeneous task types suggest the design is general, but the absolute improvements (~1 point) leave room for further gains from richer interference models or non-diagonal metrics.
