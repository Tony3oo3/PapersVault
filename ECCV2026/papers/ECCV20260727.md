## Problem

Self-collision in SMPL-based human pose estimation and motion generation produces physically implausible meshes, yet existing post-hoc resolvers either operate in mesh space (incompatible with pose-parameter optimization), rely on heuristic penetration penalties, or learn collision classifiers without enforcing the regularity conditions required by gradient-based constrained solvers. The paper identifies that the Linear Independence Constraint Qualification (LICQ) is the missing theoretical link: without a non-vanishing constraint gradient, standard solvers like SLSQP lack convergence guarantees in pose space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260727/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a self-colliding SMPL pose (pose parameters θ in 6D rotation representation with a fixed shape β) and returns the nearest collision-free pose, ignoring global translation and rotation since self-collision is invariant to them. For motion sequences, the same learned constraint is applied frame-by-frame as a generator-agnostic post-hoc corrector, requiring no access to or retraining of the underlying diffusion or flow-matching model. The constraint margin C_l provides a tunable trade-off between collision resolution success rate and geometric fidelity to the input pose.

## Method

PoseShield learns a scalar neural constraint g(θ) (a 12-layer MLP, hidden dim 512) in SMPL pose space whose superlevel set approximates the collision-free region, trained with a PINNs-style objective combining sign supervision from an exact FCL collision detector and an Eikonal regularization that enforces ‖∇g‖ ≈ 1. The key theoretical insight is that this Eikonal property guarantees LICQ globally, providing convergence guarantees for SLSQP and augmented Lagrangian solvers on the constrained problem min ‖θ − θ₀‖² s.t. g(θ) ≥ C_l. In practice, the gradient loss is replaced by a symmetric Temporal-Difference loss (inspired by offline RL) that avoids second-order derivatives and improves training stability. The learned constraint is then directly reused for motion sequences by summing per-frame soft constraint violations plus a motion-preservation term, enabling collision correction without retraining the motion generator.

## Results

On the newly introduced HwC dataset, PoseShield achieves a 95.8% Success Rate (SCC) and 98.2% Penetration Depth Reduction (PDR), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline COAP (44.6% SCC, 83.2% PDR). On the PROX benchmark, PoseShield reaches 80.0% SCC and 89.3% PDR versus COAP's 56.0% and 77.5%. The Mean Vertex Distance (MVD) on HwC is 0.059, indicating that corrections are made with minimal pose deviation from the original configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoseShield decisively outperforms all three external baselines on the primary metric (SCC). Against COAP, the strongest prior method, PoseShield more than doubles the success rate on HwC (95.8% vs. 44.6%) and improves it by 24 points on PROX (80.0% vs. 56.0%), while also achieving higher PDR on both datasets. Against Torch-mesh-isect, the gap is even more dramatic (95.8% vs. 10.0% SCC on HwC), as its local face-level penetration loss fails to resolve deep self-penetrations. The classifier baseline, which replaces the Eikonal-regularized field with a simple cross-entropy classifier, performs worst (5.6% SCC on HwC), confirming that the learned SDF-like structure is essential for effective gradient-based collision resolution. The only metric where a baseline edges out PoseShield is MVD on PROX, where COAP achieves 0.016 versus PoseShield's 0.021, though the difference is marginal and PoseShield still dominates on resolution quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoseShield sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) neural collision handling for articulated bodies (N-Penetrate, LCollision), which learn differentiable collision signals but without enforcing the regularity conditions needed for robust constrained optimization; (2) volumetric human models (COAP, VolumetricSMPL), which learn occupancy or SDF fields in 3D space rather than in the low-dimensional pose parameterization; and (3) Eikonal-equation-based SDF learning (DeepSDF, PINNs), which the paper transplants from 3D Euclidean space into the high-dimensional SMPL pose space. The key conceptual departure is framing collision resolution as a constrained optimization problem in pose space and using Eikonal regularization to guarantee LICQ for standard solvers like SLSQP, rather than relying on heuristic penalties or 3D-space occupancy queries. The temporal-difference loss variant draws on Ni et al.'s physics-informed TD metric learning, and the motion-sequence extension reuses DNO's conditional synthesis formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generator-agnostic, post-hoc nature of PoseShield makes it a practical drop-in module for any SMPL-based pipeline (pose estimation, motion diffusion, flow matching) without retraining the underlying model, which is a significant practical advantage over methods that bake collision penalties into training. The theoretical link between Eikonal regularization and LICQ provides a principled explanation for why the learned constraint works with standard solvers, and the volume bound (Proposition 1) quantifies the trade-off between training accuracy and solver reliability. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more expressive models (SMPL-X with hands and face), multi-person or scene-level collisions, and whether the per-shape training assumption can be relaxed. The HwC dataset, while useful, is constructed from a single shape parameter, limiting generalization claims across body types.
