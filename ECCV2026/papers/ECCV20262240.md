## Problem

Projection-based 3D VLMs like LLaVA-3D generate thousands of geometry-aware tokens per scene (e.g., 1410), creating prohibitive computational and memory costs for 3D question answering. Existing token compression methods from 2D VLMs rely on attention-based or CLS-based selection that ignores the structured spatial nature of 3D tokens, and purely spatial aggregation (voxelization) cannot resolve object-level redundancy because tokens from the same object's different surfaces remain spatially separated, leading to a long-tailed token distribution across objects.

## Input / Output

The method takes M multi-view RGB-D observations (each view processed by a pretrained image encoder to yield N_2D 2D visual tokens) and a text query. The 2D tokens are back-projected into world coordinates via depth maps and camera poses, then augmented with learnable 3D positional embeddings to form N = M × N_2D geometry-aware 3D tokens. The output is a compressed token set of K tokens (128, 64, or 32) that replaces the full set before the LLM, producing a text answer. The entire compression pipeline is training-free and operates as a plug-and-play module, with voxel size δ=0.2 m and spatial threshold τ_g=5 as the only hyperparameters.

## Method

3DZip is a three-stage training-free compression pipeline that decomposes redundancy into point-level density, object-level duplication, and geometric consistency. Stage 1 applies coarse voxelization (δ=0.2 m) with mean pooling to collapse overlapping multi-view observations at nearly identical 3D locations. Stage 2 selects K anchor tokens via a Determinantal Point Process using a cosine-similarity kernel on L2-normalized voxel features, which explicitly promotes feature-space diversity to balance object coverage rather than merely spreading tokens across space. Stage 3 merges each remaining non-anchor token into its nearest feature-space anchor only when the grid-space distance is within τ_g=5, enriching anchors with complementary context while preserving geometric coherence; tokens violating the spatial constraint are discarded. The key insight, validated by ablation, is that feature-space diversity (not spatial XYZ diversity) is the correct criterion for anchor selection, as spatially distant regions can still contain redundant content (e.g., multiple parts of a large wall), whereas feature diversity directly captures semantically distinct objects.

## Results

On three 3D question answering benchmarks (ScanQA, SQA3D, OpenEQA), 3DZip retains 94.7% of the full LLaVA-3D performance (55.7 EM on SQA3D) using only 128 tokens (53.2 EM), achieving a 1.92× inference speedup (178 ms vs. 342 ms per sample). At 64 tokens, it preserves 92.3% relative performance (52.8 EM on SQA3D), and even at 32 tokens it maintains 88.9% (51.1 EM), consistently outperforming all baselines. The method reduces FLOPs from 9.18T to 0.90T (−90.2%) and KV cache from 722 MB to 101 MB (−86.0%) while incurring only a 2.5-point EM drop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DZip outperforms all five baselines across every benchmark and token budget. Against the 2D-based methods, the gap is most pronounced on spatially demanding tasks: at 128 tokens, 3DZip scores 53.2 EM on SQA3D versus 52.0 (VisPruner, +1.2), 51.5 (VisionZip, +1.7), 50.9 (FastV, +2.3), and 50.3 (SparseVLM, +2.9). Among 3D-aware baselines, DTC (CVPR'25) is the closest competitor at 51.3 EM on SQA3D with 128 tokens, yet 3DZip still leads by +1.9 EM and also surpasses it in practical latency (178 ms vs. 196 ms). The advantage widens under aggressive compression: at 32 tokens, 3DZip achieves 51.1 EM on SQA3D while the best 2D baseline (VisPruner) reaches only 49.1 and DTC reaches 48.8, a margin of +2.0 to +2.3 EM. On OpenEQA, 3DZip's gains are largest in attribute recognition (+5.8 over the second-best at 128 tokens) and object recognition (+6.7 at 32 tokens), confirming that feature-diversity-based selection preserves semantically distinct objects that attention-based and purely spatial methods tend to miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of projection-based 3D VLMs (building on LLaVA-3D's multi-view feature lifting into world coordinates) and the token compression literature developed primarily for 2D VLMs. It departs from 2D compression methods (FastV, SparseVLM, VisionZip, VisPruner) by demonstrating that their attention-based or CLS-based selection criteria fail to preserve the geometric structure needed for 3D spatial reasoning. More directly, it challenges the 3D-aware DTC baseline by showing that spatial-only aggregation (voxelization or XYZ-DPP) cannot resolve object-level token imbalance, and instead repurposes Determinantal Point Processes from the recommendation diversity literature as a feature-space diversity mechanism for 3D token selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reducing 1410 tokens to 128 while retaining 94.7% accuracy and achieving 1.92× speedup makes projection-based 3D VLMs viable for real-time embodied applications that were previously bottlenecked by memory and latency. The empirical finding that feature-space diversity, rather than spatial dispersion, is the critical criterion for balancing object coverage in multi-view 3D scenes offers a reusable design principle for future 3D VLM architectures. Open questions include whether fixed hyperparameters (voxel size δ, spatial threshold τ_g) can be adapted per scene, how the method scales to larger or more cluttered environments, and whether the coarse voxelization step can be made adaptive to preserve small-object detail without sacrificing redundancy reduction.
