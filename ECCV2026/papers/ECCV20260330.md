## Problem

Existing generative embedding methods produce Chain-of-Thought reasoning that is limited to textual analysis of queries and is not aligned with the retrieval of targets, introducing noise and hallucinations. Moreover, jointly optimizing contrastive and next-token prediction objectives causes severe gradient conflicts, while text-only CoT fails to exploit multimodal signals such as visual-spatial cues and video-temporal information, leading to embedding alignment bias on fine-grained and cross-modal retrieval tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260330/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a multimodal query (text, image, video, or interleaved text-image) and first generates a structured Traceability CoT (T-CoT) in a three-part format: <thinking> extracts modality-specific cues (2D bounding boxes for images, keyframes for video, text keywords), <rethink> refines retrieval-relevant reasoning, and <answer> summarizes core information. The T-CoT is concatenated with the original input and recropped bounding-box/keyframe regions to form the Embedder's input sequence ending with a special <emb> token, whose last-layer hidden state is extracted as the final embedding vector. The Reasoner (Qwen3-VL-8B) and Embedder (Qwen3-VL-2B/4B) operate as separate, decoupled components.

## Method

Embed-RL introduces a decoupled two-stage training pipeline: first, the Embedder is contrastively trained with InfoNCE loss on a 1.83M-sample multimodal dataset; second, the frozen Embedder serves as a reward model in an Embedder-Guided Reinforcement Learning (EG-RL) stage that fine-tunes the Reasoner via GRPO. The reward function combines three components: a format reward enforcing T-CoT template compliance, an outcome reward measuring top-k retrieval accuracy weighted by the softmax-normalized similarity margin between positive and hard negatives (computed symmetrically for both query and target directions), and a process reward using an independent VLM discriminator to verify that query and target T-CoT outputs are semantically aligned via listwise selection. The T-CoT explicitly integrates bounding boxes, video keyframes, and text keywords into the reasoning trajectory, enabling the model to focus on retrieval-relevant regions and filter redundant visual elements. This design resolves the gradient conflict between generative and embedding objectives while grounding reasoning in multimodal evidence, yielding state-of-the-art results on MMEB-V2 and UVRB with computationally efficient LoRA-based training.

## Results

On MMEB-V2 (78 tasks across image, video, and visual document), Embed-RL-4B achieves the best overall score of 68.1, outperforming the strongest baseline UME-R1-7B (64.5) by 3.6 points, while Embed-RL-2B scores 66.8. On the UVRB video retrieval benchmark, Embed-RL-4B leads in Coarse-grained (60.7 mAP) and Fine-grained (55.6 mAP) retrieval, and Embed-RL-2B leads in Long-context retrieval (86.9 mAP), both surpassing the best baseline GVE-3B by 4–10 points. Notably, the 2B model already exceeds all 7B-scale baselines, demonstrating strong parameter efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Embed-RL-4B outperforms all 11 baselines on MMEB-V2 overall, with the largest margin over UME-R1-7B (+3.6) and TTEs-2B (+5.0), the two closest generative-reasoning competitors. On the video modality specifically, both Embed-RL models (52.1/53.0) substantially exceed UME-R1-7B (47.5) and CAFe-7B (42.4), confirming that T-CoT's keyframe extraction and temporal localization are critical for video retrieval. On UVRB, Embed-RL-4B beats GVE-3B (the strongest video-retrieval baseline) by 5.5 points on Coarse-grained and 1.5 on Fine-grained, while Embed-RL-2B leads Long-context by 10.5 points over GME-7B. The VisDoc out-of-domain gap is particularly striking: Embed-RL-4B reaches 67.1 OOD versus 42.1 for VLM2Vec-V2-7B, a 25-point improvement indicating strong generalization. The 2B model already surpasses all 7B baselines (GME-7B, VLM2Vec-V2-7B, CAFe-7B, UME-R1-7B), highlighting the efficiency gains from the decoupled EG-RL training paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decoupled Reasoner-Embedder paradigm introduced by TTE [6], which separated generative CoT reasoning from discriminative embedding to avoid gradient conflicts, but left the Reasoner untrained and misaligned with retrieval objectives. It also builds on UME-R1 [22], which first introduced reasoning-driven generative embeddings but suffered from conflicting optimization of contrastive and next-token-prediction losses. The key departure is the Embedder-Guided RL (EG-RL) framework, where a frozen contrastively-trained Embedder serves as a reward model to optimize the Reasoner via GRPO [12], combined with a novel multimodal Traceability CoT (T-CoT) that incorporates bounding boxes, video keyframes, and text keywords—going beyond the text-only CoTs of prior generative embedding methods. The data construction follows the VLM2Vec-V2 [31] paradigm, and the overall architecture is grounded in the Qwen3-VL MLLM family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that targeted RL optimization of reasoning trajectories, guided by a frozen embedding model, can substantially improve cross-modal retrieval quality without the gradient conflicts of joint training or the computational cost of large-model distillation. The 6.6-point performance collapse when T-CoT is removed entirely underscores that structured multimodal evidence in reasoning is not merely decorative but functionally critical for fine-grained matching. Open questions include whether the EG-RL framework generalizes beyond the Qwen3-VL family, how it scales to larger model sizes or longer video contexts, and whether the process reward (VLM discriminator for T-CoT alignment) can be replaced by cheaper proxies. The practical efficiency of achieving 7B-level performance with 2B/4B models makes this approach attractive for deployment-constrained settings.
