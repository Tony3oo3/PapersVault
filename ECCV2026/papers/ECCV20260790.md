## Problem

Despite strong text-image alignment and world knowledge, state-of-the-art T2I models (GPT-Image-1, Qwen-Image, FLUX) still produce visibly artificial images with characteristic AI artifacts such as overly smooth skin and oily facial sheens. Existing RL-based approaches that use human preference scores (PickScore, HPSv2) as rewards do not directly measure photorealism and introduce stylistic biases, while human-curated realism datasets (e.g., FLUX-Krea) are costly and subjective. The paper identifies the lack of an objective, scalable, human-free metric for quantifying image realism as the critical bottleneck.

## Input / Output

The system takes a short user text prompt as input. An LLM component (Qwen3-4B-Instruct) first rewrites and enriches the prompt with detailed scene descriptions, realistic imperfections, and auxiliary cues (e.g., 'shot on iPhone'), which then conditions a FLUX.1-dev diffusion model (with fine-tuned LoRA layers) to produce a photorealistic RGB image. During inference, a portion of prompts bypass the LLM rewrite to maintain generalization across varying prompt lengths.

## Method

RealGen introduces a 'Detector Reward' mechanism inspired by adversarial generation, where synthetic image detectors serve as reward models that the generator must 'escape.' The multi-objective reward combines a semantic detector (Forensic-Chat, an MLLM-based detector analyzing visible artifacts like skin texture and hand anomalies), a feature-level detector (OmniAID, detecting frequency and noise-pattern artifacts), and a Long-CLIP text-alignment score to prevent prompt drift. Training proceeds in two GRPO stages: Stage 1 freezes the diffusion model and optimizes the LLM to produce richer, more effective prompts; Stage 2 freezes the LLM and optimizes the diffusion model using a modified Flow-GRPO framework where full denoising inference is used for reward evaluation while only a short window of consecutive steps is designated for stochastic exploration, resolving the training-inference mismatch that causes noisy intermediate images to mislead detectors. Both stages use batch-normalized advantage fusion across the three reward dimensions.

## Results

On RealBench, RealGen (without LLM prompt rewriting) achieves the best detector-avoidance scores among all open-source models: 70.59 on Forensic-Chat, 37.85 on OmniAID, 31.71 on Effort (held-out), and 67.43 on SIDA (held-out). With the LLM component (Ours*), these improve to 80.84, 47.20, 38.35, and 73.10 respectively. In Arena-Scoring, RealGen achieves a 43.41% win rate against real photographs (50.15% with LLM), the highest among all 13 competing models, and a 74.80% win rate in model-vs-model battles (84.85% with LLM). On the HPDv3 aesthetic benchmark, RealGen scores 10.37 overall, outperforming FLUX-Krea (9.44) and Qwen-Image (7.48) while maintaining photorealism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RealGen outperforms all 13 baselines on the held-out detectors Effort and SIDA by large margins (e.g., 31.71 vs. 24.73 for SRPO on Effort; 67.43 vs. 50.62 for SRPO on SIDA), confirming robustness beyond reward hacking. Against the strongest photorealism-specialized baselines, RealGen beats FLUX-Krea by +13.29 on Effort and +29.97 on SIDA, and beats SRPO by +6.98 on Effort and +16.81 on SIDA. In Arena-Scoring vs. real images, RealGen (43.41%) surpasses the next-best Nano-Banana (42.17%) and SRPO (40.70%), while 8 of 13 competitors fall below 30%. The only metric where a baseline leads is Forensic-Chat, where GPT-Image-1 scores 75.63 vs. RealGen's 70.59 (though Ours* with LLM reaches 80.84). On the HPDv3 aesthetic benchmark, RealGen (10.37) outperforms FLUX-Krea (9.44), Qwen-Image (7.48), and Nano-Banana (9.57), while FLUX.1-Dev (10.60) and Kolors (10.71) score slightly higher but are noted to have been exposed to HPSv3 training data, inflating their scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

RealGen builds directly on Flow-GRPO for the diffusion-model RL stage but makes a key conceptual departure: replacing human-preference reward models (PickScore, HPSv2) with synthetic-image detectors (Forensic-Chat, OmniAID) as the optimization signal, effectively transplanting the adversarial 'generator-vs-discriminator' dynamic from GANs into the RL post-training era. The LLM prompt-rewriting component connects to the prompt-engineering literature (BeautifulPrompt, PromptEnhancer) but is jointly optimized with the generator via GRPO rather than used as a static preprocessing step. Unlike prior detector-feedback methods that apply post-hoc inpainting or local correction, RealGen uses detector scores as a global reward to shape the entire denoising trajectory, and unlike FLUX-Krea's reliance on large-scale human-curated data, it requires no human preference annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'Detection for Generation' paradigm offers a scalable, human-free alternative to preference-based RL for photorealism, which is practically important as preference models introduce color/style biases and conflate aesthetics with realism. RealBench's dual protocol (Detector-Scoring + Arena-Scoring with real-image baselines) addresses a genuine gap in automated photorealism evaluation, and the 75–87% human-alignment validation strengthens confidence. However, a key open question is whether the generator truly learns robust realism or merely learns to evade specific detector architectures; the held-out detectors (SIDA, Effort) partially mitigate this concern but do not eliminate the adversarial-game risk. It remains unclear whether this approach generalizes beyond photorealism to other quality dimensions (e.g., physical consistency, material fidelity) or whether the detector-reward signal degrades as detectors themselves improve.
