## Problem

Pose-based video anomaly detection methods are inherently human-centric and produce no anomaly signal for non-human objects, yet they already run detection and tracking pipelines that generate per-instance bounding-box trajectories for every detected class. These trajectories encode rich kinematic information (translation, scale, aspect-ratio dynamics) but have been treated merely as bookkeeping for identity association, leaving multi-class bounding-box trajectories as a largely unexplored primary anomaly modality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262679/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw video and internally runs YOLOX detection with ByteTrack multi-class tracking to produce per-instance bounding-box trajectories, which are Kalman-smoothed, segmented into overlapping windows of T frames (12–24 depending on dataset), and encoded as 27-dimensional feature vectors per frame. Output is a per-frame anomaly score computed as the segment-normalized negative log-likelihood under a learned normal distribution. The trajectory-only variant (TrajVAD-T) requires no pose estimation; the pose variant (TrajVAD-P) additionally ingests 17-keypoint AlphaPose estimates but only activates them for person-class tracks with sufficient keypoint confidence.

## Method

TrajVAD models the distribution of normal multi-class bounding-box trajectory segments using a normalizing flow (ActNorm followed by K alternating affine coupling layers with causal 1-D convolutions at increasing dilation), trained solely on normal data to minimize negative log-likelihood. Each trajectory segment is represented by 27 features spanning state, temporal dynamics (velocity, acceleration, jerk, curvature), geometric dynamics, pseudo-physical proxies, perspective-normalized quantities, and detector confidence, with a learnable class embedding concatenated at every timestep to capture category-specific normality. TrajVAD-T scores anomalies purely from this trajectory flow. TrajVAD-P adds a second normalizing flow for 17-keypoint poses whose coupling layers are conditioned on the trajectory latent, and whose contribution is scaled by a reliability gate g = g_cls · g_valid · q̄ (class is person, pose is valid, mean keypoint confidence), so that unreliable or non-person poses are seamlessly excluded and the model reduces exactly to TrajVAD-T when g = 0.

## Results

On ShanghaiTech, TrajVAD-P achieves the best AUROC of 88.6 and AP of 90.9, surpassing all compared pose-based and trajectory-based methods. TrajVAD-T, which requires no pose estimation, reaches 84.9 AUROC and 87.7 AP, exceeding STG-NF and SeeKer by 10.1 and 7.7 AP points respectively. On MSAD-HR, TrajVAD-T achieves 69.7 AUROC, outperforming SeeKer by 8.6 points and STG-NF by 14.0 points—the largest gain across all three benchmarks. On UBnormal, TrajVAD-P reaches 73.8 AUROC but trails SeeKer (77.9) by 4.1 points due to noisy synthetic detector confidence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ShanghaiTech, TrajVAD-P (88.6 AUROC, 90.9 AP) outperforms the strongest pose-based baselines STG-NF (85.9, 77.6) and SeeKer (85.5, 80.0) by 2.7 AUROC and 10.9 AP points, and 3.1 AUROC and 10.9 AP points respectively. TrajVAD-T, without any pose estimation, still exceeds both on AP (87.7 vs. 77.6 and 80.0) while trailing by only 1.0 AUROC points versus STG-NF. On MSAD-HR, the multi-class trajectory advantage is most pronounced: TrajVAD-T (69.7 AUROC) beats SeeKer (61.1) by 8.6 points and STG-NF (55.7) by 14.0 points, because pose-based methods produce no signal for non-human anomalous objects like vehicles in traffic accidents. On UBnormal, TrajVAD-P (73.8 AUROC) loses to SeeKer (77.9) by 4.1 points, attributed to unstable detector confidence in synthetic imagery that degrades the trajectory representation. On full MSAD, TrajVAD-T (57.2 AUROC) leads STG-NF (53.8) but all methods drop sharply because categories like Explosion and Fire yield no trackable objects. In terms of efficiency, TrajVAD-T runs at 107.2 ms/segment versus 145.6 ms for STG-NF and 2,993.8 ms for MoCoDAD, saving 31 ms/frame by eliminating pose estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes bounding-box trajectories—already produced as a byproduct of the detection-and-tracking pipeline that pose-based VAD methods (STG-NF, SeeKer, MoCoDAD) execute—as the primary anomaly signal, rather than treating them as mere identity-association bookkeeping. Architecturally, it adapts the normalizing-flow scoring framework introduced by STG-NF for skeleton sequences to a 27-dimensional trajectory feature vector (position, velocity, acceleration, box geometry, perspective-normalized quantities, detector confidence), and extends prior trajectory-based VAD work (TrajREC, TSGAD, BiPOCO) from center-point paths to full multi-class bounding-box kinematics. The reliability-gated pose branch in TrajVAD-P conditions a second flow on the trajectory latent, making pose an optional refinement rather than a required input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is twofold: eliminating the pose-estimation stage saves ~31 ms/frame and removes a failure mode (skeleton corruption under occlusion or far-field views), while multi-class trajectory coverage extends anomaly detection to non-human objects (vehicles, industrial equipment) that pose pipelines simply cannot score. Key limitations remain: performance is tightly coupled to upstream detector quality (UBnormal's noisy synthetic detections degrade results), and events producing no trackable foreground (explosions, fire) are entirely out of scope, as quantified by the 12.5-point AUROC gap between MSAD-HR and full MSAD. Open questions include whether open-world or class-agnostic detectors can close the coverage gap, and whether richer trajectory features (e.g., inter-object relational dynamics) could further narrow the remaining AUROC gap to pose-based methods on human-centric benchmarks.
