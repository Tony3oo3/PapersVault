## Problem

Most prototype-based interpretable networks learn flat or independently learned prototype sets at a single abstraction level, failing to capture the hierarchical part-whole structure that underlies human visual perception. Even multi-level extensions do not explicitly learn compositional prototype-to-prototype dependencies across abstraction levels, and existing evaluation metrics rely on part annotations and assess only single-layer grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261205/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard RGB image and extracts feature maps at three backbone depths (approximately 1/4, 1/16, and 1/32 of input resolution) to support low-, mid-, and high-level prototype matching. Beyond the final class label, it produces a three-tier explanation: texture-level prototype patches (5×5 kernels), part-level patches (3×3 kernels), and object-level patches (5×5 kernels), each with spatial activation heatmaps that exhibit a nested containment structure (low ⊂ mid ⊂ high) across abstraction levels.

## Method

ProtoMappingNet organizes learnable prototypes into three abstraction levels and introduces bidirectional cross-layer prototype mappings—bottom-up projections (low→mid, mid→high) and top-down projections (high→mid, mid→low)—implemented as small convolutional projection heads that align prototype representations between adjacent levels. Cross-layer cluster and separation losses enforce class-consistent alignment: projected prototypes are encouraged to match same-class prototypes in the target level while being separated from different-class ones. Prototype matching uses cosine similarity (rather than ProtoPNet's Euclidean distance) for scale invariance across multi-level feature spaces, and intra-layer orthogonality losses (from TesNet) promote diversity within each class. Training follows a four-stage schedule (warm-up, joint, push, fine-tune) where the push stage snaps prototypes to actual feature patches for interpretability, and the fine-tune stage optimizes classifiers and mapping weights jointly. The paper further introduces two annotation-free evaluation metrics—Hierarchical Relational Consistency (HRC) and Hierarchical Spatial Stability (HSS)—that measure whether learned cross-level mappings agree with inference-time activations and whether mapped activation regions exhibit spatial containment, respectively.

## Results

On CUB-200-2011 with ResNet-50 (iNaturalist-pretrained), ProtoMappingNet achieves 86.5±0.2% top-1 accuracy, competitive with the strongest baselines (EvalProtoPNet 87.1%, Deformable ProtoPNet 86.4%). On ConvNeXt-Tiny, it reaches 86.1% on CUB-200-2011 and 92.4% on Stanford Cars, outperforming PIPNet (84.3%/88.2%) and LucidPPN (81.5%/91.6%). On PartImageNet with ConvNeXt-Tiny, it achieves 86.2±0.4%, surpassing PIPNet (85.0%) and LucidPPN (84.1%). The hierarchical relational metrics (HRC, HSS) show that bidirectional cross-level mappings improve overall HRC from 0.17% (no mapping) to 18.27% and overall HSS from 34.72% to 65.36%, while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CUB-200-2011 with ResNet-50, ProtoMappingNet (86.5%) is within 0.6% of the best baseline EvalProtoPNet (87.1%) and slightly above Deformable ProtoPNet (86.4%), ProtoPool (85.5%), and ProtoArgNet (85.4%), while clearly outperforming ProtoTree (82.2%), PIPNet (82.0%), MCPNet (80.2%), and LucidPPN (75.5%). On ConvNeXt-Tiny, ProtoMappingNet achieves the best accuracy across all three datasets: 86.1% on CUB (vs. PIPNet 84.3%, MCPNet 83.5%, LucidPPN 81.5%), 92.4% on Stanford Cars (vs. LucidPPN 91.6%, PIPNet 88.2%), and 86.2% on PartImageNet (vs. PIPNet 85.0%, LucidPPN 84.1%). In the Transformer extension (Table 4), ProtoMappingNet with Swin-T outperforms ProtoViT with DeiT-S on CUB (87.19% vs. 85.37%) but trails on Stanford Cars (90.97% vs. 91.84%). The ablation in Table 3 confirms that the bidirectional mapping design yields the highest overall HRC (18.27%) and HSS (65.36%) compared to bottom-up-only (12.92%/54.41%) and top-down-only (5.28%/46.24%) variants, while all variants maintain comparable accuracy (~86.5–86.8%), demonstrating that the structural gains come at negligible accuracy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtoMappingNet extends the ProtoPNet paradigm (cluster/separation losses, multi-stage training) and TesNet's orthogonality constraint into a three-level hierarchical architecture, but its core departure is the introduction of learnable bidirectional cross-layer prototype mappings that explicitly enforce parent-child compositional dependencies between texture-, part-, and object-level prototypes. Prior multi-level prototype methods (ProtoTree, HPnet, MCPNet, FPN-IAIA-BL) either anchor prototypes to a single feature level, rely on predefined class taxonomies, or learn multi-scale prototypes without explicit prototype-to-prototype relational constraints. Post-hoc approaches like VCC recover cross-layer concept connections but do not integrate them into the predictive computation, limiting faithfulness. The paper also draws on cognitive science (part-whole perception, GLOM) to motivate the hierarchical structure, positioning itself at the intersection of interpretable deep learning and structured visual reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in prototype-based interpretability: most existing models learn prototypes independently at each level, leaving no explicit mechanism to verify that the learned hierarchy is structurally coherent. The annotation-free HRC and HSS metrics provide a practical way to evaluate cross-level relational and spatial consistency without part annotations, which is valuable for the broader interpretable-ML community. However, the absolute HRC scores remain low (≈18% overall), suggesting that enforcing true compositional consistency across abstraction levels is still an open challenge. The authors note future work on sharing lower-level prototypes across classes for efficiency, and the method's extension to Transformers (Swin-T) hints at broader applicability, though the primary evidence is concentrated on fine-grained benchmarks.
