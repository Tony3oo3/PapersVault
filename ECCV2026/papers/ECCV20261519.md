## Problem

DETR-based video temporal grounding methods inherit rigid tIoU-based one-to-one Hungarian matching from 2D object detection, which fundamentally conflicts with the temporal fluidity of video events. A semantically correct sub-span (moment) that covers only part of the annotated event is incorrectly labeled as a negative, causing repeated penalization of valid semantic predictions during training. This 'forced semantic suppression' prevents the model from learning multi-granularity temporal representations and ultimately degrades grounding accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an untrimmed video (processed via a frozen InternVideo2 or CLIP+SlowFast backbone into per-clip features) and a natural language prompt (token-level text features), both projected into a shared 256-dim hidden space. At inference, only the event head's final-layer predictions are output as a set of temporal spans (start/end timestamps) with no NMS or other post-processing; the moment head and moment-query mining are strictly training-time mechanisms that add negligible inference cost. The architecture operates on a 5-level temporal feature pyramid with a 4-layer deformable transformer decoder.

## Method

ME-DETR introduces a dual-head architecture with role-specialized supervision: an event head (classification + boundary regression) for precise localization and a moment head (classification only) for semantic exploration. Queries are initialized with pyramid-shaped multi-scale span priors, then refined by a standard transformer decoder. During training, one-to-one Hungarian matching is performed exclusively on the event head to designate primary event queries; from the remaining unmatched queries, auxiliary moment queries are mined by selecting those with high temporal containment (≥0.7) within a matched event span and high moment-head confidence (≥0.3), up to K=2 per event. The critical distinction from one-to-many DETR variants is that moment queries receive classification-only supervision in the moment head and are explicitly exempt from regression loss, allowing them to learn semantically rich sub-events without conflicting boundary signals. At inference, the model reduces to a standard NMS-free DETR outputting event-head predictions, preserving end-to-end simplicity.

## Results

On the QVHighlights test set, ME-DETR achieves 53.87% Avg. mAP with the InternVideo backbone (1-layer features), improving +1.87% over the strongest 1-layer baseline Flash-VTG (52.00%) and +0.56% over the 4-layer SDST (53.31%). With the CLIP & SlowFast backbone, ME-DETR reaches 50.02% Avg. mAP, a +2.43% gain over Flash-VTG (47.59%). On Charades-STA, ME-DETR attains 61.9% mIoU (InternVideo), surpassing SDST (61.2%), and on TACoS it reaches 43.0% mIoU, outperforming SDST (42.2%). All results are NMS-free at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ME-DETR outperforms all 15 baselines on the QVHighlights test set under both backbone settings. With CLIP & SlowFast (1-layer), it leads Flash-VTG by +2.43% Avg. mAP (50.02 vs. 47.59), TD-DETR by +3.33%, and the 4-layer R²-Tuning by +3.85%. With InternVideo (1-layer), it surpasses Flash-VTG by +1.87% (53.87 vs. 52.00), TD-DETR by +2.44%, and the 4-layer SDST by +0.56%. On Charades-STA, ME-DETR achieves 61.9% mIoU (InternVideo), beating SDST (61.2%) and BAM-DETR (52.3% with Clip&SlowFast); on TACoS it reaches 43.0% mIoU, exceeding SDST (42.2%) and BAM-DETR (39.3%). The gap is most pronounced on the strict mAP@0.75 metric (55.52 vs. 53.85 for Flash-VTG with InternVideo), confirming that the moment-event decoupling specifically improves precise boundary localization rather than coarse retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

ME-DETR builds directly on the DETR-style set prediction paradigm (Carion et al., ECCV 2020) and the Moment-DETR baseline (Lei et al., NeurIPS 2021), which established learnable queries with one-to-one Hungarian matching for NMS-free video temporal grounding. The key conceptual departure is identifying that this rigid tIoU-based matching, inherited from 2D object detection, is fundamentally mismatched for temporal events due to 'temporal fluidity'—where semantically correct sub-spans are incorrectly penalized as negatives. The paper also explicitly contrasts with one-to-many DETR variants (Group DETR, H-DETR, MS-DETR) that assign multiple queries to the same GT for regression acceleration, arguing that their shared regression objective is inappropriate for the part-whole structure of events; ME-DETR instead uses auxiliary queries for classification-only semantic supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a previously unacknowledged training conflict in DETR-based VTG—forced semantic suppression of valid sub-event predictions—that plausibly caps the representation quality of all prior end-to-end VTG models. The practical impact is a consistent SOTA across three benchmarks and two backbones without any inference-time overhead, making it a drop-in improvement for existing DETR-VTG pipelines. Open questions include whether the pyramid query initialization contributes independently of the moment-event supervision, whether the approach extends to multi-event or streaming settings, and whether the insight generalizes to other 1D temporal tasks (e.g., audio event detection, time-series anomaly localization).
