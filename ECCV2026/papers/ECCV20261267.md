## Problem

mmWave radar–IMU extrinsic calibration is fundamentally harder than camera- or LiDAR-based calibration because radar returns are sparse, noisy, and carry only coarse azimuthal resolution (10°–30°), making point-level geometric correspondences unreliable. Existing targetless methods either require restrictive motion assumptions (constant velocity, static scenes) or depend on dedicated calibration targets, and under sparse reflections the resulting optimization on the calibration manifold frequently diverges or produces ill-conditioned solutions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized raw Radar ADC (IF) samples from a TI AWR1843BOOST FMCW MIMO radar and IMU linear-acceleration/angular-velocity streams from a MicroStrain 3DM-GX5-45, both acquired at ROS2 timestamps. It outputs the full SE(3) extrinsic transform T_I^R = [R_I^R | t_I^R] (rotation in SO(3), translation in R³) together with a temporal offset τ, estimated over 50-second calibration windows. The radar detections are first range-compressed and Doppler-binned into complex snapshots per receive element before entering the spectral pipeline, and the pose-consistency residual is evaluated only over short sliding windows of a few frames to suppress integration drift.

## Method

RadarCalib's central mechanism is a temporal synthetic aperture (TSA) model: as the sensor rotates, successive radar frames are coherently integrated along a virtual aperture whose span equals the accumulated ego-rotation, sharpening azimuthal resolution beyond the physical MIMO array limit. The key calibration signal is a rotation-dependent azimuthal shift in the TSA-enhanced spectral energy profile, which is scene-agnostic; rather than regressing fragile frame-to-frame angular changes, the method exploits a cycle-invariance property (the azimuthal spectrum repeats after a full 2π rotation) and constructs a soft reference profile via a Gaussian-weighted aggregation of frames near one full turn, yielding a differentiable spectral residual that directly substitutes the unobservable kinematic angular-velocity term. This rotation anchor is coupled with two additional SE(3) residuals—a Doppler-based velocity consistency term that activates the lever-arm cross-product ω×t to make translation observable under rotational excitation, and a short-horizon pose consistency term enforcing local trajectory agreement between radar-reconstructed and IMU-preintegrated motion. All three residuals are jointly minimized in Ceres Solver using minimal axis-angle perturbations on SO(3) in a two-stage schedule (rotation-only refinement followed by full 6-DoF refinement), converging in ~45 iterations from a coarse initialization.

## Results

On the self-collected RadarCalib dataset (TI AWR1843BOOST + MicroStrain 3DM-GX5-45), RadarCalib achieves a median rotation error of ~0.25° and median translation error of ~0.17 cm across four indoor/outdoor scenes and two IMU placements, compared to ~0.85° and ~0.55 cm for iKalibr. The method converges in 45 Ceres iterations (20.25 s total), 2.4× faster than iKalibr's 120 iterations (48.00 s). In downstream Radar-IMU SLAM, RadarCalib reduces ATE-RMSE by up to 20% (e.g., 0.12 m vs. 0.18 m in the Lab scene) and narrows the 3 dB azimuthal mainlobe by 20%, resolving adjacent targets 2°–3° closer than single-frame baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is iKalibr [8], a unified targetless spatiotemporal calibrator that relies on point-based geometric correspondences. RadarCalib dominates iKalibr on every rotation and translation axis across all four scenes and both IMU placements: median rotation error drops from ~0.85° (iKalibr) to ~0.25° (RadarCalib), and median translation error drops from ~0.55 cm to ~0.17 cm. The gap is largest in the indoor Hall and outdoor scenes where sparse Radar returns make point-based correspondences most ambiguous (e.g., yaw error 0.22° vs. 0.95° in Hall P.1). In downstream Radar-IMU SLAM, RadarCalib reduces ATE-RMSE by 17–20% (0.12–0.20 m vs. 0.18–0.28 m) and RPE-RMSE by ~25% (0.08–0.12° vs. 0.12–0.20°). Computationally, RadarCalib converges in 45 iterations (20.25 s) versus 120 iterations (48.00 s) for iKalibr, a 2.4× speedup attributable to the stronger TSA-based rotational constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant point-correspondence paradigm in radar-IMU calibration (exemplified by iKalibr and earlier target-based methods) by shifting the calibration signal from the spatial point domain to the temporal spectral domain. It repurposes the temporal synthetic aperture concept from SAR imaging—not to reconstruct reflectivity, but to extract a rotation-dependent azimuthal shift that serves as a scene-agnostic orientation constraint on SO(3). The cycle-invariance formulation (matching spectral profiles separated by ~2π of accumulated rotation) is a novel regularization that has no direct analogue in prior radar calibration or hand-eye calibration literature, though it draws on the broader manifold-optimization framework shared with iKalibr and continuous-time calibration methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

mmWave radar is becoming a critical sensor for autonomous driving and robotics, yet its inherently coarse angular resolution (10°–30°) has made targetless extrinsic calibration a persistent bottleneck; RadarCalib's sub-degree accuracy without calibration targets or restrictive motion patterns addresses a practical deployment gap. Key limitations include the requirement for sufficient rotational excitation (the TSA and cycle-invariance both depend on accumulated rotation), evaluation on a single radar model (TI AWR1843BOOST) with only one external baseline, and a self-collected dataset of modest scale. Open questions include whether the spectral-domain approach extends to 4D imaging radars with elevation diversity, multi-radar arrays, or purely translational motion regimes where the synthetic aperture degenerates.
