## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks over-rely on visual heuristics while marginalizing auditory cues, effectively reducing MLLMs to 'silent observers' that bypass genuine cross-modal reasoning. Dense frame sampling for long videos further creates an evidence-context trade-off where capturing more evidence causes attention distraction and token explosion, and traditional answer-accuracy metrics cannot distinguish genuine multimodal reasoning from visually-biased hallucinations.

## Input / Output

The method ingests dense visual frames from long-form videos (600–7500 s), a continuous audio stream, and a text query, producing a fixed-budget compressed sequence of 8192 visual tokens and 2048 audio tokens for downstream LLM autoregressive decoding. The benchmark additionally requires models to output precise spatio-temporal timestamps grounding each claim in both visual and auditory channels, not merely a multiple-choice label. Unlike ratio-based compression methods, the framework operates on a strict absolute token budget, enabling it to scale to arbitrary frame counts without memory bottlenecks.

## Method

The Audio-Text Guided Token Compression framework fuses two complementary scoring streams: a lightweight text-guided selector (Qwen2.5 0.5B) distilled from Qwen2.5-VL 72B via Spearman rank-correlation loss to predict cross-modal attention relevance, and a training-free audio-guided scorer that identifies salient audio anchors through self-attention in the audio encoder and propagates their saliency to visual tokens via max cosine similarity. The fused importance scores drive a Masked Differentiated Compression strategy: the top-scoring Core Set undergoes fine-grained spatio-temporal deduplication (merging adjacent tokens exceeding a cosine-similarity threshold via average pooling), dynamically freeing token quota that is then reallocated to the Background Set through aggressive M:1 pooling to preserve macro-context. This fixed-budget design avoids the attention dilution that plagues both naive top-K truncation and uncompressed full-token baselines, empirically surpassing the full-token baseline on multiple benchmarks. For evaluation, the paper introduces a Spatio-Temporal Evidence-Aware Metric combining Gaussian-decayed temporal alignment with F₀.₅-weighted semantic matching, strictly conditioning the final score on answer correctness to penalize ungrounded guessing.

## Results

On the Video-HolmesV2 benchmark (784 long-form videos, avg. 43 min), Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the highest overall score of 73.2, while the best open-source model Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B reaches 55.3. The proposed Audio-Text Guided Token Compression framework (applied to Qwen2.5-Omni-7B) achieves an overall score of 44.5, outperforming the uncompressed Qwen2.5-Omni-7B baseline (40.7) by +3.8 points despite aggressive token reduction. On token compression benchmarks (AVUTBench, VideoMME, ShortVid-Bench), the method achieves 101.5% relative performance versus the Full Tokens baseline, surpassing OmniZip (99.1% at 45% retention) and DyCoke (97.5% at 50% retention).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary Video-HolmesV2 benchmark, the proposed method (44.5) outperforms the uncompressed Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (40.7) by +3.8 points and Qwen2.5-VL-32B (43.6) by +0.9, demonstrating that token compression can improve rather than degrade reasoning. However, it trails larger open-source models like Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B (55.3) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (50.2), as well as proprietary models Gemini-2.5-Pro (73.2) and GPT-4o (46.9). In the token compression comparison (Table 4), the method surpasses OmniZip (99.1% at 45% retention) and DyCoke (97.5% at 50% retention) on AVUTBench/VideoMME/ShortVid-Bench, and uniquely exceeds the Full Tokens baseline (101.5% vs. 100%), whereas all ratio-based baselines fall below full-token performance. On WorldScene (Table 5), the method achieves 47.2 average across all eight categories, beating Full Tokens (46.8) and OmniZip at 35% retention (45.3) by +1.9 points. The evidence-aware evaluation (Table 3) reveals that Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B's 55.1 MCQ accuracy is largely illusory (evidence score only 8.5, overall 34.9), while Gemini-2.5-Pro shows healthier reasoning-grounding alignment (evidence 35.2, overall 54.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Video-Holmes V1 line of work by introducing deep audio-visual coupling and spatio-temporal evidence grounding, moving beyond the visual-centric paradigm that dominates long-video benchmarks like Video-MME, EgoSchema, and LongVideoBench. For token compression, it departs from unimodal or ratio-based strategies (FastV, DyCoke, OmniZip) by fusing text-guided subjective intent with audio-guided objective saliency in a dual-stream framework, then applying differentiated intra-set deduplication and extra-set scavenging rather than naive top-K truncation. The evidence-based evaluation metric draws on process-level annotation ideas from recent calls for reasoning verification but is the first to strictly condition final scores on valid spatio-temporal grounding in an audio-visual long-video setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical 'illusion of accuracy' problem: models like Qwen3-VL-235B achieve 55% MCQ accuracy but only 8.5/100 evidence scores, revealing that correct answers are often parametric guesses rather than grounded reasoning. This has broad implications for how the field evaluates video understanding, suggesting that answer-only metrics systematically overestimate model capabilities. The token compression method's ability to surpass full-token baselines (101.5% relative) by acting as a noise filter is practically significant for deploying omni-models on long videos. Open questions include whether the evidence-based evaluation generalizes beyond multiple-choice formats, whether the audio-visual coupling requirement is too strict for real-world applications where one modality may genuinely suffice, and whether the compression strategy transfers to other modalities or longer contexts beyond 7500 seconds.
