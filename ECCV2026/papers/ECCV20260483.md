## Problem

Existing medical MLLM benchmarks evaluate performance only on clean, high-quality images, leaving robustness under clinically realistic degradations (noise, motion artifacts, sparse-view CT, MRI undersampling) largely unassessed. Moreover, no prior benchmark systematically quantifies whether models remain calibrated—i.e., whether their self-reported confidence tracks their actual accuracy—as image quality deteriorates, a gap that is critical for safe clinical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260483/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a degraded medical image (one of 7 modalities, corrupted by one of 18 degradation types at severity L1 or L2) with a multiple-choice clinical question drawn from OmniMedVQA, GMAI-MMBench, or MedXpertQA. Models produce a single letter answer; confidence is estimated via voting-based prediction consistency over T=10 stochastic trials (temperature 1.0), yielding a normalized-entropy-based confidence score per sample. The benchmark outputs per-sample accuracy, aggregate accuracy across 30 fine-grained capability dimensions, and the Calibration Shift metric (mean confidence minus accuracy) at each severity level.

## Method

MedQ-Deg is built on a dual-tier hierarchy: a capability axis decomposing clinical competence into 30 fine-grained skills across 6 mid-level tasks (anatomical recognition, imaging perception, clinical understanding, basic science, diagnostic reasoning, treatment reasoning), and a degradation axis covering 18 corruption types across 7 imaging modalities, each instantiated at 3 expert-calibrated severity degrees (L0–L2). Degradation parameters are calibrated by three board-certified radiologists who independently select feasible L1/L2 ranges over 9 parameter levels per modality-degradation pair, with only consensus intervals retained. A human-in-the-loop filtering step (92.5% unanimous agreement) discards samples where degradation obliterates diagnostic features or the question is answerable from text alone, yielding 24,894 QA pairs. The novel Calibration Shift metric (Δ_calib = mean voting-based confidence − accuracy) is used to formalize two forms of Dunning-Kruger-like overconfidence: intra-model (accuracy drops while calibration shift widens from L0 to L2) and inter-model (lower-accuracy models show disproportionately higher overconfidence). The benchmark is validated for ecological validity via t-SNE feature-space overlap with 2,000 real-world degraded images and Spearman rank correlation (0.90–0.92) between simulated and real-world model rankings.

## Results

Across 40 evaluated MLLMs, average accuracy on MedQ-Deg (L1&L2) is 36.6%, with a mean drop of +3.0 points from the clean L0 baseline. GPT-5 leads at 64.5% average accuracy, while DeepSeek-VL2 trails at 23.2%; commercial MLLMs average 49.2%, medical-specialized models 35.6%, and open-source general models 31.6%. Clinical Understanding is the strongest dimension (63.3% avg) while Treatment planning is the weakest (19.8% avg), with multiple open-source models collapsing to near-zero. All 40 models exhibit positive Calibration Shift that widens from L0 to L2, confirming systematic Dunning-Kruger-like overconfidence under degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedQ-Deg is compared against six prior medical image degradation benchmarks (Table 1). It substantially exceeds all of them in scope: 24,894 evaluation QA pairs vs. 1,500 (RobustMed-Bench) or 6,600 (VLM Artefacts); 7 imaging modalities vs. 1–9; 30 fine-grained capability dimensions vs. 1 (single-task) in all prior work; and 18 degradation types vs. 5–17. MedMNIST-C (520k samples, 9 modalities, 17 degradations) is the closest in degradation count but covers only classification with no QA or capability decomposition. RobustMed-Bench (1,500 QA, 3 modalities, 6 degradations) is the most directly comparable MLLM benchmark but omits capability decomposition, severity calibration, and confidence analysis. ROOD-MRI and Pathology Corruptions are restricted to single modality (MRI and pathology, respectively) and single task (segmentation and classification). The key differentiator is that MedQ-Deg is the only benchmark combining multi-dimensional capability assessment, expert-calibrated 3-level severity, and systematic confidence calibration (Calibration Shift) across 40 MLLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate research threads: (1) image corruption robustness evaluation, pioneered by ImageNet-C and extended to MLLMs via R-Bench and Bench-C, and (2) medical MLLM benchmarking (GMAI-MMBench, OmniMedVQA, MedXpertQA), which evaluates only on clean images. Prior medical degradation benchmarks (MedMNIST-C, ROOD-MRI, VLM Artefacts, RobustMed-Bench) are confined to single modalities, single tasks, or lack confidence analysis. MedQ-Deg carves new ground by combining the corruption-evaluation paradigm with fine-grained medical capability decomposition and introducing the Calibration Shift metric for metacognitive assessment, a dimension absent from all prior medical or general MLLM robustness benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Dunning-Kruger-like overconfidence finding—that all 40 models maintain high perceived confidence even as accuracy collapses under severe degradation—poses a direct patient-safety risk by preventing appropriate human oversight from being triggered, making calibration under degradation a critical open problem for clinical deployment. The benchmark's scale (24,894 QA pairs, 18 degradation types, 7 modalities, 30 capability dimensions) and the validation that simulated degradations align with real-world distributions (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.90–0.92) position it as a practical infrastructure for developing robust medical MLLMs. Open questions include whether the voting-based confidence proxy adequately captures true metacognitive awareness, how to architecturally address the overconfidence deficit, and whether the 'cliff effect' (nonlinear collapse at L2) reflects a fundamental limitation of current vision-language integration.
