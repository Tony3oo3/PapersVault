## Problem

In fine-grained image classification, visually similar subcategories produce highly overlapping feature distributions, making standard uncertainty estimates unreliable and diversity sampling ineffective for active learning. Existing active learning methods—whether uncertainty-based, diversity-based, or hybrid—struggle to identify truly informative samples when inter-class differences are subtle, and feature-fusion approaches like ALFA-Mix may overlook samples that exhibit consistent patterns across multiple categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260966/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled image pool and a small initial labeled pool, processed through a pretrained feature encoder (ResNet50 or ViT-Small initialized with DINO/DINOv2) and a linear classifier. Per active learning cycle, it outputs a batch of B = K·N_c images selected for oracle annotation, along with per-sample discrepancy-confusion uncertainty scores and cluster assignments. A key implementation detail is the pooled-gradient mask that restricts local feature fusion to only the top-η most informative feature dimensions, making the fusion operation comparable across different backbone architectures.

## Method

DECERN introduces a two-stage sampling strategy. First, it computes a discrepancy-confusion uncertainty score by performing local feature fusion between each unlabeled sample's features and per-class anchors (averaged labeled features), where the fusion strength α_j equals the predicted probability for category j and a pooled-gradient mask M restricts interpolation to top-η gradient dimensions. The resulting mixing representations are scored with cross-entropy (discrepancy, measuring structural stability) and entropy (confusion, measuring category directionality), combined via a cosine-similarity-weighted exponent β_j. Second, candidates exceeding a dynamic skewness-based threshold undergo uncertainty-weighted K-Means clustering, and from each cluster the selected sample maximizes a weighted combination of proximity to the cluster centroid (local representativeness) and distance from all labeled class anchors (global diversity), controlled by hyperparameter ξ.

## Results

Across 7 fine-grained image datasets (Caltech101, BronzeDing, CUB-200-2011, Flowers102, Food101, Oxford-IIIT Pet, StanfordDogs) and 39 experimental settings (varying backbone, annotation budget K∈{1,2}, and DINO/DINOv2 pretraining), DECERN consistently outperforms all 15 baselines in top-1 accuracy. On Caltech101 with ResNet50, the full method reaches 82.95% (B=1·Nc) and 88.49% (B=2·Nc) top-1 accuracy, compared to 74.14% and 82.93% when both uncertainty and diversity components are removed. The paper also reports that DECERN achieves near-optimal time efficiency per AL cycle, with significantly lower sampling time than BADGE, NoiseStability, and UHerding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DECERN outperforms all 15 baselines across all 7 datasets and both budget settings (K=1, K=2). Diversity-based methods (K-Means, CoreSet) and pure uncertainty methods (NoiseStability, Margin) are 'significantly hindered by fine-grained examples' due to overlapping feature distributions and lack of discriminative features between similar subcategories. Hybrid methods (BADGE, CLUE) achieve competitive performance but remain suboptimal, as they rely on gradient or embedding clustering that cannot resolve the subtle inter-class differences in fine-grained settings. The closest method, ALFA-Mix (feature-mixing-based active learning), is outperformed because its reliance solely on label variability of perturbed samples overlooks fine-grained instances that maintain the same prediction pattern across multiple categories. Additional baselines in the appendix (Confidence, Entropy, CoreGCN, ActiveFT, BALQUE, DropQuery) are also surpassed, confirming the generality of the advantage. The t-SNE visualizations (Fig. 4) further show that DECERN selects samples proximal to decision boundaries while maintaining diversity, whereas methods like CoreSet and K-Means select redundant or off-boundary points.

## Relation to Prior Work

DECERN extends the feature-fusion-based active learning line initiated by ALFA-Mix [39], which interpolates between labeled and unlabeled representations to measure label variability, by decomposing the resulting uncertainty into two distinct components: discrepancy uncertainty (structural stability under perturbation) and confusion uncertainty (category directionality erosion). It also builds on CLUE's [41] uncertainty-weighted K-Means clustering for diversity sampling, but augments it with a calibration step that explicitly balances local representativeness (proximity to cluster centroids) against global diversity (distance from labeled-data anchors). The key conceptual departure from prior feature-fusion AL is the use of a pooled-gradient mask to restrict fusion to informative feature dimensions and the asymmetric fusion strength (large α for the predicted class, small α for others) to separately probe structural stability and category directionality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in specialized domains (archaeology, medicine, natural species) where expert annotation is expensive and fine-grained visual similarity makes standard uncertainty or diversity signals unreliable. The consistent gains across 7 datasets and 39 settings, with near-optimal time efficiency, make DECERN a practical option for fine-grained AL pipelines. However, the approach is limited to classification; the authors acknowledge that efficacy on segmentation or detection remains unproven, and the method's reliance on a pretrained feature encoder (DINO/DINOv2) and a trained classifier per AL cycle may limit applicability in truly low-data or few-shot regimes where such pretraining is unavailable.
