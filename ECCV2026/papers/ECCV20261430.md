## Problem

Point-based neural renderers lack fixed connectivity, which provides reconstruction flexibility but causes holes, surface discontinuities, and see-through artifacts under large non-rigid deformations. Existing remedies reintroduce proxy geometry (cages, meshes) or spatial regularizations that constrain editing to rigid topologies and complicate part-level manipulations. The paper asks whether a point-based renderer can be made robust to arbitrary unseen deformations without ground-truth dynamic data or explicit geometry proxies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained PAPR point cloud (≈30K points with learnable 3D positions and feature vectors, learned from static multi-view RGB images) and an analytical 3D deformation field (e.g., twisting, scaling, bending) as input during a lightweight ~500-step self-supervised fine-tuning phase. At inference, the user applies a deformation to the point positions and the model produces deformed RGB renderings and differentiable surface-point estimates from arbitrary novel viewpoints. The surface points are extracted as attention-weighted convex combinations of the K nearest points per ray, making them differentiable with respect to both point positions and attention weights.

## Method

P-CORE enforces a self-supervised surface-consistency constraint: the surface predicted from the deformed point cloud must equal the deformation field applied to the surface predicted from the canonical point cloud, with a stop-gradient on the canonical surface points to prevent model collapse. The key architectural choice is PAPR's attention-based interpolation kernel, which fills gaps between extent-free points via learned convex weights over K nearest neighbors, so deformation only requires moving point positions without adjusting spatial extents (unlike 3DGS covariance transforms). Two complementary deformation modes are used simultaneously: a points-only mode that keeps camera origins fixed and recomputes ray directions toward deformed termination points, and a rays-and-points mode that repositions ray origins near the surface and deforms them, diversifying the ray configurations the attention model sees. The total loss combines canonical photometric rendering, the geometric surface-consistency term, and a texture consistency loss anchoring deformed appearance to the canonical rendering. The entire fine-tuning takes roughly 500 steps (<1 min), about 1/500 of pre-training, making it a lightweight post-processing step that substantially reduces holes and tearing under large non-rigid edits.

## Results

On the Objaverse dataset, P-CORE achieves state-of-the-art rendering quality with PSNR 28.30, SSIM 0.965, and LPIPS 0.039, outperforming all baselines including NeuralEditor (PSNR 28.02, SSIM 0.956, LPIPS 0.048) while using only 30K primitives versus NeuralEditor's ~1M. On the Neural Editor dataset, P-CORE (PSNR 25.31, SSIM 0.942, LPIPS 0.054) matches or slightly trails NeuralEditor (PSNR 25.62) on PSNR but leads on SSIM and LPIPS, again with 30K primitives. The fine-tuning step requires only ~500 steps (<1 min), roughly 1/500 of the pre-training duration, making it a lightweight post-processing step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P-CORE outperforms all five baselines on the Objaverse dataset across every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), with the largest margins over Deforming-NeRF (+12.4 dB PSNR) and SC-GS (+3.0 dB PSNR). On Neural Editor, P-CORE slightly trails NeuralEditor by 0.31 dB in PSNR (25.31 vs. 25.62) but leads on SSIM (0.942 vs. 0.939) and LPIPS (0.054 vs. 0.078), while using ~33× fewer primitives (30K vs. ~1M) and ~7 days less training time. Against proxy-free baselines, P-CORE clearly dominates: it beats SC-GS by 6.76 dB PSNR on Neural Editor and 3.01 dB on Objaverse, and beats Mani-GS by 4.72 dB and 2.26 dB respectively. Compared to vanilla PAPR (the same architecture without self-supervised fine-tuning), P-CORE gains 1.09 dB PSNR on Neural Editor and 5.15 dB on Objaverse, confirming that the self-supervised surface-consistency framework is the primary source of robustness. In the user study, P-CORE was preferred over PAPR, Mani-GS, and SC-GS in 94.7% of 525 trials overall, with win rates above 89% in every individual baseline–dataset pairing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PAPR (Proximity Attention Point Rendering), an attention-based point renderer that fills inter-point gaps via a learned interpolation kernel rather than spatial extents like 3D Gaussian Splatting. It departs from the dominant proxy-based editing paradigm (cages in Deforming-NeRF/Neural Editor, meshes in Mani-GS) by requiring no explicit geometry proxy, and from proxy-free spatial-extent methods (SC-GS) by exploiting the fact that extent-free points need no covariance adjustment under deformation. The key conceptual departure is a self-supervised surface-consistency loss that enforces the surface prediction commutes with an analytically defined deformation field, eliminating the need for ground-truth deformed observations that dynamic-scene methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a fundamental tension in point-based neural rendering—topological flexibility for reconstruction versus surface continuity under editing—with a lightweight (~500-step, <1 min) self-supervised fine-tuning step that is broadly applicable to any attention-based point renderer. The downstream Neural Blend Skinning application demonstrates practical utility for parameterized editing without motion capture data. However, the paper acknowledges that excessive fine-tuning leads to over-smoothed renderings, and the method is currently validated only on synthetic benchmarks with ground-truth deformations plus qualitative real-world results; open questions include integration with physically based simulation, multi-level editing controls, and whether the approach transfers to other point-based architectures beyond PAPR.
