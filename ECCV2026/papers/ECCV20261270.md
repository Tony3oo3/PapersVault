## Problem

Proactive failure prevention in embodied agents typically relies on visual world models that simulate and render future states, incurring prohibitive latencies (e.g., ~4 s per decision on enterprise GPUs) that make real-time control impractical. This paper challenges the assumption that visual simulation is necessary, hypothesizing that a trained policy's latent state already encodes sufficient information to anticipate action outcomes, thereby making pixel-level re-rendering redundant for failure prevention.

## Input / Output

At inference, DILLO consumes only the frozen policy's continuous latent vector z_t, a candidate continuous action chunk a_{t:t+k}, and a text task prompt—no raw visual observations are accessed. It produces a structured two-part output: a free-form natural language description of the expected physical interaction (e.g., gripper motion, object contact) followed by a single binary verdict token (POSITIVE or NEGATIVE). The model is invoked as a batched rejection-sampling filter over N=5 independently sampled action chunks, selecting the first candidate receiving a POSITIVE verdict before any physical action is committed.

## Method

DILLO is trained via cross-modal knowledge distillation: a simulator-privileged VLM teacher (with access to ground-truth RGB frames, 6DoF end-effector/object poses, and episode outcome signals) annotates offline policy rollouts with semantic descriptions and binary verdicts, while a latent-conditioned LLM student (Gemma-1B or Gemma-4B, using only the language backbone without the SigLIP visual encoder) learns to reproduce these annotations from the policy's compact latent and action chunk alone. Two learnable linear projectors map the continuous latent z_t and each action in the chunk into the LLM's embedding space, forming the input sequence [TASK_PROMPT] ⊕ P_z(z_t) ⊕ P_a(a_{t:t+k}). Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) projector alignment with a frozen LLM via next-token prediction, (2) description distillation using LoRA adapters to reconstruct the teacher's natural-language rationale, and (3) joint optimization of description loss and verdict cross-entropy. The core architectural insight is the Latent Sufficiency Hypothesis—that the mutual information between (z_t, a_{t:t+k}) and the future semantic outcome approximates that of the full observation history—enabling a text-only inference path that achieves a ~14× speedup over visual world-model baselines while matching or exceeding their prediction fidelity.

## Results

On MetaWorld, DILLO-4B raises episode success rate from 65.0% to 80.0% on Soccer (+15 pp) and from 75.0% to 90.0% on Drawer-Open (+15 pp), while DILLO-1B achieves 75.0% and 70.0% respectively. On LIBERO, DILLO-4B improves success from 64.0% to 74.0% on LIBERO-10 and from 82.0% to 88.0% on LIBERO-Object, with an average improvement of 9.3 pp across all tasks. The binary verdict achieves 91.4% average classification accuracy (DILLO-4B) without any visual input at inference. Inference latency is 0.26 s (1B) / 0.38 s (4B) per decision step, a ~14× speedup over the Forewarn visual baseline (3.70 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DILLO against three vision-based baselines (Zero-Shot, Few-Shot, and CAP—an oracle that receives the actual future observation) on both T2O fidelity and LLM-judged semantic scores. DILLO-4B consistently outperforms FS and ZS across nearly all tasks on both benchmarks; for example, on MetaWorld Drawer-Open it scores 0.705 T2O vs. 0.555 (FS) and 0.553 (ZS), and 0.628 LLM-score vs. 0.497 (FS) and 0.252 (CAP). Notably, DILLO-4B even exceeds the CAP oracle on Drawer-Open T2O (0.705 vs. 0.609), suggesting the policy latent is a superior predictive statistic to raw pixels. DILLO-1B also beats FS and ZS on most tasks, approaching CAP on LIBERO-Goal (0.727 vs. 0.723 T2O). The main exception is LIBERO-90, where the 90-task diversity degrades DILLO relative to CAP, and LIBERO-Spatial, where absent goal-position conditioning limits generalization. For steering, DILLO-4B improves over the unsteered base policy on 7 of 8 tasks, with the sole exception being LIBERO-90 (70.5% vs. 71.4% base), where the authors attribute the gap to a single global rejection threshold being suboptimal for heterogeneous tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of world models and knowledge distillation by proposing that a policy's latent state, combined with planned actions, is a sufficient statistic for predicting semantic action outcomes—eliminating the need for visual simulation entirely. It departs from reactive post-hoc failure analysis (Aha, REFLECT, SAFE) by being proactive, and from visual world-model steering (Forewarn, Dreamer) by replacing expensive pixel/latent rendering with a text-only inference path. The method repurposes cross-modal distillation (Hinton et al.) and LoRA fine-tuning to transfer a VLM teacher's privileged foresight into a lightweight latent-conditioned LLM, while the rejection-sampling steering mechanism builds on standard MPC-style candidate evaluation but operates in language space rather than state space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14× inference speedup (0.26 s vs. 3.7 s) makes proactive safety checking feasible on consumer hardware, a practical barrier that has kept visual world-model steering out of real-time control loops. The Latent Sufficiency Hypothesis, if it generalizes beyond the manipulation tasks tested here, could simplify world-model architectures broadly by removing the visual generation bottleneck. Key open questions include whether the approach transfers to long-horizon or highly dynamic tasks, whether it works without a simulator-privileged teacher (e.g., using estimated poses), and how to handle the task-conditioned calibration needed for large multi-task suites like LIBERO-90, where a single global rejection threshold proved insufficient.
