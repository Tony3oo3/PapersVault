## Problem

Monocular dynamic Gaussian Splatting methods overfit to training views and produce unreliable reconstructions under occlusion or complex scene motion, because purely geometric smoothness priors cannot propagate correct motion into regions hidden by occlusion or where off-the-shelf depth/track estimates are noisy. The paper identifies that real-world motion is structured by semantic coherence—spatially close, semantically related regions share consistent dynamics—and that this semantic structure is underexploited as a regularization signal for dynamic Gaussian optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260741/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single monocular video along with off-the-shelf predictions: 2D point tracks, per-frame depth (sensor depth on Dycheck, or Depth Anything 3 metric depth on the SMG dataset), and dynamic/static masks from a flow model. It produces a set of dynamic 3D Gaussians parameterized by reference pose, scale, opacity, and spherical-harmonic color, which can be rendered to novel views at arbitrary timesteps. As a byproduct, the lifted semantic tracks yield long-range 3D point tracks evaluated with end-point error and threshold accuracy metrics.

## Method

SMG constructs a graph whose nodes are 3D-lifted 2D tracks and whose edges connect only spatially local neighbors that also pass a DINOv3 cosine-similarity semantic gate, ensuring motion propagates within coherent semantic groups rather than across unrelated objects. Two uncertainty-handling mechanisms regularize the graph during optimization: C-ARAP modulates each directed edge weight by a per-frame node confidence (visibility × motion-consistency × rigidity-consistency) so that reliable nodes guide unreliable ones in an asymmetric as-rigid-as-possible loss, and LRM penalizes local velocity residuals that cannot be explained by a weighted least-squares rigid twist, suppressing structurally inconsistent velocities in weakly constrained regions. Each dynamic Gaussian is anchored to K semantic neighbors of its nearest SMG node and its pose at time t is obtained by dual-quaternion blending of those nodes' rigid motions from the reference frame. The full pipeline jointly optimizes photometric rendering losses (RGB, depth, mask, track) together with the C-ARAP and LRM graph regularizations, with density control applied over time to adapt the Gaussian population.

## Results

On the Dycheck (iPhone) dataset, SMG achieves mPSNR 19.54, mSSIM 0.718, and mLPIPS 0.250, outperforming MoSca (19.32/0.706/0.264) and the reproduced OriGS (19.43/0.695/0.281) on SSIM and LPIPS, while OriGS's paper-reported mPSNR (19.69) is marginally higher. On the NVIDIA dataset, SMG attains the best PSNR of 26.87 (vs. MoSca 26.72) with competitive LPIPS of 0.068. On the newly introduced SMG Dataset with challenging ego-exo camera motion, SMG leads all baselines across all three metrics (PSNR 13.74, SSIM 0.559, LPIPS 0.396 vs. OriGS 13.52/0.543/0.432 and MoSca 13.31/0.542/0.421). For 3D tracking on Dycheck, SMG achieves the lowest EPE (0.052) and highest δ.10 (91.6%) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMG outperforms the two strongest recent baselines, MoSca and OriGS, on the Dycheck dataset in mSSIM (0.718 vs. 0.706/0.695) and mLPIPS (0.250 vs. 0.264/0.281), while OriGS's paper-reported mPSNR (19.69) edges out SMG's 19.54 by 0.15 dB; the reproduced OriGS (19.43) is clearly below SMG. On the NVIDIA dataset, SMG achieves the highest PSNR (26.87) over MoSca (26.72) and DynPoint (26.53), with LPIPS matching DynPoint at 0.068. On the proposed SMG Dataset with complex ego-exo motion, SMG leads both MoSca and OriGS by a consistent margin across all scenes (e.g., +0.62 dB PSNR over MoSca on 'laptop2'), with the gap widening under heavy occlusion and large viewpoint changes. For 3D tracking, SMG reduces EPE to 0.052 (vs. 0.055 for MoSca, 0.057 for OriGS) and improves δ.10 to 91.6% (vs. 89.6%/89.7%). Older NeRF-based and Gaussian-based baselines (4DGS, Dyn. Gauss., Gauss. Marbles) trail SMG by 5–12 dB in PSNR, confirming that semantic motion regularization is critical under sparse monocular observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMG extends the dynamic Gaussian Splatting line initiated by 4DGS and MoSca, which regularize per-Gaussian motion using geometric scaffolds and off-the-shelf depth/track priors. The key conceptual departure is replacing purely geometric proximity with semantic coherence as the organizing principle for motion propagation: Gaussians are driven by a graph whose edges are gated by DINOv3 feature similarity, so motion is restricted to local semantic groups rather than spatial neighbors. The C-ARAP and LRM mechanisms further differentiate SMG from MoSca/OriGS by explicitly modeling per-node confidence and local rigid velocity consistency, addressing uncertainty that prior scaffold-based methods propagate uniformly. The dual-quaternion blending from MoSca is retained as the motion interpolation operator, making SMG a direct architectural extension rather than a from-scratch redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The semantic motion graph offers a principled regularization that addresses a persistent failure mode in monocular dynamic reconstruction—motion leakage across unrelated objects and geometry collapse under occlusion—without requiring additional sensors or dense multi-view capture. The introduced SMG Dataset (ego-exo setup with complex camera motion and human-object interaction) fills a gap left by Dycheck's simpler rotation-dominated scenes, providing a more realistic stress test for dynamic Gaussian methods. However, the method remains per-scene test-time optimization and depends on the quality of upstream depth/track/semantic priors, limiting scalability to real-time or large-scale applications; the authors note that feed-forward 4D Gaussian approaches are a promising direction for overcoming this bottleneck.
