## Problem

Existing prototype-based federated learning methods aggregate client or class features into single coarse centroids, implicitly assuming unimodal within-class distributions. In multi-center psychiatry, patients sharing the same diagnosis exhibit highly polymorphic manifestations (e.g., agitated vs. melancholic depression), forming multi-peak topological structures that coarse averaging collapses into semantically ambiguous 'chimera' prototypes, causing severe representation collapse and blurred decision boundaries.

## Input / Output

On the psychiatric benchmark (Psycare), the method consumes aligned tri-view sequences (audio prosody, facial video, and text transcripts) from psychiatric interviews and outputs one of five diagnosis labels (HC, BD1, BD2, MDD, SZ). On vision benchmarks, it takes RGB images (32×32 for Digits, 224×224 for Office-Caltech) and outputs class labels. Unusually, clients upload subsampled sample-level embeddings grouped by (class, view) pairs alongside model weights, rather than only exchanging network parameters or client-level mean prototypes.

## Method

FedMental introduces a closed-loop server-client architecture with three key mechanisms. On the server, Topology-Aware Prototype Refinement (TAPR) aggregates subsampled sample-level features by class-view pair and applies parameter-free FINCH clustering to discover an adaptive number of latent mode prototypes per pair, avoiding the premature averaging that destroys intra-client diversity. On the client, Intra-View Mode Alignment (IVMA) computes a soft-assignment over same-view mode prototypes via cosine similarity and anchors each local feature to a dynamically weighted mixture prototype, stabilizing training against center-specific shifts. Cross-View Semantic Contrast (CVSC) then applies an InfoNCE objective that pulls features toward same-class prototypes from other views while repelling different-class prototypes, enforcing view-invariant semantics. The total client loss combines cross-entropy supervision with weighted IVMA and CVSC terms, and the global model is updated via standard data-size-weighted averaging each communication round.

## Results

On the primary psychiatric benchmark Psycare, FedMental achieves 46.32% per-class average accuracy, a +8.83% absolute improvement over FedAvg (37.49%) and +8.82% over the strongest baseline FedDyn (37.50%). On the supplementary vision benchmarks, FedMental reaches 83.61% average accuracy on Digits (+4.41% over FedAvg) and 66.32% on Office-Caltech (+11.42% over FedAvg), outperforming the best prototype-based baseline FedPLVM by +2.09% and +6.99% respectively. FedMental is the top-performing method across all three benchmarks and all individual domains/classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedMental outperforms all 10 baselines on every benchmark. On Psycare, the gap is most dramatic: FedMental (46.32%) surpasses the nearest competitor FedDyn (37.50%) by 8.82 points, while standard methods like FedAvg (37.49%), FedProx (36.91%), and MOON (37.25%) all cluster within 1% of each other, confirming that coarse aggregation fails under psychiatric polymorphism. On Digits, FedMental (83.61%) leads the strongest prototype baseline FedPLVM (81.52%) by 2.09 points and FPL (81.14%) by 2.47 points, with the largest per-domain gain on SYN (+5.64% over FedAvg). On Office-Caltech, the advantage is widest: FedMental (66.32%) beats FedPLVM (59.33%) by 6.99 points and FedRDN (59.20%) by 7.12 points, with a particularly large +14.34% gain on the hardest domain (DSLR). Notably, several regularization-based baselines (FedProx, FedOPT, FedDyn) actually underperform plain FedAvg on Office-Caltech, suggesting that proximal penalties are counterproductive under extreme domain shift, whereas topology-aware prototype refinement consistently helps.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedMental extends the prototype-based federated learning lineage initiated by FedProto and advanced by FPL and FedPLVM, but departs from them in a critical architectural way: rather than averaging features at the client level (which the authors argue destroys intra-client polymorphic diversity before it reaches the server), it uploads subsampled sample-level embeddings and applies parameter-free FINCH clustering on the server over the global feature pool. The dual client-side alignment strategy (IVMA soft-assignment to mode prototypes + CVSC cross-view InfoNCE contrast) draws on self-supervised contrastive learning and prototype anchoring, but is specifically designed to balance view-specific stability against cross-view generalization under within-class multi-peak structure. The paper positions itself against clustered FL and domain generalization methods as addressing a distinct heterogeneity axis—within-class polymorphism—rather than the between-class label skew or domain shift those methods target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, topology-aware alternative to the dominant unimodal-averaging assumption in prototype-based FL, with direct relevance to multi-center clinical AI where privacy regulations (HIPAA, GDPR) preclude data centralization and where diagnostic categories (e.g., MDD) genuinely span divergent symptom subtypes. However, the experimental evidence is limited to three datasets (one small clinical benchmark with 5-fold CV and two standard vision benchmarks), the privacy analysis is deferred to supplementary material, and the multi-view setup on Psycare is simulated with only 7 clients. Open questions include whether the approach scales to dozens of centers and modalities, whether formal differential-privacy guarantees can be layered onto the subsampled feature uploads, and whether the FINCH-based mode discovery remains stable as the number of latent subtypes grows with data volume.
