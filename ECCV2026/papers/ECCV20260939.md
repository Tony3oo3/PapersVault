## Problem

Prior 3D hand trajectory prediction methods treat egocentric motion as a continuous homogeneous signal, conflating intent-driven actions with incidental egocentric noise and ignoring the stage-aware interaction structure (approach, manipulation, release) that organizes real hand-object interactions. Without explicit stage-level supervision, models struggle to separate purposeful motion from noise and generalize across diverse interactions, and existing VLA systems couple reasoning to motion through implicit tokens or lengthy chains that are inefficient and lack physical interpretability.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single egocentric RGB frame, H past 6DoF wrist trajectory points (3D position + 6D rotation per wrist at 10 FPS, expressed in the camera coordinate frame of the final pre-interaction frame), and a free-form language intent query. It produces T future 6DoF trajectory points for both wrists via a Flow Matching decoder, conditioned on four structured trajectory tokens (<ACT>, <START>, <CONTACT>, <END>) that encode action semantics and key spatiotemporal waypoints (approach onset, manipulation onset, manipulation completion) with predicted timestamps, 3D positions, and 6D rotations.

## Method

EgoMAN couples an Interaction-Structured Reasoning module (built on Qwen2.5-VL) with a Motion Expert (encoder-decoder transformer using Flow Matching) through a compact Trajectory-Token Interface of four tokens that serve as spatiotemporal anchors bridging high-level intent reasoning to continuous 6DoF generation. The reasoning module is pretrained on 1M structured QA pairs (semantic, spatial, motion) to predict both natural-language answers and numeric trajectory tokens, supervised with next-token prediction, a CLIP-based action-semantic contrastive loss, and Gaussian-windowed Huber waypoint losses. The Motion Expert is separately pretrained with Flow Matching on ground-truth waypoints to learn physically plausible dynamics, then jointly fine-tuned with the reasoning module so that predicted tokens (rather than ground-truth) condition trajectory generation, resolving the distribution mismatch between the two components. This progressive three-stage strategy—reasoning pre-training, motion pre-training, then joint alignment—enables stable long-horizon, intent-consistent 6DoF trajectory prediction while keeping the reasoning-to-motion interface to just four interpretable tokens.

## Results

On EgoMAN-Unseen, EgoMAN achieves ADE 0.124 m, FDE 0.179 m, DTW 0.111 m, and rotation error 32.75°, reducing ADE by 27.5% over the strongest external baseline HandsOnVLM* (0.171 m). On the out-of-distribution HOT3D-OOD set, EgoMAN attains ADE 0.141 m and FDE 0.217 m, a 27.3% ADE improvement over HandsOnVLM*. For waypoint prediction, EgoMAN reduces contact distance to 0.192 m (vs. 0.290–0.342 m for affordance baselines) while running at 3.45 FPS, over 70× faster than VRB* and VidBot. In motion-to-verb retrieval, EgoMAN achieves R@3 of 43.9% and FID 0.04, substantially outperforming all trajectory baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoMAN outperforms all six external baselines on every trajectory metric across both test splits. Against the strongest trajectory baseline HandsOnVLM* [4], EgoMAN reduces ADE from 0.171 m to 0.124 m (−27.5%) on EgoMAN-Unseen and from 0.194 m to 0.141 m (−27.3%) on HOT3D-OOD, with similar margins on FDE and DTW. The state-space model USST* [5] and diffusion-based MMTwin* [34] trail by 47% and 40% in ADE respectively on EgoMAN-Unseen, reflecting their lack of explicit intent conditioning and stage-aware structure. For waypoint prediction, EgoMAN (Contact 0.192 m, Traj 0.127 m) beats HAMSTER* [27] (0.342 m / 0.297 m), VRB* [1] (0.300 m / 0.271 m), and VidBot [10] (0.290 m / 0.269 m) by 33–44% in contact distance and 53–57% in trajectory-warp distance, while running 70–115× faster (3.45 FPS vs. <0.05 FPS). In motion-to-verb retrieval, EgoMAN's R@3 of 43.9% exceeds HandsOnVLM* (27.9%), MMTwin* (22.9%), and USST* (15.0%), confirming superior semantic alignment between generated trajectories and intent descriptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoMAN departs from the dominant paradigm in 3D hand trajectory prediction, where prior methods (USST, MMTwin, HandsOnVLM) treat motion as continuous signal regression decoupled from semantic intent. It also moves beyond VLA systems (π0, OpenVLA, RT-2) that couple reasoning to action through implicit feature routing or lengthy reasoning chains, by introducing an explicit four-token Trajectory-Token Interface that encodes interaction stages as sparse spatiotemporal anchors. The Motion Expert builds directly on Flow Matching (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) for smooth probabilistic trajectory generation, while the reasoning module leverages Qwen2.5-VL as a VLM backbone. Conceptually, the paper bridges the gap between affordance-based methods (which require brittle perception pipelines) and end-to-end motion predictors (which lack interpretability) by grounding generation in explicit interaction-stage structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EgoMAN dataset (219K 6DoF trajectories, 3M structured QA pairs) and EgoMAN-Bench provide the first large-scale stage-aware egocentric benchmark, filling a gap between controlled datasets with limited diversity and large-scale egocentric corpora with noisy, unstructured trajectories. The Trajectory-Token Interface offers a compact, interpretable design pattern for bridging VLM reasoning to continuous motion generation that could generalize to other embodied tasks. Key limitations include wrist-level modeling only (no finger articulation), coarse two-stage interaction structure (approach + manipulation), and evaluation restricted to egocentric hand trajectories. Open questions include whether the stage-aware formulation extends to multi-object interactions, dexterous manipulation, and direct transfer to robotic control systems.
