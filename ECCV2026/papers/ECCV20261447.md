## Problem

Scaling diffusion-based virtual try-on to native 2K multi-garment synthesis is blocked by two compounding barriers: the quadratic memory explosion from naively concatenating K high-resolution garment conditions into the attention sequence, and the spectral bias of the standard MSE denoising objective that acts as an aggressive low-pass filter at extreme spatial scales, destroying high-frequency fabric textures into plastic-like surfaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261447/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a clothing-agnostic person image, a heterogeneous set of K reference garments (1–6 items spanning apparel, footwear, and fine accessories), and a layer-aware textual prompt, producing a single native 2048×1536 try-on image with correct multi-garment occlusion and depth ordering. Notably, no explicit segmentation masks are required at inference; the model learns physical depth ordering implicitly through a 'Real Ground-Truth, Synthesized Condition' training paradigm where the agnostic model is inversely synthesized from authentic 2K photographs.

## Method

WearWow builds on the Qwen-ImageEdit diffusion backbone with a Qwen2.5VL vision-language encoder and introduces two core mechanisms. First, Adaptive 2D Token Packing (ATP) formulates multi-garment condition injection as a 2D bin-packing problem: K garments are dynamically scaled and tightly packed onto a unified spatial canvas with fixed token capacity L, then background void tokens are pruned via binary valid-region masks, reducing the effective conditional sequence from K·N to (L−m) and bounding attention cost to O((N_base+L−m)²) while preserving 2D positional encodings. Second, the Multi-dimensional Try-on Reward (MTR) is a DPO-inspired post-training alignment pipeline that synergizes a Cloth Distribution Reward (CDR), which uses implicit velocity-field prediction errors between a trainable and frozen reference model to anchor the generative trajectory to high-fidelity texture distributions, with a Semantic Guidance Reward (SGR), which projects single-step denoised latents to t=0 and computes CLIP-based cosine similarities against positive (detailed material) and negative (over-smoothed) textual anchors to explicitly drive tactile restoration. The dual objective prevents reward hacking: CDR provides the physical anchor against structural collapse while SGR accelerates convergence. Training proceeds in two phases—80K steps of supervised fine-tuning at native 2K followed by 800 steps of MTR alignment at a reduced learning rate.

## Results

On the native 2K multi-garment WearWow-2K benchmark, WearWow achieves FID 7.54, KID 0.26, LPIPS 0.077, SSIM 0.846, and Human Evaluation 0.254, outperforming the strongest commercial baseline (Nano Banana Pro: FID 8.18, KID 0.83, LPIPS 0.111) by 8% on FID and 68% on KID. On the single-garment VITON-HD/DressCode benchmark, WearWow attains FID 4.25, LPIPS 0.069, KID 0.65, and HE 0.408, surpassing the best open-source method CatVTON (FID 4.57, LPIPS 0.075, KID 1.03) on all perceptual and distributional metrics, though CatVTON edges ahead on SSIM (0.895 vs. 0.882).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WearWow achieves the best score on every metric in both evaluation settings. In the multi-garment 2K setting (Table 2), it outperforms the closest commercial competitor Nano Banana Pro by 8% FID (7.54 vs. 8.18), 68% KID (0.26 vs. 0.83), and 30% LPIPS (0.077 vs. 0.111), while also leading on SSIM and Human Evaluation. Open-source OminiTry and QWen-image lag substantially (FID 12.4 and 14.4 respectively), confirming that general-purpose diffusion models struggle with strict multi-garment spatial control at 2K. In the single-garment setting (Table 1), WearWow beats CatVTON on FID (4.25 vs. 4.57), KID (0.65 vs. 1.03), LPIPS (0.069 vs. 0.075), and HE (0.408 vs. 0.074), though CatVTON retains a marginal SSIM advantage (0.895 vs. 0.882) that the authors attribute to SSIM's bias toward over-smoothed low-frequency outputs. IDM-VTON and Leffa, the strongest prior open-source single-garment methods, are surpassed by WearWow by 29% and 29% on FID respectively, with KID gaps of 63% and 35%.

## Relation to Prior Work

WearWow extends the diffusion-based virtual try-on lineage (TryonDiffusion, IDM-VTON, OOTDiffusion, CatVTON) from single-garment, sub-2K settings to native 2K multi-garment mask-free generation, and adapts DPO-style preference alignment (Rafailov et al., 2023) to the velocity-field formulation of flow-matching diffusion models. Its key methodological departure is twofold: Adaptive 2D Token Packing replaces naive 1D concatenation of garment tokens with a spatially-aware 2D bin-packing plus sparsity-pruning strategy, and the Multi-dimensional Try-on Reward (MTR) combines a distributional velocity-field reward (CDR) with a CLIP/HPSv2-based semantic contrastive reward (SGR) to counteract the spectral bias of MSE denoising. The framework is built on Qwen-ImageEdit and Qwen2.5VL as the backbone and vision-language encoder, positioning it as a specialized vertical solution rather than a general-purpose text-to-image model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WearWow is the first end-to-end framework to perform native 2K multi-garment try-on without cascaded super-resolution, and the released WearWow-2K dataset (~100K triplets at 2048×1536) provides a much-needed benchmark for ultra-HD digital fashion. The ATP mechanism offers a broadly applicable recipe for reducing multi-condition sequence length in any token-based diffusion backbone, potentially transferable beyond VTON. Open questions remain around back-view occlusion handling, 3D view consistency, and extension to video try-on with dynamic relighting, all acknowledged by the authors as future directions.
