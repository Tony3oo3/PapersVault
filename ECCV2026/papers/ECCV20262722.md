## Problem

Existing Vision-Language-Action models are largely restricted to short-horizon, tabletop manipulation and rely on Markovian control where each decision depends solely on the current observation. This prevents consistent reasoning over extended task sequences and hinders long-term spatial understanding, which are essential for mobile manipulation where an agent must coordinate navigation and manipulation under changing spatial contexts and resolve non-Markovian ambiguities (e.g., distinguishing 'cabinet opened' from 'about to open').

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images from three fixed cameras (encoded by a frozen SigLIP vision tower), depth observations fused into a point cloud (encoded by a trainable PointAttn backbone), a natural-language instruction (encoded by a frozen SigLIP text tower), and proprioceptive robot states (mapped through a small MLP). These are concatenated into a unified token sequence that drives memory retrieval and conditions the action head. The output is a pair of continuous action vectors—base motion and arm manipulation—generated independently by separate denoising diffusion processes rather than a single monolithic action token.

## Method

EchoVLA introduces a neuro-inspired dual-memory system: a persistent scene memory stored as a 3D voxel Key-Value bank (spatial indices as keys, aggregated PointAttn features as values) that is updated via a discrepancy-driven EMA rule, and a time-indexed episodic memory implemented as a fixed-size FIFO buffer of recent multimodal token sequences. Retrieval follows a coarse-to-fine hierarchy: the current voxelized 3D feature map queries scene memory via coarse cross-attention to recover stable spatial structure, while the current multimodal state tokens query episodic memory via fine cross-attention to recover task-progress context. The two retrieved representations are concatenated and used as conditioning for a per-part diffusion policy, where independent denoisers predict noise for base and arm action subspaces separately. The model is trained by imitation learning on the MoMani benchmark, which combines MLLM-guided automated trajectory generation in simulation with real-robot teleoperated demonstrations on a holonomic mobile platform.

## Results

On the RoboCasa simulator, EchoVLA achieves the highest average success rate of 0.52 on Manipulation/Navigation tasks and 0.31 on Mobile Manipulation tasks, outperforming the strongest baseline π0.5 (0.32 and 0.20, respectively) by +0.20 and +0.11. In real-world experiments on the TidyBot++ platform across a 7 m × 7 m arena, EchoVLA attains a mean success rate of 0.44 over six mobile manipulation tasks, surpassing π0.5 (0.33) and Diffusion Policy (0.32). The largest gains appear on spatially demanding tasks such as PickPlaceSinkToCounter (0.68 vs. 0.20 for π0.5) and the longest-horizon real-world task EnP (0.10 vs. 0.00 for π0.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoVLA outperforms all five baselines on both Manipulation/Navigation and Mobile Manipulation categories in RoboCasa simulation. Against the strongest baseline π0.5, EchoVLA gains +0.20 average SR on Manipulation/Navigation (0.52 vs. 0.32) and +0.11 on Mobile Manipulation (0.31 vs. 0.20), with the largest per-task margin on PickPlaceSinkToCounter (0.68 vs. 0.20). Diffusion Policy and BC-T perform poorly, with Diffusion Policy near zero on most manipulation tasks (Avg SR 0.01) and BC-T dropping from 0.28 to 0.05 when base-arm coordination is required. DP3 and WB-VIMA occupy the middle range (0.22 and 0.15 on Manip/Nav; 0.06 and 0.11 on Mobile Manip), confirming that neither 3D-diffusion nor whole-body VLA approaches alone match the dual-memory design. In real-world trials, EchoVLA (0.44) leads π0.5 (0.33) and Diffusion Policy (0.32), with the gap widening on long-horizon tasks: EchoVLA scores 0.50 on knob rotation (RK) where Diffusion Policy manages only 0.10, and 0.10 on the cross-room EnP task where π0.5 scores 0.00.

## Relation to Prior Work

EchoVLA extends the VLA lineage (RT-2, OpenVLA, π0/π0.5) by replacing the Markovian, current-observation-only assumption with an explicit dual-memory architecture: a persistent voxel-based scene memory (analogous to parahippocampal spatial encoding) and a time-indexed episodic token buffer (analogous to hippocampal episodic traces). This departs from concurrent work like MemoryVLA, which caches perceptual features without explicit 3D spatial structure, and from BSCNav, which uses landmark descriptors for LLM-based planning rather than conditioning a diffusion policy. The per-part diffusion action head follows π0.5's base/arm decomposition, while the MoMani benchmark extends existing mobile-manipulation suites (RoboCasa, BEHAVIOR-1K, RoboTwin 2.0) by adding an MLLM-guided automated trajectory generator and real-robot demonstrations on a holonomic platform.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mobile manipulation—coordinating navigation and manipulation over long horizons in changing spatial contexts—remains a critical gap in VLA research, and this paper provides both a principled memory mechanism and a scalable data pipeline (MoMani) to address it. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include sensitivity to odometry drift and 'ghosting' in the voxel scene memory, vulnerability to dynamic occlusion (e.g., opening a refrigerator door), and a cold-start problem in novel environments where the 3D memory is initially sparse. Open questions include whether integrating loop-closure or visual-SAM would resolve spatial misalignment, whether active exploration or generative 3D priors can mitigate cold-start, and whether the neuro-inspired dual-memory framing generalizes beyond kitchen-scale environments to more complex, multi-room settings.
