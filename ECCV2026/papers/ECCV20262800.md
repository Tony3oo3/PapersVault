## Problem

In federated learning with severe class imbalance and label skew, standard aggregation over-emphasizes dominant clients and degrades minority-class performance. Existing data-free contribution estimation methods rely on similarity-to-average assumptions in gradient or update space, which systematically undervalue informative clients carrying rare classes (Mavericks) and fail to suppress non-contributing free-riders. The paper addresses this gap by proposing a data-free, class-wise contribution estimation mechanism that requires no raw data, client metadata, or auxiliary validation sets at the server.

## Input / Output

The server receives client model weight updates and the previous global model parameters; no raw images, class histograms, or self-reported sample counts are needed. Probing begins from random Gaussian noise (or warm-started images from the prior round) and produces a per-client, per-class logit evidence score. The final output is a simplex-constrained contribution weight vector c^(t) ∈ R^N used for weighted model aggregation, yielding the updated global model. During warm-up, only the backbone is shared with clients (local classifier heads are retained to preserve class-discriminative signal); after warm-up, the full model including the classifier head is broadcast.

## Method

CELM performs class-wise logit maximization: for each client model and each class, the server optimizes a synthetic input via gradient ascent (Adam, 200 steps) to maximize the pre-softmax logit for that class under ℓ₂ regularization, yielding a raw evidence score. These scores are de-biased by subtracting a global-model reference baseline and ReLU-suppressed, then normalized per-class across all clients to obtain relative class shares, which are averaged into a single contribution score per client. Simplex normalization and exponential moving average (β=0.5) smoothing ensure stable, bounded weights. The key design choice is a warm-up-and-freeze schedule: full probing runs only for the first 5% of rounds, after which contribution weights are frozen, reducing per-round overhead to standard weighted aggregation while preserving the class-aware signal. This class-share normalization is what distinguishes CELM from similarity-to-average methods, as it rewards relative competence on rare classes rather than absolute alignment with the cohort mean.

## Results

On FashionMNIST, CELM achieves the best accuracy in 4 of 5 non-IID splits, with the largest gain over FedAvg under Pure Label Skew (83.70% vs. 80.62%) and Dirichlet(0.05) (83.64% vs. 81.63%). On CIFAR-10, CELM is the strongest method across all five splits, reaching 71.96% under Step Label Skew (+4.56 over FedAvg) and 67.16% under Dirichlet(0.05). On the naturally partitioned clinical dataset FedISIC, CELM achieves 70.18% balanced accuracy, outperforming the next-best method CFFL (62.60%) by +7.58 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CELM outperforms FedAvg (uniform weighting) on 10 of 11 main-table splits, with the largest margins under strong label skew: +2.29 on CIFAR-10 PLS and +4.56 on CIFAR-10 SLS. Against the validation-based CFFL, CELM wins on all CIFAR-10 splits and FedISIC (by +7.58), but CFFL edges out CELM on FashionMNIST SLS (88.13 vs. 87.00). CGSV (cosine-similarity-to-average gradient) collapses to near-random performance across nearly all splits (e.g., 18.25% on CIFAR-10 Dir.(0.01), 0.00% rare-class accuracy under Maverick), confirming that similarity-to-average scoring is fundamentally incompatible with rare-class clients. ShapFed (class-wise gradient alignment) is the closest competitor, but CELM surpasses it on 9 of 11 splits, with the gap widening under stronger heterogeneity (e.g., +2.05 on CIFAR-10 SLS, +7.87 on FedISIC). In the additional comparison against client-side heterogeneity methods (Table 4), CELM dominates all Maverick settings (e.g., 68.60 vs. 62.49 for FedRS on CIFAR-10), while the combined CELM(RS) variant further improves PLS performance to 64.05 on CIFAR-10, surpassing FedRS alone (62.90).

## Relation to Prior Work

CELM repurposes activation/logit maximization—a technique well-established in the interpretability literature (Erhan et al., Yosinski et al., Mahendran & Vedaldi)—as a server-side probing mechanism for contribution scoring in federated learning, which the authors claim is the first such use. It departs from prior data-free contribution estimators (CGSV, ShapFed) that operate in gradient or update space by instead measuring class-wise discriminative evidence directly from client model logits, and from validation-based methods (CFFL) by requiring no auxiliary data. The warm-up-and-freeze aggregation strategy with EMA smoothing positions it as a drop-in replacement for FedAvg's uniform or sample-count weighting, while the class-share normalization explicitly targets the Maverick-client and label-skew regimes that similarity-to-average methods are known to underweight.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: how to fairly weight clients in FL when data is non-IID and no validation set or metadata is available, a setting common in cross-silo deployments. The consistent gains across FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10, and the clinical FedISIC dataset, combined with strong Maverick and free-rider discrimination, suggest the approach could be adopted as a lightweight server-side module alongside existing client-side heterogeneity methods (demonstrated via the CELM+RS combination). Key limitations include the warm-up compute overhead (O(T_w(N+1)LC_LM)), experiments restricted to 5–20 clients and up to 47 classes, and dependence on model calibration quality; scaling to hundreds of clients, high-resolution vision tasks, or adversarial participation remains open.
