## Problem

General MLLMs applied to industrial anomaly QA often produce evidence-inconsistent responses: a correct multiple-choice answer paired with an invalid bounding box, a hallucinated defect location on a visually normal sample, or a rationale that contradicts the final prediction. Supervised fine-tuning alone cannot penalize such structured inconsistencies, and applying preference or reinforcement learning directly to a base MLLM is unstable because the model has not yet learned the required output format or spatial grounding. The paper addresses this by asking how to progressively align an MLLM so that its answer, spatial location, and textual rationale are jointly consistent and grounded in localized visual evidence.

## Input / Output

The model receives a fixed pair of RGB images (first = query/test image, second = normal reference from the same object category) together with a natural-language inspection question, and produces a single structured EDDP response containing <evidence>, <logic>, <location>, and <answer> fields. The <location> field is always a list of normalized bounding boxes in a [0, 1000] coordinate system; for normal, negative-region, or non-spatial samples the correct output is the empty list <location>[]</location>, which also serves as the trigger for suppressing mask rendering. A separate BGSR step maps any non-empty predicted boxes to pixel coordinates and feeds them to SAM3 for dense mask visualization, but this rendering step does not alter the MLLM's textual answer.

## Method

EGVLR is a four-stage progressive alignment pipeline built on a unified EDDP output schema. Stage 1 (PVE-FT) is a vision-only pre-alignment that trains the model on synthetic localized anomalies (CutPaste patch replacement, DTD texture insertion) with 3×3 grid grounding, local verification decoys, and null-hypothesis calibration to establish spatial evidence grounding before any domain reasoning is introduced. Stage 2 (KG-IT) injects industrial-domain QA behavior via a 50:35:15 mixture of domain QA, one-normal visual QA, and comparative inspection QA while preserving the same EDDP format. Stage 3 (GS-DPO) uses a GRPO-style objective with a decoupled five-channel reward—format validity, answer correctness, box geometry (IoU/DIoU with count penalty), BGE-embedding-based rationale semantics (comparing generated evidence against normal/abnormal/domain prototype banks), and a semantic coupling term that jointly requires answer-location-rationale agreement—thereby penalizing spatial reward hacking and contradictory traces. Stage 4 (BGSR) is a non-training rendering step that converts predicted boxes to pixel-level masks via SAM3, explicitly separating cognitive reasoning from dense visualization.

## Results

On the MMAD industrial anomaly benchmark (1-shot anchor-guided setting), EGVLR (Qwen3-VL-8B backbone) achieves the best average accuracy of 82.55% among all open-source and anomaly-specialized models, surpassing the strongest specialized baseline JUDO (80.73%) by 1.82 points and the unmodified Qwen3-VL-8B base (74.62%) by 7.93 points. The largest gains appear on reasoning-dependent tasks: Defect Localization (76.85% vs. 75.95% for JUDO), Defect Description (84.42%), and Defect Analysis (87.82%). EGVLR also reduces spatial false positives substantially, with FPR_img dropping from 42.6% (base) to 13.9% and average false-positive box count from 0.78 to 0.18 on normal samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGVLR outperforms all anomaly-specialized baselines on average accuracy: it beats JUDO (80.73%) by 1.82 pts, OmniAD (79.90%) by 2.65 pts, and AnomalyR1 (77.29%) by 5.26 pts. The most consistent advantage is on Defect Localization, where EGVLR (76.85%) leads JUDO (75.95%), OmniAD (75.50%), and AnomalyR1 (70.72%), reflecting the benefit of explicit box-geometry and coupling rewards. On Object Classification, EGVLR (93.30%) trails OmniAD (96.00%), JUDO (94.24%), and the commercial models (GPT-4o 94.98%, Gemini 94.88%), suggesting that general object recognition remains a relative weakness. Against the unmodified Qwen3-VL-8B base, EGVLR gains 7.93 points on average, with the largest single-task jump on Defect Localization (+17.77 pts) and Defect Description (+10.88 pts), confirming that the staged alignment pipeline—not backbone scaling—drives the improvement. EGVLR also surpasses Gemini 2.5 Pro (81.19%) by 1.36 points on average, though Gemini retains an edge on Anomaly Discrimination (83.07% vs. 71.50%) and Object Classification (94.88% vs. 93.30%).

## Relation to Prior Work

EGVLR extends the line of MLLM-based industrial anomaly reasoning (AnomalyGPT, OmniAD, AnomalyR1, IAD-R1, JUDO) by introducing a staged alignment protocol that explicitly separates visual evidence grounding (PVE-FT), domain knowledge injection (KG-IT), and consistency optimization (GS-DPO), rather than learning all three jointly as prior methods do. The decoupled reward design in GS-DPO builds on GRPO-style rule-based RL but adds geometry-aware box rewards and BGE-embedding-based rationale semantic scoring, addressing a gap the authors identify in prior work where spatial and semantic consistency are under-specified. The unified EDDP schema and the treatment of segmentation as a post-hoc rendering step (BGSR with SAM3) depart from approaches that conflate reasoning and mask generation, drawing on reference-guided comparison conventions from industrial inspection literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment concern—evidence inconsistency between answer, location, and rationale—that prior anomaly QA methods do not explicitly penalize, and the 3× reduction in image-level false-positive rate on normal samples (42.6% → 13.9%) is directly relevant to reducing manual verification cost in industrial settings. Limitations include reliance on BGE embeddings for semantic scoring (not full NLI), incomplete handling of multi-defect images, and evaluation on a single benchmark (MMAD), leaving open whether the staged protocol generalizes to other industrial domains or scales to larger model families. The finding that the alignment pipeline compensates for a backbone's native localization weakness (Qwen3-VL-8B underperforming Qwen2.5-VL-7B before EGVLR but surpassing it after) suggests the training protocol may be more important than backbone choice for spatially grounded tasks, a question worth further investigation.
