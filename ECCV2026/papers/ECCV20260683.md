## Problem

The prior spatial supersensing benchmark VSI-Super relies on synthetic long videos formed by concatenating random short clips, is restricted to household scenes, and emphasizes object-centric probing while underrepresenting agent- and environment-centered world states. This leaves real-world temporal continuity, scene diversity, and the full cognitively grounded triad of world modeling (agent, objects, environment) underexplored for evaluating whether MLLMs can construct and maintain coherent spatiotemporal world models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260683/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes genuinely long in-the-wild panoramic videos (442 videos, up to 4+ hours, average 38.62 minutes) projected into four orthogonal perspective views (Front/Back/Left/Right), uniformly sampled at 128 frames at 1080P. Models produce either a four-way multiple-choice answer (VMR, VPO, VOO) or a direct integer count (VOC) across 6,980 human-verified Q&A pairs. Notably, VPO query frames are yaw-rotated to different headings to prevent trivial frame matching, and VOO explicitly separates first- vs. last-occurrence subtypes to probe state updating.

## Method

VSI-Super-WILD is constructed via a semi-automatic pipeline: crawling in-the-wild panoramic videos from YouTube across 8 scene categories, generating temporal metadata (object appearances via YOLO-World filtering + SAM3 instance masks) and spatial metadata (time-varying camera poses via Depth-Anything-3), then synthesizing rule-based Q&A pairs with human-in-the-loop verification. The task suite is grounded in neuronal vector coding from cognitive science and probes the agent-object-environment triad through four tasks: VMR (motion orientation recall, agent anchor), VPO (place temporal ordering under viewpoint change, environment anchor), VOO (object temporal ordering with first/last occurrence, object anchor), and VOC (continuous object counting, integrating all anchors). Evaluation uses 128 uniformly sampled frames at 1080P with a Shuffle24 protocol for multiple-choice questions to eliminate option-order bias. The paper benchmarks 13 MLLMs and diagnoses four recurring failure modes—spatial collapse, semantic shortcuts, insufficient update, and instance confusion—revealing that current models default to frame-level 2D semantics rather than maintaining stable, queryable 3D world-state representations.

## Results

On VSI-Super-WILD, the strongest model Gemini-3.1-Pro achieves only 44.36% overall accuracy, while GPT-5.4 reaches 34.52% and the best open-source model Cambrian-S-7B reaches 34.22%. Performance is particularly weak on agent-centric (VMR: best 37.76%) and environment-centric (VPO: best 63.84% for Gemini-3.1-Pro, but only 24–26% for most others) tasks, and the numeric counting task VOC remains challenging even for top models (best open-source: InternVL3.5-8B at 36.74% MRA). Across temporal horizons, the open-source average drops from 35.0% (0–10 min videos) to 26.3% (120+ min videos), confirming that longer streams substantially degrade world-state tracking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, VSI-Super-WILD evaluates 13 MLLMs rather than proposing a new method; the key finding is that all models perform near or below chance on most tasks. Gemini-3.1-Pro is the clear leader at 44.36% overall, driven by a strong 63.84% on VPO (place ordering), yet still lags on VMR (34.21%) and VOC (38.16%). GPT-5.4 (34.52% overall) leads on VMR (37.76%) but trails Gemini-3.1-Pro by ~10 points on VPO. Among open-source models, Cambrian-S-7B (34.22%) and Qwen3.5-9B (33.87%) are the strongest, both excelling on VOO (~40–41%) but dropping to ~25–26% on VMR and VPO. InternVL3.5-8B achieves the best open-source VOC score (36.74% MRA) but is weaker overall (32.18%). Spatial-TTT-nano, despite its streaming design, scores only 27.85% overall, and Qwen2-VL-7B is the weakest open-source model (26.67%), particularly on VOC (18.72%). The consistent pattern across all baselines—stronger object-centric (VOO) performance versus weak agent- and environment-centric (VMR, VPO) performance—indicates a systematic gap in 3D spatial world modeling rather than a single-model deficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends VSI-Super (Cambrian-S), which first formalized spatial supersensing as the ability of MLLMs to maintain an implicit 3D world model from continuous video. The key departures are threefold: replacing synthetic household clips with genuinely long in-the-wild panoramic videos, expanding evaluation from object-centric probing to a cognitively grounded agent–object–environment triad, and eliminating generative editing or clip concatenation. It also positions itself against broader video understanding benchmarks (Video-MME, MVBench, EgoSchema) and spatial reasoning benchmarks, arguing that those rely on linguistic priors and frame-level semantic shortcuts rather than true world-state tracking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a fundamental architectural gap: even frontier models (Gemini-3.1-Pro, GPT-5.4) score near chance (34–44%) on tasks requiring coherent world-state maintenance over long horizons, and the four diagnosed failure modes (spatial collapse, semantic shortcuts, insufficient update, instance confusion) provide a concrete roadmap for what representations must improve. The systematic finding that agent- and environment-centric queries are substantially harder than object-centric ones suggests that current MLLMs lack heading-invariant place representations and stable ego-motion tracking, not merely better object recognition. Open questions include whether streaming architectures, explicit 3D scene graphs, or longer-context training can close this gap, and whether the observed degradation with temporal horizon reflects a fundamental memory bottleneck or a training-data limitation.
