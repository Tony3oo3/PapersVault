## Problem

Existing neighbor-based person Re-ID methods rely exclusively on affinity relations to aggregate neighbor features, which works well only when selected neighbors are reliable. In challenging scenarios—fuzzy queries, small-scale galleries, cross-domain retrieval—noisy neighbors are inevitable, and affinity-only interaction propagates irrelevant or conflicting features, degrading retrieval. No prior method explicitly models the negative influence of false-positive neighbors to keep samples distinguishable from them.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a query image and a set of gallery images (RGB or infrared), each resized to 384×192, and produces enhanced per-image feature vectors via graph message passing over a k-nearest-neighbor graph. Unlike re-ranking methods that only reorder an initial ranking list, ANFI operates at the representation level: each image's feature is updated by interacting with its neighbors' features through both positive (affinity) and negative (discrepancy) weighted edges, yielding a single mixed feature per image that is then used for standard cosine-similarity retrieval.

## Method

ANFI augments the standard affinity relation with a discrepancy relation that assigns negative weights to neighbors, so that noisy neighbors push the query feature *away* rather than pulling it toward them. Discrepancy weights are derived from a novel neighborhood similarity that aggregates pairwise similarities over shared third-party neighbors (N_i ∩ N_j), capturing structural information beyond direct pairwise distance. A sample-wise adaptive mixing module (softmax over two linear projections of the affinity and discrepancy features) determines per-sample how much to trust each relation. For training, Noisy Relation Supervision (NRS) injects controlled noise into features during training to simulate noisy test-time neighborhoods, and a relationship regularization loss (KL divergence between classifier outputs on predicted vs. ground-truth relational features) directly supervises the graph to separate true from false neighbors.

## Results

ANFI achieves the best mAP across all five evaluated datasets: 95.7% on Market1501, 90.7% on CUHK03, 82.9% on MSMT17, 81.1% on SYSU-MM01, and 94.4% on RegDB. On the standard Re-ID benchmarks, ANFI surpasses the strongest single-image method Instruct-ReID by 2.2%/5.3%/10.5% mAP on Market1501/CUHK03/MSMT17, and outperforms the best neighbor-based method GCR by 0.6%/1.7%/6.0% mAP respectively. In the comprehensive benchmark (Table 3), ANFI is the only method with positive ΔmAP in all seven neighbor-distribution settings, with gains ranging from +0.2% (cross-domain M→S) to +5.4% (standard Market1501).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ANFI outperforms all 19 compared baselines across the five datasets. Against the strongest single-image method Instruct-ReID [5], ANFI gains 2.2%/5.3%/10.5% mAP on Market1501/CUHK03/MSMT17, with the largest margin on the hardest dataset (MSMT17). Among neighbor-based methods, ANFI beats GCR [35] by 0.6%/1.7%/6.0% mAP and Cheb-GR [28] by 2.5%/5.3% mAP on Market1501/CUHK03, while also surpassing NFormer [23] by 2.7%/14.3%/20.7% mAP. For re-ranking methods, ANFI exceeds CA-Jaccard [1] by 1.2%/8.6% mAP on Market1501/MSMT17 and K-reciprocal [38] by 1.2%/0.5%/2.1% mAP under the same-backbone protocol (Table 2). In cross-modal Re-ID, ANFI improves over CIFT [13] by 6.3%/3.0% mAP on SYSU-MM01/RegDB and over SAAI [2] by 4.1%/2.6% mAP, demonstrating stronger robustness under the noisier neighbor distributions inherent to visible-infrared retrieval. In the comprehensive benchmark (Table 3), ANFI is the only method maintaining positive ΔmAP in all seven settings (standard, 1-shot, 2-shot, hard query, poor model, M→C, M→S), whereas all other methods degrade in 2–4 settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neighbor-based Re-ID line (NFormer, GCR, Cheb-GR, SFT) that models cross-image interactions via graph message passing with affinity-only positive weights. It departs from this lineage by introducing a complementary discrepancy relation with negative weights, derived from a new neighborhood similarity that leverages shared third-party neighbors rather than pairwise similarity alone. The training strategy (Noisy Relation Supervision) builds on the CIFT cross-modal Re-ID loss but adds noise simulation and relational regularization specifically to handle unreliable neighbor distributions. The paper also positions itself against re-ranking methods (K-reciprocal, CA-Jaccard, ECN) by operating at the feature level rather than the ranking-list level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The comprehensive benchmark (Table 3) revealing that most existing neighbor-based and re-ranking methods actively degrade under noisy neighbor distributions (1-shot galleries, hard queries, cross-domain transfer) is a valuable diagnostic for the field, as standard evaluation protocols with large galleries mask this failure mode. ANFI's plug-and-play design and consistent positive gains across all seven tested distributions make it a practical drop-in improvement for gallery-based Re-ID pipelines. Open questions include whether the discrepancy-relation concept generalizes to other retrieval tasks (object, vehicle, face), whether the neighborhood similarity formulation scales to very large galleries without the kNN bottleneck, and whether the noise simulation strategy transfers to unsupervised or semi-supervised Re-ID settings.
