## Problem

Cross-modal super-resolution on real-world data is hindered by complex spatial misalignments arising from cross-sensor discrepancies, viewpoint variation, and object motion, yet existing self-supervised methods either assume well-aligned inputs or rely on simulated training data and suboptimal alignment strategies that overlook cross-modal dependencies. No prior approach jointly handles unsupervised cross-modal/cross-resolution alignment and faithful source enhancement without training data, ground-truth supervision, or pre-alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260748/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution source image (depth or NIR, 288×320) and a high-resolution RGB guide (576×640 for ×2 or 1152×1280 for ×4) that may be arbitrarily misaligned. The LR source is bilinearly upsampled to the guide's spatial size before feature extraction. The model is optimized online per image pair for 1000 iterations with no training data, no ground-truth SR target, and no pre-alignment step, producing a single HR super-resolved source image at the guide's resolution.

## Method

RobSelf jointly optimizes two components under a self-supervised consistency loss (L1 between downsampled predictions and the LR source). A misalignment-aware feature translator derives a multi-level cross-modal deformation field to warp the guide feature into source-aligned space, then weakly supervises a translation head that mimics the source modality, yielding an aligned guide feature that retains both essential structures and redundant modality-specific content. A content-aware reference filter then computes a gradient-based importance map to discriminatively apply large or small correlation-based kernels on the source pixels, using the aligned guide feature only as a reference for weight determination rather than as a fusion target. This reference-based self-enhancement avoids injecting redundant guide content while recovering fine details. Two variants exist: RobSelf-De uses deformable convolution for alignment (2k² offset dimensions) and RobSelf-Re uses simple spatial resampling (2 offset dimensions), with the latter being faster.

## Results

On real-world misaligned RGB-guided depth SR, RobSelf-De achieves 2.18 RMSE (×2) and 8.6 DSS (×4), outperforming the next-best self-supervised method CMSR (2.53 RMSE, 6.7 DSS) by 14% and 28% respectively. On synthesized misaligned depth SR, RobSelf-De reaches 1.43 RMSE (×4) and 2.49 RMSE (×8), beating all supervised and self-supervised baselines. On real-world RGB-guided NIR SR, RobSelf-Re achieves 3.09 RMSE (×2) and RobSelf-De achieves 7.60 NIQE (×4), surpassing all cross-domain baselines. RobSelf is also up to 15.3× faster than prior self-supervised methods (e.g., 64 s vs. 982 s for P2P on Task III ×2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobSelf outperforms all 13 baselines across all three tasks without requiring training data, ground-truth supervision, or pre-alignment. Among self-supervised methods, CMSR [38] is the closest competitor (2.53 RMSE ×2, 6.7 DSS ×4 on real-world depth), but RobSelf-De improves by 0.35 RMSE and 1.9 DSS; P2P [30], MMSR [12], and SSGNet [40] all require pre-alignment and still trail RobSelf by 0.5–1.6 RMSE. Among supervised depth SR methods, SGNet [49] (2.98 RMSE with pre-alignment), DORNet [47] (3.04), C2PD [19] (2.06 on synthesized ×4), DCTNet [69] (3.14), and DCNAS [72] (3.27) are all surpassed by RobSelf, which uses only 0.70–0.81 M parameters versus 3–65 M for supervised competitors. On the NIR task, cross-domain supervised methods (Fusion-ArF [35]: 6.58 RMSE, Fusion-CAP [26]: 4.40, PAN [8]: 5.19, HSI SR [63]: 4.52) are all outperformed by RobSelf-Re (3.09 RMSE ×2, 7.63 NIQE ×4), demonstrating that the self-supervised misalignment-aware translation generalizes better than domain-specific supervised alignment. Notably, two-stage methods that rely on pre-alignment (via MINIMA [36]) degrade significantly on real-world data due to residual misalignment, while RobSelf handles alignment jointly within the SR optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

RobSelf extends the self-supervised cross-modal SR line initiated by MMSR [12], reusing its consistency-loss formulation and correlation-based kernel weights, but fundamentally repositions alignment from a separate pre-processing step (as in two-stage pipelines using MINIMA or off-the-shelf deformation layers) to a joint weakly-supervised translation subtask co-optimized with SR. It departs from prior self-supervised methods (P2P, CMSR, SSGNet) that either assume well-aligned inputs or rely on suboptimal, modality-agnostic alignment, and from supervised methods (DORNet, SGNet, C2PD) that require large domain-specific training sets. The content-aware reference filter further departs from the dominant fusion paradigm by using the aligned guide solely as a weight-determination reference rather than as a feature to be blended into the source.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a practical gap: real-world multimodal sensors inherently produce misaligned pairs, yet most prior SR methods either ignore this or depend on simulated misalignment and pre-alignment that fails under complex, in-the-wild conditions. The self-supervised, training-free design with a 15.3× speedup makes the approach deployable in settings where no ground truth or labeled data exists, which is the norm for new sensor configurations. Limitations include evaluation on a single device (Azure Kinect DK), relatively small test sets (≤112 groups per modality pair), and restriction to RGB-guided scenarios; open questions include generalization to other modality combinations, larger misalignment magnitudes, and whether the emergent 'structure synthesis' behavior is reliable or an artifact of the weak supervision signal.
