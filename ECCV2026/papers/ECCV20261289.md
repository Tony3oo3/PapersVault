## Problem

Prior work demonstrated that pretrained vision encoders encode image metadata (JPEG compression, camera parameters) in their features, but the origin of this sensitivity remained unexplained. This paper identifies the cause as shortcut learning driven by metadata-semantics correlations naturally present in large-scale pretraining datasets, and shows that the strength of these correlations directly governs how strongly models exploit metadata traces.

## Input / Output

The diagnostic framework operates on standard RGB images processed with controlled metadata parameters (JPEG quality levels, chroma subsampling modes, camera settings). Frozen model features are probed with linear classifiers for metadata prediction (MP), k-NN classifiers for semantic prediction (SP), and retrieval-based metrics for semantic prediction distraction (SPD). The post-hoc mitigation takes a frozen D-dimensional feature vector and produces a transformed D-dimensional representation via a learned linear layer, preserving semantic utility while removing metadata traces.

## Method

The paper first measures metadata-semantics correlations in ImageNet (Cramér's V = 0.047 for IN1k, 0.067 for IN21k) and Re-LAION-2B (via BERTopic clustering of captions), then verifies causality by synthetically introducing controlled correlations at varying strengths (parameter p_i) and showing that metadata sensitivity scales monotonically with correlation strength across ResNet50, ViT, ConvNeXt, and Swin backbones. For mitigation during training, the combination of color jitter, grayscale conversion, and Gaussian blur consistently reduces sensitivity for both processing and acquisition metadata. For post-hoc mitigation, a linear layer f: R^D → R^D is trained adversarially against a metadata predictor h using three loss terms: dot-product preservation with the original feature, contrastive separation of features sharing the same metadata label, and entropy maximization of metadata predictions. A key finding is that mitigation guided solely by JPEG compression parameters generalizes to reduce sensitivity to unseen attributes like resizing and camera model, indicating models exploit a broad class of low-level pixel traces rather than attribute-specific artifacts.

## Results

The paper establishes that JPEG-semantics Cramér's V correlations are 0.047 in ImageNet-1k and 0.067 in ImageNet-21k, and that models pretrained on IN21k consistently show higher metadata prediction (MP) and semantic prediction distraction (SPD) than IN1k counterparts across ConvNeXt, FlexiViT, Swin, and ViT backbones. In controlled experiments, increasing the JPEG-semantics correlation parameter p_i from 0 to 100 monotonically raises MP and SPD while degrading semantic prediction (SP) on the original test set. Post-hoc adversarial mitigation (a single linear layer) reduces metadata sensitivity across CLIP, ConvNeXt, SigLIP, ViT, DINO, and DINOv2 while preserving or slightly improving SP, and improves OOD accuracy (e.g., ImageNet-R: 34.5→37.7, ImageNet-Sketch: 26.4→30.0 for ResNet50). More metadata-sensitive models (p_i=100%) detect generated images at 60.4% average accuracy versus 57.3% for the original IN1k model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an analysis paper without a single proposed method benchmarked against external baselines; comparisons are internal, contrasting training conditions and model families. IN21k-pretrained models outperform IN1k-pretrained counterparts on both MP and SPD across all four architectures (ConvNeXt, FlexiViT, Swin, ViT), confirming that stronger natural correlations (Cramér's V 0.067 vs 0.047) yield more metadata-sensitive features. In the controlled LAION experiments, the stronger-correlation subset (Cramér's V 0.396) produces the highest SPD and lowest SP, while the weaker subset (0.166) shows the opposite, validating the correlation-strength hypothesis under caption supervision. For mitigation, the combination of color jitter + grayscale + blur augmentations (the DINOv2 recipe) most effectively reduces sensitivity to both processing and acquisition metadata, consistent with DINOv2 being among the least sensitive foundational models. Post-hoc adversarial mitigation guided solely by JPEG labels generalizes to reduce sensitivity to resizing, camera make, and aperture as well, and yields the largest OOD gains for the most sensitive models (CLIP-family and supervised ConvNeXt) while sometimes slightly hurting already-robust models like DINOv2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the shortcut learning literature, which has predominantly focused on visible, human-interpretable biases (object-background correlations, texture-vs-shape, color-label associations, watermarks), by identifying invisible pixel-level metadata traces—JPEG compression parameters, camera acquisition settings—as a distinct and previously unexplained source of shortcut learning in vision encoders. It builds directly on Ramos et al. [50], who demonstrated that pretrained encoders encode metadata and that this encoding degrades downstream performance, but left the causal mechanism unclear; here the authors hypothesize and verify that metadata-semantics correlations in pretraining data are the origin. The post-hoc mitigation adapts the adversarial embedding-removal framework of Alvi et al. [1] to specifically target metadata traces, and the analysis of cross-attribute generalization of mitigation deliberately contrasts with Li et al. [32], who found that mitigating one shortcut amplified others.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes the imaging pipeline itself as a systematic source of bias, with practical implications: the authors show that a single linear-layer post-hoc mitigation guided by JPEG labels generalizes to unseen metadata attributes and improves OOD performance on ImageNet-C/R/Sketch, offering a lightweight fix for widely deployed encoders. The dual nature of metadata sensitivity—harmful for semantic generalization yet beneficial for generated-image detection—reframes recent observations about CLIP's forensic utility and suggests that metadata invariance may need to be a first-class training objective. Open questions remain about how deeply metadata sensitivity contaminates standard downstream benchmarks and whether the Stable Diffusion finding (that synthetic images implicitly carry acquisition/processing signatures) extends to other generative architectures.
