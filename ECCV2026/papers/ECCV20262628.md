## Problem

Existing benchmarks for subject-driven text-to-image generation suffer from three critical gaps: insufficient subject diversity (e.g., DreamBench has only 6 categories and 30 subjects), no systematic stratification by subject difficulty or prompt scenario, and reliance on expensive GPT-4o-based evaluation that incurs over $400 per model run while still underperforming human-aligned metrics. These limitations prevent granular diagnosis of where and why models fail, obscuring actionable guidance for future research.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262628/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes a reference subject image (one of 459 curated images across 58 fine-grained categories, split into photorealistic and non-photorealistic domains) paired with a text prompt (one of 5,508 prompts spanning six scenarios: background change, viewpoint/size variation, interaction with entities, attribute change, style transfer, and imagination). Evaluated models produce a single generated image per subject-prompt pair, which is then scored on three dimensions—Subject Preservation (SICS), Prompt Following (CLIP-T), and Image Quality (HPSv2)—and aggregated into a final harmonic-mean score S_h with weights λ=1.5, γ=1.5, μ=1.

## Method

DSH-Bench constructs its dataset via a three-tier hierarchical taxonomy (Photorealistic/Non-photorealistic → Humans/Animals/Objects → 58 fine-grained categories derived from COCO and ImageNet ontologies), collecting 459 images from Unsplash using 400 taxonomy-derived keywords. Each subject image is assigned a difficulty level (easy/medium/hard) based on surface complexity, validated against model-independent metrics (Canny edge density, GLCM contrast, JPEG bits/pixel) showing monotonic increase and significant Spearman correlation. For evaluation, the paper introduces SICS, a subject-preservation metric obtained by fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on 5,000 human-annotated image pairs with explanatory reasoning, which explicitly prioritizes subject-level consistency over global semantics and achieves 9.4% higher Kendall's τ correlation with human judgment than GPT-4o-based DreamBench++ evaluation. The benchmark evaluates 19 models (from optimization-based fine-tuning to encoder-based and closed-source approaches) and derives diagnostic insights by cross-analyzing performance across difficulty tiers, prompt scenarios, and fine-grained categories.

## Results

On the DSH-Bench leaderboard (19 models, 459 subjects, 5,508 prompts), Nano-Banana (closed-source) achieves the highest overall score S_h = 0.272, followed by FLUX.1 Kontext [dev] (0.256) and UNO (0.252) among open-source models. The proposed SICS metric for subject preservation outperforms GPT-4o (DreamBench++'s evaluator) by 9.37% in Kendall's τ and 5.31% in Spearman correlation with human judgments, while being far cheaper than ~20,000 GPT-4o API calls per evaluation. DSH-Bench proves substantially harder than prior benchmarks: most models score lower on DSH-Bench than on DreamBench or DreamBench++ for subject preservation and image quality, confirming that the hierarchical taxonomy sampling better reflects the true data distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For the SICS metric, the paper benchmarks against GPT-4o (DreamBench++), CLIP-B/L, DINO, and DINOv2 on a held-out set of 1,600 image pairs; SICS achieves the highest Kendall's τ (0.677 avg) and Spearman ρ (0.734 avg) across all 17 evaluated models, beating GPT-4o by 9.37% (KDV) and 5.31% (SCV) on average. Among the 19 generation models, a clear trade-off emerges between subject preservation and prompt following: DreamBooth and Textual Inversion excel at prompt following (0.321, 0.299) but rank near the bottom in subject preservation (0.158, 0.109), while FLUX.1 Kontext [dev] and UNO lead in subject preservation (0.424, 0.409) with moderate prompt following. All methods show pronounced degradation on 'hard' difficulty subjects (e.g., objects with fine-grained textures and multi-scale geometric details), and no single model performs well across all 58 categories—'Book' and similar text-heavy categories are consistently the weakest. Compared to DreamBench and DreamBench++, DSH-Bench yields lower subject-preservation and image-quality scores for the majority of models, confirming it is a more challenging and representative evaluation. The interaction-with-entities (IE) prompt scenario is the hardest across all models, while background-change (BC) is the easiest, and attribute-change (AC) prompts are uniformly weak, suggesting a gap in current training data coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the DreamBench lineage (DreamBench → DreamBench++ → DSH-Bench), addressing three specific gaps in DreamBench++: limited subject diversity (6 categories/30 subjects in DreamBench vs. 58 categories/459 subjects here), ambiguous difficulty criteria, and the prohibitive cost of GPT-4o-based evaluation (~20,000 API calls, >$400 per run). The hierarchical taxonomy draws on COCO and ImageNet ontologies, and the SICS metric repurposes Qwen2.5-VL fine-tuned on human-annotated subject-consistency pairs as a cheaper, more human-aligned alternative to both CLIP/DINO embedding distances and GPT-4o scoring. Conceptually, the paper reframes benchmarking from a flat collection of test cases to a stratified diagnostic instrument that disentangles subject complexity from prompt scenario complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark's stratified design (difficulty × scenario × category) enables targeted diagnosis that flat benchmarks cannot provide, and the finding that no model dominates across all 58 categories—combined with the consistent performance cliff on hard subjects—gives the community concrete, actionable failure modes to target. The SICS metric offers a practical efficiency gain over GPT-4o evaluation while improving human alignment, making large-scale re-evaluation feasible. Open questions include whether the difficulty taxonomy generalizes as models improve, how findings extend to multi-subject generation, and whether the observed subject-preservation/prompt-following trade-off is fundamental or an artifact of current architectures.
