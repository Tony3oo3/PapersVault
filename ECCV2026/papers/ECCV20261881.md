## Problem

Classical mesh texturing bakes shading and shadows directly into UV textures, destroying relighting fidelity, while existing neural inverse rendering methods fail to generalize to large-scale, in-the-wild scenes with complex illumination and fine-grained textures. Diffusion-based intrinsic decomposition models offer generalizable albedo priors but produce 3D-inconsistent, detail-poor outputs that cannot support coherent free-viewpoint rendering. The paper bridges this gap by integrating diffusion priors into a 3D-consistent neural texture while recovering fine albedo details from raw imagery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261881/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-reconstructed, UV-parameterized mesh (from off-the-shelf tools like Metashape Pro) and hundreds to thousands of unstructured, unconstrained multiview photographs as input. It produces a hash-encoded neural albedo texture in UV space that is then baked into a standard UV texture map at user-specified resolution (up to 8192² for in-the-wild scenes), directly importable into graphics engines like Blender for physically-based relighting. The entire optimization runs in 5–8 minutes on a single RTX 3090, consuming 0.8–3.3 GB of GPU memory depending on scene scale.

## Method

DANTE-W represents diffuse albedo as a high-resolution 2D multi-resolution hash-encoded neural field in UV space and irradiance as a low-resolution 3D volumetric hash field with per-image embeddings, exploiting the frequency-band discrepancy (V_La >> V_Ls) so that fine spatial details are forced into the albedo component during differentiable rendering. View-space albedo priors from pretrained diffusion models (RGB↔X or DiffusionRenderer) are distilled into the neural texture via rasterization-based supervision, providing strong low-frequency guidance. A physically principled neural rendering loss then recovers high-frequency albedo details from the raw input images, with the frequency separation resolving the inherent albedo-irradiance ambiguity. The full loss jointly optimizes hash entries and MLP decoders with equal weighting (λ=1.0) between the diffusion regularization and rendering terms, and per-image exposure scalars compensate for unconstrained camera response.

## Results

On the GigaLit synthetic benchmark, DANTE-W (with DiffusionRenderer prior) achieves 25.99 dB PSNR and 0.091 LPIPS for diffuse albedo recovery, and 27.77 dB PSNR and 0.071 LPIPS for relighting. This represents +2.63 dB PSNR and ~24% lower LPIPS over the strongest diffusion-based baseline (DiffusionRenderer), and +4.49 dB PSNR over vanilla mesh texturing (Metashape Pro). The method also runs in 5–8 minutes per scene on a single RTX 3090, over 7× faster than Metashape Pro's texturing pipeline while maintaining comparable visual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DANTE-W outperforms all six baselines on both albedo recovery and relighting. Against vanilla mesh texturing methods (Waechter et al., Bi et al., Metashape Pro), the gains are +4.49 dB PSNR in albedo and +3.94 dB in relighting, confirming that baked-in shading severely limits relighting fidelity. Against the strongest diffusion-based renderer, DiffusionRenderer [38], DANTE-W improves albedo PSNR by +2.63 dB and relighting PSNR by +6.13 dB, with ~24–45% lower LPIPS, demonstrating that 3D-consistent neural texture aggregation resolves the per-view hallucinations and detail loss inherent to raw diffusion outputs. RGB↔X [66] performs worse than DiffusionRenderer on both tasks (21.65 vs. 23.36 dB albedo PSNR), and DANTE-W gains +3.85 dB (albedo) and +7.15 dB (relighting) over it. LightSwitch [43] is the weakest baseline by a large margin (16.89 dB albedo PSNR), struggling to generalize to complex, varied illumination and unstructured viewpoints. The ablation without physically principled neural rendering (w/o PR) still beats all external baselines but shows ~0.3–0.5 dB lower PSNR and notably higher LPIPS, confirming the frequency-discrepancy design is critical for recovering fine-grained 3D-consistent details.

## Relation to Prior Work

DANTE-W bridges three research threads: classical mesh texturing (Metashape Pro, Bi et al.), which bakes lighting into UV maps; neural inverse rendering (NerFactor, TensorIR), which struggles to generalize to in-the-wild scenes due to lighting-material ambiguity; and diffusion-based intrinsic decomposition (RGB↔X, DiffusionRenderer), which provides strong albedo priors but lacks 3D consistency and fine detail. The paper's key conceptual move is to lift per-view diffusion albedo outputs into a coherent 2D hash-encoded neural texture, then resolve the residual albedo-irradiance ambiguity by exploiting a frequency-band gap—high-resolution 2D hash for albedo versus low-resolution 3D hash for irradiance—motivated by the known low-frequency nature of irradiance. This positions the method explicitly as a drop-in texturing step within established 3D reconstruction pipelines rather than a standalone inverse rendering system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GigaLit benchmark addresses a real gap: prior intrinsic decomposition and relighting benchmarks are confined to object-centric, structured captures with simple textures, making them ill-suited for evaluating in-the-wild, large-scale scene texturing. The practical pipeline integration (5–8 min per scene, direct UV-map baking, Blender-compatible) lowers the barrier for production use in VR, film, and gaming. However, the method is restricted to near-Lambertian surfaces and does not handle specular materials, limiting its applicability to scenes with glossy or metallic elements. Extending the frequency-separation principle to non-Lambertian BRDFs and to alternative scene representations (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting) is identified as a natural next step.
