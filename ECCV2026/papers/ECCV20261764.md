## Problem

Existing 4D Gaussian Splatting methods for monocular dynamic scene reconstruction rely on appearance-based supervision without physical priors, producing fragmented trajectories, temporal flickering, and non-physical deformations—especially under occlusion. These methods are 'kinematically blind,' modeling how objects appear in each frame rather than how they move, so reappearing objects exhibit abrupt transparency changes and disconnected motion. The paper addresses this gap by embedding momentum continuity as an explicit physical constraint on Gaussian particle dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video sequence along with per-frame depth estimates (from DepthAnythingV2) and optical flow (from Unimatch) as 2D priors; the first frame serves as the canonical reference for back-projecting particles into 3D. Output is a 4D Gaussian Splatting scene organized hierarchically (anchors → momentum-carrying particles → Gaussians) that supports real-time novel view synthesis of the dynamic scene. Evaluation metrics are reported both globally and within dynamic-object regions segmented by U²-Net, and the representation requires only ~25% of the storage of 4DGaussians.

## Method

KineticGS introduces a three-level momentum hierarchy built on the Scaffold-GS/4DGaussians codebase. First, momentum-guided particle initialization computes a per-pixel momentum field (depth-based geometric confidence as mass × residual optical flow as velocity) and adaptively samples particles into high-energy regions, concentrating representational capacity where non-rigid motion occurs. Second, a Learnable Energy-Flow Activation module assigns each particle a smooth double-sigmoid temporal window (learnable center and span via a lightweight MLP on spatial coordinates), gating positional updates as a binary switch and modulating visual attributes continuously, with KL-divergence regularization promoting balanced activation distributions. Third, Holistic Kinetic Synchronization serializes particles along randomly alternating Hilbert and Z-order curves to build spatially coherent neighborhoods, then enforces a coherence loss constraining both location offsets and activation center times among neighbors, suppressing drift without explicit segmentation. All components are jointly optimized under a unified objective combining photometric rendering loss, flow-depth consistency, activation regularization, and kinetic coherence, with a warm-up schedule for momentum-related losses.

## Results

On the DyCheck-iPhone dataset, KineticGS achieves the best overall mPSNR of 14.65 dB, mSSIM of 0.342, and mLPIPS of 0.511, outperforming all baselines. On HyperNeRF-interp, it reaches 27.92 dB mPSNR and 0.828 mSSIM, again leading in photometric and structural metrics. Within dynamic object regions on DyCheck-iPhone (Table 1), KineticGS delivers a +0.91 dB average mPSNR gain (20.12 vs. 19.21) over the second-best method MoDec-GS, with the largest per-scene improvements on Apple (+1.02 dB) and Paper-windmill (+1.48 dB). The method also requires only ~25% of the storage consumed by 4DGaussians.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KineticGS outperforms all four baselines on the DyCheck-iPhone dataset across every metric (mPSNR, mSSIM, mLPIPS). Against the strongest baseline MoDec-GS [17], KineticGS gains +0.23 dB mPSNR and +0.006 mSSIM on DyCheck-iPhone overall, and +0.91 dB mPSNR within dynamic object regions (Table 1). On HyperNeRF-interp, KineticGS leads in mPSNR (+0.14 dB) and mSSIM (+0.001) over MoDec-GS but trails by a negligible 0.002 in mLPIPS (0.221 vs. 0.219), indicating comparable perceptual quality. Against 4DGaussians [41], the gap is larger: +0.41 dB mPSNR on DyCheck-iPhone and +0.33 dB on HyperNeRF. Deformable 3DGS [48] and SC-GS [12] are clearly outperformed, with KineticGS exceeding them by 2+ dB mPSNR on DyCheck-iPhone. The per-frame PSNR analysis (Fig. 4) further shows that KineticGS's advantage is concentrated in high-motion frames, confirming that the momentum-driven design specifically targets the dynamic regions where baselines degrade.

## Relation to Prior Work

KineticGS builds directly on the Scaffold-GS anchor-based hierarchical representation and the 4DGaussians dynamic Gaussian splatting framework, extending them with a momentum-driven physical prior. It departs from prior monocular 4DGS methods (Deformable 3DGS, 4DGaussians, MoDec-GS) that rely on appearance-based supervision and 2D cues (depth, optical flow) without explicit motion modeling, and from PhysGaussian which embeds Newtonian dynamics but requires unavailable material/force parameters. The key conceptual departure is reframing Gaussian primitives as momentum-carrying particles whose dynamics are governed by a three-level hierarchy (energy-weighted sampling, learnable temporal activation, neighborhood kinetic synchronization) rather than by a single deformation MLP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The momentum-based formulation offers a physically interpretable mechanism for maintaining temporal coherence through occlusion without requiring explicit segmentation or material parameters, which is practically valuable for monocular settings where such information is unavailable. However, the method's reliance on the first frame as a canonical reference limits its applicability to scenes with multiple interacting entities or frequent object entry/exit, an open problem the authors acknowledge. The +0.91 dB gain in dynamic regions, while meaningful, is achieved on relatively short sequences, and generalization to longer, more complex open-world videos remains untested. Future work on adaptive canonical representations and handling reciprocal motions would be needed to realize the full potential of the momentum framework.
