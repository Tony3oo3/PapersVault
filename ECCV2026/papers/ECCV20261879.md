## Problem

Large-scale scene digitization inevitably produces unstructured view sampling, where certain regions suffer from view scarcity due to scene complexity, capture cost, or accessibility constraints. Reconstruction-based methods like 3DGS degrade severely under such view scarcity, while generation-based approaches struggle with 3D consistency, controllability, and generalization to complex real-world scenes. No existing method can repair under-observed regions of a 3DGS reconstruction without additional data collection while preserving the quality of well-covered areas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261879/cropped_page2_idx10.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated multi-view images (thousands per scene in GigaNVS) with full camera intrinsics and extrinsics, plus a single target viewpoint pose indicating the worst-reconstructed region. This target pose serves as an anchor for generating virtual viewpoints via interpolation with K nearest training views. The output is a refined 3DGS field renderable from arbitrary viewpoints; the pipeline is iterative, so a second round can target a different under-observed region without degrading previously repaired areas.

## Method

InceptionGS operates in two stages. Stage I adapts a pretrained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) via ControlNet, conditioning it on a G-buffer (planar normals, opacity mask, multiresolution hash features) rendered on-the-fly from the initial PGSR reconstruction, and trains on local image patches to learn the scene-specific geometry-appearance correspondence. Stage II generates virtual viewpoints by interpolating between the target view and nearest training views, then refines the 3DGS field by softly injecting the adapted generative prior: photometric blending warps real training views with visibility-weighted fusion to recover high-frequency texture, while view-adaptive sampling via Langevin Monte Carlo selectively applies generative supervision only where the 3DGS rendering diverges most from the generated appearance, preventing over-exposure of the generative signal from degrading photometric optimization.

## Results

On GigaNVS, InceptionGS achieves PSNR 17.24, LPIPS 0.360, and FID 64.42, outperforming the best diffusion-based baseline Difix3D+ (FID 71.39, LPIPS 0.418) by 10% in FID and 14% in LPIPS. On MipNeRF360, it reaches PSNR 20.72, LPIPS 0.297, and FID 62.78. Compared to the initial PGSR reconstruction (FID 91.27 on GigaNVS), the generative bootstrapping reduces average FID by 32%, demonstrating substantial improvement in visual quality for under-observed regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InceptionGS outperforms all 12 baselines on both GigaNVS and MipNeRF360 across all three metrics. Against the strongest diffusion-based method Difix3D+ [42], it improves FID from 71.39 to 64.42 (10% reduction) and LPIPS from 0.418 to 0.360 (14% reduction) on GigaNVS, while also surpassing it on MipNeRF360 (FID 62.78 vs. 66.73). Among video-diffusion baselines, NVS-Solver [48] and ViewCrafter [50] are the closest competitors but still trail by 15–20 FID points on GigaNVS, reflecting their difficulty generalizing to complex large-scale scenes with unstructured viewpoints. Among per-scene 3DGS optimization methods, 3DGS-MCMC [23] achieves the best PSNR (16.54) but lags significantly in FID (109.92 vs. 64.42), indicating that reconstruction-only methods cannot recover fine appearance in under-observed regions. The ablation rows confirm that the scene-adaptive generative model (removing finetuning drops FID to 176.40) is the dominant contributor, while photometric blending and view-adaptive sampling each provide additional 1–2 FID points of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

InceptionGS positions itself at the intersection of reconstruction-based NVS (3DGS, PGSR) and generation-based NVS (diffusion models), departing from both extremes. It builds on PGSR's planar regularization and G-buffer for geometry quality, and repurposes a pretrained LDM via ControlNet for scene-adaptive appearance generation. The key conceptual departure from prior generative NVS methods (Difix3D+, ViewCrafter, NVS-Solver) is conditioning on 3D-consistent geometry rather than artifact-prone RGB renderings or Plücker embeddings, and from prior reconstruction methods is the 'soft' injection of generative supervision via photometric blending and Langevin Monte Carlo view sampling rather than direct pseudo-label supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuinely practical bottleneck in large-scale scene digitization—unstructured view sampling caused by real-world capture constraints—and provides both a method and a benchmark (simulated on GigaNVS) for evaluating it. The 32% FID reduction and iterative bootstrapping capability (repairing different underobserved regions without degrading previously good areas) have direct practical value for VR/film/gaming pipelines. However, the two-stage pipeline requires ~55 minutes and 30 GB peak VRAM per scene, and the authors acknowledge that test-time training or more efficient optimization schemes are needed for broader scalability. An open question is whether the geometry-conditioned generative prior generalizes to scenes with highly non-rigid or dynamic content where planar assumptions break down.
