## Problem

Existing Gaussian Splatting SLAM systems rely on photometric and geometric residuals that degrade in low-texture and repetitive indoor environments, causing drift, floaters, and inconsistent geometry. Current mitigations—loop closure requiring trajectory revisits and global Manhattan/Atlanta assumptions—are either opportunistic or brittle, and none maintain persistent structural entities that provide reliable geometric constraints throughout mapping.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261869/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes an RGB-D video stream and operates online with incremental keyframe insertion triggered by both Gaussian co-visibility overlap and structural change in dominant planes. Beyond standard 6-DoF poses and a 3D Gaussian map, it outputs Planar Gaussian Instances—persistent planar surfaces each carrying a continuous 3D identity embedding rendered as an additional instance-map channel—and supports novel-view synthesis from the resulting map.

## Method

The core representation augments 3D Gaussians with a continuous plane-instance embedding (a 3-vector supervised via a rendered instance map with edge-aware Charbonnier loss), grouping co-planar Gaussians into persistent Planar Gaussian Instances (PGIs) that are associated across frames through geometric gating and projected IoU matching solved by the Hungarian algorithm. A key mechanism is structure closure: when two non-adjacent keyframes co-observe at least two matched non-parallel PGIs, the relative pose is estimated from plane normals and offsets (refined by multi-plane ICP) and inserted as a pose-graph edge, providing drift correction without requiring image-level revisits. A sliding-window optimizer jointly refines active poses and Gaussian parameters under a combined objective of PGI instance consistency, texture-adaptive photometric-depth loss (Sobel-gated L1 + gradient + SSIM + Charbonnier depth), and planar geometry regularization (flattening and normal-alignment losses), while a small set of Sparse Global Viewpoints anchors the optimization to prevent long-horizon drift.

## Results

On the Replica dataset, the proposed method achieves the best average ATE of 0.22 cm, outperforming LoopSplat (0.26 cm), Loopy-SLAM (0.29 cm), and MonoGS (0.57 cm), and ranks top-1 on 5 of 8 sequences. On TUM RGB-D, it achieves the lowest average ATE of 1.56 cm versus 2.07 cm (Loopy-SLAM) and 2.77 cm (LoopSplat). For novel-view rendering on Replica, the method attains the highest average PSNR of 39.70 dB and best LPIPS of 0.042, surpassing MonoGS (39.31 dB, 0.045) and LoopSplat (36.63 dB, 0.112). In the extreme low-texture sequence fr3/notex_near, the method achieves 16.36 cm ATE while MonoGS degrades to 55.59 cm and LoopSplat fails entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on average ATE across both Replica (0.22 cm) and TUM RGB-D (1.56 cm). Against the closest Gaussian-splatting competitor LoopSplat (0.26 cm on Replica, 2.77 cm on TUM), the advantage is 0.04 cm and 1.21 cm respectively, with the gap widening substantially in low-texture sequences (fr3/notex_near: 16.36 vs. failure). Compared to the loop-closure-based Loopy-SLAM (0.29 cm on Replica), the method achieves a 24% reduction in ATE without requiring image-level revisits. In rendering quality, the method leads MonoGS by 0.39 dB PSNR and 0.003 LPIPS on average, and surpasses LoopSplat by 3.07 dB PSNR and 0.070 LPIPS, indicating that structure-aware optimization improves both geometry and appearance. The largest absolute gains appear in texture-poor scenes (fr3/notex_far: 7.47 vs. 16.63 cm for MonoGS) and in sequences with repetitive layouts where loop closure is unreliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gaussian Splatting SLAM lineage (MonoGS, SplaTAM, GS-SLAM) by promoting planar surfaces from transient tracking cues to persistent, optimizable map entities called Planar Gaussian Instances (PGIs). It departs from loop-closure-based drift correction (LoopSplat, Loopy-SLAM) by introducing 'structure closures'—pose constraints derived from matched non-parallel planes that do not require trajectory revisits—drawing conceptually on classical plane-based SLAM (Structure-SLAM, ManhattanSLAM) but embedding planes directly into the differentiable Gaussian representation. The adaptive RGB-Depth consistency loss and Sparse Global Viewpoints mechanism build on MonoGS's sliding-window optimization while addressing its known weakness in texture-poor regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structure closure mechanism offers a principled alternative to loop closure for drift correction in indoor environments where visual revisits are rare or ambiguous, and the PGI representation provides a clean way to inject structural priors into Gaussian maps without imposing global Manhattan/Atlanta assumptions. However, the approach is inherently dependent on abundant planar structure, limiting its applicability to outdoor or highly non-planar scenes. Open questions include how PGIs behave in dynamic environments, whether the plane-instance embedding generalizes to curved or non-planar surfaces, and whether the method scales to large-scale multi-room or multi-floor mapping without the SGV anchor set growing unboundedly.
