## Problem

The two dominant generative planner paradigms for end-to-end autonomous driving—continuous diffusion policies and discrete token-based VLAs—each suffer from critical weaknesses: diffusion policies exhibit cross-modal misalignment, poor generalization under reward fine-tuning, and limited training efficiency, while token-based VLAs are constrained by autoregressive decoding latency, cumulative error, and irreversible token generation that prevents self-correction. No existing framework combines the parallel efficiency and multimodal alignment of diffusion LLMs with an explicit trajectory refinement mechanism.

## Input / Output

DriveFine takes a single front-view RGB image (partitioned into 8 patches of 384×384 by a SigLIP-384 vision tower) and a textual instruction as input. The output is a discretized driving trajectory where spatial coordinates (longitudinal and lateral, range ±100 m) are quantized into 4,000 bins at 0.05 m resolution and heading angle (±90°) into 1,800 bins at 0.1° resolution, all appended to the LLM vocabulary. At inference, the trajectory is produced via 12 parallel unmasking steps followed by a single refinement step, yielding a complete trajectory in approximately 280 ms with 4-step generation.

## Method

DriveFine builds on the LLaDA-8B masked diffusion LLM, where trajectory tokens are generated by iterative parallel unmasking with bidirectional attention rather than autoregressive decoding. The key architectural contribution is a plug-and-play block-level Mixture-of-Experts: the last 4 transformer blocks of LLaDA are duplicated to form a refinement expert, while the first 28 blocks and the vision tower remain shared with the generation expert. During training, the generation expert is optimized with masked cross-entropy (loss only on masked tokens) plus GRPO reinforcement, while the refinement expert is trained with a hybrid online-offline objective—offline pairwise reward differences among sampled trajectories provide dense zero-mean advantages, and online refined trajectories supply additional exploration signal—with gradients strictly confined to the refinement blocks to prevent cross-task interference. This decoupling preserves the pretrained dLLM's generative capability while injecting a lightweight self-correction stage that can fix collisions, off-road deviations, and decoding outliers in a single refinement pass.

## Results

On NAVSIM v1, DriveFine achieves 90.8 PDMS with standard inference (12 denoising steps + 1 refinement step), surpassing all VLA baselines and matching the strongest diffusion-based planner (ReCogDrive, 90.4). With score-based reinforcement fine-tuning, DriveFine reaches 91.8 PDMS, and under best-of-6 sampling it attains 94.2 PDMS, exceeding the human baseline of 94.8 by only 0.6 points. On NAVSIM v2, DriveFine sets a new SOTA of 89.7 EPDMS, outperforming DiffusionDrive (88.1) and DriveVLA-W0 (86.5). On the out-of-distribution NavHard benchmark, DriveFine achieves 74.4 EPDMS in Stage 1, a 5.5-point improvement over ReCogDrive (68.9) and 7.7 points over DiffusionDrive (66.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveFine outperforms all token-based VLA baselines on NAVSIM v1: it beats AutoVLA (89.1) by 1.7 PDMS, AdaThinkDrive (90.3) by 0.5, DriveVLA-W0 (90.2) by 0.6, and CuriousVLA (90.3) by 0.5, while also surpassing the strongest diffusion-based VLA ReCogDrive (90.4) by 0.4. Under best-of-6 sampling, DriveFine reaches 94.2 PDMS, exceeding AdaThinkDrive† (93.0) and DriveVLA-W0† (93.0) by 1.2 points and AutoVLA† (92.1) by 2.1 points. On NAVSIM v2, DriveFine (89.7 EPDMS) leads DiffusionDrive (88.1) by 1.6 and DriveVLA-W0 (86.5) by 3.2, while also surpassing ReCogDrive (83.6) by a substantial 6.1 points. On the out-of-distribution NavHard benchmark, DriveFine achieves 74.4 EPDMS in Stage 1, outperforming ReCogDrive (68.9) by 5.5 and DiffusionDrive (66.7) by 7.7, demonstrating superior generalization. In Stage 2, DriveFine (41.0) edges out DiffusionDrive (40.5) by 0.5 but trails ReCogDrive (37.8) is actually beaten by DriveFine by 3.2 points. The efficiency analysis shows DriveFine with 4-step generation + 1-step refinement achieves 90.51 PDMS at 280 ms latency, significantly faster than ReCogDrive-8B.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveFine sits at the intersection of two dominant generative planning paradigms in end-to-end autonomous driving: continuous diffusion policies (ReCogDrive, DiffusionDrive) that offer parallel iterative denoising but suffer from cross-modal misalignment and poor generalization under RL, and discrete token-based VLAs (AutoVLA, AdaThinkDrive) that generalize well but are constrained by irreversible autoregressive decoding. The paper departs from both by adopting a masked diffusion LLM (LLaDA) as the base planner, inheriting parallel decoding and bidirectional attention from the diffusion side while retaining the discrete token representation and cross-modal alignment of the VLA side. The key architectural departure is the plug-and-play block-MoE refinement module, inspired by Mixture-of-Transformers, which decouples generation and refinement into separate experts with isolated gradients—a design not present in either prior paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that masked diffusion LLMs can serve as a unified backbone for driving planners, resolving the efficiency-vs-generalization trade-off that has forced practitioners to choose between diffusion heads and autoregressive token decoders. The plug-and-play refinement expert is practically attractive because it can be attached to any pretrained dLLM without retraining the generation path, and the hybrid offline-online RL strategy for the refiner avoids the reward-hacking issues observed in diffusion-based RFT. Open questions include whether the single refinement step generalizes to more complex multi-agent or long-horizon scenarios, whether the approach extends to multi-camera or LiDAR-augmented inputs beyond the single front-view setting, and whether the modest per-component gains (0.4–0.7 PDMS from refinement) compound at scale or in more challenging distributions.
