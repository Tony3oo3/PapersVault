## Problem

Training-free video token pruning for MLLMs faces a fundamental trade-off: query-guided methods are computationally cheap but sacrifice content diversity needed for open-ended dialogue, while diversity-preserving methods must operate on full high-dimensional features (D≥2048), incurring prohibitive N×N similarity costs on long videos. This paper asks whether a compact low-dimensional representation can guide diversity-aware pruning without the overhead of high-dimensional feature matching.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the high-dimensional visual token sequence (D≥2048) produced by the vision projector, along with each token's spatio-temporal coordinates (t, h, w), and outputs a pruned subset of tokens at a user-specified retention ratio (10%–50%). Because the pipeline is entirely query-agnostic, the pruned token set is computed once per video and can be cached and reused across multiple dialogue turns with zero additional pruning cost. In experiments, a 64-frame video at 1344×768 resolution yields 32,256 tokens before pruning.

## Method

P³ (Perceptual Projection Pruning) is a training-free 'Project, Cluster, Prune' pipeline. First, each token's high-dimensional feature is projected into a 3D perceptual space defined by three complementary saliency scores computed from local spatio-temporal neighborhoods: Dynamic Saliency (mean magnitude of local motion vectors), Regional Saliency (center-surround cosine distance with credibility weighting), and Focal Saliency (local reconstruction error via softmax-weighted neighbor interpolation). Second, the video is partitioned into temporal segments (uniform or adaptive). Third, within each segment, tokens are clustered via K-Means on a 6D vector concatenating the three saliency scores with spatio-temporal coordinates, and a stratified sampling strategy allocates the per-cluster token budget proportionally across the three saliency strata, selecting the top-scoring tokens within each stratum. A final global temporal coalescing step removes static tokens (zero motion) that persist across frames, preserving only their first appearance. The key design insight is that this 3D projection captures the essential diversity cues needed for pruning while reducing the clustering and selection cost from O(N²·D) to O(N·K·6), yielding only ~0.10s overhead even on 32k-token sequences.

## Results

On Qwen3-VL-8B at 25% token retention, P³ retains 97.3% of vanilla model performance (avg. across VideoMME, LongVideoBench, MLVU, MVBench), and at 50% retention retains 99.2%. At the aggressive 10% retention setting, P³ still retains 92.8%. On the larger Qwen3-VL-32B model at 25% retention, P³ retains 97.5% and outperforms the strongest query-aware baseline (CDPruner, 96.2%). In terms of efficiency, P³ introduces only 0.10s of pruning overhead, achieving a 4.05× net end-to-end speedup at 25% retention, and an 8.8× speedup on 1024-frame inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P³ is compared against five baselines: DivPrune (iterative diversity sampling), PruneVid† (vision-only pruning), FastV (attention-based query-aware pruning), CDPruner (conditional diversity query-aware pruning), and PruMerge (iterative token merging). On Qwen3-VL-8B at 25% retention, P³ (97.3%) outperforms all query-agnostic baselines—DivPrune (95.1%), PruneVid† (92.5%), FastV (90.6%), and PruMerge (96.6%)—while trailing the query-aware CDPruner (98.1%) by only 0.8 points. On the 32B model, P³ (97.5%) actually surpasses CDPruner (96.2%), suggesting the gap narrows or reverses at scale. On InternVL3.5-8B at 25%, P³ (94.3%) is slightly behind CDPruner (95.3%) and PruMerge (95.4%), indicating model-dependent variance. Critically, P³'s 0.10s pruning overhead yields a 4.05× net speedup, whereas DivPrune (3.43s overhead) and PruMerge (1.88s) are actually slower than the unpruned model (0.57× and 0.96× net speedup), making P³ the only method that is both accurate and genuinely faster end-to-end on long videos.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established paradigms in training-free token reduction: query-aware pruning (FastV, CDPruner) and content-aware diversity preservation (DivPrune, PruneVid, PruMerge). Rather than building directly on any single prior method, it repurposes the classic vision concept of multi-channel saliency detection (motion, center-surround contrast, local reconstruction error) as a compact 3D proxy for the high-dimensional feature space that content-aware methods operate in. The pipeline also draws explicitly on video compression principles (I-frame vs. P-frame redundancy) to motivate its temporal coalescing step, bridging a gap between codec-era redundancy removal and modern MLLM token management.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that iterative pruning/merging methods (DivPrune, PruMerge) become net *slower* than vanilla inference on dense long-video inputs (32K+ tokens) is an important correction to the field's assumptions about these methods' scalability. The query-agnostic, cacheable nature of P3 makes it particularly suited for multi-turn interactive video understanding, a setting where query-aware methods must recompute masks per turn. Key limitations include the inherent information loss from projecting 2048-dim features to 3D (degrading on fine-grained semantic tasks like Charades STA) and the method's video-specificity (S_M degenerates on static images). Open questions include whether intermediate-dimensional projections or learned saliency axes could close the remaining gap to query-aware methods without sacrificing the caching property.
