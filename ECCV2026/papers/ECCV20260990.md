## Problem

Existing depth prior-based 2D-3D correspondence pruning methods formulate the problem as a regular Sim(3) registration with a single global scale, but monocular depth priors inevitably carry per-correspondence noise due to scale ambiguity. This mismatch causes standard Sim(3) solvers to overfit to false-positive inliers and fail under disturbed, unseen, or low-inlier conditions, leaving a gap in robust, non-learning pruning that explicitly accounts for depth noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260990/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of N candidate 2D-3D correspondences (pixel coordinates and 3D point coordinates) produced by a pre-trained I2P matcher (MATR), a monocular RGB image from which metric depth is predicted via Depth Anything v2 (ViT-Large), and the camera intrinsic matrix. It outputs binary inlier/outlier labels for every correspondence and a refined camera pose (rotation R and translation t). The pipeline is entirely non-learning: no additional network is trained, and the depth prior is used solely to back-project 2D pixels into 3D-3D correspondences before geometric pruning.

## Method

Ex-Sim(3)-Reg reformulates correspondence pruning as an extended Sim(3) registration where each inlier obeys p_i = s_i·R·q_i + t with a correspondence-specific scale s_i = s·δ_i that absorbs multiplicative depth noise. Because this yields 2N+6 unknowns (underdetermined), the algorithm approximates the solution by: (i) sampling K scale hypotheses ζ_k around a coarse TEASER-style estimate, (ii) constructing a family of second-order extended compatibility graphs G_SOG(ζ_k) whose edges encode pairwise distance consistency under each ζ_k, (iii) extracting maximum cliques from each graph and applying a scale-correction function φ to convert the inlier candidates into a standard SE(3) problem, and (iv) refining the pose with RANSAC-P3P. A theoretical analysis shows that the union of practical-graph maximum cliques over {ζ_k} approximates the maximum clique of the ideal extended compatibility graph, justifying the heuristic. The full pipeline runs in ~74 ms with K=6, making it practical for real-time I2P registration pipelines.

## Results

On the 7-Scenes dataset under disturbed conditions, Ex-Sim(3)-Reg achieves a mean Inlier Ratio (IR) of 0.548 (+28.2% over baseline) and Registration Recall (RR) of 0.472 (+18.0% over baseline). Under the most challenging low-inlier setting on RGBD-v2, the method reaches 0.542 RR, a 24.7% absolute gain over the baseline. Cross-dataset generalization (7-Scenes → ScanNet) yields 0.635 RR (+20.4%), and integration into existing I2P registration pipelines (e.g., MATR, FreeReg, Top-I2P, MinCD) consistently improves both IR and RR by 10–20 percentage points on ScanNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ex-Sim(3)-Reg outperforms all five regular Sim(3) pruning baselines on both IR and RR across every dataset and condition tested. The strongest regular Sim(3) baseline, Sim(3)-TurboReg, achieves 0.343 mean RR on 7-Scenes (disturbed) versus 0.472 for Ex-Sim(3)-Reg, a 12.9-point gap; Sim(3)-SC2-PCR++ performs worst among baselines (0.073 RR), as its strict inlier criterion is ill-suited to noisy depth priors. On the low-inlier RGBD-v2 setting, Ex-Sim(3)-Reg reaches 0.542 RR while the best baseline (Sim(3)-SC2-PCR) only achieves 0.355. When integrated into existing I2P registration methods on ScanNet, Ex-Sim(3)-Reg improves MATR from 0.842→0.943 RR, FreeReg from 0.780→0.932, Top-I2P from 0.901→0.950, and MinCD from 0.898→0.957, confirming plug-and-play utility. Notably, Sim(3)-MAC fails entirely (N/A) on the ScanNet cross-dataset evaluation due to computational instability under severe outlier contamination, whereas Ex-Sim(3)-Reg remains stable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the depth prior-based correspondence pruning line (Bie et al., Murai et al., Yu et al.), which lifts 2D-3D matches to 3D-3D via monocular depth and casts pruning as Sim(3) registration. The key departure is replacing the single global scale factor with per-correspondence scale perturbations (s_i = s·δ_i), reformulating the problem as an underdetermined 'extended Sim(3)' registration. The algorithmic machinery draws on the compatibility graph / maximum clique framework from SE(3) registration (SC2-PCR, MAC, FastMAC, TurboReg), parameterized by scale hypotheses to bridge the gap between the underdetermined extended problem and a solvable SE(3) subproblem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: monocular depth priors from foundation models are inherently noisy, and this paper shows that ignoring that noise (as all prior Sim(3)-based pruning does) can make pruning actively worse than doing nothing. The 24.7% RR gain across four datasets under disturbed, unseen, and low-inlier conditions, combined with a non-learning ~73 ms runtime, makes this a drop-in improvement for existing I2P pipelines. The stated limitation—that even K→∞ recovers only a subset of true inliers—leaves open whether richer graph constructions or learned scale distributions could close the remaining gap.
