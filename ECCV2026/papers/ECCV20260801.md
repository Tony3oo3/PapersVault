## Problem

Existing pathology foundation models rely on the [CLS] token from tile-level ViTs for slide-level aggregation, which discards spatially informative tokens encoding fine-grained pathological patterns. While retaining all spatial tokens would enrich representations, the quadratic complexity of Transformers makes this computationally prohibitive for gigapixel WSIs (potentially ~1M tokens per slide). The paper tackles the fundamental trade-off between efficiency and representational richness by identifying high redundancy among spatial tokens and selectively retaining only the most informative subset.

## Input / Output

The method consumes whole slide images partitioned into 7168×7168-pixel regions, each further divided into 224×224 tiles processed by a frozen pathology FM (UNI-1 or Conch-1.5) to yield 196 patch tokens per tile. After TokRet retains ~10% of spatial tokens per tile, each region produces ~20k tokens, scaling to ~200k tokens for full-slide inference. Outputs are slide-level predictions: class labels for tumor grading (BRACS), biomarker status (BCNB ER), and gene mutation (TCGA-LUAD TP53), plus disease-specific survival c-index across six TCGA cancer cohorts.

## Method

TokRet is a training-free, plug-and-play token retention module that formulates spatial token selection as a Max-Min Diversity Problem and solves it via greedy iterative selection: starting from the token with maximum average distance to all others, it repeatedly adds the token most dissimilar to the already-selected set, preserving embedding distribution coverage while eliminating redundancy. Built on TokRet, PathTokScale performs slide-level self-supervised pretraining with a DINOv2 multi-view consistency objective over ~2 billion tokens (20× prior WSI studies) using only ~10k public WSIs. To handle the resulting long sequences efficiently, the slide-level Transformer employs block-based sparse attention (inspired by Mixture-of-Block-Attention) where each query computes coarse block-level scores and performs fine-grained attention only within the top-M=4 most relevant blocks of size 512, reducing complexity to near-linear. Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) in 2D enables generalization from 7168×7168 pretraining regions to arbitrary full-slide resolutions during downstream fine-tuning.

## Results

On slide-level classification (Table 1), PathTokScale with Conch-1.5 achieves the best F1 on BRACS (0.767), BCNB ER (0.809), and TCGA-LUAD TP53 (0.725), with AUCs of 0.910, 0.898, and 0.771 respectively, outperforming TITAN (F1: 0.696/0.813/0.702) and GigaPath (F1: 0.677/0.791/0.670) across all three tasks. On survival prediction (Table 2), PathTokScale+Conch-1.5 achieves an average c-index of 0.731 across six TCGA cancer types, matching TITAN (0.729) while using only ~10% of its pretraining slides (30k vs. 330k). The method also shows consistent gains over the [CLS]-only UNI-2+ABMIL baseline (avg c-index 0.620) and PathVQ (avg c-index 0.679) on survival.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PathTokScale+Conch-1.5 outperforms TITAN on BRACS (F1 0.767 vs. 0.696, AUC 0.910 vs. 0.891) and TCGA-LUAD TP53 (F1 0.725 vs. 0.702, AUC 0.771 vs. 0.765), while TITAN retains a narrow edge on BCNB ER (F1 0.813 vs. 0.809, AUC 0.905 vs. 0.898); notably, PathTokScale uses only ~10% of TITAN's pretraining slides. Against GigaPath, the proposed method wins on every classification metric (e.g., BRACS F1 0.767 vs. 0.677) and on survival (avg c-index 0.731 vs. 0.687). Compared to the token-compression baseline PathVQ, PathTokScale improves BRACS F1 by 3.7 points (0.767 vs. 0.730) and TCGA-LUAD F1 by 4.1 points (0.725 vs. 0.684), and on survival the average c-index rises from 0.679 to 0.731. The [CLS]-only UNI-2+ABMIL baseline is clearly surpassed (BRACS F1 0.767 vs. 0.698; survival avg c-index 0.731 vs. 0.620), confirming that retaining spatial tokens is more effective than simply scaling the tile-level ViT. Traditional MIL baselines (ABMIL, CLAM-SB, TransMIL, MambaMIL, GMMamba) are all outperformed by 5–15 F1 points on BRACS, with the largest gap over TransMIL (0.767 vs. 0.592). On survival, CHIEF (avg c-index 0.702) is outperformed by 2.9 points, while prototype-based methods (PANTHER 0.702, OT 0.684) and MIL survival baselines (AttnMISL 0.596, ILRA 0.605) trail by 3–13 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant paradigm in pathology foundation models (TITAN, GigaPath, UNI-2) that scales performance by expanding slide collections and model capacity while relying on [CLS] tokens as tile-level summaries. It repurposes the Max-Min Diversity Problem from combinatorial optimization as a training-free token selection criterion (TokRet), drawing on visual token pruning work (DivPrune, FastV) but replacing attention-based heuristics with a greedy diversity-maximizing strategy. The efficient long-context encoder borrows the coarse-to-fine block routing mechanism from Mixture-of-Block-Attention and Native-Sparse-Attention, adapted for the 20k–200k token sequences arising from WSI regions. The pretraining objective is DINOv2 self-distillation applied at the region level, extending a tile-level SSL framework to the slide level without retraining the frozen tile encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central claim—that the representational bottleneck in pathology FMs is token information loss rather than data or parameter scale—challenges the field's default scaling strategy and, if validated at larger scale, could redirect significant compute budgets. TokRet's plug-and-play design (no retraining of the tile-level FM, no additional data) makes it immediately adoptable, and the 10%-of-TITAN-data result suggests substantial efficiency gains for labs without massive slide archives. Open questions include whether the greedy MMDP approximation remains effective at much higher retention ratios, whether the approach transfers to other gigapixel imaging domains (e.g., aerial, satellite), and whether the frozen-backbone assumption limits ultimate performance compared to end-to-end training. The paper's evaluation, while consistent across three classification tasks and six survival cohorts, is limited in dataset diversity and does not include gene-expression prediction or segmentation, leaving the generality of the token-retention hypothesis partially untested.
