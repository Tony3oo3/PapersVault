## Problem

Autoregressive video diffusion models extended beyond their training horizon suffer progressive identity drift, fidelity degradation, and motion stagnation because sliding-window KV caches irrecoverably discard past context. Existing mitigations—static attention sinks frozen at generation start or discrete attention-score-based token selection—either cannot reflect evolving scene content or converge to a stagnant token set that causes abrupt visual shifts at each cache update. No prior training-free method maintains a smoothly evolving, fixed-size representation of the full generation history.

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt, MemRoPE generates video autoregressively in chunks of 3 latent frames using a 4-step denoising schedule at 480×832 resolution and 16 fps. The KV cache is fixed-size (3 sink tokens, 2 memory tokens, 4 local-window frames) regardless of total generated duration, enabling hour-scale generation without growing memory. The method is entirely training-free and plugs into any pretrained autoregressive video diffusion model (evaluated on Self-Forcing and LongLive built on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B) without architectural modification.

## Method

MemRoPE introduces two co-designed mechanisms. First, Memory Tokens maintain dual exponential moving average (EMA) streams—a long-term stream (α_L=0.01) capturing stable scene identity and a short-term stream (α_S=0.1) tracking recent dynamics—by spatially pooling each evicted frame's keys and values into single vectors and updating both streams continuously. Second, Online RoPE Indexing stores all cached keys in their raw, unrotated form and applies block-relative RoPE indices dynamically at each attention call, mapping the entire cache to [0, C−1]. This position-free storage is essential because RoPE does not distribute over addition, so averaging keys with conflicting rotary phases would produce invalid representations; decoupling position from content makes EMA aggregation well-defined while simultaneously resolving positional extrapolation by keeping all indices within the training range. The three-tier cache (sink, memory, local) is concatenated with the current noisy chunk at every denoising step, and the full pipeline adds only ~3.83 ms and 0.07 GB overhead per step.

## Results

On VBench-Long, MemRoPE achieves the highest average score across all tested durations and base models. At 240 s on Self-Forcing, MemRoPE scores 84.89 average (vs. 83.04 base, 81.66 Deep Forcing, 82.84 ∞-RoPE), and at 240 s on LongLive it reaches 86.22 (vs. 85.33 base, 85.48 Deep Forcing, 85.21 ∞-RoPE). At the 1-hour mark on LongLive, MemRoPE leads all six VBench-Long metrics with an average of 87.99 versus 87.01 for ∞-RoPE. The largest per-metric gains are in Imaging Quality (+6.25 over Self-Forcing base at 240 s) and Aesthetic Quality (+4.54), while ∞-RoPE retains a slight edge on Motion Smoothness and Temporal Flickering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MemRoPE outperforms all five baselines on average VBench-Long score at every tested duration. Against Self-Forcing (the base model), MemRoPE gains +0.98 at 120 s and +1.85 at 240 s, with the gap widening as duration increases. Deep Forcing is the weakest competitor: it actually degrades the Self-Forcing base by 1.38 points at 240 s (81.66 vs. 83.04) due to abrupt visual shifts from its discrete token-selection mechanism, while MemRoPE's continuous EMA aggregation avoids this failure mode. ∞-RoPE is the closest rival on local smoothness metrics (Motion Smoothness, Temporal Flickering) but trails MemRoPE by 1.85–3.12 points on average at 240 s and by 0.98 points at 1 hour, because it discards past context via FIFO eviction rather than maintaining an evolving memory. On LongLive, MemRoPE edges out Deep Forcing by 0.94 (120 s) and 0.74 (240 s) on average, and surpasses ∞-RoPE by 0.87 and 1.01 respectively. In the 2AFC user study, MemRoPE is preferred over Rolling Forcing 82.6% of the time overall and over Self-Forcing 98.3%, confirming the quantitative advantage in perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

MemRoPE builds on autoregressive video diffusion models (Self-Forcing, LongLive) and addresses two bottlenecks in long-horizon generation: KV cache management and positional extrapolation. It departs from the static attention-sink paradigm (inherited from StreamingLLM) and Deep Forcing's discrete Participative Compression by replacing them with a continuous EMA-based dual memory that evolves smoothly as frames are evicted. Its Online RoPE Indexing generalizes Rolling Forcing's partial position-free storage (limited to sink tokens) to the entire cache, and contrasts with ∞-RoPE's approach of re-rotating already-encoded keys by instead applying block-relative RoPE only at attention time. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that RoPE does not distribute over addition, which makes temporal aggregation of position-encoded keys mathematically ill-defined and motivates the position-free caching design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-play method with negligible overhead (~0.6% latency, 0.3% memory), MemRoPE can be immediately adopted by any autoregressive video diffusion pipeline to extend generation from minutes to hours without retraining. The clean mathematical observation that RoPE's non-distributivity over addition precludes averaging rotated keys provides a principled explanation for why prior cache-compression methods cannot perform temporal aggregation, guiding future work in this direction. The main limitation is that EMA aggregation is inherently lossy, bounding precise recall of distant content; incorporating learned or adaptive memory compression is a natural next step. Open questions include whether the dual-rate EMA design generalizes to other positional encoding schemes and whether the fixed memory budget can be made content-adaptive.
