## Problem

Dense 3D image registration is fundamentally ill-posed with multiple plausible deformations, yet existing probabilistic methods are limited: variational inference assumes (often factorized) Gaussian posteriors that cannot capture multi-modality, while MCMC and sampling-based approaches are too slow for high-dimensional dense registration. The expressivity of the posterior is further bottlenecked by the memory cost of full covariance matrices over 1.6 million displacement dimensions, and the resulting uncertainty estimates are poorly calibrated relative to actual registration error.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of fixed and moving brain MRI volumes (160×192×224 at 1 mm³ isotropic resolution, cropped to a 96×72×80 prediction grid of 552,960 voxels) and produces a 3×N_v dense displacement field along with a full posterior distribution over deformations, characterised by 80 importance-resampled candidates drawn from 1,200 initial proposals. The output includes calibrated per-voxel uncertainty (label entropy) that correlates negatively with segmentation error, enabling downstream confidence-weighted use of the registration.

## Method

The core contribution is a Sampled Importance Resampling (SIR) training procedure combined with a novel memory-efficient covariance parameterisation. The covariance is decomposed as Σ = RRᵀ + (LLᵀ)⁻¹, where R ∈ ℝ^{N_v×25} captures global low-rank correlations and L is a sparse lower-triangular Cholesky factor with a 3×3×3 spatial kernel and cross-channel (x,y,z) correlations, making sampling and log-likelihood evaluation tractable via Woodbury's lemma. During training, N_s=1200 samples are drawn from the proposal without gradients, importance-weighted using a Boltzmann NCC likelihood and diffusion prior, then multinomially resampled to N_k=80 high-weight candidates through which gradients flow, decoupling the expensive evaluation pass from the backpropagation pass. A U-Net with separate prediction heads for μ, L, and R is trained with the SOAP optimiser and a dynamic temperature scaling on the importance weights to prevent collapse to a single dominant sample.

## Results

On the OASIS brain MRI registration benchmark (2,450 test pairs, 1.6M-dimensional deformation field), the full model S+LC achieves an Oracle DSC of 0.812 and a resampled-mean DSC of 0.801, with a mean-prediction DSC of 0.794. For uncertainty calibration, S+LC attains an AUSE of 0.0659 and ECE of 0.1064, substantially outperforming the variational counterpart V+LC (AUSE 0.0844, ECE 0.1274) and the 5-model Voxelmorph ensemble (AUSE 0.1008, ECE 0.1224). The reduced-rank variant S+LC 10L further improves calibration to AUSE 0.0636 and ECE 0.1028 while raising Oracle DSC to 0.814, at the cost of increased voxel folding (0.292% vs 0.163%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is a 5-model Voxelmorph ensemble (Vxm5). S+LC outperforms Vxm5 on Oracle DSC (0.812 vs 0.804) and on both calibration metrics (AUSE 0.0659 vs 0.1008; ECE 0.1064 vs 0.1224), with the Spearman correlation between label entropy and DSC shifting from −0.410 (Vxm5) to −0.325 (S+LC), both indicating that higher uncertainty corresponds to lower accuracy. However, Vxm5 produces zero voxel folding while S+LC incurs 0.163% Oracle fold, a trade-off the authors acknowledge. Against the internal variational baseline V+LC, S+LC gains +0.006 Oracle DSC and reduces AUSE by 22% (0.0659 vs 0.0844) and ECE by 16% (0.1064 vs 0.1274). The naive importance-sampling variant I+LC performs essentially identically to the variational baseline, confirming that the resampling step in SIR is what unlocks the calibration gains. The 10-component variant S+LC 10L further narrows the AUSE gap to 0.0636 and ECE to 0.1028, the best calibration in the study, while pushing Oracle DSC to 0.814.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the amortised importance sampling framework introduced by the Importance Weighted Autoencoder (Burda et al., 2015) by adding a resampling step (SIR) that focuses gradient computation on high-weight samples, and combines it with a novel covariance parameterisation that unifies the sparse Cholesky precision structure from Structured Uncertainty Prediction Networks (Dorta et al., 2018) with a low-rank component. It departs from the dominant variational inference paradigm in registration (Simpson et al., 2015; Dalca et al., 2019), which is constrained to unimodal Gaussian posteriors, and from hierarchical approaches like PULPo that couple sampling complexity to network depth. The SIR algorithm itself draws on the classical sampling-resampling perspective of Smith and Gelfand (1992), but its application to a 1.6-million-dimensional dense 3D deformation field is, to the authors' knowledge, novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that importance-weighted resampling with a structured covariance can produce registration uncertainty that is substantially better calibrated than both variational baselines and a 5-model Voxelmorph ensemble, with the uncertainty direction actually correlating negatively with error (i.e., high uncertainty where the model is wrong) rather than positively as in variational models. This is practically important for clinical decision-making where confidence intervals must be trustworthy. Key limitations include increased voxel folding in the more exploratory SIR samples, evaluation on a single dataset (OASIS subset), and the trade-off between calibration quality and topological regularity. Open questions include whether diffeomorphic constraints (e.g., stationary velocity fields) can be integrated without sacrificing the multi-modal posterior, whether the approach generalises to other high-dimensional inverse problems (optical flow, CT reconstruction), and whether post-training optimisation of the temperature parameter can further tighten calibration.
