## Problem

Unified document parsing models that jointly recognize text and formulas typically require billion-scale parameters, incurring high computational cost despite text and formulas accounting for 97.43% of document regions and 87.90% of parsing time. Scaling-law analysis shows these tasks saturate around 0.1B parameters, yet no lightweight model exists that unifies multi-level (character through document) text and formula recognition. Two specific challenges remain unsolved at small scale: structural variability across hierarchical levels and semantic entanglement when a shared tokenizer conflates text and formula tokens.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single RGB document image at native resolution (aspect ratio preserved, dimensions capped at 960×1408 px) and autoregressively generates a token sequence that may contain plain text, LaTeX formulas, or a mix of both. The output is structured with explicit hierarchical markers: line-break tokens (<|ln|>) are stripped and paragraph-break tokens (<|pn|>) are converted to double newlines during post-processing, yielding a clean multi-paragraph text or formula string. The same model handles all five granularity levels (character, word, line, paragraph, multi-paragraph) without task-specific heads.

## Method

UniRec-0.1B uses a FocalNet image encoder (producing a 768-dim feature map at 1/32 spatial resolution) feeding a 6-layer Transformer decoder with cross-attention, trained from scratch on the newly constructed UniRec40M dataset (40M bilingual samples) with a 56,371-token vocabulary. Two key techniques address the small-model challenges: (1) Hierarchical Supervision Training inserts line-level and paragraph-level tokens into the target sequence, explicitly teaching the model spatial layout dependencies that flat-sequence training ignores; (2) a Semantic-Decoupled Tokenizer trains separate BPE tokenizers on plain text and on LaTeX formulas, then merges the formula tokens as special tokens into the text vocabulary so that symbols like 'sum', 'frac', and 'left' carry distinct embeddings per modality, eliminating the cross-modal ambiguity that disproportionately harms sub-1B models. Training uses AdamW (lr 1e-4, batch 64) for 10 epochs with one-cycle scheduling and standard augmentations (rotation, blur, noise) on 8×A800 GPUs (~80 hours).

## Results

On UniRec-Bench, UniRec-0.1B (0.1B params) achieves an average edit distance of 0.100, matching PaddleOCR-VL (0.9B) while using only 11% of its parameters. On formula recognition, UniRec-0.1B (0.134) outperforms PaddleOCR-VL (0.125) and substantially beats Dolphin-1.5 (0.365) and Pix2Tex (0.337). On OmniDocBench, integrating UniRec-0.1B into MinerU2.5 improves full-page edit distance from 0.143 to 0.120 (2.3% gain), and into PaddleOCR-VL from 0.115 to 0.113, reaching new SOTA. Inference speed reaches 0.37 s per block and 6.2 s per page, yielding 5–7× speedup over PaddleOCR-VL and MinerU2.5 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor PaddleOCR-VL (0.9B), UniRec-0.1B matches its average edit distance (0.100 vs. 0.100) while using only 11% of the parameters, and slightly edges it on text (0.038 vs. 0.041) and formula (0.134 vs. 0.125). Compared to Dolphin-1.5 (0.3B), UniRec-0.1B improves formula recognition by 23.1% (0.134 vs. 0.365) and text by 1.2%, attributed to the semantic-decoupled tokenizer. Against larger VLM-based parsers like MinerU2.5 (1.2B) and dots.ocr (3B), UniRec-0.1B achieves substantially lower edit distances on text (0.038 vs. 0.167 and 0.113) and formula (0.134 vs. 0.140 and 0.221). On OmniDocBench full-page parsing, replacing MinerU2.5's recognition module with UniRec-0.1B reduces overall edit from 0.143 to 0.120, and replacing PaddleOCR-VL's module improves it from 0.115 to 0.113 (new SOTA). In inference speed, UniRec-0.1B is >5× faster than PaddleOCR-VL (0.37 s vs. 1.88 s per block) and ~7× faster than MinerU2.5 (6.2 s vs. 42.72 s per page), while remaining competitive in accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'one large VLM unifies all document tasks' paradigm (Nougat, GOT, MinerU2.5, PaddleOCR-VL, dots.ocr) by arguing that text and formula recognition saturate at ~0.1B parameters and should be handled by a dedicated lightweight model, while tables continue to benefit from larger models. Architecturally, it repurposes the standard FocalNet encoder + Transformer decoder framework but introduces two novel training/tokenization techniques—Hierarchical Supervision Training (inserting line/paragraph tokens into labels) and a Semantic-Decoupled Tokenizer (separate vocabularies for text vs. formula)—that, the authors note, are absent from all existing document parsing systems. The 40M-sample UniRec40M dataset and the block-level UniRec-Bench benchmark fill gaps left by prior line-only or page-only resources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: replacing the recognition modules in MinerU2.5 or PaddleOCR-VL with UniRec-0.1B yields 2–9× end-to-end parsing speedup while matching or exceeding accuracy, making high-quality document parsing feasible on edge or low-latency deployments. The 40M multi-level bilingual dataset and the fine-grained UniRec-Bench (character through multi-paragraph, across 9 domains) provide infrastructure the field lacked for evaluating block-level recognition. Open questions include whether the divide-and-conquer strategy generalizes to other document elements (charts, figures), how well the model handles truly degraded or novel layouts beyond its training distribution, and whether the scaling-saturation finding at 0.1B holds as task complexity grows (e.g., multi-page reasoning).
