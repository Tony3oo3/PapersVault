## Problem

Existing audiovisual video captioning models and benchmarks prioritize descriptive completeness but largely overlook the accuracy of dialogue descriptions—specifically, precise utterance transcription and correct speaker attribution. Current models consistently fail to discern 'who said what' in multi-party dialogue scenarios, and no dedicated benchmark exists to systematically evaluate these two dimensions of dialogue fidelity in audiovisual captions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260320/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes audiovisual video clips (segmented to ≤20 seconds via PySceneDetect to fit context windows) and produces free-form audiovisual captions that include structured dialogue descriptions pairing speaker identities with transcribed utterances. Evaluation follows a two-stage protocol: predicted and ground-truth dialogue tuples are first aligned via adaptive-merging dynamic programming to compute an ASR (transcription) score, then speaker consistency within matched pairs is judged by a VLM to yield a REF (attribution) score, both reported as F1 values.

## Method

DiaDem builds on the AVoCaDO 7B base model and applies a two-phase post-training pipeline. For SFT, a dedicated data pipeline exploits complementary Gemini strengths—Gemini-2.5-Pro for high-fidelity utterance transcription and Gemini-3-Pro for speaker attribution correction—to synthesize 70K dialogue-aware captions, supplemented by 15K non-dialogue captions to preserve general captioning ability. For RL, a difficulty-partitioned two-stage GRPO strategy is applied on 3K human-annotated samples: Stage 1 trains on the full filtered set, while Stage 2 doubles the high-difficulty subset to sharpen learning on challenging cases. The GRPO reward combines the REF+ASR dialogue scores with checklist-based and length-regularized rewards, and overly simple samples (where rollout rewards show near-zero variance) are discarded to avoid uninformative gradients.

## Results

On DiaDemBench, DiaDem achieves 65.9 REF (speaker attribution) and 79.3 ASR (utterance transcription), surpassing the best Gemini-series model (Gemini-2.5-Pro: 63.6 REF, 74.8 ASR) by 2.3% and 4.5% respectively. Among open-source models, DiaDem leads by a wide margin over the next-best Qwen3-Omni-Captioner (43.9 REF, 58.8 ASR) and its base model AVoCaDO (38.7 REF, 51.7 ASR). On general audiovisual captioning, DiaDem attains 75.6 average on UGC-VideoCap (outperforming Gemini-2.5-Pro's 72.6 by 2.4%) and 36.5 total error on the video-SALMONN-2 testset, confirming that dialogue-focused training does not degrade holistic captioning quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiaDem outperforms all three Gemini-series models (2.5-Pro, 3-Pro, 2.5-Flash) on both REF and ASR, with the largest gap over Gemini-3-Pro (65.9 vs 63.1 REF; 79.3 vs 71.0 ASR). Against the strongest open-source baseline Qwen3-Omni-Captioner (43.9 REF, 58.8 ASR), DiaDem gains +22.0 REF and +20.5 ASR, and over its own base model AVoCaDO (38.7 REF, 51.7 ASR) it improves by +27.2 REF and +27.6 ASR. On the general captioning benchmark UGC-VideoCap, DiaDem (75.6 avg) surpasses Gemini-2.5-Pro (72.6) by 2.4 points and AVoCaDO (73.2) by 2.4 points, showing no degradation in holistic quality. On the video-SALMONN-2 testset, DiaDem (36.5 total error) is slightly worse than Gemini-2.5-Pro (31.3) but better than AVoCaDO (37.3), indicating a small trade-off in general captioning error while gaining substantially in dialogue accuracy. In challenging sub-scenarios (N≥3 speakers, overlapping speech), DiaDem still lags behind Gemini-2.5-Pro, which the authors identify as a key direction for future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiaDem builds directly on AVoCaDO, a 7B audiovisual captioning model with strong holistic captioning capability, and enhances it through targeted post-training focused on dialogue description. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior audiovisual captioning work (video-SALMONN-2, UGC-VideoCaptioner, Qwen3-Omni-Captioner) that prioritizes descriptive completeness, by treating speaker-utterance attribution accuracy as a first-class objective. The training strategy draws on the speaker diarization literature's modular pipeline (VAD → embeddings → clustering → ASR) but reframes the problem end-to-end within a multimodal LLM, using GRPO with a dialogue-specific reward that combines the paper's own REF/ASR metrics. The data construction pipeline is novel in exploiting complementary Gemini variants (2.5-Pro for transcription, 3-Pro for attribution) to synthesize high-quality training captions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even top commercial models (Gemini-2.5-Pro: 63.6 REF) achieve less than two-thirds of human-level speaker attribution accuracy (97.9), exposing a substantial and previously unquantified gap in a capability critical for downstream narrative understanding and video generation. The finding that a 7B open-source model can surpass the Gemini series on dialogue description through targeted RL suggests that dialogue accuracy is not purely a scale problem but benefits from explicit reward shaping. However, DiaDem still lags behind Gemini in the hardest sub-scenarios (3+ speakers, overlapping speech), and the paper identifies underexplored failure modes (off-screen speakers, small facial regions, audiovisual mismatches) that remain open challenges for the field.
