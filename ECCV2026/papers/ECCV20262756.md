## Problem

Extending 3D Gaussian Splatting to joint RGB-TIR reconstruction is hindered by a fundamental spectral mismatch: RGB encodes reflected illumination while TIR captures emitted radiance governed by the Stefan-Boltzmann law. Existing multi-modal methods treat TIR as an auxiliary intensity channel and enforce a single opacity field across both modalities, which is physically invalid when cross-spectral visibility differs (e.g., glass is transparent in RGB but opaque in TIR), causing gradient interference and thermally implausible reconstructions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized, strictly aligned multi-view RGB-TIR image pairs captured by an iPhone-mounted FLIR One Pro LT stereo camera (ThermoNeRF dataset, 16 scenes at object and building-façade scales). Beyond standard novel-view RGB and TIR images, the output includes per-Gaussian physically interpretable attributes—emissivity, normalized temperature, and reflectance—enabling direct temperature inversion with reported MAE in degrees Celsius, rather than merely regressing grayscale thermal intensity.

## Method

RaViGS factorizes each Gaussian's TIR response into three learnable physical attributes (emissivity, temperature, reflectance) parameterized via spherical harmonics, with per-primitive radiance computed as the sum of a Stefan-Boltzmann emission term and an ambient-reflection term, shifting optimization from heuristic intensity regression to physics-grounded radiometric inference. A dual-opacity rendering mechanism assigns modality-specific opacities (α_rgb, α_ir) to shared geometric primitives, decoupling cross-spectral visibility while retaining joint geometric supervision; a co-support pruning criterion (min(α_rgb, α_ir) < τ) prevents degeneration into modality-specific subsets. Training jointly optimizes photometric reconstruction (L1 + D-SSIM), a Kirchhoff's-law physical prior constraining ε+ρ≤1, RGB-gradient-guided TIR smoothness, and SH coefficient regularization, with a two-stage schedule that first stabilizes shared geometry before decoupling opacity fields.

## Results

On the ThermoNeRF dataset (16 scenes), RaViGS achieves 32.48 dB PSNR, 0.975 SSIM, and 0.068 LPIPS for TIR novel-view synthesis, along with a temperature MAE of 0.472 °C. For RGB reconstruction, it reaches 22.01 dB PSNR, 0.713 SSIM, and 0.337 LPIPS. The method outperforms the strongest TIR baseline (OMMG) by 0.28 dB PSNR and 0.005 SSIM, and surpasses the channel-concatenation baseline (3DGS+IR) by 1.57 dB PSNR and 0.016 SSIM on TIR. On RGB, RaViGS leads 3DGS+IR by 0.13 dB PSNR and Mip-Splatting by 1.48 dB PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RaViGS outperforms all five baselines on TIR PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, with the largest margins over Thermal3DGS (+4.08 dB PSNR) and MSMG (+4.32 dB PSNR). Against the closest competitor OMMG, RaViGS gains 0.28 dB PSNR and 0.005 SSIM but trails on MAE_ROI (1.425 vs. 1.148 °C), suggesting OMMG's coupled radiance regression captures local temperature extremes slightly better in ROI regions. Compared to the channel-concatenation baseline 3DGS+IR, RaViGS improves TIR PSNR by 1.57 dB and LPIPS by 0.094, which the authors attribute to resolving the radiometric mismatch inherent in treating TIR as an auxiliary color channel. On RGB, RaViGS leads 3DGS+IR by 0.13 dB PSNR and Mip-Splatting by 1.48 dB PSNR, validating that the dual-opacity mechanism shields high-frequency RGB textures from low-frequency thermal gradient conflicts. Thermal3DGS, despite its physics-inspired design, underperforms RaViGS by 4.08 dB PSNR on TIR, indicating that its single-modality focus limits cross-spectral geometric regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

RaViGS extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework to joint RGB-TIR reconstruction by introducing two key departures from prior thermal 3DGS methods (ThermalGaussian, Thermal3DGS) and NeRF-based approaches (ThermoNeRF): (1) an Intrinsic Radiative Attribute Factorization that decomposes each Gaussian's thermal response into emissivity, temperature, and reflectance governed by the Stefan-Boltzmann law, rather than regressing TIR as a grayscale intensity channel; and (2) a dual-opacity rendering mechanism that assigns modality-specific opacity fields to shared geometric primitives, resolving cross-spectral occlusion conflicts that single-opacity 3DGS cannot handle. The work also draws on multi-modal NeRF literature for the joint optimization objective but re-grounds the TIR branch in radiative physics (Kirchhoff's law, ambient reflection) rather than treating it as an auxiliary appearance signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a physically motivated gap in multi-modal 3D reconstruction: enforcing a single opacity across RGB and TIR is invalid when spectral transmittance differs (e.g., glass), and the dual-opacity design provides a clean architectural solution within the 3DGS paradigm. However, the physical factorization's benefit for temperature accuracy (MAE) is modest and inconsistent in ablations, and the method assumes opaque, static scenes, leaving translucent materials and dynamic thermal evolution as open problems. The framework is relevant to practical applications in nighttime surveillance, industrial inspection, and disaster response, but its current scope (single dataset, 16 scenes) limits generalizability claims.
