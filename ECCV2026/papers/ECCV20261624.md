## Problem

Existing DPO-based alignment methods for LVLMs rely on data augmentation and preference ranking but fail to address the perceptual gap between vision and language modalities, where the same model produces inconsistent outputs when responding to an image versus a text description of that image. This modality-specific discrepancy distorts preference signals during training, leading to suboptimal alignment and degraded hallucination mitigation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261624/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text instruction/question as input. It internally generates two candidate responses: an image-based response from the LVLM (LLaVA-1.5 with CLIP ViT-L/14@336px encoder) and a description-based response from a language-only model (Llama 2) conditioned on an auto-generated caption of the image. The final output is a preference-aligned text response, and the training data consists of 11.5k self-generated preference pairs extracted from 16k image-instruction pairs in the RLAIF-V dataset, fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 128, alpha 256).

## Method

VLC-DPO constructs preference data and optimizes alignment through a Vision-Language Consistency (VLC) score that combines sentence-level semantic consistency (cosine similarity of NLI-model embeddings between the two responses) and image-text matching (generative likelihood of 'yes' from a CLIP+Flan-T5 model per sentence). A threshold τ=0.7 gates pair selection, and noise injection (mixture of word replacement, masking, and order shuffling) is applied to the lower-scoring response when scores are too close, ensuring a clear reward margin. The DPO loss is modified by scaling the rejected-response log-likelihood term by |w_c|, the absolute difference in VLC scores between the image-based and description-based responses (scaled ×10, capped at 3), so that pairs with larger consistency discrepancies receive stronger penalties, adaptively steering the model toward vision-language-consistent outputs.

## Results

On hallucination benchmarks, VLC-DPO (7B) achieves 83.1% accuracy and 87.2 F1 on AMBER, and 85.2% accuracy on POPE (adversarial), setting new state-of-the-art among open-source methods without ground-truth labels or proprietary feedback. On zero-shot VQA, VLC-DPO (13B) reaches 66.5 on A-OKVQA (a 15.1% relative improvement over the prior best of 57.8) and 59.2 on OK-VQA (a 5.3% gain over the prior best of 56.2). The 7B variant also outperforms its LLaVA-1.5 baseline by large margins (e.g., MMHal-Bench score 2.95 vs. 1.86; POPE accuracy 85.2% vs. 80.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLC-DPO (7B) matches RLAIF-V (7B) on MMHal-Bench score (2.95 vs. 2.95) but surpasses it on POPE accuracy (85.2% vs. 81.6%) and AMBER F1 (87.2 vs. 84.5), while using only a single LLaVA-1.5 feedback pass instead of four LLaVA-NeXT iterations. Against OPA-DPO (7B, GPT-4V feedback), VLC-DPO wins on MMHal-Bench score (2.95 vs. 2.83), hallucination rate (0.33 vs. 0.45), and POPE (85.2% vs. 82.6%), though OPA-DPO retains a slight edge on HalBench mention-level rate (4.25 vs. 5.0). Compared to HSA-DPO (13B, GPT-4/GPT-4V), VLC-DPO (13B) achieves substantially higher MMHal-Bench score (2.98 vs. 2.61) and POPE accuracy (85.6% vs. 84.0%), but HSA-DPO still leads on HalBench response-level (5.3 vs. 10.1) and mention-level (3.2 vs. 5.6) hallucination rates. On zero-shot VQA, VLC-DPO (13B) outperforms the prior best Brote-IM-XXL by 8.7 points on A-OKVQA (66.5 vs. 57.8) and VCTP by 3.0 points on OK-VQA (59.2 vs. 56.2). Overall, VLC-DPO is the strongest open-source method that requires no ground-truth labels or proprietary model feedback, trailing only GPT-4V on MMHal-Bench score (2.98 vs. 3.49) and AMBER F1 (86.8 vs. 87.4) for the 13B variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) to LVLMs by introducing a vision-language consistency signal that serves dual roles: automatic preference data construction and adaptive loss weighting. It departs from prior DPO-based alignment methods (mDPO, OPA-DPO, HSA-DPO, RLAIF-V) that rely on proprietary model feedback (GPT-4V), human annotations, or rule-based criteria for constructing preference pairs, instead using a self-generated VLC score combining semantic consistency (via NLI-based sentence embeddings) and image-text matching (via CLIP+Flan-T5). The description-bridging strategy—converting images to captions and generating text-only responses with Llama 2—follows the caption-bridge idea from Wang et al. (2025) and Li et al. (2024) but repurposes it specifically for creating a second modality signal to measure cross-modal agreement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a fully open-source, self-supervised pipeline (no GPT-4V, no human labels) can match or exceed methods that depend on proprietary feedback for hallucination mitigation, which is practically significant for resource-constrained settings. The 15.1% relative gain on A-OKVQA over prior SOTA is notable, though it compares against architecturally diverse baselines rather than direct DPO variants. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include response-level (not token/object-level) optimization and the issue of shared hallucinations across both responses in some preference pairs, leaving open whether finer-grained consistency signals or object-level rewards would further close the gap. The approach's model-agnosticism and reliance on standard components (CLIP, Flan-T5, NLI models) make it relatively easy to adopt, but generalization beyond LLaVA-1.5 backbones is only briefly validated in the appendix.
