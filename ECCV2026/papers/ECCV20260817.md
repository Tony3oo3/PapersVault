## Problem

Existing video generation models produce high-fidelity clips but cannot handle the complex narrative control required for coherent multi-shot audio-visual generation. Specifically, they suffer from temporal misalignment between audio and visual events, lack controllability over multi-subject vocal timbres, and rely on incomplete scripts that omit audio-related narratives such as speech and sound effects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260817/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MAVIN accepts a free-form text prompt along with optional per-role reference images (for visual appearance) and reference audio clips (for vocal timbre), and produces a synchronized multi-shot video (3–15 s, 24 fps, 480p, 1–6 shots) with its accompanying audio track. A multi-agent scripting pipeline first converts the free-form prompt into hierarchical captions at global, shot, and role levels, explicitly specifying shot boundaries, dialogue intervals with decisecond-level timestamps, and character attributes, which then condition the diffusion process.

## Method

MAVIN builds on the OVI dual-tower diffusion Transformer (video tower + audio tower) trained with flow matching, and introduces three key mechanisms. First, boundary-aware attention replaces standard cross-attention with a hierarchical text-attention layer that uses a binary routing matrix to restrict each latent token's visibility to only the caption tokens whose temporal boundaries contain that token, preventing semantic leakage across shots. Second, ID-aware propagation concatenates learnable role-ID embeddings and a pretrained timbre encoder output with the target latents, while an identity-aware mask in every attention layer ensures reference anchors interact only with their own role's tokens, preventing identity blending. Third, a lightweight three-agent scripting pipeline (structure parser → identity aligner → narrative refiner) transforms free-form user prompts into the structured hierarchical captions. Training follows a three-stage progressive schedule: text-driven generation, single-modality identity anchoring, then joint multi-condition optimization.

## Results

On the 1K-sample MAVINSet benchmark, MAVIN achieves the best score across all 13 evaluated metrics, including FVD of 231.6 (vs. 289.7 for the strongest joint baseline LTX-2), FAD of 6.8, WER of 0.048, and Sync of 6.032. For multi-shot consistency, MAVIN reaches a Shot Transition Accuracy (STA) of 0.9897 and Character Inter-shot Consistency (CISC) of 0.6319, substantially outperforming the next-best joint model LTX-2 (STA 0.5733, CISC 0.6015). The Time Alignment Metric for Speech (TAMS) reaches 0.8104, nearly 1.5× the best baseline (LTX-2 at 0.5343), confirming precise dialogue-boundary adherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAVIN outperforms all 10 baselines across every applicable metric. Among cascaded T2V+V2A models, the strongest visual consistency comes from CineTrans (SC 0.9425, BC 0.9543) and StoryDiffusion+Wan (BC 0.9654), but none can produce intelligible speech (WER/TAMS inapplicable) and their STA scores remain below 0.61, indicating poor shot-transition execution. Among joint T2AV models, LTX-2 is the closest competitor (FVD 289.7 vs. MAVIN's 231.6; FAD 8.6 vs. 6.8), yet MAVIN leads by a wide margin on TAMS (0.8104 vs. 0.5343), STA (0.9897 vs. 0.5733), and CISC (0.6319 vs. 0.6015). OVI, the architectural backbone, shows the largest gap in TAMS (0.4432) and STA (0.4992), highlighting the contribution of boundary-aware attention and the multi-agent scripting pipeline. JavisDiT and UniVerse-1, which lack multi-shot consistency metrics entirely, trail MAVIN on all shared quality and alignment scores (e.g., JavisDiT FVD 512.6 vs. 231.6). The user study further confirms these quantitative gaps: MAVIN receives 36.8% AVQ, 42.2% MIC, 66.4% ANA, and 64.6% VNA preference, versus at most 9.4% for the best cascaded baseline (CineTrans) and 7.8% for the best joint baseline (OVI).

## Relation to Prior Work

MAVIN builds directly on OVI's dual-tower diffusion Transformer for joint audio-visual generation, extending it from single-shot to multi-shot narratives. It departs from the multi-shot video generation line (ShotAdapter, EchoShot, VideoDirectorGPT) by adding synchronized audio generation with vocal timbre control, and from joint AV generation models (JavisDiT, LTX-2, UniVerse-1) by introducing explicit temporal boundary enforcement via boundary-aware attention and hierarchical caption routing. The ID-aware propagation mechanism generalizes prior visual-only identity customization (Video Alchemist, IC-LoRA) to jointly bind appearance and vocal timbre, while the multi-agent scripting pipeline extends prior story-planning agents by incorporating audio-specific narrative elements (dialogue intervals, sound effects) that were previously omitted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAVIN establishes the first end-to-end framework where multi-shot narrative structure, audio-visual synchronization, and multi-subject identity control are jointly addressed, opening a path toward generative models usable in professional filmmaking pipelines. The MAVINSet dataset (800K training samples, 1K verified benchmark) fills a notable data gap for multi-shot audio-visual generation with hierarchical temporal annotations. Key limitations remain: the model is capped at 15 seconds and 3 customized characters, and practical deployment still requires iterative prompting and manual post-processing. Open questions include whether boundary-aware attention scales to longer sequences with more shots, whether the multi-agent scripting pipeline can be replaced by a single end-to-end model, and how the approach generalizes to real-time or interactive generation settings.
