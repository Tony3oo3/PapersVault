## Problem

The Segment Anything Model (SAM) depends on the massive manually annotated SA-1B dataset, creating a fundamental bottleneck for scaling. Existing unsupervised alternatives either infer objectness from static appearance cues alone or overfit to motion-salient regions, failing to generalize to static objects and lacking multi-granularity understanding. No prior method learns a transferable, appearance-driven objectness prior from large-scale unlabeled video that supports segment-anything-style inference on arbitrary images.

## Input / Output

During training, the pipeline consumes ~10,000 hours of unlabeled video (Kinetics-700, BDD100K, YouTube8M) at 512×512 resolution to generate 21 million motion pseudo-labels, then trains the final segment-anything models on only 1% of SA-1B images. At inference, the system accepts a static RGB image (processed via multi-scale sliding windows for high-resolution input) and optionally a single point prompt, producing a set of segmentation masks spanning multiple granularities from whole objects down to parts. The PGM backbone operates at 512×512 with 16×16 patches, while the final Mask2Former and Semantic-SAM models handle full high-resolution images.

## Method

MoSA is a three-stage unsupervised framework. Stage 1 (MGMS) trains two complementary modules on synthetic video—ROSM (SOLOv2 on bidirectional optical flow for rigid instance masks) and MFSM (U-Net for whole moving-foreground masks)—then applies them to real videos, filtering candidates via a combined maskness/sharpness quality score and NMS. Stage 2 trains a Perceptual Grouping Model (ViT-base with 4 parallel linear heads, each assigned to a scale range) using a novel Perceptual Grouping Contrastive Learning (PGCL) loss: for each pseudo-mask, an anchor patch's cosine similarity to all other patches is supervised by a sigmoid-BCE objective, enforcing intra-object compactness and inter-object separability without penalizing unannotated regions as background. Stage 3 transfers the learned objectness prior by running PGM inference (with CRF refinement and multi-scale sliding windows) on 1% of SA-1B images to generate masks, then training a Mask2Former (whole-image) and a Semantic-SAM-based model (point-promptable) on those masks. The key differentiator is PGCL's patch-level contrastive formulation, which avoids both the sparsity failure of standard segmentation losses and the fixed-capacity limitation of slot attention, enabling zero-shot generalization to static, unseen objects.

## Results

On the whole-image segmentation task (measured by Average Recall), MoSA achieves an average AR of 42.1% across seven benchmarks using only a ResNet-50 backbone (23M params) and 1% of SA-1B images, matching the fully supervised SAM (ViT-B, 11M images) on average. MoSA notably surpasses SAM on PartImageNet (52.7 vs. 28.3 AR) and PACO (28.4 vs. 18.1 AR), indicating superior part-level segmentation. On point-based promptable segmentation (COCO), MoSA achieves 41.6% MaxIoU and 63.4% OracleIoU, outperforming the previous unsupervised SOTA UnSAM by +1.3% and +3.9% respectively, while requiring 3.4× fewer parameters and 100× less SA-1B data than supervised SAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoSA outperforms all four unsupervised baselines (FreeSOLO, CutLER, SOHES, UnSAM) on every one of the seven evaluation datasets, with the largest margins over FreeSOLO (+34.8 avg AR) and CutLER (+20.3 avg AR). Against the closest competitor UnSAM (which is not strictly unsupervised due to its CascadePSP refiner), MoSA gains +2.9 avg AR, with particularly strong improvements on LVIS (+4.5), ADE (+2.7), and SA-1B (+6.3). Compared to the fully supervised SAM, MoSA matches the average AR (42.1 vs. 42.1) while using 100× less training data and 3.7× fewer parameters; MoSA decisively wins on part-level benchmarks (PtIn: 52.7 vs. 28.3; PACO: 28.4 vs. 18.1) but trails SAM on whole-entity benchmarks (e.g., COCO: 43.5 vs. 49.6; SA-1B: 48.2 vs. 60.8). On promptable segmentation, MoSA beats UnSAM by +1.3% MaxIoU and +3.9% OracleIoU on COCO, though it remains below supervised SAM (52.1% MaxIoU, 68.2% OracleIoU).

## Relation to Prior Work

MoSA sits at the intersection of two established lines: unsupervised image segmentation (CutLER, UnSAM, SOHES) that relies on DINO-style self-supervised features and clustering, and motion-to-objectness bootstrapping (DyStaB, RCF, DIOD) that extracts object masks from video optical flow. It departs from both by introducing a contrastive grouping objective (PGCL) that decouples the learned 'objectness' concept from the motion signal itself, and by extending motion-based methods from instance-level to multi-granularity segmentation. The final adaptation stage reuses the UnSAM/Semantic-SAM architecture, positioning MoSA as a drop-in replacement for the pseudo-label generation pipeline rather than a new segmentation architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Demonstrating that 10,000 hours of unlabeled video can produce a segment-anything model matching fully supervised SAM (42.1% avg AR) without a single manual annotation is a strong practical result, and the non-saturating scaling curve (16.8%→36.6% AR from 100h→10,000h) suggests further gains are available with more data. The surprising part-level superiority over SAM on PartImageNet (+24.4% AR) hints that motion-derived supervision may capture fine-grained structure that human annotators systematically miss. Open questions include whether the 1% SA-1B image requirement in the final adaptation stage can be eliminated entirely, whether the synthetic-video training of the motion modules is truly necessary, and whether the approach generalizes to video segmentation at inference time.
