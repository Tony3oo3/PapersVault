## Problem

End-to-end pixel-space diffusion models struggle to jointly model low-frequency global semantics and high-frequency spatial details in the high-dimensional raw pixel space. Existing solutions rely on architecturally complex pixel decoders (e.g., DeCo, PixelDiT) that impose significant computational overhead, yet the paper reveals these decoders primarily compensate for optimization difficulties inherent to v-prediction and become largely redundant under the x-prediction paradigm. The key question is whether simple, zero-cost architectural components can replace heavyweight decoders while maintaining or improving generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261461/cropped_page8_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

PixelU takes a class label from ImageNet-1K as conditioning (via in-context class tokens) and generates a full-resolution RGB image at 256×256 or 512×512 directly in pixel space, bypassing any VAE encoder/decoder. Sampling uses 50 Heun steps with classifier-free guidance (CFG scale 4.0) applied over a limited interval, producing images without the lossy compression artifacts inherent to latent-space pipelines.

## Method

PixelU is a minimalist single-stage U-shaped Diffusion Transformer that achieves frequency decoupling through two classic, zero-cost mechanisms: (1) skip connections that route uncorrupted high-frequency spatial details from shallow encoder layers directly to the decoder, replacing complex pixel decoders; and (2) a single spatial down-sampling stage (H/2 × W/2) with strictly constant channel dimension that acts as a natural low-pass filter, compressing deep features into a compact low-frequency semantic manifold. The model is trained under the x-prediction objective within a flow matching framework, where the network predicts the clean image and the loss is computed as a dynamically reweighted MSE on the implied velocity. This design requires only ~1/3 the GFLOPs of the JiT-G baseline while achieving superior FID (1.63 at 256×256, 1.92 at 512×512), with REPA representation alignment applied at the middle block for additional convergence acceleration.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, PixelU-H/16 achieves a FID of 1.63 and IS of 305.88 after 600 training epochs, using only 137 GFLOPs and 1.17B parameters. On ImageNet 512×512, PixelU-H/32 reaches FID 1.92 and IS 322.10 after 600 epochs with 138 GFLOPs. Notably, PixelU surpasses the much larger JiT-G baseline (FID 1.82, 383 GFLOPs, 2B params) while requiring only ~1/3 of the computational cost. At just 320 epochs, PixelU already achieves FID 1.77, outperforming PixNerd-XL/16 (1.95) and PixelFlow-XL/4 (1.98) trained for similar durations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixelU-H/16 outperforms the strongest pixel-space baseline JiT-G (FID 1.63 vs. 1.82) while using only 137 GFLOPs versus 383 GFLOPs (~1/3 the cost) and 1.17B vs. 2B parameters. It also beats DeCo-XL/16 (FID 1.63 vs. 1.69), PixelFlow-XL/4 (1.63 vs. 1.98), and PixNerd-XL/16 (1.63 vs. 1.95) on 256×256, and surpasses JiT/H/32 (1.92 vs. 1.94), DeCo-XL/16 (1.92 vs. 2.22), and PixNerd-XL/16 (1.92 vs. 2.84) on 512×512. Against latent-space baselines, PixelU outperforms DiT-XL/2 (FID 1.63 vs. 2.27) and SiT-XL/2 (1.63 vs. 2.06) with fewer training epochs (600 vs. 1400), but still trails the strongest latent models DDT-XL (FID 1.26) and RAE-XL (FID 1.13) that rely on pre-trained autoencoders. The paper notes that concurrent closed-source methods PixelDiT (FID 1.61) and SiD2 (FID 1.38) report slightly lower FIDs but are excluded from the main table due to unavailable code and training pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixelU builds directly on JiT's x-prediction paradigm and Flow Matching training objective, but departs sharply from the concurrent trend of appending complex pixel decoders (DeCo, PixelDiT, DiP) to flat DiT backbones. The paper's central empirical claim—that these decoders primarily compensate for v-prediction's optimization difficulties and become redundant under x-prediction—motivates a return to classic U-Net principles (skip connections, spatial downsampling) in a drastically simplified, constant-channel, single-stage form. This positions PixelU as a conceptual counterpoint to both the flat DiT lineage (DiT, SiT) and the multi-stage, channel-expanding U-shaped designs (U-ViT, U-DiT, DiT-UNet), arguing that frequency decoupling can be achieved through architectural minimalism rather than additional modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that zero-cost skip connections plus a single constant-channel down-sampling stage can match or beat heavyweight pixel decoders under x-prediction offers a practical, compute-efficient recipe for pixel-space diffusion that is likely to influence future architecture design. However, a notable gap remains: advanced latent-space models with pre-trained autoencoders (DDT at FID 1.26, RAE at 1.13) still outperform PixelU, and the paper acknowledges this without proposing a path to close it. Open questions include whether the single-stage, constant-channel design scales gracefully to even higher resolutions or video, and whether the x-prediction + skip-connection synergy generalizes beyond ImageNet-class-conditional generation to text-to-image or other modalities.
