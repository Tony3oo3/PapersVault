## Problem

AR-Diffusion hybrid models suffer from a dual speed bottleneck: sequential autoregressive token generation and iterative multi-step diffusion denoising in the visual decoder. Existing acceleration methods (speculative decoding for AR, distillation for diffusion) address each bottleneck in isolation without a unifying principle, and distillation-based approaches still require multi-step inference. The paper identifies that per-position prediction entropy in continuous-space AR models simultaneously governs draft prediction quality and local generation difficulty, yet this dual role has not been exploited for joint acceleration.

## Input / Output

The method accepts class labels (ImageNet 256×256) or text prompts (GenEval, MJHQ-30K) as conditioning and produces a single generated image. The AR transformer generates continuous latent features sequentially, and the drifting decoder performs exactly one forward pass (1-NFE) to produce the final image, eliminating all iterative denoising steps. Notably, the per-position entropy signal is computed once during speculative decoding and directly reused as the variance parameter for the decoder's initial Gaussian prior, requiring no additional uncertainty estimation or forward pass.

## Method

Drift-AR unifies two acceleration mechanisms through a shared entropy signal. For AR acceleration, it adapts EAGLE-style speculative decoding to continuous latent spaces via a continuous-feature regression loss and a causal-normalized entropy loss that aligns draft-target entropy distributions, resolving the overconfidence mismatch that causes excessive draft rejection. For visual decoding, it replaces the iterative diffusion decoder with an anti-symmetric drifting field whose initial distribution is an entropy-parameterized Gaussian: the AR feature serves as the prior mean while entropy-derived variance (via a bounded exponential temperature mapping) controls the spread, so high-entropy positions receive stronger drift toward the data manifold and low-entropy positions yield vanishing drift. Training uses a two-phase annealed schedule: Phase I jointly optimizes the AR prior and drifting decoder with a linearly decaying loss weight, then Phase II freezes the AR module and optimizes only the drifting field over a stationary prior, restoring the equilibrium condition q=p ⇒ V=0 that guarantees single-step generation.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, Drift-AR achieves 5.53× speedup on MAR-L (FID 1.76, IS 297.4) and 5.16× on MAR-H (FID 1.53, IS 304.6), while 4.96× on TransDiff-L (FID 1.61, IS 295.8) and 5.06× on TransDiff-H (FID 1.57, IS 298.1), all with genuine 1-NFE visual decoding. On text-to-image (NextStep-1), Drift-AR reaches 3.81× speedup with GenEval 0.66, FID 6.66, and CLIP 29.02, surpassing the original model on all three metrics. The drifting decoder at 1 step matches the 20-step diffusion baseline (FID 1.57 vs. 1.55), whereas vanilla diffusion at 1 step collapses to FID 14.72.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Drift-AR dominates all acceleration baselines on both speed and quality. Against DMD (distribution matching distillation), Drift-AR achieves roughly 2× higher speedup (e.g., 5.53× vs. 2.67× on MAR-L) while improving FID (1.76 vs. 1.81) and IS (297.4 vs. 295.5); DMD still requires multi-step sampling and degrades quality at 1 step (FID 2.93 on TransDiff-H). Against LazyMAR (MAR-specific feature caching), Drift-AR more than doubles the speedup (5.53× vs. 2.32× on MAR-L) and improves FID (1.76 vs. 1.93). Against vanilla speculative decoding (EAGLE-style), Drift-AR achieves 2.7× higher speedup on TransDiff-L (4.96× vs. 1.81×) while avoiding the severe quality collapse that SD causes (IS 295.8 vs. 283.6). On text-to-image with NextStep-1, Drift-AR (3.81×, FID 6.66, CLIP 29.02) outperforms both SD (2.03×, FID 6.90, CLIP 27.96) and DMD (1.66×, FID 8.33, CLIP 25.19) on every metric. Relative to the unaccelerated baselines (MAR, TransDiff, NextStep-1), Drift-AR matches or slightly improves FID/IS/GenEval while delivering 3.8–5.5× end-to-end speedup, confirming that the entropy-unified acceleration does not sacrifice generation fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously independent acceleration strategies for AR-Diffusion hybrids: speculative decoding (adapted from EAGLE [15] to continuous latent spaces with a causal-normalized entropy loss) and single-step generation via anti-symmetric drifting fields [5]. Prior work (MAR, TransDiff, NextStep-1) treats the AR stage and diffusion decoder as separate bottlenecks accelerated by separate techniques (speculative decoding vs. DMD/consistency distillation), while this paper identifies per-position prediction entropy as a shared signal that simultaneously governs draft-target alignment and the physical variance of the drifting prior. The key conceptual departure is replacing the iterative diffusion decoder entirely with a 1-NFE drifting field whose initial distribution is parameterized by AR entropy, eliminating the need for a multi-step teacher or distillation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1-NFE visual decoder matching 20-step diffusion quality (FID 1.57 vs. 1.55 on TransDiff-H) while delivering 3.8–5.5× end-to-end speedup across three architectures is a practically significant efficiency win that removes the iterative denoising bottleneck from AR-Diffusion hybrids without distillation instability. The entropy-as-unifying-signal insight is elegant and zero-cost (computed once during speculative decoding), making the framework broadly applicable to any continuous-space AR model with causal attention. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or higher-resolution generation where spatial entropy patterns are more complex, whether attention-map-based entropy estimation degrades under distribution shift, and whether the two-phase annealed training schedule can be shortened. The reliance on a frozen feature encoder (copy of the target's penultimate layer) as the drifting-space projector is a practical simplification whose optimality remains unexplored.
