## Problem

Existing 3D medical MLLMs for CT report generation rely predominantly on global volume-level representations, which are insufficient for capturing fine-grained regional pathology and can miss clinically critical abnormalities. The paper addresses this by introducing a region-aware framework that explicitly steers the model's attention to diagnostically salient anatomical structures, emulating the hierarchical coarse-to-fine workflow radiologists follow during interpretation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262146/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D chest CT volume standardized to 448×448×128 resolution and an instruction prompt as input. Pseudo-masks for six predefined clinical regions (lungs, airway, mediastinum, heart, musculoskeletal system, abdomen) are generated on-the-fly via TotalSegmentator and drive all three tokenization pathways. The output is a structured radiology report partitioned into those same six organ-specific sections, a format the authors construct by reorganizing unstructured reports from RadGenome-Chest CT and an internal AMC dataset using a DSPy-based NLP pipeline.

## Method

MedRegion-CT processes 3D CT slice-wise through a pre-trained MedSigLIP 2D encoder, then converts the resulting features into three complementary token streams fed to LLaMA-3.1-8B (fine-tuned with LoRA). The Region-based SlowFast Tokenizer splits tokens into a Fast pathway (average-pooled per organ-bearing slice, filtered by a holistic foreground mask to discard background-only slices) and a Slow pathway (region-guided spatial pooling with kernel 2 restricted to masked coordinates), jointly preserving global context and local pathology at reduced sequence length. The Mask-Driven Visual Extractor, adapted from Osprey, pools multi-level features (layers 6, 12, 18, 24) through per-organ volumetric mask pooling and fuses them via independent linear projectors into one condensed token per region, injected at fixed <region> prompt positions. The Lesion Attribute Extractor applies deterministic geometric computation (volume, max bounding-box diameter, spatial location) to lesion pseudo-masks and encodes the results as structured textual prompts, providing quantitative clinical metadata that the vision encoder alone cannot reliably convey. Training proceeds in two stages: first aligning the multimodal connector and mask projector, then fine-tuning on the structured report benchmark.

## Results

On the internal RadGenome-Chest CT benchmark (n=1,551), MedRegion-CT achieves the best scores across all six metrics: BLEU 0.3435, ROUGE 0.4225, METEOR 0.5205, GREEN 0.4555, CRG 0.3959, and GPT-CA 0.4003. On the external AMC benchmark (n=500, zero-shot), it leads on all three LM-based clinical metrics (GREEN 0.1572, CRG 0.3822, GPT-CA 0.2642) while ranking second on NLG metrics (BLEU 0.1275, ROUGE 0.1572, METEOR 0.4467), trailing only RadFM on surface-level lexical overlap. The ablation confirms that the full R+Attr+Mask configuration outperforms every component subset and both external encoder baselines (M3Denc., LITAenc.) on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the internal RadGenome-Chest CT benchmark, MedRegion-CT outperforms all five baselines on every metric, with the largest margins over RadFM (BLEU +0.028, GREEN +0.153) and CT-CHAT (BLEU +0.035, GPT-CA +0.074). The closest competitor is Med3DVLM, which trails by 0.017 BLEU and 0.067 GREEN. On the external AMC zero-shot benchmark, the picture splits: RadFM leads on NLG metrics (BLEU 0.1464 vs. 0.1275, ROUGE 0.1928 vs. 0.1572), but MedRegion-CT dominates all three clinical metrics—GREEN (0.1572 vs. 0.0977 for RadFM, +61%), CRG (0.3822 vs. 0.3476), and GPT-CA (0.2642 vs. 0.2018)—indicating superior clinical faithfulness despite lower lexical overlap. The authors attribute RadFM's NLG advantage to institution-specific phrasing rather than diagnostic accuracy. Among the remaining baselines, Med3DVLM is the strongest clinical competitor externally (CRG 0.3791, GPT-CA 0.2366) but still falls short of MedRegion-CT on all three LM-based scores. The ablation further shows that replacing the Region-based SlowFast Tokenizer with either the conventional LITA SlowFast encoder or the M3D 3D ViT encoder degrades both internal and external performance, confirming the benefit of region-prioritized tokenization over generic 3D encoders.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the SlowFast dual-pathway tokenization strategy from video recognition (Feichtenhofer et al., ICCV 2019) to 3D CT by constraining both fast and slow pathways to organ-bearing regions via pseudo-masks, departing from prior work that applied uniform spatial averaging or indiscriminate slice sampling. It extends the Osprey mask-driven visual extraction framework (Yuan et al., CVPR 2024) from 2D to volumetric CT through a multi-level mask pooling mechanism, and complements these visual pathways with deterministic geometric attribute extraction from segmentation masks—a textual prompting strategy inspired by prior work on diagnosis-driven and quantifiable-attribute prompts. The paper positions itself against a growing family of 3D medical MLLMs (RadFM, M3D, CT-CHAT, Med3DVLM, MedM-VL) that rely predominantly on global volumetric representations, arguing that region-specific encoding is necessary to capture fine-grained pathological details.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured six-region report benchmark addresses a real gap in evaluation: prior 3D CT report generation work lacked standardized, organ-specific ground-truth formats, making cross-method comparison inconsistent. The observation that NLG metrics (BLEU) and clinical metrics (GREEN, GPT-CA) diverge substantially under cross-institutional domain shift is a useful caution for the field, suggesting that surface-level lexical overlap can mask poor clinical fidelity. Key limitations include dependence on upstream segmentation quality (small lesions ≤5 mm³ remain undetected) and the current restriction to chest CT with six predefined regions; extending the framework to other body parts or to open-ended report generation without a fixed section template remains open.
