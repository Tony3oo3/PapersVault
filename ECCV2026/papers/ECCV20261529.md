## Problem

Feed-forward online 3D reconstruction models (e.g., CUT3R, STream3R) regress absolute camera poses relative to a fixed first-frame anchor, forcing extrapolation far beyond their training distribution on long sequences. This causes small feature drifts to accumulate into catastrophic geometric collapse on kilometer-scale trajectories, even though per-frame depth and local geometry remain stable throughout the failure. The paper identifies this decoupling between intact local geometry and broken global pose regression as the root cause.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261529/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming sequence of RGB image frames in an intrinsic-free setting (no camera intrinsics required), processing them incrementally frame-by-frame. It outputs a globally consistent camera pose trajectory, per-frame 3D point cloud reconstructions, and pairwise relative pose estimates between the current frame and multiple historical reference keyframes. The frozen backbone's pointmap head produces local geometry unchanged, while the new relative pose head and PGO backend jointly yield the final trajectory.

## Method

Scal3R reformulates online reconstruction as multi-reference relative pose querying on a completely frozen backbone (CUT3R or STream3R). A shared learnable query token is dynamically conditioned on each reference frame's camera token via a lightweight MLP, and these pose tokens are injected into the frozen decoder through asymmetric attention: pose tokens attend to image features as queries, while image tokens compute self-attention exclusively among themselves, preserving the pretrained pointmap quality. A lightweight MLP head decodes each pose token into a 6D relative SE(3) transformation, supervised with rotation and scale-aligned translation losses. At inference, an online pose-graph optimization (iSAM2) aggregates multi-reference relative constraints with a gap-dependent noise model and Huber robust kernel, while a DINOv2+SALAD-based loop closure detector re-injects past keyframe tokens as additional reference slots to add high-confidence long-range edges. The entire system introduces only ~1% new parameters and converges in 8 hours on a single A100 GPU using 4-view TartanAir training samples.

## Results

On KITTI (11 sequences, 0.4–5.1 km), Scal3R (CUT3R backbone) achieves an average ATE of 69.7 m, reducing error by over 60% compared to the strongest online competitor TTT3R (182.2 m) and by ~66% over the CUT3R backbone (207.2 m). On Virtual KITTI, Scal3R (CUT3R) attains an average ATE of 5.63 m, surpassing all streaming methods by a large margin and approaching the offline method π³ (2.04 m) while remaining fully online. On indoor/synthetic benchmarks, Scal3R achieves state-of-the-art ATE on Sintel (0.168), TUM-Dynamic (0.018), and ScanNet (0.049), and the best Normal Consistency on 7-Scenes (0.579 mean).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Scal3R decisively outperforms all 12 baselines on the primary KITTI benchmark. Against the strongest online competitor TTT3R [10], Scal3R (CUT3R) reduces average ATE from 182.2 m to 69.7 m (−62%), with the gap widening on long sequences (e.g., Seq. 00: 45.3 vs. 178.4 m; Seq. 02: 139.9 vs. 280.8 m). The frozen backbones CUT3R [76] and STream3R [38] suffer catastrophic drift (207.2 and 227.3 m avg. ATE), confirming the paper's diagnosis that global pose regression breaks down while local geometry remains intact. Offline methods (VGGT [74], Fast3R [86], DA3 [45], π³ [80]) either fail entirely on long sequences (OOM/tracking loss) or are restricted to short clips, making them non-competitive on kilometer-scale KITTI sequences. Among streaming baselines, WinT3R [42], StreamVGGT [98], MUSt3R [5], and Point3R [83] all exhibit ATE values 2–4× higher than Scal3R, while MASt3R-SLAM [52] (200.9 m) remains far behind. On vKITTI, Scal3R (CUT3R) achieves 5.63 m avg. ATE, beating TTT3R (25.28 m) by 4.5× and approaching the offline π³ (2.04 m); on Sintel, TUM-Dynamic, and ScanNet, Scal3R sets new state-of-the-art ATE (0.168, 0.018, 0.049 respectively), outperforming all streaming and SLAM baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

Scal3R builds directly on two frozen online 3D reconstruction backbones—CUT3R (persistent-state) and STream3R (causal-transformer)—but fundamentally departs from their global-anchor pose regression paradigm, which anchors every frame to a fixed first-frame coordinate system. Conceptually, it draws from the parameter-efficient prompt-tuning literature (adapters, prefix tokens, soft prompts) and the visual odometry principle that relative formulations generalize better than absolute ones, repurposing both for a 3D reconstruction setting. The asymmetric attention injection mechanism is a novel architectural contribution that allows learnable pose-query tokens to extract geometric cues from the frozen decoder without perturbing the image-token self-attention that produces pointmaps. At the backend, it integrates classical pose-graph optimization (iSAM2) and appearance-based loop closure (DINOv2 + SALAD + FAISS) into the streaming pipeline, bridging the feed-forward 3D reconstruction and SLAM communities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical scalability bottleneck: existing online 3D reconstruction methods suffer catastrophic geometric collapse on kilometer-scale sequences, limiting their practical deployment in autonomous driving and large-scale mapping. The diagnostic observation that per-frame depth remains stable while global pose diverges provides a clear rationale for the frozen-backbone + relative-query design, and the practical efficiency (~1% additional parameters, 8-hour single-GPU training) makes the approach accessible. However, performance remains bounded by the frozen backbone's local geometry quality (occlusion, textureless regions), loop closure relies on appearance matching that can fail under extreme viewpoint or illumination change, and keyframe/loop thresholds are hand-tuned—open questions include whether the approach generalizes to backbones beyond the two tested and whether learned (rather than threshold-based) keyframe and loop detection can further improve robustness.
