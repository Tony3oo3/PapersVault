## Problem

Diffusion models suffer from exposure bias during iterative sampling, where small prediction errors accumulate and shift the distribution away from the target. Prior correction methods assume frequency-uniform errors or rely on hand-designed rules, but this paper reveals that the spectral mismatch between training and inference is frequency-dependent, model-specific, and varies in direction across timesteps, making fixed corrections ineffective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262038/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in add-on to any pretrained diffusion or flow-matching model, consuming the standard inputs (text prompt or class label, initial noise) plus a pre-computed per-channel, per-timestep spectral prior S(t,f) fitted offline from 10K training samples. It produces a generated image (256×256 for pixel-space models, 512×512 or 1024×1024 for LDMs) with the same interface as the base model. The spectral prior is a lightweight parametric model (four scalar parameters per channel per timestep), and the per-step overhead is only 3–4% of total sampling time since it requires no additional network evaluations.

## Method

Spectral Alignment (SPA) is a two-stage, guidance-based calibration method. In the offline stage, the Radially Averaged Power Spectrum (RAPS) of single-step denoised predictions x̂_{0|t} is extracted from training data via FFT and fitted to a parametric power-law model S(t,f) = p_t·f^{−q_t} + r_t·f + s_t per channel and timestep, with cubic spline interpolation for smooth temporal coverage. During inference, at each denoising step the RAPS of the current x̂_{0|t} (computed via Tweedie's formula) is compared to the target spectrum in log-space using an asymmetric MSE loss that penalizes under-shooting the target more strongly than over-shooting. The gradient of this loss with respect to x_t is computed efficiently through the FFT and radial-averaging operations (no backpropagation through the neural network), and x_t is updated by a small step before the standard sampler update. The method is applied after CFG combination and is compatible with any CFG variant or noise-prediction modification.

## Results

On ADM (ImageNet 256×256), SPA achieves FID 7.81 and KID 10.25, outperforming vanilla (FID 9.29) and all three baselines (ε-rescaling 8.00, Time-shift 15.35, Wavelet reg. 8.35). On SDXL, SPA yields the largest relative gain among text-to-image models, improving HPSv3 from 8.426 to 8.829 (~4.5%) and ImageReward from 0.791 to 0.829. On SD3.5, HPSv3 improves from 9.782 to 9.975, and on FLUX.1 [dev] the gain is modest (HPSv3 12.53→12.57 at w=2.5). The method adds only 3.86% computational overhead per denoising step on SDXL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPA outperforms all three baselines on ADM (ImageNet): FID 7.81 vs. ε-rescaling 8.00, Time-shift 15.35, and Wavelet reg. 8.35, while also improving Density (1.226 vs. 1.133 vanilla) and Coverage (0.6184 vs. 0.5939). On SDXL, SPA achieves the largest margin over any baseline (HPSv3 8.829 vs. Wavelet reg. 8.576, ε-rescaling 8.528, Time-shift 8.352), a ~4.5% gain over vanilla. On DDPM (CelebA-HQ), SPA (FID 48.63) is slightly worse than ε-rescaling (48.53), which the authors attribute to the weaker single-step prediction quality of the DDPM model. Time-shift sampling consistently degrades performance across all models (e.g., ADM FID jumps from 9.29 to 15.35; DDPM FID from 49.44 to 90.19), likely due to resolution mismatch with the original experiments. On FLUX.1 [dev], a guidance-distilled model, SPA's gains are smaller and less statistically significant at higher CFG scales (w=3.5), suggesting that guidance distillation partially addresses spectral mismatch during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exposure-bias correction line of work initiated by Ning et al. (ε-rescaling) and Li et al. (time-shifted sampling), which treated the train-inference mismatch as a scalar or frequency-uniform SNR error. Yu et al. first noted frequency-dependent structure via wavelet reweighting, but still relied on hand-designed band corrections. SPA departs from all of these by showing that the spectral mismatch is model-specific, timestep-specific, and directionally inconsistent across architectures, and by replacing fixed rules with a data-driven parametric spectral prior. The guidance mechanism itself is adapted from Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS), repurposed here to enforce a spectral constraint rather than a data-consistency constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPA offers a nearly free (3–4% overhead), training-free plug-in that consistently improves FID, HPSv3, and ImageReward across six architectures spanning pixel-space, latent, and flow-matching models, making it immediately deployable in existing pipelines. The empirical finding that guidance distillation (as in FLUX) only partially eliminates spectral mismatch—especially at lower guidance scales—opens a question about whether distillation objectives should explicitly account for frequency-dependent error. Key limitations include the assumption of a single unconditional spectral prior (no conditioning-dependent spectra), the use of a simple unweighted log-MSE loss, and the reliance on single-step predictions for prior fitting, which may be inaccurate for weaker models like DDPM.
