## Problem

Existing sign language translation methods are trained and fine-tuned in isolation per dataset and language, discarding previously learned representations when adapting to new sign languages. While sign languages differ in grammar and lexicon, they share underlying articulatory structure, yet no prior framework exploits motion-level knowledge transfer across languages to improve translation in low-resource or unseen sign languages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261850/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D skeletal keypoint sequences (x, y, confidence) extracted via RTMPose, partitioned into four anatomical regions (body, left hand, right hand, face), and produces an autoregressively generated translated text sentence. For the secondary recognition task, the same pipeline outputs a discrete sign class label. Notably, the system operates entirely on pose data without RGB frames or gloss annotations, and backbone features can be pre-computed and cached since the ST-GCN experts remain frozen after Stage I.

## Method

SIGNET treats independently pretrained ST-GCN backbones (one per sign language: CSL, ASL, BSL) as frozen visual experts in a Mixture-of-Experts architecture, where each expert encodes distinct motion-level specialisations from its pretraining language. Routing is driven by hand-prior features—temporal mean-pooled outputs of the hand ST-GCN modules—aggregated via scaled dot-product attention into a global expert descriptor that guides a two-layer MLP router. During training, Gumbel-Softmax sampling enables differentiable stochastic expert selection; at inference, deterministic top-k selection (k=2) sparsely activates the most relevant experts. Lightweight LoRA-style adapters (1.5M trainable parameters) apply low-rank residual updates on frozen expert embeddings, and a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss aligns fused visual embeddings with textual embeddings for languages unseen during pretraining. The three-stage pipeline (large-scale pretraining → cross-modal contrastive alignment → downstream fine-tuning) keeps all backbone parameters frozen, requiring only ~24 GPU-hours for downstream adaptation versus 21,504 A100-hours for comparable single-language methods.

## Results

On Phoenix14T (DGS), SIGNET achieves 27.82 BLEU-4 and 53.05 ROUGE-L using only skeletal keypoints, outperforming the prior best gloss-free method PGG-SLT (27.32 BLEU-4) by +0.50. On CSL-Daily, SIGNET reaches 28.51 BLEU-4 and 58.66 ROUGE-L, surpassing Geo-Sign (27.42 BLEU-4) by +1.09. On How2Sign (ASL), SIGNET attains 15.4 BLEU-4 and 49.5 BLEURT, matching the compute-intensive SSVP-SLT-LSP+ (15.5 BLEU-4) while requiring 64× fewer GPUs. On MeineDGS (DGS, unseen during pretraining), SIGNET achieves 2.75 BLEU-4 and 33.58 BLEURT, substantially outperforming the best prior method Sub-GT+GPT (1.55 BLEU-4, 29.72 BLEURT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among gloss-free pose-based methods, SIGNET outperforms PGG-SLT by +0.50 BLEU-4 on Phoenix14T and Geo-Sign by +1.09 BLEU-4 on CSL-Daily, while using only skeletal input (no RGB). On How2Sign, SIGNET (15.4 BLEU-4) edges out Geo-Sign (15.1) and Uni-Sign (14.9), and is within 0.1 BLEU-4 of SSVP-SLT-LSP+ (15.5), which required 64 A100 GPUs for two weeks of pretraining on both YT-ASL and How2Sign. On MeineDGS, where DGS was absent from pretraining, SIGNET (2.75 BLEU-4, 33.58 BLEURT) more than doubles the BLEU-4 of the best prior method Sub-GT+GPT (1.55) and surpasses its BLEURT by +3.86. On WLASL sign recognition, SIGNET achieves 93.26% per-instance accuracy on WLASL100 and 64.87% on WLASL2000, outperforming Uni-Sign (92.25% / 63.52%) and Geo-Sign (63.64% / 61.89%) without any model modifications. The leave-one-expert-out analysis confirms that language-matched backbones dominate when available (e.g., removing the CSL expert drops CSL-Daily BLEU-4 by 6.56), while for unseen languages (DGS) the framework distributes reliance across all three experts.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIGNET builds on the ST-GCN-based sign language backbone lineage (Yan et al., Uni-Sign, Geo-Sign) and the gloss-free SLT paradigm (Sign2GPT, SignLLM, MMSLT), but departs fundamentally from the single-model-per-language training paradigm. Rather than fine-tuning one backbone per dataset, it repurposes independently pretrained backbones as frozen visual experts and introduces a hand-prior-driven routing mechanism that borrows from the Mixture-of-Experts literature (Shazeer et al., Fedus et al.) while adding a domain-specific articulator-level inductive bias absent in standard MoE. The lightweight adaptation layer follows the LoRA formulation (Hu et al. 2022), and cross-modal alignment uses a symmetric InfoNCE objective, but the combination of frozen multi-lingual experts with kinematic routing is novel to the sign language domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that motion-level representations learned from one sign language transfer meaningfully to others, opening a path toward scalable multilingual SLT without requiring massive per-language training. The finding that frozen backbones consistently outperform trainable ones in cross-lingual settings (Table 6) challenges the default assumption that fine-tuning pretrained encoders is always beneficial and suggests a broader principle for cross-domain transfer. Open questions include how the routing mechanism scales beyond three experts, whether the approach extends to sign languages with fundamentally different articulatory structures (e.g., two-handed vs. one-handed dominant systems), and whether the contrastive alignment stage can be made more adaptive to avoid the slight degradation observed on CSL-Daily. The 1.5M trainable parameter budget matching methods requiring 64× more GPUs is a practically significant efficiency result for the field.
