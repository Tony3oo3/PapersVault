## Problem

Deploying high-accuracy on-device gaze estimation for each new AR/VR device generation is costly and slow because hardware changes (camera placement, optics, illumination) invalidate prior models, and collecting labeled real gaze data takes weeks to months. Off-the-shelf visual foundation models like DINOv3 fail to transfer directly to near-eye infrared imagery—their embeddings cluster by subject identity rather than gaze direction—yet they encode a usable gaze signal that could be unlocked with in-domain adaptation. The paper addresses how to rapidly adapt a VFM to the eye-tracking domain using only synthetic labels and unlabeled real images, then compress the result into a deployable on-device model.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a synchronized pair of monochrome near-infrared eye images (left and right) at 320×240 resolution, captured by global-shutter cameras under diffused IR illumination at 20 fps. A shared-weight backbone extracts per-eye features that are concatenated into a binocular representation, from which a regression head outputs four values: yaw and pitch angles for each eye. The final deployed student uses an FBNet backbone with only 256K parameters, suitable for real-time on-device inference.

## Method

DistillGaze operates in two stages. In stage 1, DINOv3 ViT-B (86M params) is adapted via a teacher-student self-distillation loop where the teacher receives weakly augmented images and the student receives strongly augmented images (including MTF filtering, motion blur, glint inpainting, and illumination jitter); the combined loss weights synthetic supervised gaze regression, MSE-based self-distillation between teacher and student embeddings, and pseudo-label supervision from the teacher on unlabeled real data, with the teacher updated via EMA. In stage 2, the optimized VFM is distilled into a 256K-param on-device student using three complementary objectives: VIC-KD feature-space alignment with the VFM teacher, pseudo-label gaze regression from the teacher, and L2 alignment plus pseudo-label supervision from an EMA student, with a cosine schedule that shifts emphasis from teacher distillation to EMA self-distillation over training. The key insight is that DINOv3's subject-clustered embeddings contain smooth within-cluster gaze-correlated gradients, so self-distillation on unlabeled real data reshapes representations toward gaze-relevant features without any real gaze labels.

## Results

On the Project Aria eye tracking benchmark (2,222 participants, no real gaze labels used), DistillGaze achieves 1.44° E50U50 and 8.45° E90U90 with only 256K parameters, reducing median gaze error by 58.6% and tail error by 43.1% relative to the synthetic-supervised on-device baseline (3.48° / 14.84°). The optimized 86M-parameter VFM teacher reaches 1.33° E50U50 and 8.32° E90U90, while the fully supervised upper bound (using unavailable real labels) is 0.82° / 5.91°. The distilled student closely matches the teacher across user percentiles, demonstrating effective knowledge transfer at 336× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DistillGaze (1.44° E50U50) outperforms all on-device baselines: the synthetic-supervised baseline (3.48°), DARE-GRAM UDA (2.96°), and self-distillation without VFM (2.20°). Among distillation methods using the optimized VFM teacher, DistillGaze achieves the best E50U50, beating SP (1.59°), VIC-KD (1.46°), and Community KD (1.48°), though Community KD edges it out on E75U75 (3.24° vs. 3.29°) and pseudo-label-only training achieves the best E90U90 (8.19° vs. 8.45°). Against the 86M-parameter DINOv3 variants, DistillGaze at 256K parameters surpasses DINOv3 SynFT (2.01°) by 28% on E50U50 and DINOv3 linear probe (5.47°) by 74%, while the optimized VFM teacher itself (1.33°) remains the strongest single model. The authors note that tail-metric differences among distillation variants are small because a strong teacher makes direct pseudo-label regression already robust, limiting marginal gains from additional feature-space losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established threads: (1) visual foundation models (DINOv3, SAM3) pretrained on web-scale data, (2) self-supervised representation learning via EMA teacher-student self-distillation (BYOL, DINO, VICReg), and (3) knowledge distillation for model compression (Community KD, VIC-KD). It departs from prior VFM transfer work by showing that off-the-shelf DINOv3 features are fundamentally misaligned for near-eye IR gaze estimation—embeddings cluster by subject identity rather than gaze direction—and by combining synthetic supervision with unlabeled real-data self-distillation to reshape VFM representations before distilling to a 256K on-device student. The synthetic-data-for-ET line (Wood et al., Lin et al.) and UDA methods (DARE-GRAM) serve as baselines, but the paper's key departure is treating the VFM as an optimizable teacher rather than a frozen feature extractor or a direct distillation source.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for AR/VR product development: by eliminating the need for labeled real gaze data, DistillGaze compresses the ET deployment cycle from months to days when hardware changes across device generations. The 58.6% median-error reduction over synthetic-only baselines with no increase in model size is a strong practical result. However, a gap to the fully supervised upper bound remains (especially in the tail, E90U90), and all experiments are on a single device configuration; extending to multi-device generalization, closing the tail gap for atypical users, and exploring online adaptation at inference time are important open questions. The finding that a simple MSE self-distillation objective outperforms the DINO soft-clustering objective for continuous regression is a useful negative result for the SSL community.
