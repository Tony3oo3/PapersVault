## Problem

Existing real-time online 3D scene graph generation methods either rely on computationally expensive SLAM pipelines or approximate each object with a single 3D Gaussian (as in FROSS), which discards fine geometric structure and causes fragile, unstable merging of object candidates across views. This leads to under-merging of thin or planar structures and progressive degradation of the global scene graph over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262285/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of RGB-D frames (one RGB image plus its corresponding depth map and known camera pose per timestep) and incrementally updates a global 3D semantic scene graph. Each object node is represented by a fixed-size set of 256 3D particles in world coordinates rather than a parametric distribution, and the graph contains directed edges labeled with predicate classes. The 2D scene graph per frame is produced by a pretrained RT-DETR-EGTR detector, and the top-20 relationships are retained to reduce edge dropout.

## Method

NoPA replaces FROSS's single-Gaussian object model with a fixed-size non-parametric particle set: pixels within each 2D bounding box are back-projected via depth and camera pose to form 3D particles, over which a kernel density estimate (KDE) is constructed. Online merging uses a two-stage criterion: a constant-time Hellinger distance pre-filter with a margin band resolves unambiguous pairs, while Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) between KDEs handles borderline cases where moment matching is unreliable. After each merge, the union of particles is resampled to a fixed size via MCMC (uniform index sampling plus Gaussian perturbation), preserving geometric support while maintaining constant memory. A relationship propagation step builds affinity clusters from MMD scores and recovers missed predicate edges via majority voting within clusters, improving graph completeness without additional runtime cost.

## Results

On the 3DSSG dataset, NoPA (n=256) achieves 53.2% relationship recall, 69.0% object recall, and 61.4% predicate recall, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline FROSS (25.7% / 60.6% / 30.7%) while maintaining a comparable 27 ms per-frame latency and 1206 MB VRAM. Even with only 128 particles, NoPA (49.9% Rel., 68.5% Obj., 58.5% Pred.) already surpasses all baselines. On ReplicaSSG, NoPA (n=256) reaches 36.9% relationship recall and 39.5% predicate recall versus FROSS's 22.3% and 27.5%, a 65.5% relative improvement in relationship recall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoPA dominates all three baselines on the 3DSSG dataset. Against FROSS (the closest prior method), NoPA more than doubles relationship recall (53.2% vs. 25.7%) and predicate recall (61.4% vs. 30.7%) while adding only 5 ms of latency (27 ms vs. 22 ms) and negligible VRAM overhead (1206 vs. 1204 MB). Against JointSSG, NoPA achieves 2.3× higher relationship recall (53.2% vs. 23.4%) and 2.3× higher predicate recall (61.4% vs. 27.0%), at roughly 1/10th the latency (27 ms vs. 284 ms) and 1/3rd the VRAM (1206 vs. 3252 MB); JointSSG retains a slight edge in predicate mRecall (35.3% vs. 29.4%). Against Kim's framework, NoPA is overwhelmingly superior across every metric (53.2% vs. 0.9% relationship recall) and runs 18× faster (27 ms vs. 488 ms). On ReplicaSSG, NoPA again outperforms FROSS on all recall metrics, with the largest gain in relationship recall (36.9% vs. 22.3%, a 65.5% relative increase).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FROSS, which achieves real-time online 3D scene graph generation by representing each object as a single 3D Gaussian and merging via Hellinger distance. NoPA departs from this parametric formulation by replacing each Gaussian with a fixed-size non-parametric particle set and a kernel density estimate, then redesigning the merging criterion around Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) in a two-stage pipeline (Hellinger pre-filter + MMD for ambiguous pairs). It also introduces a relationship propagation mechanism that clusters objects by MMD affinity and recovers missing predicate edges via majority voting, a component absent from FROSS and other online 3D SSG methods (SceneGraphFusion, MonoSSG, Kim et al.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-time 3D scene graph generation is a prerequisite for deployed robotics, navigation, and autonomous-driving systems, and this work shows that non-parametric object representations can match the speed of parametric Gaussian methods while substantially improving accuracy (nearly 2× relationship recall over FROSS). The key limitation is that performance is capped by the upstream 2D scene graph predictor, so gains in 2D detection and relation quality will directly translate to 3D SSG improvements. Open questions include whether the fixed particle count (256) can be made adaptive per object complexity, how the approach scales to very large or dynamic scenes, and whether the MMD-based merging generalizes to open-vocabulary or multi-modal settings.
