## Problem

High-performance SSL encoders deployed as Encoder-as-a-Service are highly vulnerable to contrastive model stealing attacks (e.g., RDA, ContSteal) that exploit high-dimensional embeddings to replicate target representations at minimal query cost. Existing active defenses—random noise, top-k truncation, rounding, or heuristic distance maximization—fail to achieve a satisfactory privacy-utility Pareto frontier, while passive defenses (watermarks, dataset inference) cannot prevent functional leakage at the time of theft. The paper identifies the absence of a simple, single-module active perturbation defense specifically designed to disrupt contrastive alignment without degrading downstream utility.

## Input / Output

The defense operates per-query: an input RGB image is passed through the frozen target encoder to obtain a feature vector, to which a bounded perturbation δ (‖δ‖∞ ≤ ε, typically ε=0.3) is added before L2-normalization, yielding the returned embedding z_adv. The perturbation is computed dynamically per query via iterative FGSM-style updates over a batch of n features, so the returned embedding is an active, query-specific trap rather than a static noise offset. The output is a single normalized feature vector of the same dimensionality as the original encoder output, indistinguishable in format from an undefended response.

## Method

UniTrap replaces distance-oriented perturbation with explicit global entropy optimization: it minimizes a Gaussian potential function L_uni = Σ_{i≠j} exp(−‖z_i − z_j‖²/t) over all pairs in a batch, which forces the defended embeddings into an isotropic, uniform distribution on the unit hypersphere. The perturbation is computed via dynamic FGSM-style iterative updates (δ^{k+1} = Proj(δ^k − α·sign(∇_δ L_uni))), making each returned embedding an active part of the trap. The key theoretical insight is that under perfect target uniformity, the attacker's contrastive push gradients (from the LogSumExp negative-sample term) integrate to a purely radial vector on the hypersphere; projecting onto the tangent space (Riemannian gradient) yields zero, permanently stalling surrogate optimization. This single-module mechanism achieves a state-of-the-art privacy-utility trade-off, suppressing RDA stolen accuracy from 77.90% to 35.26% while preserving 90.18% target utility.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 against the RDA attack, UniTrap (ε=0.3) suppresses Stolen Accuracy from 77.90% (no defense) to 35.26% while preserving Target Accuracy at 90.18% (vs. 91.20% undefended). Against StolenEncoder on CIFAR-10, SA drops from 74.67% to 36.27%. Across all five evaluation datasets (CIFAR-10, STL-10, SVHN, F-MNIST, CIFAR-100), UniTrap consistently achieves the lowest SA among all defense strategies while keeping TA loss within 1–3% of the undefended baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniTrap outperforms the Distance-Oriented (Cosine) baseline on nearly all dataset–attack combinations: on CIFAR-10 it reduces RDA SA by an additional ~5% (35.26% vs. 40.35%) and StolenEncoder SA by ~8% (36.27% vs. 44.10%), while on SVHN the RDA gap widens to ~16% (25.96% vs. 42.43%). Against traditional static defenses (Top-k, Gaussian Noise, Rounding) evaluated on CIFAR-10, UniTrap achieves a markedly superior privacy–utility Pareto frontier: Top-k must sacrifice TA down to 73.44% to reach a comparable SA of 46.53%, whereas UniTrap holds TA at 90.18% with SA at 35.26%; Gaussian Noise at intensity 0.5 yields SA=40.8% but collapses TA to 71.85%; Rounding fails to push SA below 76% at any setting. The one notable exception is F-MNIST under ContSteal, where UniTrap (82.87%) is slightly worse than Distance-Oriented (73.27%), suggesting the uniformity trap is less effective on this simpler, lower-dimensional feature space. Overall, UniTrap wins or ties on 17 of 20 dataset–attack cells against the Distance-Oriented baseline and dominates all static perturbation baselines on the SA–TA trade-off curve.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniTrap adapts the gradient-targeting perturbation philosophy of Prediction Poisoning (Orekondy et al.) from supervised classifiers to the unlabeled, high-dimensional SSL encoder setting, while grounding its design in the alignment-uniformity framework of Wang & Isola for contrastive representation learning. It departs from prior active defenses (top-k truncation, Gaussian noise, B4B's user-discrimination assumption) by replacing heuristic distance maximization with a principled geometric objective: minimizing a Gaussian potential to enforce isotropic uniformity on the unit hypersphere. The paper positions itself as the first single-module active perturbation defense specifically tailored to contrastive-based encoder stealing (ContSteal, RDA), contrasting with multi-module or passive (watermarking, dataset inference) approaches that cannot prevent functional leakage at query time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tangent-space gradient cancellation argument provides a clean theoretical justification for why uniformity—not distance—is the correct geometric target for disrupting contrastive alignment, offering a design principle that could generalize beyond the specific attacks tested. However, the defense is validated only against contrastive-based stealing paradigms; its robustness against adaptive, non-contrastive, or multi-stage attacks (e.g., BESA's perturbation recovery) remains untested, and the notably weaker results on F-MNIST (SA only dropping to 81.20% under RDA) suggest the method's efficacy may depend on the intrinsic structure of the feature space. Open questions include whether the uniformity trap holds under larger perturbation budgets, deeper architectures, or when the attacker is aware of the defense and adapts their loss function accordingly.
