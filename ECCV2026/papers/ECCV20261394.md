## Problem

3D bounding box estimation for open-world objects remains far from solved due to a profound data-annotation disparity: metric 3D datasets are orders of magnitude smaller than their 2D counterparts, and existing methods are either locked to closed-set taxonomies, require heavy 3D supervision for both detection and localization, or cannot handle heterogeneous depth inputs (sparse point clouds vs. dense maps) and diverse camera models. There is no universal interface that bridges powerful open-vocabulary 2D semantics with metric 3D geometry while remaining agnostic to sensor modality.

## Input / Output

Boxer consumes a sequence of posed, calibrated RGB images (pinhole or fisheye) along with 2D bounding boxes from an off-the-shelf open-world detector (e.g., DETIC, OWLv2, SAM3) prompted with text queries, plus optional depth in the form of a sparse SLAM/SfM point cloud or a dense depth map. The output is a set of globally consistent, de-duplicated, static 7-DoF 3D bounding boxes (center, width, height, depth, yaw) in a gravity-aligned metric world frame, each with a combined 2D+3D confidence score. The 7-DoF representation assumes a gravity estimate from an IMU; extending to full 9-DoF (quaternion) is noted as straightforward.

## Method

BoxerNet is a transformer-based lifting network that decomposes 3D detection into 2D localization (delegated to existing open-world detectors) and metric 3D lifting (the learned component). The encoder extracts DINOv3 image features, per-patch mean depth (supporting both sparse and dense inputs, replacing CuTR's dense-depth-only ViT), and ray encodings for camera calibration, then processes them through a shared self-attention encoder. A cross-attention decoder lets each 2D box token independently attend over all patch tokens (permutation-invariant), feeding two MLP heads: one regressing the 7-DoF box and one predicting an aleatoric uncertainty σ. Training uses a Chamfer loss on box corners weighted by exp(−σ) plus a σ regularization term, trained on 1.22M unique 3D boxes across four device types. A hand-engineered multi-view temporal fusion step merges per-frame detections via 3D IoU gating, text-embedding semantic consistency, confidence-weighted averaging (with circular mean for yaw), and 3D NMS to produce the final scene-level box set.

## Results

With ground-truth 2D boxes and dense depth (GT2D+BoxerNet), Boxer achieves 0.532 mAP on NymeriaPlus, 0.317 on Aria Digital Twin, 0.412 on CA-1M, and 0.585 on Omni3D-SUN-RGBD, substantially outperforming CuTR (0.010, 0.102, 0.250, 0.219 respectively). With a real open-world 2D detector (OWLv2) and depth, Boxer reaches 0.297 mAP on NymeriaPlus and 0.204 on CA-1M, compared to 0.005 and 0.178 for OWLv2+CuTR. After multi-view temporal fusion (per-scene), GT2D+BoxerNet further improves to 0.400 mAP on NymeriaPlus and 0.434 on CA-1M with depth, versus 0.244 and 0.305 for GT2D+CuTR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BoxerNet decisively outperforms CuTR (the closest architectural baseline) across all settings: with GT2D+depth, the gap is 0.532 vs 0.010 mAP on NymeriaPlus (53×), 0.412 vs 0.250 on CA-1M, and 0.585 vs 0.219 on Omni3D-SUN. Against 3D-MOOD, BoxerNet improves per-frame mAP when used as a lifting module (e.g., 0.340 vs 0.271 on Omni3D-SUN RGB; 0.449 vs 0.271 with depth), and also surpasses it as a standalone RGB+D method on CA-1M (0.204 vs 0.174 for OWLv2). Compared to CubeRCNN, BoxerNet yields consistent gains (0.290 vs 0.264 on Omni3D-SUN with depth; 0.204 vs 0.104 on CA-1M). For multi-view fusion, Boxer's clustering+averaging approach beats BoxFusion on CA-1M (0.204 vs 0.176 mAP; 0.547 vs 0.321 P@0.2; 0.421 vs 0.309 R@0.2). The one setting where BoxerNet does not clearly dominate is image-only on Omni3D-SUN, where 3D-MOOD+BoxerNet (0.340) and CubeRCNN+BoxerNet (0.286) are competitive, reflecting that closed-set 3D detectors benefit from dedicated 3D supervision on that specific benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

Boxer extends the CuTR [20] DETR-style 3DBB lifting architecture by replacing its pixel-level dense-depth ViT with a flexible mean-depth patch encoder that supports both sparse point clouds and dense depth, adding a factorized aleatoric uncertainty head, and scaling training to over 1.2M unique 3DBBs across four device types. It shares the 2D-to-3D lifting paradigm with 3D-MOOD [48] and DetAny3D [50] but departs by conditioning on optional metric-scale depth from calibrated sensors rather than computing dense depth internally. The pipeline also introduces a hand-engineered multi-view temporal fusion step (3D IoU + semantic clustering + NMS) that is absent from prior single-frame lifting methods, and it deliberately decouples detection from lifting by delegating 2D localization to off-the-shelf open-vocabulary detectors (DETIC, OWLv2, SAM3).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decomposing open-world 3D detection into 2D detection plus metric 3D lifting, Boxer sidesteps the severe annotation bottleneck that has kept 3D datasets orders of magnitude smaller than 2D counterparts, making the approach scalable to the long tail of object categories. The practical release of code and a model trained on diverse camera types (pinhole, fisheye, panoramic) and depth modalities provides a drop-in component for AR, robotics, and digital-twin pipelines. Key limitations remain: the system assumes a static world (failing on in-hand or moving objects), requires calibrated poses and gravity estimates, and inherits the egocentric blind spots of its 2D detector backbones. Open questions include whether the 7-DoF gravity-aligned representation generalizes to fully unconstrained 9-DoF settings and whether the fusion step can be learned end-to-end.
