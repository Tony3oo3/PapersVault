## Problem

Predicting an absolute target position from a language instruction and a single front camera image requires both grounding the instruction to the correct visual landmark and localizing it in metric ego-centric coordinates without depth cues. Existing methods either ignore spatial structure or rely on metric-projection BEV representations that reduce semantics to a few coarse categories, yielding weak cross-view alignment and large localization errors (10+ m RMSE) that even exceed human performance under the same single-image constraint.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 224×224 front camera image and a natural language navigation instruction (tokenized and embedded via Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B) as input. It outputs an absolute target position (x̃, ỹ) in ego-centric meters and a radius r̃ defining a circular acceptable region, e.g., (x,y,r) = (−3.0 m, 10.0 m, 2.0 m). The target is assumed to be visible in the front image, and the method does not predict a trajectory—only the destination position and its spatial uncertainty.

## Method

AiSCREAM synthesizes an Aerial Semantic (AiS) overhead view from the front image via a text-conditioned image-to-image generator (Nano Banana or Qwen Image Edit), preserving appearance-level scene characteristics that metric-projection BEV loses. Both views are segmented with a language-conditioned model (SAM3) to obtain cross-view consistent semantic masks, then encoded with DINOv3 and fused via bidirectional cross-attention within each view and across views to produce a joint geometric-semantic representation. A two-stage localizer first predicts an initial target position and distribution through front-segmentation, AiS-segmentation, and absolute-position heads trained with a Height-based Cross-view loss (L1 position error + AiS segmentation term + obstacle-overlap penalty), then refines the prediction with an MLLM Reshape Localizer (GPT-5.2) that conditions on the overlaid prediction and instruction to correct implausible outputs. The key distinction from prior BEV approaches is that alignment occurs in the image domain with fine-grained per-pixel semantics rather than in a coarsely categorized projected feature space.

## Results

AiSCREAM achieves an RMSE of 4.72 m on GRiN-Drive and 5.00 m on DRAMATiST, with AED of 3.95 m and 4.03 m respectively, and mIoU of 11.89% and 6.16%. These results outperform the strongest baseline (GENNAV at 10.51 m / 8.56 m RMSE) by 5.79 m and 3.56 m, and also surpass human performance (7.45 m / 7.00 m RMSE) on both benchmarks. The model has ~41.5M trainable parameters with ~9 ms inference time for the core model (though end-to-end latency is ~10.3 s due to AiS view synthesis).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AiSCREAM outperforms all 11 baselines on both benchmarks across every reported metric. The closest competitor is GENNAV [28], which achieves 10.51 m RMSE on GRiN-Drive and 8.56 m on DRAMATiST, still trailing AiSCREAM by 5.79 m and 3.56 m respectively. Among MLLM baselines, GPT-5.2 [40] is the strongest (12.44 m / 11.29 m RMSE), while Qwen3-VL-8B [2] is weaker (27.05 m / 18.25 m). Segmentation-based methods (Rufus et al. [44], GSVA [55], SAM3 [5]) perform poorly, with RMSE ranging from 22–74 m, indicating that 2D mask-to-3D conversion introduces substantial error. Zero-shot VLA methods (SimLingo [43], ORION [17], Alpamayo-R1 [52]) achieve 21–31 m RMSE, confirming that trajectory-oriented policies are ill-suited for precise absolute position prediction. Notably, AiSCREAM also surpasses human performance (7.45 m / 7.00 m RMSE), though the authors note humans were restricted to a single front image without full environmental context.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of Referring Navigable Region (RNR) methods (GENNAV, Rufus et al., GSVA) that predict 2D navigable masks, and from metric-projection BEV representations (BEVFormer, GaussianLSS) that encode scene semantics into coarse categorical grids. Rather than extending either formulation directly, AiSCREAM repurposes text-conditioned image-to-image generation (Nano Banana, Qwen Image Edit) to synthesize an overhead 'Aerial Semantic' view that preserves appearance-level scene characteristics, then performs cross-view geometric-semantic alignment via bidirectional cross-attention between the front and AiS views. The MLLM Reshape Localizer draws on the broader VLA/MLLM-for-driving literature (ORION, SimLingo) but applies it as a second-stage prediction-conditioned refinement rather than an end-to-end policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a generated aerial view outperforms metric BEV projections (BEVFormer v2, GaussianLSS) for language-conditioned absolute localization challenges the working assumption that metric geometric accuracy in BEV is the critical factor; semantic and appearance preservation in the image domain appears more important for grounding natural-language instructions. The DRAMATiST benchmark addresses a real gap in geographic and traffic-system diversity beyond nuScenes-based evaluations. However, the 10.3-second end-to-end inference time (dominated by ~8 s of AiS generation) limits near-term deployment, and the authors themselves flag accelerating or eliminating the generation step as key future work. The failure on object-centered frames of reference (e.g., 'drive around the bus') highlights an unresolved limitation in spatial-reasoning that remains open for the field.
