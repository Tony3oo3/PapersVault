## Problem

Video virtual try-on models are limited by two intertwined gaps: the absence of large-scale, in-the-wild triplet video data with explicit cross-garment supervision (existing datasets are studio-only, image-based, or provide only pairwise supervision), and the fragility of fine-grained garment masks under real-world motion, occlusion, and cluttered scenes, which forces a trade-off between spatial precision and temporal robustness.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 720×1280, 121-frame original video, a temporally consistent human-mask video (from SAM2), a DWPose pose video, a single garment reference image, a garment line map (AniLines), and a global text prompt, and produces a temporally coherent try-on video at the same resolution. A notable design choice is the 'reverse training' paradigm: the synthesized garment-swapped video serves as the model input while the authentic source video is the ground-truth target, ensuring supervision always comes from real footage. The human mask replaces traditional per-garment masks as the sole spatial conditioning signal.

## Method

TripVVT is a Diffusion Transformer framework built on the MagicTryOn/Wan2.1-Fun-14B-Control backbone that replaces fragile garment masks with a coarse human-mask prior to balance spatial control and real-world robustness. A VAE encoder extracts spatio-temporal features from the original video, which are concatenated with resized human-mask features; in parallel, a garment encoder converts the reference image into garment tokens, and a line-map encoder injects structural cues into the DiT blocks. The network is conditioned on a global text prompt describing the scene, person, and outfit. Training follows a three-stage progressive strategy (512×384 → mixed 512×384/592×334 → 832×624/960×544) over 55.5k steps on 8 H100 GPUs, first on supplementary data then on the full TripVVT-10K dataset. The key insight is that a simple, stable human mask confines edits to the body region without the over-constraints of pixel-accurate garment masks, enabling reliable background preservation and robustness to pose changes and occlusions in in-the-wild video.

## Results

On the proposed TripVVT-Bench (100 in-the-wild triplets), TripVVT achieves the best video quality (VFID-I 20.72, VFID-R 0.32) and the highest try-on success rate (Gemini-SR 91%), outperforming the strongest academic baseline MagicTryOn (VFID-I 22.92, Gemini-SR 43%) and the commercial Kling model (VFID-I 22.42, Gemini-SR 78%). It also leads in temporal consistency (CLIP-F 0.9606) and perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.1053). On the ViViD-S studio test set, TripVVT similarly attains the best VFID-I (16.44) and CLIP-F (0.9670), though it trails MagicTryOn slightly on background preservation (BG-L1 0.0624 vs. 0.0493). In a 50-participant user study, TripVVT receives 67.6% of first-place votes, far ahead of Kling (17.2%) and MagicTryOn (12.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MagicTryOn (the strongest academic baseline), TripVVT wins on video quality (VFID-I 20.72 vs. 22.92), temporal consistency (CLIP-F 0.9606 vs. 0.9495), and try-on success rate (91% vs. 43%), but loses on background preservation (BG-L1 0.0852 vs. 0.0515; BG-DINO 0.0059 vs. 0.0036) and SSIM (0.8538 vs. 0.8641), which the authors attribute to the coarser human mask forcing full-person reconstruction. Against the commercial Kling model, TripVVT dominates on video quality (VFID-I 20.72 vs. 22.42), SSIM (0.8538 vs. 0.6662), LPIPS (0.1053 vs. 0.1353), and Gemini-SR (91% vs. 78%), while Kling edges ahead slightly on CLIP-I (0.7148 vs. 0.7110) and CLIP-F (0.9504 vs. 0.9606 is actually TripVVT winning). ViViD and CatV2TON are clearly outperformed across nearly all metrics, with CatV2TON showing particularly poor VFID-R (3.33 vs. 0.32) and Gemini-SR (4% vs. 91%). On the ViViD-S studio set, TripVVT leads in VFID-I (16.44 vs. 17.57 for MagicTryOn) and CLIP-F (0.9670 vs. 0.9647) but is slightly behind MagicTryOn on BG-L1 (0.0624 vs. 0.0493), confirming the trade-off between robustness and pixel-level background fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

TripVVT builds directly on the DiT-based architecture of MagicTryOn [24] and the Wan2.1-Fun-14B-Control base model [2], but departs from the field's dominant reliance on fine-grained garment masks by substituting a coarse, temporally stable human mask as the spatial prior. This positions the work as a deliberate middle ground between mask-based methods (ViViD, CatV2TON, MagicTryOn) that are precise but fragile under real-world motion and occlusion, and mask-free methods (PEMF-VTO, MF-VITON) that are flexible but struggle to preserve backgrounds. The dataset construction pipeline is itself novel, leveraging Wan-Animate for guided video inpainting and Nano Banana for garment reference synthesis in a reverse-training paradigm where real source videos serve as ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TripVVT-10K and TripVVT-Bench fill a clear gap: no prior public resource provided high-resolution, in-the-wild video triplets with cross-garment supervision, and no benchmark jointly assessed fidelity, background preservation, and temporal consistency under realistic conditions. The practical finding that a simple human mask outperforms both no mask and fine garment masks in the wild offers a low-cost, robust conditioning strategy likely to be adopted by subsequent work. However, the acknowledged limitation—coarse masks occasionally alter non-target regions (e.g., a backpack disappearing)—leaves open the question of how to achieve precise, disentangled garment editing without sacrificing the robustness that makes the human-mask approach work in cluttered scenes.
