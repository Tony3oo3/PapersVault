## Problem

Creating large-scale, navigable 3D urban scenes is hindered by the scarcity of high-quality 3D scans and the difficulty of registering noisy internet imagery. Direct 3D reconstruction from satellite imagery fails because limited parallax and large occluded regions (e.g., building facades) produce floaters, blurry textures, and geometric artifacts, while existing city-generation methods over-simplify geometry by relying on semantic maps and overfitting to small domain-specific datasets.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB satellite imagery (e.g., WorldView-3 at 35 cm/pixel, 2048×2048) captured from diverse off-nadir viewpoints and dates, along with RPC camera parameters converted to perspective intrinsics/extrinsics via SatelliteSfM. It outputs a standard 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (appearance embeddings fused into static SH coefficients at inference) that supports real-time free-flight navigation at 60 FPS on a MacBook Pro M4 Pro, with no dependency on street-level data, 3D annotations, or domain-specific training.

## Method

Skyfall-GS is a two-stage pipeline. The reconstruction stage fits a 3DGS model with three satellite-specific additions: (1) appearance modeling via per-image and per-Gaussian embeddings fed through a lightweight MLP to produce affine color corrections for multi-date illumination variation, (2) an entropy-based opacity regularization that pushes opacities toward binary values to suppress floaters, and (3) pseudo-camera depth supervision using MoGe monocular depth estimates with a Pearson-correlation loss from near-ground virtual cameras. The synthesis stage applies a curriculum-based Iterative Dataset Update over five episodes: camera viewpoints orbit look-at points at progressively decreasing elevations (high-to-low), each rendered view is refined by FlowEdit (prompt-to-prompt editing with FLUX.1-dev) to remove artifacts and synthesize occluded facades, multiple independent diffusion samples per view (N_s=2) are averaged implicitly through the photometric loss to enforce cross-view 3D consistency, and the 3DGS is retrained on the refined renders mixed with original satellite views (75/25 split) to preserve satellite-observed geometry.

## Results

On DFC2019, Skyfall-GS achieves FID_CLIP of 27.03 and CMMD of 2.110, dramatically outperforming the best satellite-reconstruction baseline (CoR-GS at FID_CLIP 84.95, CMMD 5.692). On the GoogleEarth dataset, Skyfall-GS attains FID_CLIP 10.29, CMMD 1.959, PSNR 14.42, and SSIM 0.302, surpassing both city-generation baselines (CityDreamer, GaussianCity) and 3D-reconstruction baselines (CoR-GS, Mip-Splatting) on distribution metrics, while Mip-Splatting retains a marginal LPIPS advantage (0.379 vs. 0.393). User studies with 44 participants confirm dominant human preference, with win rates of ≈90–94% on DFC2019 and ≈79–82% on GoogleEarth across geometric accuracy, spatial alignment, and overall perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Skyfall-GS is compared against six baselines spanning two categories: satellite 3D reconstruction (Sat-NeRF, EOGS, CoR-GS, Mip-Splatting) and city generation (CityDreamer, GaussianCity). On DFC2019, Skyfall-GS achieves a 3.1× reduction in FID_CLIP over the best reconstruction baseline (27.03 vs. 84.95 for CoR-GS) and a 2.3× reduction in CMMD (2.110 vs. 4.788 for Sat-NeRF), indicating far superior generative quality in previously occluded facade regions. On GoogleEarth, Skyfall-GS outperforms both city-generation methods by large margins (FID_CLIP 10.29 vs. 36.66 for CityDreamer and 28.76 for GaussianCity) and beats the strongest reconstruction baseline Mip-Splatting on FID_CLIP (10.29 vs. 16.09) and CMMD (1.959 vs. 2.086), though Mip-Splatting retains a slight LPIPS edge (0.379 vs. 0.393), which the authors attribute to Mip-Splatting's smoother reconstructions inflating pixel-level scores. The qualitative comparisons confirm that baselines exhibit severe artifacts—Sat-NeRF produces fog, EOGS yields excessive darkness, CoR-GS suffers heavy blurring, Mip-Splatting shows floaters, and city-generation methods over-simplify geometry—while Skyfall-GS recovers sharp building contours, detailed facades, and scene-specific features such as the red pavement in scene 010.

## Relation to Prior Work

Skyfall-GS sits at the intersection of satellite-based 3D reconstruction (Sat-NeRF, EOGS, SatMVS) and diffusion-guided 3D scene refinement (Instruct-NeRF2NeRF, LucidDreamer, WonderWorld), but departs from both in key ways. From the reconstruction side, it extends 3DGS with entropy-based opacity regularization and pseudo-camera depth supervision specifically to tame the limited-parallax, multi-date satellite setting, building on WildGaussians' appearance modeling. From the diffusion side, it adapts the Iterative Dataset Update paradigm but replaces inpainting-based editing with prompt-to-prompt (FlowEdit) refinement, motivated by the observation that satellite imagery already captures facade appearance at oblique angles and thus provides structural constraints that inpainting would discard. The curriculum-based high-to-low elevation schedule is a novel scheduling strategy not present in prior IDU methods, which typically sample from fixed orbits or original training views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By requiring only multi-view satellite imagery as input—no 3D scans, no semantic maps, no street-level photos—Skyfall-GS removes the primary data bottleneck for large-scale urban scene creation and enables real-time (60 FPS) free-flight navigation, making it directly applicable to simulation, gaming, and robotics planning. Key limitations remain: the fixed heuristic camera trajectory can leave blind spots in complex geometries, strict pedestrian-level synthesis (<10° facade-close) is out of scope due to transient objects and reference distortion, and purely nadir satellite input is unhandled. Open directions include city-wide scaling beyond the ~1 km × 512 m multi-block demo, dynamic scene modeling, and whether the curriculum schedule generalizes to other sparse-view or multi-sensor reconstruction settings.
