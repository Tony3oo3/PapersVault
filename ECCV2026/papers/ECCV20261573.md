## Problem

Existing lightweight denoising Transformers rely solely on first-order inter-patch statistics in self-attention, effectively performing a learnable non-local averaging that ignores second-order intra-patch pixel correlations critical in structured regions like edges and textures. This omission forces lightweight models to use more attention heads and larger windows to compensate, yet they still trail full-size Transformer denoisers by a notable margin in PSNR.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261573/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a noisy RGB or grayscale image (AWGN with σ ∈ [10,55] or real sensor noise from SIDD/DND) and outputs a denoised image of the same size. The input is consumed as overlapping patches extracted by a 3×3 convolution, processed through a 4-level U-shaped encoder-decoder with feature dimensions [20,40,80,160], and reassembled by a final 3×3 convolution. Training uses 128×128 patches with L1 loss; the model is blind to noise level at inference.

## Method

NLformer reinterprets self-attention through a Bayesian lens, deriving a unified estimator that combines a non-local weighted average (inter-patch, first-order) with a linear correction term H_i·Y_i (intra-patch, second-order), where H_i is the optimal Wiener coefficient. Architecturally, each Transformer block contains a Dual-Branch Window Self-Attention (DWSA) module: the primal branch computes standard softmax-weighted value aggregation, while the dual branch computes a global second-order statistic H (average of outer products of value tokens) plus a per-patch rank-2 low-rank correction ΔH_i. A Woodbury Inversion Feed-Forward Network (WIFN) then efficiently inverts Φ₂^V(Y_i) = H + ΔH_i via the Woodbury matrix identity and applies the resulting linear correction, directly implementing the optimal Bayesian estimator. The full model uses a 4-level U-shaped multi-scale structure with pixel-unshuffle/shuffle, overlapped attention windows (W=8), and 2.44M parameters, achieving state-of-the-art lightweight denoising with 22.19 GFLOPs.

## Results

On real-world denoising, NLformer achieves 39.88 dB PSNR / 0.959 SSIM on SIDD and 39.92 dB / 0.956 SSIM on DND with only 2.44M parameters and 22.19G FLOPs, outperforming all lightweight baselines by 0.16–0.36 dB. On synthetic benchmarks (Urban100), it reaches 34.87 / 32.67 / 29.68 dB at σ = 15 / 25 / 50, the best among lightweight models. Compared to full-size Transformers (Restormer: 40.02 dB on SIDD; ART: 40.05 dB), NLformer narrows the gap to ≤ 0.17 dB while using roughly 10× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NLformer outperforms every lightweight baseline on both SIDD and DND. On SIDD it leads LIDFormer (39.63 dB) by 0.25 dB, Restormer-lw (39.53 dB) by 0.35 dB, and RAMiT (39.52 dB) by 0.36 dB; on DND it leads LIDFormer (39.76 dB) by 0.16 dB and Restormer-lw / RAMiT (39.70 dB) by 0.22 dB. Against full-size Transformers the gap is small: Restormer (40.02 dB) and ART (40.05 dB) exceed NLformer by only 0.14 and 0.17 dB on SIDD, respectively, while using 10–11× more parameters. On synthetic benchmarks (Table 2), NLformer similarly tops all lightweight models on Urban100 (34.87 dB at σ=15 vs. ART-lw's 34.44 dB) and Kodak24 (35.34 dB vs. ART-lw's 35.00 dB), trailing full-size Restormer by ≤ 0.23 dB. Inference time on SIDD is 19.5 s, the lowest among all compared Transformers (vs. 400.6 s for Restormer and 119.1 s for RAMiT).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets standard self-attention through the lens of non-local Bayesian inference (NL-Bayes, Lebrun et al. 2013), showing that ViT-style SA is essentially a learnable non-local means that captures only first-order inter-patch statistics. It then extends this by incorporating second-order intra-patch correlations (the Wiener estimator from NL-Bayes) into a dual-branch attention design, bridging two previously separate lines of work: classical non-local denoising and Transformer architectures. Architecturally, NLformer builds on the windowed attention of SwinIR, the multi-scale pixel-unshuffle/shuffle structure and gated FFN of Restormer, and the patch-to-token paradigm of ViT, but replaces the standard FFN with a Woodbury-inversion-based module that explicitly approximates the optimal Bayesian linear estimator. Compared to prior lightweight Transformers (LIDFormer, RAMiT), NLformer provides a principled Bayesian justification for their underperformance: they model only inter-patch correlations and lack the intra-patch second-order term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual unification—showing that standard SA is a first-order non-local estimator and that adding a second-order intra-patch branch recovers the NL-Bayes correction—gives practitioners a clear theoretical recipe for where Transformer denoisers lose information and how to recover it cheaply. Practically, achieving 39.88 dB on SIDD with 2.44M parameters (vs. 40.02 dB at 26.11M for Restormer) demonstrates that a ~10× parameter reduction is possible with only a 0.14 dB gap, which is meaningful for deployment-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the low-rank (r=2) approximation of the second-order statistics is sufficient for higher-resolution or more structured images, whether the Woodbury-FFN design transfers cleanly to other restoration tasks (deblurring, super-resolution) beyond the supplementary experiments, and whether the Bayesian interpretation can guide the design of attention mechanisms in non-denoising vision tasks.
