## Problem

Repair-based novel-trajectory synthesis methods fail under out-of-distribution rendering artifacts, while LiDAR-based camera-controlled methods produce 3D-inconsistent results due to sparse geometric cues in occluded or distant regions. Additionally, prior camera-controlled approaches train on longitudinal trajectory segments, creating a train-test gap when generalizing to lateral viewpoint shifts at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261031/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 121-frame source trajectory video at 480×832 resolution, a novel-trajectory 3DGS rendering (encoded by the same 3D VAE), and a relative SE(3) camera pose as inputs, producing a laterally shifted novel-trajectory video (offsets of ±1 m to ±4 m). Notably, during training the source input is a degraded 3DGS rendering while at inference the source is the clean recorded video; the relative pose is encoded separately from an identity pose to disambiguate source and target latents, and a learnable frame embedding provides temporal correspondence cues.

## Method

ReCamDriving replaces sparse LiDAR projections with dense novel-trajectory 3DGS renderings as the primary geometric control signal, leveraging their structural completeness over their artifact-free quality. A two-stage progressive training paradigm prevents shortcut learning: Stage 1 trains DiT self-attention blocks on relative camera pose for coarse viewpoint transformation, then Stage 2 freezes those parameters and introduces Rendering Attention (refining spatio-temporal features within the 3DGS latent space) and Cross Attention (fusing rendering features with already-transformed diffusion latents) for fine-grained geometric alignment. The model is initialized from the Wan2.1 video diffusion foundation model with a ReCamMaster camera encoder, trained via flow matching. A 3DGS-based cross-trajectory data curation strategy renders laterally shifted trajectories from reconstructed scenes to serve as training source inputs, enabling large-scale lateral-trajectory supervision from single-pass driving videos and yielding the 110K-pair ParaDrive dataset.

## Results

On Waymo Open Dataset, ReCamDriving achieves the best performance across all metrics at every lateral offset (±1 m to ±4 m). At ±1 m, it reaches FID 13.76, FVD 13.27, and CLIP-V 97.96, substantially outperforming the next-best StreetCrafter (FID 28.18, FVD 20.51, CLIP-V 96.01). At the challenging ±4 m offset, the advantage widens: FID 32.36 vs. StreetCrafter's 68.73 and Difix3D+'s 78.08, while temporal consistency (TCE) remains at 4.30 versus 10.49–14.35 for baselines. On NuScenes, ReCamDriving scores FID 25.68 and FVD 18.98, more than 2× better than Difix3D+ (FID 65.14, FVD 41.37).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCamDriving outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric across both WOD and NuScenes, with the margin growing as lateral offset increases. Against the LiDAR-based camera-controlled methods (FreeVS, StreetCrafter), the advantage is most pronounced in view consistency: at ±4 m, ReCamDriving's FID of 32.36 is more than 2× lower than StreetCrafter's 68.73 and 3.3× lower than FreeVS's 107.04, reflecting the structural incompleteness of sparse LiDAR projections in occluded and distant regions. Against the repair-based Difix3D+, ReCamDriving achieves a 2.5× lower FID on NuScenes (25.68 vs. 65.14) and a 4× lower FVD (18.98 vs. 41.37), confirming that repair models degrade under out-of-distribution rendering artifacts. Camera accuracy (Table 2) also favors ReCamDriving at all offsets; at ±4 m its translation error is 2.73 m versus 3.03 m (StreetCrafter) and 3.12 m (Difix3D+), while rotation error is 1.57° versus 2.87° and 2.68° respectively. DriveStudio, which renders directly from 3DGS without any generative model, is consistently the weakest baseline, particularly at larger offsets where its FID exceeds 140 on WOD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: reconstruction-then-repair pipelines (DriveStudio, Difix3D+, GSFixer) and camera-controlled video generation (ReCamMaster, FreeVS, StreetCrafter). It departs from the repair paradigm by arguing that local artifact correction cannot generalize to out-of-distribution rendering degradations, and from LiDAR-based camera control by replacing sparse point projections with dense 3DGS renderings as the geometric conditioning signal. Architecturally, it initializes from the Wan2.1 video diffusion backbone and ReCamMaster's camera encoder, then introduces a two-stage progressive training scheme (pose-only → pose + 3DGS rendering) with auxiliary Rendering Attention and Cross Attention modules to prevent the model from collapsing into trivial artifact restoration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the LiDAR dependency, ReCamDriving opens a pathway to apply camera-controlled video generation to web-scale driving video corpora that lack expensive LiDAR annotations, and the 110K-pair ParaDrive dataset provides a reusable resource for the community. The practical limitation is the offline 3DGS reconstruction step (hours per scene), though the authors note this is a one-time cost and will shrink as feed-forward 3D reconstruction matures. An open question is whether the dense-rendering conditioning strategy generalizes beyond lateral offsets to arbitrary 6-DoF trajectory changes, and whether the two-stage anti-shortcut-learning design transfers to other conditioning modalities.
