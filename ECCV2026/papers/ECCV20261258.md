## Problem

Knowledge distillation training is computationally expensive because students are typically distilled on the full dataset under a large teacher, yet existing data pruning methods are not designed for KD: trajectory-based scores (EL2N, Forgetting, AUM) require costly retraining unavailable with pretrained teachers, while CE-loss-based scores are weakly discriminative, and heuristic sampling rules (top-k, fixed windows) cannot adapt to varying teacher-student dynamics across pruning regimes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained teacher model (ResNet, WideResNet, or ViT), a labeled training set of RGB images, a held-out validation set, and a target pruning ratio r. It first fine-tunes the teacher with SAM on the validation set to stabilize curvature estimation, then computes per-sample influence scores and learns a Beta-distribution policy that outputs a binary mask of size (1−r)N. The pruned subset is then used to distill a student model via standard KD loss; the inner-loop proxy is a linear classifier on frozen teacher features, so the full student is only trained once after the mask is finalized.

## Method

IF-Beta unifies two components: (1) an influence-function score estimator under Flat Validation Minimum (FVM), where the teacher is fine-tuned with SAM on the validation set and per-sample influence is computed as g̃_trᵀ H̃_val⁻¹ g̃_tr with the inverse Hessian approximated by a diagonal Fisher matrix, yielding a retraining-free difficulty score; and (2) a learnable Beta Policy that parameterizes the selection probability of each rank-normalized score via a two-parameter Beta density, replacing fixed-threshold or fixed-window heuristics with a shape-adaptive distribution. The policy is optimized through a bilevel objective: the inner loop trains a linear head on frozen teacher features with the KD loss (a cheap proxy for the full student), while the outer loop updates the Beta parameters via an unbiased policy-gradient estimator (Theorem 1), avoiding costly implicit differentiation. This design keeps the entire pruning search in the teacher's feature space, making it computationally negligible relative to full student training, and the resulting mask can be applied to any student architecture or KD objective.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, IF-Beta achieves 95.69% top-1 accuracy at 30% pruning, surpassing the full-data distillation baseline (95.50%). On CIFAR-100, 90% pruning yields 79.53% vs. 79.38% for full-data, and on ImageNet, 50% pruning reaches 74.02% vs. 73.54% full-data. In the efficient KD comparison (ResNet-34 → MobileNet on ImageNet, 70% data), IF-Beta achieves 71.20% accuracy in 19.24 hours, less than half the training time of the full-data baseline (39.86h) while outperforming it by 1.63 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IF-Beta consistently outperforms all retraining-based baselines (EL2N, GraNd, Forgetting, DUAL) across every dataset and pruning ratio in Figure 4, with DUAL (requiring 60 epochs of partial retraining) being the closest competitor yet still trailing IF-Beta. Among retraining-free methods, IF-Beta surpasses Random pruning and Medium-Difficulty (Chen et al., ICLR 2025) by clear margins; for example, at 70% pruning on CIFAR-100, IF-Beta reaches 75.60% versus 72.22% for IF-CCS and 71.75% for IF-BWS. In the efficient KD comparison on ImageNet (Table 3), IF-Beta achieves 71.20% in 19.24h, beating Zipf's LS (69.59%, >39.86h), USKD (70.38%, >39.86h), and Medium-Difficulty* (70.92%, 36.98h) on both accuracy and training time. The ablation in Table 5 confirms that the learnable Beta Policy is the key differentiator: when paired with the same IF-FVM scores, IF-Beta beats the fixed-threshold IF-CCS by up to 3.38 points (CIFAR-100, 70% pruning) and the fixed-window IF-BWS by up to 3.89 points (CIFAR-100, 90% pruning). In the score-estimation comparison (Figure 1), IF-FVM achieves higher Spearman correlation with trajectory-based difficulty metrics (EL2N, AUM) than both the CE-loss baseline and IF-LiSSA, validating its use as a scoring mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of influence-function-based data valuation and knowledge distillation, building directly on Koh & Liang's influence function formulation and Ye et al.'s Flat Validation Minimum (FVM) stabilization to obtain a retraining-free, post-hoc sample importance score. It departs from prior KD-pruning work (Baruch et al., Chen et al.) that relied on trajectory-based metrics (EL2N, Forgetting, AUM) or the teacher's CE loss as a difficulty proxy, arguing both are either computationally prohibitive or weakly discriminative. For the sampling stage, it replaces fixed heuristic rules (top-k, CCS stratified cutoffs, BWS sliding windows) with a learnable Beta-distribution policy optimized via bilevel policy-gradient, borrowing the probabilistic coreset-selection framework of Zhou et al. (ICML 2022) but instantiating the inner loop as a linear probe on frozen teacher features for efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that KD training cost can be reduced by 10–20% in wall-clock time while simultaneously matching or slightly exceeding full-data student accuracy, which is attractive for resource-constrained deployment pipelines. The finding that a carefully pruned subset can outperform the full dataset in KD challenges the implicit assumption that more distillation data is always better, suggesting that some samples actively harm student generalization. Open questions include whether the proxy-student (linear head on frozen teacher features) faithfully captures the dynamics of deeper or architecturally dissimilar students, whether the approach extends to non-vision modalities (NLP, audio), and how the Beta policy behaves under extreme pruning ratios (>95%) or very large-scale datasets where the combinatorial mask space becomes intractable.
