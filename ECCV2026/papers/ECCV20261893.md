## Problem

Existing test-time scaling (TTS) and reflection methods for image generation rely on external Visual Large Language Models as verifiers, introducing additional computational overhead and fragmented pipelines that separate verification from the core generation process. Because verification inherently requires visual understanding, integrating it into a single unified model that handles both comprehension and synthesis remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261893/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts three input modes: text prompts for generation, image-plus-text pairs for editing and verification, and images for understanding tasks. Outputs include generated or edited images at 512×512 or 1024×1024 resolution, a binary verification score from a dedicated classification head, structured reflection text explaining semantic mismatches, and free-form textual descriptions. During TTS, the reflection text is not used as a raw editing prompt but is encoded into a specialized reflection embedding that conditions the diffusion decoder, enabling in-model refinement without external editing models.

## Method

UniReflect couples a frozen Qwen3-VL 4B backbone with a Diffusion Transformer (initialized from OmniGen2) through a novel Semantic Perceiver—a multi-layer transformer block that processes task-specific tokens (<verifier>, <reflect>, <img>) extracted from the VLLM's final hidden states, creating a bidirectional bridge between understanding and generation. A decoupling verification strategy separates scoring (a lightweight classification head on the <verifier> token) from explanation (next-token prediction), improving verification reliability over prior approaches that output True/False text directly. Training proceeds in two stages: first aligning the Semantic Perceiver and DiT on generation and editing data, then jointly fine-tuning across generation, editing, and verification tasks with a unified loss combining cross-entropy for text, classification loss for verification, and MSE for visual reconstruction. At inference, the TTS pipeline forms a closed loop—generate, verify, reflect, refine—where the reflection embedding E_R conditions the DiT alongside the original semantic instruction E_S, allowing the model to preserve correct regions while correcting identified errors in a single regeneration pass.

## Results

UniReflect (4B+4B params) achieves 85.1 MMBench, 67.4 MMMU, and 73.7 MathVista for understanding; 0.83 GenEval (0.87 with prompt engineering) and 86.69 DPG-Bench for generation; 3.28 ImgEdit and 5.14 GEdit-EN for editing; and 0.579 ViVerBench for verification. With Test-Time Scaling (TTS), GenEval rises to 0.89 and GEdit-EN to 6.07. On the compositional GenEval++ benchmark, UniReflect scores 0.654 (0.680 with TTS), substantially outperforming FLUX.1-dev (0.314) and BAGEL (0.371). Notably, UniReflect matches or exceeds BAGEL (14B params, 1.6B training pairs) on GenEval and DPG-Bench while using only 4B trainable parameters and 4M image-text pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest unified baseline BAGEL (14B trainable params, 1.6B training pairs), UniReflect (4B params, 4M pairs) achieves comparable GenEval (0.83 vs 0.82; 0.87† vs 0.88†) while surpassing it on DPG-Bench (86.69 vs 85.07) and understanding (67.4 vs 55.3 MMMU). UniReflect clearly outperforms OmniGen2 on all understanding metrics (85.1 vs 79.1 MMBench, 67.4 vs 53.1 MMMU, 73.7 vs 62.3 MathVista) and DPG-Bench (86.69 vs 83.57), but trails on image editing (3.28 vs 3.44 ImgEdit, 5.14 vs 6.42 GEdit-EN). Versus BLIP3-o and UniWorld-V1, UniReflect wins decisively on MMMU (67.4 vs 50.6 and 58.6) and DPG-Bench (86.69 vs 81.6 and 81.38). On visual verification (ViVerBench), UniReflect's in-model verifier scores 0.579, beating the dedicated OmniVerifier (0.559) and Qwen 2.5-VL 7B (0.523) without any external module. On the harder compositional GenEval++ benchmark, UniReflect (0.654) nearly doubles the scores of FLUX.1-dev (0.314) and BAGEL (0.371), and TTS further lifts it to 0.680. The main weakness is image editing, where UniReflect lags behind OmniGen2 and Step1X-Edit, which the authors attribute to limited editing data in the training mix.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniReflect extends the assembled unified-model paradigm (BLIP3-o, UniWorld, OmniGen2) that pairs a frozen VLLM with a Diffusion Transformer for joint understanding and generation, initializing its DiT backbone from OmniGen2. Its key architectural departure is the Semantic Perceiver module, a shared multi-layer transformer that fuses task-specific tokens (<verifier>, <reflect>, <img>) from the VLLM's hidden states into a unified semantic space, replacing the simple prompt-encoder role that prior unified models assign to the VLLM. The decoupling verification strategy (a dedicated classification head for scoring plus autoregressive explanation) departs from prior verifiers like OmniVerifier and ReflectionFlow that output binary True/False text, and the in-model TTS loop eliminates the external VLLM verification step that OmniGen2 and ReflectionFlow require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By collapsing verification, reflection, and regeneration into a single 4B-parameter model, UniReflect removes the latency and information-loss overhead of cascading separate VLLM verifiers and editing models, making test-time scaling practical for real-time or resource-constrained deployment. The finding that a straightforward supervised fine-tuning on 20K verification samples outperforms OmniVerifier's RL-based training (0.579 vs. 0.559 on ViVerBench) suggests that the verification signal is more effectively learned through multi-task co-training than through dedicated RL. Open questions include whether the frozen-VLLM design caps verification quality on complex compositional scenes, whether the approach extends to video generation, and whether the modest editing scores (3.28 vs. OmniGen2's 3.44 on ImgEdit) reflect a fundamental trade-off with the verification objective or simply a data-imbalance issue.
