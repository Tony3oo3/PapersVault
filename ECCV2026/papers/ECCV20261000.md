## Problem

MeanFlow enforces an identity coupling interval-wise mean velocity and instantaneous velocity via Jacobian-vector products (JVP), which is computationally expensive, hard to scale, and weakens fused-kernel and compiler-level optimization in modern training stacks. The paper asks whether identity-based supervision can be preserved without JVP by treating finite differencing not as a drop-in operator replacement but as an error-driven design tool that exposes controllable approximation structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261000/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (with optional CFG dropout) as input and generates a 256×256 RGB image in a single forward pass (NFE=1) or two passes (NFE=2). Generation occurs in the latent space of a pre-trained VAE tokenizer (32×32×4 latents), which are then decoded to pixel space. The backbone is a SiT-XL/2 transformer (676M params) trained from scratch with no external teacher or distillation.

## Method

DuoFlow decomposes the JVP-free identity supervision error into two coupled channels—step-truncation error and trajectory-velocity error—and designs each component to suppress one. For truncation error, it uses stochastic signed one-sided differencing (random ±1 sign, one extra forward pass) that cancels the O(h) bias in expectation, recovering O(h²) behavior. For trajectory-velocity error, it jointly predicts mean velocity u_θ(z_t, r, t) and instantaneous velocity v_θ(z_t, t) at the same sampled state with shared parameters but different temporal interval conditions, keeping the two estimates trajectory-consistent. A progressive self-bootstrapping mechanism dynamically computes a self-trust weight κ from batch-level cosine alignment between the model's velocity and the CFG-guided external target, gradually shifting supervision toward model-consistent dynamics and driving trajectory velocity toward a fixed-point-like self-consistent tendency. The unified loss uses detached normalization and a κ-dependent weight to balance the two velocity objectives as self-bootstrapping strengthens.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, DuoFlow-XL/2 achieves an FID of 2.48 with a single function evaluation (1-NFE), improving over the matched MeanFlow-XL/2 baseline (FID 3.43) by approximately 28%. In the 2-NFE setting, DuoFlow-XL/2 reaches FID 1.96 versus MeanFlow-XL/2's 2.93, a ~33% relative gain. The same trend holds at smaller scales: DuoFlow-L/2 (FID 3.14) outperforms MeanFlow-L/2 (FID 3.84). With extended training (640 epochs), DuoFlow-XL/2 further improves to FID 1.99 at 1-NFE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DuoFlow consistently outperforms all four compared from-scratch baselines on ImageNet 256×256. Against the closest competitor, MeanFlow [14] (same architecture, same VAE tokenizer, JVP-based identity training), DuoFlow-XL/2 improves FID from 3.43 to 2.48 at 1-NFE (~28% relative gain) and from 2.93 to 1.96 at 2-NFE (~33% relative gain). Against iCT-XL/2 [44] (FID 34.24 at 1-NFE) and Shortcut-XL/2 [13] (FID 10.60 at 1-NFE), DuoFlow achieves substantially lower FID, reflecting the advantage of MeanFlow-style interval transport over consistency or shortcut objectives. Against iMM-XL/2 [53] (FID 7.77 at 2-NFE), DuoFlow-XL/2 (FID 1.96) also wins decisively. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that the gains over the reproduced MeanFlow JVP baseline (FID 14.13 at 80 epochs) come from the combination of signed differencing, joint velocity estimation, and progressive self-bootstrapping, with the full model reaching FID 9.17 (35.1% relative improvement).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MeanFlow (Geng et al., 2025), which introduced an identity coupling interval-wise mean velocity and instantaneous velocity for one-step transport learning, but departs from it by eliminating the Jacobian-vector product (JVP) that MeanFlow's identity supervision relies on. Rather than treating finite differencing as a drop-in operator replacement (as in classical numerical analysis or zeroth-order optimization), the authors adopt an error-driven perspective that decomposes the approximation into step-truncation and trajectory-velocity channels, then designs stochastic signed differencing, same-state joint velocity estimation, and progressive self-bootstrapping to control each channel. The broader lineage includes flow matching, rectified flow, and consistency-style one-step generation, but the specific contribution is a principled JVP-free training recipe for MeanFlow-style identity objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: JVP paths in modern training stacks weaken fused-kernel execution and compiler-level optimization, so removing them while improving quality (30% FID gain over matched JVP baseline) and achieving 2.5× wall-clock speedup makes MeanFlow-style training significantly more scalable. The error-decomposition framework is a reusable analytical lens that could extend to other identity-based or derivative-supervised training objectives beyond MeanFlow. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to text-to-image, video, and higher-resolution regimes, and whether it composes with concurrent improvements like REPA, LightningDiT, or iMF. The method is currently validated only on ImageNet 256×256 with a fixed VAE tokenizer, so generalization to other domains and architectures remains to be established.
