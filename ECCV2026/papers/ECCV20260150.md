## Problem

Existing Animal Re-Identification methods rely on holistic global features that overfit to background elements, or on global pattern extraction that remains sensitive to occlusion and environmental interference, failing to highlight localised, identity-relevant cues. Furthermore, current multimodal approaches for Animal ReID use only simple cross-modal alignment or post-hoc feature fusion, lacking dense cross-modal interactions at the region level that could enrich semantic guidance towards fine-grained discriminative evidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260150/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB animal images (resized to 384×384 with padding) alongside region-referenced textual prompts containing placeholders for segmentation-derived localised regions; at inference, only the visual modality is used, producing a similarity score matrix between query and gallery samples for re-identification ranking. The region mining pipeline autonomously extracts biologically informative regions per image using SAM 3 segmentation, Sobel gradient magnitude on grayscale masks, and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control, with the number of regions per image set to the dataset-level mode of statistically significant candidate counts.

## Method

RAMI projects segmentation-derived region tokens (encoded by SigLIP's SoViT-400m vision encoder) into a shared text-token space via a lightweight TokenProjector, then interleaves them with text tokens from region-referenced prompts (e.g., 'A photo of a {species} individual with identity {id}. This individual has ⟨reg1⟩...⟨regN⟩ patterns.') to form a multimodal token sequence. A shallow Transformer-based Interleaver module (single multi-head self-attention layer) with a prepended learnable pooling token structures dense cross-modal interactions across all region and text tokens. Training proceeds in two stages: a 10-epoch warm-up of the TokenProjector and Interleaver under a SigLIP sigmoid contrastive loss, followed by 60 epochs of joint optimisation with SigLIP's vision encoder under a combined objective of identity loss (with label smoothing), triplet loss, and SigLIP alignment loss. This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first application of multimodal interleaving tailored for a fine-grained discriminative task within pretrained vision-language foundation models, as opposed to its prior use in generative or reasoning-oriented LMMs.

## Results

RAMI achieves the best mAP across all five Animal ReID datasets, with headline values of 63.2% on ATRW, 69.3% on Giraffes, 35.3% on Nyala-Data, 30.1% on SealID, and 66.8% on Stoat. The largest gain is on Nyala-Data, where RAMI improves mAP by 18.7 percentage points over the strongest baseline (CARE, 16.6%). On Rank-1 accuracy, RAMI leads on four of five datasets (e.g., 97.4% on ATRW, 58.8% on Nyala-Data), with only a marginal 0.3-point deficit to CARE on Stoat (91.6% vs. 91.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAMI outperforms all five baselines on mAP across every dataset. Against the strongest baseline CARE, RAMI gains +6.2 mAP on ATRW (63.2 vs. 57.0), +9.3 on Giraffes (69.3 vs. 60.0), +18.7 on Nyala-Data (35.3 vs. 16.6), +8.5 on SealID (30.1 vs. 21.6), and +2.7 on Stoat (66.8 vs. 64.1). On Rank-1, RAMI leads CARE on four of five datasets (e.g., +2.0 on ATRW, +29.8 on Nyala-Data, +12.5 on SealID) but trails by a negligible 0.3 points on Stoat (91.6 vs. 91.9). The older CNN/ViT-based baselines TransReID and MDReID are substantially outperformed, with RAMI achieving roughly 14–29 mAP points higher. The pattern-based GloPER baseline, which relies on global unsupervised segmentation, is the weakest overall (e.g., 9.3 mAP on Nyala-Data vs. RAMI's 35.3), confirming that global pattern extraction is highly sensitive to background interference. CLIP-ReID, which uses simple cross-modal alignment without dense interactions, is consistently surpassed by RAMI by 9–22 mAP points, underscoring the benefit of the proposed region-level interleaving mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAMI builds directly on SigLIP's pretrained vision-language architecture, repurposing its vision encoder, text encoder, and sigmoid contrastive objective as the foundation for a re-identification framework. It departs from prior Animal ReID methods (CARE, CLIP-ReID, GloPER) that rely on holistic global features or simple post-hoc cross-modal alignment by introducing dense, region-level cross-modal interactions via a shallow Transformer Interleaver. The multimodal interleaving paradigm itself is borrowed from Large Multimodal Models (e.g., LLaVA-Interleave) designed for generation and reasoning over multi-image inputs, but RAMI is the first to adapt it for fine-grained discriminative tasks within a VLM. The region-mining pipeline (SAM 3 segmentation + Sobel gradient scoring + Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control) is a lightweight, trainable-free preprocessing step that distinguishes the approach from learned pattern extraction methods like GloPER.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 18.7% mAP gain on Nyala-Data and consistent improvements across all five datasets demonstrate that localised, region-aware multimodal interactions substantially outperform both holistic and global-pattern approaches for animal re-identification, offering a practical path toward more reliable ecological monitoring from camera traps. A notable limitation is that inference is purely visual (no text modality at test time), meaning the multimodal training acts as a regularisation signal rather than a deployment-time advantage, which raises open questions about whether the interleaving benefit transfers to truly multimodal inference settings. The counterfactual region-ablation analysis provides useful interpretability but remains a post-hoc diagnostic rather than a training-time guarantee. Generalisation beyond the five evaluated species and the reliance on SAM 3 segmentation quality for region mining are practical constraints that limit immediate deployment in low-resource field settings.
