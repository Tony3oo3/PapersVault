## Problem

Existing 3D generative models either rely on implicit volumetric representations that enforce watertight topology (failing on thin-shell and open-boundary structures like garments) or use binary occupancy maps in geometry images whose resolution-dependent boundary encoding produces staircase artifacts and makes surface reconstruction a non-differentiable post-processing step disconnected from the learning pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262519/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts three types of conditioning signals—class labels, single front-view images (rendered normal maps or real RGB photos), and 2D sewing pattern silhouettes—and produces a 3D triangle mesh with native UV coordinates. Internally, the 3D surface is encoded as a 256×256×4 geometry image tensor (3-channel position map + 1-channel TSDF) that is compressed to a 32×32×4 VAE latent; the mesh is recovered via Differentiable Marching Squares, yielding UV-aligned meshes directly usable in physics simulation and material editing pipelines without post-hoc unwrapping.

## Method

DiffGI replaces the binary occupancy channel of prior geometry-image methods with a continuous 2D Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF) that encodes boundary position at subpixel precision within a fixed grid, eliminating resolution-dependent staircase artifacts even under aggressive downsampling. A tensor-based Differentiable Marching Squares (DMS) module formulates vertex positions as continuous functions of the TSDF via analytical linear interpolation (with a regularization constant for numerical stability), enabling 3D surface losses to backpropagate through the mesh reconstruction step into the 2D latent space. The DiffGI-VAE, initialized from Stable Diffusion 1.5 weights, is trained with a geometry-aware normal rendering loss that renders the DMS-reconstructed mesh through nvdiffrast and minimizes L1 error against ground-truth normal maps, preserving high-frequency curvature under 8× spatial compression. On top of the 32×32×4 latent, a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with flow-matching scheduling performs conditional generation, with DINOv2-Large embeddings injected via cross-attention for image conditioning and a lightweight UNet-Tiny for occupancy-conditioned garment draping.

## Results

On the GarmageSet image-to-3D benchmark, DiffGI achieves the best Chamfer Distance (1.35×10⁻²), F1-score (0.48), Hausdorff Distance (8.42×10⁻²), and Boundary Chamfer Distance (2.91×10⁻²) while producing meshes with only 23K vertices on average—roughly 5–23× fewer than competing methods. For VAE reconstruction on GarmageSet, DiffGI-VAE achieves CD 0.46×10⁻³ and NC 0.96, substantially outperforming GarmageNet (CD 1.89×10⁻³, NC 0.90) and Omages (CD 1.31×10⁻³, NC 0.95). In label-conditioned generation on ABO, the DiffGI diffusion model reduces mean P-FID from 31.14 (Omages) to 19.91 and mean P-KID from 0.12 to 0.06. Inference runs in ~1.2 s on a consumer RTX 4070 (3.22 GB VRAM) and ~8.5 s on CPU-only hardware.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Omages [46], DiffGI wins on both reconstruction (CD 0.83 vs. 0.89×10⁻³ on ABO; CD 0.46 vs. 1.31×10⁻³ on GarmageSet) and label-conditioned generation (mean P-FID 19.91 vs. 31.14; mean P-KID 0.06 vs. 0.12), with the largest P-FID gains on Chair (50% reduction) and Lamp (49% reduction). Against GarmageNet [19], DiffGI achieves a 4× lower CD on GarmageSet reconstruction (0.46 vs. 1.89×10⁻³) and a 3.2× lower CD on image-to-3D (1.35 vs. 4.31×10⁻²), while also producing meshes with 23× fewer vertices (23K vs. 526K). Against the volumetric foundation model TRELLIS [43], DiffGI reduces CD by 2.5× (1.35 vs. 3.44×10⁻²) and HD by 1.8× (8.42 vs. 15.38×10⁻²) with 4.7× fewer vertices, and avoids the excessive thickness artifacts that TRELLIS introduces on thin-shell garments due to its watertight assumption. Against TRELLIS.2 [42], the advantage is even more pronounced: DiffGI achieves 8× lower CD (1.35 vs. 11.01×10⁻²), 8.3× lower HD (8.42 vs. 69.70×10⁻²), and 4.3× lower BCD (2.91 vs. 12.44×10⁻²) while using 16× fewer vertices (23K vs. 380K). The one metric where DiffGI does not lead is ABO reconstruction NC (0.83 vs. Omages' 0.89), which the authors attribute to information loss from 8× spatial compression on flat furniture surfaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines: geometry-image-based 3D generation (Omages, GIMDiffusion, GarmageNet), which parameterizes surfaces onto 2D UV grids but relies on resolution-dependent binary occupancy maps, and differentiable iso-surface extraction (DMTet, FlexiCubes, DMC), which makes 3D-to-mesh conversion part of the optimization loop but operates on volumetric grids with cubic memory scaling. DiffGI's core departure is transplanting the differentiable extraction idea into the 2D geometry-image setting via a Differentiable Marching Squares module over a continuous 2D TSDF, replacing the hard binary mask with a subpixel-precise signed distance field. The VAE is initialized from Stable Diffusion 1.5 weights, and the generative backbone uses a DiT with flow matching, following the broader latent-diffusion paradigm for 3D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for industrial garment and thin-shell 3D pipelines: the 32×32×4 latent space enables real-time generation on consumer GPUs (and even CPU), and the UV-friendly output is directly usable for physics simulation and material editing—something implicit volumetric methods cannot provide. Key limitations remain: cross-chart seams are visible where neighboring UV patches meet, TSDF linear interpolation rounds extreme sharp edges, and no texture or PBR attributes are generated. Open questions include whether cross-chart boundary consistency can be enforced within the differentiable framework, whether the approach extends to high-frequency sharp-edge domains (e.g., mechanical parts), and whether the compact latent space can be expanded to jointly encode geometry and appearance.
