## Problem

Existing LVLM personalization methods either require inference-time training for each new concept (reducing deployment efficiency) or rely on vocabulary expansion with separate retraining, and they struggle to maintain accuracy in complex multi-image, multi-concept settings where each concept is defined by multiple diverse reference images. The paper asks how to generate continuous prompts that encode multimodal concepts with sufficient clarity for reliable reasoning across multiple concepts simultaneously, while avoiding any inference-time training.

## Input / Output

The method takes 1–6 reference images per concept (of varying sizes), a query image, a text query, and generic concept labels (e.g., <sks1>) as input. The reference images are processed by the LVLM's own frozen vision encoder to extract hierarchical patch-level features, which are then distilled into a variable-length continuous visual prompt (up to 20 tokens, dynamically pruned per concept) and a single label embedding. The output is a standard text response—VQA answer, caption, existence recognition, or multiple-choice selection—generated by the frozen LVLM with the in-context prompts injected into its prompt, using only 13.6 tokens per concept on average versus 16 for fixed-length baselines.

## Method

ICPT simulates in-context learning entirely in the latent space of a frozen LVLM via an Adaptive Concept Projector (ACP) that fuses early- and deep-layer patch features from the built-in vision encoder, then distills them through a single-layer cross-attention module with learnable latent queries into a full-capacity visual prompt and a dedicated label embedding. A Dynamic Token Router (DTR) adaptively assigns per-token sigmoid scores to prune redundant visual tokens, yielding variable-length prompts matched to each concept's visual complexity. Two geometric regularizations ensure robustness: Contextual Variation Memory (CVM) maintains a FIFO queue of pairwise environmental-difference directions and constrains prompts to be orthogonal to this subspace (with a formal interference bound proven in Theorem 1), while Margin-constrained Concept Separation (MCS) enforces a soft-margin upper bound on cosine similarity between different concepts' visual prompts and label embeddings, preserving shared semantic priors. Training optimizes a three-term objective (VQA cross-entropy + geometric constraints + L1 sparsity on DTR scores) updating only the ACP parameters while keeping the entire LVLM backbone frozen.

## Results

On LLaVA-NeXT-7B, ICPT achieves weighted scores of 0.868 (Recognition), 0.791 (MVQA), 0.702 (OVQA), and 0.654 (Captioning), outperforming the strongest baseline MC-LLaVA by 0.057, 0.058, 0.058, and 0.067 respectively. ICPT also surpasses GPT-4o+ICL across all four task types while using only 13.6 tokens per concept on average (vs. 16 fixed tokens for MC-LLaVA). Gains are largest in multi-concept settings, e.g., +0.067 on OVQA-multi and +0.086 on Captioning-multi over MC-LLaVA. ICPT generalizes consistently across LLaVA-NeXT-34B, InternVL3-8B, and Qwen3VL-8B, achieving weighted Recognition scores of 0.904, 0.932, and 0.950 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICPT outperforms all seven baselines across every task type and setting. Against the strongest prior method MC-LLaVA (multi-concept, vocabulary-expansion based), ICPT gains +0.057 on Recognition, +0.058 on MVQA, +0.058 on OVQA, and +0.067 on Captioning (weighted scores), while using fewer tokens per concept (13.6 vs. 16). PLVM, which avoids inference-time training via an auxiliary vision encoder, is the second-strongest baseline but trails ICPT by 0.099 on Recognition and 0.095 on Captioning; notably, on InternVL3 and Qwen3VL, PLVM actually underperforms plain ICL on several tasks, whereas ICPT maintains consistent gains. MyVLM and Yo'LLaVA, which require per-concept vocabulary expansion and retraining, are limited to single-concept evaluation and score 0.718 and 0.686 on Recognition respectively, well below ICPT's 0.868. PVIT and RAP, which rely on post-training or retrieval augmentation, achieve mid-range scores (0.712 and 0.690 on Recognition) and do not close the gap to ICPT. Even the closed-source GPT-4o+ICL baseline (0.702 Recognition) is surpassed by ICPT, confirming that the learned in-context prompts provide a more efficient and accurate personalization mechanism than raw few-shot prompting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the soft-prompt personalization paradigm (MyVLM, Yo'LLaVA, MC-LLaVA) by eliminating the need for vocabulary expansion and per-concept inference-time training, instead simulating in-context learning entirely within the frozen LVLM's latent space. It departs from PLVM's reliance on an auxiliary vision encoder by repurposing the LVLM's own built-in encoder with hierarchical multi-scale feature extraction, and introduces two geometric regularizations (CVM and MCS) that have no direct analogue in prior personalization work. The Dynamic Token Router's adaptive prompt-length allocation also represents a departure from the fixed-length prompt convention used by all prior soft-prompt methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ICPT provides a practical, training-free-at-inference personalization pipeline that scales to multi-concept, multi-image settings and generalizes across four LVLM backbones, making it a strong candidate for deployment in user-facing LVLM systems. The observation that PLVM underperforms direct ICL on newer, stronger LVLMs (InternVL3, Qwen3VL) raises an open question about whether auxiliary-encoder-based personalization methods will remain viable as base models improve. Remaining limitations include the need for a curated training set to initialize the ACP, the fixed L_max=20 capacity that may constrain very complex concepts, and the lack of evaluation on extremely large concept sets (beyond 6 simultaneous concepts).
