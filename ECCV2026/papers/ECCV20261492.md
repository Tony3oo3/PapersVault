## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods, particularly rotation-based techniques (QuaRot, SpinQuant) designed for integer formats, suffer severe performance collapse when applied to MXFP4 due to a fundamental format mismatch: global orthogonal rotations transfer outlier energy across the 32-element quantization blocks, disrupting local scaling factors and inducing bimodal activation distributions that waste the limited 4-bit range. Even block-wise Hadamard rotations (BRQ, MR-GPTQ) fail to suppress extreme outliers within individual blocks and still produce bimodal distributions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full-precision model (Qwen3-8B or Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) and a small calibration set (128 text sequences of length 2048 for LLMs, or 128 image-text pairs from GQA for MLLMs) to learn per-layer transformation parameters over 5 epochs. The output is a quantized model with MXFP4 weights and activations, configurable KV cache precision (16/8/4-bit), where weight-side affine transformations are fused offline into the linear layers and activation-side transformations are applied online at inference, adding minimal runtime overhead.

## Method

BATQuant introduces a Block-wise Affine Transformation (BAT) that partitions the transformation matrix into block-diagonal components strictly aligned with the MXFP quantization granularity (g=32), preventing cross-block outlier energy transfer while relaxing orthogonality constraints to learn format-optimal affine matrices. To reduce the N·g parameter overhead, a Global and Private Kronecker (GPK) decomposition factors each block matrix P_i = B_i ⊗ A, sharing a global basis A (size g₁×g₁) across all k blocks while retaining block-specific private matrices B_i (size g₂×g₂), cutting parameters by >74% versus FlatQuant. A Block-wise Learnable Clipping mechanism further suppresses residual outliers by applying sigmoid-constrained, per-block dynamic thresholds to the min/max values. All parameters are optimized by minimizing layer-wise output MSE between full-precision and quantized layers over the calibration set, with the GPTQ weight quantizer applied on top for additional accuracy gains.

## Results

On Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct under the aggressive W4A4KV16 MXFP4 configuration, BATQuant achieves a 96.43% average recovery rate across five multimodal benchmarks (MME, OCR-Bench, DocVQA, RealWorldQA, VLMBlind), outperforming the strongest baseline FlatQuant (94.79%) by 1.64%. Under the milder W4A8KV16 setting, BATQuant reaches 99.29% recovery—the only method with less than 1% degradation. On Qwen3-8B reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH-500, AIME24/25, GPQA-D), BATQuant achieves 92.45% recovery under W4A4KV16, surpassing GPTQ (90.10%) and FlatQuant (86.78%) by 2.35% and 5.67% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BATQuant consistently outperforms all six baselines across every quantization configuration tested. Against the strongest affine-transformation baseline FlatQuant, BATQuant gains 1.64% recovery on W4A4KV16 multimodal (96.43% vs. 94.79%) and 0.92% on W4A8KV16 reasoning (97.46% vs. 95.99%). Rotation-based methods (QuaRot, SpinQuant) suffer the largest collapse under W4A4KV16, dropping to 89.69% and 88.32% respectively on multimodal benchmarks—nearly 8 points below BATQuant—confirming the paper's central claim that global rotations are incompatible with MXFP block-wise scaling. Block-rotation BRQ (90.74%) and SmoothQuant (95.01%) also trail BATQuant by 5.7% and 1.4% on W4A4KV16 multimodal. On reasoning tasks under W4A4KV16, BATQuant (92.45%) leads GPTQ (90.10%) by 2.35% and FlatQuant (86.78%) by 5.67%, with the gap widening on AIME24 (62.08 vs. 57.50 for GPTQ) where multi-step error accumulation is most severe. Under the milder W4A8KV16 setting, all methods perform comparably (97–99% recovery), but BATQuant is the only method exceeding 99% on multimodal and 97% on reasoning, indicating near-lossless quantization.

## Relation to Prior Work

BATQuant extends the affine-transformation line of work initiated by FlatQuant [45], which demonstrated that relaxing orthogonality improves quantization accuracy, but restricts the transformation scope to match MXFP's 32-element block granularity to prevent cross-block outlier propagation. It also builds on BRQ [44], which first identified the incompatibility between global rotations and MXFP4 and proposed block-wise Hadamard rotations, but replaces the fixed orthogonal Hadamard with a learnable affine matrix to address BRQ's residual outliers and bimodal distribution artifacts. The GPK decomposition is a novel parameterization that shares a global Kronecker factor across blocks while retaining per-block private components, reducing storage by over 74% compared to FlatQuant's approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MXFP4 becomes the native precision on next-generation accelerators (NVIDIA Blackwell, AMD CDNA4), accurate PTQ for this format is a practical bottleneck for deploying LLMs and MLLMs at scale; BATQuant's 96.43% recovery under W4A4KV16 and near-lossless W4A8KV16 results make it a strong candidate for production pipelines. The key open question is whether the block-alignment principle generalizes to other microscaling variants (e.g., NVFP4 with different block sizes) or to pre-training/fine-tuning scenarios. The method still requires a calibration set and per-layer optimization, and the ~3.5% residual gap under the most aggressive setting suggests further headroom for improvement, particularly on reasoning-heavy tasks where error compounding is most severe.
