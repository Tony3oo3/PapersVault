## Problem

CLIP-style video-text models inherit entangled representations from image-text pretraining, and two video-specific challenges exacerbate this: temporal misalignment (captions describe only constrained temporal windows, leaving other frames text-irrelevant) and semantic asymmetry (a sparse, bidirectional, non-equivalent relevance between frame-level visual details and caption-level concepts). No prior method provides both a theoretical identification guarantee and a practical mechanism to disentangle frame-specific concepts from global caption semantics in long video-text pairs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip (up to 64 frames) and a paired text caption (up to 248 tokens, exceeding CLIP's 77-token limit) and produces three outputs: a global video embedding and a global text embedding for retrieval, plus per-frame binary text masks (selecting which caption concepts are relevant to each frame) and per-frame binary visual concept masks (selecting which visual factors in each frame correspond to the active text subset). Masks are learned via sigmoid outputs binarized with a straight-through estimator, operating in the shared latent space dimension of the CLIP encoder.

## Method

MoVA learns dual asymmetric projections: a Temporal Mask Network (two-layer Transformer) that takes each frame's embedding and positional encoding as a query to attend over all text tokens, outputting a per-frame binary mask over the global text representation; and a Concept Mask Network (single-layer Transformer with attention pooling) that selects which visual-dimension factors in a frame align with the already-selected text subset. Training combines a global CLIP4Clip-style retrieval loss with a frame-level contrastive loss in the text→video direction (pulling each frame to its masked text while pushing away from other frames' masked texts) and two modular contrastive terms in the video→video direction—Same-Frame Different-Mask and Different-Frame Same-Mask—that enforce disentanglement of frame-specific factors. Sparsity penalties on both mask sets encourage minimal active supports, and the paper proves that under smoothness, full joint support, temporal stability, and view-diversity conditions, the learned representations are block-wise identifiable, guaranteeing recovery of frame-text correspondences from weak clip-caption supervision.

## Results

On ActivityNet, MoVA achieves R@1 of 47.8 (Text→Video) and 46.7 (Video→Text), outperforming the strongest baseline VideoCLIP-XL (46.9 / 37.7) by 0.9 and 9.0 points respectively. With DSL post-processing, MoVA† further reaches R@1 of 53.6 / 54.4. On long-caption benchmarks, MoVA leads by a wide margin: 62.4 R@1 on VideoUFO (vs. 57.4 for VideoCLIP-XL) and 58.5 on UltraVideo (vs. 51.8 for ProST). On VBench for text-to-video generation, MoVA attains the highest average score of 0.8708, surpassing SmartCLIP (0.8659) and CLIP (0.8456).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoVA outperforms all eight video-text retrieval baselines on ActivityNet across every reported metric. The closest competitor, VideoCLIP-XL [49], leads on Text→Video R@1 (46.9 vs. 47.8, a 0.9-point gap) but is substantially weaker on Video→Text R@1 (37.7 vs. 46.7, a 9.0-point gap), highlighting MoVA's stronger bidirectional alignment. Among the remaining baselines, ProST [29] and CLIP4Clip [30] are the strongest on Video→Text R@1 (43.7 and 42.9 respectively), yet both trail MoVA by 3+ points. On long-caption datasets the advantage widens: MoVA beats VideoCLIP-XL by 5.0 R@1 on VideoUFO and 6.7 on UltraVideo, while ProST leads the other baselines on UltraVideo (51.8) but still falls 6.7 points short of MoVA. For text-to-video generation (VBench), MoVA surpasses both CLIP [37] (0.8456 avg) and SmartCLIP [61] (0.8659 avg) with an average of 0.8708, with the largest gains in aesthetic quality (0.7126 vs. 0.6271 for CLIP) and imaging quality (0.7023 vs. 0.6822).

## Relation to Prior Work

MoVA extends SmartCLIP's modular vision-language alignment from the image-text domain to video by introducing dual asymmetric projections: a Temporal Mask Network (text→frame) and a Concept Mask Network (frame→text), where SmartCLIP only learns a single-direction visual→text mask. Theoretically, it builds on multi-view latent variable identification frameworks [47, 68] but is the first to derive identification conditions for temporally indexed video-text data without requiring explicit segment labels. Architecturally, it inherits the CLIP-style contrastive learning paradigm and ViT backbones from CLIP4Clip and subsequent video-text retrieval work, but replaces global alignment with frame-level modular contrastive objectives and sparsity-regularized mask selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first temporal identification guarantee for CLIP-style video-text models, giving a principled justification for why modular, frame-level alignment should outperform global alignment on long videos with temporally sparse captions. The finding that unidirectional masking (SmartCLIP's approach) degrades sharply in the video setting (R@1 drops ~12 points) validates the bidirectional asymmetry argument and suggests that future video-text foundation models should incorporate frame-aware text selection rather than treating captions as monolithic. Open questions include whether the dual-mask framework scales to multi-event narratives spanning minutes, how it interacts with autoregressive video generation beyond encoder replacement, and whether the identification conditions hold under the noisier, less-curated captions found in web-scale video corpora.
