## Problem

Most VLA methods treat action representation as a secondary design choice: discrete tokenization is non-differentiable and prone to codebook collapse, raw regression lacks structural constraints, and diffusion-based heads require iterative inference. The paper argues that action space design is a primary bottleneck and asks how to learn a structured continuous action latent space so that vision-language-to-action mapping becomes explicit alignment to meaningful motion primitives rather than implicit alignment achieved through scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260162/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes two time-synchronized RGB views (third-person and wrist-mounted), a proprioceptive state vector encoding current EEF pose and gripper state, and a natural-language instruction. It outputs a continuous action chunk of T=8 timesteps in relative end-effector pose space, produced in a single forward pass by predicting a latent code and decoding it through a frozen Stage-1 decoder—no iterative denoising or autoregressive token generation is required at inference.

## Method

LEAP-VLA is a two-stage framework that decouples action structure learning from multimodal grounding. Stage 1 learns a coarse-to-fine continuous latent action space via Multi-level Soft Residual Quantization (MSRQ): a BiCT encoder maps action trajectories to latent codes, which are then quantized across L=4 residual levels using soft attention (L2-distance softmax) over K=256 learnable prototypes per level with learnable temperature and scale, and a BiCT decoder reconstructs actions; training uses only action data with L1 reconstruction and entropy-regularization losses. Stage 2 freezes the latent space and trains a lightweight external Aligner (~100M params) on a frozen InternVL3-1B VLM (LoRA rank 32) that extracts multi-layer visual and textual features, fuses them with proprioception via a 3-way joint Aligner Attention mechanism (self, language-proprioception, and visual channels with a learnable visual gate), and predicts the latent code through a Prototype-Aware Output Head that constrains outputs to convex combinations of the frozen Stage-1 prototypes, guaranteeing predictions remain within the learned action manifold.

## Results

On LIBERO, LEAP-VLA achieves a 97.1% average success rate across all four suites (Spatial 98.4%, Object 99.4%, Goal 97.6%, Long 93.0%), the highest among all compared methods, while using only a 1B backbone (InternVL3-1B) with ~100M trainable parameters and no embodied pretraining. On CALVIN ABC→D, LEAP-VLA achieves a mean completed sequence length of 4.28 (96.5% on task 1, 73.0% on task 5), surpassing all baselines including VLM4VLA (4.06) and OpenVLA-OFT (4.10). The action representation ablation on LIBERO-Spatial shows that moving from raw action regression to latent-space prediction yields +2.2% (92.4→94.6%), and adding the Prototype-Aware Output Head adds a further +3.8% (94.6→98.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEAP-VLA (1B, no robot pretraining) outperforms all 10 LIBERO baselines, achieving 97.1% average success rate versus the next-best UniVLA (95.2%, 7B, OXE-pretrained) and π₀ (94.2%, 3B, OXE-pretrained), a margin of +1.9% and +2.9% respectively. On CALVIN ABC→D, LEAP-VLA's 4.28 mean sequence length surpasses VLM4VLA (4.06, 8.3B, no robot PT) by +0.22 and OpenVLA-OFT (4.10, 7B, OXE-pretrained) by +0.18, while using 8.3× fewer parameters than VLM4VLA. Among quantization baselines in Stage 1, MSRQ achieves the lowest reconstruction MAE (1.5×10⁻³ on LIBERO-Spatial) with near-perfect codebook utilization (99.6% entropy ratio), outperforming SoftVQ (4.9×10⁻³, 90.6%) and all discrete RVQ variants (MAE ≥ 7.7×10⁻³). The action representation ablation confirms that the structured latent target (+2.2%) and Prototype-Aware Output Head (+3.8%) are the primary drivers of the gap over raw-action regression (92.4%), rather than backbone scale or pretraining data. Notably, LEAP-VLA matches or exceeds methods 3–8.5× larger in parameter count without any large-scale embodied pretraining, supporting the paper's central claim that action-space structure is a primary performance lever.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEAP-VLA extends the soft-attention quantization mechanism of SoftVQ-VAE from single-level image tokenization to a multi-level residual hierarchy (MSRQ) for robot actions, replacing non-differentiable hard codebook assignment with fully differentiable soft prototype selection. Architecturally, it departs from both discrete-tokenization VLAs (OpenVLA, UniVLA, VQ-VLA) and iterative denoising approaches (π0, GR00T N1) by formulating action generation as single-pass prediction in a frozen continuous latent space, and it adopts the multi-layer VLM feature extraction insight from VLA-Adapter and GR00T N1 while keeping the VLM entirely unmodified via external LoRA adapters. The two-stage decoupling—learning action structure from action-only data first, then aligning multimodal features to that frozen space—is a deliberate departure from end-to-end VLA training that conflates representation learning with grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that structured action representation is a primary performance lever comparable to or exceeding model scale and pretraining data—challenges the field's default assumption that larger backbones and more embodied data are the main drivers of VLA progress. If validated beyond simulation, the MSRQ latent space could serve as a reusable, embodiment-agnostic action vocabulary, and the single-pass inference pipeline removes the latency cost of iterative denoising. Key open questions include whether the gains transfer to physical robots, whether a shared MSRQ space enables cross-embodiment generalization, and whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or multi-arm action spaces beyond the 7-DoF EEF setting evaluated here.
