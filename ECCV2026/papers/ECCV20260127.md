## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models suffer from 'spatial blindness,' struggling with fine-grained geometric reasoning and physical dynamics because their discriminative encoders focus on semantic presence rather than precise spatial structure. Existing remedies rely on explicit 3D inputs (point clouds, depth) or complex geometric scaffolding (BEV reconstruction, 3D distillation), which are bottlenecked by 3D data scarcity and task-specific annotation pipelines. This paper asks whether the implicit 3D priors already learned by large-scale video generation models—trained to enforce geometric and physical consistency for temporal coherence—can be directly repurposed as label-free spatial anchors for MLLMs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32 uniformly sampled RGB frames per 3D scan plus a text instruction. The video is first compressed by a VAE into a latent representation, then perturbed along the Flow Matching noising path at a fixed intermediate timestep (k=300, t=0.3) before being fed through the frozen DiT backbone with an empty text prompt to suppress semantic hallucination. Outputs are standard MLLM text responses (grounding boxes, dense captions, QA answers), spatial-reasoning answers on VSI-Bench, or robot action policies on LIBERO, with the generative branch contributing only at the visual-token level and leaving the language backbone and action head unchanged.

## Method

VEGA-3D introduces a dual-branch visual encoding that pairs a discriminative semantic encoder (SigLIP) with a frozen video diffusion model (Wan2.1-T2V 1.3B) repurposed as a Latent World Simulator. The key extraction step injects Gaussian noise into the VAE-encoded latent at an intermediate denoising timestep (t=0.3) and reads features from the 20th DiT layer, where the model is forced to engage its learned physics to restore 3D structure, yielding dense spatiotemporal geometric cues. A token-level Adaptive Gated Fusion module then projects both feature streams into the LLM hidden dimension and computes a per-token sigmoid gate over their layer-normalized concatenation, producing a convex combination that dynamically arbitrates between semantic and geometric priors for each spatial location. The video generator remains entirely frozen throughout training; only the fusion module, projectors, and MLLM parameters are updated via standard cross-entropy supervision, making the framework plug-and-play and scalable to any future video generation backbone.

## Results

On 3D scene understanding (Table 1), VEGA-3D achieves the best average rank (2.2) among all generalist methods, with headline numbers of ScanRefer Acc@0.5 = 56.2, Multi3DRefer F1@0.5 = 55.1, and SQA3D EM = 61.3, consistently outperforming the Video-3D LLM baseline (51.7, 52.7, 58.6 respectively). On VSI-Bench spatial reasoning, VEGA-3D attains an average score of 50.5, surpassing the Qwen2.5VL-7B baseline (48.9) and the strongest spatial-enhanced model VG-LLM-8B (50.1). On LIBERO robotic manipulation, VEGA-3D achieves 97.3% average success rate, marginally exceeding the OpenVLA-OFT baseline (97.0%) and substantially outperforming prior VLA methods like UniVLA (95.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VEGA-3D outperforms its direct baseline Video-3D LLM on every grounding metric (ScanRefer Acc@0.5: 56.2 vs 51.7; Multi3DRefer F1@0.5: 55.1 vs 52.7) and SQA3D (61.3 vs 58.6), while incurring a minor CIDEr drop on Scan2Cap (83.2 vs 83.8). Against the strongest geometric-supervision baselines, VEGA-3D beats 3DRS on ScanRefer (63.2 vs 62.9) and Multi3DRefer (60.8 vs 60.4) but trails on ScanQA CIDEr (106.3 vs 104.8) and SQA3D (61.3 vs 60.6); it also surpasses Ross3D on ScanRefer (63.2 vs 61.1) and Multi3DRefer (60.8 vs 59.6) yet loses on ScanQA (106.3 vs 107.0) and SQA3D (61.3 vs 63.0), indicating that explicit BEV supervision still aids QA-style tasks. On VSI-Bench, VEGA-3D (50.5) edges out VG-LLM-8B (50.1) and the finetuned Qwen2.5VL-7B baseline (48.9), and substantially outperforms 3DRS-7B (45.9) and SpaceR-7B (45.6). On LIBERO, VEGA-3D (97.3%) marginally improves over OpenVLA-OFT (97.0%) with the largest gain on the Object suite (99.4% vs 98.3%), while remaining competitive with UniVLA (95.2%) without any action-conditioned training. Overall, VEGA-3D achieves the best average rank (2.2) without any explicit 3D annotations, demonstrating that label-free generative priors are competitive with methods requiring depth, camera poses, or BEV reconstruction targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

VEGA-3D departs from the two dominant paradigms for 3D scene understanding with MLLMs—explicit 3D modalities (point clouds, depth) and geometric scaffolding (BEV reconstruction, 3D distillation)—by instead repurposing a frozen video diffusion model (Wan2.1-T2V) as a 'Latent World Simulator' that supplies implicit spatial priors without any 3D supervision. It builds directly on Video-3D LLM as the MLLM backbone, Qwen2.5VL for spatial reasoning, and OpenVLA-OFT for manipulation, adding a plug-and-play generative branch with an Adaptive Gated Fusion module. The conceptual lineage draws on the observation that video generators must internalize 3D geometry for temporal coherence, but the paper is the first to systematically extract and fuse these intermediate denoising features into a discriminative MLLM pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that advances in video generation models translate directly into stronger 3D understanding at zero annotation cost, offering a scalable alternative to data-hungry 3D supervision pipelines. The finding that intermediate noise levels (t≈0.3) and intermediate DiT layers are most informative provides a concrete recipe for feature extraction. However, the inference overhead of running a 1.3B video diffusion model per scene (mitigated by caching) limits real-time deployment, and the paper acknowledges the need for distillation into lightweight encoders. Open questions include whether these priors generalize to dynamic scenes, whether the approach scales to larger VLA models for closed-loop control, and whether the modest CIDEr drop on captioning reflects a fundamental semantic-geometry trade-off.
