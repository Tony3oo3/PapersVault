## Problem

Specialized text spotters excel on natural scene text but fail on complex artistic text, while MLLMs offer strong recognition yet produce unreliable bounding-box coordinates. The paper identifies a complementary weakness: SFT improves detection but suffers from instance order dependency, whereas GRPO improves recognition but provides only sparse, coarse-grained reward signals insufficient for precise coordinate learning. No prior work jointly addresses both limitations within a single training framework for the artistic text spotting scenario.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262570/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image containing text (including stylized, overlapping, or artistically arranged text) and produces a structured JSON-like list of dictionaries, each with a 'bbox' field (four numerical coordinates forming a quadrilateral) and a 'text' field (the recognized string). The output is generated autoregressively by the MLLM, with a maximum sequence length of 1024 tokens, and is parsed post-hoc for evaluation. Training uses LoRA fine-tuning on Qwen2.5-VL (3B/7B) or Qwen3-VL-8B with a combined dataset of ~30k samples from five benchmarks.

## Method

SupGRPO augments standard GRPO training with a matching-based online SFT loss applied exclusively to coordinate tokens. During each GRPO step, the model samples G=8 output sequences; for each sequence, predicted bounding boxes are matched to ground-truth boxes using a dual criterion (identical text content AND IoU > 0), and a cross-entropy loss is computed only on the GT coordinate tokens of successfully matched pairs, conditioned on the sampled context. This design avoids the arbitrary instance ordering imposed by standard SFT while providing the fine-grained token-level supervision that GRPO's sparse rewards (format, word-level F1, IoU precision, IoU recall) cannot supply for localization. The combined objective is L_SupGRPO = −J_GRPO + λ·E[L_SFT-coord] with λ=1e-4, trained end-to-end via LoRA on 4 A100 GPUs for one epoch.

## Results

On the artistic text spotting benchmark ATS, TS-VL-8B (Qwen3-VL-8B + SupGRPO) achieves 92.4 word-level F1 for recognition and 88.8 for detection, substantially outperforming all generic MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 72.7/27.4) and specialized spotters (e.g., Bridge at 67.2 end-to-end). On standard scene text benchmarks, TS-VL-8B reaches 93.4 (Total-Text Full), 95.1 (ICDAR 2015 S), and 92.5 (CTW1500 Full) for recognition, while its detection scores of 90.1 (Total-Text) and 90.5 (ICDAR 2015) match or exceed the best specialized detectors (Bridge: 89.2/90.5). The 3B variant (TS-VL-3B) already achieves 84.4 recognition and 71.6 detection on ATS, demonstrating strong scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TS-VL-8B with SupGRPO outperforms all generic MLLM baselines by large margins: on ATS recognition it scores 92.4 vs. 81.6 (Gemini 3.1-Pro), 80.8 (HunyuanOCR), and 72.7 (vanilla Qwen2.5-VL-7B); on ATS detection it reaches 88.8 vs. 27.4 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and 21.2 (InternVL3.5-8B). Against specialized text spotters, TS-VL-8B's detection of 90.1 on Total-Text and 90.5 on ICDAR 2015 matches or exceeds Bridge (89.2/90.5) and DeepSolo (87.3/90.0), while its end-to-end spotting score of 83.7 on ATS far surpasses the best specialist (Bridge at 67.2). The ablation baselines confirm the synergy: SFT alone yields 63.3 detection / 77.1 recognition on ATS, GRPO alone yields 62.0 / 80.0, and the two-stage SFT→GRPO pipeline reaches 70.6 / 87.2, all inferior to SupGRPO's 71.6 / 84.4 (3B) or 88.8 / 92.4 (8B). Notably, GRPO alone improves recognition over SFT (80.0 vs. 77.1) but worsens detection (62.0 vs. 63.3), validating the paper's core motivation for joint training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath to the text spotting task, introducing task-specific reward functions (format, text F1, IoU precision, IoU recall) that directly optimize non-differentiable evaluation metrics. It departs from prior MLLM-based OCR work (TextMonkey, Vary, Qwen2.5-VL) that treats recognition and localization as a single generation problem, by explicitly addressing the complementary weaknesses of SFT (fine-grained coordinate supervision but instance-order dependency) and GRPO (order-agnostic set-level optimization but sparse coordinate signal). The matching-based online SFT mechanism is the key conceptual bridge: it applies token-level cross-entropy loss only to coordinate tokens of successfully matched predictions, inheriting SFT's precision without its sequence-ordering artifact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that MLLMs, when trained with the right joint objective, can match or exceed specialized text spotters on both detection and recognition, including on the newly introduced artistic text benchmark (ATS) where prior specialized methods plateau around 65–67% detection. The complementary SFT-for-detection / GRPO-for-recognition finding, validated through clean ablations, suggests a general recipe for tasks requiring both precise spatial output and open-ended reasoning. Open questions include the cold-start problem for architectures lacking spatial grounding, the fragility of the IoU+text matching heuristic in extremely dense scenes, and whether the joint SFT+RL paradigm transfers to other fine-grained localization tasks beyond text spotting.
