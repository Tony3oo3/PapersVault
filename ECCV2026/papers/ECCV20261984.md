## Problem

Reconstructing animatable human avatars from monocular video requires modeling both global articulated motion and local non-rigid deformations (e.g., clothing wrinkles), but sparse monocular observations introduce self-occlusions and invisible surfaces that cause severe geometric artifacts. Existing photometric-loss-based methods (NeRF, 3DGS) do not enforce temporal consistency or geometric fidelity, while implicit SDF-based approaches suffer from mesh extraction artifacts and entangled geometry-texture representations. The paper addresses how to learn pose-conditioned semi-rigid deformations that are temporally stable and geometrically coherent despite these monocular ambiguities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single monocular RGB video along with per-frame body pose parameters (SMPL-X) and camera intrinsics/extrinsics. Pseudo ground-truth signals (normal maps, segmentation masks) are extracted by the Sapiens foundation model, and a pretrained optical flow network provides temporal supervision. The output is a hybrid representation: an explicit animatable mesh (initialized from MHR template at LoD 3, subdivided twice) with per-face 3D Gaussians for photorealistic rendering, plus the learned neural Jacobian fields that enable real-time pose-driven animation of both geometry and appearance.

## Method

JacobianAvatar predicts per-triangle deformation gradients via two hierarchical MLPs (coarse at 64-resolution tri-plane, fine at 128-resolution) conditioned on body pose, with the fine network receiving coarse latent features as additional input. The predicted Jacobian matrices are converted to vertex displacements by solving a screened Poisson equation (adding a λ‖Φ−V‖² term to the standard NJF objective), which anchors invisible/occluded vertices to their canonical positions. Three geometric constraints address monocular ambiguities: (1) the screened Poisson solver prevents large stretching in unobserved regions, (2) an SDF-based regularizer computes approximate normals from a voxel grid of the initial mesh and enforces cosine similarity on deformed face/vertex normals to preserve global shape, and (3) a deformation-guided residual flow loss projects 3D vertex displacements to 2D, feeds them as initial estimates to a pretrained flow network, and minimizes the residual flow to enforce sub-pixel temporal consistency. Training proceeds sequentially (coarse Jacobian → fine Jacobian → appearance → Gaussians) using a mesh renderer for geometry and a GS rasterizer for high-fidelity appearance, all self-supervised from the monocular video.

## Results

On MonoPerfCap (view extrapolation), JacobianAvatar achieves the best PSNR of 31.83 dB and lowest LPIPS of 1.67, outperforming the strongest baseline ExAvatar (30.47 dB, 2.00 LPIPS) by 1.36 dB. On DNA-Rendering (loose clothing, novel views), it leads all metrics with PSNR 29.90, SSIM 0.971, and LPIPS 2.19. On SynWild geometry, the explicit mesh approach achieves the best Chamfer Distance (2.46×10⁻³), Normal Error (0.091), and F1@2cm (0.681), surpassing implicit SDF-based methods by a wide margin (e.g., CD 2.46 vs. 9.30 for GoMAvatar). On NeuMan, results are competitive (PSNR 29.75) but slightly behind GaussianAvatar (29.94) and 3DGS-Avatar (29.75) in LPIPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JacobianAvatar consistently outperforms all SDF-based baselines (Vid2Avatar, LSAvatar, FacAvatar) across every dataset and metric, with the largest gaps on MonoPerfCap (e.g., +3.87 dB PSNR over LSAvatar) and SynWild geometry (CD 2.46 vs. 2.55–2.91). Against the strongest 3DGS-based competitor ExAvatar, it wins on MonoPerfCap (+1.36 dB PSNR), DNA-Rendering (+1.36 dB), and SynWild geometry (CD 2.46 vs. 9.39), but trails slightly on NeuMan (29.75 vs. 29.37 PSNR is actually a win; however LPIPS 1.94 vs. 1.87 is a narrow loss). On NeuMan, GaussianAvatar achieves the best LPIPS (1.24) and PSNR (29.94), edging out JacobianAvatar (1.94, 29.75), likely due to its dedicated Gaussian deformation field for clothing dynamics. GoMAvatar, despite its mesh-anchored Gaussians, lags significantly on geometry (CD 9.30 on SynWild) and rendering (28.35 PSNR on MonoPerfCap), confirming that explicit mesh deformation via NJF yields superior geometric coherence. Overall, the method establishes state-of-the-art on 3 of 4 datasets for rendering and achieves the best geometry on SynWild, with NeuMan being the only dataset where a 3DGS-based method (GaussianAvatar) marginally leads in perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies Neural Jacobian Fields (NJF) [2]—previously used for general mesh deformation—to human avatar reconstruction, combining them with a screened Poisson solver to handle the partial observations inherent in monocular video. It departs from the dominant NeRF/implicit-SDF line (Vid2Avatar, LSAvatar, FacAvatar) by using explicit mesh deformation, and from 3DGS-only methods (ExAvatar, GoMAvatar) by grounding Gaussians on a deformed mesh rather than free-floating splats. The coarse-to-fine hierarchical Jacobian training and the deformation-guided residual flow loss are novel contributions that address temporal consistency and occlusion artifacts not handled by prior NJF or 3DGS avatar work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit mesh-based deformation via NJF can match or exceed implicit SDF methods on geometric fidelity while enabling real-time rendering through anchored 3DGS, offering a practical path for animatable avatar pipelines. The combination of screened Poisson, SDF-based Jacobian regularization, and residual flow provides a principled toolkit for handling monocular ambiguities (self-occlusion, invisible surfaces) that plagues all single-view avatar methods. Key open questions remain: the method cannot model loose-fitting garments that decouple from body motion, lacks facial expression modeling, and the 10-hour training time on a single GPU limits scalability to large datasets or interactive applications.
