## Problem

Existing multimodal deception detection methods apply a single, fixed fusion strategy across all samples and rely on one encoder per modality, ignoring two critical phenomena: (1) modality conflict, where a liar's visual and audio cues may contradict each other, making some modalities unreliable for specific samples; and (2) cue heterogeneity, where different individuals express deception through entirely different behavioral patterns (e.g., eye movements vs. subtle facial expressions) within the same modality. These limitations restrict both the adaptability of cross-modal weighting and the representational capacity for diverse deceptive cues.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video segment and extracts two streams: face clips (64 uniformly sampled frames resized to 160×160 via AlphaPose, encoded by a 2D-CNN to 256-dim features) and raw speech (resampled, encoded by a 1D-CNN to 512-dim features). Both are linearly projected to 768 dimensions, and a third 'interaction' modality is derived via cross-modal attention between the visual and audio embeddings. The output is a binary deception label (truthful vs. lying) produced by a feed-forward network on the fused representation, evaluated with ACC, F1, and AUC.

## Method

MoMCE introduces a dual-layer Mixture-of-Experts architecture. At the cue level, each modality instantiates M experts (default 8) on a shared frozen ViT or Wav2Vec 2.0 backbone, differentiated by learnable expert prompts that bias the encoder toward distinct feature subspaces; a prompt-aware routing network computes per-sample expert weights from gating prompts, and a cue diversity loss (cosine-similarity penalty across expert representations) prevents collapse onto a single dominant cue. At the modality level, the three modality-level features (visual, audio, interaction) are treated as experts and dynamically weighted by a second prompt routing mechanism; a consistency-aware expert weight loss then constrains the routing entropy—pushing toward balanced weights (H*=1.0) for low-conflict samples and toward concentrated weights (H*=0.0) for high-conflict samples, where conflict is measured by pairwise cosine dissimilarity of modality representations. The total loss combines cross-entropy with weighted auxiliary terms for both the consistency-aware and diversity losses.

## Results

On the DOLOS dataset, MoMCE achieves 79.10% ACC, 82.38% F1, and 78.09% AUC, outperforming the strongest baseline DLF-BRAM (78.86% ACC, 82.27% F1, 77.72% AUC) and AFFAKT (68.10% ACC, 72.26% AUC). On Bag-of-Lies, MoMCE reaches 61.09% ACC and 60.93% AUC, surpassing DLF-BRAM (60.05% ACC, 61.07% AUC) in accuracy. On MU3D, MoMCE attains 58.58% ACC, 60.35% F1, and 58.69% AUC, leading all baselines by at least 1.95% in accuracy. Overall, MoMCE achieves the best result on 7 out of 9 metrics across the three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoMCE outperforms all fusion-based baselines (LieNet F+A, PECL F+A, AFFAKT F+A) by a wide margin on DOLOS, gaining 11.0% ACC over PECL (64.75→79.10) and 11.0% over AFFAKT (68.10→79.10). Against the strongest overall baseline DLF-BRAM (which uses face+limb), MoMCE wins on DOLOS ACC (+0.24%), BOL ACC (+1.04%), and MU3D ACC (+1.95%), but trails slightly on BOL AUC (60.93 vs. 61.07) and BOL F1 (52.91 vs. 55.63), which the authors attribute to DLF-BRAM's use of limb cues that are informative in the Bag-of-Lies dataset. On MU3D, MoMCE leads DLF-BRAM by 1.95% ACC, 1.93% F1, and 2.95% AUC. Compared to PECL (the most recent audio-visual baseline), MoMCE improves DOLOS AUC by 15.38 points (62.71→78.09) and MU3D AUC by 3.38 points (55.31→58.69). The only metric where MoMCE does not lead is BOL F1, where FCN's handcrafted facial features yield higher recall (63.26 vs. 52.91), though FCN underperforms substantially on ACC and AUC across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm—previously applied to multimodal learning for tasks like dynamic fusion and modality missingness—into a dual-layer architecture where both modalities and intra-modality cues are treated as experts. It builds on Ding et al. (CVPR 2019) for the interaction-modality formulation via cross-modal attention and on Fang et al. (CVPR 2025) for the routing-entropy regularization, but departs from prior deception detection work (PECL, AFFAKT, DLF-BRAM) that applies a fixed fusion strategy to all samples by introducing prompt-aware, instance-specific routing at both the modality and cue levels. The key conceptual departure is using learnable prompts to instantiate multiple experts over a shared frozen backbone, enabling heterogeneous cue capture without duplicating encoder parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two underexplored failure modes in multimodal deception detection—modality conflict and inter-individual cue heterogeneity—with a principled MoE solution that is validated across three datasets and cross-domain settings. However, the absolute gains over the strongest baseline (DLF-BRAM) are modest (≤1% ACC), and the method's reliance on pre-trained ViT/W2V2 backbones plus AlphaPose face extraction limits its applicability to settings where such infrastructure is unavailable. Open questions include whether the prompt-based expert differentiation scales to more than 8 experts, whether the consistency-aware loss generalizes beyond binary deception labels, and whether the dual-layer MoE design transfers to other multimodal affective or forensic tasks.
