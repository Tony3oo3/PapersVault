## Problem

Existing diffusion-based makeup-transfer methods struggle to simultaneously achieve precise region-specific control, faithful makeup fidelity, and identity preservation. Pixel masks misalign with attention-space token interactions, causing makeup spillover into non-target areas; two-image conditioning blurs the boundary between what to transfer (makeup) and what to preserve (identity); and high-resolution, identity-consistent, region-labeled training pairs are scarce, forcing reliance on low-quality synthetic pseudo-pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261899/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source face image, one or more reference makeup images (which can be mixed across regions, e.g., eyes from one reference and lips from another), a text prompt specifying the edit, and binary region masks (face, eyes, or lips) at 1024×1024 resolution. It produces a single output face image where makeup is transferred only within the masked region while all other areas remain pixel-identical to the source. During training, the text prompt is restricted to three predefined classes, and the non-makeup image serves as source while the real makeup image serves as ground truth.

## Method

MagicMakeup builds on FLUX's MM-DiT backbone (fine-tuned via LoRA, rank 256) and introduces two key mechanisms. Token-Aligned Region Gating (TARG) projects pixel-level region masks onto the token grid, producing binary query and key gates; an additive logit mask (0 for in-ROI pairs, −∞ otherwise) is added to the latent-to-reference attention matrix, hard-blocking information flow outside the target region and eliminating spillover. Cross-Modal Perception Guidance (CMPG) extracts a preservation concept (identity cues) and a transfer concept (makeup attributes) from the text prompt, then at each denoising step performs joint self-attention between concept and image tokens, selects top-k concept-aligned visual queries, and uses cross-attention with AdaLayerNorm heads to produce span-aware text modulations and image modulations that are injected into the DiT streams, explicitly separating what to preserve from what to transfer. Training data is constructed by applying region-specific makeup removal (via FLUX-Kontext) to real 1024×1024 makeup photos, followed by cascaded identity-consistency, color-histogram, and region-precision filters, yielding 6,772 clean pairs across face, eye, and lip splits.

## Results

On both 1024×1024 benchmarks (MakeupHQ-Synthetic and MakeupHQ-Real), MagicMakeup achieves the best DINO-I (0.660 / 0.623), CLIP-I (0.816 / 0.799), L2M (56.4 / 49.8), and FID (59.2 / 76.3), outperforming all eight baselines on these four metrics. On the 256×256 Makeup-Wild dataset, it leads in CLIP-I (0.665) and L2M (25.8) but trails Stable-Makeup and Flux-Makeup on FID (92.9 vs. 85.1 / 88.7) and DINO-I (0.581 vs. 0.619). Face-ID scores are competitive but not always the highest, which the authors attribute to faithful makeup transfer inherently altering identity-related cues.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MagicMakeup decisively outperforms all GAN-based baselines (CPM, EleGANt, SSAT, CSD-MT) across every metric and dataset, with particularly large margins on L2M (e.g., 56.4 vs. 1610.9 for CPM on MakeupHQ-Synthetic) and FID. Among diffusion-based methods, it beats SHMT, MAD, Stable-Makeup, and Flux-Makeup on DINO-I, CLIP-I, L2M, and FID on both 1024×1024 benchmarks. The closest competitor is Flux-Makeup, which leads on Face-ID (0.806 vs. 0.730 synthetic; 0.792 vs. 0.706 real) and on Makeup-Wild FID (88.7 vs. 92.9), but MagicMakeup still surpasses it on all other metrics. On the lower-resolution Makeup-Wild set, Stable-Makeup edges ahead on DINO-I (0.619 vs. 0.581) and FID (85.1 vs. 92.9), which the authors attribute to distribution differences at 256×256 rather than a transfer failure. The Face-ID gap versus SHMT and Flux-Makeup is acknowledged as a trade-off: more faithful makeup transfer inherently shifts identity embeddings, so higher Face-ID can indicate less complete transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

MagicMakeup builds on the FLUX.1 Kontext MM-DiT architecture as its base diffusion transformer, fine-tuned via LoRA, and positions itself against a lineage of makeup-transfer methods spanning GAN-based approaches (CPM, EleGANt, SSAT, CSD-MT) and diffusion-based ones (SHMT, MAD, Stable-Makeup, Flux-Makeup). Its two core technical departures are conceptual: Token-Aligned Region Gating addresses the pixel-to-attention misalignment that prior mask-based methods (notably MAD) suffer from by projecting masks directly into the token grid, and Cross-Modal Perception Guidance explicitly disentangles transfer vs. preservation concepts in two-image conditioning, a problem prior methods handle implicitly. The data pipeline also departs from the common practice of synthesizing makeup onto bare faces, instead using region-specific makeup removal from real photos to construct training pairs, arguing this better preserves identity and texture fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of MakeupHQ (a 1024×1024 benchmark with dedicated face/eye/lip splits, cross-identity references, and both synthetic and real test sets) fills a notable gap, as prior datasets were low-resolution and lacked region annotations. The token-aligned gating mechanism is a generalizable technique for any diffusion-based editing task requiring spatially constrained attention, potentially applicable beyond makeup. However, the method does not yet support finer-grained components (blush, contour), degrades under extreme side-view poses due to data scarcity, and the Face-ID scores remain below some baselines that transfer less makeup, leaving open the question of how to maximize transfer fidelity without sacrificing identity metrics. The makeup-removal-based data pipeline offers a practical recipe for constructing high-quality paired data in other appearance-transfer domains.
