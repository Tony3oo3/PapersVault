## Problem

Saliency and attribution methods applied to Vision-Language Models frequently produce hallucinated explanations, highlighting image regions for objects absent from the scene (e.g., lighting up a dog when prompted 'cat'). While hallucination in model outputs is well-characterized, no formal mathematical analysis existed linking this failure to the geometry of multimodal embedding spaces. The paper identifies the root cause as Linear Semantic Leakage: the unavoidable non-orthogonality of text embeddings in contrastive VLMs, which causes query vectors to project onto distractor directions and inject ghost saliency signals.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a target text prompt, and a dictionary of ~40 semantically related distractor embeddings (organized into Visual Confusers, Co-occurring Context, and Semantic Hierarchy groups, generated via LLM prompts or drawn from ImageNet/WordNet). It produces a unit-normalized orthogonalized text embedding (the OMP residual) that is then substituted into any existing saliency method in place of the original query embedding. For diffusion-based attribution (DAAM), the intervention is applied per-layer and per-head in key space with a tunable suppression parameter β, rather than on the global text embedding. The entire pipeline is inference-time only, requiring no retraining, and runs in under one second.

## Method

OSP is grounded in Theorem 1 (Linear Semantic Leakage), which proves that for any saliency method linear in the text embedding, the attribution map for a distractor class B on an image of class A decomposes as V_B(x) = cos(θ_AB)·V_A(x) + residual, making hallucination directly proportional to the embedding angle. To eliminate the ghost term, OSP applies Orthogonal Matching Pursuit to the target embedding against a dictionary of distractor embeddings: OMP iteratively selects the most correlated distractor atom and recomputes the residual via orthogonal projection, guaranteeing the final residual is strictly orthogonal to all selected distractors. This residual replaces the original query embedding in the saliency computation, so any contribution aligned with a distractor direction vanishes by construction. The approach is architecture-agnostic and plug-and-play, validated across CLIP, SigLIP, and Stable Diffusion 2 with five attribution methods (GradCAM, LeGrad, CheferCAM, AttentionCAM, DAAM), consistently improving AUROC by 1–15 points while preserving or enhancing positive-prompt localization (mIoU, mAP).

## Results

On ImageNet-Segmentation, OSP consistently improves AUROC (hallucination detection) across all 9 method–model pairs, with the largest gain being +15.05 for AttentionCAM on CLIP (52.68 → 67.73) and +7.17 for CheferCAM on SigLIP (55.47 → 62.64). Positive-prompt mIoU also improves in most cases, e.g., GradCAM on CLIP rises from 44.68 to 51.43 (+6.75) and AttentionCAM on CLIP from 40.14 to 47.96 (+7.82), while negative-prompt mIoU decreases or stays flat, confirming suppression of hallucinated attributions. DAAM on Stable Diffusion 2 shows the smallest absolute gains (AUROC +2.41, mIoU Pos +0.63), likely because cross-attention maps are already more spatially selective. All improvements are achieved with a runtime overhead of less than one second.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSP is a plug-and-play module applied on top of each baseline, so every comparison is 'Base method' vs. 'Base method + OSP.' AttentionCAM on CLIP benefits the most, with AUROC jumping from 52.68 to 67.73 (+15.05) and positive mIoU from 40.14 to 47.96 (+7.82), indicating that attention-based attribution is particularly susceptible to semantic leakage. GradCAM shows strong gains on both CLIP (AUROC +6.43, mIoU Pos +6.75) and SigLIP (AUROC +5.94, mIoU Pos +4.57). CheferCAM on SigLIP improves AUROC by +7.17 (55.47 → 62.64), while on CLIP the gain is more modest (+2.51). LeGrad, already the strongest baseline in AUROC (79.62 on CLIP), sees only marginal improvements (+1.02 CLIP, +0.31 SigLIP), suggesting its gradient-tracing mechanism partially mitigates leakage on its own. DAAM on Stable Diffusion 2 shows the smallest deltas (AUROC +2.41, mIoU Pos +0.63), consistent with cross-attention maps being more spatially selective than gradient-based methods. In all 9 method–model pairs, OSP either improves or maintains positive-prompt mIoU while reducing or holding negative-prompt scores, confirming that the geometric intervention suppresses hallucinated attributions without degrading true localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP), a classical sparse-recovery algorithm, as a geometric purification tool for text embeddings in vision-language models, departing from prior work that either decomposes image representations (TextSpan) or applies sparse autoencoders to activations. It unifies five heterogeneous saliency methods (GradCAM, LeGrad, CheferCAM, AttentionCAM, DAAM) under a single linear-attribution framework (Linear Semantic Attribution), then shows that hallucination is a direct consequence of non-orthogonality in the text embedding space rather than an architecture-specific defect. The approach is conceptually distinct from CHILI, which disentangles image embeddings for concept bottleneck models, by instead operating on the text query side before attribution is computed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formal proof that hallucination scales with cos(θ_AB) between text embeddings provides a diagnostic that is computable without any image data or model inference, enabling pre-deployment safety checks for VLM explanations. The plug-and-play nature (sub-second runtime, no retraining) makes it immediately deployable across CLIP, SigLIP, and diffusion pipelines, and the 200-participant user study validates that the geometric correction translates into measurable human trust gains. Open questions include whether the linearity assumption in Theorem 1 holds for autoregressive MLLMs, how OSP interacts with multi-object scenes where distractors are themselves present, and whether the LLM-generated dictionary strategy generalizes beyond ImageNet-scale taxonomies to open-vocabulary or domain-specific (e.g., medical) concept spaces.
