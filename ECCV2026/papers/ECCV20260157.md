## Problem

Existing reasoning video object segmentation methods are developed and evaluated in an offline regime where the full video is accessible, enabling retrospective disambiguation with future frames and assuming a fixed referent throughout the sequence. This deviates from real-world streaming deployments (robotics, embodied agents, on-device analytics) that require strictly causal, frame-by-frame decisions, and fails to handle referent shifts where the queried entity changes as events unfold. No prior benchmark or method explicitly evaluates or supports this online, causality-constrained, referent-alterable setting.

## Input / Output

The model receives a streaming video where frames arrive sequentially (only the prefix V_{≤t} is observable at step t) and a fixed natural-language query specifying the target. At each time step it consumes the current frame, a K=4-frame sliding context window, and aggregated memory tokens from a bounded reservoir, then outputs a binary mask M_t ∈ {0,1}^{H×W} for the referred object in the current frame. The critical constraint is that the prediction at time t must not depend on any future frame, and the referent identity may shift across the sequence (averaging 3.66 shifts per query in ORVOSB).

## Method

The proposed framework builds on Chat-UniVi-7B (adapted via rank-8 LoRA) and introduces two key mechanisms for online reasoning. First, a continually-updating segmentation prompt (CUSP) generates a frame-specific target-aware token at each step via the MLLM, then applies affinity-guided adaptive fusion to inject weighted context-frame segmentation features into the target token, allowing the prompt to evolve as evidence accumulates. Second, a structured temporal token reservoir maintains bounded long-term memory using dense-to-sparse retention (a nonlinear time-warping function that samples recent steps densely and distant steps sparsely) followed by query-based aggregation through a 2-layer, 8-head Transformer encoder with 32 learnable query tokens. The fused prompt is projected into a SAM-2 mask decoder for pixel-level prediction, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end under a streaming unrolling protocol with a joint cross-entropy + BCE + Dice loss.

## Results

On the newly introduced ORVOSB benchmark, the proposed method achieves 61.3% J&F overall, outperforming the strongest prior baseline READ (51.5%) by +9.8% and VideoLISA (50.3%) by +11.0%. Gains are consistent across all five reasoning categories, with the largest improvements on Spatial (+12.5% over READ) and External Knowledge (+13.1% over READ). On the standard offline ReVOS benchmark, the method achieves 54.3% J&F, which is competitive with GLUS (54.8%) but trails the best offline method UniPixel (63.7%) by 9.4%, reflecting the inherent cost of strict causal inference without future-frame access.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all seven baselines on ORVOSB, the benchmark specifically designed for online causal segmentation with referent shifts. The largest margins are over segment-then-track methods: VISA (33.3%) and VRS-HQ (43.4%) lag by 28.0% and 17.9% respectively, because their fixed-target propagation cannot adapt when the queried identity changes mid-video. Among frame-level methods, READ (51.5%) and VideoLISA (50.3%) are the closest competitors, but both lack an explicit causal prompt-update mechanism, yielding gaps of 9.8% and 11.0%. On the offline ReVOS benchmark, the method (54.3%) is competitive with GLUS (54.8%) but trails UniPixel (63.7%) and VRS-HQ (59.1%), which exploit full-video global context unavailable under strict causality. The paper argues this gap is expected and that the ORVOSB results are the more meaningful comparison for the proposed online setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reasoning video object segmentation (RVOS) line of work—where MLLMs like Chat-UniVi map language queries to segmentation tokens—by imposing a strict online causality constraint and introducing referent shifts as first-class evaluation targets. Prior RVOS methods (VISA, VRS-HQ, GLUS, VideoLISA, UniPixel) all assume full-video access at inference, enabling retrospective disambiguation; the online streaming video understanding literature (VideoLLM-Online, StreamingVLM, LiveVLM) addresses sequential processing but only at the language level, not pixel-level segmentation. The proposed architecture departs from the segment-then-track paradigm by maintaining a continually-updating segmentation prompt fused via affinity-guided adaptive fusion, paired with a dense-to-sparse temporal token reservoir for bounded-memory long-horizon reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ORVOSB fills a concrete gap: no prior benchmark evaluates frame-by-frame causal segmentation with explicit referent-shift annotations, which is essential for real-world deployments in robotics, embodied agents, and on-device video analytics where future frames are unavailable. The finding that strong offline RVOS methods (e.g., VISA at 33.3% J&F) degrade substantially under strict causality underscores that offline-trained models do not transfer to streaming settings without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much longer videos or higher-resolution inputs, whether the 210-video benchmark is sufficient for robust conclusions, and how to handle more complex multi-entity referent shifts beyond the current five query categories.
