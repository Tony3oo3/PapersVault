## Problem

Unsupervised debiasing methods rely on heuristics or annotated validation sets to determine when to stop training an auxiliary biased model for bias mining, but stopping too early yields uninformative underfitting while stopping too late leads to complete memorization of bias-conflicting samples. This 'when to stop' dilemma remains an open issue, with existing solutions requiring computationally expensive generative models or external foundation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261793/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for most benchmarks, 32×32 for Corrupted CIFAR-10) and produces class labels through a debiased classifier. Unusually, the debiasing signal is extracted from frozen intermediate feature representations at each network block via lightweight linear probes, and the output includes per-sample reweighting coefficients (combining surprisal and a loss-ratio term) that modulate the cross-entropy loss during retraining of a randomly re-initialized backbone.

## Method

AracNet pre-trains a backbone (ResNet or ViT) on the biased dataset, freezes it, and attaches a linear classifier (Shallow Monitor) to every network block. After training the monitors on frozen intermediate features, a bimodality score computed on each monitor's confidence distribution automatically selects the layer where bias-aligned and bias-conflicting samples are most separable, eliminating the need for early stopping or validation sets. The backbone is then randomly re-initialized and retrained with a per-sample loss weight that multiplies a surprisal term (high for low-confidence, i.e., bias-conflicting samples) by a loss-ratio term inspired by Learning from Failure, both derived from the selected monitor. The monitor and debiased model are updated in an alternating scheme each epoch, and the entire pipeline introduces only negligible computational overhead from training a few additional linear layers.

## Results

On Waterbirds, AracNet achieves a Worst Group Accuracy (WGA) of 91.27±0.56, the highest among all unsupervised debiasing methods, surpassing DDB (90.81) and SaMyNa (90.62). On BFFHQ (ρ=0.995), AracNet reaches 75.93±0.76 conflicting-sample accuracy, edging out DeNetDM (75.70) and DDB (74.67). On BAR, AracNet attains 76.22±1.47 (ρ=0.990) and 85.00±0.73 (ρ=0.950), matching or exceeding LoYS. On UrbanCars with multiple biases, AracNet achieves an ID.ACC of 85.95±1.01 with all subgroup gaps below 2.4%, simultaneously mitigating both background and co-occurring-object biases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AracNet achieves the best WGA on Waterbirds (91.27), outperforming the closest competitors DDB (90.81, generative-based) and SaMyNa (90.62, vision-language foundation model) by ~0.5%, while requiring only ~50 minutes of compute versus DDB's hours-long diffusion model training. On BFFHQ, AracNet (75.93) narrowly beats DeNetDM (75.70) and DDB (74.67), and substantially outperforms earlier methods like LoYS (68.50) and LfF (65.19). On BAR, AracNet (76.22 at ρ=0.990) edges out LoYS (75.92) and DeNetDM (73.84), and ties with LoYS and DisEnt+BE at ρ=0.950 (85.00). On Corrupted CIFAR-10, AracNet is competitive but not uniformly best: it matches or beats DeNetDM at ρ=0.950 (56.80 vs 56.30) and ρ=0.980 (47.48 vs 47.35), but trails DeNetDM at higher correlations (33.23 vs 38.93 at ρ=0.995). On UrbanCars, AracNet achieves the best CoObj-Gap (−1.51, meaning the conflicting subgroup outperforms ID.ACC) and a BG-Gap of 2.35, comparable to DDB (1.85) while maintaining a higher ID.ACC (85.95 vs 86.39) and far lower computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

AracNet departs from the dominant 'when to stop' paradigm in unsupervised debiasing (LfF, JTT, MoDAD), which relies on heuristic early stopping or annotated validation sets to mine bias before conflicting memorization occurs. Instead, it shifts to a 'where to look' paradigm by attaching shallow linear monitors to frozen intermediate layers of a fully-trained (even overfit) backbone, drawing conceptual inspiration from linear probing and architecture-based approaches like SIFER that probe intermediate representations. The self-mitigation loss, combining a surprisal-based weight with a monitor-loss-ratio term, repurposes the LfF reweighting idea but grounds it in spatial (layer-wise) rather than temporal (epoch-wise) bias signals. No single prior method is directly extended; the contribution is a new synthesis of linear probing, bimodality-based layer selection, and per-sample reweighting into a unified pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practically attractive solution to the conflicting-memorization bottleneck: negligible computational overhead (a few linear classifiers), no need for bias-annotated validation sets, and robustness to arbitrarily long pre-training (verified up to 200 epochs). The byproduct interpretability—showing that background bias localizes in early layers while semantic bias (e.g., gender) localizes in later layers—provides a diagnostic tool for understanding how spurious correlations propagate through a network. Limitations include reliance on a pre-trained biased backbone, a heuristic bimodality score for layer selection, and evaluation limited to relatively small-scale benchmarks; the paper itself flags multi-modal biases and dynamic monitor architectures as future directions. Whether the approach generalizes to larger-scale, real-world deployment settings with heterogeneous and unknown bias structures remains an open question.
