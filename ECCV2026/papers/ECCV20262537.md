## Problem

Existing egocentric assistants are either reactive (responding only to explicit queries) or semi-proactive (monitoring for predefined events under a fixed task instruction), but neither paradigm reasons over the user's accumulated history, habits, and current activity to autonomously decide whether, when, and how to intervene. No prior benchmark or method addresses this context-dependent decision problem of proactive assistance over continuous egocentric video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262537/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a continuous egocentric video stream segmented into non-overlapping short clips (30s for long recordings, 10s for shorter benchmarks), processing each clip incrementally without ever requiring access to the full video. At each timestep it outputs a binary intervention decision d_t ∈ {0,1} and, when d_t=1, a service response r_t that must be temporally precise (within a dataset-specific tolerance window δ), category-correct (one of 10 service subcategories), and contextually grounded. In reactive mode, a user query triggers the same pipeline with d_t=1 by default, producing a QA answer.

## Method

EgoMemo is a training-free, memory-augmented agent that incrementally constructs three complementary memory representations from each incoming clip: (1) a three-level temporal hierarchy of clip-level captions, activity-level summaries, and session-level summaries; (2) an evolving semantic knowledge graph built via LLM-based entity/relation extraction with name-based entity resolution; and (3) a visual embedding archive of keyframes encoded by ImageBind. At each timestep, the agent invokes three parallel retrieval pathways—dense temporal search, graph-based 1-hop semantic expansion, and visual similarity matching—followed by a VLM-based caption reconstruction step that synthesizes retrieved snippets into a query-focused narrative. An LLM reasoner then integrates the current caption with the reconstructed multi-source context to produce the intervention decision and response. The key design insight is that no single retrieval pathway subsumes another: temporal retrieval captures fine-grained evidence, the graph links semantically related events across distant timesteps, and visual retrieval recovers details absent from text, making heterogeneous parallel retrieval essential for cross-session proactive reasoning.

## Results

On the proposed EgoServe benchmark, EgoMemo achieves an overall F1 of 8.0, nearly doubling GPT-5-mini (4.7) and Qwen3-VL-Plus (3.5), with the largest gains on long-term services (Routine Optimization: 11.8 vs. 0.0; Memory Link: 4.9 vs. 0.0). On OVO-Bench, EgoMemo attains the best real-time perception score of 75.15, surpassing GPT-4o (64.46) by over 10 points. On offline egocentric QA, EgoMemo achieves state-of-the-art on EgoSchema (74.8, +7.2 over EgoThinker) and QAEgo4D (68.0 vs. 66.2), while remaining competitive on EgoTaskQA (60.9 vs. 64.4). On ESTP-Bench, EgoMemo leads on explicit proactive tasks (27.6 vs. EyeWO's 23.6) but trails on implicit tasks (34.7 vs. 52.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On EgoServe, EgoMemo (8.0 overall F1) substantially outperforms both proprietary baselines: GPT-5-mini (4.7) and Qwen3-VL-Plus (3.5), with the gap concentrated on long-term and episodic services where both baselines score 0.0 on Memory Link and Routine Optimization. On OVO-Bench real-time perception, EgoMemo (75.15) decisively beats GPT-4o (64.46), LLaVA-OneVision (64.02), and all open-source streaming models (Dispider 54.55, EyeWO 32.76), but trails GPT-4o on backward tracing (49.60 vs. 60.75). On ESTP-Bench, EgoMemo wins explicit proactive tasks (27.6 vs. EyeWO 23.6, Qwen2-VL 15.4) but loses implicit proactive tasks to EyeWO (34.7 vs. 52.5), the only trained model in that comparison. On offline egocentric QA, EgoMemo achieves the best EgoSchema score (74.8) over EgoThinker (67.6), Qwen2-VL (63.3), and LLaVA-OneVision (60.1), and leads QAEgo4D (68.0 vs. EgoThinker 66.2), but trails EgoThinker on EgoTaskQA (60.9 vs. 64.4) where fine-grained procedural reasoning favors direct visual perception over memory retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vinci2 directly extends the on-device egocentric assistant Vinci [20], advancing it from reactive response to autonomous proactive intervention. It departs from event-triggered streaming systems (StreamBridge, EWO, ProAssist) that conflate predefined event detection with the decision to intervene, and from memory-augmented video agents (MovieChat, VideoRAG, WorldMM) that assume offline access to complete recordings. The key conceptual departure is treating intervention as an explicit reasoning outcome conditioned on multi-scale retrieved historical context, rather than as a byproduct of event detection or a response to a user-provided task instruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoServe is the first benchmark to evaluate whether an agent should speak up at all in continuous egocentric video, filling a gap between offline video QA, streaming comprehension, and task-completion proactive dialogue benchmarks. The finding that even GPT-5-mini scores 0.0 on all long-horizon proactive categories without accumulated context underscores that perception alone is fundamentally insufficient for proactive assistance. Key open questions include learning intervention timing from human preferences, incorporating audio, and extending to multi-turn and multi-user settings; the moderate absolute scores (8.0 F1) indicate the task remains largely unsolved.
