## Problem

Facial expression recognition in the wild is degraded by subjective annotation ambiguity and noisy crowd-sourced labels. Existing neighborhood-based label distribution learning methods rely on static or heuristic weighting to aggregate neighbor information, which cannot dynamically model complex inter-sample dependencies and may amplify noise rather than suppress it under ambiguous supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262676/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB facial image as input and produces a 7-class emotion prediction. During training, each center sample is paired with K=8 nearest neighbors retrieved in a valence-arousal auxiliary space; both center and neighbors pass through a shared ResNet-18 backbone to obtain predictive label distributions. The final supervision is a fused soft distribution (a convex combination of the original one-hot label and the neighbor-aggregated distribution), and the framework introduces no additional computational cost at inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage iterative neighbor weight refinement pipeline. First, a Cross-Attention Routing (CAR) module projects center and neighbor features into N_l=16 local semantic routes, computes route-level cross-attention scores between center and each neighbor, and averages them to produce initial neighbor weights. Second, a Mamba-based selective state space model iteratively (T=3 steps) updates these weights by balancing a learned state-retention term (preserving historical weighting) with an input-absorption term (incorporating the current aggregated neighbor distribution), progressively suppressing unreliable neighbors. A learnable scalar λ then fuses the refined neighbor distribution with the original logical label to form the final supervision, trained with a cross-entropy loss plus a class-prototype discriminative loss.

## Results

Under symmetric label noise, Mamba-NWR achieves top-1 accuracy of 89.97%, 88.38%, and 87.62% on RAF-DB at 10%, 20%, and 30% noise rates, respectively. On AffectNet, it reaches 66.18%, 65.66%, and 63.91%, and on FERPlus, 88.11%, 87.62%, and 87.15%. The method consistently outperforms all nine compared baselines across every dataset and noise level, with the largest margins observed on AffectNet (up to +1.81% over LDLVA at 10% noise).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mamba-NWR outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and noise level tested. On RAF-DB, it surpasses the strongest competitor MCR by 0.69%, 0.77%, and 1.54% at 10%, 20%, and 30% noise, respectively, and also exceeds SOFT (89.05/87.86/86.08) and LA-Net (88.75/87.12/85.33). On AffectNet, the gains over LDLVA (the best AffectNet baseline) are 1.81%, 1.77%, and 1.34%, while it leads MCR by 3.85%, 4.31%, and 4.41% at the same noise rates. On FERPlus, it edges out LA-Net (88.02/86.85/86.01) by 0.09%, 0.77%, and 1.14%. The advantage grows with noise level on most datasets, indicating that the iterative Mamba refinement becomes increasingly beneficial as label corruption intensifies. Under asymmetric noise on RAF-DB, the method also beats SOFT by 1.29%, 1.09%, and 1.64% and DMUE by 15.12%, 13.77%, and 17.47% at 10%, 20%, and 30% noise, respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the label distribution learning (LDL) line of work for FER under noisy labels (LA-Net, LDLVA) by replacing their static or heuristic neighbor weighting with a two-stage dynamic process: a cross-attention routing (CAR) module for multi-route semantic alignment initialization, followed by a Mamba-based state space model for iterative weight refinement. The use of the Valence-Arousal auxiliary space for KNN neighborhood construction follows prior LDL-FER work, while the Mamba component repurposes a sequence-modeling architecture (originally designed for NLP) as a recurrent state updater over neighbor weight vectors—a departure from the single-pass aggregation paradigm common in neighborhood-based FER methods. The learnable λ fusion and discriminative loss are standard components borrowed from the broader noisy-label literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled way to progressively correct biased neighbor estimates under noisy supervision, and the Mamba-based iterative refinement is a genuinely novel architectural choice for FER that could inspire similar applications in other recognition tasks with ambiguous labels. However, the practical impact is tempered by modest gains (≤1.8% over the strongest baseline), evaluation limited to a single backbone (ResNet-18), and a small number of refinement iterations (T=3), leaving open whether the Mamba component's advantage persists at scale or with deeper architectures. A key open question is whether the iterative refinement could be replaced by a simpler learned weighting (e.g., a small MLP) with comparable performance, which would reduce training complexity without sacrificing robustness.
