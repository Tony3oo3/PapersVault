## Problem

Current table recognition methods, whether specialized small models or multimodal LLMs, produce high TEDS scores yet fail to accurately predict merged-cell attributes (colspan/rowspan), which are critical for structurally complex tables. This gap persists because the standard TEDS metric is largely insensitive to merged-cell errors (a table with 0% merged-cell accuracy can still score 72.7% TEDS), existing open-source datasets contain very few tables with more than 10 merged cells, and end-to-end autoregressive SFT overlooks fine-grained structural details in long HTML sequences.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single table image (scanned or photographed, averaging 1623×1351 resolution in the training set) and produces a two-stage output: first a merged-cell recognition (MCR) sequence listing each merged cell's rowspan, colspan, and textual content, then the final HTML table sequence generated conditioned on the MCR output as contextual guidance. The MCR output is not merely an auxiliary prediction but is explicitly fed back into the MLLM prompt to guide subsequent HTML generation, creating an intrinsic dependency between the two stages.

## Method

Table-MCR2TR introduces MCATR, a training framework built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B that jointly optimizes three complementary tasks—Table Recognition (TR), Merged-Cell Recognition (MCR), and MCR2TR Alignment—via a progressive recognition paradigm where the model first predicts merged-cell cues and then generates HTML conditioned on them. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) supervised fine-tuning on all three tasks using the newly constructed MCT-350K dataset (350K tables with high merged-cell counts, built via MLLM-synthesized HTML rendering, web-crawled tables with cross-model consistency and structure checking, and UVDoc-based photorealistic warping), followed by (2) GRPO-based merged-cell-aware collaborative reinforcement learning with a tripartite sequence-level reward combining TEDS, MCR F1, and an alignment score that enforces consistency between predicted merged cells in the MCR stage and those embedded in the final HTML. The key distinction from prior RL-for-TR work is that MCR is not treated as a mere auxiliary signal but is explicitly learned and then transferred to TR through the alignment task, and the MCR F1 reward provides a sparser, more discriminative optimization signal than TEDS alone for guiding the policy toward correct merged-cell identification.

## Results

Table-MCR2TR achieves an average MCR F1 of 83.9% and average TEDS of 92.3% across seven table recognition benchmarks, setting a new state of the art. On the most challenging CC-OCR benchmark (150 scanned + 150 photographed tables), it reaches 76.6/90.5 (F1/TEDS) on Scan and 71.2/83.0 on Photo, substantially outperforming all prior methods. On SynthTabNet, where nearly all tables contain merged cells, it attains 87.6% MCR F1 and 98.7% TEDS, compared to only 6.4% F1 for the strongest general MLLM (InternVL3.5-38B). Downstream table QA (WTQ, TabFact, PubHealthTab) also improves, with the two-stage pipeline (Table-MCR2TR → Qwen3-32B) reaching 77.6/89.8/75.8 accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Table-MCR2TR achieves the best average MCR F1 (83.9%) and TEDS (92.3%) across all benchmarks, outperforming the strongest proprietary model Gemini-2.5-Pro by 17.6 points in MCR F1 and 8.1 points in TEDS on average. Against specialized MLLMs, it leads on every benchmark except OmniDocBench, where PaddleOCR-VL scores higher (92.0 vs. 83.9 TEDS), and PubTabNet MCR F1, where RapidTable edges ahead by 0.9 points (81.5 vs. 80.6) due to low image quality affecting merged-cell detection. On the most challenging CC-OCR benchmark, Table-MCR2TR substantially surpasses all baselines (e.g., 90.5 vs. 89.4 TEDS on Scan vs. Gemini-2.5-Pro; 83.0 vs. 82.2 on Photo), with gaps widening to over 20 points against general MLLMs like Qwen3-VL-32B. Among general MLLMs, even the strongest (InternVL3.5-38B, 84.2% avg TEDS) trails by 8.1 points, and its MCR F1 on SynthTabNet is only 6.4% versus Table-MCR2TR's 87.6%, highlighting the critical role of explicit merged-cell modeling. For downstream table QA, the two-stage pipeline (Table-MCR2TR + Qwen3-32B) outperforms direct MLLM QA (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5, QVQ-72B) and, under a controlled same-QA-model setting, beats GPT-4o, MinerU2-VLM, and DeepSeek-OCR as the parsing front-end.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-based table recognition line (Dolphin, MonkeyOCR, MinerU) that relies on SFT to directly generate HTML, and the emerging RL-for-MLLMs line (Table2LaTeX-RL, MSRL) that uses TEDS as a sequence-level reward. It departs from both by introducing merged-cell recognition as an explicit intermediate task whose output conditions the final HTML generation, and by replacing the single TEDS reward with a tripartite reward (TEDS + MCR F1 + alignment score) optimized via GRPO. The data pipeline builds on UVDoc's warping geometry for photorealistic synthesis and leverages MLLM-based cross-model consistency checking for annotation quality, but the core conceptual contribution is the progressive MCR→TR recognition paradigm rather than any single prior method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that TEDS remains at 72.7% even when all merged-cell spans are wrong exposes a systematic blind spot in the field's primary evaluation metric, suggesting that reported TEDS improvements may overstate structural fidelity. The MCR2TR alignment strategy offers a practical recipe for injecting fine-grained structural priors into autoregressive generation, potentially transferable to other structured-output tasks (LaTeX, code with nested syntax). A notable limitation is the near-complete loss of general MLLM capability after training, and the approach's reliance on a 3B base model limits its applicability where general-purpose reasoning is also needed. Open questions include whether the MCR-first paradigm generalizes to other fine-grained structural attributes beyond merged cells, and whether the alignment reward can be made more efficient at inference time.
