## Problem

Reward-guided diffusion sampling faces a fundamental trade-off: mean-shifted gradient methods (e.g., DPS) steer generation toward high-reward regions but push intermediate states outside the noise-compatible regime the pretrained model was trained on, degrading quality and enabling reward hacking; search-based methods preserve sample quality but forgo gradient signal entirely. No prior method simultaneously achieves gradient guidance and noise compatibility in a single draw per step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260722/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt, a differentiable reward function (e.g., Aesthetic Score, PickScore, VideoReward), and a pretrained flow-matching or diffusion model (FLUX for images, Wan2.1 for video), operating entirely at inference time with a fixed 25-step sampling budget. At each denoising step, the reward is evaluated on the Tweedie posterior mean estimate of the clean sample, and its gradient is computed and whitened before injection. The output is a single reward-aligned image or video per trajectory, with the option to run multiple independent trajectories (Best-of-N) and select the highest-reward result, scaling total NFE linearly with the number of trajectories.

## Method

NTRK keeps the pretrained reverse-kernel mean μ_θ(x_t, t) entirely fixed and routes all reward information through the noise term, replacing the standard ε_t ~ N(0,I) with a tilted draw ε̃_t = √ρ_t · w_t + √(1−ρ_t) · ε_t, where w_t is a whitened reward direction and ρ_t controls guidance strength. The central mechanism is a whitening operator W that converts the structured reward gradient into a noise-compatible perturbation via a sequence of two-level order-statistic (2OS) projections: the vector is reshaped into tiles, doubly sorted (within-tile then across-tile), each rank-pair entry is clipped to its 99.99% confidence interval under the standard normal, and the sort permutations are inverted (sort-clip-unsort). This projection is applied to value-level, tile-mean, and centered-energy statistics across multiple orthogonally transformed domains, suppressing spatially correlated and localized structure while preserving the gradient's directional signal. Because the whitened direction is itself typical Gaussian noise, the Gaussian mixing identity guarantees the combined draw remains N(0,I)-distributed, so the standardized perturbation η_t = ε̃_t stays within the noise-compatible regime the pretrained model expects—achieving gradient-informed steering with only one sample per step and no kernel-mean displacement.

## Results

On aesthetic image generation (target: Aesthetic Score), NTRK achieves 7.4510 with only 25 NFE, surpassing the best 500-NFE baseline (SVDD at 7.1363) by a 20× compute reduction. With 500 NFE (NTRK†), the score rises to 7.9656, the highest across all methods. On text-aligned image generation (target: PickScore), NTRK at 25 NFE reaches 0.2224, outperforming all 500-NFE baselines (best: SVDD at 0.2204), and NTRK† at 500 NFE achieves 0.2327. On preference-aligned video generation, NTRK scores 3.465 on VideoReward versus −0.130 for DPS and −0.211 for FreeDoM at the same 25 NFE budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NTRK at 25 NFE outperforms every 500-NFE baseline on both target rewards: Aesthetic Score 7.4510 vs. SVDD 7.1363, RBF 6.9900, ψ-Sampler 7.0116, DAS 6.9384, FreeDoM† 6.8406, DPS† 6.7647, and BoN 6.7310; PickScore 0.2224 vs. SVDD 0.2204, RBF 0.2202, DPS† 0.2147, BoN 0.2146, FreeDoM† 0.2133, DAS 0.2139, and ψ-Sampler 0.2120. On held-out image quality, NTRK (25 NFE) leads on ImageReward (0.5257) and VQA Score (0.8210) while remaining competitive on HPSv2 (0.2601 vs. SVDD's 0.2699). At 500 NFE, NTRK† extends its lead on Aesthetic Score (7.9656) and PickScore (0.2327) and achieves the best HPSv2 (0.2817). On video generation, NTRK (3.465 VideoReward) dramatically outperforms DPS (−0.130) and FreeDoM (−0.211) at the same 25 NFE, also leading on Dynamic Degree, Aesthetic Quality, and Subject Consistency. When applied on top of the fine-tuned MixGRPO model, NTRK† (0.2545 PickScore) surpasses DPS† (0.2235) and the fine-tuned base (0.2166) on all metrics, confirming composability with parameter-level alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

NTRK departs from the dominant mean-shifted gradient guidance paradigm (DPS, FreeDoM, DAS, ψ-Sampler) by keeping the pretrained reverse-kernel mean entirely fixed and routing reward information through the stochastic noise term instead. The central enabling mechanism is a whitening operator built on two-level order-statistic (2OS) projections, which upgrades the hard-constraint projection approach of WGNC [23] to confidence-interval-based projections that suppress structured components while leaving genuine Gaussian noise nearly unchanged. This positions NTRK as the only method simultaneously achieving gradient guidance, noise compatibility with the pretrained model, and a single draw per step, resolving a trade-off the paper argues no prior method has closed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: NTRK matches or exceeds 500-NFE baselines using only 25 NFEs (a 20× compute reduction) across aesthetic image generation, text-aligned generation, and video generation, while also composing cleanly with fine-tuning (e.g., MixGRPO) for additional gains. The method is a drop-in, training-free replacement applicable to any pretrained diffusion or flow model, making it broadly adoptable. Open questions include how the whitening operator's quality scales to higher-dimensional latent spaces, whether the 99.99% confidence-interval threshold is optimal across timesteps, and whether the approach extends to non-Gaussian noise schedules or continuous-time formulations.
