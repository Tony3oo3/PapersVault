## Problem

Speculative decoding accelerates LLM inference effectively, but state-of-the-art methods like EAGLE-3 yield limited speedups for visual autoregressive models because visual tokens exhibit flat, non-discriminative probability distributions that cause drafter-target misalignment and verification collapse under stochastic sampling. The paper identifies that this misalignment is spatially heterogeneous—peaking at complex textures—and that standard pointwise token matching is overly restrictive for the locally concentrated yet globally diffuse nature of visual token distributions.

## Input / Output

The framework wraps around existing autoregressive image generation targets (LlamaGen-XL-T2I/C2I, Lumina-mGPT-7B), consuming text prompts or ImageNet class labels and producing decoded RGB images via the target's VQ codebook. The drafter (EAGLE-3 architecture) proposes candidate token trees that are verified in a single target forward pass; the output image is structurally identical to vanilla decoding but produced with fewer sequential steps. Evaluation uses 5,000 MS-COCO 2017 validation prompts for T2I and 20,000 ImageNet-1K samples for C2I, with FID, CLIP score, IS, Precision, and Recall as quality metrics.

## Method

LISA combines two complementary mechanisms. During training, Locality-Informed Distillation reweights supervision by computing a locality-mismatch score that identifies positions where the target distribution is geometrically concentrated (via probability-weighted centroids in the VQ codebook embedding space) but the drafter's top-1 falls outside that neighborhood; a progressive soft gating factor modulates the weight based on KL alignment progress, and the final loss is a weighted combination of truncated cross-entropy and KL distillation over the joint top-K support. During inference, Geometry-Aware Soft Verification (GASV) replaces rigid exact-match acceptance with two soft confidence signals—geometric drift in a whitened output-embedding space compared against cluster-specific budgets, and regional posterior mass from log-sum-exp over vocabulary clusters—combined via an OR-style gate that reweights the target distribution before rejection sampling. Uncertainty-modulated thresholds (driven by normalized entropy and logit margin) dynamically relax or tighten acceptance, enabling higher token acceptance under stochastic sampling without degrading output quality.

## Results

On the T2I task, LISA achieves a 2.11× speedup (accept length 3.05) on LlamaGen-XL-T2I and a 4.12× speedup (accept length 4.79) on Lumina-mGPT-7B, while maintaining FID of 46.7 and 31.0 respectively—comparable to or better than all baselines. On the C2I task (LlamaGen-XL-C2I, ImageNet-1K), LISA reaches up to 1.53× speedup with accept length 2.69 at a more aggressive setting (κ=1.1, λ=0.4), with FID of 12.60 versus 9.78 for vanilla. The headline 4.12× result on Lumina-mGPT-7B is the largest speedup reported among all compared methods, with FID (31.0) and CLIP (31.5) remaining within ~1 point of the vanilla model (30.1 / 33.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LISA outperforms all eight baselines on speedup across both T2I and C2I tasks. On Lumina-mGPT-7B, LISA's 4.12× speedup exceeds GSD (3.77×) by 0.35×, LANTERN (2.56×) by 1.56×, and EAGLE-3 (1.86×) by 2.26×, while keeping FID at 31.0 versus 33.1 (GSD) and 33.9 (LANTERN). On LlamaGen-XL-T2I, LISA (2.11×) beats LANTERN (1.70×) and SJD (1.61×), though EAGLE-2 actually degrades below vanilla (0.97×) on this model. On the C2I task, LISA (1.53× at aggressive setting) surpasses LANTERN (1.04×) and both EAGLE variants (0.7–0.8×), at the cost of FID rising from 9.78 (vanilla) to 12.60. AR-Sample achieves a comparable FID (30.9) to LISA on Lumina but suffers a large CLIP drop (26.1 vs. 31.5), indicating degraded semantic alignment. The Medusa-style drafter generalization (Table 4) shows LISA's training-side distillation alone lifts accept length from 1.19 to 1.53, and adding GASV further raises it to 2.25 with 1.82× speedup, confirming both components contribute independently.

## Relation to Prior Work

LISA builds directly on the EAGLE-3 drafter architecture and the classic speculative decoding paradigm (drafter proposes, target verifies in parallel), but departs from prior visual speculative decoding methods (LANTERN, SJD, ZipAR, GSD) by targeting the root cause of low acceptance—drafter-target distributional misalignment—rather than modifying only the verification rule or drafting topology. The geometric calibration procedure (whitening, clustering, budget estimation) draws on LANTERN's visual-token geometry analysis, but LISA repurposes this structure for a training-side distillation objective, a use not explored in prior work. The paper positions itself as the first to jointly address alignment at both training and inference stages for visual AR models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As AR image generation models (LlamaGen, Lumina-mGPT, Janus-Pro) scale up, inference latency becomes a primary deployment bottleneck; a 4.12× speedup with preserved FID/CLIP quality is practically significant for real-time or high-throughput applications. The diagnostic showing that standard speculative decoding's acceptance collapses under stochastic sampling for visual tokens—while working fine for text—provides a clear explanation for why LLM acceleration techniques do not transfer, which is useful context for the broader community. Open questions include whether the geometric locality assumption generalizes to all VQ codebooks (e.g., those without semantic clustering), whether the per-model offline calibration can be amortized, and whether the framework extends to video or multimodal LLM settings as the authors suggest.
