## Problem

Multi-animal tracking is severely challenged by uniform appearance, high density, and irregular motion, which render appearance-driven query-based trackers unreliable for identity association. While heuristic methods exploit geometric cues effectively, they lack end-to-end optimizability; conversely, directly injecting continuous geometric signals into attention-based architectures amplifies small coordinate perturbations and can actually degrade association performance. The paper addresses how to robustly incorporate geometric dynamics into an end-to-end query-based tracker without the instability of continuous representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video sequences processed by a Deformable DETR detector, from which it extracts normalized bounding-box coordinates (center, width, height divided by frame dimensions) and inter-frame velocity. Velocity is represented in polar coordinates (magnitude ρ and direction θ) rather than Cartesian components to decouple motion speed from heading. At inference, where no direct temporal correspondence exists, velocity is estimated via a lightweight KNN-weighted regression over historical trajectory states using spatial proximity and directional alignment kernels. The output is per-frame tracked bounding boxes with consistent identity labels across the sequence.

## Method

HieDG builds on the MOTIP query-based tracking framework and introduces a hierarchical residual quantization module that discretizes position, scale, and velocity cues into stable structured tokens. Each geometric component is independently projected through an MLP into a 64-dim latent space, then quantized by a two-stage residual codebook (K=64 codewords per stage) in a coarse-to-fine manner, where the second stage captures fine-grained variations residual after the first. The resulting discrete embeddings are concatenated, linearly projected to 256 dimensions to match the visual feature space, and concatenated with appearance features and one-hot ID embeddings before cross-attention in a Transformer decoder for identity prediction. Training jointly optimizes detection loss, ID classification loss, and a vector-quantization loss (with straight-through estimator for gradient flow through the non-differentiable nearest-codeword operation), and a 0.3 dropout on velocity embeddings regularizes against motion uncertainty. The key insight is that mapping fluctuating continuous geometry to a finite set of learned codewords constrains perturbation-induced embedding variance, yielding more stable cross-frame attention and identity matching under noisy, appearance-ambiguous conditions.

## Results

On AnimalTrack, HieDG achieves HOTA 56.2, AssA 58.4, and IDF1 64.4, outperforming the strongest query-based baseline MOTIP (54.1/55.0/61.7) by +2.1 HOTA and +3.4 AssA. On BFT, HieDG reaches HOTA 71.3, AssA 72.2, and IDF1 82.5, improving over MOTIP by +2.1 HOTA and +4.9 AssA. The most dramatic gain appears on BuckTales (UAV-based antelope tracking), where HieDG achieves HOTA 52.4 and AssA 64.0, surpassing the previous best CO-MOT (47.2/51.3) by +5.2 HOTA and +12.7 AssA. On generic multi-person benchmarks, HieDG also leads with HOTA 70.3 on DanceTrack and 72.8 on SportsMOT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HieDG outperforms all 19 compared baselines across all five benchmarks. Against the strongest query-based baseline MOTIP [11], HieDG gains +2.1 HOTA and +3.4 AssA on AnimalTrack, +2.1 HOTA and +4.9 AssA on BFT, and +0.7 HOTA and +0.7 AssA on DanceTrack. The largest margin is on BuckTales, where HieDG surpasses CO-MOT [41] (the previous best) by +5.2 HOTA and +12.7 AssA, indicating that discretized geometry is especially beneficial under severe UAV-induced geometric perturbations. Notably, the continuous-geometry variant HieDG* underperforms the MOTIP baseline on AnimalTrack (54.6 vs. 54.1 HOTA) and BuckTales (41.3 vs. 45.6 HOTA), confirming that raw continuous geometric signals destabilize attention-based association. Among heuristic-based methods, OC-SORT [6] and ByteTrack [45] remain competitive on BFT (66.8 and 62.5 HOTA respectively) but are substantially outperformed by HieDG (71.3). On SportsMOT, HieDG edges out MOTIP by +0.2 HOTA and +0.7 AssA, showing consistent but smaller gains in less appearance-ambiguous settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

HieDG builds directly on MOTIP (Gao et al., CVPR 2025), which formulates multi-object tracking as an ID classification problem via cross-frame attention, and on residual vector quantization (Lee et al., CVPR 2022) for discretizing continuous latent representations. The key conceptual departure is from both heuristic trackers (SORT, ByteTrack, OC-SORT), which use handcrafted geometric rules but cannot be end-to-end optimized, and from standard query-based trackers (TrackFormer, MOTR, MOTIP), which are heavily appearance-driven and fragile under low visual discriminability. HieDG bridges these paradigms by discretizing geometric cues (position, scale, velocity) into structured tokens via a two-stage residual codebook and injecting them into the query-based attention mechanism, effectively making geometric reasoning differentiable and jointly optimable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in tracking for low-discriminability scenarios (uniform-appearance animals, crowded human scenes) where appearance embeddings alone are insufficient for stable identity association. The finding that continuous geometric embeddings can actively degrade attention-based association—while their discretized counterparts improve it—offers a useful design principle for any attention architecture that ingests noisy continuous signals. Limitations include reliance on a fixed Deformable DETR backbone, a heuristic KNN-based velocity estimator at inference, and additional computational overhead from the codebooks; open questions include whether the discretization strategy extends to 3D tracking, whether alternative quantization schemes (e.g., learned VQ-VAE) could further improve stability, and how the approach scales to very large object counts.
