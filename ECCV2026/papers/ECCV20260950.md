## Problem

Mixed-resolution denoising in diffusion transformers (DiTs) produces blur and unstable artifacts even when token positions are rescaled into a unified coordinate system, yet the root cause has remained unexplained. This paper shows that RoPE imposes a structured sinusoidal scale bias on attention scores as a function of relative token distance, so any global remapping that compresses or stretches distances across resolutions shifts token pairs to incompatible phases of this periodic function, systematically corrupting attention.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a text prompt and internally generates a saliency map (via off-the-shelf detectors like DeepGaze) from a coarse low-resolution denoising result to identify which spatial regions should be processed at high resolution. For video, the default schedule is 15 steps at 480p followed by 35 mixed-resolution steps with 15% HR tokens; for images, 5 steps at 512 followed by 10 mixed-resolution steps with 60% HR tokens. The output is a full-resolution image or video where salient regions retain fine-grained detail while background regions are rendered at lower cost.

## Method

Phase-Aligned Mixed-Resolution Attention (PMA) is a training-free mechanism that, for every query-key pair, rescales the key's position to the query's native resolution grid before computing the RoPE-rotated dot product, ensuring the relative offset Δ is always measured in the same positional units the model saw during pre-training. Concretely, an HR query stretches LR key positions by the scale ratio, while an LR query compresses HR key positions (with strided downsampling of HR keys) to the LR grid. A lightweight Boundary Expand-and-Replace (BER) module then dilates the LR/HR masks by a few tokens and bidirectionally exchanges re-noised upsampled and downsampled latent content within the boundary band to smooth local discontinuities. The full pipeline pairs PMA+BER with a coarse-to-fine denoising schedule (coarse LR → mixed-resolution → optional fine HR) and requires no architectural changes or retraining of the base DiT.

## Results

On Wan2.1-1.3B video generation, PMA achieves DOVER Overall 75.34 and VBench Total 77.04 at 43.2 s (4.0× speedup), approaching the HR baseline (DOVER 79.12, VBench 76.56 at 172.1 s) while being 4× faster. On FLUX.1-dev image generation, PMA reaches ImgReward 0.978, CLIP-IQA 0.623, and MUSIQ 71.81 at 2.4 s, closely matching the HR reference (ImgReward 1.062, CLIP-IQA 0.621, MUSIQ 70.47 at 3.4 s). When combined with MagCache or DMD step-distillation, the method extends to 7.8× and 30.7× speedups respectively while retaining near-HR quality (DOVER Overall 75.57 and 87.97).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against all RoPE interpolation baselines (PI-LR, PI-HR, NTK, PI+NTK, YaRN), PMA wins decisively on every metric: DOVER Overall improves from 35.04–66.38 (baselines) to 75.34, and VBench Total from 66.18–72.46 to 77.04, at identical 43.2 s runtime. The gap is largest against PI-HR (DOVER +40.3) and NTK (DOVER +30.8), confirming that simply rescaling positions into a unified grid fundamentally corrupts RoPE's sinusoidal phase structure. Against diffusion acceleration methods at comparable speed (TeaCache 4.0×, MagCache 3.8×, UniPC 3.8×, DPM++ 3.9×, ToMe 3.6×), PMA achieves the highest VBench Total (77.04 vs. 60.80–73.88) and DOVER Overall (75.34 vs. 48.47–74.33). For image generation at 4× speedup, PMA outperforms RALU (ImgReward 1.027 vs. 0.940), Bottleneck Sampling (1.027 vs. 0.903), and ToCa (1.027 vs. 0.956), and at 6× speedup it leads all baselines by a wide margin (ImgReward 0.929 vs. 0.345–0.900). PMA is also composable: stacking it with MagCache yields 7.8× speedup with negligible quality loss (DOVER 75.57 vs. 75.34), and combining with 4-step DMD reaches 30.7× speedup while retaining DOVER Overall 87.97 (vs. 88.95 for DMD alone).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RoPE as the standard positional encoding in diffusion transformers, but departs fundamentally from the line of RoPE extension work (PI, NTK-aware scaling, YaRN) that attempts to remap positions into a unified coordinate space. The closest prior work, RALU, uses mixed-resolution as an intermediate pipeline stage but relies on linear RoPE interpolation and compensates with extra noise and steps; this paper identifies that the failure is structural—RoPE's sinusoidal phase filter is calibrated to a single positional scale and cannot be consistently resampled at two scales simultaneously. The proposed PMA mechanism is orthogonal to and composable with existing acceleration techniques (caching, distillation, token merging), positioning it as a drop-in attention fix rather than a new pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides both a clear theoretical diagnosis (RoPE's periodic scale bias breaks under mixed resolution) and a practical, training-free fix that achieves 4×–30× speedups with near-HR quality across both image and video DiTs, making high-resolution generation substantially more accessible. The insight that no single position remapping can preserve RoPE's oscillatory structure across two scales simultaneously is a useful constraint for future positional encoding design. Open questions include whether the boundary refinement module remains necessary at higher resolution ratios, whether the approach generalizes to non-RoPE encodings (ALiBi, XPos), and whether the sinusoidal phase structure observed here holds identically across all DiT architectures or is model-specific.
