## Problem

Existing outdoor navigation benchmarks either rely on 3D simulators with insufficient photorealism and structural complexity, or on Google Street View imagery that lacks dynamic elements and continuous navigable spaces. No prior benchmark provides a photorealistic, district-scale urban environment with rich street-level dynamics (pedestrians, vehicles, signage) that supports smooth egocentric navigation and comprehensive multi-task evaluation of embodied agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262059/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step the agent receives a spherical 360° video frame (projected onto a sphere in Unity), optionally a 2D map image with a marker indicating current position and heading, and a task-specific prompt (natural-language instruction, reference landmark image, or map with start/goal points). The agent selects from 7 discrete actions (forward, tilt up/down, rotate left/right, reset viewpoint, output answer), augmented at branching points with directional choices (left branch, right branch). Final outputs are task-dependent: grid coordinates for Localization, Euclidean position for navigation tasks (evaluated at 10 m or 20 m thresholds), free-text for Relational Spatial Reasoning, or a numerical count for Object Count (scored via mean relative accuracy over confidence thresholds 0.5–0.95).

## Method

The benchmark is built on a Realistic Virtual World (RVW) of Tokyo's Akihabara district, reconstructed from 602 360° video segments covering 85 streets (~750 m × 650 m), organized into a connected pose graph of 193 nodes and 305 edges (mean branching degree 3.16) rendered in Unity. One hundred seventy-five human-crafted tasks span three categories—Environment Understanding (Localization, Landmark Search with Language/Image), Path Reasoning (Map Navigation, VLN), and Spatial Reasoning (Relational Spatial Reasoning, Object Count)—with 25 tasks per subcategory labeled Easy/Medium/Hard based on distance, ambiguity, and exploration depth. Agents are modeled as POMDPs with a memory buffer, and six LMMs (GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini-2.5 Flash, Qwen2.5-VL-32B, InternVL3.5-8B/38B) are evaluated under four scoring protocols (exact match, GPT-5 fuzzy match, coordinate match, MRA). The paper further ablates the effect of providing explicit self-location on a map and performs a five-category failure-cause analysis (Action, Grounding, Perception, Explore, Planning) to diagnose model-specific weaknesses.

## Results

On 360CityArena, the strongest LMM (Gemini-2.5 Flash) achieves only 17.1% overall accuracy, far below the human baseline of 77.3%. No LMM exceeds 12% on Localization, 28% on Landmark Search with Language, or 48% on Landmark Search with Image, while Map Navigation scores 0% for all models except Claude Sonnet 4.5 (4%). The largest human–model gaps appear on Map Navigation (92% vs. 0–4%), Relational Spatial Reasoning (92% vs. 0–32%), and Landmark Search with Image (92% vs. 0–48%), indicating that city-scale embodied navigation and spatial reasoning remain substantially unsolved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks five LMM-based agents (GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini-2.5 Flash, Qwen2.5-VL-32B, InternVL3.5-8B/38B) against a local-expert human baseline on 175 tasks. Gemini-2.5 Flash leads overall (17.1%) and is best on Localization (12.0%), Landmark Search with Language (28.0%), and Object Count (24.0%), but no single model dominates all tasks. GPT-5 achieves the highest Landmark Search with Image score (48.0%) and Relational Spatial Reasoning (32.0%), while InternVL3.5-8B/38B tie for best VLN (12.0%) and Claude Sonnet 4.5 is the only model to score non-zero on Map Navigation (4.0%). All LMMs trail the human baseline by 30–92 percentage points per task, with the most severe gaps on Map Navigation (≤4% vs. 92%) and Relational Spatial Reasoning (≤32% vs. 92%). Performance degrades monotonically with difficulty across all models, and removing explicit location information yields inconsistent effects (e.g., GPT-5 Rel Reason drops from 48% to 32% without location, while Landmark-Lang drops from 24% to 16%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Realistic Virtual World (RVW) framework of Takenawa et al. [41], which reconstructs city-scale environments from 360° video segments into a navigable pose graph. It departs from prior urban navigation benchmarks along two axes: (1) unlike 3D simulators (EmbodiedCity, MetaUrban, CARLA) that sacrifice photorealism, and GSV-based environments (StreetLearn) that lack dynamics and continuity, 360CityArena achieves both photorealistic street-level observations and continuous trajectory-based navigation; (2) unlike aerial VLN (CityNav) or map-only route planning (RUN, Xing et al.), it targets ground-level egocentric exploration with rich visual detail. The benchmark unifies seven established task types (localization, object/image-goal navigation, VLN, map navigation, relational spatial reasoning, object counting) under a single realistic urban setting, rather than introducing new task formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a stark capability gap—best LMM (Gemini-2.5 Flash) achieves only 17.1% overall versus 77.3% for local-expert humans—highlighting that current vision-language models remain far from practical urban deployment. A notable open question is why explicitly providing self-location information does not consistently improve (and sometimes degrades) performance, suggesting that map-to-visual alignment is an underexplored bottleneck. Key limitations include restriction to a single district (Akihabara), constrained navigation along pre-recorded trajectories rather than free 3D movement, and absence of physical interaction, all of which limit cross-regional generalization and real-world transfer. The benchmark also serves as a practical template for constructing training data in photorealistic virtual worlds for embodied AI.
