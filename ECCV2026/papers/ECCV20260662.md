## Problem

Existing Depth from Defocus (DfD) methods are trained and evaluated in-domain on small, low-quality datasets, resulting in poor zero-shot generalization to unseen scenes and domains. Additionally, no high-quality real-world DfD benchmark exists that provides diverse scenes, realistic defocus effects, and accurate dense depth ground-truth suitable for evaluating generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a focus stack of M pixel-aligned RGB images (typically 5, but as few as 2) captured at the same aperture with varying focus distances, along with the corresponding focus distances in meters as metadata. The output is a single dense metric depth map at the same spatial resolution as the input images. In the ZEDD benchmark, images are 4K (4104×2736) with focus distances ranging from 0.8 to 8.1 m across 9 settings, and the model is trained with a stack size of 5 but generalizes to other stack sizes.

## Method

FOSSA is a Vision Transformer architecture with two stages: (1) focus stack feature extraction, where weight-shared ViT blocks are interleaved with a novel stack attention layer that embeds each image's focus distance via a 2-layer MLP and performs self-attention along the stack dimension (over M images per patch location), enabling efficient cross-image defocus cue aggregation with quadratic cost only over M rather than spatial tokens; and (2) feature collapse by averaging over the stack dimension, followed by additional refinement ViT blocks and a DPT head for dense depth regression. The model is initialized from DepthAnythingv2 pretrained weights and trained entirely on synthetic focus stacks generated from large-scale RGBD datasets (Hypersim + TartanAir, ~373k samples) using a circle-of-confusion-based PSF with randomized kernel shapes, f-numbers, and focus distance distributions to maximize zero-shot generalization.

## Results

On the new ZEDD benchmark, FOSSA (ViT-B) achieves δ₁.₀₅=0.505, δ₁.₂₅=0.918, and AbsRel=0.089, reducing AbsRel by 55.7% over the best monocular baseline DepthPro (0.201) and by 75.9% over the best DfD baseline DFF-FV (0.369). On Infinigen Defocus, FOSSA (ViT-S) reaches AbsRel=0.085, a 51.7% improvement over DepthPro (0.176). After fine-tuning on DDFF, FOSSA (ViT-B) reduces MSE by 40.4% over the previous state-of-the-art DualFocus (2.8 vs. 4.7 ×10⁻⁴), and on DIODE it cuts AbsRel by 44.6% over DepthPro (0.160 vs. 0.289).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOSSA decisively outperforms all ten baselines across every benchmark. Against monocular depth methods, FOSSA (ViT-B) reduces AbsRel by 55.7% over DepthPro (0.089 vs. 0.201) and 69.5% over DAv2 (0.089 vs. 0.337) on ZEDD, while also surpassing UniDepthV2 (0.253) and MoGe-2 (0.278). Among DfD-specific baselines, the gap is even wider: DFF-FV (AbsRel 0.369) and DFF-DFV (0.573) are both far behind FOSSA, and HybridDepth fails catastrophically on ZEDD (AbsRel 0.754) due to domain gap. On DDFF after fine-tuning, FOSSA (ViT-B) achieves MSE 2.8×10⁻⁴, beating DualFocus (4.7×10⁻⁴) by 40.4%, DFF-DFV (5.7×10⁻⁴) by 50.9%, and DefocusNet (8.6×10⁻⁴) by 67.4%. On real-world RGBD datasets (iBims, DIODE, HAMMER), FOSSA leads all baselines; notably on DIODE it reduces AbsRel by 44.6% over DepthPro and by 87.2% over DERE D (0.160 vs. 0.886), while on HAMMER it matches the best DfD baseline DFF-DFV† (AbsRel 0.044) and slightly outperforms it (0.017 vs. 0.044 for ViT-B).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established DfD literature (DefocusNet, DFV, HybridDepth, DualFocus, DEReD), which relies on custom network designs trained and evaluated in-domain on small datasets (<1000 samples). Instead, FOSSA repurposes the Vision Transformer backbone from DepthAnything and introduces a stack attention layer with focus distance embedding as the sole task-specific component, enabling a scalable, zero-shot approach. The training pipeline also breaks from prior work by generating synthetic focus stacks from existing large-scale RGBD datasets (Hypersim, TartanAir) with randomized PSF shapes and focus distributions, rather than requiring purpose-collected focus stack data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot framing addresses a critical practical gap: prior DfD models cannot transfer to new scenes or domains without retraining, limiting real-world deployment. The ZEDD benchmark (100 scenes, 4K resolution, real multi-aperture defocus, LiDAR ground truth) fills a significant resource gap over DDFF and MobileDepth. Open questions include how well the approach handles dynamic scenes, very large outdoor scenes where defocus cues become negligible at long range, and whether the synthetic-to-real gap in PSF modeling limits further accuracy gains. The finding that DfD baselines fail catastrophically in zero-shot settings (some performing worse than monocular methods) underscores how much prior work overfit to narrow evaluation protocols.
