## Problem

Prior language-based indoor localization methods stop at coarse scene retrieval—identifying which room a description refers to—but cannot resolve a precise 2D position and heading within that scene. Visual localization avoids this ambiguity but requires camera access, raising privacy and bandwidth concerns. LangLoc closes this gap by estimating a full intra-scene pose from a free-form text description alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260695/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form natural-language description (parsed into a text scene graph via GPT-4o-mini) and a database of 3D indoor scenes, each providing a reconstructed mesh with semantic instance segmentation and a typed scene graph. It outputs a ranked scene list, a 2D floor position and unit heading vector estimated on a 0.25 m grid at 1.6 m eye height, and—when the posterior is multimodal—targeted yes/no questions about object visibility or spatial relations. The fine-localization score combines a ray-cast visibility count with an exponential proximity bonus, converted to a softmax distribution over grid cells.

## Method

LangLoc is a three-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (retrieval) uses a dual-branch GATv2 encoder: a geometric branch propagates messages over k=5 spatial neighbors conditioned on relative position and bounding radii, while a relational branch embeds relation strings with CLIP so semantically similar predicates are close in embedding space; a learned per-node gate fuses the two branches, and the whole encoder is trained with InfoNCE contrastive loss. Stage 2 (fine localization) greedily matches text entities to 3D objects via Word2Vec cosine similarity, then scores every cell of a dense floor grid by casting a single ray from the 3D eye position to each matched object centroid against the scene mesh, summing a visibility indicator and an exponential proximity term; heading is estimated as the normalized mean of visible-object directions within the best-scoring FoV frustum. Stage 3 (dialog) maps the grid posterior onto a compact set of reference frames via KNN, then iteratively selects the yes/no question maximizing expected information gain (or a balanced-split heuristic) and updates the frame posterior via Bayes' rule with a reliability parameter, returning the MAP pose once the posterior concentrates above a confidence threshold.

## Results

On scene retrieval (ScanScribe, 10-scene pool), LangLoc achieves 76.7% Top-1 recall, surpassing the strongest prior method Text2SGM (68.6%) by 8.1 percentage points, and reaches 98.9% at Top-5. For fine localization on the full 1,300-scene LangLoc dataset, LangLoc without dialog achieves a Top-10 median position error of 0.95 m and a median angular error of 39.8°. Adding the Bayesian dialog module yields dramatic gains: on ScanNet the median position error drops to 0.07 m and median angular error to 5.1°, with 3D frustum IoU rising from 0.236 to 0.593.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Text2SGM [14] (the strongest prior scene-retrieval method), LangLoc wins by 8.1 pp on Top-1 recall (76.7% vs. 68.6%) in the 10-scene pool and by 7 pp on Top-5 in the full 55-scene pool (83.3% vs. 76.3%), with the gap widening under LLM-generated queries (59.5% vs. 34.2% Top-1). Against Text2Pos [23], LangLoc dominates by a wide margin (76.7% vs. 11.6% Top-1), confirming that graph-structured matching with CLIP features is far more effective than joint text-point-cloud embeddings for indoor scene retrieval. For fine localization, LangLoc outperforms the zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-2B [41] baseline on angular error (46.1° vs. 85.5° mean on 3RScan) and 3D frustum IoU (0.172 vs. 0.062), though the VLM's raw position error is comparable because both methods struggle in small rooms. The midpoint baseline (floor centroid) achieves a deceptively low raw position error (~1.3 m) due to small room sizes but provides no heading or viewpoint-specific information, making it a vacuous comparison. With dialog, LangLoc achieves the lowest position error across all settings (0.80 m median on 3RScan, 0.07 m on ScanNet), outperforming even a human annotator on position while the human retains an edge on heading accuracy (15.3° vs. 40.3° median in the 10-scene pilot).

## Relation to Prior Work

LangLoc extends the language-based scene retrieval line initiated by Chen et al. [14] (Text2SGM) and the dual-branch GATv2 encoder architecture from SceneGraphLoc [26], both of which stop at identifying the correct room from a text query. The key departure is moving from coarse scene identification to fine-grained intra-scene pose estimation (2D position + heading), which the authors explicitly frame as an open problem. The fine-localization stage repurposes classical ray-cast visibility scoring on a dense floor grid rather than learning a pose regression, and the disambiguation stage borrows Bayesian posterior updates from the embodied dialog navigation literature [38,43].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end pipeline that recovers a usable 2D pose and heading from a free-form text description without any camera, offering a privacy-preserving and bandwidth-light alternative to visual localization in camera-prohibited settings (hospitals, labs, emergency dispatch). The 13k+ pose-indexed description dataset over 1,300+ scans fills a clear gap in the 3D vision-language benchmark landscape. However, the method still requires a pre-built 3D scene graph with known object labels and a detailed mesh, and performance degrades in large, cluttered scenes where many viewpoints share similar object configurations; extending to open-vocabulary or incrementally constructed graphs, and relaxing the full-mesh assumption toward coarser floor plans, are the most pressing open questions.
