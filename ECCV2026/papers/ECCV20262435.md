## Problem

Diffusion-based trajectory prediction models require 10+ iterative denoising steps at inference, creating latency that is prohibitive for real-time autonomous driving. Fast-sampling variants (DDIM, informed initial noise) either still need multiple steps or are constrained by the quality of a pretrained prior. Consistency Models enable true single-step generation but have not been successfully adapted to trajectory prediction due to the lack of standardized pretrained diffusion models for distillation and the difficulty of training from scratch.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 50 past frames (5 s) of (x,y) agent positions, vector map polylines with position/orientation/magnitude attributes, and a standard Gaussian noise vector. It also ingests marginal future predictions and their scores from a pretrained QCNet as prior anchor context. Output is K=6 joint multi-agent future trajectories spanning 60 future frames (6 s) in (x,y) space, generated in a single forward pass. Internally, trajectories are linearly compressed to 10-dimensional latent vectors for all denoising and loss computation.

## Method

ECTraj is a conditional Consistency Model trained from scratch (no distillation) using a student-teacher paradigm where the teacher is an EMA of the student. Two key enhancements distinguish it from standard CMs: (1) an enhanced consistency objective that fuses the teacher's predicted trajectory with ground-truth midpoint and endpoint waypoints via a binary mask before mapping back to latent space, providing the student with higher-quality supervision anchors; and (2) a best-of-K training loss that samples K=6 independent noise vectors, denoises all K modes for both student and teacher, selects the best mode by ADE for each, and computes the consistency distance only between those best modes—something infeasible for noise-predicting diffusion models. The denoiser uses composite attention blocks (cross-attention with history, map, and neighbor encodings; self-attention among agents; cross-attention with marginal priors) and follows the ECT hybrid diffusion-consistency scheduling, transitioning from reconstruction to pure consistency training over 60 epochs with an adaptive discretization curriculum (N: 10→40).

## Results

On Argoverse 2, ECTraj achieves a state-of-the-art FDE6 of 0.96 m, outperforming the closest competitor QCNeXt (1.02 m) and the diffusion baseline OptTrajDiff (1.31 m). It ties QCNeXt on ADE6 (0.50 m) and ADE1 (0.94 m), while trailing only slightly on FDE1 (2.37 vs. 2.29 m) and b-FDE6 (1.68 vs. 1.65). Critically, ECTraj achieves these results with a single denoising step (1 NFE) versus 10 steps for OptTrajDiff, yielding a ~10× inference speedup (2.97 ms vs. 29.71 ms for the denoising component).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECTraj outperforms the diffusion baseline OptTrajDiff [32] on every Argoverse 2 metric (e.g., FDE6 0.96 vs. 1.31, ADE6 0.50 vs. 0.60) while using only 1/10 the inference steps. Against the strongest non-diffusion baseline QCNeXt [39], ECTraj ties on ADE6 (0.50) and ADE1 (0.94), wins on FDE6 (0.96 vs. 1.02) and MR6 (0.13 vs. 0.13, tied), but trails slightly on FDE1 (2.37 vs. 2.29) and b-FDE6 (1.68 vs. 1.65). ECTraj clearly surpasses DONUT [12] (FDE6 0.96 vs. 1.13), DeMo [36] (0.96 vs. 1.24), and FutureNet-LOF [31] (0.96 vs. 1.25) on the primary FDE6 metric. The remaining baselines—FJMP [20], GNet [4], Forecast-MAE [1], and RealMotion [26]—are all substantially outperformed, with FDE6 gaps of 0.28–0.93 m. For inference speed, ECTraj's denoising component takes 2.97 ms per scenario versus 29.71 ms for OptTrajDiff and 24.84 ms for QCNet alone, adding only ~12% overhead on top of the shared QCNet encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

ECTraj transplants the Consistency Model (CM) framework from image generation into multi-agent trajectory prediction, building directly on the student-teacher consistency training paradigm (Song et al., 2023), the adaptive discretization and lognormal sampling refinements (Song et al., 2024), and the ECT hybrid diffusion-consistency scheduling (Geng et al., 2025). Architecturally, it adopts the QCNet encoder–denoiser–decoder pipeline from OptTrajDiff (Wang et al., 2024) but replaces the iterative diffusion denoiser with a single-step consistency function conditioned on standard Gaussian noise rather than an informed prior. The two key departures from prior CM work are the enhanced teacher output (fusing ground-truth midpoint and endpoint waypoints into the teacher prediction) and a best-of-K training loss that exploits CMs' direct denoising to sample multiple modes during training—something infeasible for noise-predicting diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× reduction in inference steps (1 NFE vs. 10) makes trajectory prediction viable for real-time autonomous driving deployment, while matching or exceeding the accuracy of the diffusion baseline on all Argoverse 2 metrics. The finding that partial ground-truth fusion into the teacher outperforms both pure consistency and full reconstruction objectives reveals a useful inductive-bias trade-off that may generalize to other CM applications. A key open question is the continued reliance on pre-trained QCNet marginal priors; the authors acknowledge this as a limitation and point toward end-to-end or self-supervised prior generation as future work. Whether the best-of-K training strategy transfers to other CM domains (e.g., video, 3D) remains unexplored.
