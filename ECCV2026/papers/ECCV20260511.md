## Problem

Existing diffusion MoE routers condition on noise-corrupted latents throughout the denoising process, which obscures structural and textural information and prevents the router from distinguishing salient tokens from background. This 'noisy routing' problem results in saliency-insensitive expert allocation, where computational resources are not preferentially directed toward regions with high detail, ultimately limiting generative fidelity.

## Input / Output

SharpMoE is a post-training framework applied to already-converged pretrained diffusion MoE backbones (TC-DiT, EC-DiT, DiffMoE at S/B/L scales) for class-conditional 256×256 ImageNet generation. At inference, the model takes a class label and random Gaussian noise and iteratively denoises over 250 Flow Matching Euler steps to produce a 256×256 RGB image. The key operational detail is that the saliency-harnessing router consumes the predicted clean latent x̂₀ from the preceding denoising timestep rather than the current noisy latent, requiring a recursive rollout at both training and inference time.

## Method

SharpMoE introduces a dual-router mechanism where a new saliency-harnessing router R_sal (a two-layer MLP, zero-initialized for smooth integration) processes the predicted clean latent x̂₀ from the preceding timestep, and its output is added to the pretrained router's scores to form the final gating signal. To make this recursive dependency available during training, the paper proposes a Recursive Full-Trajectory Training scheme that simulates T=10 consecutive denoising steps per iteration, propagating x̂₀ predictions forward through the rollout. A Trajectory Routing Loss is then imposed, computing the KL divergence between the normalized cumulative expert-assignment scores (aggregated across all T steps and all MoE layers) and a Laplacian-based saliency map of the target image, thereby explicitly aligning total computational budget with visual significance. The entire framework requires only 100K post-training steps with a learning rate of 1e-4 and a routing loss weight of 0.001, making it a lightweight plug-and-play enhancement to converged models.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 (50K samples, 250-step Flow Matching Euler), SharpMoE applied to DiffMoE-L achieves the best FID of 3.10 and IS of 228.88 at CFG=1.5, improving over the DiffMoE-L baseline (FID 3.86, IS 203.00). At the B scale, DiffMoE-B + SharpMoE reaches FID 6.66 / IS 155.64 (CFG=1.5), surpassing Dense-DiT-B (FID 10.07 / IS 122.15). All gains are obtained with only 100K post-training steps on top of 500K-pretrained checkpoints, with the saliency-harnessing router adding roughly 2–12M parameters depending on scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SharpMoE consistently outperforms all four baseline families (Dense-DiT, TC-DiT, EC-DiT, DiffMoE) across every model scale (S, B, L) and both CFG settings. Against the strongest MoE baseline DiffMoE-L, SharpMoE reduces FID from 3.86 to 3.10 (−0.76) and lifts IS from 203.00 to 228.88 (+25.88) at CFG=1.5. Compared to the dense Dense-DiT-L (FID 4.74, IS 183.54), the best SharpMoE configuration (DiffMoE-L + SharpMoE) achieves a 1.64-point FID reduction and a 45.34-point IS gain while activating only 470M of 1.99B total parameters. Gains are largest at the L scale (e.g., EC-DiT-L FID drops from 4.09 to 3.27) and remain positive even for TC-DiT, which uses uniform token-to-expert assignment, confirming that the saliency-harnessing router can improve routing in architectures that originally lack dynamic allocation. All improvements are achieved with only 100K post-training steps, requiring no retraining from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion MoE line of work initiated by TC-DiT, EC-DiT, and DiffMoE, which introduced dynamic expert allocation for visual generation. Its key departure is identifying that existing saliency-aware routers are effectively saliency-insensitive because they condition on noise-corrupted latents, and proposing to route on the predicted clean latent x̂₀ from the preceding timestep instead. The recursive full-trajectory training scheme and the trajectory routing loss (KL divergence between cumulative expert allocation and Laplacian saliency) are novel training mechanisms that have no direct counterpart in prior diffusion MoE work, which typically trains in a single-step denoising paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play post-training nature is practically significant: practitioners can enhance already-converged diffusion MoE checkpoints with only ~100K additional steps, avoiding costly retraining. However, the evaluation is limited to class-conditional ImageNet 256×256, leaving open whether the clean-latent routing advantage persists in text-to-image, video generation, or higher-resolution settings where saliency structure is more complex. An open question is whether the trajectory routing loss's reliance on a Laplacian-based saliency proxy generalizes to tasks without a clear high-frequency/low-frequency saliency distinction, and whether the recursive dependency introduces instability at very long generation trajectories.
