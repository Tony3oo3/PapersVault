## Problem

Large-scale motion foundation models (LFMs) such as HY-Motion learn strong generative priors from massive text-to-motion pretraining, yet these priors remain locked to a single task. Prior work adapts pretrained motion models to each downstream task (inpainting, editing, spatial control, multi-person interaction) with dedicated architectures and conditioning modules, preventing knowledge sharing and cross-task generalization. The paper asks how to unlock the full breadth of these priors for diverse unseen tasks within a single unified framework without modifying the pretrained backbone.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (natural-language description, editing instruction, parameterized trajectory specification, or spatial constraint sentence) encoded by a frozen Qwen3-8B LLM and CLIP, plus a source motion sequence of T frames (each 201-dim: root translation, 6D root orientation, 21×6D joint rotations, 22×3D joint positions at 30 fps) with a per-frame assignment of one of three meta-operations (preserve, generate, edit). The output is a full 3D human motion sequence in the same 201-dim representation. Notably, all geometric specifications—Bézier curves, obstacle positions and radii—are serialized as structured text prompts rather than fed through dedicated spatial conditioning modules, and the source motion is set to zero for frames with no reference (generation) or to the original motion for preservation and editing.

## Method

UMO's core insight is that per-frame intent in any in-context motion task reduces to exactly one of three mutually exclusive meta-operations: preserve (retain the frame), generate (synthesize from scratch), or edit (modify the source). Three learnable frame-level embeddings (each ℝ²⁰¹) are added element-wise to the source motion to form a task-aware input, which is encoded by an MLP (initialized as a copy of the pretrained input encoder) and injected into the HY-Motion DiT backbone via temporal fusion—simple element-wise addition to the noisy motion embedding before the transformer blocks. All task conditions are expressed uniformly as text and processed by the same pretrained LLM encoder, requiring no task-specific conditioning module. The entire system adds only 0.207M parameters and ~0.01 s latency over the base model, and is jointly fine-tuned across all tasks (temporal inpainting, instruction-based editing, geometric constraints, dual-identity reaction) for 100k steps on 4 B200 GPUs. Ablations against sequential concatenation, AdaLN, and ControlNet confirm that temporal fusion best preserves per-frame granularity while maintaining generation quality.

## Results

On text-to-motion (HumanML3D), UMO-Unified achieves FID 9.46 and R@1 0.774, surpassing dedicated T2M models like MotionStreamer (FID 11.79) and MoMask (FID 12.23). On temporal inpainting, UMO-Unified attains near-zero FID across all four subtasks (0.040–0.057) while maintaining [P]-MPJPE ≤ 1.61 cm, outperforming CondMDI and MotionLab by large margins. On instruction-based editing (MotionFix), UMO achieves R@3 = 100% under batch-level retrieval and R@1 = 61.70% under full-set retrieval, versus 90.21% and 27.27% for the best prior method (PartMotionEdit). For geometric-constrained generation, UMO-Unified reaches 18.78 cm trajectory error at 0.759 s latency (~90× faster than OmniControl's 68.10 s) with 95% obstacle-avoidance success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On temporal inpainting, UMO-Unified dominates CondMDI [7] and MotionLab [19] across all four subtasks in FID (e.g., 0.056 vs. 1.592 for prediction; 0.040 vs. 0.803 for keyframe infilling) and R-Precision (R@1 0.553 vs. 0.146/0.179 for prediction), while the training-free HY-Motion inversion baseline [56] fails substantially (FID 18–37). On instruction-based editing, UMO achieves R@3 = 100% (batch) and R@1 = 61.70% (full-set), surpassing PartMotionEdit [70] (90.21% / 27.27%), SimMotionEdit [32] (88.12% / 25.49%), and MotionLab [19] (87.89% / –). For geometric-constrained generation, UMO-Unified (18.78 cm, 0.759 s) matches OmniControl [60] in trajectory error (17.89 cm) while being ~90× faster, and outperforms TLControl w/o Opt. [54] (61.15 cm) in accuracy at similar latency; optimization-based methods (TLControl w/ Opt., MaskControl [45], DNO [26]) achieve lower trajectory error (2.9–3.1 cm) but at 3–175 s latency. On dual-identity reaction generation, UMO-Unified achieves the best FID (2.055) versus InterMask [22] (2.990) and InterGen [33] (52.89), though InterMask retains a slightly higher R@1 (0.462 vs. 0.431). On text-to-motion, UMO-Unified (FID 9.46) outperforms MotionStreamer [59] (11.79), MoMask [17] (12.23), T2M-GPT [72] (12.48), and MDM [52] (23.45), while the base HY-Motion [56] without fine-tuning scores poorly (FID 61.04) due to a distribution gap with HumanML3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

UMO builds directly on HY-Motion, a large-scale DiT-based flow-matching text-to-motion model, repurposing its pretrained generative priors for tasks entirely outside its original training scope. Conceptually, it draws on the unified-task paradigm established in image and video generation (e.g., ACE, InstructX) but is the first to apply such a formulation to 3D human motion, replacing task-specific architectures (CondMDI, MotionLab, MotionFix) with three learnable frame-level meta-operation embeddings and a lightweight temporal fusion module. The language-conditioning design—expressing even precise 3D geometric constraints as structured text prompts—departs from prior spatial-control methods (OmniControl, TLControl, MaskControl) that rely on dedicated conditioning branches or iterative optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides strong evidence that large-scale T2M pretraining encodes broadly transferable motion priors that can be unlocked for diverse downstream tasks with minimal architectural overhead (0.207M parameters), offering a practical path toward consolidating the fragmented motion-generation literature into a single model. The finding that geometric constraints encoded purely as language achieve trajectory errors comparable to dedicated spatial modules while being ~90× faster challenges the assumption that spatial reasoning requires specialized conditioning. Key limitations include whole-body-only granularity (no part-level control) and the absence of audio modalities, leaving open whether the three meta-operations form a truly minimal basis or whether finer decompositions would further improve performance.
