## Problem

Generative video composition—integrating a dynamic foreground subject into a novel background while preserving identity, motion, and achieving physically plausible placement—remains unsolved by existing methods. Image-based pipelines (object insertion + I2V) discard temporal dynamics, naive copy-paste violates affordance, and naively fine-tuned video generators suffer from shortcut learning and identity drift. A further barrier is the absence of large-scale multi-layer video datasets with foreground/background annotations, making supervised training infeasible.

## Input / Output

The method takes a foreground video (49×480×720) with a binary mask, a background video of the same dimensions, and a text prompt describing the target scene. During training, the foreground is randomly augmented (horizontal flip, resized crop, color jitter) before encoding, while at inference augmentation is disabled and the ground-truth latent is replaced by sampled noise. The output is a composed video at the same resolution where the foreground subject is harmonized with the background in lighting, shadows, and placement.

## Method

StM is a self-supervised framework that first decomposes unlabeled videos into foreground/background layers using off-the-shelf tools (Segment-Any-Motion for masks, MiniMax Remover for background inpainting, InternVL for captions), then trains a Composer to reconstruct the original video from the separated layers. The Composer is a CogVideoX-I2V latent diffusion transformer augmented with three components: (1) multi-layer conditional fusion, where the noisy ground-truth latent, augmented foreground latent, and background latent are channel-wise concatenated and projected via an MLP to provide dense spatio-temporal conditioning; (2) transformation-aware augmentation applied only to the foreground to prevent copy-paste shortcuts and force the model to learn affordance-aware placement; and (3) an identity-preservation loss that separately weights foreground and background reconstruction errors (normalized by region area) to balance subject fidelity against compositional harmony. The approach introduces no new parameters beyond the projection layer, keeping inference cost identical to the base model.

## Results

On the DAVIS-based evaluation set (93 triplets), StM achieves the best scores on four of five metrics: FG Identity Preservation (M1: 84.82), BG Identity Preservation (M2: 92.88), Semantic Action Alignment (M3: 1.22, lower is better), and BG Motion Alignment (M4: 16.36, lower is better). The largest margin is on BG Motion Alignment, where StM scores 16.36 versus 24.72 (LayerFlow-BG) and 279.23 (SkyReels). StM trails on Textual Alignment (M5: 19.81) compared to text-driven methods like SkyReels (24.40) and Qwen+I2V (24.20), which the authors attribute to StM's layer-conditioned design where text provides complementary rather than primary guidance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StM outperforms all five baseline groups on the four composition-fidelity metrics (M1–M4) while trailing on textual alignment (M5). Against PBE+I2V, StM gains +11.3 on FG ID, +12.7 on BG ID, and reduces BG Motion error from 98.08 to 16.36. Against Qwen+I2V and SkyReels, StM improves FG ID by +2.8 and +4.6 respectively, and reduces BG Motion error by 58.41 and 262.87 points, reflecting that image-based methods discard temporal dynamics. Against AnyV2V, StM leads on all four fidelity metrics (e.g., BG ID 92.88 vs. 56.36) but AnyV2V scores higher on M5 (24.13 vs. 19.81). Against LayerFlow, StM dominates: LayerFlow-FG excels on FG ID (83.02) but collapses on BG ID (60.21) and BG Motion (143.47), while LayerFlow-BG is the reverse (BG ID 91.73, FG ID 50.27); StM achieves strong scores on both simultaneously. The M5 gap is explained by design: text-driven methods (SkyReels, Qwen+I2V) use the prompt as the sole generative signal, naturally yielding higher text alignment at the cost of identity and motion fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

StM builds directly on CogVideoX-I2V (a latent diffusion transformer) as its generative backbone, adding a multi-layer conditional fusion module and a lightweight projection layer. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant image-composition-then-animate pipeline (PbE+I2V, Qwen+I2V, SkyReels) by conditioning on full foreground and background *videos* rather than static frames, thereby preserving temporal dynamics. It also distinguishes itself from LayerFlow, which generates all layers from text, by explicitly composing two pre-existing video layers while preserving their identity and motion. The 'Split-then-Merge' self-composition training paradigm—decomposing unlabeled videos with off-the-shelf tools and training to reconstruct them—is the paper's core methodological departure from prior work that relies on manual layer annotations or domain-specific heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of StM-50K, the first multi-layer video dataset, provides a practical resource that was previously absent and should enable future layer-based video generation research. The annotation-free training pipeline (Decomposer + self-composition) offers a scalable path to training composition models without expensive manual labeling, which is significant given the data hunger of diffusion models. Key open questions remain: the method still struggles with relative scale reasoning (e.g., placing a duck at the correct size next to a boat), and its performance is partially coupled to the quality of the off-the-shelf Decomposer. Whether the self-composition paradigm generalizes to more complex multi-object or multi-layer scenes beyond the two-layer setting tested here is an important direction for future work.
