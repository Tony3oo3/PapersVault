## Problem

VLA policies treat action generation as a terminal, open-loop step with no mechanism for dynamic supervision, causing compounding errors and low success rates in dynamic environments. Existing world models that could provide predictive feedback require continuous frame-by-frame rollouts across the entire task horizon, making them computationally prohibitive for real-time control. This paper asks whether a lightweight world model can be repurposed as an event-triggered corrector that intervenes only at critical decision points to rescue potential failures before they materialize.

## Input / Output

The system consumes an RGB image, proprioceptive state (end-effector pose, gripper state), and a language instruction at each timestep, but the correction loop activates only when the base VLA proposes a gripper-close transition (the keyframe). The discriminator receives the current observation plus a structured VQA prompt and outputs a binary feasibility label; the generator takes a rolled-back observation (default 15 steps prior) and a high-level goal text to synthesize a short video (5–20 frames). The final output is a corrected 7-dimensional action sequence (Δxyz, Δrpy, gripper) over a horizon of T=5 steps, formatted as a JSON array of 4 elements each containing 7 floating-point numbers.

## Method

World-in-Loop implements a Propose-Evaluate-Imagine-Correct loop around a frozen base VLA (e.g., CogACT). At the grasp keyframe, a fine-tuned Qwen-VL 2.5 discriminator (trained on 100k balanced frames from BridgeV2, Fractal, and real-world data) evaluates feasibility via a VQA formulation; if failure is predicted, the system rolls back k steps to a healthier state and a WAN image-to-video diffusion model generates a short physically plausible video of a successful grasp. Crucially, the same VLM weights that serve as the discriminator are repurposed as an Inverse Dynamics Model (action decoder) through multi-task QA training, decoding the generated video into a corrected action sequence without requiring a separate network. The base VLA is never fine-tuned, and the entire correction pipeline is event-triggered rather than continuous, keeping the average intervention cost to roughly 4 seconds (3B discriminator + 5B generator) while achieving 12–21% success-rate gains across simulation and real-world platforms.

## Results

On SIMPLER with WidowX, MemoryVLA+WiL achieves the highest average grasp/success of 78.2%/71.9%, improving over the MemoryVLA baseline (71.9%/64.6%) by +6.3/+7.3 points. CogACT+WiL reaches 61.5%/51.1% average, a +26.4% relative improvement over CogACT alone (48.5%/40.5%). On real-world dual-arm platforms, the framework lifts average grasp success from 70.9% to 85.4% (+14.5%) and task success from 63.6% to 76.1% (+12.5%), with the largest gains on precision-critical tasks like 'Grasp Watercup' (+16.7% success) and 'Grasp Block into Plate' (+17.3% success).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed World-in-Loop framework consistently outperforms all compared baselines. Against RT-1-X (Brohan et al., 2022) and RT-2-X (Zitkovich et al., 2023), the gap is dramatic: MemoryVLA+WiL achieves 78.2% grasp vs. RT-1-X's 11.5% and RT-2-X's near-zero success on WidowX. Compared to Octo-Small (58.4%/29.5%) and OpenVLA (14.6%/1.0%), the framework yields substantially higher grasp and task success. Against the strongest VLA baselines—SpatialVLA (51.2%/34.4%) and CogACT (48.5%/40.5%)—adding WiL improves average success by +8.3 and +10.6 points respectively. In the real-world comparison (Table 6), the full WM-based correction (RollBack+WM+IDM) outperforms a lightweight diffusion-policy replanner (π0.5, Intelligence et al., 2025) by +6.3% grasp and +5.3% success on the real robot. For video generation quality (Table 7), the 14B WAN generator used in Setting-2 achieves the best FVD (131) and LPIPS (0.026), surpassing Cosmos-predict (FVD 146, LPIPS 0.030), confirming superior visual fidelity for the imagined correction trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes world models from their traditional role as continuous, horizon-long dynamics predictors (PlaNet, Dreamer, WorldVLA) into an event-triggered, on-demand correction module that intervenes only at critical grasp keyframes. It departs from prior online-correction approaches (RoboReflect, Phoenix) that rely on post-failure reflection or human oversight by introducing a proactive, pre-failure discriminative-evaluation-plus-generative-imagination loop. The composite WM design—pairing a fine-tuned VLM discriminator (Qwen-VL) with a video diffusion generator (WAN) and sharing weights with an inverse-dynamics action decoder—is a novel architectural combination not seen in prior VLA or world-model literature. The failure-driven training strategy, using both successful and failed demonstrations to learn decision boundaries, contrasts with prior WMs trained exclusively on positive trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in robotic deployment: low base-policy success rates make real-world data collection inefficient, and the event-triggered correction loop simultaneously improves task success and harvests higher-quality execution data by rescuing potential failures before they materialize. Its plug-in design (no base-policy fine-tuning required) makes it broadly applicable across heterogeneous VLA architectures and platforms. Key limitations remain: the system cannot resolve transport-phase failures (object drops) due to the absence of tactile or force feedback, video generation suffers from hallucinations at long rollback depths (>20 steps), and the current focus on grasping leaves open the question of generalization to continuous manipulation skills like folding. Extending the correction paradigm to handle irreversible physical failures and non-grasp state transitions is the natural next step.
