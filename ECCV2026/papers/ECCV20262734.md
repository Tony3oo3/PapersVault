## Problem

Existing adversarial patch attacks overwhelmingly use fixed shapes (rectangles, circles), causing both attack generators and detectors to develop a shape bias that provides a false sense of security. This paper demonstrates that smooth, deformable patches significantly outperform fixed-shape patches in attack success while evading state-of-the-art detectors (NAPGuard, AdvPatchXAI) and even VLM-based recognition, exposing a critical gap in adversarial patch defense.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262734/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target class label and a set of N=25 radial anchor points (initially near-circular with r₀=40) as input, and produces a universal adversarial patch whose smooth deformable contour is area-normalized to 2500 pixels (~5% of a 224×224 image). The same optimized patch is applied to many different source images via the mask equation x^adv = (1−μ)⊙x + μ⊙δ, and must remain effective under random affine transformations (rotation ±π/8, translation ±68 px) to ensure physical-world robustness. Two shape families are generated per target class: one deformable (SmuDPatch) and one circular baseline, yielding 20 patches across 10 ImageNet classes.

## Method

SmuDPatch uses a two-stage sequential optimization: first, an evolutionary algorithm (population 30, 20 generations, one-point crossover + Gaussian mutation) searches over the 25-dimensional radial vector to find the best smooth contour, where each candidate shape is scored by the inverse of a short inner-loop texture loss; the contour is rendered via centripetal Catmull-Rom spline interpolation to guarantee smooth, loop-free boundaries. Second, with the shape fixed, gradient descent optimizes the patch texture over an ensemble of three ImageNet models (ViT, ResNet50, MobileNetV2) for 2000 epochs, accumulating gradients across models and sampling random affine transformations at each step to ensure physical-world transferability. The key design choices—sequential rather than joint shape-texture optimization, spline-based smoothness, and area normalization—distinguish SmuDPatch from prior deformable methods like DAPatch, which optimize per-image and produce irregular, hard-to-fabricate shapes.

## Results

On the ensemble models used for optimization, SmuDPatch reduces ResNet50 robust accuracy from 76.7% to 28.5% (ASR 62.1%), MobileNetV2 from 71.9% to 31.6% (ASR 44.2%), and ViT from 80.9% to 65.5% (ASR 20.4%). Transferability to unseen models is strong: GoogLeNet drops from 69.8% to 46.0% and ConvNeXt-L from 83.6% to 76.0%. In the physical world, ResNet50 accuracy collapses from 66.6% to 2.5% and MobileNetV2 from 53.3% to 4.2%. For object detection, average mAP drops from 41.86 (clean) to 36.63 under SmuDPatch, with YOLO-v11 suffering the largest drop (45.66 → 35.09).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SmuDPatch decisively outperforms DAPatch [7] in the universal attack setting: on ResNet50, SmuDPatch achieves 28.5% robust accuracy and 62.1% ASR versus DAPatch's 66.9% and 3.5%, because DAPatch optimizes shape and texture per-image rather than learning a single universal patch. Against Pintor et al.'s fixed 50×50 circle patches [36], SmuDPatch also wins across all model groups (e.g., ResNet50: 28.5% vs 49.7% robust; GoogLeNet: 46.0% vs 55.3%). On the defense side, SmuDPatch is substantially harder to detect than fixed-shape patches: NAPGuard [55] mAP drops from 64.07% (square) to 45.97% (SmuDPatch), AdvPatchXAI [24] detection accuracy for patch-8 is 14% lower, LLaVA-NeXT [27] detects only 765/2000 SmuDPatch images versus 1704/2000 square patches, and SAM-3 [6] mIoU collapses from 39.54% to 9.41% with successful localizations falling from 3212/8000 to 778/8000. These results collectively show that existing detectors and VLMs are biased toward fixed geometric shapes and generalize poorly to smooth deformable boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal adversarial patch attack framework of Brown et al. [5] and the ImageNet-Patch evaluation protocol of Pintor et al. [36] by replacing fixed rectangular/circular shapes with smooth deformable contours. The shape optimization draws on the evolutionary approach of Wei et al. [51] but departs from it by using a multi-generation Catmull-Rom spline parameterization over 25 radial anchor points rather than Differential Evolution on a single image pair, and by optimizing shape and texture sequentially (evolutionary search then gradient descent) to produce a single universal patch. The key conceptual departure from DAPatch [7] is that the resulting shapes are smooth, star-convex, and practically fabricable, rather than the irregular per-image polygons that DAPatch produces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a significant blind spot in adversarial patch defense: SOTA detectors (NAPGuard, AdvPatchXAI) and even VLMs (LLaVA-NeXT) and segmentation models (SAM-3) that perform well on fixed-shape patches degrade substantially on smooth deformable ones, suggesting that current defense benchmarks provide a false sense of security. The 140,000-image datasets (ImageNet and COCO) offer a concrete resource for training and evaluating shape-agnostic detectors. Open questions include whether the modest ASR on adversarially robust models (0.2–0.6%) can be improved, whether the deformable-shape advantage persists under more realistic physical conditions (lighting, occlusion, distance), and how to build defenses that generalize across the full shape space rather than overfitting to a few canonical geometries.
