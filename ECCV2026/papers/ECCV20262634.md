## Problem

Fine-grained vision-language alignment requires high-quality preference data, but its creation is bottlenecked by the cost and subjective noise of human annotation and the non-scalability of VLM-as-a-judge evaluators. Existing automated metrics like CLIP Score capture only coarse global matching and fail to discriminate subtle attribute-level differences, leaving no solid, deterministic supervisory signal for fine-grained preference data generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262634/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The scoring model takes a reference RGB image and a text caption as input and outputs a scalar consistency reward computed as the difference between a cross-modal score (image-conditioned caption representation) and a text-only quality score, explicitly penalizing text-quality shortcuts. The iterative pipeline consumes a pool of candidate captions from four heterogeneous VLMs (Qwen3-VL-8B, InternVL3.5-8B, Gemma-3n-E4B, LLaVA-1.5-7B) and produces scored preference trajectories of 5–8 captions per image, from which adjacent best-second pairs with a DreamSim score gap exceeding a tunable threshold (0.03 or 0.05) are extracted as DPO training triplets.

## Method

The core mechanism is 'reconstruction as evaluation': a caption's fidelity to a reference image is measured by feeding it into a deterministic few-step T2I generator (Z-Image-Turbo) and scoring the reconstructed image against the reference with DreamSim perceptual distance, converting abstract semantic matching into an observable visual comparison. An iterative closed loop then refines captions: a visual critic (GPT-5.1) compares the reconstructed image to the reference, identifies specific semantic deviations, and produces a corrected caption that is re-reconstructed and re-scored until the perceptual gap falls below a convergence threshold or a maximum of K=4 rounds is reached. The resulting scored trajectories yield preference pairs with continuous quality gradients rather than binary heuristic negatives. A dedicated scoring model (frozen InternVL3.5-1B encoder + 4 cross-attention blocks + score decomposition subtracting a text-only branch) is trained on these pairs with a Bradley-Terry ranking loss plus contrastive and cross-image auxiliary losses, enabling inference-time consistency scoring without image generation. Finally, DPO fine-tunes VLMs on the extracted best-second preference pairs, achieving competitive benchmark accuracy with roughly 20% of the training data volume used by the closest competitor.

## Results

On zero-shot public preference benchmarks, the T2I-Consistency-Score model achieves 74.12% on RLHF-V and 75.1% on VLFeedback, substantially outperforming all baselines (best competitor: Cyclereward-Combo at 65.25% and 59.38% respectively). On POVID, the model scores 67.43%, trailing Cyclereward-T2I (86.49%) and HPSv2 (82.69%), which the authors attribute to a distributional mismatch with real-photo hallucination preferences. For DPO alignment, the 3K-sample reconstruction-driven dataset (Thr > 0.05) improves Qwen3-VL-2B Hallusion from 46.27 to 56.99 and MM-Bench from 0.66 to 0.70, while matching or exceeding 3.5K human-annotated RLHF-V data on InternVL3.5-1B (RealWorldQA 0.52 vs 0.51, MMStar 0.34 vs 0.33).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Cyclereward family [2], the proposed scorer wins decisively on RLHF-V (74.12 vs 65.25 for Combo) and VLFeedback (75.10 vs 59.38), but loses on POVID (67.43 vs 86.49 for T2I variant) and RLAIF-V (53.47 vs 55.46), which the authors attribute to POVID's real-photo hallucination domain being closer to Cyclereward's training distribution. HPSv2 [53] is outperformed on RLHF-V (74.12 vs 63.52) and VLFeedback (75.10 vs 53.83) but dominates on POVID (82.69 vs 67.43). PickScore [24] and LongCLIP-L [58] are consistently outperformed on RLHF-V and VLFeedback by 10–25 points, though both lead on POVID. For DPO training data, the 3K/5K reconstruction-driven datasets match or exceed 3.5K human-annotated RLHF-V [55] on InternVL3.5-1B (RealWorldQA 0.52 vs 0.51, MMStar 0.34 vs 0.33) and InternVL3.5-2B (POPE 88.66 vs 88.56, MME-P 1532.34 vs 1518.54), while using roughly 20% of the triplet volume consumed by Cyclereward-Combo's training set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the preference-alignment pipeline for VLMs by replacing the standard data-construction bottleneck (heuristic negatives, VLM-as-a-judge, or coarse CLIP-style scoring) with a reconstruction-driven feedback loop. Conceptually it is closest to Cyclereward (Bahng et al., 2025), which also uses cycle consistency as a reward signal, but departs by (i) employing a deterministic few-step diffusion generator (Z-Image-Turbo) to suppress stochastic noise, (ii) introducing an iterative critic-guided refinement loop that produces continuous quality gradients rather than single-shot pairs, and (iii) training a dedicated cross-attention scoring model that internalizes the reconstruction signal for inference-time use. The alignment step itself is standard DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024), and the perceptual metric is DreamSim (Fu et al., 2023), so the novelty is concentrated in the data-generation and scoring-model architecture rather than in the optimization objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a ~5× reduction in preference-data volume (20% of Cyclereward's triplets) while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy on fine-grained discrimination tasks, which directly lowers the annotation cost barrier for VLM alignment. The reconstruction-as-evaluation framing offers a general recipe—swap the deterministic generator and perceptual metric—to extend the approach to other modalities or domains. Open questions remain: the scoring model's zero-shot transfer degrades on real-photo benchmarks (POVID, RLAIF-V), the 1B backbone shows a capacity-limited trade-off between hallucination calibration and fine-grained comprehension, and the reliance on a proprietary visual critic (GPT-5.1) for the refinement loop limits full reproducibility. Whether stronger-determinism generators (e.g., control-conditioned diffusion) can close the OOD gap is a natural next step.
