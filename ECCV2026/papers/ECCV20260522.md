## Problem

Upgrading embedding models in retrieval systems typically forces expensive database re-indexing because new query embeddings are incompatible with existing database embeddings. Existing Backward Compatible Training (BCT) methods enforce compatibility during training but require backbone updates (LoRA-tuning or full retraining), which is impractical for large MLLMs due to prohibitive compute cost, risk of performance regression, and inaccessibility of proprietary model weights. No prior adapter-only BCT approach existed for multi-modal retrieval settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted, L2-normalized embeddings from a frozen new model (e.g., GME-7B or UniME-7B) across image, text, image+text composition, visual document, and video modalities, and produces a single projected embedding in the old model's space (e.g., CLIP ViT-L/14) via a 2-layer MLP adapter (LayerNorm-Linear-GELU-Dropout-Linear). No forward or backward passes through either backbone are needed during training or inference; the adapter adds only 0.25 ms per batch of 32 (0.01% of the original backbone forward pass). An auxiliary reverse adapter g (old→new) is trained jointly but discarded at inference time.

## Method

The key insight is that naively fitting new embeddings to the weaker, noisier old embedding space via L2 loss destroys the improved representational structure of the new model, undermining backward compatibility. MKP-Adapter addresses this with a multi-level preservation loss at three geometric granularities: (1) point-wise cycle-consistency (project new→old via h, reconstruct old→new via g, minimize reconstruction error), (2) distance-wise preservation of relative cosine distances over both global random pairs and local m-nearest-neighbor pairs, and (3) angle-wise preservation of angular relations among embedding triplets, inspired by relational knowledge distillation but applied self-supervisedly. A focal re-weighting scheme (γ=1.0) scales each loss term by (Δ_i/Δ̄)^γ to emphasize harder samples across modalities and semantic contexts. The total objective is L_base + λ·L_multi-preservation^focal (λ=1.0), trained with AdamW on 558K paired samples in 1–2 hours on 8 A100 GPUs, with the backbone remaining entirely frozen.

## Results

On standard I2T/T2I retrieval (MS-COCO, Flickr30K, Urban1K), MKP-Adapter achieves average Recall@1 of 74.13/61.85 (CLIP→GME), 77.76/63.07 (CLIP→UniME), and 82.03/75.27 (E5-V→UniME), consistently exceeding the old model's performance by +5.43% (I2T) and +7.63% (T2I) on average across scenarios. On the more challenging MMEB benchmark (36 sub-datasets), MKP-Adapter achieves 49.52, 49.56, and 51.30 Precision@1 across the three upgrade scenarios, a +10.53% average gain over the old model. On visual document retrieval (ViDoRe-V1, nDCG@5), MKP-Adapter reaches 45.35 vs. 28.30 for the old model (+17.05%), and on video retrieval (MSR-VTT/MSVD/VATEX, Recall@1) it achieves 33.60/43.88/22.18 vs. 28.60/36.27/20.43 for the old model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MKP-Adapter outperforms all three adapter-only baselines across every upgrade scenario and benchmark. Against the Base adapter (L2 projection, [37]), MKP-Adapter gains +5.50/+11.63 (I2T/T2I avg) in the CLIP→GME case and +14.41/+6.68 in the CLIP→UniME case, where the base adapter severely degrades I2T (63.35 vs. 69.80 old model). Against the EC-style adapter (distance-preserving, [47]), MKP-Adapter leads by +4.69/+11.11 (CLIP→GME) and +12.15/+6.07 (CLIP→UniME) on average I2T/T2I. Against the CL-based adapter (InfoNCE contrastive, [38]), MKP-Adapter wins on I2T in all scenarios (e.g., 77.76 vs. 67.23 in CLIP→UniME) and on T2I in the CLIP→GME and CLIP→UniME cases (61.85 vs. 56.38; 63.07 vs. 59.29), though the CL-based adapter is competitive on T2I in the E5-V→UniME scenario (72.43 vs. 75.27). Notably, the L2-based baselines (Base and EC-style) frequently underperform the old model itself on T2I, while the CL-based adapter underperforms on I2T in the CLIP→UniME setting; MKP-Adapter is the only method that consistently exceeds the old model on both I2T and T2I across all three upgrade scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first adapter-only Backward Compatible Training (BCT) method for MLLMs, departing from prior BCT approaches (XBT, Shen et al., Meng et al., Zhou et al.) that all require backbone updates via LoRA or full retraining. Conceptually, it repurposes ideas from Forward Compatible Training (Ramanujan et al., CVPR 2022) by reversing the projection direction (new→old instead of old→new), and adapts knowledge distillation mechanisms—cycle consistency, relational knowledge distillation (RKD), and focal loss—into a self-supervised setting where the new embeddings serve as their own preservation targets rather than relying on an external teacher. The multi-level preservation loss (point-wise, distance-wise, angle-wise) is a novel combination of these borrowed components, but each individual piece has clear prior art.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for production retrieval systems: upgrading an embedding model no longer requires expensive database re-indexing or access to proprietary model weights, and the 2-layer MLP adapter adds only 0.01% inference latency while training in 1–2 hours on 8 A100s. A key open question is whether the approach generalizes to even larger-scale databases (beyond the 5M tested) or to modalities like audio, and whether repeated sequential upgrades (old→mid→new) compound errors. The finding that 558K training pairs suffice (with marginal gains at 4M) suggests the method is data-efficient, but long-term stability across multiple upgrade generations remains untested.
