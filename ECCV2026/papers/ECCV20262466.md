## Problem

Visual State Space Duality (VSSD) backbones fuse multi-directional scans into a single global, position-agnostic aggregation that acts as an implicit low-pass operator, systematically attenuating the high-frequency responses that encode boundaries and small-scale objects in remote sensing imagery. No prior work calibrates the SSM aggregation itself to recover this lost spectral content while preserving the linear-complexity advantage that makes state-space models attractive at ultra-high resolutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262466/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes high-resolution RGB remote sensing images (512×512 random crops during training) and outputs a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask over 5 classes (Potsdam, Vaihingen) or 7 classes (LoveDA). The decoder produces logits via max-response matching between L2-normalized pixel descriptors and K=3 learned sub-prototypes per class, so each pixel is assigned to whichever sub-prototype of a given class it is most similar to, rather than to a single class centroid.

## Method

CRISP couples two components. The Duality Calibration Operator (DCO) is inserted after VSSD's fusion step and performs three near-identity calibrations: (i) state-level residual injection that reuses the backbone's own aggregation weights to recover the contrast removed by per-token value rescaling, (ii) token-level high-pass modulation that re-amplifies de-meaned boundary responses, and (iii) channel-level DC/HC rebalancing that redistributes energy between low-frequency semantics and high-frequency detail; all gates are initialized near zero so DCO starts as an identity map and adds negligible overhead (<10⁻³ M params, 3.19 G FLOPs) while preserving O(L) complexity. The Orthogonal Multi-Prototype (OMP) head generates K content-adaptive sub-prototypes per class via a hyper-network and bidirectional cross-attention, then classifies pixels by max cosine similarity across sub-prototypes, regularized by an intra-class orthogonality penalty and an inter-class angular margin loss to prevent prototype collapse. The full system is trained end-to-end with a standard cross-entropy segmentation loss plus the two prototype regularizers, achieving ~32 M parameters and state-of-the-art mIoU on all three benchmarks.

## Results

On ISPRS Potsdam, CRISP (32.32M params) achieves 88.77 mIoU and 93.93 mF1, outperforming the VSSD backbone (85.57 mIoU) by +3.20 and AerialFormer (87.17 mIoU) by +1.60. On Vaihingen, CRISP reaches 83.00 mIoU (+3.25 over VSSD, +0.97 over AerialFormer), and on LoveDA it attains 51.56 mIoU (+4.41 over VSSD, +1.15 over AerialFormer). The only method surpassing CRISP is D2LS (94.0M params), which leads by 0.40 mIoU on Potsdam, 1.06 on Vaihingen, and 1.97 on LoveDA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRISP (32.32M) decisively outperforms its VSSD backbone (56.1M) by +3.20 mIoU on Potsdam, +3.25 on Vaihingen, and +4.41 on LoveDA, confirming that the DCO calibration and OMP head recover detail lost to global aggregation. Against Transformer-based methods, CRISP beats AerialFormer (43.2M) by +1.60/+0.97/+1.15 mIoU and UNetFormer (11.7M) by +2.18/+4.36/+1.68 mIoU across the three benchmarks. Among Mamba-based baselines, CRISP surpasses PyramidMamba (125.1M) by +4.15 mIoU on Potsdam and +3.68 on Vaihingen despite using roughly 4× fewer parameters. The sole method exceeding CRISP is D2LS (94.0M), which leads by a narrow 0.40 mIoU on Potsdam and 1.06 on Vaihingen, but by a wider 1.97 on LoveDA; CRISP thus achieves near-SOTA accuracy at roughly one-third the parameter count of D2LS. Gains are most pronounced on high-variance classes (Low-veg, Tree, Forest), consistent with the paper's motivation of restoring attenuated high-frequency boundary responses.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRISP builds directly on VSSD (ICCV 2025), which itself extends Mamba-2's State Space Duality to 2D images via multi-directional scanning and global aggregation. The key conceptual departure is identifying that this aggregation step acts as an implicit low-pass operator, and proposing the Duality Calibration Operator as the first in-place calibration of the SSM aggregation itself—distinct from prior frequency-domain methods (FreqMamba, FreqFusion) that bolt on parallel spectral branches, and from locality-restoration approaches (LocalMamba) that add windowed scans. The OMP decoder draws on orthogonal-prototype projection from few-shot segmentation (Liu et al., CVPR 2023) but adapts it to dense pixel-level matching with end-to-end learned sub-prototypes rather than offline clustering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic diagnosis (progressive high-frequency energy decay across VSSD layers) that explains why state-space backbones underperform on boundary-sensitive remote sensing tasks, and offers a lightweight fix that preserves linear complexity. The ablation revealing that OMP alone actually degrades performance below the baseline (85.63 vs. 87.32) is a useful caution: multi-prototype matching is only beneficial when the backbone has already restored the intra-class spectral diversity it needs to exploit. Open questions include whether spatially adaptive calibration (conditioned on local frequency content) could further improve results, and whether the DCO concept transfers to other VSSD-based architectures beyond remote sensing segmentation.
