## Problem

Reaction diagram parsing suffers from two critical bottlenecks: severe data scarcity (the dominant RxnScribe dataset contains only 1,240 training images, insufficient for MLLM training) and output instability, where complex sequential dependencies between reactions cause minor prefix errors to cascade into full-sequence failures. Standard next-token prediction training exacerbates instability through shortcut learning (relying on preceding reaction cues) and the oracle prefix problem (training only on ground-truth prefixes while inference uses the model's own imperfect predictions).

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reaction diagram image (RGB) from chemical literature as input and autoregressively generates a structured token sequence encoding all reactions in the diagram. Each reaction is serialized with explicit structural markers (<rxn>, <rct>, <cnd>, <prd>) and contains bounding box coordinates (x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max) followed by a category token (<mol> or <txt>) for every object, enabling downstream OCSR/OCR to recognize molecular and text content. The output format unifies object localization and chemical relationship modeling into a single generation task.

## Method

RobustRDP builds on Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct and employs a three-stage progressive training strategy. First, a layout-driven synthesizer procedurally generates 60,000 synthetic reaction diagrams across four layouts (single-line, multiple-line, tree, graph) by sampling molecules from PubChem and rendering them with the Indigo toolkit, providing pretraining data for initial adaptation. Second, multi-task SFT jointly optimizes three objectives on 4,240 real images plus 63,600 augmented images: vanilla reaction parsing (VRP), region-guided reaction parsing (RGRP, which constrains the model to parse a single reaction within a given ROI to disrupt shortcut learning), and prefix-perturbed reaction parsing (PPRP, which injects coordinate scaling, object omission, and redundant object errors into preceding reactions to train recovery from imperfect prefixes). Third, DPO is applied using 14,169 preference pairs where rejected responses are the SFT model's own high-probability erroneous outputs (hard-match F1 < 0.8), explicitly suppressing failure-prone generation patterns. An efficient annotation platform integrated with a YOLO26 detector enables semi-automated labeling, reducing manual effort by ~40% and supporting the creation of the larger RobustRDP-train (4,240 images) and RobustRDP-test (500 images, 2,634 reactions) datasets.

## Results

On the RxnScribe-test benchmark, RobustRDP achieves a Hard Match F1 of 81.7% and Soft Match F1 of 90.8%, outperforming the strongest sequence-based baseline (RxnCaption-VL) by 9.0 and 4.6 percentage points respectively. On the larger and more diverse RobustRDP-test benchmark (500 images, 2,634 reactions), RobustRDP attains a Hard Match F1 of 82.5% and Soft Match F1 of 91.0%, yielding gains of 17.0 and 13.8 F1 points over RxnScribe. Traditional cascaded-pipeline methods (RxnDE, OChemR) remain far behind, with Hard Match F1 scores below 4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobustRDP decisively outperforms all five baselines on both benchmarks. Against the two traditional cascaded-pipeline methods, RxnDE (Wilary 2023) and OChemR (Martori 2022), the gap is overwhelming: their Hard Match F1 scores are 1.9% and 3.4% respectively on RxnScribe-test, roughly 78 points below RobustRDP, reflecting the fragility of hand-crafted geometric heuristics on diverse layouts. Among sequence-generation baselines, RobustRDP surpasses RxnScribe (Qian 2023, Pix2Seq backbone) by 12.6 Hard F1 points on RxnScribe-test and 17.0 points on RobustRDP-test, where RxnScribe's recall drops sharply (61.7% vs. 82.4%), indicating poor generalization to unseen layouts. RxnIM (Chen 2025, MLLM-based) is the closest competitor on RxnScribe-test (72.1 Hard F1) but degrades to 53.6 on RobustRDP-test, a 28.9-point gap that RobustRDP does not exhibit. RxnCaption-VL, which offloads localization to MolYOLO and only predicts relationship sequences, achieves 72.2 Hard F1 on RxnScribe-test but was not evaluated on RobustRDP-test, leaving its generalization unverified.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coordinate-sequence generation paradigm for reaction diagram parsing established by RxnScribe (Pix2Seq backbone) and advanced by RxnIM (MLLM backbone) and RxnCaption-VL (visual prompting via MolYOLO). Its primary conceptual departure is a diagnosis of two training-pathology issues—shortcut learning from causal attention over preceding reactions and the oracle-prefix train-inference mismatch—and a targeted three-stage training strategy (synthetic pretraining, multi-task SFT with region-guided and prefix-perturbed auxiliary tasks, DPO) to address them. The synthetic-to-real data scaling scheme, combining a layout-driven procedural synthesizer with a semi-automated annotation platform, departs from prior work's reliance on the small 1,240-image RxnScribe-train set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, reproducible recipe for scaling reaction diagram parsing data (60K synthetic + 4,240 real images) and a new, substantially larger evaluation benchmark (RobustRDP-test, 500 images / 2,634 reactions vs. RxnScribe-test's 138 / 392), which the community can adopt for fairer comparison. The prefix-perturbation and region-guided auxiliary tasks are generalizable techniques for any autoregressive structured-output task where sequential dependencies cause cascading failures. Open questions include whether the synthetic-to-real gap can be further narrowed, whether the approach transfers to other scientific document parsing tasks (e.g., mechanism diagrams, flowcharts), and whether integrating explicit chemical transformation knowledge (acknowledged as future work) would close the remaining ~17% hard-match error.
