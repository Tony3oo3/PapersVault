## Problem

Traditional Visual Grounding relies exclusively on textual descriptions to localize objects, a paradigm that inherently struggles with linguistic ambiguity (e.g., 'pass me that one') and ignores the non-verbal deictic cues—particularly hand-pointing gestures—that humans use in real-world egocentric interactions. No large-scale dataset or benchmark existed for first-person hand-pointing referring expression resolution, leaving a critical gap for embodied AI and human-robot collaboration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260980/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the primary EDG task, the model receives an egocentric RGB image, a 2D bounding box of the pointing hand, and a deliberately underspecified linguistic referent (e.g., 'this object' or 'that boy') that identifies category or intent but lacks spatial discriminability, and must output the 2D bounding box of the target object. The hand bounding box is not fed as raw pixel coordinates but is quantized via a spatial quantization function into discrete tokens over N=1000 bins, serving as 'Spatial Anchors' that ground the LLM's attention to the gestural region. A secondary D-VQA task additionally takes a specific question about the referred object and produces a free-text answer, while ablation tasks (D-REC, POG) isolate either the linguistic or geometric cue alone.

## Method

SV-CoT is a zero-shot inference framework instantiated on Qwen3-VL that reformulates grounding as a structured Visual Chain-of-Thought reasoning process rather than direct coordinate regression. The hand bounding box is first mapped into discrete spatial anchor tokens to bridge continuous visual coordinates with the LLM's discrete vocabulary. The model then executes a three-step latent reasoning chain: (1) Deictic Intent Parsing, where it analyzes hand orientation within the anchor to instantiate a 2D directional primitive; (2) Geometric Trajectory Simulation, where a virtual ray from the hand centroid along that direction prunes spatial distractors into a candidate set via intersection constraints; and (3) Spatio-semantic Verification, where a multi-modal alignment score between the linguistic query and pruned candidates resolves the target, with an explicit confidence threshold for rejection. The target distribution is formally modeled by marginalizing over this sequence of geometric rationales, enforcing a structural constraint that mitigates spatial hallucination. No task-specific training is performed; the entire pipeline operates through structured prompting on the frozen MLLM.

## Results

On the EDG task (Real-World split), SV-CoT achieves P@0.5 of 0.818 and P@0.7 of 0.727, surpassing the strongest open-source baseline (Qwen3-VL-8B at P@0.5 = 0.701) by 11.7 percentage points. On the Hybrid split, SV-CoT reaches P@0.5 = 0.756 and P@0.7 = 0.718, a 5.4-point gain over Qwen3-VL-4B (0.702). The Human Study upper bound sits at P@0.5 ≈ 0.91–0.92, leaving a ~10-point gap that indicates substantial room for improvement in hand-object spatial reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SV-CoT outperforms all nine compared model families on both Hybrid and Real-World EDG splits. The closest competitors are the Qwen3-VL and Qwen2.5-VL series: SV-CoT gains 5.4 points over Qwen3-VL-4B (0.756 vs. 0.702) and 11.7 points over Qwen3-VL-8B (0.818 vs. 0.701) at P@0.5 on Real-World, with the gap widening to ~13 points at P@0.7. LLaVA-OneVision-4B and InternVL-3.5-8B trail by 28 and 34 points respectively at P@0.5 (Real-World), and both collapse sharply at P@0.7 (0.129 and 0.214 vs. SV-CoT's 0.727). GroundingDINO-based GroundingGPT, DeepSeek-VL2, LLaVA-v1.5-13B, Ferret-7B, and Florence2 all score below 0.36 at P@0.5, indicating that dedicated grounding architectures without explicit gesture-language fusion are poorly suited to deictic egocentric scenes. Notably, most baselines perform slightly better on Real-World than Hybrid, suggesting the synthetic noise and extreme augmentations in the Hybrid split stress-test spatial reasoning beyond natural capture distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three previously separate threads: (1) classical Referring Expression Comprehension (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, GuessWhat?), which is language-only and third-person; (2) small-scale gesture-pointing datasets from human-robot interaction (PointAt, ReferAt, IPO, IMHF), which are lab-captured, exocentric, and contain only 220–278 images; and (3) recent MLLM-based grounding (GroundingGPT, Florence-2, Qwen-VL series), which has not incorporated deictic hand cues. The SV-CoT baseline repurposes the chain-of-thought reasoning paradigm—previously applied to text-only or image-only reasoning—into a structured spatial inference chain (direction → ray → semantic verification) instantiated as a zero-shot prompt on Qwen3-VL, rather than introducing a new trained architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a concrete and quantified gap: even the strongest MLLMs trail human performance by over 26% at P@0.5 on EDG, and collapse to near-chance on pointing-only grounding (POG), revealing that current multimodal models lack reliable geometric intent inference from hand configuration alone. This has direct implications for embodied AI, AR-assistive devices, and human-robot collaboration, where deictic disambiguation is the primary interaction channel. Key open questions include whether the findings generalize to dynamic (video) pointing, non-canonical gestures, and 3D spatial reasoning; the current dataset is limited to static images with index-finger pointing, and the SV-CoT baseline is a zero-shot prompt rather than a trained model, so the 11.7% gain may not transfer to other backbones without adaptation.
