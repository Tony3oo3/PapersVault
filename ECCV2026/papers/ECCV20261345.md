## Problem

Knowledge distillation from a robust teacher to a small student improves overall performance, but under unknown covariate shift—where certain subgroups are entirely absent from training data—the student exploits spurious features and fails on unseen test groups. Existing diffusion-based data augmentation methods generate samples based only on the training data or the student alone, so they cannot specifically target the student's reliance on spurious correlations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261345/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes real training images (e.g., 250 per class on CelebA-HQ, 100 per class on BAR), a pre-trained teacher (CLIP ViT-L/14 or BEiTv2-B), and an auxiliary student (ViT-T) that has been distilled on the biased real data to inherit its spurious-feature biases. It outputs one synthetic augmented image per real training image, which replaces the real image with probability α=0.5 during downstream student training. The generation is conditioned on a CLIP text encoding of the target class label and uses Stable Diffusion 1.4 in latent space, with computational cost matched to baselines via FLOP accounting.

## Method

ConfiG generates challenging augmentations by maximizing teacher–student disagreement in latent space using the DiG-IN guided diffusion framework. The pipeline first warm-starts with inter-class mixing: null-text inversion on the original image followed by prompt-to-prompt editing toward a randomly sampled target class different from the original. It then performs latent optimization (Eq. 7) that minimizes the student's confidence on the target class while simultaneously maximizing the teacher's confidence on that class, using a nonlinear exponent γ=2 to overcome vanishing gradients and a foreground-aware distance regularization to preserve identity. The auxiliary student, which has already absorbed spurious correlations from the biased training set, serves as the optimization signal so that generated images specifically expose the student's shortcut features. Theoretically, the method targets the distribution-shift component Ω of the generalization gap, reducing reliance on spurious covariates without requiring explicit group annotations.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, ConfiG achieves 97.76% SMA, 95.67% GMA, and 88.15% WGA, outperforming all six diffusion-based augmentation baselines and closing the gap to the CLIP teacher (99.03% SMA) to just 1.3% in SMA and 3.7% in WGA. On SpuCo Birds, ConfiG reaches 73.38% SMA and 39.50% WGA, improving WGA by over 26 points over real-data-only training (12.97%). On BAR, ConfiG achieves 58.66% SMA, second only to Diff-Mix (59.28%). On ImageNet-100, ConfiG attains the best validation accuracy (80.02% for ViT-T, 91.68% for ViT-S) and best SpuScore (62.36% and 75.49% respectively) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConfiG outperforms all six diffusion-based augmentation baselines (GIF, DistDiff, DA-Fusion, Diff-Mix, Diff-II, ActGen) on CelebA-HQ across all three metrics, with the largest margin over ActGen in WGA (88.15% vs. 75.68%) and over Diff-Mix in GMA (95.67% vs. 93.07%). On SpuCo Birds, ConfiG leads by a clear margin in both SMA (73.38% vs. 71.45% for DA-Fusion) and WGA (39.50% vs. 32.13% for Diff-Mix). The sole exception is BAR, where Diff-Mix edges out ConfiG by 0.62% in SMA (59.28% vs. 58.66%), though ConfiG still substantially outperforms all other baselines. On ImageNet-100, ConfiG achieves the best SMA and SpuScore for both ViT-T (80.02%/62.36%) and ViT-S (91.68%/75.49%), beating the closest competitor ActGen by 1.66% SMA and 1.88% SpuScore on ViT-T. When combined with model-centric bias mitigation methods (ERM, LfF, uLA), ConfiG augmentations yield large gains—for example, ERM+ConfiG reaches 90.25% WGA on CelebA-HQ versus 57.84% for ERM alone—demonstrating that the data-centric and model-centric approaches are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConfiG sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: diffusion-based data augmentation for classification (GIF, DistDiff, DA-Fusion, Diff-Mix, Diff-II, ActGen) and knowledge distillation under distribution shift. Its core technical pipeline repurposes DiG-IN's guided latent optimization, null-text inversion, and prompt-to-prompt editing, but introduces a novel objective: maximizing teacher-student confidence disagreement rather than minimizing student loss alone (as in ActGen) or matching a target distribution (as in Diff-Mix). Conceptually, the paper frames this as combining explicit KD (teacher soft labels) with implicit KD (diffusion model as generative teacher), guided by the observation that disagreement regions empirically coincide with groups absent from training data. It also departs from group-robustness methods that require group labels or assume all groups are present, operating without any group annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: when training data systematically omits certain subgroups, distilled students lock onto spurious features and fail catastrophically on unseen groups (WGA drops to ~13% on SpuCo Birds with real data only). ConfiG's 34-point WGA gain on CelebA-HQ and its complementary combination with model-centric debiasing methods (Table 8) suggest a practical recipe for robust distillation in data-scarce, biased settings. Limitations include restriction to global image classification (no detection/segmentation), dependence on a strong pre-trained teacher, and the need for an auxiliary student to guide generation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to location-aware tasks, whether weaker or self-supervised teachers suffice, and how it interacts with emerging single-step diffusion backbones beyond the preliminary Hyper-SD results.
