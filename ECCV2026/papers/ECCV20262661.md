## Problem

VAR models, despite their efficient next-scale prediction paradigm, suffer from spatial misplacement, semantic confusion, and detail degradation in complex multi-object scenes. Existing diffusion-based enhancement methods (iterative refinement, local attention) fail on VAR because its cross-scale conditioning—where each scale depends on all previous scales—causes early errors to propagate and accumulate in ways that single-step diffusion models do not exhibit. No training-free framework had been designed to address this unique error-propagation structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing a complex scene and decomposes it into individual concepts, each assigned a global spatial region (h, w) via an MLLM (used only for evaluation convenience, not required at inference). It operates as a plug-and-play module on top of existing VAR models (Infinity, Switti) without any retraining, producing a high-resolution RGB image. The generation follows the standard VAR coarse-to-fine multi-scale token prediction from 1×1 to H×W, with SynVAR interventions applied at specific early steps and throughout the process.

## Method

SynVAR introduces a spatial-semantic collaborative correction mechanism with three components applied at different stages of VAR generation. (1) Global guidance, applied at step 2, decomposes the prompt into per-concept text embeddings, performs cross-attention between each concept's K/V and the current token map's Q, then crops and concatenates the resulting maps according to assigned spatial regions to inject explicit spatial priors. (2) Receptive field constraint, also at step 2, adds a Gaussian-distance-based bias (with decay factor σ) to self-attention scores, modulated by a binary mask that identifies cross-region token pairs, thereby suppressing excessive inter-region information coupling while preserving necessary context. (3) High-frequency compensation, applied at every step, performs a 2D Fourier transform on each transformer layer's input, multiplies by a linear high-pass filter H(u,v) = 1 + δ·D(u,v), and inverse-transforms back, enhancing fine-grained texture details. The key insight is that VAR's early stages fix spatial and semantic structure (validated by perturbation experiments), so targeted intervention at step 2 prevents error propagation through the cross-scale dependency chain.

## Results

On the Geneval benchmark, SynVAR improves the Infinity base model's overall score from 58.00 to 70.00 (+12.00) and Switti from 28.00 to 40.50 (+12.50). On T2I-CompBench, SynVAR raises Infinity's overall score from 49.90 to 55.66 (+5.76) and Switti's from 49.61 to 52.44 (+2.83). The largest single-metric gain is on Geneval Position, where Infinity jumps from 26.00 to 63.00 (+37.00), demonstrating the method's strength in spatial alignment. Inference time increases modestly from 2.60s to 3.10s per image, and combining SynVAR with FastVAR actually reduces time to 2.27s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynVAR is compared against two VAR base models (Infinity and Switti) and two diffusion-based enhancement methods (DenseDiffusion and RAG-Diffusion). Critically, both DenseDiffusion and RAG-Diffusion degrade performance on VAR models: on Infinity, DenseDiffusion drops Geneval overall from 58.00 to 52.50 and RAG-Diffusion to 44.75; on Switti, RAG-Diffusion collapses Geneval overall from 28.00 to 18.00. In contrast, SynVAR consistently outperforms all baselines across every metric on both benchmarks. The most dramatic gain is on spatial metrics: Infinity's Geneval Position score improves from 26.00 (base) to 63.00 (SynVAR), while T2I-CompBench Spatial rises from 62.90 to 66.23. On Switti, the T2I-CompBench Complex score jumps from 19.09 to 22.14, and Shape improves from 74.83 to 78.54. A user study further confirms that over 68% of human evaluators prefer SynVAR-enhanced images in both aesthetics and text-image alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VAR next-scale prediction paradigm (Tian et al., NeurIPS 2024) and its downstream models Infinity and Switti, positioning itself as the first training-free enhancement framework specifically designed for the VAR generation process. It explicitly departs from diffusion-based enhancement techniques (MultiDiffusion, RAG-Diffusion, DenseDiffusion), demonstrating that these methods degrade VAR performance due to the fundamentally different cross-scale conditioning structure. The method repurposes ideas from cross-attention spatial guidance, attention masking for region decoupling, and frequency-domain filtering into a unified spatial-semantic collaborative correction mechanism tailored to VAR's autoregressive multi-scale generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: VAR models, despite their efficiency and quality advantages over diffusion, struggle with complex multi-object scenes, and no prior training-free solution existed for this specific failure mode. The finding that diffusion-based enhancement methods actively harm VAR performance is a useful negative result that clarifies the boundary between generative paradigms. Limitations include dependence on the base model's prior knowledge (if a concept is absent from the prior, the correction cannot recover it) and empirical selection of the optimal intervention step. Future work on automatic step selection and extension beyond VAR to other autoregressive or hierarchical generative models could broaden the impact.
