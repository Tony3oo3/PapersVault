## Problem

Current video generation models produce visually realistic outputs but fail to maintain physical and causal consistency when simulating complex dynamic events such as collisions, support failures, or fluid impacts. The paper attributes this to existing models fitting visual data distributions that entangle dynamics with appearance, lacking explicit physical priors to govern abrupt state transitions. Prior physics-aware approaches either depend on external simulators with high computational overhead or focus on general spatiotemporal smoothness, making them ineffective for event-driven scenarios requiring causal reasoning about sudden state changes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a first-frame image (I₀), a free-form scene description, and a structured event description following the format <Event Type> | <Involved Entities> | <Mechanism of Interaction> | <Description of State Change> as input, and produces a future video sequence of T frames. Videos are standardized to 480×832 resolution, 81 frames at 16 fps. At inference, a lightweight Mapper predicts continuous physical features from the first frame and event prompt, which are then quantized against a pretrained codebook before injection into the denoising backbone.

## Method

PhysAlign builds on Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (fine-tuned via LoRA rank 16) and introduces three coupled modules. First, a Physical Dynamics Extractor applies photometric augmentation to disrupt appearance cues, then uses event-conditioned cross-attention queries to retrieve spatiotemporal physical patterns, which are quantized into a discrete Physical Codebook serving as an information bottleneck that filters visual noise. Second, an Event-Aware Temporal Gating module generates self-supervised temporal labels from cosine-distance feature change rates (aggregated via top-K spatial pooling, Gaussian smoothing, and temperature-scaled softmax), producing per-frame gate values that amplify physical constraint injection during critical event windows and attenuate it during static phases. Third, a Physics-Guided Alignment module distills spatial and temporal token relationship matrices from a frozen V-JEPA2 teacher into the DiT backbone at mid-layer 18, using a margin-thresholded L1 loss to align structural topology without overfitting to teacher noise. The total loss combines flow matching, VQ commitment, KL-divergence temporal gating, and alignment objectives.

## Results

On the PhysicsIQ benchmark, PhysAlign achieves the highest Semantic Adherence (SA) of 31.54 and Physical Commonsense (PC) of 29.75, outperforming the strongest baseline VLIPP (29.15/26.85). On the EventPhy dataset, it records SA 16.35 and PC 34.83, and on VideoPhy it reaches SA 53.98 and PC 40.54. On VBench, PhysAlign attains the best overall Quality Score of 79.05, with leading scores in Subject Consistency (97.31), Dynamic Degree (80.00), and Imaging Quality (71.46), confirming that physical reasoning gains do not come at the cost of visual fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysAlign outperforms all five baselines on PhysicsIQ, EventPhy, Physion1.5, and VideoPhy in both SA and PC. Against the strongest physics-enhanced baseline VLIPP, PhysAlign gains +2.39 SA and +2.90 PC on PhysicsIQ, +0.71 SA and +3.60 PC on EventPhy, and +2.81 SA and +4.53 PC on VideoPhy. Compared to VideoREPA-5B, the gains are larger: +2.17 SA and +4.46 PC on PhysicsIQ, and +6.69 SA and +4.93 PC on VideoPhy. The one notable exception is VideoPhy2, where PhyT2V and VLIPP achieve higher SA (26.67) and Joint (26.67/25.33) scores than PhysAlign (23.67/23.33), though PhysAlign still leads on PC (66.67 vs. 65.00/63.33). On VBench, PhysAlign surpasses all baselines in overall Quality Score (79.05 vs. 75.22 for VLIPP), confirming that the physical-prior injection does not degrade visual generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysAlign builds on the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B DiT backbone and adopts the Token Relationship Distillation strategy from VideoREPA for its Physics-Guided Alignment module, but departs from both the explicit-constraint line (PhysGen, PhyT2V, VLIPP) and the implicit-representation line (VideoREPA, MoAlign, PhysMaster) by explicitly decoupling physical dynamics from visual appearance through a discrete codebook and an event-aware temporal gating mechanism. Where prior implicit methods rely on general spatiotemporal smoothness as a proxy for physical consistency, PhysAlign targets the specific failure mode of dynamical events (collisions, support failures) that trigger abrupt state changes, using photometric augmentation to suppress appearance shortcuts and a self-supervised feature-change-rate signal to localize when physical priors should be injected.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces both a new task formulation (Dynamical Event-Driven Physically Consistent Video Generation) and a 26K-video dataset (EventPhy) with structured VLM-generated causal annotations, filling a gap in benchmarks that test event-level physical reasoning rather than general smoothness. The discrete-codebook-as-information-bottleneck design for physical priors is a reusable architectural pattern that could transfer to other generation tasks requiring decoupled latent factors. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-body interactions beyond the 16 predefined event types, whether the Mapper's reliance on a single first frame limits its applicability to scenes with occluded or partially visible initial states, and whether the self-supervised temporal gating signal remains reliable for events with subtle or gradual state transitions rather than sharp discontinuities.
