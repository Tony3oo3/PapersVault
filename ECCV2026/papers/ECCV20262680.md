## Problem

INT4 quantized FlashAttention for DiT-based video generation models with 3D RoPE faces two unresolved challenges: (1) standard rotation matrices for outlier mitigation cannot be fused with RoPE's block-diagonal structure, and online rotation is too costly for compute-bound video DiTs; (2) symmetric quantization of the non-negative attention probability matrix P wastes half the 4-bit dynamic range. Existing LLM-oriented quantized attention methods do not transfer because video DiTs require simultaneous Q and K quantization and operate in a compute-bound regime.

## Input / Output

The method is applied as a drop-in INT4 attention kernel inside existing DiT video generation pipelines (Wan2.2 T2V/I2V at 480P/81 frames, HunyuanVideo T2V at 480P/129 frames). T2V models take a text prompt; I2V models take a reference image plus text prompt. The output is a generated video at the same resolution and frame count as the FP16 baseline. The method selectively falls back to FP16 for the first/last few denoising steps and DiT blocks (e.g., 68–81% of attention operations run in INT4 depending on the model), so the external I/O contract is unchanged.

## Method

RotateAttention introduces two core techniques. First, RoPE-aware Rotation uses block-diagonal 2×2 orthogonal matrices to equalize RoPE-induced outliers in Q and K: Interleaved Rotation groups adjacent channel pairs and is mergeable into the RoPE weights at zero inference cost, while Half Rotation pairs each dimension with its counterpart in the other half-segment, exploiting the observed pattern that outliers concentrate in one half of each spatiotemporal segment. Second, Range-optimized P Quantization replaces symmetric INT4 mapping (0–7) with a fixed affine transform (scale 15, zero-point −8) that maps the normalized attention probabilities onto the full [−8, 7] INT4 range, doubling quantization resolution. A standard Hadamard transform is additionally fused offline into the V and output projections to mitigate V outliers at zero runtime cost. The orthogonality constraint on the rotation is proven necessary: non-orthogonal transforms introduce asymmetric singular-value scaling between Q and K that amplifies quantization error in one branch.

## Results

RotateAttention achieves a 2.2× kernel-level speedup over FP16 attention and end-to-end speedups of 1.51× (Wan2.2-I2V), 1.68× (Wan2.2-T2V), and 1.61× (HunyuanVideo-T2V) while maintaining near-FP16 video quality. The best INT4 configurations reach Cosine Similarity of 0.9824 (Half Rotation, I2V), 0.9562 (Interleaved Rotation, T2V), and 0.9751 (Half Rotation, HunyuanVideo), with PSNR up to 23.18 dB (I2V) and 19.21 dB (T2V). Range-optimized P quantization and RoPE-aware rotation together close most of the gap to the FP16 baseline (Cosine 1.0, PSNR ∞), with the remaining gap concentrated in MSE (e.g., 493 vs. 0 for I2V).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SageAttention (INT4 with QKV smoothing but no rotation or optimized P quantization). RotateAttention consistently outperforms SageAttention across all three model configurations: on Wan2.2-I2V, Half Rotation + Optim-P improves Cosine from 0.9809 to 0.9824 and PSNR from 22.83 to 22.90 dB; on Wan2.2-T2V, Interleaved Rotation + Optim-P improves Cosine from 0.9458 to 0.9562 and PSNR from 17.83 to 18.54 dB (a ~0.7 dB gain); on HunyuanVideo-T2V, Half Rotation + Optim-P improves Cosine from 0.9663 to 0.9751 and PSNR from 18.33 to 18.97 dB. The gains are most pronounced on T2V tasks where attention distributions are more diffuse, while on I2V (sharper, lower-entropy attention) the improvement is marginal. Full Rotation (D×D Hadamard) achieves the highest PSNR on I2V (23.18 dB) and T2V (19.21 dB) but at higher computational cost; the paper recommends Half Rotation as the default for its consistent performance and ~3% overhead relative to full rotation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FlashAttention block-wise online softmax framework to INT4 precision and adapts rotation-based outlier mitigation techniques from LLM quantization (QuaRot, SpinQuant, FlatQuant) to the distinct setting of 3D-RoPE video DiTs. The key departure from prior work is the identification that LLM rotation strategies—which typically keep Q in FP16 and rotate only K for KV-cache quantization—do not transfer when both Q and K must be quantized simultaneously in compute-bound video generation. The paper further introduces a range-optimized P quantization that exploits the full INT4 interval, a technique orthogonal to and complementary with token-reordering methods like PAROAttention and channel-smoothing methods like SageAttention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.5–1.7× end-to-end speedup with near-lossless video quality makes INT4 attention practically deployable for production video generation pipelines, where attention dominates compute cost at 10K+ token sequences. The demonstrated non-transferability of LLM rotation strategies to 3D-RoPE DiTs is an important caution for the community attempting to port quantization techniques across architectures. Open questions include whether the rotation strategies generalize beyond the two tested model families (Wan2.2, HunyuanVideo), how they interact with emerging sparse-attention methods on shorter sequences, and whether learned rotations with proper calibration can further close the remaining gap to FP16.
