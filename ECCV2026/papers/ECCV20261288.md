## Problem

Fine-tuning Earth Observation foundation models is computationally expensive, yet existing efficiency methods address only one side of the cost: parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, adapters) reduces training cost but retains full inference depth, while post-hoc structured pruning reduces inference cost but requires completing expensive full fine-tuning first. No prior method simultaneously reduces both training and inference costs by making architecture decisions before adaptation begins.

## Input / Output

SIMPLER takes a pre-trained vision transformer (Prithvi-EO-2, TerraMind, or ViT-MAE) and a small unlabeled sample set of 500 task-domain images, requiring no labels or gradients for the selection step. It outputs a standard dense model with a reduced number of transformer blocks (e.g., 5 of 24 blocks), which is then fine-tuned with a task-specific head to produce segmentation masks, multi-label class predictions, or crop-type labels. Because the output is a conventional dense architecture rather than a sparse one, it deploys directly on any PyTorch/TensorFlow runtime without specialized sparse inference libraries.

## Method

SIMPLER exploits the observation that deep layers in pre-trained ViTs produce nearly identical representations on downstream task data, revealing redundancy before any fine-tuning occurs. It extracts layer-wise features from 500 unlabeled task samples, computes an L×L similarity matrix using Centered Kernel Alignment (invariant to orthogonal transforms), then partitions this matrix at each candidate cutoff c into a retained block (top-left, c×c) and a pruned block (bottom-right, (L−c)×(L−c)). An automated scoring function selects the cutoff that maximizes Δ_TL − Δ_BR, where Δ measures mean absolute consecutive-row difference: high variability in retained layers indicates rich, diverse features, while low variability in pruned layers confirms redundancy. This requires no hyperparameters, gradients, or magnitude thresholds. The selected reduced architecture is then fine-tuned (optionally combined with LoRA for compound efficiency), yielding up to 79% parameter reduction with 94% performance retention and 2.1×/2.6× training/inference speedups.

## Results

On MADOS semantic segmentation with Prithvi-EO-2 (300M), SIMPLER prunes 79% of parameters (303.90M → 64.57M) while retaining 94% of baseline mIoU (62.8% vs 66.9%), achieving 2.1× training speedup and 2.6× inference speedup. On BigEarthNetv2 multi-label classification, SIMPLER achieves 83% compression (51.98M params) with 97% mAP retention (71.2% vs 73.4%) and 4.2× training speedup. On Sen4Map time series, 70% parameter reduction retains 96% F1-macro (63.8% vs 66.6%) with 3.3× inference speedup. Generalization to ViT-MAE on CIFAR-100 yields 87% parameter reduction with 82% accuracy retention (72.8% vs 88.8%) and 6.9× inference throughput improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA (which reduces trainable params to 2.42M but retains full 303.90M inference architecture), SIMPLER achieves 2.7× higher inference throughput (88.72 vs 33.02 img/s) at comparable mIoU (62.8% vs 59.6%), while SIMPLER+LoRA combines both benefits with only 0.55M trainable parameters and 4.31 min training time. Against post-hoc structured pruning (magnitude-based, 20% and 40% compression with retraining), SIMPLER achieves substantially higher compression (79% vs 40%) with better mIoU (62.8% vs 47.9%) and lower training time (7.46 min vs 22.51 min), since post-hoc pruning requires full fine-tuning before any pruning can occur. The critical distinction is that SIMPLER reduces both training and inference costs simultaneously, whereas LoRA only reduces training cost and post-hoc pruning only reduces inference cost after incurring full training cost. On BigEarthNetv2, SIMPLER retains 97% mAP (71.2% vs 73.4%) at 83% compression, outperforming LoRA's 72.2% mAP at zero compression. On Sen4Map, LoRA achieves slightly higher F1-macro (66.9% vs 63.8%) but at 5.5× slower training (304 min vs 55.6 min) and zero inference savings, making SIMPLER preferable for deployment-critical scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIMPLER positions itself at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, adapters) and post-hoc structured pruning, addressing a gap the authors identify: PEFT reduces training cost but leaves inference complexity unchanged, while structured pruning reduces inference cost only after expensive full fine-tuning. The method repurposes representation similarity metrics (CKA, SVCCA, Jaccard) that prior work has used exclusively for post-hoc analysis of trained models, applying them instead to pre-trained representations on unlabeled task data to make architecture decisions before any adaptation occurs. This 'before' stage of operation is the key departure from both PEFT (which operates during training in weight space) and structured pruning (which operates after training on task-specific weight statistics).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact for Earth Observation deployment is substantial: 79% parameter reduction with 94% performance retention, 2.1× training and 2.6× inference speedup, producing standard dense models deployable on satellites and edge devices without specialized sparse inference libraries. The cross-scale finding that a SIMPLER-reduced large model outperforms a natively smaller architecture (TerraMind-Large at 53M beats TerraMind-Small at 22M) advocates a 'reduce once' paradigm that could reshape how EO foundation models are developed and deployed. A key limitation is that the method relies on representation stabilization characteristic of MAE-style self-supervised pre-training; models using contrastive or collapse-prevention objectives may not exhibit the required similarity patterns, leaving extension to those paradigms as the principal open question.
