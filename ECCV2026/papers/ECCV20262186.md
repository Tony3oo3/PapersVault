## Problem

Existing 4D perception methods decouple geometry from motion: static multi-view reconstruction assumes a rigid world with fixed camera poses, while dynamic tracking frameworks rely on explicit pose regression heads or per-object motion models. This separation produces brittle pipelines that struggle to generalize across diverse scenes and motion types, and the scarcity of ground-truth scene flow annotations for dynamic scenes further limits training. The paper asks whether a single, coordinate-invariant representation—relative scene flow—can unify 3D structure, camera ego-motion, and dynamic object motion under one compact formulation.

## Input / Output

Flow4R takes a pair of RGB images at different viewpoints and timestamps and, in a single feedforward pass, predicts a per-pixel property set for each view: a 3D point position map (H×W×3), a scene flow vector (H×W×3) mapping each point to its location in the other frame's coordinate system, a normalized pose weight map (H×W, values in (0,1) summing to 1), and a confidence map (H×W, values in (1,∞)). The formulation is symmetric—both views receive identical predictions with shared network parameters—and from this compact set, camera pose, object motion, optical flow, focal length, and 3D tracking trajectories are all derived analytically without additional regression heads. For sequential data, anchored pairs (each frame paired with a single reference frame) enable scale-aligned tracking without global bundle adjustment.

## Method

Flow4R employs a two-view Vision Transformer with a shared encoder-decoder-head architecture (initialized from CroCo, 0.4B parameters) that predicts the property set symmetrically for both input views, eliminating the need for dedicated pose regression heads or post-optimization. The key training insight is a joint supervision strategy: for static scenes, rigid flow computed from depth and relative poses supervises the predicted flow, while for dynamic scenes, the pose weight map W is trained self-supervised by backpropagating through a differentiable weighted least-squares pose solver, forcing W to segment static reference regions and down-weight dynamic, distant, or occluded pixels. A rigid motion loss further augments supervision by regressing the predicted point map against the ground-truth rigid projection, weighted by the (stop-gradient) pose weight map to avoid contaminating W. The network directly predicts P_vt (the point in the other view's frame) rather than the flow F, which ablations show yields superior tracking and reconstruction accuracy. Training proceeds in two stages (224-resolution linear head, then 512-resolution DPT head) over a mixture of 21 static and dynamic datasets, taking approximately four days on eight A100/H100 GPUs.

## Results

On world-coordinate 3D point tracking (APD3D ↑), Flow4R achieves 78.6 on Aria Digital Twin, 78.5 on Dynamic Replica, and 71.1 on Point Odyssey for all points, outperforming the closest competitor St4RTrack (76.0, 73.7, 68.0) while using fewer parameters (0.4B vs 0.7B). For dynamic points specifically, Flow4R reaches 77.2 on Dynamic Replica and 72.9 on Point Odyssey, surpassing St4RTrack (68.1, 68.7) by a wide margin. On 3D reconstruction, Flow4R attains 81.00 APD and 0.182 EPE on Point Odyssey, beating St4RTrack (78.73, 0.205), though it trails St4RTrack on TUM-Dynamics (79.87 vs 83.42 APD). Flow4R also saves over 50% VRAM (3152 MB vs 6711 MB) at comparable throughput (26.8 vs 27.9 pairs/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flow4R outperforms St4RTrack, the strongest baseline, on 5 of 7 tracking metrics (all-points ADT, DR, PO; dynamic DR, PO) but loses on Panoptic Studio all-points (64.3 vs 69.7) and TUM-Dynamics reconstruction APD (79.87 vs 83.42). Against POMATO, Flow4R dominates on all-points tracking across all four datasets (e.g., 78.6 vs 57.2 on ADT) but POMATO edges ahead on dynamic-point ADT (78.1 vs 70.9), suggesting Flow4R's pose-weight mechanism occasionally over-suppresses dynamic regions in that specific setting. MonST3R and DUSt3R/MASt3R are clearly outperformed: Flow4R gains 4.2 APD3D over MonST3R on ADT all-points and 35.6 over DUSt3R on Point Odyssey reconstruction APD. SpaTracker, the only camera-coordinate tracker, trails Flow4R by 32.9 APD3D on ADT all-points, highlighting the advantage of world-coordinate formulation. Notably, Flow4R achieves these results with only 0.4B parameters versus 0.7B for St4RTrack, MonST3R, and POMATO, and uses 53% less VRAM than St4RTrack at comparable throughput.

## Relation to Prior Work

Flow4R builds directly on the two-view transformer lineage of DUSt3R and MASt3R, adopting their cross-attention architecture and CroCo pretraining, but departs fundamentally in formulation: rather than regressing pointmaps in a shared coordinate frame or appending separate regression heads for different timestamps (as in POMATO, Dynamic Point Maps, or St4RTrack), it predicts a compact, coordinate-invariant property set centered on scene flow. The self-supervised pose weight map, trained by backpropagating through a differentiable pose solver, is a novel mechanism absent from prior DUSt3R-family methods. For sequential processing, it follows St4RTrack's anchored-connection paradigm but leverages the symmetric two-view prediction to perform simple metric scale alignment without post-optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The flow-centric formulation offers a conceptually clean unification: a single compact output (point position, scene flow, pose weight, confidence) supports reconstruction, bidirectional tracking, optical flow, and focal length estimation without dedicated heads or bundle adjustment, and the 50% VRAM reduction over St4RTrack makes it more deployable. However, the approach remains constrained by the scarcity of real-world scene flow annotations, operates only in a pairwise two-view setting (no multi-view attention as in VGGT), and has not been optimized for real-time online tracking. Extending the formulation to multi-view attention and reducing dependence on synthetic training data are the most impactful open directions.
