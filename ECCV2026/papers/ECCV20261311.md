## Problem

GRPO-style RL alignment for VQ-based autoregressive image generation suffers a compute-coverage bottleneck: increasing the group size G improves success-support coverage (measured by SuccessSetMass) but linearly multiplies rollout cost, while small-G training assigns ID-specific credit only to sampled code IDs, leaving many locally substitutable VQ alternatives without positive supervision. The paper asks how to recover the coverage benefits of large groups without paying for additional rollouts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261311/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and autoregressively generates a sequence of VQ codebook token IDs (hundreds to thousands per image), which is decoded into an RGB image. Training consumes sequence-level scalar rewards—either a binary GenEval verifier success indicator or a continuous HPSv2.1 preference score—computed on the decoded image. The graph-diffused soft targets are computed over a fixed precomputed K-NN graph on the codebook embedding space, so no additional rollouts or reward evaluations are needed beyond the standard G per prompt.

## Method

GR-GRPO densifies token-level supervision by diffusing positive evidence from successful rollouts over a precomputed codebook K-NN graph (cosine similarity, K=64). At each position, it aggregates sparse counts of code IDs observed in positive rollouts, propagates them to K-NN neighbors via softmax-weighted diffusion, and normalizes over the observed-plus-neighborhood support to form a soft target distribution q_t. An auxiliary cross-entropy loss L_GR pulls the policy toward q_t, replacing the implicit one-hot target of standard GRPO with a set-valued target over locally substitutable alternatives. A confidence-adaptive gate (sigmoid of negative top-1/top-2 logit margin) suppresses diffusion when the policy is already confident, preventing over-smoothing. A difficulty-aware budget allocation scheme further assigns more rollouts to harder prompts, and the full objective combines L_GRPO + λ_KL·L_KL + λ_GR·L_GR with negligible overhead since targets are computed on sparse supports with precomputed neighbors.

## Results

On GenEval (compositional alignment), GR-GRPO-1B achieves an overall success rate of 0.87, improving over Janus-Pro-1B+GRPO (0.84) and GCPO-1B (0.85); GR-GRPO-7B reaches 0.92, surpassing GCPO-7B (0.90) and STAGE (0.89). On DrawBench (preference/quality alignment), GR-GRPO-7B attains HPSv2.1 = 28.25 and ImageReward = 0.99, the best among all compared methods. In terms of compute efficiency, GR-GRPO at G=8 matches vanilla GRPO at G=32 with 3.3× less rollout time, and with difficulty-aware budget allocation surpasses GRPO at G=64 with ~4.6× less rollout time on Janus-Pro-1B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, GR-GRPO-1B (0.87) outperforms both Janus-Pro-1B+GRPO (0.84) and GCPO-1B (0.85), with the largest gains on Counting (+0.08 over GRPO) and Two Obj. (+0.03). On the 7B scale, GR-GRPO-7B (0.92) beats GCPO-7B (0.90), STAGE (0.89), and Janus-Pro-7B+GRPO (0.87), leading on Counting (0.91 vs. 0.90/0.82/0.71) and Position (0.96). On DrawBench, GR-GRPO-7B achieves the best HPSv2.1 (28.25 vs. STAGE's 27.76) and ImageReward (0.99 vs. 0.96), though STAGE edges ahead on UniReward (3.10 vs. 3.07). GR-GRPO-1B similarly leads GCPO-1B on HPSv2.1 (27.55 vs. 27.18) and ImageReward (0.74 vs. 0.71). The key differentiator is compute efficiency: GR-GRPO at G=8 matches GRPO at G=32 with 3.3× less rollout time, and the full system (with difficulty-aware budget allocation) surpasses GRPO at G=64 with ~4.6× less rollout time, making it the most compute-efficient method in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (originally from DeepSeekMath [36]) to VQ-based autoregressive image generation, building on the same group-relative advantage and clipped surrogate framework used by recent AR image RL methods (GCPO [58], STAGE [28], AR-GRPO [56]). The key departure is in token-level credit assignment: whereas all prior GRPO variants for image generation apply ID-specific (one-hot) credit only to sampled code IDs, GR-GRPO exploits the geometric structure of the VQ codebook to diffuse positive evidence over a precomputed K-NN graph, converting sparse point-wise supervision into set-valued soft targets. This is orthogonal to sample-efficiency techniques that modify which trajectories are explored (tree search, selective rollouts, speculative decoding) or that densify reward granularity (token-level rewards, critical-step identification); instead, it densifies the supervision signal on already-collected rollouts without additional sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.6× rollout-time reduction while matching or surpassing G=64 GRPO is a practically significant efficiency gain for anyone running RL alignment on AR image generators, where sampling dominates compute. The structural observation that VQ codebook neighborhoods encode local token substitutability (validated via K-NN substitution experiments) provides a principled, low-overhead mechanism that is easy to plug into existing GRPO pipelines. Limitations include evaluation only on the Janus-Pro backbone and VQ-tokenized models; the paper itself notes that extending graph-based credit densification to continuous-token or hybrid token-diffusion generators requires learning or defining neighborhood structure, which remains open. Whether the static precomputed K-NN graph remains optimal as the policy shifts during training, and whether the approach composes cleanly with other efficiency techniques (branching rollouts, speculative decoding), are natural follow-up questions.
