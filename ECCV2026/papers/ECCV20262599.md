## Problem

Modern VLMs suffer from granularity mismatch: their contrastive training objective encourages coarse semantic alignment while ignoring high-frequency textures and small objects, leading to low-frequency bias and object hallucination. Although noise-based corruption-to-reconstruction (C2R) pretraining theoretically provides the high-frequency signal needed to remedy this, existing methods (DiffMAE, MaskDiT) yield negligible gains on recognition benchmarks because their Decoder-style architectures prevent noise-induced signals from being deeply integrated into the transferable encoder representations.

## Input / Output

During pretraining, ECC consumes 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet-1K, partitioned into ViT-B patches where a subset of tokens are replaced by learnable mask tokens and a complementary subset receives Gaussian noise injected at an intermediate encoder block (optimal at block 2). The output is a pretrained ViT encoder (no decoder retained) that is transferred to downstream tasks—producing class labels, segmentation masks, detection boxes, or instance masks—or plugged into VLMs (CLIP, AIM-v2, LLaVA) where it processes RGB images alongside text prompts to yield aligned multimodal embeddings.

## Method

ECC unifies masking and noising under three design principles. First, Encoder-Centricity places both corruption and reconstruction inside the encoder so that the transferable representations are directly shaped by handling corrupted signals, rather than offloading restoration to a discarded decoder. Second, Feature-Level Noising injects Gaussian noise at intermediate encoder layers (block 2) rather than at pixel space, exploiting the finding that feature-space perturbations better capture high-frequency textures critical for fine-grained discrimination. Third, Task Disentanglement introduces a disruption loss that operates on the self-attention affinity matrix, explicitly suppressing cross-attention between masked tokens and noised visible tokens to prevent the two reconstruction objectives from interfering with each other. The corruption strategy is Conjunctive (masked and noised tokens coexist alongside clean visible tokens), and the model is trained with a combined MSE reconstruction loss plus the disruption loss over 400 epochs on ImageNet-1K.

## Results

On fine-grained visual categorization (FGVC), ECC achieves 81.76% on CUB-200-2011, 81.16% on NABirds, and 84.42% on Aircraft, surpassing the strongest MIM baseline (MAE) by 2.62%, 3.30%, and 2.86% respectively. The largest gain is on iNaturalist 2018, where ECC reaches 72.26% versus 64.08% for DiffMAE (+8.18%). On standard tasks, ECC attains 83.41% top-1 on ImageNet, 43.54 mIoU on ADE20K, and 48.44 AP^bb on COCO, all leading the comparison set. When integrated into VLMs, ECC improves CLIP zero-shot accuracy by +1.48 (83.35→84.83), AIM-v2 by +0.88, and LLaVA-1.5 by +0.66, while reducing object hallucination (CHAIR_s: 48.4→46.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECC outperforms all four vision-only baselines across every benchmark. Against SimMIM [39] (Encoder-style MIM), ECC gains +6.36% on CUB and +10.62% on Aircraft, confirming that adding feature-level noising and task disentanglement to the Encoder-style paradigm yields substantial improvements over plain MIM. Against MAE [14] (Decoder-style MIM), ECC leads by +2.62% on CUB and +2.86% on Aircraft, validating the core hypothesis that Encoder-centric corruption shapes more transferable representations. Against the two noise-based C2R baselines, DiffMAE [38] and MaskDiT [42], ECC achieves the largest margin on iNaturalist 2018 (+8.18% over DiffMAE) and Aircraft (+4.68% over DiffMAE), demonstrating that prior noise-based methods fail to convert high-frequency signals into recognition gains due to their Decoder-style design. On the VLM side, ECC consistently improves zero-shot accuracy: CLIP [27] gains +1.48 (83.35→84.83), AIM-v2 [11] gains +0.88 (86.23→87.11), and LLaVA [22] gains +0.66 (84.76→85.42). Additionally, on LLaVA-1.5, ECC reduces object hallucination (CHAIR_s 48.4→46.5, CHAIR_i 14.0→12.9) and improves cross-modal retrieval (Flickr30K R@1 69.1→70.2), confirming that the sharpened visual grounding translates beyond vision-only recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Masked Image Modeling (MAE, SimMIM) and denoising diffusion (DDPM, LDM), specifically addressing the noise-based C2R extensions DiffMAE and MaskDiT that attempted to marry the two. It departs from these prior methods on three structural axes: moving corruption and restoration from the decoder into the encoder (Encoder-Centricity), injecting noise at intermediate feature layers rather than pixel space (Feature-Level Noising), and introducing a disruption loss to disentangle mask reconstruction from denoising (Task Disentanglement). The disruption loss is a variant of the masked token optimization (MTO) idea, repurposed to suppress cross-type attention between masked and noised tokens within the encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic for why a theoretically motivated approach (adding Gaussian noise to MIM) has underperformed in practice: the Decoder-style architecture prevents noise-induced signals from shaping the transferable encoder representations. This reframing shifts the design question from 'what corruption to apply' to 'where and how to apply it,' which is a useful principle for future pretraining strategies. Open questions include whether the optimal noise-injection block (block 2) generalizes across different ViT depths and patch sizes, and whether the disruption loss remains necessary at larger scales where attention patterns may self-organize differently. The consistent gains across CLIP, AIM-v2, and LLaVA suggest broad applicability, but all VLM experiments use a single backbone size per framework, leaving the scaling behavior partially unexplored.
