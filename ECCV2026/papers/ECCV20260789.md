## Problem

Existing camera-controlled video editing methods are limited to extrinsic pose manipulation (camera position and orientation) along smooth, continuous trajectories, lacking support for intrinsic parameter control such as focal length and native resolution adaptation. Moreover, the tight coupling between intrinsic and extrinsic parameters in cinematic operations (e.g., the dolly zoom) makes disentangled modeling particularly challenging, and no prior framework unifies multi-shot transitions, re-focal, and resolution editing in a single generation process.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (always single-shot, fixed-focal) together with a target camera specification comprising extrinsic poses (R, T), focal length (via hfov), and output resolution (H_t × W_t), and produces a re-shot video at the target resolution and camera configuration. Source and target tokens are concatenated along the frame dimension (forming a 2N-length sequence) so that every self-attention layer performs direct spatiotemporal cross-attention between source and target. Resolution editing is native: the model re-generates the full frame at the target aspect ratio without cropping, padding, or outpainting, with training resolutions drawn from a bucket set ranging from 320×320 to 832×480.

## Method

CameraAnything builds on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B (a DiT with 3D VAE) and introduces per-pixel Plücker ray embeddings (6-DoF: ray origin + direction) that encode both intrinsic and extrinsic camera geometry at every spatial token. These embeddings are injected into the self-attention layers as learned phase shifts added to the standard 3D RoPE (following the RoCE mechanism), so camera geometry modulates query-key attention similarity without perturbing pretrained feature distributions. For native resolution adaptation, the spatial coordinates of 3D RoPE are recomputed directly from the target-resolution latent grid, allowing the positional encoding to adapt to any output size without architectural changes. Training uses an orthogonal strategy that independently samples extrinsic type (same-camera / different single-shot / single-to-multi-shot), focal-length change (p=0.5), and aspect-ratio change (p=0.2), yielding up to 12 task combinations. A scalable synthetic pipeline in Unreal Engine 5 renders 20 synchronized clips per scene across three groups (base views, multi-shot variants, re-focal variants) to provide paired training data, with only the self-attention layers, camera encoder, and camera adapter modules trained (zero-initialized) while the backbone remains frozen.

## Results

On the synthetic benchmark, CameraAnything achieves PSNR 15.88 / SSIM 0.482 / LPIPS 0.179 for extrinsic+focal control, substantially outperforming TrajectoryCrafter (12.24/0.379/0.654) and ReCamMaster (12.87/0.379/0.607), with FVD of 231.0 vs. 462.9 and 351.5. For resolution editing, it reaches PSNR 20.54 / LPIPS 0.174 / FVD 325.8, beating Follow-Your-Canvas (19.91/0.206/526.8) and VACE (19.45/0.194/422.7). On real-world DAVIS videos, CameraAnything achieves the lowest camera tracking error (RotErr 2.76°, TransErr 0.33) versus 5.12°/0.46 for ReCamMaster and 13.99°/0.99 for TrajectoryCrafter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TrajectoryCrafter [35], CameraAnything wins decisively on every metric: PSNR improves from 12.24 to 15.88 (synthetic) and RotErr drops from 13.99° to 2.76° (real-world), reflecting TrajectoryCrafter's reliance on monocular depth reconstruction that introduces geometric errors. Against ReCamMaster [2], CameraAnything achieves substantially better reconstruction (PSNR 15.88 vs. 12.87) and camera accuracy (RotErr 2.76° vs. 5.12°), but ReCamMaster scores slightly higher on Subject and Background consistency in VBench because it preserves the first frame identically and avoids multi-shot cuts, synthesizing fewer novel regions. For resolution editing, CameraAnything outperforms Follow-Your-Canvas [4] on PSNR (20.54 vs. 19.91), LPIPS (0.174 vs. 0.206), and FVD (325.8 vs. 526.8), and beats VACE [17] on PSNR (20.54 vs. 19.45), LPIPS (0.174 vs. 0.194), and FVD (325.8 vs. 422.7). VACE retains a small edge on Subject and Background consistency (0.9014/0.9225 vs. 0.9030/0.9100) because its outpainting pipeline preserves the source crop, whereas CameraAnything re-generates the full frame for native resolution adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CameraAnything sits at the intersection of three prior threads: ReCamMaster's frame-dimension token concatenation for video-to-video conditioning, ReDirector's RoCE mechanism for injecting camera geometry into 3D RoPE, and CameraCtrl's per-pixel Plücker ray representation. The key departure is unifying intrinsic (focal length, resolution) and extrinsic (position, orientation) camera control within a single DiT-based framework, whereas prior camera-controlled editing methods (ReCamMaster, ReDirector) handle only extrinsic pose along a single continuous trajectory. The synthetic multi-camera dataset and orthogonal training strategy are novel contributions that address the data scarcity problem specific to joint intrinsic-extrinsic control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to treat focal length and native resolution as first-class camera parameters alongside extrinsic pose, enabling practical cross-platform content adaptation (e.g., portrait for mobile, landscape for broadcast) without cropping or outpainting. The fact that a model trained entirely on Unreal Engine 5 synthetic data generalizes to real-world DAVIS videos with competitive camera accuracy (RotErr 2.76°) suggests the geometric formulation is robust. Open questions include scalability to longer multi-shot sequences, handling of dynamic scene content beyond the synthetic animated characters, and whether the approach extends to more complex cinematic operations like dolly zooms where intrinsic and extrinsic changes are intentionally coupled rather than independent.
