## Problem

The prevailing filter-first paradigm in text-to-image generation aggressively discards over 50% of raw training data based on quality signals, assuming low-quality samples are detrimental. This approach is both statistically wasteful and creates a fundamental knowledge gap: the model never learns the boundary between high- and low-quality content, losing exposure to rare concepts and characteristic patterns present only in uncurated data. The paper asks whether these discarded samples can instead be repurposed to improve generation quality.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (with captions standardized via GPT-4o) and a 5-dimensional continuous quality vector [aesthetic score, watermark probability, clarity, entropy, luminance] as joint conditions, producing RGB images at 512×512 or 1024×1024 resolution. The quality vector is not a discrete class label but a set of continuous scalars that are converted into soft, multi-anchor embeddings at training time. At inference, users can set each quality dimension to a target value (e.g., high aesthetic, zero watermark) to explicitly control output characteristics.

## Method

LACON (Labeling-and-Conditioning) reframes the T2I objective from v_θ(x_t, t, y) on filtered data to v_θ(x_t, t, s, y) on the full uncurated dataset, where s encodes per-sample quality attributes. Each continuous quality score is mapped to a soft embedding via Gaussian-weighted Cluster Centroids (GCC): N fixed anchor points per attribute carry learnable centroid tokens, and the score's affinity to each anchor is computed with a Gaussian RBF kernel, yielding a weighted sum that smoothly interpolates across the quality spectrum. The resulting attribute embedding is concatenated with the noisy latent as context conditioning (rather than cross-attention), while the text prompt enters via standard cross-attention. Two inference strategies are provided: LACON-S applies classifier-free guidance only on text with quality held at a target value, and LACON-A applies independent CFG on text and each of the five quality attributes separately, enabling per-dimension guidance scaling. The framework is architecture-agnostic, validated on both a DiT-based flow-matching diffusion model (Sana) and an autoregressive VQ-VAE model (Qwen3).

## Results

On Sana-1.6B at 512×512, LACON-A achieves GenEval 71.6, DPG 78.1, and FID 11.2, outperforming the strongest filtered-data baseline (Baseline-B: 68.0 / 76.1 / 13.1) by +3.6 GenEval, +2.0 DPG, and −1.9 FID while training on 100% of the raw data. At 1024×1024, LACON-A further extends the lead to GenEval 71.5, DPG 78.8, FID 11.3 versus Baseline-B's 68.3 / 76.3 / 12.9. On the autoregressive Qwen3-1.7B backbone, LACON-A reaches GenEval 70.3, DPG 80.1, FID 10.9, surpassing Baseline-B (66.4 / 78.2 / 12.1) by a wide margin, confirming the framework generalizes beyond diffusion models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LACON is compared against four baselines: Baseline-A (≈5% heavily filtered), Baseline-B (≈65% loosely filtered, the strongest filtered-data reference), Baseline-C (100% raw data, no quality conditioning), and Baseline-D (100% raw data + negative-prompt guidance at inference). LACON-S and LACON-A consistently outperform all four baselines on every metric (GenEval, DPG, FID) across both Sana-0.6B and Sana-1.6B at both 512×512 and 1024×1024. The most notable gap is against Baseline-B: LACON-A gains +3.6 GenEval and −1.9 FID at 512×512 on Sana-1.6B, and +3.2 GenEval and −1.6 FID at 1024×1024. Against Baseline-C/D (unconditioned raw data), LACON-A improves FID by 1.3–1.5 points, showing that explicit quality conditioning is essential to avoid degradation from low-quality samples. On the autoregressive Qwen3-1.7B backbone, LACON-A extends the advantage to +3.9 GenEval and −1.2 FID over Baseline-B, confirming the framework is architecture-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

LACON departs from the dominant filter-first paradigm in T2I training (used by Stable Diffusion, Qwen-Image, Hunyuan-Image, Seedream) by repurposing the very quality signals typically used for data discarding— aesthetic scores, watermark probability, clarity, entropy, luminance—as explicit conditioning labels. Conceptually, it is related to Coherence-Aware Diffusion and MIRO, which condition on annotation reliability or multi-reward signals, but LACON specifically targets the full quality spectrum of uncurated data and introduces a Gaussian RBF-based cluster-centroid embedding to encode continuous quality scores. The framework is model-agnostic, demonstrated on both flow-matching DiT (Sana) and autoregressive (Qwen3 + VQ-VAE) backbones, and extends standard classifier-free guidance with per-attribute guidance scales at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that 100% of uncurated data with explicit quality conditioning outperforms the best filtered subset (~65% retention) across GenEval, DPG, and FID on both diffusion and autoregressive architectures at multiple scales, LACON challenges the field's core assumption that low-quality data is purely detrimental. The knowledge-gap analysis (long-tail concepts lost to filtering) and the joint controllability over multiple quality dimensions suggest a broader shift from destructive curation to constructive conditioning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video or 3D generation, how it interacts with RLHF/preference-based fine-tuning, and whether the quality-scoring pipeline itself introduces biases that propagate into the conditioning space.
