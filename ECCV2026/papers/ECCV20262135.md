## Problem

Existing video hallucination benchmarks either rely on text-based perturbations (confounding errors with instruction-following bias) or pair visually dissimilar videos, making it impossible to attribute model failures to foreground semantic misinterpretation versus background variation. No prior benchmark constructs adversarial video pairs with highly similar backgrounds but distinctly different query-relevant foregrounds, which is necessary for precise, controlled attribution of hallucination sources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262135/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark presents 15-second video clips (three 5-second segments at 480p) in adversarial pairs alongside text questions spanning binary QA, multiple-choice QA, and open-ended description tasks. Each pair shares a near-identical background (verified via DINOv2, LPIPS, and SSIM on keyframes) while differing in foreground semantics across 10 spatial or temporal aspects. Models produce yes/no answers, ranked clip-level descriptions for MCQ, or free-text descriptions, evaluated with pair-level consistency metrics (qAcc, vAcc, wAcc) and bias metrics (FP rate, Yes Percentage Difference).

## Method

PAIRFLOW is a three-stage synthesis pipeline that constructs background-consistent, foreground-divergent adversarial video pairs with minimal human intervention. Stage 1 (Story Composition) uses GPT-4.1 to generate paired narratives with placeholder values ensuring logical consistency and semantic distinctness. Stage 2 (Video Clip Generation) produces keyframes via FLUX, applies foreground-only edits via SeedEdit to preserve backgrounds, then synthesizes sequential 5-second clips with Wan2.1/2.2 where each clip V_i = f(V_{i-1}^L, S_i) conditioned on the preceding clip's last frame and the story script. Stage 3 (Video Assembly) creates adversarial pairs at three granularities: video-level (full content replacement), temporal-level (complete sequence reversal), and spatial-level (clip masking or segment replacement). The resulting VIDPAIR-HALLUC benchmark (1K pairs, 11.5K QA) is then used to evaluate 15 LVMs, with a four-level human audit pipeline ensuring background similarity and foreground semantic clarity in every retained pair.

## Results

On VIDPAIR-HALLUC, Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the best overall performance with 49.15% binary wAcc, 13.07% FP, 67.32% MCQ F1, and 54.68% open-ended description score, yet remains far below the human upper bound of 74.32% wAcc and 89.21% F1. GPT-5-mini attains the highest video-pair accuracy (vAcc 45.28%) among all models. Among open-source LVMs, Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct leads with 41.66% wAcc and 61.50% F1, while Video-LLaMA2 achieves the best open-source MCQ F1 at 62.91%. All 15 evaluated models show substantial degradation relative to human performance, confirming persistent difficulty with fine-grained adversarial video understanding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-2.5-Pro dominates across nearly all metrics, achieving the highest binary wAcc (49.15%), lowest FP (13.07%), best MCQ F1 (67.32%), and best open-ended description score (54.68%), outperforming GPT-5-mini (29.33% wAcc, 64.82% F1) and GPT-4 (26.97% wAcc, 59.15% F1) by 19.8 and 22.2 points in wAcc respectively. GPT-5-mini, however, edges out Gemini-2.5-Pro on video-pair accuracy (45.28% vs. 43.36%), suggesting stronger per-video consistency. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct leads in wAcc (41.66%) but suffers a high FP rate (48.71%), indicating a calibration gap; Video-LLaMA2 achieves the best open-source MCQ F1 (62.91%) but with the worst FP (43.00%). Compared to the prior benchmark VIDHALLUC, most models show substantial performance degradation on VIDPAIR-HALLUC, confirming that background-controlled foreground divergence is significantly harder. All models remain 25+ points below the human wAcc upper bound (74.32%), underscoring that no current LVM achieves robust fine-grained video hallucination resistance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lineage of video hallucination benchmarks (Vript-HAL, EventHallusion, VideoHallucer, VIDHALLUC) by introducing a controlled-background condition that prior paired-video benchmarks lacked. Where VIDHALLUC and HallusionBench rely on naturally dissimilar videos or CLIP/DINO-based retrieval—allowing background, camera motion, and foreground to vary simultaneously—VIDPAIR-HALLUC fixes the background and alters only query-relevant foreground semantics, enabling cleaner error attribution. The PAIRFLOW construction pipeline repurposes the automated adversarial-sample generation paradigm from image VQA (AutoHallusion, CounterCurate) to the video domain by chaining FLUX (T2I), SeedEdit (image editing), and Wan2.1/2.2 (video generation), sidestepping the scalability bottlenecks of mining real videos or applying unstable video-editing tools frame-by-frame.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a concrete and actionable failure mode: t-SNE and attention analyses show that mainstream LVMs underweight foreground semantics and are biased toward background cues, meaning their hallucinations are partly a representational issue rather than purely a language-prior issue. The large human–model gap (74% vs. ~49% wACC for the best model, Gemini-2.5-Pro) underscores that even frontier systems lack robust fine-grained video understanding under adversarial conditions. Limitations include the 15-second / 3-clip video length, the reliance on synthetic generation (which may not capture all real-world background-foreground entanglements), and the fact that the controlled setting, while diagnostically clean, may overstate the difficulty relative to natural video. The PAIRFLOW pipeline also positions the dataset as a reusable source of hard negatives for future contrastive or preference-based alignment training.
