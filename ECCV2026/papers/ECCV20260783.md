## Problem

Discrete video VAEs, which are essential for autoregressive next-token-prediction video generation, suffer from unstable training, prolonged convergence, and degraded reconstruction quality—especially at high compression ratios—compared to their continuous counterparts. Prior work treats discrete and continuous tokenization as independent problems, overlooking that FSQ-based discretization is geometrically close to continuous latents and could be leveraged to stabilize discrete training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260783/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 17-frame video clips at 256×256 resolution during training (finetuned on 33-frame clips) and is evaluated on 33×512×512 videos. A single unified network simultaneously produces both a discrete token sequence (via FSQ rounding) and a continuous latent representation, with the active path selected stochastically per training step. Compression ratios range from 8×8×4 up to 16×16×4 (spatial×spatial×temporal), and the multi-token variant splits each latent channel into two independently quantized sub-vectors, effectively doubling per-position codebook capacity without increasing the token count.

## Method

OneVAE introduces a joint discrete-continuous dual-path training strategy where, at each step, the latent is randomly routed through either an FSQ quantizer (discrete path) or passed directly (continuous path), and the corresponding reconstruction loss is backpropagated. This is combined with a progressive training pipeline that warm-starts from a low-compression (8×8×4) continuous VAE and incrementally extends to higher-compression and discrete settings, yielding a ~5× convergence speedup over training FSQ from scratch. Two architectural enhancements further boost high-compression discrete reconstruction: multi-token quantization, which decomposes each latent vector along the channel dimension into two independently FSQ-quantized sub-vectors to increase effective representational capacity by nearly 1 dB PSNR; and first-frame enhancement, which allocates a lower compression ratio to the anchor frame in causal VAEs to mitigate the limited-context degradation that disproportionately affects early frames under high temporal compression.

## Results

On Panda70M, OneVAE achieves PSNR 32.48 / SSIM 0.9335 / LPIPS 0.0430 / FVD 59.16 as a continuous VAE at 8×8×4 compression, outperforming CV-VAE (31.12) and Open-Sora-v1.2 (31.67). As a discrete VAE at the same compression, OneVAE reaches PSNR 30.67 / SSIM 0.9228, surpassing Cosmos (30.34) and Emu-3 (29.45). At higher compression (16×16×4), OneVAE-FE achieves PSNR 28.11 / FVD 213.04, substantially outperforming Cosmos (26.80 / 350.87). For video generation on UCF-101, OneVAE with an AR generator achieves gFVD 37 (vs. reconstructed GT), beating LARP-L (43) under the same architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneVAE dominates all continuous VAE baselines at 8×8×4: it beats CV-VAE by +1.36 dB PSNR and halves FVD (59 vs. 101), and outperforms Open-Sora-v1.2 by +0.81 dB PSNR with a 43% FVD reduction. At 16×16×4 continuous, OneVAE gains +1.82 dB PSNR over Cosmos (32.43 vs. 30.61) and cuts FVD by 35%. Among discrete VAEs at 8×8×4, OneVAE edges out Cosmos by +0.33 dB PSNR and +0.007 SSIM, though Cosmos retains a slight FVD advantage (83 vs. 109); the gap over OmniTokenizer is dramatic (+6.94 dB PSNR). The multi-token variant (OneVAE-MT) at 8×8×8 achieves PSNR 31.80, surpassing Cosmos at the same ratio by +4.63 dB and reducing FVD from 303 to 78. For video generation on UCF-101, OneVAE's AR generator achieves gFVD 37 (vs. reconstructed GT), improving over LARP-L (43) by 14%, confirming that the unified discrete-continuous training does not hinder downstream autoregressive generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing paradigm of training discrete and continuous video VAEs as entirely separate systems. It builds on FSQ (Finite Scalar Quantization) as the discrete quantization mechanism and on CV-VAE's continuous VAE architecture and training protocol, but introduces a conceptual bridge: by showing that FSQ's rounding-based discretization is geometrically closer to continuous latents than codebook-lookup VQ, the authors justify a joint dual-path training objective and a progressive low-to-high-compression pipeline. The two architectural additions—multi-token quantization and first-frame enhancement—extend prior causal VAE designs (e.g., Cosmos Tokenizer) to address specific failure modes at high compression ratios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical 5× convergence speedup and the ability to obtain a single model that performs competitively in both continuous and discrete modes are directly useful for teams building multi-modal LLMs that need both diffusion-style latents and next-token-prediction tokens. However, the training is limited to WebVid10M at 256×256, and the downstream generation validation is restricted to UCF-101 at 128×128 with a single AR architecture, leaving open whether the unified training strategy and architectural gains hold at the scale and diversity of modern video generation pipelines. An open question is whether the balanced dual-path ratio (R_dis = 0.5) generalizes across compression ratios or requires per-configuration tuning.
