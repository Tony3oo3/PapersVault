## Problem

Long-range world-consistent video generation—where the camera traverses extended trajectories and revisits previously generated regions—remains largely unsolved. Existing geometry-aware methods operate within a single generation chunk, and memory mechanisms such as point-cloud caches or stored RGB frames lack principled 3D fusion, leading to geometric drift and inconsistency when the camera returns to earlier areas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260715/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a single RGB image (with depth estimated via MoGe), a user-specified camera trajectory, and a text prompt, the model autoregressively generates 36-frame RGB-D chunks at 480×832 resolution, progressively building a global 3D scene mesh. The trajectory is split into chunks of 36 frames; at each step, the current OctMap is rendered to the target camera poses to produce RGB-D spatial conditions and visibility masks that condition the next diffusion step, while packed history frames supply temporal context.

## Method

OctWorld extends the 14B FramePack video diffusion model to 15B parameters by adding depth-prediction channels, a control branch for rendered world-memory conditioning (following VideoPainter's DiT-block injection), and a noise-level parameter for robustness. The core contribution is OctMap, a sparse octree-based TSDF memory that adaptively allocates voxel resolution based on per-pixel depth (s = z/√(f_x·f_y)), subdividing nodes only where image evidence demands finer geometry. Generated RGB-D frames are fused into the octree via standard TSDF integration with level-dependent truncation, and a mesh is extracted per chunk using Marching Cubes over leaf-node cubes. Camera control is provided through Plücker ray embeddings processed by a lightweight convolutional adapter, and training uses noise augmentation on history and memory inputs to close the training-inference gap.

## Results

On the WorldScore benchmark (200 scenes, 108 frames each), OctWorld achieves the highest overall WorldScore Static of 50.31, surpassing Voyager (49.93), AETHER (46.59), Gen3C (45.48), and VMem (42.4). It leads in 3D Consistency (71.67 vs. Gen3C's 70.52) and Photometric Consistency (77.01 vs. Gen3C's 68.46), while ranking second in Camera Control (39.12 vs. Voyager's 46.60) and Style Consistency (78.17 vs. AETHER's 83.49). On the spatial memory maintenance task (Re10K reversed trajectory), OctWorld achieves LPIPS 0.1633 and Chamfer Distance 0.0345, outperforming Gen3C (0.1933 / 0.0457) and VMem (0.4010 / 0.0620). In a 433-frame long-range user study, OctWorld receives 53.8% preference versus 39.5% for Gen3C and 0.9% for VMem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OctWorld wins decisively on the two metrics most relevant to world consistency: 3D Consistency (71.67) and Photometric Consistency (77.01), beating the closest competitor Gen3C by 1.15 and 8.55 points respectively, and outperforming all other baselines by larger margins. On Camera Control, Voyager leads (46.60 vs. 39.12), and AETHER dominates Style Consistency (83.49 vs. 78.17), indicating OctWorld trades some trajectory precision and long-horizon style stability for superior geometric and appearance coherence. On the spatial memory maintenance task, OctWorld reduces LPIPS by 15.5% over Gen3C (0.1633 vs. 0.1933) and by 59% over VMem (0.4010), with similar gaps in Chamfer Distance. In the 433-frame long-range user study, OctWorld is preferred by 53.8% of participants, nearly 14 points ahead of Gen3C (39.5%), while VMem is effectively non-competitive at 0.9%. Overall, OctWorld's advantage is most pronounced in geometric fidelity and cross-chunk consistency, while it remains competitive but not dominant in camera controllability and stylistic coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

OctWorld builds on FramePack's autoregressive video diffusion architecture (with its long-horizon context packing and anti-drift properties) and adapts it to joint RGB-D generation. Its 3D memory module, OctMap, draws directly from classical SLAM volumetric fusion (TSDF) and adaptive multi-resolution octree mapping, but repurposes these for open-domain generative settings where scene scale is unknown and unbounded. The key departure from prior world-consistent video generation methods (Gen3C, Voyager, VMem) is the use of a principled, globally fused volumetric representation rather than point-cloud accumulation or feature-bank retrieval, and the introduction of depth-adaptive voxel resolution to handle varying scene scales without a fixed grid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in explorable world generation: maintaining geometric and photometric consistency when the camera revisits previously generated regions after long trajectories. The adaptive octree TSDF offers a clean solution to the scale problem that plagues fixed-resolution volumetric methods in open-world settings, and the reprojection quality experiment (Table 5) provides a clear, isolated demonstration of its advantage. Limitations include training exclusively on static scenes, a 15B-parameter model requiring 32 H100 GPUs, and inference at ~3.6 s per diffusion step, which constrains real-time interactivity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, whether the octree can be made differentiable for end-to-end optimization, and how it scales to truly unbounded or multi-room environments.
