## Problem

Local feature matching, a core component of SfM and visual localization pipelines, has stagnated relative to dense and feed-forward reconstruction methods that benefit from orders of magnitude more training data. Existing benchmarks derived from successful 3D reconstructions are near saturation, obscuring remaining failure modes and making it difficult to measure genuine progress in matching robustness and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260934/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images and processes them at a fixed 560×560 resolution during training (4096 keypoints at inference). DaD keypoints are detected per image, DeDoDe descriptors are computed, and the LightGlue-based matcher refines descriptors through 9 alternating self/cross-attention blocks. At inference, correspondences are extracted as mutual maxima from the dual-softmax assignment matrix with a confidence threshold of 0.1, and a per-keypoint matchability score is predicted via a separate linear head. Layer-wise supervision enables early stopping at layers 3, 5, or 9 to trade accuracy for throughput (up to ~300 pairs/sec on A100 for LoMa-B at L=3).

## Method

LoMa combines the DaD keypoint detector, DeDoDe descriptor, and LightGlue matcher under a unified large-scale training recipe. The key contribution is a 17-dataset training mixture spanning indoor, outdoor, aerial, driving, synthetic, and forward-motion scenes (with MVS-generated ground truth for three datasets), replacing the single-dataset (MegaDepth) training used by prior sparse matchers. The descriptor is trained for 50K steps with a dual-softmax (infoNCE) loss, then frozen while the matcher trains for 250K steps with layer-wise dual-softmax plus binary cross-entropy matchability supervision. Three model variants (B/L/G) share the same 9-block transformer architecture with RoPE in self-attention but differ in embedding dimension (256/512/1024). Ablations show the data mixture contributes ~9 points, the detector/descriptor swap ~6 points, and model scaling ~1.5 points on HardMatch, with consistent gains from both data and capacity scaling.

## Results

On the newly introduced HardMatch benchmark, LoMa-G achieves 54.3 mAA@10px, outperforming the previous SotA sparse matcher ALIKED+LightGlue (35.7) by +18.6 points and the best dense matcher RoMa (48.1) by +6.2 points. On WxBS, LoMa-G reaches 73.4 mAA@10px (+29.5 over ALIKED+LightGlue). On relative pose estimation, LoMa-L achieves 72.2 AUC@10° on MegaDepth and 51.7 AUC@10° on ScanNet, surpassing all prior sparse matchers by 8.4 and 12.9 AUC respectively. On visual localization (InLoc DUC2 at 1m/10°), LoMa-G scores 89.3%, a +21.4 point gain over ALIKED+LightGlue (67.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoMa-G decisively outperforms the previous SotA sparse matcher ALIKED+LightGlue across all benchmarks: +18.6 mAA on HardMatch (54.3 vs 35.7), +29.5 on WxBS (73.4 vs 43.9), +24.2 AUC on RUBIK@10° (73.2 vs 49.0), and +12.4 mAA on IMC 2022 (89.3 vs 76.9). Against the strongest dense matcher RoMa, LoMa-G wins on HardMatch (54.3 vs 48.1) and WxBS (73.4 vs 72.6), while RoMa v2 remains competitive on MegaDepth AUC (77.0 vs LoMa-L's 72.2). Feed-forward reconstruction methods (MASt3R, VGGT) are substantially weaker on HardMatch (33.6 and 28.4 mAA respectively), confirming that the benchmark is genuinely challenging for non-sparse approaches. On ScanNet relative pose, LoMa-L (51.7 AUC@10°) surpasses even RoMa v2 (56.2) in the 5° setting but trails slightly at 10°, while on InLoc DUC2 LoMa-G (89.3% at 1m/10°) exceeds all prior sparse matchers by over 20 points. The ablation in Table 5 shows the gains decompose into: switching from ALIKED to DaD+DeDoDe (+5.7), scaling training data from MegaDepth to 17 datasets (+9.1), longer training (+0.4), and larger model capacity (+1.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not introduce new architectures but instead applies modern data-driven scaling to the existing DeDoDe descriptor, DaD keypoint detector, and LightGlue matcher, arguing that the 'death of the local feature matcher' narrative (driven by detector-free methods like LoFTR/RoMa and feed-forward reconstruction like MASt3R/VGGT) is premature. The key departure from prior sparse matching work (SuperGlue, LightGlue, ALIKED) is the training data mixture: 17 diverse 3D datasets with MVS-generated ground truth, approaching the data volume used by feed-forward reconstruction models, combined with longer training and larger model capacity. The paper also introduces HardMatch, a 1000-pair hand-annotated benchmark that extends the WxBS evaluation protocol to a much larger and more diverse set of challenging pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results challenge the field's working assumption that sparse detector-based matching is fundamentally outperformed by dense or feed-forward approaches, showing that with sufficient data scale and compute, the classical paradigm can match or exceed them on challenging benchmarks. HardMatch addresses a real gap: existing benchmarks (MegaDepth, WxBS) are near saturation, obscuring remaining failure modes and encouraging overfitting to benchmark artifacts. Open questions include whether the data-scaling gains continue to hold at even larger scales (the paper notes descriptor training tends to overfit), whether in-plane rotation invariance can be integrated without performance loss, and whether the geographic/temporal biases in HardMatch limit its generalizability as a benchmark.
