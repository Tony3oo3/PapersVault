## Problem

Conventional WSI diagnosis treats clinical labels as flat, mutually exclusive categories, ignoring the inherently hierarchical coarse-to-fine structure of pathology reasoning. Existing remedies—discrete hierarchical classifiers and unconstrained VLMs—either fail to encode semantic continuity between taxonomically related diagnoses or produce taxonomic hallucinations and parent-child label violations, leaving no method that simultaneously achieves structured reasoning, taxonomic faithfulness, and interpretable text output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262066/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a gigapixel-scale WSI, decomposed into patch instances encoded by the frozen UNI foundation model, and produces a pair of autoregressively generated text strings: a coarse-level diagnostic category (e.g., 'Malignant') and a fine-level subtype or grade (e.g., 'Ductal Carcinoma In Situ'). Evaluation uses strict exact-match on the full token sequence after removing special tokens, so partial or semantically similar but non-identical outputs are counted as incorrect.

## Method

TaxoMIL splits the MIL-aggregated slide embedding into coarse and fine halves, projects each through a lightweight adaptor, and feeds them as visual tokens into a shared GPT-2 small autoregressive backbone with two lightweight generation heads. Each head receives a conditioning prefix concatenating the visual token, a learnable <|img|> token, a label-context token (mean of all base label embeddings at that granularity), and a sampled prompt template. Training combines four objectives: token-level cross-entropy for label-text generation, a hierarchy alignment loss (HAL) plus sibling margin loss (SML) that structure the label embedding space to respect the clinical taxonomy, an image-text alignment loss (ITAL) that grounds visual embeddings in the taxonomy-structured label space, and a supervised contrastive loss on visual embeddings. A cyclic loss schedule alternates emphasis between intra-modal structuring (HIER, CL) and cross-modal alignment (ITAL) via a cosine-squared modulator to prevent optimization instability.

## Results

Under the strict holistic evaluation protocol (requiring exact match at both coarse and fine levels), TaxoMIL achieves 64.55% ACC on GastWSI, 75.78% ACC on BRACS, and 54.60% ACC on PANDA, outperforming all 12 baselines on every dataset. At the coarse level, TaxoMIL reaches 88.56%, 90.74%, and 71.29% ACC respectively; at the fine level, 65.86%, 77.78%, and 55.07% ACC. The largest margin is on BRACS fine-level, where TaxoMIL leads the next-best method (ABMIL at 70.37%) by 7.41 percentage points in ACC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TaxoMIL outperforms all 12 baselines on all three datasets under the strict holistic protocol. On GastWSI, it leads CAMP (63.26% ACC) by +1.29% and ABMIL (61.02%) by +3.53%; on BRACS, it surpasses ABMIL (68.52%) by a substantial +7.26% ACC and +0.0638 W-F1; on PANDA, it beats WSI-VQA (50.28%) by +4.32% ACC. No single baseline is consistently competitive across all three datasets: CAMP is best on GastWSI but drops to 47.47% on PANDA, ABMIL leads BRACS among MIL methods but falls to 49.06% on PANDA, and WSI-VQA is strongest on PANDA yet only 56.74% on GastWSI. Among hierarchical MIL baselines, HMIL and Chang et al. underperform even flat MIL methods on several datasets, suggesting that discrete multi-branch classification does not fully exploit taxonomic structure. The VLM-based baselines (SlideChat, CAMP) show the largest inconsistency, with SlideChat dropping below 52% ACC on both GastWSI and BRACS, highlighting the difficulty of unconstrained text generation for WSI diagnosis.

## Relation to Prior Work

TaxoMIL carves new ground at the intersection of three established lines of work: flat MIL classifiers (TransMIL, CLAM, ABMIL, MambaMIL), hierarchical MIL with discrete class indices (HMIL, Chang et al., ViLa-MIL, HiClass), and unconstrained VLM-based text generation for pathology (WSI-VQA, SlideChat, CAMP). It departs from hierarchical classifiers by replacing multi-branch discrete classification with a dual-head autoregressive decoder that generates label text, and from VLMs by imposing explicit taxonomy-guided objectives (hierarchy alignment, sibling margin, image-text alignment) that structure the embedding space according to the clinical hierarchy. The MIL backbone is repurposed not as a classifier but as a conditioning mechanism that feeds granularity-specific visual tokens into the text decoder, effectively bridging the representation-learning strength of MIL with the interpretability of generative output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine clinical gap: pathologists reason coarse-to-fine, yet most WSI methods treat labels as independent indices, and existing VLMs produce taxonomically incoherent text. By embedding the clinical taxonomy directly into the loss landscape, TaxoMIL reduces parent-child violations and hallucinations while maintaining competitive accuracy, offering a template for any hierarchical diagnostic task. Key limitations include the assumption of a fixed taxonomy (no dynamic adaptation), residual parent-child violations, and the additional compute cost of the autoregressive decoder. Open questions include extending to deeper or evolving taxonomies, scaling to multi-label or open-vocabulary settings, and whether the taxonomy-guided objectives transfer to other medical imaging domains.
