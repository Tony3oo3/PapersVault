## Problem

Current generation-based and hybrid dexterous grasp methods overlook the impact of hand pose initialization on generation quality: initial latent samples aligned with the target object's geometry and grasp type are far more likely to yield physically plausible grasps, yet no prior work systematically retrieves such geometry-aware priors. Additionally, existing grasp-type classifiers (e.g., Dexonomy's) predict only the single most probable grasp type, collapsing multiple feasible grasp modes into one prediction and thereby limiting grasp diversity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view partial point cloud of the target object (mimicking realistic perception) as primary input, which is first completed by a point cloud completion network into a full point cloud. At inference, it additionally consumes grasp-type success statistics aggregated from geometrically similar retrieved training objects. The output is a set of dexterous grasp poses, each comprising three sub-poses (pre-grasp, grasp, squeeze) with 22-DoF joint angles, a global rotation in SO(3), and a 3D translation, along with a selected grasp type from a 31-type taxonomy.

## Method

RAGrasp comprises two components. The Geometry-Aware Initialization (GAI) strategy trains a mobius normalizing flow on SO(3) for global rotation plus MLPs for joint angles, then generates and physics-simulates grasps for all training objects to build a distribution-aligned retrieval database of model-generated successful grasps (rather than ground-truth grasps, avoiding distribution shift). At inference, geometrically similar training objects are retrieved via feature-space distance, their successful grasps are inverted through the flow to obtain latent representations, and these serve as geometry-aligned initial samples for forward flow generation on the test object. The Diversity-Aware Selection (DAS) network is a dual-head classifier whose multi-label head predicts per-type feasibility above a tunable threshold τ (enabling random selection among multiple feasible types for diversity) and whose single-label head acts as a fallback when no type exceeds τ, naturally adapting to object difficulty.

## Results

On BODex, RAGrasp achieves a Grasp Success Rate (GSR) of 86.7%, an 8.2% relative improvement (6.6% absolute) over the second-best method UniDexGrasp (80.1%), while also attaining the lowest Penetration Depth (3.38) and matching the best Self-Penetration Depth (0.04). On Dexonomy under the type-conditioned setting, RAGrasp reaches 71.5% GSR (11.9% relative gain over nflow's 63.9%) while maintaining competitive diversity (D=26.2). Under the type-unconditioned setting, RAGrasp achieves the best average success rate of 39.1% across 31 grasp types, a 13.0% relative improvement over nflow (34.6%) and diffusion (24.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAGrasp outperforms all five baselines across both datasets. On BODex, it surpasses the strongest baseline UniDexGrasp (UDG) [46] by 6.6 percentage points in GSR (86.7% vs. 80.1%) and achieves lower Penetration Depth (3.38 vs. 3.47), while the older regression-based methods GTTA [16] (14.8%) and ISAG [7] (20.7%) and the diffusion-based DP3 [48] (62.4%) are substantially outperformed. On Dexonomy type-conditioned, RAGrasp (success-oriented mode) improves GSR from 63.9% (nflow [3]) to 71.5%, a 7.6-point absolute gain, while the diversity-oriented mode matches nflow's diversity score (25.5 vs. 25.7) with a slightly higher GSR (65.6% vs. 63.9%). On Dexonomy type-unconditioned, RAGrasp beats both nflow (34.6%) and diffusion (24.6%) baselines from [3] by 4.5 and 14.5 percentage points in average success rate respectively, with consistent gains across all 31 grasp types including large-diameter (57.8% vs. 55.5%), precision sphere (56.8% vs. 46.9%), and writing tripod (46.1% vs. 40.2%). The ablation in Table 4 further confirms that the distribution-aligned data collection module is essential: using ground-truth poses for retrieval (Base+Comp+Ret) actually degrades performance below the baseline, validating the need for model-generated grasps in the retrieval database.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the hybrid architecture of Dexonomy (mobius normalizing flow for SO(3) rotation/translation plus regression MLPs for joint angles) and extends it with two retrieval-augmented components. The GAI strategy repurposes the 'initial noise matters' insight from diffusion-model literature (InitNO, golden noise) into the normalizing-flow setting, where no analogous initialization mechanism previously existed. The DAS component generalizes Dexonomy's single-label grasp-type classifier into a dual-head (multi-label + single-label) formulation, explicitly treating grasp-type selection as a diversity-vs-reliability tradeoff rather than a pure argmax problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distribution-alignment finding—that retrieving ground-truth (analytically generated) grasps actually degrades performance compared to retrieving model-generated grasps—provides a practical design principle for any retrieval-augmented generative pipeline where the retrieval source and the generative model have different data distributions. The flexible diversity knob (threshold τ) in DAS is a simple but useful mechanism for deployment scenarios that trade off exploration vs. reliability. Limitations include the two-stage dependency (a trained flow model must exist before retrieval can be constructed), the modest real-world validation (10 objects, 3 trials, 70% success), and the open question of whether the retrieval database scales to truly open-world object distributions without degrading similarity matching.
