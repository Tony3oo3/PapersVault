## Problem

CLIP-based models struggle with long-context descriptions (>77 tokens) because they are pretrained on short captions with absolute positional encoding, and prior extensions remain highly sensitive to semantically preserving perturbations such as sentence reordering, word dropout, or partial omission. The paper attributes this fragility to the Euclidean contrastive objective, which enforces strict one-to-one matching and lacks explicit mechanisms for modeling hierarchical part-whole relationships between a global description and its constituent semantic elements.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images and text descriptions of varying length (short captions up to 77 tokens and long captions up to 248 tokens via interpolated positional encoding), producing both a global sentence-level embedding and token-wise embeddings for each modality in the Lorentz hyperbolic manifold. For retrieval, it outputs image-text similarity scores (negative geodesic distance) used to rank candidates; when integrated with SDXL, hyperbolic text embeddings are mapped back to the Euclidean tangent space via the logarithmic map to condition image generation. Training uses paired short and long captions for the same image, with the short-text pair serving as the distillation reference.

## Method

HyFL-CLIP fine-tunes a pretrained Open-CLIP model by projecting its embeddings into the Lorentz hyperbolic manifold and optimizing four coupled objectives. First, a cross-manifold similarity distillation loss aligns the hyperbolic student's pairwise similarity distribution (negative geodesic distance) with the Euclidean teacher's cosine-similarity distribution over short text-image pairs via KL divergence. Second, a bidirectional hyperbolic geodesic contrastive loss (InfoNCE in Lorentz space) aligns long text-image and short text-image pairs. Third, a hierarchical entailment loss aggregates token-wise features via a similarity-weighted Einstein midpoint (weights derived from cross-modal geodesic distance) and constrains the global embedding to lie within a hyperbolic entailment cone centered at the midpoint, providing a geometric tolerance margin that preserves alignment under token-level perturbations. Finally, a radius-entropy regularizer stabilizes the distribution of hyperbolic radii across the batch.

## Results

On zero-shot long-caption cross-modal retrieval (Table 1), HyFL-CLIP with ViT-L/14 achieves 85.39% T2I on DOCCI, 76.19% T2I on DCI, 63.93% T2I on Long-DCI, and 94.30% T2I on Urban-1k, consistently outperforming all Euclidean baselines. Under caption perturbations (Table 2), HyFL-CLIP exhibits the smallest performance degradation across all perturbation types, with only a 9.21% drop under 50% word dropout versus 27.82% for the next-best method (HiMo-CLIP), and even a slight improvement (+1.27%) under sentence order shuffling. On short-caption retrieval (COCO, Flickr30K), HyFL-CLIP maintains competitive performance (e.g., 41.50% T2I on COCO), confirming that hyperbolic distillation preserves the original CLIP alignment. The paper reports up to 19.5% relative improvement over the best prior method on long-text cross-modal retrieval under textual perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyFL-CLIP outperforms all seven Euclidean long-context CLIP baselines on the majority of retrieval metrics. The closest competitor is HiMo-CLIP (ViT-L/14), which edges out HyFL-CLIP by 0.23 points on DOCCI I2T (82.35 vs. 82.12) and 0.14 points on Long-DCI I2T (62.06 vs. 61.92), but HyFL-CLIP wins on all T2I metrics (e.g., 85.39 vs. 84.59 on DOCCI, 76.19 vs. 74.54 on DCI, 94.30 vs. 93.20 on Urban-1k). FineLIP is competitive on DOCCI I2T (82.20) but lags significantly on DCI and Long-DCI T2I. Under caption perturbations, the gap widens substantially: HyFL-CLIP drops only 9.21% under 50% word dropout while HiMo-CLIP drops 27.82% and FineLIP drops 26.59%, and HyFL-CLIP is the only method that improves (+1.27%) under sentence order shuffling. On short-caption retrieval (COCO/Flickr30K), HyFL-CLIP matches or slightly exceeds HiMo-CLIP (41.50 vs. 40.71 T2I on COCO), confirming that the hyperbolic distillation does not degrade the original CLIP alignment. On text-to-text intra-modality retrieval, HyFL-CLIP achieves the best mAP on four of five datasets (e.g., 60.63 on Flickr30K vs. 58.02 for HiMo-CLIP, 36.69 on 20News vs. 35.49), with only a marginal tie on IMDB (52.60 vs. 52.65).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: long-context CLIP fine-tuning (Long-CLIP, HiMo-CLIP, FineLIP, TULIP) and hyperbolic vision-language representation learning (Desai et al. 2023, Pal et al. 2024). It departs from the long-context line by arguing that Euclidean contrastive objectives are fundamentally limited in modeling part-whole relationships, and from the hyperbolic VLM line by being the first to fine-tune a pretrained Euclidean CLIP into hyperbolic space via cross-manifold similarity distillation rather than training from scratch. The hierarchical entailment loss adapts the hyperbolic entailment cone formulation from Ganea et al. (2018) and Le et al. (2019) to the cross-modal setting, combined with the geodesic InfoNCE from Desai et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode of long-context CLIP models—sensitivity to semantically preserving perturbations like sentence reordering or word dropout—and shows that hyperbolic entailment cones provide a geometric margin of tolerance that Euclidean point-to-point matching lacks. The 19.5% improvement under perturbations is practically meaningful for applications where descriptions are noisy, reordered, or partially available. Open questions include whether the benefit persists at much longer contexts (>512 tokens), whether the added hyperbolic operations and multi-term loss introduce prohibitive training overhead at scale, and whether the distillation from short-text pairs remains effective when the long-text distribution diverges substantially from the short-text distribution. The successful integration into SDXL suggests broader applicability, but the approach still inherits CLIP's architectural constraints.
