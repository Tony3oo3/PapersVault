## Problem

Weakly supervised 3D semantic segmentation suffers from 'boundary starvation': sparse supervision rarely reaches boundary regions, and existing consistency-based or pseudo-label self-training methods systematically under-supervise boundaries. The paper demonstrates that naively increasing loss weight on boundary points actually degrades performance, and that high-confidence pseudo-label filtering inherently discards boundary labels, leaving object contours poorly delineated in complex scenes.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a point cloud (XYZ + RGB per point), sparse weak labels, and calibrated multi-view 2D observations (RGB images, depth maps, surface-normal maps, camera intrinsics and extrinsics) to generate boundary-constrained pseudo-labels. At inference, only the raw point cloud is required—no 2D views or external foundation models are needed. The output is a per-point semantic class assignment over the full scene.

## Method

Bound3D comprises three modules. First, a Boundary Illumination Module (BIM) fuses depth-filtered Canny edges with normal-discontinuity masks across multiple calibrated views and back-projects them into a 3D pseudo-boundary set. Second, a Boundary-constrained Pseudo Label Generator (BPLG) propagates weak labels toward these boundaries using a manifold-density constraint (valid paths must maintain local point density above a threshold, preventing cross-object leakage) and Farthest Angle Sampling for angular diversity; a Boundary Arrival Rate (BAR) metric dynamically triggers iterative refinement rounds with position-aware confidence thresholds (lower at boundaries than interiors) and safe seed selection from core regions only. Third, a Position-Aware Divide-and-Conquer (PADC) strategy routes interior pseudo-labels to a weakly-perturbed auxiliary head (with stop-gradient) for stable intra-class calibration, while boundary pseudo-labels supervise a strongly-perturbed main head with distance-aware weighting, combined with standard consistency and GT losses.

## Results

On S3DIS Area 5 under 0.01% supervision, Bound3D-MinkNet achieves 61.2% mIoU and 54.5% B-mIoU, outperforming PointMatch (59.9%), CPCM (59.8%), and AADNet (60.8%). Under the 1 pt/obj setting, Bound3D-PTv2 reaches 63.5% mIoU, surpassing AO-PTv2 (62.7%) by 0.8 points. On ScanNetV2 test, Bound3D-MinkNet achieves 63.2% mIoU at 0.1% supervision (vs. SQN's 56.9%) and 62.4% at 20 pts/scene (vs. OTOC's 59.4%). The BCLP pseudo-label generator achieves 94.4% overall accuracy and 92.7% boundary accuracy on S3DIS, substantially exceeding SAM-based baselines (77.1% P-Acc, 66.1% B-Acc for PP2S).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAM-based pseudo-labeling methods, Bound3D wins decisively on pseudo-label quality: BCLP achieves 94.4% P-Acc and 92.7% B-Acc on S3DIS versus PP2S/AO-PTv2's 77.1%/66.1% and You et al.'s 71.6% (no boundary metric reported). On final segmentation, Bound3D-PTv2 (63.5%) beats AO-PTv2 (62.7%) by 0.8 mIoU under the same 1 pt/obj protocol, and Bound3D-MinkNet (61.9%) still outperforms OTOC (50.1%), DAT (56.5%), and RAC-Net (58.4) under the OTOC 0.02% setting. Among consistency-based methods at 0.01% supervision, Bound3D (61.2%) surpasses PointMatch (59.9%), CPCM (59.3–59.8%), and AADNet (60.8%) by 0.4–1.9 mIoU points. On ScanNetV2, Bound3D (63.2%) outperforms SQN (56.9%) by 6.3 mIoU at 0.1% supervision, and at 20 pts/scene it beats MIL (54.4%), DAT (55.2%), and OTOC (59.4%) by 8.0, 7.2, and 3.0 points respectively. The only method that exceeds Bound3D is You et al.-PTv3 (64.4%) under the OTOC 0.02% setting, which uses a higher annotation budget (0.02% vs. 0.004% for 1 pt/obj) and a Point Transformer V3 backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the consistency-based training paradigm for weakly supervised 3D semantic segmentation (primarily DAT [46] as the backbone framework) but departs from it by diagnosing a fundamental 'boundary starvation' problem: consistency losses blend semantic distinctions at class boundaries rather than providing explicit supervision. It contrasts with the recent SAM-based pseudo-label pipeline (PP2S [17], SAM3D [51], You et al. [52]) by generating pseudo-labels through 2D geometric cues (Canny edges, depth/normal discontinuities) and boundary-constrained 3D propagation, avoiding cross-modal topological bias and the need for expensive foundation models. The Position-Aware Divide-and-Conquer (PADC) strategy further differentiates the work from uniform-weight consistency training by applying asymmetric perturbations to interior versus boundary regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, foundation-model-free alternative for generating high-purity pseudo-labels in weakly supervised 3D segmentation, which is valuable given the compute and licensing costs of SAM-based pipelines. However, the method requires calibrated multi-view RGB/depth/normal observations during training, limiting applicability to single-view or LiDAR-only settings. The authors acknowledge that thin or small objects (e.g., clutter) remain challenging due to weak 2D boundary observations, and extension to outdoor LiDAR-dominated scenes with sparser viewpoints is left as future work. The broader implication is that boundary-aware supervision design, rather than simply increasing loss weight, is the key to improving weakly supervised segmentation at class transitions.
