## Problem

Existing differentiable vectorization methods formulate image-to-vector reconstruction as a flat optimization problem where hundreds of randomly initialized Bezier curves simultaneously compete for per-pixel loss reduction. This disordered dynamics causes topology collapse—macroscopic structures get distorted by high-frequency noise, producing a redundant, uneditable 'polygon soup' that defeats the core advantage of vector graphics: editability. The paper identifies the root cause as a mathematical imbalance between area and boundary gradients in the splatting formulation, compounded by the absence of any structural hierarchy in the optimization process.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB raster image (evaluated on Kodak at 768×512 and DIV2K at higher resolution) and produces a set of closed Bezier curves with filled interiors, parameterized by control points, color, opacity, and depth. Curve budgets are fixed at 256, 512, or 1024 for fair comparison, and the hierarchical construction naturally supports level-of-detail rendering by truncating the densification schedule at intermediate curve counts. The output is structured with explicit temporal Z-ordering (newer, smaller curves always render on top of older, larger ones), making layers directly editable in professional vector software.

## Method

Vector Scaffolding is a three-component hierarchical optimization framework built on the Bezier Splatting renderer. First, Interior Gradient Aggregation restores the missing positional gradients from curve interiors via the Reynolds transport theorem, correcting the O(r²)-vs-O(r) scale imbalance between area and boundary terms that destabilizes large base curves. Second, Progressive Stratification monotonically doubles the curve population every 100 iterations (starting from N₀=16), spawning new curves exclusively in high-residual regions with a scale-decaying initialization radius (r_rad=0.5) and strict temporal Z-ordering, aligning the representation with the natural power law of image frequency. Third, Rapid Inflation Scheduling exploits the resulting structural stability to run at 50× higher learning rates (0.5 for color/opacity, 0.01 for control points) without divergence, reaching the full 1024-curve budget in just 600 iterations and completing 5k-iteration training in 2.5× less wall-clock time than Bezier Splatting while improving PSNR by up to 1.4 dB.

## Results

On the DIV2K benchmark, Vector Scaffolding achieves 21.37 / 22.64 / 23.99 dB PSNR at 256 / 512 / 1024 curves, outperforming the state-of-the-art Bézier Splatting by 0.5–0.6 dB across all budgets. On Kodak, the margin widens to 1.0–1.4 dB PSNR over Bézier Splatting, with LPIPS improving from 0.546 to 0.450 at 1024 curves. The method also accelerates total optimization by 2.5× in wall-clock time compared to Bézier Splatting while using the same differentiable rasterization backend.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Bézier Splatting [14] (the primary SOTA baseline sharing the same rasterizer), Vector Scaffolding wins on PSNR by 0.5–0.6 dB on DIV2K and 1.0–1.4 dB on Kodak at every curve budget, while also achieving the best LPIPS at 512 and 1024 curves; the only metric where it trails is SSIM at 256 curves on DIV2K (0.564 vs 0.580), which the authors attribute to their geometric regularization favoring coherent fills over local texture matching. Against DiffVG [13], the gap is larger (≈0.7–1.0 dB PSNR on DIV2K), and against LIVE [17] it is ≈1.3–0.9 dB. LIVSS [23] is the weakest baseline, trailing by 3.6–4.2 dB PSNR on DIV2K, largely because it relies on an external diffusion prior that limits scalability. The 2.5× wall-clock speedup is measured specifically against Bézier Splatting, the fastest prior method, confirming that the gains come from optimization restructuring rather than renderer changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Bezier Splatting renderer [14] as its differentiable rasterization backend, but departs fundamentally from its flat optimization paradigm where all curves compete simultaneously for per-pixel loss reduction. It also contrasts with prior layered vectorization methods (LIVE [17], LIVSS [23], O&R [8]) that introduce hierarchy but rely on external priors such as diffusion models or sequential path initialization. The key conceptual departure is treating the optimization *structure*—not the renderer—as the primary lever for quality and speed, introducing Interior Gradient Aggregation (grounded in the Reynolds transport theorem), Progressive Stratification (scale-decaying curve spawning), and Rapid Inflation Scheduling as a unified hierarchical framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that optimization structure can yield a 2.5× wall-clock speedup and up to 1.4 dB PSNR gain *without changing the renderer* challenges the field's prevailing focus on improving differentiable rasterization backends, suggesting that how curves are organized and scheduled matters as much as how they are rendered. The natural LoD support (progressive curve inflation with fixed hyperparameters) aligns with professional vector-art workflows and could enable scalable editing of high-resolution vectorizations. Limitations include the tight coupling between the hierarchical schedule and the extreme learning rate (flat optimization diverges at ×50 LR), a visible SSIM trade-off from geometric regularization, and evaluation restricted to closed Bezier curves on two datasets—open questions remain about generalization to open strokes, 3D vectorization, or other primitive types.
