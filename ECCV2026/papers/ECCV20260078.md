## Problem

LLM-based RefVOS methods must select informative keyframes from long videos, but existing strategies either rely on handcrafted heuristics (first-K, uniform) that miss action-critical moments, or depend on separately trained external keyframe models (CLIP, Grounded-VideoLLM, LLaMA-VID) that add pipeline complexity. The paper asks whether a single model can natively identify text-relevant key moments without external modules or explicit timestamp encodings, and how those moments can be leveraged to improve both segmentation quality and tracking robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260078/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video of T frames and a natural language referring expression, processing them at two resolution levels: low-resolution uniformly sampled frames (≤16 tokens/frame) supply global temporal context to the LMM, while high-resolution frames at MCS-selected key moments feed the SAM2 decoder for pixel-level masks. A single [SEG] token encodes the referential target across the entire video, and a [FIND] token produces a per-frame similarity distribution used for moment localization. Outputs are binary segmentation masks for all T frames plus a temporal segment boundary for the TSG task.

## Method

MomentSeg builds on Qwen2.5-VL + SAM2 and introduces a [FIND] token trained with a contrastive cosine-similarity loss against frame-level temporal tokens, enabling native key-moment identification without timestamp encoding or external models. At inference, Moment-Centric Sampling (MCS) converts the [FIND] similarity distribution into a sampling plan: a sliding window locates the moment center c*, remaining budget is allocated to left/right partitions proportional to their cumulative similarity mass, and InverseCDF stratified sampling draws indices so that high-relevance regions receive dense coverage while the rest of the timeline is sparsely represented. Bidirectional Anchor-updated Propagation (BAP) then initializes SAM2 tracking at c* and propagates masks in both temporal directions, adaptively clearing the memory cache at sampled anchor points when cumulative tracking confidence falls below a threshold relative to the current prediction score, thereby correcting accumulated drift. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with LoRA, jointly optimizing the TSG matching loss and the RefVOS segmentation loss.

## Results

On the primary RefVOS task, MomentSeg-3B achieves J&F of 62.0 on MeViS(valu), 54.8 on MeViS(val), 72.0 on Ref-YoutubeVOS, and 76.4 on Ref-DAVIS17, setting new SOTA across all four benchmarks. On Temporal Sentence Grounding, MomentSeg-3B reaches R@0.3 of 76.1 and mIoU of 50.2 on Charades-STA, surpassing the previous best (NumPro-LongVA-7B) by 12.3 and 8.8 points respectively. On reasoning segmentation, MomentSeg-3B scores 62.6 J&F on ReVOS and 61.7 on ReasonVOS, and on Ref-SAV it achieves 64.0 J&F without fine-tuning, exceeding Sa2VA-8B by 22.7 points. Scaling to 7B further improves all metrics, e.g., 57.1 J&F on MeViS(val) and 77.4 on Ref-DAVIS17.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MomentSeg-3B outperforms the strongest prior LLM-based method, Sa2VA-Qwen2.5-VL-3B, by 4.5 J&F on MeViS(valu), 4.8 on MeViS(val), 1.0 on Ref-YoutubeVOS, and 2.0 on Ref-DAVIS17. Against GLUS-7B, the largest competing 7B model, MomentSeg-3B leads by 1.1 on MeViS(valu) and 3.5 on MeViS(val), while MomentSeg-7B extends the gap to 5.8 on MeViS(val). On reasoning VOS, MomentSeg-3B surpasses VRS-HQ-7B by 3.5 J&F on ReVOS and ViLLa-6B by 6.3 on ReasonVOS. On the TSG task, MomentSeg-3B (a 3B model) beats the previous best NumPro-LongVA-7B by 12.3 R@0.3 and 8.8 mIoU on Charades-STA, and by 11.9 R@0.3 and 6.6 mIoU on ActivityNet-Grounding. On Ref-SAV, MomentSeg-3B without fine-tuning (64.0 J&F) exceeds Sa2VA-8B with fine-tuning (50.0) by 14.0 points, demonstrating strong zero-shot transfer. On image segmentation, MomentSeg-3B leads Sa2VA-4B by 3.2–5.2 cIoU across RefCOCO/+/g, and MomentSeg-7B surpasses GLaMM-7B by 2.0–3.3 mIoU on GCG.

## Relation to Prior Work

MomentSeg builds directly on the Sa2VA/VideoLISA 'One Token Seg All' paradigm, where a single [SEG] token feeds a SAM2 decoder to produce per-frame masks, and on LISA's image-level [SEG] token design. Its key departure from prior LLM-based RefVOS methods (VISA, ViLLA, VRS-HQ, GLUS) is eliminating the dependency on external keyframe-selection models: instead of calling LLaMA-VID, Grounded-VideoLLM, or CLIP to identify relevant timestamps, MomentSeg introduces a [FIND] token that performs frame-level cosine-similarity matching against temporal tokens, natively grounding the query within the video. The Moment-Centric Sampling strategy and Bidirectional Anchor-updated Propagation are novel inference-time mechanisms that exploit this native similarity signal, positioning the work as a self-contained alternative to multi-model pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying TSG and RefVOS in a single model and removing external keyframe selectors, MomentSeg simplifies deployment and reduces the engineering burden of multi-model pipelines, while the [FIND]-token mechanism offers a clean, training-free alternative to explicit timestamp encodings. The practical impact is moderate: gains over the strongest same-scale baseline (Sa2VA-Qwen2.5-VL-3B) are 1–3 J&F points on most RefVOS benchmarks, though the 22.7-point jump on Ref-SAV and the 5–6% gains on MeViS/ReVOS are meaningful. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very long videos (minutes-to-hours) where the TSG-stage similarity computation and SAM2 propagation cost grow, and whether the single-[SEG]-token assumption remains sufficient for multi-object or rapidly changing referents.
