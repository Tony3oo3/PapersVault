## Problem

Generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting methods produce severe geometric artifacts (floaters, duplicated surfaces, distorted shapes) under sparse-view conditions because 2D photometric supervision alone cannot resolve depth and correspondence ambiguities. Existing remedies—depth priors, diffusion-based image priors, or projection-level consistency—provide only indirect or proxy constraints and rarely enforce explicit identity-level coupling between corresponding 3D primitives across views, leaving the reconstruction ill-posed when overlap is limited.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of uncalibrated RGB images as input, requiring no ground-truth camera poses; at test time, poses are estimated by the MASt3R backbone from the input views themselves. The Gaussian prediction head outputs a dense per-pixel map of 3D Gaussian parameters (3D centroid, 3×3 covariance matrix, scalar opacity, and spherical harmonic coefficients) aligned to the descriptor grid, which are rasterized via differentiable grid sampling to render novel views. During training, ground-truth camera-to-world transforms are used to lift Gaussians into a shared world frame for the consistency loss.

## Method

MAC-Splat augments the MASt3R matching backbone with a frozen DINOv3 encoder through a lightweight Residual Semantic Fusion MLP that adds a learned residual to the baseline descriptors, improving match distinctiveness in ambiguous regions. Reciprocal nearest-neighbor matching on the fused descriptors, gated by a confidence threshold, yields a sparse set of high-confidence 2D correspondences per image pair. The core contribution is the Multi-Attribute Consistency (MAC) loss, which lifts each matched pixel pair to its associated Gaussians in world space and jointly penalizes discrepancies in position (Huber loss on centroids), shape (Huber loss on log-eigenvalue differences of world-frame covariance matrices, making the metric rotation-invariant and anti-degeneracy), and appearance (L1 on opacity and SH coefficients), all weighted by the joint match confidence. This direct 3D attribute coupling, combined with standard photometric losses, stabilizes optimization under wide baselines and suppresses the collapsed or floating artifacts that plague photometric-only training.

## Results

On ScanNet++, MAC-Splat achieves PSNR of 22.76 / 22.60 / 22.09 / 21.34 dB across the Close, Medium, Wide, and Very Wide difficulty splits, with only a 1.42 dB drop from Close to Very Wide—compared to >8 dB drops for PixelSplat and MVSplat. It attains the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on Medium, Wide, and Very Wide, and the best LPIPS (0.146) on Close. Averaged over all four splits, MAC-Splat improves PSNR over Splatt3R by more than 4.5 dB and reduces LPIPS by approximately 44%. On the zero-shot DTU cross-dataset evaluation, MAC-Splat reaches 17.32 dB PSNR, outperforming PixelSplat (13.33), MVSplat (14.03), and Splatt3R (12.13).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAC-Splat outperforms Splatt3R by more than 4.5 dB in average PSNR and reduces LPIPS by ~44% across all four difficulty splits, with the gap widening as overlap decreases (e.g., 21.34 vs 16.16 dB on Very Wide). Against the correspondence-based baselines DUSt3R and MASt3R, MAC-Splat wins on every split and every metric, confirming that explicit 3D Gaussian representation plus multi-attribute consistency substantially surpasses colored point-cloud rendering. PixelSplat achieves the highest Close-split PSNR (23.98 vs 22.76 dB) but degrades sharply to 15.36 dB on Very Wide, whereas MAC-Splat holds 21.34 dB—a 5.98 dB advantage under the hardest conditions. NoPoSplat is competitive on Close and Medium but trails MAC-Splat by 3.47 dB (Wide) and 2.60 dB (Very Wide) in PSNR. MVSplat wins only on the Close split (22.64 vs 22.76 dB PSNR is actually a MAC-Splat win; MVSplat's 22.64 is close) but collapses to 13.91 dB on Very Wide, far below MAC-Splat's 21.34 dB. On the zero-shot DTU transfer, MAC-Splat leads all three 3DGS baselines by 3.3–5.2 dB in PSNR, indicating that the multi-attribute consistency objective generalizes beyond the training domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAC-Splat builds directly on the MASt3R geometric backbone for correspondence matching and augments it with frozen DINOv3 features via a lightweight residual fusion module. It departs from prior generalizable 3DGS methods (PixelSplat, MVSplat, Splatt3R, NoPoSplat) that rely solely on 2D photometric supervision, and from external-prior approaches (monocular depth, diffusion priors) that provide proxy geometry rather than explicit cross-primitive coupling. The key conceptual departure is the Multi-Attribute Consistency loss, which enforces identity-level agreement on position, shape (via log-eigenvalue comparison of world-frame covariance matrices), and appearance of matched Gaussians in a shared coordinate frame—a form of supervision that prior intrinsic-regularization methods (dropout, binocular guidance) do not explicitly target at the primitive-attribute level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated robustness to wide baselines (only 1.42 dB PSNR drop from Close to Very Wide, versus 8+ dB for PixelSplat and MVSplat) addresses a core practical limitation of generalizable 3DGS in real-world capture scenarios where overlap is limited. The zero-shot transfer from ScanNet++ to DTU suggests the consistency objective captures geometry-agnostic structure rather than dataset-specific statistics. Open questions include whether the approach extends to outdoor scenes with unbounded geometry, dynamic content, or fully pose-free settings where MASt3R pose estimation itself becomes unreliable; the current evaluation is confined to indoor scenes and object-centric benchmarks, and the frozen DINOv3 encoder limits the semantic signal to what that single model encodes.
