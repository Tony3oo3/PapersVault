## Problem

Most deep hyperspectral-panchromatic fusion architectures perform cross-modal interaction through stacked layers, implicitly coupling global PAN-to-HS information transfer to network depth. This coupling prevents independent control over cross-modal interaction strength and local spatial refinement, limiting the ability to balance spectral preservation against spatial enhancement in regions with differing requirements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260153/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution hyperspectral image (C_H × h × w) and a single-band high-resolution panchromatic image (1 × H × W) with a 4× spatial upscaling factor, and reconstructs a high-resolution hyperspectral image at PAN resolution. LRHS inputs are simulated via Wald's protocol (8×8 Gaussian blur followed by 4× downsampling), and the final HRHS output is produced by a lightweight coordinate-conditioned convolutional decoder that concatenates a normalized spatial coordinate grid with the refined leader representation to break translation invariance.

## Method

LGD-Net explicitly decouples global cross-modal interaction from local multi-scale reconstruction through a leader-guided dynamic pipeline. A gated depthwise-dilated feature pyramid extracts multi-scale features (scales 1,2,4,8) per modality, which are refined via discrepancy-aware gated graph propagation between adjacent pyramid levels. The refined multi-scale features are then aggregated into compact modality-specific leaders, and only these leaders evolve through a continuous cross-modal dynamical system (Neural ODE integrated with RK2) featuring bilinear interaction terms, spatial gating, and a learnable bounded step size. After T integration steps, the evolved HS leader is injected back into the multi-scale hierarchy via structured residual modulation, providing global guidance without increasing architectural depth. The network is trained end-to-end with L1 loss using Adam and cosine annealing over 1600 epochs.

## Results

LGD-Net achieves the best performance across all three benchmarks on every reported metric. On Botswana, it reaches PSNR 41.86 dB, SSIM 0.958, SAM 1.695°, CC 0.987, RMSE 0.011, and ERGAS 1.016. On Chikusei, it attains PSNR 47.81 dB, SSIM 0.991, SAM 3.996°, CC 0.992, RMSE 0.005, and ERGAS 2.240. On Pavia Center, it records PSNR 44.83 dB, SSIM 0.984, SAM 3.297°, CC 0.992, RMSE 0.007, and ERGAS 1.693, with 2.39M parameters and 34.18 ms inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGD-Net outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The largest gains appear on Pavia Center, where LGD-Net exceeds the strongest competitor MCIFNet by +3.47 dB PSNR (44.83 vs 41.37) and reduces SAM from 3.903° to 3.297°. On Chikusei, it surpasses DHP-Darn by +2.41 dB PSNR (47.81 vs 45.40) and Lformer by +2.92 dB, while also achieving the lowest RMSE (0.005 vs 0.006/0.007). On Botswana, the margin over DHP-Darn is +1.71 dB PSNR (41.86 vs 40.15) with SAM reduced from 1.906° to 1.695°. In terms of efficiency, LGD-Net (2.39M params, 25.73G FLOPs, 34.18 ms) sits between lightweight CNNs like HyperPNN (0.14M, 2.17 ms) and heavier models like FPFNet (47.34M, 75.19 ms), delivering the best accuracy-to-complexity trade-off among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

LGD-Net departs from the dominant stacked-architecture paradigm in hyperspectral-panchromatic fusion (HyperPNN, FPFNet, LFormer, DHP-DARN), where global cross-modal interaction is implicitly governed by network depth. It repurposes the Neural ODE / continuous-depth modeling idea (previously applied to hyperspectral classification and super-resolution) but applies it only to a compact 'leader' representation rather than all feature nodes, combining this with graph-based inter-scale propagation and gated feature pyramids. The design is conceptually closest to graph-based relational reasoning (GCPNet, graph-in-graph) in its structured message-passing view, but replaces iterative graph updates with a single continuous dynamical evolution of a global state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit decoupling of global cross-modal state evolution from local spatial refinement offers a design principle that could generalize to other multi-modal fusion tasks where controlling interaction strength matters. However, the optimal configuration uses only T=2 RK2 steps, which somewhat undermines the 'continuous dynamics' framing and suggests the mechanism may be better understood as a two-step gated cross-modal update. Open questions include whether this leader-evolution paradigm scales to higher-resolution scenes or more modalities, and whether the large ablation gap for continuous dynamics (PSNR 44.9→32.7) reflects a genuine architectural insight or sensitivity to a specific training regime. The method's moderate parameter count (2.39M) and competitive inference time make it practically relevant, but the evaluation is limited to three standard benchmarks with Wald-protocol simulation.
