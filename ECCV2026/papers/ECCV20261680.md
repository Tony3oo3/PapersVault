## Problem

Thermal-to-visible face translation suffers from geometric discontinuities, semantic attribute mismatches, and identity degradation that single-modal diffusion approaches (e.g., T2V-DDPM, DiffTV) cannot fully resolve. Existing multimodal conditioning frameworks like Uni-ControlNet lack domain-specific identity constraints for cross-spectral synthesis, and conventional fusion protocols fail to handle the geometric misalignment between sparse depth features and low-resolution thermal imagery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261680/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 thermal face image as primary input, augmented by a depth map (generated via Depth Anything) and a structured text description (generated via LLaVA capturing demographics, physical features, facial structure, and expression). It produces a 256×256 visible-spectrum face image through iterative denoising in the frozen Stable Diffusion latent space. Depth and text serve as auxiliary conditions that can be independently dropped at inference (modality dropout), with the model degrading gracefully to thermal-only operation.

## Method

MTVDiff builds on a frozen Stable Diffusion backbone (1.3B params) and trains only 334M parameters in dual ResNet-18 encoders and SFT injection layers. The core DBCAF module extracts multi-scale features (64×64, 32×32, 16×16) from thermal and depth inputs via parallel encoders, then fuses them through bidirectional cross-attention with attention-adaptive channel weighting (softmax over MLP outputs) to resolve geometric misalignment. A Gated Text-to-Visual Feature Alignment layer uses CLIP text embeddings with learnable tanh(γ) gating scalars to selectively incorporate semantic guidance without disrupting layout structure. Multi-scale fusion features are injected into UNet residual blocks via Spatial Feature Transformations (GroupNorm modulation with per-layer scale and bias), and the gating parameters learn a stage-dependent strategy where scale values decrease and bias values increase across diffusion timesteps, enabling coarse-structure generation early and fine-detail refinement late.

## Results

On MCXFace, MTVDiff achieves FID 75.33, LPIPS 0.1128, PSNR 24.05, and SSIM 0.8355, outperforming the best prior method (DiffV2IR) by 5.1% in FID and 19.5% in LPIPS. On SpeakingFaces, MTVDiff reaches FID 14.37 and LPIPS 0.1307, a 48.3% FID reduction over DiffV2IR. For face verification, MTVDiff attains 87.26% Rank-1 accuracy on MCXFace (+8.92 pp over DiffV2IR) and 93.76% on SpeakingFaces, with VR@1% of 80.11% versus DiffTV's 7.54%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTVDiff outperforms all seven baselines on both datasets across every reported metric. Against the strongest single-modal diffusion baseline T2V-DDPM, MTVDiff reduces FID from 120.65 to 75.33 on MCXFace (37.6% improvement) and from 36.33 to 14.37 on SpeakingFaces (60.4%). Versus the closest multimodal competitor DiffV2IR (text + segmentation), MTVDiff achieves 5.1% lower FID and 19.5% lower LPIPS on MCXFace, and 48.3% lower FID on SpeakingFaces. Uni-ControlNet, despite supporting simultaneous thermal/depth/text conditioning, underperforms DiffV2IR on MCXFace (FID 97.01 vs. 79.33) and trails MTVDiff by 22.87 FID points on SpeakingFaces, confirming that generic multi-condition injection without domain-specific identity constraints is insufficient. On face verification, MTVDiff's 93.76% Rank-1 on SpeakingFaces surpasses Uni-ControlNet (86.19%) by 7.57 pp and BBDM (86.59%) by 7.17 pp, while its VR@1% of 80.11% dwarfs DiffTV's 7.54%, indicating substantially better inter-subject discriminability. Even the thermal-only ablation (MTVDiff w/ Thermal) beats DiffTV on SpeakingFaces (FID 20.14 vs. 34.02), showing architectural gains independent of additional modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

MTVDiff extends the thermal-to-visible face translation line initiated by T2V-DDPM and advanced by DiffTV (the first LDM for this task) by introducing multimodal conditioning—depth maps and text descriptions—alongside the thermal input. Architecturally, it builds on Stable Diffusion's frozen backbone with SFT-based injection (similar to StableSR) and draws the DBCAF module from 3D-priors-guided face restoration work, while the gated cross-attention for text integration follows the DiffX layout-conditioning paradigm. The key departure from general multi-condition frameworks like Uni-ControlNet is the incorporation of domain-specific identity-preserving mechanisms (bidirectional thermal-depth cross-attention, stage-aware gating) rather than treating all modalities as interchangeable control signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for surveillance and security applications where nighttime identity verification is needed, and the 48.3% FID reduction and 8.9-point Rank-1 improvement over prior methods represent a meaningful step toward deployable cross-spectral face recognition. However, the framework is constrained to 256×256 resolution, depends on an auxiliary vision-language model (LLaVA) for text generation at inference, and still degrades measurably when depth and text are both absent. Open questions include whether the DBCAF encoder design scales to higher resolutions, whether automatic prompt-quality estimation can replace the structured template, and whether the approach generalizes to more challenging acquisition conditions (sensor noise, thermal drift, varying distances).
