## Problem

In RPL-based exemplar-free continual representation learning, randomly initialized projection layers lack expressivity under severe domain gaps between pre-trained features and target domains. While inflating the RPL dimension to >10k bases theoretically restores separability, it produces highly ill-conditioned feature matrices that destabilize the recursive ridge-regression updates of the linear classifier. No principled mechanism existed to construct a compact yet expressive RPL that simultaneously preserves the numerical stability required by analytic continual updates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images sequentially across T disjoint class-incremental tasks (e.g., 200 or 300 classes split into 5- or 10-class batches) with no access to task IDs or stored exemplars from prior stages. A frozen pretrained backbone (ViT-B/16-IN21K or DINO-v2) extracts features, which pass through the adaptively constructed RPL and a linear head to produce class-label scores. The RPL hidden dimension L* is not fixed a priori but determined by a residual convergence threshold (ε=0.01), typically yielding a compact projection far smaller than the 100k+ dimensions used by prior methods.

## Method

SCL-MGSM replaces random RPL initialization with the MemoryGuard Supervisory Mechanism, a data-guided, block-wise constructive procedure that uses initial-task features to progressively select target-aligned random bases. At each iteration, B_max candidate blocks of s hidden units are sampled from an adaptively rescaled Gaussian distribution N(0, ξ²); a block is accepted only if it satisfies a relaxed target-aligned residual criterion (Theorem 1) guaranteeing residual convergence to zero. Unlike the greedy SCSM criterion, MGSM's relaxed acceptance admits basis combinations that are jointly effective rather than individually optimal, producing near-orthogonal bases and well-conditioned Gram matrices. The resulting compact RPL is frozen during incremental stages, where only the linear classifier is updated via recursive ridge regression on the sufficient statistic (W_β, P_t), avoiding the ill-conditioning and external stabilization mechanisms required by high-dimensional random projections.

## Results

SCL-MGSM achieves the best A_last and A_avg across all eight evaluation settings (4 datasets × 2 incremental protocols) using ViT-B/16-IN21K. On ImageNet-R (Inc-10), it reaches 77.12% A_last and 82.23% A_avg, surpassing the second-best method (AnaCP) by +3.52% in A_last. On ObjectNet (Inc-10), margins over the runner-up reach +1.77% A_last and +2.49% A_avg. On OmniBenchmark (Inc-5), SCL-MGSM attains 79.83% A_last, exceeding the joint linear probe oracle (78.56%) despite operating in a strictly incremental, exemplar-free setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCL-MGSM outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and protocol. Among RPL-based methods, the closest competitor is AnaCP [19], which SCL-MGSM surpasses by +1.19 to +3.52% in A_last depending on the setting (e.g., 77.12 vs. 73.60 on ImageNet-R Inc-10; 66.45 vs. 64.68 on ObjectNet Inc-10). LoRanPAC [25], which relies on extremely high-dimensional random projections (>10k bases) plus a stabilization mechanism, is consistently outperformed by 1.3–3.5% in A_last. RanPAC [16] and G-ACIL [41], the earlier RPL-based baselines, trail SCL-MGSM by 3–10% in A_last. Prototype-based methods (EASE [39], SimpleCIL [38]) and prompt/LoRA-based methods (CODA-Prompt [27], APT [2], SD-LoRA [33]) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 10% on most benchmarks. The kernel-based method KLDA [18] is competitive on OmniBenchmark (75.27 vs. 79.83) but lags by 4–10% on the other three datasets. Notably, SCL-MGSM also surpasses the joint linear probe oracle on OmniBenchmark (79.83 vs. 78.56) while operating in a strictly incremental, exemplar-free regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RPL-based analytic continual learning paradigm (RanPAC, LoRanPAC, G-ACIL, AnaCP), which freezes a pretrained model and recursively updates a linear head via ridge regression over random-projected features. It departs from the standard random initialization of the projection layer by borrowing the progressive unit-selection philosophy of Stochastic Configuration Networks (SCN/SCSM), but replaces SCSM's strict greedy residual criterion with a relaxed, target-aligned acceptance rule (MGSM) that avoids over-specialization to the initial task. The work also builds on First-Session Adaptation (FSA) from RanPAC, but goes one step further by using initial-task data not only to fine-tune the PTM but also to guide the construction of the projection layer itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a practical tension in RPL-based CIL: the 'wider-is-better' heuristic for expressivity directly conflicts with the numerical stability required by recursive analytic updates, and prior work addressed this only by inflating dimensions to >10k and adding external stabilization. MGSM offers a principled, data-guided alternative that adaptively determines RPL dimension and yields well-conditioned feature matrices without external compensations, making the approach more memory-efficient and computationally tractable. Open questions include how well the method generalizes when the initial task is highly dissimilar to subsequent tasks, whether the convergence guarantee holds under distribution shift across stages, and whether the construction overhead becomes prohibitive for very long task sequences or larger backbones.
