## Problem

Action-conditioned robot world models are trained under teacher forcing (predicting from ground-truth history) but deployed autoregressively, where each generated clip becomes the context for the next. This train/test distribution mismatch causes errors to compound rapidly, with manipulated objects losing structural identity and scene configurations decohering within seconds. No amount of standard supervised training can resolve this, because imperfect self-generated history is absent from the training distribution by construction.

## Input / Output

At each autoregressive step the model receives a history buffer of H=6 recent frame latents from three synchronized cameras (two external, one wrist-mounted), the corresponding past end-effector poses, and a sequence of L=5 future EEF pose targets (7-DoF each). It simultaneously generates L=5 future frames across all three views, which are then VAE-encoded and appended to the history buffer to condition the next step. For post-training, the model starts from a single ground-truth observation and rolls out autoregressively, with the reward computed by comparing generated frames against held-out ground-truth frames at the clip level.

## Method

The paper introduces an RL post-training scheme that adapts the contrastive forward-process objective of DiffusionNFT to the x0-prediction parameterization used by Ctrl-World's SVD backbone, showing that the policy-improvement guarantees carry over exactly because the mapping from network output to clean-data prediction is affine. The key structural insight is that the frozen history buffer at any rollout step plays the role of a shared 'prompt': K=16 independent candidate continuations are branched from it, scored with multi-view perceptual rewards (LPIPS, SSIM, PSNR averaged over 3 cameras and L frames), and group-normalized to form relative advantages. A variable prefix length P~Unif{0,…,9} is sampled per training step to expose the model to the full spectrum of error accumulation, from near-clean early-step buffers to heavily degraded late-step ones. The contrastive loss constructs implicit positive and negative x0 predictions by extrapolating along the direction between the current model and a frozen EMA reference, weighted by the normalized reward, avoiding backpropagation through the denoising chain entirely. Only LoRA adapters (rank 64) and the action encoder receive gradient updates; the backbone and VAE remain frozen.

## Results

On the DROID validation split, PersistWorld achieves SSIM 0.86 / PSNR 24.42 dB / LPIPS 0.070 on external cameras and SSIM 0.67 / PSNR 19.39 dB / LPIPS 0.277 on the wrist camera over 14-step (~11 s) autoregressive rollouts. Compared to the Ctrl-World baseline, this corresponds to a 14.0% LPIPS reduction and +1.40 dB PSNR on external views, and a 9.1% SSIM improvement and +1.59 dB PSNR on the wrist camera. In 1-to-1 paired comparisons, the method wins on ~98% of validation samples, and a blind human preference study yields an 80% preference rate (Elo 884.8 vs. 715.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: WPE, IRASim, and Ctrl-World (the base model). Against Ctrl-World, PersistWorld improves external-camera PSNR by 1.40 dB and reduces LPIPS by 14.0%, while on the wrist camera it gains +9.1% SSIM and +1.59 dB PSNR—the largest relative gains, reflecting better capture of fine-grained object contact. Against WPE and IRASim, the margins are substantially wider: external PSNR improves by 4.09 dB and 3.06 dB respectively, with LPIPS reductions of 46.6% and 40.2%. On object-masked and robot-masked pixels (Table 2), gains are even more concentrated (e.g., external LPIPS drops 16.3% on object regions vs. 14.0% full-frame), confirming improvements come from task-relevant dynamics rather than background preservation. The method also generalizes beyond DROID: post-training on Bridge and RT-1 embodiments improves all three metrics (e.g., RT-1 LPIPS 0.1683 → 0.1305). In a policy-improvement experiment, rollouts from PersistWorld yield a 19% higher average task progression (0.667 vs. 0.558) than Ctrl-World rollouts when used to fine-tune a π₀ policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Ctrl-World's action-conditioned x0-prediction video diffusion backbone and adapts DiffusionNFT's contrastive forward-process RL objective (originally derived for velocity-prediction flow-matching models) to the x0-prediction parameterization, showing the policy-improvement guarantees carry over without σ-dependent corrections. It departs from Self-Forcing-style approaches, which require a bidirectional teacher for distillation and are unavailable in the robot WM setting, by instead using a variable-length autoregressive prefix as shared context from which K candidate continuations are branched and contrastively scored. The multi-view perceptual reward design (LPIPS + SSIM + PSNR aggregated at clip level) is specific to the robot manipulation regime and differs from the HPSv3-based rewards used in the contemporaneous camera-pose world model work [36].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Stabilizing autoregressive rollouts is a prerequisite for using video world models as scalable virtual simulators for robot policy evaluation and improvement, and this paper demonstrates that a relatively lightweight RL post-training stage (LoRA + action encoder, 2 days on 8×H200) can substantially close the exposure-bias gap. The reward-agnostic contrastive objective is modular, opening a path toward physics-informed or task-specific rewards beyond visual fidelity. Key open questions include whether the K=16 candidate sampling overhead can be reduced, whether purely visual rewards suffice or explicit physical/geometric constraints are needed for downstream policy learning, and whether the approach integrates cleanly into an end-to-end policy optimization loop rather than remaining a separate post-training step.
