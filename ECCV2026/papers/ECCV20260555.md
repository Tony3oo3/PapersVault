## Problem

Current Video LLMs produce textual answers without verifiable visual grounding, making them black boxes whose errors are nearly impossible to trace. Existing explainability efforts rely on textual rationales or sparse bounding boxes on keyframes, which cannot capture complex video dynamics such as severe occlusions, non-rigid deformations, or identity preservation through crossovers. The paper argues that true evidence-backed video understanding requires dense, pixel-level spatio-temporal tracking as a formal component of the answer, not merely an intermediate scratchpad.

## Input / Output

The model receives a video clip (5–200 s) and a text question, and must produce a triplet: a semantic answer (selected from options), a set of non-overlapping temporal evidence segments (start/end timestamps), and dense tracked segmentation masklets at 6 FPS identifying the key objects or regions supporting the reasoning. Two evaluation variants exist: ST-Evidence-Gen requires the model to synthesize all three components (with a two-step proxy for general LLMs that lack native mask heads, where the LLM outputs referring expressions parsed by a frozen UniPixel-3B segmenter), while ST-Evidence-MCQ reformulates all three subtasks as four-way multiple-choice selections over candidate segments and mask images.

## Method

The paper introduces the E-VQA task, the ST-Evidence benchmark (1,298 MCQ + 2,706 generative samples, human-verified via a three-stage Sample-Annotate-Verify pipeline), and ST-Evidence-Instruct, a 160k-scale instruction-tuning dataset built through two automated pathways: (1) Grounding-to-Semantics, where ViCaS masks and captions are used to synthesize grounded QA pairs via dual-VLM generation with confidence filtering, and (2) Semantics-to-Grounding, where existing QA pairs are augmented with evidence through a three-step decomposition (VLM identifies evidence objects → bounding box prediction with degenerate-box filtering → SAM-3 dense mask propagation with IoU>0.9 deduplication). The baseline model fine-tunes UniPixel (Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B + SAM-2.1-Base+ mask decoder) using LoRA on the visual encoder and LLM with full mask-decoder training, optimizing a joint loss combining next-token prediction (for answer and temporal segments) and mask decoding losses (for spatial evidence), while mixing in existing segmentation and video understanding data to preserve general capabilities.

## Results

On ST-Evidence-Gen, the fine-tuned Ours-7B model achieves 76.0% QA accuracy, 29.1 t-mean for temporal evidence, and 54.1 J&F for spatial evidence, yielding gains of +1.0, +27.2, and +13.8 over the size-matched UniPixel-7B baseline. Among all evaluated models, Gemini-2.5-Pro leads in QA (83.7%) and temporal evidence (49.9 t-mean), but the paper's Ours-7B model achieves the highest spatial evidence score (54.1 J&F), surpassing even Gemini-2.5-Pro (44.0) and Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B (41.9). On ST-Evidence-MCQ, most open-source General Video LLMs perform near the 25% random-guess baseline on spatial evidence selection, while Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B reaches 86.90% S-Evidence accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's fine-tuned models (Ours-3B/7B, built on UniPixel) achieve dramatic gains over their direct UniPixel baselines: +27.2 t-mean and +13.8 J&F for the 7B variant, confirming that the ST-Evidence-Instruct data effectively bridges reasoning and grounding. Against the strongest General Video LLM (Gemini-2.5-Pro), Ours-7B wins decisively on spatial evidence (54.1 vs. 44.0 J&F) but trails on QA (76.0 vs. 83.7) and temporal evidence (29.1 vs. 49.9 t-mean), indicating that proprietary models still lead in holistic reasoning. Compared to the largest open-source model Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B, Ours-7B again wins on spatial evidence (54.1 vs. 41.9 J&F) while losing on QA (76.0 vs. 83.6) and temporal evidence (29.1 vs. 44.5). Against the other grounded Video LLM, Sa2VA-Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Ours-7B dominates on all three subtasks (QA 76.0 vs. 76.8 is a near-tie, but T-Evidence 29.1 vs. 0.0 and S-Evidence 54.1 vs. 26.2 are decisive). On ST-Evidence-MCQ, most open-source General Video LLMs (Video-LLaMA3, LLaVA-OV, InternVL-3.5, Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B) score near the 25% random-guess level on spatial evidence, while Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B reaches 86.90%, highlighting a sharp generational gap in discriminative grounding ability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds architecturally on UniPixel (which couples a VLM with SAM-2.1 for pixel-level video segmentation) but departs conceptually from prior grounded video reasoning in two key ways: it shifts from 'answer grounding' (locating the entity that is the answer) to 'evidence grounding' (locating the visual cues that causally support the answer), and it replaces sparse bounding boxes on keyframes with dense, tracked masklets at 6 FPS. The data construction pipelines bridge two previously disjoint dataset families—semantic video QA (NeXT-QA, STAR, CLEVRER) and grounded video segmentation (ViCaS)—through automated generation, a synthesis strategy not seen in prior work that typically treats reasoning and localization as separate training objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central empirical finding—that high QA accuracy (80%+) coexists with near-random spatial evidence selection in most open-source models, and that scaling within a model family yields only marginal grounding gains—challenges the assumption that improved video comprehension automatically yields improved visual perception. The ST-Evidence benchmark and 160k-scale ST-Evidence-Instruct dataset provide the first infrastructure for evaluating and training evidence-backed video understanding, which is critical for high-stakes applications (autonomous driving, medical analysis) where verifiable visual justification is required. Open questions include whether the QA-grounding gap can be closed through architectural changes (e.g., native mask generation in VLMs) versus data-centric approaches, and whether the automated annotation pipeline's quality (62.8 J&F vs. human-verified ground truth) is sufficient for scaling to more complex reasoning scenarios.
