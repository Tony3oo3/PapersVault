## Problem

Text-guided image-to-video (I2V) models can animate any publicly shared image into a convincing video, amplifying privacy and copyright risks beyond static misuse. Prior I2V protection methods like I2VGuard require generating auxiliary reference videos during optimization, making them computationally expensive, and standard video-quality benchmarks provide limited insight into protection-specific failures. The paper also identifies that text guidance plays a broader stabilizing role in I2V generation—spanning subject preservation, structural coherence, and temporal consistency—than previously recognized, motivating a more targeted disruption strategy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image to be protected and produces a perturbed version with an L∞-bounded adversarial perturbation (ε=8/255), optimized via PGD (K=50 steps, α=1/255). The perturbation is learned by unrolling T=10 denoising steps and operating on intermediate attention statistics rather than generating full videos, with target resolutions of 720×480 (49 frames, 8 FPS) for CogVideoX and 704×480 (121 frames, 25 FPS) for LTX-Video. The protected image is then fed to any I2V model with an arbitrary text prompt, where it induces visible generation failures while remaining visually indistinguishable from the original.

## Method

Anti-Prompt is motivated by the empirical observation that removing text prompts from I2V models causes broad failures in subject preservation, structural coherence, and temporal consistency—not just motion quality. The method optimizes a perturbation that (1) suppresses text-conditioned interactions by penalizing video-to-text attention logits (full-attention) or cross-attention residual energy (cross-attention), (2) amplifies visual-only pathways by increasing video-to-video/image attention logits or self-attention residual energy to shift softmax competition away from text, and (3) applies an encoder attack pushing VAE representations toward an uninformative target (black image). All three losses operate on intermediate attention statistics within a single denoising pass, eliminating the need for expensive reference video generation and reducing peak memory by ~20% compared to I2VGuard. The paper also introduces a Video-LLM-assisted failure-finding evaluation protocol that scores generated videos on four interpretable dimensions (Subject Preservation, Structural Consistency, Dynamic Consistency, Artifact Suppression) using frame-grounded observations before discrete 1–5 scoring.

## Results

On the proposed Video-LLM failure-finding protocol (lower = stronger protection), Anti-Prompt achieves average scores of 3.25/2.99 (seen/unseen prompts) on CogVideoX and 3.12/2.38 on LTX-Video, compared to I2VGuard's 3.92/3.86 and 3.28/2.46 respectively. On standard VBench I2V metrics, Anti-Prompt reduces Subject Consistency to 87.54 on CogVideoX (vs. 93.32 for I2VGuard and 95.06 clean) and to 89.36 on LTX-Video (vs. 90.25 for I2VGuard). In terms of efficiency, Anti-Prompt requires only 26.82 PFLOPs total for CogVideoX-5B optimization versus 54.25 PFLOPs for I2VGuard, a 51% reduction, with peak memory of 48.98 GB vs. 51.56 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is I2VGuard (CVPR 2025), the most closely related prior work on I2V image protection. On the paper's proposed Video-LLM protocol, Anti-Prompt consistently outperforms I2VGuard across all four dimensions on both architectures: on CogVideoX, the average gap is 0.67 (seen) and 0.87 (unseen), with the largest margin on Structural Consistency (3.24 vs. 4.18 seen) and Dynamic Consistency (2.91 vs. 3.81 seen). On LTX-Video, Anti-Prompt leads by 0.16 (seen) and 0.08 (unseen) in average score, with the advantage concentrated in Subject Preservation and Artifact Suppression. On standard VBench metrics, Anti-Prompt clearly wins on CogVideoX (e.g., Subject Consistency 87.54 vs. 93.32) but shows a smaller or mixed advantage on LTX-Video (Subject Consistency 89.36 vs. 90.25). In black-box cross-architecture transfer, Anti-Prompt is generally favorable, with the clearest advantage when optimizing on LTX-Video and generating with CogVideoX. On imperceptibility, Anti-Prompt achieves lower LPIPS (0.196 vs. 0.246 on CogVideoX) and higher PSNR (35.10 vs. 32.86), indicating visually less noticeable perturbations while delivering stronger protection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the I2V image protection task introduced by I2VGuard (CVPR 2025) and the encoder-attack formulation from PhotoGuard (ICML 2023), but departs from both in its core mechanism: rather than degrading spatio-temporal consistency through reference-video generation, Anti-Prompt directly targets the architectural pathways through which text conditioning stabilizes denoising. The key conceptual departure is the empirical observation that text guidance in modern I2V models plays a broader stabilizing role than previously assumed—its removal causes subject deformation, structural collapse, and temporal incoherence, not merely motion degradation. The method also introduces a Video-LLM-assisted failure-finding evaluation protocol, positioning itself methodologically beyond the VBench-only evaluation used by I2VGuard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As I2V models become more accessible and photorealistic, the practical urgency of protecting publicly shared images against unauthorized animation grows, and this paper offers a more efficient (no reference video generation) and more transferable (architecture-level targeting) protection mechanism. The Video-LLM evaluation protocol is a useful methodological contribution for the broader image-protection literature, providing interpretable, frame-grounded diagnoses that standard video-quality benchmarks cannot capture. Open questions include how the approach scales to larger or architecturally different I2V models, whether adaptive attackers can reverse the perturbation, and whether the protection holds under future model updates that may alter the text-guidance dynamics the method exploits.
