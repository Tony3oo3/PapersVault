## Problem

Existing feedforward 3D reconstruction methods (DUSt3R, VGGT, AnySplat) predict per-pixel point maps or pixel-aligned Gaussians that are fundamentally 2.5D and view-anchored, capturing only visible surfaces within observed viewpoints. This view-anchoring causes holes and artifacts in novel views that deviate from input cameras, because the model cannot allocate geometry to occluded or unseen regions. The paper asks whether a fixed set of explicit 3D queries can provide a scene-level representation that decouples geometry from input viewpoints while remaining efficient enough for feedforward inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261824/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N unposed RGB images (2–64 views, variable resolution up to 448²) of the same scene and produces a fixed budget of 4,096 learnable 3D queries, each spawning 64 Gaussians (262K total primitives), along with per-frame camera poses and point maps. All queries, spawned Gaussians, and rendering cameras share a single non-metric scene coordinate system predicted by the backbone, so the output is a scene-level representation independent of any particular input view. The query budget is constant regardless of the number or resolution of input views, unlike per-pixel methods whose primitive count scales with N×H×W.

## Method

UNIQUER introduces learnable 3D queries—each carrying an explicit 3D coordinate and a latent embedding—as the core scene representation, inspired by DETR-style query architectures. A frozen Pi3 backbone (DINOv2 + alternating-attention transformer, 891M params) extracts per-frame image tokens and predicts camera poses and point maps; a 12-layer Query Transformer (502M trainable params) then refines the queries via a decoupled attention design: cross-attention from queries to image tokens followed by query-only self-attention, reducing complexity from O((Q+NT)²) to O(QNT+Q²). Queries are hybrid-initialized (half sampled from predicted point maps for geometric grounding, half uniformly random) and each spawns K=64 Gaussians via learned local offsets around a deformed query center. Training supervises the spawned Gaussians by differentiable splatting to a superset of views (inputs plus held-out novel poses) with RGB (L1 + LPIPS), scale-invariant depth, and camera losses, encouraging the model to fill unobserved regions rather than merely reproducing input-view surfaces.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360 sparse-view NVS, UNIQUER achieves 22.70 PSNR (3 views) and 21.80 PSNR (6 views), outperforming AnySplat (20.08 / 18.29) and NoPoSplat (18.21 / 16.06). On VR-NeRF, UNIQUER reaches 21.99 PSNR (3 views) and 20.87 PSNR (6 views), leading NoPoSplat and AnySplat on PSNR. On the 150-scene RealEstate10K two-view benchmark, UNIQUER scores 20.50 PSNR / 0.672 SSIM / 0.196 LPIPS, surpassing AnySplat (18.93 / 0.615 / 0.226) and FLARE (17.74 / 0.586 / 0.371). In terms of efficiency, UNIQUER uses 14.7× fewer Gaussians (262K vs 3.85M), 39% less GPU memory, and 2.35× less inference time than AnySplat, while also reducing rendered-depth abs-rel error from 0.062 to 0.038.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NoPoSplat, UNIQUER wins decisively on PSNR across all sparse-view settings (e.g., +4.49 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 3-view, +2.32 dB on VR-NeRF 3-view) and is substantially faster (0.213 s vs 0.416 s). Against AnySplat, UNIQUER leads on PSNR for Mip-NeRF 360 (22.70 vs 20.08 at 3 views; 21.80 vs 18.29 at 6 views) and VR-NeRF (21.99 vs 19.67 at 3 views; 20.87 vs 17.25 at 6 views), though AnySplat retains a slight edge on SSIM and LPIPS for VR-NeRF 3-view (0.745 vs 0.708 SSIM; 0.313 vs 0.446 LPIPS). On RealEstate10K, UNIQUER outperforms both AnySplat and FLARE on all three metrics. For camera pose estimation, UNIQUER is comparable to Pi3 (99.99 RRA@30 on RealEstate10K) with a small AUC gap (83.69 vs 85.90), attributed to Pi3's use of additional private training data. In the dense-view (32/64 view) feedforward setting, UNIQUER trails AnySplat slightly on PSNR (21.51 vs 22.32 on Mip-NeRF 360 32-view), but when used as initialization for per-scene 3DGS optimization, UNIQUER's Gaussians yield the best results (25.14 PSNR at 32 views, 26.00 at 64 views), surpassing 3DGS+AnySplat (24.99 / 23.71) and 3DGS+VGGT (22.61 / 23.78).

## Relation to Prior Work

UNIQUER sits at the intersection of two research threads: feedforward 3D reconstruction (DUSt3R, VGGT, Pi3, AnySplat, NoPoSplat) and query-based detection (DETR, DETR3D, PETR). It departs from the dominant pixel-aligned paradigm—where Gaussians or point maps are generated per input pixel and thus remain view-anchored—by introducing learnable 3D queries in a shared scene coordinate system, each spawning a local Gaussian cluster. The frozen backbone (DINOv2 + alternating-attention transformer + point-map head) is inherited directly from Pi3, while the novel query transformer with decoupled cross-attention and the Gaussian spawning module are new. Conceptually, the paper transplants the DETR-style 'sparse queries attend to dense features' mechanism from object detection into dense scene reconstruction, with Gaussian splatting serving as the differentiable rendering bridge for 2D supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical contribution is a 14.7× reduction in Gaussian primitives (262K vs. 3.85M) with simultaneously better depth accuracy and lower memory, making feedforward scene reconstruction substantially more efficient for robotics and embodied AI applications. The novel-view supervision strategy—rasterizing to held-out poses beyond the inputs—elegantly forces the model to fill occluded geometry without any 3D ground truth. Key limitations remain: the fixed query budget (4096) may underrepresent complex scenes, the method assumes static scenes, and it depends on a 1.4B-parameter frozen backbone. Open questions include adaptive query allocation, extension to dynamic scenes, and whether lighter backbones can preserve the geometric priors that the hybrid initialization relies on.
