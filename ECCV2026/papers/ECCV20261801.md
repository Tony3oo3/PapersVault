## Problem

Conversational talking face generation requires simultaneously supporting an arbitrary number of participants in multi-round dialogues (flexibility), producing coherent non-verbal feedback and smooth role transitions (naturalness), and running in real time for long-term interactive applications (efficiency). Existing methods either model speaker and listener separately with manual role switching, rely on computationally heavy video diffusion backbones, or are confined to single-character scenes with limited partner-feedback modeling, leaving all three requirements unmet.

## Input / Output

The system consumes one audio track per participant (16 kHz), a reference identity image per participant, and motion feedback (disentangled lip, eye, and head-pose signals) from all other participants within a ~3-second context window. It produces 512×512 per-participant frames that are spatially stitched into a cohesive multi-person scene video at 30 FPS, with the generated motion explicitly decomposed into lip, eye, and head-pose channels for targeted refinement. The framework also supports a video-dubbing mode where existing video frames serve as the driving keypoint source, preserving original body motion and background.

## Method

InterTalk comprises three stages: a Responsive Context Encoder (RCE) that encodes each participant's audio via Wav2Vec 2.0 and disentangled motion (lip/eye/pose) via separate encoders, fuses them with cross-attention and a bi-directional LSTM, then aggregates across all participants through adaptive attention pooling; an Interactive Motion Generator (IMG) built from a Transformer encoder, a causal-masked temporal alignment module adapted from FaceFormer, a Transformer decoder, and a feed-forward layer projecting into per-component subspaces, followed by three lightweight refinement modules (a Sync-Enhancer trained on large-scale single-speaker data, a Blink-Enhancer, and a Pose-Initializer); and a keypoint-based rendering pipeline initialized from LivePortrait that warps identity embeddings and stitches multi-participant outputs. A key differentiator is the Iterative Generation Strategy, which runs the IMG for multiple cycles, feeding each participant's previous-iteration motion back into the RCE so that all characters progressively incorporate mutual feedback without incurring real-time latency. Training uses L2 and velocity losses on motion plus a SyncNet synchronization loss for the enhancer, and a 3D FLAME-to-keypoint data augmentation pipeline converts DualTalk's 3D coefficients into implicit keypoint supervision to enrich the limited multi-person dataset.

## Results

On the HDTF single-speaker benchmark, InterTalk achieves the best FID (23.07), NIQE (13.29), FVD (178.73), and Sync-C (8.30) among all compared methods. On the ViCo interactive head generation benchmark, it attains the lowest FD-exp (18.33), MSE-exp (0.48), and highest SID-exp (4.85). Most critically, on the multi-person InterTalk dataset, it achieves 31.23 FPS (real-time) versus MultiTalk's 0.61 FPS, while also improving FD from 41.74 to 22.67 and RPCC from 0.24 to 0.09. In the user study, InterTalk scores highest on all four perceptual dimensions (lip-sync 4.03, interaction fluency 3.96, motion naturalness 4.55, visual realism 4.12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-speaker baselines on HDTF, InterTalk outperforms SadTalker, EchoMimic, Hallo2, and Sonic on FID, NIQE, and FVD, with the sync-enhancer giving a slight Sync-C edge over Sonic (8.30 vs 8.26). For interactive head generation on ViCo, InterTalk beats DIM, INFP, ARIG, and DualTalk on FD-exp, MSE-exp, and SID-exp, though ARIG matches it on RPCC-pose (0.01) and DualTalk edges it on FD-pose (0.04 vs 0.04 tied). In the user study, InterTalk surpasses all three interactive baselines (DIM, INFP, ARIG) on every perceptual metric, with the largest margin over DIM (e.g., motion naturalness 4.55 vs 1.19). For multi-person conversation, InterTalk dominates MultiTalk on Sync-C (8.30 vs 7.94), FD (22.67 vs 41.74), RPCC (0.09 vs 0.24), and FPS (31.23 vs 0.61), but trails on SID (4.28 vs 5.14), which the authors attribute to the stronger diversity of large video-generation backbones. Overall, InterTalk wins or ties on the majority of metrics across all three evaluation settings, with the most dramatic advantage being the 51× speedup over MultiTalk enabling real-time deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

InterTalk sits at the intersection of two lines of work: motion-based talking face generation (building on LivePortrait's keypoint rendering pipeline and FaceFormer's temporal attention) and conversational interaction modeling (extending INFP, ARIG, and DIM from dyadic/single-person settings). It departs from the video-diffusion-based multi-person approaches (MultiTalk, Veo3, Sora2) by adopting an explicit motion-generation architecture that trades some visual diversity for 50× real-time speed, and from prior interaction methods by supporting an arbitrary number of participants through an iterative feedback loop rather than fixed speaker/listener roles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30 FPS multi-person conversational generation is practically significant for deployment in virtual companions, live-streaming, and remote meetings where video-diffusion models are infeasible. However, the dataset is small (143 identities, 205 clips), the SID metric trails MultiTalk (suggesting less motion diversity), and the 2D keypoint-based rendering limits 3D consistency and occlusion handling. Open questions include whether the iterative strategy scales gracefully beyond 4–5 participants, how well the method handles emotional or adversarial conversational dynamics, and whether the 3D FLAME-based augmentation can be replaced by larger-scale 3D conversational corpora as they become available.
