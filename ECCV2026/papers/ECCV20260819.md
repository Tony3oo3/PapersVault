## Problem

Existing joint diffusion methods for panorama generation (e.g., MultiDiffusion) process overlapping local views independently and fuse predictions only post-hoc, confining self- and cross-attention within each view. This prevents the model from forming a holistic, layout-aware representation of all objects and their spatial relationships, leading to fragmented, duplicated, or inconsistent entities across view boundaries. Fine-grained, instance-level layout control via bounding boxes on wide-aspect-ratio canvases remains largely unexplored, and retraining layout-to-image models for panoramic scales is infeasible due to memory costs and data scarcity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a global scene prompt P, a set of N bounding boxes B defined in the full panoramic canvas coordinate space, and N per-object text descriptions D, and produces a single wide-aspect-ratio panoramic image (evaluated at 1:2, 1:3, and 1:4 aspect ratios). The panoramic canvas is internally decomposed into overlapping square views of standard resolution for memory-efficient processing, but all layout constraints and attention operations are formulated over the unified global canvas rather than individual crops. The pipeline is entirely training-free, operating at inference time on a pre-trained SDXL + IFAdapter backbone with 30 denoising steps.

## Method

InstaPano replaces the conventional independent-view attention in MultiDiffusion with a Sync-Fuse-Dispatch (SFD) workflow executed at each U-Net attention layer during denoising. Sync aggregates latent features from all overlapping views into a single global canvas (averaging in overlap regions); Fuse applies multi-level attention on this global context—first a Global Self-Attention for long-range structural coherence, then a Combined Cross-Attention that jointly computes global-text cross-attention (for semantic/stylistic guidance) and layout-guided cross-attention (using Fourier-encoded bounding-box position embeddings with hard spatial masks for precise instance placement); Dispatch splits the enriched global latent back to each local view. A Conditional Position Mask (CPM) modulates the layout-guided cross-attention output with a center-focused Gaussian decay for single-object boxes (suppressing peripheral duplication) while keeping uniform weighting for multi-object boxes. The key distinction from prior work is that all instance boxes and prompts interact within a shared global attention context rather than being assigned to separate views and merged only after independent denoising, enabling true holistic layout-aware generation in a zero-shot manner.

## Results

On Pano-Layout-Bench (1,341 layout-prompt pairs across 1:2, 1:3, and 1:4 aspect ratios), InstaPano achieves the best layout fidelity among joint-diffusion baselines with 0.63 mIoU, 0.25 AP, and 0.44 AP50, outperforming MAD by +0.06 mIoU and +0.09 AP50. It also leads in text-image consistency (30.59 CLIP, 26.74 Local CLIP) and stylistic coherence (0.5665 Intra-LPIPS, the lowest). With a holistic prompt (InstaPano*), layout fidelity further improves to 0.71 mIoU and 0.49 AR, while CLIP rises to 32.37 and aesthetic score to 6.12.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three joint-diffusion baselines, InstaPano wins on every layout-fidelity and consistency metric: it surpasses MultiDiffusion by +0.06 mIoU and +0.15 AP50, beats SyncDiffusion by +0.11 mIoU and +0.19 AP50, and edges out MAD by +0.06 mIoU and +0.09 AP50, while also achieving the lowest Intra-LPIPS (0.5665 vs. 0.6007 for MAD). The only metric where InstaPano trails is aesthetic score (5.81 vs. 6.07 for SyncDiffusion), a small gap the authors attribute to the trade-off between layout precision and visual appeal. In the direct-generation comparison (Table 2), InstaPano* outperforms IFAdapter on mIoU (0.71 vs. 0.69), AR (0.49 vs. 0.48), CLIP (32.37 vs. 31.53), and aesthetic score (6.12 vs. 5.97), though IFAdapter retains a slight edge on AP (0.26 vs. 0.25) and AP50 (0.47 vs. 0.45) due to a precision-recall trade-off. Both InstanceDiffusion (0.46 mIoU) and CreatiLayout (0.34 mIoU) suffer severe layout collapse under panoramic inference, confirming the out-of-distribution problem that joint diffusion addresses. A controlled ablation (Table 3) further shows that replacing InstaPano's holistic attention fusion with MAD's post-hoc sample fusion on the same IFAdapter backbone drops mIoU from 0.71 to 0.56, validating that the improvement stems from the SFD mechanism rather than the backbone choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

InstaPano sits at the intersection of two established lines: joint diffusion for panorama generation (MultiDiffusion, SyncDiffusion, MAD) and layout-to-image (L2I) models (GLIGEN, InstanceDiffusion, IFAdapter). It departs from the dominant post-hoc sample fusion paradigm—where each view is denoised independently and outputs are merged—by instead performing holistic layout-aware attention on a shared global canvas, leveraging the L2I model's pre-trained masked cross-attention for spatial grounding. The Conditional Position Mask (CPM) is a targeted addition addressing object duplication in large bounding boxes, a failure mode not previously characterized in the joint diffusion literature. Conceptually, the paper reframes panorama generation from 'stitching independent crops' to 'jointly planning a global scene with instance-level constraints,' a shift that MAD's attention fusion hinted at but did not exploit for layout control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free method to achieve instance-level bounding-box control on wide-aspect-ratio panoramas, removing the need for panoramic layout datasets or retraining that are prohibitively expensive. The Pano-Layout-Bench (1,341 samples across three aspect ratios) provides a much-needed evaluation standard for this task. Limitations include a precision-recall trade-off (slightly lower AP/AP50 than IFAdapter due to semantically consistent instances outside annotated boxes), reliance on an LLM-based agent to classify single- vs. multi-object prompts for CPM, and the observation that holistic prompts substantially outperform background-only prompts, suggesting the method's full potential is not yet unlocked under the standard evaluation protocol. Open questions include whether the SFD workflow generalizes to 360° spherical panoramas or to even wider canvases (beyond 1:4), and whether the Gaussian CPM can be replaced by a learned spatial prior.
