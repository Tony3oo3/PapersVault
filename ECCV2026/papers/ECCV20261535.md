## Problem

Current expressive avatar systems depend on visual cues (cameras, motion tracking) and fail when faces are occluded by head-mounted displays or when emotions remain internally experienced. While EEG has been used for coarse emotion classification, no prior work has decoded non-invasive EEG signals into continuous, high-fidelity 3D facial geometry, leaving a critical gap between neural states and visual expression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261535/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 16-channel EEG signals sampled at 125 Hz, segmented into overlapping windows of 375 timesteps (reshaped to a 1×16×375 tensor) and produces a dense 256×256×3 position map in UV space where each pixel encodes (x, y, z) surface coordinates. The position map is resampled into a topology-consistent mesh of ~65k vertices, which drives a per-triangle 3D Gaussian Splatting renderer to produce view-consistent photorealistic avatar images. The system is subject-specific: each participant requires a dedicated trained model, and the position map is aligned to a canonical frame via Procrustes to isolate non-rigid expression from head pose.

## Method

The encoder follows an EEG-Conformer-style architecture: two sequential convolutions (temporal with kernel 40, then spatial across all 16 channels) reduce the EEG window to ~19 feature patches, which are processed by six Transformer encoder layers (10 heads, 4× FFN expansion) to yield a compact latent embedding. A randomly initialized VAE decoder projects this embedding to a 4×8×8 latent map and upsamples via two transposed convolutions to the full 256×256×3 position map, trained with masked MSE plus Laplacian smoothing loss. For rendering, the paper modifies GaussianAvatars by replacing the FLAME blendshape parameterization with the dense position map: one anisotropic Gaussian is bound per mesh triangle, and per-face rigid transforms (rotation, translation, scale) are updated from the predicted geometry at each frame. The key design choice is using a high-degree-of-freedom position map (~65k vertices) rather than 52-dim FLAME blendshapes, which the ablation shows preserves mid-frequency details like wrinkles and avoids the 'averaged-out' predictions that the low-dimensional basis imposes on noisy EEG signals.

## Results

The paper reports point-to-surface distance (in mm) between predicted and ground-truth facial geometry across six emotion trials for two subjects. The position map-driven avatar achieves mean errors of 0.31–1.02 mm (Subject 2: 0.31–0.71 mm) and places 62–97% of vertices within 1 mm of the surface, consistently outperforming the blendshape-driven baseline (mean 0.33–1.77 mm; 42–95% within 1 mm) on all trials except Neutral. On the held-out EMOSTIM cross-trial evaluation, the position map avatar reaches 0.52 mm mean error (Subject 1) and 0.71 mm (Subject 2), versus 0.68 mm and 1.51 mm for blendshapes, confirming generalization to unseen stimulus content.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a pioneering work, Mind-to-Face has no direct external baseline; the paper explicitly states 'there is no direct baseline to compare against.' The primary quantitative comparison is internal: position map-driven avatars versus blendshape-driven (FLAME 52-coefficient) avatars. The position map representation wins on 5 of 6 trials for both subjects in mean point-to-surface distance, with the largest gap on Disgust (1.02 mm vs. 1.77 mm for Subject 1) and Angry (0.50 mm vs. 1.08 mm for Subject 1); the sole exception is Neutral, where both representations perform comparably (~0.32–0.40 mm). The <1 mm vertex accuracy gap is even more pronounced, with position maps exceeding blendshapes by 20–40 percentage points on expressive trials. Qualitatively, the paper also compares its face-tracking pipeline against the VHAP-based tracking used in GaussianAvatars [28], demonstrating that the standard GaussianAvatars pipeline produces incorrect expressions or completely collapsed geometry under the headcap occlusion and extreme expressions present in their capture setup, while the proposed photogrammetry-based tracking remains robust.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: EEG-based affective computing (which has historically produced only discrete emotion labels via classifiers like EEGNet or EEG-Conformer), neural avatar synthesis (GaussianAvatars, NeRF-based head models), and 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering. It departs from prior EEG-to-expression work by replacing discrete label prediction with continuous dense 3D position map regression, and from prior avatar pipelines by replacing FLAME's low-dimensional blendshape parameters with a 256×256×3 position map that preserves mid-frequency geometric detail. The CNN-Transformer encoder follows EEG-Conformer's architecture, while the rendering pipeline is a modified GaussianAvatars system with a custom face-tracking and skinning stage to handle occlusion from the EEG headcap and extreme expressions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a proof-of-concept that 16-channel non-invasive EEG contains sufficient signal to drive subject-specific, photorealistic facial animation — a capability that could enable expressive telepresence when faces are occluded by HMDs or when emotions are internally experienced. However, the evidence is explicitly exploratory: only two subjects are evaluated, the model is subject-specific with no cross-subject generalization, and there is no direct quantitative baseline for the EEG decoding step (the authors note no comparable system exists). The released synchronized EEG-to-3D dataset is a genuine first and will likely support follow-up work on cross-subject generalization, real-time decoding, and stimulus-conditioned disentanglement of perceptual vs. affective signals.
