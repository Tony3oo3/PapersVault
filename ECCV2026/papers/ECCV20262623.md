## Problem

Character swapping requires transferring a full identity (face and clothing) onto a target image while strictly preserving pose, background, and geometric structure, yet existing general image editing models exhibit a fundamental trade-off: pursuing identity similarity leads to over-editing and background corruption, while preserving structure yields insufficient identity transfer. The root cause is that standard DPO-based preference optimization relies on global MSE, which is dominated by large-area background differences and dilutes the crucial identity gradients concentrated on the face and foreground.

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB images as input: a reference identity image (I_ref) providing the target face and appearance, and a source scene image (I_src) providing background, illumination, composition, and body pose. The output is a single composite image at up to 1024×1024 resolution where the reference identity is seamlessly transferred onto the source scene, replacing both facial features and clothing while maintaining the original pose, occlusions, and global layout. Unlike face swapping, the task demands holistic identity transfer including clothing contours and complex occlusions, not just localized facial region replacement.

## Method

SIPA is a two-stage training framework (SFT then DPO) built on QwenImage-Edit 2509 with LoRA, operating within the Flow Matching velocity-field formulation. The key spatial contribution, Region-Weighted Preference (RWP), replaces the global MSE in the DPO denoising error with a mask-weighted variant that amplifies gradients in identity-critical regions (face, body) by a factor (1 + λ_local·M), suppressing background interference and reducing reliance on perfectly aligned preference pairs. The key temporal contribution, Time-Gated Identity Preference (TIP), performs a one-step denoising reconstruction at each diffusion step and injects an ArcFace-based identity similarity penalty only when t ≤ τ (low-noise window), ensuring perceptual identity supervision is applied only when facial structure is sufficiently formed. The two mechanisms are unified into a single log-sigmoid DPO objective where the win/lose margins combine the RWP velocity-field difference and the gated TIP identity advantage, optimized with β=10, λ_local=2, λ_tip=3, and τ=0.1.

## Results

On the 321-sample character swapping validation set, SIPA achieves the best overall image quality with SSIM 0.8957, PSNR 19.54, and FID 51.80, outperforming all four baselines. It also leads on all foreground/background preservation metrics (FG-CLIP 0.8788, BG-CLIP 0.7328, FG-SSIM 0.8636, BG-SSIM 0.9277) and reduces pose alignment error to 100.09, a >50% improvement over Qwen-Image-Edit (220.11). The only metric where SIPA trails is absolute ID-Sim (0.5975 vs. 0.7044 for NanoBanana Pro), though the paper attributes the higher ID-Sim of baselines to copy-and-paste artifacts that destroy pose fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIPA outperforms all four baselines on every metric except absolute ID-Sim. Against Flux v2, SIPA improves FID by 42% (51.80 vs. 89.41), reduces PoseErr by 76% (100.09 vs. 416.26), and gains substantial margins on all CLIP/SSIM preservation metrics. Against Qwen-Image-Edit 2509, SIPA cuts PoseErr by 55% (100.09 vs. 220.12) and improves FID by 40% (51.80 vs. 86.79); the paper notes that Qwen's reported ID-Sim of 0.7143 drops to 0.4573 after removing copy-and-paste samples, making SIPA's 0.5975 the more reliable identity score. Against GPT-Image 1.5, SIPA achieves a 21% lower FID (51.80 vs. 65.34) and 39% lower PoseErr (100.09 vs. 163.08), while GPT-Image retains a slight ID-Sim edge (0.6133 vs. 0.5975). Against NanoBanana Pro, SIPA wins on FID (51.80 vs. 78.69), all four preservation metrics, and PoseErr (100.09 vs. 181.99), but NanoBanana Pro leads on ID-Sim (0.7044 vs. 0.5975), which the authors attribute to global repainting that sacrifices structural fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO-for-diffusion line (Wallace et al. 2024; Yang et al. 2024) by decomposing the preference signal along two axes that prior work treats globally: a spatial mask that reweights the velocity-matching loss toward identity-critical regions (RWP), and a temporal gate that injects ArcFace identity supervision only at low-noise steps (TIP). It builds on Flow Matching as the generative backbone and QwenImage-Edit as the base model, but departs from face-swapping methods (SimSwap, FSGAN) by targeting full-body identity transfer under strict pose and background preservation constraints. The two-stage SFT-then-DPO training paradigm with Unreal Engine synthetic data followed by real-world preference quadruplets is a practical contribution distinct from prior identity-conditioned generation work (DreamBooth, IP-Adapter, InstantID), which focuses on synthesis rather than constrained editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spatial-temporal decomposition of preference gradients offers a generalizable recipe for any constrained diffusion editing task where the desired change is spatially localized and the preservation constraint dominates the remaining pixels. However, the method's ID-Sim (0.5975) remains below NanoBanana Pro (0.7044) and GPT-Image 1.5 (0.6133), and the paper acknowledges failure under severe occlusion, extreme pose discrepancy, and dramatic illumination shifts. Open questions include whether the RWP/TIP decomposition transfers to video character swapping, whether the reliance on curated preference quadruplets can be relaxed with synthetic rejection sampling, and whether explicit 3D facial priors (mentioned as future work) could close the remaining identity gap without sacrificing pose stability.
