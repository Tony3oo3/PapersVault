## Problem

Existing multimodal visual recognition methods handle missing modalities primarily through feature reconstruction, which suffers from distribution inconsistency between synthesized and real features due to non-deterministic inter-modality mapping and inherent modality distribution gaps. Prior prompt-based approaches for missing modalities lack a missing-type-aware mechanism to control information transfer across layers, leading to interference between prompts of different missing scenarios. This paper tackles both gaps by moving the completion problem from the feature space to the prompt space and introducing a global hierarchical control signal conditioned on the missing pattern.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 224×224, tokenized into 16×16 ViT patches) and a text sequence (up to 77 CLIP tokens), along with a discrete missing-type indicator specifying which modality is absent. Missing modalities are represented by zero-filled feature tensors rather than learned dummies. Prompts of length 36 are prepended to features at 6 selected transformer layers of a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone. The output is a class label: multi-label genre scores (23 genres) for MM-IMDb, 101-way food classification for UPMC Food-101, and a binary hateful/benign score for Hateful Memes.

## Method

The framework introduces two complementary prompts injected at each of 6 transformer layers. HyperPrompt uses a shared hypernetwork conditioned on a missing-type embedding and a layer-id embedding to generate per-layer, modality-specific mapper weights; these mappers transform concatenated previous-layer prompts into current-layer prompts, providing global, missing-type-aware control over cross-layer information flow. AlignPrompt factorizes each instance's prompt into a modality-shared low-rank factor B and modality-specific factors C^(m)(x) derived from self-attention over shallow features; when a modality is missing, a lightweight completion head predicts the absent factor from the visible side, and training maximizes the Barber-Agakov lower bound on mutual information between the predicted and true factors to ensure semantic consistency. The overall loss combines the MI-based completion objective, prompt reconstruction and moment-matching terms, and a CLIP-style contrastive loss, with mask training that simulates one modality as missing on complete samples. Only 5.6M parameters (3.8% of the frozen 149M CLIP backbone) are trained, yielding state-of-the-art results across three benchmarks while remaining parameter-efficient.

## Results

On the default 70% missing-rate setting, BIP achieves average F1-Macro improvements of 3.08, 1.37, and 3.35 points over the strongest baseline DCP on MM-IMDb, UPMC Food-101, and Hateful Memes, respectively. On Hateful Memes (AUROC), gains are particularly large—reaching over 10 points in the image-missing scenario at 70% rate (e.g., 69.56 vs. DCP's 62.82). Under parameter-matched conditions (7.9M trainable params), BIP scores 54.32% F1-Macro on MM-IMDb versus 51.37% for DCP and 47.21% for MMP, confirming the gains stem from architecture rather than parameter count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIP outperforms all five prompt-based baselines (CoOp, MMP, MaPLe, DePT, DCP) across every dataset and missing-rate configuration. The strongest baseline, DCP (NeurIPS 2024), is surpassed by an average of 3.08 F1 points on MM-IMDb, 1.37 Acc on Food-101, and 3.35 AUROC on Hateful Memes; the gap is widest on Hateful Memes where image-missing scenarios yield gains exceeding 6 points (e.g., 69.56 vs. 62.82 at 70% rate). Against feature-reconstruction methods (Table 4, 50% image-missing on MM-IMDb), BIP's AlignPrompt alone reaches 55.89 F1-Macro versus Knowledge Bridger's 51.95, MMIN's 49.56, and DiCMoR's 43.71, and the full model reaches 57.39. In the parameter-matched comparison (Table 6), BIP leads at every budget from 2.8M to 7.9M trainable parameters, with a 2.95-point margin over DCP at 7.9M (54.32 vs. 51.37), confirming the advantage is architectural rather than parametric. On the tri-modal CMU-MOSI benchmark, BIP extends naturally and achieves 72.96 ACC / 73.70 F1 (double-missing) and 80.41 ACC / 80.66 F1 (single-missing), beating both MMP and DCP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-learning line for missing modalities initiated by MMP [20] and advanced by DCP [11], which introduced missing-aware and inter-layer-correlated prompts respectively. It departs from the dominant feature-reconstruction paradigm (MMIN, DiCMoR, MMPred) by arguing that distribution inconsistency is inherent to cross-modal feature prediction, and instead operates in prompt space: AlignPrompt completes modality-specific factors via a Barber-Agakov mutual-information lower bound [2], while HyperPrompt uses a shared hypernetwork [8] conditioned on the missing-type embedding to generate per-layer transfer mappers. The key conceptual departure is treating semantic representation as modality-invariant information to be completed at the prompt level rather than reconstructing raw features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from feature reconstruction to prompt-level semantic completion offers a principled way to sidestep the non-deterministic inter-modality mapping problem that plagues reconstruction-based methods, and the dual-prompt design (instance-level completion + global hypernetwork control) provides a clean separation of concerns. The ablation revealing that structural robustness (HyperPrompt) increasingly dominates over semantic completion (AlignPrompt) at high missing rates is a practically useful insight for deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond CLIP-based backbones, whether the MI completion loss is strictly necessary versus simpler regression objectives, and how the method scales to more than three modalities or to generative tasks.
