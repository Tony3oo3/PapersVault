## Problem

Depth foundation models pretrained on perspective imagery (e.g., Depth Anything) degrade markedly on 360° panoramas due to severe geometric distortion, yet existing remedies either require large-scale panoramic fine-tuning data or introduce multi-view fusion overhead. The authors further identify that naively fusing complementary projection features (ERP and cubemap) into a single representation actually perturbs pretrained feature statistics and harms performance, leaving an open question of how to adapt perspective priors to the panoramic domain without overwriting them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260780/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 360° equirectangular-projection (ERP) RGB panorama as input, trained at 504×1008 resolution with predictions upsampled to 512×1024 for evaluation. It outputs a corresponding monocular depth map in ERP format. Notably, the frozen DAM v2 ViT-L backbone is never directly modified; only lightweight SCAdaLN-Zero modules inserted into odd-numbered transformer layers are trained, requiring roughly 1% of the panoramic training pairs used by prior fine-tuning methods (1K vs. 120K).

## Method

RePer-360 reframes panoramic adaptation as guidance-based normalization modulation rather than feature fusion. A Geometry-Aligned Guidance (GAG) module first projects ERP features into the cubemap domain, performs parameter-free channel-wise statistical alignment between CP and ERP features via instance normalization, and then applies a learned spatial gate to produce a geometry-aware 2D guidance signal. This signal is fed into a Self-Conditioned AdaLN-Zero (SCAdaLN-Zero) module that generates pixel-wise scale, shift, and gate parameters for the attention and MLP paths of the frozen backbone, with zero-initialization ensuring the module degenerates to a standard Transformer at the start of training. A cubemap-domain E2C Consistency Loss (ECCLoss) using SSI-MAE is added to the standard SILog and gradient losses to counteract the polar-region pixel imbalance inherent in ERP supervision. The key design choice is that cross-projection information enters only through normalization parameters, preserving the pretrained perspective feature content while enabling distortion-aware refinement.

## Results

On Matterport3D, RePer-360 achieves Abs Rel 0.0691, RMSE 0.3164, and δ1 95.67%, outperforming the previous best PanDA-L (Abs Rel 0.0788, RMSE 0.3827) by 12.3% and 17.3% respectively, while using only 8K in-domain training samples versus PanDA-L*'s 120K pretraining samples. On Stanford2D3D, the gains are even larger: Abs Rel 0.0580 and RMSE 0.2474, representing 34.2% and 22.3% relative improvements over PanDA-L. In the zero-shot setting (trained only on Structured3D + Deep360), RePer-360 improves Abs Rel by 42.3% and RMSE by 14.0% over PanDA-L on Stanford2D3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RePer-360 outperforms all 12 baselines on both Matterport3D and Stanford2D3D across every reported metric. Against the strongest baseline PanDA-L (non-pretrained, in-domain only), it improves Abs Rel by 12.3% (Matterport3D) and 34.2% (Stanford2D3D), and RMSE by 17.3% and 22.3% respectively. Notably, it also surpasses PanDA-L*—which was first semi-supervised pretrained on 120K panoramic images from four datasets—using only 8K (Matterport3D) or 1K (Stanford2D3D) in-domain samples, a ~1% data ratio. Among earlier fusion-based methods (BiFuse, UniFuse, HRDFuse, Elite360D), RePer-360 achieves the lowest RMSE by a wide margin (e.g., 0.3164 vs. 0.4433 for HRDFuse on Matterport3D). In the zero-shot setting (trained only on synthetic Structured3D + Deep360), RePer-360 beats PanDA-L by 42.3% in Abs Rel and 14.0% in RMSE on Stanford2D3D, and matches it on Matterport3D (0.1033 vs. 0.1036 Abs Rel), confirming strong cross-domain generalization without any real panoramic supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the frozen Depth Anything v2 (DAM v2) ViT backbone and adapts the AdaLN-Zero conditioning mechanism from DiT, repurposing it from external 1D signals (timesteps, class labels) to internal geometry-derived 2D guidance. It departs from the two dominant paradigms in panoramic depth estimation—projection-based inference-and-fusion (MoGe-2, ST²360D) and direct fine-tuning with LoRA (PanDA)—by reframing complementary-projection information as a modulation signal for normalization layers rather than a feature to be fused. The authors explicitly report that their initial feature-fusion attempt yielded only marginal gains, motivating the shift to the current self-conditioned modulation design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is extreme data efficiency: matching or exceeding PanDA-L with roughly 1% of its training pairs (1K–8K vs. 120K), which lowers the barrier to adapting depth foundation models to new geometric domains. Conceptually, the finding that explicit cross-projection fusion (cross-attention, residual mixing) actively degrades performance relative to a no-conditioning baseline, while normalization-based modulation improves it, challenges the field's default assumption that fusing complementary views is the right way to exploit multi-projection information. Limitations include evaluation restricted to indoor datasets (Matterport3D, Stanford2D3D) and a zero-shot setting on SUN360 that shows only marginal gains on Matterport3D; whether the modulation strategy generalizes to other geometrically mismatched domains (e.g., fisheye, multi-camera) remains open.
