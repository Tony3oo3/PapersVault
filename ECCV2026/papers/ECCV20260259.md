## Problem

DETR-based oriented object detectors have not achieved real-time inference because adapting DETR from horizontal to oriented bounding boxes introduces three architectural bottlenecks: semantic-geometric coupling that causes feature collapse when angle is encoded in positional queries, periodicity mismatch that destabilizes Euclidean angle refinement at angular boundaries, and slow convergence in the expanded 5-dimensional search space. Existing oriented DETRs rely on heavy attention and sampling modules to chase peak accuracy, making them 5–10× slower than CNN-based real-time detectors.

## Input / Output

The model consumes single RGB aerial images (1024×1024 patches for DOTA/FAIR-1M, 800×800 for DIOR-R) and produces NMS-free, end-to-end oriented bounding boxes parameterized as (cx, cy, w, h, θ) with θ ∈ [0, π), along with class labels. Inference is measured on a single NVIDIA T4 GPU using TensorRT FP16, with RiO-DETR-n achieving 2.7 ms end-to-end latency. The architecture is fully end-to-end without NMS, distinguishing it from anchor-based CNN detectors that require post-processing.

## Method

RiO-DETR builds on RT-DETRv2 with an OBB head and introduces three task-native designs. First, Content-Driven Angle Estimation decouples orientation from positional queries (encoding only cx, cy, w, h positionally) and uses Rotation-Rectified Orthogonal Attention that splits attention heads into two groups sampling at θ and θ+π/2 to capture both axial and lateral features without extra parameters. Second, Decoupled Periodic Refinement replaces Euclidean additive updates with a bounded coarse-to-fine periodic mechanism (tanh-scaled, layer-wise decaying offsets normalized to [0, π)) paired with a Shortest-Path Periodic L1 Loss that measures angular distance along the shorter arc. Third, Oriented Dense O2O applies independent discrete rotations (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) to each quadrant of stitched training images, injecting angular diversity into dense supervision at zero computational cost to accelerate angle convergence.

## Results

On DOTA-1.0 (single-scale), RiO-DETR-n achieves 78.4 AP₅₀ at 2.7 ms latency (4.0M params), while RiO-DETR-x reaches 81.8 AP₅₀ at 29.9 ms (62.5M params), outperforming YOLO26x-obb (80.4%, 30.5 ms) and RHINO-DETR with Swin-T (79.4%, 242.6 ms). Under multi-scale evaluation, RiO-DETR-x attains 81.76 AP₅₀ with the lowest latency among high-precision models. On DIOR-R, RiO-DETR-x achieves 77.43 AP₅₀ at 17.31 ms, surpassing YOLO26x-obb (76.48%, 17.66 ms); on FAIR-1M-2.0, RiO-DETR-x sets a new SOTA at 47.4 AP₅₀, beating YOLO26x-obb (46.7%) and LSKNet-S (46.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RiO-DETR-x (81.8 AP₅₀, 29.9 ms) outperforms the strongest real-time CNN baseline YOLO26x-obb (80.4%, 30.5 ms) by 1.4 AP₅₀ at comparable latency, and surpasses the best non-real-time DETR baseline RHINO-DETR/Swin-T (79.4%, 242.6 ms) by 2.4 AP₅₀ while being ~8× faster. At the small scale, RiO-DETR-n (78.4%, 2.7 ms) is marginally below YOLO26n-obb (78.8%, 2.8 ms) in AP₅₀ but uses 1.6× more parameters; however, RiO-DETR-s (85.2%, 5.2 ms) substantially exceeds YOLO26s-obb (79.7%, 4.9 ms) by 5.5 AP₅₀. On DIOR-R, RiO-DETR-x (77.43%) beats YOLO26x-obb (76.48%) and RHINO-DETR/Swin-T (72.67%) by 0.95 and 4.76 AP₅₀ respectively. On FAIR-1M-2.0, RiO-DETR-x (47.4%) sets a new SOTA, exceeding YOLO26x-obb (46.7%), LSKNet-S (46.3%), and ReDet (43.2%). Notably, RiO-DETR-m (80.9%, 8.8 ms) dominates RTMDet-R-l (78.8%, 30.2 ms) in both accuracy (+2.1) and speed (3.4× faster), demonstrating that the transformer-based approach achieves a superior speed-accuracy frontier across all three benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

RiO-DETR builds directly on RT-DETRv2's efficient hybrid encoder-decoder architecture, extending it to oriented bounding boxes by incorporating angle representation and denoising from RHINO-DETR, the universal matching strategy from D-FINE, and Dense O2O from DEIM. It departs from prior oriented DETRs (AO2-DETR, EMO2-DETR, ARS-DETR, RHINO-DETR, Oriented-DETR) that prioritize peak accuracy through heavy attention designs and complex sampling modules, instead targeting real-time inference with task-native redesigns of query encoding, angle refinement, and dense supervision. The key conceptual departure is treating orientation as a content-driven semantic quantity rather than a geometric coordinate symmetric to (cx, cy, w, h), which motivates the geometry-decoupled query design and orthogonal attention splitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first real-time oriented DETR, RiO-DETR closes the long-standing efficiency gap between transformer-based and CNN-based oriented detectors, making end-to-end NMS-free oriented detection viable for edge deployment in aerial and remote sensing applications. The practical impact is significant: matching YOLO26 latency across all model scales while outperforming it in AP50 suggests the DETR paradigm can now compete in the real-time oriented detection regime. The paper acknowledges that designing a backbone specifically tailored to oriented/remote sensing detection remains open, and the current reliance on HGNet (a general-purpose backbone) may leave performance on the table. The decoupled periodic refinement and content-driven angle estimation principles are broadly applicable to any task involving periodic geometric parameters.
