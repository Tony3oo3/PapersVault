## Problem

Broadband VIS-SWIR sensors (400–1700 nm) enable transformative material classification and all-weather imaging, but refractive optics cannot focus this entire spectrum simultaneously without complex, heavy multi-element assemblies. Mirrors are inherently achromatic and compact, yet simple concave-mirror objectives suffer from severe Petzval field curvature and off-axis aberrations (coma, astigmatism) that render flat-sensor imaging impractical at medium focal lengths (50–100 mm) with high apertures. No purely mirror-based objective with large light-gathering capability and full-field-of-view sharpness on a flat sensor has existed for this regime.

## Input / Output

The system captures a sparse focal stack of 2–4 images (typically 3) from a single concave mirror objective (50 mm F/1 or 100 mm F/2) paired with a Sony IMX990 VIS-SWIR sensor, with the sensor translated axially at fixed 200 μm (50 mm) or 100 μm (100 mm) steps via a motorized stage. Each image is a full-frame panchromatic or filtered (blue/green/red/NIR/SWIR) measurement at 1.3 MP resolution covering 18° (50 mm) or 9° (100 mm) FoV. The output is a single sharp, all-in-focus image per spectral band, with the same PSF model and focus settings applied across all five bands without per-wavelength recalibration.

## Method

The core contribution is SeidelConv, a physics-inspired spatially-varying PSF model that parametrizes each focal-stack measurement as a weighted sum of Q=31 basis components, where each component applies a bounded affine warp (inspired by Seidel aberration geometry) followed by a learned 11×11 blur kernel, combined via per-pixel weights. This affine warping provides the geometric degree of freedom needed to model the stretching and rotation of PSFs (coma, astigmatism) that purely convolutional or eigen-decomposed PSF models cannot capture. SeidelConv parameters are calibrated via a monitor-based procedure displaying random dot patterns (10 images, SGD with L1 kernel sparsity), and the resulting forward operator is wavelength-invariant due to the achromatic nature of mirrors. Reconstruction solves a multi-image deconvolution (Eq. 4) using a Plug-and-Play framework with DPIR as the denoiser prior, fusing the sparse focal stack into a single all-in-focus image. The key insight is that Petzval curvature guarantees different FoV regions are sharp at different axial positions, so a minimal 3-image stack ensures every pixel is near-focus in at least one measurement, making the inverse problem well-posed.

## Results

On the USAF test target and Kodak validation set, SeidelConv with a 3-image focal stack achieves 27.1 dB PSNR / 0.74 SSIM off-axis, outperforming all baselines by more than 2 dB in the off-axis region. On-axis performance is comparable across methods (~27.6 dB PSNR), confirming that the key advantage of SeidelConv lies in correcting severe geometric distortions at the periphery. Multi-image deconvolution with 3 images (27.1/0.74 off-axis) substantially outperforms focal-stack averaging (23.1/0.64), Petzval-surface imaging (23.9/0.67), and single-image deconvolution (20.9/0.49). Performance saturates at 3 images, with diminishing returns beyond that point.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeidelConv decisively outperforms CoordGate [32] and patch-wise convolution on off-axis metrics (27.1 vs. 24.8 dB PSNR), confirming that the affine warping component is essential for modeling coma and astigmatism in high-aperture mirrors; on-axis, all three methods are nearly identical (~27.6 dB). Against the focal-stack-summing approach of Yokoya & Nayar [23] (20→1), SeidelConv with only 3 images gains 4.0 dB off-axis (27.1 vs. 23.1), demonstrating that multi-image deconvolution is far superior to collapsing the stack into a single spatially-invariant blur. The Petzval-surface evaluation of Matsunaga & Nayar [24] (20→1) also trails by 3.2 dB off-axis (23.9 vs. 27.1), as it still requires a spatially-invariant deconvolution that cannot handle residual off-axis aberrations. Single-image deconvolution [33] with SeidelConv (20.9 dB) and Ring Deconvolution [29] (18.7 dB) are both substantially worse, highlighting the necessity of the focal stack for covering Petzval curvature. Qualitatively, the 50 mm F/1 mirror system produces sharp broadband images (400–1700 nm) with no wavelength-dependent refocusing, whereas a comparable 100 mm SLR lens blurs across the full VIS-SWIR range and a plano-convex lens is degraded by chromatic aberration at every wavelength.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the focal-stacking line of work (Yokoya & Nayar 2015; Matsunaga & Nayar 2015) by replacing dense 20–30 image stacks with a sparse 2–4 image set and performing joint multi-image deconvolution rather than collapsing to a single image on the Petzval surface. For the spatially-varying PSF model, SeidelConv builds on the CoordGate/EigenCWD family of learned blur operators but introduces bounded affine warping inspired by classical Seidel aberration theory, providing the geometric degrees of freedom needed to model coma and astigmatism in high-aperture mirrors. The calibration procedure borrows the monitor-based dot-pattern approach from Lin et al. (2025), and the reconstruction leverages the DPIR plug-and-play prior. The key conceptual departure is treating a single simple concave mirror—not a multi-element anastigmat—as a viable broadband imaging optic, with all aberration correction deferred to the computational backend.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a 60 g single-mirror objective can produce sharp, full-field-of-view images across the entire 400–1700 nm spectrum without wavelength-dependent refocusing, a capability that previously required bulky, expensive multi-element glass assemblies or multi-camera rigs. The achromaticity of mirrors means one PSF calibration transfers across all spectral bands, a practical advantage with no refractive equivalent. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even shorter focal lengths (where Petzval curvature demands more stack images), whether end-to-end feedforward networks trained with SeidelConv as a differentiable forward model can achieve real-time performance, and how motion in dynamic scenes affects the multi-measurement requirement. The iterative PnP solver's computational cost also limits current deployment to near-static scenes.
