## Problem

Existing semantic correspondence benchmarks conflate local concept recognition with object-relative identity (e.g., front-left vs. rear-right wheel) and provide no mechanism for evaluating cross-category transfer (e.g., a wheel on a car vs. a bus). This ambiguity prevents systematic diagnosis of whether vision foundation models truly encode structured object understanding or merely recognize local appearance, and the absence of language descriptions blocks evaluation of large vision-language models on fine-grained part-level correspondence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260067/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

For vision foundation models, the input is a source image with a query keypoint and a target image; the output is the predicted corresponding point via argmax cosine similarity over the target feature map, scored with PCK@0.1 (radius = 0.1 × max bounding-box dimension). For LVLMs, the task is reformulated as a 4-choice multiple-choice VQA: the target image displays candidate keypoints labeled A–D, and the query is specified as a marked source image (Vis.), a text description (Desc.), or both (Vis.+Desc.), evaluated under the CircularEval protocol requiring consistency across four answer permutations. The SOCO dataset provides 4,000 images across 100 categories, ~73k within-category SOC pairs, and ~1.3M cross-category pairs, each keypoint carrying a programmatic language description (e.g., 'Center point of the front left wheel of a bus').

## Method

The core contribution is a taxonomy-driven decomposition of semantic correspondence into three progressively harder levels: Concept Correspondence (CC, matching the same local semantic concept regardless of instance), Semantic Object Correspondence (SOC, matching the same concept with identical object-relative positional attributes such as left/right/front/rear), and Cross-SOC (matching object-relative keypoints across related categories via shared taxonomy concepts). The SOCO dataset is constructed from ImageNet images (man-made objects via ImageNet3D pose metadata, animals via Animal3D), with keypoints annotated by three independent annotators (median-aggregated, 0.85% per-keypoint std) and organized into a hierarchical taxonomy spanning 100 categories in four super-classes. Vision models are evaluated zero-shot via nearest-neighbor feature matching, while LVLMs are evaluated as multiple-choice VQA under CircularEval. A dedicated SOC-geo variant isolates purely geometric disambiguation by restricting the argmax to all instances of the same concept within a target image. The paper further demonstrates that SOC performance correlates more strongly with dense downstream tasks (segmentation, tracking, 3D pose, 3D detection) than ImageNet kNN classification, positioning it as a practical zero-shot diagnostic of representation quality.

## Results

On SOCO (PCK@0.1), DINOv2 achieves the strongest overall correspondence performance with 78.9% CC, 60.4% SOC, and 54.9% Cross-SOC, while SD 2.1 attains the best SOC-geo score (76.8%) and DINOv3 leads on Furniture (59.9%). All models show a consistent CC→SOC drop of 11–19 points, revealing that even the strongest backbones struggle to disambiguate repeated object parts. For LVLMs evaluated under a 4-choice protocol, DINOv2 adapted to the same setting scores 81.0%, far exceeding the best LVLM (Qwen3-VL-8B at 54.0% in the description-only setting), and all LVLMs perform substantially better with text descriptions than with visual-reference queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among vision foundation models, DINOv2 leads on CC (78.9%) and SOC (60.4%), outperforming DINOv3 (69.7/55.5) and C-RADIOv3 (69.0/51.1), confirming that dense self-supervised objectives yield the strongest semantic correspondence features. However, DINOv3 surpasses DINOv2 on SOC-geo (76.0 vs. 71.9) and on the Furniture supercategory (59.9 vs. 45.5), while SD 2.1 achieves the highest SOC-geo score (76.8%), indicating that diffusion-based features encode part geometry more effectively. Global-alignment models (CLIP at 25.2% CC, QWEN-L at 36.8%) and reconstruction-based models (MAE at 14.4%, CroCoV2 at 15.1%) lag far behind, with CLIP's spatial variant PE-Spatial (63.2% CC) partially closing the gap. For LVLMs, DINOv2 adapted to the 4-choice protocol (81.0%) outperforms every LVLM by at least 27 points; among LVLMs, Qwen3-VL-8B is strongest (34.2% Vis., 54.0% Desc.), followed by GPT-4o (30.2% Vis., 37.6% Desc.) and InternVL3.5-8B (24.9% Vis., 39.6% Desc.), while LLaVA-OV-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B remain near random (2.9% and 5.2% Vis.). A consistent finding across all LVLMs is that description-only queries (Desc.) outperform visual-reference queries (Vis.) by 10–36 points, exposing a gap between language-grounded localization and cross-image visual matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semantic correspondence benchmarking line (SPair-71k, MISC210K, PF-PASCAL) by introducing a taxonomy-driven formulation that decomposes the task into concept correspondence (CC), semantic object correspondence (SOC), and cross-category SOC, explicitly separating local concept recognition from geometric disambiguation and cross-category transfer. It builds on Probe3D (El Banani et al., CVPR 2024) for the downstream-task correlation analysis, extending that framework with SOCO as an additional probe. The LVLM evaluation extends the BLINK benchmark's limited correspondence questions by adding language descriptions and a structured multi-setting protocol (visual, visual+description, description-only), enabling a finer-grained analysis of where LVLMs succeed and fail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOCO provides the first large-scale, taxonomy-driven SC benchmark with cross-category pairs and language annotations, filling a critical gap in how foundation models are evaluated for structured object understanding. The finding that SOC correlates with dense downstream tasks (segmentation, tracking, 3D pose, 3D detection) more strongly than ImageNet kNN positions it as a practical zero-shot diagnostic that could complement or partially replace costly fine-tuning-based evaluations. Open questions include whether the CC→SOC gap (repeated-part confusion) can be closed by architectural or training changes, whether cross-category transfer improves with scale, and whether LVLMs can close the gap between text-prompted localization and visual-reference matching as they scale further.
