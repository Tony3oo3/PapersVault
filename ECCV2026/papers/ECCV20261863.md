## Problem

Diffusion-based super-resolution methods that repurpose large text-to-image (T2I) backbones as priors suffer from semantic drift: when the low-resolution observation is weakly informative, the broad text-driven prior dominates and hallucinates globally plausible but factually incorrect details. Additionally, classifier-free guidance (CFG) required by T2I pipelines doubles the number of network evaluations at inference. The paper asks whether a purely visual, reference-conditioned prior can replace the T2I text prior to achieve both semantic consistency and lower inference cost.

## Input / Output

The model takes a low-resolution RGB image (processed as 512×512 patches) and a single high-resolution reference image retrieved from an external database via learned neural hashing. No text prompts or classifier-free guidance are used at any stage. The output is a high-resolution super-resolved image generated in the latent space of a frozen VAE (Stable Diffusion 2-base) over 50 DDIM sampling steps, with the reference encoded only once and cached for reuse across all denoising steps.

## Method

ReAL repurposes a pretrained Stable Diffusion 2-base U-Net into a pure Reference-to-Image (R2I) model by removing the text branch and CFG entirely, conditioning solely on the LR latent (concatenated channel-wise with the noisy latent) and a retrieved HR reference. The core mechanism is a Reference Fusion Module (RFM): the reference is passed through the U-Net once at the start of inference, and the key and value tensors from every self-attention layer are cached; during each denoising step, these cached tensors are concatenated with the main path's key and value along the token dimension so that every LR query token can attend to reference features at all layers and timesteps. The reference is injected on the key/value side rather than the query side, preserving the LR-anchored denoising trajectory while allowing each token to selectively borrow reference texture. A contrastive-learning hash network (InfoNCE loss on VGG features) retrieves the most relevant reference from the database, and the entire U-Net is trained end-to-end with only the standard DDPM ℓ2 noise-prediction loss, avoiding auxiliary perceptual or adversarial objectives.

## Results

On DIV2K Valid, ReAL achieves the best PSNR (19.650 dB), SSIM (0.507), and LPIPS (0.391) among all non-GAN diffusion baselines, outperforming iRAG by 0.37 dB in PSNR and CoSeR by 0.036 in LPIPS on DRealSR. On RealSR, ReAL attains the highest PSNR (21.044 dB) and SSIM (0.608) among diffusion methods, while on DRealSR it leads in all three distortion metrics (PSNR 24.836, SSIM 0.725, LPIPS 0.359). For perceptual quality, ReAL is competitive but trails DiffBIR and SeeSR on CLIP-IQA+ and MUSIQ (e.g., 0.646 vs. 0.727 CLIP-IQA+ on DIV2K), reflecting a mild perception–fidelity trade-off. ReAL also achieves the lowest inference latency (2.65 s) and memory (5.83 GB) among reference-conditioned diffusion methods, thanks to its CFG-free design and single-pass reference caching.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two GAN baselines (ESRGAN, BSRGAN), ReAL consistently trails on PSNR and SSIM (e.g., 19.650 vs. 20.067 dB on DIV2K) but matches or beats them on LPIPS (0.391 vs. 0.395), indicating comparable or better perceptual fidelity. Among diffusion-based methods, ReAL dominates on distortion metrics: it outperforms CoSeR by 0.07–0.10 dB in PSNR and iRAG by 0.37 dB on DIV2K, while also leading SSIM and LPIPS across all three benchmarks. However, ReAL cedes the top perceptual scores to DiffBIR and SeeSR on CLIP-IQA+ (0.646 vs. 0.727 on DIV2K) and MUSIQ (63.3 vs. 69.4), suggesting those T2I-driven models produce more 'aesthetically pleasing' but less faithful outputs. Compared to the closest reference-based competitor iRAG, ReAL wins on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on DIV2K and DRealSR, but iRAG edges ahead on LPIPS (0.372 vs. 0.375) and CLIP-IQA+ (0.621 vs. 0.612) on RealSR and DRealSR respectively. Overall, ReAL's advantage is most pronounced in structural fidelity and hallucination resistance, while T2I-based methods retain a perceptual-quality edge at the cost of semantic drift.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReAL builds on the Latent Diffusion Model framework (Stable Diffusion 2) as its generative backbone and adopts the hash-based retrieval module from iRAG for selecting reference images. However, it departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm of adapting frozen T2I diffusion models (as in CoSeR, iRAG, SeeSR, FaithDiff) by retraining the U-Net as a pure Reference-to-Image model that conditions solely on the LR latent and a retrieved HR reference, eliminating both text prompts and classifier-free guidance. The key architectural novelty is the Reference Fusion Module, which caches reference key-value tensors once and injects them into self-attention at every denoising step, replacing the indirect textual/semantic conditioning used by prior RefSR methods with direct visual-to-visual feature transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated failure mode of T2I-based SR—semantic drift and hallucination under extreme degradation—and shows that a purely reference-conditioned design can mitigate it while achieving competitive fidelity. The efficiency gains are substantial: ReAL runs in 2.65 s with 5.83 GB memory versus 16.85 s / 14.94 GB (CoSeR) and 22.35 s / 22.48 GB (iRAG), making reference-conditioned diffusion SR far more deployable. Open questions remain around robustness under heavy blur or cross-domain references, the cost of end-to-end retraining versus fine-tuning, and whether the KV-caching fusion pattern generalizes to other restoration or editing tasks beyond SR.
