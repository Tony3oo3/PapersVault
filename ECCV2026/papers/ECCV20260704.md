## Problem

Single-view 3D generation is fundamentally ill-posed: the occluded back-view is synthesized stochastically from limited 3D training data, producing geometries that are either physically implausible or misaligned with user intent. Existing models lack the ability to align unobserved-region synthesis with user-specified semantic constraints, and directly injecting VLM representations into 3D networks fails because they are too abstract and lack geometric grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260704/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single front-view RGB image and a natural-language text prompt describing desired back-view components (e.g., 'two straps on the back') as input, and produces a complete 3D geometry via TRELLIS2's two-stage sparse-voxel pipeline (coarse structure → fine geometry). The text prompt is component-level rather than a full scene description, and the conditioning signal for 3D generation is not the decoded back-view image but rather intermediate MMDiT hidden states extracted at denoising timestep t=0.25, which encode both spatial layout and semantic content without requiring pixel-level fidelity.

## Method

Know3D uses a fine-tuned Qwen-Image-Edit (Qwen2.5-VL + MMDiT) as an intermediate bridge that translates VLM semantic knowledge into image-space structural priors. The key insight is extracting hidden states from intermediate MMDiT layers at a specific denoising timestep (t=0.25) rather than using fully denoised VAE latents or DINOv3 features, as these intermediate states retain both spatial awareness and semantic robustness. These hidden states are concatenated across n layers, linearly projected, and injected into TRELLIS2's 3D backbone via a parallel cross-attention branch with zero-initialized output scaling, preserving the original self-attention and image-conditioned cross-attention layers. Qwen-Image-Edit is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 64) on front-back view pairs using a stochastic prompting strategy (50% probability of including the back-view description) under a Conditional Flow Matching objective, while the 3D generation stage is trained with LoRA on the TRELLIS2 backbone and full fine-tuning on the new cross-attention modules.

## Results

On HY3D-Bench, Know3D achieves the best ULIP score of 0.2174 and Uni3D score of 0.3518 on the test split, and 0.2127 / 0.3512 on the validation split, outperforming all seven baselines on both metrics. The closest single-view competitor, TRELLIS, scores 0.2143 ULIP and 0.3421 Uni3D on test, while the multi-view baseline Hunyuan3D-2MV reaches 0.2108 ULIP and 0.3413 Uni3D. Know3D's advantage over the strongest single-view baseline (Hunyuan3D-2.1: 0.2140 ULIP, 0.3434 Uni3D) is approximately 0.0034 in ULIP and 0.0084 in Uni3D on the test split.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Know3D outperforms all seven baselines on both ULIP and Uni3D across both test and validation splits of HY3D-Bench. Among single-view methods, it leads TRELLIS (0.2143/0.3421) and Hunyuan3D-2.1 (0.2140/0.3434) by roughly 0.003–0.008 in the respective metrics, with the largest margin over TRELLIS.2 (0.1948/0.3308), which is notably weaker on both metrics. The multi-view baseline Hunyuan3D-2MV (0.2108/0.3413), which directly feeds the generated back-view as an additional input, is also surpassed, validating that injecting MMDiT hidden states as structural-semantic conditioning is more effective than simply providing a synthesized view. Qualitatively, Know3D produces more plausible back-view geometries than Hunyuan3D-2MV, which generates distorted structures on novel viewpoints (Fig. 5). The ablation further confirms that MMDiT intermediate hidden states (IoU 0.352, CD 2.262) outperform both VAE latents (IoU 0.308) and DINOv3 features (IoU 0.342) as the conditioning signal for 3D generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Know3D builds on TRELLIS2's two-stage sparse-voxel 3D generation paradigm and Qwen-Image-Edit's VLM-diffusion architecture, but departs from both the autoregressive shape-LLM line of work and the direct VLM-feature-injection approach by using intermediate MMDiT hidden states as a semantic-structural bridge. The paper positions itself at the intersection of unified multimodal models and 3D generation, arguing that 3D generation lags behind the 2D multimodal domain in semantic controllability due to limited 3D training data. Rather than treating the back-view as a purely stochastic completion problem, it reframes it as a knowledge-guided synthesis task where a fine-tuned VLM-diffusion model translates textual intent into image-space structural priors that condition the 3D backbone via a parallel cross-attention branch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical pain point in single-view 3D generation—uncontrollable and often implausible unseen-region synthesis—by demonstrating that language instructions can steer back-view geometry, which is directly relevant to content-creation pipelines in gaming and film. A key open question is whether this approach generalizes beyond the 180° back-view to arbitrary novel viewpoints or multi-part semantic editing, and whether the dependence on the underlying MLLM's comprehension limits scalability. The finding that intermediate diffusion states (at t=0.25) outperform both pixel-space VAE latents and DINOv3 features for 3D guidance suggests a broader principle about where in a generative pipeline the most transferable structural-semantic information resides, which could inform future cross-modal conditioning designs.
