## Problem

Proactive streaming video understanding requires deciding when to respond to a user query at every frame, demanding rich visual-semantic reasoning at 5–10 fps. Existing approaches formulate this as a per-frame decision problem using MLLMs or gating modules, creating an unresolved tension between the complexity of accurate triggering and the strict real-time computational budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260342/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At query time, the system receives a natural language instruction plus 5 seconds of video history at 1 fps, and produces a set of structured visual proposals (averaging ~4 cues of ~14 words each). During streaming, a 2-second sliding window at 2 fps is consumed by the matching module, which outputs a binary trigger signal when a cosine-similarity surge exceeds a tunable threshold θ (default 0.04). Upon triggering, a downstream MLLM generates a free-form text response. The framework operates on arbitrarily long video sessions without context-length degradation.

## Method

Em-Garde decomposes the triggering decision into a query-time semantic parsing stage (IGPP) and a streaming-time visual matching stage (LPMM), shifting all expensive reasoning outside the per-frame loop. IGPP, built on Qwen2.5VL-7B, is trained in two stages—SFT on the curated Parse2Prop-1K dataset followed by GRPO reinforcement learning that rewards correct trigger timing and penalizes false positives—producing perceptually grounded, temporally localizable visual cues. LPMM uses the off-the-shelf Ops-MM-V1-2B embedding model to encode both the sliding-window video segment and each proposal into a shared embedding space, computing cosine similarity per proposal per frame. A trigger fires when the maximum similarity increase across proposals exceeds θ, after which an MLLM responder verifies and generates the response. A visual encoding cache reuses overlapping frame encodings across the sliding window, yielding a 2–3× speedup and enabling 10–15 fps throughput on A100 GPUs.

## Results

On OVO-Bench Forward Active Responding (FAR) tasks, Em-Garde achieves an average F1 of 30.99, outperforming the next-best method MMDuet-2 (20.51) by over 10 points. On StreamingBench Proactive Response (PO), Em-Garde reaches 38.0% accuracy at 2 fps, surpassing MMDuet-2 (34.6%) by 3.4 points. On ProactiveVideoQA, Em-Garde is competitive with MMDuet-2, winning on the EGO split (52.3 vs. 33.6 PAUC) while trailing on WEB (44.3 vs. 53.3). The streaming loop runs at 10–15 fps on A100 GPUs without throughput degradation as video length grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Em-Garde decisively outperforms all six baselines on the two primary proactive-response benchmarks. On OVO-Bench FAR, it leads MMDuet-2 by 10.5 F1 points (30.99 vs. 20.51) and StreamForest by 17 points (30.99 vs. 13.95); VideoLLM-Online and FVStream score below 7 F1. On StreamingBench PO, Em-Garde (38.0%) beats MMDuet-2 (34.6%), StreamAgent (28.9%), and Dispider (25.3%), with a 34-point margin over VideoLLM-Online (4.0%). On ProactiveVideoQA, the picture is mixed: MMDuet-2 edges ahead on the WEB split (53.3 vs. 44.3 PAUC), but Em-Garde wins on EGO (52.3 vs. 33.6) and matches MMDuet-2 on VAD (27.4 vs. 28.9) while producing far fewer duplicate replies (1.4% vs. 15.2%). For online video understanding (Table 4), Em-Garde is on par with the SOTA StreamForest (76.7 vs. 77.3 on StreamingBench RT-VU; 63.0 vs. 61.2 on OVO-Bench RT-VP), confirming that the propose-match decomposition does not sacrifice response quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant per-frame decision-making paradigm in proactive streaming video understanding (VideoLLM-Online, StreamMind, Dispider, FVStream, StreamForest, MMDuet-2, StreamAgent), where a heavy MLLM or gate must perform full semantic reasoning at every timestep. Instead, Em-Garde introduces a propose-match decomposition: a large MLLM (Qwen2.5VL-7B) performs all semantic parsing once at query time (IGPP), while a lightweight 2B multimodal embedding model (Ops-MM-V1) handles continuous frame-level matching in the streaming loop (LPMM). This repurposes off-the-shelf embedding models for a triggering role they were not originally designed for, and leverages GRPO-based RL to align generated proposals with the perception module's capabilities — a training strategy not previously applied to streaming trigger optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical resolution to the efficiency-accuracy tension in real-time proactive video understanding, achieving 10–15 fps on A100 without throughput degradation as context grows, which is a meaningful deployment advantage over prior models. The explicit threshold knob (θ) gives practitioners a transparent control over the precision-recall tradeoff for safety-critical vs. convenience applications. Key open questions remain: the triggering mechanism is sensitive to sudden scene changes and visually correlated but semantically irrelevant transitions, and the paper does not address long-horizon reasoning or joint optimization of the triggering and response-generation stages, leaving the full proactive assistant pipeline partially unresolved.
