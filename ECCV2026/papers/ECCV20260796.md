## Problem

Existing RGB-T salient object detection methods focus on fusion strategies but implicitly assume that modality features extracted during encoding remain reliable. In practice, modality degradation (low illumination, motion blur, noise for RGB; contrast compression, block artifacts for thermal) corrupts features early in the network hierarchy, and standard backbones' fixed transformations propagate and amplify these unreliable signals. Reliability-aware representation learning that explicitly models and propagates modality trustworthiness across encoding, decoding, and fusion stages remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of spatially aligned RGB and thermal images, both resized to 352×352, and produces a single saliency map S ∈ [0,1]^{H×W} at the original resolution. The output is not a single-head prediction but a linear aggregation of three independently predicted saliency maps from parallel RGB, Thermal, and Fusion decoding streams, each with its own lightweight prediction head. The backbone (Res2Net-50 or Swin-B) is frozen and shared across both modalities, with only the reliability-conditioned modulation branches and decoding streams being trained.

## Method

RA-SOD introduces a hierarchical reliability modeling framework with three coupled components. First, a Reliability-Conditioned Backbone Representation (RCBR) augments the frozen backbone with a parallel residual branch that combines a shared Mixture-of-Experts bank (K=3 experts) with modality-specific low-rank adapters, where routing weights are predicted from the base features to adaptively compensate degraded modalities while preserving structural priors. Second, an Uncertainty-Guided Dual-Stream Decoding mechanism predicts explicit uncertainty maps at each top-down decoding stage, which gate bidirectional cross-modal correction so that unreliable regions receive less cross-modal influence during recursive refinement. Third, a Pixel-wise Modality Competition (PMC) module replaces fixed fusion weights with spatially varying softmax logits that dynamically select RGB or thermal cues at each location based on local reliability. All three streams are trained jointly with deep supervision using a combined BCE and IoU loss, and the final prediction is the sum of the three stream outputs.

## Results

On the large-scale VT5000 benchmark, RA-SOD achieves the best score on all five metrics: S_m=0.897, F_β=0.870, F_β^w=0.855, E_m=0.937, and M=0.029, outperforming the strongest baseline ConTriNet by +0.010 in F_β and +0.009 in F_β^w. On VT1000, RA-SOD leads in F_β (0.915), F_β^w (0.913), E_m (0.951), and M (0.018). On the challenging VT-IMAG benchmark designed for severe sensor failures, RA-SOD attains the highest S_m (0.829), F_β (0.765), F_β^w (0.735), and E_m (0.908), improving F_β by 0.020 over ConTriNet on its own benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RA-SOD outperforms all 13 compared baselines on every metric of VT5000, the largest benchmark, with the most notable gains over ConTriNet [35] (+0.010 F_β, +0.009 F_β^w, +0.003 E_m) and SMR-Net [59] (+0.011 F_β, +0.010 F_β^w). On VT1000, RA-SOD leads in F_β, F_β^w, E_m, and M, while CAVER [29] retains a slight edge in S_m (0.936 vs. 0.932) and M (0.017 vs. 0.018). On VT821, RA-SOD achieves the best S_m, F_β, F_β^w, and E_m, with M tied at 0.031 alongside SMR-Net. On the VT-IMAG challenge set (Table 3), RA-SOD wins 4 of 5 metrics over ConTriNet, improving F_β by 0.020 (0.765 vs. 0.745) and E_m by 0.006, though ConTriNet retains a marginal 0.002 advantage in M. Under synthetic degradations (Table 2), RA-SOD surpasses the best competitor ConTriNet by +0.006 in F_β and +0.004 in F_β^w, confirming that reliability-aware modeling provides consistent robustness gains when one modality is corrupted. With a Swin-B Transformer backbone (Table 5), RA-SOD extends its lead, achieving the best scores on all five VT5000 metrics (F_β=0.904, E_m=0.963, M=0.017) over ConTriNet and PCNet [47].

## Relation to Prior Work

RA-SOD builds directly on ConTriNet's tri-stream (RGB/Thermal/Fusion) decoding architecture and its MFM encoder fusion module, extending this skeleton with three reliability-aware mechanisms: a MoE-based residual modulation branch in the backbone, uncertainty-guided cross-modal refinement during decoding, and pixel-wise modality competition at fusion. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm in RGB-T SOD—where prior methods (attention-based weighting, two-stream interaction, graph learning) treat modality features as reliable during encoding and address cross-modal inconsistency only at later fusion stages—by arguing that degradation propagates early through the network hierarchy and must be modeled at every level. The use of mixture-of-experts routing for input-adaptive feature correction borrows from the broader MoE literature in vision, while the uncertainty maps draw on evidential/aleatoric uncertainty estimation, repurposing both for the specific problem of modality-dependent reliability in salient object detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world RGB-T sensors degrade in ways (low light, motion blur, thermal contrast compression, block artifacts) that existing fusion-centric methods do not explicitly handle, and the synthetic degradation experiments plus the VT-IMAG challenge set provide concrete evidence that reliability-aware modeling yields measurable robustness gains. However, the improvements over the strongest baseline (ConTriNet) are modest (~1% F_beta on standard benchmarks, ~2% on VT-IMAG), and the M metric on VT-IMAG is actually slightly worse than ConTriNet, suggesting the reliability mechanism trades some pixel-level precision for structural robustness. Open questions include whether the reliability-aware framework generalizes beyond SOD to other multi-modal tasks (tracking, detection, segmentation), whether the MoE component is truly necessary compared to simpler per-modality adapters, and how the approach performs under more diverse or compound degradations not covered by the five synthetic settings tested.
