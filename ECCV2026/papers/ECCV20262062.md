## Problem

State-of-the-art diffusion and flow-based 3D generators are trained at fixed voxel-grid or latent resolutions due to cubic memory and compute growth, capping the detail they can produce. Users, however, may need arbitrarily high-resolution assets, and existing workarounds (e.g., LATTICE) require multi-scale high-resolution supervision or heavy multi-GPU training. The paper asks whether a pretrained object-level 3D generator can be adapted into a memory-efficient, progressive super-resolution model that synthesizes fine details beyond its original training resolution on a single GPU.

## Input / Output

The method takes a coarse textured mesh (typically from TRELLIS.2 or Hunyuan3D) and one or more reference images with estimated camera parameters (via MapAnything). The mesh is encoded into a structured sparse latent at 64³ resolution (1024³ o-voxels), and the SR pipeline progressively doubles the latent resolution per round, reaching 2560³ after two rounds from a 640³ initial encoding. The output is a high-resolution textured mesh decoded from the refined structured latent, with the option to feed the output latent back as the next round's LR condition for further upscaling.

## Method

PLSR decomposes global super-resolution into independent patch-wise sub-tasks via an associative input decomposition scheme that extracts aligned triplets (noisy HR latent patch, LR latent patch, and a projected image crop) using relative voxel coordinates to enforce resolution independence. The model is a LoRA-fine-tuned version of TRELLIS.2's sparse latent DiT, augmented with a lightweight LR attention branch that computes local cross-attention between each HR voxel and its neighboring LR cells (extracted via the first two VAE decoder blocks), avoiding strict spatial alignment issues from remeshing. Training uses standard Conditional Flow Matching on 32³ patches sampled from 64³-encoded Objaverse-XL meshes, bypassing the need for actual high-resolution training data. At inference, an iterative patch-wise denoising pipeline splits the global latent into overlapping windows at every denoising step, processes each patch independently, and stitches predictions via center-weighted averaging, so that neighboring patches maintain mutual awareness across iterations. This progressive, localized design enables ×2 resolution increase per round with only ~50 GPU-hours of fine-tuning on a single A100.

## Results

On the pseudo-GT test set, PLSR (2560³) achieves the best geometry metrics across all measures: LPIPS 0.109, FID 55.89, HFR 1.088, CD 0.0139×10⁻³, and F1 0.145, outperforming TRELLIS.2 at both 1536³ and 2560³ resolutions. For texture, PLSR attains LPIPS 0.125, FID 59.89, and HFR 1.646, surpassing MVPaint (LPIPS 0.154, FID 77.18, HFR 1.052). On the open-world VLM-based ELO evaluation, PLSR leads in geometry detail richness (1982) and overall quality (1855), and in texture detail richness (1756) and overall quality (1633), while ranking second on artifact-freeness and texture alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLSR outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against TRELLIS.2 at its native 1536³ resolution, PLSR reduces geometry LPIPS from 0.122 to 0.109, FID from 60.37 to 55.89, and CD from 0.0247 to 0.0139 (a 44% reduction), while increasing HFR from 0.981 to 1.088 and F1 from 0.059 to 0.145. Notably, TRELLIS.2 at 2560³ (its training-free upscaling) actually degrades in FID (63.19 vs. 60.37) and HFR (0.923 vs. 0.981) compared to 1536³, confirming limited generalization beyond training resolution, whereas PLSR improves over both. UltraShape and DetailGen3D, which target geometry-only refinement, show substantially worse LPIPS (0.167 and 0.187) and FID (72.03 and 93.35) than PLSR, with UltraShape's CD of 1.3982 being two orders of magnitude higher. For texture, PLSR beats MVPaint by a wide margin: LPIPS 0.125 vs. 0.154, FID 59.89 vs. 77.18, and HFR 1.646 vs. 1.052, indicating that PLSR recovers genuinely high-frequency texture detail rather than merely amplifying noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TRELLIS.2's structured sparse latent representation and flow-based DiT architecture, repurposing the pretrained object-level generator into a localized super-resolution model via LoRA fine-tuning. It departs from prior 3D SR approaches (SDF-Diffusion, 3DSR, SuperCarver) that either require paired high-resolution supervision or are tied to specific representations (SDF, radiance fields, mesh normals), instead operating entirely in a compact latent voxel space with associative patch decomposition. The progressive, patch-wise denoising pipeline draws conceptual parallels to iterative image SR and multi-diffusion stitching, but is the first to apply this strategy to structured 3D latents conditioned on both LR geometry and local image crops.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: achieving 2560³ output from a 640³ coarse mesh on a single A100 with ~50 GPU-hours of fine-tuning makes high-resolution 3D asset generation accessible without multi-GPU clusters or multi-scale HR training data. The finding that naively scaling TRELLIS.2 to 2560³ degrades performance underscores that progressive localized refinement is not merely an efficiency trick but a necessary strategy for extreme-resolution generation. Key limitations remain: the method depends on accurate camera alignment, cannot correct large structural errors in the coarse mesh, and is tied to the TRELLIS.2 latent representation, requiring adapter retraining for alternative encodings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other sparse latent VAEs and how well selective (region-specific) SR performs in production pipelines.
