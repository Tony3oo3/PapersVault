## Problem

Self-supervised contrastive learning produces high-quality visual representations but encodes demographic biases from training data, and existing fairness-aware methods address this by redesigning or replacing the base SSL objective, limiting portability across the rapidly evolving landscape of SSL frameworks. ProtoFair asks whether a lightweight plug-in regularizer can make existing SSL methods fairer without modifying their training objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262793/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (face images at standard resolution from CelebA/UTKFace, chest X-rays from NIH) together with binary sensitive attribute labels (e.g., gender, ethnicity) during training, but requires no target task labels. A ResNet-18 encoder produces L2-normalized d-dimensional embeddings via two separate MLP projection heads: a contrastive head for the base SSL loss and a cluster head for prototype-based cluster assignment. Downstream evaluation freezes the encoder and trains a linear classifier, reporting accuracy and equalized odds across sensitive groups.

## Method

ProtoFair introduces a fairness-aware contrastive regularizer added as an auxiliary term (λ·L_CF) to any existing SSL loss. It maintains K momentum-updated prototypes (initialized via K-Means, refined via EMA) that assign each sample to a cluster serving as a proxy for semantic content; samples sharing the same cluster but belonging to different sensitive groups form pseudo-counterfactual pairs that are pulled together in a contrastive objective, while same-cluster same-group and different-cluster pairs appear only in the denominator. A FIFO cross-batch queue stores detached representations, cluster assignments, and sensitive labels from past batches to expand pair discovery under group imbalance. Cluster assignments are detached from the computational graph (EM-style), preventing the model from collapsing all samples into one cluster to trivially satisfy the fairness criterion. The regularizer activates only after a warmup period, once the encoder has learned meaningful representations.

## Results

On CelebA with SupCon as the base loss, ProtoFair reduces equalized odds (EO) substantially across all target/sensitive-attribute pairs: EO drops from 30.5 to 14.2 on (Attractiveness, Male), from 16.9 to 6.9 on (Big Nose, Young), and to 1.9 on (Bags Under Eyes, Young), matching the dedicated fairness method FSCL (EO 1.8) while retaining 1.3 points higher accuracy. In the fully self-supervised setting (SimCLR, no target labels), ProtoFair reduces EO from 29.4 to 21.9 at higher accuracy than SimCLR+GRL (73.3% vs. 72.3%). On UTKFace, EO decreases from 10.6 to 6.6 at α=4, 8.4 to 4.8 at α=3, and 4.5 to 2.8 at α=2, with minimal accuracy loss. On NIH Chest X-rays, ProtoFair narrows the AUROC gap between sex groups from 0.03 to 0.01 and lowers EO from 1.87 to 1.79 while slightly improving overall AUROC (0.70→0.72).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dedicated fairness method FSCL [21], ProtoFair matches or approaches its EO on CelebA (e.g., EO 1.9 vs. 1.8 on Bags Under Eyes/Young) while retaining 1.3 points higher accuracy, and on UTKFace ProtoFair narrows the gap (EO 6.6 vs. 2.7 at α=4) but still trails FSCL's stronger fairness. Compared to adversarial debiasing baselines GRL [22] and LNL [15], ProtoFair achieves competitive or superior EO across most CelebA pairs while maintaining 1–3 points higher accuracy. Against the disentangled representation method FD-VAE [20] and the distillation method MFD [12], ProtoFair consistently delivers lower EO at higher accuracy. In the self-supervised (no target labels) setting, ProtoFair on SimCLR matches SimCLR+GRL [22] on EO (21.9) with 1 point higher accuracy, though FSCL* [21] still achieves the best EO (14.8) due to its specialized contrastive sampling. ProtoFair also extends to non-contrastive SSL objectives: on BarlowTwins [26] and BYOL [7], it reduces EO by 0.1–6.1 points across all four target/sensitive pairs at a minor accuracy cost of 0.1–1.6 points, confirming plug-and-play compatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtoFair repurposes the momentum-prototype clustering mechanism from Prototypical Contrastive Learning (PCL) [17] for a fundamentally different goal: instead of using clusters to improve representation quality, it uses cluster assignments as pseudo-content labels to construct pseudo-counterfactual pairs for a fairness regularizer. Conceptually, it draws on the counterfactual fairness framework of Kusner et al. [16] but avoids the need for a causal graph by approximating counterfactuals through unsupervised cluster co-membership. Unlike prior fairness methods for contrastive learning (FSCL [21], adversarial debiasing [15]) that replace or fundamentally alter the base SSL objective, ProtoFair is positioned as an additive plug-in term that leaves the original loss untouched, building on the cross-batch queue design of MoCo [9] to expand pair discovery beyond the mini-batch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design is practically significant: practitioners can add fairness to any existing SSL pipeline (SimCLR, SupCon, BarlowTwins, BYOL) without re-deriving or re-architecting the base objective, and the method requires only sensitive attribute annotations rather than target labels. A key limitation is the assumption that cluster assignments capture task-relevant content rather than sensitive attributes; if clusters become entangled with the sensitive group, the pseudo-counterfactual pairs become unreliable. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-attribute or continuous sensitive variables, whether it scales to larger backbones and datasets beyond the ResNet-18 / ~200K-image regime tested here, and whether the warmup-then-regularize schedule is optimal or whether joint training from the start could be more effective.
