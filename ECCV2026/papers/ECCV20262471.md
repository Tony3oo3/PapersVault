## Problem

Existing self-supervised pretraining methods for 3D medical imaging treat multi-phase CT scans (non-contrast and contrast-enhanced) as isolated samples, ignoring the rich anatomical correspondence inherent in paired acquisitions. Although contrast agents drastically shift voxel intensities, the underlying anatomical topology remains largely consistent across phases, creating a massive untapped supervisory signal. No prior framework has exploited this natural NCCT-CECT pairing to learn phase-invariant anatomical representations in an unsupervised manner.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired NCCT and CECT volumetric CT scans resampled to 1.5×0.7×0.7 mm spacing and cropped to 96×224×224 patches, with 75% block-wise masking applied to both inputs. The NCCT branch is the primary learning target while the CECT branch provides structure-amplified target features; the two are co-registered at the patient level. The output is a shared ResEnc U-Net 3D encoder whose multi-scale features (from the last three stages) can be fine-tuned with a U-Net decoder for segmentation or an MLP head for classification on either NCCT or CECT downstream tasks.

## Method

CAST employs a shared ResEnc U-Net encoder with a lightweight predictor that maps NCCT features into the CECT target feature space, optimized by three complementary losses. Beyond standard intra-phase MAE reconstruction, two feature-level constraints decouple anatomy from contrast shifts: (1) a Spectral Consistency module applies 3D Discrete Wavelet Transform to align low-frequency LLL subbands directly (preserving global anatomical context) while matching only the channel-wise energy of seven high-frequency subbands (capturing boundary presence without hallucinating contrast brightness); (2) a Geometry-Aware Topological Consistency module mines 1024 salient keypoints via 3D Top-Hat filtering of the NCCT-CECT difference map, constructs K=27 KNN relational graphs in the downsampled latent space, and aligns L2-normalized directional edge vectors to preserve local structural topology independent of phase-specific appearance. The total loss equally weights all three terms (λ=1.0 each), with hierarchical energy weights (20/10/5) penalizing boundary mismatches more heavily at finer resolutions.

## Results

On NCCT segmentation benchmarks, CAST achieves an average Dice of 73.06%, outperforming the strongest baseline (Models Genesis, 72.16%) by 1.24%, with notable gains on boundary-sensitive tasks like AIIB23 (91.63% vs. 90.53%) and SegThor (90.35% vs. 90.28%). On CECT segmentation, CAST reaches 81.24% average Dice, surpassing Spark3D (80.44%) by 0.80%, with top scores on MM-WHS (91.05%) and Parse22 (84.63%). For classification, CAST achieves 69.14% AUC on PE-NCCT, 76.78% on PE-CECT, and 93.63% on coronary stenosis, all leading among compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAST outperforms all 10 baselines on average across both NCCT and CECT segmentation tasks. On NCCT, the closest competitor is Models Genesis (72.16% avg. Dice), which CAST surpasses by 1.24%, with the largest margin on AIIB23 airway segmentation (91.63% vs. 90.53% for VolumeFusion). On CECT, Spark3D is the strongest baseline at 80.44% average Dice; CAST leads by 0.80% and achieves the best single-task scores on MM-WHS heart segmentation (91.05% vs. 90.97%) and Parse22 pulmonary vessels (84.63% vs. 84.57% for BrainMVP). For classification, CAST leads on all three tasks, with the most substantial margin on coronary stenosis (93.63% vs. 91.79% for VolumeFusion). Reconstruction-based methods (MAE, SwinUNETR) and contrastive methods (SimCLR, VoCo) consistently underperform CAST, while the closest cross-modal baseline BrainMVP trails by 1.64% on NCCT and 1.64% on CECT averages, confirming that true patient-level NCCT-CECT pairing provides supervision beyond generic multi-phase diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAST extends the 3D medical SSL line (MAE, VoCo, Spark3D, Models Genesis) by introducing cross-phase pairing as a supervisory signal, whereas all prior methods treat each volume as an isolated sample. It also departs from supervised cross-phase methods (CPMN, MULLET, cross-phase attention) by operating entirely in an unsupervised pretraining regime, using CECT as a structure-amplified privileged target rather than a co-input to a task-specific head. The spectral (3D DWT) and topological (KNN graph) consistency modules repurpose ideas from wavelet-based segmentation and GNN-based anatomical modeling, but combine them for the first time as unsupervised cross-phase alignment losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The released 13,850 paired NCCT-CECT dataset addresses a genuine scarcity of aligned multi-phase volumes and provides a reusable benchmark for cross-phase pretraining research. The phase-invariant representations benefit both NCCT and CECT downstream tasks, which is practically valuable since clinical workflows routinely produce both phases. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond chest CT to abdominal or whole-body protocols, whether the specific wavelet/graph hyperparameters transfer across anatomies, and whether the framework extends to other multi-phase modalities (e.g., multi-sequence MRI) where intensity shifts are even more pronounced.
