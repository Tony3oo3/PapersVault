## Problem

While query-level routing has been well-studied for text-only LLMs, extending it to multimodal LLMs introduces unique challenges—modality fusion, wide variation in computational cost across heterogeneous models, and the absence of a standardized, budget-aware evaluation protocol. No prior benchmark isolates the multimodal routing problem with fixed candidate sets, unified cost models, and modality-aware inputs, making it impossible to rigorously compare routing strategies or determine whether genuinely multimodal cues are required beyond unimodal signals.

## Input / Output

The routing system consumes a multimodal query (text prompt paired with an RGB image, or either modality alone as specified by a modality availability vector) plus an optional compute budget signal, and produces a probability distribution over K=10 candidate MLLMs from which a single model is selected to generate the response. Evaluation is entirely offline: the benchmark provides a precomputed outcome table of over 100k instance-model pairs with task-specific utilities in [0,1] and normalized monetary costs, so routers index into frozen scores rather than invoking models at test time. Routers may access only inference-time features (embeddings, token counts, scenario tags) and static model metadata, but never ground-truth labels or per-instance utilities on the evaluation split.

## Method

MMR-Bench constructs an offline, budget-aware evaluation environment spanning 11,000 instances across 10 heterogeneous MLLMs (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5, Claude, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, Gemma) and 8 datasets covering OCR, general VQA, and multimodal mathematical reasoning. For each instance, frozen ViT and language-encoder embeddings are fused via either equal-weight averaging or an adaptive scheme that estimates per-modality confidence and combines weighted sum, product, and absolute-difference interactions, exposing cross-modal agreement or mismatch. Routing policies—ranging from a linear scorer to k-NN retrieval, graph-based compatibility prediction, and regret-minimization with softmax objectives—are trained on a 2:8 split and evaluated by tracing a performance-cost curve parameterized by a cost weight λ, summarized via nAUC, Peak Score, and Quality-Neutral Cost. The key empirical finding is that multimodal fusion features consistently dominate unimodal variants on the cost-accuracy frontier, enabling the routed system to match the strongest single model's accuracy at roughly 33% of its cost, and that policies trained on a subset of models and tasks transfer zero-shot to unseen datasets and text-only benchmarks without retuning.

## Results

On the full-dataset average across all 8 benchmarks, the Linear router achieves the best nAUC of 0.7042 and peak score (P_s) of 0.7533, outperforming all other routing baselines. The Oracle upper bound reaches nAUC 0.8897 and P_s 0.9188, while the best single model attains P_s 0.7412. The paper's key efficiency claim is that the routed system matches the strongest single model's accuracy at roughly 33% of its cost, and surpasses it at higher budgets. Per-scenario, k-NNRouter leads on MathVista (nAUC 0.7807), AvengersPro leads on OCRBench (nAUC 0.9126), and RM-Softmax leads on MMStar and RealWorldQA (nAUC 0.7625 and 0.8123 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the four external routing baselines, Linear (a simple learned scorer) is the strongest on aggregate nAUC (0.7042), beating EmbedLLM (0.6913), AvengersPro (0.6885), GraphRouter (0.6754), and RM-Softmax (0.6927). However, the comparison is highly dataset-dependent: EmbedLLM wins P_s on MMStar (0.7817) and is competitive on OCRBench (nAUC 0.8952); AvengersPro dominates OCRBench nAUC (0.9126) and achieves the best QNC transition (∞→1.0585) under adaptive fusion; GraphRouter leads on MathVision nAUC (0.5734) but trails on all other datasets; RM-Softmax is best on RealWorldQA (nAUC 0.8123) and MMStar (nAUC 0.7625). No single external baseline dominates across all scenarios, and all four are outperformed by Linear on the full-dataset nAUC. The paper's key finding is that the choice of routing method matters less than the quality of the multimodal input features: adaptive fusion yields the largest gain for AvengersPro (ΔnAUC +0.3403) and consistent improvements for EmbedLLM and RM-Softmax, while k-NNRouter and GraphRouter benefit less from fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-only LLM routing literature (RouterBench, RouterEval, EmbedLLM, LLMRouterBench) to the multimodal setting, where routing must account for modality fusion, heterogeneous visual encoders, and cross-modal difficulty signals. It departs from intra-model routing approaches (MoE-LLaVA, AutoMix, dynamic expert paths) that select among experts within a single MLLM, instead targeting inter-model selection across a heterogeneous zoo of architecturally diverse MLLMs. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a modality availability vector and the demonstration that unimodal (text-only or image-only) routing signals are insufficient, requiring genuinely multimodal fusion features for optimal routing decisions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMR-Bench fills a clear gap as the first standardized, offline, cost-aware benchmark for MLLM routing, enabling reproducible comparison without re-running expensive models. The practical finding that routing matches the strongest single model's accuracy at roughly 33% of its cost is directly relevant to production MLLM deployment under budget constraints. Open questions include scaling to additional modalities (video, audio), online/adaptive routing with multiple model calls per query, and whether the offline precomputed-outcome paradigm generalizes to dynamic model pools where new MLLMs are continuously released. The benchmark's fixed 10-model candidate set and 11k-instance scale, while sufficient for initial comparison, may limit conclusions about routing at larger zoo sizes or with more diverse task distributions.
