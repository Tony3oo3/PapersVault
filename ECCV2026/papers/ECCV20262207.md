## Problem

State-of-the-art visual grounding VLMs require large model sizes (e.g., 235B parameters) to handle semantically complex prompts, making them impractical for edge deployment. The paper identifies that small VLMs (1B–8B) primarily fail on 'COMPLEX-PROMPT' cases—where the text instruction is semantically intricate and multiple similar candidates exist in the image—due to weaker language understanding rather than inferior visual encoding, since the ViT is shared across model sizes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262207/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a natural-language referring expression (e.g., 'the empty part of the blue plate on the left') and produces a free-form reasoning trace followed by a bounding box in coordinate format. For the amodal grounding variant, the output box must cover both the visible and occluded portions of the target object, requiring the reasoning to explicitly identify occluders and estimate extension directions. The reasoning trace is generated at inference time, adding latency but enabling the small model to resolve complex prompts that would otherwise cause mis-localization.

## Method

EGM equips small VLMs (1B–8B) with multimodal reasoning via a two-stage SFT-RL pipeline. In the SFT stage, a proprietary VLM generates detailed reasoning traces for each grounding sample (analyzing distinguishing features, spatial relations, and for amodal tasks, occlusion causes and extension directions), which are appended to the training data to teach the model a structured thinking pattern. In the RL stage, GRPO with token-level mean loss (DAPO-style) is applied using a reward that combines IoU and binary grounding success (α=0.5), with KL penalty and entropy regularization to prevent pattern collapse. RL training data is curated via a learnability filter: only samples where the learner model fails but a strong reference model (InternVL-3.5-241B) succeeds are included, mixed with equally-sized easy samples to prevent overfitting. The core efficiency insight is that many mid-quality reasoning tokens from a small model are cheaper end-to-end than a few high-quality tokens from a 235B model, yielding 5.9× lower latency at higher accuracy.

## Results

On the RefCOCO benchmark (8-split average accuracy), EGM-Qwen3-VL-8B achieves 91.4%, surpassing the much larger Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct (90.5%) and Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking (90.7%) while running 5.9× faster (737 ms vs. 4,320 ms GPU latency on L20). EGM consistently improves all tested small models: Qwen3-VL-2B/4B/8B-Thinking gain +6.0/+3.8/+3.6 points and InternVL-3-1B/2B/8B gain +5.2/+1.7/+1.1 points. On the newly introduced amodal grounding benchmark (COCO-Amodal), EGM-InternVL-3-8B improves from 62.0% to 73.5% (+11.5), and EGM-Qwen3-VL-2B improves from 66.5% to 73.4% (+6.9), matching or exceeding the 235B Qwen3-VL baseline (74.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen3-VL, EGM-Qwen3-VL-8B (91.4%) outperforms both Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct (90.5%) and Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking (90.7%) on average RefCOCO accuracy while achieving 5.9× lower GPU latency (737 ms vs. 4,320 ms); EGM-Qwen3-VL-4B (91.0%) also beats the 235B models. Against InternVL-3.5, EGM improves all three tested sizes (1B/2B/8B) by +5.2/+1.7/+1.1 points, with EGM-InternVL-3-8B (90.7%) approaching but not matching InternVL-3-78B (91.4%). Against proprietary models, EGM-Qwen3-VL-8B (91.4%) substantially outperforms Gemini-3-Pro (85.2%) and GPT-5 (40.1%) on RefCOCO, and EGM-InternVL-3-8B (73.5%) on amodal grounding exceeds Gemini-3-Pro (71.9%) and GPT-5 (24.7%). On the amodal grounding task, EGM-Qwen3-VL-2B (73.4%) nearly matches Qwen3-VL-235B (74.1%) while being orders of magnitude smaller, and EGM-InternVL-3-8B (73.5%) surpasses the 78B InternVL-3 baseline (51.0%) by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the SFT-then-GRPO reasoning paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1 [14] and DeepSeekMath [32] to the visual grounding task, incorporating the token-level mean loss from DAPO [44]. Its key conceptual departure is a diagnostic finding: small VLMs underperform large ones primarily due to text-interpretation failures on complex prompts rather than visual-encoding limitations, motivating test-time compute (reasoning tokens) as the remedy. The paper also extends the grounding task to amodal prediction (recovering occluded object extents), a capability absent from prior VLM grounding work, and constructs the first VLM-level amodal grounding benchmark from COCO-Amodal and TAO-Amodal data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge deployment: an 8B model matching or exceeding a 235B model at 5.9× lower latency directly addresses the cost-latency tradeoff in robotics and autonomous driving. The amodal grounding benchmark opens a new evaluation axis for VLMs that prior proprietary and open-source models have not addressed. Open questions include whether the reliance on proprietary VLMs for SFT data curation limits reproducibility, whether the reasoning-token overhead degrades in real-time streaming settings, and whether the COMPLEX-PROMPT diagnosis generalizes to other VLM tasks beyond grounding.
