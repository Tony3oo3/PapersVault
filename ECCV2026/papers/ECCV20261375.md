## Problem

Existing on-the-fly category discovery methods decide whether to assign a sample to an existing category or spawn a new one via fixed-radius or hash-based matching rules, treating birth as an implicit fallback from failed assignment rather than an explicit hypothesis with its own statistical evidence. These rules also impose isotropic, prototype-centered decision regions that ignore anisotropic feature geometry, and use fixed thresholds that do not contract as category evidence accumulates, making early false births hard to correct.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single-pass stream of RGB images, each encoded to a 768-dimensional feature vector by a frozen DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 backbone fine-tuned on a labeled support set (50% of known-class samples). For each arriving sample, the output is a discrete decision—assign to one of the existing categories or birth a new one—followed by an online update of that category's sufficient statistics. No ground-truth labels are available at test time; the only supervision is the offline support set used to calibrate the global prior and warm-start known-category posteriors.

## Method

DP-BOA formulates the assign-versus-birth choice as a Bayesian evidence comparison: for each incoming feature, it computes the posterior-predictive score for every existing category (a multivariate Student-t density under a Normal-Inverse-Wishart posterior) weighted by its Dirichlet-process Polya Urn prior n_k/(α+N), and compares this against the birth score (a Student-t density under the global NIW prior) weighted by α/(α+N). The NIW hyperparameters are calibrated via empirical Bayes from the labeled support set, and known categories are warm-started with their posterior statistics, so the birth hypothesis is governed by a data-driven standard rather than a fixed radius. Full-covariance Gaussians capture anisotropic category geometry, and conjugate NIW updates cause predictive uncertainty to contract naturally as evidence accumulates. A lightweight variant (DP-BOA-L) replaces full covariance with a rank-r Frequent Directions approximation, reducing per-sample cost from O(d³+Kd²) to O(Kdr+dr²) with only a small accuracy drop.

## Results

DP-BOA achieves the best All-class accuracy on 8 out of 10 OCD benchmarks without any external knowledge. Headline numbers include 53.4% All / 51.6% Novel on CUB, 59.0% All / 56.6% Novel on Oxford-IIIT Pet, and 50.7% All / 43.7% Novel on Animalia. On novel-class discovery specifically, DP-BOA gains +10.7 points over the next-best method (Sync) on CUB and +14.0 points over DiffGRE on Mollusca. The only dataset where DP-BOA does not lead in All accuracy is ImageNet100 (33.8% vs. Sync's 44.0%), where Sync relies on CLIP and text.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest pure-visual baselines SMILE [11] and PHE [57], DP-BOA wins on all 10 datasets in All accuracy, with the largest margins on fine-grained data (e.g., CUB 53.4 vs. 36.4 for PHE, +17.0; Mollusca 48.9 vs. 39.9, +9.0). On novel-class accuracy the gap widens further: DP-BOA reaches 51.6% on CUB vs. 27.0% for PHE (+24.6) and 46.3% on Mollusca vs. 26.5% for PHE (+19.8). Against methods that use external knowledge, DP-BOA still leads on 6 of the 8 overlapping datasets; it trails Sync [3] on ImageNet100 (33.8 vs. 44.0 All) and OxfordPets (59.0 vs. 61.6 All), where Sync leverages CLIP text embeddings. Against DiffGRE [28], DP-BOA wins on every shared dataset, most notably on Mollusca Novel (46.3 vs. 32.3, +14.0) and CUB Novel (51.6 vs. 36.5, +15.1). The older baselines SLC [18], RankStat [16], and WTA [22] are consistently outperformed by 10–30+ points in All accuracy across all ten benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Dirichlet-Process Gaussian Mixture Model (DP-GMM) with Normal-Inverse-Wishart conjugate priors—originally developed for unsupervised density estimation (Rasmussen 2000; Blackwell & MacQueen 1973)—to the supervised, single-pass On-the-Fly Category Discovery (OCD) setting. It departs from prior OCD methods (SMILE, PHE, DiffGRE, Sync), which implement the assign-versus-birth decision through fixed-shape matching rules (Hamming balls, Euclidean radii, angular caps) that treat category birth as an implicit fallback from failed assignment. The key conceptual shift is formulating birth as an explicit hypothesis with its own posterior-predictive evidence, scored within the same Bayesian decision framework as assignment, and using labeled support-set statistics to calibrate the shared NIW prior and warm-start known-category posteriors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DP-BOA provides a principled, evidence-driven alternative to heuristic matching rules for streaming category discovery, with the DP prior naturally yielding calibrated cluster counts (175/177 vs. 200/196 ground truth on CUB/StanfordCars, versus 924 for SMILE) and the NIW posterior-predictive mechanism allowing uncertainty to contract as evidence accumulates. The finding that a simple supervised cross-entropy encoder combined with this probabilistic head outperforms methods relying on more sophisticated representation learning or external knowledge (CLIP, diffusion models) suggests the decision mechanism itself is the bottleneck in current OCD. Open questions include whether single-Gaussian-per-category modeling suffices for streams with multi-modal or non-elliptical intra-class structure, whether the fixed hyperparameter defaults (α, n₀) can be made stream-adaptive, and how the full-covariance overhead scales to very high-dimensional or very large-K regimes beyond what DP-BOA-L addresses.
