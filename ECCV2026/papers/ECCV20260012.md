## Problem

Existing feed-forward Gaussian splatting models are limited to a single forward pass, bounding their quality by network capacity and making them fragile to out-of-distribution inputs (unseen datasets, view counts, resolutions). While per-scene optimization (3DGS) achieves high quality through iterative gradient updates, it requires thousands of steps and is 100× slower. The paper asks whether a learned recurrent network can capture the benefits of iterative refinement in a gradient-free, feed-forward manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260012/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N posed RGB images (2–32 views) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, at resolutions ranging from 256×256 to 540×960. It outputs a compact set of 3D Gaussian primitives (position, opacity, covariance, spherical harmonics) with 16× fewer Gaussians than per-pixel baselines (N×HW/16 instead of N×H×W), which are rasterized for real-time novel view synthesis. At inference, the user can choose 1–4 recurrent refinement iterations to trade off speed against quality with a single trained model.

## Method

ReSplat operates in two stages. First, a compact initialization model (based on DepthSplat with a ViT backbone) predicts per-view depth at 1/4 resolution, unprojects to a 16× subsampled 3D point cloud, and uses alternating kNN and global attention blocks to decode initial Gaussian parameters plus a hidden state. Second, a weight-sharing recurrent network iteratively refines the Gaussians: at each step it renders the input views, computes a combined pixel-space and ResNet-18 feature-space rendering error, propagates this error to all Gaussians via global attention, and predicts incremental parameter updates using kNN attention over the concatenation of current Gaussians, hidden state, and aggregated error. The key insight is that the rendering error acts as a rich, data-adaptive feedback signal that requires no explicit gradient computation, enabling the network to correct its own predictions and generalize across domains. Training is two-stage (initialization, then recurrent model end-to-end with exponentially weighted rendering loss), and the 16× subsampling keeps the 3D-space recurrent updates computationally tractable while yielding 4× faster rendering.

## Results

On DL3DV with 8 input views at 512×960, ReSplat (4 recurrent iterations) achieves 27.70 dB PSNR, 0.868 SSIM, and 0.160 LPIPS using only 246K Gaussians and 0.82 s reconstruction time — outperforming optimization-based 3DGS (23.46 dB at 4K iterations, 70 s) by 4.2 dB PSNR and per-pixel feed-forward DepthSplat (24.17 dB, 3932K Gaussians) by 3.5 dB. On the 16-view 540×960 DL3DV setting, ReSplat reaches 23.51 dB PSNR with 518K Gaussians, surpassing Long-LRM (22.69 dB, 2073K Gaussians) by 0.8 dB with 4× fewer Gaussians. On the 2-view 256×256 RealEstate10K benchmark, ReSplat achieves 29.75 dB PSNR, outperforming all prior feed-forward 3DGS methods by at least 1.2 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReSplat decisively outperforms all optimization-based baselines: it beats 3DGS by 4.2 dB PSNR (27.70 vs 23.46) while being 100× faster (0.82 s vs 70 s), and surpasses Mip-Splatting (20.88 dB) and Scaffold-GS (22.13 dB) on the 16-view setting by 2.6 and 1.4 dB respectively. Among feed-forward per-pixel methods, ReSplat improves over DepthSplat by 3.5 dB and MVSplat by 5.2 dB PSNR on 8-view DL3DV, while using 16× fewer Gaussians (246K vs 3932K) and rendering 4× faster. On the 16-view 540×960 benchmark, ReSplat outperforms Long-LRM by 0.8 dB PSNR (23.51 vs 22.69) with 4× fewer Gaussians (518K vs 2073K). On the 2-view RealEstate10K setting, ReSplat (29.75 dB) beats GS-LRM (28.10 dB) by 1.65 dB, Long-LRM (28.54 dB) by 1.2 dB, and is competitive with LVSM's decoder-only variant (29.67 dB) while offering a 20× faster explicit 3D Gaussian representation. Cross-dataset generalization to ACID shows ReSplat leading GS-LRM by 1.03 dB (29.87 vs 28.84). Against SplatFormer, ReSplat achieves 2.04 dB higher PSNR (29.07 vs 27.03) on scene-level data, where SplatFormer's object-centric design struggles with unbounded scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReSplat builds directly on the DepthSplat architecture for its initial Gaussian reconstruction and the 3DGS representation for rendering, but departs from the single-step feed-forward paradigm dominant in this line of work (MVSplat, DepthSplat, GS-LRM, Long-LRM, AnySplat, WorldMirror) by introducing a weight-sharing recurrent refinement loop. It draws on the 'learning to optimize' literature (RAFT, DROID-SLAM, ITeMS) where iterative updates with a shared network outperform single-shot regression, but identifies the Gaussian rendering error as the feedback signal rather than feature correlations or explicit gradients (as in G3R or DeepView). The 16× subsampled 3D space with kNN and global attention for context aggregation is a key architectural departure from per-pixel Gaussian models, and the method is positioned as a scene-level generalization of SplatFormer's object-centric refinement approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReSplat offers a compelling efficiency-quality trade-off: 100× faster than per-scene 3DGS optimization, 4× faster rendering than per-pixel feed-forward models, and 16× fewer Gaussians, while achieving state-of-the-art PSNR across DL3DV, RealEstate10K, and ACID. The rendering-error feedback mechanism provides a principled explanation for the observed cross-dataset, cross-resolution, and cross-view-count generalization, suggesting a path toward adaptive test-time compute for 3D reconstruction. Key open questions include whether the fixed Gaussian count can be relaxed with adaptive pruning/densification, how to extend the recurrent loop beyond its current 4-iteration saturation point, and whether the approach generalizes to streaming or dynamic scenes.
