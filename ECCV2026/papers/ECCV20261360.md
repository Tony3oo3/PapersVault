## Problem

Pre-trained vision encoders (e.g., DINOv3, SigLIPv2) are trained exclusively on 2D image data and consequently lack 3D spatial awareness, leading to sub-optimal performance on tasks requiring geometric understanding such as depth estimation, 3D scene reasoning, and vision-based robot control. Existing remedies—multi-view pretraining or 3D-feature prediction—require carefully curated multi-view datasets or simulation environments, limiting scalability. The paper asks whether dense 3D spatial knowledge can be injected into existing encoders using linguistic descriptions as a scalable supervision signal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes single-view RGB images (100K from SA1B) and multi-view image pairs (200K from Ego4d, ScanNet, DTU, Mip-NeRF 360, NeRF), from which 3D point clouds are extracted via depth estimation (Depth Pro), segmentation (SAM 2), and 3D reconstruction (VGGT) models. These point clouds are converted into 12-turn multi-turn spatial reasoning conversations spanning pixel-level depth queries, object-level bounding-cube and relative-position questions, and scene-level inter-object distance questions, plus a final general scene caption. The output is the enhanced feature vectors from the vision encoder's final layer, which serve as drop-in replacements for the original encoder in any downstream linear-probing or DPT-head pipeline.

## Method

SpatialBoost is a three-stage training pipeline that couples a frozen LLM (Qwen-2.0-7B) with a pre-trained ViT encoder through a trainable MLP projector. Stage 1 aligns the projector to the LLM's token space; Stage 2 fine-tunes the projector and LLM on multi-view VQA data; Stage 3 fine-tunes the vision encoder on the multi-turn spatial reasoning dataset while keeping the LLM frozen. The key architectural innovation is a dual-channel attention module: each original attention layer is paired with a twin layer initialized identically, and their outputs are merged via a per-dimension learnable sigmoid gate α (zero-initialized), so only the twin parameters and α are updated—preserving pre-trained knowledge while gradually incorporating spatial information. The multi-turn CoT dataset is hierarchically ordered (pixel → object → scene), and ablations confirm that this forward ordering outperforms shuffled or reversed sequences, and that combining single-view and multi-view data yields complementary gains over either alone.

## Results

SpatialBoost consistently improves pre-trained vision encoders across all evaluated tasks. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, DINOv3 + SpatialBoost achieves 59.7 mIoU (linear probing), a 3.8-point gain over DINOv3 (55.9), setting a new state-of-the-art. On 3D scene understanding (SQA3D), DINOv3 + SpatialBoost improves from 51.4 to 54.9 BLEU-1, and on NYUd depth estimation, DINOv3's RMSE drops from 0.31 to 0.25. Notably, general vision capabilities also improve: DINOv3's ImageNet-1K linear probing accuracy rises from 88.4% to 90.2%, and CortexBench robot learning average score increases from 72.8 to 80.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialBoost applied to DINOv3 achieves the best or near-best results across all task categories, outperforming every baseline on ADE20K segmentation (59.7 vs. 51.3 for V-JEPAv2, 50.6 for dino.txt, 49.9 for TIPS, 41.5 for PE-Core) and SQA3D 3D reasoning (54.9 vs. 51.7 for PE-Core, 50.4 for dino.txt). On NYUd depth estimation, DINOv3 + SpatialBoost (0.25 RMSE) ties PE-Core (0.25) but surpasses all other baselines including TIPS (0.35) and DINOv2 (0.37). On ImageNet-1K classification, DINOv3 + SpatialBoost (90.2%) edges out PE-Core (89.5%) and SigLIPv2 + SpatialBoost (90.0%), confirming that spatial knowledge injection does not degrade general recognition. On robot learning (CortexBench), DINOv3 + SpatialBoost (80.8 avg) substantially outperforms PE-Core (69.0) and TIPS (66.0), highlighting the practical benefit of enhanced spatial representations for embodied tasks. The only notable exception is image retrieval on Met (GAP), where PE-Core (15.9) and TIPS (20.1) underperform DINOv3 + SpatialBoost (57.0), but DINOv3 + SpatialBoost still leads all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpatialBoost builds on the LLaVA two-stage alignment pipeline (feature alignment followed by visual instruction tuning) and repurposes the dual-channel attention mechanism from CogVideo to prevent catastrophic forgetting during spatial fine-tuning. It departs from prior multi-view learning approaches (MV-MWM, time-contrastive networks) that require carefully curated multi-view data or simulation environments, and from joint text-image pretraining methods (CLIP, SigLIP, dino.txt, TIPS) that demand training both modalities from scratch. The key conceptual departure is using an LLM as a frozen decoder to convert dense 3D spatial information—extracted via depth estimation, segmentation, and 3D reconstruction—into multi-turn linguistic reasoning, thereby injecting spatial knowledge into existing encoders without retraining them jointly with a text encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpatialBoost offers a practical, plug-and-play recipe for upgrading any pre-trained vision encoder with 3D spatial awareness, demonstrated consistently across DINOv3, DINOv2, SigLIPv2, and OpenCLIP on six task families. The finding that LLM-based language supervision outperforms direct pixel-level 3D decoders (VGGT, SAM) for spatial tasks challenges the assumption that geometric supervision must be pixel-aligned to be effective. Open questions include whether the linguistic bottleneck limits precision for fine-grained spatial tasks, how the approach scales to even larger encoders or real-time robotic deployment, and whether the 300K-sample dataset is sufficient or whether further scaling yields diminishing returns.
