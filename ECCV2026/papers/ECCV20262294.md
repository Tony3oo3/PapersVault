## Problem

Curvilinear structures (vessels, cracks, neuronal branches) are extremely sparse and topologically fragile, yet modern segmentation pipelines rely on frozen foundation encoders whose heavy downsampling destroys fine spatial detail. Two recurring post-encoder failure modes—passive isotropic upsampling that blurs thin structures and rigid global thresholding that either severs faint branches or admits noise—remain unaddressed by existing plug-in adapters that operate without explicit morphological or target-specific conditioning.

## Input / Output

PEPA is a post-encoder plug-in that consumes low-resolution decoder/head features (e.g., 16× downsampled ViT tokens) together with a target descriptor (prompted mask token for SAM variants, learnable class embedding for semantic models) and produces a high-resolution binary segmentation mask. At test time, a strict GT-derived bounding box prompt is used for all quantitative evaluation; during training, prompts are randomly sampled from box, 10-point, or noisy-mask inputs following the SAM-HQ protocol. The adapter adds only ~0.26M parameters and ~0.22G FLOPs on top of the frozen encoder, with end-to-end latency increasing from 99.0 ms to 103.0 ms at 1024×1024 resolution.

## Method

PEPA couples two target-adaptive modules. TCSU replaces standard upsampling with continuous snake-shaped sampling: it predicts a dynamic odd chain length conditioned on the target descriptor, accumulates incremental deformation offsets from the center outward along X-major and Y-major snake paths, differentially samples features at the resulting subpixel coordinates, and fuses the result with a bilinear shortcut via 1D depthwise convolutions. TADT predicts a bounded per-target threshold in the logit domain and replaces the non-differentiable step function with a smooth sigmoid surrogate, enabling end-to-end optimization of the binarization boundary. To prevent degenerate bias-shifting, TADT applies three safeguards: spatial logit centering to isolate relative confidence, computing topology-aware losses (clDice) directly on the soft surrogate so the threshold is guided by structural connectivity, and a local perturbation consistency loss that penalizes disagreement between surrogates evaluated at threshold ± Δ. The composite training objective combines BCE, Dice, clDice, and consistency terms, all evaluated on the binarization surrogate, while the massive foundation encoder remains strictly frozen.

## Results

PEPA, a ~0.26M-parameter post-encoder plug-in, yields average absolute gains of +2.6% IoU and +2.8% clDice when attached to frozen VFM encoders across five curvilinear segmentation benchmarks. The most striking gains appear in topological connectivity: on CHASEDB1, adding PEPA to SAM and MedSAM boosts clDice by +5.6% and +6.0% respectively, while IoU gains are more modest (+4.0% and +4.4%). In direct comparison against fully fine-tuned domain-specific architectures, PEPA SAM achieves the best clDice on four of five datasets (e.g., 85.3% on XCAD, 85.5% on CHASEDB1) while keeping the ViT-B encoder entirely frozen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEPA SAM (frozen ViT-B encoder + PEPA adapter) outperforms all fully fine-tuned domain-specific baselines on XCAD, CHUAC, CHASEDB1, and Crack500 in both IoU and clDice; the largest margin is over TVS-Net (+1.7 IoU, +2.1 clDice on XCAD) and Mid-Net (+2.6 IoU, +3.0 clDice on XCAD). Against the closest prompt-based competitor FPBE SAM [16], PEPA SAM consistently wins on clDice (+2.2% on XCAD, +2.3% on CHASEDB1, +1.1% on CHUAC) while matching or slightly exceeding IoU, confirming a topology-specific advantage. On DRIVE, HM-Mamba [42] achieves a marginally higher clDice (83.2 vs. 84.2 for PEPA SAM—actually PEPA SAM wins here too at 84.2), and GCC-UNet [43] is competitive on DRIVE IoU (71.4 vs. 72.8) but trails substantially on all other datasets. The plug-and-play ablation (Table 1) shows PEPA improves every tested VFM backbone: SAM (+2.9 IoU, +3.2 clDice avg), SAM-HQ (+2.7, +3.4), MedSAM (+2.9, +3.1), EfficientSAM (+3.0, +3.5), DINOv3+Mask2Former (+2.0, +1.8), and DINOv3+MaskDINO (+2.1, +1.9), with the largest clDice jumps on thin-structure datasets (CHASEDB1: +5.6 to +6.0). Under a matched 0.26M parameter budget, PEPA outperforms encoder-side LoRA by +1.7 clDice on average, validating the post-encoder design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

PEPA sits at the intersection of two established lines: differentiable binarization (originating from DBNet for scene text detection) and snake convolution for tubular structure extraction. The paper repurposes snake convolution from a target-agnostic feature extractor into a target-conditioned reconstruction operator (TCSU), and extends differentiable binarization from single-object text detection to dense multi-target curvilinear segmentation with explicit anti-degeneration safeguards (TADT). It also positions itself against the growing family of VFM feature upsamplers (CARAFE, DySample, AnyUp, LoftUp), arguing that their globally-attended, morphology-agnostic reconstruction is insufficient for fragile curvilinear topologies. The overall framing—treating the post-encoder stage as the locus of two decouplable bottlenecks (reconstruction and decision) rather than modifying the frozen encoder—distinguishes PEPA from both encoder-side PEFT (LoRA) and decoder-only fine-tuning approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PEPA offers a genuinely lightweight (~0.26M params, ~4 ms latency) plug-in that can be attached to any frozen VFM pipeline with accessible target descriptors, making it immediately practical for practitioners who cannot afford full fine-tuning of large encoders. The consistent pattern of clDice gains exceeding IoU gains across all six backbones and five datasets provides empirical validation that post-encoder structural recovery is a distinct and addressable failure mode. Open questions include whether the target-conditioning mechanism generalizes to 3D curvilinear structures (e.g., vascular trees, neuronal morphologies), whether the approach extends to fully unsupervised or open-vocabulary settings where no explicit target descriptor is available, and whether the snake-sampling inductive bias remains beneficial for non-curvilinear but still topology-sensitive targets (e.g., airway branching).
