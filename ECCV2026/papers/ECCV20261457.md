## Problem

Streaming feed-forward 3D reconstruction models process frames sequentially with limited historical context and lack explicit mechanisms to identify dynamic objects, causing camera drift and geometric artifacts in scenes with moving content. Existing dynamic-scene approaches rely on external flow estimators or segmentation modules that add overhead and generalize poorly. This paper asks whether the internal representations of RayMap-trained streaming models already contain a usable signal for dynamic identification without any additional training or external models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous stream of RGB images along with known camera intrinsics, processing one frame at a time with constant GPU memory (~9.4 GB) regardless of sequence length. At each timestep it produces a camera pose (rotation + translation), a per-pixel depth map with confidence, and a 3D point cloud. Unusually, the inference is dual-branch: the main branch decodes from image + RayMap tokens while a second RayMap-only branch decodes from pose-derived RayMap tokens against the same frozen state, and the per-pixel depth discrepancy between the two branches is the internal signal used to gate memory updates.

## Method

RayMap3R is a training-free inference-time framework built on CUT3R's recurrent implicit-memory architecture. Its core mechanism is a dual-branch scheme: the main branch predicts depth and pose from image + RayMap tokens, then the predicted pose is remapped into a new RayMap that feeds a second branch querying the same frozen state, producing a static-biased prediction; the per-pixel relative depth difference between branches is pooled into image-token scores via confidence-weighted averaging, projected to state-token scores via decoder cross-attention, and converted to staticness weights through a sigmoid of a robustly normalized (median/IQR) score that gates the state update (s_t = s_{t-1} + α_t ⊙ Δs_t). Two auxiliary components stabilize long sequences: reset metric alignment estimates a Sim(3) transformation from the repeated frame before and after each memory reset to correct scale/pose misalignment, and state-aware smoothing adaptively filters inter-frame displacements using a coefficient β_t = 1/(1 + λ|a_t × sc_t|) that combines trajectory acceleration with the magnitude of proposed state change, attenuating jitter during high-uncertainty intervals while preserving steady motion.

## Results

On camera pose estimation (Table 2), RayMap3R achieves the lowest ATE among all streaming methods across all three datasets: 0.166 on Sintel (vs. 0.208 for CUT3R and 0.210 for TTT3R), 0.018 on TUM-dynamics (vs. 0.019 for TTT3R), and 0.064 on ScanNet (vs. 0.065 for TTT3R). For video depth estimation (Table 1), RayMap3R leads streaming methods on KITTI (Abs Rel 0.098 per-sequence, 0.104 metric-scale) and Bonn (0.057/0.085), though StreamVGGT achieves lower Sintel per-sequence error (0.323 vs. 0.401). On 3D reconstruction (Table 3), RayMap3R attains the best streaming accuracy (mean 0.023) and Chamfer distance (mean 0.024) on 7-Scenes. The method runs at 13.8 FPS with 9.4 GB GPU memory, maintaining constant memory across 50 to 1000 views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among streaming baselines, RayMap3R outperforms CUT3R on all pose metrics across all three datasets, with the largest gap on Sintel ATE (0.166 vs. 0.208, a 20% reduction) and TUM-dynamics RPE_r (0.287° vs. 0.303°). Against TTT3R, the closest streaming competitor, RayMap3R wins on Sintel ATE (0.166 vs. 0.210) and TUM-dynamics ATE/RPE_t/RPE_r (0.018/0.005/0.287 vs. 0.019/0.008/0.292), while TTT3R edges ahead on ScanNet ATE by a negligible 0.001. Spann3R, Point3R, and StreamVGGT are substantially outperformed on all dynamic-scene metrics, with StreamVGGT showing the worst TUM-dynamics ATE (0.061) and ScanNet ATE (0.161) among streaming methods. On depth estimation, RayMap3R leads all streaming methods on KITTI and Bonn under both per-sequence and metric-scale protocols, but StreamVGGT achieves lower Sintel per-sequence Abs Rel (0.323 vs. 0.401) at the cost of memory that scales with sequence length. Against offline methods (DUSt3R, MASt3R, MonST3R, VGGT), RayMap3R is outperformed on static ScanNet ATE (0.064 vs. VGGT's 0.035) and Sintel ATE (0.166 vs. MonST3R's 0.111), but matches or approaches their accuracy on TUM-dynamics while operating at real-time speed with constant memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

RayMap3R builds directly on CUT3R's recurrent implicit-memory architecture for streaming 3D reconstruction and the RayMap per-pixel camera-ray representation introduced in 'Cameras as Rays.' It departs from the dominant paradigm for handling dynamic scenes—external flow estimators, learned segmentation, or tracking modules—by instead exploiting an emergent static-scene bias in RayMap-only predictions as a training-free dynamic identification signal. The dual-branch inference scheme, reset metric alignment, and state-aware smoothing are all inference-time additions that require no retraining, positioning the work as a lightweight extension of the CUT3R/TTT3R streaming lineage rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free dynamic identification via RayMap static bias is practically appealing: it adds no external modules, no retraining, and no domain-specific dependencies, yet achieves SOTA among streaming methods on dynamic benchmarks. However, the approach is tightly coupled to CUT3R's specific architecture and its RayMap-only querying capability, and the dual-branch design incurs a ~30% speed penalty (13.8 vs. 19.7 FPS). Open questions include whether the static bias generalizes to other RayMap-based backbones (e.g., offline feed-forward models), how the method behaves in scenes with minimal static content, and whether the moderate IoU (~0.2) of the dynamic map limits its utility for fine-grained dynamic object reasoning beyond soft memory gating.
