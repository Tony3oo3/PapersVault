## Problem

Most existing rPPG methods rely predominantly on time-domain modeling, making them vulnerable to motion artifacts and illumination fluctuations where weak physiological signals are overwhelmed by noise. While a few recent works incorporate spectral cues as auxiliary losses or representation enhancements, none explicitly leverage the intrinsic physiological frequency priors—namely the band constraint ([0.66, 3.0] Hz) and dominant peak property—that characterize cardiac-driven rPPG signals to guide the denoising process itself.

## Input / Output

The method takes a facial video clip and preprocesses it into a multi-scale temporal map (MSTmap) of shape T×N×C (300×63×C) via landmark alignment and pixel-level average pooling over color channels. It outputs a clean rPPG signal of length T=300, from which heart rate, HRV (LF, HF, LF/HF), and respiration frequency are derived. The diffusion process uses K=1000 steps with DDIM sampling at inference, and the model is remarkably lightweight at 0.86M parameters with 11.70 ms inference latency on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

FreqPhys is a frequency-aware conditional diffusion framework that integrates a three-stage Physiological Frequency Denoiser (PFD) directly into every diffusion denoising step: (i) a Physiological Bandpass Filter (PBF) applies an ideal bandpass mask in the DFT domain to remove out-of-band noise outside [0.66, 3.0] Hz; (ii) a Physiological Spectrum Modulation (PSM) block learns complex-valued weights to modulate real and imaginary spectral components, which by the frequency-domain convolution theorem is equivalent to a content-adaptive circular convolution in time; and (iii) an Adaptive Spectrum Selection (ASS) module uses a learnable energy threshold with the Straight-Through Estimator to dynamically suppress residual in-band noise. A Cross-domain Representation Learning module then fuses the denoised spectral representation with time-domain features via L=4 alternating cross-attention layers. The reverse diffusion process is conditioned on both the time-domain MSTmap and the physiological frequency condition C^P, and the paper proves (Proposition 1) that this additional frequency conditioning strictly reduces the conditional entropy of the posterior, yielding more accurate signal reconstruction. Training uses a combined Pearson correlation loss in the time domain and MSE loss on the DFT spectra.

## Results

On the six intra-dataset benchmarks, FreqPhys achieves the best RMSE across all datasets: 0.41 on UBFC-rPPG, 0.25 on PURE, 1.31 on BUAA, 6.78 on MMPD, 6.34 on VIPL-HR, and 6.08 on MR-NIRP-Car. The most dramatic gains appear on the hardest datasets—BUAA (extreme illumination) where FreqPhys reduces RMSE from 7.89 (PHASE-Net) to 1.31, and MR-NIRP-Car (real driving) where it cuts RMSE from 8.58 (PhysDiff) to 6.08. In cross-dataset transfer (e.g., PURE→MMPD), FreqPhys achieves RMSE 14.34 vs. 16.70 for RhythmMamba, confirming stronger domain generalization. The method also leads on HRV (LF/HF r=0.989) and RF (r=0.845) estimation on UBFC-rPPG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreqPhys outperforms all 22 baselines in RMSE on every dataset where it is evaluated. The largest margins are on the most challenging datasets: on BUAA (extreme illumination), FreqPhys achieves RMSE 1.31 versus 7.89 for PHASE-Net and 14.22 for RhythmMamba; on MR-NIRP-Car (real driving), it reaches 6.08 versus 8.58 for PhysDiff and 10.03 for RhythmMamba. On the controlled UBFC-rPPG and PURE benchmarks, FreqPhys ties Dual-TL at RMSE 0.41 on UBFC-rPPG but clearly leads on PURE (0.25 vs. 0.34 for RhythmMamba and 0.54 for PhysDiff). Among diffusion-based competitors, FreqPhys consistently beats PhysDiff by 0.31–2.50 RMSE across datasets, confirming that frequency-domain conditioning adds value beyond time-domain diffusion alone. In cross-dataset transfer (Table 3), FreqPhys also leads all baselines, e.g., PURE→MMPD RMSE 14.34 vs. 16.70 (RhythmMamba) and UBFC-rPPG→MMPD RMSE 12.86 vs. 17.14 (RhythmMamba), indicating that the physiological frequency prior captures domain-invariant cardiac structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreqPhys extends the diffusion-based rPPG line initiated by PhysDiff (AAAI 2025) and the foundational DDPM framework, but departs from both by injecting physiological frequency priors directly into the iterative denoising process rather than treating them as auxiliary losses or post-hoc spectral regularizers. Prior frequency-aware rPPG methods (PhysFormer++, ContrastPhys+, RhythmMamba) incorporate spectral cues only at the training-loss or representation level, whereas FreqPhys applies a three-stage frequency denoiser (bandpass filtering → complex spectral modulation → adaptive threshold selection) at every diffusion step as an internal structural constraint. The cross-domain representation learning module further distinguishes the work by fusing frequency-domain priors with time-domain features via alternating cross-attention, a design not present in prior diffusion-based rPPG pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly encoding the two well-known spectral properties of cardiac signals (band constraint and dominant peak) as an online denoising constraint yields meaningful robustness gains, particularly under motion and illumination variation where time-domain methods struggle most. The plug-in generality of the PFD module—showing consistent improvements when grafted onto RhythmMamba and PhysDiff—suggests a broadly applicable component for the rPPG community. Open questions include whether the fixed [0.66, 3.0] Hz band generalizes to atypical heart-rate populations (athletes, children, tachycardia), whether the diffusion framework is strictly necessary versus a simpler frequency-guided regression, and how the approach scales to multi-signal estimation (HR + HRV + RF simultaneously) without per-signal band tuning.
