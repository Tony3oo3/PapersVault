## Problem

Weakly-supervised video scene graph generation relies on off-the-shelf detectors that indiscriminately detect all visible objects, unlike fully-supervised detectors that implicitly filter for relation-relevant ones. This shifts the burden of filtering non-interactive pairs onto the relation model, which is trained exclusively on matched (interactive) pairs yet must rank among a detection space dominated by non-interactive pairs at inference, creating a severe train-test distribution gap that prior WS-VSGG methods leave unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw video frames and, during training only, unlocalized triplets (subject class, predicate, object class) annotated solely on the middle frame of each clip. An off-the-shelf detector (VinVL) generates object proposals for all frames, and a one-time preprocessing step (RAM) uses GroundingDINO with relation-aware text queries to refine pseudo-labels. At inference, the model outputs per-frame scene graph triplets with bounding boxes and a scalar pair affinity score per subject-object pair, which modulates the final triplet ranking.

## Method

The framework introduces three synergistic components. First, Relation-Aware Matching (RAM) constructs relation-aware text queries from each triplet (e.g., 'cup that the person is holding') and uses GroundingDINO's [CLS] cross-attention map to select the most relation-consistent detection among same-class candidates, with a spatial-concentration reliability score gating whether grounded or class-level matching is applied. Second, Pair Affinity Learning and Scoring (PALS) introduces a separate pair affinity embedding P alongside the relation embedding R, both jointly refined through spatial and temporal attention layers; P is trained with a class-balanced binary cross-entropy loss over matched and unmatched pairs, and at inference the resulting PA score is multiplied into the triplet ranking to suppress non-interactive pairs. Third, Pair Affinity Modulation (PAM) constructs a pairwise affinity matrix G_i = P_i P_i^T within each attention block and gates the attention logits via element-wise multiplication with σ(G_i), suppressing information flow from low-affinity pairs in both spatial and temporal attention; a triplet ranking loss on the final G_L enforces that interactive pairs attend more strongly to each other than to non-interactive ones.

## Results

On the Action Genome dataset (SGDet protocol), the best configuration (PLA + Ours on STTran) achieves R@10 of 22.24 (With Constraint) and 23.20 (No Constraint), R@20 of 26.48 and 30.24, and R@50 of 28.00 and 37.47. Across all four baseline–backbone combinations, the method yields average gains of +5.47 in R@10 (With Constraint) and +6.43 (No Constraint). The best weakly-supervised result reaches 88.3% and 94.3% of the fully-supervised STTran upper bound at R@10 under the two protocols, using only single-frame unlocalized annotations without bounding boxes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PLA (the primary weakly-supervised baseline), the method improves R@10 by +6.85 (With Constraint) and +7.37 (No Constraint) on STTran, and by +6.41 and +7.47 on DSG-DETR, respectively. Against TRKT (reproduced as TRKT†), gains are +4.51 and +5.40 on STTran, and +4.09 and +5.48 on DSG-DETR at R@10. The zero-shot RLIPv2 baseline is far behind, scoring only 5.06 (W)R@10 versus 22.24 for the best weakly-supervised configuration. Compared to the fully-supervised upper bounds, the gap narrows from ~39% (PLA vs. STTran full) to ~12% (PLA+Ours vs. STTran full) at R@10 under With Constraint, and from ~36% to ~6% under No Constraint. The method consistently outperforms all weakly-supervised baselines across both backbones and all K values, with the largest relative gains at strict budgets (R@10) where suppressing non-interactive pairs is most critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weakly-supervised video scene graph generation (WS-VSGG) framework established by PLA, which introduced single-frame unlocalized triplet supervision, and builds on the two-stage detection-then-relation pipeline used by PLA and TRKT. The key departure is identifying and addressing the detection distribution shift: off-the-shelf detectors in the weakly-supervised regime produce far more non-interactive object proposals than fully-supervised detectors, a problem prior WS-VSGG methods overlooked by directly transferring fully-supervised relation prediction architectures. The method repurposes vision-language grounding models (GroundingDINO, GLIP) as a one-time preprocessing tool for pseudo-label refinement, and introduces pair affinity as a new learnable signal that is absent from all prior WS-VSGG approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly modeling interactivity between detected pairs, the framework narrows the weak-to-full supervision gap to within 88–94% of the upper bound at R@10, a substantial improvement over prior WS-VSGG methods that leave a much larger gap. The pair affinity mechanism is plug-and-play across attention-based backbones (verified on both STTran and DSG-DETR), making it broadly applicable to future WS-VSGG architectures. However, the approach remains bounded by the quality of the matched/unmatched partition: RAM's reliance on a pretrained VL model and its heuristic reliability estimation mean that incorrect grounding can still corrupt supervision, and annotation incompleteness introduces false negatives. The authors note that developing more principled grounding reliability assessment is an important open direction.
