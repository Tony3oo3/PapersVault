## Problem

VLM-based cooperative driving methods either transmit raw sensory data (incurring heavy bandwidth overhead) or impose large on-board computational demands that exceed resource-constrained vehicles, while still lacking robust semantic reasoning under practical communication constraints. The paper asks how to balance semantic reasoning capacity, communication efficiency, and ego autonomy in vehicle-infrastructure cooperative driving.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262658/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The infrastructure side ingests multi-agent camera images fused via a shared 3D coordinate space along with ego-transmitted perception queries, processed by a Qwen2.5-7B VLM; the ego side consumes its own camera images and textual instructions through a Qwen2.5-0.5B VLM. The infrastructure outputs a compact latent guidance vector (aggregated from 8 intermediate hidden layers over a 5-frame temporal window) plus optional text-based reasoning for interpretability, transmitted at a 5:1 communication ratio. The ego vehicle produces a driving trajectory via a two-stage forward pass: a standard pass on local observations, followed by a refined pass conditioned on the received latent guidance.

## Method

DH-VLM decouples global-reasoning horizon (infrastructure) from local-planning horizon (ego vehicle) in an asymmetric, loosely coupled architecture. On the infrastructure side, a Hierarchical Latent Aggregation (HLA) module performs attention-based token aggregation within each of 8 selected intermediate VLM layers, combines them via learnable layer-wise weights, and applies temporal window smoothing to produce a stable latent guidance vector. On the ego side, an Infrastructure-Driven Latent Evolution (IDLE) module first projects the infrastructure latent into the ego's feature space via a lightweight MLP adapter (addressing cross-model misalignment), then fuses it with ego hidden states through cross-attention, and finally runs a second VLM forward pass to embed the cooperative signal into the ego's reasoning dynamics. When guidance is unavailable (packet loss, latency), the ego falls back to its single-pass pipeline, preserving autonomous operation. Training proceeds in three stages: infrastructure scene-understanding fine-tuning on a cooperation-oriented QA dataset (80k+ pairs covering fundamental scene description and ego-personalized counterfactual reasoning), ego model training, and joint HLA+IDLE optimization for latent-level cooperation.

## Results

On DAIR-V2X, DH-VLM achieves the best average L2 planning error of 1.87 m and the lowest average collision rate of 0.19%, outperforming the previous cooperative SOTA LangCoop (2.19 m L2, 0.51% CR) by 14.6% in L2 error and V2X-VLM (0.26% CR) by 26.9% in collision rate. The advantage is most pronounced at long horizons: at 5 s, DH-VLM reduces L2 error to 3.72 m versus 4.58 m for LangCoop and 5.34 m for V2X-VLM. In terms of deployment cost, DH-VLM uses 4.54 GB GPU memory (25.5% less than UniMM-V2X) and transmits only 3.45×10⁵ BPS, a 57.3% reduction over the query-based UniV2X (8.09×10⁵ BPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-intelligence baselines, DH-VLM (1.87 m L2, 0.19% CR) substantially outperforms the strongest single-agent method OpenDriveVLA (2.89 m, 0.53%) by 35.3% in L2 error and 64.2% in collision rate, confirming that cooperative latent reasoning provides a clear advantage over ego-only VLM planning. Among cooperative methods, DH-VLM beats the previous best L2 method LangCoop [5] (2.19 m) by 14.6% and the previous best collision-rate method V2X-VLM [36] (0.26%) by 26.9%, while also surpassing both on the other metric. Compared with query-based cooperative frameworks UniV2X [38] (3.18 m, 0.41%) and UniMM-V2X [22] (2.60 m, 0.42%), DH-VLM reduces L2 error by 29.0% and 28.1% respectively, and collision rate by 49.5% and 54.8%, with the gap widening at 5 s (3.72 m vs. 5.24 m and 5.00 m). In deployment cost, DH-VLM (640.6 M params, 4.54 GB, 3.45×10⁵ BPS) uses 25.5% less GPU memory than UniMM-V2X and 57.3% less bandwidth than UniV2X, while LangCoop [5] requires 7.38 B params and 17.74 GB memory—nearly 4× more—despite transmitting only ~4×10³ BPS of text. The robustness analysis (Table 4) further shows that under noisy infrastructure guidance (>4 m L2 error in infra trajectories), DH-VLM's latent-level fusion degrades gracefully (2.19 m avg L2) whereas text-level injection collapses to 5.66 m, a 95.8% increase over the no-cooperation baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three active threads: VLM-based end-to-end driving (DriveVLM, OpenDriveVLA, AutoVLA), cooperative driving via V2X communication (UniV2X, UniMM-V2X, V2X-VLM, LangCoop), and latent-space reasoning in foundation models (LatentVLA, latent transition models). It departs from prior cooperative VLM methods by replacing raw-sensor transmission (V2X-VLM) or text-based advice (LangCoop) with compact latent-level guidance, and from single-agent dual-system designs by making the two horizons physically asymmetric (7B infrastructure vs. 0.5B vehicle) rather than co-located. The HLA and IDLE modules are new architectural primitives that have no direct counterpart in the cooperative perception or end-to-end driving literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gains (57.3% communication reduction, 25.5% GPU memory reduction) make VLM-based cooperative driving more deployable on resource-constrained vehicles, addressing a key barrier to real-world adoption. The robustness finding—that naive text-level cooperation can degrade performance beyond the no-cooperation baseline by over 95% in L2 error—provides a concrete argument for latent-level as a safer communication medium. However, evaluation is limited to open-loop metrics on DAIR-V2X and V2X-Sim, the framework remains within the VLM paradigm (not yet VLA), and the single infrastructure-vehicle setup leaves open questions about scalability to dense multi-vehicle networks and closed-loop safety validation.
