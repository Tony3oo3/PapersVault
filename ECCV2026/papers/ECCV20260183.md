## Problem

Existing vector quantization (VQ) methods for pre-trained ViTs achieve high compression ratios but sacrifice fine-tuning efficiency and multi-task storage, while scalar-quantization-based Q-PEFT methods degrade severely below 2 bits. The paper identifies Coupled Vector Quantization Noise (CVQN) as the root cause: the interdependence among LoRA parameters amplifies Straight-Through Estimator gradient errors across coupled sub-vectors, distorting update directions and causing incorrect codeword reassignments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260183/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained ViT linear-layer weights (ViT-B, ViT-L, or SAM-H) and a downstream task dataset as input, and produces a compressed model consisting of a codebook and codeword assignments per layer, plus lightweight task-specific LoRA parameters and codebooks for cloud storage. At deployment, the full compressed model is reconstructed by applying LoRA to the frozen pre-trained weights and reassigning codewords via nearest-neighbor lookup, requiring less than 1 bit per parameter of stored task-specific data. The compressed model then processes standard RGB images to produce class labels or segmentation masks.

## Method

VQT jointly fine-tunes LoRA parameters and VQ codebooks in a unified single-stage procedure, with two key mechanisms. First, stochastic sub-vector replacement: during each forward/backward pass, each weight sub-vector is randomly represented as either its continuous counterpart (continuous-anchored mode, receiving true gradients for LoRA) or its vector-quantized counterpart (VQ-anchored mode, receiving true gradients for the codebook), with a Bernoulli(p) switch that breaks the coupling amplifying STE-induced errors. Second, progressive alignment: the replacement probability p is annealed from p₀ toward zero via a cosine schedule over training epochs, so early stages mitigate CVQN for stable LoRA updates and correct codeword reassignment, while later stages converge to the fully quantized objective matching deployment. For multi-task deployment, only the lightweight LoRA parameters and task-specific codebooks are stored in the cloud, and the full compressed model is reconstructed on demand via nearest-neighbor codeword reassignment.

## Results

On the VTAB-1K benchmark with ViT-B, VQT achieves 72.6% average accuracy at 2-bit, 70.8% at 1.5-bit, and 65.6% at 1-bit while fine-tuning only 0.6–0.7% of parameters (~0.6M out of 85.8M). At the extreme 1-bit setting, VQT outperforms the strongest VQ baseline PQF by 10.0% (65.6% vs. 55.6%) and the SQ-based EfficientQAT by 20.8% (65.6% vs. 44.8%). On ViT-L at 1-bit, VQT reaches 66.4% average accuracy, surpassing 1.25-bit SSVQ by 7.2% while updating only 0.6% of parameters. For semantic segmentation on SAM-H at 1-bit, VQT outperforms SSVQ (1.25-bit) across all four scenarios, e.g., +0.015 S_α on CAMO, while fine-tuning only 1.2% of parameters versus SSVQ's near-full fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQT consistently outperforms all VQ baselines at high compression ratios. Against SSVQ (the strongest advanced VQ method), VQT gains +0.7% at 2-bit, +4.2% at 1.5-bit, and +7.6% at 1-bit (vs. SSVQ's 1.25-bit), while fine-tuning only 0.6% of parameters versus SSVQ's 99%+ (85M vs. 0.6M). Against PQF (which fixes codeword assignments), VQT improves by +1.5% at 2-bit, +5.1% at 1.5-bit, and +10.0% at 1-bit, confirming that codeword reassignment is critical for downstream adaptation. Against SQ-based Q-PEFT methods (PEQA, EfficientQAT), VQT leads by +2.6% at 2-bit and by +20.8% at 1-bit, demonstrating VQ's advantage at extreme low bit-widths where SQ degrades severely. Compared to the FP LoRA reference (69.4%), VQT at 2-bit incurs only a 1.3% drop while achieving 16× compression. In the post-training comparison (Table 2), VQT at 1-bit (67.5%) surpasses VQ4DiT (60.9%) by 6.6% and matches VQ4DiT† (66.4%) while eliminating the need for a separate post-training procedure and using 30× fewer trainable parameters (0.69M vs. 21.33M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: Quantized PEFT (Q-PEFT) methods like PEQA and EfficientQAT, which combine scalar quantization with LoRA in a unified training loop, and advanced vector quantization methods like VQ4DiT, SSVQ, and PQF, which achieve extreme compression ratios but rely on post-training distillation or large numbers of independently learnable parameters. VQT departs from Q-PEFT by replacing scalar quantization with vector quantization to reach sub-2-bit compression, and departs from advanced VQ by using LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning rather than full-model updates or sign-bit splitting. The key conceptual departure is the identification of Coupled Vector Quantization Noise (CVQN), a previously unaddressed interaction between LoRA's interdependent parameter updates and the Straight-Through Estimator's gradient approximation error under VQ, which the paper formalizes and then mitigates via stochastic sub-vector replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VQT demonstrates that 1-bit compression of ViTs is practically viable with only ~0.8% parameter fine-tuning and sub-1-bit-per-parameter multi-task storage, which is significant for edge deployment where both model size and per-task storage overhead matter. The CVQN analysis provides a useful theoretical lens for understanding why naive LoRA+VQ combinations underperform, potentially guiding future work on other PEFT+quantization interactions. Open questions include whether the approach extends to even larger models (beyond ViT-L/SAM-H), whether inference-time VQ reconstruction overhead is acceptable in latency-sensitive settings, and whether the stochastic replacement strategy generalizes to other non-differentiable operations beyond VQ.
