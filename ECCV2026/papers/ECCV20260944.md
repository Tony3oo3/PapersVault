## Problem

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction (F3R) transformers rely on cross-view global self-attention whose quadratic complexity in token length becomes the dominant bottleneck for long image sequences. Existing efficient alternatives (token merging, static sparse attention) apply homogeneous sparsity configurations across all layers and heads, overlooking the fact that F3R attention patterns are highly heterogeneous across heads and dynamically input-dependent, leading to either discarded geometric information or wasted computation.

## Input / Output

SAF3R operates as a training-free inference-time modification on pre-trained F3R models (VGGT, π³, DA3), taking multi-view RGB image sequences of 10–700 frames at 480×640 resolution as input. The DINO encoder produces patch tokens that flow through alternating local and global attention blocks; SAF3R replaces only the global attention computation while leaving all other components unchanged. Outputs are camera poses (rotation/translation) and dense 3D point maps, evaluated under both sparse (10–128 frames) and dense (300–700 frames) settings.

## Method

SAF3R categorizes global attention heads into four functional types—position, vertical-line, correspondence, and scanning—each with a tailored O(N) sparse kernel: a static fixed-pattern kernel, a Query-Probe Top-K kernel (selecting keys via a single mean-query probe vector), a DINO Top-K kernel (selecting keys by cached cosine similarity of DINO patch features), and a uniform-stride sampling kernel. An offline profiling stage uses progressive local substitution on a small calibration set to assign each head its optimal kernel and hyperparameters under a constraint that per-head FLOPs do not exceed a uniform-sampling baseline. During inference, input-dependent heads (Query-Probe and DINO Top-K) dynamically adapt their key selections per scene via lightweight online computation and index caching, achieving up to 7× end-to-end speedup while preserving pose and reconstruction accuracy.

## Results

SAF3R achieves up to 7.3× end-to-end speedup on π³ (700 images) and 4.7× on VGGT (700 images) while preserving camera pose and reconstruction quality. On camera pose estimation (AUC@30), SAF3R outperforms all efficient baselines across most datasets: e.g., 89.19% on Co3D-v2 for VGGT (vs. 87.78% AVGGT, 86.15% Co-Me, 84.84% FastVGGT, 82.83% SparseVGGT) and 90.14% for π³ (vs. 89.27% Aπ³, 87.56% Fastπ³, 82.14% Sparseπ³). On the stringent AUC@3 metric, SAF3R shows only negligible degradation from full attention (e.g., 52.68% vs. 58.26% for VGGT on Co3D-v2), while baselines drop substantially (e.g., 18.59% for SparseVGGT). For point cloud reconstruction, SAF3R maintains Chamfer Distance and F1-score close to full-attention baselines while delivering substantially higher speedup than token-merging methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against token-merging baselines (FastVGGT, Co-Me), SAF3R consistently achieves higher AUC@30 across all three models and most datasets (e.g., 89.19 vs. 84.84 on Co3D-v2 for VGGT; 90.14 vs. 87.56 for π³), and delivers substantially larger speedups (up to 7.3× vs. 2.0–4.0×) because it avoids the dense anchor-sampling overhead of merging. Against sparse-attention baselines (SparseVGGT, AVGGT), SAF3R wins on nearly all AUC@30 and AUC@3 metrics; the gap is most pronounced on AUC@3, where SAF3R retains 52.68% on Co3D-v2 (VGGT) versus 18.59% (SparseVGGT) and 47.10% (AVGGT), indicating that head-specific dynamic sparsity preserves fine-grained pose accuracy better than uniform or stage-level sparsity. Against the closest competitor AVGGT/Aπ³/ADA3, SAF3R gains 1.4–4.1 AUC@30 points on most datasets and 5–10 AUC@3 points, while also achieving higher speedup (4.7× vs. 4.6× for VGGT at 700 images; 7.3× vs. 6.7× for π³). On point cloud reconstruction, SAF3R is competitive with FastVGGT (which sometimes slightly outperforms it on Accuracy/Completeness) but achieves 1.5–2× higher speedup, and clearly outperforms SparseVGGT and ADA3 on Chamfer Distance and F1-score across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAF3R extends the sparse-attention-for-efficiency line of work (MInference, DuoAttention, FlexPrefill) from LLMs and generic vision transformers to feed-forward 3D reconstruction (F3R) transformers, where the multi-task architecture (jointly modeling sparse camera poses and dense point maps) induces qualitatively different attention behaviors. It departs from prior F3R acceleration methods (FastVGGT, SparseVGGT, AVGGT, Speed3R) that apply static, model- or stage-level sparsity by introducing head-level heterogeneous kernels and a two-stage offline-profiling/online-adaptation pipeline. The DINO Top-K kernel repurposes pre-trained DINOv2 patch features as a lightweight correspondence estimator, a design not previously exploited in F3R attention sparsification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

F3R transformers are rapidly becoming the standard for end-to-end 3D reconstruction, yet their quadratic cross-view attention severely limits sequence length and real-time deployment; SAF3R's training-free, up-to-7× speedup with minimal accuracy loss makes long-sequence inference practically accessible without retraining. The systematic head-level analysis (four attention types) provides reusable diagnostic insight for the F3R community. Open questions include whether the DINO Top-K kernel generalizes to scenes with textureless regions or large viewpoint changes, whether query-side sparsity or integration with token merging can yield further gains, and whether the offline profiling cost is acceptable for rapidly evolving F3R architectures.
