## Problem

Standard photometric tracking of 3D Gaussian Splatting assets fails catastrophically under large spatial misalignment: because Gaussian primitives have compact local support, zero overlap between the rendered object and its target causes the directional gradient to vanish exactly, stranding the optimizer in spurious local minima. Existing workarounds—deep feature losses like LPIPS or category-specific pose priors—still fundamentally depend on local spatial overlap or sacrifice generality, leaving a gap for a purely optimization-based, class-agnostic objective that provides valid gradients across the entire image domain.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-initialized canonical (rest-pose) 3DGS model, a target video with known camera parameters, and per-scene foreground masks (obtained via 3D segmentation) as input, and produces per-frame deformation parameters—either TimeNet MLP weights or direct sparse control-point offsets—that deform the canonical Gaussians to match each target frame. Only foreground Gaussians are optimized; background Gaussians are recombined at render time. The spectral loss is a drop-in replacement for the spatial loss term, requiring no modification to the underlying deformation architecture.

## Method

SpectralSplats replaces the spatial photometric loss with a Spectral Moment objective that projects the rendered image onto a set of complex sinusoidal basis functions, so that a spatial displacement manifests as a phase shift yielding non-zero, directional gradients even under zero pixel overlap. To avoid the phase-wrapping local minima that high frequencies introduce, the paper derives a principled Frequency Annealing schedule from first principles: the phase-wrapping constraint |ωᵀd| < π requires the active frequency magnitude to scale as 1/‖d‖, and under the resulting exponential error decay this translates to a strictly linear expansion of the frequency index on a logarithmic spectral grid. In practice, a conservative warm-up phase holds the bandwidth at low frequencies before a sub-exponential (linear-in-ω) expansion begins, keeping the optimizer safely below the wrapping threshold. Once the annealed spectral moments secure local spatial overlap, the pipeline transitions to standard pixel and LPIPS losses for high-frequency refinement, making the spectral phase a seamless initialization-robust front-end to any existing 3DGS tracking pipeline.

## Results

On the GART Dog dataset under a spatial shift radius of 0.6, SpectralSplats achieves a mean PSNR of 22.06, SSIM of 0.907, and LPIPS of 0.217, outperforming the pixel-only baseline (PSNR 20.15, SSIM 0.892, LPIPS 0.259) and both global-loss baselines. On SC4D under a shift radius of 0.5, the method improves PSNR by up to 9 dB over pixel-only supervision (e.g., 27.15 vs. 23.61 for MLP+LPIPS), with consistent gains across all deformation parameterizations. The advantage grows with increasing misalignment: at larger shift radii, pixel-based PSNR degrades sharply while SpectralSplats remains stable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpectralSplats outperforms both external global-loss baselines across all three metrics on GART. Against Pyramid of Gaussians (PoG) [25], the proposed method achieves a mean PSNR of 22.06 vs. 17.09 (+5.0 dB) and LPIPS of 0.217 vs. 0.277, with PoG suffering catastrophic failures on individual dogs (e.g., English: PSNR 8.97). Against the Euclidean Distance Transform (DT) baseline [23], SpectralSplats wins on mean PSNR (22.06 vs. 20.92) and LPIPS (0.217 vs. 0.226), while DT edges ahead on SSIM (0.900 vs. 0.907 — actually Ours wins here too at 0.9071 vs. 0.8996). Compared to the pixel-only baseline, SpectralSplats improves mean PSNR by ~1.9 dB and reduces LPIPS by ~16%, with the gap widening substantially at larger shift radii. The one exception is the English dog, where pixel supervision marginally matches SpectralSplats on LPIPS (0.2367 vs. 0.2418), likely due to that sample's smaller effective displacement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3DGS tracking pipeline of GSGD [2] and the deformation parameterizations of Deformable 3D Gaussians [36] and SC-GS [12], but replaces their spatial photometric losses with a spectral moment objective. The frequency annealing strategy is conceptually inherited from BARF [22] and Nerfies [28], which annealed positional encodings in implicit NeRF representations; SpectralSplats transposes this idea to explicit 3DGS by supervising the rendered output directly with complex sinusoidal projections. The key departure is a first-principles derivation showing that the linearly scaling frequency index used heuristically in prior work is in fact the theoretical upper bound for safe expansion under a phase-wrapping constraint, providing a rigorous justification where prior works relied on NTK theory or signal bandwidth arguments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The vanishing gradient problem under large spatial misalignment is a well-known practical bottleneck in model-based 3DGS tracking, and this paper offers a principled, drop-in loss replacement that resolves it without modifying the underlying deformation model. The main limitation is that the framework assumes a pre-initialized canonical asset, restricting it to model-based tracking rather than full dynamic reconstruction from uncalibrated video. An open question is whether the spectral moment formulation generalizes to highly complex, non-rigid dynamics where the phase-shift-under-translation assumption breaks down, and whether the conservative sub-exponential frequency expansion can be made adaptive to per-scene optimization dynamics.
