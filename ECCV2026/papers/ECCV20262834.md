## Problem

Large-scale aerial semantic segmentation for UAVs is bottlenecked by the high cost of dense pixel-level annotation and the difficulty of achieving accurate RGB-thermal alignment on off-the-shelf platforms. Existing datasets contain only a few thousand samples and rely on hardware-synchronized sensor rigs or extensive manual refinement, limiting both scale and practical deployability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262834/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes geo-referenced RGB images (5472×3648 from DJI Phantom 4 RTK or 4000×3000 from DJI Mavic 3 Enterprise) and thermal LWIR images (640×512) along with per-modality camera poses and intrinsics. It produces dense 15-class semantic segmentation masks for both modalities, pixel-registered RGB-T image pairs aligned in the thermal distortion space, and a semantically labeled 3D point cloud. Only 2.84% of RGB images require manual annotation; the remaining 97% of RGB and 100% of thermal labels are generated automatically via 3D projection.

## Method

The core contribution is a geometry-driven 2D-3D-2D paradigm: (1) SfM+MVS reconstructs a dense metric RGB point cloud, and manual labels from a spatially stratified <3% subset are lifted to 3D via back-projection with multi-view majority voting; (2) a four-step rendering pipeline (Z-buffered projection, depth-aware occlusion filtering, back-to-front splatting densification, and depth-guided kNN label propagation) projects the semantic point cloud into all target RGB and thermal views to produce dense pseudo-labels; (3) RGB-T registration is achieved by independently reconstructing thermal geometry, aligning it to the RGB point cloud via ICP, and transferring lens distortion to produce pixel-aligned pairs entirely in software. The Firefly baseline uses a frozen DINOv3 encoder with a lightweight MLP head for RGB segmentation, then applies Rein adapters in a three-stage training scheme (RGB pre-training → self-supervised RGB-T domain adaptation → thermal fine-tuning) to transfer RGB knowledge to the thermal modality parameter-efficiently.

## Results

On SegFly-RGB, Firefly achieves 49.70% accuracy and 50.44% FWmIoU, outperforming UPerNet (48.38/46.73) and SegFormer (48.01/44.46). On SegFly-RGB-T with the full three-stage pipeline (RGB pretraining → RGB-T adaptation → thermal fine-tuning), Firefly-RGB-T reaches 41.06% accuracy and 43.83% FWmIoU, substantially ahead of UPerNet+Rein (34.74/31.98) and SegFormer+Rein (35.75/33.48). The 2D-3D-2D pseudo-label pipeline generates RGB labels at 91.27% accuracy from only 2.84% manual annotation, and thermal labels at 87.65% accuracy with zero manual annotation. RGB-T registration achieves 87.05% pixel-level accuracy without hardware synchronization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Firefly consistently outperforms both UPerNet and SegFormer across all evaluation settings. On SegFly-RGB (evaluated on the SegFly-RGB test split), Firefly leads UPerNet by +1.32% accuracy and +3.71% FWmIoU, and SegFormer by +1.69% accuracy and +5.98% FWmIoU. The gap widens dramatically on SegFly-RGB-T with the full three-stage pipeline: Firefly-RGB-T achieves 41.06/43.83% Acc/FWmIoU versus 34.74/31.98% for UPerNet+Rein and 35.75/33.48% for SegFormer+Rein, a margin of +6.32% accuracy and +11.85% FWmIoU over the strongest baseline. For vision foundation models, fine-tuning CatSeg on SegFly-RGB improves zero-shot accuracy on CART from 4.32% to 11.35% (+7.03%), and fine-tuning AnyThermal on SegFly-RGB-T improves zero-shot accuracy on Kust4K from 46.02% to 58.73% (+12.71%), demonstrating that SegFly supervision generalizes beyond the training domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D-to-3D label lifting paradigm demonstrated in OccuFly [17] (aerial 3D scene completion) into a full 2D-3D-2D pipeline that renders semantic labels back into both RGB and thermal 2D views, and further repurposes the same geometric framework for cross-modal RGB-T registration via ICP alignment of independently reconstructed point clouds. It departs from prior RGB-T datasets (CART, MVUAV, Kust4K) that rely on hardware-synchronized sensor rigs or SIFT-based 2D registration, instead achieving pixel-level alignment entirely in software through 3D geometry as an intermediate space. The Firefly baseline builds on the recent DINOv3 + Rein adapter stack for parameter-efficient domain adaptation, positioning it within the vision-foundation-model fine-tuning trend rather than the classical UPerNet/SegFormer lineage it benchmarks against.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SegFly provides the first large-scale aerial RGB-T segmentation benchmark (20K+ RGB, 15K+ aligned RGB-T pairs) generated with under 3% manual annotation, making multi-modal aerial segmentation research accessible without custom hardware or hundreds of labeling hours. The software-only RGB-T registration (87% pixel accuracy) is practically significant: it removes the hardware-synchronization barrier that has limited RGB-T dataset construction to specialized rigs. Key limitations include the static-scene assumption (dynamic objects are excluded), restriction to daytime thermal imagery, and inherent pseudo-label noise (~9–12% error) that may cap downstream model performance. Open questions include whether the 2D-3D-2D paradigm can extend to 4D dynamic scenes, nighttime thermal, and whether fully automated label generation (zero manual annotation) is achievable with current foundation models.
