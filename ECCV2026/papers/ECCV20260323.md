## Problem

Existing dual-tower Text-to-Sounding-Video (T2SV) methods suffer from two fundamental issues: shared text captions fed to modality-specific backbones cause semantic interference and a distribution gap between dense training captions and concise user prompts, and the optimal cross-modal fusion mechanism for synchronizing pretrained video and audio towers remains unresolved.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method accepts a concise user text prompt (e.g., 'a worker is hammering iron'), which the CRR framework expands into dense, modality-specific video and audio captions matching the training distribution. The output is a 5.4-second synchronized sounding video: 81 frames at 15 fps and 480p resolution (video tower: Wan 2.1 1.3B with UMT5-XXL text encoder) paired with 44.1 kHz audio (audio tower: Stable Diffusion Audio Open 1.0 with T5-base text encoder).

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary components. First, the Cross-Referential Rewriter (CRR) is a dual-agent caption pipeline: a Semantic Checker cross-references raw descriptions from a Video LLM (Qwen2.5-VL) and an Audio LLM (Qwen2-Audio) to extract grounded Semantic Anchors (entities, environments, actions, sounds), filtering hallucinations; a Cross-Modal Rewriter then generates disentangled, modality-pure captions (T_V, T_A) strictly from these anchors, preventing cross-modal contamination by construction. Second, BridgeDiT is a dual-tower diffusion transformer that inserts four lightweight interaction blocks between the frozen video (flow matching) and audio (v-prediction diffusion) DiT streams, using Dual Cross-Attention (DCA) as the fusion mechanism. DCA updates each tower's latent via symmetric cross-attention (video queries attend to audio keys/values and vice versa), preserving each tower's pretrained feature manifold while enabling bidirectional information exchange—contrasted with Full-Attention (which forces a shared space) and unidirectional cross-attention (which neglects one modality). Systematic ablation under identical conditions identifies DCA as the optimal fusion strategy for the frozen-backbone dual-tower setting.

## Results

On AVSync15, BridgeDiT achieves the best FVD (765.74), FAD (5.34), CLAP (35.95), and AV-Align (0.275) among all 11 baselines, with only CLIPSIM (28.52) slightly below JointDiT (29.94) and VA-IB (34.59) below SeeingHearing (35.87). On VGGSound-SS, it leads with FVD 615.28, FAD 6.01, and AV-Align 0.362; on Landscape, FVD 628.07, FAD 4.78, and AV-Align 0.258. In the user study (20 raters, 150 samples), BridgeDiT is rated highest across all five perceptual criteria (Overall: 3.34/5), outperforming the next-best pipelined method Wan+MMAudio (3.06/5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BridgeDiT outperforms all 11 baselines on the majority of metrics. Against the strongest pipelined baseline Wan+MMAudio, it improves FVD by 7.7% (765.74 vs. 828.33), FAD by 33% (5.34 vs. 7.98), and AV-Align by 13% (0.275 vs. 0.243). Among joint-generation methods, it beats the closest competitor JavisDiT on FVD (765.74 vs. 878.70), FAD (5.34 vs. 13.48), and AV-Align (0.275 vs. 0.158), and substantially outperforms SSVG, MTV, and CoDi across all metrics. Two narrow losses are noted: CLIPSIM is 1.4 points below JointDiT (28.52 vs. 29.94), attributed to JointDiT's stronger T2I backbone (Qwen-Image), and VA-IB is 1.3 points below SeeingHearing (34.59 vs. 35.87), which directly optimizes ImageBind as classifier guidance at inference. The retrained baselines SSVG and MTV (sharing identical CRR captions and data) confirm the architectural advantage of DCA over additive fusion and unidirectional conditioning. In the user study, BridgeDiT leads all methods on every perceptual criterion, with the pipelined Wan+MMAudio ranking second overall (3.06 vs. 3.34).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operates within the dual-tower paradigm for T2SV generation, building on pretrained Wan 2.1 (video) and Stable Diffusion Audio (audio) backbones connected by a lightweight interaction module, a lineage established by SSVG, MTV, and JavisDiT. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) replacing shared or unified captions with a Cross-Referential Rewriter (CRR) framework that uses a structured Semantic Anchor bottleneck to enforce modality purity, and (2) arguing that Dual Cross-Attention is superior to Full-Attention (MMDiT-style) specifically in the frozen-backbone setting, where features reside on disjoint manifolds and limited trainable parameters make a unified attention matrix an overly difficult optimization target. The systematic head-to-head comparison of fusion strategies (DCA vs. Full-Attention vs. Additive vs. Unidirectional) under identical conditions is itself a contribution not present in prior T2SV work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides actionable architectural guidance for the T2SV community: DCA is the right fusion choice when backbones are frozen, and prompt expansion via a structured anchor pipeline is the single largest lever for closing the training-inference distribution gap. Limitations include evaluation on relatively small benchmarks (≤5K videos), no support for speech or music generation, and no post-training refinement (e.g., RLHF). Open questions include whether the DCA-over-FA advantage persists when backbones are fine-tuned jointly, how the approach scales to longer durations and more diverse acoustic scenes, and whether the CRR framework generalizes to other multi-modal generation tasks beyond foley/sound effects.
