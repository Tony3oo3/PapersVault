## Problem

Scaling Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems to long-horizon robotic manipulation is hindered by the separation of data collection, policy learning, and deployment into independent pipelines, which forces heavy reliance on manual environment resets, introduces semantic mismatches between training and execution, and makes multi-policy execution brittle to cascading errors. No existing framework unifies these stages under a single decision mechanism that maintains consistent task semantics across the full robot lifecycle.

## Input / Output

The VLM agent consumes RGB observations, structured memory (role identity, task-level subtask progress, and working memory with tool-call history), and performs chain-of-thought reasoning to emit skill invocation decisions and MCP tool calls. Notably, the language instruction conditioning the underlying π₀.₅ VLA policy is not human-provided but dynamically generated by the agent at each MCP invocation. The VLA policy outputs action chunks of length H (short-horizon motor commands) via a conditional flow-matching head, while the agent-level output is a sequence of tool calls (Start Policy, Change Policy, Env Summary, Call Human) that orchestrate the full task.

## Method

RoboClaw employs an off-the-shelf VLM as a meta-controller operating in a closed-loop perception-reasoning-action cycle over structured memory, with a three-level hierarchy of Skills (reusable procedures), Tools (MCP interfaces), and Policies (π₀.₅ VLA models fine-tuned via LoRA with a flow-matching objective). The key mechanism for autonomous data collection is Entangled Action Pairs (EAP): for each manipulation task, a forward policy and a deliberately simpler inverse reset policy are paired so the robot alternates between executing the task and restoring the initial state, forming a self-resetting loop that continuously generates on-policy trajectories without human resets. During deployment, the same agent monitors subtask completion via environment summaries, retries or switches policies on failure, invokes learned recovery behaviors for degrading failures, and escalates to human intervention only for safety-critical situations. Deployment trajectories are re-integrated into the training dataset under identical contextual semantics, closing a lifecycle loop where execution continuously refines the policy pool.

## Results

On the vanity table long-horizon organization task, RoboClaw achieves a 25% improvement in success rate over the π₀.₅ baseline (trained on the same dataset without the agentic framework) and substantially outperforms the expected success rate computed as the product of four independent subtask success rates. For data collection efficiency, RoboClaw reduces human time investment by 53.7% (manual baseline requires ~2.16× more human time) and reduces human intervention during rollout by ~8.04×. Forward policy success rates improve steadily across five EAP rollout iterations, e.g., Body Lotion rises from 42% (21/50) to 86% (43/50) and Lipstick insertion from 4% (2/50) to 46% (23/50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RoboClaw against two baselines on the long-horizon vanity table organization task: (1) a π₀.₅ model trained on the same dataset but deployed without the RoboClaw agentic framework, and (2) an expected success rate computed as the product of four independent subtask policy success rates. RoboClaw outperforms both baselines by approximately 25% in absolute success rate, with the gap widening across iterations as the agent accumulates recovery policies. For data collection, the manual teleoperation baseline requires ~2.16× more human time and ~8.04× more human intervention per trajectory compared to RoboClaw's EAP-driven autonomous collection. The inverse reset policies (which enable the self-resetting loop) achieve 72–86% success rates (36–43 out of 50 trials), confirming that the EAP mechanism is reliable enough to sustain continuous data collection with minimal human resets.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboClaw builds on the VLA model lineage (π0, π0.5, RT-2, OpenVLA) for low-level policy execution and on hierarchical planning systems (SayCan, HAMSTER, Inner Monologue) for high-level task decomposition, but departs from both by unifying data collection, policy learning, and deployment under a single VLM-driven agent loop rather than treating them as separate pipeline stages. The Entangled Action Pairs mechanism for self-resetting data collection extends the closed-loop data generation line (RoboCopilot, Genie Centurion, FieldGen) by eliminating manual resets entirely through learned inverse policies, while the MCP-based tool interface borrows from agentic LLM architectures to give the VLM direct control over policy invocation and environment queries. Unlike simulation-based generators (MimicGen, RoboCasa) or semi-automated systems requiring human-in-the-loop corrections, RoboClaw targets fully autonomous real-world data acquisition with the same agent that later executes the learned policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 53.7% reduction in human effort and 25% success-rate gain on long-horizon tasks address a practical bottleneck in scaling VLA systems to real-world deployment, and the closed-loop lifecycle (where execution trajectories feed back into training) offers a path toward continuously improving robot skill libraries without repeated human annotation. However, the framework's generality is constrained by the assumption that practical inverse reset behaviors exist for each manipulation task, and the evaluation is limited to a single dual-arm platform (AgiBot G01) with four subtask types, leaving open whether EAP generalizes to tasks without natural forward-inverse symmetry (e.g., irreversible actions like pouring or cutting). Cloud-based VLM latency and the need for reliable inverse policies remain open challenges for broader adoption.
