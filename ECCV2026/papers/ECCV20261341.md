## Problem

Existing 3D part segmentation methods either lift 2D SAM results to 3D (introducing boundary ambiguities and 3D-consistency failures) or require user-provided prompts, part counts, or text queries, preventing full automation. No prior method achieves precise, robust, and fully automatic part segmentation on arbitrary complex 3D objects using only native 3D supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261341/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a mesh from which 100,000 points with normals are sampled, plus a single 3D point prompt indicating the target part. It outputs three multi-scale binary point-level masks and three predicted IoU scores; the highest-IoU mask is selected as the final prediction. For automatic segmentation, 400 FPS-sampled prompts are processed, masks are merged via NMS (IoU threshold 0.9), and point-level labels are projected onto mesh faces with flood-fill to produce a complete face-level part label map. Point features are computed once per object, enabling ~3 ms per additional prompt for interactive use.

## Method

P³-SAM uses Sonata (a self-supervised pretrained Point Transformer V3 with reduced voxel size) as a frozen feature extractor, aggregating multi-scale features through a weight-shared MLP into 512-dim point features. A two-stage, three-head segmentor predicts masks: Stage 1 applies three MLPs to (point features, coordinates, prompt) independently; Stage 2 introduces a global feature (max-pooled MLP over stage-1 outputs) and re-predicts refined masks conditioned on both the global feature and stage-1 masks, encouraging each head to specialize at a different scale. An IoU predictor (MLP + max-pooling) scores all three stage-2 masks, and during training the minimum-loss head receives backpropagation to enforce scale diversity. The automatic pipeline over-samples 400 FPS prompts, applies NMS to collapse redundant masks, projects surviving masks to faces by voting, and fills gaps via flood fill. The model is trained on a 3.7M-mesh dataset with automated part labels derived from connected-component decomposition and area-threshold merging, using Dice + Focal loss on masks and MSE on predicted IoU, with augmentation including random noise, 30% normal dropout, and 80% watertight sampling.

## Results

On PartObj-Tiny, P³-SAM achieves 59.88 mIoU for full segmentation without connectivity, outperforming the best baseline (PartField at 53.93) by ~6 points. With connectivity, P³-SAM reaches 81.14 mIoU vs. PartField's 79.18 and SAMesh's 56.86. For interactive segmentation, P³-SAM scores 51.23 mIoU, more than doubling Point-SAM's 27.91. On the watertight PartObj-Tiny-WT and point-cloud PartNetE benchmarks, P³-SAM maintains its lead (58.10 and 65.39 mIoU respectively), demonstrating robustness across data types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P³-SAM outperforms all five baselines across every task and dataset. Against the 2D-lifting method SAMesh, P³-SAM gains ~24 mIoU on segmentation without connectivity (59.88 vs. 56.86 with connectivity) and is the only method that handles watertight meshes without failing. Among 2D-data-engine methods, P³-SAM beats PartField by ~6 mIoU on segmentation without connectivity (59.88 vs. 53.93) and by ~2 mIoU with connectivity (81.14 vs. 79.18), while PartField degrades significantly on watertight data (51.54) and point clouds (59.1) where P³-SAM holds 58.10 and 65.39. SAMPart3D (53.47) and Find3D (21.28) are clearly outperformed on the primary benchmark. For interactive segmentation, P³-SAM more than doubles Point-SAM's score (51.23 vs. 27.91 mIoU), attributed to its multi-scale mask prediction and IoU-based selection mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

P³-SAM adapts the SAM architecture (feature extractor + segmentation heads + IoU predictor) to 3D point clouds but deliberately simplifies it: a single positive point prompt replaces SAM's multi-type prompts, and Sonata (pretrained Point Transformer V3) serves as the backbone rather than a custom encoder. The key departure from prior 3D part segmentation work (SAMPart3D, PartField, Point-SAM, Find3D) is the elimination of 2D SAM or 2D data engines entirely; supervision comes from native 3D part labels derived from connected components of artist-created meshes at 3.7M scale. The fully automatic pipeline (FPS prompt sampling + NMS mask merging + flood fill) also distinguishes it from methods requiring user-specified part counts, text queries, or manual prompt selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.7M-model dataset with native 3D part labels is the most significant contribution, as it removes the field's dependence on 2D-lifted supervision and provides a scalable resource for training class-agnostic 3D segmenters. The demonstration that a relatively lightweight Sonata+MLP architecture trained on this data outperforms heavier 2D-data-engine methods across watertight meshes, non-watertight meshes, and point clouds suggests that data quality and scale may matter more than architectural complexity for part segmentation. The stated limitation—reliance on surface geometry without spatial volume understanding—points to an open question about whether volumetric or interior-aware features are needed for truly general part understanding, especially for scanned or AI-generated watertight models.
