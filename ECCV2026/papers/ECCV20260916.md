## Problem

SPAD-based LiDAR systems suffer from flare caused by excessive photon returns and pile-up effects, producing spurious obstacles and distorted depth in point clouds. Existing suppression methods operate at the hardware or signal-processing level using rule-based, configuration-dependent procedures that lack learnable representations and do not generalize across diverse sensing scenarios. No prior work leverages the intrinsic multi-echo structure of SPAD signals in a data-driven framework for flare discrimination.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 7-channel range-view image (H×W) constructed from raw SPAD measurements: first-echo intensity, depth, and pulse width; second-echo intensity, depth, and pulse width; and an ambient illumination channel. No external imaging sensor is required. The output is a pixel-wise 3-class segmentation mask (flare, core, background) that drives echo-level correction—replacing flare-contaminated first-echo entries with second-echo data—to produce a corrected depth map compatible with downstream perception pipelines.

## Method

PILF processes the multi-modal input through two branches (single-echo and multi-echo) that share a Physics-Aware Encoder (PAE) using depth-wise convolutions, a suppressive meta-function to mitigate physically unstable responses, and residual group-convolution gating to avoid modality-specific aliasing. The multi-echo branch first applies Depth Decomposition, discretizing depth into N=16 bins to aggregate spatially coherent flare/object features across echoes while dispersing random noise. Echo-Aware Fusion (EAF) then adaptively combines first-echo and depth-bin features using learned per-bin relevance coefficients (from global max-pooled descriptors) and spatial sigmoid gates, conditioned on ambient illumination to separate flare from intrinsic reflectivity. The fused representation is processed by a U-Net backbone with a single-directional multi-head attention decoder that captures the anisotropic spreading pattern of flare. Training uses cross-entropy plus Dice loss with a SPAD-aware augmentation strategy (FlareAug) that crops and fuses flare-core pairs to address the severe 100:2:1 class imbalance.

## Results

On the FLARE dataset, PILF achieves 79.32% mIoU (78.58% Flare IoU, 80.06% Core IoU), outperforming the strongest 2D baseline DeepLabV3+ (72.14%), the best 3D baseline Cylinder3D (71.37%), and the top range-view baseline SalsaNext (71.20%) by 7.2–8.1 mIoU points. In depth-level evaluation, flare suppression reduces MSE in flare regions from 0.2275 to 0.0321 (85.89% reduction) and MAE from 0.3849 to 0.0613 (84.08% reduction), with comparable improvements in foreground regions (84.85% MSE, 83.11% MAE reduction). End-to-end inference runs at 37 ms per frame on a single RTX 3090, compatible with 10 fps acquisition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PILF outperforms all 13 baselines across both Flare and Core IoU metrics. Among 2D segmentation methods, DeepLabV3+ is the closest competitor at 72.14% mIoU, yet PILF still leads by 7.18 points; transformer-based 2D models (SegFormer, Mask2Former) perform notably worse (~61% mIoU), suggesting that standard attention mechanisms struggle with the multi-echo SPAD signal structure. In the 3D category, Cylinder3D achieves the highest mIoU (71.37%) but still trails PILF by 7.95 points, and PVCNN is particularly weak (38.75%), indicating that point-based architectures cannot exploit the dense range-view structure. Among range-view methods, SalsaNext (71.20%) and RangeViT (56.86%) are the strongest and weakest respectively; PILF's advantage over SalsaNext (8.12 mIoU) highlights the benefit of incorporating second-echo and ambient illumination channels that RV methods do not natively access. The input representation ablation further confirms that even when OCRNet and DeepLabV3+ are given the full 7-channel multi-echo input, they improve only marginally over first-echo-only settings, underscoring that the architectural design (PAE, EAF, DD) is essential for exploiting multi-echo cues.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established hardware- and signal-processing-level approaches to SPAD LiDAR flare suppression (peak suppression, temporal gating, optical isolation) by reframing the problem as a learnable semantic segmentation task over range-view images. It repurposes the general 2D/3D/range-view segmentation literature (U-Net, DeepLabV3+, OCRNet, Cylinder3D, SalsaNext, etc.) for a new sensing-specific application, but introduces physically-informed architectural components—Depth Decomposition, Physics-Aware Encoder, and Echo-Aware Fusion—that encode SPAD-specific multi-echo and photometric priors absent from generic segmentation backbones. The key conceptual departure is treating first and second echoes plus ambient illumination as distinct modalities to be fused, rather than discarding later echoes as conventional point-cloud pipelines do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first learning-based, data-driven approach to SPAD LiDAR flare suppression, complementing (rather than replacing) existing hardware and signal-level mitigations, and the released FLARE dataset is the first dedicated benchmark for this problem. The 85% depth-error reduction and 37 ms/frame latency suggest practical viability for autonomous-driving pipelines at 10 fps. However, the evaluation is limited to a single sensor (IMX459) and a single dataset of ~2,500 frames, and the 'suppression' step is essentially a mask-guided echo swap, so generalization to other SPAD architectures, more complex multi-reflection scenarios, or different flare geometries remains an open question. Whether the physically-informed fusion components transfer to other SPAD-specific artifacts (e.g., crosstalk, afterpulsing) is unexplored.
