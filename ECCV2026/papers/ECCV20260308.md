## Problem

Video diffusion models produce visually sharp frames yet routinely violate basic physical constraints—support, motion continuity, and object permanence—because their denoising objective entangles low-dimensional physical states with high-entropy appearance nuisances rather than learning predictive temporal structure. The paper argues this is an 'objective problem' rather than a data problem: the training loss allocates capacity to stochastic appearance variation instead of the causal dynamics that make sequences physically legible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260308/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a post-training procedure applied to pretrained video diffusion models (CogVideoX-5B, Open-Sora, Wan2.2-T2V-A14B), taking either a text prompt (text-to-video) or an initial image (image-to-video) as conditioning and producing a generated video clip. Training videos are resized to 49×480×720 (VideoPhy setting) or 32×256×256 (PisaBench setting), and the frozen V-JEPA 2-ViT-G world model processes generated rollouts with a sliding window of 16 frames (8 context, 8 predicted) to compute dynamics rewards.

## Method

LDO (Latent Dynamics Optimization) injects causal dynamics into a pretrained diffusion model via two complementary mechanisms without altering its sampling procedure. First, Predictive Dynamics Alignment (PDA) partitions the temporal axis into K bins and aligns the Gram-matrix geometry of intermediate DiT features (at layer 18, via LoRA rank 128) with the causally predicted latent dynamics from a frozen V-JEPA 2 predictor, transferring relational structure while remaining invariant to feature coordinates. Second, GRPO with a structure-aware reward treats the diffusion model as a stochastic policy (stochastic DDIM, η>0), generates G=16 rollouts per prompt, scores each with V-JEPA 2's cosine-similarity-based predictability over sliding windows, and applies PPO-clipped group-relative policy gradients on a random fraction (ρ=0.6) of denoising steps. The two losses are interleaved—PDA on standard steps, GRPO every m steps—to fit within GPU memory, grounded in an Information Bottleneck analysis showing that predictive JEPA objectives naturally discard nuisance entropy to isolate minimal sufficient statistics of the causal state.

## Results

On VideoPhy, LDO (built on CogVideoX-5B) achieves an overall Physical Commonsense (PC) score of 45.6, a +13.6 absolute improvement over the CogVideoX-5B base (32.0), while also increasing Semantic Adherence (SA) from 69.2 to 71.8. The largest category gains are +20.0 PC in Fluid-Fluid and +14.7 PC in Solid-Solid interactions. On PisaBench, LDO reduces trajectory L2 error by 11.39% (real) and 14.29% (simulated) relative to the PISA-psft baseline, and improves Chamfer Distance by up to 15.79%, while maintaining competitive IoU (0.145 real, 0.154 simulated).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On VideoPhy, LDO achieves the highest overall PC (45.6) among all 12 compared methods, surpassing the strongest physics-specific baseline PhyT2V (42.0) by +3.6 and the representation-alignment baseline VideoREPA (37.8) by +7.8. Over its own CogVideoX-5B base, LDO gains +13.6 PC while also improving SA by +2.6, confirming that dynamics gains do not sacrifice prompt fidelity. On PisaBench, LDO outperforms the PISA-psft baseline on trajectory L2 (0.070 vs 0.079 real; 0.030 vs 0.035 simulated) and Chamfer Distance (0.170 vs 0.194 real; 0.064 vs 0.076 simulated), though it marginally trails on simulated IoU (0.154 vs 0.155, −0.65%). Against commercial models, LDO dramatically reduces trajectory error: Sora (0.174 L2 real), Kling-V1.5 (0.155), and Runway Gen3 (0.187) all exceed 0.15, whereas LDO achieves 0.070. The ablation (Table 3) confirms both PDA and GRPO contribute independently, with their combination yielding the best PC (45.6) versus PDA alone (39.2) or GRPO alone (40.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

LDO sits at the intersection of three threads: (1) latent predictive world models (V-JEPA 2) that learn dynamics-aligned representations by predicting masked future latents, (2) representation alignment for diffusion (VideoREPA's Gram-matrix relational distillation, which LDO directly reuses for its PDA loss), and (3) GRPO-style policy optimization for generative models (originating from DeepSeekMath, recently adapted to diffusion/flow-matching). The paper's key conceptual departure is framing the physics gap as an Information Bottleneck failure—diffusion's likelihood objective forces modeling of irreducible nuisance entropy, whereas JEPA's prediction objective implicitly discards it—and then operationalizing this by using a single frozen world model as both a structural teacher (feature-level) and a dynamics critic (rollout-level), rather than hardcoding physics, using language priors, or relying on human preference signals as prior physics-aware video generation methods do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IB analysis provides a clean, testable explanation for why scaling diffusion models alone does not yield physical grounding, reframing the problem as an objective mismatch rather than a data gap. Practically, LDO is attractive because it is a post-training recipe that leaves the diffusion sampler untouched and requires only a frozen teacher, making it easy to bolt onto existing video generators. However, the approach is fundamentally bounded by the world model's coverage: if V-JEPA 2 has not learned a particular physical phenomenon, LDO cannot inject it. The paper also surfaces a real tension—strong physics priors can cause the model to ignore explicit user instructions (the stationary-bottle failure)—and resolving this instruction-vs-plausibility trade-off remains an open problem. Generalization beyond the two evaluated benchmarks (one VLM-scored, one narrow object-dropping task) and beyond a single teacher architecture are also untested.
