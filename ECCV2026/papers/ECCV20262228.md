## Problem

Multi-focus image fusion (MFIF) has been largely confined to low-resolution images (below 1080P), facing three critical barriers in ultra-high-definition (UHD) scenarios: the absence of large-scale 4K multi-focus datasets, the lack of architectures specifically adapted to UHD scales, and the inability of existing methods to deploy on mobile or even desktop hardware due to prohibitive computational costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262228/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a spatially aligned pair of near-focus and far-focus RGB images at native 3840×2160 (4K) resolution and produces a single all-in-focus fused image at the same resolution. A key design constraint is that the model must operate at full 4K resolution without the memory overhead of full-resolution convolutions, which is achieved by performing the bulk of decision-making on down-sampled representations and only applying lightweight full-resolution operations for edge refinement. The resulting model contains only 0.0081M parameters and achieves 90 FPS on an RTX 3090, with sub-second inference on a Huawei P60 Pro smartphone.

## Method

UMF-LUT is a scale-decoupled lookup-table framework that replaces conventional convolutions with learnable LUTs to avoid full-resolution memory bottlenecks. The Coarse-Region LUT (C-LUT) operates on down-sampled images and uses four cues—Sobel gradient difference, dilated regional gradient energy, maximum gradient, and a lightweight CNN-extracted semantic feature—as indices into a 4D lookup table, with quadrilinear interpolation over 16 vertices to produce a differentiable region-level fusion weight map. The Detail-Edge LUT (D-LUT) operates at full resolution using two second-order Laplacian cues (difference and maximum) as indices into a 2D table, producing a sparse high-frequency residual via bilinear interpolation that refines focus boundaries. The final decision map is obtained by up-sampling the C-LUT output, adding the D-LUT residual, and clamping to [0,1]. Training is fully unsupervised, combining reconstruction losses (intensity, gradient, SSIM) with a binarization loss to enforce decisive C-LUT outputs and an L1 sparsity loss to keep the D-LUT residual zero-dominant except at true focal boundaries.

## Results

On UHD-MFF-Syn (4K resolution), UMF-LUT achieves the best score on 6 of 7 metrics: SD 49.91, EN 7.1672, VIF 1.4335, Q^{AB/F} 0.7498, AG 1.9416, and SF 6.7443, trailing only MSFIN-Fusion on MI (6.40 vs 6.79). On the zero-shot UHD-MFF-Real set, it leads on 6 of 7 metrics (MI 4.80, EN 6.96, VIF 1.38, Q^{AB/F} 0.73, AG 3.03, SF 8.86), with GIFNet slightly ahead on SD (43.67 vs 41.84). Inference latency is 11.03 ms per 4K frame (≈90 FPS) on an RTX 3090 with only 0.0081 M parameters and 1133 MB peak memory, and the model runs in 0.97 s on a Huawei P60 Pro smartphone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UMF-LUT outperforms all nine baselines on 6 of 7 quality metrics on UHD-MFF-Syn, with the closest competitors being CCSR-Net (SD 49.88, VIF 1.42) and TC-MoA (EN 7.1661, Q^{AB/F} 0.7491); the margins are narrow but consistent (e.g., +0.0305 SD, +0.0149 VIF over CCSR-Net). The sole loss is on MI, where MSFIN-Fusion scores 6.79 versus 6.40 for UMF-LUT, suggesting the multi-scale interaction network captures slightly more cross-image information content. On the zero-shot UHD-MFF-Real set, UMF-LUT leads on 6/7 metrics; GIFNet edges it on SD (43.67 vs 41.84) but suffers severe luminance and color distortions noted qualitatively. In computational efficiency, UMF-LUT is an order of magnitude faster than the next-best method (11 ms vs 150 ms for SMFuse) and ~500× faster than TC-MoA (5715 ms), while using 1/42,000th the FLOPs (67.8 G vs 94,370 G). For mobile deployment on a Huawei P60 Pro, only SESF (55.68 s) and UMF-LUT (0.97 s) are viable, with all other methods failing to run at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own LUT-Fuse framework (designed for infrared-visible fusion) to the multi-focus domain, repurposing the learnable lookup-table paradigm with a scale-specialized twist: a 4D C-LUT operating on downsampled Sobel/semantic cues for region-level decisions, and a 2D D-LUT operating on full-resolution Laplacian cues for edge refinement. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant CNN/GAN/transformer MFIF literature (SMFuse, MSFIN, TC-MoA, CCSR-Net) by replacing heavy convolutional backbones with discrete table lookups and interpolation, trading model capacity for extreme efficiency. The UHD-MFF dataset itself is novel, as all prior MFIF benchmarks (Lytro, Real-MFF, MFFW2) are confined to sub-1080P resolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first large-scale UHD multi-focus dataset (1,950 pairs at 3840×2160) fills a critical benchmark gap and enables future research on high-resolution fusion that was previously infeasible. The 90 FPS 4K inference with only 8.1K parameters and successful smartphone deployment (0.97 s on Huawei P60 Pro) open a practical pathway for on-device multi-focus photography that no prior method could achieve. Open questions include whether the LUT paradigm generalizes to other UHD fusion tasks (e.g., multi-exposure, multi-modal) and whether the modest quality margins over strong baselines (e.g., CCSR-Net) persist on more challenging real-world scenes beyond the 150-pair real test set.
