## Problem

Existing spatiotemporal predictors for global ionospheric TEC rely on naive concatenation of space weather drivers, which fails to capture the pronounced region-dependent responses that arise under identical forcing due to latitudinal heterogeneity and strong non-stationarity during geomagnetic storms. The paper addresses how to efficiently inject time-varying external control parameters into a convolutional predictor so that local dynamics adapt in a spatially structured, latitude-aware manner rather than uniformly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260202/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 48 hourly TEC maps on a 72×72 latitude-longitude grid (1 channel, derived from IGS 71×73 maps with longitude deduplication and latitude replicate padding) together with a per-timestep space weather parameter vector (F10.7, SSN, W_low, W_high, C, M, X, Dst, X-ray, Ap, and timestamp). It produces 24 future TEC maps at the same resolution, formulated as a dual-baseline residual: a deterministic baseline blending the last observation with a diurnal reference, plus a network-predicted residual capturing non-stationary dynamics. Earth-aware padding (circular in longitude, replicate in latitude) and a fixed validity mask ensure spatial continuity and exclude padded regions from the loss.

## Method

PMGC-SimVP builds on a SimVP encoder–translator–decoder backbone, replacing the dense temporal projection with a two-stage low-rank adapter (grouped temporal convolution followed by pointwise channel mixing) that cuts adapter FLOPs by over 90%. The core PMGC Block uses a Slow-Fast parallel design: a Slow branch applies axial depthwise convolutions for global spatial structure, while a Fast branch performs multi-scale depthwise convolutions with spatially adaptive routing. External parameters are encoded via multi-query attention pooling into a global context vector, then injected through two mechanisms: (1) band-wise FiLM-style feature modulation where latitudinal band embeddings produce per-row scale/shift parameters gated by content-dependent spatial attention, and (2) global dynamic kernel adaptation that reweights a shared prototype kernel bank per convolutional path. A dual-baseline residual strategy (linearly decaying blend of last-frame inertia and diurnal periodicity) stabilizes long-horizon prediction, and training uses a masked Charbonnier loss over valid grid locations.

## Results

On global TEC forecasting (Overall Average), PMGC-SimVP achieves MAE 1.774, RMSE 3.051, and SSIM 0.967, outperforming the strongest baseline WaST (MAE 1.882, RMSE 3.248, SSIM 0.964) by 5.7% in RMSE. Under stormy 2023 conditions, the advantage widens: PMGC-SimVP records RMSE 4.159 versus 4.439 for WaST and 4.500 for Met2Net. On generic benchmarks, the backbone (without parameter injection) achieves the lowest MSE on WeatherBench (1.026) and the best MSE/MAE/PSNR on TaxiBJ (28.3/14.5/39.88 dB). The model also uses only 9.89M parameters and runs at 1745 fps, roughly 1.8× faster than SimVP and 5× faster than WaST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMGC-SimVP outperforms all six TEC-specific baselines on every reported metric (MAE, RMSE, SSIM) in both overall and stormy settings. The closest competitor is WaST [22], which PMGC-SimVP beats by 0.107 in MAE and 0.197 in RMSE overall, with the gap widening to 0.280 in RMSE during 2023 storms. Met2Net [16] is second-closest overall (RMSE 3.288 vs 3.051) but degrades more under storms (RMSE 4.500 vs 4.159). Transformer-based Stormer [21] and frequency-domain TAU [30] trail by 0.2–0.5 in RMSE overall. Recurrent baselines SimVP [8] and PredRNN-v2 [37] show the largest gaps (RMSE 3.515 and 3.809 respectively), confirming that purely history-driven extrapolation struggles under non-stationary space-weather forcing. On generic video benchmarks (WeatherBench, TaxiBJ) without parameter injection, the backbone still achieves the best MSE on WeatherBench (1.026 vs 1.098 for WaST) and best MSE/MAE/PSNR on TaxiBJ (28.3/14.5/39.88 vs 30.8/14.9/39.73 for WaST), though SSIM on TaxiBJ is marginally lower (0.980 vs 0.984 for WaST and E3D-LSTM).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SimVP purely convolutional backbone, which decouples spatial and temporal modeling for efficient spatiotemporal prediction, and augments it with a parametric conditioning mechanism inspired by FiLM and dynamic convolution from the vision literature. The key departure is replacing the standard concatenation-based conditioning (common in prior TEC forecasting and generic conditional predictors) with a latitudinally structured, band-wise FiLM modulation combined with multi-scale gated depthwise convolution and a global dynamic kernel bank. The two-stage low-rank temporal adapter further differentiates the architecture from dense temporal projections used in SimVP and TAU, targeting the high temporal dimensionality of long input sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a practical, efficient framework for operational global TEC forecasting where GNSS integrity and satellite communications depend on accurate short-term predictions under rapidly changing space weather. The band-wise structured modulation offers a generalizable template for any geophysical field with pronounced spatial heterogeneity (e.g., atmospheric or oceanic variables), suggesting that conditioning should respect known physical symmetries rather than applying uniform global modulation. Limitations include degradation during extreme superstorms at later horizons (where the dual-baseline residual reverts to diurnal priors that no longer hold), and the approach is tied to latitude-longitude grids; extending to higher-resolution or multi-source settings remains open.
