## Problem

Video object removal faces a fundamental quality-efficiency trade-off: traditional flow-based methods produce blurry, artifact-laden reconstructions with limited generative capacity, while diffusion-based methods improve realism but require tens of denoising steps and suffer from hallucination under weak priors. No existing approach achieves both high-fidelity background reconstruction and practical one-step inference speed without relying on external draft generation pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261214/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates in the latent space of CogVideoX-5B-I2V at 480×720 resolution over 25-frame clips. D2DF-DG takes a masked video latent, a binary object mask, and an external draft latent (e.g., ProPainter output) as concatenated conditioning; D2DF-DF removes the external draft entirely, instead generating a pseudo-draft internally via the SGFP module from unmasked regions only. The output is a fully reconstructed object-removed video latent decoded back to pixel space, with total end-to-end processing under 10 seconds including encode/decode.

## Method

D2DF is a three-stage progressive distillation pipeline. Stage I trains a multi-step diffusion teacher (D-LDM) conditioned on draft + masked video latents. Stage II introduces Prior-Privileged Consistency Distillation (PPCD): during distillation, the teacher's draft condition is replaced with ground-truth latents to produce a stable 'golden' trajectory, while the student still consumes the actual draft as input, yielding a one-step draft-guided refiner (D2DF-DG). Stage III adds the Self-Guided Fast Planting (SGFP) module—a 4-layer Temporal Masked Transformer that reconstructs masked regions in latent space using only valid (unmasked) patches as attention keys/values with spatial window constraints—then applies PPCD again with D2DF-DG as teacher to produce the fully autonomous, draft-free one-step model D2DF-DF. The key insight is that injecting GT as a privileged prior during teacher trajectory generation eliminates the unstable, draft-dependent solution paths that plague standard consistency distillation, enabling reliable one-step convergence.

## Results

On RORD, D2DF-DG achieves the best PSNR (32.20 dB), SSIM (0.9318), and LPIPS (0.0902) among all compared methods, while D2DF-DF reaches 31.56 dB PSNR and 0.9202 SSIM without any external draft. On ROVI, D2DF-DG leads with 42.41 dB PSNR and 0.9888 SSIM, and D2DF-DF is nearly matched at 42.25 dB PSNR and 0.9889 SSIM. On VPLM, both variants surpass all baselines in PSNR (43.31 and 43.05 dB respectively) and SSIM (0.9877 and 0.9864). In terms of efficiency, D2DF-DF completes a 25-frame 480×720 video in 1.05 s total (1 denoising step), making it over 40× faster than ROSE (45.76 s, 50 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

D2DF-DG (draft-guided, 1-step) achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on all three benchmarks, outperforming the strongest traditional baseline ProPainter by +1.23 dB PSNR on RORD (32.20 vs 30.97) and +1.55 dB on VPLM (43.31 vs 41.15), and the strongest diffusion baseline MiniMax-Remover by +1.60 dB on RORD (32.20 vs 30.60). D2DF-DF (draft-free, 1-step) also leads all baselines in PSNR on every dataset (e.g., 42.25 dB on ROVI vs 41.21 for FuseFormer, the next best), confirming that the SGFP pseudo-draft preserves most of the quality of the external ProPainter draft. On VFID (temporal consistency), D2DF-DG is competitive but not always best: ProPainter (0.230) and MiniMax-Remover (0.224) edge it out on RORD (0.251), suggesting a slight trade-off in frame-to-frame smoothness. In efficiency, D2DF-DF runs in 1.05 s total versus 45.76 s for ROSE (50 DDIM steps) and 5.13 s for DiffuEraser (2 steps), a >40× speedup over the prior SOTA diffusion method. On the zero-shot Camera-Bench, D2DF-DG with FuseFormer drafts (no optical flow) achieves 27.08 dB PSNR, beating ROSE (26.45) and MiniMax-Remover (26.63), while D2DF-DF reaches 26.57 dB with the best flow-warping error (0.27×10⁻⁴), demonstrating strong generalization beyond its training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: traditional video inpainting (ProPainter, FuseFormer, FGT), diffusion-based video generation (ROSE, DiffuEraser, FLoED), and consistency distillation for one-step generation (LCM, Song et al.). It departs from prior diffusion-based inpainting work by reframing the problem as a *refinement* task—using a coarse draft from optical-flow methods as a strong conditional prior—rather than as a from-scratch generative task. The key methodological departure is PPCD, which injects ground-truth into the teacher's conditioning during distillation to stabilize the consistency trajectory, a trick not present in standard consistency distillation. The SGFP module further extends the lineage by replacing external pixel-space drafts with a lightweight latent-space Temporal Masked Transformer, drawing on masked-modeling ideas but applied in the diffusion latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40× speedup over competing diffusion methods (≈1 s vs. ≈45 s for 25 frames) makes high-quality video object removal practically deployable in real-time editing pipelines, and the draft-free D2DF-DF variant removes the need for any external prior at inference. The PPCD 'truth-injection' strategy is a generalizable distillation trick that could stabilize consistency distillation in other conditional generation settings where the conditioning signal is noisy or imperfect. The authors acknowledge residual blurring under one-step denoising as a limitation, and the framework's reliance on a large pre-trained video diffusion backbone (CogVideoX-5B) raises open questions about scalability to longer videos or higher resolutions. Whether the progressive draft-to-draft-free paradigm transfers to other video editing tasks (e.g., relighting, style transfer) remains unexplored.
