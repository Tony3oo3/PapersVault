## Problem

Open-vocabulary 3D scene graph methods operate in two sequential stages—first reconstruct, then enrich with vision-language models—leaving the graph unqueryable during exploration. Real-time mappers support querying but are restricted to closed-set vocabularies or shallow feature matching. The paper argues this sequential coupling is unnecessary: the instability of incremental tracking that motivated postponing VLM reasoning can itself be mitigated by the VLM, and VLM cost can be made affordable through selective scheduling.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a sequence of posed RGB-D frames (I_t^rgb, I_t^depth, θ_t) and incrementally constructs a 3D scene graph G_t = (V_t, E_t) that is queryable at every step. Each node carries a 3D point cloud, a selected CLIP visual embedding, a consensus label, and VLM-enriched fine-grained attributes (material, color, texture, state); edges encode directional spatial predicates (left, under, etc.) derived deterministically from axis-aligned bounding boxes relative to a virtual room-center anchor. The graph grows in semantic richness over time as background agents refine nodes, rather than appearing only after a complete offline reconstruction.

## Method

ThinkGraphs decouples a lightweight online mapper from two asynchronous VLM background agents. The frontend uses Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct to generate context-aware noun prompts, which feed Grounded-SAM v2 (GroundingDINO + SAM2) to produce grounded 2D instances with labels and confidence scores. The backend adapts OpenVox's probabilistic voxel-consistency association, replacing SBERT with CLIP text-label embeddings for viewpoint-invariant track scoring, and adds confidence-weighted label voting plus text-guided visual embedding selection (scoring candidate crops against the consensus label's CLIP text anchor). The Critic Agent performs semantic loop closure: a two-gate filter (geometric IoU + SBERT label similarity) proposes duplicate track pairs, which are verified via Set-of-Mark prompted VLM calls that return MERGE or KEEP decisions. The Description Agent enriches nodes with fine-grained attributes using a multi-target frame scheduler that greedily selects up to K frames per tumbling window to maximize joint target coverage, so a single multi-image VLM call annotates several objects at once, reducing total VLM calls by over two orders of magnitude versus per-object-per-frame prompting.

## Results

On open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation, ThinkGraphs achieves 0.58 mAcc / 0.37 mIoU / 0.61 f-mIoU on Replica and 0.75 mAcc / 0.44 mIoU / 0.46 f-mIoU on ScanNet, outperforming all prior methods on every metric for Replica and achieving the best mIoU and f-mIoU on ScanNet. On 3D visual grounding, the method reaches 43.0 A@0.25 on Sr3D+, 41.8 A@0.25 on Nr3D, and 52.9 A@0.25 on ScanRefer, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art (Open3DSG) by 15.3, 18.8, and 18.3 A@0.25 respectively. The online mapping path runs at 1.45 s per keyframe with VLM agents off the critical path, using only ~26 VLM calls per scene versus ~9,300 for naive per-object prompting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ThinkGraphs outperforms all 11 baselines on the primary metrics. On segmentation (Replica), it leads every prior method by at least 0.07 mAcc and 0.03 mIoU over the closest competitor (Octree-Graph, 0.51/0.34), with the largest gap over BBQ-CLIP (+0.20 f-mIoU). On ScanNet, it matches OpenVox on mAcc (0.75 vs 0.76) but surpasses it on mIoU (0.44 vs 0.43) and f-mIoU (0.46 vs 0.39). On visual grounding, the margins are substantially larger: ThinkGraphs beats Open3DSG by 15.3 A@0.25 on Sr3D+ (43.0 vs 27.7), 18.8 on Nr3D (41.8 vs 23.0), and 18.3 on ScanRefer (52.9 vs 34.6). Against BBQ [19], the gap is 20.3 A@0.25 on Sr3D+ and 22.8 on Nr3D. On ScanRefer, ThinkGraphs also surpasses LLM-Grounder (52.9 vs 17.1 A@0.25), OpenScene (52.9 vs 13.0), and LERF (52.9 vs 4.4), confirming that the asynchronous VLM enrichment and semantic loop closure collectively yield a large advantage over both batch-based scene graph methods and query-time grounding pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: batch-based open-vocabulary 3D scene graph methods (ConceptGraphs, BBQ, Open3DSG) that require complete reconstruction before semantic enrichment, and real-time incremental mappers (Hydra, Clio, SceneGraphFusion) that support querying but are limited to closed-set vocabularies. It builds directly on OpenVox's probabilistic voxel-consistency formulation as its association backbone and on BBQ's spatial edge derivation and evaluation protocol, but departs from both by introducing an asynchronous architecture where VLM agents (a Critic for semantic loop closure and a Description Agent for attribute enrichment) run in the background without blocking the online mapping loop. The multi-target frame scheduler and text-guided embedding selection are novel scheduling and representation mechanisms not present in either lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that deferring all semantic enrichment to a post-hoc stage is unnecessary: an incremental pipeline with asynchronous VLM agents can match or exceed batch-based methods on segmentation and substantially outperform them on grounding (15–19 A@0.25), while keeping the graph queryable throughout exploration. This is practically important for embodied AI and AR applications where the agent must answer spatial queries mid-exploration rather than after a full reconstruction. Key limitations include the frontend (Qwen3-VL + Grounded-SAM) preventing true real-time operation, and the inherent lag of background agents behind the mapping frontier, which degrades early-sequence grounding quality. Open questions include whether the asynchronous agent design extends to richer relation prediction, higher-level scene reasoning, or other structured representations beyond scene graphs.
