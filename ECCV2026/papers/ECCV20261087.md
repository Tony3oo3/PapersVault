## Problem

Existing monocular 4D reconstruction methods rely on pixel-level supervision and cannot recover regions beyond visible camera limits, leaving significant gaps in novel view synthesis. While camera-controlled video diffusion models could fill these invisible regions, their inherent stochasticity introduces severe artifacts when naively applied to reconstruction, and the L×4×4 Cartesian parameter space for optimal trajectory planning is computationally intractable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261087/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a casually captured monocular video as input, augmented with precomputed depth maps (UniDepth), camera poses (BootsTAPIR), and dynamic masks (RAFT). It produces a 4D Gaussian Splatting scene (MoSca representation, optimized for 6000 steps) capable of rendering novel views at 38 FPS that extend beyond the original camera's field of view. The panoramic representation is 2048×2048 pixels, with 8 evenly-spaced expansion directions and 49-frame trajectories generated by TrajectoryCrafter (50 diffusion steps).

## Method

PASTEL's core contribution is panoramic scene alignment, which reformulates intractable 3D Cartesian trajectory search into a 2D directional expansion on a spherical panorama. All video frames are back-projected into 3D and reprojected onto a unified panoramic surface, where M=8 evenly-spaced angular directions define expansion rays; starting cameras are selected at the visible boundary along each direction using a depth-constrained inner-product maximization. Trajectories are constructed via SLERP interpolation between original and novel camera poses, providing geometrically stable conditioning for TrajectoryCrafter. A comprehensive view expansion merges static point clouds across all timestamps with reprojected dynamic objects to form warped guidance frames. Finally, strategic supervision applies generative outputs only to invisible regions (uncovered by warping) and unreliable regions (SSIM below threshold ε=0.3), preventing diffusion stochasticity from corrupting well-reconstructed areas.

## Results

On the DyCheck IPhone dataset, PASTEL achieves 18.54 full-image PSNR, 0.582 SSIM, and 0.335 LPIPS, outperforming the best reconstruction baseline USPLAT4D (17.63 PSNR) by 0.9 dB and the best generation baseline CogNVS (15.42 PSNR) by 3.1 dB. On Kubric-4D with large camera displacements, PASTEL achieves 22.75 full-image PSNR and 0.212 LPIPS, the best among all compared methods, while remaining competitive with CogNVS on SSIM (0.733 vs. 0.760). Reconstruction time is 1.5 hours with a rendering speed of 38 FPS, comparable to other Gaussian Splatting-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASTEL outperforms all reconstruction-based baselines on DyCheck IPhone: it beats USPLAT4D (the strongest reconstruction method) by 0.9 dB in full-image PSNR (18.54 vs. 17.63) and by 0.006 in LPIPS, while matching its SSIM (0.582 vs. 0.583). Against MoSca, the underlying 4D representation, PASTEL gains 1.21 dB PSNR and 0.010 SSIM, confirming that the panoramic alignment and strategic supervision add substantial value beyond the base representation. Among generation-based methods, PASTEL dramatically outperforms TrajectoryCrafter (18.54 vs. 12.71 PSNR) and CogNVS (18.54 vs. 15.42 PSNR), demonstrating that unconstrained generative priors introduce severe artifacts when applied directly to 4D reconstruction. On Kubric-4D with large camera displacements, PASTEL achieves the best full-image PSNR (22.75) and LPIPS (0.212), edging out CogNVS (22.63 PSNR, 0.232 LPIPS), though CogNVS retains a slight SSIM advantage (0.760 vs. 0.733). Overall, PASTEL is the only method that simultaneously achieves top performance on both covisibility-masked and full-image metrics, validating its ability to extend rendering beyond observable camera boundaries without degrading in-view fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

PASTEL sits at the intersection of two active research threads: monocular 4D reconstruction via Gaussian Splatting (MoSca, 4DGS, Shape-of-Motion) and camera-controlled video diffusion (TrajectoryCrafter, CogNVS, GCD). It departs from both by introducing a panoramic scene alignment that reformulates the intractable L×4×4 Cartesian trajectory search into a 2D directional expansion, borrowing the spherical projection idea from classical panorama stitching but repurposing it as a structural prior for conditioning generative models. The strategic supervision mechanism—masking generative outputs to invisible/unreliable regions only—represents a principled departure from prior work that either ignores invisible regions entirely or naively blends generated content into reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck for VR and embodied AI: monocular 4D reconstructions leave large gaps beyond the camera's field of view, degrading immersion. The panoramic reformulation offers a reusable conceptual tool for any task requiring structured camera exploration in 3D, and the strategic supervision insight (that unconstrained generative priors actively harm reconstruction quality) is broadly applicable to hybrid reconstruction-generation pipelines. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences, sensitivity to depth/pose estimation errors in uncontrolled settings, and whether the 1.5-hour reconstruction pipeline can be accelerated for near-real-time use.
