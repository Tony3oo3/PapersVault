## Problem

Generating realistic global illumination with diffusion models faces two coupled challenges: precisely aligning output to geometric and material constraints (avoiding hallucinations), and maintaining temporal stability across video sequences without the high computational and memory cost of video diffusion models. Prior generative relighting methods either produce misaligned results or require expensive multi-step or video-diffusion pipelines, making them impractical for real-time or long-sequence applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261473/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 512×512 direct lighting image (demodulated by albedo and μ-law tone-mapped to [0,1] before VAE encoding), along with G-buffer channels (normals, albedo, roughness, metallic), a single-sample noisy GI image, radiance cues, and motion vectors. It produces a 512×512 indirect illumination image in linear HDR space, which is composited with the original direct lighting to yield the final global illumination output. The albedo demodulation/modulation step is critical: it strips material color from the input so the diffusion model focuses purely on lighting, then restores texture detail in the output.

## Method

The core architecture is a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 2.1 Turbo UNet operating in a single denoising step, where direct lighting (encoded via the frozen SD-VAE) serves as the noisy latent input and multiple conditioning signals (normals, albedo/roughness/metallic, noisy GI, radiance cues) are injected at different UNet levels through T2I-Adapter modules rather than concatenated to the input, avoiding distribution shift. For temporal stability, a Temporal VAE (TVAE) decoder replaces the standard VAE decoder at inference: it accepts the current latent plus the previous frame's output reprojected via motion vectors, processed by a small history-encoder CNN whose features are injected through zero convolutions, causing the decoder to produce pixel values close to the previous frame where latents are similar. The diffusion UNet and adapters are jointly fine-tuned with L1+LPIPS pixel-space losses for 50 epochs, while the TVAE is trained separately for 10 epochs with L1 loss plus a parallel DIV2K regularization pass (previous frame set to zero) to prevent drift. This plug-and-play design means the TVAE can be swapped in at inference without retraining the diffusion model, achieving long-sequence temporal coherence at 0.29 s/frame on an RTX 3090.

## Results

Across five test scenes (White Room, Kitchen, Living Room, Staircase, Shadow Room), the method achieves PSNR of 21.12–33.80 dB, SSIM of 0.86–0.98, LPIPS of 0.04–0.14, and FovVDP of 5.88–7.07, outperforming all baselines on every metric in every scene. The largest gains are over the diffusion-based baselines: e.g., on Staircase, PSNR is 30.23 (Ours) vs. 18.98 (RGB↔X) and 13.84 (DiffusionRenderer). On temporal quality (FovVDP), the method scores 5.88–7.07 versus 0.67–4.55 for RGB↔X and 2.27–3.12 for DiffusionRenderer. Inference runs at 0.29 s per 512×512 frame on an RTX 3090, roughly 11× faster than RGB↔X (3.25 s) and 179× faster than DiffusionRenderer (52.09 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against three baselines: BCNN [55] (a bilateral-CNN neural GI method), RGB↔X [60] (a latent-diffusion intrinsic-decomposition + forward-rendering pipeline), and DiffusionRenderer [30] (a video-diffusion inverse/forward rendering system). Against BCNN, the proposed method wins on all four metrics in all five scenes, with the largest PSNR gap on Staircase (+4.37 dB) and Shadow Room (+1.73 dB); BCNN struggles with glossy reflections and diffuse bounces that the diffusion prior captures. Against RGB↔X, the advantage is dramatic: PSNR is 2–3× higher (e.g., 33.80 vs. 10.21 on White Room), and FovVDP is 10–20× higher, because RGB↔X generates each frame independently without temporal conditioning and exhibits severe hallucinations (misaligned geometry, over-bright output). Against DiffusionRenderer, the method again leads by a wide margin on spatial quality (e.g., PSNR 30.23 vs. 13.84 on Staircase) and on temporal stability (FovVDP 6.44 vs. 3.12), since DiffusionRenderer's 24-frame video segments introduce visible discontinuities when concatenated into long sequences. The one-step inference also makes the method 11× faster than RGB↔X and 179× faster than DiffusionRenderer per frame.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Stable Diffusion Turbo one-step generation paradigm (adversarial diffusion distillation) and the T2I-Adapter mechanism for multi-condition injection, repurposing both for the specific task of screen-space global illumination synthesis from G-buffer signals. It departs from prior diffusion-based rendering work (RGB↔X, DiffusionRenderer) by replacing multi-step or video diffusion with a single-step model and a plug-in Temporal VAE decoder, and from prior neural GI methods (BCNN, Deep Illumination) by leveraging generative diffusion priors rather than purely discriminative regression. The conditioning strategy—routing geometry/materials/lighting through adapters rather than concatenating latents—represents a deliberate departure from the concatenation-based conditioning standard in prior diffusion rendering papers, motivated by distribution-shift concerns in the one-step regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 11–179× runtime reduction (0.29 s vs. 3.25–52 s per frame) makes generative GI practically viable for interactive or near-real-time pipelines, and the TVAE decoder offers a lightweight, drop-in solution for temporal coherence that avoids the memory and compute overhead of video diffusion models. However, the method's reliance on motion vectors for TVAE re-projection limits direct applicability to real-world video (where only approximate optical flow is available), and the small synthetic training set (31k frames) constrains generalization to complex multi-bounce lighting and distant reflections. Open questions include whether a motion-vector-free TVAE can match the current quality, how the approach scales to higher resolutions and longer sequences, and whether the adapter-based conditioning advantage persists in multi-step or higher-fidelity diffusion settings.
