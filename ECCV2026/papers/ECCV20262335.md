## Problem

Under extreme degradation (scale factors >8×), critical facial ID cues in the low-resolution input are severely lost, making face super-resolution highly ill-posed and causing existing methods to hallucinate unrealistic facial details. Reference-based approaches partially address this but fail to disentangle the ID information carried by the degraded LR input from that provided by reference images, leading to over-reliance on unreliable LR cues and poor recovery of fine-grained ID attributes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262335/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution face image whose facial region is masked out (converting the task into an inpainting problem) and a same-ID reference image that is landmark-warped to spatially align with the LR input. During pretraining, GT ID embeddings extracted by a frozen ArcFace encoder serve as an additional condition; during fine-tuning, this is replaced by a single learnable embedding vector optimized on a few target-ID images. The output is a high-resolution face image generated via 20-step DDIM sampling, evaluated at 8× and 16× upsampling scales.

## Method

IDFSR is a two-stage diffusion-based framework built on a U-Net backbone (initialized from DiffAE weights) that explicitly decouples style and identity through three conditioning pathways: (1) the masked LR image is concatenated with the noisy latent as a structural prior, (2) a style embedding extracted from the warped reference is injected via AdaGN layers to provide coarse appearance guidance, and (3) an ID embedding is injected through cross-attention to supply fine-grained identity priors. In the pretraining stage, the ID embedding comes from a frozen ArcFace encoder applied to GT images, while the U-Net and style encoder are trained end-to-end with a standard DDPM denoising objective. In the fine-tuning stage, all backbone parameters are frozen and only the ID embedding is optimized on a small set of target-ID images, enabling personalized fitting without catastrophic forgetting. The authors show that imperfect landmark warping acts as natural data augmentation, pushing the model to rely on the ID embedding rather than copying textures from the reference, which improves robustness to pose and alignment errors.

## Results

On CelebRef-HQ at 8× upscaling, IDFSR (fine-tuned) achieves the best PSNR (28.85 dB), SSIM (0.7604), LPIPS (0.1031), CLIPIQA (0.7092), MUSIQ (73.73), and ID similarity (0.2242), outperforming the next-best method DPI by ~4.8 dB in PSNR and ~0.11 in IDS. At the more extreme 16× setting, IDFSR (FT) leads on all seven metrics, with PSNR 24.09, FID 35.50, and IDS 0.3625, while reference-based baselines ASFFNet and DMDNet degrade severely (IDS 0.7955 and 0.8291 respectively). In facial attribute verification at 16×, IDFSR achieves 89.6% ID verification accuracy and 96.6% race accuracy, substantially exceeding the best competitor DiffFace (73.0% IDV, 77.3% race).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDFSR (FT) dominates all baselines at 16× on every metric, with the largest margins over reference-based methods ASFFNet (IDS 0.3625 vs. 0.7955) and DMDNet (IDS 0.3625 vs. 0.8291), which collapse under extreme degradation due to their reliance on precise spatial alignment. At 8×, IDFSR (FT) leads on PSNR (+4.8 dB over DPI), LPIPS, CLIPIQA, MUSIQ, and IDS, while ASFFNet retains a slight FID edge (26.25 vs. 26.69) and DPI is competitive on SSIM (0.7564 vs. 0.7604). Among non-reference methods, CodeFormer and DifFace are the strongest at 8× on pixel metrics but trail IDFSR (FT) by 4+ dB in PSNR and ~0.11 in IDS. In attribute verification at 16× (Table 2), IDFSR (FT) achieves 89.6% ID verification accuracy versus 73.0% for DiffFace and below 50% for CodeFormer/PGDiff/DR2, while also leading in race accuracy (96.6% vs. 77.3% for DiffFace); it trails DiffFace slightly on emotion accuracy (66.2% vs. 67.4%), attributed to the masking strategy discarding expression cues from the LR input. The pretrained (PT) variant without fine-tuning already surpasses most baselines on IDS at 16× (0.5227 vs. 0.5532 for DPI), confirming that the decoupled conditioning design itself provides substantial ID preservation even before personalized fitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion autoencoder framework of DiffAE (Preechakul et al., CVPR 2022) for its U-Net backbone and style-encoder design, and on DDPM (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2020) as the generative backbone. It departs from prior reference-based FSR methods (GFRNet, ASFFNet, DMDNet) that rely on precise spatial alignment of reference images by instead masking the degraded LR face and explicitly decoupling style (injected via AdaGN from a warped reference) from identity (injected via cross-attention from ArcFace embeddings). The key conceptual shift is treating the LR face as an unreliable ID source under extreme degradation and replacing it with a learnable, personalized ID embedding that is fine-tuned on a handful of target-ID images, rather than relying on pixel-level structural priors from the input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage pretrain-then-fine-tune paradigm is practically attractive: 20 minutes of fine-tuning on a single GPU yields a large ID-consistency gain (89.6% vs. 50.3% ID verification under 16×), and inference takes ~1 second with 20 DDIM steps. However, the method's dependence on reference images for optimal performance and its lower emotion-prediction accuracy (66.2%) relative to some baselines highlight an unresolved tension between ID fidelity and expression preservation. Open questions include whether the personalized ID embedding approach extends to video FSR, multi-ID scenes, or settings where no reference images are available at all, and whether the masking strategy generalizes beyond face to other structured objects.
