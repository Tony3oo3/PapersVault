## Problem

Large vision-language models suffer from severe hallucinations in multi-image tasks because the standard causal attention mechanism enforces unidirectional information flow between images (later images attend to earlier ones but not vice versa), introducing a position bias that undermines symmetric cross-image relational modeling. Existing mitigation methods either target single-image hallucinations, rely on decoding-time adjustments that do not alter the underlying attention structure, or treat each image as an independent context without explicitly modeling inter-image dependencies.

## Input / Output

The method takes N RGB images (each encoded into a sequence of visual tokens by a visual encoder) interleaved with corresponding text segments to form a unified token sequence, along with a multi-image question. It produces a free-form text response. The key architectural detail is that the attention mask is modified at the decoder-layer level: odd-numbered layers apply a selective cross-image mask while even-numbered layers retain the original causal mask, and the final attention is an equal-weight fusion of causal and cross-image attention patterns. Training uses LoRA (rank 16) on 3.6K multi-image samples, with positive responses refined by a Qwen3 model.

## Method

CAPL introduces two complementary components. First, a selective cross-image token interaction mechanism reactivates bidirectional attention between key visual tokens from different images (selected by top-ρ embedding L2-norm) while preserving causal attention within each image, implemented via an alternating odd/even layer mask strategy and equal-weight fusion of causal and cross-image attention matrices. Second, a cross-image attention-guided DPO training strategy constructs positive samples by generating responses under the enhanced cross-image attention (refined by Qwen3) and negative samples by completely truncating all inter-image attention connections, forcing the model to rely solely on individual images and language priors to expose its hallucination patterns. The training objective combines the DPO preference loss with a negative log-likelihood loss on positive samples (weighted by λ), encouraging both preference alignment and token-level imitation of cross-image reasoning trajectories.

## Results

On the multi-image hallucination benchmarks, CAPL achieves consistent gains across all three base models: Qwen2.5-VL improves from 54.60 to 57.76 on BLINK and from 58.42 to 62.00 on MUIRBench; InternVL2.5 improves from 54.81 to 55.76 (BLINK) and 48.54 to 52.12 (MUIRBench); GLM4.1VBase improves from 58.17 to 61.33 (BLINK) and 57.84 to 60.57 (MUIRBench). On general multi-image benchmarks (NLVR2, QBench2, MIBench, MIRB), performance remains stable or slightly improves, e.g., Qwen2.5-VL reaches 80.05 on NLVR2 and 56.55 on MIRB. Single-image capabilities are preserved, with POPE accuracy increasing from 81.23 to 82.94 on Qwen2.5-VL and CHAIRs decreasing from 22.0 to 18.4 on GLM4.1VBase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAPL outperforms all nine baselines on both hallucination benchmarks (BLINK and MUIRBench). On BLINK, the strongest baseline is GLM4.1VBase at 58.17, which CAPL surpasses to 61.33 (+3.16); on MUIRBench, the gap is even larger, with CAPL reaching 60.57 versus 57.84 (+2.73). Against the two dedicated multi-image alignment methods (SOFA and MIA-DPO) under identical architectures, CAPL consistently wins: on Qwen2.5-VL, CAPL scores 57.76/62.00 (BLINK/MUIRBench) versus SOFA's 54.92/59.34 and MIA-DPO's 56.50/56.15; on InternVL2.5, CAPL achieves 55.76/52.12 versus SOFA's 55.02/49.07 and MIA-DPO's 54.45/47.88; on GLM4.1V, CAPL reaches 61.34/60.27 versus SOFA's 58.18/57.23 and MIA-DPO's 59.76/57.23. Notably, MIA-DPO actually degrades MUIRBench performance on Qwen2.5-VL (56.15 vs. base 58.42) and InternVL2.5 (47.88 vs. 48.54), while CAPL improves in all settings. On general multi-image tasks, CAPL maintains or slightly improves scores (e.g., NLVR2: 80.05 vs. 79.85 on Qwen2.5-VL; MIBench: 71.70 vs. 70.86 on GLM4.1VBase), confirming that the hallucination-focused training does not sacrifice general reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for preference alignment but departs from prior multi-image hallucination work (e.g., MIA-DPO, SOFA) by explicitly modeling inter-image attention structure rather than treating each image as an independent context. Where prior decoding-based methods adjust only the output distribution and prior training-based methods still rely on unidirectional causal attention across images, CAPL introduces a selective cross-image bidirectional attention mechanism at the architectural level and uses a truncated-attention variant to construct hallucination-targeted negative samples for DPO. The selective token selection via embedding energy and the alternating odd/even layer mask strategy are novel design choices that balance cross-image interaction with autoregressive stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: multi-image hallucination has received far less attention than single-image hallucination, and the finding that even strong models like GLM4.1VBase-9B benefit from explicit cross-image attention suggests the causal attention asymmetry is a structural limitation rather than a capacity issue. The model-agnostic design (applicable to Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL2.5, GLM4.1VBase) and the use of only 3.6K training samples with LoRA make the approach practical. Open questions include whether the method scales to more than a few images, whether the alternating mask and token selection heuristics generalize to video or long multi-image sequences, and whether the modest 1–3.5 point gains on hallucination benchmarks translate to meaningful improvements in real-world multi-image applications.
