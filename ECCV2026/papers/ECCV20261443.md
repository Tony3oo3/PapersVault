## Problem

Recent RLVR-based reasoning segmentation methods, despite empirical success, fail to fully elicit the latent visual reasoning capabilities of their base MLLMs, and the specific design components responsible for this bottleneck remain underexamined. Additionally, the primary benchmark (ReasonSeg) suffers from annotation errors, limited reasoning depth, and an undefined reasoning taxonomy, making systematic evaluation of advanced methods difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261443/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with an implicit, goal-oriented text query (e.g., 'the object I need to cut the paper') rather than an explicit category name. The MLLM first generates a chain-of-thought reasoning trace, then emits structured JSON predictions containing a semantic label, bounding box coordinates, and a representative point for each target instance. These geometric predictions are passed as visual prompts to a frozen SAM 2 Large model, which produces the final binary segmentation masks; at test time, 32 parallel rollouts are aggregated via IoU-based mask clustering for majority voting.

## Method

StAR retrofits the VisionReasoner framework across four axes: (1) replacing full-parameter tuning with LoRA (rank 64, lr 1e-5, no KL penalty) to preserve the base model's pre-trained knowledge while reducing training cost by >2×; (2) introducing a tiered mask-IoU reward (6 levels from 0 to 5) that incorporates SAM into the RLVR loop, providing fine-grained, curriculum-like feedback that discriminates rollouts more effectively than binary accuracy rewards; (3) proposing REST (Rollout-Expanded Selective-Tuning), which expands the rollout pool from 8–16 to 128–256 and updates only on the m/2 highest- and m/2 lowest-advantage samples, exploiting the compute asymmetry between parallel rollout generation and memory-intensive policy updates to explore a 16× larger reasoning space at ~2× wall-clock cost; and (4) requiring the model to predict a short semantic label immediately before coordinates, which anchors grounding to query semantics and increases visual attention during coordinate prediction from 5.3% to 6.3%. Training proceeds in two stages—Stage 1 on 5k referring-segmentation samples (LVIS, RefCOCOg, gRefCOCO) and Stage 2 on 240 ReasonSeg-X samples using REST—followed by optional test-time scaling via mask-level majority voting with greedy IoU clustering (threshold 0.85) and SAM quality-score-based instance selection.

## Results

On the ReasonSeg test set, StAR (Qwen3-VL 32B + majority voting) achieves 72.7 gIoU / 68.1 cIoU, while the 7B variant reaches 68.5 / 65.0. On the more challenging ReasonSeg-X test set, StAR-32B + MV attains 64.1 overall gIoU (with 60.8 P/F, 74.0 C/KI, 70.2 C/R, and 50.3 C/MH), substantially outperforming VisionReasoner (42.2 overall) with the same 7B backbone. On MMR, StAR-7B achieves 32.4 gIoU (zero-shot), surpassing M2SA models trained on MMR (30.9–31.6). On RefSpatial-Bench, StAR-8B scores 62.00 (Location) and 38.96 (Unseen), outperforming both RoboRefer-8B and Gemini-2.5-Pro.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VisionReasoner (the direct RLVR baseline sharing the same Qwen2.5-VL 7B backbone), StAR-7B + MV improves RS test gIoU from 63.6 to 68.5 (+4.9), RS-R from 64.8 to 70.7 (+5.9), and RS-X test overall from 42.2 to 50.3 (+8.1), with the largest gains on complex reasoning types (C/MH: 23.8 → 30.5). StAR-7B + MV (50.3 RS-X test gIoU) matches or slightly exceeds the much larger SAM 3 Agent 72B (49.8) on ReasonSeg-X, while SAM 3 Agent 72B leads on the simpler RS test (71.8 vs. 68.5). On MMR, StAR-7B (32.4 gIoU zero-shot) outperforms both VisionReasoner (28.4) and M2SA models that were explicitly trained on MMR (30.9–31.6). On RefSpatial-Bench, StAR-8B (62.00 Location / 38.96 Unseen) surpasses RoboRefer-8B (52.00 / 37.66) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (46.96 / 27.14) by 10 and 15 points respectively on the Location split. StAR also dominates all supervised/RL fine-tuning baselines (LISA, SegLLM, READ, CoReS, CoPRS, SAM-R1, SAM-Veteran) by 5–17 gIoU on RS test, confirming that the RLVR + LoRA + REST design substantially outperforms prior approaches across the board.

## Relation to Prior Work

StAR is a systematic retrofit of the VisionReasoner framework (Liu et al., ICLR 2026), which itself decouples an MLLM reasoning module from a frozen SAM 2 mask generator and trains the MLLM via GRPO. The paper departs from VisionReasoner along four axes—replacing full-parameter tuning with high-rank LoRA, augmenting MLLM-level accuracy rewards with a tiered mask-IoU reward (extending SAM-R1's idea), introducing Rollout-Expanded Selective-Tuning (REST) as a drop-in GRPO modification, and adding a label-prediction step to the answer format. It also positions its parallel test-time scaling (mask-level majority voting) as an alternative to the sequential agentic refinement explored in SAM 3 (Carion et al., ICLR 2026), and introduces ReasonSeg-X as a deeper, taxonomy-structured extension of the original ReasonSeg benchmark (Lai et al., CVPR 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic ablation of RLVR design choices (tuning scheme, reward granularity, rollout strategy, output format) for reasoning segmentation provides a practical design space that practitioners can adopt without re-deriving each component. The ReasonSeg-X benchmark, with its four-type reasoning taxonomy and 1,169 carefully verified samples, addresses a real gap: prior benchmarks lacked clear reasoning-type coverage and contained annotation errors that could mislead method comparisons. Open questions include whether REST's exploration-then-selective-update strategy generalizes to other RLVR tasks beyond segmentation, whether parallel TTS can ultimately match or surpass sequential agentic pipelines at larger scales, and whether the four-type taxonomy captures the full spectrum of reasoning needed in embodied settings.
