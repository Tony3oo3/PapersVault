## Problem

Multimodal classifiers achieve superior accuracy but are impractical to deploy due to high inference costs and the scarcity of perfectly paired multi-sensory data. While generative AI can synthesize missing modalities to enable cross-modal distillation, existing pipelines force undecoded generative latents through an expensive decoder into noisy raw signals (the Decode-Encode Loop), wasting computation and degrading the semantic signal. Furthermore, standard L2-based distillation imposes a rigid geometric isomorphism that causes capacity interference, forcing a purely visual student to distort its native features to mimic a high-dimensional multimodal topology.

## Input / Output

At inference the student consumes only a single RGB image and outputs a class label with zero additional overhead. During training, the multimodal teacher additionally receives continuous 3D spatial latents (from Hunyuan3D-2's DiT flow-matching pipeline) and discrete audio codebook tokens (from AudioGen's 4 parallel EnCodec codebooks), both extracted before any waveform or mesh decoding. The student is a ViT-B/16 trained from scratch on standard image patches, while the teacher fuses these heterogeneous inputs via Perceiver cross-attention into a unified token sequence with learnable [SEP] delimiters.

## Method

The framework has three stages. First, Direct Latent Augmentation (DLA) intercepts undecoded generative latents—continuous 3D latents and discrete audio codes—bypassing the decode-encode loop entirely, yielding a cleaner supervision signal with 87.7% less 3D generation time and 23.8% less audio VRAM. Second, a multimodal ViT-B/16 teacher fuses image patches with resampled 3D and audio tokens (via Perceiver cross-attention compressing variable-length latents into 100 tokens each) and is trained with cross-entropy plus aggressive modality dropout (p=0.8) to prevent over-reliance on synthetic inputs. Third, MESSy distillation replaces rigid L2 embedding matching with lightweight 2-layer MLP projection heads on the student's CLS tokens that predict the teacher's average-pooled auxiliary-modality summaries across the last K=3 layers, a predictive objective that lets the student internalize acoustic and geometric priors without collapsing its native visual manifold. The total loss combines cross-entropy, logit-level KD (T=2.0, α=0.25), and the MESSy term (β=1.0); at inference the auxiliary heads are discarded.

## Results

On the three main benchmarks, the MESSy student (ViT-B/16, RGB-only inference) achieves 84.23% on Imagenette2-320, 63.52% on Caltech101, and 68.54% on ImageNet-100, recovering roughly 70–75% of the gap to the multimodal DLA teacher upper bound (93.10 / 66.47 / 71.59). The DLA latent teacher itself outperforms the raw-synthetic-data teacher by +4.82 pp on Imagenette2, +7.39 pp on Caltech101, and +6.19 pp on ImageNet-100, while reducing 3D generation time by 87.7% and audio VRAM by 23.8%. On full ImageNet-1K, the MESSy student reaches 69.05% top-1 (+4.88 pp over the 64.17% image-only baseline), confirming the framework scales predictably.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MESSy consistently outperforms both baselines across all three benchmarks. Against standard logit-level KD [19], MESSy gains +3.57 pp (Imagenette2), +7.03 pp (Caltech101), and +4.57 pp (ImageNet-100), confirming that logit matching alone cannot transfer the geometric and acoustic semantics embedded in the teacher's intermediate representations. Against LUGPI [30], which applies rigid L₂ alignment to the same DLA latent teacher, MESSy adds +2.38 pp, +3.40 pp, and +2.84 pp respectively, demonstrating that the predictive-head objective avoids the capacity interference inherent in forcing a unimodal embedding to geometrically mimic a multimodal manifold. A CIFAR-100 linear-probe evaluation (a purely visual task unseen during training) further isolates this effect: MESSy scores 48.1% versus LUGPI's 42.4% (+5.7 pp), with both students sharing identical training images and teacher, so the gap is attributable solely to the distillation objective. On ImageNet-1K, MESSy recovers 68% of the teacher gap (+4.88 pp over image-only), consistent with the smaller-benchmark ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LUGPI paradigm (Menadil et al., ICPR 2024), which demonstrated that generated privileged information improves student classification, from the text–image domain to scarce physical modalities (3D geometry, audio) and from fully decoded raw signals to undecoded generative latents. Architecturally, MESSy adopts the predictor-head pattern from AV-data2vec (Lian et al., ASRU 2023) and JEPA-style methods, but redirects the prediction target from self-features or the teacher CLS token to pooled summaries of unobserved auxiliary-modality tokens, explicitly to avoid the capacity interference that rigid L₂ alignment induces in asymmetric cross-modal distillation. The Perceiver-based latent resampler for fusing heterogeneous generative latents with image patches draws on the Flamingo/Perceiver lineage, while the information-theoretic justification (Data Processing Inequality along the Z→X̂→F chain) formalizes why skipping the decoder should help.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decode-encode loop is a practical bottleneck for anyone using generative models to synthesize auxiliary modalities for training; showing that undecoded latents are both cleaner (7–11 pp teacher accuracy gain) and dramatically cheaper (87.7% 3D time reduction, 23.8% audio VRAM reduction) gives a concrete, adoptable recipe. The capacity-interference evidence (LUGPI degrading visual discriminability on a held-out linear probe relative to MESSy) is a useful diagnostic for the broader cross-modal distillation community. Limitations include reliance on frozen, off-the-shelf generators with no task-specific latent co-adaptation, evaluation confined to classification, and the audio pipeline's dependence on an LLM captioning step that adds a potential failure point. Open questions include whether co-training the generator with the teacher closes the remaining gap to the synthetic upper bound, and whether the approach transfers to detection or segmentation tasks.
