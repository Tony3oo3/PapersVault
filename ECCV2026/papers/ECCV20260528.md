## Problem

State-of-the-art multi-object tracking methods depend on dense, frame-by-frame bounding box annotations that are prohibitively expensive to scale, especially in crowded or panoramic scenes. Point-level supervision is far cheaper (0.7–0.9 s per point vs. 7–10 s per box) but introduces a fundamental precision-ambiguity paradox: a scale-less point lacks geometric structure, causing spatial leakage into backgrounds and identity drift across frames. No prior framework effectively recovers stable, identity-consistent instance representations from such sparse, scale-free seeds in a video tracking setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260528/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the model is fully point-free, consuming only RGB video frames (30-frame clips) and producing tracked bounding boxes with persistent identity labels, identical in interface to standard MOT systems. During training, the sole supervision signal is a single pixel coordinate per object per frame; pseudo-bounding boxes are generated offline via SAM prompted with positive/negative points and Kalman-projected motion priors, then fed as soft targets. The PEWA module and SAM calls are bypassed at test time, so inference speed matches the underlying MOTIP backbone.

## Method

PS-Track orchestrates a coarse-to-fine point-to-instance evolution across three levels. At the data level, Temporal-Feedback Prompting (TFP) feeds SAM a positive point plus negative cues sampled from spatial neighbors and a Kalman-projected motion box, producing temporally consistent pseudo-boxes with a Joint Quality Score that fuses visual confidence and motion consistency. At the model level, Point-Excited Wavelet Attention (PEWA) applies a single-level Haar DWT to decouple features into low-frequency context and high-frequency edges; a Gaussian heatmap centered at the annotated point generates a sigmoid excitation mask that selectively amplifies HF wavelet coefficients, hallucinating sharp boundaries without heavy upsampling, then IDWT reconstructs the refined feature. At the loss level, Uncertainty-Guided Gaussian Learning (UGL) treats each pseudo-box as a Gaussian observation whose variance is inversely proportional to the Joint Quality Score, so the negative log-likelihood loss dynamically down-weights unreliable targets and prevents overfitting to segmentation noise. The full system is built on MOTIP (Deformable DETR + ResNet-50) and is plug-and-play across association-based, autoregressive, and query-based tracking paradigms.

## Results

On DanceTrack, PS-Track achieves 52.3 HOTA and 53.4 IDF1 using only point annotations, surpassing fully-supervised CenterTrack (41.8 HOTA) by +10.5 and FairMOT (39.7 HOTA) by +12.6. On the embodied EmboTrack QuadTrack dataset, PS-Track delivers 33.9 HOTA, outperforming ByteTrack (20.7) and OC-SORT (20.8) by over 13 points. On JRDB, PS-Track reaches 20.72 HOTA, exceeding TrackFormer (19.16) and DiffMOT (19.96). However, PS-Track trails the strongest fully-supervised baseline MOTIP by 17.3 HOTA on DanceTrack (52.3 vs. 69.6) and by 27.4 HOTA on SportsMOT (45.2 vs. 72.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PS-Track (point-supervised) beats several fully bounding-box-supervised methods: on DanceTrack it surpasses CenterTrack by +10.5 HOTA and FairMOT by +12.6 HOTA, and on EmboTrack QuadTrack it outperforms ByteTrack by +13.2 HOTA and OC-SORT by +13.1 HOTA. On JRDB, PS-Track (20.72 HOTA) exceeds TrackFormer (19.16) and DiffMOT (19.96), though it trails the strongest panoramic method OmniTrack++DA (27.03) by 6.3 HOTA. Against the top fully-supervised query-based tracker MOTIP, PS-Track lags by 17.3 HOTA on DanceTrack (52.3 vs. 69.6) and 27.4 HOTA on SportsMOT (45.2 vs. 72.6), reflecting the inherent information gap between a single point and a full bounding box. On SportsMOT, PS-Track (45.2 HOTA) underperforms all listed fully-supervised baselines, with the closest being FairMOT (49.3), suggesting that rapid pose deformations in sports scenes remain challenging for point-only supervision. The paper also demonstrates that a naive TBD pipeline coupling Point2RBox-v3 with off-the-shelf trackers collapses to ~11 HOTA, whereas integrating PS-Track's unified framework with YOLOX+ByteTrack recovers 42.9 HOTA, highlighting that the temporal and uncertainty components are essential rather than optional.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes Point-supervised MOT (PS-MOT) as a new task, drawing on the point-to-box detection lineage (P2BNet, Point2RBox series) but extending it to the temporal tracking domain where prior work has been largely absent. Architecturally, it builds on MOTIP (a query-based Transformer tracker) and leverages SAM as a foundation-model prior for pseudo-label generation, while the PEWA module adapts wavelet-based frequency decoupling (inspired by WaveFormer) to the tracking context. The loss design borrows from heteroscedastic uncertainty modeling (Kendall & Gal) but applies it specifically to the noisy pseudo-labels produced by SAM-based segmentation, creating a data-to-loss feedback loop that is novel in the MOT setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 64% reduction in annotation time (0.7–0.9 s per point vs. 7–10 s per box) is practically significant for embodied and panoramic tracking scenarios (JRDB, EmboTrack) where dense BBox annotation is prohibitively expensive, and the plug-and-play versatility across TBD, autoregressive, and query-based paradigms broadens its applicability. However, the performance gap to fully-supervised SOTA remains substantial (52.3 vs. 69.6 HOTA on DanceTrack; 45.2 vs. 72.6 on SportsMOT), and the reliance on SAM for offline pseudo-label generation introduces a dependency that may not transfer to real-time or resource-constrained deployments. Open questions include whether the framework can operate without any foundation-model prior at training time, how it scales to even sparser supervision (e.g., one point per tracklet rather than per frame), and whether the wavelet-based boundary hallucination generalizes to 3D or LiDAR tracking.
