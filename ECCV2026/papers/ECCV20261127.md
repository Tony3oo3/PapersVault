## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting is vulnerable to adversarial perturbations in input views that degrade rendering quality, inflate training time and memory usage, and can cause server denial-of-service, yet effective defense strategies remain largely unexplored. Standard adversarial defenses (adversarial training, bi-level optimization) are incompatible with 3DGS because it lacks a fixed network architecture, has non-invertible attack objectives, and operates without clean ground-truth supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view posed RGB images (potentially adversarially perturbed) along with their camera poses, and produces a defended 3D Gaussian Splatting scene representation plus rendered images for downstream use. The wavelet filtering is applied channel-wise to each RGB image before 3DGS training begins, and the method explicitly does not require access to clean ground-truth images. An optional fine-grained filter additionally uses the TSP-ordered camera trajectory and pairwise feature matching (SuperPoint + LightGlue) to selectively retain cross-view-consistent high-frequency details.

## Method

DefenseSplat applies a one-level Discrete Wavelet Transform to decompose each input image into LL, LH, HL, and HH subbands, then zeros out all high-frequency subbands and reconstructs the image via inverse DWT using only the LL subband, exploiting the observation that adversarial perturbations concentrate in high-frequency components while the LL subband retains over 95% of total image energy. During 3DGS training, the method adds a ReLU-based scale regularization loss on the normalized variance of Gaussian scales to suppress elongated Gaussians that would otherwise overfit to residual adversarial textures, without penalizing small spherical or large flat Gaussians that encode genuine scene structure. An optional fine-grained cross-view consistency filter orders cameras via a TSP tour (solved with Lin-Kernighan) and uses SuperPoint/LightGlue matching on wavelet subbands to selectively retain high-frequency coefficients that are consistent across neighboring views, recovering authentic details lost by the coarse filter. The 3DGS optimization itself further suppresses residual artifacts because Gaussians fitting low-consistency regions average colors across views, naturally blurring view-inconsistent perturbations.

## Results

Under Poison-Splat attack (ε=16/255), DefenseSplat+ReLU achieves the best PSNR on both Mip-NeRF 360 (27.47 dB) and Tanks-and-Temples (26.42 dB), outperforming 3DGS (25.18/24.88), CompactGS (24.95/24.37), and Difix3D+ (24.52/24.75). It also attains the lowest GPU memory (11,400 MB / 5,858 MB), fastest training time (33:49 / 19:19), and highest rendering speed (258 / 498 FPS) across both datasets. On clean inputs, the method degrades PSNR by only ~1.75% and SSIM by ~2.5%, confirming minimal impact on legitimate reconstruction quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DefenseSplat+ReLU dominates all three baselines on PSNR, SSIM, GPU memory, training time, and FPS across both Mip-NeRF 360 and Tanks-and-Temples. Against vanilla 3DGS, it improves PSNR by +2.29 dB (Mip-NeRF) and +1.54 dB (T&T) while reducing GPU memory by ~46% and ~72% respectively. Compared to CompactGS, which reduces Gaussian count but still overfits to adversarial perturbations, DefenseSplat+ReLU achieves higher PSNR (+2.52/+2.05 dB) and SSIM (+0.165/+0.183) with fewer Gaussians. Against Difix3D+, the diffusion-based baseline, DefenseSplat+ReLU wins on PSNR (+2.95/+1.67 dB) and FPS (+121/+208), though Difix3D+ retains a slight LPIPS edge on Tanks-and-Temples (0.3031 vs. 0.3134). The paper notes that Difix3D+ introduces spurious textures or removes genuine details (e.g., rust on truck hubs), whereas DefenseSplat preserves authentic scene structure while suppressing adversarial artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard adversarial defense paradigm (adversarial training, bi-level optimization) that is inapplicable to 3DGS due to its self-supervised, non-fixed-architecture nature, and instead repurposes Discrete Wavelet Transform—a tool previously applied to 3D reconstruction quality (FreGS, 3D-GSW)—as a defense mechanism. It builds on Poison-Splat as the primary attack target and leverages established feature-matching tools (SuperPoint, LightGlue) combined with a TSP-based camera ordering to perform cross-view consistency analysis in the wavelet domain. The method also draws on the observation that 3DGS's adaptive density control makes it uniquely vulnerable to perturbations that inflate Gaussian counts, motivating the ReLU-based scale regularization loss as a complement to frequency filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3DGS moves toward commercial cloud deployment, the demonstrated vulnerability to computation-cost attacks (inflated training time, GPU memory, and Gaussian counts) poses a real denial-of-service risk that this work addresses with a simple, plug-and-play filter requiring no clean ground-truth supervision. A key limitation is that evaluation is restricted to Poison-Splat and its Adaptive Attack variant; generalizability to future, structurally different 3D attacks remains an open question. The fine-grained cross-view consistency filter, while effective, adds preprocessing complexity, and the all-or-nothing coarse filter's slight degradation on clean inputs (PSNR −1.75%) may matter in latency-sensitive or quality-critical pipelines.
