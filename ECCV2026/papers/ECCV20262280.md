## Problem

Formal robustness verification has achieved significant success for image classifiers but remains intractable for object detection due to non-linear coordinate transformations (offset-to-box decoding) and the Intersection-over-Union metric. Prior OD verification methods were limited to shallow regression toy models that lack the backbone-neck-head structure, multi-scale prediction heads, and anchor-based decoding of real detectors like SSD and YOLO, making them unable to certify practically relevant architectures.

## Input / Output

The verifier takes a trained anchor-based detector (SSD, YOLOv2-tiny, or YOLOv3-tiny with 8.7–11.3M parameters), a single-object RGB image at 64×64 or 128×128 resolution, a perturbation specification (brightness, contrast, or motion blur with budget ε), and a ground truth box with class label. It outputs a three-valued verdict (ROBUST, NONROBUST, or UNKNOWN) together with the computed IoU bounds and confidence-score bounds for the top-scoring candidate box. Verification is performed offline on 50 correctly classified images per dataset, and the single-object setting means correctness depends only on the IoU between one prediction and the ground truth, with NMS reduced to selecting the highest-confidence box above a threshold.

## Method

IoUCert introduces a coordinate transformation that reparameterises the IoU bounding problem from corner coordinates back to anchor offsets, exploiting the injectivity of the box-decoding map ψ∘φ to avoid propagating bounds through non-linear decoding functions. This enables a novel Interval Bound Propagation scheme that derives provably optimal IoU bounds by enumerating 169 candidate extreme points (13 per 2D plane: 4 feasible-region corners, 8 ground-truth-line/boundary intersections, and 1 ground-truth corner) and evaluating IoU at each. For YOLOv3's LeakyReLU activations, the paper derives a closed-form optimal lower relaxation that minimises local relaxation error by choosing the slope based on the sign and magnitude of the input interval. The full pipeline is integrated into the Venus branch-and-bound verifier, where tighter IoU bounds prune subproblems and branching resolves remaining UNKNOWN cases to achieve completeness.

## Results

IoUCert improves IoU bound tightness by 50–65% over the Cohen et al. [19] baseline across all IoU ranges, with the largest gains (≈65%) at mid-range IoU (0.10–0.20). At high IoU ranges (0.50–0.99), the tighter bounds prune over 95% of branch-and-bound subproblems that the looser baseline would explore. On the YOLOv3-tiny model trained on LARD at 64×64, all 50 test images are certified robust (50/50) for brightness, contrast, and 0° motion blur perturbations up to ε = 0.30, with mean verification times of 3–56 s. For the larger SSD model (11.3 M params) on LARD at 128×128, 48/50 images are robust at ε = 0.01 brightness (mean time 29 s), degrading to 9/50 at ε = 0.30.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole direct numerical comparison is against the IoU bounding method of Cohen et al. [19] (Verifiou), which the authors reimplement within the same Venus verification framework. IoUCert's optimal IoU bounds are consistently 50–65% tighter than Cohen et al.'s across every IoU range, with the peak improvement of 65.09% at IoU 0.10–0.20. The practical impact is most dramatic at high IoU values (≥ 0.50), where the tighter bounds cause 95–99% of branch-and-bound subproblems to be pruned entirely, whereas at low IoU (< 0.50) the pruning benefit is minimal (< 1%) because the verification tree is shallow. The authors note that the two methods yield similar end-to-end verification outcomes: IoUCert's tighter bounds cost more per evaluation call but reduce the number of branches explored, while Cohen et al.'s looser bounds are cheaper per call but require more branching. No head-to-head wall-clock comparison table is provided; the comparison is framed as a bound-tightness and branch-pruning analysis rather than a direct runtime race.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Venus branch-and-bound verifier (Kouvaros & Lomuscio, IJCAI 2021) as its computational backbone and extends the LeakyReLU relaxation of Mellouki et al. (IEEE Access 2023) with an optimal choice of the lower-bound slope. It departs sharply from prior OD verification work (Cohen et al. 2025, Raviv et al. 2024, Chowdhury et al. 2025, Nirala & Sarkar 2025), which either bounded IoU on raw corner coordinates or encoded it as a network layer, and was limited to shallow regression toy models. The key conceptual departure is a coordinate transformation that re-expresses the IoU optimisation directly in anchor-offset space, bypassing the non-linear box-construction map entirely and enabling optimal (not merely sound) IoU bounds for the first time on real anchor-based architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IoUCert is the first method to provide formal robustness certificates for foundational anchor-based detectors (SSD, YOLOv2, YOLOv3) at realistic resolutions and on multi-class datasets, opening a path toward safety-certified deployment in domains like runway detection. The single-object scope and the exclusion of NMS, multi-scale head competition, and transformer-based detectors (DETR) are significant open problems; the paper itself identifies NMS-aware pairwise IoU bounding as the most promising next step. The practical finding that replacing MaxPool with AvgPool (a <0.3 mAP cost) is decisive for verifiability highlights a broader tension between standard architecture choices and formal-verification tractability that the field will need to address as verification matures.
