## Problem

Neural simulators for autonomous driving have largely focused on RGB imagery and LiDAR, leaving radar as an open frontier despite its ubiquity in production vehicles. Radar's distinctive sensing characteristics—extreme sparsity, non-uniform sampling, and sensor-specific attributes like RCS and Doppler—make it fundamentally harder to model from vision than LiDAR, and no existing generative framework can produce scene-level radar point clouds from multi-view camera images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261048/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view camera images at two consecutive timesteps (t and t+Δt) along with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, which are processed through pretrained foundation models (UniDepthV2, Mask2Former, UniFlow) to produce BEV-aligned conditioning maps. The output is a sparse, unordered radar point cloud in the ego-vehicle frame, where each point carries 2D planar coordinates (x, y), radar cross-section (RCS), and Doppler velocity, restricted to a ±50 m range on a 512×512 BEV grid. Unlike LiDAR, radar detections arise from peak-based extraction (CFAR) and cannot form dense range images, necessitating the sparse point-cloud output format.

## Method

RadarGen adapts SANA's efficient image-latent diffusion (32× compression autoencoder with O(N) linear-attention DiT) to the radar domain by representing each radar point cloud as three single-channel BEV images: a Point Density Map (Gaussian-convolved sparse points), and RCS and Doppler maps (Voronoi tessellations), each replicated to three channels for autoencoder compatibility. Conditioning is provided by BEV-projected depth, semantic segmentation, and radial velocity maps from pretrained foundation models, concatenated along the channel dimension with the target latents. The DiT denoiser processes all three radar map latents jointly in a shared token sequence with modality-specific learnable embeddings to capture cross-attribute correlations while preserving per-channel statistics. At inference, the generated blurred density map is inverted via an L1-regularized non-negative deconvolution (LASSO) solved with an IRL1/FISTA scheme to recover discrete point locations, from which RCS and Doppler values are read out at the corresponding grid positions.

## Results

On MAN TruckScenes, RadarGen outperforms the RGB2Point baseline across nearly all metrics. For the entire scene, RadarGen achieves IoU@1m of 0.31 vs. 0.23, DA F1 of 0.24 vs. 0.14, and MMD Location of 0.056 vs. 0.368 (lower is better). For foreground objects, RadarGen more than doubles the Hit Rate (0.66 vs. 0.37) and reduces per-object MMD RCS by over an order of magnitude (e.g., Car RCS MMD: 0.006 vs. 0.753). A VoxelNeXt detector trained on real radar achieves NDS 0.30 on RadarGen's output versus near-zero on the baseline, confirming downstream usability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is RGB2Point [39], a feedforward multi-view-to-point-cloud model extended to predict RCS and Doppler (432M params vs. RadarGen's 592M). RadarGen wins decisively on geometric fidelity: CD Location drops from 1.84 to 1.68 (entire area) and 1.32 to 0.95 (foreground), while IoU@1m rises from 0.23 to 0.31. Radar attribute fidelity shows the largest gap—DA F1 nearly doubles (0.14→0.24) and per-object MMD RCS improves by 100×+ (Car: 0.753→0.006; Truck: 0.202→0.031). The one metric where the baseline edges ahead is CD Full on the entire area (0.038 vs. 0.040), which the authors attribute to the baseline being trained with a similar composite loss. Downstream, a VoxelNeXt detector scores NDS 0.30 on RadarGen output versus ~0 on the baseline, confirming that only RadarGen produces radar point clouds interpretable by real-data-trained perception models.

## Relation to Prior Work

RadarGen positions itself at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) efficient latent diffusion for image synthesis (SANA's 32× autoencoder + linear-attention DiT), repurposed here to generate radar BEV maps rather than natural images, and (2) the emerging literature on sensor point-cloud generation from cameras, which has so far focused on LiDAR (Ran et al., Hu et al.) or deterministic radar regression from LiDAR/RGB (Song et al., Alkanat et al.). The key departure is the BEV image representation of sparse radar (Gaussian-smoothed density + Voronoi-tessellated RCS/Doppler maps) that makes a fundamentally non-grid, sparse modality compatible with a pretrained image autoencoder, combined with BEV-aligned conditioning from frozen foundation models (UniDepthV2, Mask2Former, UniFlow) rather than raw multi-view tokens or explicit physical signal modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first probabilistic, scene-level framework that generates full automotive radar point clouds (location, RCS, Doppler) from surround cameras, opening a path to radar data augmentation via off-the-shelf image editing and to radar-capable simulation without re-recording. The demonstrated compatibility with a real-data-trained VoxelNeXt detector (NDS 0.30 vs. ~0 for the baseline) is practically significant for perception development. However, performance is bounded by upstream foundation-model quality (night scenes, reflections, occlusions), the model can hallucinate points in camera-unseen regions, and no explicit radar physics (multipath, material-dependent scattering) is modeled—addressing these would require large-scale raw radar data that is currently unavailable. Open directions include video conditioning, text-based control, and multi-dataset/multi-radar-configuration training.
