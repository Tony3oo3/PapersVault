## Problem

Recovering continuous spatio-temporal dynamics from sparse, noisy, and indirect measurements (e.g., Fourier-domain interferometric visibilities or sparse weather station readings) remains an open challenge when ground truth, reliable simulators, or known governing equations are unavailable. Existing approaches are either biased by simulation training (operator learning), require known PDEs (PINNs), or assume dense observations (standard DMD, neural fields), leaving no method that is simultaneously simulation-free, compatible with indirect measurement operators, and capable of interpretable forecasting under extreme sparsity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes either sparse image-domain samples at arbitrary (x,y,t) coordinates (e.g., ~500–5000 weather station readings per frame) or sparse Fourier-domain visibilities at time-varying (u,v,t) coordinates with per-measurement uncertainty estimates (e.g., EHT 2017 array). It produces a continuous, grid-free spatio-temporal field I(x,y,t) evaluable at any coordinate, an interpretable set of spatial modes w_j(x,y) paired with complex spectral coefficients Ω_j encoding decay rates and oscillation frequencies, and future-frame forecasts beyond the observation window. In the Fourier-domain setting, the neural field is passed through a differentiable 2D Fourier transform before comparison to visibilities, and complex gain corruptions per station are marginalized during fitting.

## Method

NeuralDMD extends optDMD by parameterizing each spatial mode w_j(x,y) as a coordinate MLP (4 blocks, 256 hidden units, SiLU activations, NeRF-style sinusoidal positional encoding) and the spectral coefficients Ω_j and initial amplitudes b_j as lightweight networks (16D learnable latent → 2-layer MLP, 64 units) rather than direct parameters. The key architectural insight is enforcing a low-rank linear dynamics prior with explicit spectral time evolution e^{Ω_j t}, which provides temporal coherence and interpretability while the neural fields enable grid-free evaluation at arbitrary coordinates. Training is per-instance and simulation-free: the model is fit directly to measurement residuals (L2 loss on sparse image samples or χ² loss on Fourier visibilities) with the real part of the spectrum constrained to be non-positive (α_j ≤ 0) to prevent unphysical exponential growth. Unlike optDMD, the initial state b_j is inferred jointly from all measurements rather than requiring direct access to X_0, and the Fourier-domain loss applies a differentiable measurement operator to the neural field, making the framework extensible to arbitrary inverse problems.

## Results

On weather data assimilation (ERA5, ~5000 stations/frame), NeuralDMD achieves RMSE 1.5±0.1, PSNR 24.7±0.5, SSIM 0.70±0.01, and LPIPS 0.32±0.01, outperforming 3D-VAR (RMSE 3.6±0.8) and SIREN (RMSE 2.3±0.3). Under extreme sparsity (~500 stations/frame), NeuralDMD maintains RMSE 2.3±0.1 while SIREN degrades to 3.0±0.3 and vanilla MLP to 2.7±0.2. On EHT 2017 Sgr A* interferometric reconstruction, NeuralDMD produces lower per-frame image-space MSE than StarWarps, resolve, optDMD, and a spatio-temporal INR throughout the fitting window, and remains stable during 1.4-hour extrapolation where the vanilla MLP diverges. On the wave-equation OOD test (40% sparsity), NeuralDMD achieves PSNR 43±1 versus CORAL's 30±0.3, demonstrating robustness to distribution shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuralDMD outperforms 3D-VAR by a wide margin at ~5000 samples/frame (RMSE 1.5 vs. 3.6; PSNR 24.7 vs. 18), and 3D-VAR is inapplicable at ~500 samples. Against spatio-temporal INR baselines (vanilla MLP and SIREN), NeuralDMD achieves lower RMSE at both sparsity levels (e.g., 2.3 vs. 3.0 for SIREN at ~500 samples) and, critically, produces stable short-term forecasts beyond the observation window where INR baselines lack explicit dynamics and degrade. On the EHT 2017 black hole imaging task, NeuralDMD yields lower per-frame image-space MSE than StarWarps, resolve, optDMD, and the vanilla MLP INR throughout the fitting window, and is the only method that supports coherent 1.4-hour extrapolation (StarWarps cannot forecast; the vanilla MLP diverges). In the wave-equation OOD comparison, NeuralDMD (PSNR 43, RMSE 0.014) dramatically outperforms the simulation-trained CORAL operator (PSNR 30, RMSE 0.05), highlighting the advantage of per-instance fitting over simulation-trained priors under distribution shift. In the EHT ablation (Table 3), NeuralDMD with a vanilla MLP backbone (RMSE 0.0349, PSNR 29.18) surpasses SIREN (RMSE 0.072), MFN (RMSE 0.13), and Fourier-feature MLP (RMSE 0.093) backbones, confirming that the low-rank dynamical inductive bias provides benefits beyond the choice of coordinate network.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuralDMD sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD/optDMD), which extracts interpretable low-rank spectral dynamics from time-ordered snapshots, and neural implicit representations (NeRF-style coordinate MLPs), which provide grid-free continuous spatial parameterization. The key departure is that standard DMD requires densely observed states on a fixed grid, while standard neural fields overfit under extreme sparsity without a dynamical prior; NeuralDMD fuses the two by parameterizing DMD spatial modes as neural fields and fitting the combined model directly to sparse measurements (image-domain or Fourier-domain) without simulation training or known PDEs. This positions it against simulation-driven operator learning (CORAL, FNO), physics-informed networks (PINNs, PI-DEF), and grid-based DMD variants, claiming to be the first approach that is simultaneously simulation-free, supports indirect measurement operators, and yields interpretable forecasting under extreme sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework is particularly relevant for scientific discovery settings where governing physics is uncertain and faithful simulations are unavailable, as it lets data inform or challenge existing models rather than constraining inference to pre-encoded simulation behavior. The interpretable output (spatial modes + continuous-time spectra) provides direct physical quantities like characteristic timescales, which is valuable for hypothesis generation in fields like black hole astrophysics and atmospheric science. The main open question is the linear-dynamics assumption: the Navier-Stokes experiments show forecasting degrades as nonlinearity increases (Re=5200), and extending the framework to genuinely nonlinear or time-varying dynamics while preserving interpretability remains unresolved. Additionally, the method's reliance on per-instance fitting (no amortization) limits scalability to very long sequences or high-dimensional state spaces.
