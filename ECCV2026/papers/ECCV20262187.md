## Problem

Autoregressive video diffusion models suffer from compounding error accumulation over long horizons, but existing mitigations (Self-Forcing, LongLive) only suppress generic noise drift while leaving a more fundamental failure mode unaddressed: irreversible structural hallucinations such as objects vanishing or characters clipping through solids. These artifacts arise because forward-only generation lacks any temporal reversibility constraint, so the model never verifies that a generated state could logically have produced its own history.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt as input and autoregressively generates latent chunks (each corresponding to a short temporal segment) in a shared Video VAE latent space, producing videos up to 60 seconds. Generation proceeds chunk-by-chunk with a KV-cache of previously generated latents as conditioning context, and the reverse model operates on the same latent manifold, requiring no pixel-space decode-flip-encode loops. The output is a temporally consistent, physically grounded video sequence evaluated on VBench (5s and 60s) and physical-consistency benchmarks (VideoPhy PC, PACE).

## Method

Cycle-World enforces temporal reversibility through a dual-phase strategy grounded in the Cycle-Bounded Drift (CBD) theorem, which proves that forward generative drift is upper-bounded by the reverse-prediction cycle-consistency error scaled geometrically with sequence length. During training (Cycle-Consistent Learning), a reverse-prediction model R_φ is jointly optimized with the forward generator G_θ via a cycle-consistency loss L_cycle that penalizes the inability to reconstruct the preceding latent chunk from the current one; a key design choice called Intrinsic Latent Reversibility formulates the reverse task directly in latent space, avoiding the intractable pixel-latent mismatch of naive temporal flipping. During inference (Cycle-Guided Inference), the frozen R_φ is repurposed as a differentiable runtime critic: at selected early denoising timesteps, the cycle discrepancy between the confirmed previous chunk and the time-flipped reverse reconstruction is computed, and gradient descent on this discrepancy refines the current latent before it is committed to the KV cache, actively pruning non-physical artifacts without any architectural modification to the forward model.

## Results

On VBench, Cycle-World (1.3B) achieves the highest Total Quality score of 84.36 and Semantic score of 86.14 at 5 seconds, and maintains 82.88 Total / 76.86 Semantic at 60 seconds — the best among all compared models. The degradation from 5s to 60s is only 1.48 points in Total score, compared to 12.45 for Self-Forcing (84.31→71.86) and 12.20 for SkyReels-V2 (82.67→70.47). On physical consistency (VideoPhy), Cycle-World scores 69.40 on Physical Commonsense and 81.91 on PACE (average 75.66), outperforming the next-best baseline Rolling Forcing (67.91 / 75.05, avg 71.48) by over 4 points in average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cycle-World outperforms all 13 baselines on every reported metric at both 5s and 60s horizons. At 5s, it edges out the closest competitors Self Forcing (84.31) and LongLive (83.72) by 0.05 and 0.64 points in Total score, respectively, while leading in Quality (86.14 vs. 85.42 for LongLive). The advantage becomes decisive at 60s: Cycle-World's Total score of 82.88 surpasses LongLive (82.62) and Context Forcing (82.45), while standard autoregressive baselines collapse dramatically (Self Forcing drops to 71.86, SkyReels-V2 to 70.47). On physical consistency (Table 2), Cycle-World leads by a wide margin — 69.40 PC and 81.91 PACE (avg 75.66) versus Rolling Forcing's 67.91/75.05 (avg 71.48) and LongLive's 61.94/78.03 (avg 69.99) — confirming that cycle-consistency enforcement yields superior physical plausibility beyond what generic long-video strategies achieve. Notably, Cycle-World matches or exceeds the 4.5B MAGI-1 model (79.18 at 5s) while using only 1.3B parameters, and its 60s Semantic score (76.86) exceeds even the bidirectional Wan2.1's 5s Semantic (80.09 is not directly comparable, but Wan2.1 has no 60s results reported).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive video diffusion lineage (Self-Forcing, CausVid, LongLive) that addresses train-inference mismatch and error accumulation, but departs from their strictly unidirectional, forward-time optimization by introducing a reverse-prediction cycle-consistency constraint. While prior methods (Diffusion Forcing, Self-Forcing, LongLive) suppress generic noise drift through distribution matching or self-rollout training, they lack bidirectional physical verification, leaving them vulnerable to irreversible structural hallucinations. The forward training objective still relies on Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD), and the reverse model is trained under the Self-Forcing paradigm, making the contribution a principled augmentation rather than a replacement of the existing pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Cycle-Bounded Drift theorem provides the first formal guarantee that forward generative drift is bottlenecked by reverse reconstruction error, giving practitioners a concrete optimization target for long-horizon stability. The plug-and-play nature of Cycle-Guided Inference (CGI) is practically significant: it can be applied to any frozen autoregressive generator sharing the same VAE without retraining, lowering the barrier to adoption. Open questions include how the Lipschitz constant C in the theoretical bound behaves in practice for very long sequences, whether the approach extends to interactive or controllable generation settings, and whether the additional reverse-model inference cost remains acceptable at real-time frame rates.
