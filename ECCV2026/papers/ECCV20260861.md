## Problem

Existing video depth estimation methods face a fundamental trade-off: discriminative models achieve per-frame geometric accuracy but suffer temporal drift from narrow context windows, while generative diffusion-based methods improve consistency and generalization but require 10M+ training samples and lack geometric precision. The paper asks whether a single model can simultaneously satisfy temporal consistency, geometric accuracy, and cross-domain generalization while being trained on a fraction of the data used by competing generative approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB video as input, which is VAE-encoded into a conditioning latent and also processed by DINOv2 to extract semantic feature maps; spatial dimensions (h, w) are additionally encoded as sinusoidal resolution embeddings. The output is a temporally consistent sequence of relative depth maps (normalized to [-1,1] via per-video min-max disparity), produced by solving a flow-matching ODE in 5 sampling steps. The model handles arbitrary aspect ratios and resolutions up to 1080p at inference, with a training token budget of H×W×T = 672×384×77 that adjusts temporal length inversely with spatial resolution.

## Method

ICDepth adapts the pretrained Wan 2.1 1.3B text-to-video diffusion transformer for depth estimation via In-Context Conditioning, where noisy depth latents and clean RGB conditioning latents are concatenated along the token dimension (rather than channel dimension) so the VDiT's native 3D self-attention directly models cross-modal interactions. Two key components address the challenges of this transfer: (1) SAND-Attention applies shared RoPE positional indices to depth and RGB tokens to enforce precise spatial-temporal alignment, then uses a decoupled attention mask that allows noisy depth tokens to attend to clean RGB tokens but blocks the reverse direction, while zeroing timestep embeddings for RGB tokens to prevent noise contamination; (2) SRFM injects DINOv2 semantic features and sinusoidal resolution embeddings into each transformer block via scale-shift modulation after the MLP layer, compensating for the generative model's lack of geometric precision and resolution awareness. Training uses a flow-matching objective with masked loss over valid depth regions, requiring only 0.8M synthetic frames (VKITTI, TartanAir, TartanGround, OmniWorld) across 8 epochs on 4 H800 GPUs.

## Results

ICDepth achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot video depth estimation on Sintel (AbsRel 0.250, δ₁ 0.749), KITTI (AbsRel 0.061, δ₁ 0.968), and Bonn (AbsRel 0.053, δ₁ 0.979), with competitive second-best performance on ScanNet (AbsRel 0.076, δ₁ 0.952). On Sintel, it improves AbsRel by 16.0% and δ₁ by 10.1% over the second-best method (Depth Any Video). Remarkably, these results are obtained with only 0.8M training frames—a 6–13× reduction compared to competing generative methods (6M–10.5M frames). On 500-frame ScanNet++ sequences, ICDepth also achieves the lowest Temporal Alignment Error (2.61) and best long-sequence accuracy (AbsRel 0.131, δ₁ 0.832).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICDepth outperforms all five baselines on Sintel, KITTI, and Bonn. On Sintel (the most challenging benchmark with dynamic scenes), it beats Depth Any Video (AbsRel 0.300→0.250, δ₁ 0.643→0.749) and DepthCrafter (0.313→0.250, 0.680→0.749) by large margins, and substantially surpasses the discriminative Video Depth Anything (0.383) and Depth Anything V2 (0.403). On KITTI, ICDepth achieves AbsRel 0.061 vs. 0.098 for Depth Any Video (38% relative improvement) and 0.105 for DepthCrafter. On Bonn, it matches Video Depth Anything's AbsRel (0.053) while achieving a higher δ₁ (0.979 vs. 0.975). On ScanNet, ICDepth is essentially tied with Video Depth Anything (AbsRel 0.076 vs. 0.075) but trails slightly on δ₁ (0.952 vs. 0.954), making it the only dataset where it does not claim the top spot. ChronoDepth is consistently the weakest baseline across all four datasets, confirming that its approach to temporal modeling is less effective than diffusion-based or dedicated temporal-head methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior generative video depth methods (DepthCrafter, Depth Any Video) that build on U-Net-based Stable Video Diffusion with factorized spatio-temporal attention, instead leveraging the native 3D attention of the Wan 2.1 video diffusion transformer. It also departs from the standard channel-concatenation conditioning used in those works, adopting the In-Context Conditioning (ICC) paradigm from the diffusion-transformer control literature (OmniControl, FullDiT) but identifying and solving two critical failures—noise contamination via bidirectional attention and spatial-temporal misalignment via RoPE—that make vanilla ICC inapplicable to dense prediction. The SRFM module additionally borrows from DINOv2 self-supervised features and sinusoidal resolution embeddings to compensate for the geometric ambiguity inherent in generative pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6–13× reduction in training data (0.8M vs. 6–10.5M frames) while achieving SOTA zero-shot performance on real-world benchmarks is a strong signal that VDiT spatial-temporal priors transfer effectively to dense prediction, potentially reshaping how the community thinks about data requirements for video perception tasks. The SAND-Attention mechanism (shared RoPE + unidirectional attention) is a clean, generalizable recipe that could apply to other ICC-based dense prediction tasks (normal estimation, optical flow, segmentation). Open questions include whether the synthetic-data training recipe generalizes to other domains without additional data, whether the 5-step inference budget suffices for all scene complexities, and whether the approach extends to absolute (metric) depth estimation.
