## Problem

End-to-end autonomous driving testing requires closed-loop simulation with high visual fidelity, multi-agent interactivity, and real-time throughput, but existing approaches inherently trade off these requirements. Game engines suffer sim-to-real gaps, generative 2D models lack 3D consistency, and static 3DGS representations couple geometry and illumination, making dynamic traffic manipulation impossible. The fundamental obstacle is that separating persistent static infrastructure from movable agents in neural scene representations creates three unresolved conflicts: memory efficiency, metric geometric alignment, and photometric consistency.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a pre-reconstructed static background 3DGS (optimized for 30k iterations), canonical vehicle 3DGS assets (20 vehicles from 3DRealCar covering sedans, SUVs, and commercial vehicles), vectorized HD map lane centerlines (from dataset annotations or MapTRv2 extraction), and planner-driven 6-DoF rigid transforms for background agents. It outputs synchronized multi-view RGB images at 45 FPS on a single RTX 4090, structured as the sensor observations fed to E2E planners (UniAD, VAD) in a fully interactive closed loop where background agents react via coupled IDM and MOBIL behavioral models at each simulation step.

## Method

DecoupleGS introduces a dual-stream architecture that represents the static background in world coordinates and dynamic agents in separate canonical local coordinate spaces, fusing them only at rasterization via SE(3) transforms with Wigner D-matrix rotation of spherical harmonic coefficients to preserve view-dependent appearance under rigid motion. Three targeted modules resolve the resulting conflicts: (1) asset compression computes explicit importance scores (visibility, color contrast, local entropy) to prune low-contribution primitives, then applies vector quantization with learnable codebooks (K=1024 for SH, K=512 for covariance) to replace floating-point attributes with compact integer indices; (2) map-guided registration uses constrained DTW to align agent trajectories to lane centerlines, SE(2) Procrustes analysis to eliminate lateral drift, and opacity-weighted depth accumulation to ground vehicles vertically without requiring a surface mesh; (3) proxy-based relighting samples local ambient SH descriptors from neighboring background gaussians, applies a pre-calibrated linear modulation operator (solved offline via least-squares regression) to transfer illumination to vehicle SH coefficients, and synthesizes parametric contact shadows scaled by the dominant light intensity. All modules operate without online neural network inference, enabling plug-and-play insertion of multiple vehicles at interactive rates.

## Results

In closed-loop E2E testing, DecoupleGS achieves a Driving Score of 0.884 and Route Completion of 0.956, outperforming HUGSIM (0.765 / 0.814), OASim (0.748 / 0.851), and RealEngine (0.682 / 0.795), while also maintaining the largest safety margin (minTTC 3.3 s vs. 2.3–2.8 s) at 45 FPS. In open-loop sim-to-real consistency, DecoupleGS yields mADE of 0.82 m (closest to the 0.76 m real-input reference) and minTTC of 3.3 s, compared to 0.94–1.15 m and 2.4–2.9 s for the other simulators. For asset compression at medium traffic density (3–5 vehicles), DecoupleGS achieves 52.4 FPS and 950 MB VRAM with PSNR-Veh of 26.85 dB, balancing the fidelity of Vanilla 3DGS (27.05 dB, 32.5 FPS, 2.1 GB) against the speed of LightGaussian (58.6 FPS, 240 MB, 25.14 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three rendering-efficiency baselines (Plenoxels, Vanilla 3DGS, LightGaussian), DecoupleGS is the only method that consistently ranks top-2 across all fidelity and efficiency metrics at every traffic density (1–10+ vehicles); it sacrifices ~0.2 dB PSNR-Veh relative to Vanilla 3DGS but gains ~60% more FPS and ~55% less VRAM, while retaining far higher fidelity than LightGaussian. Against the three simulator baselines (HUGSIM, RealEngine, OASim), DecoupleGS wins on every closed-loop metric: Driving Score by +0.119 over HUGSIM (the closest competitor), Route Completion by +0.142 over HUGSIM, and minTTC by +1.0 s over HUGSIM. It also runs at 45 FPS versus 12 FPS for HUGSIM and 18 FPS for OASim, a 3.7× and 2.5× speedup respectively. In open-loop sim-to-real consistency, DecoupleGS's mADE of 0.82 m is 0.12 m closer to the real-input reference than HUGSIM (0.94 m) and 0.33 m closer than RealEngine (1.15 m), indicating the smallest behavioral gap among all simulated inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the rendering backbone but departs from prior neural driving simulators (HUGSIM, OASim, RealEngine, DrivingGaussian) by introducing a dual-stream decoupled architecture that represents static backgrounds and dynamic agents in separate coordinate spaces, fused only at rasterization time. Where prior 3DGS-based driving simulators treat the scene as a monolithic reconstruction, DecoupleGS explicitly separates persistent infrastructure from manipulable traffic assets using an object-centric canonical representation, then addresses the three resulting conflicts (memory, geometric alignment, photometric integration) with targeted modules: VQ-based compression, DTW+Procrustes map registration, and proxy-based SH relighting. The approach is conceptually closer to game-engine scene-graph composition than to prior monolithic neural rendering, but achieves photorealism through learned 3DGS primitives rather than hand-crafted assets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DecoupleGS provides a practical, plug-and-play closed-loop sensor simulation engine that bridges the gap between the fidelity of neural rendering and the interactivity required for large-scale E2E policy evaluation, with the decoupled modules being solver-agnostic and integrable into existing simulators. Key limitations include the proxy-based relighting's inability to handle complex multi-bounce or specular lighting, dependence on reliable HD map topology for registration, and restriction to rigid agents and RGB-only simulation. Open questions include whether the decoupled 3DGS paradigm extends to non-rigid foregrounds (pedestrians, cyclists), unified RGB-LiDAR sensor simulation, and whether the VQ compression strategy generalizes to full-scene compression rather than just dynamic assets.
