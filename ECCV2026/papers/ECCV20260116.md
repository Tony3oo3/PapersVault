## Problem

Existing training for tool-using VLM agents has two gaps: supervised fine-tuning on successful trajectories provides no recovery signal when a tool call fails at test time, and sparse trajectory-level RL rewards cannot identify which intermediate step failed or how to repair it. ReGRPO addresses both by learning structured, reflection-guided correction as an explicit part of the policy rather than a test-time prompting heuristic.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260116/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a text query paired with one or more images or documents (PDF, PPTX, XLSX, screenshots) and iteratively consumes normalized tool outputs (status, payload, meta). At each step it emits a tool call with arguments; when a deterministic trigger fires (tool error, empty observation, or low token-level confidence below an online adaptive threshold), it additionally produces a schema-constrained reflection triplet (ErrorType, Evidence, FixPlan) followed by exactly one corrected action before resuming normal execution. The final output is a grounded answer derived from the corrected tool observations.

## Method

ReGRPO is a two-stage pipeline. First, a Structured Reflective Data Engine perturbs ground-truth actions from MM-Traj to synthesize near-miss failures, executes them in a sandbox to obtain real failure observations, and uses GPT-4o to generate structured Reflection-of-Thought triplets (ErrorType, Evidence, FixPlan) paired with the corrected action; these form SFT warm-start data that teaches the model to diagnose and repair errors. Second, ReGRPO extends GRPO by including reflection tokens z_i inside the optimized local trajectory, so group-relative advantages directly scale gradients on both reflection and correction tokens, while a reflection-cost penalty (η·|z_i|) discourages unnecessary reflection. At inference, a zero-verifier single-path protocol uses a deterministic gate (tool error ∨ empty observation ∨ u_i < κ_i) to open at most one local reflection-correction block per step, requiring no external verifier calls.

## Results

Under a unified single-path, zero-verifier inference protocol, ReGRPO (default, λval=0) with Qwen2-VL-7B achieves 76.35 ToolAcc and 67.66 AnsAcc on GTA, and 23.35 AnsAcc on GAIA. These are the best results among all compared open-source controllers on both benchmarks. Adding the optional deterministic verifier reward further lifts performance to 68.49 GTA AnsAcc and 24.01 GAIA AnsAcc, but the main gains are already realized in the verifier-free setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReGRPO outperforms the strongest open-source baseline, SPORT (Tuned-Qwen2-VL-7B), by +3.94 ToolAcc and +7.40 AnsAcc on GTA (76.35/67.66 vs. 72.41/60.26) and by +2.74 AnsAcc on GAIA (23.35 vs. 20.61). Against MAT-AGENT (MAT-Qwen2-VL-7B), the gains are even larger: +11.72 ToolAcc and +13.81 AnsAcc on GTA, and +6.38 AnsAcc on GAIA. ReGRPO also surpasses the GPT-4o-powered HF Agent on GTA (67.66 vs. 57.05 AnsAcc, 76.35 vs. 63.41 ToolAcc), though it trails on GAIA (23.35 vs. 33.40 AnsAcc), where the closed-source model retains a clear advantage. Among open-source controllers, ReGRPO is the only method to exceed 75 ToolAcc on GTA and 23 AnsAcc on GAIA, confirming that structured reflection-aware RL provides consistent gains over both SFT-only (MAT-AGENT) and preference-based (SPORT) training paradigms under identical backbone and tool settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReGRPO sits at the intersection of three lines of work: MAT-AGENT's trajectory-based SFT for tool-using VLMs, SPORT's self-exploration with step-wise preferences, and GRPO's group-relative policy optimization. It departs from MAT-AGENT by augmenting successful trajectories with induced near-miss failures and structured Reflection-of-Thought triplets, and from SPORT by replacing unstructured preference signals with a schema-constrained (ErrorType, Evidence, FixPlan) reflection that is jointly optimized with corrective actions. Conceptually, it moves reflection from a frozen inference-time prompting heuristic (as in Reflexion) to a learnable policy variable, while retaining GRPO's standard advantage estimator rather than proposing a new optimizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical fragility in deployed tool-using agents: when a tool call fails or returns off-target output, SFT-trained controllers have no learned recovery mechanism. The structured reflection approach provides interpretable, step-level recovery signals that go beyond scalar success/failure rewards, and the zero-verifier single-path inference keeps deployment lightweight. Open questions include whether the fixed ErrorType taxonomy generalizes to failure modes outside the training distribution, whether the approach scales to longer horizons with cascading multi-step errors, and whether the GPT-4o teacher dependency for data generation can be replaced by self-generated reflections. The evaluation is limited to two benchmarks and a single 7B backbone, so broader generalizability remains to be established.
