## Problem

VLMs achieve strong scores on spatial reasoning benchmarks, but it remains unclear whether this reflects structured 3D understanding or exploitation of statistical shortcuts in natural images—particularly the perspective-driven correlation between vertical image position and depth. Prior work has assessed individual task performance or local attention mechanisms but has not examined how spatial axes are jointly organized in representation space, leaving the geometric structure of spatial encodings unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262306/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes RGB images paired with binary spatial-relation questions (e.g., "Is A closer to the camera than B?") and extracts final-token hidden states at a fixed intermediate layer per model family. For contrastive probing, it constructs minimal question pairs by swapping the queried object order, producing delta vectors that isolate relational displacement in embedding space. SpatialTunnel additionally provides synthetic tunnel-scene renders where two objects are placed at fixed depths but swept across 16 angular positions, enabling independent manipulation of vertical image position and depth ordering.

## Method

The core contribution is a representation-level diagnostic framework built on contrastive probing: for each spatial axis (horizontal, vertical, depth), hidden states are extracted for minimally paired questions that flip the ground-truth relation, and the resulting delta vectors are analyzed for directional consistency (axis coherence) and cross-axis coupling (VD-Entanglement Index). To isolate vertical-distance entanglement from evaluation-set skew, the authors introduce SpatialTunnel, a Blender-rendered single-point-perspective corridor where objects can be positioned at any depth independently of their vertical image placement, enabling matched counterfactual pairs that flip vertical arrangement while preserving depth ordering. Models are fine-tuned at four spatial data scales (80k–2M samples) from a uniform mix of five spatial datasets to study how training scale affects representation structure. The analysis reveals that distance coherence is consistently the weakest axis, that it correlates with counter-example accuracy across benchmarks (ρ = 0.759–0.804), and that models with well-separated spatial axes (high Coh_D, low VD-EI) exhibit greater robustness across diverse spatial reasoning tasks.

## Results

Across all evaluated VLMs, a significant consistent-vs-counter accuracy gap is observed on both EmbSpatial-Bench and CV-Bench-3D, with Qwen2.5-VL-3B (2M fine-tune) showing the largest gap (60.9% vs. 24.0%, Δ=36.9 pp on EmbSpatial-Bench). On the new SpatialTunnel benchmark, base Qwen2.5-VL exhibits the strongest entanglement (v_cons=0.776 vs. v_ctr=0.360, Δ=+0.416), while RoboRefer-2B-SFT achieves the smallest gap among above-chance models (Δ=+0.046) and Qwen3-VL-235B attains the highest mean accuracy (v=0.908) with a similarly small gap (Δ=+0.068). Representation probing reveals that distance coherence (Coh_D) is consistently the weakest axis across all models, and RoboRefer achieves the highest Coh_D (0.182) and lowest VD-Entanglement Index (0.362), correlating with its superior counter-example robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an analysis paper, so the 'baselines' are the VLMs under study. All five model families (Molmo, NVILA, Qwen2.5-VL, RoboRefer, Qwen3-VL) exhibit vertical-distance entanglement, but to markedly different degrees. RoboRefer-2B-SFT, trained on 20M+ RGB/RGB-D samples, achieves the highest consistent accuracy (87.0% EmbSpatial, 98.9% CV-3D) and the smallest SpatialTunnel gap (Δ=+0.046), indicating the most disentangled spatial representation. Qwen3-VL-235B, a much larger model, reaches 90.8% mean accuracy on SpatialTunnel with a small gap (Δ=+0.068), suggesting scale alone can partially mitigate the bias. In contrast, Qwen2.5-VL-3B shows the worst entanglement: its counter accuracy actually *decreases* with more fine-tuning data (32.6%→24.0% on EmbSpatial-Bench), and its distance coherence remains nearly flat (0.043→0.052) across all scales. NVILA-2B at 2M scale achieves 93.8% on CV-3D Depth but only 62.9% on BLINK Spatial Relation, illustrating that high scores on one benchmark do not guarantee robust 3D understanding. The paper's key finding is that representation structure (high Coh_D, low VD-EI) predicts cross-benchmark robustness more reliably than any single accuracy number.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of spatial reasoning evaluation and internal representation analysis for VLMs. Prior spatial benchmarks (EmbSpatial-Bench, CV-Bench, BLINK, What's Up, COMFORT) measure whether models answer correctly but do not examine *how* spatial relations are encoded internally, while prior probing work (linear probing of depth cues, attention dynamics in ADAPTVIS, VLM-Lens) detects individual spatial primitives without examining the joint geometric organization of multiple spatial axes. The contrastive probing framework here repurposes the idea of minimal counterfactual pairs from NLP interpretability to isolate relational displacement in VLM embedding space, and the SpatialTunnel benchmark extends synthetic spatial data generation (SAT, RoboSpatial) with a specific geometric design that decouples vertical image position from depth — a confound no prior benchmark explicitly controls for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that vertical-distance entanglement is model-intrinsic and can persist or even intensify under spatial data scaling (notably Qwen2.5-VL, where 2M fine-tuning widens the consistent–counter gap from 22 to 37 points) challenges the working assumption that more spatial training data yields more robust spatial understanding. The axis coherence and VD-EI metrics offer practitioners a lightweight diagnostic to distinguish genuinely structured spatial representations from shortcut-driven ones, and SpatialTunnel provides a controlled evaluation setting that existing benchmarks lack. Open questions include whether explicit disentanglement objectives or 3D-aware pretraining can close the gap, whether the entanglement persists in frontier-scale models beyond 235B, and whether the representation metrics transfer to action-oriented settings (robotics, embodied agents) where spatial errors have physical consequences.
