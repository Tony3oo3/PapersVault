## Problem

LiDAR scene flow estimation is bottlenecked by the scarcity of dense, accurate 3D motion annotations, and self-supervised approaches on unlabeled real data yield diminishing returns due to noisy geometric proxy signals. Existing synthetic LiDAR pipelines are optimized for sensor realism or semantic tasks (detection, segmentation) rather than the dense kinematic complexity required for scene flow, leaving no scalable source of reliable motion supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262162/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The estimator consumes a temporal window of 5 LiDAR scans (32- or 64-beam) with ego odometry matrices that align all historical point clouds into the target frame's coordinate system, voxelized at 0.15 m resolution within a 38.4 m range. The output is a dense per-point 3D displacement vector for every point in the source scan, representing its rigid-body translation to the next timestep. The SynFlow-4k dataset provides 939,083 frames across 4,000 sequences with noise-free per-point flow labels, instance metadata, and validity masks stored in HDF5 containers.

## Method

SynFlow is a CARLA-based generation pipeline guided by three motion-oriented policies: (1) topological discretization via greedy lane-segment coverage to ensure diverse road geometries, (2) speed-regime coverage by including highway-structured towns under Traffic Manager control to span displacement magnitudes, and (3) multi-agent interaction by varying traffic density and agent behaviors to produce complex non-linear relative motions. Ground-truth flow labels are derived analytically from the simulator's rigid-body poses: each LiDAR point is assigned to an agent via majority-vote tag matching, then transformed through the agent's SE(3) pose change between consecutive timesteps. The model uses the ΔFlow backbone (MinkUNet sparse 3D convolutional network) trained with motion-awareness, category-balanced, and instance-consistency losses. A two-stage protocol is evaluated: zero-shot inference from synthetic-only pre-training, and label-efficient fine-tuning on 5–20% of real-world labels, demonstrating that the synthetic motion prior transfers across sensors and reduces annotation demand by an order of magnitude.

## Results

On nuScenes, SynFlow-4k trained purely on synthetic data achieves a zero-shot Dynamic Bucket-Normalized EPE of 0.242, outperforming the best self-supervised baseline (TeFlow, 0.395) by 38.7% and approaching the fully supervised ΔFlow (0.216). On TruckScenes, the zero-shot result (0.274) surpasses the SOTA supervised ΔFlow (0.402) by 31.8%. With fine-tuning on just 20% of real labels, SynFlow-4k+FT reaches 0.157 on nuScenes (27.3% better than supervised ΔFlow) and 0.266 on TruckScenes (33.8% better), while fine-tuning on only 5% of real labels (0.201) already exceeds the supervised baseline trained from scratch on 20% of data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against all four self-supervised baselines (SeFlow, VoteFlow, SeFlow++, TeFlow), SynFlow-4k zero-shot substantially outperforms on both nuScenes and TruckScenes, with the largest gap over TeFlow (0.395→0.242 on nuScenes, 0.425→0.274 on TruckScenes). Against the three supervised baselines (DeFlow, Flow4D, ΔFlow), SynFlow-4k zero-shot already matches or exceeds them on TruckScenes (beating ΔFlow by 31.8%) and is within 12% on nuScenes; after fine-tuning on 20% real labels, SynFlow-4k+FT dominates all supervised methods by 27–34% on Dynamic EPE. On the Aeva FMCW LiDAR benchmark, the complementarity experiment shows that combining SynFlow-4k with UniFlow (trained on Argo-v2 + nuScenes + Waymo) yields the best overall score (0.263), with a striking 37% improvement in the PED. category (0.398→0.251) over UniFlow alone, indicating that synthetic data contributes kinematic density for small dynamic agents that even mega-scale real datasets lack. The backbone ablation (Table 7) confirms that SynFlow-4k improves all tested architectures (DeFlow, Flow4D, ΔFlow) zero-shot on Aeva, demonstrating the dataset's backbone-agnostic utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines of work on LiDAR scene flow supervision. First, it moves away from self-supervised methods (SeFlow, VoteFlow, TeFlow) that construct noisy proxy signals from geometric consistency on unlabeled real data, arguing that scaling such data yields diminishing returns. Second, it diverges from prior synthetic LiDAR pipelines (SHIFT, CarlaScenes, ReSimAD) that optimize for semantic realism or sensor-specific noise to support detection and segmentation, instead adopting a motion-oriented generation strategy that prioritizes kinematic diversity (speed regimes, multi-agent interactions, topological variety) over photorealism. The conceptual lineage connects to broader synthetic-data-for-3D-learning results (FlyingThings3D, LRM-Zero, MegaSynth) but applies them to the specific bottleneck of dense motion annotation in LiDAR scene flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SynFlow-4k addresses a fundamental practical bottleneck: dense 3D motion annotation is prohibitively expensive at scale, and self-supervision on real data has not closed the gap to supervised performance. The finding that a motion-oriented synthetic prior transfers zero-shot to multiple real-world sensors (and even beats in-domain supervised SOTA on TruckScenes) suggests that kinematic physics, not sensor realism, is the transferable core of scene flow learning. The complementarity experiment with UniFlow further shows synthetic and real pre-training capture orthogonal knowledge (motion rules vs. sensor characteristics), pointing toward hybrid training as a practical recipe. Open questions include whether the open-loop generation can be made feedback-driven to target model failure modes, whether the approach extends to non-rigid or articulated motion beyond rigid-body agents, and whether the motion-oriented strategy generalizes to other 3D motion tasks (robotics, manipulation) where annotation is equally scarce.
