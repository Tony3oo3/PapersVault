## Problem

Existing depth completion methods either learn domain-specific RGBD-fused priors that degrade under visual shifts and heterogeneous sensor patterns, or integrate monocular depth estimation (MDE) models in a two-stage pipeline whose explicit relative-to-metric alignment introduces structured distortions and extra computation. The paper asks how to bypass intermediate coarse depth generation and seamlessly leverage MDE's domain-general geometric priors in a single forward pass that is agnostic to both visual domain and depth pattern.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260667/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a sparse depth map with arbitrary sparsity level and spatial distribution (random holes, range truncation, LiDAR lines, SfM points, or mixed sensor artifacts) and produces a dense metric depth map in one forward pass. The sparse depth is first normalized to remove global scale and bias, preserving only inter-point scale ratios as cues. The final metric output is obtained by a closed-form least-squares alignment of the predicted scale-consistent relative depth to the sparse metric anchors, requiring no additional learnable modules or refinement networks.

## Method

Any2Full reformulates depth completion as a scale-prompting adaptation of a frozen pretrained MDE backbone (Depth Anything v2). A lightweight Scale-Aware Prompt Encoder (SAPE) converts normalized sparse depth into unified scale prompts through two hierarchical modules: a Local Enrichment stage that anchors per-patch scale cues into the MDE latent space via FiLM modulation, and a Global Propagation stage that diffuses these cues across the scene using MDE geometry-guided attention (Query and Key from MDE features, Value from scale features), ensuring propagation follows geometric structure rather than irregular sampling patterns. The resulting multi-level scale prompts modulate MDE decoder features via hierarchical FiLM, steering the relative depth prediction toward global scale consistency while preserving the backbone's domain-general priors. Only the SAPE (~60M params for DA-L) is trained on 85K synthetic RGB-D pairs with randomized depth sampling strategies; the MDE backbone remains frozen throughout.

## Results

Any2Full (DA-L) achieves the lowest average rank (2.3) across all six public benchmarks and the real-world Logistic-Black dataset, with an average AbsREL of 0.010 and RMSE of 0.459 m. On iBims-1, the DA-L variant reaches 0.007 AbsREL, while the smallest DA-S variant still achieves 0.009 AbsREL at 0.09 s inference time—approximately 7× faster than PriorDA and 1000× faster than TestPromptDC. The method outperforms the prior SOTA (OMNI-DC) by 32.2% in average AbsREL and delivers a 1.4× speedup over PriorDA (0.49 s vs. 0.68 s) using the same Depth Anything-Large backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Any2Full (DA-L) outperforms the strongest pattern-agnostic baseline OMNI-DC by 32.2% in average AbsREL (0.010 vs. 0.014) and achieves a lower average rank (2.3 vs. 3.4) across all 18 dataset-pattern combinations. Against the closest MDE-integrated competitor PriorDA (same DA-L backbone), Any2Full reduces iBims-1 Hole AbsREL from 0.011 to 0.007 while running 1.4× faster (0.49 s vs. 0.68 s), and avoids the structured artifacts that PriorDA's two-stage output-level alignment introduces on Range and Sparse-SfM patterns. Test-time adaptation methods (TestPromptDC, Marigold-DC) achieve competitive accuracy on dense inputs but collapse under extreme sparsity (e.g., VOID-150: TestPromptDC AbsREL 0.850 vs. Any2Full 0.045) and incur 100–1000× higher latency. Domain-specific methods (CompFormer, DepthPrompt) degrade catastrophically on unseen domains (CompFormer AbsREL jumps from 0.011 on NYU to 0.353 on DIODE), confirming the domain-general advantage of the MDE-prompting formulation. The scale-prompting design is backbone-agnostic: applied to MoGe-2-Base, SAPE consistently improves over the raw MoGe-2 baseline across all datasets (e.g., average AbsREL 0.012 vs. 0.059), and the smallest DA-S variant (0.09 s) still surpasses every prior depth completion method in accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on pretrained monocular depth estimation (MDE) models, particularly Depth Anything v2, and positions itself against the emerging family of MDE-integrated depth completion methods. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (exemplified by PriorDA) that explicitly aligns relative MDE output with sparse metric depth before refinement, arguing that this output-level fusion introduces structured distortions. Instead, it repurposes the FiLM conditioning mechanism for feature-level scale prompting, injecting sparse depth cues as lightweight modulation signals into a frozen MDE backbone in a single forward pass, while also contrasting with test-time adaptation methods (TestPromptDC, Marigold-DC) that are too slow for real-time use.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-stage scale-prompting formulation offers a clean conceptual alternative to two-stage MDE integration, and the real-world robotic warehouse deployment (28%→91.6% grasping success on black packages) demonstrates tangible practical value beyond benchmark numbers. The 1000× speedup over test-time adaptation methods makes MDE-quality depth completion viable for real-time robotic perception. Open questions include whether the scale-prompting paradigm extends to other sensor modalities (e.g., event cameras, thermal) or tasks beyond depth completion, and whether the reliance on a frozen MDE backbone limits ceiling performance as MDE models evolve.
