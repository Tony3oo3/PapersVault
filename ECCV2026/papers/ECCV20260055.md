## Problem

Scene-level 3D texturing remains unsolved: existing methods either texture objects independently (losing global coherence), rely on optimization (producing blurry occluded regions), or use inpainting variants (causing the Janus problem and breaking long-range continuity). No prior framework simultaneously delivers high-resolution textures, strict multi-view 3D consistency, and physically plausible baked illumination in a single training-free pipeline, leaving a gap for production workflows that require UV-mapped textures with pre-computed shadows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260055/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untextured multi-mesh 3D scene, a global style prompt plus optional per-object prompts, and arbitrary light source positions, and produces per-object UV texture maps at 2048² resolution with baked geometry-consistent shadows. Generation runs in latent pixel space at 128×128 per view using Stable Diffusion 3.5 with ControlNet (depth, normal, shadow conditioning), requiring 20 inference steps on a single RTX A6000 GPU (~4 hours per scene). The output UV atlases are directly compatible with standard 3D tools like Blender, unlike panoramic-projection methods that lack UV support.

## Method

SceneHI is a three-stage, training-free pipeline that jointly denoises all camera views through a shared High-Resolution Latent Texture (HRLT) canvas, enforcing 3D consistency via an exact analytical pixel-to-texel mapping derived from the linearity of u/w, v/w, and 1/w under perspective projection. At each diffusion step, rendered latent views are denoised independently, then inverse-rendered back into UV space using mode-based aggregation (preserving high-frequency detail) and surface-normal-based selection (suppressing oblique-view distortion), before being re-rendered for the next step. A High-Definition Texture Refinement (HDTR) stage then applies patch-based diffusion on zoomed-in views, blending with low-resolution guidance latents via a cosine decay schedule to inject fine detail without breaking global style. Finally, a Geometry Consistent Shadow (GCS) stage renders analytical shadow maps as structural priors, fuses them with generated textures, and performs a shadow-aware denoising pass that bakes photorealistic, geometry-consistent illumination directly into the UV atlases.

## Results

On the 3D-FRONT benchmark (10 indoor scenes, 25 novel views each), SceneHI achieves an Aesthetic Score of 5.04, Inception Score of 2.24, and CLIP Score of 28.8, outperforming all baselines on every metric. Generation time is 240 minutes per scene, an 80% reduction compared to SceneTex (1320 min) and RoomTex (1200 min). In a 20-participant user study, SceneHI scores 4.45/5 on consistency, 4.62/5 on fine detail, and 4.68/5 on shadow realism, substantially exceeding the best baseline (RoomTex) on all three dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneHI outperforms all three scene-level baselines on every quantitative metric. Against SceneTex [6], it gains +0.38 AS, +0.17 IS, and +1.4 CS while running 5.5× faster (240 vs. 1320 min). Against RoomTex [46], it improves AS by +0.37, IS by +0.17, and CS by +3.5, at 5× lower cost (240 vs. 1200 min). The single-object method Text2Tex [7] lags further behind (AS 4.10–4.22, CS 23.4–23.7), confirming that per-object pipelines fail to maintain global scene coherence. The user study reinforces these gaps: SceneHI's shadow realism score (4.68) is nearly double that of RoomTex (2.62), and its fine-detail score (4.62) exceeds RoomTex (3.34) by 1.28 points, reflecting the HDTR refinement stage that no baseline provides. Notably, none of the baselines produce consistent or controllable shadows, a capability unique to SceneHI's GCS stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneHI extends the 2D high-resolution diffusion techniques of DemoFusion and Pixelsmith into the 3D scene-texturing domain, adapting their progressive/patch-based upscaling to operate on persistent UV atlases rather than 2D image planes. It departs from prior scene-level methods (SceneTex, RoomTex, RoomPainter) that rely on iterative optimization or inpainting branches, instead using an analytical pixel-to-texel mapping and joint multi-view denoising to enforce 3D consistency without any fine-tuning. The framework also introduces a generative shadow-baking stage that has no direct counterpart in existing scene-texturing pipelines, bridging a gap between diffusion-based texturing and production rendering workflows that require pre-computed illumination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying high-resolution texture synthesis, multi-view consistency, and controllable baked illumination in a single training-free pipeline, SceneHI addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D content production (gaming, XR, digital twins) where manual texturing is expensive and existing generative methods produce low-fidelity or illumination-inconsistent results. The 80% reduction in generation time over optimization-based scene methods makes the approach more accessible, though the ~4-hour per-scene cost on a single A6000 still limits throughput. Open questions include scalability to very large or outdoor scenes, whether the HRLT resolution heuristic generalizes beyond indoor environments, and how the method performs when the scene contains highly reflective or transparent materials that challenge the normal-based view selection.
