## Problem

Album highlight recommendation (AHR) has been stalled by a critical data gap: the inherent subjectivity and ambiguity of highlight selection make reliable annotation difficult, leaving the field without large-scale benchmarks. Beyond data, existing scoring approaches evaluate images in isolation, ignoring the comparative, album-level context that drives human highlight selection, causing sharp performance degradation as the number of recommended images grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261876/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

In the local stage, a single RGB image (max 1024×1024) paired with an instruction prompt is fed to the MLLM, which outputs a probability distribution over six discrete score tokens (0–5); the HDA module converts this distribution into a continuous highlight score. In the global stage, the target image (max 512×512 to fit multiple images in context) is jointly processed with M=5 reference images and their generated captions, producing a comparative re-score. The final output is a ranked list of highlight images for a target recommendation size (e.g., top-5% or top-15% of the album).

## Method

Highlight4U is an agentic MLLM framework built on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, fine-tuned with LoRA (r=16, α=32) via supervised learning on re-annotated PEC and CUFED albums. The key insight is that treating highlight scoring as discrete token classification discards ordinal structure and distributional information; the Highlight Distribution Aggregation (HDA) module recovers this by mapping the full softmax probability distribution over score tokens to a continuous score, either via a training-free weighted expectation or a lightweight MLP regressor. A second-stage global reference-based re-recommendation agent then selects the top-M locally scored images as multimodal references (images + generated captions) and re-scores all remaining images against this album-level context, explicitly modeling the comparative judgment humans use. This two-stage local-global pipeline is what allows Highlight4U to maintain high precision at top-5% while substantially improving recall at top-15%, where local-only scoring degrades.

## Results

On the public PEC dataset, Highlight4U achieves P@5% = 74.1, R@5% = 26.5, P@15% = 61.4, and R@15% = 60.0, outperforming the best fine-tuned visual backbone (CLIP-ViT-B Reg: P@5% = 72.4) and the best training-free MLLM (Kimi-V2.5-Flash: P@5% = 39.2). On CUFED, Highlight4U reaches P@5% = 63.1 and R@15% = 61.3. On the private C-AHR-100 dataset (owner-annotated), Highlight4U attains P@5% = 36.0, an 8.2% absolute improvement over GPT-4o (28.4), demonstrating cross-domain generalization from public consensus to personalized preferences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Highlight4U outperforms all 11 baselines across all three datasets. Against the strongest classical backbone (CLIP-ViT-B with regression head), Highlight4U gains +1.7 P@5% on PEC (74.1 vs 72.4) and a substantial +9.4 R@15% on PEC (60.0 vs 50.6), confirming that album-level context matters more at larger recommendation sizes. Against training-free MLLMs, the gap is dramatic: GPT-4o achieves only 29.7 P@5% on PEC versus Highlight4U's 74.1, and even the strongest open MLLM (Kimi-V2.5-Flash, 39.2) trails by 35 points. Against the fine-tuned MLLM baseline (Qwen3-VL-8B with direct score token prediction), Highlight4U improves P@5% by +7.2 on PEC (74.1 vs 66.9) and P@15% by +6.0 (61.4 vs 55.4), validating the HDA module's role in exploiting the full probability distribution rather than a single argmax token. On the private C-AHR-100 dataset, Highlight4U leads GPT-4o by +7.6 P@5% (36.0 vs 28.4) and the fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-8B by +5.0 (36.0 vs 31.0), showing that the agentic two-stage workflow transfers public highlight perception to individual owner preferences without any personalization training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions album highlight recommendation (AHR) as a long-neglected subtask of album-centric visual analysis, distinct from event recognition and summarization due to its reliance on subjective human cognition rather than objective content. Methodologically, it repurposes the highlight distribution aggregation (HDA) concept from numerical-prediction-optimization work on MLLMs (Q-Align, soft-labeling approaches) to convert discrete token probabilities into continuous scores, and introduces a two-stage agentic workflow (local scoring → global reference-based re-scoring) that has no direct precedent in prior AHR literature. The annotation strategy—summing binary labels from five annotators rather than averaging or majority-voting—draws on the FlickrUser interestingness benchmark but is applied here for the first time to highlight selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three constructed datasets (910 public + 100 owner-annotated albums, 97K images) address a critical data bottleneck that has stalled AHR research for years, and the C-AHR-100 dataset uniquely tests whether public highlight perception transfers to individual preferences—a question no prior benchmark could answer. The finding that naive MLLM fine-tuning underperforms frozen visual backbones, and that the HDA + global-reference pipeline is what closes the gap, offers a practical recipe for applying MLLMs to ordinal-scoring tasks more broadly. Open questions include whether the two-stage agentic workflow scales to very large albums, how sensitive performance is to the reference count M in real deployment, and whether more personalized (few-shot or preference-conditioned) variants can further close the public-to-private gap.
