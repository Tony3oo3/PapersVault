## Problem

Physics-based garment draping is computationally expensive and non-differentiable, while deep learning-based methods lack explicit physical constraints, producing interpenetration and unrealistic deformations. Existing hybrid approaches treat physics as soft loss penalties or post-processing steps, which fail to enforce strict physical fidelity and prevent joint optimization with the draping network.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260536/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a garment template mesh in canonical T-pose, SMPL body shape parameters β∈ℝ¹⁰, and pose parameters θ∈ℝ⁷² as input, and produces a single 3D draped garment mesh as output. Training is fully self-supervised on static T-pose meshes from CLOTH3D (600 training garments, 30 unseen test garments), requiring no ground-truth draped geometry. The garment mesh topology defines the graph structure, and material parameters (Lamé constants, bending stiffness, nodal mass) are either learned or manually specified per vertex.

## Method

PhysDrape uses forces as an explicit intermediary between a neural network and a physical solver. A force-driven GNN (encode-process-decode architecture with 16 message-passing layers) takes node features (vertex normals, signed distance to body, computed internal forces, material properties) and edge features (current and rest geometry) to simultaneously predict the garment mesh and per-node forces. A learnable stretching solver then performs T explicit forward steps, updating vertex positions along the force direction with a learnable compliance parameter η and decay rate γ to propagate deformation toward equilibrium. A differentiable collision handler projects penetrating vertices outward along body normals using a learnable scalar δ≥1, creating a geometric buffer. All three modules are differentiable and jointly optimized end-to-end via a self-supervised energy loss (strain + bending + collision + gravity), enabling generalization to unseen garment templates and manual control over material stiffness.

## Results

On the CLOTH3D test set (30 unseen garments), PhysDrape (T=15) achieves a strain energy of 0.15 and bending energy of 0.004, substantially lower than DrapeNet (0.43 / 0.01), DIG (6.32 / 0.01), and DeePSD (7.22 / 0.01). The Body-to-Garment interpenetration ratio drops to 0.05%, compared to 0.9% for DrapeNet, 1.8% for DIG, and 7.2% for DeePSD. Inference runs in 91 ms per garment on a single V100 GPU (T=15), remaining within the 100 ms real-time threshold.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysDrape outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor DrapeNet [9], PhysDrape (T=15) reduces strain energy by 65% (0.15 vs 0.43), bending energy by 60% (0.004 vs 0.01), and interpenetration by 94% (0.05% vs 0.9%). Against DIG [19], the gains are even more dramatic: strain energy drops from 6.32 to 0.15 (98% reduction) and B2G from 1.8% to 0.05%. DeePSD [4] is the weakest baseline, with strain energy nearly 48× higher and interpenetration 144× higher than PhysDrape. The paper notes that template-specific methods (SNUG, GAPS) and dynamic methods (Hood, SENC) are excluded from direct comparison due to different evaluation protocols, but the qualitative visualizations in Fig. 3 confirm that PhysDrape produces fewer interpenetrations and more natural draping than DrapeNet across diverse poses and garment types.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysDrape sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: physics-based cloth simulation (Mass-Spring, FEM, PBD/XPBD solvers) and deep-learning-based garment draping (DeePSD, DIG, DrapeNet, SNUG, GAPS). It departs from prior physics-informed neural methods (DrapeNet, SNUG, PBNS) that encode physical constraints only as soft loss-function penalties, instead embedding an explicit differentiable stretching solver and collision handler directly in the forward pass with learnable parameters. The force-driven GNN architecture follows the encode-process-decode paradigm of Pfaff et al. [34], while the self-supervised energy-based training protocol and evaluation metrics are inherited from DrapeNet [9], making the comparison direct and controlled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making the physical solver differentiable and jointly optimizable with the neural network, PhysDrape offers something pure deep-learning draping methods cannot: explicit control over material stiffness and a hard (rather than soft) non-penetration guarantee, while retaining generalization to unseen garment templates. The 18× reduction in interpenetration over DrapeNet (0.05% vs 0.9% B2G) and the ability to tune fabric stiffness at inference time are practically useful for virtual try-on and animation pipelines. However, the method is limited to static upper-body garments on a single dataset, does not handle multi-layer clothing or dynamic motion, and the physical solver is a simplified explicit Euler scheme (T=3–15 steps) rather than a full FEM solve, so its fidelity for highly elastic or complex fabrics remains an open question.
