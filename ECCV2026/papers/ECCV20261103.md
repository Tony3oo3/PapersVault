## Problem

Existing deep SfM methods are either track-centric and limited to small image collections (~200 views), or over-prune inliers causing registration failures, while classical global pipelines (GLOMAP, Theia) are accurate but slow on large-scale scenes. The paper asks whether a learned, track-free pose-averaging network can recover globally consistent camera extrinsics at scale (>1000 images) with accuracy competitive with classical methods but at substantially lower runtime.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261103/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The GNN consumes only edge-level geometric measurements: the SO(3) log-map of the RANSAC-decomposed relative rotation and the unit translation direction (scale-free) for each view-graph edge, with node embeddings initialized from a shared learnable token rather than image features. Point tracks (2D correspondences) are used exclusively downstream for DLT triangulation and bundle adjustment, not during pose regression. At test time the method also supports sparse retrieval-based graphs (top-30 MegaLoc neighbors) in place of exhaustive pairwise matching, and optionally optimizes intrinsics jointly with extrinsics in the uncalibrated setting.

## Method

VGPA is a permutation-equivariant, edge-conditioned GNN that performs message passing over the view graph: each edge carries an MLP-encoded relative pose, and each layer applies an edge-conditioned MLP on (h_i, h_j, e_ij) with degree-normalized mean aggregation, residual connections, LayerNorm, and Dropout. A 3-layer MLP head regresses per-camera translation and a unit quaternion from the final node embeddings. Training is fully unsupervised, minimizing a relative-pose consistency loss (geodesic rotation error plus arccos directional translation error) between network-predicted pairwise poses and the noisy RANSAC inputs, with no ground-truth supervision or reprojection term. At inference, a single forward pass yields initial poses, followed by 200 steps of test-time fine-tuning on the target scene, DLT triangulation of point tracks, and robust bundle adjustment to refine both cameras and 3D structure.

## Results

On the 1DSfM cross-dataset benchmark (trained on MegaDepth, tested on 10 urban scenes), VGPA achieves mean rotation errors of 0.28°–7.90° and translation errors of 0.044–0.670, substantially outperforming the deep factorization baseline RESfM (rotation errors 0.67°–14.54°) on most scenes. Against the strongest classical baselines, VGPA is competitive with GLOMAP and Theia, often matching or beating them on both rotation and translation. In terms of runtime, VGPA processes 180.8 images/min on average versus 45.3 for RESfM, 13.1 for COLMAP, 5.7 for Theia, and 31.5 for GLOMAP, making it roughly 3× faster than GLOMAP and 20× faster than Theia while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGPA clearly outperforms the deep track-centric baseline RESfM [16] on most 1DSfM scenes, achieving 2–30× lower rotation errors (e.g., 0.28° vs 0.82° on Ellis Island; 0.43° vs 14.54° on Yorkminster) while also registering more images (e.g., 457 vs 94 on Tower of London). Against the classical global pipelines, VGPA is competitive with GLOMAP [28] and Theia [37]: it wins on rotation for 7/10 scenes and on translation for 8/10 scenes, though GLOMAP edges ahead on a few scenes (Montreal Notre Dame, Piazza del Popolo, Tower of London, Vienna Cathedral). Compared to recent feed-forward deep 3D reconstruction methods (TTT3R [7], CUT3R [41], FAST3R [45]), VGPA is an order of magnitude more accurate in rotation (e.g., 1.35° vs 16.08°/22.32°/40.37° on Alamo). On the smaller ground-truth benchmarks (Strecha, BlendedMVS), VGPA matches or beats classical pipelines (COLMAP [30], Theia, GLOMAP) and substantially outperforms image-based deep methods (VGGT [38], MASt3R [17], VGGSfM [40]). In runtime, VGPA is ~3× faster than GLOMAP, ~16× faster than COLMAP, and ~20× faster than Theia on the same point tracks, while RESfM is ~3.5× slower than VGPA.

## Relation to Prior Work

VGPA repurposes permutation-equivariant graph neural networks—previously applied to rotation averaging (NeurORA, PogoNet) and track-centric SfM factorization (RESfM, DeepPE-SfM)—for the full motion-averaging problem, replacing iterative optimization with learned message passing over the view graph. It departs from the deep factorization line by discarding point tracks and reprojection losses entirely during pose regression, relying instead on a purely relative-pose consistency objective for self-supervised training. The classical global SfM pipeline (Theia, GLOMAP) provides the conceptual template: solve rotations, then translations, then triangulate—but VGPA learns to perform the averaging step in a single forward pass, with triangulation and robust BA as post-hoc refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for large-scale reconstruction: VGPA achieves competitive accuracy with GLOMAP and Theia while running roughly 10–20× faster, and it scales to 1000+ image collections where feed-forward deep methods (VGGT, VGGSfM) are infeasible. The robustness to sparse retrieval-based graphs (MegaLoc top-30) suggests the method could reduce the expensive exhaustive-matching bottleneck in production pipelines. Open questions include whether the self-supervised objective generalizes to highly degenerate or cyclic trajectories without the BA rescue step, and whether the reliance on SIFT+RANSAC preprocessing limits applicability to texture-poor or dynamic scenes. The evaluation on MegaDepth and 1DSfM uses COLMAP as pseudo-ground-truth, so absolute accuracy claims on those benchmarks remain somewhat circular.
