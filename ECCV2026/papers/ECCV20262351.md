## Problem

Existing diffusion-based refinement methods for sparse-view 3D reconstruction (e.g., Difix3D+) treat each rendered view as an independent 2D generation task, ignoring the fact that all views are projections of the same underlying scene. This per-view independence produces conflicting hallucinations across viewpoints, causing unstable optimization and appearance drift when refined images are distilled back into the 3D representation. No prior method enforces cross-view consistency directly during the refinement process itself.

## Input / Output

The method takes N distorted renderings from a degraded 3D reconstruction (e.g., 3DGS) and optionally the closest clean training views as reference images. All inputs are encoded into latent space via a frozen SDXL VAE, and the model operates entirely on latent tensors. At inference, the model produces refined multi-view images in a single forward pass (no iterative denoising), which can then be distilled back into the 3D representation. Although trained on view pairs (N=2), the permutation-invariant attention mechanism generalizes to an arbitrary number of views at test time.

## Method

SyncFix formulates refinement as a joint Latent Bridge Matching (LBM) problem over the product latent space of multiple views, learning a direct velocity field that transports distorted latents to clean latents in one step rather than performing iterative denoising. The key architectural modification is cross-view attention: latent tokens from all views in a batch are concatenated along the token dimension and attend globally while preserving within-view positional encodings, so the predicted velocity for each view explicitly depends on features from every other view. This coupling is permutation-invariant and requires no explicit camera poses, epipolar constraints, or reprojection losses—multi-view consistency emerges purely from learned attention correlations. The model is built on a fine-tuned SDXL U-Net and trained with a flow-matching loss combined with pixel, LPIPS, and Gram losses applied to the jointly decoded outputs, ensuring structural and textural corrections remain consistent across views.

## Results

On DL3DV, SyncFix (with reference) achieves the best PSNR of 16.94 dB, LPIPS of 0.305, DreamSim of 0.099, and CVSC of 0.880, outperforming Difix3D+ (16.17 dB, 0.343, 0.135, 0.850) and FlowR (16.32 dB, 0.369, 0.138, 0.832). On the out-of-distribution NeRFBusters set, SyncFix reaches 14.34 dB PSNR and 0.940 CVSC, substantially ahead of Difix3D+ (12.05 dB, 0.931) and FlowR (13.09 dB, 0.916). Inference is single-pass at 5.63 s per scene, roughly 5× faster than Difix3D+ (30.6 s) and 20× faster than FlowR (109.8 s). Even without reference images (SyncFix†), the method still surpasses Difix3D+ and FlowR on PSNR, LPIPS, and CVSC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Difix3D+ (the primary per-view diffusion baseline), SyncFix gains +0.77 dB PSNR, reduces LPIPS by 0.038, cuts DreamSim by 27%, and improves CVSC from 0.850 to 0.880 on DL3DV; on NeRFBusters the PSNR gap widens to +2.29 dB (14.34 vs 12.05), showing stronger out-of-distribution generalization. Against FlowR (multi-view flow matching), SyncFix wins on PSNR (+0.62 dB on DL3DV, +1.25 dB on NeRFBusters), LPIPS, DreamSim, and CVSC, while also being ~20× faster at inference. Against Fixer (single-view, no reference), SyncFix dominates on every metric, with the largest gap in CVSC (0.880 vs 0.822 on DL3DV), confirming that multi-view coupling is essential for cross-view consistency. Against raw 3DGS, SyncFix improves PSNR by +1.0 dB and reduces FID by >4×, while also raising CVSC from 0.875 to 0.880—indicating that the method not only sharpens images but also preserves the geometric consistency that raw 3DGS artifacts happen to share. On the feedforward AnySplat setting (Mip-NeRF 360), SyncFix achieves the best CVSC (0.686 vs 0.649 for AnySplat and 0.589 for Difix3D+), demonstrating that the multi-view synchronization benefit transfers beyond per-scene 3DGS training.

## Relation to Prior Work

SyncFix builds directly on Latent Bridge Matching (LBM) for single-step image-to-image translation and on SyncLight's cross-view attention mechanism, repurposing both for 3D reconstruction repair rather than relighting. It departs from the dominant per-view refinement paradigm (Difix3D+, Nerfbusters, Instruct-NeRF2NeRF) by modeling a joint conditional over multiple views instead of independent marginals, and from iterative multi-view diffusion architectures (3DEnhancer, MVDream, CAT3D) by replacing multi-step denoising with a single ODE solve. The cross-view attention design is permutation-invariant and requires no explicit camera geometry, distinguishing it from epipolar-aggregation approaches like 3DEnhancer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that multi-view consistency can be enforced during generation via attention alone, without camera poses or reprojection losses, which simplifies the pipeline and suggests generative priors encode sufficient geometric structure. The ~5× and ~20× inference speedups over Difix3D+ and FlowR make joint refinement practically viable for iterative 3D optimization loops. Open questions include whether explicit geometric constraints would further improve robustness in extreme sparse-view settings, how to evaluate under-constrained reconstructions where pixel-aligned metrics penalize plausible completions, and whether the generative prior's distributional biases can be mitigated for faithful scene recovery.
