## Problem

Faithful image editing requires modifying only the target region while strictly preserving all other content, but existing mask-free approaches suffer from two coupled failures: automatic region estimation is imprecise and temporally unstable across sampling steps, and the heuristic preservation corrections (feature fusion, KV interpolation) they employ are inconsistent with the native sampling dynamics, making fidelity-improving techniques themselves a new source of artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262728/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image and a free-form text editing instruction as input, and produces an edited RGB image along with an automatically inferred binary edit mask (no external mask required). It is entirely training-free and plugs into existing backbone editors (diffusion-based such as InstructPix2Pix and AnyEdit, or flow-matching-based such as Qwen-Image-Edit and Step1X-Edit), operating in the latent space of the backbone while performing region discrimination in decoded pixel space. The binary mask is re-estimated at each self-refinement iteration rather than computed once.

## Method

SR-Edit organizes inference into an iterative self-refinement loop where each block performs a probe-and-refine cycle: a few-step inference produces a provisional edit, from which a pixel-space difference map is computed and refined through Otsu thresholding, morphological opening/closing, and connected-component filtering to yield a precise binary edit/non-edit mask. For preservation, the paper formulates the non-edit constraint as a vanishing-noise observation and derives an additive guidance term via Doob's h-transform theory; under a plug-in approximation this reduces to a masked residual on the model's clean-image prediction, injected as a drift correction in diffusion samplers or a velocity correction in flow-matching samplers without altering the noise schedule or ODE structure. The key distinction from prior work is that the guidance is derived from a distribution transform rather than heuristic state intervention, and the region mask is re-estimated from progressively refined predictions across iterations, so both localization accuracy and preservation tighten jointly.

## Results

On ImgEdit-Bench (497 cases), SR-Edit consistently improves non-edit preservation across four backbones while maintaining or improving instruction alignment. On Qwen-Image-Edit 2511, SR-Edit achieves the best CLIP score (26.80 vs. 26.16 base) and best LPIPS (0.31 vs. 0.46 base), with SSIM improving from 0.61 to 0.67. On AnyEdit, the structural fidelity gain is most dramatic: SSIM jumps from 0.70 to 0.85 and LPIPS drops from 0.41 to 0.35. On Step1X-Edit, SR-Edit attains the strongest preservation (SSIM 0.77, LPIPS 0.27) alongside a CLIP increase to 26.09.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SR-Edit outperforms both region-aware baselines (Follow-Your-Shape and SpotEdit) on the majority of preservation metrics across all four backbones. On Qwen-Image-Edit, SR-Edit beats SpotEdit on CLIP (26.80 vs. 26.11), PSNR (17.40 vs. 16.55), DISTS (0.18 vs. 0.20), and LPIPS (0.31 vs. 0.32), while SpotEdit edges ahead only on SSIM (0.70 vs. 0.67) and Judge (3.91 vs. 3.94 is a near-tie). On Step1X-Edit, SR-Edit achieves the best SSIM (0.77 vs. 0.75) and LPIPS (0.27 vs. 0.31) over SpotEdit, though SpotEdit retains a slight Judge advantage (4.08 vs. 4.01). Follow-Your-Shape is consistently the weakest region-aware method, often matching or underperforming the unmodified backbone (e.g., on Qwen-Image-Edit its LPIPS worsens to 0.47 and Judge drops to 3.59). The clearest win is on AnyEdit, where SR-Edit lifts SSIM by 0.15 and reduces LPIPS by 0.06 over the base, a margin far larger than any other backbone comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

SR-Edit builds on the Doob's h-transform framework from Schrödinger bridge theory (Bortoli et al. 2021; Doob 1957) to derive a principled preservation guidance term, departing from the heuristic state interventions (KV interpolation, feature fusion) used by prior region-aware editors like Follow-Your-Shape and SpotEdit. It also extends the line of training-free, mask-free region identification by shifting from latent-space or attention-map discrimination to pixel-space post-processing (Otsu thresholding, morphology, connected-component filtering), and wraps both components in an iterative self-refinement loop that prior single-pass methods lack. The method is backbone-agnostic, plugging into both diffusion and flow-matching samplers without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important bottleneck in instruction-based image editing: suppressing unintended drift in non-edit regions without external masks. The h-transform formulation provides a theoretically grounded alternative to ad-hoc preservation heuristics, and the training-free, plug-and-play nature makes it immediately applicable to existing editors. Open questions include whether the iterative refinement converges reliably for complex multi-region edits, how the method scales to video or 3D content, and whether the pixel-space post-processing pipeline generalizes to very high-resolution or heavily textured images where Otsu's global threshold may be insufficient.
