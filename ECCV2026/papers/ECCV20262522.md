## Problem

Existing panoramic 3D reconstruction benchmarks are either short (a few seconds), synthetic, or captured under constrained trajectories, leaving long-horizon drift and pervasive dynamic interference in real in-the-wild 360° captures untested. Concurrently, both per-scene optimization and feed-forward panoramic reconstruction methods assume static scenes, causing severe static-dynamic confusion and motion-induced artifacts when applied to unconstrained user-generated panoramic video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262522/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes equirectangular panoramic video frames (native ≥4K, ≥30 fps, sequences >30 s) along with COLMAP-estimated camera poses and sparse point clouds, plus offline dynamic object masks extracted by OmniSAM. It produces a time-varying 4D Gaussian representation (per-Gaussian opacity, center displacement, and velocity as functions of time) that renders novel views at arbitrary viewpoints and time steps. The accompanying benchmark (OmniLife360) provides 2,407 geometrically valid sequences totaling ~66 hours across 64 scene categories and 31 action classes, with all scenes pre-processed by COLMAP.

## Method

Omni4DGS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to the temporal domain for panoramic video by parameterizing each Gaussian's center as μ(t) = μ + α(t)·v_pano and its opacity as a Gaussian decay around a peak time τ, where α(t) is a periodic harmonic function. The key geometric insight is decomposing the velocity v_pano into independently learnable radial and tangential components, combined via latitude-aware adaptive weights (w_t = (√(1−μ̂_y²))^γ) that account for the nonlinear angular distortion of ERP projection near the poles. Dynamic-static decoupling is enforced by thresholding a staticness coefficient ρ = β/l and supervising dynamic Gaussians with OmniSAM-extracted 2D masks through an ℓ₁+SSIM rendering loss and an opacity-map consistency loss. Additional velocity regularization (penalizing non-zero velocity in static regions) and a motion regularization term (constraining angular direction changes of static Gaussians between adjacent frames) further stabilize the background. The full training objective combines RGB rendering loss, dynamic mask loss, velocity regularization, and motion regularization, optimized for 30K iterations per scene.

## Results

On the full 111-scene OmniLife360 benchmark, Omni4DGS achieves 29.655 dB PSNR and 0.146 LPIPS on input views, substantially outperforming the ODGS baseline (23.727 dB / 0.385 LPIPS). On novel view synthesis, Omni4DGS reaches 20.640 dB PSNR and 0.335 LPIPS versus ODGS's 20.145 dB / 0.433 LPIPS. Across four representative dynamic scenes, Omni4DGS attains an average of 21.519 dB PSNR and 0.302 LPIPS, surpassing the best feed-forward method PanoSplatt3R (17.797 dB / 0.437 LPIPS) by roughly 3.7 dB in PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Omni4DGS outperforms the per-scene optimization baseline ODGS by +5.9 dB PSNR and −0.239 LPIPS on input views (29.655 vs. 23.727; 0.146 vs. 0.385), though ODGS retains a slight SSIM edge on dynamic scenes (0.733 vs. 0.674 average). Against all four feed-forward baselines, Omni4DGS leads by a wide margin: PanoSplatt3R (17.646 dB), OmniSplat (17.270 dB), PanSplat (15.774 dB), and Splatter-360 (14.081 dB) all trail by 3–15 dB in PSNR on the full benchmark. In the direct 4DGS comparison (Table 5), Omni4DGS surpasses both pinhole-camera 4DGS methods adapted to panoramic projection—Deformable-GS (11.183–17.063 dB) and PVG (12.379–21.020 dB)—across all four tested scenes, with the largest gap on the 'bike' scene (+1.7 dB over PVG). The consistent LPIPS advantage (0.302 vs. 0.416 for ODGS, 0.437 for PanoSplatt3R) indicates substantially fewer perceptual artifacts in motion-affected regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

Omni4DGS extends the static panoramic Gaussian Splatting paradigm established by ODGS into the temporal domain, borrowing the periodic harmonic displacement and opacity decay formulation from Periodic Vibration Gaussian (PVG) but re-deriving the motion parameterization for spherical geometry. The key conceptual departure is the decomposition of a single Euclidean velocity vector into radial and tangential components with latitude-aware adaptive weighting, which addresses the geometric mismatch between Cartesian 4DGS formulations and equirectangular projection. The OmniLife360 benchmark itself carves new ground by filling the gap between short synthetic/controlled panoramic datasets (OmniBlender, HM3D) and the long-horizon, behavior-driven in-the-wild captures that consumer 360° cameras actually produce, using a human-AI collaborative collection pipeline with geometry-aware temporal slicing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2,407-sequence, 66-hour benchmark provides the first large-scale testbed for evaluating long-horizon stability and dynamic robustness in panoramic reconstruction, a setting where prior datasets were either static, synthetic, or limited to a few seconds. The finding that state-of-the-art methods degrade substantially under strong ego-motion and pervasive dynamics (e.g., feed-forward methods dropping to ~17 PSNR vs. ~29 for the proposed method on input views) quantifies a gap the community has lacked a rigorous measure for. Open questions include whether the periodic motion assumption generalizes to non-periodic or multi-object dynamics, whether the OmniSAM mask supervision can be replaced by learned or self-supervised alternatives, and whether the spherical velocity decomposition transfers to other projection models (e.g., cubemap, fisheye pairs).
