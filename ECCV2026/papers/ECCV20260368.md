## Problem

Most 3D reconstruction algorithms assume camera intrinsics remain constant throughout a video, but real-world footage frequently violates this due to zoom and focus changes. Two obstacles limit progress: training data with diverse, per-frame varying intrinsics is scarce, and existing benchmarks like InFlux lack sufficient scene and camera-motion diversity to properly evaluate prediction methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260368/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The task takes single RGB video frames (1280×720 for synthetic, 3424×2202 for real) and predicts per-frame camera intrinsics: focal lengths (f_x, f_y), principal point (c_x, c_y), and Brown-Conrady distortion coefficients (k_1, k_2, p_1, p_2). A subset of InFlux++ Synth additionally provides per-frame camera pose, depth maps, and surface normals. Evaluation uses LUT-reliable endpoint error (EPE) recall at multiple pixel thresholds and percent-error recall for individual intrinsic parameters, with a revised visibility filter that restricts EPE computation to the monotonic branch of the radial distortion mapping.

## Method

InFlux++ Synth is built on Infinigen's procedural scene generator and Blender renderer, parameterizing the effective focal length (CFL) via the thin-lens equation as a function of lens focal length (LFL) and lens-to-object distance (LTO) rather than setting CFL directly. Temporal variation is produced by bounded random walks on LFL (8–100 mm) and on a normalized focus position α within the ray-traced visible depth range, with Bézier interpolation between keyframes; this naturally induces lens breathing. A data loader applies Brown-Conrady distortion, color transforms, and noising on-the-fly, while the renderer produces defocus blur and dynamic objects. InFlux++ Real captures 334 high-resolution videos using zoom lenses that record per-frame LFL and FD metadata, with a novel large-FOV calibration procedure that moves the camera around a static 5.45 m × 3.06 m AprilTag screen instead of using drone-based experiments. Finetuning AnyCalib on InFlux++ Synth for 15 epochs improves f_x/f_y recall across both InFlux++ Real and InFlux, demonstrating the value of synthetic supervision for dynamic intrinsics prediction.

## Results

On the combined test splits of InFlux++ Real and InFlux, AnyCalib is the strongest baseline, achieving 25.2% f_x recall@10% and 25.1% LUT-reliable EPE recall@50 px, indicating that dynamic intrinsics prediction remains highly challenging. Finetuning AnyCalib on InFlux++ Synth improves focal length estimation substantially: f_x recall@10% rises from 25.2% to 31.2% and f_x recall@20% from 41.3% to 50.4% on the combined set. However, finetuning degrades principal point recall (c_x@1% drops from 76.5% to 67.2%) and EPE recall@50 px (25.1% → 18.7%), likely because AnyCalib's FOV-field loss is not directly aligned with EPE near image boundaries where distortion is most pronounced.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyCalib [29] dominates all other baselines on focal length estimation (25.2% f_x recall@10% vs. 19.7% for GeoCalib and 14.0% for UniDepthV2) and on EPE recall@50 px (25.1% vs. 16.7% for GeoCalib), confirming its status as SOTA for dynamic intrinsics prediction. GeoCalib [30] is the strongest on principal point estimation (82.3% c_x recall@1% vs. 76.5% for AnyCalib), suggesting it better captures lens center offsets. UniDepthV2 [17] and WildCamera [32] trail significantly on focal length (14.0% and 12.9% f_x@10% respectively) and EPE, while Perspective Fields [7], DroidCalib [6], and COLMAP [23] are weakest overall, with EPE recall@50 px below 5%. Finetuning AnyCalib on InFlux++ Synth wins on f_x and f_y recall at all thresholds (e.g., 31.2% vs. 25.2% at @10%) but loses on c_x, c_y, and EPE recall, indicating that synthetic supervision currently benefits focal length more than distortion or principal point estimation. The gap between the best method (AnyCalib) and the second (GeoCalib) on EPE@50 px is roughly 8 percentage points, and even the best method achieves only 25.1% EPE recall@50 px, underscoring that the task remains far from solved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the InFlux benchmark (NeurIPS 2025), which established the first real-world evaluation for per-frame dynamic intrinsics prediction, by addressing two identified gaps: the scarcity of diverse training data and the limited scene/motion coverage of existing benchmarks. For synthetic data, it builds on Infinigen's procedural scene generation and Blender rendering pipeline but introduces a physically grounded thin-lens parameterization (LFL + LTO) that produces realistic lens breathing and defocus blur, departing from prior synthetic datasets that use fixed focal lengths or simple pinhole models. For real data, it follows InFlux's zoom-lens metadata and LUT calibration methodology but introduces a novel board-based calibration procedure for large field-of-view settings, eliminating the need for drone-based experiments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dynamic intrinsics prediction remains severely under-solved: the best method (AnyCalib) achieves only 25.2% f_x recall@10% and 25.1% EPE recall@50px, meaning most 3D pipelines that assume fixed intrinsics will produce incorrect results on real-world video. The finding that synthetic supervision improves focal length estimation but simultaneously degrades EPE and principal point recall is an important open question—suggesting that the training loss (FOV-field) is misaligned with the evaluation metric (EPE), particularly under lens distortion. The large-scale data suite (441K+ synthetic, 514K+ real frames) provides the infrastructure needed to systematically close this gap, and the live leaderboard positions it as a community resource for future method development.
