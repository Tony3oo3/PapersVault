## Problem

Under few-shot supervision, even moderate label noise can disrupt prompt optimization in vision-language models like CLIP, yet existing noisy-label defenses (sample filtering, robust losses) either discard scarce training data or risk confirmation bias through aggressive label rewriting. The paper addresses how to perform high-precision, conservative label correction that fixes noisy annotations without corrupting clean ones, while providing theoretical justification for when such rewriting is safe.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262430/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB images (processed by a frozen ResNet-50 CLIP image encoder) and noisy class labels, producing a learned prompt that maps new images to class predictions at inference. Unusually, the training loop is closed: OT-derived pseudo-labels are tracked across epochs, and the observed training labels themselves are mutated in-place when temporal-stability and confidence criteria are jointly satisfied, so the supervision signal evolves over the course of training rather than remaining fixed.

## Method

TecoPrompt solves a balanced entropic optimal transport problem (via Sinkhorn iterations) between image embeddings and prompt-conditioned text prototypes in the shared CLIP space to obtain globally consistent pseudo-label candidates. It then verifies each candidate by checking that the OT assignment remains unchanged within a K-epoch stability window and that an EMA-smoothed confidence score stays above a threshold; only samples passing both gates undergo hard label rewriting. Rewritten and unrewritten samples are partitioned into clean, mid, and noisy groups, each optimized with a tailored loss (cross-entropy, generalized cross-entropy with q=0.5, and MAE respectively) under a weighted tri-group objective. A warmup phase of K−1 epochs precedes any rewriting to avoid confirmation bias from unstable early predictions, and Theorem 1 provides a sufficient margin condition showing that increasing K drives the probability of correct rewriting arbitrarily high.

## Results

TecoPrompt achieves the highest accuracy across all seven synthetic-noise datasets and the real-world Food101N benchmark under both symmetric and asymmetric label noise (12.5%–75%). The advantage grows with noise severity: on OxfordPets at 50% asymmetric noise, TecoPrompt reaches 84.3% accuracy versus 77.5% for NLPrompt (+6.8 pp), and at 75% asymmetric noise the gap widens to 75.3% vs. 48.6% (+26.7 pp). On Flowers102 at 75% asymmetric noise, TecoPrompt scores 66.6% versus 55.3% for NLPrompt. On the real-world Food101N dataset, TecoPrompt also attains the best accuracy among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TecoPrompt consistently outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and noise level tested. Against the strongest baseline NLPrompt (which also uses OT-based purification), TecoPrompt gains 1–11 pp under symmetric noise and 2–27 pp under asymmetric noise, with the largest margins at 75% noise (e.g., OxfordPets Asym 75%: 75.3% vs. 48.6%; Flowers102 Asym 75%: 66.6% vs. 55.3%). CoOp degrades most severely, dropping below 25% accuracy on most datasets at 75% noise, while CoOp+GCE is more stable but still lags TecoPrompt by 5–15 pp at high noise. JoAPR is competitive at moderate noise (25–37.5%) but collapses at 75% asymmetric noise on several datasets (e.g., Flowers102: 13.3% vs. TecoPrompt's 66.6%). The key differentiator is TecoPrompt's temporal-consistency gate: by requiring K-epoch stability before hard label rewriting, it avoids the confirmation-bias amplification that limits NLPrompt's purely OT-based soft supervision, as confirmed by the supervision-strategy comparison in Table 3 where TecoPrompt beats NLPrompt+ST and NLPrompt+LS at all noise levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

TecoPrompt extends the OT-based prompt purification paradigm established by PLOT (ICLR 2023) and NLPrompt (CVPR 2025), which use entropic optimal transport in the CLIP embedding space to generate globally consistent pseudo-labels for noisy-label prompt learning. The key departure is introducing a temporal verification layer: rather than accepting OT candidates immediately, TecoPrompt requires a K-epoch stability window and EMA-smoothed confidence gate before committing to hard label rewriting, drawing on temporal consistency principles from semi-supervised learning (Laine & Aila, 2017; Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017). The tri-group loss objective (CE / GCE / MAE on clean/mid/noisy subsets) refines the binary clean/noisy split used by NLPrompt into a three-tier supervision strategy, and the theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) provides a margin-based justification for why longer stability windows yield higher rewriting precision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: in few-shot prompt learning, discarding or downweighting noisy samples wastes scarce supervision, so high-precision label correction is preferable. The temporal-consistency criterion offers a principled, tunable mechanism (via K) to balance correction coverage against the risk of corrupting clean labels, and the 94.87% rewriting precision on OxfordPets demonstrates that this balance is achievable. Limitations include the need to tune K per dataset, the intentional under-coverage of frequently-flipping (forgettable) samples, and the additional per-epoch overhead of maintaining OT history and EMA state. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger backbones (ViT-L/14), to open-vocabulary or multi-label settings, and whether the theoretical margin conditions hold under the non-convex dynamics of actual prompt optimization.
