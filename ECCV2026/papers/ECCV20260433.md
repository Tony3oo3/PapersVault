## Problem

Existing point-level diffusion-based methods for 3D LiDAR scene completion (LiDiff, LiDPM) rely on approximations—such as constraining noise locally or truncating the reverse denoising process—to handle scene-scale data, introducing a mismatch between the initial distributions used during training and inference. This paper addresses the gap by proposing the first point-level flow matching formulation for scene-scale LiDAR completion that uses a consistent initial distribution across both phases.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single LiDAR scan (18,000 points sampled via farthest point sampling within a 50 m range), upsamples it 10× to 180,000 points by concatenation, and adds local Gaussian noise to form the initial point cloud. It produces a complete 3D scene point cloud of 180,000 points after 10 Euler integration steps; optionally, a refinement network upsamples the output by a factor of 6 to 1,080,000 points for denser reconstruction.

## Method

LiFlow formulates scene completion as a flow matching problem where a neural network (MinkUNet with Instance Normalization) regresses a time-dependent vector field that transports a noised, upsampled LiDAR scan to a complete scene. Two key losses drive training: Nearest Neighbor Flow Matching (NFM) establishes point-wise correspondences by assigning each initial point to its nearest neighbor in the target scene, defining a data-dependent coupling that directly aligns scans to complete scenes; Chamfer Distance Matching (CDM) adds a mutual-alignment term that encourages the generated cloud to spread out and cover the full target geometry. Classifier-free guidance conditions the vector field on the observed scan, and inference proceeds with 10 Euler steps at a guidance weight of 6.0. The consistent use of the noised scan as the initial distribution in both training and inference eliminates the distribution mismatch inherent in prior diffusion-based approaches, enabling fewer inference steps while maintaining generation quality.

## Results

On the SemanticKITTI validation set (sequence 08), LiFlow with the refinement network achieves the best Chamfer Distance (0.228 m) and Jensen-Shannon Divergence (0.367 m), outperforming LiDiff† (0.375 m CD, 0.416 m JSD) and LiDPM† (0.377 m CD, 0.403 m JSD). LiFlow† also leads in coarse Voxel IoU at 0.5 m resolution (37.2%), while LiDPM† retains a slight edge at finer resolutions (0.2 m: 25.8% vs 25.1%; 0.1 m: 14.9% vs 12.9%). Crucially, LiFlow achieves these results in only 10 Euler steps, compared to 50 DPM-Solver steps for LiDiff and 20 for LiDPM. On the unseen Apollo dataset, LiFlow† maintains its CD advantage (0.282 m at 10 steps) over both LiDiff† (0.350 m) and LiDPM† (0.406 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two point-level diffusion baselines, LiFlow† clearly wins on Chamfer Distance (0.228 m vs 0.375 m for LiDiff† and 0.377 m for LiDPM†) and JSD (0.367 m vs 0.416 m and 0.403 m), while also achieving the best coarse Voxel IoU at 0.5 m (37.2% vs 32.4% and 36.6%). However, LiDPM† retains a modest advantage on fine-grained Voxel IoU at 0.2 m (25.8% vs 25.1%) and 0.1 m (14.9% vs 12.9%), which the authors attribute to the lack of explicit one-to-one point matching in NFM. All four voxel-level baselines (LMSCNet, LODE, MID, PVD) are substantially outperformed by LiFlow on CD (0.228 m vs 0.503–1.256 m) and JSD, confirming the advantage of point-level generative modeling over voxel-grid approaches. On the unseen Apollo dataset, LiFlow† generalizes better than both LiDiff† and LiDPM† across all inference step counts (5–50), with CD improvements of 0.068 m over LiDiff† and 0.124 m over LiDPM† at 10 steps, while also being faster (7.4 s vs 7.5 s and 7.5 s per frame). The key efficiency gain is that LiFlow reaches its best quality in 10 Euler steps, whereas LiDiff requires 50 DPM-Solver steps and LiDPM requires 20, making LiFlow roughly 2–5× faster at comparable or superior quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiFlow extends the Flow Matching paradigm (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) from single-object 3D point cloud generation to scene-scale multi-object LiDAR completion, a setting previously addressed only by diffusion-based methods (LiDiff, LiDPM). It departs from those diffusion approaches by replacing the DDPM forward/reverse process with a data-dependent coupling that directly aligns a noisy single LiDAR scan to the complete scene, thereby eliminating the train-inference distribution mismatch that plagues LiDiff's local-noise surrogate and LiDPM's truncated reverse process. The method reuses the MinkUNet backbone from LiDPM but introduces two novel loss terms—Nearest Neighbor Flow Matching and Chamfer Distance Matching—to establish point-wise correspondences and encourage global scene coverage, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiFlow demonstrates that flow matching can replace diffusion for scene-scale LiDAR completion with fewer inference steps (10 Euler steps vs. 50 DPM-Solver steps for LiDiff), offering a practical efficiency gain for real-time autonomous driving pipelines. The clean resolution of the distribution mismatch problem is a conceptual contribution that may inform future generative approaches for other scene-scale 3D tasks. However, fine-grained Voxel IoU (0.1 m) remains below LiDPM†, and the authors acknowledge that the lack of explicit one-to-one point matching in NFM limits fine-grained occupancy—a gap that future work on OT-based couplings or permutation-aware losses could address.
