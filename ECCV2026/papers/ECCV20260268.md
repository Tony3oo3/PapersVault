## Problem

Embodied robots must interpret spatially constrained instructions (e.g., 'hover 1–5 cm above each flower from left to right') by generating 3D positional sequences called spatial traces, a task requiring multi-step, metric-grounded reasoning that combines 3D spatial referring with absolute-scale measuring. Existing VLMs produce only 2D visual traces or lack absolute metric understanding, creating a fundamental gap between 2D perception and the 3D, scale-aware reasoning that real-world manipulation demands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260268/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (optionally augmented with camera intrinsics, absolute depth, or poses via the universal spatial encoder) and a free-text instruction, and outputs an ordered sequence of 6–12 sparse 3D keypoints, each represented in a decoupled (u, v, d) format—image-plane coordinates plus absolute depth—that is trivially convertible to (x, y, z) via intrinsics and projectable to 2D by dropping depth. A separate scale decoder simultaneously regresses a numeric scale factor from a <SCALE> token, providing explicit metric awareness even from RGB-only inputs. The geometric inputs are optional and can be added at inference without retraining, enabling geometry-adaptive precision.

## Method

RoboTracer builds on NVILA and introduces two architectural modules aligned to the LLM via projectors: a universal spatial encoder (initialized from the MapAnything feed-forward 3D geometry model) that flexibly ingests arbitrary geometric cues, and a scale decoder supervised by a regression loss on the predicted metric scale factor to heighten absolute-scale awareness. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) a two-step SFT—first aligning the spatial encoder and scale decoder with geometric annotations, then fine-tuning all components on TraceSpatial (4.5M samples, 30M QA pairs, 48.2% metric-grounded) mixed with RGB-only and RGB+X inputs to preserve general VQA ability; (2) an RFT stage using GRPO with metric-sensitive process rewards (format, accuracy on key perceptual steps for referring/measuring) in addition to outcome-based rewards (point and trace alignment), where process rewards are scaled by 0.25 and are order-invariant. The key insight is that supervising intermediate perceptual cues—object localization and metric measurement—during RFT provides crucial evidence for accurate multi-step trace generation, outperforming purely outcome-based rewards by 4% overall success rate and yielding substantially larger gains on 3D metrics.

## Results

On the novel TraceSpatial-Bench, RoboTracer-RFT with RGB+intrinsics+depth input achieves 45% overall success rate, surpassing Gemini-2.5-Pro (3%) by 42 percentage points and the next-best baseline RoboRefer-2B (28%) by 17 points. On spatial understanding benchmarks (CV-Bench, BLINK, RoboSpatial, EmbSpatial), RoboTracer-8B-SFT attains an 85.7% average success rate, exceeding Gemini-2.5-Pro by 8.58%. On spatial measuring (Q-Spatial, MSMU), RoboTracer-8B-SFT reaches 73.27% on Q-Spatial Plus and 70.00% on MSMU Refer Estimation, outperforming all baselines. On RoboTwin 2.0 hard tasks, RoboTracer achieves 64.0% total average success rate without any task-specific training, versus 8.6% for the best end-to-end VLA baseline (π0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboTracer outperforms all 15 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against the strongest general VLMs (Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-4o, Qwen3-VL-8B), RoboTracer-8B-SFT leads by 8.58% average on spatial understanding and by 16.83% on Q-Spatial Plus measuring, while RoboTracer-RFT exceeds Gemini-2.5-Pro by 42 points on TraceSpatial-Bench overall SR (45% vs. 3%), revealing that general VLMs produce floating or colliding 3D traces due to poor depth estimation. Among robotics-specific baselines, RoboTracer surpasses RoboBrain 2.0-7B (66.40% vs. 54.87% on RoboSpatial referring), MolmoAct (0.1449 vs. 0.7764 DFD on ShareRobot-Bench), and Embodied-R1-3B (45% vs. 13% on TraceSpatial-Bench 2D End SR). For 3D-input baselines, RoboTracer-RFT (R+I+D) beats RoboRefer-2B finetuned with depth (45% vs. 28% overall) and LEO-7B with point clouds (45% vs. 27%), demonstrating that learned metric-grounded reasoning complements raw 3D geometry. On RoboTwin 2.0, RoboTracer (64.0%) outperforms all end-to-end VLAs (π0: 8.6%, RDT: 5.7%, DP3: 2.5%) and all VLM baselines (Gemini-2.5-Pro: 0.5%, Qwen3-VL-8B: 0%) without task-specific training, with the gap widening to 32.8% on unseen tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboTracer builds on NVILA as its base VLM, adopts GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) for reinforcement fine-tuning, and leverages MapAnything's feed-forward metric 3D reconstruction as the backbone of its universal spatial encoder. It departs from prior 2D visual trace generation work (RoboBrain, HAMSTER, MolmoAct) by formulating the problem in 3D with absolute metric grounding, and from existing spatial reasoning VLMs (SpatialVLM, SpatialBot) by introducing a scale decoder with regression loss and metric-sensitive process rewards that supervise intermediate perception steps rather than only final outputs. The decoupled (u,v,d) point representation is a deliberate design choice that enables seamless projection to 2D traces and co-training with existing 2D datasets, distinguishing it from direct (x,y,z) 3D coordinate prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark or large-scale dataset existed for compositional 3D spatial tracing with multi-step metric-grounded reasoning, and the 30M-QA TraceSpatial dataset plus TraceSpatial-Bench provide the infrastructure the field lacked. The practical impact is significant—spatial traces as an intermediate representation enable zero-shot generalization across robot embodiments (UR5, G1 humanoid) on spatially constrained tasks where end-to-end VLAs fail entirely, and the framework can generate training data to improve VLA models. The stated limitation is that motion planning is needed to recover 6D end-effector poses, making the approach less effective for rotation-rich manipulation; dense spatial traces that encode orientation are identified as a natural next step.
