## Problem

Reinforcement learning for chest X-ray report generation suffers from training instability because a fixed KL regularization coefficient either stifles productive exploration or fails to prevent destabilizing policy drift, while a static reference policy accumulates KL pressure over time until the policy collapses. Existing adaptive-β methods operate at the batch level (masking harmful individual trajectories) or require auxiliary critics or preference data, and EMA-based reference updates are vulnerable to reward hacking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262464/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single chest X-ray image as input, which is encoded by a pretrained LVLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) into visual tokens. During Stages 2 and 3, the prompt is augmented with instruction tokens and up to 14 disease-category labels predicted by a frozen CheXpert classifier, forming a composite prompt [I, D, V]. The output is a free-text radiology report; during RL, G candidate reports are sampled per image and scored by BLEU-4 against the reference report to compute group-relative advantages.

## Method

REVA-PO is a three-stage training pipeline (warm-up → classifier-guided SFT → RL) built on a pretrained LVLM, with the RL stage using a GRPO-style objective modified by two mechanisms. Response-Weighted Regularization (RER) assigns each sampled response a per-response KL weight β_i that is a piecewise-linear function of its group-relative advantage A_i and the token-normalized reference-policy entropy: for positive-advantage responses, higher reference entropy lowers β_i toward β_min (shielding novel good behavior), while for negative-advantage responses, higher entropy raises β_i toward β_max (pulling the policy back). Validation-Anchored Policy Reset (VAPR) periodically resets both the reference and current policies to the best validation checkpoint every M epochs, zeroing accumulated KL divergence and expanding the reachable exploration space without the lag or reward-hacking risk of EMA-based reference tracking.

## Results

On MIMIC-CXR, REVA-PO achieves BLEU-4 of 0.143 (+5.1% over AM-MRG's 0.136), BLEU-3 of 0.195 (+4.3%), and ROUGE-L of 0.293. On IU-Xray, it reaches BLEU-4 of 0.199 (+3.6% over AM-MRG) and BLEU-3 of 0.261 (+3.2%). For clinical efficacy on MIMIC-CXR, REVA-PO sets new records with CheXpert F1 of 0.506 (+4.5% over AM-MRG) and RadGraph F1 of 0.246 (+12.8% over STREAM), demonstrating superior clinical factual consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REVA-PO outperforms all 16 baselines on every reported NLG metric across both datasets. On MIMIC-CXR, the closest competitors are MambaXray-VL [45] and STREAM [58] (BLEU-4 = 0.133), which REVA-PO surpasses by 7.5% (0.143); AM-MRG [46] is not evaluated on MIMIC-CXR in the NLG table but leads on CE metrics, where REVA-PO still wins CheXpert F1 by 4.5% (0.506 vs. 0.484) and RadGraph F1 by 12.8% over STREAM (0.246 vs. 0.218). On IU-Xray, REVA-PO beats the strongest non-LLM method CMCRL [5] (BLEU-4: 0.199 vs. 0.190) and the strongest LLM method AM-MRG [46] (BLEU-4: 0.199 vs. 0.192; BLEU-3: 0.261 vs. 0.253), while tying MambaXray-VL on METEOR (0.216). Notably, LLM-based baselines (B-LLM, CoD, STREAM, MambaXray-VL) that rely on contrastive image-encoder pretraining degrade on the smaller IU-Xray dataset, whereas REVA-PO's LVLM-based approach maintains consistent gains, suggesting reduced overfitting risk. Among CE baselines, AM-MRG [46] achieves the highest CheXpert precision (0.555) but REVA-PO surpasses it on both precision (0.569) and F1 (0.506), while STREAM [58] leads in recall (0.407) yet trails REVA-PO on F1 (0.460 vs. 0.506) and RadGraph F1 (0.218 vs. 0.246).

## Relation to Prior Work

REVA-PO extends the GRPO objective (from DeepSeek-R1 and DeepSeekMath) by replacing the fixed KL coefficient with a response-level adaptive weight (RER) conditioned on advantage and reference-policy entropy, and by periodically resetting both the reference and current policies to the best validation checkpoint (VAPR). It departs from prior CXR-RG methods that rely on contrastive learning to align a separately trained image encoder with an LLM, instead adopting a pretrained LVLM (Qwen2.5-VL) to eliminate that alignment step. The RER mechanism is positioned as a lighter-weight alternative to batch-level β scheduling in PPO, preference-data-dependent methods like β-DPO and ε-DPO, and critic-based instance-level scaling, while VAPR addresses the reward-hacking vulnerability of EMA-based reference tracking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a practical bottleneck—RL training instability and limited exploration in medical report generation—without requiring auxiliary critics or expensive radiologist preference annotations, making the approach accessible to groups with limited annotation budgets. The 12.8% RadGraph F1 gain over the prior best suggests that stabilizing the optimization process (rather than merely improving the reward signal) can yield meaningful clinical-accuracy improvements. Open questions include whether RER and VAPR generalize beyond CXR-RG to other LVLM fine-tuning tasks, whether BLEU-4 as a proxy reward adequately captures clinical nuance, and whether the three-stage pipeline's complexity is justified relative to simpler two-stage alternatives.
