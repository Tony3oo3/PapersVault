## Problem

Existing VLA efficiency work focuses on reducing per-step inference latency (token pruning, quantization, KV-cache reuse) but leaves the intrinsic policy efficiency bottleneck unaddressed: unreliable tail predictions in action chunks force conservative execution windows, and redundant physical steps inflate the total number of forward inference calls. This paper identifies these two factors—effective executable chunk length and total physical steps—as the primary deployment bottleneck and shows they are orthogonal to and multiplicative with compute-centric acceleration.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a post-training stage on pre-trained VLA models, consuming the same inputs the base policy already uses: RGB visual observations and a language instruction. The output is an action chunk of up to H actions predicted in parallel per inference call, but the key structural detail is the execution window h = ⌊γH⌋, which determines how many of those predicted actions are actually executed before the robot re-observes. PolicyTrim's goal is to push γ toward 1 (executing the full chunk reliably) while simultaneously reducing the total number of such inference calls needed to complete a task.

## Method

PolicyTrim is a two-stage GRPO-based RL post-training framework that requires no architectural changes or additional expert data. Stage 1 (Reliable Chunk Extension) assigns each trajectory in a sampled group a distinct acceptance ratio γ from a discrete set Γ, turning each group into a parallel reliability sweep across short, intermediate, and long chunk positions; a horizon reward scaled by γ is activated only when at least one trajectory in the group succeeds, progressively pushing the trustworthy prediction frontier toward the architectural limit H. Stage 2 (Redundancy-Aware Step Reduction) introduces a step-saving reward proportional to (S_base − S(τ))/S_base for successful trajectories, coupled with a group-anchored tanh stability penalty that penalizes trajectories deviating from the group mean step count, preventing the policy from collapsing onto fragile, irreproducible shortcuts. Both stages optimize via GRPO's group-normalized advantages with a clipped surrogate objective and per-token KL regularization to the reference policy, preserving manipulation priors from pre-training.

## Results

On LIBERO, PolicyTrim reduces physical execution steps by up to 51.4% (π0.5 on Object: 125.0 → 64.3) while maintaining or slightly improving success rates (e.g., 99.1% → 98.5%). Action chunk execution length triples from 5 to 15 for π0.5 and GR00T, yielding up to 5.83× end-to-end speedup. On ManiSkill and Meta-World, PolicyTrim achieves 2.36× and 2.52× speedup respectively with π0.5, and real-world deployment on an Agilex Piper arm shows a 1.86× wall-clock speedup with maintained or improved success rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolicyTrim is applied as a post-training stage on top of three VLA baselines (π0.5, OpenVLA-OFT, GR00T) rather than competing against them head-to-head. For π0.5, PolicyTrim cuts physical steps by 44–51% across all LIBERO subsets while keeping success rates within 0.6 pp, and triples the reliable chunk length from 5 to 15, yielding 2.91–5.83× speedup. For GR00T, steps drop 10–16% and chunk length doubles from 5 to 10, giving 2.08–2.37× speedup with SR gains of 0.6–3.1 pp. For OpenVLA-OFT, chunk extension is inapplicable due to its parallel-decoding architecture, so only the step-reduction stage is applied, still achieving 1.40–1.97× speedup with steps halved. Against the compute-centric baseline VLA-Cache (1.26× speedup), PolicyTrim is orthogonal: combining both yields up to 2.48× on LIBERO-Object, confirming multiplicative rather than redundant gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolicyTrim builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) for its group-relative advantage computation and clipped surrogate objective, adapting this LLM-oriented RL framework to the VLA manipulation setting. It departs from the dominant line of VLA efficiency work (token pruning, quantization, KV-cache reuse, speculative decoding), which treats the learned policy as fixed and targets per-inference latency, by instead optimizing the policy itself to reduce the total number of inference calls. Within RL post-training for VLAs, it moves beyond the binary success rewards used by prior GRPO-based methods, introducing a step-saving reward and a dynamic horizon exploration mechanism that jointly address two previously conflated inefficiency sources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying intrinsic policy efficiency as an orthogonal axis to compute-centric acceleration, the paper opens a multiplicative speedup pathway that existing inference-optimization techniques leave entirely unaddressed, as confirmed by the compounded gains when combined with VLA-Cache. The practical impact is substantial for real-world deployment: halving physical steps and tripling chunk utilization on a physical robot arm translates directly to throughput and energy savings. Limitations include the requirement for RL post-training infrastructure (8 A100s, 64 parallel envs), the need for supervised fine-tuning on real demonstrations for sim-to-real transfer, and the finding that parallel-decoding architectures like OpenVLA-OFT cannot benefit from chunk extension, restricting the full two-stage pipeline to diffusion-based decoders. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-stage or bimanual tasks and whether on-device continuous learning could further adapt efficiency to unseen environments.
