## Problem

General-purpose vision-language models degrade under scientific imaging domain shift because visual statistics (grayscale atomic-scale lattices, modality-dependent contrast), specialized materials-science terminology, and experimental reasoning all fall outside natural-image pretraining distributions. In TEM specifically, the problem is compounded by severe data scarcity: large-scale annotated datasets are unavailable, and existing deep-learning approaches optimize single-task outputs without supporting interactive, language-conditioned interpretation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261977/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single TEM sub-image (one of four verified modalities: CTEM, HRTEM, STEM, SAED) cropped from compound paper figures, paired with a natural-language prompt. For TEM-VQA it produces free-form scientific explanations; for TEM-MCQ it selects among four answer options spanning visual perception, scientific reasoning, and experimental methodology. At inference time the model receives only the image and prompt—parent figure captions and article metadata used during data generation are never supplied, preserving realistic evaluation conditions.

## Method

ATOMIC adapts LLaVA-v1.5 (CLIP-ViT-L-336px vision tower + Vicuna-7B/13B language model) purely through data-centric supervision without architectural modification. A two-stage pipeline first trains only the MLP connector on 60k–120k GPT-generated alignment targets, then jointly fine-tunes the language model and connector on 30k–60k multi-turn conversations. The key design insight is decomposing supervision into two complementary signals: VisionGround, which conditions solely on the TEM image to enforce visual grounding, and DomainContext, which additionally injects expert figure-caption context to supply domain terminology and experimental information; their combination (Blend) yields consistent gains across both perception- and knowledge-intensive queries. Training data is constructed entirely from peer-reviewed Nature-series literature via automated figure-caption mining, YOLOv11 + ResNet-50 modality filtering, and GPT-based instruction generation—eliminating manual annotation.

## Results

On TEM-MCQ, ATOMIC-7B-Blend achieves 75.2% overall accuracy and ATOMIC-13B-Blend reaches 76.5%, both outperforming all locally deployable baselines (LLaVA-v1.5-7B: 70.9%, LLaVA-v1.5-13B: 74.0%, BLIP-2: 73.5%, InstructBLIP: 61.6%). On TEM-VQA, ATOMIC-7B-Blend attains 37.5% Answer Word Coverage (AWC), narrowing the gap to GPT-4o (40.4%) by only 2.9 percentage points while enabling local, API-free deployment. On the independent MatCha TEM subset, ATOMIC-7B-Blend+FT achieves 56.6% overall accuracy, surpassing LLaVA-v1.5-7B+FT (38.5%) by 18.1 percentage points under identical fine-tuning conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATOMIC-7B-Blend outperforms all four locally deployable baselines on both TEM-VQA and TEM-MCQ: it leads LLaVA-v1.5-7B by 4.2 pp on AWC (37.5% vs. 33.3%) and 4.3 pp on MCQ accuracy (75.2% vs. 70.9%), and surpasses BLIP-2 and InstructBLIP by 25+ pp on AWC (37.5% vs. 12.3%/12.6%). Against the frontier zero-shot references, ATOMIC-7B-Blend narrows the TEM-VQA AWC gap to GPT-4o to 2.9 pp (37.5% vs. 40.4%) but trails substantially on TEM-MCQ accuracy (75.2% vs. 92.3%), reflecting the difficulty of closed-ended discrimination without task fine-tuning. On the independent MatCha benchmark, ATOMIC-7B-Blend+FT achieves 56.6% overall accuracy versus 38.5% for LLaVA-v1.5-7B+FT (18.1 pp gain) and 47.7% for ATOMIC-7B-Blend without fine-tuning, confirming that staged TEM adaptation provides a stronger initialization for downstream fine-tuning than the general-domain LLaVA checkpoint. The 13B variant (ATOMIC-13B-Blend) edges out the 7B on MCQ (76.5% vs. 75.2%) but does not improve TEM-VQA, suggesting that in this data-scarce setting, structured supervision matters more than parameter scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

ATOMIC builds directly on the LLaVA-v1.5 architecture (CLIP-ViT-L-336px vision tower + Vicuna language model + MLP connector) and its two-stage training paradigm, deliberately holding the architecture fixed to isolate the effect of data-centric supervision design. It extends the GPT-based self-instruct recipe (Self-Instruct, Alpaca, LLaVA) into a scientific-imaging domain by mining peer-reviewed Nature-series articles for figure-caption pairs and generating TEM-specific instruction data, a pipeline analogous to LLaVA-Med's approach for biomedical images but targeting materials-science TEM. The key conceptual departure from prior domain-adaptive VLMs is the explicit separation of two supervision signals—VisionGround (image-only grounding) and DomainContext (caption-conditioned scientific context)—enabling controlled analysis of how visual evidence versus textual domain knowledge each contribute to TEM interpretation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides a reproducible, annotation-free pipeline for adapting VLMs to data-scarce scientific imaging domains, which is practically important for labs that cannot send proprietary microscopy data to cloud APIs. The finding that structured domain-specific supervision (not model scale) drives performance in this regime—7B outperforming 13B on open-ended VQA—suggests a cost-effective path for domain adaptation. However, the training data is biased toward publication-quality figures, and the identified failure modes (quantitative scale estimation, periodic structure interpretation) indicate that data-level adaptation alone has a ceiling; architectural advances or additional supervision sources remain open questions for closing the gap to frontier models.
