## Problem

Existing visual text generation and editing models are either UNet-based (suffering from structural distortions and localized artifacts) or DiT-based but restricted to a single task, preventing cross-task synergy and consistent visual style. This limitation is particularly acute for non-Latin scripts like Chinese, where intricate glyph structures and small-scale text demand precise structural regularity that current models fail to maintain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260005/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For editing, the model receives a masked image, a glyph image (the target text rendered in a font), and a binary mask, all concatenated along the spatial dimension to form a single input tensor; for generation, the glyph image is concatenated with a fully black canvas in the same manner. All inputs are resized to 512×512 pixels. The output is a single 512×512 RGB image containing the rendered or edited text seamlessly integrated into the background.

## Method

InnoText builds on the Flux.1 Fill DiT backbone and unifies generation and editing through an in-context learning input paradigm where the glyph image is spatially concatenated with either a masked image (editing) or a black canvas (generation), allowing a single model to switch tasks via input configuration alone. A Font Size-Aware Modulation (FSAM) module constructs a grayscale size map from detected text bounding boxes (using inverse font height to emphasize small text), patchifies it into token embeddings, and feeds them through two parallel MLP branches that produce scale (γ) and shift (β) parameters to modulate DiT hidden states, enabling scale-invariant attention across font sizes. A Small-Character Aware Augmentation (SCAA) strategy stochastically magnifies sub-threshold text regions during training to distill sub-pixel structural priors, while a Task-Specific Region Weighted Loss applies region-weighted flow matching for editing (emphasizing masked areas) and global flow matching for generation. Training uses LoRA (rank 128) with the Prodigy optimizer on 8 A100 GPUs for 30K steps, alternating between tasks at equal probability.

## Results

On the editing task, InnoText achieves the highest sentence accuracy in both English (0.7988) and Chinese (0.7257), along with the lowest LPIPS in English (0.0786), outperforming the closest competitor TextFlux (0.7732 / 0.7164 Sen. Acc). On the generation task, InnoText dominates all baselines across every metric, reaching 0.6586 English and 0.5907 Chinese sentence accuracy with LPIPS of 0.4787 and 0.5311 respectively—substantially ahead of AnyText2 (0.5638 / 0.5262) and far exceeding TextFlux, which is essentially non-functional for generation (Sen. Acc ≈ 0.007–0.019).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Flux-Fill (the unmodified base model), InnoText improves editing sentence accuracy from 0.1342 to 0.7988 (English) and from 0.0191 to 0.7257 (Chinese), confirming that the text-specific modules are essential. Versus AnyText and AnyText2 (UNet-based unified models), InnoText wins on sentence accuracy and LPIPS for editing in English (0.7988 vs. 0.7893; 0.0786 vs. 0.1739) and sweeps all six generation metrics, with a 9.5-point English Sen. Acc gap over AnyText2. Against TextFlux (the strongest DiT-based editing baseline), InnoText leads on English Sen. Acc (0.7988 vs. 0.7732) and Chinese Sen. Acc/NED (0.7257/0.8579 vs. 0.7164/0.8498), while TextFlux retains a marginal LPIPS edge in Chinese editing (0.0567 vs. 0.0591). For generation, InnoText is the only method that performs competitively—TextFlux drops to near-zero accuracy (≈0.007–0.019 Sen. Acc), revealing that it is not designed for from-scratch text synthesis, whereas InnoText's unified in-context paradigm handles both tasks in a single model.

## Relation to Prior Work

InnoText builds directly on the Flux-Fill DiT backbone, adapting an inpainting model into a unified visual text framework via in-context learning with specialized input concatenation patterns. It departs from the UNet-based lineage (AnyText, AnyText2) that dominates the visual text generation/editing space, and from single-task DiT approaches like TextFlux, by demonstrating that a single DiT model can handle both generation and editing through input paradigm switching rather than architectural separation. The Font Size-Aware Modulation module extends the standard DiT conditioning mechanism with a spatially-varying size map, while the Small-Character Aware Augmentation and Task-Specific Region Weighted Loss address scale sensitivity and task-specific optimization in ways not present in prior visual text methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of generation and editing in one DiT model reduces redundant training pipelines and enables consistent visual style across tasks, which is practically important for production text-rendering systems. The InnoText-30K bilingual dataset addresses a real gap in high-quality Chinese visual text data, though its construction relies on a chain of external models (Seedream, Intern-VL3, DeepSeek-VL2) that may introduce compounding biases. Open questions remain around failure modes with large mask regions, extremely small or densely-stroked Chinese characters, and whether the in-context input paradigm generalizes beyond the two tasks demonstrated. The moderate margins over TextFlux and AnyText2 suggest the unified DiT approach is promising but not yet a decisive architectural win over well-tuned UNet baselines.
