## Problem

Existing diffusion-based novel view refinement methods (e.g., DIFIX3D+) process rendered novel views and reference views in a single network, conflating structural and appearance information. This causes the refined output to blur as the network cannot simultaneously preserve the perspective/structure of the rendered view and match the appearance of the reference view. The paper demonstrates that DIFIX actually performs worse with reference views than without them, and blurriness worsens as the angular distance between views increases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260938/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a sparse set of RGB input views (3, 6, 9, or 24 views), an initial 3DGS reconstruction, and renders novel views from it. Each rendered novel view is paired with its closest ground-truth training view as a reference. Refinement operates in VAE latent space at a fixed low timestep (t=200), simulating single-step denoising rather than full diffusion. The output is a refined novel view decoded back to pixel space, which is then incorporated into 3DGS optimization with a confidence-weighted mask.

## Method

DualDiff employs two parallel diffusion branches (both SD-Turbo U-Nets initialized with DIFIX weights): a structure branch that processes the low-quality rendered novel view to preserve geometric/perspective consistency, and an appearance branch that processes the reference view to extract appearance features. A Structure-Appearance Attention (SAA) module replaces self-attention in the structure branch, using queries from the structure branch's hidden states and keys/values from the concatenation of both branches' hidden states, enabling selective appearance injection without disrupting structure. The broader DualDiff3D framework wraps this in a Render-Refine-Optimize loop with a Progressive Sampling and Filtering strategy (interpolating viewpoints near existing cameras and filtering by confidence) and a Confidence-Driven Weighting process that combines refine-variance, pixel-discrepancy, and reprojection confidence into a per-pixel mask for stable 3DGS optimization, with a validation-and-rollback mechanism to prevent quality degradation.

## Results

On the DL3DV dataset, DualDiff3D achieves PSNR of 12.56 (3-view), 14.11 (6-view), 15.60 (9-view), and 20.49 (24-view), consistently outperforming DIFIX3D by 0.7–1.2 dB across all view counts. On LLFF, DualDiff3D reaches PSNR 16.34 (3-view) and 21.89 (9-view), improving over DIFIX3D by 1.13 and 0.36 dB respectively. On Mip-NeRF360, DualDiff3D attains the best 3-view PSNR (15.70) and the best LPIPS across all view counts, though GSFixer edges it by ~0.1–0.2 dB PSNR at 6 and 9 views. The refinement model alone (DualDiff) also outperforms DIFIX by up to 0.8 dB PSNR in the inference-only setting without any additional training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualDiff3D consistently outperforms vanilla 3DGS by 1.8–2.2 dB PSNR across all view counts on both DL3DV and LLFF, confirming that the RRO loop with diffusion refinement substantially improves sparse-view reconstruction. Against DIFIX3D (the closest prior method using a single-branch image diffusion prior), DualDiff3D wins by 0.7–1.2 dB PSNR on DL3DV and 0.36–1.13 dB on LLFF, with the gap largest in the most challenging 3-view setting. On Mip-NeRF360, DualDiff3D surpasses FSGS (15.70 vs 14.17 PSNR at 3 views) and GenFusion (15.70 vs 15.29) at 3 views, and beats 3DGS-Enhancer (reported values) by a wide margin. However, GSFixer slightly edges DualDiff3D on PSNR at 6-view (17.27 vs 17.22) and 9-view (18.63 vs 18.45) on Mip-NeRF360, though DualDiff3D still achieves lower LPIPS (0.451 vs 0.478 and 0.405 vs 0.420), indicating better perceptual quality. The refinement model DualDiff also demonstrates a 'free lunch' property: even without any fine-tuning (inference-only), it outperforms DIFIX by 0.7 dB PSNR, validating the structural decoupling design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DIFIX3D+ [35], which introduced single-step diffusion-based refinement of 3DGS-rendered novel views, and on 3DGS [12] as the underlying 3D representation. The key conceptual departure is identifying that DIFIX3D+'s single-network design—concatenating reference and novel view features along the spatial dimension—fundamentally conflates two distinct information types (structural consistency vs. appearance consistency), and proposing a dual-branch architecture with a Structure-Appearance Attention module to decouple them. The dual-branch design draws inspiration from virtual try-on and image animation pipelines, while the reliability-enhanced RRO loop with Progressive Sampling and Filtering and Confidence-Driven Weighting extends the basic render-refine-optimize paradigm with mechanisms to prevent 3DGS optimization instability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in sparse-view 3DGS reconstruction where rendered novel views contain artifacts that degrade downstream optimization. The 'free lunch' finding—that simply reorganizing DIFIX's pre-trained weights into a dual-branch architecture yields a 0.7 dB PSNR gain without any additional training—provides strong evidence that the single-network design is inherently suboptimal, which could inform future diffusion-based refinement architectures more broadly. Limitations include high VRAM usage (~12.5 GB peak), non-real-time inference, and moderate (1–2 dB) reconstruction gains over DIFIX3D. Open questions include whether the structure-appearance decoupling generalizes to other image-to-image diffusion tasks and whether the confidence-weighting machinery can be simplified without sacrificing stability.
