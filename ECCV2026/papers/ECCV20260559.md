## Problem

Existing Document VQA methods either produce ungrounded chain-of-thought steps or rely on coarse rectangular bounding boxes that cannot accurately localize complex structures such as pie-chart wedges, flowchart nodes, curved text, and table cells. No prior approach provides step-wise grounded reasoning with pixel-level segmentation masks across diverse document types (charts, tables, flowcharts, reports), leaving intermediate evidence unverified and spatially imprecise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260559/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a document image (arbitrary resolution) and a free-form text question as input. It autoregressively generates an ordered sequence of intermediate CoT steps and a final answer, with special [GND] tokens interleaved at each evidence reference. Each [GND] token triggers prediction of a binary segmentation mask (not a bounding box) that precisely localizes the supporting region, which may be a text span, a chart wedge, a flowchart node, a table cell, or a combination of textual and graphical elements. A single QA pair can produce multiple masks across steps, and the mask set is empty when no supporting evidence exists.

## Method

DoCoG integrates a VLM backbone (Qwen3-VL-8B) with a promptable segmenter (SAM) through a novel Grounding Interaction Module (GIM), a 3-layer transformer encoder that alternates self-attention among [GND] token embeddings and cross-attention with the segmenter's visual features to produce refined grounding prompts. During decoding, each emitted [GND] token's final-layer hidden state is passed through GIM to generate a mask via SAM's prompt encoder and mask decoder, enabling multi-type grounding over both textual and graphical elements. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) supervised fine-tuning with a joint cross-entropy language modeling loss and Dice+BCE segmentation loss, and (2) Direct Preference Optimization on 20K ranked CoT outputs, where an anchor loss constrains [GND] hidden states to remain close to their SFT values to prevent mask-quality degradation from VLM parameter updates. The key architectural insight is that grounding tokens require both mutual token interaction (for cross-step consistency) and direct visual context incorporation (for spatial alignment), as ablations show removing either component substantially degrades grounding quality.

## Results

On the unified DoCoG-Bench (3K documents, 10K QA pairs across charts, flowcharts, tables, and documents), DoCoG achieves AQ=85.8, CQ=82.0, GQ_all=78.2, and GQ_fa=81.9, outperforming the best commercial model (Gemini 3 Pro: AQ=84.2, GQ_fa=11.4) and the best open-source model (Qwen3-VL: AQ=75.7, GQ_fa=5.5). On domain-specific benchmarks, DoCoG reaches 92.7 on ChartQA, 85.5 on FlowVQA, 78.4 on MISS-QA, 89.4 on TabFact, and 89.7 AQ on the documents split of DoCoG-Bench. The most striking gap is in grounding quality: DoCoG's GQ_fa of 81.9 exceeds Gemini 3 Pro (11.4) by ~70 points and Qwen3-VL (5.5) by ~76 points, demonstrating that explicit mask-based step-wise grounding is the key differentiator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoCoG (9B) outperforms all open-source and fine-tuned baselines across every benchmark and metric. Against the strongest open-source VLM (Qwen3-VL 8B), DoCoG gains +10.1 AQ on DoCoG-Bench (85.8 vs 75.7) and +76.4 GQ_fa (81.9 vs 5.5); on MISS-QA the gap is especially large (78.4 vs 46.2), reflecting the benefit of step-wise grounding over flowchart connectors. Against the best fine-tuned baseline (InternVL 3.5 FT), DoCoG still leads by +9.7 AQ and +17.3 GQ_fa on DoCoG-Bench, confirming that architectural contributions (GIM, mask supervision) add value beyond dataset alone. Against commercial models, DoCoG matches or exceeds Gemini 3 Pro on AQ (85.8 vs 84.2) and G_eval (85.2 vs 84.1) while dominating on grounding (GQ_fa 81.9 vs 11.4); however, GPT-5 retains an edge on FlowVQA (91.9 vs 85.5), AI2D (89.5 vs 88.3), WTQ (78.8 vs 64.4), and TabMWP (93.7 vs 80.7), suggesting that very large proprietary models still have advantages on certain structured-reasoning tasks. On chart benchmarks, DoCoG surpasses the chart-specialized Chart-R1 (92.7 vs 91.0 on ChartQA; 52.1 vs 44.0 on ChartQA-Pro) and all other chart models, while on table benchmarks it leads TabFact (89.4 vs 81.8 for Qwen3-VL) and WTQ (64.4 vs 57.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

DoCoG carves new ground at the intersection of three previously separate threads: promptable segmentation (SAM), step-wise Chain-of-Thought reasoning (VisualCoT, ChartPoint), and preference optimization (DPO). Prior grounded document QA systems (DOGR, BoundingDocs) rely on coarse bounding boxes and ground only the final answer, while ChartPoint extends step-wise grounding but restricts it to bounding boxes within the charts domain. The paper's key architectural departure is the Grounding Interaction Module (GIM), a transformer encoder that refines [GND] token embeddings through mutual self-attention and cross-attention with segmenter visual features before passing them to SAM's prompt encoder — a mechanism absent from prior VLM-grounding pipelines that typically feed token embeddings directly to a segmentation head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 325K-document, 1.5M-pair DoCoG-QA dataset and the human-verified DoCoG-Bench fill a significant gap: no prior resource provides pixel-level mask supervision for step-wise grounded CoT across heterogeneous document types (charts, tables, flowcharts, reports, infographics). The finding that commercial models (GPT-5, Gemini 3 Pro) achieve competitive answer accuracy but grounding scores below 15 on DoCoG-Bench suggests that answer correctness and evidence faithfulness are largely decoupled in current VLMs, raising open questions about whether scaling alone will close this gap or whether explicit grounding supervision is necessary. Limitations include dependence on SAM as a heavy segmenter, the synthetic nature of the data engine's QA generation (mitigated but not eliminated by multi-stage filtering), and the fact that the [GND] token mechanism requires vocabulary modification, limiting drop-in applicability to arbitrary VLMs.
