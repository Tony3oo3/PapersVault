## Problem

Existing lane topology reasoning methods follow a first-detect-then-associate paradigm that decouples centerline detection from topology prediction, causing geometric inconsistency at connected endpoints and structurally incomplete graphs under occlusion. The paper asks whether a model can be endowed with an intrinsic geometry prior over lane graph structures—similar to human intuition—so that predictions are globally coherent rather than a collection of independent instances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261723/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view surround-view images as input and produces a directed lane graph where each centerline is parameterized by four Bézier control points (quantized into a 64-bin vocabulary over a ±52 m × ±32 m BEV range) and topology edges are determined by a 0.5 m endpoint distance threshold. Unlike DETR-style baselines that emit hundreds of candidate lanes with confidence scores, TopoGPT directly generates all lanes in a scene as a single autoregressive token sequence, with a <SEP> token delimiting each lane group.

## Method

TopoGPT uses a two-stage training strategy built on an Autoregressive Lane Sequence Transformer (ALST), a decoder-only transformer with M-RoPE. In pre-training, a Bézier-based lane tokenizer serializes each centerline into four quantized control-point tokens, a scene context encoder rasterizes the full lane graph into a direction-field pseudo-image and extracts global scene tokens via a CNN, and the ALST learns next-token prediction conditioned on these scene tokens over 3.3 M map-only scenes. In fine-tuning, a ResNet-50 BEV encoder lifts multi-view features into a BEV feature map, and a flow-matching-based Perception Adapter (a lightweight DiT) transports BEV features into the pre-trained scene-token space; the ALST is then adapted via LoRA to condition on these BEV tokens. Condition degradation during pre-training (randomly removing lanes from the scene image) forces the model to internalize topology structure rather than merely copying the rasterized input.

## Results

On OpenLane-V2 subset A, TopoGPT achieves 45.6 M-P, 42.6 M-R, and 24.4 M-T (lane-level), and 54.4 G-IoU, 60.3 G-F1, 47.4 T-F1, 35.7 JT-F1, 28.7 APLS (point-level), outperforming the best prior method (TopoPoint) by an average of +6.4 on lane-level and +11.6 on point-level metrics. On subset B, TopoGPT similarly leads with 43.1 M-P, 39.2 M-R, 23.4 M-T, and 55.4 G-IoU, 58.9 G-F1, 46.3 T-F1, 36.8 JT-F1, 29.5 APLS. The largest single-metric gains are on M-R (+8.6 over TopoPoint on subset A) and APLS (+11.3), reflecting the geometry prior's ability to complete occluded lanes and infer correct path connectivity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoGPT outperforms all four DETR-style baselines (TopoNet, TopoMLP, TopoLogic, TopoPoint) across every metric on both subsets. Against the strongest baseline TopoPoint [10], TopoGPT gains +2.9 M-P, +8.6 M-R, and +7.8 M-T on subset A, with the recall gap indicating the geometry prior recovers lanes missed by the detector. On point-level metrics, the advantage widens: +13.8 G-IoU, +12.8 G-F1, +12.9 T-F1, +11.5 JT-F1, and +11.3 APLS over TopoPoint on subset A, showing substantially better geometric structure and connectivity inference. TopoLogic [9] and TopoMLP [49] trail TopoPoint on most metrics, with TopoLogic weakest on SDA (11.5 vs. 19.6) and APLS (10.4 vs. 17.4), while TopoMLP is closest to TopoPoint on G-F1 (47.5 vs. 47.5, tied) but lags on topology (M-T 13.9 vs. 16.6). TopoNet [24] is the weakest baseline overall, particularly on topology (M-T 10.1) and path similarity (APLS 9.8). On subset B, the same ordering holds with even larger absolute gaps, e.g., TopoGPT achieves 43.1 M-P vs. 35.4 for TopoPoint (+7.7) and 55.4 G-IoU vs. 31.9 (+23.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

TopoGPT departs from the dominant detect-then-associate paradigm (TopoNet, TopoMLP, TopoLogic, TopoPoint) by reframing lane topology reasoning as autoregressive sequence generation, building directly on the LLaMA-Gen decoder-only architecture. It also extends prior sequential graph generation methods (LaneGraph2Seq, SeqGrowGraph) that serialize lanes but train directly from perceptual inputs, by introducing a two-stage strategy: pre-training on 3.3M map-only scenes to internalize a geometry prior, then fine-tuning via a flow-matching adapter that transports BEV features into the pre-trained scene-token space. The Bézier-based lane tokenizer draws on Pix2seq's coordinate quantization, and the instance-merging pipeline follows SLEDGE's non-branching path aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a geometry prior learned purely from vectorized map data (no images) transfers effectively to sensor-conditioned lane graph prediction, yielding large gains (+6.4 lane-level, +11.6 point-level) over strong DETR-style baselines on OpenLane-V2. The dramatic ablation gap (M-P 7.4 from scratch vs. 45.6 with pre-training) underscores that the prior, not the architecture, drives performance. Open questions include whether the autoregressive latency is acceptable for real-time deployment, how the prior generalizes to road layouts absent from the pre-training corpus, and whether the flow-matching adapter can be replaced by simpler alignment to reduce inference cost.
