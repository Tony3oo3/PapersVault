## Problem

Existing text-to-motion diffusion models must choose between Transformer denoisers (quadratic in sequence length) and CNN denoisers (limited receptive fields), making it difficult to simultaneously achieve semantic alignment, long-horizon temporal coherence, and efficient generation. Simply inserting a vanilla Mamba/SSM layer into a motion diffusion pipeline underperforms because language-oriented SSMs lack task-specific mechanisms for multi-scale motion dynamics and explicit text-motion aggregation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261493/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural-language prompt (encoded to a text embedding of shape L×d via a language encoder) and produces a 3D human motion sequence of N frames parameterized by per-frame SMPL coefficients of dimension D (joint positions or 6-DoF pose). Inference runs 10 DPM-Solver++ steps with classifier-free guidance scale 3, yielding a complete motion clip in roughly 0.19 s per sentence on an A100 GPU.

## Method

SFM augments a UNet diffusion denoiser with two parallel state-space modules inside each Spatial-Frequency State Space Block (SFSSB). The Frequency State-Space Module (F-SSM) applies a DCT to project motion features into the frequency domain, then uses a text-gated cross-attention mechanism (motion feature as query, text embedding as key/value) to produce a soft frequency mask that adaptively emphasizes semantically relevant spectral components before inverse DCT. The Spatial State-Space Module (S-SSM) performs bidirectional selective state-space scanning (forward and backward) in the spatial domain to capture long-range spatial dependencies. The two branch outputs are summed and normalized, and a semantic attention layer (text as key/value, motion as query) further fuses linguistic context at each UNet level. Training follows standard diffusion with a linear β schedule over 1000 timesteps, classifier-free guidance via 10% unconditional dropout, and AdamW at lr 1e-4 for 200k steps.

## Results

On HumanML3D, SFM achieves the best R-Precision across all ranks (Top-1: 0.561, Top-2: 0.749, Top-3: 0.845) and the second-best FID (0.066), outperforming the strongest Transformer baseline MoMask (Top-3: 0.807, FID: 0.045) and the strongest CNN baseline StableMoFusion (Top-3: 0.779, FID: 0.189). On KIT-ML, SFM attains the best R-Precision (Top-3: 0.798) with a competitive FID of 0.228. On the long-sequence HumanML3D-LS benchmark (>190 frames), SFM leads all methods with Top-3 R-Precision of 0.801 and FID of 0.190, while running at 0.19 s per sentence—roughly 80× faster than the MDM Transformer baseline (15.33 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFM outperforms all 19 baselines on R-Precision (the primary metric) across both HumanML3D and KIT-ML. Against the strongest Transformer baseline MoMask, SFM improves Top-3 R-Precision from 0.807 to 0.845 and reduces MM-Dist from 2.958 to 2.758, though MoMask retains a slight FID edge (0.045 vs. 0.066). Versus the best CNN baseline StableMoFusion, SFM gains +0.066 in Top-3 R-Precision (0.845 vs. 0.779) and reduces FID from 0.189 to 0.066, at only a 0.06 s increase in inference time. Compared to the naive SSM baseline Motion Mamba, SFM achieves substantially better alignment (Top-3: 0.845 vs. 0.792) and fidelity (FID: 0.066 vs. 0.281), confirming that task-specific frequency and spatial state-space designs are critical. On the long-sequence HumanML3D-LS subset, SFM leads all methods by a wide margin (Top-3: 0.801 vs. 0.727 for MoMask, 0.713 for Motion Mamba, 0.665 for StableMoFusion), demonstrating superior long-range coherence. In human evaluation, SFM achieves 75.2% semantic accuracy and an 80.3% pairwise win rate over MoMask, MMM, and StableMoFusion combined.

## Relation to Prior Work

SFM builds directly on Mamba's selective state-space scan mechanism but departs from both dominant text-to-motion paradigms: Transformer denoisers (quadratic in sequence length) and CNN UNets (limited receptive fields). Rather than adopting a vanilla SSM as in Motion Mamba, the paper introduces two task-specific modules—F-SSM (DCT-based frequency decomposition with text-gated adaptive masking) and S-SSM (bidirectional spatial state transitions)—embedded within a UNet diffusion backbone. This positions SFM as a hybrid architecture that retains the UNet's inductive structure while replacing local convolutional mixing with linear-time state-space operations along two complementary axes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that task-aware SSM design (frequency-domain text alignment + bidirectional spatial modeling) can close the gap between Transformer fidelity and CNN efficiency in text-to-motion, achieving the best R-Precision on both HumanML3D and KIT-ML with sub-0.2s inference. The frequency-preference analysis (low-frequency dominance in early denoising steps, high-frequency refinement later) provides a mechanistic explanation for why the DCT-based decomposition works, and the text-gated mask offers a reusable pattern for conditioning SSMs on external signals. Open questions include whether the approach extends to higher-dimensional motion representations (e.g., full-body with object interaction), whether the UNet backbone is truly necessary versus a pure SSM stack, and how the method scales to sequences well beyond 200 frames where even linear-time SSMs face memory pressure from the state dimension.
