## Problem

Reconstructing high-quality geometric surfaces from sparse views using 3D Gaussian Splatting remains challenging due to limited geometric cues and the inherent discreteness of Gaussian primitives, which leads to ambiguous depth and defective surface extraction. While recent 3DGS-based sparse-view methods achieve novel view synthesis, they still produce incomplete or discontinuous surfaces, and existing approaches that leverage normal priors only apply them as regularization without exploiting their potential for depth supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260958/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3–20 sparse RGB images with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics (3 small-overlapping views on DTU, 5/10/20 views on Tanks-and-Temples, 3 views on self-captured scenes) and produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation from which a surface mesh is extracted. A pretrained monocular normal estimator (Metric3Dv2) provides per-pixel normal priors, and an optional MASt3R point cloud can be used for Gaussian initialization. Training runs for 10K iterations on a single RTX 3090 in approximately 10 minutes with 3 GB GPU memory.

## Method

The core contribution is a normal-guided depth propagation constraint that extends reliable depth supervision from high-confidence regions to under-constrained areas. Depth confidence is jointly estimated from multi-view geometric consistency (reprojection error thresholded at 1 pixel) and patch-based photometric consistency (NCC), and high-confidence depths are iteratively propagated to neighbors under a local planar assumption using the normal prior, progressively expanding the supervision mask over 10 iterations. To handle residual depth discontinuities in regions far from high-confidence anchors, an abnormal depth edge-aware smoothing loss detects depth edges that lack corresponding normal edges and enforces local planarity by penalizing the dot product between the surface normal and the vector connecting back-projected neighboring points. The total loss combines RGB rendering error, the depth propagation loss, the edge smoothing loss, and a standard normal prior L1 loss, all optimized within a 3DGS framework using the PGSR planar renderer and an opacity decay strategy.

## Results

On the DTU dataset with 3 small-overlapping views, the proposed method achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 1.02 (without MASt3R initialization) and 0.85 (with MASt3R initialization), outperforming all 14 baselines. The closest competitor, SparseRecon, achieves a mean CD of 1.06, while the best generalizable method UFORecon reaches 1.40 and the best dense-view 3DGS method PGSR reaches 2.38. On the Tanks-and-Temples dataset, the method achieves F1 scores of 0.081/0.173/0.293 for 5/10/20 views, surpassing MAtCha (0.072/0.156/0.218) and FatesGS (0.014/0.025/0.033). Training requires only 10 minutes and 3 GB GPU memory, matching the efficiency of other 3DGS-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 14 baselines on the DTU dataset. Against the closest scene-specific competitor SparseRecon (CD 1.06), the method achieves a 3.8% improvement (1.02), and with MASt3R initialization the gap widens to 0.85 vs. MAtCha#'s 1.16 (26.7% improvement). Among 3DGS-based sparse-view methods, the method beats MAtCha (1.48→1.02, 31% improvement) and FatesGS (1.37→1.02, 25.5% improvement). Generalizable methods such as UFORecon (1.40) and ReTR (2.63) show substantially larger gaps, confirming that scene-specific optimization with the proposed depth propagation is more effective than learned priors for this task. On the 360-degree TNT dataset, the method consistently outperforms MAtCha across all view counts (e.g., 0.293 vs. 0.218 F1 at 20 views, a 34% relative gain) while FatesGS fails to produce meaningful reconstructions in most scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3DGS framework (Kerbl et al.) with PGSR's planar-based renderer and an opacity decay strategy, positioning itself as a scene-specific optimization method for sparse-view surface reconstruction. It departs from prior 3DGS-based sparse-view methods (FatesGS, MAtCha, Sparse2DGS) that rely on direct multi-view consistency losses or monocular depth priors, instead using geometric and photometric consistency to compute a depth confidence map and then leveraging normal priors to actively propagate high-confidence depth into low-confidence regions. This goes beyond the common practice of applying normal priors purely as a regularization term (as in SolidGS, GausSurf, MonoSDF) by using normals as a geometric guide for depth extension along local planes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves state-of-the-art Chamfer Distance on DTU (1.02 mean) and F1 on Tanks-and-Temples with training time and memory comparable to other 3DGS-based approaches (~10 min, 3 GB), making it practically attractive for sparse-view geometry recovery. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that depth confidence estimation degrades in texture-less regions, where multi-view consistency cues become unreliable and erroneous depths can propagate, causing local distortions. Open questions include whether the local planar assumption underlying depth propagation holds for highly curved or non-planar surfaces, and how the method scales to larger, more complex 360-degree scenes beyond the TNT benchmark.
