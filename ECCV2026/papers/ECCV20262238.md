## Problem

Unified multimodal models treat photographic composition as a passive aesthetic attribute rather than an explicit, controllable structure, making them unreliable at fine-grained composition recognition and unable to convert composition intent into layout-controllable generation. No prior framework unifies expert-level composition perception with reference-guided compositional generation in a single system, and the scarcity of systematic composition-annotated data and the entanglement of layout with content in reference images make this dual capability particularly challenging.

## Input / Output

For perception, the model takes an RGB image plus a natural-language instruction and outputs both a free-form instruction-following response and an 11-class multi-label composition vector (Rule of Thirds, Center, Horizontal, Symmetric, Diagonal, Curved, Vertical, Triangle, Pattern, Leading Lines, Fill the Frame). For generation, the reference image is physically decoupled via grayscale conversion and aggressive pixelization before entering the model, and the text prompt specifies new semantic content; the output is a generated image that inherits the reference's layout while remaining faithful to the prompt. A single learnable expert token τ_c is shared across both tasks as the intent anchor, with its hidden representation directly supervised for composition classification and injected into the diffusion denoiser as a global conditioning signal.

## Method

COMPASS builds on the Ming-Lite-Uni MoE backbone and introduces a Composition-specific Mixture-of-Experts (C-MoE) branch: a parallel MoE module with its own router and expert MLPs is added to each sparse FFN layer, activated by a task flag, so that only the new composition experts are trained while the frozen backbone preserves general multimodal competence. A single learnable expert token τ_c is prepended to the assistant response and supervised with a weighted multi-label binary cross-entropy loss, concentrating compositional cues into a dedicated representation. For reference-guided generation, the paper employs a self-supervised structural bottleneck (grayscale pixelization of the reference) to suppress semantic appearance while preserving coarse layout, combined with a customized causal attention mask that forbids all post-image text and learnable-query tokens from attending to reference-image tokens (except τ_c), and a cross-attention refinement module that re-anchors learnable queries to the prompt representation for text faithfulness. The τ_c hidden state is projected and injected into the diffusion transformer via AdaLN-style timestep modulation, steering the denoising trajectory toward the intended composition. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage I trains C-MoE and τ_c on perception data with next-token prediction plus classification loss, then Stage II freezes the C-MoE experts and trains generation-specific parameters while re-training τ_c under the perturbed-reference domain shift.

## Results

On the Comp-11 perception test split, COMPASS achieves a mean AP of 90.6%, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline Qwen3-VL (66.4%) by 24.2 points and the unified-model baseline BAGEL (63.2%) by 27.4 points, with consistent gains across all 11 composition categories. On composition-guided generation, COMPASS attains the best CLIPScore (0.83), composition consistency (0.74), and expert-token cosine similarity (0.62) among all compared methods, while its FID of 8.2 is slightly higher than Step1X-Edit (7.5) and BAGEL (7.8). The ablation confirms that removing C-MoE drops mAP to 78.2 and removing the expert token τ_c drops it further to 71.1, validating both architectural components.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On composition perception, COMPASS dominates all seven baselines by a wide margin: it outperforms the strongest understanding-only model Qwen3-VL (66.4% mAP) by 24.2 points, the aesthetic specialist AesExpert (49.5%) by 41.1 points, and the unified models Janus-Pro (54.2%), BAGEL (63.2%), and its own backbone Ming-Lite-Uni (58.2%) by 36.4, 27.4, and 32.4 points respectively. On composition-guided generation, COMPASS achieves the highest CLIPScore (0.83 vs. 0.72 for Step1X-Edit, 0.76 for BAGEL, 0.64 for Ming-Lite-Uni) and the best composition-consistency score (0.74 vs. 0.60, 0.59, 0.53), confirming superior layout transfer and text faithfulness. However, COMPASS's FID of 8.2 is slightly worse than Step1X-Edit (7.5) and BAGEL (7.8), indicating a modest trade-off in overall image quality for the gain in composition control. The expert-token cosine similarity (0.62 for COMPASS vs. 0.51, 0.48, 0.44 for baselines) further validates that the shared τ_c representation carries meaningful compositional structure across the perception-to-generation pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Ming-Lite-Uni's MoE-based unified multimodal backbone, extending it with a parallel composition-specific MoE branch (C-MoE) and a learnable expert token that serves as a shared intent anchor across perception and generation. It departs from aesthetic LMMs (AesExpert, UniPercept, UNIAA) that treat composition as a passive descriptive attribute within holistic scoring, and from prior composition literature (KU-PCP, CADB) that focuses on classification or post-hoc cropping, by elevating composition to an explicit, controllable structural signal. On the generation side, it extends reference-guided image editing (IP-Adapter, style-transfer methods) by targeting the harder problem of transferring high-level layout intent while suppressing semantic content leakage, using a self-supervised structural bottleneck (grayscale pixelization) rather than requiring paired same-layout/different-content training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Comp-11 dataset (389K images, 1.2M VQA pairs, 11-class taxonomy) fills a notable gap in systematic, instruction-following composition supervision that prior aesthetic datasets lacked, and could serve as a reusable benchmark for the broader LMM community. The structural bottlenecking strategy—using pixelization as a self-supervised proxy for 'same layout, different appearance'—is a lightweight, generalizable trick that avoids the infeasible requirement of paired data, and may transfer to other structural-factor transfer problems. Open questions include whether the 11-class taxonomy captures the full diversity of professional composition, whether the expert-token mechanism scales to more complex or multi-level layout specifications, and whether the small FID cost relative to baselines (8.2 vs. 7.5) persists at higher resolution or with more denoising steps.
