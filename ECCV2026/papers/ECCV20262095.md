## Problem

In low-parallax and near-rotational regimes, the Schur-reduced camera system in classical Levenberg-Marquardt bundle adjustment becomes ill-conditioned, causing the LM step to move along poorly supported directions that yield small reprojection error but inaccurate relative poses and unstable focal calibration. Existing stability-oriented methods (square-root BA, variable projection, PoBA) improve conditioning or efficiency through alternative factorizations or reparameterizations but do not directly constrain the LM update direction itself under weak geometric conditioning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the standard BA state: camera poses with intrinsics, 3D point coordinates, and image observations (reprojection residuals with their Jacobians). It produces refined camera and point parameters identical in form to any Schur-LM solver. The key structural detail is that the method operates entirely on the Schur-reduced camera increment vector: point variables are eliminated via the standard Schur complement, and the subspace restriction applies only to the camera-increment direction, so the output increment is a full-sized camera vector whose admissible direction is confined to a low-dimensional Ritz basis at each iteration.

## Method

Gate-Guided CSS-BA is a solver-side modification of Schur-LM that restricts each LM step to a geometrically informed low-dimensional search subspace without altering the objective, residuals, Jacobians, or variable set. A geometry-aware gate (parallax magnitude, rotational agreement, view connectivity) precomputes a reliable camera support set S; at each iteration, CSS scores blocks in S by their local Schur predicted decrease and selects a top-k subset K_t. A block projector localizes the damped Schur operator onto K_t, and m-step Lanczos on this localized operator yields Ritz vectors forming a compact basis V_t (optionally augmented with one complement direction outside K_t). The projected LM system (V_t^T H̃ V_t) y = −V_t^T g̃ is solved in this r_t-dimensional subspace, and the increment Δc = V_t y is lifted back to full camera space before standard trust-region acceptance. This concentrates updates on well-conditioned curvature directions, improving trust-region acceptance and relative-pose accuracy in weak geometry while remaining a drop-in replacement for existing Schur-BA pipelines.

## Results

On PhoneSweep (near-spherical, low-parallax motion), CSS-BA achieves AUC@30 of 79.97 (iPhone13Mini) and 83.78 (Nexus5X), compared to 13.34/12.06 for Normal-LM and 42.62/33.01 for PoBA. On Nexus5X, CSS-BA reduces Absolute Focal Error (AFE) from 265.35 (Normal-LM) and 121.18 (PoBA) to 0.89, approaching the SphericalSfM+BA reference (0.97) without shared intrinsics. On BAL (well-conditioned large-scale reconstructions), CSS-BA achieves objective improvements comparable to Normal-LM and PoBA across all three families (ladybug, trafalgar, dubrovnik), confirming no severe degradation in standard settings. Runtime overhead is approximately 2.0× Normal-LM and 6.8× PoBA on the weak-geometry timing subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Normal-LM, CSS-BA wins decisively on PhoneSweep: AUC@30 improves from 13.34 to 79.97 (iPhone13Mini) and from 12.06 to 83.78 (Nexus5X), and AFE drops from 265.35 to 0.89 on Nexus5X. Against PoBA, CSS-BA also wins on PhoneSweep AUC@30 (79.97 vs 42.62; 83.78 vs 33.01) and AFE on Nexus5X (0.89 vs 121.18), though on iPhone13Mini AFE Normal-LM (115.56) is actually lower than CSS-BA (158.01). On BAL, CSS-BA is not consistently best: it slightly underperforms Normal-LM on ladybug (mean r 0.0172 vs 0.01556) and dubrovnik (0.0053 vs 0.00484), while marginally beating both on trafalgar (0.0046 vs 0.00484/0.004707), confirming the subspace restriction does not harm well-conditioned problems. Compared to the SphericalSfM+BA reference (which assumes shared intrinsics), CSS-BA approaches but does not match its AUC@30 (79.97 vs 91.45 on iPhone; 83.78 vs 90.43 on Nexus5X) and AFE (158.01 vs 0.25 on iPhone; 0.89 vs 0.97 on Nexus5X), yet does so without any shared-intrinsic assumption or explicit motion prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Column Space Search (CSS), originally developed for inverse problems and neural network training (Hyde et al., 2021), to the Schur-reduced bundle adjustment system, using it to construct a compact Krylov/Ritz basis that restricts each Levenberg-Marquardt step to a geometrically informed subspace. It deliberately departs from both efficiency-oriented BA work (PoBA, matrix-free solvers, distributed methods) and stability-oriented work (Square Root BA, variable projection, expOSE), which modify factorizations or eliminate variables, by instead preserving the full objective, variable set, and trust-region mechanism while constraining only the update direction. Unlike keyframe-selection or state-selection approaches (iSAM2, double-window, relative BA) that alter the active variable set, CSS-BA keeps all cameras in the problem and uses the selected blocks solely as a basis-construction support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a well-known practical failure mode—Schur-LM producing geometrically unreliable poses under low-parallax or near-rotational motion—without requiring shared intrinsics, explicit motion priors, or changes to the BA formulation, making it a drop-in replacement for existing pipelines. The 2× runtime overhead relative to Normal-LM (and 6.8× relative to PoBA) limits its use to settings where pose stability is the primary concern, and the gate ablation reveals that the geometric gate contributes little on easier sequences, suggesting the CSS scoring and Ritz basis carry most of the benefit. Open questions include adaptive subspace sizing, whether the approach extends to rolling-shutter or calibrated BA variants, and whether the fixed top-k budget can be made data-driven without per-scene tuning.
