## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs for audiovisual video captioning suffer from two fundamental failures: cross-modal binding errors (misattributing sounds to the wrong visual source, e.g., speech to the wrong speaker) and temporal incoherence (failing to preserve chronological and causal ordering between audio and visual events). These deficiencies manifest as 'modality detachment,' where models narrate visual and auditory content in decoupled sequential passes rather than as a synchronized, interleaved narrative.

## Input / Output

The model takes an audiovisual video clip (typically 5–30 seconds) as input and produces a high-density, temporally-anchored caption with sub-second timestamps (e.g., [01.2s–01.5s]), explicit entity-specific sound attribution, and structured coverage of audio (speech, foley, ambient noise, music), visual (subjects, actions, scene, cinematography), and aesthetic dimensions. The TCA-Bench evaluation further structures ground truth into three layers: dense captions, Audio-Visual Binding Lists (JSON [sound, source] pairs), and Temporal Relational Lists ([first_event, second_event, relation] triples encoding A→V, V→A, or AV_simultaneous orderings).

## Method

TCA-Captioner is built by LoRA fine-tuning the Qwen3-Omni 30B-A3B backbone on a curated High-Density Human Interaction dataset (3,500 clips) supplemented by FineVideo and TikTok-10M samples, all annotated via the Observer-Checker-Corrector (OCC) pipeline. The OCC framework is an iterative data-synthesis loop: a Global-Perceptive Observer (Gemini-3-Pro) generates an initial high-density description; a Tripartite Forensic Checker independently audits it—Audio Forensic (Gemini-3-Pro) verifies speech fidelity and acoustic attributes, Visual Forensic (Doubao-1.8) validates entity dynamics and spatio-temporal logic on silent video, and an A/V Clip Checker (Gemini-3-Flash) processes 2-second sliding windows to detect lip-sync discrepancies and cross-modal misalignments; finally, an Iterative Refinement Corrector (Gemini-3-Pro) fuses all audit reports to produce the final caption. For evaluation, TCA-Bench introduces a Decoupled Evaluation Protocol that separates Base Perception (uni-modal 0–10 LLM-judge scoring) from Audiovisual Integration (structured checklist verification of binding accuracy and temporal F₁ using a three-way correct/incorrect/skipped verdict), enabling granular diagnosis of where models fail.

## Results

On the proposed TCA-Bench, TCA-Captioner achieves 61.2 (Audio), 63.6 (Visual), 73.2 (AV Binding), and 76.9 (AV Temporal), outperforming all open-source baselines across every dimension. On public benchmarks, TCA-Captioner attains a video-SALMONN-2 Total error of 31.5 (lower is better) and a UGC-VideoCap average of 74.2, surpassing the strongest open-source competitor AVoCaDO (37.3 and 73.2 respectively). Compared to its Qwen3-Omni backbone, TCA-Captioner nearly doubles AV Binding (42.5→73.2) and AV Temporal (47.9→76.9) scores, demonstrating the impact of the OCC data pipeline and HDI dataset. Against the top closed-source model Gemini-3.0-Pro, TCA-Captioner trails on Audio (61.2 vs 70.2) and AV Temporal (76.9 vs 80.0) but leads on Visual (63.6 vs 60.9) and AV Binding (73.2 vs 76.8 is close).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCA-Captioner outperforms all open-source baselines on TCA-Bench: it leads AVoCaDO (the strongest open-source competitor) by +6.0 on AV Binding (73.2 vs 67.2) and +8.6 on AV Temporal (76.9 vs 68.3), while also edging ahead on Audio (+0.4) and Visual (+0.8). Against its own backbone Qwen3-Omni, the gains are dramatic—AV Binding nearly doubles (42.5→73.2) and AV Temporal improves by 29 points (47.9→76.9)—validating the OCC data synthesis and HDI dataset. On public benchmarks (Table 3), TCA-Captioner achieves the lowest video-SALMONN-2 Total error (31.5) among all open-source models, beating AVoCaDO (37.3) and video-SALMONN-2 (38.8), and the highest UGC-VideoCap average (74.2) among open-source models, surpassing AVoCaDO (73.2). Against closed-source Gemini-3.0-Pro, TCA-Captioner trails on Audio (61.2 vs 70.2) and AV Temporal (76.9 vs 80.0) but surpasses it on Visual (63.6 vs 60.9), attributed to the dedicated Visual Checker using Doubao-1.8. TCA-Captioner also beats Gemini-3.0-Flash across all four dimensions, confirming competitive parity with mid-tier closed-source systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Qwen3-Omni as its architectural backbone, applying LoRA fine-tuning to specialize it for audiovisual captioning. Conceptually, it departs from prior audiovisual captioners (Video-SALMONN-2, AVoCaDO, UGC-VideoCaptioner) by reframing the core challenge as one of cross-modal binding and temporal coherence rather than holistic detail recall, and by introducing an iterative Observer-Checker-Corrector data synthesis pipeline that replaces single-pass LLM annotation. The Decoupled Evaluation Protocol also breaks from prior benchmarks (which use event matching or holistic LLM scoring) by structurally separating unimodal perception from cross-modal relational reasoning, a distinction none of the compared benchmarks make.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Decoupled Evaluation Protocol and structured annotations (Binding Lists, Temporal Relational Lists) offer a diagnostic framework that could be adopted by the broader MLLM community to pinpoint where models fail in multimodal reasoning, rather than receiving a single opaque score. However, several limitations temper the impact: the benchmark is small (459 clips), the evaluation relies on LLM judges that may share blind spots with the models being evaluated, and the OCC pipeline's ~22K-token cost per 10-second video raises scalability questions for large-scale data generation. An open question is whether the gains observed on high-density human interaction content transfer to other audiovisual domains (e.g., nature documentaries, music videos) where the binding and temporal structures differ substantially.
