## Problem

ERM-trained classifiers rely on spurious correlations, causing severe performance drops on minority-group samples, yet prior work shows the frozen backbone still encodes informative features and the failure largely resides in the linear head. Existing remedies either require explicit group labels, hand-crafted augmentation rules, or simply retrain the head on a balanced validation subset (e.g., DFR), leaving the classifier's evolving failure cases unaddressed. The paper asks whether one can directly target the classifier's current hard examples through a lightweight, label-free optimization that moves beyond the support of the original training set.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images only to extract frozen backbone features (ResNet-50, ViT-B/16, ConvNeXt, or CLIP ViT-H); all subsequent optimization operates on d-dimensional feature vectors, never on pixels. The learnable variables are a small set of support embeddings initialized from training-set features, and the primary output is a class label from the adapted linear head. A secondary output is the optimized embedding itself, which can be decoded through Stable Diffusion unCLIP to produce interpretable visualizations of the model's bias directions.

## Method

HSFM formulates a bilevel meta-learning objective in the frozen backbone's feature space. In the inner loop, a linear head (W, b) is adapted for T gradient steps on a class-balanced support set of learnable embeddings H; in the outer loop, H is updated via meta-gradients to minimize the cross-entropy loss on a hard set Q, defined as the top-K_highest-loss validation examples per class under the current classifier. This alternating procedure is repeated for a fixed number of epochs, with Q refreshed each epoch to track the classifier's evolving failure modes. Because optimization occurs at the backbone output rather than end-to-end, training requires only a few minutes on a single GPU. The key distinction from DFR is that supervision is not a balanced validation subset but the explicit loss on identified hard examples, which drives feature-space edits that move support embeddings toward minority-group configurations.

## Results

On the four spurious-correlation benchmarks (ResNet-50 backbone), HSFM achieves the best worst-group accuracy on Waterbirds (93.1%) and Dominoes (90.4%), and is competitive on CelebA (89.2%) and MetaShift (77.2%), all without using group labels during training. On fine-grained classification, HSFM-Pretrained (frozen ImageNet ResNet-50, no dataset fine-tuning) reaches 85.10% top-1 on Stanford-Cars, surpassing the fully fine-tuned ERM baseline (83.98%), and HSFM-ERM achieves 97.27% on Oxford Flowers. The method trains in only a few minutes on a single GPU, matching the efficiency of DFR while delivering consistent worst-group gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HSFM outperforms DFR (its primary baseline) on all four spurious-correlation benchmarks: Waterbirds 93.1 vs 92.3, CelebA 89.2 vs 88.3, MetaShift 77.2 vs 72.8, and Dominoes 90.4 vs 90.0 worst-group accuracy. Against data-augmentation methods, HSFM beats DDB on Waterbirds (93.1 vs 93.0), CelebA (89.2 vs 85.8), and Dominoes (90.4 vs N/A), but trails DDB on MetaShift (77.2 vs 81.2) and DaC on MetaShift (77.2 vs 78.3), which the authors attribute to MetaShift's very small validation set (81 samples). HSFM also surpasses GroupDRO (93.1 vs 91.4 on Waterbirds; 77.2 vs 66.0 on MetaShift) and LISA (93.1 vs 89.2 on Waterbirds; 77.2 vs 59.8 on MetaShift) by wide margins, while matching LISA on CelebA (89.2 vs 89.3). In the controlled CelebA ablation (Table 5), HSFM dominates the No-bilevel baseline (which trains the head directly on the hard set) at every validation size—e.g., 89.0 vs 79.4 at full validation—and also outperforms AFR (89.0 vs 61.7) and DFR without balancing (89.0 vs 41.1). On fine-grained tasks, HSFM-Pretrained beats ERM on Stanford-Cars (85.10 vs 83.98) and Oxford Flowers (97.10 vs 91.07), while DFR-Pretrained collapses (32.75 on Stanford-Cars), confirming that feature-space meta-optimization is essential beyond simple head re-fitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

HSFM builds directly on DFR's observation that an ERM-trained backbone retains informative representations and that the linear head is the primary source of spurious-correlation failure. It departs from DFR by replacing simple balanced-subset retraining with a bilevel meta-learning objective that explicitly targets the classifier's current hard examples, and from MAML by repurposing the inner/outer loop structure for robustness on a single model's failure cases rather than cross-task adaptation. The method also contrasts with data-augmentation approaches (DaC, DDB, MaskTune) by deriving its augmentation signal from the model's own loss landscape in frozen feature space, avoiding hand-crafted rules or external generative priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency (minutes on a single GPU, no group labels, no generative model) makes HSFM an accessible post-hoc robustness tool that can be applied to any pretrained backbone, and its extension to fine-grained classification and CLIP-based visualization broadens its utility beyond the standard spurious-correlation benchmarks. A key limitation is the reliance on the frozen backbone already encoding sufficient core features; when the backbone itself is weak or misaligned, the method's advantage over end-to-end fine-tuning diminishes. Open questions include whether the bilevel structure remains beneficial when the support set must be much larger (e.g., ImageNet-scale) and whether the learned feature edits transfer across tasks or are strictly model-specific.
