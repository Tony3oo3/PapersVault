## Problem

Extending diffusion-based dense prediction to a unified multi-task model typically requires parameter-heavy adapters, Mixture-of-Experts modules, or learnable task tokens, introducing computational redundancy and gradient conflicts that cause negative transfer. The paper asks whether the pre-trained diffusion infrastructure already contains a latent, parameter-free mechanism capable of decoupling multiple tasks without any architectural modification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261607/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

A single RGB image is encoded by a frozen VAE into a latent vector, which is then fed (optionally concatenated with Gaussian noise in the generative variant) into a shared U-Net or DiT alongside a scalar timestep value. At inference, the same image is stacked three times with three distinct timesteps to simultaneously produce a depth map, a surface normal map, and an RGB reconstruction in a single forward pass; each output latent is decoded by the VAE. The deterministic variant (MUSE-d) removes the noise input entirely, so the U-Net receives only the image latent and the task timestep embedding.

## Method

MUSE repurposes the native sinusoidal timestep embedding of a one-step diffusion model as a parameter-free categorical task switch: each task (RGB reconstruction, depth, normal) is bound to a fixed, distinct integer timestep (e.g., t_rgb=991, t_depth=995, t_normal=999), and the single fully parameter-shared U-Net is fine-tuned with a simple per-sample MSE loss where the timestep conditions which task manifold the output should lie on. During training, a steering policy randomly assigns each sample in a mini-batch to one of the three tasks, feeding the corresponding timestep so the model learns to associate each embedding with a distinct geometric projection. The authors formalize this as Manifold Decoupling: orthogonal timestep keys guide the network to project the image latent onto separate, non-interfering task manifolds in the VAE latent space, eliminating gradient conflicts without any extra parameters, task tokens, or loss-balancing strategies. The mechanism is validated to generalize from the SDv2 U-Net backbone to an SD3 MMDiT (DiT) backbone under Conditional Flow Matching, confirming it is a model-agnostic property of the timestep conditioning channel rather than an architectural artifact.

## Results

MUSE-d (deterministic, one-step) achieves AbsRel 5.1 / δ₁ 97.1 on NYUv2 depth, 5.8 / 96.2 on ScanNet, and 9.2 / 90.8 on KITTI, while simultaneously producing surface normals with Mean 16.6° / 58.6% on NYUv2 and 15.4° / 61.3% on ScanNet — all from a single 0.95B-parameter model trained on only 59K synthetic samples. The generative variant MUSE-g is slightly weaker (e.g., AbsRel 5.8 on NYUv2 depth) but still competitive. Across 10 benchmarks, MUSE-d matches or exceeds other multi-task diffusion methods (Orchid, Diception, GeoWizard) on most metrics while remaining behind the strongest single-task specialists (Lotus-2, FE2E, Metric3Dv2) by 1–3 AbsRel points on depth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among multi-task diffusion methods, MUSE-d outperforms Diception on nearly all reported metrics (e.g., NYUv2 depth AbsRel 5.1 vs. 7.2; ScanNet normal Mean 15.4° vs. 19.4°) and beats GeoWizard on depth (NYUv2 5.1 vs. 5.2; KITTI 9.2 vs. 9.7) while being competitive on normals. Against Orchid, MUSE-d is slightly behind on depth (NYUv2 5.1 vs. 5.7 — actually MUSE wins here) but trails on normal estimation (iBims-1 16.7° vs. 16.3°; Sintel 35.2° vs. 31.7°). Compared to the single-task specialists Lotus-2 and FE2E, MUSE-d lags by roughly 1–3 AbsRel on depth (e.g., NYUv2 5.1 vs. 4.1 for Lotus-2) and 1–2° on normal Mean error, which is expected given it handles both tasks with one shared 0.95B model. MUSE-d clearly surpasses the earlier multi-step Marigold baseline on every depth metric (e.g., DIODE AbsRel 23.8 vs. 31.0) and is on par with Lotus-d (its closest single-task predecessor) while adding normal estimation capability. The DiT-based variant (MUSE-d-DiT) confirms the mechanism generalizes to flow matching, though with ~3 AbsRel degradation due to FP16/8-bit training constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

MUSE builds directly on the one-step deterministic diffusion paradigm established by Lotus and DMP, which showed that iterative denoising is unnecessary for discriminative dense prediction tasks. It departs from the prevailing multi-task diffusion approaches (Orchid, Diception, UniDense, TaskDiffusion) that introduce parameter-heavy adapters, Mixture-of-Experts, or learnable task tokens, by instead repurposing the native sinusoidal timestep embedding—originally a noise-level indicator—as a parameter-free categorical task switch. The theoretical grounding draws on the manifold learning literature (Stanczuk et al., He et al.), reframing timestep conditioning as steering the model's output onto task-specific low-dimensional manifolds in the VAE latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free, single-model multi-tasking protocol offers a practically attractive path toward unified perception models without the computational overhead of expert routing or adapter stacking, and its generalization to both U-Net and DiT/flow-matching backbones suggests the mechanism is architectural-agnostic. However, the paper acknowledges a clear boundary: the approach degrades on discrete semantic tasks (e.g., 41-class segmentation) due to the continuous VAE's inability to encode non-smooth class ID maps, leaving the extension to full semantic decomposition open. The empirical selection of timestep values is also ad hoc, and automated discovery of optimal orthogonal task keys within the time-embedding space remains an unexplored direction.
