## Problem

Existing video diffusion models can generate either long single-view videos via temporal autoregression or short multi-view clips via bidirectional attention, but no method produces videos that are simultaneously unbounded in temporal length and geometrically consistent across an arbitrary number of viewpoints. Bidirectional attention over the full time-view grid scales quadratically and prevents streaming, while naively chaining temporal autoregression across views causes rapid geometric drift without an explicit 3D anchor.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262122/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and N camera sequences (6-DoF extrinsic parameters per frame) as input and produces N temporally coherent videos, one per viewpoint, at user-specified temporal length. Generation is fully autoregressive: each new view is conditioned on the single preceding view's clean latent plus a geometric prior rendered from CUT3R's accumulated 3D state, and each new temporal block is conditioned on the KV cache of prior blocks. The distilled student operates as a few-step generator (Q denoising steps per block), enabling streaming inference at arbitrary temporal and view counts.

## Method

MV-Forcing composes temporal and view-wise autoregression within a single causal diffusion transformer by introducing CUT3R, a recurrent 3D reconstruction model, as a persistent geometric bridge between sequentially generated views. As each view is generated, its decoded frames are incrementally integrated into CUT3R's latent state, which is then queried from the next target camera to produce a geometric rendering and confidence map; this rendering is encoded and injected into the diffusion model via a zero-initialized Conv3d residual, grounding each new viewpoint in the accumulated 4D scene structure. To close the train-inference exposure bias along both axes, the authors introduce Spatio-Temporal Self-Forcing: during DMD training, the student unrolls autoregressive generation along both the temporal and view dimensions, conditioning on its own imperfect outputs rather than ground truth. A joint denoising regime (both view slots initialized from noise with probability p) unifies first-view text-to-video generation and view-sequential conditioning in a single architecture, and the entire pipeline is distilled from the bidirectional SynCamMaster teacher via Distribution Matching Distillation into a causal few-step student.

## Results

In the long-sequence setting (3 views, 162 frames), MV-Forcing outperforms all baselines across every metric in both real-world and synthetic evaluations. On real-world data, it achieves FID 153.27, FVD 1309.68, RotErr 3.88°, and 239.37K matching pixels, compared to 156.78/1363.23/4.89°/146.81K for SF+ReCamMaster+SF and 157.57/1397.42/4.74°/127.48K for SF+ReCamMaster. In the short-sequence setting (2 views, 81 frames), MV-Forcing matches the bidirectional SynCamMaster teacher on visual quality (FID 167.90 vs 166.57) while surpassing it on camera accuracy (RotErr 3.64° vs 3.83°) and view synchronization (Mat.Pix 251.13K vs 236.72K, CLIP-V 91.81 vs 90.13).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the bidirectional SynCamMaster teacher (short setting, 2 views × 81 frames), MV-Forcing wins on all camera accuracy metrics (RotErr 3.64° vs 3.83°, TransErr 8.26 vs 8.83) and view synchronization metrics (Mat.Pix 251.13K vs 236.72K, CLIP-V 91.81 vs 90.13), while slightly trailing on FID (167.90 vs 166.57) and FVD (1468.84 vs 1451.44), reflecting the expected cost of distilling a many-step bidirectional model into a few-step causal student. In the long-sequence setting (3 views × 162 frames), MV-Forcing dominates both composed baselines on every metric in both real-world and synthetic evaluations. The gap is most pronounced in view synchronization: Mat.Pix reaches 239.37K for MV-Forcing versus only 146.81K (SF+ReCamMaster+SF) and 127.48K (SF+ReCamMaster) on real data, a 63–87% improvement, because the baselines generate views independently without shared geometric context. SF+ReCamMaster degrades beyond its 81-frame training window due to independent per-segment generation, while SF+ReCamMaster+SF recovers temporal continuity but still lacks cross-view conditioning, causing visible geometric drift between viewpoints. The consistent performance gap across both real-world and synthetic settings indicates the advantage is architectural (persistent 4D geometric prior + view-sequential autoregression) rather than driven by domain alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper composes two previously orthogonal lines of work—temporal autoregressive video generation (Self-Forcing, CausVid) and multi-view video synthesis (SynCamMaster, CVD)—into a unified framework. The key architectural departure is introducing a recurrent 3D reconstruction model (CUT3R) as a persistent geometric bridge between sequentially generated views, replacing the dense bidirectional cross-view attention that prior multi-view methods rely on. The training recipe extends Self-Forcing's DMD-based distillation to the view axis via spatio-temporal self-forcing unrolling, and adds a joint denoising regime to unify first-view text-to-video generation with view-sequential conditioning in a single model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to generate multi-view videos at unbounded temporal length and arbitrary viewpoint counts with a single few-step model, addressing a gap where prior methods handled either long single-view or short multi-view but not both simultaneously. The 4D geometric bridge concept—using a recurrent reconstruction model's persistent state to ground each new viewpoint in accumulated scene geometry—offers a scalable alternative to quadratic cross-view attention and could inform future multi-view generation architectures. Key limitations include primary training on synthetic data, DMD supervision limited to pairwise view consistency (never directly supervising >2 simultaneous views), and residual temporal degradation over very long horizons that could be addressed by integrating newer temporal stability techniques.
