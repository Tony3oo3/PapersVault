## Problem

Estimating accurate 3D structures (camera poses, scene geometry, object motion) from casual dynamic videos is hindered by moving objects that violate epipolar constraints in bundle adjustment. Existing approaches train motion segmentation networks to filter dynamic regions, but their performance is bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale motion segmentation datasets, yielding inaccurate masks on in-the-wild videos and degrading downstream 3D understanding.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw video sequence, uniformly subsamples keyframes, and feeds them to a VLM that outputs, for each detected dynamic object, a salient frame index and a free-text description of the object's appearance and location. These (frame, description) pairs are then consumed by a text-prompted segmentation model (Sa2VA + SAM2) to produce per-object binary masks at the keyframe, which are propagated across all frames via SAM2's memory mechanism and merged by element-wise union into a single H×W binary mask per frame. The resulting mask sequence serves as a plug-and-play filter for three downstream optimizers (bundle adjustment, consistent video depth, and 4D track refinement), each of which requires only the mask as an additional input alongside their standard depth, flow, and pose estimates.

## Method

DYNAPO is a fully training-free, two-stage pipeline. In the first stage, a VLM (GPT-4o) is prompted with a structured chain-of-thought template to reason over subsampled keyframes: it identifies all moving objects, determines their visibility intervals, selects the most salient frame for each, and writes a spatial-appearance description. In the second stage, Sa2VA (an MLLM coupled to a SAM2 decoder) converts each (keyframe, description) pair into a precise binary mask, and SAM2's built-in memory mechanism propagates that mask temporally across the entire video without any fine-tuning. The key insight is replacing geometric motion cues (optical flow, epipolar residuals) with semantic reasoning—asking a VLM *which* objects are moving rather than inferring motion from pixel displacements—which eliminates the need for task-specific training data. The resulting masks are then injected into three state-of-the-art 3D pipelines: as a hard filter in AnyCam's bundle-adjustment reprojection loss, as a replacement for MegaSaM's learned uncertainty map in consistent video depth optimization, and as a binary motion score in Stereo4D's 4D track optimizer, each yielding consistent improvements over the original methods' own motion estimates.

## Results

On motion segmentation, DYNAPO achieves state-of-the-art Jaccard index of 83.3 on SegTrackv2 and 84.0 on FBMS-59, surpassing the previous best (LRTR at 81.2 and ABR at 81.9 respectively), while remaining competitive on DAVIS-2016 (81.6 vs. LRTR's 82.2). For camera pose estimation on LightSpeed, integrating DYNAPO masks into bundle adjustment reduces ATE by up to 0.096 (Easi3r: 0.214→0.118) and RRE by up to 1.238 (SpatialTrackerV2: 2.487→1.249) compared to the unmasked baseline. For depth optimization, DYNAPO masks improve abs-rel from 0.2369 to 0.2135 on Sintel and from 0.1083 to 0.1069 on DyCheck over MegaSaM's own uncertainty map.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On motion segmentation, DYNAPO outperforms all 12 baselines on SegTrackv2 (83.3 vs. best prior LRTR 81.2, +2.1) and FBMS-59 (84.0 vs. best prior ABR 81.9, +2.1), but trails LRTR by 0.6 on DAVIS-2016 (81.6 vs. 82.2), where the dataset's simpler, single-object scenes offer less advantage to semantic reasoning. For camera pose estimation on LightSpeed, DYNAPO masks consistently outperform both the no-mask baseline and each method's own predicted masks: e.g., Easi3r ATE drops from 0.214 (no mask) to 0.118 (DYNAPO) vs. 0.150 (own mask), and SpatialTrackerV2 ATE drops from 0.188 to 0.103. For depth optimization, DYNAPO masks improve over MegaSaM's learned uncertainty map on all three datasets (Sintel abs-rel 0.2135 vs. 0.2369; DyCheck 0.1069 vs. 0.1083; TUM-RGBD 0.1574 vs. 0.1598), with the largest gain on Sintel where dynamic content is most challenging. For 4D track optimization, the comparison with Stereo4D is qualitative only, showing visually more coherent trajectories but no quantitative metric reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

DYNAPO departs from the dominant paradigm of training dedicated motion segmentation networks (flow-based, trajectory-based, or learned uncertainty maps) by instead leveraging the emergent reasoning of VLMs (GPT-4o) to semantically identify dynamic objects, then using Sa2VA/SAM2 for precise pixel-level segmentation and temporal propagation. It builds directly on SAM2's memory mechanism for video mask propagation and Sa2VA's MLLM-to-SAM2 decoder interface, but the key conceptual shift is replacing geometric motion cues (optical flow, epipolar constraints, trajectory length) with chain-of-thought semantic reasoning about what is moving. For downstream 3D understanding, it is explicitly plug-and-play: it slots into AnyCam's bundle adjustment, MegaSaM's consistent video depth optimization, and Stereo4D's 4D track optimization by simply replacing their learned or heuristic motion masks with the VLM-derived binary mask.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent bottleneck in dynamic SfM/SLAM: the scarcity of large-scale motion segmentation training data, which limits the generalization of learned motion filters to in-the-wild videos. By showing that a training-free VLM+SAM2 pipeline achieves SOTA motion segmentation and consistently improves pose, depth, and trajectory estimation across multiple baselines, it offers a practical, drop-in solution that practitioners can adopt without retraining. Key limitations remain: the three downstream tasks (pose, depth, 4D tracks) are optimized independently rather than jointly; the method can fail when a large dynamic object dominates the frame; and the text-prompt interface between the VLM and SAM2 is ambiguous for same-category objects (e.g., a flock of birds). A cohesive joint-optimization framework and feature-level (rather than text-level) communication between the reasoning and segmentation stages are identified as promising next steps.
