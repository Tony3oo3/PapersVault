## Problem

Event-based video reconstruction has been constrained by the E2VID-style end-to-end regression paradigm, which relies on small paired event-image datasets and compact networks, yielding low-quality grayscale outputs with perceptually inferior results. Bringing large diffusion models to this task faces two obstacles: the prohibitively high computational cost of generating high-frame-rate video, and the near-impossibility of collecting large-scale event-image paired data. This paper addresses both by reformulating reconstruction as a one-step diffusion problem trained on surrogate degraded images from large-scale natural image datasets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monochromatic asynchronous event stream (sparse events with pixel coordinates, timestamps, and polarity) as the sole input, converts it to synchronous voxel grids, and produces chromatic (full-color) video frames at 512×512 or 1024×1024 resolution. Notably, the output is colorful despite the input being monochromatic, and the reconstruction is generated in a single forward pass rather than iterative denoising steps. The method operates frame-by-frame with temporal consistency maintained through the recurrent EvEncoder rather than cross-frame attention.

## Method

EvDiff decomposes event-to-video reconstruction into an EvEncoder (mapping events to latent representations) and a one-step diffusion model (SD3-based DiT) that refines the latent in a single forward pass. The EvEncoder uses an Efficient Temporal Fusion (ETF) module with a lightweight gated mechanism to reduce temporal complexity from O((THW)²·C) to O((HW)²·T·C), enabling high-frame-rate generation. The key training innovation is a three-stage Surrogate Training Pipeline: Stage 1 trains the DiT on 1.8M Places365 images paired with synthetic E2VID-style degradations (simulating event generation ambiguity, sensor imperfections, and algorithmic blur); Stage 2 distills the VAE encoder into the event-specific EvEncoder; Stage 3 jointly fine-tunes the full model on small-scale real event-RGB data. The E2VID-style Degradation Model synthesizes characteristic artifacts (blotchy textures, edge degradation, motion blur, spatial low-pass filtering) from high-quality images, bridging the domain gap between large image datasets and the event reconstruction task.

## Results

On BS-ERGB, EvDiff achieves MSE 0.0463, LPIPS 0.4023, FID 148, and FVD 984, improving by 32.2%, 8.5%, and 37.8% over the second-best method on MSE, LPIPS, and FID respectively. On DSEC, it reaches MSE 0.0476, LPIPS 0.4226, FID 129, and FVD 1491, with gains of 20.8%, 14.0%, and 44.9% over the runner-up on the same metrics. EvDiff is the only method that produces chromatic video from monochromatic events, and its one-step inference requires only 2.19B parameters and 2.18T FLOPs at 512×512, versus 8.23B parameters and 26.62T FLOPs for a 10-step ControlNet baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvDiff achieves the best MSE, LPIPS, and FID on both BS-ERGB and DSEC, with the largest margin on FID (148 vs. 238 for E2VID+ on BS-ERGB; 129 vs. 234 for SPADE-E2VID on DSEC). On SSIM, EvDiff trails E2VID+ (0.3394 vs. 0.3710) on BS-ERGB and SPADE-E2VID (0.3677 vs. 0.4578) on DSEC, reflecting the trade-off between pixel-level fidelity and perceptual realism. On FVD, EvDiff leads on BS-ERGB (984 vs. 1562 for ET-Net) but is slightly behind SPADE-E2VID on DSEC (1491 vs. 1277). Against the ControlNet-based multi-step diffusion counterpart, EvDiff uses 3.7× fewer parameters (2.19B vs. 8.23B) and 12× fewer FLOPs at 512×512 (2.18T vs. 26.62T for 10 steps), while producing more temporally consistent and faithful reconstructions without requiring GT-derived prompts. All regression-based baselines (E2VID, FireNet, ET-Net, HyperE2VID, etc.) produce only grayscale outputs, whereas EvDiff is the sole method generating full-color video from monochromatic events.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant E2VID-style end-to-end regression paradigm for event-to-video reconstruction by decomposing the task into a degradation-to-restoration pipeline, leveraging Stable Diffusion 3's DiT architecture as a one-step generative backbone. The key conceptual bridge is the Surrogate Training Pipeline, which synthesizes E2VID-style degradation patterns on large-scale natural images (Places365) to train the diffusion model without requiring scarce event-image pairs, then distills the VAE encoder into a purpose-built EvEncoder using ConvLSTM-inspired gated temporal fusion. The one-step diffusion formulation follows recent distillation work (DMD, TSD-SR) but is adapted here to handle the high temporal length inherent to event cameras, where multi-step ControlNet approaches become computationally prohibitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Surrogate Training Pipeline offers a general recipe for bringing large pretrained generative models to domains with severe paired-data scarcity, potentially applicable beyond event cameras to other sensor modalities. The one-step inference (2.18T FLOPs at 512×512 vs. 26.62T for 10-step ControlNet) makes high-frame-rate video reconstruction practically feasible, which is critical for the microsecond-level temporal resolution that motivates event cameras. However, residual temporal flicker, SD3-induced grid artifacts, and the frame-wise (rather than truly video-native) diffusion limit the approach; whether the surrogate degradation model faithfully captures all real-sensor failure modes at scale remains an open question, and extending to truly video-level diffusion with temporal attention could close the remaining gap.
