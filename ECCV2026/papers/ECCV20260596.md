## Problem

Monocular depth estimators fail on transparent objects because refraction and transmission violate the photometric appearance-geometry assumptions these networks are trained on, producing unstable or incorrect depth. Prior approaches require fine-tuning depth models on large-scale paired transparent RGB-depth data, making them model-dependent and incompatible with rapidly evolving foundation depth backbones. The paper asks whether transparent-object appearance can be corrected as a front-end problem without modifying or retraining the depth model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260596/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image containing one or more transparent objects and produces a per-pixel metric depth map. At inference, each transparent object is localized via a coarse-to-fine segmentation pipeline (Trans4Trans + SAM 3), a local patch centered on its bounding box is cropped and resized to 512×512 for the diffusion model, and the opacified result is composited back into the original image before being fed to a frozen depth backbone (Depth Anything V3 or MoGe-2). Processing one image takes approximately 7.65 s on a single H100 GPU, and all depth-model evaluations are conducted zero-shot without any dataset-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

SeeClear is a two-stage plug-and-play framework. Stage 1 is a conditional latent diffusion model (initialized from Paint-by-Example) that maps a transparent object's refractive appearance to a geometry-consistent opaque counterpart; it is conditioned on the noisy latent, the VAE-encoded transparent image, a downsampled segmentation mask for spatial guidance, and a CLIP class token for semantic identity, and is trained with a standard DDPM noise-prediction loss plus an LPIPS perceptual loss applied only at low-noise timesteps. Stage 2 composites the generated opaque region back into the original scene using a lightweight Mask Refinement Module (three 3×3 convolutions + 1×1 projection) that predicts a soft blending mask trained with BCE plus a mid-value penalty, then binarizes it for pixel-level compositing. The entire pipeline is trained on SeeClear-396k, a synthetic dataset of 396k rendered images across 66k paired transparent-opaque configurations with aligned depth, normals, and masks, constructed via Blender with systematic sampling of viewpoints, lighting, and anisotropic shape deformations. The composited image is then passed to an off-the-shelf foundation depth model without any architectural modification or retraining, decoupling appearance correction from depth inference.

## Results

On ClearGrasp (ToM region), SeeClear+DA3 achieves RMSE 21.45 mm, AbsRel 0.033, and δ₁.₀₂₅ 51.27%, while SeeClear+MoGe-2 achieves RMSE 26.62 mm and δ₁.₀₂₅ 42.75%. On the full-image (All) region, SeeClear+MoGe-2 attains the best performance across all eight metrics (RMSE 17.87 mm, AbsRel 0.016, δ₁.₀₂₅ 80.63%). On TransPhy3D, SeeClear+MoGe-2 reaches RMSE 38.91 mm and δ₁.₀₂₅ 94.43%, surpassing the fine-tuned DKT on AbsRel (0.012 vs. 0.014) without any dataset-specific training. On Booster, SeeClear+DA3 reduces ToM MAE by 32.2% (18.94→12.85 mm) over DA3 alone, generalizing to unseen categories like doors and windshields.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeeClear+DA3 outperforms all zero-shot baselines on the ToM region of ClearGrasp: it beats Depth4ToM (RMSE 21.45 vs. 24.83 mm), GenPercept (21.45 vs. 24.09 mm), Marigold (21.45 vs. 28.10 mm), GeoWizard (21.45 vs. 31.54 mm), MODEST (21.45 vs. 39.31 mm), and D4RD (21.45 vs. 39.95 mm). Against the fine-tuned DKT, SeeClear+DA3 loses on δ₁.₀₂₅ (51.27% vs. 52.69%) and δ₁.₀₅ (78.13% vs. 81.29%) in the ToM region but wins on RMSE (21.45 vs. 22.43 mm), AbsRel (0.033 vs. 0.035), and all other metrics; on the All and Other regions, SeeClear decisively outperforms DKT (e.g., RMSE 34.85 vs. 137.48 mm on All). SeeClear+MoGe-2 dramatically improves over the MoGe-2 baseline (RMSE 56.83→26.62 mm, δ₁.₀₂₅ 5.63%→42.75% on ToM) and achieves the best All-region scores across all eight metrics. On TransPhy3D, SeeClear+MoGe-2 surpasses the fine-tuned DKT on AbsRel (0.012 vs. 0.014) and δ₁.₀₂₅ (94.43% vs. 89.20%) while remaining competitive on RMSE (38.91 vs. 33.20 mm), all without dataset-specific training. The plug-and-play design means both DA3 and MoGe-2 backbones benefit from opacification without any retraining, confirming the framework's backbone-agnostic nature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in transparent-object depth estimation, where prior methods (Depth4ToM, D4RD, MODEST, DKT) fine-tune depth networks on paired transparent RGB-depth data, making them backbone-dependent and requiring retraining as new depth models emerge. Instead, SeeClear reframes the problem as an appearance-editing task: a conditional latent diffusion model (initialized from Paint-by-Example) converts transparent regions into geometry-consistent opaque appearances, which are then fed to any off-the-shelf depth estimator without modification. The approach borrows the LDM architecture and VAE latent-space formulation from standard diffusion pipelines, but introduces a CLIP class-token semantic condition, a mask refinement module for boundary-aware compositing, and a purpose-built 396k paired transparent-opaque synthetic dataset for training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design is practically significant because foundation depth models (Depth Anything, MoGe-2) evolve rapidly; SeeClear avoids the retraining cost that plagues fine-tuning-based transparent-depth methods and remains compatible with future backbones. The 396k paired dataset and the failure-mode-driven curation strategy (inspired by Booster) provide a reusable resource for the community. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to specular, translucent, and multi-layer transparent materials, whether it extends to video with temporal consistency, and how it behaves when segmentation masks are severely inaccurate. The paper acknowledges failure on large, nearly invisible glass panes with limited contextual cues, suggesting the method's ceiling is bounded by the quality of available visual evidence.
