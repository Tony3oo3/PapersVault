## Problem

Visual grounding (localising language-referred objects) remains entirely unexplored in agriculture, where crops and weeds are visually similar, appear at extreme scale ranges (0.01%–0.97% of image area), and the referred target may be absent from the scene. No benchmark dataset existed to evaluate grounding models under these field conditions, and state-of-the-art models like GroundingDINO achieve only 33.9 mAP on agricultural images, exposing a substantial domain gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261946/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution field image (1445 px) and a template-based referring expression encoding category, position (3×3 grid), and size (tiny/small/medium/large), including negative expressions where the target is absent. It produces a two-level output: a global existence score (whether the referred object appears at all) and per-instance relevance rankings with bounding boxes. The dataset gRef-CW provides 8,034 images with 82,592 annotations split 70:15:15, uniquely including 9,186 negative instance-level expressions and image-level absence sentences.

## Method

Weed-VG is a modular framework that extends GroundingDINO (Swin Transformer + BERT-base) with a Hierarchical Relevance Scoring (HRS) module. HRS decomposes grounding into Level-0 global existence detection (multiclass over image-level sentences) and Level-1 instance relevance (contrastive ranking via Multi-Head Cross-Attention and FFN fusing sentence-level and word-level text–vision similarities with a learnable weight). A hierarchical constraint enforces logical consistency by setting the instance-level loss to max(L_lvl1, L_lvl0), preventing instance discrimination when existence is uncertain. For bounding box regression under extreme scale variation, the method uses InterpIoU (an auxiliary IoU term on a linearly interpolated box at α=0.99) combined with a distance- and size-aware Hungarian matching cost. Training proceeds in two stages: first fine-tuning the box head with InterpIoU for 100 epochs, then training the HRS projection and attention layers for 60 epochs with adaptive loss weights depending on image type (mixed, single-category, or empty).

## Results

On the gRef-CW test set, Weed-VG achieves 62.42% Top-1 accuracy, 55.44% R@0.5, 57.25% mIoU, and 78.35% Negative Accuracy, substantially outperforming all baselines. The strongest baseline, SAM3, reaches only 34.88% Top-1, 46.65% R@0.5, 32.76% mIoU, and 25.53% Neg-Acc. GroundingDINO-L (the backbone used within Weed-VG) scores 20.38% Top-1 and 28.73% R@0.5, while MDETR achieves the highest mIoU among baselines (54.19%) but with negligible recall (7.78% R@0.5) and near-random ranking (10.16% Top-1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Weed-VG outperforms all three baselines across every reported metric on the gRef-CW test set. Against SAM3 (the strongest baseline), Weed-VG gains +27.5 points in Top-1 (62.42 vs 34.88), +8.8 points in R@0.5 (55.44 vs 46.65), +24.5 points in mIoU (57.25 vs 32.76), and +52.8 points in Neg-Acc (78.35 vs 25.53). Against GroundingDINO-L (its own backbone), Weed-VG improves Top-1 by +42.0 points and Neg-Acc by +70.8 points, showing that the HRS module and InterpIoU regression are the primary sources of gain rather than detector capacity. MDETR achieves the highest baseline mIoU (54.19%) but with only 7.78% R@0.5 and 10.16% Top-1, indicating it localises accurately when it finds the right instance but almost never retrieves it. SAM3's dense proposal strategy boosts recall to 46.65% but its Neg-Acc of 25.53% reveals it still fails to reject absent targets in roughly three out of four negative cases, whereas Weed-VG's hierarchical existence detection resolves this with 78.35% Neg-Acc.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalised visual grounding (gVG) framework—previously studied in remote sensing and medical imaging—to the agricultural domain, where it had not been explored. Architecturally, it builds on GroundingDINO as a proposal generator and adopts InterpIoU for bounding box regression, then layers a novel Hierarchical Relevance Scoring (HRS) module on top that decomposes grounding into global existence detection and instance-level ranking. The key departure from prior gVG work is the systematic inclusion of negative expressions (absence queries) at both image and instance levels, combined with a hierarchical constraint that logically couples the two levels, which is absent from standard referring expression benchmarks like RefCOCO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of gRef-CW fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark evaluated grounding models under agricultural conditions with extreme scale variation (0.01%–0.97% of image area), high visual similarity between crops and weeds, and explicit absence queries. The finding that state-of-the-art grounding models (GroundingDINO, SAM3) collapse to near-random performance on this domain, with Neg-Acc of only 3–25%, underscores that current open-vocabulary grounding is not transferable to precision agriculture without domain-specific adaptation. Open questions include whether the template-based annotation scheme limits generalisation to free-form language, whether the HRS approach transfers to other dense-scene domains, and how performance holds in real-world deployment beyond top-down field imagery.
