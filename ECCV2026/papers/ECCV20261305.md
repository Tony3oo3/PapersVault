## Problem

Current VLMs are forced to reason about continuous, high-dimensional visual information (depth, boundaries, spatial layout, geometry) through a discrete text bottleneck, which loses fine-grained perceptual cues and can actually degrade visual reasoning performance. The paper asks whether VLMs can learn to reason directly in continuous visual space—'thinking visually' rather than translating everything into words—while remaining self-contained and efficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261305/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard RGB image and text prompt as input, processed through a frozen vision encoder and language encoder. During generation, the VLM autoregressively produces a mixed sequence of text tokens and ~20 continuous visual tokens (8 segmentation, 4 depth, 4 edge, 4 DINO). The primary output is a text answer, but the visual tokens can optionally be decoded via lightweight task-specific decoders into dense predictions (segmentation masks, depth maps, edge maps) for interpretability; at inference without visualization, reasoning occurs entirely in the latent visual space without any decoding step.

## Method

CoVT augments a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B or LLaVA-v1.5-13B, fine-tuned with LoRA) with four types of continuous visual tokens, each supervised by a lightweight vision expert: SAM (prompt-level mask decoding with Hungarian matching + dice/focal loss), DepthAnything v2 (BMM-based depth reconstruction with L1 loss), PIDINet (1×1 convolutional edge reconstruction with L1 loss), and DINOv2 (patch-level feature matching with MSE). The alignment strategy is granularity-aware: task-oriented experts are aligned at the prompt/decoder level to preserve fine-grained spatial detail, while the representative DINOv2 model is aligned in feature space. Training follows a four-stage curriculum—comprehension (inserting tokens after <image>), generation (guiding token production), reasoning (integrating tokens into the thinking chain), and efficient reasoning (randomly dropping token subsets to teach flexible selection)—with a joint loss combining cross-entropy and weighted visual reconstruction terms. The key architectural insight is that these compact continuous tokens serve as an implicit, differentiable 'tool-use' mechanism embedded in the latent space, enabling the VLM to internalize dense perceptual knowledge without external tool calls or image generation.

## Results

CoVT (with 3 visual tokens: Segmentation, Depth, DINO) achieves a 5.5% overall gain on CV-Bench (80.0 vs. 74.5 baseline), with a striking 14.0% improvement on the depth subtask (86.8 vs. 72.8) and 7.0% on distance (82.5 vs. 75.5). Across broader vision-centric benchmarks, CoVT delivers consistent gains: +3.7 on MME-RealWorld, +3.0 on RealWorldQA, +2.7 on MMVP, +4.3 on HRBench-4K, and +4.5 on HRBench-8K. On non-vision-centric benchmarks, CoVT maintains competitive performance with a 1.2% average gain across eight tasks, confirming no degradation in generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline, CoVT wins on every vision-centric benchmark, with the largest margins on CV-Bench Depth (+14.0%), HRBench-4K (+4.3%), and HRBench-8K (+4.5%). Compared to LVR (Latent Visual Reasoning), CoVT outperforms on CV-Bench (80.0 vs. 77.2), RealWorldQA (71.6 vs. 69.7), MMStar-P (69.2 vs. 67.7), HR4K (72.9 vs. 69.6), HR8K (69.4 vs. 65.6), and MMVP (58.7 vs. 50.7), but trails on V* Bench (78.0 vs. 81.7). Against Monet, CoVT leads on CV-Bench (80.0 vs. 75.7), RealWorldQA (71.6 vs. 69.3), MMStar-P (69.2 vs. 67.1), HR4K (72.9 vs. 71.0), HR8K (69.4 vs. 68.0), and MMVP (58.7 vs. 56.0), while Monet retains an edge on V* (83.3 vs. 78.0). On the LLaVA-v1.5-13B backbone (Table 4), CoVT with depth tokens surpasses Aurora-depth by 12.9% on BLINK relative depth, and CoVT with segmentation tokens beats Aurora-count by 26.6% on BLINK count and 45.2% on relative depth, demonstrating that CoVT generalizes across base architectures and consistently outperforms the closest latent-reasoning competitor.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoVT sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) text-based Chain-of-Thought reasoning in VLMs, (2) latent-space reasoning in LLMs (Coconut, CCoT), and (3) perception-token approaches for VLMs (Aurora, LVR, Monet). It departs from tool-augmented reasoning (ViperGPT, Visual ChatGPT) by internalizing perceptual capabilities via continuous visual tokens rather than delegating to external models, and from image-generation approaches (MCoT, VChain) by operating in a compact latent space (~20 tokens) instead of pixel space. The alignment strategy—prompt-level for task-oriented experts (SAM, DepthAnything, PIDINet) and feature-level for representative models (DINOv2)—is a novel design choice that distinguishes CoVT from prior latent-reasoning work that typically uses a single modality or a uniform loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that text-only CoT actively degrades visual reasoning (Qwen3-VL-Thinking underperforms Qwen3-VL-Instruct by >5% on spatial benchmarks) challenges the prevailing assumption that longer reasoning chains universally help, and motivates a rethinking of how VLMs should handle perception-intensive tasks. CoVT's self-contained design (no external tools, ~20 tokens, LoRA-only training) makes it practically deployable, and the interpretability of decoded tokens offers a window into model reasoning that is absent in purely latent approaches. Open questions include whether the framework scales to video or 3D inputs, whether the specific expert choices (SAM, DepthAnything, PIDINet, DINOv2) are optimal, and whether the gains persist as base VLMs improve their native visual encoders.
