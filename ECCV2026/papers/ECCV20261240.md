## Problem

Existing diffusion-based grasp generation methods either rely on deterministic global encoders that collapse multimodal grasp distributions or fully stochastic backbones that destroy geometric consistency, failing to disentangle structural certainty (e.g., object geometry) from contextual uncertainty (e.g., which object to act on first) in cluttered, partially observed scenes. This entanglement limits both the controllability of grasp generation under uncertainty and the geometric stability of predicted poses.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an N-point partial point cloud (sampled into K depth views from virtual cameras to handle self-occlusion) and a corresponding RGB image that provides scene-level contextual features. It produces a dense, per-point 6-DoF grasp field (rotation R and translation T at each point), a continuous affordance field, and a smooth harmonic potential over the object surface. The local latent representation h_L ∈ R^{N×32} is decoded via a variational grasp decoder, yielding geometrically precise yet contextually diverse grasp hypotheses rather than a single deterministic pose.

## Method

The core contribution is a split-latent hierarchical diffusion that decomposes the global scene representation into a fixed latent h_fixed (deterministic geometric/semantic priors extracted by a multi-view Point Transformer encoder pretrained with masked autoencoding and contrastive alignment) and a diffused latent h_diffused (stochastic context generated by a Global Conditional Diffusion whose Gaussian parameters are learned from h_fixed and RGB features). A Local Conditional Diffusion then performs point-level denoising with a 4-scale U-Net using PVConv and cross-attention to inject the composite global latent h_G = [h_fixed; h_diffused] into dense local latents. A Harmonic Grasp Field regularizes these latents by enforcing ∇²Φ = 0 with Dirichlet boundary conditions (zero at obstacles, one at contact surfaces), projecting each local latent along the harmonic gradient to ensure smooth, obstacle-aware grasp manifolds. Training proceeds in two stages: encoder pretraining (8k epochs) followed by joint optimization of GCD, LCD, HGF, and a grasp VAE via an ELBO objective (24k epochs).

## Results

On DexGraspNet-2.0 (dexterous grasping), the method achieves grasp success rates of 93.1/85.9/76.1 on GraspNet-1Billion (Dense/Random/Loose) and 83.2/87.8/74.9 on ShapeNet, outperforming the strongest baselines DexGraspVLA (91.7/85.0/75.5) and ClutterDexGrasp (91.6/85.1/75.2). On GraspClutter6D (gripper grasping), it reaches 86.2% GSR and 87.3% DR on Packed-5, with the largest margins under Pile-15 clutter (79.0% GSR, 56.8% DR). On GraspNet-1Billion (RealSense), it attains 68.4/55.7/25.1 AP (Seen/Similar/Novel), and in A² policy rollouts it improves success by up to 10.3% while reducing planning steps by up to 19.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 27 baselines across four benchmarks. In dexterous grasping (Table 1), it leads DexGraspVLA [49] by 1.4 pp (Dense) and ClutterDexGrasp [3] by 1.5 pp, while the gap over geometry-only methods like HGC-Net [18] (46.0) and DDGC [20] (47.9) exceeds 45 pp in Dense scenes. For gripper grasping on GraspClutter6D (Table 2), it surpasses AffordGrasp [34] by 1.1 pp GSR and 0.6 pp DR on Packed-5, and ThinkGrasp [26] by 1.1 pp GSR on Pile-15, with the largest DR margin (1.6 pp) also at Pile-15. On GraspNet-1Billion (Table 3), it beats GSNet [37] by 1.3–2.4 AP and DexGraspNet-2 [46] by 12.4–2.5 AP across Seen/Similar/Novel splits. In A² policy rollouts (Table 4), integrating the grasp model into A²-G-Pick [41] yields +4.3% success and −16.4% planning steps (Seen), while A²-G-Place [41] gains +10.3% success, confirming that the grasp quality improvement translates to downstream manipulation performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three largely independent research threads—diffusion-based generative modeling (DDPM, LDM), point-transformer 3D encoders with masked autoencoding pretraining, and variational grasp latent spaces—into a unified hierarchical architecture. Its key conceptual departure from prior diffusion-for-grasping work (DexDiffuser, DexMGNet, DexGraspVLA) is the explicit split-latent decomposition: rather than conditioning a single diffusion backbone on scene features, it separates a deterministic geometric prior (fixed latent) from a stochastic contextual component (diffused latent) generated by a separate Global Conditional Diffusion. The Harmonic Grasp Field, which regularizes the local diffusion output via Laplacian constraints on a potential surface, has no direct precedent in the grasp-generation literature and borrows from classical potential-field robotics planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine structural gap—existing methods either collapse multimodal grasp distributions through deterministic regression or entangle geometric certainty with strategic uncertainty in a single latent—yet the practical gains over the strongest baselines (DexGraspNet-2, DexGraspVLA) are modest, typically 1–2 percentage points in grasp success rate. The multi-stage training protocol (8k-epoch encoder pretraining, then 24k-epoch joint diffusion training) and the need for both point-cloud and RGB inputs at inference raise practical deployment questions that the paper does not fully resolve. Open questions include whether the split-latent decomposition is strictly necessary compared to a simpler conditional diffusion with geometric regularization, how the harmonic field scales to articulated or deformable objects, and whether the framework's benefits persist under real-sensor noise beyond the synthetic and RealSense settings evaluated.
