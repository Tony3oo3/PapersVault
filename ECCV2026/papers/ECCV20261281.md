## Problem

Adapter-based zero-shot personalized text-to-image generation suffers from three intertwined limitations: an irreconcilable trade-off between concept preservation and prompt following caused by entangled concept-specific and concept-agnostic information in reference features, loss of fine-grained visual details when relying solely on deep-layer CLIP features, and poor scalability from single-subject to multi-subject personalization without dedicated multi-subject training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261281/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes one or more reference RGB images (encoded by OpenCLIP ViT-L/14-336 at 336×336), a free-form text prompt, and optionally binary region masks for multi-subject scenarios (auto-generated via grounding/segmentation tools or user-annotated). It outputs a 1024×1024 personalized image generated by the FLUX.1-Dev MM-DiT backbone, with the adapter contributing ~1.4B trainable parameters on top of the frozen base model. For multi-subject generation, N reference images each paired with a spatial mask are injected via region-level cross-attention, requiring no additional multi-subject training.

## Method

DynaIP introduces two key mechanisms on top of the FLUX.1-Dev MM-DiT. First, the Dynamic Decoupling Strategy exploits MM-DiT's intrinsic dual-branch decoupling behavior: during training, reference image features perform cross-attention with both the noisy image and text branches, causing the text branch to absorb concept-agnostic information (pose, illumination, perspective) while the image branch retains concept-specific identity; at inference, cross-attention is restricted to the image branch alone, dynamically stripping concept-agnostic interference and improving both CP-PF balance and multi-subject compositional harmony. Second, the Hierarchical Mixture-of-Experts Feature Fusion Module (HMoE-FFM) extracts CLIP features from three depth levels (layers 10, 17, 24), processes each through a dedicated expert network (linear + GELU + LayerNorm), and fuses them via a routing MLP that dynamically weights expert outputs based on class tokens, preserving fine-grained textures from shallow layers alongside semantic coherence from deep layers while enabling user-controllable visual granularity. Training proceeds in two stages (20K intra-pair + 80K cross-pair iterations) with flow-matching loss, and multi-subject extension is achieved purely through mask-guided region-level feature injection without any multi-subject training data.

## Results

On Dream-Bench++ (single-subject PT2I), DynaIP achieves the highest Prompt Following score (PF=0.934) and the best Nash utility CP·PF=0.650, surpassing FLUX.1 Kontext Dev (0.641) and Qwen-Image-Edit (0.638). On the newly introduced DynaIP-Bench (multi-subject PT2I), DynaIP attains the top scores across all three metrics: CP=0.617, PF=0.997, and CP·PF=0.615, outperforming the next-best method Qwen-Image-Edit (CP·PF=0.602) by a clear margin. Notably, DynaIP achieves these results while being trained exclusively on single-subject datasets, whereas most multi-subject baselines require dedicated multi-subject training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For single-subject PT2I, DynaIP's CP·PF of 0.650 edges out FLUX.1 Kontext Dev (0.641) and Qwen-Image-Edit (0.638), while substantially outperforming adapter-based methods like IP-Adapter-Plus (0.493) and FLUX.1 IP-Adapter (0.408). DynaIP's CP score (0.696) is marginally below IP-Adapter-Plus (0.738) and FLUX.1 Kontext Dev (0.718), but the authors attribute this to those methods producing near-copy-paste outputs that inflate CP while severely degrading PF (0.668 and 0.893 respectively). For multi-subject PT2I, DynaIP leads all 17 baselines on every metric, with CP·PF=0.615 versus Qwen-Image-Edit's 0.602, OmniGen2's 0.526, and MIP-Adapter's 0.276. The gap is most pronounced against finetuning-free multi-subject methods: MS-Diffusion (0.496), XVerse (0.488), and UNO (0.436) all trail by 0.08–0.34 in CP·PF. Critically, DynaIP achieves these multi-subject results without any multi-subject training data, whereas MS-Diffusion, MIP-Adapter, UNO, and XVerse all rely on curated multi-subject datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IP-Adapter paradigm (decoupled cross-attention for injecting reference image features) from U-Net-based diffusion models to the MM-DiT architecture used in FLUX.1. Its central departure is the observation that MM-DiT's dual-branch design (separate text and noisy-image token streams) induces an emergent decoupling during training: the text branch absorbs concept-agnostic attributes (pose, illumination) while the image branch retains concept-specific identity. By training with dual-branch cross-attention but inferring with image-branch-only cross-attention, the Dynamic Decoupling Strategy exploits this property without architectural changes. The HMoE-FFM component extends prior single-layer CLIP feature usage to a multi-layer MoE fusion, building on observations from MLLM literature about multi-layer visual features but adapting them to the generation-oriented PT2I setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling finding offers a general principle for leveraging MM-DiT's dual-branch structure in any conditioning task, potentially applicable beyond PT2I to other image-conditioned generation scenarios. The ability to extend single-subject training to multi-subject inference without multi-subject data is practically significant, reducing the data curation burden that plagues current MS-PT2I methods. However, the method still requires ~1.4B trainable parameters and 100K training iterations, and the single-subject CP·PF margin over FLUX.1 Kontext Dev (0.650 vs. 0.641) is modest. Open questions include whether the decoupling behavior generalizes to other DiT variants or training regimes, and whether the MoE routing can be made more interpretable or controllable at scale.
