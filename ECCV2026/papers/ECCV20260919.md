## Problem

Existing Lifelong Person Re-Identification (LReID) methods assume each person's clothing remains consistent across the data stream. In the more practical Cloth-Hybrid LReID (CH-LReID) setting, cloth-consistent and cloth-changing datasets arrive alternately, causing the model to repeatedly overwrite one type of knowledge with the other. This cyclic knowledge conflict dramatically exacerbates catastrophic forgetting, and prior cloth-changing ReID methods that rely on auxiliary signals (clothing labels, gait, body contours) are impractical for efficient lifelong learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB person images resized to 256×128 pixels, processed in mini-batches of 128 (32 identities × 4 images each) through a ResNet-50 backbone. Training proceeds sequentially over five datasets (Market-1501, MSMT17-V2, CUHK03, LTCC, PRCC) in two predefined orders, with the type of each incoming dataset (cloth-consistent or cloth-changing) unknown in advance. At inference, only the backbone extracts a fixed-dimensional feature embedding per image for cross-camera person retrieval; no subprototypes or auxiliary modules are needed at test time.

## Method

SCORE assigns k learnable multi-dimensional Gaussian subprototypes (center + diagonal covariance) to each identity, enabling the model to partition intra-identity feature space into subdistributions that can separately encode cloth-consistent and cloth-changing cues. Three complementary losses shape these subprototypes: Prototype Separating (cross-entropy + triplet on prototype centers), Instance Assembling (pull each instance toward its nearest same-identity subprototype center via maximum-likelihood matching), and Distribution Purifying (binary cross-entropy on whether the highest-likelihood subprototype belongs to the correct identity). To prevent forgetting, a Distributional Knowledge Reinforcement module samples features from the previous stage's subdistributions, builds a knowledge-shift matrix, and enforces KL-divergence alignment between old and new subprototype relation structures. An Instance-oriented Structural Knowledge Retention module additionally distills cross-instance relational structure from the frozen old backbone into the new feature space. The overall loss combines the base ReID supervision (cross-entropy + triplet), the subdistribution optimization loss, and the two knowledge-retention terms, followed by a simple model-averaging post-processing step after each stage.

## Results

On the CH-LReID benchmark (Training Order-1: Market → LTCC → PRCC → MSMT17 → CUHK03), SCORE achieves an Overall Average mAP of 44.7% and R@1 of 59.7%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art DKC (40.2% mAP, 55.8% R@1) by 4.5%/3.9%. On the Cloth-Consistent subset, SCORE reaches 57.5% mAP / 71.2% R@1, and on the Cloth-Changing subset, 36.4% mAP / 31.1% R@1. Under Training Order-2, SCORE further improves to 46.4% mAP / 57.9% R@1 overall, outperforming DKC by 2.7%/3.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCORE outperforms the strongest CH-LReID baseline DKC [2] by 4.5% mAP and 3.9% R@1 on Overall Average (Order-1), and by 2.7%/3.3% on Order-2. Against the best LReID method DASK [33], SCORE gains 1.7% mAP / 1.8% R@1 overall (Order-1), with a larger margin of 6.3% mAP / 1.7% R@1 on the Cloth-Consistent subset (57.5 vs 51.2 mAP). On the Cloth-Changing subset, SCORE leads DKC by 0.9% mAP / 6.6% R@1 (36.4/31.1 vs 35.5/24.5), indicating stronger retention of clothing-irrelevant identity cues. The gap over traditional LReID methods (LwF, AKA, PatchKD, LSTKC, USP) is even larger, with SCORE exceeding them by 6–14% mAP on Overall Average, confirming that existing cloth-consistent lifelong learning strategies fail to handle the knowledge conflict introduced by alternating cloth-changing data. The cloth-changing ReID method DSIFLF† [19], augmented with an anti-forgetting strategy, trails SCORE by 4.7% mAP / 9.7% R@1 overall, highlighting the limitation of relying on auxiliary clothing-removal features in a lifelong setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lifelong Person Re-Identification (LReID) line of work, which has primarily addressed cloth-consistent scenarios (LwF, AKA, PatchKD, LSTKC, DKP, DASK), to the recently introduced Cloth-Hybrid LReID (CH-LReID) setting where cloth-consistent and cloth-changing data alternate. Its most direct predecessor is DKC (Cui et al., CVPR 2025), which first formulated CH-LReID and used unlearnable statistical distributions to consolidate knowledge; SCORE departs by introducing learnable Gaussian subprototypes per identity and a collaborative alignment loss that actively transfers subdistributional knowledge across stages. The method also draws on distribution learning (DKP's instance-to-prototype aggregation) and knowledge distillation (LSTKC's structural consistency), but repurposes them through the lens of intra-identity subdistribution modeling rather than single-prototype or patch-level distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CH-LReID setting is arguably the most realistic formulation of lifelong ReID, since long-term deployment inevitably encounters clothing changes; SCORE's subdistribution approach offers a general principle—modeling intra-identity diversity as multiple learnable subdistributions—that could transfer to other continual learning tasks with conflicting knowledge types. However, the cloth-changing performance gains remain modest (0.1–1.5% over DKC), and the method is validated only on five datasets with a single ResNet-50 backbone, leaving open questions about scalability to longer streams, larger backbones, and whether the fixed subdistribution count k (default 5) suffices as identity diversity grows over many stages. The reliance on a frozen old backbone for ISK also constrains memory and compute in very long lifelong settings.
