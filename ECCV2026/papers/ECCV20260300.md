## Problem

Video diffusion transformers are bottlenecked by quadratic spatiotemporal attention, yet existing training-free sparse attention methods either impose fixed positional priors that ignore model-specific patterns or incur inference-time profiling overhead. The paper identifies that a large fraction of token-to-token attention connections are consistently negligible across different prompts and noise initializations, but that the specific sparsity and repetition patterns vary significantly across layers, heads, and diffusion timesteps—making a one-size-fits-all mask suboptimal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260300/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes a text prompt and a pre-computed dictionary of binary block masks (at 128×128 FlashAttention block granularity) indexed by (timestep, layer, head), along with flags marking which heads exhibit spatial row repetition. It produces a generated video at the target resolution (480p or 720p) and frame count (81 or 85 frames) matching the underlying model's configuration. The one-time calibration requires 64 prompts from MovieGenBench and produces masks whose memory footprint is 21.5 GB for Wan 2.1 14B at 720p (reducible to 3.6 GB).

## Method

CalibAtt performs a one-time offline calibration that, for each (timestep, layer, head) combination, computes per-prompt block-level attention energies and selects the minimum set of key blocks per query block whose cumulative energy reaches a timestep-dependent threshold ε(t) following an exponential schedule. Per-prompt binary masks are then averaged across 64 calibration prompts and thresholded at an agreement level ρ=0.5 to yield a single data-independent mask per (t,l,h). Separately, the method detects attention heads where spatial rows within a frame exhibit near-identical attention patterns (cosine similarity > 0.87) and marks them for anchor-row broadcasting (k=5 equispaced rows per frame). At inference, a custom FlashAttention3-based CUDA kernel loads pre-computed skip lists for non-repetitive heads to skip zero-masked blocks, while for repetitive heads it computes attention only on anchor rows and broadcasts results to neighboring spatial rows—achieving up to 1.58× end-to-end speedup with 62–74% attention sparsity while preserving VBench quality scores.

## Results

On Wan 2.1 14B at 720p (81 frames, 50 timesteps), CalibAtt achieves 62.5% average attention sparsity and a 1.58× end-to-end speedup (785s vs. 1244s dense FA3) while maintaining VBench Total score of 81.41 (vs. 81.27 dense). At 480p, CalibAtt reaches 68.1% sparsity with 1.45× speedup and VBench Total 82.30. On the 4-step distilled LightX2V model at 720p, CalibAtt achieves 73.9% sparsity and 1.57× speedup (30.6s vs. 48.3s), demonstrating that the method remains effective even in few-step regimes where inference-time overhead from other methods becomes prohibitive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CalibAtt outperforms all three training-free baselines on both sparsity and end-to-end latency across every model and resolution tested. On Wan 2.1 14B 720p, CalibAtt achieves 62.5% sparsity and 1.58× speedup, surpassing SVG2 (46.3%, 1.47×), RadialAttention (54.7%, 1.32×), and SpargeAttention (49.9%, 1.33×). On VBench quality, CalibAtt matches or slightly exceeds dense FA3 (81.41 vs. 81.27 Total at 720p), while SVG2 drops 0.05 points and RadialAttention drops 1.4 points. A critical differentiator emerges on the 4-step distilled LightX2V model: SVG2 actually *slows down* generation (0.60× at 480p, 1.02× at 720p) due to its inference-time clustering overhead, whereas CalibAtt still delivers 1.29× and 1.57× speedups respectively. SpargeAttention consistently achieves ~50% sparsity but its online block-importance estimation adds per-step overhead that limits speedup to 1.05–1.33×, well below CalibAtt's 1.16–1.58×. RadialAttention's fixed radial decay mask is the closest competitor on VBench Semantic (76.69 vs. 72.80 at 480p) but still trails CalibAtt on sparsity (49.0% vs. 68.1%) and latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

CalibAtt builds directly on FlashAttention3's block-tiling infrastructure, extending it with pre-computed per-(timestep, layer, head) skip lists. It departs from prior training-free sparse attention methods in a key conceptual way: rather than assuming fixed positional priors (RadialAttention) or estimating block importance online at inference time (SpargeAttention, LiteAttention), it performs a one-time offline calibration that learns data-independent sparsity patterns specific to each model's attention structure. The spatial repetition detection mechanism, which broadcasts anchor-row attention outputs across spatial rows within a frame, is a novel complementary strategy not present in prior block-sparse methods, and the paper shows the two strategies target largely disjoint attention maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 1.58× end-to-end speedup on 720p video generation (reducing 1244s to 785s) with no quality degradation is a meaningful efficiency gain for a field where inference cost is a major adoption barrier, and the training-free design means the method can be applied to any video diffusion model without retraining. Key limitations include the 21.5 GB mask memory overhead at 720p, the one-time calibration cost (though reducible to ~14 GPU-hours with 16 prompts), and the inherent ceiling imposed by data-independent masks that cannot capture prompt-specific attention structure. Open questions include whether cross-timestep attention correlations could yield further savings, whether more compact skip-list representations can reduce memory, and whether the calibration framework generalizes to image diffusion or language models.
