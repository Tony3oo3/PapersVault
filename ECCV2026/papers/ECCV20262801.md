## Problem

Existing video summarization pipelines rely on discrete keyframe/keyshot selection before summarization, which irreversibly discards low-scored segments, reduces temporal coherence, and underutilizes the MLLM's native attention mechanism. The paper asks how to use a continuous, query-conditioned highlight distribution as a soft steering signal that guides MLLM attention toward salient moments at inference time while preserving the full video context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262801/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous video as a sequence of T frames (no pre-selection or downsampling) plus a natural-language query/prompt q, and produces a free-form summary text O*. The highlight generator outputs clip-level scores that are linearly interpolated to frame level and min-max normalized to [0,1]; each frame score is then replicated across P visual tokens to form a steering vector V ∈ R^{TP}. The MLLM backbone remains fully frozen throughout; only a small set of per-head gate and strength parameters {a, β} are optimized once on a held-out validation set.

## Method

HAS operates in two stages. First, an off-the-shelf highlight generator produces raw temporal scores that are interpolated to length T and min-max normalized into a smooth continuous distribution h ∈ [0,1]^T. Second, h is lifted to token level via V = log(Repeat(h, P) + ε), where the log-space formulation makes V directly usable as an additive bias on pre-softmax attention logits. During autoregressive decoding, for a sparse subset of visual cross-attention heads S, HAS applies a gated row-wise bias Ã = A + σ(a_{ℓ,m})·β_{ℓ,m}·V, leaving all other heads unchanged. The steering parameters {a, β} are optimized once by minimizing teacher-forcing NLL plus a sparsity penalty on the gates, with the entire MLLM frozen; at test time the procedure is purely forward-only, making HAS a plug-and-play, training-free inference module adaptable to any video MLLM backbone.

## Results

On SumMe, HAS achieves Kendall's τ = 0.298 and Spearman's ρ = 0.335, matching or exceeding the strongest LLM-centric baseline V2Xum-LLaMA (τ = 0.296, ρ = 0.378) on τ while trailing on ρ. On TVSum, HAS leads all baselines with τ = 0.224 and ρ = 0.299, surpassing V2Xum-LLaMA (τ = 0.222, ρ = 0.293) and LLMVS (τ = 0.211, ρ = 0.275). On VideoXum cross-modal summarization, HAS achieves the best scores across all nine metrics (e.g., CIDEr = 28.0, F1 = 32.0, FCLIP = 0.963), outperforming V2Xum-LLaMA-7B/13B. On VISTA long-form scientific summarization, HAS (zero-shot, averaged over backbones) reaches FactVC = 50.11 and VideoScore = 2.03, approaching GPT-o1 zero-shot (FactVC = 51.36) while substantially outperforming open-source zero-shot baselines (e.g., mPLUG-Owl3: FactVC = 42.07).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAS outperforms all visual-only baselines (VASNet, DSNet, DMA-Sum, PGL-SUM, MSVA, iPTNet, CSTA) by a wide margin on both SumMe and TVSum, with the closest visual-only competitor CSTA reaching only τ = 0.246/0.194 versus HAS's 0.298/0.224. Among visual+text methods, HAS surpasses CLIP-It, A2Summ, SSPVS, and the large-scale pretraining baseline of Argaw et al. on both datasets. Against LLM-centric methods, HAS edges out LLMVS (τ = 0.253/0.211) and V2Xum-LLaMA (τ = 0.296/0.222) on Kendall's τ for both SumMe and TVSum, though V2Xum-LLaMA retains a lead on SumMe Spearman's ρ (0.378 vs. 0.335). On VideoXum, HAS dominates all cross-modal baselines including V2Xum-LLaMA-7B and 13B across every metric (BLEU-4, METEOR, ROUGE-L, CIDEr, F1, Spearman, Kendall, FCLIP, Cross-FCLIP). On VISTA, HAS in zero-shot mode (FactVC = 50.11) approaches proprietary models GPT-o1 (51.36) and Gemini 2.0 (52.02) while significantly outperforming open-source zero-shot backbones like mPLUG-Owl3 (42.07) and LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave (40.12), though it remains well below fully fine-tuned mPLUG-Owl3 (71.94). On the zero-shot MR.HiSum transfer test, HAS (τ = 0.45) marginally beats LLMVS (0.440) and substantially outperforms classical methods like VASNet (0.364) and PGL-SUM (0.375).

## Relation to Prior Work

HAS repurposes the inference-time attention steering paradigm from NLP (PASTA's head reweighting, attention biasing) and multimodal MLLMs (FarSight's causal-mask intervention) into the video summarization setting, where prior work has exclusively treated highlight scores as a hard selection signal (score → select keyframes → summarize, as in LLMVS, CLIP-It, V2Xum-LLM). The highlight prior itself is borrowed from query-conditioned moment retrieval and highlight detection (QVHighlights, QD-DETR), but rather than being the final prediction target, it is calibrated into a smooth temporal distribution and injected as a log-space additive bias on a sparse subset of visual cross-attention heads. This positions HAS as a conceptual bridge: it treats video highlighting as a soft, continuous guidance signal analogous to distillation or quantization, rather than a discrete extraction step, while keeping the full video context intact during MLLM inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of HAS makes it immediately applicable to any frozen video MLLM without retraining, which is practically valuable given the cost of fine-tuning large multimodal models. However, the absolute gains over strong LLM-centric baselines (e.g., V2Xum-LLaMA, LLMVS) are modest, and the method's effectiveness is gated by the quality of the external highlight generator, which remains a black-box dependency. Open questions include whether the coarse continuous steering can be combined with finer temporal localization for abrupt events, how the approach interacts with stronger or fully fine-tuned backbones, and whether the sparsity-regularized head selection generalizes beyond the calibration set. The coverage-saturation argument (Fig. 2) that soft steering retains dispersed evidence better than hard selection under fixed budgets is the most compelling conceptual contribution, but it is supported by a single visualization without a detailed ablation isolating the mechanism.
