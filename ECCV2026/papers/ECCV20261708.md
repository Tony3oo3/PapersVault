## Problem

VLA models are extremely sensitive to parameter pruning, and the prevailing paradigm treats post-pruning performance collapse as an inevitable cost to be repaired via fine-tuning or low-rank adaptation. This paper challenges that premise: if a pruned model must re-learn to function, the removed parameters were likely not truly redundant but were vital parameters undergoing functional reorganization during VLM-to-VLA adaptation. The gap is the absence of a redundancy criterion grounded in the adaptation process itself, rather than generic importance scores that conflate pruning damage with structural redundancy.

## Input / Output

The VLA models under study (OpenVLA, π₀.₅) consume RGB visual observations and natural-language task instructions to produce continuous robot action sequences for a Franka Panda arm. The pruning method itself operates on paired VLM and VLA weight tensors, computing per-head and per-channel relative L2 divergence (ΔW_rel) to construct binary structured masks at the granularity of individual attention heads or FFN channels. Evaluation is conducted on the LIBERO benchmark (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long sub-datasets) via direct inference with no post-pruning fine-tuning, measuring task success rate.

## Method

The core insight is that VLM-to-VLA parameter divergence (ΔW) is not random noise but a structured signal whose utility is module-heterogeneous: in some modules (e.g., DINOv2 attention heads, Llama-2 FFN channels), high-divergence parameters are critical and low-divergence ones are redundant, while in others (e.g., DINOv2 FFN channels) the relationship inverts. The paper uses controlled pruning as a diagnostic probe—removing top-r% vs. bottom-r% ΔW parameters without any recovery—to causally link divergence signals to functional importance. Based on the discovered heterogeneity, a multi-module joint pruning scheme assigns differentiated ratios and selection criteria (high-diff vs. low-diff) per module: protecting the fragile Projector bottleneck, pruning high-diff channels in language FFNs and DINOv2 FFNs, pruning low-diff heads in DINOv2 attention, and exploiting SigLIP's robustness for additional compression. This yields 12–30% parameter reduction on OpenVLA and π₀.₅ while retaining ~90% of baseline performance with zero post-pruning recovery, whereas standard criteria (LLM-Pruner, FLAP, Wanda) collapse to near-zero success under the same recovery-free constraint.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, the proposed multi-module joint pruning scheme reduces OpenVLA from 7.5B to 5.7–6.6B parameters (12%–24% reduction) while maintaining 70.4%–52.5% average success rate (vs. 76.5% baseline) with no post-pruning fine-tuning. For π₀.₅, the Moderate configuration reduces the model from 3.6B to 2.8B parameters (22% reduction) with average SR dropping only from 96.9% to 89.0%. In contrast, all three conventional pruning baselines (LLM-Pruner, FLAP, Wanda) suffer near-total performance collapse under the same recovery-free evaluation, with average SR dropping by 48–69 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three widely-used pruning criteria—LLM-Pruner, FLAP, and Wanda—all evaluated under identical recovery-free conditions on LIBERO. At matched capacity (~6.2B params, 12.4 GB), the proposed Moderate configuration achieves 70.5% Spatial and 39.0% Long-horizon SR, while LLM-Pruner reaches only 23.4% Spatial and 1.0% Long, and FLAP collapses to 0.2% Spatial and 0.0% Long. Wanda performs slightly better than FLAP/LLM-Pruner in its Sparse Lang.BB variant (28.6% avg SR) but still trails the proposed method by over 33 percentage points. The gap is most dramatic on long-horizon tasks, where baselines drop to 0–12% SR versus 29.5–46.8% for the proposed scheme. The paper's key claim is that these baselines fail not because pruning is inherently destructive, but because their importance criteria (Taylor expansion, fluctuation, weight×activation magnitude) do not account for the structured parameter reorganization that occurs during VLM-to-VLA adaptation, leading them to remove functionally critical parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'prune-then-recover' paradigm in VLA efficiency research (exemplified by RLRC and GLUESTICK), which treats post-pruning fine-tuning as an unavoidable compensation step. Rather than building on a specific prior pruning method, it repurposes the VLM-to-VLA adaptation process itself—specifically the parameter divergence ΔW between the source VLM and adapted VLA—as a structured redundancy signal, a perspective absent from prior pruning criteria (LLM-Pruner, Wanda, FLAP) that operate on the final VLA weights alone. The controlled-pruning-as-diagnostic-probe framework is a novel analytical lens that reframes pruning from an optimization objective to a causal intervention, distinguishing this work from both the VLA architecture literature and the classical structured-pruning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely accepted assumption in VLA efficiency research—that performance recovery after pruning is an inevitable cost—by showing that the need for recovery is actually a signal of mis-identified redundancy. The revealed module heterogeneity (e.g., the 'sensitivity reversal' where high-ΔW parameters are critical in DINOv2 attention but redundant in DINOv2 FFN, while the opposite holds in Llama-2) provides actionable guidance for architecture-aware compression. Limitations include evaluation on a single benchmark (LIBERO) and only two model pairs, leaving open whether the ΔW-based criteria generalize to other VLA architectures, longer training horizons, or settings where the original VLM weights are unavailable. A key open question is whether the adaptation signal can be approximated without access to the pre-adaptation VLM, which would be necessary for practitioners who only have the final VLA checkpoint.
