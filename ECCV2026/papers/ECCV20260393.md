## Problem

Sparse-view X-ray CT reconstruction is severely ill-posed, and existing methods are fundamentally bounded by the limited angular information in the acquired projections. Neuromorphic event cameras adaptively capture high-frequency angular changes at microsecond resolution, yet their application to 3D CT reconstruction has remained entirely unexplored. The core challenge is integrating asynchronous, pixel-level event streams—whose temporal structure is fundamentally different from synchronous frame projections—into a coherent volumetric reconstruction pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes two complementary inputs: (1) a small set of sparse X-ray projections (5–50 views) acquired at uniform angular intervals, providing absolute line-integral attenuation measurements, and (2) a synchronized asynchronous event stream where each event records pixel location, timestamp, and polarity, triggered when the log-intensity change exceeds a contrast threshold C. Events are consumed by accumulating polarities over randomly sampled angular intervals [t_a, t_b] to form accumulated event maps that encode relative angular-change information. The output is a continuous 3D volumetric attenuation field f(x) parameterized by an implicit neural network, from which projections at any angle can be rendered via numerical ray integration.

## Method

ENAF represents the 3D attenuation field as a continuous function f_Φ(x) = MLP(γ(x); Φ) using multi-resolution hash encoding (following NAF), and is trained self-supervised with a hybrid loss combining two terms. The projection loss enforces absolute scale by minimizing MSE between rendered and measured projections at the acquired angles. The key novelty is the event-based supervision: two angles θ_a and θ_b are randomly sampled, the predicted projection difference ΔÂ_{a,b} is rendered from the neural field, and its normalized spatial pattern is matched against the accumulated event map E_{a,b} over the corresponding time interval using a normalized MSE loss (borrowed from E-NeRF) that avoids requiring exact threshold calibration. This interval-based formulation makes the method robust to timestamp jitter and spurious events, since individual event errors are averaged out during accumulation. The approach requires no paired training data and operates entirely on the measured projections and events.

## Results

ENAF achieves the highest PSNR and SSIM across all four synthetic anatomical datasets (Chest, Abdomen, Foot, Jaw) at every tested sparsity level (5, 10, 25, 50 views). The advantage is most dramatic under extreme sparsity: on 5-view Chest, ENAF reaches 29.79 dB PSNR / 0.9330 SSIM, surpassing NAF (17.75 dB) by 12 dB and SAX-NeRF (20.52 dB) by 9.27 dB. At 50 views the gap narrows (e.g., Jaw: ENAF 35.54 dB vs SAX-NeRF 35.34 dB), but ENAF still matches or leads on most datasets. On the real-world lattice dataset, ENAF produces visibly sharper node structures and clearer lattice boundaries than all frame-only baselines at 5, 10, and 25 views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ENAF is compared against four baselines: two classical iterative methods (FDK, SART) and two state-of-the-art INR-based methods (NAF, SAX-NeRF). Against FDK and SART, ENAF wins decisively at every sparsity level; at 5 views the PSNR gap exceeds 10 dB on all datasets. Against NAF, ENAF consistently outperforms in all 16 dataset–view combinations, with the largest margin at 5 views (e.g., +12.04 dB on Chest, +6.89 dB on Jaw). Against SAX-NeRF, the closest competitor, ENAF leads by 9.27 dB on 5-view Chest and 6.76 dB on 5-view Jaw, but the gap shrinks to under 1 dB at 50 views (e.g., Jaw: 35.54 vs 35.34 dB; Abdomen: 35.11 vs 34.95 dB), where dense projections reduce the value of event-based angular-change supervision. On the real-world lattice dataset, ENAF produces visibly sharper node structures and clearer lattice connectivity than all four baselines at 5, 10, and 25 views, with the advantage most apparent at 5 views where FBP and SART show severe streak artifacts and NAF/SAX-NeRF exhibit blurred boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NAF (Neural Attenuation Fields) as its volumetric representation backbone, adopting the same multi-resolution hash encoding + MLP architecture for the 3D attenuation field. It extends the INR-based sparse-view CT line (NAF, SAX-NeRF) by introducing a second sensing modality—async event streams—as angular-change supervision, drawing the normalized MSE loss formulation from E-NeRF. Conceptually, it departs from the event-based 3D reconstruction literature (EventNeRF, E-NeRF, E2NeRF), which models optical radiance or scene appearance, by repurposing events to constrain X-ray line-integral differences rather than radiance values, a shift necessitated by the Beer-Lambert physics of CT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that neuromorphic event sensing can serve as a practical supplementary modality for X-ray CT, opening a new acquisition paradigm where microsecond-resolution angular-change information complements sparse frame projections. The 12 dB PSNR gain at 5 views is substantial, but the real-world validation is limited to a single lattice specimen under controlled synchrotron conditions, and synthetic events are generated from an idealized threshold-crossing model without sensor noise, dead pixels, or temperature drift. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to medical CT with lower contrast, whether dedicated event-camera acquisition protocols can be designed with explicit dosimetry, and whether the idealized event simulation faithfully captures the behavior of real X-ray event sensors at higher fluxes.
