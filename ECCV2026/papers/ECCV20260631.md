## Problem

Prior diffusion-based image restoration methods corrupt LQ inputs with random noise, fixed timesteps, or no corruption at all, creating a mismatch with the Gaussian-corrupted distribution on which pretrained diffusion models were trained. This misalignment prevents the model from fully exploiting its denoising prior, leading to unreliable score estimates during distillation and suboptimal perceptual quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260631/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality RGB image (512×512×3 during training, generated via ResShift degradation) and produces a restored high-quality image in a single diffusion step. All diffusion operations occur in the latent space of a pretrained VAE, and at inference the LQ input is 4× bicubic-upsampled before encoding (except for RealPhoto, which remains at original resolution). A fixed text prompt conditions the UNet denoiser, and the pipeline can optionally be iterated multiple times for additional perceptual refinement at the cost of increased distortion.

## Method

IAC-IR introduces an input-aware corruption strategy that predicts, per image, both an optimal timestep and noise to place the LQ latent on the pretrained diffusion trajectory. A lightweight 12-layer ViT estimator predicts the timestep by matching the residual noise (latent minus scaled clean component) to Gaussian statistics (mean, variance, skewness, kurtosis), while a LoRA-adapted UNet predicts the noise component, supervised self-supervisedly by requiring the frozen pretrained denoiser to recover it consistently. The encoder, timestep predictor, and LoRA-adapted UNet are jointly trained with cross-entropy timestep loss, noise-consistency loss, L1/VGG/adversarial restoration losses, and a novel input-aware score-based distillation loss that computes target scores using the predicted (rather than random) corruption, yielding more reliable gradients and more stable single-step distillation.

## Results

IAC-IR achieves the best no-reference perceptual scores (MUSIQ, CLIPIQA, MANIQA) across all six evaluation datasets. On RealSR, it reaches MUSIQ 69.85, CLIPIQA 0.709, and MANIQA 0.589, surpassing the next-best OSEDiff (69.09, 0.668, 0.476) and HYPIR (66.25, 0.638, 0.476). On the challenging RealPhoto benchmark (no bicubic upsampling), IAC-IR attains MUSIQ 74.09 and MANIQA 0.635, outperforming HYPIR (73.05, 0.581) by a clear margin. Distortion metrics (PSNR/SSIM) are competitive but not always the highest, reflecting the known perceptual-vs-fidelity trade-off; for example, on DRealSR DiffIR retains a higher PSNR (27.04 vs. 24.61) while IAC-IR leads on all perceptual scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IAC-IR consistently outperforms all five baselines on perceptual metrics (MUSIQ, CLIPIQA, MANIQA) across every dataset. Against the closest single-step competitor HYPIR, IAC-IR gains +0.95 MUSIQ and +0.054 MANIQA on RealSR, and +1.04 MUSIQ and +0.054 MANIQA on RealPhoto, where HYPIR is the strongest baseline. Versus OSEDiff, IAC-IR leads by +0.76 MUSIQ and +0.041 CLIPIQA on RealSR, and by +1.73 MUSIQ on RealSet80. InvSR, which also predicts noise but uses a fixed timestep and lacks explicit noise supervision, is outperformed by IAC-IR on all metrics for all datasets; the gap is especially large on RealPhoto where InvSR collapses (MUSIQ 30.17 vs. 74.09). DiffIR and SinSR, being multi-step or less degradation-aware methods, trail IAC-IR by 8–15 MUSIQ points on real-world benchmarks. On distortion metrics (PSNR/SSIM), DiffIR retains the highest values on DRealSR and RealSR due to its multi-step refinement, but IAC-IR remains competitive and surpasses all other single-step methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the line of single-step diffusion-based image restoration methods (SinSR, OSEDiff, InvSR, HYPIR) that fine-tune pretrained diffusion models (Stable Diffusion 2.1) with LoRA to restore images in one denoising step. Its key departure is the observation that these methods all rely on heuristic corruption—random noise, fixed timesteps, or no corruption at all—and instead learns input-specific timestep and noise via an indirect supervision scheme grounded in the Gaussianity of diffusion training. The score-based distillation component extends conventional SDS/DMD by replacing random corruption with the predicted input-aware corruption, yielding more reliable teacher scores. Architecturally, it introduces a lightweight ViT-based timestep estimator as a new module alongside the standard UNet+LoRA pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The input-aware corruption principle—that the optimal corruption for a given degraded image should be predicted rather than chosen heuristically—is a clean and generalizable insight that could transfer to other diffusion-based low-level tasks (inpainting, editing). The finding that strict KL-based Gaussianity enforcement is *worse* than a softer moment-matching objective, due to spatially heterogeneous degradations, is a useful practical lesson for anyone designing distribution-matching losses in restoration. The main open question the authors flag is the perceptual-vs-distortion trade-off: IAC-IR consistently wins on no-reference perceptual metrics but does not always maximize PSNR/SSIM, and a controllable mechanism to let users dial this trade-off remains future work.
