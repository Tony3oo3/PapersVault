## Problem

Multimodal CAD generation is fundamentally limited by scalability: design-history methods are confined to small annotated datasets (e.g., DeepCAD-160K, Fusion360-8K), while BRep topology is discrete and non-differentiable, preventing gradient-based learning on the millions of unannotated 3D meshes that already exist. No prior method can leverage large-scale mesh data for CAD geometry generation without explicit CAD-specific annotations.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts text prompts, single RGB images, or point clouds (normalized to [-0.5, 0.5] with optional surface normals) and produces a set of bicubic rational Bézier patches defined by 16 control points and 16 non-negative weights per patch, typically fewer than 10,000 patches per shape. These patches are differentiably tessellated into meshes at configurable resolution (4×4 to 16×16), and via a downstream topology-recovery stage (fine-tuned Qwen3-4B), the output can be exported as editable STEP files. Text-to-CAD operates in two stages: a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 3.5 generates an image from the prompt, which then conditions the image-to-CAD pipeline; inference takes ~15 s for image/point inputs and ~30 s for text inputs.

## Method

DreamCAD represents each CAD shape as a set of C⁰-continuous rational Bézier patches whose C⁰ continuity is enforced structurally rather than by optimization: a flood-fill algorithm extracts surface quads from a 32³ sparse voxel grid, and adjacent patches share boundary control points by construction. A Sparse Transformer VAE (following SLAT) encodes meshes—augmented with DINOv2 embeddings, normals, and SDF values from 150 rendered views—into per-voxel structured latents, which a decoder refines into the final parametric surface via bounded deformations (tanh) and softplus-weighted updates, trained end-to-end with Chamfer distance, G1 tangent continuity, Laplacian smoothness, and KL losses. For conditional generation, a coarse-to-fine pipeline uses two Flow Transformer decoders trained with flow-matching objectives: the first produces a coarse voxel grid from the conditioning input (DINOv2 for images, PointNet++ for points), and the second generates fine-grained SLAT features that the pretrained parametric decoder converts into the final surface. This design eliminates the need for BRep annotations or design-history logs, enabling training on 1.3M meshes from 10 public datasets and achieving state-of-the-art results across all three modalities.

## Results

On the in-distribution ABC benchmark, DreamCAD reduces Chamfer Distance to 0.93×10⁻³ in Point2CAD (68% lower than Cadrille's 2.98 and 75% lower than CAD-Recode's 3.73) while achieving an F1 of 92.12. For Image2CAD, it attains 76.12% GPT-5 and 77.03% human preference with CD of 4.12×10⁻³, versus 16.13% and 20.69×10⁻³ for BRepDiff. In the most challenging Text2CAD task, DreamCAD scores 85.62% GPT and 85.40% user preference with CD of 20.32×10⁻³, a 62% reduction over the closest competitor NURBGen (50.84×10⁻³). Out-of-distribution results on Objaverse confirm consistent gains across all three modalities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamCAD outperforms all eight baselines across every modality and both evaluation datasets. In Point2CAD, it reduces CD by 68% over Cadrille (0.93 vs 2.98) and 75% over CAD-Recode (0.93 vs 3.73) on ABC, with F1 jumping from 78.86 to 92.12; DeepCAD is an order of magnitude worse (CD 51.10). In Image2CAD, DreamCAD's 76.12% GPT preference dwarfs BRepDiff's 16.13% and Cadrille's 7.75%, while its CD of 4.12 is 80% lower than BRepDiff's 20.69. The text-to-CAD gap is the most dramatic: all baselines score below 5% GPT preference (NURBGen 4.21%, BRepDiff 4.34%, CADFusion 2.35%), whereas DreamCAD reaches 85.62%, and its CD of 20.32 is 62% lower than NURBGen's 50.84. On the out-of-distribution Objaverse set, DreamCAD maintains its lead (e.g., 87.31 F1 in Point2CAD vs 57.49 for Cadrille; 82.32% GPT in Text2CAD vs 8.18% for NURBGen), confirming that the gains are not dataset-specific. The authors note a data-scale disparity since baselines like DeepCAD and BRepDiff were trained on much smaller subsets, and they provide supplementary results on the DeepCAD dataset to partially address this.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamCAD departs from the dominant design-history and UV/BRep-diffusion paradigms (DeepCAD, BRepGen, BRepDiff) by decoupling parametric geometry learning from topology recovery. It builds on SLAT's structured latent encoding of sparse voxels [64] and differentiable tessellation of parametric surfaces [60], but repurposes them for a CAD-specific setting where rational Bézier patches with structurally enforced C0 continuity replace mesh or SDF outputs. The flow-matching generative framework [34] is applied in a coarse-to-fine two-stage pipeline (voxel grid → per-voxel latents → parametric surface), and the text-to-CAD path borrows the text-to-image-then-image-to-3D decomposition from recent text-to-3D work, fine-tuning Stable Diffusion 3.5 on the newly introduced CADCap-1M captions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the core scalability bottleneck in CAD generation: prior methods are confined to small annotated datasets (DeepCAD-160K, Fusion360-8K) because BRep topology is discrete and non-differentiable. By showing that point-level supervision on 1.3M meshes suffices to learn high-fidelity parametric surfaces, DreamCAD opens a path to leveraging the millions of unannotated 3D meshes already available. The 99.2% valid topology recovery via a lightweight Qwen3 fine-tune is a promising feasibility signal, but complete, semantically correct CAD topology (feature trees, parametric constraints, assembly relationships) remains explicitly open. CADCap-1M, as the first million-scale CAD captioning resource, is likely to become a standard training set for text-to-CAD research independent of this specific architecture.
