## Problem

While MLLMs excel at offline video understanding, no existing benchmark evaluates whether they can serve as real-time procedural coaches that continuously monitor execution, detect errors, and provide corrective guidance in a closed-loop streaming interaction. Prior procedural video datasets offer step-level annotations or post-hoc error labels but none support the full instruct-observe-correct cycle under realistic streaming constraints where the assistant must proactively decide when to intervene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260201/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each discrete timestamp, the model observes the most recent 60 seconds of video through a sliding window (bounding visual memory to enforce the online setting) together with the full accumulated textual dialogue history of all prior assistant utterances and their timestamps. The model must output either the single word 'Silent' or one of three structured responses: a next-step instruction, completion feedback, or corrective feedback specifying both the mistake and the fix. Two sampling strategies are supported: Dense (querying every 1 s) and Anchor-Based (querying at ground-truth intervention points plus an equal number of silent timestamps, the default).

## Method

GuideMe is constructed via a three-stage automated annotation pipeline that transforms four heterogeneous procedural datasets (EgoPER, CaptainCook4D, HoloAssist, QEVD) into 47,775 unified streaming interaction samples: (1) extracting ordered atomic actions categorized as correct, wrong, or correction; (2) generating task knowledge (descriptive names and canonical step sequences) via LLM prompting with parallel sampling (N=10) and second-round refinement; and (3) generating natural conversational dialogues aligned to action boundaries with timestamps. Evaluation uses a three-component framework: temporal-semantic bipartite matching that solves a min-weight assignment per video segment using combined cosine-similarity textual cost and Gaussian-decay temporal distance cost to yield soft precision/recall/F1; behavioral classification that categorizes each instance as Correct Silent, False Alarm, No Response, or Partly Correct to measure intervention timing; and LLM-as-a-Judge scoring (0–5 rescaled to 0–100) for content quality of matched pairs. The paper is a benchmark and empirical study rather than a new model, evaluating 12 proprietary, open-source, and streaming MLLMs zero-shot plus one fine-tuned variant.

## Results

On the GuideMe test set (473 videos, 9,876 interaction samples), no zero-shot model achieves reliable closed-loop coaching. Gemini 3 Pro attains the best balanced performance with sF1 = 36.5 and Score = 44.8, while Qwen3.5-397B-A17B reaches the highest Score (45.7) primarily through conservative silence (CS = 35.2) rather than accurate interventions (PC = 19.0). Fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B on GuideMe-Train improves sF1 from 35.7 to 39.7 and Score from 31.9 to 42.1, but still trails the best zero-shot proprietary models. Across all models, performance degrades sharply on Error and Correction categories (e.g., Gemini 3 Pro sF1 drops from 42.0 on Instruction to 33.8 on Error), exposing a fundamental gap in detecting and correcting execution mistakes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini 3 Pro leads on both sF1 (36.5) and Score (44.8), outperforming GPT-5.2 (sF1 32.5, Score 38.9) and Doubao-Seed-1.8 (sF1 36.8, Score 38.8); Gemini 3.1 Pro is slightly weaker overall (Score 37.8) despite the highest PC rate (49.9). Among open-source models, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B achieves the highest Score (45.7) but does so by remaining silent 35.2% of the time and correctly intervening at only 19.0% of anchored instances, whereas Qwen3-VL-8B and Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B intervene more aggressively (PC ≈ 50%) yet score lower (31.9 and 33.3) due to high false-alarm rates (FA ≈ 37%). Streaming-specialized models collapse: Dispider and MMDuet2 produce near-zero sF1 (< 1) with NR > 55%, while VideoLLM-online over-responds (FA 43.6, CS 0.0) and LiveStar scores lowest overall (Score 25.0). Fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B on GuideMe-Train improves sF1 to 39.7 (best overall) and Score to 42.1, but the model becomes more conservative (PC drops from 50.5 to 35.3, NR rises from 6.7 to 20.6), still trailing Gemini 3 Pro on Score. The per-category breakdown (Table 3) confirms that all models degrade on Error and Correction relative to Instruction, with Gemini 3 Pro sF1 dropping from 42.0 to 33.8/34.3 and Doubao from 49.6 to 41.9/42.7, indicating that error detection and corrective guidance remain the hardest sub-tasks across the board.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying four previously separate research threads—procedural video annotation (Epic-Kitchen, COIN, YouCook2), error-aware datasets (CaptainCook4D, EgoPER, HoloAssist, Assembly101), single-domain coaching (QEVD), and streaming MLLM evaluation (StreamingBench, OVO-Bench, ProactiveVideoQA)—into a single closed-loop streaming benchmark. Prior procedural datasets provide step-level instructions or post-hoc error labels but never model the full instruct-observe-correct cycle where the assistant's corrective guidance conditions the user's subsequent actions within the same session. The evaluation framework (temporal-semantic bipartite matching, behavioral classification, LLM-as-a-Judge) is designed specifically for the silence-versus-speak decision that no prior streaming benchmark addresses, departing from fixed-timestamp QA protocols used by StreamingBench and OVO-Bench.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a fundamental capability gap: current MLLMs can narrate procedures but cannot reliably coach them, with error detection and corrective guidance being the weakest links across all tested models. The finding that streaming-specific architectures (Dispider, MMDuet2) collapse to near-silence and that scaling (Qwen3.5-397B) does not improve temporal alignment challenges the assumption that more parameters or streaming pretraining solves proactive intervention. Open questions include how to close the error-detection gap, whether fully autonomous deployment (without ground-truth dialogue history) can approach the evaluated performance, and whether task-specific fine-tuning can generalize beyond the training domains.
