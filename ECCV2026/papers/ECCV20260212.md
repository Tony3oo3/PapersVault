## Problem

Raindrops and reflections frequently co-occur on glass surfaces (e.g., windshields), creating a composite degradation that prior raindrop-removal and reflection-removal methods cannot jointly address. The physical compositing model reveals that the two degradations are mutually coupled through a cross-term, so cascaded or all-in-one approaches that treat them independently fail. No real-shot dataset or dedicated pipeline existed for this unified task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260212/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image corrupted by both raindrops and reflections (captured at 4752×3168 in the RDRF dataset, resized to 1080×720 for training) and produces a clean restored image. A key design choice is that the low-quality input itself is fed as a condition alongside the restoration-stage output, since regions without raindrops or reflections in the LQ image are already clean and can be selectively preserved. The final output is generated in latent space via 50-step DDPM sampling and decoded by the VAE, followed by patch-wise color normalization against the restoration-stage result.

## Method

DiffUR³ is a two-stage pipeline: a DRSformer restoration stage produces an initial estimate I_s, which together with the original LQ image serves as dual conditions for a Stable Diffusion 2.1-based generation stage. The core architectural contribution is a Modulate&Gate module in the ControlNet-style control branch: a Modulate block uses cross-attention to align each condition latent with the timestep-varying noisy latent, and a Gate block applies spatial attention to adaptively weight between the LQ and restoration conditions at each spatial location. A second contribution is a Fidelity Encoder that shares the VAE encoder architecture and is trained independently to inject multi-scale structural features from I_s and I_lq into the VAE decoder via zero convolutions, correcting compression-induced distortions that the diffusion model cannot fix. The entire system is trained end-to-end on the newly collected RDRF dataset (9003 image pairs across 216 scenes) with supervised L1 loss for the fidelity encoder and standard diffusion training for the control branch.

## Results

On the RDRF-testing set (277 image pairs), DiffUR³ achieves PSNR 29.41 dB, SSIM 0.9372, LPIPS 0.0813, MUSIQ 74.72, CLIPIQA+ 0.6705, and HyperIQA 0.7046, ranking first on all six metrics. The best re-trained all-in-one competitor (DiffUIR†) reaches only PSNR 28.39 dB, a gap of ~1.0 dB. The best single-task method (DAI for reflection removal) scores PSNR 27.48 dB, and the best cascaded pipeline (UMAN+DAI) scores 27.50 dB, both substantially below DiffUR³. On the in-the-wild RDRF-wild set, DiffUR³ also produces visually superior results with fewer residual artifacts compared to all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffUR³ outperforms all 9 baseline categories on every metric. Against single-task raindrop removal methods (AGAN, UMAN), it gains 3.8–4.2 dB PSNR, confirming that dedicated raindrop removers cannot handle the coupled reflection component. Against reflection removal methods (RDNet, DSIT, DAI), the advantage is 1.9–3.0 dB PSNR, with DAI being the closest single-task competitor at 27.48 dB. Cascaded pipelines (e.g., UMAN+DAI at 27.50 dB) improve over single-task methods but still trail DiffUR³ by ~1.9 dB, validating the paper's argument that sequential processing overlooks the cross-term coupling between raindrops and reflections. Among all-in-one methods, the re-trained DiffUIR† (28.39 dB) is the strongest competitor, yet DiffUR³ still leads by 1.02 dB PSNR and by a large margin on no-reference metrics (MUSIQ 74.72 vs. 71.83, HyperIQA 0.7046 vs. 0.6311). A user study with 20 experts on 50 test images further confirms that DiffUR³ is preferred over all five compared alternatives in the majority of pairwise comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DiffBIR two-stage restoration-plus-generative-diffusion architecture and the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion framework, adapting them to a new composite degradation setting. It departs from prior raindrop removal (AGAN, UMAN, DRSformer) and reflection removal (RDNet, DSIT, DAI) literature, which treats each degradation in isolation, by formally modeling the coupled nested compositing structure where raindrop and reflection layers interact multiplicatively. The Modulate&Gate module and Fidelity Encoder are novel architectural contributions that address the specific challenges of multi-condition fusion and VAE compression artifacts, respectively, going beyond the simple channel-wise concatenation used in prior diffusion-based restoration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RDRF dataset is the first real-shot benchmark for the composite raindrop-and-reflection degradation, filling a clear gap where prior datasets contained only a single degradation type. The finding that re-trained all-in-one restoration methods (Histoformer†, DiffUIR†) still underperform the diffusion-based pipeline by a notable margin suggests the coupled degradation is fundamentally harder than simple superposition, motivating generative approaches. Open questions include whether the controlled-environment dataset generalizes to truly uncontrolled driving scenarios, how the method handles video sequences with temporal consistency, and whether the 'void information' regions (completely occluded by dense raindrops or intense reflections) can be recovered more faithfully than the current inpainting-like behavior.
