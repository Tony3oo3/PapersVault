## Problem

Existing monocular novel view synthesis methods either require task-specific fine-tuning with camera conditioning or enforce view alignment via stepwise hard denoising guidance, both of which disrupt the native generation process of pre-trained video models and produce artifacts with compromised global scene consistency. All prior approaches share the assumption that the noise-to-video mapping must be modified; this paper challenges that assumption by showing a valid NVS solution already exists within the pre-trained model's output data manifold and only needs to be located.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260032/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image or a monocular video clip (F frames) plus a user-specified target camera trajectory as input. Depth maps and source camera poses are recovered via VGGT or VIPE, and the source content is warped to each target view to produce a partial warped prior with a binary visibility mask. The output is a temporally coherent novel-view video rendered from the target trajectory, generated by the Wan2.2-I2V-A14B backbone in 15 denoising steps with a UNIPC solver.

## Method

NeoMap reframes NVS as searching for the optimal initial noise latent that, when decoded by a pre-trained flow-matching video model, naturally yields a view-consistent result. It constructs this noise through three modules: a Novel View Prior Module that warps source content via depth to establish geometric constraints; an Anchored Manifold Projection (AMP) module that projects the state toward the video data manifold via a one-step Euler approximation of the reverse flow and adaptively blends with the prior using a cosine-similarity mask; and a Pixel-Constrained Projection (PCP) module that decodes to pixel space, enforces the binary visibility mask to prevent spatial bleeding, and re-encodes to latent. The core mechanism is alternating projection between AMP (pulling toward the data manifold) and PCP (projecting back onto strict geometric constraints), which monotonically converges to the intersection of semantic realism and 3D view alignment. An auxiliary trajectory re-anchoring step in the first 3 denoising iterations prevents ODE integration drift, and the entire pipeline is training-free with no task-specific fine-tuning or generation guidance.

## Results

On Tanks-and-Temples, NeoMap achieves the best PSNR (15.25), LPIPS (0.486), FID-192 (3.71), FID-2048 (83.34), and CLIP-S (0.935), outperforming the strongest baseline FlexWorld (PSNR 14.66, FID-192 4.94) by a clear margin. On LLFF, NeoMap leads in FID-192 (3.59 vs. 12.55 for FlexWorld) and CLIP-S (0.953 vs. 0.930) while maintaining competitive camera accuracy (ATE 0.551 vs. 0.618). On the dynamic DAVIS benchmark, NeoMap achieves the best FID-192 (1.54), FVD (234.6), and ATE (0.534), roughly halving the FID-192 of the next-best method LanPaint (2.44) and reducing ATE by nearly 50%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeoMap outperforms all eight baselines on the primary visual quality metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, FID-192, FID-2048, CLIP-S) across both static (TNT, LLFF) and dynamic (DAVIS) benchmarks. Against the strongest warping-and-inpainting baseline FlexWorld [12], NeoMap gains +0.59 PSNR and reduces FID-192 from 4.94 to 3.71 on TNT, and from 12.55 to 3.59 on LLFF, while matching or exceeding its camera accuracy. The training-free guidance method NVS-Solver [62] and the inpainting baseline LanPaint [67] are clearly outperformed on FID and CLIP-S, confirming that stepwise latent blending during denoising degrades global scene consistency. Camera-controlled methods TrajectoryAttention [58] and ReCamMaster [3] suffer the largest visual quality gaps (FID-192 of 14.4 and 9.5 on TNT vs. 3.7 for NeoMap), reflecting overfitting to limited training trajectories. The noise-manipulation baselines (Reversed Noise [35], Flow Warped Noise [9]) perform worst overall, demonstrating that merely warping or inverting noise without manifold-aware projection is insufficient for NVS. On camera accuracy, NeoMap is competitive but not always best: FlexWorld achieves slightly lower ATE on TNT (0.005 vs. 0.008), while NeoMap leads on LLFF ATE (0.551 vs. 0.618) and DAVIS ATE (0.534 vs. 0.709 for ReCamMaster).

## Relation to Prior Work

NeoMap departs from both dominant NVS paradigms—camera-conditioned fine-tuning (TrajectoryAttention, ReCamMaster) and stepwise denoising guidance via warped priors (NVS-Solver, ViewCrafter)—by reframing the problem as locating the correct initial noise rather than modifying the generation trajectory. It builds on the deterministic ODE flow formulation from rectified flow / flow matching [35] and the depth-warping prior construction from NVS-Solver [62], but replaces trajectory-level guidance with an alternating projection between the video data manifold (via one-step Euler denoising) and a strict pixel-space geometric constraint (via VAE decode-encode blending). The noise-manipulation literature (inversion, flow-warped noise) provides the conceptual precedent that initial noise encodes structural layout, which NeoMap extends by adding explicit geometric anchoring and manifold projection for the unobserved regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating task-specific fine-tuning and denoising guidance entirely, NeoMap offers a practical path to NVS with off-the-shelf video generation models, avoiding the overfitting to limited camera distributions and the artifacts from hard stepwise constraints that plague prior methods. Key limitations include dependence on depth estimation quality (VGGT/VIPE), which degrades significantly in dynamic scenes, and validation on a single backbone (Wan2.2-I2V-A14B), leaving open whether the alternating projection framework generalizes across architectures. The auxiliary re-anchoring trade-off—where zero re-anchoring steps yield the best visual quality but worst camera accuracy—suggests that the Euler approximation error in the initial noise search is a non-trivial source of drift that future work could address with higher-order solvers or adaptive step strategies.
