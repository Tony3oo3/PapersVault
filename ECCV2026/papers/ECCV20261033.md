## Problem

Existing visual token pruning methods combine query relevance and token diversity within a unified selection objective, but under aggressive compression these goals conflict: relevance-driven selection overconcentrates the budget on correlated local evidence, while diversity-driven selection can displace indispensable query-critical tokens or retain visually distinct yet uninformative regions. No prior method provides an explicit mechanism to protect non-substitutable query evidence before diversity influences the remaining budget allocation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261033/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a visual token sequence (576 to 2,880 tokens depending on the VLM and input resolution) produced by a vision encoder from an RGB image or multi-frame video, together with a text instruction. It outputs a pruned subset of K visual tokens (e.g., 160 out of 2,880) restored to their native spatial/temporal order, which is passed directly to the unmodified language model. The framework is training-free and architecture-aware, computing relevance scores either in the pre-projector CLIP space (for CLIP-aligned models like LLaVA) or in the post-projector embedding space (for non-CLIP models like Qwen2.5-VL).

## Method

AnchorPrune is a two-stage, training-free pruning framework. Stage 1 constructs a protected relevance anchor by ranking tokens with an architecture-specific query-conditioned priority score (negated CLIP patch-text similarity for CLIP-aligned models; token-wise max cosine similarity in post-projector space for non-CLIP models), then adaptively determines the anchor size K_rel from the novelty profile of the relevance-ranked sequence using a patience parameter P and novelty threshold τ, so the anchor grows only while sufficiently novel candidates keep appearing. Stage 2 allocates the remaining budget K_ctx through greedy importance-weighted novelty: at each iteration it selects the candidate maximizing the product of a global importance prior (mean attention mass from a [CLS] token or all-to-all attention) and the anchor-conditioned novelty (minimum cosine distance to the current retained set). The multiplicative objective simultaneously suppresses visually distinct but uninformative tokens and globally important but redundant ones, ensuring each added token is both informative and complementary to the protected anchor.

## Results

On LLaVA-NeXT-7B (2,880 visual tokens), AnchorPrune achieves 99.4% relative performance retention with 640 tokens, 98.3% with 320 tokens, and 97.6% with only 160 tokens (94.4% compression), outperforming the strongest baseline (CDPruner) by 2.7, 2.4, and 4.7 percentage points respectively. On the compact LLaVA-1.5-7B (576 tokens), it retains 98.7%, 96.2%, and 93.9% at 128, 64, and 32 tokens. The method also generalizes to the non-CLIP Qwen2.5-VL-7B (80.8% at 64/1,296 tokens) and the video model LLaVA-Video-7B (94.1% at 512/2,704 tokens), consistently achieving the best aggregate retention at every evaluated budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnchorPrune outperforms all seven baselines at every evaluated budget across all four model architectures. The largest margins appear under severe compression: at 160/2,880 tokens on LLaVA-NeXT-7B, AnchorPrune (97.6% Rel.) exceeds CDPruner (92.9%) by 4.7 pp, VisionZip (86.9%) by 10.7 pp, and DivPrune (80.7%) by 16.9 pp. CDPruner, which jointly optimizes relevance and diversity via a log-det objective, is the strongest competitor at moderate budgets (e.g., 96.7% vs. 99.4% at 640 tokens) but degrades faster under aggressive pruning because diversity can displace query-critical tokens. VisionZip and DivPrune, which rely on query-agnostic saliency or diversity respectively, show the largest gaps on text-heavy benchmarks (TextVQA, DocVQA) where query-specific evidence is concentrated in small regions. On the non-CLIP Qwen2.5-VL-7B, AnchorPrune leads DivPrune by 1.6–3.5 pp and CDPruner by 4.8–6.2 pp across budgets, confirming that the anchoring principle is not tied to CLIP-aligned representations. On video (LLaVA-Video-7B), the advantage is narrower (0.5–2.4 pp) but consistent, with AnchorPrune best on EgoSchema and TempCompass where temporal grounding benefits from protected relevance cues.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnchorPrune carves new ground within the visual-token-pruning literature by identifying an ordering limitation in existing methods: saliency-based approaches (FastV, VisionZip) ignore query specificity, diversity-based methods (DivPrune) fragment locally coherent evidence, and unified relevance-diversity objectives (CDPruner) provide no explicit protection for indispensable tokens before diversity influences selection. Rather than building on a single prior formulation, the paper repurposes two well-known signals—query-conditioned relevance scoring and feature-space novelty—into a strictly sequential two-stage pipeline where a protected anchor is fixed before any contextual expansion occurs. The adaptive anchor budget, driven by a novelty-profile patience rule, departs from the fixed-ratio allocation used by most prior pruning methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, architecture-agnostic design makes AnchorPrune immediately deployable across CLIP-aligned and non-CLIP VLMs without retraining, and the 97.6% retention at 94.4% token compression on LLaVA-NeXT-7B demonstrates that severe visual-token reduction need not sacrifice task performance. Open questions include the sensitivity of the adaptive anchor to its hyperparameters (K_min, τ, P) across diverse query types, whether the negated-CLIP-similarity anchoring signal generalizes to future encoder designs, and whether the ordering principle extends to other compression settings such as token merging or quantization. The paper does not explore interaction with other efficiency techniques (e.g., KV-cache compression or speculative decoding), leaving the full efficiency stack uncharacterized.
