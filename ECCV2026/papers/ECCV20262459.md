## Problem

Existing dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting methods either use monolithic deformation MLPs that entangle geometry with motion (causing the 'moving target' optimization instability and geometric overfitting) or employ global time-basis factorizations that cannot span heterogeneous, localized non-rigid motion without catastrophic rank explosion. Both paradigms also critically depend on pre-computed SfM point clouds for initialization, which systematically fail on moving subjects, making these methods fragile in practice.

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular RGB video sequences and a random point cloud initialization (no COLMAP/SfM required), and produces a compact dynamic 3D Gaussian representation (~39K Gaussians, ~11 MB per scene) that renders novel views at >163 FPS. The key operational distinction is that the spatially-varying basis acts as an implicit neural scaffold, enabling high-fidelity geometry and motion recovery entirely from random initialization, and time-invariant caching of the basis vectors eliminates MLP overhead at render time, reducing deformation to a lightweight dot product.

## Method

MoBa-GS performs a structural inversion from a global Time-Basis to a Spatially-Varying Motion Basis: a low-frequency Base Motion Network (4-layer MLP, L=4) first learns a dynamic canonical space, then a time-invariant Basis Predictor (2-layer MLP) maps each static canonical position to K=8 local basis vectors forming a point-specific kinematic dictionary, and a time-varying Weight Predictor (8-layer MLP, L=10) outputs sparse blending weights conditioned on the dynamic state. The residual deformation is synthesized as a linear combination of these basis vectors, constraining motion to a locally low-dimensional manifold. Training is stabilized by three synergistic strategies: Motion-Guided Densification (allocating geometric capacity only where both view-space gradient and predicted motion magnitude exceed thresholds), Positional Annealing (progressively freezing canonical positions in late training to prevent overfitting), and Decoupled Alternating Optimization (alternating between deformation-network-only and geometry-only updates to break the moving-target feedback loop).

## Results

On the NeRF-DS dataset, MoBa-GS achieves the highest mean PSNR (24.14 dB) and SSIM (0.857) across all seven scenes, outperforming the closest competitor D-MiSo (23.90 dB, 0.851 SSIM). On HyperNeRF, MoBa-GS again leads with 22.73 dB PSNR and 0.63 SSIM, with the largest gains in the 'Banana' scene (25.62 dB vs. 25.22 for D-MiSo). The method also achieves a 16× training speedup over D-MiSo (~13 min vs. ~3 h 27 min) and real-time rendering at 163 FPS average with only ~11.3 MB storage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoBa-GS outperforms all six baselines on mean PSNR and SSIM across both NeRF-DS and HyperNeRF. On NeRF-DS, it beats the closest competitor D-MiSo by 0.24 dB PSNR and 0.006 SSIM, while D-MiSo retains a lower LPIPS (0.151 vs. 0.197); the authors attribute this to VGG-based perceptual metrics rewarding over-smoothed artifacts on motion-blurred scenes. Against Deformable-GS, MoBa-GS gains 0.38 dB PSNR and 0.009 SSIM on NeRF-DS and 0.67 dB on HyperNeRF. The gap over 4DGS is larger (1.60 dB PSNR on NeRF-DS), and MoBa-GS surpasses Motion-GS by 0.43 dB PSNR. On HyperNeRF, where deformations are more extreme, MoBa-GS leads D-MiSo by 0.26 dB and Deformable-GS by 0.67 dB, with the most pronounced advantage in the 'Banana' scene (25.62 vs. 25.22 dB). Notably, MoBa-GS also trains 16× faster than D-MiSo and renders at 163 FPS, whereas baselines require hours of training.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoBa-GS builds on the dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting lineage (Deformable-GS, 4DGS, SC-GS, D-MiSo, MotionGS) that extends static 3DGS to temporal scenes via learned deformation fields, and on motion factorization approaches like DynMF and K-planes that decompose 4D fields into structured components. Its key departure is a 'structural inversion': rather than a monolithic MLP predicting per-Gaussian deformations or a globally shared set of temporal basis trajectories, it learns a spatially-varying, per-point local motion dictionary (the Basis Predictor) combined with time-varying blending weights (the Weight Predictor), effectively replacing a global time-basis with a local space-basis. This is further coupled with training strategies—motion-guided densification, positional annealing, and decoupled alternating optimization—that have no direct analogue in prior dynamic 3DGS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of SfM/COLMAP point-cloud priors is practically significant, as SfM systematically fails on moving subjects and has been a persistent bottleneck for dynamic scene reconstruction; the implicit neural scaffold framing offers a principled explanation for why random initialization suffices. The 16× training speedup and real-time rendering (>163 FPS) with ~11 MB storage make the method far more accessible for iterative research and deployment. However, the quality gains over the strongest baseline (D-MiSo) are modest (~0.24 dB PSNR on NeRF-DS mean), and the LPIPS argument (attributing D-MiSo's lower LPIPS to VGG-based metric bias toward over-smoothing) is asserted rather than rigorously validated. Open questions include whether the spatially-varying basis generalizes to multi-object scenes with occlusion, whether the K=8 sweet spot holds for more extreme deformations, and how the method compares to the unreleased DynMF baseline.
