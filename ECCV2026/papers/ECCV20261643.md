## Problem

Existing trajectory-based intermediate representations for robot manipulation predict waypoints in 2D image space, suffering from intrinsic depth ambiguity, or lift 2D trajectories via depth back-projection (2.5D), which remains ill-defined in free space and sensitive to depth noise. This limits reliable 3D spatial reasoning and generalization of vision-language-action (VLA) models across new tasks and embodiments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261643/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB camera views, a natural-language instruction, and known camera intrinsics/extrinsics as input. The VLM outputs multi-view consistent 2D waypoints encoded as pixel-coordinate tokens in text format, which are then geometrically triangulated into a simplified 3D TCP trajectory in world coordinates. The resulting 3D waypoints can either drive a fixed top-down grasping and motion-planning controller directly, or be adaptively windowed and concatenated with robot proprioceptive state to condition a foundation VLA (π₀) for action prediction.

## Method

The core reformulation avoids direct 3D point prediction by decomposing the task into (1) predicting multi-view consistent 2D waypoints with a fine-tuned VLM and (2) recovering 3D positions via standard multi-view triangulation using known camera parameters. The VLM backbone (NVILA-2B/8B/15B) is trained in two stages: first on RoboPoint for pixel-level point accuracy, then on ~134k trajectories from RLBench, DROID, and RH20T where ground-truth 3D TCP trajectories are simplified via the Ramer-Douglas-Peucker algorithm and projected into per-view 2D supervision. Supervision uses the standard language-modeling objective with waypoints serialized as text tokens, preserving the VLM's vision-language priors without requiring custom regression heads. For policy integration, an adaptive temporal window of nearby 3D waypoints is selected based on the current end-effector position and concatenated with robot state, enabling π₀ to leverage spatially grounded motion cues during in-distribution fine-tuning without architectural modification.

## Results

On RLBench (10 rotation-invariant tasks), 3DWay-TD achieves 84.8% average success rate under the seen setting and 64.0% under the unseen setting, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline π₀.5 (61.5% seen, 18.8% unseen). On VLABench, 3DWay-TD attains a 37.7% average success rate across five generalization dimensions (ID, CC, CS, SI, UT), exceeding π₀.5 by 6.4 points. In the few-shot integration experiment (Table 2), augmenting π₀ with zero-shot 3DWay raises the average success rate from 17.6% to 38.1% with only 10 demos per task, and with few-shot 3DWay to 46.1%—matching the standard fine-tuned baseline that uses 50 demos per task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DWay-TD decisively outperforms all three foundation VLA baselines on every reported metric. Against OpenVLA-OFT, the gap is overwhelming: 84.8% vs. 8.8% on RLBench seen and 37.7% vs. 0.4% on VLABench average, indicating that OpenVLA-OFT's architecture is poorly suited to these manipulation tasks. Against π₀, 3DWay-TD more than doubles the RLBench seen success rate (84.8% vs. 39.6%) and more than quintuples the unseen rate (64.0% vs. 12.0%), while also leading on every VLABench dimension. Against the strongest baseline π₀.5, 3DWay-TD gains +23.3 points on RLBench seen, +45.2 points on RLBench unseen, and +6.4 points on VLABench average. The largest relative advantage appears in the unseen RLBench setting, where foundation VLAs suffer a sharp performance drop (π₀.5 falls from 61.5% to 18.8%) while 3DWay-TD retains 64.0%, underscoring its superior generalization to novel tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trajectory-based intermediate representation paradigm for robot manipulation (RT-Trajectory, HAMSTER, MOKA) by moving from 2D image-space trajectories and depth-lifted 2.5D waypoints to fully 3D consistent waypoints. The key conceptual departure is from depth-based 3D lifting—which suffers from free-space ambiguity and depth noise—to multi-view geometric triangulation, which enforces spatial consistency across views. The method builds on NVILA as the VLM backbone for pixel-level waypoint prediction (initialized via RoboPoint fine-tuning) and integrates with π₀ as the foundation VLA, treating 3D waypoints analogously to proprioceptive state in the action prediction pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

3DWay offers a practical bridge between the 2D discrete token modality of VLMs and the 3D continuous action space of robots, enabling both zero-shot execution on simple tasks and data-efficient fine-tuning of foundation VLAs. The demonstrated 2.6× improvement in π₀ success rate under 10-demo-per-task conditions and stable cross-embodiment transfer (AgileX PIPER → KUKA iiwa14) suggest a broadly applicable intermediate representation. Key limitations remain: no explicit orientation/SE(3) modeling restricts applicability to rotation-dependent tasks, the dual-view calibrated-camera assumption limits robustness in cluttered scenes, and the integration strategy is still preliminary. Open questions include whether self-calibrating triangulation and learnable waypoint-VLA fusion can close the gap to fully dexterous manipulation.
