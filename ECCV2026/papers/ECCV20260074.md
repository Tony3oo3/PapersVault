## Problem

Extending autoregressive (AR) models to layout-conditioned image generation is challenging because layout conditions are inherently sparse (bounding boxes with short descriptions) rather than dense visual cues, and a vanilla causal attention mask causes cross-object semantic interference that degrades spatial precision. Prior AR-based conditional generation methods either require architectural modifications or rely on dense spatially-aligned conditions like edge maps, leaving the unified-sequence paradigm underexplored for layout control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260074/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a global text prompt and a layout specification consisting of per-object bounding boxes (normalized top-left/bottom-right coordinates) paired with short textual descriptions, and produces a 512×512 RGB image. Layout tokens are constructed by concatenating the text-tokenized region description with Fourier embeddings of the box coordinates along the sequence dimension, then passing through a zero-initialized MLP with residual connection. The three token types (prompt, layout, image) are concatenated into a single sequence fed to the AR transformer, with image tokens progressively unmasked during next-set inference.

## Method

SMARLI introduces a structured attention masking strategy that governs interactions among prompt, layout, and image tokens without any architectural modification: layout tokens attend to the global prompt (Global Context Awareness), follow a causal mask within their own object (Intra-object Coherence), but are fully isolated from other objects' layout tokens (Inter-object Isolation); image tokens attend to all image tokens, the global prompt, and only the layout tokens of objects whose bounding boxes contain that spatial position (Region-wise Separation). For post-training, the authors adapt GRPO to the next-set paradigm by recording the retained token set and spatial positions at each step as a composite action, then replaying the state-action trajectory to compute per-step log-probabilities under identical contexts. A composite layout reward combines a VLM-based VQA score (semantic/attribute consistency), mIoU, and precision (from Grounding DINO detection) to penalize both misplacement and false positives, weighted together with an HPS v2.1 image-quality reward to balance fidelity and controllability during policy optimization.

## Results

On LayoutSAM-Eval, SMARLI (1.3B params) achieves 95.55 Spatial, 87.35 Color, 90.90 Texture, 89.82 Shape, 81.31 mIoU, and 81.84 Precision, outperforming all baselines on attribute and spatial metrics except a marginal 0.12 gap in Spatial vs. SiamLay-Flux (22B). On OverLayBench-Complex (overlapping layouts), SMARLI leads with 56.42 mIoU, 29.59 O-mIoU, 80.52 SRE, and 87.81 SRR, surpassing the next-best SiamLay-FLUX by 1.9 mIoU and 10.8 SRE. On T2I-CompBench, adding layout conditions to the base Show-o model improves Spatial adherence from 39.6 to 49.7 and Numeracy from 62.3 to 68.4, while also beating DALLE-3 on Color (79.6 vs. 77.9) and Texture (72.3 vs. 70.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMARLI (1.3B) decisively outperforms all diffusion-based baselines on attribute control: it leads Color (87.35 vs. 80.71 for SiamLay-Flux, 82.69 for PlanGen), Texture (90.90 vs. 83.53), and Shape (89.82 vs. 82.80) on LayoutSAM-Eval, while using roughly 1/17th the parameters of SiamLay-Flux (22B). On the harder OverLayBench-Complex with overlapping bounding boxes, SMARLI achieves 56.42 mIoU and 80.52 SRE, beating SiamLay-FLUX (54.50 mIoU, 69.72 SRE) and EliGen (52.53 mIoU, 74.03 SRE) by clear margins. The closest AR competitor, PlanGen (1.5B), trails SMARLI by 3.34 in Color, 4.37 in Texture, and 4.46 in Shape, and also scores lower on mIoU (78.04 vs. 81.31). On T2I-CompBench, SMARLI surpasses DALLE-3 on Spatial (49.7 vs. 28.7), Color (79.6 vs. 77.9), Texture (72.3 vs. 70.4), and Numeracy (68.4 vs. 58.8), though DALLE-3 retains a slight edge on Shape (62.1 vs. 59.2). The only metric where SMARLI does not lead is FID on LayoutSAM-Eval (18.16 vs. 16.12 for SiamLay-Flux and 13.91 for PlanGen), indicating a small gap in perceptual quality despite superior layout fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMARLI builds on Show-o's next-set-based autoregressive framework and adapts GRPO (originally from DeepSeekMath) to the visual generation setting. It departs from the dominant diffusion-based L2I paradigm (GLIGEN, InstanceDiffusion, SiamLayout) that injects layout via additional attention blocks or separate branches, and from ControlNet-style feature-space injection methods that require dense visual conditions. The closest prior AR work, PlanGen, also concatenates layout tokens into the input sequence but relies on vanilla causal masking; SMARLI's key conceptual departure is a structured attention mask that enforces inter-object isolation and region-wise separation without any architectural modification, combined with a layout-aware GRPO post-training stage that has not been explored for L2I generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 1.3B-parameter AR model with no architectural changes can match or exceed 22B-parameter diffusion models on layout control, suggesting that attention-level inductive biases (structured masking) are a viable alternative to parameter-heavy conditional branches. The GRPO adaptation to next-set-based AR models, with a composite layout reward (VQA + mIoU + precision), provides a reusable recipe for post-training AR image generators beyond layout. Open questions include scalability to higher resolutions (experiments are at 512×512), the dependence on external models (Grounding DINO, a VLM) for reward computation during training, and whether the structured masking strategy generalizes to other sparse conditional signals such as depth or segmentation maps.
