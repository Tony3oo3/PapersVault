## Problem

Universal medical image segmentation across diverse modalities is hindered by negative transfer: conflicting low-level visual cues (e.g., a dark region indicating malignancy in ultrasound but healthy fluid in MRI) cause feature collapse when naively jointly trained. Existing universal methods either require manual visual prompts, depend on the quality of retrieved reference images, or lack any structured intermediate representation to reconcile cross-modal ambiguities. The paper asks whether explicitly disentangling modality-agnostic geometric priors from modality-specific semantic concepts can eliminate this negative transfer without any inference-time prompts or references.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 384×384 medical image as input with no additional prompts, points, boxes, or reference image-mask pairs at inference time. It outputs foreground and background segmentation masks along with 9 regressed geometric attributes (bounding box, area, perimeter, aspect ratio, compactness, centroid, eccentricity, orientation, solidity). During training, the model also consumes MLLM-generated structured clinical reports (9 diagnostic dimensions from Qwen3-VL-Plus, encoded to 768-d embeddings via PubMedBERT) as supervision signals for the semantic tokens, but these are entirely absent at test time.

## Method

C2P introduces two sets of learnable tokens that are explicitly disentangled: [GEO] tokens supervised via MSE to regress 9 physical attributes (enforcing modality-agnostic shape/location priors) and [SEM] tokens aligned via cosine embedding loss to MLLM-derived clinical concept embeddings (capturing modality-specific texture and signal characteristics). A Style-Content Fusion Module injects modality awareness (from shallow style statistics and deep content features) exclusively into [SEM], preserving the universality of [GEO]. Bidirectional Cross Attention then lets tokens query image features for instance-specific evidence while simultaneously guiding pixel-level features with high-level priors. A Token-Guided Dynamic Head synthesizes per-sample convolutional kernels from the aggregated tokens and global image context, replacing a static segmentation head. At inference, a Geometry-Aware Consensus mechanism weights predictions from perturbed views by penalizing masks whose pixel-level area or centroid contradicts the regressed geometric expectations, effectively filtering unreliable outliers.

## Results

C2P achieves an average Dice of 88.22% across eight datasets spanning seven modalities, surpassing the best specialized model (RWKV-UNet, 87.38%) by 0.84% and the best prior universal model (Spider, 87.32%) by 0.90%. In zero-shot evaluation on unseen X-ray modalities, C2P reaches 87.60% Dice on Montgomery and 53.31% on Covidquex without any reference images, outperforming Spider (83.89%, 50.52%) and SR-ICL (67.00%, 46.80%). However, C2P fails on ISBI Electron Microscopy (26.79% Dice) due to a topological mismatch between its compact-lesion geometric prior and the filamentous neuronal structures in EM images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against specialized models, C2P (88.22% avg Dice) outperforms the strongest baselines RWKV-UNet (87.38%) and nnUNetV2 (86.68%) by 0.84% and 1.54% respectively, with the largest gains on Ultrasound BUSI (+0.30% over RWKV-UNet) and OCT AMD-SD (+0.62% over nnUNetV2). Among universal models, C2P surpasses the prior SOTA Spider (87.32%) by 0.90% and SR-ICL (87.25%) by 0.97%, with notable improvements on Polyp (+0.73% over Spider) and COVID-19 CT (+0.77% over Spider). In zero-shot transfer to unseen X-ray modalities, C2P (87.60% on Montgomery) beats Spider with 64 references (83.89%) by 3.71% and SR-ICL (67.00%) by 20.60%, demonstrating that prompt-free geometric priors generalize better than reference-based in-context learning under extreme domain shift. However, C2P loses badly on ISBI Electron Microscopy (26.79% vs. SR-ICL's 83.21% and Spider's 64.63%), as its compact-lesion geometric prior conflicts with the elongated neuronal topology in EM images. On unseen datasets within seen modalities, C2P matches or exceeds Spider and SR-ICL on TN3K (75.14 vs. 74.24/70.59), PH2 (90.89 vs. 90.47/90.70), and ACDC (71.87 vs. 63.26/59.95), but trails slightly on BUSBRA (83.39 vs. 85.16) and GlaS (53.99 vs. 56.42).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal medical image segmentation line (UniverSeg, Tyche, Spider, SR-ICL) by replacing prompt- or reference-based conditioning with explicit, disentangled concept tokens. It draws on dynamic/conditional convolution (Dynamic Filter Networks, Conditional Convolutions) for the Token-Guided Dynamic Head, on MLLM-based knowledge distillation (using Qwen3-VL-Plus and PubMedBERT) to inject clinical semantics, and on disentangled representation learning in cardiac imaging for the geometric-semantic split. The key departure from prior universal methods is the explicit geometric supervision (regressing physical attributes like area, centroid, perimeter) combined with a Geometry-Aware Inference Consensus mechanism that uses those regressed values as a self-check at test time, rather than relying on external references or iterative refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

C2P demonstrates that a single prompt-free model can match or exceed task-specialized nnUNetV2 across eight modalities while also generalizing zero-shot to unseen datasets and even an entirely unseen modality (X-ray at 87.6% Dice), suggesting that explicit geometric priors transfer across imaging physics in ways that pixel-level features do not. The honest failure on ISBI EM (26.79% Dice) reveals a fundamental tension: the compactness prior that enables cross-modal generalization actively conflicts with filamentous topologies, pointing to an open question about how to make geometric supervision topology-agnostic. For practitioners, the prompt-free, reference-free inference pipeline is more deployable than ICL-based alternatives, but the reliance on offline MLLM-generated reports and pre-computed geometric attributes adds a non-trivial preprocessing dependency.
