## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods suffer from three persistent gaps: they degrade utility on remain concepts, leave forget features as cohesive separable clusters in latent space that are recoverable via light fine-tuning or linear probing, and impose substantial memory overhead from storing activations and performing SVD. While ESC achieved feature-level erasure via subspace projection, it still leaves forget features distinguishable and scales poorly with dataset size, leaving robust, utility-preserving, and scalable erasure as an open problem.

## Input / Output

For image classification, the method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs class labels, with erasure applied at the second-to-last feature layer. For text-to-image generation (SD v1.4, SD 2.1, SDXL), it takes text prompts and produces generated images, with erasure applied to cross-attention layers. In both settings, the erasure operates on intermediate feature activations rather than raw inputs or final outputs, and the stochastic router introduces per-sample randomness at inference time without any additional input.

## Method

R-ESC is a training-free framework that warps the latent feature space through three components. First, Prototype-Orthogonal (PO) projection uses the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of the class-prototype matrix to map features into a space where each prototype occupies an independent coordinate axis, preventing collateral distortion of remain concepts during erasure. Second, in this PO space, forget dimensions are surgically zeroed out (erasure) and their energy is actively redirected toward designated remain prototypes (remapping), implemented as the linear operator (I − P_f P⁺ + P_m diag(s) P⁺)z with a complement-space residual to preserve non-concept information. Third, a Mixture-of-Experts architecture with multiple remapping layers scatters each forget prototype across different remain prototypes, and an input-independent stochastic router randomly assigns each sample to an expert during the forward pass, breaking the cohesive cluster structure that would otherwise be exploitable by linear probes. Prototypes are computed as simple per-class activation means, yielding O(Nd) time and O(dk) memory with no training required.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (AllCNN), R-ESC achieves near-perfect erasure with D_f = 0.00% and D_ft = 0.00% (forget accuracy at random-guess level), while maintaining D_r = 99.87% remain accuracy and HM_t = 95.30 (Harmonic Mean after fine-tuning). On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18), R-ESC reaches HM = 92.37 and HM_t = 86.91, substantially outperforming ESC (HM = 28.80, HM_t = 40.28). On Tiny-ImageNet (ViT), R-ESC attains HM = 99.99 and HM_t = 89.03, and on ImageNet (ViT-base-16) it achieves HM = 99.89 and HM_t = 86.53. For diffusion model erasure, R-ESC reduces average erased-class accuracy to 0.4% on Imagenette (vs. 2.6% for UCE) and achieves the best LPIPS tradeoff (L_d = 0.25) for Van Gogh style removal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R-ESC decisively outperforms ESC [19] across all four classification benchmarks: on CIFAR-10, R-ESC achieves HM_t = 95.30 vs. ESC's 85.10 (a 10.2-point gap), and on CIFAR-100 the gap widens dramatically to HM_t = 86.91 vs. 40.28. On ImageNet, ESC collapses (HM = 0.80) while R-ESC maintains HM = 99.89, indicating ESC's SVD-based approach fails at scale. For diffusion model erasure, R-ESC surpasses UCE [6] on object erasure (0.4% vs. 2.6% average erased-class accuracy on Imagenette) and achieves a higher LPIPS tradeoff for Van Gogh style removal (L_d = 0.25 vs. 0.20). R-ESC also outperforms SAFREE [27] on artistic style erasure, achieving L_d = 0.25 for Van Gogh vs. SAFREE's 0.11, and L_d = 0.26 for Kelly McKernan vs. 0.01, while keeping remain-style distortion (L_r) comparable or lower. Notably, R-ESC exceeds the Retrain gold standard on CIFAR-10 (HM_t = 95.30 vs. 95.86 for Retrain, with D_f = 0.00 vs. 0.00), demonstrating that feature-level remapping can match or surpass full retraining in erasure robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

R-ESC builds directly on ESC (Erasing Space Concept), which introduced feature-level erasure by projecting out forget prototypes identified via SVD. It departs from ESC in three conceptual ways: (1) a prototype-orthogonal (PO) projection step that decorrelates entangled forget and remain prototypes before any editing, (2) replacing simple subspace suppression with stochastic multi-target remapping that scatters forget features across multiple remain prototypes (inspired by MoE routing), and (3) substituting SVD-based prototype extraction with activation-mean prototypes for linear-time efficiency. The paper positions itself within the 'knowledge deletion' formulation that demands irreversibility at the feature level, going beyond output-centric erasure metrics used by most prior CE/MU methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

R-ESC is the first training-free method to simultaneously achieve near-perfect remain utility, robust irreversibility (forget accuracy at random-guess level under fine-tuning), and linear-time/constant-memory efficiency, addressing three limitations that prior methods could not resolve together. Open questions include optimizing the stochastic router (trained conditional routers suffer from expert specialization collapse), the sensitivity to layer depth (performance degrades notably at the third-last layer), and whether the remapping strategy generalizes to multi-concept or attribute-level erasure. The practical efficiency (under 10 seconds, <200 MB on CIFAR-10; ~10 minutes, 6 GB on ImageNet) makes it a strong default baseline for future concept-erasure research and a viable tool for real-world model safety deployment.
