## Problem

Unsupervised point cloud registration suffers from a self-referential collapse loop: geometric ambiguity in outdoor scenes produces noisy pseudo-labels that progressively corrupt the teacher, which in turn degrades student supervision. The paper reveals that registration networks natively develop semantic awareness correlated with registration accuracy, but this latent structure is fragile and rapidly eroded under noisy unsupervised supervision—a failure mode the authors term 'semantic collapse.' Existing semantic-aware registration methods either require segmentation models at inference time (adding overhead) or rely on hard class predictions that are brittle at boundaries, leaving the unsupervised regime largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of LiDAR point clouds P ∈ R^{m×3} and Q ∈ R^{n×3} and outputs a rigid transformation T = {R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R^3} aligning P to Q. During training, a frozen pretrained 3D segmentation model (e.g., SphereFormer) additionally provides per-point semantic embeddings (512-d) and class logits, but these are discarded at inference, yielding zero extra test-time cost. The underlying registration backbone is FCGF with RANSAC for transformation estimation, and progressive training gradually extends from adjacent (I=1) to distant frame pairs (I_max up to a predefined I_end).

## Method

CAESAR is a teacher-student framework that uses a frozen 3D segmentation model exclusively at training time to provide stable semantic anchors, preventing semantic collapse. For the teacher, Dual-Cue Guided Re-Matching (DCRM) shifts from rejecting noisy matches to reselecting the best correspondence from top-K candidates, scoring each by a product of geometric saliency (covariance eigenvalue ratio) and semantic similarity (entropy-weighted JSD plus embedding cosine). Semantic-Geometric Label Mining (SGLM) then refines the teacher via a compact per-batch adapter (0.07M params) with a residual extractor and gated point-wise refiner, optimized through an EM loop with a contrastive re-ranking loss (differentiable surrogate of average rank) and a volumetric regularizer, followed by multi-hypothesis transformation selection. For the student, Semantic Predictive Distillation (SPD) encourages contextual semantic reasoning by reconstructing a point's semantic embedding from its geometric neighborhood (with stochastic channel dropout and Gaussian noise) via a convolutional decoder, rather than directly mimicking high-dimensional semantic features.

## Results

On KITTI, CAESAR achieves 86.9% mRR and 61.3% RR@[40,50), outperforming the previous unsupervised SOTA INTEGER (84.0% mRR, 54.2% RR@[40,50)) by +2.9% and +7.1% respectively. On the sparser nuScenes benchmark, gains are far more pronounced: CAESAR reaches 79.5% mRR and 43.1% RR@[40,50), surpassing INTEGER by +16.4% mRR and +13.7% RR@[40,50). Cross-dataset transfer (trained on KITTI, tested on nuScenes) yields 74.1% mRR, exceeding INTEGER by +11.5%. Training time is reduced by 17.2% (62.9 h vs. 76.0 h for INTEGER) while testing time remains ~0.16 s/pair, identical to purely geometric methods since all semantic modules are discarded at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unsupervised methods, CAESAR dominates across all settings: on KITTI it improves mRR over INTEGER by +2.9% (86.9 vs. 84.0) and over EYOC by +3.7% (86.9 vs. 83.2); on nuScenes the margins widen dramatically to +16.4% over INTEGER (79.5 vs. 63.1) and +17.8% over EYOC (79.5 vs. 61.7), confirming that semantic anchoring is most critical for sparse, low-resolution LiDAR. Against supervised baselines, CAESAR (unsupervised) surpasses all inference-time-semantic methods (S²Reg, ML-SemReg) by large margins on nuScenes (79.5 vs. 43.5 mRR for ML-SemReg) while matching the inference cost of purely geometric models. On KITTI, CAESAR's 86.9% mRR is close to the supervised Predator (87.9%) but exceeds all other supervised baselines (FCGF 77.4%, D3Feat 83.0%, CoFiNet 82.1%). In the cross-dataset transfer setting (trained on KITTI, tested on nuScenes), CAESAR achieves 74.1% mRR, outperforming INTEGER by +11.5% and EYOC by +18.8%, indicating that stable semantic guidance yields more domain-generalizable features. Training efficiency also improves: CAESAR trains in 62.9 h versus INTEGER's 76.0 h (−17.2%), while testing time remains ~0.16 s/pair, identical to all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised teacher-student registration lineage established by EYOC (progressive training with spatial filtering) and INTEGER (full-teacher per-batch contextual mining), but departs fundamentally by introducing an external semantic anchor—a frozen 3D segmentation model used exclusively at training time. Prior semantic-aware registration methods (S2Reg, ML-SemReg) couple semantic models to the inference pipeline, incurring computational overhead and domain-fragility; CAESAR instead treats semantics as a training-time stabilizer that prevents the 'semantic collapse' the authors identify as a key failure mode. The conceptual shift from rejecting noisy matches to reselecting within candidate sets (DCRM) and the compact batch-specific adapter (SATR, 0.07M vs. 8.76M parameters) represent targeted departures from both the filtering paradigm of EYOC and the full-model adaptation of INTEGER.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-inference-overhead design makes semantic-guided registration practically deployable in autonomous driving stacks where adding a segmentation model at test time is prohibitive, and the demonstrated robustness to imperfect or out-of-domain semantic priors (including unsupervised sim-to-real models with 43% mIoU) broadens applicability beyond curated datasets. The identification of native semantic awareness in registration networks—and its fragility under noisy pseudo-labels—opens a conceptual thread: if semantic structure is intrinsic to the registration task, other external signals (e.g., depth, surface normals) might similarly serve as training-time anchors. Open questions include whether the approach extends to even sparser sensors (e.g., 16-beam), whether the EM-based batch refinement scales to very large scenes, and whether the semantic anchoring can be replaced by self-generated pseudo-semantic labels to eliminate the dependency on any external model entirely.
