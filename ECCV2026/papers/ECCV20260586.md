## Problem

Video foundation models now reach human-level accuracy on physical-reasoning benchmarks, yet accuracy alone cannot distinguish whether models perform human-like forward simulation or merely exploit statistical regularities in visible scenes. Two systems can achieve identical scores while making errors on entirely different samples, implying fundamentally different underlying strategies. This paper asks whether near-identical accuracy on the Physion benchmark reflects convergence to the same solution or a systematic divergence invisible to standard evaluation.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes 8 frames from 5-second Physion video clips (fractional step 5.75, ~1.5 s coverage) encoded by frozen ViT-L backbones, plus 89 physical simulation features extracted from HDF5 state trajectories, and produces binary contact predictions per sample. The key outputs are population-level distributions rather than single accuracy numbers: 8-seed prediction matrices, ~100-rater response matrices, three-level Cohen's κ values, per-sample entropy quadrants, and 37-dimensional Ridge-regression strategy fingerprint vectors. Both model instantiations (seeds) and human raters are treated as populations, enabling consensus, uncertainty, and strategy comparison that a single point estimate cannot support.

## Method

The core contribution is a distributional evaluation framework that treats 8 random probe initializations on frozen encoders and ~100 human raters per stimulus as populations, enabling three levels of analysis beyond aggregate accuracy. A three-level agreement hierarchy (κ_HH, κ_MM, κ_MH) computed via split-half bootstrap quantifies how much each population agrees internally versus cross-population; entropy decomposition classifies samples into four quadrants to expose model blind spots versus confident divergences; and strategy fingerprinting Ridge-regresses per-sample correctness vectors against 37 physical features, using pairwise cosine similarity to test whether models share a non-human strategy cluster. A fair comparison protocol standardizes V-JEPA2, VideoMAEv2, and DINOv2 to ViT-L with identical frame sampling, E2E attentive probes (depth 4, 16 heads), and hyperparameter grids, isolating architecture effects. Feature attribution with the expanded 89-feature set tests whether observable scene properties (Layers A+B) or unobservable outcome features (Layer C) predict model-human divergence, finding that observable features yield negative R² (−0.32) while unobservable features achieve R² = 0.29, consistent with divergence tied to forward simulation rather than scene encoding.

## Results

On the Physion benchmark (1,200 test samples, binary contact prediction), V-JEPA2 achieves 73.16% ± 0.95% test accuracy, within 1.0 pp of the human mean (74.2%), while VideoMAEv2 and DINOv2 reach 67.65% and 66.90% respectively. Despite near-human accuracy, V-JEPA2's model-human disagreement rate is 26.4% (5.5× the human-human baseline of 4.8%), with Cohen's κ_MH = 0.484 versus κ_HH = 0.906, revealing systematic behavioral divergence invisible to accuracy alone. On Physion++ (latent physical properties, 10 scenarios), V-JEPA2 reaches 70.44% accuracy but model-human disagreement rises to 50.1%, confirming the divergence is not an artifact of the original split.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three ViT-Large frozen encoders (V-JEPA2, VideoMAEv2, DINOv2) under a unified probing protocol (8 frames, identical E2E attentive probe, 8 random seeds) on Physion. V-JEPA2 outperforms VideoMAEv2 by 5.5 pp (73.16% vs. 67.65%) and DINOv2 by 6.3 pp (73.16% vs. 66.90%), with all differences significant (McNemar, p < 0.001). Against the human reference (74.2%), V-JEPA2 trails by only 1.0 pp, yet its model-human disagreement (26.4%) is 5.5× the human-human disagreement (4.8%), and κ_MH (0.484) is less than half κ_HH (0.906). Per-scenario, V-JEPA2 beats humans on geometric scenarios (linking +11.8 pp, collision +3.5 pp) but underperforms on future-dynamics scenarios (rolling/sliding −11.8 pp, dominoes −10.5 pp, clothiness −6.8 pp). DINOv2 (image-only) matches V-JEPA2 on clothiness and dominoes but collapses on linking (60.7% vs. 75.6%), indicating that temporal chain dynamics require video representations. All three models share a tight non-human strategy cluster (pairwise cosine 0.60–0.75) while none aligns with humans (cosine 0.27–0.34, all p > 0.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Physion benchmark's frozen-encoder probing protocol and human rater population, and adapts the 'beyond accuracy' error-consistency methodology of Geirhos et al. to physical reasoning. It departs from prior model-human comparisons that rely solely on aggregate accuracy by introducing a distributional evaluation framework that treats model seeds and human raters as populations, enabling consensus (Cohen's κ), uncertainty (entropy), and strategy (Ridge-regression fingerprinting) comparisons. Conceptually, it operationalizes the cognitive-science simulation-vs-pattern-matching debate by testing whether the locus of model-human divergence lies in visible scene encoding or in unobserved future dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that near-identical accuracy (73.2% vs. 74.2%) coexists with 5.5× higher model-human than human-human disagreement, structured by scenario type, challenges the common interpretation of 'human-level' scores in physical reasoning and motivates adopting agreement metrics alongside accuracy in benchmark reporting. The distributional framework is generalizable to any domain with per-sample human judgments and multiple model runs, making it a reusable evaluation tool. Key open questions include whether the divergence persists with fine-tuned or generative models, whether interventional experiments can confirm the causal role of forward simulation, and whether the scenario-structured pattern holds beyond the Physion item distribution.
