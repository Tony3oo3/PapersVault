## Problem

Existing unified dense prediction models assume all tasks are co-annotated on the same images, but in practice geometric, semantic, and intrinsic annotations come from fundamentally disjoint, domain-specific datasets (e.g., indoor RGB-D scans vs. internet images vs. synthetic renders). Forcing these into a single co-annotated set is infeasible, and pseudo-labeling pipelines are costly and must be re-run for every new task. The paper asks whether a single video model can jointly predict eight dense scene properties learned entirely from these disjoint sources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262484/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB video clip (or single image) as input and produces eight dense prediction maps simultaneously in one forward pass: depth, surface normals, semantic segmentation, instance boundaries, human parts, albedo, shading, and material segmentation. At inference, a parameter-free extended self-attention memory bank (configurable queue length M, default 16 frames) provides temporal context for streaming video, and the per-task decoders (VAE decoder + pixel projector) are shared across all tasks so the backbone is computed only once. Adding a new task requires only a new specialist and a new latent projector, with no architectural change or re-annotation of existing data.

## Method

UniD uses a two-stage distillation pipeline built on a pretrained Stable Diffusion v2 latent diffusion model. First, K task-specific specialists (U-Net backbone + single 3×3 pixel projector) are fine-tuned on their own disjoint datasets via single-step diffusion, leveraging the LDM's generative priors to generalize beyond each task's limited domain. Second, a unified U-Net backbone is trained to reconstruct all K task latents simultaneously through lightweight DPT-head latent projectors, supervised by the frozen specialists' latent outputs computed on-the-fly—eliminating pseudo-labeling and avoiding gradient propagation through the decoder. Temporal consistency is achieved via extended self-attention over a memory bank of past frames plus a temporal gradient matching loss that enforces frame-to-frame latent smoothness, propagating temporal coherence to tasks that lack video annotations. The key insight is that the diffusion backbone's internet-scale visual priors are sufficient to bridge the domain gaps between disjoint training sources, enabling robust generalization to scene-task combinations never seen during training.

## Results

On monocular depth estimation, UniD (G_φ) achieves AbsRel of 0.059 on NYUv2, 0.063 on ScanNet, and 0.301 on DIODE, outperforming all unified baselines (DINOv3-H: 0.092/0.089/0.314; DICEPTION: 0.060/0.071/0.304) and matching its own specialist. For surface normals, UniD achieves 14.6° mean error on ScanNet and 16.2° on NYUv2, the best among unified methods. On segmentation, the unified model shows a gap (37.8 mIoU on Cityscapes vs. 64.1 for the specialist), which a lightweight projector-only fine-tuning step closes to 58.4 mIoU. Temporal consistency (mVC-4/16) reaches 84.7/75.8 on normals (InteriorNet) and 94.5/85.2 on shading, surpassing all unified baselines by 6–13 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary unified baseline DINOv3-H (frozen backbone + DPT heads), UniD consistently wins on all OOD geometric and intrinsic tasks: depth AbsRel improves from 0.092→0.059 (NYUv2) and 0.089→0.063 (ScanNet); surface normal mean error drops from 16.8°→16.2° (NYUv2) and 16.0°→14.6° (ScanNet); albedo WHDR improves from 0.289→0.207; shading P@0.7 rises from 87.8→94.6; and boundary odsF increases from 55.0→57.2 (Mapillary). However, UniD loses to DINOv3-H on segmentation without fine-tuning (37.8 vs. 65.6 mIoU on Cityscapes), a gap that projector-only fine-tuning narrows to 58.4. Against DICEPTION, UniD wins on depth (0.059 vs. 0.060 NYUv2 AbsRel), normals (14.6° vs. 19.3° ScanNet mean), temporal consistency (84.7 vs. 68.0 mVC-4 on normals), and cross-task depth↔normal consistency (22.6° vs. 27.3° mean error). Against 4M-21-XL and StableMTL, UniD dominates across all geometric and intrinsic tasks by wide margins. Compared to its own per-task specialists, UniD matches in-domain performance and surpasses them on OOD benchmarks, with the unified model even outperforming the normal specialist on NYUv2/ScanNet/Sintel due to cross-task regularization from depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion v2) as a pretrained backbone, adopting their VAE encoder/decoder and U-Net architecture, but departs from iterative denoising by using a single-step forward pass for dense prediction. It extends the extended self-attention mechanism from streaming video translation and the temporal gradient matching loss from video depth estimation into a general multi-task latent formulation. The key conceptual departure from prior unified dense prediction methods (4M-21, DICEPTION, StableMTL) is the elimination of co-annotation or pseudo-labeling: rather than forcing all tasks into a shared annotation space or RGB output space, UniD distills task-specific latent embeddings from independently trained specialists into a unified backbone via lightweight DPT projectors, leveraging diffusion priors to bridge domain gaps between disjoint training sources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental practical bottleneck in unified scene understanding: real-world annotations are inherently fragmented across incompatible domains (RGB-D scans for geometry, manual labels for semantics, synthetic data for intrinsics), making co-annotated training infeasible. The latent distillation approach is naturally extensible—adding a new task requires only training one specialist and one projector, with no re-annotation of existing data—and the finding that temporal consistency propagates to tasks without video annotations through unified training has broad implications for video perception. A notable limitation is that ℓ₁ latent reconstruction is insufficient for classification-based tasks (segmentation), requiring a separate projector-only fine-tuning step to close the gap with specialists, suggesting that future work should explore objectives that better preserve high-frequency categorical structure in the latent space.
