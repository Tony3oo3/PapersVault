## Problem

Detector-free matchers (EDM, JamMA, LoFTR) achieve superior accuracy-latency trade-offs for pairwise matching, but their implicit keypoint selection—where keypoints emerge as a by-product of matching—produces different keypoint locations depending on the partner image, fragmenting tracks across views. This multi-view inconsistency renders standard object tracking, SfM, and SLAM pipelines unusable. Prior remedies rely on coarse quantization followed by heavy post-processing, restricting them to batch-based SfM and precluding incremental or real-time applications.

## Input / Output

Given an image pair (I_A, I_B) and a sub-pixel track location q_A in I_A (carried over from a previous frame), the method produces a matching probability map P_q over the fine-grid pixels of I_B (at stride Δ_f = 2 px within a κ×κ window) and a refined sub-pixel correspondence q_B. The method operates entirely at inference time on pre-trained detector-free matchers, requiring only the fine feature maps F_A, F_B already computed by the backbone; no retraining or architectural modification is needed. Iterating the procedure across a sequence of frames yields connected sub-pixel tracks, with an optional newborn-track mechanism seeding new tracks from high-confidence detect-by-pair-matching maxima to compensate for lost tracks.

## Method

DeT exploits the fact that the row-wise softmax of a detector-free matcher's local similarity matrix S represents the conditional matching probability from an anchor pixel to target pixels, given the true correspondence lies within the κ×κ crop. To construct the probability map P_q for an arbitrary sub-pixel query q_A, DeT steers the target crop center to a pseudo-track q_B^ps (computed by translating q_A by the offset of the nearest detect-by-pair-matching keypoint pair) and steers the anchor crop to q_A via bilinear interpolation of the fine feature map. The key enabling observation is the translational equivariance of the similarity matrix—softmax(S_d^{α,β}) ≈ softmax(S_0^{α−d,β})—which, extended to non-integer shifts, allows reading the probability map at the sub-pixel anchor as P_q = softmax(S_0^{q_A, q_B^ps}). The sub-pixel correspondence q_B is then obtained by argmax over P_q followed by the base matcher's standard regression head. This inference-time modification adds only ~2% FLOPs over the base matcher and can be plugged into off-the-shelf matchers (EDM, JamMA) without any retraining.

## Results

On multi-view pose estimation (AUC@5°), DeT-JamMA achieves 84.0/77.0/61.0 on IMC (Reichstag/Sacre Cœur/St-Peters) and 72.4/46.3 on MegaDepth (0015/0022), outperforming the SIFT+LightGlue baseline (69.0/81.8/60.7/46.3/48.2) on 4 of 5 sequences. In incremental SLAM, DeT-JamMA reaches 68.7/88.0/69.6/65.5/44.9 AUC@5°, surpassing SIFT+LightGlue (64.9/82.3/56.3/61.8/34.7) on all five sequences while registering over 4× more 3D points. In the tracking benchmark, DeT-JamMA retains more than 5× as many correct tracks as the SuperPoint+LightGlue pipeline over 5-frame sequences. The two-view ablation (Table 3) confirms that DeT preserves the base matcher's accuracy with only a ~2–3° drop in pose AUC@5° while gaining trackability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeT-JamMA decisively outperforms its own detector-free backbone without tracking (NN-JamMA) by 49–50 AUC@5° points across all sequences (e.g., 84.0 vs 35.0 on Reichstag), confirming that the multi-view inconsistency of vanilla detector-free matching is the dominant failure mode. Against the detector-based SIFT+LightGlue pipeline, DeT-JamMA wins on 4 of 5 sequences (Reichstag +15.0, St-Peters +0.3, MD-0015 +26.1) but trails on Sacre Cœur (77.0 vs 81.8) and MD-0022 (46.3 vs 48.2). Compared to SuperPoint+LightGlue, DeT-JamMA leads on every sequence, with the largest margin on St-Peters (+14.0) and MD-0015 (+14.4). DeT-EDM, while trackable, still lags behind both detector-based baselines on most sequences, suggesting that EDM's smaller fine-matching window and lack of a mixing function limit its tracking potential. In the two-view ablation (Table 3), DeT-JamMA retains 90.9% correct tracks (vs 88.9% for vanilla JamMA) with only a 2.7° drop in pose AUC@5°, indicating that the tracking constraint imposes a modest accuracy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the coarse-to-fine detector-free matching paradigm (LoFTR, EDM, JamMA), where keypoints emerge implicitly as argmax of a dual-softmax similarity matrix rather than from an explicit detector. It departs from prior attempts to make detector-free methods SfM-compatible (Detector-Free SfM, Dense-SfM), which relied on coarse grid quantization followed by transformer-based or Gaussian-splatting refinement, by instead exploiting the translational equivariance of the local similarity matrix to steer crops and produce a per-query connection probability map at inference time. The conceptual shift—from 'detect by pair matching' to 'detect by tracking'—repurposes an existing component (the row-wise softmax of the fine-level correlation) as a tracking signal, requiring no architectural changes or retraining of the base matcher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeT removes the primary barrier to deploying detector-free matchers in incremental SLAM, visual odometry, and object tracking: multi-view inconsistency that fragments tracks. The zero-shot, inference-time nature means practitioners can immediately plug DeT into off-the-shelf matchers (EDM, JamMA) without retraining, which is practically significant for the SLAM/VO community. Key limitations remain: the pseudo-track relies on a translational-motion assumption that breaks under depth discontinuities or non-rigid motion, the conflict problem in detector-free matchers caps the number of connected tracks in zoom-out sequences, and DeT introduces a small but measurable negative effect on two-view matching accuracy. Open directions include integrating RCM to raise the track-count ceiling, extending DeT to dense matchers (RoMA, DKM), and exploring loop-closure consistency.
