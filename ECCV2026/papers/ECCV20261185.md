## Problem

Existing multi-view multi-object tracking (MVMOT) methods and benchmarks assume fixed camera configurations with static view correspondence, failing to model the unconstrained dynamic flight of real-world UAV fleets where field-of-view coverage and overlapping view groups change unpredictably. This gap leaves tracking and identity matching under arbitrary, dynamically varying camera setups inadequately addressed, with no systematic study or benchmark for such scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261185/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized video frames from an arbitrary number C of UAV cameras (3–5 in MDMOT) at 1920×1080 resolution, where views may overlap or be fully independent and camera positions are unconstrained. It produces per-view tracklets (bounding boxes, category labels, and identity IDs) plus cross-view identity associations linking the same physical target across different camera feeds, yielding globally consistent trajectories. The system is designed to handle random target entry/exit, motion blur, and severe cross-view appearance discrepancies inherent to drone imagery.

## Method

FusionTrack replaces the decoupled track-then-associate pipeline with a unified end-to-end framework built on a DETR-style Transformer (ResNet-50 backbone + Transformer encoder/decoder) that jointly optimizes single-view tracking and cross-view re-identification. The core mechanism is bidirectional object fusion between a Tracklet Memory Pool (TMP), which buffers recent track queries for temporal continuity, and a Trajectory Identity Pool (TIP), which caches cross-view identity embeddings updated via a momentum rule; an Object Fusion Module refines track queries through cross-frame temporal attention with exponential time-decay and cross-view projection of TIP features back into the tracking space, forming a closed loop where tracking informs ReID and ReID informs tracking. For inference, a View-aware Hierarchical Clustering (VHC) procedure with mutual top-k neighbor filtering, a Neighborhood Filtering Mechanism enforcing spatial context consistency, and Sliding-Window Voting for temporal stability performs scalable cross-view association at O(N² log N) complexity. Training uses a staged schedule (single-view tracking → ReID introduction → full joint refinement) with an uncertainty-weighted combined loss that includes a bidirectional reconstruction regularizer to prevent feature-space collapse between tracking and identity embeddings.

## Results

On the proposed MDMOT benchmark, FusionTrack achieves 81.8 CVMA and 75.5 CVIDF1, outperforming the strongest prior method GMT (79.9/73.8) by +1.9 and +1.7 points respectively. Across four public multi-view benchmarks (CAMPUS, WILDTRACK, MvMHAT, DIVOTrack), FusionTrack attains the best CVMA and CVIDF1 on all four, with the largest gain on DIVOTrack (+4.3 CVIDF1 over GMT). For single-view tracking on MDMOT, FusionTrack achieves 88.75 MOTA, 93.06 IDF1, and 84.88 HOTA, ranking first among all Transformer-based trackers with the fewest ID switches (81) and missed tracks (19).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FusionTrack outperforms all 21 baselines on every evaluated dataset. On the proposed MDMOT benchmark, it surpasses the strongest prior multi-view tracker GMT [7] by +1.9 CVMA and +1.7 CVIDF1, and leads CrossMOT [13] by +3.3 CVMA and +2.6 CVIDF1. The gap widens substantially against ReID-only baselines: OSNet [42] trails by 31.7 CVMA, and AGW [36] by 32.5 CVMA on MDMOT, highlighting the limitation of decoupled track-then-associate pipelines. On public benchmarks, FusionTrack consistently wins: +2.2 CVMA over GMT on CAMPUS, +0.8 CVMA on WILDTRACK, +0.7 CVMA on MvMHAT, and a notable +4.3 CVIDF1 on DIVOTrack where identity preservation is most challenged. For single-view tracking on MDMOT (Table 3), FusionTrack leads all Transformer-based methods (MOTR [38], MeMOTR [11], COMOT [23], MOTIP [10]) in MOTA, IDF1, HOTA, and ID switches, though CNN-based trackers like HybridSORT [34] and OC-SORT [2] still edge it slightly on MOTA/MOTP, suggesting room for improvement in pure detection accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

FusionTrack extends query-based Transformer tracking (MOTR, MeMOTR) from single-view to multi-view settings, but its key departure is from the dominant two-stage track-then-associate paradigm in MVMOT. Rather than generating per-view tracklets and then linking them via ReID matching, homography, or matrix factorization, it jointly optimizes tracking and cross-view identity association through a bidirectional closed loop between a Tracklet Memory Pool and a Trajectory Identity Pool. The View-aware Hierarchical Clustering inference strategy with sliding-window voting and neighborhood filtering is a new post-processing pipeline that replaces standard Hungarian matching for cross-view association.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MDMOT benchmark (122K frames, 24.81M annotations, 3–5 drones across 6 urban scenes) is the first to model unconstrained, dynamically changing UAV camera configurations, filling a clear gap left by fixed-camera MVMOT datasets. The end-to-end formulation addresses cascaded error accumulation inherent in decoupled pipelines, and the 4.3 CVIDF1 gain on DIVOTrack suggests the joint optimization particularly benefits identity preservation under viewpoint change. However, the 3.9 fps inference speed on a single A800 limits real-time deployment, and the authors acknowledge that parallel tracking processing and robustness to extreme environmental perturbations remain open.
