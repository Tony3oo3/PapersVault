## Problem

Existing benchmarks for evaluating stereotype bias in Large Multimodal Models rely on synthetic images, single-actor scenes, limited bias taxonomies, or closed-ended multiple-choice formats, making it difficult to assess how LMMs allocate social status when forced to make comparative judgments over multiple actors in visually ambiguous real-world settings. BBQ-V addresses this gap by providing a comprehensive, real-image, multi-actor, open-ended evaluation framework spanning nine bias domains and 50 sub-categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262808/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark sample consists of a real-world multi-actor image (faces blurred via MediaPipe Gaussian blur, 51×51 kernel) paired with an anonymized ambiguous text context (identity and attribute cues scrubbed by a Visual Information Remover) and a bias-probing question. The model must produce a free-form rationale grounded in the image plus a final answer; an LLM judge (GPT-4.1-mini) then scores the response on five 1–5 ordinal dimensions (Fairness, Stereotype, Prior Bias, Ambiguity, Faithfulness), with the harmonic mean across all nine categories yielding the overall fairness score.

## Method

BBQ-V is built via a semi-automated pipeline that starts from 5,194 ambiguous context-question pairs in the BBQ text benchmark. A Visual Query Generator (GPT-4o-mini) converts each context into a simplified web-search query; retrieved images undergo three-stage filtering (PaddleOCR text-heaviness pruning, a CLIP + Qwen2.5-VL + GPT-4o-mini semantic-alignment committee, and synthetic/cartoon removal), yielding 4,497 real images and 14,144 image-question pairs. The multi-actor design is central: by placing two or more actors in a single scene under visual ambiguity, the benchmark elicits comparative social inference that single-actor setups cannot probe. Evaluation uses open-ended generation scored by an LLM-as-a-judge framework validated against human annotators (weighted κ = 0.81), and the paper further ablates blind-vs-vision, base-LLM-vs-LMM, model-scale, MCQ-vs-open-ended, and real-vs-synthetic-image conditions to isolate the sources of observed bias.

## Results

BBQ-V evaluates 19 LMMs using an LLM-as-a-judge framework (GPT-4.1-mini) scoring five dimensions—Fairness, Stereotype, Prior Bias, Ambiguity, and Faithfulness—on a 1–5 ordinal scale, reported as percentage fairness (harmonic mean across nine bias categories). The best proprietary model, Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite, achieves 80.89% average fairness, followed by GPT-4o at 75.38%. Among open-source models, Phi-4-MM-Instruct leads at 74.30%, while the three thinking/reasoning models (GLM-4.1V-Thinking, SophiaVL-R1, Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking) score notably lower at 53.58%, 65.51%, and 69.47%, respectively. The weakest model, LLaMA-3.2-Vision-11B, scores only 43.36%, and Physical Appearance is the hardest category for nearly all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite (80.89%) outperforms GPT-4o (75.38%) by 5.51 points on average fairness, with the gap widening in Race/Ethnicity (85.18% vs. 80.18%) and Socio-Economic Status (88.35% vs. 84.31%). GPT-4o-mini (61.53%) trails full GPT-4o by 13.85 points, showing that scale within the same family substantially reduces bias. Among open-source models, Phi-4-MM-Instruct (74.30%) leads, narrowly beating Phi-3.5-Vision (72.59%) and Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (72.00%), while LLaMA-3.2-Vision-11B (43.36%) and Molmo-7B (45.03%) are the weakest, falling 30+ points below the top open-source models. A striking finding is that all three thinking/reasoning models underperform their non-reasoning counterparts: GLM-4.1V-Thinking (53.58%) scores below even LLaVA-OneVision-7B (53.19%), and Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking (69.47%) trails Qwen2.5-VL-7B (65.53%) by only ~4 points despite being a later-generation model, suggesting reasoning chains amplify stereotypical associations. Within the Qwen family, Qwen2.5-VL-7B (65.53%) outperforms Qwen2-VL-7B (66.22%) by a narrow margin, while the 72B variant (reported in supplementary) further improves Race/Ethnicity to 79.25% versus 69.20% at 7B. The blind-vs-vision ablation confirms that visual input consistently degrades fairness scores (e.g., Phi-4-MM drops from 89.44% blind to 74.30% with vision), validating that BBQ-V specifically probes visually grounded stereotype reasoning rather than textual priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

BBQ-V is a direct multimodal extension of the text-only BBQ bias benchmark [55], inheriting its nine social bias categories and ambiguous context-question pairs but grounding them in real, multi-actor images. Prior vision-language bias benchmarks (VLStereoSet, VLBiasBench, SocialCounterfactuals, BiasDora, PAIRS, VisBias) each address a subset of the relevant factors—real images, multi-actor scenes, open-ended generation, or ambiguous context—but study them in isolation or rely on synthetic imagery. BBQ-V's key departure is combining all four factors simultaneously so that visually grounded stereotype reasoning can be tested directly, rather than attributing observed bias to a single confounded variable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark's scale (14.1k pairs, 9 domains, 50 sub-domains) and use of real multi-actor images make it a practical, deployment-relevant evaluation tool that the field will likely adopt as a standard for LMM fairness auditing. The finding that reasoning/thinking models (GLM-4.1V-Thinking, SophiaVL-R1, Qwen3-VL-Thinking) produce *more* biased outputs than their non-reasoning counterparts challenges the assumption that extended chain-of-thought improves social calibration, pointing to a concrete failure mode in RLHF-style reasoning training. The paper acknowledges a boundary: BBQ-V detects whether stereotype-driven assumptions are made under ambiguity or certainty but does not measure fine-grained calibration of biased beliefs under partial evidence, leaving open how to quantify and mitigate the *degree* of bias rather than its binary presence.
