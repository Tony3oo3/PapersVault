## Problem

Free-viewpoint driving scene synthesis requires simultaneously preserving geometric consistency at observed viewpoints and generating realistic content at unseen viewpoints, yet existing reconstruction methods blur off-trajectory views while generative methods distort geometry. Prior hybrid approaches that combine both paradigms rely on per-scene optimization or expensive annotations (LiDAR, bounding boxes), making them impractical for large-scale feed-forward deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260821/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse N-view RGB images from a single driving trajectory along with their camera intrinsics/extrinsics; depth maps are estimated on-the-fly via an off-the-shelf monocular model (Metric3D v2) rather than requiring LiDAR or 3D bounding boxes. It outputs a 6-frame RGB video at 768×432 resolution (preserving the original 1600×900 aspect ratio) rendered at free-viewpoint trajectories with lateral shifts up to ±4 m, alongside the underlying 3D Gaussian representation. The entire pipeline runs in a single forward pass with no per-scene optimization.

## Method

FreeGen couples a feed-forward 3DGS reconstruction branch (initialized from OmniScene, using a multi-view U-Net pixel encoder, Triplane Transformer with deformable attention, and Gaussian decoders) with a geometry-aware video diffusion refinement branch (initialized from Stable Video Diffusion). The reconstruction branch renders color, depth, and opacity maps via tile-based rasterization; these three channels form geometry conditions that are encoded by a learnable condition encoder and channel-concatenated with the noisy latent before being fed into the frozen-architecture diffusion model for noise prediction. The key architectural contribution is a bidirectional co-training loop: in Step 1, off-trajectory poses are rendered by the frozen 3DGS and refined by the frozen diffusion model to produce pseudo-labels that update the 3DGS (distilling generative priors into geometry); in Step 2, the updated 3DGS re-renders those off-trajectory views back to original-trajectory viewpoints, and the diffusion model refines them under the new geometric conditions supervised by original ground-truth frames (strengthening geometry-aware generation). This alternating scheme, with one branch frozen at a time and an auxiliary loss weight of 0.1, progressively aligns structural consistency and visual realism without test-time optimization.

## Results

On nuScenes off-trajectory evaluation, FreeGen (SVD backbone) achieves FID 9.47 / 11.25 / 14.30 and FVD 34.11 / 43.28 / 66.19 at ±1 m / ±2 m / ±4 m lateral shifts, outperforming the same-backbone baseline DiST-4D (FID 10.12 / 12.97 / 17.57; FVD 45.14 / 68.80 / 105.29) by 6–37% on FVD. On on-trajectory interpolation, FreeGen matches PVG's PSNR (23.34 vs 23.33 at dense) while reducing FID from 39.47 to 9.61 and LPIPS from 0.41 to 0.30, demonstrating that feed-forward reconstruction plus generative refinement preserves geometric fidelity without sacrificing realism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeGen outperforms DiST-4D [16] (same SVD backbone, image-warping condition) by 24–37% on FVD across all shift levels, confirming that feed-forward 3DGS geometry conditions are superior to image warping. Against the concurrent DiT-based methods, FreeGen beats PhiGensis [28] on every metric (e.g., FVD 34.11 vs 43.80 at ±1 m) and is competitive with WorldSplat [54], which relies on HDMaps and 3D bounding boxes: FreeGen achieves comparable FID (9.47 vs 8.25 at ±1 m) and better FVD at ±2 m (43.28 vs 47.41) without any spatial annotations. All reconstruction-only baselines (PVG, EmerNeRF, StreetGaussian, OmniRe) are far behind, with FVD values 2–8× higher, confirming that generative refinement is essential for off-trajectory realism. DIFIX3D+ [46] (per-scene 3DGS + SD-Turbo) and FreeVS [42] (LiDAR-conditioned SVD) are also substantially outperformed, with FreeGen reducing FVD by 76% and 85% respectively at ±2 m. On on-trajectory interpolation, FreeGen matches PVG [7] on PSNR/SSIM while achieving 4× lower FID (9.61 vs 39.47 at dense), showing that the co-training loop preserves geometric accuracy without the overfitting typical of per-scene reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeGen sits at the intersection of feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction (building directly on Omni-Scene [45]) and video diffusion-based generation (initialized from Stable Video Diffusion [3]), following the condition-encoding paradigm of DiST-4D [16]. It departs from prior hybrid pipelines (DIFIX3D+, ReconDreamer, DriveX, FreeSim) that require per-scene optimization by making both branches feed-forward, and from methods using non-learnable conditions (LiDAR projections in FreeVS, image warping in DiST-4D) by constructing learnable geometry conditions from the 3DGS rendering itself. The key conceptual departure is the bidirectional co-training loop, where generative priors are distilled into the reconstruction model and refined geometry in turn supervises the generation model, rather than the unidirectional 'reconstruct-then-refine' design of concurrent works like WorldSplat and PhiGensis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreeGen demonstrates that high-quality free-viewpoint driving scene synthesis can be achieved in a single forward pass without per-scene optimization or expensive annotations (LiDAR, 3D bounding boxes), which is practically important for scalable closed-loop simulation and large-scale data generation for autonomous driving. The co-training strategy offers a general recipe for bridging reconstruction and generation that could transfer to other 3D content creation tasks. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (nuScenes) and a single driving domain, and the co-training procedure adds significant training complexity (alternating two models, two stages each). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-driving scenes, longer video sequences, or more extreme viewpoint extrapolation beyond the tested ±4 m lateral shifts.
