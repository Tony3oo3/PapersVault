## Problem

Existing Generative Face Video Super-Resolution (GFVSR) methods treat restoration as full generation from pure noise, incurring expensive multi-step sampling and suffering from a 'distribution generation trap' where over-pursuit of perceptual metrics produces unnatural artifacts and low fidelity. The face-specific structural properties of LQ inputs (preserved low-frequency information, well-defined subject areas) remain underexploited, and no prior work provides an interpretable, objective mechanism for determining how much generative sampling is actually needed given the degree of information loss.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality face video clip degraded by real-world distortions (blurring, noise, frame drops) and produces a high-quality face video at 512×512 spatial resolution. The LQ is consumed in two parallel forms: as VAE-encoded latent tokens and as DINOv3-extracted visual features sampled at the VAE's temporal downsampling intervals (first and every third frame among four). The Discriminative Guide, a lightweight ViT, additionally predicts a per-pixel information-loss map and a low-frequency residual from the LQ latent, which together determine the dynamic starting point of the generative sampling.

## Method

DTI reformulates GFVSR as input-driven directional restoration on a pretrained Wan2.1-1.3B DiT backbone (fine-tuned with LoRA for only 20k iterations). A novel enhancement-and-injection conditioning mechanism concatenates pure noise tokens with LQ tokens and DINOv3 feature tokens into a single sequence, using bidirectional self-attention within the condition subsequence (computed once per forward) and unidirectional cross-attention from noise to conditions at selected DiT blocks, preserving flow-matching consistency while reducing compute. A Discriminative Guide (lightweight ViT) is trained with objective SNR-alignment supervision to predict both a local information-loss level (yielding a starting timestep via t = RMSE/(1+RMSE)) and a coarse low-frequency residual, enabling the sampling to begin from a dynamically determined intermediate latent z_start = (1−t_start)·z_anchor + t_start·ε rather than from pure noise. A hyperparameter λ linearly interpolates between the predicted timestep and t_max=1, providing explicit control over the perception-distortion trade-off. This design reduces NFE by ~76% on average while improving full-reference fidelity metrics across all benchmarks.

## Results

On VFHQ, DTI achieves the best full-reference scores across all metrics: PSNR 26.35, SSIM 0.74, LPIPS 0.17, IDS 0.87, LMD 4.20, and TLME 3.72, outperforming all five baselines. On CelebV-HQ, it similarly leads with PSNR 25.52, SSIM 0.75, LPIPS 0.22, LMD 5.26, and TLME 4.01. With the Discriminative Guide (DG), NFE drops from 50 to 12 (76% reduction) while PSNR further improves to 26.85, at the cost of slightly lower no-reference scores (MUSIQ 65.17 vs. 71.54 without DG). On VoxCeleb2 (no-reference only), DTI scores MUSIQ 68.41 and CLIP-IQA 0.67, trailing Vivid-VR (73.87) and FlashVSR (72.57) on MUSIQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTI decisively outperforms all five baselines on every full-reference metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, IDS, LMD, TLME) across both VFHQ and CelebV-HQ, with the largest margins over the generative methods Vivid-VR (PSNR +6.53 on VFHQ) and SeedVR2 (PSNR +6.55), and a more modest but consistent lead over the discriminative PGTFormer (+0.72 PSNR) and SVFR (+0.81 PSNR). On no-reference metrics, FlashVSR leads on MUSIQ (75.33 vs. 72.54 on VFHQ) and CLIP-IQA (0.71 vs. 0.56), which the authors attribute to FlashVSR's large-scale post-training on a 160k closed-source dataset; Vivid-VR similarly leads on VoxCeleb2 MUSIQ (73.87 vs. 68.41). Among the multi-step generative baselines (SVFR at 80 NFE, Vivid-VR at 50 NFE), DTI matches Vivid-VR's 50 NFE without DG but reduces it to 12 NFE with DG while simultaneously improving PSNR from 26.35 to 26.85, demonstrating a clear efficiency-fidelity advantage. The one-step methods (SeedVR2, FlashVSR) achieve competitive no-reference scores but sacrifice fidelity substantially (PSNR ~19.6–19.8 on VFHQ vs. 26.35 for DTI), confirming the perception-distortion trade-off the paper discusses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Wan2.1-1.3B DiT backbone and flow-matching diffusion framework, but departs fundamentally from prior GFVSR methods (Vivid-VR, FlashVSR, SeedVR2) that treat super-resolution as a full conditional generation task. It reformulates GFVSR as an input-driven directional restoration, introducing a novel enhancement-and-injection conditioning mechanism (using DINOv3 features alongside LQ tokens) that replaces heavier alternatives like ControlNet or concatenation-MLP. The Discriminative Guide, trained via objective SNR alignment to estimate the appropriate diffusion starting timestep, extends the SNR-based noise-level estimation idea from prior vision work into a supervised, interpretable training paradigm for restoration guidance — a formulation absent from earlier generative VSR approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 76% reduction in NFE (from 50 to 12) while simultaneously improving fidelity metrics demonstrates that dynamic trajectory initialization is a practical path toward efficient generative video restoration without the large-scale post-training that competitors like FlashVSR require. The controllable perception-distortion trade-off via the λ hyperparameter offers practitioners a useful knob for deployment. However, the DG is trained from scratch on a single dataset (VFHQ, 15k iterations), limiting its generalizability to unseen degradation distributions, and the authors acknowledge that leveraging pretrained ViT-like models for the DG remains unexplored. The empirical finding that LPIPS best captures the perception-distortion trade-off in this setting is a useful but narrow evaluation insight.
