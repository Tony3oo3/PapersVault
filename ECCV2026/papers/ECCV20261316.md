## Problem

Existing aerial geo-localization systems handle only single retrieval directions (e.g., text-to-drone or drone-to-satellite), and universal multimodal retrieval paradigms that rely on textual instructions for cross-modal alignment fail in this domain because AMGL data forms dense, fine-grained sub-distributions where coarse global mean/variance shifts are insufficient to reconstruct the distinct distribution structures needed for precise cross-view alignment. Additionally, prior multi-view geo-localization methods require strictly aligned tri-view-text data, limiting deployment flexibility.

## Input / Output

The system accepts drone images, satellite images, and text descriptions as query or candidate data, with a textual instruction (e.g., 'Find the satellite image of the place shown in this drone photo') prepended to the query to specify retrieval direction. Visual inputs are resized to 384×384 and text truncated to 256 tokens before being processed by a frozen Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B backbone to produce d-dimensional embeddings. The output is a ranked list of candidates from a test pool, evaluated via Recall@K (R@1, R@5, R@10) across six directed retrieval sub-tasks spanning UAV-text, satellite-text, and UAV-satellite image pairs.

## Method

REFL formulates cross-view alignment as a two-stage conditional distribution transport process built on Flow Matching and FlowBind. In Stage 1 (Velocity-Prior Flow Learning), with the MLLM backbone frozen, per-view Gaussian statistics are extracted by a prior encoder and fused via inverse variance weighting to compute a shared latent anchor z*; parameterized MLP velocity fields are then trained to fit ODE trajectories from z* to each view's feature distribution, with the loss decoupled into a flow-matching term (t∈(0,1]) and a t=0 term to prevent trivial solutions. In Stage 2 (Trajectory-Guided Embedding Rectification), the backbone is fine-tuned via LoRA (r=64, α=128) while the query embedding is transported through reverse ODE integration (t: 1→0) to the latent manifold and then forward ODE integration (t: 0→1) along the target view's velocity field, producing a rectified query feature that is matched against the static candidate embedding via InfoNCE contrastive loss; the joint objective combines the ER-CL loss with a weighted flow regularization term (λ=0.5) using 16 ODE integration steps.

## Results

On the Aerial MVGL Benchmark (10 sub-tasks spanning drone/satellite image-text and cross-view retrieval), REFL achieves an average R@1 of 44.83% and R@10 of 65.85%, setting a new state of the art. The strongest baseline, Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B, reaches 40.77% R@1 and 62.29% R@10, so REFL improves by +4.06% R@1 and +3.56% R@10. Cross-view image retrieval (Tasks 5–6) yields the highest recalls (up to 99.89% R@10 on SUES-200), while cross-modal image-text retrieval (Tasks 1–4) remains the most challenging, with R@1 ranging from 14.23% to 23.85%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REFL outperforms all five baselines on every sub-task and in aggregate. Against the strongest general-retrieval baseline Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B (40.77% R@1), REFL gains +4.06% R@1 and +3.56% R@10, with the largest per-task margin on Task 2 (image→text, +5.54% R@1). VLM2Vecv2-2B (37.85% R@1) and XVLM_fs (39.57% R@1) trail REFL by 6.98% and 5.26% R@1 respectively, indicating that instruction-tuned universal retrievers without explicit distribution transport struggle with fine-grained aerial scenes. CLIP_fs (25.05% R@1) and the multi-view GeoBridge (12.04% R@1) are substantially weaker; GeoBridge's reliance on strictly aligned tri-view-text pairs limits it to 12.04% R@1 versus REFL's 44.83%, a 32.79-point gap. Notably, REFL also surpasses Qwen3-VL on zero-shot transfer to four unseen benchmarks (ERA, RSICD, RSITMD, DenseUAV), achieving 19.74% average R@1 versus 17.46%, confirming that the flow-based training does not degrade out-of-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Flow Matching and FlowBind frameworks—originally developed for generative modeling and cross-modal generation—into a retrieval alignment setting, using ODE-based velocity fields to transport query embeddings across distribution domains rather than relying on a static shared space. It departs from the universal multimodal retrieval paradigm (UniIR, MM-EMBED, VLM2Vec), where textual instructions provide only coarse global semantic biases, by explicitly modeling fine-grained non-linear distribution transformations via continuous flow trajectories. It also moves beyond GeoBridge's requirement for strictly aligned tri-view-text data by supporting directed source-to-target retrieval without joint multi-view constraints, positioning aerial geo-localization as a directed cross-domain transport problem rather than a symmetric matching task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Aerial MVGL Benchmark consolidates six previously fragmented aerial retrieval sub-tasks under a single evaluation protocol, filling a gap in standardized multi-view geo-localization assessment. The flow-based transport mechanism offers a principled alternative to instruction-conditioned alignment that could generalize to other fine-grained retrieval domains where distribution shifts are subtle and non-linear. However, the approach is validated only on a 2B-parameter backbone with 16 ODE integration steps, and the ~4% R@1 improvement over the strong Qwen3-VL-Embedding baseline, while consistent, is moderate; whether the flow transport overhead is justified at scale or in latency-sensitive deployment remains an open question. The finding that Stage 1 pre-training alone degrades zero-shot discriminability (C1 vs. C0) also raises questions about the stability of flow-learned priors when decoupled from contrastive supervision.
