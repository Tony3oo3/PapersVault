## Problem

Existing LVLM bias benchmarks rely on attribute-inferring prompts (e.g., 'Is this person a CEO or a secretary?') that are frequently refused by safety-guardrailed models such as GPT and Claude, with refusal rates reaching 80–100% on popular benchmarks. This makes statistical bias measurement unreliable or impossible for the most widely deployed models. The paper asks how to evaluate societal bias in LVLMs in a way that is agnostic to safety guardrails.

## Input / Output

The method takes face-centric images from FairFace (annotated with gender and race) and attaches them as provisional user context via a brief textual prefix ('I've attached my photo.'), paired with person-irrelevant prompts that never ask about the depicted person. Outputs are free-form text (stories, explanations, or multiple-choice answers) from 20 LVLMs, which are then parsed by an LLM assistant (Qwen3-32B) into structured attributes or difficulty judgments. The final output is a Total Variation Distance (TVD) bias score per prompt, aggregated into overall gender and racial bias scores per task.

## Method

The core mechanism decouples the evaluation task from the depicted person through two design changes: (1) replacing attribute-inferring prompts with person-irrelevant ones (e.g., 'Write a fictional story about an imaginary person,' 'Teach me about linear algebra in math,' MMLU multiple-choice questions), and (2) reassigning the image's role from prompt target to provisional user information that implicitly provides demographic cues. Under the hypothesis that an unbiased model's outputs for person-irrelevant prompts should be statistically independent of user demographics, bias is quantified as the TVD between output distributions across demographic groups. The framework is instantiated across three tasks of increasing constraint—story generation (most open-ended), term explanation (constrained to a specific term), and exam-style QA (predefined answers)—to probe different facets of bias. This design achieves zero refusals across all 20 evaluated models, including strongly guardrailed proprietary systems, enabling reliable bias measurement where prior benchmarks fail.

## Results

The proposed guardrail-agnostic method achieves 0% refusal rate across all 20 evaluated LVLMs, compared to 35–100% refusal rates on four prior benchmarks (SBBench, ModScan, VLA-gender, Pairs). On the bias scores (TVD × 100, where 0 = no bias), story generation shows the highest disparity (open-source avg ≈ 29.29, proprietary avg ≈ 18.99 for gender), while exam-style QA shows the lowest (1.66 vs. 0.90). GPT-5 achieves the lowest overall bias (14.53/16.80 gender/race in story generation; 0.50/0.36 in exam-style QA), while LLaVA-1.6-7B exhibits the highest gender bias in story generation (47.81). All models, including the most safety-aligned proprietary ones, still show non-negligible societal bias across every task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against four prior LVLM bias benchmarks—SBBench [Narnaware et al., 2025], ModScan [Jiang et al., EMNLP 2024], VLA-gender [Girrbach et al., ICLR 2025], and Pairs [Fraser et al., EACL 2024]—on the critical metric of refusal rate. The proposed method achieves 0% refusal across all tested models (Qwen2.5-VL-32B, Gemma3-27B, InternVL3.5-38B, GPT-5, Claude 3.7 Sonnet), while the prior benchmarks suffer 35–100% refusal rates (e.g., SBBench: 80–100%, ModScan: 49–98%, VLA-gender: 71–98%, Pairs: 35–81%). This means the proposed method is the only one that can reliably evaluate strongly guardrailed proprietary models like Claude 3.7 Sonnet (which refuses 81–100% of prior benchmark prompts) and GPT-5 (49–97% refusal). The comparison is not about which method yields lower bias scores, but about which method produces enough valid outputs for statistical analysis; the prior benchmarks fail this requirement for most modern models, rendering their bias measurements unreliable or impossible. The paper also notes that captioning-style prompts (used in some prior work) avoid refusals but introduce contextual confounds from non-person image features, a limitation the proposed person-irrelevant design avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in LVLM bias evaluation, where prior benchmarks (SBBench, ModScan, VLA-gender, Pairs) use attribute-inferring prompts that ask models to identify or describe the depicted person's attributes. The key conceptual shift is decoupling the evaluation task from the person: images become provisional user context rather than the prompt target, and prompts become person-irrelevant (story generation, term explanation, exam QA). This design draws on persona-based LLM analysis for the user-context framing and on Total Variation Distance for quantifying distributional disparities, but the specific combination—using face images as implicit demographic cues while probing bias through tasks that never reference the person—is novel. The paper also builds on the FairFace dataset and MMLU benchmark as evaluation substrates, repurposing them in a fundamentally different protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework resolves a practical dead-end: as safety guardrails strengthen across both proprietary and open-source LVLMs, the standard attribute-inferring evaluation paradigm becomes inapplicable (refusal rates of 49–100% in Table 1), leaving the field without a reliable way to measure bias in the most widely deployed models. The finding that all 20 models exhibit demographic-dependent outputs even on person-irrelevant tasks, and that bias does not generalize across tasks (correlations as low as −0.11), challenges the assumption that a single bias score captures a model's fairness profile and motivates multi-task, continuous monitoring. Open questions include whether the framework extends to other demographics (age, disability), whether the LLM-assistant-based scoring introduces residual bias at scale, and whether the observed proprietary-vs-open-source gap narrows as open-source models adopt sustained post-deployment monitoring.
