## Problem

Current Remote Sensing Foundation Models (RSFMs) pursue generalization by scaling single monolithic backbones to hundreds of millions or billions of parameters, incurring prohibitive computational costs, large carbon footprints, and limited accessibility for academic or operational users. This paper asks whether an ensemble of lightweight, independently trained specialist encoders can instead produce a balanced generalist feature extractor that matches or exceeds the performance of much larger models while remaining modular, prunable, and federated-learning-friendly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262297/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts multi-band remote sensing images with heterogeneous channel layouts (3–14 bands spanning RGB, SAR, multispectral Sentinel-2, and infrared-Red-Green composites). A spectral-aware band adaptation layer maps the available input channels to each encoder's expected training bands using a priority scheme (exact band-name match → same center-wavelength → nearest-wavelength within modality → cross-modal fallback), so encoders trained on different sensors can all process the same input. A UperNet decoder attached to the frozen ensemble produces task-specific outputs: segmentation masks, scene class labels, canopy-height regression values, or change-detection maps.

## Method

EoS-FM composes 22 ConvNeXtV2-Atto encoders (3.4 M parameters each), each independently fine-tuned in a supervised manner on a distinct dataset, task, and modality (17 datasets, 4 modalities, ~1.09 M samples total). At inference, a differentiable top-k selection layer assigns a learnable scalar weight to each encoder, regularized by a top-k-tail-ℓ₁ penalty that encourages sparsity; in the full model all 22 encoders contribute (k=N), while the compact EoS-FM Small variant sets k=6 to retain only the six most relevant encoders per task, reducing the backbone to 22 M parameters. Selected feature maps are fused by a single 1×1 convolution to a target channel size, then passed to a task-specific decoder. For any new downstream task, all encoders remain frozen and only the selection weights, fusion layer, and decoder are trained, making the system trivially extensible (new specialists can be added without retraining existing ones) and naturally compatible with federated or distributed training.

## Results

On the 11-task Pangaea benchmark, EoS-FM (76M params) achieves the best Average Distance To Best (Avg DTB) of 4.58, on par with TerraMind Large (303M, 4.65) while using roughly 4× fewer parameters. The compact EoS-FM Small variant (22M, k=6 encoders) reaches 5.13 Avg DTB, still outperforming all other RSFMs. Under a 10%-label regime, EoS-FM further improves to 3.67 Avg DTB, the best among all tested models, while EoS-FM Small (7.78) even surpasses the full ensemble on several individual datasets (HLS Burn Scars, MADOS, Sen1Floods11). EoS-FM ranks first on FiveBillionPixels, HLS Burn Scars, and SpaceNet 7 Change Detection, and second on AI4SmallFarms and xView2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EoS-FM (76M) achieves the best Avg DTB of 4.58, narrowly beating TerraMind Large (303M, 4.65) and substantially outperforming CROMA (303M, 8.05), DOFA (112M, 9.41), and Scale-MAE (303M, 13.11). Only EoS-FM and TerraMind surpass the fully supervised UNet baseline (6.01), closing the gap between frozen-encoder transfer and task-specific training. On AI4SmallFarms, EoS-FM scores 44.1 mIoU versus 27.47 for TerraMind and 26.86 for Prithvi, making it the only frozen model to approach the supervised UNet (46.34). The compact EoS-FM Small (22M, k=6) reaches 5.13 Avg DTB, outperforming all 87M–303M RSFMs (CROMA 8.05, GFM-Swin 10.97, S12-DINO 10.78) with roughly 4–14× fewer parameters. Under 10% labels (Table 3), EoS-FM's advantage widens to 3.67 Avg DTB versus 5.36 for Thor Base and 5.72 for TerraMind Base, while EoS-FM Small (7.78) still beats CROMA (8.79) and all S12 variants. EoS-FM is the only model to report results on all 11 Pangaea tasks (including xView2 and BioMassters), whereas TerraMind and Thor omit two datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes classical model ensembling from a task-level performance technique into a representation-level mechanism for building foundation models. It departs from the dominant RSFM paradigm of jointly pretraining a single large backbone (Scale-MAE, CROMA, TerraMind) by instead composing 22 independently trained ConvNeXtV2-Atto specialists, each fine-tuned on a distinct dataset and modality. The learned top-k selection layer draws conceptual inspiration from Mixture-of-Experts routing, but unlike MoE the experts are trained sequentially on different tasks rather than jointly, and the fusion operates on frozen encoder features rather than gating within a single network. The band-adaptation module is inspired by SMARTIES' spectrum-aware projection, but applied here to align heterogeneous inputs to pre-trained specialists rather than to a unified encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EoS-FM offers a concrete, resource-efficient alternative to the ever-larger RSFM trend: a 76M-parameter ensemble matches a 303M model (TerraMind Large) on the Pangaea benchmark while requiring roughly 5× fewer FLOPs, and a 22M pruned variant remains competitive. The modular design naturally supports federated training, incremental specialist addition, and task-specific pruning, making it attractive for collaborative and edge-deployment settings in Earth Observation. Key open questions include disentangling genuine cross-task generalization from specialist-coverage overlap with the evaluation suite, enabling standalone feature extraction without prohibitively large concatenated representations, and validating whether the approach scales beyond 22 encoders or to truly unseen modalities.
