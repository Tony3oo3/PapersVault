## Problem

Active view selection for X-ray CT reconstruction using Gaussian Splatting remains largely unexplored. Existing methods such as FisherRF are designed for natural-light scenes and rely on surface occlusions and view-dependent color parameters to estimate uncertainty, but X-ray imaging operates under the Beer-Lambert law with purely linear, occlusion-free ray integrals and isotropic attenuation, causing the diagonal Fisher Information Matrix approximation to produce severely biased information gain estimates and redundant view selections.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of initial 2D X-ray projections (H×W images) and a pool of 448 candidate scanner poses uniformly sampled across two concentric hemispheres (all optical axes oriented toward the volume center), then iteratively selects one next-best view per step until a target budget (24 or 36 views) is reached. The internal representation is a radiative Gaussian Splatting model with M primitives parameterized by density, 3D position, and 3D covariance. Outputs include the selected view sequence, a progressively refined 3D volumetric density field, and rendered projections for both tomographic reconstruction and novel view synthesis evaluation.

## Method

Built on the R2-Gaussian radiative Gaussian Splatting framework, the key insight is that low-density Gaussian primitives (bottom α=10% by density) correspond to underconstrained boundaries, needle-like artifacts, and background noise, making them natural proxies for epistemic uncertainty. Rather than training N independent ensemble models, the Perturbed Gaussian Ensemble stochastically scales the density of these vulnerable primitives by a factor (1+ε) where ε ~ Uniform(−β, β) with β=0.5, generating N=10 perturbed density fields from a single trained model. For each candidate view, the method renders projections through all N perturbed ensembles, computes SSIM between the base and each perturbed projection, and selects the view whose SSIM scores exhibit the highest sample variance as the next best view. This structural-variance criterion is preferred over pixel-wise L1 or PSNR because SSIM's luminance and contrast normalization decouples absolute intensity shifts (inevitable under linear X-ray integration) from genuine geometric disagreement, yielding a more discriminative uncertainty signal.

## Results

On the synthetic 24-view protocol, the proposed Perturbed Gaussian Ensemble achieves 34.08 dB 3D PSNR and 0.896 3D SSIM, surpassing the best 3D-based baseline FisherRF (33.35 dB / 0.887) by 0.73 dB and outperforming all rule-based and 2D-based heuristics. Under the 36-view protocol, the gap widens to 0.68 dB in 3D PSNR (36.23 vs. 35.55 dB). On the real-world FIPS dataset, the method leads by 0.22 dB 3D PSNR over FisherRF at 36 views (37.48 vs. 37.26 dB) and by 0.19 dB at 24 views (36.40 vs. 36.21 dB), while also achieving the best 2D novel-view synthesis PSNR of 46.85 dB (synthetic, 36-view).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks against three categories of baselines: rule-based heuristics (Random, Uniform, FPS), 2D-based no-reference IQA metrics (TOPIQ, MANIQA, MUSIQ), and the 3D-based FisherRF. The proposed method wins on every metric across all four evaluation settings (synthetic/real-world × 24/36 views). Against FisherRF, the closest 3D-based competitor, the gains are 0.73 dB 3D PSNR (synthetic 24-view) and 0.68 dB (synthetic 36-view), narrowing to 0.19–0.22 dB on real-world data. Notably, FisherRF actually underperforms the simple FPS heuristic on the synthetic 24-view setting (33.35 vs. 33.51 dB), which the authors attribute to the diagonal Fisher Information Matrix approximation breaking down under X-ray's transmissive, highly-coupled ray geometry. All three 2D-based IQA methods (TOPIQ, MANIQA, MUSIQ) cluster within 0.5 dB of each other and consistently trail the 3D-based approaches, confirming that pixel-level quality scores are insufficient proxies for volumetric information gain. The rule-based baselines (Random, Uniform, FPS) serve as lower bounds, with FPS being the strongest heuristic but still 0.5–0.7 dB below the proposed method on synthetic data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on R2-Gaussian [80] for the underlying radiative Gaussian Splatting representation, adopting its rectified projection model and training losses. It departs from the gradient-based active view selection paradigm (FisherRF [25], ActiveNeRF [55]) by arguing that the diagonal Fisher Information Matrix approximation and view-dependent color assumptions are fundamentally incompatible with X-ray physics, where the Beer-Lambert law produces linear, occlusion-free, isotropic projections that strongly couple Gaussians along each ray. Instead of backward-propagated information gain, the paper introduces a forward, sampling-based Perturbed Gaussian Ensemble that stochastically scales the densities of low-confidence primitives and measures structural disagreement via SSIM variance in projection space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck in dose-reduced CT: even with fast radiative GS reconstruction, the quality ceiling is set by which views are captured, and no prior method was designed for the transmissive, isotropic physics of X-ray imaging. The finding that FisherRF underperforms a simple FPS heuristic in X-ray settings highlights a broader caution against transferring natural-light active learning heuristics to tomographic modalities. Open questions include whether the density-guided perturbation strategy generalizes to 4D/dynamic CT, whether it extends to other attenuation-based modalities (e.g., ultrasound), and whether the modest gains (≤0.7 dB) translate into clinically meaningful diagnostic improvements at very low view budgets.
