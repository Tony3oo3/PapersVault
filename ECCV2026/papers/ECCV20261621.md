## Problem

Long video generation with diffusion transformers suffers from spatiotemporal inconsistencies because the fixed attention window causes frames outside it to be effectively forgotten. Existing RNN-based memory solutions (Mamba, TTT) introduce training-inference gaps and, in chunk-wise autoregressive modes, force all pixel-level continuity across chunk boundaries to be carried solely through compressed hidden states, leading to visible degradation. The paper asks whether a simpler recurrent architecture combined with a better autoregressive paradigm can resolve these issues.

## Input / Output

The model receives a context video (a sequence of initial frames) and a corresponding action sequence (e.g., keyboard/mouse inputs for Minecraft or navigation commands for the maze), both encoded in latent space via a pretrained VAE. It autoregressively generates subsequent frames one at a time using a sliding window of size C, producing a longer video that maintains consistency with the context. The action sequence is injected via adaLN into both attention and RNN blocks, enabling interactive, action-conditioned generation.

## Method

RAD inserts an RNN block (LSTM, Mamba2, or TTT) after the temporal attention in every DiT layer, operating along the temporal axis to compress and retrieve global memory beyond the attention window. The key architectural choice is frame-wise autoregressive inference with a sliding window of stride 1, so consecutive frames share overlapping attention context and pixel-level details propagate directly through attention rather than through hidden states. To make frame-wise training tractable, a hidden-state pre-fetching scheme first runs the RNN sequentially over clean frames to cache all hidden states, then computes attention in parallel across the full sequence using those cached states, eliminating the training-inference gap. Systematic ablations show that LSTM's explicit separation of short-term (previous output) and long-term (cell state) memory gives it an edge in chunk-wise mode, while in frame-wise mode all RNN variants converge because attention handles local continuity and the RNN focuses on global signals.

## Results

On the Minecraft dataset, frame-wise RAD with TTT achieves the best PSNR of 16.72 and LPIPS of 0.47, outperforming the Diffusion Forcing baseline (PSNR 15.65, LPIPS 0.53) and VRAG (PSNR 16.11, LPIPS 0.50). On the Memory Maze dataset, chunk-wise RAD with LSTM achieves the highest PSNR of 15.64 and SSIM of 0.43, while frame-wise RAD with LSTM reaches LPIPS 0.45, matching VRAG's 0.43. Across both datasets, frame-wise autoregression with overlapping sliding windows consistently outperforms chunk-wise variants, with the gap being most pronounced on the visually complex Minecraft scenes (e.g., Mamba2-f PSNR 16.70 vs. Mamba2-c PSNR 12.72).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diffusion Forcing (DF), frame-wise RAD with any RNN type (LSTM, Mamba2, or TTT) achieves substantially better PSNR on Minecraft (16.59–16.72 vs. 15.65) and lower LPIPS (0.46–0.47 vs. 0.53), confirming that the RNN memory mechanism adds meaningful long-range consistency beyond what the sliding-window attention alone provides. Against VRAG, which explicitly retrieves historical frames as context, frame-wise RAD matches or slightly exceeds its PSNR on Minecraft (16.59–16.72 vs. 16.11) while achieving lower LPIPS (0.46–0.47 vs. 0.50), and on Maze the two methods are comparable (LSTM-f LPIPS 0.45 vs. VRAG 0.43). Notably, chunk-wise RAD variants (all RNN types) underperform both DF and VRAG on Minecraft, with Mamba2-c dropping to PSNR 12.72, demonstrating that non-overlapping windows impose an unrealistic compression burden on the hidden state for visually dense scenes. The key takeaway is that the frame-wise sliding window with stride 1 is the critical design: it allows attention to carry pixel-level local continuity while the RNN focuses on global memory, a division of labor that no baseline replicates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DiT-based latent video diffusion framework by inserting RNN memory blocks (LSTM, Mamba2, or TTT) after the temporal attention in each layer, building directly on Diffusion Forcing's per-frame noise training paradigm. It departs from prior memory-augmented diffusion approaches (VRAG's context retrieval, Mamba-based hidden-state compression, TTT's weight memory) by unifying context memory and hidden-state memory through frame-wise sliding windows with full overlap, so that local pixel details flow through attention while the RNN carries only global history. The hidden-state prefetching mechanism is a novel training trick that decouples sequential RNN updates from parallel attention computation, addressing the train-inference gap that plagues chunk-wise TTT-style models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that a 1997 LSTM matches or beats Mamba2 and TTT in chunk-wise settings, and that frame-wise overlapping attention largely erases RNN architecture differences—gives practitioners a clear, low-complexity recipe for long video generation without chasing the latest RNN variant. The Minecraft results, where all chunk-wise RNNs underperform the plain Diffusion Forcing baseline, highlight that hidden-state compression alone is insufficient for visually dense scenes, pointing toward attention-based local context as the critical component. Open questions include whether the constant-sized RNN hidden state can scale to truly ultra-long contexts (minutes to hours), and whether the ~36% additional FLOPs from prefetching remain acceptable at production scale.
