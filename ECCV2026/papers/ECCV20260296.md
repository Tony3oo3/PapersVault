## Problem

While pre-trained vision transformers are widely adopted as perception backbones for robotic navigation, there is no systematic large-scale study of which encoder architectures, pre-training strategies, and feature compression schemes actually matter for real-world deployment. This paper addresses that gap by asking: can perception be learned from actions alone, how much visual information is truly necessary, and whether the sim-to-real gap can be closed to negligible size for fast-moving agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260296/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a single forward-facing RGB image (160×120 at 3 Hz, zero-padded and up-scaled to the encoder's expected resolution), 2D odometry (position, orientation, linear and angular velocities), and a fixed goal vector in polar coordinates relative to the episode start. It outputs one of 28 discrete velocity pairs (linear velocity ∈ {0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.7} m/s; angular velocity ∈ {−3,…,3} rad/s) at 3 Hz. The visual encoder is kept frozen throughout RL training; only the projection head, a 2-layer GRU memory (hidden size 1024), and a linear policy head are learned, with an auxiliary linear head predicting the instantaneous relative goal for spatial reasoning.

## Method

The paper introduces Pure Attention Projection (PAP), a new pooling mechanism that discards the value projections of cross-attention and passes only the softmax attention distributions (one heatmap per learned query token) to the downstream agent, yielding interpretable spatial maps that encode affordances while maintaining navigation performance. The core finding is that as few as 1 scalar per image patch suffices for real-world navigation, and that spatially-preserving projectors (PAP, LPR) outperform or match the standard Cross-attention Pooling (CAP) in both simulation and deployment. The study compares five frozen ViT backbones (DinoV2, DinoV3, VC-1, AM-Radio, Dune) under four projection heads, trained with PPO in a Habitat simulator augmented with a second-order dynamical motion model identified from real robot trajectories. A key experimental protocol trains an agent first on privileged 360° Lidar-like input for 850M steps, then fine-tunes on RGB-only for 100M steps, showing that privileged pre-training transfers to vision-only deployment and reduces inter-encoder performance gaps. The entire pipeline is validated across 966 real navigation episodes (24 km) in an office building, demonstrating that the sim-to-real gap is negligible when pre-trained encoders and realistic motion modelling are combined.

## Results

In the primary comparison (Table 1, trained from scratch for 500M steps), pre-trained ViTs dramatically outperform a ResNet-18 trained from scratch (SR=53.5% sim). Among pre-trained encoders, Dune achieves the best simulation SR (90.7%) while AM-Radio achieves 100% real-world SR across 42 episodes. The sim-to-real gap is negligible: e.g., VC-1 scores 86.3% SR in simulation vs. 95.2±6.7% in the real building. A key finding is that only 1 scalar per image patch (via Pure Attention Projection) suffices for strong navigation, with DinoV2+PAP K=1 achieving 92.9% real SR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the ResNet-18 from-scratch baseline, all pre-trained ViTs substantially outperform it (e.g., Dune 90.7% vs. 53.5% sim SR), confirming that pre-training is essential for real-world transfer. Among pre-trained encoders, the heterogeneous distillation models Dune (90.7% sim SR) and AM-Radio (100% real SR) outperform the generalist self-supervised models DinoV2 (63.4% sim SR) and VC-1 (86.3% sim SR), suggesting complementary multi-teacher skills benefit navigation. DinoV3, despite outperforming DinoV2 on standard CV benchmarks, completely fails (0% SR) with the standard Cross-attention Pooling projector across multiple seeds and model sizes, but recovers to 79.7% sim SR with the simpler Pure Attention Projection (K=8), indicating its globally coherent low-frequency features are harder to decode from weak RL signals. VC-1 (MAE-based) surprisingly matches or exceeds DinoV2, likely due to its robotics-targeted data mix of 4.3M egocentric images. When finetuned from a privileged Lidar-trained agent (Table 2), the gaps between encoders shrink considerably (all reach 87–90% sim SR), and the Lidar agent itself achieves 94.6% sim SR, demonstrating that privileged 3D information during pre-training transfers to RGB-only deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agent architecture and realistic motion modelling framework of Janny et al. [38] by replacing the multi-sensor input (RGB + Lidar-like scan) with RGB-only perception, then systematically interrogating which pre-trained vision encoder and projection mechanism best serves downstream navigation. It departs from the standard Perceiver-Resampler / cross-attention pooling paradigm widely used in VLMs and embodied AI by introducing Pure Attention Projection (PAP), which discards value projections and elevates the attention distribution itself to the representation, and by exploring heterogeneous multi-teacher distillation (AM-Radio, Dune) as a source of complementary geometric and semantic skills. The work positions itself against the 'Bitter Lesson' argument for end-to-end perception-action loops, arguing that pre-trained ViTs remain an indispensable component for near-term robotics given data sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single scalar per image patch (via PAP with K=1) preserves navigation quality is a striking efficiency result with direct practical implications for on-board computation, and the emergent interpretability of affordance maps from an unsupervised bottleneck offers a rare window into what frozen ViTs actually encode for spatial reasoning. The 966-episode real-world evaluation at 0.7 m/s provides unusually strong evidence for the sim2real gap closure claim, though the study is limited to a single building, a single static point-goal task, and one robot platform, leaving open whether these findings transfer to dynamic scenes, language-conditioned navigation, or outdoor environments. The systematic failure of DinoV3 under expressive projectors (CAP, SAP) but success under spatially-preserving ones (PAP, LPR) raises open questions about the interaction between encoder feature geometry and projector complexity under weak RL signals.
