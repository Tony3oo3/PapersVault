## Problem

Existing trajectory-based one-step video super-resolution methods suffer from trajectory drift under complex real-world degradations, caused by two compounding limitations: (1) initial state mismatch, where uncalibrated LR inputs are out-of-distribution relative to the pre-trained generative prior's expected noise states, and (2) a global timestep constraint, where a single fixed integration length cannot simultaneously handle smooth and heavily degraded regions within the same frame.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a low-resolution video sequence, encodes it into the frozen Wan2.1 VAE latent space to obtain z_LR, and produces high-resolution video frames via a single Euler integration step in latent space followed by VAE decoding. The key unusual aspect is that the one-step update is spatially modulated: each latent token receives its own integration length from a predicted T_map rather than sharing a global scalar, and the starting latent is geometrically rectified before the DiT query. Training uses 33-frame clips at 640×960 (Stage 1) and 17-frame clips at 480×640 (Stage 2).

## Method

ART-VSR introduces a state-time adaptation paradigm built on the Wan2.1 DiT with two coupled modules. The Adaptive Timestep Estimator (ATE) predicts a continuous token-wise timestep map T_map by fusing a parameter-free heuristic branch (local texture variance, Sobel gradients, and radial FFT high-pass energy, smoothed and tanh-normalized) with a learnable dual-path neural branch (latent semantic features plus a pixel-frequency path that gates the 2D real-FFT spectrum via a learned mask), combined as a convex combination with λ=0.25. The Latent Trajectory Rectifier (LTR) decomposes z_LR into low-frequency structure and high-frequency detail, adaptively modulates the detail component with Gaussian noise scaled by T_map, and applies a non-linear rectification with a global residual connection; a Geometric Projection Loss (L_LTR) supervises the rectifier by penalizing orthogonal deviation from the ideal noise-to-HR trajectory and encouraging longitudinal alignment of the rectified state with the T_map-predicted integration position. The fine-tuned DiT is then conditioned on both the rectified latent z_rect and T_map to synthesize the HR latent in one step, trained in two progressive stages (latent-space fine-tuning followed by RGB-space perceptual optimization) with LPIPS and the geometric loss jointly guiding the objective.

## Results

ART-VSR achieves the best LPIPS across all four benchmarks that report it (0.242 on UDM10, 0.325 on MVSR4x, 0.180 on YouHQ40, 0.197 on REDS) and the best CLIPIQA on all five benchmarks (e.g., 0.623 on UDM10, 0.711 on MVSR4x, 0.640 on YouHQ40). It also leads on DOVER across all five datasets (0.546 on UDM10, 0.738 on YouHQ40, 0.442 on REDS) and on MUSIQ for MVSR4x (66.563), YouHQ40 (70.917), and REDS (64.965). On pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR/SSIM), DOVE and RealViformer remain stronger, but ART-VSR is competitive as a one-step method (e.g., 26.302 PSNR on UDM10 vs. DOVE's 26.944). The paper emphasizes perceptual quality over PSNR/SSIM, arguing that pixel-wise metrics favor over-smoothed regression outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest one-step trajectory-based competitors DOVE and FlashVSR, ART-VSR wins on LPIPS across all four reported datasets (e.g., 0.197 vs. 0.252 on REDS; 0.180 vs. 0.165 on YouHQ40 where DOVE is slightly ahead) and on CLIPIQA by a wide margin (0.560 vs. 0.270 on REDS; 0.640 vs. 0.456 on YouHQ40). DOVE retains an edge on PSNR/SSIM (26.944 vs. 26.302 on UDM10; 27.824 vs. 25.780 on YouHQ40) and on NIQE for UDM10 and REDS, reflecting its multi-step sampling advantage in pixel fidelity. FlashVSR is competitive on NIQE (3.715 on UDM10, 2.711 on REDS) and MUSIQ on UDM10 (67.618) but trails ART-VSR on LPIPS, CLIPIQA, and DOVER. Against the regression baseline RealViformer, ART-VSR is far behind on PSNR/SSIM but substantially ahead on perceptual metrics (e.g., LPIPS 0.197 vs. 0.248 on REDS; CLIPIQA 0.560 vs. 0.337). Multi-step generative methods (SeedVR, STAR, UAV) are consistently outperformed on both perceptual and fidelity metrics, confirming the benefit of the one-step trajectory formulation. DLoRAL is the strongest competitor on VideoLQ MUSIQ (60.530 vs. 55.272) but ART-VSR leads on CLIPIQA, DOVER, and NIQE for that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trajectory-based one-step VSR paradigm established by DOVE and FlashVSR, which fine-tune a pre-trained flow-matching DiT (Wan2.1) to map LR latents directly to HR targets in a single Euler step. The key departure is the diagnosis of 'trajectory drift'—attributed to initial state mismatch (uncalibrated LR inputs lying off the reference generative path) and a global timestep constraint (a single scalar timestep cannot handle spatially heterogeneous degradation). To address both, ART-VSR introduces a token-wise Adaptive Timestep Estimator (ATE) that replaces the global scalar with a spatial map, and a Latent Trajectory Rectifier (LTR) that geometrically projects off-path LR latents toward the reference trajectory before the single integration step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

One-step VSR is critical for latency-sensitive deployment (streaming, mobile), and this paper provides a principled geometric explanation for why existing one-step methods degrade under in-the-wild conditions, along with a targeted fix. The token-wise timestep map is a natural but effective mechanism for handling spatially heterogeneous degradation without requiring a manual degradation taxonomy. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the Wan2.1 backbone, whether the two-stage latent-then-RGB training is strictly necessary, and how the method scales to higher resolutions or longer sequences where the frozen VAE latent space may introduce additional constraints.
