## Problem

Existing prior-guided infrared-visible image fusion methods suffer from three fundamental limitations: hard-constraint priors (e.g., saliency masks as static regularization) force the network to sacrifice fine-grained textures for rigid boundary fitting; joint optimization with downstream tasks introduces multi-objective conflicts between visual fidelity and task accuracy; and extrinsic semantic priors from foundation models (CLIP, DINO, SAM) create a granularity mismatch between abstract high-level representations and the pixel-level nature of fusion. The paper argues that priors should be endogenous to the modalities themselves and dynamic rather than static or externally sourced.

## Input / Output

The method takes a paired infrared-visible image pair as input. The infrared branch is guided by a binary thermal saliency map (pedestrians and vehicles as foreground, all else as background) generated by a pre-trained SegFormer-B2 teacher, while the visible branch receives a scalar quality score from a BRISQUE-based no-reference evaluator. The output is a single fused image that preserves both thermal target salience and visible textural detail, which can then be fed into downstream detectors (YOLOv7s) or segmenters (SegFormer-B5) without retraining the fusion network.

## Method

P² Fusion instantiates a 'Teach-to-Fuse' paradigm with two core mechanisms. First, Dual-Teacher Prompt Distillation converts two intrinsic priors—ir thermal saliency and visible spatial quality—into learnable dynamic prompts that are embedded into dual-branch features via element-wise summation, replacing hard-coded prior losses with soft distillation objectives (BCE for the spatial saliency prompt, MSE for the scalar quality prompt). Second, a Gated Dynamic Expert Recalibration (GDER) module performs decoupled feature refinement using two specialized experts: a Prior-Responsive Modality Expert that consumes the prompts to anchor salient regions, and a Prior-Agnostic Attention Expert (CBAM-based) that captures global structural dependencies independent of the prompts. A reliability-based gating network adaptively weights the two experts' contributions, enabling the network to balance foreground target fidelity against background texture preservation on a per-region basis. The overall training is unsupervised, combining perceptual losses (gradient, intensity, SSIM) with the two distillation terms, and the framework is prior-agnostic in design, extensible to other auxiliary cues beyond the two demonstrated.

## Results

P² Fusion achieves state-of-the-art fusion quality on 12 out of 16 metrics across MSRS, M3FD, FMB, and RoadScene (Table 1), with headline values of FQI_E = 0.850 / 0.743 / 0.826 / 0.617 and VIF = 0.445 / 0.429 / 0.448 / 0.388 respectively. For downstream perception, it delivers +3.2% mAP₅₀₋₉₅ on MSRS object detection (0.748 vs. 0.692), +0.5% mAP on M3FD (62.37 vs. 61.87), and +0.9% mAP on the OOD DroneVehicle benchmark (0.604 vs. 0.595). On FMB semantic segmentation, it attains the best mPA (64.67) and mIoU (57.46), marginally ahead of FreqGAN (64.47 / 57.28).

## Baselines & Comparisons

P² Fusion outperforms all 11 baselines on FQI_E, VIF, and Q_abf across every dataset, with the largest margins over SAGE (+0.087 FQI_E on MSRS, +0.084 on M3FD) and FreeFusion (+0.130 FQI_E on FMB). The only metrics where it does not rank first are Mutual Information on M3FD (3.819 vs. LutFuse's 3.987) and RoadScene (3.021 vs. LutFuse's 3.644 and TIM's 3.611), where LutFuse's look-up-table distillation preserves more raw information content at the cost of perceptual quality. On downstream object detection, P² Fusion leads by +3.2% mAP₅₀₋₉₅ on MSRS (0.748 vs. TarDal's 0.692), +0.5% on M3FD (62.37 vs. FreeFusion's 61.87), and +0.9% on the OOD DroneVehicle set (0.604 vs. TIM's 0.595). For FMB semantic segmentation, it edges out FreqGAN by +0.20 mPA and +0.17 mIoU. The consistent lead across all five benchmarks—without any domain-specific fine-tuning on DroneVehicle—indicates that the dual-prompt distillation and GDER recalibration generalize better than both foundation-model-guided methods (SAGE, CMFS) and purely data-driven architectures (DDFM, DiTFuse).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from three established prior-guided IVIF routes—hard-constraint regularization (STDFusionNet), joint multi-task optimization (TarDal), and extrinsic foundation-model semantic guidance (SAGE, CMFS)—by reframing prior knowledge as learnable, dynamic prompts distilled from image-intrinsic signals (thermal saliency via SegFormer, spatial quality via BRISQUE). The Gated Dynamic Expert Recalibration module repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm not for capacity scaling but for functional decoupling of modality-specific and context-aware feature refinement, a use distinct from standard MoE in LLMs or vision transformers. Conceptually, the 'Teach-to-Fuse' paradigm inherits the knowledge-distillation lineage but applies it to the fusion process itself rather than to a student network, making the prior a procedural regulator rather than a fixed target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The prompt-based, prior-agnostic formulation offers a practical path to incorporating diverse auxiliary cues (depth, semantics, quality) without redesigning the loss landscape, which could accelerate method development in IVIF and related multimodal fusion tasks. The strong OOD generalization on DroneVehicle (aerial, degraded scenes) without domain-specific fine-tuning suggests the dynamic prompt mechanism genuinely reduces overfitting to training-domain prior distributions—a property that static-constraint methods lack. However, the 'intrinsic' priors are still generated by external pre-trained models (SegFormer, BRISQUE), so the framework's true endogeneity remains partially aspirational; whether the approach scales to priors that are genuinely computed from the input pair without any pre-trained teacher is an open question. The framework is also validated primarily on pedestrian/vehicle-centric scenes, leaving open its behavior in medical or industrial fusion where saliency and quality priors may be less well-defined.
