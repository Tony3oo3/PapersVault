## Problem

Existing audio-visual synchronization metrics (SyncNet, PEAVS, AlignNet) assume structural correctness and reduce evaluation to temporal offset detection, failing on modern generative outputs that exhibit semantic hallucinations, asymmetric cross-modal relations, and temporally diffuse events. A further methodological paradox arises because human annotators judge synchronization through relative pairwise comparisons, yet a deployable metric must be reference-free and emit an absolute continuous score for a single video-audio pair.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261736/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluator consumes a 5-second video clip at 140×140 resolution and 12 FPS alongside a generated audio track, processing both through a multimodal context sequence. Rather than decoding discrete text tokens, the model appends a [SCORE] inference token and extracts a single continuous scalar via a lightweight MLP projection head applied to that token's hidden representation, yielding a reference-free synchronization score for any (video, audio) pair without requiring a ground-truth audio reference.

## Method

Built on Qwen2.5-Omni-3B, the architecture replaces the discrete language head with a continuous latent projection MLP, converting the Omni-LLM into a differentiable scalar regressor. Training proceeds in two stages: first, a Bradley-Terry-Luce preference objective with curriculum pacing (annealing from large-rank-discrepancy pairs to fine-grained adjacent comparisons) maps relative human rankings into a globally consistent absolute value space; second, R-GRPO reparameterizes the deterministic scalar output as the mean of a Gaussian policy, samples N parallel perturbed trajectories per anchor video, and optimizes a composite listwise ranking reward (structural attenuation, monotonic topological order, extreme target bounding) using group-relative advantages and a clipped surrogate loss with KL regularization against the reference backbone. The training data, SynthSync, consists of authentic generative failures produced by 10 state-of-the-art V2A models on shared visual anchors, annotated via ~306K pairwise expert comparisons aggregated into global ordinal rankings.

## Results

On the SynthSync benchmark, the full R-GRPO framework achieves NDCG 0.9435, MRR 0.7674, Kendall's τ 0.4899, Spearman 0.5837, Top-1 accuracy 61.40%, and Pairwise Accuracy 72.38%, establishing state-of-the-art across nearly all metrics. The R-GRPO stage alone contributes a 5.6% absolute gain in Top-1 accuracy and a 1.31-point gain in Pairwise Accuracy over the PFT-only variant (Ours w/o R-GRPO). The only metric where a baseline leads is Bottom-1 accuracy, where VQ-Insight scores 62.80% versus 55.00% for the proposed method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 11 baselines on NDCG, MRR, Kendall's τ, Spearman, Top-1 accuracy, and Pairwise Accuracy. Among off-the-shelf Omni-LLMs, Gemini-3-flash is the strongest baseline (NDCG 0.9225, Pair Acc 63.20%), yet the proposed method still leads by 2.1 points in NDCG and 9.2 points in Pairwise Accuracy. Among non-LLM synchronization metrics, JavisScore is the closest competitor (NDCG 0.9284, Pair Acc 69.36%), trailing the proposed method by 1.5 NDCG points and 3.0 Pair Acc points. Among trained Omni-LLM evaluators, VQ-Insight is the nearest rival (NDCG 0.9248, Pair Acc 65.85%) and is the sole method that surpasses the proposed approach on Bottom-1 accuracy (62.80% vs. 55.00%), while the proposed method leads VQ-Insight by 1.9 NDCG points and 6.5 Pair Acc points. Traditional signal-level metrics (AV-Align, DeSync) and smaller Omni-LLMs (Qwen2.5-3B/7B) are substantially outperformed, with Pairwise Accuracy gaps exceeding 15 points, confirming that structural-correctness assumptions in legacy metrics fail on authentic generative failures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Qwen2.5-Omni-3B multimodal LLM by replacing its discrete language decoding head with a continuous latent projection MLP, transforming a generative model into a differentiable scalar scorer. The reinforcement post-training stage adapts the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeek-R1 to real-valued Gaussian policies (R-GRPO), extending a framework originally designed for discrete token sequences to continuous score distributions. Conceptually, the work departs from both traditional AV-sync metrics (SyncNet, DeSync, JavisScore) that assume structural correctness and simple temporal offsets, and from LLM-as-Judge paradigms that emit discrete linguistic categories, by formulating synchronization evaluation as continuous reference-free causal-semantic scoring trained from relative human preferences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As AV-Gen systems (Sora-2, Veo-3.1, Seedance) evolve toward world simulation, the field lacks a standardized, human-aligned metric that can assess causal-semantic synchronization beyond low-level signal correlation; SyncBench and the proposed continuous metric address this gap for six cutting-edge models. The dual utility of the metric—as both an evaluator and a Best-of-N reward signal with strong cross-metric generalization—makes it practically deployable for model selection and test-time scaling. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond 5-second clips at 140×140 resolution, whether the 10-model V2A pool adequately captures the full failure topology of native joint-generation systems, and whether the expert-annotation dependency (20 annotators, ~306K pairwise judgments) limits scalability to longer or more diverse content.
