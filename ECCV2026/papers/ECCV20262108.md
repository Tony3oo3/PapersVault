## Problem

Existing inference-time diffusion guidance methods (DPS, FreeDoM, MPGD, DSG, ADMMDiff) inject external gradients in ambient space without respecting the stepwise isotropic Gaussian latent distribution at each DDIM timestep. This introduces distributional drift (the Jensen gap), causing off-manifold deviations that degrade clean-sample quality, especially at large step sizes. The paper asks whether one can design guidance that provably keeps the guided latent within the original Gaussian structure while still steering toward the conditioning signal.

## Input / Output

For image restoration, the input is a degraded observation y = A(x) + n (where A is a task-specific linear operator such as a binary mask, 4×/8×/12× downsampling, or Gaussian/motion blur kernel) plus a pre-trained diffusion model; the output is the reconstructed clean image x̂₀. For conditional generation, the input is a conditioning image y (segmentation map, sketch, or face) whose features are extracted by a frozen encoder (BiSeNet, AODA, or ArcFace); the output is a generated image aligned to that condition. All experiments use 256×256 RGB images (FFHQ, CelebA-HQ, ImageNet) or 512×512/768×768 outputs via Stable Diffusion, with DDIM sampling schedules of 100 or 1,000 steps.

## Method

DiffRGD exploits the polar decomposition of the isotropic Gaussian latent at each DDIM timestep: x_t = μ_t + σ_t·r·u, where r ~ χ(n) and u ~ Unif(S^{n-1}) are statistically independent. By fixing the sampled radius r, it constructs a spherical manifold S_{t,r} = {x : ‖x − μ_t‖₂ = σ_t·r} that exactly matches the radial density of the original Gaussian, and formulates per-step guidance as min_{x ∈ S_{t,r}} L(x̂₀(x, t−1), y). This constrained problem is solved by Riemannian Gradient Descent: the Euclidean guidance gradient is projected onto the tangent space (removing the radial component), and a retraction maps the updated point back onto the sphere, guaranteeing every iterate remains on the distribution-induced manifold. The method requires only K=3 inner RGD iterations per timestep, converges to a stationary point at rate O(1/√k) under L-smoothness, and is plug-and-play—no retraining or fine-tuning of the diffusion model is needed.

## Results

On FFHQ 256×256 with 1,000 DDIM steps, DiffRGD achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FID on inpainting (34.04 / 0.926 / 0.096 / 21.88) and 4× super-resolution (27.77 / 0.783 / 0.220 / 63.94), outperforming the next-best method (ADMMDiff) by 1.66 dB PSNR and 7.64 FID on inpainting. On conditional generation (CelebA-HQ, 100 DDIM steps), DiffRGD attains the highest mIoU (0.804) and lowest FID (96.10) for segmentation-map guidance, and the lowest Sketch-ℓ₂ (19.48) and FID (87.82) for sketch guidance. With only 100 DDIM steps, DiffRGD maintains strong performance while DPS and ADMMDiff degrade noticeably, e.g. 27.46 PSNR on Gaussian deblurring vs. 26.01 for ADMMDiff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffRGD outperforms all five baselines on inpainting and 4× super-resolution across every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID), with the largest margin over DPS (e.g., +3.6 dB PSNR on inpainting) and MPGD (e.g., +10.3 dB PSNR on SR). Against the closest competitor ADMMDiff, DiffRGD gains 1.66 dB PSNR and 7.64 FID on inpainting, and 1.29 dB PSNR and 32.75 FID on SR; however, ADMMDiff retains a slight edge on motion deblurring PSNR (27.26 vs. 25.84) and SSIM (0.778 vs. 0.736). DSG is competitive on Gaussian deblurring PSNR (27.45 vs. 26.80) but DiffRGD wins on LPIPS and FID for that task. On conditional generation (CelebA-HQ), DiffRGD beats FreeDoM by a wide margin (mIoU 0.804 vs. 0.622 for segmentation; Sketch-ℓ₂ 19.48 vs. 30.85 for sketches) and surpasses DSG and ADMMDiff on mIoU, FID, and KID for segmentation, and on all three metrics for sketch guidance. Overall, DiffRGD wins or ties on 13 of 16 task-metric groups in Table 1 and all 9 metric groups in Table 5, with the only losses being motion-deblurring PSNR/SSIM to ADMMDiff.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDIM sampling framework (Song et al., 2021), where each timestep defines an isotropic Gaussian latent distribution, and on the constrained-optimization formulation for per-timestep guidance introduced by MPGD (He et al., 2024). It departs from prior inference-time guidance methods in a specific way: rather than applying unconstrained Euclidean gradients (DPS, FreeDoM), projecting onto a linear data manifold via a VAE encoder (MPGD), or constraining to a fixed-radius hypersphere that only approximates the Gaussian (DSG), DiffRGD exploits the polar decomposition of the isotropic Gaussian to construct a spherical manifold whose radius is sampled from the exact chi-distribution of the latent, then solves the constrained problem via Riemannian Gradient Descent instead of the slower ADMM inner-loop used by ADMMDiff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distribution-induced spherical manifold provides a principled, exact geometric constraint that preserves the stepwise Gaussian structure of diffusion sampling, addressing the off-manifold drift that plagues unconstrained gradient methods. The practical appeal is strong: RGD requires only K=3 iterations per timestep (no inner-loop tuning, no proximal operator), making it a genuinely plug-and-play drop-in for any pre-trained diffusion model. Open questions include whether the isotropic-Gaussian assumption holds for anisotropic or non-Gaussian latent structures (e.g., rectified-flow or flow-matching models), whether the spherical constraint remains tight at very high resolutions where the chi-distribution concentrates, and how the method interacts with classifier-free guidance or multi-condition settings.
