## Problem

Customized Portrait Generation (CPG) models can reproduce real-person identities from a single input image, creating serious privacy risks, yet no method exists for selectively erasing a specific identity from a CPG pipeline. Existing generative identity unlearning approaches fine-tune the denoising U-Net, which risks catastrophic forgetting of generative priors and tightly couples unlearning to a single generator, limiting cross-pipeline transferability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260981/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 face image of a target identity to be unlearned and a text prompt specifying the desired edit, and produces a generated portrait that no longer resembles the target identity while preserving fidelity for all other identities. Offline, it also consumes Face-Swap image pairs (original and identity-swapped versions) to compute a dimensional mask. The unlearned CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder is the sole artifact produced and can be plugged into any CPG generator (e.g., FastComposer, PhotoMaker) sharing the same encoder without further training.

## Method

IREU performs all updates in the image encoder's feature space while keeping the diffusion model frozen. First, it localizes identity-related features by encoding Face-Swap pairs with the original CLIP encoder, computing the embedding difference Δz, and selecting the top-k (k=0.4) dimensions by |Δz| magnitude as identity-sensitive via a binary mask M. Second, it constructs a virtual identity z̃₋ = z₋ + α(M ⊙ Δz) that shifts the target embedding only along identity-related dimensions (α=100), then trains the unlearned encoder εᵤ to minimize ‖εᵤ(x₋) − z̃₋‖₂ while simultaneously minimizing ‖εᵤ(x₊) − ε(x₊)‖₂ for retained identities. This localized perturbation avoids the global feature-space distortion that degrades fidelity in the baseline, and because only the encoder is modified, the resulting unlearned encoder transfers plug-and-play across different CPG generators without retraining.

## Results

On FastComposer under the oneID setting, IREU achieves the best unlearning–retaining trade-off with ΔID = 0.64, compared to BIA (0.52) and the global-perturbation baseline (0.48), while maintaining the lowest target identity similarity (ID_target^o = 0.01) and the highest retained-identity similarity (ID_other^g = 0.85). Under the more challenging fiveID setting, IREU further widens the gap to ΔID = 0.55 versus BIA's 0.22 and the baseline's 0.07, with ID_other^g = 0.78. On fidelity, IREU achieves PSNR 28.88 / SSIM 0.92 / ΔFID 1.02 (oneID) and PSNR 28.25 / SSIM 0.91 / ΔFID 1.59 (fiveID), substantially outperforming BIA (PSNR 20.51 / 19.46, ΔFID 4.14 / 2.76) and the baseline (ΔFID 4.33 / 28.37).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is BIA (Black Hole-driven Identity Absorbing, CVPR 2025), which edits the diffusion U-Net bottleneck. IREU outperforms BIA on the key trade-off metric ΔID in both settings (0.64 vs. 0.52 for oneID; 0.55 vs. 0.22 for fiveID), driven primarily by much stronger retention of other identities (ID_other^g: 0.85 vs. 0.82 oneID; 0.78 vs. 0.65 fiveID) while achieving comparable or better unlearning (ID_target^o: 0.01 vs. 0.05 oneID; 0.02 vs. 0.15 fiveID). On fidelity, IREU improves PSNR by ~8 dB and reduces ΔFID by 3–4× over BIA (e.g., ΔFID 1.02 vs. 4.14 oneID). The paper's own global-perturbation baseline, while achieving the lowest ID_target^g (0.03 oneID), collapses on retention in the fiveID setting (ID_other^o drops to 0.01, ΔFID explodes to 28.37), demonstrating that indiscriminate feature-space perturbation is far more damaging than IREU's identity-related masking. IREU also generalizes to PhotoMaker without fine-tuning, achieving ΔID = 0.7007 there, whereas the baseline only reaches 0.1108.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new sub-problem—identity unlearning specifically for Customized Portrait Generation (CPG) pipelines—by repurposing the broader machine-unlearning-in-diffusion-models literature (concept erasure, generative unlearning) to a targeted, encoder-only setting. It departs from prior identity-unlearning methods (GUIDE for GANs, BIA for diffusion U-Nets, APDM for DreamBooth-style fine-tuning) by freezing the denoising network entirely and operating solely in the CLIP image-encoder embedding space, which enables plug-and-play transfer across generators sharing the same encoder. The Face-Swap-based dimensional masking strategy borrows the face-swap paradigm as an offline diagnostic tool to identify which embedding dimensions encode identity, a repurposing not seen in prior unlearning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The encoder-only unlearning paradigm is practically significant for GDPR compliance: a service provider can erase a specific identity from their CPG service without retraining the expensive diffusion backbone, and the unlearned encoder transfers to new generators (PhotoMaker, multi-subject FastComposer) without additional training. Key limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (CelebA-HQ), a single encoder architecture (CLIP ViT-L/14), and only one external baseline (BIA), leaving open whether the dimensional-masking strategy generalizes to other encoders or more diverse identity distributions. An open question is whether the one-shot setting (one image per identity) suffices for robust real-world deployment where identities may be represented by highly variable images.
