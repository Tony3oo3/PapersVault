## Problem

Diffusion model training is prohibitively expensive, and while prior work identified that mixing of diffusion trajectories from different images (miscibility) hinders denoising, the existing remedy—batch-wise linear assignment—suffers from O(n³) complexity at large batch sizes and raises unresolved concerns about generation diversity. This paper asks whether the miscibility-reduction principle can be decoupled from expensive assignment and whether it genuinely threatens output diversity.

## Input / Output

At inference time the method is identical to the base diffusion model: it consumes Gaussian noise (optionally conditioned on a class label, text prompt, partial image, or robot state) and produces a generated image, completed image, or action sequence. The modification is purely at training time—noise is either selected via KNN proximity to the image or the image is scaled by a constant factor before the standard forward-diffusion step—so no architectural or inference-time changes are required. Experiments span 32×32 (CIFAR-10), 256×256 (ImageNet, MS-COCO), and 2D robot state vectors (PushT).

## Method

The paper redefines immiscible diffusion as any training procedure that reduces the overlap of diffusion trajectories originating from different images, rather than requiring explicit image-noise pairing. It first demonstrates via perturbation experiments that vanilla diffusion models already exhibit a stable image-to-noise-origin correlation (20% Gaussian perturbation leaves generated images nearly unchanged), resolving diversity concerns. Two new implementations follow: (1) KNN noise selection, which samples k Gaussian noises per image and keeps only the L2-closest one (O(n) vs. O(n³) for linear assignment, with negligible KL-divergence shift from strict Gaussianity), and (2) image scaling, which multiplies pixel values by a constant >1 to increase inter-image L2 distances and thereby reduce trajectory overlap without any noise reassignment. Feature-level analysis (tSNE of predicted images across denoising steps, FID of intermediate denoised outputs) shows that miscibility reduction clarifies the denoising target at the noisiest layers, yielding up to 4× fewer training steps to reach the same FID across DDIM, Flow Matching, Consistency Models, and Stable Diffusion.

## Results

Across three diffusion architectures (DDIM, Flow Matching, Consistency Models) on CIFAR-10, the KNN implementation of immiscible diffusion achieves FID 4.45, 3.56, and 9.16 respectively, improving over vanilla baselines (6.63, 4.92, 10.22) by up to 2.18 FID points. The method requires 2.5–4.5× fewer training steps to reach the best FID, with the KNN variant adding only 0.2 ms overhead per batch of 256 (vs. 5.4 ms for linear assignment). On ImageNet class-conditional training with Stable Diffusion, immiscible diffusion also accelerates convergence while maintaining identical CLIPScore (28.55) for prompt-image alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDIM [30], the KNN variant reduces FID from 6.63 to 4.45 (−2.18) and the assignment variant to 5.14 (−1.49), with both requiring substantially fewer training steps. Against Flow Matching [23], KNN improves FID from 4.92 to 3.56 (−1.36), while the assignment variant slightly underperforms vanilla (5.02 vs. 4.92). Against Consistency Models [32], both variants win: KNN reaches 9.16 (−1.06) and assignment reaches 8.54 (−1.68) versus the vanilla 10.22. For image editing with Stable Diffusion [28], immiscible KNN outperforms vanilla SD on in-painting (FID 17.32 vs. 18.35) and out-painting (27.57 vs. 29.34). In robotics planning (PushT) with Diffusion Policy [3], immiscible KNN (k=2) improves average area coverage from 79.56% to 82.83% and max coverage from 85.71% to 86.74%. When superposed with other acceleration methods (Kim et al. [14], Choi et al. [4]), immiscible diffusion consistently yields lower FID at both 100k and 200k steps (e.g., 4.28 vs. 6.43 for FM+[14] at 100k).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Immiscible Diffusion [20], which introduced the concept of reducing trajectory mixing via batch-wise linear assignment, by generalizing it into an implementation-agnostic principle: any method that reduces the miscibility of diffusion trajectories qualifies. It positions itself against the broader line of trajectory-straightening work (Flow Matching [23], Rectified Flow [24], batch-wise OT [27,34]), arguing that these methods reduce image-noise distance by only ~2% and denoising variance by ~4%, and that their training-efficiency gains are better explained as incidental realizations of immiscibility. The paper also builds on prior observations of image-noise correlation in diffusion inversion [33,39,1] to resolve the diversity concern that had limited adoption of the original immiscible diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× training speedup, validated across DDIM, Flow Matching, Consistency Models, and Stable Diffusion on CIFAR-10, ImageNet, and MS-COCO, plus extensions to image editing and robotics planning, makes this a practically significant and easy-to-adopt technique (KNN adds ~0.2 ms overhead). The conceptual reframing—identifying trajectory miscibility as a fundamental bottleneck rather than trajectory length or curvature—opens a new axis for future efficiency research beyond the current OT/straightening paradigm. Open questions include whether the KNN parameter k and image scaling factor transfer cleanly to very high-dimensional latent spaces (e.g., video or 3D generation), and whether the approach composes optimally with emerging architectures like DiT-based models at scale.
