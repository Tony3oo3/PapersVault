## Problem

Text-to-video diffusion models produce visually realistic clips that frequently violate basic physical laws (gravity, fluid dynamics, thermal behavior, material properties). Existing remedies are either explicit simulators that cannot generalize to open-world phenomena or implicit alignment methods that lack a mechanism to inject precise, controllable physical guidance. The paper identifies the specific gap that no prior method exploits explicit spatiotemporal video references as physical priors directly inside the latent denoising process.

## Input / Output

At inference the system takes a free-form text prompt and retrieves one reference video from a manually curated 170-video database (17 physical-phenomenon categories × 10 exemplars) using VideoCLIP-XL embeddings and FAISS nearest-neighbor search. The retrieved video is pre-encoded offline by VideoMAE-V2 into a token sequence F_v ∈ R^{L×C_v}. The output is a 49-frame video at 704×480 resolution generated by the Wan2.2-5B DiT backbone, now conditioned on both the text and the distilled physical prior.

## Method

PhysRAG introduces a Query Inject module that acts as a learnable information bottleneck: 128 query tokens cross-attend to the VideoMAE-V2 features of the retrieved video, selectively distilling physically relevant dynamics while discarding irrelevant visual noise. The resulting compact physical-prior tokens are projected to the DiT hidden dimension, concatenated with the DiT token sequence, fused by a lightweight projector, and injected through a gated residual connection (H'_DiT = H_DiT + α·H_fuse) into the first three DiT blocks (layers 0, 1, 2). Training jointly fine-tunes the Wan2.2-5B backbone and the query adapter on 7K videos curated from WISA-80K via a two-stage Qwen3-VL filtering pipeline (text-only pre-filter retaining top 10%, then multimodal key-frame grounding retaining 90%). Ablations confirm that the query-bottleneck design outperforms direct concatenation or raw cross-attention, that multi-layer front injection beats single-layer variants, and that joint (rather than frozen-backbone) training is essential for the model to internalize retrieved physical priors.

## Results

On PhyGenBench, PhysRAG (Wan2.2+PhysRAG) achieves the highest overall average of 0.58, surpassing the best closed-source model Gen-3 (0.51) and the best open-source baseline DiT-Mem (0.56). Compared to the Wan2.2-5B backbone alone (0.54), PhysRAG yields absolute gains of +0.07 in Thermal and +0.08 in Material. On VBench, PhysRAG achieves the best Low-Avg (65.48%) and High-Avg (82.88%), improving over Wan2.2 (62.10% / 81.18%) while also outperforming DiT-Mem (64.96% / 82.82%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysRAG outperforms all 12 baselines on PhyGenBench. Against the closest open-source competitor DiT-Mem (0.56 avg), PhysRAG leads by +0.02, with the gap widening in Thermal (+0.06) and Material (+0.06) where DiT-Mem's general-domain memory tokens lack explicit physical priors. Against the Wan2.2-5B backbone (0.54), the RAG module adds +0.04 average, confirming the retrieval mechanism rather than data quality alone drives the gain. Among closed-source systems, PhysRAG beats Gen-3 (0.51) by +0.07 and Kling (0.49) by +0.09, despite using a 5B open-source backbone. On VBench, PhysRAG edges out DiT-Mem on Low-Avg (65.48% vs 64.96%) and High-Avg (82.88% vs 82.82%), while the larger gap over Wan2.2 (+3.38% Low-Avg, +1.70% High-Avg) shows physical-prior injection also improves general visual fidelity without degrading semantic alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysRAG sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) physics-aware video generation, which has been approached via explicit simulators (rigid, non-generalizable) or implicit alignment (DPO, RL — hard to control); (2) retrieval-augmented generation, previously applied to text and image generation but not to physics-grounded video synthesis; and (3) query-based feature fusion (Q-Former, BLIP-2), repurposed here as an information bottleneck that distills physical dynamics from retrieved exemplars. The key departure from prior work is treating real-world physical videos as non-parametric priors and injecting their VideoMAE-encoded spatiotemporal features into a Video DiT via learnable cross-attention queries, rather than encoding physics as scalar rewards or prompt-side reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RAG-for-physics paradigm offers a practical middle ground between the brittleness of explicit simulators and the uncontrollability of implicit alignment, with minimal overhead (+2.28% parameters, +1.24% latency). However, the physical video database is small (170 videos, 17 manually curated categories), raising open questions about scalability to long-tail phenomena and whether automated curation can replace manual collection. The finding that RAG with unfiltered data actually degrades performance below pure SFT highlights that retrieval quality is a first-order concern, not a free lunch. Broader impact depends on whether this retrieval-injection pattern generalizes beyond the curated 17 categories to truly open-world physics.
