## Problem

Existing self-supervised neural garment simulators model cloth as purely elastic material, ignoring plasticity, which prevents them from generating persistent (static) wrinkles that remain after deformation. This simplification, while enabling straightforward energy-minimization training, produces visually unrealistic garments that lack the sharp, lasting folds commonly seen in real fabrics. The paper addresses this by introducing the first self-supervised simulator that explicitly models elasto-plastic bending to reproduce persistent wrinkles.

## Input / Output

The simulator takes an initial garment state (vertex positions and velocities of a thin-shell triangle mesh with N vertices and D edges), a body motion sequence represented as SMPL skeleton joint features (9D per joint: position and orientation), and a body shape parameterization. It outputs a full sequence of deformed garment meshes over T frames along with per-edge rest bending values (plastic strain) that encode the accumulated irreversible deformation. The garment is attached to the body via skinning, and the body surface is represented by the SMPL model.

## Method

The method introduces two coupled networks: an Elastic Net (E-Net), a recurrent encoder-decoder that predicts garment deformation conditioned on rest bending, and a Plastic Net (P-Net), a hinge-graph GNN with three message-passing steps that predicts the evolving per-edge rest bending (plastic strain). The key physical insight is a dynamic rest bending computed via a perfect plastic model: when the elastic bending strain on an edge exceeds a yield threshold ε_y, the excess is irreversibly converted to plastic strain, making the rest state shift over time. Training is formulated as a moving energy minimization problem (implicit Euler with a bending energy term that depends on the unknown, evolving RB), which creates a chicken-egg dependency between the two networks. To resolve this, the paper proposes a physics-inspired curriculum learning scheme that alternates between training E-Net with the current RB and training P-Net with a target RB computed from E-Net's predictions, gradually transitioning the material from purely elastic to elasto-plastic over a few alternating cycles.

## Results

On unseen motions (Table 1), the proposed method achieves the best performance across all seven metrics, with the largest gains in wrinkle-specific measures: BED of 0.28714 rad (45.34% better than the best baseline SENC at 0.52529) and PE of 0.03206 rad (74.36% better than all baselines at 0.12506). Geometric fidelity also improves, with MED of 0.02355 m (26.19% better than SNUG) and CD of 0.00027 m² (32.50% better than NCS). The method achieves the lowest bending energy (BEN 0.02999, 12.10% better than NCS) while maintaining competitive collision handling (COL 0.00129 m, tied with NCS for best).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five state-of-the-art self-supervised cloth simulators: PBNS, SNUG, NCS, HOOD, and SENC. The proposed method wins on all seven metrics, with the most dramatic advantage in the wrinkle-specific metrics BED and PE, where all baselines share an identical PE of 0.12506 rad (since they do not model rest bending, their effective RB is zero) while the proposed method achieves 0.03206 rad—a 74.36% reduction. On geometric metrics, the method outperforms the best baselines by 26.19% in MED (vs. SNUG) and 32.50% in CD (vs. NCS). The bending energy (BEN) is 12.10% lower than NCS, the best baseline on that metric. On collision (COL), the method ties with NCS at 0.00129 m, confirming that improved wrinkle modeling does not degrade collision handling. HOOD and SENC, which use graph-based architectures, show the worst geometric accuracy (MED 0.06081 and 0.05486 m respectively) and highest bending energy, suggesting their local interaction modeling struggles with global deformation fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised garment simulation line (PBNS, SNUG, NCS, HOOD, SENC) by replacing their static, purely elastic bending energy with a dynamic rest-bending term that models elasto-plastic material behavior, drawing on the perfect plastic model from physics-based cloth simulation (O'Brien et al. 2002; Narain et al. 2013). The E-Net architecture follows the recurrent encoder-decoder design of NCS, while the P-Net adopts the hinge-graph message-passing structure of HOOD, but repurposes it to predict an evolving plastic strain rather than transient dynamics. The key conceptual departure is the curriculum learning scheme that resolves the chicken-egg problem of training with a moving energy minimization target, a training paradigm not previously explored in this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that self-supervised neural simulators can produce physically plausible persistent wrinkles, closing a visible realism gap that has limited the adoption of SSL garment simulators in production pipelines. The plug-and-play design (P-Net as an add-on to any existing E-Net) and the 4-cycle convergence make the approach practically accessible. However, the method cannot simulate wrinkles being flattened by stretching, does not handle loose or multi-layered garments, and relies on skinning-based body attachment that constrains draping behavior. Open questions include whether the curriculum learning generalizes to more complex material models (time-dependent plasticity, strain hardening) and whether the hinge-graph P-Net scales to high-resolution meshes with thousands of hinges.
