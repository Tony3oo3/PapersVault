## Problem

Generating coherent 3D avatar motion from concurrent multi-modal inputs (e.g., a text description of walking alongside a speech audio clip) remains unsolved because aligned multi-modal training data is scarce. Existing approaches either process modalities sequentially or combine their outputs via weighted sum, producing mismatched or unrealistic movements where one modality dominates and the other is suppressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261753/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts up to three concurrent control signals: a text description (CLIP 512-d embedding), speech audio (Wav2Vec2 N×768 frame-level features), and an optional XY trajectory (N×2 positions). The output is a full-body SMPL-X motion sequence of N×491 dimensions per frame, decomposed into a 205-d body pose and a 286-d detailed facial-expression-and-hand-pose vector. Conditions can be synchronous (all signals share the same time interval) or asynchronous (different signals occupy different temporal segments), with the latter handled via an LLM-generated motion timeline.

## Method

MOCO builds on the Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) with four independently trained transformer denoisers (text-to-motion, speech-to-gesture, trajectory-to-velocity, speech-to-details) that require no aligned multi-modal data. The core mechanism is decoupled denoising: at each diffusion step, the model independently predicts upper-body motion from audio and lower-body motion from text, then fuses them via a binary body-part mask (head/arms ← audio; spine/legs ← text). The fused motion is re-noised and fed back as the input for the next denoising step, so each modality iteratively refines its contribution in the context of the whole-body state, progressively harmonizing cross-modal coordination. For trajectory control, a velocity denoiser is guided by differentiable forward kinematics optimized with LBFGS at every step. For complex asynchronous conditions, an LLM decomposes the input into a motion timeline of elementary actions with explicit body-part assignments, and per-step masked composition (STMC-style) with DiffCollage blending handles transitions.

## Results

On the purpose-built Multi-Modal Benchmark (1,000 test clips with concurrent text and audio conditions), MOCO achieves the best text-to-motion scores across all four metrics: FID+ 0.862, R1 24.6, M2T 0.649, and M2M 0.639, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline SynTalker (FID+ 0.985, R1 9.8). On speech-to-gesture metrics, MOCO attains FID-A 3.83, BC 2.72, and L1div 8.62, remaining competitive though not always best (Weighted Sum leads on FID-A at 2.17, SynTalker on BC at 2.95). A 51-participant user study further confirms MOCO's advantage over STMC on audio synchronization and naturalness (p < 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOCO dominates all text-to-motion metrics over every baseline: it improves R1 from 9.8 (SynTalker) to 24.6 and FID+ from 0.985 to 0.862, indicating far better alignment with text instructions and more realistic lower-body motion. Against Weighted Sum [61] and Pseudo-Text [34], MOCO wins on FID+, R1, M2T, M2M, and BC, while those baselines retain a narrow edge on FID-A (2.17 and 2.22 vs. 3.83), reflecting their stronger speech-conditioned realism at the cost of text fidelity. Compared to SynTalker [12], MOCO achieves lower FID-A (3.83 vs. 6.60) and higher R1 (24.6 vs. 9.8), but SynTalker edges ahead on BC (2.95 vs. 2.72) and L1div (9.12 vs. 8.62), suggesting slightly better beat synchronization and gesture diversity. In the dedicated comparison with STMC [43] (adapted to the concurrent multi-modal setting), MOCO produces more natural and better-synchronized motion, confirmed by a 51-participant user study where MOCO is preferred with statistical significance (p < 0.05) on both audio synchronization and naturalness.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOCO builds directly on the Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) as its generative backbone and extends STMC's per-step body-part composition paradigm from homogeneous text-only conditions to heterogeneous audio, text, and trajectory control. It departs from prior multi-modal approaches (FreeTalker, SynTalker, MCm) that combine modality-specific predictions via weighted sum or pseudo-labels, instead decoupling the denoising process spatially so that each modality independently refines its assigned body region at every diffusion step. The trajectory control module borrows the differentiable forward-kinematics loss guidance from InterControl, and the LLM-based motion planning pipeline adapts STMC's timeline masking to handle asynchronous multi-condition inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled denoising strategy offers a clean, data-efficient solution to the aligned multi-modal data scarcity problem that has constrained the field, and the purpose-built 1,000-clip benchmark provides a controlled evaluation protocol for concurrent multi-modal motion generation that did not previously exist. However, the fixed upper/lower body-part assignment is a clear limitation: it cannot handle text instructions involving arm movements or audio-driven lower-body dynamics, and the benchmark's controlled construction (40 fixed text prompts, standing-pose-biased audio) limits claims about open-ended generalization. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned body-part assignment, residual blending, or context-aware fusion can replace the rigid spatial rules while preserving the interpretability and data efficiency of decoupled generation.
