## Problem

Image-based virtual cell models trained with pixel-level flow-matching objectives can generate visually realistic but biologically implausible cell images, such as nuclei appearing outside the cytoplasm or cells with incorrect morphological profiles for a given drug. This mismatch between local pixel fidelity and global physical constraints limits the practical utility of virtual cells for drug discovery, where structural and functional correctness is essential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262322/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 96×96 three-channel fluorescence microscopy image of an unperturbed (control) cell and a perturbation condition (chemical compound) as input, and produces a predicted 96×96 three-channel image of the cell's morphology after treatment. Because imaging is destructive and paired before/after observations are unavailable, the model learns a distribution-to-distribution transformation conditioned on same-batch control images to mitigate batch effects, rather than a pointwise image-to-image mapping.

## Method

CellFluxRL post-trains a pretrained CellFlux flow-matching model using the DiffusionNFT online RL algorithm, which avoids intractable log-likelihoods by operating on the forward noising process. Seven non-differentiable biological reward functions—spanning mode-of-action classification, nucleus-in-cytoplasm containment, nuclear roundness, and four morphological statistics (nucleus/cytoplasm size and count)—are aggregated into a weighted combined reward. At each iteration, a group of candidate images is generated, scored, and split into positive and negative subsets; a single policy is then optimized via contrastive updates using implicit positive/negative velocity parameterizations that simultaneously fit high-reward samples and repel low-reward ones, with KL regularization to the pretrained policy to prevent reward hacking. The same reward functions also enable test-time scaling via best-of-N selection at inference, yielding monotonic quality improvements as N increases.

## Results

On BBBC021, CellFluxRL improves the overall biological reward from −2.44 (CellFlux) to 0.46, and test-time scaling with best-of-4 selection pushes it further to 3.15. The largest relative gain is on MoA classification reward (0.26 → 0.34 → 0.56 with TTS), while structural and morphological metrics (Nuc-in-Cyto, Roundness, NucSize, CytoSize, NucCount, CytoCount) all improve monotonically. CellFluxRL achieves the best KID (0.014) among all methods, though its FID (24.01) is slightly higher than CellFlux's (20.36), indicating a minor trade-off in pixel-level distributional fidelity for biological correctness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CellFluxRL outperforms all three baselines on every biological reward metric. Against the direct predecessor CellFlux, the overall reward improves from −2.44 to 0.46 (a 2.9-point gain), with the most dramatic improvement on NucSize (−2.21 → −1.04) and MoA (0.26 → 0.34). Against the two prior generative baselines, CellFluxRL's MoA reward of 0.34 more than doubles PhenDiff (0.18) and nearly triples IMPA (0.12), and its overall reward of 0.46 exceeds PhenDiff (−5.20) and IMPA (−3.19) by 5.6 and 3.6 points respectively. On generation quality, CellFluxRL achieves the best KID (0.014 vs. 0.015 for CellFlux) but a slightly worse FID (24.01 vs. 20.36), suggesting RL post-training trades a small amount of pixel-level distributional fidelity for substantially better biological correctness. Adding test-time scaling (best-of-4) to CellFluxRL yields the best scores on all biological metrics (e.g., MoA 0.56, Overall 3.15) while keeping FID competitive at 23.19.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CellFlux, a flow-matching model for distribution-to-distribution cellular perturbation modeling, and on DiffusionNFT, an online RL algorithm for flow matching that avoids intractable log-likelihoods via forward-process probability estimation. It positions itself within the physics-aware generative modeling literature, specifically the objective-based approach of incorporating domain constraints into training, but departs from prior work by applying this to a distribution-to-distribution (rather than standard Gaussian-prior) flow matching setting and by designing a multi-scale suite of non-differentiable biological evaluators as RL rewards. The test-time scaling component draws on best-of-N selection strategies from the reasoning-model literature, repurposing the same reward functions as inference-time verifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a relatively simple RL post-training loop with hand-crafted biological rewards can systematically improve physical plausibility across all seven metrics without degrading image quality, offering a practical recipe for aligning generative models with domain-specific constraints. The triple-use of rewards (evaluation, training signal, test-time selection) is a clean design that lowers the barrier to incorporating new biological constraints. Key limitations include the manual engineering of rewards (the authors suggest LLMs could automate this), evaluation on a single dataset (BBBC021) and single base model, and the modest FID degradation. The framework is explicitly domain-agnostic, suggesting applicability to other scientific imaging tasks governed by non-differentiable physical rules.
