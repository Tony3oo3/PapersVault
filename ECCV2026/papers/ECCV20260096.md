## Problem

When geometry tokens from pretrained 3D foundation models are naively fused with 2D vision tokens and fine-tuned under standard spatial-reasoning supervision, VLMs tend to ignore the geometry stream and fall back on appearance-driven 2D shortcuts. The paper shows this underutilization is reproducible: in static scenes geometry injection yields only marginal gains, and in dynamic scenes it can even degrade performance below the no-geometry baseline. The core question is how to compel a VLM to treat injected geometry as actionable evidence rather than dispensable auxiliary context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260096/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of monocular video frames (or image frames for static scenes) and a text prompt; a frozen pretrained 3D geometry model (VGGT for static, π³ for dynamic) extracts implicit geometry tokens from the same frames without requiring explicit depth sensors or multi-stage reconstruction. During training, a binary mask is applied to a subset of 2D vision tokens (disabled at inference), and the fused token sequence—concatenated with text tokens and, for dynamic scenes, the compact QFormer bottleneck output Z_G—is fed to the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone to produce a spatial-reasoning answer (numerical estimate or multiple-choice selection).

## Method

GeoSR introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, Geometry-Unleashing Masking suppresses 2D appearance shortcuts during training: for static scenes it applies MAE-style random masking (ratio γ=0.8, enable probability β=0.5) over vision tokens, while for dynamic scenes it derives a per-token relevance score from QFormer cross-attention weights and performs TopK masking on the most geometry-critical regions, forcing the model to consult the geometry stream. Second, Geometry-Guided Fusion replaces naive additive or concatenation fusion with a learned sigmoid gate α = σ(W_g[LN(V)||LN(G)] + b_g) that produces a token- and channel-wise mixing coefficient, adaptively amplifying geometry contributions where geometric evidence is informative. For dynamic scenes, the compact bottleneck evidence Z_G is first redistributed back to full spatiotemporal resolution via a second cross-attention before gating. Only the fusion module and VLM backbone are updated; all tokenizers and the 3D geometry model remain frozen, keeping the parameter overhead minimal (~0.07B extra) and inference cost negligible.

## Results

On VSI-Bench (static spatial reasoning), GeoSR achieves an average score of 51.9, outperforming the strongest spatial-reasoning baseline VG-LLM (50.7) and all proprietary models (GPT-4o: 47.8, Gemini-1.5-Pro: 48.8). On DSR-Bench (dynamic spatial reasoning), GeoSR achieves an average of 66.1, surpassing the prior best spatial-reasoning method GSM (58.9) by 7.2 points and VG-LLM (38.4) by 27.7 points, while also exceeding all proprietary APIs (Gemini-2.5-Pro: 31.7, GPT-5: 30.8) and general video models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 23.5). The gains are most pronounced on dynamic subtasks such as absolute direction (84.4 vs. GSM's 73.8) and relative distance (79.5 vs. GSM's 75.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the dynamic DSR-Bench, GeoSR (66.1 avg) decisively outperforms the closest spatial-reasoning competitor GSM (58.9) by 7.2 points, with the largest margins on absolute direction (+10.6), absolute speed (+9.5), and relative speed (+15.9). Against the other geometry-injection method VG-LLM (38.4), GeoSR leads by 27.7 points, demonstrating that adaptive gated fusion and query-guided masking are far more effective than naive QFormer concatenation. All proprietary APIs (GPT-4o: 26.4, GPT-5: 30.8, Gemini-2.5-Pro: 31.7) and general video models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 23.5, Qwen3-VL-30B: 31.1, LLaVA-Video-7B: 25.9, InternVL3.5-38B: 26.7) are substantially outperformed, confirming that explicit geometric evidence is critical for dynamic spatial reasoning. On the static VSI-Bench, GeoSR (51.9) edges out VG-LLM (50.7) by 1.2 points and Spatial-MLLM (48.4) by 3.5 points, while also surpassing the strongest proprietary model Gemini-1.5-Pro (48.8) and the largest open model InternVL3-78B (48.4). The inference-time ablation (Table 5) further confirms that GeoSR genuinely depends on geometry tokens at test time: removing them drops the dynamic score from 66.1 to 63.2 (−2.9), whereas the same intervention on the GSM baseline actually improves it slightly (62.8 → 63.1), indicating that prior methods treat geometry as dispensable.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoSR builds directly on the geometry-token injection paradigm established by VG-LLM (additive fusion of VGGT features for static scenes) and GSM (QFormer-based fusion of π³ features for dynamic 4D reasoning). Both prior works follow the same recipe—extract implicit geometry from a pretrained 3D foundation model, fuse it with 2D vision tokens, and fine-tune under spatial QA supervision. GeoSR departs from this by identifying that the naive fusion + standard fine-tuning pipeline leaves geometry tokens underutilized, and introduces two targeted interventions: MAE-style masking of vision tokens during training to suppress appearance shortcuts, and a learned sigmoid gate for fine-grained, token-and-channel-wise routing of geometry evidence at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that geometry injection can be neutral or even harmful under standard training challenges a core assumption in the geometry-aware VLM literature—that adding 3D priors should monotonically improve spatial reasoning. This reframes the design question from 'how to inject geometry' to 'how to make the model actually use it,' which has implications beyond spatial reasoning for any auxiliary-signal integration in VLMs. Open questions include whether the masking ratio and gating mechanism transfer to other auxiliary modalities (e.g., depth, optical flow), whether the approach scales to larger backbones or longer video contexts, and whether the reliance on specific 3D foundation models (VGGT, π³) limits generality. The computational overhead is minimal (~0.04s, ~0.07B params), making the technique practical for adoption.
