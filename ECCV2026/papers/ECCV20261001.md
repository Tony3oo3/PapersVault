## Problem

Most end-to-end autonomous driving systems learn a single average driving style, ignoring the inherent diversity of human driving behavior shaped by individual habits and cognitive patterns. Achieving truly individual-level personalization is blocked by three gaps: the absence of large-scale datasets with driver-identity annotations supporting closed-loop evaluation, the lack of quantitative metrics to measure inter-driver style differences, and the absence of algorithms that learn continuous stylized representations directly from user trajectories rather than relying on coarse categorical labels.

## Input / Output

The system consumes synchronized multi-modal sensor data (6 RGB cameras, 1 LiDAR, 5 radars, 1 IMU/GNSS, 1 BEV camera, HD-Map) sampled at 10 Hz, together with historical trajectory sequences, to produce a future driving trajectory as control output. A separate lightweight reward model maps any trajectory to a 10-dimensional continuous style vector, which serves as the interface for style evaluation (via MMD and KL divergence) and as the supervision signal during personalization fine-tuning. The style vector is scenario-normalized using per-route min-max scaling to enable fair cross-context comparison.

## Method

The PDSA framework operates in three stages: (1) a DiffusionDrive-based E2E model is pretrained on Bench2Drive using BEV features and historical trajectories; (2) a lightweight MLP reward model (two hidden layers of 256 units, ReLU) is trained to map trajectories to 10-dimensional style vectors, with the BEV map encoded as a global scene vector injected at each trajectory timestep to incorporate environmental context; (3) personalization fine-tunes only the trajectory prediction head by computing MSE between the reward model's style-vector output on predicted trajectories and the target driver's ground-truth style vectors, leaving the pretrained backbone frozen. This plug-and-play design preserves baseline driving safety while injecting individual style, and the style vector indicators are selected via median normalization and variance-based discriminability to capture the most inter-driver-differentiating dimensions.

## Results

On the Person2Drive dataset, PDSA achieves an average MMDSS of 0.6800 (vs. 0.6652 for the baseline), with per-driver improvements of +5.590×10⁻³ (Route 1) and +24.151×10⁻³ (Route 2), all statistically significant (p<0.001, Wilcoxon signed-rank test). Intra-person MMDSS (0.941) substantially exceeds inter-person MMDSS (0.681), confirming the evaluation framework captures individual style. Crucially, style-guided fine-tuning does not degrade driving ability: on Person2Drive the driving score rises from 58.22 to 70.13 (+11.91) with only a 0.5% drop in success rate, and on Bench2Drive the score improves from 54.91 to 55.69 while success rate increases from 13.27% to 15.45%. In a 50-participant human preference study, PDSA trajectories are preferred over the baseline in 76% (Route 1) and 72% (Route 2) of cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDSA outperforms direct fine-tuning (DFT) by +0.0040 in average MMDSS (0.6800 vs. 0.6760) and the no-BEV variant (PDSA-WB) by +0.0015, confirming that BEV-conditioned style supervision yields the tightest style alignment. Against coarse-grained ANC category-level fine-tuning (best ANC model: 0.6174 MMDSS), personalized fine-tuning achieves 0.6800, a gap of +0.0626, demonstrating that individual-level trajectory supervision substantially surpasses three-way style labels. Among alternative style-learning formulations, PDSA (0.7055 MMDSS) beats behavior cloning (0.6811), preference learning (0.6796), direct style-vector supervision (0.6690), and inverse reinforcement learning (0.6591). On driving safety, PDSA improves the driving score by 11.91 points on Person2Drive and 0.78 points on Bench2Drive over the baseline, while maintaining or improving success rate, showing that personalization does not compromise core driving competence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DiffusionDrive as its base E2E-AD model and extends the Bench2Drive data collection infrastructure into a human-in-the-loop, individual-level personalization pipeline. It departs from prior personalized driving work (HITL datasets, StyleDrive, PADriver) by replacing coarse categorical style labels (aggressive/normal/conservative) with continuous, individual-level style vectors learned via a lightweight MLP reward model, and by restricting fine-tuning to only the trajectory prediction head for plug-and-play personalization. The evaluation framework introduces MMDSS and KL divergence as distribution-level style metrics, filling a gap where no standardized quantitative measure of inter-driver style difference existed for E2E-AD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Person2Drive is the first closed-loop, individual-level personalized E2E-AD benchmark, providing a reproducible foundation for studying how driving styles vary across 50 real human drivers and how E2E models can adapt to them. The finding that style-guided fine-tuning slightly improves (rather than degrades) driving safety scores is practically encouraging, though it is demonstrated only with a single base architecture (DiffusionDrive) in CARLA simulation. Key open questions include whether the learned style vectors transfer to real-world driving, whether the approach generalizes beyond 1 km routes and 50 drivers, and whether the plug-and-play head-only fine-tuning strategy holds for other E2E backbones.
