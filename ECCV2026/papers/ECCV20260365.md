## Problem

Existing text-based person anomaly search (TPAS) models learn a single joint embedding in which appearance, action, and background cues are entangled, causing over-reliance on identity-like shortcuts, poor alignment for action-centric queries, and weak generalization under distribution shift. No prior method provides an explicit mechanism to structurally separate and independently align these semantic factors across both visual and textual modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260365/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image paired with a rendered 2D pose map (detected body joints drawn as an image) and a natural-language caption capped at 56 tokens. During training only, each caption is additionally decomposed by Qwen3-8B into appearance, action, and background sub-captions that serve as supervision anchors. The output is a structured token set per modality: a generalized [CLS] retrieval embedding plus three factor-specific tokens (appearance, action, background), where the image [CLS] is excluded from the final recombination to avoid reintroducing globally entangled information already redistributed into the semantic tokens.

## Method

SeDA introduces a Semantic Token Projection (STP) module that projects the global [CLS] token into three semantic queries (appearance, action, background) via a learned linear layer, then uses sigmoid-scored soft attention to aggregate modality tokens relevant to each factor, concatenates each query with its aggregated feature, and projects the result into a compact disentangled token; the three tokens are recombined into a unified retrieval embedding. A Feature Decoupling Loss (FDL) enforces factor-specific semantics by encoding LLM-decomposed sub-captions through the same STP and applying a contrastive objective that aligns each image and full-text semantic token with the corresponding sub-caption token, while a semantic-aware image-text matching loss extends ITM supervision to each factor pair. The architecture builds on the X-VLM/CMP three-encoder design (Swin-B pose-aware image encoder, BERT text encoder, cross-modal encoder) and is trained with a combined objective of contrastive loss, global and semantic ITM losses, masked language modeling, and FDL, with in-batch hard negative mining applied at both the global and factor levels.

## Results

On the PAB dataset (1M pairs), SeDA achieves 86.45% R@1, 99.44% R@5, 99.85% R@10, and 92.59% mAP, surpassing the previous SOTA (CMP) by +1.52 R@1. Notably, SeDA trained on only 0.1M samples already reaches 85.14% R@1, exceeding CMP's full-data result of 84.93%. Under multi-weather conditions, SeDA improves average R@1 by +2.43 over CMP, and on the out-of-distribution UCC evaluation it gains +3.74 R@1 (58.97% vs. 55.23%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeDA outperforms all nine baselines across every reported metric on PAB. Against the closest competitor CMP [23] (the TPAS-specific baseline), SeDA gains +1.52 R@1 and +0.93 mAP at full data scale, and the advantage widens to +3.74 R@1 under OOD evaluation on UCC. A particularly striking result is that SeDA trained on only 0.1M samples (85.14% R@1) already surpasses CMP trained on the full 1M dataset (84.93% R@1), indicating substantially better data efficiency. Among the earlier TBPS methods, the gap is large: CLIP [12] reaches 77.60% R@1, RaSa [2] reaches 80.79%, and X-VLM [27] reaches 81.95% (all at 0.1M), each trailing SeDA's 0.1M result by 7.5–14.6 R@1 points. On the OOD UCC benchmark, SeDA (58.97% R@1) also beats the next-best method CMP (55.23%) and substantially outperforms CLIP (51.60%) and X-VLM (52.33%), confirming that the disentangled representation generalizes better under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CMP framework (Yang et al., ICCV 2025), which introduced the TPAS task and the PAB benchmark, by replacing its single joint embedding with an explicit three-factor decomposition (appearance, action, background) in both visual and textual modalities. The architectural backbone is inherited from X-VLM (Zeng et al., 2021), a multi-grained vision-language pre-training model with pose-aware Swin-B image and BERT text encoders. The key conceptual departure is the Semantic Token Projection module, which derives factor-specific queries from the global [CLS] token and uses sigmoid-weighted soft aggregation to produce disentangled tokens, combined with a Feature Decoupling Loss that leverages LLM-based (Qwen3-8B) caption decomposition as training-time supervision — a mechanism absent from prior TBPS/TPAS methods that learn entangled representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit factorization addresses a concrete failure mode in TPAS: over-reliance on identity cues at the expense of action-centric and contextual queries, which are central to anomaly detection. The +3.74 R@1 gain under OOD evaluation (UCC) and the multi-weather robustness improvement suggest that structured disentanglement yields better generalization than monolithic embeddings, a finding with potential transfer to other cross-modal retrieval settings. However, the approach is tied to a fixed three-factor decomposition, the LLM-based caption splitting adds training overhead (+16% time, +19.4% memory), and the main-benchmark improvement over CMP (+1.52 R@1) is modest. Open questions include whether the factorization generalizes to more than three semantic axes, whether the LLM decomposition step can be replaced by learned soft labels, and whether the STP mechanism transfers to other CLIP-based retrieval tasks beyond person search.
