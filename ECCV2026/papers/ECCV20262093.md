## Problem

State-of-the-art flow-matching image editing models require 30+ sequential denoising steps, creating significant inference latency that hinders practical deployment. Existing acceleration techniques (pruning, quantization, distillation, caching) are largely adapted from general generation and do not exploit the specific requirement of image editing to simultaneously preserve structure and apply semantic changes, often degrading fidelity. The paper asks whether a learnable noise prediction module can shift the starting point of the generative trajectory closer to the target, thereby eliminating redundant denoising steps without quality loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262093/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

InstaEdit consumes three inputs: a reference RGB image (typically 1024×1024), a free-form text editing instruction, and a random Gaussian noise tensor in the foundation model's latent space. It outputs a single optimized noise tensor of the same shape as the input noise, which is then fed into the frozen (or LoRA-tuned) foundation model for only 4 denoising steps to produce the final edited image. The InstaEdit module itself adds fewer than 5% of the foundation model's parameters and introduces roughly 2–4 seconds of overhead on an A100 GPU, but this is offset by the reduction from 30 to 4 sampling steps.

## Method

The architecture has two sequential components. First, a Reparameterization Module (built on a VQ-GAN encoder with self-attention) predicts the mean and variance of a Gaussian distribution conditioned on the reference image and a scheduled starting timestep τ_S, then resamples to produce a refined noise z_rep that embeds global layout constraints. Second, a Singular Value Prediction (SVP) Module applies SVD to z_rep, freezes the singular vectors U and V^T, and learns a cross-modal interaction function F that conditionally modifies the singular value matrix Σ using text embeddings (processed via CFG and AdaGroupNorm), after which an inverse SVD reconstructs the final optimized noise z'_svp. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage I trains only InstaEdit against a frozen foundation model using L2, LPIPS, GAN, and a statistical regularization loss (constraining output noise to μ≈0, σ≈0.1); Stage II introduces LoRA fine-tuning of the foundation model for joint optimization. At inference, InstaEdit runs once to produce the optimized noise, and the foundation model then executes only 4 denoising steps, yielding 3.9–4.2× end-to-end speedup while matching or exceeding the 30-step baseline quality on GEdit-Bench, ImgEdit-Bench, and Kontext-bench.

## Results

On GEdit-Bench, InstaEdit achieves overall VIEScore of 5.67 (SC 6.33, PQ 6.61) with only 4 steps on FLUX.1-Kontext (3.88× speedup), 6.84 (SC 7.34, PQ 7.32) on Step1X-Edit (4.13× speedup), and 7.40 (SC 7.78, PQ 7.52) on FLUX.2-klein-base-9B (4.22× speedup), all matching or exceeding the 30-step baselines. On ImgEdit-Bench, the corresponding overall scores are 6.57, 6.73, and 7.47 with 3.90×, 4.13×, and 4.21× speedups respectively. The method introduces only 2–4 s of additional inference overhead and adds fewer than 5% extra parameters to the foundation model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GEdit-Bench, InstaEdit (4 steps) outperforms RegionE (overall 5.67 vs 5.65 on FLUX.1-Kontext; 6.84 vs 6.82 on Step1X-Edit) and TeaCache (5.67 vs 5.38; 6.84 vs 6.67) while also being faster in wall-clock time (8.98 s vs 14.54 s; 12.75 s vs 20.48 s). Against FlowFast (10 steps, overall 5.45) and LCM (8 steps, overall 5.40), InstaEdit achieves higher overall scores with fewer steps. On FLUX.2-klein-base-9B, InstaEdit at 4 steps (overall 7.40) surpasses the official 8-step distilled variant FLUX.2-klein-9B (overall 7.36), demonstrating that noise optimization can match or exceed distillation-based acceleration. On ImgEdit-Bench, the same pattern holds: InstaEdit leads all baselines on overall score for every foundation model tested. The only case where InstaEdit does not clearly win is on FLUX.2-klein-4B, where the 4-step InstaEdit result (overall 6.95) is close to the 8-step distilled baseline (6.94) but the speedup is limited to 2.57× due to the already-small model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant acceleration paradigm for diffusion/flow-matching models (distillation, caching, pruning, quantization) by instead learning a better starting point on the generative trajectory. It builds conceptually on the noise-optimization line of work (InitNo, TINO-Edit, Qi et al.) that showed initial noise quality affects generation, but repurposes VAE reparameterization and SVD decomposition in a combined architecture specifically conditioned on both reference images and text prompts for the editing task. The SVP module's decision to freeze singular vectors and predict only the singular value spectrum is a design choice informed by matrix perturbation theory, distinguishing it from prior noise-selection methods that operate in the full latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A plug-and-play module adding less than 5% parameters that delivers ~4× speedup across three different foundation models (FLUX.1-Kontext, Step1X-Edit, FLUX.2-klein) while matching or slightly exceeding baseline quality is practically significant for deployment of instruction-based editing at scale. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video editing or multi-turn editing, how it behaves at 1–2 step regimes, and whether the limited additional gain when stacked on already-distilled models (Table 5) reflects a fundamental ceiling or a training-data bottleneck. The paper also leaves the Kontext-bench results and additional qualitative analyses to supplementary material, so the full generality claim is only partially evidenced in the main text.
