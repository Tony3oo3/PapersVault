## Problem

Video reasoning segmentation requires pixel-accurate tracking across hundreds of frames under complex language queries, but the quadratic self-attention cost of dense spatiotemporal tokens makes long-video processing prohibitive. Existing token compression methods operate on raw encoder features lacking temporal context, committing to reduction before the model has understood spatiotemporal structure — yet acquiring that awareness at full resolution incurs the very cost compression aims to eliminate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262583/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sequence of T frames (each 3×H×W) and a natural language query as input. Frames are encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 at 224×224 resolution, yielding 256 tokens per frame with 1024-dimensional features. The output is a set of binary segmentation masks m_t ∈ {0,1}^{H×W} per frame, decoded by SAM2 from language-aligned embeddings. Crucially, the MLLM (LLaVA-7B) ingests only ~15% of the original visual tokens after adaptive compression, and the causal temporal design permits frame-by-frame online inference with constant ~200 ms per-frame latency regardless of sequence length.

## Method

STAC's core insight is that state-space model recurrence inherently produces a redundancy signal: tokens already absorbed by the recurrent state yield near-identical enriched representations, making content redundancy assessable at O(T) cost without full-resolution attention. The architecture has three cascaded stages: (1) a State-informed Spatiotemporal Aggregator (SSA) enriches encoder features via decoupled bidirectional spatial scanning (for symmetric within-frame context) and strictly causal temporal scanning (for motion evolution), both implemented with cascaded Mamba selective-scan modules; (2) a Hierarchical State-adaptive Compression (HSC) module performs temporal-then-spatial token reduction using adaptive thresholds estimated online via exponential moving averages, merging redundant frames (60–70% reduction) and static spatial patches (40–50% reduction) while preserving motion boundaries; (3) task-grounded differentiable compression propagates segmentation gradients through discrete compression decisions via straight-through estimation, so the downstream mask objective directly calibrates which tokens are retained. This enrichment-before-compression strategy, combined with the spatial/temporal decoupling, achieves ~85% token reduction and 1.8× speedup while surpassing compression-free baselines on reasoning segmentation benchmarks in a zero-shot, streaming-compatible setting.

## Results

STAC achieves 73.6 J&F on Ref-DAVIS17, 49.9 on MeViS, 65.2 on Ref-YouTube-VOS, 52.3 on ReasonVOS, and 48.4 on ReVOS, while operating on only ~15% of visual tokens (85% reduction) and delivering a 1.8× speedup. Notably, STAC is trained exclusively on referring segmentation data (MeViS, Refer-YouTube-VOS) yet transfers zero-shot to reasoning benchmarks, outperforming the strongest compression-free baseline (GLUS) on ReasonVOS by 2.5 J&F. The method maintains a constant ~200 ms per-frame inference latency in streaming mode regardless of sequence length, a capability unavailable to bidirectional baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest compression-free MLLM baseline GLUS [46], STAC wins on Ref-DAVIS17 (73.6 vs 71.9), Ref-YouTube (65.2 vs 64.8), and ReasonVOS (52.3 vs 49.8, zero-shot), while trailing slightly on MeViS (49.9 vs 50.3) and ReVOS (48.4 vs 49.5), where GLUS's full bidirectional temporal access provides a marginal advantage. Against other MLLM baselines, STAC leads substantially: +4.8 J&F over VideoLISA-3.8B (Post) on ReasonVOS, +7.1 over TrackGPT-13B on Ref-DAVIS17, and +12.0 over LISA-13B on MeViS. Among specialized referring architectures, STAC surpasses DsHmp [29] by 8.7 J&F on Ref-DAVIS17 and 3.5 on MeViS, though DsHmp retains a 1.9 J&F edge on Ref-YouTube. The compression-based SSM baseline BIMBA [32] and Perceiver [33] are evaluated in Fig. 3 rather than Table 2; STAC maintains superior J&F across all context lengths (2–64 frames) while BIMBA requires full video access and Perceiver saturates early due to its fixed query set. Overall, STAC is the only method combining competitive segmentation accuracy, 85% token reduction, and streaming-compatible causal inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAC builds on the selective state-space model lineage (Mamba, S4/S5, Vision Mamba) but repurposes SSMs not as a processing backbone (as in VideoMamba, BIMBA, STORM) but as a feature-enrichment stage that precedes compression. It departs from prior video token compression methods (FastV, TempMe, LongVU, TSPO) that operate on raw encoder features by arguing that redundancy is only assessable after temporal context has been integrated, and from unified bidirectional SSM video architectures by decoupling spatial (bidirectional) from temporal (causal) scanning to preserve streaming compatibility. The task-grounded differentiable compression via straight-through estimation further distinguishes STAC from heuristic or reconstruction-based compression policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 85% token reduction with 1.8× speedup while matching or exceeding compression-free baselines, combined with streaming-compatible causal design, makes long-video reasoning segmentation deployable in settings where full-sequence access is unavailable. The zero-shot transfer from referring to reasoning benchmarks suggests the learned compression policy captures generalizable motion-critical structure rather than dataset-specific patterns. Open questions include the relationship between recurrent state capacity and achievable compression ratios, the online-vs-offline gap on reasoning queries (48.8 vs 52.3 J&F on ReasonVOS), and whether the framework extends to multimodal streams like audio-visual reasoning.
