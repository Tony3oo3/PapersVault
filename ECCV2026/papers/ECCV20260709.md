## Problem

Existing video text QA datasets (M4-ViteVQA, EgoTextVQA) contain only 13–18% of questions that genuinely require multi-frame temporal perception, allowing models to bypass temporal reasoning by relying on single-frame OCR. This gap means current MLLMs are neither trained nor evaluated on the core challenge of integrating textually distributed cues across video timestamps to form complete semantic interpretations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260709/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes uniformly sampled video frames (up to 768 frames at 448×448 resolution, sourced from 720p videos spanning ~363 hours across 30 domains) alongside a natural-language question. Output is a structured temporal perception chain—interleaving timestamp-tagged text spans with bounding-box locations inside a <think> block—followed by a final answer in an <answer> tag. Keyframes are selected via SimHash-based perceptual hashing, and OCR detections are fused into time spans (text, t_start, t_end, bbox) that preserve both temporal order and spatial persistence of each text instance.

## Method

ViTexQA is built through a three-round human annotation pipeline (generation → quality scoring → revision) that enforces every question be unanswerable from any single frame, yielding 5,147 videos and 6,864 QA pairs with temporally-grounded CoT annotations. FrameThinker, built on Qwen3-VL, employs a two-stage training paradigm: (1) CoT-Guided SFT maximizes the likelihood of generating the full perception-answer sequence with explicit spatiotemporal tags, teaching the model to enumerate detected text per timestamp before deriving an answer; (2) Temporally-grounded RL via GRPO refines this with three composite rewards—format compliance, temporal grounding (measuring overlap between predicted and ground-truth timestamps weighted by text similarity), and binary content correctness—encouraging the model to explore alternative perception paths beyond supervised imitation. The key distinction from prior approaches is the explicit preservation of 'when' and 'where' each text appears (Temporal-Tagged OCR Fusion) rather than simple deduplication, forcing the model to reason about temporal dependencies between text instances.

## Results

On the ViTexQA benchmark (ROUGE-L), FrameThinker (8B, 768 frames) achieves an average score of 73.5%, outperforming the strongest closed-source model Gemini-3 Pro (71.6%) by 1.9 points and the best open-source baseline MiniCPM-V4.5 (70.1%) by 3.4 points. After fine-tuning on ViTexQA training data with CoT-guided SFT and Temporally-grounded RL, FrameThinker reaches 83.5% on ViTexQA and 88.1% on MME-VideoOCR, a +10.0 and +9.8 gain over its zero-shot baseline. All models perform worst on the Rolling Text scenario (FrameThinker: 62.3%), confirming that dynamic cross-frame text integration remains the hardest sub-task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FrameThinker (8B) achieves the highest average ROUGE-L of 73.5% on ViTexQA, beating every compared baseline. Among closed-source models, it surpasses Gemini-3 Pro (71.6%) by 1.9 points, Seed-1.6 (61.1%) by 12.4 points, and GPT-4o (51.6%) by 21.9 points; the paper attributes the open-source advantage to the 128-frame input cap of closed-source APIs versus 768 frames for open-source models. Among open-source baselines, FrameThinker outperforms the closest competitor MiniCPM-V4.5 (70.1%) by 3.4 points, Qwen3-VL 8B (66.7%) by 6.8 points, InternVL3 (64.2%) by 9.3 points, Qwen2.5-VL 7B (63.5%) by 10.0 points, and Qwen3-VL 4B (62.3%) by 11.2 points. Smaller or less capable models trail further: VideoLLaMA3 (52.4%), MiniCPM-V2.6 (50.7%), Keye-VL (39.9%), LongVU (43.1%), and LLaVA-Video (36.0%) all fall 20+ points below FrameThinker. Notably, FrameThinker leads on every individual scenario column (Driving, Advertising, Education, Rolling Text), with the largest margin on Rolling Text (+2.2 over MiniCPM-V4.5) where all models struggle most.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video text QA line of work (M4-ViteVQA, EgoTextVQA, RoadTextVQA, NewsVideoQA) by identifying and addressing a critical gap: the vast majority of questions in existing benchmarks are answerable from a single frame, rendering them effectively static OCR tasks rather than genuine temporal perception problems. Methodologically, FrameThinker builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeek-V3, adapting it with custom multi-frame coherence rewards, and combines it with CoT-guided SFT in the multimodal LLM training paradigm. The Temporal-Tagged OCR Fusion module departs from conventional OCR deduplication by explicitly preserving spatiotemporal metadata (timestamps, bounding boxes, persistence spans) across keyframes, which is essential for constructing the perception chains that anchor both the dataset annotations and the training signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that only 13–18% of questions in leading video text QA datasets genuinely require multi-frame integration is a significant diagnostic for the field, suggesting that prior evaluations have substantially overestimated models' temporal text perception capabilities. ViTexQA, with its enforced cross-frame dependency and ~363 hours of diverse content, provides a much-needed stress test, while FrameThinker demonstrates that explicit temporal perception training (CoT SFT + temporally-grounded RL) yields meaningful gains over standard fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the 100% multi-frame requirement is overly restrictive for practical deployment, whether the approach generalizes to non-textual temporal reasoning, and whether the performance gap between open-source (768-frame) and closed-source (128-frame) models reflects a fundamental architectural limitation or simply an input-budget constraint.
