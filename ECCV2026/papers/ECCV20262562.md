## Problem

Existing Clothes-Changing Person Re-Identification (CC-ReID) research is limited by two gaps: datasets lack comprehensive multi-factor diversity (culture, season, weather, lighting) and fine-grained semantic annotations, and methods treat identity-irrelevant interfering factors (clothes, environment) in isolation rather than constructing a unified subspace for global elimination, severely limiting generalization in real-world scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262562/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single RGB person image and outputs an identity feature embedding via the EVA02-CLIP-L visual backbone (patch size 14). During training, it additionally consumes three types of text descriptions per image—biometric, clothes, and environment—encoded by a pre-trained EVA02-CLIP text encoder, but these textual inputs impose no extra computational cost at test time. The CGCC dataset provides 217,248 images across 4,101 identities and 15 cameras, with each identity having five generated variants coupling different cultural, seasonal, and environmental factors.

## Method

The TGIR framework decomposes textual semantics into identity-relevant biometric texts and identity-irrelevant clothes/environment texts, then applies two synergistic modules. BGPA computes a semantic consistency score between each visual feature and its biometric text via an MLP adapter, uses this score to adaptively weight a momentum-based identity prototype update (suppressing contamination from noisy samples), and enforces intra-class compactness via a prototype contrastive loss. SAOP concatenates clothes and environment text embeddings into an identity-irrelevant matrix, performs SVD to extract principal directions of non-identity variation, and applies a spectrum-weighted orthogonal loss that minimizes the projection of visual features onto this subspace, with weights proportional to singular values to prioritize dominant interference directions. The overall training objective combines standard ID and triplet losses with ITC, BGPA, and SAOP losses in an end-to-end joint optimization.

## Results

Trained on CGCC and evaluated cross-dataset, TGIR achieves the best average performance across LTCC, PRCC, and DeepChange with 54.8% Rank-1, 65.9% Rank-5, and 32.8% mAP, outperforming the strongest competitor MADE (52.7% R1, 32.1% mAP) by 2.1% R1 and 0.7% mAP. On the intra-dataset CGCC benchmark under the challenging clothes-changing (CC) setting, TGIR reaches 57.3% Rank-1 and 28.1% mAP, surpassing Eva02-CLIP (54.7% R1, 26.3% mAP) and MADE (51.2% R1, 23.3% mAP). On LTCC-CC, TGIR attains 57.9% R1 and 32.7% mAP, edging out Differ (57.4% R1, 31.6% mAP) and substantially exceeding MADE (47.4% R1, 24.4% mAP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGIR consistently outperforms all baselines on the cross-dataset generalization benchmark (Table 3), achieving the highest Rank-1 on all three unseen targets (LTCC: 40.3%, PRCC: 62.0%, DEEP: 62.1%) and the best average mAP (32.8%). Against the strongest text-guided competitor MADE [37], TGIR gains +2.1% R1 and +0.7% mAP on average, with the largest margin on DEEP (+2.5% R1, +1.5% mAP). Differ [28], the most recent CVPR'25 method, trails TGIR by 5.0% R1 and 3.4% mAP on average, indicating that its isolated treatment of interfering factors is less effective than TGIR's unified subspace approach. On the intra-dataset LTCC-CC setting, TGIR (57.9% R1) narrowly beats Differ (57.4% R1) but substantially surpasses MADE (47.4% R1) and all earlier methods. On PRCC-CC, TGIR achieves the best R1 (66.3%) but slightly trails Differ on mAP (61.7% vs. 62.6%), while on CGCC-CC TGIR leads all methods by a clear margin (57.3% R1 vs. 54.7% for Eva02-CLIP). TransReID [15] and CAL [11], which lack any clothes-changing-specific mechanism, remain far behind (35.2% and ~30% average R1 respectively), confirming the necessity of explicit clothes-irrelevant feature elimination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CC-ReID line of work that has progressed from structural-cue methods (contours, 3D shapes, gait) to language/attribute-supervised approaches like MADE and Differ. It departs from prior semantic-supervision methods by arguing they treat clothes and environment interference in isolation, and instead proposes a unified identity-irrelevant subspace constructed jointly from both text types, then eliminated via SVD-based orthogonal projection. The dataset construction repurposes MLLMs (Qwen3-VL) and controllable image editing (Qwen-Image-Edit) to synthesize multi-factor diversity (culture, region, season, weather, light) around MSMT17 seed identities, a pipeline not previously applied to CC-ReID benchmarking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CGCC dataset (4,101 IDs, 217K images with fine-grained semantic annotations) fills a genuine gap: no prior CC-ReID benchmark simultaneously covers cultural, regional, seasonal, and weather variations with structured text supervision, making it a practically important resource for the community. The TGIR framework's SVD-based subspace projection offers a clean, interpretable mechanism for interference removal, though the performance gains over the strongest baseline (MADE) are modest (~2% R1, ~0.7% mAP average). Key open questions include whether the synthetic-to-real generalization gap is truly closed, whether the SVD subspace approach scales to more complex or adversarial interference, and whether the MLLM-generated annotations introduce systematic biases that limit downstream robustness.
