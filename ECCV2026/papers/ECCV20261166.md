## Problem

Existing 3DGS stylization methods apply 2D feature-matching losses independently per rendered view, causing two distinct failures: within a single view, nearest-neighbor matching degenerates into many-to-one assignments that produce repetitive textures, and across viewpoints, the same 3D content is matched to different style patterns, yielding blurred or inconsistent stylization from novel angles. No prior work addresses both issues within the matching mechanism itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261166/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of multi-view RGB images of a scene (forward-facing LLFF or unbounded 360° scenes from T&T and Mip-NeRF 360) plus a single 2D style reference image from WikiArt. It produces a stylized 3DGS scene where only the color (spherical-harmonic) parameters of the Gaussians are updated while all geometric attributes remain frozen. Depth supervision during reconstruction is obtained from a pretrained depth estimator (Depth Anything V2), and style/content features are extracted with a frozen VGG-16 network.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, an enhanced reconstruction augments vanilla 3DGS training with a depth L1 loss, a shape regularization penalizing anisotropy (longest/shortest axis ratio), and a scale regularization bounding the longest axis, yielding smaller, more uniform Gaussians suitable for fine-grained texture. Second, the stylization stage introduces a Capacity-Controlled Feature Transport (CCFT) loss that reformulates nearest-neighbor feature matching as an entropy-regularized semi-balanced optimal transport problem on a KNN bipartite sparse support, with a tunable column-capacity constraint (enforced via KL divergence and solved by Sinkhorn-Knopp) that suppresses many-to-one matching and controls style diversity. A cross-view matching guidance term further biases the nearest-neighbor search by incorporating a guidance map computed from the previous viewpoint's transport matrix, encouraging the same 3D content to match consistent style patterns across adjacent viewpoints. A VGG-feature MSE content loss preserves scene structure, and color transfer is applied before and after optimization to maintain palette consistency with the style image.

## Results

On 60 test cases (10 scenes × 6 style images) across LLFF, Tanks and Temples, and Mip-NeRF 360, the proposed method achieves the best score on all four metrics: ArtFID 22.801 (style quality), Structure Loss 0.0318 (content preservation), Short MEt3R 0.1196, and Long MEt3R 0.2795 (multi-view consistency). The closest competitor, StylizedGS, trails by 0.27 in ArtFID and 0.0144 in Structure Loss. A user study with 25 participants (150 rankings per aspect) confirms the quantitative lead, with the proposed method receiving 32.22% top-1 preference for style, 37.61% for content, and 37.61% for consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on every metric. Against StylizedGS (the closest competitor using NNFM loss), the gap is modest on ArtFID (22.801 vs. 23.068) but substantial on Structure Loss (0.0318 vs. 0.0462), reflecting better content preservation. ABC-GS, which uses a FAST loss to mitigate many-to-one matching, is competitive on Structure Loss (0.0357) but still trails the proposed method by 0.004 in that metric and by 2.2 in ArtFID. SGSST (Gram-matrix-based) and CLIPGaussian (CLIP-based) are clearly outperformed, with SGSST showing the worst multi-view consistency (Long MEt3R 0.3064) and CLIPGaussian the second-worst ArtFID (27.293). StyleGaussian (feed-forward AdaIN) lags most dramatically on style quality (ArtFID 31.855), confirming that low-order statistic alignment cannot capture fine-grained brushstroke or texture details. The ablated 'Ours (3DGS)' variant (vanilla 3DGS reconstruction + CCFT stylization) already beats all baselines, indicating that the capacity-controlled transport loss is the primary driver of improvement, while the enhanced reconstruction provides a small additional boost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS-based stylization line (StylizedGS, ABC-GS, SGSST) by reformulating the Nearest-Neighbor Feature Matching (NNFM) loss inherited from ARF as a semi-balanced optimal transport problem with explicit column-capacity constraints. Rather than augmenting features with global descriptors (MultiStyleGS) or adding geometric regularizers as a side constraint, it addresses the matching mechanism itself: the capacity parameter directly controls how many content features can be assigned to each style feature, suppressing the many-to-one degeneracy that plagues all NNFM-based 3D stylization. The cross-view matching guidance, inspired by Guided Correspondence Distance, is a novel addition that no prior 3DGS stylization method has incorporated into the feature-matching loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The capacity-controlled OT formulation provides a principled, single-knob mechanism for trading off style diversity against matching fidelity, which is practically useful for artists and content creators who need controllable stylization. The cross-view guidance addresses a gap that all prior 3DGS stylization methods ignored, making the approach more suitable for AR/VR and virtual production where multi-view coherence is essential. Key limitations remain: the method requires per-scene optimization (no feed-forward inference), and the cross-view guidance introduces a measurable trade-off between consistency and content preservation that has no clean resolution. Future work on feed-forward or real-time variants would significantly broaden applicability.
