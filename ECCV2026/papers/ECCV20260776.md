## Problem

2D foundation models (DINOv2, CLIP, SAM) produce inconsistent features for the same 3D point across different viewpoints, creating a fundamental bottleneck for 3D-aware applications. Existing lifting methods either process images independently and fail under large viewpoint changes, or recover explicit geometry at the expense of the semantic manifold (catastrophic forgetting). No prior method simultaneously achieves strong multi-view 3D consistency while preserving the native zero-shot semantic capabilities of the base model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260776/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes M RGB images (512×512) of a static scene along with their camera poses as input and produces a per-pixel dense feature map for each image in the same channel dimension as the base model, such that corresponding 3D points yield consistent features across views. A pose-free variant omits the ray-embedding concatenation, trading some geometric accuracy for applicability when camera data is unavailable. The output features are designed to be directly usable as dense descriptors for downstream tasks (correspondence, normal estimation, segmentation) without additional per-scene optimization.

## Method

The core idea is to 'inflate' a pretrained 2D transformer by interleaving multi-view attention adapters after each transformer block, where each adapter flattens all patches across all M views into a single joint sequence for global cross-view self-attention. The adapter output is merged back via a zero-initialized linear projection (zero-gate), guaranteeing identity mapping at initialization so the pretrained semantic manifold is preserved. Camera geometry is injected through Plücker coordinate ray embeddings (origin and direction per pixel) projected into the latent space and concatenated channel-wise. Training uses LoRA fine-tuning (rank 32) on the backbone alongside full fine-tuning of the adapters, with a geometry-aware dense correspondence loss (SoftArgMax over cosine similarity maps) that penalizes Euclidean distance between predicted and ground-truth correspondences, plus a regularization term enforcing cosine similarity and norm preservation relative to the original features.

## Results

On correspondence localization, the proposed method reduces error from 0.1029 (DINOv2) to 0.0247 on ScanNet++ and from 0.1404 to 0.0782 on the 8-dataset generalization set — a 4× improvement over the base model. For 3D-aligned surface normal estimation on NAVI, the method achieves 32.17° RMSE versus 56.00° for DINOv2 and 51.00° for the best lifting baseline (FiT3D), with 69.3% recall at 30° threshold. On semantic preservation, the adapted features maintain 0.9376 cosine similarity to original DINOv2 features and 58.02% zero-shot Top-1 ImageNet accuracy (vs. 62.50% for unmodified DINOv2), substantially outperforming all lifting baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against three feed-forward lifting baselines (MEF, FiT3D, SnD) and the unmodified DINOv2 backbone. On correspondence localization, Ours achieves 0.0247 error on ScanNet++, outperforming FiT3D (0.0858), SnD (0.0876), and MEF (0.0978) by 3–4×, and generalizes to 0.0782 on unseen scenes where FiT3D degrades to 0.1378. On semantic preservation, Ours maintains 0.9376 cosine similarity to DINOv2 features, far exceeding FiT3D (0.7484) and SnD (0.6125), while MEF actually produces negative similarity (−0.0249). For 3D-aligned normal estimation, Ours achieves 32.17° RMSE, beating FiT3D (51.00°), SnD (53.61°), and MEF (54.77°) by 19–23°. The method also generalizes to CLIP, DINOv3, and SAM backbones without architectural changes, reducing localization error by 5–8× across all three (e.g., SAM: 0.1846→0.0244 on ScanNet++).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'inflation' paradigm from 3D/video generation (where 2D layers are expanded to handle higher-order dimensions) into the feature-lifting domain, applying it to make 2D foundation model features 3D-consistent. It departs from prior feed-forward lifting methods (FiT3D, MEF, SnD) that process each image independently and from geometry-focused approaches (VGGT, π³) that sacrifice the semantic manifold, by interleaving 3D-attention adapters directly within pre-trained transformer layers for joint multi-view processing. The training pipeline builds on the geometry-aware dense correspondence loss of Zhang et al. [49] but replaces explicit 3D reconstruction (NeRF, 3DGS) with COLMAP-derived correspondences, decoupling geometric supervision from per-scene optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides a practical, unified recipe for adding 3D awareness to any transformer-based foundation model without per-scene optimization or model-specific data pipelines, which is a significant practical simplification over prior lifting methods. The pose-free variant broadens applicability to settings where camera parameters are unavailable. Open questions include scalability to very large view counts, applicability to dynamic or non-static scenes, and whether the correspondence-based training can be replaced with self-supervised or synthetic signals to reduce dependence on COLMAP. The framework's generality across DINOv2, DINOv3, CLIP, and SAM suggests it could become a standard preprocessing step for 3D-aware applications built on 2D foundation models.
