## Problem

Most semi-supervised medical image segmentation (SSMIS) frameworks assume labeled and unlabeled data are i.i.d., defaulting to random sampling for the labeled set. In low-data regimes (e.g., 1/16 ratio), random sampling causes severe representation bias—selected samples clump into local sub-clusters and fail to anchor the global anatomical manifold, weakening initial supervision. The paper reframes the labeled/unlabeled split as a domain shift problem and addresses both the biased source domain and the cross-domain distribution gap during training.

## Input / Output

The method accepts 2D medical images (ultrasound, X-ray, dermoscopy) and 3D volumes (MRI) converted to 2D slices, all resized to 518×518 for training. For 3D cases, a Structural Centering strategy extracts M central slices to form a fixed-length feature tensor, preserving core anatomical regions while discarding uninformative end slices. The model (DINOv2-Small backbone + DPT decoder) outputs pixel-level segmentation masks, evaluated at 256×256 (ACDC) or 224×224 (other datasets) using DSC, IoU, ASD, and HD95.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. Stage 1 (offline) projects all images into a 1536-dim embedding space using four intermediate DINOv2 layers with global average pooling and L2 normalization, then applies a Density-K-Center algorithm that greedily selects samples maximizing density-weighted distance to already-chosen anchors—balancing global diversity with local typicality to build a representative labeled set. Stage 2 (online) introduces the Memory-guided Copy-Paste (MCP) module, which runs three branches (labeled, unlabeled, mixed) and maintains category-specific Semantic Memory Banks (separate foreground and background queues of 1024 patches) to retrieve semantically consistent patches via KL-divergence matching, directly addressing the extreme class imbalance in medical scans. A progressive activation schedule (10% epoch ramp-up) introduces branches in easy-to-hard order—labeled first, then mixed, then unlabeled—to suppress early pseudo-label noise. The entire pipeline builds on the UniV2 teacher-student architecture with DINOv2-Small and DPT decoder.

## Results

On the six medical datasets, the proposed method achieves the best DSC across all labeled ratios. At the most challenging low-label settings, it reaches 79.8% DSC on BUSI (1/16), 52.9% DSC on PMTCXR (1/16), 88.7% DSC on ISIC (1/80), 91.0% DSC on CAMUS (1/16), 87.3% DSC on PROMISE (1/16), and 90.4% DSC on ACDC (1/20). The largest gains over the primary baseline UniV2 appear at extreme scarcity: +5.2% DSC on BUSI, +5.3% on PROMISE, and +5.3% on PMTCXR at 1/16. Boundary metrics also improve substantially, e.g., HD95 drops from 48.8 to 36.2 pixels on BUSI (1/16) and from 52.0 to 46.0 on PMTCXR (1/16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven SSMIS baselines (BCP, MiDSS, ABD, RCP, AdaMix, SynFoc, UniV2) on every dataset and every labeled ratio. The largest margins are against UniV2 (the strongest baseline using the same DINOv2-S encoder): +5.2% DSC on BUSI (1/16), +5.3% on PROMISE (1/16), and +5.3% on PMTCXR (1/16), where most competing methods collapse below 40% DSC while the proposed method maintains 52.9%. Against SynFoc (the second-strongest method using MedSAM), gains are +8.5% DSC on BUSI (1/16) and +14.1% on PMTCXR (1/16). On the 3D PROMISE dataset at 1/16 (only 2 labeled cases), the method achieves 87.3% DSC versus 82.0% for UniV2 and 85.7% for SynFoc. In the sampling-algorithm ablation (Table 5), Density-K-Center (DKC) outperforms K-Means by +2.3% DSC, Facility Location by +3.1% DSC, and standard K-Center by +0.4% DSC at 1/16 on BUSI, confirming the density-weighted selection is critical. The plug-and-play experiments (Table 6) further show that simply replacing random sampling with DKC improves AdaMix by +6.5% DSC and SynFoc by +5.5% DSC at 1/16, validating the sampling module as a standalone contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the UniV2 framework (DINOv2-Small + DPT decoder, teacher-student architecture) and extends the Copy-Paste augmentation lineage initiated by BCP, adding a category-specific Semantic Memory Bank with KL-divergence retrieval and a progressive activation schedule. Conceptually, it departs from the prevailing i.i.d. assumption in SSMIS by reframing the labeled/unlabeled split as a domain shift problem, drawing on unsupervised domain adaptation principles. The offline sample selection component extends the K-Center coreset algorithm with a density-weighted objective, leveraging frozen Vision Foundation Model embeddings to identify representative anchors rather than relying on random sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the Distribution-Aware Sample Selection is the most practically impactful contribution: it requires no architectural changes and can be dropped into any existing SSMIS pipeline, yielding substantial gains (up to +6.5% DSC) in extreme low-label regimes where clinical annotation budgets are most constrained. The paper's limitation of processing 3D volumes slice-by-slice (rather than natively in 3D) and its dependence on a specific VFM (DINOv2) for the offline stage are notable constraints for broader adoption. Open questions include whether the density-weighted selection generalizes to truly heterogeneous multi-site/multi-modality settings and whether the memory bank mechanism scales to 3D volumetric copy-paste without prohibitive memory costs.
