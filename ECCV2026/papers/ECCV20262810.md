## Problem

Egocentric trajectory prediction for AR-based assistive navigation and social robotics remains underexplored because existing datasets are captured from bird's-eye or static cameras, are restricted to indoor environments, lack synchronized gaze data, or involve only a single participant. No prior outdoor egocentric dataset jointly provides 6DoF head pose, per-frame 3D gaze vectors, RGB video, and scene annotations from diverse participants navigating self-chosen urban routes on a consumer AR headset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262810/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes a 1.5-second observation window of 30 Hz ego-translation and rotation (position, quaternion, linear/angular velocity), calibrated gaze projected to normalized (u,v) pixel coordinates, and optional auxiliary streams (YOLOv8-Pose pedestrian keypoints, OneFormer semantic segmentation, Depth Anything V2 relative depth) to predict a 3.5-second future trajectory and head rotation. All modalities are time-synchronized per frame in HDF5 files, with gaze-to-pixel alignment verified via a per-session quadratic calibration model fitted to the MQPro's eye-tracking cameras.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is the EgoTraj dataset (75 sessions, 10.7 hours, 1.15M frames from Meta Quest Pro) paired with a standardized benchmarking protocol. For evaluation, they adapt CXA-Transformer's cascaded cross-attention architecture to progressively fuse ego-motion, social context, scene segmentation, depth, and gaze streams, and also adapt EgoCast's Transformer forecasting module for direct trajectory prediction. Ablation studies with CXA-Transformer quantify each modality's contribution, showing that gaze (u,v) and scene segmentation provide the largest individual gains over ego-motion alone, and that the full combination (Y+P+S+G) achieves the best ADE of 0.12 m. Scene annotations are generated by Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct with an example-driven prompt enforcing egocentric narration, achieving 96% structural compliance and Cohen's κ of 0.83–0.96 against human annotators.

## Results

On the EgoTraj held-out test split (80/10/10 session split, T_obs=1.5 s → T_pred=3.5 s), the adapted EgoCast model achieves the best trajectory forecasting with ADE 0.16 m and FDE 0.28 m, while CXA-Transformer achieves the lowest head-rotation error (L1_head 0.69). Kinematic baselines from LookOut (Const_Vel, Lin_Ext) perform substantially worse, with Lin_Ext yielding the worst rotation accuracy (L1_head 1.39). The full multimodal ablation (Y+P+S+G with CXA-Transformer) further reduces ADE to 0.12 m and FDE to 0.23 m, confirming that gaze and scene cues add complementary signal beyond ego-motion alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks five adapted methods on EgoTraj, drawing baselines from three prior egocentric works. EgoCast [4] (adapted from a full-body pose forecaster) wins on trajectory prediction, achieving ADE 0.16 m and FDE 0.28 m, outperforming CXA-Transformer [31] (ADE 0.19 m, FDE 0.29 m) and the LookOut [26] Transformer baseline (ADE 0.20 m, FDE 0.32 m). However, CXA-Transformer [31] achieves the best head-rotation accuracy (L1_head 0.69), beating EgoCast (0.78) and M_Transformer (0.74), indicating its cascaded cross-attention better captures orientation dynamics. The three LookOut [26] kinematic baselines (Const_Vel, Lin_Ext, M_Transformer) are consistently the weakest, with Lin_Ext producing the worst overall errors (ADE 0.26 m, FDE 0.39 m, L1_head 1.39), confirming that simple per-axis regression fails on nonlinear head motion. No single method dominates all three metrics, highlighting the complementary strengths of attention-based and pose-forecasting architectures for egocentric trajectory prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoTraj extends the egocentric trajectory prediction line initiated by LookOut, EgoCogNav, and EgoNav, which each provided partial combinations of pose, gaze, or video but were limited in scale, environment, or modality coverage. It departs from prior work by using a consumer AR headset (Meta Quest Pro) rather than custom sensor rigs or research-grade devices, and by jointly providing synchronized 6DoF head pose, per-frame 3D gaze, egocentric RGB video, and VLM-generated scene annotations across 75 participants navigating self-chosen outdoor routes. The benchmarking builds on CXA-Transformer and EgoCast architectures, adapting them to the egocentric 6DoF setting, while the scene annotation pipeline repurposes Qwen2.5-VL-32B for structured navigation-relevant narration—a novel application of VLMs to trajectory dataset curation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoTraj fills a concrete gap for AR-based assistive navigation and socially compliant robotics by providing the first outdoor egocentric dataset with full gaze-6DoF-video-annotation synchronization at a scale (75 sessions, 10.7 h) that enables meaningful train/test splits. The ablation confirming that gaze adds complementary predictive signal beyond ego-motion and scene context supports the broader hypothesis that visual attention is a first-class cue for intent-aware trajectory forecasting. Key limitations include the single-headset constraint, urban-only environments, and the demonstrated failure on abrupt ~90° turns, which points to the need for uncertainty-aware or intent-disambiguation models. Open questions include whether the multimodal gains transfer to longer horizons, more diverse geographies, and real-time deployment on the headset's own compute.
