## Problem

Multimodal instruction tuning datasets contain a substantial fraction of visually redundant samples (solvable from text alone) and multimodally misaligned samples (where the image contradicts the annotation), yet existing data selection methods treat samples holistically and never explicitly measure the visual modality's independent contribution. This means selected subsets can still favor linguistically easy shortcuts or retain harmful misaligned supervision that degrades cross-modal reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261028/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard multimodal instruction-tuning triplets (RGB image, textual instruction, target response) and produces a scalar Visual Necessity Score per sample, followed by a filtered and ranked training subset. A key implementation detail is the 'Blind Forward Pass': image token positions are replaced with padding tokens and their attention contributions are zeroed via the attention mask, so the model architecture and parameters remain identical to the standard pass while visual information is fully suppressed. The final output is a task-diverse subset (e.g., 15% of the original dataset) selected via intra-cluster top-r% ranking after discarding all samples with non-positive VisNec scores.

## Method

VisNec computes a per-sample score as the difference between a text-only (blind) loss and the standard multimodal loss, directly quantifying how much the visual input reduces predictive uncertainty beyond linguistic context. This counterfactual comparison is grounded in V-usable information theory: a positive score indicates the image genuinely resolves uncertainty (vision-critical), near-zero indicates redundancy, and negative indicates misalignment where the image actively misleads the model. To preserve task diversity, the method applies K-Means clustering (K=20) over question embeddings to group samples by semantic intent, then performs intra-cluster selection by discarding all samples with S_VisNec ≤ 0 and retaining the top-r% by score within each cluster. The entire scoring pipeline requires only two forward passes per sample (no backpropagation, no external APIs), making it substantially cheaper than gradient-based or API-dependent selection methods while achieving 100.2% of full-data performance on LLaVA-665K and 115.8% on Vision-Flan-186K using just 15% of the data.

## Results

On LLaVA-665K with a 15% sampling budget (98K samples), VisNec achieves 100.2% relative performance, surpassing the full-data baseline and all 10 baselines (best baseline: PreSel/CoIDO at 97.7%). On the more challenging Vision-Flan-186K dataset (28K samples), VisNec reaches 115.8% relative performance, exceeding full-data training by 15.8% and the best baseline (CoIDO, 103.6%) by 12.2%. The method also transfers across architectures: Qwen2.5-VL at 3B/7B/32B scales achieves 103.8%/104.0%/102.4% relative performance using only 15% of LLaVA-665K. Total computational cost is 23.0 GPU-hours (12.0 for selection + 11.0 for fine-tuning) versus 76.0 GPU-hours for full-data training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisNec outperforms all 10 baselines on both datasets. On LLaVA-665K, the closest competitors are PreSel and CoIDO (both 97.7%), followed by XMAS (97.3%) and ICONS (97.1%); VisNec leads these by 2.3–3.1 percentage points in relative performance. The largest margins are over Self-Filter (89.3%, +10.9 pp) and EL2N (91.9%, +8.3 pp), which rely on generic importance or loss-based signals without modality-aware contrast. On Vision-Flan-186K, the gap widens substantially: CoIDO (103.6%) and ICONS (103.2%) are the nearest baselines, yet VisNec still leads by 12.2 and 12.6 pp respectively, while weaker methods like IFD (91.6%) and CLIP-Score (92.2%) trail by over 23 pp. Notably, VisNec is the only method that exceeds 100% relative performance on both datasets, and it uniquely surpasses full-data training on hallucination-sensitive benchmarks (MM-Vet: 32.1 vs. 30.0; POPE: 86.0 vs. 86.4 on LLaVA-665K), confirming that filtering misaligned samples improves robustness beyond what any baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisNec extends the V-usable information framework (Ethayarajh et al., 2021) from single-modal text quality assessment to the multimodal setting, introducing a counterfactual 'blind forward pass' that isolates the visual modality's marginal contribution to predictive loss. This departs from prior data selection methods (IFD, EL2N, gradient-based approaches like ICONS) that score samples holistically without disentangling visual from textual informativeness, and from API-dependent methods (PreSel, CoIDO) that outsource quality judgment to external models. The semantic clustering component follows standard diversity-preserving selection (K-Means over instruction embeddings), but is applied specifically to maintain task coverage while ranking by visual necessity within each cluster.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 15% VisNec-selected data matches or exceeds full-data performance (100.2% on LLaVA-665K, 115.8% on Vision-Flan-186K) with 70% compute reduction suggests that a substantial fraction of current multimodal instruction data is not merely redundant but actively harmful, degrading cross-modal reasoning. This reframes the data curation problem from 'find the most important samples' to 'find samples where vision is genuinely necessary,' with practical implications for scaling next-generation MLLMs. Open questions include whether the blind forward pass generalizes to multi-image or video inputs, whether the approach transfers to pre-training stages, and whether the out-of-distribution blind input introduces biases that could matter in absolute (rather than relative) scoring regimes.
