## Problem

Multimodal LLMs align individual visual tokens to language semantics via projection adapters, but this identity-level alignment does not guarantee that pairwise relational structures between concepts survive the crossing from language to vision. As a result, models that can textually reason about spatial or categorical relationships still fail to apply those same relationships in visual contexts, because the geometric configuration of visual tokens remains inconsistent with the language-side relational geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261526/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MIRROR operates as a training-time regularizer on standard MLLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5 and LLaVA-NeXT at 7B/13B scales), taking RGB images and text prompts as input and producing text responses as output. It introduces no new parameters or inference overhead: all geometric quantities (self-attention matrices, cross-attention coupling) are extracted from the forward pass already computed during training, and the SI-GW loss is active only during fine-tuning. At test time, the model architecture and computational cost are identical to the baseline MLLM.

## Method

MIRROR formulates cross-modal relational alignment as a Semi-Inverse Gromov-Wasserstein (SI-GW) problem: given the text-space distance matrix D^t (derived from symmetrized, log-transformed self-attention) and the cross-attention coupling C as fixed anchors, it solves for the ideal visual distance matrix D̂^v that best preserves language relational structure under C. The key theoretical result is that this inverse problem admits a unique closed-form solution D̂^v = (C^T D^t C) ⊘ (bb^T), reducing the quartic GW objective to a Frobenius-norm loss computable in O(n_t²n_v + n_v²n_t) rather than O(n_t²n_v²). Three extraction strategies ensure stability at scale: layer decoupling (text geometry from an early LLM layer, visual geometry from the final ViT layer, coupling from an intermediate LLM layer), selective head aggregation (retaining only the top-k lowest-entropy cross-attention heads), and position-aware soft filtering of non-semantic tokens. The total training objective is L_total = L_LM + λ·L_SI-GW with λ = 0.002, applied as a single-epoch fine-tuning step on the standard MLLM training data.

## Results

On GQA structured relational reasoning, MIRROR yields consistent overall gains across all four model configurations: +0.77% (LLaVA-1.5-7B), +0.34% (LLaVA-1.5-13B), +0.82% (LLaVA-NeXT-7B), and +0.62% (LLaVA-NeXT-13B), with the largest improvements in Global tasks (+1.04% to +3.16%) and Relation tasks on LLaVA-NeXT (+1.07% to +1.44%). On BLINK cross-level reasoning, all configurations improve by +1.4% to +2.5%, with notable gains in spatial reasoning (+4.9%), counting (+4.2%), and semantic correspondence (up to +7.9%). On general benchmarks, LLaVA-1.5-7B + MIRROR achieves 81.1 on VQAv2 and 86.3 on POPE, while LLaVA-NeXT-13B + MIRROR reaches 66.1 on GQA and 86.5 on POPE, matching or exceeding all external baselines at the same scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-1.5 (7B/13B), MIRROR improves GQA by +0.77/+0.34, VQAv2 by +1.8/+0.4, and POPE by +0.6/+0.5, with no degradation on any metric. Against LLaVA-NeXT (7B/13B), MIRROR improves GQA by +0.7/+0.7 and POPE by +0.4/+0.3, while VQAv2 is essentially unchanged (−0.1/+0.1). Among external 7B baselines, LLaVA-NeXT-7B + MIRROR (GQA 65.5, POPE 87.1) substantially outperforms InstructBLIP-7B (GQA 49.2, POPE 74.4) and Qwen-VL-Chat (GQA 57.5†), and among 13B models it surpasses BLIP-2 (GQA 41.0) and InstructBLIP-13B (GQA 49.5) by wide margins. The gains are most pronounced on relational and spatial sub-tasks (GQA Global +2.13–3.16, BLINK spatial +4.9, counting +4.2) while local feature tasks (Object, Attribute, low-level visual) remain near-baseline, confirming that MIRROR specifically targets inter-concept relational geometry rather than uniformly boosting all capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the Gromov-Wasserstein distance (Mémoli 2011) as a semi-inverse problem: rather than searching for an optimal coupling between two fixed metric spaces, it fixes the coupling (from cross-attention) and the text geometry, then solves in closed form for the ideal visual geometry. This inversion is motivated by the Platonic Representation Hypothesis (Huh et al.) and evidence that LLMs encode structured relational priors in text, positioning language as a geometric 'teacher' for vision. The method departs from standard MLLM alignment (LLaVA, BLIP-2, InstructBLIP), which optimizes token-level identity via next-token prediction, by adding a structural regularizer that explicitly preserves pairwise relational geometry across modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete structural blind spot in projection-based multimodal alignment—identity-level matching without relational consistency—and provides a parameter-free, inference-free fix grounded in optimal transport theory. However, empirical gains are modest (0.3–0.8% on GQA, 1.4–2.5% on BLINK) and validated only on LLaVA-family models at 7B/13B scale, leaving open whether the approach transfers to other architectures, longer sequences, or generative tasks. The closed-form solution and its O(n_t²n_v + n_v²n_t) complexity make it practically deployable, but the sensitivity to extraction hyperparameters (layer, head, token filtering) and the divergence observed when layer decoupling is removed suggest the method is fragile outside its carefully tuned regime.
