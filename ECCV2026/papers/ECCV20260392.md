## Problem

On-policy exploration in Vision-Language Navigation exposes agents to diverse states but creates a semantic supervision gap: when the agent deviates from the expert path, the original instruction no longer describes the executed visual stream, leaving most exploratory trajectories without aligned linguistic supervision. This limits the full utilization of on-policy rollouts and forces reliance on large volumes of expert demonstrations.

## Input / Output

The navigation policy consumes four non-overlapping panoramic views (centered on the front-facing direction) rendered by Habitat at each timestep, along with a natural language instruction, and outputs a discrete action from the continuous-environment action space. The Hindsight Speaker Agent (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) takes the full sequence of panoramic frames from an exploratory trajectory plus one expert trajectory-instruction exemplar as context, and produces a single path-level hindsight instruction. The alignment scoring module additionally consumes CLIP image and text embeddings of the trajectory frames and landmark nouns extracted from the generated instruction via spaCy.

## Method

Φ-Nav wraps around existing on-policy algorithms (DAgger or scheduled sampling) with a three-stage dual-supervision cycle: (1) the agent samples a trajectory while receiving expert action corrections via cross-entropy loss; (2) a zero-shot LVLM-based Hindsight Speaker Agent, conditioned on a single expert trajectory-instruction pair via expert-in-context learning, synthesizes a path-level instruction describing the agent's actual visual observations; (3) the agent performs a second imitation pass treating the synthesized trajectory-instruction pair as an additional expert demonstration. To filter hallucinated or weakly grounded instructions, a Trajectory-Instruction Alignment Weighting (TIAW) module computes a coarse CLIP-based video-sentence similarity and a fine-grained landmark-noun-to-frame bidirectional similarity (harmonic mean), whose average adaptively scales the hindsight loss. The total objective is L = L_expert + λ·L_hindsight, where λ is the per-episode TIAW score, ensuring only semantically faithful hindsight signals contribute to policy optimization.

## Results

On R2R-CE (val unseen), Φ-Nav applied to ETPNav (scheduled sampling) improves SR from 57.21% to 62.58% (+5.37 pp) and SPL from 49.15% to 52.74% (+3.59 pp), slightly surpassing the recent SOTA g3D-LF (SR 61%, SPL 52%). Under the DAgger paradigm, Φ-Nav boosts CMA-D-P-A's SR from 32.10% to 34.08% (+1.98 pp). On RxR-CE (val unseen), Φ-Nav improves ETPNav's SR by 1.04 pp (54.79→55.83) and SPL by 1.06 pp (44.89→45.95), with smaller gains attributed to the longer, linguistically denser instructions in RxR-CE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under scheduled sampling on R2R-CE, ETPNav w/ Φ-Nav (SR 62.58%, SPL 52.74%) outperforms all listed baselines: it beats g3D-LF (SR 61%, SPL 52%) by 1.58 pp SR, HNR (SR 61%) by 1.58 pp, BEVBert (SR 59%) by 3.58 pp, ENP-ETPNav (SR 58%) by 4.58 pp, and VLN-BERT (SR 44%) by 18.58 pp. Under DAgger on R2R-CE, Φ-Nav consistently improves CMA variants by ~1–2 pp SR (e.g., CMA-D-P-A: 32.10→34.08). On RxR-CE, the gains are more modest: ETPNav w/ Φ-Nav (SR 55.83%, SPL 45.95%) edges out ENP-ETPNav (SR 55.27%) and VLN-BERT (SR 27.08%) but remains slightly below HNR (SR 56.39%, SPL 46.73%), likely due to the greater difficulty of generating accurate hindsight instructions over RxR-CE's ~120-word instructions and longer trajectories. The paper also demonstrates sample efficiency: Φ-Nav surpasses the full-data baseline using only 90% of expert demonstrations, and ablations confirm that the adaptive TIAW weighting outperforms fixed λ values (SR 62.58 vs. 59.12 at λ=0.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

Φ-Nav sits at the intersection of three research threads: on-policy imitation learning in VLN (DAgger, scheduled sampling as used by CMA and ETPNav), hindsight experience replay (HER and its extensions like THER and HSL), and LVLM-based navigation instruction generation (Speaker-Follower, EnvDrop, Marky, and recent zero-shot/fine-tuned LVLM speakers). The key conceptual departure is from prior hindsight work, which relabels terminal states or scalar rewards within predefined goal spaces; Φ-Nav extends hindsight to temporally-continuous, open-ended path-level instruction synthesis. It also differs from prior instruction generation literature, which targets offline data augmentation or pretraining, by embedding instruction generation directly inside the on-policy training loop to supervise exploratory rollouts that would otherwise lack aligned linguistic signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The semantic supervision gap in on-policy VLN training is a real and previously under-addressed bottleneck: exploratory trajectories that deviate from expert paths receive action-level correction but no coherent linguistic grounding, wasting a large fraction of collected data. Φ-Nav's demonstration that competitive navigation performance is achievable with only 90% of expert demonstrations suggests a practical path toward reducing annotation costs in embodied AI. However, the framework incurs significant per-episode LVLM inference overhead, the alignment scoring (TIAW) is acknowledged as lightweight and reference-free in a limited sense, and the improvements, while consistent across R2R-CE and RxR-CE, are moderate (1–5 points). Open questions include whether the approach scales to substantially longer horizons, multilingual settings beyond RxR-CE's three languages, and broader vision-language-action tasks where the instruction space is even more open-ended.
