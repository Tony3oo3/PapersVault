## Problem

Metadata-based raw image reconstruction frameworks rely on context models to estimate entropy parameters, but at 4K resolution these models become prohibitively expensive: convolutional contexts have limited receptive fields, and self-attention scales quadratically with feature-map size. No prior method efficiently couples long-range spatial modeling with content-adaptive computation for JPEG-guided raw reconstruction at high resolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260272/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes a scene-referred raw image (3×H×W, normalized to [0,1]) together with its aligned in-camera 8-bit JPEG preview and emits a compact metadata bitstream (reported in bpp, excluding the JPEG). The decoder reconstructs the raw image from the decoded metadata plus the same JPEG preview. The target operating resolution is true 4K (3840×2160), where the CNN baseline approaches 22.8 GB memory while MambaRaw stays within 10.2 GB.

## Method

MambaRaw inserts a Spatial-Energy Coupled Context Model into the entropy-parameter network of a two-level JPEG-conditioned VAE (backbone from Beyond-R2LCM). The first module, TileMambaBlock, partitions the latent feature map into T×T tiles, scores each by mean L2 energy, and applies a VMamba VSS block (4-direction cross-scan, linear-time selective scanning) only to the top-k highest-energy tiles (keep ratio ρ=0.5), reducing FLOPs by ~56% while preserving global context on informative regions. The second module, Energy-Aware Refinement (EAR), computes a per-pixel spatial energy map along channels, uses it to sigmoid-gate a lightweight 1×1-conv residual branch, and is zero-initialized so training starts as an identity mapping; this adapts entropy features to the long-tail energy distribution of raw signals without discarding local spatial granularity. JPEG guidance is injected at every scale via channel-wise concatenation of bilinearly-resized preview features, and the model is trained end-to-end with a rate-distortion loss across multiple λ values.

## Results

On the NUS dataset (4× downsampled), MambaRaw achieves 57.91 / 60.45 / 59.58 dB PSNR on Samsung / Olympus / Sony at 0.3612 bpp metadata, outperforming Beyond-R2LCM (56.74 / 59.04 / 58.21 dB at 0.3763 bpp) by 1.2–1.4 dB while using fewer bits. On AdobeFiveK, MambaRaw reaches 58.55 dB at 3.15×10⁻⁴ bpp, surpassing Beyond-R2LCM (58.44 dB at 3.76×10⁻⁴ bpp) by 0.11 dB with ~16% fewer bits. At true 4K resolution (3840×2160), MambaRaw improves PSNR by 1.37 dB over the baseline while reducing FLOPs by ~56% and end-to-end latency by ~9%, with peak memory dropping from 22.8 GB to 10.2 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaRaw consistently outperforms all five baselines across both datasets. Against the strongest baseline Beyond-R2LCM [44], MambaRaw gains 1.2 dB (Samsung), 1.4 dB (Olympus), and 1.4 dB (Sony) PSNR on NUS while using 4% fewer metadata bits (0.3612 vs. 0.3763 bpp). On AdobeFiveK, MambaRaw beats Beyond-R2LCM by 0.11 dB at the low-bitrate operating point (58.55 vs. 58.44 dB) with ~16% fewer bits, and by 0.09 dB at the higher-rate point (59.18 vs. 59.09 dB) with ~16% fewer bits. The gap over earlier metadata-based methods is substantial: MambaRaw exceeds R2LCM [43] by ~7 dB and CAM* [30] (with test-time fine-tuning) by ~8 dB on Samsung, reflecting the advantage of learned context modeling over hand-crafted or convolutional entropy models. Against InvISP [46] (which has no explicit metadata bitrate), MambaRaw achieves 58.55 dB vs. 52.69 dB on AdobeFiveK, though the comparison is not directly apples-to-apples since InvISP does not allocate a metadata budget. The efficiency advantage is most pronounced at 4K: MambaRaw cuts FLOPs by 56% and memory by 55% relative to Beyond-R2LCM while simultaneously improving PSNR by 1.37 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

MambaRaw extends the metadata-based raw reconstruction lineage (SAM → CAM → R2LCM → Beyond-R2LCM) by replacing the convolutional entropy-parameter context model with a State Space Model (VMamba's VSS block) operating on selectively chosen tiles. It departs from prior SSM-for-compression work (MambaIC, CMIC, RAWMamba) by inserting the SSM specifically into the entropy-parameter estimation branch rather than the reconstruction backbone, and by coupling it with a lightweight energy-guided refinement (EAR) that preserves local spatial granularity. The tile-based selective scanning mechanism, scored by L2 energy, is the paper's primary architectural novelty, drawing on the broader trend of content-adaptive computation in high-resolution processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making 4K raw reconstruction feasible on consumer hardware: a 56% FLOPs reduction and halving of peak memory (22.8→10.2 GB) while simultaneously improving PSNR by ~1.4 dB over the strongest baseline is a meaningful efficiency-quality trade-off for computational photography pipelines. The selective tile processing principle (apply expensive long-range modeling only to information-dense regions) is generalizable to other high-resolution compression or restoration tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to raw video (temporal SSMs), whether the L2-energy tile selection remains optimal under different sensor characteristics or extreme dynamic ranges, and whether hardware-aware mapping of selective SSM scanning to mobile accelerators can close the remaining latency gap for real-time use.
