## Problem

Existing autoregressive video diffusion models are restricted to forward (causal) temporal generation, where each block is conditioned only on previously generated content. Practical video creation workflows—backward extension, inbetween generation, looping, and visual story assembly—require conditioning on future context or both past and future context, but no unified autoregressive model supports arbitrary temporal directions. A fundamental obstacle is that Causal 3D VAEs encode latents with strict past-context dependency, causing severe inter-block flickering when generation proceeds backward.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt plus any combination of conditioning frames: past frames (for forward extension), future frames (for backward extension), or both head and tail frames (for inbetween generation). Generation proceeds autoregressively in blocks of B=3 latents (each encoding 4 consecutive frames), with KV caching over previously generated blocks to avoid redundant computation. At test time, the block size can be flexibly set to any value up to the training maximum attention horizon of 21 latents, enabling variable-length inbetween infilling between arbitrary head and tail blocks.

## Method

UniTemp introduces blockwise anchor latents: during backward generation, each block is expanded from B to P+B latents, where P auxiliary latents representing preceding video tokens are jointly denoised with the target block under full-sequence self-attention, restoring the missing past context that the Causal 3D VAE decoder expects; these anchor latents are discarded after denoising and never appear in the output. The training framework unifies forward and backward generation in a single student model G^θ and a single fake critic μ_fake^φ, supervised by a frozen 14B teacher (Wan2.1 T2V) via Self-Forcing with Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD), avoiding the memory and flexibility costs of direction-specific separate models. At inference, the shared model conditions on any combination of past and/or future KV caches, and RoPE temporal indices are repositioned to enable both-end-guided sink latents for stable long-horizon generation, inbetween infilling, looping, and scene transitions without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

On inbetween video generation (Table 4), UniTemp outperforms GI [31] by +2.81 on image quality (66.68 vs 62.88), +5.56 on aesthetic quality (65.75 vs 60.19), and +3.23 on head boundary consistency (99.52 vs 96.29), while also achieving lower FVD (25.00 vs 34.66). Compared to Wan FLF2V 14B [29], UniTemp achieves higher image quality (66.68 vs 64.59), aesthetic quality (65.75 vs 64.38), and head consistency (99.52 vs 98.88), with a lower FVD (25.00 vs 27.77). On 100s long video generation with both-end sink latents, UniTemp maintains subject consistency of 97.30 and aesthetic quality of 62.30, surpassing Self-Forcing (96.14 / 56.85) and Infinity-Rope (94.93 / 56.26) on stability metrics while trading some dynamic degree for reduced content drift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GI [31] on inbetween generation, UniTemp wins decisively on image quality (+3.80), aesthetic quality (+5.56), head boundary consistency (+3.23), FID (−9.27), and FVD (−9.66), while GI retains a slight edge on subject consistency (+0.67) and smoothness (+0.29). Against Wan FLF2V 14B [29], UniTemp achieves higher image quality (+2.09), aesthetic quality (+1.37), and head consistency (+0.64), with a lower FVD (−2.77), though Wan FLF2V edges ahead on dynamic degree (+1.56) and FID (−1.63). On long video generation (Table 3), UniTemp with both-end sink latents outperforms Self-Forcing [12] at 100s on subject consistency (97.30 vs 96.14), background consistency (95.80 vs 94.84), and aesthetic quality (62.30 vs 56.85), while Self-Forcing retains higher dynamic degree (62.50 vs 44.50) due to UniTemp's stability-oriented sink latent design. UniTemp also surpasses Infinity-Rope [36] at 100s on subject consistency (97.30 vs 94.93) and aesthetic quality (62.30 vs 56.26). On short 5s video generation (Table 2), UniTemp's unified model matches or slightly exceeds the Self-Forcing baseline (83.81 vs 83.42 total VBench score forward; 84.23 vs 84.26 backward), confirming no quality loss from the shared architecture. Overall, UniTemp trades a small amount of dynamic degree for substantially improved boundary consistency, aesthetic quality, and long-horizon stability relative to all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniTemp extends the Self-Forcing autoregressive distillation framework (Huang et al., NeurIPS 2025) and its DMD-based training objective (Yin et al., NeurIPS 2024) from forward-only generation to bidirectional generation. The key departure is identifying the Causal 3D VAE's inherent forward temporal bias as the root cause of inter-block flickering in backward generation, and introducing blockwise anchor latents as a lightweight fix that requires no modification to the frozen VAE or teacher. Unlike prior flexible-conditioning methods (MCVD, RaMViD, VACE) that rely on full-sequence attention with input masks, UniTemp maintains the efficient KV-cached autoregressive inference paradigm while supporting arbitrary temporal conditioning through RoPE position indices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By training a single autoregressive student for both forward and backward generation, UniTemp unlocks a range of practical video creation workflows (backward extension, inbetween infilling, looping, scene transitions, visual stories) without task-specific fine-tuning, which is practically valuable for video editing pipelines. The diagnostic that Causal 3D VAE latents are the source of backward-generation artifacts—rather than the generation model itself—is a useful insight for the broader video diffusion community. However, anchor latents add per-block computation for backward generation, and the underlying causal VAE bias is mitigated but not eliminated; exploring bidirectional VAEs compatible with existing pre-trained teachers remains an open direction.
