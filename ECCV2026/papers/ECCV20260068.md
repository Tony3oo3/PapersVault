## Problem

Existing Video-LLM benchmarks and methods assume a fixed camera viewpoint, leaving it unclear whether models achieve consistent temporal understanding when the same event is observed from egocentric versus exocentric perspectives. This paper reveals that current models achieve cross-view consistency scores barely over half their single-view performance, and that naively fine-tuning on both viewpoints introduces conflicting priors that actually degrade consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260068/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes synchronized ego-exo video pairs (clips ≥2 minutes, with longer videos segmented around ground-truth moments) paired with human-refined temporal event queries where all described entities and actions are verified visible from both viewpoints. For temporal verification, the model outputs a binary yes/no; for temporal grounding, it outputs start and end timestamps. The View-GRPO method additionally requires structured output in a <think>...<answer>...</answer> format, where the think block contains step-by-step temporal reasoning and the answer block contains the final prediction.

## Method

View-GRPO adapts Group Relative Policy Optimization for cross-view temporal reasoning by designing a composite reward with three components: a format reward enforcing structured output, an accuracy reward (tIoU for grounding, binary correctness for verification), and a reasoning reward that combines semantic alignment with GPT-5-generated reference reasoning (via an LLM judge) and cross-view structural consistency (Jaccard overlap of action and object sets extracted with spaCy from both viewpoints' reasoning chains). Training data (View30K) consists of 3.3k videos with 30k reasoning instances, filtered to retain only samples where GPT-5 produces valid reasoning with tIoU > 0.7 in both views. The key insight is that rather than enforcing identical outputs across views, the method encourages viewpoint-specific reasoning traces that converge on consistent temporal conclusions, avoiding the conflicting-prior problem observed with naive multi-view SFT. Experiments use Qwen2.5-VL and InternVL3.5 backbones at 2 FPS with frozen visual encoders and LoRA on the LLM, trained on 8×A100 GPUs.

## Results

On EgoExo-Con, View-GRPO (Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone) achieves 45.1% cross-view verification consistency (V-ExoEgo) and 18.7% cross-view grounding consistency (G-ExoEgo), improving over SFT by +3.7 and +3.8 points respectively, and over vanilla GRPO by +5.3 and +4.4 points. The largest gains appear on cross-view consistency metrics rather than single-view scores, confirming the method's focus on view-invariant reasoning. On generalization benchmarks, View-GRPO reaches 69.7% on Video-MME and 25.0% R1@0.5 on TVGBench, surpassing all open-source baselines. Even so, a substantial gap remains versus human performance (89.4% V-ExoEgo, 67.3% G-ExoEgo), underscoring the difficulty of the task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against closed-source models, View-GRPO (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) still trails GPT-5 (52.5% V-ExoEgo) and Gemini-2.5 Flash (52.3% V-ExoEgo) on verification consistency, and is slightly behind on grounding consistency (18.7% vs. 20.1% and 20.8%), but narrows the gap substantially compared to open-source baselines. Among open-source general-purpose models, View-GRPO outperforms VideoChat2 (23.4% V-ExoEgo), Video-LLaMA2 (27.9%), and Video-LLaMA3 (36.6%) on cross-view verification consistency, and surpasses all on grounding consistency (18.7% vs. 4.0%, 7.5%, 16.2% respectively). Among time-aware models, View-GRPO beats TimeChat-VT (42.1% V-ExoEgo, 16.3% G-ExoEgo), TimeSuite (25.6%, 18.7%), TimeChat (25.1%, 12.8%), and VTimeLLM (23.5%, 6.5%) on both tasks. On the generalization benchmark TVGBench, View-GRPO achieves 25.0% R1@0.5, outperforming TimeSuite (18.0%), Qwen2.5-VL (19.5%), and TimeChat (11.9%), while on Video-MME it reaches 69.7% versus 61.1% for Qwen2.5-VL and 46.3% for TimeSuite. The key finding is that View-GRPO's advantage is most pronounced on cross-view consistency metrics (V-ExoEgo, G-ExoEgo) rather than single-view scores, validating its design goal of view-invariant temporal reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) from DeepSeekMath to the video domain, extending its group-wise reward normalization to enforce cross-view consistency in temporal reasoning. It positions itself against existing ego-exo benchmarks (CharadesEgo, LEMMA, EgoExo-4D, EgoExoBench) that either target domain-specific tasks, lack synchronized viewpoints, or evaluate only action ordering via multiple-choice rather than open-ended temporal prediction. Conceptually, it departs from prior ego-exo learning work (contrastive representation alignment, cross-view distillation) by shifting the focus from feature-level invariance to whether Video-LLMs produce consistent *temporal predictions* across viewpoints, and from supervised fine-tuning to reinforcement learning with structured reasoning rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that cross-view consistency scores are barely half of single-view performance across all evaluated models—including GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5 Flash—exposes a fundamental blind spot in current Video-LLM evaluation and training. The negative result that naive multi-view SFT underperforms single-view training challenges the common assumption that more diverse data always helps, suggesting conflicting view-specific priors actively degrade temporal signals. Open questions include whether the 37–47% gap to human consistency can be closed with larger models or longer training, whether View-GRPO's reasoning-reward design generalizes beyond two synchronized viewpoints, and whether the approach extends to non-temporal tasks (e.g., spatial reasoning) where view invariance is equally critical.
