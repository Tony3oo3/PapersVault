## Problem

Open-vocabulary object detection relies on CLIP-like VLMs for semantic recognition, but their image-text contrastive pre-training acts as a low-pass filter that suppresses high-frequency spatial details, causing coarse localization and imprecise bounding boxes. Existing approaches compensate via distillation, pseudo-labeling, or prompt tuning, but they treat pre-trained VLM features as fixed and fail to explicitly recover the missing spatial spectra. The paper identifies this as the Semantic-Spatial Discrepancy: semantic features carry strong identity cues but diffuse boundaries, while object-centric features like DINOv2 preserve sharp spatial structure yet lack language alignment.

## Input / Output

The detector consumes an RGB image and a set of free-form text category names (e.g., 'a photo of a {}'), which are encoded offline via CLIP's text encoder with 80 prompt templates and projected to a 256-d visual space through a learnable MLP. It outputs 1000 object queries, each yielding a class probability (softmax-normalized cosine similarity to text embeddings) and a bounding box parameterized as center coordinates, width, and height. Both the CLIP and DINOv2 backbones are fully frozen; only 3M parameters in the adapter, fusion modules, and detector head are trained.

## Method

ESSA-OVD introduces a dual-stream architecture pairing a frozen CLIP semantic stream with a frozen DINOv2 spatial stream on top of Deformable DETR. A lightweight Spatial Adapter (1×1 conv + GroupNorm + ReLU) projects DINOv2 features from 1024 to 768 channels and standardizes their distribution to match the CLIP manifold. A Frequency-Aware Fusion module then decomposes the adapted spatial features via average-pooling subtraction (an unsharp-masking operation) to isolate high-frequency structural residuals, which are injected into the semantic stream through a zero-initialized learnable gate and an IsoNorm rescaling that preserves feature magnitude to prevent semantic drift. A Pure Dense Strategy routes the fused features only to the encoder-oriented multi-level layers for localization, while the classification-oriented dense feature bypasses fusion entirely, keeping the CLIP semantic manifold pristine for zero-shot text matching.

## Results

On OV-COCO, ESSA-OVD with ViT-L/14 achieves 47.6 AP₅₀^Novel, surpassing the previous best CCKT-Det++ (Swin-B, 46.0) by 1.6 points and CLIPSelf (ViT-L/14, 44.3) by 3.3 points. On OV-LVIS, the same backbone attains 41.3 mAP_r on rare categories, outperforming OV-DQUO (ViT-L/14, 39.3) by 2.0 and CLIPSelf (ViT-L/14, 34.9) by 6.4. Cross-dataset transfer from LVIS to Objects365 yields 17.1 AP, exceeding CCKT-Det++ (15.2) by 1.9 AP without any fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESSA-OVD outperforms all 20 compared baselines on both primary benchmarks. On OV-COCO, it leads CCKT-Det++ (the prior SOTA) by 1.6 AP₅₀ with ViT-L/14 vs. Swin-B, and by 3.3 AP₅₀ over CLIPSelf at the same ViT-L/14 scale. On OV-LVIS, the margin widens substantially: ESSA-OVD (41.3 mAP_r) surpasses CLIPSelf (34.9) by 6.4, CoDet (37.0) by 4.3, and even RO-ViT with a larger ViT-H/16 backbone (34.1) by 7.2 mAP_r. The method also wins on cross-dataset transfer to Objects365 (17.1 vs. 15.2 AP for CCKT-Det++), confirming that the gains stem from the alignment mechanism rather than backbone scale. Notably, ESSA-OVD with the smaller ViT-B/16 backbone (40.0 AP₅₀ on OV-COCO, 32.8 mAP_r on OV-LVIS) still exceeds several larger-backbone competitors, demonstrating that the explicit semantic-spatial alignment strategy is effective across model capacities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Deformable DETR as its detection backbone and positions itself against the dominant OVD paradigm of treating frozen CLIP features as a fixed semantic source. Prior methods (ViLD, CCKT-Det++, OV-DQUO, CLIPSelf) address localization weakness through external supervision signals—knowledge distillation, pseudo-labeling, or prompt tuning—while leaving the underlying feature representation unchanged. ESSA-OVD departs by reframing the problem as a spectral deficiency: CLIP's contrastive pre-training acts as a low-pass filter that discards high-frequency boundary information, and the fix is to explicitly inject DINOv2's object-centric spatial priors via a frequency-aware fusion mechanism rather than relying on additional training signals. The Pure Dense Strategy, which routes fused features only to the encoder (localization) while keeping the classification feature map pristine, is a design choice not seen in prior dual-stream OVD work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-domain lens on the semantic-spatial gap offers a useful conceptual reframing that could inform future OVD and dense VLM adaptation work beyond detection. Practically, achieving SOTA with only 3M trainable parameters and frozen backbones makes the approach attractive for deployment where retraining large models is infeasible. However, the frequency decomposition relies on a simple 3×3 average-pooling approximation rather than true spectral analysis, and the method's computational cost doubles due to running two ViT backbones in parallel. Open questions include whether the staged alignment (adapter → frequency fusion) generalizes to other VLM pairs or tasks like segmentation, and whether the Pure Dense decoupling remains beneficial when the semantic backbone is fine-tuned rather than frozen.
