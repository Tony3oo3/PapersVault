## Problem

Graph-based caching schedulers for diffusion models (e.g., MeanCache) formulate cache planning as a constrained shortest-path problem, implicitly assuming additive independence of per-edge errors. This surrogate becomes unreliable in the low-NFE regime where large integration steps cause earlier caching decisions to interact nonlinearly with later ones, and existing methods solve each budget independently, ignoring the structural regularity that optimal schedules across budgets evolve smoothly rather than forming unrelated solutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261890/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt and a target NFE budget B (e.g., 10 or 15 out of 50 original steps), OTCache produces a discrete caching schedule specifying which timesteps require full velocity-field evaluation versus feature reuse. The schedule is generated offline per prompt-seed pair via a three-stage pipeline (reference computation, anchor search, interpolation), after which the cached sampling loop runs the diffusion model at the specified resolution (1024×1024 for FLUX.1, 1664×928 for Qwen-Image, or video frames for HunyuanVideo) to yield the final image or video.

## Method

OTCache treats caching schedules as probability measures over a normalized progress domain and models their evolution across NFE budgets as a smooth trajectory in policy space, inspired by Optimal Transport. Stage 1 obtains a high-fidelity reference schedule via a generic graph-based method (MeanCache) under a conservative budget (B_ref=20). Stage 2 performs a lightweight black-box search (Optuna + CMA-ES, ~200 trials) to find a near-optimal anchor schedule at an extreme low budget (B_anc=8) by directly minimizing end-to-end LPIPS against the full-step reference. Stage 3 lifts both endpoint schedules to continuous warping curves via PCHIP interpolation, then predicts the target-budget schedule through a log-conditioned weighted quantile interpolation (Wasserstein geodesic in 1D), with locality and confidence priors weighting the two endpoints. A power-law warping exponent ρ=1.3 biases NFE density toward the early reverse ODE to stabilize high-variance sampling, and a projection operator rounds to valid integer timesteps while preserving the maximum timestep constraint.

## Results

On FLUX.1 [dev] (1024×1024), OTCache (B=15) achieves a 3.04× speedup with LPIPS 0.126 and PSNR 26.03, outperforming MeanCache (2.91×, LPIPS 0.142, PSNR 24.83); at B=10 it reaches 4.50× with LPIPS 0.254 versus MeanCache's 4.12× and LPIPS 0.272. On Qwen-Image (1664×928), OTCache attains 4.70× acceleration (B=10) with LPIPS 0.171 and SSIM 0.864, substantially ahead of MeanCache (4.56×, LPIPS 0.236). On HunyuanVideo, OTCache (B=10) achieves 3.66× speedup with VBench 80.37% and LPIPS 0.252, surpassing MeanCache (3.59×, LPIPS 0.269) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTCache consistently outperforms MeanCache (the closest graph-based competitor) across all three models and both budget levels: on FLUX.1 at B=10 it reduces LPIPS from 0.272 to 0.254 while achieving a higher speedup (4.50× vs 4.12×); on Qwen-Image at B=10 the LPIPS gap widens dramatically (0.171 vs 0.236); on HunyuanVideo at B=10 it improves LPIPS from 0.269 to 0.252 with a faster 3.66× vs 3.59×. Against LeMiCa, OTCache wins on every metric at every budget tested (e.g., FLUX.1 B=15: LPIPS 0.126 vs 0.153, PSNR 26.03 vs 24.45). Threshold-based methods (TeaCache, DiCache) and forecasting methods (TaylorSeer) suffer severe quality collapse at high acceleration—TeaCache's LPIPS degrades to 0.504 at 3.66× on FLUX.1—while OTCache maintains LPIPS below 0.26 even at 4.50×. On Qwen-Image, DBCache (r=0.6) achieves only 2.74× speedup with LPIPS 0.298, far behind OTCache's 3.20× and LPIPS 0.069. For video generation, ToCa and DuCa (both N=5) reach ~3× speedup but with LPIPS 0.454, whereas OTCache at B=12 achieves 3.21× with LPIPS 0.162, a 2.8× reduction in perceptual error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on graph-based caching schedulers (MeanCache, LeMiCa) that formulate feature-reuse planning as a constrained shortest-path problem on a timestep DAG. It departs from this lineage by identifying the additive-independence assumption's breakdown in the low-NFE regime and reframing the problem: rather than independently solving a discrete optimization per budget, OTCache treats schedules as probability measures whose evolution across budgets follows a smooth trajectory in policy space, instantiated via 1D Wasserstein geodesic (quantile interpolation) between a conservative reference and a low-budget anchor. The anchor itself is obtained through a lightweight Optuna/CMA-ES search over an end-to-end LPIPS objective, a practical departure from the surrogate-based edge-weight optimization used by prior graph methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training-free caching acceleration is one of the most deployment-friendly ways to reduce inference cost for large diffusion/flow-matching models, and OTCache's 4.5–4.7× speedups with improved fidelity over MeanCache make it a practical option for interactive or resource-constrained settings. The OT-inspired perspective—modeling budget-conditioned schedule evolution as a geodesic in policy space—offers a principled alternative to per-budget discrete search, though the actual 1D interpolation reduces to a weighted linear combination of quantile curves, so the geometric framing is more conceptual than computationally heavy. Open questions include whether the structural regularity across budgets generalizes to architectures with very different ODE dynamics, whether the per-prompt anchor search cost (≈50–200 trials) is acceptable in production, and whether the approach extends to multi-modal or conditional generation settings where the schedule space is higher-dimensional.
