## Problem

Existing panoramic spatial reasoning benchmarks (e.g., OSR-Bench, ODI-Bench) rely on simplistic local questions requiring at most two-hop inference, failing to exploit the full 360° field of view that makes panoramas valuable for embodied intelligence. They also evaluate only final-answer correctness without assessing whether intermediate reasoning steps are geometrically coherent, and they lack real-world data to measure the sim-to-real gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260240/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an equirectangular-projection (ERP) panoramic image paired with a spatial reasoning question and three spatial anchors—object-level reference points with known coordinates and orientation (e.g., 'positive X-axis pointing east')—that establish a unified spatial frame without exposing the full scene layout. The output is a structured chain-of-thought trace containing 2–4 explicit reasoning steps grounded in panoramic evidence (bearings, proximity, angular displacements), followed by a final answer. The CoT is scored on both general quality (Precision, Recall, F1) and panorama-specific spatial metrics (Viewpoint Consistency, Spatial Evidence Sufficiency, Reasoning Feasibility).

## Method

OmniCoT decomposes panoramic spatial reasoning into three progressive dimensions—See (multi-hop viewpoint transformation), Locate (inter-object relational reasoning), and Move (embodied action simulation)—and generates questions via a hierarchical pipeline that converts 3D scene geometry into structured language, produces candidate questions, and filters them through dual-LLM judges (DeepSeekv3.2 and Qwen3-Max) scoring on six criteria before expert review. For training OmniCoT-R1, the paper applies a two-stage post-training strategy to Qwen2.5-VL-7B: SFT (freezing vision and projection modules, training only the language model) anchors each reasoning step to explicit panoramic evidence such as bearings and proximity, then GRPO (full-model fine-tuning with 8 generations per step) uses a composite reward combining format compliance, geometry-grounded task accuracy, and anti-repetition regularization to penalize geometrically incoherent reasoning paths and consolidate global 360° spatial consistency.

## Results

On OmniCoT-B (6.7K QA pairs), GPT-5 achieves the highest overall accuracy at 39.72%, followed by Doubao-1.8 (38.90%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (35.26%). A universal bottleneck persists in the Locate dimension, where no model surpasses 25% accuracy, highlighting the difficulty of multi-hop topological reasoning in panoramas. Among CoT quality metrics, Gemini3-Flash achieves the highest Precision (79.30%) but suffers from sparse reasoning (Recall 22.46%), while Qwen3-VL-8B leads in Viewpoint Consistency (85.09%) and Spatial Evidence Sufficiency (79.05%). The proposed OmniCoT-R1 (SFT+GRPO) dramatically outperforms all baselines, reaching 59.54% overall accuracy and 63.21% RF, compared to the 23.53% of its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5 leads among all 21 evaluated MLLMs with 39.72% overall accuracy and 42.71% on the Move dimension, while Doubao-1.8 closely follows at 38.90% overall. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B achieves the best See accuracy (52.03%) and overall (35.26%), but all open-source models trail closed-source counterparts by 4–16 points. No model exceeds 25% on the Locate dimension, with GPT-5 (24.40%) and Doubao-1.8 (23.87%) marginally leading, confirming multi-hop inter-object reasoning as a universal weakness. CoT prompting yields divergent effects: it improves open-source models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B gains +8.75%) but degrades closed-source ones (Gemini3-Flash drops −11.53%, GPT-5 drops −2.94%), suggesting cumulative hallucination risk in verbose reasoning for top-tier models. The proposed OmniCoT-R1 (SFT+GRPO on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) surpasses all 21 baselines by a wide margin, reaching 59.54% overall accuracy (+36.01 over its base model) and 51.82% on Locate, effectively doubling the best prior Locate score.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from existing panoramic spatial reasoning benchmarks (OSR-Bench, ODI-Bench, VQA 360°, PanoEnv-QA) by arguing they are fundamentally under-specified: they rely on local visual cues and at most two-hop inference, ignoring the full 360° global context that panoramas uniquely provide. Conceptually, it extends the MME-CoT evaluation framework with panorama-specific spatial metrics (Viewpoint Consistency, Spatial Evidence Sufficiency, Reasoning Feasibility) and introduces a 'See-Locate-Move' taxonomy that decomposes panoramic reasoning into viewpoint transformation, inter-object relational reasoning, and embodied action simulation. For training, it adapts the GRPO paradigm (as used in 360-R1) to panoramic geometry, adding a structured SFT stage that anchors reasoning to bearings and proximity before RL-based consolidation of global spatial consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a universal bottleneck: no evaluated MLLM exceeds 25% on the Locate (inter-object relational) dimension, suggesting that multi-hop topological reasoning in 360° scenes remains largely unsolved regardless of model scale or closed-source advantage. The substantial sim-to-real gap on OmniCoT-Real (e.g., GPT-5 drops from ~40% to ~29% overall) underscores that synthetic benchmarks alone are insufficient for evaluating real-world panoramic generalization. Open questions include whether the 'linguistic blind-navigation' phenomenon (high CoT quality but low accuracy) can be resolved through better cross-modal grounding, and whether the SFT+GRPO recipe generalizes beyond the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base to other architectures or larger scales.
