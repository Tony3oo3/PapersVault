## Problem

Current VLA models for robotic manipulation depend on large teleoperation-collected datasets restricted to static tabletop scenes with limited object diversity, making it prohibitively expensive to verify whether new hardware configurations (dexterous hands, mobile bases, additional sensors) actually improve performance before committing to real-world data collection. No large-scale simulation benchmark exists that simultaneously supports mobile-based robots with both parallel grippers and dexterous hands across diverse objects, skills, and realistic scenes.

## Input / Output

The VLA model consumes four synchronized 520×520 images per timestep (head-view and wrist-view RGB and depth, each reshaped to 224×224×3 for the SigLIP encoder), a natural-language instruction in the form '<skill> <object>' (e.g., 'open faucet'), and a 6-d robot wrist pose expressed in the mobile base coordinate frame. It outputs a chunk of N=16 actions, each (6+D)-dimensional (7-d for the G1 parallel-gripper robot, 18-d for the XHand dexterous-hand robot), where the first 6 dimensions encode wrist pose displacement and the remaining D dimensions encode end-effector joint targets; at inference, an adaptive ensemble over K=4 overlapping chunks smooths the trajectory.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, MobileManiRL trains a universal 4-layer MLP policy (hidden dims 1024-1024-512-512) per robot-object-skill triplet using keypoint-based state inputs (gripper/hand points, object grasp point, goal point) and a composite reward that blends distance, approach, grasp, move, and success terms, achieving 89.6% (G1) and 92.9% (XHand) mean success across 1,182 combinations. Second, these RL policies autonomously generate 300K annotated trajectories across 100 realistic scenes, which train MobileManiVLA—a CogACT-style architecture with a PaliGemma-2 backbone (SigLIP vision encoder + Gemma-2 language model) producing a cognition token, and a diffusion-transformer action head conditioned on that token plus an MLP-encoded wrist pose state, trained end-to-end with MSE on Gaussian noise. The key design insight is that RL-based trajectory generation in Isaac Sim eliminates teleoperation entirely, enabling scalable, reproducible data collection with full multi-modal annotations (RGB-D-segmentation, states, actions, language) at 30 FPS.

## Results

On the full MobileManiBench evaluation (unseen objects and scenes, 10 episodes per combination), MobileManiVLA achieves mean success rates of 56.7% (G1 robot) and 57.3% (XHand robot) across 5 mobile manipulation skills, while the underlying MobileManiRL policy reaches 89.6% and 92.9% respectively on seen objects. On the challenging-skill subset (open, pull, pick) used for cross-model comparison, MobileManiVLA (3B) attains 28.2% success rate, substantially outperforming all evaluated VLA baselines. The largest per-skill gaps appear on 'open' (42.9% G1 / 34.5% XHand) and 'pull' (34.4% / 29.4%), while 'push' is easiest (85.1% / 90.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MobileManiVLA (3B) outperforms all four baselines on the G1 robot challenging-skill benchmark, achieving 28.2% success rate versus 18.8% for π₀.₅, 11.2% for π₀, 6.8% for CogACT, and 4.5% for OpenVLA. The gap over the strongest baseline (π₀.₅) is approximately 9.4 percentage points, attributable to MobileManiVLA's use of multi-view RGB-D images (head + wrist) plus wrist-pose state, whereas π₀.₅ uses only head and wrist RGB with wrist pose. OpenVLA and CogACT receive only a single head-view RGB image, which the ablation studies show is insufficient for mobile manipulation (7.9% with head RGB alone). Notably, MobileManiVLA matches or exceeds the 7B-parameter models (OpenVLA, CogACT) while using only 3B parameters, suggesting that input modality richness matters more than model scale in this setting. The paper also notes that π₀.₅'s relative strength stems from its pretraining on additional mobile manipulation trajectories, partially closing the gap with MobileManiVLA.

## Relation to Prior Work

MobileManiBench extends the simulation-based robotic manipulation benchmark lineage (RLBench, Robosuite, LIBERO, CALVIN, RoboCasa) by introducing mobile-base platforms and dexterous hands alongside parallel grippers, addressing a gap the authors identify in both real-world datasets (DROID, Open X-Embodiment, AgiBotWorld) and prior simulation benchmarks that are predominantly fixed-base and tabletop. The VLA architecture (MobileManiVLA) directly follows CogACT's three-module design (vision-language-action with diffusion-transformer) and PaliGemma-2 backbone, while the data generation pipeline departs from teleoperation-based collection by training universal RL policies (MobileManiRL) with keypoint-based parameterization to autonomously produce trajectories. The paper positions itself as a simulation-first verification framework, enabling controlled ablation of sensing modalities and robot embodiments before costly real-world deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MobileManiBench provides the first large-scale, unified benchmark simultaneously covering mobile manipulation, both parallel gripper and dexterous hand embodiments, and multi-view RGB-D-segmentation inputs, enabling systematic study of how these factors interact in VLA models. Key empirical findings—multi-view RGB-D nearly quadruples success over single-view RGB (28.2% vs 7.9%), unseen objects are harder than unseen scenes, and dexterous hands paradoxically degrade VLA performance on open/pull skills due to finger-object collisions—offer actionable guidance for model design. However, the benchmark is entirely simulation-based with moderate VLA success rates (~57%), leaving the sim-to-real gap and whether these findings transfer to physical mobile manipulation as critical open questions. The 300K-trajectory dataset and public release position it as a potential standard testbed for the mobile manipulation community.
