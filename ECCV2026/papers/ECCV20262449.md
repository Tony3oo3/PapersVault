## Problem

While egocentric benchmarks have grown, none systematically evaluate whether MLLMs can predict the final state of an entire scene after executing a long sequence of atomic actions from a first-person viewpoint. Existing state-prediction evaluations focus on short-horizon, single-object changes and lack structured annotations (objects, attributes, relations) needed for fine-grained quantitative scoring of scene-level predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262449/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each instance provides a single egocentric initial-scene image paired with a sequence of atomic action descriptions (average 113 actions, range 11–694) drawn from real first-person videos. The model must produce a free-text description of the final scene that enumerates object categories, their visual attributes (shape, color, size, texture, state), and inter-object relations (spatial and interaction). Evaluation decomposes the generated text into sentence-level sub-descriptions, matches candidate objects to ground-truth via Sentence-BERT similarity, and scores attribute and relation descriptions on a 0–5 LLM-judged scale, combined into a weighted unified score (S_uni).

## Method

EXPLORE-Bench is constructed via a multi-stage annotation pipeline: object tags are extracted from final-scene images (Recognize Anything Plus) and action text (spaCy), grounded with Grounding DINO, then enriched with Qwen3-VL-235B-generated attributes and relation triplets, and finally corrected by GPT-5.2 with human-in-the-loop quality control. The evaluation protocol, inspired by CompreCap, computes object-level coverage via mutual-maximum matching in a Sentence-BERT similarity matrix, and attribute/relation scores via LLM-as-judge (Qwen3-8B) on a 0–5 scale, yielding a weighted unified metric. For test-time scaling, the authors segment action sequences (even partition or sliding window) and compare single-turn versus multi-turn stepwise inference on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, finding that multi-turn inference with fine-grained segmentation (window_size=10) improves long-horizon performance by ~3.4 points at the cost of multiplicative inference time.

## Results

On the full EXPLORE-Bench (1,157 instances), the best model Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking achieves S_uni = 50.96, while humans score 59.08 on the 100-instance tiny subset, leaving a 7.39-point gap. Among proprietary models, Gemini-3-Pro leads with S_uni = 49.66 (Full) and S_uni = 48.31 (Long subset), slightly ahead of GPT-5.2-Chat (48.19 Full). Notably, the open-source Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (50.65 Full) outperforms all proprietary models on the full set, though Gemini-3-Pro retains an edge on the Long subset (48.31 vs. 48.00). On abnormal cases (20 instances), the best model GPT-5.2-Chat scores S_abn = 62.79 versus 91.64 for humans, highlighting a persistent failure to describe physically anomalous states.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (S_uni = 50.65) outperforms all proprietary models on the full set, beating Gemini-3-Pro (49.66) by 0.99 and GPT-5.2-Chat (48.19) by 2.46; however, Gemini-3-Pro reverses this on the Long subset (48.31 vs. 48.00), suggesting proprietary models handle very long action sequences slightly better. Among open-source thinking models, Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking (50.96) is the overall best, edging out Step3-VL-10B (48.87) and GLM-4.6V-Flash (48.31), though thinking mode does not universally help: Ovis2.5-2B, Ovis2.5-9B, and MiMo-VL-7B-RL-2508 all perform worse in thinking mode than their non-thinking counterparts. Embodied-Reasoner (32.57) and EgoThinker (38.38), despite being specifically trained for embodied/egocentric tasks, score 8–18 points below general-purpose MLLMs and even below their own base model Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct (38.35), indicating that long-horizon egocentric scene prediction remains largely unaddressed by existing embodied training. On the abnormal-case evaluation (Table 3), GPT-5.2-Chat leads models with S_abn = 62.79, but all models trail the human score of 91.64 by at least 29 points, with Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking (58.22) and Gemini-3-Pro (56.00) close behind.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new task formulation at the intersection of egocentric vision and long-horizon reasoning: predicting a full scene state after executing a long sequence of atomic actions from a first-person viewpoint. It departs from existing egocentric benchmarks (EgoTaskQA, EgoSchema, EgoPlan-Bench, ERQA) that evaluate short-term or localized state changes, and from long-horizon video benchmarks that test coarse event-level comprehension, by requiring global, multi-object scene prediction grounded in structured action sequences rather than raw video. The evaluation protocol draws on CompreCap's scene-graph-based captioning metrics, and the annotation pipeline repurposes off-the-shelf tools (Grounding DINO, Recognize Anything Plus, spaCy, Qwen3-VL) in a multi-stage pipeline to produce object-attribute-relation annotations at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial and consistent gap between current MLLMs and humans (best model ~51 vs. human ~59 on S_uni), and shows that models specialized for embodied or egocentric reasoning (Embodied-Reasoner, EgoThinker) do not outperform general-purpose MLLMs on this task, suggesting long-horizon scene prediction has been largely overlooked. The stepwise-reasoning analysis provides actionable guidance: multi-turn decomposition with adaptive windowing helps on long sequences but incurs multiplicative compute cost, while naive single-turn decomposition actively hurts by causing the model to drop invariant scene elements. Open questions include developing more efficient test-time scaling strategies, expanding coverage of rare/abnormal physical interactions, and constructing dedicated training sets to close the human-model gap.
