## Problem

Cascaded end-to-end driving pipelines compress multi-modal sensor inputs into a compact latent context before passing it to a planner, creating an information bottleneck that discards fine-grained geometric and semantic details needed for precise trajectory prediction. This one-way interface also prevents the planner from leveraging the full expressive power of modern vision foundation models and forces reliance on auxiliary supervision tasks or predefined trajectory anchors to stabilize generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260279/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three downscaled camera views (front, 60° left, 60° right) concatenated to a 1024×256 resolution, raw LiDAR point clouds, and a discrete high-level driving command (e.g., turn left, go straight). It outputs an 8-waypoint trajectory over a 4-second horizon, where each waypoint encodes 2D position and heading angle [x, y, θ]. At inference, the trajectory is initialized as pure Gaussian noise and denoised over 2 diffusion steps, with the command and diffusion step injected via adaptive layer normalization.

## Method

MOJITO replaces the cascaded perception-to-planning pipeline with three structurally identical 12-block Transformer branches (hidden dim 384) for image (DINOv3-S+), LiDAR (Uni3D-S with a novel PillarGroup BEV-grid tokenizer that assigns points to 32×32 physical pillars, selecting top-512 non-empty pillars with 64 points each), and action planning (Diffusion Transformer). The core mechanism is Modal Joint Attention: at every block, tokens from all three branches are concatenated along the sequence dimension and processed through a shared multi-head self-attention layer, enabling bidirectional interaction where action tokens directly query fine-grained sensor features while perception tokens adapt to the planning context. This eliminates the need for predefined anchors or auxiliary supervision, as the deep cross-modal interaction naturally constrains the diffusion process to feasible trajectory regions. The image and LiDAR branches are fine-tuned from their pretrained weights while the action planner is trained from scratch using a standard diffusion denoising objective with trajectory supervision only.

## Results

On NAVSIM-v1 navtest, MOJITO achieves 88.9 PDMS with zero anchors, surpassing the previous best anchor-free method TransFuser (84.0) and anchor-based methods DiffusionDrive (88.1) and WoTE (88.3). On the stricter NAVSIM-v2 navtest, MOJITO sets a new state-of-the-art with 88.4 EPDMS, outperforming DiffusionDriveV2 (85.5) by 2.9 points. On NAVSIM-v2 navhard (two-stage protocol, camera-only), MOJITO reaches 29.0 EPDMS, exceeding GuideFlow (27.1) and DiffusionDrive (24.2). The model is compact at 127M parameters, compared to 2B–8B for VLA-based competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOJITO outperforms all end-to-end baselines on NAVSIM-v1: it beats DiffusionDrive (88.1) by 0.8 PDMS, WoTE (88.3) by 0.6, Hydra-MDP (86.5) by 2.4, DRAMA (85.5) by 3.4, TransFuser (84.0) by 4.9, UniAD (83.4) by 5.5, and VADv2 (80.9) by 8.0, all while using zero anchors versus 20–8192 for competitors. Against VLA methods on NAVSIM-v1, MOJITO (88.9 PDMS, 127M params) surpasses the imitation-learning variants of ReCogDrive (86.5), AutoVLA (80.5), and AdaThinkDrive (86.2), but trails their RL-finetuned counterparts (90.8, 89.1, 90.3 respectively) by 1.2–1.9 points. On NAVSIM-v2 navtest, MOJITO leads all baselines: DiffusionDriveV2 (85.5) by 2.9 EPDMS, ARTEMIS (83.1) and DriveSuprim (83.1) by 5.3, Hydra-MDP++ (81.4) by 7.0, and TransFuser (76.7) by 11.7. On the harder NAVSIM-v2 navhard two-stage protocol (camera-only), MOJITO (29.0 EPDMS) beats GuideFlow (27.1) by 1.9, GTRS-DP (23.8) by 5.2, DiffusionDrive (24.2) by 4.8, and LTF (23.1) by 5.9. Notably, MOJITO achieves these results without any VLM backbone, reinforcement learning, or auxiliary supervision tasks, relying solely on trajectory supervision within a 127M-parameter unified Transformer.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOJITO departs from the dominant cascaded two-stage paradigm (UniAD, TransFuser, DiffusionDrive) that compresses multi-modal sensor inputs into a fixed latent context before handing it to a separate planner. It repurposes the Uni3D architecture for LiDAR tokenization (replacing FPS+KNN with the proposed PillarGroup grid) and the DiT architecture for the action planner, but the key conceptual departure is the block-wise Modal Joint Attention that concatenates image, LiDAR, and action tokens into a single self-attention sequence at every depth, enabling bidirectional perception–planning interaction rather than one-way conditioning. This positions MOJITO as a unified alternative to both anchor-based diffusion planners (DiffusionDrive, VADv2) and large VLA+RL systems (ReCogDrive, AutoVLA), arguing that the information bottleneck and auxiliary supervision in prior work are architectural artifacts rather than necessities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a 127M-parameter, anchor-free model matches or exceeds 2B–8B VLA+RL systems on NAVSIM, suggesting that architectural unification can substitute for model scale and reinforcement learning in trajectory planning. Open questions include whether the 187 ms inference latency (vs. 88 ms for TransFuser) is acceptable for real-time deployment, whether the approach transfers to reactive closed-loop settings where environment feedback matters, and whether the PillarGroup tokenization generalizes to denser or sparser LiDAR configurations. The paper does not explore multi-step planning or long-horizon consistency, which remain important for real autonomous driving.
