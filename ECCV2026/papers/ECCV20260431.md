## Problem

Flow-matching methods for 3D shape assembly, such as Rectified Point Flow, learn pointwise velocity fields supervised only by an endpoint reconstruction loss, leaving the model to implicitly discover which sparse cross-part contact regions should drive the motion. This lack of explicit intermediate guidance about mating relations reduces robustness under symmetry ambiguity and domain shift. The paper asks whether pretrained 3D geometric priors can be injected into the flow backbone via teacher-student distillation to provide the missing relational structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260431/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K unposed part point clouds {P_k} with variable point counts N_k, concatenates all point tokens into a single sequence of length N = ΣN_k, and conditions a DiT-based transformer on per-point features from a frozen overlap-aware encoder. One part is fixed as an anchor (T_1 = I), and the remaining K−1 poses are predicted relative to it. At inference, the learned velocity field is integrated from t=1 (Gaussian noise) to t=0 to recover assembled positions, from which per-part SE(3) transformations are extracted via closed-form Procrustes/SVD alignment—adding strictly zero test-time overhead.

## Method

TORA augments the RPF flow-matching backbone with a frozen teacher-student alignment branch: a pretrained 3D encoder (Uni3D) produces target representations from clean point clouds, and a 3-layer MLP projector maps the flow model's intermediate features at a selected late transformer layer (l*=5) into the teacher space. The primary alignment objective is Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA), which computes n×n Gram matrices (with n=1024 randomly subsampled tokens for tractability), centers them, and minimizes the negative normalized Frobenius inner product between student and teacher Gram matrices—explicitly matching the pairwise 'who-is-similar-to-whom' relational topology rather than individual feature vectors. The total training loss is L_CFM + 0.5·L_CKA. Systematic analysis shows that geometry- and contact-centric teacher properties (mating-surface segmentation, part silhouette) predict downstream assembly gains far better than semantic classification accuracy, and that aligning at late layers where spatial structure emerges yields the largest improvements, with up to 6.9× faster convergence and improved zero-shot transfer.

## Results

On Breaking Bad (2–20 parts), TORA with CKA alignment achieves 95.7% Part Accuracy, 8.6° rotation error, and 2.1 cm translation error, improving over the RPF baseline (93.2% PA, 16.0° RE, 4.3 cm TE). In the harder 21–33 part regime, TORA (Cos-dist) reaches 72.4% PA versus RPF's 62.1%. On PartNet-Assembly and TwoByTwo, CKA yields the largest gains: 69.1% PA (vs. RPF 59.8%) and 71.5% PA (vs. RPF 65.4%), respectively. Zero-shot transfer to unseen datasets (FRACTURA, Fantastic Breaks, Breaking Bad-Artifact) shows the most pronounced improvements, with CKA achieving 76.0% PA on FRACTURA (vs. RPF 68.1%) and 94.4% on Breaking Bad-Artifact (vs. RPF 88.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TORA consistently outperforms the RPF baseline [42] across all six benchmarks: on Breaking Bad [2,20], CKA improves Part Accuracy from 93.2% to 95.7% and halves rotation error (16.0° → 8.6°); on the harder [21,33] split, Cos-dist lifts PA by 10.3 points (62.1% → 72.4%); on PartNet-Assembly and TwoByTwo, CKA gains 9.3 and 6.1 PA points respectively. Against correspondence-based methods (Jigsaw [34], PMTR [26], CMNet [27]), TORA wins by large margins—e.g., 95.7% vs. 69.7–80.4% PA on Breaking Bad [2,20]—and the gap widens dramatically in the many-part regime where these methods collapse (12–25% PA vs. 72%). Against the generative GARF [29], TORA CKA achieves 95.7% vs. 92.4% PA on Breaking Bad [2,20] and 72.4% vs. 25.1% on [21,33], while also surpassing GARF on zero-shot FRACTURA (76.0% vs. 44.2% PA). Notably, the NT-Xent variant underperforms RPF on TwoByTwo (60.4% vs. 65.4% PA), indicating that per-point contrastive discriminability is counterproductive in inter-object settings, whereas CKA's relational matching is consistently the strongest objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

TORA builds directly on the Rectified Point Flow (RPF) architecture for flow-matching-based 3D shape assembly, augmenting its DiT-based flow matcher with a teacher-student alignment branch. Conceptually, it adapts the REPA paradigm—aligning generative model features to a frozen pretrained encoder—to the 3D point-flow setting, but departs from the 2D literature by showing that relational topology matching (CKA) outperforms token-wise objectives and that geometry/contact-centric teacher properties, rather than semantic classification ability, govern alignment effectiveness. The CKA objective itself is borrowed from kernel alignment theory [Cortes et al., 2012], repurposed here to preserve pairwise 'who-is-similar-to-whom' structure among point tokens during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-inference-overhead design (frozen teacher, cached features) makes TORA a drop-in training-time improvement for any flow-matching assembly pipeline, and the 6.9× convergence speedup is practically significant for training budgets. The empirical finding that geometry/contact probes predict teacher quality far better than semantic classification provides a concrete, reusable guideline for selecting 3D foundation models as distillation teachers in assembly and related geometric tasks. Open questions include whether the CKA objective and late-layer alignment strategy transfer to other 3D generative tasks (e.g., shape completion, novel view synthesis), and whether the approach extends to even larger part counts or continuous (non-rigid) assembly settings.
