## Problem

Feed-forward 3D foundation models for novel view synthesis and open-vocabulary segmentation assume all input views depict the same static scene, but real-world videos contain moving humans, animals, and vehicles whose regions cannot be reconciled into a single static 3D representation. This causes ghosting artifacts in RGB rendering and inconsistent semantic predictions. The paper tackles the gap of jointly performing photometric reconstruction and semantic understanding from sparse, unposed views of dynamic environments without requiring ground-truth motion mask annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262645/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2–4 unposed RGB images at 256×256 resolution (16×16 patch size) from dynamic indoor or outdoor scenes, along with optional text prompts for open-vocabulary queries, and produces a rendered novel-view RGB image, continuous LSeg/CLIP-aligned semantic feature embeddings (not fixed class logits), and per-pixel dynamic region probability maps in a single forward pass. At inference, predicted masks can be optionally refined with SAM2 to convert soft spatial priors into precise binary masks. The model accepts flexible numbers of input views (2, 3, or 4) with corresponding target views (6, 5, or 4) for evaluation.

## Method

SPAR is a Transformer-based encoder-decoder that jointly encodes photometric and semantic tokens into a compact latent scene representation. A Cross-View Dynamic Region Predictor (CV-DRP) concatenates per-view semantic features, photometric tokens, and Plücker ray tokens, then processes paired source–reference view features through stacked self-attention layers to produce dynamic region probability maps; these masks suppress moving-foreground tokens before scene-level aggregation. The scene encoder concatenates learnable global scene tokens with the masked photometric and semantic tokens and processes them through 8 Transformer layers, while a 12-layer rendering decoder uses target-view Plücker ray query tokens to decode both RGB and semantic embeddings via a dual-head MLP. The key training innovation is a dynamic-region-aware optimization: predicted static-region probability maps spatially weight both photometric and semantic reconstruction losses, focusing supervision on multi-view consistent static areas, while a regularization term prevents the network from trivially labeling the entire image as dynamic. This self-supervised scheme requires no ground-truth motion masks, and a copy-paste augmentation strategy provides auxiliary mask supervision during training.

## Results

On the D-RE10K benchmark, SPAR achieves state-of-the-art feed-forward novel view synthesis in dynamic scenes, reaching 23.33 dB PSNR and 0.263 LPIPS with 4 input views, and 22.15 dB PSNR with 3 views. For motion mask prediction, the self-supervised Cross-View Dynamic Region Predictor attains 88.5% mIoU (n=3) with SAM2 refinement, surpassing the leading self-supervised baseline WildRayZer (52.1%) by over 36 percentage points. On ScanNet, SPAR achieves 0.5271 mIoU and 0.8061 accuracy for open-vocabulary segmentation while simultaneously rendering novel views at 26.43 dB PSNR, outperforming LSM on both semantic and photometric metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPAR outperforms the closest dynamic NVS baseline WildRayZer [4] by +0.17 dB PSNR and −0.031 LPIPS at 3 views, and by a larger +0.95 dB PSNR and −0.027 LPIPS at 4 views, while trailing WildRayZer's reported 2-view result (21.78 dB) but exceeding the authors' own reproduction (19.47 dB). Against static-scene feed-forward methods LSM [9] and SIU3R [39], SPAR gains roughly 10–11 dB PSNR at 3 views, confirming that explicit dynamic-region handling is essential in unconstrained environments. For motion mask prediction, SPAR (self-supervised, no GT masks) achieves 88.5% mIoU at n=3, beating the supervised Segment Any Motion [12] (41.2%) by 47 points and the self-supervised WildRayZer (52.1%) by 36 points. On ScanNet, SPAR surpasses LSM [9] in both mIoU (0.5271 vs 0.5078) and PSNR (26.43 vs 24.39), and exceeds SIU3R [39] in PSNR (26.43 vs 25.10), though SIU3R retains a lower LPIPS (0.1043 vs 0.2407) and LSeg [20] holds a marginal mIoU edge (0.5281 vs 0.5271) by segmenting ground-truth images directly. Optimization-based methods (NeRF On-the-go, 3DGS, T-3DGS, Spotless-Splats, WildGaussians) are all outperformed by 4–10 dB PSNR, highlighting the advantage of feed-forward generalization over per-scene optimization in dynamic settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPAR extends the RayZer line of self-supervised, pose-free feed-forward view synthesis by incorporating WildRayZer's dynamic-environment handling (transient-region masking and loss gating) and LSM/SIU3R's joint semantic-geometric encoding into a single unified architecture. The key conceptual departure is the Cross-View Dynamic Region Predictor, which uses paired-view transformer attention to estimate motion masks without any ground-truth labels, combined with a dynamic-region-aware loss that spatially weights both photometric and semantic supervision. Unlike WildRayZer, which handles dynamics but renders only RGB, or LSM/SIU3R, which jointly model semantics but assume static scenes, SPAR is the first to unify all three—dynamic NVS, open-vocabulary semantics, and self-supervised motion estimation—in one feed-forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-supervised motion mask prediction reaching 88.5% mIoU without any GT mask annotations is practically significant for scalability, as it removes the need for expensive dynamic-object labeling in training pipelines. The demonstrated inter-task synergy—where the semantic branch acts as a structural regularizer that improves photometric fidelity—suggests that joint multi-task 3D reconstruction may be more beneficial than previously assumed, potentially guiding future unified scene-understanding architectures. Limitations include reliance on SAM2 for test-time mask refinement, evaluation primarily on indoor D-RE10K sequences, and a 2-view setting where SPAR still trails WildRayZer's reported numbers. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer video sequences, more complex outdoor dynamics, and whether the semantic-photometric synergy persists at larger model scales.
