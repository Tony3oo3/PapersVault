## Problem

Current vision foundation models remain fragmented across narrow specializations—image semantic encoders, offline video models, and geometric reconstruction experts—none of which provides a single backbone that simultaneously captures semantics, temporal dynamics, and 3D structure in a strictly causal, streaming regime. The paper asks whether one unified streaming visual backbone can be trained whose frozen representations transfer across perception, reconstruction, reasoning, and embodied control without task-specific fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260205/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The backbone consumes an RGB video stream (or a single image as a degenerate T=1 case) at 224×224 or 512×512 resolution, processing each frame causally via a persistent KV-cache so that frame t attends only to frames ≤ t, yielding O(T) per-step complexity. Outputs are structured as dense spatiotemporal patch features from selected intermediate layers, a global [CLS] token per frame, and a dedicated [CAM] token; lightweight heads on top of these frozen features produce depth maps, ray maps, camera poses (quaternion + translation + FoV), segmentation masks, text responses via an LLM, or robot actions via an MLP action expert.

## Method

OmniStream extends DINOv3 ViT-L with two architectural modifications: (1) causal spatiotemporal attention that masks future-frame keys, enabling incremental KV-cache inference without recomputation over past frames, and (2) 3D-RoPE that re-purposes the per-head dimension with a 2:3:3 split across (time, height, width), interleaving temporal components into DINOv3's original 2D RoPE while preserving pretrained spatial priors. Training couples three complementary objectives under a single loss: a DINOv3-style student-teacher distillation (global + patch-level) unifying image and causal video SSL, a streaming geometric reconstruction branch with a dual-DPT depth/ray head and an MLP camera head supervised by L1 losses on depth, rays, point maps, and poses, and a vision-language alignment branch using a Qwen3-0.6B autoregressive decoder trained on captioning, OCR, and grounding. The model is trained on ~200M frames from 29 datasets over two stages (60K steps at 224², then 120K steps at 512²) with T=16 frame sequences, and all downstream evaluations use a strictly frozen backbone with only task-specific heads or LLM/projector modules trained.

## Results

On image probing, OmniStream achieves 84.7% ImageNet top-1 accuracy, 0.377 RMSE on NYUv2 monocular depth, and 49.1 mIoU on ADE20K segmentation, matching DINOv3 on depth while trailing slightly on classification and segmentation. On video probing, it reaches 68.5% SSv2 accuracy (vs. 54.0% DINOv3) and 71.6 J&F on DAVIS'17 VOS (vs. 44.2% V-JEPA 2), demonstrating strong temporal understanding. For streaming 3D reconstruction, OmniStream achieves 0.314 Abs Rel on Sintel video depth (vs. 0.417 CUT3R) and 0.076 ATE on ScanNet pose (vs. 0.099 CUT3R). As a frozen VLM backbone, it scores 70.6 on VSI-Bench spatial reasoning (vs. 35.6 LLaVA-Video) and 60.9 on EgoSchema (vs. 57.3), while as a VLA backbone it reaches 3.89 on CALVIN (vs. 2.55 OpenVLA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DINOv3 (image specialist), OmniStream trades a small image-domain gap (−2.0% ImageNet, −2.4 mIoU ADE20K) for large video-domain gains (+14.5% SSv2, +1.1% K400) and near-parity on DAVIS'17 VOS (71.6 vs. 73.2 J&F). Against V-JEPA 2 (video specialist), OmniStream dramatically outperforms on DAVIS'17 VOS (71.6 vs. 44.2 J&F) and K400 (85.7 vs. 85.1), though V-JEPA 2 retains a 5.2% edge on SSv2. Against CUT3R (streaming 3D specialist), OmniStream wins on Sintel depth (0.314 vs. 0.417 Abs Rel, a 25% relative improvement) and ScanNet pose (0.076 vs. 0.099 ATE), while CUT3R edges ahead on KITTI depth (0.122 vs. 0.136) and Sintel/TUM pose by small margins. Against LLaVA-Video (VLM baseline), OmniStream achieves a striking 35-point gap on VSI-Bench spatial reasoning (70.6 vs. 35.6) and consistent wins on EgoSchema (+3.6) and PerceptionTest (+1.3), with only a marginal 1.1-point loss on VideoMME. Against OpenVLA (VLA baseline), OmniStream substantially outperforms on CALVIN long-horizon manipulation (3.89 vs. 2.55 success rate) but trails on Simpler-Bridge real-to-sim transfer (45.8% vs. 53.7%), suggesting the frozen-backbone protocol still leaves room for domain-specific visual adaptation in real-world settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniStream extends DINOv3's image ViT (with its 2D RoPE and student-teacher distillation framework) into a causal streaming backbone by introducing causal spatiotemporal attention with a persistent KV-cache and 3D-RoPE adapted from VideoRoPE formulations. Conceptually, it departs from the fragmented landscape of specialized vision models (image encoders, offline video models, geometric experts) and from output-level unification approaches (Florence, OFA, Unified-IO) by targeting representation-level unification: a single frozen backbone whose features serve image probing, video understanding, streaming 3D reconstruction, VLM reasoning, and VLA control without task-specific fine-tuning. The multi-task pre-training recipe couples DINOv3-style SSL, feed-forward geometric heads (dual-DPT depth + camera pose), and an autoregressive language decoder, each addressing a different axis (semantics, geometry, language) that prior work has typically handled in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single 400M-parameter streaming backbone, kept strictly frozen, can be competitive with 600M+ specialized experts across five distinct domains, which is practically significant for deployment scenarios (robots, AR devices) where retraining per task is infeasible. The ablation revealing that early vision-language alignment in the backbone is critical to prevent catastrophic VLM integration failures (12.4% VSI-Bench drop without captioning) offers a useful design principle for future VLM/VLA architectures. However, the model does not uniformly surpass specialized SOTA (e.g., KITTI depth is worse than CUT3R and Point3R), and the authors explicitly leave model scaling as future work, so the remaining gaps with task-specific approaches remain open. The broader question of whether a truly general-purpose visual backbone can match or exceed the sum of its specialized parts at scale is left unresolved.
