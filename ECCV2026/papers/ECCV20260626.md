## Problem

Existing weather synthesis methods suffer from limited diversity in weather appearance and poor control over particle dynamics. Text-prompt-only approaches are inherently underspecified, and general-purpose video editors trained on clean, high-quality footage tend to suppress heavy weather phenomena, making dense particle effects (e.g., heavy snowfall or torrential rain) difficult to elicit reliably.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260626/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an input RGB video (from diverse sources including aerial and ego-centric perspectives) along with user-specified weather parameters (type, severity, duration, wind strength/direction) and produces a temporally consistent weather-synthesized video. Internally, the input video is decomposed via Depth Anything V3 into per-frame depth maps, a 3D point map, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics, which are then fused with physics-simulated particle projections to form the structured conditioning signals consumed by the video diffusion editor.

## Method

The framework factorizes weather synthesis into three complementary conditioning signals that jointly steer an off-the-shelf video diffusion model (Wan2.1-VACE) without fine-tuning. (1) Semantic-aware appearance anchoring uses GPT-4.1 to parse scene semantics and reason about weather-specific effects, then FLUX.1-Kontext Pro edits the initial frame to establish a target appearance anchor with consistent global lighting. (2) Physics-informed dynamic simulation represents each weather particle as an anisotropic Gaussian primitive and evolves the particle field under gravity, wind, and divergence-free curl-noise turbulence via explicit Euler integration, producing temporally coherent motion cues. (3) Geometry-grounded video synthesis aligns the canonical simulation gravity with the scene's estimated ground-plane normal, projects the evolving particle field into each frame using recovered camera parameters, and composites particle-augmented depth maps with original scene depths as conditioning. The key insight is that injecting structured particle visual primitives and trajectories into the conditioning space activates latent weather priors already present in pretrained video editors, enabling dense, physically plausible particle effects without additional training.

## Results

On the 58-video evaluation set, the proposed method achieves the highest overall CLIP-D (0.17) and VLM score (0.83) across both snow and rain conditions, outperforming the strongest conditional baseline VACE (CLIP-D 0.16, VLM 0.75) and prompt-only baselines CogVideoX (CLIP-D 0.07, VLM 0.54) and WeatherEdit (CLIP-D 0.13, VLM 0.41). In human evaluation, the method receives the highest photorealism (4.16/5) and physical-realism (4.12/5) ratings, and is selected as best by 41.3% of users for rain and 56.5% for snow. As a data augmentation tool, synthesized weather data boosts mIoU by up to 14.5% for HRDA on ACDC rain and 10.4% on ACDC snow.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against four video-editing baselines (CogVideoX, WeatherEdit, LTX-Video, VACE) and two segmentation models (DAFormer, HRDA) used for downstream augmentation. On the primary weather-synthesis metrics, the proposed method wins on CLIP-D (prompt alignment) and VLM (perceptual quality) against all four editing baselines, with the largest margin over WeatherEdit (VLM 0.83 vs. 0.41) and CogVideoX (VLM 0.83 vs. 0.54). The closest competitor is VACE, which achieves a slightly higher CLIP-S (0.82 vs. 0.81) and marginally higher snow CLIP-D (0.18 vs. 0.19), but the proposed method leads on overall VLM by 0.08 points. WeatherEdit attains the highest CLIP-S (0.91) due to its conservative editing that barely alters the scene, but this comes at the cost of the lowest VLM score (0.41), indicating weak weather expressiveness. For the downstream segmentation task, augmenting training with synthesized weather data improves DAFormer by +5.4 to +7.7 mIoU and HRDA by +9.0 to +14.5 mIoU across ACDC and MUSES snow/rain splits, with the largest gain (14.5%) on HRDA under ACDC rain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant lines in weather synthesis: reconstruction-based pipelines (NeRF/3DGS re-rendering) that require costly per-scene optimization, and simulator-curated diffusion models (e.g., WeatherWeaver) whose appearance is locked to a fixed training distribution. Instead, it repurposes an off-the-shelf conditional video editor (Wan2.1-VACE) by injecting structured tri-prior conditioning—MLLM-derived appearance anchors, physics-simulated Gaussian particle fields, and geometry-projected depth maps—thereby activating latent weather priors already present in the pretrained model without any fine-tuning. The semantic-aware anchoring strategy (VLM parsing → LLM reasoning → I2I editing) extends prior prompt-based editing by grounding instructions in scene semantics, while the physics-informed simulation and geometry grounding draw on classical particle dynamics and 3D scene understanding rather than learned weather representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, zero-shot path to diverse and physically plausible weather synthesis that avoids per-scene optimization and retraining, with demonstrated utility for augmenting autonomous-driving segmentation (up to 14.5% mIoU gain on ACDC/MUSES). Key limitations include error propagation from upstream geometry estimation (fragile gravity recovery in groundless scenes) and an inherent ceiling set by the pretrained diffusion model's particle-generation fidelity. Open questions include whether joint optimization of the anchoring, simulation, and projection stages can mitigate cascading errors, and whether domain-adapted or stronger diffusion priors can push particle realism beyond what current off-the-shelf editors support.
