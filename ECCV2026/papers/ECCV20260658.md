## Problem

Existing single-source domain generalization (SDG) methods for medical image segmentation improve cross-domain performance but suffer from an overlooked phenomenon the authors term Catastrophic Forgetting in SDG (CF-SDG): aggressive style perturbation and feature alignment destroy source-domain discriminative features, degrading performance on the original deployment domain. The authors trace this to models exploiting spurious appearance shortcuts (a confounding back-door path S←D→A) rather than learning stable causal anatomical structure, and to the absence of any explicit mechanism to preserve source knowledge during generalization training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single 3D medical image volume from one source domain (abdominal CT or MRI, cardiac bSSFP or LGE) and produces a pixel-level multi-organ segmentation mask (liver, kidneys, spleen, or LVC/MYO/RVC). In addition to the segmentation output, an auxiliary reconstruction branch simultaneously generates a source-domain image reconstruction, which serves as the memory anchor for the anti-forgetting constraint. Training uses only labeled data from the single source domain; no target-domain data is accessed at any stage.

## Method

CCL-Net builds on a U-Net backbone and introduces a dual-path causal intervention mechanism grounded in a Structural Causal Model. The front-door path (Orthogonal Structure Disentanglement) uses a dual-branch encoder to separate structural and stylistic representations, enforcing their decorrelation via a soft orthogonality loss on pooled projections. The back-door path (Anatomy-Guided Local Causal Intervention) approximates do(A) by performing per-organ, mask-guided affine style transfer from a randomly sampled batch reference, then enforcing prediction consistency between original and counterfactual features. To counter CF-SDG, a Collaborative Learning Constraint couples the segmentation branch with an auxiliary reconstruction branch through two dynamic mechanisms: a local bias that gates per-layer feature interaction based on inter-step drift, and a global bias that modulates task weights via an 'Effective Optimization Gain' derived from directional consistency between branches. The total loss combines Dice, SSIM, orthogonality, consistency, and the collaborative MSE terms with learned hyperparameters.

## Results

On the cross-modality abdominal dataset, CCL-Net achieves 86.23% average Dice (CT→MRI) and 90.05% (MRI→CT), outperforming the strongest baseline ICMSeg by 0.6% and 1.07% respectively. On the cross-sequence cardiac dataset, it reaches 86.41% (bSSFP→LGE) and 88.42% (LGE→bSSFP), surpassing ICMSeg by 1.41% and 0.79%. Critically, CCL-Net reduces the Source Forgetting Metric (SFM) to 1.07–3.29% across all four source domains, compared to 4.13–14.90% for all baselines, demonstrating that it generalizes without sacrificing source-domain performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCL-Net outperforms all six baselines across every generalization direction on both datasets. The closest competitor is ICMSeg [4] (WACV 2025), a causal-mechanism method; CCL-Net exceeds it by 0.6–1.4% average Dice on the abdominal dataset and 0.79–1.41% on the cardiac dataset. Style-based methods (MixStyle [32], Cutout [7]) suffer the largest degradation under cross-modality transfer, with MixStyle dropping to 62.44% average Dice on MRI→CT, roughly 28 points below CCL-Net. The causal DG method CSDG [18] performs moderately (82.91–83.31% avg Dice on abdominal) but still trails CCL-Net by over 3%. On the Source Forgetting Metric, CCL-Net's SFM of 1.07–3.29% is dramatically lower than all baselines (4.13–14.90%), with the gap most pronounced against augmentation-based methods (Cutout: up to 14.90%, MixStyle: up to 14.03%), confirming that aggressive style perturbation destroys source-domain discriminative features while CCL-Net's collaborative constraint preserves them.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal domain generalization line for medical segmentation (CSDG, ICMSeg) by adding a back-door intervention path (anatomy-guided local style transfer) alongside the front-door structural disentanglement already present in prior causal DG work. The disentanglement mechanism draws directly on the Domain Separation Networks paradigm (Bousmalis et al., 2016) and disentangled representation learning (Lee et al., 2018), but applies it to a dual-branch encoder with an explicit orthogonality loss rather than adversarial training. The most distinctive departure is the import of catastrophic-forgetting mitigation from continual learning into the SDG setting, where prior methods have implicitly accepted source-domain degradation as the price of generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CF-SDG framing exposes a practical blind spot: clinicians deploying a 'generalized' model may find it performs worse on their own hospital's data than a simple ERM baseline, undermining trust. The ablation finding that the collaborative learning constraint (CL) contributes more to generalization Dice than either causal intervention module alone suggests that explicit source-memory anchoring may be a more effective regularizer than purely causal feature surgery, a point worth testing in broader settings. Open questions include whether the U-Net-specific local bias constraint generalizes to transformer or diffusion-based backbones, whether the SFM metric gains community adoption, and whether the approach scales to more than two target domains or to 2D datasets with larger domain gaps.
