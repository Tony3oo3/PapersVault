## Problem

Point-based 3D object detectors preserve fine geometric fidelity but are crippled by two systemic bottlenecks: computationally prohibitive and structure-agnostic downsampling (e.g., FPS) and expensive dynamic neighbor queries (e.g., k-NN) with costly continuous interactions. Voxel-based methods avoid these costs but sacrifice geometric detail through quantization, leaving a persistent accuracy-efficiency gap that no existing point-based architecture has closed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260318/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw, unstructured LiDAR point clouds (no voxelization or pillarization) and produces 3D bounding boxes with class labels via a Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) projection head. Points are partitioned into local spatial cells (0.3×0.3×0.25 m on nuScenes, 0.32×0.32×0.1875 m on Waymo) for the sampling stage, and the backbone operates on the retained subset through four stacked LAM blocks before BEV feature extraction. The architecture is trained end-to-end in a supervised single-stage detection framework built on OpenPCDet.

## Method

PointLAM introduces two synergistic operators. The Laplacian Point Sampler (LPS) replaces FPS with a Deviation Network that computes each point's feature deviation from its local neighborhood mean—formulating an implicit discrete Laplacian high-pass filter that highlights structural boundaries—followed by Doubly Sorted Sampling, a deterministic two-step sort (by saliency score, then by region index) that selects top-k points per region in O(n log n) time. The Local Hadamard Aggregator (LHA) decouples spatial indexing from feature representation by using a transient voxel grid purely as an O(1) deterministic router for sparse convolutional aggregation, then applies a Hadamard product between a point's intrinsic gating vector and its aggregated local context for topology-aware channel modulation, eliminating the need for continuous relative positional embeddings. These local modules are interleaved with Bi-Directional Mamba layers that serialize points along X and Y axes for linear-complexity global sequence modeling, forming the Local Attentive Mamba (LAM) block; the 5×5×5 receptive field is factorized into two 3×3×3 sparse convolutions to reduce indexing overhead while preserving spatial context.

## Results

On nuScenes, PointLAM achieves 72.2 NDS / 67.8 mAP (validation) and 73.0 NDS / 68.8 mAP (test), matching Voxel Mamba (73.0 NDS) and surpassing DSVT (72.7 NDS) on the test set while using only 8.6M parameters and 90.7G FLOPs. On Waymo, it reaches 73.6 L2 mAPH (validation) and 74.4 L2 mAPH (test), matching Voxel Mamba on validation and approaching SAFDNet (74.6 L2) on test. Inference latency is 93.1 ms on a single A800 GPU, roughly 2× faster than LION (195.3 ms) and using 37% of Voxel Mamba's FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the nuScenes test set, PointLAM (73.0 NDS) exactly matches Voxel Mamba (73.0 NDS) and outperforms DSVT (72.7 NDS) by +0.3 NDS, while delivering a +16.6 NDS improvement over the previous point-based baseline 3DSSD (56.4 NDS). On nuScenes validation, it surpasses DSVT (71.1 NDS) and LION (72.1 NDS) but trails UniMamba (72.6 NDS) by 0.4 NDS. On Waymo validation, PointLAM (73.6 L2 mAPH) outperforms DSVT (72.1) and HEDNet (73.4) and ties Voxel Mamba (73.6); on the Waymo test set it reaches 74.4 L2, just 0.2 below SAFDNet (74.6). In terms of efficiency, PointLAM uses 8.6M parameters and 90.7G FLOPs versus LION's 10.1M / 165.8G (45% fewer FLOPs, 2× lower latency) and Voxel Mamba's 15.1M / 246.2G (63% fewer FLOPs), while HEDNet is slightly smaller (4.6M) but slower (96.7 ms) and less accurate (71.4 NDS). The paper also shows PointLAM consistently outperforms LION under extreme sparsity (1/16 density: 25.08 vs. 23.72 L1 mAPH) and on small objects (Size 1.0 m³: +1.24 L1 mAPH over a k-NN variant), validating the geometric-fidelity advantage of the point-based design.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointLAM extends the emerging line of Mamba-based 3D detectors (PointMamba, Mamba3D, Voxel Mamba, UniMamba) by applying selective state-space sequence modeling to raw point clouds rather than voxelized or pillarized representations. It departs from the dominant point-based pipeline (PointNet/PointNet++ style) by replacing two costly operations—Farthest Point Sampling for downsampling and k-NN for local aggregation—with a Laplacian high-pass filter plus deterministic sorting (LPS) and a transient-grid sparse convolution with Hadamard gating (LHA), respectively. The architectural coupling of these local operators with Bi-Directional Mamba layers for global context is the paper's core structural contribution, positioning it as a point-based alternative that targets the efficiency gap with voxel-based SpCNN and Transformer detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PointLAM demonstrates that a point-based detector can match or exceed strong voxel-based baselines (e.g., 73.0 NDS on nuScenes test, tying Voxel Mamba) while using roughly 45% fewer FLOPs and 2× lower latency than LION, offering a practical path to high-accuracy 3D detection without geometric quantization. The robustness experiments (outperforming LION at 1/16 input density and on 5-point objects) reinforce the intrinsic advantage of preserving fine-grained geometry, which is particularly relevant for small-object detection in autonomous driving. Open questions include whether the point-based efficiency advantage persists in multi-sensor (LiDAR-camera) fusion settings, whether the axis-scan serialization strategy generalizes to multi-frame or tracking tasks, and how the architecture scales to even denser urban scenes beyond the current single-frame, single-sensor evaluation.
