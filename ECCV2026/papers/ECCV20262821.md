## Problem

While prior probing studies have shown that generic vision backbone features and CNN-based detectors encode some 3D scene knowledge, no work has examined whether the object-level query embeddings produced by transformer-based 2D detectors (the DETR family) contain recoverable 3D information. This paper asks: to what extent do pre-trained 2D DETR models, trained entirely without 3D supervision, encode object depth and 3D location in their latent query embeddings?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262821/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The probe takes as input a single frozen query embedding vector (256-dim for most DETR variants, 384-dim for LW-DETR) extracted from the last decoder layer of a pre-trained 2D detector, and outputs either a scalar depth value (meters from camera at the bounding box center) or a 3D center coordinate (x, y, z in camera frame, corresponding to the bottom-center of the KITTI 3D box). The underlying images are 1242×375 synthetic outdoor scenes (Virtual KITTI2), 640×480 real indoor RGBD pairs (NYUv2), or KITTI driving images, but the probe itself never sees the image—only the embedding.

## Method

The core method is a two-stage probing protocol: (1) run each pre-trained 2D DETR model on images to obtain query embeddings, filter by an objectness threshold (τ=0.5), and pair each embedding with ground-truth 3D property values extracted from dataset annotations; (2) train a lightweight probe—either a single linear map or a two-layer ReLU MLP (hidden dim 256)—on the frozen embeddings to regress the target 3D property, using MSE loss with Adam (lr=1e-3, 1000 epochs, cosine decay). To enable fair cross-model comparison, a dataset alignment step greedily matches detections across models via IoU≥0.5, retaining only objects detected by all models. The paper further analyzes compressibility via PCA (showing depth information saturates at moderate dimensionality) and ablates embedding probes against 2D bounding-box probes (showing embeddings carry substantially more depth-relevant signal). Results are benchmarked against zero-shot and fine-tuned Depth Anything 2 and MonoDETR, revealing that MLP probes on DETR embeddings match or exceed zero-shot Depth Anything 2 on depth and achieve 3D localization within ~0.2 m MAE of MonoDETR.

## Results

On monocular depth estimation (Virtual KITTI2), the best 2D DETR probe (DETR + MLP) achieves MAE 0.54 m, AbsRel 3.62%, and δ₁ 98.81%, outperforming zero-shot Depth Anything 2 (MAE 1.49 m) and zero-shot MonoDETR (MAE 3.83 m). On NYUv2, Conditional-DETR + MLP reaches MAE 0.50 m and δ₁ 66.78%, again beating both zero-shot 3D baselines. For 3D object center prediction on KITTI, DETR + MLP achieves a center MAE of 0.50 m (AbsRel 2.40%), only 0.2 m worse than MonoDETR (0.30 m, AbsRel 1.41%), despite having no 3D supervision during pre-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Depth Anything 2, all five 2D DETR models with MLP probes outperform the zero-shot baseline on both Virtual KITTI2 (best DETR MAE 0.54 vs. DA2 1.49) and NYUv2 (best DETR MAE 0.50 vs. DA2 0.61). When DA2 is given a task-specific MLP head, it narrows the gap on Virtual KITTI2 (0.50 vs. 0.54) and clearly wins on NYUv2 (0.36 vs. 0.50), but the zero-shot comparison is the headline result. Against MonoDETR, the zero-shot gap is dramatic: DETR MLP probes achieve MAE 0.54 m on Virtual KITTI2 versus MonoDETR's 3.83 m, and 0.50 m on NYUv2 versus MonoDETR's 10.59 m. With a fine-tuned MLP head, MonoDETR slightly edges out DETR on Virtual KITTI2 (0.47 vs. 0.54) but is still beaten by Conditional-DETR and Deformable-DETR probes on NYUv2 (0.75 vs. 0.50/0.54). On the 3D location task (KITTI), MonoDETR retains a clear but modest advantage (0.30 m vs. 0.50 m center MAE, 1.41% vs. 2.40% AbsRel), a gap of only ~0.2 m at a median object distance of 25.3 m. Real-time variants (RT-DETR v2, LW-DETR) consistently underperform the standard DETR variants across all tasks, with RT-DETR v2 showing 2.27 m x-axis MAE versus DETR's 0.33 m on 3D location.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probing methodology (linear and MLP probes on frozen representations) previously applied to generic vision backbone features, dense feature maps, and CNN-based detectors [10, 21, 29, 33] to a new target: the object-level query embeddings produced by transformer-based 2D detection models (DETR family). While prior probing studies of 3D awareness in vision models (e.g., DINOv2, Stable Diffusion) operate on pooled or dense representations, this work specifically interrogates the per-object decoder outputs of DETR, Deformable DETR, Conditional DETR, RT-DETR, and LW-DETR. The 3D prediction baselines (Depth Anything 2, MonoDETR) come from the monocular 3D detection and dense depth estimation literature and serve as reference points rather than methodological ancestors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 2D DETR embeddings, trained with zero 3D supervision, can be probed to recover depth and 3D location at near-3D-model quality suggests that 3D geometric structure is an emergent byproduct of 2D detection training, with implications for how 3D DETR architectures are designed (e.g., whether explicit 3D supervision is necessary or whether 2D-pretrained embeddings suffice as a strong initialization). The consistent under-performance of real-time and lightweight variants (RT-DETR, LW-DETR) raises a practical question about the trade-off between latency and representational richness for downstream 3D tasks. Open questions include whether this 3D information generalizes to other properties (orientation, size), whether it persists under domain shift, and whether it can be leveraged to reduce the 3D annotation burden in 3D detection training.
