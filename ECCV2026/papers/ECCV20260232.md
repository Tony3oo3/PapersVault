## Problem

Current 3D object detection systems are confined to closed-set paradigms that fail when encountering novel object categories in open-world driving scenarios. Existing open-vocabulary 3D detection methods either enforce costly cross-modal contrastive alignment (CLIP-style) that degrades geometric accuracy in sparse outdoor point clouds, or rely on offline self-training that accumulates errors across iterations and discards the informative image modality entirely.

## Input / Output

During training, the framework consumes synchronized LiDAR point clouds and 6-camera RGB images alongside ground-truth 3D boxes for seen categories and text-only category names for unseen ones. At inference, only the lightweight LiDAR student (VoxelNet-based) processes raw point clouds without any vision-side overhead, producing 3D bounding boxes with class labels spanning both seen and unseen categories. The partial-label setting means no 3D annotations exist for unseen classes during training, and the student must generalize purely from distilled pseudo-labels and heatmap supervision.

## Method

BEVOpen3D employs an online teacher-student distillation architecture where an image-based teacher (Swin-Transformer + 3DETR decoder) and a LiDAR student (VoxelNet) are trained simultaneously. The teacher first generates coarse 3D proposals via an open-vocabulary 2D detector and Greedy Box Seeker, then refines them through a Seen-Guided Local Query that max-pools BEV features within each proposal region and decodes geometric corrections learned from seen-category ground truth. A Triple-Source Label Refinement module fuses the original, locally rectified, and globally re-examined proposals via 3D-NMS to produce high-quality pseudo labels for unseen categories, blocking error accumulation. Knowledge transfer to the student occurs through Heatmap Proposal Distillation, which aligns top-K matched BEV heatmap responses between teacher and student using a weighted L2 prior loss and L1 score loss, avoiding unstable cross-modal feature alignment while preserving vision-grounded semantic priors.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set with 4 novel classes, BEVOpen3D (Seed-Seed configuration) achieves 44.10% mAP, 46.25% NDS, and 33.11% AP_U on unseen categories, while the Focal-Trans variant reaches the highest unseen performance at 34.80% AP_U. In the more challenging 7-novel-class setting, the Trans-Focal configuration yields 30.10% mAP, 31.26% NDS, and 16.26% AP_U. The method maintains stable NDS scores (28–31%) across both settings, indicating robust velocity and attribute prediction even when most categories are unseen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OV-Uni3DETR, BEVOpen3D achieves a substantial advantage on unseen-category detection: 33.11% vs. 17.79% AP_U in the 4-novel setting (+15.32 points) and 16.26% vs. 3.25% AP_U in the 7-novel setting (+13.01 points). OV-Uni3DETR's NDS drops dramatically from 30.82% to 10.87% as unseen categories increase, while BEVOpen3D maintains stable NDS (46.25% → 31.26%), indicating far better geometric and attribute prediction for novel objects. Against Find n' Propagate, BEVOpen3D edges ahead on AP_U (33.11% vs. 32.67% in 4-novel; 16.26% vs. 14.93% in 7-novel) and matches or slightly exceeds overall mAP, confirming that the Triple-Source Label Refinement effectively prevents the error accumulation that plagues the baseline's multi-round self-training. The gap widens in the 7-novel setting, validating that active geometric rectification is more effective than relying solely on offline heuristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the open-vocabulary 3D detection line initiated by OV-3DET and CoDA, which transfer 2D vision-language knowledge to point clouds via contrastive alignment, and directly builds on Find n' Propagate's offline greedy-search-plus-self-training pipeline. It departs from the indoor two-stage paradigm (class-agnostic detector + cross-modal contrastive learning) by arguing that end-to-end feature alignment is ill-suited for outdoor LiDAR scenes, and instead repurposes the teacher-student distillation concept at the heatmap-proposal level to sidestep the image–point-cloud domain gap. The Seen-Guided Local Query and Triple-Source Label Refinement modules are novel architectural contributions that reframe pseudo-label generation as a learnable geometric-correction problem rather than a fixed heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling heavy vision-based reasoning (teacher) from lightweight LiDAR inference (student), BEVOpen3D offers a practical path to open-world 3D detection that avoids the error-accumulation trap of iterative self-training and eliminates the need for 3D annotations on unseen classes. The heatmap-level distillation strategy is a clean, low-overhead alternative to cross-modal feature alignment that could be adopted in other multi-modal distillation settings. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (nuScenes) with only 10 categories, dependence on a vision teacher during training, and the partial-label protocol that may not capture truly open-world novelty. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hundreds of unseen categories, whether the teacher can be replaced by a frozen pre-trained model to reduce training cost, and how performance holds on other outdoor benchmarks like Waymo or Argoverse.
