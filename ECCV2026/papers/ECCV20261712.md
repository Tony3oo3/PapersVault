## Problem

Existing multimodal sentiment analysis methods assume all modalities are available, yet real-world deployments frequently encounter missing or corrupted inputs due to noise, hardware failures, or privacy restrictions. Prior approaches to incomplete modalities (generative reconstruction or knowledge distillation) still follow conventional fusion paradigms and fail to account for the importance differences between missing and complete modalities, nor do they address the feature phase shifts in high-dimensional space that missing data induces, which can distort the well-learned representations of intact modalities during fusion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three modality streams per video segment: 35-dimensional facial action unit features (OpenFace), 74-dimensional COVAREP acoustic descriptors, and 300-d GloVe or 768-d BERT language embeddings, each with variable sequence lengths. Missing modalities or frames are substituted with zero vectors, and the framework handles both inter-modality missingness (entire modality absent) and intra-modality missingness (random frame-level dropout at rates 0–0.9). Output is a sentiment class prediction (evaluated via F1, ACC-7/5/2) and a continuous sentiment intensity score (evaluated via MAE).

## Method

PRLF comprises two core modules. The Adaptive Modality Reliability Estimator (AMRE) dynamically scores each modality's reliability by fusing per-sample classification confidence with the trace of the Fisher Information Matrix, using a sigmoid-gated weight that shifts from confidence-dominant in early training to FIM-dominant as gradient signals stabilize, thereby identifying a dominant modality per sample. The Progressive Interaction module (ProgInteract) then performs iterative refinement over 4 steps: at each step, modalities undergo self-attention refinement and modality-importance-weighted cross-modal attention, with a time-dependent coefficient λ_t that emphasizes unimodal features early and cross-modal features later. A Decomposer sub-module projects the dominant modality's features into each auxiliary modality's space via a learned gating vector, enforces a phase-orthogonality loss between the projection and residual to balance alignment with complementarity, and applies a denoising network to the residual before updating the auxiliary feature for the next iteration. The total objective combines task cross-entropy, unimodal classification loss, and the phase consistency loss.

## Results

On CMU-MOSI, PRLF achieves an average F1 of 77.02% across all missing-modality conditions and 85.78% under full modality ({l,a,v}), outperforming the strongest baseline HRLF (76.74% avg, 84.15% full). On CMU-MOSEI, PRLF reaches 76.24% average F1 and 85.44% full-modality F1, again surpassing HRLF (75.63% avg, 82.93% full). On the Chinese SIMS dataset, PRLF attains the highest average F1 of 81.19%, edging out LNLN (80.86%) and EMOE (80.12%). Under extreme intra-modality missingness (90% dropout), PRLF retains F1 scores of ~60 on MOSI and ~70 on MOSEI, substantially above all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRLF outperforms all ten baselines on the average F1 across both CMU-MOSI and CMU-MOSEI. The closest competitor is HRLF [18], which trails PRLF by 0.28% on MOSI average (76.74 vs 77.02) and 0.61% on MOSEI average (75.63 vs 76.24); PRLF also leads HRLF by 1.63% on MOSI full-modality F1 (85.78 vs 84.15). Among distillation-based methods, UMDF [17] and CorrKD [19] are the strongest, but PRLF surpasses them by 1.46% and 2.33% on MOSI average, respectively. EMOE [6] achieves a slightly higher full-modality F1 on MOSI (85.40 vs 85.78 — PRLF still wins) and MOSEI (85.30 vs 85.44), but its average F1 is notably lower (73.31 on MOSI, 73.17 on MOSEI), indicating weaker robustness under missing conditions. On SIMS, PRLF (81.19% avg) beats LNLN [50] (80.86%) and EMOE [6] (80.12%), while traditional fusion methods like MISA [9] (66.32%) and Self-MM [45] (73.13%) degrade substantially under missing modalities. Under extreme intra-modality dropout (90%), PRLF retains F1 of ~60 (MOSI) and ~70 (MOSEI), far exceeding TETFN [29] and all other baselines, confirming its superior noise robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant strategies for handling incomplete modalities in MSA—generative reconstruction (DiCMoR, MRAN) and knowledge distillation from complete-modality teachers (UMDF, CorrKD, HRLF)—by instead treating modality reliability as a dynamic, per-sample property. The key conceptual departure is the use of the Fisher Information Matrix trace as a modality-importance signal, which the authors show can detect information loss that classification confidence alone misses (e.g., a model reporting high confidence despite missing key frames). The progressive interaction design, which schedules intra-modal refinement before cross-modal alignment, repurposes the curriculum-learning intuition in a novel way for cross-modal feature alignment rather than for training stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FIM-based reliability estimator offers a principled, gradient-based signal for modality trustworthiness that is complementary to confidence and could be adapted to other multimodal tasks beyond sentiment analysis. However, the method is currently validated only on three moderate-scale datasets with three modalities, and the per-epoch FIM tracking introduces non-trivial computational overhead that may limit scalability to longer sequences or more modalities. Open questions include whether the progressive schedule (4 iterations) generalizes across architectures, whether the phase-alignment loss is necessary in settings where modalities are naturally well-aligned, and how the framework performs under adversarial or semantically corrupted (rather than randomly missing) inputs.
