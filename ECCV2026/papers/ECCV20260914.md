## Problem

Existing reasoning segmentation methods rely on 7B+ parameter LLMs to interpret implicit textual queries, incurring prohibitive computational and memory costs (e.g., 18.4 GB, 320 ms per image) that preclude practical deployment. No prior work has demonstrated that a small language model (SLM) can perform reasoning segmentation, and naively scaling down introduces a critical gap: SLMs lack the capacity to encode sufficient spatial instruction cues within the <SEG> token for dense pixel-level mask prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260914/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (resized to 1024×1024 for segmentation, yielding 256 visual tokens via DINOv3) and a natural-language reasoning instruction (e.g., 'which appliance is commonly used to reheat food quickly') as input. It produces a binary segmentation mask at full image resolution. The text instruction is processed by a Qwen2.5-0.5B SLM alongside 16 learnable register tokens appended to the sequence, and the final mask is generated by a SAM-style decoder that fuses the <SEG> token's hidden state with text-conditioned spatial features upsampled to the image grid.

## Method

LReSeg introduces two key architectural designs to make reasoning segmentation feasible with an 805M-parameter SLM. First, a unified DINOv3 encoder replaces the conventional dual-encoder (CLIP + SAM-H) setup, ensuring the visual features used for language reasoning and mask decoding are identical, eliminating redundant computation. Second, 16 learnable register tokens are appended to the SLM input; at a selected intermediate layer (l=6), the top-k visual tokens most attended by the reasoning text (identified via the SLM's own text-to-visual attention map) are fused into the register tokens through an MLP, allowing them to carry text-conditioned dense spatial cues through subsequent self-attention layers. At decoding time, a dual-path strategy combines the <SEG> token (sparse semantic prompt) with the register-token-enhanced visual features (dense spatial guidance) via cross-attention, and the mask is produced by projecting the <SEG> hidden state against the upsampled fused feature map. Training is multi-task, jointly optimizing segmentation loss (BCE + Dice) and LLaVA-style language supervision, with a two-stage MSLM pre-training (projector alignment on CC3M, then full fine-tuning on LLaVA instruct data) preceding 5-epoch segmentation fine-tuning.

## Results

On the ReasonSeg benchmark, LReSeg (805M params) achieves 52.8 cIoU (val) and 50.0 cIoU (test), outperforming the strongest baseline READ* (50.3/47.1 cIoU) by 2.5 and 2.9 points respectively. In terms of efficiency, LReSeg runs at 98 ms per image with 3.6 GB memory and 1.42 TFLOPs, compared to 320 ms / 18.4 GB / 7.16 TFLOPs for LISA-7B — a 3.3× speedup and ~5× memory reduction. On RefCOCO, LReSeg reaches 75.1/78.2/70.4 cIoU (val/testA/testB), surpassing all prior referring segmentation methods including LAVT (72.7/75.8/68.8) and ReLA (73.8/76.5/70.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LReSeg outperforms all 15 baselines on ReasonSeg. Against the closest reasoning segmentation baselines (LISA* and READ*, both reconfigured with the same Qwen2.5-0.5B SLM), LReSeg gains +2.5/+2.9 cIoU over READ* and +4.5/+4.8 cIoU over LISA* on val/test, while using fewer parameters (805M vs. 1.5B) and running 2.4× faster (98 ms vs. 233–238 ms). Among MSLM baselines, LReSeg surpasses LLaVA-OneVision (897M) by +4.5/+4.0 cIoU and InternVL3 (1.0B) by +4.2/+4.9 cIoU, despite having fewer parameters. Against segmentation specialists (GRES, SEEM, OVSeg, X-Decoder, Grounded-SAM), LReSeg leads by 20–35 cIoU points, confirming that language-reasoning-based segmentation substantially outperforms purely visual approaches on implicit queries. On RefCOCO, LReSeg also beats all referring segmentation baselines (MCN, VLT, CRIS, LAVT, ReLA, HieA2G) by 1.8–12.7 cIoU, and still exceeds READ* by +2.8/+2.1/+2.6 cIoU on refCOCO val/testA/testB, demonstrating strong transferability beyond its primary reasoning segmentation task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reasoning segmentation framework introduced by LISA (which couples an MLLM with a SAM decoder via a <SEG> token) by replacing the 7B-scale LLM with a 0.5B small language model (Qwen2.5-0.5B). The key architectural departure is twofold: (1) introducing learnable register tokens that aggregate text-conditioned spatial cues through the SLM's self-attention to compensate for the SLM's limited dense spatial grounding, and (2) adopting a unified DINOv3 encoder for both reasoning and mask decoding, eliminating the redundant dual-encoder (CLIP + SAM-H) design used by LISA and READ. The two-stage training strategy and instruction-tuning data composition follow LLaVA, while the segmentation decoder retains a SAM-like architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LReSeg demonstrates that reasoning segmentation—a task previously assumed to require multi-billion-parameter LLMs for contextual reasoning—can be performed competitively with a model 10× smaller, 3.3× faster, and using 5× less memory, opening the door to on-device and edge deployment of language-guided pixel-level understanding. The register token mechanism is a lightweight, general-purpose technique that could transfer to other SLM-based vision-language tasks requiring dense spatial prediction. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video reasoning segmentation, multi-object or interactive settings, and whether even smaller backbones (e.g., 0.1B) can maintain performance with appropriate architectural adjustments.
