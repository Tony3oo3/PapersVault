## Problem

High-fidelity 4D physical simulation requires accurate per-voxel physical parameters (Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, density), but existing automation methods either demand hours of per-scene optimization or rely solely on visual cues that cannot disambiguate physically distinct but visually similar materials (e.g., rigid vs. flexible rubber). No prior approach provides fast, generalizable, and semantically controllable physical property assignment in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261556/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 15 multi-view RGB images rendered from the upper hemisphere of a 3D asset plus a free-form text prompt (ranging from a simple 'This is a [object name]' to specific descriptions like 'This is mahogany that might sink to the bottom'). It outputs a dense 3D voxel field encoding three physical parameters—Young's modulus E, Poisson's ratio ν, and density ρ—where E and ρ are predicted in log-space to handle their multi-order-of-magnitude variation. The voxel representation is solver-agnostic and can be directly transferred to meshes, point clouds, or Gaussian Splatting for downstream MPM or FEM simulation without per-scene optimization.

## Method

AutoPhyX operates in two stages: first, it lifts 2D OpenCLIP features into a 3D voxel grid via differentiable NeRF volume rendering (using a frozen pre-trained RGB NeRF density to handle occlusion correctly, unlike naive multi-view averaging), then fills unobservable interior voxels through hierarchical ray-casting with the even-odd rule. Second, a 3D U-Net decodes the voxel field into physical parameters, with FiLM conditioning layers at multiple scales where text embeddings (from a frozen OpenCLIP text encoder) predict channel-wise scaling γ and shifting β via dedicated MLPs, dynamically modulating visual features based on linguistic cues. Because both visual and text features share the OpenCLIP latent space, no cross-modal projection is needed. The model is trained end-to-end with MSE loss on occupied voxels over the Text2PhyX dataset (1,700 objects × 8 text-conditioned variants = 13,600 triplets), which is generated by an automated VLM-driven pipeline that performs part segmentation, queries engineering databases for plausible parameter ranges, and validates assignments via simulation-based consistency checks.

## Results

On Text2PhyX, AutoPhyX with OpenCLIP achieves a PSNR of 26.012 and SSIM of 0.921, outperforming the best feed-forward baseline Pixie (CLIP) by ~2.6 dB in PSNR and reducing average physical property error from 0.078 to 0.059. On the out-of-distribution ABO-500 real-world benchmark, AutoPhyX reduces Average Displacement Error (ADE) to 9.433 kg (a 20% improvement over NeRF2Physics at 11.826 kg) and achieves the best ALDE (0.652) and MnRE (0.582). Inference completes in under 2 seconds per object, orders of magnitude faster than optimization-based methods that require minutes to hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoPhyX decisively outperforms all five baselines on both datasets. Against the optimization-based methods DreamPhysics and OmniPhysGS, AutoPhyX achieves substantially higher PSNR (26.01 vs. 19.22 and 17.88) in a single forward pass (~2 s) versus minutes-to-hours of per-scene optimization, and additionally predicts all three physical properties (E, ν, ρ) whereas those methods only recover E. Against the VLM-based baselines NeRF2Physics and Gemini, AutoPhyX reduces average physical error by over 90% (0.059 vs. 0.841 and 0.218), demonstrating that direct VLM prompting suffers from uncalibrated internal physics knowledge and hallucination. Against the closest feed-forward competitor Pixie (CLIP input), AutoPhyX gains 2.6 dB PSNR and cuts log E error from 0.062 to 0.042, attributable to the FiLM-based text conditioning that resolves visual ambiguity. On the real-world ABO-500 mass estimation benchmark, AutoPhyX beats both Pixie (ADE 9.43 vs. 12.57 kg) and NeRF2Physics (ADE 9.43 vs. 11.83 kg), confirming generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoPhyX extends the feed-forward physical property prediction line (Pixie, VoMP) by introducing text as a conditioning signal to resolve the visual ambiguity that purely visual methods cannot overcome. The cross-modal fusion mechanism is a direct application of FiLM (Perez et al., 2018) within a 3D U-Net, repurposing a well-established conditioning layer for the novel domain of volumetric material parameter regression. The paper departs from optimization-based approaches (DreamPhysics, OmniPhysGS) by eliminating per-scene fitting, and from direct VLM prediction (NeRF2Physics, Gemini) by using VLMs only in a structured data-generation pipeline rather than as the inference engine.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play voxel-field output that transfers across Gaussian Splatting, meshes, and point clouds without re-optimization is practically valuable for robotics and interactive simulation pipelines that need diverse rendering/solver backends. The Text2PhyX dataset (1,700 assets × 8 variants) provides the first large-scale supervised resource for text-conditioned physics, though it is limited to three solid-body properties (E, ν, ρ) and excludes friction, damping, and fluid parameters. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to anisotropic or composite materials, whether higher-resolution fields are needed for fine-grained structures, and whether the VLM-driven data pipeline can scale beyond the current 1,700-asset base without quality degradation.
