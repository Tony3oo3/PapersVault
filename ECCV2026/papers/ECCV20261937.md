## Problem

Existing methods for transferring 2D foundation model knowledge into 3D either require expensive per-shape optimization (e.g., Diff3F, DFD) or produce task-specific features that do not generalize beyond their training objective (e.g., PartField, DenseMatcher). MeshFM challenges the assumption that 3D tasks need specialized 3D supervision, arguing that the main barrier is imperfect feature distillation—specifically aliasing from ViT patchification and feature bleeding across part boundaries—rather than a fundamental lack of 3D signal in 2D features.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D mesh normalized to the unit cube, sampled as a point cloud of shape C×3, and outputs a continuous per-point feature field parameterized as a triplane tensor (3×D×H×W) that can be queried at arbitrary 3D coordinates via bilinear interpolation and summation across the three planes. No 3D annotations, textures, or canonical orientations are required; the only supervision comes from 2D foundation model features extracted from rendered views. The resulting features are rotation-robust across the full SO(3) group and can be applied zero-shot to segmentation, correspondence, deformation, and classification.

## Method

MeshFM uses a two-stage distillation pipeline. In Stage 1, multi-view renderings of each training mesh are passed through DINOv2 to obtain 2D feature maps, which are back-projected to 3D via barycentric coordinates (following DFD) and used to optimize a per-shape neural feature field; a key innovation is SAM-based feature aliasing correction, where pixels whose features deviate beyond a threshold from their segment's channel-wise median are replaced by that median, eliminating boundary bleeding inherent to ViT patchification. In Stage 2, a feedforward network (PVCNN encoder projecting to triplanes, followed by a 2D CNN + Transformer + transposed CNN decoder) is trained to regress the frozen Stage 1 teacher fields via L2 loss on uniformly sampled surface query points. SO(3) rotation augmentation is applied with a linearly increasing maximum rotation angle to achieve full rotation robustness, enabling single-pass inference on arbitrarily oriented shapes without test-time optimization.

## Results

On PartObjaverse-Tiny segmentation, MeshFM achieves 0.549 mean IoU (original) and 0.539 (SO(3)-rotated), outperforming PartField (0.542/0.393) and Diff3F (0.515/0.488). On PartNetE, MeshFM reaches 0.520/0.510 mIoU vs. PartField's 0.517/0.499. For dense correspondence on DenseCorr3D, MeshFM attains AUC 0.52 and geodesic error 9.6 (original), matching or exceeding DenseMatcher (0.50/10.2) and Diff3F (0.46/11.8), while on TOSCA it achieves AUC 0.34/Err 18.8 vs. Diff3F's 0.48/17.1. On Manifold40 classification, MeshFM features yield 92% accuracy (original) and 85% (rotated) with a 3-layer MLP, substantially outperforming all baselines under rotation (next best: PartField at 41%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeshFM is compared against three SOTA baselines: PartField (segmentation), Diff3F (correspondence), and DenseMatcher (textured correspondence). On segmentation, MeshFM marginally outperforms PartField on both PartObjaverse-Tiny (0.549 vs. 0.542) and PartNetE (0.520 vs. 0.517) without using any 3D annotations, while PartField is trained directly with 3D segmentation labels. Under SO(3) rotation, the gap widens dramatically: MeshFM maintains 0.539 mIoU while PartField drops to 0.393 and Diff3F to 0.488, revealing a strong canonical-orientation bias in both baselines. On DenseCorr3D correspondence, MeshFM achieves the best AUC (0.52) and lowest geodesic error (9.6) among all methods, slightly beating DenseMatcher (0.50/10.2) and Diff3F (0.46/11.8); on TOSCA, MeshFM (AUC 0.34) trails Diff3F (0.48) on original shapes but far exceeds it under rotation (0.33 vs. 0.28). For classification on Manifold40, MeshFM features are the most discriminative (92% vs. 88% for Diff3F, 85% for PartField, 82% for DenseMatcher), and the advantage is most pronounced under rotation where MeshFM retains 85% accuracy while all baselines collapse below 42%.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshFM builds directly on the barycentric feature distillation framework of DFD [24] for the Stage 1 teacher field optimization, and extends the broader 2D-to-3D distillation line (Diff3F, LERF, DFF) by replacing per-shape optimization with a feedforward predictor. It departs from task-specific 3D feature learners like PartField and DenseMatcher, which use 2D priors only as training signals for a single downstream task, by instead distilling a general-purpose feature field and showing it transfers zero-shot across segmentation, correspondence, and deformation. The SAM-based feature aliasing correction is a novel technical contribution that addresses a structural limitation of ViT patchification in prior distillation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides empirical evidence that properly distilled 2D foundation model features, combined with a feedforward 3D predictor, can match task-specific 3D methods without any 3D annotation or task-specific fine-tuning, simplifying the 3D feature learning pipeline considerably. The SO(3) rotation robustness is a practically important gap that prior methods (especially PartField) fail to address, making MeshFM more applicable to in-the-wild shapes. Key limitations include inheriting DINOv2's left-right ambiguity and the modest (though consistent) performance margins on non-rotated shapes; open questions include whether the approach extends to textured or multi-material shapes and whether the triplane representation is optimal for very high-frequency geometric detail.
