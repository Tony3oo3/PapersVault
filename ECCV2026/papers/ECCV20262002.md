## Problem

In crowded scenes, multi-object tracking association scores suffer from mode-dependent miscalibration: occlusions corrupt appearance embeddings, uniform appearance induces visual confusion, and fast motion weakens geometric continuity. Existing trackers apply a single global scoring rule with fixed thresholds, ignoring that hard errors concentrate into a small set of recurring regimes and that pair correctness depends on set-level competition among candidates rather than isolated pairwise scoring.

## Input / Output

MCRC is a plug-in module inserted at the association stage of any tracker without modifying its detector or motion model. It consumes the native pairwise cost matrix, per-pair geometric differences (position, velocity, scale, aspect ratio), set-level rank statistics computed by sorting competing detections per track, and appearance/re-identification embeddings. It outputs a calibrated cost matrix via an additive residual, a two-way mode probability distribution (spatial and motion), and—only for uncertainty-gated pairs—a refined detection embedding that replaces the corrupted appearance feature before final cost integration.

## Method

MCRC follows a diagnose-then-correct pipeline: an MLP with unit-norm projection encodes structured error cues into a latent space, where prototype-based mode diagnosis (spherical k-means-initialized centers updated via EMA) yields softmax probabilities over discrete spatial and motion error regimes. A multi-level residual vector quantization codebook discretizes the feature space into a reusable vocabulary of recurring failure configurations, with the primary codebook anchor conditioning downstream semantic repair. A lightweight MLP regressor then predicts an additive cost residual conditioned on the inferred mode probabilities and baseline cost. For pairs whose combined uncertainty score (crowd confusion plus motion displacement gated by low IoU) exceeds a threshold, a four-token transformer encoder selectively refines the corrupted detection embedding, and the final cost blends the geometric and semantic corrections via a sigmoid-gated interpolation. Deterministic acceptance checks—an embedding margin reduction and an amplitude clip—ensure the semantic repair is committed only when it provably improves similarity, preserving baseline behavior on easy pairs.

## Results

On DanceTrack test, CAMEL+MCRC achieves 72.0 HOTA, 63.1 AssA, and 76.2 IDF1, improving over the CAMELTrack baseline by +2.7 HOTA and +4.2 AssA while maintaining stable DetA (82.1 vs 81.8). On SportsMOT, MCRC reaches 81.6 HOTA (+1.2 over CAMELTrack), and on MOT20, ByteTrack+MCRC gains +2.4 HOTA and +4.7 AssA over plain ByteTrack. The module adds only 2.7M parameters and 0.66 GFLOPs, with end-to-end FPS dropping from 13.5 to 12.8 on CAMELTrack and from 23.5 to 22.2 on ByteTrack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCRC is evaluated as a plug-in module on top of four distinct tracker architectures (CAMELTrack, ByteTrack, MOTIP, MeMOTR) and consistently improves identity-preservation metrics. On DanceTrack, CAMEL+MCRC (72.0 HOTA) surpasses the strongest prior tracker FDTA (71.7 HOTA) by 0.3 HOTA and MOTIP (69.6) by 2.4 HOTA, while the AssA gain of +4.2 over CAMELTrack confirms the method targets association errors rather than detection. On MOT17, MCRC lifts CAMELTrack from 62.4 to 64.5 HOTA (+2.1), outperforming ByteTrack (63.1) and GHOST (62.8) in the same table. On SportsMOT, CAMEL+MCRC (81.6 HOTA) edges out Deep-EIoU (77.2) and DiffMOT (76.2) by 4.4 and 5.4 HOTA respectively. On MOT20, ByteTrack+MCRC (63.7 HOTA, 64.3 AssA) beats StrongSORT (62.6 HOTA) and OC-SORT (62.4 HOTA), demonstrating generalization to a different base tracker. The plug-in evaluation (Table 13) shows the largest absolute gain on the weakest baseline: ByteTrack improves by +4.2 HOTA, while transformer-based trackers (MOTIP +1.8, MeMOTR +2.1) still benefit, confirming MCRC's architecture-agnostic design.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCRC positions itself as a calibration layer inserted between a tracker's native association scoring and its bipartite matching step, drawing on three distinct lineages: (1) global confidence calibration and uncertainty modeling from classification, which it reinterprets as mode-conditioned rather than sample-agnostic; (2) residual vector quantization from discrete representation learning, repurposed to build a reusable vocabulary of recurring geometric error configurations; and (3) feature-refinement/denoising approaches (diffusion-based, denoising-representation models) that it applies selectively via an uncertainty gate rather than uniformly. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior MOT work by arguing that association miscalibration is not a single global bias but a small set of recurring, set-level competitive regimes, and that correcting them requires diagnosing the regime before adjusting costs. It does not build on a single prior tracker but is designed as a tracker-agnostic plug-in validated on heuristic (ByteTrack, CAMELTrack) and transformer-based (MOTIP, MeMOTR) architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in MCRC's plug-in design: 2.7 M parameters and 0.66 GFLOPs added to an existing tracker yield consistent 1–4 HOTA gains across four diverse benchmarks without modifying the detector, motion model, or appearance backbone, making it an attractive retrofit for production tracking pipelines. The empirical audit showing that hard association errors cluster into a small number of recurring modes (partial occlusion, crowd confusion, fast-motion discontinuity) provides a useful diagnostic lens for the community. The primary open limitation is the fixed prototype and codebook capacity, which cannot adapt to genuinely novel camera geometries or scene types at zero-shot inference; the authors flag online dynamic prototype updates as the natural next step. Whether the mode-conditioned framing generalizes beyond the four tested datasets to, e.g., aerial or indoor tracking remains untested.
