## Problem

Existing multimodal neural rendering methods require expensive, calibrated captures from every sensor modality for each new scene, making them impractical when only RGB or a handful of frames from other sensors are available. Single-view RGB-to-modality conversion methods (e.g., MST++, PolarAnything) can predict missing modalities but produce results that lack multi-view consistency, rendering them unreliable for 3D rendering tasks. The paper addresses this gap by learning cross-modality correlations across scenes during a one-off pre-training phase, enabling multi-view consistent rendering of any modality from RGB-only fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262798/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During fine-tuning, the method takes multi-view RGB images (45 views) of a new scene, optionally supplemented by a very small number of frames (1–25) from one additional modality (MS, Pol, or NIR), and produces a scene-specific 3D radiance field that can be rendered from arbitrary novel viewpoints in all five modalities (RGB, NIR, Mono, Pol, MS). The pre-training phase consumes 27 object-centric scenes from MMS-DATA with complete multimodal coverage. All training and evaluation use raw mosaicked sensor frames with metrics computed only on masked foreground regions, and the FT converges in just 30k iterations with ~300k trainable parameters.

## Method

SPoILeR replaces the multi-resolution hash grids of MultimodalStudio with a basis/coefficient field decomposition (inspired by Dictionary Fields): a shared basis field encodes cross-scene, cross-modality radiance correlations, while a per-scene coefficient field captures scene-specific geometry and appearance. The radiance module chains a projection function, a latent encoder (concatenating viewing direction and SDF geometry features), and per-modality MLP decoders, keeping most model capacity in the feature grids rather than the MLPs. Training proceeds in two phases: a 1M-iteration multi-scene pre-training that alternately freezes shared and scene-specific modules (80/20 split per scene batch), and a 30k-iteration single-scene fine-tuning that optimizes only the coefficient field and geometry while keeping all shared modules frozen. Three regularization losses stabilize the latent space: a latent space geometry loss (KL divergence between pairwise latent and modality distance distributions), an inverse function loss (MSE between the actual latent and one predicted by a frozen MLP from decoded radiance), and a modality-to-luma loss (MSE between per-modality estimated luminance and RGB luminance) that prevents decoupling of infrared-sensitive modalities during RGB-only supervision.

## Results

In the standard case (RGB-only training), SPoILeR achieves 30.07 PSNR on RGB, outperforming MMS-FW (29.53 PSNR), and additionally renders Mono (25.78), NIR (26.55), Pol (24.25), and MS (25.45) PSNR—modalities that MMS-FW cannot produce without their supervision. In the upper-bound setting (all modalities available), MMS-FW leads by approximately 2 dB in PSNR due to its larger capacity (~12M vs ~4.1M parameters) and single-scene training, while SSIM values are comparable. In unbalanced-modality tests with only 1 frame of a second modality, SPoILeR gains 6–8 dB over MMS-FW, demonstrating the value of pre-trained cross-modality knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MMS-FW in the standard RGB-only setting, SPoILeR wins on RGB PSNR (30.07 vs 29.53) and uniquely renders four additional modalities (Mono, NIR, Pol, MS) that MMS-FW simply cannot produce without their supervision. In the upper-bound all-modality setting, MMS-FW outperforms SPoILeR by ~2 dB in PSNR across all modalities, attributable to its larger model capacity and single-scene optimization. Against the two-stage pipeline of MMS-FW + MST++ for MS rendering, SPoILeR achieves 25.45 PSNR versus 21.99 PSNR (a 3.46 dB gain), because MST++'s single-view conversions lack multi-view consistency and propagate errors into the 3D volume. Against MMS-FW + PolarAnything for Pol rendering, SPoILeR reduces MAngE from 44.13° to 30.05° (14.08° improvement) and MAbsE from 0.089 to 0.045, again due to PolarAnything's inability to produce multi-view consistent polarization estimates. In unbalanced-modality experiments, SPoILeR's advantage over MMS-FW is largest when only 1 frame of the second modality is available (gains of ~8 dB for MS, ~7 dB for Pol, ~6 dB for NIR), narrowing to ~2.5 dB at 10 frames and vanishing at 25 frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPoILeR builds directly on MultimodalStudio (MMS-FW) [42] for its multimodal NeRF backbone and on Dictionary Fields (DiF) [9] for the basis-coefficient decomposition that separates scene-shared from scene-specific information. It departs from prior multimodal rendering methods (X-NeRF, NeSpoF, MultimodalStudio) by eliminating the requirement for complete multimodal captures per scene, instead learning cross-scene, cross-modality correlations during a one-off pre-training phase. It also contrasts with single-view modality conversion approaches (MST++, PolarAnything) by producing multi-view consistent renderings through a shared 3D representation rather than per-frame feed-forward prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: it removes the need for expensive, calibrated multimodal sensor rigs for every new scene, making spectral and polarimetric novel view synthesis accessible with only standard RGB cameras. Key limitations remain—spectral estimation is reliable only for materials seen during pre-training, a new pre-training is required for any new modality, and the modality-to-luma loss assumes visible-spectrum correlation (ruling out thermal). Open questions include robustness to uncontrolled illumination, varying sensor properties, and whether the pre-training knowledge can be made more transferable across material classes without re-training.
