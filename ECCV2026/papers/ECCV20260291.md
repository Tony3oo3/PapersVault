## Problem

Existing online error detection methods for procedural videos suffer from critical limitations: they detect only the first error in a video, primarily address procedural errors while overlooking nuanced execution errors, or rely on heavy LLMs, hand-pose inputs, and erroneous training data. This paper asks whether the intrinsic prediction behavior of lightweight action detectors—specifically their sensitivity to input dynamics and temporal context—can be exploited to detect both error types in real time without complex architectures or specialized supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260291/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes egocentric video frames causally (only past and current frames), with frame-level features pre-extracted by TimeSFormer (pre-trained on Ego4D) at 10 FPS for EgoPER or at 30/60 FPS for Assembly-101-O and EPIC-Tent-O. At each time step t, four detectors process sequences of s or l frames ending at t to produce action class predictions, which are then combined via majority voting over four pairwise agreement checks to yield a binary error flag z_t ∈ {0,1}. A causal mode filter (window size 30) smooths predictions, introducing approximately a one-second detection delay.

## Method

ESTANet is an ensemble of four lightweight detectors (each a single-layer GRU + linear head) that detects errors through prediction inconsistencies. Two standard detectors produce stable predictions, while two error-sensitive detectors incorporate a Temporal-Aware Dynamic (TAD) module that generates input-dependent, temporal-aware weights and biases for an affine transformation of frame features, making them more reactive to out-of-distribution execution errors. A temporally-varying attribute is introduced by training detectors with different window sizes: s is set to the shortest action duration (short-range dependency), and l is determined by a robust strategy ensuring the window covers β=2 complete preceding actions for θ=50% of actions (long-range dependency). During inference, four comparison pairs are formed—two pairing standard vs. error-sensitive detectors at the same window (capturing execution errors) and two pairing small- vs. large-window detectors of the same type (capturing procedural errors)—and a frame is flagged as erroneous if at least three pairs mismatch. Training uses only error-free videos with cross-entropy and temporal smoothing losses, following the miniROAD framework.

## Results

ESTANet achieves state-of-the-art online error detection across all three benchmarks. On EgoPER, it reaches 47.2% F1@10, 37.8% F1@25, and 21.6% F1@50, surpassing the strongest baseline DTGL (39.6/33.8/20.9). On Assembly-101-O and EPIC-Tent-O, ESTANet attains 59.6% and 70.2% Avg-F1 respectively, outperforming DTGL by 6.1 and 23.7 points. The method also achieves the highest E-F1 scores (49.6% and 92.4%) while maintaining real-time inference at 24.4 FPS with only 18.8M parameters in the error-detection module.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESTANet outperforms all five baselines on every dataset where they overlap. Against DTGL (the strongest non-supervised baseline), ESTANet gains +7.6 F1@10 on EgoPER, +6.1 Avg-F1 on Assembly-101-O, and +23.7 Avg-F1 on EPIC-Tent-O, with the largest margin on EPIC-Tent-O where DTGL's task-graph approach struggles with unseen execution errors. Against PREGO, which relies on an LLM for anticipation, ESTANet improves F1@10 by 6.0 points on EgoPER and Avg-F1 by 30.2 points on EPIC-Tent-O, while also being ~18× faster (24.4 vs 1.33 FPS). Against MistSense (the only baseline trained with erroneous videos), ESTANet still leads by 10.4 Avg-F1 on EPIC-Tent-O despite using only error-free training data. On Assembly-101-O, ESTANet also surpasses the older graph-based baselines MSGI and MSG² by 26.5 and 13.4 Avg-F1 points respectively, demonstrating that prediction-inconsistency-based detection substantially outperforms subtask-graph matching for procedural error localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

ESTANet builds directly on MiniROAD's minimal GRU-based online action detection framework, adopting its training loss and causal inference setup. It departs conceptually from recent online error detection methods (PREGO, DTGL, MistSense) that rely on LLM-based anticipation, learned task graphs, or hand-pose inputs, instead exploiting a simpler principle: prediction inconsistencies among a small ensemble of lightweight detectors with varying sensitivities and temporal contexts. The TAD module (input-dependent affine transformation via a GRU) is a novel architectural addition that makes detectors more reactive to out-of-distribution execution errors, while the temporally-varying window-size strategy repurposes known GRU behavior under different clip durations to amplify procedural-error signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving real-time (24.4 FPS combined) online error detection with only 18.8M parameters for the error-detection head, making it deployable on wearable devices like HoloLens or Vision Pro where LLM-based competitors run at 1–2.5 FPS. The paper demonstrates that a lightweight, training-on-error-free-videos-only pipeline can outperform heavier LLM- and graph-based methods across three benchmarks, suggesting that intrinsic detector behavior is an underexploited signal. Limitations include sensitivity to subtle execution errors (insufficient spatial information in features), over-reliance on temporal priors at video onset, and false positives from long background gaps—open questions include whether richer spatial features or adaptive windowing could close these gaps and whether the approach generalizes to open-vocabulary or multi-task procedural settings.
