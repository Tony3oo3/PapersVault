## Problem

Current video MLLMs trained via supervised fine-tuning act as passive 'Observers' that describe ongoing events but cannot rigorously evaluate how far a robotic manipulation task has progressed toward its goal. They are brittle to unseen objects, cannot explain their predictions, and often assign high progress scores to failed attempts whose visual trajectories merely resemble successful motions. This limits their utility as reliable process supervisors for deriving dense reward signals in long-horizon manipulation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a structured temporal tuple: an initial state image, a process video sequence, a current state image, and a natural-language task instruction, with the video explicitly sandwiched between the two boundary images to provide visual anchoring. It produces a structured output consisting of a Chain-of-Thought reasoning chain (enclosed in think tags) followed by a scalar progress estimate in [0, 100] (enclosed in answer tags). The same trained model is also applied zero-shot to discrete failure detection on the RoboFail benchmark, outputting a binary success/failure judgment without any additional fine-tuning.

## Method

PRIMO R1 builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and is trained in two stages: first, SFT on 116k samples with CoT annotations to establish output format and basic progress estimation; second, GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) on 182k samples to elicit self-correcting reasoning without dense intermediate supervision. The key architectural choice is a structured temporal input that explicitly anchors the video sequence between initial and current state images, transforming the task from generic temporal perception into a state-alignment verification problem. The RL reward is composite: a format reward enforces the think/answer structure to prevent the policy from collapsing into direct guessing, and a bounded linear-decay accuracy reward provides dense numerical feedback proportional to prediction error. GRPO replaces the expensive value-function critic of PPO with group-relative advantage estimation over G sampled outputs, making it feasible for video MLLMs where maintaining a separate value network is memory-prohibitive. The core insight is that optimizing for continuous progress reasoning via outcome-based RL intrinsically constructs the temporal context representations needed for discrete failure detection, enabling zero-shot generalization to unseen benchmarks.

## Results

PRIMO R1 (7B) achieves an average MRA of 82.90 and MAE of 15.52 across four environments (AgiBot, BEHAVIOR-1k, RoboTwin, Real Humanoid), outperforming the 72B Qwen2.5-VL-72B by +9.10 MRA points and reducing MAE by approximately 50% relative to specialized reasoning baselines (Video R1 7B: 29.49, Robobrain 7B: 27.56). On the RoboFail failure-detection benchmark, PRIMO R1 achieves 67.0% accuracy, matching Gemini 2.0 Flash and surpassing GPT-4o (63.0%), OpenAI o1 (61.0%), and Cosmos-Reason1-56B (66.2%). The model leads on three of four progress-estimation environments, with the sole exception being Real Humanoid where ProgressLM's dedicated input configuration yields higher MRA (84.24 vs. 72.32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cosmos-Reasoning 7B (66.52 MRA / 29.12 MAE), PRIMO R1 improves average MRA by +16.38 points and cuts MAE by 47%, and on RoboFail outperforms Cosmos-Reason1-7B (60.0%) by 7.0 points and Cosmos-Reason1-56B (66.2%) by 0.8 points. Video R1 7B (66.87 MRA / 29.49 MAE) and Robobrain 7B (67.94 MRA / 27.56 MAE) are both surpassed by a wide margin: PRIMO R1 gains +16.03 and +14.96 MRA respectively while halving their MAE. Among specialized progress models, VLAC (74.90 MRA / 25.10 MAE) is outperformed on average by +8.00 MRA and −9.58 MAE, though VLAC retains an edge on AgiBot MRA (84.92 vs. 87.67 is actually PRIMO winning; VLAC leads on no single environment in MRA). ProgressLM (78.32 MRA / 20.87 MAE) is the closest competitor, trailing PRIMO R1 by −4.58 MRA and +5.35 MAE on average; however, ProgressLM's dedicated real-world configuration gives it a significant advantage on the Real Humanoid split (84.24 vs. 72.32 MRA), the only environment where PRIMO R1 does not lead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the R1 paradigm of outcome-based RL for Chain-of-Thought elicitation (DeepSeek-R1, Video-R1, VLM-R1) from static or short-video reasoning to long-horizon robotic process supervision, where the 'answer' is a continuous progress scalar rather than a discrete label. It departs from prior progress-estimation models (VLAC, Robo-Dopamine, ProgressLM) that rely on SFT-based regression or explicit reference demonstrations, instead using GRPO to incentivize self-organized reasoning chains without dense intermediate annotations. The structured temporal input—anchoring the video sequence between initial and current state images—builds on boundary-state prompting ideas from planning literature (SCIZOR, GRPO-CARE) but applies them specifically to progress estimation rather than action selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that optimizing a 7B model for continuous progress reasoning intrinsically enables zero-shot discrete failure detection (67% on RoboFail, matching Gemini 2.0 Flash) suggests a previously unexplored representational link between the two tasks, potentially reducing the need for dedicated failure-detection training. The PRIMO Dataset and Benchmark provide the first systematic evaluation of out-of-domain generalization (cross-task, cross-environment, sim-to-real) for video-based progress estimation, filling a gap where prior models were only tested in their training domains. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly long-horizon tasks (>10 minutes), whether the structured input overhead is acceptable for real-time deployment, and whether the Sim-to-Real gap (72.32 MRA on Real Humanoid vs. ~87% in simulation) can be closed with more diverse teleoperated data.
