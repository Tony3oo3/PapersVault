## Problem

Existing cross-spectral (VIS-to-IR) image generation methods rely on pixel-level nonlinear mapping that fails to explicitly model shared structural semantics and modality-specific spectral properties, resulting in semantic misalignment, over-smoothing, and unrealistic thermal characteristics. The paper asks whether VIS and IR data share a common representable structure at the semantic level and whether the modality gap can be accurately captured through an explicit decoupling-reconstruction mechanism rather than implicit end-to-end translation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261365/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single VIS RGB image (3 channels) as input and produces a corresponding single-channel IR image, operating in an unpaired setting where strictly aligned VIS-IR pairs are not required at inference. A reverse generator simultaneously maps IR to VIS for cycle-consistency training. Experiments use images at 434×434 (AVIID), 256×256 (IRVI), and 640×512 (DroneCoast), with batch size 1 and training on a single RTX 4090 GPU.

## Method

SHASP is a CycleGAN-style bidirectional GAN whose generator is split into an encoding-decoupling module and a decoding-reconstruction module. The encoding-decoupling module contains a structure content encoder (dual VIS/IR branches with shared residual weights in deep layers) that extracts domain-invariant shared structural features, and two modality-specific spectral feature encoders (reverse-pyramid convolutional networks with adaptive global pooling) that capture unique spectral attributes such as color/texture in VIS and thermal radiation patterns in IR. The decoding-reconstruction module concatenates the shared and target-modality-specific feature vectors along the channel dimension, then decodes via multi-level residual blocks with instance normalization and transposed convolutions to produce the target image. A semantic structure discriminator is applied adversarially on the shared encoder outputs to enforce finer cross-modal alignment beyond what weight-sharing alone provides. Training combines a domain cycle-consistency loss, standard GAN adversarial losses on both domains, and the semantic structure loss, with the optimal configuration weighting the semantic loss at 2× relative to the others.

## Results

On the AVIID dataset, SHASP achieves the best SSIM (0.852) and PSNR (27.44 dB), with a FID of 41.08 (second-best after RGB-TIR's 36.80). On IRVI, SHASP leads all metrics: SSIM 0.679, PSNR 21.93 dB, and FID 84.30. On the self-built DroneCoast aerial dataset, SHASP attains the best SSIM (0.760), PSNR (24.89 dB), and KID (0.0379), while ranking third on FID (125.28 vs. PID's 117.15). The model uses 24.81 M parameters and runs at 50.7 ms per 256×256 image, offering a favorable accuracy–efficiency trade-off relative to heavier diffusion-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHASP outperforms all 11 baselines on SSIM and PSNR across all three datasets, with the largest margins over CycleGAN (+0.14 SSIM on AVIID, +0.12 on IRVI) and DR-AVIT (+0.22 SSIM on AVIID). On FID, SHASP is best on IRVI (84.30 vs. next-best 89.11 from Swin-UNIT) but trails RGB-TIR on AVIID (41.08 vs. 36.80) and PID on DroneCoast (125.28 vs. 117.15), suggesting the decoupling strategy excels at structural fidelity while diffusion-based methods capture slightly better global intensity statistics. On the DroneCoast KID metric, SHASP achieves the lowest value (0.0379), beating StegoGAN (0.0401) and DiffV2IR (0.0492), indicating superior local semantic consistency. In terms of efficiency, SHASP (24.81 M params, 50.7 ms) is substantially faster than diffusion-based PID (309 M, 460.5 ms) and DiffV2IR (162 M, 185.9 ms), while remaining competitive with lightweight GAN baselines like CycleGAN (11.4 M, 46.7 ms) at a modest parameter overhead. The ablation confirms that the semantic refinement loss (α=2) is the single most impactful design choice, improving SSIM from 0.846 to 0.852 and FID from 43.77 to 41.08 over the uniform-weight baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHASP builds directly on the CycleGAN bidirectional generator-discriminator paradigm with cycle-consistency training, but departs from prior cross-spectral generation methods that treat VIS-to-IR translation as a pixel-level style transfer problem. The key conceptual shift is reframing the task as explicit decoupling of modality-shared structural features (via a weight-sharing structure content encoder) from modality-specific spectral features (via separate spectral feature encoders), followed by a two-stage reconstruction. This connects to the broader cross-modality knowledge representation literature (joint, collaborative, encoding-decoding paradigms) but is the first to unify these representation ideas within a single generative architecture for cross-spectral image synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling-reconstruction framework provides an interpretable mechanism for bridging the VIS-IR modality gap, which is practically valuable for data augmentation in IR-limited scenarios (aerial monitoring, autonomous driving). The downstream object detection experiments confirm that generated IR images transfer effectively to detection tasks, though the evaluation is limited to small datasets (≤17K pairs) and a single detector (YOLOv9). Open questions include whether the shared-specific decomposition generalizes to other spectral pairs (e.g., NIR, hyperspectral), whether the approach scales to video or higher-resolution imagery, and how the method performs under domain shift beyond the three tested scenarios. The batch size of 1 and reliance on paired training data for the encoders also limit practical deployment.
