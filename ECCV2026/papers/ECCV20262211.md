## Problem

Existing large multimodal models for retrieval are designed for global-level tasks and fail to produce effective region-level representations when a region-of-interest (ROI) is specified. Naive prompting strategies (cropping, visual marks, auxiliary image concatenation) either discard background context needed to interpret the region or introduce irrelevant distractors into the embedding, and no large-scale region-level contrastive dataset or comprehensive multi-task benchmark exists to support training and evaluation.

## Input / Output

Queries and candidates may be images with ROI bounding boxes, text descriptions, or interleaved image-text combinations; the full image is encoded by the context encoder while the ROI crop is processed separately by the Region-Aware Encoder, and both sets of visual tokens are fed to the LLM backbone. The final embedding is extracted from the last hidden state following a special [EMB] token appended to the instruction prompt, and retrieval is performed via cosine similarity between query and candidate embeddings over a candidate pool of up to ~5k items per subtask.

## Method

RegRet introduces a Region-Aware Encoder (RAE) that operates in a layer-wise coordination paradigm with the native vision encoder (CE): at each ViT layer, the RAE applies self-attention to refine regional features and cross-attention (gated by a learnable scalar α initialized to zero) to selectively incorporate background context from the corresponding CE layer, enabling it to capture fine-grained cues from shallow layers while retaining high-level semantics from deeper ones. A critical design choice is using separate projectors for RAE and CE tokens with CE parameters frozen, preventing region-level training from degrading the model's global retrieval capability. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: (1) RAE pretraining via detailed localized captioning under next-token prediction, (2) pure-text contrastive learning with InfoNCE to convert the LLM's generative capability into embedding capability, and (3) regional contrastive learning on a mixture of global and region-level pairs, where a mixed visual prompting strategy feeds only RAE tokens for region-level tasks and concatenates CE+RAE tokens for context-level tasks. The paper also introduces REGMB, a 225k-pair benchmark covering four retrieval tasks (image-to-text, text-to-image, image-to-image, and image-text-to-image) with ROI annotations sourced from six datasets including proprietary social-media collections.

## Results

On the REGMB benchmark, RegRet-8B (fine-tuned) achieves an average Recall of 86.2% across four region-level retrieval tasks, outperforming the strongest LMM baselines (mmE5 and RzenEmbed, both at 67.5%) by 18.7 points and the best CLIP-based method (SigLIP2, 62.9%) by 23.3 points. Even in the zero-shot setting (RegRet-8B-zs, trained only on global-level M-BEIR data), the model reaches 78.8% average, already exceeding all baselines. On public region-level benchmarks (ROxford, DeepFashion2, ILIAS), RegRet-8B achieves 56.1% average versus 47.2% for the best baseline (LamRA). On the global-level M-BEIR benchmark, RegRet-8B† maintains 57.3% average, marginally surpassing LamRA† (57.1%) and significantly outperforming UniIR-CLIPSF (49.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegRet-8B (86.2% avg on REGMB) decisively outperforms all 14 baselines. Among LMM-based methods, it surpasses mmE5 (11B, 67.5%) and RzenEmbed (8B, 67.5%) by 18.7 points, and LamRA (8B, 65.7%) by 20.5 points; notably, even the 3B RegRet variant (79.9%) exceeds the 11B mmE5 by 12.4 points. Among CLIP-based methods, SigLIP2 (0.4B, 62.9%) is the strongest but still trails RegRet-8B by 23.3 points, while FG-CLIP (58.5%), DreamLIP (55.2%), and FineCLIP (51.8%) lag further behind. On public region-level benchmarks (Table 2), RegRet-8B achieves 56.1% average (ROxford mAP 45.0, DeepFashion2 R@1 20.2, ILIAS-I2I mAP@50 86.4, ILIAS-T2I 72.6), beating LamRA (47.2%), mmE5 (34.2%), RzenEmbed (31.1%), MMEMBED (30.4%), and VLM2VEC (23.5%). On the global-level M-BEIR benchmark (Table 3), RegRet-8B† (57.3%) marginally edges out LamRA† (57.1%) and substantially outperforms UniIR-CLIPSF (49.4%), SigLIP (38.8%), and BLIP2 (26.1%), confirming that region-level training does not degrade global retrieval. The zero-shot RegRet-8B-zs (78.8% on REGMB, 48.3% on public benchmarks) already matches or exceeds the fine-tuned LamRA, demonstrating the architectural advantage of the Region-Aware Encoder independent of additional training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of LMM-based multimodal retrieval (LamRA, VLM2Vec, MME5, RzenEmbed) and region-level representation learning (RegionCLIP, FGCLIP, FineCLIP, DAM). It departs from prior LMM retrieval work, which assumes global-level inputs, by introducing a Region-Aware Encoder that uses layer-wise cross-attention with the native vision encoder to adaptively balance ROI-specific features against background context. It also departs from prior region-level methods that rely on ROIAlign cropping or auxiliary-image concatenation, which the paper shows to be suboptimal for retrieval due to either lost context or introduced distractors. The REGMB benchmark extends the limited region-level evaluation landscape (ROxford, DeepFashion2, ILIAS) from narrow I2I settings to a comprehensive four-task multimodal retrieval suite supporting both training and evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Region-level retrieval is a practical bottleneck in e-commerce search, medical imaging, and RAG systems where users specify ROIs, yet no prior work provided both a dedicated architecture and a large-scale training/evaluation benchmark for this setting. The finding that decoupling RAE and CE with separate projectors is critical (without it, M-BEIR performance collapses from 57.3 to 27.2) offers a generalizable design principle for multi-granularity LMMs. Open questions include whether the inherent tension between local and global representation (Task 4 vs. Tasks 1–3) can be further resolved, and how RegRet extends to visual document retrieval and production RAG pipelines as the authors note.
