## Problem

State-of-the-art deep spiking neural networks depend on dynamic batch normalization variants (e.g., TEBN, TAB) to stabilize training, but these introduce runtime multiplications and statistics tracking that undermine the accumulation-oriented hardware efficiency that motivates SNN deployment. Simply removing normalization causes catastrophic firing-rate decay, collapsing accuracy in deep architectures. The paper identifies this signal-propagation barrier and asks whether intrinsic, hardware-fusible stabilization can replace dynamic BN entirely.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either static RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 ImageNet) or asynchronous event streams (DVS-Gesture, CIFAR10-DVS), which are encoded into binary spike trains over T timesteps (T=4–16 for static images, T=16 for DVS-Gesture). The network outputs a single class label per input. The architecture is modality-agnostic: the same IS-SNN stabilization mechanism applies to VGG, SEW-ResNet, 7B-Net, VGGSNN, and Spikformer backbones without modification.

## Method

IS-SNN removes all activation-normalization layers by enforcing signal homeostasis through two mechanisms: (1) topology-aware weight standardization, where each layer's scaling factor γ_ℓ = 1/σ_ℓ is analytically derived from SNN-specific variance propagation (incorporating the empirically estimated neuron output variance σ_g and, for residual networks, the cumulative variance growth across blocks), and (2) a modified residual connection x_{ℓ+1} = α·SN(f(x_ℓ)) + x_ℓ with α=0.5 to cap variance growth. During training, weights are dynamically standardized each step; after training, the standardization is folded into static synaptic weights offline, so the deployed network is structurally identical to a raw BN-free SNN requiring only adders and comparators at inference. The neuron output variance σ_g is estimated once by stimulating each LIF configuration with N(0,1) pre-activations, making the method applicable to any neuron model. This decoupling eliminates all runtime normalization multiplications while maintaining firing rates in a stable range across depths up to ResNet-152.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-34, IS-SNN achieves 66.61% top-1 accuracy at 128 epochs and 68.05% at 400 epochs, compared to only 32.58% for the naive BN-free baseline and 68.00% for TET. On CIFAR-100, IS-SNN reaches 76.98% on ResNet-152 (vs. 17.10% without BN) and 76.47% on ResNet-19 (vs. 69.98% without BN). On the neuromorphic DVS-Gesture dataset, IS-SNN attains 96.88% with 7B-Net, approaching the 97.92% BN-equipped baseline while the BN-free variant drops to 77.08%. With strong augmentation (Mixup + CutMix), IS-SNN achieves 96.02% on CIFAR-10 and 79.97% on CIFAR-100 with ResNet-19, surpassing TEBN and TAB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IS-SNN outperforms all dynamic BN variants on CIFAR-100: it beats TEBN (76.47% vs. 76.41%), TET (76.47% vs. 74.72%), tdBN (76.47% vs. 71.12%), and Spike-Norm (76.47% vs. 70.90%) on ResNet-19. On ImageNet, IS-SNN at 400 epochs (68.05%) slightly trails TEBN (68.28%) but matches TET (68.00%), while at 128 epochs it surpasses tdBN (66.61% vs. 63.72%) and Spike-Norm (66.61% vs. 65.47%). On CIFAR-10, IS-SNN (94.51%) edges out TET (94.50%) and tdBN (93.16%), and substantially exceeds BNTT (90.5%) and Spike-Norm (91.55%). On DVS-Gesture, IS-SNN (96.88%) surpasses the prior BN-free method [31] (95.49%) but remains 1.04% below the BN-equipped 7B-Net (97.92%). With strong augmentation, IS-SNN on ResNet-19* beats TAB (96.02% vs. 96.09% on CIFAR-10, 79.97% vs. 76.82% on CIFAR-100) and TEBN (96.02% vs. 95.60%, 79.97% vs. 78.76%), and when applied to Spikformer it improves CIFAR-100 accuracy from 77.86% to 79.03%. The naive BN-free baseline collapses on deep architectures (17.10% on ResNet-152/CIFAR-100, 32.58% on ResNet-34/ImageNet), highlighting the critical role of IS-SNN's topology-aware stabilization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the normalization-free ANN design principles of Brock et al. (2021) — signal propagation analysis, scaled weight standardization, and residual branch scaling — to the spiking domain, where discrete, temporally integrated activations make direct transfer nontrivial. It departs from prior BN-free SNN attempts (Qiao et al. 2023, OTTT) by deriving the per-layer scaling factor γ_ℓ from SNN-specific variance propagation (empirical neuron output variance σ_g and topology-dependent residual accumulation) rather than applying generic weight standardization. The modified residual connection with a fixed α=0.5 scaling is a deliberate departure from standard skip connections, motivated by the need to control variance growth across residual blocks under spiking dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IS-SNN resolves a practical tension in neuromorphic computing: deep SNNs previously required dynamic BN variants (TEBN, TAB) that reintroduce runtime multiplications, undermining the accumulation-oriented hardware efficiency that motivates SNN deployment. By folding all standardization into static weights offline, the architecture enables deep SNNs (ResNet-152, ImageNet) with no inference-time normalization overhead and a 96.4% FPGA LUT reduction, making accurate neuromorphic deployment more practical for edge applications. Key limitations include the assumption of fixed weights (incompatible with online learning without additional hardware), the complex and non-monotonic interaction with max-pooling, and the absence of full end-to-end energy evaluation on dedicated neuromorphic hardware. Open questions include extending the framework to sequence-centric tasks, unsupervised learning, and hardware-friendly online training paradigms.
