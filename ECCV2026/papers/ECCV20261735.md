## Problem

Existing error slice discovery methods model slices as clusters in representation space or combinations of predefined atomic tags, formulations that are effective for image-level classification but insufficient for instance-level tasks like object detection and segmentation, where failures are driven by contextual, relational, and spatially grounded visual patterns. Additionally, no principled benchmark exists for evaluating instance-level slice discovery, with prior benchmarks targeting only image-level classification and artificial spurious correlations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261735/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes evaluation units defined as individual object instances (correct predictions, false negatives, false positives, localization/segmentation discrepancy regions, or whole images) along with their bounding boxes or masks, model predictions, ground-truth annotations, and a concise task description. The VLM receives each hypothesis as natural-language text paired with grounding signals (bounding box coordinates or point locations) overlaid on the image, and the matching score is extracted from token-level logits over 'yes'/'no' tokens rather than a dedicated classification head. Output consists of a ranked set of grounded natural-language hypotheses, per-instance continuous matching scores in [0,1], and a scalar trend statistic per hypothesis that determines whether it constitutes a verified systematic error slice.

## Method

GH-ESD is a generate-and-verify framework with three stages. First, a grounded hypothesis space is constructed from two complementary sources: knowledge-driven hypotheses generated by an LLM (Gemini-2.5-Pro) from a task description, and data-driven hypotheses derived from a bottom-up pipeline where a VLM produces region-grounded captions, an LLM infers recurring attribute-value pairs, and these are synthesized into well-formed natural-language conditions. Second, each hypothesis is matched to every evaluation unit by prompting a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) with the hypothesis text and spatial grounding signals, computing a continuous score as the softmax over 'yes'/'no' token logits. Third, a statistical verification module tests whether model error increases monotonically with hypothesis intensity: units are restricted to a high-confidence region, sorted by matching score, and a sliding-window local linear regression computes the slope between score and error; a hypothesis is retained only if the maximum window slope exceeds a threshold, filtering out VLM hallucinations and spurious matches. This continuous-intensity formulation replaces the binary partitioning used by prior tag-based methods, and the monotonic trend test replaces fixed error-rate thresholding, making the approach robust to noisy grounding and open-ended hypothesis spaces.

## Results

On the GESD instance-level benchmark, GH-ESD achieves Precision@10 of 0.73, outperforming the strongest baseline HiBug2 (0.63) by 0.10 absolute, and leading across all k values (P@5: 0.78, P@20: 0.66). On image-level bias benchmarks, GH-ESD matches the best baselines (ViG-FACTS, FACTS) at 1.00 P@10 on Waterbirds and CelebA, and achieves the best NICO++ scores at 75% and 95% correlation strengths (0.64, 0.69). For model repair, slice-aware fine-tuning with GH-ESD improves bicycle mAP from 28.58 to 33.59 and mAR from 41.14 to 47.09 over standard fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GH-ESD outperforms all four instance-level baselines on GESD. The closest competitor, HiBug2 with Gemini-2.5-Pro, achieves 0.77 P@5 but drops to 0.63 P@10, while GH-ESD maintains 0.73 P@10 and 0.66 P@20, indicating that the gap widens as more samples are retrieved. The tag-based baselines HiBug* (0.31 P@10) and FACTS* (0.28 P@10) are substantially weaker, confirming that atomic tag combinations struggle with relational and contextual failure patterns. On image-level bias benchmarks (Table 2), GH-ESD ties ViG-FACTS and FACTS at 1.00 P@10 on Waterbirds and CelebA, and leads on NICO++ at 75% (0.64 vs. 0.60) and 95% (0.69 vs. 0.65) correlation strengths, though ViG-FACTS edges ahead at 90% (0.67 vs. 0.66). For model repair (Table 4), GH-ESD achieves the best Worst Group Accuracy on both Waterbirds (0.904) and CelebA (0.882), surpassing LADDER (0.896/0.878), Domino (0.898/0.827), and FACTS (0.889/0.600).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the slice-discovery literature from image-level classification (Domino, FACTS, LADDER) and early instance-level tagging (HiBug, HiBug2, VISLIX) to a grounded, hypothesis-driven formulation for detection and segmentation. It departs from the dominant 'tag-then-slice' paradigm by replacing discrete attribute combinations with continuous, natural-language hypotheses matched via VLM grounding, and from embedding-cluster methods by using statistical monotonic trend verification rather than fixed error-rate thresholds. The introduction of GESD as the first expert-annotated, spatially grounded instance-level slice benchmark fills a gap left by image-level benchmarks like Waterbirds and NICO++.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GESD provides the community with a principled evaluation testbed for instance-level slice discovery, a setting where prior benchmarks were absent. The demonstrated model-repair gains (e.g., +5 mAP on bicycle detection via GroupDRO reweighting) show that discovered slices are actionable, not merely descriptive. Key open questions remain: the hypothesis space is inherently open-ended and may miss underrepresented failure modes, and the authors acknowledge that scalable discovery will require tighter human-in-the-loop integration. The framework's reliance on a specific VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) for grounding also raises questions about robustness across model families.
