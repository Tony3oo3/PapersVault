## Problem

In cross-view localization between ground and Bird's Eye View images, yaw estimation is tightly coupled to translation and relies on height-dependent projection assumptions that break down under large yaw ambiguity (±180°) and position uncertainty (±20 m). Existing methods assume yaw is approximately known or only slightly perturbed, leaving a gap for robust sub-degree yaw estimation when both position and orientation are unknown.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261409/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an undistorted, gravity-aligned ground-level image and a top-down BEV image along with camera intrinsics; a 4th heading-embedding channel is appended to the ground image before encoding. The 2D camera position is not given but exhaustively searched over a noise range. Output is a 3D discrete score tensor S(θ, (x,y)) over all candidate yaw angles and 2D positions, from which the estimated yaw is read as the argmax bin; no ground-height or known-position assumption is required.

## Method

LAYS formulates yaw estimation as a line-alignment problem: ground pixel features are aggregated column-wise via learned softmax weighting into column features, each encoding a unique relative yaw independent of ground height. BEV pixels are matched to the most similar ground column using confidence-weighted cosine similarity (probabilistic sampling at training, hard argmax at inference). For every candidate 2D pose and matched BEV pixel, the estimated yaw is computed as the absolute angle to the pixel minus the column's relative yaw, and the match score is accumulated into a 3D voting tensor over (yaw, x, y). The key geometric property (Proposition 1) is radial invariance: all candidate positions collinear with a matched BEV pixel vote for the identical yaw, so correct correspondences concentrate into a sharp peak along the radial line while incorrect matches disperse. Training uses a softmax cross-entropy loss over the multi-resolution score tensor, with a pose-plausibility prior added to the matching score.

## Results

Under the most challenging setting of ±180° unknown yaw with ±20 m location noise, LAYS achieves 34.81% sub-degree (<1°) yaw accuracy on MGL, compared to 6.55% for the next-best baseline (CCVPE) and <1% for BoostAcc and G2S. On KITTI (same-area), LAYS reaches 52.98% <1° accuracy versus 8.96% for CCVPE and 1.62% for FG2. On the Ford highway dataset (±45° yaw noise), LAYS attains 67.05% <1° accuracy, a 25.69-point improvement over CCVPE (41.36%). On VIGOR with omnidirectional cameras, LAYS leads at <1° and <2° thresholds (34.16% and 50.27% same-area) while FG2's two-stage variant slightly edges it at the looser <4° threshold (62.11% vs 59.32%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAYS decisively outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and noise level. Against CCVPE [35], the strongest baseline under unknown yaw, LAYS improves <1° accuracy from 6.55% to 34.81% on MGL (5.3×) and from 8.96% to 52.98% on KITTI (5.9×). BoostAcc [24] and G2S [22] collapse to near-zero accuracy (<1%) under ±180° yaw noise, confirming that their joint 3-DoF optimization frameworks cannot handle full yaw ambiguity. FG2 [34] is the closest competitor, reaching 3.13% <1° on MGL and 19.05% on VIGOR same-area, but LAYS still leads by 10–15 points at the strictest thresholds. The one exception is VIGOR same-area at the <4° threshold, where FG2's two-stage variant (62.11%) marginally exceeds LAYS (59.32%), attributed to LAYS's voting concentrating scores in narrow angular peaks optimized for sub-degree precision rather than coarse angular bins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in cross-view localization where yaw is jointly optimized with 2D translation via point-to-point correspondences and height-dependent projections (OrienterNet, FG2, CCVPE, BoostAcc, G2S). Rather than building on any single prior method, LAYS repurposes the geometric voting/consensus idea from PnP-style solvers but applies it to a fundamentally different primitive: column-to-radial-line alignment instead of pixel-to-pixel matching. The key conceptual departure is Proposition 1's radial invariance, which shows that a single ground column matched to a BEV pixel induces the same yaw for all camera positions along the radial direction, thereby eliminating the ground-height and known-location assumptions that prior projection-based methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By isolating yaw as a standalone subproblem solvable under ±20 m location noise and ±180° yaw ambiguity, LAYS provides a reusable yaw prior that demonstrably improves downstream 3-DoF localization (e.g., reducing FG2's median error from 12.26 m to 1.48 m on KITTI). The main limitation is that the radial voting formulation cannot localize the camera position itself—scores are distributed along lines rather than concentrated at a point—so the method must be paired with a separate localization stage. Open questions include whether the column-to-line formulation extends to non-gravity-aligned cameras, whether it handles occluded or highly repetitive urban scenes, and whether the 3D voting tensor can be made more efficient for real-time deployment.
