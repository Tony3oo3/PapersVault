## Problem

Existing Category-Agnostic Pose Estimation (CAPE) methods rely on direct coordinate regression or local heatmap matching, which become unreliable under severe occlusion because invisible keypoints cannot be recovered from local visual evidence alone. The paper identifies that occlusion in CAPE is a coupled challenge of correspondence ambiguity and structure-aware reasoning for novel categories, requiring a formulation that can progressively refine pose hypotheses while preserving semantic and topological consistency—something deterministic one-shot regression cannot provide.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262550/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image (resized to 256×256), a skeleton graph defining keypoint topology, and per-keypoint text descriptions encoded by a frozen gte-base-v1.5 text encoder as the support cue (no support image is used). It outputs 2D keypoint coordinates in normalized [0,1]² space, decoded to pixel coordinates by scaling with image dimensions. The text-based support paradigm follows CapeX, meaning the 1-shot setting uses a single set of keypoint descriptions rather than multiple annotated examples.

## Method

FlowCape reformulates CAPE as conditional flow matching on a pose manifold, transporting a heatmap-guided initial state to the target pose via a learned velocity field. A Heatmap-Guided Initialization module computes per-keypoint similarity maps between query image features (from Swin-V2) and text embeddings, converts them to coordinates via soft-argmax, and blends with a centered anchor using confidence-adaptive weighting to produce a meaningful x₀. A shared Riemannian Pose Head with a Graph Flow Encoder predicts the conditional velocity by alternating self-attention over keypoint tokens, cross-attention to query image memory, graph smoothing over the skeleton, and AdaLN modulation by global text context. Training uses a hybrid objective combining a skeleton-aware Laplacian flow-matching loss, Euclidean flow loss, initialization L1 loss, t=0 velocity regularization, and unrolled ODE consistency loss. At inference, the pose is refined by explicit Euler ODE rollout over K steps, with an additional heatmap-potential gradient injected at each step to anchor the trajectory.

## Results

On the proposed Occ80 occlusion benchmark, FlowCape (SwinV2-Small) achieves 89.12% PCK@0.2, surpassing the strongest baseline CapeX by +1.05% and GraphCape by +5.75%. With SwinV2-Tiny, FlowCape reaches 84.51% PCK@0.2, outperforming CapeX (83.72%) by +0.79%. On the standard MP-100 benchmark (1-shot), FlowCape-S attains 91.65% average PCK@0.2, marginally exceeding CapeX-S (91.50%), while FlowCape-T (88.40%) is slightly below CapeX-T (88.81%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Occ80, FlowCape achieves the best PCK@0.2 across all three category splits with both Tiny and Small backbones, beating CapeX (the strongest text-based CAPE method) by +0.79% (Tiny) and +1.05% (Small), and outperforming graph-based methods GraphCape and GenCape by 1.6–7.2% depending on backbone scale. On MP-100 (1-shot), FlowCape-S (91.65%) narrowly edges out CapeX-S (91.50%) by +0.15%, while FlowCape-T (88.40%) trails CapeX-T (88.81%) by 0.41%, indicating the flow-matching advantage is more pronounced under occlusion stress than in the standard setting. Among image-based baselines on MP-100, FlowCape-S surpasses EdgeCape (89.42%), GenCape (88.09%), SCAPE (87.55%), SDPNet (87.36%), GraphCape (87.23%), MetaPoint (86.17%), CapeFormer (85.31%), ESCAPE (82.74%), and POMNet (79.70%). The largest relative gains appear on Occ80 Split-3 (the most occlusion-heavy partition), where FlowCape-S leads CapeX-S by +0.44% and GraphCape-S by +5.40%, confirming that the flow-matching formulation specifically benefits from structural reasoning when local visual evidence is degraded.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowCape extends the CAPE lineage from POMNet through CapeFormer, GraphCape, and CapeX by replacing the standard discriminative regression or heatmap-matching paradigm with a conditional flow-matching formulation that models pose prediction as structured transport on a skeleton-aware Riemannian manifold. It inherits the text-based support cue from CapeX and the graph-structured keypoint representation from GraphCape, but departs from both by introducing a heatmap-guided initialization that anchors the ODE trajectory near a plausible pose, and by supervising the velocity field with a hybrid skeleton-Laplacian loss rather than plain Euclidean regression. The Riemannian Flow Matching framework of Chen & Lipman provides the geometric backbone, adapted here to a category-agnostic setting where the underlying manifold depends on the novel category's skeleton topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of Occ80 fills a genuine evaluation gap: prior CAPE benchmarks like MP-100 do not explicitly stress-test severe occlusion, yet occlusion is arguably the defining failure mode for category-agnostic estimation. The flow-matching formulation offers a principled alternative to diffusion-based pose models (e.g., DiffPose) with fewer integration steps, making it more practical for real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the Riemannian structure provides meaningful gains over a well-regularized Euclidean flow, whether the approach extends naturally to 3D or multi-view settings, and whether the modest 0.5–1% PCK margins over CapeX will widen on more extreme occlusion or larger category gaps.
