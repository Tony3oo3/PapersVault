## Problem

Autoregressive VLA models are severely limited by sequential token-by-token decoding, which is impractical for high-frequency dexterous manipulation. While Jacobi decoding was proposed to predict multiple tokens in parallel per iteration, it delivers only a marginal 1.28× speedup because AR-trained VLAs cannot reliably predict correct tokens when preceding tokens are wrong. This paper addresses both the limited parallel prediction capability and the redundant iterations caused by strict convergence conditions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (from depth cameras in real-world deployment or standard frames in simulation) paired with a natural-language instruction, and outputs a sequence of discrete action tokens decoded via parallel Jacobi iterations rather than sequential AR steps. Action chunking (e.g., 3 or 5 tokens per chunk) is integrated to produce temporally coherent action sequences, and the decoding process terminates early once all token confidences exceed a threshold rather than requiring exact fixed-point convergence.

## Method

CEED-VLA introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, consistency distillation trains a student model (initialized from the teacher VLA) to map any intermediate Jacobi trajectory state directly to the final fixed point, using a KL-divergence consistency loss between teacher and student distributions at randomly sampled intermediate iterations, combined with an auxiliary AR loss to preserve the teacher's action distribution. Second, adaptive early-exit decoding replaces the strict convergence criterion (Y^(k)=Y^(k-1)) with a confidence threshold σ on the minimum token probability, halting iteration as soon as all tokens are sufficiently confident. At inference, prefix token KV states are cached across iterations to avoid redundant computation. The training pipeline requires only ~12 hours of additional distillation on pre-collected Jacobi trajectories (60K samples suffice), making it a lightweight decoder-side acceleration that is architecture-agnostic and composable with other efficiency techniques.

## Results

CEED-VLA achieves up to 4.1× inference speedup (225.2 vs. 54.4 tokens/s) and 4.3× execution frequency (25.62 vs. 5.95 Hz) on OpenVLA/LIBERO-Long while maintaining a 62.3% success rate (vs. 60.4% for AR baseline). On LLaVA-VLA/CALVIN, it reaches 79.0 tokens/s (2.0×) and 7.26 Hz (3.3×) with an average task length of 3.68 (vs. 3.70 for AR). On π₀-FAST and UniVLA (LIBERO), CEED-VLA delivers 3.9× and 4.0× speedups respectively with negligible success-rate drops (≤0.5%). In real-world dual-arm experiments, CEED-VLA raises average success rate from 33.25% (LLaVA-VLA) to 77.50% at 13.0 Hz (3.9× frequency).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CEED-VLA outperforms all compared acceleration methods on both speed and frequency. Against PD-VLA (the closest parallel-decoding baseline), CEED-VLA achieves 2.0× vs. 1.3× speedup on CALVIN and 4.1× vs. 1.6× on LIBERO, while matching or slightly exceeding PD-VLA's success rate (62.3% vs. 62.4% on LIBERO). VLM-specific acceleration methods FastV and SparseVLM actually degrade performance (0.7× and 0.8× speed) when applied to VLA models, confirming that generic VLM tricks do not transfer well. On π₀-FAST (LIBERO), CEED-VLA matches the base model's 85.5% average success rate while delivering 3.9× speed (420.6 vs. 107.5 tokens/s). On UniVLA, CEED-VLA achieves 4.0× speedup (200.3 vs. 50.6 tokens/s) with only a 0.4% average SR drop (94.4% vs. 94.8%). In real-world experiments, CEED-VLA more than doubles the success rate of LLaVA-VLA (77.50% vs. 33.25%) and substantially outperforms PD-VLA (77.50% vs. 57.50%), particularly on dexterous tasks (80% vs. 45% for pour water; 75% vs. 35% for fold towel).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PD-VLA's application of Jacobi (parallel) decoding to VLA models by identifying why vanilla Jacobi decoding underperforms (the model, trained strictly autoregressively, cannot handle incorrect preceding tokens) and addressing it through consistency distillation, a technique borrowed from the consistency-model literature in diffusion. The adaptive early-exit decoding further departs from prior work by relaxing the strict fixed-point convergence condition with a confidence threshold. Unlike architecture-level accelerations (layer skipping, quantization, token caching), CEED-VLA operates purely at the decoder level and is explicitly positioned as composable with those complementary techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AR-based VLAs (OpenVLA, π₀-FAST, UniVLA) remain the dominant paradigm for instruction-following and unified understanding-generation in robotics, yet their sequential decoding has been a practical barrier to high-frequency dexterous control. The demonstrated 4× speedup across four distinct base models and tokenization schemes, combined with real-world evidence that faster inference directly improves success rates on dexterous tasks (e.g., 15%→80% on water pouring), makes AR VLAs more deployable without sacrificing their training-efficiency and instruction-following advantages. Open questions include whether the confidence threshold requires per-task calibration at scale, how the method behaves on very long action sequences beyond the 3–5 token chunks tested here, and whether consistency distillation can be made fully training-free or amortized across embodiments.
