## Problem

Connector-based video unified models require fine-tuning a large diffusion generator (e.g., Wan2.1-14B) within the unified training loop to align understanding-derived semantics with generation, which is computationally prohibitive and limits achievable visual fidelity. Additionally, existing video generation benchmarks focus on perceptual quality but lack systematic evaluation of reasoning-driven generation—whether a model can translate inferred intent into coherent video content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261943/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text prompt as input, which is processed by a VLM understanding block into world-knowledge-grounded semantic embeddings. These embeddings are injected via a connector into the diffusion generator's conditioning space. Output is a 480p video of 81 frames (5 seconds at 16 FPS) or a single image, generated with 50 sampling steps and CFG scale 5. The small generator receives VLM-aligned embeddings as conditioning while the large generator receives direct textual embeddings, enabling asymmetric semantic-to-detail handoff.

## Method

Lumos-Nexus uses a two-stage design exploiting a homogeneous latent space shared between a small (Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B) and large (Wan2.1-T2V-14B) diffusion generator. During training, only the small generator and connector are fine-tuned within the Omni-Video unified framework to absorb reasoning-driven semantic priors from the understanding block. At inference, Unified Progressive Frequency Bridging (UPFB) progressively transfers generative responsibility from the small to the large generator without additional training: a cosine temporal gating schedule controls the handoff sharpness, a time-varying Gaussian low-pass filter decomposes velocity fields into low-frequency (global layout) and high-frequency (texture) components, asymmetric fusion weights the small generator's low-frequency output for semantic coherence while the large generator dominates high-frequency detail, and RMS alignment normalizes velocity magnitudes to prevent energy mismatch. This training-free bridging is seamlessly insertable into existing connector-based unified model inference pipelines.

## Results

On VBench-T2V, Lumos-Nexus achieves the highest overall score of 84.12, surpassing the strongest video generation baseline CausVid (83.88) and the baseline unified model Omni-Video (83.82), with notable gains in semantic alignment (80.52 vs. 79.10 for Omni-Video) and object class accuracy (96.20 vs. 94.56). On the newly introduced VR-Bench, Lumos-Nexus scores 79.28 overall among Wan2.1-level models, outperforming Omni-Video (72.78) and Wan2.1-14B (78.23), with the strongest results in High-Level Commonsense Reasoning (77.57) and Embodied Physical Reasoning (81.54). When upgraded with Wan2.2-A14B as the large generator (Lumos-Nexus*), the overall VR-Bench score rises to 81.90, exceeding Wan2.2-A14B alone (80.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On VBench-T2V, Lumos-Nexus (84.12) outperforms all video generation baselines including CausVid (83.88), Wan2.1-14B (83.69), and HunyuanVideo (83.43), as well as all video unified model baselines including Omni-Video (83.82), UniVideo (82.58), and Show-o2 (81.34). The most notable gain is over Omni-Video, the direct baseline: semantic alignment improves from 79.10 to 80.52, object class from 94.56 to 96.20, and scene consistency from 46.80 to 52.47, confirming that UPFB alleviates the 'short-board effect' where the small generator limits the understanding block's semantic guidance. On VR-Bench, Lumos-Nexus (79.28) beats Omni-Video (72.78) by 6.5 points and Wan2.1-14B (78.23) by 1.05 points, with the largest margin in Embodied Physical Reasoning (81.54 vs. 73.33 for Omni-Video). The upgraded variant Lumos-Nexus* (81.90) surpasses Wan2.2-A14B (80.98) by nearly 1 point, demonstrating that the framework generalizes across generator scales. However, both closed-source models—Veo 3.1 (93.95) and Kling 2.6 (91.13)—still substantially outperform all open-source methods on VR-Bench, indicating a significant gap in reasoning-driven video generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Omni-Video, a connector-based video unified model that aligns a VLM understanding block with a diffusion generator via a learned connector. The key departure is from the standard practice of fine-tuning the full-scale diffusion generator (e.g., Wan2.1-14B) inside the unified training loop, which is computationally prohibitive. Instead, Lumos-Nexus trains only a lightweight generator (Wan2.1-1.3B) in the unified loop and introduces a training-free inference-time strategy (UPFB) that progressively bridges to a larger generator (Wan2.1-14B) operating in the same homogeneous latent space, using temporal gating and frequency-domain decomposition to combine their complementary strengths. The approach is conceptually related to model distillation and progressive refinement, but applied specifically to the connector-based unified video generation setting where the small generator's role is to absorb VLM-derived semantic priors rather than to approximate the large generator's output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in decoupling the training cost of semantic alignment from the inference cost of high-fidelity synthesis, making it feasible to deploy large video generators within unified model pipelines without prohibitive fine-tuning. The introduction of VR-Bench addresses a genuine gap: prior video generation benchmarks (VBench, etc.) measure perceptual quality but not whether the model correctly executes inferred reasoning, which is the core value proposition of unified models. However, the approach is constrained by the homogeneous latent space requirement (currently satisfied only within the Wan2.x family), limiting immediate generalizability to arbitrary generator pairs. Open questions include whether UPFB can be adapted to non-homogeneous latent spaces, whether the frequency-bridging strategy transfers to other unified model architectures (e.g., joint-attention designs), and whether the gains persist at higher resolutions or longer video durations.
