## Problem

Real-world pathology clinical queries are inherently interleaved (e.g., multiple images plus diagnostic text), but existing dual-encoder retrieval models lack any mechanism to deeply fuse such composed inputs, and directly applying generative MLLMs exposes both a Task Mismatch (latent space optimized for generation, not discriminative metric learning) and a Domain Mismatch (no pathology morphological priors). The paper formalizes Pathology Composed Retrieval (PCR) and introduces the first benchmark to evaluate this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262465/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts arbitrary interleaved sequences of pathology images (processed at native resolution as multiples of 14×14 patches, not a fixed 336×336), free-form text, and pathology videos (via frame-rate sampling with interleaved multi-RoPE). It outputs a single dense embedding vector extracted as the last hidden state immediately preceding a special <emb> token, using an Explicit One-word Limitation (EOL) prompt that forces the causal LLM to compress all preceding multi-modal context into one token. Retrieval is then performed via cosine similarity against a candidate set, with Recall@K as the primary metric.

## Method

HOMIE is a model-agnostic, two-stage adaptation framework that transforms any generative MLLM into a pathology retrieval expert. Stage 1 (Task Mismatch resolution) trains LoRA adapters on text-only contrastive data (NLI, MedNLI, pathology-filtered MedMCQA) to reshape the LLM's latent space from generative to discriminative metric learning before any visual input is seen. Stage 2 (Domain Mismatch resolution) fine-tunes the full MLLM on curated pathology image-text pairs with three pathology-specific adaptations: (i) stain augmentation and normalization to learn stain-invariant morphology, (ii) native-resolution processing via SigLIP-2 ViT with 2D-RoPE to preserve fine-grained cellular detail, and (iii) a progressive knowledge curriculum that first builds morphological priors on PathGen-1.6M, then associates them with diagnostic knowledge on PathCap and bootstrap-filtered Quilt-1M. The architecture pairs a SigLIP-2 ViT vision encoder, an MLP projector, and an LLM backbone, trained with InfoNCE contrastive loss and LoRA (r=64/α=128 in Stage 1, r=128/α=256 in Stage 2) on 8×H100 GPUs.

## Results

On the novel Pathology Composed Retrieval (PCR) benchmark, HOMIE (Qwen3-VL-8B) achieves 79.8% Recall@1 on Multi-Image→Text retrieval (Bookset), 52.7% on Image-Text→Image, 35.8% on Image-Text→Text (Quilt-VQA), 57.5% on Quilt-VQA-Red, and 30.7% on Video→Text. The lightweight 2B variant reaches 75.2% R@1 on the same Multi-Image→Text task, surpassing the best dual-encoder (MUSK, 44.3%) by over 30 points and the best pathology MLLM (PathoR1-7B, 14.7%) by over 60 points. On traditional simple retrieval, HOMIE (8B) also sets a new SOTA with 34.4/31.5% R@1 (i2t/t2i) on Bookset, outperforming the best dual-encoder Patho-CLIP (26.7/27.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOMIE decisively outperforms all 16 baselines on the composed retrieval benchmark. Among dual-encoders, MUSK (43.0–47.9% R@1) and Patho-CLIP-L (44.3% R@1) are the strongest, yet both are surpassed by HOMIE-2B by 28–35 points; their architectural inability to fuse interleaved inputs causes them to degenerate into shallow visual matching. Among general MLLMs, even Qwen2.5-VL-7B (3.5% R@1) and the retrieval-adapted LamRA-7B (5.8% R@1) fail catastrophically, confirming the Domain Mismatch. Pathology-specific MLLMs fare slightly better—PathoR1-7B reaches 14.7% R@1—but still trail HOMIE-2B by over 60 points, isolating the Task Mismatch (generative latent space vs. discriminative metric space). On simple retrieval (Table 3), HOMIE-8B (34.4/31.5% R@1 i2t/t2i on Bookset) also beats the best dual-encoder Patho-CLIP (26.7/27.6%) and all MLLM baselines, demonstrating that the two-stage adaptation does not sacrifice traditional retrieval capability. The framework's model-agnostic nature is confirmed by Table 4: applying HOMIE to PathoR1-7B yields 78.5% R@1 (vs. 14.7% zero-shot), showing that domain priors synergize with the retrieval adaptation rather than being redundant.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOMIE builds on two key prior ideas: the EOL prompting strategy for extracting dense representations from causal LLMs (Jiang et al., 2024) and the BLIP-style bootstrap filtering for data quality (Li et al., 2022). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant dual-encoder paradigm in pathology (CONCH, MUSK, PathoCLIP) by replacing separate image/text encoders with a unified MLLM backbone, and from general-domain retrieval MLLMs (E5-V, LamRA, GME) by introducing a pathology-specific two-stage curriculum. The framework is explicitly model-agnostic, positioning itself as an adaptation layer over any generative MLLM rather than a new architecture, which distinguishes it from both the CLIP lineage and the VLM2Vec/GME prompt-tuning approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PCR Benchmark is the first to systematically evaluate interleaved multi-modal queries in pathology, filling a clear evaluation gap that prevents fair comparison of composed retrieval capabilities. The finding that a 2B adapted model outperforms 7B generative pathology MLLMs by 60+ points on composed retrieval challenges the assumption that domain-specific generative knowledge transfers to discriminative retrieval, and provides a practical recipe for converting existing MLLMs into retrieval tools without retraining from scratch. Open questions include whether the two-stage curriculum generalizes to other medical imaging domains, how performance scales with larger candidate sets (current benchmark is ~600–700 samples per task), and whether the framework extends to truly novel modalities like spatial transcriptomics or genomics as the authors envision.
