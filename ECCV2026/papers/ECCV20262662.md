## Problem

Existing image forgery detection methods either exploit low-level, semantics-agnostic artifacts (JPEG compression, noise residuals, edge discontinuities) or rely on MLLMs with high-level semantic knowledge, but these two information streams are highly heterogeneous in paradigm and reasoning, making it difficult to unify them or model their cross-level interactions. Current MLLM-based IFD approaches lack structured forensic procedures and explicit tool-aware reasoning, limiting their fine-grained manipulation analysis and interpretability in sensitive scenarios.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a single RGB image and operates in a multi-turn interactive loop within a sandboxed Python environment, where each turn it emits either executable Python code (for cropping/enhancement) or structured tool calls (for the 12 low-level forensic operators), receives text and visualization outputs fed back into its context, and iterates until producing a final prediction. The output is a three-class verdict (authentic, synthetic, tampered) wrapped in a structured tag, accompanied by forged-region localization (naming the manipulated object for tampered cases) and a step-by-step forensic reasoning chain that mirrors a human investigator's workflow.

## Method

ForenAgent equips a Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone with a 12-tool low-level forensic toolbox (FFT/DWT frequency analysis, SRM/PRNU noise residuals, Sobel/Laplacian edge detection, JPEG ghost, median-filter traces, local correlation) plus basic image processing (cropping, enhancement), and trains it via a two-stage pipeline: a Cold-Start stage that distills ~200k multi-turn agent-interaction trajectories from GPT-4.1 into supervised fine-tuning data, followed by Reinforcement Fine-Tuning using GRPO with a composite reward that decomposes tool usage into four verifiable process components—global forensic priority (apply low-level tools before local cropping), tool logic (syntactically correct invocations), crop sensitivity (class-weighted crop usage), and reasoning coherence (locate-then-investigate chaining). This reward structure encodes a human-inspired dynamic reasoning loop of global perception → local focusing → iterative probing → holistic adjudication, enabling the agent to develop emergent reflective behaviors such as self-correcting a mislocalized crop without explicit supervision on the correction.

## Results

On FABench (100k images, 3-class: authentic/synthetic/tampered), ForenAgent achieves 89.5% overall accuracy and 89.4% F1, outperforming the strongest trained detector Effort (81.2% Acc, 81.1% F1) by over 8 points and the best zero-shot MLLM GPT-4.1 (51.4% Acc, 46.9% F1) by more than 38 points. On the external SIDA-Test benchmark, ForenAgent reaches 83.1% accuracy and 82.9% F1, surpassing the previous best Effort (78.5% Acc, 78.4% F1) by ~4.6 points. Per-class, ForenAgent achieves F1 scores of 91.4 (authentic), 90.3 (synthetic), and 86.5 (tampered), showing the largest gains on the tampered class where most baselines struggle (e.g., GPT-4o achieves only 25.4% F1 on tampered).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ForenAgent outperforms all 18 baselines on both FABench and SIDA-Test. Against the strongest trained detectors, it beats Effort (81.2% Acc) by 8.3 points and UnivFD (81.1% Acc) by 8.4 points on overall accuracy, with the largest per-class gains on synthetic images (90.3% vs. 79.3% F1 for Effort) and tampered images (86.5% vs. 78.4% F1 for Effort). Among zero-shot MLLMs, even the strongest (GPT-4.1 at 51.4% Acc) falls far short, and equipping them with the same forensic toolbox (GPT-4.1 with Tools at 64.7% Acc) still trails ForenAgent by 24.8 points, indicating that the two-stage training pipeline (Cold Start + RFT with process rewards) is critical beyond mere tool access. On SIDA-Test, ForenAgent (83.1% Acc) surpasses Effort (78.5%) and SIDA (77.2%), confirming cross-dataset generalization. Notably, UnivFD achieves the best authentic-class F1 (93.2%) but collapses on synthetic (71.8%), whereas ForenAgent maintains balanced performance across all three classes (86.5–91.4% F1), demonstrating its advantage as a general-purpose detector.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two previously separate IFD paradigms: low-level artifact-based detectors (SRM, PRNU, JPEG Ghost, FreqNet) and MLLM-based semantic approaches (FakeShield, SIDA, AIGI-Holmes). Rather than end-to-end learning, ForenAgent repurposes the 'Thinking with Images' agentic paradigm (PyVision, DeepEyes, ViperGPT) and RL-based tool-use training (GRPO) specifically for forensic reasoning, letting an MLLM autonomously generate and execute Python code that invokes 12 classical forensic operators. The two-stage Cold-Start + RFT pipeline with process-level rewards (global priority, tool logic, crop sensitivity, reasoning coherence) is a novel training formulation not seen in prior IFD or general tool-use work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ForenAgent demonstrates that a 7B MLLM equipped with a small forensic toolbox can substantially outperform both much larger zero-shot MLLMs and dedicated forensic detectors, suggesting that structured tool-augmented reasoning is a viable path toward interpretable, general-purpose IFD. FABench (100k images from 7+ modern generators) addresses a real gap: prior benchmarks relied on outdated GAN outputs and narrow inpainting pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video forensics, pixel-level localization (the paper acknowledges this limitation), real-time deployment cost of multi-turn tool calls, and whether the 12-tool set is sufficient as forgery techniques evolve. The reliance on a single backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and one primary benchmark leaves generalization claims somewhat under-tested.
