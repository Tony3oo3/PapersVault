## Problem

Existing quality-aware face recognition methods rely solely on feature magnitude as a quality proxy, which is susceptible to identity-agnostic noise (blur, occlusion, illumination) that can artificially inflate embedding norms and degrade quality estimates. Additionally, standard margin-based losses enforce only intra-class compactness without explicitly pushing non-target class features apart, leaving a residual risk of inter-class overlap under challenging conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262699/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes aligned face crops (or video frames) and processes them through an iResNet-100 backbone to produce a 512-dimensional face embedding. A lightweight squeeze-and-excitation branch (reduction ratio 16) plus a two-layer MLP appended after global average pooling yields a semantic quality score in [0,1], which is fused with the magnitude-derived quality to produce a single quality estimate. This quality score is model-specific and is used both for adaptive margin scaling during training and as a standalone FIQA signal at inference.

## Method

DQM-Face introduces a Dual Quality Margin (DQM) loss that unifies two quality cues—feature-norm magnitude and a squeeze-and-excitation semantic attention score—into a fused quality q = α·q_sem + (1−α)·q_mag, which linearly scales an adaptive attraction margin m₁(q) applied to the target-class logit. Simultaneously, an explicit repulsion margin m₂ is subtracted from all non-target class logits (z_j = s·cos(θ_j − m₂)), creating an asymmetric decision boundary that enforces a clearance zone around each identity. The repulsion margin follows a three-stage curriculum schedule (0.05 → 0.10 → 0.15 across 25 epochs) to stabilize early cluster formation before enforcing stronger inter-class separation. The total training objective also includes MagFace's magnitude regularization term to maintain the norm–quality correlation, and the entire framework is trained with SGD on MS1MV2 using a fixed scale s=64.

## Results

On the large-scale IJB-B benchmark, DQM-Face (α=0.4) achieves the best TAR at all three FAR thresholds: 96.71% at FAR=10⁻³, 95.38% at FAR=10⁻⁴, and 91.35% at FAR=10⁻⁵. On IJB-C, it similarly leads with 97.65%, 96.58%, and 94.83% TAR at the same FAR levels. On single-image benchmarks, DQM-Face (α=0.5) attains 99.83% on LFW, 98.35% on AgeDB-30, 98.83% on CFP-FP, and 93.30% on CPLFW (average 97.58%), ranking first or second across all four. For FIQA, the learned quality score achieves the best pAUC on LFW (0.0061), Adience (0.0404), and CPLFW (0.0220), outperforming nine state-of-the-art quality estimators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DQM-Face (α=0.4) achieves the best TAR at every FAR threshold on both IJB-B and IJB-C, outperforming the strongest prior methods: QMagFace (96.48/94.70/90.29 on IJB-B) by +0.23/+0.68/+1.06 points, and CoReFace (95.09/91.33 on IJB-B) by +0.29/+0.02 points at 10⁻⁴/10⁻⁵. On IJB-C, it surpasses QMagFace (97.62/96.19/94.27) by +0.03/+0.39/+0.56 and CoReFace (96.43/94.73) by +0.15/+0.10 at 10⁻⁴/10⁻⁵. On single-image benchmarks, DQM-Face (α=0.5) leads on CFP-FP (98.83%) and CPLFW (93.30%), tying for best on LFW (99.83%) with ArcFace and MagFace, while QMagFace retains a slight edge on AgeDB-30 (98.50 vs. 98.35). For FIQA, the fused quality score (pAUC 0.0061 on LFW, 0.0404 on Adience, 0.0220 on CPLFW) outperforms all nine compared estimators including eDiffIQA, CR-FIQA, and MagFace, while the semantic-only variant (α=1) achieves the best XQLFW score (0.1346), indicating complementary strengths of the two quality branches.

## Relation to Prior Work

DQM-Face extends the quality-aware margin-based face recognition lineage (MagFace → QMagFace → AdaFace) by addressing two specific gaps: (1) magnitude-only quality estimation is vulnerable to identity-agnostic noise (blur, occlusion, illumination), and (2) prior methods enforce intra-class compactness without explicit inter-class repulsion. The paper introduces a semantic quality branch via squeeze-and-excitation attention to complement the magnitude signal, and adds a curriculum-scheduled repulsion margin m₂ that pushes non-target class centers away. Conceptually, it unifies the ArcFace angular margin framework with MagFace's magnitude regularization into a single dual-margin objective, while the semantic attention module borrows from the SE-Net architecture in a lightweight post-pooling configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-quality formulation (magnitude + semantic) offers a principled way to make quality estimation identity-aware, which is a practical concern in unconstrained deployments where blur or occlusion can inflate embedding norms. The explicit repulsion margin with curriculum scheduling is a simple but effective mechanism to reduce inter-class overlap, and the fact that the learned quality signal transfers to FIQA (competitive pAUC vs. dedicated FIQA methods) validates its alignment with the recognition objective. Limitations include the model-specificity of the quality score (it relies on architecture-dependent cues rather than universal generalization), the fixed three-stage curriculum for m₂ (not learned end-to-end), and the modest absolute gains on standard benchmarks where differences are not statistically significant. Open questions include whether the semantic quality branch generalizes across different backbones and whether the repulsion margin could be made fully adaptive per-sample rather than globally scheduled.
