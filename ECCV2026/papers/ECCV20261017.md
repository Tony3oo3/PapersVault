## Problem

Existing precipitation forecasting models are tied to fixed discrete temporal intervals, creating a structural mismatch with the continuous physical evolution of precipitation. Densifying predictions via autoregressive rollouts accumulates error, many-to-many architectures scale poorly in compute, and implicit models lack explicit temporal consistency. The paper asks whether precipitation evolution can be reformulated as a continuous-time dynamical system that supports arbitrary-time inference while maintaining derivative-consistent dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261017/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of six radar reflectivity frames (384×384 for SEVIR, 224×224 for RAPID) as context and produces future precipitation intensity fields at any queried time point within the forecast horizon, not just at predefined intervals. On SEVIR the horizon is 60 minutes (12 frames at 5-min spacing); on RAPID it extends to 6 hours with 10-, 30-, or 60-minute inter-frame intervals. A key property is that a model trained at 1-hour intervals can be queried at 10-minute resolution without retraining, enabling dense temporal inference from a single continuous latent trajectory.

## Method

RainODE encodes input radar frames into latent representations via a 2D spatial encoder, then a Translator network predicts discrete future latent states. A Neural ODE, conditioned on the terminal latent state z(S), integrates a continuous trajectory between the first and last predicted latents using an RK4 solver, producing derivative-consistent intermediate states that are decoded back to observation space. Training uses a dual-objective loss: reconstruction loss on the Translator's discrete output plus a consistency loss aligning the ODE trajectory with the discrete predictions. A second stage introduces Stochastic Source Modeling (SSM), a Brownian Bridge diffusion process that bridges the deterministic ODE prediction toward the ground truth, injecting controlled stochastic perturbations to restore high-frequency intensity structures and localized variability that the deterministic ODE over-smooths. The decomposition mirrors the physical advection–diffusion–source/sink structure of precipitation dynamics, with the ODE capturing coherent advective transport and the SSM capturing unresolved non-advective evolution.

## Results

On SEVIR, RainODE achieves CSI-M of 0.430/0.464/0.544 (P1/P4/P16) and CSI-th of 0.766/0.304/0.177 (16/160/219), with RMSE 0.060 and FSS 0.564. The advantage is most pronounced on RAPID-60min (6-hour lead time), where RainODE reaches CSI-M 0.220/0.270/0.358 and CSI-th 0.400/0.201/0.056, while Earthformer collapses to near-zero CSI scores and CasCast degrades to CSI-M 0.033/0.053/0.112. RainODE also enables continuous-time inference: trained at 1-hour intervals, it generalizes to 10-minute evaluation without retraining, outperforming autoregressive baselines at high-intensity thresholds (CSI-160, CSI-219).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SimVP (CVPR'22), RainODE wins on CSI-M P4/P16, CSI-th 160/219, FSS, and HSS on SEVIR (e.g., CSI-th 219: 0.177 vs. 0.131), with the gap widening dramatically on RAPID-60min where SimVP scores CSI-M 0.141/0.145/0.155 versus RainODE's 0.220/0.270/0.358. Against Earthformer (NeurIPS'22), RainODE leads on most metrics at SEVIR, but the most striking result is on RAPID-60min where Earthformer produces near-zero CSI scores across all thresholds, indicating complete failure at 6-hour lead time. Against PreDiff (NeurIPS'23), RainODE dominates on CSI-M P1/P4, CSI-th 16, RMSE, FSS, and HSS on SEVIR, though PreDiff edges ahead on CSI-M P16 (0.454 vs. 0.544 is actually RainODE winning) — in fact RainODE wins on all CSI-M and most spatial metrics, with PreDiff's main advantage being lower FAR on SEVIR (0.505 vs. 0.399 is RainODE winning). Against CasCast (ICML'24), RainODE outperforms on CSI-M P1/P4, CSI-th 16, RMSE, FSS, and HSS on SEVIR, while CasCast is competitive on CSI-M P16 (0.540 vs. 0.544) and CSI-th 219 (0.160 vs. 0.177); on RAPID-60min the gap is extreme, with CasCast collapsing to CSI-M 0.033/0.053/0.112 and FAR 0.953 versus RainODE's 0.220/0.270/0.358 and FAR 0.596.

## Relation to Prior Work

RainODE extends the Neural ODE line of work in weather forecasting (ClimODE, WeatherODE) from global, spatially closed atmospheric systems to open-system, high-resolution precipitation nowcasting, where advective transport dominates short-term dynamics. The deterministic component builds on SimVP as the discrete forecasting backbone, while the stochastic refinement module repurposes Brownian Bridge diffusion (BBDM) from image-to-image translation to model residual intensity variability. The key conceptual departure from prior discrete-time paradigms (autoregressive, many-to-many, implicit) is the explicit reformulation of precipitation evolution as a continuous latent trajectory, enabling derivative-consistent dynamics and arbitrary-time inference without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to train at coarse intervals (1-hour) and infer at fine resolutions (10-minute) without retraining is practically significant for operational forecasting systems that need flexible temporal output. The RAPID dataset, with its 10/30/60-minute configurations and Korean Peninsula HSP radar data, fills a gap in multi-interval benchmarking beyond the single 5-minute-interval SEVIR protocol. However, the radar-only setting fundamentally limits long-horizon performance (CSI drops to ~0.14 at +6 hours), and the authors acknowledge that multi-modal extension incorporating broader atmospheric variables is needed to address this. Whether the continuous-time formulation generalizes beyond precipitation to other geophysical forecasting tasks remains an open question.
