## Problem

Token aggregation methods (e.g., ToMe) reduce ViT inference cost in a plug-and-play manner, but when naively combined with existing TTA methods they cause significant accuracy drops (over 3% even at modest compression). The paper identifies the root cause: token aggregation is a lossy many-to-one mapping that strictly reduces mutual information between features and labels, and the deterministic LayerNorm tuning used in standard TTA cannot recover this discarded information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261659/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on unlabeled OOD image batches (batch size 32 for ViT-L/16, 64 for ViT-B/16) during inference, with no access to source-domain labels at deployment time. A one-time pre-deployment step caches source-domain feature statistics (mean and variance per layer) from ~64 sampled images. Each incoming batch undergoes a single forward pass with ToMe token reduction (r tokens merged per layer) before back-propagation, producing class probability predictions; the [CLS] embedding bias and shallow-layer [CLS] biases are then updated via SGD.

## Method

NAVIA neutralizes token aggregation's information loss through two complementary [CLS]-token augmentation mechanisms. At the input level, a learnable bias vector δ is added to the [CLS] embedding before the first aggregation step, and Theorem 1 shows this increases the mutual information upper bound between the compressed token sequence and the label. At the feature level, learnable bias vectors δ_l are applied to the [CLS] token in the first L_bgt shallow encoder blocks (typically 4–6), providing layerwise compensation for the progressively accumulating information loss as tokens are merged deeper in the network. The overall TTA objective combines entropy minimization with a feature-statistics discrepancy loss aligning target-domain layer statistics to cached source statistics, while standard LayerNorm tuning handles basic distribution shift. The design of augmenting only shallow layers is motivated by three observations: the aggregation matrix depends on the [CLS] token, shallow biases act as pre-emphasis before compression, and residual connections propagate shallow updates to all subsequent blocks.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (severity 5) with ViT-L/16, NAVIA₂ achieves 71.9% average accuracy and NAVIA₄ achieves 70.7%, outperforming the TTA state-of-the-art FOA_BP (69.9%) by up to 3.1% while using 23% fewer GFLOPs (42.98 vs. 55.60) and 25% less wall-clock time (0.44s vs. 0.59s per batch). On ImageNet-R/V2/Sketch with ViT-L/16, NAVIA₂ reaches 68.7% average accuracy versus FOA_BP's 67.1%. With ViT-B/16, NAVIA₄ achieves 70.7% on ImageNet-C and 66.0% on ImageNet-R/V2/Sketch, consistently surpassing all token aggregation baselines (ToMe, EViT, Tofu, TCA) by 1–6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAVIA outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every setting. Against the TTA state-of-the-art FOA_BP, NAVIA₂ (ViT-L/16) gains +2.0% on ImageNet-C (71.9% vs. 69.9%) and +1.6% on ImageNet-R/V2/Sketch (68.7% vs. 67.1%), while NAVIA₄ further reduces GFLOPs by 23% and wall-clock time by 25%. Compared to the compute-heavy FOA_CMA, NAVIA₄ is over 17× faster (0.44s vs. 7.73s) while delivering +2.5% accuracy. Among token aggregation baselines, NAVIA₂ surpasses ToMe₂ by +3.0% (71.9% vs. 68.9%), Tofu₂ by +3.8%, and TCA₂ by +5.8% on ImageNet-C (ViT-L/16); the gap widens at higher compression (r=4), where EViT₄ collapses to 54.4% while NAVIA₄ maintains 70.7%. Against lightweight TTA methods (Tent, LAME, SAR, CoTTA, DeYO), NAVIA achieves 2–11% higher accuracy with comparable or lower compute. In the parameter-budget ablation (Table 5), NAVIA (43.7K params) outperforms Prompt Tuning with 3 tokens/layer (52.2K params, 69.0%) and LoRA r=3 (121.3K params, 68.8%) by 0.8–1.9% on ImageNet-C, confirming gains stem from the augmentation mechanism rather than parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of test-time adaptation (TTA) and token aggregation, two lines of work that have developed largely independently. On the TTA side, it builds on norm-tuning methods (Tent, FOA, SAR) and the entropy-minimization + feature-statistics-discrepancy objective from FOA [37]. On the efficiency side, it adopts ToMe [2] as the token aggregation backbone. The key departure is the mutual information analysis (Corollary 2) showing that LayerNorm tuning is fundamentally incapable of recovering information destroyed by non-injective token merging, which motivates the [CLS]-based information augmentation as a complementary mechanism to norm-tuning rather than a replacement for it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: TTA methods are increasingly deployed in resource-constrained settings, and NAVIA delivers 14–26% wall-clock speedup while simultaneously improving accuracy over the TTA SOTA (FOA) by up to 3.1%, making efficient adaptation a realistic deployment option. The theoretical framing via mutual information provides a principled explanation for why the naive 'just add ToMe' approach fails, which should guide future work combining compression with adaptation. Open questions include whether the [CLS]-centric augmentation generalizes to architectures without a dedicated classification token (e.g., DETR, MAE-style encoders), whether it transfers to other token aggregation strategies beyond ToMe's bipartite matching, and whether the shallow-layer bias design holds for much deeper ViTs (ViT-H, ViT-G).
