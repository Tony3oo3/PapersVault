## Problem

Video-LLMs frequently attend to irrelevant frames rather than the visual evidence that supports their answers, a failure mode that is especially costly for sports coaching tasks requiring precise temporal localization to identify technique errors. Existing remedies—expensive human frame-level annotations or pseudo-labels from other models—are either prohibitively costly or risk propagating hallucinations about which frames matter. The paper asks whether the model can be trained to ground its attention in the correct frames without any additional frame-level supervision during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 16 uniformly sampled video frames (encoded to 5 tokens per frame via visual pooling in PerceptionLM-8B) paired with a text task prompt, and produces a standard autoregressive text response (coaching feedback, multiple-choice QA answer, or open-ended assessment). Unusually, the same video is processed twice per training step under two different prompts—a generation prompt and a verification prompt—so the effective input is a (video, prompt_gen, prompt_ver) triple. The verification prompt is auto-constructed by rephrasing the generation prompt into a yes/no question that embeds the ground-truth answer, and no gradients flow through this pathway.

## Method

The core mechanism is a dual-pathway self-consistency loss that enforces attentional agreement between a harder generation task and an easier verification task on the same video, exploiting the observation that both must localize the same keyframes. A fixed visual layer (layer 8, selected offline via a composite score of attention sharpness, vision-centricity, and keyframe AUROC) is targeted, and within it the top-K=5 heads are dynamically selected per forward pass using the Visual Non-Sink Ratio to avoid sink-token-dominated heads. Matched head pairs across pathways are established via a two-stage Head Bipartite Matching procedure (exact index matching followed by Hungarian-algorithm assignment on asymmetric KL cost). The training objective combines three terms with equal weight: standard cross-entropy on the generation output, an entropy regularization that sharpens the generation pathway's visual attention, and a KL-divergence consistency loss that pulls the generation pathway's attention maps toward the stop-gradiented verification pathway's maps, analogous to the fixed target network in BYOL/MoCo. Training uses LoRA (rank 16) on the language model only, with the visual encoder frozen, requiring no frame-level annotations at training time.

## Results

On the three sports coaching benchmarks, the proposed dual-pathway self-consistency method (applied on top of finetuned PerceptionLM-8B) achieves 87.5% accuracy on ExAct, 88.2% accuracy on FitnessQA, and 38.4 BERTScore on ExpertAF. These represent gains of +3.0, +14.1, and +0.9 respectively over the supervised finetuning baseline (84.5 / 74.1 / 37.5). The method requires no additional frame-level annotations during training and no architectural changes, using only a self-consistency loss over select vision-centric attention heads.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all four baselines on every dataset where a comparison is available. Against the most direct baseline (finetuned PerceptionLM-8B), gains are +3.0 on ExAct, +14.1 on FitnessQA, and +0.9 BERTScore on ExpertAF. The 8B open-source model with self-consistency training surpasses the much larger closed-source Gemini-3-Flash by 8.5 points on ExAct (87.5 vs 79.0) and by 34.0 points on FitnessQA (88.2 vs 54.2). Compared to Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, the method leads by 47.3 points on ExAct (87.5 vs 40.2) and 32.8 points on FitnessQA (88.2 vs 55.4), while Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct trails by 54.9 points on FitnessQA (88.2 vs 33.3). On ExpertAF, the method edges out Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (38.4 vs 27.3) and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (38.4 vs 27.1) by roughly 11 BERTScore points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the self-supervised learning paradigm from BYOL/MoCo (stop-gradient on a target pathway) but applies it to visual attention maps across complementary task views rather than to representations across augmented image crops. It departs from prior temporal grounding work that relies on architectural modifications, chain-of-thought supervision, or iterative refinement by instead targeting the internal attention allocation directly, without any frame-level annotations during training. The head-matching procedure (exact index matching + Hungarian algorithm) and the Visual Non-Sink Ratio for dynamic head selection are novel components that address the non-trivial problem of establishing cross-pathway correspondence in a transformer without modality-specific modules. The empirical analysis on VidDiffBench identifying which layers and heads are sensitive to temporal grounding extends prior spatial-grounding analyses from the image domain to video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that attention misallocation is an independent, addressable bottleneck for temporal grounding in Vid-LLMs, separate from visual representation quality or model scale, which shifts the focus of future work toward internal attention structure. The annotation-free training signal (self-consistency between generation and verification pathways) is attractive in principle, but generalization beyond sports coaching and beyond the single PerceptionLM base model remains untested. Open questions include whether the method transfers to domains without naturally paired generation-verification tasks, whether it composes with chain-of-thought supervision or RL post-training, and whether the small training sets (800 samples per dataset) limit the observed gains. The finding that an 8B open-source model can surpass Gemini-3-Flash on fine-grained sports understanding suggests that targeted attention training may be a more efficient path than scaling for domain-specific temporal tasks.
