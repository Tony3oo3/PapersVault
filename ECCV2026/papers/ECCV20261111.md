## Problem

Existing human-centric video datasets suffer from structural deficiencies across three dimensions: limited scene and camera diversity (mostly controlled environments), sparse multi-person interaction modeling (less than 3% of videos), and insufficient individual attribute alignment (lip-sync, identity, speech). These gaps prevent audio-video joint generation models from producing high-fidelity human-centric content in complex real-world scenes, and existing benchmarks lack perception-aligned metrics for interaction naturalness, audio-visual assignment, and physical contact plausibility.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests raw video clips with audio from open, complex scenarios, standardizing them to 30 FPS and 44.1 kHz before progressive filtering. The output is 1 million annotated video-audio pairs (1,800 hours, 80K identities) at HD or higher resolution, each carrying a structured subject set with per-identity bounding boxes, 134-keypoint poses, speech transcripts with speaker IDs and emotions, and long-form narrative captions with identity anchors. For evaluation, OHBench takes generated 5-second videos at ~720P from various models and produces scores across 7 dimensions at 3 hierarchical levels (global, relational, individual).

## Method

The core contribution is a fully automated four-module pipeline: (1) four-tier video preprocessing (TransNetV2 shot segmentation, Demucs source separation, CLIP/DOVER aesthetic filtering, UniMatch flow-based motion consistency), (2) spatio-temporal multi-object tracking (YOLOv11 + MOTRv2) with DWPose-L 134-keypoint whole-body pose and ArcFace identity assignment, (3) audio-visual alignment via 3Dspeaker diarization + SyncNet cross-correlation for speaker-to-face matching with 3-frame tolerance, and (4) two-stage Qwen3-Omni caption generation using a placeholder mechanism ([speech_m], [lyrics_m]) to prevent hallucination while preserving narrative fluency. The companion OHBench introduces a three-level, seven-dimension evaluation system combining expert models (MUSIQ, RAFT, ArcFace, SyncNet, DNSMOS, ImageBind, CLAP) with Gemini-3 MLLM judgments for background plausibility, interaction naturalness, and contact realism. Fine-tuning LTX-2 on just 20% of OmniHuman (180K single-person + 20K two-person samples) yields consistent gains across all metrics, with the largest improvements in audio quality (KL +25.9%) and multi-modal alignment (T-A +25.0%), validating the dataset's value for closing the gap between open-source and closed-source models.

## Results

Fine-tuning LTX-2 on only 20% of OmniHuman (180K single-person + 20K two-person samples) yields improvements across all 11 OHBench metrics. The most substantial gains appear in audio quality (KL divergence reduced by 25.9%, FD by 12.3%, AbS improved by 11.9%) and multi-modal alignment (T-A +25.0%, V-A +11.1%), while dynamic degree improves by 10.7% and identity consistency by 6.1%. Among all evaluated models, Veo3 leads in overall video quality and audio-visual alignment, while LTX-2 (pre-fine-tuning) already surpasses all closed-source models in listener realism (LR=8.94 vs. Veo3's 8.84). After fine-tuning, LTX-ft narrows the gap to closed-source models substantially, achieving the best open-source scores in 9 of 11 metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's core empirical claim is validated by fine-tuning LTX-2 on 20% of OmniHuman: the fine-tuned model (LTX-ft) outperforms the LTX-2 baseline on 10 of 11 metrics, with the largest relative gains in KL divergence (−25.9%), T-A alignment (+25.0%), and AbS (+11.9%), while listener realism (LR) shows a negligible −0.1% drop. Against other open-source models, LTX-ft surpasses Ovi in audio quality (AbS 4.13 vs. 3.75) and V-A alignment (0.30 vs. 0.23), beats MOVA in all interaction metrics (OC 7.12 vs. 5.98, CN 7.32 vs. 5.37), and outperforms UniAVGen in identity consistency (IC 0.696 vs. 0.599) and lip-sync (Sync 7.11 vs. 4.45). Compared to closed-source models, LTX-ft still trails Veo3 in IQ (0.721 vs. 0.737), AbS (4.13 vs. 5.40), and T-A (0.35 vs. 0.39), but narrows the gap to within ~10% on most metrics and matches or exceeds SeedDance1.5-pro in V-A alignment (0.30 vs. 0.31) and DD (0.665 vs. 0.897). In the S2V task (Table 4), InfiniteTalk leads overall (IC=0.775, Sync=7.63), while the paper's benchmark reveals that no S2V model handles person-person interaction well (all show '−' for IC*, IN, LR, ES), highlighting a gap that OmniHuman's multi-person data is designed to address.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by simultaneously addressing structural gaps in both human-centric video datasets and their evaluation benchmarks, rather than extending a single prior method. It repurposes a suite of existing tools (YOLOv11, MOTRv2, DWPose, SyncNet, ArcFace, Demucs, Qwen3-Omni) into a novel fully automated hierarchical annotation pipeline that no prior dataset (VoxCeleb, HDTF, OpenHumanVid, SpeakerVid-5M) has attempted at this level of granularity across video, frame, and individual levels. The benchmark (OHBench) departs from prior evaluation suites (VBench, VABench, MTAVG-Bench) by introducing a three-level diagnostic hierarchy (global scene → relational interaction → individual attribute) and explicitly filtering out metrics that misalign with human perception, a design choice not present in earlier benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1M-video, 80K-identity scale combined with the three-level annotation structure makes OmniHuman a potential standard training resource for human-centric audio-video generation, and the demonstrated 20%-data fine-tuning gains on LTX-2 suggest practical utility even for resource-constrained labs. However, the dataset is 80% English-dominant and limited to single- and two-person scenes, leaving multi-person (>2) interactions and cross-lingual generalization as open questions. The benchmark's reliance on Gemini-3 for several subjective metrics (background plausibility, interaction naturalness, contact naturalness) raises questions about evaluator bias and reproducibility, and the fine-tuning evidence is limited to a single open-source model, so broader generalizability of the data-efficiency claim remains untested.
