## Problem

State-of-the-art unified multimodal models (UMMs) that jointly handle understanding and generation are typically trained from scratch with massive compute, creating a significant gap between proprietary systems and fully open models. Existing efficient approaches that couple frozen VLMs to diffusion decoders rely on shallow or late-stage fusion, limiting cross-modal expressiveness. This paper asks whether competitive UMM performance can be achieved by strategically fusing publicly available specialized models with minimal additional training.

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the model takes a text prompt and produces an image at up to 1024px long side (NaViT-style native aspect ratio, min short side 512px). For image editing, it accepts a source image plus a text instruction, extracting both ViT tokens (224–532px range) and VAE latents (16× spatial, 4× temporal compression) from the source as dual conditioning signals. For understanding tasks, it processes interleaved text and image tokens and outputs text responses. The maximum sequence length is 20,480 tokens, and classifier-free guidance is applied with random dropping of text (0.1), VAE (0.1), and ViT (0.5) tokens during training.

## Method

LightFusion introduces a Double Fusion architecture that interleaves zero-initialized multimodal self-attention blocks between the layers of a frozen QWen2.5-VL-7B (understanding) and a Wan2.2-TI2V-5B DiT (generation), enabling deep cross-modal interaction from the earliest layers while preserving each base model's original capabilities. The multimodal self-attention uses a generalized causal mask (causal for language tokens, bidirectional for visual tokens) adopted from BAGEL, allowing all token types—text, ViT, and VAE—to attend to one another. A key design choice is the dual visual pathway: ViT tokens carry high-level semantics while VAE latents provide low-level spatial detail, and the ablation shows their combination substantially outperforms either alone for editing. Training uses only ~35B tokens over 70K steps with a three-stage curriculum that progressively increases high-quality T2I and editing data ratios, and the understanding branch remains frozen throughout. The ablation confirms that '0% depth' deep fusion (each DiT block conditioned on the corresponding VLM block) consistently outperforms shallow fusion where all DiT blocks receive only the final VLM output.

## Results

LightFusion achieves a GenEval score of 0.91 (best among unified models, surpassing UniPic 2.0 at 0.90 and BAGEL at 0.88), 82.16 on DPG-Bench, 6.06 on GEdit-Bench-EN, and 3.77 on ImgEdit-Bench, all while being trained on only ~35B seen tokens. On visual understanding, it matches the strongest baselines with 83.5 on MMBench, 58.6 on MMMU, and 67.1 on MM-Vet by freezing the QWen2.5-VL-7B understanding branch. The model uses 7B+5B+3B parameters (understanding + generation + multimodal self-attention blocks) and is fully open-source (model, data, and code).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, LightFusion (0.91) outperforms all unified baselines including UniPic 2.0 (0.90), Ovis-U1 (0.89), BAGEL (0.88), UniPic (0.86), and OmniGen2 (0.86), while being trained on over an order of magnitude fewer tokens. On DPG-Bench, LightFusion (82.16) trails the strongest unified models—Show-o2 (86.14), UniPic (85.50), BAGEL (85.07), and OmniGen2 (83.57)—but clearly surpasses the fully open competitors BLIP3-o 8B (81.60) and UniWorld-V1 (81.38). For image editing on GEdit-Bench-EN, LightFusion (6.06) beats UniWorld-V1 (4.85) and UniPic (5.83) by large margins but remains below BAGEL (6.52), OmniGen2 (6.42), and UniPic 2.0 (7.10). On ImgEdit-Bench, LightFusion (3.77) surpasses OmniGen2 (3.44), UniPic (3.49), BAGEL (3.20), and UniWorld-V1 (3.26), yet falls short of Ovis-U1 (3.98) and UniPic 2.0 (4.06). On visual understanding (MMB/MMMU/MM-Vet), LightFusion exactly matches UniWorld-V1, UniPic 2.0, and BLIP3-o 8B at 83.5/58.6/67.1, confirming that freezing the VLM branch preserves understanding without degradation. The overarching trade-off is that LightFusion achieves the best GenEval score and strong editing results with far fewer training tokens (~35B) than competitors trained on orders of magnitude more data, at the cost of slightly lower DPG-Bench and GEdit-Bench scores versus the most heavily trained unified models.

## Relation to Prior Work

LightFusion builds directly on BAGEL's generalized causal attention mechanism and KV-caching strategy for cross-modal interaction, but departs fundamentally from the 'shallow fusion' paradigm adopted by BLIP3-o, MetaQueries, and UniWorld, which condition the generator only on the final-layer VLM output. Unlike LMFusion and BAGEL, which initialize the generation branch with duplicated LLM weights and require massive private training data, LightFusion fuses two architecturally distinct public models (QWen2.5-VL-7B and Wan2.2-TI2V-5B) via zero-initialized multimodal self-attention blocks interleaved at every layer, preserving each base model's pretrained capabilities while enabling deep cross-modal interaction. The dual-pathway design integrating both ViT (high-level semantics) and VAE (low-level spatial) tokens for image editing is a further departure from prior unified models that rely on a single visual tokenizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving competitive or state-of-the-art results across generation and editing benchmarks with only ~35B seen tokens—orders of magnitude fewer than UniPic, OmniGen2, or BAGEL—LightFusion demonstrates that efficient UMM construction is feasible without training from scratch, substantially lowering the barrier to entry for the research community. The fully open release of code, weights, and training data addresses a critical gap between proprietary systems and open-source UMMs. Key limitations include the frozen understanding branch (constraining end-to-end co-adaptation) and the acknowledgment that true mutual enhancement between understanding and generation remains unrealized; the paper explicitly frames this as future work, leaving open whether the double-fusion architecture can be extended to jointly fine-tune both pathways for synergistic gains.
