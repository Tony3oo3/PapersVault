## Problem

Existing zero-cost proxies (ZCPs) for training-free NAS either rely on static architectural or initialization-time signals that ignore how optimization behavior evolves during training, or they adopt a node-homogeneous aggregation that treats all components as equally important. This leads to systematic inconsistencies in architecture ranking across tasks and search spaces, limiting the reliability of training-free NAS.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260254/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only a single forward pass of an untrained network with a random input (Gaussian or uniform distribution at moderate resolution); no real data, gradients, or training steps are needed. It outputs a continuous scalar score per architecture (ER-Score for general architectures, TER-Score for CNNs), which can be directly used to rank candidate architectures or guide evolutionary search. The ablation shows that random inputs at medium resolution yield the strongest correlation, while real images or extreme resolutions degrade performance.

## Method

ER-Score is grounded in the training-dynamics theory of over-parameterized networks, where the loss decay rate is governed by the eigen-spectrum of the limiting Gram matrix H^∞. Since H^∞ is intractable for DNNs, the paper approximates it via per-layer feature map correlation matrices C_l computed from a single forward pass, and defines the score as the effective rank (exponential of spectral entropy) of each C_l, aggregated across layers. A higher effective rank indicates a broader, more balanced eigen-spectrum and thus more stable optimization. For CNNs, TER-Score extends ER-Score by modeling the network as a DAG and applying four topology-weighting strategies (degree, connectivity, shortest-path, PageRank) to assign structural importance weights to each edge, so that components in different topological positions contribute proportionally to the final score. The PageRank-weighted variant (ER-Score + P.R.) is selected as the final TER-Score based on its superior discrimination among elite architectures on NAS-Bench-301.

## Results

On the DARTS search space, TER-Score achieves the lowest test error of 2.41% on CIFAR-10 and 23.55% on ImageNet-1k, outperforming all training-free, predictor-based, and one-shot NAS baselines while requiring only 0.09 GPU days. In terms of ranking consistency, ER-Score+P.R. (the final TER-Score variant) achieves the best average Spearman rank correlation across 10 image classification tasks (average rank 4.05), surpassing AZ-NAS (5.10) and ER-Score without topology weighting (5.70). On ViT-Bench-101, ER-Score integrated into Auto-Prox achieves the highest Spearman correlation with distillation accuracy in both AutoFormer (95.36% on C100) and PiT (96.33% on Flowers) search spaces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TER-Score achieves the best final test error on both CIFAR-10 (2.41%) and ImageNet-1k (23.55%), beating the closest training-free competitor SWAP-NAS by 0.07% and 0.45% respectively, and outperforming predictor-based methods PRE-NAS (2.49%/24.0%) and PINAT (2.54%/24.9%). Among zero-cost proxy baselines, ER-Score+P.R. attains the highest average Spearman rank correlation across 10 classification tasks (rank 4.05), edging out AZ-NAS (5.10) and the unweighted ER-Score (5.70); on individual benchmarks like NB201-C10, ER-Score+P.R. reaches 0.84 correlation versus 0.75 for AZ-NAS and 0.85 for SWAP. On ViT-Bench-101, ER-Score substantially outperforms TF-TAS and Auto-Prox in the AutoFormer search space (95.36% vs. 50.84% and 63.87% Spearman on C100) and leads in the PiT space (96.33% vs. 92.94% on Flowers). In evolutionary search on NB301, TER-Score converges to the highest final accuracy with the lowest variance across 5 seeds, while several baselines (e.g., FLOPs, Params) exhibit mid-search performance drops attributed to their bias toward high-parameter architectures. The method also generalizes to downstream transfer tasks (segmentation, jigsaw, autoencoding) in TransNAS-Bench-101, where topology-weighted variants maintain top-3 Spearman correlations across all 10 transfer tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper grounds its zero-cost proxy in the training dynamics theory of overparameterized networks (Du et al., ICLR 2019), specifically the Gram matrix framework that characterizes loss decay via eigen-spectra. It operationalizes the theoretical insight from MeCo (Jiang et al., NeurIPS 2023) that the minimum eigenvalue of feature correlation matrices governs convergence, replacing the intractable infinite-width Gram matrix with the effective rank of per-layer correlation matrices. Departing from prior ZCPs (SWAP, AZ-NAS, NEAR, ZiCo) that rely on static or single-pass signals, ER-Score explicitly targets optimization stability, and the topology-weighted extension (TER-Score) builds on Dong et al.'s observation that node-wise statistics contribute heterogeneously, replacing their sinusoidal heuristic with four graph-theoretic weighting strategies (degree, connectivity, shortest-path, PageRank).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically motivated, architecture-agnostic zero-cost proxy that achieves SOTA ranking consistency across CNN and ViT search spaces with negligible compute (0.09 GPU days), making training-free NAS practically viable for resource-constrained settings. However, the topology-weighting strategies remain heuristic without rigorous theoretical justification for why specific graph metrics should capture component importance, and TER-Score does not naturally extend to Transformers (though ER-Score does). Open questions include whether attention-based or token-level structural priors can replace DAG-based topology weighting for ViTs, and whether the effective-rank criterion generalizes to non-convex, underparameterized regimes where the Gram matrix is not stationary.
