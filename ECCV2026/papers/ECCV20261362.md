## Problem

Training-free image editing with MM-DiT models faces a fundamental fidelity-editability trade-off: global KV-injection rigidly over-preserves the source image, preventing meaningful text-driven edits, while strictly masked injection degrades the task into unconstrained localized inpainting that destroys the edited object's structural identity. Existing methods rely on fixed or heuristic KV-injection policies across all transformer blocks, leaving the critical layer selection problem unsolved.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image, a source text prompt, and a target text prompt, and produces an edited image with preserved background and anchored object structure. A binary editing mask is generated automatically via a single forward pass extracting cross-attention maps from a calibrated subset of blocks, followed by Otsu binarization and morphological refinement—eliminating the need for user-provided masks or external segmentation models like SAM. The method operates on FLUX.1-dev (15 sampling steps, CFG 1.0/2.0) and SD3.5-medium (28 steps, CFG 1.0/7.5).

## Method

LayerVerse decouples KV-injection into two distinct roles—Masked Injection to protect the background and a limited budget of Global Injections to anchor the edited object's structure—then formulates optimal layer allocation as a combinatorial problem solved via Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP). The editing error is modeled as a second-order quadratic function capturing pairwise interactions between transformer layers, with coefficients calibrated from a small hold-out dataset (115 MS-COCO images) by evaluating errors at boundary configurations (empty, single, and pair activations). For FLUX.1-dev, the solver identifies 8 active blocks (7 masked + 1 global anchor); for SD3.5-medium, 13 blocks (9 masked + 4 anchors), reflecting architecture-specific structural fragility. The resulting layer configuration acts as a solver-agnostic prior: calibrated once with a standard Euler sampler, it transfers directly to advanced solvers like FireFlow and RF-Solver without recalibration, yielding simultaneous improvements in background preservation, structural consistency, and prompt adherence on PIE-Bench.

## Results

On PIE-Bench with FLUX.1-dev, FireFlow + LayerVerse achieves the best overall Average Rank (2.88), dramatically improving background preservation (LPIPS drops from 64.59 to 37.82) and human-aligned editing quality (EditScore jumps from 3.60 to 5.05) over FireFlow alone. RF-Solver + LayerVerse also reaches a strong Avg. Rank of 3.38 with LPIPS of 37.62 and SSIM of 92.66. On SD3.5-medium, FireFlow + LayerVerse secures the best Structure Distance (13.52) and SSIM (89.70) with an Avg. Rank of 2.44, confirming the solver-agnostic transfer of the optimized layer configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FireFlow + LayerVerse (Avg. Rank 2.88) decisively outperforms FireFlow alone (5.88), cutting LPIPS by 41% (64.59→37.82) and boosting EditScore by 40% (3.60→5.05), while maintaining near-identical Structure Distance (9.48→9.36). Against UniEdit-Flow, which achieves the best individual Structure Distance (8.82), FireFlow+LV wins on LPIPS (37.82 vs 55.35), HPSv3 (5.07 vs 4.27), and EditScore (5.05 vs 4.73), yielding a better overall rank (2.88 vs 3.50). Compared to DNAEdit, FireFlow+LV dominates on preservation metrics (Structure Distance 9.36 vs 17.00; LPIPS 37.82 vs 86.78) but concedes on HPSv3 (5.07 vs 5.80). FlowEdit and RF-inversion achieve higher CLIP and HPSv3 scores (reflecting aggressive, unconstrained generation) but suffer catastrophic background degradation (LPIPS 112.99 and 177.11 respectively) and structural collapse (Structure Distance 27.96 and 42.00). On SD3.5-medium, FireFlow+LV (Avg. Rank 2.44) edges out DNAEdit (2.50) on Structure Distance (13.52 vs 13.64) and LPIPS (50.85 vs 74.50), while FlowEdit leads on CLIP similarity but trails on all preservation metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

LayerVerse builds on the KV-injection mechanism adapted from LLM attention caching (MAS-CTRL, attention sinks) and applies it to rectified-flow image editing, but departs from prior work by treating layer selection as a combinatorial optimization problem rather than a fixed heuristic. It positions itself as orthogonal to existing training-free editors (FireFlow, RF-Solver, FlowEdit, DNAEdit, UniEdit-Flow), operating as a solver-agnostic prior that can be plugged into any inversion-based method. The key conceptual departure from KV-Edit is identifying the 'Masked Inpainting Pitfall'—that purely masked injection destroys the edited object's structural identity—and resolving it by introducing a small budget of global structural anchors alongside masked background protection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The solver-agnostic, architecture-specific layer configuration provides a practical drop-in improvement for anyone using inversion-based flow editors on FLUX or SD3.5, with the MILP calibration being a one-time offline cost (~30 GPU hours with 10 images). The demonstration that exact block identity matters (random selection catastrophically fails at structure preservation) validates that the combinatorial structure of transformer layers is non-trivial and not interchangeable. Limitations include the per-architecture calibration requirement, the pairwise interaction approximation (higher-order synergies are ignored), and inapplicability to style transfer. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to newer or larger DiT architectures and whether the quadratic model can be refined to capture higher-order interactions without combinatorial explosion.
