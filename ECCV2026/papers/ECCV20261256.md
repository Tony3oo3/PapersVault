## Problem

Current VLMs fail to make physically consistent metric judgments (object sizes, inter-object distances, 3D positions) from single images, and existing spatial benchmarks rely on multiple-choice or relative comparisons that let models bypass rigorous geometric reasoning. The paper asks whether VLMs can infer absolute metric quantities by leveraging in-image reference objects with known physical dimensions—mimicking how humans calibrate scale—without access to camera intrinsics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261256/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a single RGB indoor image (from ScanNetV2 ego-view frames) alongside a text query that specifies 2–4 reference objects with their known metric measurements (e.g., 'the keyboard has a length of 0.45 m, the cup has a height of 0.12 m') and asks for a specific metric property of a target object. The output is a structured two-part response: a chain-of-thought reasoning block enclosed in <thinking> tags that walks through spatial localization, scale anchoring, and geometric inference, followed by a final numerical answer in <Final answer> tags—either a single scalar (length, width, height, depth, distance, elevation in meters) or a 6-element 3D bounding box [x, y, z, width, height, length] in camera coordinates.

## Method

Metric-Bench is constructed from ScanNetV2 by converting 3D annotations to camera coordinates, filtering for sharp frames, and generating 1,340 QA pairs across five categories (size, position, depth, distance, 3D bounding box) using templated queries that pair reference metrics with target metric questions. For training, MetricReasoner applies GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning to Qwen3-VL-8B with a composite reward: a binary format reward for structural compliance, an Exponential Precision (EP) reward that decays as e^{−|ŷ−y|} to provide continuous accuracy signal, and a Binned Proximity (BP) reward that discretizes error into hierarchical intervals (0.25 m, 0.5 m, 1 m) for coarse-grained calibration. A structured prompt guides the model through seven explicit reasoning steps—defining the reference anchor, establishing spatial pose, locating the target, constructing a spatial relationship graph, estimating relative offsets, deriving the absolute quantity via calculation, and documenting uncertainty—thereby replacing supervised numerical regression with verifiable, reward-driven optimization over metric accuracy and reasoning coherence.

## Results

On Metric-Bench, MetricReasoner achieves a mean MRA of 48.59, RMSE of 0.5323 m, and δ₁ of 0.4705, outperforming the strongest spatial-specialized baseline VST-7B-SFT (MRA 39.61) by 9.0 points and the best proprietary model GPT-5-mini (MRA 37.84) by 10.8 points. On downstream embodied benchmarks, MetricReasoner reaches 55.14 on RoboSpatial and 44.00 on ERQA, surpassing its base model Qwen3-VL-8B by 2.0 and 1.75 points respectively. On general-purpose benchmarks, it improves over Qwen3-VL-8B by 6.81 points on V*Bench (68.59% vs 61.78%) and 23.21 points on BLINK (81.82% vs 58.61%), demonstrating that the RFT strategy does not compromise general VLM capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetricReasoner outperforms all eight baseline families on Metric-Bench MRA. Against proprietary models, it surpasses GPT-5-mini (37.84) by 10.75 points and Gemini-2.5-Flash (33.95) by 14.64 points in MRA, while also reducing RMSE from ~0.78 to 0.53 m. Among open-source general VLMs, it beats the strongest, Qwen3-VL-32B (39.24), by 9.35 MRA points, and outperforms all InternVL3.5 and LLaVA-OneVision variants by 15–28 points. Against spatial-specialized models, it exceeds VST-7B-SFT (39.61) by 8.98 MRA points and SpaceR-7B (28.97) by 19.62 points, with the largest gap in the 3D bounding box category (41.90 vs 26.86 for VST). On downstream embodied benchmarks, MetricReasoner wins over both Qwen3-VL-8B and VST-7B-RL on every metric: RoboSpatial 55.14 vs 53.14/42.29, ERQA 44.00 vs 42.25/40.25, V*Bench 68.59% vs 61.78%/59.16%, and BLINK 81.82% vs 58.61%/43.32%. Notably, VST-7B-RL degrades substantially on general benchmarks relative to its base model (BLINK drops from 58.61% to 43.32%), whereas MetricReasoner improves across the board, confirming that the RFT recipe avoids the catastrophic forgetting typical of spatial-specialized fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two main lines of prior work. First, metric-scale estimation methods (Metric3D, Depth Pro, MOGE) resolve monocular scale ambiguity through camera intrinsics or dedicated architectures, whereas Metric-Bench and MetricReasoner instead emulate human perception by using in-image reference objects with known physical dimensions as in-context anchors, eliminating the need for intrinsics entirely. Second, existing spatial VLM benchmarks (3DSR-Bench, OmniSpatial, VSI-Bench, ERQA, RoboSpatial) predominantly rely on multiple-choice or relative-comparison formats that allow models to bypass rigorous geometric reasoning; Metric-Bench requires direct absolute metric prediction. On the training side, the paper extends GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning (as used in 3D-R1, VLN-R1, and other RFT methods) by designing task-specific verifiable rewards (exponential precision + binned proximity) that supervise both reasoning structure and numerical accuracy, rather than relying on outcome-level or coarse spatial constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a concrete gap: prior spatial VLM evaluations use qualitative formats that mask a model's inability to perform precise geometric reasoning, while Metric-Bench forces absolute metric prediction grounded in sparse references. The finding that pure RFT (without SFT warmup) not only achieves the best metric accuracy but also substantially improves general VLM capabilities (e.g., BLINK 58.61%→81.82%) challenges the common assumption that task-specific fine-tuning causes catastrophic forgetting, though this is demonstrated on a single base model (Qwen3-VL-8B). Key limitations remain: distance estimation is still the weakest category across all models, absolute performance (48.59 MRA) is far below human level (76.83), and the approach has not been validated across multiple architectures or at video scale. Open questions include whether reference-grounded metric learning transfers to dynamic/video settings and whether the RFT recipe generalizes beyond indoor ScanNet scenes.
