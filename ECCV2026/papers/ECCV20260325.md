## Problem

Zero-Shot Compositional Action Recognition (ZS-CAR) models suffer from object-driven shortcuts: they predict verbs by relying on the labeled object class rather than temporal evidence, because sparse and skewed compositional supervision creates strong co-occurrence priors and objects are visually easier to learn than verbs. This causes unseen verb-object compositions to collapse toward frequent seen pairs, severely limiting compositional generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260325/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (T frames) as input and produces three outputs: a verb label, an object label, and a joint composition label (verb-object pair). Inference operates over the full Y^V × Y^O composition space under an open-world protocol—no restriction to seen compositions and no test-set-tuned bias calibration—making it more realistic than the closed-world protocol used in prior ZS-CAR work. Composition logits are computed by aggregating factorized verb and object logits with conditional terms, following the C2C architecture.

## Method

RCORE introduces two complementary regularizers on top of an adapter-tuned encoder-based VLM backbone (AIM for CLIP, LoRA for InternVideo2). Co-occurrence Prior Regularization (CPR) synthesizes new verb-object compositions by injecting a different object's static cue into the high-motion regions of a source video, assigns soft labels to these synthesized videos, and applies a margin-based penalty that forces the target composition to outrank frequent seen hard negatives; a batch-adaptive label-space expansion makes these novel compositions trainable without destabilizing closed-world optimization. Temporal Order Regularization for Composition (TORC) constructs reversed and temporally shuffled frame sequences, then minimizes cosine similarity between original and reversed verb features (L_cos) to enforce discrimination of opposite temporal semantics, and maximizes the entropy of verb predictions on shuffled features (L_ent) to prevent confident verb inference from static spatial cues alone. The total training objective combines standard verb/object cross-entropy, composition cross-entropy over the expanded label space, and the two regularizers with learned hyperparameters.

## Results

On Sth-com under an open-world protocol (full Y^V × Y^O inference, no test-tuned bias), RCORE achieves 33.90% unseen composition accuracy with CLIP (vs. 30.08% for C2C) and 43.98% with InternVideo2 (vs. 39.53% for C2C), improving composition H.M. by 2.2 and 2.2 points respectively. Notably, RCORE is the only method that yields a positive compositional gap (Δ_CG) on unseen compositions (+0.66 with CLIP, +0.95 with IV2), whereas all baselines remain negative. On the sparser EK100-com dataset, RCORE further widens the gap, improving unseen composition accuracy by 6.9 points (CLIP) and 7.0 points (IV2) over C2C.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCORE consistently outperforms C2C [19] (the current SOTA) on unseen composition accuracy across both backbones and both datasets: +3.8 pts (CLIP) and +4.5 pts (IV2) on Sth-com, and +6.9 pts (CLIP) and +7.0 pts (IV2) on EK100-com. Against Jung et al. [16], RCORE gains 8.7 pts in unseen composition accuracy with CLIP on Sth-com (33.90 vs. 25.25), while Jung et al. achieves higher seen composition accuracy (48.41 vs. 44.99), reflecting a strong seen-bias in that method. The independent-modeling baselines (AIM [42] for CLIP, LoRA [13] for IV2) are substantially weaker, with unseen composition accuracy 15+ points below RCORE, confirming that conditional learning is necessary but insufficient without the proposed regularizers. In the closed-world setting (Table 3), RCORE (enhance) achieves the best H.M. (46.1) and AUC (27.5), surpassing LogicCAR [43] (45.2 / 27.0) and C2C (enhance) [19] (44.5 / 26.0). The key differentiator is that RCORE is the only method producing a positive Δ_CG on unseen compositions, indicating genuine compositional reasoning rather than independent component recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on C2C's (Li et al., ECCV 2024) component-to-composition conditional learning architecture, adding two training-time regularizers (CPR and TORC) rather than proposing a new model design. It draws on the shortcut-learning literature (Geirhos et al., Nam et al., Scimeca et al.) to frame object-driven verb prediction as a spurious-correlation problem, and on image-domain compositional zero-shot learning (CZSL) for the broader compositional generalization context. A key departure from prior ZS-CAR work is the shift from closed-world, test-set-tuned evaluation to an open-world protocol over the full verb×object space, and the introduction of diagnostic metrics (FSP/FCP) that make shortcut reliance measurable rather than merely hypothesized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central practical contribution is showing that stronger video pretraining (InternVideo2) does not eliminate object-driven shortcuts, which challenges the implicit assumption in the field that scaling the backbone solves compositional generalization. The open-world evaluation protocol and the EK100-com dataset (with only 7.5% label coverage) expose failure modes that closed-world benchmarks hide, giving the community a more honest yardstick. Limitations include the reliance on encoder-based VLMs (decoder-based models are excluded), the somewhat ad-hoc video synthesis in CPR (injecting object cues via motion masks), and evaluation on only two datasets. Open questions include whether the temporal-order regularization generalizes to longer or more complex action sequences, and whether the batch-adaptive label-space strategy scales to much larger verb-object vocabularies.
