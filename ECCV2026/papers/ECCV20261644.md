## Problem

Existing text-to-3D face generation methods jointly synthesize identity, expression, and texture, making fine-grained, independently controllable expression synthesis difficult and computationally expensive. Additionally, no public dataset provides paired fine-grained textual descriptions (both explicit feature-level and implicit context-level) with precise 3D expression parameters, leaving a critical data gap for training expression-specific models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261644/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts natural language prompts in two forms—explicit descriptions specifying observable facial features (e.g., mouth shape, eye tension) and implicit descriptions referencing evocative scenarios (e.g., 'just failed an exam')—as well as face images for multimodal supervision. It outputs a 50-dimensional FLAME expression parameter vector and a 6D jaw rotation, which are combined with a neutral identity template to yield an identity-isolated 3D head mesh. The expression is decoded deterministically in a single forward pass from the hidden state of a dedicated <Expr> token, avoiding iterative diffusion sampling.

## Method

EmoteGPT builds on LLaVA-1.5-7B (MLLM with Vicuna-v1.5 backbone) and introduces a dedicated <Expr> token in the vocabulary whose 4096-dimensional hidden state is projected by a lightweight MLP decoder (5120→5120→50, GeLU activations) into FLAME expression parameters. The LLM is fine-tuned with LoRA while the expression decoder is trained from scratch, and the model is supervised with three data streams: paired text-to-expression data from the new Txt2Emote dataset (30k samples with explicit and implicit captions generated by GPT-4o), large-scale image-to-expression data from CelebA/AffectNet/FFHQ processed with EMOCAv2, and general VQA instruction data. Training minimizes a combined objective of cross-entropy on text tokens, L2 loss on FLAME parameters, and a region-weighted L1 geometric loss on the rendered mesh. The key design insight is grounding expression as a distinct modality in language space via the <Expr> token, enabling deterministic, real-time regression rather than expensive iterative 3D generation, while multimodal image supervision strengthens the language-to-expression alignment.

## Results

On the Txt2Emote benchmark, EmoteGPT (trained with both explicit and implicit descriptions) achieves the best emotion recognition scores: V-CCC 0.68, A-CCC 0.62, and E-ACC 0.59 on explicit prompts, and V-CCC 0.57, A-CCC 0.48, E-ACC 0.44 on implicit prompts. Geometrically, it attains L1Face of 0.98 (explicit) and 1.09 (implicit), outperforming the CLIP baseline (1.07/1.24) and Vicuna-1.5-7B (1.08/1.21). Against diffusion-based text-to-3D head methods, EmoteGPT reduces L1Face from 1.51 (Portrait3D) to 1.01 on explicit prompts and from 1.74 to 1.19 on implicit prompts. In a 25-participant user study, EmoteGPT is preferred over Portrait3D in 75% of cases versus 14%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmoteGPT consistently outperforms all three regression baselines (Describe3D, CLIP, and Vicuna-1.5-7B) across every metric on both explicit and implicit prompts. Against the strongest regression baseline (CLIP trained on both description types), EmoteGPT improves V-CCC by 0.04 (0.68 vs 0.64), A-CCC by 0.06 (0.62 vs 0.56), and E-ACC by 0.07 (0.59 vs 0.52) on explicit prompts, with even larger gaps on implicit prompts (V-CCC 0.57 vs 0.49, A-CCC 0.48 vs 0.41). Against diffusion-based text-to-3D head methods, EmoteGPT achieves substantially lower geometric error: L1Face of 1.01 vs 1.51 (Portrait3D) and 1.75 (HumanNorm) on explicit prompts, and 1.19 vs 1.74/1.80 on implicit prompts (Table 3). In the perceptual user study, EmoteGPT is preferred in 75% of cases (44% strong + 31% weak) versus only 14% for Portrait3D, with 8% indifferent. Notably, EmoteGPT trained only on explicit descriptions still generalizes well to unseen implicit inputs (V-CCC 0.49, A-CCC 0.44), surpassing CLIP and Vicuna trained on both types, indicating strong cross-domain generalization from the MLLM architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant diffusion-based text-to-3D head generation paradigm (HumanNorm, Portrait3D, HeadStudio) by reframing the problem as regression in the disentangled FLAME expression parameter space, decoupling expression from identity and pose. Architecturally, it builds on LLaVA-1.5-7B as an MLLM backbone but introduces a dedicated <Expr> token whose hidden state is decoded by a lightweight MLP into 50-dim FLAME parameters, treating facial expression as a distinct modality within the language model's vocabulary. The dataset contribution (Txt2Emote) addresses a gap the authors identify in prior face-text datasets (CelebA-Dialog, MMCelebA-HQ, CelebAText), which provide only coarse or partial expression captions and lack implicit contextual descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing identity-isolated FLAME expression parameters, EmoteGPT plugs directly into existing FLAME-based avatar pipelines (GaussianAvatar, HeadStudio, DiffusionRig), enabling text-driven expressive control without retraining downstream systems. The Txt2Emote benchmark (30k samples with both explicit and implicit annotations) fills a notable data gap for fine-grained text-to-expression research. Key limitations include FLAME's limited expressiveness and incomplete identity-expression disentanglement, and reliance on GPT-4o-generated captions that may lack cultural diversity; extending the framework to richer blendshape or neural facial representations remains an open direction.
