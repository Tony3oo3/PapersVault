## Problem

Existing image manipulation localization methods extract patch-level and edge-level forensic cues with fixed parameters, causing authentic texture information to increasingly suppress critical edge-level forensic traces as tampering techniques produce more realistic manipulated content. This leads to a catastrophic imbalance of forensic evidence across regions, degrading localization accuracy especially under diverse and evolving manipulation types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261274/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 512×512 as input and produces a pixel-level binary manipulation mask (forged vs. authentic) as output. During training, it additionally generates auxiliary patch-level forgery-proportion labels (fraction of forged pixels per patch) and binary boundary masks derived from the ground-truth manipulation mask, which supervise the disentangling branches at multiple Transformer layers across all four encoder stages.

## Method

DG-Force builds on a SegFormer multi-scale Transformer backbone and introduces a Dual Forensic Disentangling and Gathering (DFDG) module inserted between Transformer layers. DFDG contains a Patch-based Forensic Disentangling (PFD) branch that applies token-independent MLPs to extract local texture cues, and an Edge-based Forensic Disentangling (EFD) branch that uses 3×3 convolutions on reshaped 2D feature maps to capture boundary-sensitive discontinuities; a lightweight per-token gating network (MLP + Softmax) then adaptively weights the two disentangled cue streams so that each spatial location emphasizes whichever cue type carries stronger forensic evidence. A Multi-scale Forensic Transfer (MFT) module further propagates patch and edge tokens from shallow to deep layers within each stage (intra-scale) and from high-resolution to coarser stages via cross-attention (cross-scale), preserving fine-grained forensic evidence through the hierarchy. Training uses a combined loss of binary cross-entropy on the final mask plus auxiliary BCE losses on patch and edge predictions at multiple supervised layers, optimized with AdamW and cosine learning-rate decay over 150 epochs.

## Results

On the four standard IML benchmarks (CASIA v1, COVERAGE, NIST16, Columbia), DG-Force achieves an average pixel-level F1 of 69.73%, IoU of 64.59%, and AUC of 94.69%, outperforming the strongest baseline (Mesorch) by 2.02%, 1.03%, and 1.8% respectively. On AI-generated tampering datasets (CoCoGlide, AutoSplice), DG-Force reaches an average F1 of 80.49% and AUC of 91.99%, improving over the best prior method (TruFor) by 3.41% F1 and 2.76% AUC. Under five common degradations (rotation, noise, JPEG, scale, color), DG-Force leads all baselines by 2.27–6.29% in average pixel-F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DG-Force outperforms all eight baselines on average across the four standard benchmarks, with the largest margin over Mesorch (the previous SOTA): +2.02% F1, +1.03% IoU, +1.8% AUC. Per-dataset, DG-Force wins on COVERAGE (F1 61.63 vs. 58.62), Columbia (F1 93.83 vs. 89.03), and NIST16 (F1 42.85 vs. 39.21), but trails Mesorch on CASIA v1 (F1 80.63 vs. 83.98), where compression-artifact-based cues dominate. On AI-generated tampering (CoCoGlide + AutoSplice), DG-Force leads TruFor by 3.41% F1 and 2.76% AUC, and SparseViT by 4.30% F1, confirming better generalization to unseen manipulation types. In robustness tests under five degradations, DG-Force surpasses Mesorch by 6.29% (rotation), 3.3% (noise), 2.27% (JPEG), 4.44% (scale), and 4.53% (color) in average pixel-F1, with the largest gap on geometric rotation. CAT-Net and MVSS-Net remain the weakest baselines across nearly all settings, while IML-ViT and SparseViT show strong AUC but lower F1/IoU, indicating calibration issues under fixed-threshold evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established IML paradigm of combining patch-level (texture/noise) and edge-level (boundary discontinuity) forensic cues, as pursued by mixed methods like TruFor, Mesorch, and ObjectFormer. Its key departure is the explicit *disentangling* of these two cue types through separate supervised branches (PFD and EFD) followed by a per-token adaptive gating mechanism, rather than the fixed-parameter fusion used by prior mixed approaches. The multi-scale Transformer backbone (SegFormer) and the CAT-Net training protocol are inherited from the broader IML literature, while the Multi-scale Forensic Transfer module extends standard skip-connection ideas with gated residual injection across both intra-scale and cross-scale hierarchies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As AI-generated manipulations (diffusion-based inpainting, instruction-driven editors) produce increasingly realistic textures, the fixed-weight fusion of forensic cues in prior methods becomes less reliable; DG-Force's adaptive reweighting offers a principled way to preserve the most informative evidence under shifting cue reliability. The consistent robustness gains across five degradation types and the strong generalization to unseen AI-generated tampering (CoCoGlide, AutoSplice) suggest the disentangling-and-gathering strategy is broadly applicable. However, the authors note that computational cost is sensitive to architectural configuration (number of active DFDG/MFT layers), and the method has not yet been validated on large pre-trained vision models, leaving open whether the adaptive gating mechanism transfers effectively to foundation-model-scale backbones.
