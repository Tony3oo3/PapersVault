## Problem

Existing controllable driving video generators either support only a subset of scene factors or inject multiple conditions through shared pathways, resulting in entangled generation that prevents independent manipulation of scene structure, object identity, and ego actions. This entanglement makes it impossible to systematically compose rare, safety-critical adversarial scenarios—such as combining common traffic elements into dangerous configurations—for stress-testing autonomous driving planners.

## Input / Output

The method takes four types of conditioning signals: (1) per-element 3D bounding box sequences (F×7) projected to 2D spatiotemporal layouts via camera intrinsics/extrinsics, (2) a single reference image per scene element for identity specification, (3) a frame-wise continuous camera trajectory (F×4×4) for ego action, and (4) a first-frame image anchoring scene identity. The output is a multi-view driving video clip of 33 frames at 10 Hz, trained at mixed resolutions of 33×256×512 and 33×480×960. Identity editing supports pose-agnostic replacement from a single reference image across diverse element poses and categories, with an inpainting region automatically created when the reference is smaller than the original element area.

## Method

CompoSIA is a Flow Matching-based DiT (initialized from Wan2.1-T2V 1.3B) with three explicitly disentangled conditioning branches injected at different levels of the diffusion process. Structure conditioning projects 3D boxes to 2D layouts, encodes them through the VAE and a lightweight convolutional adapter, and adds them via a zero-initialized projection. Identity conditioning uses a noise-level injection strategy: at high-noise timesteps (t > T_id), the latent in masked element regions is hard-replaced with the encoded reference image, trained via a select-and-repaint pairing strategy that enables pose-agnostic identity transfer from a single image. Ego action is modeled by a hierarchical dual-branch mechanism: a local residual modulation branch (AdaLN on relative frame-to-frame displacements) accelerates early convergence, while a global PRoPE attention branch operating in a reduced 1/8-dimensional subspace provides precise long-range camera trajectory guidance, both injected through zero-initialized projectors to preserve the pretrained generative prior. Training uses a 0.6:0.3:0.1 modality ratio for [action], [structure+identity+action], and unconditional inputs, with the first-frame background replaced by a clean latent to anchor scene identity.

## Results

On the identity control task, CompoSIA achieves FVD 149.15 and VBench 80.30%, outperforming the best baseline (LoRA-Edit, FVD 161.32) by ~7.5% and DriveEditor (FVD 179.57) by 17%. On action control, CompoSIA attains FVD 137.21 and VBench 80.79%, with trajectory errors of RotErr 0.55° and TransErr 7.37 (×1000), representing 30% and 47% reductions over MagicDrive-V2 (0.76°, 13.66). In the downstream planner stress-test on nuScenes, editing structure, identity, or action increases the average 3-second collision rate by up to 390% relative to following ground truth, confirming the adversarial value of the synthesized scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CompoSIA outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated task. On identity control, it beats DriveEditor [43] by 17% in FVD (149.15 vs. 179.57) and TTM [67] by 35% (149.15 vs. 231.17), while also surpassing LoRA-Edit [18] (161.32) in both FVD and VBench score. On action control, CompoSIA reduces rotation error by 30% (0.55° vs. MagicDrive-V2's 0.76°) and translation error by 47% (7.37 vs. 13.66) relative to MagicDrive-V2 [21], and by 51% and 64% relative to ReCamMaster [3] (1.12°, 20.35). Vista [23] is also clearly outperformed on action following (RotErr 0.55 vs. 0.81, TransErr 7.37 vs. 14.25) and action editing (RotErr 1.54 vs. 2.33, TransErr 12.15 vs. 28.12). Notably, MagicDrive-V2, which injects multiple conditions through shared pathways, shows the worst action-control FVD (279.61), illustrating the quality degradation from entangled conditioning that CompoSIA's disentangled design avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

CompoSIA builds on the Flow Matching DiT backbone (Wan2.1-T2V) and the broader driving world model lineage (MagicDrive-V2, Vista, GAIA-2, DriveEditor), but departs from these by explicitly decomposing conditioning into three architecturally distinct branches—spatiotemporal layout tokens, noise-level identity injection, and hierarchical dual-branch ego-action modulation—rather than injecting multiple signals through shared pathways. The identity mechanism adapts TTM's noise-level editing idea to a supervised, pose-agnostic driving setting via a select-and-repaint training strategy, while the action branch combines AdaLN residual modulation (for fast convergence) with PRoPE camera attention (for long-range precision), a pairing not seen in prior driving models. The paper positions itself against the entanglement problem in unified world models, arguing that factor-specific signals must be injected at different levels of the diffusion process to achieve truly independent control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is strongest in safety validation: by enabling systematic combination of safe elements into dangerous configurations, CompoSIA exposes planner failure modes (173% average increase in 3s collision rate) that standard benchmarks miss, offering a principled stress-testing pipeline for end-to-end driving systems. Key limitations include the semi-automatic 3D bounding box size estimation (currently relying on LLMs like Gemini) and restricted identity generalization to out-of-distribution categories due to driving-only training data. Open questions include whether the disentangled control paradigm scales to fully automatic spatial grounding, interactive refinement interfaces, and broader scene categories beyond typical traffic elements.
