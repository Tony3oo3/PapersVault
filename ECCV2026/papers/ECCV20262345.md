## Problem

Existing temporal action detection methods localize when actions occur but do not assess execution quality, while action quality assessment methods assume pre-segmented clips with known boundaries. The Ego-Exo4D proficiency demonstration benchmark requires jointly detecting individual skilled actions at the timestamp level in untrimmed ego-exo video and classifying each as good execution or tip for improvement, yet only a minimal baseline existed and the paper shows that seven state-of-the-art TAD architectures grade near-randomly (BA within 6 points of chance) on this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262345/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen Omnivore video features (C=1536) from one egocentric and four exocentric synchronized views, plus a 118-dimensional 3D body pose sequence per frame (51 raw COCO keypoint coordinates and 67 kinematic features such as joint angles, pairwise distances, and inter-frame velocities). At test time, ego pose is recovered from the Aria camera trajectory and exo pose from ViTPose 2D keypoints with calibrated multi-view RANSAC-DLT triangulation, ensuring no ground-truth leakage. The output is a set of detections, each a (timestamp in seconds, quality label ∈ {good, tip}, confidence score) triple, evaluated via greedy timestamp matching at radii {0.25, 0.5, 1.0} s.

## Method

SkillSpotter builds on an ActionFormer temporal backbone that produces an 8-level feature pyramid (D=512) and adds three task-specific modules. Bidirectional cross-view attention lets ego and exo streams (processed through a shared-weight backbone) attend to each other via 4-head multi-head cross-attention at every pyramid level before gated fusion, preventing the classification collapse observed with naive concatenation. Gated 3D body pose fusion encodes pose with a separate CNN and injects it via a learnable scalar gate (initialized to −2.0) at each pyramid level, adding skeletal kinematics as a complementary quality signal. Adaptive temporal suppression replaces fixed-radius NMS with a per-detection suppression radius predicted by a two-layer MLP conditioned on detection features and a scenario embedding, applying exponential-decay suppression only within the same quality class so that co-occurring good/tip events at the same timestamp are preserved; the module is trained end-to-end with an auxiliary binary cross-entropy loss against Gaussian targets centered on ground-truth timestamps. The full model (67.37 M parameters) is trained for 15 epochs with AdamW using a combination of sigmoid focal loss, CDF-based temporal regression loss, and the suppression auxiliary loss.

## Results

On the Ego-Exo4D demonstration proficiency benchmark (Ego view), SkillSpotter achieves class-specific mAP of 21.82, class-agnostic mAP of 27.89, balanced accuracy of 60.40%, and macro-F1 of 60.02%, improving class-specific mAP by +76% (12.40 → 21.82) and BA by +4.41 points over the strongest baseline (ActionFormer). Gains are consistent across all view settings: Exos (21.12 mAP_S, 60.59 BA) and Ego+Exos (21.34 mAP_S, 60.39 BA). On HoloAssist, SkillSpotter reaches F1=53.3, close to the SOTA DR-MoE (F1=57.0), while performing joint detection and classification on untrimmed video rather than pre-segmented clips.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkillSpotter substantially outperforms all seven re-implemented TAD baselines across every view setting, with the largest margin over the strongest baseline ActionFormer (+9.42 mAP_S, +4.41 BA on Ego). The original Ego-Exo4D benchmark baseline (mAP_S 3.27) is surpassed by a factor of ~6.7×. Notably, several baselines degrade under naive Ego+Exos feature concatenation (e.g., ActionFormer BA drops from 55.99 to 50.34), whereas SkillSpotter's cross-view attention recovers BA to 60.39, matching single-view performance. On HoloAssist, SkillSpotter (F1=53.3) trails the SOTA DR-MoE (F1=57.0) by 3.7 points but outperforms the original HoloAssist baseline (F1=36.2) by 17.1 points, while performing joint detection and classification on untrimmed video rather than pre-segmented clips. The paper also reports that SkillSpotter's adaptive suppression and pose fusion modules transfer to five other TAD backbones (TriDet, CausalTAD, VideoMambaSuite, TemporalMaxer, DyFADet), yielding consistent mAP_S gains of +8 to +11 points over their respective Soft NMS configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SkillSpotter builds on the temporal action detection (TAD) literature—specifically the ActionFormer backbone and the broader family of one-stage and DETR-style architectures unified under OpenTAD—but departs from standard TAD in three ways: it requires per-timestamp quality grading (good vs. tip), operates on synchronized multi-view ego-exo input, and must handle dense co-occurring events of different classes. It also extends action quality assessment (AQA), which assumes pre-segmented clips, into the untrimmed timestamp-localization regime. The three proposed modules (adaptive temporal suppression, gated 3D pose fusion, bidirectional cross-view attention) each address a specific gap: no prior TAD method learns activity-specific suppression radii for class-aware co-occurring events, pose fusion for skill grading in ego-exo settings is unexplored, and cross-view attention for ego-exo skill assessment had not been attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that seven state-of-the-art TAD architectures grade near-randomly (BA within 6 points of 50%) reveals a fundamental gap between temporal localization and execution-quality assessment, suggesting that detection and grading require distinct inductive biases. The first empirical inter-annotator agreement ceiling (BA 64.6, Cohen's κ = 0.29) calibrates expectations for the field and shows SkillSpotter already reaches 94% of it, implying further BA gains are bounded by label subjectivity. Open challenges remain: performance is highly uneven across scenarios (mAP_S 1.57 for Music vs. 40.76 for Basketball), fine-grained hand-object interactions are poorly captured by the current appearance-plus-body-pose representation, and effective multi-view fusion for skill grading (rather than merely preventing collapse) is still unresolved.
