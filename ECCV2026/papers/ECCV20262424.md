## Problem

Existing HOI detection methods are tightly coupled to predefined interaction vocabularies and dataset-specific supervision, limiting generalization to open-world and compositional scenarios. While recent work leverages MLLMs, these models are used primarily as feature extractors or semantic aligners rather than for open-ended interaction reasoning, leaving their multimodal reasoning capabilities underexplored for discovering and disambiguating complex interactions in the wild.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262424/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and (optionally) predefined object and action category sets as input; it also operates without any predefined vocabulary, generating arbitrary object and action labels. The MLLM is queried at the image level across three reasoning rounds, while the grounding model (GroundingDINO) is invoked per-instance for spatial localization. Output is a set of structured HOI triplets, each containing human and object bounding boxes, one or more action labels, an object category, and a combined confidence score that fuses MLLM classification confidence with grounding reliability via a tunable exponent λ.

## Method

AgentHOI is a training-free agentic framework that orchestrates an MLLM (GPT-4o or Qwen2.5-VL-72B) for open-ended semantic reasoning and GroundingDINO for spatial grounding, requiring no HOI-specific training data or parameter updates. Two key mechanisms address core challenges: (1) Context-aware Multi-round Reasoning performs three staged queries—initial HOI identification, HOI re-mining that uses previously found interactions as anchors to recover missed triplets, and action reassignment guided by object-specific fine-grained action priors (adapted from FreeA)—progressively improving interaction recall in multi-HOI scenes; (2) Multifaceted Interaction Localization prompts the MLLM to generate instance-specific descriptions integrating spatial, appearance, and semantic cues, which are then fed to the grounding model as enriched referential phrases to disambiguate visually similar human-object pairs. The final confidence score multiplicatively combines the MLLM's averaged action/object classification confidence with the geometric mean of grounding scores raised to a balancing coefficient λ.

## Results

On HICO-DET (original test set), AgentHOI achieves 29.85 mAP on Unseen Verb (UV) and 38.64 mAP on Rare-First Unseen Composition (RF-UC), outperforming the best supervised zero-shot method HOLa (27.91 and 30.61 respectively). Under distribution shifts (style transfer and image degradation), AgentHOI maintains substantially higher performance than all supervised baselines, e.g., 32.55 mAP on RF-UC under style transfer vs. 20.66 for HOLa. On the weakly-supervised HICO-DET setting, AgentHOI achieves 29.61 mAP overall and 40.33 mAP on the Rare split, surpassing the best weakly-supervised method OpenCat (25.82 and 24.35). On SWIG-HOI unseen categories, AgentHOI reaches 11.61 mAP without any HOI-specific training, exceeding CMD-SE (10.70) but trailing the fully supervised SGC-Net (12.46).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AgentHOI outperforms all supervised zero-shot methods on UV (29.85 vs. best 27.91 by HOLa) and RF-UC (38.64 vs. best 30.61 by HOLa), with margins of +1.94 and +8.03 mAP respectively. However, it trails HOLa on UO (33.70 vs. 36.45) and NF-UC (28.04 vs. 35.25), which the authors attribute to NF-UC's reliance on frequent training-distribution components and annotation incompleteness for 'no interaction' classes. Under style-transfer and degradation shifts, AgentHOI's advantage becomes decisive: e.g., on RF-UC under style transfer it scores 32.55 vs. 20.66 (HOLa), a +11.89 gap, while supervised methods drop 8–10 mAP. Against weakly-supervised baselines on the full HICO-DET split, AgentHOI (29.61) exceeds OpenCat (25.82) by +3.79 mAP and dominates the Rare split (40.33 vs. 24.35). On SWIG-HOI unseen categories, AgentHOI (11.61) surpasses CMD-SE (10.70) but remains below the fully supervised SGC-Net (12.46), indicating that training-free reasoning approaches are competitive but not yet fully on par with heavily supervised open-vocabulary detectors on large-vocabulary benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant supervised and weakly-supervised HOI detection paradigm (which learns interaction classifiers on annotated datasets) and from recent CLIP-based open-vocabulary methods that still require task-specific training. It also moves beyond prompt-based MLLM approaches that use foundation models merely as feature extractors or semantic aligners. Instead, AgentHOI reformulates HOI detection as a structured reasoning-and-grounding pipeline, orchestrating an MLLM (GPT-4o or Qwen2.5-VL-72B) for open-ended compositional reasoning and a grounding model (GroundingDINO) for spatial localization, borrowing the fine-grained object-action prior concept from FreeA [32] for its action reassignment stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AgentHOI demonstrates that no HOI-specific training data or parameter updates are needed to achieve competitive or superior detection performance, particularly on rare/long-tail categories and under distribution shifts (style transfer, image degradation) where supervised methods degrade substantially. This opens a path toward scalable, annotation-free HOI understanding in open-world settings. Key limitations include higher inference latency (multiple MLLM calls per image), slightly weaker NF-UC performance attributed to 'no interaction' annotation gaps, and evaluation primarily on HICO-DET and SWIG-HOI. Open questions include whether the agentic multi-round reasoning pattern generalizes to other structured scene-understanding tasks and how to reduce inference cost without sacrificing completeness.
