## Problem

Text-guided video editing with diffusion models is impractically slow because every frame requires tens to hundreds of denoising steps plus multi-step inversion, making even a 5-minute video take ~24 hours. One-step diffusion models have achieved competitive image quality but have never been successfully adapted to video editing, where three coupled challenges arise: inversion-generation mismatch, structural collapse from concentrating control signals into a single pass ("over-steering"), and the absence of temporal consistency mechanisms that rely on multi-step structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (sequence of RGB frames) and a natural-language editing prompt as input. Each frame is VAE-encoded into a latent, the prompt is encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder, and the output is a temporally coherent edited video at the same resolution. For long videos, processing is chunked into sliding windows of 7 frames with a stride of 5, with a globally selected anchor frame (medoid in DINO feature space) prepended to each window to prevent drift; the anchor's output is discarded after generation.

## Method

OSVE replaces multi-step inversion with a learnable encoder I_φ (initialized from the one-step U-Net) that predicts the initial noise latent in a single forward pass. The encoder is trained with a Structure-Aware Editing (SAE) loss on generator-aligned image pairs created by prompt perturbation (adding small Gaussian noise to the CLIP embedding while sharing the same initial noise), which forces the predicted latent to preserve spatial structure when decoded under a different prompt—eliminating the need for external control signals that fail in one-step generation. For temporal consistency, Unified-Frame Editing (UFE) concatenates the inverted latents of consecutive frames along the width dimension into a single tensor, allowing the U-Net's self-attention to align corresponding features across all frames in one forward pass without any auxiliary temporal module. A sliding-window strategy with a DINO-medoid anchor frame extends UFE to long videos while maintaining both local and global coherence.

## Results

On short (20-frame) videos, OSVE achieves the highest Balanced Quality Score (BQS) of 0.678, surpassing the best Multi-to-Multi baseline (FRESCO, 0.674) and all Multi-to-One baselines (best: ControlNet Depth, 0.646). On long (90-frame) videos, OSVE maintains BQS of 0.677, again outperforming all baselines. In terms of speed, OSVE runs at 15.6–15.8 FPS, approximately 171× faster than RAVE (0.091 FPS) on short videos and 155× faster on long videos (0.102 FPS), making it the first method to achieve sub-second per-frame editing latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSVE outperforms all five Multi-to-Multi baselines (FLATTEN, TokenFlow, FRESCO, RAVE, COVE) on BQS (0.678 vs. best 0.674 for FRESCO) while being 155–171× faster (15.6 FPS vs. 0.041–0.102 FPS). Against the Multi-to-One baselines, OSVE leads on BQS by a clear margin (0.678 vs. 0.646 for ControlNet Depth, 0.548 for Prompt-to-Prompt), with particularly large gaps on per-frame quality (AQ 0.678 vs. 0.658 for ControlNet Depth; IQ 0.703 vs. 0.673). Plug-and-Play, while matching OSVE on temporal flickering (TF 0.995 vs. 0.978), suffers severe per-frame degradation (IQ 0.220, BQS 0.345), confirming that naive feature injection fails in the one-step regime. On long (90-frame) videos, OSVE maintains its BQS lead (0.677 vs. 0.659 for RAVE) and speed advantage, with the sliding-window + anchor strategy preventing the temporal drift that degrades frame-wise approaches. The user study (26 participants) corroborates these findings, with OSVE scoring highest on both Temporal Consistency (3.85/5) and Visual Quality (3.65/5).

## Relation to Prior Work

OSVE sits at the intersection of two mature but previously disconnected lines of work: one-step diffusion distillation (DMD2, InstaFlow, SDXL-Turbo) and multi-step video editing (FLATTEN, TokenFlow, RAVE, COVE). Prior video editing methods all operate within an iterative denoising loop, using cross-frame attention control or noise shuffling to enforce temporal consistency, while one-step generators had been applied exclusively to image synthesis. The paper's key conceptual departure is shifting structure preservation from an external control signal applied during generation (as in Prompt-to-Prompt or ControlNet) to an internal property of the initial latent, learned via a dedicated SAE loss, and replacing iterative inversion with a single-pass learned encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 155–171× speedup over the fastest prior method (RAVE) transforms video editing from a batch-processing task (hours per video) into a near-real-time operation, opening the door to interactive and streaming applications that were previously infeasible. The broader insight—that structure can be encoded into the initial noise rather than imposed through iterative control—may generalize to other one-step editing and generation settings beyond video. Limitations include reliance on a single T2I backbone (DMD2), evaluation on relatively short clips (20–90 frames), and the fact that current one-step T2V models are not yet mature enough to serve as backbones, leaving the natural extension to native video diffusion models as an open question.
