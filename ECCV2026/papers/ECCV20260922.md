## Problem

Mainstream trajectory forecasting models are designed as conditional Gaussian mixture models but trained with a winner-take-all (WTA) loss that hard-assigns each sample to its nearest mode. This creates a modeling-training mismatch: the WTA objective is actually a K-means-like clustering criterion, causing over-segmentation of the trajectory space and uninformative mode probabilities that misrank plausible futures. No prior work had formally identified this GMM-vs-K-means disconnect or proposed principled post-hoc corrections to recover meaningful likelihoods.

## Input / Output

The method operates on existing WTA-trained forecasters (Wayformer, MTR-e2e, EMP) that take agent history trajectories and scene map context as input and produce K=64 over-complete trajectory candidates, each parameterized as a per-timestep Gaussian (mean and covariance) with an associated mixture weight. The post-hoc processing compresses these 64 modes to M=5 final trajectories for downstream planning. The critical output detail is that the mode probabilities from WTA training are unreliable—individual modes that collectively represent a dominant intention each carry only a small probability mass, making naive top-M selection by score ineffective.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a theoretical mapping showing the WTA regression term is mathematically equivalent to K-means (the optimal prediction for each mode is the mean of its hard-assigned cluster), which explains both the over-segmentation and the uninformative probabilities. Building on this, two lightweight post-hoc treatments are proposed: (1) test-time posterior-weighted merging, which iteratively clusters the 64 predicted trajectories into M centroids using weighted K-means (weights are the predicted mode probabilities), directly counteracting over-segmentation; and (2) a one-step EM update that computes soft responsibilities via Bayes' rule over the predicted Gaussians and then finetunes a small probability head on the training set to replace hard one-hot labels with soft cross-entropy supervision, yielding calibrated, spatially-aware mode posteriors. Both methods require no architectural changes or full retraining and are validated across three models on NuScenes and WOMD, showing significant improvements in minADE, minFDE, and NLL over greedy or NMS selection.

## Results

On NuScenes (M=5 final modes), clustering-based merging dramatically outperforms greedy selection across all three WTA-trained models: Wayformer minADE5 drops from 3.302 (greedy) to 1.439 (merging), MTR-e2e from 2.115 to 1.268, and EMP from 1.987 to 1.187. One-step EM finetuning further improves greedy selection by 20–31% in minFDE5 (e.g., Wayformer: 8.287→5.702) and reduces top-5 GMM NLL by 59% (4404.5→1796.4). On WOMD, merging similarly reduces Wayformer minADE5 from 4.143 (greedy) to 2.025, though the gap to direct training (1.562) is larger than on NuScenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four post-hoc selection strategies (greedy, NMS, clustering-based merging, and direct M-mode training) within three WTA-trained architectures (Wayformer, MTR-e2e, EMP). Clustering-based merging consistently outperforms both greedy selection and NMS by large margins on all displacement metrics, confirming that WTA-trained mode probabilities are uninformative for naive top-k selection. For example, on NuScenes, Wayformer's minFDE5 improves from 8.287 (greedy) to 3.050 (merging), a 63% reduction. Direct training to M modes achieves the best overall accuracy but sacrifices test-time flexibility; merging comes within 1–3% of direct training on minADE5 while retaining the ability to output any number of modes. One-step EM finetuning narrows the gap between greedy/NMS and merging: Wayformer greedy minADE5 improves from 3.302 to 2.419 (−27%), and MTR-e2e greedy from 2.115 to 1.648 (−22%), though it still trails merging. On WOMD, the advantage of merging over greedy is even more pronounced (Wayformer minADE5: 4.143→2.025), but the gap to direct training widens, suggesting dataset-dependent difficulty in recovering true modes from over-segmented predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the winner-take-all (WTA) training paradigm introduced by MultiPath (Chai et al., 2019) and widely adopted by Wayformer, MTR, MultiPath++, and EMP through a formal equivalence to the K-means clustering objective (Lloyd, 1982). Rather than proposing a new architecture, it provides a theoretical lens explaining why WTA-trained GMM outputs exhibit over-segmentation and uninformative mode probabilities, and then offers two lightweight post-hoc corrections—weighted K-means merging and one-step EM—that align inference-time probabilistic assumptions with the actual training objective. The work also unifies and contextualizes existing ad-hoc post-hoc selection strategies (greedy, NMS, clustering) that have been deployed across these models without a shared theoretical framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GMM-to-K-means reframing gives practitioners a principled explanation for a long-observed but poorly understood failure mode: why predicted mixture weights in WTA-trained forecasters are unreliable for mode ranking. The proposed corrections are practical (no retraining of the backbone, only a small finetuning of the probability head for EM) and applicable across architectures, offering a drop-in improvement for downstream planning stacks that depend on calibrated mode probabilities. Open questions include whether full iterative EM (rather than one-step) would yield further gains, whether the analysis extends to non-Gaussian output heads (e.g., implicit latent variable models), and whether the over-segmentation diagnosis holds when the number of true behavioral modes is genuinely large rather than small.
