## Problem

Pre-trained flow-based generative models lack a direct mechanism for disentangling and customizing individual visual concepts from one-shot real-world references. Existing multi-concept personalization methods like TokenVerse depend on a per-token modulation space absent from most DiT architectures and ignore the temporal dimension of concept formation—failing to address *when* a concept should be manipulated to maximize effect and minimize interference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261983/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (or video) containing one or more visual concepts, paired with a text prompt specifying the target composition, and produces compositional images at 704×704 (single-concept) or 704×1280 (multi-concept), videos at 704×1280 with 49 frames at 16 fps, or edited images via FlowEdit integration. The one-shot constraint means all concept offsets are learned from exactly one reference per concept, with data augmentation (random cropping, mirror flipping) applied during the 2000-step training.

## Method

A differential probing technique—computing the velocity-field difference between the full prompt and a partially-masked prompt retaining only one concept token—reveals a universal three-stage generative framework: a Blueprint Stage (early t) for coarse structure, an Instantiation Stage (mid t) where content concepts peak in intensity and become naturally disentangled, and a Refinement Stage (late t) for fine details. Guided by this, ConceptWeaver trains a lightweight Semantic Offset Module that learns additive offsets for each concept token's key/value representations in cross-attention, using a stage-aware flow matching loss that concentrates optimization exclusively at each concept's peak-influence stage. At inference, the ConceptWeaver Guidance (CWG) mechanism extends CFG by injecting per-concept weighted velocity shifts only during their corresponding stage—structural offsets in the Blueprint Stage, content offsets in the Instantiation Stage—providing architecture-agnostic, temporally-aligned compositional control without requiring a modulation space.

## Results

On single-concept generation, ConceptWeaver achieves the best text alignment (S_CLIP^T = 35.22), concept alignment (S_CLIP^I = 76.13), and MLLM score (S_GPT^4o = 3.80), though MS-Diffusion slightly edges it on DINO similarity (70.69 vs. 69.57). On the more challenging multi-concept composition task, ConceptWeaver leads all metrics decisively, with a particularly large margin on the GPT-5.5 composition score (4.14 vs. 3.08 for MS-Diffusion and 2.47 for TokenVerse*). In the user study, ConceptWeaver scores 4.06/5 on Concept Consistency and 4.12/5 on Aesthetic Quality, outperforming the next-best method (MS-Diffusion) by 0.31 and 0.37 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TokenVerse* (reproduced on Wan2.2), ConceptWeaver dominates across every metric, with the gap most dramatic on concept alignment (S_CLIP^I: 76.13 vs. 61.75 single; 77.06 vs. 54.89 multi) and MLLM scores, reflecting TokenVerse's reliance on a text-conditioned modulation path absent in Wan2.2. Against MS-Diffusion, the closest competitor, ConceptWeaver wins on text alignment, CLIP concept similarity, and both MLLM scores in both settings; MS-Diffusion retains a narrow 1.1-point lead on DINO similarity for single-concept (70.69 vs. 69.57), but ConceptWeaver pulls ahead by 2.4 points on DINO for multi-concept (69.02 vs. 66.59). Against DreamBooth, ConceptWeaver leads on all metrics in both settings, with the largest gap again on the multi-concept GPT-5.5 score (4.14 vs. 2.98). For video generation (R2V), ConceptWeaver is compared qualitatively against VACE-14B, where it demonstrates superior concept disentanglement and reduced copy-paste artifacts, though no quantitative video metrics are reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConceptWeaver extends the flow-matching personalization line by adding a temporal dimension that prior methods lack. TokenVerse [8] achieves multi-concept control by learning offsets in a per-token modulation space, but this requires a specific architectural feature (text-conditioned channel modulation) absent from most DiTs; ConceptWeaver instead injects learned offsets into cross-attention key/value pairs, making it architecture-agnostic. The differential probing technique is inspired by CFG's contrastive velocity computation but applied per-concept-token rather than per-prompt, revealing a three-stage generative framework (Blueprint → Instantiation → Refinement) that no prior work had explicitly characterized. For the editing application, the paper integrates with FlowEdit [22] to transfer learned concept offsets into a source image while preserving structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-stage framework provides a principled, model-agnostic window for concept manipulation that could inform other flow-based editing and personalization techniques beyond this specific method. The one-shot, architecture-agnostic design broadens applicability to any flow-matching model with cross-attention, not just Flux-family DiTs. Key limitations include the manual categorization of concepts as structural vs. content (determining which stage to target), the somewhat arbitrary 10/10/10 stage partition, and evaluation limited to a single base model (Wan2.2). The authors identify automatic learning of temporal profiles as a natural next step, and it remains open whether the three-stage structure generalizes identically across all flow architectures or is specific to the rectified-flow formulation used here.
