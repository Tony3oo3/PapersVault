## Problem

Video matting requires both high-level temporal tracking and low-level fine-grained alpha estimation, but existing methods conflate these by training end-to-end on small, expensive video matting datasets that are narrowly human-centric. This limits out-of-domain generalization and compromises the tracking robustness that large-scale VOS pretraining would otherwise provide, raising the question of whether robust video matting can be achieved without such costly annotations.

## Input / Output

Per frame, the method consumes an RGB image, a binary SAM2 mask (obtained from a single initial-frame prompt and propagated by the frozen tracker), and multi-scale feature maps extracted from the SAM2 Hiera-B+ backbone. It outputs a full-resolution alpha matte in (0,1) per pixel. Notably, the SAM2 mask acts as soft feature-level guidance rather than a hard spatial constraint, allowing the matting heads to correct tracking errors. The model supports arbitrary input resolutions with stable VRAM (~3.4 GB) and >27 FPS throughput.

## Method

SAM2Matting decouples video matting into a frozen SAM2 tracker for temporal consistency and dedicated matting components trained solely on eight diverse image matting datasets. An ROI Detector performs pixel-wise binary classification across multiple scales to identify matting-critical regions (fine details, semi-transparency), fusing image features, the raw frame, and the SAM2 mask at each scale before hierarchical aggregation. A Progressive Alpha Predictor then generates and refines the matte through a coarse-to-fine cascade, where each scale's prediction is upsampled and concatenated as guidance for the next, with deep supervision at every intermediate scale. Two tailored losses—matte-mask consistency (anchoring the thresholded matte to the SAM2 mask via focal+dice) and smooth-L1 on ROI boundaries—preserve foreground integrity and reduce jagged artifacts. The entire matting pipeline is trained with the SAM2 backbone frozen, enabling zero-shot video matting that generalizes to in-the-wild scenarios without any video matting annotations.

## Results

On video matting (the primary task), SAM2Matting achieves zero-shot state-of-the-art across all four benchmarks. On the challenging V-HIM60-Hard split, it reaches MAD 18.20 and dtSSD 5.10, surpassing the best supervised baseline MatAnyone (MAD 30.09, dtSSD 6.72) by 11.89 in MAD. On VideoMatte-SD, it attains MAD 4.83 and dtSSD 1.15, outperforming MaGGIe (MAD 5.49, dtSSD 1.39) and MatAnyone (MAD 5.15, dtSSD 1.18). On image matting, the full model achieves MAD 3.81 on P3M-500-NP (vs. MAM's 15.40) and MSE 1.59 on AM-2K (vs. Matte Anything's 3.30, a 24.8% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM2Matting outperforms all 13 baselines on every reported metric across all four video matting benchmarks, despite being evaluated zero-shot while all competitors are trained on video matting datasets. The largest margin is on V-HIM60-Hard, where it beats MatAnyone by 11.89 MAD (18.20 vs. 30.09) and MaGGIe by 3.03 MAD (18.20 vs. 21.23). On VideoMatte-SD, it edges out MatAnyone (MAD 4.83 vs. 5.15) and MaGGIe (MAD 4.83 vs. 5.49) while also achieving the lowest dtSSD (1.15 vs. 1.18 and 1.39), confirming superior temporal consistency. Raw SAM2 segmentation performs poorly across all metrics (e.g., MAD 32.29 on V-HIM60-Hard), validating that the dedicated matting components are essential. On image matting, SAM2Matting (full) reduces MAD by 11.35 over MAM on P3M-500-NP (3.81 vs. 15.40) and cuts MSE by 24.8% over Matte Anything on AM-2K (1.59 vs. 3.30). The flickering metric (F_warp) further confirms SAM2Matting's temporal stability, achieving the lowest values on all four benchmarks (e.g., 15.6 on V-HIM60-Hard vs. MatAnyone's 17.0 and MaGGIe's 24.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

SAM2Matting builds directly on SAM2 (Ravi et al., ICLR 2025) as a frozen tracking backbone, but departs fundamentally from prior video matting methods (MatAnyone, MaGGIe, RVM, FTP-VM) that jointly learn tracking and matting on expensive video matting datasets. Instead, it decouples the problem: SAM2 handles high-level temporal tracking while lightweight, image-trained matting components (a learned ROI Detector and a Progressive Alpha Predictor with cascade refinement) handle low-level alpha estimation. The ROI Detector replaces the rule-based morphological operations or raw-mask strategies used by prior matting pipelines, and the progressive multi-scale alpha refinement draws on deep supervision ideas from Mask2Former [2] and prior cascade matting architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is eliminating the need for costly pixel-level video matting annotations entirely—training on static image matting data alone yields SOTA video matting in a zero-shot setting, with real-time speed (>27 FPS) at arbitrary resolutions. The ablation showing that fine-tuning on video matting data actually degrades out-of-domain generalization (overfitting to narrow human-centric scenarios) challenges the field's assumption that more domain-specific video data is always beneficial. Limitations include degradation under extreme overexposure where visual cues are lost, and continued dependence on SAM2's initial prompt quality. Open questions include whether the decoupling paradigm extends to other fine-grained video tasks (e.g., video depth, video inpainting) and how well it handles interactive or multi-object matting settings.
