## Problem

Computational systems lack the ability to predict how objects will move in 3D space as a result of human interaction, a capability humans perform effortlessly. Existing world models operate in pixel or latent space without explicit 3D grounding, and robot manipulation datasets provide limited, scripted trajectories with explicit action supervision. No large-scale dataset of metrically grounded 3D object trajectories from in-the-wild human videos existed, and no model predicted future 6-DoF object motion directly in SE(3) space from passive egocentric observation.

## Input / Output

The model takes C=3 context frames of egocentric RGB video with corresponding monocular depth maps, an object mask in the anchor frame, and the past 6-DoF poses of the manipulated object, all expressed in anchor-frame camera coordinates to disentangle ego-motion from object motion. It outputs a distribution over H=8 future 6-DoF poses (translation plus 6D rotation) in the same anchor-frame coordinates, sampled via DDIM to produce diverse, physically coherent trajectories. Internally, poses are reparameterized into depth-normalized 9D tokens (u, v, log z, r_6D) to reduce dynamic range and improve numerical stability during diffusion.

## Method

ObjectForesight combines a PointTransformerV3 scene encoder with a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) temporal backbone. The anchor-frame depth map is backprojected into a point cloud, encoded by PTV3 with FiLM conditioning on a motion-context vector (obtained via anchor-query attention over past pose tokens and bounding boxes), and pooled into a 512D geometry-aware scene embedding injected into DiT blocks via AdaLN-Zero. The DiT denoises a length-H sequence of depth-normalized 9D pose tokens using v-parameterization with a cosine noise schedule, p2 SNR reweighting, and horizon-aware weighting that emphasizes later forecast steps. Training additionally applies auxiliary SE(3) losses on decoded poses (geodesic rotation error, translation error, velocity and acceleration smoothness) weighted by the diffusion signal-to-noise ratio. A large-scale data curation pipeline converts 2M+ EPIC-Kitchens clips into metrically grounded trajectories using EgoHOS, SAM2, TRELLIS, FoundationPose, and SpaTrackerV2, with VLM-based quality gating and bidirectional tracking with re-registration.

## Results

On EPIC-Kitchens, ObjectForesight-DiT achieves the best overall performance with an ADE of 0.016 m, FDE of 0.029 m, and ARE of 2.30°, outperforming the Constant Velocity baseline by 41% on ADE and 45% on FDE. On HOT3D-Clips (larger, more complex motions), ObjectForesight-DiT again leads with 0.021 m ADE and 8.92° ARE, while Constant Velocity degrades severely (0.136 m ADE, 38.70° ARE). In a direct comparison against the Luma AI Ray3 video-generation model on a 20-video subset, ObjectForesight-DiT reduces ADE from 0.084 m to 0.029 m and ARE from 12.86° to 7.29°, demonstrating the advantage of predicting motion directly in SE(3) rather than recovering poses from synthesized frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ObjectForesight-DiT wins on every metric against both the Constant Velocity baseline and its own autoregressive variant (ObjectForesight-AR) on both EPIC-Kitchens and HOT3D-Clips. Against Constant Velocity, the gains are most dramatic on HOT3D-Clips, where ADE drops from 0.136 m to 0.021 m (85% reduction) and ARE from 38.70° to 8.92°, indicating that complex multi-modal motions cannot be captured by linear extrapolation. The autoregressive variant actually underperforms Constant Velocity on most EPIC-Kitchens metrics (e.g., ADE 0.067 m vs. 0.027 m), suggesting that without the diffusion formulation the model fails to capture the inherent one-to-many nature of future object motion. Against the Luma AI Ray3 video-generation baseline (evaluated on a 20-video subset due to computational cost), ObjectForesight-DiT achieves roughly 3× lower ADE (0.029 m vs. 0.084 m) and 43% lower ARE (7.29° vs. 12.86°), reinforcing that explicit SE(3) trajectory prediction is more accurate than recovering 6-DoF poses from synthesized video frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

ObjectForesight sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) 3D object pose estimation and reconstruction (TRELLIS, FoundationPose, SpaTrackerV2), which it repurposes as a data-curation pipeline rather than a prediction target; (2) affordance and manipulation-cue learning from human videos, which typically predicts 2D contact maps or categorical actions rather than continuous SE(3) trajectories; and (3) world/dynamics models that operate in pixel or latent space, which lack explicit 3D grounding. The core prediction architecture is a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with AdaLN-Zero conditioning, combined with a PointTransformerV3 geometry encoder, making the methodological contribution primarily one of integration and task-specific design (depth-normalized pose tokens, FiLM motion conditioning, v-parameterization with p2 reweighting) rather than a new architectural primitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a new task formulation—predicting future 6-DoF object trajectories from passive egocentric human video—and provides the first large-scale dataset (2M+ pseudo-ground-truth trajectories) to support it, which is a significant resource for the embodied-AI community. The finding that an autoregressive transformer underperforms a simple Constant Velocity baseline highlights the critical importance of modeling the inherent multimodality of future object motion, a point that may influence how similar prediction tasks are formulated. Key limitations include rigid-object-only modeling, short 8-frame horizons, and reliance on pseudo-ground-truth from a multi-stage pipeline whose error compounds; open questions include extending to articulated/deformable objects, longer horizons, and integrating predictions into closed-loop manipulation policies.
