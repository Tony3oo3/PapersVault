## Problem

Personalized image retouching requires adapting to individual user preferences from a few reference examples, but existing methods either demand user-specific fine-tuning for each new user or rely on weak style representations (classification, metric learning, simple auto-encoding) that entangle retouching style with image content. This prevents faithful transfer of a user's color and tone adjustments to unseen query images, and none of these approaches can coherently fuse styles from multiple references in a content-aware manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query RGB image and a small set of user-provided reference pairs (original + retouched versions, typically 1–100 pairs) as input, producing a retouched RGB image at the same resolution. The decoder operates per-pixel in RGB color space (R³→R³), making it resolution-agnostic; training uses 384×384 random crops while evaluation preserves original resolution (512×512 for VCIRB, native resolution for MIT-FiveK and PPR10K). For photorealistic style transfer, the content image is reused as a pseudo-input paired with the unpaired style reference as target, requiring no architectural change.

## Method

RefRetouch employs an asymmetric auto-encoder: a Siamese SigLIPv2 encoder with LoRA adapters (rank 16) processes the (original, retouched) pair and projects pooled features into a 2048-dim content-disentangled style latent, while a lightweight three-layer conditional MLP decoder (hidden dims 256→512→3, per-pixel in color space) reconstructs the retouched image from the original plus the latent. The asymmetry—expressive encoder paired with a simple per-pixel decoder—forces the latent to encode only color and tone transformations independent of scene semantics. For multi-reference personalization, Retrieval-Augmented Retouching (RAR) computes cosine similarity between the query's and references' content embeddings (from the same LoRA-adapted SigLIPv2 backbone), retrieves the top-K most relevant pairs, and aggregates their style latents via a softmax-weighted average (temperature τ=0.1, K=3) before passing the composite latent to the decoder. Training minimizes L1 reconstruction loss over 180K steps on 800 Adobe Lightroom presets applied to 95K LAION images, with no user-specific fine-tuning at test time.

## Results

On the VCIRB single-style benchmark (1 reference), RefRetouch achieves PSNR 29.13 / SSIM 0.963 / LPIPS 0.048, outperforming the strongest baseline MSM† (24.35 / 0.900 / 0.097) by ~4.8 dB in PSNR. On PPR10K-groups with 6 consistent references, it reaches PSNR 25.27 / SSIM 0.961 / LPIPS 0.043, again leading all baselines (best: VisualCloze† at 20.98 / 0.812 / 0.112). On the diverse-style MIT-FiveK benchmark with 100 references, RefRetouch attains PSNR 23.34 / SSIM 0.920 / LPIPS 0.067, matching or exceeding training-based methods RSFNet (22.66 / 0.86 / 0.072) and AdaInt (22.49 / 0.854 / 0.078) without any user-specific fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RefRetouch decisively outperforms all tuning-free generic personalization baselines (StarEnhancer, MSM, VisualCloze) across every benchmark and reference count, with PSNR gaps of 4–18 dB depending on the setting. Against the re-trained variants (MSM†, VisualCloze†), the advantage narrows but remains consistent: on VCIRB with 4 references, RefRetouch leads by ~5 dB PSNR over MSM† and ~5 dB over VisualCloze†. On the diverse-style MIT-FiveK and PPR10K-user benchmarks (Table 4), RefRetouch matches or slightly exceeds the training-based methods RSFNet and AdaInt on SSIM while being competitive on PSNR/LPIPS, all without any user-specific fine-tuning. On PPR10K with 100 references, AdaInt edges out RefRetouch by 0.35 dB PSNR and 0.005 LPIPS, though RefRetouch retains the SSIM lead (0.942 vs 0.932). For photorealistic style transfer (Table 2), RefRetouch achieves a Style Score of 0.917, matching PhotoWCT2 (0.916) while trailing on Content Score (0.742 vs 0.913 for DeepPreset), indicating strong style fidelity at a modest cost to structural preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of autoencoding, metric learning, and retrieval-augmented generation for the specific problem of personalized image retouching. It departs from prior personalized retouching methods (StarEnhancer, PieNet, MSM) that rely on style classification, contrastive embeddings, or U-Net reconstruction—each of which entangles style with content or cannot handle multi-reference diverse styles—by proposing an asymmetric autoencoder where a deliberately simple per-pixel MLP decoder forces the Siamese encoder to produce truly content-disentangled style latents. The retrieval-augmented retouching (RAR) module further distinguishes the work by borrowing the retrieve-then-aggregate paradigm from RAG, applying it to select and weight reference styles based on content similarity rather than averaging all references globally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating per-user fine-tuning entirely, RefRetouch makes personalized retouching practical for consumer devices (the paper is from vivo BlueImage Lab), where retraining a model for each user is infeasible. The finding that a simple per-pixel decoder outperforms a UNet decoder for style disentanglement offers a useful architectural principle: constraining the decoder's capacity can improve the quality of the learned latent representation. Key limitations remain: the method only performs global (uniform) retouching and cannot handle region-aware or spatially varying edits, and the retrieval step, while effective, introduces a dependency on the quality of the content embedding for selecting relevant references. Whether the RAR paradigm generalizes to other content-adaptive editing tasks beyond retouching is an open question.
