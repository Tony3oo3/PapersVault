## Problem

Diffusion-based category-level pose estimation achieves strong accuracy but requires many iterative denoising steps, making real-time inference (>30 FPS) impractical for robotic manipulation. Additionally, applying standard Euclidean flow matching to the 9-DoF pose space corrupts rotational structure because valid rotations reside on the non-Euclidean SO(3) manifold, causing geometric collapse during iterative ODE integration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260194/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image, a segmented depth point cloud (from an upstream Mask R-CNN), and a category prior feature vector, processed by ResNet-18 and PointNet encoders respectively. It outputs a full 9-DoF pose — an SO(3) rotation matrix, a 3D translation vector, and a 3D scale vector — generated in just 3 ODE steps from a near-identity 'warm start' prior (R=I, T=0, S=0) rather than from isotropic Gaussian noise, keeping the short trajectory stable.

## Method

G2FM decouples the pose into an SO(3)×R³×R³ product manifold and defines a geodesic flow where rotations are transported along SO(3) geodesics via exp/log maps while translation and scale follow linear interpolation. The conditional vector field has a closed-form constant tangent-space representation (log(R₁R₀ᵀ), T₁−T₀, S₁−S₀), so the network learns a deterministic velocity field through a simulation-free Conditional Flow Matching loss, enabling convergence in only 3 ODE steps. At inference, a training-free geometric guidance mechanism computes Chamfer distance gradients between the transformed canonical model and observed points, projects the rotation gradient onto the so(3) tangent space to obtain a valid angular velocity correction, and subtracts it from the predicted velocity at each ODE step — injecting structural priors without any retraining.

## Results

On REAL275 with an ImageNet-pretrained segmenter, G2FM achieves 80.0% 3D₅₀ and 44.8% 3D₇₅ (IoU3D), outperforming the strongest diffusion baseline Diff9D (76.5%, 41.7%) while running at 31.5 FPS versus Diff9D's 19.2 FPS. On Wild6D, G2FM reaches 77.54% 3D₅₀, marginally ahead of Diff9D (76.48%). In the extreme zero-shot transfer to Omni6DPose (trained only on 6 CAMERA25 categories), G2FM attains 52.91% 3D₂₅, whereas Diff9D collapses to 0.72%, demonstrating the robustness of the SO(3) geodesic formulation under severe domain shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary diffusion baseline Diff9D [39], G2FM wins on the IoU-based metrics 3D₅₀ (80.0 vs 76.5) and 3D₇₅ (44.8 vs 41.7) on REAL275, and on 3D₅₀ on Wild6D (77.54 vs 76.48), while Diff9D retains a slight edge on 5°5cm (43.9 vs 41.2) and 10°5cm (70.0 vs 67.6) on REAL275. The gap widens dramatically under zero-shot transfer to Omni6DPose, where G2FM scores 52.91% 3D₂₅ versus Diff9D's 0.72%, confirming that the SO(3) geodesic path prevents the geometric collapse that plagues Euclidean diffusion. Compared to the regression baseline SecondPose [9], G2FM achieves higher 3D₅₀ (80.0 vs 76.0) on REAL275 and is nearly 2× faster (31.5 vs 16.4 FPS). Against earlier discriminative methods (SPD, SGPA, STG6D, SAR-Net), G2FM leads on 3D₅₀ by 11–32 points and on 3D₇₅ by 27–30 points, though SGPA and SAR-Net retain small advantages on the loosest 10°5cm threshold. On Wild6D, G2FM edges out Diff9D on 3D₅₀ and 10°5cm but trails slightly on 3D₇₅ (36.20 vs 38.16) and 5°2cm (23.18 vs 25.52), suggesting the geodesic formulation trades a small amount of fine rotation-translation precision for superior global pose and size consistency. In terms of speed, G2FM at 31.5 FPS with only 3 ODE steps is 1.6× faster than Diff9D (19.2 FPS) and 4.7× faster than DiffusionNOCS (6.77 FPS), making it the only method in the comparison that meets real-time robotic manipulation requirements.

## Relation to Prior Work

G2FM builds directly on Conditional Flow Matching (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) as its generative backbone, replacing the multi-step denoising of diffusion-based pose estimators (Diff9D, GenPose, DiffusionNOCS) with a few-step ODE solver. It extends the Riemannian flow matching line (Chen & Lipman 2024; RFMPose, NeurIPS 2025) from 6-DoF rigid-body SE(3) to the more complex 9-DoF category-level setting by decoupling the pose into an SO(3)×R³×R³ product manifold and parameterizing rotation as a Lie-algebra velocity along geodesic paths. The key conceptual departure from prior FM work is the 'warm start' near-identity prior and the plug-and-play training-free Chamfer-distance guidance that projects structural corrections onto the SO(3) tangent space without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that respecting the SO(3) geometry during the flow trajectory—not merely at the endpoint—is critical for robustness under extreme domain shift, as evidenced by the near-total collapse of Euclidean FM on Omni6DPose zero-shot. The 3-step ODE inference at >30 FPS makes generative pose estimation practical for latency-sensitive robotic manipulation, a regime where diffusion models have been infeasible. Open questions remain: the method still depends on an upstream segmenter, symmetric objects are not explicitly handled, and the encoder ablation showing that DINOv2/SigLIP2 underperform ResNet18 suggests foundation-model features need task-specific adapters before they can benefit 9-DoF pose estimation.
