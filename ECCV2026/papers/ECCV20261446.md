## Problem

Existing typographic-attack robustness evaluations for LVLMs are object-centric, making it impossible to distinguish whether a defense achieves robustness by selectively ignoring inserted distractor text or by broadly suppressing all text sensitivity. This is a critical oversight because real-world LVLMs must simultaneously recognize objects and read scene text (e.g., reading traffic signs while detecting pedestrians), so a defense that simply ignores all text is unacceptable in practice.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs an RGB image (sourced from TextVQA/Open Images) with a text question that is either object-centric (multiple-choice with four options, or open-ended object naming) or text-centric (open-ended scene-text reading). The same image is evaluated under four counterfactual conditions by crossing question intent with text condition (clean vs. attacked), where attacks insert distractor text via randomized digital overlays or scene-coherent generative rendering. Outputs are scored with task-specific metrics: standard accuracy for MC, a novel R-CLIP-M retrieval metric for open-ended object answers (which explicitly penalizes retrieving the attack word), and standard VQA accuracy for text answers.

## Method

RIO-Bench is constructed by leveraging dual annotations on identical TextVQA images: original text-reading Q&A pairs form the Text-Clean subset, while Open Images object labels are used to generate paired object-centric questions (MC with hierarchy-based negative sampling and open-ended) for the Obj-Clean subset. Attacked variants are created by inserting distractor text outside OCR-detected regions, using two complementary attack types: controlled digital overlays with semantic/spatial difficulty levels, and SceneTAP scene-coherent generative insertions. The proposed RIO-RT defense performs LoRA-based supervised fine-tuning on a balanced mixture of Obj-Attack and Text-Attack instances (16K samples, 1 epoch), directly optimizing the dual read-or-ignore objective, in contrast to IT-RT which trains only on Obj-Attack and thus learns to suppress text sensitivity broadly. Ablations show that the read-or-ignore behavior is primarily governed by the language model component rather than the vision encoder, and attention visualizations confirm that RIO-RT selectively attends to relevant text while disregarding distractors, rather than globally suppressing text sensitivity.

## Results

On RIO-Bench, RIO-RT achieves near-perfect object-attack robustness (98.2–99.6% Obj-MC Attack AVG across six LVLMs) while preserving or improving scene-text reading (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B Text-VQA Clean rises from 49.5→51.4, Text-Attack AVG from 45.1→52.8). In contrast, the text-ignoring baseline IT-RT reaches comparable Obj-Attack scores (98.4–99.6%) but catastrophically degrades Text-VQA (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B Text-Clean drops 49.5→30.3, Text-Attack AVG 45.1→23.3; LLaVA-1.5-13B Text-Clean 54.5→15.3). The inference-time CoT defense [7] yields only marginal robustness gains (e.g., 70.0 vs. 71.6 Obj-MC Attack on LLaVA-7B) while typically reducing Text-VQA accuracy by 3–16%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is CoT defense [7] (Cheng et al., ECCV 2024), the only prior LVLM-focused typographic-attack defense. CoT provides at best marginal robustness gains (e.g., +11% Obj-MC Attack on Llama-3.2-Vision, but −1.6% on LLaVA-1.5-7B) while consistently degrading Text-VQA by 3–16% across all six models, because its object-centric prompting shifts attention away from scene text. The paper's IT-RT baseline (adapted from Defense-Prefix [2]) achieves the highest Obj-Attack scores (98–100% MC) but at a severe cost: Text-VQA Clean accuracy drops by 19–75% (e.g., 54.5→15.3 on LLaVA-1.5-13B), confirming that object-centric robustness training induces blanket text suppression. RIO-RT, the paper's proposed data-driven defense, matches IT-RT on Obj-Attack within 0.5–1.5% while preserving or improving Text-VQA (e.g., +7.7% Text-Attack AVG on LLaVA-7B, +6.1% on Llama-3.2-Vision), demonstrating that balanced read-and-ignore training avoids the text-ignoring failure mode. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B, RIO-RT also surpasses the original model's Text-Attack accuracy (84.8 vs. 77.7 and 84.6 vs. 78.5), indicating that the training signal generalizes beyond mere robustness to improved text reading under distractors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the typographic-attack robustness literature (Goh et al., TypoD, SCAM, SceneTAP) and the defense line (Defense-Prefix, CLIP-M, CoT prompting) by arguing that their object-centric evaluation scope is fundamentally misaligned with real-world LVLM requirements. It builds on TextVQA and Open Images dual annotations to construct same-scene counterfactual pairs, and adapts CLIP-M into the attack-aware R-CLIP-M metric. The key conceptual departure is framing robustness not as 'ignore all text' but as context-dependent read-or-ignore behavior, a requirement absent from prior protocols that evaluated robustness and scene-text reading on separate scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical blind spot: object-centric defenses can achieve near-perfect robustness (e.g., 98%+ on Obj-Attack) by simply suppressing text sensitivity, at the cost of 40–75% drops in scene-text reading—something invisible under prior evaluation. This has direct implications for deployment in autonomous driving, embodied AI, and accessibility, where models must simultaneously recognize objects and read signs or instructions. Open questions include whether the read-or-ignore behavior generalizes beyond VQA to generation or action-planning tasks, whether the LoRA-based RIO-RT recipe scales to larger models or more diverse attack distributions, and whether the language-model-centric finding (vision-only tuning fails) holds across architectures.
