## Problem

Holograms encode dense, high-frequency interference and diffraction patterns that are poorly captured by conventional image representations: implicit neural representations favor smooth low-frequency content, autoencoder priors trained on natural images do not generalize to hologram structures, and per-pixel CGH optimization incurs a large, non-scalable parameter search space. While Gaussian primitives have been applied to natural image encoding and 3D novel-view-synthesis CGH, no prior work has exploited them for compact single-hologram optimization with explicit wave-optical propagation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target RGB image (3×1024×640) and an optional monocular depth map (from Depth Anything v2 or MiDaS) and produces a complex-valued hologram field (amplitude and phase per channel) parameterized by N complex 2D Gaussians at a 5:1 reduction ratio (0.8M vs. 4.0M dense per-pixel parameters). The complex hologram is then converted to device-compatible formats: a Smooth POH via double-phase-amplitude coding on a checkerboard pattern, or a Random POH via structural-guided phase optimization. Reconstructions are evaluated at L=2 or 3 parallel depth planes spaced 2 mm apart, centered at a 3 mm propagation distance, using 639/532/473 nm wavelengths and a 3.74 µm pixel pitch.

## Method

Each complex-valued 2D Gaussian primitive is parameterized by 12 values (2D position, 2D scale, rotation, C-channel color amplitude, C-channel phase, and opacity), unifying amplitude and phase in a single primitive rather than pairing two real-valued Gaussians, which reduces parameters by 1/3 and enforces spatial alignment. The design is grounded in Gabor's uncertainty principle: the 2D Gaussian attains the minimum space-frequency uncertainty bound, making it the most compact basis for representing holograms whose Fourier content defines reconstruction angles. A custom CUDA rasterizer accumulates primitives into the complex hologram field, and a GPU-optimized BLASM kernel performs free-space propagation in the frequency domain, achieving 50% faster optimization and 30% lower VRAM than PyTorch. Training supervises multi-plane focal-stack reconstructions with a combined reconstruction loss (MSE + masked terms from Kavakli et al.) and SSIM loss, using the Adan optimizer over 2000 steps. For Random POH conversion, the complex hologram serves as structural guidance: both the complex field and a randomly initialized phase-only field are propagated in parallel, and a joint loss couples their reconstructions in both intensity and complex-field domains.

## Results

On the DIV2K test set (50 images, 3×1024×640 resolution), the method achieves 29.0 dB PSNR / 0.81 SSIM / 0.38 LPIPS for Smooth POH and 29.4 dB PSNR / 0.81 SSIM / 0.34 LPIPS for Random POH, using only 0.8M parameters (5:1 reduction over dense per-pixel). The CUDA implementation reduces VRAM by 29–36% and accelerates optimization by 40–50% over PyTorch, while rendering at 2.13 ms per frame—approximately 3200× faster than Gaussian Wave Splatting (6840 ms). Under a 10:1 parameter reduction, PSNR degrades gracefully to 29.4 dB, confirming the Gabor-atom efficiency claim.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against learned CGH methods, the proposed approach outperforms NH3D [15] by +0.7 dB PSNR while using 5× fewer parameters (0.8M vs 3.9M) and 3× less VRAM (2.4G vs 7.1G), with training time reduced from 90 min to 1.4 min. Compared to TensorV2 [51], it gains +1.9 dB PSNR at a fraction of the memory (2.4G vs 8.7G) and 57× faster training. Against GWS [13], the method achieves +0.8 dB PSNR and is ~3200× faster at rendering (2.13 ms vs 6840 ms), though GWS uses slightly less VRAM (2.5G vs 2.4G). For Random POH, the method surpasses Wirtinger Holography [8] by +4.1 dB PSNR and +0.34 SSIM, and outperforms Naive Opt and Multi-color [26] by +9.1 dB and +9.1 dB PSNR respectively, demonstrating that structural guidance from the complex-valued Gaussian representation substantially improves per-pixel phase optimization. In the representation comparison (Table 1), the method achieves 30.7 dB PSNR, exceeding the best Gaussian-based natural-image method (Instant-GI [70], 23.5 dB) by +7.2 dB and all learned representations (MLP, SIREN, TAESD) by 19+ dB, confirming that explicit wave-optics-aware Gaussian primitives are essential for hologram fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the complex-valued holographic radiance field formulation [66] from volumetric 3D scenes (multi-view NVS) to a single-hologram optimization setting, replacing 3D Gaussians with 2D complex-valued primitives on the SLM plane. It adapts the tile-based differentiable rasterizer architecture from 3D Gaussian Splatting [28] to handle complex-valued contributions in a single pass, and adopts the multi-plane reconstruction loss from Kavakli et al. [25] for depth-aware supervision. Conceptually, the key departure from natural-image Gaussian representations [64,69,70] is the explicit integration of free-space light propagation (BLASM) during optimization, and the invocation of Gabor's minimum space-frequency uncertainty to justify why Gaussians are the theoretically optimal primitive for encoding holographic interference patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5:1 parameter reduction with graceful quality degradation (down to 10:1) offers a practical path toward scalable hologram estimation on resource-constrained hardware, and the 3200× rendering speedup over GWS makes real-time or near-real-time CGH more feasible. The Gabor-theoretic grounding elevates the contribution beyond a purely empirical representation choice, suggesting that further compression or learned pruning of Gaussian primitives could be principled. Open questions include whether the representation can directly render Random POH without the indirect conversion step, whether it extends to feedforward video-rate holography, and how it interacts with multiplexing and camera-in-the-loop techniques for perceptual quality improvement.
