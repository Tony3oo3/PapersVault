## Problem

Existing AVQA systems fuse text, video, and audio through deeply stacked sequential cross-attention layers, where each layer can only interact two modalities at a time. This sequential stacking causes subtle inter-modal cues to be lost at earlier layers and errors to accumulate across depth, degrading three-way reasoning. The paper asks whether a shallow, parallel fusion design can capture the same cross-modal interactions without the information loss inherent in deep attention stacks.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video segmented into T contiguous 2-second clips, from which it extracts frame-level CLIP embeddings (D=1024), patch-level CLIP tokens (P=256 patches at dim 1280 per frame), and frame-aligned ImageBind audio embeddings (D=1024), alongside a single question encoded as a CLIP sentence embedding. A question-guided token filtering step first reduces the patch tokens to a keep ratio of 50% before any attention is applied. The output is a single answer class label produced by a classification head over the fused representation.

## Method

Q-TriM replaces deep sequential attention stacks with a single parallel fusion stage built on a Conditional Similarity Score (CSS) framework that defines a joint distribution over video and audio frames conditional on the question text. Unrolling the conditional expectation under this distribution yields not only standard cross-attention (Attn(t,V,V) and Attn(t,A,A)) but also a novel Tri-Modal Attention operator where Query, Key, and Value are drawn from three distinct modalities (e.g., Attn(t,A,V)), enabling direct three-way interaction in one step. Four such attention paths are computed in parallel, their keys and values are modulated by the question via FiLM, and their outputs are combined with question-conditioned gating scalars. A lightweight token-filtering module (dot-product scoring + straight-through estimator, no attention) prunes irrelevant patches before fusion, and a final self-attention block over the concatenated gated outputs feeds a classification layer.

## Results

On the MUSIC-AVQA-R benchmark (which probes robustness on rare/out-of-distribution answer categories), Q-TriM achieves 70.89% overall accuracy, surpassing the previous best QA-Tiger (67.99%) by 2.9 points. On the standard MUSIC-AVQA test set, Q-TriM reaches 77.68% overall, edging out QA-Tiger (77.62%) and TSPM (76.79%). On MUSIC-AVQA-v2.0, Q-TriM attains 78.40% on the bias test and 75.31% on the balanced test (both trained on the biased split), outperforming QA-Tiger by 1.5 and 1.4 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most direct competitor is QA-Tiger [13], which uses four sequentially stacked cross-attention blocks with question-aware Gaussian experts. Q-TriM outperforms QA-Tiger on MUSIC-AVQA-R by a substantial 2.9 points (70.89% vs. 67.99%), on MUSIC-AVQA by a narrow 0.06 points (77.68% vs. 77.62%), and on MUSIC-AVQA-v2.0 bias test by 1.5 points (78.40% vs. 76.93%); however, on the v2.0 balanced test with balanced training, Q-TriM trails QA-Tiger by a small 0.14-point margin (76.29% vs. 76.43%). Against TSPM [17], which uses prompt-guided temporal and spatial selection, Q-TriM leads by 4.6 points on MUSIC-AVQA-R (70.89% vs. 66.30%) and 0.9 points on MUSIC-AVQA (77.68% vs. 76.79%). The robustness-focused MCCD [27] is surpassed by 3.9 points on MUSIC-AVQA-R (70.89% vs. 66.95%). Among earlier methods, LAVISH [22] (74.46%), PSTP-Net [18] (73.52%), and COCA [15] (72.33%) on MUSIC-AVQA are all clearly outperformed, with Q-TriM gaining 3.2, 4.2, and 5.4 points respectively. The largest single-category gain appears in Visual QA Local on MUSIC-AVQA-R, where Q-TriM reaches 89.45% (Head) / 78.95% (Tail) versus QA-Tiger's 84.76% / 75.59%, suggesting the shallow parallel Tri-Modal attention better preserves spatial cues that would otherwise degrade through deep sequential stacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

Q-TriM departs from the dominant AVQA paradigm of deeply stacked cross-attention blocks (e.g., QA-TIGER's four sequential cross-attention layers) by proposing a single-stage, parallel fusion of text, video, and audio. The Tri-Modal Attention operator—where Query, Key, and Value are drawn from three distinct modalities—is derived from a Conditional Similarity Score framework that unifies standard cross-attention and the new tri-modal form as special cases of a 2-D softmax distribution. The token-filtering module repurposes the straight-through estimator from vision-transformer efficiency literature (DynamicViT, A-ViT) for question-guided patch selection, while CLIP and ImageBind serve as frozen multi-modal backbones, positioning the method within the transfer-learning AVQA lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the implicit assumption in multimodal fusion that deeper sequential attention stacks are necessary for capturing inter-modal dependencies, showing that a shallow parallel design can match or exceed them while reducing error accumulation. The substantial margin on MUSIC-AVQA-R (70.89% vs. 67.99% for QA-TIGER) suggests the approach is more robust to distribution shift, which is critical for real-world deployment. However, evaluation is confined to music-domain AVQA benchmarks, leaving open whether the tri-modal attention primitive and shallow-parallel philosophy generalize to broader video-audio-text tasks (e.g., egocentric action, general scene understanding). The authors note future work on exploring additional CSS score variants and extending to other multi-modal tasks.
