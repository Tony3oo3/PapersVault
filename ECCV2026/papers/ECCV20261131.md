## Problem

Existing test-time training (TTT) methods for videos rely on sliding windows that reprocess all k frames at every timestep, making compute grow linearly with window size and become intractable for hours-long videos. Additionally, TTT is wastefully triggered even when temporally adjacent frames are nearly identical, and prior benchmarks for this problem contain only ~5-minute clips, leaving the multi-hour regime unexplored.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a streaming video frame-by-frame (no future access) along with a timestep index injected via a RoPE-encoded temporal module. Depending on the frozen downstream head, it outputs per-pixel segmentation masks (instance, panoptic, or semantic), per-pixel depth maps, or a single action-class label. A key operational detail is that per window transition the model processes only three frames—the exiting frame, the current frame, and the next frame—rather than the full window, and the SSL head is trained to predict the next frame rather than reconstruct the current one.

## Method

FFN exploits the sliding-window invariant (only one frame exits and one enters per step) to replace full-window recomputation with a three-frame 'forget–anticipate–adapt' procedure. The Memory Restoration Mechanism (MRM) conditions the backbone on the exiting frame's timestep via a 3-layer MLP with RoPE positional encoding, then performs a single gradient step to restore pre-adaptation features (L2 loss), effectively 'forgetting' that frame's influence. The Adaptive Window Algorithm (AWA) uses an anticipative head to predict the next frame and computes a surprise metric S_t = log(1 + v_visual) × (1 − A), where v_visual is normalized pixel difference and A is cosine similarity between the current latent feature and the most-recently-adapted latent feature stored in a memory buffer. An adaptive threshold τ_t = μ_t + σ_t (mean plus one standard deviation of recent surprise values) dynamically gates whether TTT is triggered, so the model performs a full backpropagation step only when genuinely new information arrives, otherwise it executes a single forward pass. This yields 0.7 s/frame versus 4.1 s/frame for the full sliding-window TTT baseline while improving segmentation accuracy.

## Results

On COCO-Videos, FFN achieves 45.1 AP for instance segmentation and 29.6 PQ for panoptic segmentation, surpassing the strongest streaming baseline Online TTT-MAE (sliding window) by +7.5 and +7.9 respectively, and even exceeding the offline oracle (33.6/19.6) that has access to the entire video. On KITTI-STEP, FFN reaches 57.3 mIoU (val) and 59.5 mIoU (test), outperforming Self-Train (54.7/54.0) and TTT-Online (55.4/54.3) while running at 0.7 s/frame versus 4.1 s/frame for TTT-Online. On the proposed EpicTours dataset (up to 3-hour videos), FFN gains +6.5 mIoU for semantic and +5.3 AP for instance segmentation over TTT-Online. For video depth estimation, FFN achieves the best AbsRel across all six benchmarks (e.g., 0.059 on KITTI, 0.049 on NYUv2), beating both TTT-Online and the zero-shot Video Depth-Anything model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FFN decisively outperforms the primary TTT baseline (Online TTT-MAE with sliding window) on every segmentation metric: +7.5 AP on COCO-Videos instance, +7.9 PQ on panoptic, +1.9 mIoU on KITTI-STEP val, and +5.2 mIoU on test, while also being ~6× faster (0.7 s vs 4.1 s per frame). Against TTA baselines, FFN substantially exceeds Tent (16.6/14.6 on COCO) and Self-Train (54.7/54.0 on KITTI), confirming that self-supervised weight updates during inference yield larger gains than entropy minimization or pseudo-labeling alone. Notably, FFN surpasses the offline oracle (Offline TTT-MAE All Frames, 33.6/19.6 on COCO) that can access the entire video, validating the principle of locality. For depth estimation, FFN beats both TTT-Online and the strong zero-shot Video Depth-Anything across all six datasets (e.g., AbsRel 0.059 vs 0.071 vs 0.083 on KITTI). On action classification, FFN achieves 95.0% on UCF101 (beating Online TTT's 94.2% and DINOv3's 93.5%) but trails V-JEPA 2 on SSv2 (74.1% vs 75.4%), suggesting the temporal-reasoning challenge of direction-sensitive actions remains partially open. On the multi-hour EpicTours dataset, FFN leads all baselines by a wide margin (+6.5 mIoU semantic, +5.3 AP instance over TTT-Online), and unlike TTT-Online which degrades beyond 50 minutes, FFN maintains stable performance over 3-hour videos.

## Relation to Prior Work

FFN builds directly on TTT-Online (Wang et al., JMLR 2025), which established test-time training on video streams via a sliding window that re-processes all k frames at every timestep. The key departure is the 'forget, anticipate, adapt' decomposition: instead of recomputing the full window, FFN operates on only three frames (exiting, current, entering) using a Memory Restoration Mechanism and an Adaptive Window Algorithm. Conceptually, the forgetting mechanism repurposes ideas from safety-alignment unlearning for representational restoration, while the surprise metric draws an analogy to video compression's frame-difference coding but learns it end-to-end via an anticipative head. The temporal conditioning via RoPE borrows from transformer positional encodings, and the overall framing connects to the principle of locality from Vapnik et al. and local learning algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine practical bottleneck: existing TTT-on-video methods scale linearly with window size, making hours-long videos computationally intractable (8 hours for a 2-hour video). The introduction of EpicTours (up to 3-hour videos, 2.49M frames) fills a critical gap, as prior TTT video benchmarks were limited to ~5 minutes. Open questions remain: whether TTT can be accelerated further via local learning (forward-forward, no-prop), how to handle stationary-camera periods where no adaptation occurs (sleeping phases), and whether the SSL task itself can be meta-learned. The finding that a streaming model outperforms an offline oracle that sees the entire video strongly validates the principle of locality for video understanding.
