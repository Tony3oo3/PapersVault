## Problem

Constructing semantic blendshape sets for non-humanoid head avatars is expensive because expression-consistent supervision is scarce, generated 4D assets lack vertex correspondence across expressions, and non-humanoid facial motion is highly localized and species-dependent. Existing approaches either rely on human anatomical priors (e.g., 3DMM) that fail on non-humanoid geometry, or require per-instance optimization-based registration that is too slow to scale to diverse identities.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of unregistered expression meshes (one neutral plus T target expressions) for a single non-humanoid identity, each voxelized into a 64³ grid with 9-channel tokens (8-channel structured voxel latent plus unprojected eye-region segmentation from 15 near-frontal views). It outputs a corresponded semantic blendshape set where all expressions share topology and vertex correspondence, enabling standard linear blending. For retargeting, a small MLP maps human 3DMM tracking coefficients to the non-humanoid blendshape weight vector in a single forward pass, with tracked head pose applied to the normalized blendshapes.

## Method

RegHead amortizes correspondence recovery into a feed-forward network that predicts per-anchor pivoted similarity transforms from the neutral mesh to each target expression in one pass. Motion is parameterized by K=3000 stochastic anchors resampled each iteration on the neutral surface, with deformations propagated to 30,000 dense query points via linear blend skinning over K'=10 nearest anchors. The registration uses a coarse-to-fine design: a global matcher performs cross-attention between anchor tokens and randomly subsampled global voxel tokens to predict a coarse transform, then a structured local matcher refines fine-grained motion using validity-aware multi-radius stencil neighborhoods (thin shell bands at multiple radii) around each coarsely warped anchor. Training uses a differentiable point-based Gaussian splat renderer comparing predicted and target expressions under random camera views (RGB + segmentation + LPIPS losses) plus a Chamfer distance on coarsely deformed anchors, requiring no ground-truth point correspondences or deformation fields.

## Results

On the 200-identity test set, RegHead achieves 23.84 PSNR, 0.9078 SSIM, and 0.0551 LPIPS on rendered appearance, and 0.00358 visible Chamfer distance, 0.00613 depth error, 0.9192 normal consistency, and 0.9865 silhouette IoU on visible geometry. Inference runs in ~5 s per identity, matching the feed-forward baseline ActionMesh in speed while outperforming it by +1.57 PSNR and −0.0103 LPIPS. Compared to optimization-based methods, RegHead is 20× faster than V2M4 (~100 s) and 360× faster than T2Bs (~1800 s) while still achieving higher fidelity on every reported metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegHead outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against the feed-forward baseline ActionMesh [31], RegHead gains +1.57 PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.0654 to 0.0551 at the same ~5 s runtime, with the gap attributed to ActionMesh's lack of an explicit local matching mechanism and its domain gap to non-humanoid geometry. Against the optimization-based V2M4 [4], RegHead improves PSNR by +1.06 and LPIPS by −0.0109 while running 20× faster (~5 s vs ~100 s); V2M4 also shows the worst visible geometry (CD 0.00959, Sil. IoU 0.9567). Against T2Bs [22], the closest competitor, RegHead still leads by +0.41 PSNR, −0.0017 LPIPS, and +0.0014 Sil. IoU, while being 360× faster (~5 s vs ~1800 s). Qualitatively, RegHead better captures fine localized deformations (e.g., partial eyelid closure) where both optimization-based methods exhibit underfitting or artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

RegHead sits at the intersection of feed-forward 4D mesh deformation (ActionMesh, DriveAnyMesh) and optimization-based non-rigid registration (V2M4, T2Bs), but departs from both by amortizing correspondence recovery into a single-pass network trained without ground-truth correspondences. It builds on voxel-latent shape representations (Xiang et al.) for encoding expression-specific geometry, and on the broader head-avatar literature (TextToon, LeGO, HeadEvolver), but explicitly avoids parametric priors like 3DMM that constrain geometry to human anatomy. The key architectural departure from skeleton-based animation methods (RigAnything, UniRig) is the use of dense stochastic anchors with linear blend skinning rather than sparse bones, motivated by the highly localized, species-dependent nature of non-humanoid facial motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~20k-identity non-humanoid head dataset with a shared semantic expression vocabulary fills a notable gap in publicly available resources for non-humanoid avatar research, and the feed-forward pipeline (≈5 s per identity vs. ≈1800 s for T2Bs) makes scalable blendshape construction practical for AR and gaming pipelines. The real-time retargeting from human face tracking to diverse non-humanoid characters demonstrates a stable, interpretable animation interface that avoids per-frame optimization. Open questions include whether the fixed 7-expression vocabulary captures the full range of species-specific motions, how the method generalizes to long-tail anatomies (insect-like heads, ambiguous facial regions), and whether the upstream 3D/4D generation quality bottleneck can be further decoupled from the registration stage.
