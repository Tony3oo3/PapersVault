## Problem

Existing LiDAR-4D radar fusion detectors for 3D object detection operate under a closed-world assumption where test-time weather must be represented during training in both type and severity. In practice, weather is open-ended and continuously varying, causing substantial performance drops on unseen type-severity combinations. This paper formalizes the open-weather generalization gap and constructs a structured benchmark with held-out type, severity, and joint type-severity protocols to expose this limitation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LiDAR BEV feature maps (extracted from degraded point clouds) and 4D radar BEV features as a conditioning signal, operating at the feature level rather than on raw point clouds. It outputs refined LiDAR BEV features that replace the original degraded features before downstream fusion and detection. At inference, a lightweight discriminator router can bypass the entire diffusion refinement for inputs classified as confidently Normal, reducing latency from 70.86 ms to 59.59 ms while retaining most of the robustness gain.

## Method

DCDA formulates degraded LiDAR feature recovery as a radar-conditioned DDPM diffusion process that starts from a noised observation (not pure noise) to retain coarse input structure, using a U-Net that predicts a clean-weather feature estimate at each reverse step conditioned on the 4D radar feature. Two frozen critics guide the diffusion trajectory: a detection-guided critic (a Normal-weather-trained detector head) enforces task-level semantic and localization fidelity, while a weather adversarial critic (a Normal vs. non-Normal discriminator) enforces global distributional alignment with the clean manifold. Training proceeds in two stages—identity-preserving warm-up on Normal data, then adversarial alignment on the full set with the diffusion loss weight annealed toward zero—requiring no paired clean-degraded supervision or explicit weather type/severity labels as conditional inputs.

## Results

On the K-Radar type-open protocol (holding out Fog, HeavySnow, LightSnow, and Overcast), DCDA achieves 72.65% mean unseen BEV AP and 42.25% mean unseen 3D AP, surpassing the strongest baseline L4DR by 3.83 and 7.73 points respectively. Under the severity-open protocol (held-out Medium/Heavy synthetic levels), DCDA improves unseen BEV/3D AP by 1.28/2.32 points over L4DR. In the most challenging type+severity-open setting (only Normal + Fog-Light in training), DCDA attains the highest mean unseen 3D AP, exceeding L4DR by 2.18 points. Averaged across all three open-weather protocols, DCDA improves mean 3D AP over the best baseline by 5.2 (type-open), 3.0 (severity-open), and 1.8 (type+severity-open) percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCDA outperforms all five baselines on mean unseen-weather performance across all three open-weather protocols. Against the strongest baseline L4DR [19], DCDA gains +3.83 BEV AP and +7.73 3D AP on unseen types (type-open), +1.28/+2.32 on unseen severities (severity-open), and +2.18 on mean unseen 3D AP (type+severity-open). V2X-R [18] is competitive on seen conditions but degrades more sharply on unseen types (e.g., 28.65 vs. 41.91 3D AP on Fog); DCDA surpasses it by 1.76 pp on mean unseen 3D AP in the type+severity-open setting. InterFusion [35] and RTNH [25] trail substantially on unseen conditions, with RTNH dropping to 10.36 3D AP on Fog versus DCDA's 41.91. SpikingRTNH [24], evaluated only in the type-open protocol, is the weakest baseline overall (e.g., 8.07 3D AP on LightSnow). Notably, DCDA also maintains or improves seen-condition accuracy (e.g., 68.39 vs. 68.33 BEV AP on Normal), indicating the dual-critic guidance does not sacrifice in-distribution performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCDA builds on the LiDAR-4D radar fusion line (L4DR, V2X-R, InterFusion) and the broader diffusion-based feature refinement paradigm (DiffBEV, BEVDiffuser, V2X-R's denoising diffusion), but departs from both in a key way: rather than conditioning on explicit weather type/severity labels or requiring paired clean-degraded supervision, it formulates recovery as a weather-agnostic manifold alignment problem steered by two frozen critics (a detection head and a Normal-vs-non-Normal discriminator). This positions DCDA as a conceptual shift from 'model the weather' to 'align to the clean manifold regardless of what the weather is,' drawing on adversarial distribution-matching ideas from domain adaptation but applying them at the feature level within a diffusion loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most durable contribution is the structured open-weather benchmark (joint type + severity holdout on K-Radar), which makes the generalization gap in current fusion detectors concrete and reproducible. The weather-agnostic design (no weather labels, no paired data) is practically attractive for deployment where conditions are unbounded. However, the severity evaluation relies on synthetic corruptions, the clean reference is fixed to Normal, and gains shrink at extreme severities where LiDAR geometry is nearly destroyed—open questions that limit immediate real-world transfer. Extending to real severity annotations and heterogeneous clean references is the natural next step.
