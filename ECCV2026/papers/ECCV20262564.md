## Problem

Existing continual VQA methods address forgetting primarily through parameter interference (regularization, replay, isolation), but overlook a distinct failure mode: sequential adaptation can decouple the question from its relevant visual evidence, causing the model to answer from language priors rather than image grounding. This question-evidence coupling drift is especially harmful for compositional generalization, where the model must recombine previously learned skills with new visual concepts.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a natural-language question as input and generates a free-form text answer via autoregressive decoding. At inference time no task identifier is provided; the shared controller computes a question-only pooled representation and a prompt-only attention-drift diagnostic to route experts and steer attention. The frozen MLLM backbone (LLaVA-style) handles all visual and language encoding, while only LoRA experts, the controller, and low-rank ICR bases are trainable.

## Method

Q-STEER separates plasticity from retention through a shared question-conditioned controller that produces factorized old/new MoE-LoRA routing logits, a shared interpolation gate λ, and ICR steering coefficients. For plasticity, a probe-based self-expansion mechanism temporarily exposes preallocated inactive expert slots after each task and promotes them only when their measured utilization exceeds a threshold, changing only the active mask without altering parameter shapes. For retention, a KL-divergence-based attention-drift signal (computed between current and consolidated late-layer attention distributions) conditions a low-rank correction injected into attention logits before softmax at answer-query positions; critically, this drift is diagnostic rather than a loss, so shifted tasks are free to adopt new attention patterns. Factorized normalization of old and new expert banks eliminates the direct cross-derivative between new-bank logits and old-bank routing weights, and a local Lipschitz analysis yields a sufficient margin condition under which old predictions are preserved.

## Results

On the continual VQA-v2 benchmark (10 sequential question types), Q-STEER achieves 53.68% average performance (AP) and 4.51% average forgetting (AF), improving AP by 9.52 points and reducing AF by 4.46 points over the strongest baseline QUAD (44.16 / 8.97). On the heterogeneous CVIT stream (6 diverse visual-instruction datasets), Q-STEER reaches 85.15 AP and 4.33 AF, outperforming SMoLoRA (83.02 / 6.50). On compositional generalization splits, Q-STEER scores 51.00 (Novel) and 51.48 (Seen), exceeding QUAD by 8.53 and 8.24 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-STEER outperforms all nine baselines on both AP and AF across both evaluation protocols. On VQA-v2, the largest margin is over regularization-based methods: EWC (37.28 AP / 15.27 AF) and MAS (37.71 / 14.91) trail by roughly 16 AP points and 10–11 AF points. Replay-based baselines ER (41.95 / 10.20) and DER (41.16 / 11.28) are closer but still 11–12 AP points behind. The strongest VQA-specific baselines—VQACL (43.49 / 9.10), CL-MoE (42.63 / 10.56), and QUAD (44.16 / 8.97)—are surpassed by 9.5–11.2 AP points and 4.5–6.1 AF points. On the heterogeneous CVIT stream, Q-STEER edges out SMoLoRA (83.02 / 6.50) by 2.13 AP and 2.17 AF, and also achieves the best native score on 5 of 6 constituent datasets. No baseline matches or exceeds Q-STEER on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

Q-STEER extends the modular expert routing paradigm introduced by CL-MoE for continual VQA, but departs from it in two key ways: it factorizes old/new expert banks with a shared interpolation gate to eliminate direct routing competition, and it adds a drift-aware attention-logit steering mechanism (ICR) that has no counterpart in prior continual VQA methods. The broader continual-learning lineage (EWC, MAS, ER, DER, VQACL, QUAD) addresses forgetting through parameter regularization, replay, or task-specific replay, but none explicitly model the cross-modal coupling between question and visual evidence as a distinct failure axis. The probe-based self-expansion mechanism draws on the self-expansion literature (Wang et al., 2025) but applies it to preallocated LoRA slots with mask-based promotion rather than architectural growth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying question-evidence coupling drift as a forgetting mode orthogonal to parameter interference, the paper reframes how continual VQA should be evaluated and designed: preserving individual concepts is insufficient if the model no longer binds the right visual region to the right question. The practical impact is a task-agnostic method that achieves large gains (≈9.5 AP, ≈4.5 AF) without replay, with moderate overhead (+6.5% FLOPs). Open questions remain: the KL drift signal is a proxy rather than a causal grounding measure, the sufficient margin condition (Eq. 12) is local rather than universal, and the shared gate couples routing and steering in ways that may be suboptimal when they should vary independently. Future work on compressible expert banks, richer drift estimators, and causal grounding tests would strengthen the framework.
