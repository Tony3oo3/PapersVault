## Problem

Existing driving world models predominantly generate a single modality (camera video or LiDAR), and the few multimodal approaches (e.g., UniScene, Genesis) rely on cascaded or sequential pipelines where one modality is explicitly conditioned on the other's output. This unidirectional information flow prevents bidirectional RGB–LiDAR interaction during synthesis, undermining cross-modal geometric consistency and overall generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261161/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a first-frame multi-camera image (6 views at 432×768) and a first-frame LiDAR range map (32×1024, single-channel, with each row repeated 4× to mitigate sparsity), together with structured scene layout (3D bounding boxes and HDMap projected separately into colored camera views and single-channel range views) and multi-view text prompts encoded by umT5. It produces a 17-frame multi-camera video sequence and a 17-frame LiDAR range sequence jointly in a single forward pass, with both modalities decoded from their respective VAE decoders.

## Method

UniDriveDreamer is a single-stage architecture that jointly generates camera video and LiDAR in one diffusion pass, eliminating intermediate representations or cascaded modules. Two modality-specific VAEs (a LiDAR VAE with single-channel I/O sharing the Wan-2.1 backbone, and a standard video VAE) encode inputs into a shared latent space; Unified Latent Anchoring (ULA) then aligns the LiDAR latent distribution to the RGB VAE prior via a parameter-free affine transformation derived from empirical per-dataset statistics, stabilizing joint training without learnable parameters. A layout encoder (spatial + spatiotemporal downsampling blocks) projects 3D scene structure into per-modality condition latents, and a diffusion transformer with modality-specific patchifying/unpatchifying modules processes the concatenated camera and LiDAR token sequences through shared transformer blocks—using self-attention for intra- and cross-modal spatiotemporal modeling and cross-attention for text conditioning. Training uses a flow-matching objective initialized from Wan-2.1 T2V-1.3B pretrained weights, with the LiDAR VAE trained separately using L1 + KL + LPIPS losses before joint fine-tuning.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, UniDriveDreamer achieves an FID of 2.81 and FVD of 11.44 for multi-camera video generation, and an MMD of 0.27 and JSD of 0.039 for LiDAR range-map synthesis, outperforming all prior single-modal and multimodal baselines. For LiDAR reconstruction fidelity, the proposed LiDAR VAE attains a Chamfer Distance of 0.154 and F-Score of 0.900, surpassing OmniGen (0.793 / 0.742) by ~80.6% and 21.3% respectively. In downstream 3D object detection with BEVFusion, augmenting training data with UniDriveDreamer-generated samples yields +0.8 mAP and +0.52 NDS over the real-data-only baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest prior multimodal method UniScene [20], UniDriveDreamer reduces FID from 6.12 to 2.81 (−54%), FVD from 70.52 to 11.44 (−84%), MMD from 1.53 to 0.27 (−82.3%), and JSD from 0.072 to 0.039 (−45.8%), demonstrating substantial gains in both visual and geometric fidelity. Compared to Genesis [13], the best prior method on camera metrics (FID 4.24, FVD 16.95), UniDriveDreamer improves FID by 33.7% and FVD by 32.5% while additionally producing LiDAR outputs that Genesis does not report. Versus single-modal camera baselines, the best FID (DriveDreamer-2, 11.20) and FVD (DriveDreamer-2, 55.70) are exceeded by 75% and 79% respectively. On LiDAR synthesis, UniDriveDreamer's MMD of 0.27 and JSD of 0.039 surpass the best single-modal LiDAR method RangeLDM [16] (MMD 1.90, JSD 0.054) by 85.8% and 27.8%. For LiDAR reconstruction, the proposed VAE outperforms OmniGen [33] by 80.6% in Chamfer Distance and 21.3% in F-Score. Finally, downstream BEVFusion detection improves by +0.8 mAP and +0.52 NDS when trained with UniDriveDreamer-augmented data, confirming practical utility beyond generation metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniDriveDreamer builds directly on the Wan-2.1 video generation backbone (VAE architecture and diffusion transformer) and the flow-matching training framework, adapting them to a driving-specific multimodal setting. It departs from the dominant cascaded paradigm in prior multimodal driving generation (UniScene, Genesis), where LiDAR synthesis is sequentially conditioned on an intermediate occupancy or latent video representation, by instead concatenating camera and LiDAR tokens into a single shared transformer stack for bidirectional interaction. The LiDAR range-map representation and row-repetition trick are inherited from Cosmos-Drive-Dreams and RangeLDM, while the novel Unified Latent Anchoring mechanism introduces a parameter-free affine calibration to align heterogeneous latent distributions before joint diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the cascaded pipeline, UniDriveDreamer offers a cleaner and more scalable architecture for multimodal sensor simulation, and the parameter-free ULA generalizes across datasets (nuScenes → Waymo) without retraining, making it a practical tool for deploying unified world models on new sensor suites. However, the downstream perception gains remain modest (+0.8 mAP, +0.52 NDS), and the evaluation is confined to a single sensor configuration (6 cameras + 1 roof LiDAR on nuScenes), leaving open questions about scalability to more cameras, different LiDAR placements, or additional modalities such as radar. The modest downstream lift also raises the question of whether the large generation-quality improvements (FID, MMD) translate proportionally into perception benefits at scale.
