## Problem

Pretrained Vision Transformers (DINOv2, MAE) produce query-agnostic features that collapse to the most salient object in an image, offering no mechanism to redirect attention toward less prominent concepts. While Multimodal LLMs accept text prompts, their representations become language-dominant and lose the visual fidelity needed for generic downstream tasks. No existing architecture simultaneously achieves text steerability and preserves the underlying ViT's representation quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed at 336×336 resolution) and a free-form text prompt as input. The frozen RoBERTa-Large text encoder produces variable-length token embeddings that are projected into the ViT's embedding space via a two-layer MLP. Outputs include steerable global (CLS) and patch-level visual features, patch-level segmentation probabilities from a linear head, and—when applied to anomaly detection—upsampled heatmaps. At inference, a scalar gating factor ω ∈ [0,1] scales the cross-attention gates to continuously interpolate between the unaltered ViT and the fully text-conditioned representation.

## Method

SteerViT inverts the MLLM paradigm by conditioning a frozen visual encoder on language rather than conditioning language on vision. Lightweight gated cross-attention layers are interleaved into every other block of a frozen ViT (e.g., 6 layers for ViT-B/12), where visual patch tokens serve as queries and adapted text tokens serve as keys and values—the inverse of Flamingo's language-to-vision attention. Each cross-attention output is added to the residual stream through a tanh gate with a zero-initialized learnable scalar α_ℓ, ensuring the model starts as the identity of the frozen ViT and gradually activates the conditioning pathway during training. The entire system is trained with a referential segmentation pretext task (patch-level soft cross-entropy) on 2.28M image-text pairs from RefCOCO/+/g, Visual Genome, LVIS, and Mapillary Vistas, adding only 21M trainable parameters. This early-fusion design allows text to reshape intermediate visual representations at multiple depths, enabling both global semantic steering and targeted local attention without degrading the base ViT's transferable feature quality.

## Results

On the proposed CORE benchmark (text-conditioned image retrieval), SteerViT achieves 96.0% top-1 retrieval accuracy, far surpassing DINOv2 (44%), MAE (20%), CLIP/SigLIP (poor), FLAIR (81.3%), and InternVL3-1B (47%). On MOSAIC localization, SteerViT reaches 50.2% PR-AUC versus 14.3% for DINOv2. On zero-shot anomaly segmentation (MVTec AD), SteerViT scores 82.1 PRO, matching dedicated methods like FADE (84.5) and outperforming WinCLIP (64.6) and SAM3 (54.5). On personalized object discrimination (PODS), SteerViT with descriptive prompts achieves 58.1% PR-AUC, surpassing fine-tuned DINOv2 (48.0%) with a single model instead of 100 object-specific ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SteerViT decisively outperforms unimodal encoders (DINOv2, MAE) on steerability: 96% vs 44% on CORE and 50.2% vs 14.3% PR-AUC on MOSAIC, while preserving representation quality (FG-CLS 87.7 vs 89.0). Against cross-modal encoders (CLIP, SigLIP), SteerViT wins by a wide margin on CORE, confirming that late fusion cannot steer frozen visual features. FLAIR, the closest prior steerable method, trails SteerViT by 14.7 points on CORE (81.3% vs 96.0%). Among MLLMs, SteerViT outperforms InternVL3-1B by 49 points and InternVL3-2B by 20 points on CORE, using only 21M trainable parameters versus billions. On MVTec AD anomaly segmentation, SteerViT (82.1 PRO) slightly trails the dedicated FADE method (84.5) but substantially outperforms WinCLIP (64.6), DIVAD (73.3), and SAM3 (54.5), demonstrating zero-shot OOD transfer. On PODS, SteerViT with detailed prompts (58.1% PR-AUC) surpasses object-specific fine-tuned DINOv2 (48.0%) using a single model, and on GeneCIS conditional retrieval it reaches 25.4% R@1 versus 9.6% for DINOv2.

## Relation to Prior Work

SteerViT inverts the MLLM paradigm by conditioning a frozen visual encoder on language rather than conditioning language on vision, drawing directly on Flamingo's gated cross-attention mechanism but reversing the attention direction (vision queries attend to language keys/values). It departs from cross-modal encoders (CLIP, SigLIP) that use text only for training supervision and cannot be steered at inference, and from MLLMs whose features reside in language-dominant space at billion-parameter scale. The closest prior attempt, FLAIR, applies text-conditioned attention pooling after the visual encoder (late fusion), whereas SteerViT injects text into intermediate ViT layers (early fusion), enabling the visual encoding process itself to be redirected. The paper also introduces the CORE benchmark and MOSAIC Localization as new evaluation tools for measuring representational steerability, a property no prior benchmark explicitly targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 21M-parameter overhead (two orders of magnitude below MLLMs) makes text-steerable visual features practically deployable, and the tanh-gated cross-attention provides a continuous inference-time knob to trade off steerability against representation quality without retraining. The finding that a single SteerViT model with detailed text prompts can match or exceed 100 object-specific fine-tuned DINOv2 models on personalized discrimination suggests a path toward unified, prompt-adaptive visual encoders. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video or 3D representations, how it handles multi-object or negation-based prompts, and whether the referential segmentation pretext task is optimal or whether other grounding objectives could further improve steerability without quality loss.
