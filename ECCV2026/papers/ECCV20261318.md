## Problem

T2I-based super-resolution methods rely on ControlNet-style branches or spatial conditioning tokens whose memory and compute grow superlinearly with resolution, forcing training at a single fixed scale and causing severe hallucinations at other resolutions. The paper identifies that mask-conditioned inpainting Diffusion Transformers already possess a natively pixel-aligned interface that is structurally better suited for pixel-level restoration, yet repurposing state-of-the-art inpainting DiTs for blind SR remains underexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality RGB image (normalized to [-1,1]), VAE-encodes it, and bicubic-upsamples the latent to the target spatial grid. The LQ latent is injected directly into the inpainting DiT's native masked-image slot under a full-image mask, so no separate control branch or growing token stream is needed. Output is a high-resolution RGB image; the pipeline supports 2×/4×/8× upscaling with stable performance across 512/1024/2048-pixel outputs, and fits 1536² inference on a single 32 GB GPU. A fixed-length 768-token Redux embedding of the LQ image provides optional semantic stabilization at zero additional spatial cost.

## Method

Fill2SR repurposes the FLUX-Fill inpainting DiT by introducing the Inpainting-Interface Evidence Adapter (IIEA), which writes the VAE-encoded LQ latent into the native masked-image slot under a full-image mask (m≡1), converting the inpainting interface into a dense pixel-aligned evidence channel without any ControlNet-style branch. Training uses a reverse-degradation conditional rectified flow: the model learns a velocity field transporting pure Gaussian noise to the HQ latent, conditioned on the degraded observation, optimized with LoRA-only tuning (rank 256, ~0.65 B trainable parameters) on attention and FFN projections. To cover real-world degradation statistics beyond synthetic pipelines, RCDT is an offline data-synthesis step that uses a frozen VLM (Qwen3-VL) to distill degradation descriptors from unpaired real LQ images and a frozen instruction-based editor to transfer those degradations onto clean targets, producing paired LQ-HQ supervision injected only in the final 20% of training. The bounded conditioning cost of IIEA enables mixed-resolution, mixed-aspect-ratio training up to native QHD (2560×1440) within 48 GB VRAM, which is the key enabler for stable generalization across output scales.

## Results

On synthetic benchmarks (averaged over D1–D3), Ours-Base (w/o RCDT) achieves the best LPIPS on DIV2K (0.3866) and LSDIR (0.3878), outperforming FaithDiff (0.4051/0.4083) and SeeSR (0.4165/0.4126). On real-world benchmarks, Ours-Full (+RCDT) attains the best MUSIQ, MANIQA, and CLIPIQA on RealPhoto60 at both 2× and 4×, and on RealLQ250 at 2× and 4× (e.g., CLIPIQA 0.7158 at 2×, 0.6287 at 4×). At the extreme 8× setting on RealLQ250, DiT4SR leads on MUSIQ (61.84 vs. 59.30) and CLIPIQA (0.5403 vs. 0.4784), while Ours-Full still wins MANIQA (0.5876 vs. 0.5766). Fill2SR fits within a single 32 GB GPU at 1536² inference with 75 s per image (28 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On synthetic benchmarks, Ours-Base (w/o RCDT) achieves the best LPIPS on DIV2K (0.3866) and LSDIR (0.3878), beating FaithDiff (0.4051/0.4083), SeeSR (0.4165/0.4126), and DiffBIR (0.4168/0.4090); regression-based Real-ESRGAN leads PSNR/SSIM but trails on all perceptual metrics. On real-world benchmarks, Ours-Full dominates at 2× and 4× on both RealPhoto60 and RealLQ250, e.g., CLIPIQA 0.7158 (2×) and 0.6287 (4×) on RealLQ250, surpassing DiT4SR (0.6244/0.5617) and FaithDiff (0.6299/0.5269). At the extreme 8× setting, DiT4SR is the strongest competitor, winning MUSIQ (61.84 vs. 59.30) and CLIPIQA (0.5403 vs. 0.4784) on RealLQ250, while Ours-Full still leads MANIQA (0.5876 vs. 0.5766). FaithDiff is the closest overall competitor at 2×, narrowly edging Ours-Full on MANIQA for RealLQ250 (0.6869 vs. 0.6861). SUPIR and SeeSR remain competitive on MUSIQ at moderate scales but degrade sharply at 4×/8×, while StableSR and Real-ESRGAN are consistently outperformed across all no-reference metrics. The RCDT ablation confirms that the real-world gains come from domain-transfer supervision rather than architectural changes: removing RCDT drops RealLQ250 CLIPIQA from 0.6076 to 0.5350 (4×) while slightly improving synthetic PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

Fill2SR departs from the dominant paradigm of adapting text-to-image diffusion backbones (UNet or DiT) with ControlNet-style branches or spatial conditioning tokens for super-resolution, arguing that these add superlinear memory/compute overhead with resolution and constrain training to a fixed scale. Instead, it repurposes the native masked-image inpainting interface of FLUX-Fill as a dense pixel-aligned evidence channel (IIEA), combined with a reverse-degradation rectified flow objective inherited from flow matching literature. The offline RCDT pipeline for synthesizing real-world degradation pairs via frozen VLMs and instruction-based editors is a novel data-generation strategy that complements, rather than replaces, standard synthetic degradation training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bounded conditioning cost of IIEA enables mixed-resolution, mixed-aspect-ratio training up to native QHD while remaining feasible on a single 32GB GPU at 1536², addressing a practical bottleneck that has limited prior diffusion-based SR methods to fixed training resolutions. The well-characterized trade-off between synthetic-domain fidelity (LPIPS/PSNR) and real-world perceptual quality (MUSIQ/MANIQA/CLIPIQA) when injecting RCDT pairs provides useful guidance for practitioners balancing these objectives. Open questions include whether distillation or faster solvers can close the 75-second inference gap versus feed-forward regressors, whether the IIEA conditioning behavior generalizes beyond FLUX-Fill to other inpainting DiTs, and whether RCDT's reliance on frozen instruction-based editors can be made more robust to subtle geometric drift.
