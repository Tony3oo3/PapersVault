## Problem

Traditional Shot Boundary Detection (SBD) formulates the task as frame-wise binary classification targeting isolated cut points, which fundamentally fails on gradual transitions (dissolves, fades, wipes), subtle cuts, and special-effect transitions, yielding corrupted shots with dirty transitional frames. General-purpose VLMs can perceive complex transitions but miss abrupt cuts due to their bias toward static spatial semantics and reliance on sparse low-FPS inputs. The paper reformulates the task as Shot Transition Detection (STD), requiring detection of continuous temporal segments rather than ambiguous boundary points.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 6-channel fused frames (3-channel RGB + 3-channel optical-flow visualization where flow magnitude maps to saturation and orientation to hue) at 5 FPS, processed in 10-second overlapping windows (9-second stride) to handle arbitrary video lengths without memory overflow. A text prompt instructs the VLM to output a structured JSON array of (start, end) timestamp tuples. The channel-level fusion is designed so the visual token sequence length remains identical to a standard 3-channel input, imposing zero additional computational cost on the language backbone.

## Method

TransVLM builds on Qwen3-VL 4B Instruct and injects optical flow (extracted via NeuFlow v2) as a motion prior through a minimal architectural modification: the Vision Patch Embedding layer's input channels are expanded from 3 to 6, with the new flow-channel weights zero-initialized so the model starts identical to the pretrained VLM and progressively learns motion sensitivity without catastrophic forgetting. For inference on arbitrary-length videos, a temporal sliding-window strategy partitions the stream into overlapping 10-second segments, generates local segment predictions per window, and merges them via temporal Non-Maximum Suppression into a continuous global output. Training uses a scalable data engine that synthesizes 218K+ videos with 59 distinct transition effects (powered by FFmpeg) alongside reformatted public datasets, with a quality-aware mixed sampling strategy (0.7/0.2/0.1 weights) to prevent noisy public labels from dominating gradient updates. The model is fine-tuned for 5,000 steps with AdamW (lr 1e-5, cosine decay) on 8×H100 GPUs, achieving an end-to-end RTF of 0.72.

## Results

On the proposed STD benchmark, TransVLM (4B, 5 FPS) achieves 78.3% segment-level F1 and 56.2% frame-level F1 on public data, with an Absolute Boundary Error of 1.58 s. On the synthetic test set (containing subtle cuts, gradual, and long transitions), it reaches 89.5% segment F1, 93.8% frame F1, and a remarkably low mABE of 0.11 s. The end-to-end Real-Time Factor is 0.72 (faster than real-time), including NeuFlow v2 optical-flow extraction. These results surpass the best SBD baseline (AutoShot: 75.1% / 53.2% segment/frame F1 on public data) and all evaluated VLMs by wide margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TransVLM outperforms the strongest SBD baseline AutoShot by 3.2 points in segment F1 (78.3% vs 75.1%) and 3.0 points in frame F1 (56.2% vs 53.2%) on public data, while on the synthetic set AutoShot's frame F1 collapses to 10.2% versus TransVLM's 93.8%. Against the heuristic PySceneDetect, TransVLM gains roughly 10 points in segment F1 on public data and over 70 points on synthetic data. Among VLM baselines, TransVLM dramatically surpasses Qwen3-VL 4B Instruct (78.3% vs 12.4% segment F1 on public data) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (78.3% vs 54.2%), confirming that the optical-flow injection and sliding-window inference are the key differentiators rather than raw model scale. The TAL baseline ActionFormer achieves competitive boundary precision (0.34 s ABE) but lags far behind in segment retrieval (46.2% vs 78.3% segment F1), showing that boundary regression alone cannot substitute for visual-semantic understanding of transition content. Overall, TransVLM is the only method that maintains strong performance across all three transition categories (abrupt cuts, gradual, and long/special-effect) simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the long-standing Shot Boundary Detection (SBD) paradigm—exemplified by heuristic tools (PySceneDetect) and CNN-based detectors (TransNetV2, AutoShot)—which frames the problem as frame-wise binary classification of isolated cut points. It also diverges from the Temporal Action Localization (TAL) line (ActionFormer, BSN), which predicts segments but targets visually coherent actions rather than discontinuous inter-shot transitions. The key architectural departure is repurposing a general-purpose VLM (Qwen3-VL) for a low-level temporal detection task by injecting optical flow via a minimal channel expansion at the patch-embedding layer, a design that has no direct precedent in either the SBD or VLM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The segment-level STD benchmark (5,215 videos, 45K transitions with precise start/end annotations) fills a genuine gap: no prior dataset provides the continuous temporal spans needed to evaluate transition detection beyond single-frame boundaries. The practical stakes are high for video generation pipelines, where inaccurate transition labels in training data directly cause models to produce unintended cuts in generated videos. Open questions include whether the external NeuFlow v2 dependency can be replaced by native ViT temporal-attention signals for ultra-low-latency streaming, whether the framework extends to transition-type classification (dissolve vs. wipe vs. fade), and whether the zero-padding channel-expansion trick generalizes to other modalities (audio, depth) without the same stability guarantees.
