## Problem

Implicit adversarial prompts—inputs that appear benign textually yet elicit harmful images from T2I models—are far harder to detect and defend against than explicit harmful prompts, yet existing agentic red-teaming methods either operate only in the text modality or fail to effectively construct and exploit feedback signals across iterations. The paper tackles two concrete gaps: how to build informative, noise-free feedback that tracks directional progress toward harmful outputs, and how to leverage that feedback at the token level to steer generation toward implicitly harmful directions without model training.

## Input / Output

The system starts from 30 GPT-4o-generated safe seed prompts per harmful category (11 categories total) and iteratively refines them over 20 rounds (3 images per iteration) by querying a black-box T2I model with no parameter access. A prompt is deemed a successful implicit attack only if it passes OpenAI's Moderation API as textually safe while at least one generated image is flagged harmful by a vision-based judge (Gemma3-4B, LLaVA Guard, or Google SafeSearch). The policy model is LLaVA-v1.6-mistral-7B and the judge is Gemma3-4B, both kept frozen throughout.

## Method

AdvPIE is a training-free, black-box agentic framework with two core mechanisms. First, a global-relative feedback strategy: the judge agent returns modality-specific evaluations (safety label, harmfulness score, violation category, rationale) for both text and image, plus two score-based guidance signals—a global top-k reference set of the most harmful prompts found so far and a relative contrast between the two most recent iterations—to provide directional progress without the noise of raw historical aggregation. Second, Cumulative Adversarial Decoding (CAD) reweights token logits during policy-model sampling using each token's cumulative average harmfulness score (computed as ω^img·s^img − ω^txt·s^txt, with ω^img=0.8), scaled by the logit standard deviation to preserve relative probability structure, and modulated by a repetition penalty [1/(1+βn)]^1_{s̄>0} that decays the boost for frequently used tokens to maintain diversity and prevent convergence on known patterns. This token-level intervention allows the system to learn from accumulated visual feedback at inference time without any gradient updates or fine-tuning.

## Results

AdvPIE achieves the highest Attack Success Rate (ASR) across four open-source T2I models and the commercial Stable Diffusion API. On FLUX.1-schnell, AdvPIE reaches 58.71% ASR under the Gemma judge, outperforming FLIRT (49.34%), ART (43.82%), and Groot (24.33%). On the commercial Stable Diffusion API, AdvPIE achieves 50.45% ASR (Gemma), a +10.31% improvement over the strongest baseline ART (40.14%). The only marginal exception is safe-sd-v1-5 under Gemma, where FLIRT edges out AdvPIE (33.26% vs. 30.45%), attributed to the model's lower visual fidelity reducing feedback informativeness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdvPIE outperforms all three baselines on 3 of 4 open-source models across most judges. Against FLIRT (the strongest baseline), AdvPIE wins by +7.77% on sd-3.5-large and +9.37% on Flux under Gemma, but loses marginally on safe-sd-v1-5/Gemma (30.45% vs. 33.26%). Against ART, AdvPIE gains +12.75% on sd-3.5-large and +14.89% on Flux under Gemma, and on the commercial Stable Diffusion API it improves by +10.31% (Gemma) and +7.95% (LLaVA Guard). Against Groot, the gap is largest: AdvPIE achieves roughly 2.4× the ASR of Groot on Flux (58.71% vs. 24.33%) and 2.6× on sd-3.5-large (57.83% vs. 22.24%). Under the conservative SafeSearch judge, all methods show very low ASR (<2%), but AdvPIE still leads on 3 of 4 models, confirming robustness even with narrow-category detectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agentic red-teaming paradigm (policy agent + judge agent) from text-only LLM jailbreaking to the multimodal T2I setting, where harmful intent is only observable in the generated image. It departs from prior black-box T2I red-teaming methods (FLIRT, Groot, ART) in two specific ways: (1) replacing single-iteration or fixed-group evaluation with a global-relative feedback mechanism that maintains a top-k memory of successful prompts and contrasts recent iterations, and (2) introducing Cumulative Adversarial Decoding, a token-level logit reweighting strategy that accumulates harmfulness scores across iterations without any model fine-tuning. Unlike ART, which requires supervised fine-tuning of the policy model, and unlike white-box methods (P4D, MMA-Diffusion) that need gradient access, AdvPIE operates entirely in a training-free, black-box regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that AdvPIE works on commercial APIs with restricted internal access and requires no model weights or fine-tuning, making it accessible to auditors and safety teams who cannot inspect model internals. A key open question is whether the implicit vulnerabilities it discovers persist as T2I models incorporate stronger safety alignment, and whether defenders can repurpose the same global-relative feedback and token-level scoring ideas for adaptive filtering. The paper acknowledges the dual-use risk: the same feedback-guided refinement loop that helps auditors could lower the barrier for adversaries, and the reliance on MLLM judges (Gemma 3, LLaVA Guard) as safety oracles introduces a potential circularity if those judges share blind spots with the target model.
