## Problem

Adapting 2D foundation models like SAM and SAM2 to volumetric (3D) or spatiotemporal (3D+T) segmentation remains challenging: existing adapters apply uniform transformations across all slices, cross-slice communication incurs extra parameters and compute, and no single architecture lifts both SAM (2D→3D) and SAM2 (2D+T→3D+T) with the same design. This paper addresses all three gaps with a unified, parameter-efficient framework that requires only ~2.8–3.7% trainable parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260329/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For 3D segmentation, the input is a 128×128×128 CT volume cropped around a single point prompt and replicated to three channels; the output is a 3D mask volume refined by a lightweight 3D convolution head. For 4D tracking, per-timestep inputs are 3×32×256×256 crops (2.0× padding around the target cell) spanning T=16 timesteps, and the output is a 4D instance-segmentation sequence with tracking; new point prompts are issued only when cells divide or enter the field of view, otherwise a single initial prompt drives end-to-end propagation via SAM2's memory bank.

## Method

SAM+D inserts two lightweight, model-agnostic modules into every frozen transformer block: (1) Depth-Routed LoRA (DRLoRA), which maintains E=4 parallel low-rank experts (rank r=16) and blends them via a small MLP router conditioned on the normalised slice depth z, injecting depth-dependent corrections into the Q and V projections while leaving K frozen; and (2) Depth Shift Module (DSM), which shifts α=25% of feature channels forward and backward between adjacent slices before self-attention, providing cross-slice context at zero added parameters or MAC operations. The same pair of modules lifts SAM's ViT-B from 2D to 3D and SAM2's Hiera-B+ from 2D+T to 3D+T without architectural redesign; in the 4D setting, LoRA is additionally injected into SAM2's memory attention to adapt temporal propagation to volumetric data. Training uses supervised DiceCE loss with only ~2.36 M (3D) or ~3.11 M (4D) trainable parameters out of ~84 M total, and the patch-free design processes all slices jointly in a single forward pass, preserving global spatial context.

## Results

On four 3D CT tumor segmentation benchmarks (KiTS, Pancreas, LiTS, Colon), SAM+D with the SAM prompt decoder (1 pt/volume) achieves Dice of 84.74%, 59.68%, 63.33%, and 63.70% respectively, with only 2.57 M trainable parameters (~2.8% of the 83.78 M total). On the 4D cell-tracking benchmark (Fluo-N3DH-SIM+), SAM2+D reaches SEG 56.79%, DET 87.32%, TRA 87.15%, and OP_CTB 71.97% under the Cellpose-detection setting with 3.11 M trainable parameters (~3.7%), surpassing all prior tracking baselines that plateau at ~54% SEG. In the GT-segmentation tracking mode, SAM2+D achieves TRA 99.31% and OP_CTB 99.47%, approaching the near-perfect scores of dedicated tracking algorithms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the 3D setting, SAM+D (SAM decoder, 1 pt/vol) outperforms the closest SAM-based adapter 3DSAM-adapter by +4.58 Dice on KiTS (84.74 vs. 80.16), +5.99 on Pancreas (59.68 vs. 53.69), +5.31 on LiTS (63.33 vs. 58.02), and +4.41 on Colon (63.70 vs. 59.29), while using only 2.57 M trainable parameters versus 25.46 M. It also surpasses Med-SA by +14.01 Dice on KiTS and +11.72 on Pancreas, and MA-SAM by +15.49 on KiTS and +24.96 on Pancreas, despite MA-SAM using 63.04 M trainable parameters. Against fully-supervised 3D methods, SAM+D beats nnU-Net on KiTS (+11.65 Dice), Pancreas (+17.98), and Colon (+19.32), though nnU-Net retains a slight edge on LiTS NSD (75.60 vs. 73.52). In the 4D setting, SAM2+D breaks the ~54% SEG ceiling shared by all CellPose-based baselines (BGU-IL 53.80, Ultrack 54.51, TrackASTRA 54.15, SAM2 slice-based 54.19) to reach 56.79% SEG and 71.97% OP_CTB, with the largest gains in DET (+2.12 over Ultrack) and TRA (+1.98 over Ultrack). Under GT-segmentation tracking, SAM2+D achieves TRA 99.31%, trailing only BGU-IL (99.96%) and TrackASTRA (99.98%) while using 3.11 M trainable parameters versus 127.70 M and 6.90 M respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAM+D sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, MoE-LoRA variants) and volumetric adaptation of 2D foundation models. It departs from prior SAM-to-3D adapters (3DSAM-adapter, Med-SA, MA-SAM) that inject 3D convolutions or factorised attention by instead using depth-position-routed LoRA experts and a zero-parameter channel-shift operator repurposed from TSM (originally for video). Unlike existing MoE-LoRA methods that route on token content and require load-balancing losses, DRLoRA routes on normalised slice depth, eliminating those auxiliary losses. The paper's key conceptual departure is unifying the SAM→3D and SAM2→4D lifts under a single module pair, whereas all prior adapter work targets one base model and one dimensionality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-4% trainable parameter budget makes SAM+D practical for domains where 3D/4D annotated data is scarce (medical imaging, cell tracking), and the unified design means new SAM-family models can be lifted without redesigning adapters. However, the 4D evaluation rests on a single synthetic dataset (Fluo-N3DH-SIM+), 4D training is computationally expensive (~876 GPU-hours), and the 3D pipeline treats only one anatomical axis as depth. Open questions include whether the approach extends to SAM3's text-prompted multimodal setting, whether multi-axis (sagittal/coronal/transverse) processing improves 3D quality, and whether the same dimensional-lifting principle transfers to the temporal axis for 2D→video.
