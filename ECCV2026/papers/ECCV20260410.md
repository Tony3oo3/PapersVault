## Problem

One-step diffusion video super-resolution models built on DiT backbones suffer from quadratic attention cost over dense spatio-temporal token sequences, making them impractical for real-time use. Existing token compression strategies (uniform merging or pruning) are applied indiscriminately across all depths and ignore motion dynamics, leading to irreversible detail loss and temporal flickering that cannot be corrected without multiple denoising steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260410/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality video (×4 upscaling factor) and produces a high-resolution video, operating entirely in the latent space of a pretrained VAE with a single forward pass through a one-step DiT denoiser. Notably, at inference time no text prompt or caption is required; all routing decisions are derived from the video content itself. The token drop ratio ρ serves as a single hyperparameter controlling the speed-quality trade-off.

## Method

TRaM-VSR performs adaptive token allocation by jointly leveraging video-context priors and network-level priors. An offline planner computes per-layer update magnitudes and end-to-end perturbation shifts to identify a small set of non-overlapping 'safe' depth intervals where compression is least risky. At each interval entry, tokens are scored by fusing text-similarity importance with Temporal Curvature Guidance (TCG), which measures frame-to-frame velocity direction changes to highlight motion-critical regions; top-K tokens are kept while the rest are mean-pooled into a compact global stream. Inside routed layers, kept tokens are processed in a high-fidelity local stream alongside the global stream, reducing attention cost from O(N²) to O((K+G)²). At the route exit, an identity restoration mechanism populates dropped positions with their original entry-time features, preventing irreversible detail loss while retaining the computational savings of the compacted layers.

## Results

TRaM-VSR achieves the best PSNR across all five HR-referenced benchmarks (e.g., 26.73 dB on UDM10, 23.18 dB on SPMCS, 24.38 dB on YouHQ40, 22.37 dB on RealVSR, 22.56 dB on MVSR4x) while also attaining the lowest flow-warping error E*warp on every dataset (1.73, 0.97, 1.70, 3.23, 0.75 respectively), confirming superior temporal consistency. On perceptual metrics, it is competitive with the strongest baselines: LPIPS of 0.2678 (UDM10) and 0.3373 (MVSR4x, best overall), and DISTS of 0.1450 (UDM10) and 0.2245 (MVSR4x, tied-best). All results are obtained under a strictly one-step inference setting without any text prompt at test time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest one-step competitor DOVE, TRaM-VSR wins on PSNR (e.g., 26.73 vs. 26.48 on UDM10; 22.37 vs. 22.32 on RealVSR) and E*warp (1.73 vs. 1.77 on UDM10; 0.97 vs. 1.04 on SPMCS) across all five datasets, while DOVE retains a slight edge on CLIP-IQA (0.5107 vs. 0.4547 on UDM10) and marginally on LPIPS/SSIM. Compared to the larger multi-step SeedVR2-7B, TRaM-VSR surpasses it on PSNR, E*warp, and CLIP-IQA on every dataset, though SeedVR2 edges ahead on LPIPS (0.2507 vs. 0.2678 on UDM10) and DISTS (0.1263 vs. 0.1450 on UDM10). Against earlier diffusion-based VSR methods (Upscale-A-Video, MGLD-VSR, STAR), TRaM-VSR consistently leads on PSNR and temporal consistency, with STAR showing the largest gap (e.g., 17.43 dB PSNR on RealVSR vs. 22.37 for TRaM-VSR). Non-diffusion baselines (Real-ESRGAN, ResShift, RealBasicVSR) are clearly outperformed on both fidelity and perceptual quality, with TRaM-VSR gaining 2–4 dB PSNR and substantially lower E*warp. The ablation against uniform token merging (DOVE-ToMeSD) and uniform pruning (DOVE-TinySR) on the identical DOVE backbone confirms that the importance-aware routing, rather than mere token reduction, is responsible for the quality gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRaM-VSR builds directly on one-step DiT-based VSR backbones (DOVE, OS-DiffVSR, UltraVSR) and extends the token-compression line (ToMe, VVidToMe, TinySR, LD-Pruner) by replacing uniform merging/pruning with a two-level adaptive scheme: content-driven importance scoring (text-similarity + temporal curvature guidance) coupled with a depth-calibrated offline planner that selects safe compression intervals. The key conceptual departure is the two-stream local/global architecture with identity restoration at route exits, which explicitly prevents the irreversible detail loss that plagues skip-free DiT stacks under aggressive token reduction. Unlike prior token-merging work that treats all layers identically, TRaM-VSR treats the DiT hierarchy as a multi-granularity system where different depths warrant different compression budgets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses the primary practical bottleneck of one-step DiT VSR—quadratic attention over dense spatio-temporal token sequences—without sacrificing the perceptual quality that makes diffusion priors attractive for restoration. The identity restoration mechanism is a clean, generalizable solution to the detail-loss problem in skip-free architectures, and the offline planner provides a predictable speed-quality knob for deployment. Limitations include residual oscillation in fine textures and fast motion, dependence on VAE decoding cost for end-to-end speedup, and the need for recalibration when switching backbones or degradation regimes. Open questions include whether routing intervals can be determined online or adaptively per input, and whether the framework transfers to multi-step or non-DiT restoration architectures.
