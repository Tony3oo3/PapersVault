## Problem

Existing visual token pruning methods for large vision-language models conflate two distinct sources of redundancy— intra-modal visual duplication and cross-modal task irrelevance—into a single scoring metric, leading to suboptimal pruning decisions. The paper demonstrates that these two factors are statistically orthogonal and should be addressed by separate, dedicated criteria rather than a coupled heuristic.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261669/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

ToDRE accepts RGB images (with or without AnyRes dynamic tiling producing global thumbnails plus local crops) or multi-frame video inputs, along with a natural-language question. After the vision encoder and projector, it retains only k diverse visual tokens (e.g., 288 out of 2880, ~10%) in the LLM embedding space, then further removes all remaining visual tokens at an adaptively identified decoder layer. The final output is the standard LLM text response, with the pruned token sequence fed through the remaining decoder layers and the full decoding phase.

## Method

ToDRE is a two-stage, training-free, plug-and-play framework. Stage 1 (Diversity-driven Token Selection) operates in the LLM embedding space after the vision encoder: it selects a pivot token via [CLS] attention from the last vision-encoder layer, then applies a greedy max-sum diversification algorithm that iteratively adds the token minimizing its cumulative cosine similarity to the already-selected set, retaining k maximally diverse tokens. Stage 2 (Relevance-driven Token Reduction) exploits the observed 'information migration' phenomenon—cross-modal attention (both visual-to-text and text-to-visual) is high in early decoder layers but fades in deeper layers—by computing global cross-modal attention ratios at a few selected deeper layers and, once both ratios fall below a threshold τ, dropping all remaining visual tokens from that layer onward. A theoretical analysis under an orthogonal subspace model proves that intra-modal diversity and cross-modal relevance are uncorrelated (zero covariance), justifying the two-stage decomposition. The framework is compatible with FlashAttention and requires no retraining, achieving 90% visual token pruning with a 2.6× inference speed-up while retaining 95% of original performance.

## Results

On LLaVA-NeXT-7B across eight image-language benchmarks, ToDRE achieves a 98.2% average performance ratio at 25% token retention (720 tokens) and 95.0% at 10% retention (288 tokens), outperforming the second-best method (DivPrune) by 1.6% and 1.5% respectively. On the 13B variant, ToDRE reaches 97.3% and 93.6% at the same ratios. In terms of efficiency, ToDRE reduces FLOPs by 80.9%, peak GPU memory by 14.5%, and achieves 1.9x throughput (2.9 samples/s on POPE) while maintaining the highest accuracy among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToDRE outperforms all five baselines at both 25% and 10% retention ratios on LLaVA-NeXT-7B. At 25% retention, ToDRE (98.2%) beats DivPrune (96.6%), FasterVLM (96.0%), GlobalCom2 (95.6%), FastV (95.4%), and SparseVLM (93.1%) by 1.6%, 2.2%, 2.6%, 2.8%, and 5.1% respectively. At the more aggressive 10% retention, ToDRE (95.0%) leads DivPrune (93.5%), GlobalCom2 (92.2%), FasterVLM (91.4%), FastV (88.8%), and SparseVLM (86.3%) by 1.5%, 2.8%, 3.6%, 6.2%, and 8.7%. On the 13B model, ToDRE (97.3% / 93.6%) similarly surpasses DivPrune (96.6% / 92.5%), GlobalCom2 (96.0% / 90.8%), and FasterVLM (95.6% / 89.1%). FastV and SparseVLM are excluded from the 13B comparison because their 7B-tuned pruning strategies degrade substantially when transferred, highlighting ToDRE's superior cross-scale robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

ToDRE builds on the greedy max-sum diversification algorithm [7] for its Stage 1 token selection, repurposing a classical submodular optimization technique for the novel context of visual token retention in LVLMs. Conceptually, it departs from prior token pruning methods (FastV, SparseVLM, FasterVLM, DivPrune) by explicitly disentangling intra-modal visual redundancy from cross-modal task-irrelevance, which existing attention-score, similarity, and divergence-based metrics conflate into a single scalar. The 'information migration' observation—that cross-modal attention is concentrated in early decoder layers and fades in deeper ones—provides a new empirical rationale for Stage 2, a modality-level pruning step not present in prior single-stage approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.6× inference speedup with 95% performance retention, achieved in a training-free and architecture-agnostic manner, makes ToDRE immediately deployable across diverse LVLM backbones (LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL2) without retraining. The clean two-stage decomposition (diversity then relevance) offers a principled template that could guide future token compression designs. Open questions include how the method behaves under very long-form generation (where decoding-stage gains would be larger), whether the orthogonality assumption holds for next-generation multimodal models with tighter vision-language coupling, and whether the fixed threshold τ for layer selection generalizes across tasks with varying visual dependence.
