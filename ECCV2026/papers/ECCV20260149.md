## Problem

Makeup transfer lacks real paired training data, forcing existing methods to rely on either weak unpaired priors (cycle consistency, domain adversarial losses) or synthetic pseudo-targets generated by external editing models. The pseudo-target paradigm imposes an inherent ceiling: the model systematically inherits the biases, identity drift, and fine-grained detail losses of its synthetic targets and can never surpass them. This paper addresses how to break that ceiling by anchoring supervision to the real reference image itself.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source face image and a reference makeup image (both RGB, up to 2048×2048) and produces a single makeup-transferred portrait that adopts the reference's cosmetic style while preserving the source's identity and geometry. Internally, it also requires a bare-skin counterpart of the reference (extracted by a frozen auxiliary makeup remover) and a synthetic pseudo-target (generated by an external editing model) to construct training triplets. All computation occurs in the latent space of a pretrained VAE, with condition injection via token concatenation of source, reference, and noisy target latents.

## Method

ART is a two-stage Diffusion Transformer framework built on FLUX.1-Kontext-dev, fine-tuned via LoRA with a Flow Matching objective. Stage I initializes the transfer model by regressing to synthetic pseudo-targets (establishing global makeup placement) and trains an auxiliary one-step makeup remover. Stage II introduces a reality-anchored refinement cycle: the model predicts a differentiable makeup carrier in latent space via a single Euler step at a controlled noise level σ_tr, then the same DiT (shared weights) reconstructs the real reference latent from its bare-skin counterpart conditioned solely on that carrier. Because the carrier remains differentiable, the reconstruction gradient back-propagates into the initial transfer prediction, directly penalizing omitted fine-grained cosmetic details and overriding pseudo-target artifacts. A controlled-noise bottleneck loss at σ_tr=0.6 preserves the coarse structural placement as a regularizer, preventing degenerate solutions while allowing the refinement cycle to recover high-fidelity textures from the authentic reference.

## Results

On the four evaluation sets (MT, LADN, MT-Wild, MF2K), ART achieves the highest VLM-based makeup similarity (MSim_G) across all sets, reaching 9.22 on the artistic MF2K set (vs. 8.43 for the next-best GPT Image 1.5) and 8.96 on MT. It also attains the lowest background-edit distance (L2-M) on every set (3.54–4.37), substantially outperforming StableMakeup (8.91–9.91) and commercial editors (10.38–43.59). Identity preservation (ArcFace ID) is best on MT (0.87) and competitive elsewhere (0.74–0.85), while FID is slightly higher than StableMakeup due to out-of-domain artistic textures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ART outperforms all seven baselines on VLM-based makeup similarity (MSim_G) across every test set, with the largest margin on the complex MF2K artistic set (9.22 vs. 8.43 for GPT Image 1.5 and 8.34 for Nano Banana Pro). Against the strongest diffusion baseline StableMakeup, ART improves identity preservation from 0.49 to 0.87 (MT) and reduces background edits from 9.55 to 3.94 (L2-M), while also gaining ~0.6 in MSim_G. Commercial editors (Nano Banana Pro, GPT Image 1.5) achieve competitive makeup similarity but suffer severe identity drift (ID 0.28–0.67) and large background modifications (L2-M 10–44), which ART avoids. SHMT retains a slight edge on identity (0.82–0.88 vs. 0.74–0.85) but at a substantial cost in makeup fidelity (MSim_G 3.11–5.87 vs. 8.77–9.22) and background stability. GAN-based methods (PSGAN, EleGANt) and MAD are clearly outperformed on all metrics, with MAD additionally unable to report background stability. The user study (21 participants, 864 ratings) confirms these quantitative findings: ART ranks first on Makeup Similarity (3.67/5), ID Consistency (4.03/5), and Image Quality (3.83/5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FLUX.1-Kontext-dev DiT backbone and the Flow Matching training objective, extending them with a two-stage makeup transfer strategy. It departs from the dominant pseudo-target regression paradigm (StableMakeup, EvoMakeup) and the weak unpaired priors of earlier GAN/diffusion methods (CycleGAN, SHMT, MAD) by introducing a reality-anchored refinement cycle: instead of supervising the output against a synthetic target, the model is constrained to reconstruct the real reference from its bare-skin counterpart through a differentiable latent carrier. This is conceptually distinct from traditional cycle consistency, which only enforces invertibility back to the source, because the reconstruction target is the authentic reference itself, providing instance-level detail supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pseudo-target ceiling is a fundamental limitation in current makeup transfer: a model trained to imitate synthetic targets can never surpass their quality. ART's refinement cycle offers a general recipe for breaking this ceiling in DiT-based editing, and the authors explicitly frame it as applicable beyond makeup to other image editing tasks. The introduction of MF2K, the first 2K-resolution in-the-wild makeup dataset, addresses a practical gap for high-resolution applications (cinematic post-production, virtual avatars). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other editing domains without task-specific auxiliary models (e.g., the makeup remover), and the acknowledged limitation that physical lighting and reflectance consistency are not enforced, which can produce contradictory results under illumination mismatch.
