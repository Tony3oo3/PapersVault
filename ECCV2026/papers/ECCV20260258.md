## Problem

Imitation learning for end-to-end autonomous driving assumes that geometric proximity to expert trajectories implies behavioral safety, but driving exhibits a critical 'safety asymmetry' where trajectories with nearly identical imitation losses can lead to drastically different outcomes (recoverable vs. collision). Existing multi-modal generation and scoring methods can identify unsafe trajectories at inference but discard them without ever using them for policy optimization, leaving the learned policy without explicit safety boundaries between good and bad imitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260258/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The planner consumes a front-view RGB image, LiDAR point cloud (N×3, for the multi-modal TransFuser v7 variant), ego-vehicle state (velocity and acceleration), and a discrete navigation command, producing an 8-waypoint trajectory over a 4-second horizon at 2 Hz. Trajectories are parameterized in first-order differences (Δx, Δy, sin h, cos h) rather than raw coordinates to avoid heading-angle discontinuity and smooth the learning target. The flow matching generator internally produces 64 candidate trajectories per scene, from which a single hard negative is selected via a two-stage safety-then-proximity filter.

## Method

BeyondDrive is a two-stage failure-aware contrastive framework. In Stage 1, a conditional flow matching model (Transformer Decoder + LayerNorm + FFN, trained with L1 velocity regression) generates diverse trajectory candidates near expert demonstrations using classifier-free guidance (w=0.5) and enlarged sampling noise (σ_sample=2.0σ) to prevent mode collapse; a two-stage selection then picks the trajectory that is both unsafe (PDMS below threshold) and spatially closest to the expert. In Stage 2, a Repulsive Distance Loss (RD Loss) defined as −min(‖Δτ̂ − Δτ_neg‖₁, C) is added to the standard L1 imitation loss, simultaneously pulling predictions toward expert trajectories and pushing them away from the synthesized hard negative. This contrastive objective is architecture-agnostic and requires only a single additional loss term, yielding consistent PDMS/EPDMS gains when applied to uni-modal (LTF), diffusion-based (DiffusionDrive), flow-based (MeanFuser), and world-model-based (WoTE) planners.

## Results

On the NAVSIM v1 navtest benchmark, LTFv7 (camera-only) achieves 89.7 PDMS and TransFuser v7 (camera+LiDAR) achieves 89.6 PDMS, surpassing all prior methods including VADv2 (89.3), AutoVLA (89.1), and MeanFuser (89.0). On the reactive NAVSIM v2 benchmark, both variants exceed 90 EPDMS (90.1 and 90.0 respectively), outperforming MeanFuser (89.6) and DiffusionDrive (88.3). On the zero-shot HUGSIM closed-loop benchmark, LTFv7 achieves 34.8 HD-Score overall, improving over LTF by +10.0 and UniAD by +5.9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LTFv7 (89.7 PDMS) outperforms all 20+ baselines on NAVSIM v1, with the largest margins over Hydra-MDP (+3.2), DiffusionDrive (+1.6), and WoTE (+1.4), and the closest competition from VADv2 (+0.4) and AutoVLA/DriveLaW (+0.6). On NAVSIM v2, LTFv7 (90.1 EPDMS) beats MeanFuser (+0.5) and DiffusionDrive (+1.8), while TransFuser v7 (90.0) similarly surpasses all baselines. On HUGSIM zero-shot evaluation, LTFv7 (34.8 HD-Score) substantially outperforms LTF (+10.0), UniAD (+5.9), and both VAD and MeanFuser (+22.5), demonstrating strong cross-dataset generalization. The gains are most pronounced in safety-critical metrics: NC improves by +1.0 and DAC by +5.1 over LTF, indicating the hard-negative contrastive learning primarily reduces collisions and drivable-area violations rather than merely improving progress.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the imitation-learning paradigm for end-to-end driving (TransFuser, UniAD, VAD) by introducing contrastive safety optimization via synthesized hard negatives, building on flow matching (Lipman et al.) and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans) as the generative backbone for negative trajectory synthesis. It departs from the multi-modal generation-and-scoring line (DiffusionDrive, MeanFuser, VADv2, WoTE), which identifies unsafe trajectories at inference but discards them, by explicitly feeding expert-proximate failures back into policy training through a Repulsive Distance Loss. The framework is applied on top of the Latent TransFuser (LTF) baseline from the NAVSIM benchmark, positioning itself as a training-time augmentation rather than an architectural change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in imitation-based driving: the implicit assumption that geometric proximity to expert trajectories guarantees behavioral safety, and shows that contrastive repulsion from synthesized failures yields consistent (if modest) gains across uni-modal and multi-modal planners. Its practical appeal lies in modularity—adding one loss term improves DiffusionDrive, MeanFuser, and WoTE without architectural changes—making it an easy drop-in for existing pipelines. Limitations include trajectory-level safety only (no long-horizon interaction modeling), dependence on a scoring function for negative selection, and constrained integration with vocabulary-based planners like VADv2. Open questions include whether the flow-matching generator can capture rare, multi-agent failure modes and whether the approach transfers to fully reactive closed-loop settings beyond NAVSIM v2.
