## Problem

Existing generative approaches to monocular geometry estimation either train independent task-specific models (depth or normals separately), losing inter-task correlations, or jointly fine-tune modified image diffusion backbones with architectural changes (cross-attention, switchers) that widen the pretraining–finetuning gap and demand substantially more labeled data. Neither family explicitly models image–geometry interactions, leading to cross-modal inconsistencies and degraded geometric fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261244/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and jointly produces an affine-invariant depth map (predicted in disparity space, d = 1/d′) and a 3-channel surface normal map. The single-channel depth is tiled to three channels for VAE encoding and averaged back at decode; both geometry targets are normalized to [−1, 1] to match the VAE input range. Training resolutions are 576×768 (Hypersim) and 352×1216 (Virtual KITTI), and inference uses only 5 EDM denoising steps with a 5-run noise-seed ensemble.

## Method

GeoNeXt repurposes a pretrained Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) image-to-video model by reformulating geometry estimation as next-frame prediction: the input RGB image is the first frame, and depth and surface normals are the subsequent frames in a shared denoising trajectory. The image latent is replicated into the geometry slots and concatenated with the noisy geometry latents to form the conditioning input, while the CLIP cross-attention branch is removed to avoid geometry-distorting image resizing. Only the denoising U-Net is fine-tuned (frozen SD VAE, EDM objective) on ~59K RGB–depth–normal triplets from Hypersim and Virtual KITTI. The key insight is that the temporal-attention priors learned during video pretraining naturally capture cross-frame dependencies, which are repurposed to model image↔geometry consistency, enabling data-efficient joint estimation without architectural modifications that would break transfer from pretraining.

## Results

On zero-shot affine-invariant depth estimation, GeoNeXt (trained on only 59K samples) achieves AbsRel of 5.3 on NYUv2, 8.2 on KITTI, 5.6 on ETH3D, 5.9 on ScanNet, and 22.6 on DIODE, with corresponding δ₁ values of 96.8, 92.6, 97.2, 95.8, and 74.3. For surface normal estimation, GeoNeXt achieves mean angular errors of 16.7° (NYUv2), 16.0° (ScanNet), 16.4° (iBims-1), 33.0° (Sintel), and 22.8° (OASIS). Notably, GeoNeXt surpasses the large-scale discriminative model DepthAnything (62.6M training images) on ETH3D (5.6 vs. 12.7 AbsRel) and DIODE (22.6 vs. 26.0 AbsRel) while using ~100× less data, and outperforms the unified generative baseline GeoWizard (208K samples) on every depth and normal benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unified generative baseline GeoWizard (208K samples, 3.5× more data), GeoNeXt wins on all five depth benchmarks, with the largest margins on KITTI (8.2 vs. 14.4 AbsRel) and DIODE (22.6 vs. 33.0 AbsRel), and on all five normal benchmarks (e.g., 16.4° vs. 19.4° on iBims-1, 33.0° vs. 40.3° on Sintel). Among depth-only generative methods at similar scale, GeoNeXt outperforms Lotus-G (59K) on NYUv2 (5.3 vs. 5.4 AbsRel), KITTI (8.2 vs. 8.5), and ETH3D (5.6 vs. 5.9), and beats Marigold/E2E-FT (74K) on ETH3D and DIODE. Against the large-scale discriminative model DepthAnything (62.6M images), GeoNeXt achieves superior ETH3D (5.6 vs. 12.7 AbsRel) and DIODE (22.6 vs. 26.0) results but trails on NYUv2 (5.3 vs. 4.3), KITTI (8.2 vs. 7.6), and ScanNet (5.9 vs. 4.3). For surface normals, GeoNeXt surpasses the discriminative baselines EESNU (2.5M) and DSINE (160K) on most datasets, and outperforms the generative normal-only method StableNormal (250K) on NYUv2 (16.7° vs. 18.6°), ScanNet (16.0° vs. 17.1°), iBims-1 (16.4° vs. 18.2°), Sintel (33.0° vs. 36.7°), and OASIS (22.8° vs. 26.5°). Overall, GeoNeXt is the only method that simultaneously achieves top or near-top performance on both depth and normals with the least training data (59K), validating the data-efficiency of the video-diffusion next-frame formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoNeXt extends the Marigold-style paradigm of repurposing pretrained diffusion models for geometry estimation, but departs from image diffusion backbones (Stable Diffusion) by adopting a video diffusion model (Stable Video Diffusion) and reformulating geometry prediction as next-frame prediction. This reframing allows the model's native temporal attention to serve as a mechanism for joint image-geometry modeling, addressing a limitation of prior image-diffusion approaches (Marigold, GeoWizard, Lotus-G) that either train separate task-specific models or require architectural modifications that widen the pretraining-to-finetuning gap. The method also builds on the EDM preconditioned denoiser formulation for stable training across noise levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is data efficiency: GeoNeXt achieves competitive or superior zero-shot depth and normal estimation using only ~59K synthetic training samples, rivaling discriminative models trained on 62.6M+ images, while unifying both geometric targets in a single model. This suggests that video generative priors encode sufficient structural knowledge to transfer to geometry with minimal task-specific supervision. Open questions include whether the approach scales to DiT-based video models (mentioned but deferred to supplementary), whether it extends to metric depth or other geometric targets, and whether the 5-step ensemble at inference can be further distilled for real-time deployment.
