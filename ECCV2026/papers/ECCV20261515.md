## Problem

Existing VLM-based anomaly detection methods degrade in industrial settings because they rely on frame-level captions and external prompt engineering rather than tracking how individual objects physically transform over time. Traditional VAD methods require per-class training on normal samples and emit only binary scores without semantic explanations. The paper identifies that the core bottleneck is not language reasoning but the absence of object-centric, spatio-temporal state evidence—the very cue human inspectors use to detect anomalies during manufacturing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261515/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw industrial process video (no annotations, labels, or domain specifications required) and produces a structured, open-ended anomaly report per detected event: a free-form anomaly type, severity rating (none/severe), the affected object index, a grounded frame range, and a natural-language causal explanation. Video- and frame-level anomaly scores are derived by max-pooling over per-object confidences. The entire pipeline is training-free and taxonomy-free, so anomaly types are generated as free-form text rather than selected from a fixed set, enabling detection of novel failure modes.

## Method

O-VAD is a three-stage, training-free agentic pipeline that shifts the unit of analysis from holistic frames to individual objects. Stage 1 discovers all task-relevant objects by sampling multiple frames, querying a VLM (GPT-5) for a structured inventory, and segmenting each with SAM3. Stage 2 constructs spatiotemporal tubelets via CropFormer entity segmentation and SAM2 temporal propagation, recovers tracks lost during object transformations using spatial-proximity and CLIP-based semantic-consistency priors, and queries the VLM at key temporal transitions to produce open-ended state-change events (type, cause, severity, frame range). Stage 3 feeds the accumulated per-object state trajectories into a cascaded six-step chain-of-thought (process understanding → observation → expectation → comparison → causation → classification) that separates expected mechanical responses from genuine failures, followed by confidence-tiered visual verification that re-examines evidence frames to suppress false positives via multiplicative confidence gating.

## Results

O-VAD achieves the best video-level AUROC among all methods (both trained and training-free) on all three benchmarks: 0.584 on Phys-AD, 0.692 on LiquidAD, and 0.565 on IPAD. On Phys-AD, it also attains the highest type-level BERTScore (0.803) and LLM-judge score (0.595) among training-free methods, indicating the most semantically faithful anomaly descriptions. On LiquidAD, O-VAD reaches 0.948 video-level recall and 0.929 F1, substantially outperforming direct-prompting VLMs that collapse to near-zero recall. The method is entirely training-free, requiring no domain-specific knowledge, labels, or fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

O-VAD outperforms all six baselines on video-level AUROC across all three datasets. Against the two trained traditional VADs, O-VAD (training-free) exceeds MNAD.p by 0.089 AUROC on Phys-AD (0.584 vs 0.495) and by 0.041 on IPAD (0.565 vs 0.522), and surpasses S3R by 0.029 on Phys-AD (0.584 vs 0.555) and 0.041 on LiquidAD (0.692 vs 0.651). Among direct-prompting VLMs, O-VAD beats GPT-5 by 0.082 AUROC on Phys-AD (0.584 vs 0.502) and 0.227 on LiquidAD (0.692 vs 0.465), and Qwen3-VL-32B by 0.071 on Phys-AD and 0.203 on LiquidAD. Against the two agentic workflows, O-VAD leads URF-ZS-HVAA by 0.158 AUROC on Phys-AD (0.584 vs 0.426) and 0.327 on LiquidAD (0.692 vs 0.365), and VERA by 0.128 on Phys-AD and 0.158 on LiquidAD. Notably, on LiquidAD frame-level recall, O-VAD achieves 0.614 versus 0.000 for both Qwen3 and GPT-5, highlighting that caption-level prompting fails to localize per-instance anomalies in multi-object scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

O-VAD departs from both traditional VAD (reconstruction/embedding-based, requiring per-class training) and recent VLM-based anomaly detection methods that rely on caption-level prompting or frame-level spatio-temporal fusion. Its conceptual lineage draws on object-tracking infrastructure (TubeletGraph, SAM2/SAM3, CropFormer) and repurposes it within a VLM-driven agentic pipeline, shifting the unit of analysis from holistic frames to per-object state trajectories. The closest prior work—agentic frameworks like URF-ZS-HVAA and VERA—also chain reasoning steps but operate on caption or frame-level evidence; O-VAD's key departure is maintaining structured, open-ended per-object state change events as the grounding substrate for downstream chain-of-thought reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, object-centric formulation offers a practical path toward deployable industrial inspection that produces interpretable, open-ended anomaly reports (type, affected object, frame range, causal explanation) without per-class retraining or domain-specific prompt engineering. However, the multi-stage pipeline incurs substantial latency scaling with object count, and the method's reliance on VLM-internalized commonsense creates a hard boundary for anomalies defined by invisible physical properties or precise numerical specifications. Open questions include whether few-shot in-context learning with process specifications can close this gap, whether multi-granularity captioning (as hinted by the screw ablation) can adapt reasoning to the anomaly's spatio-temporal scale, and how to integrate lightweight domain priors for perception-ambiguous defects like stuck buttons or degaussed magnets.
