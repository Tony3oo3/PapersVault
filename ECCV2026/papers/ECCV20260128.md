## Problem

The problem is to estimate the geographic location of a given image. Existing methods struggle with ambiguous or occluded visual cues, and lack of comprehensive geographic coverage. This paper addresses this limitation by introducing a new dataset with richer geographic annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260128/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a street-view image. The output is the geographic location (country, region, city) and a reasoning trajectory.

## Method

The method uses a vision-language model to jointly reason over visual and textual geographic features, combining a multimodal encoder with a location knowledge base.
![Method overview](../imgs/ECCV20260128/cropped_page7_idx2.webp)

## Results

On the GWS15k benchmark, GaGA achieves 63.06% country-level and 6.28% city-level administrative accuracy, surpassing the strongest MLLM baseline GLOBE (47.09% / 3.44%) by 15.97% and 2.84% respectively, and outperforming the traditional Hybrid classifier (58.49% / 3.36%) by 4.57% and 2.92%. At the coordinate level, GaGA reaches 33.9% Acc@200km and 82.2% Acc@2500km with a Geoscore of 3113.0, the highest among all reproducible models. On OSV-5M-test, GaGA secures the best city-level accuracy (7.4%) and a Geoscore of 3413, exceeding the Hybrid baseline (6.0% city, 3361 Geoscore).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaGA decisively outperforms all general-purpose MLLMs (LLaVA-Llama3, InternVL2-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B) by large margins, with country-level accuracy ranging from 1.76% to 39.10% versus GaGA's 63.06%. Among specialized geolocation MLLMs, GaGA surpasses GeoReasoner (40.63% country) by 22.43% and GLOBE (47.09%) by 15.97% at the country level, while also leading on city-level accuracy (6.28% vs. 1.11% and 3.44%). Against the strongest traditional classifier, Hybrid [2], GaGA gains +4.57% (country) and +2.92% (city), and achieves a higher Geoscore (3113.0 vs. 2944.9). On coordinate accuracy, GaGA leads all MLLM baselines (GeoReasoner, GRE, GLOBE) at every threshold from 25 km to 2500 km, though Pigeon [16] retains a slight edge at 2500 km (85.1% vs. 82.2%). On OSV-5M-test, GaGA achieves the best city-level accuracy (7.4%) but trails RFM S2 [13] on Geoscore (3413 vs. 3767) and country-level accuracy (71.4% vs. 76.2%), indicating that the generative approach still lags behind the strongest diffusion-based model on macro-scale metrics while excelling at fine-grained city identification.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaGA builds directly on the LLaVA-style modular MLLM architecture (CLIP-ViT encoder + MLP projector + Llama3-8B) and extends the generation-based geolocation line (GeoReasoner, GLOBE, GRE) by introducing a multi-turn interactive reasoning loop rather than a single-shot prediction. The key conceptual departure is the formalization of the 'Similarity Trap'—where geographically widespread visual motifs cause over-confident misclassification—and the Tiered Interaction Protocol that lets user-provided anchors prune the hypothesis space. The MG-Geo dataset itself is positioned as the first to combine planet-scale street-view metadata (from OSV-5M) with expert-guided chain-of-thought dialogues (from GeoGuessr/Tuxun communities and GPT-4V), bridging the gap between static geolocation benchmarks and reasoning-oriented multimodal corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interactive geolocation paradigm reframes a traditionally static regression/classification problem as an iterative, evidence-accumulating dialogue, which is arguably more aligned with how humans actually localize unfamiliar places. The MG-Geo dataset (4.87M meta entries, 70K clue samples, 73K dialog samples across 210 countries) is a substantial open resource that could benefit the broader MLLM geolocation community. However, absolute city-level accuracy remains modest (~6–7%), the interactive gains depend on the user supplying high-specificity anchors, and the 8B parameter scale limits deployment in latency-sensitive settings. Open questions include whether the Similarity Trap formalization generalizes to non-street-view imagery (satellite, indoor) and whether the CoT Introspection mechanism can be replaced by learned self-correction without the 25 km Haversine threshold heuristic.
