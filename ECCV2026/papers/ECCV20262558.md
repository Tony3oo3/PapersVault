## Problem

Standard physics-ready 3D reconstruction pipelines decouple surface extraction from tetrahedralization, introducing error-prone intermediate steps. Recent Lagrangian methods like TetSphere Splatting bypass this by optimizing volumetric primitives directly, but their homeomorphic constraints force a fixed set of disjoint tetrahedral spheres that cannot adapt topology during optimization, producing fragmented meshes unsuitable for physical simulation.

## Input / Output

The method takes 120 multi-view RGB images per scene (with every 10th frame held out as test views) and produces a single, topologically coherent tetrahedral mesh with 96.7% single-component rate, directly usable for FEM-based physical simulation without any post-hoc tetrahedralization. It also renders novel views at 50 FPS using 7.56 GB of GPU memory on a single RTX 4090. The tetrahedral mesh quality is characterized by a minimum dihedral angle of 39.5°, aspect ratio of 2.554, and only 0.017 inverted ratio after a lightweight vertex-reordering cleanup.

## Method

HoloTetSphere initializes a coarse watertight tetrahedral mesh via 2DGS point cloud → convex hull → Delaunay tetrahedralization, then performs end-to-end topology and geometry optimization. The key topological mechanism couples each tetrahedron to a Gaussian sphere whose center is barycentrically interpolated from the tetrahedron's vertices; a continuous vertex implicit field (φ) is assigned per vertex, and element opacity is derived via barycentric averaging followed by a sigmoid mapping, enabling differentiable pruning that preserves spatial coherence across adjacent elements. Geometry refinement uses an alternating schedule (5k-iteration blocks) between tetrahedral and Gaussian optimization, where Gaussian-rendered normals supervise tetrahedral mesh normals via a masked cosine loss. A two-stage HC-Laplacian energy (first pushing out concavities, then global smoothing) combined with a weighted bi-harmonic term that reduces regularization on surface vertices prevents the shrinkage and detail loss that uniform smoothing would cause after topological updates.

## Results

On the combined 3D dataset (Thingi10k, DeepFashion3D, Objaverse, Google Scanned Objects), HoloTetSphere achieves the best Chamfer distance (0.0102), Chamfer L2 (0.0163), and Hausdorff distance (0.1118), outperforming all Lagrangian baselines and the Eulerian NeuS2. It also attains near-best Volume IoU (0.7268 vs. NeuS2's 0.7275) and Recall (0.7442 vs. NeuS2's 0.7754). For physical simulation suitability, the method produces a 96.7% single-connected-component rate with an average of 1.03 components, compared to TetSphere's 0% single-component rate and 61.21 average components. Rendering quality reaches 40.68 PSNR and 0.024 LPIPS, surpassing 2DGS (40.25 PSNR) and NeuS2 (37.73 PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeuS2 (Eulerian), HoloTetSphere wins on all distance-based metrics (Chamfer 0.0102 vs. 0.0118, Hausdorff 0.1118 vs. 0.2419) and matches its Volume IoU (0.7268 vs. 0.7275), while NeuS2 retains a slight edge on Recall (0.7754 vs. 0.7442). Against 2DGS, the method improves Chamfer by 22% (0.0102 vs. 0.0130) and Hausdorff by 17% (0.1118 vs. 0.1337), and also surpasses it in rendering (40.68 vs. 40.25 PSNR). Against DMesh, HoloTetSphere leads on every geometry metric, with the largest gap on Chamfer L2 (0.0163 vs. 0.0213, a 23% reduction). Against TetSphere (the closest prior method), the advantage is most pronounced: Chamfer improves by 41% (0.0102 vs. 0.0173), Hausdorff by 37% (0.1118 vs. 0.1784), and Volume IoU by 27% (0.7268 vs. 0.5712). Critically for physical simulation, HoloTetSphere produces a 96.7% single-component mesh rate versus TetSphere's 0%, and reduces the inverted tetrahedra ratio from 5.7% to 1.7%, making the output directly usable in FEM without post-processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TetSphere Splatting (Guo et al., ICLR 2025), which introduced tetrahedral spheres as Lagrangian volumetric primitives for multi-view 3D reconstruction, by replacing its fixed homeomorphic deformation with a topology-adaptive pruning mechanism. The key departure is a vertex-shared continuous opacity field (inspired by classical topology optimization à la Bendsøe & Sigmund) that enables differentiable element removal, breaking TetSphere's reliance on initialization quality and its production of disjoint primitives. The alternating geometric refinement draws on HC-Laplacian smoothing and weighted bi-harmonic energy from mesh processing literature, while the Gaussian coupling for rendering inherits from 3D/2D Gaussian Splatting. The paper positions itself against the standard two-stage pipeline (surface extraction followed by TetGen tetrahedralization) and against Eulerian implicit methods like NeuS2 that lack volumetric coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in producing a single, connected, topologically coherent tetrahedral mesh directly from multi-view images, eliminating the fragile and error-prone tetrahedralization step that causes 10–33% failure rates in surface-based pipelines (NeuS2, 2DGS, DMesh). This opens a more direct path to FEM-based physical simulation of reconstructed objects. However, the approach is limited to pruning (it cannot add material), struggles with structures thinner than ~0.34 relative thickness, and requires a lightweight post-hoc cleanup for occasional element inversions. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-object scenes, non-uniform materials, or topology changes beyond deletion, and whether the simulation fidelity generalizes beyond the simple gravity-drop benchmarks tested here.
