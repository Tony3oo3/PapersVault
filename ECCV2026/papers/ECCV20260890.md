## Problem

Existing reference-guided image inpainting methods are typically optimized for specific object domains (garments, portraits, rigid objects) using shared parameter pathways, leading to significant cross-category performance degradation. Additionally, these methods rely heavily on pixel-accurate segmentation masks, making them fragile when users provide coarse or imprecise spatial guidance. Existing training datasets further compound the problem with highly homogeneous category distributions and limited coverage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260890/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a reference image containing the object to insert, a target image where the object should be placed, a user-provided mask at arbitrary precision (from pixel-accurate segmentation to a simple bounding box), and an optional text prompt. All images are processed at 1024×1024 resolution. The output is a single edited image where the reference object is seamlessly integrated into the masked region of the target scene, preserving object identity while maintaining natural consistency with the surrounding context.

## Method

A²-Edit builds on the FLUX.1 Fill DiT backbone and introduces a Mixture of Transformers (MoT) architecture that extends expert routing beyond the conventional FFN-only design to both Attention and FFN modules, using lightweight LoRA adapters (rank 32, 8 experts) as category-specialized subnetworks. An Anchor-Guided Routing strategy keeps one backbone expert always active for universal knowledge while conditionally activating assistant experts based on routing weights, enabling differentiated modeling of rigid vs. non-rigid objects. Training follows a three-stage Mask Annealing Training Strategy (MATS): fine-mask training, augmented rough-mask training (Perlin-noise-perturbed contours), and bounding-box-only training, progressively shifting the model from spatial-constraint reliance to contextual semantic reasoning. The entire system is trained on the newly constructed UniEdit-500K dataset (500,104 pairs across 8 categories and 209 subcategories) for 6000 steps with a 3000/1500/1500 step split across the three mask-precision stages.

## Results

On VITON-HD with fine masks, A²-Edit achieves DINO-I 64.07, CLIP-I 81.53, and LPIPS 0.0677, outperforming the strongest baseline Insert Anything (62.15 / 79.96 / 0.0728) by +1.92 DINO-I and −0.0051 LPIPS. Under rough masks, the advantage widens: A²-Edit reaches DINO-I 63.79 and LPIPS 0.0685 versus Insert Anything's 60.87 and 0.0809, a gap of +2.92 DINO-I. On the multi-category AnyInsertion benchmark, A²-Edit leads all baselines on every metric in both fine- and rough-mask settings, with the largest margin over FLUX.1-Fill (e.g., +11.36 DINO-I on rough masks). On the UniEdit-500K test set, A²-Edit further attains the best VLM score (82.3 fine / 79.3 rough) and lowest FID (57.72 / 54.62), confirming cross-category generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A²-Edit outperforms all six baselines on every metric across both VITON-HD and AnyInsertion, in both fine- and rough-mask settings. The closest competitor is Insert Anything, which A²-Edit surpasses by +1.92 DINO-I on VITON-HD fine masks and by +2.92 DINO-I / −0.0124 LPIPS on VITON-HD rough masks, indicating that the Mask Annealing Training Strategy yields the largest gains when mask quality degrades. ACE++ is competitive on fine masks (within ~2 DINO-I) but falls further behind under rough masks (−5.01 DINO-I on VITON-HD), confirming its sensitivity to mask precision. FLUX.1-Kontext, the strongest text-guided baseline, trails A²-Edit by 3.98 DINO-I on VITON-HD fine masks and 7.64 DINO-I on rough masks, highlighting the advantage of explicit mask conditioning over purely text-driven editing. The older U-Net-based methods (FLUX.1-Fill, AnyDoor, MimicBrush) are consistently 4–11 points behind on DINO-I, with MimicBrush showing the largest degradation under rough masks (LPIPS 0.2011 vs. A²-Edit's 0.0903 on AnyInsertion). On the broader UniEdit-500K test set, A²-Edit extends its lead with the best VLM score (82.3 vs. 78.7 for Insert Anything) and lowest FID (57.72 vs. 59.99), demonstrating that the Mixture-of-Transformers architecture and multi-category training data translate into genuine cross-category generalization rather than domain-specific tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reference-guided image inpainting line (AnyDoor, MimicBrush, ACE++, Insert Anything) by replacing the shared-parameter DiT backbone with a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture that routes features through category-specific LoRA experts in both Attention and FFN layers, departing from the standard NLP MoE design that restricts sparsity to FFN only. The Mask Annealing Training Strategy (progressive mask degradation via Perlin-noise perturbation and bounding-box relaxation) is a novel curriculum-learning mechanism not seen in prior mask-guided editing work. The UniEdit-500K dataset generalizes beyond the single-domain focus of VITON-HD (garments) or FreeEdit (animals/plants) to eight major categories, addressing the homogenization bottleneck the authors identify in existing training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in decoupling editing quality from mask precision and domain specificity, which are the two main barriers to deploying reference-guided inpainting in open-ended creative tools (e-commerce, virtual try-on, scene composition). The UniEdit-500K dataset and the MoT+MATS recipe provide a reusable recipe for building unified editors, though the approach is tied to the FLUX.1 Fill backbone and 1024×1024 resolution, limiting immediate transfer to other architectures. Open questions include whether the 8-expert routing truly encodes semantic category structure or merely adds capacity, how the method scales to long-tail or compositional objects, and whether the VLM-based evaluation metric is sufficiently reliable for fine-grained boundary quality assessment.
