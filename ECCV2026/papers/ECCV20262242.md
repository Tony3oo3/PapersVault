## Problem

Existing 4D reconstruction methods assume globally consistent topology or smooth deformation fields, making them ill-suited for plants that undergo topology changes as new organs emerge, exhibit asynchronous organ-level growth, and lack temporal correspondences for newly formed tissue. No prior approach jointly models continuous non-rigid deformation, organ identity, and topology changes under sparse, noisy longitudinal acquisition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262242/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a time-ordered sequence of LiDAR point clouds where every point carries a semantic instance label identifying its organ (stem or leaf), with per-organ PCA-based canonical alignment applied to leaves before modelling. It produces a shared continuous velocity field f_θ(x, t, z_i) that, when integrated via Euler steps from each organ's initial point cloud, yields dense 4D trajectories at arbitrary timesteps; the full-plant output is the union of all organ trajectories reprojected to the global frame via per-frame rigid transforms (SLERP-interpolated for unobserved frames).

## Method

GrowFields decomposes the plant into organs, aligns each leaf to a canonical frame (principal axis → Z, base → origin), and learns a single shared SIREN network that maps (x, t, z_i) to a 3D velocity, where z_i is a learnable per-organ latent code initialised from N(0, σ²I) and regularised toward zero. Following the auto-decoder paradigm, no encoder is used; latent codes and network weights are optimised jointly by minimising a multi-step bidirectional Chamfer distance between integrated trajectories and ground-truth point clouds at every intermediate timestep. The shared field captures cross-organ geometric and temporal priors while the latent codes encode organ-specific identity and growth characteristics, enabling asynchronous development within one unified representation. At inference, each organ's initial point cloud is propagated forward through the field and reprojected to global coordinates, with newly observed organs simply incorporated upon first appearance rather than being predicted.

## Results

On the TrackPlant3D benchmark (8 full-plant sequences, 4 species), GrowFields (full) achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 0.86 mm² and mean End-Point Error of 1.47 mm on annotated leaf tips, the best across all evaluated methods. The closest part-aware competitor, COAP, scores 1.69 CD and 4.23 EPE, while the strongest full-plant baseline DPF reaches 2.47 CD and 15.15 EPE. Global deformation-field methods (NDF, NVFi, CanFields) are orders of magnitude worse in CD (615–2229 mm²) and tracking (42–44 mm EPE), confirming that organ-aware decomposition is critical for plant growth modelling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GrowFields outperforms all six baselines on both CD and EPE in the full-plant setting. Against the strongest full-plant baseline DPF (CD 2.47, EPE 15.15), GrowFields reduces CD by ~65% and EPE by ~90%, showing that organ-level decomposition and latent conditioning are far more effective than a single global point deformation field. The part-aware COAP baseline (CD 1.69, EPE 4.23) is the closest competitor; GrowFields halves the EPE (1.47 vs 4.23) and reduces CD by ~49%, demonstrating that a shared latent-conditioned field captures inter-organ correlations better than independent per-part fields. Global SDF and diffeomorphic-flow methods (NDF, NVFi, CanFields, DSR) are 2–3 orders of magnitude worse in CD, as they cannot handle topology changes from organ emergence. In the per-organ evaluation (Table 2), GrowFields still leads (CD 0.87, EPE 1.62 vs DPF 1.05/2.06 and COAP 1.69/6.26), confirming that modelling cross-organ interactions remains beneficial even when organs are evaluated in isolation. Statistical significance is confirmed via Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (p < 0.001 vs COAP on both metrics; p < 0.001 vs DPF on CD, p = 0.004 on EPE).

## Relation to Prior Work

GrowFields extends the compositional neural field paradigm from articulated-object modelling (COAP, DANBO, Neural Body) to the fundamentally different setting of topology-changing plant growth, where new organs emerge and old ones decay rather than parts simply articulating under fixed topology. It adopts the SIREN network architecture and the auto-decoder latent code design from DeepSDF, but repurposes them for a shared continuous velocity field conditioned on per-organ identity codes rather than a static shape representation. The approach also builds on the organ-segmentation and tracking infrastructure from TrackPlant3D and PSegNet, positioning itself as a downstream dynamic model that consumes their outputs, while departing from global deformation field methods (NDF, NVFi, CanFields) and the reverse-time formulation of GrowFlow that do not explicitly model organ identity or asynchronous development.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first organ-aware, topology-changing 4D neural field framework for plant growth, enabling continuous measurement of organ-specific growth rates and leaf-tip trajectories from sparse LiDAR scans — capabilities directly relevant to agricultural phenotyping and stress detection. The annotated leaf-tip benchmark on TrackPlant3D offers a practical evaluation protocol that the plant phenotyping community can adopt. Key open questions remain: the model cannot predict organ emergence (new organs are only incorporated upon first observation), rapidly growing regions become spatially sparse without adaptive densification, and very short sequences leave the velocity field underconstrained. The authors suggest L-system priors, generative models, and cross-dataset harmonisation as promising directions to address these gaps.
