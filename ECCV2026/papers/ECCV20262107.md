## Problem

Cloud-based VLM inference services expose sensitive visual prompts to honest-but-curious servers, yet existing defenses fail for VLMs: cryptographic protocols (MPC, TEE) incur prohibitive overhead on high-dimensional visual signals, and differential-privacy perturbations irreversibly destroy cross-modal semantic alignment. The covariant obfuscation paradigm, proven effective for text-only LLMs, is structurally incompatible with the heterogeneous vision encoders (convolutions, ViT, spatial aggregators) that modern VLMs require.

## Input / Output

The client uploads an RGB image (dynamic resolution bounded within [1280, 16384]×28×28 pixels) and a text prompt to a cloud server hosting the VLM, and receives a text response. During online inference the client performs only lightweight preprocessing (image flattening, text tokenization), while all heavy obfuscation (matrix reparameterization, parameter tuning) is completed offline before deployment. The obfuscated visual tensor is transmitted with dimensional expansion—7.68× over lossless PNG or 32.15× over lossy JPG by default, reducible to 1.30× via 64-bin quantization at a 2.39% accuracy cost.

## Method

COVERT adapts covariant obfuscation to the VLM visual pipeline through exact architectural reparameterization: the convolutional patcher is vectorized into a matrix product and obfuscated with a stochastic transformation pair plus null-space noise that cancels exactly at the server; the ViT feature extractor inherits AloePri's primitives but resolves additive biases via dimensional augmentation (appending a constant unit channel absorbed into obfuscated weights) and handles RoPE-constrained QKV projections with structured masks; the semantic projector uses a Kronecker-product block-diagonal inverse to neutralize spatial aggregation and a shared alignment mask to bridge into the obfuscated LLM. To defend against hidden-state inversion attacks that exploit shallow ViT attention patterns, COVERT injects private-seed Gaussian noise into the first ViT block's attention matrices and fine-tunes them (freezing FFN and the second normalization layer) to maximize cosine similarity with the original block output, preserving utility while rendering attention scores non-invertible. The LLM backbone directly inherits AloePri's text obfuscation, yielding an end-to-end framework with zero commutation error and under 3% average accuracy degradation.

## Results

COVERT achieves an average accuracy degradation of only 1.53%–2.81% across five multimodal benchmarks (General VQA, Multidisciplinary Reasoning, Vision-Centric VQA, Scientific Diagram Understanding, Mathematical Reasoning) on Qwen2.5-VL (7B/32B/72B), MiMO-7B, and Fara-7B. Against hidden-state inversion attacks on the vulnerable ViT Layer 1, tailored tuning drops SSIM by 54.46% (0.3404 → 0.1550) and raises MSE by 39.75% (3275 → 4577), aligning Layer 1 security with naturally safe deeper layers. Online inference on vLLM incurs only +0.86% TTFT, +8.65% TPOT, and −5.93% throughput relative to plaintext, while communication expansion can be reduced to 1.30× via 64-bin quantization at a 2.39% accuracy cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary quantitative baseline is DP-Forward [9], a differential-privacy method that injects calibrated noise after visual patch and LLM embeddings. DP-Forward catastrophically fails on every dataset at every tested privacy budget: even at the most relaxed ε=128, General VQA accuracy collapses from 82.97% to 0.07% (−82.90 pp) and Scientific Diagram Understanding drops from 85.01% to 13.76% (−71.25 pp). COVERT, by contrast, retains 79.79% and 83.87% on those same datasets, a gap of roughly 80 and 70 percentage points respectively, confirming that stochastic perturbation is fundamentally incompatible with cross-modal semantic alignment. The paper also contrasts COVERT qualitatively with PrivMLLM [32], an MPC-based cryptographic baseline that requires a three-server setup, 2.69 GB per-request communication, and >5 minutes WAN latency—rendering it impractical for real-time VLMaaS. COVERT operates within a standard two-party client-server architecture with <6% throughput overhead, making it the only evaluated method that simultaneously satisfies robust privacy, preserved utility, and lightweight inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

COVERT directly extends AloePri's covariant obfuscation framework [18]—which jointly transforms discrete text tokens and LLM weights via mutually canceling matrix operations—to the continuous, spatially-structured domain of visual encoders. It departs from perturbation-based defenses (DP-Forward [9], Split-and-Denoise [21]) that inject stochastic noise and destroy cross-modal semantic alignment, and from cryptographic protocols (MPC-based PrivMLLM [32], TEE-based approaches [23,31]) whose computational cost is prohibitive for high-dimensional visual signals. The key conceptual leap is designing exact architectural reparameterizations for convolutional patchification and ViT attention (handling RoPE constraints, additive biases, and non-linear gating) that preserve zero-error commutation while neutralizing the spatial invertibility of shallow ViT representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COVERT fills a concrete gap: no prior framework simultaneously achieves robust visual privacy, <3% utility loss, and <6% inference overhead for VLMaaS, making it the first deployable solution in this category. However, the 7.68× communication expansion (or 32× for lossy formats) and the requirement for white-box model access during offline obfuscation limit immediate adoption in bandwidth-constrained or black-box API settings. Open questions include whether the shallow-layer tuning strategy generalizes to deeper or architecturally different vision encoders, how the defense holds against multi-round adaptive adversaries who accumulate partial information across sessions, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D visual inputs.
