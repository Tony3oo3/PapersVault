## Problem

Current multi-turn RL methods for VLM agents rely on either token-wise optimization (which over-discounts intermediate reasoning tokens) or turn-wise optimization (which provides uniform credit across all tokens within a turn), and no prior work had formally shown that these two levels are theoretically equivalent in gradient while differing only in advantage estimation. This paper addresses the resulting credit-assignment gap by deriving a hybrid advantage that simultaneously captures fine-grained within-turn differentiation and turn-level reward preservation, and by proving that a single unified critic can serve both levels.

## Input / Output

At each turn, the VLM receives a visual observation (RGB image from the environment), a text instruction or feedback string, and the accumulated history of prior observations, actions, and rewards. It autoregressively generates a full token sequence encoding intermediate reasoning followed by a natural-language action (discrete commands like 'up/down/left/right' in grid worlds, or continuous parameterized actions like 'pick(x,y,z)' in manipulation tasks). The environment executes the action, returns a scalar reward and a new observation, and the loop repeats over T turns until task completion or timeout.

## Method

HyGAE formulates multi-turn VLM interaction as a POMDP and proves (Theorem 1) that the turn-wise and token-wise policy surrogates share the same gradient, differing only in their advantage estimators. It then constructs a hybrid advantage as a linear combination α·A_turn + (1−α)·A_token (Eq. 13), where both advantages are computed via GAE with a single shared value model. The key enabling result (Theorem 2) shows that setting the turn-level discount to γ^I_t (where I_t is the turn length) makes the turn-wise value exactly equal to the token-wise value at the final token, so one critic suffices for both levels. The hybrid return (Eq. 17) broadcasts the turn-wise return to intermediate token positions by summing discounted rewards, providing a precise training target that stabilizes value learning. Bias-variance analysis (Theorems 3–4) confirms the turn-wise estimator has lower bias but higher variance while the token-wise estimator has the opposite profile, and the hybrid combination achieves a better trade-off than either alone.

## Results

HyGAE achieves an average success rate of 0.91 across five multi-turn decision-making benchmarks (Sokoban, FrozenLake, Navigation, Primitive Skill, VIRL), outperforming the best proprietary model (GPT-5, 0.63) by 28 percentage points and the best prior RL method (Token-PPO, 0.81) by 10 percentage points. On Primitive Skill, HyGAE reaches a near-perfect average of 1.00, and on VIRL it achieves 0.86 (ID: 0.72, OOD: 1.00). The method also demonstrates substantially lower variance across runs (0.8222 ± 0.0711) compared to standard PPO (0.5797 ± 0.2105) on Sokoban.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyGAE outperforms all 13 baselines on the average success rate metric. Against proprietary models, HyGAE (0.91) surpasses GPT-5 (0.63), Gemini 2.5 Pro (0.52), and Claude Sonnet 4.5 (0.51) by 10–39 percentage points, with the largest gap on VIRL (0.86 vs. 0.31–0.56). Against frozen open-source VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Gemma-3-4B, VLM-R1-3B, Cosmos-R1-7B, Qwen3-VL-4B), HyGAE's advantage is overwhelming (0.91 vs. 0.02–0.09), confirming that multi-turn RL training is essential for agentic capabilities. Among RL baselines, HyGAE beats the strongest competitor Token-PPO (0.81) by 10 points, VAGEN-Base (0.74) by 17 points, RL4VLM (0.55) by 36 points, Turn-PPO (0.47) by 44 points, and GRPO (0.38) by 53 points. The gap is most pronounced on VIRL (HyGAE 0.86 vs. Token-PPO 0.55, VAGEN-Base 0.45) and Primitive Skill (HyGAE 1.00 vs. Turn-PPO 0.25, GRPO 0.25), where turn-level credit assignment and fine-grained token-level guidance are both critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GAE and PPO as the foundational actor-critic machinery, extending them to the multi-turn VLM agent setting. It departs from prior hierarchical approaches (e.g., ARCHER, VAGEN) that combine token-level and turn-level optimization through complex multi-level models or heuristic discounting, by proving that the two levels are theoretically equivalent (identical policy gradient) and can be merged via a simple convex combination of advantages. The unified critic result—that a single value model suffices for both turn-wise and token-wise estimation under a specific discount-factor relationship—further simplifies the architecture compared to methods requiring separate value networks at each level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical equivalence between token-wise and turn-wise RL resolves a long-standing design tension in VLM agent training, giving practitioners a principled basis for combining fine-grained language-generation credit with turn-level environmental feedback without hierarchical overhead. The 91% average success rate (vs. 81% for the best single-level baseline) across five diverse environments demonstrates practical value, and the unified critic reduces memory and compute costs. Open questions include whether the hybrid advantage generalizes to larger models (7B+), more complex or open-ended environments, and whether the specific discount-factor coupling (γ_t = γ^{I_t}) remains optimal when turn lengths are highly variable or when intermediate rewards are dense rather than sparse.
