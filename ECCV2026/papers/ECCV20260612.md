## Problem

Event cameras offer microsecond temporal resolution ideal for high-speed imaging, but when both the scene and atmospheric turbulence are dynamic, they produce largely indistinguishable events, making it impossible to separate scene motion from turbulence with a single view. All existing turbulence mitigation methods—whether hardware-based (adaptive optics) or software-based (lucky imaging, deep learning)—rely on conventional frame sensors whose fixed exposure irrecoverably blends scene dynamics and turbulence fluctuations, capping output at standard frame rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260612/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N=9 asynchronous event streams from a kaleidoscope-based light field camera (Prophesee EVK4), where each 256×256 (simulated) or 176×176 (experimental) sub-aperture view is independently corrupted by a different turbulence realization. Events are binned into 5 temporal voxel grids per view, then stacked along the channel dimension to form a joint NB×H×W input tensor. The output is a single reconstructed clean video frame per temporal window, supporting reconstruction rates up to 12,000 fps—far exceeding any conventional sensor's frame rate.

## Method

The key insight is that scene dynamics produce events strongly correlated across sub-aperture views (same scene, different turbulence paths), while turbulence produces events weakly correlated across views, providing the cross-view structure needed to disentangle the two. The architecture is E2VID (a recurrent ConvLSTM encoder-decoder) with minimal modification: each view's voxel grid is stacked as additional input channels, and the recurrent state accumulates temporal evidence across windows to produce the reconstructed frame. Training is fully supervised on a simulated dataset (P2S turbulence applied to REDS 120 fps frames, then V2E event synthesis, repeated 9× with different random seeds for independent turbulence realizations), using LPIPS loss with geometric, noise, and pause augmentations plus spectral normalization on ConvLSTM layers. The network is trained exclusively on simulated data and generalizes to real tabletop experiments with candle- and heater-generated turbulence, demonstrating that the light field geometry—not architectural novelty—drives the performance gain.

## Results

On the simulated REDS dataset, the 3×3 light field configuration (256×256 per view) achieves PSNR 15.40, SSIM 0.505, LPIPS 0.4332, ITF 25.16, and E_warp 0.0022, outperforming a resolution-matched single-view baseline (768×768) across all metrics. On tabletop experiments, the light field improves PSNR from 12.37 to 12.99 and reduces warping error from 0.0085 to 0.0051 relative to single-view. The system reconstructs video at up to 12,000 fps and captures a Nerf dart traveling at 16,000 pixels per second through turbulence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary quantitative comparison is against a single-view baseline using the identical E2VID architecture trained on turbulence-degraded data from only the central sub-aperture. The light field wins on every metric: +0.56 dB PSNR, +0.044 SSIM, −0.085 LPIPS, +2.45 ITF, and −0.0028 E_warp on simulated data, and +0.62 dB PSNR, +0.016 SSIM, −0.012 LPIPS, +2.43 ITF, and −0.0034 E_warp on tabletop data. Notably, the light field outperforms even the single-view baseline at 768×768 (three times the spatial resolution), confirming that angular diversity, not pixel count, drives the improvement. Against MambaTM (the state-of-the-art frame-based turbulence mitigation method [56]), the comparison is visual only: the paper reports that MambaTM paired with a $100k Photron UX100 high-speed camera still exhibits residual blur and distortion, while the event light field at ~20× lower cost produces sharper reconstructions. Direct quantitative comparison with event-based turbulence methods EvTurb [29] and EGTM [27] is not possible as neither has released code, and both require conventional frames in addition to events.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends E2VID [39], a recurrent ConvLSTM encoder-decoder for event-to-video reconstruction, by stacking multi-view voxel grids as additional channels, and pairs it with the kaleidoscope-based event light field camera hardware from Event Fields [38]. The key conceptual departure from prior event-based turbulence work (EvTurb, EGTM) is that those methods still depend on a conventional frame sensor for final image formation, capping output at standard frame rates; here the light field geometry provides the cross-view correlation structure needed to disambiguate scene motion from turbulence without any frame reference. The paper also departs from prior light-field turbulence mitigation (which used conventional sensors) by operating entirely on asynchronous event streams, and from single-view event reconstruction by exploiting the fact that scene dynamics are correlated across sub-apertures while turbulence realizations are not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that event-only sensing can image dynamic, non-rigid scenes through strong turbulence at kHz frame rates, breaking the fundamental exposure-integration bottleneck of all prior turbulence mitigation. The practical impact is significant for applications like long-range surveillance and remote sensing where both scene motion and atmospheric distortion are present simultaneously. Key limitations include the ~1 MP spatial resolution inherited from event sensors, the assumption of near-optical-infinity scenes (negligible parallax), and the gap between tabletop candle/heater turbulence and real outdoor conditions. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to close-range or biomedical imaging, whether higher-resolution event sensors will close the resolution gap, and whether the 3.4 ms reconstruction latency can be reduced for truly real-time deployment at higher resolutions.
