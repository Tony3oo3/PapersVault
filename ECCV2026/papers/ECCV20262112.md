## Problem

Existing efficient VLM pre-training methods that adjust semantic data balance (e.g., MetaCLIP, DBP) pursue an 'aim for even' philosophy that flattens the topic distribution, which disproportionately removes rare long-tail concepts from the training corpus. As a result, long-tail concepts remain insufficiently represented and are not effectively captured during training, leading to poor zero-shot performance on underrepresented categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262112/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes large-scale image-text pair datasets (LAION-400M at ~298M pairs, DataComp-DFN at ~130M pairs) where images are resized to 112×112 during pre-training and 224×224 during a short fine-tuning step, and text is truncated to 32 tokens. The trained ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/16 image encoder and Transformer text encoder produce contrastive embeddings whose cosine similarity yields a score used for zero-shot classification (via 80 text prompts per class) and bidirectional image-text retrieval.

## Method

DynamiCS combines two mechanisms: (1) Cluster Scaling, which generates image embeddings with a frozen DINOv2-ViT-B/16, clusters them via k-means into 50k clusters (merging centroids with cosine similarity > 0.7), then resamples each cluster to size S_i = c_i^α / Σc_j^α · T with α = 0.2, downsampling the semantic fat head while upsampling the long tail while preserving relative cluster-size ordering; and (2) Dynamic Sampling, which at every epoch draws a fresh random subset of S_i samples from each cluster with per-sample probability P_i = S_i/c_i, ensuring cross-epoch data diversity and making upsampling of small clusters practical without inflating total training cost. The key design choice is the 'aim for utility' philosophy: rather than flattening the distribution (α=0) or ignoring it (α=1, random sampling), a moderate α preserves the natural ordering of concept frequencies while amplifying underrepresented clusters, and the dynamic per-epoch resampling prevents the model from overfitting to a fixed subset.

## Results

DynamiCS achieves 72.6% zero-shot top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K and 52.0% on the long-tail Let It Wag! benchmark when pre-trained on DataComp-DFN with 2.56B samples seen (299 GPU hours), outperforming full-scale OpenCLIP (67.1%, 39.1%) and MetaCLIP (70.8%, 46.5%) while using only ~3% of their training cost. On LAION-400M, DynamiCS-2.56B reaches 67.5% ImageNet-1K and 45.5% Let It Wag! with 299 GPU hours, surpassing cost-saving baselines RECLIP (62.9%), CLIPA (63.2%), and FLIP (60.9%) by 2.1–4.1% on ImageNet-1K and 5.7–6.1% on Let It Wag!. Even the smallest configuration (DynamiCS-0.64B on DataComp-DFN, 95 GPU hours) achieves 69.2% ImageNet-1K and 46.5% Let It Wag!, already exceeding full-scale OpenAI-WIT CLIP (68.3%, 37.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynamiCS consistently outperforms all cost-saving baselines: on LAION-400M, DynamiCS-1.28B beats CLIPA by 1.8% and RECLIP by 2.1% on ImageNet-1K, and by 5.7% and 6.1% on Let It Wag!, while using only 50% of the samples seen and ~60% of GPU hours. Against dual-purpose filtering baselines on DataComp-DFN, DynamiCS-0.64B surpasses DFN* by 0.5% (ImageNet-1K) and 4.1% (Let It Wag!), and DynamiCS-1.28B exceeds HQ-CLIP by 0.7% and 12.0% respectively, using only ~6% of HQ-CLIP's GPU hours. Compared to full-scale training, DynamiCS-2.56B on DataComp-DFN (299 GPU hours) outperforms OpenAI-WIT CLIP by 4.3% (ImageNet-1K) and 14.1% (Let It Wag!), OpenCLIP by 5.5% and 12.9%, and MetaCLIP by 1.8% and 5.5%, all at roughly 3% of the training cost. DynamiCS-0.64B matches WhatIf's 69.2% ImageNet-1K accuracy while requiring substantially fewer compute resources, and outperforms the Captioning baseline by 9.4%. The only baseline DynamiCS does not surpass is OpenVision (73.9% ImageNet-1K), which uses a different multi-resolution training schedule and 12.8B samples seen. On robustness benchmarks (Table 7), DynamiCS-2.56B on DataComp-DFN achieves 59.1% average, exceeding full-training OpenAI-WIT (55.9%), MetaCLIP (57.5%), and OpenCLIP (54.1%) by 3.2%, 1.6%, and 5.0% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'aim for even' philosophy in semantic data balancing (MetaCLIP's metadata capping, DBP's density homogenization) by proposing an 'aim for utility' approach that downsamples large clusters while upsampling small ones, preserving relative cluster-size ordering rather than flattening the distribution. It also repurposes the dynamic sampling idea from LLM training (ClusterClip) into the VLM pre-training setting, where a different subset is drawn each epoch to improve cross-epoch diversity and make upsampling practical without inflating cost. Conceptually, the cluster-scaling formula (Eq. 1) is analogous to the power-law sampling in word2vec, but applied to image-text pairs rather than tokens, and the method is positioned as a dual-purpose alternative to filtering approaches (DFN, DataComp, HQ-CLIP) that require a separate pre-trained VLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DynamiCS achieves competitive or superior zero-shot performance to full-scale CLIP training (12.8B samples at 224×224) using roughly 3% of the GPU hours, which could democratize VLM pre-training for smaller labs. The finding that moderate cluster scaling (α=0.2) outperforms full flattening (α=0) challenges the prevailing assumption that uniform semantic distributions are optimal, suggesting the field should reconsider how aggressively to rebalance long-tail data. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes when clustering cannot rely on a strong pre-trained vision encoder (DINOv2), whether it transfers to generative VLMs or MLLMs, and whether the dynamic sampling benefit persists at much larger scales or with different cluster counts.
