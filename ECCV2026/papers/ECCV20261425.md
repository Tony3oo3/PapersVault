## Problem

Vision foundation models for depth estimation and segmentation are trained on perspective images and produce erroneous outputs on fisheye imagery due to a covariate shift in latent embeddings caused by radial distortion. Existing remedies—explicit rectification to canonical projections, camera-specific inductive biases, or large-scale fisheye training—introduce resampling artifacts, limit architectural flexibility, or sacrifice generalizability. The paper asks whether a lightweight, architecture- and task-agnostic adapter can correct this shift directly in feature space without modifying the backbone or requiring test-time calibration.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a raw fisheye RGB image (no rectification or intrinsics required) and optionally text labels for open-vocabulary segmentation, producing a spherical range map R (rather than Cartesian depth z) or per-pixel class logits. A notable design choice is the output parameterization switch from z-depth to Euclidean range R, which better matches the geometry of high-FoV projections and counteracts radial attenuation. As a byproduct, the Extender activations can be decoded via a linear probe into four KB distortion coefficients, enabling camera calibration without separate optimization.

## Method

DEX inserts a pair of learnable parameters into each block of a frozen backbone: a set of M Extender vectors E ∈ R^{M×D} acting as discrete 'centers' in latent space, and a low-rank selection matrix W = W₁ᵀW₂ that computes per-embedding affinities A = softmax(XSᵀ/√D) where S = EW. Each layer's output is modulated by adding the convex combination AE to every token or feature vector, so the image's own features determine which Extenders (and in what proportion) correct its distortion. Training is self-supervised and requires only calibrated perspective images: each image is synthetically distorted via the KB model, the DEX-augmented fisheye output is inverse-warped back to the perspective frame, and a log-abs-difference loss aligns it with the frozen backbone's perspective output. For depth, the supervision signal is converted from z to R coordinates; for segmentation, the loss is computed on image embeddings in latent space rather than on final logits, preserving the text-image affinity structure. The same set of Extenders generalizes across indoor and outdoor scenes and across both CNN (VNL, SED) and Transformer (MiDaS, DepthAnything, UniDepthV2, LSeg) backbones with only ~3 MB of additional parameters.

## Results

On ScanNet++ (indoor fisheye), DEX with UniDepthV2 achieves RMSE 0.200 and δ₁ 0.872, outperforming UniK3D (RMSE 0.223, δ₁ 0.825) and improving over the base model by 39% in RMSE. On KITTI-360 (outdoor fisheye, higher FoV), DEX with UniDepthV2 reaches RMSE 1.663 and δ₁ 0.842, a 77% RMSE reduction over the base model and a 44% improvement over UniK3D (RMSE 2.969). Across all four backbone models, DEX yields an average 19% RMSE and 15% δ₁ improvement over LoRA and Calibration Tokens on ScanNet++, and 11% on both metrics on KITTI-360. For open-vocabulary segmentation on WoodScape, DEX with LSeg achieves mIoU 0.362 vs. 0.305 baseline and 0.321 with Calibration Tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEX consistently outperforms both LoRA and Calibration Tokens across all four backbone models on both datasets; the gap is largest for DepthAnything on KITTI-360 (RMSE 2.022 vs. 2.897 for LoRA and 2.224 for Calibration Tokens). Against the strongest external method UniK3D, DEX with UniDepthV2 wins on both ScanNet++ (RMSE 0.200 vs. 0.223) and KITTI-360 (RMSE 1.663 vs. 2.969, a 44% reduction), and also beats DepthAnyCamera on both (0.200 vs. 0.390 indoor; 1.663 vs. 3.641 outdoor). DEX is the only method evaluated that works with CNN backbones (VNL), where it reduces RMSE from 0.680 to 0.372 on ScanNet++ and 7.719 to 2.222 on KITTI-360—something Calibration Tokens cannot do due to its Transformer-only design. For open-vocabulary segmentation on WoodScape, DEX with LSeg achieves mIoU 0.362, beating Calibration Tokens (0.321) by 13% and the unadapted baseline (0.305) by 19%; with SED, DEX improves mIoU from 0.439 to 0.449. On the multitask PanopticDepth framework, DEX simultaneously improves depth (RMSE 7.273→3.583) and segmentation (mIoU 0.160→0.282), confirming task-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEX is most closely related to the concurrent Calibration Tokens work, which also extends perspective-trained FMDEs to fisheye cameras via trainable parameters in Transformer blocks; DEX differentiates by being architecture-agnostic (applicable to CNNs as well as Transformers), requiring no manual token insertion/removal, and using a task-agnostic alignment loss. The training pipeline is derived from AugUndo's synthetic-distortion-plus-inverse-alignment scheme, while the modulation mechanism draws conceptual parallels to LoRA and cross-attention but replaces learned weight matrices with a codebook of 'centers' selected by feature affinity. DEX deliberately departs from the dominant transformation-based approaches (ERP, tangent, cube-map projections) that require explicit geometric rectification and calibration at test time, instead operating entirely in latent feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEX offers a practically deployable solution for mixed-camera systems (autonomous driving, XR, robotics) where perspective-trained foundation models must handle fisheye inputs without retraining, rectification, or maintaining multiple camera-specific models. The minimal overhead (2.8 MB, +0.3 ms) and the fact that a single set of Extenders generalizes across indoor and outdoor scenes with different distortion parameters make it attractive for real-world deployment. A key limitation is that performance is upper-bounded by the frozen backbone's quality on perspective images, and the approach has only been validated on KB-model fisheye distortion; whether it extends to other distortion models (e.g., omnidirectional, freeform) or to tasks beyond depth and segmentation remains open. The byproduct of decoding KB distortion coefficients from DEX activations is an interesting but preliminary calibration signal with high error on higher-order terms.
