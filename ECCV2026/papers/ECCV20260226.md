## Problem

Existing concept customization methods bind rare tokens (e.g., <sks>) to target concepts, but these tokens lack semantic grounding in pretraining data, causing unstable generation when combined with other prompts, and they cannot encode the inherent knowledge about the concept. The paper introduces knowledge-aware concept customization—a new task requiring the model to identify and integrate multiple pieces of natural-language knowledge with a visual concept during generation—and identifies cross-modal knowledge transfer as the mechanism enabling this.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260226/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of reference images for a target concept, a set of N natural-language knowledge items (e.g., 'the bronze sculpture in Copenhagen harbour'), and a free-form generation prompt. Knowledge items are converted into question-answer pairs where the answer is replaced by the anchor representation; the generation prompt can combine any subset of the updated knowledge with additional scene descriptions. Output is a single high-fidelity image that faithfully reconstructs the concept's appearance while respecting the knowledge context, evaluated on both direct reconstruction and compositional generation with diverse prompts.

## Method

MoKus operates in two stages on a Qwen-Image backbone (LLM text encoder + MMDiT diffusion transformer). Stage 1 (Visual Concept Learning) fine-tunes LoRA parameters on the MMDiT self-attention layers to bind the target concept's visual appearance to a rare token, which serves as an anchor representation. Stage 2 (Textual Knowledge Updating) converts each knowledge item into a question, feeds it through the LLM encoder to extract hidden states and gradients, then computes a closed-form parameter shift via regularized least-squares (Eq. 8) and adds it to the Gate/Up Projection matrices of MLP layers 18–26. The key insight is cross-modal knowledge transfer: modifying the answer to a knowledge query in the text encoder propagates to the visual generation, so the updated knowledge naturally drives high-fidelity concept generation. Each knowledge update completes in seconds, and multiple knowledge items can be combined during generation without retraining.

## Results

On KnowCusBench, MoKus achieves the best CLIP-I-Seg scores for both reconstruction (0.764) and generation (0.718), the highest prompt fidelity (CLIP-T 0.305), and the best human preference (Pick Score 21.30), while completing training in ~6 minutes. On the WISE world-knowledge benchmark subset, textual knowledge updating improves WiScore from 0.81 to 1.33, Consistency from 0.76 to 1.26, and Realism from 0.70 to 1.42. MoKus slightly trails Naive-DB on raw CLIP-I for reconstruction (0.867 vs. 0.874) but surpasses it on the segmentation-aware CLIP-I-Seg metric, which the authors argue better reflects true concept fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Naive-DB (DreamBooth adapted per-knowledge), MoKus wins on CLIP-I-Seg for both reconstruction (0.764 vs. 0.758) and generation (0.718 vs. 0.717), on prompt fidelity CLIP-T (0.305 vs. 0.291), on Pick Score (21.30 vs. 20.80), and on training time (~6 min vs. ~27 min), while losing marginally on raw CLIP-I reconstruction (0.867 vs. 0.874). Against Enc-FT (fine-tuning the LLM encoder, the strategy used by concurrent works GapEval and UniSandbox), MoKus dominates across every metric: CLIP-I reconstruction 0.867 vs. 0.582, CLIP-I-Seg 0.764 vs. 0.553, CLIP-T 0.305 vs. 0.197, and Pick Score 21.30 vs. 18.34, indicating that direct encoder fine-tuning severely degrades the latent-space alignment needed for coherent generation. The paper also demonstrates that MoKus extends to virtual concept creation, concept erasure, and WISE world-knowledge improvement without additional architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: concept customization (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) and knowledge editing in LLMs (UltraEdit, ROME). It departs from the standard rare-token paradigm by repurposing the knowledge-editing mechanism (closed-form parameter shift on MLP layers) to bind natural-language knowledge to a visual anchor representation, rather than to a meaningless token. The architectural backbone (Qwen-Image's MMDiT with LoRA) follows the current DiT-based generation paradigm, while the textual knowledge updating stage directly inherits UltraEdit's training-free editing formulation. The key conceptual departure is the observation that edits to the LLM text encoder's internal knowledge propagate to the visual generation output—a cross-modal transfer that concurrent works (GapEval, UniSandbox) explored but did not confirm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces both a new task formulation and the first dedicated benchmark (KnowCusBench, 5,975 images across 35 concepts), giving the community a structured way to evaluate knowledge-grounded customization. The practical efficiency gain—updating a piece of knowledge in seconds versus minutes of retraining—makes multi-knowledge customization feasible at scale. However, the evaluation is limited to a single backbone (Qwen-Image), a modest concept count, and the cross-modal transfer observation, while compelling, has not been validated across architectures or at scale. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video generation, whether end-to-end joint optimization of visual and textual knowledge is possible, and whether the current CLIP-based metrics adequately capture knowledge fidelity.
