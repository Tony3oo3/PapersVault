## Problem

The VLA field is highly fragmented: systems differ in architectures, pretraining data, embodiment configurations, and benchmark-specific engineering, making it impossible to attribute performance gains to specific design choices. This paper asks whether a strong VLM backbone paired with minimal architectural and data complexity can already achieve competitive performance, and systematically re-evaluates commonly adopted complexities (action heads, robot pretraining, data engineering) under controlled conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260215/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes raw RGB images and a natural-language instruction as input, with no proprioceptive states, history frames, or benchmark-specific formatting. It outputs a chunk of continuous actions regressed from the hidden state of a designated action token via a lightweight MLP head. Actions are normalized to zero mean and unit variance using the training split only, and for multi-embodiment generalist training, action vectors from robots with fewer degrees of freedom are zero-padded to a uniform 32-dimensional space.

## Method

StarVLA-α couples the Qwen3-VL (4B) backbone with a single MLP action head that reads the hidden state of a dedicated action token and directly regresses a continuous action chunk, following the OpenVLA-OFT-style design. The data pipeline is deliberately minimal: raw RGB plus language instruction, no state conditioning, no history stacking, and no benchmark-specific preprocessing. The paper then isolates the effect of each design axis by swapping in alternative action heads (FAST discrete tokens, GR00T dual-system flow matching, π₀-style diffusion flow matching), adding robot-specific pretraining (OXE, InternData-A1, RoboTwin-Rand), or introducing data engineering (proprioception, history frames, delta/relative actions), all while holding the backbone, data scale, and training protocol fixed. The key finding is that continuous action prediction is essential, but among continuous variants the simple MLP head matches or exceeds diffusion and dual-system designs; out-of-domain pretraining can degrade cross-embodiment generalization; and data engineering yields only modest gains that vanish with sufficient task-specific data. For cross-embodiment generalist training, a simple zero-padding strategy to 32 dimensions outperforms specialized multi-action-head or RDT-style parameterization, and a batch size of 512 is identified as critical for stable generalist convergence.

## Results

StarVLA-α (specialist) achieves 98.8% average success on LIBERO, 70.2% on SimplerEnv Google VM, 76.0% on RoboTwin 2.0 (clean), and 53.8% on RoboCasa-GR1, outperforming all prior VLA baselines on most benchmarks. Under the generalist (all-in-one) training setting, a single jointly trained model reaches 57.3% on RoboCasa-GR1 and 88.7% on RoboTwin clean*, surpassing specialist-trained baselines. On the real-world RoboChallenge benchmark, StarVLA-α achieves 33.6% success rate and 54.5 progress score, exceeding π₀.₅ (12.7% SR, 27.6 score) by over 20 percentage points in success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StarVLA-α outperforms OpenVLA-OFT on every reported benchmark, with the largest gaps on SimplerEnv Google VM (+15.9%) and RoboTwin clean (+13.0%). Against π₀ and π₀+FAST, StarVLA-α leads by wide margins on all benchmarks (e.g., +13.6% on LIBERO Long vs. π₀+FAST, +17.2% on SimplerEnv G.VM vs. π₀). Compared to the strongest baseline π₀.₅, StarVLA-α wins on LIBERO avg (+6.4%), SimplerEnv G.VM (+1.8%), RoboTwin clean (+3.3%), and RoboCasa-GR1 (+16.8%), while π₀.₅ retains a narrow edge on SimplerEnv WidowX (96.9 vs. 98.8 is actually StarVLA-α winning; π₀.₅ leads only on RoboTwin clean* at 82.7 vs. 88.2 — actually StarVLA-α wins there too). Versus GR00T-N1.6, StarVLA-α surpasses it on LIBERO avg (+4.4%), SimplerEnv G.VM (+4.9%), and RoboCasa-GR1 (+6.2%), though GR00T-N1.6 edges ahead on SimplerEnv WidowX (97.0 vs. 98.8 — StarVLA-α actually wins). On the real-world RoboChallenge, StarVLA-α's 33.6% SR is 2.6× that of π₀.₅ (12.7%) and 9.3× that of π₀ (3.6%), confirming the advantage transfers to physical hardware.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the StarVLA codebase [16] and positions itself as a methodological counterpoint to the rapidly fragmenting VLA landscape, where systems like OpenVLA-OFT [31], π₀/π₀.₅ [6,27], GR00T [5], and FAST [49] each introduce distinct architectural and engineering choices that confound comparison. Rather than proposing a new architecture, StarVLA-α deliberately strips away complexity—using a pretrained Qwen3-VL backbone with a plain MLP action head and minimal data processing—to isolate which design axes actually drive performance. It departs from the prevailing trend of adding sophistication (diffusion heads, dual-system flow matching, robot-specific pretraining on OXE or InternData-A1) by demonstrating that these additions yield only context-dependent gains when the VLM backbone is already strong.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a controlled, reproducible baseline across four benchmarks and five embodiments, the paper gives the VLA community a common substrate for attributing performance gains to specific design choices rather than experimental variation. The finding that a single generalist model trained jointly across benchmarks (with simple action padding) matches or exceeds specialist models—particularly on challenging humanoid tasks—suggests the field may be over-investing in per-benchmark engineering. Open questions include whether the 4B model-size sufficiency finding holds at larger training scales, whether the simple padding strategy generalizes to truly novel embodiments with very different action spaces, and whether the real-world RoboChallenge results (limited to 11 tabletop tasks on one robot) reflect broader physical robustness.
