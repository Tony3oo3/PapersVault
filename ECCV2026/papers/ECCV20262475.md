## Problem

Existing unlearnable example (UE) methods inject pixel-space noise to prevent AI models from learning on personal images, but this approach suffers from two critical weaknesses: (1) fragility against relearning strategies such as adversarial training, grayscale conversion, and lossy compression, and (2) significant degradation in perceptual quality as robustness increases, making protected images visually unusable for real-world sharing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262475/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and its class label, encodes the image via a pre-trained Diffusion Autoencoder (DiffAE) into a 512-dimensional semantic code z_sem and a stochastic latent x_T, then outputs a defensive semantic noise z_δ (constrained by an L_p-norm radius ρ_u) and the reconstructed unlearnable image x_u produced by conditional DDIM decoding. The semantic noise radius is empirically calibrated per dataset (0.20 for CIFAR-10/100, 0.35 for ImageNet) to correspond to a maximum pixel shift of 4/255, which is 50% smaller than the 8/255 pixel-space budgets used by baselines.

## Method

DIFFUE replaces pixel-space perturbation with semantic-space perturbation by operating within the latent space of a diffusion autoencoder. The input image is encoded into a high-level semantic code z_sem (512-dim, capturing global style/content) and a stochastic code x_T (preserving local texture), and a defensive noise z_δ is optimized via Projected Gradient Descent to minimize a surrogate model's classification loss on the DDIM-reconstructed image, subject to an L_p-norm constraint. The overall procedure is a min-min bi-level optimization: the inner loop updates z_δ for 10 PGD steps while the outer loop updates the surrogate model parameters θ for Q steps, iterating until the model reaches >99% training accuracy (i.e., it has 'learned' nothing). Because the perturbation acts on high-level semantic features rather than individual pixels, the resulting modifications manifest as natural, coherent changes (subtle lighting or tone shifts) that are resistant to pixel-domain filtering, compression, and adversarial retraining, while preserving near-clean perceptual quality (FID ~5.4 vs. ~10–12 for baselines).

## Results

Under adversarial training with ρ_t = 4/255, DIFFUE maintains test accuracy of 17.11% on CIFAR-10, 4.92% on CIFAR-100, and 17.20% on ImageNet Subset, compared to 76.95%/64.59%/67.86% for EM, 64.64%/32.22%/45.49% for REM, and 46.85%/26.19%/38.57% for SEM. Against relearning strategies, DIFFUE achieves 14.19% under UEraser and 33.97% under LUE on CIFAR-10, versus 90.23%/90.33% for EM. On perceptual quality (CelebA-HQ), DIFFUE achieves FID of 5.355, SSIM of 0.814, and PSNR of 32.438, roughly halving the FID of all baselines while matching clean-image usability (98.5% acceptability in user study).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFFUE dominates all three pixel-space UE baselines (EM, REM, SEM) across every relearning scenario. Under the strongest adversarial training (ρ_t = 4/255), DIFFUE holds at 17.11% on CIFAR-10 while EM collapses to 76.95%, REM to 64.64%, and SEM to 46.85%—a gap of 50–60 percentage points. Against the GrayScale relearning strategy [21], DIFFUE remains at 13.99% versus 83.23% for EM and 63.87% for REM, confirming that semantic-space perturbations are immune to channel-based neutralization. Under UEraser [26] (adversarial augmentation), DIFFUE achieves 14.19% compared to 90.23% (EM), 87.39% (REM), and 81.56% (SEM). The diffusion-based LUE purification [12] is the hardest relearning attack, lifting DIFFUE to 33.97%, yet still far below the 89–90% range of all baselines. On perceptual quality, DIFFUE achieves FID 5.355 on CelebA-HQ (vs. 10.1–11.6 for baselines) and PSNR 32.44 (vs. 29.4–30.1), while maintaining 98.5% user acceptability—matching clean images—whereas REM and EM drop to 19.2% and 56.5% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unlearnable examples (UE) paradigm introduced by Huang et al. [9] and its robustness-focused successors (REM [4], SEM [20]) by relocating the defensive perturbation from pixel space to the semantic latent space of a diffusion autoencoder [25]. The bi-level optimization formulation is directly inherited from the original UE framework, but the inner minimization now operates on a 512-dimensional semantic code rather than raw pixel values, with the final image reconstructed via conditional DDIM decoding. Conceptually, the approach draws an analogy to the adversarial examples literature, where photorealistic, semantically-aligned perturbations are known to resist adversarial training better than high-frequency pixel noise, and applies this insight to the defensive (unlearnability) setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing UE methods that achieve strong robustness (e.g., REM, SEM) do so at the cost of visible pixel-space artifacts that make images unusable for real-world sharing, while DIFFUE achieves near-clean perceptual quality (FID ~5.4 vs. ~10–12 for baselines) with a 50% smaller effective noise budget. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond classification to detection or segmentation tasks, how sensitive the method is to the choice of diffusion autoencoder backbone, and whether adaptive relearning strategies specifically designed for semantic-space perturbations (analogous to LUE for pixel-space) will emerge. The controlled-modification capability (attribute/style steering) opens an interesting but underexplored direction for user-facing privacy tools.
