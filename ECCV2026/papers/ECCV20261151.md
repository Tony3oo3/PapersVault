## Problem

Existing dermatology vision-language models face three interrelated gaps: heterogeneous datasets with inconsistent diagnostic ontologies, the absence of clinically grounded reasoning supervision that captures hierarchical disease taxonomies and differential diagnosis, and poor scalability when transferring reasoning from small densely-annotated datasets to large sparsely-labeled collections. No prior Med-VLM integrates textbook-grounded diagnostic trajectories with reinforcement learning to address all three simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261151/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a dermoscopic or clinical skin image paired with an instruction prompt and produces a structured three-part response: a free-text reasoning trajectory (CoT) that walks through visual feature interpretation and differential diagnosis, a predicted disease label drawn from a hierarchical taxonomy, and a lesion category (benign, malignant, or precancerous in situ). All evaluation tasks are framed as multiple-choice VQA, and the model is instantiated on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct with LoRA (r=64, alpha=32) for both SFT and RL stages.

## Method

Skin-R1 introduces a three-stage training pipeline. First, it constructs SkinRationale, a dataset of 2,020 diagnostic trajectories synthesized from Fitzpatrick's Dermatology by combining 220 textbook exemplars with a differential-diagnosis graph (211 nodes, 245 edges) and a disease taxonomy (458 nodes, 473 edges), applying hierarchical label completion and DDx reasoning enrichment to each trajectory. Second, supervised fine-tuning on these trajectories establishes a clinically grounded reasoning foundation, reducing format failures from ~30% to ~2% compared to the base model. Third, GRPO-based reinforcement learning generalizes this reasoning to six large sparsely-annotated dermatology datasets using a composite reward that combines format compliance, a granularity-wise reward proportional to the depth of the predicted label within the disease taxonomy (encouraging hierarchical reasoning), and a malignancy discrimination reward. Ablations confirm that SFT before RL is essential—RL-only training plateaus well below the full pipeline—and that the granularity-aware reward yields stronger out-of-distribution generalization than a standard binary accuracy reward.

## Results

On in-distribution (ID) disease diagnosis across six benchmarks (BCN20k, HAM10k, PAD, Derm12345, Derm7pt, DermNet), Skin-R1 achieves an average accuracy of 0.6385, surpassing the next-best model (InternVL3-8B at 0.4430) by +0.1955. On out-of-distribution (OOD) diagnosis (ISBI16, ISIC18, ISIC19, ISIC20, Monk22), Skin-R1 reaches 0.7171 average accuracy, edging out MedGemma-4B (0.6887) by +0.0284. For lesion condition classification (benign/malignant/precancerous), Skin-R1 attains the best average accuracy (0.6928) and Macro-F1 (0.4287), outperforming the next-best models by +0.0297 and +0.0633 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Skin-R1 outperforms all general-purpose VLMs by a wide margin on ID diagnosis: the strongest general model, InternVL3-8B (0.4430), trails by 0.1955, while LLaVA-v1.6-13B (0.3960) and Qwen2.5-VL-32B (0.4307) lag by 0.24 and 0.21 respectively. Among medical VLMs, MedGemma-4B is the closest OOD competitor (0.6887 vs. 0.7171, a gap of only 0.0284), but Skin-R1 leads by 0.28 on ID (0.6385 vs. 0.3548); LLaVA-Med-7B and HuatuoGPT-Vision-7B are outperformed by 0.40 and 0.21 on ID respectively. The dermatology-specific baseline SkinVL-PubMM fails to produce meaningful diagnostic responses on most evaluation tasks (reported as '–'), making direct comparison infeasible. On lesion condition classification, Skin-R1 achieves the best Macro-F1 (0.4287), surpassing Qwen2.5-VL-32B (0.3654) by 0.0633 and LLaVA-Med-7B (0.2714) by 0.1573, while also leading in accuracy (0.6928 vs. 0.6631 for LLaVA-Med-7B). Notably, several baselines exhibit severe class biases—MedGemma-4B predicts 'precancerous in situ' for 94% of cases, and LLaVA-Med-7B predicts 'benign' for 66%—further widening the performance gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

Skin-R1 sits at the intersection of two recent trends in medical VLMs: (1) RL-based reasoning induction via GRPO (as in Med-R1, MedVLM-R1, and DeepSeekMath), and (2) textbook-grounded knowledge extraction for medical image-text models (following Kim et al.'s pipeline). The key departure is from RL-only paradigms that skip SFT: the paper argues that Med-R1's observation that SFT can hurt performance stems from ungrounded supervision, and that textbook-derived diagnostic trajectories provide the missing clinical grounding. It also extends SFT-based dermatology models (SkinVL-PubMM, LLaVA-Med) by adding a GRPO stage with a hierarchical reward that encodes disease taxonomy structure, a design not present in prior medical RL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a concrete recipe for bridging the gap between small, densely annotated clinical knowledge sources and large, sparsely labeled dermatology datasets—a practical problem for deploying medical VLMs. However, several limitations temper its impact: evaluation is answer-level (multiple-choice accuracy) rather than assessing reasoning faithfulness, the taxonomy and DDx graph are static and also generate evaluation distractors (creating partial circularity), and the method is validated only on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Open questions include whether the SFT+GRPO recipe transfers to other backbones or medical domains, how to incorporate process-level rewards for intermediate reasoning steps, and how to address observed performance degradation across Fitzpatrick skin types.
