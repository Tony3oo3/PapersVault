## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM systems are purely visual and degrade under motion blur, low texture, exposure changes, and low frame rates—conditions common in real-world deployment. The few prior visual-inertial 3DGS approaches suffer from naive pairwise IMU fusion, fixed bias assumptions, reliance on depth sensors, or decoupled alternating optimization, resulting in reduced accuracy and blurry renderings. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a tightly-coupled visual-inertial 3DGS SLAM system with robust initialization and consistent loop-closure handling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260845/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a sequence of RGB frames (grayscale on EuRoC, color on other datasets) at the camera's native frame rate alongside high-frequency IMU readings (linear acceleration and angular velocity). Keyframes are selected online based on optical-flow magnitude to the last keyframe or a maximum time interval t_kf, and each new keyframe is initialized via IMU pre-integration from the previous keyframe. The output is a metric-scale camera trajectory (poses, velocities, per-keyframe IMU biases) and an incrementally built 3D Gaussian Splatting map that supports photorealistic novel-view rendering via alpha-blending.

## Method

VIGS-SLAM jointly optimizes camera poses, per-keyframe disparities, velocities, and time-varying IMU biases by fusing a learned dense visual reprojection residual (GRU-refined correspondences from DROID-SLAM) with IMU pre-integration residuals (rotation, position, velocity, and bias-smoothness terms) in a single normal-equation solve per Levenberg-Marquardt iteration, implemented via custom CUDA kernels. A three-stage IMU initialization (pure vision → inertial-only gravity/scale recovery → joint visual-inertial refinement) ensures stable coupling before full tracking begins. Loop closure uses Pose Graph Bundle Adjustment in Sim(3) with dense two-view relative-pose constraints, followed by an efficient batch Gaussian update that transforms each keyframe-anchored Gaussian's mean and covariance into the corrected world frame without re-optimizing the full map. The Gaussian map is refined per keyframe with 10 iterations minimizing a weighted combination of color L1 loss, depth L1 loss, and isotropic regularization, using direct RGB colors instead of spherical harmonics.

## Results

On the EuRoC dataset, VIGS-SLAM achieves the best average ATE RMSE of 2.79 cm, outperforming the next-best RGB+IMU method ORB-SLAM3 (4.30 cm) by 35% and the best RGB-only method HI-SLAM2 (2.94 cm) by 5%. On the challenging FAST-LIVO2 outdoor dataset, VIGS-SLAM achieves 6.08 cm average ATE RMSE while all classical VIO baselines (ORB-SLAM3, OPEN-VINS, VINS-Mono) fail entirely, and the closest RGB-only competitor HI-SLAM2 reaches 10.22 cm. For rendering quality, VIGS-SLAM leads on all three datasets in PSNR (22.21/20.87/23.15 dB on RPNG/UTMM/FAST-LIVO2), SSIM, and LPIPS, surpassing HI-SLAM2 by 1.1–1.7 dB in PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIGS-SLAM outperforms all 15 baselines across every dataset. Against the strongest RGB-only Gaussian Splatting SLAM method HI-SLAM2, VIGS-SLAM reduces average ATE RMSE from 2.94 cm to 2.79 cm on EuRoC, from 8.10 cm to 3.24 cm on UTMM, and from 10.22 cm to 6.08 cm on FAST-LIVO2. Against the best classical VIO system ORB-SLAM3, VIGS-SLAM achieves 35% lower error on EuRoC (2.79 vs. 4.30 cm) and 61% lower on RPNG (1.68 vs. 4.33 cm), and is the only method that succeeds on all FAST-LIVO2 sequences where ORB-SLAM3 fails on every one. Against the closest visual-inertial Gaussian Splatting competitor VINGS-Mono, VIGS-SLAM reduces error by 89% on EuRoC (2.79 vs. 24.47 cm) and 94% on FAST-LIVO2 (6.08 vs. 104.80 cm). For rendering, VIGS-SLAM surpasses HI-SLAM2 by 1.1–1.7 dB PSNR and 0.03–0.05 SSIM across RPNG, UTMM, and FAST-LIVO2, while VINGS-Mono and Splat-SLAM produce substantially blurrier results (PSNR 10–17 dB vs. 22–23 dB). On the self-captured dataset with motion blur, exposure changes, and dynamic objects, VIGS-SLAM maintains tracking where HI-SLAM2 drifts significantly, as shown by the cumulative success curve and AUC metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIGS-SLAM sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: learning-based dense visual SLAM (DROID-SLAM's GRU-based correspondence refinement and per-pixel disparity optimization) and 3D Gaussian Splatting mapping (Kerbl et al.'s explicit Gaussian primitives with alpha-blending rendering). Its key architectural departure is from prior visual-inertial 3DGS-SLAM systems (DBA-Fusion, VINGS-Mono, MM3DGS-SLAM), which either cascade visual and inertial optimization in separate stages or use naive pairwise IMU constraints; VIGS-SLAM instead fuses visual and inertial Hessian contributions within a single normal-equation solve via custom CUDA kernels, enabling true tightly coupled joint optimization over poses, velocities, and biases. The loop-closure strategy (PGBA from HI-SLAM2) and mapping components (direct RGB colors, isotropic regularization from MonoGS) are adopted from prior 3DGS-SLAM work, while the three-stage IMU initialization and per-keyframe bias modeling are novel contributions specific to this system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper demonstrates that tight visual-inertial coupling within a unified 3DGS-SLAM framework yields substantially more robust tracking than either purely visual 3DGS-SLAM or cascaded VIO approaches, particularly under motion blur, low texture, exposure variation, and low frame rates—conditions where purely visual methods and even classical VIO systems (ORB-SLAM3, VINS-Mono, OPEN-VINS) fail entirely. The self-captured 18-sequence dataset covering real-world degradations provides a useful stress-test benchmark for future systems. A notable open question raised by the authors is that the Gaussian map currently does not feed back into tracking (rendering-based pose refinement yields only marginal gains), suggesting that tighter integration between the dense map and the tracking loop remains an unsolved challenge for next-generation Gaussian-SLAM systems.
