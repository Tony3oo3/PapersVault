## Problem

Cross-identity character animation requires transferring motion from a driving video to a reference character that often differs in scale, global position, and skeletal proportions (pose-reference misalignment). Existing diffusion-based pipelines implicitly assume geometric congruence between the two, and remedies like pose retargeting or late-stage feature fusion either lose information or entangle structural calibration with appearance rendering, producing artifacts and unstable motion transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261972/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image, a driving video with its DW-Pose 2D keypoint sequence, and SMPL articulation parameters (θ) estimated via ScoreHMR—explicitly discarding subject-specific shape parameters (β) to ensure identity-agnostic motion. The reference image is VAE-encoded and concatenated along the temporal axis with noisy video latents using a large positional offset to separate static identity from dynamic flow. Output is a 41-frame video at 832×480 resolution, generated via Flow Matching ODE integration on a 1.3B DiT backbone.

## Method

RASA disentangles spatial mapping from motion control through a dual-injection strategy within a Wan2.1 1.3B DiT backbone. The Spatial Prior Calibrator (SPC) performs frame-wise cross-attention with 2D RoPE, using the temporally evolving driving pose latent as query and the static reference pose latent as shared key/value, producing an aligned structural prior that is element-wise added to the noisy latent at every denoising step to continuously rectify 2D geometric mismatches. The Inherent Motional Guider (IMG) encodes shape-agnostic SMPL articulation parameters into a 263-dimensional motion vector, projects it through a pretrained motion encoder, and injects the resulting latent into intermediate DiT blocks (2–15) via 14 learnable projection layers, providing anatomically grounded 3D semantic guidance that complements the 2D spatial calibration. During training, stochastic spatial perturbations (scaling, translation, proportional skeletal dropout) are applied to driving poses to simulate realistic cross-identity discrepancies, and the model is trained with a standard Flow Matching velocity-prediction loss.

## Results

On the TikTok dataset, RASA achieves the best image-level metrics across all baselines: PSNR 22.03, SSIM 0.830, LPIPS 0.189, and FID 18.93, outperforming the next-best Human-DiT (PSNR 20.50) and MTVCrafter (FID 19.46). On FID-VID, RASA scores 3.32, nearly halving the best competitor MTVCrafter (6.98). On the cross-identity CIM-Bench, RASA dominates all four baselines on every metric, achieving FVD 667.96 (vs. 1312.60 for Animate-X), FID 60.96 (vs. 105.14), and Sim-Arc 0.72 (vs. 0.51), while also being the fastest (61 s) and most memory-efficient (11,951 MiB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On TikTok, RASA outperforms all 13 baselines on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID, and FID-VID, with the closest competitor being MTVCrafter on FID (18.93 vs. 19.46) and FID-VID (3.32 vs. 6.98). The sole metric where RASA trails is FVD: MTVCrafter (140.60) and Champ (160.82) both beat RASA (190.82), though the authors note MTVCrafter uses a 5B-parameter CogVideoX backbone and Champ requires five distinct motion representations. On the cross-identity CIM-Bench, RASA wins every metric against all four baselines (Animate-X, MTVCrafter, Wan-Animate, One-to-All-1.3B), with the largest gaps in FVD (667.96 vs. 1312.60 for Animate-X) and identity preservation (Sim-Arc 0.72 vs. 0.51, Face-FID 90.63 vs. 146.55). RASA also achieves the lowest inference latency (61 s) and VRAM usage (11,951 MiB) among all CIM-Bench competitors, compared to 109 s / 14,263 MiB for Animate-X and 327 s / 42,291 MiB for Wan-Animate.

## Relation to Prior Work

RASA builds on the Wan2.1 (1.3B) DiT backbone and the flow-matching video generation paradigm, extending the pose-driven character animation line (Animate Anyone, MagicAnimate, Champ, Animate-X) with a key architectural departure: rather than treating pose as a single localized condition or relying on heuristic preprocessing (retargeting, augmentation) and late-stage feature fusion to handle structural misalignment, it injects two disentangled priors at different depths of the transformer. The Spatial Prior Calibrator performs continuous 2D geometric calibration at the generative onset via frame-wise cross-attention, while the Inherent Motional Guider provides shape-agnostic SMPL articulation guidance in intermediate layers—complementing rather than replacing the base pose signal. This hierarchical, dual-injection strategy contrasts with prior methods that conflate spatial alignment and motion execution in a single conditioning pathway.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The disentangled spatial-motional prior framework offers a clean, modular principle that other DiT-based animation pipelines could adopt, and CIM-Bench fills a genuine evaluation gap by providing realistic cross-identity structural discrepancies rather than synthetic linear perturbations on same-subject data. However, the 1:1 body-part correspondence assumption limits applicability to non-humanoid topologies, and the reliance on 2D pose for hands and face (without SMPL-X/MANO) constrains expressiveness. Open questions include whether the dual-injection depth strategy generalizes to other DiT backbones or scales, and whether explicit topology-aware correspondence could extend the framework beyond humanoid characters.
