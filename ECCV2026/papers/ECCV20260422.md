## Problem

Existing visual token pruning methods for VLMs fail under tight budgets and fine-grained queries due to two bottlenecks: (1) dense cross-modal scoring is corrupted by a 'noise floor' from functional words and punctuation that produce uniformly dispersed similarity responses, drowning out localized semantic peaks; and (2) standard Top-K selection causes feature fragmentation, over-concentrating on a few discriminative regions while leaving other integral semantic parts unrepresented and wasting the token budget on redundant local maxima.

## Input / Output

EADP is a plug-and-play, training-free module that takes an RGB image (encoded into N visual tokens by the VLM's vision encoder, projected into CLIP embedding space) and a text prompt (encoded by CLIP's text encoder into M+1 tokens including EOS) and outputs a compact subset of K visual tokens (K << N) for the downstream LLM. It operates across diverse token counts—576 (LLaVA-1.5), 2880 (LLaVA-NeXT at 672×672), 1296 (Qwen2.5-VL at 1008×1008), 1024 (Qwen3-VL), and 64×169 per frame (LLaVA-Video)—with pruning ratios from 60% to 94%. The intermediate instruction relevance score map is a per-token scalar in [0,1] after min-max normalization, which drives both spatial smoothing and the submodular selection.

## Method

EADP reformulates pruning as a structured compression problem in two stages. In Stage 1 (Entropy-Aware Dense Scoring), it computes dense cosine similarities between all non-EOS CLIP text tokens and visual tokens, then uses the information entropy of each text token's temperature-softmaxed spatial distribution to identify dispersed noise tokens; high-entropy tokens are filtered via a q-quantile threshold, and retained low-entropy tokens are weighted by a softmax of negative entropy before aggregation. This denoised dense score is fused with the global EOS score via a balancing coefficient μ. In Stage 2 (Submodular Selection), the fused score map undergoes 3×3 Gaussian spatial smoothing for structural continuity followed by exponential score polarization (raising to power β) to re-amplify salient peaks. Token selection is then cast as a facility location submodular maximization—maximizing the weighted sum of each token's maximum cosine similarity to the selected subset—solved greedily with a theoretical 1−1/e approximation guarantee, explicitly ensuring holistic semantic coverage while penalizing local redundancy.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B with 128 retained tokens (77.8% pruned), EADP achieves 63.5 average accuracy across 9 benchmarks, surpassing the strongest baselines CDPruner (63.2) and DivPrune/VisionZip (62.5). Under extreme compression to 32 tokens (94.4% pruned), EADP maintains 60.9 average, outperforming CDPruner (60.4) and DivPrune (58.4) by 0.5 and 2.5 points respectively. On LLaVA-NeXT-7B at 640 tokens, EADP matches the unpruned upper bound at 66.3 average. Efficiency-wise, retaining 128 tokens yields a 1.75× prefill speedup and 66% FLOPs reduction over the full 576-token model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EADP consistently outperforms all 10 baselines on average across every token budget and architecture tested. On LLaVA-1.5-7B at 128 tokens, EADP (63.5 avg) beats the closest competitor CDPruner (63.2) by 0.3 and DivPrune (62.5) by 1.0; the gap widens at 32 tokens where EADP (60.9) leads CDPruner (60.4) by 0.5 and DivPrune (58.4) by 2.5. On Qwen2.5-VL at 128 tokens, EADP (68.4) surpasses DivPrune (66.4) by 2.0 and CDPruner (58.2) by a substantial 10.2, indicating that entropy-based noise filtering is especially critical for architectures with tightly coupled visual tokens. On LLaVA-NeXT-7B at 640 tokens, EADP (66.3) matches the unpruned upper bound exactly, while CDPruner (66.0) and DivPrune (65.3) fall slightly short. EADP's main trade-off is a modest prefill overhead (~10–20 ms more than DivPrune/HiPrune) due to the dense cross-modal similarity computation, but this is negligible relative to the LLM attention cost and is more than offset by the accuracy gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

EADP builds directly on the CDPruner paradigm of using CLIP cross-modal similarity for instruction-relevance scoring, but departs from its reliance on a single global EOS token by introducing dense per-token text-visual similarity with entropy-based denoising. For the selection stage, it replaces the universal Top-K heuristic (and CDPruner's DPP-based diversity objective) with a facility location submodular maximization formulation inherited from the classic Nemhauser-Wolsey-Fisher framework, coupled with Gaussian spatial smoothing and score polarization priors. Conceptually, the paper reframes visual token pruning from a scoring-and-thresholding problem into a structured compression problem that must simultaneously handle noise-robust saliency and holistic coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that naive dense cross-modal guidance is actively harmful—because functional words and punctuation create a uniform noise floor that submerges semantic peaks—is a useful and non-obvious insight for anyone designing instruction-conditioned pruning. The submodular formulation provides a principled 1-1/e approximation guarantee and empirically resolves the feature-fragmentation failure mode of Top-K selection, which is particularly relevant as VLMs move toward higher resolutions (2880+ tokens) where redundancy is most severe. Open questions include whether the entropy-based text filtering generalizes to non-CLIP text encoders, how the greedy submodular optimizer scales to very large N (e.g., video with thousands of frames), and whether the modest but consistent gains over CDPruner/DivPrune (0.3–1.5 points) justify the additional prefill overhead in latency-critical deployments.
