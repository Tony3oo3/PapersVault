## Problem

High-resolution image perception in MLLMs suffers from fine-detail loss when images are uniformly resized to fixed input resolutions. Existing training-free methods (e.g., ZoomEye, RAP) rely on geometric partitioning—uniform grids or fixed-scale patches—that fragments objects at boundaries and introduces task-irrelevant background. The paper's controlled decoupling experiments reveal that the primary gain of cropping comes from background removal rather than target refinement, exposing a fundamental limitation of geometry-agnostic partitioning strategies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution RGB image (4K–8K resolution, up to 5000×5000 in MME-RealWorld) and a natural-language question as input, and produces a free-form text answer. It operates entirely as a test-time wrapper around a frozen MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL2.5-8B, or LLaVA-OneVision-7B) with no retraining or architectural modification; the original image is never globally resized but is instead decomposed into progressively finer entity-level crops that are individually fed to the base model at its native input resolution.

## Method

HEE formulates HR perception as an entity-centric hierarchical tree search. At each level, a frozen object detector (DINO-X) extracts object-level bounding boxes from the current region; these entities are clustered via K-Means in semantic embedding space, their boxes merged into coherent sub-regions, and residual uncovered areas are appended to guarantee full spatial coverage. A dual scoring mechanism—combining SigLIP vision-text similarity S(q,r) with the VLM's own answer confidence M(r,q) via a weighted sum (λ=0.5)—determines whether the current region already contains sufficient evidence or which sub-region to expand next. Recursive exploration proceeds only on the top-scored region until a confidence threshold (τ=0.5) is met, the entity count drops below a minimum, or a depth limit is reached. If all final-level entities fall below threshold, a confidence-guided backtracking step returns to the previous level and re-expands the next-best candidate, preventing irrecoverable single-path errors.

## Results

On Visual Probe, HEE applied to Qwen2.5-VL-7B improves accuracy from 39.1% to 67.4% (Easy), 26.0% to 47.0% (Medium), and 23.9% to 50.0% (Hard). On HR-Bench, HEE lifts overall accuracy from 66.5% to 73.9% (4K) and 62.1% to 71.1% (8K) for Qwen2.5-VL-7B, with similar gains for InternVL2.5-8B (+5.8%/+11.7%) and LLaVA-OneVision-7B (+6.9%/+10.6%). On MME-RealWorld, HEE brings consistent improvements across five real-world tasks, with the largest gains on Remote Sensing (+7.17%) and Monitoring (+7.50%). In terms of efficiency, HEE processes 8.3 samples/min (24 min total on HR-Bench-4K), compared to 4.3/min for ZoomEye (46 min) and 1.6/min for RAP (126 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEE consistently outperforms both training-free baselines, RAP and ZoomEye, across all benchmarks and model families. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, HEE beats RAP by +14.9/+15.3/+16.0 on Visual Probe Easy/Medium/Hard and by +2.8/+1.7 on HR-Bench 4K/8K overall; it beats ZoomEye by +12.1/+11.5/+8.5 on Visual Probe and by +2.5/+3.2 on HR-Bench 4K/8K. On LLaVA-OneVision-7B, the margins are even larger: HEE surpasses RAP by +4.3/+4.8/+7.5 on Visual Probe and +6.6/+7.6 on HR-Bench 4K/8K, and beats ZoomEye by +5.6/+7.5 on HR-Bench 4K/8K. In terms of efficiency, HEE is 5.25× faster than RAP (24 min vs. 126 min) and 1.9× faster than ZoomEye (24 min vs. 46 min) while achieving the highest accuracy. Against closed-source references, HEE+Qwen2.5-VL-7B surpasses GPT-4o on Visual Probe (67.4% vs. 47.5% Easy) and HR-Bench 4K (73.9% vs. 59.0%), though GPT-4o retains an edge on HR-Bench 8K FSP (62.0% vs. 88.5% — actually HEE wins here too). The gains are most pronounced on the harder Visual Probe subsets and 8K HR-Bench, where geometric partitioning methods suffer most from entity fragmentation and background interference.

## Relation to Prior Work

HEE carves new ground at the intersection of two existing lines of work: training-free hierarchical image exploration (ZoomEye's recursive grid search, RAP's fixed-scale patch retrieval) and object-detection-based scene understanding. Rather than building directly on either, it repurposes a frozen open-vocabulary detector (DINO-X) as the structural backbone of the search tree, replacing the geometric partitioning that both ZoomEye and RAP rely on. The paper's decoupling experiments (background masking and zoom-in/zoom-out) provide empirical motivation that the dominant failure mode of geometric partitioning is background interference rather than insufficient target resolution, a finding that reframes the design space for HR perception methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HEE offers a practical, training-free plug-in that delivers large accuracy gains (+28% on Visual Probe Easy, +9–12% on HR-Bench 8K) and a 5× speedup over RAP across three MLLM families without any retraining, making it immediately deployable. However, the ablation showing that 24.8% of errors persist even when the detector correctly localizes the target reveals that the reasoning bottleneck, not the perception bottleneck, is the next frontier. Open questions include whether entity-driven exploration extends to video or multi-image settings, whether the K-Means clustering step is optimal versus learned grouping, and how the method behaves when the detector misses small or occluded objects entirely.
