## Problem

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction foundation models (e.g., DA3, VGGT) suffer from quadratic memory growth with sequence length, making them infeasible beyond a few dozen frames on consumer GPUs, and their lack of explicit global geometric constraints causes severe Sim(3) trajectory drift over kilometer-scale monocular sequences. Existing chunk-based stitching strategies provide only loose inter-chunk pose alignment without deep cross-frame geometric coupling, failing to maintain global consistency in large-scale unbounded scenes.

## Input / Output

The system consumes uncalibrated monocular RGB video (no camera intrinsics or depth supervision required), with each frame resized to 504 px width and the other dimension rounded to a multiple of 14 to match ViT patch partitioning. It produces a globally consistent SE(3) camera trajectory, a dense 3D point cloud, per-pixel metric depth maps, and a sparse ray-field representation. Processing is organized in chunks of 60 frames with 10-frame overlap, and all outputs are aligned to LiDAR ground truth via a global Sim(3) transformation before metric evaluation.

## Method

GRF-Recon builds on DA3 as a perception backbone, injecting high-frequency monocular geometric priors via a re-normalized LoRA adapter (r=8) on the DINOv2 encoder's QKV projections, trained with a dual-branch distillation loss (depth pseudo-labels + 3D point regression) that adds <3% parameters while preserving cross-view matching. The core novelty is a hybrid-weight sparse ray-field: DA3's ray head outputs per-pixel 7-channel rays (direction, origin, confidence) fused with depth predictions, sparsely sampled via a composite confidence-and-gradient score, and matched across frames using a weighted combination of ray-distance, gradient-similarity, and photometric-error terms with bidirectional symmetry verification. A global factor graph jointly optimizes all camera poses under Huber-robust ray-field constraints and multi-frame loop-closure pose priors (detected via DINOv2 cosine similarity), solved by Levenberg-Marquardt, explicitly coupling cross-view 3D geometry to suppress accumulated drift. Coarse-to-fine chunk alignment via SVD-based Sim(3) registration provides metric-scale initialization before the sparse backend refinement.

## Results

On KITTI (excluding high-speed Seq.01), GRF-Recon achieves 7.18 m ATE RMSE, outperforming the best calibrated SLAM baseline ORB-SLAM3 (7.93 m) and the strongest feed-forward streaming method DA3-Streaming (12.72 m). On Waymo, it reaches 1.39 m ATE RMSE, beating DA3-Streaming (1.76 m) and VGGT-Long (2.03 m). For monocular depth, the LoRA-enhanced DA3 reduces KITTI Rel from 5.14 to 4.50 and raises δ₁ from 92.4 to 97.1, approaching the MoGe teacher (Rel 4.39, δ₁ 97.2). On Waymo 3D reconstruction, GRF-Recon achieves the best Accuracy (e.g., 0.910 m on Seq.163), Completeness (1.980 m), and Chamfer Distance (1.560 m) across most sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRF-Recon outperforms all 11 baselines on the primary KITTI tracking benchmark. Against calibrated SLAM systems, it beats ORB-SLAM3 (7.18 vs 7.93 m avg ATE RMSE) and dramatically surpasses DROID-SLAM (7.18 vs 74.88 m), DPVO (7.18 vs 54.79 m), and DPV-SLAM++ (7.18 vs 24.25 m). Among uncalibrated feed-forward methods, it improves over DA3-Streaming (7.18 vs 12.72 m), VGGT-Long (7.18 vs 19.78 m), and Pi-Long (7.18 vs 19.01 m), while MASt3R-SLAM, VGGT, and raw DA3 all fail entirely (tracking lost or OOM). On Waymo, GRF-Recon (1.39 m) beats DA3-Streaming (1.76 m), VGGT-Long (2.03 m), DROID-SLAM (5.43 m), MASt3R-SLAM (5.01 m), and CUT3R (9.99 m). For depth estimation, the LoRA-enhanced model approaches but does not fully match the MoGe teacher (avg Rel 6.95 vs 5.62; avg δ₁ 91.4 vs 93.8), though it substantially improves over the unmodified DA3 backbone (avg Rel 7.73 → 6.95).

## Relation to Prior Work

GRF-Recon builds directly on DA3 as its perception backbone, extending the Fin3R monocular knowledge-distillation paradigm through a re-normalized LoRA mechanism that preserves the frozen decoder's input distribution. It departs from prior chunk-stitching approaches (VGGT-Long, DA3-Streaming, Pi-Long) that rely on pairwise Sim(3) alignment between overlapping chunks by introducing an explicit hybrid-weight sparse ray-field as a geometric constraint in a global factor graph, thereby coupling cross-view 3D geometry rather than merely aligning poses. The system also draws on classical SLAM concepts—loop closure detection via DINOv2 descriptors and Levenberg-Marquardt factor-graph optimization—but applies them to a feed-forward ray representation rather than sparse feature correspondences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that kilometer-scale uncalibrated monocular reconstruction is practically achievable on a single 24 GB GPU, a regime where prior feed-forward models (VGGT, DA3) simply run out of memory. The ray-field constraint mechanism offers a reusable geometric coupling strategy that could inform other long-sequence 3D tasks beyond driving. However, the system remains limited to quasi-static scenes (dynamic objects are acknowledged as a challenge), requires a 24 GB GPU, and the trajectory accuracy, while competitive with calibrated SLAM, still lags behind ORB-SLAM3 on several sequences. Open questions include whether the ray-field formulation generalizes to indoor or highly textured environments and whether the chunk-based pipeline can be further parallelized for real-time operation.
