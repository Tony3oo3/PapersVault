## Problem

Existing online 3D scene graph generation methods treat the 2D-to-3D fusion as a fully deterministic pipeline, discarding three distinct sources of uncertainty: partial/truncated observations, soft 2D model distributions collapsed to hard argmax labels, and noisy depth-based 3D back-projection. This rigidity causes systematic failures in node association (binary accept/reject gates), evidence redistribution (no soft merging across near-tie candidates), and edge completion (sparsely co-observed relationships are lost).

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of RGB-D frames with known camera poses, processed by a 2D SGG model (EGTR + RT-DETRv2-M) that outputs full softmax class and relation distributions rather than hard labels. The output is a persistent 3D scene graph in which every node carries a Dirichlet distribution over its class label space and every directed edge carries a Dirichlet distribution over its relationship label space, with 3D geometry represented as either a Gaussian (mean + covariance) or a discrete voxel set. The framework is plug-and-play and training-free: it wraps any 2D SGG model without architectural modification and requires no additional neural-network training.

## Method

PUF reformulates scene graph node association as a probabilistic likelihood that factors into a spatial term (Bhattacharyya coefficient for Gaussians, voxel containment score for voxels) and a semantic term (exponential of negative Jensen-Shannon divergence between Dirichlet posteriors), then marginalizes over all existing nodes with a birth term following the Joint Probabilistic Data Association Filter. Upon association, both node class and edge relation evidence are accumulated via Dirichlet updates weighted by the per-candidate association probability, redistributing soft 2D evidence across all plausible candidates rather than committing to a single argmax. An optional class-conditional relationship prior, factorized into class-conditional, spatial-decay, and existence-gate components, is added as a Bayesian prior to complete edges for sparsely or never co-observed object pairs. The framework is representation-agnostic, validated with both 3D Gaussian and 3D voxel backends, and adds only ~2 ms of fusion latency over the 2D SGG inference.

## Results

On the 3DSSG test set, PUF-Gaussian achieves 46.0% relationship recall, 69.7% object recall, and 51.4% predicate recall, improving relationship recall by 18.1 points over the strongest online baseline FROSS (27.9%) while maintaining 15 ms per-frame latency. On ReplicaSSG (no training split, no relationship prior), PUF-Gaussian reaches 25.3% relationship recall and 35.6% predicate recall versus FROSS's 22.5% and 28.0%, respectively. The voxel variant also outperforms FROSS on both benchmarks (40.3% vs 23.7% relationship recall on 3DSSG), confirming representation-agnostic gains. PUF-Gaussian is an order of magnitude faster than all RGB-D+SLAM baselines (15 ms vs 245–454 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PUF-Gaussian outperforms the strongest online baseline FROSS [11] by 18.1 points in relationship recall (46.0% vs 27.9%), 7.2 points in object recall (69.7% vs 62.5%), and 18.2 points in predicate recall (51.4% vs 33.2%) on 3DSSG, while adding only 2 ms of latency (15 ms vs 13 ms). Against offline point-cloud methods, PUF-Gaussian surpasses OCRL [10] (25.2% rel.) and VL-SAT [33] (23.5% rel.) by over 20 points in relationship recall, though OCRL retains a slight edge in mRecall Obj. (49.6% vs 65.8% — actually PUF wins here too). Among RGB-D+SLAM online methods, PUF-Gaussian exceeds SCRSSG [40] (25.7% rel., 350 ms) by 20.3 points in relationship recall and is 23× faster. On ReplicaSSG, where no relationship prior is available, PUF-Gaussian still improves over FROSS by 2.8 points in relationship recall (25.3% vs 22.5%) and 7.6 points in predicate recall (35.6% vs 28.0%), confirming that the core uncertainty-aware fusion mechanism — not the prior — drives the gains. The voxel variant (PUF-Voxel) also beats FROSS on both benchmarks (40.3% vs 23.7% rel. on 3DSSG; 22.4% vs 18.6% rel. on ReplicaSSG), demonstrating that the framework generalizes across 3D representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the deterministic 2D-to-3D merging pipelines used by all existing online 3D scene graph generation methods (FROSS, SGFN, MonoSSG, SCRSSG) by reformulating node association as a probabilistic data association problem, directly inspired by the Joint Probabilistic Data Association Filter from the multi-target tracking literature. It also extends the 2D uncertainty-aware scene graph work (Yang et al.'s probabilistic ambiguity modeling, Li et al.'s Bayesian classifier reparameterization) by propagating the full softmax distributions through the 3D fusion stage rather than discarding them at the 2D-to-3D boundary. The Dirichlet evidence accumulation for both node semantics and edge labels, combined with a class-conditional relationship prior, represents a novel combination not present in either the tracking or scene graph literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free design makes PUF a practical drop-in wrapper for any 2D SGG model, and the representation-agnostic validation (Gaussian and voxel backends) suggests broad applicability across 3D scene understanding pipelines. The uncertainty calibration byproduct (per-node/edge entropy) opens a path for downstream consumers to filter unreliable predictions or trigger re-observation. Open questions include whether the per-observation independent normalization suffices in very dense scenes where joint hypothesis enumeration would be more principled, whether the class-conditional prior generalizes to open-vocabulary or outdoor settings, and whether the framework's gains persist when the 2D backbone is weaker or the depth sensor is noisier.
