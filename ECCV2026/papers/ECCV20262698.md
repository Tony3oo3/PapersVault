## Problem

Existing closed-loop benchmarks for embodied world models rely on learned Inverse Dynamics Models (IDMs) to extract robot actions from generated videos, but IDMs overfit to their training distributions and fail on novel trajectories, creating an unavoidable attribution ambiguity between world-model errors and extractor errors. No prior framework provides a closed-loop evaluation that directly tests physical feasibility without this confounding dependency.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a generated video sequence (frame-by-frame RGB), a language instruction, an initial observation, and the gripper's CAD model, producing per-frame 6D end-effector poses (position + orientation in SE(3)) that are injected into ManiSkill3 for closed-loop rollout. Beyond binary task success, it outputs two continuous kinematic diagnostics: a SPARC score (frequency-domain trajectory smoothness, where values near 0 indicate smooth motion) and a Maruyama Manipulability Index (integral of Jacobian determinant over the trajectory, where sharp drops signal proximity to singularities). The CAD model is critical: it provides rigid-body geometric constraints that suppress frame-level jitter while remaining sensitive to genuine physical hallucinations like gripper vanishing.

## Method

KineBench replaces implicit IDM-based action inference with a modular geometric grounding cascade: a fine-tuned YOLOv11 segments the end-effector mask, a two-stage fine-tuned MoGeV2 produces absolute metric depth maps, and FoundationPose performs zero-shot 6D pose tracking via a Render-and-Compare paradigm that iteratively refines SE(3) poses by minimizing photometric and geometric residuals against the CAD template. The recovered pose sequence is executed in ManiSkill3, which renders the next observation and initiates the subsequent generation-and-rollout cycle, enabling true closed-loop evaluation without modifying the evaluated world model. Two classical 3D kinematic metrics are incorporated: SPARC measures trajectory smoothness in the frequency domain (insensitive to high-frequency noise that invalidates jerk-based metrics), and the Maruyama Manipulability Index characterizes whether generated poses respect the robot's kinematic boundaries via inverse kinematics. The benchmark comprises 20 manipulation tasks in ManiSkill3 organized into four progressive suites—basic execution, task transfer, visual OOD generalization, and complexity-conditioned scaling—enabling systematic probing of capability boundaries and scaling behaviors.

## Results

On the basic execution suite (Suite 0), the best-performing model Wan 2.2 5B (full fine-tune, 7.5k steps) achieves a 56.32% closed-loop success rate, followed by CogVideoX 2B at 46.17% and Wan 2.1 1.3B at 43.96%, while LoRA variants and closed-source APIs (Wan 2.6, Hailuo-V2) drop to 6.6–19.0%. On the task-transfer suite (Suite 1), CogVideoX 2B surprisingly leads at 57.78%, far ahead of Wan 2.2 5B (11.90%), suggesting a trade-off between task-specific execution and transferability. In the complexity-scaling suite (Suite 3), increasing optimization steps from 1.5k to 15k on Wan 2.1 1.3B raises success from 44.83% to 73.33%, while scaling training trajectories from 10 to 50 yields diminishing or negative returns (53.67% → 51.17%), revealing task-complexity-conditioned nonlinear scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary methodological comparison is against Dino3DFlowIDM [10], the IDM-based action-extraction approach used in prior closed-loop benchmarks (WoW-World-Eval). On seven unseen test tasks, KineBench's explicit kinematic grounding pipeline achieves substantially lower translational pose error (≈1.5–3 cm with MoGeV2 depth) compared to the IDM's errors on the order of ten centimeters. Under controlled visual perturbations (gripper deformation, occlusion, reduced contrast, motion blur, image noise), KineBench maintains a much more concentrated error distribution with far fewer long-tail failures, whereas the IDM exhibits large variance and catastrophic breakdowns. Rotation error is slightly higher in some KineBench configurations (≈10° residual), but the overall robustness advantage is clear. This comparison validates the paper's central claim: replacing learned IDMs with modular geometric grounding (YOLOv11 + MoGeV2 + FoundationPose) reduces the attribution ambiguity between world-model errors and action-extraction errors, making closed-loop evaluation more reliable and diagnosable.

## Relation to Prior Work

KineBench builds on the closed-loop evaluation paradigm established by WoW and WoW-World-Eval, which ground generated videos into physics simulators for execution-based assessment, but departs fundamentally from their reliance on learned Inverse Dynamics Models for action extraction. Instead of end-to-end neural IDMs, it replaces action inference with a modular geometric pipeline (YOLOv11 segmentation → MoGeV2 depth → FoundationPose 6D pose tracking), drawing on classical robotics metrics (SPARC, Maruyama Manipulability Index) that had not previously been applied to generative video evaluation. This positions the work at the intersection of video generation benchmarking and robot kinematics, extending the evaluation axis from 2D pixel fidelity to 3D kinematic feasibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the IDM bottleneck, KineBench resolves a persistent attribution ambiguity in closed-loop EWM evaluation—whether execution failures stem from the world model or the action extractor—making diagnostic signals more interpretable. The complexity-conditioned scaling finding (more data hurts simple tasks but helps complex ones) provides early empirical guidance for data-scaling strategies, though the evidence is limited to a single architecture and a small task set. Key limitations include reliance on motion-planned (not teleoperated) trajectories, residual depth-induced translational error (~1.5–3 cm) and orientation error (~10°), and the need to validate scaling trends across broader architectures and data regimes.
