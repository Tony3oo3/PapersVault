## Problem

Existing deep pansharpening methods depend on large-scale pretraining and generalize poorly to unseen real-world pairs, while prior zero-shot approaches require per-image retraining and are restricted to a fixed output resolution. No prior framework simultaneously achieves zero-shot learning, weight reuse across image pairs, and arbitrary-scale output generation for pansharpening.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261748/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution PAN image (H×W), a low-resolution LRMS image (h×w×c), and a continuous target scale factor N as input. The PAN is channel-duplicated to match the LRMS band count and the LRMS is upsampled to PAN resolution before encoding; the INR point-query module then synthesizes the output at any requested resolution (H×N × W×N × c), supporting non-integer scales such as ×1.6 or ×3.4. No ground-truth HRMS reference is required at any stage.

## Method

G-ZAP uses an INRConv backbone (EDSR encoder → LIIF-style area-weighted MLP point query → lightweight conv decoder) where the MLP receives local features, relative coordinates, and a cell-size encoding of the target scale, enabling continuous-resolution synthesis from a single discrete feature map. Training follows a three-level semi-supervised strategy: Level 0 enforces unsupervised spectral consistency at full resolution via MTF degradation and downsampling; Level 1 adds constructed supervision at one degraded resolution using the original LRMS as a proxy ground truth; Level 2 introduces a second degradation level and supervises the model at both N=1 and N=4 to learn scale-aware representations. All three levels share the same INRConv parameters, and the combined loss (weighted L1 terms) is optimized for 500 epochs on a single test image pair. Because the feature-based INR formulation decouples spatial sampling from the learned function, the trained weights transfer directly to unseen image pairs and arbitrary output scales without retraining.

## Results

On the in-domain GF2 dataset (4-band), G-ZAP achieves the best HQNR of 0.9706 and the best spatial distortion D_s of 0.0115, outperforming the strongest pretrained baseline FusionMamba (HQNR 0.9536, D_s 0.0295). On WV3 (8-band), G-ZAP reaches HQNR 0.9585 and D_s 0.0233, again leading all baselines. On the cross-sensor WV2 dataset, the weight-reuse variant G-ZAP* attains the best HQNR of 0.9367 and best D_λ of 0.0195, surpassing UCL (HQNR 0.9201) and WFANet (HQNR 0.9128) without any retraining. The weight-reuse variant also reduces inference time from ~79 s (per-image training) to 0.14–4.23 s per image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-ZAP (per-image zero-shot) achieves the best HQNR on both in-domain datasets: 0.9706 on GF2 (vs. FusionMamba 0.9536, UCL 0.9427) and 0.9585 on WV3 (vs. FusionMamba 0.9550, UCL 0.9482), while also attaining the lowest D_s (0.0115 on GF2, 0.0233 on WV3). On the cross-sensor WV2 dataset, the weight-reuse variant G-ZAP* leads with HQNR 0.9367 and D_λ 0.0195, outperforming UCL (0.9201 / 0.0228) and WFANet (0.9128 / 0.0526) without any retraining. Among zero-shot baselines, G-ZAP surpasses ZS-Pan by ~1.4 HQNR points on GF2 and ~1.7 on WV3, and outperforms PsDip by ~2.7 and ~3.7 points respectively. The pretrained DL methods (FusionMamba, WFANet) remain competitive on D_λ (spectral fidelity) but are consistently outperformed on D_s (spatial quality) and overall HQNR by G-ZAP. Notably, G-ZAP* (weight reuse) retains within 0.001–0.014 HQNR of the full per-image G-ZAP while reducing inference time from ~79 s to 0.14–4.23 s, demonstrating that the INR-based backbone transfers effectively across scenes and sensors.

## Relation to Prior Work

G-ZAP builds directly on the feature-based INR fusion paradigm introduced by INF3 [39], which demonstrated that appending lightweight convolutional layers after an INR network alleviates overfitting in image fusion, and on LIIF [5]'s area-weighted coordinate query mechanism for continuous signal interpolation. It departs from prior zero-shot pansharpening methods (PsDip, ZS-Pan, UCL) that require per-image optimization and are locked to a fixed output scale, by introducing a three-level semi-supervised training strategy that enables both arbitrary-scale inference and cross-image weight reuse. The framework also diverges from existing feature-based INR fusion approaches (INF3, FeINFN) that rely on a pretraining-then-testing paradigm, instead performing all learning at test time from a single PAN/LRMS pair.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: G-ZAP* achieves sub-second inference (0.14 s on WV2) with near-parity quality to per-image zero-shot training, making arbitrary-scale pansharpening viable for real-time or large-scale deployment where per-pair retraining is prohibitive. The cross-sensor weight-reuse result (WV3-trained weights applied to WV2) suggests that INR-based feature interpolation captures transferable structural priors in remote sensing imagery that purely convolutional backbones lack. Open questions include whether the three-level training strategy generalizes to hyperspectral bands (beyond 4/8), whether performance holds at larger spatial extents than 512×512 crops, and whether the MTF-based constructed supervision remains valid for sensors with more complex point-spread functions.
