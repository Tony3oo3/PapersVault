## Problem

Self-supervised axial super-resolution for volume microscopy faces a trilemma: regression-based networks over-smooth due to regression-to-the-mean, pure diffusion models produce structural hallucinations, and all diffusion-based approaches incur prohibitive inference latency for gigapixel biological volumes. The authors identify the shared root cause as the absence of a domain-adapted structural skeleton that captures the highly nonlinear deformations of biological ultrastructures, which would simultaneously anchor topology and enable effective truncated diffusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261187/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an anisotropic 3D volume with d sparse axial slices (physically sectioned, not merely downsampled) and synthesizes intermediate slices to produce an isotropic volume of (d−1)×r+1 slices, where r≥2 is the upsampling factor (r=8 in experiments). Operationally, it processes pairs of adjacent authentic slices I₀, I₁ with a relative position τ∈(0,1) to generate each missing intermediate slice, operating on 256×256 pixel patches. No paired isotropic ground truth is required; the high-resolution lateral (XY) planes serve as the internal supervisory signal.

## Method

SkelEM decouples ASR at the training-signal level into two stages with disjoint objectives and no shared gradients. Stage 1 trains a frozen RIFE/IFNet flow network on a synthetic pseudo-HR volume to produce a deterministic low-frequency structural skeleton via bidirectional optical-flow warping, deliberately discarding the detail refiner to prevent synthetic hallucination leakage. Stage 2 trains a DDPM-based diffusion refiner first on the synthetic volume, then fine-tunes it on sparse real slices via a bidirectional cycle-consistency loss that enforces direction-agnostic reconstruction of observed central slices. A lightweight ResNet-9 residual estimator, trained with FFT-domain L1 loss on cycle-consistent differences, extracts authentic high-frequency details from the skeleton. At inference, the skeleton plus predicted residual initializes the reverse diffusion at an intermediate timestep (t_S), requiring only 3 denoising steps (≤5) to produce the final slice, achieving over an order-of-magnitude speedup over existing diffusion-based ASR methods.

## Results

On FIB-25, SkelEM achieves 3D-PSNR 26.24 dB with the best LPIPS in axial views (XZ: 0.3658, YZ: 0.3687) among all self-supervised methods, while D2R-AENet leads in fidelity (3D-PSNR 27.62, SSIM-XY 0.7373) but trails in perception (LPIPS-XZ 0.4136). On EPFL, SkelEM scores 3D-PSNR 25.56 dB and again achieves the best axial LPIPS (XZ: 0.3213, YZ: 0.3298). For downstream membrane segmentation on EPFL, SkelEM attains the best F1 (0.8643), IoU (0.7611), ARE (0.2129), and VoI (0.4811), surpassing even supervised baselines. Inference requires only 151 ms per 256×256 patch (S=3 steps), over an order of magnitude faster than competing diffusion methods (vEMDiffuse ~2071 ms, DiffuseIR ~8503 ms, TPDM ~402788 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkelEM consistently achieves the best LPIPS (perceptual quality) in the axial XZ and YZ views across FIB-25, EPFL, and the zero-shot BRAVE-ASR benchmark, outperforming the strongest fidelity-oriented baseline D2R-AENet by 0.048–0.158 in axial LPIPS (e.g., 0.3658 vs. 0.4136 on FIB-25 XZ). However, D2R-AENet retains a clear fidelity advantage in 3D-PSNR (27.62 vs. 26.24 on FIB-25; 26.35 vs. 25.56 on EPFL) and SSIM, confirming the fidelity-perception trade-off that SkelEM navigates rather than eliminates. Among diffusion-based self-supervised baselines, SkelEM outperforms Lee et al. [22] (LPIPS-XZ 0.3658 vs. 0.3862 on FIB-25), DiffuseIR [32] (0.3658 vs. 0.4394), and Sparse3Diff [20] (0.3658 vs. 0.5664) in axial perception while matching or exceeding their fidelity. On the zero-shot instrument transfer (EPFL→BRAVE-ASR), SkelEM maintains the best axial LPIPS (XZ 0.3203, YZ 0.3093) while D2R-AENet leads in 3D-PSNR (24.15 vs. 23.48), and VFI-based InterpolAI [18] degrades severely (LPIPS-XZ 0.6475). For downstream membrane segmentation on EPFL, SkelEM achieves the best F1 (0.8643), IoU (0.7611), ARE (0.2129), and VoI (0.4811) among all methods, surpassing even the supervised vEMDiffuse-i [27] (F1 0.8610, IoU 0.7560), demonstrating that its perceptual quality translates to structural utility beyond pixel-level metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

SkelEM repurposes the RIFE/IFNet flow-estimation architecture as a frozen topological skeleton, deliberately discarding its detail refiner to enforce a strict topology-texture separation that standard VFI pipelines do not. It builds on the pseudo-HR volume distillation strategy from D2R-AENet (the authors' prior work) as the synthetic training manifold, but departs fundamentally by introducing a diffusion refiner trained on disjoint objectives with no shared gradients, plus a cycle-consistent bidirectional self-alignment on sparse real slices. The prior-truncated sampling strategy connects to the broader truncated-diffusion literature (SD-Edit, DPM-Solver) but is specifically enabled by the physically grounded residual prior extracted via cycle-consistency, which the authors argue is the missing ingredient that makes truncation structurally reliable in the microscopy domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves the long-standing fidelity-perception trilemma in self-supervised axial super-resolution by showing that a domain-adapted deterministic skeleton simultaneously prevents regression over-smoothing and diffusion hallucination, while enabling ≤5-step inference that is over an order of magnitude faster than existing diffusion-based ASR methods. The introduction of BRAVE-ASR as a standardized zero-shot instrument-transfer benchmark (FIB-SEM → Plasma-FIB) addresses a practical gap in the field where cross-hardware generalization has been largely untested. Open questions include whether the heuristic forward-simulated initialization (Eq. 6) can be made more principled, whether the approach scales to gigapixel volumes without patch-based processing, and whether the training-signal decoupling principle generalizes to other anisotropic imaging domains beyond microscopy.
