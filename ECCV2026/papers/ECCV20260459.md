## Problem

Multi-view visual reasoning from sparse, discrete viewpoints remains largely unexplored: existing benchmarks focus on single images, temporally dense video, or non-sparse human-centric viewpoints with predominantly low-hop perceptual queries. Collecting large-scale real-world multi-view data with accurate per-view geometric and semantic annotations is prohibitively difficult, leaving the field without a controlled setting to systematically study how reasoning depth, viewpoint sparsity, and visibility constraints affect model performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260459/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a group of 2+ sparse-view RGB images of the same static scene (from heterogeneous viewpoint types: drone, bird-eye, egocentric, or surveillance cameras) alongside a text question, where no single view is sufficient to resolve the query. Outputs vary by task type: a multiple-choice letter, an integer count, or axis-aligned bounding boxes for visual grounding detection. The proposed Grounded CoT variant additionally produces intermediate reasoning steps that explicitly reference object bounding boxes in specific views before arriving at the final answer, making the reasoning trace visually verifiable.

## Method

The paper builds a Blender-based 3D data engine that synthesizes 2,000 diverse scenes from 1,000+ photorealistic assets with real-world scale calibration, renders multi-view images under four canonical viewpoint configurations, and deterministically derives 3M grounded QA pairs from engine-level geometric metadata (3D poses, occlusion ratios, cross-view spatial relations). On top of this, VIEW2SPACE provides a 3,591-question evaluation split and a 300K-sample disjoint training split spanning three task types (MCQ, counting, detection) with graded reasoning difficulty defined by relational-hop count and key-object visibility difficulty defined by ray-cast occlusion. The key training contribution is Grounded Chain-of-Thought with Visual Evidence, where the model is supervised to output intermediate bounding boxes in the relevant view at each reasoning step, anchoring linguistic reasoning to observable visual cues rather than freeform text. Trained via full-parameter SFT on Qwen3-VL-4B, this grounded CoT strategy yields up to +52% mIoU over instruction tuning on detection and generalizes to real-world MINDCUBE data without fine-tuning, outperforming official MINDCUBE checkpoints by 9.24% absolute accuracy.

## Results

On VIEW2SPACE-v1, most SOTA models perform only marginally above random guessing: the best open-source MLLM (Qwen3-VL-4B) achieves 35.19% MCQ accuracy and 16.53% detection mIoU, while the best closed-source model (GPT-5) reaches 59.86% MCQ but only 8.18% mIoU. The proposed Grounded CoT with Visual Evidence (Qwen3-VL-4B) substantially outperforms all baselines, achieving 64.93% MCQ accuracy, 54.99% counting accuracy, and 69.34% detection mIoU — a +52.81% absolute gain in mIoU over the next-best model. On the real-world MINDCUBE-Tiny benchmark, the VIEW2SPACE-trained 4B model (70.00% overall) surpasses the official MINDCUBE-Plain checkpoint (60.76%) by 9.24% absolute accuracy without any fine-tuning on MINDCUBE data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Grounded CoT model (Qwen3-VL-4B) outperforms GPT-5 [47] by 5.07% on MCQ (64.93% vs. 59.86%) and by a massive 61.16% on detection mIoU (69.34% vs. 8.18%), demonstrating that targeted multi-view training surpasses even the strongest closed-source generalist. Among open-source MLLMs, Qwen3-VL-4B [1] is the strongest baseline (35.19% MCQ, 16.53% mIoU), but the Grounded CoT model improves mIoU by over 4x. Specialized spatial models (Molmo2 [12], RoboBrain2.0 [49], Spatial-MLLM [52], OpenView [5]) show no consistent advantage over general MLLMs and largely fail on grounding tasks (mIoU ≤ 1.51%), indicating that single-view or robotic spatial training does not transfer to sparse multi-view reasoning. Notably, GPT-5.2 [43] underperforms both GPT-5 [47] and GPT-5-mini on MCQ (37.07% vs. 59.86% and 49.00%), suggesting its grounding/coding focus does not address multi-view spatial alignment. On the cross-dataset MINDCUBE-Tiny evaluation, the VIEW2SPACE-trained 4B model (70.00%) beats the official MINDCUBE-Plain checkpoint [55] (60.76%) by 9.24% absolute accuracy without any MINDCUBE fine-tuning, confirming strong synthetic-to-real generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of multi-view spatial reasoning and vision-language model evaluation. Prior spatial reasoning benchmarks (MINDCUBE, InternSpatial, SpatialVLM) either restrict to human-centric viewpoints, temporally dense video, or low-hop perceptual queries, while spatially-focused models (Molmo, RoboBrain, Spatial-MLLM) rely on single-view inputs or explicit 3D supervision. The paper departs from the standard CoT paradigm by proposing Grounded Chain-of-Thought with Visual Evidence, which explicitly anchors intermediate reasoning steps to bounding boxes in the corresponding view, and from the simulation-to-real literature by using Blender-based physically grounded rendering with precise per-view geometric metadata to generate 3M training pairs. No single prior work combines sparse heterogeneous viewpoints, graded multi-hop reasoning difficulty, and progressively stricter visual-evidence constraints (MCQ → counting → detection) in one benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that multi-view spatial reasoning is largely unsolved: even GPT-5 achieves only ~60% on the most permissive MCQ task (vs. 25% chance), and near-zero on detection, exposing a fundamental gap in current VLMs that single-view or video benchmarks mask. The scaling analysis is particularly important: while visibility difficulty is a learnable perceptual regime that improves with data and model scale, reasoning complexity shows poor scaling efficiency beyond ~50K samples, suggesting that linear CoT is structurally inadequate for multi-hop cross-view inference and that tree-based or graph-search mechanisms may be needed. The synthetic-to-real result (outperforming MINDCUBE-trained checkpoints by 9% without any MINDCUBE data) validates the simulation-to-real pipeline and suggests the benchmark's supervision transfers to real-world settings. Open questions include whether structured search (e.g., Tree-of-Thoughts) can overcome the CoT bottleneck, and whether the near-random performance reflects a data-scale issue or a fundamental architectural limitation.
