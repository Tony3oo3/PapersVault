## Problem

Most end-to-end autonomous driving systems implicitly couple actor existence to instantaneous observations, causing actor hypotheses to degrade or vanish during prolonged occlusion and removing critical agents from downstream prediction and planning. Standard benchmarks like nuScenes compound this by filtering unobservable actors from both supervision and evaluation, so models are never trained or rewarded for maintaining persistent representations across observability gaps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262816/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes synchronized multi-view camera images (6 cameras at 2 Hz keyframes) and produces a persistent scene representation containing both observable and unobservable actors, each with a 3D bounding box, predicted motion trajectory, and a binary observability state. Unlike standard E2E-AD outputs that only reflect currently visible agents, the actor set explicitly retains hypotheses for occluded actors, which are then fed to motion prediction and planning modules. The ego vehicle trajectory is produced as a planned path over a multi-second horizon.

## Method

BeyondSight extends SparseDrive's sparse-query architecture with a three-stage actor belief update inspired by Bayesian filtering: a Temporal Prior Decoder propagates actor queries from the previous frame using only motion-conditioned self-attention (no image features), an Observation Decoder updates hypotheses via deformable attention over current BEV features, and a Posterior Fusion Decoder reconciles the two by retaining the higher-confidence proposal per actor. The key architectural insight is that the temporal prior decoder operates entirely without image features, so unobservable actors persist through the query set without requiring the visual branch to hallucinate non-visible evidence. Training extends detection and motion supervision to unobservable actors (via the nuScenes-Permanence annotation extension) and adds a binary cross-entropy observability classification loss on the posterior decoder, explicitly decoupling existence from observability in the learned representation.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, BeyondSight achieves 0.54 L2_avg planning error and 0.07% average collision rate, outperforming the strongest baseline BridgeAD (0.58 L2_avg, 0.08% CR) and the direct parent SparseDrive (0.61 L2_avg, 0.08% CR). On perception, it reaches 0.427 mAP and 0.536 NDS, and on tracking 0.401 AMOTA, all best among compared methods. On the proposed nuScenes-Permanence benchmark, the headline result is mAP_unobs improving from 0 (SparseDrive) to 0.249 (BeyondSight), while mAP_obs remains nearly unchanged (0.415 → 0.421), confirming that gains are specific to occluded actors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BeyondSight outperforms all six baselines on planning: it reduces L2_avg from 0.61 (SparseDrive), 0.58 (BridgeAD), and 0.60 (MomAD) to 0.54, and lowers CR_avg from 0.08–0.09% to 0.07%. On standard nuScenes perception, it achieves the best mAP (0.427 vs. 0.423 for BridgeAD/MomAD and 0.415 for SparseDrive) and NDS (0.536 vs. 0.534 for BridgeAD). Tracking AMOTA improves from 0.372 (SparseDrive) to 0.401, and EPA from 0.492 to 0.502. The most striking comparison is on nuScenes-Permanence: SparseDrive scores 0 mAP_unobs (it simply drops occluded actors), while BeyondSight reaches 0.249 mAP_unobs and 0.285 EPA_unobs, with minADE_unobs dropping from 0.615 to 0.479. On a high-occlusion subset (600 samples with occluded agents near the ego path), BeyondSight halves the collision rate (0.08 vs. 0.163 CR_avg) and reduces L2_avg from 0.64 to 0.56 relative to SparseDrive. Notably, these gains come without modifying the planner; improvements stem entirely from the richer persistent scene representation fed to downstream modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

BeyondSight extends SparseDrive's sparse-query end-to-end driving architecture by inserting a Bayesian-filtering-inspired three-stage actor update (temporal prior → observation → posterior fusion) that explicitly decouples actor existence from instantaneous visual evidence. Conceptually, it draws on tracking-through-occlusion literature (OPNet, PermaTrack) and temporal query propagation (StreamPETR, BridgeAD), but departs from these by treating persistence as a first-class architectural and supervisory requirement rather than an emergent side-effect of short-horizon feature recurrence. The introduction of nuScenes-Permanence and its observability-conditioned evaluation protocol represents a benchmark-level departure from standard nuScenes, which implicitly equates observability with existence by filtering zero-LiDAR annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: ~30% of actors in nuScenes are fully unobservable at any timestep, yet standard E2E driving pipelines silently drop them, degrading planning in occlusion-heavy scenarios like intersections. The nuScenes-Permanence benchmark and its adaptive-tolerance evaluation protocol provide the field's first systematic way to train and measure persistence-aware models, which is likely to influence future E2E-AD research. Key limitations remain: persistent hypotheses can drift during long occlusions, the benchmark only covers approximately continuous motion (not abrupt hidden maneuvers), and the absolute mAP_unobs of 0.249 indicates substantial headroom. Open questions include uncertainty-aware memory, simulation-based privileged annotations, and multi-observer evaluation.
