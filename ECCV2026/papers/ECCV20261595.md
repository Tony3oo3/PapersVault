## Problem

Reconstructing 4D animals from monocular video is hindered by extreme inter-species shape variation, complex non-rigid articulations, and the absence of reliable multi-view or motion-capture data. Existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: category-specific parametric templates achieve fidelity but fail to generalize across species, while generative video-to-4D models produce plausible content but sacrifice faithfulness to the input subject. The paper argues this tension stems from treating shape priors as rigid constraints rather than flexible initializations that can be progressively corrected.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular video of an animal (typically 15–200 frames) along with Grounded-SAM segmentation masks as geometric supervision, and a Fauna single-image prediction to bootstrap canonical shape, skinning weights, and initial camera/joint parameters. It produces a canonical 3D Gaussian representation with a learnable voxel-grid skinning field, per-frame deformed Gaussians, and enables real-time free-viewpoint 4D rendering at 512×512 resolution (~105 FPS on an A6000). Optimization is per-video (test-time), taking roughly 13 minutes for a 10K-iteration run, with no shared parameters across subjects.

## Method

The core contribution is a progressive two-stage test-time optimization on 3D Gaussian Splatting that disentangles articulated pose from non-rigid deformation. Stage 1 (pose refinement) estimates per-joint LBS transformations via a self-attention block over learnable part anchors (J=20, D=8) and a symmetry-aware temporal encoding that factorizes 2D frame flip state from 3D camera reference, absorbing systematic flip-induced camera drift rather than injecting it as conflicting supervision. Stage 2 (deformation) uses the same part anchors to build per-Gaussian part-identity embeddings (z_i = w_i^T A) that, together with posed geometric attributes, query joint-specific temporal features through cross-attention to predict residual non-rigid geometry. A bilateral symmetry augmentation strategy constructs two complementary camera-frame supervision groups to double viewpoint coverage, while a learnable skinning field (32³ voxel grid) continuously corrects initial binding inaccuracies. Progressive stabilization anneals pose blending from the Fauna prior to free optimization over 7K iterations, and stage-aware rendering losses (silhouette-only for pose, full photometric for deformation) prevent appearance gradients from corrupting geometric articulation.

## Results

On DAVIS and Online, the method achieves the best input-view PSNR (26.32 and 26.21 dB), SSIM (0.920 and 0.923), and LPIPS (0.089 and 0.091), outperforming the strongest reconstruction baseline D-3DGS* by ~0.5 dB PSNR. On APTv2 (15-frame sequences), DreamMesh4D leads on input-view PSNR (26.00 vs. 24.94) due to its strong generative prior compensating for sparse temporal coverage. On the synthetic Artemis benchmark with ground-truth geometry, the method achieves 24.03 PSNR, 0.0156 normalized Chamfer distance, and 0.439 F@1%, surpassing D-3DGS* (20.05 PSNR, 0.0181 CD) and GART* (15.90 PSNR, 0.0256 CD) by substantial margins across all 2D and 3D metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the reconstruction-based baselines, the method clearly dominates: on DAVIS it beats D-3DGS* by 0.54 dB PSNR and GART* by 4.97 dB, while on Online it leads D-3DGS* by 0.55 dB and GART* by 3.08 dB. On APTv2, the generative DreamMesh4D outperforms the method on input-view PSNR (26.00 vs. 24.94) and novel-view FVD-F (629 vs. 996), benefiting from its strong pre-trained prior on short 15-frame sequences with limited camera variation; however, the method still leads DreamMesh4D on novel-view KID-16V (0.189 vs. 0.188 is a near-tie, but FVD-Diag 916 vs. 720 favours DreamMesh4D). GVFDiffusion is consistently the weakest baseline across all datasets, with PSNR 3–9 dB below the proposed method, reflecting its generative hallucination of structures absent in the input. On the Artemis benchmark with ground-truth 3D geometry, the method achieves 4.0 dB higher PSNR and 14% lower Chamfer distance than D-3DGS*, and 8.1 dB higher PSNR than GART*, confirming superior geometric fidelity when the prior (D-SMAL template) mismatches the target species.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the geometric representation and adopts the deformation-based dynamic scene paradigm from D-3DGS, while using Fauna as a coarse shape/pose initializer and GART's voxel-grid skinning field as a starting point. Its key departure is reframing the shape prior from a rigid constraint (as in GART's D-SMAL template or category-specific models) to a flexible initialization that continuously evolves during test-time optimization, coupled with a progressive two-stage pipeline that disentangles articulated pose from non-rigid deformation via shared learnable part anchors. The symmetry-aware temporal encoding is a novel mechanism to exploit bilateral mirror cues while absorbing systematic camera-estimation drift, addressing a practical failure mode that prior 3DGS-based animal pipelines (GART, D-3DGS) handle by treating pose and deformation as isolated stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that high-fidelity 4D animal reconstruction across diverse species is achievable without category-specific templates or heavy generative priors, offering a practical path for wildlife monitoring and content creation where annotated multi-view data is unavailable. However, several limitations remain open: robustness under severe camera instability, dependence on imperfect Grounded-SAM masks, and unresolved ambiguity in head motion under limited viewpoints. The broader principle—treating priors as initializations rather than constraints and letting progressive optimization correct them—could transfer to other articulated non-rigid objects, but the evaluation on in-the-wild videos relies on rendering-based proxies rather than direct 3D geometric ground truth, leaving a gap in how faithfully the reconstructions capture true anatomy.
