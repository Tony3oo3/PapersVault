## Problem

Appearance-based gaze estimation typically relies on face normalization, which requires accurate and costly landmark detection and head pose estimation. While crop-based alternatives have been explored, their geometric properties and performance trade-offs relative to normalization have remained underexplored, with no systematic comparison across datasets, pose distributions, and preprocessing conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261766/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a face crop defined by a bounding box (from annotations or a face detector like InsightFace) and the original camera intrinsic matrix as input; the crop is resized to 256×256 for the network. It outputs a 3D gaze direction (pitch and yaw) converted back to the original camera coordinate system via the CROp rotation, plus a 3 DoF head pose in SVDO+ 9D representation. Unlike normalization, no facial landmarks or explicit head pose are required at inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is Constrained Rotation Optimization (CROp), which formalizes the coordinate change induced by face cropping as a constrained rotation between crop and camera coordinate systems. Given the crop bounding box and camera intrinsics, CROp computes a rotation R* that aligns the crop's optical axis with the crop center (hard constraint) while minimizing the projection error of the four corner points (soft objective), solved efficiently via the Kabsch algorithm. This rotation converts gaze vectors between crop and camera coordinates, analogous to the rotation in normalization but derived purely from crop geometry without landmarks. A multi-task model (model-agnostic, tested with ResNet18, ResNet50, GazeTR-Hybrid) jointly regresses gaze (angular loss) and head pose (geodesic loss on SVDO+ 9D rotations), recovering the head orientation lost in cropping while improving gaze accuracy through the natural eye-head coupling.

## Results

In cross-dataset evaluation (Table 1), CROp with multi-task head pose supervision outperforms normalization in most settings. Under ideal GT preprocessing, CROp reduces angular error by 6.52% on XGaze (29.37° vs 31.23°) and 10.61% on EYEDIAP (15.46° vs 17.68°) when trained on MPIIFaceGaze. Under realistic detector-based preprocessing (Det), CROp achieves the best performance in virtually all cross-dataset cases, e.g., 8.51° vs 9.53° on MPIIFaceGaze when trained on EVE. Within-dataset evaluation confirms CROp's advantage, particularly under Det settings (e.g., 5.55° vs 6.45° on XGaze), and the full pipeline saves 23% total runtime (15.97 ms vs 20.74 ms) by eliminating landmark detection and image warping.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard normalization pipeline (Norm), which uses facial landmarks and PnP-based head pose estimation to rectify face images before gaze regression. CROp wins in the majority of cross-dataset comparisons: under GT preprocessing it achieves lower error on XGaze, EVE, and EYEDIAP (trained on MPII), and on MPIIFaceGaze and EVE (trained on XGaze); under Det preprocessing it wins in virtually every cell of Table 1. The largest margins appear on EYEDIAP (10.61% relative reduction under GT) and on MPIIFaceGaze when trained on EVE (8.51° vs 9.53° under Det). Normalization retains a narrow edge only in a few cases: XGaze→MPII under GT (7.19° vs 7.41°) and XGaze→EYEDIAP under GT (12.15° vs 12.77°), which the authors attribute to XGaze's rich pose distribution steering the multi-task model toward head pose. Under synthetic bounding-box noise up to ±30%, CROp still outperforms normalization, but at 40–50% noise the gap closes as normalization's landmark step partially compensates for box inaccuracies. Under occlusion, CROp degrades more gracefully (16.3% increase at 50% occlusion vs 17.2% for Norm), confirming its robustness advantage in realistic deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes the informal crop-based gaze estimation paradigm (previously explored in Gaze360 [23] and GazeOnce [44]) by deriving an explicit constrained rotation (CROp) that maps between crop and camera coordinate systems, making it directly comparable to the standard normalization pipeline [37, 47]. It borrows the coordinate-transformation idea from head pose estimation on crops (Img2Pose [2], IntrApose [34]) and applies it to gaze, while adopting SVDO+ [27] for differentiable multi-task rotation regression. The key departure from prior work is treating cropping not as a heuristic preprocessing step but as a well-defined geometric operation solvable via the Kabsch algorithm, enabling rigorous side-by-side comparison with normalization for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with concrete, evidence-based guidelines for choosing between normalization and crop-based preprocessing: CROp excels under extreme head poses, noisy detection, and occlusions, while normalization retains an edge when accurate landmarks are available in controlled settings. A notable limitation is the assumption that the gaze origin coincides with the bounding box center rather than the true anatomical origin, which the authors identify as a direction for future work. The finding that normalization—despite its geometric precision—still degrades at extreme poses challenges the field's default reliance on landmark-based rectification and suggests that simpler pipelines may be preferable for real-world deployment with consumer cameras.
