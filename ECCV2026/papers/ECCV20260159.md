## Problem

Existing single-domain generalized object detection (SDGOD) methods rely on two-stage detectors, extra auxiliary modules, or test-time adaptation, making them unsuitable for strict real-time deployment where the inference pipeline must remain fixed. This paper formalizes RT-SDGOD, requiring cross-domain robustness to come entirely from training-time representation learning with zero extra inference overhead, and identifies that DETR-based real-time detectors degrade under domain shift primarily through missed detections caused by over-reliance on a few fragile local discriminative cues.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and outputs bounding boxes with class labels for 7 categories (person, car, bike, rider, motor, bus, truck), evaluated at mAP@50. During training, an augmented view of the same image (generated via MAD) is jointly fed into the detector for dual-view consistency learning, but at inference only the single image is processed through the unchanged RF-DETR-L pipeline, preserving the base model's 33.9M parameters and 125.6 GFLOPs exactly.

## Method

RT-SDGDet builds on RF-DETR and introduces three training-only components: (1) refined one-to-many (O2M) supervision that assigns up to 6 queries per object to form stable object-specific query groups; (2) Discriminative Evidence Diversity Learning (DEDL), which constructs explicit evidence descriptors from deformable cross-attention weights and their sampling locations, then applies a margin-based diversity loss to encourage queries within the same group to attend to complementary spatial regions; and (3) Dual-view Evidence Consistency Learning (DvECL), which pairs cross-view queries of the same object via mutual nearest-neighbor matching on evidence descriptor similarity and enforces cosine-distance consistency on their features. The total loss combines standard O2O detection loss, O2M detection loss, DEDL diversity loss, and DvECL consistency loss, all applied only during training so that the test-time inference graph and computational cost remain identical to the base detector.

## Results

On the SDGOD benchmark (BDD100K, 5 weather domains, mAP@50), RT-SDGDet achieves 54.4% average mAP, outperforming the RF-DETR-L baseline (51.9%) by 2.5 points with identical parameter count (33.9M) and GFLOPs (125.6). The largest gains appear under severe degradation: +5.5 mAP on Night-Rainy (33.5→39.0) and +3.6 mAP on Dusk-Rainy (52.6→56.2). RT-SDGDet also surpasses the strongest domain-augmentation baseline (+MAD, 53.3%) by 1.1 mAP and the best standalone real-time detector (DEIMv2-L, 48.7%) by 5.7 mAP, all at zero extra inference overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RT-SDGDet (54.4% avg. mAP) outperforms all 14 baselines across every unseen target domain. Against standalone real-time detectors, it leads the strongest competitor DEIMv2-L (48.7%) by 5.7 mAP and RT-DETRv4-L (47.4%) by 7.0 mAP, with the gap widening sharply on Night-Rainy (39.0 vs. 32.9 and 27.5 respectively). Against domain-augmentation methods applied to the same RF-DETR-L backbone, RT-SDGDet beats the best (+MAD, 53.3%) by 1.1 mAP and +NP/+PhysAug (53.2%) by 1.2 mAP, while the weakest augmentation (+OA-DG, 52.5%) trails by 1.9 mAP. Notably, RT-SDGDet achieves these gains with identical inference cost (33.9M params, 125.6 GFLOPs) as the RF-DETR-L baseline, whereas augmentation methods also share the same inference budget but rely on additional training-time data synthesis. The advantage is most pronounced under severe degradation (Night-Rainy: +5.5 over baseline, +2.1 over +MAD), confirming that evidence-diversity learning addresses the missed-detection failure mode that domain augmentation alone cannot fully resolve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on RF-DETR (a DETR-based real-time detector) and repurposes the one-to-many (O2M) supervision mechanism from MS-DETR as a structural foundation for multi-query collaboration. It departs from existing SDGOD methods (CLIP the Gap, DG-DETR, G-NAS, SA-DETR) that rely on two-stage backbones, test-time adaptation, or extra inference modules, by introducing a stricter formulation (RT-SDGOD) where all generalization gains must come from training-time representation learning with zero additional inference cost. The two proposed training-time losses—DEDL (diversity regularization on deformable cross-attention evidence descriptors) and DvECL (cross-view consistency via MAD-generated augmented views)—are novel contributions that operate on the query-level attention structure rather than on global features or image-level augmentations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment gap: real-time detectors in autonomous driving and surveillance face weather-induced domain shifts, yet most generalization methods add inference overhead incompatible with latency budgets. The diagnostic finding that missed detections (not class confusion) dominate degradation under domain shift provides a useful lens for future work on robust detection. Limitations include evaluation on a single benchmark (five BDD100K weather splits) and a single base architecture (RF-DETR), leaving open whether the evidence-diversity and cross-view-consistency principles transfer to other DETR variants or one-stage detectors. The moderate but consistent gains (+2.5 mAP average, up to +5.5 on the hardest domain) suggest the approach is a practical training-time add-on rather than a paradigm shift.
