## Problem

Existing Text-to-Image models produce scientifically plausible yet structurally incorrect diagrams, creating a persistent visual-logic divergence that limits their utility for multimodal reasoning. No prior benchmark rigorously evaluates whether generated scientific images satisfy domain-specific structural and relational constraints, and the trade-offs between pixel-based and code-driven generation paradigms for this task remain uncharacterized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260336/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generation task takes a scientific text description (e.g., a circuit topology, molecular structure, or geometric construction) and produces either a direct RGB image (pixel-based) or executable Python rendering code that deterministically produces the image (code-based). SciGenBench comprises 1.4K problems across 5 subjects and 25 image types, each paired with atomic visual quizzes that must be answerable only from the image. For downstream training, the synthetic images are paired with masked textual cues to enforce visual reliance during RL fine-tuning of Qwen3-VL-8B.

## Method

The paper introduces ImgCoder, a code-driven pipeline that decouples reasoning from rendering via an explicit 'Understand → Plan → Code' prompting strategy, where the planning stage specifies image content, layout, labels, and drawing constraints before emitting executable Python code for deterministic rendering. For evaluation, SciGenBench employs a hybrid protocol combining five-dimensional LMM-as-Judge scoring (correctness, layout precision, readability, scientific plausibility, expressiveness on a 0–2 scale), an inverse quiz validation metric that checks whether a VQA model can answer all associated atomic questions from the image alone, and standard pixel-level metrics. Downstream utility is assessed by RL fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B on synthetic images from different generation pipelines (with and without quality filtration), revealing that higher-fidelity teachers and filtered data yield consistent reasoning gains with log-linear scaling up to 1.4K samples.

## Results

On SciGenBench, Gemini-3-Pro-ImgCoder achieves the highest inverse validation rate (R_inv) of 77.87%, surpassing the best pixel-based model Nanobanana-Pro (73.41%) by 4.5 points. Among pixel-based models, closed-source systems dominate: Nanobanana-Pro scores 1.59/2 on Correctness & Fidelity and 1.87/2 on Layout & Precision, while open-source models (HunyuanImage-3.0, Qwen-Image) remain below 40% R_inv and under 0.8 on structure-sensitive dimensions. For downstream reasoning, fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B on Nanobanana-Pro (Filt) synthetic images yields the best average score of 58.2 on MathVision + Geometry3K, a 3.7-point gain over the 54.5 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Both ImgCoder variants (Gemini-3-Pro-ImgCoder at 77.87% and Gemini-3-Flash-ImgCoder at 76.93% R_inv) outperform all pixel-based models, including the strongest Nanobanana-Pro (73.41%), with the gap widening on structure-sensitive dimensions like Correctness & Fidelity (1.82 vs. 1.59) and Scientific Plausibility (1.93 vs. 1.72). Among pixel-based baselines, a clear closed-vs-open gap exists: Nanobanana-Pro and GPT-Image-1.5 lead at 73.41% and 63.52% R_inv respectively, while open-source HunyuanImage-3.0 (30.79%) and Qwen-Image (38.86%) trail by over 30 points. The earlier code-based method AutomaTikZ performs poorly (8.80% R_inv), confirming that the 'Understand → Plan → Code' pipeline in ImgCoder is critical rather than code generation alone. On downstream reasoning (Table 6), Nanobanana-Pro (Filt) achieves the best average (58.2), marginally ahead of Gemini-ImgCoder (58.0), while Qwen-Image (57.1) lags, indicating that both high expressiveness and structural rigor contribute to training utility. The paper also notes a precision-expressiveness trade-off: code-based methods excel on mathematical/physics diagrams but produce schematic outputs for organic or textured scenes where pixel-based models like Nanobanana-Pro offer richer visual detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three lines of work: (1) T2I generation (diffusion transformers, autoregressive models), (2) code-based scientific diagram synthesis (AutomaTikZ, VisCoder), and (3) reasoning-oriented T2I evaluation (ScImage, SridBench, T2I-ReasonBench). It departs from prior T2I benchmarks by shifting evaluation from pixel-level fidelity to scientific structural correctness via inverse quiz validation and multi-dimensional LMM-as-Judge scoring. It extends code-based synthesis by introducing ImgCoder, a structured 'Understand → Plan → Code' pipeline that decouples reasoning from rendering, and it is the first to formally connect the quality of synthesized scientific images to downstream multimodal reasoning performance via RL fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The precision-expressiveness trade-off between pixel-based and code-driven paradigms provides a clear conceptual framework for practitioners choosing synthesis strategies, and the finding that code-based methods outperform even the best pixel-based SOTA on structural correctness challenges the assumption that visual richness implies scientific utility. The demonstrated log-linear scaling of downstream reasoning gains with synthetic data volume (without saturation) suggests a viable path to scaling multimodal reasoning analogous to text-domain data engines, though the evidence is currently limited to a single generator and a 50–1.4K sample range. Open questions include whether hybrid pixel+code approaches can capture both expressiveness and precision, whether the scaling trend persists beyond 1.4K samples, and whether the 'spiral co-evolution' between code-rendered training data and pixel-based model improvement is empirically real.
