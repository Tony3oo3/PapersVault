## Problem

Existing real-time streaming avatar generation methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: (1) causal attention restricts the receptive field to a few recent chunks, causing temporal drift and visual inconsistency over long-duration generation, and (2) the audio-driven paradigm produces only lip-synced speech with coarse gestures, failing to generate semantically aligned actions that respond to explicit user intent (e.g., glancing at a watch when asked the time).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260266/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single avatar reference image and continuous user speech audio as input; the audio is transcribed via ASR and processed by an LLM to produce action prompts and response audio. Output is a real-time streaming video at 576p (1024×576, 16:9) generating 3 latent frames per chunk, achieving ~26.7 FPS with a time-to-first-frame of approximately 2.6 seconds on a single H100 GPU using pipeline parallelism between the DiT and VAE.

## Method

InteractiveAvatar builds on the Wan2.2 5B DiT backbone and introduces two key mechanisms. The Long-Short Visual Memory (LSVM) decomposes history into a short-term FIFO buffer of recent compressed tokens and a long-term buffer of N representative tokens, with a Dynamic Key-Frame Selection strategy that uses SigLIP2 semantic features and a redundancy score to adaptively promote or discard frames, preserving global visual diversity without uniform temporal sampling. The Reasoning-Reaction Module (RRM) leverages an LLM to infer user intent and emit action prompts, response audio, and stable-state prompts, with a State-Cycling strategy for smooth action-to-rest transitions and a Cache-Switching mechanism that recomputes prompt-conditioned KV tensors of adjacent chunks to reduce latency on prompt changes. Training follows a four-stage pipeline: AI2V pretraining, memory compression pretraining, ODE initialization with block-causal attention, and Self-Forcing DMD distillation into a few-step autoregressive generator for real-time inference.

## Results

InteractiveAvatar achieves the best object-level temporal consistency (OBJ = 85.2) and text-video alignment (TV = 25.93) among all compared methods, while also delivering the highest inference speed at 26.68 FPS—over 1.2× faster than the next real-time method LiveAvatar (21.94 FPS) and orders of magnitude faster than non-streaming baselines (≤0.81 FPS). On identity preservation (ID = 4.51) and lip-sync distance (SynD = 7.91), it is competitive with or slightly behind the best baselines (LiveAvatar 4.53, OmniAvatar 7.87). In terms of raw video quality (IQA, ASE, FID, FVD), InteractiveAvatar is mid-pack, trailing HYAvatar (FID 76.5) and StableAvatar (FVD 654.1), but the paper emphasizes that its primary contribution is consistency and interactivity rather than peak per-frame fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest real-time competitor LiveAvatar, InteractiveAvatar wins decisively on object consistency (OBJ 85.2 vs. 76.9, +8.3 points) and text-video alignment (TV 25.93 vs. 25.78), while LiveAvatar edges ahead on identity (ID 4.53 vs. 4.51) and lip-sync confidence (SynC 4.91 vs. 4.86); InteractiveAvatar is also ~22% faster (26.68 vs. 21.94 FPS). Compared to the non-streaming baselines (StableAvatar, OmniAvatar, HYAvatar, Hallo3, EchoMimicV3, WanS2V), InteractiveAvatar achieves the best OBJ and TV scores across the board, with the largest margin over Hallo3 (OBJ +10.1, TV +1.01) and OmniAvatar (OBJ +2.4, TV +1.36). On raw quality metrics, HYAvatar leads in FID (76.5) and FVD (632.6), and EchoMimicV3 leads in IQA (3.96), but these methods run at ≤0.81 FPS, making them impractical for real-time interaction. The paper's qualitative analysis (Fig. 4) further shows that OmniAvatar fails to follow action instructions and exhibits abrupt camera changes, WanS2V degrades over time, and LiveAvatar suffers from rapid object shape/color drift—issues that InteractiveAvatar avoids through its LSVM and RRM modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the causal autoregressive distillation line (CausVid, Self-Forcing, LongLive) for real-time streaming video generation and the DiT-based audio-driven avatar paradigm (Wan2.2, EchoMimic, Hallo3). It departs from prior streaming methods that rely solely on a limited causal attention window by introducing a Long-Short Visual Memory mechanism that explicitly compresses and selectively retains historical frames, and from purely audio-driven interaction paradigms by inserting an LLM-based Reasoning-Reaction Module that interprets user intent and generates coordinated action prompts. The combination of DMD distillation, SigLIP2-guided keyframe selection, and prompt-conditioned KV-cache switching into a single real-time pipeline is the paper's architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InteractiveAvatar demonstrates that real-time (≈27 FPS) streaming avatar generation can simultaneously maintain long-duration visual consistency and respond to complex user intents with semantically aligned actions, a combination that prior systems could not achieve. The LSVM mechanism offers a general recipe for combating temporal drift in autoregressive video generation beyond avatars, and the RRM's State-Cycling + Cache-Switching design provides a practical template for integrating LLM reasoning into latency-sensitive generative pipelines. Open questions remain around objects absent from the reference frame appearing abruptly, degradation under large scene changes, and whether end-to-end training at larger scale can further smooth interaction transitions.
