## Problem

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models like VGGT achieve strong geometry recovery on land but generalize poorly to underwater scenes due to light attenuation, scattering, and view-dependent degradation. The fundamental blocker is the absence of large-scale, high-quality 3D annotations for underwater environments, making it infeasible to directly train or fine-tune these data-hungry models for underwater use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262000/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of 2–12 multi-view underwater RGB images (longest side resized to 518 px) and, in a single forward pass, predicts per-view camera parameters (a 9-D vector encoding rotation quaternion, translation, and FOV relative to the first view), a dense depth map (H×W), and a 3D point map (3×H×W) expressed in the first camera's coordinate frame. During training, the student also consumes unlabeled real underwater video frames (359k total from ~5,504 filtered clips) alongside synthetic underwater renderings of on-land annotated scenes.

## Method

Wat3R adapts VGGT to the underwater domain via a Mean Teacher semi-supervised framework that requires zero annotated underwater data. Labeled supervision comes from synthetic underwater images generated by applying a simplified underwater image formation model (with randomly sampled attenuation and backscatter coefficients) to VGGT's original on-land training set; unlabeled supervision comes from large-scale real underwater video. The key training innovation is a Cross-View Consistency Loss: teacher depth maps are backprojected into 3D and reprojected into other views, and an L1 loss is applied only on pixels selected by a static mask (two-cluster K-means foreground × multi-view depth-consistency threshold), which compensates for per-view information loss caused by scattering. Sequence-level augmentation (teacher sees 24–36 shuffled frames; student sees 2–12 subsampled frames with random rotations, color jitter, and blur) prevents pose collapse on unlabeled video. The teacher is updated via exponential moving average of student parameters, and the overall objective combines VGGT's supervised loss, a per-view pseudo-label loss, and the cross-view consistency term.

## Results

On the Shuffle 10-view multi-view depth evaluation, Wat3R achieves Rel=0.167 and δ₁=0.946 on Sea-thru, and Rel=0.119 and δ₁=0.885 on FLSea Stereo, outperforming the VGGT baseline by 12.1% (Rel) and 13.1% (Rel) respectively. On the full 100-frame subsequence protocol, Wat3R maintains its lead with Rel=0.170 (Sea-thru) and Rel=0.120 (FLSea Stereo). On the newly constructed Water3D dataset, Wat3R (Depth+Cam) achieves an Overall score of 0.406, a 34.9% improvement over VGGT (0.624), with the largest gain in Completeness (52.0% improvement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Wat3R outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluated metric and dataset. Against the strongest feed-forward competitors (π³ and DA3), Wat3R improves Rel by 9.7–13.1% and δ₁ by 3.7–4.2% on multi-view depth, with the largest gains in log10 (18.6–20.0%) and RMSE (21.2–23.7%). The two-stage UIE pipelines (Semi-UIR, PSPL) actually degrade VGGT's performance, confirming that enhancement introduces view-inconsistent artifacts rather than recovering geometric cues. On the Water3D point-cloud benchmark, Wat3R (Depth+Cam) achieves 34.9% lower Overall error than VGGT and 31.6% lower than π³, with a striking 52.0% Completeness improvement over VGGT. For monocular depth, Wat3R surpasses all underwater-specific models (Udepth, UW-Depth, WaterMono) and general-purpose models (DAv2, DA3, MapAnything) on all four datasets, e.g., Rel=0.061 vs. WaterMono's 0.074 on FLSea-VI. On camera pose (SeaThru-NeRF), DA3 remains the strongest at AUC@5° (0.731 vs. Wat3R's 0.540), but Wat3R still improves 37.8% over VGGT, indicating the cross-view consistency loss helps but the depth-ray formulation of DA3 retains an advantage for pose specifically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the VGGT feed-forward 3D reconstruction architecture to the underwater domain using a Mean Teacher semi-supervised paradigm, combining simulated underwater degradation on land-annotated data with unlabeled real underwater video. It departs from prior underwater 3D work that relies on optimization-based pipelines (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) or on underwater image enhancement as a preprocessing step, instead learning degradation-aware geometry directly during training. The cross-view consistency loss is a novel training objective that leverages multi-view geometric redundancy to compensate for per-view information loss, a mechanism not present in prior feed-forward reconstruction methods (DUSt3R, MASt3R, Fast3R, DA3, MapAnything, π3) which assume clean, well-annotated training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Wat3R establishes a practical paradigm for adapting powerful feed-forward 3D reconstruction models to annotation-scarce domains using only unlabeled video, which could transfer to other challenging environments (industrial, medical, aerial). The finding that underwater image enhancement as a two-stage preprocessing actually degrades multi-view depth estimation challenges the common assumption that cleaning the image before 3D inference is beneficial. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to highly dynamic scenes (marine life, divers), extremely turbid open-water conditions where the static mask becomes too sparse, and whether the semi-supervised recipe transfers to other degradation types beyond underwater scattering.
