## Problem

10B-level industrial inpainting models (e.g., FLUX.1-Fill-Dev) achieve state-of-the-art generation quality but are computationally prohibitive for practical deployment. Naively compressing diffusion backbones with lightweight operators (DWConv, linear attention) triggers a severe representation bottleneck that catastrophically degrades inpainting quality. The paper asks whether a rigorously designed 0.2B specialist can bridge this massive scale gap and rival 10B-level generalists.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261342/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 512×512 masked RGB image and a binary inpainting mask, both encoded into latent space via a pre-trained SDXL VAE. Additionally, Latent Categories Guidance (LCG) embeddings (K×D semantic category distributions extracted from the unmasked image) are injected as external global priors. The output is the fully inpainted RGB image decoded from the denoised latent, with a perception window of r=15 for local spatial aggregation and 20 sampling steps at inference.

## Method

Moebius replaces the standard U-Net attention and convolution blocks with LλMI blocks, each comprising three submodules: Local-λ (self-attention equivalent that summarizes spatial context into fixed-size linear matrices λ_c and λ_p via softmax(K)ᵀV and a 3D positional convolution), Interactive-λ (cross-attention equivalent that integrates LCG embeddings using the same fixed-matrix formulation with a lightweight positional embedding), and Mix-FFN (depthwise-augmented feed-forward for extreme parameter reduction). Spatial feature extraction uses Depthwise Residual Blocks instead of standard convolutions. To compensate for the representational capacity lost by this extreme compression (0.22B vs. 0.86B teacher), an adaptive multi-granularity distillation strategy operates entirely in latent space: coarse-grained L2 alignment at 16×16, fine-grained L2 alignment plus task loss at 64×64, and E-LatentLPIPS perceptual loss, all dynamically weighted by gradient-norm-based balancing to stabilize joint optimization without manual hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

Moebius (0.226B params) achieves FID 0.92 / LPIPS 0.091 on Places2 (Small), FID 5.39 / LPIPS 0.122 on CelebA-HQ (512), and FID 8.15 / LPIPS 0.125 on FFHQ (256), rivaling or surpassing the 10B-level FLUX.1-Fill-Dev (11.9B) on portrait benchmarks while using less than 2% of its parameters. In total inference time, Moebius completes 20 steps in 0.52 s versus 8.05 s for FLUX.1-Fill-Dev (50 steps) and 4.23 s for SD3.5 Large-Inpainting (28 steps), yielding a >15× speedup. Single-step latency is 26.01 ms with 0.154 TFLOPs, compared to 161.01 ms and 9.927 TFLOPs for FLUX.1-Fill-Dev.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 10B-level FLUX.1-Fill-Dev, Moebius wins decisively on portrait benchmarks (CelebA-HQ: 5.39 vs 10.13 FID; FFHQ: 8.15 vs 11.19 FID) and ties on Places2 Small (0.92 vs 0.94 FID), while trailing slightly on Places2 Test (9.48 vs 8.02 FID) and Places2 Large (2.35 vs 1.86 FID). Against SD3.5 Large-Inpainting (8.05B), Moebius dominates across all benchmarks, with the most dramatic gap on FFHQ (8.15 vs 109.42 FID). Compared to its teacher PixelHacker (0.86B), Moebius is marginally worse on FID (e.g., 0.92 vs 0.82 on Places2 Small, 5.39 vs 4.75 on CelebA-HQ) but runs 1.8× faster per step (26.01 vs 46.89 ms). Among academic diffusion-based baselines (MAT, HD-Painter, DDNM, RoRem, PowerPaint), Moebius achieves the best or near-best FID on all Places2 and CelebA-HQ splits. In the user study, Moebius (31.76% preference) matches its teacher PixelHacker (32.18%) and significantly outperforms FLUX.1-Fill-Dev (23.70%) and SD3.5 Large-Inp. (12.36%), with the highest preference (32.27%) in portrait scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

Moebius builds directly on the Latent Diffusion Model framework and the Latent Categories Guidance (LCG) paradigm introduced by PixelHacker, which it uses as both architectural template and distillation teacher. It departs from prior efficient-architecture work (DWConv, GLA, Mix-FFN) by demonstrating that naive operator substitution in diffusion backbones triggers a severe representation bottleneck, and instead proposes the LλMI block—a fixed-size linear-matrix formulation that unifies self- and cross-attention equivalents. The adaptive multi-granularity latent distillation strategy extends classical knowledge distillation to the diffusion setting, targeting architectural capacity transfer rather than the more common timestep/sampling acceleration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 0.22B specialist can match or exceed 10B-level generalists on fine-tuned inpainting benchmarks with >15× total inference speedup is practically significant for edge and latency-sensitive deployment. However, the comparison is inherently asymmetric: Moebius is a task-specific model fine-tuned on standard academic benchmarks, while FLUX.1-Fill-Dev and SD3.5 are zero-shot generalists, so the 'bridging the scale gap' claim should be read within the specialist evaluation paradigm. Open questions include whether the LλMI + distillation recipe transfers to other generative tasks (segmentation, super-resolution), whether the approach works without a high-capacity teacher, and whether the fine-tuning requirement limits real-world versatility.
