## Problem

High-resolution 3D raw FMCW radar ADC data is fundamentally scarce: commodity mmWave sensors have only tens of antennas (limiting angular resolution to several degrees), public datasets provide only post-processed 2D range-azimuth maps or sparse point clouds, and hardware scaling or SAR scanning is impractical at fleet scale. Existing differentiable RF renderers (Sionna-RT, SFCW inverse rendering, InverTwin) are either forward-only, model only single-bounce or narrowband signals, or detach path geometry from the computation graph so that vertex positions and normals receive no gradients. What is missing is an end-to-end differentiable wideband FMCW renderer that can learn physically interpretable scene parameters directly from real radar captures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a LiDAR-derived triangle mesh (screened Poisson reconstruction from ~50 sweeps, GPU-aligned to the radar frame), synchronized real FMCW ADC captures from the ColoRadar dataset, and antenna/sensor parameters (Tx/Rx positions, beam patterns). It produces optimized per-vertex ITU-R P.2040 material parameters (complex permittivity, RMS roughness, correlation length, KA/SPM blend factor, slab thickness), vertex normals, and antenna beam patterns. After freezing these parameters, the scene is re-rendered from arbitrarily large virtual apertures (up to 100×100 elements, 10,000 virtual elements) to synthesize dense 3D RAE radar ADC at 256×127×127 resolution, from which a sparse 3D occupancy point cloud is extracted at the 99.9th intensity percentile. The training loss operates on min-max-normalized range-azimuth magnitude rather than raw ADC, because absolute phase at 77 GHz carries no reliable supervisory signal.

## Method

mmIR builds a phase-coherent MIMO forward model grounded in the bistatic radar equation, where multi-bounce Monte Carlo paths are accumulated into complex FMCW chirp samples via s_ij[k] = a_ij exp(j2π(f_c + St_k)τ), and the entire pipeline (ray tracing, BSDF evaluation, phasor accumulation, ADC synthesis) executes inside a single DrJit computation graph so that automatic differentiation propagates gradients from the RA loss back through all bounces to per-vertex materials, normals, and antenna patterns. The surface interaction is governed by a physically-based mmWave BSDF with six per-vertex learnable ITU parameters, decomposing into coherent (GGX microfacet + von Mises-Fisher specular lobes blended by a learnable KA/SPM factor) and incoherent (directional + Lambertian) components, gated by a Rayleigh roughness factor and a slab Fresnel energy gate, with Jones polarization tracked through every bounce. Ray generation combines Rx-centric reservoir sampling (cosine-weighted hemisphere proposals retaining K scene-hitting directions), Specular Manifold Sampling extended with Implicit Function Theorem Jacobian storage for backpropagation through vertex positions, and a free-space diffraction BSDF extended with material-dependent edge opacity and Jones polarization for MIMO phase coherence. MIMO coherence is preserved by sharing path topology across all Tx-Rx pairs (only the Tx→x₁ and x_D→Rx legs are vectorized per pair), reducing cost from O(N_Tx·N) to O(N). Optimization minimizes an L2 loss on min-max-normalized RA magnitude over 500 iterations (~11 min per scene), with projective boundary gradient sampling available for silhouette-edge corrections when geometry or pose gradients are enabled.

## Results

On the ColoRadar dataset (7 outdoor + 6 indoor scenes), mmIR achieves a mean Pearson range-azimuth correlation of 0.914 (outdoor 0.919, indoor 0.909) versus 0.307 for Sionna-RT, with mean RA PSNR of 37.6 dB (outdoor) and 42.9 dB (indoor) versus 22.3 and 24.5 dB respectively. In cross-sensor transfer (cascaded 12Tx×16Rx → single-chip 3Tx×4Rx), mmIR retains 0.554 mean RA correlation without re-training, compared to 0.114 for Sionna-RT. For 3D occupancy from a dense 100×100 virtual array, mmIR achieves mean Precision 0.618, Recall 0.257, and F1 0.352 against LiDAR, substantially outperforming the ColoRadar cascaded-radar baseline (0.319/0.083/0.129) and reducing RMSE from 2.63 m to 1.64 m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Sionna-RT [18], an open-source differentiable ray-tracing renderer for radio propagation. mmIR outperforms Sionna-RT on every scene and every metric in the training evaluation: mean RA Pearson correlation is 0.914 vs. 0.307 (a 3× gap), mean RA PSNR is 37.6 dB vs. 22.3 dB outdoors, and mean RA RMSE is 0.0137 vs. 0.0808. The gap is attributed to three architectural differences: mmIR's per-vertex six-parameter ITU BSDF captures spatially varying reflectance, joint optimization of normals and antenna beam patterns corrects LiDAR tessellation errors and gain biases, and end-to-end gradient flow through the full ray-tracing path (vs. Sionna-RT's detached path topology). In cross-sensor transfer, mmIR achieves 0.554 mean RA correlation on the single-chip radar versus 0.114 for Sionna-RT, confirming that the optimized scene encodes transferable physics. For 3D occupancy (Table 4), mmIR's dense virtual-array rendering outperforms the ColoRadar cascaded-radar point cloud baseline on all metrics: Precision 0.618 vs. 0.319, Recall 0.257 vs. 0.083, F1 0.352 vs. 0.129, RMSE 1.64 m vs. 2.63 m, and R-CD 0.141 vs. 0.208, with the largest gains in scenes with multiple in-view surfaces (e.g., S1-F185: Precision 0.997, R-CD 0.067).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable rendering paradigm—well established for RGB, LiDAR, and acoustics—into the mmWave FMCW radar domain, where prior RF differentiable renderers (Sionna-RT, SFCW inverse rendering, InverTwin) each cover only a fragment: Sionna-RT propagates gradients through field coefficients but not through ray-tracing topology or geometry; SFCW assumes known geometry with single-bounce propagation; InverTwin addresses multipath gradients but validates only on synthetic data. mmIR unifies these by placing the entire phase-coherent MIMO forward model (multi-bounce SBR, polarization-aware BSDFs, free-space diffraction, FMCW ADC synthesis) inside a single DrJit computation graph, enabling end-to-end gradients from raw ADC back to per-vertex ITU materials, normals, and antenna patterns. It also repurposes optical rendering techniques—Specular Manifold Sampling, free-space diffraction BSDF, projective boundary sampling—and adapts them to the sub-wavelength mesh and MIMO coherence constraints specific to 77 GHz radar.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and acknowledged bottleneck: the near-total absence of high-resolution, 3D, raw FMCW ADC training data for radar perception, which limits detection, segmentation, and occupancy prediction. The virtual-aperture re-rendering framework (100×100 elements) offers a practical path to synthesizing dense 3D RAE data from a single LiDAR-assisted fit, and the cross-sensor transfer result (0.554 correlation without re-training) provides evidence that the optimized scenes capture transferable physics rather than sensor-specific artifacts. Key open questions remain: single-frame, static-scene supervision is severely ill-posed (no shape-from-radar), the method is bottlenecked by LiDAR mesh quality and alignment, and it does not model transmission, refraction, volumetric scattering, or Doppler from ego-motion. Validation is confined to one dataset (ColoRadar) in clear weather, and inferred materials have not been verified against ground-truth dielectric properties.
