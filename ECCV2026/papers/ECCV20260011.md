## Problem

RenderFormer, the prior transformer-based neural renderer, is limited to scenes with fewer than 4k triangles, a hard-coded GGX BRDF, and at most 8 triangular diffuse light sources, with brute-force self-attention that both scales quadratically and loses focus on large meshes. RenderFormer-V2 addresses these bottlenecks to enable neural rendering of scenes with over 100k primitives, heterogeneous geometry (triangles, voxels, environment maps), and flexible material specifications without per-scene training or specialized code.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of heterogeneous tokens—textured triangles (with 32×32 material/normal/displacement patches encoded via a pretrained VAE), scattering voxels, up to 8 colored triangular light sources, LDR+HDR environment maps at 512×256, and 8×8 camera ray bundles—and outputs a single rendered image with full global illumination at resolutions up to 2048×2048. All spatial information is encoded via RoPE on primitive centroids (20 frequencies), making the representation translation-invariant, and the view-dependent stage operates in world coordinates rather than camera coordinates.

## Method

RenderFormer-V2 retains RenderFormer's two-stage transformer pipeline but replaces the view-independent stage's full self-attention with a combined sparse mechanism: geometry tokens are serialized along Hilbert curves to enable a 256-token sliding window for local transport, while a rendering-informed attention sink (16 global register tokens, all light-source tokens, and mean-pooled summarization tokens over every 64 consecutive primitives) captures global illumination and occlusion. The view-dependent stage uses SWIN windowed attention (window 8, shift 4) for resolution-independent scaling. Material appearance is decoupled from any specific BRDF model via a 9D latent embedding learned by a CNN autoencoder on rendered sphere images under the Uffizi Gallery light probe, with spatially varying materials encoded through a pretrained VAE on 32×32 texture patches. Training follows a five-stage curriculum (1k→4k→16k→64k primitives, 256²→512²→2048² resolution, progressively adding SVBRDFs, environment lighting, and volumes) totaling 19 days on 32 A100 GPUs, with a weighted L1 + LPIPS loss on log-encoded images.

## Results

RenderFormer-V2 achieves PSNR 28.25, SSIM 0.8982, LPIPS 0.0997, and HDR-FLIP 0.4200 on randomly generated test scenes (16k primitives, 512×512 resolution). Notably, the sparse attention mechanism outperforms full attention (PSNR 27.91, SSIM 0.8953), which the authors attribute to full attention suffering from focus loss over large token sequences. The model scales to 64k primitives and 2048×2048 resolution, and qualitatively maintains render fidelity at 83.2k primitives where RenderFormer exhibits visible darkening due to attentional focus loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative and qualitative baseline is RenderFormer [41], the direct predecessor. RenderFormer-V2 outperforms RenderFormer on render quality at high primitive counts: at 83.2k triangles, RenderFormer produces a visibly darkened image due to loss of attentional focus, while RenderFormer-V2 maintains correct illumination (Figure 9). In terms of efficiency, RenderFormer-V2 is substantially faster at scaling with mesh complexity (Figure 7) and resolution (Figure 8), thanks to the combined windowed-attention and attention-sink mechanism in the view-independent stage and SWIN attention in the view-dependent stage. The authors note that training RenderFormer beyond its 4k-triangle limit is unstable, making a direct quantitative comparison at higher primitive counts infeasible. No comparison against other neural rendering methods (e.g., DiffusionRenderer, Deep Shading) is provided; the paper positions RenderFormer-V2 as complementary to physics-based renderers rather than competing with other learned approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

RenderFormer-V2 is a direct architectural successor to RenderFormer [41], which first cast global light transport as a sequence-to-sequence transformer problem over triangle tokens. The key departure is replacing RenderFormer's brute-force self-attention in the view-independent stage with a rendering-informed sparse attention mechanism that combines SWIN-style windowed attention [19], Hilbert-curve serialization (inspired by Point Transformer v3 [34]), and attention sinks [35] repurposed with rendering semantics (light-source tokens and geometry summarization tokens). Additionally, the paper decouples material specification from a hard-coded GGX BRDF by introducing a learned 9D latent material appearance space (inspired by perceptual BRDF similarity work [24]) and supports heterogeneous primitives (voxels, environment maps) that RenderFormer could not handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By scaling from 4k to 100k+ primitives and supporting caustics, volumetric scattering, displacement mapping, and environment lighting without per-scene training or specialized code, RenderFormer-V2 makes learned neural rendering substantially more practical for complex scenes. The latent material space's ability to encode measured BRDFs outside the training distribution suggests a path toward truly general material handling. Key open questions include: whether adding new primitive types can avoid full retraining, how to enforce temporal coherence for animation, and whether the fixed 32×32 per-triangle texture resolution can be made adaptive. The 19-day / 32×A100 training cost remains a barrier to rapid iteration, though the authors emphasize no fine-tuning is needed at inference time.
