## Problem

Current RGB-only 3D reconstruction pipelines produce monolithic scene representations that are defined up to an arbitrary global rotation and entangle rigid foreground objects with background geometry, making them fundamentally unsuitable for stable physical simulation. Existing work recovers interactivity by replacing reconstructed objects with retrieved CAD assets, but this sacrifices scene-specific geometric fidelity and introduces a slow, brittle retrieval-and-alignment stage. The paper asks whether one can factorize unstructured multi-view RGB into a physically grounded, structurally disentangled representation without any external CAD models.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes multi-view RGB images (resized to a maximum side length of 504 px) and a single 2D bounding box on a reference view, which can be drawn by a user or generated by a vision-language model. It outputs a structured hybrid scene representation in a unified Gravity-View coordinate frame: explicit standalone rigid meshes with refined 6-DoF poses for user-specified objects, a cleaned background as either a colored point cloud or 3D Gaussian Splatting, and a directly importable MuJoCo physics environment where the floor is a static collision body and objects are dynamic rigid bodies. The dual-representation design means the same scene simultaneously supports rigid-body collision dynamics (via meshes) and photorealistic rendering (via point cloud or 3DGS).

## Method

GARDEN's core insight is to use gravity as a universal physical prior that resolves the gauge ambiguity inherent in multi-view reconstruction and anchors objects consistently. Stage 1 (Gravity-View alignment) extracts global camera tokens from a frozen DepthAnything3 backbone, feeds them through a Context Transformer Decoder that cross-attends a reference-view query against all-view keys/values, and regresses a 6D rotation to the gravity-aligned frame via an MLP head, supervised with L1 loss on rotation matrices from simulation datasets. Stage 2 (object generation and layout) cascades SAM-3 for 2D mask extraction, SAM-3D for amodal 3D mesh reconstruction, and FoundationPose for 6-DoF refinement; because the mesh is axis-aligned and gravity fixes the vertical axis, the rotational search reduces to a single yaw angle plus translation. Stage 3 (background disentanglement) employs a Transformer-based conditional point classifier that takes scene points cropped by a gravity-aligned relaxed bounding box and condition points sampled from the generated mesh, predicting per-point binary labels to remove duplicate object geometry while preserving background structures; it is trained on InternScenes with artifact-oriented 3D augmentations (multi-layer ghosting, thin-part deformation, virtual multi-view scanning, pose/scale jittering) to bridge the sim-to-real gap.

## Results

On the holistic scene benchmark, GARDEN (point cloud, post-simulation) achieves RMSE 0.1616 and LPIPS 0.4081, substantially outperforming LiteReality (RMSE 0.2664, LPIPS 0.6522) while maintaining comparable SSIM (0.5735 vs. 0.5818). On the object-centric benchmark under the stricter post-MuJoCo protocol, GARDEN reduces LPIPS from 0.5854 (LiteReality) to 0.4444 and RMSE from 0.2163 to 0.1887. For gravity estimation, the GV predictor achieves 1.40° mean angular error on Hypersim and 1.56° on TartanAir with 0% failure rate above 10°, outperforming GeoCalib+RANSAC (1.71°/2.69°). End-to-end inference latency drops from 4332.8 s (LiteReality) to 560.2 s, a 7.7× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GARDEN consistently outperforms all retrieval-based baselines on both object-centric and holistic scene metrics. Against LiteReality (the strongest baseline), GARDEN improves RMSE by ~39% (0.2664→0.1616) and LPIPS by ~37% (0.6522→0.4081) on the holistic scene benchmark, while SSIM is slightly lower (0.5735 vs. 0.5818), which the authors attribute to the absence of a dedicated texture-prediction stage and minor post-simulation pose perturbations. Against Phone2Proc and ACDC (and their MIR/Sem&Vis variants), GARDEN wins on all three metrics by wide margins (e.g., RMSE 0.1616 vs. 0.3046–0.3653). On gravity estimation, GARDEN's GV predictor (1.40° mean on Hypersim, 1.56° on TartanAir, 0% Fail@10) surpasses GeoCalib+RANSAC (1.71°/2.69°, 0%/9.4%) and COLMAP's Manhattan alignment (2.62°/8.37°, 0%/12.5%). In computational efficiency, GARDEN runs in 560 s on average versus LiteReality's 4333 s, a 7.7× speedup, by eliminating the CAD retrieval and layout-estimation stages.

## Relation to Prior Work

GARDEN sits at the intersection of multi-view 3D reconstruction (DepthAnything3, VGGT, Pi3) and simulation-ready scene generation (LiteReality, Phone2Proc, ACDC), but departs from both lines in a key way: rather than producing a monolithic scene or replacing objects with retrieved CAD assets, it uses gravity as a universal physical prior to resolve the global orientation gauge ambiguity that plagues all prior RGB-only reconstructions. The pipeline composes existing components—SAM-3D for amodal mesh generation, FoundationPose for 6-DoF refinement, and a custom Transformer point classifier for background disentanglement—into a unified gravity-aligned factorization framework, with the Gravity-View alignment module (inspired by GVHMR) being the novel architectural contribution that ties everything into a physically consistent coordinate system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the CAD retrieval-and-replacement stage, GARDEN addresses a practical bottleneck (7.7× speedup) while preserving scene-specific geometric fidelity that proxy assets inherently compromise, making it a more scalable path toward simulation-ready digital twins for embodied AI. Limitations include the dependence on user- or VLM-provided bounding boxes for object selection, the absence of a dedicated texture-prediction stage (reflected in slightly lower SSIM), and training of the point classifier on simulation data with augmentation to bridge the domain gap. Open questions include how the pipeline scales to scenes with many interactive objects, whether the gravity-alignment module generalizes to outdoor or highly dynamic environments, and whether the hybrid mesh-plus-point-cloud representation can support more complex contact-rich interactions beyond simple rigid-body dynamics.
