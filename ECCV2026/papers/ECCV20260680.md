## Problem

Large-scale 3D vision foundation models (e.g., VGGT) provide strong geometric priors for manipulation, but their per-frame inference latency is prohibitive for real-time robot control. Standard diffusion policies rely on generic 2D encoders that lack explicit 3D spatial perception and effective temporal modeling, leading to poor performance on geometry-sensitive tasks. The paper addresses how to inject 3D foundation model features into manipulation policies while preserving real-time inference speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260680/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (head and front cameras), ground-truth camera intrinsics and extrinsics from robot-mounted sensors, and proprioception. VGGT is queried only on sparse keyframes every τ inference steps (τ=4 or 8), while TFPNet predicts features for all intermediate frames from a single historical feature vector plus the current RGB frame. The output is a future action sequence of 8 frames uniformly spaced by 8 timesteps (effective temporal window of 64 frames), generated via iterative DDPM denoising conditioned on the fused 3D-aware visual embedding.

## Method

R3DP introduces an Asynchronous Fast-Slow Collaboration (AFSC) module where a frozen VGGT backbone (slow system) processes sparse keyframes to produce high-fidelity 3D features, while a lightweight TFPNet (fast system) propagates these features to intermediate frames using DINOv2-S encoding, four Alternating-Attention Transformer blocks for cross-view aggregation, and cross-attention to the previous frame's predicted feature. TFPNet is pre-trained via cosine-similarity distillation against VGGT outputs over 4-frame sequences with random 1–8 frame gaps, and this temporal prediction also reduces temporal scale drift (LogDrift drops from 0.0272 to 0.0066). A Multi-View Feature Fuser (MVFF) then fuses per-view 2D ResNet features with 3D features via cross-attention, followed by PRoPE (Projective Positional Encoding) that explicitly encodes camera intrinsics and extrinsics into attention to enforce geometric consistency across views. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a unified diffusion loss, updating only the diffusion denoiser head while keeping VGGT and TFPNet frozen, achieving 44.8% lower inference latency than naive per-frame VGGT integration.

## Results

On the RoboTwin benchmark (10 dual-arm manipulation tasks), R3DP (τ=4) achieves a 69.0% average success rate, outperforming single-view Diffusion Policy (36.1%) by 32.9% and multi-view Diffusion Policy (17.6%) by 51.4%. R3DP also surpasses the depth-based DP3 baseline (57.6%) by 11.4% and the VLA baselines π₀ (59.9%) and π₀.₅ (66.7%) by 9.1% and 2.3% respectively. In terms of efficiency, R3DP (τ=8) reduces observation encoding latency to 40.3 ms, a 44.8% reduction over the naive DP+VGGT integration (73.1 ms), while maintaining a success rate within 3.3% of the τ=4 setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R3DP (τ=4) wins decisively over both single-view DP (36.1%) and multi-view DP (17.6%), with the multi-view DP actually underperforming single-view DP, indicating that naive feature concatenation fails to exploit cross-view geometry. Against the depth-based DP3 (57.6%), R3DP leads by 11.4%, and the gap widens dramatically to 40.8% over DP3+DA2 (28.2%), showing that R3DP's RGB-only 3D priors are far more robust than noisy estimated depth. R3DP also outperforms the VLA baselines π₀ (59.9%) and π₀.₅ (66.7%) by 9.1% and 2.3% respectively, though the margin over π₀.₅ is modest and R3DP loses on individual tasks like Blocks Stack Easy (69% vs 86%) and Dual Shoes Place (24% vs 51%). On RoboTwin 2.0, R3DP achieves 63.8% average, beating DP (30.3%), DP3 (60.3%), π₀ (55.3%), and SpatialVLA (52.8%). In real-world experiments, R3DP reaches 71.7% average success, surpassing DP (30.8%) by 40.9% and DP3 (48.4%) by 23.3%, while reducing observation encoding latency from 105.1 ms (DP+VGGT+MVFF) to 62.18 ms.

## Relation to Prior Work

R3DP builds on Diffusion Policy as its action-generation backbone and on VGGT as a frozen 3D vision foundation model, but departs from prior 3D-aware imitation work (e.g., DP3, eVGGT) by avoiding per-frame 3D inference through an asynchronous fast-slow collaboration inspired by SlowFast networks. The multi-view fusion component extends PRoPE's projective positional encoding into a dedicated fuser (MVFF) that explicitly injects camera intrinsics and extrinsics, going beyond the implicit or heuristic multi-view merging used in prior diffusion-policy and VLA approaches. The lightweight TFPNet, pre-trained via cosine-similarity distillation from VGGT, repurposes the temporal-prediction idea from video understanding to propagate 3D features across intermediate frames in real time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design—swapping a 2D visual encoder for the R3DP perception module—makes it straightforward to retrofit 3D priors onto existing imitation policies without re-architecting the action head, which is practically valuable for the robotics community. The finding that a lightweight temporal predictor (TFPNet) produces more temporally stable 3D features than the heavy VGGT backbone itself (LogDrift 0.0066 vs. 0.0272) suggests that temporal regularization can compensate for per-frame geometric inconsistency, an insight that may generalize to other foundation-model-in-the-loop settings. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer-horizon, contact-rich tasks, whether it transfers to other 3D backbones beyond VGGT, and how it interacts with reinforcement-learning-based policy improvement, which the authors flag as future work.
