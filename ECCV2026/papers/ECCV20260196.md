## Problem

Existing semi-supervised semantic segmentation methods that incorporate VLM text embeddings treat them as domain-invariant, single-level representations applied uniformly to all images, ignoring dataset-specific and image-specific contextual cues. This generic guidance, combined with pixel-centric decoders that keep language as a weak auxiliary signal, leads to poor intra-class discrimination, confusion between visually similar classes, and unstable vision-language alignment under limited supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260196/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (randomly cropped to 512×512 for Pascal VOC, COCO, and ADE20K; 801×801 for Cityscapes) alongside class names enriched with learnable dataset-specific attribute prompts. The frozen VLM text encoder processes these prompts to produce per-class embeddings, which are then transformed into multi-scale object queries. The output is a pixel-level segmentation mask computed as the product of per-pixel embeddings and class-specific mask embeddings, combined with per-query class logits to yield the final per-pixel class prediction.

## Method

HVLFormer builds on Mask2Former's mask-transformer decoder but replaces randomly initialized object queries with domain-aware hierarchical textual queries generated by the HTQG module, which uses learnable dataset-specific prompts to produce attribute-rich class descriptions, encodes them via a frozen VLM text encoder, and projects them into E=3 multi-scale queries (coarse-to-fine) via MLP heads aligned with pixel-decoder feature maps, while a Semantic Relevance Estimator adaptively weights each query by its estimated class presence in the image. The PTRM module then performs bidirectional spatially-guided conditioning: decomposed attention maps (text-guided, pixel-guided, and fused consensus) inject local visual cues into textual queries and class-level priors into pixel features, making queries spatially aware and discriminative. Finally, CMCR enforces mask, class-prediction, and pixel-text attention consistency across three augmented views (original, weak, strong) at every decoder layer, extracting supervisory signals from unlabeled data to stabilize vision-language alignment and prevent error accumulation during iterative decoding.

## Results

On Pascal VOC with the SigLIP2 backbone, HVLFormer achieves 89.1 mIoU with only 92 labeled images (1/11592 split) and 91.7 mIoU with 1,464 labels (1/71464 split), setting new SOTA across all splits. On COCO, it reaches 58.8 mIoU with 232 labels and 67.2 mIoU with 3,700 labels, surpassing the previous best (SemiVL) by +8.7 and +10.7 mIoU respectively. On ADE20K, HVLFormer attains 40.3 mIoU with just 158 labels, a +15.0 mIoU gain over its TQDM baseline. On Cityscapes, it achieves 79.9 mIoU with 100 labels, outperforming SemiVL by +3.7 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HVLFormer (SigLIP2) outperforms all 16 baselines across all four datasets and all label splits. Against the strongest prior VLM-based method SemiVL (ViT-B), it gains +5.1 mIoU on Pascal VOC (89.1 vs 84.0 at 92 labels), +8.7 mIoU on COCO (58.8 vs 50.1 at 232 labels), +12.1 mIoU on ADE20K (40.3 vs 28.1 at 158 labels), and +3.7 mIoU on Cityscapes (79.9 vs 76.2 at 100 labels). Compared to its direct architectural baseline TQDM (SigLIP2), the gains are +13.0 mIoU on Pascal VOC, +18.6 mIoU on COCO, +15.0 mIoU on ADE20K, and +10.8 mIoU on Cityscapes at the lowest label splits, confirming that domain-aware hierarchical queries and CMCR are the primary sources of improvement. Against the strongest non-VLM baseline UniMatch V2 (DINov2-B), HVLFormer leads by +2.8 mIoU on Pascal VOC (91.7 vs 90.8) and +3.9 mIoU on COCO (67.2 vs 63.3) at the highest label splits. Even with the smaller EVA02-S backbone (40M params), HVLFormer surpasses all VLM-based SOTA methods including SemiVL and Pseudo-SD (both 88M params), demonstrating superior label efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

HVLFormer builds directly on the Mask2Former masked-attention decoder architecture and the TQDM textual-query-driven mask transformer, which first demonstrated that CLIP text embeddings can initialize object queries for segmentation. The key departure is a shift from domain-invariant to domain-aware text conditioning: while TQDM targets domain generalization (where invariance is desirable) and SemiVL treats CLIP embeddings as single-level, static regularizers, HVLFormer argues that SSS under limited labels requires dataset-specific and image-specific adaptation of textual queries. This is operationalized through hierarchical multi-scale query generation, semantic relevance estimation to suppress absent-class queries, and a bidirectional pixel-text refinement module that injects spatial context back into the queries before decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete bottleneck in VLM-augmented SSS: generic, domain-invariant text embeddings provide insufficient semantic grounding when labeled data is scarce, leading to confusion between visually similar classes and weak intra-class discrimination. The consistent SOTA across four benchmarks (Pascal VOC, COCO, ADE20K, Cityscapes) with particularly large gains at the lowest label splits validates the domain-awareness hypothesis. However, gains on Cityscapes are notably smaller, attributed to small/distant object classes absent from VLM pre-training captions, suggesting the approach inherits VLM coverage limitations. Open questions include whether the hierarchical query structure generalizes to truly open-vocabulary or long-tail settings, and whether the 107–109M parameter cost of the VLM backbone is justified relative to lighter DINOv2-based methods that achieve competitive (though lower) performance.
