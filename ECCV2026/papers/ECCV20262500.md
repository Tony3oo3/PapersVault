## Problem

VFM-based iterative stereo pipelines under-exploit three information pathways: multi-scale backbone features are collapsed into single-level correlations, the backbone's geometric priors remain unused at initialization, and the recurrent update operator propagates context only over local neighborhoods. This backbone-update interface bottleneck becomes especially severe under degraded photometric conditions such as underwater scenes, where reliable disparity must be propagated from well-matched regions into textureless or scattered areas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262500/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a rectified stereo image pair (I_L, I_R) and produces a dense disparity map through T=8 iterative refinement steps (reducible to T=2 for real-time at 12.5 FPS on 480×640). A frozen Depth Anything V3 backbone (DINOv2 ViT-B/14 + Dual-DPT) is run once to extract multi-scale features at 1/4, 1/8, and 1/16 resolutions for correlation, and a monocular depth map for initialization. SIFT keypoints matched along epipolar lines provide sparse correspondences for metric scale-shift alignment of the monocular depth into disparity space.

## Method

LinStereo's core is the Position-Aware Linear Attention (PALA) block, which replaces the local ConvGRU update with a global O(N) attention operator that aggregates context across the entire feature map at each refinement step. Spatial awareness is restored to vanilla linear attention via 2D Rotary Position Embeddings applied asymmetrically—only the numerator uses position-augmented queries and keys while the denominator retains original values for stable normalization—preserving linear complexity while encoding relative spatial structure. Two enabling components make PALA effective: Hierarchical Semantic Cost Volumes (HSCV) build a per-scale correlation volume from each VFM feature level with an internal 4-level disparity pyramid, giving PALA semantically aligned matching evidence at every iteration; and Depth Prior Initialization (DPI) converts the backbone's affine-invariant monocular depth into a metrically calibrated disparity warm start via least-squares scale-shift fitting to SIFT matches. Three PALA updaters (one per scale) are updated in coarse-to-fine order with a gated residual connection that lets each pixel selectively incorporate new correlation evidence, enabling reliable disparity to propagate into uncertain regions across iterations.

## Results

On underwater benchmarks (the paper's primary differentiator), LinStereo trained only on SceneFlow achieves AbsRel 0.04 and RMSE 2.08 on TartanAir-UW, and AbsRel 0.04 and RMSE 0.90 on SQUID, outperforming all baselines including those with extra training data. This corresponds to 31% lower RMSE than FoundationStereo on TartanAir-UW and 26% lower AbsRel than IGEV++ on SQUID. On standard benchmarks, LinStereo (ViT-B, SceneFlow-only) matches or beats the larger ViT-L BridgeDepth on 13/14 metrics and achieves 37% lower EPE than the previous best on Middlebury occluded regions (1.33 vs. 2.11). At T=2 iterations, LinStereo runs at 12.5 FPS while still surpassing all efficient stereo baselines on underwater metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On underwater benchmarks, LinStereo achieves the best result on every metric for both TartanAir-UW and SQUID, surpassing even methods trained with additional domain-specific data: it beats FoundationStereo (ViT-L, extra data) by 31% in RMSE on TartanAir-UW (2.08 vs. 3.01) and IGEV++ (extra data) by 26% in AbsRel on SQUID (0.04 vs. 0.06). Among SceneFlow-only baselines, the gap is even larger—Stereo Anywhere (0.06 AbsRel) and DEFOM-Stereo (0.05) trail by 33–50% on TartanAir-UW. On standard benchmarks, LinStereo (ViT-B, SceneFlow-only) is competitive with but does not uniformly beat the strongest ViT-L + extra-data baseline FoundationStereo, which leads on most KITTI/Middlebury metrics due to its larger backbone and additional training data. However, LinStereo matches or exceeds BridgeDepth (ViT-L, no extra data) on 13/14 metrics and outperforms MonSter (ViT-L) on 9/14, with the largest margin on Middlebury occluded regions (EPE 1.33 vs. 2.11 for DEFOM-Stereo, a 37% improvement). On the close-range laboratory tank, LinStereo achieves AbsRel 0.04 and RMSE 0.07, outperforming IGEV++ (0.05/0.12) and FoundationStereo (0.07/0.18) by substantial margins, confirming robustness under extreme near-range underwater conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

LinStereo extends the iterative stereo refinement paradigm established by RAFT-Stereo, which uses a local ConvGRU operator to progressively refine disparity by querying a fixed correlation volume. The key departure is replacing this local recurrent update with Position-Aware Linear Attention (PALA), a global aggregation operator at linear complexity, motivated by the observation that VFM backbones (here Depth Anything V3 with DINOv2) produce semantically rich multi-scale features that local updaters under-exploit. Two enabling components—Hierarchical Semantic Cost Volumes (HSCV) for scale-aligned per-level correlations and Depth Prior Initialization (DPI) for a metrically calibrated warm start from monocular depth—address the remaining information pathways that prior VFM-based methods (FoundationStereo, DEFOM-Stereo, MGStereo, Stereo Anywhere) leave untapped within the update loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that widening the update interface from local to global (at linear cost) is a more effective use of VFM backbone capacity than simply scaling the encoder, yielding strong cross-domain generalization to underwater scenes without any domain-specific training data. The per-iteration latency of PALA being comparable to ConvGRU (3.50 ms vs. 3.63 ms) while providing full spatial context makes the technique practically attractive for real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the frozen DA3 backbone (94% of parameters) can be distilled for edge deployment, whether the handcrafted SIFT-based DPI can be replaced by a learned sparse matcher, and whether the PALA module transfers to other iterative refinement tasks beyond stereo.
