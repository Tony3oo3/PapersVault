## Problem

Current UMMs are assessed on spatial intelligence only through passive 2D multiple-choice QA, which can be gamed with statistical shortcuts and never tests whether a model can actively construct or transform 3D structures in pixel space. Moreover, standard image-similarity metrics (SSIM, CLIP-S) cannot detect when multi-view generations are locally plausible yet globally geometrically inconsistent as a single 3D scene. The paper asks whether models possess genuine 3D spatial intelligence by demanding active construction and volumetric verification rather than passive recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261487/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Models receive a target structure image plus a text instruction (e.g., 'delete the purple cube,' 'rotate the top prism 90° clockwise,' or 'generate the next assembly step') and must produce the corresponding edited or generated image; for multi-step tasks the output is an interleaved sequence of reasoning text and intermediate state images spanning up to 40+ assembly steps. Evaluation is performed via 3DGS-Eval: eight generated views are split into two disjoint subsets, each independently reconstructed as a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene, and the two reconstructions are compared on appearance (PSNR/SSIM), depth (mean absolute error), and geometry (Chamfer distance over sampled Gaussian points).

## Method

MindBlock is a benchmark of 2,839 structures (16,336 intermediate states) synthesized via the Genesis physics engine with 8 egocentric camera viewpoints per structure, covering six tasks spanning atomic deletion/completion, iterative forward assembly/reverse deconstruction, and local/global spatial equivariance. The key evaluation contribution is 3DGS-Eval, which reconstructs a volumetric scene from model-generated multi-view images using 3D Gaussian Splatting, exposing geometric contradictions that are invisible to purely 2D metrics. For multi-step evaluation, the authors perform supervised continual training on the BAGEL-zebra-CoT backbone, jointly supervising forward assembly and reverse deconstruction across four views per state transition to enforce viewpoint-invariant spatial reasoning. Diagnostic analyses compare CoT-augmented vs. direct generation (showing CoT acts as a symbolic scaffold that reduces Chamfer distance by 47.6%) and discriminative MCQ vs. constructive execution (revealing a 24.3% perception-execution gap in BAGEL), disentangling whether spatial consistency relies on textual scaffolding or native generative intuition.

## Results

On the MindBlock benchmark, Step1X-Edit achieves the best overall performance across both 2D and 3DGS-Eval metrics: CLIP-S 0.86, PSNR 15.20, SSIM 0.78, Depth Error 68.50, and Chamfer Distance 9.10 on Spatial Assembly, and CLIP-S 0.83, PSNR 13.80, SSIM 0.80, Depth Error 74.20, CD 9.30 on Spatial Structure. A critical 2D–3D consistency gap is exposed: Blip3-o-next attains a higher CLIP-S (0.81) than BAGEL (0.77) yet produces substantially worse volumetric reconstructions (Depth Error 83.20 vs. 75.97; CD 13.05 vs. 11.72), indicating that perceptual similarity does not guarantee geometric coherence. All models degrade on Spatial Structure relative to Spatial Assembly, confirming that viewpoint equivariance remains a major bottleneck. In multi-step assembly (Table 2), the trained MindBlock-Semantic variant leads with PSNR 20.4 and CD 5.9 in forward assembly, while CoT augmentation (UniCoT) reduces Chamfer Distance by 47.6% over direct BAGEL generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Step1X-Edit dominates both 2D and 3DGS-Eval metrics across all four task settings, achieving the lowest Chamfer Distance (9.10 Assembly, 9.30 Structure) and highest PSNR (15.20, 13.80), outperforming the next-best Ovis-U1 by roughly 2.7–3.6 dB in PSNR and 1.1–0.9 in CD. BAGEL is competitive on 3D metrics (CD 11.72 Assembly, 10.73 Structure) but trails Step1X-Edit by ~2.6 in CD; notably, Blip3-o-next scores higher CLIP-S than BAGEL (0.81 vs. 0.77) yet has worse Depth Error (83.20 vs. 75.97) and CD (13.05 vs. 11.72), exemplifying the 2D–3D consistency gap. ILLUME and Omni-Gen are the weakest performers, with Omni-Gen's Depth Error exceeding 108 on Assembly and 118 on Structure—nearly double that of Step1X-Edit. In multi-step assembly (Table 2), the trained MindBlock-Semantic variant surpasses both BAGEL-zebra-CoT (CD 11.8→13.2) and ThinkMorph (CD 9.6→10.7) in both forward and reverse phases, achieving CD 5.9 and 6.8 respectively. Finally, the CoT ablation (Table 3) shows UniCoT reducing Chamfer Distance from 12.4 to 6.5 (−47.6%) relative to BAGEL's direct generation, confirming that explicit symbolic reasoning acts as a scaffold for 3D structural integrity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of evaluating spatial intelligence via passive, discriminative VQA (as in PhyBlock, BrickGPT, and numerous MLLM spatial benchmarks) by reframing the task as active generative construction in pixel space. Methodologically, it builds on MVGBench's multi-view 3DGS reconstruction pipeline to introduce 3DGS-Eval, a volumetric consistency metric that goes beyond standard 2D image similarity. For multi-step evaluation, it extends the BAGEL unified multimodal backbone via continual training, positioning itself at the intersection of UMM research and robotic assembly planning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a fundamental limitation in current UMMs: high 2D perceptual similarity does not guarantee geometrically coherent 3D structure, and models that correctly *recognize* a spatial configuration often fail to *generate* it (a 24.3% perception-execution gap for BAGEL). The 3DGS-Eval protocol offers a reusable, model-agnostic diagnostic that the field can adopt to validate multi-view generative outputs beyond pixel-level metrics. Open questions include whether the perception-execution gap can be closed with geometry-aware training objectives, whether spatial drift scales predictably with structural complexity beyond 40 blocks, and whether the active-construction paradigm generalizes to articulated or deformable objects.
