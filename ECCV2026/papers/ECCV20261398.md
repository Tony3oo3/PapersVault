## Problem

Online egocentric procedure parsing must infer the current action step from a streaming first-person video using only past observations, but heavy ego-motion, transient occlusions, and high intra-class variability cause learned temporal models to over-segment and structurally collapse. Existing approaches depend heavily on annotated training data and learned transition dynamics, making them brittle under domain shift and impractical when retraining is infeasible. The paper asks whether procedural structure and frozen foundation model features alone—without any gradient update—can recover coherent, causally valid action sequences.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming egocentric video at 10–15 fps, processing only a sliding buffer of the most recent 2L frames (L=10–20) so that inference is strictly causal and bounded in memory. It emits segment-level predictions rather than per-frame labels: each detected boundary triggers a discrete action segment that is assigned a label via prototype matching, and a fixed-lag commitment strategy freezes predictions older than a few seconds. The final output is an ordered sequence of (action label, start, end) tuples that respects the induced task-graph transition constraints.

## Method

VidParse is a four-stage, training-free pipeline. First, Manipulation-Anchored Features (MAFs) are extracted by spatially averaging frozen DINOv2 ViT-L/14 patch tokens within a bounding region produced by a frozen hand-object detector, isolating interaction-relevant signal. Second, a Gaussian-tapered checkerboard kernel is convolved with a local cosine-similarity matrix over the MAF stream to produce a novelty score; online peak detection with a minimum-gap constraint emits action boundaries without any learned boundary predictor. Third, each segment is labeled by nearest-neighbor matching against a library of micro-prototypes (short temporal slices of agglomeratively clustered training sequences) using cosine distance. Fourth, a graph-constrained beam search decodes the full action sequence over an induced directed task graph, assigning infinite cost to transitions that violate procedural prerequisites, and a gap-rectification rule retroactively fills short background segments caused by occlusions. The key insight is that the semantic stability of frozen DINOv2 features makes classical signal-processing boundary detection sufficient, while hard graph constraints during decoding prevent the structural collapse that plagues purely local predictors.

## Results

On GTEA, VidParse (online, training-free) achieves 89.1% Accuracy, 87.4 Edit distance, and 80.5 F1@0.5, surpassing the strongest online baseline ProTAS (73.19% Acc, 54.87 F1@0.5) by 16 and 26 percentage points respectively. On EgoPER, it reaches 80.7% Acc, 88.7 Edit, and 77.6 F1@0.5, improving over ProTAS (65.50% Acc, 51.31 F1@0.25) by roughly 15–26 points across metrics. The method also achieves up to 5×–10× higher AUC on the proposed N-step transition metric for 5- and 7-step procedural dependencies, and runs at 8.6 FPS on CPU versus 4.5 FPS for the GPU-accelerated ProTAS baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest online baseline ProTAS [36], VidParse wins decisively on every metric: on GTEA it gains +15.9 Acc, +15.6 Edit, and +25.6 F1@0.5; on EgoPER it gains +15.2 Acc, +24.4 Edit, and +26.3 F1@0.5. Compared to the offline MS-TCN [34] upper bound, VidParse actually exceeds it on GTEA (89.1 vs 79.35 Acc) while remaining competitive on EgoPER (80.7 vs 87.52 Acc, though it leads on Edit at 88.7 vs 92.34 and F1@0.5 at 77.6 vs 86.12). The online variant of MS-TCN collapses dramatically (47.28 Acc on GTEA, 25.40 on EgoPER), highlighting how poorly standard temporal models transfer to the causal setting. On the proposed N-step transition metric, VidParse's AUC for 5-step and 7-step transitions is 5×–10× higher than ProTAS, indicating far better preservation of long-range procedural structure. Inference speed also favors VidParse: 8.6 FPS on CPU versus 4.5 FPS for ProTAS on GPU, with the parsing/decoding stage taking only 32.8 s per video on CPU versus 706 s on GPU for ProTAS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical Gaussian-tapered checkerboard kernel for self-similarity matrix boundary detection (originally developed for audio/video scene cuts by Foote and Cooper) and applies it to frozen DINOv2 patch features, effectively reviving a signal-processing technique within a modern foundation-model pipeline. It departs from the dominant online action segmentation paradigm (ProTAS, OnlineTAS, MATR) that learns temporal transition dynamics from annotated data, instead formulating parsing as MAP inference over an induced task graph with hard transition constraints enforced during beam search. The Manipulation-Anchored Feature construction, which spatially crops DINOv2 tokens to hand-object interaction regions, bridges the gap between generic visual features and the manipulation-centric nature of egocentric procedures, a design choice not present in prior OAS methods that operate on full-frame or CLS-token representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a completely gradient-free pipeline—combining frozen features, a classical novelty detector, and hard graph constraints—can match or exceed trained online baselines on standard segmentation metrics offers a practical alternative for settings where retraining is impractical or annotated data is scarce. However, the 'training-free' label is qualified: the task graph and micro-prototypes are still induced from training-sequence annotations, so the method is parameter-free rather than data-free, limiting true zero-shot generalization to unseen procedures. The introduced N-step transition metric is a useful addition for evaluating long-range procedural coherence, but its self-introduction and evaluation on only two datasets (GTEA, EgoPER) leave open whether the 5–10× gains over ProTAS generalize to more diverse or longer-horizon tasks. Key open questions include whether probabilistic (soft) task graphs can handle open-world or loosely structured procedures, and whether the segment-level granularity is sufficient for ultra-low-latency deployment.
