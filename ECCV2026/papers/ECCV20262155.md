## Problem

Existing depth foundation models, trained predominantly on uniformly lit data, degrade sharply in multi-illumination scenes where shadows, specular highlights, and exposure shifts distort local appearance cues and cause severe depth inconsistency. Direct adaptation is challenging because ground truth depth is scarce for multi-illumination datasets, and synthetic relighting introduces geometric distortions that undermine depth-related fine cues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262155/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image and produces a dense depth map at the same spatial resolution. During training, it consumes groups of N RGB images depicting the same static scene under different lighting conditions (e.g., sunlight, room light, torchlight) alongside conventional RGB-D pairs with ground truth depth. The depth prediction operates in a VAE latent space, with the final depth map decoded from the latent representation.

## Method

LumiDepth is built on a latent diffusion pipeline (Stable Diffusion v2 U-Net with x0-prediction, initialized from Marigold or Lotus checkpoints) and introduces two training modules. The Disagreement-Calibrated Probabilistic Pseudo Supervision (DCPS) module computes per-hypothesis cross-illumination disagreement scores, filters outliers via a median threshold, and samples pseudo labels using a temperature-scaled softmax over confidence weights, preserving supervision diversity while avoiding brittle single-choice selection. The Frequency-aware Consistency and Distillation (FaCD) module enforces low-frequency geometric agreement via a Gaussian-filtered L1 loss on decoded depth, while transferring high-frequency edge structure through a bidirectional stop-gradient distillation on depth gradients weighted by reliability, preventing the 'consistent but blurry' failure mode of naive consistency regularization. The total objective combines standard GT diffusion loss, pseudo-label reconstruction, and the two FaCD terms with fixed weights (λ1=0.5, λ2=0.5, λ3=0.1).

## Results

On the newly introduced ReMID benchmark, LumiDepth (1-step) achieves the best overall performance with MeanDV 0.030, AbsRel 0.085, and δ₁ 0.914, reducing depth variation by 30.2% and absolute relative error by 24.8% compared to the strongest generative baseline Lotus. On nighttime driving datasets, LumiDepth attains the lowest AbsRel on both NuScenes-Night (0.223) and RobotCar-Night (0.217), while also achieving the best average rank (2.0) across all three multi-illumination/nighttime benchmarks. On standard zero-shot depth benchmarks (NYUv2, KITTI, ETH3D, DIODE), LumiDepth remains competitive with Lotus and outperforms the direct self-training baseline, confirming that gains are not attributable to naive RGB-only adaptation alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest generative baseline Lotus [20], LumiDepth (1-step) wins on every ReMID metric: MeanDV drops from 0.043 to 0.030 (−30.2%), AbsRel from 0.113 to 0.085 (−24.8%), and δ₁ rises from 0.850 to 0.914 (+7.5%); on NuScenes-Night AbsRel improves from 0.280 to 0.223 (−20.3%). Compared to discriminative foundation models DAv2/DAv3, LumiDepth achieves substantially lower MeanDV (0.030 vs. 0.049–0.051) and lower AbsRel on ReMID (0.085 vs. 0.118–0.119), though DAv3 edges out on NuScenes-Night δ₁ (0.695 vs. 0.636). Among robust MDE methods, DA-AC [51] is the strongest competitor on NuScenes-Night δ₁ (0.719 vs. 0.636), but LumiDepth dominates on ReMID stability (MeanDV 0.030 vs. 0.047) and accuracy (AbsRel 0.085 vs. 0.117). On general zero-shot benchmarks (Table 3), LumiDepth matches or slightly beats Lotus on NYUv2 (0.052 vs. 0.054) and KITTI (0.079 vs. 0.085), while remaining within 0.001 on ETH3D and DIODE, confirming no degradation in uniform-lighting settings. The direct self-training ablation (Lotus-st) degrades on all general benchmarks (e.g., DIODE 0.278 vs. 0.229), showing that the DCPS+FaCD design—not merely additional RGB-only data—drives the improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

LumiDepth builds directly on latent diffusion-based depth estimators (Marigold, Lotus) initialized from Stable Diffusion v2, adopting their x0-prediction parameterization and VAE latent space. It departs from prior robust MDE methods (DA-AC, D4RD, WeatherDepth) that require a clean reference image and synthetic degradations, instead operating on grouped same-geometry RGB images with no canonical lighting condition. The two core modules—DCPS (disagreement-calibrated probabilistic pseudo-labeling) and FaCD (frequency-decomposed consistency with bidirectional stop-gradient distillation)—are novel combinations: DCPS replaces deterministic aggregation with confidence-weighted sampling, while FaCD explicitly separates low-frequency geometric alignment from high-frequency edge transfer, a decomposition not previously applied to cross-illumination depth consistency. The work also extends the synthetic-only multi-illumination MDE study of Liang et al. [35] to real-world, label-free settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of ReMID, a real-world multi-illumination RGB-D benchmark with 1000 annotated samples across 100 scenes, fills a concrete gap in the depth estimation evaluation landscape and provides the community with stability metrics (MeanDV, MaxPIV) that go beyond standard accuracy measures. The label-free training paradigm is practically significant because multi-illumination RGB-D data is inherently scarce, and the demonstrated generalization to non-illumination corruptions (weather, sensor noise via RoboDepth) suggests the DCPS+FaCD framework could serve as a general consistency-training recipe for any appearance-shift setting with limited depth supervision. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes (the current formulation assumes static geometry), how it scales to larger illumination groups, and whether the frequency decomposition thresholds generalize across scene scales and resolutions without manual tuning.
