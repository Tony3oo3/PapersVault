## Problem

Prior DINO-based monocular depth estimation methods uniformly sample a fixed set of intermediate ViT layers for multi-scale features, implicitly assuming 3D geometric knowledge is evenly distributed across depth. This paper shows that assumption is wrong: deeper DINOv3 layers encode substantially richer and more explicit 3D information, yet receive no special treatment in standard pipelines. The gap is a principled mechanism to exploit this nonuniform layer-wise distribution of geometric knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (640×480 for indoor NYU/SUN scenes, 1241×376 for outdoor KITTI/Argoverse) and produces a pixel-wise depth map. Internally, the DINOv3 ViT backbone processes the image into a token grid at 1/16 spatial resolution, and the final depth prediction is a softmax-weighted sum of per-level depth maps, where weights are derived from recomposed class tokens rather than a single decoder output.

## Method

The LFR module sits between the frozen DINOv3 backbone and a DPT decoder. It treats the final-layer image and class tokens as a geometric anchor, then selects K=4 intermediate layers whose class tokens have the lowest average cosine similarity to the last-layer class token (minimal-similarity criterion), maximizing semantic complementarity. Each selected auxiliary feature is concatenated channel-wise with the last-layer feature and passed through a compact bottleneck adapter (linear down-projection → nonlinearity → linear up-projection) with a tanh-gated residual connection to produce recomposed features. The DPT decoder is modified so that each recomposed level independently predicts a depth map, and the final output is a weighted sum where weights come from a linear projection of the recomposed class tokens followed by softmax. Training uses a scale-invariant log loss combined with a hierarchical normalization loss that enforces multi-scale depth consistency within patches of sizes 1, 4, and 8.

## Results

On NYU Depth v2, DINOv3-L+LFR achieves AbsRel 0.057, RMSE 0.206, log10 0.025, and δ1 0.984, improving over the DINOv3-L baseline (AbsRel 0.060, RMSE 0.212) and matching or exceeding DAv1/DAv2 (AbsRel 0.056, RMSE 0.206). On KITTI, the method attains the best AbsRel (0.043), RMSE (1.711), and δ1 (0.988) among all compared methods, outperforming DAR-L (AbsRel 0.044, RMSE 1.799) and DAv2 (AbsRel 0.045, RMSE 1.861). Zero-shot transfer to SUN RGB-D yields AbsRel 0.099 and δ1 0.907, and to Argoverse yields AbsRel 0.204 and δ1 0.730, both surpassing prior methods without fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NYU Depth v2, DINOv3-L+LFR (AbsRel 0.057, RMSE 0.206, δ1 0.984) is within 0.001 of DAv1/DAv2 (AbsRel 0.056, RMSE 0.206, δ1 0.984) and slightly trails DAR-L (AbsRel 0.056, RMSE 0.205) on AbsRel/RMSE, but achieves a higher δ1 (0.984 vs. 0.982) and avoids the 2B-parameter autoregressive inference cost. On KITTI, the method clearly wins: AbsRel 0.043 and RMSE 1.711 beat DAR-L (0.044, 1.799), DAv2 (0.045, 1.861), and all generative baselines (EcoDepth 0.048, DDP 0.050). Against older supervised methods (DORN, AdaBins, DPT, PixelFormer, MIM), the improvement is substantial—AbsRel drops from 0.09–0.115 to 0.057 on NYU and from 0.050–0.072 to 0.043 on KITTI. For zero-shot transfer, DINOv3-L+LFR outperforms all listed baselines on both SUN RGB-D (AbsRel 0.099 vs. best prior 0.101 for DINOv3-L, 0.112 for DAR-L) and Argoverse (AbsRel 0.204 vs. 0.219 for DINOv3-L, 0.277 for P3Depth), confirming that the LFR module extracts more transferable geometric priors from the backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DINOv3 vision foundation model and the DPT decoder architecture, departing from the standard practice of uniformly sampling intermediate transformer layers for multi-scale dense prediction (as in Depth Anything, VGGT, and prior DPT-based MDE methods). The key conceptual departure is a systematic layer-wise analysis showing that 3D geometric knowledge in DINOv3 is non-uniformly distributed, with deeper layers encoding richer depth-predictive information. Motivated by this, the authors propose the LFR module, which treats the final layer as a geometric anchor and adaptively selects complementary intermediate layers via a minimal-similarity criterion, fusing them through lightweight linear adapters rather than the conventional fixed-interval sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The layer-wise analysis of DINOv3 provides the first empirical evidence for how 3D geometric knowledge is organized across transformer depth, offering a principled basis for designing transfer strategies beyond ad-hoc uniform sampling. The LFR module is lightweight, plug-and-play, and requires no backbone modification, making it easily adoptable in existing DINOv3-based pipelines. However, the performance gains over the DINOv3+DPT baseline are modest (e.g., 0.046→0.043 AbsRel on KITTI), and the differences between selection strategies (Table 6) are small, leaving open whether the minimal-similarity criterion is truly optimal or whether the gains would hold for other VFMs or 3D tasks. The authors note future work on extending LFR to other dense prediction problems, but the analysis is limited to DINOv3-L on two training datasets.
