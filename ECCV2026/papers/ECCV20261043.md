## Problem

Correspondence-based category-level pose estimation suffers from two structural weaknesses: prior-free methods internalize the canonical frame in network weights, biasing predictions toward category-typical orientations and limiting cross-category generalization, while prior-based methods follow a serial deform-then-align pipeline where underconstrained canonical completion corrupts correspondences and cascades errors into pose. The paper argues that deformation and alignment are both fundamentally correspondence problems and should be solved jointly rather than in separate stages.

## Input / Output

The method takes a segmented RGB-D frame (cropped to 224×224 RGB and 1024 back-projected 3D points) together with a 512-point category mean-shape prior as an explicit canonical reference. It produces three outputs in a single forward pass: per-point NOCS coordinates for the visible observation, a displacement field that deforms the prior into a full canonical instance, and a similarity transform (R, t, s) regressed from the induced correspondences. The prior is a fixed point cloud per category, not a parametric CAD model, and the network weights are shared across categories.

## Method

PriorPose uses a reference-guided seeded transformer that embeds observation tokens (PointNet++ geometry + DINOv2 image features + coordinate embeddings) and prior tokens (learnable embeddings + coordinate embeddings) into a shared 256-d space, then fuses them through a compact pool of 160 FPS-selected seeds with within-stream kNN gating and a parameter-free geometry-quality bias to stabilize attention under sparse or noisy depth. From the fused features, two complementary heads jointly predict a per-point NOCS field and a canonical deformation of the prior, while a deep pose head regresses (R, t, s) from the induced correspondences. Training couples these outputs via a two-part shape-consistency objective: a canonical-space one-sided Chamfer loss tying the filtered NOCS field to the reconstructed instance, and a camera-space one-sided Chamfer loss requiring the pose-placed reconstruction to agree with observed geometry, alongside standard NOCS regression, symmetry-aware pose, and Chamfer reconstruction losses. This joint formulation keeps the canonical reference external to the parameters (enabling category-agnostic weights) and avoids the error cascades inherent in serial deform-then-align pipelines.

## Results

On the standard NOCS benchmarks, PriorPose achieves 61.8 mAP at 5°/2cm and 68.2 at 5°/5cm on REAL275, and 81.1 / 85.0 on CAMERA25, setting new state-of-the-art on strict pose thresholds. On HouseCat6D (stronger occlusion and intra-class variation), it reaches 26.5 / 27.3 mAP at 5°/2cm and 5°/5cm. On the larger-category benchmarks Omni6DPose and PACE, PriorPose attains 46.3 / 26.4 IoU25 / IoU50 and 75.1 / 35.5 respectively, outperforming the strongest baselines by 3.8 and 5.7 IoU50 points. Zero-shot transfer to Wild6D yields 43.1 mAP at 5°/2cm, improving over AG-Pose by +1.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest prior-free baseline SpotPose [29], PriorPose gains +2.1 and +3.4 mAP at 5°/2cm and 5°/5cm on REAL275, and +0.7 and +1.2 on CAMERA25, while matching or slightly exceeding its IoU50/IoU75 scores. Against the best prior-based method GCE-Pose [17], PriorPose improves by +4.8 and +3.1 mAP at 5°/2cm and 5°/5cm on REAL275 (GCE-Pose does not report CAMERA25). On HouseCat6D, PriorPose outperforms SpotPose by +2.7/+2.8 and GCE-Pose by +1.7/+1.6 at the strict 5° thresholds, confirming that the joint deformation-alignment formulation helps under heavy occlusion. On the larger-category benchmarks, PriorPose beats AG-Pose [22] by +3.8 IoU50 on Omni6DPose and +7.4 IoU50 on PACE, and surpasses the benchmark-specific baselines GenPose++ [36] and CPPF++ [35] on both IoU and strict pose metrics. In the zero-shot Wild6D transfer, PriorPose edges out AG-Pose by +1.2 IoU50 and +1.3 mAP at 5°/2cm, suggesting that the explicit prior reference stabilizes correspondences under domain shift. PriorPose does not report a direct comparison with SpherePose [28] on HouseCat6D, but on REAL275 it leads SpherePose by +3.6 mAP at 5°/2cm.

## Relation to Prior Work

PriorPose sits at the intersection of two established lines in category-level pose estimation: prior-free correspondence methods (NOCS, AG-Pose, SpotPose) that regress canonical coordinates directly from local features, and prior-based deform-then-align pipelines (SPD, SGPA, DPDN, GCE-Pose) that first complete a canonical shape and then align. The paper's core departure is reframing deformation and alignment as a single joint problem in a shared feature space rather than a serial pipeline, using an explicit mean-shape prior as a reference token set fused with the partial observation via a seeded transformer (architecturally inspired by SeedFormer's patch-seed mechanism). The shape-consistency losses in canonical and camera space are a novel coupling that ties the NOCS field, the deformed prior, and the predicted pose together, going beyond the standard NOCS regression and Chamfer reconstruction objectives used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By keeping the category prior explicit and jointly optimizing deformation with alignment, PriorPose addresses two concrete failure modes—canonical-orientation memorization in prior-free weights and error cascades in serial deform-then-align pipelines—that have limited generalization under shape variation and domain shift. The category-agnostic weight sharing enabled by the explicit prior is practically important for multi-category deployment in robotics and AR/VR. However, the method still requires a per-category mean-shape prior, remains in the RGB-D correspondence regime, and the evaluated categories are relatively small (25–275); whether the joint formulation scales to very large or open-vocabulary categories, or to monocular-only settings, remains open. The moderate but consistent gains across six benchmarks suggest the approach is robust, yet the absolute margins over the strongest prior-free baselines (SpotPose, SpherePose) are modest, leaving room for further improvement.
