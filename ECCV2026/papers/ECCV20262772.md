## Problem

Deployed 3D Gaussian Splatting assets often suffer localized geometric degradation (cloudy floaters, structural collapse) in regions with missing or sparse viewing angles. Existing repair strategies are inadequate: global retraining is prohibitively expensive for large scenes, naive fine-tuning on new repair images triggers catastrophic forgetting that destroys the unobserved background, and geometric stitching of a sub-model introduces Z-fighting and boundary artifacts. The paper asks how to efficiently integrate a small set of newly captured local views into a pre-existing 3DGS PLY asset without access to the original training data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained 3DGS PLY file (the degraded base model), a small set of newly captured repair images with camera poses preregistered into the global coordinate system, and sparse SfM tie points (e.g., from COLMAP) associated with those new images to anchor the adaptive depth prior. It produces a refined PLY model in which the targeted local region is geometrically completed while all frozen background Gaussian parameters remain bit-identical to the input. Notably, no historical Kept training images are required, and the repair runs for only 3,000 iterations (versus 30,000 for a full retrain), completing in 2–4 minutes on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

Flash-Refine computes a point-wise binary mask over every Gaussian in the base PLY by projecting points into the new camera frustums, requiring multi-view consensus (≥τ_obs cameras), and bounding the active depth shell via the median + γ·MAD of SfM tie-point depths (Adaptive Depth-Bounded Prior). During optimization, a Masked Gradient Lock multiplies all parameter gradients by the mask, permanently freezing background Gaussians and preventing any parameter drift. Because 3DGS densification (cloning/splitting) is triggered by positional gradient magnitude, zeroing gradients on frozen points forces all new parameter allocation into the active region—termed Local Parameter Allocation—allowing native densification to rapidly reconstruct missing geometry from peripheral seed points. An Anti-Deletion Shield zeroes the 2D radii accumulator of frozen points before each pruning step to prevent close-up repair views from misclassifying background Gaussians as floaters, and the mask state is dynamically inherited by offspring during densification so the active region can grow without manual retuning.

## Results

Flash-Refine achieves the best Dropped and Kept PSNR among all practical repair methods across all four scenes (Bicycle, Counter, Bedroom, DrJohnson), completing repairs in 2–4 minutes on an RTX 4090 versus ~40 minutes for full global retraining. On the Bedroom scene, it reaches 27.17 dB Dropped PSNR (slightly exceeding the 27.05 dB Upper Bound) while preserving 28.71 dB Kept PSNR, compared to 24.30 dB for Naive Fine-Tuning. On the Counter stress case, Flash-Refine attains 29.66 dB Dropped PSNR versus 28.93 dB (Naive FT) and 28.63 dB (Stitching), with Kept PSNR of 27.47 dB versus 20.21 dB and 26.78 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Bedroom scene (the only scene where all baselines are reported), Flash-Refine outperforms SplaTAM by 0.75 dB on Dropped PSNR (27.17 vs. 26.42) and by a large 3.62 dB on Kept PSNR (28.71 vs. 25.09), while running 5× faster (~2 min vs. ~10 min). Against Inpaint360GS, Flash-Refine edges out on Dropped PSNR (27.17 vs. 27.10) but leads decisively on Kept PSNR (28.71 vs. 25.67) and All PSNR (28.29 vs. 26.14), completing the repair in ~2 min versus ~30 min. Across all four scenes, Flash-Refine also beats the two in-house practical baselines—Naive Fine-Tuning and Geometric Stitching—on both Dropped and Kept PSNR in every scene, with the largest Kept-PSNR margin on Counter (27.47 vs. 20.21 for Naive FT, a 7.26 dB gap) where catastrophic forgetting is most severe. The method approaches or matches the global-retraining Upper Bound on Dropped PSNR (e.g., 27.17 vs. 27.05 on Bedroom) while preserving Kept views far better than any practical alternative.

## Relation to Prior Work

Flash-Refine builds directly on the native 3DGS optimization pipeline (Kerbl et al., 2023), repurposing its gradient-driven densification and pruning mechanisms as the repair engine rather than introducing a new renderer or representation. It departs from global-retraining and naive fine-tuning paradigms by introducing a Point-Wise Consensus Mask with an Adaptive Depth-Bounded Prior to define a bounded active set, then enforcing zero-gradient locking on all background Gaussians. Conceptually, it sits between SLAM-based incremental mapping (SplaTAM, GS-SLAM), which prioritizes tracking over fidelity, and 3D inpainting methods (Inpaint360GS, SPIn-NeRF), which hallucinate content via diffusion models; Flash-Refine instead performs physically grounded geometric completion from actual new photographs. The key departure from continual-learning approaches like CL-Splats is the elimination of historical replay and change detection, operating solely on a deployed PLY asset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15× speedup (2–4 min vs. ~40 min) for local repair makes 3DGS assets practically maintainable in digital-twin, VR, and cultural-heritage pipelines where global retraining is infeasible. The 'Local Parameter Allocation' observation—that gradient gating on background points naturally concentrates native densification in the active region without any new optimizer—is a clean, reusable insight for any gradient-driven explicit representation. However, the Occlusion Bleeding limitation (newly split active Gaussians altering unobserved-view renderings) and the photometric-consistency assumption constrain real-world deployment; open questions include depth/visibility regularization to prevent active-region expansion into kept-view frustums, per-camera appearance compensation, and robustness to pose noise in uncontrolled capture settings.
