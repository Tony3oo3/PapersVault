## Problem

Existing diffusion-based dehazing methods reconstruct clean images from pure Gaussian noise, ignoring the structured, spatially varying physics of haze formation described by the Atmospheric Scattering Model (ASM). This misalignment causes stochastic deviations from the original scene and limits restoration fidelity, particularly under heavy haze where most scene information is lost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260512/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single hazy RGB image as input and produces a dehazed RGB image as output. HNDiff operates as a plug-and-play prior generator in the latent space of an existing dehazing backbone (e.g., FocalNet, ConvIR, SGDN), requiring only paired hazy-clean training data without explicit transmission maps, scattering coefficients, or depth maps. During training, 256×256 patches are used, and the diffusion process requires only T=4 steps.

## Method

HNDiff redefines the diffusion forward process as a joint haze-noise diffusion that embeds the ASM as an inductive bias: at each step, the image is degraded by both an ASM-based haze shift (attenuating scene radiance and blending atmospheric light) and Gaussian noise, with a haze-aware noise scheduler (HANS) that assigns higher noise to hazier pixels (to trigger generative reconstruction) and lower noise to clearer pixels (to preserve detail). The reverse process is a physically consistent dehazing-denoising step that iteratively removes both haze and noise using two dedicated U-Net estimators (a haze estimator for the scattering-depth product and a noise estimator for atmospheric noise), derived via a reparameterization trick that eliminates the need for explicit transmission maps. For practical deployment, Latent HNDiff performs the diffusion in an encoded latent space and injects the recovered clean priors into a dehazing backbone through a Feature Gating Module (FGM) that applies learned per-channel affine gating to multi-scale features, enabling seamless integration with diverse architectures.

## Results

HNDiff consistently improves three dehazing backbones (FocalNet, ConvIR, SGDN) across six benchmark datasets, yielding average PSNR gains of +0.54 dB (NH-HAZE), +0.87 dB (O-HAZE), +0.41 dB (Dense-HAZE), +0.40 dB (RW²AH), +0.47 dB (SOTS-Indoor), and +0.72 dB (SOTS-Outdoor), for an overall average of +0.57 dB PSNR and +0.009 SSIM. On the real-world RTTS benchmark, RIDCP+HNDiff achieves the best no-reference scores: 0.417 FADE, 5.08 NIMA, and 16.09 BRISQUE, outperforming all compared methods. The best individual results include ConvIR+HNDiff at 21.23 dB PSNR on NH-HAZE and FocalNet+HNDiff at 26.32 dB PSNR on O-HAZE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HNDiff is evaluated as a plug-and-play prior generator integrated into three modern dehazing backbones (FocalNet, ConvIR, SGDN) and one codebook-based method (RIDCP), and compared against seven standalone baselines (MSBDN, FFA-Net, Dehamer, MB-Taylor, DAD, PSD, RIDCP). Every HNDiff-enhanced model outperforms its own baseline on all six paired datasets, with the largest gains on O-HAZE (+0.87 dB avg) and SOTS-Outdoor (+0.72 dB avg), indicating the haze-aware diffusion prior is most beneficial under dense or spatially varying haze. Against standalone methods, ConvIR+HNDiff achieves the best PSNR on NH-HAZE (21.23 dB vs. 20.66 for Dehamer and 20.65 for ConvIR alone), while FocalNet+HNDiff leads on O-HAZE (26.32 dB vs. 25.46 for FocalNet and 25.11 for Dehamer). On the no-reference RTTS benchmark, RIDCP+HNDiff substantially outperforms all competitors: it reduces FADE from 0.920 (PSD) to 0.417, increases NIMA from 4.43 (RIDCP) to 5.08, and lowers BRISQUE from 18.78 (RIDCP) to 16.09, confirming superior perceptual quality on real-world hazy images. The method also outperforms a parameter-matched enlarged FocalNet variant (FocalNet* with 8.28M params, 20.51 dB) by 0.38 dB using fewer parameters (7.82M) and lower FLOPs (36.38G vs. 64.05G), showing that the physics-informed diffusion prior is more effective than simply scaling network capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

HNDiff extends the degradation-aware diffusion paradigm established by BlurDM (He et al., 2025) and Residual Denoising Diffusion (Liu et al., 2024) to the dehazing domain, but departs from both by embedding the Atmospheric Scattering Model (Narasimhan & Nayar, 2003) directly as an inductive bias in the forward process rather than treating degradation as a generic residual. Unlike prior diffusion-based dehazing methods (Yang et al., Wang et al., Liu et al.) that initialize from pure Gaussian noise, HNDiff's forward process jointly adds structured haze (via ASM) and adaptive noise, with the Haze-Aware Noise Scheduler (HANS) modulating noise injection by local haze density—a design absent from both conventional DDPM and recent physics-guided dehazing diffusion (Zhou et al., 2025). The latent-space formulation and Feature Gating Module follow the LDM (Rombach et al., 2022) and BlurDM training scheme, positioning HNDiff as a plug-and-play prior generator rather than a standalone restoration model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine conceptual gap: conventional diffusion models assume isotropic Gaussian corruption, while haze is structured, spatially varying, and governed by a known physical model. By aligning the diffusion trajectory with ASM, HNDiff provides a principled mechanism for allocating generative capacity where information is most lost (heavy haze) versus preserving fidelity where it is retained (light haze). The main limitation is that the framework is tightly coupled to the ASM and cannot transfer to other degradations (blur, rain, low-light) without redesigning the forward process. Open questions include whether the haze-aware scheduling principle generalizes to other spatially-varying degradations and whether the modest 4-step diffusion budget can be further reduced without quality loss.
