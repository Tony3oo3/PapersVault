## Problem

Current MLLMs are trained in a direct input-to-answer paradigm that provides no supervision over intermediate visual reasoning steps, leading to shortcut learning and vulnerability to hallucination on multi-step visual queries. Existing visual-CoT datasets are small, domain-specific, or collapse reasoning into single-step QA pairs, and they predominantly offer 2D annotations with limited supervision for ordinal-depth and occlusion-aware spatial reasoning. This paper addresses these gaps by introducing a large-scale, multi-domain dataset with explicit multi-round, RoI-grounded, depth-augmented reasoning traces.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a textual query, then autoregressively generates a sequence of T reasoning steps where each step consists of a textual rationale snippet and a normalized bounding box (x₁, y₁, x₂, y₂ ∈ [0,1]⁴) specifying the next region of interest. At each step, the original image is cropped to the predicted RoI and the cropped visual features are appended to the context, creating an iterative zoom-and-verify loop. For VisReason-Pro samples, ordinal-depth cues derived from monocular depth estimation and semantic segmentation signals are additionally available to ground spatial relations such as 'in front of' or 'behind'.

## Method

The dataset is constructed by first enriching each image with pseudo-depth (monocular depth estimation) and semantic segmentation cues, then prompting GPT-4.1-Nano (or GPT-4.1-Mini for the Pro subset) to generate multi-round visual CoT traces that follow a global-to-local workflow: scene summary → predicted RoI → rationale, with iterative zoom-and-verify refinement (≤3 rounds for VisReason, ≤4 for Pro) that terminates when the RoI area is ≤2× the ground-truth area. An adaptive zoom-in strategy skips iterative cropping for objects occupying >30% of the image, providing a single detailed step instead. Training uses supervised fine-tuning with LoRA on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, where the model is trained end-to-end to produce both textual reasoning tokens and serialized bounding-box coordinates in a standard autoregressive negative log-likelihood objective. The VisReason-Pro subset further incorporates depth-augmented spatial QA pairs whose relations jointly involve 2D layout and ordinal depth, enabling the model to learn relative-depth reasoning from single-view cues without claiming metric 3D reconstruction.

## Results

On the Visual-CoT benchmark (11 test sets across 4 domains), VISREASON-Pro-7B achieves the highest sample-size-weighted average score of 0.802, surpassing its Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone (0.770) and all compared open-source MLLMs. The largest gains appear on fine-grained recognition (Birds-200-2011: 0.681 → 0.831) and spatial relation reasoning (Open Images: 0.498 → 0.805), directly reflecting the RoI-grounded, global-to-local supervision. On the VISREASON-PRO held-out suite, the model also leads in RoI localization (IoU@0.5 = 0.34 vs. 0.29 for the next-best baseline) and ordinal-depth grounding (Grounded Ratio = 0.276 vs. 0.039 for LLaVA-NeXT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISREASON-Pro-7B (avg 0.802) outperforms all six baselines on the overall Visual-CoT benchmark. Against its own backbone Qwen2.5-VL-7B (0.770), the gain is +3.2 points on average, driven by +15.0 points on fine-grained recognition (0.681→0.831) and +11.2 points on spatial relation reasoning (0.598→0.710 domain avg). Compared to the strongest prior CoT method CoF-SFT-7B (0.737), VISREASON-Pro-7B leads by +6.5 points, with the largest margin on spatial reasoning (+9.9 points). Against InternVL-2.5-8B (0.738) and LLaVA-NeXT-8B (0.705), the advantage is +6.4 and +9.7 points respectively. The one domain where VISREASON-Pro-7B trails its Qwen backbone is Doc/Text (0.890 vs. 0.918), but the authors show this is a format-mismatch artifact: under a matched direct-QA prompt the same checkpoint recovers to 0.916, essentially matching the baseline. On the VISREASON-PRO held-out detection/grounding suite, VISREASON-Pro-7B achieves IoU@0.5 of 0.34 (vs. 0.29 LLaVA-NeXT, 0.27 VISREASON, 0.14 MiniGPTv2) and a Grounded Ratio of 0.276 (vs. 0.039 LLaVA-NeXT, 0.035 Qwen2.5-VL), confirming that depth-informed process supervision yields substantially better RoI localization and ordinal-depth alignment than any compared baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Visual-CoT line of work [48] by scaling up from a single-step or limited multi-round visual CoT dataset to a 489K-example corpus with explicit multi-round RoI-grounded supervision and depth-augmented spatial annotations. It departs from prior dataset-oriented efforts (V*, VPD, DualFocus, Chain-ofSpot, CogCoM, VoCoT) that the authors identify as suffering from limited scale, insufficient multi-round stepwise supervision, and predominantly 2D annotations lacking ordinal-depth cues. The training pipeline is standard SFT with LoRA on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, positioning the contribution squarely in the dataset-and-benchmark space rather than introducing a new model architecture or training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VisReason provides a much-needed large-scale, multi-domain resource for training MLLMs in interpretable, spatially grounded visual reasoning, and the VisReason-Pro subset with monocular-depth-derived ordinal cues opens a path toward depth-aware spatial reasoning without requiring metric 3D ground truth. Key open questions include the cascading localization failure (no zoom-out or backtracking mechanism), the format mismatch between verbose multi-round CoT inference and standard short-answer QA protocols, and whether the pseudo-depth supervision generalizes beyond the GQA-derived subset. The paper also notes that RL-based cropping policies remain an unexplored but promising direction, and that the current SFT approach provides useful trajectory supervision for future agentic or RL training.
