## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models render explicit prompts (surface attributes like color, shape, texture) with high fidelity but produce counter-factual images when given implicit prompts that require deep world knowledge spanning natural sciences, cultural commonsense, and spatio-temporal reasoning. The paper traces this failure to a fundamental dislocation in training data distributions: comprehensive world knowledge resides in text-only corpora, while text-image pairs are restricted to superficial visual descriptions, leaving the diffusion model's latent knowledge structures chaotic and unaligned.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260600/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired text prompts—an implicit prompt requiring world-knowledge reasoning and its corresponding explicit prompt that directly describes the visual content—along with a set of failure samples generated from explicit prompt synthesis. No image pairs are required for training; the approach is purely text-driven. It is applied as LoRA fine-tuning on the attention layers of pre-trained diffusion backbones (FLUX.1-dev, Qwen-Image) and produces standard RGB images that are factually consistent with the world knowledge encoded in the implicit prompt.

## Method

ERUDIFF refactors the diffusion model's internal knowledge through two complementary mechanisms. First, Diffusion Knowledge Distribution Matching (DK-DM) minimizes the KL divergence between the image distribution generated from an implicit prompt and that from its explicit anchor, computed via a stop-gradient pseudo-MSE loss over full timesteps (not just the final denoising step), with gradient truncation to only the last denoising step to control memory. To prevent catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge, an anti-forgetting consolidation term re-anchors the model on explicit and foundational (noun-phrase) sub-prompts at lower frequency (probabilities 0.8/0.1/0.1), and a timestep-aware curriculum learning schedule with exponential decay (λ=0.1) prioritizes early denoising steps where prompt-dependency is strongest. Second, Negative-Only Reinforcement Learning (NO-RL), a KTO variant operating exclusively on failure samples from explicit prompt synthesis, corrects residual rendering biases without requiring paired preference data. The two losses are optimized via independent backpropagation steps with exponential moving average normalization (decay 0.99) to balance their different scales.

## Results

On the WISE benchmark, FLUX.1-dev+ERUDIFF achieves an Overall WiScore of 0.64, a +0.14 absolute gain over the base FLUX.1-dev (0.50) and surpassing Qwen-Image (0.62), which leverages a multimodal LLM encoder. On SCIENCE-T2I, FLUX.1-dev+ERUDIFF reaches 33.70/34.54 SciScore (S/C splits), outperforming SFT (28.64/30.01), OFT (30.95/32.31), GRPO (31.68/32.19), and GPT-4o prompt rewriting (32.90/33.19). When applied to Qwen-Image, ERUDIFF further pushes scores to 35.43/38.48, the best among all evaluated configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On WISE, ERUDIFF (applied to FLUX.1-dev) achieves 0.64 Overall WiScore, outperforming all pure diffusion baselines (FLUX.1-dev 0.50, SD 3.5-large 0.46, PixArt-α 0.47, SD XL 0.43) and the MLLM+diffusion model Qwen-Image (0.62). It also surpasses unified multimodal models Janus-Pro-7B (0.35), Emu3 (0.39), BLIP3o-8B (0.52), and BAGEL (0.52), though it trails BAGEL+CoT (0.70), which uses chain-of-thought reasoning. On SCIENCE-T2I, ERUDIFF outperforms SFT (+5.06/+4.53), OFT (+2.75/+2.23), GRPO (+2.02/+2.35), and GPT-4o prompt rewriting (+0.80/+1.35) on the FLUX.1-dev backbone. When applied to Qwen-Image, ERUDIFF (35.43/38.48) also beats Qwen-Image+GPT-4o prompt rewriting (34.52/37.86), demonstrating that internal knowledge refactoring surpasses external prompt augmentation even when the base model already incorporates a multimodal LLM encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

ERUDIFF is directly inspired by Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD), repurposing its KL-divergence-based distribution alignment from the single/few-step distillation setting to a knowledge-refactoring problem where the 'student' and 'teacher' are the same diffusion model conditioned on implicit vs. explicit prompts. For the correction stage, it adapts KTO (Kahneman-Tversky Optimization) into a negative-only variant that avoids the need for paired preference data. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant paradigm of importing world knowledge via LLM-based prompt rewriting or unified multimodal architectures, instead arguing that the knowledge already resides latently within the diffusion model and can be reorganized through text-only distribution matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that world-knowledge injection into T2I models can be achieved with text-only corpora (no expensive text-image pairs or domain-specific reward models), making the approach far more scalable than SFT or RL-based alternatives. The anti-forgetting consolidation mechanism addresses a real deployment concern: naive knowledge refactoring erodes pre-trained capabilities, a failure mode that would limit adoption. Open questions include whether the 10K-sample Knowledge-10K dataset is sufficient for broader generalization, whether the approach extends to multi-hop or compositional world-knowledge reasoning, and whether the gains persist as base models continue to scale with integrated MLLM encoders.
