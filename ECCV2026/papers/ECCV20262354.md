## Problem

State-of-the-art feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (DUSt3R, VGGT, MASt3R) are trained almost exclusively on pinhole-camera data, causing severe geometric degradation on wide-angle imagery from fisheye or panoramic sensors. Existing work either undistorts images before reconstruction (losing peripheral information and breaking end-to-end pipelines) or learns ray representations only in monocular settings, leaving the multi-view, camera-agnostic reconstruction problem unsolved.

## Input / Output

CAM3R consumes uncalibrated, unposed RGB image pairs (or unordered collections for multi-view) from any combination of pinhole, fisheye, and panoramic lenses, resized to 512 px on the long edge. Rather than regressing Cartesian (X,Y,Z) pointmaps, the network outputs per-pixel unit ray directions (parameterized as Spherical Harmonics coefficients up to degree L), per-pixel radial distances with confidence maps, and a relative rigid pose (rotation in SO(3) plus unit translation direction with a separate scale factor). For multi-view input, the Ray-Aware Global Alignment fuses all pairwise predictions into a single globally consistent 3D point cloud and camera trajectory.

## Method

The architecture bifurcates into a shared Ray Module (geometric encoder + Transformer Ray Encoder producing SH coefficients, inverted to a continuous ray field) and a Cross-view Module (Siamese ViT encoder feeding a dual transformer decoder with cross-attention, topped by DPT heads for radial distance/confidence and a relative pose head). The key design choice is decoupling ray direction from radial distance, making the representation projection-invariant: radial distance is well-defined for any lens geometry, unlike Cartesian depth. Training uses an asymmetric angular loss (quantile regression penalizing angular underestimation) for rays, scale-normalized MSE for local pointmaps, and geodesic-rotation plus scale-anchored MSE for relative pose, under a two-phase curriculum that first trains on homogeneous camera pairs then introduces heterogeneous pairs. For multi-view reconstruction, a Ray-Aware Global Alignment prunes the scene graph via symmetric pose consistency and mutual-nearest-neighbor overlap checks, then alternately optimizes camera poses and per-image scales against frozen consensus ray-and-distance fields, replacing DUSt3R's pinhole-assumed bundle adjustment.

## Results

On two-view relative pose estimation, CAM3R achieves 97.7% RRA@15 and 94.3% RTA@15 on 2D3DS (panoramic), 96.0%/91.0% on 360Loc, and 99.0%/95.0% on ADT (fisheye), substantially outperforming all baselines on wide-angle imagery. On the zero-shot CO3Dv2 benchmark, CAM3R reaches 88.2% RTA@15, the highest among all methods. On pinhole-only MegaDepth, CAM3R (96.8% RRA@15, 94.2% RTA@15) is competitive with π³ (99.8%/93.3%) and VGGT (98.0%/88.2%). In multi-view settings, CAM3R with Ray-Aware Global Alignment achieves 73.5% mAA@30 on 2D3DS and 82.6% on 360Loc, versus 7.6–9.6% for VGGT and π³, and reduces ATE RMSE to 2.7 on 360Loc (vs. 5.8–6.3 for baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAM3R dominates on wide-angle and cross-modality scenarios: on 2D3DS (panoramic), it reaches 97.7% RRA@15 versus 10.6% (DUSt3R), 18.3% (MASt3R), 7.5% (Pow3R), 11.8% (VGGT), and 16.8% (π³); on 360Loc, DUSt3R and Pow3R score 0.0% while CAM3R achieves 96.0%. On the zero-shot CO3Dv2 benchmark (mixed pinhole-fisheye), CAM3R's 88.2% RTA@15 exceeds π³ (22.7%), VGGT (29.4%), DUSt3R (43.1%), MASt3R (33.4%), and Pow3R (38.3%) by a wide margin. On pinhole-only MegaDepth, π³ edges out CAM3R on RRA@15 (99.8% vs. 96.8%), but CAM3R leads on RTA@15 (94.2% vs. 93.3%) and matches VGGT's 98.0% RRA within 1.2 points. In multi-view evaluation, CAM3R with Ray-Aware Global Alignment achieves 73.5% mAA@30 on 2D3DS and 82.6% on 360Loc, compared to 7.6%/19.5% (VGGT) and 9.6%/17.8% (π³), and reduces trajectory ATE RMSE from 5.8–6.3 to 2.7 on 360Loc. The ablation (Table 5) confirms that simply fine-tuning DUSt3R on distorted data yields only 17.8% RRA@15 on 2D3DS, far below CAM3R's 97.7%, demonstrating that the decoupled ray+radial architecture—not just training data—is the source of the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAM3R sits at the intersection of two lines of work: feed-forward 3D reconstruction (DUSt3R, VGGT, MASt3R, Pow3R, π³) and universal camera ray estimation (UniK3D). It departs from DUSt3R's Cartesian pointmap regression by decoupling per-pixel ray directions (via Spherical Harmonics in a Ray Module) from radial distances (via a Cross-view Module), a representation that is well-defined for any central lens geometry. It extends UniK3D's monocular ray framework into a two-view cross-attention setting and replaces DUSt3R's pinhole-based global alignment with a Ray-Aware Global Alignment that optimizes in a ray-consistent 3D space rather than assuming linear pixel-to-3D correspondence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored failure mode: current 3D foundation models collapse to near-zero accuracy on panoramic and fisheye imagery, yet such sensors are ubiquitous in AR/VR, robotics, and autonomous driving. The ablation showing that fine-tuning DUSt3R on identical distorted data yields only 17.8% RRA@15 on 2D3DS versus CAM3R's 97.7% demonstrates that the architectural representation (rays + radial distance vs. entangled Cartesian coordinates) is the binding constraint, not data exposure. Open questions include the O(N²) pairwise inference cost for scene-graph construction, the memory overhead of separate ViT backbones, and whether the ray-based formulation can be folded into a single multi-view transformer to eliminate the two-stage pipeline.
