## Problem

Existing diffusion-based VTON models focus on preserving garment texture and body pose but neglect garment fit—how a garment aligns with the wearer's body—which is a fundamental determinant of holistic fashion style. This oversight produces try-on images with mismatched tightness or silhouette (e.g., a loose T-shirt rendered as slim), leading to unnatural results that fail to match user preferences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260880/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a person image, a garment image, and a categorical fit label (slim/regular/loose for tops; tapered/straight for bottoms) and produces a photorealistic try-on image where the garment conforms to the specified fit. It operates as a plug-in on top of existing VTON models, so the base model's input format is preserved; FitController only adds the fit label as an extra conditioning signal. Training and evaluation run at 384×512 resolution with FP16, while the Fit4Men dataset is captured at 768×1024.

## Method

FitController follows a two-stage design: a fit-aware layout generator (a U-Net repurposed from Stable Diffusion) redraws a three-class body-garment segmentation map conditioned on the fit label via FiLM modulation in each decoder ResNet block, and a lightweight multi-scale fit injector (97M params) maps the generator's intermediate features into the ControlNet interface of the target VTON model. A key enabler is a redesigned garment-agnostic preprocessor that reconstructs masks and dense poses from human keypoints and canonical body proportions, eliminating the 'shape leakage' where standard representations implicitly encode the original garment contour and bias the model toward replicating it. The layout generator is pretrained once and frozen; only the fit injector requires per-model training (7,500 steps), making the plug-in cheap to attach to any diffusion-based VTON architecture. The approach is validated on the newly constructed Fit4Men dataset (13,000 body-garment pairs across five fit types) and two proposed contour-based fit consistency metrics (Hu moments and Hausdorff distance).

## Results

On Fit4Men, FitController consistently improves fit consistency across all five integrated VTON models. For short-sleeve T-shirts (paired setting), Hu drops by 18–42% and Hd by 16–45% depending on the backbone, with the largest gains on FitDiT (Hu 0.65→0.38, Hd 12.36→6.77) and Leffa (Hu 0.56→0.40, Hd 9.18→7.05). For trousers, improvements are similarly consistent (e.g., FitDiT: Hu 1.26→0.75, Hd 13.36→7.59). Notably, improved fit also yields gains in standard image-quality metrics (FID, KID, SSIM, LPIPS), suggesting fit is an underexplored contributor to perceived realism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FitController is evaluated as a plug-in on five VTON backbones. FitDiT [15] benefits the most, with Hu improving by 41.5% (0.65→0.38) and Hd by 45.2% (12.36→6.77) on short-sleeve, because its square-mask preprocessor already removes garment shape leakage. Leffa [50] shows strong gains (Hu −28.6%, Hd −23.2%) but slightly less than FitDiT, as its original mask/dense-pose partially leaks garment contours. CatVTON [6] and its FLUX variant both see ~21–23% Hu reduction, while IDM-VTON [5] and StableVITON [17] show more modest 18–26% Hu improvements. In the ablation (Table 3), the proposed multi-scale fit injector outperforms both ControlNet [46] (FID 12.13 vs. 13.14; KID 0.49 vs. 0.91) and T2I-Adapter [27] (FID 12.13 vs. 13.50; KID 0.49 vs. 1.48) with fewer parameters (97M vs. 361M/77M) and faster convergence. A prompt-conditioning baseline (IDM-VTON + fit text) also underperforms FitController on both Hu and Hd, confirming that explicit spatial layout modeling is necessary for precise fit control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of diffusion-based VTON and controllable image generation. It builds on the single-branch VTON architectures (CatVTON, Leffa, FitDiT) that concatenate garment and person features, and on ControlNet-style conditional injection, but departs from both by generating the conditional layout image itself (via a fit-aware U-Net) and repurposing its intermediate features rather than training a separate encoder. In the customized-VTON line, it contrasts with landmark-based methods (which control local styles like sleeve rolling) and text-prompt approaches (which entangle fit with language semantics), arguing that fit is a holistic, spatially grounded attribute requiring explicit body-garment layout modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies garment fit as a previously neglected factor in VTON realism and shows that correcting it improves not only fit-specific metrics but also standard quality metrics (FID, KID, LPIPS), suggesting fit was a hidden contributor to perceived naturalness. The plug-in design (frozen layout generator + lightweight injector) makes the approach broadly applicable across VTON backbones without retraining the base model. Key limitations include restriction to menswear, a narrow set of fit categories (slim/regular/loose, tapered/straight), and limited body-shape diversity, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to womenswear, complex garment categories, and diverse body types. The Fit4Men dataset and Hu/Hausdorff fit-consistency metrics provide the first dedicated evaluation infrastructure for this sub-task.
