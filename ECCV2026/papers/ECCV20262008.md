## Problem

Existing language-guided image editing methods rely on purely textual Chain-of-Thought or coordinate-based prompts to reason about edits, which fundamentally cannot represent intricate visual layouts, fine-grained spatial relationships, or pixel-level cues such as object reflections and irregular shapes. This absence of an explicit visual reasoning process causes failures on complex tasks involving object intersections, where models may erase an object while leaving its reflection intact, violating physical consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262008/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image and a free-form text editing instruction (e.g., 'swap the tv for a lizard') and autoregressively produces an interleaved sequence of textual reasoning steps and visual tokens—where visual tokens are either positional masks for edit regions or rendered representations of new content—delimited by special start/end tokens, followed by the final edited image. The entire generation is conditioned on the full preceding sequence via a shared KV cache within a single unified model, rather than invoking external segmentation or generation tools.

## Method

MURE fine-tunes the BAGEL unified understanding-and-generation model to emit a natively interleaved text-image CoT that decomposes editing into subtasks (mask prediction, new object rendering, final compositing), trained with a combined cross-entropy loss on text tokens and a Rectified Flow MSE loss on visual latents. To counter the stochasticity of diffusion-based visual generation, the MMDC paradigm performs a tree search at each visual step: N candidate images are generated in parallel, scored by a Qwen2.5-VL-7B reward model via a deep-confidence score, and only the highest-scoring branch is retained to condition subsequent reasoning. This unified single-model design enables efficient KV cache reuse across all intermediate steps and inherently mitigates the error propagation that plagues multi-tool agent pipelines, while the interleaved training acts as a logic regularizer that improves even purely text-guided editing tasks.

## Results

On the MagicBrush test set, MURE achieves L1=0.049, CLIP-I=0.943, and DINO=0.877, outperforming the strongest non-CoT baseline ICEdit (L1=0.060, CLIP-I=0.928, DINO=0.853) and the text-CoT unified model Bagel (L1=0.067, CLIP-I=0.923, DINO=0.856). On the Emu test set, MURE reaches CLIP-I=0.920 and DINO=0.897, surpassing all baselines, though it trails slightly on CLIP-Out (0.301 vs. 0.308 for Bagel). On the SmartEdit benchmark, MURE leads in both Spatial Reasoning (PSNR=25.611, SSIM=0.897, LPIPS=0.065) and World Model (PSNR=28.694, SSIM=0.883, CLIP Score=21.298) scenarios. The paper reports an average 7.5% gain on MagicBrush over prior SOTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MURE outperforms all 11 baselines on every MagicBrush metric (L1, CLIP-I, DINO), with the largest margin over diffusion-based methods like FLUX.1 Fill (L1 0.192→0.049) and ACE++ (L1 0.195→0.049). Against the closest competitors—ICEdit (best non-CoT) and Bagel (text-CoT unified model)—MURE improves L1 by 18% and 27% respectively, and CLIP-I by 1.5 and 2.0 points. On the Emu test set, MURE achieves the best CLIP-I (0.920) and DINO (0.897), but trails Bagel (0.308) and RF-Solver Edit (0.309) on CLIP-Out, suggesting a minor trade-off in caption-image alignment. On SmartEdit, MURE dominates both Spatial Reasoning and World Model scenarios, beating Bagel by 1.8 dB in PSNR and 0.044 in SSIM for spatial tasks, and by 0.6 dB in PSNR and 0.044 in SSIM for world model tasks. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that the interleaved text-image CoT contributes the bulk of the gain (L1 0.067→0.058), while MMDC adds a further 15.5% L1 reduction (0.058→0.049).

## Relation to Prior Work

MURE builds directly on the BAGEL unified multimodal model as its backbone, extending it from textual-only CoT editing to natively interleaved text-image reasoning chains. It departs from prior CoT-for-editing approaches (purely textual CoT, GoT with bounding-box coordinates) by treating visual intermediates—masks, new-object renderings, intermediate states—as first-class autoregressive tokens within a single model, rather than as outputs of external tools like SAM. The MMDC paradigm draws on tree-search and reward-model guidance from LLM reasoning literature (e.g., Deep Think with Confidence) but applies it to the stochastic visual generation steps of a diffusion-based unified model, a setting where such pruning has not been previously explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit visual rationales (masks, intermediate renderings) within the reasoning chain substantially improve complex spatial editing, and that this training acts as a 'logic regularizer' transferring benefits even to purely text-guided subtasks—a finding with implications beyond editing. However, the approach is tied to a single unified-model backbone (BAGEL), relies on a zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-7B reward model with a noted semantic-over-spatial bias, and the 14K dataset, while first-of-its-kind, is modest in scale. Open questions include whether the interleaved CoT paradigm generalizes to video editing or 3D scenes, whether the MMDC tree search scales efficiently with more reasoning steps, and whether the logic-regularizer effect persists across different backbone architectures.
