## Problem

Existing benchmarks for unified multimodal models evaluate either visual question answering (text output from image input) or text-to-image generation (image output from text input), but none assess the joint generation of both images and text in response to a single query. Moreover, no standardized, reproducible evaluation methodology exists for scoring open-ended multimodal generation, where prior approaches rely on data-independent generic prompts or expensive pairwise human comparisons.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20260210/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of the 1,000 questions is a text prompt that requires the model to produce both one or more generated images and accompanying text explanations in a single response. Closed-ended tasks (space, textbook, diagram, paper) expect a single illustrative image with a grounded factual explanation, while open-ended guide tasks (art, life, tech, exercise) require a multi-step visual sequence where the number of steps is left to the model's discretion. The evaluation is scored against 10,417 question-specific rubric criteria, roughly half targeting image quality and half targeting text accuracy.

## Method

UEval introduces a data-dependent rubric-based scoring framework: for each question, a frontier MLLM (Gemini-2.5-Pro) drafts an itemized rubric conditioned on the question and a human-collected reference image-text pair, and human annotators then refine the rubric by consolidating redundancies and adding missing criteria. Rubrics for closed-ended tasks check whether outputs contain specific factual details from the reference, whereas rubrics for open-ended tasks evaluate higher-level generation qualities such as temporal consistency and step-by-step coherence. At evaluation time, a judge MLLM scores each model response against every rubric criterion, and the final score is the fraction of satisfied criteria, yielding an automated, reproducible metric. The authors validate this pipeline by showing ~90% criterion-level agreement and a Pearson correlation of r=0.88 between the MLLM judge and human evaluators, and they further demonstrate that appending a reasoning trace from GPT-5-Thinking to the prompt improves visual outputs of non-reasoning models, suggesting chain-of-thought benefits transfer to multimodal generation.

## Results

On UEval (1,000 questions across 8 tasks, scored via 10,417 rubric criteria), GPT-5-Thinking achieves the highest average score of 66.4/100, followed closely by Gemini-2.5-Flash (66.0) and GPT-5-Instant (65.2). The best open-source model, Emu3.5, reaches only 49.1, a 17.3-point gap behind the top proprietary model. Reference answers score 92.2 on average, indicating substantial headroom for all current models. Multi-step planning tasks (Art, Life, Exercise) yield the lowest scores across all models, while knowledge-based tasks (Space, Textbook) are comparatively easier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UEval is a benchmark rather than a method, so the 'comparison' is across 9 unified models. GPT-5-Thinking (66.4) edges out Gemini-2.5-Flash (66.0) and GPT-5-Instant (65.2) by narrow margins, with the largest per-task advantage on Space (+6.7 over GPT-5-Instant) and Exercise (+3.8). Among open-source models, Emu3.5 (49.1) dominates BAGEL (31.0) by 18.1 points, which in turn outperforms Janus-Pro (22.9), Show-o2 (22.6), and MMaDA (14.4). The proprietary-to-open-source gap is 17.3 points on average, with the widest per-task gap on Paper (51.9 vs. 31.6). A key finding is that reasoning models outperform their non-reasoning counterparts: GPT-5-Thinking beats GPT-5-Instant on 6 of 8 tasks, and appending GPT-5-Thinking's reasoning trace to GPT-5-Instant's prompt improves its visual outputs, though weaker models like BAGEL show no such benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

UEval carves out a new evaluation dimension for unified multimodal models by requiring joint text-and-image generation in response to a single query, departing from the two dominant paradigms of VQA (text-only output) and T2I (image-only output). Conceptually, it adapts the data-dependent rubric framework from HealthBench [3] to the multimodal setting, where rubric criteria must cover both visual and textual dimensions of a response. While prior unified-model benchmarks [2, 35, 41, 60, 66] evaluate interleaved or unified generation, UEval distinguishes itself by using fine-grained, per-question rubrics validated by human experts rather than generic scoring prompts or pairwise comparisons, and by spanning 8 diverse real-world tasks with both closed-ended and open-ended designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UEval provides the first standardized, reproducible benchmark specifically targeting the joint text-and-image output that characterizes real-world multimodal reasoning, filling a gap that limits progress in unified model development. The finding that reasoning models outperform non-reasoning variants—and that externally generated CoT traces can improve image quality in some (but not all) non-reasoning models—raises open questions about whether explicit reasoning will become a standard component of multimodal generation pipelines. Limitations include reliance on a single default judge model (though validated across alternatives), a moderate dataset size (1,000 questions), and the observation that open-ended multi-step tasks remain particularly challenging, suggesting the benchmark will need expansion as models improve.
