## Problem

Mixed-precision post-training quantization (MPQ) for Vision Transformers requires assigning different bit-widths per layer under a size budget while minimizing model-level output distortion. Existing approaches either optimize local layerwise surrogates that decouple from the true accuracy objective, or rely on expensive non-analytical global solvers (reinforcement learning, genetic algorithms, gradient-based search) that scale poorly to modern ViTs. The paper shows the global MPQ problem is actually solvable by an efficient analytical algorithm with linear complexity while preserving global optimality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained ViT (e.g., ViT-S/B, DeiT-S/B), a small calibration set of 512 ImageNet images (no augmentations), and a target total bit budget R. It produces integer per-layer bit-widths for both weights and activations (B_l^W, B_l^A ∈ [1,10]) that jointly define the quantized model. Unlike many prior MPQ methods that only allocate weight bits, BLOB-Q co-optimizes weight and activation bit-widths within the same budget, and the entire calibration requires only a single forward+backward pass on the calibration set.

## Method

BLOB-Q rests on two key discoveries. First, systematic signal analysis shows that quantization errors in converged ViTs are small perturbations (the 3rd moment of |ΔW| is negligible for ≥4-bit), so the global ℓ₂ model-output distortion admits a valid quadratic Taylor expansion around the full-precision operating point. Second, this quadratic form reveals an additivity property: the total model distortion decomposes into a sum of independent per-layer distortion terms, each depending only on that layer's bit-width. This structural decomposition converts the NP-hard nonlinear global optimization into a separable integer programming problem, which is then solved exactly via dynamic programming in O(2LRB²) time (linear in the number of layers). The per-layer distortion values are calibrated efficiently using an empirical Fisher Hessian approximation with matrix-level parallelism along the data dimension, requiring only one forward+backward pass. The DP solver runs in seconds on a single GPU, yielding the globally optimal mixed-precision configuration without any retraining.

## Results

On ImageNet, BLOB-Q achieves state-of-the-art mixed-precision PTQ accuracy across six ViT/DeiT models. At 4-bit average, BLOB-Q reaches 76.85% (ViT-S), 82.84% (ViT-B), 80.30% (ViT-B/384), 77.07% (DeiT-S), 80.95% (DeiT-B), and 81.64% (DeiT-B/384) top-1 accuracy. At 6-bit, BLOB-Q matches or exceeds full-precision accuracy on all six models (e.g., 85.07% vs. 84.54% FP32 on ViT-B, 81.83% vs. 81.85% on DeiT-B), with accuracy loss below 1%. The method completes optimization in under 2 minutes on average (33 s for ViT-S, 121 s for ViT-B on a single L40 GPU), compared to hours for RL- or GA-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BLOB-Q outperforms the strongest 4-bit baselines (FIMA-Q, APHQ-ViT, LAMPQ) on 5 of 6 models: +0.69% over FIMA-Q on ViT-S, +0.45% on DeiT-S, +0.74% on DeiT-B, +2.82% on ViT-B/384, and +2.54% on DeiT-B/384. The sole exception is ViT-B at 4-bit, where FIMA-Q edges ahead by 0.10% (82.94% vs. 82.84%) but requires 7.3× more calibration time (3.4 h vs. <1 s). At 6-bit, BLOB-Q achieves the best accuracy on all six models, beating FIMA-Q by 0.24% on ViT-S and 0.25% on ViT-B, and APHQ-ViT by 0.78% on ViT-S and 0.24% on ViT-B. Against uniform-precision methods (RepQ-ViT, NoisyQuant, EasyQuant), BLOB-Q's mixed-precision allocation yields consistent 1–3% gains at 4-bit and 0.5–1% at 6-bit. In the plug-and-play experiment (Table 5), BLOB-Q stacked on ERQ improves 4-bit accuracy by +3.51% on ViT-B and +5.31% on DeiT-S, confirming its generality across underlying quantizers.

## Relation to Prior Work

BLOB-Q departs from both major camps in mixed-precision quantization: methods that optimize local layerwise surrogates (VT-PTQ, HAWQ) and methods that optimize global objectives via heuristic search (RL-based HAQ, genetic-algorithm P²-ViT, gradient-based LAPQ). It builds on RepQ-ViT as the underlying weight quantizer and on the empirical Fisher approximation for Hessian estimation, but its core contribution is a conceptual reframing: by showing that quantization errors in converged ViTs are small perturbations whose third moments vanish, the NP-hard global distortion minimization reduces to a separable integer program solvable by dynamic programming in linear time. This analytical route to global optimality is distinct from all prior MPQ solvers, which either sacrifice global optimality for tractability or sacrifice tractability for global optimality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: BLOB-Q achieves 6-bit PTQ with no measurable accuracy loss on ViT/DeiT models and substantially narrows the 4-bit accuracy gap, all in under 2 minutes versus hours for RL-based alternatives. The additivity property, if it generalizes beyond ViTs to other architectures (e.g., CNNs, LLMs), could unlock efficient global bit-allocation for a much broader class of models. Open questions include whether the small-perturbation assumption holds below 4 bits (the paper notes 2-bit breaks the quadratic approximation), whether the additivity property survives in deeper or more heterogeneous architectures, and whether the empirical Fisher approximation remains accurate for very large models where full Jacobian storage becomes prohibitive.
