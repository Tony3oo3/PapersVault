## Problem

CLIP-based open-vocabulary segmentation relies on frozen text prototypes that encode language priors calibrated to natural images, causing systematic misalignment when target domains (remote sensing, autonomous driving) exhibit different visual statistics. Existing adaptation methods either require labeled target data or modify only the visual encoder, leaving the text-side bottleneck unaddressed. The paper asks whether this language prior bias can be mitigated using only unlabeled target images at test time, without updating any model weights.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of unlabeled target-domain images (no ground-truth masks), a list of K class names rendered through 80 averaged OpenAI ImageNet templates, and a fully frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 model. It produces K adapted text prototype vectors on the unit hypersphere, which are then used directly for pixel-level cosine-similarity matching to yield segmentation masks. All network parameters remain untouched; only the prototype directions change, and the entire adaptation loop (R=5 rounds) takes roughly 15 minutes on two RTX 4090 GPUs with negligible inference overhead.

## Method

VPTA iteratively refines text prototypes through a two-stage loop: (1) evidence collection, where each pixel is assigned by cosine similarity and only pixels exceeding a margin threshold τ_m contribute to a per-class visual prototype, and (2) reliability-controlled update, where a per-class score s_c (combining pixel count, mean margin, and margin variance) scales the interpolation step β_c = β_max·s_c so that well-supported classes move more aggressively toward target evidence while poorly-supported classes stay near their CLIP initialization. To prevent visually similar prototypes from collapsing, a reliability-ordered Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization is applied, with a weight w_jc = s_j/(s_j+s_c+ε) ensuring that less reliable classes yield to more reliable anchors. The loop repeats for R rounds, with each round re-segmenting the target images using the updated prototypes to obtain cleaner pseudo-evidence, and the final adapted prototypes are used directly for inference.

## Results

VPTA achieves state-of-the-art mIoU on all ten remote sensing datasets, with the largest gains on Vaihingen (+8.1 mIoU over SegEarth-OV under the original-label protocol, reaching 37.2%) and WHU Aerial building extraction (+7.2, reaching 56.3%). On the remaining multi-class RS benchmarks, gains over SegEarth-OV range from +0.9 to +3.7 mIoU (e.g., LoveDA 40.5 vs. 36.8, UDD5 52.0 vs. 50.4). Generalization beyond remote sensing is confirmed with +3.2 mIoU on both Cityscapes (23.5) and PASCAL VOC 2012 (56.3) using the same fixed hyperparameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPTA outperforms all five baselines on every dataset where a direct comparison is available. Against the strongest baseline SegEarth-OV [17], VPTA gains +0.9 to +8.1 mIoU across the seven multi-class RS datasets and +1.7 to +7.2 on the three single-class extraction tasks, with the largest margin on Vaihingen (original labels) and WHU Aerial. ClearCLIP [13], the best training-free visual-encoder method, is surpassed by 7–10 mIoU on multi-class RS (e.g., 37.2 vs. 27.3 on Vaihingen-A) and by 2–5 mIoU on single-class and driving benchmarks. OVRS [4] and RSKT-Seg [14] trail VPTA by 6–16 mIoU on the multi-class RS datasets where both report numbers, and neither extends to the driving or natural-scene benchmarks. Raw CLIP [20] without any adaptation remains the weakest baseline, scoring below 15 mIoU on most RS datasets, underscoring the magnitude of the domain-shift gap that VPTA addresses.

## Relation to Prior Work

VPTA sits at the intersection of two lines of work: CLIP-based open-vocabulary segmentation (SegEarth-OV, ClearCLIP, CLIP-DINOiser) and test-time adaptation of vision-language models (TPT, PromptAlign). Prior OVSS methods predominantly improve the visual encoder or add task-specific heads while leaving text prototypes frozen; prior adaptation methods either require labeled data (CoOp, CoCoOp, CLIP-Adapter) or operate per-image on classification tasks. VPTA departs from both by identifying frozen text prototypes as the primary bottleneck under domain shift and adapting them directly using only unlabeled target images, with reliability-controlled updates and Gram-Schmidt-style separation preservation. It is explicitly complementary to visual-side improvements and is validated as a plug-in on top of SegEarth-OV, ClearCLIP, GEM, and RemoteCLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a label-free, frozen-model, ~15-minute adaptation that plugs into existing OVSS pipelines with negligible inference overhead, making it immediately deployable in remote sensing and autonomous driving workflows where labeled target data is scarce. The diagnostic insight that text prototypes (not visual features) are the dominant source of error under domain shift redirects attention in the field toward the text side of the alignment. Key limitations remain: the method fails on very small objects with too few confident pixels, breaks under spectral-band mismatch (e.g., RGB vs. IR-R-G), and applies a single global prototype per class without accounting for intra-domain variation. Open questions include whether multi-scale or instance-level evidence aggregation can address rare classes, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D segmentation settings.
