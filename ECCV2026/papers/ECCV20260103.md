## Problem

No prior method generates geometrically consistent multi-view scenes from a single freehand sketch. Existing multi-view diffusion approaches require high-fidelity photographs or text prompts that already resolve geometric ambiguity, while sketch-to-3D methods are limited to single objects or demand multiple sketch views and costly per-scene optimisation. Freehand sketches are simultaneously spatially informative yet geometrically unreliable, making them the most challenging conditioning signal for multi-view generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single freehand scene sketch and a text caption as input and produces N=33 photorealistic views spanning a full 360° azimuth orbit at four elevation angles, all at 480×480 resolution. All views are synthesised jointly in one forward denoising pass of a video diffusion transformer—no reference photograph, iterative refinement, or per-scene optimisation is required. The sketch is encoded and denoised directly, bypassing any intermediate sketch-to-photograph translation stage.

## Method

The framework builds on the Wan 2.1 video DiT (1.3B params) with frame replication (N→4N+1) to preserve view identity through the VAE's 4× temporal compression. Two lightweight modules are added in parallel: (i) Camera-Aware Attention Adapters (CA3, ~35.4M params, 2.7% overhead) that inject Projective Rotary Position Encoding (PRoPE) using virtual camera matrices placed on a sphere, so attention scores reflect relative projective geometry between views; and (ii) a Sparse Correspondence Supervision Loss (CSL) that applies a weighted InfoNCE objective to the adapter's query-key projections at four mid-to-deep layers, using positive pairs extracted from COLMAP Structure-from-Motion reconstructions of pseudo ground-truth views. LoRA (rank 16) on the backbone self-attention handles sketch-domain adaptation, and a curriculum schedule ramps λ_corr from 0 to 0.01 after flow-matching and LoRA stabilise. Training uses a curated 9,222-sample S2MV dataset constructed by generating frontal views with FLUX.2, selecting the best seed via segmentation mIoU, and synthesising multi-view outputs with Qwen Image Edit Angles.

## Results

On the S2MV test set (477 samples, N=33 views, 3 seeds), the proposed single-stage method achieves FID 18.49, Corr-Acc 0.199, LPIPS 0.632, CLIP-I 0.828, and PSNR 12.169, outperforming both two-stage baselines on five of six metrics. The FID improvement over SEVA (46.34) and ViewCrafter (48.22) exceeds 60%, while Corr-Acc improves by 23% over SEVA (0.161) and 46% over ViewCrafter (0.136). Inference time is ~50 s per sample, yielding a 3.7× speedup over SEVA (~3.1 min) and a 42× speedup over ViewCrafter (~35 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two two-stage baselines that first translate the sketch to a photorealistic front view via FLUX.2-dev before generating multi-view output. Against SEVA (Plücker-conditioned latent diffusion), the proposed method wins on PSNR (12.169 vs 11.310), LPIPS (0.632 vs 0.705), FID (18.49 vs 46.34), CLIP-I (0.828 vs 0.756), and Corr-Acc (0.199 vs 0.161), while SEVA retains a narrow SSIM edge (0.265 vs 0.302 in favour of Ours — actually Ours wins here too; ViewCrafter is the SSIM winner). Against ViewCrafter (point-cloud-guided video diffusion via DUSt3R), the proposed method wins on PSNR (12.169 vs 11.148), LPIPS (0.632 vs 0.737), FID (18.49 vs 48.22), CLIP-I (0.828 vs 0.773), and Corr-Acc (0.199 vs 0.136), but ViewCrafter achieves the highest SSIM (0.338 vs 0.302), attributed to its smoother, lower-frequency outputs. The single-stage design also provides a 3.7× speedup over SEVA and a 42× speedup over ViewCrafter, eliminating the intermediate sketch-to-photo generation step entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of video diffusion transformers (Wan 2.1) and camera-aware attention mechanisms (PRoPE), repurposing the temporal axis of video DiTs as a viewpoint axis for multi-view synthesis. It departs from prior multi-view methods (SEVA, ViewCrafter, CAT3D) that require photorealistic input by conditioning directly on freehand sketches, and from sketch-to-3D methods (Sketch2NeRF, SketchDream) that demand multiple views or per-scene optimisation. The architectural design follows ControlNet's zero-initialised parallel branch paradigm, while the correspondence supervision loss (CSL) is distinguished from the concurrent CAMEO by operating sparsely on a lightweight adapter rather than densely on the main attention of a UNet backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to produce geometrically consistent multi-view scenes from a single freehand sketch in one forward pass, opening sketching as a lightweight, accessible interface for 3D content creation. The mutually reinforcing design (curated dataset + CA3 + CSL) provides a template for injecting geometric inductive bias into generative models with explicit supervision, a combination not previously explored. Key limitations include the small training set (~9k samples), 480×480 resolution, and reliance on generated (not rendered) ground truth, leaving open whether the approach scales to higher fidelity or more complex scenes. The dramatic ablation showing CA3 removal causes near-catastrophic failure (FID 266) underscores how critical explicit camera geometry is when the input lacks dense 3D cues.
