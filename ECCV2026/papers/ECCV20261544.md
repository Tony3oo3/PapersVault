## Problem

Scaling monocular 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM to kilometer-level outdoor scenes is bottlenecked by two coupled failures: geometric solvers collapse under planar degeneracies, pure rotations, and dynamic occlusions, causing irreversible cumulative drift, while unconstrained Gaussian primitive growth exhausts GPU memory over long trajectories. Existing systems either inherit fragile multi-view geometry frontends or rely on computationally heavy foundation models for continuous pose estimation, and their mapping strategies either accumulate redundant floaters or apply local heuristics that ignore multi-view consistency.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a monocular RGB video stream (no LiDAR, IMU, or stereo) processed sequentially in real-time on a single GPU, with a metric depth model providing geometric priors and MASt3R invoked only as an on-demand fallback. It produces an SE(3) camera pose trajectory, a hierarchical voxel-structured 3D Gaussian scene map (as few as 1.14M primitives for an 11.6 km sequence), and photorealistic novel-view renderings. The system scales to sequences exceeding 13,000 frames while maintaining peak VRAM under 24 GB.

## Method

KiloGS-SLAM couples a motion-adaptive hybrid tracker with a lifecycle-managed Gaussian mapper. The tracker employs a condition-triggered three-tier pipeline: sparse optical flow statistics (MAD-based outlier rejection) filter dynamic objects; a dual-modal estimator adaptively switches between Essential-matrix and PnP/Homography solvers via a GRIC-inspired score to handle geometric degeneracies; and a sliding-window constant-velocity prior verifies each pose against rotational consistency (geodesic distance in SO(3)) and a non-holonomic forward-axis constraint, triggering on-demand MASt3R dense matching only upon anomaly detection. The mapper builds on Scaffold-GS voxelized anchors with a hierarchical Level-of-Detail structure, initializing new Gaussians probabilistically via a combined DoG texture edge and normalized depth-gradient saliency map (with rendered-residual penalty to avoid redundancy). During optimization, multi-view photometric errors are evaluated within local keyframe chunks, and global densification (OR-rule across chunks) and pruning (max-rule across chunks) are applied to suppress floaters while preserving high-frequency detail, keeping the primitive count minimal without sacrificing rendering fidelity.

## Results

On KITTI (10 sequences, up to 5 km), KiloGS-SLAM achieves the lowest average ATE RMSE of 3.46 m with loop closure (4.26 m without), outperforming the closest 3DGS-SLAM baseline GigaSLAM (5.46 m) and all VO/SLAM systems by large margins. On the ultra-long KITTI-360 (up to 11.6 km, 13,888 frames), it secures 25.7 m average ATE versus 64.7 m for GigaSLAM and 32.5 m for DPV-SLAM++. For rendering, it achieves the best PSNR across all three datasets (28.36 dB Waymo, 22.52 dB KITTI, 21.60 dB KITTI-360) while maintaining the lowest Gaussian count (1.14 M on KITTI-360) and minimal storage (380 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KiloGS-SLAM outperforms all 13 baselines on KITTI tracking (3.46 m avg ATE vs. 5.46 m for the closest competitor GigaSLAM, and 22.3 m for the best VO system DPV-SLAM++). On the extreme KITTI-360 benchmark, the gap widens dramatically: 25.7 m vs. 64.7 m (GigaSLAM) and 32.5 m (DPV-SLAM++), while foundation-model-based systems like VGGT-Long (344 m) and DA3-Streaming (243 m) suffer catastrophic drift. On short Waymo sequences, the margin is tighter (0.51 m vs. 0.59 m Splat-SLAM, 0.87 m GigaSLAM), but KiloGS-SLAM still leads. For rendering, it achieves the highest PSNR on all three datasets (28.36 dB Waymo vs. 26.84 S3PO-GS; 22.52 dB KITTI vs. 20.99 GigaSLAM; 21.60 dB KITTI-360 vs. 20.92 GigaSLAM). In efficiency, it uses only 1.14 M Gaussians and 380 MB storage on KITTI-360, compared to 6.09 M / 2009 MB for GigaSLAM and 31.1 M / 7336 MB for OnTheFly-NVS, while Splat-SLAM and OpenGS-SLAM simply run out of memory on long sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (Kerbl et al.) and its structured voxelized variant Scaffold-GS (Lu et al.), adopting the hierarchical Level-of-Detail architecture from GigaSLAM (Deng et al.) for managing depth variation in outdoor scenes. It departs from the dominant trend of using 3D foundation models (MASt3R, VGGT) as primary pose solvers by instead treating them as on-demand fallbacks within a condition-triggered three-tier geometric pipeline (Essential matrix → PnP → foundation model). The lifecycle-managed mapping strategy with probabilistic DoG-based initialization and chunk-based multi-view consistency scoring represents a systematic departure from both naive densification and local heuristic pruning used in prior 3DGS-SLAM systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first monocular-only 3DGS-SLAM system to simultaneously achieve SOTA tracking accuracy, rendering quality, and memory efficiency at kilometer scale (13,000+ frames, 11.6 km) on a single GPU, addressing a practical bottleneck that has limited deployment of photorealistic SLAM in autonomous driving. The non-holonomic constraint and vehicle-specific motion priors, while effective for driving sequences, raise open questions about generalizability to pedestrian, aerial, or robotic platforms where Ackermann geometry does not apply. Whether the chunk-based multi-view consistency approach can be further parallelized or whether the on-demand foundation model activation can be made truly real-time (rather than a fallback) remain important directions for future work.
