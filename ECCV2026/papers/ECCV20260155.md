## Problem

Existing 3D body fitting methods are limited to single-modal inputs (point clouds or multi-view images) and typically assume known metric scale, a constraint that fails for AI-generated 3D human assets where scale distortion is prevalent. No prior approach simultaneously handles multi-modal inputs, scale ambiguity, and partial geometry in a unified, 3D-native framework.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a point cloud of 5,000–20,000 points (sampled from meshes, 3DGS positions, or RGBD sensors), optionally augmented with a single front-view RGB image, and can process scale-normalized or partial (single-view) inputs. It outputs 600 dense 3D landmarks (120 head, 300 body, 180 hands) corresponding to predefined SMPL-X template vertices, which are then used to iteratively optimize SMPL-X parameters (shape β, pose θ, expression ψ, translation t). A separate scale predictor outputs a scalar factor S that rescales normalized point clouds to canonical human size before landmark prediction.

## Method

The core is a Perceiver-style conditional transformer decoder: a Point-BERT encoder tokenizes the input point cloud into patch features, and 600 learnable landmark embeddings serve as queries that cross-attend to these features over 24 layers before an MLP regresses 3D landmark coordinates. A plug-and-play image adapter adds a parallel cross-attention branch (using DINOv2 features) in each decoder layer without modifying the base architecture, enabling optional RGB fusion at inference. A scale predictor, sharing the Point-BERT backbone with an additional learnable scale token, estimates the scale factor from a normalized point cloud. Training is supervised end-to-end with MSE loss on landmarks; the image adapter is fine-tuned with the backbone frozen, and the scale predictor is trained independently. At inference, SMPL-X parameters are recovered via a three-stage optimization (translation → shape+pose → all parameters) minimizing alignment to the predicted landmarks.

## Results

On CAPE, OmniFit achieves a V2V error of 0.672 cm and MPJPE of 0.659 cm, reducing errors by 57.1% and 67.1% respectively over the second-best method ETCH. On 4D-DRESS, the gains are even larger: V2V drops to 0.589 cm (−75.5%) and MPJPE to 0.662 cm (−80.9%) versus ETCH. OmniFit is the first body fitting method to reach millimeter-level accuracy on both benchmarks, with particularly pronounced improvements on hands (up to 83.7% V2V reduction) and head regions. Adding the optional image adapter further reduces 4D-DRESS V2V to 0.539 cm and MPJPE to 0.622 cm, surpassing multi-view optimization baselines EasyMoCap (3.047/4.029) and DiffProxy (2.093/2.630).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniFit outperforms all five point cloud fitting baselines by a wide margin on both CAPE and 4D-DRESS. Against the strongest point cloud baseline ETCH [31], OmniFit reduces V2V by 57.1% (CAPE) and 75.5% (4D-DRESS), and MPJPE by 67.1% and 80.9% respectively, with the largest per-region gains on hands (up to 83.7% V2V reduction on 4D-DRESS). The earlier methods IPNet [6], PTF [62], NICP [42], and ArtEq [19] are all substantially worse, with V2V errors 2.5–12× higher than OmniFit. In the multi-view comparison (Table 2), OmniFit with point cloud alone (V2V 0.589, MPJPE 0.662) already surpasses EasyMoCap [1] (3.047/4.029) and DiffProxy [61] (2.093/2.630), and the image adapter variant further narrows the gap to 0.539/0.622. The paper notes this comparison is not strictly apples-to-apples since multi-view methods consume rendered RGB + camera poses while OmniFit uses mesh-sampled points, but the result demonstrates that a 3D-native paradigm can be more effective than render-and-compare optimization when 3D geometry is available.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniFit sits at the intersection of learning-based body fitting (IPNet, PTF, NICP, ArtEq, ETCH) and correspondence prediction, but departs from the dense-correspondence and marker-aggregation paradigms by using a Perceiver-style conditional decoder that directly regresses 600 predefined SMPL-X landmark coordinates from Point-BERT-encoded point cloud features. The plug-and-play image adapter borrows the parallel cross-attention fusion idea from MV-Adapter, and the scale predictor reuses the Point-BERT backbone with an additional learnable token. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against both optimization-based multi-view pipelines (EasyMocap, DiffProxy) and prior learning-based single-modal methods, arguing that a 3D-native, multi-modal, scale-agnostic formulation is the missing unification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving sub-centimeter V2V error for the first time on CAPE and 4D-DRESS, and surpassing multi-view optimization baselines with a 3D-native approach, shifts the practical default for body fitting away from render-and-compare pipelines when 3D assets are available. The scale predictor addresses a real bottleneck in AIGC pipelines where generative models normalize to unit bounding boxes, making OmniFit directly applicable to AI-generated avatar workflows. Key limitations remain: SMPL-X parameters are still optimized iteratively from predicted landmarks (adding inference latency), the image adapter supports only a single front view, and the multi-view comparison is acknowledged as not strictly apples-to-apples. Extending the adapter to multi-view inputs and exploring direct parameter regression are natural next steps.
