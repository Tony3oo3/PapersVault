## Problem

Existing negative-signal guidance methods for diffusion and flow-matching models (SAG, SEG, ERG, PAG, S²) produce coarse, semantically divergent degradations that represent failure modes modern denoisers can already avoid, yielding 'easy' negatives. These methods also lack fine-grained control over the difficulty of the negative and typically use fixed target-layer selection, limiting their ability to expose the subtle, fine-grained artifacts that most reduce perceptual quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261039/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

EMAG is a training-free, inference-time modification applied to diffusion transformers (DiT-XL/2 at 256×256/512×512 and SD3-Medium at 1024×1024). It takes the same inputs as the base model—Gaussian noise plus a text prompt or class label—and produces a single generated image, but internally runs an additional forward pass through a perturbed attention branch at selected timesteps. The method is applicable in both conditional (text-to-image, class-conditional) and unconditional generation settings, and composes with orthogonal guidance strategies (APG, CADS) without architectural changes.

## Method

EMAG maintains an exponential moving average (EMA) of softmax-normalized self-attention maps across reverse-diffusion timesteps, then at selected middle layers replaces the current attention map with its EMA (or a convex blend controlled by λ), effectively suppressing high-frequency refinements while preserving global structure. A statistics-based adaptive layer-selection rule picks, at each timestep, the layer whose attention map has the largest mean absolute deviation from its EMA, ensuring the perturbation targets the layer most actively inserting fine-grained content. The decay factor β (default 0.988, half-life ≈ 2T) directly controls negative hardness: smaller β yields subtler, harder negatives. The resulting degraded score estimate is contrasted with the original via the auto-guidance formula (Eq. 14), and the combined signal is then passed through a standard CFG update (Eq. 15), yielding a two-stage guidance that rectifies subtle artifacts without altering global semantics.

## Results

On SD3-Medium (1024×1024, COCO-2014 val), EMAG achieves the highest Human Preference Score (HPS) of 29.76, a +0.54 improvement over CFG (29.22), while remaining competitive on FID (22.89 vs. 22.88). When combined with APG, EMAG+APG reaches HPS 29.79 with FID 21.82, matching APG's fidelity while adding preference gains. In unconditional generation, EMAG attains the lowest FID (74.98 on SD3; 29.85 on DiT-XL/2 256×256) and highest Coverage (0.112 and 0.768 respectively) among all tested guidance methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMAG outperforms all attention-perturbation baselines on HPS: it beats SAG+CFG (29.28), SEG+CFG (29.25), PAG+CFG (28.73), ERG (29.35), and S² (29.15) by 0.41–1.03 HPS points, while also matching or improving FID over SAG, SEG, PAG, and ERG. Against the orthogonal guidance methods, EMAG trades a small FID cost for a clear HPS gain over APG (29.76 vs. 29.45 HPS; 22.89 vs. 21.82 FID) and CADS (29.76 vs. 28.36 HPS; 22.89 vs. 18.32 FID), and the combination EMAG+APG recovers APG's FID advantage (21.82) while pushing HPS to 29.79. In unconditional generation (Table 3), EMAG achieves the best FID on both SD3 (74.98 vs. 82.11 for I-ERG) and DiT-XL/2 (29.85 vs. 39.17 for SEG), with the highest Coverage metric, confirming gains beyond the conditional setting. The paper also notes that negative hardness alone does not fully determine quality: S² and ERG show comparable hardness to EMAG in cosine-similarity measurements yet underperform on the FID–HPS Pareto frontier, indicating the perturbation mechanism itself matters.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMAG sits within the training-free attention-perturbation guidance family (ERG, PAG, SEG, SAG) that instantiates the auto-guidance strong/weak contrast framework of Karras et al. (2024) without requiring a separate weaker model. Its key departure is the use of temporal exponential moving averages of attention maps as the perturbation mechanism, which—unlike Gaussian blurring, identity replacement, or stochastic layer dropping—selectively suppresses high-frequency refinements while preserving global semantics, yielding controllable 'hard negatives.' The statistics-based adaptive layer selection (choosing the layer with maximum EMA-to-current MAE at each timestep) further differentiates it from prior methods that fix target layers. EMAG is also positioned as composable with orthogonal guidance strategies (APG, CADS) rather than a replacement for them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hard-negative framing provides a useful conceptual lens: prior attention-perturbation methods produce coarse 'easy' degradations that modern diffusion models can already avoid, whereas EMA-induced subtle artifacts expose failure modes the denoiser can actually rectify. The practical limitation is significant computational overhead (+213% wall-clock, +75% peak memory for full EMAG; +95% for the EMAG-Q variant), which limits adoption in latency-sensitive settings. Open questions include whether the greedy layer-selection rule can be replaced with a learned or optimized schedule, whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation, and whether the EMA mechanism generalizes beyond self-attention to cross-attention or MLP pathways.
