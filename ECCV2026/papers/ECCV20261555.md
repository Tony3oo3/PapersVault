## Problem

Aligning generative real-world SR models with human preference is bottlenecked by two issues: (1) existing IQA metrics are either full-reference (requiring unavailable ground truth) or no-reference (lacking fine-grained sensitivity for subtle generative SR differences), and static linear aggregation of FR/NR scores ignores the initial degradation severity; (2) offline RL frameworks like DP²O-SR suffer from pseudo-diversity, where noise-seed variations under rigid SR spatial constraints produce random texture hallucinations rather than genuine structural diversity, causing exploration collapse and reward hacking.

## Input / Output

The SR pipeline takes a low-resolution RGB image suffering from complex, unknown real-world degradations (blur, noise, compression, etc.) together with an enhanced text prompt, and produces a 512×512 high-resolution image at 4× upscaling. The COMPASS reward model separately consumes an LR–SR image pair and outputs a fidelity score F∈[0,1], input quality Q_LR∈[1,5], output quality Q_SR∈[1,5], and a short textual rationale; the final scalar reward is computed via the quality-adaptive formula R = F·Q_LR + F^(Q_LR/γ)·ΔQ. The generative backbone is Qwen-Image-Edit (a flow-matching model), with LoRA adapters (rank 32) trained on top of the base weights and merged into the SFT checkpoint at inference.

## Method

OARS couples a process-aware MLLM reward (COMPASS, built on Qwen3-VL-8B) with a three-stage progressive online RL procedure. COMPASS evaluates the LR→SR transition by jointly modeling Fidelity (DISTS-based content preservation) and Perceptual Gain, with an input-quality-adaptive gate F^(Q_LR/γ)·ΔQ that tightens the fidelity constraint for high-quality inputs and relaxes it for severely degraded ones; it is trained on COMPASS-20K (2,400 inputs × 12 enhancement algorithms) using a three-stage annotation pipeline combining global anchor scoring, exhaustive intra-group pairwise ranking, and per-group linear calibration. The RL training proceeds as: (1) cold-start flow-matching SFT on paired LR-HR data (LSDIR), (2) full-reference RL where DISTS consistency rewards stabilize early optimization, and (3) non-reference RL on unpaired real-world LQ images guided solely by COMPASS. A key design choice is performing LoRA shallow fine-tuning on the base generative model rather than the SFT model, exploiting the base model's higher sampling stochasticity for on-policy exploration while reducing reward hacking; the policy objective is a negative-aware contrastive loss that interpolates positive and negative velocity fields (v_θ⁺, v_θ⁻) weighted by group-normalized reward probabilities, with variance-based filtering to discard low-discriminative sample groups.

## Results

On RealSR, OARS achieves the best NR metrics among all compared methods: LIQE 4.30, MUSIQ 71.41, MANIQA 0.528, Q-Insight 3.701, and TOPIQ 0.680, while maintaining competitive FR scores (PSNR 22.36, SSIM 0.648). On DIV2K, OARS leads on all NR metrics (LIQE 4.67, MUSIQ 74.07, TOPIQ 0.696) with PSNR 17.32 and SSIM 0.407. The COMPASS reward model achieves 83.1% pairwise preference accuracy on SRIQA-Bench, surpassing the best GT-Ref method A-FINE (82.4%) and the best GT-Free method Q-Insight (78.8%). In a user study with 27 expert raters, OARS receives 47.62% of votes versus 27.68% for the strongest baseline DP²O-SR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On RealSR, OARS outperforms all diffusion-based SR baselines (StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR, OSEDiff, SinSR) on every NR metric, with the largest gaps over StableSR (LIQE +1.25, MUSIQ +9.76) and DiffBIR (LIQE +0.75, MUSIQ +6.16), while trailing OSEDiff and SeeSR on PSNR/SSIM by 0.7–2.0 dB. Against the perception-oriented autoregressive method PURE and the unified VLM UARE, OARS wins on both FR and NR metrics (e.g., PSNR 22.36 vs 21.31/21.38; LIQE 4.30 vs 3.79/4.07), demonstrating that the process-aware reward balances fidelity and perceptual quality more effectively. Compared to its own cold-start model Qwen-SFT, OARS yields consistent NR gains (LIQE +0.49, MUSIQ +2.84 on RealSR; LIQE +0.33, MUSIQ +1.72 on DIV2K) with negligible FR degradation. For the reward model, COMPASS achieves 83.1% All-Acc on SRIQA-Bench, beating the best GT-Ref method A-FINE (82.4%) and the best GT-Free method Q-Insight (78.8%), confirming its superiority as a preference signal. In the DPO comparison (Table 4), applying OARS's non-reference RL on top of DP²O-SR yields further gains (e.g., MUSIQ +4.93, LIQE +0.36 on RealSet80), indicating that online process-aware optimization provides complementary refinement beyond offline DPO-style alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

OARS sits at the intersection of two active threads: generative SR via diffusion/flow models (StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR) and preference alignment via RLHF/DPO. It most directly builds on DiffusionNFT's forward-process RL paradigm with a negative-aware contrastive objective, adapting it to the SR setting with LoRA-based shallow optimization on the base model. The key conceptual departure from prior SR alignment work (notably DP²O-SR) is twofold: (1) the reward is process-aware, scoring the LR→SR transition rather than the SR output in isolation, and (2) alignment is performed online with on-policy exploration from the base model's higher stochasticity, rather than offline over a fixed pool of noise-seeded samples from a single SFT policy. The COMPASS reward itself extends MLLM-based IQA (Q-Insight, Q-Align) by introducing an input-quality-adaptive gating mechanism that dynamically trades fidelity against perceptual gain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, end-to-end recipe for aligning generative SR models with human preference that addresses two concrete failure modes: reward hacking (mitigated by the fidelity-gated reward and progressive FR→NR RL) and exploration collapse (mitigated by base-model stochasticity via LoRA). The COMPASS-20K dataset and its three-stage annotation pipeline are reusable resources for the broader IQA and SR communities. Open questions include whether the MLLM-based reward generalizes to other restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring, video SR), the computational cost of online RL with 24 rollouts per sample, and whether the quality-adaptive exponent mechanism transfers to settings where degradation severity is not well-captured by a single scalar score.
