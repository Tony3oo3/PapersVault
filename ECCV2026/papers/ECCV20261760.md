## Problem

Cross-modal image registration (e.g., optical-SAR, visible-infrared) is fundamentally challenged by severe nonlinear radiometric discrepancies and geometric distortions that create a highly non-convex matching landscape. Existing deterministic regression and optimization methods lack uncertainty awareness, causing them to accumulate errors in ambiguous regions and overconfidently commit to incorrect updates. No prior work formulates dense cross-modal flow estimation as a closed-loop recurrent Bayesian estimation problem with uncertainty-driven feedback to the optimization process.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of images from heterogeneous sensors (optical+SAR or RGB+infrared) and produces a dense pixel-wise 2D optical flow field along with a per-pixel posterior covariance map. During training, images are processed as 64×64 patches; at inference the full framework operates at 640 resolution. The flow is explicitly modeled as pixel-wise dense correspondences rather than low-dimensional global affine parameters, deliberately absorbing local radiometric anomalies and mitigating error propagation across peripheral regions.

## Method

RBE-Flow reformulates dense cross-modal flow estimation as a closed-loop recurrent Bayesian estimation on learned feature manifolds, coupling two modules in a 7-iteration loop. The Recurrent Manifold Optimization (RMO) block solves a damped Levenberg-Marquardt least-squares problem on a multi-scale feature correlation volume (fused via Spectrum-Entropy-Guided Fusion) to generate a flow observation and its uncertainty, with the LM damping factor adaptively inflated by the previous step's posterior covariance trace. The Uncertainty-Adaptive Probabilistic Update (UAPU) block propagates 5 deterministic sigma points through a learnable non-linear observation network to compute an MMSE-optimal Kalman gain, fusing the RMO observation with the prior belief and updating the posterior covariance, which is then fed back to RMO. Training uses a hybrid loss combining L1 supervision on the global flow initialization with a Geometry-Aware Rectified NLL that imposes a dynamic variance lower bound scaling with geometric error to prevent variance collapse, applied with deep supervision across all iterations. The encoder is initialized from XoFTR pretrained weights and the full framework is fine-tuned end-to-end.

## Results

RBE-Flow achieves state-of-the-art AEPE across all three benchmarks: 0.77 px on OSdataset (45.4% reduction over ADRNet's 1.41 px), 0.53 px on WHU-OPT-SAR (≈3× improvement over ADRNet's 1.43 px), and 0.49 px on RoadScene. At the 1 px CMR threshold, RBE-Flow reaches 78.5%, 91.0%, and 90.8% on the three datasets respectively, and maintains strong sub-pixel accuracy with 57.3%, 80.0%, and 85.8% CMR at 0.7 px. The method is particularly dominant under strict sub-pixel criteria (0.3 px), where most baselines drop sharply while RBE-Flow retains 11.6%, 24.0%, and 37.7% CMR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RBE-Flow outperforms all nine baselines on AEPE across every dataset. Against the strongest dense baselines, it reduces AEPE from 1.41→0.77 px (ADRNet) and 1.51→0.77 px (GDROS) on OSdataset, from 1.43→0.53 px (ADRNet) and 2.87→0.53 px (GDROS) on WHU-OPT-SAR, and from 0.68→0.49 px (GDROS) and 0.98→0.49 px (ADRNet) on RoadScene. The gap widens dramatically at sub-pixel thresholds: on WHU-OPT-SAR at 0.7 px, RBE-Flow achieves 80.0% CMR versus 33.0% (ADRNet) and 19.8% (GDROS). The sole exception is OSdataset at the strictest 0.3 px threshold, where GDROS edges ahead (16.6% vs. 11.6% CMR), though RBE-Flow leads at all other thresholds on that dataset. Handcrafted sparse methods (HOWP, LNIFT, MSG, RIFT) and semi-dense Transformer methods (XoFTR, GMFlow) are far behind, with AEPE values an order of magnitude larger, confirming that explicit dense flow estimation with probabilistic refinement is essential for cross-modal registration.

## Relation to Prior Work

RBE-Flow sits at the intersection of three research threads: recurrent dense flow estimation (RAFT, GMFlow, FlowFormer), differentiable optimization layers embedded in networks (OptNet, DROID-SLAM), and uncertainty-aware registration (PDC-Net, VBReg). It departs from prior dense flow methods by replacing learned recurrent updates with an explicit Levenberg-Marquardt solver on a feature manifold, and from existing uncertainty-aware approaches by making the posterior covariance an *active* regulator of the optimization damping rather than a passive confidence output. The sigma-point (UKF-style) projection for non-linear belief propagation is borrowed from state estimation but is, to the authors' knowledge, the first application to 2D dense cross-modal flow, extending VBReg's 3D outlier-rejection setting into a fully recurrent 2D registration loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-loop design—where calibrated posterior uncertainty directly modulates the Levenberg-Marquardt damping—offers a principled mechanism to prevent overconfident updates in ambiguous, occluded, or low-texture regions that plague deterministic cross-modal matchers. This is practically important for high-stakes applications (autonomous navigation, remote sensing, medical registration) where sub-pixel reliability under strict thresholds matters. Limitations include evaluation on only three datasets (all with relatively small displacements), a 2D-only formulation, and reliance on XoFTR pretraining for feature initialization; the authors themselves flag extension to 3D multi-modal scene reconstruction as future work. Whether the Bayesian feedback loop generalizes to larger geometric deformations or to modality pairs with even greater spectral gaps (e.g., thermal-SAR) remains open.
