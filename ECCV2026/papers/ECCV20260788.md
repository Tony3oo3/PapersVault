## Problem

Existing prompt-based visual counting methods can only specify what to count via inclusion prompts, with no mechanism to explicitly exclude visually similar distractors. In cluttered scenes containing confusable object categories (e.g., nylon vs. metal washers), this leads to ambiguity and overcounting, and naive workarounds like subtracting per-category density maps ignore the relational context between what should and shouldn't be counted.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a required positive text prompt, plus any combination of optional inputs: positive exemplar bounding boxes (typically 3 per category), a negative text prompt, and negative exemplar bounding boxes. It must flexibly handle all input configurations from text-only to full multimodal prompts. Output consists of detection boxes with confidence scores; the final count is obtained by summing detections above a confidence threshold, and an auxiliary density map is predicted during training for dense supervision.

## Method

CountEx builds on LLMDet's query encoder to produce separate positive and negative query sets from the same image conditioned on their respective prompts. The core contribution is a three-stage Discriminative Query Refinement (DQR) module: (1) Shared Feature Identification learns r context-aware prototypes via cross-attention over both query sets, capturing category-level attributes common to positives and negatives; (2) Exclusive Feature Extraction selects the m negative queries most distant from the shared prototype space and projects them onto C to retain only the negative-exclusive residual; (3) Selective Query Refinement applies cross-attention where positive queries attend to the negative-exclusive references, then subtracts the attended features through a gated residual connection, suppressing distractor patterns while preserving shared visual attributes. Training combines focal classification loss, L1 point-localization loss, an FPN-based density prediction loss, and prototype learning losses (shared-similarity and diversity) in an end-to-end objective.

## Results

On the introduced CoCount benchmark, CountEx achieves 26.61 MAE / 38.86 RMSE under the Novel-Category setting and 12.72 MAE / 23.99 RMSE under the Known-Category setting, outperforming the best baselines (GroundingREC at 29.29 MAE and CountGD at 15.55 MAE) by 8.5% and 18% respectively. On LOOKALIKES, CountEx sets a new zero-shot state-of-the-art with 18.53 MAE, surpassing all zero-shot baselines. On FSC-147 (single-category, no exclusion cues available), CountEx achieves 8.63 MAE, competitive with DAVE (8.66) but trailing the specialized CountGD (5.74).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CountEx outperforms all four CoCount baselines (CAD-GD, GroundingREC, CountGD, LLMDet) on both NC and KC settings, with the largest gap over its base architecture LLMDet (19.9% MAE reduction on NC, 24.4% on KC). Three naive negative-prompt strategies built on LLMDet (Detection-Suppression, Density-Subtraction, Count-All-then-Halve) all underperform CountEx, with Density-Subtraction being particularly harmful (47.71 MAE on NC), confirming that joint discriminative reasoning is essential. On LOOKALIKES, CountEx (18.53 MAE) beats all zero-shot baselines including OWLv2 (37.25), GroundingDINO (33.89), PseCo (59.82), DAVE (56.10), CountGD (22.34), and LLMDet (22.86); FiGO achieves a lower MAE (10.00) but requires per-category synthetic data generation and 5–7 minutes of test-time adaptation. On FSC-147, where exclusion prompts are unavailable, CountEx (8.63 MAE) trails the specialized CountGD (5.74) but edges out DAVE (8.66) and CACViT (9.13), indicating the exclusion mechanism is not fully exploitable in single-category scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

CountEx builds on the query-based open-vocabulary detection paradigm (specifically LLMDet) and extends the class-agnostic counting line of work (CountGD, GroundingREC, CAD-GD) by introducing a Discriminative Query Refinement module that jointly processes positive and negative prompts. The key conceptual departure from prior counting methods is the explicit modeling of exclusion intent: rather than treating negative cues as a post-hoc subtraction (which the paper shows is catastrophically ineffective), DQR decomposes negative queries into shared and exclusive components before selectively suppressing only the exclusive patterns from positive queries. The CoCount dataset also departs from prior counting benchmarks (FSC-147, LOOKALIKES) by featuring paired co-occurring categories with dense point annotations, enabling systematic evaluation of fine-grained discrimination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CoCount benchmark (10,086 annotated frames, 97 category pairs) fills a critical gap: prior datasets either contain a single dominant category or too few instances per category to train discriminative models, leading to dataset bias where models ignore prompts and count the majority class. The finding that naive density subtraction performs far worse than no exclusion at all (MAE 47.71 vs. 33.22) is a useful negative result that validates the need for structured decomposition over simple arithmetic on predictions. A notable limitation is that CountEx underperforms SOTA on FSC-147 (MAE 8.63 vs. 5.74 for CountGD), where exclusion cues are unavailable, suggesting the architecture's inductive bias toward discrimination may be a liability in single-category settings. Open questions include generalization to more than two co-occurring categories and whether the DQR mechanism transfers to other open-vocabulary tasks beyond counting.
