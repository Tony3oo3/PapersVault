## Problem

Existing spatio-temporal video grounding (STVG) methods process all frames simultaneously and are designed for short videos (typically under one minute), making them inapplicable to real-world scenarios where videos span minutes or hours. Processing an entire long video at once introduces severe computational bottlenecks and makes it difficult to capture long-range spatio-temporal dependencies while filtering out extensive irrelevant content. This paper introduces the Long-Form STVG (LF-STVG) problem and proposes the first framework specifically designed to handle it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20261181/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method ingests a long-form video (evaluated at 1, 3, or 5 minutes, sampled at 3.2 FPS with frames resized to a short side of 420) and a free-form textual query (up to 30 tokens). Unlike parallel STVG methods that see the entire video at once, ART-STVG consumes frames autoregressively one at a time. For each frame it outputs a 4-element bounding box (center x, center y, width, height) and two scalar probabilities indicating the likelihood that the frame is an event start or end boundary, which are combined across frames to recover the full spatio-temporal tube.

## Method

ART-STVG is a memory-augmented autoregressive transformer that processes video frames sequentially. A multimodal encoder fuses ResNet-101 appearance features, VidSwin motion features, and RoBERTa text features via a 6-block self-attention encoder. Two memory banks (spatial and temporal), each partitioned into K slots corresponding to K decoder blocks, store historical query features; spatial memory selection retrieves the top-N_s memories most similar to the text query, while temporal memory selection uses a TextTiling-inspired cosine-similarity threshold to segment events and retain only memories from the current event. A key architectural choice is the cascaded spatio-temporal design: the spatial decoder's predicted box is used to RoI-pool fine-grained motion features that condition the temporal decoder, allowing spatial localization to inform temporal boundary detection. Training is supervised with KL divergence on start/end probabilities plus L1 and IoU losses on boxes, initialized from MDETR weights, and the model uses only 7.9 GB GPU memory versus ~25 GB for parallel baselines.

## Results

On the newly introduced LF-STVG-3min benchmark (3-minute videos), ART-STVG achieves m_tIoU 23.0 and m_vIoU 15.3, outperforming the strongest baseline TA-STVG (m_tIoU 13.9, m_vIoU 8.5) by 9.1 and 6.8 points respectively. The advantage grows with video length: on LF-STVG-5min, ART-STVG reaches m_tIoU 15.0 vs. TA-STVG's 7.7 (+7.3), while on LF-STVG-1min the gap is smaller (+0.7 m_tIoU). On the original short-form HCSTVG-v2 validation set, ART-STVG achieves m_tIoU 59.2 and m_vIoU 39.2, trailing only TA-STVG (60.4 / 40.2) by 1.2 and 1.0 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ART-STVG outperforms all four compared STVG methods (TubeDETR, STCAT, CG-STVG, TA-STVG) on every metric across all three long-form benchmarks. The largest gains appear at longer video lengths: on LF-STVG-3min, ART-STVG surpasses TA-STVG by 9.1 m_tIoU and 6.8 m_vIoU, and on LF-STVG-5min by 7.3 m_tIoU and 5.5 m_vIoU, while existing methods degrade sharply (e.g., TA-STVG drops from m_tIoU 38.4 at 1 min to 7.7 at 5 min, whereas ART-STVG only drops from 39.1 to 15.0). On the short-form HCSTVG-v2 validation set, ART-STVG is competitive but slightly behind TA-STVG (59.2 vs. 60.4 m_tIoU; 39.2 vs. 40.2 m_vIoU), confirming that the autoregressive streaming design trades a small amount of short-video accuracy for substantially better long-video robustness. Compared to the paper's own baseline (same architecture without memory modules), ART-STVG gains 9.0 m_tIoU on 1-min, 6.8 on 3-min, and 5.8 on 5-min videos, isolating the contribution of selective memory. In terms of efficiency, ART-STVG uses only 7.9 GB GPU memory versus 25.1–25.9 GB for TA-STVG and CG-STVG, at the cost of slightly longer inference time (1.09 s vs. 0.69–0.71 s for 64 frames).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in spatio-temporal video grounding (STVG), where methods like TubeDETR, STCAT, CG-STVG, and TA-STVG process all frames of a short video simultaneously to make a single global prediction. ART-STVG instead repurposes the autoregressive, frame-by-frame processing paradigm (familiar from language modeling and streaming video understanding) for grounding, combined with memory banks inspired by long-video QA systems like MA-LMM. The cascaded spatio-temporal decoder design—where spatial localization output feeds into temporal localization via RoI-pooled motion features—is a structural departure from the parallel spatial/temporal decoders used in all prior one-stage STVG methods. The temporal memory selection strategy borrows the TextTiling segmentation idea, adapted to use inter-frame memory similarity as a proxy for event boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap between STVG research (videos under one minute) and real-world deployment (minutes to hours), and the autoregressive streaming design offers a practical GPU memory advantage (7.9 GB vs. ~25 GB for parallel methods) that makes long-video grounding feasible on consumer hardware. However, the evaluation is limited to a single benchmark family (HCSTVG-v2 extended to 1/3/5 minutes on the validation set only), and the model is trained exclusively on 20-second clips in the main experiments, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to truly hour-long videos or benefits substantially from long-video pretraining. The finding that indiscriminate memory use actively degrades performance (m_tIoU drops from 16.7 to 9.6) underscores that selective context is critical, but the optimal selection strategy and its interaction with video length remain underexplored.
