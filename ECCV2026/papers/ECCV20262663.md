## Problem

Existing Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) suffer from three intertwined issues: inherent learning conflicts between visual understanding and generation objectives, fragmented visual spaces (semantic for understanding, VAE latent for generation) that impede scalability, and task-specific training data that fails to exploit the natural duality between text-image understanding and generation. These limitations prevent UMMs from achieving the hypothesized mutual promotion between the two capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262663/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For visual generation, the model takes a text prompt and produces an image up to 1024×1024 resolution in the Flux-VAE latent space (16 channels, 8× downsample), sampled with an Adams-2nd solver at 25 steps and CFG=4. For understanding, it accepts a noisy image at any timestep t∈[0,1] (with t=1.0 for clean evaluation) plus a text prompt, and autoregressively decodes a text response. Training uses native aspect ratio to avoid center-crop mismatches, and the Noisy ViT encoder consumes either raw pixels or VAE latents depending on the chosen visual space.

## Method

UniDDT comprises a Noisy ViT encoder (interleaved Attention/FFN blocks with AdaLN-zero timestep injection), an LLM backbone (Qwen3 or Qwen3-VL), and a separate diffusion decoder. The Noisy ViT encoder extracts semantic features z_t from noisy input x_t and timestep t; the LLM backbone then either autoregressively decodes text (understanding) or injects prompt semantics into z_t to produce refined features ẑ_t (generation), which condition the diffusion decoder's velocity estimation via cross-attention rather than AdaLN. The key architectural insight is decoupling semantic modeling (ViT+LLM) from high-frequency visual decoding (diffusion decoder) while unifying both tasks in a single latent visual space. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) warmup via knowledge distillation from a pre-trained VFM teacher to the Noisy ViT, then frozen-encoder diffusion decoder training; (2) joint training where the same image-text pairs are formatted in dual 'generate' and 'describe' templates, applying cross-entropy loss to text and flow-matching loss to images simultaneously; (3) post-training that freezes the understanding branch and fine-tunes only the diffusion decoder by maximizing the understanding likelihood of intermediate denoising states, directly improving semantic consistency of generated images.

## Results

On visual generation, VLM-UniDDT achieves 0.87 overall GenEval score and 86.9 DPG-Bench score, while Native-UniDDT-XL reaches 0.89 GenEval and 87.1 DPG. On multimodal understanding, VLM-UniDDT (4B+1B params) attains 1699.5 on MME, 76.5 on SEED-Bench, 82.2 on MMB, and 52.6 on MMMU, outperforming most unified models of comparable or larger scale. The model uses the Flux-VAE latent space (16 channels, 8× downsample) as its unified visual representation and is trained on ~70M images with native aspect ratio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DPG-Bench, VLM-UniDDT (86.9) outperforms all unified baselines including Show-o2-7B (86.14), Janus-Pro-7B (84.19), MetaQuery-XL (82.05), and BLIP3-o-8B (81.60), as well as the generation-only SD3-Medium (84.08). On GenEval, VLM-UniDDT (0.87) surpasses Show-o2-7B (0.76†), Janus-Pro-7B (0.80), and BLIP3-o-8B (0.84), though BAGEL-7B reports a marginally higher 0.88† (with prompt rewriting). On understanding benchmarks, VLM-UniDDT leads on MME (1699.5 vs. BAGEL's 1687.0 and Show-o2's 1620.5) and SEED-Bench (76.5 vs. Mogao's 74.6 and Show-o2's 69.8), but trails BAGEL-7B on MMB (82.2 vs. 85.0) and MMMU (52.6 vs. 55.3). The model achieves these results with only 4B+1B parameters, compared to 7B–14B for most competing unified models, suggesting favorable parameter efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniDDT extends the DDT (Decoupled Diffusion Transformer) architecture by replacing its monolithic condition encoder with a Noisy ViT encoder plus an LLM backbone, thereby unifying semantic extraction for understanding and semantic encoding for generation within a single pathway. It departs from prior native UMMs (Janus, Show-o, BAGEL) that use parallel branches or task-specific decoders, instead framing understanding as a prerequisite for generation and using the LLM to perform semantic injection into visual features before diffusion decoding. The duality-based data construction—deriving both understanding and generation training samples from the same image-text pairs—contrasts with the task-specific data stitching used by most existing UMMs, and the post-training stage that feeds intermediate generation states back into the understanding branch has no direct precedent in prior unified models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that decoupling semantic modeling (Noisy ViT + LLM) from high-frequency visual decoding (diffusion decoder) within a unified latent space can achieve competitive performance on both generation (0.87 GenEval, 86.9 DPG) and understanding (1699.5 MME, 76.5 SEED) without the severe trade-offs seen in parallel-branch UMMs. The duality-based training and post-training strategies offer a practical recipe for leveraging limited data, though the Native-UniDDT variant's inability to follow instructions (only captioning) highlights that data richness remains a bottleneck. Open questions include whether the pixel-space formulation could close the gap with latent space once JiT-style prediction is incorporated, and whether the duality approach scales beyond the ~70M image dataset used here.
