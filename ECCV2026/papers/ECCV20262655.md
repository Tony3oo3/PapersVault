## Problem

Diffusion models frequently produce visual artifacts (hallucinations) during generation, yet existing training-free refinement methods either degrade semantic consistency (FreeU), fail on Transformer backbones (ASCED), or introduce over-saturation (PAG). The paper identifies that artifact-associated dynamics are concentrated in early denoising stages and reflected in deep internal latents, but no prior work provides a unified, backbone-agnostic mechanism to detect and suppress these anomalies without additional training.

## Input / Output

DUNE operates entirely at inference time on a pre-trained diffusion model, consuming the internal latents (U-Net h-space at 1280×32×32 for SDXL, or self-attention token representations at 3/4 depth for Transformers) at each denoising step alongside the standard text-prompt conditioning. It produces a refined image with suppressed artifacts while preserving the original semantic content, verified via CLIP similarity. No additional parameters, training, or memory overhead are introduced; the only hyperparameters are a detection percentile (p=0.9) and a suppression strength κ fixed per backbone.

## Method

DUNE partitions denoising into a detect phase (1.0T–0.6T) and an optional detail phase (0.6T–0.0T). During detection, it computes a score-normalized latent z_t = −h_t/σ_t, tracks its exponential moving average (γ=0.7), and flags regions where the log-ratio Δ = log|z_t/z̄_{t+1}| exceeds a quantile threshold, yielding a binary anomaly mask. Suppression is backbone-specific: U-Net h-space channels are shrunk via a channel-aware scaling S_UNet = κ(n_t·h_t) to preserve per-channel semantic specialization, while Transformer self-attention latents use masked EMA blending S_Tr = κ·h_t + (1−κ)·h̄_t. Theoretical analysis (Propositions 1–2, Theorem 3) justifies operating in these deepest latents by showing they inherently contract noise (via small kernel norms or diffuse attention weights) and that masked suppression provably increases latent SNR and bounds score perturbation.

## Results

DUNE improves FID over the original generator on all five tested backbones (e.g., 18.86→18.75 on SDXL, 22.53→22.41 on LCM, 21.36→20.76 on Kandinsky 3) while dramatically reducing the HADM anatomical-defect metric (0.227→0.074 on SDXL, 0.152→0.052 on LCM, 0.250→0.095 on Kandinsky 3). CLIP similarity between original and DUNE-refined images remains high (0.925–0.988), confirming minimal semantic drift. DUNE also reduces the excess target-vs-random score-acceleration gap by 24.9% on SDXL and 18.1% on PixArt-Σ, validating that the detector-selected regions are the true artifact sources.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUNE outperforms all three baselines on every backbone where they are jointly supported. Against FreeU, DUNE improves FID by 12.47 (SDXL: 18.75 vs 31.22) and 5.16 (LCM: 22.41 vs 27.57) while maintaining CLIP similarity above 0.92 versus FreeU's 0.76–0.78, indicating FreeU introduces substantial semantic drift. Against ASCED, DUNE achieves lower FID on all three shared backbones (e.g., 18.75 vs 22.07 on SDXL) and avoids the complete failure (pure noise) that ASCED exhibits on both Transformer backbones (PixArt-Σ and Hunyuan-DiT). Against PAG, DUNE matches or beats FID on SDXL (18.75 vs 21.72) and PixArt-Σ (27.80 vs 32.61), while PAG catastrophically degrades LCM FID (35.87 vs 22.41) and drops CLIP similarity to 0.362. On HADM, DUNE achieves the largest reductions across all backbones (e.g., 0.227→0.074 on SDXL), whereas PAG's HADM gains are modest (0.227→0.210 on SDXL) and come with over-saturation artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

DUNE sits at the intersection of three lines of training-free diffusion refinement: FreeU's component reweighting, ASCED's temporal score-dynamics analysis, and PAG's attention-level perturbation guidance. It departs from FreeU by replacing uniform scaling with a detector-gated, phase-aware correction that avoids the semantic drift and over-saturation FreeU exhibits; from ASCED by intervening on internal deep latents rather than directly editing the score trajectory (which the paper shows produces pure noise on Transformer backbones); and from PAG by using backbone-specific suppression operators instead of channel-agnostic masking. The theoretical grounding—Propositions 1 and 2 showing that downsampling convolutions and self-attention both contract isotropic noise toward deep latents—provides a principled justification for why h-space and mid-to-deep self-attention layers are the correct intervention points, unifying the U-Net and Transformer cases under a single detect-suppress criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The phase-aware decomposition (detect phase at 1.0T–0.6T for artifact correction, optional detail phase at 0.6T–0.0T for stylistic control) offers a practical, training-free recipe that practitioners can plug into any U-Net or DiT pipeline without retraining, and the substantial HADM reductions (e.g., 0.227→0.074 on SDXL) suggest meaningful gains in anatomical fidelity that FID alone would miss. The finding that direct score-trajectory editing (ASCED) catastrophically fails on high-resolution Transformer models is a useful caution for the field. Open questions include fully automatic threshold selection (the current percentile p=0.9 and per-backbone κ require a small sweep), whether the detect-suppress principle extends to video or 3D diffusion, and whether the early-phase concentration of artifacts holds under different schedulers or few-step samplers beyond the DDPM formulation used here.
