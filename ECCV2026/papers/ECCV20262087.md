## Problem

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models frequently execute visually plausible actions even when the language instruction is semantically contradictory to the scene, a failure mode the authors term linguistic blindness. Existing benchmarks measure task success under valid instructions and cannot distinguish whether success arises from genuine language grounding or purely visual heuristics, leaving this vulnerability undiagnosed and unaddressed in deployed robotic policies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20262087/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The VLA policies consume a single RGB observation frame (from wrist and third-person cameras in the real-robot setup) paired with a natural-language instruction, and output a continuous robot action sequence. Under ICBench, the visual observation is held fixed while the instruction is perturbed with controlled semantic contradictions (attribute substitutions, spatial relation swaps), so the only variable is the text. IGAR itself introduces no new input or output channels; it operates entirely within the forward pass, adding roughly 7 ms of per-step latency on an RTX 5090 for OpenVLA-OFT.

## Method

IGAR is a train-free, plug-and-play inference-time intervention that restores linguistic grounding by correcting vision-dominant attention sink imbalance in transformer-based VLA backbones. It proceeds in three stages: (1) attention sink token detection via per-token RMS norm combined with a per-dimension spike ratio (max/mean) to identify localized extreme activations characteristic of sinks; (2) grounding head selection, which flags cross-modal heads where action-query tokens allocate meaningful attention to visual tokens but are not already dominated by visual sinks; (3) attention redistribution, which scales down sink-token attention by a fixed decay factor (p=0.6) and reallocates the freed budget proportionally to non-sink instruction tokens. The method requires no gradient updates, no additional training data, and no architectural modification, and is applied uniformly across the first 16 transformer layers with fixed thresholds, making it directly deployable on existing VLA policies.

## Results

Under ICBench contradictory instructions, IGAR substantially increases the Linguistic Grounding Score (LGS) across all three VLA architectures. For π0, LGS rises from a baseline range of 0.6–19.4 to 5.2–59.4, with the largest gain in the Goal suite (V4: LGS 59.4, SR dropping from 76.4% to 36.4%). OpenVLA-OFT also shows strong improvements (LGS up to 39.6 in Goal V4), while π0.5 exhibits the most limited gains (LGS up to 14.8). Under normal instructions, IGAR preserves baseline performance with only a −0.4% average SR change for π0 and +0.5% for OpenVLA-OFT, adding just ~7 ms inference latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper applies IGAR as a plug-and-free module on three VLA architectures (π0, π0.5, OpenVLA-OFT) and compares each model's performance with and without the intervention under ICBench contradictory instructions. π0 benefits the most: LGS increases from 19.4 to 59.4 in Goal V4 (a 3× improvement), and SR drops from 76.4% to 36.4%, indicating the policy now correctly refuses to execute impossible instructions. OpenVLA-OFT also shows substantial gains, with LGS rising from 7.8 to 39.6 in Goal V4 and SR dropping from 90.2% to 58.4%. In contrast, π0.5 exhibits the weakest response to IGAR, with LGS improving only modestly (e.g., 10.2→14.8 in Goal V2) and SR remaining above 82% in most settings, suggesting its action generation is more deeply locked into visual priors. Under normal (non-contradictory) instructions, all three models retain near-identical success rates with IGAR (average change ≤1.5%), confirming the intervention does not degrade in-distribution performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the attention-sink phenomenon identified in LLM and MLLM literature (Xiao et al. 2024; Kang et al. 2025) for a fundamentally different setting: robotic action generation in VLA models. While prior attention-sink mitigation methods target text generation quality, IGAR is positioned as the first inference-time intervention designed specifically for cross-modal attention in VLA policies. On the evaluation side, the paper departs from data-centric strategies (CAST, CounterfactualVLA) and perturbation-robustness benchmarks (LIBERO-PLUS) by introducing a controlled diagnostic that isolates language-action coupling through structured instruction contradictions, rather than measuring task success under valid inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that VLA models complete tasks at >90% success under logically impossible instructions raises a serious safety concern for real-world deployment, where blindly executing visually plausible actions can cause physical damage. IGAR's train-free, plug-and-play nature makes it immediately deployable without retraining, which is practically valuable. However, the evaluation is limited to 30 LIBERO tasks and three architectures, and IGAR's effectiveness is highly inconsistent (π0.5 shows minimal improvement), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to longer-horizon tasks, more diverse embodiments, or architectures with fundamentally different action decoders. The single real-world validation on one Franka task also limits confidence in the safety claims.
